## Executive Summary

TransMedics owns the only FDA-approved portable, multi-organ warm perfusion platform and has wrapped it in a service business — the National OCS Program — that includes an owned fleet of transplant aircraft. That combination is genuinely difficult to replicate, and it is still producing above-market growth: Q2 2026 revenue of $189.9M grew 20.7% against underlying U.S. heart/liver/lung transplant volume growth of roughly 5%. On August 4 management raised the low end of FY2026 guidance to $737–757M and posted a sequential margin recovery (GAAP operating margin 7.6% in Q1 to 12.5% in Q2). The stock fell roughly 9% the following session.

The reason the market keeps discounting operational progress is that the earnings line is going the wrong way and the balance sheet changed character in a single quarter. Consensus 2026 EPS has been cut from $2.46 to $1.81 over 90 days (-26.2%) and 2027 from $3.68 to $2.87 (-22.1%), with the 30-day revisions still negative (-8.1% and -10.4%). GAAP operating margin has fallen from 21.3% in H1 2025 to 12.5% in Q2 2026 while R&D nearly doubled, driven mostly by outsourced consultants. FY2025's headline $190.3M of net income exceeded pretax income of $107.5M because of an $82.8M net tax benefit from a valuation-allowance release; tax-normalized FY2025 earnings were closer to $80.6M, and management now guides a 25–27% effective rate. H1 2026 free cash flow was negative $1.9M (roughly +$28M excluding $30M of one-time Somerville land purchases), against +$52.7M a year earlier.

Layered on top: a $404.3M Somerville headquarters finance lease created January 8, 2026 lifted total liabilities 59% to $946.3M and liabilities/equity from 1.26x to 1.83x, with a $374.6M purchase option priced to encourage exercise by December 31, 2027; a $60.0M CIBC facility secured by substantially all assets including IP, amortizing to a July 2027 maturity; and $460.0M of converts due June 2028 struck at $94.00, 22% above spot. That is roughly $895M of contractual and conditional calls through mid-2028 against $491.1M of cash and restricted cash, which points toward a financing event at a depressed equity price. Management concluded disclosure controls were **not** effective at June 30, 2026 — a sixth consecutive quarter with an unremediated inventory-movement material weakness, sitting precisely where the product gross margin narrative is being told. The securities class action survived a motion to dismiss on July 21, 2026 and is in discovery with nothing accrued. Revenue concentration in U.S. liver rose from 75.9% of FY2025 to 78.9% of H1 2026 while lung revenue fell 43% and heart grew 1.4%. We move to **AVOID**, with the explicit caveat that 34.8% short interest and 7.2 days to cover make this unattractive to short.

## Company Overview

TransMedics operates one reportable segment. The Organ Care System (OCS) replaces static cold storage with normothermic machine perfusion, allowing organs to be assessed and optimized outside the body. OCS Heart, Lung and Liver each hold Pre-Market Approval for both donation-after-brain-death and donation-after-circulatory-death organs — a regulatory position competitors must rebuild organ by organ.

The commercially decisive asset is the National OCS Program, a turnkey outsourced service under which TransMedics performs procurement, perfusion management and transplant logistics for U.S. centers. Following the 2023 Summit Aviation acquisition, the balance sheet carries $295.6M gross of transplant aircraft plus $3.7M of flight-school aircraft. Service revenue carries 31–35% gross margin versus 77–81% for product. Q2 2026 split $111.2M product (+16%) and $78.8M service (+29%), so mix shift toward service is structurally margin-dilutive at the gross line even when execution is good.

Organ and geographic mix is the dominant structural fact. Q2 2026: U.S. liver $148.0M (+27.7%, 77.9% of revenue), U.S. heart $33.4M (+3.8%), U.S. lung $2.25M (-45.8%), all non-U.S. $5.24M (2.8%). This is not customer concentration — the 10-Q states no customer exceeded 10% of revenue or receivables in any 2025 or 2026 period presented. The exposure is to one organ, one country, and one allocation regime.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2023 $241.6M; FY2024 $441.5M (+82.7%); FY2025 $605.5M (+37.1%); Q1 2026 $173.9M (+21.2%); Q2 2026 $189.9M (+20.7%). FY2026 guidance of $737–757M implies +21.7% to +25.0% and excludes any PAD Aviation revenue and any contribution from the ENHANCE Part B (heart) or DENOVO (lung) trials. Implied H2 2026 of $373.1–393.1M versus $304.6M a year ago requires roughly flat-to-modestly-higher sequential quarters from Q2 — attainable rather than heroic. Q2 revenue of $189.95M beat the ~$184M Zacks consensus by ~3%.

**Margins.** GAAP operating margin by period: H1 2025 21.3%; H2 2025 ~14.6% (derived from a rounded FY2025 margin input, ±~$2M of precision); Q1 2026 7.6%; Q2 2026 12.5%. Adjusted operating margin was 13.6% in Q2. Total gross margin ~58% in Q1 to 60% in Q2; product gross margin fell from 81% to 77% on freight, inventory provisioning, hub stocking and clinical-trial product costs that management describes as temporary; service gross margin improved to 35% from 32%.

Two observations. First, compression began in H2 2025, before the 2026 investment cycle — visible to anyone bridging the FY2025 10-K against the H1 2025 10-Q. Second, Q2's ~490bp sequential operating-margin recovery is real, and much of the compression is chosen: Q2 R&D of $31.6M versus $15.9M was driven by +$10.6M of consulting and third-party services for the next-generation OCS and OCS Kidney, with internal R&D personnel up only $1.8M. Outsourced spend is throttleable. That cuts both ways: margin recovery is within management's control, and the Gen 3.0/Kidney know-how is being built outside the company.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 net income of $190.3M on pretax income of $107.5M reflects an ~$82.8M net tax benefit from the Q4 2025 valuation-allowance release ($103.3M gross). The screen-level FY2025 ratios that follow from it — 31.4% net margin, 40.2% ROE, 17.8% ROA — are not repeatable, and the reported trailing P/E of 18.1x built on $4.87 of trailing EPS is an artifact. On TTM net income excluding the tax benefit (~$68.9M, ~$1.75/share), the trailing multiple is ~41.9x. Q2 2026 GAAP net income was $14.7M ($0.41 diluted); adjusted $16.2M ($0.44) versus $34.9M ($0.92) a year earlier when GAAP equalled adjusted. Non-GAAP adjustments in Q2 totalled only $2.055M against $23.7M of GAAP operating income — modest and clearly itemized, which is a point in the company's favor.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow $41.8M (H1 2025: $88.8M); capex $43.7M; free cash flow **-$1.9M** versus +$52.7M. Excluding the two Somerville parcels bought for $15.0M each on January 8, 2026, normalized H1 FCF is roughly +$28M — still down ~47% year over year. The operating decline reflects lower net income ($22.0M versus $60.6M) plus $19.9M of receivable build and $10.2M of inventory build; receivables grew 23.5% against 20.7% revenue growth, a modest divergence. Transplant volume is described in MD&A as "generally unpredictable," so quarterly cash flow should not be annualized.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $472.7M plus $18.4M restricted at June 30, 2026 (the restricted balance is the $17.9M letter of credit posted to BioMed Realty). Total assets rose to $1,464.4M from $1,068.4M and total liabilities to $946.3M from $595.3M, driven by the $347.7M finance lease liability. Current ratio 6.62x. Liabilities/equity 1.83x from 1.26x. Gross contractual debt — $460.0M converts, $60.0M CIBC, $347.7M finance lease — totals $867.7M; net of $491.1M cash and restricted cash, net debt is ~$376.6M.

Recomputing the same Altman Z components on June 30 data gives ~1.92 versus 2.61 at December 31, 2025, with all five components deteriorating. Two qualifications matter: the lease liability is offset by a $332.5M right-of-use asset for a building the company intends to own, and Altman does not distinguish a real-estate finance lease from operating leverage. The score decline overstates near-term insolvency risk; it correctly flags that the capital structure changed character abruptly for an asset that generates no revenue.

**Capital structure specifics.** The 1.50% converts ($460.0M principal, ~$94.00 conversion, 4,893,805 underlying shares) are ~22% out of the money; TransMedics elects cash, share or mixed settlement, and purchased $52.1M of capped calls struck at $94.00 with a $141.88 cap. The company's own call right and holders' conversion right both require the stock to trade at $122.20 for 20 of 30 days — not met. Reported fair value of $501.4M (109% of par) is explicitly a Level 2 mark from a trade "in an inactive market." The CIBC term loan is guaranteed by all material subsidiaries, secured by substantially all assets including IP, amortizes monthly from July 2026 to a July 2027 maturity ($20.0M current), and carries a covenant requiring net revenue of at least 75% of the plan presented to CIBC plus a material-adverse-change default. In compliance at June 30.

The Somerville lease is the largest single commitment: 18 years, ~498,286 sq ft, base rent starting January 2028 at $23.9M/year escalating 2% then 3%, plus a purchase option **held** by TransMedics at $374.6M if exercised on or before December 31, 2027. Because exercise was judged reasonably certain, ASC 842 requires finance-lease treatment — $404.3M of combined consideration, a $336.6M ROU asset, a 4.4% incremental borrowing rate. Management separately estimates $200–240M of buildout capex through 2030, of which only $12.7M was contracted at June 30.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth remains well above the addressable market's ~5% volume growth, so essentially all of it is penetration of existing transplant volume. That is powerful while penetration is low and mathematically self-limiting as U.S. liver saturates, which is the most plausible explanation for the 83% → 37% → 22–25% path. The two programs that would reset the ceiling — OCS Kidney (by far the largest transplant category by volume) and non-U.S. expansion (2.8% of revenue after four-plus years with CE marks) — are precisely the programs currently consuming margin, and neither contributes revenue in the guidance window.

Within the existing franchise, growth is narrowing rather than broadening. H1 2026 U.S. heart grew 1.4%; U.S. lung fell 42.9% and neither the 10-K nor the 10-Q explains why. The most internally consistent hypothesis is that commercial lung cases were redirected into the DENOVO trial (guidance explicitly assumes no incremental trial revenue, and the 2025 proxy set a ~150-case lung clinical objective), but the same proxy set a parallel ~150-case heart objective and heart revenue held roughly flat rather than falling 43%. Competitive share loss to XVIVO, whose franchise is strongest in lung, remains a live alternative. Until the company explains it, lung has to be treated as an unresolved negative.

The earnings side of growth is contracting. Tax-normalized FY2025 net income of ~$80.6M equates to roughly $2.23 per diluted share (on ~36.0M H1 2026 diluted shares); FY2026 adjusted net income of $63–67M (holding Q2 opex roughly flat at a 25–27% tax rate) equates to roughly $1.75–1.85. Consensus at $1.81 for 2026 is consistent with that; consensus at $2.87 for 2027 requires a ~59% EPS rebound driven by margin expansion the company has not yet demonstrated for two consecutive quarters. The revision trend is the single most important forward datapoint here, and it points down: 2026 estimates cut 8.1% in 30 days and 26.2% in 90; 2027 cut 10.4% and 22.1%. The last two quarters missed EPS by 52.0% and 8.5% after two large beats.

At $73.305, market cap is ~$2,538M and enterprise value including the finance lease is ~$2,915M ($2,567M excluding it).

| P/E on 2026 consensus $1.81 | 40.5x |
| P/E on 2027 consensus $2.87 | 25.5x |

The bull framing is that 3.4x forward EV/sales for a 22–25% grower with a PMA moat and a hard-to-replicate service network is not demanding, and that 25.5x 2027 consensus sits below the 27.2x peer median. The problem is that the 2027 denominator is the number being cut fastest. Marking 2027 EPS to $2.40 — a conservative but not punitive haircut given the 30-day revision pace and the fact that FY2026 requires product gross margin to recover from 77% toward 81% — puts the stock at 30.5x. Holding EV/sales at 3.1x on FY2027 revenue of ~$900M yields $67 per share after net debt. Neither route produces a margin of safety.

The 2027–2028 obligation stack independently compresses the multiple the market will pay. Stacking disclosed items: $60.0M CIBC through July 2027, the $374.6M Somerville option by December 31, 2027, $460.0M of converts on June 1, 2028, plus $200–240M of buildout capex through 2030. Against $491.1M of cash and restricted cash and, at a generous $150M/year of normalized FCF, roughly $790M of cumulative resources versus ~$895M of calls before buildout. With the stock 22% below the $94.00 strike, refinancing into equity is unavailable on favorable terms, which leaves a mortgage or sale-leaseback on Somerville (plausible and cheap — the 4.4% incremental borrowing rate suggests as much), a new convertible, or equity. Two mitigants deserve weight: the purchase option is a right, and walking away substitutes $23.9M/year of escalating rent from January 2028 for a $374.6M outlay; and a building is the best collateral class available. An equity that carries a probable financing event at a depressed price does not re-rate before that event resolves.

Analyst targets span $80 (low) to $125 (high), mean $106, median $113, with sentiment characterized as degrading and the rating mix drifting from 2/6/3 to 2/5/4 over three months. On a stock where estimates are falling this fast, targets are lagging indicators rather than floors — the multiple-based reason to treat consensus targets as stale (cheap stock, rising estimates) is absent here.

Valuation score of 5.2 reflects a fair-to-slightly-rich multiple on the earnings the company is actually delivering, with the identified catalyst (financing) pointing the wrong way. Cheapness on stale trailing figures is not an argument.

## Competitive Landscape

The device-level moat is narrowing while the service-level moat widens. Terumo acquired OrganOx, the leading normothermic liver perfusion competitor, in October 2025; Getinge acquired Paragonix in September 2024; XVIVO competes independently across lung, heart and liver. TransMedics' PMA breadth and multi-organ platform remain unmatched, but two strategics with far larger balance sheets now own the closest alternatives. Large strategics typically compete on price and channel rather than building outsourced service businesses, so the near-term risk is disposable pricing pressure rather than NOP displacement, though Terumo and Getinge can fund an aviation-plus-logistics answer if they choose.

Two non-device substitutes matter more than device share. Static cold storage remains the incumbent standard of care. Normothermic regional perfusion, a surgical technique performed in the donor, lets a center improve DCD organ utilization without buying a disposable or a service — a structural threat invisible in perfusion-device share data.

Regulatory structure is the third variable. HRSA has expanded transparency through a public dashboard for allocation-out-of-sequence events while moving OPTN from a sole-vendor UNOS contract (extended only through December 29, 2026 in optional three-month tranches) to a multi-vendor model. Management states the impact is uncertain. Out-of-sequence and open-offer placements are one mechanism by which organs that would otherwise go unused reach an OCS-equipped center, so greater scrutiny is a plausible constraint on the liver NOP model; equally, transparency could validate that OCS-enabled placements convert declined organs into transplants. The direction is unresolved, and ~78% of revenue is exposed to it.

Within liver, TransMedics is clearly still taking share (+27.7% against ~5% market volume growth). That is why competitive position scores 6.0 rather than lower — the trajectory in the core franchise is positive even as mix, substitutes and competitor ownership deteriorate around it.

## Risk Assessment

**Internal control and reporting quality.** The inventory-movement material weakness identified with the FY2024 audit is unremediated after six quarters, and the CEO and CFO concluded disclosure controls and procedures were **not effective** at June 30, 2026. Remediation controls were implemented during Q2 but must operate before testing. Separately, Q1 2026 recorded a $2.5M out-of-period adjustment reducing deferred tax assets. The specific gap — interim inventory movement within the manufacturing network — sits exactly where management is attributing product gross margin's fall from 81% to 77% (inventory provisioning, hub stocking). This is not evidence of misstatement, and auditors permitted an unqualified conclusion, but interim product-margin figures warrant more than usual skepticism until the FY2026 10-K resolves remediation.

**Litigation.** The consolidated *Jewik/Collins* action (D. Mass., class period February 28, 2023 – January 10, 2025) survived in part on July 21, 2026; the answer was due August 18, 2026 and the case proceeds to discovery. No loss range estimated, nothing accrued. The class period ends at the January 2025 activist short report, which also generated an FDA citizen petition seeking suspension of the OCS PMA, particularly OCS Liver, on off-label-use allegations, plus $2.358M of H1 2025 legal expense. Item 3 of the FY2025 10-K discloses no government investigation or enforcement action, and the petition's disposition is not stated in any filing reviewed.

**Concentration.** U.S. liver at 78.9% of H1 2026 revenue, up from 75.9% in FY2025, with the growing service line itself predominantly liver NOP logistics. A single adverse development in liver allocation practice, reimbursement or competitive standing hits nearly four-fifths of revenue simultaneously.

**Operational single points of failure.** One manufacturing facility (Andover, MA; Italy under construction); Fresenius Kabi as single-source supplier of OCS Solutions for Lung and Heart; $10M-per-occurrence product liability insurance for a life-critical device; and a new aviation-specific risk paragraph citing FAA compliance and pilot availability against service revenue that is 41% of the total.

**Financing.** Covered above. The CIBC facility's revenue-plan covenant and MAC default clause tighten the linkage between an operating stumble and a credit event.

**Undisclosed deal terms.** The July 1, 2026 PAD Aviation service GmbH investment has no disclosed ownership percentage, consideration or consolidation treatment. Guidance excludes revenue "attributable to" it, which implies consolidation, but that is an inference.

Put/call skew is balanced at 1.0%, and put/call volume is 0.96, so there is no directional conviction in flow. Combined with 34.8% short interest and 7.2 days to cover, the positioning picture is crowded-bearish with hedges lifted — a configuration that supports sharp counter-trend rallies on good news without changing the fundamental setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($105–115).** The Q1 2026 GAAP operating margin of 7.6% was the trough. Product gross margin recovers toward 81% as freight, hub stocking and trial-related product costs roll off; outsourced R&D consulting is throttled once Gen 3.0 architecture completes; FY2026 lands at the top of the raised range and FY2027 revenue approaches $920M with adjusted operating margin back near 18%. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches ~$3.20, and the market pays 34x for a moated 20%+ grower with kidney optionality, or ~4.3x EV/sales — either route reaches the $106 consensus mean and $113 median. Somerville is financed with a mortgage at or near the 4.4% borrowing rate, removing the funding overhang without equity. A short base of 7.7M shares against ~1.0M average daily volume amplifies any upside re-rating. This case requires two consecutive clean quarters of margin expansion and a disclosed financing solution.

**Bear case ($42).** Estimate revisions continue at the observed pace; FY2027 adjusted EPS settles near $2.05 as service mix, kidney and Gen 3.0 spending, a 25–27% tax rate and finance-lease interest ($3.8M in Q2 alone) absorb operating leverage. Lung erosion proves competitive rather than trial-related and heart stays flat, leaving the whole story dependent on a liver franchise facing AOOS transparency scrutiny. The 2027 financing arrives as equity or an equity-linked deal at a discount, and discovery in the securities case produces adverse disclosures. 18x $2.05 gives $37; 2.2x FY2027 sales less ~$400M net debt gives $42. Call it -43% from spot.

**Base case ($66, -10%).** Guidance is met, margins recover partially but not to 2025 levels, FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $2.40 (16% below current consensus), and the multiple stays capped by the financing overhang until it resolves. 25x $2.40 = $60; 3.1x FY2027 revenue of $900M less $377M net debt over ~35.8M shares = $67. Blended base $66.

The distribution is wide in both directions, which is why this is not ownable at the current multiple: the base case is flat-to-down, the bear case is -43%, and the bull case requires two things (margin recovery and a benign financing outcome) that have not yet been demonstrated even once.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** There is no cleanly dated bearish catalyst. The nearest candidates are the Q3 2026 print (early November 2026), where a third consecutive quarter of product gross margin below 80% or a guidance trim would confirm the bear case; the FY2026 10-K (approximately late February 2027), which resolves whether the material weakness is remediated; and the Somerville purchase-option decision window closing December 31, 2027, which forces the financing question. The most probable price-moving event — a financing announcement — is not dated and could be benign (a mortgage at ~4.4%).

**Timing window.** Six to eighteen months for the financing question to resolve; three months to the next earnings checkpoint. That is a long carry for a thesis whose fundamental leg (revenue growth) is still working.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 34.8% of float short, 7,715,332 shares, 7.2 days to cover against ~1.01M average daily volume. This is among the most crowded shorts in mid-cap health care equipment. The August 3–4 move from $76.51 to $81.55 on no company-specific news demonstrates how quickly the book can move against a short.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options are not confirming a bearish thesis. The put/call open-interest collapse from 7.05 to 2.39 over three weeks means the protection that existed into the print has been lifted. Near-term IV at 69.7% versus 58.6% realized makes put-based expressions expensive, and the flat term structure offers no cheap tenor.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear case $42 (-43%); realistic squeeze risk to the $105–115 analyst zone on two clean quarters (+43% to +57%). That is roughly 1:1, well short of the 2:1 threshold, and the asymmetry worsens once borrow cost and the crowded book are priced.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish analysis is sound but the trade is not. At 34.8% short interest, 53% below the December 2025 high, with hedges unwound and no dated catalyst, the remaining downside does not compensate for squeeze risk. Avoid owning; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID. Expected duration of headwinds: 3–6 quarters.**

**Re-entry criteria — any two of the following would move this to HOLD; three would support a BUY:**

1. **Two consecutive quarters of margin expansion**, specifically product gross margin at or above 80% and GAAP operating margin at or above 16%, demonstrating that the freight/hub/trial cost items management calls temporary actually were. Q2's 12.5% is one quarter, not a trend.
2. **A disclosed, non-dilutive solution to the 2027–2028 stack** — a mortgage or sale-leaseback on Somerville at a rate near the 4.4% incremental borrowing rate, or explicit abandonment of the purchase option in favor of the $23.9M/year rent path, or a refinancing of the converts without equity issuance.
3. **Estimate revisions turning positive** — 30-day revisions for the next fiscal year moving above zero after four consecutive negative readings. At present 2027 consensus is being cut 10.4% per month.
4. **Remediation of the material weakness confirmed** in the FY2026 10-K, with disclosure controls concluded effective.
5. **Organ-mix broadening** — U.S. heart returning to double-digit growth, an explanation and stabilization of U.S. lung, or non-U.S. revenue exceeding 6% of total.
6. **Price.** Below $55 — roughly 2.4x FY2027 EV/sales and ~23x a haircut $2.40 FY2027 EPS — the financing and litigation overhangs would be adequately compensated even without operational confirmation.

**Conditions that would deepen the negative view:** a guidance cut at the Q3 print; U.S. liver revenue growth decelerating below 15%; product gross margin below 77%; an adverse development in the securities case or the FDA citizen petition; a CIBC covenant amendment or waiver; or an equity raise at a discount to spot.

**Next natural reassessment point:** the Q3 2026 report, expected early November 2026, with the securities-case docket (answer filed August 18, 2026; early discovery thereafter) monitored in the interim.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory for this name has fallen from 6.8 in March to 4.9 now, and the current data confirms rather than reverses the direction. The prior two HOLDs rested on the view that the margin trough was a chosen investment cycle in an otherwise intact 20%+ growth franchise. Q2 supplies partial support — sequential operating margin recovered 490bp, service gross margin improved, revenue beat, and the low end of guidance rose. It also supplies three new negatives: consensus for 2027 was cut another 10.4% in 30 days, the securities class action survived dismissal and entered discovery, and the June 30 balance sheet made the $347.7M finance lease and 1.83x liabilities/equity visible for the first time. The market's ~9% response to the print is consistent with that balance.

What keeps this from being ownable is the intersection of three items. Earnings estimates are falling 8–10% per month while the stock trades at 40x the current-year number and 25x an out-year number that is the fastest-falling figure in the model. Management itself concludes its disclosure controls are ineffective, in the exact area where the reported margin recovery is being measured. And roughly $895M of obligations come due through mid-2028 against $491M of cash, with the conversion strike 22% above spot, which caps re-rating until a financing path is disclosed. Any one of these would be manageable. Together, with 79% of revenue in one organ in one country under a regulatory regime in transition, they justify standing aside.

The business is real, the moat is real, and the shares are 53% below their December 2025 high — which is why the short side is unattractive at 34.8% short interest and 7.2 days to cover. **AVOID.** Revisit on demonstrated margin recovery plus a disclosed financing solution, or in the mid-$50s.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the tax-normalized FY2025 per-share figure was corrected from ~$2.30 to ~$2.23 and the TTM ex-tax-benefit trailing multiple from ~38.6x to ~41.9x; neither change alters the AVOID thesis but both push valuation modestly richer, reinforcing rather than undercutting the standing-aside conclusion.
