**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

eBay has reaccelerated after years of stagnation. After GMV growth of roughly 2% in 2024, the platform grew GMV 7% in 2025 and 14% on an FX-neutral basis in Q1 2026 (+18% reported to $22.2B), with revenue up 19% YoY to $3.09B. The reacceleration is broad-based: U.S. revenue +29%, Advertising +31%, with strength in Collectibles, Motors Parts & Accessories, Fashion and Electronics, and C2C outpacing B2C. The strategic repositioning toward enthusiast "Focus Categories" (authentication-backed collectibles, luxury, refurbished, sneakers) plus a high-margin first-party Promoted Listings business is producing measurable results. The Q1 2026 earnings beat ($1.66 vs $1.61 estimate) extends a clean three-beats-one-inline four-quarter record.

The offsetting concern is margin. Operating margin fell from 22.5% in 2024 to 20.5% in 2025 and to 19.8% in Q1 2026 (from 23.6% a year earlier), driven by restructuring and executive bonuses ($104M in Q1 2026), a 70% YoY jump in transaction (fraud) losses, ramping shipping-program costs, and legal accruals. Whether this compression is transitory (restructuring) or structural (shipping subsidies, fraud recovery, ad-attribution costs) is the central debate. Two structural headwinds compound it: AI-driven shopping shifting product discovery away from eBay's surfaces (it depends heavily on Google referral traffic), and the cascading elimination of de minimis exemptions (U.S. done, EU in July 2026, UK to follow), which taxes eBay's cross-border Greater China profit pool.

The stock has appreciated ~39% over the trailing twelve months to ~$108, sitting near its $119.31 52-week high. At ~17.6x forward EPS (consensus FY26 ~$6.13), it trades at a slight discount to the Broadline Retail peer median of ~20.7x — fair, not cheap. Layered on top is GameStop/Ryan Cohen's unsolicited ~$56B takeover bid, rejected by the board on May 12, 2026 as "neither credible nor attractive," a low-probability but live overhang. We rate EBAY a HOLD (growth_hold): the growth thesis is real and worth owning, but the risk/reward at current levels is symmetric. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$90s or on evidence that margins have stabilized.

## Company Overview

eBay operates one of the world's largest third-party online marketplaces, connecting ~135–136 million active buyers with sellers across 190+ markets. The model is capital-light: eBay carries no inventory and earns a take rate (~13.9%) on Gross Merchandise Volume (~$80B in 2025). Revenue divides into Marketplace (final-value fees, listing/store subscriptions, shipping — $9.1B in 2025) and the faster-growing Advertising business, primarily first-party Promoted Listings ($2.0B in 2025, +22%, ~18% of revenue). Roughly half of GMV is international, concentrated in the UK and Germany plus cross-border exports from Greater China and Japan.

The competitive differentiation rests on enthusiast and recommerce categories where trust programs (Authenticity Guarantee, eBay Refurbished, PSA trading-card partnership) and the breadth of unique secondhand supply create separation from generalist rivals. eBay spun off PayPal in 2015 and monetized its Adevinta, Adyen and Gmarket stakes in 2024, leaving a focused marketplace operator with 12,300 employees, headquartered in San Jose. Recent tuck-in acquisitions (Depop ~$1.2B cash, signed Feb 2026, closing Q3 2026; Caramel in auto; Tise in resale) extend the Gen-Z/recommerce push.

## Financial Analysis

The reacceleration is the dominant fact in the numbers. Quarterly net revenue progressed sequentially through 2025 ($2,585M → $2,730M → $2,820M → $2,965M) and then jumped to $3,089M in Q1 2026. A weak-USD FX tailwind contributed +$78M in Q1 2026, so on a constant-currency basis growth is closer to 14–15% — still the strongest in years.

Margin is the counterweight. The drivers of the compression are identifiable: restructuring plus executive bonuses, the increase in transaction losses (fraud/recovery-rate deterioration), rising shipping-program costs, and elevated legal expense. The fraud-loss jump is the line item I would watch most closely: if it reflects a structural change in the cost of running the marketplace rather than a one-quarter spike, the earnings power of the franchise is lower than trailing figures suggest.

Cash generation remains strong. 2025 operating cash flow was $1.96B and FCF $1.43B (12.9% FCF margin); Q1 2026 OCF rose to $970M from $755M a year earlier. Capital return is aggressive: $2.5B of buybacks and $531M of dividends in 2025, with an incremental $2.0B repurchase authorization added in February 2026 (atop the $5.0B from 2024, ~$2.3B remaining). Share count has fallen from 558M (2022) to ~444M, a meaningful per-share tailwind. The quarterly dividend is $0.31 (~1.1% yield).

One balance-sheet item deserves explicit correction. The Altman Z-Score of 1.67 nominally places eBay in the "distress zone," but this is a false signal: years of buybacks have shrunk stockholders' equity to ~$4.4B against $54.3B of accumulated treasury stock, depressing the equity-to-assets ratio the Z-Score weights heavily. With 44% ROE, 11.5% ROA, $1.4B+ FCF, $5.1B of cash/investments, investment-grade laddered debt ($6.74B maturing 2026–2051), and full compliance with a 4.0x leverage covenant, there is no realistic near-term solvency concern. The capital structure is efficient rather than stressed.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest it has been in the post-PayPal era. The mechanism is concrete: eBay narrowed its strategic focus to enthusiast categories where it has structural supply advantages (30 years of category data, authentication infrastructure, unique secondhand inventory), and those categories are growing faster than the generalist base. Advertising compounds this — Promoted Listings is a high-incremental-margin revenue stream layered on existing GMV, and at +31% it is meaningfully outpacing the Marketplace take rate. The Depop acquisition adds a Gen-Z fashion-resale audience that eBay's core has historically underpenetrated.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest. Current-year consensus rose +1.6% over 90 days to $6.13; next-year +1.9% to $6.76. These are healthy upward revisions consistent with a business beating expectations, but they fall well short of the +30% revision velocity that would flag a structural mispricing. The four-quarter beat record suggests management guides conservatively and consensus is well-calibrated — confidence in the forward estimates is reasonable, but there is no evidence the Street is far behind reality.

Durability is the open question. The C2C reacceleration and recommerce tailwind (consumers seeking value and circular-economy options) are genuine, and the breadth of the Q1 2026 strength argues against a one-quarter fluke. Against that, FX provided a tailwind that can reverse, and the two structural headwinds (AI discovery, de minimis) sit directly on top of the cross-border and traffic-acquisition engines. I weight Growth Prospects at 7.0: the reacceleration is confirmed in the data, but I am not extrapolating it to the premium growth tier given the headwinds and the FX-flattered comparison.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$108, EBAY trades at ~17.6x consensus FY26 EPS of $6.13 and ~24.9x trailing EPS of $4.26. Against the Broadline Retail peer median P/E of 20.7x, the forward multiple is a slight discount and the trailing multiple a premium; the difference reflects the earnings growth embedded in the forward number. Among the named peers, ETSY trades at 28.0x and AMZN at 28.9x (both higher-growth or higher-multiple stories), while Macy's (10.3x) and Kohl's (7.3x) are structurally challenged legacy retailers — a heterogeneous set that makes the median a loose anchor. On a PEG basis, ~17.6x forward against low-double-digit revenue growth and high-single-digit EPS growth puts EBAY near PEG 1.5–2.0, fair rather than cheap.

The valuation has migrated from attractive to fair over the past several months. Twelve months ago the stock sat in the mid-$70s; the appreciation has tracked the fundamental improvement (the re-rating is earned, not speculative), but it has also consumed the discount that made the prior setup compelling. The analyst price-target distribution (low $65, mean ~$108, median $110, high $130) places the current price essentially at the mean, with the median offering only ~2% upside. Estimate revisions are mild (+1.6% / +1.9%) rather than the +30% velocity that would render targets backward-looking, so treating consensus targets as stale floors waiting to be revised up would be unwarranted.

The GameStop bid introduces an optionality layer to the valuation. A credible takeout would presumably price well above the current level (Cohen's commentary implies meaningful synergies), but the board's rejection on financing grounds and eBay's ~4x size advantage over GameStop make execution highly improbable. I do not assign material value to a deal closing, but the overhang does provide a modest floor on sentiment. Net, I score Valuation 5.5: the multiple is reasonable for the quality and growth, but there is no identifiable, falsifiable mispricing at $108, and the margin-compression risk argues against paying up. Cheapness, where it exists in this peer set, sits in the structurally impaired names.

## Competitive Landscape

eBay occupies a structurally competitive, slow-growth slice of e-commerce, pressed between scale generalists (Amazon, Walmart, Temu/Shein) and nimble specialists (Whatnot in live commerce, StockX/GOAT in sneakers, Vinted/Poshmark/Depop in fashion resale). Barriers to entry in marketplaces are low, and larger ecosystems can outspend eBay on traffic acquisition and logistics.

The trajectory is improving rather than eroding, which is what matters for scoring. The Focus-Category strategy is taking share in enthusiast/recommerce verticals where authentication and unique supply create genuine differentiation, and the GMV reacceleration validates that the repositioning is working. Advertising growth indicates eBay is monetizing its first-party data more effectively. The Depop acquisition is a defensive-and-offensive move into Gen-Z fashion resale where eBay was underexposed.

The two forces that could reverse the trajectory are not yet visible in the trailing data but are strategically material. First, AI-driven shopping: eBay's own 10-K warns that consumers increasingly shop via AI chatbots and agents, and that if those tools do not refer traffic at the rate of traditional search, platform traffic could decline. eBay's heavy reliance on Google referrals makes this an acute, not theoretical, risk. eBay is responding with an AI shopping-agent pilot and Gen-AI listing tools, but concedes it may move slower than better-resourced rivals. Second, trade-policy fragmentation: the elimination of de minimis exemptions raises cost and complexity for cross-border sellers and pressures the Greater China export pool. On balance I score Competitive Position 6.3: the near-term trajectory is positive and earns a score above neutral, but the unpriced structural threats to the discovery model cap it below the 7.0 the recent operating momentum alone might suggest.

## Risk Assessment

**AI disruption of discovery (most strategically important).** eBay's traffic funnel depends heavily on Google. If AI agents and chatbots intermediate product search and fail to route buyers to eBay surfaces, GMV and ad revenue both suffer. This is the single largest long-term risk to the thesis and is difficult to quantify or hedge; it is partially but not fully priced.

**De minimis elimination.** The U.S. ended the $800 exemption (China/HK May 2025, all parcels Aug 29 2025); the EU eliminates its €150 exemption in July 2026 (with a €3/parcel fee) and the UK is expected to follow. This raises cost/complexity for cross-border sellers, increases canceled orders, and pressures a meaningful profit pool. The EU/UK actions are forward-dated catalysts that have not yet flowed through results.

**Margin durability.** The transaction-loss jump and ongoing shipping-program/legal costs could prove structural. If operating margin settles below 20% rather than recovering toward the 22%+ historical range, the earnings power supporting the current multiple is overstated.

**Regulatory/legal.** The EU Digital Services Act (active German regulator inquiry, fines up to 6% of global revenue), EU AI Act, UK OSA/DMCCA, product-safety regimes, and prior DOJ Controlled Substances settlement ($59M, 2024) create episodic expense volatility.

**FX.** Roughly half of revenue is GBP/EUR. The recent weak-USD tailwind can reverse and flatter-then-pressure reported growth.

**GameStop/Ryan Cohen overhang.** The unsolicited bid remains a live wildcard. Low probability of consummation given financing doubts and the 4x size differential, but Cohen's stated intent ("whatever we need to do," "own eBay forever") keeps it an open item — two-sided for the stock (sentiment floor vs. distraction/uncertainty).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 55.0% against 1-year historical vol of 38.6% — rich, with IV at the 82nd percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 55.0% > long-term 38.4%), which the market reads as pricing imminent stress, though the nearest-expiry implied move is only ±0.9% and the 1-week put/call skew is balanced (1.0%). The put/call volume ratio of 1.59 is elevated, but the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.51 is benign, so the heavy put volume looks more like short-dated hedging or positioning around the GameStop/de minimis news flow than entrenched bearish conviction. The 8.5pp IV increase since early May (46.4% → 55.0%) on a roughly flat price suggests the market is pricing in event risk, consistent with the active M&A overhang and the July 2026 EU de minimis date. The elevated near-term IV makes this a poor moment to pay up for the stock; it does not contradict the fundamental thesis but argues for patience on entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($125–135).** The reacceleration is durable: Focus Categories and Advertising compound at low-double-digit rates, Depop adds a Gen-Z resale audience, and the margin compression proves transitory as restructuring rolls off and fraud losses normalize. Operating margin recovers toward 21–22%, FY27 EPS approaches $7+, and the stock re-rates to ~19x ($133). Aggressive buybacks (~$2.3B authorized) shrink the share count further. A credible takeout from Cohen (low probability) would add upside.

**Bear Case ($82–90).** AI-driven discovery begins diverting traffic, GMV growth decelerates back toward the low single digits, and de minimis elimination in the EU/UK pressures cross-border GMV through H2 2026. Margins stay compressed below 20% on structurally higher fraud and shipping costs. The FX tailwind reverses. Multiple compresses toward ~14x on lower growth and lower margins, taking EPS power and the multiple down together — roughly the early-2026 trading range.

**Base Case ($110–118).** Growth moderates from the Q1 2026 peak to high-single/low-double digits as FX normalizes, Advertising remains a bright spot, and margins stabilize near 20% with restructuring rolling off but fraud costs sticky. EPS lands near consensus ($6.13 FY26, $6.76 FY27), and the stock holds an ~18x multiple, producing modest appreciation roughly in line with earnings growth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Timeframe: 12 months.**

**Base case target: $115.** This applies ~18x to a blended forward EPS of ~$6.40 (between FY26 $6.13 and FY27 $6.76), reflecting continued mid-teens-to-high-single-digit growth with margins stabilizing near 20%. That is ~6% above the current ~$108.

**Bull case target: $133** (~+23%). ~19x on FY27 EPS approaching $7 if margins recover toward 21–22% and growth durability is confirmed.

**Bear case target: $86** (~−20%). ~14x on compressed EPS power if AI-discovery and de minimis headwinds bite and margins stay below 20%.

The reward to base case (~6%) and bull case (~23%) against ~20% downside to the bear case is roughly symmetric — sufficient for a hold but not for a buy at this price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating margin stabilizes or recovers above 21% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the Q1 2026 compression was transitory.
- A pullback toward the low-$90s that restores a forward P/E discount to the peer median while estimate revisions remain positive.
- Evidence that the AI shopping-agent pilot is preserving or growing referral traffic, removing the largest structural overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- GMV growth decelerates below ~5% FX-neutral on confirmed AI-driven traffic loss or material de minimis impact.
- Operating margin falls below 18% on structurally higher fraud/shipping costs.
- Transaction losses accelerate further, signaling the Q1 2026 jump is a new baseline rather than a spike.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, late July) and the EU de minimis implementation in July 2026 are the natural reassessment points.

## Conclusion

eBay has earned its re-rating. The growth reacceleration is genuine, broad-based, and validated by a high-quality first-party advertising business, strong free cash flow, and disciplined buybacks. The trajectory of the past two scoring cycles — Growth Prospects rising from 5.5 to 7.0, Competitive Position improving to the low 7s — is confirmed by the new data, not reversed.

What holds the recommendation at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of a fair (not cheap) valuation near the 52-week high and at the analyst mean target; margin compression whose durability is unresolved; two structural headwinds (AI discovery, de minimis) that are not yet visible in trailing results but sit directly on the traffic and cross-border engines; pervasive insider selling with zero open-market buying; and a live, low-probability GameStop takeover overhang. The Valuation decline in the prior cycle (7.0 → 5.5) reflected the stock's appreciation consuming its discount, and that remains the correct read: the per-share story has improved, but so has the price. We hold for the growth thesis and would look to add on a pullback toward the low-$90s or on confirmation that margins have stabilized.
