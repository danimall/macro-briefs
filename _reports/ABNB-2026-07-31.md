**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Airbnb enters its August 6 second-quarter print with the best fundamental momentum it has shown in three years and the least attractive entry price it has offered in twelve months. Q1'26 revenue grew 18% (15% constant currency) against FY2025's 10%, gross booking value rose 19%, and all four reporting regions accelerated, including North America moving from +4% FY2025 revenue growth to +8%. Operating income more than doubled to $86M and Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 19% from 18% even as sales and marketing spending rose 33%. On the strength of the business model — $9.5B net cash, capex of 0.27% of revenue, cash collected at booking, $4.5B of remaining buyback authorization with no indenture restrictions — this is one of the higher-quality balance sheets in consumer discretionary.

The valuation math requires a correction that materially changes the conclusion. The supplied market capitalization of $63.1B corresponds to Class A shares alone (417.9M × $151.07 = $63.1B). Class B (175.6M shares, economically identical and one-for-one convertible) brings total shares to 593.5M and equity value to approximately $89.7B, with enterprise value of roughly $80.2B after $2.5B of debt and $12.0B of cash and short-term investments. On that basis Airbnb trades at 35.7x FY2025 net income, 18.7x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, and 26.6x free cash flow after deducting $1.59B of stock compensation. Forward multiples are 29.7x the $5.09 current-year consensus and 25.0x the $6.03 FY2027 consensus. The stock closed at $152.08 after touching a 52-week high of $156.50 on July 29, against a consensus mean target of $157.38.

Three things keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. First, GAAP EPS has missed consensus in three consecutive quarters (-7.5%, -16.4%, -11.9%), which argues for discounting the $5.09 full-year figure — particularly with a new ~$0.16-per-share annual interest burden from the March refinancing and stock compensation running at 15.3% of Q1 revenue. Second, a meaningful and unquantifiable portion of the Q1 acceleration is likely FIFA World Cup 2026 supply and pricing in North American host cities, which creates a hard 2027 comparison rather than a durable trend. Third, Experiences and Services — the entire strategic case for re-rating — is not separately disclosed, so its unit economics cannot be verified from filings while its cost is plainly visible in the S&M line. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the score declines to 5.9 from 6.1, entirely on the valuation dimension following a 7.4% price advance on flat 30-day estimate revisions.

## Company Overview

Airbnb operates a two-sided marketplace for stays, experiences, and services across more than 220 countries, with over 5 million hosts and approximately 8,200 employees supplemented by roughly 13,000 third-party support workers. The company reports one operating segment; the CEO is the chief operating decision maker and performance is assessed on consolidated net income. The consequence for analysis is direct: the redesigned Experiences product and new Services offering, both launched in May 2025, have no publicly disclosed revenue, take rate, or profitability.

Revenue is a service fee on gross booking value, collected at booking and recognized at guest check-in. At March 31, 2026, unearned fees were $2,733M (from $1,743M at year-end, a seasonal build), and the company held $10,550M of funds receivable on behalf of customers offset exactly by $10,550M of funds payable. Those customer funds are pass-through and are not corporate liquidity.

Geographic concentration is genuinely low: no single city accounts for more than 2% of revenue or 1% of active listings. Regulatory concentration, by contrast, is the defining feature of the risk profile — Airbnb collects and remits lodging taxes across approximately 37,000 jurisdictions.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue, margin, and the composition of the Q1 inflection

| $M | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1'25 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 9,917 | 11,102 | 12,241 | 2,272 | 2,678 |
| Growth | +18% | +12% | +10% | — | +18% |
| Operating income | 1,518 | 2,553 | 2,544 | 38 | 86 |
| Operating margin | 15.3% | 23.0% | 20.8% | 1.7% | 3.2% |
| Net income | 4,792 | 2,648 | 2,511 | 154 | 160 |
| Diluted shares (M) | 662 | 645 | 623 | 632 | 608 |

FY2023 net income is not comparable — it includes a negative $2,690M tax line from a deferred tax valuation allowance release. Any trend drawn through it is an artifact.

FY2025 operating margin fell 220bp on deliberate spending. Sales and marketing rose 20% to $2,588M, within which "field operations and policy" rose 43% to $993M. Product development rose 14% to $2,354M. Q1'26 continued the pattern with S&M +33% to $751M, or 28% of revenue versus 25%. Management attributes this to emerging-market growth initiatives and third-party spend supporting Experiences and Services supply. The "field operations and policy" line bundles market-launch field teams with regulatory and policy work and is not disaggregated, so the split between growth investment and non-discretionary compliance cost is unknown. This matters because the two have very different reversibility.

The interest-income cushion is eroding: $818M in FY2024, $705M in FY2025, and $155M in Q1'26 versus $173M. That is a rate effect on a large cash balance, not operating deterioration, but it removes a tailwind that flattered prior pretax income.

Q1'26 net income of $160M contains two roughly offsetting one-time items: a $70M realized gain on sale of a privately held equity investment carried at zero, and a $121M tax provision (43% effective rate versus 11%) inflated by a $69M deferred tax adjustment from Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax changes. The cleaner read on progress is operating income of $86M against $38M.

### Free cash flow and the float reset

FY2025: operating cash flow $4,646M, capex $33M, FCF $4,613M (37.7% margin), down from 40.4% in FY2024. Q1'26 FCF was $1,704M, a 64% margin against 78% in Q1'25 — declining in absolute dollars despite 18% revenue growth and 19% GBV growth.

Management's explanation is specific: adoption of "Pay Less Upfront" and "Reserve Now, Pay Later" means guests pay closer to check-in, so unearned fees "remained relatively flat" despite GBV growth. This is a reduction in the *level* of working-capital float, exchanged for higher booking conversion. Whether it is a one-time reset during an adoption ramp or a permanent step-down in conversion is the open question. I have not annualized Q1 FCF: the 10-K states FCF is highest in H1 as unearned fees build and lowest in Q4, so a Q1 run-rate of ~$6.8B contradicts the disclosed seasonality. A normalized frame is FY2026 FCF conversion in the low-to-mid 30s% of revenue pre-SBC and mid-to-high 20s% after deducting stock compensation.

Stock-based compensation was $1,592M in FY2025 (13.0% of revenue) and $410M in Q1'26 (15.3% of revenue, up 15% year over year). RSUs outstanding rose from 32.2M to 39.6M in one quarter. Because Airbnb repurchases stock at roughly 2.4x the pace of SBC, dilution is fully offset — but that means a substantial share of what appears as "capital return" is funding compensation. FY2025 FCF less SBC is $3,021M, or 24.7% of revenue. The two treatments produce materially different valuations, and I use the after-SBC figure as the economic base.

### Balance sheet and the March refinancing

At March 31, 2026: cash $7,037M plus short-term investments $4,968M = $12.0B corporate liquidity, an undrawn $1.0B revolver, and $2.5B of debt. Net cash is approximately $9.5B, or about $16 per share on 593.5M shares.

The reported Altman Z-Score of 2.08 and "D/E of 1.71" are both artifacts. The Z-Score's working-capital and equity-to-liability inputs are depressed by $10.55B of pass-through customer funds in current liabilities matched dollar-for-dollar in current assets, plus $2.7B of unearned fees that represent cash already collected. The 1.71 figure is total liabilities divided by equity, not debt to equity. Actual leverage is negative against roughly $4.3B of annual Adjusted EBITDA. Bankruptcy risk is not a live concern. Equity fell from $8,199M to $7,636M in Q1'26 because buybacks are charged against accumulated deficit, which reconciles exactly (-$5,502M + $160M - $1,061M = -$6,403M).

On March 16, 2026 Airbnb issued $2.5B of senior unsecured notes — $850M at 4.40% due 2029, $850M at 4.65% due 2031, $800M at 5.25% due 2036 — using roughly $2.0B to retire 0% convertible notes at maturity and retaining ~$500M. Annual cash interest is ($850M × 4.40%) + ($850M × 4.65%) + ($800M × 5.25%) = $118.9M, versus zero cash coupon previously. At a ~20% effective tax rate that is ~$95M after tax, or approximately $0.16 per diluted share on 608M shares — a permanent, mechanical EPS headwind absent from FY2025. The company also entered $1.7B notional of fixed-to-floating swaps on the 2031 and 2036 notes, adding floating exposure with a disclosed 100bp sensitivity of ~$17M.

The new indenture "does not restrict us or our subsidiaries' ability to incur additional debt, pay dividends, repurchase securities," and the lien covenant applies only to "principal properties," of which Airbnb owns none. For equity holders this is maximum flexibility; it also removes any external brake on future leverage. The 2022 credit facility retains leverage and fixed-charge covenants, in compliance at quarter-end.

### Capital allocation

FY2025: 29.7M shares repurchased for $3.8B, about 82% of FCF. Q1'26: 8.1M shares for $1.1B at an average $129.26, including February purchases at $123.98 — well below today's $151. $4.5B remains under the August 2025 authorization. Diluted shares fell from 645M (FY2024) to 608M (Q1'26), a 5.7% reduction over five quarters. No dividend. The execution on price is genuinely good, whether by design or timing luck.

## Growth Analysis

### Regional detail

| Region | FY25 revenue | % total | FY25 rev growth | FY25 nights | Q1'26 rev growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| North America | $5,196M | 42% | +4% | +3% | +8% |
| EMEA | $4,729M | 39% | +14% | +7% | +25% |
| Latin America | $1,160M | 10% | +20% | +18% | +31% |
| Asia Pacific | $1,156M | 9% | +17% | +15% | +23% |

North America at 42% of revenue growing nights 3% in FY2025 is the structural drag: Airbnb's most penetrated market is mature. Every region accelerated in Q1'26, which is the bull case in one line.

Two qualifications matter. First, 3 percentage points of the 18% Q1 revenue growth was currency (18% reported versus 15% constant currency), so constant-currency acceleration over FY2025's 10% was 5 points, not 8. Second, the composition is unusual: nights and seats booked grew only 9% (143M to 156M) while GBV grew 19%, implying GBV per night up about 9% reported. Some of that is mix and seat pricing in the new Services line. A material portion is plausibly FIFA World Cup 2026 bookings in North American host cities at elevated nightly rates, booked in Q1 and recognized at check-in in Q2 and Q3. Press reporting (unconfirmed by filings) describes 100,000-plus new listings across 16 host cities. If that is the mechanism, the +8% North America reacceleration is partly a non-recurring event effect that creates a difficult 2027 comparison rather than evidence of renewed structural growth in the largest region. Management itself cites lengthening booking lead times from deferred-payment adoption, which pulls GBV recognition forward without changing underlying demand, and the 10-K flags Easter and holiday timing as quarterly distortions.

### Monetization

Derived take rate on a full-year basis (the only defensible basis given the booking-versus-check-in recognition gap): FY2025 $12,241M / $91,273M = 13.41% versus FY2024 $11,102M / $81,784M = 13.57%, a 16bp decline. Q1'26 shows the same directional gap (revenue +18% versus GBV +19%). This is small, and mix shift toward faster-growing, lower-ADR emerging markets is at least as plausible as fee-rate erosion. Airbnb does not disclose a take rate, so this is a proxy.

### Estimates and delivery

Consensus stands at $5.09 for the current year (+3.1% over 90 days, flat over 30) and $6.03 for next year (+5.0% over 90 days, -0.2% over 30). These are positive but well short of an inflection signature; the 30-day trend is flat to marginally negative, which argues against treating consensus targets as stale floors.

The delivery record is the more important input. GAAP EPS missed by 7.5%, 16.4%, and 11.9% in the last three quarters after a single 6.6% beat. Q1'26 already missed at $0.26 versus $0.30. That leaves $4.83 of the $5.09 full-year estimate to be earned across Q2-Q4 against a new $0.16 annual interest drag and stock compensation growing faster than revenue. A realistic FY2026 outcome closer to $4.70-4.90 would put the forward multiple at 31-32x rather than 29.7x.

## Valuation Assessment

Correcting the share count is the single largest analytical adjustment. On 593.5M shares at $151.07:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Equity value | ~$89.7B |
| Enterprise value | ~$80.2B |
| P/E, FY2025 net income ($2,511M) | 35.7x |
| P/E, FY2026 consensus ($5.09) | 29.7x |
| P/E, FY2027 consensus ($6.03) | 25.0x |
| EV / FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA ($4,297M) | 18.7x |
| EV / FY2025 FCF ($4,613M) | 17.4x |
| EV / FY2025 FCF less SBC ($3,021M) | 26.6x |

Peer medians are P/E 19.7x on wide dispersion: HLT 52.1x and MAR 41.6x (asset-light franchisors), EXPE 19.7x (the closest business-model comparable), RCL 17.5x and CCL 12.6x (cruise). Airbnb at 37.3x trailing and 25.0x FY2027 sits between the franchisors and the OTA. A premium to Expedia is defensible on growth (18% versus high single digits), net cash, and 0.27% capital intensity. A discount to Marriott and Hilton is equally defensible on the regulatory and tax tail that hotel franchisors do not carry.

Adjusting for the ~$16 per share of net cash, the operating business trades at roughly $135, or 22.4x FY2027 consensus. That is the most favorable framing available and it still does not produce asymmetry: the mean analyst target of $157.38 is 4% above spot, the median $160 is 6% above, and the stock sits 3% below its 52-week high after a 12.9% one-year advance.

PEG on FY2027 earnings growth of ~18% is approximately 1.4, technically inside the range where rising estimates can lead price. But the estimate revisions supporting that framing are +3-5% over 90 days and flat over 30 — not the velocity that reliably precedes re-rating — and the earnings-miss streak means the denominator carries downside risk. There is no identified mispricing here. The market is paying a full multiple for an acceleration whose durability the August 6 print has yet to confirm and whose largest single driver (Experiences and Services) is undisclosed.

## Competitive Landscape

Airbnb's original moat was non-substitutable supply: individual homes not listed elsewhere, supported by brand strength that permits "lower reliance on paid marketing channels," a trust system (reviews, risk scoring, AirCover with up to $3M property damage and $1M liability protection), payments infrastructure across ~20 local methods, and host tooling including the Co-Host Network. It does not currently meet EU Digital Markets Act gatekeeper thresholds, a modest advantage relative to Booking.com, which has been designated.

The trajectory is where the concern lies. S&M rose to 28% of Q1 revenue from 25%, which is consistent with rising customer acquisition cost in the markets now driving growth. The expansion into Experiences and Services moves Airbnb into categories where supply is commoditized and entrenched specialists (Viator, GetYourGuide, Klook) already operate, alongside fragmented vertical marketplaces in photography, beauty, fitness and food. The cost of that expansion is visible in the filings; the revenue is not.

The 10-K names the AI risk directly: "If consumers become less reliant on search engines for travel searches and instead use AI apps and other channels, we may not be able to optimize for searches on these emerging channels and may risk losing traffic to competitors." The competitor list includes Google "and those powered by AI, including its travel search products." This attacks the specific asset Airbnb claims as its edge — direct, brand-driven traffic. The counter-argument is that Airbnb's supply is largely exclusive to its platform, so an agent seeking a specific home may transact through Airbnb regardless of where discovery occurred. Which force dominates is unresolved and is the central long-term debate on the equity.

Regulatory asymmetry versus hotels is structural. Marriott and Hilton operate under settled zoning and licensing regimes. Airbnb faces ~37,000 lodging-tax jurisdictions, a de facto New York City ban, the EU short-term-rental regulation effective May 2026, approximately $957M of cumulative Italian settlements (€576M for 2017-21, €139M for 2022, €179M for 2023), a disputed and unaccrued €65M Spanish fine, and active municipal litigation including a June 2026 Chicago suit naming Airbnb itself. This is the most defensible argument for a permanent discount to hotel and OTA peers.

The proxy provides the market's cumulative assessment: five-year TSR of 92.45 for Airbnb versus 258.38 for its own selected peer group, with the gap widening every year. Revenue grew 46% from FY2022 to FY2025, FCF exceeded $4.6B annually, and share count fell from 680M to 608M, while $100 invested became $92.45. Some of that reflects post-COVID peers recovering from severely depressed bases, which flatters the comparison. The persistence is nonetheless unambiguous, and it is Airbnb-specific rather than sector-wide. Competitive position stays at 6.0: Q1's broad regional acceleration offsets the take-rate drift, North American maturity and rising acquisition cost, without resolving them.

## Risk Assessment

**IRS transfer pricing — the largest quantified contingency.** A December 2020 Notice of Proposed Adjustment on the 2013 sale of international IP to a subsidiary implies $1.3B of additional tax plus penalties and interest, exceeding recorded reserves "by more than $1.0 billion." Appeals failed, a Statutory Notice of Deficiency arrived in May 2024, and Airbnb petitioned the Tax Court in July 2024. Unreserved exposure above $1.0B equals roughly 11% of corporate liquidity and 40% of FY2025 net income. IP-valuation disputes commonly settle well below notice amounts and Tax Court timelines run years, so this is a persistent overhang rather than a near-term cash event.

**EU short-term-rental regulation, effective May 2026.** Registration, data-sharing and transparency requirements against EMEA's 39% revenue weight, with no quantified operational impact anywhere in the filings. Spain's Supreme Court voided the national STR registry in May 2026 (per Reuters via Skift), which is directionally favorable but fragments compliance back to the regional level. Cumulative indirect-tax payable plus estimated lodging and withholding liabilities rose from $1,132M to $1,501M between December and March, partly seasonal build; reasonably possible losses above accruals are $25-35M (lodging) and $195-215M (other non-income taxes).

**Governance and key-person.** Class B controls approximately 89.4% of voting power on 29.6% of the economic interest, the board is classified with staggered terms, and 2.0B non-voting Class C shares are authorized but unissued. Bloomberg reported on June 4, 2026 (corroborated by TechCrunch with a person familiar, plus Skift and others) that Brian Chesky is starting a separate AI model lab funded with personal and outside capital, led by someone else, while remaining CEO. Nothing about this appears in any filing I reviewed. Three unresolved issues follow: CEO attention, potential future related-party exposure if the venture's technology ever transacts with Airbnb, and the implication that frontier AI capability is judged strategically relevant to travel yet is being built outside the P&L. I do not infer diverted opportunity — the evidence does not support that — but the disclosure gap is material and warrants monitoring. An 8-K filed November 21, 2025 under Item 5.02 remains unread and unexplained.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director in the window reviewed. Co-founder Joseph Gebbia's reported Class A position went from ~3,435,755 shares to 215 shares between June 1 and July 28, 2026 at $132-153, exceeding $450M gross, under a 10b5-1 plan adopted February 27, 2026 with a 3,450,000-share maximum. Chesky sold roughly 900K-plus Class A shares in late May and June under a plan adopted February 26, retaining 10,701,685 shares. Plan adoption preceded both the sales and the June 4 AI-lab report by over three months, and the mechanical cadence matching plan maxima is consistent with scheduled diversification. Motive is not inferable. What is decision-relevant: with estimate revisions running only +3-5% over 90 days rather than the velocity that would override the signal, the complete absence of insider conviction buying at a price that has produced a five-year cumulative loss carries some weight.

**Macro and consumer.** Middle East conflict is characterized in the Q1 10-Q as having "a slight impact on near-term booking trends, including increased cancellations in EMEA and Asia Pacific" — worth watching given EMEA's 39% weight and Q3 being the seasonal peak. Trade-down from hotels to homes is a partial offset in a consumer slowdown, but the direction and magnitude are unproven at this company's current scale.

**Vendor concentration.** AWS is the primary cloud host with a minimum commitment of at least $1.7B through 2031; Google Maps supplies location data; the majority of community support is outsourced to a limited number of providers covering ~13,000 workers.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term ATM IV of 156.2% is a one-day-expiry artifact and should be discarded. The informative reading is the term structure: the August 14 tenor prices 54.0% IV and an 8.3% implied move, which brackets the August 6 earnings release; September 11 prices 42.3% and 11.1%; December 18 prices 36.1% against one-year realized volatility of 30.7%. Put/call volume of 0.55 and open-interest ratio of 0.63 are call-tilted, short interest is only 3.4% of float with 3.7 days to cover, and overall put/call skew is described as balanced at 0.9%. Positioning is not bearish. What the chain does say is that the market prices a ±8% binary event in six days on a stock 3% below its 52-week high with a three-quarter miss streak. Near-dated volatility is rich to realized, which favors waiting for the print rather than paying up before it. The prior-snapshot comparison (ATM IV 35.2% to 170.8% alongside a 7.4% price advance, P/C OI 0.53 to 0.63) reflects the same expiry mechanics plus modest hedge accumulation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $185 (+22%)

Q2 on August 6 confirms constant-currency revenue growth at or above 15%, with North American nights growth holding above 5% and Adjusted EBITDA margin expanding again. World Cup check-ins deliver an outsized Q2/Q3 and, critically, management provides enough color on Experiences and Services to establish that the new categories are contributing revenue at acceptable contribution margin rather than only cost. Emerging markets — LatAm +31%, APAC +23%, EMEA +25% in Q1 — compound off small bases and lift the consolidated rate for multiple years. The $4.5B remaining authorization retires another 4-5% of shares annually at prices management has shown willingness to pay only below $135. FY2027 EPS reaches $6.25-6.50 and the multiple holds at 29-30x on demonstrated 15%-plus growth with net cash. That is $185, consistent with the $181 high analyst target.

### Bear Case — $118 (-22%)

Q1's acceleration proves substantially event-driven and currency-assisted. Nights growth of 9% versus GBV growth of 19% turns out to have been World Cup pricing in host cities, and the FY2027 comparison is punishing. North American nights revert to 3%, EMEA absorbs registration and data-sharing friction from the May 2026 EU regulation, and S&M above 28% of revenue proves to be permanent customer-acquisition inflation rather than discretionary investment. GAAP EPS misses a fourth consecutive quarter, FY2026 lands near $4.70 against $5.09, and the new $119M interest expense plus SBC at 15% of revenue compress the after-SBC cash yield. AI-mediated discovery begins visibly diverting traffic. The multiple resets toward Expedia's 19.7x on FY2027 EPS of $5.90-6.00, which is $118 — matching the $115 low analyst target.

### Base Case — $162 (+7%)

27x FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.03 equals $163; I use $162. That multiple is a premium to Expedia's 19.7x justified by 18% growth, $16 per share of net cash and negligible capital intensity, and a discount to Marriott's 41.6x and Hilton's 52.1x justified by the unreserved IRS exposure, the unquantified EU regulatory event, and the take-rate drift. Roughly 7% upside against 22% downside is not a risk/reward that supports adding at $151.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Rationale: uncertain. The Q2 print, the Experiences/Services economics, and the EU regulatory impact are all unresolved, and the price no longer compensates for that uncertainty.

**Reassessment date:** August 6, 2026, after market close (Q2'26 results). The next natural checkpoint after that is the Q3 print, which will contain the World Cup check-in quarter and the first full quarter under the EU short-term-rental regime.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q2 constant-currency revenue growth ≥15% *with* North American nights growth ≥5% and Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion year over year — establishing the inflection as structural rather than event-driven, and specifically demonstrating the largest region is not simply mature.
2. Disclosure that permits estimating Experiences and Services revenue and contribution margin (segment reporting, a stated revenue figure, or quantified unit economics), showing the S&M step-up is generating returns.
3. Price of $125-130, which puts FY2027 consensus at 21-22x and the ex-cash operating business near 19x — the level at which management itself repurchased $1.1B in Q1.
4. IRS matter resolved or settled at a figure at or near the existing reserve.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Constant-currency revenue growth decelerating below 12% in Q2 or Q3, with North American nights back to ≤3%.
2. S&M sustained above 30% of revenue without corresponding revenue acceleration — evidence that acquisition cost inflation is structural and the brand-driven traffic advantage is eroding.
3. A quantified EU short-term-rental compliance cost or booking impact material to EMEA's 39% revenue contribution, or a fourth consecutive GAAP EPS miss.
4. Disclosure of any commercial, licensing, or investment relationship between Airbnb and the CEO's separate AI venture without a clearly documented independent board process.
5. Adverse Tax Court development materially above the recorded reserve.

## Conclusion

Airbnb is a high-quality business at a full price. The balance sheet is genuinely strong — $9.5B net cash, $12.0B liquidity, capex at 0.27% of revenue, an undrawn revolver, and no meaningful covenant constraints — and the Q1'26 acceleration to 18% revenue growth with all four regions improving is the best operating news the company has produced since 2023. Buyback execution has been disciplined, retiring 5.7% of shares over five quarters at average prices well below today's.

Against that, the correct equity value is approximately $89.7B rather than the $63.1B a Class-A-only share count implies, which puts the stock at 35.7x FY2025 earnings, 26.6x free cash flow after stock compensation, and 25.0x an FY2027 consensus that three consecutive earnings misses give reason to discount. The largest driver of the growth case cannot be verified because Experiences and Services is not separately disclosed. A meaningful part of the Q1 acceleration is plausibly World Cup-related and therefore creates a 2027 comparison problem. The May 2026 EU regulation lands against 39% of revenue with zero quantification, more than $1.0B of the IRS claim is unreserved, and the March refinancing added a permanent ~$0.16 per share interest cost. Insiders have sold heavily and bought nothing.

At $151, three percent below the 52-week high and four percent below the consensus mean, with a ±8% event six days away, the appropriate action is to hold existing positions and wait for the August 6 print. Own it; do not add here. The buy case re-opens on confirmed North American nights growth above 5% with disclosed Experiences economics, or on a decline toward $125-130.
