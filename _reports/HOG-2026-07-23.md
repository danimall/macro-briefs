**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Harley-Davidson is in the middle of a deliberate structural transformation that makes its reported financials difficult to read in both directions. The company has restructured its captive finance arm (HDFS) into a capital-light model, bringing in KKR and PIMCO as ~9.8% non-controlling investors, executing a Forward Flow Agreement to sell roughly two-thirds of future retail loan originations, and deconsolidating securitization VIEs. This shrank total assets from $12.4B (Q1'25) to $7.2B (Q1'26) and long-term debt from ~$5.0B to ~$1.1B. The trade-off is that HDFS operating income is guided to collapse from $490M in 2025 to roughly $45-60M in 2026, because Harley no longer earns spread on a large owned loan book, only a servicing fee. Standardized data pulls that flag Harley's YoY revenue and earnings declines as pure operational collapse are missing that a large piece of the decline is a chosen balance-sheet shift, not demand deterioration.

Underneath the accounting noise, the core motorcycle business (HDMC) is showing genuine signs of recovery. Q2 2026 (reported July 23, 2026) delivered EPS of $0.75, an ~18% beat, with core motorcycle revenue up ~6%, a third consecutive quarter of North American retail growth, and raised full-year guidance. US 601+cc heavyweight share rose to 37.8% in Q1 2026 from 36.2% a year earlier, and dealer inventory was cut ~22% YoY to a much healthier ~44,000 units. Forward EPS estimates are rising sharply, with next-year consensus revised +26.9% over 90 days to $1.94, while the stock trades at roughly 14x forward earnings, a meaningful discount to the Consumer Discretionary peer median near 25x.

The bull case is that results look terrible while the trajectory looks better, with an unusually cheap starting multiple. The bear case is that the volume-and-affordability pivot ("Back to the Bricks") under a first-time-in-industry CEO dilutes the premium brand equity that has historically defined margins, while two large off-income-statement risks sit unreserved: a $140M-$450M brake-hose recall contingency and a BBB-/CreditWatch Negative rating that directly threatens funding costs in a finance model now more dependent on capital markets. The improving trajectory and cheap valuation warrant holding over avoiding, but the evidence is not yet sufficient for a confident BUY. We rate HOG HOLD.

## Company Overview

Harley-Davidson operates in three reportable segments. **HDMC (Harley-Davidson Motor Company)** is the core motorcycle, parts & accessories (P&A), apparel, and licensing business, and the primary profit engine. Its largest product family, Grand American Touring, represents roughly 58% of shipments and the highest margins. **HDFS (Harley-Davidson Financial Services)** is the captive finance arm providing retail loans to buyers and wholesale floorplan financing to dealers; historically it contributed a stable ~$250-490M in operating income. **LiveWire**, a publicly listed but HOG-controlled electric motorcycle subsidiary, is chronically loss-making (~$70-80M expected operating loss in 2026).

The competitive moat rests on an iconic brand, an exclusive dealer network, and dominant US heavyweight (601+cc) share. That share is 37.8% as of Q1 2026, up year-over-year but well below the ~40%+ Harley held historically. The core customer skews aging, affluent, and touring-oriented, the structural demographic headwind the new strategy explicitly targets by pushing toward affordability and volume (the return of the Sportster and a new "Sprint" model).

Market capitalization is roughly $2.8B on ~105-110M diluted shares, with the stock recently trading in the mid-$20s to high-$20s after a rally from March 2026 lows near $17.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture requires reading the segments and cash flows rather than the consolidated headline.

**Revenue** has declined from ~$4.9B (2022) toward ~$3.6B (2025), driven by falling motorcycle volume and, most recently, the HDFS restructuring. Q2 2026 revenue was ~$1.23B, down ~5.9% YoY, but core motorcycle revenue grew ~6%; the divergence reflects the finance-income reset, not a collapse in the motorcycle franchise.

**HDMC core profitability** deteriorated sharply then began recovering. In Q1 2026, HDMC operating margin fell to 1.8% (from 10.8% a year earlier), with operating income falling to $18.9M from $116.3M. The drivers were lower shipments, ~$45M of tariff costs, unfavorable manufacturing leverage (fewer units over fixed costs), heavy sales incentives to clear dealer inventory, and higher people/recall costs, partly offset by a ~$41M EU tariff refund. Full-year 2026 HDMC guidance is weak, between a $40M operating loss and $10M operating income. Q2 2026, by contrast, showed EPS of $0.75 beating consensus by ~18%, core motorcycle revenue growth of ~6%, and a management guidance raise for global retail sales and wholesale shipments.

**Cash flow** looks alarming at the headline level, with Q1 2026 operating cash flow at negative $228M, but this is largely mechanical. Originations of held-for-sale receivables now flow through operating activities under the new model. Underlying HDMC cash generation is weaker on lower volume and higher cost, but the headline overstates the deterioration. This is a genuine analytical trap for anyone reading the number in isolation.

**Balance sheet and leverage** are substantially improved. Post-transaction long-term debt fell to ~$1.1B, and cash was $1.8B at Q1'26 (down from $3.1B at YE'25 after debt paydown and buybacks). The offsetting risk is the credit rating: S&P BBB-/CreditWatch Negative, Moody's Baa3/Stable, Fitch BBB/Negative, one notch above junk and under pressure. In a finance model now more dependent on the Forward Flow Agreement and capital-markets funding, a downgrade would raise HDFS funding costs as the segment tries to rebuild earnings toward the ~$125-150M targeted by 2029.

**Earnings surprise track record** is 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters, with the miss being the seasonally weak Q4 2025 (actual -$2.44 vs -$1.05 estimate, distorted by transition costs). The two most recent quarters beat by 18.1% and 11.7% respectively, which supports confidence in the rising forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is a stabilization-and-recovery thesis, not a secular-growth thesis. Three data points give it credibility:

1. **Three consecutive quarters of North American retail growth** (Q4'25 → Q2'26). Q1 2026 worldwide retail rose +8.1% and US retail +15.8% (aided by incentives), while US 601+cc industry registrations grew ~10.8% YoY and Harley gained share to 37.8%, a combination of a rising market and rising share.

2. **Dealer channel health.** Inventory was cut ~22% YoY to ~44,000 units, removing the overhang that had forced margin-destroying incentives.

3. **Rising forward estimates.** Next-year consensus EPS was revised +26.9% over 90 days to $1.94, and current-year +56.8% to $0.45. Under the calibration framework, a +30%+ 90-day revision combined with revenue growth is a strong buy signal, but the guardrail matters here. Current-year revenue is not growing >30%; it is roughly flat-to-down given the HDFS reset. The revision velocity is real and positive, but it partly reflects a rebound from an artificially depressed base rather than a demand-led acceleration. We weight it as a genuine positive for the growth and valuation scores, but not as the unambiguous recovery signal the framework reserves for accelerating-revenue names.

The "Back to the Bricks" strategy (2026-2029) targets ≥$150M in cost reductions vs the 2025 base (fully realized 2028), ≥$225M total HDMC benefit, mid-single-digit CAGR in retail units and P&A/apparel/licensing, HDMC gross margin of 25-30%, and opex below 20% of HDMC revenue. These are credible but entirely unproven, and the central tension is whether cheaper Sportster/Sprint volume is incremental or cannibalistic to the premium touring franchise that generates most of the margin.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$26-28 the stock trades at roughly 14x forward earnings and ~14x trailing, with a ~2.6% dividend yield. The Consumer Discretionary peer set (PATK, OSW, PENN, PHIN, RH) shows a median P/E of ~25x. On a headline basis, Harley trades at roughly half its peer multiple.

The discount is not accidental. It reflects (a) genuine uncertainty about the durability of the turnaround, (b) the HDFS earnings reset that structurally lowers the near-term earnings base, (c) the binary recall and credit-rating risks, and (d) the depressed, transition-year earnings that make the trailing multiple less informative. The relevant question is whether the market is over-discounting.

The cycle-adjusted case argues yes: on the $1.94 next-year consensus, the stock trades at ~13.5x forward, and if the cost program and HDFS rebuild deliver, normalized earnings power sits meaningfully above the trough. The analyst median target of $28 and high of $32 (against a low of $15) imply modest upside to fair-to-attractive. The sentiment trend is improving, and 6 of 16 analysts now rate it buy versus 5 a quarter ago.

Two calibration disciplines temper this. First, a low multiple by itself is not a buy signal: cheapness has been reliably anti-predictive in this system, and Harley has been "cheap" for the entire declining period since 2022. The multiple compression partly reflects real structural concerns (demographic decline, brand-dilution risk from the volume pivot), which is the definition of a potential value trap. Second, Consumer Discretionary carries a structural negative alpha in this framework, so a positive raw return here would need to clear a higher bar of company-specific evidence. The rising estimates and improving retail trend are the strongest arguments against the value-trap reading, but they are not yet decisive. We score valuation at a neutral 5.0: the discount is real but so are the reasons for it, and the asymmetry is not yet clearly favorable.

## Competitive Landscape

Harley remains the dominant US heavyweight brand at ~38% share and gained share in Q1 2026 into a growing market. But the competitive environment is intensifying. Primary rival Indian Motorcycle (Polaris) ran attack ads in June 2026 targeting Harley's new CEO and past brand positioning, competing directly on price, product, and cultural positioning. In the adventure and lightweight segments Harley is trying to grow, it faces BMW, Triumph, and KTM. Europe share is small (~3.5%), and the Hero MotoCorp India relationship is strategically relevant but immaterial to near-term numbers.

The trajectory is the key input. Share is rising, the channel is healthier, and the industry grew ~10.8% in Q1. The affordability pivot could expand the addressable market and reverse demographic decline, or it could erode the premium brand equity and margin structure that define Harley. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving on share, but the strategic bet introduces real execution risk to the moat. We score competitive position at a neutral 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Brake-hose recall contingency (binary, unreserved).** An ongoing NHTSA matter since 2022, with Harley petitioning for "Inconsequentiality Determinations" and accruing nothing while disclosing a potential recall cost range of $140M-$450M. Against a ~$2.8B market cap and a $45-60M near-term HDFS earnings base, an adverse ruling near the top of that range would be highly material. This is the single largest idiosyncratic risk not reflected in the valuation.

**HDFS earnings reset and funding dependence.** The near-term earnings hole, reliance on the Forward Flow Agreement and capital markets, and negative credit-rating watch converge into a real vulnerability. Managed 30-day delinquencies rose to 4.63% (from 4.47%). A downgrade below investment grade would raise funding costs in a model now structurally more dependent on capital-markets access.

**Execution risk on the turnaround.** This was the risk factor management explicitly added/modified in the Q1 2026 10-Q. A first-time-in-industry CEO (Artie Starrs, from Topgolf/restaurants, effective October 1, 2025), a brand-new strategy unveiled in Q2 2026, and a wholesale pivot from premium/touring toward affordability/volume are all unproven.

**Tariffs.** ~$45M hit in Q1 2026, full-year estimate $75-90M, policy- and litigation-dependent.

**Structural demand and demographics.** Aging core customer, high rates suppressing big-ticket discretionary purchases, and dependence on dealer financial health.

**Brand and competitive attacks.** New risk-factor language about "activist collective actions, calls for boycotts and other brand-damaging behaviors," compounded by Indian Motorcycle's attack ads.

The concentration of large, binary, off-income-statement risks, particularly the unreserved recall range and the credit-watch, justifies a below-average risk score of 4.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV spiked to ~95.6% versus 1-year historical vol of 41.1%, rich by 54 points, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (95.6% near-term vs 48.1% at six months), which typically signals an imminent binary event (the recent Q2 earnings print on July 23). The put/call skew is elevated at 68.7% with a put/call volume ratio of 1.39, indicating meaningful hedging or bearish positioning into the event. Short interest is very high at 21.9% of float with 5.7 days to cover, which cuts both ways: it confirms a widely held bearish view but also creates squeeze potential on positive surprises, and the Q2 beat and guidance raise are the kind of catalyst that can force covering. The IV spike will collapse post-event, so the current elevated premium reflects event uncertainty rather than a durable directional signal. For a fundamental holder, the takeaway is that the crowd is positioned bearish and the setup is volatile, not that the fundamental thesis has changed.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The turnaround is recovering in real time: three consecutive quarters of North American retail growth, a Q2 EPS beat with a guidance raise, ~22% lower dealer inventory, and rising share into a growing market. The HDFS restructuring de-risked the balance sheet (long-term debt from ~$5.0B to ~$1.1B). Forward estimates are rising sharply (+26.9% next-year over 90 days) against a ~14x forward multiple, roughly half the peer median. If the cost program (≥$225M HDMC benefit by 2028) and HDFS rebuild toward $125-150M by 2029 deliver, normalized earnings power well above the trough supports a re-rating. The 21.9% short interest adds squeeze fuel to positive surprises. Bull case ~$32-34 (analyst high of $32, plus modest re-rating toward the low end of the peer multiple on delivered earnings).

**Bear Case.** The volume/affordability pivot dilutes premium brand equity and margins, and cheaper Sportster/Sprint volume proves cannibalistic rather than incremental. The brake-hose recall rules against Harley toward the $450M end of the range with no accrual. The credit rating is cut below investment grade, raising HDFS funding costs as it tries to rebuild. Demographic decline and high rates keep big-ticket discretionary demand soft. Under this scenario the stock retraces toward the analyst low of $15 and the March 2026 lows near $17. Bear case ~$17-18, roughly 33-38% downside from current levels.

**Base Case.** The turnaround holds but proves gradual and uneven; HDMC returns to modest profitability, HDFS rebuilds slowly, and the recall is resolved without a top-of-range outcome. The stock grinds toward the mid-to-high-$20s consensus zone. Base case ~$28-29.

The setup is two-sided, which is why this is a hold rather than a conviction call in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The improving trajectory and cheap starting multiple justify holding over avoiding, but the evidence is not yet sufficient for a confident BUY given the unproven strategy, the binary recall and rating risks, and the sector-level alpha caution.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is Q3 2026 earnings (late October/early November 2026), which will test whether the fourth consecutive quarter of North American retail growth materializes and whether the guidance raise holds. The credit-rating resolution (S&P CreditWatch) and any NHTSA determination on the brake-hose matter are event-driven checkpoints that could move sooner.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A fourth consecutive quarter of North American retail growth in Q3 2026 with HDMC operating margin recovering above ~6%, confirming the volume pivot is incremental rather than cannibalistic.
- Resolution of the credit-rating CreditWatch to Stable (or an upgrade), removing the funding-cost overhang on HDFS.
- Continued next-year EPS revision velocity above consensus with the stock still below ~16x forward, a clearer cycle-adjusted asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse NHTSA brake-hose ruling requiring an accrual toward the upper end of the $140M-$450M range.
- A downgrade below investment grade, materially raising HDFS funding costs.
- Evidence the Sportster/Sprint volume is cannibalizing high-margin touring sales (declining HDMC gross margin below the low-20s alongside flat or falling touring mix), or a reversal in the North American retail growth streak.

## Conclusion

Harley-Davidson is a show-me turnaround where the reported financials understate the underlying picture in both directions: the deconsolidation makes the balance sheet look de-risked while cratering near-term finance earnings, and the negative operating cash flow is a mechanical accounting artifact rather than a liquidity crisis. Beneath the noise, the operating trajectory is genuinely improving: three straight quarters of North American retail growth, a Q2 beat and guidance raise, healthier dealer inventory, rising share, and sharply rising forward estimates against a ~14x multiple.

Weighed against that are two large binary risks that sit off the income statement, the unreserved $140M-$450M recall contingency and the one-notch-above-junk credit rating on CreditWatch Negative, plus the fundamental uncertainty of an affordability pivot executed by a first-time-in-industry CEO that could either expand the market or erode the premium brand. The score has been improving steadily (4.0 in March to ~4.9 in June), and the new Q2 data confirms rather than reverses that trend, supporting the move from AVOID to HOLD. But the improvement is incremental and the asymmetry is not yet clearly favorable. We rate HOG **HOLD**, and would move to BUY on a confirmed fourth quarter of retail growth with margin recovery, or resolution of the credit-rating and recall overhangs.
