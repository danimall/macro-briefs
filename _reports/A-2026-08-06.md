## Executive Summary

Agilent delivered a strong quarter. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended April 30, 2026) revenue of $1,835M grew 10.0% reported and 6.3% core, non-GAAP operating margin expanded 130bps year over year to 26.4%, and management raised FY2026 guidance to $7.39-7.49B revenue and $6.00-6.10 non-GAAP EPS with 85bps of full-year margin expansion. Roughly 200bps of strategic pricing landed in the quarter — unusual for an instrument franchise facing Chinese low-cost competition — and the $64M increase in segment operating income on $167M of incremental revenue implies a 38% incremental margin. The stock gapped 16.9% on the print (from $115.84 to $135.38) and has since worked to $141.11, roughly 30% above the May 20 low of $108.35 and 12% below the November high of $160.27.

The operating improvement is real, but three specific findings prevent an upgrade. First, cash conversion has weakened for two consecutive comparable periods: H1 FY2026 operating cash flow fell 16.4% to $545M while net income rose 20.8% to $644M, putting OCF/net income at 0.85x versus 1.22x a year earlier, and $295M of the outflow sits in an aggregated "other assets and liabilities" line whose composition is not disclosed. On a normalized FY2026 basis this implies a 2.1-2.8% free cash flow yield against a 23.3x forward earnings multiple. Second, Agilent CrossLab — 41.4% of revenue, 50.1% of segment operating income, and the only genuinely annuity-like part of the business at a 32.0% operating margin — grew just +2% core with margin down 40bps, so the current revenue mix is shifting toward the 22-23%-margin product segments. Third, Asia Pacific (30.1% of revenue) grew 0.9% with China down 9%, and management's full-year China view depends on delayed stimulus revenue.

The scoring trajectory (6.5 → 6.5 → 6.0 → 6.0 → 6.3 → 6.2) now moves to 6.05, a fifth consecutive HOLD. The reason is not the price move — a higher price on improving fundamentals is legitimate re-rating — but the compression of the opportunity: at $112 in March the stock was 18.5x FY2026 guidance; at $141 it is 23.3x, and the incremental evidence gathered since (comp-design changes that weaken alignment, confirmed absence of insider buying through a 28% drawdown, quantified mix deterioration in CrossLab) argues for holding rather than adding. Base case $153, bull $180, bear $112.

## Company Overview

Agilent operates three reportable segments serving analytical and clinical laboratories. The most useful framing of the business is mix: in Q2 FY2026, instrumentation was $618M of $1,835M revenue (33.7%); non-instrumentation and other was $1,217M (66.3%).

| Segment | Q2 FY26 Revenue | % Total | Op Income | Op Margin | % Segment Profit | Core Growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Life Sciences & Diagnostics | $732M | 39.9% | $161M | 22.0% | 33.3% | +9% |
| Agilent CrossLab | $759M | 41.4% | $242M | 32.0% | 50.1% | +2% |
| Applied Markets | $344M | 18.8% | $80M | 23.3% | 16.6% | +11% |
| **Total segment** | **$1,835M** | 100% | **$483M** | 26.3% | 100% | +6.3% |

Segment operating income of $483M reconciles exactly to reported non-GAAP operating income; GAAP operating income of $399M equals that figure less $84M of unallocated corporate charges. Agilent's non-GAAP operating margin is effectively its segment margin before corporate transformation, amortization, and restructuring costs.

CrossLab is the structural asset. It bundles services (repair, preventive maintenance, compliance qualification, asset-management consulting, SaaS), consumables (GC/LC columns, sample prep, custom chemistries), and OpenLab informatics, and much of it is vendor-neutral, servicing laboratories regardless of whose instruments they own. That converts competitors' installed bases into an Agilent revenue pool and is the mechanical reason CrossLab earns 32% margins against 22-23% for the product segments.

End-market spread is genuinely diversified and no customer is disclosed as a concentration risk: pharma and biotech $663M (36.1% of Q2 revenue, +10.0%), chemicals and advanced materials $412M (22.5%, +11.7%), diagnostics and clinical $295M (16.1%, +15.7%), environmental and forensics $186M (10.1%, +17.0%), food $147M (8.0%, flat), academia and government $132M (7.2%, −2.2%).

The $950M all-cash acquisition of Biocare (10-Q counterparty: BC Midco I, Inc.), signed March 6, 2026 and reported complete in late June, adds immunohistochemistry antibodies, reagents, and staining instruments into the pathology franchise. The June 25, 2026 8-K carried both Item 1.01 and Item 2.03 (creation of a direct financial obligation), indicating new debt or drawn commercial paper funded the close; the terms are not verified in the material I reviewed.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2023 $6,833M → FY2024 $6,510M (−4.7%, the post-COVID instrument destocking trough) → FY2025 $6,948M (+6.7%, roughly 2pp from BIOVECTRA) → H1 FY2026 $3,633M (+8.5% reported, of which ~3pp FX). The guided FY2026 midpoint of $7.44B implies H2 revenue of $3.807B against $3.599B, or +5.8% reported, a deceleration from H1 on tougher comparisons that management has flagged.

**Margin.** FY2025 was a margin-down year (GAAP gross margin −190bps to 52.4%, operating margin 21.3% from 22.9%) on second-half tariffs and BIOVECTRA's dilutive CDMO gross margin. FY2026 reversed it: H1 gross margin 53.3% versus 52.7%, Q2 GAAP operating margin 21.7% versus 18.0%, non-GAAP 26.4% versus 25.1%.

**The GAAP headline overstates the operating gain.** Q2 pretax income rose $148M to $408M. Decomposing it: segment operating income +$64M (43% of the gain), lower unallocated corporate charges +$35M (24%, with restructuring falling to $8M from $56M), and an other income/expense swing to +$21M from −$25M (+$46M, 31%). GAAP diluted EPS rose 60% to $1.20; non-GAAP EPS rose 14% to $1.49. An investor anchoring on the GAAP figure misreads the trend by a wide margin. The 38% incremental segment margin is the honest signal and it is strong.

The favorable Q2 unallocated comparison is a timing artifact. On a half-year basis unallocated charges *rose* to $173M from $165M — restructuring fell $29M and amortization fell $17M, but transformational-initiative costs nearly doubled to $58M from $30M and "other" rose to $34M from $11M. Backing out Q2, Q1 FY2026 unallocated was ~$89M versus ~$46M. The costs of the "Ignite" transformation are growing and are excluded from non-GAAP results.

**Cash conversion is the clear soft spot.**

| | H1 FY26 | H1 FY25 | FY2025 | FY2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $545M | $652M | $1,559M | $1,751M |
| Capex | $169M | $211M | $407M | $378M |
| Free cash flow | $376M | $441M | $1,152M | $1,373M |
| Net income | $644M | $533M | ~$1,303M | — |
| OCF/net income | **0.85x** | **1.22x** | ~1.20x | — |

FY2025 OCF fell 11% on a 6.7% revenue increase; H1 FY2026 OCF fell 16.4% while net income rose 20.8%. Identified drivers more than account for the $107M decline: cash income taxes paid $320M versus $248M (+$72M), variable compensation paid out $150M versus $98M (+$52M), and inventory build using $85M versus $41M (+$44M). Two of those are benign — the bonus payout reflects FY2025 performance already earned, and inventory build was described as partly deliberate tariff mitigation. The cash tax step-up is structural. The unresolved item is a $295M outflow in the aggregated "other assets and liabilities" line versus $14M a year earlier, equal to ~54% of half-year OCF; consistent balance-sheet movements (other current assets +$76M, other non-current assets +$117M, other accrued liabilities −$108M) point to prepaid or refundable tax positions and possibly capitalized tariff costs awaiting refund, but the disclosure does not permit a conclusion.

I will not annualize H1 free cash flow — FY2025 H1 OCF was 42% of the full year, variable pay is a Q1 outflow, and capex is guided to ~$450M against $169M spent. Two labelled sensitivities: if FY2026 OCF holds the FY2025 H1 proportion, OCF ≈ $1.30B and FCF ≈ $0.85B; if working capital normalizes and OCF matches FY2025's $1.559B, FCF ≈ $1.11B. Against a $39.86B market cap that is a **2.1%-2.8% FCF yield**.

**Tax normalization is the largest quantifiable earnings headwind and is under-discussed.** FY2025's 9.2% GAAP effective rate was built on ~$57M of intra-entity IP transfer benefit, ~$29M of FDII benefit, and ~$28M of reserve releases, on top of a Singapore holiday running to 2030 worth ~$102M / ~$0.36 of annual EPS. H1 FY2026 GAAP ETR has already normalized to 16.6% and the non-GAAP tax assumption rose to 14.5% from 11.5%. Applying the 7.4pp increase to H1 pretax income of $772M gives ~$57M, or ~$0.20 per diluted share, in the first half alone — roughly $0.40-0.45 annualized on GAAP, ~$0.20 on non-GAAP. That Agilent still guides to +8.2% non-GAAP EPS growth ($6.05 versus $5.59) while absorbing that implies underlying pretax earnings growth nearer 12%. This is the strongest bull point in the FY2026 guide and it is not obvious from the headline. The symmetry is that FY2025's $5.59 was itself tax-flattered, so the FY2023-25 adjusted EPS series ($5.44 → $5.29 → $5.59, +2.8% cumulative) understates FY2026's operating progress and overstates FY2025's.

**Balance sheet** (April 30, 2026): cash $1,807M, total debt ~$3,355M, net debt ~$1,548M; current ratio 2.10x; equity $7,122M (54.5% of assets); debt/equity 0.47x; interest coverage ~15x on H1 operating income of $752M against $50M of interest. Estimated annualized GAAP EBITDA of ~$1.8B puts net debt/EBITDA at ~0.85x standalone and ~1.4x pro forma for a fully debt-funded Biocare. $300M of 3.05% notes mature September 22, 2026 and are reclassified short-term; a $1.5B revolver to June 2028, a $1.5B CP program, and a $300M money-market line were all undrawn at quarter end. Commercial paper usage fell sharply year over year ($360M borrowed and repaid in H1 versus $882M/$782M). This is an investment-grade balance sheet with no solvency question; the financing terms of Biocare remain the open item in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Capital allocation contains an inconsistency.** H1 repurchases were 1.600M shares for $217M (versus $255M in H1 FY2025), including only 550,000 shares for $65M at an average $118.90 in Q2 — the quarter in which the stock traded as low as $110.31 and insider Form 4s establish market prices of $111.75 (March 19) and $113.98 (March 31), roughly 28% below the November high of $154.37. Shares outstanding fell only 0.30% over six months (283.054M → 282.218M) while buybacks plus $144M of dividends consumed 96% of H1 free cash flow. The defensible reading is deliberate sequencing to preserve capacity for Biocare, ~$450M of capex, and the September note maturity; blackout constraints around the March 6 Biocare signing may also have mechanically limited windows. Either way, shareholders did not capture the drawdown and net share retirement was negligible relative to cash deployed.

## Growth Analysis

The instrument replacement cycle has turned, and this is the durable part of the story. Chromatography systems carry 7-10 year lives with methods validated into regulatory filings; the FY2024 air pocket was a deferral, not a demand loss. Management cited high-single-digit instrument growth in Q2 with low-double-digit growth in LC, LC/MS, and GC, plus new platform launches (9500 ICP-MS and upgraded flagship GC at ASMS). Applied Markets' +11% core growth is the cleanest read on that cycle.

Pricing power is the standout finding. Roughly 200bps of strategic pricing in a single quarter, with management stating they expect to exceed the initial full-year pricing goal, validates the switching-cost thesis: regulated laboratories with validated methods and installed Agilent fleets have limited practical ability to substitute on price. The qualifier is that this pricing is also the mechanism by which tariffs were "fully mitigated," so part of it is cost pass-through rather than permanent margin capture and may not be additive in FY2027.

Forensics is a small, high-quality growth vector: environmental and forensics +17.0% reported with forensics growth cited above 50%, a TSA contract contributing $5M in the quarter, and a positioning claim on further aviation-security tenders. This runs through molecular spectroscopy (Raman, IR) and is uncorrelated with pharma capex.

Against that, three things temper the growth score:

**Growth quality is FX- and Americas-dependent.** Of Q2's +10% reported growth, ~3.7pp was currency ($61M). Regionally, Americas $764M (+12.7%), Europe $518M (+17.2%, of which 9-11pp was FX by segment), Asia Pacific $553M (+0.9%). FY2026 guidance embeds ~1.8pp of FX benefit (6.3-7.8% reported versus 4.5-6.0% core) that reverses on dollar strength.

**Roughly 15% of revenue is flat-to-declining.** Food was exactly flat at $147M in Q2 and −0.6% in H1; academia and government fell 2.2% in Q2 and 3.7% in H1 to $262M. The 10-K added explicit language on federal appropriations, shutdowns, and federal workforce reduction — this is Agilent's only structurally shrinking end market and the drag has persisted multiple quarters.

**CrossLab's +2% is the central tension.** The highest-margin, most recurring, most defensible segment is growing at a third the rate of the product segments, and its margin slipped 40bps. The filing attributes APAC weakness to China consumables destocking after a prior-year tariff-driven pull-forward, which is a timing explanation implying reacceleration. If instead +2% reflects Chinese domestic consumables substitution or laboratory consolidation compressing service-contract value, then the guided 85bps of margin expansion is a one-year event rather than the start of a multi-year path.

Consensus is consistent with mid-single-digit revenue and high-single-digit EPS growth: current-year $6.06 (revised +1.4% over 90 days), next-year $6.61 (+0.5%). Estimates are stable, not rising — the revision velocity that flags a genuine inflection trade is absent here. The earnings track record is also mixed: three consecutive small non-GAAP misses (−1.6%, −1.6%, −2.6%) before the Q2 beat, which argues for taking the implied Q4 non-GAAP figure of ~$1.71 (FY26 midpoint $6.05 less H1 $2.85 less Q3 guide midpoint $1.49) as an execution risk rather than a formality.

## Competitive Landscape

Agilent competes against Thermo Fisher and Danaher (both materially larger and more acquisitive), Waters and Bruker (closer in scale, more focused), Shimadzu and Sartorius, and a widening field of Chinese domestic instrument makers benefiting from localization preferences. The FY2025 10-K explicitly flags consolidation among competitors as a risk; management expects scale pressure.

Trajectory assessment, which matters more than level:

**Improving.** Realized pricing of ~200bps demonstrates that the regulated installed base and validated-method lock-in are economically live, not just rhetorical. Instrument growth at high single digits with LC/LC-MS/GC at low double digits implies share retention or gain in the core franchise during a replacement wave. Forensics is opening a government-funded adjacency. Biocare consolidates a fragmented IHC reagent niche directly adjacent to the existing pathology platform (OMNIS, IHC/ISH/H&E) at a time when diagnostics and clinical is Agilent's second-fastest end market (+15.7% reported).

**Deteriorating.** The vendor-neutral service annuity — the piece of the business that most resembles a moat — is growing at +2% with margin down 40bps, and APAC CrossLab revenue actually declined 1%. Mix is therefore shifting from a 32%-margin recurring pool toward 22-23%-margin product segments, which mathematically caps sustainable margin expansion regardless of pricing. China at −9% raises the question of domestic consumables substitution in a market representing a meaningful share of the 30.1% APAC contribution. The full-year China assumption depends on delayed stimulus revenue, which makes that assumption a genuine risk rather than a formality.

Net: 6.5. The core instrument franchise is strengthening; the annuity is not, and the annuity is what justifies a premium multiple in this sector.

## Risk Assessment

**Tax structure volatility.** The Singapore holiday to 2030 is worth ~$102M / $0.36 of annual EPS and is conditional on meeting investment and employment thresholds, with clawback exposure. Pillar Two adds $30M of top-up taxes expected within three years, and long-term income tax liabilities rose to $54M from $28M. Agilent's tax line is a structurally uncertain earnings input.

**China and APAC.** Management called the market stable and retained the full-year view; the guide depends on stimulus-linked revenue materialising in H2.

**Government and academic funding.** Academia and government revenue is declining, with newly expanded risk-factor language on appropriations, shutdowns, and federal workforce reductions.

**Advanced Therapeutics capex against cancellable demand.** ~$450M of FY2026 capex, principally nucleic acid / advanced therapeutics capacity at Frederick, Colorado, following the ~$862M BIOVECTRA acquisition. The 10-K states directly that the drug programs underlying both the CDMO business and companion diagnostics may be cancelled on clinical trial failures. Fixed long-lived capacity against contract-level binary demand, in a segment with dilutive gross margin. Partial offsets: oligonucleotide GMP capacity is scarce, qualification creates switching costs once a program enters an NDA, and some spending may qualify for reimbursement incentives.

**Biocare financing and integration.** The June 25 8-K's Item 2.03 indicates a new direct financial obligation whose amount, structure, tenor, and pricing I could not verify. Pro forma leverage of ~1.4x EBITDA is an estimate assuming full debt/cash funding. Biocare's standalone revenue, growth, and margin are unknown, so the $950M multiple cannot be assessed. Integration lands on a management team where CEO, CFO, and General Counsel have all changed within 24 months.

**Executive turnover.** At least eight Item 5.02 8-Ks in roughly fourteen months. CEO McDonnell since May 2024; CFO Adam Elinoff since November 2025 following a four-month interim period, and on a domestic short-term assignment arrangement rather than permanent relocation; General Counsel Bret DiMarco separated March 31, 2026. One 8-K (April 3, 2026) is indexed under Item 5.01 "Changes in Control of Registrant," but the June 1 10-Q identifies its Exhibit 10.1 as the DiMarco separation agreement, describes Agilent as an independent NYSE registrant, and discloses no change of control anywhere. This is almost certainly a mis-tag for Item 5.02; **no change of control should be assumed.**

**Operational concentration.** Manufacturing consolidation has created single points of failure, with California and Japan facilities in high seismic-risk zones and back-office accounting and tax functions concentrated in India and Malaysia.

**Regulatory.** EU IVDR clinical-evidence, QMS, and post-market surveillance requirements bear on pathology and companion diagnostics, and Biocare increases that exposure.

**Unresolved disclosure.** The $295M "other assets and liabilities" H1 cash outflow, the March 20, 2026 charter and bylaw amendments (content not reviewed), and the FY2026 grant mix that replaced discontinued LTPP-EPS awards and stock options.

Offsetting all of this: no disclosed customer concentration, genuinely diversified end markets, an investment-grade balance sheet, no material pending litigation, and no binary regulatory event. The risk set is diffuse and mostly priced. Score 6.3.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to ~$180)

The replacement cycle is multi-year, not a single-year catch-up: chromatography lives of 7-10 years and the depth of the FY2024 air pocket (−4.7% revenue) imply several years of above-trend instrument demand, and Applied Markets' +11% core plus low-double-digit LC/GC growth are the leading indicators. Pricing of ~200bps proves the switching-cost moat converts to margin. Tax normalization is masking underlying pretax growth of roughly 12%, so the FY2027 earnings base is stronger than the +8.2% guided EPS growth suggests, and the drag does not repeat at the same magnitude. CrossLab's +2% is China destocking after a tariff pull-forward and reaccelerates to mid-single digits, restoring positive mix and extending margin expansion beyond FY2026's 85bps. Restructuring savings were raised to $90-95M from $75-80M and complete by fiscal year-end. The tariff refund contingency delivers unrecognized cash. Biocare adds a fragmented-niche consolidation into a +15.7% end market. FY2027 EPS reaches ~$6.90 and the multiple re-rates to 26x — a modest premium to MTD — for $180.

### Bear Case (to ~$112)

FX reverses and takes ~1.8pp out of reported growth; core decelerates as the H2 comparison tightens (already guided from +8.5% H1 to +5.8% H2 reported) and the implied Q4 non-GAAP step-up to ~$1.71 misses, extending the pattern of three small misses that preceded Q2. CrossLab's +2% proves structural — Chinese domestic consumables substitution plus laboratory consolidation compressing service-contract value — so the mix shift continues and margin expansion stops at FY2026. China stimulus does not arrive; APAC turns negative rather than flat. Cash conversion stays near 0.85x, making the 2%-range FCF yield the operative valuation metric and compressing the multiple toward 18x on ~$6.20 of FY2027 EPS, or $112 — approximately the May 20 low of $108.35 and below the lowest analyst target of $135. Biocare integration consumes management attention at a company that has replaced its CEO, CFO, and General Counsel inside two years, and the debt taken on to fund it carries a higher-for-longer coupon.

### Weighing the two

The bull case requires CrossLab reacceleration to work fully, and there is one quarter of evidence, attributed to a timing artifact. The bear case requires the FX and China risks to compound, which is plausible but not the modal path given management retained its China view with the full year intact and delivered a raise. The middle outcome — mid-single-digit core growth, FY2027 EPS near consensus $6.61, multiple roughly unchanged — produces about 8-9% total return over twelve months. That is worth holding and not worth paying up for, particularly in a sector where selection has historically added little above the market.

The five-year record supports caution on paying a premium: indexed total shareholder return from FY2020 = 100 reached 148.32 for Agilent versus 193.57 for its own selected peer group in FY2025, a cumulative +48.3% against +93.6%, with Agilent behind in each of FY2023, FY2024, and FY2025. Adjusted EPS grew 2.8% cumulatively from the FY2023 peak of $5.44 to FY2025's $5.59. FY2026 is precisely the catch-up case, and the market has begun to price it; how much is left is the open question.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** fiscal Q3 FY2026 results, expected late August 2026 (quarter ended July 31), is the near-term checkpoint; the 3-month options tenor implies a ±10.1% move spanning it. FY2027 initial guidance with the Q4 print in late November 2026 is the decisive event.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **CrossLab core growth returning to ≥4%** in Q3 or Q4 FY2026, with segment operating margin at or above 32.0%. This is the single metric that converts the FY2026 margin guide into a multi-year path and the one I would weight most heavily.
2. **Operating cash flow conversion restored to ≥1.0x net income** on a nine-month FY2026 basis, with disclosure clarifying the $295M "other assets and liabilities" outflow. That would move the FCF yield toward 2.8%+ and remove the principal valuation objection.
3. **Price toward $120-125** with FY2027 consensus intact at ~$6.60 (18-19x forward), which restores base-case upside above 20%.
4. Verified Biocare financing at investment-grade terms plus disclosed acquired revenue and margin permitting an assessment of the $950M multiple, combined with resumption of buybacks at a pace that shrinks the share count more than 1% annually.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **FY2026 revenue or non-GAAP EPS guidance cut** at the Q3 print, or a Q4 non-GAAP result materially below the ~$1.71 implied figure, which would confirm the H2 execution risk and the pre-Q2 miss pattern.
2. **Asia Pacific revenue turning negative year over year** with CrossLab APAC declining further, indicating China substitution rather than destocking.
3. **Full-year OCF below ~$1.25B**, holding conversion under 0.90x for a second consecutive year and making the FCF yield untenable against a 23x multiple.
