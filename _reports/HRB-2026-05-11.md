## Executive Summary

H&R Block has declined 47% from its 52-week high of $64.62 to $36.06, creating a valuation of approximately 7x forward earnings and a 13% FCF yield on a business generating nearly $1B in annual EBITDA with investment-grade credit ratings. The Q3 FY2026 report (March quarter) showed 5.3% revenue growth, EPS of $6.02 vs. $5.38 prior year, and prompted a full-year guidance raise. The stock surged 24% on the earnings release (May 7) but remains well below prior levels.

The decline reflects legitimate structural concerns: assisted tax volumes fell 0.9% in FY2025, AI-driven disruption could compress the value of human preparers over time, and a CEO transition in January 2026 introduces execution risk. However, the magnitude of decline appears disproportionate to the near-term operational trajectory. The business continues to generate substantial FCF, maintains pricing power (net average charge up 5.1%), and benefits from the IRS Direct File shutdown under the current administration. Short interest at 19% of float suggests meaningful institutional skepticism that could fuel a re-rating if fundamentals continue to hold.

The prior AVOID recommendation (April 13 at score 4.8) captured a stock trading around $31. The stock has since bounced 16% on strong earnings. I am upgrading to HOLD based on the Q3 beat, raised guidance, and a valuation that now more than compensates for the known structural risks.

## Company Overview

H&R Block is the largest retail tax preparation company in the United States, operating 6,701 company-owned and 2,013 franchise offices. The company filed or facilitated 22.8 million tax returns in FY2025. Revenue breaks down as: assisted tax preparation (64%, $2.41B), DIY software (10%, $384M), international (7%, $247M), franchise royalties (5%, $193M), Wave small business solutions (3%, $109M), and financial services/other.

The company's fiscal year ends June 30, with substantially all revenue earned between February and April during U.S. tax season. Curtis Campbell replaced long-tenured CEO Jeffrey Jones on January 1, 2026. Campbell previously served as CEO of TaxAct (2018-2023) and held VP roles at Intuit.

Consensus has FY2026 EPS at $5.16 (current year) and FY2027 at $5.82. The 30-day revision trend shows modest upward movement (+3.2% current year, +4.4% next year) following the Q3 beat. This confirms stability and modest improvement, not explosive revision velocity.

Growth is real but modest — mid-single-digit revenue, high-single-digit EPS (boosted by buybacks). This is a mature, cash-generative franchise growing at GDP+ rates with significant capital return optionality.

**Why is the market pricing this at 7x?**
The market appears to be pricing a structural decline scenario — that AI will erode the value of human tax preparers and compress revenue over time. The 19% short interest and 47% decline from highs suggest institutional conviction in this thesis.

My assessment: At 7x earnings with 13% FCF yield, the market is pricing in approximately 3-4% annual revenue decline in perpetuity. The company is currently growing at 4-5%. For the market to be correct, growth would need to decelerate by 7-9 percentage points. This is possible over a 5-10 year horizon but unlikely in the next 2-3 years given the complexity of U.S. tax code, the IRS Direct File shutdown, and the company's own AI investments.

## Competitive Landscape

The company maintained assisted channel market share for the third consecutive year. In DIY, it gained share against TurboTax's dominance. Wave is growing in the fragmented small-business bookkeeping space.

**Structural threats:**
- AI could handle increasingly complex tax situations, reducing the value proposition of $300+ assisted prep
- Intuit is investing heavily in AI-first tax filing and expanding to physical stores (hybridizing)
- New AI-native entrants are emerging (Column Tax, etc.)

**Structural defenses:**
- U.S. tax code complexity remains a barrier — 70,000+ pages of regulations, frequent changes
- Audit protection, guaranteed accuracy, and human accountability remain valued by customers
- Physical presence in 8,700+ locations creates distribution advantage for less tech-savvy demographic
- Brand trust accumulated over 70 years
- IRS Direct File shutdown removes the most existential competitive threat (a free government alternative)

The competitive trajectory is flat to slightly deteriorating. Volume declines in assisted prep are being offset by pricing power, but this strategy has a finite runway. The company's pivot toward AI-augmented services and small business solutions represents a credible but unproven rebalancing.

## Risk Assessment

**1. AI Disruption of Assisted Tax Preparation (High Severity, Medium-Term Timeline)**
If AI achieves sufficient accuracy for complex returns within 3-5 years, the 64% of revenue from assisted prep faces compression. Current AI can handle simple returns but struggles with multi-state, small business, and investment-intensive situations. The timeline for meaningful disruption is 3-7 years, not 1-2.

**2. Declining Assisted Volumes (Active, Gradual)**
If volume declines accelerate to 3-5% annually, pricing power cannot fully offset. This is the fundamental structural concern and justifies a discount to peer multiples.

**3. CEO Transition Risk**
Campbell is experienced but new. His strategic vision hasn't been fully articulated. The board appointing someone with Intuit/TaxAct background suggests a digital transformation push, which could involve near-term investment spending before benefits materialize.

**4. Vendor Concentration**
Pathward N.A. handles all banking products (Emerald Card, refund transfers). Contract extends only through June 2027. Loss would be severely disruptive to financial products revenue.

**5. Short Squeeze or Continued Short Pressure**
At 19% short interest with 7.1 days to cover, the stock is susceptible to violent moves in both directions. The May 7 earnings pop demonstrated this dynamic.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

2. New CEO articulates credible strategic plan with specific digital/AI milestones at Q4 FY2026 earnings
3. Stock declines below $30 with no fundamental deterioration (would push forward P/E below 6x)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**

2. Management announces large acquisition (>$500M) that dilutes the capital return story
3. IRS Direct File reinstated with national rollout announcement
4. FY2027 EPS estimate falls below $5.00 (indicating fundamental deterioration)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (Q4 FY2026, likely August 2026). The full-year report will provide the first complete dataset under the new CEO and the final word on whether the raised FY2026 guidance was achieved.

## Conclusion

H&R Block at $36 and 7x forward earnings presents a deeply discounted cash-generative franchise where the market is pricing in structural decline that hasn't yet manifested in operating results. The Q3 FY2026 beat, raised guidance, and 5.3% revenue growth contradict the decline narrative in the near term. However, the secular risk to assisted tax preparation from AI is genuine, the CEO transition introduces execution uncertainty, and 19% short interest indicates informed bearish positioning.

The prior AVOID call at $31 captured the low, but at $36 with improving fundamentals and post-earnings confirmation of the business trajectory, the risk/reward has shifted. The stock is cheap enough that modest execution can drive meaningful returns from multiple expansion, but the structural overhang prevents full conviction for a BUY. I am upgrading to HOLD with a growth orientation — the thesis is that operational stability at these valuations creates positive expected value, with the next catalyst being the new CEO's first full strategic articulation.
