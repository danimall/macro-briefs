## Executive Summary

SEI Investments is a $10.9B financial technology and asset management company generating $2.3B in annual revenue across five segments that serve investment managers, private banks, RIAs, and institutional investors. The business model combines technology outsourcing (~57% of revenue) with asset management fees (~38%), creating an integrated platform with high switching costs and exceptional capital efficiency: ROE of 29.2% and FCF margins of 25.5%.

The company is in an accelerating growth phase. Q1 2026 revenue grew 12.9% YoY to $622M with adjusted EPS of $1.44, beating consensus by 7.1%. Operating margins have expanded from 22.1% in 2023 to 27.3% in 2025, and every business segment improved profitability last year. The $527M Stratos acquisition (closed December 2025) extends SEI into direct advice delivery, adding 360+ advisors and $38.4B in client assets. Analyst estimates are rising across all time horizons, with current-year EPS revised up 6.4% over the past 90 days.

At 15.4x trailing earnings and approximately 15.4x forward estimates, SEIC trades at a meaningful discount to its peer median P/E of 18.5x despite superior growth, margins, and capital returns. The stock has rallied 16% off its March lows following the Q1 beat but remains 3.4% below its 52-week high. Key risks include market-sensitive revenue, LSV underperformance, Stratos integration complexity, and broad-based insider selling that signals management does not view shares as notably cheap. The options market is pricing near-term stress (30.8% IV vs. 22.3% HV, backwardated term structure), which creates a less favorable entry point for the next 30-45 days but does not alter the 12-month thesis.

## Company Overview

SEI Investments operates as a technology and operations outsourcer that also manages money. The majority of revenue comes from technology infrastructure and operational services, not pure asset management fees. The company manages, advises, or administers approximately $1.9 trillion in assets.

**Segment Breakdown (2025 Revenue):**
- **Investment Managers** (35%, $815M): Front-to-back outsourced operations for alternative and traditional asset managers. Serves 43 of the top 100 global managers. $1.5T in AUM/AUA including $1.0T in alternatives.
- **Private Banks** (26%, $573M): Technology and asset management for bank wealth management businesses via the SEI Wealth Platform and TRUST 3000.
- **Investment Advisors** (25%, $577M): Wealth management technology for independent RIAs, banks, and broker-dealers.
- **Institutional Investors** (12%, $283M): OCIO services for retirement plans, healthcare, higher education.
- **New Businesses** (2%, $50M): SEI Private Wealth Management, SEI Sphere, Stratos (beginning to contribute).

SEI also owns ~38.5% of LSV Asset Management, a deep value equity specialist managing ~$99.2B, which contributed $132M to pretax income in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings Trajectory:** Revenue grew from $1.92B in 2023 to $2.30B in 2025 (9.4% CAGR), with operating income growing faster at 21.4% CAGR ($425M → $627M). This operating leverage is the most important financial characteristic — technology platform costs are largely fixed, so incremental revenue drops through to profit at elevated rates. Net income of $715M in 2025 includes a $94.4M one-time gain from the Family Office Services divestiture; adjusted net income of ~$621M still represents ~17% YoY growth.

**Margin Expansion Across All Segments:**
| Segment | 2024 Op. Margin | 2025 Op. Margin |
|---------|----------------|----------------|
| Investment Managers | 38% | 39% |
| Private Banks | 15% | 17% |
| Investment Advisors | 44% | 46% |
| Institutional Investors | 46% | 48% |
| New Businesses | -24% | -22% |

Private Banks turning from a persistent drag (10% margin in 2023) to 17% margin is a structurally meaningful improvement. Investment Advisors and Institutional Investors at 46-48% margins demonstrate the profitability potential of the platform model at scale.

**Balance Sheet:** Minimal leverage (D/E 0.27), no long-term debt, $500M undrawn revolver extended to 2030, current ratio of 3.29. Cash declined from $840M to $400M primarily due to Stratos ($441M) and buybacks ($616M). The Altman Z-Score of 4.8 and Piotroski F-Score of 4/5 confirm strong financial health.

**Capital Returns:** SEI returned $740M to shareholders in 2025 (126% of operating cash flow) through $616M in buybacks and $124M in dividends. Share count declined ~5.7% YoY. This is the third consecutive year of accelerating buyback activity ($311M → $512M → $616M). The company has reduced its share count from ~134M to ~120M over the past three years, providing a significant per-share earnings tailwind.

**Free Cash Flow:** $585M in 2025 (25.5% FCF margin) on minimal capex of $23M. This asset-light profile is a structural advantage.

**Q1 2026 Performance:** Revenue of $622.2M (+12.9% YoY), adjusted EPS of $1.44 (+23% YoY) beating consensus of $1.34 by 7.1%. Record sales in Investment Managers.

The growth picture is net positive: the expanding segments (Investment Managers, Investment Advisors, Private Banks) collectively represent 86% of revenue, while the shrinking segment (Institutional) is 12%.

## Valuation Assessment

SEIC trades at 15.4x trailing EPS ($5.63) and approximately 15.4x FY2026E consensus ($5.89). Adjusting for the $0.58/share Family Office divestiture gain, the normalized trailing P/E is approximately 18.0x, which is roughly in line with the peer median of 18.5x.

**Peer Comparison:**
| Metric | SEIC | Peer Median |
|--------|------|-------------|
| P/E | 15.4x | 18.5x |
| Div Yield | 1.1% | 2.6% |
| ROE | 29.2% | ~15% (industry avg) |
| FCF Margin | 25.5% | ~15-20% |
| 3Y Rev CAGR | 4.9% | ~3-5% |

The discount to peers is difficult to justify given SEI's superior ROE (29.2% vs. industry ~15%), expanding margins, accelerating growth, and aggressive buyback program. The lower dividend yield reflects a preference for buybacks over dividends, which is a more tax-efficient capital return mechanism at current valuations.

**Valuation Math:** At 17x FY2026E EPS of $5.89, fair value is $100. At 18x (peer median), fair value is $106. The analyst consensus median target of $107 and mean of $104 bracket this range. At 18x FY2027E EPS of $6.62, the stock reaches $119.

The market appears to be applying a discount for: (1) asset-sensitivity risk in a volatile market environment, (2) Stratos integration uncertainty, and (3) general skepticism about traditional asset managers. The first concern is valid but partially mitigated by the technology revenue mix. The second is time-limited. The third mischaracterizes SEI's business model, which is primarily a technology outsourcer.

## Competitive Landscape

SEI occupies a defensible niche as one of few providers offering integrated technology, custody, operations, and investment management on a single platform. Competitors typically offer one or two of these services:
- **SS&C Technologies** competes in technology/operations but lacks SEI's custody capabilities
- **State Street/BNY Mellon** compete in custody/administration but as much larger, less focused organizations
- **Envestnet/Orion** compete in RIA technology but without SEI's operational outsourcing depth
- **FIS/FNZ** compete in bank technology but without the asset management integration

The $1.0T in alternative asset administration is a high-growth competitive moat. As private credit and alternative strategies proliferate, the operational complexity (NAV calculations, investor reporting, compliance) of these assets creates demand for specialized administrators. SEI's early investment in this capability positions it well for a multi-year secular trend.

**Trajectory assessment:** SEI's competitive position is improving. Investment Managers segment revenue growth of ~12% indicates share gains. The Stratos acquisition extends the platform into direct advice delivery, a new competitive vector. Private Banks margin recovery suggests the SWP platform is gaining traction against competitors. However, Institutional Investors is losing share as the OCIO market consolidates around larger players.

## Risk Assessment

**Market Sensitivity:** Approximately 38% of revenue is directly linked to AUM levels. A sustained 20% equity market decline would reduce asset-based revenue by roughly $175M, compressing earnings by an estimated $0.70-0.85/share. In the current environment with elevated equity risk premiums, this is a material near-term risk.

**LSV Structural Decline:** The 38.5% stake in LSV contributed $132M to pretax income but LSV's AUM growth has stalled and client outflows persist. If value equity remains out of favor, LSV's contribution could decline 10-20% over the next 2-3 years. SEI has limited control over LSV's operations.

**Stratos Integration:** At $527M total commitment for 57.5% ownership with management retaining operational control, this is a complex structure. The Mexico affiliate acquisition introduces additional regulatory and currency risk. If Stratos fails to grow or key advisors depart, the acquisition premium (goodwill nearly doubled to $355M) could require impairment.

**UK FCA Regulatory Matter:** SEI's UK subsidiary is working with the FCA to determine the nature and scope of remedial actions to meet FCA expectations. The scope and financial impact remain uncertain. Given the current regulatory environment in the UK, this warrants monitoring.

**Balance Sheet Thinning:** Cash declined from $840M to $400M (~$147M freely accessible post-Stratos). The buyback program at 126% of operating cash flow is unsustainable at this pace without renewed cash generation. A material market downturn combined with Stratos cash needs could force a reduction in buyback activity, removing a per-share earnings growth lever.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty: ATM IV of 30.8% is 8.4 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 22.3%. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 30.8% > long-term 25.8%), indicating the market expects a specific near-term catalyst to resolve uncertainty. Put/call OI ratio surged from 1.16 to 2.67 over the past week, a significant increase in institutional put positioning relative to calls.

This elevated near-term vol likely reflects post-earnings positioning adjustment (earnings reported April 22) and profit-taking after the 16% rally from March lows. The backwardated structure and elevated put skew (8.1%) suggest institutional hedging of the recent gains. For a 12-month investor, the current options pricing does not invalidate the fundamental thesis but does suggest the next 30-45 days may see elevated price volatility and a potential pullback from the post-earnings spike.

## Conclusion

SEI Investments is a high-quality financial technology and operations business experiencing accelerating growth, expanding margins, and rising analyst estimates. At 15.4x earnings with 29.2% ROE and 25.5% FCF margins, it trades at a discount to the asset management peer median of 18.5x despite superior profitability metrics.

The primary concerns are market sensitivity in the current macro environment, Stratos integration risk, broad insider selling, and elevated near-term options vol suggesting institutional caution. These risks warrant standard position sizing (2-3%) rather than an overweight allocation. With estimates rising 6-7% over 90 days, every segment improving margins, and meaningful per-share earnings accretion from buybacks, the 12-month risk/reward is favorable. The analyst consensus target range of $69-$122 (median $107) brackets a reasonable outcome distribution that skews positively from the current $91 price.

Rated BUY with a $104 base case target and 2-3% position sizing. The specific risk preventing a higher-conviction call is the 38% asset-based revenue exposure combined with the absence of any insider buying and the options market's elevated put positioning (OI P/C 2.67). These factors cap the appropriate allocation but do not outweigh the fundamental case at this valuation.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following correction may warrant revisiting this recommendation: The normalized trailing P/E (adjusting for the $0.58 one-time gain) is approximately 18.0x, not 17.9x, and is roughly in line with (not a discount to) the peer median of 18.5x, somewhat weakening the valuation discount argument on a trailing basis. The forward P/E discount remains intact.
