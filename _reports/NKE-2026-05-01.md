## Executive Summary

Nike is executing a multi-year turnaround under CEO Elliott Hill, but the financial results through Q3 FY2026 (ended February 2026) show the recovery is partial and uneven. FY2025 revenue fell 10% to $46.3B with net income declining 44%. The most recent quarter delivered flat revenue on a reported basis (down 3% currency-neutral) with gross margin compressing another 130 bps, driven primarily by tariff-related product cost increases of 270 bps in North America. The single encouraging datapoint — North America wholesale grew 11% in Q3 — is overwhelmed by Greater China declining 10%, Converse collapsing 37%, and NIKE Direct continuing its slide.

The stock trades at $44.41, down 45% from its 52-week high and 55% below the $97.57 average price at which Nike repurchased $12.1B of its own shares. At ~29x trailing EPS, the multiple prices in meaningful recovery, but consensus estimates are moving sharply lower: current-quarter EPS has been cut 47% in the last 30 days, and next-year estimates are down 19%. Insider buying from CEO Hill, director Tim Cook, and others at the $42 level is genuine, but the fundamental trajectory and macro backdrop argue against holding the stock at current valuations.

## Company Overview

Nike is the world's largest athletic footwear and apparel company, generating $46.3B in FY2025 revenue through two brands: NIKE Brand (~97% of revenue) and Converse (~3%). The company operates across four geographies: North America (42% of NIKE Brand revenue), EMEA (27%), Greater China (15%), and Asia Pacific & Latin America (14%). Distribution splits between wholesale (58%) and NIKE Direct (42%, including owned stores and digital). Nike owns no manufacturing, sourcing from contract manufacturers primarily in Vietnam, Indonesia, and China.

CEO Elliott Hill took over in October 2024, replacing John Donahoe, whose DTC-first digital strategy had alienated wholesale partners and led to excessive discounting on Nike.com. Hill's turnaround rests on three pillars: reducing supply of oversaturated lifestyle franchises (Air Force 1, Dunk, Air Jordan retros) to restore scarcity; repositioning NIKE Digital as full-price; and reinvesting in wholesale distribution and sport-authentic brand marketing.

FY2025 was the worst annual performance in over a decade. Every channel and geography declined: NIKE Direct fell 13% (digital down 20%), wholesale fell 7%, Jordan Brand dropped 16%, and Converse declined 19%.

Q3 FY2026 (ended February 2026) shows selective improvement offset by continued deterioration:

- **North America**: Revenue up 3% currency-neutral; wholesale surged 11%, validating the reinvestment in retail partners. NIKE Direct fell 8%.
- **Greater China**: Revenue down 10% currency-neutral; digital down 21%. EBIT declined further. Management explicitly guided continued negative impacts through fiscal 2027.
- **EMEA**: Revenue up 1% currency-neutral, but comparable store sales collapsed 19% in Q3, suggesting severe in-store traffic loss.
- **Converse**: Revenue down 37% currency-neutral to $264M. Gross margin 36.7% with 970 bps of compression. The brand is generating negative EBIT.

Q3 gross margin fell 130 bps to 40.2%. EBIT margin compressed to 5.6%.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow deteriorated to $1.2B in the first nine months of FY2026 versus $3.2B in the prior-year period, primarily from working capital absorption related to increased wholesale shipments. Cash and short-term investments of $8.1B provide adequate liquidity. Long-term debt stands at $7.0B with debt-to-equity of 2.77x — elevated but supported by investment-grade ratings (A+/A2, though both recently downgraded one notch).

The capital allocation history is damaging: Nike repurchased $12.1B under its current $18B program at an average price of $97.57 per share, more than double the current price. This translates to approximately $6.6B of value destruction from buybacks at the top. Retained earnings are negative (-$727M at FY2025 end). Share buybacks have been paused.

The dividend continues at approximately $1.63 annualized, yielding 3.7%. At 9-month FY2026 OCF of $1.2B versus dividend payments of ~$1.2B annualized, coverage is thin but sustainable given the $8.1B cash position.

Nike has $16.2B in contractual endorsement obligations and $7.9B in purchase commitments — massive fixed costs that compress profitability in a declining revenue environment.

### Capital Efficiency

ROIC declined from 34.9% (FY2024) to 20.2% (FY2025) and is likely tracking below 15% in FY2026 based on Q3 results. The Altman Z-Score of 2.42 places Nike in the "grey zone," reflecting compressed earnings and elevated leverage rather than genuine bankruptcy risk, but it signals meaningful financial stress for a company of this caliber.

## Growth Analysis

Nike's growth profile is negative across nearly every vector. Consensus estimates tell the deterioration story:

- **Current quarter (Q4 FY2026) EPS**: $0.11, down from $0.22 estimate 30 days ago (47% cut)
- **Next quarter EPS**: $0.45, down from $0.63 (28% cut)
- **Current year (FY2026) EPS**: $1.50, down from $1.57 (5% cut)
- **Next year (FY2027) EPS**: $1.86, down from $2.31 (19% cut)

These are accelerating negative revisions. The 30-day revisions are as severe as or worse than the 90-day revisions across every period, indicating deteriorating analyst visibility into the turnaround timeline.

The bull case for growth rests on three pillars, each with material qualification:

1. **North America wholesale recovery** (+11% in Q3) is genuine and the strongest single growth datapoint. However, it is partially a comparison effect against a depressed prior-year base and requires sustained expansion.

2. **New product innovation** — management has discussed accelerated product pipelines, but no specific revenue contribution from new franchises is visible in the financial data yet. The deliberate pullback of legacy franchises (AF1, Dunk, Jordan retros) creates a revenue gap that new products must fill.

3. **DTC margin improvement** — once the shift to full-price is complete, NIKE Direct margins should improve. But comparable store sales are negative across every geography, and digital traffic continues to decline, suggesting the transition is destroying volume faster than it is building pricing power.

Against these, macro conditions are hostile. Nike's average selling points ($100+ for footwear) place it firmly in the discretionary zone affected by consumer spending pressure.

## Competitive Landscape

Nike faces its most competitive athletic footwear/apparel market in decades. On Running, Hoka (Deckers Outdoor), Adidas, and New Balance are all gaining share in segments where Nike is retreating. Adidas is explicitly targeting larger U.S. market share during Nike's transition. On Running has established a premium running position that overlaps Nike's core market.

Nike's competitive advantages remain substantial: the brand remains the most recognized in athletic wear globally, the endorsement portfolio locks in athlete partnerships, and the wholesale distribution network gives Nike shelf space that competitors cannot match at scale. The company's innovation pipeline (Air Max Dn, Pegasus Premium, Vomero) provides differentiation.

However, the trajectory is negative. Management's deliberate supply reduction in lifestyle categories creates whitespace that competitors fill. Greater China's decline reflects both macro weakness and competitive share loss to local brands like Anta and Li-Ning. Converse has effectively lost competitive relevance.

The wholesale recovery in North America is a genuine competitive reversal — retailers are allocating more shelf space to Nike as the brand increases allocation to wholesale. But this comes at the expense of Nike Direct, making the net competitive impact ambiguous.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**Tariff exposure**: Nike paid approximately $1.0B in IEEPA tariffs. With 47% of footwear manufactured in Vietnam (subject to significant tariff rates), continued trade policy uncertainty creates ongoing cost headwinds.

**Greater China**: Management explicitly guided negative impacts through fiscal 2027. A 10% currency-neutral decline with 21% digital contraction suggests structural share loss rather than cyclical softness.

**Converse**: Tracking toward $1.2B annualized revenue (down from $2.4B in FY2023) with negative EBIT. Management has stated Converse is not for sale. This is a capital and management attention drag.

**Consumer macro environment**: Discretionary consumer spending faces headwinds that create a structurally adverse demand environment for $100+ athletic footwear and apparel.

**Accelerating negative estimate revisions**: When consensus is falling at this pace, the risk of further downside surprises is elevated.

**$16.2B endorsement obligations**: In a declining revenue environment, the endorsement-to-revenue ratio rises mechanically, compressing profitability regardless of operational improvements.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV at 39.3% is essentially at parity with 1-year historical vol (39.0%), indicating fair pricing. The term structure is in contango (near-term 39.3% < 6-month 44.9%), suggesting the market expects elevated uncertainty around the FY2026 earnings report and tariff developments. Put/call OI ratio of 1.13 shows modest put positioning — not extreme but directionally bearish. Unusual activity in near-term $44 puts (3-4x volume/OI) suggests hedging around current levels.

ATM IV collapsed from 62.3% to 39.3% over the past month while put/call OI ratio fell from 4.20 to 1.13, meaning the intense hedging activity around the April 1 earnings release has largely unwound. This is consistent with the stock stabilizing in the $42-46 range over the past month.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$60-65, +35-47%)
North America wholesale recovery accelerates and expands internationally. New product franchises gain traction, filling the gap left by legacy pullbacks. Greater China stabilizes. The $1B tariff refund materializes. Gross margin recovery begins in FY2027 as tariff headwinds moderate and full-price selling takes hold. FY2028 EPS reaches $2.50+. At 25x, the stock reaches $62. Insider buying proves prescient. The 3.7% yield provides downside support during the recovery.

### Bear Case (~$28-32, -28-37%)
Consumer spending contraction deepens. Greater China deterioration accelerates. Tariff costs persist and refund does not materialize. New product launches fail to generate sufficient demand to offset legacy franchise pullbacks. Converse continues to bleed cash. FY2027 EPS comes in below $1.50 as estimates continue falling. Competitive share losses to On Running, Hoka, and Adidas prove permanent. The dividend is cut to preserve cash. At 20x $1.50, the stock falls to $30.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The Q4 FY2026 earnings report (expected late June 2026) presents a specific catalyst. The tariff impact on the full quarter, plus guided Greater China deterioration, creates risk of either a miss or devastating forward guidance. Management's characterization of tariff refund prospects could also move the stock.

**Timing window**: 6-8 weeks to Q4 FY2026 earnings. The stock has consolidated in the $42-46 range for a month, but estimates continue to deteriorate, which historically leads to further price compression.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest at 5.9% of float with 2.1 days to cover is manageable. Institutional ownership is high but the stock has been declining for over a year, reducing squeeze risk from forced covering.

**Borrow and options signal**: Options IV is fair (39.3% vs 39.0% HV), indicating no elevated borrow cost reflected in options. The unwinding of put/call OI from 4.20 to 1.13 means the post-earnings hedging wave has passed, leaving room for put positioning to rebuild if fundamentals deteriorate further.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Base case downside to $32-35 on continued estimate cuts (20x $1.50-$1.75). Upside risk to $50-52 on tariff relief or better-than-expected earnings. At current $44.41, this implies roughly $10-12 of downside vs $6-8 of upside — a reward:risk ratio of approximately 1.4:1.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The reward:risk ratio does not meet the 2:1 threshold. The 3.7% dividend yield creates carrying cost for shorts. The insider buying pattern — four insiders buying at $42-43 in April — introduces meaningful bounce risk. The stock is one to avoid owning rather than actively short. The thesis is a deteriorating turnaround in a hostile macro environment, a directional view without sufficiently asymmetric payoff at current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: Upgrade to HOLD if two of the following occur: (1) Greater China revenue stabilizes (flat or positive currency-neutral for two consecutive quarters); (2) FY2028 consensus EPS revisions stabilize or turn positive; (3) the stock reaches $32-35 (20-22x FY2028 consensus EPS), where the turnaround is not priced in. Upgrade to BUY if all three conditions are met simultaneously or if tariff refund materializes ($1B+ recovery) and gross margin trajectory reverses to expansion.

**Timeline**: The turnaround requires at least 12-18 months of evidence before the risk/reward profile shifts favorably. The next natural assessment points are Q4 FY2026 earnings (late June 2026) and Q1 FY2027 earnings (late September 2026). By Q1 FY2027, the company will be through a full year of the Hill turnaround and tariff/trade policy should have greater clarity.

## Conclusion

Nike possesses one of the most valuable consumer brands in the world, a credible turnaround CEO, and the most bullish insider buying pattern observable in the large-cap consumer discretionary universe. These are real positives. However, the financial trajectory is deteriorating: revenue declining, margins compressing, estimates falling at accelerating rates, and tariff headwinds creating structural demand and cost pressures. At ~29x trailing earnings, the stock requires recovery that has not yet materialized in the aggregate financial data. North America wholesale at +11% is encouraging but insufficient to offset weakness in Greater China, Converse, declining DTC, and tariff-driven margin compression. The prior AVOID recommendation from the April 1 assessment was appropriate, and subsequent developments reinforce it. The insider buying warrants close monitoring and will be a key input for any future upgrade, but conviction follows earnings momentum, and earnings momentum here is unambiguously negative.
