## Executive Summary

ASE Technology Holding is the world's largest outsourced semiconductor assembly and test provider, and it is in the middle of the strongest operating inflection in its history. First-half 2026 revenue of NT$364.7bn rose 22.0% year over year, while operating income of NT$38.7bn rose 94.6% and the operating margin expanded 396bp to 10.60%. The Assembly, Testing and Materials (ATM) segment did the work: Q2 ATM revenue of NT$126.1bn grew 36.3% year over year with a 15.7% operating margin versus 9.5% a year earlier, and testing revenue grew 42% — faster than packaging's 35%. Management guided Q3 consolidated revenue up 21–22% sequentially with ATM gross margin of 28–29%, and raised full-year growth for the *non-LEAP*, conventional ATM business from +13% to +20%. Two cycles appear to be turning at once: AI-driven advanced packaging and the mature automotive/industrial/consumer packaging and test base.

The problem is what has been paid for that inflection and what is being spent to sustain it. At $38.24 the ADS trades at roughly 44.9x my corrected trailing EPS of US$0.851, 4.1x EV/sales, 41.5x trailing EV/EBIT, and approximately 6.5x a back-solved book value — against year-end multiples of 6.96x (2022) and 17.96x (2023) for the same company. On my own FY2026 earnings build of US$1.21–1.30 per ADS the forward multiple is 29–32x. Simultaneously, ASE raised its 2026 capital budget twice this year to a record US$10.5bn from an original US$8.5bn, is building 13 new sites and repurposing 8 factories, and has produced negative free cash flow in four of the last five quarters (TTM FCF −NT$39.9bn). Combined FY2025–FY2026 capex of roughly NT$495bn implies, on 5–7 year equipment lives, something in the range of NT$70–99bn of incremental annual depreciation — comparable to the entire current TTM operating income of NT$69.6bn. The 2027–28 margin outcome therefore depends almost entirely on whether new capacity is loaded at rates near today's 80–85% blended utilization.

The market's own reading is informative: the ADS fell about 10% on the day of a materially better-than-expected Q2 print accompanied by the capex raise. Investors are not disputing the demand narrative; they are disputing the return on the incremental capital and the multiple. My probability-weighted expected return from $38.24 is only modestly positive with wide, roughly symmetric dispersion. Combined with unusually thin disclosure — no 10-K, no Form 4s, no proxy, no readable 20-F text, no customer concentration data, no debt maturity schedule — the appropriate rating is **HOLD**.

## Company Overview

ASE Technology Holding is the Taiwan-domiciled holding company for Advanced Semiconductor Engineering and Siliconware Precision Industries (SPIL) on the packaging and test side, plus Universal Scientific Industrial (USI) in electronics manufacturing services. It reports Packaging, Testing, EMS, and Other; Packaging + Testing + Materials are grouped as ATM.

FY2025 segment revenue (NT$mn): Packaging 308,343 (+17.8%), Testing 71,900 (+31.8%), EMS 257,193 (−5.2%), Other 7,952. ATM was ~60% of FY2025 revenue; by Q2 2026 it was 66% (NT$126,148mn of NT$191,064mn).

The business model is contract service provision. Fabless designers and IDMs own the silicon and pay ASE to bump, package, and test their die; ASE owns the equipment and the buildings and bears the capital cost. In EMS, USI builds modules and boards for OEM brand owners. ASE is a supplier to chip and device companies, not a co-owner of their products — a distinction that matters when assessing pricing power.

Q2 2026 ATM service mix: bumping/flip-chip/wafer-level/system-in-package 49%, wire bonding 24%, testing 19%, other services 6%, materials 2%. End-application mix: Computing 30% (from 24%), Communications 41% (from 46%), Automotive/consumer/other 29%.

Management's leading-edge portfolio is branded LEAP. Original 2026 guidance was for LEAP revenue above US$3.5bn (+10%); as of the July 30 call, management said LEAP is tracking ahead of that, and the CFO stated an aim to double LEAP revenue in 2027.

**Disclosure regime.** As a foreign private issuer ASE files Form 20-F annually and Form 6-K interim. There are no 10-Ks, 10-Qs, 8-Ks, DEF 14As, or Section 16 filings. The FY2025 20-F (filed April 1, 2026) could not be retrieved in text form for this review, so risk factors, business detail, governance, major shareholders, and financial statement notes are unread. Standard financial-health screens (Altman Z, Piotroski F), XBRL structured financials, and insider transaction records returned nothing for this issuer. This is an information disadvantage that should be priced, and it is one reason the analysis below leans on reconstructed arithmetic.

Management did cite "a more favorable New Taiwan dollar environment" as a gross margin tailwind.

**Segment profitability.** Q2 disclosed margins: ATM operating margin 15.7%; EMS gross margin 8.9% and operating margin 2.4% (down from 3.0% in Q1 on mix and component costs). Applying those to segment revenue: ATM operating income ≈ NT$19.8bn, EMS ≈ NT$1.6bn, summing to NT$21.4bn against consolidated NT$21.1bn. Approximately 93% of consolidated operating profit comes from 66% of revenue. ASE should be valued as an ATM business with a low-margin manufacturing services appendage, and consolidated revenue growth is a poor proxy for earnings growth in either direction.

H1 2026 cash capex of NT$125.8bn (~US$4.0bn) against a US$10.5bn budget implies roughly US$6.5bn in the second half — a steep acceleration, and one reason to treat the budget figure as a commitment basis that may not equal in-year cash outflow.

**Balance sheet.** Cash plus current financial assets rose to NT$107.4bn at 6/30/26. A roughly NT$500bn two-year capital program funded approximately half from operating cash flow and half from incremental debt, in a business that lost 13.3% of revenue in a single year as recently as 2023, is the central financial risk.

## Growth Analysis

The demand evidence is well corroborated and broad. Advanced packaging is the physical constraint on AI accelerator supply — TSMC's CoWoS capacity has been publicly identified as the bottleneck on competing accelerator ramps, and Taiwan foundry revenue grew 54% year over year in June 2026. ASE's own blended ATM utilization of 80–85% with wire bonding, wafer sort, and final test described as tight is consistent with an industry-wide constraint rather than a company-specific story.

Q3 2026 guidance: consolidated revenue +21% to +22% sequentially (NT$231–233bn), gross margin 20.5–21.5%, operating margin 11.5–12.5%, ATM revenue +11% to +13% with ATM gross margin 28–29%, EMS revenue approximately +40% with 3.2–3.4% operating margin.

Two guidance revisions matter more than the LEAP headline. First, non-LEAP ATM growth was raised to +20% from +13%, which implies the conventional automotive, industrial, consumer, and wire-bond cycle is also recovering. Second, testing grew 42% year over year — faster than packaging — with testing revenue moving from NT$54.6bn (FY24) to NT$71.9bn (FY25) to an annualized ~NT$95bn run rate in Q2'26. Test intensity rises with device complexity, test capacity is constrained, and foundries have largely left test to OSATs. This is the cleanest structural growth line in the company.

The Q3 EMS guide of approximately +40% sequential is at the top of the historical seasonal range (prior Q2→Q3: +37% in 2022, +17.5% 2023, +19% 2024, +17% 2025) and would be a record EMS quarter. At a 3.2–3.4% operating margin it adds only ~NT$1.4–1.5bn of operating profit, roughly 5% of the quarter — high revenue optics, low profit consequence.

**On estimate revisions.** Coverage is one analyst, which caps how much weight the revision data can carry. Current-year consensus of $1.12 is up 3.4% over 90 days; next-year $1.84 is up 16.1% over 90 days but down 7.6% over 30 days. The headline "estimates broadly falling" reads as an artifact of a single-estimate base: the current-year figure of $1.12 sits below both the annualized Q2 run rate ($1.16) and my own FY2026 build ($1.21–1.30), which suggests staleness rather than a genuine earnings ceiling. The threshold pattern that would constitute a strong forward buy signal — 90-day revisions above +30% on a stock at a moderate forward multiple — is not present here. The reported earnings surprise record (four consecutive beats, surprises of 19.1%, 9.0%, 14.8%, 16.3%, on what appears to be a local-currency per-common-share basis) does support confidence in the direction of estimates.

My FY2026 construction: Q3 revenue NT$231–233bn at the guided margin gives NT$27–29bn of operating income; a flat-to-modestly-up Q4 puts FY2026 revenue near NT$830–845bn (+29–31%) and operating income near NT$95–100bn (~11.5% margin). Applying H1's realized 91% net-to-operating conversion gives net income of NT$86–91bn and EPS of NT$38–41 per ADS (US$1.21–1.30). That conversion embeds the non-operating income discussed above, which is the main fragility in the build.

| P/E on my FY2026E ($1.21–1.30) | 29.4–31.6x |
| P/E on single-analyst FY2027 ($1.84) | 20.8x |
| P/B on back-solved equity | ~6.5x |
| Dividend yield | 0.80% |
| FCF yield (TTM) | negative |

Two anchors matter. The first is history: the same company ended 2022 at 6.96x and 2023 at 17.96x earnings. The re-rating to a growth multiple is complete, and the current price embeds a permanent narrowing of the Taiwan-domicile and cyclicality discounts. The second is the 6.5x book multiple, which is extreme for a capital-intensive contract manufacturer whose returns depend on utilization of a fixed-asset base it is doubling. That figure rests on a back-solved equity number and should be treated as approximate, but no reasonable equity figure produces a modest P/B.

The forward case is not unreasonable on its own terms. If LEAP roughly doubles in 2027 from above US$3.5bn — adding perhaps NT$110bn of revenue at incremental ATM margins — and non-LEAP ATM grows 20%, 2027 operating income of NT$130–150bn is defensible even absorbing part of the depreciation step-up, implying EPS of roughly US$1.55–1.77 per ADS. At 22x the midpoint that is about $36.5; at 25x, $41.5. The single-analyst target of $48 (the only target in the data, so low, high, and mean are identical) requires roughly 26x on the upper end of that range.

Against that, the depreciation bridge is the item not visible in any trailing multiple. FY2025 capex of NT$164.6bn plus roughly NT$330bn in 2026 is approximately NT$495bn over two years. On 5–7 year average lives for the equipment-weighted portion, incremental annual depreciation lands in the NT$70–99bn range — comparable to the entire TTM operating income of NT$69.6bn. This is a sensitivity built from disclosed capex and assumed lives, not a company figure; buildings carry 20–40 year lives, roughly US$1bn of the recent raise was facilities, and assets placed in service late in 2026 contribute little 2026 depreciation. Directionally, though, it establishes that the 2027–28 earnings outcome is a utilization question rather than a demand question.

Valuation therefore scores below average. There is no identifiable mispricing to exploit at 29–32x forward on a business with negative free cash flow, a doubling asset base, an unresolved tariff overhang, and a third of the recent pretax improvement sitting below the operating line. Prior appreciation is not the argument — the argument is that current price already discounts successful execution of the entire capital program.

## Competitive Landscape

ASE is the largest OSAT by scale and the only one with the full ATM stack plus interconnect materials and an EMS arm. ATM operating margin expanding from 9.5% to 15.7% in four quarters is evidence of pricing and mix power in a constrained market.

The most consequential competitive datapoint is negative. On June 16, 2026, TSMC and Amkor announced a 10-year partnership to expand advanced packaging and test capacity in the United States. TSMC — the foundry that owns CoWoS and anchors the highest-value 2.5D/3D pool — selected ASE's closest scale competitor as its long-term US partner. Two implications follow. The leading-edge value pool for the largest AI accelerators is substantially captured by the foundry itself, with OSATs serving as complementary, overflow, or geographically mandated capacity; LEAP revenue above US$3.5bn is large in absolute terms (~13% of my FY2026 revenue estimate) but represents participation in a pool anchored elsewhere. And on US onshoring specifically — strategically valuable given the unresolved Section 232 semiconductor measures — ASE now looks disadvantaged relative to Amkor for TSMC-adjacent work. The geographic distribution of ASE's 13 new sites was not itemized in available disclosure.

Where the position looks strongest is breadth across the AI supply chain outside the accelerator die: storage, connectivity, power components, and above all test. Testing is capacity-constrained, grew 42% year over year, and is a business foundries have not vertically integrated. That, plus the raised non-LEAP guidance, supports a two-cycle rather than one-cycle read.

What cannot be substantiated from available disclosure: proprietary technology moats, switching costs, or long-term take-or-pay contracts. Those claims would require the 20-F business section. Treat ASE's moat as scale-and-breadth based, with the highest-margin tier of advanced packaging contested by a customer that is also a competitor.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Capex commitment against a cyclical demand base.** US$10.5bn budgeted for 2026, raised twice in one year, 13 new sites, 8 factory conversions, deployed ahead of the revenue it serves. The 2023 precedent (revenue −13.3%, gross margin −430bp) on a far smaller asset base defines the downside mechanism: reverse operating leverage amplified by roughly double the fixed assets. The ADS fell about 10% on the day of the beat-and-raise, which is the market pricing return-on-capital risk rather than celebrating growth.

**2. Customer concentration, unquantified.** The application mix shift toward Computing (30% of ATM from 24%) says nothing about customer diversification. One tier-3 secondary source describes SPIL as a major packaging supplier for Nvidia's AI chips; that is unconfirmed in any filing or company statement, and describes a supplier relationship, not co-development. No top-5 or top-10 customer percentages are available. Concentration is neither confirmed nor refuted, and that ambiguity is itself a risk at a 45x trailing multiple.

**3. Section 232 semiconductor tariffs.** Taiwan secured preferential treatment for most exports *other than* semiconductors, with proposed semiconductor measures unresolved. ASE's assembly and test footprint is concentrated in Taiwan, China, Korea, and Malaysia. A US tariff regime touching packaged devices would hit both ASE's cost position and customers' sourcing decisions. The company itself flags "the recent shift in United States trade policies" in its own enumerated risk list.

**4. Cross-Strait geopolitical tail.** ASE names the ROC-PRC relationship as a filed risk factor. It is unmitigable at the company level and is part of why Taiwan OSATs have historically traded at single-digit to mid-teens multiples. The current multiple assumes that discount has permanently narrowed.

**5. EMS drag and seasonality.** Approximately 34% of revenue at a 2.4% operating margin, declining 5.2% in FY2025, consuming working capital and contributing ~7% of operating profit, with consumer-electronics seasonality and component cost pass-through unrelated to the AI thesis.

**6. FX reversal.** Implied USD/TWD was 31.58 in Q2'26 versus 31.16 in Q2'25, and 31.45 in June 2026 versus 29.81 in June 2025 — roughly 5.5% NT$ depreciation on the monthly comparison. Revenue is largely USD-denominated and costs largely NT$-denominated, so this is a genuine margin tailwind that reverses if the NT$ appreciates. The 2025 within-quarter swing shows how fast that can move.

**7. Unquantified environmental liabilities.** The company's own risk language references the ability "to resolve environmental liabilities," implying existing unresolved items. Legal proceedings and the environmental note were not readable.

**8. Disclosure and governance visibility.** No proxy, no compensation table, no say-on-pay, no insider transaction record. A 6-K/A amendment filed May 8, 2026 is unexplained; amendments to interim reports are not routine, though a corrected exhibit or translation is the common benign explanation. The FY2025 dividend declaration is unverified — relevant because a company entering a NT$330bn capex year with negative free cash flow faces real tension between a historically generous payout (NT$8.80/5.20/5.30 per common share for FY2022/23/24) and funding the build without further leverage.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 71.4% against 54.3% one-year realized is rich by 17 points, with a flat term structure (69.9% at six months) and a ±10.3% implied move on the nearest expiry. The market is pricing sustained, evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a single dated binary — consistent with a capex/utilization debate that resolves over quarters, not with an imminent event. Positioning signals conflict: the put/call volume ratio of 3.28 is heavy, but open interest is call-tilted at 0.40, so the volume skew looks like short-dated hedging layered on a longer-standing bullish book rather than accumulated bearish positioning. Short interest of 0.6% of float and 0.8 days to cover means essentially no organized bearish position exists. For a holder, rich near-term IV makes covered-call overwriting a reasonable way to monetize the elevated premium; for a prospective buyer, it argues against paying up for calls and in favor of waiting for the Q3 print. None of this changes the fundamental read, but it does confirm that no one is being paid to be short and that the debate is about multiple and capital returns rather than solvency.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $54 (+41%)

Advanced packaging and test remain physically constrained through 2027. LEAP doubles from above US$3.5bn to roughly US$7bn as management targets, non-LEAP ATM compounds at 20%, and testing continues growing faster than packaging. The 13 new sites and 8 conversions come online into loaded demand at 85% utilization, so incremental depreciation is absorbed by incremental gross profit rather than eroding margin. ATM gross margin holds at the guided 28–29% or better, EMS stabilizes at 3%+ operating margin on record volume, and the NT$ stays weak. FY2027 EPS reaches roughly US$1.90–1.95 per ADS, and the market awards 28x on the view that OSAT advanced packaging is a structural rather than cyclical franchise. That path is corroborated by four consecutive earnings beats, repeated upward guidance revisions, and industry-wide capacity tightness confirmed by multiple independent participants.

### Bear Case — $23 (−40%)

AI accelerator order momentum decelerates as hyperscaler capex digests, or a Section 232 semiconductor tariff regime disrupts customer sourcing. Utilization on a fixed-asset base roughly twice its prior size falls into the low 70s, and NT$70–99bn of incremental annual depreciation lands on a shrinking revenue line — the 2023 pattern amplified. The NT$4.6bn quarterly non-operating contribution proves transitory, removing roughly a third of the recent pretax improvement. The NT$ appreciates, reversing the gross margin tailwind. Debt, already up 47% in 18 months, keeps rising to fund committed capex into falling cash flow. FY2027 EPS lands near US$1.15–1.50 and the multiple compresses back toward 15–20x as the market re-applies the historical Taiwan-OSAT cyclical discount. Multiple compression alone on flat earnings is worth more than 40% from here.

### Base Case — $40 (+4.6%)

FY2026 delivers roughly as guided: revenue NT$830–845bn, EPS US$1.21–1.30. FY2027 revenue grows another ~18–20% with LEAP roughly doubling, but incremental depreciation absorbs a meaningful share of incremental gross profit, and the non-operating contribution normalizes. FY2027 EPS lands near US$1.72 — below the single-analyst $1.84 — and the market applies 23x, reflecting real structural growth in test and advanced packaging against genuine geopolitical, tariff, and capital-intensity discounts. 23x × $1.72 ≈ $40.

Weighting base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25% gives an expected return of roughly +2.5% over twelve months. That is approximately fair, with dispersion that is wide and close to symmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD. Reassessment point: Q3 2026 results (late October 2026).**

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 print delivers ATM gross margin at or above the guided 28–29% *and* discloses or clarifies the composition of non-operating income as recurring, while operating cash flow covers at least 70% of quarterly capex. That would resolve both the earnings-quality and self-funding questions.
2. A price near $30–31 (roughly 24x my FY2026E EPS of $1.25 and ~17x the single-analyst FY2027 figure) without fundamental deterioration. At that level the base case offers ~30% upside against a bear case that is largely already priced.
3. Company disclosure of the FY2027 depreciation trajectory or asset-life detail showing incremental annual D&A materially below the NT$70bn low end of my sensitivity, or disclosure of long-term capacity reservation or prepayment arrangements with customers that de-risk the build.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Blended ATM utilization falling below 75%, or ATM gross margin dropping below 25%, in any quarter — the first evidence of the reverse-operating-leverage mechanism.
2. A third capex raise, or 2027 capex guided above US$10bn, alongside a fourth consecutive quarter of negative free cash flow and net debt/equity above 0.65.
3. Section 232 semiconductor measures enacted with material coverage of packaged devices assembled in Taiwan, or a customer-driven relocation announcement that implies ASE loses share to US-located competitors.
4. Any quarter in which operating income growth decelerates below revenue growth while the non-operating line contributes more than 25% of pretax income.

**Position management for existing holders.** The rich near-term implied volatility (71.4% versus 54.3% realized) makes covered-call overwriting against existing exposure a sensible way to be paid for the flat-term-structure uncertainty. New capital should wait for either the Q3 print or the $30–31 area.

## Conclusion

ASE Technology is executing well on the strongest demand environment in OSAT history. First-half operating income nearly doubled, ATM operating margin expanded 620bp year over year to 15.7%, testing revenue grew 42%, and management raised guidance for the non-AI base business as well as for LEAP. Four consecutive earnings beats support confidence in near-term estimates, and industry-wide packaging capacity tightness is corroborated by multiple independent sources.

The stock already reflects that. At $38.24 it carries ~45x corrected trailing earnings, ~29–32x my FY2026 build, ~4.1x EV/sales, and roughly 6.5x back-solved book, against historical year-end multiples of 7x and 18x. The company is funding a roughly NT$500bn two-year capital program approximately half from debt, has produced negative free cash flow in four of the last five quarters, and faces NT$70–99bn of prospective incremental annual depreciation against a NT$69.6bn TTM operating income base. About a third of the year-over-year pretax improvement came from below the operating line, and its composition is unverified. TSMC selected Amkor, not ASE, as its 10-year US advanced-packaging partner. Section 232 semiconductor measures remain unresolved, customer concentration is unquantified, and the disclosure regime provides no insider, compensation, or governance signal at all.

The probability-weighted expected return is modestly positive with roughly symmetric 40%-magnitude tails on both sides. That combination — real operating momentum, no margin of safety, and several unresolved verification gaps that bear directly on the 2027–28 earnings power — supports **HOLD**, classified as uncertain rather than as a conviction growth hold. The Q3 print in late October, and specifically the ATM gross margin against the 28–29% guide alongside the capex-to-operating-cash-flow relationship, is the next event that meaningfully changes the answer.
