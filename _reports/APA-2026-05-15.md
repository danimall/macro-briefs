## Executive Summary

APA Corporation is a geographically diversified E&P company with operations in the U.S. Permian Basin, Egypt's Western Desert, and the U.K. North Sea, plus transformational exploration upside in Suriname (GranMorgu, first oil 2028) and Alaska. The stock has rallied 104% from its 52-week low of $16.15 to $37.11 (May 14), driven by oil price strength and four consecutive earnings beats averaging +40% surprise.

The valuation case is straightforward: at ~6.1x current-year consensus EPS of $6.05 (which has been revised up 186% in 90 days), APA trades at roughly half the peer median P/E of 13.0x. However, this discount reflects real structural issues: an $881M-$1.2B GOA decommissioning liability, a terminal North Sea operation, recurring Egypt payment risk, and Waha negative gas pricing that forced production curtailments in Q1 2026. These complexities explain why APA has delivered 100 percentage points less TSR than its peer group over 5 years despite operating in the same commodity environment.

The stock warrants holding for the Suriname catalyst (mid-2028 first oil on a $10.5B project where APA pays only 12.5% of first $10B), continued commodity tailwinds from the current geopolitical environment, and attractive shareholder returns (60% of FCF framework). Upgrading to BUY requires either resolution of the GOA liability or confirmation that Suriname economics are tracking to plan — neither of which is imminent.

## Company Overview

APA Corporation operates through three geographic segments plus frontier exploration:

- **United States (62% of production):** ~288,000 boe/d primarily from the Permian Basin (Midland + Delaware), enhanced by the April 2024 Callon acquisition. Running 5 rigs, down from 7 in 2025, reflecting improved capital efficiency.
- **Egypt (31% of production):** ~144,000 boe/d from the Western Desert, where APA holds 7.5M gross acres as the largest operator. Sinopec holds a 1/3 minority interest.
- **U.K. North Sea (7% of production):** ~32,000 boe/d, in managed terminal decline — APA expects to cease all production before 2030 due to the 38% Energy Profits Levy.
- **Suriname/Alaska:** GranMorgu (220,000 b/d FPSO, first oil 2028) and a successful Alaska exploratory well (2,700 b/d flow rate).

Proved reserves total 1.056 billion boe (74% U.S., 17% Egypt, 7% Suriname), with 71% liquids weighting.

FY2025 delivered $7.23B in oil & gas revenue on 464,000 boe/d production, down from $8.20B in FY2024 due to a 14% decline in average realized oil price ($66.92 vs. $78.08/bbl). Net income of $1.43B represented strong profitability despite weaker prices, aided by $350M in cost savings and Callon integration synergies.

Q1 2026 demonstrated the oil price inflection: net income of $446M ($1.26/share, beating estimates of $1.13 by 22%) on U.S. oil of $72.50/bbl and international oil of $85.70/bbl. With current-quarter consensus at $1.97, Q2 2026 implies substantial further earnings acceleration.

**Cash Flow & Capital Efficiency:**

- CapEx: $2.7B, yielding ~$1.85B of free cash flow
- FCF yield on current market cap ($13.6B): ~14% annualized at FY2025 levels
- ROE: 45.8% (FY2025) — exceptional capital efficiency

**Balance Sheet:**

Debt reduction of $1.6B during 2025 (from $6.0B to $4.5B) materially improved the capital structure. Net debt (total debt minus cash: $4.49B - $0.52B = ~$3.97B) represents meaningful deleveraging. The company has $4.0B in undrawn revolver capacity. The Altman Z-Score of 9.39 confirms zero near-term solvency risk. However, the current ratio of 0.82 and the retained earnings deficit of -$721M (legacy impairments) are mild negatives.

**Shareholder Returns:**

$640M returned in FY2025 ($360M dividends + $280M buybacks) at a framework target of 60% of FCF. 98.2M cumulative shares repurchased since Q4 2021 at an average 2025 price of $21.73 — significantly below the current price, indicating disciplined execution.

**Constraints on growth thesis:**

## Competitive Landscape

**Permian Position:** APA holds ~623,000 gross acres post-Callon (406K Midland + 217K Delaware) with 100% development well success rate and declining per-unit costs (LOE fell 13% to $8.86/boe in 2025). However, APA's Permian position is smaller and less concentrated than pure-plays like Diamondback (FANG) or Devon (DVN), which trade at premium multiples due to operational simplicity.

**Differentiation:** The geographic diversification that defines APA is both an advantage (uncorrelated assets, Egypt fiscal terms, Suriname optionality) and a liability (management complexity, disclosure opacity, higher G&A). The market has consistently penalized this complexity relative to cleaner Permian-focused operators.

**Suriname as competitive advantage:** The carry structure with TotalEnergies (87.5% of first $10B funded by TotalEnergies) provides access to a world-class deepwater development with minimal capital burden. Few E&Ps of APA's size have access to a 220,000 b/d greenfield project with this economic structure. But the value is contingent on successful execution over 2+ years.

## Risk Assessment

**1. GOA Decommissioning ($881M-$1.2B):** The most material idiosyncratic risk. Cash outlays will be spread over multiple years but are volatile (estimates swung from +$273M cost in 2024 to -$60M gain in 2025). The total could increase if barge rates or regulatory requirements intensify.

**2. Commodity Price Sensitivity (Asymmetric):** Oil below $80 would compress earnings toward $3/share or less. The commodity environment is currently supportive, but any diplomatic resolution or demand weakening could rapidly reprice APA given its beta to crude.

**3. Waha Gas Pricing (Structural):** Negative $0.35/Mcf in Q1 2026 forced 88 MMcf/d curtailment. APA holds 750K MMBtu/d of firm transport and has hedged ~1/3 via basis swaps. New pipeline capacity should gradually alleviate this, but near-term episodes will recur.

**4. Egypt Payment & Sovereign Risk:** Government-facilitated payments "nearly eliminated past due receivables" in Q3 2025. This has resolved before and recurred — it's a chronic condition. Egypt contributes 36% of oil & gas revenue.

**5. North Sea Wind-Down (Known, Manageable):** Terminal decline by 2030 removes ~7% of production and creates ongoing decommissioning cash demands.

**6. Management Turnover:** Five officer changes in 2025 suggest significant organizational restructuring. Post-Callon integration appears on track ($350M of $450M savings target achieved), but stability is not yet confirmed.

## Conclusion

APA offers genuine commodity leverage and an underappreciated Suriname growth option at a deep discount to Permian peers. The ~14% FCF yield is attractive. However, the market's persistent discount reflects quantifiable risks that have cost shareholders 100pp of relative TSR over 5 years. The current oil price environment supports earnings well above consensus, but APA's idiosyncratic liabilities prevent confident upgrading to BUY at $37-38. The correct approach is to hold for the Suriname catalyst timeline and revisit on any pullback to the $30-32 range where risk/reward becomes more asymmetric.

The score trajectory showing declining overall scores (5.7 → 5.6 → 5.5) reflected valuation deterioration as the stock rallied without fundamental clarity improvements.
