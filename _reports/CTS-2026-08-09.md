## Executive Summary

CTS Corporation is a designed-in components manufacturer — sensors, connectivity products and actuators — that has spent three years shifting revenue mix away from a shrinking transportation base toward medical, aerospace & defense and industrial customers. The 2026 operating results validate that effort. Q2 2026 revenue of $144.8M grew 7.0% year over year, GAAP gross margin reached 41.5% (+270bps), adjusted EBITDA margin hit 25.4%, and adjusted diluted EPS of $0.74 beat consensus by roughly 21%. Diversified end markets are now 59% of revenue and grew 15% year over year, with medical up 45% in the quarter on a 1.21 book-to-bill and no acquisition contribution. Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $565-585M and adjusted EPS to $2.55-$2.70. Trailing free cash flow of $91.3M converts at roughly 130% of adjusted net earnings, and the balance sheet carries $52.5M of net cash with 19x interest coverage.

Three things temper the enthusiasm at $66.23. First, the quality of the 2026 step-up is partly non-operational: management attributes roughly $961K of Q2 gross margin benefit to FX (about 66bps of the 270bps expansion), and states that FX plus customer reimbursements added $0.07 to the $0.74 adjusted EPS — 9.5% of the print. The R&D line fell to 3.3% of sales from 4.7% specifically because of a $1,634K one-time customer reimbursement. Second, the company's own guidance implies material deceleration: the midpoint requires H2 revenue of $291.0M (+3.9% versus H2 2025) and H2 adjusted EPS of $1.275 (+4.5%), against +8.8% and +33.7% in 1H. Third, the effective tax rate has moved from 18.4% (1H25) to 24.1% (1H26) with Q2 at 27.0%, driven by valuation allowances against tax credits — a structural drag worth roughly $0.12 per share at guided pretax income, not a timing item.

The valuation has absorbed the good news. CTS repurchased 1.35M shares in FY2025 at an average of ~$42.05, roughly 19x adjusted EPS of $2.23. At $66.23 the stock is 25.2x the guided FY2026 midpoint of $2.625, 22.2x the FY2027 estimate of $2.98, 13.6x EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA and 20.1x EV/TTM free cash flow, for a 4.8% FCF yield. The single available sell-side target sits at $65.00 — below spot. My base case of $71 offers about 7% upside against a bear case near $48 (-28%), which is a HOLD, not an accumulation setup. The prior trajectory (6.5/BUY in May → 6.0/HOLD in June) declines again to 5.7, driven not by the flat price but by three specific new disclosures: the H2 guidance decomposition, the composition of the 1H margin gain, and the June insider and buyback record.

## Company Overview

CTS was incorporated in Indiana in 1929, traces its origins to 1896, and is headquartered in Lisle, Illinois. It organizes products under a "Sense, Connect and Move" taxonomy: sensing inputs to electronic systems, connectivity components for system synchronization, and actuators for controlled mechanical motion. Customers are OEMs, tier-one suppliers, distributors and the U.S. Government, served from manufacturing sites in North America, Asia and Europe.

An important structural limitation: CTS reports **one reportable segment**. The CEO, as chief operating decision maker, evaluates the business on consolidated net earnings and, per the Q2 10-Q, "does not use discrete balance sheet information in assessing performance and allocating resources." For goodwill testing the company maintains **three reporting units**. Investors therefore receive end-market revenue disaggregation but no segment profitability — which makes it impossible to assess whether the 2024 SyQwest defense acquisition earns its cost of capital.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and margin: the inflection is real but recent

Trailing-twelve-month revenue through Q2 2026 is $564.2M ($541.3M FY25 − $261.1M 1H25 + $284.0M 1H26), a five-year CAGR of roughly 2.7%. Revenue troughed in 2024 and is recovering, but the multi-year growth rate is low single digits.

The margin story requires decomposition. GAAP operating margin improved from 13.6% (2023) to 15.3% (2025) to 18.5% (Q2 2026). On the company's own adjusted basis, which strips restructuring, environmental and acquisition items, the series is flatter: 15.7% (2023) → 15.5% (2024) → 16.0% (2025) → 17.7% (1H26) → 18.9% (Q2 26). Most of the 2023-2025 GAAP improvement came from restructuring charges rolling off ($7.1M → $4.7M → $1.4M) as the Juarez-to-Matamoros consolidation completed. The genuine operating step-up began in 2026.

Within 2026, roughly a quarter of the 270bp Q2 gross margin expansion was FX ($961K on $144.78M of sales ≈ 66bps), and R&D dropped 140bps as a percentage of sales because of a $1,634K one-time customer reimbursement. Excluding FX and the reimbursement, adjusted EPS would have been approximately $0.67 versus $0.57 — still ~17% growth, but well below the reported 30%.

### The buyback-versus-earnings record

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Q2'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adjusted net earnings | $70.0M | $65.3M | $66.3M | $75.0M |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $2.22 | $2.12 | $2.23 | $2.57 |
| Diluted WASO | 31,579K | 30,717K | 29,806K | ~28,951K (1H26) |

Adjusted net earnings *fell 5.3%* from 2023 to 2025 while adjusted EPS *rose 0.5%*. The entire per-share result over that window came from a 5.6% reduction in the share count. Only in 2026 does earnings growth take over: TTM adjusted net earnings of $75.0M is 13% above FY2025. That is a genuine change, and it is the core of the bull case — but it is two quarters old, and the guidance implies it flattens in H2.

### Cash flow and balance sheet: the strongest elements

Operating cash flow: $88.8M (2023) → $98.2M (2024) → $102.1M (2025) → $50.7M (1H26). Free cash flow (OCF less capex): $74.1M → $79.6M → $86.4M, a 16.0% FCF margin in FY2025. TTM FCF is $91.3M. Conversion is high because $34.5M of D&A — including ~$16M/year of non-cash acquired intangible amortization on $153.6M of intangibles — runs against only $15.7M of capex. As a sensitivity rather than a forecast: annualizing 1H26 FCF gives $82.3M, lower than TTM because 1H absorbed a $10.5M working-capital outflow ahead of second-half shipments; FY2025 split $36.2M/$50.2M, so the business is second-half weighted on cash and TTM is the better anchor.

At 6/30/26: cash $107.5M, long-term debt $55.0M drawn on a $300M revolver maturing November 2030, net cash $52.5M. Current ratio 2.61x, debt/equity 0.10x, FY2025 interest coverage 19.2x, gross debt/EBITDA 0.47x. Solvency risk is negligible. Two qualifications matter: $97.6M of the $107.5M cash (91%) is held outside the U.S., which constrains funding of U.S. buybacks and domestic M&A; and goodwill of $208.1M plus intangibles of $144.1M equal 47% of $791.5M of total assets.

**A reconciliation note:** the market-data feed reports trailing and forward P/E both at 27.71 with EPS of $2.19. Those are mutually inconsistent ($66.23/$2.19 = 30.2x). The $2.19 figure corresponds to FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS; the 27.7x multiple implies EPS near $2.39. I use the company's own adjusted series and guidance throughout, which are internally consistent.

## Growth Analysis

**Bookings support the near-term case.** Q2 2026 overall book-to-bill was 1.1. By market: medical 1.21 with bookings +14% YoY; A&D 1.23 with bookings +48%; industrial 1.11; diversified aggregate 1.17. Derived from the disclosed ratios and revenue, diversified orders were roughly $99.9M against $85.5M of diversified revenue. Backing that out of the total ($1.1 × $144.8M = $159.3M of orders) leaves approximately $59.4M of transportation orders against $59.25M of transportation revenue — **an implied transportation book-to-bill of ~1.00.** Transportation orders are replacing revenue, not growing it, on 42-43% of the business.

**Medical is the cleanest organic growth engine:** +45% in Q2 and +36.7% in 1H26, with management citing sustained diagnostics and therapeutic demand and next-generation product development with customers. No medical acquisition occurred in the period.

**Aerospace & defense is structurally attractive but lumpy and partly acquired.** A&D grew from $51.3M (2023) to $82.8M (2025), +61% headline — but SyQwest contributed $22.3M of 2025 revenue, implying organic A&D growth of roughly $9.2M, or about an 8.6% CAGR. Q2 2026 A&D revenue *fell 15%* on award timing even as bookings rose 48%.

**The concentration improvement is genuine but partly subtraction.** Toyota plus Cummins fell from ~$151.4M (2023) to ~$106.1M (2025) while total revenue fell only $9.1M, meaning all other revenue grew ~9.1% over two years (~4.4% CAGR). In 1H26 Toyota fell to ~$23.6M from ~$31.3M while total revenue rose 8.8%, so other transportation customers grew roughly $7.9M — real within-segment diversification. Cummins revenue declined an estimated 45% from 2023 to 2025, and management's FY2026 assumption that commercial vehicle volumes partially offset flat-to-down light vehicle production is effectively a recovery call on the account that has been the largest drag.

**Guidance implies deceleration.** At the $575M/$2.625 midpoint, H2 revenue of $291.0M is +3.9% versus H2 2025's $280.2M, and H2 adjusted EPS of $1.275 is +4.5% versus $1.22. At the top of the range ($585M/$2.70) H2 would be +7.4% revenue and +10.7% EPS. This is either conservatism from a new CEO in his first guided period, or an acknowledgment that FX, the customer reimbursement and favorable mix do not repeat. Both readings are defensible; the market appears to be pricing the former.

**Estimate revisions are positive but thin.** Current-year consensus of $2.72 is up 11.9% in 30 days and 12.9% in 90 days; next year $2.98 is up 10.0% in 90 days. Two caveats: the revision table reflects a **single analyst**, and $2.72 sits *above* the top of company guidance ($2.70). Revisions of this magnitude are normally a strong signal, but that signal is calibrated on inflection stocks trading cheaply against rising estimates. Here the stock trades at 22-25x forward, and the earnings base includes one-time items — so I weight the revisions as confirmatory of near-term momentum rather than as evidence of systematic underestimation.

**Enterprise value:** $1,891.2M market cap + $55.0M debt − $107.5M cash = **$1,838.7M**.

|---|---|---|

| EV / TTM adj. EBITDA | $135.4M | 13.6x |
| EV / TTM adj. operating earnings | $98.4M | 18.7x |
| EV / TTM FCF | $91.3M | 20.1x |
| Price / TTM adj. diluted EPS | $2.57 | 25.8x |
| Price / FY26 guided adj. EPS midpoint | $2.625 | 25.2x (range 24.5x-26.0x) |
| Price / FY27 consensus | $2.98 | 22.2x |
| FCF yield on market cap | $91.3M | 4.8% |

**Peer comparison is not usable as an anchor here.** The Electronic Manufacturing Services set shows P/Es of 94.6 (BHE), 163.6 (IPGP), 42.1 (PLXS), 52.3 (SANM) and 101.8 (TTMI), a median of 94.6. Those multiples reflect trough or recovering earnings at contract manufacturers and laser companies with structurally different margin profiles — CTS earns 41.5% gross margins against EMS norms in the 8-12% range. Concluding CTS is "cheap" at 25x against a 94.6x median would be an error of comparability. The more informative benchmark is CTS's own history: the company repurchased 1.35M shares in FY2025 at ~$42.05, roughly 19x that year's $2.23 adjusted EPS. The current 25.2x on guided FY2026 represents roughly a third of multiple expansion on top of the earnings improvement.

**What would justify the current multiple?** A 25x multiple on a components manufacturer requires either sustained double-digit earnings growth or a durable margin step-up. CTS has the second in prospect — the Evolution 2030 PSU targets are explicitly weighted to sales (70%) and gross margin percentage (30%) — but the guided H2 EPS growth of 4.5% and the 2.7% five-year revenue CAGR do not support the first. The rising tax rate works against it: moving from 18.4% to 22% on ~$97M of guided pretax income costs roughly $3.5M of net earnings, or ~$0.12 per share.

**FCF cross-check.** TTM FCF of $91.3M is $3.20 per share. At the current 4.8% yield the stock is fairly valued on trailing cash. If FY2027 FCF reaches $95-100M ($3.33-3.50/share) and the market applies 4.5-5.0%, the range is $67-78. That brackets the base case rather than pointing to material undervaluation.

**Analyst targets.** The single available target is $65.00 high, $65.00 low, $65.00 mean — 1.9% *below* spot. The principle that consensus targets act as floors on inflection stocks applies when a name is cheap against rising estimates; at 25x guided earnings with a target below spot and a one-analyst sample, that framing does not hold. I treat the target as low-information rather than as a ceiling.

**Valuation score: 4.5.** Not a value trap — the business is improving and the balance sheet is clean — but the multiple prices the improvement, the trailing earnings base contains ~$0.07/quarter of non-recurring benefit, and there is no identified mispricing to exploit.

## Competitive Landscape

Three pieces of filing evidence support genuine pricing power. Gross margin of 38.4% (FY2025) and 41.5% (Q2 2026) is high for a component supplier. Acquired customer relationships are amortized over long lives (SyQwest customer lists 15.0 years; maglab 13 years), reflecting management's and the auditor's assessment of relationship durability. And the transportation business operates under long-term agreements aggregating $1.2 billion of expected remaining revenue, economics that exist because re-qualifying a safety-relevant sensor mid-platform is expensive for an OEM. Q2 new business included a large sensor award with a North American OEM, a new EV customer, a defense satellite-communication customer for RF filters, and two new industrial customers.

The offsets are equally clear. Transportation, still 42-43% of revenue, contracted 22% from 2023 to 2025 with book-to-bill at ~1.0. CTS does not quantify market share and its own risk disclosure frames competition generically ("competition from other suppliers," "pricing pressures," "rapid technological change"). I was unable to obtain a verified competitor-by-competitor share analysis, so the competitive assessment rests on margin, contract-duration and intangible-life evidence rather than share data.

Trajectory matters more than level for this dimension. CTS is gaining in medical (organic, high book-to-bill), gaining in A&D bookings, expanding gross margin roughly 300bps over three years, and reducing direct customer concentration in dollar terms. It is losing ground in transportation, where the largest historical accounts have shrunk 25-45%. Net: modestly improving. **Score 6.3**, unchanged from the prior review.

Management flags further M&A — the Q2 deck captions the balance sheet slide "Solid Foundation for Strategic M&A," and $52.5M of net cash plus $243.5M of undrawn revolver provide capacity. Given the SyQwest integration record discussed below, that optionality cuts both ways.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Two rounds of accounting corrections on one acquisition within twelve months.** In Q1 2025 CTS identified SyQwest revenue and COGS errors originating both before and after the July 2024 acquisition, plus FX effects on long-term debt payments, restating FY2024 net earnings down $2,642K (diluted EPS $1.89 → $1.81). In Q4 2025 the company identified *additional* immaterial prior-period errors on the same acquisition, again in revenue and COGS, reducing FY2025 pretax earnings by $893K and adjusting goodwill by $2,194K. Management concluded disclosure controls were effective and Grant Thornton issued an unqualified ICFR opinion with no material weakness. Immaterial corrections at recently acquired private companies are not rare, and both management and the auditor had full facts. The narrower and decision-relevant reading: this is evidence about pre-acquisition diligence quality and post-close control integration, at a company signaling further acquisitions.

**2. Goodwill concentration tied to contracts not yet awarded.** Grant Thornton designated goodwill a critical audit matter, disclosing a quantitative test as of October 1, 2025 on one of three reporting units carrying $48.8M of goodwill, with procedures including corroborating "management's probability evaluation of future contract awards." SyQwest's corrected acquisition goodwill of $46,600K plus the $2,194K Q4 adjustment equals $48,794K — an exact arithmetic match, but goodwill by reporting unit is not disclosed, so identifying the unit as SyQwest is an inference, not a fact. No impairment was recorded, and quantitative testing of a recent acquisition is common policy rather than proof of a thin cushion. Separately, the SyQwest earnout is carried at $3,561K against a $15,000K maximum, and Q2 A&D revenue fell 15% on award timing — consistent with tracking below the stretch case, though earnout caps are routinely set above the acquirer's central expectation.

**3. Rising tax rate with valuation allowances.** U.S. pretax earnings were $(395)K in 2025, $2,677K in 2024 and $(9,265)K in 2023, against $84,166K of non-U.S. pretax earnings in 2025 — even though 56% of sales originate in the U.S. Valuation allowances rose to $6,593K from $5,592K, and both Q1 and Q2 2026 tax rate increases are attributed to allowances against certain tax credits. The effective rate went 18.4% (1H25) → 24.1% (1H26), Q2 at 27.0%, with FY2026 guidance assuming 21-23% ex-discrete. This is a persistent ~$0.12/share drag, not a timing artifact.

**4. Legacy Superfund liability, recorded but not resolved.** Asheville, NC and Mountain View, CA are on the EPA's National Priorities List. Undiscounted reserves rose from $12,192K (12/31/24) to $16,450K (12/31/25) to $16,468K (6/30/26); FY2025 remediation expense was $5,465K versus $1,701K. On March 16, 2026 CTS, other PRPs and the EPA agreed in principle to a $7,610K settlement (CTS share $6,711K), subject to final EPA approval including notice and comment. Superfund liability is joint and several, and approval has not occurred.

**5. Customer and end-market concentration.** Toyota was 11.2% of FY2025 sales and 8.3% in 1H26; Cummins fell to 8.4% from 15.0% in 2023, below the disclosure threshold. Transportation cyclicality remains the dominant revenue exposure, and FY2026 assumes light vehicle production flat to slightly down.

**6. Trade, tariff and input-cost exposure.** Long-lived tangible assets sit largely outside the U.S. (China $21.8M, Mexico $19.1M, Czech Republic $9.6M, Taiwan $4.7M vs. U.S. $30.7M) while 56% of sales originate domestically. Risk factors specifically cite China/Canada/Mexico tariffs, the future of USMCA, and rare earth availability and cost. FY2026 guidance adds precious metals and petroleum-based products to the monitoring list — consistent with the new platinum position carried in PP&E at $4,637K, undepreciated, with physical losses expensed as depletion. Immaterial at 0.6% of assets today, but a new commodity and impairment exposure.

**7. Governance and management alignment (detailed in the next section).** Zero open-market insider purchases in ~18 months, a $8.48M clustered CEO sale three weeks before the succession announcement, a buyback that stopped in June 2026, and a five-year TSR of 27.4% against 136.6% for the company's own selected peer group.

**8. FX dependence in the margin story.** Roughly 66bps of the 270bp Q2 gross margin gain was FX, net of hedges. A reversal removes a visible portion of the 2026 step-up without any operational deterioration.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 45.7% is rich against 35.6% one-year realized vol (a 10.1-point spread), the term structure is in backwardation (45.7% at 11 days versus 34.7% at 158 days), and the nearest expiry prices a ±6.4% move. Skew is call-heavy (-28.9% at the front tenor, flattening to -3.6% and -3.1% further out). Two observations. First, the chain is thin — 59 contracts across four expirations, no reported put/call volume or open-interest ratios — so the signal is low-confidence and the front-month backwardation may be a liquidity artifact rather than a priced event, particularly with the July 28 earnings release already behind. Second, to the extent the positioning is informative, it is call-tilted, which does *not* corroborate a bearish thesis and is consistent with continued momentum interest after the beat-and-raise. Short interest is low at 3.8% of float and 2.4 days to cover, so there is neither crowded bearish positioning nor squeeze fuel. Net read: the options market is paying up for near-term movement with an upward tilt; it does not contradict a HOLD, and it argues against an aggressive short posture.

## Management & Governance

**CEO transition.** On June 25, 2026 CTS announced Pratik Trivedi's promotion to President and CEO effective July 6, 2026, succeeding Kieran O'Sullivan, who remains on the Board as Executive Chair. Trivedi's Form 4 titles trace a rapid elevation: SVP (Feb 2025-Feb 2026) → COO (November 2025) → CEO (July 2026). He received a 14,253-share grant on July 6, bringing holdings to 23,635 shares — modest for a new CEO of a $1.9B company, which limits equity-based alignment near term.

**Insider transactions.** Across roughly eighteen months of Form 4 data, there were **no open-market purchases by any officer or director**. Every acquisition is a grant; every disposition is tax withholding or an open-market sale. Two sales stand out: O'Sullivan sold 130,000 shares over June 1-3, 2026 at $63.31-$66.67 for ~$8.48M gross, reducing holdings 29.7%, approximately three weeks before the June 25 succession announcement; and CFO Ashish Agrawal sold 25,000 shares at $53.00 on February 2, 2026, about a week before the Q4 earnings release. Both are consistent with routine diversification by long-tenured executives holding concentrated positions, and both may have been executed under pre-existing Rule 10b5-1 plans — the Q2 10-Q's Item 5 disclosure addresses only plans adopted, modified or terminated during the quarter and is silent on pre-existing arrangements. I flag the pattern without inferring motive from timing. Judged against the calibration that matters — clustered C-suite selling, majority-of-holdings disposals, or selling alongside deteriorating fundamentals — this is two insiders over four months, at 20-30% of holdings, against *improving* results. That is a caution, not a bearish signal on its own.

**Buyback behavior.** The company repurchased 1,352,313 shares in FY2025 at ~$42.05 (a ~$32M mark-to-market gain on $56.9M deployed — genuinely value-accretive ex post). In 1H26 it bought only 240,439 shares for $12.1M, roughly half the 1H25 pace: 44,000 shares in April at $52.51, 19,530 in May at $58.69, and **zero in June 2026**. $78.3M of the November 2025 $100M authorization remains. The June gap is very plausibly a blackout around material non-public information on the CEO succession rather than a valuation judgment; neither explanation is confirmed. What is confirmed is that the company slowed buying materially as the price rose from a ~$42 average to a ~$50 average.

**Pay versus performance.** CEO total compensation was $4,758,899 in FY2025, up 13.6% from FY2024 and 48.7% from FY2023, against adjusted net earnings that fell 5.3% over that window. The proxy's own pay-versus-performance table shows cumulative TSR on $100 invested at $127.35 for CTS versus $236.57 for its selected peer group at 12/31/2025 — a 109-point five-year shortfall, with the gap widening sharply in 2025. Offsetting positives: the performance measure set is broad and cash-aware (adjusted diluted EPS, sales, three-year sales growth, three-year operating cash flow, adjusted operating earnings, relative TSR, controllable working capital), and the June 2025 "Evolution 2030" PSUs vesting in 2028 and 2030 tie pay explicitly to sales (70%) and gross margin percentage (30%) — a genuinely long-dated construct aligned with the margin expansion now underway. Grant Thornton has audited since 2005, a 21-year tenure worth noting alongside the two rounds of corrections.

**Dilution.** Q2 2026 diluted share count of 28,909K versus 28,580K basic implies ~1.15% current dilution. Maximum contingent issuance from outstanding awards (317,585 service RSUs plus performance RSUs at a 200% maximum ≈ 445,558 units) totals roughly 763,143 units — 2.67% of shares outstanding, or 2.60% post-issuance ownership dilution — subject to service and multi-year sales, cash-flow, margin and relative-TSR conditions; 89,108 PSU units were forfeited in FY2025 alone. Buybacks in 1H26 (240,439 shares) ran roughly five times award issuance (49,357), so net share count continues to fall.

**Management Quality score: 5.0.** Capital allocation execution has been competent (accretive 2025 buybacks, well-structured 2030 refinancing, sensible incentive metrics), but the combination of zero insider buying at $50-67, a large pre-announcement CEO sale, a decelerating buyback, comp growth against flat adjusted earnings, a 109-point TSR shortfall versus self-selected peers, and repeated corrections on a single acquisition places this below average.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $84 (+27%)

The 2026 operating inflection is durable and CTS is early in a mix-driven margin re-rating. Diversified markets at 59% of revenue with a 1.17 book-to-bill, medical growing 36.7% organically on a 1.21 book-to-bill, and A&D bookings up 48% on a 1.23 ratio imply the diversified base can grow low-to-mid teens for several years while transportation stabilizes at ~1.0 book-to-bill. Adjusted operating margin reaches 20%+ as mix shifts and the Matamoros footprint delivers, taking FY2027 adjusted EPS to roughly $3.10 on modest buyback support. The $52.5M net cash position and $243.5M undrawn revolver fund accretive bolt-ons in medical or defense. The market applies 27x — a premium justified by 41.5% gross margins, 16% FCF margins and improving end-market quality — for $84. Commercial vehicle recovery at Cummins provides upside to the transportation assumption embedded in guidance.

### Bear Case — $48 (-28%)

The 2026 step-up proves partly transitory. FX reverses, removing ~66bps of gross margin; the $1.6M customer reimbursement does not repeat; the tax rate settles at 24-25% rather than 21-23%. H2 2026 comes in at the low end ($565M/$2.55), confirming the deceleration the guidance already implies. Transportation, at 42-43% of revenue with a 1.00 book-to-bill, turns down as light vehicle production softens and tariff or rare-earth cost pressure compresses margin. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $2.65, and the multiple reverts toward the ~19x the company itself was paying in 2025 — $48-51. A goodwill impairment at the $48.8M contract-award-dependent reporting unit, or a third accounting correction, would accelerate the derating. This scenario requires no dramatic event, only mean reversion in the multiple plus the loss of the non-recurring items.

### Base Case — $71 (+7%)

Diversified growth continues at high single digits, transportation is flat, and FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches roughly $2.95-3.00 (5% revenue growth to ~$604M, ~18.5% adjusted operating margin, 22% tax rate, ~28.2M diluted shares). The market holds a 23.5-24x multiple — a premium to the historical ~19x reflecting the better mix and margin, a discount to the current 25.2x reflecting single-digit earnings growth — for $70-71. ## Conclusion

CTS has done the operational work. Gross margin of 41.5% is genuinely high for a components manufacturer, free cash flow of $91.3M converts at 130% of adjusted earnings, the balance sheet carries net cash with 19x interest coverage, direct customer concentration is falling in dollar terms rather than merely being diluted, and medical revenue growth of 36.7% in 1H26 is organic and supported by a 1.21 book-to-bill. Three of the last four quarters beat, the last two by 18-20%, and management raised FY2026 guidance.

The problem is price. At $66.23 — 25.2x the guided FY2026 midpoint, 22.2x FY2027 consensus, 4.8% FCF yield, against the ~19x the company itself paid for stock in 2025 — the improvement is in the multiple. The earnings base includes roughly $0.07 per quarter of FX and customer-reimbursement benefit; the company's own guidance implies H2 revenue growth of 3.9% and H2 EPS growth of 4.5%; the tax rate has risen 570bps year over year on valuation allowances; and the five-year revenue CAGR is 2.7%. Management alignment is the weakest link: no open-market insider purchase in eighteen months, a clustered $8.48M CEO sale three weeks before the succession announcement, a buyback halved and then paused, and a 109-point five-year TSR shortfall against the company's own peer group.

Base-case upside of about 7% against a bear case 28% lower, for a probability-weighted expected return near +3%, is a fair-value outcome. Existing holders should stay — the operating trend, bookings and cash generation justify continued exposure, and the score decline from 6.0 to 5.7 reflects specific new disclosures (H2 guidance decomposition, the composition of the 1H margin gain, the June insider and buyback record) rather than the flat price. Prospective buyers should wait for either H2 delivery at the high end of guidance with margin held ex-FX, or a price in the $55-57 range that restores asymmetry. **HOLD.**
