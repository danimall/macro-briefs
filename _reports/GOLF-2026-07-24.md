**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Acushnet is the highest-quality franchise in golf equipment, anchored by Titleist golf balls (72% of balls used on 2025 worldwide pro tours, roughly 7x the nearest competitor) and FootJoy, the number-one golf shoe and glove. The business generates recurring, rounds-driven consumable revenue (balls and gloves ≈ 40% of sales), holds an 850+ US golf-ball patent portfolio, and has grown revenue at a steady low-to-mid single-digit pace for four consecutive years to $2,559M in FY2025. Competitive positioning is not the issue.

The issue is price versus fundamentals. The stock has run ~25% over the trailing year to $103.64 (near its $119.65 52-week high) and now trades at ~37x trailing and forward earnings, more than double the peer-median P/E of 15.5x for a company delivering ~4% constant-currency revenue growth and *declining* net income (FY2025 net income fell to $188.5M from $214.3M). Gross margin compressed in both FY2025 (47.7% vs 48.3%) and Q1 2026 (47.2% vs 47.9%) on tariff costs. The company has missed consensus EPS in all four of the last four quarters. Operating cash flow has fallen for three straight years, from $372M (FY23) to $194M (FY25), while total debt has roughly doubled to ~$1.13B and capex is stepping up.

Layered on top is a binary long-term overhang: the USGA/R&A golf-ball distance rollback affects a category that is 32% of revenue, with a proposed single game-wide implementation date of January 2030. Management frames Acushnet as a rules-constrained-market outperformer, which is plausible given its R&D depth, but the outcome is genuinely uncertain. We maintain HOLD. The quality is real and largely priced; the growth needed to justify a 37x multiple is not visibly present, and the risk/reward is not asymmetric at this level.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1932 and public since November 2016, Acushnet designs, manufactures, and distributes performance golf products across three reportable segments (FY2025):

- **Titleist golf equipment** — $1,596M, 62% of sales (golf balls $821M + golf clubs $775M). Sub-brands include Pro V1 (best-selling ball globally since 2000), Vokey Design wedges, and Scotty Cameron putters.
- **FootJoy golf wear** — $570M, 22% (shoes, gloves, apparel, outerwear).
- **Golf gear** — $245M, 10% (bags, gloves, headwear, plus KJUS, Club Glove, Pinnacle, PG Golf recycled balls).

The company's competitive strategy is the "pyramid of influence": validation by the best players drives aspirational demand among "dedicated golfers" who pay premium prices and replace on predictable cycles. A two-year product cadence (Pro V1 refreshes in odd years) smooths demand. More than 40% of sales are international, with the US, Japan, Korea, UK, and Canada the top five markets.

Governance is defined by Magnus Holdings, the Korean entity (tied to the Yoon family / Fila Korea lineage) that owns ~50.6% and controls the board. Acushnet is a "controlled company" under NYSE rules. CEO David Maher and CFO Sean Sullivan run operations.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is steady but modest.** FY2022 $2,270M → FY2023 $2,382M → FY2024 $2,457M → FY2025 $2,559M (+4.1% reported, +4.2% constant currency). Q1 2026 came in at $753.0M, +7.1% reported / +4.8% constant currency, led by Titleist equipment and Golf gear. The through-line is a low-single-digit organic grower, not an accelerating one.

**Margin compression is the operative theme.** The FY2025 and Q1 2026 gross-margin declines were driven primarily by incremental tariff costs on FootJoy footwear (Vietnam) and gloves (Thailand). Net income declined despite higher sales, hit by higher opex, restructuring, a $17.0M debt-extinguishment loss, and a higher tax rate (21.9% vs 19.2%). Adjusted EBITDA was $410.4M (16.0% margin, down from 16.5%). The flagship Titleist equipment segment saw operating income fall 10.6% in FY2025 to $244.9M, then rebound +11.1% in Q1 2026 to $84.2M, supporting management's read that the dip was cyclical (tariffs + launch-year A&P/R&D + a tough 2024 PTO-accounting comp) rather than structural. FootJoy remains the margin laggard, with Q1 2026 segment operating income down 14.3% to $21.0M on tariffs.

**Cash-flow quality is softening.** Operating cash flow has fallen three years running: $372M (FY23) → $245M (FY24) → $194M (FY25), attributed to ERP investment and working-capital needs. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was ‑$143.7M (seasonal, with receivables building to ~$505M). Capex is guided up to ~$95M in 2026 (from $74.3M). With debt service rising and buyback appetite intact, free-cash-flow coverage of the combined dividend + buyback + interest deserves monitoring.

**Leverage has climbed.** Total debt rose from $527M (2022) to ~$1.13B (Q1 2026): a $627.6M revolver draw plus $500M of 5.625% notes due 2033. D/E is ~2.0x; stockholders' equity is ~$784M. The Altman Z-Score of 2.59 sits in the grey zone (not distressed, but not comfortably safe). The current ratio is a healthy 2.38. The November 2025 refinancing (issued $500M 2033 notes, redeemed $350M 7.375% 2028 notes, extended the revolver to 2030) improved the maturity profile but left ~$657.5M of variable-rate debt exposed to rates; a 1-point move is ~$6.6M pre-tax. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this is a live headwind for a company already leaning on financing. ROE of 23.9% and ROA of 8.0% are flattered by leverage and by buyback-driven share shrinkage.

Applying the calibration lesson that financial health is the most reliable winner/loser discriminator: Acushnet does not screen as strong on this dimension. Falling operating cash flow, rising leverage into a rising-rate environment, a grey-zone Altman Z, and three consecutive quarters of margin compression net to a below-average score, offset only by the recurring consumable revenue base and manageable liquidity.

## Growth Analysis

The structural backdrop is favorable but decelerating. Worldwide rounds played rose +2% in 2025 (+22% vs 2019), US rounds +1% (+25% vs 2019), marking an 8th consecutive year of US participation growth with broadening demographics (women, juniors, Gen Z). US rounds up only +1% in 2025 is a meaningful step-down from the boom years.

Company growth prospects are correspondingly moderate. Consensus and history point to low-single-digit revenue growth with margins gated by tariffs. The earnings-surprise track record is a caution flag: Acushnet has missed EPS in all four of the last four quarters (Q1 2026 ‑5.5%, Q4 2025 ‑2.3%, Q3 2025 ‑6.2%, Q2 2025 ‑6.3%). A consistent miss pattern signals that consensus visibility is soft and management guidance is not being cleared, the opposite of the beat-and-raise cadence that supports paying up.

There is a genuine, unrecognized upside optionality: the tariff-refund contingency. The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating certain IEEPA tariffs, the March 2026 Court of International Trade refund order, and the April 2026 refund portal collectively create a gain contingency that Acushnet has deliberately not booked. If refunds materialize, there is unquantified earnings/cash upside and forward relief on the margin pressure that dragged FY2025 and Q1 2026. This is real but binary and unquantified, not a base-case growth driver.

Growth prospects are directionally positive (a resilient category, a share leader, and tariff-refund optionality) but thin on velocity. The four-quarter miss streak and decelerating rounds tilt this toward the middle of the range rather than the top.

## Valuation Assessment

At $103.64, GOLF trades at ~37x trailing and forward earnings (EPS $3.11) against a peer-median P/E of 15.5x. The peer set (Brunswick, Polaris, Mattel, Thor, Yeti) is heterogeneous, but the profitable comps (Mattel 9.3x, Thor 15.5x, Yeti 25.5x) cluster well below Acushnet. GOLF carries the highest multiple in the group by a wide margin.

The cycle-adjusted valuation framework does not rescue the multiple here. This is not an earnings-trough recovery story: net income declined year-over-year in FY2025, estimate revisions are not surging (the company is missing, not beating), and revenue growth is ~4%, well short of the >40% threshold that would justify ignoring the trailing multiple. A 37x multiple on a ~4% grower with compressing margins implies a PEG far above 2.0.

The calibration evidence is directly relevant. Cheapness has been anti-predictive (value traps), so a low multiple is not itself a buy signal, but the inverse also holds: a rich multiple on a slow grower is a warning, not something to underwrite on quality alone. Within Consumer Discretionary, the sector has been among the worst for BUY alpha, which raises the bar for rewarding any name here. GOLF is expensive relative to its own growth and quality; the objection is not the "already run" price action but that ~37x forward does not offer asymmetric upside for a business growing earnings in the low single digits at best. A reasonable base-case fair value on ~25x forward earnings (already generous for a 4% grower) sits closer to $78-82, below the current price. The valuation score is low.

There is no analyst price-target data in the dataset (target low/high are null), so we cannot triangulate against consensus targets; the reported "target" of $103.64 is simply the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Competitive position is Acushnet's clearest strength. Titleist's ball share on the pro tours and FootJoy's #1 golf shoe (since 1945) and #1 glove positions anchor the franchise. The moat rests on tour validation (the pyramid of influence), the golf-ball patent portfolio (~775 club patents), owned/controlled design-manufacturing-distribution, and a disciplined two-year launch cadence.

Competition is well-capitalized and consolidating — Callaway/Topgolf, TaylorMade, Ping, Srixon/Dunlop, and Bridgestone in equipment; Nike, Adidas, Ecco in shoes; Peter Millar, GFore, Under Armour in apparel — but Acushnet's share leadership in balls and FootJoy shoes/gloves is durable and validated by usage data. The Q1 2026 Titleist equipment rebound reinforces that the FY2025 margin dip was execution/cost-driven rather than share loss.

The one caveat pulling the score off the top: the distance rollback threatens to reset the entire golf-ball installed base, and while Acushnet argues it wins in rules-constrained environments, that is a forward-looking claim rather than a proven outcome. Regional softness in Korea (‑5.3% FY2025, ‑7.6% Q1 2026) is a second, smaller drag. Trajectory is stable-to-modestly-eroding at the margin, consistent with the prior report's step-down from 7.5 to 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Golf-ball distance rollback (defining long-term risk).** The December 2023 ODS Notice reduces conforming ball distance — effective for elite/professional play January 2028, with recreational golfers able to use current balls until January 1, 2030. A January 16, 2026 "Area of Interest" proposes a single game-wide implementation date of January 2030. Because golf balls are the highest-margin consumable, a rollback forces costly redesigns and could disrupt the category. A parallel "Driver Notice" may tighten driver testing. This is a binary, company-specific risk that the current premium multiple does not appear to discount.

**2. Tariffs — live and two-sided.** 2025 US tariffs are actively compressing gross margin (FootJoy footwear from Vietnam, gloves from Thailand). The offsetting optionality is the unrecognized tariff-refund contingency from the Feb–April 2026 court rulings.

**3. Controlled-company / Magnus overhang.** Magnus controls the board and receives recurring liquidity through matched buyback tranches ($125M directly from Magnus in FY2025). Magnus's Acushnet shares have historically been its primary asset and have at times been pledged as loan collateral, creating latent change-of-control/forced-sale risk. A change of control triggers if a non-permitted party reaches 35%. This is a structural governance discount, not a passing issue.

**4. Rising leverage and rate exposure.** ~$657.5M of the debt is variable-rate, into a higher-for-longer environment.

**5. Discretionary/weather/demographic demand.** A large consumables mix makes Acushnet *more* exposed to rounds-played declines than durable-heavy peers. The boom is decelerating.

**6. ERP implementation.** Multi-year global cloud ERP with internal-control and cost-overrun risk, already a visible cash-flow drag.

**7. Regional softness.** Korea, a top-five market, declined in FY2025 and Q1 2026.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Acushnet is a moat-protected consumer franchise with pricing power and recurring consumable revenue, riding an 8th consecutive year of US golf participation growth. The Titleist equipment margin dip was cyclical (the Q1 2026 rebound confirms), tariffs abate or reverse via refunds (unrecognized upside), FootJoy's supply-chain shift to Vietnam completes and margins normalize, and disciplined buybacks (share count down ~19% since 2022) compound EPS. On a normalized post-tariff earnings base with a category leader's multiple, the stock reaches the low-to-mid $120s (roughly its 52-week high plus the tariff-refund optionality).

**Bear case.** A ~37x multiple on a ~4% grower with three straight quarters of margin compression and four straight EPS misses is unsustainable if the golf boom continues to decelerate or if the distance rollback disrupts the ball category. Operating cash flow has fallen sharply over two years while leverage roughly doubled and rates stay high, pressuring FCF coverage. The controlled-company structure caps the multiple, and a discretionary-demand air pocket hits Acushnet harder than durable-heavy peers. The multiple compresses toward ~20-22x, taking the stock to the high-$70s to mid-$80s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (uncertain). The business quality justifies ownership at the right price; the current price does not offer asymmetric upside, and the setup lacks a confident directional catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A valuation reset to ~25x forward earnings or below (roughly low-$80s) with fundamentals intact — the cleanest path, given the quality is real and the objection is price.
- Recognition of a material tariff refund (a specific dollar gain contingency booked) that lifts margins and demonstrates the compression is reversing, combined with a return to beating consensus (an end to the four-quarter miss streak).
- Favorable resolution or clear de-risking of the distance-rollback impact on the golf-ball category.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fifth consecutive EPS miss combined with a downward revenue-guidance revision, signaling demand deceleration is accelerating.
- Operating cash flow failing to recover in FY2026 while leverage rises further, pressuring dividend/buyback sustainability.
- A distance-rollback rule finalized in a form clearly disruptive to Titleist's ball economics, or a Magnus-related change-of-control/forced-sale event.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (the Q2 print, around early August given the May 6 last-earnings date), which the options market is pricing for a ±7.9% move. A pullback into the low-$80s or a confirmed margin/earnings inflection would each warrant a fresh look.

## Conclusion

Acushnet is an excellent golf franchise trading at a price that already reflects its quality and then some. At ~37x earnings for a low-single-digit revenue grower with compressing margins, a four-quarter earnings-miss streak, deteriorating cash-flow quality, rising leverage into a higher-for-longer rate regime, a controlling-shareholder overhang, and a binary distance-rollback threat on 32% of revenue, the risk/reward is not asymmetric. The competitive moat is the strongest part of the story (score 7.0), but financial health (5.8), growth (5.2), valuation (4.0), management alignment (5.0), and idiosyncratic risk (4.8) each land at or below the midpoint. This is our fourth consecutive HOLD, and the higher bar for continuing an unchanged thesis is met by the stock having appreciated ~25% into an even more stretched multiple without a commensurate improvement in growth or earnings delivery. We would become buyers on a reset toward the low-$80s or on a confirmed post-tariff margin and earnings inflection, and sellers on evidence of demand deceleration or a disruptive rollback outcome. **HOLD.**
