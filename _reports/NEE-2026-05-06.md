## Executive Summary

NextEra Energy reported a strong Q1 2026 beat ($1.09 vs. $0.97 consensus, +12.5%) on April 23, pushing shares to within 3% of their 52-week high at $96.28. The company reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $3.92-$4.02 and its 8%+ growth target through 2032, supported by a record 33 GW backlog following 4 GW of new originations in Q1 alone. FPL's new rate agreement delivers $945M in base revenue increases for 2026, while NEER's competitive position in renewable + storage development remains unmatched at scale.

The growth justifies scrutiny of whether a 24.5x forward P/E — a 27% premium to the peer median of 19.3x — is warranted. NEE's growth rate (8%+) is genuinely differentiated within utilities, and the AI/data center demand catalyst provides multi-year visibility. However, the stock's ascent from $63.88 to $96.28 over 12 months (+51%) has compressed the forward risk/reward. Long-term debt has surged 62% to $89.6B in three years, interest expense has risen 150% to $3.5B, and the clean energy tax credit phase-out creates a structural headwind post-2030 that is not yet fully visible in near-term earnings. The position warrants holding for its growth characteristics but does not offer sufficient asymmetry for new capital at current levels.

## Company Overview

NextEra Energy is North America's largest electric power and energy infrastructure company, operating ~80 GW of net generation and storage capacity through two segments:

**Florida Power & Light (FPL):** The largest electric utility in the US, serving 6M+ customer accounts. FPL earns regulated returns on a rate base that grew ~$5.5B in 2025. Generating capacity: ~36,000 MW (natural gas dominant, 7,932 MW solar, 3,502 MW nuclear, 991 MW battery storage). FPL contributed $5.01B of 2025 net income (up 10.3% YoY).

**NextEra Energy Resources (NEER):** One of the largest competitive clean energy developers globally. Capacity: ~37,505 MW across 44 states and 4 Canadian provinces (22,404 MW wind, 10,504 MW solar, 5,177 MW battery storage, 2,292 MW nuclear). 95% of capacity is contracted under long-term agreements averaging ~14 years remaining. NEER contributed $2.98B of 2025 net income (up 29.4% YoY).

Battery storage ITCs extend through 2033+, and nuclear credits apply to Duane Arnold.

## Competitive Landscape

NEE holds the strongest competitive position in US clean energy development through several structural advantages:

1. **Scale in renewable development:** 33 GW backlog is multiples larger than any competitor. Construction and procurement economics improve with each additional GW.
2. **95% contracted capacity:** Minimizes merchant exposure; weighted average ~14 years remaining.
3. **Tax credit monetization expertise:** Decades of experience structuring differential membership interests and transferable tax credits.
4. **Dual platform:** FPL's regulated growth provides stable funding and operating synergies for NEER's competitive business.
5. **Data center positioning:** Both FPL (new large-load tariff for 50+ MW, 85%+ load factor customers) and NEER (renewable + storage development) can serve hyperscale demand.
6. **Battery storage leadership:** 5,177 MW operational, with the largest integrated battery pipeline in North America.

The primary competitive threat comes from Constellation Energy (CEG) in the nuclear-for-data-centers space, but the companies serve different market segments (CEG = existing nuclear baseload under PPAs; NEE = new-build renewable + storage).

## Risk Assessment

**1. Interest Rate / Debt Burden (HIGH):** In a "higher for longer" environment with 30Y yields above 5%, NEE's cost of capital rises and return spreads on new projects compress. A 100bp increase in average borrowing cost would reduce annual earnings by ~$900M pre-tax. This is partially mitigated by $47.3B in interest rate hedges but creates earnings volatility in the Corporate & Other segment (which posted -$1.15B in 2025).

**2. Tax Credit Phase-Out (MEDIUM-HIGH):** Wind/solar credits terminate for projects placed in service after 2030. Post-2030 growth requires pivoting to battery/nuclear (which retain credits) or accepting lower unlevered returns on renewable projects. The negative effective tax rate (-18% consolidated) means this issue is existential to the growth model over a 5+ year horizon.

**3. Regulatory Risk (MEDIUM):** Federal wind permitting pause; potential for Florida regulatory pushback on rate increases (intervenors have filed motions for reconsideration of the 2025 rate agreement). FPL's reserve amortization mechanism provides near-term smoothing but could face scrutiny.

**4. Execution Risk (MEDIUM):** $24.6B annual capital deployment requires sustained access to capital markets, skilled labor, and supply chain continuity. Tariff exposure on imported generation equipment. Duane Arnold nuclear restart is technically complex with limited US precedent at this scale.

**5. Hurricane Exposure (MEDIUM):** FPL collected ~$1.2B in storm surcharges in 2025. New rate agreement caps initial surcharges, but a catastrophic hurricane season could require emergency rate proceedings.

## Conclusion

NextEra Energy is the premier US clean energy growth utility with a defensible competitive position and 8%+ EPS growth visibility through 2032. The Q1 2026 beat confirms execution remains strong. However, at $96.28 (24.5x forward, 27% premium to peers, within 3% of both the 52-week high and consensus median target), the stock offers approximately symmetric risk/reward with modest 5% base case upside plus 2.6% dividend yield.

The specific concern preventing a BUY recommendation: **estimate revisions are flat (+0.1% over 90 days for current year)**, meaning consensus has already incorporated the Q1 beat and guidance affirmation. Without upward estimate momentum, the stock requires P/E expansion from an already elevated 24.5x — which requires either declining rates or a demonstrable new growth catalyst (data center contracts announced but not yet signed). The flat revision trajectory despite a 12.5% earnings beat suggests analysts view the beat as non-recurring or already embedded in full-year guidance. Until estimates rise or the stock pulls back to $85-88, the risk/reward does not justify deploying new capital.

HOLD with a growth thesis: existing holders benefit from 8%+ compounding with ~2.6% yield support. New buyers should wait for either a valuation reset or estimate revision acceleration.
