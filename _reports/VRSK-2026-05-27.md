**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Verisk Analytics trades at $172 after a 47% decline from its 52-week high of $323, driven by decelerating organic growth (4.7% in Q1 2026, down from 7%+ in prior years), aggressive leverage increases ($4.75B debt, up $1.7B YoY), and growing market concern about AI's potential to disrupt the company's proprietary data advantages. The business remains fundamentally sound with 54% EBITDA margins, $1.4B operating cash flow, and 83% subscription revenue, but the trajectory is deteriorating on multiple dimensions.

The core question for investors is whether Q1 2026's organic deceleration represents a cyclical trough (management's view) or the beginning of structural erosion as P&C carriers reduce analytics spending and explore AI-driven alternatives. At 26x trailing earnings and ~22x FY26 estimates, the stock is neither cheap enough to compensate for uncertainty nor expensive enough to warrant aggressive shorting. The drawdown magnitude (>47%) demands a specific explanation of what the market is wrong about, and certainty is insufficient to make that claim with conviction.

## Company Overview

Verisk Analytics is the dominant data analytics and technology provider to the U.S. property & casualty insurance industry. Founded in 1971 as Insurance Services Office (a not-for-profit rating bureau), the company went public in 2009 and now operates as a pure-play insurance analytics business after divesting Energy operations in 2023 for $3.1B.

The business comprises four core functions: (1) forms, rules, and loss costs (industry-standard policy language and actuarial services), (2) underwriting data on ~143M residential and 16.3M commercial properties, (3) catastrophe modeling covering 120+ countries, and (4) claims solutions built on 1.9B claims records receiving 193,000 new records daily.

Verisk is licensed as a statistical agent in all 50 states, serves all top-100 U.S. P&C insurers, and maintains 38.9 billion statistical records from decades of insurer data contributions. Revenue is ~83% subscription-based with prepaid quarterly/annual billing cycles.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory shows meaningful deceleration:**
- FY2023: $2,681M (+7.4%)
- FY2024: $2,882M (+7.5%)
- FY2025: $3,073M (+6.6%)
- Q1 2026: $783M (~4.7% organic constant currency)

Subscription revenue grew 7% in Q1 2026, but total organic growth at 4.7% marks the slowest quarterly rate in recent history. Management attributed this to a Q1 "trough" and expects gradual improvement through the year, but the underlying driver — decelerating P&C premium growth (10.4% in 2023 → 5.1% through 9 months 2025) — is structural rather than seasonal.

**Profitability remains exceptional:**
- EBITDA margin: 54.3% (FY2025)
- Operating margin: 43.7%
- FCF margin: 38.8% ($1,192M)
- Operating cash flow: $1,436M (+25.5% YoY)

**Balance sheet leverage has escalated materially:**
- Total debt: $4,750M (Dec 2025) vs. $3,050M (Dec 2024) — +$1.7B in one year
- Stockholders' equity: $309M (D/E ratio: ~15x)
- Interest expense: $171M (+37% YoY)
- Net debt/EBITDA: ~1.5x (manageable given cash flow, but direction is concerning)
- $1.6B in debt obligations due within one year

The Z-Score of 1.4 is misleading for an asset-light subscription business generating $1.4B in annual operating cash flow with 54% EBITDA margins. However, the direction of leverage is unambiguously negative — debt grew 56% in a single year while organic growth decelerated 200+ basis points.

**Capital allocation prioritizes returns over balance sheet resilience:**
- Share count declined from 158M (2022) to 140M (2025) — 11.4% reduction
- FY2025 shareholder returns: $624M buybacks + $251M dividends = $875M
- Total ~$4.6B returned to shareholders over 2023-2025 (buybacks + dividends)
- New $1.5B accelerated share repurchase announced Q1 2026
- Total authorization expanded to $9.0B

This capital return is funded by debt issuance, not excess free cash flow. The strategy works in a stable/growing environment but creates genuine fragility if growth slows further or rates remain elevated.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the primary area of deterioration. Several vectors warrant separate examination:

**Cyclical headwind (near-term):** P&C premium growth decelerating from 10.4% to 5.1% directly compresses Verisk's revenue growth because many contracts are priced on carrier activity levels (policies written, claims volume, premium-based fees).

**Structural headwind (medium-term):** Management's own MD&A notes carriers are "not spending as much as they have in the past to drive new policy volume." If this persists, it represents a shift in customer behavior rather than cycle timing.

**AI opportunity/threat (long-term):** The 10-K explicitly identifies AI as a risk to Verisk's proprietary data advantages. If large insurers can replicate analytical insights using AI trained on alternative data sources, the value of Verisk's 50-year data accumulation may erode. Conversely, Verisk is integrating generative AI into claims assessment and medical record review, which could expand margins and win new customers.

**Estimate revisions confirm deceleration:** Current year EPS consensus has fallen 0.7% over 90 days (from $7.70 to $7.65). Next-year estimates are modestly positive (+1.0% over 90 days, from $8.57 to $8.66). The revision profile is flat at best.

Management's full-year guidance of $7.45-$7.75 adjusted EPS brackets the consensus at $7.65, suggesting limited upside surprise potential.

## Valuation Assessment

At $172, VRSK trades at:
- 26.2x trailing EPS ($6.48)
- ~22.5x FY2026E EPS ($7.65)
- ~19.9x FY2027E EPS ($8.66)
- ~4.7% FCF yield on EV (rough estimate using $1.2B FCF / ($22.5B market cap + $2.6B net debt))

Versus peer medians: the Industrials peers shown (CRS at 45.7x, ATI at 53.6x, WWD at 42.1x) are poor comparables — they're cyclical manufacturers, not subscription analytics businesses. More appropriate comparables would be S&P Global (SPGI, ~35x), MSCI (~40x), or Moody's (MCO, ~35x). Against this cohort, VRSK at 22-26x represents a meaningful discount.

The discount exists for identifiable reasons: (1) organic growth is the slowest in its peer group, (2) leverage is rising while growth is falling, and (3) AI disruption risk is more acute for Verisk's proprietary-data-based model than for rating agencies or index providers whose moats are regulatory/contractual.

Analyst consensus target of $220 implies ~28% upside. The range ($185-$275) is wide, reflecting genuine disagreement about the company's terminal growth trajectory. At the low end ($185), only 7.5% above current price, bears see continued deceleration. At the high end ($275), bulls see AI integration expanding margins and catastrophe frequency driving sustained demand.

The valuation is not obviously cheap enough to compensate for the uncertainty around growth re-acceleration. A 22x multiple on $7.65 in EPS is reasonable if growth reaccelerates to 7-8%; it's expensive if organic growth remains at 4-5%.

## Competitive Landscape

Verisk's competitive moat is among the strongest of any mid-cap company:

1. **Data network effects:** 38.9 billion records accumulated over 50+ years, receiving 3.6 billion new records annually from insurers who contribute data to receive industry-standard analytics — a self-reinforcing loop.
2. **Regulatory entrenchment:** Licensed statistical agent in all 50 states. Insurers require Verisk's services to meet regulatory reporting obligations.
3. **Universal adoption:** All top-100 U.S. P&C insurers are customers.
4. **High switching costs:** Deeply embedded in insurer workflows; relationships span decades.

These advantages are real and durable against traditional competition (Moody's RMS, CoreLogic, Mitchell). Whether they're durable against AI-powered alternatives that could replicate insights from alternative data sources without requiring 50 years of accumulation remains unresolved.

The competitive position itself is stable to slightly improving (2025 acquisitions of SuranceBay and Simplitium expanding the platform). The failed AccuLynx acquisition (terminated December 2025) represents a missed opportunity to deepen claims ecosystem integration.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Growth deceleration persisting beyond cyclical trough.** If Q1 2026's 4.7% organic growth represents a new normal rather than a temporary low, the stock at 22x forward earnings would trade toward 18-20x, implying $138-$153.

**2. Leverage in a rising-rate environment.** With $1.6B due within 12 months and interest expense growing at 37%, if refinancing costs rise further, the buyback-funded-by-debt model becomes progressively more expensive.

**3. AI disruption of proprietary data moat.** Large insurers (State Farm, GEICO, Progressive) have massive internal data sets and increasing AI capability. If they reduce reliance on Verisk's analytics, the subscription base erodes gradually.

**4. Customer concentration.** ~70% of revenue from U.S. P&C insurance. No meaningful geographic or industry diversification.

**5. Terminated AccuLynx acquisition.** The acquisition agreement expired per its own terms on December 26, 2025. This signals either regulatory opposition or inability to execute strategic M&A, both concerning for a company that needs inorganic growth to supplement decelerating organics.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($220-$240; +28-40%)
- Q1 2026 was the trough; organic growth reaccelerates to 6-7% in H2 as carrier spending normalizes
- AI integration into claims/underwriting products drives margin expansion and new product adoption
- $1.5B ASR reduces share count by ~6%, mechanically lifting EPS toward the high end of guidance
- Catastrophe frequency (California wildfires, $483B in losses 2020-2025) creates structural demand for risk analytics
- Multiple re-rates toward historical 30-35x as growth stabilizes

### Bear Case ($135-$155; -10% to -20%)
- Organic growth remains at 4-5%, confirming structural deceleration as carriers build internal AI capabilities
- Rising rates make leverage more expensive; refinancing $1.6B at higher rates compresses margins
- Large insurers begin reducing Verisk subscription levels as AI-powered alternatives emerge
- Multiple contracts to 18-20x reflecting lower-growth reality
- No inorganic growth catalyst after AccuLynx failure

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 or Q3 2026 organic growth reaccelerates to >6.5%, confirming management's "Q1 trough" thesis with data
2. Specific AI product revenue quantified by management (showing AI as revenue enhancer, not just risk)
3. Stock declines below $155 (20x FY26E EPS) providing sufficient margin of safety for the uncertainty

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of organic growth <5% (confirming structural deceleration)
2. Management cuts full-year guidance below $7.45 EPS floor
3. Any major customer (top-10 carrier) publicly announces reducing Verisk subscription levels or building alternative internal capabilities

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely July 2026 for Q2 results). Q2 organic growth rate is the single most important data point — if it improves to 6%+, the thesis shifts bullish. If it remains at 4-5%, deterioration continues.

## Conclusion

Verisk Analytics is a high-quality business with structural advantages facing genuine uncertainty about its forward growth trajectory. The 47% drawdown from highs reflects legitimate concerns: organic growth has decelerated from 7.5% to 4.7%, leverage has increased 56% in one year, and AI presents a credible long-term threat to the proprietary data model. Against these concerns, the business generates $1.4B in operating cash flow, maintains 54% EBITDA margins, and holds regulatory/data advantages built over 50 years.

At 22x forward earnings, the stock is not obviously mispriced in either direction. It's cheaper than premium data/analytics peers (SPGI, MSCI at 35-40x) for identifiable reasons. The market is pricing in lower-quality growth going forward, and the evidence from Q1 2026 supports that concern. I cannot articulate a specific, falsifiable thesis for why the market is wrong at this price level — the uncertainty is genuine and requires more data to resolve. HOLD with reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings.
