**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Verisk Analytics is a dominant data analytics provider to the global insurance industry with a deeply entrenched competitive position: 38.9 billion proprietary underwriting records, statutory roles as a statistical agent in all 50 U.S. states, and multi-year subscription contracts serving all 100 top U.S. P&C insurers. The company generates $1.19B in annual free cash flow on 54% EBITDA margins, reflecting genuine pricing power and network effects.

The stock has declined 47% from its 52-week high of $322.92 to $172.38, driven by two converging concerns: (1) revenue growth decelerating from 7%+ organic to approximately 4-5% in 2026, and (2) the market increasingly pricing AI disruption risk against Verisk's data-driven business model. At 26x forward earnings, the stock is no longer expensive relative to peers, but the growth deceleration is real and the AI debate remains unresolved. Three board members purchased shares at ~$180 in February 2026, and the stock currently trades below those levels. This is a high-quality business at a reasonable price facing a genuine structural question that may take 12-18 months to resolve.

The recommendation is HOLD. The competitive moat justifies continued ownership for existing holders, but the combination of slowing growth, unresolved AI risk, and estimates trending slightly negative does not warrant fresh capital deployment at current levels.

## Company Overview

Verisk operates as a pure-play insurance data analytics company after divesting its Energy business ($3.1B sale, Feb 2023) and Marketing Solutions ($80M sale, Dec 2025). Revenue comes from two functional areas: Underwriting (71%, $2.18B in 2025) and Claims (29%, $893M). The business model is 83% subscription-based with 1-5 year auto-renewing contracts.

The company's core data asset — 38.9 billion underwriting statistical records augmented by ~3.6 billion new records annually — sits at the center of a network effect: insurers contribute their claims and premium data to Verisk, which aggregates, normalizes, and returns industry-wide insights. This data contribution relationship is decades old, deeply embedded in workflow, and referenced by regulators. ISO forms, rules, and loss costs are the de facto standard for U.S. P&C insurance pricing.

On the claims side, Xactimate is the industry-standard property estimating platform used by adjusters, contractors, and insurers to settle property damage claims. This product's dominance was implicitly validated by the FTC's concern regarding Verisk's $2.35B acquisition of AccuLynx (roofing contractor software), which was terminated after the FTC failed to complete its antitrust review by the agreement's termination date.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is decelerating:**
- FY2023: $2,681M (+7.4%)
- FY2024: $2,882M (+7.5%)
- FY2025: $3,073M (+6.6%)
- Q1 2026: $782.6M (+3.9% YoY)
- FY2026 guidance midpoint: ~$3.22B (~4.8% growth)

The slowdown reflects decelerating P&C premium growth (5.1% in first 9 months of 2025 vs. 9.6% in 2024), some carrier spending restraint, and the disposition of Marketing Solutions.

**Profitability remains exceptional:**
- EBITDA margin: 54.3% (2025)
- Operating margin: 43.7% (2025)
- Net margin: 29.6% (2025)
- FCF: $1.19B (38.8% FCF margin)
- Operating cash flow grew 25.5% YoY to $1.44B in 2025

**Balance sheet reflects financial engineering, not distress:**
Total debt is $4.75B against only $309M in stockholders' equity (D/E ratio of ~15x). This is entirely an artifact of $4.4B+ in cumulative buybacks over 2023-2025 that depleted the equity base. Net debt (after subtracting the $2.18B cash position, elevated from the cancelled AccuLynx transaction) is approximately $2.57B, yielding a net leverage ratio of ~1.5x EBITDA. The company is in full compliance with its 3.75x funded debt leverage covenant and has $1.25B in undrawn revolver capacity.

**EPS:** Trailing $6.48 (per profile data), with FY2026 consensus at $7.65 and FY2027 at $8.66. This implies ~18% EPS growth in 2026 and ~13% in 2027, driven by the combination of modest revenue growth, margin expansion, and continued buyback-driven share count reduction.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the primary concern. Organic revenue growth has decelerated from 7.2% (2024) to approximately 4-5% (2026 guidance). The drivers:

1. **Insurance premium cycle slowing:** P&C direct written premium growth decelerated from 10.4% (2023) to 5.1% (first 9 months 2025). This directly impacts Verisk's volume-linked revenue streams.

2. **Carrier spending restraint:** Management noted some insurers are "not yet spending as much as they have in the past to drive new policy volume." This creates near-term headwinds to transactional revenue.

3. **International growth partially offsetting:** London market and specialty business solutions provide some diversification, but the U.S. P&C concentration (70% of revenue) means domestic cycle dynamics dominate.

4. **Catastrophe modeling demand structurally growing:** $483B in insured catastrophe losses during 2020-2025 vs. $235B in 2014-2019. This drives demand for Verisk's probabilistic modeling capabilities. California wildfires ($38B in 2025) and active hurricane seasons are persistent tailwinds.

Estimate revisions are mildly negative: current quarter EPS consensus fell 1.9% over 90 days, current year fell 0.7%. Next year rose 1.0%. Estimates are roughly flat with slight downward bias near-term.

## Valuation Assessment

At $172.38 with FY2026 consensus EPS of $7.65, VRSK trades at 22.5x forward earnings (using current year estimates) or ~26x on trailing. On FY2027 estimates of $8.66, the multiple is 19.9x.

Relative to peers: The industrials peer group provided (CRS at 45.7x, ATI at 53.6x, WWD at 42.1x, VLTO at 22.4x, EXPD at 25.6x) shows a median of 42.1x. However, these are imperfect comparators — Verisk's true peers are data/analytics businesses like SPGI, MCO, FDS, and IHS. Those typically trade at 25-35x forward earnings. At 22.5x forward, VRSK sits at the low end of its historical range (typically 30-40x) and below comparable data franchises.

The valuation compression reflects the market pricing in either: (a) permanently lower growth (sub-5% vs. the prior 7%+), (b) AI disruption risk, or (c) some combination. If growth reaccelerates to 6-7% and the AI narrative shifts from "threat" to "enabler," the multiple would expand back toward 30x ($230 on FY2026 EPS). If growth permanently settles at 4-5% with AI risk unresolved, the current ~22x forward may be appropriate.

The analyst consensus target of $220 (median) implies 28% upside — but consensus targets are stale given the recent decline, with the low target at $185 (7.3% upside) and high at $275 (59% upside).

## Competitive Landscape

Verisk's competitive position is exceptional and improving in some dimensions:

- **Regulatory entrenchment:** No substitute exists for Verisk's role as statistical agent in all 50 states. Insurers are legally required to report data to Verisk for these purposes.
- **Network effects:** The database grows by 3.6B records annually. Each new participant makes the dataset more valuable for all others.
- **Switching costs:** ISO forms and loss costs are embedded in policy language, regulatory filings, and actuarial models. Replacing them would require years of parallel operation.
- **Xactimate dominance:** Industry-standard for property claims estimating, validated by the FTC's antitrust concern.

The AI question introduces uncertainty about durability. Bears argue that LLMs trained on public insurance data could replicate some underwriting insights. Bulls argue that Verisk's data is proprietary (contributed under contract), regulatory-grade (legally authoritative), and increasingly positioned as the trusted input layer for AI systems (Anthropic Claude MCP integration announced May 5, 2026).

The Anthropic integration is strategically significant: by making its data accessible through natural language AI interfaces, Verisk is attempting to ensure that AI adoption by insurers increases rather than decreases dependency on Verisk data. Whether this strategy succeeds is the central investment question.

## Risk Assessment

**AI Disruption (High uncertainty, potentially high impact):** The 10-K explicitly acknowledges that AI could "result in the replacement of our existing products." Generic AI-enhanced datasets could compete more effectively with Verisk's offerings over time. The company's ~40 deployed AI solutions and Anthropic partnership represent the counter-strategy.

**Growth Deceleration (Moderate probability, moderate impact):** If organic growth settles permanently at 3-4%, the business deserves a utility-like multiple (18-22x), implying limited upside from current levels.

**FTC/Antitrust Constraint (Confirmed, moderate impact):** The AccuLynx termination limits inorganic growth options. Capital deployment will likely tilt further toward buybacks, which have diminishing returns at sub-5% growth rates.

**Insurance Cycle Risk (Moderate):** Revenue is correlated to P&C premium growth. A soft insurance market or underwriting losses that reduce carrier technology budgets would pressure growth further.

**Leverage (Low risk):** Net leverage at 1.5x EBITDA is very manageable. The $2.18B cash pile provides substantial flexibility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($230-240, 34-40% upside):**
- Anthropic integration and AI strategy prove successful, with Verisk becoming the trust layer for AI in insurance and deepening customer dependency
- P&C insurance premium growth reaccelerates in 2027 as catastrophe-driven rate hardening continues
- FCF of $1.2-1.3B annually deployed into buybacks drives 4-5% annual share count reduction, lifting EPS growth to 12-15% even on modest revenue growth
- Multiple re-rates from ~22x to 28-30x as AI narrative shifts from "threat" to "enabler"
- Board insider purchases at $180 prove well-timed

**Bear Case ($130-140, 20-24% downside):**
- AI tools enable insurers to reduce dependency on Verisk's data products, eroding organic growth to 2-3%
- Premium growth stalls or contracts in a soft market cycle
- FTC restricts Verisk's market position further, limiting pricing power
- Multiple compresses to 16-18x on permanently impaired growth
- The subscription revenue base provides a floor but doesn't prevent gradual erosion

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Organic revenue growth reaccelerates to 6%+ for two consecutive quarters, indicating the insurance spending pause was cyclical rather than structural
2. Evidence of AI strategy success: measurable increase in customer adoption of AI-integrated products or pricing power demonstrated through Anthropic/MCP connector usage
3. Stock reaches $155-160 range (representing 20x FY2026 EPS), providing sufficient margin of safety on the growth deceleration alone

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Organic growth decelerates below 3% for two consecutive quarters without a clear cyclical explanation
2. Major insurance customer publicly announces transition away from Verisk products toward AI-native alternatives
3. Loss of statistical agent designation in any material state jurisdiction

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026) will be the next critical data point. Revenue growth trajectory, management commentary on AI adoption, and any update on the February 8-K material agreement will inform whether growth stabilization is occurring.

## Conclusion

Verisk Analytics possesses one of the strongest competitive moats in data analytics: regulatory entrenchment, network effects, and decades of proprietary data accumulation that cannot be replicated quickly. The 47% decline from the 52-week high reflects genuine concerns about growth deceleration and AI disruption, not irrational selling. At 22.5x forward earnings, the stock is no longer expensive, but the growth profile (4-5% revenue, mid-teens EPS via buybacks) doesn't clearly justify aggressive deployment either.

Whether AI disrupts or deepens Verisk's moat requires 2-3 more quarters of evidence. The Anthropic integration, board insider purchases at higher prices, and the subscription revenue base provide comfort that this is not a value trap. But the declining estimate revision trend and growth deceleration prevent a BUY recommendation until evidence emerges that organic growth is stabilizing above 5%.

Score trajectory note: the declining trend (7.1 → 6.2 → 6.0) reflects the deteriorating growth picture and unresolved AI risk. The current 6.05 score is appropriate for a high-quality business facing a structural question with no near-term resolution catalyst.
