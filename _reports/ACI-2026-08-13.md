## Executive Summary

Albertsons cut fiscal 2026 guidance on July 23, 2026, reducing Adjusted EBITDA by $300M at the midpoint (–7.7%, to $3.550–3.625B), Adjusted EPS by $0.47 (–20.7%, to $1.75–1.85), and identical sales from 0.0%/+1.0% to –1.5%/–0.5%. The shares fell approximately 22% that day and touched $10.86 the following session. They have since recovered to $12.415. The guidance revision matters less as a single event than as confirmation of a trend: on the company's own selected performance measure, Adjusted EBITDA has fallen for four consecutive fiscal years ($4,677.0M in fiscal 2022 → $3,901.5M in fiscal 2025, and fiscal 2025 included an extra 53rd week worth an estimated $68M). Revised guidance implies a fifth decline, to roughly 23% below the fiscal 2022 peak.

The apparent cheapness invites a value case: 4.15x EV/fiscal 2026E Adjusted EBITDA, 6.9x guided Adjusted EPS, and a 5.5% dividend yield on a business generating $2.37B of operating cash flow. Two calculations argue against acting on it. First, the forward multiple has expanded, not compressed, through the drawdown — at $14.60 pre-announcement the stock traded at 6.4x the then-current-year consensus of $2.27; at $12.415 it trades at 6.9x the revised $1.79. The market absorbed the cut as a level reset, so the "cheap on forward earnings" framing was more true before the crash than after it. Second, my full-year free-cash-flow sensitivity — Adjusted EBITDA midpoint $3,587.5M less ~$540M cash interest, ~$275M cash taxes, $1,950M capex, and roughly $500M of transformation, merger-litigation and opioid settlement cash outflows that Adjusted EBITDA excludes — implies about $285M before a ~$332M dividend. Any share repurchase in fiscal 2026 is debt-funded, and $1.86B of repurchase authorization remains against a $6.03B market cap.

Set against this: three of the most senior officers (CEO Susan Morris, President/CFO Sharon McCollam, EVP Thomas Moriarty) bought roughly $2.5M of stock in the open market between July 27 and July 31 at $11.38–$11.51, with McCollam buying after her own retirement had been announced. The Delaware Chancery trial on ACI's $600M Kroger termination-fee claim begins October 19, 2026 — a recovery worth approximately 10% of the current market capitalization. ACI Edge, the consolidation of 11 divisions into four regions with centralized center-store merchandising, targets roughly $200M of run-rate savings weighted to fiscal 2027 (management's figure, sourced from call coverage rather than a filing). These are real optionality. They are not enough to make the risk/reward favorable at a probability-weighted expected return of roughly +4% against a 28%+ bear case that includes an unaccrued opioid verdict currently being tried.

## Company Overview

Albertsons operated 2,244 stores in 35 states and D.C. under 22 banners as of February 28, 2026, supported by 1,713 in-store pharmacies, 405 fuel centers, 22 distribution centers and 19 manufacturing plants. It employs approximately 280,000 associates, roughly 190,000 of whom are covered by collective bargaining agreements, and serves 36.5 million customers weekly. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $83,172.5M.

The company reports a single segment, which is an analytical constraint rather than a detail: there is no segment P&L for pharmacy, fuel, digital or retail media, so mix effects must be inferred from revenue-category disclosure and management commentary. Pharmacy reached $11,414.9M in fiscal 2025, up 18.9%, or roughly 13.7% of revenue, and was the primary driver of the +2.0% fiscal 2025 identical-sales result. Own Brands generated $16.9B of retail sales across more than 14,000 items, though only 9.9% of Own Brands units were manufactured in company-owned facilities. The loyalty program has 51.2M members. Cerberus-affiliated holders own approximately 30% of the equity.

## Financial Analysis

**Fiscal 2025.** Revenue rose 3.5% to $83,172.5M on +2.0% identical sales and +21% digital growth. Gross margin fell 50bp to 27.2%, attributed to pharmacy mix and higher delivery/handling costs. Operating income fell 52.9% to $727.6M and net income fell to $217.4M from $958.6M, driven by a $773.8M pre-tax opioid settlement charge (~$599.8M after tax). Excluding that charge, Adjusted EBITDA still declined to $3,901.5M from $4,004.7M, with margin compressing from 5.0% to 4.7%. The GAAP collapse is legal; the adjusted deterioration is operational.

**Q1 fiscal 2026.** Revenue was essentially flat at $24,941.6M (+0.2%) with identical sales at –0.8%, a 360bp swing from +2.8% a year earlier. The category detail is worse than the headline: non-perishables fell 2.2% to $11,875.7M and fresh fell approximately 1.3%, with the only growth in pharmacy (+4.7%, decelerating from +18.9% in fiscal 2025) and fuel (+~19%, low margin). Gross margin fell 50bp to 26.6% (–23bp ex-fuel/LIFO) while S&A rose 20bp to 25.6% of revenue (+42bp ex-fuel). Operating income fell 41.3% to $263.6M; Adjusted EBITDA fell 8.8% to $1,013.2M, a 4.1% margin. Net interest expense rose 17.6% to $166.7M. Roughly 50bp of deflation was an additional headwind — the worst price environment for a fixed-cost retailer, because negative price/mix arrives with no unit offset.

**The EPS-versus-EBITDA guidance asymmetry is arithmetic, not a hidden problem.** A $300M pre-tax EBITDA cut at a 24.5% tax rate is $226.5M after tax, or $0.447 on 506.7M adjusted diluted shares, against an actual $0.47 midpoint cut. The ~$0.02 residual is consistent with modestly higher interest and D&A. The same gearing works in reverse: $300M of EBITDA recovery is worth roughly $0.45 of Adjusted EPS.

**Cash flow and capital returns.** Fiscal 2025 operating cash flow of $2,366.7M less $1,833.6M capex left $533.1M, against ~$327M of dividends and $1,492.5M of repurchases. Total debt rose $1,126.5M to $8,946.6M and equity fell from $3,385.9M to $1,836.2M. In Q1 fiscal 2026, operating cash flow of $728.9M less $522.1M capex less $28.0M of acquisitions plus $42.4M of asset-sale and other proceeds left $221.2M available, against $308.9M of dividends plus repurchases; net new borrowings of $215.4M bridged the gap, and total debt rose to $9,162.8M.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt $8,869.4M; company-reported net debt ratio 2.33x, up from 1.96x a year earlier, and approximately 2.47x on guidance midpoint before further debt-funded buybacks. Capitalizing operating leases (~$1.0–1.1B annual cost; the input is approximate) puts adjusted leverage near 3.3x. Cash is $293.4M, of which $136.6M is already segregated for the opioid settlement's first payment. Goodwill and intangibles of $3,283.5M exceed $1,612.7M of book equity, so tangible book value is roughly –$1.67B — less alarming for a retailer with $9,867.9M of net PP&E than for an asset-light business, but the impairment cushion is thin.

**Repurchase execution.** Fiscal 2025 and Q1 fiscal 2026 combined: 92.1M shares for $1,719.0M, an average of roughly $18.66. At $12.415, the unrealized shortfall is approximately $576M, or 9.6% of the current market capitalization. Diluted shares did fall 13.4% year over year (575.4M to 498.2M), so the mechanics worked; the timing did not. I do not infer that management knew of the Q1 inflection when the accelerated repurchase was executed at a $17.57 average.

**Estimate revisions.** Current-quarter consensus is $0.33 versus $0.46 ninety days ago (–29.0%); current year $1.79 versus $2.29 (–21.6%); next year $1.89 versus $2.46 (–23.3%). Eighteen analysts on the current-year figure, uniformly lower. The earnings-surprise record is three beats and one miss over four quarters, but the miss was the most recent and the largest (–23.1%), which reduces confidence in the revised numbers holding.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to underwrite in fiscal 2026. Identical sales are guided to –1.5%/–0.5%, including an estimated 150bp headwind from the Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program, effective January 1, 2026. The IRA economics are dollar-negative and rate-positive: lower negotiated prices reduce pharmacy revenue (the category that carried comparable sales for three years) while improving pharmacy gross margin rate, which management explicitly cited as a partial offset in Q1. The program is legislated to expand to as many as 100 drugs by 2031, so this is a recurring reset rather than a one-year comparison issue.

Beneath the pharmacy effect, the core format is contracting. Non-perishables down 2.2% and fresh down 1.3% in dollars is the competitive scoreboard for a supermarket, and both lines are negative. Roughly 50bp of deflation means units held up better than dollars, and trade-down into Own Brands lowers dollars per unit while potentially preserving penny profit — so the dollar decline overstates the profit and share loss somewhat. It does not reverse the direction.

The genuine positives are narrow but real. Digital grew 13%, e-commerce reached profitability for the first time (call-sourced), and digital penetration is roughly 10.5%. ACI Edge targets ~$200M of run-rate savings, largely in fiscal 2027, against ~$50M of transition cost — worth roughly $0.30 of Adjusted EPS if delivered in full, or about 5.6% of guided EBITDA. Centralizing supplier relationships and center-store merchandising is directed at exactly the cost-of-goods gap versus Walmart and Aldi. Management is explicit that the sequencing is unfavourable in the near term: it is "choosing to accelerate investments in our customer value proposition and the customer experience ahead of expected productivity benefits." Fiscal 2026 is a pure cost year.

The Albertsons Media Collective could be the highest-margin incremental profit pool in the business, and it is not disclosed. Media advertising revenue is booked either in revenue or as a reduction in cost of sales depending on the arrangement, so it cannot be sized, its growth cannot be separated from core grocery, and the extent to which prior-year gross margin was media-funded cannot be determined. Non-disclosure is common in the sector and is not evidence of weakness, but it means this cannot carry a growth thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

At $12.415:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market capitalization | ~$6.03B |
| Enterprise value (ex-operating leases) | ~$14.90B |
| EV / FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA ($3,587.5M mid) | 4.15x |
| EV / rolling-4Q Adjusted EBITDA ($3,803.7M) | 3.92x |
| Price / FY2026E Adjusted EPS ($1.80 mid) | 6.90x |
| Price / FY2027E consensus ($1.89) | 6.57x |
| Dividend yield ($0.68) | 5.48% |
| Trailing GAAP P/E | 153x (distorted by the $773.8M opioid charge — not usable) |

These are distressed-adjacent multiples for a company with 2.33x reported net leverage and no near-term solvency question. Three considerations keep me from scoring this as a mispricing.

**The denominator is unstable and has been revised down twice.** A 6.9x multiple on an EPS figure cut 21% in 90 days, from a company guiding its fifth consecutive year of EBITDA decline, is not a reliable anchor. The relevant question is what normalized EBITDA is, and the four-year trend plus a pure investment year in fiscal 2026 makes $3.5B a plausible new base rather than a trough.

**The forward multiple expanded through the drawdown.** Pre-announcement: $14.60 on $2.27 = 6.43x current year; $14.60 on $2.44 = 5.98x next year. Now: $12.415 on $1.79 = 6.94x; on $1.89 = 6.57x. The stock is 8–10% more expensive on forward earnings than before the cut. This is the cleanest evidence that the sell-off was a reset to a lower earnings level rather than a capitulation in the multiple, and it removes the "the market overshot" argument.

**The peer set provided is not a usable comparison.** Median P/E of 27.6x across SFM (16.5x), CART (27.6x), CASY (43.5x), UNFI (–75.2x) and GO (–2.5x) mixes a high-growth natural grocer, a technology platform, a convenience operator and two loss-makers. None shares ACI's conventional-supermarket economics or leverage. Dispersion this wide means the sector is heterogeneous and relative-multiple work has little content here. The one legitimate observation is that ACI's absolute EV/EBITDA sits below where conventional grocery has typically cleared (roughly 5–7x), and that discount is doing the work of pricing declining EBITDA plus unquantified legal liability.

**Support for the equity.** Absolute yield of 5.5% is meaningful, though my FCF sensitivity shows it consuming essentially all discretionary cash flow at guidance. The Kroger claim is worth approximately $1.24 per share pre-tax if fully recovered, roughly 10% of market cap, and is not in any estimate I can see. The owned real estate is genuinely valuable and unquantified — I did not verify the owned-versus-leased split, so I do not assign it a number.

**Scenario values.**
- **Bear $9.00 (–27.5%)**: fiscal 2027 EBITDA slips to ~$3.3B on continued price investment and H2 supplier cost increases; 4.0x EV/EBITDA less ~$9.2B net debt; dividend cut.
- **Base $13.00 (+4.7%)**: EBITDA lands at guidance and stabilizes near $3.6B in fiscal 2027 as partial ACI Edge savings offset investment; ~7.2x guided Adjusted EPS and ~4.2x EV/EBITDA. Consistent with the $13.00 analyst median.
- **Bull $18.00 (+45%)**: identical sales inflect positive by mid-fiscal 2027, ACI Edge delivers the full $200M, multiple re-rates to ~5.0x, and ACI recovers a material portion of the $600M Kroger fee.

Weighting bear 35% / base 45% / bull 20% and adding the dividend gives roughly +4% expected total return. For a 2.5x-levered retailer with negative comparable sales and an unaccrued opioid verdict in trial, that is below a fair required return.

## Competitive Landscape

ACI sits in the conventional-supermarket middle, compressed by Walmart and Costco on price and scale, Aldi and Lidl on hard-discount private label, and Amazon on digital convenience. Management's own diagnosis is that lower-income customers are shifting spend to Walmart and Amazon, that trade-down into private label and cheaper proteins is accelerating, and that weakness is concentrated in the West. BMO's post-print view is that ACI is not lowering prices fast enough to defend against Walmart and Aldi; Citi downgraded to Neutral on July 27. Both are secondary reports of analyst opinion.

The differentiated assets are the 51.2M-member loyalty file, the $16.9B Own Brands franchise (well positioned for trade-down), premier West Coast and Northeast real estate, and 1,713 pharmacies creating a health-services attach that pure grocers lack. These support the view that ACI is not structurally doomed. They have not prevented four years of EBITDA erosion.

ACI Edge is a strategic reversal worth flagging. The 10-K filed April 27, 2026 described "Locally Great, Nationally Strong," which "empowers decision making at the local level, which we believe better serves our customers and communities," as a competitive advantage. Three months later the company centralized center-store merchandising under a single enterprise team and consolidated 11 divisions into four regions. Management evidently concluded that local autonomy cost more in lost scale leverage than it delivered in local relevance, and the supplier-negotiation element is the highest-value piece. The counter-risk is that centralizing assortment across 22 banners and 35 states degrades the local relevance and fresh differentiation that justify ACI's price premium. There is no track record, and the reorganization is being executed while replacing the CFO with five of nine executive officers less than 18 months into their roles.

Trajectory, which matters more than level, is negative on share, on gross margin, and on comparable sales.

## Risk Assessment

**Washington State opioid liability (unaccrued).** Washington opted out of the $773.8M settlement framework; trial began July 13, 2026 in King County Superior Court, with press reports alleging more than 6.5 million red-flagged prescriptions between 2006 and 2022 (externally reported, not verified from the pleading). ACI has recorded no accrual, citing inability to estimate. "No accrual" under ASC 450 does not mean management expects zero. ACI is a materially smaller dispenser than the chain-pharmacy and distributor defendants whose Washington settlements have been reported in the hundreds of millions, so comparables scale down — but any adverse figure lands on $1.61B of book equity, negative tangible book, $293.4M of cash, and roughly $285M of estimated fiscal 2026 free cash flow. Washington is also the state that enjoined the Kroger merger and won a $28.4M fee award for which ACI is jointly and severally liable. A separate DOJ investigation under the Controlled Substances Act and False Claims Act remains open. This is the largest unquantifiable exposure on the file and the primary reason risk factors score 3.5.

**Kroger litigation is two-sided.** Trial October 19, 2026 in Delaware Chancery on ACI's $600M termination-fee claim plus damages, against Kroger counterclaims. The claim is a filing fact; recovery probability and quantum are not established, and Kroger's litigation position was not researched. Meanwhile the case is a current cash cost: $41.6M of merger-related expense in Q1 alone, $106.7M rolling four quarters, all excluded from Adjusted EBITDA.

**Dividend coverage.** At guidance, my sensitivity puts free cash flow before dividends near $285M against a ~$332M dividend. Working capital, opioid payment timing and cash-tax timing could each move that by $100M+, so this is a scenario rather than a forecast. The directional conclusion is robust: repurchases in fiscal 2026 are debt-funded, and the 13% dividend increase announced April 14, 2026 was decided 100 days before the guidance cut.

**Labor and pensions.** Agreements covering ~22,000 associates expire in fiscal 2026 against a 4.1% Q1 Adjusted EBITDA margin. ACI participates in 28 multiemployer plans, 16 in critical, critical-and-declining or endangered status, with $583.3M contributed in fiscal 2025 and ~$610M expected in fiscal 2026. Withdrawal liability is an off-balance-sheet tail that would crystallize only in restructuring or divestiture scenarios.

**Governance and overhang.** CFO retirement announced the same day as the miss; five Item 5.02 8-Ks since March 2025; Cerberus at ~30% with no identified pending secondary (absence of evidence, not evidence of absence). A plaintiff-firm investigation notice was issued July 27 — a solicitation, not a filed complaint, and should not be treated as pending securities litigation.

**Verification gaps.** I did not read the debt footnote, so maturity dates, tranche coupons, ABL borrowing-base headroom and covenant thresholds are unverified — for a company levering up to fund returns, that is the most material remaining gap. Owned-versus-leased real estate is unquantified. ACI Edge dollar targets are call-sourced, not filing-confirmed. The proxy's CEO compensation extraction ($546,277 total, –$1,092,318 actually paid) is implausible for a full-year CEO and almost certainly a transition-year column artifact; it should not be cited.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is unusually relaxed for a stock that just fell 22% in a session. Near-term ATM IV is 23.6% against 39.6% one-year realized volatility (a –16 point spread), IV sits in the 1st percentile of the current chain, the put/call volume ratio is 0.19 and open-interest ratio 0.32 (down from 0.45 three weeks ago), and the term structure is in contango (23.6% near versus 30.2% at six months). The nearest expiry prices a ±3.8% move. The interpretation I take: with the guidance reset already delivered, near-dated event risk is being priced as low, while longer-dated vol at 30.2% carries the October 19 Kroger trial and the Q2 print. Cheap near-term optionality relative to realized volatility means hedging or expressing a view via options costs little here, and it does not corroborate the fundamental caution — the options market is priced for calm, not for stress. Skew values are reported without a directional inference given the mixed signs across tenors.

Separately, short interest at 14.8% of float with 4.9 days to cover, combined with a binary October litigation catalyst that could deliver a cash windfall worth ~10% of market cap, makes bearish positioning awkward. That is a reason to reduce exposure rather than to press a short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($18, +45%).** Fiscal 2026 is the trough by construction: management front-loaded price and experience investment ahead of savings, so the EBITDA base is artificially depressed. ACI Edge delivers $200M of run-rate savings in fiscal 2027 (+$0.30 EPS), centralized supplier negotiation narrows the cost-of-goods gap against Walmart and Aldi, e-commerce is now profitable at 10.5% penetration, and the undisclosed retail media business scales on 51.2M loyalty members. Identical sales inflect positive as the IRA headwind anniversaries in January 2027. ACI recovers a material portion of the $600M Kroger fee, funding deleveraging or repurchases at a fraction of the $18.66 historical average. The CEO, CFO and a senior EVP bought in the open market at $11.40 — the highest-information transaction type — and at 4.15x EV/EBITDA with $2.4B of operating cash flow, a modest re-rating to 5.0x produces most of the return without any operational heroics.

**Bear case ($9.00, –27.5%).** The four-year Adjusted EBITDA decline is structural share loss in the conventional supermarket format, not a normalization from a pandemic base. Core grocery is already contracting in dollars while pharmacy — the category that masked the trend for three years — is now permanently impaired by legislated pricing that expands to 100 drugs by 2031. Price investment required to defend traffic is open-ended, and management flagged potential supplier cost increases in the second half of fiscal 2026, producing the worst combination of deflationary retail prices and inflationary input costs. Centralizing merchandising across 22 banners degrades the local relevance that justifies ACI's premium, executed by a C-suite five of nine of whom are under 18 months in role, without a CFO. The dividend, raised 13% in April, is not covered by free cash flow at guidance and is cut. An adverse Washington opioid verdict adds an unaccrued multi-year cash claim, and leverage drifts through 3.0x reported as EBITDA falls faster than debt.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly +4% (bear 35% at –27.5%, base 45% at +4.7% price plus 5.5% dividend, bull 20% at +45%) is below a fair required return for a 2.5x-levered retailer with negative comparable sales, uncovered dividend coverage at guidance, and an unaccrued liability in trial. This is a downgrade from AVOID in the prior review, which I want to be explicit about: the prior report was scored on pre-announcement data, so the guidance cut is new information relative to it, and the price decline since is justified by fundamentals rather than an improvement in risk/reward. The upgrade from AVOID reflects three specific things — cluster C-suite open-market buying, the October 19 Kroger claim worth ~10% of market cap, and the absence of any near-term solvency question against $2.4B of operating cash flow — which together make full avoidance and any bearish position unattractive relative to trimming.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into strength above $13.00, which is my base case and the analyst median. Do not initiate. For holders unwilling to sell before October, near-dated implied volatility at 23.6% against 39.6% realized makes protective puts through the Q2 print and the Kroger trial unusually inexpensive relative to the stock's own realized movement.

**Upgrade to HOLD requires** any two of: (1) Q2 fiscal 2026 identical sales at or better than the –0.5% guidance high end with gross margin ex-fuel stable sequentially; (2) reaffirmed Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $3.550B or better with no further capex reduction used to fund it; (3) a filing-confirmed ACI Edge savings target with realization milestones; (4) resolution of the Washington opioid matter at a quantified amount below ~$250M.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires**, in addition, either a favorable Kroger judgment or settlement recovering a material portion of the $600M, or two consecutive quarters of positive identical sales excluding fuel with Adjusted EBITDA margin stabilizing at or above 4.3%. A price near $10.00–10.86 — the low analyst target and the 52-week low, roughly 5.6x guided Adjusted EPS and ~3.8x EV/EBITDA — would also materially improve the arithmetic on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade to AVOID on** any of: a further Adjusted EBITDA guidance reduction at the Q2 print; a dividend cut or suspension (which would confirm the free-cash-flow gap and remove the only current holder compensation); an adverse Washington opioid verdict above roughly $400M; net leverage exceeding 3.0x reported; or debt-funded repurchases resuming under the remaining $1.86B authorization while free cash flow is negative after dividends.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q2 fiscal 2026 earnings (mid-October, ahead of the Kroger trial) is the first decision point; the October 19 Chancery trial and the November 10 False Claims Act appeal oral argument are the second; the Washington verdict is unscheduled and could arrive at any time.

## Conclusion

Score trajectory has run 5.3 → 4.5 → 3.9 → 4.7 → 4.0 and now 3.9, with valuation and competitive position each down 2.0 points over the sequence. Current data confirms the decline rather than reversing it: fifth consecutive year of guided Adjusted EBITDA contraction, identical sales guided negative, core grocery revenue contracting in absolute dollars, consensus EPS cut 21–23% in 90 days, and a dividend that consumes essentially all discretionary free cash flow at guidance. The dimension that improved is management quality, on cluster open-market buying by the CEO, CFO and a senior EVP — genuine information, though $2.5M against a $6.03B market cap is meaningful only as a signal, and it sits alongside $1.72B of repurchases executed at a ~$18.66 average and a 13% dividend increase decided 100 days before a $300M EBITDA cut.

The valuation is low and the temptation to treat 4.15x EV/EBITDA and a 5.5% yield as sufficient is the trap here. The forward multiple expanded from 6.4x to 6.9x through the drawdown, which means the sell-off repriced the earnings level rather than the multiple, and the earnings level is still being revised down. I would rather own this after the October catalysts resolve — Kroger, the Q2 print, and ideally a quantified Washington outcome — than in front of them at a price offering roughly 5% upside to base case against 28% downside. REDUCE.
