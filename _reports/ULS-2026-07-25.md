**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UL Solutions is the largest North America-headquartered provider in the global Testing, Inspection & Certification (TIC) oligopoly, built around the century-old Underwriters Laboratories certification mark. The business is asset-light, oligopolistic, and generates a large recurring revenue stream (~33% of revenue from ongoing certification services) that re-validates previously certified products annually. FY2025 revenue grew 6.4% to $3,053M with adjusted EBITDA margin expanding roughly 300 basis points to 25.9%, and the momentum accelerated in Q1 2026 (revenue +7.5%, operating margin 18.2% vs 15.5%, adjusted EPS +35% to $0.50). Margins are expanding through self-help restructuring in the lower-margin Consumer segment plus operating leverage in the higher-margin Industrial segment.

Capital allocation over 2026 has been disciplined: selling non-core software (EHS to Peak Rock for ~$202M) and a minority stake (DQS to Montagu for ~€105M) at roughly $290M of combined pre-tax gains, and redeploying into the ~€575M/$670M acquisition of Eurofins' Electrical & Electronics testing business, the company's largest deal to date. This concentrates capital in regulated hard-asset TIC where the moat is strongest. The balance sheet is under-levered (net debt minimal, $1.0B revolver drawn only $57M), providing ample capacity.

The problem for the stock is price, not the business. At $85.46 the shares trade at roughly 37x current-year consensus EPS ($2.30) and ~33x next-year ($2.58), a substantial premium to the Industrials peer median of 21.5x and to most TIC comparables. Estimate revisions are stable but modest (+4.2% current-year over 90 days), well short of the velocity that would justify paying up further. The governance structure, in which the nonprofit parent controls ~94% of voting power, caps takeover optionality and warrants a persistent discount. The quality is real, the growth is durable, and the recurring base is enviable, but at this multiple the risk/reward is roughly balanced. We rate ULS a HOLD (growth hold), with the Eurofins integration in 2H26 and continued Consumer margin recovery as the catalysts that would justify an upgrade.

## Company Overview

UL Solutions traces to 1894 and owns the UL-in-a-circle certification mark, one of the most recognized safety symbols globally. It serves 80,000+ customers across 110+ countries with ~14,600 employees, roughly 9,900 of them technical staff. The company organizes revenue into four service categories: Certification Testing (~28%), Ongoing Certification Services (~33%, recurring and sticky), Non-certification Testing & Other (advisory/technical), and Software (~9%). Once a manufacturer's product carries the UL mark, ongoing annual certification is high-margin, high-retention, and difficult to displace.

Effective Q1 2026, the company reorganized into three reportable segments. Industrial (~50% of revenue) covers energy, industrial automation, engineered materials, and the built environment, and is the highest-margin unit (Q1'26 operating margin 26.9%, adjusted EBITDA margin 32.8%). Consumer (~42%) spans consumer electronics, medical devices, appliances, HVAC, and lighting, and carries a much lower margin (Q1'26 operating margin 9.4%), the primary target of the November 2025 restructuring. Risk & Compliance Software (~9%) is the SaaS/compliance layer complementary to TIC.

The moat rests on brand trust, regulatory accreditations and delegations of authority, and an installed base of certified products that generates recurring revenue. Approximately 65% of strategic accounts cross-buy software or advisory. The TIC industry is a consolidating global oligopoly (Bureau Veritas, SGS, Intertek, Eurofins, and TÜV are the other principal players) with high barriers to entry from accreditations, brand, and installed base.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue reached $3,053M, up 6.4% (organic +6.2%), with Industrial +6.9% and Consumer +6.5%. Operating income rose to $522M (17.1% margin, up from 16.1%) and adjusted EBITDA to $792M. Net income of $345M was roughly flat year-over-year, held back entirely by the effective tax rate jumping to ~26.6% from 16.9% on the Pillar Two global minimum tax. GAAP diluted EPS was $1.60 (vs $1.62) while adjusted EPS rose to $1.99 from $1.70. Free cash flow expanded sharply to $403M (13.2% margin) from $287M, on capex of $197M.

The balance sheet ended FY25 with cash of $295M, total debt of $494M (down from $747M), and equity of $1,262M, producing ROE of ~27% and ROA of ~12%. The Altman Z of ~2.8 sits in the grey zone, which is benign and normal for an asset-light services firm with modest leverage; it does not signal distress.

Q1 2026 revenue of $758M grew 7.5% (organic +5.7%, FX +1.8%), with Industrial the standout at +10.3% (+8.2% organic). Operating income of $138M lifted the margin to 18.2% from 15.5%; adjusted EBITDA margin was 26.0%. Diluted EPS of $0.45 rose 36%, and adjusted diluted EPS of $0.50 compared with $0.37. Operating cash flow of $219M grew 42%, and FCF of $150M represented a 19.8% margin. Long-term debt fell to $357M with the $1.0B revolver drawn only $57M, creating the M&A capacity used for Eurofins.

The read across both periods is consistent: margins are expanding through restructuring plus operating leverage, cash conversion is improving, and growth is led by the highest-margin Industrial segment. FCF conversion (FCF/net income) exceeds 100%, signaling high-quality earnings. The one persistent drag is the structural tax rate; investors should model a mid-20s% rate rather than the old high-teens, since the ~10-point increase is what flattened FY25 GAAP net income despite 6% revenue growth. The January 2026 OECD "side-by-side" safe-harbor guidance for US-headquartered multinationals partially mitigates this going forward.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth in the mid-single digits, augmented by consistent M&A, characterizes the underlying trajectory. The Industrial segment is directly levered to secular tailwinds (electrification, EV/battery safety, hydrogen, energy transition, and industrial automation) and delivered +8.2% organic in Q1'26, the fastest-growing area. Consumer growth is slower but the restructuring is visibly lifting its margin (9.4% in Q1'26 vs 7.2% prior year), which converts modest revenue growth into faster earnings growth.

The earnings surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats in the last four quarters (Q3'25 +18.6%, Q4'25 +16.6%, Q1'26 +20.1%) against one small miss (Q2'25 -4.9%). The beats are consistent enough to suggest management guides conservatively. Estimate revisions, however, are stable rather than accelerating: current-year EPS is $2.30 (+4.2% over 90 days, +0.2% over 30 days) and next-year $2.58 (+2.9% over 90 days). This is directionally positive but well below the +30% revision velocity that would flag a true acceleration deserving a re-rating. The margin story is real and self-help driven, but the revenue growth ceiling is mid-single-digit organic, so the earnings-growth algorithm is best characterized as high-single to low-double-digit compounding.

The Eurofins E&E acquisition adds scale to the electrical/electronics testing footprint when it closes (expected Q4 2026), consolidating a competitor's capability directly into the core certification business. If integration goes cleanly, it accelerates the growth and margin trajectory in 2027. The pending divestitures (EHS software, DQS stake) trim adjacencies and sharpen focus on the recurring certification core, which should modestly improve the blended growth-quality mix.

## Valuation Assessment

At $85.46, ULS carries a market cap of ~$6.8B on ~$3.1B revenue. Against the Industrials peer median P/E of 21.5x, and against the closer TIC comparables (Intertek, Bureau Veritas, SGS), ULS commands a clear premium. The dividend yield of ~0.67% offers negligible valuation support.

The bull case for the multiple is that ULS deserves a premium for its recurring-revenue mix, brand moat, superior margin trajectory, and disciplined capital allocation. A TIC compounder growing adjusted EPS at low-double-digits with 100%+ FCF conversion is worth more than the sector median. But 37x is a full multiple even for a high-quality name, and the estimate revisions do not show the acceleration that would justify paying up beyond current levels. This is not a cyclical trough where trailing P/E understates earnings power; margins are already near cycle-high and expanding, so the forward multiple is a fair representation of what investors are paying.

Analyst targets bracket a range of $78 (low) to $120 (high), mean $108.95, median $112. At a forward P/E under 30x with strong revisions, analyst targets would function as floors; here, with the stock at ~33x forward and revisions modest, the targets are better read as a spread of scenarios than as reliable floors. The consensus mean implies ~27% upside, but that requires the multiple to stay elevated on continued margin execution.

The calibration lesson that a "cheap" screen is anti-predictive does not apply here: ULS is expensive, not cheap. The concern is the opposite, paying a premium multiple where the growth is good but not exceptional, and where a governance discount arguably should apply. We assign a valuation score of 4.8; the price already reflects most of the quality, and the asymmetry from here is roughly balanced. This is a modest improvement over the prior 5.0-4.5 range, appropriate given the stock has pulled back from its ~$107 high to $85 while fundamentals improved, which compresses the forward multiple somewhat.

## Competitive Landscape

The TIC industry is a consolidating oligopoly with high structural barriers: accreditations and delegations of authority take years to secure, brand trust is decades in the making, and the installed base of certified products creates switching costs and recurring revenue. ULS's competitive position is stable-to-improving. Its Industrial segment is levered to the fastest-growing demand pools (electrification, battery, hydrogen), and the Eurofins E&E acquisition is a classic roll-up that takes share in electrical/electronics testing. The ~65% cross-buy rate among strategic accounts and the recurring ongoing-certification stream deepen customer lock-in.

The competitive vulnerabilities are real but slow-moving. AI-driven testing automation could, over time, reduce demand for certain testing services or shift value toward software; management explicitly flags this. A regulatory drift toward self-declaration of conformity, rather than mandatory third-party testing, would erode the demand base; the post-Chevron (Loper Bright) environment introduces some uncertainty about how aggressively agencies mandate third-party certification. Neither is an acute threat, but both cap the long-term terminal-growth assumption.

The prior score trajectory shows competitive position declining from 7.8 to 7.0 over recent reports. The current data supports holding at 7.0: the moat is intact and the Eurofins deal strengthens the core, but the AI-automation and self-certification threats justify not returning to the higher prior mark.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is governance. The nonprofit parent UL Standards & Engagement (ULSE), itself controlled by UL Research Institutes, owns ~61.6% of the stock and ~94% of voting power via 10-vote Class B shares. ULSE holds extensive consent rights over large M&A, new business lines, debt that would trigger a sub-IG downgrade, and dividend policy, and cannot be forced to accept any CEO other than the incumbent. Public Class A holders have minimal control; ULSE could sell control privately with no premium to minorities. The sunset is only ~7 years post-IPO (IPO April 2024) or when Class B falls below 35% of the post-IPO level. This caps the control-premium/takeover optionality and warrants a persistent valuation discount. The relationship is also symbiotic-but-conflicted: ULSE owns the standards ULS tests against (ULS pays ~$20M/yr for access) and receives ~$72M/yr in dividends, with the corporate-opportunity doctrine waived and overlapping directors.

Second is China exposure via the 70/30 UL-CCIC JV with CCIC, a Chinese state-owned enterprise (JV expires January 2033). CCIC performs a large share of global ongoing-certification inspections. The FCC de-recognized UL-CCIC as a testing lab in August 2025, which is not financially material but is a live US-China decoupling signal, and SAFE currency controls limit cash repatriation (UL-CCIC dividends to UL were $39M in 2025).

Third is reputational tail risk. The February 2026 putative class action (Martucci v. Underwriters Laboratories, N.D. Ill.) alleges UL-listed burglar/fire alarm control units were non-compliant with UL/NFPA 72 standards and defects were concealed. Financial materiality today is low (loss not estimable), but for a company whose entire value rests on the trustworthiness of its mark, this is a non-trivial tail. Fourth is execution/integration risk on the Eurofins deal, the largest in company history, with regulatory approvals pending and a €34.5M break fee. Fifth is the structural tax rate (Pillar Two), a permanent EPS headwind now partly mitigated.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV has risen from 38.6% to 50.2% over the past month while the stock fell 7.7%, and near-term IV (50.2%) is rich to one-year historical vol (41.8%) by ~8 points. The term structure is in backwardation (1-month 50.2% vs 6-month 40.4%), signaling the market expects an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings print and Eurofins deal updates. Put/call skew is balanced (0.7% near-term, turning modestly negative at longer tenors), so there is no pronounced directional put demand contradicting the fundamental view, but the ±11.4% implied move on the nearest expiry argues for patience on entry until the event-driven vol crushes. Short interest is moderate at 6.4% of float (3.1 days to cover), neither a squeeze setup nor a crowded bearish position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($112-120):** ULS is a durable TIC compounder with a recurring-certification moat, and the margin expansion is self-help driven. Consumer restructuring completes by Q1 2027, lifting the segment margin from single digits toward the low-teens; Industrial continues +8% organic on electrification tailwinds; and the Eurofins E&E acquisition closes cleanly in Q4 2026, adding scale and accretion in 2027. Adjusted EPS compounds at low-double-digits. A high-quality compounder with 100%+ FCF conversion sustains a premium multiple, delivering the analyst high target of $120 (~40% upside).

**Bear Case ($68-72):** The premium multiple compresses toward the TIC peer average as revenue growth stays mid-single-digit organic and revision velocity fails to accelerate. Governance overhang, China/UL-CCIC decoupling headlines, or a stumble in the Eurofins integration triggers a de-rating. At 27x forward on $2.58, the stock falls to ~$70, roughly 18% downside from current levels. The February 2026 alarm-systems class action escalates into a reputational event, or the structural tax rate compresses net income further.

The realistic path sits between these. The business quality supports the price but does not clearly justify paying above it, and the governance structure argues for a discount that the market is not currently applying.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth hold with a 12-month horizon tied to the Eurofins deal close (expected Q4 2026), the completion of Consumer restructuring (Q1 2027), and the trajectory of estimate revisions.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions accelerate above +10% over a 90-day window, combined with organic growth sustaining above 7%, indicating the margin expansion is compounding faster than consensus.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s (near the $78 analyst low target), which would compress the forward multiple toward ~28x and restore asymmetric upside.
- Eurofins E&E closes on schedule with clear accretion guidance and no material integration surprises.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The February 2026 alarm-systems class action develops into a quantified, material liability or a broader reputational event that impairs the mark's credibility.
- Consumer segment margin recovery stalls (operating margin flat or declining sequentially over two quarters), signaling the restructuring is not delivering.
- The Eurofins deal breaks (triggering the €34.5M fee) or closes at materially worse economics, or leverage rises meaningfully to fund it.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (early August 2026) and the Q4 2026 Eurofins close are the two natural checkpoints. With the options market pricing an imminent move, the near-term print is the immediate catalyst to watch.

## Conclusion

UL Solutions is a high-quality, moaty TIC compounder executing well: margins are expanding through restructuring and operating leverage, cash conversion is strong, and management is recycling capital from non-core adjacencies into the highest-moat electrical/electronics testing core via the Eurofins acquisition. The recurring ongoing-certification stream and brand-trust moat are durable. The offsets are the ~94%-voting nonprofit-parent governance structure that caps takeover optionality and warrants a discount, China/UL-CCIC geopolitical exposure, reputational tail risk from the new class action, and a full ~33-37x forward multiple that already reflects most of the quality after the stock roughly doubled since its 2024 IPO.

The recommendation is HOLD (growth hold). The business is worth owning through the margin expansion and Eurofins integration, but at $85 the risk/reward is roughly balanced: the premium multiple leaves little room for error, and revision velocity is too modest to justify chasing. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the mid-$70s or on accelerating estimate revisions, and more cautious if the Consumer restructuring stalls or the Eurofins deal disappoints. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the thesis is unchanged in substance: excellent business, full price. Overall score 6.1.
