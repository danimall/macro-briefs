## Executive Summary

Peabody Energy is the world's largest private-sector coal company, operating across U.S. and Australian markets in both thermal and metallurgical coal. The stock has appreciated 74% over the past year, driven primarily by geopolitical energy tailwinds (Hormuz disruption supporting thermal coal substitution demand), but the underlying fundamentals have deteriorated sharply. The company posted an operating loss of $80M in FY2025, turned FCF-negative for the first time in years, and missed EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters — including a -350% miss in Q1 2026.

The critical near-term story is the Centurion Mine ramp-up in Queensland, which turned the entire Seaborne Metallurgical segment EBITDA-negative in Q1 2026 due to commissioning cost overruns. Cash declined from $1.3B in 2022 to $493M in Q1 2026, approaching the $400M minimum liquidity floor required by the surety bond agreement expiring December 2026. EPS estimates have collapsed: current-quarter consensus fell from $0.79 to -$0.25 in 30 days (-132% revision). Management has established personal stock sale plans while fundamentals deteriorate and compensation rises.

The geopolitical energy environment provides a near-term floor on seaborne thermal prices, but this is priced into the stock's 74% rally. At $24.29 with negative trailing earnings, pending convertible dilution of ~14% of shares outstanding, and a binary Centurion bet consuming cash with no returns yet, the risk/reward is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Peabody operates 17 active mining operations across four segments:

- **Seaborne Thermal** (Australia-NSW): Export thermal coal to Asian markets. Revenue/ton $58.97 in 2025, down 20% YoY.
- **Seaborne Metallurgical** (Australia-QLD/NSW + Alabama): Hard coking coal for steelmaking. EBITDA/ton collapsed from $22.20 to $6.57 in 2025, then went negative (-$7.0M EBITDA) in Q1 2026 due to Centurion commissioning.
- **Powder River Basin** (Wyoming): High-volume, ultra-low-margin sub-bituminous coal for U.S. utilities. 84.5M tons sold in 2025 at $1.12/ton EBITDA in Q1 2026.
- **Other U.S. Thermal** (IL, IN, NM, CO): Higher-sulfur bituminous coal for domestic utilities. Customer concentration risk flagged in SEC filings.

The company controls 2.0 billion tons of proven/probable reserves and employs approximately 5,400 people (39% unionized).

## Growth Analysis

### Centurion Mine — The Binary Catalyst

Centurion is the sole material growth driver. The underground longwall mine in Queensland commenced production in February 2026 but encountered mechanical/electrical issues constraining cutting speeds and causing roof condition problems. Management expects full production in H2 2026 with the longwall panel move shifted to early 2027.

If successful: 2.5M tons of high-quality HCC production near port, transforming the met coal segment from a cost center to a profit driver. Full-run-rate EBITDA contribution could be $150-250M annually at mid-cycle HCC prices.

If problems persist: continued cash burn of $50-70M/quarter in excess costs with no offset, accelerating the approach to the liquidity floor.

### Estimate Revisions — Sharply Negative

Current-quarter EPS estimates collapsed from $0.79 to -$0.25 in 30 days (-132% revision). Full-year 2026 estimates fell from $2.73 to $1.12 (-59%). Next-year estimates declined 13% to $3.65. Only 2 analysts provide estimates, creating thin coverage with high uncertainty.

### Secular Headwinds

U.S. coal generation share fell to ~16% and declining. Wind/solar now at 19-20%. The PRB segment operates at near-breakeven margins ($1.12/ton EBITDA) and is purely a volume play. Further coal plant retirements compress this segment's contribution.

### Geopolitical Tailwind — Already Priced

The Hormuz disruption supports thermal coal substitution demand and met coal logistics disruption. Newcastle thermal averaged $119/ton in Q1 2026 (up from $106 in 2025). However, the stock's 74% rally over 12 months already incorporates this tailwind. The tailwind is also inherently temporary and reverses if the conflict de-escalates.

## Valuation Assessment

BTU trades at a negative trailing P/E (EPS -$0.43) and a forward P/E that is also negative (-24.3x) based on the most recent data. Using the company's FY2026 consensus estimate of $1.12 EPS, forward P/E is approximately 21.7x — well above the sole comparable with a positive P/E (PARR at 6.4x).

Alternative valuation approaches:
- **EV/EBITDA**: Market cap $2.96B + debt $320M - cash $493M = EV ~$2.79B. On FY2025 EBITDA of $455M = 6.1x. On current-year estimate (implying ~$500-600M EBITDA if H2 ramp works) = 4.7-5.6x.
- **P/Book**: $2.96B / $3.5B equity = 0.85x book. Discount to book reflects the market's skepticism about asset quality and earnings power.

The valuation appears optically reasonable on an EV/EBITDA basis, but this multiple applies to trough/declining earnings with negative estimate momentum. The analyst median target of $33 implies 36% upside, but targets were set before the Q1 2026 miss and B. Riley's downgrade. The low target of $30 is 23% above current — suggesting even bears among covering analysts think the stock is cheap. However, with only 6 analysts and thin coverage, targets may lag fundamental deterioration.

The stock is not expensive on asset value or mid-cycle earnings, but it is also not clearly cheap given execution risk, cash burn, and potential dilution.

## Competitive Landscape

Peabody retains advantages from scale (largest private coal company globally), geographic diversification (U.S. + Australia), product diversification (thermal + met), and 2.0B tons of reserves providing long mine life.

Disadvantages are structural:
- ESG exclusion from many institutional mandates constrains buying interest
- Limited access to traditional bank financing (coal lending retreat)
- Chinese coal production/export policies dominate global pricing
- Alternative steelmaking (hydrogen DRI, EAF) threatens long-term met coal demand
- NSW policy prohibiting new greenfield mines caps future growth optionality

Versus peers (ARCH, Warrior Met Coal, CONSOL, Whitehaven), Peabody differentiates on scale and diversification but faces Centurion execution risk that more focused operators avoid.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Centurion Mine Execution (HIGH PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT)**: Already cost $63.2M in Q1 excess costs. Full production delayed. If problems persist through H2 2026, FY2026 estimates face further material downgrades.

2. **Surety Bond Agreement Expiration (Dec 31, 2026)**: ~$1.0B in surety bonds outstanding for reclamation obligations. Failure to renew could require massive additional collateral or halt mining permits. The company must maintain minimum liquidity of the greater of $400M or $479M (current threshold) AND net leverage ≤1.5x — with trailing EBITDA declining, the leverage test tightens.

3. **Anglo American Arbitration**: Unquantified contingent liability from ICC arbitration in London. Anglo claims wrongful termination of acquisition agreements. Potential damages are large given the transaction scale.

4. **Convertible Dilution**: 16.8M shares of potential dilution (~14% of current shares) from deep-in-the-money converts due 2028.

5. **Cash Burn Trajectory**: At current rates (~$83M/quarter including capex), the company approaches its $400M liquidity floor within 2 years absent improvement.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV at 120.9% vs. 57.6% historical vol represents extreme richness (63pp spread). The term structure is in steep backwardation. Put/call volume ratio at 0.12 and call-heavy skew (-6%) suggest speculative call buying (geopolitical punt) rather than institutional accumulation. ATM IV rose 12.3pp in a single session (from 108.6% to 120.9%) while the stock fell 1.9%, indicating rapidly increasing uncertainty around current levels. The options market is pricing elevated probability of large moves in either direction.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: Next earnings report (likely early August 2026) provides the clearest catalyst. If Centurion commissioning issues persist into Q2, another EPS miss is highly probable given consensus still embeds meaningful H2 recovery. The surety bond agreement expiration (December 2026) is a secondary catalyst — if renewal terms require significantly more collateral, it compresses the equity value proposition.

**Timing window**: 2-6 months. Q2 earnings (August) and surety renewal (December) bracket the key events.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: At 15.1% of float with 4.1 days to cover, the short is moderately crowded. Not extreme enough to preclude new positioning, but squeeze risk exists — particularly around geopolitical escalation headlines that temporarily spike energy names. The stock moved from $12.58 to $41.14 in 7 months on the geopolitical trade, demonstrating violent upside potential.

**Borrow and options signal**: Options pricing extreme uncertainty (120.9% near-term IV) makes put protection expensive. The call-heavy skew suggests speculative longs are present. High IV makes outright shorting via equity preferable to puts, but the 74% rally means a stop-loss needs to be wide.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Base downside target $18-20 (if Centurion fails + geopolitical de-escalation) = 18-26% downside. Upside risk to $33-35 on Centurion success + sustained coal prices = 35-44% upside. Risk/reward at approximately 1:1.5 to 1:2 — unfavorable for active short.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE** — The thesis is directionally correct (deteriorating fundamentals, negative estimate momentum, insider selling), but the geopolitical optionality creates asymmetric upside risk that makes active shorting imprudent. The stock traded at $41 just two months ago on an escalation headline. A Hormuz-related supply disruption or further Middle East escalation could drive 30-50% upside regardless of company fundamentals. Avoid owning, but do not short into a geopolitically-sensitive energy name during an active conflict.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: Would reconsider if (a) Centurion achieves sustained full-production for 2+ consecutive quarters with EBITDA margins above $20/ton, (b) cash balance stabilizes above $600M with positive FCF, (c) surety agreement renewed with manageable terms, (d) stock declines below $18 (implying ~5x mid-cycle EV/EBITDA), or (e) estimate revisions turn positive for 30+ days.

**Timeline**: Expect headwinds to persist through at least Q3 2026 (August earnings). If Centurion delivers on H2 2026 timeline, fundamental reassessment warranted in Q4 2026. If problems persist, avoid through 2027.

## Conclusion

Peabody Energy presents a deteriorating fundamental picture masked by a geopolitically-driven stock rally. The company has posted 3 misses in 4 quarters, turned FCF-negative, faces a binary Centurion Mine execution risk, approaches structural liquidity constraints, and carries unquantified arbitration liability. Estimate revisions are sharply negative. Management is establishing personal stock sale plans while compensation rises against declining operating results.

The geopolitical energy tailwind is real and provides near-term price support, but it is already reflected in the 74% rally. The risk/reward at $24.29 is unfavorable: the bear case risks are specific, quantifiable, and have defined timelines (Centurion, surety renewal, arbitration), while the bull case requires successful execution on a mine that has already missed its initial targets and a geopolitical premium that could evaporate on any diplomatic progress.

This is a third consecutive AVOID recommendation. Scores continue declining as fundamentals deteriorate while the stock remains elevated on temporary external factors.
