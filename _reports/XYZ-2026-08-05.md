## Executive Summary

Block enters its Q2 2026 print (scheduled for release after the close on August 5, 2026) with the operating thesis in better shape than the reported financials suggest and the valuation asymmetry in worse shape than the multiple suggests. Q1 2026 gross profit rose 27% year over year to $2,909.2M, with Cash App gross profit up 38%, yet the company reported a GAAP operating loss of $(172.0)M. That gap is almost fully accounted for by three identifiable items: a $495.3M restructuring charge tied to a workforce reduction exceeding 40%, a $330.6M year-over-year increase in transaction and loan losses, and a $253.8M incremental litigation accrual. Excluding only the restructuring charge, operating income was $323.3M against $329.3M a year earlier — flat despite 27% gross profit growth, because credit provisioning and legal accruals absorbed the entire operating leverage.

The forward case rests on two mechanics. First, management guided to $800-900M of annualized net compensation savings, equal to 47-53% of FY2025 operating income of $1,708.4M, with the program substantially complete by the end of Q2 2026 and the filings stating plainly that Block plans to continue operating at the smaller size. Second, gross profit has compounded at a 20.0% three-year CAGR from $5,991.9M (FY2022) to $10,359.9M (FY2025), and estimate revisions are positive across every horizon (+7.4% for the current year, +5.4% for next year over 90 days). At $84.91 the shares trade at 21.7x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.91 and 16.8x FY2027 consensus of $5.07, or roughly 4.0x forward EV/gross profit.

What prevents an upgrade is the combination of price and unresolved binaries. The stack is 2.1% below its 52-week high of $86.75 and 76.1% above the February low of $48.21; consensus mean target of $93.03 sits 9.6% above spot. Against that, the DOJ has presented resolution terms on an inquiry Block accrued at $240M while stating reasonably possible losses could exceed the accrual and be material; two shareholder suits survived motions to dismiss on January 6, 2026, opening discovery with losses described as not estimable; and the 60-89 day delinquency bucket on consumer loans held for investment rose from 2.47% to 4.66% of amortized cost in a single quarter, in the segment now driving the majority of profit growth. We maintain HOLD with a $95 base case, and would upgrade toward a price in the low $70s or on Q2/Q3 disclosure that stabilizes the credit picture.

## Company Overview

Block runs two reportable segments plus a Corporate and Other bucket. **Square** is the merchant ecosystem — payments hardware and software, point of sale, payroll, banking, and Square Loans originated through Square Financial Services, an FDIC-insured Utah industrial bank. Q1 2026 Square revenue was $2,112.3M (+14%) on gross profit of $981.5M (+9%), with gross payment volume up 13%. **Cash App** is the consumer ecosystem — P2P transfers, the Cash App Card, direct deposit, investing, bitcoin, and a fast-scaling credit stack of Borrow, Afterpay Pay-in-4, and Pay Monthly. Q1 2026 Cash App revenue was $3,870.7M (-0.2%, dragged by bitcoin) on gross profit of $1,908.1M (+38%); excluding bitcoin, revenue grew 37%. Corporate and Other holds TIDAL, Proto and Bitkey.

The structural fact that governs any analysis of Block is that bitcoin transaction revenue is large and nearly margin-free. In Q1 2026 bitcoin was approximately 30% of revenue ($1,796.4M of $6,056.8M) and roughly 2% of gross profit. That is the mechanical explanation for FY2025 revenue growth of +0.3% alongside gross profit growth of +16.5%, and it means revenue-based metrics — price/sales, revenue growth, net margin, asset turnover — measure a pass-through commodity flow rather than the business. Gross profit is the correct top line throughout this report.

Geographic concentration is heavy: Q1 2026 US revenue was $5,530.8M (91.3%) against international $526.1M (8.7%, +24%). The Chief Operating Decision Maker identified in the 10-K is Jack Dorsey, and the primary segment measures are revenue and gross profit rather than operating income.

Three data-integrity items warrant correction before proceeding. The provider market capitalization of $46.0B implies 540.9M shares, approximately the Class A count alone; total shares at March 31, 2026 were 595,271,301 including 59,991,088 Class B. The provider P/E fields (trailing and forward both 64.29) reconcile to neither the reported EPS of $2.10 nor the current price. And the standardized FY2025 long-term debt tag of $897.9M understates non-current notes of $5,718.1M at March 31, 2026 by roughly six times. All multiples below are computed from filing-level share counts and consensus estimates.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Gross profit rose $5,991.9M → $7,504.9M → $8,889.0M → $10,359.9M across FY2022-2025. Operating income swung from $(624.5)M to $1,708.4M over the same span. Measured against gross profit rather than revenue, operating margin expanded from 10.0% in FY2024 to 16.5% in FY2025.

**The FY2025 net income decline is a tax artifact.** FY2024 net income of $2,897.0M exceeded FY2024 pretax income of $1,357.2M because of a $1,509.3M tax benefit from a deferred tax asset valuation allowance release. FY2025 recorded a $385.7M provision on $1,689.9M pretax — a 22.8% effective rate. On a pretax basis, income rose 24.5%. Any screen reading "net income fell 55%" is measuring the 2024 tax credit.

**Q1 2026 reconciliation.** The $501.3M year-over-year swing in GAAP operating income decomposes as: gross profit +$619.6M, restructuring -$495.3M (including $100.3M of non-cash SBC), transaction/loan/consumer-receivable losses -$330.6M (from $169.5M to $500.1M, +195%), incremental accrued litigation/regulatory expense -$253.8M, residual opex growth -$41.2M. Excluding restructuring alone, operating income was $323.3M versus $329.3M. Excluding restructuring and the incremental litigation accrual, approximately $577.1M, or +75%. Non-GAAP measures were Adjusted Operating Income $727.7M (+56%), Adjusted EBITDA $1,010.2M (+24%), and Adjusted diluted EPS $0.85 against $0.56.

Within opex, the composition is informative. Product development ex-restructuring fell approximately 9.3% year over year to $687.5M. Sales and marketing ex-restructuring rose approximately 20% to roughly $605.2M, with Cash App P2P, overdraft, card-issuance and referral costs up 46% to $296.7M. Block is cutting build cost, not customer-acquisition cost — appropriate if the savings are to be preserved as operating leverage rather than reinvested into growth.

**Cash flow is not comparable across periods.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $965.6M against $133.3M, and FY2025 OCF was $2,579.7M with capex of only $155.0M. A reclassification of consumer loans from held-for-sale to held-for-investment, begun in Q2 2025 and completed July 1, 2025, moved origination outflows from operating to investing activities. Q1 2026 shows $(10,645.8)M of held-for-investment originations and $(7,788.4)M of consumer receivable originations in investing, with only $(1,799.1)M of held-for-sale originations remaining in operating. This is correct accounting following a genuine change in business intent, but it means the reported FY2025 free cash flow of $2,424.7M is not comparable to prior years and Q1 2026 OCF should not be annualized. Q1 also carries $327.2M of accrued-but-unpaid severance and $1.3B of Square Loans sold for $72.1M of net gains.

**Balance sheet (March 31, 2026).** Total assets $39,986.1M, liabilities $18,305.1M, equity $21,681.0M. Goodwill of $11,956.3M is 55% of book equity and 30% of assets, largely Afterpay. Disclosed total available liquidity was $9.07B (approximately $8.17B of cash, restricted cash and marketable securities plus a $900M undrawn revolver), down $120.5M in the quarter, with a further $960.9M available under warehouse facilities. Debt totals $7,350M of notes plus $542.5M drawn on warehouses, putting net cash at roughly +$278M. Customer funds of $5,941.2M and customers payable of $7,868.5M are custodial and are not company liquidity. Current maturities of $1,574.4M fell due in May-June 2026 — covered, but they consumed cash that Q2 results will reflect. The revolver was amended January 14, 2026 to $900M with a January 2031 maturity, a new maximum total net leverage covenant, and a springing maturity if liquidity falls below $250M. Ratings are split across the investment-grade boundary: Fitch BBB-, Moody's Ba1, S&P BB+.

The Altman Z of 2.69 sits in the grey zone but is miscalibrated here — the model was built for industrial manufacturers, and Block's EBIT/assets of 0.043 is structurally depressed by carrying $5.9B of custodial float, roughly $7.6B of loans and receivables, and $12.0B of goodwill, while asset turnover of 0.61 is inflated by pass-through bitcoin revenue. Net cash and $9.07B of liquidity are the better read on solvency.

**Capital allocation.** The November 2025 authorization increase brought cumulative capacity to $9B, of which $4.3B was executed through March 31, 2026 with $4.7B remaining. Q1 repurchases were 10.7M shares for $636.0M, with monthly detail of 4.202M at $65.82 (January), 5.483M at $54.08 (February), and 1.015M at $61.94 (March). The February tranche is up approximately 57% against today's price. Share count fell 602.078M → 595.271M. Against $338.7M of Q1 SBC (11.6% of gross profit; $238.4M or 8.2% excluding restructuring SBC), buybacks exceeded dilution by roughly $297M. Unrecognized SBC of $1.8B over 2.4 years implies approximately $750M annually, or 7% of FY2025 gross profit. Contingent anti-dilutive shares of 41.409M equal 7.0% of current shares outstanding, which if fully issued would represent 6.5% post-issuance ownership dilution; the 2026 and 2027 converts are expected to settle in cash, with the if-converted value below principal.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is Cash App monetization, and its composition matters more than its rate. Q1 Cash App gross profit grew 38% while segment revenue was flat; the re-presented Financial solutions revenue line grew 51%. Cash App Borrow revenue rose $369.4M year over year on origination volume up 175%. Loan losses rose to $368.4M from $67.0M. The growth is being generated by extending unsecured short-duration credit to the existing user base, not by user acquisition — the filings do not report accelerating actives, and separately caution that transacting actives may double-count users with shared aliases or multiple accounts.

Square is the weaker half. Gross profit grew 9% against GPV growth of 13%, implying compression in gross profit per dollar of volume. The filings do not decompose price, mix and product attach, so the cause is unestablished; plausible explanations include competitive pricing against Toast, Shopify, Clover, Stripe and Adyen, mix shift toward larger sellers at lower take rates, or software attach at different economics. The divergence is the most useful competitive datapoint in the quarter and runs opposite to Cash App.

The cost program is the second growth lever and the more reliable one. A workforce reduction exceeding 40% with $800-900M of guided annualized net compensation savings is 7.7-8.7% of FY2025 gross profit and 47-53% of FY2025 operating income. Q2 2026 should be the first quarter to show meaningful realization. Offsetting fixed cost is real: cloud commitments of $2,236.0M ($281.7M for the remainder of 2026, $427.3M in 2027), with cloud fees up $50.9M year over year in Q1 — the cost side of substituting AI tooling for headcount.

Estimate revisions corroborate the direction without signalling an inflection. Current-quarter consensus is $0.87 (+7.4% over 90 days), current-year $3.91 (+7.4%), next-year $5.07 (+5.4%), across 34-40 contributing analysts. These are healthy but well short of the magnitude that would signal the market is systematically underestimating a step-change. The earnings track record tempers confidence in the near-term number: one beat and three misses over the last four quarters, with the single beat (Q1, +24.4%) occurring in the restructuring quarter on an adjusted basis. Note that consensus figures here are non-GAAP and should be compared to Block's Adjusted diluted EPS of $0.85 in Q1, not to GAAP EPS of $(0.52).

The headline 16.8x on FY2027 looks inexpensive against 29.7% implied growth. Two adjustments compress that. First, adjusted EPS excludes approximately $1.35B of annual SBC (roughly 13% of gross profit) and the restructuring charges. Adding back SBC on an after-tax basis to the FY2027 adjusted figure of roughly $3.0B of net income yields approximately $2.0B of GAAP-equivalent net income, or roughly $3.35 per share — about 25x. Second, the $800-900M savings target is a guided, not realized, number, and Q1 demonstrated that credit provisioning and legal accruals are fully capable of absorbing operating leverage as it arrives.

Peer comparison requires care. The median peer P/E of 9.0 (PayPal 7.7, Fiserv 9.0, Corpay 21.0, Global Payments 25.8, FIS 7.5) reflects a group trading at distressed multiples on structural-disruption fears — Fiserv is 70% below its 52-week high, FIS 52%, PayPal 29%. Block at 2% below its high is the group's outperformer and trades at a premium to the median, in line with Corpay and below Global Payments. On EV to gross-profit-equivalent, Block's roughly 4.0x forward compares to PayPal at approximately 3.3x on transaction margin dollars — a modest premium for materially faster growth, which is defensible.

The constraint is asymmetry rather than absolute level. Base case at 18.7x FY2027 consensus is $95, 11.9% above spot and close to the analyst mean of $93.03 and median of $93. Bull case at 22.7x FY2027 is $115, matching the analyst high, 35.4% above spot. Bear case assumes FY2027 adjusted EPS resets toward $4.10 on structurally higher credit costs and a DOJ settlement above accrual, at 15x, giving $62 — 27.0% below spot. Base-case reward to bear-case risk is 0.44:1; bull-to-bear is 1.31:1. A gross-profit-multiple framework would support higher numbers (5.0x FY2027E gross profit of roughly $15.1B implies the high $120s), but gross profit excludes credit losses and SBC, which are the two costs actually consuming the leverage. We anchor on earnings for that reason.

The 84% buy-or-better analyst posture (10 strong buy, 28 buy, 7 hold, 0 sell) is not independent confirmation at a price 2% below the high; it reflects the same view embedded in the stock.

## Competitive Landscape

Block's asserted moat is the two-sided ecosystem — a merchant network and a consumer network that can be cross-connected via Afterpay and Cash App Pay. The Q1 evidence on that moat is mixed and trending sideways at best.

Cash App's competitive position is improving in monetization depth against PayPal/Venmo, Chime, Zelle, Robinhood and the neobanks, and Afterpay competes with Klarna, Affirm and Zip. But the mechanism of improvement — credit extension at 175% origination growth — converges Cash App's economics toward a consumer lender's: cyclical, capital-consuming, and regulated, rather than a payments network's asset-light interchange stream. The reclassification of loans to held-for-investment is the accounting expression of that strategic shift.

It would be a mistake to read the changing revenue mix as diversification. The shift from bitcoin toward Financial solutions changes mix, but counterparty concentration moved the wrong way: settlements receivable concentration among four payment processors went from 36% / 25% / 11% / 10% at December 31, 2025 to 47% / 14% / 11% / 11% at March 31, 2026 — 83% across four counterparties with the top one gaining 11 points in a quarter. Geographic concentration is static at 91.3% US.

Square's take-rate compression (+9% gross profit on +13% GPV) is the clearest negative trajectory datapoint. And the >40% headcount reduction introduces a competitive question the filings raise themselves: a modified growth risk factor states reduced headcount "may limit our capacity to pursue new product development, geographic expansion, acquisitions, or other growth initiatives" — against competitors that did not cut on that scale.

## Risk Assessment

**DOJ inquiry (binary, active).** Stemming from a March 2023 short-seller report, the DOJ presented potential resolution terms in March 2026. Block disputes the basis and methodology, made a counterproposal in April 2026, and discussions continue. Block has accrued $240M and states reasonably possible losses could exceed the accrual and be material. The $253.8M Q1 increase in accrued litigation expense is consistent with establishing or topping up this accrual. The $240M should not be treated as the expected settlement. Offsetting positively: the SEC concluded its investigation in March 2026 with no enforcement action recommended, and a multi-state Attorneys General matter reached agreement in principle in April 2026 with final terms not expected to be material.

**Shareholder litigation past the pleading stage.** A securities class action (N.D. Cal., filed January 17, 2025, alleging AML/compliance misstatements) and consolidated derivative actions both had motions to dismiss denied on January 6, 2026. A separate derivative action filed October 9, 2025 remains unconsolidated, and the board formed a special litigation committee in April 2026. Denial of a motion to dismiss is not a merits finding, but it opens discovery, raises defense cost, and shifts settlement leverage. Losses are stated as not estimable and potentially material.

**Consumer credit — the most important unresolved item.** Consumer loans held for investment: 60-89 days past due rose from $78.6M to $154.0M, or 2.47% to 4.66% of amortized cost (+219bp in one quarter), while non-delinquent balances were roughly flat (+1.1%). Allowance coverage rose from 10.69% to 13.18%. On consumer receivables, the 61-90 day bucket rose from $30.0M to $57.6M (1.03% to 2.28%) even as total amortized cost fell from $2,910.2M to $2,523.7M. Classified loans rose to $467.3M from $381.0M. Management states in the same filing that underlying loan loss rates and portfolio credit performance remained stable.

Both can be true. Borrow and Afterpay Post-Purchase loans write off at 90+ days and carry roughly monthly duration, so with originations up 175% a rapidly growing book mechanically inflates early-delinquency stock at constant per-cohort loss rates. Measured against originations — the right denominator for monthly-duration product — Q1 write-offs of $278.3M on $10,645.8M of held-for-investment originations is 2.61%, and consumer receivable charge-offs of $80.4M on $7,788.4M is 1.03%, blending to roughly 1.95%. Those are not alarming for unsecured short-duration consumer credit, and the 249bp increase in allowance coverage suggests reserving ahead rather than under-reserving. The alternative — genuine normalization in the thin-file consumer, or a deliberately widened credit box to sustain Borrow's growth — cannot be excluded, because the filings disclose no vintage loss curves, approval rates or credit-score mix.

**Restructuring execution.** The Q1 10-Q added a standalone risk factor describing loss of institutional knowledge, increased workloads and error rates, reduced innovation capacity, retained-talent attrition, and dependence on AI tools replacing human work. It also states that AI supporting "legal, regulatory compliance and risk management functions" may limit capacity and introduce oversight risks that could increase regulatory inquiries, enforcement, control deficiencies and penalties, and acknowledges the possibility of regulator-mandated independent monitors. Notably, the Q1 risk factors explicitly supersede the 10-K's. The filings do not disclose headcount by function, and no source establishes that compliance staff were reduced — consent orders customarily mandate compliance staffing, so that function may well have been protected. But the combination of a January 2025 AML consent order, an active DOJ AML matter, and a surviving AML-related securities class action makes the possibility that savings are partly recaptured by remediation a live scenario.

**Other specific items.** Square Financial Services operates under an FDIC Capital and Liquidity Maintenance Agreement requiring a leverage ratio of at least 20%, with Dorsey named individually as the controlling shareholder agreeing to cause Block to perform, and Block board representation on SFS capped at 25% — a structural constraint on moving capital in and out of the bank. Bitcoin remeasurement produced a $172.8M Q1 loss, larger than the entire GAAP operating loss, on a 9,032 BTC investment position whose fair value fell from $777.5M to $617.3M despite adding coins. A San Francisco gross receipts tax dispute has consumed $114.1M of deposit assets over two payments. Hardware relies on single- and limited-source suppliers with tariffs explicitly named.

**Governance.** Class B shares carry 10 votes and controlled approximately 53% of voting power on roughly 10% of shares outstanding at March 31, 2026. Dorsey's total compensation was $2.75 in each year from 2021 through 2025, which eliminates pay-for-failure concern at the CEO level but also removes standard incentive-design levers. The proxy's five-year TSR index shows Block at 29.91 against a peer index of 228.99 — approximately -70% versus +129%. There have been four Item 5.02 officer/director 8-Ks between August 2025 and June 2026, two of them in the two months after the restructuring announcement. Amrita Ahuja serves as both CFO and COO.

**Insider activity.** All transactions reviewed through August 3, 2026 are sales with no open-market purchases. Anthony Eisen (director, Afterpay co-founder) sold approximately 363,000 shares — roughly 18% of his direct stake — over about seven weeks, including blocks of 100,000 at $84.00 and 35,000 at $82.50 on July 15 and 30,000 at $80.00 on July 2, under a 10b5-1 plan modified March 2, 2026. Brian Grassadonia sold 43,348 shares at $80.00 on July 2. Ahuja adopted a new plan on March 2, 2026. Jim McKelvey terminated a 1,000,000-share plan on February 19, 2026, days before the stock traded near $54. The pattern is fully explained by legacy-position diversification under pre-arranged plans, and we do not infer motive. But the absence of any open-market purchase, against estimate revisions of +5-7% rather than the +30% magnitude that would clearly override the signal, leaves it as a modest negative rather than noise.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a large, imminent move with positioning tilted long. Near-term ATM IV is 145.6% against 46.1% one-year historical volatility — an 89th-percentile reading within the current chain — in steep backwardation (145.6% at one week versus 49.6% at six months). Implied moves are ±9.1% on the August 7 expiry, ±11.3% on August 21, and ±13.9% on September 11. Near-term skew is balanced at +0.4%. Put/call volume ratio is 0.07 and put/call open interest 0.48, both call-tilted, and since August 1 ATM IV rose 55.1 percentage points while the price gained 4.7% and put/call OI fell from 0.67 to 0.48. Short interest is 3.5% of float at 2.6 days to cover.

The read: positioning into the print is concentrated on the call side with no meaningful hedging or bearish institutional presence. That structure is consistent with speculation on an upside surprise rather than protection against a downside one, and it means a miss would land into a crowded long with no offsetting short cover. For a HOLD, the practical implications are to avoid buying premium at these levels and to recognize that the setup offers no cushion if credit disclosure disappoints.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($115, +35.4%).** The $800-900M of savings lands substantially intact and shows in Q2/Q3 GAAP operating income; the delinquency increase proves to be cohort seasoning, with the 60-89 day bucket stabilizing as origination growth moderates; the DOJ resolves at or near the $240M accrual; Square's take-rate compression stabilizes as software attach scales. FY2027 adjusted EPS delivers $5.07 or better, and the stock re-rates to 22-23x on the demonstrated combination of high-teens gross profit growth and a permanently smaller cost base. The $4.7B of remaining buyback authorization, deployed with the discipline shown in February, compounds per-share results.

**Bear case ($62, -27.0%).** The delinquency deterioration reflects genuine normalization or a widened credit box rather than seasoning; loss rates on Borrow prove structurally higher than the legacy mix, so normalized operating income is permanently below the $577.1M Q1 run-rate implied ex-restructuring and ex-litigation. The DOJ settles materially above $240M, possibly with a monitor requirement that recaptures part of the savings. Square's gross profit growth decelerates below 5% as competitors price aggressively into a 40%-thinner product organization. FY2027 adjusted EPS resets toward $4.10 and the multiple compresses toward the 15x area that the distressed end of the peer group already occupies.

**Which is more likely.** The origination-denominated loss rates (1.95% blended) and the 249bp increase in allowance coverage favor the seasoning explanation, and the operating-leverage mechanism is arithmetically sound. But neither can be confirmed from the filings available, and the price already embeds a substantial share of the bull case: 76% above the February low, 2% below the 52-week high, with the analyst mean only 9.6% higher. The distribution is asymmetric against new capital at $84.91.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive and specific — realization of $800-900M in annualized savings against gross profit compounding in the low-to-mid twenties — but base-case appreciation of 11.9% does not compensate for 27.0% bear-case downside with two unresolved binaries.

- **Base case: $95** — 18.7x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $5.07, consistent with roughly 4.4x FY2026E gross profit and with the analyst mean of $93.03. Upside +11.9%.
- **Bull case: $115** — 22.7x FY2027 consensus. Upside +35.4%.
- **Bear case: $62** — 15x a reset FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.10. Downside -27.0%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive information arriving at the Q2 print (August 5, 2026), the Q3 print in late October, and any 8-K disclosing DOJ resolution.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q2 or Q3 disclosure showing 60-89 day past-due consumer loans held for investment stabilizing at or below approximately 4.7% of amortized cost with allowance coverage flat to down and write-offs at or below 2.6% of originations.
2. DOJ resolution disclosed at or near the $240M accrual, without a monitor or mandated staffing requirement.
3. Two consecutive quarters of GAAP operating income above $600M excluding restructuring, evidencing that savings are reaching the operating line rather than being absorbed by provisions.
4. Price toward $70-72 (approximately 14x FY2027 consensus) with fundamentals intact, which restores base-case upside above 30% against unchanged bear risk.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. DOJ accrual increased materially above $240M, or imposition of an independent monitor or mandated compliance staffing.
2. 60-89 day past-due exceeding roughly 6% of amortized cost, or allowance coverage above roughly 16%, accompanied by a change in management's characterization of underlying loss rates.
3. Square segment gross profit growth decelerating below 5% with continued GPV/gross-profit divergence.
4. FY2027 consensus cut more than 10% from $5.07, or a second consecutive adjusted EPS miss.

**Next reassessment:** the Q2 2026 release after the close on August 5, 2026 — specifically the delinquency tables, realized savings disclosure, and any update on the DOJ counterproposal.

## Conclusion

Block's operating story is better than its GAAP financials and roughly as good as its stock price now assumes. Gross profit growth of 27%, a three-year gross profit CAGR of 20.0%, and $800-900M of guided savings against FY2025 operating income of $1,708.4M constitute a genuine earnings-power expansion, and at 16.8x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS the shares are not expensive against that trajectory on a headline basis. The February buyback execution at an average $54.08 was materially value-accretive.

Three things hold the rating at HOLD and the score at 5.5, a marginal decline from 5.6 that reflects valuation compression rather than fundamental deterioration. The price has risen approximately 8% since the prior review with fundamentals broadly unchanged, cutting base-case upside to 11.9% against 27.0% downside. The DOJ matter is open with resolution terms tabled and Block disputing them, against a $240M accrual the company itself says may prove insufficient. And the segment generating the majority of profit growth has become an unsecured consumer lending book whose early delinquency stock doubled as a share of amortized cost in one quarter. The seasoning explanation is the more probable one, but it is unconfirmed, and the options market's 145.6% near-term implied volatility with call-tilted positioning offers no cushion if the Q2 tables disappoint.

Own it if you hold it; the fundamental direction is favorable and the cost base is permanently lower. New capital should wait for the credit tables or a price in the low $70s.
