## Executive Summary

Chemed's 2025 earnings problem has resolved, and the market has paid for the resolution. The FY2025 trough was driven by three identifiable items — an $18.7M incremental Medicare Cap headwind (a $27.2M revenue reduction versus $8.4M in FY2024), a roughly $10.5M pretax swing in self-insurance casualty experience, and a 41.8% increase in Roto-Rooter implicit price concessions to $38.0M concentrated in water restoration. Two of the three have reversed. Q2 2026 revenue rose 8.8% to $673.3M, operating income rose 31.0% to $89.2M, and adjusted diluted EPS of $6.06 beat the non-GAAP consensus of $5.55 by roughly 9%. Management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance for a second time to $25.00–$25.75, lifted VITAS average daily census growth guidance to 5.75%–6.25% from 4.5%–5.5%, cut the expected full-year cap limitation to $7.0M from $9.5M, and reported no cap limitation in the Florida combined program — the epicenter of the 2025 issue.

The stock has responded. From a March 24 low of $365.20 the shares are up 50%, and at $549.07 they trade within 0.6% of the 52-week high of $552.50 and essentially at the $549 analyst median target, with three of four covering analysts at hold. That leaves the arithmetic of the guidance raise as the decisive question, and it is less impressive than the headline. Against FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS of $21.55 on approximately 14.463M implied diluted shares (adjusted net income roughly $311.6M), the guidance midpoint of $25.375 on the assumed 13.5M shares implies adjusted net income near $342.6M — growth of about $31M, or 9.9%, converted into +17.7% EPS by a 6.7% reduction in share count. The $20.2M pretax cap reversal ($27.2M to $7.0M) is worth roughly $15.3M after tax at the guided 24.5% rate, or about half of that $31M. Underlying operating earnings growth net of higher interest expense is therefore near 5%.

We rate CHE **HOLD**. Financial health is genuinely strong — FY2025 operating cash flow of $388.3M against $62.8M of capex, and gross leverage near 0.3x against a 3.50x covenant — and management's decision to lever a zero-debt balance sheet to repurchase 710,000 shares at a $404.96 average in H1 2026 has worked. But at 21.6x guided adjusted EPS and 20.0x FY2027 consensus of $27.52, on mid-single-digit underlying growth, with one-third of revenue contracting on volume and only $139.8M of repurchase authorization remaining at June 30, the expected 12-month return is roughly 3–4% probability-weighted against a 15% drawdown in the de-rating case. Existing holders should stay; new capital has no entry signal here.

## Company Overview

Chemed is a holding company with two unrelated operating subsidiaries and no business of its own.

**VITAS Healthcare** (65–66% of revenue) provides hospice care through interdisciplinary teams, billing four levels of care on a per-diem basis: routine home care, general inpatient, continuous home care, and respite. The structural fact that governs the equity is that VITAS has no pricing power — government rates are administratively set and standard across all providers, and commercial rates are "generally structured to closely mirror the Medicare reimbursement model." Growth comes from census days and the annual CMS market-basket update, which was a geographically weighted 2.4% in Q2 2026. Q2 average daily census was 23,687 patients at $209.98 revenue per patient day (+1.43% year over year), comprising $188.62 for routine home care and $1,152.14 for high-acuity care. VITAS operates 33 Medicare provider numbers.

**Roto-Rooter** (34–35% of revenue) provides plumbing, drain cleaning, excavation and water restoration, reaching over 90% of the U.S. population through three channels: company-owned branches (50.4% gross margin in Q2 2026), independent contractors operating Chemed-owned territories under the trademarks and remitting a share of weekly collections (cancellable on 90 days' notice), and franchisees paying CPI-indexed monthly fees under 10-year contracts (cancellable on 60 days' notice). The 10-Q identifies the right to use the registered trademarks as "the primary value" to contractors and franchisees, which locates the moat in brand and emergency-response density rather than in technology or scale economics. Residential jobs are collected at completion, so working capital intensity is minimal.

Corporate overhead of $42.0M in H1 2026 and intercompany interest are unallocated, so segment "net income" figures include intercompany interest income that nets to zero at the consolidated level.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was the trough.** Revenue of $2,530.0M rose 4.1% (VITAS $1,630.1M, +6.5%; Roto-Rooter $899.9M, roughly flat), but operating income fell 7.7% to $338.2M and net income fell 12.2% to $265.2M. GAAP diluted EPS was $18.34, adjusted diluted EPS $21.55 versus $23.13. Adjusted EBITDA of $458.7M represented an 18.1% margin against 20.7% the prior year — 260 basis points of compression, essentially all attributable to the three items identified above.

**Cash conversion is better than headline screens suggest.** FY2025 net cash provided by operating activities was $388.3M against $62.8M of capex, for free cash flow of $325.5M (12.9% of revenue) and 123% conversion of net income. That figure was flattered by the April 1, 2025 refund of the $50.3M Medicare overpayment deposit, which flowed through the "other assets" line; normalizing removes roughly $47M, giving approximately $341M of operating cash flow and $278M of free cash flow (11.0% of revenue) in a year when margins were compressed. On $7.14bn of market cap that is a 3.9% trough-year free cash flow yield.

H1 2026 operating cash flow of $173.0M looks flat against $171.4M, but the comparison is distorted by the same deposit line: stripping it from both periods gives roughly $182.5M in H1 2026 versus $122.9M in H1 2025, a 48% improvement in underlying generation. H1 2026 free cash flow was $140.4M before $33.5M of acquisitions. We have not annualized this — management guided roughly 55% of 2026 earnings to the second half, and VITAS receives a federal payment in excess of $63.0M every other Friday, so period-end timing materially distorts receivables. VITAS days of revenue outstanding were 39.7 excluding unapplied Medicare payments (37.5 a year earlier) and 26.9 including them (flat).

**Q2 2026 margin recovery was broad.** Consolidated gross margin was 32.9% versus 29.8%; VITAS 23.8% versus 19.1%; Roto-Rooter 50.4% versus 49.0%. H1 2026 revenue rose 5.1% to $1,330.8M with net income up 7.9% to $134.0M and adjusted diluted EPS up 18.3% to $11.71 — the wedge between 7.9% and 18.3% is share repurchase.

**The balance sheet changed policy.** Chemed carried zero long-term debt at December 31, 2025 (cash $74.5M, equity $979.4M) and $140.0M at June 30, 2026 (cash $40.2M, equity $830.5M). On April 10, 2026 it replaced its facility with a five-year $450.0M revolver including $100.0M for letters of credit and a $250.0M expansion feature, priced at SOFR + 100bps at quarter-end. Covenants are leverage below 3.50x and interest coverage above 3.00x, both comfortably met; $47.3M of standby letters of credit leave $262.7M undrawn and available. Against H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $238.1M, a trailing figure in the $480–500M range implies gross leverage near 0.3x and net leverage near 0.2x. H1 revolver activity of $491.5M drawn against $351.5M repaid indicates active working-capital management rather than a single term borrowing. Note that goodwill of $699.4M now exceeds book equity of $830.5M — wait, goodwill of $699.4M is below book equity of $830.5M, but the gap is narrow, leaving tangible equity thin, which is normal for this business model but limits the book-value floor.

Financial health scores 7.5: strong cash conversion, negligible leverage relative to covenant and to the revolver's capacity, minimal working-capital intensity, and no refinancing wall. The offsets are a cash balance down to $40.2M, thin tangible equity, and a newly floating-rate liability (the 10-Q quantifies $100,000 of annual interest per $10M borrowed per 100bps; the combined interest expense and interest income swing was roughly $3.7M pretax in Q2 alone).

## Growth Analysis

**VITAS momentum is the real story and it is genuine.** Q2 admissions rose 9.0% and ADC rose 6.1%. Guidance for ADC growth was raised to 5.75%–6.25% and revenue growth excluding Medicare Cap to 8.25%–9.25% from 6.5%–7.5%, with EBITDA margin excluding cap raised to 19.0%–19.5% from 18.0%–18.5%. Against low-single-digit historical census norms, that is a step change.

The mechanism deserves scrutiny. Average length of stay fell to 101.2 days in Q2 2026 from 137.1 a year earlier, with median falling to 16.0 from 20.0. Because the Medicare Cap denominator is the count of first-time beneficiaries at each provider number, admissions growth is the direct lever for cap headroom, and admitting more shorter-stay patients simultaneously expands headroom and compresses ALOS. Management's framing — "initiatives to return to a normal growth pattern after managing the 2025 Medicare Cap issue" — is consistent with a deliberate admissions-led remedy. A 36-day one-year ALOS decline is large, and the filings do not permit separating an admissions-mix shift from a prior-year cohort of very-long-stay patients working through the system. The financially relevant consequence is that acuity mix is a persistent revenue drag (−115bps in Q2, −120bps in H1), high-acuity days fell to 2.2% of total, and continuous care — the highest-rate level — declined 16.4% in days and 18.0% in revenue.

**Roto-Rooter is the offsetting problem.** H1 2026 job counts fell in all three core lines: plumbing −5.3%, drain cleaning −7.6%, excavation −4.7%, with revenue growth delivered entirely by price and mix of +12.0%, +9.5% and +8.3%. Water restoration revenue fell 8.9%. Advertising rose 18.1% to $43.8M. Segment H1 net income fell 5.8% to $67.7M and H1 adjusted EBITDA fell 5.4% to $102.0M, with Q2 margin down 77bps to 21.1%. Management left Roto-Rooter guidance unchanged at 3.0%–3.5% revenue growth and 21.5%–22.5% adjusted EBITDA margin while raising the consolidated number twice. Q2 was better than Q1 (revenue +3.3%, excavation job count +3.5%, commercial revenue +6.8% versus residential +1.7%), so stabilization is plausible. But a third of consolidated revenue is currently raising price 8–12% while losing 5–8% of jobs and spending more per acquired customer, and the asset-light channels are shrinking — independent contractor revenue fell 2.6% in H1.

Chemed's response is to buy the network in: four franchise acquisitions in H1 2026 for $33.5M cash, adding $32.4M to goodwill. Converting a franchise fee into 100% of territory revenue at a ~50% gross margin is mechanically accretive and is included in the 3.0%–3.5% guide, so organic growth is below that. The recurring roughly $9.4M annual "amortization of reacquired franchise agreements" add-back ($4.704M in H1) is an artifact of this strategy and corresponds to cash consideration actually paid.

**Consolidated growth math.** Guided +17.8% adjusted EPS decomposes into roughly 7.1 percentage points of share-count effect and, within the residual +9.9% net income growth, roughly $15.3M after-tax from cap normalization against roughly $15.7M of genuine operating improvement. Underlying operating earnings growth is therefore near 5%. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $27.52 against FY2026 of $25.52 implies +7.8% — mid-to-high single digit including continued buyback. Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current year +4.8% over 90 days, next year +2.4%, current quarter +7.6%. That is real earnings momentum, well short of the magnitude that signals a mispriced inflection.

Growth prospects score 5.5.

Analyst targets function as stale floors on cheap inflection names; at 21.6x forward on mid-single-digit underlying growth, targets clustered at spot read as a fair-value verdict rather than a lagging one.

Two structural offsets to the bull case on multiple:

First, **the buyback that generated 7 points of guided EPS growth is nearly out of authorization.** Only $139.8M remained at June 30, roughly half of one half-year's recent pace ($287.5M in H1 2026). Continuation requires a new Board authorization, which is plausible but not committed.

Second, **part of the buyback benefit is being recaptured by option holders.** The 10-Q discloses 1.3 million options excluded as anti-dilutive in Q2 2026 versus 336,000 a year earlier, when the average market price was in the low $400s. At $549 many are in the money. Under the treasury-stock method, incremental dilution is far below 1.3M — illustratively, at a roughly $450 average strike (Form 4 exercises cluster at $443.79–$469.56), about 234,000 shares, or 1.8% of the 13.2M diluted base. This is an illustrative bound, not a computed figure, and 1.3M must not be read as fully diluted capitalization. It does help explain why guidance assumes 13.5M diluted shares when H1 actual was 13.442M and repurchases continued.

Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, and we do not treat it as one. The issue is the level: 21.6x forward on approximately 5% underlying operating growth, with a 3.9% normalized free cash flow yield and 0.44% dividend, leaves no identifiable mispricing. Valuation scores 4.5, essentially unchanged from the prior review despite the guidance raise, because price rose 7.6% since July 19 against a 4.1% increase in the guidance midpoint and a 4.8% increase in full-year consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

**Hospice supply is regulated, and the regulator has temporarily closed the door.** On May 13, 2026 HHS/CMS imposed a nationwide six-month moratorium on new Medicare enrollments by home health and hospice providers, framed as an anti-fraud measure and confirmed by The Hill, USA Today/Reuters and a critical Health Affairs Forefront piece. A July 6, 2026 CMS proposed rule would expand authority to revoke and deny Medicare enrollment and broaden disclosure obligations. For an incumbent with 33 existing provider numbers, a freeze on competing entrants constrains the small-operator formation that has driven hospice capacity growth, particularly in Sun Belt markets, and plausibly supports the durability of the raised ADC guidance.

Three caveats keep this from being scored as a moat. Chemed has not mentioned or quantified the moratorium in any filing, and has not attributed any ADC upside to it. The measure is six months, implying expiry around mid-November 2026. And the same enforcement posture that blocks entrants produced VITAS's own $50.3M audit demand; the July proposed rule raises revocation tail risk for incumbents as well.

Hospice demand is demographic and largely acyclical. Chemed's Q2 census mix — cerebrovascular/dementia 44.2%, cardio 16.6%, neurological 11.1%, cancer 9.5%, respiratory 8.0% — is dominated by long-duration chronic conditions, while admissions skew to cancer (24.7% of admissions, 9.5% of census). That mismatch is precisely the arithmetic that generates Medicare Cap exposure.

**Roto-Rooter's competitive read is unresolved.** We could not obtain independent home-services industry data, so we cannot determine whether the 5–8% job-count declines reflect market-wide residential softness or share loss. What the filings show is that both asset-light channels are shrinking or barely growing, and that Chemed is responding by acquiring territories. A benign reading is available: the water-restoration decline coincides with a 16.3% reduction in implicit price concessions to $14.8M, consistent with a deliberate retreat from insurer-disputed revenue that would be margin-accretive while depressing reported growth. Both readings can be partly true.

Net trajectory: VITAS improving, Roto-Rooter deteriorating on volume with unchanged guidance. Competitive position scores 5.5, unchanged from the prior review.

## Risk Assessment

**Medicare Cap is a recurring structural constraint, not a resolved one.** The cap is an annual per-beneficiary aggregate limit measured September 28 to September 27 and applied separately at each provider number, so consolidated cushion does not protect against local breaches. Thirty of 33 programs use the streamlined method. Q2 2026 disclosure: 22 of 33 provider numbers have anticipated full-year cushion of 10% or greater, seven have cushion between 0% and 10%, and four already carry a billing limitation totaling $7.0M. The FY2025 experience — $8.4M to $27.2M in one year — demonstrates the conversion speed. Seven programs inside a 10% cushion is a live exposure priced into a stock at its 52-week high.

**Payer concentration is near-absolute at VITAS.** At June 30, roughly 58% of VITAS receivables were Medicare and 36% state Medicaid or managed Medicaid; government payers represent approximately 76% of consolidated net receivables. A single federal rulemaking sets revenue per day.

**Program-integrity exposure, with a favorable precedent.** VITAS was selected by the HHS OIG for an audit of elevated level-of-care services covering 100 of 50,850 inpatient and continuous care claims; its MAC demanded $50.3M on August 29, 2022, which VITAS deposited under Immediate Recoupment. On February 3, 2025 an Administrative Law Judge ruled that VITAS's care met Medicare's standards as billed on all but one appealed claim, the overpayment was reduced to a de minimis amount, and the deposit was refunded April 1, 2025. That is substantive evidence on billing practices and is underweighted by the generic bear case. It took two and a half years and $50.3M of trapped cash, the sample was small and selected for elevated acuity rather than random, and high-acuity care is now only 2.2% of days.

**Labor and self-insurance.** H1 2026 VITAS cost of services was $663.2M, of which $498.5M (75%) was wages plus $93.0M of patient care expense — unhedgeable. Accrued insurance rose to $72.5M from $62.1M at year-end; the FY2025 casualty swing of roughly $10.5M pretax with no operational change shows this line moving consolidated margin by 40+ basis points in either direction.

**Cybersecurity.** An October 24, 2025 incident at VITAS generated class actions now consolidated and settled for a non-material amount fully covered by insurance per the Q2 10-Q. Regulatory exposure (for example HIPAA/OCR) is not disclosed as resolved or quantified.

**Information flow.** Management stated that "barring any unusual developments, updating guidance once per year in conjunction with our second quarter press release is our on-going expectation." Reduced guidance frequency raises print-to-print volatility.

**Governance.** Reported CEO total compensation rose every year from $11.66M (2021) to $12.90M (2025) while cumulative TSR fell to $81.68 per $100 invested against a peer group at $182.29 — Chemed trailed peers in each of the five years shown, predating the cap event. The mitigant is that compensation *actually paid* collapsed to $4.08M in 2025 from $19.20M in 2023 (−79%), with NEO average actually-paid comp falling to $1.87M from $5.75M; the equity mark-to-market mechanism is working. February 2026 grants are a balanced 50/50 split of TSR-relative and EPS-based PSUs. One definitional item to flag: the proxy reports FY2025 Adjusted EPS of $22.61 as the company-selected measure against the 10-K's $21.55 adjusted diluted EPS, and we did not locate the reconciliation.

Risk factors score 5.5. The cap is a genuine program-level binary that the current price does not cushion, and government-payer concentration is absolute; offsetting, leverage is negligible, demand is acyclical, and the largest litigation item resolved favorably.

### Options Market Signal

The event has been priced out. ATM implied volatility collapsed from 37.7% on July 19 to 22.2% now, against 29.7% trailing realized volatility — a −7.4 point spread and a 5th-percentile reading within the current chain. Term structure is in contango (22.2% at 13 days, 21.2% at 41 days, 25.5% at 132 days), and open interest is call-tilted at a 0.16 put/call ratio. Near-term put skew of 7.3% coexists with −3.0% at the six-month tenor. Short interest is 5.0% of float at 2.8 days to cover.

The composite reads as complacency at the high: post-earnings vol crush, call-heavy positioning, no crowded short. For a holder, cheap implied volatility relative to realized makes protective puts or a collar unusually inexpensive to carry into the Q3 print and the mid-November moratorium expiry. For a prospective buyer, low IV at a 52-week high is not an entry signal — it means the market is pricing little near-term uncertainty precisely when the cap-cushion disclosure and Roto-Rooter volume trend remain unresolved. This does not change the fundamental view; it identifies the cheapest way to hold the position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($660, +20%).** VITAS ADC growth of 5.75%–6.25% proves durable rather than a one-year admissions catch-up, aided by the CMS enrollment freeze constraining new competitors and by a possible extension past mid-November. The FY2026 cap limitation lands below $7.0M, pushing EPS to or above the $25.75 top of guidance. Roto-Rooter's Q2 stabilization extends: excavation job counts continue positive, commercial growth (+6.8%) keeps outpacing residential, and the retreat from insurer-disputed water restoration lifts segment margin back toward the 22.5% guide top. The Board authorizes a new repurchase program, extending 3–4% annual share reduction. FY2027 EPS reaches $29–30 and the multiple holds at 22–23x on a demonstrably acyclical franchise with 0.3x leverage.

**Bear case ($465, −15%).** The cap problem recurs at one or more of the seven programs with sub-10% cushion, or the July 2026 CMS revocation rule creates new incumbent exposure. ALOS normalization proves to be a mix shift with a persistent revenue-per-day drag (already −115bps in Q2) that offsets census gains. Roto-Rooter's 5–8% volume declines resume as the price-led strategy exhausts, with advertising up 18% failing to arrest it, and segment EBITDA continues its 5.4% H1 decline. The repurchase authorization runs down without replacement, removing 7 points of EPS growth, while re-entering in-the-money options add roughly 2% dilution. The multiple compresses to 17x FY2027 as investors reprice a mid-single-digit grower. A severe cap recurrence combined with multiple compression could take the shares toward $400 (−27%), which is above the March 2026 low of $365.

**Our base case ($580, +5.6%)** applies 21.1x to FY2027 consensus of $27.52 — the current forward multiple held roughly flat while earnings compound at high single digits including buyback. Probability-weighting 55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear gives roughly +3.3% price return plus a 0.44% dividend, or 3–4% total. That is below the 4.20% two-year Treasury and does not compensate for the −15% de-rating scenario.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month path is positive but modest: earnings compound at high single digits while the multiple has no obvious room to expand from 21.6x forward.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price at $470–$490** with FY2027 consensus intact at $27.00 or better — 17.5x–18x forward, which restores a 15%+ margin of safety on an acyclical franchise with 0.3x leverage. This is the cleanest path.
2. **Roto-Rooter job counts turning positive in two consecutive quarters** in at least two of the three core lines (plumbing, drain cleaning, excavation), with segment adjusted EBITDA margin at or above 22.0%. That would convert a third of revenue from price-led decay to genuine growth and justify a higher multiple.
3. **A new repurchase authorization of $300M or more** combined with the Q3 disclosure showing 26 or more of 33 provider numbers at 10%+ cap cushion. That would extend the share-count tailwind and de-risk the primary earnings binary simultaneously.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Any Q3 or Q4 2026 Medicare Cap billing limitation above $10.0M**, or the count of programs with sub-10% cushion rising above seven. This is the specific number to check in each 10-Q concentration and cap disclosure.
2. **VITAS revenue per patient day declining year over year**, or the acuity-mix drag widening beyond −150bps, which would mean census growth is being fully offset by rate mix.
3. **Roto-Rooter adjusted EBITDA margin falling below 20.5%** for two consecutive quarters, or the FY2027 guide coming in below 3.0% revenue growth with job counts still negative.
4. **Repurchase authorization exhausted without replacement** by the Q4 2026 report, removing the mechanical EPS support that guidance depends on.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October) are the first checkpoint, carrying the cap-cushion update for the September 27 measurement-period close and the Roto-Rooter volume trend. The CMS moratorium expiry around mid-November is the second. Management's shift to annual guidance updates at the Q2 print means the Q4 release (late February 2027) will carry the FY2027 guide and the full-year cap outcome — the highest-information event in the calendar.

**Position management for existing holders:** implied volatility at the 5th percentile of the current chain and 7.4 points below realized makes downside protection through the Q3 print and moratorium expiry cheap relative to its usual cost. Adding to the position at $549 is not supported.

## Conclusion

Chemed has done what it needed to do. The Medicare Cap issue that cost $27.2M of FY2025 revenue and 260 basis points of EBITDA margin has been managed down to a guided $7.0M, Florida is clean, VITAS census is growing 6%, and margins have recovered — Q2 consolidated gross margin of 32.9% versus 29.8%, operating income up 31%. Management funded a 710,000-share repurchase at $404.96 in H1 2026, four times the prior-year pace at a 30% lower price, using a balance sheet that had been sitting idle at zero debt; those shares are marked 36% above cost. The financial position remains close to unlevered, with 0.3x gross leverage against a 3.50x covenant and $262.7M of undrawn capacity.

What the market now pays for that is 21.6x guided adjusted EPS, 20.0x FY2027 consensus, and 15.8x trailing EV/EBITDA, at 99.4% of the 52-week high and at the $549 analyst median with three of four analysts at hold. The guided +17.8% EPS growth is roughly 7 points share count and roughly half of the remainder cap base effect, leaving underlying operating growth near 5%. The repurchase authorization is down to $139.8M and in-the-money options are re-entering the diluted count. One-third of revenue is losing 5–8% of jobs annually while raising price 8–12% and spending 18% more on advertising, and management left that segment's guidance unchanged while raising the consolidated number twice.

The result is a well-run, cash-generative, defensively positioned business at a fair price with no margin of safety. Probability-weighted 12-month return of 3–4% against a −15% de-rating case does not justify new capital. Hold, monitor the Q3 cap-cushion disclosure and Roto-Rooter job counts, and treat $470–$490 as the level at which the risk/reward becomes attractive again.
