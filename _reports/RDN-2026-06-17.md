**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Radian is executing the largest strategic change in its roughly 50-year history. The February 2, 2026 acquisition of Inigo, a Lloyd's of London specialty insurer (Syndicate 1301), for $1.67B converts what was a pure-play U.S. private mortgage insurer into a two-segment "global multi-line specialty insurer." Concurrently, management is winding down three non-core businesses (Mortgage Conduit, Title, Real Estate Services). The legacy mortgage business remains in excellent shape: $281.7B insurance-in-force, a Q1 2026 combined ratio of 30.2%, a default rate improving to 2.51%, and a PMIERs capital cushion of $1.6B (41% over minimum). That business is funding the transformation.

The stock trades at approximately $35.23 against book value of $35.67 (0.99x P/B) and roughly 6.9x annualized adjusted operating EPS ($1.27 × 4). For a franchise generating ~15% adjusted operating ROE with $200-250M of annual buyback capacity and rising consensus estimates, the discount reflects specific skepticism rather than a generic value-trap multiple. The skepticism is warranted: the specialty pivot introduces catastrophe and large-loss volatility that did not exist in the monoline, it was bought into a softening property-cat pricing cycle, and the $600M intercompany note used to part-fund the deal now requires Pennsylvania Insurance Department approval for Radian Guaranty's dividends for three to five years, gating the holding company's primary cash source.

We rate RDN a HOLD with a constructive bias. The incoming CEO's ~$5.77M open-market purchase at ~$34 in early June, four straight earnings beats, and estimate revisions of +10-13% over 90 days are genuine positives. But the company is in motion: integration risk, an unproven specialty franchise under Radian ownership, a CFO departure, and a regulator-gated cash engine keep this below conviction-buy territory until a clean cat season and a couple of combined-entity quarters confirm the diversification thesis. The prior cycle of HOLD → BUY → HOLD reflects this: the May BUY was issued near the highs around $37-38, and the subsequent pullback to ~$35 improves the risk/reward at the margin, but the open structural questions are unchanged.

## Company Overview

Radian now operates two reportable segments:

**Mortgage (Radian Guaranty)** — the legacy core. A leading U.S. private mortgage insurer providing credit protection on low-down-payment first-lien residential loans, with $74.7B risk-in-force (RIF) as of 3/31/26. High-margin and cash-generative, generating ~$221M adjusted pretax operating income. Risk-to-capital is just 10.2:1 against a 25:1 regulatory limit.

**Specialty (Inigo)** — acquired primarily with cash plus 646,014 Radian shares. Inigo writes property, casualty, financial lines, and other specialty insurance/reinsurance globally through Lloyd's Syndicate 1301 (launched 2021), with roughly 80% U.S. risk exposure. In its two-month Q1 stub it contributed $164M net premiums earned at an 85.3% combined ratio, already 41% of consolidated net premiums earned.

The strategic logic is non-correlation: pair the U.S. housing-credit cycle with a global specialty/cat cycle to smooth through-the-cycle earnings and allow dynamic capital allocation. The trade-off is that Radian exchanged a simple, highly profitable, well-understood monoline for a more complex, more volatile, harder-to-value combination.

## Financial Analysis

**Consolidated Q1 2026 (first quarter with Inigo, two months only):**
- Total revenues $466M, +58% YoY
- Net income from continuing operations $129M ($0.93 diluted EPS), down from $152M ($1.03) YoY — depressed by ~$22M one-time acquisition costs, ~$23M purchase-accounting (VOBA) amortization, and intangible amortization
- Adjusted diluted operating EPS $1.27, +22% YoY
- Adjusted operating ROE 14.7%, up ~130bps
- Book value per share $35.67, +10% YoY

The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap of roughly $0.34/share is the single most important interpretive point in the quarter. A standardized data pull shows declining headline net income and EPS and would misread the franchise; the underlying operating trajectory is positive. The four-quarter beat record (2.2%, 8.1%, 4.9%, 2.9%) supports confidence in forward estimates.

**Multi-year context:** Net income ~$583M (FY2025) vs ~$604M (FY2024); revenue ~$1.20B vs $1.29B. Net margin ~49%, ROE ~12%, D/E ~0.7. The Altman Z of 2.15 is normal for an insurer balance-sheet structure and is not a distress signal.

**Mortgage fundamentals (healthy):**
- NIW $13.5B, +42% YoY, with refinance share jumping to 21% from 4% as rates eased
- Persistency 81.3%; roughly half of IIF carries note rates ≤5.5%, insulating the book from refinance churn
- Default rate improved to 2.51%; cures (13,737) exceeded new defaults (13,584); $36M favorable prior-year reserve development
- ~95% of NIW at ≥680 FICO

**Capital structure:** Total assets rose from $8.1B to $10.7B; loss reserves grew from $400M to $1.82B (added ~$1.4B of specialty reserves). Goodwill is minimal ($5M), but $419M of intangibles plus $166M net VOBA were recognized. Holdco leverage rose to 20.2% (from 18.3%) on acquisition financing, with management targeting below 20% by year-end 2026. The investment portfolio now stands at $7.1B. A $450M senior note matures in March 2027; management plans to refinance late-2026/early-2027 and is comfortable in high-teens leverage. This is a manageable maturity given the cash-generation profile but worth monitoring against the rate backdrop.

**Capital return:** Share count is down ~17% since 2022 (168M→135M); $115M repurchased YTD through April 2026, with $200-250M of full-year buyback capacity guided. Radian Guaranty is expected to upstream $600M+ in dividends to the holdco in 2026. Quarterly dividend is $0.255/share (~3% yield).

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two engines. The mortgage book is benefiting from a refinance pickup without sacrificing the embedded value of the rate-locked back book — half of IIF sits below 5.5%, so refinance activity is concentrated in newer vintages while the high-margin legacy book persists. This is a favorable dynamic, though it means lower rates that accelerate refinancing would eventually erode the in-force book.

The specialty engine is the new variable. Inigo adds globally diversified premium. The question is durability: Inigo is a young (2021) franchise bought into a softening property-cat pricing market, and its discipline ("profitability over volume") is unproven under Radian ownership through a full cycle.

Estimate revisions are the clearest near-term positive. Current-quarter consensus rose +13.1% over 90 days (+8.6% in 30 days), current-year EPS rose +10.3% over 90 days to $5.31, and next-year sits at $5.51. Rising estimates against a flat-to-down stock price is the textbook setup where consensus is catching up to a fundamental change. The revision magnitude (roughly +10-13% over 90 days at the quarter level) is meaningful but falls just short of the >30% velocity that would mark a clean earnings inflection, and the driver here is partly the mechanical addition of Inigo premium rather than organic acceleration. We weight it as a genuine positive without treating it as the strongest possible buy signal.

We score growth prospects 6.3 — the most reliable dimension for sorting outcomes, and here it supports the thesis: two engines, rising estimates, a 22% adjusted operating EPS gain. It is held back from a higher mark by the dependence on an unproven specialty franchise and the cyclical sensitivity of both books.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$35.23, RDN trades at roughly 0.99x book and ~6.9x annualized adjusted operating EPS. On forward consensus of $5.31 (current year) the forward P/E is ~6.6x, with a trailing P/E of ~8.0x on $4.39 trailing EPS. Against P&C peers — RLI 12.6x, SIGI 12.6x, MCY 6.5x, AGO 8.7x, PLMR 16.1x (peer median 12.6x) — Radian sits well below the median and near the low end alongside Mercury and Assured Guaranty.

The relevant comparison set, however, is mortgage insurers (MGIC, Essent, Arch, Enact, National MI), which structurally trade near or modestly above book on mid-teens ROEs. Radian at ~book on a ~15% adjusted operating ROE is not screamingly cheap relative to that cohort, but it is reasonable, and the discount to the broader P&C median is partly explained by the unproven specialty mix and the dividend-gating issue rather than pure mispricing.

The bull framing is that book value compounds (+10% YoY), buybacks at sub-book are accretive, and a successful integration re-rates the multiple toward the diversified-specialty cohort. The RBC Outperform with a $47 target (~34% upside), consensus mean $43.17, and range $37-48 sit well above current price. On an inflection-with-rising-estimates name trading below ~15x, those targets function more as floors than ceilings, and analysts should revise upward as combined quarters print.

The bear framing is that the discount is earned: catastrophe volatility, soft specialty pricing, and a regulator-gated cash engine justify a structurally lower multiple than a clean monoline. Cheapness alone is not a buy signal; the record on low-multiple names is that they often stay cheap absent a catalyst. Here the catalyst (integration proof + clean cat season) is identifiable but not yet delivered. We score valuation 6.0 — modestly constructive given the specific re-rating path and book-value support, but not a high mark, because the discount reflects real structural questions, not just market neglect.

## Competitive Landscape

**Mortgage:** Competes with five other private MIs plus FHA/VA and potential GSE-sponsored alternatives. Radian differentiates on proprietary data/analytics and decades of underwriting history. The industry has enjoyed a favorable credit cycle (strong home-price appreciation, low defaults, high-quality post-GFC underwriting). The rate-locked back book is a structural advantage supporting persistency and embedded value. Regulatory developments are roughly neutral: FHFA's move toward VantageScore 4.0 / FICO 10T is expected to be operational but not financially material, and March 2026 Basel III proposals maintain favorable capital treatment of mortgage insurance.

**Specialty/Lloyd's:** Inigo competes in a global specialty market in a softening pricing phase after several strong, benign-cat years, with intensifying competition from traditional reinsurers and alternative capital (cat bonds). Inigo's claimed edge is disciplined, data-driven underwriting — credible but unproven through a full cycle under Radian.

The competitive-position trajectory is the dimension where we are most cautious. The transformation trades a strong, well-understood monoline moat for a more complex platform whose specialty leg has not been tested under the new owner. The Q1 adverse development on Inigo's 2021-2022 underwriting-year general liability/casualty signals that the full risk profile of the acquisition is not yet understood, which argues for patience. We score competitive position 5.8, consistent with the recent downward drift in this dimension, reflecting a deteriorating clarity of moat during transition rather than an actual loss of share in the core book.

## Risk Assessment

The most consequential company-specific risks, several of them newly introduced by the transformation:

1. **Catastrophe & volatility (NEW).** The Specialty segment adds U.S. wind, earthquake, aviation war, political violence, and cyber exposure absent from the monoline. Q1 benefited from a benign cat environment; a major season could create material earnings volatility. Mitigated by a $580M cat-bond limit and excess-of-loss programs, but unproven under Radian.

2. **Intercompany Note / dividend restriction (NEW, material).** Radian Guaranty lent $600M to the holdco (10-yr, 6.5%) to help fund Inigo. As a condition, all Radian Guaranty dividends now require prior Pennsylvania Insurance Department approval for three years (up to five), plus a $500M minimum surplus floor. This reduced the PMIERs cushion and makes the holdco's buyback/dividend/debt-service capacity dependent on a regulator approving distributions from the cash engine. This is the most underappreciated risk in the structure.

3. **Soft specialty pricing cycle (timing).** Management repeatedly flagged softening property and property-cat reinsurance rates; they bought into a softening market.

4. **Integration & execution.** First international operations, Lloyd's regulatory regime (Solvency UK, FAL requirements), FX exposure, and Inigo's ICFR excluded from the 2026 internal-control assessment. Inigo runs as a standalone unit, which preserves culture but complicates control integration.

5. **Mortgage claim severity creeping higher.** Average direct claim paid rose to $61.7K from $39.3K YoY on higher loan balances and a fading HPA mitigation benefit. Still favorable versus pre-COVID coverage levels, but a watch item.

6. **Persistency erosion if rates fall** would shrink the high-margin in-force book.

7. **C-suite churn.** CEO Thornberry retires December 2026; CFO Sumita Pandit departed (separation agreement Feb 2026), with Daniel Kobell as interim CFO. A cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks corroborates broad transition.

We score risk factors 5.2. These are largely idiosyncratic, several are newly minted by the deal, and the dividend-gating mechanism in particular is not obviously priced. This is below a neutral mark because the transformation front-loads execution and structural risk before the diversification benefits are proven.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface is distorted by an imminent expiry artifact. Near-term ATM IV reads 195.5% on the 1-day-to-expiry tenor — a single-expiry noise spike, not a fundamental signal, given the 1-month tenor sits at a far more normal 37.4% and the 6-month at 25.0% versus 24.0% one-year historical vol. The term structure is technically in backwardation because of that front spike, but the 3- and 6-month tenors do not price meaningful stress. The 1-month put/call skew of 6.2% indicates modestly elevated put demand, and the 6-month skew is mildly negative (-2.8%), so there is no structural collapse being priced. Short interest is low (3.5% of float, 2.9 days to cover). The options market is not flagging a fundamental warning that contradicts the constructive lean; the headline IV is mechanical.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$47, RBC target / ~+34%):** Inigo integrates cleanly, a benign-to-normal cat season validates the diversification, specialty pricing stabilizes, and adjusted operating ROE holds in the mid-teens. Book value compounds at ~10%, sub-book buybacks ($200-250M/yr) are accretive, and the multiple re-rates toward the diversified-specialty cohort (~1.2-1.4x book). The incoming CEO's $5.77M open-market purchase at ~$34 signals personal conviction in this path. Estimate revisions continue upward as combined quarters print.

**Bear Case (~$28-30 / -15-20%):** A major cat season exposes specialty earnings volatility, soft pricing pressures Inigo margins, adverse development on the 2021-2022 casualty years continues, and the PA Insurance Department restricts Radian Guaranty dividends, throttling holdco capital return. The market keeps the stock at or below book on complexity and execution risk, and the 2027 note refinancing lands at a less favorable rate. The multiple compresses rather than re-rates.

**Base Case (~$40 / ~+14%):** Integration proceeds without major surprise, a normal cat season produces some volatility but no impairment, mortgage stays pristine, and the stock re-rates modestly toward ~1.1x book / ~7.5-8x forward EPS as consensus catches up to the combined entity. Math: ~$5.31 current-year EPS × ~7.5x ≈ $40, broadly consistent with ~1.1x a compounding book value.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, anchored to (a) the next two quarters confirming combined-entity earnings power and Inigo combined-ratio stability, (b) the 2026 Atlantic hurricane season outcome, and (c) the late-2026/early-2027 senior-note refinancing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive clean combined quarters with Inigo combined ratio holding ≤90% and no further adverse development on 2021-2022 casualty years
- A normal-to-benign cat season passes without material specialty impairment, plus confirmed PA Insurance Department approval of planned Radian Guaranty dividends
- Continued estimate revisions and the stock holding sub-1.1x book — i.e., re-rating runway intact while fundamentals confirm

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A cat event or specialty reserve charge that drives the combined Specialty combined ratio above ~105% for a quarter, or material adverse development beyond the 2021-2022 vintages
- Regulator restriction or denial of Radian Guaranty dividend upstreaming that forces a buyback/dividend cut
- Mortgage default rate breaking back above ~3.5% with rising claim severity, signaling a housing-credit turn

## Conclusion

Radian is a financially sound company executing a genuine strategic change, trading at roughly book value with rising estimates, a clean legacy book, and a notable insider buy from the incoming CEO. Those are real positives. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the transformation front-loads risk: catastrophe exposure that has never been tested under Radian, a specialty franchise bought into a softening cycle and showing early adverse development, a cash engine whose distributions are now gated by a state regulator for three to five years, and a simultaneous CEO and CFO transition. The market's near-book discount is partly mispricing and partly an earned reflection of these unknowns.

The recommendation history (HOLD → BUY → HOLD) and the recent score drift down to 6.1 reflect a fair conclusion: the May BUY was issued near the highs, and the pullback to ~$35 improves entry, but no new information has resolved the structural questions. We classify this as a growth-oriented HOLD: there is a credible 12-month appreciation path to ~$40 base / ~$47 bull, but it depends on integration proof and a clean cat season that have not yet arrived. We would add on confirmation rather than on cheapness alone. For existing holders, the ~3% yield, sub-book buybacks, and rising estimates support continued ownership; new capital can wait for the upgrade triggers or a pullback toward the low-$30s.
