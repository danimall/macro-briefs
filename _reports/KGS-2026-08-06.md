## Executive Summary

Kodiak Gas Services runs the largest U.S. contract natural-gas compression fleet — 4,477,398 fleet horsepower across 4,670 units at 3/31/2026, roughly 80% of it large horsepower and 82.8% of it in the Permian and Eagle Ford. The operating business is performing at or near its practical ceiling. Fleet utilization hit a record 98.0% in Q1 2026, up from 96.9% a year earlier. Contract Services adjusted gross margin expanded from 65.7% (FY2024) to 68.4% (FY2025) to 70.6% in Q1 2026. Adjusted EBITDA grew 17.3% in FY2025 against 12.8% revenue growth, which is genuine operating leverage rather than mix noise. The contract structure explains much of the margin level: customers supply fuel gas at no cost to Kodiak, reimburse ad valorem and sales taxes, pay mobilization charges, and pay a fixed monthly fee independent of throughput, with the majority of contracts carrying annual escalators.

On April 1, 2026 Kodiak closed the $726.3M acquisition of Distributed Power Solutions (rebranded Kodiak Power Solutions), adding behind-the-meter power generation for data-center, microgrid and industrial customers, and began reporting a third segment in Q2. On July 8, 2026 it announced a multi-year agreement under which Baker Hughes will supply gas turbines and generators, a framework for up to 1.8 GW anchored by an initial award enabling roughly 1 GW of capacity by 2030. The commercial roles matter here: Baker Hughes is the equipment vendor, Kodiak is the buyer and asset owner, and Kodiak's data-center and industrial customers are the parties who would pay for power. No revenue accrues from the Baker Hughes agreement and it confirms no customer.

That gap between secured turbine access and undisclosed customer economics is the whole investment question. Not one filing read discloses Kodiak Power Solutions' standalone revenue or EBITDA (the ASC 805 purchase-price allocation was explicitly incomplete at the May 11 10-Q), nor the tenor, take-or-pay structure, pricing or counterparty credit quality of any power contract. The observable trend lines have softened: Q1 2026 revenue grew only 4.9%, consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut 5.5% in 30 days, next-quarter estimates are down 7.5% over 90 days, and pro forma leverage sits near 4.0x against a power capex ambition of 300-500 MW per year. The stock is 23.4% below its 52-week high of $77.68 and still 98% above its 52-week low. We stay at HOLD, classified as uncertain, with the Q2 print — the first quarter of Power Infrastructure segment reporting — as the resolving event.

## Company Overview

Kodiak IPO'd in July 2023, is headquartered in The Woodlands, Texas, and employed roughly 1,300 people at FY2025 year-end. It consolidated CSI Compressco on April 1, 2024 and its sponsor, EQT AB, exited fully during 2025 (43.1% ownership at the start of the year to zero by December 2). Kodiak itself repurchased more than 2.7M shares directly from EQT affiliates; the secondary offerings generated no proceeds to the company.

Three revenue lines as of Q2 2026: Compression Infrastructure (formerly Contract Services, 90.3% of FY2025 revenue at 68.4% adjusted gross margin), Power Infrastructure (new), and Other Services — the aftermarket parts business inherited from CSI Compressco, $126.8M of FY2025 revenue at a 16.1% adjusted gross margin, shrinking 4.7% year over year. Other Services is a margin-dilutive appendage and its decline is not a concern.

Unit count is falling while horsepower rises (units down 6.6% in FY2025 and 5.5% year over year in Q1 2026; horsepower per unit 970 to 977). Retiring small units and adding large ones reduces the number of physical machines requiring service per revenue dollar, which is mechanically margin-accretive and consistent with the observed gross margin path.

Two qualifications on the growth optics. First, CSI Compressco contributed nine months to FY2024 and twelve to FY2025, so part of the 12.8% FY2025 revenue increase is the acquisition annualizing rather than organic expansion; the disclosures reviewed do not permit isolating the organic component. Second, Q1 2026 revenue growth of 4.9% is the cleanest read on underlying momentum and it is materially slower than the two prior years.

**Cash flow quality is the central judgment.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $599.7M against net income of $81.6M, with D&A of $276.2M bridging most of the gap. Capex split: growth $199.5M, other $62.8M, maintenance $76.0M ($338.3M total). Maintenance capex is therefore 27.5% of D&A. If compression assets genuinely last 30-plus years, GAAP depreciation overstates economic consumption and reported earnings understate cash earning power. If maintenance is understated — and the $62.8M "other" line is unallocated in what we reviewed — true sustaining capex is higher and cash coverage is thinner. This is unresolvable from the filings and it determines whether the dividend is income or partial return of capital.

The distribution arithmetic supports treating the question seriously. FY2025 dividends of $159.6M plus buybacks of $104.0M totaled $263.6M against GAAP net income of $81.6M. Retained earnings fell from $178.1M (FY2023) to $93.6M (FY2024) to $12.8M (FY2025) and to a $12.4M accumulated deficit at 3/31/2026. On the company's own metrics, dividend coverage is 2.9x on Discretionary Cash Flow ($461.7M) and 1.44x on company-defined free cash flow ($229.6M). In Q1 2026, company-defined FCF of $37.0M did not cover the $42.6M of dividends paid, driven by a $69.1M working-capital build (receivables +$41.9M) and elevated growth capex. One quarter of under-coverage in a deployment quarter is not alarming; it does show how thin the FCF cushion becomes when capex accelerates.

**The balance sheet's best feature is timing.** Total debt at 3/31/2026 was $2,849.9M (ABL $449.9M; 5.875% 2031 notes $1,000.0M; 6.50% 2033 notes $770.0M; 6.75% 2035 notes $630.0M). The March 2026 refinancing issued $1.0B of 5.875% 2031 notes and defeased the $750M 7.25% 2029 notes at a $36.5M extinguishment loss ($27.2M cash premium plus $7.8M non-cash write-off plus $1.5M accrued interest). Saving 137.5bp on $750M is roughly $10.3M annually against a $27.2M premium, a 2.6-year payback against five years of remaining benefit. Blended note coupon post-refi is 6.31%; including the ABL, roughly 6.21%. There are no maturities before 2030 (ABL September 5, 2030; notes 2031/2033/2035), and liquidity was ~$1.6B.

FY2025 gross leverage was 3.66x ($2,614.6M average-basis debt over $715.0M EBITDA) with interest coverage of 3.60x ($715.0M over $198.4M). Pro forma for the $587.3M cash paid at the DPS close against $94.4M of cash on hand, debt is approximately $3.34B, or roughly 4.0x the $840M FY2026 guidance midpoint against a 5.25x covenant ceiling — implying about $1.07B of incremental debt capacity, which binds before the $1.5B of nominal revolver availability. The April 2 Fifth Amendment temporarily permitted deducting the 2031 note proceeds from total indebtedness through June 30, 2026. This is most plausibly a bridge accommodation covering the window when both the new proceeds and the not-yet-redeemed 2029 notes sat on the balance sheet, with the notes redeemed April 10. It does mean management sought relief rather than relying on existing baskets.

Cash taxes are near zero (Q1 2026 cash taxes paid: $54 thousand; effective rate 13.4% versus 25.3%), attributed to restored full expensing of qualified capex under the July 2025 tax legislation. This is a timing shield building deferred tax liabilities ($125.5M at 3/31/26), not permanent tax alpha.

The FY2025 Altman Z-Score of 1.17 and sub-1.0 current ratio are poorly calibrated here: low asset turnover (0.30) is intrinsic to owning $4.6B of gross equipment, and the current ratio is suppressed by pre-billing customers ($92.4M of contract liabilities), which is cash-flow positive. Screen mis-calibration does not make the debt smaller. Roughly 4.0x leverage with 3.6x coverage is real capital-structure risk; what the screens overstate is *timing* risk, which the term-out has largely pre-empted.

**Unresolved share count.** The Q1 10-Q cover reports 88,771,027 shares outstanding as of May 6, 2026. The market data used here reflects 99,334,407 shares. The ~10.6M-share gap is most plausibly a mid-2026 equity offering into post-deal strength (~$68-70 in May), which would raise roughly $650-750M and reduce pro forma leverage toward 3.1-3.2x. No filing reviewed confirms an issuance. Enterprise value is fairly robust either way, approximately $8.6-9.0B, so the valuation conclusion is more stable than the leverage conclusion, but per-share metrics swing about 11% and this must be resolved from the Q2 filing.

## Growth Analysis

Compression growth is now structurally capped on the volume axis. Utilization cannot rise from 98.0%. Incremental growth must come from three sources: annual contract escalators repricing a fully absorbed fleet (the primary driver of the 65.7% to 70.6% margin path); new-build horsepower, which carries long lead times at roughly $2,200-2,500 per horsepower by industry convention; and buying existing fleets. The March 2026 purchase of more than 20,000 horsepower from a Permian producer, alongside a $24.0M used-asset line within growth capex, implies roughly $1,200 per horsepower if those are the same transaction — about half new-build cost, and the highest-return growth path available. Whether that recurs is worth tracking; it depends on producers continuing to elect outsourcing, which is a cycle variable rather than a permanent preference.

Contracted visibility is solid but should not be mistaken for backlog. Contract Services remaining performance obligations at 3/31/2026 totaled $1,632.9M (rest of 2026 $689.4M; 2027 $594.8M; 2028 $251.1M; then $63.2M, $23.6M, $10.7M), plus $31.4M of Other Services obligations due by year-end. RPO excludes the 9.0% of revenue-generating horsepower on month-to-month terms and excludes power entirely. Rest-of-2026 RPO covers roughly 75% of nine months of Contract Services revenue at the Q1 annualized run rate — good coverage, not a full-revenue backlog.

Power is where the growth case now lives, and it is the least verified part of the story. Management's FY2026 guidance of $820-860M adjusted EBITDA and $520-570M discretionary cash flow, plus a 300-500 MW per year target through 2030, is sourced to secondary reporting quoting the CFO on the Q1 call rather than to a primary filing. Taking the $840M midpoint at face value implies +17.5% over FY2025 and roughly $80M of incremental contribution from power plus compression growth over nine months of ownership. If power contributes $80-100M annualized, the $726.3M purchase price implies roughly 7-9x EV/EBITDA — a reasonable entry multiple, but this is inference from guidance arithmetic, not a disclosed figure, and it could be materially wrong depending on how much of the raise is compression.

The capital intensity gap is the most under-appreciated item. Behind-the-meter gas generation typically runs $1.0-1.6M per installed MW by industry convention; at $1.2M/MW, 300-500 MW annually implies $360-600M of annual growth capex against total FY2025 capex of $338.3M. Disclosed power commitments are only $244.8M over roughly four years plus $18.0M of power capex incurred in Q1. That gap cannot be reconciled from the filings. Either the pace is back-end loaded, customers pre-fund equipment, structures such as JVs or leases keep spend off balance sheet, or the program will consume the covenant headroom within roughly two years absent equity. The Baker Hughes agreement helps on one dimension the market may be underweighting: it is described as a "multi-year rolling agreement" providing "flexibility to align capacity commitments with evolving data center demand," meaning Kodiak's purchase commitments are not firm. That meaningfully limits stranded-capital risk.

Consensus is moving the wrong way. Current-quarter EPS estimates fell 5.5% in 30 days to $0.71; next-quarter fell 4.0% in 30 days and 7.5% in 90 days to $0.64; current-year fell 3.0% in 90 days to $2.62. Next-year is the one bright spot, up 3.9% in 30 days to $3.08 though still down 6.8% over 90 days. The estimate-revision signal that would justify a BUY — sustained double-digit upward revisions alongside accelerating revenue — is absent here. Revenue growth of 4.9% and net negative revisions point the other way.

## Valuation Assessment

The vendor forward P/E of 78.7x is a data artifact: it equals the trailing P/E and applies trailing GAAP EPS of $0.89. On consensus adjusted EPS, KGS trades at 22.7x current-year ($2.62) and 19.3x next-year ($3.08). On FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS of $1.54 it is 38.7x.

The more appropriate lens for a levered asset owner is enterprise value. At $59.54 with ~99.3M shares, equity value is ~$5.91B. Adding pro forma net debt — ~$3.25B if no equity was raised, or ~$2.6-2.7B if the share-count gap reflects an offering used to repay the ABL — gives EV of roughly $8.6-9.2B. That is:

- 12.0-12.9x FY2025 actual adjusted EBITDA of $715.0M
- **10.2-11.0x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $840M**
- Dividend yield of 3.3% ($1.96 annualized)

Against peers, the closest true comparable is Archrock at a 19.6x P/E and 2.5% yield with a lower growth profile; the broader equipment-and-services median P/E is also 19.6x, with wide dispersion (OII 11.8x, WFRD 15.8x, WHD 55.0x, NOV 85.3x). KGS at 19.3x next-year consensus is essentially at the peer median on earnings and above Archrock on yield-adjusted terms. That is defensible for the higher-margin, higher-utilization asset base and the power optionality, but it is not a mispricing.

Two adjustments cut the other way and are invisible in EV/EBITDA. First, roughly $66-71M of residual Texas sales-and-use tax liability (the contingent balance moved $70.1M at 12/31/24 to $102.3M at 12/31/25 to $66.0M at 3/31/26, with ~$37M of cash apparently paid in Q1) is a near-term cash claim excluded from adjusted EBITDA and from the discretionary cash flow guidance — roughly 40% of one year's dividend. Second, the unresolved maintenance-capex question means the discount rate applied to reported cash flow should be higher than for a business where sustaining capex is cleanly disclosed.

Netting this: 10.4x on roughly $900M of FY2027 EBITDA, less ~$2.75B of net debt and the residual tax claim, produces a base fair value near **$67** — about 13% above the current price. Sell-side targets (low $72, mean $83.53, high $93, with all 15 covering analysts at Buy or Strong Buy and zero holds) sit well above that. On a stock with rising estimates and a sub-20x multiple, consensus targets often act as floors. Here estimates are falling and no analyst has moved to hold through a 23% drawdown, which makes those targets more likely to be cut than met. We treat the $72-93 band as the published range rather than as independent confirmation.

Valuation scores 5.0: modestly improved from the prior review because the price fell while next-year estimates rose 3.9% over the trailing 30 days, but not to the point of asymmetry, because the compression is only partially fundamental and the power multiple rests on undisclosed economics.

## Competitive Landscape

Compression is a consolidating, capacity-constrained oligopoly, and the evidence sits in the filings rather than in commentary. A 98.0% utilization rate combined with a limited number of compression-package manufacturers and no long-term supply contracts means new horsepower cannot be added quickly and existing horsepower is fully absorbed. Escalators convert that tightness into realized price. Derived revenue per revenue-generating horsepower rose from roughly $271 per year in FY2025 to roughly $280 annualized on Q1 2026 — about 3.3%, and a sensitivity rather than a forecast, though compression revenue is relatively non-seasonal given fixed monthly fees.

Kodiak's specific advantages are the cost pass-throughs (fuel, property and sales tax, mobilization), basin density in the two best associated-gas plays, asset ownership and residual value retention, and a technician pipeline — the in-house BEARS Academy graduated 270-plus employees from a three-week program in 2025 with a larger Midland facility opening in summer 2026. In a market where the 10-K names Permian technician scarcity as a specific risk, that pipeline is an unglamorous but real moat. The offset is mechanical availability guarantees of 95-98%, with three consecutive months of failure creating a customer termination right.

The named competitive risk is customer vertical integration: producers electing to own and operate compression rather than contract it. This is the mirror image of the used-fleet purchases, and it is the same variable read in reverse.

In power, the transferability of these advantages is only partial. The competitive set includes oilfield-service entrants, dedicated power-solutions providers, large mobile-generation and rental companies, and independent power producers, several with more capital and longer power-market track records. The counterparty profile also changes materially: compression customers are investment-grade S&P 500 E&Ps, while data-center power counterparties range from hyperscalers with pristine credit to speculative colocation developers with none. Whether Kodiak's power contracts are long-term, take-or-pay, and with creditworthy counterparties is the decisive question for whether the segment deserves a compression-like multiple, and it is exactly what is not disclosed.

Competitive position scores 6.5 — the strongest dimension, driven by an improving trajectory in the core franchise (utilization, margin, per-horsepower pricing all rising), tempered by an unproven position in the new segment.

## Risk Assessment

**Power capital intensity against covenant headroom.** The most consequential risk. A 300-500 MW annual program at industry-convention cost implies $360-600M of annual growth capex against roughly $1.07B of incremental debt capacity at 4.0x leverage and $174-193M of annual dividends. Something adjusts: capex pacing, the dividend, leverage, or equity. The flexible Baker Hughes commitment structure is the mitigant.

**Mexico FCPA/OFAC/DOJ matter — unquantified.** An internal investigation determined that certain payments made in Mexico before Kodiak acquired the business through CSI Compressco were likely made to persons associated with an organization designated a Foreign Terrorist Organization and Specially Designated Global Terrorist. Kodiak self-reported to the DOJ, OFAC and the SEC, states the aggregate amount was not material and the apparent purpose was employee safety and site access under threat, and sold the Mexico operations on September 30, 2025 at a $33.3M loss on $34.3M of net assets. Material-support exposure is a more serious statutory category than ordinary books-and-records issues, and no reserve is disclosed, which typically means no loss estimate is yet possible.

**Texas sales tax.** Roughly $66-71M of remaining cash outflow, outside adjusted EBITDA and outside the guidance framework. Now largely quantified, converting an open contingency into a scheduled payment.

**Concentration.** 82.8% of compression assets in the Permian and Eagle Ford. Top four customers were 31% of Q1 2026 revenue, 32% in FY2025, 32% in FY2024, 33% in FY2023, with one customer above 10% in each of the last three years. Power diversifies *demand drivers* (data-center load is uncorrelated with Permian oil economics) but does not yet diversify *counterparties*, and an early-stage power business is typically more concentrated at inception, not less. With investment-grade E&P counterparties, 32% concentration is a negotiating-leverage issue more than a credit issue.

**Regulatory tailwind reversibility.** The FY2025 10-K documents an unusually favorable environment — Congressional disapproval of the methane waste-emissions charge, proposed Subpart W suspension to reporting year 2034, extended Subpart OOOO deadlines, rescission of the 2009 GHG endangerment finding. These reduce near-term compliance cost and are administration-dependent.

**Governance.** A classified board with three staggered classes, restrictions on director removal and special meetings, board authority to amend bylaws and to issue blank-check preferred without a shareholder vote. These were sponsor-era provisions that now persist without a controlling holder. A May 7, 2026 8-K carried both an articles/bylaws amendment item and a shareholder-vote item; that filing was not reviewed and may represent declassification.

**Supplier concentration.** A limited number of compression-package manufacturers with no long-term supply contracts; power adds turbine dependence, partially addressed by the Baker Hughes framework.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning has shifted decisively toward near-term protection into the Q2 print. One-month ATM implied volatility is 63.4% against 38.4% trailing realized — a 25-point premium at the 81st percentile of the current chain — with a pronounced backwardation (63.4% at one month versus 51.6% at three and 44.4% at six). Put/call skew is elevated at 14.3% in the front month but flips to -0.3% at three months and -7.9% at six. The nearest expiry prices a ±10.7% move. Since the July 19 snapshot, ATM IV rose 9.5 points (53.9% to 63.4%) while the price fell 11.3%, and front-month put volume has been heavy. Open interest, however, remains call-tilted at a 0.30 put/call ratio, indicating the standing book is long-biased while recent flow hedges the event.

The market is pricing an imminent, event-specific dislocation without repricing the longer-dated distribution. That is consistent with a genuine binary at the Q2 report — first Power Infrastructure segment disclosure, the ASC 805 allocation, and any confirmation of an equity issuance — rather than a structural change in view. Short interest of 9.5% of float at 3.6 days to cover is moderate and neither confirms nor contradicts. For a HOLD, rich front-month vol argues against adding into the print and makes waiting for post-print disclosure the low-cost course.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$82, +38%).** Q2 discloses a Power Infrastructure segment with long-tenor, take-or-pay contracts with creditworthy counterparties, validating the roughly 7-9x implied entry multiple on DPS and confirming the 300-500 MW annual pathway. The share-count gap resolves as an equity raise at $68-70 that took pro forma leverage to 3.1-3.2x, removing the funding constraint on the power buildout. Compression continues to convert 98% utilization into 3%-plus annual per-horsepower escalation while used-fleet purchases at roughly half new-build cost add horsepower at high returns. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands at or above the $860M top of guidance and FY2027 reaches $940M-plus, supporting 11.5x. The Texas tax matter closes at the accrued amount and the Mexico matter resolves without a material penalty.

**Bear case (~$42, -29%).** Power turns out to be a merchant or short-tenor business with developer counterparties, and the first segment disclosure shows sub-scale EBITDA against $360-600M of required annual capex. Leverage runs toward the 5.25x covenant as capex accelerates, forcing a dividend reset or a dilutive equity raise at a de-rated multiple. Compression revenue growth stays near the Q1 pace of 4.9% as utilization has no room to rise, estimate revisions continue lower, and the multiple compresses toward 8.5x on $820M of EBITDA. The Mexico matter produces a quantified penalty, and higher scrutiny of sustaining capex forces the market to discount the reported cash flow.

**Base case (~$67, +13%).** Power ramps more slowly than the headline megawatt targets but contributes real EBITDA; compression escalators and selective used-fleet purchases deliver high-single-digit EBITDA growth; leverage stabilizes in the mid-3s; the dividend holds. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $900M at 10.4x EV/EBITDA, net of ~$2.75B pro forma net debt and the residual Texas tax claim, yields approximately $67 per share.

The score trajectory has declined from 6.3 in May to 5.4, and this is the fifth consecutive HOLD. Current data neither reverses nor accelerates that decline: valuation improved as the price fell while next-year estimates rose 3.9% over 30 days, but risk deteriorated as leverage rose pro forma for DPS, front-month volatility repriced sharply higher, and revenue growth decelerated to 4.9%. Growth prospects step down from 6.3 to 6.0 on the revenue deceleration and net negative revisions, and management quality from 5.3 to 5.0 on the continued absence of any open-market insider purchase through a 23% drawdown while several executives sold. The composite is unchanged at 5.4 with a different composition.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** immediate. Q2 2026 results were pending as of the information cutoff and constitute the first Power Infrastructure segment disclosure plus, potentially, the completed ASC 805 allocation and a share-count reconciliation. The next natural checkpoint after that is the Q3 report, which provides the first sequential read on power.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. Power segment disclosure showing contracted, multi-year, take-or-pay capacity with named or credit-characterized counterparties and disclosed segment EBITDA supporting an entry multiple below 10x on the $726.3M purchase price.
2. Confirmation that the ~10.6M-share gap reflects an equity issuance whose proceeds reduced pro forma leverage to 3.3x or below, restoring capacity to fund 300-500 MW annually without a dividend or covenant constraint.
3. A price near $48-50 (roughly 8.7x FY2027E EBITDA of $900M) with FY2026 guidance reaffirmed at or above $840M — the level at which the base case offers 35%-plus upside against a defined bear case.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $800M, or the power segment disclosed at materially sub-scale EBITDA relative to $726.3M of consideration.
2. Pro forma net leverage above 4.5x, or growth capex guided above $600M annually without a matching disclosed funding plan.
3. A dividend reduction, or two consecutive quarters in which company-defined free cash flow fails to cover the dividend (Q1 2026 was the first).
4. A quantified reserve or settlement in the Mexico OFAC/DOJ matter exceeding roughly $75M, or a debarment-type remedy affecting operations.

**What we are watching in the near term:** whether the used-horsepower purchase pattern (~$1,200/HP versus $2,200-2,500 new-build) recurs; whether Contract Services adjusted gross margin holds above 70%; whether month-to-month horsepower rises from 9.0%; and whether any insider makes an open-market purchase at these levels.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Kodiak's compression franchise is performing about as well as an asset of this type can — record 98.0% utilization, 70.6% adjusted gross margin, cost pass-throughs that transfer fuel and property tax to the counterparty, no debt maturity before 2030, and ~$1.6B of liquidity. That is a genuinely good business, and the March 2026 refinancing and the used-fleet purchases show capital allocation being done competently.

The stock is not obviously mispriced against it. At $59.54 it trades near 10.2-11.0x an unverified FY2026 EBITDA guidance midpoint and 19.3x next-year consensus, roughly at the peer median, with a 3.3% dividend funded substantially out of a depreciation add-back whose relationship to true sustaining capex cannot be resolved from the filings. The upside case rests almost entirely on a power segment whose revenue, EBITDA, contract tenor and counterparty credit quality appear in no filing read, and whose capital requirements at management's own megawatt targets exceed the company's entire FY2025 capex program. The Baker Hughes agreement secures scarce turbine capacity but it is a purchase agreement, not contracted revenue.

Revenue growth has decelerated to 4.9%, consensus estimates are net lower across three of four horizons, the share count reconciliation is open, front-month implied volatility is 25 points above realized with elevated put demand, and no insider has bought a share on the open market through a 23% decline while several executives sold. All 15 covering analysts remain at Buy or Strong Buy with a mean target 40% above spot, which is more likely a sign of stale targets than of independent confirmation. Own it if you own it; new capital should wait for the Q2 disclosure that answers the power question, or for a price near $48-50 where the base case pays for the uncertainty.
