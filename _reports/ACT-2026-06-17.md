**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Enact Holdings is a monoline U.S. private mortgage insurer (PMI) operating as an ~81% subsidiary of Genworth Financial. The business protects lenders against losses on low-down-payment residential mortgages, making it a structurally integral part of the GSE housing-finance chain. The franchise is highly profitable and stable: net income has held in a $666M–$704M band for four consecutive years ($674M in FY2025), net margins run ~55%, and book value per share has compounded from $24.70 (2021) to $40.10 (2025). At $43.13, the stock trades at ~9.3x trailing/forward EPS, broadly in line with PMI peers Essent (8.2x) and NMI (7.5x), with a 2.3% dividend yield.

The central analytical tension is earnings quality. The headline Q1 2026 loss ratio of 15% understates the underlying credit trend because results are still flattered by large favorable prior-year reserve releases ($39M in Q1'26, down from $47M in Q1'25). Current-accident-year provisioning was $79M, the delinquency rate ticked up to 2.61% from 2.34% YoY, and the delinquent loan count rose to 24,670 from 22,349. The reserve-release tailwind that has supported EPS for years is shrinking while new-delinquency dollars grow. This does not threaten solvency (PMIERs sufficiency is 162%, ~$1.92B above required, and the risk-to-capital ratio is 10.0:1 versus a 25:1 regulatory cap) but it does cap forward earnings momentum.

We rate ACT a HOLD. The investment case rests on capital return rather than growth: a dividend raised to $0.24/quarter (+30% over two years), ~6% share-count reduction over the past year, and a fresh $500M buyback authorization ($467.9M remaining). Premium growth is structurally flat as quota-share cession climbs from 16% (2023) to 34% committed (2027), and the Genworth ownership structure limits free float and requires parent sign-off on dividend decisions. We see total-return compounding in the high single digits driven by buybacks and dividends, with limited near-term multiple-expansion catalyst.

## Company Overview

Enact operates through Enact Mortgage Insurance Corporation (EMICO), domiciled in North Carolina, licensed in all 50 states plus DC, and an approved GSE insurer. Its core product allows borrowers putting down less than 20% to access conforming loans by transferring default risk to the insurer, which permits the loan to be sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Enact Re Ltd. (Bermuda) reinsures EMICO's book under quota-share and pursues credit-related reinsurance.

Scale metrics: primary insurance-in-force of ~$272.5B, primary risk-in-force of ~$71.2B, ~944,000 policies, and a market capitalization near $6.0B. The PMI industry is a six-player oligopoly (MGIC, Radian, Essent, Arch MI, National MI, and Enact), competing primarily on proprietary risk-based pricing engines (Enact's is branded Rate360) and underwriting discipline in a low-volume origination market.

The ownership structure is distinctive. Genworth Financial IPO'd 18.4% of Enact in September 2021 and has maintained its ~81% stake. Enact repurchases shares from Genworth alongside open-market buybacks to keep Genworth's ownership percentage constant. Genworth manages Enact's investment portfolio for related-party fees (~$1.9M/quarter), and all Enact dividend decisions require Genworth's consent. Genworth's ~$4.7B Enact stake exceeds Genworth's own market capitalization, making Enact the parent's primary value engine.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is consistent and high-margin.** Net income progression: $547M (2021), $704M (2022), $666M (2023), $688M (2024), $674M (2025). Net margin runs ~55%. Q1 2026 net income was $167.8M (+1% YoY) with diluted EPS of $1.21 ($1.21 actual vs. $1.18 estimate, a 2.4% beat), supported by net investment income up 12% to $70.9M and lower expenses.

**Revenue is mature and slow-growth.** Q1'26 premiums of $242.9M were down 1% YoY, pressured by two structural forces. First, rising ceded premiums: the quota-share ceding percentage has climbed from 16.1% (2023) to 27.2% (2025), with 34.25% committed for 2027. This generates ~$1.94B of PMIERs required-asset credit and stabilizes the risk-to-capital ratio, but it mechanically suppresses net premium growth. Second, lapse-driven rate decline as older, higher-rate policies run off. Investment income is now the primary earnings growth engine, with reinvestment yields at 4.5% pre-tax on a $6.1B portfolio (99% investment grade, 4.9-year duration).

**Credit is the trend to watch.** The loss ratio rose from ~4% (FY2024) toward 11–12% (FY2025) and 15% in Q1 2026 (vs. 12% in Q1'25). As the 2020–2023 vintages season and home-price appreciation slows, the magnitude of releases will shrink, pushing the reported loss ratio up toward the underlying accrual. Delinquency counts are already rising. A headline read of stable net income misses this dynamic: the bottom line looks steady while the loss accrual deteriorates.

**The balance sheet is fortress-like.** PMIERs available assets are $5.0B versus $3.1B required. Leverage is modest: a single $750M 6.25% senior note due 2029, a $435M undrawn revolver (upsized from $200M in September 2025), and D/E of ~0.29. Altman Z of ~3.36 confirms financial strength. One caveat: AOCI swung to −$82.7M (from −$30.1M at YE25) on rate-driven mark-to-market losses on AFS securities, depressing GAAP equity. This is non-cash and does not affect statutory capital or PMIERs.

**Capital return is aggressive for an insurer.** The dividend was raised repeatedly to $0.24/quarter (announced May 2026). Completed buyback authorizations of $250M (2024) and $350M (2025) were followed by a fresh $500M authorization in February 2026. Share count fell from 151.8M (Q1'25 diluted) to 142.6M (Q1'26). EMICO paid a $150M dividend to the holdco in March 2026 to fund this. The pace signals management views capital as excess relative to organic growth opportunities.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is structurally constrained. Premium revenue is flat-to-down, and the two suppressing forces (rising cession and policy lapse) are both intensifying. The cession ratio is committed to rise further to 34.25% by 2027, so net premium growth faces a continued headwind regardless of insurance-in-force trends.

The bright spot is new insurance written (NIW), which jumped 30% YoY to $12.8B in Q1'26 as a refinance surge (refis rose from 7% to 23% of NIW) responded to a brief dip in rates. NIW quality is high: 52% of Q1'26 NIW carried FICO scores above 760, weighted toward 90–95% LTV purchase loans. But the refi surge cut both ways: persistency fell to 80% from 84% as refinancing lapsed older policies, partially offsetting the volume gain in terms of in-force.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive. Current-year EPS consensus rose to $4.80 (+1.1% over 30 days, +2.9% over 90 days), while next-year consensus slipped to $4.98 (−1.1% over 30 days, −2.4% over 90 days). The next-year softening likely reflects analysts pricing in the shrinking reserve-release tailwind and rising credit costs. This is not the >30% revision velocity that would flag an earnings inflection; it is a mature business with low-single-digit forward EPS growth, where per-share growth comes predominantly from buybacks rather than earnings expansion.

The growth thesis is therefore capital-return-led: flat-to-modest net income, mid-single-digit share-count reduction, and a growing dividend produce high-single-digit total-return potential, not a re-rating story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $43.13 the stock trades at ~9.3x trailing/forward EPS of $4.52–$4.80, with ~1.08x book value. Against PMI peers, ACT is in the normal range, with Walker & Dunlop's 25.9x reflecting a different, advisory-heavy business model. The peer median P/E is 8.2x and median yield 2.4%.

ACT's slight premium to Essent/NMI is defensible given its ratings trajectory (Moody's upgraded EMICO to A2 Stable in August 2025; S&P A-/Positive, Fitch A/Stable, AM Best A-/Positive) and its capital return. But the multiple is not depressed enough to constitute a clear mispricing, and the structural premium-growth headwind plus the credit-normalization trajectory argue against material re-rating. A low P/E on a mature, slow-growth franchise with deteriorating loss accruals is appropriately priced, not a value opportunity. We assign a moderate valuation score because the cheapness is real but lacks an independent catalyst to close any gap.

Analyst price targets cluster in a tight $44.00–$48.00 band (mean $45.75, median $45.50), implying roughly 2–11% upside from current price. With consensus revisions flat-to-positive on the current year but negative on next year, and the stock already near the low end of the target range, the asymmetry is modestly favorable but not compelling. On a sum-of-parts basis, the more durable support is the ~$40 book value plus the buyback floor; the franchise is unlikely to trade much below book given the capital cushion and ratings strength.

We do not apply the inflection-stock framework here: there is no earnings trough, no >30% revision velocity, and no margin expansion underway. This is a steady-state monoline valued like one.

## Competitive Landscape

Enact holds a stable position in a rational six-player oligopoly. The industry's structural moat is regulatory: PMIERs eligibility and GSE approval are required to operate, creating high barriers to entry. Competition centers on risk-based pricing engines and reinsurance optimization rather than price wars, which supports industry-wide profitability even in a low-volume origination environment.

Enact's competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-positive. Its NIW quality is high, its ratings are improving, and management characterizes pricing as within its risk-adjusted return appetite. The pivot to investment income and reinsurance-driven capital efficiency is shared across the industry, so it confers parity rather than differentiation. The franchise is neither gaining nor losing meaningful share; it is executing competently in a mature, regulated market.

The defining industry variable is GSE privatization. As of mid-2026, the Trump administration's plan to release Fannie and Freddie from conservatorship has stalled, with FHFA Director Pulte's additional role as acting DNI (effective June 30, 2026) seen as a further delay. The near-term read-through is benign: the status quo persists, PMIERs and GSE reliance on private MI continue. Longer term, an eventual release could reshape MI and capital requirements in either direction, a genuine but unquantifiable tail.

## Risk Assessment

**Reserve-release dependence and credit normalization.** Earnings quality is propped by favorable prior-year development. As the 2020–2023 books season, releases shrink and underlying loss ratios rise. New delinquencies are already climbing (count up ~10% YoY). This is the most likely source of a forward earnings disappointment.

**GSE/regulatory dependence.** The business model depends entirely on PMIERs eligibility and the GSEs continuing to require private MI on low-down-payment loans. Any change to GSE charters, guaranty fees, FHA pricing, or MI displacement would hit the franchise directly. Currently benign given the privatization stall.

**Genworth relationship.** Genworth controls ~81% of votes; all dividends require its consent; it manages Enact's portfolio. Genworth's own liquidity needs could pressure Enact's capital-return cadence, and minority holders have limited control. Free float will not grow organically given the share-repurchase-from-parent structure. To date Genworth has signaled commitment to maintaining its stake.

**Macro/housing sensitivity.** A recession, rising unemployment (currently 4.3%), or home-price declines would drive delinquencies and claim severity. Management explicitly flags tariffs, inflation (CPI rose to 3.3% in March 2026), and geopolitical tensions; a sharper housing downturn would test the loss-ratio normalization more aggressively than the base case assumes. The PMIERs cushion provides substantial buffer.

**Reinsurance reliance.** Rising cession (~34% by 2027) generates PMIERs credit but introduces counterparty dependence and suppresses retained premium. Loss of affordable reinsurance capacity would pressure capital.

**Concentration.** Monoline, U.S.-only, single-segment, no diversification cushion.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility spiked from 60.6% (May 6) to 152.4% (June 17) while the price was essentially flat (−1.0%). A move of this magnitude in IV without a corresponding price move is unusual for a mature insurer; it may reflect a data anomaly, a thin options market amplifying a small positioning shift, or hedging ahead of a perceived catalyst. Short interest is elevated at 15.8% of float with 12.2 days to cover, indicating meaningful bearish institutional positioning and creating modest squeeze potential on any positive surprise. The combination of high short interest and the IV spike suggests the options market is pricing more uncertainty than the steady fundamentals would imply; we treat this as a flag to monitor rather than a directional signal, given the stock's low intrinsic volatility profile.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** ACT compounds at high single digits through capital return. The ~6% annual share-count reduction plus a growing dividend and modest investment-income growth drive EPS higher even with flat net income. Credit normalization proves benign: high-quality NIW vintages (52% FICO >760) and home-price stability keep the loss ratio contained. The ratings trajectory continues to improve, and the stock re-rates modestly toward the high end of the peer range. A GSE-privatization outcome that preserves private MI's role removes a tail. Upside to ~$48 (~11%), driven by buyback-fueled EPS growth and a slight multiple uptick.

**Bear Case.** The reserve-release tailwind exhausts faster than expected as delinquencies climb in a housing slowdown, pushing the reported loss ratio toward the underlying accrual and compressing net income. Premium growth stays negative as cession rises to 34%. A Genworth liquidity event forces a slower capital-return cadence. The stock de-rates toward book value (~$40) or below, with downside to ~$37 (−14%). A disruptive GSE-privatization framework introduces structural uncertainty.

**Base Case.** Steady-state execution: flat-to-modestly-higher net income, ~6% share-count reduction, dividend growth, and a maintained ~9–9.5x multiple. Fair value near $45–46, roughly 5–7% above current price plus the 2.3% yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold)** — held primarily for yield and buyback-driven per-share compounding rather than a growth or re-rating thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August) for the credit trend and reserve-release magnitude; the GSE-privatization developments around the June 30, 2026 FHFA leadership change.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The stock pulls back toward or below book value (~$40) with credit metrics stable, improving the entry into the buyback floor.
- Loss-ratio normalization proves materially slower than feared (delinquency rate stabilizing below ~2.6% and current-accident-year provisioning flattening) while capital return accelerates.
- A GSE-privatization framework emerges that explicitly preserves or expands private MI's role, removing the structural tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The underlying loss ratio (ex-releases) accelerates materially — delinquency rate breaking above ~3.0% or current-accident-year provision rising >25% sequentially.
- A Genworth liquidity event forces a reduction or suspension of the buyback/dividend program.
- A GSE-privatization proposal that reduces or displaces private MI requirements.

## Conclusion

Enact is a well-run, fortress-capitalized monoline insurer returning capital aggressively in a mature, rational industry. The fundamentals are sound: 162% PMIERs sufficiency, an A2-rated operating entity, ~55% net margins, and a buyback retiring ~6% of shares annually. But the investment case is bounded: net premium growth is structurally flat as cession rises, earnings quality is at a credit inflection where the reserve-release tailwind is shrinking, and the ~9.3x multiple is appropriately priced rather than cheap. The Genworth ownership structure caps free float and introduces parent-dependency risk.

The stock offers a reasonable total-return profile (high-single-digit compounding from buybacks and a 2.3% yield) but lacks a catalyst for meaningful re-rating, and the credit trajectory is a genuine headwind to forward earnings momentum. We rate ACT a HOLD, attractive for income-oriented holders willing to accept the credit-normalization and Genworth-overhang risks, with a preferred re-entry for new capital closer to book value.
