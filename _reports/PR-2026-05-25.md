## Executive Summary

Permian Resources is a pure-play Delaware Basin E&P that achieved investment-grade ratings from all three major agencies in early 2026, reshaping its capital structure and cost of financing. At $20.44, the stock trades at approximately 10.6x forward FY2026 consensus EPS of $1.92 — a meaningful discount to E&P peer multiples in the 13-14x range despite PR's superior production growth trajectory (6% guided for 2026), industry-leading cost structure ($675/ft D&C target), and record free cash flow per share generation.

Trailing EPS of $1.28 substantially understates normalized earnings power. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.05 was depressed by a $339.9M unrealized derivatives loss tied to the March 2026 oil price spike to $103/Bbl — while GAAP EPS missed consensus significantly, adjusted EPS was $0.39, beating the adjusted consensus estimate. FY2026 consensus EPS has risen materially to $1.92, confirming that the earnings inflection is being recognized but not yet fully reflected in the stock price. Multiple analysts maintain Buy-equivalent ratings with recent target increases from BofA ($22), Scotiabank ($25), and KeyBanc ($25).

The primary risks are binary commodity price exposure (100% Permian concentration), heavy insider selling post-reorganization, and the structural Waha natural gas pricing crisis that resulted in negative $0.29/Mcf realized prices in Q1 2026.

## Company Overview

Permian Resources operates exclusively in the Delaware Basin portion of the Permian, the most prolific tight oil province in North America. The company was built through a series of transformative mergers — Colgate Energy Partners (2022) and Earthstone Energy (2023) — that scaled daily production from approximately 170,000 Boe/d to 412,850 Boe/d as of Q1 2026.

The January 2026 corporate reorganization initiated the simplification of the equity structure, reducing noncontrolling interest to approximately 4%. During Q1 2026, the remaining Class C shareholders converted their shares, such that by March 31, 2026, no Class C shares remained outstanding and noncontrolling interest was eliminated entirely. Over 96% of PR's net acreage is held by production, providing development optionality without lease expiration pressure.

## Financial Analysis

**Cash Flow Generation:** PR generated $3.61B in operating cash flow in FY2025 on $5.1B revenue, producing $1.64B in free cash flow (32.4% FCF margin). Q1 2026 delivered record FCF per share of $0.60 despite lower realized gas prices. With WTI front-month futures trading near $96.60 as of late May 2026 (and accounting for ~40% hedged production), Q2 2026 cash flow has potential to exceed Q1, though realized prices, hedge settlements, NGL/gas weakness, working-capital movements, and capex timing introduce uncertainty.

**Balance Sheet Transformation:** The achievement of investment-grade ratings from Fitch, S&P, and Moody's enabled a transition from a secured reserve-based lending facility to a $3.0B unsecured revolving credit facility maturing 2031 (SOFR + 150bps). The April 2026 redemption of $550M in 8% senior notes eliminates $44M annually in gross coupon expense; net interest savings will depend on the funding source used for redemption, which has not been disclosed. At March 31, 2026, total debt was $3.575B with cash of $170.8M, yielding net debt of $3.404B, representing approximately 0.8x net debt-to-LQA EBITDAX. Pro forma for the $550M redemption, net debt is estimated at approximately $2.8-3.0B depending on funding source.

**Operating Efficiency:** LOE per Boe declined to $5.26 (from $5.45 YoY), cash G&A per Boe fell 11% to $0.83, and the company targets $675/lateral foot D&C costs — among the lowest in the basin. These unit costs provide significant margin protection in a commodity downturn.

**Capital Allocation:** FY2026 capex guidance of $1.75B-$1.95B is flat-to-declining versus FY2025's $1.97B while guiding 6% production growth. Dividends are running at $0.64/share annualized (3.1% yield). The $73.7M in buybacks executed in FY2025 at an average of $11.57/share was deeply accretive versus the current $20.44 price.

## Growth Analysis

PR guides 6% production growth in 2026 on flat-to-declining capex — a function of improving capital efficiency (5-10% more TILs per dollar) and high-quality Delaware Basin inventory. Oil growth was approximately 10% YoY in Q1 2026, reaching 192,349 Bbls/d.

FY2026 consensus EPS estimates have risen significantly in recent months, reaching $1.92 — reflecting both the March 2026 oil price spike's cash flow impact and recognition of PR's operational momentum (record production, declining unit costs, investment-grade benefits).

The Delaware Basin inventory provides 10+ years of tier-1 drilling locations at current activity levels. The 29% PUD reserve component represents future development that requires execution but also provides embedded growth at known economics.

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.44, PR trades at:
- 10.6x FY2026 consensus EPS ($1.92)
- 9.8x FY2027 consensus EPS ($2.09)
- Forward dividend yield of 3.1%
- Approximately 4.5x EV/EBITDAX (estimated at ~$5B for FY2026)

PR's 10.6x forward multiple represents a discount of approximately 19-24% versus E&P peer multiples in the 13-14x range, despite superior production growth (6% vs. flat for most peers) and investment-grade credit. The market mispricing centers on the trailing P/E of 24x, which reflects Q1 2026's $339.9M unrealized derivatives loss. Adjusted Q1 EPS was $0.39, which annualizes to $1.56 as a trough — above the trailing $1.28 figure.

Recent analyst activity supports the bullish thesis: BofA raised its target to $22 (Neutral), Scotiabank raised to $25 (Outperform), and KeyBanc initiated at Overweight with a $25 target. With estimates accelerating, consensus targets may still understate the 12-month opportunity given the earnings revision trajectory.

The PEG ratio at 10.6x forward P/E divided by estimated 15-20% normalized growth rate (volume growth + oil price tailwind) approximates 0.5-0.7 — well below 1.0.

## Competitive Landscape

PR occupies a strong position as a mid-cap pure-play Delaware Basin operator with investment-grade credit. Recent M&A transactions in the Delaware Basin confirm asset values at premium levels: Devon Energy acquired 16,300 net undeveloped acres for approximately $2.6B (~$160K/acre) in May 2026. This transaction validates PR's acreage position at a meaningful premium to implied per-acre values in PR's market capitalization.

PR's cost structure ($5.26/Boe LOE, $0.83/Boe cash G&A, $675/ft D&C target) positions it in the lowest quartile of basin operators. The company's scale (412,850 Boe/d) provides midstream bargaining power and operational flexibility that smaller operators cannot match, while its pure-play focus avoids the bureaucratic overhead of integrated majors.

The structural Waha gas pricing crisis is basin-wide — affecting all Delaware operators — and PR's mitigation strategy (firm transportation commitments, Henry Hub hedges, purchased gas arbitrage) is among the most developed in the basin. This problem should gradually resolve as Matterhorn, Permian Highway, and other pipelines reach full capacity through 2028-2030.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (High):** PR's proved reserve PV-10 declines by $2.2B (23%) on a 10% decline from the $66/Bbl reserve pricing assumption. At recent WTI levels near $96-100/Bbl, economics are extremely favorable — but the company's fortunes are directionally tied to oil prices. Hedges protect the downside at ~$67-69/Bbl for 2026 but limit participation in the current spike.

**Waha Gas Crisis (Moderate):** Negative gas realizations directly reduce cash flow. At 676,835 Mcf/d of gas production, each $1/Mcf improvement translates to approximately $247M in annual revenue. This is a structural issue until 2028+ and cannot be solved by any single operator.

**Insider Selling (Moderate):** Co-CEOs sold ~$40M combined in January-March 2026. While contextualized by the Class C-to-Class A reorganization (which created the first opportunity to sell previously illiquid positions), the magnitude is substantial. Both retain ~$190M positions at current prices, maintaining strong alignment.

**Geographic Concentration (Moderate):** 100% Delaware Basin exposure provides no diversification against basin-specific regulatory or infrastructure risks (New Mexico seismicity restrictions, pipeline capacity constraints).

**Share Count Context (Low):** Basic Class A weighted-average shares increased from 704.0M in Q1 2025 to 812.2M in Q1 2026, primarily reflecting the Class C-to-Class A conversions and convertible note redemption. However, adjusted diluted weighted-average shares (which include the economic interest of all share classes) were relatively stable at 852.3M in Q1 2026 vs. 847.8M in Q1 2025. The Class A increase thus largely reflects structural simplification rather than incremental economic dilution.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($26-30, 27-47% upside):** Oil prices remain elevated ($95-110/Bbl) through 2026 due to Hormuz supply constraints, driving EPS well above current $1.92 consensus. Investment-grade benefits (lower interest expense, revolver flexibility) compound with 6% production growth and declining D&C costs. At 12-13x FY2027 EPS of $2.50+ (bull scenario with sustained $100 oil), the stock reaches $30+. M&A transactions in the Delaware Basin create a floor valuation for acreage.

**Bear Case ($14-16, 22-32% downside):** Oil prices decline to $65-75/Bbl as Hormuz reopens or demand deteriorates. EPS compresses toward $1.20-1.40 as hedges expire and production growth moderates. Waha gas crisis worsens. At 11-12x trough EPS, the stock reverts to $14-17. The recent 72% rally from the October 2025 low of $11.92 would partially reverse.

**Base Case ($25-26, 22-27% upside):** Oil averages $85-95/Bbl through 2026, PR delivers on 6% production growth, FY2026 EPS comes in at $1.85-2.00, and the stock re-rates toward the peer median of 13-14x forward earnings as the market recognizes the investment-grade credit and earnings trajectory. Dividend yield provides 3.1% floor return.

## Conclusion

Permian Resources at $20.44 represents a well-positioned Delaware Basin E&P trading at a meaningful discount to forward earnings (10.6x FY2026E vs. 13-14x peer median) in an environment where oil prices are structurally elevated due to Hormuz supply constraints. The investment-grade upgrade completed in early 2026 is transformational for cost of capital and financial flexibility. While insider selling is elevated and geographic concentration creates binary commodity risk, the risk/reward at approximately 2:1 (base case upside vs. bear case downside) with a 3.1% dividend floor supports a **BUY** recommendation.

The specific market error is anchoring on trailing P/E (24x), which reflects the Q1 2026 unrealized derivatives loss, rather than forward earnings power that incorporates production growth, cost improvements, and elevated commodity pricing now visible in the estimate trajectory. This recommendation is conditional on sustained oil prices above $80/Bbl and confirmation of PR's 6% production growth guidance through Q2 2026 results.
