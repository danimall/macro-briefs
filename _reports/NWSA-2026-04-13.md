## Executive Summary

News Corp is a diversified media and information services company undergoing a meaningful portfolio transformation. The April 2025 divestiture of Foxtel ($2.14B) removed its most capital-intensive, structurally declining asset, leaving a focused portfolio where 94% of segment EBITDA comes from two high-quality businesses: Dow Jones (Professional Information + WSJ) and Digital Real Estate (REA Group + Realtor.com). The company generated $1.415B in segment EBITDA in FY2025 (+14% YoY), is effectively net cash ($2.4B cash vs. $1.94B debt), and has authorized $1.31B in buybacks — roughly 14% of the current $9.1B market cap.

The core investment thesis rests on a well-documented conglomerate discount. News Corp's 61.4% stake in ASX-listed REA Group (market cap ~A$30B) is worth materially more than News Corp's entire enterprise value. At ~$24.94, the stock trades at approximately 23.5x FY2026E EPS of $1.06, which appears expensive in isolation but understates the value embedded in the REA stake, Dow Jones's Professional Information Business (PIB), and nascent AI licensing revenue. The consensus analyst target of $34.20 implies 37% upside. The primary risks — AI-driven content cannibalization, Murdoch family dual-class governance, and U.S. housing market weakness at Realtor.com — are real but insufficiently large to explain a discount of this magnitude.

## Company Overview

News Corp operates across five segments following the Foxtel divestiture:

- **Dow Jones (28% of revenue, 25.2% EBITDA margin):** The Wall Street Journal (4.5M+ total subscriptions, 4.1M digital-only), Barron's, MarketWatch, and a rapidly growing Professional Information Business (PIB) encompassing Risk & Compliance, Dow Jones Energy, Factiva, and Newswires. PIB now represents 39% of Dow Jones revenue and approximately half its profits, with retention rates above 90%.

- **Digital Real Estate Services (21% of revenue, 33.4% EBITDA margin):** REA Group (61.4% owned), Australia's dominant property portal with 132M monthly visits and near-monopoly positioning, plus Move/Realtor.com (80% owned). REA grew 12% in FY2025; Move grew 1%.

- **Book Publishing (25% of revenue, 13.8% EBITDA margin):** HarperCollins Publishers, the world's second-largest consumer book publisher. 64% of consumer revenues derive from backlist titles, providing recurring stability.

- **News Media (26% of revenue, 7.1% EBITDA margin):** News Corp Australia, News UK (The Times, The Sun), New York Post, Sky News Australia. In secular decline; print circulation falling, digital advertising exposed to platform algorithm changes.

- **Other:** Corporate overhead and U.K. Newspaper Matters legal costs.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement

FY2025 revenue on a continuing operations basis was $8.45B, up 2% from $8.25B in FY2024. The modest topline growth masks significant divergence across segments: Digital Real Estate +9%, Dow Jones +4%, Book Publishing +3%, and News Media -4%. Total segment EBITDA grew 14% to $1.415B, with margin expansion of 170 basis points to 16.7%. Net income from continuing operations surged 71% to $648M.

The GAAP operating margin of 1.3% is distorted by restructuring charges, impairments, and one-time items. Adjusted segment EBITDA margins are the more relevant profitability metric, and the 14% EBITDA growth rate signals genuine operational improvement.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is strong for a media conglomerate. Cash of $2.4B against $1.94B in long-term debt puts the company at effectively net cash (approximately negative $0.4B net debt). The current ratio of 1.84x is healthy. Goodwill and intangibles at $6.3B (41% of total assets) are substantial but primarily tied to Dow Jones and REA Group — both demonstrably high-quality franchises. Retained earnings remain negative at -$747M, a legacy of historical losses and write-downs, but this is improving rapidly (from -$2.3B in FY2022) and should turn positive within 12-18 months at current profitability.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow of $978M less capex of $407M yielded $571M in FCF (6.8% FCF margin). Capital expenditures rose 14% YoY as Dow Jones invests in PIB technology and digital platforms. Capital allocation priorities include: $150M in buybacks in FY2025 (5.3M shares), $115M in dividends ($0.20/share, ~0.8% yield), and $203M in debt repayment. The newly authorized $1.31B buyback program, combined with Foxtel proceeds, should meaningfully accelerate the repurchase cadence — management explicitly stated intent for a "significantly larger total and significantly faster tempo."

### Earnings Quality

Three beats and one in-line result over the last four quarters, with average surprise of ~12.7% when excluding the in-line quarter. This suggests consensus estimates are set conservatively. FY2026E EPS of $1.06 and FY2027E of $1.24 imply 26% and 17% YoY growth respectively. Estimate revisions over 90 days have been modestly positive: FY2026 +2.2%, FY2027 +4.6%. The revision trend is not accelerating, but neither is it deteriorating.

## Growth Analysis

### Organic Growth Drivers

**Dow Jones PIB (most important):** The Professional Information Business is the highest-quality growth engine. Risk & Compliance revenue grew 21% in Q4 FY2025. Enterprise compliance spending is structurally expanding as KYC/AML regulations tighten globally. Retention rates above 90% and the new LSEG partnership for digital distribution position this business for sustained high-single-digit to low-double-digit growth. PIB at 39% of Dow Jones revenue and approximately half of segment profits is approaching the point where its growth rate determines Dow Jones's overall trajectory.

**AI Content Licensing:** The OpenAI deal (~$50M/yr over 5 years, signed May 2024) and Meta deal (~$50M/yr, signed March 2026) combine for $100M+ annually in high-margin licensing revenue. This is a new revenue stream with minimal incremental cost — effectively 100% margin. Additional deals with other AI companies are plausible. The total addressable market for content licensing to AI platforms is difficult to size but clearly expanding.

**REA Group International Ambitions:** REA's failed Rightmove bid signals appetite for global property portal consolidation. While the withdrawal was prudent at Rightmove's valuation, REA's dominant Australian position generates substantial free cash flow for future M&A.

**HarperCollins Manga Expansion:** The Crunchyroll manga acquisition extends HarperCollins into a high-growth publishing vertical, though the financial impact is modest relative to the $2.15B segment.

### Growth Constraints

**News Media secular decline** (-4% in FY2025) is a persistent drag. Digital advertising at The Sun was materially impacted by platform algorithm changes — a risk News Corp cannot control. Print circulation continues to fall. This segment's 7.1% margin and 26% revenue share act as an anchor on consolidated growth.

**Realtor.com market share challenges:** Despite being the #2 U.S. property portal by traffic (256M visits), Realtor.com faces a dominant Zillow and an emerging CoStar-backed Homes.com. Lead volumes declined 9% in FY2025 amid elevated mortgage rates. Revenue grew only 1%. In a higher-for-longer rate environment with potential recession, this segment faces continued headwinds.

## Valuation Assessment

At $24.94, NWSA trades at approximately 31.9x trailing EPS ($0.84, which is depressed by noise from the Foxtel reclassification) and 23.5x FY2026E EPS of $1.06. On FY2027E of $1.24, the multiple compresses to 20.1x.

Relative to peers: NYT trades at 33.2x, NWS (Class B) at 34.0x, John Wiley at 10.9x (different business model). The peer median of 33.2x suggests NWSA is trading at a discount even before accounting for the conglomerate structure. The discount is even more pronounced when considering quality mix — 94% of profits from the two highest-margin, highest-growth segments.

**Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) Framework:**

The SOTP math is the most compelling aspect of the investment case:

- **61.4% of REA Group** (ASX market cap ~A$30B = ~US$19.5B): 61.4% = ~$12.0B
- **Dow Jones** at 15x segment EBITDA ($586M): ~$8.8B
- **HarperCollins** at 8x segment EBITDA ($296M): ~$2.4B
- **News Media** at 5x segment EBITDA ($154M): ~$0.8B
- **Net cash:** ~$0.4B
- **Less: minority interests, corporate costs, taxes:** ~($3.0-4.0B)
- **Implied SOTP:** ~$21-24B, or roughly $36-41/share

Against a current market cap of ~$9.1B, the SOTP implies 100%+ upside. Even applying aggressive haircuts to account for the dual-class discount, holding company tax friction, and governance risk, the gap between market price and intrinsic value is substantial.

The consensus analyst target of $34.20 (range: $27-$41) is consistent with a partial closing of this discount.

## Competitive Landscape

**Dow Jones** occupies a unique position at the intersection of premium financial journalism and enterprise data/compliance. In professional information, it competes with RELX (LexisNexis), LSEG (Reuters), and S&P Global — all larger but with different product focus. WSJ's brand strength in financial news is unmatched, and the PIB business benefits from regulatory tailwinds that are difficult to reverse.

**REA Group** has near-monopoly status in Australian property portals. Domain (owned by Nine Entertainment) is the only significant competitor, and REA's traffic and engagement lead is wide. This type of platform dominance — where both consumers and agents converge on a single marketplace — is extremely difficult to dislodge.

**HarperCollins** is the #2 global consumer publisher, competing with Penguin Random House (#1), Simon & Schuster (KKR), Hachette, and Macmillan. The 64% backlist ratio provides recurring revenue insulation against title-by-title risk.

**News Media** faces the most challenging competitive dynamics. Digital news competes with free alternatives, algorithmic aggregation, social media distribution, and now AI-generated summaries. The segment's value increasingly depends on premium differentiation (The Times) rather than mass-market digital advertising (The Sun).

## Risk Assessment

### AI Content Cannibalization (HIGH)

The 10-K elevates this to the most prominent risk factor. Generative AI tools trained on News Corp content may reduce traffic to and subscriber demand for its digital products. Even as News Corp monetizes through licensing ($100M+/yr), the net effect could be negative if AI substitutes rather than complements the subscription product. The company's approach — license to those who pay, litigate against those who don't — is the correct strategy, but litigation outcomes (including potential "fair use" rulings) are uncertain.

### Dual-Class Governance (HIGH)

The Murdoch Family Trust controls ~40.6% of Class B voting stock. Lachlan Murdoch simultaneously serves as Chair of News Corp and CEO of Fox Corporation. Class A shareholders (NWSA) have limited ability to influence strategic direction. This governance structure is a permanent feature and a meaningful reason for the conglomerate discount.

### U.S. Housing Market / Move Exposure (MEDIUM-HIGH)

Realtor.com is directly exposed to mortgage rate levels and housing transaction volumes. With rates elevated at 7%+, lead volumes fell 9% in FY2025. The NAR settlement changes to cooperative compensation rules add further uncertainty. In a recession scenario with rising unemployment, this segment could experience meaningful revenue declines.

### Platform Algorithm Dependency (MEDIUM)

News UK's The Sun experienced material digital advertising declines from algorithm changes at Google and Meta. News Corp has no control over these changes and they can impact traffic and revenue with no advance notice.

### Foreign Currency Exposure (MEDIUM)

Substantial operations denominated in AUD (REA Group) and GBP (News UK). A strengthening USD would compress reported earnings.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 31.9% sits 8.1 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 23.7%, indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (31.9% near-term vs. 26.6% at 3 months), which typically signals near-term event risk. Put/call skew at 5.5% shows modestly elevated put demand. Given the macro environment — US naval blockade, consumer sentiment at record lows, oil above $102 — this elevated pricing reflects general market stress rather than company-specific event risk. For a fundamental buyer, the elevated IV suggests waiting for a clearer entry may be prudent, but the magnitude is modest.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($34-38)

The conglomerate discount partially closes as management executes on the $1.31B buyback at depressed prices (potentially retiring 8-10% of shares outstanding over 12-18 months). PIB continues growing at low-double-digit rates, driving Dow Jones margins higher. AI licensing revenue reaches $150M+ annually as additional deals are signed. REA Group announces another international acquisition. FY2027 EPS reaches $1.30+, and the market re-rates toward 27-30x forward earnings. SOTP analysis increasingly enters mainstream investor discourse as the Foxtel simplification removes the conglomerate complexity argument.

### Bear Case ($18-20)

Recession materializes, compressing advertising revenue across News Media and Dow Jones (-10-15%). U.S. housing market deterioration drives Realtor.com revenue down 15-20%. AI content tools meaningfully reduce WSJ digital subscription growth. REA Group's Australian housing market softens on rate hikes. AUD depreciates. GAAP earnings decline to $0.60-0.70 and the market applies a 27-30x trough multiple. Dual-class discount widens as Murdoch family makes value-destructive strategic decisions.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $32** — 20.5x FY2027E EPS of $1.24, plus approximately $2/share credit for buyback accretion and AI licensing revenue not yet in estimates. This represents 28% upside from $24.94.

**Bull case target: $38** — partial SOTP re-rating as buyback execution + PIB growth + AI licensing shift market perception. Represents 52% upside.

**Bear case target: $19** — recessionary scenario with compressed earnings and multiple. Represents 24% downside.

**Upside/downside ratio: 28% base / 52% bull vs. 24% bear = approximately 1.2:1 base case, 2.2:1 bull case.**

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts include: (1) FY2026 Q3 earnings (next report, likely May 2026) — PIB growth rate and buyback execution update; (2) additional AI licensing deal announcements; (3) any strategic announcement regarding News Media (divestiture, restructuring).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Dow Jones PIB revenue growth decelerates below 5% for two consecutive quarters, signaling competitive pressure in the enterprise compliance market
2. Management slows or suspends the buyback program, suggesting internal cash needs or balance sheet deterioration
3. WSJ digital subscription count declines YoY, indicating AI cannibalization is materializing faster than licensing revenue can offset

## Conclusion

News Corp at $24.94 offers a compelling risk-reward profile driven by a persistent and quantifiable conglomerate discount. The company's two highest-quality assets — Dow Jones PIB and REA Group — are both growing, high-margin businesses that would command significantly higher valuations as standalone entities. Management is responding appropriately with the Foxtel divestiture (simplification), $1.31B buyback authorization (value realization), and AI licensing deals (monetization of content assets). The dual-class governance structure, AI cannibalization risk, and U.S. housing exposure at Realtor.com are legitimate concerns that warrant a discount, but the current pricing implies these risks overwhelm the value of the underlying assets, which the evidence does not support. At a 28% discount to even the low end of analyst targets ($27) and with a 37% discount to the consensus median ($34.20), the margin of safety is adequate for a BUY recommendation.
