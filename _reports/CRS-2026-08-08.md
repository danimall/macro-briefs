## Executive Summary

Carpenter Technology has delivered one of the more complete operating recoveries in the industrial complex. Operating income went from roughly break-even in FY2022 to $702.0M in FY2026 on revenue up only 22.5% over the same span, which means essentially all of the improvement is margin. Specialty Alloys Operations (SAO) posted a 37.8% adjusted operating margin ex-surcharge in Q4 FY2026, its eighteenth consecutive quarter of expansion, against 29.1% two years earlier. Return on invested capital of roughly 23% and return on equity near 26% are exceptional for a capital-intensive metals converter and support the argument that the market has correctly reclassified this business from a cyclical alloy producer to a qualification-moated aerospace materials franchise. Management guided FY2027 operating income to $850-880M (+21% to +25%) and established a FY2029 target of $1,200-1,300M, implying a 20-23% three-year CAGR. The balance sheet is a genuine asset: net debt/EBITDA around 0.35x, interest coverage of 19.55x against a 3.00x covenant, and no scheduled maturities until 2034 following the November 2025 refinancing.

The problem is price. At $570.66 the shares trade at 54.2x trailing GAAP EPS of $10.52, 42.6x the $13.38 FY2027 consensus, 33.8x trailing EV/EBITDA, 12.9x book value, and offer a 1.3-1.5% free-cash-flow yield. The FY2029 target, the company's own three-year yardstick, translates to roughly $20.50 of EPS. At $570.66 an investor pays 27.8x that number three years forward. Holding a 30x multiple on FY2029 earnings produces $615, a 2.5% annualized return from here. Earning 10% per year for three years requires the shares to trade at 37x FY2029 EPS in mid-2029, on a growth profile that will by then be decelerating.

This is the fifth consecutive report on Carpenter and the first that moves off HOLD. The trajectory (6.3 → 6.0 → 6.9 → 6.0, four HOLDs) reflected a business improving faster than the multiple was expanding. That relationship has now inverted. Three items are new since the June review: the FY2029 target now quantifies how little runway the current multiple leaves; the CEO succession collapsed when Brian Malloy died approximately three weeks into the role and Tony Thene was reinstated; and capex steps to $355-375M with the project's cost, incremental capacity and expected return all undisclosed. Probability-weighted twelve-month return is roughly flat to slightly negative against a bear case 36% below spot. Existing owners should trim into strength. **REDUCE.**

## Company Overview

Founded in 1889, Carpenter produces and distributes premium specialty alloys — titanium alloys, nickel- and cobalt-base superalloys, stainless and alloy steels, tool steels and metal powders — converting raw inputs through melting, hot-forming and cold-working into billet, bar, rod, wire and narrow strip. It operates its own global network of service and distribution centers, which supports just-in-time stocking and direct customer contact rather than sales through third-party distributors alone.

Two reportable segments. **SAO** is the integrated mill system across Reading and Latrobe (Pennsylvania), South Carolina and Athens (Alabama); it generated $2,056.3M of $2,330.8M of gross segment revenue in the nine months to March 31, 2026 and $553.2M of $576.2M of segment operating income — 96.0%. **Performance Engineered Products (PEP)** houses Dynamet titanium, Carpenter Additive and the Latrobe/Mexico distribution businesses; 9M FY2026 revenue of $274.5M fell 9% year over year with operating income of $23.0M at a roughly flat 9.0% adjusted margin.

End-market mix for 9M FY2026, excluding surcharge revenue: Aerospace & Defense $1,209.0M (65.4%), Industrial & Consumer $227.4M (12.3%), Medical $170.2M (9.2%), Energy $131.5M (7.1%), Transportation $54.5M (2.9%), Distribution $55.2M (3.0%). Geographically, the United States is 59.0% of net sales, Europe 22.3%, Asia Pacific 10.5%.

Two structural features drive the economics. A raw-material surcharge mechanism passes nickel, cobalt and titanium cost moves, and per the 10-Q tariffs, through to customers; surcharge revenue was $425.4M, or 18.7% of 9M FY2026 net sales. Roughly 43% of sales sit under firm-price arrangements hedged with commodity forwards. Second, premium melt capacity (vacuum induction, VAR, ESR) is scarce, and Carpenter is expanding it via brownfield investment at Athens for the first time in years. Headcount is approximately 4,500; R&D was $26.1M in FY2025, 0.9% of sales, because the technical moat resides in process metallurgy and program qualifications rather than reported R&D intensity.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Net sales moved $1,836.3M (FY2022) → $2,550.3M → $2,759.7M → $2,877.1M → $3,124.2M (FY2026). Adjusted operating income moved from roughly negative $26M to $133M, $354.1M, $525.4M and $702.0M. Adjusted operating margin ex-surcharge went 16.3% (FY2024) → 22.4% (FY2025) → 27.8% (FY2026).

The mechanical driver is not volume. Total pounds sold were 192,980 thousand in FY2025 versus 214,122 thousand in FY2023, down 9.9%, while ex-surcharge revenue and operating income rose sharply. Management reallocated a constrained asset base toward higher-value grades. Nine-month FY2026 ex-surcharge revenue per pound of $12.46 compares with $11.94 a year earlier (+4.3%); for SAO alone, $11.48 versus $10.83 (+6.0%).

One qualification on the price/mix narrative deserves attention. In Q3 FY2026, SAO volume rose 16% (51,832 versus 44,584 thousand lbs) while SAO ex-surcharge revenue rose 13%, so revenue per pound *fell* about 3.1% — yet the SAO adjusted margin still expanded 650 basis points to 35.6%. In the most recent quarter with disclosed segment volumes, margin gain came from throughput and fixed-cost absorption rather than higher realized dollars per pound. This aligns with management's disclosure that customers are newly ordering aerospace *structural* material, a product family that plausibly carries a lower price per pound than engine and fastener alloys. Revenue per pound blends grade, form and surcharge-allocation timing and is not a price index, and one quarter is not a trend, but it does shift the burden of future margin expansion toward the brownfield project landing on schedule.

**FY2026 and Q4.** Full-year net income was $529.8M, diluted EPS $10.52 GAAP and $10.76 adjusted (the $0.24 difference is the after-tax debt-extinguishment loss). Q4 net sales were $851.0M with operating income of $206.9M, up 37%, and EPS of $3.23. Against the $3.11 estimate in one dataset that is a 3.7% beat; against a $3.03 non-GAAP consensus reported elsewhere it is 6.6%. There were no special items in the quarter, so GAAP and adjusted are identical and the beat is clean on either basis. The company has beaten in four consecutive quarters by 3.1% to 11.0%.

**Cash flow.** Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow of $364.9M doubled from $182.3M, helped by higher earnings, a smaller inventory build ($45.1M versus $93.3M) and much lower pension contributions ($17.1M versus $58.5M). Full-year adjusted FCF was $362.3M on capex of $242.7M, implying operating cash flow near $605M. Nine-month operating cash flow of $364.9M against net income of $367.4M is roughly 0.99x conversion, which indicates no obvious accrual aggressiveness, though it is not evidence of unusual conservatism either — $145M of D&A and $18.6M of share-based comp are offsetting a working-capital build of roughly $150M. Q4 adjusted FCF was approximately $155M (derived: $362.3M less $207.3M for nine months). Annualizing that single quarter would produce ~$620M, well above the FY2027 guide of $400-430M; Q4 benefits from year-end collections and FY2027 carries far heavier capex. Treat $400-430M as the normalized reference.

**Balance sheet.** At March 31, 2026: cash $294.8M, current assets $1,897.6M against current liabilities $508.3M (3.73x), inventories $839.2M, net PP&E $1,433.3M, goodwill $227.3M, total assets $3,679.1M, long-term debt $690.4M, equity $2,067.6M. At June 30, 2026 cash was $393.3M and total liquidity $892.4M, putting net debt near $297M against roughly $847M of FY2026 EBITDA — about 0.35x. Covenant headroom is very wide: interest coverage 19.55x against a 3.00x minimum, net leverage 0.59x against a 3.50x maximum.

The November 20, 2025 refinancing was cleanly accretive. Carpenter issued $700.0M of 5.625% notes due 2034 and redeemed $400.0M of 6.375% 2028s and $300.0M of 7.625% 2030s, cutting annual cash coupon from $48.375M to $39.375M — about $9.0M pre-tax — against a $11.4M cash prepayment premium (roughly 15-month payback) and a $4.2M non-cash write-off. The revolver was reset to $500M maturing November 2030 with a $650M accordion. There are no scheduled maturities before 2034, and the FY2027 capex program of $355-375M is fully fundable from guided adjusted FCF plus cash on hand. Financing risk around the capex cycle has been removed.

Two accounting details matter for forward modeling. First, part of the reported interest-expense decline is capitalized interest — $4.7M in 9M FY2026 versus $1.6M a year earlier — which reverses into depreciation once assets enter service; the permanent cash saving is the ~$9.0M coupon differential. Second, the 9M FY2026 effective tax rate of 18.6% included $17.0M of discrete share-based-compensation benefits that scale with the share price and are not durable. Q3 stand-alone was 21.0% and FY2027 is guided to 20-22%. Modeling off 18.6% would overstate forward EPS by roughly 3%.

Financial health scores 8.0. The leverage, coverage, maturity profile, liquidity, conversion and returns are all strong; the modest offsets are LIFO amplification of raw-material moves into COGS (a $344.5M LIFO reserve at June 30, 2025 means reported inventory understates replacement cost), working capital as a genuine cash consumer in growth, and a pension asset allocation of 75% return-seeking that could revive contributions in an equity drawdown.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance is specific and credible on the record. FY2027 operating income of $850-880M implies +21% to +25%. Supporting inputs: capex $355-375M, D&A ~$150M, net interest $21-25M, tax 20-22%, net pension expense $6M. Q1 FY2027 segment guidance is SAO $218-222M and PEP $6-7M. The FY2029 target of $1,200-1,300M implies a 19.6-22.9% three-year CAGR.

Consensus has moved with it. FY2027 EPS estimates went from $12.72 ninety days ago to $13.38 (+5.2%), FY2028 from $14.96 to $15.88 (+6.2%), and near-quarter estimates are revising faster (+8.5% and +10.8% over thirty days). The direction is unambiguous. The magnitude, however, sits well below the threshold at which estimate momentum overrides valuation: 5-6% ninety-day revisions on a stock at 42.6x forward is confirmation of guidance, not evidence of a mispriced re-rating.

Three qualifications temper the growth score. First, composition: Medical ex-surcharge revenue fell 22% for nine months and 29% in Q3 on customer destocking; Transportation fell 15%, Distribution 13%, and PEP revenue 9% at flat margins. SAO carries 96.0% of segment operating income. One segment and one end-market are doing the work.

Second, the FY2029 target is predominantly a margin bet. Reaching the $1,250M midpoint requires $548M of incremental operating income over FY2026. If ex-surcharge revenue compounds at 8% to roughly $3,184M, the required consolidated adjusted margin is 39.3% versus 27.8% in FY2026 — implying an SAO adjusted margin in the mid-40s after backing out corporate cost and PEP. At a faster 12% revenue CAGR the required consolidated margin falls to about 35.2% and SAO to roughly 41%. Either path requires the margin streak to continue for three more years. Because the brownfield capacity commissions around early FY2028 and contributes meaningfully only in FY2029, new volume plausibly accounts for a minority of the required increment. The decomposition rests on assumed revenue growth, corporate cost and PEP contribution — none of which management has guided — so it is directional rather than precise, but it identifies where the risk sits.

Third, forward visibility is thinner than the guidance implies. Carpenter discloses no quantified backlog, having elected the Topic 606 practical expedient; contract liabilities were $6.0M at March 31, 2026. Neither the 43% firm-price share nor deferred revenue constitutes an order book. Volume visibility rests on management commentary and long-term agreements that are not quantified in the filings.

One genuinely new demand vector: Energy ex-surcharge revenue rose 23% for nine months and 44% in Q3, attributed explicitly to industrial gas turbine material driven by data-center power demand. At $131.5M and 7.1% of ex-surcharge mix it is small, but it is structurally driven and did not exist in the prior cycle. Industrial & Consumer grew 7% on semiconductor and fluid-control materials. Growth prospects score 7.0.

Valuation scores 3.0. There is no identifiable mispricing in Carpenter's favor at this price: the multiple is at a large premium to sector peers, the PEG is above 1.5, the FCF yield sits well below the risk-free rate, and the company's own three-year target is already substantially discounted.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is qualification. Aerospace and medical customers must qualify a specific material from a specific mill on a specific process route, a program-level process measured in years. The FY2025 10-K describes fewer than ten major competitors for the highest-value products; for individual qualified grades the practical count is often two or three approved sources. That is why Carpenter has raised realized value per pound into a constrained market without immediately inviting substitution, and it is the strongest support for the current margin structure being durable rather than cyclical.

The evidence that capacity rather than demand is binding is internal and strong: pounds sold in FY2025 remained roughly 10% below FY2023 while ex-surcharge revenue and margin climbed, and the company is now adding brownfield melt rather than pushing volume through existing assets. The SAO margin-expansion streak is consistent with a supplier allocating scarce capacity.

Trajectory is improving, which is what matters most for this dimension. Two offsets keep it from a higher score. The mix shift toward aerospace structural product carries a lower price per pound, which changes the character of future margin gains from mix enrichment to throughput leverage and raises dependence on the brownfield ramp. And the 10-Q enumerates the counter-risk directly: customers "may substitute alternate materials or adopt different manufacturing practices that replace or limit the suitability of our products." Competitive position scores 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

**End-market concentration is increasing.** A&D rose from 61.4% to 65.4% of ex-surcharge revenue year over year. Customer concentration is genuinely low — no customer reached 10% of net sales or receivables in FY2023, FY2024, FY2025 or the nine months to March 31, 2026 — and that is a real strength. But those are different measures: low customer concentration limits counterparty risk, while rising A&D share raises sector-cycle beta. The 10-Q forward-looking factors name "production and other challenges at The Boeing Company" explicitly. Management's own framing is that customers are gaining confidence in higher build rates and ordering structural material, which is the demand category most tied to new-build rates rather than the more resilient aftermarket. Growing there raises FY2028-FY2029 sensitivity to OEM delivery execution that Carpenter does not control.

**Physical single-point concentration.** Manufacturing depends on highly specialized equipment located primarily at Reading, Latrobe and Athens, with limited alternatives following equipment failure or a catastrophic event. Athens is also the brownfield expansion site, so growth capital and catastrophic-loss exposure are concentrated at the same location.

**Governance and succession.** Based on Form 4 titles, an Item 5.02 8-K dated July 28, 2026, and corroborating secondary reporting, Brian Malloy succeeded Tony Thene as President and CEO around July 1, 2026, died approximately three weeks later, and Thene was reinstated; the July 30 earnings materials list Thene as Chairman, President and CEO. The cause and exact mechanics come from reporting not verified against the 8-K text. Regardless of specifics, a board that had just executed a planned handoff must now run a second succession while committing the largest capital project in the company's recent history. Bench depth is an open question and Thene's reinstated tenure is presumably transitional. The countervailing reading is that continuity under the architect of the current strategy is stabilizing.

**Capex execution with undisclosed economics.** Capex goes $154.3M (FY2025) → $242.7M (FY2026) → $355-375M (FY2027), with commissioning targeted around early FY2028. The filings do not quantify total project cost, incremental melt capacity, or expected contribution, which makes an incremental ROIC test impossible. Premium melt equipment is long-lead and commissioning-intensive, and ramps in this industry frequently slip. Any delay pushes the volume contribution required for the FY2029 target to the right.

**Incentive design.** Disclosed CEO performance measures are adjusted operating income, adjusted free cash flow, adjusted EBITDA and safety. None is a return-on-capital measure, which for a company nearly tripling capex means absolute-dollar targets do not distinguish value-creating from value-destroying deployment. Adjusted FCF is defined net of capex and does impose some discipline on spending pace, which partly defuses the concern. This is a monitorable rather than evidence of intent.

**Insider activity.** The Form 4 record is predominantly dispositions across a $242 to $617 price band, with no observed C-suite open-market purchases. Insider selling into a rising share price after option exercises and RSU vesting is expected behavior and weak bearish evidence on its own. The absence of any offsetting purchase near current levels is the more relevant point. Precise Form 4 dollar amounts should be re-verified against individual filings before being cited.

**Other items.** LIFO accounting pushes rising nickel/cobalt costs into current COGS while surcharge recovery can lag. Firm-price arrangements covering 43% of sales create hedge-timing mismatch if customers deviate from consumption schedules. The Washington, Pennsylvania collective bargaining agreement expired August 31, 2025 with no filing confirming resolution. The 9M tax rate is flattered by share-based-comp windfalls that reverse if the share price falls. FY2026 figures come from earnings materials furnished July 30, 2026; the FY2026 10-K, including segment detail, updated LIFO reserve and the refreshed risk-factor set, was not available.

**Market fragility at this multiple.** Between July 6 and the July 30 intraday low the shares fell from $619.25 to $472.29, roughly 24%, into and through a beat-and-raise print with a new three-year target. They then recovered toward $570.66. That drawdown occurred with no fundamental miss, which is direct evidence of how a 42x-forward, 47.7%-realized-vol equity behaves when the narrative wobbles.

Risk factors score 4.0: specific, identifiable catalysts for material downside exist, and at a 1.4% FCF yield very little of that is priced.

Put/call open interest is 1.23 with put/call volume at 0.21, so standing protection coexists with call-heavy recent flow; reported put/call skew is 2.7% at one month and −3.6% at six months, and no directional inference follows from those signs. The options market is pricing high but fairly-valued volatility with no dislocation, which neither confirms nor contradicts the valuation-driven downgrade. For holders who want to retain some exposure while trimming, near-dated vol at the 15th chain percentile makes covered-call or collar structures relatively inexpensive.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $700 (+22.7%)

Aerospace build rates accelerate as Boeing and Airbus execute, Carpenter's structural and engine material demand runs ahead of the constrained supply base, and the brownfield melt expansion commissions on time in early FY2028. The SAO margin streak extends past twenty quarters. FY2027 lands at or above the $880M guidance high, FY2028 consensus revises from $15.88 toward $17.00, and Medical destocking reverses while data-center-driven gas turbine demand compounds off a small base. The market continues to value Carpenter as a scarce, qualification-moated aerospace materials franchise at low-40s forward earnings, a multiple already established by the aerospace components cohort. At 41x a revised $17.00 FY2028 estimate the shares reach $697, matching the $700 analyst high. Financing is not a constraint: no maturities before 2034 and $892M of liquidity fund the entire program internally.

### Bear Case — $365 (−36.0%)

The multiple carries the loss. FY2027 delivers near the guidance low ($13.00-13.20 EPS) as the brownfield ramp slips, LIFO pushes rising nickel and cobalt into COGS ahead of surcharge recovery, and Medical destocking persists longer than assumed. Aerospace structural orders prove more OEM-rate-dependent than aftermarket demand and stall on a Boeing rate reset. With 65% of ex-surcharge revenue in one end-market, 96% of segment profit in one segment, an unresolved CEO succession and no return-on-capital discipline in the incentive plan, the market re-rates toward the 27-28x it pays diversified machinery peers. 28x on $13.05 is $365. A 1.4% FCF-yield equity is among the more duration-sensitive expressions in industrials, so a higher-for-longer rate path would compound the compression. The July drawdown from $619 to $472 in seventeen sessions on no operating disappointment is the template.

### Base Case — $595 (+4.3%)

Guidance is delivered, consensus follows it, and the multiple roughly holds. FY2027 EPS lands at $13.50-14.20 given a 4-for-4 beat record averaging 5.5%; twelve months from now the market discounts FY2028 at $16.00-16.50. At 36-37x — where the shares trade on FY2028 consensus today — that is $580-610. Probability-weighted at 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear, expected value is approximately $552, or −3% from $570.66. The consensus mean target of $608.11 (+6.6%) sits slightly above my base case, with nine analysts split 1 strong buy / 7 buy / 1 hold and unchanged over three months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $595 (+4.3%), bull $700 (+22.7%), bear $365 (−36.0%). Probability-weighted twelve-month expected value ~$552 (−3%). Downside magnitude exceeds upside magnitude by roughly 1.6x at comparable probabilities, and cash currently yields a positive risk-free return against an equity carrying 47.7% realized volatility and a roughly flat expected return. That combination defines an unfavorable risk-adjusted position rather than a fair one.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength rather than at a fixed date; the position has recovered off its July 30 low and the recovery is the exit window. Reduce toward one-third to one-half of a full position now, retaining residual exposure to the FY2029 margin story for accounts with low tax basis. For holders unwilling to sell, near-dated implied volatility at the 15th chain percentile makes writing calls into the $620-650 area or collaring the position comparatively cheap. Reassess the residual at the Q1 FY2027 print (late October 2026) against the SAO $218-222M segment guide.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.**
1. Price at or below $440-460, which is 32-34x FY2027 consensus $13.38 and roughly 21-22x the FY2029 target, restoring a margin of safety. The $466 analyst low sits in this zone.
2. Disclosure of the brownfield project's total cost, incremental melt capacity and expected EBITDA contribution, permitting an incremental ROIC test — currently the single largest evidence gap.
3. A quarter that shows margin expansion continuing *with* revenue per pound rising (reversing the Q3 FY2026 divergence), which would confirm the FY2029 margin path is mix-supported rather than throughput-dependent.
4. Resolution of CEO succession with a named permanent CEO and disclosed transition plan.

**Downgrade triggers toward AVOID.**
1. FY2027 operating income guidance cut below the $850M low end, or two consecutive quarters missing the SAO segment guide.
2. Brownfield commissioning pushed beyond FY2028, which would move the volume contribution required for FY2029 to the right.
3. A&D ex-surcharge concentration above 70% of the mix with Medical still contracting — evidence the base is narrowing rather than the growth broadening.
4. Buyback resumption at scale above $550 per share (roughly 12-13x book, versus the ~$300.90 average paid in 9M FY2026), which would signal capital-allocation drift away from the ~23% ROIC reinvestment alternative. Only $164.2M of the $400M authorization remained at March 31, 2026.
5. Confirmation of a work stoppage at any of Reading, Latrobe or Athens.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q1 FY2027 results in late October 2026 (SAO guide $218-222M, PEP $6-7M), and the FY2026 Form 10-K when filed — which will provide the audited June 30, 2026 balance sheet, FY2026 segment volumes, the updated LIFO reserve and the refreshed risk-factor set, none of which is currently verified against a filed annual report.

## Conclusion

Carpenter is a high-quality business at an uncompensated price. The operating record is not in question: eighteen consecutive quarters of SAO margin expansion, operating income up more than fivefold from the FY2023 adjusted base on 22.5% revenue growth, ~23% ROIC, ~26% ROE, and a balance sheet with 19.55x interest coverage and no maturities before 2034 that funds a $355-375M capex program from internally generated cash. Management guided FY2027 up 21-25% and set a FY2029 target implying a 20-23% CAGR, and consensus has revised up 5-6% over ninety days behind it.

At $570.66 the market has already collected that. The shares trade at 42.6x FY2027 consensus, 33.8x trailing EV/EBITDA, 12.9x book, and 27.8x the company's own FY2029 target three years forward, with a 1.28-1.46% free-cash-flow yield well below the risk-free rate. Holding a 30x multiple on the FY2029 target produces a 2.5% annualized return; a 10% annualized return requires 37x FY2029 earnings in mid-2029. Against that, the specific risks that could break the path are concrete and largely unpriced: A&D concentration rising to 65.4% with explicit Boeing dependence and a mix shift toward new-build structural product, 96% of segment profit in one segment with Medical down 22% and PEP down 9%, a brownfield project whose cost and return are undisclosed, single-site equipment concentration at the same location as the expansion, and an unresolved CEO succession following the death of the incoming CEO three weeks into the role. The July 6-30 decline from $619 to $472 on no operating disappointment showed what this multiple does under stress.

Four prior reviews rated the shares HOLD while the business improved faster than the multiple. The FY2029 target now quantifies how little runway remains, and the expected return no longer compensates for the variance. Overall score 5.9. **REDUCE**, with re-engagement at $440-460 or on disclosure that permits an incremental return test on the capacity expansion.
