**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FB Financial Corporation is a $16.5B-asset Nashville-based bank holding company (parent of FirstBank) that materially reshaped itself with the July 1, 2025 acquisition of Southern States Bancshares. The deal added roughly 25% to total assets and drove Q1 2026 results that look far stronger than trailing full-year figures suggest: net income of $57.5M (up 46% YoY), diluted EPS of $1.10 (up 31%), net interest margin of 3.94% (up 39bps), ROAA of 1.43% and ROATCE of 14.7%. FY2025 GAAP earnings of $122.6M were depressed by roughly $113M of non-recurring merger items (a day-one CECL provision, integration costs, and a securities repositioning loss), so the Q1 2026 run-rate is the cleaner read on earnings power. An analyst anchored to trailing GAAP would understate this franchise.

The forward signals are less flattering than the trailing improvement. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen across every horizon over the past 90 days (current year -1.9% to $4.67, next year -2.5% to $5.07, current quarter -2.7% to $1.15), and roughly 17bps of the reported 3.94% NIM comes from purchase-accounting accretion that will amortize over the next several quarters, leaving core NIM closer to 3.77%. The balance sheet is asset-sensitive (-100bps rates → -3.6% NII) into a Fed that is cutting, and the acquired Southern States book carried an unusually high 17% purchased-credit-deteriorated classification, meaning FBK inherited a meaningfully weaker credit portfolio that needs to season.

We rate FBK a HOLD (growth_hold). The franchise quality, capital strength (CET1 11.5%), and confident post-merger capital return (raised dividend, expanded $175M buyback) support the case for continued ownership, and the score trajectory (5.2 → 6.0 → 5.85) reflects a real fundamental improvement now largely captured. But the stock trades at roughly 12x the normalized run-rate and at a ~15%+ premium to regional-bank peers on trailing P/E, while estimate revisions are negative. That combination does not offer the asymmetric upside required for a BUY, and the balance sheet is too sound and the earnings power too genuine for an AVOID. The upgrade path runs through positive estimate revisions and a clean seasoning of the acquired book.

## Company Overview

FB Financial operates through its wholly-owned subsidiary FirstBank, a Tennessee state-chartered bank running 90 full-service branches across Tennessee (Nashville, Chattanooga, Knoxville, Memphis, Jackson), Alabama (Birmingham, Huntsville, Anniston, Auburn), Kentucky, and Georgia, plus commercial services in Asheville, NC. The company reports two segments: Banking, which generated $73.5M of the $74.2M in Q1 2026 pre-tax income, and a small Mortgage segment (~$0.6M pre-tax income) that carries a $9.5B servicing portfolio and $147M of mortgage servicing rights at fair value.

The loan book ($12.5B gross held-for-investment) is well diversified: C&I 18%, CRE non-owner-occupied 23%, CRE owner-occupied 18%, 1-4 family residential 15%, construction 9%, multi-family 6%, residential lines 6%, consumer 5%. The only concentration exceeding 10% of loans is geographic (Tennessee, with growing Alabama/Georgia exposure). The competitive edge is a low-cost core deposit franchise (18.9% noninterest-bearing) supplemented by an M&A-driven growth strategy that uses stock as acquisition currency to densify in-footprint markets.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant financial story is the Southern States acquisition and the bifurcation it created between trailing GAAP and current run-rate. FY2025 net income of $122.6M (0.84% ROAA) absorbed a ~$28.4M initial CECL "double-count" provision on acquired non-PCD loans and unfunded commitments, ~$23.8M of merger and integration costs, and a ~$60.5M securities repositioning loss. Stripping those, Q1 2026 is the representative quarter.

Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025:
- Net income: $57.5M vs. $39.4M (+46%)
- Diluted EPS: $1.10 vs. $0.84 (+31%)
- Net interest income: $146.0M vs. $107.6M (+36%)
- NIM (tax-equivalent): 3.94% vs. 3.55%
- ROAA 1.43%, ROATCE 14.7%
- Efficiency ratio 55.2% (54.3% adjusted) vs. 60.9%

The NIM expansion has two moving parts worth separating. The durable component comes from declining deposit costs (cost of interest-bearing deposits fell to 2.80% from 3.13% as the Fed cut the target from 4.25-4.50% to 3.50-3.75%) combined with elevated loan yields carried over from the acquired book. The temporary component is the accretion of the purchase discount on Southern States loans ($6.3M in the quarter). As that discount amortizes, core NIM sits closer to 3.77%, and investors should not extrapolate the full 3.94%.

The balance sheet is sound. Loans HFI carry a 1.49% allowance for credit losses. Deposits of $14.1B produce an 88.8% loan-to-deposit ratio. Capital is comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds: CET1 11.5%, total risk-based 13.4%, Tier 1 leverage 10.4%, TCE/TA 9.91%. Tangible book value per share rose to $31.00 from $30.27 at year-end 2025; book value per share is $38.39. The AOCI loss narrowed sharply to -$35.3M from -$101.7M a year earlier as the AFS securities mark improved with falling rates; unrealized AFS losses of $51.5M are manageable, and the company opted out of including AOCI in regulatory capital. Uninsured/uncollateralized deposits are 28.8% of total, moderate versus the 2023-era failure cases, with liquidity coverage at 255% of that exposure.

Capital allocation signals confidence. The dividend was raised to $0.21/quarter from $0.19, $21.8M of stock was repurchased in Q1 2026, and post-quarter the board replaced the $150M buyback with a new $175M authorization through June 2027. A $35.6M dividend was upstreamed from the Bank to fund holding-company returns. Doing this within months of a 25% balance-sheet expansion, at 11.5% CET1, indicates management views the integration as on track.

## Growth Analysis

FBK's growth is a combination of M&A-driven scale (Southern States densified Birmingham/Huntsville and extended into Alabama/Georgia) and organic exposure to attractive Sun Belt markets. The 31% YoY EPS growth and 36% net interest income growth in Q1 2026 are largely the annualization of the acquisition rather than pure organic expansion, which sets a high comparison base once the deal fully laps in Q3 2026.

The forward growth picture requires care. The uniformly negative estimate-revision trend is the single most reliable forward indicator in bank analysis and argues against extrapolating the Q1 momentum. The falling estimates likely reflect the market pricing in (a) the fading accretion tailwind, (b) the asset-sensitivity headwind as the Fed continues cutting, and (c) a slowing of the merger-driven step-change once comparisons normalize.

The offsetting positives are the deposit-cost dynamic (further short-rate cuts continue to lower funding costs even as they pressure loan yields) and a re-steepening yield curve that supports the deposit-funded model. But with next-year consensus implying ~$5.07 EPS against the Q1 run-rate of ~$4.40 annualized, the Street is embedding both the acquisition annualization and modest organic growth, and it is trimming that number, not raising it. The estimate revision signal here is negative, not the +30% velocity that would flag a strong BUY.

## Valuation Assessment

On the profile's trailing/forward P/E of 20.4x (EPS $2.45, which is distorted by the merger-year GAAP figures), the headline multiple is misleading. The more useful lens is the normalized run-rate: at ~$4.40-4.67 forward EPS and $55.90, FBK trades at roughly 12x forward earnings, in line with the regional-bank peer median P/E of 12.0. On tangible book value of $31.00, the stock trades at ~1.8x TBV, a premium that a 14.7% ROATCE partially justifies but does not make cheap.

Against the peer set, FBK looks fully valued rather than mispriced. The comparables — SBCF (21.0x), WAFD (12.0x), PFS (9.9x), CUBI (9.8x), TRMK (12.0x), median 12.0x — show FBK sitting at or slightly above the median on normalized earnings while carrying above-average CRE concentration and an integration overhang. The dividend yield of 1.5% is well below the peer median of 2.5%, so there is limited income support to the valuation. The bank's ROATCE and clean efficiency ratio warrant a modest premium, but the negative estimate revisions and fading accretion argue that the current price already reflects the improved earnings power rather than mispricing it.

The analyst target range (low $63, high $67, mean $64.50) implies ~13-20% upside from $55.90, and the current price sits below the entire published target band. On a stock with rising estimates, we would treat those targets as floors. Here, with estimates falling, the targets are more likely stale on the optimistic side and vulnerable to downward revision. We do not treat the target gap as clean upside. A cheap-looking normalized multiple in a bank that faces asset-sensitivity headwinds and a seasoning acquired credit book is a fair value, not an asymmetric one. We score valuation 4.8 — the absence of a specific mispricing catalyst combined with negative revision momentum keeps this below neutral, consistent with treating apparent cheapness in regional banks as requiring an independent catalyst before rewarding it.

## Competitive Landscape

FBK is a well-run mid-cap Southeastern regional operating in structurally attractive markets. Its franchise strength rests on a low-cost core deposit base and disciplined underwriting, supplemented by a repeatable in-footprint M&A playbook. Its TSR of 169 modestly exceeded the peer group's 153, evidence of relative outperformance among regionals.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than clearly widening. The Southern States deal increased scale and market density in Alabama and Georgia, which supports the deposit franchise and lending capacity, and the NIM reflects a genuine funding-cost advantage as rates fall. The offsetting industry-wide concern is CRE competition and stress: FBK's CRE-to-capital ratio of ~260% is elevated (though below the 300% regulatory guideline), with office (~$495M) and hotel (~$510M) exposures within the non-owner-occupied book that will face competitive and credit pressure if the CRE cycle deteriorates. The bank's competitive position is defensible and modestly improving on the deposit and scale dimensions, which supports a 6.3, but not the widening-moat trajectory that would earn a 7+.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger integration and acquired credit quality** is the central risk. The 17% PCD classification is high versus the single-digit PCD typical of bank deals. Nonperforming loans HFI rose to $119.5M (0.96% of loans) from 0.79% a year earlier, with nonperforming construction jumping to $30.6M. Net charge-offs remain low (0.11% annualized) and ACL coverage of nonaccruals is a healthy ~202%, but the trend in classified and nonperforming assets bears close watching as the acquired portfolio seasons over the next several quarters.

**Asset sensitivity into a cutting Fed** is a structural headwind. The current NIM is flattered by both the accretion tailwind and the lag in deposit repricing; continued rate cuts compress the durable margin even as they lower funding costs.

**CECL methodology change** — the switch to a discounted-cash-flow model effective June 30, 2025 (coinciding with the merger) reduces comparability and introduces modeling sensitivity to macro inputs, and warrants scrutiny of provision trends.

**Governance items** are modest: the consolidation of CFO and COO responsibilities under Michael Mettee concentrates senior responsibility, and a legacy registration rights agreement with founder James Ayers is a latent share-overhang consideration. The manufactured-housing lender equity stake ($250M loan-purchase commitment, $148M on balance sheet) is an idiosyncratic higher-yield/higher-risk niche embedded in the book.

Most of these risks are known and at least partially priced, which keeps the risk score near-neutral rather than low. The acquired-book seasoning is the most credible source of an unpriced negative surprise.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.9% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 27.5% (an 11.4pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (38.9% near-term vs. 28.4% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress consistent with the July 13, 2026 Q2 earnings date. The near-term skew is call-heavy (-21.3%), not put-heavy, so options positioning does not corroborate a directional bearish thesis; it prices a two-sided event with a modest upside tilt. IV has fallen from 48.0% to 38.9% since late May as the price rose ~4.9%, suggesting some uncertainty has resolved. The implied one-month move of ±7.0% frames the Q2 print as the near-term binary. Short interest at 5.2% of float (5.8 days to cover) is uncrowded and does not signal informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($64-67).** The Southern States integration completes cleanly, cost synergies land, and the acquired book seasons without material charge-off escalation. Core NIM holds near 3.77-3.85% as deposit costs continue to fall faster than loan yields, ROATCE stays in the mid-teens, and the $175M buyback shrinks the share count. Estimate revisions turn positive as the Street catches up to the normalized run-rate, driving a re-rating toward the analyst target band and ~13-20% upside.

**Bear Case ($45-48).** The 17% PCD acquired book seasons worse than expected, nonperforming construction and CRE (office/hotel) losses climb, and the DCF-based CECL model forces larger provisions. The accretion tailwind fades faster than deposit-cost relief, compressing NIM, while continued Fed cuts pressure the asset-sensitive balance sheet. Estimates keep falling, and the ~1.8x TBV premium compresses toward peers, implying roughly -15% to -20% from current levels.

The base case sits between these: a well-capitalized franchise earning a mid-teens ROATCE, fairly valued at ~12x normalized earnings, with the accretion fade and acquired-book seasoning offsetting the deposit-cost tailwind, leaving the stock range-bound near current levels pending an estimate-revision turn.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Timeframe: 12 months, with the July 13, 2026 Q2 print and subsequent quarters (as the acquired book seasons and accretion fades) the key checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus EPS estimates for the current or next year turn positive over a 90-day window (reversing the current -1.9% to -2.5% trend), confirming the market is catching up to the normalized run-rate.
- Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-improving credit metrics on the acquired book — nonperforming loans holding at or below ~0.96% and construction NPLs declining from $30.6M — confirming the 17% PCD portfolio is seasoning cleanly.
- Core (ex-accretion) NIM holding at or above ~3.80% into H2 2026, demonstrating durable funding-cost advantage rather than a temporary accretion boost.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A step-change in credit deterioration: net charge-offs rising above ~0.40% annualized or nonperforming assets exceeding ~1.5% of loans as the acquired book seasons.
- A guidance cut or provision spike tied to the DCF-based CECL model that materially lowers the forward run-rate.
- Core NIM compressing toward ~3.5% as accretion fades and asset sensitivity bites, with no offsetting deposit-cost relief.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release on July 13, 2026, with the acquired-book seasoning story unfolding over the subsequent two to three quarters.

## Conclusion

FB Financial is a genuinely improved franchise: the Southern States acquisition lifted scale, the Q1 2026 run-rate (14.7% ROATCE, 1.43% ROAA, 55.2% efficiency ratio) is strong, capital is ample at 11.5% CET1, and management is returning capital confidently. The score trajectory from 5.2 to 6.0 reflected that real improvement, and the current 5.85 acknowledges that the trailing-versus-run-rate gap the earlier reports flagged has now largely been recognized by the market. The stock is up ~19% over the trailing year and sits at a fair ~12x normalized earnings.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of falling estimates (uniformly negative revisions across all horizons), a temporary accretion tailwind inflating reported NIM by ~17bps, an asset-sensitive balance sheet facing a cutting Fed, and a valuation that carries a premium to peers on both P/E and TBV without an identified mispricing catalyst. The 17% PCD acquired book is the credible source of an unpriced downside surprise, and it needs to season. We would upgrade on a turn in estimate revisions plus two clean credit quarters, and downgrade on any meaningful acquired-book deterioration. For now, the earnings power justifies holding for the 12-month growth thesis, but the risk/reward does not clear the bar for adding.
