## Executive Summary

PTC Inc. is a $16B industrial software company focused on product lifecycle management (PLM) and computer-aided design (CAD) for discrete manufacturers, trading at $134 — 39% below its 52-week high and at a 73% discount to its application software peer median P/E. The stock has been punished by two forces: declining estimates (-5.6% current-year EPS over 30 days) driven by manufacturing macro sensitivity, and the broader sell-off in mid-cap software. At ~17.5x current-year consensus EPS of $7.66 and a 6.4% FCF yield, the market is pricing in a meaningful deterioration that the company's 4/4 earnings beat record (average 33.5% surprise), 83.7% gross margins, and $1.1-1.3B buyback program do not support.

The prior score trajectory shows PTC declining from 7.0 (BUY) in February to 6.5 (HOLD) in March, driven by falling Financial Health and Risk Factor scores. The financial data does not support continued deterioration: Q1 FY2026 (reported February 4) showed 21% revenue growth, 45% non-GAAP operating margins, and $267M in FCF, all beating guidance. The Kepware/ThingWorx divestiture closed March 16 at $523M, providing proceeds for buybacks and sharpening the business focus. What has changed since March is the macro environment — a naval blockade driving oil above $102, a potential 45-50% recession probability, and manufacturing customers likely to delay IT spending. These risks are real but quantifiable, and PTC's 95% recurring revenue base and 31% FCF margins provide substantial downside protection. The valuation discount is too wide for a business of this quality.

## Company Overview

PTC operates in two segments: PLM (62% of software revenue) and CAD (38%). Flagship products include Windchill (PLM), Creo (CAD), Codebeamer (ALM), and ServiceMax (field service). The company serves 30,000+ customers across industrials, aerospace & defense, automotive, electronics, and medical technology. Revenue is 95% subscription/recurring, with ~50% of revenue denominated in non-USD currencies (primarily EUR, JPY, ILS, INR).

PTC completed its divestiture of the Kepware/ThingWorx IoT business to TPG for $523M in March 2026, creating the separate "Velotic" entity. This sharpens PTC's focus on core CAD + PLM — the higher-margin, more defensible parts of the business — and funds an aggressive $1.1-1.3B share repurchase program in FY2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue reached $2.74B (+19.2% YoY), though management attributes the acceleration to longer-duration contracts that front-load license recognition under ASC 606. Constant-currency ARR growth of 8.5% (9% ex-Kepware/ThingWorx in Q1 FY2026) is the more reliable measure of underlying business momentum. Q1 FY2026 revenue of $686M beat consensus by 7.4%.

**Profitability:** GAAP operating margins expanded ~1,030 bps to 35.9% in FY2025, with non-GAAP operating margins hitting 47.5%. Cost of revenue was flat at $445M on 19% revenue growth — the operating leverage from subscription license revenue (96%+ gross margin) is extraordinary. Headcount grew only 2% versus 19% revenue growth. Gross margins of 83.7% are best-in-class.

**Free Cash Flow:** $857M in FY2025 (31.3% FCF margin), up 16% YoY, with minimal capex requirements ($11M). Management guides to ~$1B FCF in FY2026 (+17%). FCF/net income ratio exceeds 100%, indicating high-quality earnings with no accrual concerns.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt reduced from $1.75B to $1.2B in FY2025 — significant deleveraging. D/E ratio of 0.73x is manageable. $4.3B in goodwill and intangibles (65% of total assets) reflects acquisition history but is typical for subscription software companies. Interest expense dropped 36% to $77M as debt was paid down. The $500M in 4.0% senior notes due February 2028 requires monitoring but is well-covered by cash generation.

**Capital Allocation:** FY2026 plan is $1.115-1.315B in share repurchases, representing 8-10% of current market cap. This is funded by operating cash flow (~$1B) plus Kepware proceeds ($523M). At $134/share, the buyback yield alone is approximately 7-8%, providing a hard floor for shareholder returns even if growth disappoints.

## Growth Analysis

ARR growth of 9% cc (ex-Kepware) is the key metric. PLM and CAD ARR both grew 13% YoY in Q1 FY2026. Three secular tailwinds support mid-to-high single-digit organic growth:

1. **Software-defined products:** Increasing software content in manufactured products drives demand for Codebeamer (ALM) and integrated PLM workflows. This is structural, not cyclical.
2. **Digital thread/twin adoption:** Multi-year manufacturing digitization trend. PTC's installed base of 30,000+ customers provides an upsell opportunity.
3. **SaaS transition:** Cloud-native versions (Windchill+, Creo+, Onshape) are earlier in the adoption cycle and carry ratable revenue recognition that will reduce quarterly lumpiness over time.

Against these tailwinds, estimate revisions are negative: current-year EPS consensus has fallen 5.6% in 30 days (from $8.12 to $7.66) and next-year has dropped 5.4% (from $8.74 to $8.27). The 90-day trend for next quarter is -12.0%. This negative momentum is the primary headwind and reflects legitimate concerns about manufacturing capex cycles in a tariff-heavy, potentially recessionary environment. However, PTC has beaten EPS estimates by an average of 33.5% over the last four quarters, suggesting consensus may again be too conservative.

## Competitive Landscape

PTC competes against Dassault Systèmes (PLM/CAD), Siemens Digital Industries (PLM), and Autodesk (CAD). Its advantages include deep specialization in discrete manufacturing, a broad product portfolio spanning design through service, and increasingly competitive SaaS offerings. Its disadvantage is narrower end-market focus: PTC's fortunes are tied to manufacturing IT budgets in a way that horizontal software companies are not.

The Kepware divestiture removes the underperforming IoT business and eliminates the complexity of competing against hyperscalers in the industrial IoT space. The remaining portfolio (Windchill, Creo, Codebeamer, ServiceMax) is well-positioned in mature, defensible categories with high switching costs. PLM systems are deeply integrated into engineering workflows; replacement cycles are measured in years, not months.

Competitive risk is moderate. PTC's moat derives from workflow integration and data lock-in rather than technological superiority. AI presents both opportunity (product data management as the foundation for AI-driven manufacturing) and risk (competitors may incorporate generative AI into CAD/PLM more quickly).

## Risk Assessment

**Manufacturing cyclicality (HIGH).** PTC's customer base consists overwhelmingly of discrete manufacturers. In an environment of $102+ oil, potential 50% China tariffs, record-low consumer sentiment (47.6), and 45-50% recession probability, manufacturing capex is at risk. PTC's own risk factors cite tariffs and supply chain disruptions as reasons customers may defer purchases. The 50% non-USD revenue exposure creates additional FX headwind when the dollar strengthens (as it typically does in risk-off environments).

**Estimate revisions are negative (MEDIUM-HIGH).** If the macro environment continues to deteriorate, further cuts are probable. The 4/4 beat record provides some comfort but does not eliminate the risk that the underlying business slows.

**CFO transition (MEDIUM).** Jennifer DiRico replaced Kristian Talvitie as CFO on January 1, 2026. Talvitie liquidated $9.2M in stock upon departure. New CFOs introduce execution uncertainty, particularly during a period of aggressive capital allocation.

**SaaS transition penalty (MEDIUM).** As PTC migrates customers from on-premises subscription to SaaS, revenue recognition shifts from upfront to ratable. This creates a mechanical headwind to GAAP revenue growth during the transition. Management has not quantified the expected timing or magnitude.

**Goodwill concentration (LOW-MEDIUM).** $4.3B in goodwill and intangibles (65% of assets) creates impairment risk if growth disappoints, though PTC's track record of integration has been reasonable.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($190-210):** Manufacturing macro stabilizes or proves more resilient than feared. PTC continues to beat estimates. FY2026 EPS lands at $8.50+ (above current consensus), reflecting the beat pattern. Buybacks reduce share count by 8-10%, providing mechanical EPS support. SaaS transition gains traction. At 22-25x $8.50 EPS, the stock reaches $187-213. Analyst consensus median of $190 represents this scenario.

**Bear Case ($110-120):** Manufacturing recession materializes. Customers defer PLM/CAD purchases. ARR growth decelerates to 4-5%. Estimates are cut another 15-20%, bringing FY2026 EPS to ~$6.50. The stock trades at 17-18x trough earnings: $110-117. This would represent a return to the 52-week low area. The buyback program provides some support even in this scenario, but earnings compression would dominate.

**Base Case ($165-175):** Moderate macro slowdown with PTC's recurring revenue base limiting the damage. FY2026 EPS lands around $7.50-8.00 (in line to slightly above consensus). The multiple re-rates toward 21-22x as the Kepware divestiture simplifies the story and buybacks compress the share count. At 21.5x $7.75 EPS = $167.

## Conclusion

PTC at $134 is a high-quality subscription software business priced as a low-quality cyclical. The 73% P/E discount to peers, 6.4% FCF yield, 83.7% gross margins, 4/4 earnings beat record, and $1.1-1.3B buyback program collectively create a risk/reward profile that justifies a BUY. The specific risk preventing max-conviction is the manufacturing cyclical exposure in an environment of rising recession probability, tariff uncertainty, and negative estimate revisions — these are real and could pressure the stock further in the near term. But PTC's 95% recurring revenue, 31% FCF margins, and aggressive capital return program limit the magnitude and duration of any drawdown. The prior HOLD recommendation at $134 does not reflect the accumulated evidence: valuation at trough multiples, improving operations, and aggressive buybacks create a setup where even moderate macro resilience generates meaningful returns.
