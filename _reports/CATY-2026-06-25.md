**Research Date: June 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cathay General Bancorp, the $4.2 billion-cap holding company for Cathay Bank, has delivered one of the cleaner operational stories among Chinese-American niche regionals over the past year. Q1 2026 net income of $86.9M ($1.29 diluted EPS) rose roughly 25% year-over-year, net interest margin expanded to 3.43% from 3.25%, and the efficiency ratio improved to a best-in-class 40.35%. The standout development is credit: non-accrual loans have fallen 42% year-over-year to $89.0M, net charge-offs ran at just 0.04% annualized in Q1, and allowance-to-non-performing-loan coverage jumped to 237.5% from 119% a year earlier. The 2024-2025 credit stress that drove a $72.6M FY2025 provision is resolving favorably, and the stock is up 37.6% over the trailing year to $61.62.

The mechanical driver of forward earnings is the funding base. With time deposits at 47% of funding and 99.7% maturing within 12 months, CATY's deposit costs reprice lower quickly as the Fed eases. Cost of interest-bearing deposits already dropped to 2.96% from 3.43% year-over-year, and that repricing should continue to lift NIM into 2026. Consensus 2026 EPS of ~$5.48 implies roughly 18-19% growth, part margin-driven and part share-count-driven (shares fell from 70.0M to 67.0M YoY, with a new $150M buyback authorized in April 2026).

The constraint is price. At $61.62 the stock trades near its 52-week high of $61.97 and above the consensus mean target of $57.40 and median of $56.00. Most of the rerating off a depressed credit-cycle base has occurred. The forward P/E of ~11.3x on the $5.48 estimate is reasonable for a bank generating an 11.9% ROE and 1.47% ROA, but it is no longer cheap, the analyst panel skews neutral-to-negative (one strong-sell among five ratings), and insiders have sold without a single open-market buy. We rate CATY HOLD as a growth hold: the operating thesis is intact and the NIM tailwind is durable, but risk/reward at the current price is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Cathay General Bancorp is the Delaware holding company for Cathay Bank, founded in 1962 to serve Chinese-American individuals and businesses. The ethnic-niche focus is its defining competitive feature: deep cultural and linguistic ties to an underserved customer base, long-tenured borrower relationships, and a low-cost relationship deposit base that insulate it from money-center competition. It operates roughly 67 branches concentrated in high-density Asian-population markets (41 in California — 24 Southern, 17 Northern — 9 in New York, plus footholds in Washington, Illinois, Texas, Maryland, Massachusetts, Nevada, and New Jersey), a full branch in Hong Kong, and representative offices in Taipei, Beijing, and Shanghai. Total assets stand at ~$24.05B.

The business model is a relationship-based commercial and CRE lender funded by sticky, community-based deposits. The loan book is CRE-dominated: commercial real estate $10.59B (52.5% of loans), residential mortgage and equity lines 29.8%, commercial 16.3%, and construction 1.4%. Its closest comparable is East West Bancorp (EWBC), a larger competitor in the same niche; smaller peers include Preferred Bank, RBB Bancorp, and Hope Bancorp.

## Financial Analysis

The quarter-over-quarter and year-over-year trends point consistently in one direction.

**Earnings and profitability.** Q1 2026 net income compares to $69.5M ($0.98) in Q1 2025. ROA of 1.47% and ROE of 11.88% are strong for a CRE-heavy regional. The efficiency ratio improved from 45.6% a year ago, ranks among the best in the regional bank space, and reflects genuine operating discipline rather than one-time items.

**Net interest margin.** NIM rose as deposit costs fell faster than asset yields. Net interest income grew ~10% to $194.2M. Time deposits repriced lower in the easing-rate environment, dropping the cost of interest-bearing deposits. This is a structural tailwind so long as the Fed eases, though it cuts the other way: the 10-Q simulation shows the balance sheet remains asset-sensitive, with a +200bps shock reducing NII by 3.7%.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $24.05B; gross loans $20.17B (essentially flat QoQ at +0.1%); deposits $20.68B (down 1.0% QoQ). Loan growth has stalled, which caps the earnings story to margin and credit normalization rather than volume.

**Capital.** CET1 of 13.47% (holdco), total capital 15.20%, and leverage 11.15% leave CATY comfortably well capitalized with room to fund both the dividend and the buyback. The dividend was raised to $0.38/quarter, a ~31% payout that is well covered, yielding ~2.5%.

**Capital quality nuance.** EPS growth has been amplified by buybacks against roughly flat absolute net income versus peak years — FY2025 net income (~$315M) was below FY2022's $360M, but EPS held up on the ~5% share-count reduction. The projected ~18.9% 2026 EPS growth is part margin-driven and part share-count-driven. Standard solvency screens (Altman Z, debt/equity) are not meaningful for a structurally leveraged bank; the regulatory capital ratios above are the relevant lens, and they are healthy. We score financial health 7.0, reflecting strong capital, excellent efficiency, and improving credit, tempered by elevated uninsured deposits (below) and flat loan growth.

## Growth Analysis

CATY's forward earnings growth rests on three legs, two durable and one finite.

**Margin repricing (durable).** The dominant driver. With half the funding base in sub-12-month time deposits, NIM should continue to expand if the Fed eases further. This is the highest-confidence component of the growth thesis and the reason the company can post mid-to-high-teens EPS growth without loan growth.

**Credit normalization (finite but real).** The reversal of the 2024-2025 credit cycle has lifted earnings by reducing the provision drag. Q1 2026 provision was $18.2M against only $2.1M of charge-offs. As the credit cycle continues to normalize, provisioning should ease, but this lever is largely spent once non-accruals stabilize — the 42% YoY decline cannot repeat indefinitely.

**Buybacks (mechanical).** The new $150M authorization on a $4.2B cap is meaningful (~3.6% of shares at current prices) and supports EPS even with flat net income.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive but not the +30% signal that would force a different read: current-year EPS revised +4.4% over 90 days to $5.48, next year +3.9% to $5.90, with both flat over the trailing 30 days. The earnings surprise record is mixed — two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with a strong +22.7% beat in Q1 2026 but small misses in Q2 and Q3 2025. This argues for moderate rather than high confidence in continued upside surprises. Flat loan growth is the principal limiter on the growth score; we assign 6.0, recognizing a credible mid-teens EPS path that is more normalization-and-financial-engineering than organic franchise expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $61.62, CATY trades at ~11.3x the $5.48 current-year consensus and ~10.4x the $5.90 next-year estimate. On trailing EPS of $4.54, the multiple is ~12.7x. For a bank earning an 11.9% ROE with a 40% efficiency ratio and improving credit, that is a reasonable, slightly-below-peer-average multiple, but not a deep discount, and the cheapness is no longer the asymmetric setup it was when the stock sat in the mid-$40s a year ago.

The analyst panel frames the ceiling: consensus mean target $57.40, median $56.00 — both below the current $61.62 — with a high of $70.00 and a low of $47.00. The stock has run past the central tendency of sell-side targets. The recommendation distribution is cautious: across the current and prior three months, the panel reads one buy, three holds, and one strong-sell, with no strong buys. This is the opposite of the rising-estimates-plus-cheap-multiple configuration where analyst targets function as floors. Here, with the stock above consensus and revisions only modestly positive, targets read more like ceilings.

On the broader macro backdrop: the regional-bank tape is being shaped by the prospect of further Fed easing, which supports the deposit-cost-repricing thesis. But CRE-credit concerns cut against CRE-heavy regionals as a group. CATY's conservative CRE book (49% weighted-average LTV, $2.0M average loan) is a relative defense, but the sector overhang is real.

We treat the compressed multiple with appropriate skepticism. A low P/E on a CRE-heavy regional near a 52-week high, sitting above consensus targets, with no fresh catalyst and an asset-sensitive balance sheet, is not by itself a buy signal. The fair-value case supports holding rather than chasing. We score valuation 5.5: the multiple is defensible but the asymmetry that would justify a higher score has compressed with the price.

## Competitive Landscape

CATY's moat is its entrenched position in the Chinese-American banking niche — relationship depth, cultural and linguistic alignment, and a sticky, low-cost deposit base that larger banks struggle to replicate. The trajectory is stable-to-improving: NIM expansion and the credit recovery suggest the franchise is gaining operating leverage rather than ceding ground. The 40% efficiency ratio is evidence of a structurally low-cost operator.

The principal competitive disadvantage is scale. Larger niche peer East West Bancorp has greater lending limits and technology budgets, and the broader regional-bank universe is consolidating. CATY's flat loan growth raises the question of whether the franchise is winning share or simply running off a stable book at higher margins. Relative shareholder return tells a cautionary story: CATY's TSR (176.7) has lagged its peer group (213.1) over the measurement period, which is reflected in the company's pay-for-performance metrics.

The idiosyncratic differentiator is Asia/China exposure. A material share of borrowers are Chinese-American import/export businesses, which gives CATY a cross-border franchise rarely seen at peer regionals and a genuine source of both opportunity and tariff/trade risk. On balance the competitive position is stable and modestly improving on margins; we score 6.7, consistent with the recent upward trajectory in this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial real estate concentration** is the most important risk. CRE plus construction totals $10.88B (~54% of loans), equal to 278% of total risk-based capital — below the 300% regulatory scrutiny threshold and the bank's 400% internal limit, and down from 287% prior. The portfolio is conservatively underwritten (49% weighted-average LTV, 86% of CRE at ≤60% LTV, $2.0M average loan). Office exposure of $1.40B (13% of CRE-and-construction) sits in the most stressed national category. The 10-Q discloses that a 100%-weighted adverse forecast would require ~$70.7M of additional reserves, a meaningful but manageable figure against the capital base.

**The construction book bears watching.** While only $289M total, the construction allowance more than doubled QoQ to $15.4M, and ~$36.6M of construction loans were modified for troubled borrowers in Q1. This is a small pocket of weakness against an otherwise improving portfolio, and it is the one area moving the wrong way.

**Uninsured deposits** are elevated at $9.21B (44.6% of total) uninsured-and-uncollateralized. Management notes available liquidity (FHLB capacity $7.95B + $1.66B unpledged securities + cash) exceeds 100% of that balance, which mitigates but does not eliminate deposit-flight tail risk.

**Geographic and macro concentration** — ~45% of CRE is in California, tying collateral values to one state. The Asia/China and tariff channel is an idiosyncratic sensitivity: the 10-K explicitly flags US-China tariffs and trade restrictions as a route through which borrower revenues and debt-service capacity could be impaired.

**Leadership transition** — long-serving CFO Heng W. Chen retired effective March 1, 2026, succeeded by Albert J. Wang. The transition was orderly and pre-announced, but the 10-K had named Chen as a key dependency. Executive Chairman Dunson K. Cheng remains a founder-era key-person concentration.

Most of these risks are at least partially priced in the modest multiple, and the credit recovery is a genuine de-risking the market may not fully credit. We score risk 5.8, slightly above the prior 5.5, reflecting the improving credit picture against the still-real CRE and uninsured-deposit overhangs.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing the kind of acute stress that would warn off a holder. Near-term ATM IV of 29.2% sits modestly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 25.5% (a 3.7pp spread) but in only the 17th percentile of the current chain. Positioning is call-heavy: put/call skew of -31.8% at the front month and a put/call OI ratio of just 0.21, up from 0.08 a month prior but still firmly bullish-tilted. The term structure is in mild backwardation (29.2% near-term vs 25.6% at six months), suggesting the market expects some event-driven movement in the next month, consistent with the next earnings cycle, but the implied 1-month move of ±8.4% is unremarkable for a bank. Net read: options sentiment is supportive rather than cautionary and does not contradict the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$70):** The NIM-repricing tailwind persists as the Fed continues to ease, lifting margin toward the mid-3.5%s while credit fully normalizes and the provision drag disappears. The $150M buyback compounds EPS toward and beyond $6.00 in 2027. The market re-rates a clean, low-efficiency-ratio, well-capitalized bank toward 12-13x forward, reaching the high analyst target. The conservative CRE book proves its quality through any sector stress, and the office and construction pockets resolve without material losses.

**Bear case (~$47):** A re-acceleration of rates reverses the deposit-repricing tailwind (the balance sheet is asset-sensitive; +200bps cuts NII 3.7%). California CRE and office stress crystallizes, forcing the ~$70.7M reserve build the downside scenario contemplates and reversing the favorable provision trend. Tariff escalation impairs the import/export borrower base. The stock derates back toward the low analyst target of $47 — roughly 24% downside from current — as the market reprices CRE-heavy regionals in a CRE-credit-stress environment.

**Base case (~$60):** EPS reaches ~$5.50-5.90 over the next 12 months on continued NIM expansion and buyback support, the multiple holds around 11x, and the stock trades roughly flat-to-modestly-higher with the dividend providing a ~2.5% carry. This is a total-return outcome in the mid-single digits, adequate but not compelling at the current entry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (expected late July), which will test whether NIM expansion is continuing and whether the construction-book weakness is contained.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$50s, which would restore the ~11x-on-a-cheap-base asymmetry that justified the prior rerating.
- Confirmation that non-accruals and the construction book have stabilized for two consecutive quarters with charge-offs remaining below 0.10% annualized.
- A resumption of organic loan growth (gross loans up >3% YoY) that would shift the story from margin/buyback engineering to franchise expansion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A reversal in credit: non-accruals rising QoQ, or the disclosed ~$70.7M adverse-scenario reserve build beginning to materialize.
- NIM compression as deposit repricing exhausts and asset yields lag, or a rate re-acceleration that turns the asset-sensitivity exposure into an earnings headwind.
- Material deterioration in the $1.40B office book or escalation of US-China tariff measures that visibly impairs the import/export borrower base.

## Conclusion

CATY is a well-run niche bank executing a clean operational recovery: NIM expansion, a 40% efficiency ratio, robust capital, and a sharp credit-quality turn. The growth thesis into 2026 is credible and rests largely on the durable deposit-cost-repricing tailwind. What holds us back from a BUY is the price. At $61.62 the stock sits near its 52-week high, above both the consensus mean ($57.40) and median ($56.00) targets, on a multiple that is fair rather than cheap, with an analyst panel that skews neutral-to-negative and insiders who have sold without buying. The asymmetry that rewarded buyers in the mid-$40s has compressed.

We maintain the HOLD that has stood through the prior two reports, classified as a growth hold: the franchise and the margin tailwind justify continuing to own it, and the improving-trajectory scores (overall 6.1 → 6.3 → 6.2, with competitive position and risk both firming) confirm a strengthening business. The bar to upgrade is a better entry price or fresh evidence of organic loan growth and durable credit stabilization. The bar to downgrade is a credit reversal or a rate re-acceleration that flips the asset-sensitivity exposure against earnings. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
