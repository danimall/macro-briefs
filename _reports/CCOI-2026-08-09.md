## Executive Summary

Cogent is no longer primarily an operating story. At $9.92 the equity is a thin residual claim — roughly 20% of a $2.47B enterprise value — sitting behind $1,591.6M of notes (pro forma for July repurchases) and $630.2M of finance-lease obligations against $263M of pro forma cash. Two dated events, not the quarterly revenue trend, will determine whether that residual is worth $20 or nothing. First, $750M of 7.00% unsecured notes (the 2027 Notes and 2027 Mirror Notes) mature June 15, 2027, have been reclassified to current liabilities, and management's stated plan is to refinance "in the third quarter of 2026, subject to market conditions." We are in that quarter and no completed refinancing has been announced. Second, the T-Mobile IP Transit stream — $700M total, $566.7M received through June 30, 2026, sixteen remaining monthly payments of $8.333M running to October 2027 — is included both in the company's reported "EBITDA as adjusted" and, per the 10-Q, in the 2032 indenture's consolidated cash flow definition. Its expiry mechanically removes ~$100M of annual cash flow and covenant capacity on a known date.

The operating improvement underneath is real and should not be dismissed. Excluding $132.0M of asset-sale gains and $1.4M of lease-termination gains, the Q2 2026 operating loss was -$14.5M versus -$31.5M a year earlier; H1 improved to -$30.9M from -$71.7M. Non-GAAP gross margin rose to 47.0% from 44.4%, network operations expense fell 9.1%, and H1 operating cash flow turned positive at +$18.0M for the first time since the Sprint acquisition. Management retired $138.8M par of 2032 Notes at an average 90.348, capturing ~$13.4M of discount and cutting annual interest by ~$9M, and sold ten data centers for $224.2M net against $93.4M of book value. These are competent decisions.

They are also being outrun by arithmetic. Revenue has declined sequentially for six consecutive quarters ($247.0M in Q1 2025 to $235.6M in Q2 2026). The off-net decline of -$17.7M year over year exceeded the combined +$5.8M from wavelength and +$3.0M from on-net. Average price per megabit on the installed net-centric base fell 34.4% year over year. Post-October 2027, pro forma cash note interest of ~$110.4M plus ~$50M of finance-lease interest totals ~$160M against ex-T-Mobile EBITDA of roughly $184M — before ~$170M of true annual capital intensity (reported capex plus non-cash finance-lease additions) and ~$46M of finance-lease principal. Refinancing $750M of 7% unsecured paper with secured debt at 9-10% adds another $15-22M of interest. The structure is not self-funding on current run-rates; it requires either continued asset sales or a step-change in organic EBITDA within five quarters. That is a solvency question, not a valuation question, and it is why the third consecutive AVOID is warranted despite the 78% decline from the 52-week high.

## Company Overview

Cogent is a facilities-based provider of IP transit (on-net and off-net internet access), private networking, optical wavelength transport, IPv4 address leasing, and data center colocation, reported as a single operating segment under CEO/Chairman David Schaeffer. Scale as of June 30, 2026: 3,627 on-net buildings across 308 metro markets and 58 countries, including 1,867 multi-tenant office buildings and 1,781 carrier-neutral data centers; 1,137 wave-enabled locations across 150 metros in North America; 1.5 million square feet and 155 MW of owned data center capacity; 7,572 access networks; and 115,839 total customer connections (65,556 net-centric, 41,326 corporate, 8,957 enterprise).

The company's shape today is a direct consequence of the May 1, 2023 acquisition of Sprint's US long-haul wireline business from T-Mobile. Cogent paid $1 for the membership interests and then paid the seller $61.1M at closing (plus $5.0M in April 2024) as a working-capital adjustment, while T-Mobile agreed to pay Cogent $700M under an IP Transit Services Agreement. Under ASC 805 Cogent concluded these receipts are consideration to acquire a distressed business — T-Mobile is explicitly not a customer under ASC 606 — so they run through investing cash flows and appear nowhere in service revenue. Management's own characterization: "the Cogent Fiber Business's cash flow was negative at the time of negotiations… it was understood that a payment from T-Mobile to any potential buyer would be required."

The acquired book was $39.5M of monthly revenue, of which 81.7% was low-margin off-net and 11.9% non-core. The strategy has been to keep the fiber and the $458.0M of acquired IPv4 addresses, convert Sprint facilities into data centers, build a wavelength business on owned fiber, and deliberately shed the low-margin acquired revenue while T-Mobile's payments fund the transition. That plan is in its final phase.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** FY2025 revenue was $975.8M, down 5.8% from $1,036.1M. Q2 2026 revenue of $235.6M fell 4.3% year over year and 1.5% sequentially — the sixth consecutive sequential decline. The composition (Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025):

| Line | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-net | $135.4M | $132.3M | +2.3% |
| Off-net | $84.5M | $102.2M | **-17.3%** |
| Wavelength | $14.8M | $9.1M | **+63.8%** |
| Non-core | $0.9M | $2.7M | -67.4% |
| Corporate | $98.6M | $109.0M | -9.6% |
| Net-centric | $107.4M | $97.3M | +10.4% |
| Enterprise | $29.5M | $39.9M | -26.0% |

Off-net remains $84.5M per quarter, 35.9% of revenue, against an acquired book of roughly $97M/quarter at closing — a substantial portion of the runoff still lies ahead. Management frames the decline as deliberate pruning, and the gross margin data supports that framing. But the shrinking lines are contracting faster in absolute dollars than the growing lines are expanding.

**Earnings quality.** Q2 2026 GAAP net income was $66.6M and diluted EPS $1.38 — entirely an asset-sale artifact. Pre-tax income of $83.1M less $132.0M of asset-sale gains, $1.4M of lease-termination gains, and $1.6M of debt-extinguishment gain implies a pre-tax loss ex-gains of -$51.9M. The GAAP "beat" against a -$0.95 consensus is not operational outperformance; the adjusted loss of $0.80 versus a -$1.12 non-GAAP consensus is the like-for-like comparison, and it does represent a fourth consecutive beat on that basis. Separately, D&A fell 29.7% year over year as acquired Sprint equipment became fully depreciated — a non-cash tailwind to reported operating income that says nothing about cash generation or asset replacement needs.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was +$18.0M (from -$7.7M), capex $84.8M (from $114.3M), IP Transit receipts $58.3M, data center proceeds $224.2M, finance-lease principal -$23.0M. The reproducible bridge: $18.0M OCF − $84.8M capex = -$66.8M; +$58.3M IP Transit = -$8.4M; −$23.0M lease principal = **-$31.4M** for the half. The 10-Q discloses that the July 2026 $8.3M payment was pulled into June 30, so normalized the figure is roughly **-$39.8M**. Cogent remains cash-consumptive before asset sales even *with* T-Mobile support. Reported capex also understates capital intensity: H1 finance-lease obligations incurred (dark-fiber IRUs) were $52.1M, so true intensity was $136.9M, or 28.8% of revenue, with a further $96.7M of committed IRU payments not yet on balance sheet.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $3,103.9M against total liabilities $3,138.0M — a stockholders' deficit of -$34.1M and an accumulated deficit of -$685.1M. Cash of $183.7M plus restricted cash of $186.0M totals $369.7M, but more than half is restricted, including ~$168.0M of after-tax data center proceeds that the June 2026 supplemental indenture requires be applied solely to retiring debt at a discount. Current assets of $606.2M against current liabilities of $959.6M produce working capital of -$353.4M, driven by the $746.3M net reclassification of the 2027 maturities.

Debt at par as of June 30, 2026: $450M 2027 Notes (7.00%), $300M 2027 Mirror Notes (7.00%), $579.6M 2032 secured Notes (6.50%), $206.0M existing IPv4 Notes (7.924%, ARD May 2029), $174.4M new IPv4 Notes (6.646%, ARD April 2030), and $630.2M PV of finance leases at an 8.3% weighted rate. Pro forma for the July repurchase of $118.4M par for $106.7M cash: notes $1,591.6M plus leases $630.2M less cash $263.0M ≈ **$1,959M net debt**.

Recomputing Altman Z on the June 30 balance sheet: working capital/assets -0.114, retained earnings/assets -0.221, market equity/liabilities 0.151, sales/assets 0.308, and TTM EBIT/assets of +0.025 on reported figures or -0.019 excluding asset-sale gains. Z lands near 0.03 reported and -0.11 ex-gains, far below the 1.81 distress line. Two caveats matter: the retained-earnings term is depressed partly because Cogent has returned $1.8B to shareholders since its IPO, and the working-capital term is negative solely because a June 2027 maturity crossed the twelve-month line. The score flags a leveraged, cash-burning structure; it does not establish imminent insolvency.

**Leverage, and why the number is contested.** Company-reported "EBITDA as adjusted" margin was 30.2% in Q2 2026, implying ~$71.1M for the quarter and ~$284M annualized. A reconciliation of the Q1 2026 margin (29.3% reported versus 18.9% ex-T-Mobile, a ~$24.9M gap equal to three $8.333M installments) indicates the reported metric includes the T-Mobile receipts. On ~$1,959M of pro forma net debt, that is ~6.9x including the payments and ~10.6x excluding them. The indentures compute below 6.0x consolidated and below 4.0x secured, with fixed-charge coverage above 2.0x — but the 10-Q states that the 2032 indenture's consolidated cash flow definition "includes cash payments from TMUSA." Covenant headroom therefore deteriorates mechanically as the stream rolls off, independent of any operating change.

**Bond marks are the most useful contrary evidence.** Level 2 marks at June 30, 2026: 2027 Notes and Mirror Notes at 99.1% of par, IPv4 Notes at 101.8%, 2032 Notes at 89.5%. Credit markets priced the June 2027 unsecured maturity as very likely to be repaid or refinanced at par and priced the IPv4 collateral above par — a materially less distressed message than a $508M equity value. The honest qualifier is that short paper trades near par mechanically as maturity approaches, and these marks predate both the Q2 print and the August 6-7 equity collapse.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on two lines. **Wavelength** revenue grew 63.8% year over year to $14.8M/quarter with connections up 66.4%, on genuinely differentiated assets: routes largely along railroad rights-of-way providing route diversity, fiber buried deeper with heavier sheathing, 1,137 on-net wave-enabled locations, and installation inside 30 business days. But wavelength ARPU fell 2.9% ($2,100 vs $2,163), so growth is entirely unit-driven in a product Cogent has said it will price aggressively — and it remains only 6.3% of revenue. **Net-centric** revenue grew 10.4% on 16% traffic growth against a 34.4% decline in price per megabit on the installed base. That equation works only while volume outruns price; a modest deceleration in traffic growth flips the segment negative.

Against that, corporate revenue fell 9.6% with connections down 6.7%, in a market where the 10-Q still says "in some markets, office occupancy rates may never return to pre-2020 levels," and enterprise fell 26.0% with connections down 23.9%. Management targeted 6-8% long-term revenue growth on the May 4, 2026 call — a figure I cannot reconcile with six consecutive sequential declines and cannot confirm has been reaffirmed.

Estimate revisions are directionally positive but on a loss-making base: next-year consensus improved to -$2.64 from -$2.95 (+10.7% over 90 days) on three analysts; current-year sits at -$3.87. This is not the >30% inflection-revision signal that argues for buying a depressed multiple, because there is no forward earnings figure to capitalize — consensus does not have Cogent profitable in the forecast window.

## Valuation Assessment

Enterprise value: $508M equity (51.216M shares at $9.92) plus $1,959M pro forma net debt = **$2.467B**, against TTM revenue of ~$957M (2.58x) and EBITDA of $284M including T-Mobile (8.7x) or ~$184M excluding it (13.4x). The peer set is not usable as a direct anchor — UNIT at 3.1x P/E, IRDM at 50.4x, LUMN at -4.9x — because all four names have distinct capital structures and earnings distortions. Cogent's 13.4x EV/EBITDA on the post-cliff base is not a discount to fiber-asset comparables that typically transact at 6-9x.

The more useful exercise is sum-of-parts, since Cogent is being liquidated in pieces:

- **IPv4 (~38M addresses, 15.2M leased):** the most under-discussed asset. The $458.0M carrying value was set at orderly liquidation value using auction prices plus a discount for market uncertainty. The April 2025 New IPv4 Notes indenture permits additional Class A notes only at a Class A Leverage Ratio ≤7.25x; with $380.4M outstanding, compliance implies annualized net cash flow of at least ~$52.5M, and restricted-cash releases of $26.9M (FY2025) and $14.2M (H1 2026) on improving leverage/DSCR suggest actual cash flow above that floor. Third parties underwrote $380.4M at 6.6-7.9% and the notes trade above par. A range of $700M-$1.2B is defensible; call it $800M-1.0B.
- **Core network ex-IPv4, ex-T-Mobile EBITDA:** roughly $131M. At 7x, ~$920M.
- **Remaining 14 data centers plus residual power capacity:** the first ten sold at $225M against $93.4M book. A further $200-250M is plausible, and AI-driven demand for powered shells is a genuine bid.

Total: **$1.92B-2.5B** against $1,959M of net debt. The midpoint leaves equity at roughly zero to $500M — that is, $0 to $10 per share. **At $9.92 the market is already paying the upper end of a generous asset-value range.** That is the core valuation finding, and it is why I do not score cheapness highly despite a 78% drawdown.

Three secondary points. The lowest published analyst target of $11.50 sits above the current price and the mean is $18.32, but Goldman's fresh cut to $12 following the Q2 print is the informative datapoint — the stale mean reflects pre-collapse models, and analyst targets are not independent confirmation when the sell side is revising downward into the event. Consensus distribution has barely moved (1 strong buy, 3 buy, 7 hold, 1 strong sell) even as the stock halved, which says the sell side has not resolved the refinancing question either. And the ~0.7% dividend (annualized ~$4.1M after a 98% cut from ~$196M) provides no valuation support.

## Competitive Landscape

Cogent's structural advantages are real where they exist. Tier 1 settlement-free peering plus a large on-net traffic share means Cogent is compensated by both originating and terminating customers when traffic stays on-net, which is the source of the 47.0% gross margin. The single-protocol Ethernet/IP architecture, deliberately narrow product set, ~380-vendor dark-fiber IRU portfolio, and equipment cascading from core to edge are all genuine cost advantages in a market management itself describes as facing "significant price deflation."

But the trajectory splits by segment. In corporate multi-tenant buildings, Cogent prices at parity and differentiates on speed and installation time into a market with a secular hybrid-work headwind that management concedes may be permanent. In net-centric, "price is typically the only differentiating factor" and Cogent is the price-setter downward — the 34.4% decline in price per megabit is the company's own weapon working against its revenue line. In wavelength, Cogent is attempting to convert a legacy asset into a growth franchise against Lumen, Zayo, Crown Castle Fiber and regional dark-fiber operators whose capacity announcements are not addressed in the filings. IPv4 leasing is the most differentiated position and likely the most undervalued, but the addresses are encumbered inside a bankruptcy-remote SPE where utilization and DSCR triggers can force amortization or, in certain circumstances, allow noteholders to direct a sale of the assets. Only 15.2M of ~38M owned addresses were leased at June 30 — spare capacity, and also evidence that monetization is slower than the asset's theoretical value implies.

Net: the mix is shifting toward higher-margin, more differentiated revenue, while the aggregate franchise shrinks. That is an improving quality trajectory on a contracting base.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The $750M June 2027 maturity (dominant).** Now current. Management "expects to refinance… in the third quarter of 2026, subject to market conditions." Nothing announced through the latest 8-K index. Liquidity is stated adequate for twelve months "and beyond the next 12 months if we execute our business plan, which includes the refinancing" — conditional language. The June 2026 supplemental indenture raised the ratio-liens secured leverage basket from 4.00x to 4.75x, which is the natural prerequisite for refinancing unsecured paper with secured debt. As a negative-equity issuer with declining revenue, Cogent would likely pay above the retiring 7% coupon in any refinancing.

**2. The October 2027 T-Mobile cliff.** $133.3M remaining across sixteen payments. Removes ~$100M/year of cash flow, reported EBITDA, and 2032-indenture covenant capacity on a known date. Derived leverage moves from ~6.9x to ~10.6x on that basis alone. Lenders refinancing in 2026 must underwrite a post-2027 cash flow materially below trailing figures.

**3. Post-refinancing cash coverage.** Pro forma note interest $110.4M + ~$50M finance-lease interest = ~$160M, rising to ~$179M if the 2027s are refinanced at 9.5%. Against ~$184M of ex-T-Mobile EBITDA, that leaves roughly nothing before ~$170M of true capital intensity and ~$46M of lease principal. Closing that gap requires ~$100M of organic EBITDA improvement within five quarters — plausible in part (network operations expense down 9.1% y/y, off-net runoff completing) but not obviously in full on a shrinking revenue base.

**4. Securities class actions.** Filed late July 2026 in the District of Columbia (including *City of Southfield Fire and Police Retirement System v. Cogent*, No. 26-cv-02609), class period February 29, 2024 to May 1/2, 2026, lead-plaintiff deadline September 21, 2026. Per plaintiff-firm descriptions the allegations concern wavelength backlog convertibility, customer demand, and dividend sustainability. These are allegations, not established facts, and Cogent has conceded nothing — but they attack precisely the growth narrative the equity case depends on, and the Q2 10-Q filed August 6 reported no material changes to risk factors after the suits were filed.

**5. IPv4 structural triggers and an October 2026 deadline.** Of $72.6M initially restricted from the April 2025 notes, $26.9M was released in FY2025 and $14.2M in H1 2026; Cogent has until October 2026 to satisfy the performance metrics or remaining restricted funds are applied to prepay the notes pro rata. The $458.0M indefinite-lived intangible has never been impairment-tested quantitatively.

**6. Governance.** FY2025 CEO reported total compensation of $25.373M was the highest of five years, in a year with a $182.2M net loss and with the TSR index falling from 113.59 to 29.83 against a peer index of 114.75. A 1,000,000-share grant to Schaeffer on May 4, 2026 equals ~2.0% of shares then outstanding and dilutes existing holders' proportionate ownership by ~1.95%; I did not read its vesting conditions. Unvested restricted stock rose from 1.66M to 3.29M shares year over year (a basic-EPS timing difference, not incremental dilution above the 51.2M outstanding). No open-market insider purchases appear in the Form 4 records reviewed, while the CFO, a VP and two directors sold at prices from ~$23 down to ~$16. The mitigating read: "compensation actually paid" fell to $8.796M for the CEO and to negative $2.181M for NEO averages, so realized outcomes did track the stock, and a retention grant into an underwater equity stack ahead of a refinancing is conventional. Related-party leases with CEO-owned entities total ~$1.6M per half, immaterial economically, cancellable on 60 days' notice, and Audit Committee reviewed.

**7. What management has done right.** The 98% dividend cut was not covenant-forced ($240.5M of restricted-payment capacity was available) and preserved ~$190M/year of cash. Retiring $138.8M par at 90.348 captured ~$13.4M of discount plus ~$9M/year of interest savings. Selling ten data centers at $225M against $93.4M book, at an implied multiple above the consolidated EV/EBITDA, is accretive to the residual. Capital allocation over the last four quarters has been rational; it is the inherited structure that is the problem.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($22, +122%)

Cogent completes the refinancing in Q3 or Q4 2026 with secured paper at 8-9%, using the newly widened 4.75x lien basket, removing the going-concern-adjacent overhang. The remaining 14 data centers sell into genuine AI-driven demand for powered capacity at multiples above the consolidated 8.7x, each transaction retiring debt at a discount and mechanically transferring value to the residual claim. Off-net runoff completes through 2027, at which point the drag reverses: on-net (+2.3%), net-centric (+10.4%) and wavelength (+63.8%) become the whole company, and gross margin moves from 47% toward the low 50s. Network operations expense declines another 8-10%, adding $40-50M. IPv4 leasing scales from 40% utilization, generating cash flow well above the ~$52.5M floor implied by the indenture leverage test, and a standalone monetization at $25-35 per address values the franchise at $950M-1.3B against $458M of book. Ex-T-Mobile EBITDA reaches $230-250M by late 2027, net debt falls below $1.7B, and the equity re-rates on 8x with $600M-plus of residual value.

### Bear Case ($3, -70%)

The refinancing prices punitively — secured at 11%+ with amortization, or a partial exchange requiring equity issuance — adding $30M+ of annual interest to a structure that already cannot fund itself. The T-Mobile stream ends in October 2027 while revenue is still declining, taking ex-T-Mobile EBITDA to ~$180M against ~$190M of cash interest and lease interest. Data center sale proceeds, contractually earmarked for debt retirement and barred from increasing restricted-payment capacity, exhaust after the remaining 14 assets. Net-centric traffic growth decelerates below the 34% annual price decline and the segment turns negative. The class actions survive dismissal and settle in the tens of millions. IPv4 utilization or DSCR triggers force amortization inside the SPE, or noteholders direct a sale of the addresses on their own timetable. Negative equity becomes a restructuring in 2028 in which the residual is impaired to a stub.

### Base Case ($10, roughly flat)

Refinancing completes at 9-10%, adding ~$18M of interest. Revenue troughs in 2027 around $900M as off-net runoff completes; ex-T-Mobile EBITDA reaches ~$205-215M by 2028 on cost reduction plus mix. Net debt drifts down toward $1.85B on further data center sales. Sum-of-parts at $800M-1.0B for IPv4, ~$920M for the core, and $200-250M for remaining real estate lands EV at $1.95-2.15B, leaving $100-250M of equity value — $2 to $5 on a conservative read, up to $10-11 on the optimistic end of each component. The base case is therefore approximately the current price with extraordinary variance around it, which is precisely why it is not ownable.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The identifiable dated catalysts are two-sided rather than one-way. A refinancing announcement is expected in Q3 2026 (by September 30) and would resolve the dominant overhang favorably; the October 2026 IPv4 restricted-cash performance deadline and the September 21, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline are modest negatives. The genuinely bearish catalyst — the T-Mobile cliff — is fourteen months out (October 2027), well beyond a reasonable carry window. There is no near-dated event whose most likely resolution is materially negative.

**Timing window.** The bearish thesis needs 12-18 months to play out. That is too long to carry a 19.4%-short-interest position through a binary refinancing headline.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 19.4% of float short, 6.94M shares, 4.7 days to cover. Below the 25% threshold but meaningfully crowded, and the stock closed at $9.92 having traded to $9.00 intraday on August 7 — at multi-year lows with a 78% drawdown already realized.

**Borrow and options signal.** Near-term IV of 128.2% versus 96.8% realized makes put protection expensive; the term structure is backwardated, so time decay works against short-dated bearish options. The supplied near-term skew reads call-heavy and there is unusual buying in September $12 calls, meaning options positioning does not confirm the bearish thesis at the front of the curve. Put/call OI of 3.52 shows protection already in place — the trade is not uncrowded.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $3 (-70%). Upside risk on a credible refinancing announcement plus a short squeeze from $9.92 is $15-18 (+50-80%), and the bond marks at 99.1 for the 2027s argue the refinancing is more likely than not. Probability-weighting a two-sided binary produces a reward:risk near 1:1, well below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish analysis is sound but the mechanics are hostile — multi-year lows, 19.4% short interest, expensive and backwardated protection, call-heavy near-term positioning, and a near-dated catalyst whose likely resolution favors the stock. Avoid owning; do not short here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** Do not own; do not initiate; do not short.

**Re-entry criteria — any two of the following would move this to HOLD, and the first two together plus evidence of a revenue trough would move it to ACCUMULATE:**

1. **Refinancing completed and disclosed** for the full $750M of 2027 maturities at an all-in coupon of 9.0% or below, without equity issuance, warrants, or amortization that consumes free cash flow. This is the single gating item. An 8-K or indenture filing is the confirming evidence.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of ex-T-Mobile EBITDA above $55M** (i.e., an annualized run-rate above $220M), demonstrated in a company non-GAAP reconciliation that separates the IP Transit receipts. This would show the ~$100M October 2027 hole is being filled organically rather than through asset sales.
3. **Sequential revenue growth in any quarter**, with off-net revenue below $70M/quarter confirming the acquired runoff is largely complete.
4. **A disclosed IPv4 valuation event** — a partial sale, third-party appraisal, or refinancing of the IPv4 Notes at a materially higher advance rate — that establishes the franchise above its $458M carrying value and outside the constraint of the existing SPE triggers.
5. **Insider open-market purchases** by the CEO or CFO. There have been none at $10-23 while four insiders sold; a cluster of open-market buys by those closest to the refinancing would be a genuine signal.
6. **Price near $4-5** with the refinancing still pending, which would price the bear case and restore option-like asymmetry to the residual. At $9.92 the equity already prices the upper end of a generous sum-of-parts.

**Downgrade to short consideration** only if: the refinancing is not announced by December 31, 2026; or a refinancing is completed with terms that raise cash interest above $200M annually; or IPv4 rapid-amortization/utilization triggers are breached.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are the Q3 2026 refinancing window (through September 30), the October 2026 IPv4 performance deadline, the September 21, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline, and the Q3 2026 print (early November), which will carry the ~$11.8M gain on the July note repurchases and the first full quarter without the ten sold data centers.

## Conclusion

The score moves from 3.3 to 3.25 and the recommendation remains AVOID for a third consecutive cycle — a call I would rather not repeat unchanged, so it is worth being explicit about what the last seven weeks added. Genuinely better: $138.8M par of 2032 Notes retired at 90.348 for ~$13.4M of discount and ~$9M of annual interest savings; ten data centers sold at $224.2M net against $93.4M book; H1 operating cash flow positive for the first time post-acquisition; gross margin up 260bp; network operations expense down 9.1%; the underlying operating loss halved. Genuinely worse: securities class actions filed attacking the wavelength-backlog narrative; a sixth consecutive sequential revenue decline; the T-Mobile stream down to sixteen payments; and the June 2027 maturity now inside twelve months with no announced refinancing. That is close to a wash on fundamentals.

The reason the 30% price decline since the last review does not make this a better opportunity is that the equity was not cheap on asset value before, and it is now approximately fair on the optimistic end of a sum-of-parts that requires IPv4 to be worth double its book value. Enterprise value is $2.47B; a generous parts valuation reaches $1.92-2.5B against $1.96B of net debt. The residual is a levered option on a refinancing that must be priced against post-October-2027 cash flow roughly $100M below trailing. Credit markets are more sanguine than the equity — the 2027s marked at 99.1 and the IPv4 notes above par on June 30 — and that is the strongest disconfirming evidence to this view. But short paper trades near par mechanically into maturity, those marks predate the August collapse, and even a successful refinancing leaves a structure whose cash interest approximates its post-cliff EBITDA before any capital spending.

Investors seeking exposure to Cogent's genuinely differentiated assets — the IPv4 franchise, the route-diverse long-haul fiber, the 155 MW of owned power — are better served by the debt than the equity. The 2032 Notes at ~90 offer a mid-single-digit-to-9% yield with a first lien, collateral covenants strengthened in June 2026, and contractual entitlement to the proceeds of every remaining asset sale. The equity is entitled to whatever is left. Until the refinancing is signed and ex-T-Mobile EBITDA demonstrates a run-rate above $220M, that is not a claim worth owning at $9.92.
