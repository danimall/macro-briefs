**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Capitol Federal Financial is the Topeka, Kansas holding company for Capitol Federal Savings Bank, a ~$9.83B-asset federal savings bank and the dominant retail deposit franchise in its Kansas/Missouri markets. The investment story is a deliberate, multi-year transition from a single-family mortgage thrift with a structurally thin net interest margin into a full-service consumer and commercial bank. That transition is working at the margin: NIM recovered to 2.24% in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (up from 1.92% a year earlier), commercial loans reached 29% of the portfolio (from 26% in September 2025), the efficiency ratio improved to 52.5% from 60.5%, and six-month net income of $40.5M ($0.32 EPS) grew roughly 32% year over year.

The stock has re-rated accordingly, rising about 34% over the trailing twelve months to $8.45. At that level it trades near 1.06x tangible book value ($7.96/share) and roughly 14x forward earnings, in line with the regional-bank peer median P/E of 14.4x. This is fair value for a bank still earning only ~8% ROE, not an obvious mispricing. The durable return here is the ~4% dividend plus book-value accretion from buybacks executed at or near book, rather than multiple expansion. The bull case requires the commercial pivot to lift ROE toward peer levels without a credit accident.

The central risk is that the same commercial growth engine that is expanding NIM is importing concentrated credit risk. Nonaccruals rose to $54.9M (0.68% of loans) from $48.1M, driven almost entirely by one commercial-real-estate borrowing relationship (~$41M+, including a $24.3M nonaccrual loan) that took a lower appraisal and forced a $4.0M specific reserve. Coverage is thin (ACL 0.33% of loans, 48% of nonaccruals), and the CRE book skews toward hotels and out-of-market lending. Combined with a company-specific bad-debt-recapture constraint on dividend capacity, this keeps the name a HOLD for income rather than a BUY for appreciation.

## Company Overview

Capitol Federal ("True Blue") historically operated as a retail mortgage thrift funded by sticky retail CDs and savings deposits. The franchise carries ~$8.1B of net loans, ~$6.9B of deposits, and ~$1.03B of equity. The defining strategic shift is the deliberate run-off of the legacy 1-4 family book (yielding ~3.5–3.8%; the correspondent mortgage-purchase channel was suspended in FY2024) and redeployment of that cash flow into commercial loans yielding ~5.8–6.9%. Management guides to ~20% commercial-loan growth for FY2026 against ~1% total loan growth, alongside build-outs in treasury management, private wealth, digital banking, and small business intended to grow fee income and cheaper commercial deposits.

The franchise's competitive strengths are a low-cost, granular deposit base (only ~11% uninsured, unusually low and a genuine post-2023 stability advantage), strong Kansas brand recognition, and a well-capitalized balance sheet (Bank CBLR 9.5%, Company CBLR 10.0%, Bank tier-1 risk-based 16.1%). The offsetting weakness is a legacy asset yield far below current market rates, which compresses profitability throughout the transition.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability is low but visibly improving as the balance-sheet mix shifts:

- **NIM:** The gain is driven by mix shift into commercial loans plus lower borrowing balances and deposit costs, not by rate cuts (six-month 2.22% vs 1.89%). Loan yield was 4.42%, deposit cost 2.13%, borrowing cost 3.65%.
- **Earnings:** Q2 net income $20.1M ($0.16 EPS) vs $15.4M ($0.12) YoY.
- **Returns:** ROA 0.82%, ROE 7.74% (annualized) — subpar for a bank and reflective of the legacy drag, but trending up.
- **Efficiency ratio:** A meaningful improvement year over year.

On capital and balance sheet, a distinctive feature is the accumulated deficit of $73.8M, a scar from the FY2023 net loss of -$101.7M created by an October 2023 securities-portfolio restructuring (selling low-coupon securities at a realized loss to reposition). The consequence is that AOCI is now positive (+$11.6M): unlike most regional banks, CFFN is not sitting on large unrealized securities losses because it already realized them. Funding is healthy: deposits grew ~$333M over six months, and the company paid down FHLB advances to $1.71B (only ~17% of Bank assets versus a 44% limit), with an estimated $4.35B of available liquidity. Management also prepaid $425M of advances at a 4.32% weighted-average rate and replaced them at 3.79%.

Free-cash-flow and accrual quality metrics that apply to industrials are not the right lens for a depository; the relevant tests are credit provisioning quality and capital-return coverage. On the provisioning side, the six-month provision jumped to $3.5M from $0.7M, almost entirely the specific reserve on one CRE relationship. Capital return is aggressive: 4.53M shares repurchased for $31.7M (~$7.00 avg) in six months plus ~1.0M more through early May, alongside a $0.085 quarterly dividend and a $0.04 January special (~4% yield). Buying back stock near tangible book is accretive to per-share value.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is idiosyncratic: an internal mix shift rather than a demand-driven top-line acceleration. Total loan growth is guided to ~1% for FY26 because the legacy residential book is running off as commercial grows ~20%. The earnings growth (six-month EPS +33%) is real, but it is a re-pricing of the asset base, not volume expansion, and it decelerates as the mix shift matures and the low-hanging NIM recovery is captured. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-quarter EPS revised +3.0% over 90 days, next quarter +8.8%, current year +3.1%, with next year nudged down 2.0%. This is a broadly-rising-but-modest revision trend, not the +30% velocity that would flag a structural re-rating.

The earnings surprise record is mixed: two beats, one in-line, one miss across the last four quarters, including a -4.9% miss in the March 2026 quarter. That pattern argues for calibrating forward estimates to consensus rather than assuming systematic upside. The durable growth question is whether the commercial pivot can lift ROE from ~8% toward the low-teens levels of diversified commercial-bank peers. The path exists — NIM and efficiency are both improving — but the guide is measured and the gains from here are incremental.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the regional-bank peer set, the P/E is right at the median: SBSI 14.4x, TRST 16.1x, CPF 12.1x, HAFC 11.7x (EGBN negative), median 14.4x. The dividend yield of ~4.0% is above the 3.2% peer median, a modest support. On price-to-book, ~1.06x is a fair-to-slightly-full multiple for an 8% ROE — banks generally trade around book when ROE approximates cost of equity, and CFFN's low-teens cost of equity roughly matches its returns.

The estimate revisions are positive but modest (single-digit), and the stock trades below the 50x-forward-P/E premium threshold, so consensus targets carry some weight. Here, though, analyst targets are clustered tightly (low $8.00, high $8.50, mean $8.25) and the current price already sits above the mean; the sell-side sees no upside to fair value at spot. This is consistent with a name that has already re-rated to fair value on the improving fundamentals.

The prior-appreciation caveat matters: the 34% trailing move was driven by genuine fundamental improvement (NIM recovery, ROE lift, EPS +33%), so "it has run" is not by itself a reason to mark it down. The forward math governs the valuation score, and today the stock is fairly priced for its returns. A cheap-looking absolute multiple in banks tends to be a fair reflection of low ROE rather than a mispricing, and the burden of proof for a high valuation score — a specific reason the market is wrong — is not met. Book-value accretion from sub-book/near-book buybacks and the ~4% yield provide a floor and a mid-single-digit total return, but not asymmetric upside from here.

## Competitive Landscape

CFFN competes against national and regional banks, credit unions, farm-credit lenders, and online players for both commercial relationships and deposits. As a subscale, historically mortgage-centric thrift, its NIM sits well below diversified commercial-bank peers — precisely the gap the commercial pivot is meant to close. The strategy mirrors an industry-wide move by thrifts into commercial banking to escape thin mortgage spreads, and it is directionally the right call, but it trades a low-risk (if low-return) residential book for higher-risk commercial credit.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On funding, CFFN is a relative winner: its low uninsured-deposit share and positive AOCI position are genuine differentiators in a post-2023 environment where deposit stability and unrealized bond losses have hurt many peers. On the asset side, the company is a new entrant in commercial lending — including out-of-market hotel and senior-housing credit — where established competitors have deeper underwriting histories, and the first sign of that inexperience has appeared in the concentrated nonaccrual. The pay-versus-performance disclosure underscores the point: CFFN's indexed TSR (~96.6) has badly lagged the peer group (~270.2), reflecting the low-ROE transition. Competitive position is improving on funding and stable-to-improving on profitability, but the commercial franchise is still unproven, which caps this dimension.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Interest-rate risk is the defining exposure.** Long-dated fixed-rate mortgage assets funded by shorter liabilities. The one-year repricing gap is negative (-$792M, -8.1% of assets, improved from -10.1%). NII sensitivity is now modestly asset-sensitive (+2.8% if +200bp, -4.9% if -200bp over 12 months), but MVPE would fall ~24% in a +200bp shock. A sharp rate decline pressures NII, and the low legacy coupon caps upside.

2. **Concentrated CRE credit risk, already materializing.** The single CRE relationship driving the nonaccrual increase is the most important watch item: it is either a contained one-off or an early crack.

3. **CRE composition and geographic concentration.** ~$2.4B gross CRE+construction, with hotels ($629.7M) the largest single collateral category, followed by senior housing ($539.8M) and multi-family. A meaningful share is out-of-market (Texas, Arizona, California, New York); the largest single loan is an $86M Arizona hotel. Portfolio WA LTV is 63% and DSCR 1.76x, but several large loans carry DSCR below 1.15x. Hotel and senior-housing exposure is cyclically sensitive.

4. **Bad-debt-recapture constraint on capital return.** The FY24 tax loss pushed the Bank into negative accumulated E&P; Bank-to-holdco distributions were taxed as pre-1988 bad-debt recapture. The Bank replenished to positive E&P only in Q4 FY25, and future distributions are explicitly capped to avoid re-triggering the tax. All distributions require OCC/FRB non-objection (current $75M buyback authorization expires February 2027). This is a company-specific limiter on the buyback/dividend engine that supports the stock.

5. **Litigation.** The *Harding* overdraft-fee class action (APPSN plus re-presentment claims) was dismissed but revived by the Kansas Supreme Court on 10/17/2025 and remanded, now an active exposure of uncertain magnitude.

6. **Activism risk**, unusually flagged as its own risk factor, consistent with a depressed valuation and low ROE inviting pressure.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose to 64.2% from 57.8% (up 6.4pp) as the price climbed 9.5% into the reading. Rising IV alongside a rising price suggests the market is pricing incremental event/uncertainty rather than complacency — plausibly around the credit trajectory and the next earnings print. This is a thinly-traded small-cap options market, so the signal is low-conviction, but it does not contradict the fundamental read of a name near fair value with a live credit watch item.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($9.50–$10.00).** The commercial pivot continues to lift NIM (toward 2.4%+) and ROE (toward double digits), the efficiency ratio holds in the low-50s, the single problem CRE relationship resolves without further reserves and proves to be a one-off, and buybacks near tangible book plus a ~4% yield deliver a compounding per-share return. At a normalized ~10% ROE the market could support ~1.3x TBV, implying the high end of the range. Positive estimate revisions and insider buying support this path.

**Bear Case ($6.50–$7.00, ~17–23% downside).** The hotel/senior-housing/out-of-market CRE book proves to be an early crack rather than a one-off; broader nonaccruals force the thin ACL sharply higher, provisions consume the NIM gains, and ROE stalls below 8%. A meaningful rate decline compresses NII, MVPE erodes, and the bad-debt-recapture ceiling forces a slowdown in buybacks/dividends. In that scenario the stock reverts toward ~0.85x TBV.

The base case sits between: fair value near 1.05–1.1x TBV with the return coming from the ~4% dividend plus modest book accretion, not multiple expansion. The insider buying at $5.50–$6.00 in 2025 (EVP Jackson repeatedly, Directors Johnson ~33K shares and Morris 15K shares — all open-market) is a genuine positive signal on management alignment, though those purchases were struck well below the current price, so the margin of safety they implied has largely been captured by the re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (income_hold). The stock is fairly valued at ~1.06x TBV and ~14x forward; the thesis for holding is the ~4% yield plus book-value accretion, not near-term appreciation, and the analyst target set (mean $8.25) implies no upside from spot.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The single problem CRE relationship resolves without further specific reserves AND aggregate nonaccruals decline for two consecutive quarters, confirming the credit issue is contained.
- NIM sustains above 2.35% and annualized ROE crosses 9%, evidencing the commercial pivot is closing the profitability gap.
- A pullback toward or below tangible book value (~$8.00 or lower) that restores a margin of safety while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Nonaccruals broaden beyond the single relationship — new CRE credits (particularly hotel/senior-housing or out-of-market) migrating to nonaccrual, or the ACL rising sharply while coverage remains thin.
- The bad-debt-recapture ceiling or an adverse OCC/FRB decision forces a cut or suspension of the buyback/dividend program.
- An adverse *Harding* class-action development with quantified material liability.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (fiscal Q3 FY26, expected late July/early August), with the credit trajectory on the problem CRE relationship and NIM as the key items to check.

## Conclusion

Capitol Federal is a well-capitalized, shareholder-friendly Kansas thrift executing a credible transition from a thin-margin mortgage lender toward a commercial bank, with NIM, efficiency, and EPS all improving and a positive-AOCI, low-uninsured-deposit funding base that stands out favorably versus peers. The stock has already re-rated to fair value, where the return is the ~4% dividend plus modest book accretion rather than multiple expansion. The offsetting risks are a concentrated CRE credit book (a single relationship already driving a $4.0M specific reserve against thin overall coverage) and a company-specific bad-debt-recapture constraint on the capital-return engine. The balance of these factors supports a HOLD for income, with an upgrade contingent on contained credit plus continued ROE improvement, or a cheaper entry back toward tangible book.
