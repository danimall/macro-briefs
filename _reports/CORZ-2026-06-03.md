**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Core Scientific is mid-transformation from a bankrupt bitcoin miner into an AI/HPC colocation landlord. The pivot is real: colocation revenue grew roughly nine-fold year-over-year to $77.5M in Q1 2026 (67% of total revenue) at a 57% gross margin, and adjusted EBITDA just crossed positive at $4.4M. The company controls approximately 1.86 GW of secured or in-progress utility power across seven states, the scarce input in the AI data-center build-out and the reason CoreWeave attempted to acquire the company in 2025. The stock has roughly doubled off its April low and sits at $29.05, near its 52-week high of $30.46, on a market cap of ~$8.8B.

The problem is that the equity has been bid to ~$8.8B against negative book equity of ($1.31B), an accumulated deficit of ($4.49B), and approximately $5.4B of total debt, while the operating business does not yet generate positive net operating profit. The growth is being financed with 7.75% senior secured notes carrying an uncapped parent completion guarantee, two new land-and-power acquisitions costing ~$650M cash, and the liquidation of nearly the entire bitcoin treasury. Sixty-seven percent of revenue and 100% of leased colocation capacity (590 MW, of which only 225 MW is billing) rests with a single customer, CoreWeave, which is itself defending securities-fraud class actions filed in early 2026 over alleged misrepresented demand and data-center delays.

The single most important fact for the equity case is governance, not growth. On March 2, 2026 the company filed a non-reliance 8-K (Item 4.02), restated prior financials, and disclosed a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting tied to accounting for the very mining-to-HPC conversions that constitute the investment thesis. As of the Q1 2026 10-Q (March 31), disclosure controls remained "not effective" and the weakness was not remediated. An unremediated control failure in a company with $5.4B of debt and a single dominant customer is a material overhang that the momentum in the stock is not pricing. We rate CORZ AVOID.

## Company Overview

Core Scientific emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January 2024, having filed in December 2022 after the crypto market collapse. It operates ~10–11 data centers across Alabama, Georgia, Kentucky, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma, and Texas, representing ~1.86 GW gross utility power capacity and ~1.275 GW of leasable customer power.

Three segments define the business in Q1 2026:

- **Colocation** — $77.5M revenue (67% of total), 57% gross margin. Leasing data-center space, power, and cooling to AI/HPC customers under 10–12 year contracts, with power passed through at cost. This is the strategic future.
- **Digital Asset Self-Mining** — $30.1M revenue (26%), *negative* 57% gross margin. Bitcoin mining for the company's own account, now structurally unprofitable and being wound down.
- **Digital Asset Hosted Mining** — $7.6M revenue (7%), 43% gross margin. Hosting third-party miners.

The defining structural fact is that the colocation business is concentrated in one customer, CoreWeave, which is the tenant (not the owner) of the converted and new-build capacity. CoreWeave attempted to acquire Core Scientific in an all-stock deal in 2025; the merger was terminated (8-K Item 1.02, October 30, 2025) after shareholders, with activist Two Seas Capital's involvement, judged the offer to undervalue the company. Core Scientific now operates independently, pursuing organic growth, with CoreWeave reverting to a pure colocation customer.

## Financial Analysis

The company is two businesses in one, with the real operating signal buried under enormous non-cash items.

| Colocation revenue | $77.5M | $8.6M | — | — |
| Gross profit | $30.1M | $8.2M | $37.9M | $121.1M |
| Operating loss | ($310.4M) | ($47.0M) | ($245.6M) | ($19.2M) |

| Operating cash flow | $249.9M | ($45.0M) | $278.3M | — |
| Capex | $389.2M | $84.0M | $729.0M | — |

GAAP net income is close to meaningless here. The +$576M Q1 2025 and ($347M) Q1 2026 swings are dominated by mark-to-market on warrant liabilities (a +$621M gain in Q1 2025, a $30.8M loss in Q1 2026) and, in Q1 2026, a one-time $266.5M non-cash impairment of mining equipment ($151.6M) and infrastructure ($114.9M). The genuine operating story is the colocation ramp: colocation gross profit moved from $0.5M to $43.9M year-over-year, and adjusted EBITDA crossed positive.

Three points qualify the optimistic reading of the cash flow statement:

1. **Annual revenue fell.** FY2025 revenue of $319M was down from $511M in FY2024 because mining revenue is collapsing faster than colocation is ramping. The turn is real at the quarterly level but the headline business is still shrinking on a trailing-twelve-month basis.

2. **Operating cash flow is funded by prepayments and asset sales, not profit.** The $249.9M of Q1 operating cash flow was driven largely by ~$130M of CoreWeave construction prepayments flowing through deferred revenue (which grew to $654M) plus $208M of bitcoin liquidation (holdings fell from 2,537 BTC / $222M to 547 BTC / $37M in one quarter). This is not recurring operating profit. The cash-generation profile depends on one counterparty continuing to fund construction; $434M of the $1.47B committed capex is pass-through to the customer.

3. **The balance sheet is deeply stretched.** Stockholders' equity is negative ($1.31B), accumulated deficit is ($4.49B), and total debt post-quarter is ~$5.4B:
   - $3.30B of 7.75% senior secured notes due 2031, secured against five data-center entities, with an **uncapped completion guarantee** from the parent.
   - $460M of 3.00% convertibles due 2029 (conversion ~$11/share, in the money, not yet converted).
   - $625M of 0.00% convertibles due 2031 (conversion ~$22.49/share, now in the money).
   - ~$1.0B of bankruptcy-emergence warrant liabilities (96.7M Tranche 1 at $6.81 strike, 7.8M Tranche 2 at $0.01).

The financing sequence over the last ~90 days is itself a risk indicator: a $1B bridge loan in March 2026, refinanced with $3.3B of secured notes in April–May, alongside ~$650M cash on two new land/power acquisitions (Hunt County, TX, ~$232.5M closed May 5; Polaris/Muskogee, OK, ~$421M expected Q3) and near-total bitcoin liquidation. The uncapped completion guarantee means construction overruns put the parent on the hook without limit.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is genuine and supported by the broader AI data-center build-out. Power and interconnection are the binding constraint, and Core Scientific's secured/in-progress capacity is a real, scarce asset. Only 225 of 1,275 leasable MW is currently billing, so there is a long runway of contracted capacity yet to come online. The 12-year, six-facility CoreWeave contract and two new site acquisitions support a multi-year ramp.

Estimate revisions, however, are moving the wrong way. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut from ($0.06) ninety days ago to ($1.04), and next-year EPS from $0.42 to $0.30. The next-twelve-months path remains loss-making on a GAAP basis, and the revision velocity is negative across every period. The operating turn is real but the reported numbers are deteriorating, partly on the impairment and partly on softer near-term estimates.

The growth is durable in the thematic sense, because demand for AI colocation is broad-based across the sector. But durability of the theme is not the same as durability of this company's claim on it, which rests on a single customer continuing to fund and occupy capacity.

## Valuation Assessment

CORZ trades at negative trailing and forward P/E (EPS of ($0.88) trailing), so earnings multiples are not usable. The relevant frame is the ~$8.8B equity value plus ~$5.4B net debt against an asset base of contracted power capacity and an early-stage colocation annuity.

The bull framing is that ~1.86 GW of power at AI-data-center valuations is worth more than the current enterprise value, which is why CoreWeave bid for the company and why shareholders rejected the bid. That argument has merit on the asset side. The problem is what sits between the asset value and the equity holder: $5.4B of debt at a 7.75% cost on the secured tranche, negative book equity, an uncapped completion guarantee, and ~182M shares of potential dilution (96.7M warrants, 69.6M from convertibles, ~15M RSUs/PSUs) against ~317M shares outstanding — roughly 57% potential dilution. The equity is a residual claim levered on top of a construction project funded by one customer.

Analyst targets span $25 low to $55 high, with a $32.05 mean and $30.00 median against the current $29.05. The stock is already trading through the median target. The wide $25–$55 range reflects the binary nature of the outcome rather than a tight consensus. At $29, the equity prices in successful execution of the conversion, continued CoreWeave funding and creditworthiness, and clean financial reporting — three things that are not yet established. The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside; the asymmetry runs the other way, given the leverage and the unremediated control weakness. We score valuation 4.5: not a structural value trap, but priced for execution that has meaningful binary risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Core Scientific competes against data-center REITs (Digital Realty, Equinix), hyperscalers building their own capacity, dedicated AI-infrastructure developers, and other ex-miners pivoting to HPC (IREN, TeraWulf, Cipher, Applied Digital). Its differentiated asset is secured utility power and interconnection across seven states, the genuine bottleneck. The colocation model (long-term contracts, power passed through at cost, customer-funded buildouts) is structurally more stable than mining and represents a real improvement in business quality.

The competitive trajectory is improving on the asset and contract side. It is undermined by counterparty concentration: 100% of leased colocation capacity is CoreWeave, and CoreWeave assigned its Dalton 1 / Denton North leases to a special-purpose vehicle, adding structural complexity. The entire ex-miner cohort is now financing growth with high-yield debt, which raises sector-wide refinancing and leverage risk if AI capex expectations cool. The competitive position is improving but not de-risked, scored 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weakness and restatement (highest priority).** The March 2, 2026 non-reliance 8-K, the restatement of prior financials, and the unremediated material weakness in controls over demolition/impairment accounting — disclosed as still "not effective" at March 31, 2026 — is the dominant idiosyncratic risk. The control failure sits in the exact accounting area the pivot generates (converting mining assets to HPC), so it is not a peripheral issue. For a company with $5.4B of debt, the integrity of reported financials is foundational, and it is currently impaired.

**Single-customer concentration.** Revenue and leased colocation capacity rest on CoreWeave, which is defending securities-fraud class actions over alleged misrepresented demand and data-center delays. Equity holders carry CoreWeave's credit and operational risk without the diversification a combined entity would have offered.

**Leverage on negative equity.** ~$5.4B debt against negative ($1.31B) book equity, with an uncapped parent completion guarantee on the $3.3B secured notes. Construction overruns or delays propagate directly to the parent without limit.

**Execution.** Only 225 of 1,275 leasable MW is billing. The thesis depends on commissioning capacity on schedule, sensitive to equipment lead times, interconnection/permitting, labor, and tariffs (cited in Q1 2026 as raising costs on non-customer-funded equipment).

**Bitcoin exposure during transition.** Self-mining is still 26% of revenue and loss-making; further BTC declines worsen losses and shrink the treasury used to fund capex.

**Dilution.** ~182M shares issuable against ~317M outstanding.

**Litigation.** Ongoing Pang securities class action (pre-bankruptcy era) and a Malikie patent suit now extended to the HPC business with trial set January 2027.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 93.7% against 70.7% one-year historical vol (IV rich by 23 points, 84th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (93.7% near-term versus 82.4% at six months), so the market expects a near-term catalyst to resolve. Put/call skew is balanced (put/call OI ratio 0.08, put/call volume 0.20), so the elevated vol is two-sided rather than directionally bearish. Unusual put activity clusters just below spot ($27–$28 strikes, June expiries, 5–6x volume/OI), consistent with hedging into the next earnings/financing window rather than a directional bearish bet. The signal reinforces that this is a high-variance, binary-outcome equity, which argues against owning the residual claim at a stretched valuation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
AI data-center demand is structural and broad-based; power capacity is the scarce input, and Core Scientific controls ~1.86 GW with a long contracted runway. The colocation annuity carries 57% gross margins with power passed through at cost, adjusted EBITDA has turned positive, and the asset base is worth more than the current enterprise value — the logic shareholders used to reject the CoreWeave bid. If conversions execute on schedule, CoreWeave funds and occupies as contracted, and the control weakness is remediated, the equity re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range ($45–$55) as billable MW scales and the mining drag disappears.

### Bear Case
The equity is a thin residual claim on a construction project funded by a single customer that is itself under securities-fraud litigation, sitting atop $5.4B of debt and negative book equity, with an uncapped completion guarantee and an unremediated material weakness in financial controls. Any combination of CoreWeave stress, construction overruns triggering the guarantee, an AI-capex slowdown that closes high-yield refinancing windows, or further negative restatement/control findings drives the equity sharply lower. Roughly 57% potential dilution caps upside even in a constructive scenario. At $29 after a 166% run, near the 52-week high, the equity prices in flawless execution that the current control environment does not support. Downside to the low-$teens (the range the stock traded for most of 2025) is plausible on any single adverse development.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: There is a credible bearish setup but no clean single dated trigger. The most concrete catalysts are (a) the Q2 2026 earnings report (expected early August), which will reveal whether the material weakness is remediated and whether billable MW is ramping on plan, and (b) any further non-reliance/restatement disclosure or adverse auditor finding flowing from the unremediated control weakness. The Polaris/Muskogee acquisition close (expected Q3 2026) adds another ~$421M cash outflow and execution checkpoint. None of these is a hard, dated event certain to move the stock down on a specific day.

**Timing window**: The next earnings/control-remediation checkpoint is roughly 8–10 weeks out (early August). That is within carry tolerance but not imminent.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is 22.8% of float (64.1M shares) with 3.9 days to cover. This is above the 20% threshold where squeeze risk becomes material, and the stock has demonstrated explosive upside moves (single-session volume spikes of 70–110M shares on prior catalysts). The options market shows balanced skew despite weak fundamentals, a warning that positioning is not one-sided bearish.

**Borrow and options signal**: Near-term IV (84th percentile) makes options expression expensive, and backwardation signals the market already expects a near-term move. Put positioning is light relative to call interest, so the bearish thesis is not crowded in options, but the equity short is crowded at 22.8% SI.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: A bear outcome targets the low-to-mid teens (~$13–$16), roughly 45–55% downside. But with 22.8% SI, demonstrated squeeze behavior, and balanced options positioning, upside risk on any positive catalyst (remediation, a new large customer diversifying away from CoreWeave, an AI-financing tailwind) is easily 30–50%. That puts reward:risk below 2:1 on a probability-weighted basis once carry and borrow are included.

**Verdict**: **CROWDED SHORT** — The bearish thesis (leverage, single-customer concentration, unremediated control weakness) is well-founded, but 22.8% short interest with a documented squeeze profile and an equity near its 52-week high make the remaining downside insufficiently asymmetric relative to squeeze risk. Avoid owning the equity; do not initiate a short here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID.

**Re-entry criteria** (conditions that would move us toward HOLD or BUY):
- **Remediation of the material weakness** confirmed in a 10-Q or 10-K, with clean disclosure controls. This is the single most important gate; without it the financials cannot be relied upon for an equity case.
- **Customer diversification** — a second large colocation customer signed at scale, reducing CoreWeave concentration meaningfully below 50% of revenue.
- **Billable MW inflection** — billable capacity rising materially from the current 225 MW toward the leased 590 MW and beyond, demonstrating that contracted capacity converts to cash on schedule.
- **Deleveraging or equity-value cushion** — positive book equity restored, or net debt/EBITDA on run-rate colocation EBITDA falling into a range a leveraged-infrastructure investor would underwrite.
- A valuation reset that prices in execution and counterparty risk, plausibly the low-to-mid teens, would also change the risk/reward.

**Timeline**: The control-environment and concentration overhangs are likely to persist through at least the next two quarters (into late 2026). We would reassess on the Q2 2026 print (early August) for remediation progress and the billable-MW trajectory.

## Conclusion

Core Scientific has a genuine asset (scarce, contracted utility power), a genuine operating turn (colocation gross profit up ~9x, adjusted EBITDA positive), and exposure to a structural demand theme with broad confirmation across the AI infrastructure complex. None of that is in dispute. What is in dispute is whether the equity holder — sitting behind $5.4B of debt, negative ($1.31B) book equity, an uncapped completion guarantee, 67% single-customer concentration in a counterparty facing its own securities litigation, and an unremediated material weakness in financial controls disclosed in March 2026 — is being compensated for that risk at $29 after a 166% run. The answer is no. The valuation prices flawless execution and clean reporting; the company has neither yet. The right posture is to avoid the equity and reassess on confirmed remediation of the control weakness, customer diversification, and a billable-MW inflection. We rate CORZ **AVOID**.
