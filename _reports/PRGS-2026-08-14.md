## Executive Summary

Progress Software is a leveraged acquisition platform that buys mature enterprise software assets, compresses their cost structure to house margins, and harvests the resulting cash flow. The model produces real cash — FY2025 free cash flow of $229.5M on $977.8M of revenue, a 23.5% FCF margin against capital expenditure of 0.6% of revenue — and the FY2026 numbers are improving as ShareFile integration costs roll off and acquisition-intangible amortization begins stepping down. Derived ROIC has moved from roughly 6.3% in FY2025 toward an estimated 8.7-9.3% in FY2026, and the disclosed amortization schedule delivers a ~$25M GAAP operating-income tailwind in FY2027 before any Domo-related amortization.

The problem is what sits underneath the reported growth. FY2025 revenue rose 29.8%, but ShareFile's incremental contribution ($240.5M) exceeded the entire consolidated revenue increase ($224.4M), which arithmetically requires the rest of the portfolio to have contracted — a derived −2.2%, corroborated by ARR growth of ~2% at both November 30, 2025 and May 31, 2026. In the Q2 FY2026 10-Q, disclosed net retention was re-stated to "generally ranged between 99% and 100%," down from "100% and 102%" in the FY2025 10-K. Recurring revenue lines in Q2 were flat to down (maintenance −2%, SaaS +1%); the entire +7% quarterly revenue gain came from point-in-time license recognition, which the filings attribute to DataDirect, Chef and MarkLogic — the same DataDirect renewal timing that management cited as an $11.4M headwind in FY2025.

The reason for downgrading rather than repeating the prior HOLD is price and balance sheet, not operations. Since the last review at $33.58 the stock has risen 31%, and it is now 85% above the April 1 low of $23.82 and 7% below the 52-week high. That rally has closed the gap to the visible sell-side cluster (DA Davidson $45, median target $46; the $53.8 mean is pulled up by a single $83 outlier). Simultaneously, the pending $400M cash purchase of substantially all of Domo's assets — announced July 22 and funded primarily on the revolver — would push drawn revolver borrowings to roughly $1.25B of the $1.5B facility, take net debt to ~$1.6B, and reverse most of FY2026's deleveraging, against a covenant package whose levels the filings do not quantify. Probability-weighting the scenarios below gives an expected return near zero with a −32% bear case. That is a trim, not a hold.

## Company Overview

Progress sells and maintains a portfolio of enterprise software products assembled through serial acquisition: Ipswitch (2019), Chef (2020), Kemp (2021), MarkLogic (2023), ShareFile (October 2024), Nuclia (June 2025), and now Domo's asset base (pending). The FY2025 10-K states the strategy without embellishment: "a total growth strategy driven by accretive acquisitions of businesses and products that meet our strict strategic, financial, and operating criteria," with an operating philosophy oriented to "predictable and stable recurring revenue and high levels of profitability."

FY2025 revenue mix: maintenance $410.2M (42%), SaaS $287.9M (29%), licenses $237.9M (24%), professional services $41.8M (4%). A dedicated risk factor states OpenEdge and ShareFile together accounted for "slightly more than half" of FY2025 consolidated revenue. Customer concentration is genuinely low (no customer, partner or non-U.S. country above 10% of revenue in the six months ended May 31, 2026); product concentration is high and, on the company's own disclosure, worsened.

The company reports as a single operating segment and states that the chief operating decision maker "does not receive profitability information at a lower level than consolidated results." For a serial acquirer this is a material analytical constraint: there is no way to verify from outside whether any individual acquired asset earns its cost of capital, and an underperforming asset would most likely surface only as an intangible impairment charge.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** H1 FY2026 revenue was $501.3M, +5% reported (+4% constant currency). Q2 was $253.5M, +7%. The composition is the issue: licenses $69.0M (+36%), maintenance $101.2M (−2%), SaaS $73.0M (+1%), services $10.3M (−6%). Recurring revenue of $174.2M was flat to declining; the growth came from the line recognized at a point in time. ARR of $868M versus $850M is +2%.

GAAP operating margin has recovered from 15.7% in FY2025 to 17.8% in Q2 FY2026 and 18.3% in H1. The driver is amortization of acquired intangibles falling from 14.9% of FY2025 revenue ($145.5M) toward 13.9% currently. The disclosed forward schedule on the existing $514.6M intangible balance runs ~$137M in FY2026, $112.2M in FY2027, $100.6M in FY2028 — mechanically worth ~$25M of GAAP operating income in FY2027, though new Domo intangibles will offset part or all of it. Gross margin compressed from 84.3% (FY2022) to 80.8% (FY2025) on SaaS hosting mix.

**Cash flow, with a caution.** FY2025 operating cash flow $235.2M less $5.7M capex gives $229.5M FCF. H1 FY2026 operating cash flow of $177.5M versus $98.9M is not a run rate: accounts receivable contributed a $46.1M inflow, gross AR fell $72.0M from year-end, and DSO fell from 73 days to 49. Stripping the receivable catch-up leaves ~$127M of underlying H1 cash generation; adding normal Q4 license seasonality supports FY2026 FCF of roughly $250-275M. Simple annualization ($346M) overstates. A legitimate tailwind is the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's immediate R&E deduction and EBITDA-based interest limitation, visible in an 18.6% H1 cash tax rate against a 26% book rate.

**Capital structure — the central issue.** Face debt fell from $1,410M at November 30, 2025 to $1,300M at May 31, 2026 ($450M of 3.5% converts due 2030 plus $850M drawn on the $1.5B revolver), with cash of $103.0M for net debt of ~$1,197M. In April 2026 Progress redeemed the $360M 1.0% convertible notes for $361.8M using revolver borrowings. That refinancing raised structural interest cost by roughly $15.7M annually ($360M × (5.37% − 1.00%)), about $0.29/share after tax, and converted the balance from fixed to floating. Every 100bp on a ~$1.25B post-Domo drawn balance is ~$12.5M pre-tax, roughly $0.23/share after tax — a live exposure with the long end under pressure and no committee guidance on the policy path.

Building a covenant-style EBITDA from FY2025 GAAP figures (operating income $153.3M + intangible amortization $145.5M + depreciation $6.2M = $305.0M, plus restructuring $13.1M, acquisition costs $5.3M, cyber response $2.8M and ~$65M of SBC) gives roughly $389M, cross-checking well against the proxy's disclosed FY2025 non-GAAP operating income of $384.8M. On that basis net leverage was ~3.4x at FY2025 year-end and ~3.1x at May 31, 2026, against a management target of ~2.8x for FY2026.

The Altman Z-Score of 0.79 overstates near-term distress. At May 31, 2026, excluding current deferred revenue (a performance obligation, not a cash claim), current assets of $336.5M against $91.6M of remaining current liabilities is 3.67x. The convertible-notes current-maturity problem resolved in April. What the screens correctly capture is the leverage itself: $1.3B of debt against ~$305M of GAAP-based EBITDA, a revolver maturity tethered to the 2030 notes, tangible book equity of approximately −$1,318M (goodwill $1,309.8M plus intangibles $514.6M equals 77.8% of total assets), and **covenant levels that are not quantified in either the 10-K or the 10-Q**. That gap is the single most important unresolved item in the credit case; pro-forma leverage estimates cannot be tested against actual headroom.

Goodwill impairment risk is less acute than the intangible-heavy balance sheet implies. As a single reporting unit, the test compares reporting-unit fair value to ~$506M of carrying equity; even at the April low (market cap ~$1.03B) headroom was substantial. The sharper exposure is finite-lived intangibles on a specific underperforming asset, which single-segment reporting would obscure until the charge landed.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was suspended at the ShareFile closing (FY2024 dividends were $31.5M), redirected to debt repayment and buybacks. Repurchases: $34.0M (FY2023), $86.8M (FY2024), $105.0M (FY2025), $54.7M in H1 FY2026, with $147.5M remaining on authorization. Q2 repurchases of 1,225,254 shares averaged $28.29 — well-timed against the current price. But share count fell only 1.024M against 1.691M repurchased, so roughly 40% of the dollar buyback offsets equity-plan dilution rather than creating per-share accretion. That is consistent with the company's stated policy and should be reflected in any per-share model.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth and underlying growth diverge sharply, and the gap is the thesis.

Organic revenue is flat at best. The derived ex-ShareFile FY2025 figure is −2.2%; adjusting for the disclosed $11.4M DataDirect timing headwind moves it close to flat. ARR growth of ~2% at two consecutive measurement dates, on a metric that already includes ShareFile in both periods, is the cleanest available proxy and it approximates inflation. The re-disclosed net retention range of 99-100% means the installed cohort in aggregate is no longer expanding; all growth must come from new customers or acquisitions.

Q2's license strength is the most likely candidate for a non-repeating item. The FY2025 10-K blamed the license decline on "timing of multi-year subscription renewals in our DataDirect product offering"; the Q2 10-Q credits the increase to DataDirect, Chef and MarkLogic. The parsimonious reading is a renewal cohort landing in FY2026 that was absent in FY2025. The alternative — genuine share gain or pricing power in data connectivity driven by AI workloads — would make the strength repeatable, but ~2% ARR growth in the same period argues against it, since ARR "generally aligns to billings." Product-level revenue is not disclosed, so this cannot be resolved from the filings.

**Estimate revisions are positive but modest**: current year $6.12 from $5.98 (+2.3%), next year $6.23 from $6.06 (+2.8%), current quarter +4.7%. Next quarter was revised −1.3%. This is not the >30% revision velocity that flags an earnings inflection; it is consensus catching up to a raised guide. The beat record supports confidence in the guide (3 beats, 1 in-line over four quarters; +13.4%, +12.8%, 0.0%, +6.6%), and the July 22 press release stated Q3 revenue and non-GAAP EPS will be "within or above the high end of previously issued guidance." Q3 reports September 30.

**Domo.** The purchase arithmetic is genuinely attractive on paper: $400M for ~$318.9M of revenue is 1.25x, against Progress's own 3.08x EV/revenue. Domo's FY2026 S&M, derived from disclosed lines, runs ~$141.8M or 44.5% of revenue versus Progress's consolidated 21%; G&A at $59.2M versus Progress's 11-12%. Converging both to house ratios would move ~$99M of annual cost and swing a −$39.1M operating loss to roughly +$60M, against $21.5M of incremental interest.

Three offsets deserve equal weight. First, Domo's revenue has been flat at $308.6M-$319.0M for four consecutive fiscal years *while* spending 44.5% of revenue on sales — cutting that spend in half is as likely to accelerate revenue decline as to drop to margin, particularly in BI where Power BI is bundled into M365. Second, in an asset purchase, assumed deferred revenue is recorded at fair value: Progress booked only $96.2M of deferred revenue on ShareFile, a business that then generated $261.6M of annual revenue, so Domo's year-one GAAP revenue contribution will sit meaningfully below its standalone run rate. Third, new intangible amortization on the ShareFile precedent could run $40-70M annually. Year one is plausibly GAAP-negative and modestly non-GAAP accretive. Whether Domo's $126.5M of funded debt is among the "certain liabilities" assumed is not established by the press release; the conservative assumption used here is that it is not, which affects pro-forma leverage by ~0.3x.

At $44.04 on 41.312M shares, market cap is $1,819M and EV, including $1,197M of net debt, is $3,016M.

The headline 7.2x is a non-GAAP construct that excludes ~$137M of FY2026 intangible amortization and ~$78M of annualized stock-based compensation. SBC grew 24% year over year in H1 ($39.0M vs $31.4M) while revenue grew 5%, concentrated in G&A. Charging SBC as an economic cost gives ~$4.72 of adjusted EPS and a ~9.3x multiple; on GAAP, H1 diluted EPS of $1.03 implies a multiple above 20x. Note that the supplied profile fields are internally inconsistent — trailing P/E of 20.85 against EPS of $1.66 implies a $34.6 price, not $44.04; at the canonical basis, $44.04/$1.66 is 26.5x. Any comparison to consensus must match basis.

The peer table is not usable for a direct read: the "median P/E 46.3" reflects a cohort containing a loss-maker (NABL at −89.3x) and high-multiple names (CVLT 87.2x, QLYS 31.9x) whose organic growth profiles bear no resemblance to a flat-organic, 3.1x-levered roll-up. The relevant comparison set is levered mature software, which clears at high-single-digit non-GAAP earnings multiples and low-teens EV/FCF — approximately where PRGS trades now.

That is the crux. The cheapness is real on cash flow but it is not new, and it has been substantially arbitraged by the 85% move off the April low. On a post-Domo basis, FY2027 FCF of $260-290M capitalized at 12-14x EV/FCF, less ~$1,597M of net debt, brings out $40-53 per share. Independently, ~7.5x a post-Domo FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of ~$6.50 gives $48.75. Base case lands at $47, roughly 7% above the current price and one dollar above the median analyst target of $46.

The guidance on treating analyst targets as floors does not apply here. That principle is for cheap stocks with accelerating estimates; PRGS has +2.3% revisions, ~2% ARR growth, and a target cluster ($45-46) sitting *at* the current price, with a mean pulled to $53.8 by one $83 outlier against five nominal Buy ratings. Five Buys and zero Holds is a divergence from this recommendation and should be acknowledged — but the published targets themselves, at $45 and $46, are where the arithmetic lands, not above it.

The valuation score of 4.8 reflects a low multiple with no identified mispricing large enough to overcome the structural problem. A compressed multiple on flat organic revenue, rising leverage, and a sub-100% net retention floor is the profile of a discount that persists rather than closes.

## Competitive Landscape

Each product line faces a different contest, and the trajectory is flat-to-deteriorating in most of them.

- **OpenEdge** competes against the customer's decision to modernize. Its defense is the cost of rewriting decades-old ISV applications, which produced maintenance revenue of $410.6M in FY2024 and $410.2M in FY2025 — stability to the tenth of a percent. The moat prevents churn without generating expansion.
- **ShareFile** competes against Microsoft OneDrive/SharePoint bundled into M365, plus Dropbox and Box, with differentiation in legal/accounting/healthcare workflow. SaaS revenue growing 1% year over year in Q2 is consistent with the bundling threat being live.
- **Chef** faces the open-source dynamic Progress itself flags in a dedicated risk factor, plus Ansible, Puppet, Terraform and AI-assisted configuration tooling.
- **Kemp** competes against F5, NetScaler and cloud-native load balancers in a category under secular pressure as workloads move to managed services.
- **MarkLogic / DataDirect / Nuclia** are the credible AI-adjacent assets and the substance behind the "context and control for AI" repositioning. They compete against Snowflake, Databricks, MongoDB and Elastic, all with far larger R&D budgets. Nuclia's FY2026 earn-out was written to zero in Q2, indicating it is tracking below revenue targets — a modest revision given the $1.1M acquisition-date fair value against a $5.0M maximum, but not a positive signal for the newest AI asset.
- **Domo (pending)** enters BI against Power BI, Tableau, Looker, Qlik and native analytics inside Snowflake and Databricks. Four years of flat revenue on 44.5%-of-revenue sales spend is a direct read on that intensity.

The AI question is two-sided and unresolved. Progress's portfolio is weighted toward application development, file sharing, CMS and (soon) BI — categories where AI-assisted code generation and AI-native tooling could compress value. The company's counter-thesis, that AI raises demand for governed, connected enterprise data, is plausible. Neither reading is validated by ~2% ARR growth.

## Risk Assessment

**MOVEit litigation with a dated catalyst and exhausted insurance.** The MDL in the District of Massachusetts has dismissed 23 of 33 bellwether claims in whole or part. Fact discovery closes September 29, 2026; **class-certification briefing begins August 28, 2026 and runs into Q4 2026**. Cyber coverage for the relevant period was $15.0M; remaining coverage fell from ~$4.5M at November 30, 2025 to ~$1.1M at May 31, 2026 — $3.4M consumed in one half-year. Prospective legal costs are essentially self-funded. No loss contingency has been recorded because damages are unspecified and certification is uncertain. Denial of certification substantially caps exposure; a grant creates a settlement dynamic of indeterminate size against ~$103M of cash and ~$1.2B of net debt. This is the clearest near-term binary and it is not obviously priced.

**Leverage into an acquisition, with undisclosed covenants.** Post-Domo, drawn revolver borrowings of ~$1.25B leave ~$250M of the $1.5B facility undrawn; net debt of ~$1.6B against $430M of FY2026 covenant EBITDA plus a Domo contribution implies 3.1-3.7x net depending on year-one synergy capture. The risk factor acknowledges that non-compliance "would result in any amounts outstanding... becoming immediately due and payable" and that failing covenants at the time of a draw makes borrowing unavailable. The levels are not quantified anywhere in the filings.

**Product concentration.** Two products exceed half of consolidated revenue, and the company's own disclosure describes this as having worsened. The FY2024→FY2025 mix shift (SaaS 6%→29%, license 33%→24%) is the mechanical consolidation of ShareFile, not organic cloud migration or diversification.

**Receivables.** Credit loss and sales allowance provisions were $7.1M in H1 FY2026 versus $2.0M a year earlier, a 3.6x increase, with Q2 G&A up 26% partly on "additional reserves related to our receivables." Gross AR fell $72.0M and DSO improved from 73 to 49 days — but reserving mechanically reduces net AR and improves DSO, so the two disclosures are not independent evidence of collection improvement. The filings do not disaggregate whether this is genuine deterioration in a specific cohort (plausibly the SMB-weighted ShareFile base), integration clean-up, or conservatism.

**Governance and incentive design.** The FY2025 pay-versus-performance table shows a PRGS TSR index of 104.61 against a peer index of 272.68, while the company-selected measure — non-GAAP operating income — compounded from $229.2M to $384.8M (+68%). LTIP PSUs are 75% weighted to three-year cumulative operating income and 25% to relative TSR. Non-GAAP operating income excludes interest expense and acquired-intangible amortization, so a debt-funded acquisition raises the dominant metric whether or not the purchase price and financing cost create equity value. This is an observation about metric construction rather than motive; Adjusted Free Cash Flow and the relative-TSR sleeve do impose discipline, visible in FY2025 comp-actually-paid falling to $6.96M from $9.79M, and the peer index is likely dominated by AI-levered growth names against which any mature cash business compares poorly.

**Insider activity.** Between February and July 2026 there were no open-market purchases by any insider, including during April when the company itself repurchased 729,071 shares at $28.05. Dispositions were mostly same-day sales after RSU vesting or option exercise — weak directional evidence, frequently under pre-existing plans, and blackout windows around the March 30 and June 30 releases would have restricted discretionary buying for much of the drawdown. Two items survive that caveat: CLO Wang reduced to a 2,544-share residual and CFO Folger sold at ~$29 (near the low), and EVP Jarrett sold ~53% of her holdings on July 29 at ~$41.6, one week after the Domo announcement. The CEO made no discretionary sales and holdings grew through vesting, an offsetting signal. On balance this is mildly negative, not decisive.

**Management continuity is a genuine positive.** No Item 5.02 filings appear in the 8-K index from January 2024 through July 2026 — no disclosed director or executive officer departures or appointments in roughly 2.5 years. Disclosure controls were effective as of May 31, 2026 with no changes in internal control during the quarter.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 63.5% against 52.6% one-year realized is rich (10.9pp spread, 78th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is backwardated (63.5% at one week versus 57.2% at six months) with a +6.0% one-week put skew described as elevated put demand. The one-week implied move of ±7.3% is notable given the next scheduled earnings date is September 30 — with only 68 contracts across four expirations, this reading carries low confidence and may reflect illiquidity rather than positioning. The more informative datapoint is the delta: ATM IV collapsed from 90.7% to 63.5% while the price rose 30.8% since June 30, and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.31 to 0.46. Uncertainty priced into the shares has fallen materially as the price recovered, which is the opposite of favorable entry timing. The six-month skew of −4.7% tilts the other way, toward call demand into the Q3 print and Domo close.

Short interest of 16.3% of float with 5.6 days to cover is a two-sided fact. It creates squeeze risk against a REDUCE — a Q3 beat on September 30 plus a clean Domo close could force covering. It also means the bearish view is already crowded, which limits the downside available if the thesis is right and argues against an outright short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($59, +34%).** Domo closes on schedule; Progress converges S&M and G&A toward house ratios and captures $50-60M of the ~$99M synergy pool without breaking revenue, adding meaningful non-GAAP EPS in FY2027. FY2027 intangible amortization steps down to $112M as scheduled, and the OBBBA cash-tax benefit compounds. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches ~$6.75; the market accepts that the roll-up earns above its cost of capital (ROIC 9%+ versus a WACC near 8%) and re-rates toward 9x, aided by short covering into consecutive beats and $147.5M of remaining buyback authorization. Class certification in the MOVEit MDL is denied, removing the tail. Net leverage returns toward 2.8x by late FY2027.

**Bear case ($30, −32%).** The FY2026 license strength proves to be the DataDirect renewal-timing reversal it most likely is, and FY2027 license revenue mean-reverts while maintenance and SaaS remain flat to down at 99-100% net retention. Domo's revenue declines 10-15% as the sales organization is cut, so the deferred-revenue haircut and new amortization deliver a GAAP-negative year with less synergy than modelled; net leverage sticks above 3.5x while the revolver is drawn to ~$1.25B, and rising long-end rates add $12.5M per 100bp on floating debt. Class certification is granted in Q4 2026 against ~$1.1M of remaining insurance, forcing a reserve. The multiple compresses back to 5-6x non-GAAP earnings.

**Base case ($47, +6.7%).** Domo closes and delivers roughly half the modelled cost convergence, offset by a revenue haircut and new amortization, leaving FY2027 non-GAAP EPS around $6.50 and FCF of $260-290M. Leverage sits near 3.2-3.4x through FY2027. The stock holds a high-single-digit non-GAAP multiple / 12-14x EV/FCF, which is where it trades today.

Probability weighting 20% bull / 50% base / 30% bear gives an expected return of roughly +0.5% over twelve months, with a −32% left tail. That combination — negligible expected return, unfavorable asymmetry, and a dated binary event inside the window — is what separates REDUCE from HOLD at this price. At $33.58 the same fundamental picture supported a HOLD; the 31% rally consumed the margin of safety without a corresponding change in organic growth, retention, or leverage trajectory.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Base case $47 (+6.7%); bull $59 (+34%); bear $30 (−32%). Horizon 12 months.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into current strength, targeting a materially reduced position ahead of the August 28 start of class-certification briefing and the September 30 Q3 print. A staged reduction — roughly half now in the $43-45 area, the balance on any move toward the 52-week high of $47.37 — captures the sell-side target cluster without conceding the squeeze risk that 16.3% short interest and a pre-announced "at or above the high end" Q3 create. Holders who wish to retain a residual position for the Domo synergy option should size it as a small speculative sleeve, not a core holding.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. Disclosed net retention returns to 101%+ and ARR growth accelerates above 4% year over year for two consecutive quarters, evidencing organic expansion rather than purchased revenue.
2. Class certification in the MOVEit MDL is denied, or the company quantifies a bounded settlement, removing an unreserved contingency running against ~$1.1M of remaining coverage.
3. Management quantifies the credit-facility covenant levels and confirms net leverage below 3.0x within four quarters of the Domo close, with Domo revenue held within 5% of its ~$319M standalone run rate.
4. Price reverts to the low $30s, restoring 40%+ upside to the $47 base case.

**Avoid triggers.**
1. Class certification granted with a recorded loss contingency, or MOVEit legal spend accelerating above the ~$5M annualized H1 pace with insurance exhausted.
2. Domo closes and pro-forma net leverage prints above 3.7x, or covenant disclosure reveals headroom under 0.5x.
3. Domo revenue declines more than 10% in the first two reported quarters of ownership, or license revenue reverts and total revenue growth falls below 2% on a constant-currency basis.
4. Any intangible impairment charge on a named acquisition, which under single-segment reporting would be the first visible evidence that a purchased asset is not earning its cost of capital.

**Reassessment timeline.** September 30, 2026 (Q3 FY2026 results and updated guidance), with an interim check on the class-certification docket through Q4 2026 and on the Domo closing 8-K, which should disclose the purchase price allocation, assumed liabilities, deferred-revenue fair value, and post-close debt balance.

## Conclusion

Progress generates real cash, has genuinely improving returns on capital as the ShareFile integration completes, and holds a switching-cost moat around OpenEdge that has kept maintenance revenue flat to the tenth of a percent for two years. Those facts justify owning it at some price. They do not justify owning it at 85% above the April low, at or above the cluster of published targets, with net leverage about to rise again on a $400M cash purchase of a business whose revenue has not moved in four years, and with a class-certification ruling due in Q4 2026 against ~$1.1M of remaining cyber insurance.

The 7.2x forward non-GAAP multiple is the argument most often made for this stock, and it is the weakest one. It excludes ~$137M of amortization and ~$78M of annual SBC that grew 24% while revenue grew 5%; the SBC-burdened multiple is ~9.3x and the GAAP multiple exceeds 20x. On EV/FCF the stock trades at 13.1x, its own recent multiple, with post-Domo capitalization of 12-14x FCF bracketing $40-53 per share around a $47 base case. Expected return is approximately flat with a −32% bear scenario and a dated catalyst inside the window.

The recommendation moves from HOLD to REDUCE on price and pro-forma leverage, not on operational deterioration — Q2 was a legitimate beat-and-raise and Q3 has been pre-announced at or above the high end. Trim into strength; the low $30s would restore an asymmetry worth underwriting.
