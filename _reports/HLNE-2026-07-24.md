**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hamilton Lane is a $3.7B market-cap private-markets asset manager whose stock has fallen roughly 48% from its August 2025 high of $161 to ~$81, while its underlying recurring-fee business accelerated. FY26 (fiscal year ended March 31, 2026) management and advisory fees grew 13.7% to $584M, fee-related earnings (FRE) grew 25% to $344.5M with margin expanding 200bps to 50%, and fee-earning AUM (FEAUM) grew 13% to $81.5B. The headline revenue figure (+6.5%) understates the franchise because it is dragged by lumpy, timing-driven incentive fees, which fell 12%. The gap between a compounding recurring engine and a nearly halved share price is the central tension of this thesis.

The decline is a multiple compression on the entire listed-alternatives complex, driven by fundraising and exit-drought fears, private-credit stress, and a higher-for-longer rate path that raises the discount rate on long-duration fee streams, rather than a company-specific fundamental break. Two facts distinguish HLNE from a value trap. First, the majority of its FEAUM earns fees on committed or net invested capital rather than mark-to-market NAV, so the recurring base is structurally insulated from the drawdown the bears are pricing. Second, the founders and the entire C-suite bought the dip: Executive Co-Chairman Hartley Rogers deployed several million dollars at $77–92 in May–June 2026, and both co-CEOs, the other Executive Co-Chairman, the COO, and a director added open-market purchases. That is one of the strongest internal signals available.

We rate HLNE a BUY with disciplined sizing. At ~13.7x forward EPS against a ~33x asset-manager peer median and a PEG below 1.0 on mid-teens recurring-fee growth, the recurring-fee base is mispriced. The redemption-gate risk the bears cite attaches to evergreen and incentive revenue (roughly a quarter of the total), not to the committed-capital management-fee base (75%+). The primary genuine headwind is a rate path that compresses growth velocity and the multiple, which is why this is a scaled-entry, not a maximum-conviction, position.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1991 and headquartered in Conshohocken, PA, Hamilton Lane is one of the largest private-markets investment-solutions firms globally, with ~785 employees across 23 offices. It operates exclusively in private markets (private equity, private credit, infrastructure, real assets, secondaries, and impact) and oversees a total asset footprint of roughly $1.05 trillion as of March 31, 2026:

- **Assets Under Management (discretionary): $141.8B** (+3% YoY)
- **Assets Under Advisement (non-discretionary): $905.3B** (+10% YoY)
- **Fee-Earning AUM: $81.5B** (+13% YoY) — the metric that drives management fees

FY26 management and advisory fees of $584M break down as: specialized funds 64% (drawdown funds plus fast-growing evergreen/semi-liquid vehicles), customized separate accounts 24%, reporting/monitoring/data (the Cobalt platform) 8%, and advisory 4%. Roughly 61% of fees come from outside the US.

The business differs from a traditional flagship-fund manager. Rather than primarily raising its own buyout funds, Hamilton Lane allocates across the industry through secondaries, co-investments, and primaries. That model generates proprietary performance data on ~$1 trillion of assets, feeds the Cobalt analytics platform, and positions the firm as a conflict-light partner for institutions and, increasingly, private-wealth platforms. Client concentration is low: no single client exceeds 2% of fees, and the top ten account for 11%.

FY26 fundamentals accelerated on the metrics that matter:

| Non-GAAP EPS | $3.92 | $5.04 | $5.90 | **+17%** |

The quality of growth is the key point. The recurring engine (management and advisory fees) grew 14% and has averaged over 75% of total revenue for five years. FRE, the cleanest read on core profitability, grew 25% with margin expanding to 50%. The apparent revenue slowdown is entirely explained by lumpy incentive fees, which management characterizes as timing on carried-interest realizations, not deterioration. Incentive fees are structurally unpredictable and will swing quarter to quarter regardless of business health.

Cash generation is excellent. Operating cash flow rose to $424.9M from $300.8M, against capex of only ~$5.8M, implying very high FCF conversion. The large investing outflow of $494.8M is dominated by consolidated-fund/partnership accounting (~$411M), not core capital expenditure, a common distortion in asset-manager cash-flow statements.

The balance sheet is conservative. Debt is $278M (down from $290M) against ~$361M cash, with facilities capped at ~$325M. On-balance-sheet investments grew to $774M, reflecting co-investment alongside clients, which aligns interests but also means the firm carries some direct private-markets exposure. Capital return is rising: the dividend was raised 11% to a $2.40 annual target (a ~2.9% yield at the current price), and the buyback authorization was raised to $100M, though only ~$20M was repurchased in FY26. The effective GAAP tax rate is ~16%.

One flag deserves explicit dismissal: the ~1.5 Altman Z-Score. That model is calibrated for industrial and manufacturing firms and penalizes asset managers whose balance sheets carry large Tax Receivable Agreement liabilities, consolidated-fund non-controlling interests, and Up-C structures. With 50% FRE margins, ~42% ROE, minimal net debt, and strong cash flow, HLNE is financially healthy; the Z-Score is a false distress signal here.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, with the last four quarters coming in at +2.2%, +14.5%, +37.0%, and +27.4% above consensus. The magnitude of the beats has narrowed over the trailing year, consistent with a moderating growth pace, but the direction is unbroken.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is evergreen and semi-liquid funds. Specialized-fund management fees rose 19% to $374M, evergreen FEAUM added ~$7.9B YoY, and evergreen incentive fees jumped to $116M for the year from $70M. Momentum continued past the fiscal year-end, with management citing over $265M in April 2026 evergreen net inflows. The fee rate on average FEAUM has risen to 0.67% from 0.59% four years ago, indicating mix shift toward higher-fee products rather than price competition eroding economics.

There is a large embedded reservoir: $1.5B of unrealized carried interest (up 23% YoY), diversified across 3,000+ assets and 120+ funds. This converts to revenue on realizations and functions as an option on a private-markets exit recovery. In the current exit/IPO drought, that carry is not being harvested, which suppresses reported incentive fees; a normalization of exit markets would release it.

Estimate revisions are broadly positive but not spectacular. Current-quarter EPS was revised +9.2% over 30 days and +15.3% over 90 days; current-year moved +2.6%/+4.3%; next-year eased −0.5%/−1.0%. The current-quarter revision velocity is meaningful, but it does not reach the +30% threshold that would flag an inflection-grade signal, and the modest next-year trim reflects the rate/cyclical headwind on growth velocity. Growth here is durable and high-quality but decelerating from its peak pace: mid-teens recurring-fee compounding rather than an accelerating breakout.

The secular tailwind is the democratization of private markets: evergreen and semi-liquid vehicles bringing private assets into wealth channels, aided by the August 2025 executive order on alternatives in retirement plans. HLNE is an early and credible player. The cyclical headwind is the current private-markets slowdown: muted institutional fundraising, weak distributions back to LPs (low DPI), and private-credit quality concerns. The firm's model is partly insulated because most FEAUM earns fees on commitments or invested capital, not NAV.

## Valuation Assessment

HLNE trades at ~13.7x trailing/forward EPS (non-GAAP EPS $5.90; current-year consensus $6.26; next-year $7.36). That is a stark discount to the Asset Management & Custody Banks peer median P/E of 33.1x. On the current-year estimate the stock is at ~13.0x; on next year, ~11.0x. With recurring-fee growth in the mid-teens, the PEG sits below 1.0.

Peer comparison requires care given wide dispersion: StepStone shows negative earnings, Federated Hermes trades at 11.2x, and Cohen & Steers/WisdomTree/Acadian span 25–41x. But even against the most conservative comparable (FHI at 11.2x), HLNE's superior FRE growth (+25%), margin (50%), and fee-rate expansion argue for a premium, not a discount. The stock has instead compressed toward the low end of the group during a sector-wide derating of listed alternatives managers.

The equity lessons embedded in this system's history counsel treating a "cheap" multiple as a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst. The discipline is warranted, and it is why this is not a maximum-conviction call. The catalysts that distinguish HLNE from a trap are specific: (1) a recurring-fee base compounding well above the multiple implies re-rating as consensus catches up (the current-quarter estimate has been revised up 15% in 90 days while the multiple compressed); (2) the $1.5B unrealized carry is an unpriced call on exit-market normalization; (3) the founder/C-suite insider-buying cluster signals that the operators view the price as mispriced. The consensus analyst targets (low $94, median $123, high $174) sit roughly 16% to 114% above the current price, and on a cheap-relative-to-growth stock those targets tend to lag reality.

We do not treat this as a stock the market is simply wrong about with no basis. The ~48% drawdown reflects two real forces: a higher-for-longer rate path that mechanically compresses the multiple on long-duration fee streams and raises the cost of the firm's (modest) financing, and legitimate uncertainty about how evergreen/interval funds behave through a full cycle. Those are the reasons the multiple compressed, and they are the reasons to size this position deliberately.

## Competitive Landscape

Hamilton Lane competes against larger diversified alternatives managers (Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Ares, Blue Owl, Carlyle) and specialized fund-of-funds/advisory peers. Its differentiation rests on data, solutions, and independence: allocating across the industry rather than raising its own flagship funds generates proprietary performance data (Cobalt) unmatched by single-manager peers, and positions it as a conflict-light partner for institutions and wealth platforms.

The competitive trajectory is improving. FEAUM growth and rising fee rates hold, and the firm holds a genuine early-mover position in evergreen/semi-liquid products, the industry's fastest-growing distribution channel. Client concentration remains low and global reach broad. The primary competitive threat is that the same large managers are building their own evergreen/wealth-channel offerings with greater brand recognition and distribution muscle, which could compress fee rates over time. For now, HLNE's data edge and independence sustain its position, and the mix shift toward higher-fee evergreen products is widening, not narrowing, unit economics.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that matter, and are not fully priced:

1. **Evergreen/semi-liquid redemption and valuation risk** — the most important emerging risk. Evergreen funds now drive growth and a rising share of revenue, but they carry redemption/repurchase rights (subject to gates) and daily/monthly NAV striking on illiquid assets with a ~3-month reporting lag. A private-markets downturn or a run on semi-liquid vehicles could force gating, damage the brand, and reverse the flow story. Valuation subjectivity is under increasing SEC scrutiny for registered/interval funds. Continued strong net inflows are reassuring but not a full-cycle test.
2. **Incentive-fee lumpiness** — carried interest is sporadic and drives the headline revenue slowdown. This will produce quarterly revenue volatility indefinitely.
3. **Non-contracted fees** — customized separate account and advisory revenue is not long-term contracted; mandates can be reduced or terminated, and industry-wide fee compression is an ongoing pressure.
4. **Private-markets cycle exposure** — a slower fundraising environment, an exit/DPI drought, or private-credit stress would slow FEAUM growth and carry realizations.
5. **Governance/structure** — Up-C structure with dual-class shares (Class B = 10 votes) and insiders holding ~74% voting power make this a controlled company with limited protections for Class A holders. A $235M TRA liability carries change-of-control acceleration features. HLI's only material asset is its interest in HLA, so dividends depend on distributions up the chain. These are meaningfully mitigated by the founders' large equity stakes and recent buying.
6. **Rate sensitivity** — a higher-for-longer path compresses the multiple on long-duration fee streams and raises the cost of the firm's modest financing, the clearest macro headwind currently weighing on the equity.

The risk score of 5.5 reflects that the evergreen redemption dynamic is a genuine, not-fully-priced binary, offset by the fee model's structural insulation (fees on commitments/invested capital, not NAV) and the already-substantial drawdown providing asymmetric recovery upside if the cyclical fears prove overdone.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning confirms elevated near-term uncertainty. ATM IV of 56.2% is rich against 40.4% one-year historical vol, with a 12.0% put/call skew indicating elevated put demand and an implied ±12.4% move on the nearest expiry. The term structure is flat (56.2% near vs. 56.5% far), so the market is distributing uncertainty evenly rather than pricing a single imminent catalyst. The put/call open-interest ratio fell sharply from 2.41 to 1.42 over the three days into July 24, suggesting some unwinding of bearish positioning. The 12.1% short interest (3.9 days to cover) is elevated but not squeeze-territory. The rich IV argues for scaling entry rather than deploying a full position at once; the vol premium can be harvested by selling puts or waiting for post-catalyst crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($141–176).** The private-markets slowdown proves cyclical, not structural. Exit markets normalize, releasing the $1.5B unrealized carry into incentive fees. Evergreen inflows sustain, FEAUM continues compounding at low-teens, and the fee rate holds at 0.67%+. As consensus estimates rise and rate fears ease, the multiple re-rates from ~13.7x toward the low end of the peer range (~18–22x). At 20x the next-year estimate of $7.36, that is $147; the high analyst target of $174 assumes both re-rating and carry harvest. The insider cluster is proven right.

**Bear Case ($62).** A private-markets recession materializes. Fundraising stalls, an evergreen redemption event forces gating and damages the brand, FEAUM growth reverses, and incentive fees stay depressed as exits remain frozen. The rate path stays higher-for-longer, keeping the multiple compressed. Recurring-fee growth decelerates to low single digits, and the stock trades toward ~10x a stagnant $6.20, roughly $62, a further ~24% below current levels.

**Base Case ($122).** Recurring fees compound at low-to-mid teens, evergreen inflows continue but at a moderating pace, incentive fees remain lumpy, and the multiple re-rates modestly toward ~17x as rate fears ease and consensus catches up to the recurring-fee reality. On next-year EPS of $7.36 at 16.5x, that is ~$122, roughly 50% above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $122** — 16.5x the next-year consensus EPS of $7.36, reflecting a partial re-rating from ~13.7x toward the lower half of the asset-manager peer range as the recurring-fee compounding is recognized. This sits between the bear ($62) and bull ($176) scenarios.

**Bull case target: $147–176** — 20x next-year EPS with carry harvest and full multiple normalization.

**Bear case target: $62** — ~10x a stagnant ~$6.20 EPS if a private-markets recession and evergreen redemption event materialize.

**Timeframe: 12–18 months**, tied to fiscal-quarter earnings, the trajectory of evergreen net inflows, and the rate path.

**Upside/downside from ~$81:** +51% to base, +81% to +117% to bull, −24% to bear. The reward/risk skews favorably (~2:1 to base against bear).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Evergreen net inflows turn negative for two consecutive quarters, or the firm gates redemptions on any evergreen/interval vehicle — this hits both the growth narrative and the multiple.
2. Management and advisory fee growth decelerates below 5% YoY, signaling the recurring engine is stalling rather than compounding.
3. FRE margin compresses below 45%, indicating fee-rate erosion or cost inflation eroding the core profitability advantage.

## Conclusion

Hamilton Lane presents a recurring-fee franchise compounding at mid-teens rates, trading at ~13.7x forward earnings, a deep discount to its peer median, after a ~48% drawdown driven by a sector-wide derating of listed alternatives managers rather than any fundamental break. The fee model's structural insulation (fees on committed and invested capital rather than NAV) distinguishes the durable recurring base from the evergreen/incentive line where the redemption-gate risk the bears cite actually attaches. The broad open-market insider-buying cluster (the founders, both co-CEOs, the COO, and a director deploying real capital into the decline) is a strong internal endorsement of the mispricing view.

The trajectory has improved steadily (6.3 → 7.0 → 7.1) with two prior BUY calls, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend: the price has fallen further while fundamentals held or improved, which strengthens the risk/reward. The score of 6.85 is marginally below the prior 7.0, reflecting the genuine, not-fully-resolved rate headwind that compresses growth velocity and the multiple, plus the modest next-year estimate trim, not any fundamental deterioration. We maintain a **BUY with disciplined sizing and scaled entry**, favoring accumulation in the mid-$70s to low-$80s given the rich near-term implied volatility, with the evergreen inflow cadence as the key variable to monitor.
