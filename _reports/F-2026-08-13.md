## Executive Summary

Ford's adjusted earnings power has recovered sharply from a FY2025 that carried $17,356M of pre-tax special items and an $8,182M GAAP net loss. H1 2026 adjusted EBIT reached $5,991M on a 6.5% margin against $3,159M and 3.5% a year earlier, driven almost entirely by Ford Blue, whose segment EBIT swung +$2,320M year over year on flat revenue as U.S. regulatory compliance costs collapsed following 2025 legislation that set CAFE civil penalties to zero and stripped California ZEV authority. Management has raised FY2026 guidance twice — adjusted EBIT from an original $8.0-10.0B to $10.0-11.0B, adjusted free cash flow from $5.0-6.0B to $6.0-7.0B — and consensus current-year EPS has risen 17.0% in ninety days to $1.87. At $13.90 the stock trades at 7.4x that figure with a 4.32% dividend yield, having given back essentially the entire May-July re-rating that carried it to $17.78 on the Ford Energy grid-storage announcement.

Three facts prevent this from being an asymmetric entry. First, the guidance raise is arithmetically a recognition of first-half outperformance rather than a second-half step-up: $10-11B for the year against $5,991M booked in H1 implies H2 adjusted EBIT of $4.0-5.0B, 16% to 33% *below* the first half, even with the Novelis aluminum recovery embedded. Second, the cash flow has not yet arrived — Company (ex-Ford Credit) adjusted free cash flow was $0.2B in H1 versus a $6.0-7.0B full-year guide, requiring roughly 97% of the annual total in the back half, and the industrial balance sheet moved from +$7.7B net cash at year-end to -$0.3B net debt at June 30 while 95% of consolidated H1 operating cash flow ($5,661M consolidated, $277M at the Company) originated inside the finance arm. Third, Ford carries exactly two of the three investment-grade ratings its credit agreements require (Moody's is already at Ba1; S&P is BBB- with a Negative outlook), and a single S&P action would trigger subsidiary guarantees across the $13.5B revolver, the $2.0B and $2.5B supplemental facilities, the $3.0B delayed-draw term loan and the newly assumed $3.8B Department of Energy note.

Consensus EPS for next year is $1.90, up only 2.9% in ninety days against +17.0% for the current year. The market is capitalizing the 2026 recovery and explicitly declining to extrapolate it. On flat forward earnings, a 7.4x multiple is close to where large-cap automakers with captive finance arms have traded through cycles, and the re-rating catalysts — Ford Energy revenue (2028 at earliest), the 8% margin aspiration (versus 5.3-5.9% implied by 2026 guidance) — sit beyond the investable horizon. We maintain HOLD on the basis of a covered 4.3% yield plus an $15.50 base case, and identify Q3 cash conversion as the specific event that would resolve the call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Ford is a Delaware-incorporated global vehicle manufacturer with approximately 169,000 employees, of whom about 56,300 U.S. hourly workers are UAW-represented. FY2025 global wholesales were 4,395,000 units, down from 4,470,000 in 2024. Reporting runs through four segments: Ford Blue (ICE and hybrid Ford/Lincoln vehicles, service parts), Ford Model e (EVs, extended-range electrics, embedded software), Ford Pro (commercial and government — Super Duty, Transit, Ranger, plus telematics and service), and Ford Credit (captive finance).

Geographic position is bifurcated. U.S. market share rose from 12.4% (2023) to 12.6% (2024) to 13.2% (2025), and Canada from 13.7% to 15.2%. China share fell from 1.8% to 1.3% with wholesales down from 467k to 379k over the same span. Worldwide dealership count fell from 9,006 to 8,226 during 2025 (-8.7%), with Ford-brand outlets down 733; the 10-K provides no attribution, and the most plausible read is China and European network rationalization consistent with the $736M European restructuring charge, though this is inference rather than disclosure.

Powertrain mix is where the operating story sits. U.S. electric sales fell 14.1% in 2025 to 84,113 units while hybrids rose 21.7% to 228,072 and ICE rose 5.5% to 1,891,939. By Q2 2026, EVs were 9,746 of 549,200 U.S. units — 1.8% of sales versus 3.8% for full-year 2025. Ford's volume and profit growth is coming from trucks and hybrids.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025.** Revenue of $187.3B (+1.3%) produced a GAAP operating loss of $9,169M and a net loss attributable to Ford of $8,182M after $17,356M of pre-tax special items. Adjusted EBIT was $6,780M (3.6% margin) and adjusted EPS $1.09. The special items itemize as roughly $8.4B of Model e asset and goodwill impairment, ~$1.1B of EV program cancellations and ~$1.2B of related cash charges, a $3,173M BlueOval SK joint-venture impairment, $1,198M for cancelling the all-electric three-row SUV, $736M of European restructuring, $723M of pension/OPEB and a ~$521M fuel-injector field service action. Two company-specific headwinds compounded the year: tariffs at roughly $3B gross and $2B net to EBIT, and the Novelis aluminum plant fires at approximately $2B.

**H1 2026.** Revenue was $91,549M against roughly $90,822M. Adjusted EBIT of $5,991M (6.5%) nearly doubled year over year, with adjusted EPS of $1.08. GAAP net income attributable was +$1,221M despite $4,405M of special items, of which the BlueOval SK unwind accounted for $3.6B. Q2 specifically: revenue $48,296M (-3.8% YoY), adjusted EBIT $2,503M (5.2%), adjusted EPS $0.42 against a $0.36 consensus, and a GAAP net loss of $1,327M.

Segment composition matters more than the aggregate:

| Segment | H1'26 EBIT | Margin | H1'25 EBIT | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ford Pro | $3,403M | 10.5% | $3,627M | 10.7% |
| Ford Blue | $3,077M | 6.2% | $757M | 1.6% |
| Ford Model e | -$1,696M | -75.1% | -$2,178M | -60.5% |
| Ford Credit (EBT) | $1,540M | — | $1,225M | — |

Ford Pro generated 57% of company adjusted EBIT on 36% of revenue, and it is decelerating: Q2 Pro EBIT fell to $1,718M (9.7% margin) from $2,318M (12.3%) on wholesales down 13%, attributed to the aluminum disruption and commodity costs. Ford Blue's swing accounts for the bulk of the improvement, and the 10-Q names "lower regulatory compliance expense" among its drivers — traceable to the ~$700M of compliance-credit expense recorded in 2025 against approximately $1.6B of remaining purchase obligations under credit agreements, some of which were terminated as requirements relaxed. That relief is real and recurring while the underlying legislation stands, but the 10-K discloses it is subject to pending legal challenge.

Model e's year-over-year improvement is largely mechanical. Segment D&A fell to $48M from $154M following the FY2025 impairment, which accounts for roughly 58% of the $184M cost tailwind in the Q2 bridge. Wholesales fell 53% to 28,000 units, and implied loss per wholesale unit deteriorated from about -$22,150 to -$32,821. H1 Model e capital spending of $2,046M was approximately 91% of the segment's $2,258M of revenue. Full-year guidance remains a ~$4.0B loss including ~$1.0B of incremental Universal EV platform and Ford Energy investment. These per-unit figures use rounded wholesale counts and Model e absorbs enterprise software and connected-vehicle spend, so they overstate the pure product economics, but the direction is unambiguous.

**Cash and balance sheet.** Company cash fell from $28.7B to $22.3B in six months and Company liquidity from $49.8B to $43.4B, while Company debt rose from $21.0B to $22.6B — moving net cash of +$7.7B to net debt of -$0.3B. Identifiable contributors: the $2.3B cash settlement of the 0.00% convertible notes on March 16, assumption of the $3.8B DOE promissory note (offset by $0.9B of acquired Kentucky plant assets and $0.1B of cash), $4,758M of capital spending, $1,206M of dividends, $311M of buybacks and roughly $0.8B of restructuring cash. Partly offsetting: $1,875M of distributions from Ford Credit versus $700M in H1 2025.

Company adjusted free cash flow was $2.1B in Q2 and $0.2B for the half, against a $6.0-7.0B full-year guide. Ford's own Item 1 language confirms genuine first-half-weighted production, which mechanically defers cash conversion, and the convertible settlement plus restructuring outflows are identifiable non-recurring drains. Even allowing for both, a ~97% second-half concentration is the sharpest single test of this guidance and we treat it as a sensitivity, not a forecast.

Equity attributable to Ford fell from $44,835M (12/31/2024) to $35,952M (12/31/2025) to $35,719M (6/30/2026), a $9,116M (-20.3%) erosion in eighteen months. Retained earnings were unchanged at $22,508M across the half — H1 net income of $1,221M was exactly offset by $1,221M of dividends declared. Book value per share is $8.96, so the stock trades at 1.55x book. Deferred tax assets of $23,107M equal 64.7% of book equity and 8.1% of total assets; excluding them entirely leaves $3.16 per share of residual equity. That is a crude sensitivity rather than a tangible-book calculation, and Ford actually released valuation allowances in South America and South Asia for a ~$1.5B net 2025 benefit with only $628M remaining against ~$20.6B expected to be recovered. But the DTA's size makes sustained U.S. taxable income load-bearing.

Consolidated leverage of roughly 7.0x equity is not the relevant industrial metric: $137.3B of the debt and $157.7B of the assets sit at Ford Credit, deliberately run at 9.4:1 against a 9-10:1 target. Industrial-calibrated distress screens are structurally unreliable for this issuer.

**Earnings quality signal.** The four-quarter surprise record is three beats and one miss: +21.2% (Sep-25), -31.9% (Dec-25), +245.4% (Mar-26), +17.7% (Jun-26). The Q1 outlier deserves scrutiny. Derived Q1 adjusted EBIT was $3,488M on $43,253M of revenue — an 8.1% margin against 5.2% in Q2 and 3.6% for FY2025. The 10-Q states that of ~$3B of expected tariff reimbursements, about $1.3B relates to IEEPA and Q1 2026 rulings by the Supreme Court and the Court of International Trade, while only ~$500M of IEEPA cash is expected in H2. A large, largely non-cash, non-repeating tariff benefit recognized in Q1 without special-item classification would explain both the outlier margin and the guided H2 step-down. The 10-Q does not state the P&L timing or line item, so this remains a hypothesis; ordinary summer-shutdown seasonality and H2-weighted Model e investment could independently produce the same shape.

## Growth Analysis

Ford's 2026 improvement is margin recovery, not growth. Revenue was +1.3% in FY2025 and approximately flat in H1 2026, with Q2 revenue down 3.8% year over year and wholesale declines in all three automotive segments including Ford Pro. Global wholesales fell in 2025 and China continues to contract toward irrelevance.

The estimate revision picture is instructive in its asymmetry. Current-quarter consensus rose 18.2% in ninety days to $0.42, current-year 17.0% to $1.87, and next-quarter 13.4% to $0.37 — but next-year consensus rose only 2.9% to $1.90. Twenty-two analysts see FY2027 EPS essentially flat against FY2026. This falls short of the profile that reliably precedes further re-rating, where a durable multi-year inflection pulls out-year estimates up alongside the near term. Here consensus is marking to the H1 print and stopping.

Guidance-implied arithmetic supports that caution. Applying the $10-11B adjusted EBIT guide, $1.4-1.5B of interest on Company debt, an adjusted tax rate of 16.6-20.0% and ~4,070M adjusted diluted shares yields adjusted EPS of roughly $1.68-$1.96, midpoint $1.82 — slightly below the $1.87 consensus. Segment guidance is Pro $7.0-7.5B, Blue $5.0-5.5B, Model e approximately -$4.0B, Ford Credit EBT above $2.5B, capex $9.5-10.5B.

Three growth vectors deserve separate treatment:

*Hybrid and truck mix.* This is the working engine. U.S. hybrids grew 21.7% in 2025 and Ford is structurally advantaged by the policy retreat from EV mandates in North America. The 10-K notes incentive activity resuming "especially with waning policy support for EVs leading to excess supply in that market segment."

*Ford Pro software and services.* Attached telematics and service revenue is the highest-quality growth in the portfolio, but the segment's margin compressed 260bp year over year in Q2 and reported subscription metrics come from secondary sources we could not verify against filings.

*Ford Energy.* The December 2025 program repurposes Glendale, Kentucky from NCM to LFP chemistry at approximately $2B of capital, targeting at least 20 GWh annually of utility-scale storage by late 2027, with the containerized 5.45 MWh "DC Block" as the product. EDF Power Solutions North America committed in May 2026 to purchase up to 4 GWh annually over five years with first deliveries in 2028. The addressable market is real — BloombergNEF puts 2025 global storage deployment above 100 GW heading toward 158 GW in 2026. But CFO Sherry House was explicit at the June 3 UBS conference that Ford is not discussing expansion beyond the first 20 GWh, and no revenue arrives before 2028. The 8% margin aspiration attributed to management compares against 3.6% actual in FY2025 and 5.3-5.9% implied by 2026 guidance — roughly $15B of adjusted EBIT versus $10-11B guided. That gap is not bridgeable within our horizon.

The peer table supplied is close to unusable for relative work: GM at 44.1x and Tesla at 304x are both trading on charge-affected or growth-extrapolated GAAP earnings, and Winnebago at 23.6x is a different business. The median of 44.1x tells us nothing about Ford's fair multiple. The more defensible comparison is Ford against its own cycle history and against the structural discount automakers carry for capital intensity, unionized cost structure and captive-finance consolidation. On that basis 7-8x forward with a covered 4%+ yield is neither expensive nor a clear mispricing.

Two considerations argue the shares are modestly cheap. Consensus mean target is $15.78 and median $15.46 against a $13.90 price, with sentiment improving — recommendation counts moved from 2 strong buy / 3 buy / 16 hold / 1 sell three months ago to 3 / 5 / 13 / 1 now, and Citi upgraded to Buy with a $20 target after the Q2 print. Separately, the stock has round-tripped from $17.78 (May 29) to $13.90 while guidance was raised, meaning the Ford Energy option value the market paid up for in May is now priced at approximately nothing. Director equity grants on May 21 were priced at $13.22, so the shares sit roughly 5% above the pre-narrative level despite two subsequent guidance raises.

Two considerations argue against paying up. The multiple is chronically low for structural reasons that have not changed: book equity is eroding, 65% of that equity is a deferred tax asset, and the industrial business did not self-fund its H1 capital spending and dividend. And flat FY2027 consensus means multiple expansion, not earnings growth, has to do the work — which requires the Q3 cash-flow confirmation to arrive. Cheapness without a dated catalyst has been a value trap in this name repeatedly; the score reflects moderate rather than compelling attractiveness.

We set base case fair value at **$15.50**, or 8.2x FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.90 — a modest multiple expansion from 7.3x that we think is warranted if H2 free cash flow lands within guidance, and which sits essentially at the analyst median of $15.46.

## Competitive Landscape

Ford's competitive trajectory is genuinely mixed rather than uniformly improving or deteriorating.

Improving: U.S. share up 80bp over two years to 13.2% and Canadian share up 150bp to 15.2%, both in the company's highest-margin markets. The North American policy environment has moved decisively toward Ford's ICE/hybrid mix — CAFE penalties at zero, ZEV authority eliminated, EPA proposing to stop regulating vehicle GHGs, Canada repealing its MY2027 ZEV requirement, Quebec planning to repeal its 2035 ICE ban. For a company deriving 85.8% of 2025 U.S. sales from ICE and 10.3% from hybrids, this is a direct and durable margin tailwind visible in Blue's H1 result. Ford Pro's Super Duty and Transit franchises face limited direct competition with sticky fleet relationships and attached service revenue.

Deteriorating: China share at 1.3% is now immaterial. Model e's unit economics worsened while volume halved. Dealer count fell 8.7% in a year. Ford Pro margin compressed 260bp in Q2 on a 13% wholesale decline, which management attributes to aluminum supply rather than competitive loss — a claim that Q3 will test.

The structural question is the low-cost EV path. Ford owns and operates BlueOval Battery Park Michigan and licenses LFP cell and manufacturing technology from CATL, with production beginning around June 2026 to supply the Universal EV platform including a reported ~$30,000 electric pickup for 2027. The 2025 legislation preserved the Section 45X advanced manufacturing production credit but attached "prohibited foreign entity" restrictions, and secondary reporting describes the Michigan plant as one Congress fought over. A technology license is structurally different from foreign ownership or control and Ford may well satisfy the FEOC tests, but no filing we reviewed states Ford's eligibility conclusion. Both Ford's cheapest-EV cost structure and Ford Energy's margin ambition depend on the answer.

Ford also carries approximately $6.4B of maximum committed battery-materials offtake purchases through 2035, a fixed obligation against a volume outlook management has repeatedly cut.

## Risk Assessment

**Rating covenant cliff (highest-conviction structural risk).** Senior unsecured ratings are Moody's Ba1/Stable (below investment grade), S&P BBB-/Negative, Fitch BBB-/Stable, DBRS BBB(low)/Stable. Credit agreements require at least two investment-grade ratings from Fitch, Moody's and S&P, failing which subsidiary guarantees become required across the $13.5B corporate revolver, $2.0B supplemental revolver, $2.5B 364-day revolver and $3.0B delayed-draw term loan. DBRS does not count. Ford therefore holds exactly two qualifying ratings and one of them is on Negative outlook. The new $3.8B DOE note carries substantially similar covenants including a $4.0B minimum Available Liquidity test. A downgrade would not accelerate debt but would structurally subordinate existing unsecured holders with no offsetting benefit to equity. Mitigants: S&P has held BBB-/Negative across at least two consecutive filings without acting, liquidity of $43.4B sits far above the $4.0B test, and Ford Credit's standalone position improved (net liquidity $27.4B, H1 EBT $1,540M).

**Warranty.** The accrual balance rose from $14,032M (1/1/2025) to $17,190M (1/1/2026) to $17,571M (6/30/2026) — roughly 9.3% of trailing revenue. Ford discloses up to ~$2.0B of reasonably possible costs in excess of accruals for material field service actions and operates under a 2024 NHTSA consent order with an independent monitor. The encouraging counterpoint is the most underappreciated positive in the filings: adverse development on pre-existing warranties fell from +$1,586M in H1 2025 to +$475M in H1 2026, a 70% reduction, with new-issue accruals also down ($3,351M to $2,939M). Two quarters is a short series and lower new-issue accruals partly reflect lower volume, but if sustained this is worth more to earnings power than any single product program.

**Aluminum.** Novelis plant fires cost ~$2B in FY2025. FY2026 guidance assumes a net $1.0B year-over-year improvement while still absorbing ~$1.5B of temporary sourcing costs, and separately embeds just above $2.0B of commodity headwinds largely from aluminum pricing. The F-150's aluminum-intensive body makes this exposure structurally larger than for steel-heavier competitors, and the 10-K attributes the commodity headwind to higher aluminum pricing due to global supply constraints.

**Tariff receivable.** Of ~$3B expected in reimbursements, about $1.3B relates to IEEPA and Q1 2026 court rulings, yet only ~$500M of IEEPA cash is expected in H2. Roughly $800M of recognized benefit has no expected 2026 cash conversion and remains exposed to further policy or judicial change. Guidance explicitly excludes unannounced tariff changes.

**Ford Credit and the consumer.** Loss-to-receivables rose to 62bp in H1 2026 from 56bp on higher repossessions and severities, and the allowance rose to $968M on $105,837M of recorded investment (0.91%) from $911M on $111,490M (0.82%) — rising coverage on a shrinking book. Group III (weakest) dealer financing rose from 2.3% to 2.9%. Offsetting: consumer delinquency was stable (>60 days flat at 0.4%, 31-60 days improved to 1.0%) and U.S. auction values were essentially flat at $32,365. Under the relationship agreement, leverage above 12.5:1 could require a parent capital contribution; current leverage is 9.4:1. A captive lender's rising loss ratio is the leading edge worth monitoring closely.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** CEO total compensation was $27,519,558 for FY2025 with "compensation actually paid" of $49,214,346 in a year carrying an $8,182M GAAP loss. The mechanical explanation is that CAP marks unvested equity to market and TSR was strong, which is how the SEC calculation works. More informative is Ford's own company-selected measure: adjusted EBIT margin declined for five consecutive years (7.3% → 6.6% → 5.9% → 5.5% → 3.6%). An April 15, 2026 Item 5.02 filing (officer/director departure or election) and December 12, 2025 Items 5.02/5.03 filings remain unread and unresolved. April 2026 credit facility amendments removed sustainability-linked pricing targets.

**Other.** In *Versata v. Ford* the Federal Circuit reinstated $82M on May 22, 2026 and remanded for a new trial on trade-secret damages; Ford petitioned for rehearing June 22. Brazilian indirect tax matters could require collateral exceeding $1B in aggregate (assessed as remote, under $100M posted). Ford discloses up to ~$0.4B of other reasonably possible losses in excess of accruals. Q2 Ford Blue wholesale declines are attributed partly to Middle East conflict, and FY2026 guidance excludes significant escalation there.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.3% sits below one-year realized volatility of 36.8% (a -5.5 point spread) at the 20th percentile of the current chain, with the nearest expiry pricing only a ±1.5% move. ATM IV has compressed 9.1 points since July 21 (40.3% → 31.3%), the expected post-earnings crush. The term structure is in contango — 29.2% at one month, 32.8% at three months, 37.6% at six months — placing the market's priced uncertainty in the December window rather than the immediate term, which is consistent with the Q3 print (late October) and full-year cash confirmation being the live catalysts. Put/call volume of 0.39 and a -4.6% skew read as call-heavy near-term flow; put/call open interest of 0.95, up from 0.70 on July 21, indicates protective positioning accumulating in the standing book rather than in current flow. The combination — cheap near-term vol, richer long-dated vol, rising put open interest — is consistent with our view that the near term is quiet and the decision point is the Q3 cash print. It provides no directional confirmation either way.

The supplied short interest figure of 7,404 shares against 3.92 billion outstanding is implausible and should be treated as a data artifact; we draw no positioning conclusion from it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $19.50 (+40%)

Adjusted EBIT lands at the top of the $10-11B guide as the Novelis recovery delivers the assumed $1.0B improvement and truck/hybrid pricing holds. H2 free cash flow arrives at $6.0B+, validating the $6.0-7.0B guide and confirming that H1's $0.2B was seasonal and convertible-settlement distortion. Warranty adverse development stays near the H1 2026 run rate of $475M rather than reverting to $1,586M, reducing a persistent earnings drag. S&P revises its outlook to Stable, removing the covenant cliff from the discount. The 4.3% yield is supplemented, signalling free cash flow above the 40-50% distribution policy's requirement. Ford Energy converts the EDF framework into firm take-or-pay volume and adds a second offtaker, restoring the option value the market priced in May. On $1.95 of FY2027 EPS at 10x — the multiple Ford commanded in prior confident cycles — the shares reach $19.50, roughly Citi's $20 target and the high end of the analyst range.

### Bear Case — $10.75 (-23%)

H2 free cash flow misses badly. Working capital does not reverse, only $500M of IEEPA tariff cash arrives against $1.3B recognized, and the aluminum sourcing premium runs above the $1.5B assumption. Full-year adjusted FCF prints at $3-4B rather than $6-7B, making the $2.39B dividend roughly two-thirds of free cash flow and inconsistent with the 40-50% policy. Company net debt deepens, and S&P downgrades to BB+ — triggering subsidiary guarantees across the bank facilities and forcing a re-rating of the whole capital structure. Ford Credit loss-to-receivables continues past 62bp as the consumer weakens, requiring higher provisioning. Ford Pro's Q2 margin compression proves competitive rather than supply-driven, removing the segment that carries 57% of company EBIT. FY2027 consensus falls from $1.90 toward $1.60, and at 6.7x the shares revisit the $11.11 52-week low area. The $11.00 analyst low target corroborates this zone.

### Base Case — $15.50 (+11.5% price, ~15.8% total return)

Adjusted EBIT lands mid-guide near $10.5B, delivering FY2026 adjusted EPS around $1.85. H2 free cash flow arrives at $4.5-6.0B — enough to cover the dividend and stabilize the industrial balance sheet, short of the top of guidance. Warranty improvement partly persists; aluminum costs partly offset it. Ratings hold. FY2027 EPS remains near $1.90 and the multiple expands modestly to 8.2x as cash conversion is verified. Ford Energy remains a 2028 story with no 2026-27 earnings contribution. The dividend adds 4.3%.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear yields roughly +8% of price return plus dividend carry, or approximately +12% total over twelve months. That is a fair-return proposition against a 23% bear case, not the asymmetry required for a BUY, and it sits at the boundary of ACCUMULATE. Sector context reinforces caution: automobile manufacturers sit within Consumer Discretionary, and the specific macro channel — rising Ford Credit loss severities and an unresolved rate path — bears directly on both unit demand and captive-finance credit quality.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income rationale).** The reliable component of expected return is the 4.32% dividend, covered at 34-40% of guided free cash flow; the appreciation component is contingent on H2 cash conversion that has not yet been demonstrated.

**Base case target: $15.50** — 8.2x FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.90, +11.5% from $13.90, +15.8% including the dividend. Reasoning: modest multiple expansion from the current 7.3x, warranted if free cash flow verifies within guidance; consistent with the $15.46 analyst median.

**Bull case: $19.50** (+40%) — 10x $1.95, top-of-guide delivery plus rating stabilization plus a firm Ford Energy contract.

**Bear case: $10.75** (-22.7%) — 5.7x a reduced $1.90, on an H2 free cash flow miss, S&P downgrade, or Ford Credit deterioration. Corroborated by the $11.00 analyst low and the $11.11 52-week low.

**Timeframe:** twelve months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 report (late October 2026).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 Company adjusted free cash flow of $3.0B+ cumulative for the nine months, putting the $6.0-7.0B full-year guide within reach and restoring positive Company net cash. This is the single highest-value confirmation available.
2. S&P revising the BBB- outlook from Negative to Stable, removing the covenant-cliff discount.
3. A price decline into the low $12s with FY2027 consensus intact at or above $1.85, which would compress the forward multiple to roughly 6.5x and improve the base-case upside to 25%+ with the yield above 4.9%.
4. Warranty adverse development at or below $500M for a third consecutive half, confirming the quality-cost bubble is rolling off.

## Conclusion

Ford has done real work in the last two quarters. Adjusted EBIT margin recovered from 3.6% to 6.5%, guidance was raised twice, the BlueOval SK unwind reduced maximum future guarantee draws from $4.9B to zero and cut maximum VIE loss from $5.2B to $275M, warranty adverse development fell 70%, and management settled $2.3B of convertibles in cash rather than issuing equity. The stock trades at 7.4x forward earnings with a 4.32% yield after surrendering the entire May-July re-rating, so the Ford Energy option value is now free.

What holds us at HOLD is the gap between adjusted earnings and cash. The raised guidance implies second-half adjusted EBIT below the first half, the second-half free cash flow requirement is roughly 97% of the annual total, the industrial balance sheet has flipped to a small net debt position, 95% of consolidated operating cash flow came from the finance arm, and Ford holds exactly two of the three investment-grade ratings its covenants require with one on Negative outlook. Next-year consensus is flat at $1.90 despite the 17% current-year revision, so the market is capitalizing the recovery without extrapolating it — and on flat forward earnings, only multiple expansion produces returns, which requires the cash to show up.

Existing holders should maintain exposure for the covered yield and an $15.50 base case. New capital has a better entry available: either the low $12s on unchanged estimates, or the Q3 print confirming that nine-month free cash flow is on a path to guidance.
