**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PJT Partners is a pure-play, advisory-focused independent investment bank with no lending or trading balance sheet, competing on advice and relationships across three franchises: Strategic Advisory (M&A, activism defense, capital-markets advisory), Restructuring & Special Situations, and PJT Park Hill (fund placement and private capital solutions). The Restructuring franchise is the differentiator — ranked #1 in global and U.S. announced restructurings in three of the last four years — and provides genuine counter-cyclicality that pure-M&A boutiques lack. The business is asset-light, debt-free, and generates high returns on a thin capital base: FY25 revenue of $1,714M (+15%), pretax income of $343M (+27%), net income to PJT Inc. of $180M (+34%), and free cash flow of $480M at a 28% margin.

The near-term picture is a tension between strong reported results and softening forward estimates. Q1'26 was strong on the surface — revenue +29% YoY, pretax income +53% — driven by strategic advisory, private capital solutions, and continued restructuring strength. Management frames a "multi-year restructuring cycle" sustained by private-credit stress, sponsor-driven liability management, and technological disruption. Against that, consensus EPS estimates have drifted down across the board over the past 90 days: current quarter -3.7%, next quarter -8.2%, current year -2.4%. That divergence — record headline revenue alongside falling forward estimates — is the central analytical question and the reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY.

At $162.99 the stock trades at roughly 23x trailing/forward earnings, essentially at the peer median P/E of 23.0, with a modest 0.6% dividend yield. The valuation offers no discount to peers to compensate for the negative estimate revisions or the structurally lumpy, milestone-driven revenue recognition. The prior trajectory (6.2 in March → 7.2 in May) reflected the strong Q1 print and the $800M buyback expansion; the softening estimate momentum and the stock's decline from its January high (~$195 to $162.99, roughly 17%) argue for tempering that improvement rather than extending it. This is a high-quality franchise fairly priced.

## Company Overview

PJT was spun off from Blackstone in October 2015, founded by Paul J. Taubman (formerly of Morgan Stanley) via the concurrent acquisition of PJT Capital. It operates as one integrated reportable segment across three franchises. The Restructuring & Special Situations business is a market leader (IFR Restructuring Advisor of the Year 2020–2023) that generates fees when defaults and liability-management exercises rise, structurally offsetting M&A weakness. Strategic Advisory covers M&A, spin-offs, activism defense, shareholder/governance advisory, and a geopolitical/policy practice. PJT Park Hill leads in alternative-asset fund placement and is increasingly weighted toward private capital solutions and GP-led secondaries, a bright spot amid a soft traditional fundraising market.

As of December 31, 2025, PJT employed 1,224 people including 133 partners, served 436 clients, and recorded 255 fees of ≥$1M. No client exceeded 10% of revenue in any of the last three years — low concentration for an advisory firm. The competitive edge rests on the #1 restructuring brand, founder-led culture, high partner talent density, integrated cross-selling, and an asset-light, high-return model. The structural vulnerability is that the business is people- and relationship-based, and the loss of Taubman or senior partners would be materially damaging.

## Financial Analysis

Growth has been broad-based and accelerating. Revenue rose from $1,026M (2022) to $1,153M (2023), $1,493M (2024), and $1,714M (2025). Advisory fees reached $1,500M (+14%) and placement fees $182M (+24%) in FY25. Q1'26 extended this: revenue of $418M vs. $325M, pretax income of $80M vs. $53M, and operating cash flow swinging to +$64M from -$76M a year earlier.

Profitability is strong for a labor-intensive advisory model. Net margin is ~18%, and free cash flow represents a 28% margin. Compensation is the dominant cost at ~68% of revenue in FY25 and ~67% in Q1'26; comp grew only 12% in 2025 while revenue rose 15%, meaning management accrued at a lower comp ratio despite higher revenue. Because comp is roughly two-thirds of revenue, small changes in the accrual ratio drive outsized earnings leverage — a positive in up years and a risk if talent-retention pressure forces the ratio higher.

The balance sheet is essentially debt-free, with $585.8M cash plus short-term investments at YE25 (declining to ~$388M at Q1'26 after the seasonal Q1 bonus payout, a normal cash cycle rather than deterioration). A $100M revolver is undrawn (matures July 2026). The broker-dealer subsidiary held $221M net capital at Q1'26, ~$220M above the regulatory minimum. The TRA obligation is modest at ~$34.6M, though a change-of-control acceleration was estimated at ~$402M.

Two accounting nuances warrant flagging. First, the Altman Z-Score of 1.39 ("distress zone") is a false signal — a mechanical artifact of the Up-C partnership structure, where most economic value sits in Partnership Units recorded as non-controlling interest ($657M at Q1'26) outside PJT Inc.'s thin GAAP equity ($273–308M), inflating reported D/E to 2.71. With zero funded debt, ~$390–586M of cash/investments, and $480M annual FCF, bankruptcy risk is negligible; a Piotroski of 4/4 confirms the healthy read. Second, cash-settled Partnership Unit exchanges are a large, variable use of cash ($136M in Q1'26 alone vs. $57M a year earlier). This reduces future dilution but competes with buybacks and dividends for capital and can swing quarter to quarter based on partner exchange requests.

Capital allocation is aggressive and shareholder-friendly. The buyback authorization was raised to $800M in April 2026 (from $500M), a strong confidence signal; Q1'26 repurchased 0.4M shares at ~$147. The quarterly dividend is $0.25/share (~0.6% yield). The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four straight beats over the trailing four quarters (1.5%, 6.9%, 6.9%, 6.6% surprises).

## Growth Analysis

The bull case for durable growth rests on the counter-cyclical mix. Restructuring is running elevated into 2026, with management citing private-credit stress, technological disruption, and sponsor-driven liability management as sustaining a multi-year cycle. On the M&A side, 2H25 momentum is carrying into 2026, with activity concentrating in larger deals (global deal count hit an 11-year low even as volumes rose). Private capital solutions and GP-led secondaries are a genuine growth vector as LPs seek liquidity in a slow-realization environment.

The offset is that forward estimates are not confirming the Q1 acceleration. Consensus EPS revisions are negative across every horizon: current quarter -1.0% (30d) / -3.7% (90d), next quarter -1.7% / -8.2%, current year flat (30d) / -2.4% (90d), next year roughly flat. The 90-day trend is broadly falling, indicating negative earnings momentum despite the strong Q1 headline. This matters because PJT's revenue is milestone-driven and lumpy — a handful of deal closings drive results, and Q1'26 fee count actually declined (61 vs. 65 fees ≥$1M) even as revenue rose. Growth is concentrating in fewer, larger transactions, which raises variability and makes any single soft quarter more impactful.

Revenue growth is real and broad-based, but the estimate-revision velocity is negative, not the +30% pattern that would flag a strong buy. On balance the growth thesis is intact but decelerating in the consensus view, which supports a mid-range score rather than a high one.

## Valuation Assessment

At $162.99, PJT trades at ~23.3x trailing/forward earnings (the two are equal given the flat forward estimate). Against the peer set — Moelis 23.2x, BGC 30.4x, Piper Sandler 19.7x, Houlihan Lokey 21.8x, StoneX 23.0x — PJT sits close to the peer median. There is no discount to compensate for the negative estimate revisions, and no premium is obviously warranted given that the restructuring franchise is already reflected in the multiple.

The analyst target range is $141 (low) to $190 (high), mean $169.8, median $175.0. At the mean target, upside is ~4%; at the median, ~7%; at the high, ~17%. With estimates drifting lower rather than rising, these targets are more likely to be genuine ceilings than the stale floors typical of positive-inflection names — treating targets as floors requires rising estimates and a cheap multiple, and PJT has neither. On current-year consensus EPS of $7.52, the stock trades at ~21.7x; on next-year $8.55, ~19.1x. Those are reasonable multiples for a high-quality, debt-free advisory franchise, but not cheap enough to argue mispricing, and the falling near-term estimates cap the case for multiple expansion.

PJT is not screening cheap, so there is no value-trap concern, but neither is there a specific identified catalyst for the market to re-rate the stock higher. Cycle adjustment does not help: unlike a trough-earnings name with tripling EPS, PJT's earnings are near cyclical strength (restructuring elevated, M&A recovering), so trailing and forward multiples are broadly representative rather than depressed. The valuation is fair, which supports a mid-range score. A meaningful pullback toward the low-$140s (the analyst floor, ~19x current-year EPS) would restore asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

PJT occupies the elite tier of independent advisory boutiques alongside Evercore, Moelis, Lazard, Centerview, Perella Weinberg, and Houlihan Lokey. The independent-advisory niche continues to take share from bulge brackets on conflict-free positioning. PJT's differentiator is the #1 global restructuring franchise, which gives it counter-cyclicality that pure-M&A boutiques lack — an advantage in a macro environment of elevated rates, private-credit stress, and tariff-driven uncertainty. The firm is winning share in private capital solutions and GP-led secondaries, an area of structural demand growth as LPs seek liquidity.

The competitive position is improving-to-stable rather than deteriorating. Segment revenue growth outpaces a subdued industry backdrop, and the restructuring brand is a durable moat. The honest constraints: PJT concedes that larger competitors offer financing and underwriting it cannot, and that Park Hill faces low barriers to entry with traditional fundraising remaining challenging. The trajectory favors PJT given the current mix (recovering M&A, elevated restructuring, growing private-capital solutions), which supports a solid competitive-position score. I temper it modestly given that the recent-window predictive value of competitive-position scoring has been weak, and lean on the growth and financial-health evidence for the core view.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is extreme quarterly revenue volatility. Fees are recognized at transaction milestones outside PJT's control, and a handful of deal closings drive results. This creates high earnings variability and share-price sensitivity to any single soft quarter. The negative 90-day estimate revisions raise the probability of a near-term disappointment relative to the strong Q1 comparison.

The counter-cyclical hedge is not absolute. M&A weakness is offset by restructuring strength and vice versa, but a simultaneous downturn — weak M&A and falling defaults — or a benign credit environment that compresses restructuring would remove the hedge. Talent risk is structural: the loss of Taubman or senior partners would be materially damaging, and there is $425M of unrecognized RSU compensation plus $27.5M of unvested Partnership Unit comp, with a growing retirement-eligible partner pool that could pressure retention. Erosion of non-compete enforceability is a flagged regulatory threat. The Q1'26 segment-reporting change (dropping the advisory-vs-placement split) reduces transparency into Park Hill's fundraising softness going forward.

Governance carries a dual-class structure: Class B holders (including Taubman) held ~28.8% of director-election voting power and ~38.1% on other matters at YE25, and executives/directors controlled ~23.5%/33.0%. Founder/partner voting concentration could deter a takeover premium — a consideration for minority Class A holders, though it also aligns with the founder's ~$0.9B stake. Most of these risks are known and reasonably priced; the primary unpriced risk is the tension between record Q1 revenue and falling forward estimates, which the market may not have fully digested.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($190, ~17% upside):** The multi-year restructuring cycle sustains as private-credit stress broadens and liability-management exercises accelerate, while M&A volumes continue recovering off the 2024–25 low. Comp-ratio discipline drives operating leverage — a lower accrual ratio on 15%+ revenue growth produces outsized earnings gains. Private capital solutions and GP-led secondaries scale into a structural demand shift. The $800M buyback shrinks the share count meaningfully at prices management considers attractive. Estimates reverse higher as the strong Q1 momentum is confirmed in subsequent prints, and the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range on ~22x rising next-year EPS.

**Bear Case ($141, ~13% downside):** A single soft quarter exposes the milestone-driven revenue lumpiness, and the negative estimate revisions prove leading rather than lagging. M&A recovery stalls while credit conditions stay benign enough to compress the restructuring pipeline, removing the counter-cyclical hedge simultaneously. Talent attrition forces the comp ratio higher, eroding margins. The mid-20s multiple compresses toward the peer low (Piper Sandler at ~20x) as growth momentum fades, pulling the stock toward the $141 analyst floor.

**Base Case ($172, ~6% upside):** Restructuring stays elevated and M&A recovers gradually, delivering low-double-digit revenue growth in line with a modestly lowered consensus. The stock holds its ~22x forward multiple on current-year EPS of ~$7.52, drifting toward the analyst median of $175 as the buyback provides support. This is roughly 20x next-year consensus EPS of $8.55, consistent with a fairly-valued high-quality franchise.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected late July 2026), which is the natural resolution point for the Q1-strength-versus-estimate-drift tension.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive — current-year consensus rises meaningfully off $7.52 over a 30–90 day window, confirming the Q1 acceleration is durable rather than a lumpy-deal artifact.
- A pullback toward the low-$140s (the analyst floor, ~19x current-year EPS) restores asymmetric upside while fundamentals remain intact.
- Restructuring backlog/pipeline commentary strengthens alongside continued M&A recovery, evidencing the counter-cyclical hedge is working in both directions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A revenue miss or fee-count deterioration that confirms the negative estimate momentum, combined with a comp-ratio increase that compresses margins.
- Departure of Taubman or a cluster of senior partners, materially impairing the franchise.
- Simultaneous M&A and restructuring softness (weak deals plus a benign credit environment) that removes the counter-cyclical hedge.

## Conclusion

PJT is a high-quality, debt-free, asset-light advisory franchise with a genuine #1 global restructuring moat, ~$480M in free cash flow, exceptional founder alignment (Taubman's ~$0.9B stake against a $1.03M structured salary), and an expanded $800M buyback that signals management's confidence. The financial health is strong once the misleading Up-C Altman Z-Score is set aside, and the competitive position is stable-to-improving. These strengths justify the improved scoring trajectory into May.

The offset that keeps this a HOLD is the divergence between record Q1'26 revenue and broadly falling forward estimates, combined with a valuation that sits essentially at the peer median with no discount to compensate. The stock is fairly priced, and the milestone-driven revenue lumpiness makes any single soft quarter a real risk near current levels. I would become a buyer on positive estimate-revision velocity or a pullback toward the low-$140s, and would reassess at the next earnings print. This is a `growth_hold` — a franchise worth owning through the restructuring cycle, but at a price and estimate-momentum backdrop that does not yet offer asymmetric upside.
