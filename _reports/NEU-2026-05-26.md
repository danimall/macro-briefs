## Executive Summary

NewMarket Corporation operates through two segments: Petroleum Additives (~93% of revenue), where subsidiary Afton Chemical is one of four global oligopolists in lubricant/fuel additive packages, and Specialty Materials (~7% of revenue), comprising recently acquired AMPAC and Calca — the leading U.S. manufacturers of ammonium perchlorate and ultra-pure hydrazine for defense and space applications, operating in markets with limited qualified domestic competition. The company generates exceptional returns on equity (23.5%), operates with a disciplined owner-operator culture under the Gottwald family, and is diversifying into defense-adjacent businesses with structural demand tailwinds.

The investment case centers on three elements: (1) a petroleum additives business with stable margins (22.1% operating) despite volume headwinds, supported by oligopolistic industry structure and 1,400 patents; (2) a specialty materials segment that will meaningfully contribute to earnings growth once AMPAC's 50% capacity expansion completes in late 2026; and (3) aggressive capital return ($127M buybacks in Q1 2026 alone, ~3% annualized share count reduction). The primary concern is that petroleum additives volumes declined 6.9% in Q1 2026, with management citing both market softness and intentional portfolio pruning. This volume trajectory must stabilize for the stock to justify further re-rating from current levels, which already reflect a significant recovery from the March 2026 trough.

## Company Overview

NewMarket Corporation is a Richmond, Virginia-based specialty chemicals holding company with approximately 2,050 employees. The core Petroleum Additives segment (via Afton Chemical) manufactures engine oil, driveline, industrial, and fuel additive packages sold globally to multinational oil companies, independent blenders, and refiners. Geographic mix is approximately 40% North America, 30% EMEAI, 20% Asia Pacific, and 10% Latin America. No single customer represents 10%+ of revenue.

The Specialty Materials segment — built through two acquisitions totaling ~$900M in 2024-2025 — positions NEU as a critical supplier in the U.S. defense industrial base. AMPAC is described in SEC filings as a leading manufacturer and seller of ammonium perchlorate globally, with foreign producers believed not qualified for most U.S. Department of War and NASA programs. Calca is the leading U.S. producer of Ultra Pure® and high-purity hydrazine for satellite propulsion, operating in a market with limited qualified producers, high regulatory barriers, and strict customer qualification requirements. Both businesses benefit from multi-decade government relationships (Calca has supplied the DOD's Defense Logistics Agency for 70+ years).

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:**

Revenue has been stable around $2.7-2.8B annually (2022-2025), with 2025 at $2.725B. Operating margins expanded from 12.8% in 2022 to 20.0% in 2025 as raw material costs normalized and mix shifted toward higher-margin specialty materials. Net income was $418.7M in 2025 ($44.44 EPS on declining share count), producing a 15.4% net margin.

Q1 2026 showed revenue of $669.7M (-4.5% YoY) with net income of $118.1M ($12.62/share). Petroleum additives volumes fell 6.9% (North America -10.7%, Asia Pacific -9.4%), though operating margins held at 22.1%. Specialty materials operating profit dropped to $12.4M from $23.2M, primarily due to product shipment mix at AMPAC — the company has noted that this segment may experience substantial quarterly variation due to the nature of the business.

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:**

Operating cash flow was $569M in 2025 (21% of revenue), and $124M in Q1 2026. With capex of $78M in 2025 (rising to $100-150M in 2026 for AMPAC expansion), free cash flow was ~$491M. The balance sheet carries $940M in long-term debt (comprising $395.6M in 2.70% fixed-rate senior notes, $150M in 3.78% fixed-rate senior notes, and $394M in revolver borrowings) at a leverage ratio of 1.36x (covenant at 3.75x), current ratio of 2.55, and $502M of unused revolver capacity. An overfunded pension plan generates ~$43M annually in net periodic benefit income — a hidden P&L tailwind.

**Capital Return:**

Management repurchased 203,543 shares in Q1 2026 (~$126.8M at ~$617 average), with $302.6M remaining under authorization through 2027. The dividend was raised to $12/year (~1.6% yield at current prices). Combined buyback + dividend yield is approximately 4.5% at current prices, though the buyback component depends on the pace of future repurchases.

**Key Metrics:**
- ROE: 23.5%
- ROA: 12.0%
- FCF Yield (2025): ~7.2% ($491M / $6.8B market cap)
- Net Debt/EBITDA: ~1.4x
- Shares outstanding declining ~2-3% annually

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is bifurcated:

**Petroleum Additives (mature, low-growth):** Global lubricant demand is approximately flat. NEU's volumes have been declining (-4.9% in 2025, -6.9% in Q1 2026), partially reflecting deliberate pruning of low-margin business and partially reflecting genuine market softness. Management's posture is margin > volume — accepting lower revenue for stable/improving operating profit per unit. EV transition is a multi-decade headwind, but NEU is investing in EV transmission fluid additives and the installed base of ICE vehicles continues growing in emerging markets.

**Specialty Materials (high-growth, defense-driven):** AMPAC's 50%+ capacity expansion (up to $100M investment, completing late 2026) is the key near-term catalyst. With the segment generating $47M operating profit on $182M revenue in 2025 (note: this includes Calca only from the October 2025 acquisition date, so a full-year contribution will be higher), a 50% capacity addition could — based on our estimates assuming similar margins and successful ramp — plausibly add $25–40M in incremental operating profit by 2027–2028. This estimate is an analyst assumption; NewMarket has not disclosed expected revenue, utilization rates, or profit contributions from the expansion. The structural demand from missile replenishment programs, expanded space launch cadence, and satellite deployment growth supports the capacity investment.

**EPS Growth Path:** With flat-to-declining petroleum additives revenue offset by specialty materials growth and 2-3% annual share count reduction, mid-single-digit EPS growth (5-8% annually) is achievable absent any petroleum additives volume recovery. If volumes stabilize in the core business, growth could reach high-single-digits.

## Valuation Assessment

At $737.72, NEU trades at 16.6x trailing EPS ($44.44). This represents a discount to specialty chemical peers. Using comparable multiples from Balchem (33.2x), Sensient (33.4x), Eastman (21.2x), and Axalta (17.0x), the median of these four peers is approximately 27.2x — meaningfully above NEU's current multiple. Axalta, the most comparable peer given its automotive-chemical focus, trades at a slight premium at 17.0x.

The discount likely reflects: (1) perceived cyclical risk in petroleum additives given EV transition narrative, (2) lack of sell-side coverage (zero analyst recommendations visible), and (3) the stock's relatively low float and institutional visibility.

The peer comparison is imperfect — NEU has higher margins (20% operating vs. peer averages of 12-15%), generates substantially more free cash flow relative to market cap (7.2% FCF yield), and the specialty materials segment has defense-adjacent characteristics that warrant a premium rather than a discount. However, at ~16.6x with declining core volumes, the stock is reasonably priced for a business transitioning from commodity-exposed to defense-diversified. The significant appreciation from the March 2026 trough already reflects much of the re-rating potential from Q1 results and AMPAC expansion visibility.

**What specifically prevents a BUY recommendation:** The petroleum additives volume decline worsened from -4.9% (full-year 2025) to -6.9% (Q1 2026). While management disclosed positive shipment trends at the end of Q1 2026, the overall guidance language — citing "market softness and the uncertain macroeconomic environment" — suggests that sustained recovery has not yet been established. At $738, the stock prices in maintenance of current margins and successful AMPAC ramp. A BUY requires either: (a) confirmation that end-of-quarter positive trends translate into sequential improvement (next data point: Q2 earnings in late July), or (b) a pullback to $650-680 where the margin of safety compensates for continued volume pressure.

## Competitive Landscape

**Petroleum Additives:** The industry operates as a stable oligopoly — Lubrizol (Berkshire Hathaway), Infineum (ExxonMobil/Shell JV), Chevron Oronite, and Afton Chemical. Barriers to entry are formidable: 1,400+ patents, multi-year OEM qualification processes, and R&D investment of $132M annually (5.2% of segment revenue). Customer switching costs are high because reformulation requires extensive testing. NEU's market position appears stable: while volumes are declining, margins have held, suggesting pricing discipline across the industry rather than share loss.

**Specialty Materials:** AMPAC's competitive position in domestic defense and space applications is highly advantaged given that foreign producers are believed not qualified for most U.S. programs. Calca operates in a market with limited qualified producers. These are high-barrier businesses — qualification of new suppliers can require extended testing, customer audits, and program approvals, creating significant competitive insulation though not absolute monopoly protection. The capacity expansion signals that demand exceeds current supply, which typically enables pricing power.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Petroleum Additives Volume Deterioration (PRIMARY NEAR-TERM RISK):**
If the decline reflects structural demand destruction rather than temporary market softness, margins will eventually compress as fixed cost absorption deteriorates. The -10.7% North America decline is particularly concerning.

**2. EV Transition (LONG-TERM, PARTIALLY PRICED):**
The lubricant additive market faces secular headwinds from EVs (fewer fluid change intervals, simpler drivetrains). However, global ICE vehicle fleet continues growing, and NEU is investing in EV-specific fluids. Timeline: 10-20 year structural shift, not an immediate threat.

**3. Specialty Materials Concentration:**
~$900M of acquisitions creating $808M in intangibles/goodwill attributed to the specialty materials segment. If defense spending cycles down or programs shift away from solid rocket motors, goodwill impairment becomes possible. Single-source production facilities create operational risk.

**4. Tariff Exposure:**
61% of revenue generated outside the U.S. with global manufacturing footprint. Tariff escalation could disrupt supply chains and compress customer demand in key markets.

**5. Government Contract Dependency:**
AMPAC and Calca are heavily dependent on U.S. government defense spending subject to congressional appropriations. Government shutdowns could impact quarterly results, and contracts can be terminated for convenience.

**6. Interest Rate Exposure:**
While approximately $546M of NEU's debt is fixed-rate (2.70% and 3.78% senior notes), the $394M revolver borrowings carry floating-rate exposure. Q1 2026 interest expense actually fell year over year due to lower average debt and a lower average rate, but future rate increases would affect the floating-rate portion.

## Conclusion

NewMarket Corporation is a well-managed, financially disciplined business with competitive advantages in both its core petroleum additives oligopoly and its emerging defense/space specialty materials segment. The Gottwald family's owner-operator culture, modest compensation, and aggressive capital return program create alignment rarely seen in public markets. At ~16.6x trailing earnings with 7.2% FCF yield and 23.5% ROE, the stock is reasonably valued but not compellingly cheap after its recovery from March lows.

The specific factor preventing a BUY is the worsening petroleum additives volume decline without confirmed sustained recovery. Management disclosed positive shipment trends at the end of Q1, which represents an encouraging early signal, but does not yet constitute evidence of stabilization given the overall macro uncertainty acknowledged in guidance. The AMPAC capacity expansion (completing late 2026) provides a concrete catalyst for growth acceleration, but this is already partially priced into the recovery to $738. The stock warrants holding for its compounding characteristics and monitoring for either a price pullback or fundamental improvement that would justify upgrading to BUY.
