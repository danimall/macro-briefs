**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RPC is a mid-tier U.S. oilfield services company whose headline financials and underlying reality have diverged sharply. Reported revenue rose 15% in 2025 to $1,627M and another 36.6% year-over-year in Q1 2026 to $454.8M, but virtually all of that growth came from the April 2025 acquisition of Pintail (the largest Permian wireline perforating provider). The legacy organic business, led by pressure pumping, is shrinking, and profitability has collapsed: net margin fell from 13.6% in 2022 to 2.0% in 2025 and 0.2% in Q1 2026, with net income of just $0.9M last quarter. The cause is structural: U.S. completion-services oversupply concentrated in pressure pumping, where E&P efficiency gains keep adding effective capacity and pricing power has shifted to operators.

Against that, two facts keep this from being an active negative call. First, the balance sheet is strong: no net debt, $201M cash, Altman Z 4.44, current ratio 3.24, equity at 75% of assets, only a $50M Pintail seller note outstanding. No insolvency or distress case applies here; RPC can outlast a prolonged downturn, fund the dividend, and act counter-cyclically. Second, the bearish structural view is already substantially in the price. The consensus mean target ($6.54) sits ~8% below the $7.27 quote, the stock has rallied ~50% over the trailing year, and Q1 2026 showed the first signs of a sequential trough forming (revenue +7% Q/Q, downhole tools +11% Q/Q, pressure pumping +20% Q/Q). On next-year consensus EPS of $0.27, RES trades ~27x in a trough-earnings year against an OFS peer median P/E of 37.3x, which does not support a conviction short.

I rate RES a HOLD. The situation is balanced and two-sided: collapsing GAAP margins and structural pressure-pumping headwinds, offset by a debt-free balance sheet, a differentiated and organically-growing downhole-tools franchise, a possible sequential trough, and a valuation where the bear case is already consensus. The classification is uncertain; I do not yet have the evidence for a confident directional call in either direction, and the path depends on whether the early sequential firming converts to organic EBITDA-margin expansion over the next two quarters.

## Company Overview

RPC, incorporated in 1984 and based in Atlanta, is a diversified U.S. oilfield services holding company operating through Cudd Energy Services, Cudd Pressure Control, Thru Tubing Solutions (TTS), Pintail Completions, and Patterson Services. It employed 2,893 people at year-end 2025. The business runs in two segments:

- **Technical Services (~94–95% of revenue):** equipment- and personnel-intensive completion/production services (pressure pumping, downhole tools, wireline, coiled tubing, cementing, snubbing, nitrogen, well control) concentrated in the Permian Basin and mid-continent.
- **Support Services (~5–6% of revenue):** off-site rental tools, pipe inspection/handling/storage, and well-control training.

FY2025 revenue mix by service line: Pressure Pumping 29.8% (declining), Downhole Tools 24.2%, Wireline 19.4%, Coiled Tubing 9.3%, Cementing 6.4%, Rental Tools 4.1%. RPC competes against the majors (Halliburton, SLB, Baker Hughes) and closer peers (Liberty, ProPetro, ProFrac, Patterson-UTI, NCS, Nine Energy). Its differentiation sits in TTS proprietary downhole tools (the patented Metal Max metal-on-metal power section and on-plug stage isolation), a no-net-debt balance sheet, and an "idle-don't-burn" asset-management discipline. RPC does not offer electric frac fleets and does not intend to grow its fleet count (10 horizontal fleets, six of which are Tier 4).

## Financial Analysis

The defining story is a multi-year earnings deterioration masked by acquisition-driven revenue growth.

Net income fell ~85% from peak even as 2025 revenue rose. The revenue increase was entirely Pintail ($295.8M contribution); organic pressure pumping declined from $772M (2023) to $485M (2025). Q1 2026 confirmed the same pattern: revenue up on Pintail, but net income just $0.9M (0.2% margin), operating income $2.6M (down from $12.4M), Adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to 11.8% (from 14.7% y/y), and free cash flow of negative $0.9M as capex now consumes nearly all operating cash flow (2026 capex guided $160–180M).

GAAP optics are further distorted by Pintail "acquisition-related employment costs" ($20.3M in 2025, $7.3M in Q1 2026), which are non-cash, permanently nondeductible, and tied to seller retention. They drive the effective tax rates (43.3% in 2025, 80.1% in Q1 2026) on low pretax income and back out of Adjusted EBITDA. The cleaner read is the adjusted margin (11.8%), and even that is compressing.

The balance sheet is the offsetting strength and the reason no distress case applies: Altman Z 4.44 (safe zone), current ratio 3.24, D/E 0.34, equity 75% of assets, ~$201M cash (down from $326M pre-Pintail), no revolver borrowings, only the $50M seller note (first $20M principal due May 2026). The $0.04/quarter dividend (~$35M/yr) is maintained but explicitly not targeted to grow. No open-market buybacks occurred in 2025 or Q1 2026 despite 12.8M shares of authorization remaining and a depressed stock.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated, and conflating the two halves is the most common analytical error on this name.

The legacy frac-centric business is in secular decline. Pressure pumping revenue fell roughly a third from 2023 to 2025, and the structural cause—completion-services oversupply, efficiency gains transferring savings to E&Ps, capital-disciplined operators—is not a temporary blip. RPC's two stacked Tier 2 diesel fleets are uneconomic to reactivate at current prices, and management has no plans to do so.

The genuine growth engine is Thru Tubing Solutions. Downhole tools is the one line growing on its own merits, up 11% sequentially in Q1 2026 with broad double-digit regional growth. The Metal Max power section is at ~15% of power-section utilization with meaningful displacement runway, and the on-plug stage-isolation technology is becoming operators' primary method. This is the highest-margin, most differentiated, most defensible part of RPC, and the constructive thesis rests more on TTS than on the frac business.

The audit correctly flags that I should engage with the sequential trajectory rather than asserting unbroken deterioration. Q1 2026 over Q4 2025 was positive across the two largest lines (pressure pumping growth was job-mix and pass-through driven), and management was "cautiously optimistic" on "less calendar white space." This is the early shape of a trough forming. It is not yet confirmation: estimate revisions are still negative across every horizon (current-year EPS revised -5.7% over 90 days, next-year -18.3%), pricing firming is "not broad-based," and incremental inquiries skew toward private rather than public operators. The trough has not been validated by margin expansion or a turn in revisions.

On cycle-adjusted figures the picture is more balanced. ## Competitive Landscape

RPC's competitive position is mixed and on net slightly deteriorating, which is why the score sits below midpoint. The U.S. completion market is in structural oversupply, E&P consolidation is creating fewer and more powerful buyers, and operators increasingly demand dual-fuel and electric fleets that RPC does not offer. In its largest line, pricing power has shifted to customers, and equipment migrating in from weak gassy basins adds Permian competition. Customer concentration is meaningful: one private E&P was ~15% of 2025 revenue.

TTS downhole tools is a genuine technology leader gaining share, and the Pintail acquisition made RPC the largest Permian wireline perforating provider, diversifying revenue away from commoditized pressure pumping. The strategic direction toward less-commoditized, differentiated, technology-driven lines is correct. The trajectory is positive in the crown-jewel segment and negative in the legacy frac core, netting to a stable-to-slightly-negative composite.

## Risk Assessment

- **Pressure-pumping pricing deflation (primary, structural):** oversupply plus efficiency gains cap demand and transfer savings to E&Ps. This is a margin headwind, not a cyclical dip.
- **Customer concentration:** ~15% of revenue from one private E&P; loss would be disproportionate.
- **Commodity/macro sensitivity:** oil averaged $65.58/bbl in 2025 (down from $76.60), operators are cautious, and Permian gas takeaway constraints in New Mexico could cap activity.
- **Iran/Hormuz (new Q1 2026 risk factor):** double-edged, capable of spiking prices favorably or impairing customer capital access. I treat any oil-price tailwind as unverified rather than a base-case input.
- **Technology lag:** no electric frac fleets; stacked diesel fleets uneconomic to reactivate.
- **ERP implementation and Pintail integration:** multi-year systems transformation with internal-control risk language; Pintail measurement period only closed April 2026.
- **Governance:** Rollins family controls >50% of voting power; ~61% insider/affiliate ownership; controlled-company exemptions mean no required independent board majority. Minority holders bear entrenchment risk, and there is a web of Marine Products related-party arrangements (Marine Products signed a merger with MasterCraft in February 2026).

Most of these are priced. The structural pressure-pumping risk is the single most consequential, but it is also the most widely understood and is reflected in consensus targets below spot. The idiosyncratic risk not yet fully priced is the governance-driven minority-holder discount plus integration execution. Net, risk scores at midpoint: material but largely discounted, with a balance sheet that removes the tail.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-mildly-bullish, which supports the decision not to press the bearish case. ATM IV eased from 53.5% to 50.6% over the recent window while the price rose 2.1%, and the put/call open-interest ratio is low at 0.32 (rising modestly from 0.26). There is no elevated put skew or rich-IV signal flagging an imminent downside catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$8.00–$9.00).** TTS continues to gain share with Metal Max and on-plug technology, the sequential firming in Q1 2026 extends into organic EBITDA-margin expansion over the next two quarters, oil prices firm enough to bring public-operator inquiries off the sidelines, and the debt-free balance sheet funds opportunistic counter-cyclical asset purchases. On a normalized $0.30+ EPS at a 28–30x trough multiple, the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear case (~$5.00–$5.50).** Pressure-pumping pricing deflation persists, organic legacy revenue keeps shrinking, estimate revisions stay negative, pass-through-flattered pressure-pumping growth reverses, and the New Mexico gas takeaway constraint caps Permian activity. The rally unwinds toward the consensus mean/median ($6.54/$6.20) and below, to the analyst low of $5.00.

The two cases are roughly symmetric around the current price, which defines this as a HOLD rather than a directional call. The balance sheet caps the downside well above any distress scenario, and the bear case is already substantially in consensus; the upside requires confirmation the trough has turned, which the data has not yet delivered.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected early August 2026) is the natural checkpoint. Two consecutive quarters of organic EBITDA-margin behavior is the decisive read on whether the trough converts to recovery.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of organic Adjusted EBITDA-margin expansion (i.e., ex-Pintail, ex-pass-through), confirming the sequential firming is durable rather than job-mix noise.
2. Estimate revisions turning positive on current-year and next-year EPS.
3. Evidence of broad-based pressure-pumping pricing recovery and/or public-operator demand returning, with TTS sustaining double-digit organic growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Loss or material reduction of the ~15%-of-revenue concentrated customer, or a further leg down in pressure-pumping pricing that pushes organic Adjusted EBITDA margin below ~10%.
2. A dividend cut or balance-sheet deterioration (cash drawdown materially below ~$150M without a clear counter-cyclical use), which would remove the downside floor that anchors this HOLD.
3. A confirmed and sustained Hormuz reopening with oil falling toward the low-$60s, compressing already-thin OFS margins further.

## Conclusion

RES is a balanced, two-sided situation. The structural negatives are real: pressure-pumping oversupply has collapsed net margin, organic legacy revenue is shrinking, estimates are still falling, and governance is entrenched. The offsets are equally real: a debt-free balance sheet that defeats any insolvency case, a differentiated and organically-growing TTS franchise, a sequential trough that may be forming, and a valuation where the bear case is already in consensus targets below spot. On next-year EPS the stock trades at a discount to the peer median in a trough year, the options market is benign, and analysts price downside to roughly the mean target.

That combination cannot support a conviction short, and it does not yet support a conviction long. The call is HOLD with an uncertain rationale: I need to see organic margin expansion and a turn in revisions before upgrading, and customer loss or balance-sheet erosion before downgrading. Q2 2026 earnings should resolve which way the trough breaks.
