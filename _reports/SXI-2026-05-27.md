## Executive Summary

Standex International has undergone a meaningful portfolio transformation over the past two years, divesting commodity businesses (Federal Industries) and acquiring into structural growth markets (Amran/Narayan for electrical grid infrastructure, McStarlite for aerospace). The result is a company with ~70% of revenue in Electronics and Aerospace & Defense, a $338M backlog (+25% YoY), and 20% grid sales growth with a 1.1x book-to-bill ratio. The operational execution is credible: gross margins expanded 120bp to 40.9%, the Electronics segment produces 26.4% operating margins, and the company is deleveraging rapidly (2.18x from ~3x post-acquisition).

The challenge is that the stock price has already incorporated much of this transformation. At $272.53, SXI trades at approximately 33x trailing EPS and ~31x FY2027 consensus estimates of $9.96, a premium to the peer median P/E of 29.6x. Estimate revisions are trending negative across all timeframes: current-year estimates have fallen 2.3% over 90 days, and next-year estimates have declined 4.0%. The most recent quarter (Q3 FY2026) delivered a slight miss on both revenue and EPS, and Zacks classified SXI as "Bear of the Day" in May. With 7.5% short interest and a flat options term structure suggesting persistent uncertainty, the risk/reward at current levels favors patience over aggression.

The path to upside requires the Croatian facility to validate the European grid thesis, continued 20%+ grid sales growth, and defense program wins materializing in FY2027. These are plausible but not yet confirmed by earnings revisions.

## Company Overview

Standex International is a $3.3B market cap diversified industrial manufacturer operating in four segments after a recent reorganization:

- **Electronics** (~53% of revenue): Reed switches, relays, sensors, magnetics, and instrument transformers for electrical grid, defense, space, and EV applications. Flagship segment with 26.4% operating margins.
- **Aerospace & Defense** (~16%): Net/near-net formed engineered components for aviation, space, defense, and energy markets.
- **Scientific** (~8%): Specialty temperature-controlled equipment for medical, pharmaceutical, and biotech markets.
- **Engraving & Hydraulics** (~20%): Mold texturing, engraving, and hydraulic cylinders for automotive and industrial applications. This is the portfolio "tail" likely earmarked for eventual divestiture.

The company's strategy centers on "customer intimacy" — delivering custom, engineered solutions that create switching costs. No single customer represents more than 5% of revenue, and geographic diversification spans the US (~59%), Europe, and Asia.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue reached $790M (+9.6% YoY), accelerating from a -2.7% decline in FY2024. The annualized Q3 FY2026 run rate is ~$898M. Growth is split between acquisition contribution (Amran/Narayan adding ~$44.5M in 9 months) and organic growth of 4.6-6.5%.

**Margins:** Gross margin expanded to 40.9% in Q3 FY2026 from 39.7% in the year-ago quarter, reflecting pricing discipline and mix improvement toward higher-margin Electronics. Electronics segment margins reached 26.4% (up from 22.9% YoY). Consolidated adjusted operating margins approximate 21.4% at the segment level.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt of $369M ($473M total debt less $104M cash) against approximately $170M in adjusted EBITDA yields leverage of 2.18x, well within the 3.5x covenant limit and declining. The company repaid $80M in net debt over 9 months. Current ratio of 3.05x is conservative. The credit facility ($825M) matures February 2028 with $188M remaining capacity.

**Cash Flow:** Nine-month operating cash flow of $49.4M supports $19.7M in capex and $12M in dividends. Free cash flow conversion appears modest relative to reported earnings, though stripping out the $56.8M non-cash divestiture gain normalizes the relationship. Capex guidance of $31-34M for FY2026 reflects ongoing investment in the Croatian facility and capacity expansion.

**Interest Expense:** Running at $32M annualized (vs. ~$20M pre-acquisition). At a 5.80% effective rate, the $473M in outstanding debt is partially hedged by $225M in interest rate swaps. Continued deleveraging should reduce this burden through FY2027.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. Electrical Grid Modernization (Primary):** Grid sales grew 20% YoY with a 1.1x book-to-bill. The Amran/Narayan acquisition positions SXI as a leading instrument transformer supplier. The European greenfield in Croatia targets $60M+ in revenue within 3-5 years and is expected to achieve ISO certification by June 2026. Management identified ~$270M in "fast-growth markets" they are targeting. Grid demand is underpinned by data center buildout, electrification, and renewable energy integration — multi-year secular drivers.

**2. Aerospace & Defense:** The renamed segment targets space commercialization, hypersonics, missile systems, and commercial aviation. Management expects significant defense growth in FY2027 as procurement programs advance. The McStarlite acquisition ($57M, Feb 2025) adds cold deep draw and bulge-formed aviation capabilities.

**3. Portfolio Optimization:** Divestiture of Federal Industries ($68.3M) and potential future divestiture of Engraving & Hydraulics would further concentrate the portfolio toward higher-growth, higher-margin businesses. Restructuring charges of $9.4M in Engraving & Hydraulics (9-month FY2026) suggest active cost reduction ahead of potential separation.

**Total backlog** of $338M (+25% YoY) with $305M schedulable within 12 months provides strong near-term revenue visibility.

**Counterpoint:** Consensus estimates are falling. The Scientific segment faces NIH funding headwinds. Organic growth outside of grid applications (which benefit from Amran/Narayan contribution) is more modest at 4.6%.

## Valuation Assessment

At $272.53, SXI trades at:
- **Trailing P/E:** ~33x (per Phase 1 data trailing P/E of 32.8x)
- **Forward P/E (FY2026E):** ~31.5x on $8.66 consensus
- **Forward P/E (FY2027E):** ~27.4x on $9.96 consensus
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~21.6x on ~$170M adjusted EBITDA

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 29.6x (driven by Kadant at 36.4x and Franklin Electric at 29.6x). SXI at 31.5x current-year is modestly above the median. SXI's growth rate (9.6% revenue, 20%+ grid sales) arguably warrants a premium, but falling estimates undermine the case for paying above-peer multiples.

**Analyst targets:** Mean of $290.80, median $290, low $260, high $323. At $272.53, the stock sits 6.5% below the mean target — limited upside implied by consensus. The low target of $260 is only 4.6% below current price, suggesting analysts see limited downside risk but also modest upside.

The stock has appreciated ~80% over the past year (from ~$150 to ~$270), largely re-rating on the portfolio transformation. With estimates now declining rather than rising, the multiple expansion phase appears exhausted. Fair value likely resides in the $260-290 range absent positive estimate revisions.

## Competitive Landscape

Standex occupies defensible niche positions across its segments:

- **Electronics:** Leading positions in reed switch technology and now instrument transformers (via Amran/Narayan). The grid infrastructure market is undersupplied relative to demand growth, creating favorable pricing dynamics. Key competitors include ABB, Siemens, and Schneider in broader grid equipment, but Standex targets smaller, custom applications where its model creates switching costs.

- **Aerospace & Defense:** Specialized forming capabilities (net/near-net forming, cold deep draw) serve applications where part qualification processes create multi-year customer lock-in. Program lifecycles of 10-20 years provide recurring revenue once qualified.

- **Diversification:** No customer >5% of revenue eliminates concentration risk. The custom nature of products (vs. catalog/commodity) supports pricing power.

**Trajectory:** The competitive position is improving. Grid market entry through Amran/Narayan is a structural upgrade. The European expansion via Croatia (if successful) would create a second geographic platform in a market where European incumbents face capacity constraints. The shift toward the portfolio "core" (Electronics + A&D) improves the average competitive intensity of the business mix.

## Risk Assessment

**Acquisition Integration (HIGH):** $298M in goodwill from Amran/Narayan, concentrated in Electronics. The redeemable NCI put option (9.9% stake at 12x trailing EBITDA) creates an escalating liability if Narayan grows as planned — already marked up from $26.7M to $44.2M in 9 months. This is an asymmetric outcome: strong performance increases the buyout cost while weak performance triggers impairment risk.

**Falling Estimates (MODERATE):** EPS revisions declining 2-8% across periods. If the grid growth thesis falters or tariff impacts materialize, the premium multiple would compress toward the peer median (25-30x), implying 10-15% downside.

**Tariff Exposure (MODERATE):** With operations and sourcing in India, China, Singapore, Japan, and 59% of revenue from the US, the company acknowledges tariff uncertainty. The Supreme Court's IEEPA tariff decisions remain a live risk.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity (LOW-MODERATE):** $473M in debt at 5.80% effective rate, partially hedged. In a rising-rate environment, unhedged portions face cost increases. The February 2028 facility maturity creates a refinancing event within 2 years.

**Scientific Segment NIH Risk (LOW):** Small segment (~8% of revenue) but directly exposed to government funding decisions. NIH cuts are already impacting organic revenue.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($310-323):** Grid sales continue 20%+ growth through FY2027. Croatian facility achieves ISO certification and begins contributing revenue in H2 FY2027. Defense programs materialize as expected. Engraving & Hydraulics divested for $100-150M, further simplifying the portfolio and funding buybacks/debt reduction. FY2027 EPS reaches $10.50-11.00 on execution. Stock re-rates to 30-32x = $315-352.

**Bear Case ($220-240):** Grid growth decelerates to single digits as backlog converts but new orders slow. Tariff impacts compress margins by 100-200bp. Croatian facility faces execution delays and cost overruns. Estimate revisions continue declining to $8.00-8.50 FY2027. Multiple compresses to 27-28x peer median on disappointing execution = $216-238.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS estimate revisions (30-day and 90-day both positive)
2. Stock pullback to $240-250 range (where forward P/E on current estimates would be ~28x, closer to peer median)
3. Croatian facility achieving ISO certification AND booking initial orders, confirming European thesis

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Electronics segment organic revenue growth declining below 5% for two consecutive quarters
2. Book-to-bill falling below 1.0x in Electronics
3. Goodwill impairment charge or NCI put option exercised at materially above current mark

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (expected late July/early August 2026 for Q4 FY2026). This will provide full-year FY2026 results, FY2027 guidance, and an update on Croatian facility progress.

## Conclusion

Standex International is a well-managed industrial company executing a credible portfolio transformation toward structural growth markets (electrical grid, aerospace, defense). The operational metrics confirm execution quality. The 5-year TSR of 285% vs. peers at 240% demonstrates a management team that delivers.

At $272.53 (~33x trailing, 31.5x forward), the stock fully prices the transformation. The CEO sold $3.8M in stock at $256 in February. Analyst targets imply ~7% upside to the mean. For a cyclical industrial with significant acquisition integration risk and tariff exposure, paying above-peer multiples while estimates decline requires a specific catalyst, and none is imminent within the next 60 days.

The stock is a HOLD for existing holders with a positive long-term thesis (grid modernization tailwind, defense program wins in FY2027, Croatian facility ramping). New capital should wait for either a valuation pullback toward $240-250 or a resumption of positive estimate revisions.
