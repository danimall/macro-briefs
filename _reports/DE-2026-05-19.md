## Executive Summary

Deere & Company presents a complex investment case at $564.49: a world-class industrial franchise navigating the trough of an agricultural equipment cycle, with genuine revenue recovery underway but margin normalization blocked by tariff headwinds and regulatory uncertainty. Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 13% YoY, Construction & Forestry surged 34%, and management explicitly identifies 2026 as the cycle bottom. However, Production & Precision Agriculture margins collapsed to 4.4% (vs. 11% prior year and 15-20% in upcycles), with $361M in tariff costs consuming margin recovery in a single quarter.

The stock has appreciated 30% from its October 2025 low of $433 to $564 today, pricing in partial recovery expectations. At 31.5x forward FY2026 EPS of $17.90, the market assumes meaningful normalization over the next 12-18 months. That assumption faces three quantifiable risks: tariff persistence (annualized ~$1.4B pretax impact), FTC right-to-repair litigation that could structurally impair aftermarket economics, and an agricultural commodity environment where farmer fundamentals remain challenged. The options market is pricing extreme uncertainty (71% near-term IV vs. 28% realized), suggesting a binary event or significant repositioning ahead.

## Company Overview

Deere operates four segments: Production & Precision Agriculture (PPA, large-scale crop equipment), Small Agriculture & Turf (SAT), Construction & Forestry (CF), and Financial Services (~$48.8B receivables portfolio). Revenue peaked at $61.3B in FY2023, declined to $45.7B in FY2025 (-25%), and showed the first recovery signal in Q1 FY2026 at $9.6B (+13% YoY). The company employs a dealer-exclusive distribution model, integrates proprietary precision technology (autonomous tractors, See & Spray AI), and maintains investment-grade ratings (A+/A1/A) despite carrying $56B in consolidated debt (predominantly Financial Services funding).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2023 $61.3B → FY2024 $51.7B → FY2025 $45.7B → Q1 FY2026 annualized run-rate ~$38.4B (seasonally weak quarter). Equipment revenue in Q1 was $8.0B (+18% YoY), with SAT +24% and CF +34% driving recovery while PPA remained flat.

**Earnings power:** FY2025 EPS of $18.50 represents trough earnings. Consensus expects $17.90 for FY2026 (slight decline reflecting tariff drag in H1) before recovering to $23.02 in FY2027 (+29%). Q1 FY2026 EPS of $2.42 beat estimates by 14.7%, but included a $1.34/share tariff headwind that masked underlying improvement.

**Free cash flow:** $6.1B in FY2025 despite the revenue downcycle, translating to a 4.0% FCF yield on the current $152B market cap. This FCF generation through the trough demonstrates the structural quality of the business model.

**Balance sheet nuance:** The headline $56B debt figure is misleading. Equipment Operations carry only $9.3B debt against $19.3B adjusted equity (0.48x D/E). Financial Services debt funds a matched receivables portfolio with 1.65% non-performing loans and declining provision charges. Credit ratings confirm appropriate leverage for a captive finance model.

**Capital allocation:** $302M in buybacks during Q1 at ~$485 average price, with $7.6B remaining authorization. Dividend of $6.48 annualized (1.1% yield). $20B domestic manufacturing investment commitment over the next decade.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture bifurcates between near-term cyclical recovery and medium-term structural questions:

**Cyclical recovery (12-18 months):** Construction & Forestry growing 34% on infrastructure spending and data center construction. SAT growing 24% on dairy/livestock strength. PPA remains the laggard — North American large ag demand is "subdued" and South American demand is declining. Consensus estimates imply FY2027 EPS of $23.02 vs. FY2026's $17.90, requiring 29% earnings growth driven by volume normalization and margin recovery.

**Structural growth thesis:** Precision agriculture technology (autonomous equipment, AI-driven applications, SaaS solutions) offers recurring revenue and margin expansion over a multi-year horizon. However, SaaS revenue is not yet material, and the FTC right-to-repair litigation directly threatens the "walled garden" technology model that would enable subscription economics.

**Estimate revisions:** Current-year estimates revised up 7.4% over 90 days (from $16.66 to $17.90), next-year up 4.2%. These are positive but modest — not the magnitude (>30%) that signals a strong inflection. The revision velocity (+0.1-0.3% over 30 days) is essentially flat, suggesting consensus has stabilized rather than accelerating.

**Headwinds to recovery:** Fertilizer costs rising (confirmed across multiple supply channels), diesel >$5.29, and interest rates at 7.5% for farm equipment loans. These factors directly compress farmer purchasing power even if commodity prices stabilize.

## Valuation Assessment

At $564.49, Deere trades at:
- 31.5x FY2026E EPS ($17.90)
- 24.5x FY2027E EPS ($23.02)
- 30.5x trailing EPS ($18.50)
- ~4.0% FCF yield (on FY2025 FCF of $6.1B)

The market is pricing significant recovery — 24.5x on FY2027 earnings that require 29% growth to achieve. This is above Deere's historical mid-cycle multiple of 16-20x, reflecting the technology premium the market assigns to precision agriculture. Whether that premium is earned depends on right-to-repair resolution timing.

Analyst consensus target is $665 (median $660), implying 18% upside. The range is $500-$793, reflecting substantial disagreement about cycle timing and tariff resolution. At 31.5x current earnings, the stock is not cheap relative to industrial peers or its own history unless FY2027+ earnings materialize as projected.

The tariff dynamic creates a specific valuation overhang: $361M per quarter annualizes to ~$1.4B pretax, or roughly $4/share after tax. If tariffs normalize (Supreme Court IEEPA ruling provides a pathway, and potential recovery of prior payments), FY2026 EPS would be closer to $22 rather than $18 — making the stock ~26x normalized earnings. But this is a conditional valuation, dependent on policy resolution with uncertain timing.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths (maintained):** Unmatched global dealer network, leading precision ag technology platform with highest farmer adoption rates, integrated financial services that drive pull-through equipment sales, and brand loyalty built over 185 years.

**Threats (intensifying):** The FTC right-to-repair lawsuit, with motion to dismiss already denied, directly challenges Deere's "walled garden" aftermarket model. If forced to fully open diagnostic tools, the switching cost moat narrows. Competitor AGCO has voluntarily embraced open repair, positioning it favorably with regulators. CNH Industrial is investing aggressively in precision technology to close the gap.

**Construction positioning:** Deere is gaining share against Caterpillar in compact/mid-size equipment. The Tenna acquisition ($440M) and worker recalls signal conviction in this segment's trajectory. Infrastructure Act spending provides a multi-year demand floor.

The competitive position score reflects industry-leading market share and technology differentiation, partially offset by regulatory risk to the aftermarket profit model and intensifying competition in precision agriculture.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Persistence (HIGH PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT):** If tariffs persist through FY2026, they consume roughly $4/share in earnings power annually. The Supreme Court IEEPA ruling provides potential relief, but timing is uncertain. Given current trade policy uncertainty, industrial tariff relief is not assured.

**2. FTC Right-to-Repair (MEDIUM PROBABILITY, HIGH STRUCTURAL IMPACT):** Motion to dismiss denied. Settlement discussions underway. Worst case: forced opening of diagnostic ecosystem undermines aftermarket margins (parts/service are among highest-margin revenue streams) and weakens the precision agriculture subscription model. Best case: modest concessions preserving the core technology platform. Binary outcome with multi-billion dollar long-term revenue implications.

**3. Agricultural Downcycle Extension:** While management calls 2026 the bottom, farmer economics (elevated input costs + lower commodity prices + high interest rates) don't clearly support a strong recovery in FY2027 large ag demand. If PPA volumes don't recover, the FY2027 $23 EPS target is unreachable.

**4. Rising Input Costs:** Steel, aluminum, and energy costs are structurally higher in the current inflationary environment. Even without tariffs, PPA margin recovery from 4.4% toward the historical 15-20% requires both volume leverage and cost containment simultaneously.

**5. Financial Services Credit Quality:** Non-performing receivables at 1.65% and modified loans doubling (0.06% → 0.13%) in a rising rate environment with stressed farmer economics. While provision charges are declining, the underlying credit trajectory warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing extreme near-term uncertainty: 71.1% ATM IV on 2-day expiration vs. 28.4% realized historical volatility — a 42.6pp spread at the 89th percentile of the IV range. Term structure is in steep backwardation (71.1% → 44.1% → 35.7% → 31.9%), indicating the market expects a significant move within days. Put/call skew is elevated at 6.6%, confirming directional demand for downside protection. This likely reflects positioning around an upcoming earnings report or pending tariff/legal catalyst. The OI put/call ratio of 1.06 suggests modest but persistent downside hedging rather than capitulatory fear.

Sophisticated market participants are paying substantially above fair value for near-term protection. This confirms elevated event risk that our fundamental analysis cannot fully identify. Waiting for resolution of the event driving this IV is prudent before initiating new positions.

## Conclusion

Deere is a high-quality industrial franchise at a mediocre price. The 31.5x forward multiple prices in recovery that faces identifiable headwinds (tariffs, ag fundamentals, regulatory risk). The score trajectory decline from 6.5 to 5.8 over recent months reflects the progressive recognition that cyclical recovery — while real — faces more friction than initially anticipated. The options market's extreme near-term IV confirms uncertainty about the path forward, and the 6.6% put skew suggests informed participants are hedging downside.

The fundamental business remains excellent: $6B+ FCF through the trough, investment-grade balance sheet, dominant market position, and a technology platform that should drive multi-year value creation. But business excellence does not guarantee attractive entry points. At $564, you're paying 24.5x FY2027 earnings that require 29% growth — growth that depends on tariff resolution, agricultural cycle turning, and FTC resolution all breaking favorably. HOLD is appropriate: the business merits continued ownership for existing holders, but new capital should wait for either price compression or catalyst resolution.
