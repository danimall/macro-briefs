## Executive Summary

Black Hills Corporation is a 142-year-old multi-utility serving approximately 227,000 electric and 1,138,000 gas customers across eight states. The company faces two potentially transformative events: a pending all-stock merger with NorthWestern Energy (expected H2 2026 close, shareholder and HSR approvals already obtained) and an unprecedented data center demand surge in Wyoming, where peak demand grew 25% year-over-year and a prospective 1.8 GW customer has already deposited $201 million for generation equipment procurement.

At $73.92, the stock trades at 18.6x trailing and forward earnings ($3.98 actual / ~$4.35 FY2026E), a modest discount to the peer median P/E of 20.4x. The 3.8% dividend yield is slightly above the peer median of 3.6%. Analysts have a mean price target of $83 with a range of $76–$90, implying 3–22% upside. The investment case is complicated by persistently negative free cash flow (~-$146M in FY2025), a 16.5% short interest (elevated for a utility), three earnings misses in four quarters, and meaningful merger execution risk.

The risk/reward at current levels is balanced. The data center growth opportunity is genuinely differentiated and could justify significant re-rating if execution materializes. The merger, if completed, creates a larger, more diversified platform with enhanced capital access. But the stock has already appreciated 20% over the past year, embedding some of this optionality, and near-term earnings visibility is poor given weather sensitivity and merger costs.

## Company Overview

Black Hills operates two segments: **Electric Utilities** (serving ~227,000 customers in CO, SD, MT, WY with 1,386 MW of owned generation) and **Gas Utilities** (serving ~1,138,000 customers across AR, CO, IA, KS, NE, WY with ~49,400 miles of pipe). The company owns a coal mine in Wyoming's Powder River Basin with 51 years of reserves.

The pending NorthWestern Energy merger (announced August 18, 2025) would create a combined entity with ~700,000 electric and ~1,500,000 gas customers. BKH shareholders would own approximately 56% of the combined company. Shareholder approval was received April 2, 2026; HSR completed April 20, 2026. FERC and state regulatory approvals remain pending; settlement agreements have been reached with intervenors in Montana, Nebraska, and South Dakota.

Separately, Wyoming Electric has become an unexpected growth engine as hyperscaler data center demand materializes. Microsoft and Meta are expanding operations near Cheyenne, and a 1.8 GW prospective customer has provided $201M in refundable deposits for generation equipment. Wyoming Electric's peak demand surged from 314 MW (2024) to 393 MW (Q1 2026).

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term (FY2026E–FY2027E):** Consensus estimates $4.35 (FY2026) and $4.62 (FY2027), implying 9.3% and 6.2% growth respectively. Estimate revisions are flat (current year: +0.2% over 90 days; next year: +0.5%).

**Data Center Catalyst:** The Wyoming data center opportunity is the differentiator. The 5-year plan includes 600 MW of load by 2030, with the 1.8 GW prospect representing additional upside beyond current projections. At $201M already deposited, this is a committed pre-payment for equipment procurement, beyond preliminary discussion. If fully realized, this single project could add billions to rate base and drive EPS accretion well beyond current consensus.

However, the timeline is uncertain (likely 3–5+ years for full buildout), execution is complex (procurement, permitting, generation construction), and there's customer concentration risk (dependency on 1–2 hyperscalers for transformational load growth).

**NWE Merger Growth:** The merger adds rate base, diversifies regulatory jurisdiction, and provides scale benefits (shared overhead, lower cost of capital). Management hasn't provided combined company EPS guidance yet. The deal is accretive only to the extent that synergies materialize and equity dilution from the 0.98 exchange ratio is offset by growth.

**Long-term EPS growth guidance:** 4–6%, targeting upper half (5–6%). With data center upside, this could trend higher. Without it, BKH is a mid-single-digit grower like most peers.

## Competitive Landscape

**1. Monopoly utility positioning (stable):** Regulated service territories in predominantly rural markets provide earnings predictability. Cost riders, weather normalization mechanisms (expanding), and multiple regulatory jurisdictions reduce single-state risk.

**2. Wyoming data center positioning (improving):** BKH's Wyoming Electric subsidiary has emerged as a beneficiary of hyperscaler AI infrastructure buildout. Wyoming's combination of available land, cold climate, low-cost power, favorable business regulation, and BKH's newly completed Ready Wyoming Transmission interconnection (December 2025) create a competitive moat for large-load customers. Microsoft's purchase of 3,200 additional acres near Cheyenne confirms this is early-stage, multi-year demand.

The NWE merger adds Montana — a state with attractive renewable resources and transmission capacity — further enhancing the combined company's ability to serve large-load customers.

**Competitive threats:** Long-term gas utility demand erosion from electrification; distributed generation/storage for electric customers; execution risk on massive generation buildout; regulatory risk if allowed returns compress.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Merger Execution (HIGH — Near-term binary risk):** FERC and state approvals still pending. While settlements have been reached with intervenors, conditions may be imposed that reduce economic value. If the merger fails, BKH loses strategic momentum and resources but the standalone plan remains viable. The $100M termination fee applies if either party walks.

**2. Earnings Predictability (MODERATE):** Three misses in four quarters undermines confidence in consensus estimates. Weather sensitivity (especially gas utilities without full normalization) introduces quarterly volatility that may not fully normalize annually.

**3. Capital Funding Pressure (MODERATE):** Persistent negative FCF requires ongoing equity dilution and debt issuance. In a tightening capital markets environment, the cost of funding $4.7B in capex could compress returns. Dividend payout ratio at 68% (above target) suggests limited buffer.

**4. Data Center Concentration (MODERATE-HIGH):** Wyoming Electric's growth is dependent on 2–3 hyperscalers. If demand materializes, it's transformational. If a customer delays or cancels, capital already deployed creates stranded investment risk. The $201M deposit mitigates but does not eliminate this risk.

**5. Short Interest (NOTABLE):** At 16.5% of float with 8.5 days to cover, there is meaningful institutional skepticism. This could reflect concerns about merger dilution, earnings quality, or the feasibility of data center buildout. It could also reflect index or pair-trade positioning.

**6. Rising Interest Costs:** Net interest expense grew from $168M to $200M over two years. With $4.7B in planned capex and ~$400M in notes maturing January 2027, refinancing at current rates will continue to pressure margins.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing elevated near-term uncertainty: ATM IV of 36.2% vs. 20.4% historical volatility (15.7pp premium) with the term structure in backwardation (36.2% near-term vs. 29.6% at 6 months). This is unusual for a utility and signals the market is pricing a specific near-term event, likely related to merger regulatory decisions or the next earnings catalyst. The put/call OI ratio increased from 0.05 to 0.20 over the past 10 days, indicating growing hedging activity. While still low in absolute terms, the directional shift warrants attention.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($85–$90, +15–22%)
- NWE merger closes H2 2026 without onerous conditions; combined entity re-rates to peer-average 20x forward P/E
- Wyoming data center demand materializes beyond current 5-year plan; 1.8 GW customer advances to formal power purchase agreement
- South Dakota rate case delivers $45–50M+ of the $51M request, recognizing decade of investment
- Combined company achieves 5–6% EPS growth trajectory with data center upside pushing to 6–8%
- At peer median 20.4x on FY2027E $4.62 = $94 fair value (merger accretion could add $0.15–0.30)

### Bear Case ($60–$65, -12–19%)
- Merger fails or is approved with value-destructive conditions; standalone BKH re-prices to pre-merger levels (~$64–66)
- Data center demand stalls or delays; capital already committed creates temporary earnings drag
- Continued earnings misses drive consensus cuts; 17x × $4.20 = $71
- Rising rates + equity dilution + high short interest create selling pressure
- Utility derating from 30Y rates above 5% compresses sector multiples

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. FERC merger approval without material adverse conditions (would reduce binary risk, confirm H2 2026 close)
2. Formal power purchase agreement or interconnection agreement for the 1.8 GW data center prospect (confirms load beyond current plan)
3. Stock pullback to ~$67–69 (15.5x FY2026E) on market-wide utility sell-off or temporary setback without fundamental deterioration

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Merger termination or approval with conditions that eliminate synergy value
2. Hyperscaler customer withdrawal from Wyoming (cancellation of deposit or public announcement of site reduction)
3. Two consecutive quarterly misses of >10% on non-weather factors, indicating structural earnings quality issues

**Reassessment Timeline:** FERC decision (expected mid-2026); next earnings release (~August 2026); merger close announcement (H2 2026).

## Conclusion

Black Hills Corp presents a credible growth story within the utility sector, differentiated by genuine data center demand in Wyoming and a pending merger that creates scale and geographic diversification. The $201M customer deposit is a concrete signal that transformational load growth is approaching.

However, the current price ($73.92) already reflects meaningful optionality pricing: the stock has rallied 20% from its 52-week low and trades within 6% of its 52-week high. The earnings miss record, elevated short interest, and elevated near-term IV suggest informed skepticism. Negative FCF and ongoing dilution are structural features, not temporary conditions.

The appropriate stance is HOLD: the thesis has specific catalysts (merger close, data center formalization, rate case resolution) that could justify upgrade over the next 6–12 months, but the current entry point offers insufficient margin of safety given execution uncertainty. Investors collecting the 3.8% yield while awaiting clearer merger confirmation and data center formalization are appropriately positioned.
