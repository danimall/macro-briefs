## Executive Summary

Waystar reported a Q2 2026 that beat on both lines — revenue $319.7M (+18.1% year over year) against roughly $316M expected, non-GAAP diluted EPS $0.43 against ~$0.40, adjusted EBITDA $136.7M at a 42.8% margin — and raised FY2026 guidance to $1.276–1.294B of revenue and $535–545M of adjusted EBITDA. The stock fell 14.6% the following session, from $24.52 on July 29 to $20.94 on July 30, with an intraday low of $20.14, before recovering to $23.29. (One secondary-source recap cited in circulation reports a ~17.9% post-earnings gain; the price record contradicts it, and we use the price record.)

The reason for the negative reaction is visible in the revenue disaggregation. Subscription revenue grew 34.5% while volume-based revenue — roughly 49% of FY2025 revenue — grew 2.8%, and *provider* volume-based revenue declined about $2.1M year over year. Iodine, acquired October 1, 2025 for $1,259.8M, is a subscription business. Net revenue retention fell from 114.6% (LTM June 2025) to 112.0% (FY2025) to 108.3% (LTM June 2026), a 6.3-point deterioration in twelve months. The company does not disclose organic growth or churn, but the arithmetic of the split implies the non-Iodine business is growing in the mid-to-high single digits against a guided 16.9% headline.

This matters because the 2024–25 growth surge was explicitly attributable to a competitor's cybersecurity failure. The FY2025 10-K states that "in 2024 following a cybersecurity incident involving one of our competitors, more than 30,000 providers... began adopting our solutions," on contracts with "initial terms of two to three years." Those contracts come up for renewal across 2026 and 2027, and the principal competitor — Change Healthcare, inside UnitedHealth Group's Optum — has recovered. We cannot resolve churn from utilization softness using the filings, and neither can the market; that ambiguity is what the ~10.9x forward EBITDA multiple is discounting. We downgrade from BUY (four consecutive) to HOLD with an uncertain rationale. Base case $27 (+16%), bull $34 (+46%), bear $17 (−27%).

## Company Overview

Waystar sells cloud software that manages the U.S. healthcare revenue cycle — the administrative and payment workflow linking provider, payer, and patient. The product set spans financial clearance (eligibility, prior authorization, price estimation), patient financial care (statements, payments, plans), claims and payer payment management, denials prevention and recovery, and — via Iodine — clinical documentation integrity and DRG underpayment detection.

The paying customers are providers: physician practices, clinics, surgical centers, labs, hospitals and health systems. Payers and patients are transaction counterparties, not the revenue source. Waystar also acts as payment facilitator, which is why the balance sheet carries $15.5M of restricted cash against $15.1M of aggregated funds payable.

Scale, per the FY2025 10-K: more than 30,000 clients, over one million distinct providers, 16 of the top 20 *U.S. News* Best Hospitals, 7.5 billion transactions processed in 2025 representing more than $2.4 trillion in gross claims. Clients generating over $100K of annual revenue rose from 1,203 to 1,391 (+15.6%). Top 10 clients were 10.8% of FY2025 revenue — genuinely low concentration for an enterprise software business. Revenue mix is roughly one-third hospitals and two-thirds ambulatory and alternate sites. Headcount is 1,700+, all U.S.-based, with 340+ in product and engineering. One reportable segment.

Revenue is split between subscription (fixed, ratable) and volume-based (per transaction, dollar volume, or provider count), together ~99% of total. Contracts run two to three years with automatic one-year renewals; clients can terminate on limited notice. Management states 98% of 2025 revenue came from clients already under contract at the start of the year. Channel partners — 500+, including EHR and practice-management vendors — are paid revenue-share fees of $77.9M in FY2025, up from $65.3M and $52.3M in the two prior years.

Revenue grew 19.3% in FY2024 and 16.5% in FY2025. Gross margin excluding D&A improved from 66.5% to 68.4%. The FY2025 swing from loss to profit is mostly mechanical: operating income rose $125.6M, of which $46.1M came from lower D&A as pre-IPO purchase intangibles finished amortizing, and interest expense fell $68.7M because IPO proceeds retired roughly $1.02B of first-lien principal in mid-2024 and the facility was repriced multiple times (SOFR+2.75% in June 2024 to +2.25% in December 2024 to +2.00% in August 2025). Those two items account for roughly $115M of the ~$131M pre-tax improvement. The operating business did also expand reported margin from 13.1% to 22.7%, so the improvement is real — but the amortization tailwind has reversed: scheduled FY2026 intangible amortization rises to $137.9M.

The tax rate is a structural drag. FY2025 effective rate was 35% against a 21% statutory rate, driven by state and local tax (+7 points), nondeductible stock compensation (+5), and nondeductible compensation (+3). NOLs are minimal and Section 382-limited.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $309.7M in FY2025 against $26.5M of capex and capitalized software, giving derived FCF of $283.2M — a 25.8% FCF margin. The bridge from $112.1M of net income is dominated by non-cash addbacks: D&A $140.5M, stock comp $42.1M, deferred taxes $45.2M. Working capital was a modest drag. Capex at 2.4% of revenue is genuinely light, with only $16.8M of internal software capitalized, so reported R&D is not being flattered materially.

One adjustment matters: cash taxes paid were $32.4M against $59.7M of book expense, and $15M of the federal payment was settled by purchasing transferable tax credits. Normalizing FCF for a full cash tax burden reduces it toward $255M, or a 5.7% yield on market cap rather than 6.3%. That is still a substantial yield, but the headline overstates steady-state conversion.

**Balance sheet (12/31/2025).** Total debt principal $1,481.2M against $61.4M cash and $24.9M of short-dated investments, so net debt of $1,394.9M (we exclude restricted cash, which is owed to clients). Against management's ~$462M FY2025 adjusted EBITDA basis, net leverage is ~3.0x; against guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $540M, 2.6x. Cash interest paid of $81.7M is covered ~3.5x by FCF. Scheduled amortization is only $14.2M annually through 2028, then $1,438.7M matures on the first lien in October 2029. The $500M revolver was undrawn at year-end, and in February 2026 the receivables facility was upsized to $100M, repriced from SOFR+1.61% to +1.10%, and extended to 2029. Swaps and a cap fix roughly $550M of the balance into early 2027; the remainder floats at SOFR+2.00% (5.69% effective at year-end). A 100bp SOFR move on ~$850M of unhedged exposure is ~$8.5M pre-tax, roughly 3% of FCF.

The asset side is the constraint. Goodwill of $4,016.8M plus net intangibles of $1,292.8M equals $5,309.7M, or 91.8% of the $5,785.1M balance sheet — the 10-K states "approximately 92%." Derived tangible book value is negative $1,430.8M. Book equity is $3,878.8M against a $4.47B market cap, a cushion of roughly 15%. The company's own impairment risk factor names "decline in our market capitalization relative to net book value for a sustained period" as a trigger, and the annual test date is October 1. No impairment was recorded in 2023, 2024, or 2025, and the test is cash-flow based rather than market-cap based, so this is a disclosed sensitivity rather than a forecast. A goodwill charge would hit GAAP earnings and book equity but not operating cash flow, adjusted EBITDA, or credit-agreement covenants.

**Iodine.** Closed October 1, 2025 for $1,259.8M: $638.9M cash plus 16,639,920 shares valued at $37.31. Goodwill was $996.8M, or 79.1% of the purchase price; identified intangibles $372.4M, including $290.6M of customer relationships on an 18-year life that KPMG designated a Critical Audit Matter for forecast subjectivity. Iodine contributed $31.0M of revenue in Q4 2025, of which $10.8M was release of acquired deferred revenue, so annualization is unreliable — a defensible band is $81–124M, implying roughly 10–15x revenue paid. Iodine represented $1,397.7M of total assets at year-end, and management excluded it from its ICFR effectiveness assessment as first-year rules permit; KPMG's ICFR opinion likewise excluded it. Roughly 24% of the balance sheet has not been through a controls audit. The purchase-price allocation remains provisional with the measurement period open to October 1, 2026.

On dilution, stated properly: the 16,639,920 shares equal 9.7% of the 172.1M shares outstanding before issuance and 8.7% of the 191.6M outstanding after. Because the shares were valued at $37.31 and the stock has not sustained that level, Waystar transferred equity worth roughly $389M at today's price against $620.8M of recorded consideration — a real, quantifiable benefit of having used stock as currency.

Unvested overhang is separate and should not be added to current diluted capitalization: 13,992,173 options at an $18.71 weighted-average strike, 4,220,158 unvested RSUs, and 396,197 PSUs vesting 0–200% on four-year relative TSR. At target that is ~18.6M units, ~9.7% of current shares. The 2024 plan carries an evergreen provision adding up to 5% of prior-year shares annually, plus an ESPP adding up to 1% — a structurally dilutive design worth modeling.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance implies 16.9% revenue growth at the FY2026 midpoint ($1.285B) with a 42.0% adjusted EBITDA margin, essentially flat on FY2025. Guidance has been raised during the year, and the beat record is 3 of 4 quarters (+7.4%, +7.8%, −9.5%, +4.4%). Consensus estimates have drifted higher: current year $1.67 (+1.5% in 30 days, +1.7% in 90 days), next year $1.88 (+1.7%/+1.9%). Twenty-three of 24 covering analysts carry Buy or Strong Buy ratings.

Those are supportive but second-order facts. The first-order fact is composition. In Q2 2026, subscription revenue grew 34.5% and volume-based grew 2.8%, with provider volume-based revenue declining ~$2.1M. Iodine is subscription. Strip the acquisition and the residual growth rate is well below the headline. Net revenue retention — the cleanest disclosed proxy for the health of the installed base — has fallen for three consecutive readings, from 114.6% to 112.0% to 108.3%.

The most plausible explanation is partial reversion of the 2024 share gain. Waystar's own 10-K attributes the 30,000+ provider surge to a competitor's breach and states those contracts carry two-to-three year initial terms, placing the renewal window squarely in 2026–27. Alternatives exist and cannot be excluded from the filings: softer provider utilization, payer-mix shifts, mechanical lapping of an extraordinary comparison, or deliberate pricing discipline on low-margin transactional volume. Waystar does not disaggregate churn from volume, and the company reports a single segment on consolidated net income.

Two related items deserve correction of common misreadings. Remaining performance obligations of $116.4M at 12/31/2025 are not backlog — the 10-K excludes contracts with original duration of one year or less and excludes variable consideration allocated to the daily transaction series, which removes essentially all volume-based revenue and most renewals. RPO equals ~9% of guided 2026 revenue and carries no demand information. Separately, deferred revenue rose from $16.3M to $73.4M, but $61.7M of that was assumed in the Iodine acquisition; organically, $21.3M was recognized out of deferred revenue against $16.7M added, a net drawdown.

The secular backdrop is genuinely favorable. U.S. healthcare was 18.0% of GDP with $4.9T of spending in 2024, of which roughly $440B is administrative cost. Hospital labor was 56% of expenses with costs up $51B year over year; 63% of revenue-cycle leaders report understaffing; HFMA estimates ~25% of revenue-cycle spend goes to error correction. Waystar's self-estimated TAM is $20B in 2025 growing ~5% annually to ~$25B by 2030, implying ~4% penetration in hospitals and ~8% in ambulatory. Note the TAM growth rate: 5% annually. Above-market growth must come from share, and share is what the NRR series is questioning.

R&D was $54.6M, or 5.0% of revenue, plus $16.8M capitalized. Waystar spends more on channel-partner fees ($77.9M) than on R&D and 3.3x as much on sales and marketing. That is defensible for a network business where value accrues from connectivity, and ten acquisitions since 2018 substitute purchased for internal development. It is a thin research base against Optum and Epic if the competitive question shifts from distribution to model capability.

The vendor-supplied "forward P/E" of 33.76 equals the trailing figure because both are computed on GAAP EPS of $0.61; it is not a forward multiple. GAAP earnings are suppressed by $137.9M of scheduled 2026 intangible amortization and ~$42M of stock compensation, so roughly half of non-GAAP EPS is add-back. FCF and EV/EBITDA are the more reliable anchors, and both are non-demanding.

Peer comparison is of limited use: the Health Care Technology set spans SOLV at 9.7x, DOCS at 20.4x, HSTM at 39.2x, OMCL at 85.9x, and CERT at negative earnings, a dispersion that reflects heterogeneous business models rather than a sector multiple. On the more relevant frame — a 42% EBITDA-margin, 26% FCF-margin software business guiding mid-teens growth — 10.9x forward EBITDA is a depressed multiple.

The question is whether it is wrong. Our judgment is that it is defensible rather than clearly mispriced, and that the cheapness carries a value-trap signature. The market is not disputing current profitability; it is discounting terminal value against three specific items the company itself documents: the renewal cliff on 2024-vintage contracts, NRR at 108.3% and falling, and an AI-commoditization risk factor that was materially sharpened in the FY2025 10-K. If organic growth settles in the high single digits with volume-based revenue flat, a 10–11x EBITDA multiple is approximately right for a 3x-levered, negative-tangible-book business with a $1.44B 2029 maturity. The multiple only looks cheap if the installed base holds.

Analyst targets span $25 to $44 with a $32 median (mean $33.22). The low end sits above spot, which normally signals a lagging consensus. Here we weight it less than usual: the targets were largely set before the Q2 disaggregation and the NRR print, and the sell-side distribution (23 of 24 positive) has not moved in three months while the underlying retention metric deteriorated.

The board's $200M repurchase authorization (May 19, 2026) is the clearest internal signal that management views the discount as excessive. Execution has been measured: $12.7M at roughly $19.24 per share, about 6% of the authorization. Buying at the local low is sensible capital allocation; the pace suggests conviction is bounded, and the credit agreement restricts buybacks and dividends.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names four competitor categories — revenue-cycle technology vendors, point solutions, EHR/PM systems providers "including certain of our strategic partners and those with which we integrate," and provider in-house build. The unnamed but unmistakable primary competitor is Change Healthcare, inside UnitedHealth Group's Optum: a subsidiary of the largest U.S. health insurer, with its own payer network and a structural incentive to reclaim clearinghouse volume.

Where the position is defensible: top-10 client concentration of 10.8% is unusually low and reduces single-customer risk substantially; direct connectivity to large national health plans, established in 2024 when the competitor's portal was unavailable, is hard to replicate; 500+ EHR/PM integrations create switching friction; the combined clinical dataset (Iodine touching roughly one in three hospital discharges) and financial dataset (7.5B transactions, $2.4T claims) is a scarce asset; management cites an ~85% win rate in competitive switch/new-buy situations and an NPS of 74.

Where it is exposed, in the company's own language. The AI risk factor now reads: large technology companies "are deploying AI tools that could commoditize revenue cycle management functions core to our platform"; "agentic AI systems could automate workflows our clients currently rely on us to perform"; EHR and PM vendors "may integrate AI capabilities directly into their platforms"; and "generative AI may also lower barriers to entry." The same document argues AI is Waystar's moat. Both can be true, and the resolution determines whether 10.9x is cheap.

Channel partners present the same duality: Waystar pays 500+ partners $77.9M annually to distribute the product, while the 10-K warns those partners "could expand their product offerings to include solutions that compete directly with" Waystar's and "may offer a choice to end-users between our solutions and similar solutions offered by competitors or by the channel partners directly." Payer direct-connect is disclosed as a separate disintermediation vector: "a significant increase in the utilization of direct links between providers and payers would reduce the number of transactions that we process and for which we are paid."

Interoperability rules cut both ways, and the filing says so: they prevent EHR vendors from blocking Waystar's integrations while also making it "easier for other similar companies to enter the market." Provider consolidation is flagged as a negative — larger systems have vendor pricing power and may standardize onto incumbent platforms or vertically integrate.

Trajectory, which matters more than level: NRR down 6.3 points in twelve months, provider volume-based revenue declining, the principal competitor recovered, and the reliability argument that won the 2024 business now weakened by Waystar's own June 2026 incident. We score competitive position below neutral on that trajectory despite a strong static asset base.

## Risk Assessment

**Renewal cliff (primary).** The 30,000+ providers won in 2024 sit on two-to-three-year contracts reaching renewal across 2026–27. The NRR series and near-flat volume-based revenue are consistent with partial reversion. The filings do not disaggregate churn, so this cannot be sized from primary sources. This is the dominant unresolved variable.

**AI commoditization.** Newly sharpened, specific, and management-authored. A $54.6M R&D budget against Optum and Epic is a thin defense if competition shifts from network density to model capability. Clients "may impose contractual restrictions on our use of AI, or seek indemnification for AI-related errors."

**Goodwill impairment test, October 1, 2026.** Goodwill and intangibles are 91.8% of assets; tangible book is negative $1,430.8M; market cap exceeds book equity by only ~15%; the company names sustained market-cap-below-book as a trigger. Non-cash if it occurs, but it would hit GAAP EPS, book equity, and balance-sheet screens.

**Iodine integration and controls.** ~24% of the balance sheet excluded from the ICFR audit, a provisional purchase-price allocation open to October 2026, a Critical Audit Matter on the $290.6M customer-relationship value, and 79% of the price in goodwill. This is where a negative surprise would most plausibly originate.

**CFO transition.** Steven Oreskovich departed effective August 1, 2026, replaced by Alpana Wegner, whose immediately prior CFO tenure at Integral Ad Science ran roughly eleven months (approximately June 2025 to May 2026). Short prior tenure frequently reflects an acquirer's timetable rather than performance, and we draw no conclusion about the circumstances, which are unverified. The timing is the issue: a new CFO arrives while the company integrates a $1.26B acquisition with unaudited controls, absorbs decelerating organic growth, and carries $1.48B of debt.

**Cybersecurity.** In June 2026 Waystar disclosed unauthorized acquisition of source-code copies and four files of inactive data, with PHI/PII affecting under 1% of clients. Scope is an order of magnitude below the 2024 competitor event, no client loss or regulatory action is disclosed, and Q2 revenue and guidance both came in above expectations afterward. The asymmetry is reputational: a vendor whose 2024 sales argument was reliability must defend its own record during the renewal window for clients won on that argument. Source-code exfiltration is also forward-looking exposure.

**Volume and reimbursement sensitivity.** Roughly 49% of revenue is volume-based, and fees above contractual minimums "are generally not required to be paid in the absence of healthcare transactions." Named threats include government reimbursement reductions, federal workforce reductions administering programs, high-deductible plan shifts, and the No Surprises Act.

**Regulatory.** HIPAA covered-entity and business-associate status, with explicit disclosure that improper de-identification of PHI used to train AI could be treated as a breach; False Claims Act exposure because the rules engine shapes claim content; card-network and NACHA payment-facilitator rules; Durbin interchange sensitivity on HSA-linked cards; FDCPA exposure from patient collections; and the December 2024 proposed HIPAA Security Rule rewrite. Note 21 states no pending matter is currently expected to be material.

**Governance.** Sponsor structure persists two years post-IPO: classified board until the Institutional Investors fall below 15% voting power, 66⅔% supermajority Protective Period, blank-check preferred, forum exclusivity, and a charter waiver of corporate-opportunity duties permitting sponsors and non-employee directors to invest in competing businesses. Bain and CPPIB affiliates held $64.8M of Waystar's own first-lien debt at year-end (up from $35.6M), receiving $3.5M of interest — equity holders with board nomination rights sitting also as creditors ahead of the 2029 refinancing. Three 2025 secondaries totaled 55,375,000 shares (~29% of current shares) with no proceeds to Waystar; registration rights remain.

**Compensation.** CEO total compensation was $37.1M in FY2025 — roughly 0.8% of market cap and 33% of net income — with average non-CEO NEO compensation of $11.0M. The pay-versus-performance mechanics did function: the TSR index fell from 177.29 to 158.21 while the peer group rose from 103.76 to 123.55, and "compensation actually paid" fell from $55.4M to $18.7M. Grant scale remains aggressive, and the evergreen plan provision institutionalizes dilution. On July 27, 2026 the company accelerated all unvested 2019-plan options to vest by April 1, 2027; the filing gives a mechanism, not a rationale, and we infer no motive.

**Insider activity — low signal in both directions.** No open-market purchases by any insider appear in Form 4s through August 1, 2026. The CEO's July 15–16 transactions were cashless exercise-and-sell of ~70,000 shares on options struck at $4.14, with post-transaction holdings unchanged at 926,816 — monetization of deep-in-the-money legacy options, not a directional bet. The June 9 cluster at $19.23 across six officers was Code F tax withholding on RSU vesting, which carries no signal. Large grants on March 9 and August 1 raised CEO holdings to 1,835,081 shares (~$43M). Alignment exists but was granted rather than purchased. The absence of buying at a 44% drawdown is the more notable observation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 58.2% against 53.0% one-year historical volatility — rich by 5.2 points, at the 79th percentile within the current chain — with backwardation into the August 21 expiry (58.2% near vs. 53.8% long-dated) and a ±10.9% implied move over 15 days. The one-month put/call skew of 7.6% is characterized by the data source as elevated put demand; the six-month tenor prints −2.7%, and we do not infer direction from the sign.

Positioning has de-risked since mid-July: ATM IV compressed 14.8 points from 73.0%, and put/call open interest fell from 1.01 to 0.72, while the price rose 5.1%. Short interest is 8.1% of float at 4.6 days to cover — moderate, neither a crowded bear consensus nor a squeeze setup. Read together, the options market prices continued elevated realized volatility and residual near-term event risk without a directional conviction that corroborates either the bull or bear case. Backwardation plus rich near-term vol argues against buying premium here and is one reason we prefer waiting for the next disclosure over adding.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $34 (+46%)

Volume softness proves to be utilization and comparison effects rather than churn, and NRR stabilizes at or above 110% in the LTM September or December 2026 readings. Iodine cross-sell into the installed base converts clinical documentation into subscription growth that offsets flat transactional volume, and $1M+ ACV wins broaden the client mix. FY2027 revenue grows 12–14% to ~$1.45B with adjusted EBITDA of ~$620M as margins hold at 42–43%. Leverage falls toward 2.2x on FCF application and the buyback resumes at scale. A 13x EV/EBITDA multiple — still a discount to software peers at similar margin and growth — yields ~$34. Supporting facts: top-10 concentration of 10.8%, 6.3% trailing FCF yield, $500M undrawn revolver, no maturity until 2029, and a secular administrative-cost pool of $440B against ~4–8% penetration.

### Bear Case — $17 (−27%)

The renewal window confirms reversion. NRR breaks below 105%, volume-based revenue turns negative outright, and FY2027 guidance lands in the high single digits. The market re-rates to 8.5x on ~$560M of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA, implying roughly $17 per share against a 52-week low of $17.26. An AI-native or EHR-embedded competitor takes visible share in one workflow, validating the commoditization risk factor. The October 1 goodwill test produces a charge that compresses book equity and reopens the balance-sheet question. Compounding factors: 92% of assets intangible, negative $1.43B tangible book, ~$850M of floating-rate debt unhedged after early 2027, and structural dilution from the evergreen plan provision at a depressed share price.

### What Would Resolve It

The disagreement is narrow and testable. Waystar does not disclose organic growth, but three metrics it does disclose — NRR, volume-based revenue growth, and provider volume-based revenue in dollars — will settle the question over the next two quarters. Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing NRR and positive provider volume-based growth would justify a BUY at this multiple. Continued deterioration would make 10.9x a fair multiple rather than a cheap one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026), which will carry both the LTM September NRR reading and the October 1 goodwill test conclusion, plus the first full quarter under the new CFO. Secondary checkpoint: the Iodine measurement period closes October 1, 2026, finalizing the purchase-price allocation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. NRR stabilizes at or above 110% for two consecutive LTM readings, or volume-based revenue growth reaccelerates above 6% with provider volume-based revenue positive year over year.
2. FY2027 revenue guidance at or above 11% growth on the FY2026 base with adjusted EBITDA margin held at 42%+, indicating the renewal cohort has been retained.
3. Repurchase execution accelerates materially — more than $75M of the $200M authorization deployed below $24 — alongside net leverage below 2.5x.
4. A price below $19 with the above metrics merely stable rather than improving would restore sufficient margin of safety on its own.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. NRR below 105%, or volume-based revenue turning negative year over year in any quarter.
2. FY2027 revenue guidance below 9% growth, or any FY2026 guidance reduction.

## Conclusion

We are downgrading Waystar to HOLD after four consecutive BUY recommendations, and the downgrade is driven by disclosure rather than by price. The stock is 4.6% higher than at our last review; what changed is the Q2 2026 filing. Subscription revenue grew 34.5% while volume-based revenue grew 2.8% and provider volume-based revenue declined; net revenue retention fell to 108.3% from 114.6% a year earlier; and the subscription strength is attributable to an acquisition that closed October 1, 2025. Those three facts together indicate the organic engine is growing well below the guided 16.9% headline, and the most plausible cause — partial reversion of a share gain the company itself attributes to a competitor's 2024 breach — arrives on a schedule Waystar has disclosed: the 2026–27 renewal window.

The offsetting case is real. Adjusted EBITDA margins of 42.8%, a 6.3% trailing FCF yield (5.7% normalized for cash taxes), top-10 client concentration of 10.8%, no debt maturity until 2029, an undrawn $500M revolver, raised guidance, three beats in four quarters, and modestly rising estimates all describe a good business at 10.9x forward EBITDA and ~14x forward non-GAAP EPS. If the installed base holds, the stock is worth materially more than $23.

We are not willing to underwrite that condition at a BUY weighting. The dimension scores reflect where the evidence sits: financial health and cash generation remain solid (6.3), valuation is genuinely low but carries a value-trap signature because the discount maps onto documented structural concerns rather than sentiment (6.0), competitive trajectory is deteriorating on the retention data despite a strong static asset base (4.8), management is aligned by grant rather than by purchase and carries aggressive compensation, evergreen dilution, and a CFO transition at an inconvenient moment (5.2), and the idiosyncratic risk stack — renewal cliff, October 1 goodwill test with a 15% cushion, unaudited Iodine controls, source-code exfiltration — is unusually specific and dated (4.3). Overall 5.4.

Hold existing positions; the FCF yield and 2029 maturity profile provide time. New capital should wait for either the October 2026 NRR reading or a price closer to $19, where the bear case is substantially pre-funded.
