**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Elevance Health is one of the largest U.S. managed-care organizations by membership (~45.4 million medical members) and the Blue Cross Blue Shield licensee across 14 states. The stock trades at $399.18, roughly 15x consensus 2026 EPS of ~$26.91, a meaningful discount to the managed-care peer median P/E of ~37x and to UnitedHealth at ~30x. After a peak-to-trough drawdown that took the stock from $427 (June 2025) to $274 (August 2025) during the sector-wide medical-cost normalization, ELV has recovered to within 5% of its 52-week high, supported by a Q1 2026 earnings beat and a full-year guidance raise.

The central analytical question is whether the franchise is intact behind noisy headline results. Q1 2026 reported net income fell 19.2% YoY and diluted EPS dropped to $8.00 from $9.61, but the decline is almost entirely attributable to two items booked in Corporate & Other: a $935M CMS loss-contingency accrual and $129M of restructuring. The three operating segments declined only low-single digits (Health Benefits operating gain -2.7%, CarelonRx -3.3%, Carelon Services -4.3%), and operating cash flow rose sharply to $4.33B. Management could raise guidance the same quarter reported income fell 19% because the underlying earnings power is more durable than the GAAP print suggests. Adjusted Q1 EPS came in at $12.58 against ~$10.93 consensus, a 15.1% beat.

We rate ELV a HOLD. The valuation is genuinely cheap relative to peers and the dividend plus aggressive buyback (3.7M shares retired in Q1 at ~$305) provide downside support and per-share accretion. What prevents an upgrade to BUY is a specific cluster of unresolved, partly binary risks: the CMS Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment sanction (which can suspend new MA enrollment and carries a disclosed accrual range from $585M below to $565M above the $935M estimate), a parallel DOJ False Claims Act suit with discovery running into 2027, and the December 31, 2025 expiration of enhanced ACA premium tax credits that structurally degrades the Individual risk pool through 2026-27. These are concrete, monitorable catalysts with asymmetric downside. Estimate revisions are flat-to-negative on the near quarters (current-quarter consensus cut 6.2% over 90 days), so the signal that would justify a BUY is not yet present.

## Company Overview

Elevance Health (formerly Anthem) operates as the exclusive Blue Cross Blue Shield licensee in 14 states including California, Georgia, New York, Ohio, Virginia, and Indiana, serving roughly 34 million Blue enrollees, plus non-Blue brands Wellpoint, Simply Healthcare, and MMM in Puerto Rico. The business runs across four reportable segments:

- **Health Benefits** (~$42.5B Q1 operating revenue): the core insurer spanning Commercial risk and fee-based, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, the Federal Employee Program, and Individual/ACA.
- **CarelonRx** ($10.6B Q1 including affiliated revenue): the pharmacy benefit manager, which delegates core PBM functions to CVS Caremark under an agreement running through December 2027, a dependency rather than a fully owned capability.
- **Carelon Services** ($7.4B Q1 including affiliated): care delivery, behavioral health, and specialty/utilization management, the diversification engine modeled on UnitedHealth's Optum.
- **Corporate & Other**.

The moat is the BCBSA license, which grants exclusive Blue branding in its territories. That same license imposes constraints: anti-takeover ownership caps and a ~$3B re-establishment fee on termination. Strategically, management is building Carelon into a diversified health-services business to offset structurally thin insurance margins, the same playbook UnitedHealth executed with Optum a decade earlier.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 vs. Q1 2025:**
- Total operating revenue $49.5B, +1.5% YoY, driven by premium rate increases partly offset by membership attrition.
- Shareholders' net income $1.76B, down 19.2%; diluted EPS $8.00 vs. $9.61 (-16.8%).
- Benefit expense ratio (MLR) rose to 86.8% from 86.4%, reflecting elevated Medicaid acuity partly offset by improved Medicare Advantage.
- Operating expense ratio jumped to 12.8% from 10.9%, but the bulk of that increase is the CMS accrual plus restructuring charge.
- Total operating gain fell 34% to $2.09B, with Corporate & Other operating loss swinging from -$140M to -$1,123M, accounting for essentially the entire decline. The operating segments held up far better than the headline.
- Operating cash flow was $4.33B versus $1.0B a year earlier, helped by favorable claims-payment and working-capital timing.

**Balance sheet and capital:**
- Total assets $125.8B; total equity $44.0B; debt-to-capital 42.0% against a 60% covenant ceiling. Long-term debt $31.1B.
- Goodwill and intangibles of ~$39.5B represent 32.5% of assets, creating real impairment risk if Carelon or acquired businesses underperform.
- Investment-grade ratings: S&P A-, Moody's Baa2, Fitch BBB+. Leverage is appropriate for a regulated insurer with stable cash flows.
- Capital return is aggressive: $1.12B of buybacks in Q1 2026 (3.7M shares at ~$305 average), $376M in dividends ($1.72/quarter, raised from $1.71), with $5.57B of repurchase authorization remaining. Share count fell from 227.5M (Dec 2024) to 217.4M.

The multi-year context matters for the growth and valuation calls: net income has been flat-to-declining, from $6.15B (2021) to $5.66B (2025), even as revenue grew, reflecting the post-pandemic medical-cost cycle compressing insurance margins. Q1's cash generation is high-quality and the buyback at depressed prices is accretive if the franchise holds, which is why financial health scores above the midpoint despite the GAAP earnings decline. We mark it down modestly from the prior 7.0 because the operating expense ratio expansion, even with one-time items stripped out, sits against a backdrop of years of stagnant net income and a large intangibles balance.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is modest (+1.5% in Q1) and constrained by membership attrition. The growth story is composed of three pieces with different durability:

1. **Carelon Services** is the intended margin and growth engine, emulating Optum. Q1 operating gain declined 4.3%, so it has not yet decoupled from the cost cycle, but the long-term thesis of capturing care-delivery and specialty economics rather than relying on thin insurance margins is sound and the model is proven by UnitedHealth.

2. **Medicare Advantage** is a genuine 2027 tailwind: Star Ratings improved sharply, with 59% of MA members in plans rated 4 stars or higher for the 2026 ratings versus 40% the prior year. Higher star ratings drive bonus payments and rebate dollars that fund richer benefits, supporting MA enrollment economics into 2027, assuming the CMS sanction does not constrain new enrollment.

3. **The Operating Model Transformation Program** (2026-27) is an efficiency lever: org-layer reduction, AI-enabled workflows, IT modernization, and targeted workforce reductions, with $129M charged in Q1 and cash outlays through 2028. This is a margin-recovery program rather than a revenue driver.

Against these, the headwinds are structural. The ACA premium tax credit expiration raises effective premiums and pushes members toward lower bronze-tier plans and out of the pool; management guides to ~1.2M individual members. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act adds more frequent Medicaid redeterminations and work requirements (2027-28), which will shrink Medicaid enrollment and leave a sicker residual pool.

Estimate revisions confirm the absence of an inflection: current-quarter consensus is flat over 30 days but down 6.2% over 90 days, next quarter down 6.3% over 90 days, with full-year 2026 up a modest 3.5% over 90 days. This is a stabilizing, not accelerating, picture. The earnings-momentum signal that would justify a BUY, sustained positive revisions of meaningful magnitude, is not present. Growth scores at 5.5, an improvement from the 5.0 trough but capped by the structural ACA and Medicaid pressures.

## Valuation Assessment

ELV trades at ~15x consensus 2026 EPS of $26.91 and ~13.6x the $29.25 next-year estimate. Against the peer set, this is a clear discount:

| HUM | 37.4 | Smaller, MA-concentrated |
| UNH | 30.2 | Largest, Optum-driven premium |
| MOH | 53.0 | Depressed earnings, -36% off 52w high |
| HQY | 33.3 | HSA platform, not a direct comp |
| CNC | n/m | Negative earnings |
| **ELV** | **~17** | **Lowest in the group** |

The peer median P/E is ~37x. Even adjusting for ELV's slower growth and regulatory overhang, the gap is wide. The market is pricing in (a) the CMS sanction risk, (b) the DOJ suit, and (c) the ACA subsidy cliff. The investable question is whether the discount overcompensates. Our view: it partly does, but not by enough to force a BUY, because the largest overhang (CMS sanctions) is genuinely binary and the ACA mix deterioration is structural rather than transient.

Cycle-adjusted, the case for the stock being cheap strengthens. Trailing earnings reflect trough-cycle medical-cost compression; a normalized MLR and a recovering MA bonus stream into 2027 would lift EPS power, making the forward multiple understate the through-cycle value. The remaining buyback authorization at sub-$400 prices is accretive. Analyst consensus carries a $400 median and $415.81 mean target with a $498 high, so the current price sits near the consensus mid-point; analysts are not extrapolating aggressive upside, which is consistent with the unresolved-overhang read. We score valuation at 6.5: cheap on peer-relative and cycle-adjusted metrics, but the discount is partly earned by identifiable risk rather than a clean mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Elevance is a scale player alongside UnitedHealth, CVS/Aetna, Cigna, and Humana. Its structural advantage is the BCBSA license granting exclusive Blue branding to ~34M enrollees in its territories. The competitive trajectory is stable rather than improving. The Carelon build-out is a deliberate emulation of UnitedHealth's Optum and represents the right strategic direction, but it lags Optum materially in scale and integration. CarelonRx's reliance on CVS Caremark for back-end PBM functions through December 2027 is a competitive vulnerability relative to UnitedHealth's fully owned Optum Rx: Elevance does not fully control its pharmacy economics and faces a renewal/insourcing decision.

The entire sector is working through post-redetermination, post-pandemic cost normalization. ACA exchange dynamics dominated Q1 earnings calls across UnitedHealth, Cigna, Humana, and Elevance, confirming the ACA morbidity issue is sector-wide rather than company-specific. Where Elevance differs is its risk-adjustment exposure (dual CMS administrative sanction plus DOJ civil suit), which is more idiosyncratic. We score competitive position at 6.0: a defensible moat with a sound diversification strategy, but the PBM dependency and the lag versus Optum cap the trajectory at stable.

## Risk Assessment

The risk cluster here is specific and partly binary, which is the core reason for the HOLD rather than BUY:

1. **CMS Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment sanction (dominant near-term overhang).** On February 27, 2026, CMS notified Elevance of intent to impose intermediate sanctions, which can suspend new MA enrollment, over alleged noncompliance on diagnosis-code submissions (service dates 2015 to April 2023). Remediation deadlines were extended (steps due July 31, 2026) and sanctions have been repeatedly deferred but not resolved. An enrollment suspension would directly impair the MA growth engine into 2027, the year the improved Star Ratings should otherwise pay off.

2. **DOJ False Claims Act lawsuit.** *United States v. Anthem* (filed 2020, S.D.N.Y.) alleges false certification and failure to delete inaccurate MA diagnosis codes. Fact discovery runs to June 2026, expert discovery to March 2027, with a court ordering a former senior executive deposed. This is a separate, longer-tail monetary exposure stacked on the CMS matter.

3. **Medical-cost trend and pricing lag.** Elevated Medicaid acuity (sicker post-redetermination pool) and ACA morbidity cannot be repriced mid-year because Medicaid rates are state-set and MA bids lock six months ahead. Per industry commentary, the cost recovery the sector claims rests on incomplete data.

4. **ACA subsidy cliff.** This is the cleaner structural risk, less likely to wash out than the cost-trend issue, degrading the Individual risk pool through 2026-27.

5. **Goodwill impairment risk** from the ~$39.5B intangibles balance, and contingent put obligations: a ~$2.7B Mosaic Health net put (exercisable 2028-2030) and a Liberty Dental put exercisable as early as July 2026 represent future cash calls carried at fair value.

These are dated, monitorable events with material, asymmetric downside if they break against the company. We score risk factors at 5.0, an improvement from the 4.5 trough as the CMS sanction has been repeatedly deferred during successful remediation, but held below the midpoint because the binary enrollment-suspension outcome and the DOJ tail remain live.

The term structure is in contango (near-term 31.5% rising to 34.5% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. The one-week put/call skew is elevated at 8.3% with a put/call volume ratio of 1.71, indicating near-dated hedging demand into the June 18 expiry, but the longer-dated skew is mildly negative (-5.8% at six months) and the put/call OI ratio fell to 0.37 from 0.49, suggesting the options market is not pricing a major directional break and that hedging is concentrated near-term. The cheap IV makes options a relatively inexpensive way to express either direction, and the absence of sustained put accumulation argues the market views the CMS overhang as a manageable, deferring risk rather than an imminent shock, consistent with the fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($460-498):** The CMS sanction resolves without a meaningful enrollment suspension (the repeated deferrals during remediation support this), the DOJ suit settles for a manageable sum, and medical-cost trend stabilizes as the 2026 guidance raise implies. The improved Star Ratings drive 2027 bonus dollars, Carelon margins recover, and the Transformation Program delivers efficiency gains. Re-rating toward even 18-20x on $29+ EPS plus continued buyback at depressed prices supports $460-498. Director Steven Collis's ~$870K open-market purchase at $289.84 on March 5, days after the CMS news, by a healthcare-industry-veteran director, is a contrarian vote that the regulatory selloff was overdone.

**Bear Case ($280-300, -25% to -30%):** CMS imposes an enrollment suspension that impairs the MA franchise into 2027, the DOJ suit produces a large damages award, and the ACA subsidy cliff plus OBBBA Medicaid tightening compress membership and worsen mix faster than priced. Medical-cost trend proves worse than the incomplete Q1 data suggested, MLR continues climbing past 87%, and a goodwill impairment surfaces against the intangibles balance. This returns the stock to its summer-2025 lows.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced from current levels: ~15-20% to base/bull versus ~25-30% to bear, with the binary CMS outcome the swing factor. That balance, not a directional conviction, is what produces the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the CMS remediation deadline (July 31, 2026), Q2 2026 earnings (late July), and the CMS sanction resolution itself, which has been deferred toward July 1.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CMS resolves the risk-adjustment matter without an enrollment suspension and with a settlement at or below the $935M accrual, removing the dominant binary overhang.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS estimate revisions of meaningful magnitude (reversing the -6.2% 90-day cut on the near quarter), confirming cost-trend stabilization is real rather than guidance optimism.
- MLR stabilizes or declines below 86.5% with confirmed (not incomplete) cost data.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CMS imposes an enrollment suspension or the accrual is materially increased toward the +$565M upper bound.
- MLR climbs above 88% on a sustained basis, or 2026 guidance is cut.
- A goodwill impairment is recorded against the Carelon/acquisition intangibles, signaling the diversification thesis is failing.
- An adverse DOJ ruling or settlement materially above expectations.

## Conclusion

Elevance is a cheap, well-capitalized, cash-generative managed-care franchise trading at a substantial discount to peers, with high-quality Q1 cash flow, an accretive buyback at depressed prices, and a 2027 MA bonus tailwind from improved Star Ratings. The headline EPS decline overstates operational deterioration, and a guidance raise alongside a strong director open-market purchase argues the regulatory selloff was overdone.

What stops short of a BUY is a specific, falsifiable risk cluster: the unresolved CMS Medicare Advantage sanction (a binary enrollment-suspension threat with a wide accrual range), a parallel DOJ False Claims Act suit running into 2027, and the structural ACA subsidy cliff that worsens the Individual risk pool through 2026-27. Estimate revisions are flat-to-negative on the near quarters, so the earnings-inflection signal that would convert peer-relative cheapness into a BUY is absent. The risk/reward from $399 is roughly balanced: modest upside to a peer-aligned re-rating versus a 25-30% drawdown if the regulatory and cost risks break unfavorably. We rate ELV a HOLD and would upgrade on a clean CMS resolution coupled with confirmed cost-trend stabilization, or downgrade on an enrollment suspension or MLR deterioration above 88%.
