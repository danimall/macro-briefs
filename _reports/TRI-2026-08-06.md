## Executive Summary

Thomson Reuters has fallen 52.4% from its 52-week high of $207.26 (August 2025) to $98.61, while the business it owns grew faster in 2026 than in 2025. Second-quarter revenue was $1,954M, up 9% reported and 8% organic; the "Big 3" segments (Legal Professionals, Corporates, Tax/Audit/Accounting), 83% of revenue, grew 10% organic; adjusted EBITDA rose 10% to $745M at a 38.1% margin; adjusted EPS rose 14% to $0.99, two cents above consensus. Management raised full-year organic growth guidance twice in 2026, from 7.5–8.0% in February to ~8.0% in August, with Big 3 growth lifted to 9.5–10.0%. Free cash flow of $727M in the quarter was up 29%.

The decline is a multiple event. At the August 2025 peak, TRI traded near 53x FY2025 adjusted EPS of roughly $3.91. Today it trades at 22.4x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $4.41 and 19.6x FY2027's $5.03, with an enterprise value of ~$45.1B against ~$3.25B of estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA (13.9x), a 4.9% free cash flow yield on guided $2.1B, and a 2.6% dividend that has been raised for 33 consecutive years. The price series confirms the two discrete repricings: February 3, 2026 saw a 15.7% single-day decline (from $109.03 to $91.95) coincident with Anthropic's release of a legal plugin, and August 5, 2026 — earnings day — traded as high as $113.45 before closing at $98.61, down 9.7% from the prior close despite a beat and a guidance raise.

The bear thesis is about terminal value, and 2026 results cannot refute it. Multi-year subscription contracts in legal research are a lagging indicator: attrition would not appear in reported revenue for several renewal cycles. Against that, the segment most exposed — Legal Professionals — accelerated from +8% organic in FY2025 to +10% in Q2 2026, attributed by management to Westlaw and CoCounsel, and 97% of that segment's revenue is recurring. Two additional issues keep this at HOLD rather than BUY: TRI has agreed to provide unquantified financial support giving KKR a minimum return on its equity in the Global Print joint venture, and shareholder returns in H1 exceeded free cash flow by $463M, funded with $1,305M of net short-term borrowing whose committed backstop I could not verify. The risk/reward is favorable; the evidence is not yet decisive.

## Company Overview

Thomson Reuters sells subscription content-and-workflow systems to professionals with personal liability for error: litigators, in-house legal and tax departments, auditors, accountants and government agencies. FY2025 revenue of $7,476M split as Legal Professionals $2,843M (+8% organic), Corporates $2,023M (+9%), Tax/Audit/Accounting $1,291M (+11%), Reuters news $853M (+1%) and Global Print $490M (−5%). Named franchises include Westlaw, Practical Law, CoCounsel (the AI assistant layer, now sold across all three Big 3 segments), CLEAR, ONESOURCE and Indirect Tax, Pagero e-invoicing, Confirmation, SafeSend, GoSystem and Checkpoint.

Three structural features matter. Recurring revenue was 82% of Q2 revenue ($1,601M of $1,954M) and 97% of Legal Professionals. The differentiator claimed is editorially curated primary law and citator apparatus rather than the delivery software — management now markets "Fiduciary-Grade AI." And the portfolio is narrowing: FindLaw and other assets were sold in FY2024 (which is why FY2025 total growth was 3% against 7% organic), and Global Print is being deconsolidated through the KKR joint venture announced July 14, 2026.

One reporting note: TRI is a Canadian MJDS filer reporting on Form 40-F with quarterly disclosure furnished on Form 6-K, under IFRS in USD. There is no 10-K, no 10-Q, no XBRL feed and no Section 16 insider reporting. Analysis therefore rests on the furnished Q2 2026 results package, which contains full IFRS statements and non-IFRS reconciliations. Standard US-domestic screens silently misprice or exclude this issuer, and the absence of insider-transaction data is an information deficit rather than a neutral signal.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported earnings overstate operating momentum; adjusted EPS is the honest number.** IFRS diluted EPS rose 47.8% to $1.02. Operating profit rose 28% to $558M, but that includes "other operating gains, net" of $68M versus $5M a year earlier; excluding those, operating profit was $490M against $431M, or +13.7%. Below the line, other finance income swung $56M favorably (largely FX on intercompany balances), and the diluted share count fell 2.8% to 438.6M. Adjusted EPS of $0.99, +14% reported and +13% constant currency, reconciles to the underlying arithmetic. Note that these gains recur in occurrence if not in amount — FY2025 contained $164M — and a further pre-tax gain is expected when the KKR transaction closes. The August 5 consensus comparison is clean: the $0.97 estimate was on an adjusted basis, matching the $0.99 actual for a 2.6% beat.

**Cash flow is strong and should not be annualized.** Q2 free cash flow was $727M, H1 $1,059M, against unchanged full-year guidance of ~$2.1B. Annualizing the quarter gives $2.9B, 38% above management's own guide; the gap is seasonality — Tax/Audit generates roughly 60% of its revenue in Q1 and Q4, and Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin is guided to ~36% versus 40.2% in H1. Guidance implies H2 free cash flow of ~$1.04B, essentially flat against H1 despite the seasonally stronger Q4, which suggests either conservatism or reversal of part of the $88M favorable working-capital swing that contributed roughly 38% of the H1 operating cash flow increase. FY2025 conversion was $1,950M of FCF on $2,936M of adjusted EBITDA, 66%; accrued capex was 8.2% of revenue, guided to ~8.0% in 2026.

**Capitalized software deserves specific attention** because an AI development cycle can hide there. FY2026 guidance is $890–910M of depreciation and software amortization, of which $680–690M is internally developed (versus $626M in FY2025); software net on the balance sheet rose from $1,645M to $1,711M in six months. Adjusted EBITDA excludes all of it, so the 39–40% margin does not reflect the cost of the build — capex at 8% of revenue is the better lens. Adjusted EPS, however, adds back only acquired software amortization and leaves internally developed amortization in earnings. Since non-IFRS measures are the stated basis for management incentive programs, management cannot inflate its own scorecard by capitalizing development spend. That is a real alignment feature and unusual in information services.

**Balance sheet: low leverage, but the funding mix changed.** At June 30, 2026: cash $577M, current indebtedness $1,618M (from $795M at year-end), long-term $1,323M, total $2,941M. Simple net debt of $2,364M is up $752M in six months. Against estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$3.25B (FY2025 revenue × 1.08 × a 40.2% margin, per guided +100bps), net leverage is ~0.73x, and net interest guidance of $180–190M implies ~17x coverage. The current ratio fell from 0.64 to 0.51; structurally negative working capital is normal for prepaid subscriptions, but the composition shifted — the increase reflects $1,305M of net short-term/commercial-paper borrowing in H1, partly offset by repayment of the $500M 3.35% notes in May. I could not verify the size or covenants of a backstop revolving facility, so I cannot assert that the current indebtedness is fully committed-backed. That roll is a live cost line: net interest guidance was already raised $30M in May.

The reason net debt rose is deliberate. H1 uses of cash were $605M return of capital, $362M buybacks, $555M dividends and $248M of acquisitions, totaling $1,770M against $1,059M of free cash flow. Shareholder returns alone exceeded H1 FCF by $463M. Roughly $500M of gross KKR proceeds arrive in Q4. On a full-year basis, $2.1B of FCF plus $500M of proceeds plausibly self-funds the program; on H1 evidence, it did not.

**Reconstructed screens** (my calculation, since standard tooling returns nothing for a 40-F filer): Altman Z ≈ 5.2, though 73% of that comes from the market-equity/liabilities term, so it declines mechanically with the share price and is not independent confirmation of safety. Piotroski ≈ 7/9, with both failures — higher leverage and a weaker current ratio — tracing to the same debt-funded return decision. The solvency case rests better on 0.73x net leverage, 17x coverage and $2.1B of guided free cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

Q2 organic growth by segment: Legal +10%, Corporates +10%, Tax/Audit +8%, Reuters +4%, Global Print −3%. Guidance moved up twice this year on both total and Big 3 growth. Recurring revenue grew 9%; transaction revenue grew 16% reported and 11% organic, with Corporates transactions +27%. The fastest growers — Pagero, Confirmation, SafeSend, Indirect Tax and CLEAR — sit largely outside the legal-research battleground, in regulatory-compliance and verification workflows where the constraint is a mandated process rather than editorial content.

Two qualifications temper the headline. First, growth quality is modestly lower than it looks: transaction revenue is volume-dependent and more cyclical than subscriptions, and it is growing faster than the recurring base. Second, revenue guidance rose while margin guidance did not. Adjusted EBITDA margin guidance remains +100bps versus 2025, unchanged since February. Legal Professionals' Q2 margin was flat at 48.1% (−10bp constant currency) on +10% organic growth; Tax/Audit's H1 margin fell 160bp; Reuters' H1 margin fell 180bp to 18.6%; corporate costs rose from $29M to $36M. Incremental revenue is arriving with proportionate or greater incremental cost, which is what funding an AI product transition looks like. The counterargument is that Corporates expanded margin 150bp in the quarter, so the mix is capable of expansion once the investment cycle normalizes.

Consensus estimates have drifted modestly lower: current year $4.41 (−1.4% over 90 days), next year $5.03 (−1.6%), current quarter $0.96 (−6.4%). These are small moves and part of the current-quarter figure likely reflects the pending restatement of guidance for Global Print as a discontinued operation from Q3. There is no positive revision momentum here — the estimate-revision signal is mildly negative, which argues against a high-conviction BUY even though the delivered results are accelerating. For context, out-year estimates for TRI have been cut by less than 2%, which is comparatively modest for a name in the AI-substitution debate.

The earnings track record supports confidence in consensus without suggesting sandbagging: four quarters of $0.99 vs $0.97, $1.23 vs $1.21, $1.07 vs $1.07, $0.85 vs $0.83 — three beats and one in line, all within 2.6%. This is a company that guides tightly and delivers tightly.

The KKR transaction is mix-accretive to reported growth. Removing a −3% to −5% organic segment with ~$490M of revenue lifts consolidated growth optics from Q3 forward, and at $500M for 51% (implying ~$980M for 100% of the equity, or roughly 5.6x my estimated ~$176M of FY2025 segment EBITDA) it is being sold at approximately one-fourteenth of TRI's own EV/EBITDA multiple. Both the segment EBITDA and the multiple are my estimates; the segment figure was not disclosed and whether the JV carries debt is unknown.

(2) The market is correctly repricing terminal value: if Westlaw's research franchise is substitutable at a fraction of its price over five to seven years, then 8% organic growth today is being extrapolated off a base that shrinks later, and 20x is not cheap on 2030 earnings. (3) Some of the August decline is prosaic — Q3 margin guided to ~36% is an optical step-down from 38.1%, net interest guidance was raised, guidance will be restated for discontinued operations, and the stock had already run 7.4% on August 4 into the print.

A cheap multiple on a franchise whose terminal value is genuinely contested is exactly the configuration in which cheapness has historically failed to pay. What supports a score above neutral is the absolute cash return: 4.9% FCF yield, a 2.6% dividend covered at 54% of FCF and raised 10% annually for five straight years, and 0.73x net leverage. That combination gives a real floor under the valuation independent of the AI debate. ## Competitive Landscape

The structure changed from a duopoly into a three-way contest, and the change is externally documented rather than filing-documented.

Incumbents (TRI, RELX's LexisNexis) argue that professionals buy AI grounded in a vetted corpus of case law, statutes and editorial annotation because hallucination in a liability-bearing profession is career-threatening. Startups — Harvey (valued at $8B) and Legora — argue lawyers will accept and manage more risk for speed and flexibility. Foundation-model vendors (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) are moving into the workflow directly; Anthropic's "Cowork" legal plugin, released February 3, 2026, is what triggered the 15.7% single-day decline visible in the price series, alongside a William Blair note describing "a structural concern" about the relevancy and moats of information-services names. Harvey builds on models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google — it is a customer of those providers, not their product.

Practitioner opinion is genuinely split. One legal-AI founder called the Anthropic plugin "very raw and not a threat," while conceding the sell-off made sense; an in-house lawyer testing it found it pulling from Wikipedia. These are anecdotes, but they speak to why grounded content still commands price in a profession that bills for accuracy.

The near-term evidence favors the incumbents. Legal Professionals accelerated from +8% to +10% organic through the exact quarters in which the AI threat was most loudly priced, with management attributing the acceleration to Westlaw and CoCounsel. TRI also shipped its own "Thomson LLM" to a first production-ready version, to be integrated into CoCounsel Legal's Tabular Analysis. The benchmarking against Anthropic, OpenAI and Google models was supplied by Thomson Reuters and is a vendor self-report, not independent evaluation; the same caution applies to the Laurel partnership claims of 30 recovered billable minutes per attorney per day.

The honest limitation is duration. Subscription revenue with multi-year terms and 97% recurring mix in Legal means renewals, not usage, determine when substitution shows up. TRI discloses no remaining performance obligations, no backlog and no renewal-rate metric, and current deferred revenue of $1,256M versus $1,251M at year-end cannot support a billings conclusion given Tax/Audit seasonality (the June 2025 balance would be required). Absent those disclosures, 2026 growth is consistent with both a holding moat and a moat eroding on a lag.

One discipline point: the revenue mix shifting toward Corporates and Tax/Audit is mix change, not verified customer diversification. No customer-concentration data is available, and the one concentration signal that is available runs the other way — Reuters' Q2 growth came "primarily" from higher Agency revenue and a contractual price increase under the news agreement with LSEG's Data & Analytics business, which pays TRI. A single counterparty's escalators appear to drive a meaningful share of an $853M, 18.6%-H1-margin segment.

Trajectory assessment: a leader whose near-term growth is accelerating and whose product response is credible, facing better-capitalized new entrants attacking its highest-margin franchise, with no disclosed metric that would let an outsider detect early erosion. That combination scores 5.8 — above impaired, below improving.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Unquantified KKR minimum-return support (the single most important open item).** The Q2 release states TRI "agreed to provide certain financial support designed to give KKR a minimum return on its equity investment in the joint venture under certain circumstances." Magnitude, cap, triggers and duration are undisclosed. TRI sells 51%, retains 49%, keeps the IP and editorial control, and licenses content exclusively to the JV for a royalty. Economically TRI converts a declining segment into $500M of cash, a royalty stream, an equity-method stake, and a contingent obligation of unknown size on a business shrinking 3–5% per year. It may be narrowly scoped and immaterial; it cannot be resolved without the definitive agreement or the Q3 disclosure.

**2. AI substitution of the research layer.** The dominant risk to terminal value, and unresolvable with 2026 data for the lagging-indicator reasons above. If it bites, the second-order consequence is balance-sheet: goodwill $8,094M plus other intangibles $3,058M plus software $1,711M equals $12.9B, or 71% of $18.1B total assets, against $11.1B of equity. Tangible book value is negative and impairment would follow any write-down of acquired legal/tax franchises.

**3. Short-term funding composition.** $1,618M of current indebtedness against $577M of cash, including ~$1,305M of H1 net short-term borrowing raised to fund returns. Commercial paper must be rolled continually, and I could not verify facility size, covenants or the committed backstop. This is a modest risk at 0.73x net leverage but a real one given the company's own retained language on "an evolving interest rate and inflationary backdrop."

**4. Margin guidance not tracking revenue guidance.** Revenue guidance up twice, margin guidance unchanged at +100bps, with segment-level compression in Tax/Audit and Reuters. Investors are receiving growth or margin, not both.

**5. Primary risk-factor text unavailable.** The 2025 annual report risk factors (pages 19–32) could not be retrieved; EDGAR returned 403 for the 40-F index and exhibits. I therefore cannot determine whether the company added or expanded generative-AI competition language for FY2025. Related gaps: no proxy or circular, so the widely assumed Woodbridge/Thomson family control stake is unverified and its governance implications are open; no executive compensation detail beyond the fact that non-IFRS measures anchor incentives; no Q2 earnings call transcript, so management's direct Q&A on Westlaw renewal rates and CoCounsel attach rates is absent.

**6. Governance/mechanics footnote.** The May 4 return of capital paid $1.435518 per participating common share alongside a share consolidation at 0.984560, structured so proportional ownership was preserved for every holder. The three disclosed figures ($605M returned, ~6.5M share reduction, 0.984560 ratio) do not reconcile cleanly on a single-class basis, likely reflecting share-class definitions or record-date timing. Low materiality, noted for completeness. I draw no inference about motive from the structure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($140).** The AI scare repriced a franchise whose numbers accelerated straight through it. Recurring revenue at 82% of total and 97% of Legal, 8% organic growth guided upward twice, 39–40% EBITDA margins, 0.73x net leverage, 33 consecutive dividend increases, and a proprietary corpus that foundation models cannot license cheaply. CoCounsel plus the production-ready Thomson LLM convert the AI threat into a pricing opportunity — the company sells the same content with a higher-value interface. The KKR deconsolidation removes a −5% drag, adds $500M of cash, a royalty stream and a pre-tax gain, and lifts consolidated growth optics from Q3. On 26x a modestly upgraded FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$5.30, the stock is $138; the high analyst target is $146. Re-rating toward the 20–25x EBITDA / 28–35x earnings range where high-quality information-services incumbents have historically traded delivers most of that without any acceleration in growth.

**Bear case ($72, −27%).** Legal research is the single most substitutable product in the portfolio and the highest-margin one (48.1%). Renewals in 2027–2029, not usage in 2026, determine the outcome, and TRI discloses no retention metric that would let an outsider see erosion early. Margin guidance already shows the cost of defending the position: revenue guidance rose, margin guidance did not, and internally developed software amortization is guided up ~$60M. Meanwhile leverage is rising to fund returns, the KKR guarantee is unquantified against a shrinking asset, and $12.9B of intangibles against negative tangible book means any franchise write-down hits equity directly. At 15x a flattening $4.60 of adjusted EPS the stock is $69 — near the June low of $76.28 and below the $85 low analyst target.

**Base case ($110, +11.6% plus 2.6% yield).** 22x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $5.03 = $110.7. This assumes organic growth holds at 7–8% with Big 3 near 9–10%, the guided +100bps of margin expansion is delivered, the KKR close lands ~$500M in Q4, and the multiple stays roughly where it is rather than re-rating in either direction. Total return of ~14% over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive twelve-month path — base case $110 plus a 2.6% covered dividend — but the two decisive unknowns (renewal durability in Legal, the size of the KKR guarantee) both resolve on a timetable I cannot control, and estimate revisions are mildly negative rather than positive.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price toward $85** (the low analyst target, ~16.9x FY2027 consensus, ~5.7% FCF yield). At that level the cash return alone compensates for unresolved terminal value.
2. **Two more quarters of Legal Professionals organic growth at 9% or better accompanied by segment margin expansion** (Q2 was flat at 48.1%). Growth plus margin together would show the AI transition is being funded out of scale rather than out of profit.
3. **Q3 disclosure that caps or narrowly scopes the KKR minimum-return obligation**, combined with restated post-Global-Print guidance that holds ~8% organic growth and the +100bps margin target.
4. **Consensus FY2027 estimates turning up** from $5.03 for two consecutive months.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Legal Professionals organic growth decelerating below 6%,** or any disclosure of declining Westlaw renewal/retention rates.
2. **A cut to the adjusted EBITDA margin guidance** or FY2026 free cash flow guidance below $2.0B.
3. **Goodwill or software impairment** in the legal/tax franchises, which would validate the terminal-value bear case in the accounts.
4. **Evidence that commercial-paper funding is not fully committed-backed,** or net debt/EBITDA rising above 1.5x without a corresponding acquisition.

**Reassessment point: the Q3 2026 report (late October / early November 2026).** That print carries the restated guidance for Global Print as a discontinued operation, the accounting for the KKR closing including any disclosure of the support obligation, the Q3 margin against the ~36% guide, and the first full quarter of the Thomson LLM in market. The KKR closing itself (expected Q4, subject to regulatory approval) is a separate discrete event.

## Conclusion

Thomson Reuters is a high-quality subscription franchise trading at 19.6x next-year consensus adjusted earnings, 13.9x EV/EBITDA, and a 4.9% free cash flow yield, after a 52% decline during which adjusted earnings grew 14% and management raised guidance twice. The entire drawdown is multiple compression, and the compression has a specific cause: the market is repricing the terminal value of legal research in a world where foundation models and well-funded startups attack the workflow directly. Reported 2026 results cannot settle that question, because 97%-recurring multi-year contracts would mask attrition for several renewal cycles, and TRI discloses no retention or backlog metric that would reveal it earlier.

What is verifiable supports patience rather than urgency. The business is compounding at 8% organic with 82% recurring revenue, 0.73x net leverage, 17x interest coverage, a dividend at 54% of guided free cash flow and raised for 33 consecutive years, and a management team whose incentive metrics conservatively include internally developed software amortization. Buybacks were executed near current levels rather than at last year's peak. Offsetting that: shareholder returns in H1 exceeded free cash flow by $463M and were financed with short-term borrowing of unverified backstop; margin guidance did not follow revenue guidance up; an unquantified minimum-return guarantee to KKR sits inside the Global Print carve-out; 71% of assets are intangible against negative tangible book; and the options market is pricing a ±8.9% two-week move with volatility in backwardation.

Hold existing positions and collect the dividend. Add at $85–90, or on two more quarters of double-digit Legal organic growth accompanied by margin expansion, or on Q3 disclosure that bounds the KKR obligation. Exit on any deceleration in Legal below 6% organic or an impairment in the legal/tax intangibles.
