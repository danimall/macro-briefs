## Executive Summary

Valero Energy is the world's largest independent petroleum refiner. Following the idling of the Benicia refinery in April 2026, the company operates 14 petroleum refineries with approximately 3.0 million barrels/day throughput capacity. The company is the purest large-cap expression of the Hormuz supply disruption thesis — no upstream production, predominantly levered to refining margins with additional Renewable Diesel and Ethanol segments. Q1 2026 earnings improved sharply year-over-year ($4.22 EPS vs. a GAAP loss of $(1.90) in Q1 2025, or $0.89 on an adjusted basis), and consensus estimates for the current year have reportedly been revised significantly upward.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) structural advantage from Gulf Coast refining concentration accessing Western Hemisphere crude at a discount to disrupted global benchmarks, (2) aggressive capital return reducing share count at a rate that amplifies per-share value through the cycle, and (3) a strategically well-timed exit from California operations that eliminates the highest-cost, highest-regulatory-burden capacity. At the current share price, the stock appears to offer attractive risk/reward if elevated refining margins persist, though investors must weigh the risk that a diplomatic resolution of the Iran conflict — an inherently difficult-to-quantify scenario — would compress margins by 60-70% within quarters.

## Company Overview

Valero refines crude oil into gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, and other products across an approximately 3.0M BPD system (post-Benicia idling) concentrated on the U.S. Gulf Coast (Port Arthur 435K BPD, Corpus Christi 370K BPD, St. Charles 340K BPD, Texas City 245K BPD). It also operates through a Renewable Diesel segment (Diamond Green Diesel JV with Darling Ingredients, ~1.2B gallons/year capacity) and an Ethanol segment (12 plants, ~1.7B gallons/year). The company has no crude oil production and no owned retail stations, making it predominantly a refining margin play among large-cap energy names, though the Renewable Diesel and Ethanol segments can materially affect results.

The Benicia, California refinery was idled in April 2026 following a $1.1B impairment charge recognized in 2025, with the Wilmington refinery (135K BPD) under strategic review. Approximately 7,000 independently owned branded outlets carry Valero family brands.

If FY2026 margins remain elevated, Valero could potentially accelerate buybacks, though projecting a specific share-count reduction requires assumptions about margin duration, working capital changes, and management's buyback pacing decisions that introduce significant uncertainty.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Earnings Power (12-18 months):**

The estimate revision trajectory is directionally very strong. Q1 2026 EPS of $4.22 significantly exceeded analyst expectations. This reflects the market rapidly incorporating Hormuz-driven margin expansion into models.

The growth here is margin-driven, not volume-driven. Valero's throughput capacity is essentially flat (declining slightly with the California exit). What's changed is the spread between crude input costs (which Valero accesses at Gulf Coast/WTI discounts) and refined product prices (which are elevated globally due to supply disruption). This spread widened dramatically in Q1 2026.

**Structural Growth Catalysts:**
1. St. Charles FCC optimization project ($230M) starting H2 2026 — enhances high-value product yield
2. Post-Benicia portfolio concentration in higher-margin Gulf Coast operations
3. Global refining capacity reductions (California closures, European shutdowns) structurally tighten utilization

**Port Arthur Status:** The Port Arthur refinery experienced a fire on March 23, 2026 in a distillate hydrotreater unit, prompting a full shutdown. As of Valero's Q1 2026 10-Q filing, the refinery had resumed operations at reduced capacity. Q2 2026 Gulf Coast throughput guidance of 1,690–1,740K BPD explicitly reflects anticipated volume reductions related to Port Arthur.

**Next-Year Outlook:**
Consensus expects some deceleration in FY2027 reflecting an expectation that peak margins fade. The direction of estimates depends entirely on conflict duration and crude price trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At current levels, Valero trades at a forward P/E that reflects the market's assessment that current-year earnings power is cyclically elevated. The key valuation question is: what normalized earnings power should this business command?

**Peer Comparison:**
- Marathon Petroleum (MPC): Closest pure-play refining peer
- Phillips 66 (PSX): Mixed refining/chemicals/midstream
- HF Sinclair: Independent refiner

Valero's forward multiple appears below peers, which partly reflects the market's correct assessment that peak-cycle refining earnings are unsustainable long-term, and partly reflects the stock's higher beta to the Hormuz situation.

At mid-cycle (somewhere between 2024's ~$9 EPS and a potential FY2026 peak), a reasonable through-cycle EPS estimate is $14-18, which at 12-14x would yield a fair value range of $168-252. The critical question is whether the current geopolitical environment extends the elevated-margin period beyond what historical mid-cycle analysis would suggest.

I score valuation at 5.5 — not expensive for current earnings power, but requiring margin duration to justify current price levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Valero's competitive position is strengthening on multiple dimensions:

1. **Feedstock access advantage.** Gulf Coast refineries access WTI, Western Canadian Select, and heavy Maya crude at discounts to Brent/Dubai benchmarks. With Middle East crude availability disrupted for European/Asian refiners, this differential advantage has widened substantially.

2. **Complexity advantage.** Valero's refineries are configured to process heavy/sour crudes (which trade at steeper discounts during supply disruptions) and convert them into the highest-value products. Nelson Complexity indices for Port Arthur and Corpus Christi are among the highest in the U.S.

3. **California exit improves portfolio quality.** Removing high-cost, heavily-regulated capacity concentrates remaining operations in structurally favorable markets.

4. **Capacity rationalization supports industry.** Global refinery closures (Valero's California exit, European shutdowns, Russian outages from Ukraine strikes) reduce global capacity while demand remains stable, supporting utilization rates for remaining operators.

5. **Scale in logistics.** Deepwater port access, pipeline connectivity, and storage infrastructure at the Gulf Coast create barriers to new refining entrants in the most favorable crude-access markets.

The competitive position trajectory is positive: gaining margin share through geographic concentration in advantaged basins while competitors face regulatory/capacity headwinds.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geopolitical Resolution Risk (Primary — analyst-estimated low probability, very high impact)**
A diplomatic resolution reopening the Strait of Hormuz would collapse crude differentials and product margins within weeks. Historical precedent: Valero's operating income fell from $15.7B (2022) to $3.8B (2024) when pandemic-era refining margins normalized. A similar 60-70% margin compression from current levels would push EPS back toward $10-12, making the stock expensive at current prices.

**Important context on oil price trajectory:** The EIA's March 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook forecast that Brent would remain above $95/bbl for approximately two months following the Hormuz disruption, then fall below $80/bbl in Q3 2026 and to around $70/bbl by year-end. This is materially less bullish than a sustained "supercycle" framework and suggests the elevated margin environment may be more compressed in duration than the most optimistic scenarios imply.

**2. Renewable Diesel Segment Losses — Recovering but Uncertain**
The DGD JV swung from +$507M (2024) to -$156M (2025) — a $663M negative impact from tariffs on foreign feedstocks and tax credit restructuring. However, Q1 2026 showed a meaningful rebound to $139M operating income (vs. -$141M in Q1 2025), driven by higher product prices and clean fuel production credits. While the segment appears to be recovering, further tariff escalation or regulatory changes could reverse gains.

**3. Earnings Cyclicality and Estimate Volatility**
Refining is among the most cyclical sub-sectors in energy. The same estimate revisions that moved sharply upward can reverse on a single diplomatic headline. The gap between trailing and forward earnings illustrates this cyclical sensitivity.

**4. Port Arthur Operational Risk**
The March 2026 fire and subsequent reduced-capacity operations at Port Arthur (435K BPD, Valero's largest refinery) represent a near-term headwind to throughput and margins. Q2 guidance explicitly reflects this reduction.

**5. Texas Infrastructure Risk**
Heavy concentration in Texas (6 refineries, ~1.6M BPD) exposes Valero to grid scarcity pricing and water supply challenges — both explicitly noted in the 10-K as ongoing issues.

**6. Regulatory/Litigation Overhang**
Climate litigation (Oregon, California), cost-recovery programs (New York, Vermont), and GHG disclosure laws create an accumulating compliance and legal cost burden.

### Options Market Signal

Options market data suggests elevated event risk pricing, with implied volatility significantly above historical levels. - FY2026 EPS reaches $28-32 (above consensus) as refineries run at high utilization
- Share buybacks accelerate with elevated FCF

- Continued aggressive buybacks

## Conclusion

Valero Energy remains a **BUY** with a $280 base case target and $300 bull case. The investment case is grounded in Valero's structural positioning as the world's largest pure-play refiner with Gulf Coast concentration that directly benefits from widened crude differentials during the Hormuz disruption. The sharp Q1 2026 earnings improvement, upward estimate revisions, and 22% share count reduction since 2022 provide tangible evidence of value creation.

The primary risk is binary: a diplomatic resolution of the Iran conflict would compress margins rapidly. Additionally, the EIA's own forecast suggests oil prices may normalize faster than the most bullish supercycle scenarios assume, with Brent potentially falling below $80/bbl by Q3 2026. Position sizing should reflect this asymmetry and the commodity cyclicality — a 2-3% allocation is appropriate. This is a highly event-driven refining-margin trade dependent on geopolitical conflict duration, not a normal-course long-term compounder thesis.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Forward EPS estimates, current share price, forward P/E multiples, and upside/downside calculations could not be fully verified against current consensus data; EIA's oil price forecast is materially less bullish than the report's sustained-supercycle framing; these factors may reduce the attractiveness of the risk/reward at current prices.
