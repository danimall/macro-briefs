**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

American Tower is one of the largest communications-infrastructure REITs globally, owning 131,726 towers, operating another ~17,224 under finance-lease arrangements, and running 30 U.S. data centers (~3.8M net rentable sq ft) through CoreSite. The tower model is structurally attractive: 5–10 year tenant leases with ~3% U.S. escalators and inflation-linked international escalators, very high incremental margins on tower co-location, and over $50 billion of contracted future lease revenue. The investment question is whether the U.S. organic growth slowdown, customer concentration, leverage, and a pending arbitration are adequately priced at a ~28x forward earnings multiple.

The FY25 10-K and Q1 2026 10-Q describe a business where international and data-center segments are doing the work while the legacy U.S. tower base contracts. U.S. & Canada revenue fell 3% in Q1 2026 on DISH churn (now resolved by AMT terminating the contract effective June 2, 2026) and a roll-off of straight-line revenue, even as LatAm grew 20%, Europe 22%, Africa & APAC 13%, and Data Centers 18%. AFFO from continuing operations rose ~10% in FY25, a cleaner read than the FX-distorted headline net income, which jumped 76% in Q1 on non-cash currency translation. Net leverage sits near 5.0x against a 6.0x covenant ceiling, leaving roughly $1.0B of EBITDA cushion and a heavy 2026–2027 refinancing calendar into a higher-rate environment.

The stock has fallen ~16% over the past year and trades at $181, down from a 52-week high of $234. Estimates have been broadly stable (current-year EPS revised +1.4% over 90 days, current-quarter -4.5%), so this is partly multiple compression on rate sensitivity and partly recognition that the U.S. growth driver investors once underwrote (a fourth carrier in DISH) has collapsed. The August 2026 AT&T Mexico arbitration — roughly 3% of revenue — is a genuine binary overhang. We rate AMT HOLD on an income basis: a 3.9% yield with a covered, growing dividend and real rate-cut beta, against capped near-term appreciation given the leverage and unresolved disputes.

## Company Overview

AMT operates across six segments: U.S. & Canada, Africa & APAC, Europe, Latin America, Data Centers, and Services. Property operations generate ~98% of revenue. The core economics rest on multi-tenant towers: the first tenant covers fixed site costs, and each incremental tenant adds revenue at near-100% gross margin. Leases are long-dated with contractual escalators, producing high revenue visibility.

The competitive moat is built on scale, irreplaceable site locations protected by zoning and permitting friction, entrenched master lease agreements with the major U.S. carriers, and a ~108,000-site international footprint that could, in theory, replicate the U.S. densification cycle over a longer horizon. CoreSite adds an interconnection-rich U.S. data-center platform positioned for hybrid-cloud and early-stage AI/inference demand. Steven Vondran is CEO; Rodney Smith is CFO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and segment mix.** FY2025 revenue was $10,644.6M, up 5% from FY24's $10,127.2M; FY23's higher $11,144.2M included the since-divested India business. Q1 2026 revenue was $2,737.5M, up 7% YoY. The international and data-center segments are carrying the consolidated growth rate; the U.S. tower base, historically the engine, is in modest contraction.

**Profitability and cash flow.** FY25 Adjusted EBITDA was $7,130M (+5%), AFFO to common $5,041.6M (+2% reported, +10% from continuing operations after stripping divestiture effects), operating cash flow $5,464M, and net income $2,628.5M. Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was $1,835.2M (+5%), AFFO $1,324.1M (+3%), diluted EPS $1.84 versus $1.04 a year earlier. The EPS jump is overwhelmingly FX-driven and does not reflect operational acceleration.

**FX distorts the GAAP optics.** FY25 booked $809M of non-cash FX losses (largely on intercompany notes), versus $308M of gains in FY24. With 34% of revenue and 42% of opex foreign-denominated, translation swings dominate reported income. The 20% FY25 decline in Nareit FFO is mostly the FY24 base effect (a $1.2B India exit loss) plus FX, not operating deterioration. In June 2025 AMT designated ~€4.7B (~$5.4B) of senior notes as a net-investment hedge to dampen future translation volatility. AFFO from continuing operations remains the cleaner operating gauge.

**Balance sheet — leverage, not the screens.** Total debt was $37.5B at Q1 2026. The standardized metrics mislead: D/E shows ~14.5x only because GAAP equity is a thin $3.65B (retained earnings are -$5.09B from years of REIT distributions exceeding GAAP earnings), and the Altman Z of 21.69 is an asset-turnover artifact. Neither is a genuine distress signal for a tower REIT. The metric that matters is net leverage near 5.0x Adjusted EBITDA against a 6.0x covenant ceiling, roughly $1.0B of EBITDA cushion. The current portion of debt jumped to $6.1B at Q1 2026, reflecting heavy 2026–2027 maturities being termed out through revolvers and new senior notes. Reported liquidity of ~$10.4B is overwhelmingly undrawn revolver capacity plus ~$1.6B cash. With effectively all debt unsecured and investment-grade-dependent, rating maintenance is critical to the refinancing path, and rising rates lift interest expense (Q1 cash interest ~$435M).

**Capital allocation.** FY25 dividend was $6.72/share (~$3.1B), raised to $1.79/quarter (~$3.3B expected FY26), a ~3.9% yield well covered by AFFO. A $2B buyback is active (~$550M done through April 2026, ~$1.5B remaining), a newer lever for AMT. 2026 capex is guided to $1.8–1.91B, including ~$695M for data centers. After distributions and capex, discretionary free capital is modest, which keeps growth tethered to continued debt access.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters. Q1 2026 beat by 13.6%, Q4 2025 by 17.2%, Q3 2025 by 9.6%; the Q2 2025 "miss" of -53.6% was an FX/one-time-driven optical event rather than operational. The beat cadence supports confidence in near-term AFFO estimates, though estimate revisions have been flat-to-slightly-negative on the near-term quarters (current quarter -4.5% over 90 days).

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile has bifurcated, and our own assessment reflects it: growth prospects in our framework have stepped down from 7.0 earlier this year to 5.7 now, and the new data confirm rather than reverse that decline. The U.S. tower base, which underpins the highest-margin, lowest-risk revenue, contracted 3% in Q1 2026. The DISH collapse removed a tenant investors had underwritten as a fourth-carrier growth source; EchoStar/DISH is now selling spectrum and abandoning its 5G build. With T-Mobile, AT&T, and Verizon disciplined on capex and a roll-off of straight-line revenue, U.S. organic growth is likely to stay low-single-digit at best, carried mostly by the ~3% escalators.

The offsetting engines are international and data centers. The international segments are growing off lower bases with inflation-linked escalators and ongoing densification, though they carry FX, EM regulatory, and repatriation risks and have been the subject of active portfolio pruning (India, Australia/NZ, South Africa fiber exited; Bangladesh under review). CoreSite is the cleaner secular story — interconnection-rich U.S. data centers with hybrid-cloud and early AI/inference demand — but it is power-constrained with 12–18 month build lead times before revenue, and it is a small share of the consolidated base. The Stonepeak mandatorily convertible preferred converts around August 2026, lifting AMT's CoreSite economic stake to ~64% and ending the 5% preferred dividend drag, a modest structural positive.

Consolidated AFFO/share growth is likely to run mid-single-digit, with international and data centers compensating for U.S. stagnation. This is durable but not an accelerating trajectory that warrants a premium re-rating. Estimate revisions confirm the muted setup: current-year EPS up only 1.4% over 90 days, next-year flat. There is no +30% revision velocity here, so the strongest momentum BUY signal in our framework is absent.

## Valuation Assessment

At $181, AMT trades at ~28x forward earnings, with a 3.9% dividend yield. Against the Real Estate peer set, the headline P/E comparison is less informative because the cohort is heterogeneous: Simon Property at 14.9x, Digital Realty 48.8x, Equinix 72.1x, Realty Income 50.7x, Public Storage 33.5x (peer median 48.8x). AMT screens cheap versus that median, but the relevant comparables for a tower REIT are SBA and Crown Castle, not storage or net-lease REITs, and tower multiples have compressed across the board on rate sensitivity and the U.S. carrier-capex slowdown.

The valuation case is balanced rather than asymmetric. On the bullish side: the stock is down ~16% over twelve months and ~23% off its 52-week high while fundamentals (AFFO from continuing operations +10%, broad international growth, a covered and rising dividend) are intact, so the risk/reward has improved with the lower price. The yield provides absolute support, and AMT carries genuine rate-cut beta, since Fed easing would compress its cost of debt and lift the multiple. Analyst targets sit well above the current price (low $195, median $210, mean $216, high $260), and Bernstein's May upgrade explicitly argued the market is overstating the risks.

The skepticism is warranted, though. The >20% drawdown from the high means sophisticated investors have been selling, and the burden is on the bull case to identify what they are missing. The answer is mixed: part of the decline is rate-driven multiple compression that reverses if the Fed eases (a genuine mispricing), but part reflects the real loss of the U.S. fourth-carrier growth thesis and a building AT&T Mexico reserve, which is fundamental deterioration that justifies a lower multiple than AMT carried in its growth years. We do not treat the ~28x multiple as a value-trap discount, but neither does it offer the kind of catalyst-backed asymmetry that supports a high valuation score. A 6.0 reflects yield support and rate-cut optionality offset by the structural U.S. growth question and the binary arbitration overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

AMT competes with Crown Castle and SBA Communications in towers and with data-center REITs and hyperscalers in CoreSite. Its differentiation versus Crown Castle is global diversification (Crown is U.S.-only and burdened by a troubled fiber/small-cell strategy) and a U.S. interconnection-focused data-center platform. The moat is real: irreplaceable sites, permitting friction, entrenched master lease agreements, and scale economics on co-location.

The competitive trajectory has softened, which is why our competitive-position score has eased from 8.5 to 7.0 over recent reviews and we hold it at 6.8 now. The level of the moat remains high, but the trajectory matters more. The U.S. carrier base is consolidating and disciplined, the hoped-for fourth carrier collapsed, and RAN-sharing among carriers is a structural threat to tower-count demand. Customer concentration is extreme — 59% of revenue from four customers (T-Mobile 18%, AT&T 17%, Verizon 14%, Telefónica 10%) — which limits AMT's pricing leverage in U.S. renewals. The offsetting positive is international densification and CoreSite's interconnection moat. The net trajectory is a stable-to-modestly-eroding U.S. position offset by improving international and data-center positions.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration.** 59% of revenue from four customers. Carrier consolidation, RAN sharing, or financial stress at any one directly impairs revenue and pricing power.

**DISH default — resolved by termination.** DISH defaulted on its 2021 Strategic Collocation Agreement in January 2026; AMT terminated the SCA effective June 2, 2026. DISH was ~2% of total / ~4% of U.S. revenue, and 100% of its revenue moved into churn from January 1, 2026, pushing Q1 churn to ~5% versus the normal ~2%. The termination removes recurring revenue but caps the downside and signals AMT saw no recoverable value. It also stands as a cautionary data point on carrier credit within a concentrated base.

**AT&T Mexico arbitration — the key binary.** Roughly $300M of annual tenant revenue (~3% of total) is in dispute, with AT&T Mexico challenging the master-lease calculation and seeking retroactive and prospective rent abatement. AMT has reserved $30M (FY25) plus $10M (Q1 2026) and expects more. The hearing is scheduled for August 2026, a dated, asymmetric event that the market has not fully resolved.

**Leverage and refinancing.** $37.5B debt, ~5.0x net leverage with ~$1.0B EBITDA cushion to the 6.0x covenant, $6.1B of current maturities, and refinancing into higher rates. Manageable for an investment-grade REIT, but rate-sensitive and dependent on rating maintenance.

**International/EM exposure.** Currency devaluation, repatriation friction, and regulatory/tax retroactivity across Africa and LatAm, plus ongoing portfolio rationalization (Bangladesh under review) creating impairment risk.

**Data-center execution.** Power availability, long build lead times, and competition from better-capitalized hyperscale operators.

These risks are partly but not fully priced. The combination of the August arbitration binary, the concentration, and the thin covenant cushion warrants a below-average risk-factor score of 5.0, consistent with the downward step from 6.0 over recent reviews.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 27.1% versus 1-year historical volatility of 24.4%, a fair spread, with the IV percentile within the chain at the 2nd percentile — options are not pricing elevated near-term stress. Put/call skew is balanced (0.0%), and the term structure is flat (near 27.1% ≈ far 28.0%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a single event. The put/call OI ratio of 1.50 leans cautious but is consistent with normal income-REIT hedging. The flagged spike in the options-delta feed (ATM IV to 241.3%) is a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. The options market is neither confirming acute downside fear nor signaling a near-term catalyst; the August arbitration sits beyond the front-month window. Short interest is low at 2.2% of float with 3.1 days to cover, so there is no crowded-positioning signal in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$210–240).** Fed easing compresses AMT's cost of debt and re-rates the multiple; international densification and CoreSite sustain mid-single-digit consolidated AFFO/share growth; the AT&T Mexico arbitration resolves with limited incremental impairment; the Stonepeak conversion lifts CoreSite economics; and the 3.9% growing yield plus buyback support total return. Bernstein's $207 and Raymond James's $240 targets anchor this scenario. Implies roughly +16% to +33% from current.

**Bear case (~$150).** Rates stay higher for longer, lifting interest expense against a thin covenant cushion; U.S. tower growth stays flat-to-negative as carriers stay disciplined and RAN-sharing erodes demand; the AT&T Mexico arbitration goes against AMT with material retroactive abatement; and EM currency/regulatory shocks pressure the international engine. Implies roughly -17% from current.

The base case sits between: mid-single-digit AFFO/share growth, a stable-to-modestly-higher multiple as rates ease, and the dividend carrying a meaningful share of total return. This is a balanced setup with capped near-term upside given the leverage and the unresolved arbitration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** Own AMT for its covered, growing 3.9% yield and rate-cut beta, not for asymmetric appreciation. The recommendation history (HOLD → BUY → HOLD) and the score decline from 6.8 to 5.9 reflect the May BUY thesis losing force as the U.S. growth driver weakened and the AT&T Mexico reserve built; we are returning to HOLD because the new data confirm a balanced rather than asymmetric setup.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AT&T Mexico arbitration resolves favorably (limited or no incremental abatement) at or after the August 2026 hearing, removing the revenue uncertainty.
- Clear evidence of a Fed easing cycle compressing AMT's cost of debt and lifting AFFO/share guidance, OR the stock pulling back toward $160 (~4.2% yield) while fundamentals hold, restoring asymmetry.
- U.S. & Canada organic growth returning to positive YoY for two consecutive quarters.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net leverage breaching ~5.5x or a credit-rating downgrade threatening unsecured debt access.
- AT&T Mexico arbitration loss with material retroactive abatement, or a second major-customer credit event.
- Dividend coverage deteriorating such that AFFO payout exceeds ~70%, or a guided dividend freeze.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are Q2 2026 earnings (late July), the August 2026 AT&T Mexico hearing, and the ~August 2026 Stonepeak preferred conversion. The arbitration outcome is the single most important near-term input.

## Conclusion

American Tower is a high-quality, wide-moat communications-infrastructure REIT whose growth engine has shifted from a stagnating U.S. tower base to international densification and the CoreSite data-center platform. The FY25 and Q1 2026 results confirm broad international momentum offsetting a U.S. decline driven by the DISH collapse and straight-line roll-off. The balance sheet is leveraged at ~5.0x net with a thin covenant cushion and a heavy refinancing calendar, making the stock rate-sensitive in both directions, and the August 2026 AT&T Mexico arbitration is a genuine binary on ~3% of revenue.

At ~28x forward earnings with a covered, growing 3.9% yield, the risk/reward is balanced. The twelve-month decline has improved the entry point and the yield support, and Fed easing would provide a clear re-rating path, but the structural loss of the U.S. fourth-carrier growth thesis and the unresolved arbitration cap near-term appreciation. We rate AMT HOLD on an income basis, with an upgrade contingent on a favorable arbitration outcome and clearer evidence of falling financing costs.
