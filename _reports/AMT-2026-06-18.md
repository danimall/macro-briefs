**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

American Tower owns one of the few genuinely irreplaceable real-asset portfolios in the public market: roughly 149,000 communications sites across 20+ countries plus 30 CoreSite data centers, generating recurring revenue under multiyear non-cancellable leases with built-in escalators and over $50 billion of contracted future lease revenue. The business model is high-quality: property operations are 98% of revenue, incremental colocation margins are strong, and the dividend (just raised to $1.79/quarter) is covered by AFFO. This is the kind of asset that deserves a place in an income-oriented portfolio at the right price.

The complication is that the growth engine has slowed and the company's character has shifted. Headline Q1 2026 revenue rose 7% YoY to $2,737.5M, but the truer operating metrics, Adjusted EBITDA +5% and AFFO +3%, show deceleration from AMT's historical double-digit cadence. The U.S. & Canada segment, the mature cash cow, actually declined 3% as DISH churn and lower straight-line revenue bit. Reported net income jumped 76% to $878.5M, but that is almost entirely a $400M+ swing in foreign-currency gains/losses, not operational improvement. Growth now leans on FX-vulnerable emerging markets (Europe +22%, LatAm +20%, much of it currency translation) and the CoreSite data center business, which inflected to positive segment gross margin on AI/cloud demand.

Two unresolved customer disputes and a refinancing wall keep this a HOLD rather than a buy. DISH is in formal default as of January 2026 with 100% of its revenue in churn; AT&T Mexico (~$300M of 2025 tenant revenue, larger than DISH) is challenging the fundamental rent-calculation methodology of its Master Lease Agreement, with an arbitration hearing set for August 2026. An adverse ruling on the latter could permanently re-price a $300M stream and set a precedent. Layered on top is $37.5B of debt with $6.1B current and a portfolio of sub-2% legacy notes refinancing into current-market coupons. The ~4% dividend yield, P/E discount to peer Crown Castle, and management's buyback-plus-insider-purchase signal support owning the shares; the binary disputes and interest-expense drag cap the appreciation case until 2026 resolves.

## Company Overview

American Tower is one of the world's largest REITs and the leading independent owner-operator of multitenant communications real estate. The core economic engine is leasing vertical space on towers (and rooftops, DAS, and small-cell sites) to wireless carriers, broadcasters, and government agencies under long-term contracts.

As of 3/31/2026 the portfolio comprised approximately 131,700 owned towers, 17,200 operated towers, and 860 owned DAS sites. The geographic split: U.S. & Canada ~41,800 sites, Latin America ~44,600 (Brazil 20,800, Mexico 8,600), Europe ~32,400 (Germany, Spain, France), and Africa & APAC ~27,900. The CoreSite data center segment adds 30 facilities across 11 U.S. markets totaling ~3.8M net rentable square feet, a deliberate diversification away from pure tower leasing into the cloud/AI compute supply chain.

The model's durability comes from contract structure: 5–10 year initial lease terms with multiple renewals, ~3% fixed annual escalators in the U.S. and inflation-linked escalators internationally, and very low churn under normal conditions. Adding a second or third tenant to an existing tower carries minimal incremental cost, so colocation drives high operating leverage. The flip side is heavy customer concentration (T-Mobile ~18%, AT&T ~17%, Verizon ~14%, and Telefónica ~10%), which is the structural vulnerability of the entire tower industry.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results (three months ended 3/31/2026):**
- Total revenue: $2,737.5M, +7% YoY (vs $2,562.8M)
- Net income: $878.5M, +76% YoY, driven by a $68.1M FX gain vs a $345.7M FX loss in the prior-year quarter, not operations
- Diluted EPS: $1.84 vs $1.04
- Adjusted EBITDA: $1,835.2M, +5%
- AFFO attributable to common: $1,324.1M, +3%; Nareit FFO $1,278.9M
- Operating cash flow: $1,400.6M, +8%

The segment detail tells the real story. U.S. & Canada revenue declined 3% to $1,261.6M (from $1,298.3M), dragged by $25.2M of DISH churn and lower straight-line revenue. This is the mature, high-margin core now under pressure. International is carrying the growth: Europe +22%, Latin America +20%, Africa & APAC +13%, but a large share is FX translation rather than organic (LatAm's $80.9M of growth included $50.8M of FX tailwind). Data Centers grew +18% and inflected from a -$2.3M segment gross margin a year ago to +$27.2M, as CoreSite scales on AI/cloud-driven commitments, power, and interconnection demand.

**Balance sheet — the central concern.** Total debt is $37.5B, with the current portion rising to $6.1B (from $3.4B at YE2025), signaling meaningful near-term maturities. Total equity is only $10.2B, of which AMT stockholders' equity is just $3.5B and the remainder is $6.6B of noncontrolling interests, a real claim on consolidated cash flows. Reported D/E runs ~12–14x and the current ratio sits near 0.3–0.4x. These figures are extreme on their face but typical for a capital-intensive REIT with revolver access; the more relevant gauge is the Consolidated Total Leverage Ratio covenant limit of 6.00x, against which AMT retains roughly $6B of additional debt capacity / ~$1B of EBITDA cushion, with Senior Secured Leverage well within its 3.00x limit. Liquidity is $10.4B ($8.8B available under credit facilities plus $1.6B cash). About $8.7B of debt is EUR-denominated, with $5.4B designated as a net-investment hedge against European operations.

**The refinancing dynamic is a persistent profitability drag.** AMT is refinancing 2026 maturities largely through floating-rate revolver borrowings; it repaid $500M of 4.400% notes in February and $700M of 1.600% notes in April using credit-facility draws. Dozens of senior notes issued at sub-2% coupons during the low-rate era mature 2026–2030; each refinancing at current market rates raises interest expense, which already climbed 7% YoY to $347M. Liquidity is not a near-term solvency question given covenant headroom and revolver capacity, but the rolling repricing of cheap legacy debt is a structural headwind to AFFO growth in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Capital allocation** is balanced and shareholder-aware. The dividend was raised to $1.79/quarter (~$834M/quarter), covered by AFFO though consuming the bulk of it. AMT reactivated its buyback ($183.7M, 1.05M shares in Q1'26, ~$1.5B remaining under a $2B authorization), which is unusual for a leveraged REIT and signals management's view that the ~$170s share price is undervalued. 2026 capex guidance of $1.8B–$1.9B is weighted toward growth, including ~$695M for data centers and 1,700–2,300 new sites.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile has shifted from broad-based double-digit expansion to a mid-single-digit operating cadence with a changed mix. Adjusted EBITDA +5% and AFFO +3% are the metrics to anchor on; the revenue and net income headlines are flattered by FX. The durable demand drivers remain intact (5G densification, mobile data growth, and AI/cloud compute for CoreSite) but they are no longer translating into the historical growth rate.

Three forces define the forward trajectory. First, the U.S. core is contracting (-3% in Q1), a structural change as the domestic market saturates and DISH churn flows through. Second, international markets offer a longer runway because they sit earlier on the wireless-data adoption curve, but the reported growth is partly an FX illusion that reverses when emerging-market currencies (Naira, Real) depreciate; 34% of revenue and 42% of opex are in foreign currencies. Third, CoreSite is the genuine new growth optionality: the segment's margin inflection on AI/cloud demand is real and confirmed across the data center supply chain, and AMT's data-center capex positions it to participate. The Stonepeak preferred conversion (~August 2026) will dilute AMT's CoreSite ownership to ~64%, a structural offset as the asset becomes more valuable.

Net assessment: the growth thesis is solid but decelerating, with the most credible incremental upside concentrated in data centers rather than the legacy tower core. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here; this is a steady compounder, not an accelerating one.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$170s the shares trade at a forward P/E of roughly 29.1x, a discount to peer Crown Castle (~44.2x) and the broad REIT benchmark XLRE (~33.3x). For a REIT, P/E is a coarse metric; the more relevant lens is AFFO-based valuation and the dividend yield, which at ~$7.16 annualized against the share price supports an approximately 4% yield with a track record of increases.

The discount to CCI is justified and partly informative. AMT is more diversified geographically and carries data-center optionality, while CCI is a U.S. pure-play mid-turnaround with even more extreme tenant concentration (top three carriers ~90% of site rental revenue) and its own DISH exposure. AMT being cheaper than a structurally weaker peer is not a strong standalone buy argument; cheapness relative to a troubled comparable does not establish mispricing.

The case for the shares being modestly undervalued rests on the contracted cash-flow backstop, the dividend yield support, and management's willingness to buy back stock at this level alongside a director's open-market purchase at $185.30. The case against paying up is the deceleration to mid-single-digit AFFO growth, the interest-expense drag that will compress AFFO per share growth as cheap debt reprices, and two binary disputes that could permanently impair revenue. A cheap multiple here reflects real, identifiable headwinds (refinancing, U.S. core contraction, dispute overhangs) rather than a market error. The valuation is fair-to-slightly-attractive for an income holder, not asymmetric enough to justify a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

American Tower's competitive position is strong and largely stable, with a slight erosion at the margin. The owned-tower portfolio is functionally irreplaceable: zoning, permitting, and the economics of multitenant verticals create high barriers to new supply, and the switching costs for carriers (network re-engineering, coverage gaps) make incumbency durable. Against Crown Castle, AMT is the better-positioned global operator: broader geographic diversification reduces single-market regulatory and carrier risk, and the CoreSite data center business gives it AI/cloud exposure that pure tower peers lack.

The trajectory is slightly negative for three reasons. First, the U.S. market is saturating and the core segment is shrinking, a competitive-environment headwind shared across the industry but real nonetheless. Second, the customer disputes reveal that even contracted lease economics can be contested by financially stressed or aggressive tenants; the AT&T Mexico challenge to the rent-calculation methodology is the more concerning because it attacks the pricing framework itself. Third, longer-term technological substitution (satellite direct-to-device from Starlink and peers, and network virtualization) is a genuine if distant threat to tower demand density. None of these reverses the moat in the near term, but they justify a competitive-position score below the prior 8.0–8.5 reads.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration** is the defining risk. Four tenants make up the majority of revenue; a carrier capex pullback, consolidation, or default has outsized impact.

**DISH default (active).** On 9/24/2025 DISH purported to be excused from its obligations under the 2021 Strategic Collocation Agreement and is in default as of January 2026. AMT filed for declaratory judgment in the U.S. District Court (Colorado) on 10/20/2025. DISH was ~2% of total property revenue / ~4% of U.S. revenue; 100% of its revenue is now in churn as of 1/1/2026, and AMT recorded $17.5M of DISH impairments in Q1'26, the primary cause of the U.S. segment decline.

**AT&T Mexico arbitration (active, hearing August 2026).** AT&T Mexico is seeking retroactive and prospective rent abatement and withholding rents through 2025. A September 2025 interim agreement placed disputed amounts in escrow. AMT booked ~$30M of reserves in 2025 plus ~$10M in Q1'26 and expects continued reserves until resolution. This is the more dangerous of the two because an adverse ruling could permanently re-price the stream and establish a methodology precedent across the Mexican portfolio.

**Leverage and refinancing.** Current debt is refinanced largely via floating-rate revolver draws against a backdrop of rising rates repricing sub-2% legacy notes. Solvency is not the concern given covenant headroom and revolver capacity; AFFO-per-share compression is.

**FX.** Reported earnings are volatile (the entire Q1 net income "growth" was an FX swing), and emerging-market currency depreciation is a structural drag on USD results.

**Technological substitution.** Satellite direct-to-device and network virtualization are long-term threats to tower demand, low-probability over a 12-month horizon but worth monitoring.

**Governance.** A cluster of Item 5.02 executive/director filings across 2025–2026 (including elimination of the EVP/President APAC role) and a complex minority-partner structure (ATC Europe 52%-owned, CoreSite ~71%-owned with Stonepeak's preferred converting ~August 2026) warrant attention but appear to reflect normal restructuring rather than instability. Per the Q1 10-Q, there were no material changes to risk factors from the 2025 10-K.

The risk score sits at 5.0 because two specific, dated, binary disputes carry asymmetric downside that the current valuation does not fully reflect; the AT&T Mexico August 2026 hearing in particular is a genuine binary event on a $300M revenue stream.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$210, +~24% from ~$170).** Both customer disputes resolve favorably or with limited permanent impairment: DISH churn is fully absorbed by 2026 and AT&T Mexico settles near current escrowed terms. CoreSite's AI/cloud-driven margin inflection accelerates and the Stonepeak conversion crystallizes a higher data-center valuation. International organic growth proves more durable than the FX-flattered headline suggests, and the dividend continues compounding ~5%. The multiple re-rates toward the REIT-sector benchmark (~33x) as the dispute overhangs clear. The contracted lease backstop plus the buyback provides a floor and management's confidence signal is validated.

**Bear Case (~$130, -~24% from ~$170).** The AT&T Mexico arbitration rules adversely in August 2026, permanently re-pricing the $300M stream and setting a methodology precedent that pressures other emerging-market leases. The U.S. core continues contracting beyond DISH as carrier capex stays disciplined. Rates stay higher for longer, and the rolling refinancing of sub-2% legacy notes into 5%+ coupons compresses AFFO per share, pressuring dividend-growth capacity. Emerging-market currency depreciation accelerates, turning the FX tailwind into a headwind. The multiple de-rates as a growth-challenged, dispute-burdened, highly leveraged REIT.

**Base Case (~$185, +~9% from ~$170).** Operating growth holds at mid-single digits, disputes resolve with manageable but real reserves, the dividend grows ~5%, and the shares deliver a ~4% yield plus modest appreciation. Total return is reasonable but not asymmetric, a fair return for owning a high-quality contracted-cash-flow asset, with the binary disputes and refinancing drag preventing a stronger call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **HOLD** on an **income** basis. The thesis is that AMT delivers a ~4% covered dividend plus mid-single-digit growth from a defensive, contracted-cash-flow asset, while two binary 2026 disputes and a refinancing headwind keep near-term appreciation capped.

**Reassessment timeline:** The August 2026 AT&T Mexico arbitration hearing is the decisive near-term catalyst; the Stonepeak preferred conversion and the DISH litigation progress are secondary. The next earnings releases (Q2 and Q3 2026) will show whether U.S. core contraction stabilizes and whether CoreSite margin expansion sustains.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AT&T Mexico arbitration resolves with limited permanent impairment (settlement near escrowed terms or favorable ruling), removing the largest binary overhang
- AFFO-per-share growth re-accelerates above ~5% with U.S. & Canada returning to positive segment revenue growth
- Shares pull back toward the low-$150s on no fundamental deterioration, restoring an asymmetric yield-plus-growth entry (a ~4.5%+ yield with the disputes priced)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse AT&T Mexico ruling that permanently re-prices the $300M stream and signals methodology precedent across the international portfolio
- Dividend coverage erodes (AFFO payout pushing toward unsustainable levels) or a dividend cut, which would invalidate the income thesis
- Covenant cushion narrows materially (Consolidated Total Leverage approaching the 6.00x limit) or refinancing terms deteriorate sharply, signaling the rate headwind is overwhelming AFFO

## Conclusion

American Tower is a high-quality, defensively-positioned global real-asset franchise with an irreplaceable portfolio, $50B+ of contracted future lease revenue, and a sustainable, growing ~4% dividend. Those attributes justify owning the shares for income and stability. They do not, at ~$170s, justify a BUY, because the operating growth has decelerated to mid-single digits, the U.S. core is shrinking, FX is flattering the reported numbers, $37.5B of debt with $6.1B current faces a persistent repricing headwind, and two specific customer disputes (DISH default and the larger AT&T Mexico arbitration with its August 2026 hearing) put a combined revenue stream at binary risk (AT&T Mexico ~$300M of 2025 tenant revenue; DISH ~2% of total property revenue). The trajectory has improved on valuation (the prior reads correctly captured the discount to CCI), but the competitive position has softened at the margin and the risk profile is sharpened by the dated disputes. The earlier upgrade to BUY runs ahead of a risk/reward that is, on balance, fair rather than asymmetric.

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** Own AMT for the covered, growing dividend and the contracted cash-flow backstop. Reassess for an upgrade after the August 2026 AT&T Mexico hearing or on a pullback toward the low-$150s; downgrade on an adverse arbitration ruling or any erosion in dividend coverage.
