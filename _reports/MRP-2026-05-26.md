## Executive Summary

Millrose Properties is a novel publicly traded land banking REIT spun off from Lennar Corporation in February 2025. The company earns a ~9.2% weighted average yield on $8.7B of invested capital by purchasing residential land, funding horizontal development, and granting homebuilders exclusive purchase options on finished homesites. At $27.67, the stock trades at approximately 9.1x Q1 2026 annualized AFFO (~11.3x FY2025 trailing GAAP P/E) and offers an ~11% dividend yield — while generating Q1 2026 operating margins of ~85% with zero option terminations since inception.

The discount reflects legitimate concerns: 88% revenue concentration in Lennar (FY2025), Lennar's extraordinary contractual control rights, external management with potential conflicts, and the uncertainty of how a novel asset class performs through a full housing cycle. However, three factors suggest the discount is excessive: (1) diversification is proceeding rapidly, with approximately 31% of capital now deployed outside the Lennar Master Program Agreement and 17 counterparties established; (2) CEO Darren Richman made $6.4M in open-market purchases at near-current prices in May 2026; (3) covering analysts reportedly rate the stock Buy with targets in the $35-40 range, implying 27-45% upside.

The primary thesis is that the market is applying a "show-me" discount to a genuinely novel, high-yield asset with structural protections (cross-termination pooling, non-refundable deposits) that have held up during a soft housing market. As the track record lengthens and Lennar concentration declines, re-rating toward a more appropriate multiple is probable.

## Company Overview

Millrose Properties operates as the only publicly traded, large-scale land banking REIT. The business model is straightforward: acquire entitled residential land, fund horizontal development (roads, utilities, grading), and grant homebuilders exclusive options to purchase finished homesites at predetermined prices and schedules. Revenue comes from three streams: (1) non-refundable option deposits at contract inception, (2) monthly recurring option fees (the primary revenue source, effectively a yield on invested capital), and (3) takedown proceeds when builders exercise purchase options (capital recycled into new acquisitions).

The company was incorporated in Maryland on March 19, 2024 and spun off from Lennar on February 7, 2025. As of December 31, 2025, the portfolio comprised approximately 142,000 homesites across 933 communities in 30 states. Millrose is externally managed by Kennedy Lewis Land and Residential Advisors (affiliate of Kennedy Lewis Investment Management, ~$33B AUM). Millrose has no employees — all operations are performed by the Manager for a 1.25% annual fee on tangible assets.

As of Q1 2026, invested capital stands at $8.7B, counterparties have expanded to 17 homebuilders, and approximately 31% of capital is deployed outside the Lennar Master Program Agreement (up from ~12% in early FY2025).

## Growth Analysis

Growth for MRP comes from three vectors: (1) deploying additional capital at the 9-11% yield range, (2) diversifying beyond Lennar into higher-yielding counterparty relationships (10.7% vs. 8.5%), and (3) the structural tailwind of builders moving toward asset-light strategies.

**Capital Deployment:** Invested capital grew from $8.5B to $8.7B in Q1 2026. The potential Dream Finders/Beazer transaction, if it proceeds to closing, represents an incremental deployment opportunity. The expanded $1.835B credit facility provides capacity for additional acquisitions without equity dilution.

**Diversification as Growth:** Non-Lennar business yields approximately 10.7% vs. Lennar's 8.5%. Each dollar shifted from Lennar to other counterparties is approximately 220bp accretive. Moving from 12% non-Lennar to 31% in less than a year signals management is executing.

**Industry Tailwind:** The secular trend toward "land-light" homebuilding benefits MRP structurally. Builders increasingly separate land ownership from construction to improve ROE. As the only public, perpetual-capital option for this function, MRP captures a growing addressable market.

**Estimate trajectory:** According to consensus estimates (per financial data providers), FY2026 EPS is projected at approximately $3.10, growing to approximately $3.23 in FY2027 (+4.2%). These estimates appear conservative relative to the Q1 annualized run rate. Estimate revisions have been marginally negative near-term but positive longer-term, suggesting stable fundamentals.

This appears inexpensive relative to the company's yield characteristics, though direct peer comparisons are challenging given Millrose's unique business model — the company's own 10-K acknowledges a lack of publicly traded peers, which may contribute to stock price volatility.

**Specific reasons the market may be mispricing this stock:**

1. **Novel asset class discount:** The market has no precedent for pricing a land banking REIT and is applying the lowest possible multiple pending proof of concept through a full cycle. Zero terminations since the February 2025 spin-off is encouraging, but Millrose has operated independently for only approximately 15 months — too short to constitute full-cycle validation.

## Competitive Landscape

Millrose occupies a unique position as the only publicly traded, large-scale land banking REIT. Its competitors are private investment funds (DW Partners, Blackstone Credit affiliates) that operate with fund-life constraints (typically 7-10 year vehicles that must return capital), higher cost of capital, and more limited geographic footprint.

**Competitive advantages:**
1. **Perpetual capital:** Unlike fund-life-constrained private vehicles, Millrose redeploys takedown proceeds continuously, providing builders reliable long-term capital access.
2. **Scale:** 142,000+ homesites, 933 communities, 30 states — difficult to replicate quickly.
3. **Lower cost of capital:** As a public REIT accessing public equity and investment-grade debt markets (~6.3% average coupon), Millrose likely offers builders better economics than private fund alternatives that are estimated to require substantially higher returns.
4. **Cross-termination pooling (96% pooled):** A builder walking away from one community risks forfeiting deposits across the entire pool, creating powerful incentive alignment, though specific cure provisions exist in certain circumstances.

**Vulnerability:** The competitive advantage is partially dependent on the favorable terms established during the Lennar spin-off. Replicating this with new counterparties at similar economics requires the continued secular shift toward asset-light building models. The ~10.7% yield earned on non-Lennar agreements vs. 8.5% on Lennar suggests newer relationships require premium compensation — the builder market is rational about pricing.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Lennar Concentration (Highest Priority):** 88% of FY2025 option fee revenue from one counterparty. Lennar's Founder's Rights include pause periods (6 months at 50% payments), capital priority claims, and most-favored-nation rate provisions. A Lennar financial crisis would be devastating. Mitigation: Lennar has no equity ownership interest in MRP (disposed of remaining ownership via exchange offer in November 2025), though it retains substantial contractual and economic interests as MRP's largest counterparty and holder of Founder's Rights. Diversification proceeding rapidly (~31% non-Lennar Master Program Agreement capital by Q1 2026).

2. **Housing Cyclicality:** In a severe downturn, builders could elect not to exercise options. Deposits are non-refundable (providing some downside buffer), but MRP would be left holding land with potentially impaired value. Mitigation: Zero terminations through current soft market conditions; cross-termination pooling increases walk-away cost.

3. **External Management Conflicts:** Kennedy Lewis manages competing private land banking vehicles. The allocation policy allows alternation of opportunities between MRP and private funds. The 1.25% asset-based fee creates incentive to grow assets regardless of return quality. Mitigation: CEO's $6.4M personal investment aligns interests.

4. **Interest Rate Sensitivity:** Higher mortgage rates suppress housing demand and builder willingness to take down homesites. Debt service is manageable (6.3% average coupon, no maturities until 2030) but new acquisitions funded at higher rates compress net yield.

5. **Dual-Class Governance:** Miller Family controls ~43% of total votes via 10:1 Class B structure, limiting minority shareholder influence.

6. **Limited Operating History:** Millrose has operated as an independent public company only since February 2025. The company itself warns that investors have a limited basis to evaluate its standalone performance through a full housing cycle.

## Conclusion

Millrose Properties presents a compelling risk/reward at current levels. The stock trades at approximately 9.1x Q1 2026 annualized AFFO, offers an ~11% dividend yield supported by growing AFFO, and sits meaningfully below reported analyst targets — all while the CEO invested $6.4M of personal capital at essentially the same price. The concentrated Lennar relationship, novel business model, and limited operating history justify some discount, but zero terminations since inception, rapid diversification, and structural competitive advantages suggest the current discount is excessive.

The primary risk is the housing cycle: with mortgage rates elevated, housing demand faces sustained headwinds that could eventually pressure builders' willingness to exercise options. This risk is partially offset by the cross-termination structure and the secular trend toward asset-light building models, which benefits MRP regardless of cycle positioning.

At ~9.1x AFFO with demonstrated insider conviction and favorable analyst sentiment, the asymmetric setup favors buyers. The stock should trade at 11-12x AFFO within 12 months as the market gains confidence in the model's durability, yielding $34-36 plus substantial dividend income.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The peer-multiple discount claim (68% discount to 30.8x peer P/E) relies on an unvalidated peer set that may not be directly comparable to MRP's unique land-banking model; the company's own filings note a lack of public company peers. Additionally, the "proof through cycle" argument is based on only ~15 months of independent operating history since the February 2025 spin-off. These factors may reduce the confidence level of the base-case re-rating thesis.
