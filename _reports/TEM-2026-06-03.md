**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tempus AI is a Chicago-based precision-medicine platform that pairs next-generation diagnostics with a de-identified clinical data business sold to pharma. The business is growing at +36% in Q1 2026 and +83% in FY2025; the investment question is whether the quality of that growth, the governance structure, and the litigation/regulatory overhangs justify owning a still-GAAP-unprofitable name at ~$8.1B of market value while a third of its float is sold short.

The fundamental trajectory is improving, which weakens any thesis that this is a structurally impaired business heading lower. Adjusted EBITDA went from -$16.2M to -$2.8M year over year, Q4 2025 was already positive (~$12.9M), and management guides to roughly +$65M adjusted EBITDA on ~$1.59B revenue for FY2026. Operating cash burn nearly halved to -$73.3M from -$105.6M. The company has no debt maturing before 2030, more than twelve months of liquidity, and beat EPS estimates in three of its last four quarters. Current-year EPS estimates were revised up +8.8% over the past 30 days.

The reasons not to own it are equally concrete. A large and fast-growing slice of the higher-margin Data revenue ($21.8M in Q1, up from $0.6M a year ago) flows from Pathos AI, a company the CEO co-founded and chairs, while Tempus simultaneously pays Pathos $35M and employs its CEO as a paid contractor. An active EDNY civil investigative demand targets the False Claims Act, Anti-Kickback Statute, and Medicare 14-Day Rule in a business where a substantial majority of revenue depends on payer reimbursement. Guardant has filed multiple patent suits against the liquid-biopsy line. Founder Eric Lefkofsky controls the company through 30-vote Class B shares and has filed a 10b5-1 plan to sell up to ~7.05M shares through 2027, with the entire C-suite selling and zero offsetting open-market buying. The result is a balanced situation: a credible path to profitable scale against real governance and legal tail risk, with a 35.5% short position that makes pressing the stock in either direction hazardous. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Tempus operates as one reportable segment but reports across two product lines. Diagnostics (~75% of revenue) covers NGS sequencing, PCR profiling, and molecular/anatomic pathology, split between legacy Tempus oncology testing and the hereditary testing acquired with Ambry Genetics (February 2025). In Q1 2026 the company ran ~218,000 tests (85,500 oncology, 132,500 hereditary). Data and applications (~25% of revenue) licenses de-identified multimodal datasets to pharma, matches patients to clinical trials, and sells AI applications; it carries higher margins and skews to the second half of the year.

The core thesis is a data flywheel: every diagnostic test generates de-identified data that is then commercialized repeatedly to pharma partners. Tempus is one of the largest U.S. sequencers of cancer patients, used by 8,500+ physicians, 250+ biotech firms, and 19 of the 20 largest pharma companies. The company has expanded through M&A — Ambry ($692M), Paige.AI ($101.5M, digital pathology), and Deep 6 AI ($17M, trial matching) — into adjacent modalities.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth.** FY2025 revenue reached $1.27B, up ~83% YoY, with much of the lift from the Ambry acquisition adding a hereditary testing line that did not exist organically a year prior. Q1 2026 revenue of $348.1M (+36%) split between Diagnostics +35% ($261M) and Data +41% ($87M). Underlying organic oncology growth was roughly +34% in dollars — strong, though less spectacular than the headline once M&A and related-party contributions are stripped out.

**Profitability.** Q1 2026 GAAP net loss widened to -$125.9M from -$68.0M, but the comparison is distorted: the prior-year quarter included a one-time $46.2M tax benefit from the Ambry valuation-allowance release. Operating loss widened more modestly to -$84.7M from -$68.7M. Adjusted EBITDA improved to -$2.8M from -$16.2M. The caveat is that adjusted EBITDA excludes $52.7M of quarterly stock-based comp (up from $23M, more than 15% of revenue), so GAAP profitability remains distant and dilution is ongoing — share count is up ~5% YoY. Accumulated deficit stands at $2.52B.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash burn narrowed to -$73.3M from -$105.6M. Capex is light (~$8M/quarter). A recurring non-cash drag is mark-to-market losses on equity stakes in Recursion and Personalis (-$32.3M in Q1), flowing through other expense.

**Balance sheet.** Cash plus marketable securities are ~$639M ($521M cash, $118M equity securities). Total liabilities of $1.72B against $416M of equity produce D/E of ~3.6x, and the Altman Z-Score of 1.08 sits in the distress zone — a function of negative EBIT and high leverage rather than imminent insolvency given the runway. The debt stack: $750M of 0.75% convertible notes due 2030 (conversion ~$84.19, well above the current price, capped-call dilution ceiling ~$111), ~$205M Ares term loan plus a $100M revolver, and a ~$199M Google convertible note (6% PIK, tied to GCP spend, maturity extended to 2030). The binding constraint is a minimum trailing-twelve-month revenue covenant of $1.1B through Q4 2026 (comfortably cleared at the current run-rate), plus $25M minimum liquidity and a first-lien leverage test from Q1 2027. A $500M ATM is in place (~$300M remaining), signaling willingness to issue equity into strength.

The financial-health picture is the weakest of the six dimensions: real leverage and a distress-zone Z-score offset by adequate liquidity, no near-term maturities, and a clear path to cash-flow breakeven.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is strong and durable, with two qualifications. First, reported growth is heavily M&A- and related-party-aided: the +83% FY2025 figure reflects the acquired hereditary line, and the Q1 Data revenue from the Pathos related-party deal grew from $0.6M a year earlier. Adjusting for these, organic oncology growth of ~+34% is the cleaner number to anchor on. Second, the data-licensing line is exposed to biotech/pharma funding cycles, and management has flagged biotech-funding softness and hardware tariff risk as demand headwinds.

The structural growth case rests on the data flywheel — pharma demand for real-world multimodal data and AI-enabled trial matching, monetized most clearly through the Pathos/AstraZeneca oncology foundation-model arrangement (Pathos pays Tempus $200M over three years; Tempus is the data provider and an end-licensee, not the model developer). FY2026 guidance implies ~+25% growth, and consensus near-term EPS estimates are mixed — the current-year estimate has improved even as the current- and next-quarter estimates have been revised lower. The widely cited -92% next-year EPS revision is a near-zero-denominator artifact — the estimate moved from -$0.06 to -$0.12, a six-cent shift on a near-breakeven line as the company approaches the GAAP/adjusted crossover — and should not be read as a collapse in earnings power.

## Valuation Assessment

With negative EPS, conventional P/E is uninformative. The relevant frame is enterprise value against forward revenue. At ~$8.1B market cap and ~$1.59B guided FY2026 revenue, the stock trades at roughly 5.1x forward revenue. For a data-moated diagnostics platform growing ~25-36% and inflecting to positive adjusted EBITDA, that multiple is defensible relative to the precision-oncology cohort (Guardant, Natera, Exact Sciences, Caris, Personalis), where revenue multiples vary widely with growth and profitability profiles. It is not a bargain once the governance and revenue-quality discount is applied.

The analyst distribution frames the asymmetry: consensus mean target $66.53 (~+34% from $49.62), median $64.00, high $100, low $35 (~-29%). The Street is net-constructive (10 buy / 6 hold / 1 sell, with the lone sell recently upgraded to zero), and downside to the analyst floor is contained at roughly -29%. The multiple is reasonable but the discount is earned, leaving valuation roughly balanced rather than asymmetrically attractive.

## Competitive Landscape

Tempus competes directly with Guardant Health (liquid biopsy, now also a litigation adversary), Foundation Medicine (Roche), Natera, Exact Sciences, Caris, and Personalis (simultaneously a partner Tempus holds equity in and a competitor). The space is consolidating and IP-contested. Tempus's differentiator is scale in cancer-patient sequencing feeding a data layer that pharma pays for repeatedly, a network effect that competitors cannot easily replicate without comparable test volume.

The competitive trajectory is improving — expanding modalities via M&A (digital pathology, hereditary, trial software) and deepening pharma data relationships. The principal threat to the moat is the Guardant patent litigation, which targets the xF/xF+/xM liquid-biopsy products and seeks injunctive relief; an adverse ruling could remove a product line. The contested-IP nature of the field caps the competitive score below where the data flywheel alone would place it.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk load is heavy and several elements are binary:

- **EDNY civil investigative demand (March 2024):** Rolling document production. Material because only ~55% of clinical oncology NGS and ~50% of hereditary tests were collected on as of YE2025.
- **Guardant patent litigation:** Multiple suits (June 2024, August 2025) against the liquid-biopsy line, with Tempus countersuing under the Lanham Act.
- **Illinois privacy class actions (February 2026):** Four companion suits under the Illinois Genetic Information Privacy Act, seeking class status — a sector-specific exposure.
- **Related-party revenue quality:** The Pathos deal is the single most important qualitative flag. A revenue stream this large flowing from a CEO-affiliated entity, combined with Tempus paying Pathos and employing its CEO, raises arm's-length and revenue-quality questions a quantitative screen would miss.
- **Leverage and covenant floor:** D/E 3.6x, distress-zone Z-score, and a minimum-revenue covenant that is comfortably cleared today but becomes a hard constraint if growth stalls.
- **Dilution:** 15%+ of revenue in SBC plus the $500M ATM.

These risks are not fully reflected at the current multiple, which is why the risk score is low. Offsetting them: there is no near-term solvency threat (>12 months liquidity, no maturities before 2030, near-breakeven EBITDA, low-coupon converts), and the lone sell rating was recently upgraded away.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 82.9% sits rich to 62.7% one-year historical vol (a 20.2-point spread, 75th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (82.9% near-term vs. 76.9% long-dated), consistent with an imminent catalyst. Near-dated positioning is call-heavy (1-week skew -7.0%, P/C volume 0.39, P/C OI 0.95), but the 3-month tenor shows the most bearish skew at -14.4% with a P/C volume ratio of 1.76 — informed put demand concentrated a quarter out. Combined with 35.5% short interest and 5.4 days to cover, the structure reflects a contested, high-volatility name where both squeeze and downside scenarios are actively priced, reinforcing the case against a directional position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Adjusted EBITDA crosses cleanly positive in FY2026, validating operating leverage in the data flywheel. Organic oncology testing compounds in the mid-30s%, the Pathos/AstraZeneca foundation-model arrangement proves out as a template for repeatable data monetization, and the EDNY CID resolves without material penalty. With the short interest at a third of float, any clean print or favorable legal/regulatory headline can trigger a sharp squeeze toward the $66 mean and $100 high targets. Bull case ~$90-100 (+81-102%).

**Bear case.** The EDNY CID escalates into a billing/reimbursement enforcement action in a model where collection rates are already ~50-55%; Guardant wins injunctive relief against the liquid-biopsy line; the Pathos related-party revenue is reclassified or scrutinized, undercutting Data-segment growth; and continued GAAP losses plus the ATM dilute holders. Growth decelerates toward the covenant floor, forcing a financing event on poor terms. Bear case ~$35 (-29%), the analyst low.

The two cases are roughly balanced in probability-weighted terms, which is the definition of a HOLD. The fundamentals are inflecting positively, removing the structural-impairment case; the governance and legal overhangs are real enough to block a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an uncertain HOLD — the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction, and the short interest makes a tactical bet dangerous regardless of fundamental view.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- EDNY CID resolves without material monetary penalty or business restriction, removing the largest binary overhang.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive adjusted EBITDA with organic (ex-related-party, ex-M&A) oncology growth holding ≥30%, confirming the breakeven inflection is durable rather than guidance-dependent.
- A reduction in related-party Data revenue dependence (Pathos share of Data revenue declining as third-party pharma licensing scales), de-risking revenue quality.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- An adverse Guardant ruling enjoining the xF/xM liquid-biopsy products, or an EDNY action seeking material recoupment/penalties.
- TTM revenue trending toward the covenant floor, or a dilutive ATM draw at depressed prices, signaling a financing-constraint problem.
- Adjusted EBITDA reversing back into meaningful losses despite the FY2026 guide, indicating the breakeven story has stalled.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the Q2 2026 print, expected early August). The 3-month options tenor showing concentrated put demand suggests the market is positioned for a catalyst around that window. Monitor EDNY CID and Guardant docket developments in the interim.

## Conclusion

Tempus is a high-growth, data-moated precision-medicine platform that has nearly eliminated its EBITDA burn and guides to positive adjusted EBITDA in FY2026. That improving trajectory — rising guidance, upward current-year EPS revisions, three EPS beats in four quarters, and a defensible forward-revenue multiple with downside capped near the $35 analyst floor — rules out treating this as a structurally impaired business to sell or short. The related-party Pathos revenue web, the active EDNY civil investigative demand, the Guardant patent litigation, the Illinois privacy suits, dual-class founder control with uniform insider selling and no offsetting buying, and 15%+ SBC dilution are material enough to prevent a buy. With a third of the float sold short and the options market pricing imminent volatility, the asymmetry does not favor a directional position. The call is to hold, monitor the EDNY and Guardant outcomes, and reassess at the next earnings print.
