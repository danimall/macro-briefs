## Executive Summary

First Commonwealth's June quarter was the strongest set of operating metrics the company has produced in this cycle: record GAAP net income of $44.6M ($0.44 diluted), net interest margin of 4.01% (up 18bp year over year), a core efficiency ratio of 52.24% (from 54.06%), core ROATCE of 15.66%, and tangible book value per share of $11.58, up 8.9% year over year. The board raised the dividend to $0.14 and authorized a fresh $75M repurchase program equal to roughly 3.5% of market capitalization. Non-GAAP EPS came in 3.9% above non-GAAP consensus. On the operating line the franchise screens upper-quartile for a $12B bank on all three of margin, efficiency and returns, and the source of that margin is structural: 23.5% of deposits are noninterest-bearing.

The problem is what sits underneath. Nonperforming loans fell for a third consecutive quarter to 0.86% of loans (0.62% excluding $23.3M of government-guaranteed balances), but they fell because problem credits were sold, paid off or charged off. Net charge-offs ran 0.49% annualized in Q2 against 0.12% a year earlier, with commercial and industrial loans accounting for 68% of the total and H1 C&I charge-offs up roughly eleven-fold year over year. Classified loans, the leading indicator, rose 8.4% sequentially and 14.1% year over year to $148.4M, and criticized loans rose 12.4% year over year to 3.03% of loans. Against that backdrop the allowance was *released* $1.8M, to 1.35% of loans, with provision covering only 78% of charge-offs. That is the sharpest internal tension in the results, and the earnings call commentary that would resolve it (expected charge-off trajectory, criticized-loan inflow expectations, the reasoning behind the release) is not available in primary form.

Valuation now prices most of the good news. At $21.44 the stock is 1.85x tangible book, 12.4x the $1.73 current-year consensus and 10.9x the $1.97 next-year figure, with estimates essentially unchanged over both 30 and 90 days. The company repurchased 645,695 shares in Q2 at a weighted-average $18.66 (1.61x tangible book) and 1,284,457 shares in Q1 at $17.69. Buying at today's 1.85x is materially less accretive. A justified-multiple check using H1 core ROTCE of 14.56%, a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth supports roughly 1.6-1.8x tangible book; the stock is at or slightly above the top of that band while credit-grade migration is still running the wrong way. We rate FCF **HOLD**: existing holders are compensated by a 2.6% covered dividend, a 3.6% share-count reduction run rate and a large capital cushion; new capital has no urgent entry signal 4% below the 52-week high with the central credit question unanswered.

## Company Overview

First Commonwealth Financial Corporation is a Pennsylvania financial holding company operating First Commonwealth Bank, a Pennsylvania state-chartered bank and trust company supervised by the FDIC and the Pennsylvania Department of Banking and Securities. At June 30, 2026 total assets were $12.21B, portfolio loans $9.47B, deposits $10.26B and equity $1.57B. The retail franchise is 126 offices across 30 counties in western and central Pennsylvania and Ohio; commercial lending has been extended into Cleveland, Columbus and Cincinnati.

Two structural facts define the business. First, the funding base: 23.5% noninterest-bearing checking, 18% CDs and IRAs, 29% uninsured deposits. That mix is the reason the margin runs above 4% at a bank with a 6.07% loan yield. Second, the growth engine has migrated. Every whole-bank acquisition since 2015 except Centric (Harrisburg/Lancaster, 2023) was in Ohio: Columbus (2015, 2017), Canton-Massillon/Ashtabula branch purchase (2016), Cincinnati (2018 and the April 2025 CenterBank deal). Commercial real estate by state now shows Ohio at $1,371.8M versus Pennsylvania at $1,304.1M. The company competes at home from an entrenched position and in Ohio metros as a challenger.

The most consequential organic initiative is the equipment leasing and finance division, established de novo in Q1 2022 out of suburban Philadelphia, outside the branch footprint. It reached $784.8M at June 30, 2026, 8.3% of portfolio loans, growing 36.8% year over year. Loan mix at June 30: CRE 32.6%, consumer real estate 25.3%, auto/RV 14.7%, C&I and other commercial 13.6%, equipment finance 8.3%, commercial construction 4.8%.

Crossing $10B of assets at year-end 2023 brought permanent structural costs: CFPB primary consumer supervision, Volcker Rule applicability, large-bank FDIC assessment scorecard, and the Durbin interchange cap from July 1, 2024, which the FY2025 10-K quantifies as a $6.3M annual revenue loss. Sub-$10B competitors in the same markets retain the interchange exemption.

## Financial Analysis

### Net interest income is a funding story with a measurable floor

The margin expansion is entirely liability-side, and this matters for how much is left.

- Total interest income: $159.3M in Q2'26 versus $158.9M in Q2'25, up 0.25%.
- Total interest expense: $46.9M versus $52.7M, down 11.0%, or $5.8M.
- Earning-asset yield *fell* 5bp to 5.68%; loan yields were 6.07% versus 6.09%.
- Cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 28bp to 2.31%; average time deposit cost fell from 3.82% to 3.43%.

Essentially the entire $6.2M year-over-year gain in net interest income came from deposit repricing catching up to prior rate cuts, plus the Q1 repayment of $129.4M of FHLB advances (average long-term borrowings fell from $262.6M to $131.4M).

Remaining runway is quantifiable. Average time deposits of $1,769.1M repricing from 3.43% to 3.00% saves $7.6M pre-tax annually, which on $11,275.8M of average earning assets is about 7bp of margin. Maturing cash-flow hedges add a second, smaller tailwind: the drag was $1.1M in Q1'26 versus $3.7M in Q1'25, with remaining notional maturing on a known schedule through October 2026, worth perhaps $4M annualized pre-tax if not replaced. Management's own guidance of "low 4%" against 4.01% already achieved is consistent with limited upside from here.

The rate exposure is genuinely two-sided. The Q1 10-Q shows a one-year cumulative gap ratio of 0.71 (liability-sensitive on a static basis) alongside a simulation showing modest asset sensitivity (+200bp immediate = +5.71% NII; −200bp = −1.78% NII). If rates stay higher for longer the deposit-repricing benefit stalls; if the Fed hikes, the static gap suggests deposit costs move before asset yields. Neither direction offers much margin upside.

### Expenses: the cleanest positive in the results

Core noninterest expense rose 2.6% year over year ($71.0M → $72.8M) against core revenue up 6.2% ($131.3M → $139.4M). Headcount was 1,589 FTE, up 27 from a year earlier and down three sequentially. Management reiterated a $74-76M quarterly total expense guide. That is real positive operating leverage.

Quality-of-earnings scrub: Q2 included an $806K gain on early redemption of the Centric subordinated note, a $311K securities gain, a $400K vendor rebate and roughly $700K of seasonally absent snow-removal cost. Removing the first three moves the core efficiency ratio to roughly 52.8% (about 59bp worse than reported) and clean EPS to approximately $0.43. The beat survives the scrub; the 52.2% headline is modestly flattered.

### Credit: the numbers point in two directions and measure different things

Improving:
- NPLs $81.6M (0.86%) at 6/30/26 versus $92.3M (0.98%) at 3/31/26 and $99.5M (1.04%) at 6/30/25. Excluding $22.5M of government-guaranteed and $0.8M of acquired guaranteed balances, NPLs are $58.3M, or 0.62% of loans, which is benign.
- ACL/NPL coverage 156.1% from 141.7% and 133.6%.
- The Q1 10-Q's subsequent-event disclosure is direct evidence of reserve accuracy: two nonaccrual commercial credits with $5.6M of balances and $3.3M of reserves resolved in Q2 with only $0.1M of incremental charge-off.
- The $225.4M held-for-sale transfer announced in Q4 2025 (including $173.9M of CRE, $5.6M of charge-offs on transfer) was executed; HFS balances fell from $271.5M to $31.6M and the cash flow statement shows $227.0M of proceeds. Most banks work such credits out over years.

Deteriorating:
- Net charge-offs $11.4M in Q2'26 = 0.49% annualized, versus 0.35% in Q1 and 0.12% a year earlier. H1'26 0.42% versus 0.13%.
- C&I accounted for $7.8M of the $11.4M; H1 C&I charge-offs $11.4M versus $1.1M.
- Classified loans $148.4M, up 8.4% sequentially and 14.1% year over year. Criticized $286.5M (3.03%), up 12.4%. During 2025 classified assets had already risen 45%, from $96.3M to $139.4M.
- 90+ days past due and accruing $3.2M versus $1.3M; OREO $2.3M from $0.2M.
- The allowance was released $1.8M to $127.4M (1.35% of loans). Provision covered 78% of charge-offs, versus 132% in Q1 and 323% in Q2'25.

The reconciliation is straightforward: the NPL ratio measures resolution of identified problems (management cited six commercial credits totaling $6.9M resolved in the quarter); the classified and criticized buckets measure inflow. Resolution is running ahead of inflow in nonaccruals and behind it in criticized. The coverage-ratio improvement is arithmetically a falling denominator, not a rising numerator.

The largest single disclosed driver is idiosyncratic: a dealer floor plan borrower that went "out of trust on sold vehicles," a fraud event, producing $41.4M of nonaccruals and $7.6M of charge-offs in 2025, with a separate $2.5M dealer floor plan relationship generating a further $0.7M of previously-provided charge-off in Q1'26. The FY2025 10-K carries an expanded "Increasing Fraud Risk" factor. That composition argues for a company-specific read on execution rather than sector contagion. The counterargument, which cannot be adjudicated without peer C&I loss data, is that small-business borrowers across the regional bank group are absorbing three years of higher rates and FCF is simply an early mover.

The cushion is the decisive fact for solvency. Core pre-provision net revenue was $65.0M in Q2, roughly $260M annualized, against annualized H1 charge-offs of $39.2M, about 6.6x coverage. Charge-offs would need to reach roughly 2.75% of loans to consume a full year of pre-provision earnings. This is an earnings-growth problem, not a capital problem.

### Balance sheet, capital, liquidity

Total assets were flat to slightly down year over year at $12.21B. Portfolio loans fell 1.1% year over year with a deliberate remix: CRE −8.4%, C&I −6.9%, against equipment finance +36.8%, commercial construction +12.8% sequentially, HELOC +8.9% and auto/RV +3.8%. Period-end deposits fell $149.8M sequentially to $10.26B (average deposits grew $52.3M), with the gap filled by short-term borrowings rising from $22.9M to $137.9M. Loan/deposit rose to 92.7% from 90.9%.

Capital is comfortable: holding company CET1 12.6%, Tier 1 13.4%, total 15.1%, leverage 11.1%; TCE/TA 9.91%; bank-level total capital 14.0%, which the company quantifies as $393.8M of excess above the well-capitalized threshold, roughly 18% of market capitalization. The Bank could dividend $348.0M to the parent at year-end 2025 without state approval. Liquidity was $5.92B of net available sources against $3.0B of uninsured deposits. FCF made the permanent AOCI opt-out election in 2015, so the $90.4M of AFS and $51.2M of HTM unrealized losses (roughly 95% Treasuries, agencies and GSE paper) do not touch regulatory capital.

Two offsets: goodwill and intangibles are $398.3M, 25.4% of equity, a consequence of roughly fifteen transactions since 1984; and the CenterBank acquisition brought in a portfolio of which 9.6% ($29.2M of $303.7M) was classified purchased credit deteriorated at acquisition, a diligence data point, though the $46.2M all-stock price for $336.2M of assets limits the damage.

Capital return has displaced balance-sheet growth. Share count fell 3.6% year over year to 101.1M. Q2 repurchases were 645,695 shares at $18.66; Q1, 1,284,457 at $17.69. The July 28 authorization of $75.0M is a clear step-up from the $25M authorizations of December 2025 and February 2026. The dividend at $0.14 quarterly is $0.56 annualized, 2.61% on $21.44 and roughly 34% of trailing EPS of $1.64. Dilution overhang is negligible (81,914 antidilutive restricted shares; no warrants or contingent classes).

## Growth Analysis

There is no balance-sheet growth story here, and management has effectively said so. Loans are shrinking, deposits are flat on average and down at period end, and the CEO attributed loan growth of 1.97% annualized in Q2 to equipment finance, construction, home equity and indirect lending "offsetting contraction in commercial real estate and C&I." StockStory's reporting from the call flags "record commercial loan payoffs" restraining growth despite improving pipelines. Record payoffs can reflect healthy borrowers refinancing away (competitive loss) or FCF pushing marginal credits out (deliberate de-risking); the Q1 loan sale argues for some of the latter, but the company does not disclose payoff composition.

Consensus therefore embeds an EPS path driven by three levers, not volume: margin holding near 4%, expenses held to $74-76M per quarter, and provision normalizing. FY2026 consensus is $1.73 and FY2027 $1.97, implying 13.9% growth. Revisions are flat: +0.1% for the current year and +0.2% for next year over 90 days, with the current quarter unchanged at $0.46 and the next quarter down 0.3%. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal in this name; the Zacks upgrade to Rank #2 on July 31 reflects direction only, and the underlying magnitudes are trivial.

The delivery record is mediocre: over four quarters, one beat (Q2'26, +0.6% on consensus; +3.9% on the matched non-GAAP basis), one in-line, and two misses (−8.6% in Q1'26, −5.8% in Q3'25, the latter headlined on credit issues and margin headwinds). That argues for treating the $1.97 FY2027 figure as an optimistic anchor rather than a base case, since it requires provision to fall while classified balances are still building.

The equipment finance platform is the one genuine growth engine, and it is also the least-seasoned risk in the company. From Q1 data: a 1.99% allowance against 1.35% company-wide, Q1 gross charge-offs annualizing near 0.74%, nonaccruals up from $1.3M to $2.0M in one quarter, and substandard balances up 112% sequentially from $2.7M to $5.6M. The base is small (0.76% of the segment), the business is short-duration and well-collateralized, and the higher allowance means management is pricing higher loss content rather than being surprised by it. A four-year-old, out-of-footprint platform growing near 40% has no observable loss curve through a downturn.

Consumer exposures deserve a macro overlay. Consumer real estate is 25.3% of loans with HELOC up 8.9%, plausibly rate-lock substitution as homeowners tap equity rather than move. Indirect auto and RV is 14.7% of loans, underwritten up to 100% of purchase price or NADA value with terms to 84 months. Neither is alarming today; both are the channels through which a consumer slowdown would show up here.

Growth prospects score: 4.9.

## Valuation Assessment

Start with reconciliation. The data feed reports trailing and forward P/E as identical at 13.09x, which is not right: 13.09x corresponds to TTM EPS of approximately $1.64 (FY2025 GAAP $1.47 plus H1'26 $0.81 less H1'25 $0.64), and the forward multiple on $1.73 consensus is 12.4x, on $1.97 is 10.9x. The $1.47 EPS field is FY2025 GAAP, not TTM.

| Metric | FCF | Peer median (Regional Banks) |
|---|---|---|
| Trailing P/E | 13.1x | 12.1x |
| Forward P/E (FY26 cons. $1.73) | 12.4x | — |
| Forward P/E (FY27 cons. $1.97) | 10.9x | — |
| P/TBV | 1.85x | — |
| P/B | 1.38x | — |
| Dividend yield | 2.61% | 2.1% |
| Discount to 52-wk high | −4.0% | −2% to −7% |

On trailing earnings FCF trades at a slight premium to the peer median. On tangible book the multiple is the tighter constraint. A justified-multiple check — (ROTCE − g) / (COE − g) — using H1'26 core ROTCE of 14.56%, a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth gives 1.65x; using the Q2 annualized 15.66% gives about 1.81x. At 1.85x the stock is at or slightly above the top of that band. The 2025 Core ROTCE was 14.69% and 2024 was 14.95%, down more than 600bp from the 20.86% peak in 2023; the 15.66% Q2 figure is the best print in two years, not the run rate.

The most direct valuation evidence comes from the company itself. Management repurchased 645,695 shares at $18.66 during Q2 (1.61x tangible book) and 1,284,457 at $17.69 during Q1 (1.53x). At $21.44 the new $75M authorization buys roughly 3.5M shares, 3.5% of the count, but at 1.85x tangible book it reduces tangible book value per share by roughly 3% before retained earnings, meaningfully less accretive than the executions to date. Three senior officers also sold five-figure lots at $18.33-19.12 in Q2. Those transactions predate the print and the rally and say nothing about $21.44 directly, but they do establish that neither the company nor its insiders were treating the high-$18s as deeply undervalued.

Analyst targets run $23.50 low to $25.00 high, mean $24.08, a 12.3% gap to the mean. The mean-target-as-floor logic applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples; here estimates are flat, six analysts cover the name with three strong buys, one buy and two holds unchanged for three months, and the company trades at a premium to peer trailing earnings. Targets corroborate rather than confirm.

Scenario framework (base-case anchor is normalized 2027 EPS, haircutting consensus for continued elevated provision):

- **Base: $23.00.** ~12.5x normalized FY2027 EPS of roughly $1.83 — consensus $1.97 discounted for provision that stays above the current 78%-of-charge-offs run rate. Upside +7.3% plus 2.6% dividend.
- **Bull: $26.50.** Charge-offs peak at 0.49%, criticized inflows stall, FY2027 EPS reaches $2.00-2.05 with buybacks executed, and the multiple holds 13x. Upside +24%.
- **Bear: $17.00.** Classified migration converts, provision reverts to 130%+ of charge-offs, net charge-offs rise toward 0.65%, FY2027 EPS falls to ~$1.55 and the multiple compresses to 11x. Downside −21%.

Probability-weighting 50/25/25 gives roughly +4% price plus 2.6% yield, or a mid-single-digit expected total return, which is approximately fair and short of the margin of safety required for ACCUMULATE. Valuation score: 5.1, down marginally from the prior review because the price rose 4.7% since early July while estimates were unchanged and the tangible book multiple expanded from roughly 1.77x to 1.85x.

## Competitive Landscape

Operating metrics are genuinely upper-quartile: 4.01% NIM, 52.2% core efficiency and 15.66% core ROATCE at a $12B bank, with a customer satisfaction score of 91.3 and NPS of 71.3, seventh consecutive year as a top Pittsburgh-region workplace, and top-50 recognition among public banks above $10B. The 23.5% noninterest-bearing deposit share is the structural asset and it is difficult for competitors to replicate quickly.

Trajectory is more mixed. The loan book is shrinking, and "record commercial loan payoffs" implies at least some competitive loss on price or structure in CRE and C&I. The $10B threshold permanently disadvantages FCF against sub-$10B community competitors in the same markets by roughly $6.3M of annual interchange plus CFPB supervision cost, the standard reason banks crossing that line keep acquiring, which is exactly FCF's history. Credit unions' lighter tax and regulatory burden is a persistent structural issue in Pennsylvania and Ohio community banking, and Item 1 flags it explicitly. Employee turnover was 25.9% in 2025, up 2.5 points. The Ohio migration is strategically sound, targeting higher-growth metros than western Pennsylvania, but it places FCF as a challenger against entrenched incumbents in Cleveland, Columbus and Cincinnati.

Two newly added risk factors are well-targeted rather than boilerplate. The stablecoin/GENIUS Act factor bears directly on a bank whose principal economic advantage is 23.5% noninterest-bearing deposits, precisely the funding most exposed to tokenized-deposit substitution. The federal-shutdown factor cites SBA program disruption, relevant because FCF describes itself as a top SBA lender in all its regions and $23.3M of its $81.6M nonaccrual balance depends on a functioning guarantor.

Competitive position score: 5.5 — strong absolute metrics, flat-to-eroding volume trajectory, permanent regulatory cost step-up.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Provision catch-up (highest near-term probability).** Normalizing provision to equal charge-offs costs roughly $2.5M pre-tax, or about $0.02 per quarter. Restoring a 130% ratio would cost closer to $0.05 per quarter. This is the mechanical path from $0.44 to a materially lower run rate without any new credit event.

**2. C&I credit migration.** The eleven-fold increase in H1 C&I charge-offs is dominated by the dealer floor plan fraud, which is idiosyncratic, but a second dealer relationship also produced charge-offs in Q1. Whether this is a control problem now remediated or an early read on a sector-wide small-business cycle cannot be resolved from the available data, and it is the single most important open question.

**3. Equipment finance seasoning.** $784.8M, 8.3% of loans, four years old, growing 36.8%, out-of-footprint, 1.99% allowance, substandard balances up 112% in a single quarter (Q1 data; Q2 segment detail unavailable until the 10-Q). No observable loss curve through a downturn.

**4. CRE, specifically office and multifamily.** CRE is 32.6% of loans. Q1 disclosure: CRE non-pass $147.3M (4.7% of CRE), up $19.8M in the quarter; office $458.6M with 9.6% non-pass and a weighted-average 45.0% LTV that the company acknowledges rests largely on origination appraisals; multifamily non-pass jumped to 6.0% including $20.0M of substandard nonaccruing, with a $12.8M loan carrying a $2.16M specific reserve and modified with a rate reduction, term extension and payment deferral. The board cut the office concentration limit from 65% to 50% of Tier 1 plus ACL, with 31.2% utilization.

**5. Funding.** The $149.8M period-end deposit decline plugged with a $115.1M increase in short-term borrowings is one data point. If it recurs, the deposit-repricing tailwind driving all of the margin expansion reverses.

**6. Governance and disclosure gaps.** Form 4 filings show two individuals with the Chief Risk Officer title plus a new Chief Credit Officer, and there were Item 5.02 officer-change 8-Ks on 2025-08-04, 2025-11-04 and 2026-01-28 whose contents are unread. No motive should be inferred, but a risk-function refresh overlapping the credit deterioration is worth verifying. An Item 5.03 bylaw amendment filed 2026-07-31, three days after the $75M buyback authorization, is also unread; such amendments are usually routine. The incentive plan's five measures (Core ROTCE, Core EPS, Core efficiency, PPNR ROAA, TSR) contain no direct asset-quality metric, and PPNR ROAA structurally excludes provision. CEO compensation rose 20.4% to $2.0M in a year when Core ROTCE fell and five-year TSR finished 29 points behind the peer group, 25 of those points lost in 2025 alone. Offsetting: $2.0M is modest for a $12B bank, the 1.8x CEO-to-NEO ratio is unusually flat, and a clawback policy has been in place since October 2023.

**7. Litigation is genuinely clean.** Reasonably possible losses beyond recorded liabilities are disclosed at $0 to $1 million.

Standard bankruptcy screens are structurally inapplicable to a deposit-funded balance sheet; the economically meaningful solvency measures here are CET1 of 12.6%, TCE/TA of 9.91%, $393.8M of bank-level excess capital and 6.6x pre-provision coverage of current charge-offs.

### Options Market Signal

At-the-money implied volatility rose from 20.0% on July 4 to 23.3% on August 7, a 3.3-point increase alongside a 4.7% price gain. Rising implied volatility with a rising price on a $2B regional bank is mildly unusual and consistent with the market pricing wider outcomes around the credit trajectory rather than complacency at the highs. Short interest is 4.4% of float with 4.4 days to cover — uncrowded, meaning informed capital has not organized around the bearish credit read, but also that there is no squeeze support if it does. Neither signal is decisive; both are consistent with a HOLD rather than a directional call.

Risk factors score: 4.8 — the provision-catch-up path and the unseasoned equipment finance book are specific, dated, and not obviously discounted at 1.85x tangible book near the 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($26.50, +24%)

Q2 marks the credit peak. The $225M loan sale, the six commercial resolutions and the earlier dealer floor plan cleanup removed the identified problems; the classified increase reflects more conservative grading by a refreshed credit organization rather than genuine deterioration, and management's specific reserves have proven accurate (the Q1 subsequent-event disclosure resolved $5.6M of credits carrying $3.3M of reserves for $0.1M of incremental loss). Charge-offs revert toward 0.30% through 2027, provision falls, and FY2027 EPS reaches $2.00-2.05 on a 3.6% smaller share count. Margin holds at 4.0% as the remaining time-deposit runway and maturing hedges offset flat asset yields; expenses stay inside the $74-76M guide, holding efficiency near 52%. Core ROTCE re-establishes above 15%, supporting 13x earnings and a tangible book multiple approaching 2.0x on a tangible book base compounding 8-9% annually. The $75M authorization plus $393.8M of bank-level excess capital provides continuous support.

### Bear Case ($17.00, −21%)

The 78% provision-to-charge-off ratio and the $1.8M release prove premature. Criticized loans at 3.03% of loans and classified up 14% year over year convert at normal migration rates; provision reverts above 130% of charge-offs and net charge-offs rise toward 0.65% as equipment finance seasons and C&I stress broadens from an idiosyncratic fraud to a cyclical pattern. FY2027 EPS falls to roughly $1.55. Meanwhile the margin has no runway — asset yields are flat and the deposit-repricing benefit is roughly 7bp from exhaustion — and the loan book keeps shrinking as record payoffs continue, so there is no volume offset. Deposit outflows recur, forcing more short-term borrowing at a higher cost. The multiple compresses to 11x and 1.45x tangible book, roughly where the company was buying stock two quarters ago. The mediocre delivery record (two misses in four quarters) and the 2025 relative TSR gap show the market is quick to derate this name on credit news.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** The classification is deliberate: the single most decision-relevant variable — whether 0.49% net charge-offs is a cleanup peak or a new plateau — is not resolvable from available primary disclosure, and the price no longer offers compensation for guessing.

- **Base case: $23.00** (+7.3% from $21.44), 12.5x normalized FY2027 EPS of ~$1.83, plus 2.6% dividend.
- **Bull case: $26.50** (+23.6%), 13x FY2027 EPS of ~$2.03.
- **Bear case: $17.00** (−20.7%), 11x FY2027 EPS of ~$1.55 and 1.45x tangible book.
- **Reassessment point:** the Q2 2026 Form 10-Q (equipment finance grade migration, CRE by property type and state, individually analyzed loan detail) and then the Q3 2026 release in late October.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Provision at or above 100% of net charge-offs for two consecutive quarters *with* classified loans flat or declining from $148.4M.
2. Net charge-offs below 0.35% annualized in either Q3 or Q4 2026, confirming Q2's 0.49% as a peak.
3. Price near $18.75-19.25 — roughly 1.6x tangible book, the level at which the company itself was repurchasing — which would restore the base-case gap to 20%+.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following:**
1. Classified loans exceed $165M (a further 11% increase) with the allowance ratio still at or below 1.35%.
2. Equipment finance substandard balances exceed roughly 2% of that segment, or segment net charge-offs exceed 1.25% annualized.

## Conclusion

First Commonwealth is a well-run $12B franchise with a genuinely advantaged deposit base, upper-quartile margin and efficiency, decisive de-risking execution (the $225M loan sale was announced *and* completed, with $227.0M of proceeds evidenced in the cash flow statement), and a capital position that removes solvency from the discussion — pre-provision earnings cover current charge-offs about 6.6x, and charge-offs would need to reach roughly 2.75% of loans to consume a year of pre-provision income.

What the June quarter did not do is resolve the credit question. Headline nonperformers improved because problems were resolved; classified and criticized balances, which measure inflow, rose 14.1% and 12.4% year over year respectively, and the allowance was released into that. All of the margin expansion came from deposit repricing with roughly 7bp of measurable runway left, the loan book is shrinking, and estimates have not moved in 90 days. Against that, the stock has advanced 29.7% over twelve months to 1.85x tangible book — above the 1.61x at which the company repurchased shares in the same quarter, above the 1.65-1.81x range a justified-multiple check on 14.6-15.7% core ROTCE supports, and at a slight premium to the 12.1x peer median on trailing earnings.

That combination yields a mid-single-digit probability-weighted expected total return with a −21% bear case that is mechanically reachable through provision normalization alone. Overall score 5.46, essentially unchanged from the prior 5.5 — the operating improvement in financial health and the deterioration in the risk profile offset one another, while the multiple expanded. **HOLD.**
