**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Crescent Energy is an externally-managed, KKR-controlled U.S. onshore oil & gas producer running a "growth through acquisition + return of capital" strategy. After three large deals in roughly 24 months (SilverBow, Ridgemar, and the Vital Energy merger closed December 2025), the company hit record production of ~341 MBoe/d in Q1 2026, up from 258 a year earlier, with a portfolio now spanning the Eagle Ford, Permian, and Uinta basins plus a ~$100M/year minerals & royalties business. The portfolio is oil-weighted (~76% of Q1'26 revenue from oil), aligning it with the market's preference for durable free-cash-flow from liquids-rich assets.

The investment debate reduces to a clean tension. On one side: a forward P/E near 4.4x on consensus CY EPS of $2.45, against an already-cheap E&P peer median of 6.2x; a covered ~4.5% dividend; estimate revisions still rising on a 30-day basis (+3.4% to +6.3%); a track record of beating consensus by 41-52% in three of the last four quarters; manageable ~1.7x net leverage with ~$2.0B of undrawn liquidity and no revolver draw at quarter-end; and a sell-side that is 11-of-14 Buy/StrongBuy with a mean target of $17.5 and a lowest target ($13) sitting ~21% above the current price. On the other side: a per-share value problem (adjusted EPS fell $0.57→$0.53 YoY despite +32% production because the share count nearly doubled), an external-manager fee structure that mechanically rewards dilutive M&A, a controlled-company governance regime in which common holders do not vote for directors, a substantial near-term share overhang from sponsor and foundation exits, reserve marks ($65.34/bbl) above the 5-year forward curve, and no open-market insider buying.

These factors genuinely cancel. The valuation, positive levered FCF, and rising estimates rule out a structural-impairment AVOID near the bottom of the 52-week range. The dilution, governance, and per-share stagnation cap the upside and prevent a BUY. The honest call is HOLD, with the recognition that the equity is a leveraged, conflicted call option on oil holding in the $60s-plus and on Vital synergies eventually reaching per-share FCF.

## Company Overview

Crescent pursues acquisition-led rather than drill-bit-led growth, buying cash-flowing assets across commodity cycles. The asset base after the roll-up concentrates in three basins: the Eagle Ford (historic core, expanded via the 2024 SilverBow merger and January 2025 Ridgemar acquisition), the Permian (added at scale via the December 2025 Vital merger, which pivots the portfolio toward the Permian), and the Uinta. The minerals & royalties business (Crescent Royalties) provides capital-light cash flow and is being expanded — about $355M of Eagle Ford minerals were acquired in January-February 2026.

The defining structural feature is the external-management and control arrangement. A KKR affiliate (Independence Energy Aggregator L.P.) holds a non-economic Series I Preferred Stock that appoints the entire board and holds approval rights over major corporate actions; common shareholders do not vote for directors. Senior management and operating functions are provided by "the Manager," a KKR affiliate, under a Management Agreement renewed through December 2027. This is a controlled company under NYSE rules. KKR's economic sponsor vehicle has effectively exited at the holding-vehicle level (more below) while retaining control through the non-economic preferred, separating control from economic alignment.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale transformation.** Production rose to ~341 MBoe/d in Q1 2026 from ~268 in Q4'25, almost entirely from Vital and Ridgemar rather than organic growth. Weighted-average Class A shares rose from ~191M (Q1'25) to ~328M (Q1'26) following the April 2025 Up-C simplification and stock issued for acquisitions.

**Q1 2026 results.** Revenue was $1,182.8M, up 24.5% YoY, with oil revenue of $893.3M (+44%). The headline GAAP net loss of $(419.2)M, or $(1.28)/share, is misleading: it is driven by a $706.6M derivative loss (~$600.6M unrealized) as crude spiked above hedge strikes. This is hedging mechanics, not operational deterioration. Adjusted Net Income was $175.5M and Adjusted EPS $0.53, beating consensus near $0.35. Adjusted EBITDAX was $689.7M (+30%); operating cash flow $409.2M; levered FCF $191.8M (−21% YoY as development capex rose to $384.7M). Hedges cost ~$7.25/bbl (realized oil $71.00 before derivatives, $63.75 after); gas pricing was weak at $2.37/Mcf.

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt is ~$5.24B, down from ~$5.52B at YE25 after a revolver paydown but up from ~$1.7B at YE2023 — a tripling driven by acquisitions. Net LTM leverage is ~1.7x per the credit agreement, with a stated target of delevering toward ~1.0x. Liquidity is ~$2.0B against a $3.9B borrowing base with a $2.0B elected commitment, and there were no revolver borrowings at quarter-end. Cash is thin at $9.8M, but that figure is largely irrelevant given the undrawn revolver and positive levered FCF; the restricted-cash decline (from $725.7M Vital-related to $5.4M) reflects deal mechanics clearing. The debt stack carries high coupons (7.375%-9.75%) plus a new 2.75% 2031 convertible ($690M, ~$14.89 conversion, capped call to $22.48), and the company took a $17.4M extinguishment loss redeeming the 9.25% 2028 notes.

On the audit's central point: 1.7x net leverage with ~$2.0B undrawn liquidity, no revolver draw, and positive levered FCF is ordinary E&P leverage, not distress. The Altman Z ~1.4 sits in the formula's "distress zone," but the model is poorly calibrated to asset-heavy, leveraged E&Ps where large PP&E and debt loads are normal; the brief itself flags this. I am therefore scoring financial health as middling (5.0) — penalized for the high-coupon stack, reserve-borrowing-base correlation, and thin cash, but not as a terminal balance-sheet risk.

**Capital returns.** $0.12/quarter dividend ($0.48 annualized, ~4.5% yield) and a $400M buyback authorization (~$336M remaining). The company executed over $900M of non-core divestitures in 2025 to fund delevering and high-grading.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth numbers require the per-share lens. Production is up ~32% YoY and revenue +24.5%, yet adjusted EPS fell from $0.57 to $0.53 because the share count nearly doubled. The roll-up grows the enterprise faster than per-share value, and the external-manager fee structure rewards exactly that absolute growth. Growth at the company level does not automatically translate to growth at the per-share level.

On estimate revisions, I take the audit's correction seriously. The 90-day surge (+83% CY, +80% NY) is partly a commodity/production-scale artifact, reflecting both the oil spike and the step-change in volumes from Vital. But the 30-day revisions remain positive across every horizon — estimates are still rising, not rolling over. Combined with three beats of 41-52% in the last four quarters (including beats that predate the recent Iran/Israel spike), this argues that consensus is not chasing a one-off. The forward setup is constructive on an absolute-earnings basis: CY EPS of $2.45 and NY EPS of $2.44 against a $10.78 price.

The qualifier: the durability of those estimates depends on oil staying supportive and on Vital synergies converting to per-share FCF, not just absolute EBITDAX. Management cited Permian integration "ahead of plan" and raised the full-year outlook on lower capital requirements, but it explicitly flagged that the Vital merger may not be accretive to EPS. I score growth prospects at 5.5 — positive absolute trajectory and live revision velocity, discounted heavily for the per-share leakage.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$10.78, CRGY trades at roughly 4.4x forward consensus CY EPS of $2.45. The peer median in oil & gas E&P is 6.2x (Comstock 6.2x, CNX 4.4x, Magnolia 15.8x, with Talos and Northern Oil at negative trailing multiples on GAAP losses). On forward earnings, CRGY is at the cheaper end of an already-cheap group, with a covered ~4.5% dividend providing some yield support that several peers lack.

The case against using trailing P/E here is correct: trailing P/E is −10.5x on the GAAP loss, which is entirely a hedge mark-to-market artifact and meaningless. On forward and normalized earnings the stock is demonstrably cheap. The cheapness, however, is the kind that has historically functioned as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal — it requires an independent catalyst to be rewarded, and here the catalysts (delevering, synergy capture, overhang absorption) are slow and partly offset by the structural fee/dilution drag. A cheap multiple on a business that systematically dilutes per-share value is cheap for a reason.

The sell-side targets ($13 low, $17.5 mean, $22 high) imply 21-104% upside, and per the principle that analyst targets can lag on cheap, rising-estimate names, the lowest target sitting ~21% above the current price is a meaningful data point against an AVOID. But I temper this: the targets embed a constructive oil tape and full synergy capture, neither of which is assured. I score valuation 5.5 — cheap on forward earnings with real yield support, but the discount partly reflects the structural per-share leakage and is not a clean mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Crescent competes against larger, better-capitalized operators with refining/marketing reach and superior ability to outbid for acreage. Its differentiated edge is the KKR-backed acquisition engine, which buys cash-flowing assets across cycles. The oil-weighted Permian + Eagle Ford tilt plus a growing minerals/royalty stream aligns with the current investor preference for FCF durability over production growth.

The competitive position is stable rather than improving on a per-share basis. The roll-up adds scale and basin diversification, but the company is a price-taker on the commodity, faces Permian midstream/takeaway constraints, and earns weak Henry Hub realizations (~$2.37/Mcf). The minerals business is a genuine quality differentiator (capital-light cash flow). The external-management structure cuts both ways: it provides deal access and operating capability but also creates a principal-agent tension where the Manager's incentives favor acquisitive, stock-funded growth. I score competitive position 5.0 — a credible, scaled operator with a quality minerals layer, but no widening per-share moat.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are substantial and not all priced:

1. **External-manager structure and KKR conflicts (most distinctive).** The Manager earns ~$78.5M base annually that rises 1.5% of net equity proceeds from any issuance, including acquisition stock, so M&A growth mechanically increases fees. Incentive PSUs target ~2% (range 0-4.8%) of Class A shares per three-year period ($21.1M non-cash G&A in Q1'26). The Manager allocates only ≥70% of upstream opportunities to Crescent, and the corporate-opportunity doctrine is renounced. Shareholders bear dilution and fees that scale with the roll-up.

2. **Controlled-company governance.** KKR's preferred appoints the entire board and can block a sale or merger; common holders have no director vote, which can deter takeover premia.

3. **Share overhang.** The KKR sponsor vehicle (PT Independence) distributed 36.8M shares in December 2025, retaining only ~41,000; Liberty Mutual Foundation sold 32.6M shares for ~$402M at $12.33 in May 2026 (down to ~4.2M). Combined with registration rights and the convertible, this is a substantial, ongoing supply pressure that persists even as fundamentals improve.

4. **Reserve/impairment sensitivity.** Year-end PV-10 assumes $65.34/bbl oil — above the 5-year NYMEX forward curve ($56.91-$61.14) — flagging downside reserve-revision and impairment risk. Impairments were $254.6M in 2025 and $161.5M in 2024. The leverage and reserve risks are correlated: a sustained oil decline pressures cash flow and the borrowing base simultaneously.

5. **Leverage and high-cost debt.** Coupons of 7-9.75% are a meaningful cash drag, and the borrowing base is subject to semi-annual redetermination tied to reserve values.

6. **Integration risk.** Three large deals in ~24 months; $62.7M in employee-termination plus $16.4M exit costs already booked; management flags Vital may not be EPS-accretive.

I score risk factors 4.5 — the governance/fee structure, overhang, and reserve-marks-above-curve combination are specific, partly-unpriced downside catalysts, though the leverage itself is ordinary and the hedge book limits commodity downside.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing sharply elevated uncertainty. ATM implied volatility jumped from 51.8% to 176.3% (+124.6pp) between June 2 and June 17, while the price fell 10.5% to $10.78, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.24 to 0.72. An IV spike of that magnitude likely reflects a combination of the oil-binary repricing and the share overhang, rather than a clean directional signal. The shift toward put demand signals more defensive positioning, but the IV is now so rich that it argues against paying up for downside protection here and against treating the move as informed directional positioning. Short interest at 12.9% of float (4.8 days to cover) is elevated and consistent with the overhang and per-share-dilution concerns, though not at squeeze-risk levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Oil holds in the $60s-plus, Vital synergies convert to per-share FCF, the sponsor/foundation overhang clears, and the market re-rates the forward multiple toward the cheap peer median or higher. Estimates continue rising, the beat cadence persists, and the dividend plus buyback support the floor. The sell-side mean and high targets capture this path. Re-rating to ~6x forward on $2.45 EPS implies roughly $15.

**Bear case.** Oil drifts toward the forward curve (high-$50s/low-$60s), triggering reserve revisions, further impairments, and borrowing-base pressure, while the external-manager fee/dilution drag and continued overhang supply keep per-share value stagnant. The convertible and registration-rights supply weigh on the stock, and the controlled-company structure caps any takeover premium. In this path the stock revisits the high-$8s to $9s, near its 52-week low of $7.68.

The base case sits between: a leveraged, conflicted oil call where the cheap multiple and yield provide downside support but the per-share leakage caps the upside. Re-rating is possible but not catalyzed cleanly, and the dilution structure works against per-share compounding even when the company executes.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The cheap forward multiple, positive levered FCF, rising 30-day estimates, and 20%+ upside to even the most bearish covering analyst rule out an AVOID near the bottom of the range. The per-share EPS stagnation, external-manager fee/dilution drag, controlled-company governance, share overhang, and reserve marks above the forward curve rule out a BUY. These genuinely offset.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net leverage delevers to ~1.0x while levered FCF stays positive, demonstrating the roll-up is self-funding the balance sheet.
- The sponsor/foundation share overhang clears (Liberty Mutual residual ~4.2M shares sold or distributed; no new large registered blocks) and the stock holds above ~$12 on absorbed supply.
- Adjusted EPS per share recovers above the prior-year $0.57 on a sustained basis, proving Vital synergies are reaching per-share FCF rather than only absolute EBITDAX.
- A price below ~$9.50 (sub-4x forward on $2.45 EPS, near the lower third of the range) with estimates still rising would tilt the risk/reward constructive enough to upgrade.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- 30-day estimate revisions turn negative alongside a guidance cut, indicating the earnings story is rolling over rather than a one-off.
- Oil sustains below ~$55 WTI, forcing material reserve writedowns and borrowing-base contraction that pressure liquidity.
- A dilutive equity-funded acquisition that further widens the gap between absolute and per-share growth.
- Dividend coverage breaks (levered FCF turns negative for consecutive quarters).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), with interim monitoring of the WTI tape and any new registered share blocks.

## Conclusion

Crescent Energy is a cheaply-valued, oil-weighted FCF roll-up executing well operationally and integrating Vital ahead of plan, carrying ordinary (not distressed) E&P leverage with substantial undrawn liquidity. The bull case rests on the forward multiple, rising estimates, a covered yield, and a uniformly constructive sell-side with 20%+ upside to even the lowest target. The bear case rests on per-share value leakage from a fee structure and dilution machine that rewards absolute over per-share growth, controlled-company governance with no common-shareholder voice, a substantial share overhang from sponsor and foundation exits, and reserve marks above the forward oil curve. These factors cancel into a HOLD. This is a leveraged, conflicted equity that the market is correctly discounting, where the path to per-share re-rating is real but slow and partly self-defeating. I would own it only as a deliberate, sized oil-beta position, and would wait for either a clearer delevering/overhang-clearing signal or a sub-$9.50 entry before turning constructive.
