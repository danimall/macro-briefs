## Executive Summary

Everforth is ASGN Incorporated under a new name, effective April 24, 2026. The business is an IT professional services firm with ~19,600 billable professionals, roughly 90% of Commercial revenue billed on time-and-materials, and no disclosed remaining performance obligations because management classifies its contracts as short-term with termination-for-convenience provisions. Revenue peaked at $4,581.1M in 2022 and has declined every year since, to $3,980.4M in 2025 and a $3,966.8M trailing-twelve-month figure. Operating margin has compressed 408bp over the same span, from 8.94% to 4.86% TTM.

Two things happened in the last four months that pull in opposite directions. On April 23, following Q1 results and a Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin guide of 8.8-9.5% against an FY2025 run rate near 10.6%, the shares fell from a $40.43 close to a $19.53 close in a single session, a 51.7% decline on 10.0M shares. Then on July 29-30 the company reported Q2 revenue of $1,007.0M above the guided high end and a 9.6% adjusted EBITDA margin also above the high end, and guided Q3 to 9.6-10.3% margin on roughly $1.01bn of revenue. The shares have recovered 89% from the $16.90 low to $32.015. Between those events, 13 insiders — the CEO, CFO, President, Chief Legal Officer, Chief Accounting Officer and eight directors — bought roughly $1.87M of stock in the open market at $18.63-$22.67.

The operational stabilization is real, and on cash flow the equity is not expensive: $221.1M of TTM free cash flow is a 16.9% yield on market capitalization and an 8.5% yield on the $2,608M enterprise value; EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA is 6.5x. But the recovery has already repriced that. At $32.015 the stock trades above both the consensus mean ($27.50) and median ($28.00) targets, and 11% below the high target of $36. The forward evidence remains negative in the places that matter most: federal funded backlog fell 18.1% in six months to $403.9M with a 0.8x book-to-bill and TTM awards down 32.1%; the Commercial segment's operating margin fell from 8.66% to 6.24% in H1 on flat revenue; and next-year consensus EPS has been cut 5.6% over 90 days even as current-year estimates rose 4.5% over 30 days. Near-term estimates rising while out-year estimates fall is the signature of cost normalization without revenue recovery. We rate the shares REDUCE with a $34 base case.

## Company Overview

Everforth operates two reportable segments. **Commercial** ($2,790.2M FY2025 revenue, 70.1% of total, 8.89% segment operating margin) splits between Consulting ($1,290.1M) and Assignment ($1,500.1M), the latter traditional IT staffing. **Federal Government** ($1,190.2M, 29.9%, 7.84% margin) is entirely consulting across Defense & Intelligence ($526.0M), National Security ($310.0M), Federal Civilian ($224.9M) and Other ($129.3M). Corporate SG&A of $111.1M bridges $341.4M of segment operating income to $230.3M consolidated.

The operating model is bench-light: management states that for the majority of its IT consulting business it does not carry a large bench but sources from a talent database. That lowers fixed cost and utilization risk versus bench-heavy consultancies, and it is the one claimed differentiator with visible economic substance. It also caps pricing power, because the asset sold is placeable labor rather than proprietary intellectual property. Gross margin has been flat at 28.87% for three consecutive years, which is consistent with that read.

The most defensible asset in the portfolio is the federal clearance base: approximately 1,000 cybersecurity consultants of whom ~500 hold clearances, plus ~900 additional cleared AI and data professionals. That cannot be replicated quickly or offshored. Delivery includes two nearshore centers in Mexico and a growing India center; foreign operations are ~2% of revenue but ~6% of cost of services, which is why peso appreciation appears repeatedly in the 10-Q as a Commercial gross-margin headwind with no revenue offset.

Six acquisitions in five years have assembled the consulting franchise, most recently TopBloc (Workday implementation, $340M, March 2025) and Quinnox ($290M cash, March 2026, purchase accounting still preliminary). The company's $680bn TAM framing should be treated as promotional; at $3.97bn of revenue the realistically addressable pool for a T&M staffing-rooted firm is materially narrower.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze at the consolidated line. Revenue declined 2.9% in 2025, 0.7% in H1 2026, 1.3% in Q2, and Q3 guidance of roughly $1.01bn against a ~$996M H2 2025 quarterly average implies flat to slightly positive year-over-year. The best available characterization is stabilization at a level 13.1% below 2022.

**Commercial Assignment is the structural problem.** Staffing revenue fell from $2,078.9M (2023) to $1,740.5M (2024) to $1,500.1M (2025), down 27.8% in two years and 13.8% in 2025 alone. Management attributes this to macro sensitivity. A cyclical read is defensible given the 2022 post-COVID hiring peak, but three consecutive years of double-digit-trending decline is equally consistent with client insourcing, offshore substitution and AI-assisted productivity. The filings do not permit adjudicating between the two, and this is the core durability question for the equity.

**Commercial Consulting growth was purchased.** Consulting revenue rose 14.4% in 2025 to $1,290.1M, but TopBloc closed March 4, 2025 for $340M and no ASC 805 pro forma revenue or organic split was disclosed. The organic component is not determinable. Total Commercial segment revenue still fell 12.1% over two years including TopBloc.

**Federal leading indicators are deteriorating faster than reported federal revenue.**

| Federal metric | Dec 2024 | Dec 2025 | Jun 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Funded backlog | $529.0M | $492.9M | $403.9M |
| Total backlog | $3,118.6M | $2,948.5M | $2,670.7M |
| TTM new awards | $1,340.5M | $1,020.3M | $926.2M |
| Book-to-bill | 1.1x | 0.9x | 0.8x |

Only $403.9M of the $2,670.7M is funded, and management's own risk factor cautions that revenue may never be realized from some included contracts, so this is not committed revenue. But the direction is unambiguous: total backlog down 9.4% in six months, funded down 18.1%, awards down 32.1% year-over-year, and two consecutive years of sub-1.0x book-to-bill. Federal is 30.3% of revenue, 26% of consolidated revenue comes from direct prime contracts with U.S. agencies, and the 10-K attributes declines specifically to DOGE-related contract losses and a government shutdown. Within the mix, Defense & Intelligence and Federal Civilian are declining on an annualized H1 basis while National Security grows. Federal was the *positive* contributor to H1 2026 segment income (+$4.1M, +9.4%); the backlog data argues that support is temporary.

**Estimate revisions are split, and the split matters.** Current quarter +5.8% over 30 days, current year +4.5% over 30 days and +0.8% over 90 days — but next quarter only +0.3% and next year **−2.9% over 30 days and −5.6% over 90 days**. Seven analysts. The strong revision signal that would justify overriding a deteriorating revenue base requires velocity well above +30% over 90 days on rising revenue; nothing close is present. Consensus is marking up the current year on the Q2 beat and cost normalization while marking down 2027, which is the pattern you see when analysts believe margin recovery is achievable but revenue recovery is not.

## Valuation Assessment

The cheapness is real on cash-based measures and much less clear on earnings.

**On adjusted EBITDA and FCF.** EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA of 6.5x and FCF/EV of 8.5% are undemanding. Applying the current 6.5x multiple to a forward estimate is instructive: H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $180.3M (9.13% margin); Q3 guidance of 9.6-10.3% implies roughly $100M and Q4 similar, giving FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of approximately $382M, below the TTM $400.7M because H1 was weak. At 6.5x that is a $2,483M EV, $1,184M of equity, and $29.0 per share — below the current price. The market is already paying about 6.8x forward adjusted EBITDA, not 6.5x trailing.

**On GAAP earnings, the stock is not cheap versus peers.** TTM GAAP P/E of 15.9x sits essentially at the peer median of 15.4x (DXC 15.4x, ASGN 15.1x — the same entity under its prior name, so effectively a duplicate — Teradata 5.8x, NCR Voyix 27.6x, Grid Dynamics 222.7x). Median peer beta is 0.89. The dispersion in that table is wide enough that the median carries limited information, but nothing in it supports a claim that EFOR is materially mispriced on reported earnings.

**On book value.** Price/book of 0.73x looks like support until you note tangible book value per share of −$26.18, the arithmetic consequence of $2,280.4M of goodwill and $595.6M of intangibles against $1,805.1M of equity. Book value is not a floor here; it is a carrying-value question, addressed below.

**Scenario framework.**

- **Base $34** — 7.0x FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of $382M gives EV $2,674M; against approximately flat net debt of ~$1,280M that is $1,394M of equity, or $34.1 per share on 40.9M shares. Cross-check: 8.0x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $4.30 = $34.4. Upside +6.2%.
- **Bull $44** — 7.75x on $400M of adjusted EBITDA, achieved if Commercial margin recovers toward 8%+ and federal revenue holds despite backlog, gives EV $3,100M and $1,820M of equity, $44.5 per share. Upside +37.4%. The strongest lever here is capital return: $922.5M of the November 2025 $1.0bn authorization remains, equal to 70% of market capitalization, and the upsized $600M revolver leaves incremental secured borrowing capacity under the 3.50x test. Deploying $200M annually at current prices would retire a meaningful share of the float.
- **Bear $19** — 5.5x on $360M of adjusted EBITDA if federal backlog converts to revenue decline and Commercial margin does not recover, giving EV $1,980M and $681M of equity, or $16.6 per share; $19 allows for partial FCF-driven deleveraging. Downside −40.7%. The May 13 low of $16.90 establishes that this level was recently transacted.

At 45% base, 30% bear and 25% bull, expected return is approximately zero against a cost of equity meaningfully above that for a levered small-cap with negative tangible equity. The asymmetry is also unfavorable: +6% to base against −41% to bear.

**On analyst targets.** The consensus range is $17.00 to $36.00 with a mean of $27.50 and median of $28.00 — the current price is 16-17% *above* mean and median. Sentiment is improving (buys went from 1 to 2, against 4 holds and 1 sell), and on a genuinely cheap inflection name consensus targets do lag. But that reasoning requires cheapness relative to *rising* estimates. Here the out-year estimate is falling, and the shares already sit above where seven analysts think fair value is. That combination argues the re-rating from $17 to $32 has consumed the available discount rather than that targets are stale.

Valuation score of 5.5 reflects a genuine cash-flow discount offset by the absence of an identified mispricing at $32 — the discount to a services peer average is earned by three years of revenue decline, structural AI exposure and 4.17x GAAP leverage.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K describes an industry that is "highly competitive and fragmented with limited barriers to entry," where most client agreements are non-exclusive and terminable at will, and concedes that "we expect competition to continue to increase particularly as we grow our IT consulting footprint." That is an admission that moving upmarket puts the company against global systems integrators and offshore-heavy IT services majors with more capital and more delivery scale.

Trajectory by piece: Assignment is losing share of a shrinking pool (−27.8% in two years). Platform consulting is growing but the organic component is undisclosed and the two largest recent contributors were bought for $630M combined. Federal is the highest-quality franchise — the cleared workforce is a durable barrier — but it is the piece with the worst leading indicators.

Customer concentration has not improved. Federal segment share of revenue was 30.0% (2024), 29.9% (2025) and 30.3% (H1 2026); direct prime contracts with U.S. agencies were 26% of consolidated revenue. The mix shift within Commercial from Assignment to Consulting does not constitute customer diversification, and should not be credited as such.

The AI question cuts both ways and the 10-K is unusually candid about it: clients "may develop their own internal AI-related capabilities, which could lead to reduced demand," and separately, if AI value does not materialize at scale clients "may reassess their AI plans." With ~90% of Commercial revenue on time-and-materials, revenue is a function of billable hours. Any durable reduction in hours per unit of client output is a direct revenue headwind, not a mix shift. Management's counter — that AI is now a dominant driver of demand — can be true simultaneously in the near term. Whether AI adds more consulting scope than it removes billable hours is not resolvable from current filings, and it is the reason this business deserves a structurally lower multiple than a software or IP-based services firm.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Goodwill and indefinite-lived trademark impairment, with a named Critical Audit Matter.** Deloitte designated one CAM in the FY2025 audit: the Creative Circle indefinite-lived trademark, citing "recent financial performance" and requiring fair-value specialists. The FY2025 test (dated October 31, 2025) was **qualitative only** for both reporting units and all trademarks — no quantitative valuation was performed. The company's own stated triggers include "a substantial sustained decline in the market capitalization of our stock." The stock has since fallen from ~$46.66 (January 2 grant price) to $16.90 and recovered only partially. The Q2 10-Q states no non-financial fair value adjustments occurred in H1 2026. Carrying amounts: goodwill $2,280.4M (Commercial $1,462.1M, Federal $818.3M) plus indefinite-lived trademarks $305.2M, totaling $2,585.6M against a $2,608M enterprise value and $1,309M of market equity versus $1,805.1M of book equity. The October 31, 2026 annual test is a dated catalyst with an elevated probability of a quantitative test. Any charge would be non-cash: it would reduce book equity and GAAP EPS but not operating cash flow, free cash flow, lender-defined EBITDA or covenant compliance. It would nonetheless be a headline event for a stock with demonstrated gap risk. Market capitalization below book equity is an indicator, not proof — ASC 350 tests reporting units including allocated liabilities and permits control premiums, and Federal goodwill is supported by improving segment margins.

**2. The rebrand versus $272.8M of legacy trademarks.** Of the $305.2M of indefinite-lived trademarks, $32.4M arrived with TopBloc, leaving roughly $272.8M on Apex Systems, Creative Circle, CyberCoders, ECS and GlideFast. The 10-K states Everforth will unify those six brands "under a single identity." Consolidating operating brands into a master brand is the kind of entity-specific event that can require reassessing whether a trademark's life remains indefinite. Whether the legacy brands are being retired or retained as endorsed sub-brands is not disclosed, so the exposure cannot be sized.

**3. Gap risk on guidance.** The April 23 session produced a 51.7% single-day decline on in-line revenue and a 110-180bp adjusted EBITDA margin guide-down. That magnitude is larger than the disclosed guidance change alone would seem to warrant, and no independent analyst attribution was retrievable, which leaves an unresolved question about what else the market repriced. The demonstrated sensitivity of this equity to a margin guide is severe, and Q4 guidance arrives with the October print.

**4. Leverage and coverage.** Net debt of $1,298.9M is 4.17x TTM GAAP EBITDA. Q2 EBIT/interest was 2.01x (approximately 2.5x adding back acquisition/integration expense). Weighted-average borrowings rose to $1.49bn at a 5.3% cash rate. A 100bp move on $901.8M of variable-rate debt is ~$9.0M annually. The 2028 notes refinancing adds ~$0.22 per share after tax at 7%.

**5. Recomputed distress screens are worse than headline labels.** A full nine-signal Piotroski across FY2024→FY2025 scores approximately 4 of 9, not the "strong" partial reading a single-filing screen produces: ROA fell 5.1%→3.1%, long-term debt/assets rose 30.1%→31.8%, current ratio fell 2.50→2.16, asset turnover fell 1.196→1.082, gross margin flat-to-down. Altman Z recomputed on the June 30, 2026 balance sheet with TTM flows and market equity is approximately 2.04, versus 2.71 on FY2025 data. Altman systematically penalizes goodwill-heavy asset-light services firms, so this is a trend signal rather than a bankruptcy probability — near-term solvency risk is genuinely low given $221.1M of FCF and 38% covenant headroom.

**6. Quinnox purchase accounting is preliminary,** with measurement-period adjustments possible through March 2027 on the $173.6M customer-relationship intangible and $137.4M of non-deductible goodwill.

**7. Disclosure granularity was reduced.** The Q2 10-Q replaced the quarterly Commercial Consulting/Assignment split with an industry-vertical view (prior periods restated, which is correct treatment). The Consulting/Assignment split was the clearest quantification of the company's central strategic claim and the clearest measure of the Assignment decline; it is no longer visible quarterly. The stated industry-focused rationale is plausible, but the tracking capability is diminished.

**8. Litigation.** Purported collective class and PAGA actions alleging wage-and-hour and job-posting violations, with management concluding no material effect. Endemic to employer-of-record staffing; nothing quantified.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 21 contracts across three expirations, with a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.03 — so read it cautiously. Near-term ATM IV is 88.1% against 92.7% one-year historical volatility, which the source labels cheap on a −4.5 point spread, but at the 95th percentile within the current chain. Term structure is in backwardation, 88.1% at the 10-day tenor versus 66.2% at 38 days and 71.4% at 129 days, which the source characterizes as pricing imminent stress. Put/call skew is 3.9% at the near tenor with the source labeling it elevated put demand, 1.0% at three months, and −6.2% at six months. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.9%.

The reading consistent with the fundamental view: the market prices short-dated event risk and does not price a sustained regime change six months out. Short interest of 12.3% of float with 3.8 days to cover is elevated but not crowded enough to create meaningful squeeze mechanics against a REDUCE. Near-term volatility being priced below realized volatility makes protective puts a reasonable way to hold a residual position through the October print rather than exiting entirely.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($44, +37%)

Q2 marked the trough. Adjusted EBITDA margin improves from 9.13% in H1 to the 9.6-10.3% Q3 guide and holds there, validating that the FY2025 corporate SG&A bloat ($77.3M→$111.1M) was episodic — rebrand, deal costs, the Q4 2025 back-office outsourcing transition — and partially reverses. Commercial consulting bookings growing double digits year-over-year and Workday bookings strength convert to revenue, taking consolidated revenue back to modest growth in FY2027 as Quinnox annualizes. Federal book-to-bill recovers as National Security growth offsets DOGE-driven Civilian losses. The company then deploys the $922.5M remaining authorization against a $1.31bn market cap using $221M of annual FCF plus covenant-permitted secured capacity, retiring a meaningful share of the float annually. On stable adjusted EBITDA of $400M and 7.75x, plus per-share accretion from repurchase, $44+ is reachable. The 13-insider April buying cluster — every officer in the finance, accounting and legal chain plus eight directors, at $18.63-$22.67 — is consistent with management having believed the business was worth substantially more than $20.

### Bear Case ($19, −41%)

The federal backlog is telling the truth. Funded backlog down 18.1% in six months and awards down 32.1% year-over-year convert into federal revenue decline over the next four to six quarters, removing the segment that carried H1 2026 income growth. Commercial margin does not recover from 6.24%, because the pressure is competitive and structural — a bench-light T&M model has no pricing power against global SIs, and peso-denominated delivery cost keeps rising against dollar billings. Assignment continues its 13-14% annual decline. AI-driven reduction in billable hours accelerates rather than adding scope, and 2027 consensus continues to fall from $4.30. The October 31 impairment test goes quantitative and produces a Creative Circle write-down that resets book equity and headlines. Cash taxes normalize toward the book rate, cutting FCF by ~$40M. The 2028 notes refinance at 7%+. The multiple compresses to 5.5x on $360M of adjusted EBITDA and the stock revisits the May range.

### What tips the balance

Both cases are coherent. The reason we side with caution at $32.015 rather than $20 is arithmetic rather than narrative: the base case sits only 6% above the current price, the bear case sits 41% below it, and the stock already trades above the mean and median of seven analyst targets. The forward-looking evidence that would justify paying up — out-year estimate revisions, federal book-to-bill, Commercial segment margin — is negative in all three places. The evidence that has improved — one quarter's non-GAAP beat, one quarter's guide, current-year estimate marks — has already been paid for in an 89% move off the low.

On the insider cluster, we weight it positively but modestly. Breadth is the signal, but $1.87M is 0.14% of market capitalization, and the dollar amounts cluster tightly on round numbers ($25K, $50K, $100K, $1.0M), which is more consistent with execution under a coordinated ownership-guideline program during a post-earnings window than with 13 independent conviction judgments. That is an interpretation, not a fact. Cutting the other way, the company itself repurchased 382,424 shares in April at an average of $30.07 and then bought **nothing** in May or June while the stock traded near $20 — most plausibly because the $283.6M Quinnox outlay left only $180M of revolver availability ahead of a facility negotiation, or because of 10b5-1 price limits, rather than a valuation judgment. ## Conclusion

Everforth is a stabilizing but structurally challenged IT services business that has been correctly repriced and then partially re-rated. The Q2 beat and the Q3 guide are genuine, and the cash flow is genuine: $221.1M of TTM free cash flow, 6.5x trailing adjusted EBITDA, 8.5% FCF/EV, and $922.5M of unused repurchase authorization against a $1.31bn market capitalization. Those facts would support a constructive view at $20-24.

At $32.015 they do not. Revenue has declined three consecutive years and Q3 guidance implies flat at best. The Commercial segment's operating margin fell 242bp on flat revenue in H1 while management repositions it as the higher-margin business. Federal funded backlog is down 18.1% in six months with a 0.8x book-to-bill, and Federal was the only segment contributing positively to H1 income. Next-year consensus EPS has been cut 5.6% over 90 days. The shares trade above the mean and median of seven analyst price targets, tangible book value is −$26.18 per share, $2,585.6M of non-amortizing intangibles sit against a $2,608M enterprise value, and the October 31 impairment test follows an audit that named the Creative Circle trademark as its sole Critical Audit Matter after performing only a qualitative test. The base case offers roughly 6% upside against 41% downside to a level transacted three months ago, in a security that has demonstrated a 52% single-day decline on a margin guide.

**REDUCE.** Base case $34, bull $44, bear $19. Revisit at $24 or on two consecutive quarters of federal book-to-bill at or above 1.0x with Commercial segment margin above 8%.
