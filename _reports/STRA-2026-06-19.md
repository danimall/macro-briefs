**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Strategic Education ("SEI") is a for-profit, multinational post-secondary operator whose financial profile and capital structure are strong: zero drawn debt, ~$162.6M of cash and securities, $87.4M of Q1 2026 operating cash flow against only $10.1M of capex, and an Altman Z-Score around 4.59. Management returns capital aggressively, repurchasing $40.0M of stock in Q1 (at an average $81.11, above today's $75.33) and paying a $0.60 quarterly dividend. Combining the ~$13.6M/quarter dividend (~$54M annualized) with the buyback against ~$1.7B of market cap implies a mid-to-high single-digit return-of-capital yield. On headline numbers the stock looks cheap: ~10.5x the $7.19 current-year EPS estimate versus an Education Services peer median of 15.6x, a 3.2% dividend yield, and analyst targets ($80 low, $95 high) entirely above the current price.

The problem is what sits underneath the multiple. SEI's largest segment, U.S. Higher Education (~70% of revenue, Capella + Strayer), contracted 3.8% in Q1 with segment operating income down 14.9%, and per-student revenue is in structural decline. The company's growth burden rests entirely on Education Technology Services (ETS), which is compounding at 20%+ with ~47% segment margins but represents only ~14% of revenue. Layered on top is a set of specific, dated regulatory threats: the July 2026 elimination of Grad PLUS loans (a direct demand headwind for graduate-heavy Capella), Strayer's 89.64% 90/10 federal-funds ratio sitting just 36 basis points below the hard regulatory ceiling, ~$523.5M of ANZ goodwill on a unit with a thin ~17% fair-value cushion, and live Australian wage litigation. The market has expressed this clearly: cumulative total shareholder return of 97.9 versus a peer group at 198.2, roughly a halving in relative terms even as net income more than doubled from 2021 to 2025.

The score has trended down (6.8 in April to 6.5 in May to 5.6 here), and the recommendation moved from BUY to HOLD after the Q1 miss. We keep it at HOLD with an income rationale. The cheapness is real but is the kind of discount that reflects a warranted regulatory de-rating rather than a discrete mispricing, a value-trap profile until ETS growth visibly outruns the core drag or the regulatory binaries resolve. The covered yield, aggressive buyback, and strong balance sheet provide a defensible return floor; the appreciation case is not yet earned.

## Company Overview

SEI operates three reportable segments:

- **U.S. Higher Education (USHE)** — ~70% of revenue. Capella University (online-only, ~51,750 students, 58% in graduate programs) and Strayer University (~33,550 students, undergrad-heavy), plus the Jack Welch Management Institute. The student base is non-traditional: ~70% age 31+, ~72% part-time, ~75% female, ~61% minority.
- **Education Technology Services (ETS)** — the strategic growth engine. Sophia Learning (low-cost subscription gen-ed courses recommended for transfer credit) and Workforce Edge (education-benefits administration with 80 employer partners). ETS also funnels employer-affiliated enrollment into Capella/Strayer, now 34.5% of USHE enrollment.
- **Australia/New Zealand (ANZ)** — Torrens University (the only investor-funded university in Australia), Think Education, and Media Design School (NZ). ~19,500 students, ~45% international.

The differentiation rests on competency-based delivery (Capella's FlexPath), an affordability stack (Sophia, tuition caps), and a fast-growing employer-funded enrollment channel. That last channel is both a growth driver and a regulatory hedge that reduces federal-funding dependence, which the 90/10 analysis below shows is a necessity rather than an option.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet and cash generation are the strongest part of the story.

**Multi-year trajectory:** Revenue rose from $1.07B (2022) to $1.27B (2025); net income climbed from $46.7M to $126.6M over the same span, a sustained margin recovery.

**Q1 2026 (quarter ended 3/31/26):**
- Revenue $305.9M, up only 0.8% YoY. Net income $32.8M (+10.3%). GAAP diluted EPS $1.48 vs $1.24; adjusted EPS $1.42 vs $1.30. A material portion of the EPS gain came from a lower effective tax rate (22.4% vs 29.2%, on a $2.6M stock-comp windfall) and a ~7% lower share count rather than operations.
- Segment divergence is the entire story. USHE revenue fell 3.8% to $212.6M with operating income down 14.9% to $25.5M; ANZ grew 7.4% to $51.8M (mostly FX) but ran a $2.0M operating loss; ETS grew 21.0% to $41.5M with operating income up 42.2% to $19.7M. ETS now produces roughly a quarter of segment operating profit on ~14% of revenue.
- Cash flow quality is high: operating cash flow $87.4M (up from $67.7M), capex $10.1M, ~94% of revenue is tuition, and bad debt improved to 3.7% of revenue from 4.2%.

**Capital structure:** Zero drawn debt against a $250M revolver, ~$162.6M cash and securities, Altman Z ~4.59. The one watch-item touching book equity is ANZ goodwill: $523.5M plus $65.8M of indefinite-lived intangibles, against a reporting unit whose 2024 impairment test showed only a ~17% fair-value cushion.

**Capital allocation:** $0.60 quarterly dividend (~$13.6M/quarter), $40.0M of buybacks in Q1 at an average $81.11, and $173.5M of remaining authorization through December 2026. Share count fell from ~24.3M (Mar 2025) to ~22.6M (Mar 2026). The buyback above today's $75 price is shareholder-friendly in intent, though the $81 average repurchase mark sits underwater against the current quote.

Financial Health scores 7.7: the leverage profile, FCF conversion, and liquidity are all top-quartile for the peer set; the only deductions are the ANZ goodwill impairment overhang and the reliance on tax/buyback mechanics in reported EPS growth.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the thesis weakens. Total revenue grew 0.8% in Q1, and consensus puts current-year EPS at $7.19 (+2.6% revised over 90 days) and next year at $8.09 (+0.6% revised). These are stable, low-single-digit revisions, not the kind of estimate-revision velocity that signals an earnings acceleration. The earnings track record is mixed: three beats in the prior four quarters, but the most recent (Q1) missed by 6.8%, breaking a streak of 20%+ beats and triggering the negative reaction that pushed the stock toward its 52-week low.

The growth picture decomposes cleanly. USHE is a low-to-no-growth, margin-pressured segment that management is intentionally re-mixing toward lower-revenue-per-student channels (employer-funded enrollment, cheaper Sophia credit pathways). Title IV revenue per credit earned fell 6.7% on a trailing-four-quarter basis, having been as steep as -9.6%. Management frames this as strategic, and it does reduce federal dependence (persistence improved to 88.5%), but the practical effect is that the dominant segment is shrinking in revenue terms.

ETS is the genuine growth engine: +21% revenue, +42% operating income, ~47% segment margins, and employer-affiliated enrollment up to 34.5% of USHE. At ~14% of revenue it is not yet large enough to lift the consolidated growth rate above low single digits. Management has framed Q1 2026 as the "2026 low point" and guided to ~200 bps of margin expansion this year via AI productivity, ETS growth, and the employer strategy, a credible internal plan that has to be demonstrated against the OBBBA headwinds (below) before it is worth a re-rating.

The secular backdrop is unhelpful: private for-profit enrollment fell 1.2% in fall 2025, the first decline since 2021, even as total post-secondary enrollment rose 1.0%. Growth Prospects scores 4.8: the strongest forward dimension in our framework is precisely where SEI is weakest, with a shrinking core and a growth engine too small to compensate near-term.

## Valuation Assessment

On surface metrics, STRA is cheap. At $75.33, trailing P/E is 13.3x ($5.41 EPS) and forward P/E on the $7.19 estimate is ~10.5x, versus a peer median of 15.6x (PRDO 13.5, LRN 15.6, LOPE 19.1, GHC 17.1, DUOL 14.2). The 3.2% dividend yield is the highest in the group (peer median 0.0%). Analyst targets ($80 low, $86 median, $95 high) sit entirely above the current quote.

The discount is not a free lunch. The peer-relative de-rating is severe and persistent: cumulative TSR of 97.9 against the peer group's 198.2, a gap that widened from 110.7 vs 180.0 a year earlier. A discount of this magnitude, on a business whose net income more than doubled, tells you the market is pricing the regulatory overhang (OBBBA, 90/10 proximity, ANZ caps) and USHE's organic stagnation, not mispricing the earnings. A low multiple that reflects a structural, identifiable risk is a value trap until that risk resolves or a catalyst forces a re-rating.

The estimate-revision signal does not rescue the cheapness here. Revisions are only +2.6% (current year) and stable over 30 days, far below the threshold that would mark an underestimated acceleration and justify treating analyst targets as stale floors. With a flat-to-shrinking core and a small (if fast-growing) ETS engine, the consensus targets look more like fair-value anchors than lagging indicators.

What does support the valuation score is the absolute yield and capital return: the ~3.2% covered dividend plus remaining buyback authorization against market cap implies a mid-to-high single-digit return-of-capital even with flat earnings, and the balance sheet underwrites that floor. We score Valuation 5.5: moderately constructive for the yield and FCF support, but capped because the headline cheapness is a warranted regulatory discount, not a discrete catalyst-driven mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. for-profit post-secondary market is large, fragmented, and competitive, with no dominant player and intensifying competition as traditional universities expand online modalities and states push "free college" programs. SEI's position is bifurcated. In ETS, it is gaining share and building a structural advantage: the employer-funded channel is a genuine differentiator that simultaneously serves growth and regulatory-hedging objectives. In core USHE, the trajectory is stagnant-to-eroding: Capella's graduate/doctoral concentration is a structural negative against the July 2026 Grad PLUS elimination, and the deliberate re-mix toward lower-revenue channels caps segment growth.

Torrens in Australia is uniquely exposed to migration-driven international-student policy (Ministerial Direction 111 capped enrollment), which pressures both growth and the goodwill carrying value. The long-term addressable market is real — ~64M U.S. adults with no more than a high-school education and ~32M with "some college, no degree" — and SEI's affordability/employer strategy is the right response. The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving, warranting a Competitive Position score of 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by specific, dated, asymmetric regulatory catalysts, most of which are not fully discounted at a hard-stop level.

1. **Strayer's 90/10 ratio at 89.64%.** Under the post-2023 all-federal-funds methodology (which now counts VA/DoD funds as federal), Strayer derived ~89.64% of cash-basis revenue from federal funds in FY2024, only ~36 bps below the 90% ceiling. A breach triggers provisional certification; two consecutive breaches mean loss of Title IV eligibility. This is a binary, near-cliff exposure and the single most underappreciated specific risk. Capella is more comfortable at 67.88%.

2. **OBBBA, effective July 2026.** Elimination of Grad PLUS loans (with new aggregate caps) is a direct demand headwind for graduate-heavy Capella (58% grad), and a new program-level earnings-accountability framework could strip Direct Loan eligibility from underperforming programs. The exposure is not yet quantifiable but the effective date is imminent.

3. **ANZ goodwill impairment.** $523.5M of goodwill plus $65.8M of indefinite-lived intangibles on a unit with only a ~17% fair-value cushion as of the 2024 test, tied to volatile Australian immigration policy. A non-operational risk to book equity if international-student constraints persist.

4. **Torrens Fair Work litigation.** On March 17, 2026, the Full Federal Court reinstated a compliance notice alleging academic underpayment; Torrens is seeking leave to appeal to the High Court. No provision recognized; an adverse outcome could create retroactive wage liability across academic staff.

5. **Cybersecurity incident (Feb 25, 2026).** A ransomware-style attack on the U.S. network was contained within a week with no data encryption, but individual-notification and regulatory-reporting work was ongoing, leaving a residual reputational/liability tail.

6. **Borrower Defense / Sweet v. Cardona.** Capella is among ~150 schools on the automatic-relief list; institutional recoupment risk persists.

Risk Factors scores 4.0: multiple binary, near-dated regulatory events with asymmetric downside that are not fully reflected in a single-point valuation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term positioning is complacent rather than fearful. One-month ATM implied volatility is 24.0% against 32.9% trailing historical volatility (an 8.8-point discount), an IV percentile of 4%, a balanced 2.9% put/call skew, and a normal contango term structure (24.0% near vs 30.8% at six months). On its face this implies cheap optionality and no acute event being priced in the front month, though a longer-dated reading shows IV stepping up to 35.3% by August, consistent with the July 2026 OBBBA effective date and the Q3 enrollment cycle. (A separate delta feed showing ATM IV spiking to 112.6% appears to be a thin-options artifact and we treat it as non-informative.) The options market is not yet pricing the regulatory binaries that dominate the fundamental risk profile, which argues for caution rather than complacency on a multi-month view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95+):** ETS continues compounding at 20%+ with ~47% margins, the ~200 bps of guided margin expansion lands, and the employer-funded strategy lifts the non-federal revenue mix enough to defuse the 90/10 concern entirely. USHE stabilizes as the re-mix matures, OBBBA's grad-loan impact proves modest (cushioned by higher aggregate caps), and the regulatory overhang lifts. At that point the peer-relative de-rating reverses and the stock re-rates toward the peer median multiple on $8+ EPS, supported throughout by the yield and ongoing buyback. Insider buying by the Chairman ($80.82) and a director ($72.71) signals management sees the shares below intrinsic value.

**Bear Case ($60):** OBBBA's Grad PLUS elimination meaningfully dents Capella graduate enrollment in FY2026–2027, USHE revenue-per-student keeps falling faster than ETS can offset, and Strayer's 90/10 ratio breaches the 90% ceiling, triggering provisional certification and a sharp re-rating. An ANZ goodwill impairment dents book equity, and the Torrens wage litigation produces a retroactive liability. Earnings stall or decline, the dividend's appeal fades against rising regulatory tail risk, and the "value" multiple compresses further as the de-rating proves structural rather than transitory.

**Base Case ($82):** ETS grows but cannot fully offset USHE drag; consolidated revenue grows low single digits and EPS lands near the $7.19–$8.09 consensus path. The regulatory risks simmer without a hard breach, the 90/10 buffer holds on the strength of the employer strategy, and the stock grinds back toward the lower end of the analyst target range on yield support and continued buyback, with the multiple staying below peers to reflect the residual regulatory discount.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income rationale). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (late July 2026), which coincides with the OBBBA July 2026 effective date.**

This is a hold for the covered dividend plus aggressive buyback and the strong balance sheet, while the regulatory binaries resolve. The appreciation case requires evidence that ETS growth is outrunning the USHE/regulatory drag, which is not yet demonstrated.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Strayer's 90/10 ratio improving by ≥100 bps (clear daylight below the ceiling) driven by employer/Sophia mix, removing the cliff risk.
- Consolidated revenue re-accelerating above ~3% YoY with USHE revenue stabilizing (no longer declining), confirming the "2026 low point" framing, plus EPS estimate revisions turning positive by >5% over a quarter.
- Tangible evidence that OBBBA's Grad PLUS impact on Capella graduate enrollment is contained (graduate enrollment flat-to-up in FY2026 reporting).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A 90/10 breach or guidance acknowledging a likely breach (provisional certification risk).
- A material ANZ goodwill impairment, or an adverse final outcome in the Torrens Fair Work litigation with a quantified retroactive liability.
- USHE revenue declining at an accelerating rate (worse than -5% YoY) with no offsetting ETS acceleration, or a dividend/buyback cut signaling cash-flow stress.

**Reassessment point:** Late-July 2026 earnings, which will be the first read on enrollment behavior into the OBBBA effective date and on the guided 200 bps margin expansion.

## Conclusion

Strategic Education is a financially sound, cash-generative business returning capital aggressively at an optically cheap multiple. The cheapness is the market's verdict on a real, identifiable problem set — a 70%-of-revenue core in structural revenue decline, a 90/10 federal-funds ratio 36 bps from a hard regulatory cliff, and a July 2026 loan-program change that hits the company's graduate-heavy franchise — not a discrete mispricing waiting to correct. The TSR halving versus peers, against doubling net income, is the clearest evidence that the discount reflects priced regulatory risk rather than market error.

We hold for the yield, the buyback, and the balance sheet, which together underwrite a defensible return floor while the regulatory situation resolves. We do not chase the cheapness: the growth profile is weak where it matters most, the binary risks are dated and largely undiscounted in front-month options, and the sector backdrop (declining for-profit enrollment, intensifying competition) is a headwind. The score moves to 5.6 from 5.5–6.5 prior, reflecting the approaching OBBBA effective date and our growing conviction that the discount is warranted rather than opportunistic. A move to BUY requires either the 90/10 buffer widening materially or ETS growth visibly lifting consolidated revenue; a move to AVOID would follow a 90/10 breach, an ANZ impairment, or an adverse Torrens ruling.
