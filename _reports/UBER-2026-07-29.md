**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Uber has completed one of the more decisive turnarounds in large-cap technology, moving from a $9.1B net loss in 2022 to roughly $10B of net income in FY2025 while generating substantial free cash flow. The operating engine remains healthy: Q1 2026 income from operations grew 57% year-over-year to $1.9B, operating margin expanded to 15% from 11%, and volume metrics stayed robust (Gross Bookings +21% constant currency, Trips +20%, monthly active consumers +17%). Management is returning capital aggressively, repurchasing $3.0B of stock in Q1'26 with $16.2B remaining on the $20B authorization. On operating fundamentals alone, this is a compounding platform trading at a reasonable ~17.5x forward earnings after a 22% price decline over the trailing twelve months.

The debate is the autonomous-vehicle endgame, and it is why we are moving to HOLD after four consecutive BUY ratings. Uber does not build AV technology; it aggregates partners' vehicles onto its demand network. That model is asset-light in R&D terms but leaves Uber without control of the core technology, and the evidence in 2026 has turned negative on the margin. Waymo ended its Phoenix deployment on Uber in May 2026 and reabsorbed those vehicles into its own app, the two are set to compete directly in London in 2026, and Uber has committed to a minimum of 35,000 Lucid vehicles with Nuro L4 systems — a fleet-ownership commitment reportedly exceeding $10B that contradicts the pure-aggregator narrative. The stock's drawdown from its September 2025 high of $101.99 reflects the market repricing the probability that AV owners disintermediate the aggregator.

Our net view: the operating business justifies a valuation well above the current price, but the AV question is a genuine binary that we cannot resolve with the current evidence, and it is trending in the wrong direction. That combination of strong fundamentals, a cheap-ish multiple, and a deteriorating central strategic variable warrants a HOLD rather than a BUY. We would upgrade on evidence that Uber is winning the aggregator role (new exclusive AV supply, deepening Waymo economics) and downgrade further on continued partner defection.

## Company Overview

Uber operates a global technology platform across three reportable segments. Mobility (ridesharing, roughly 50% of Q1'26 Gross Bookings) connects riders with drivers and includes advertising and financial-partnership products. Delivery (Uber Eats — food, grocery, alcohol, retail; roughly 48% of GB) connects consumers with merchants and couriers and carries a fast-growing, high-margin advertising business. Freight (roughly 2% of GB) connects shippers with carriers and remains a small, loss-making segment (–$30M operating in Q1'26).

The platform reaches 15,000+ cities across 70+ countries with 199M monthly active platform consumers. Its moats are network effects from dense driver/rider liquidity, scale, brand, the multi-product "super-app" cross-sell anchored by the Uber One subscription, and the embedded advertising business. Uber recognizes revenue as an agent — net of driver/merchant earnings — in most markets. That accounting posture is central to its economics and would flip if drivers were reclassified as employees.

## Financial Analysis

The financial footing is strong and the story is operating leverage. Q1 2026 revenue was $13.2B, up 14% year-over-year (+10% constant currency), on Gross Bookings of $53.7B (+21% cc). Income from operations reached $1.9B (+57%), with segment operating income of $2.0B in Mobility (+28%) and $961M in Delivery (+43%). Free cash flow was $2.29B; operating cash flow was $2.35B.

Two reported figures require normalization before any judgment.

First, net income attributable to Uber fell 85% to $263M. That is almost entirely a $1.5B non-cash unrealized loss on minority equity stakes (Grab –$713M, Didi –$674M), not operational deterioration. Operating income grew 57% in the same quarter. Uber's GAAP net income is structurally noisy because of mark-to-market swings on its Didi ($2.3B), Grab ($1.96B), and Aurora ($1.34B) holdings, and this quarter's headline is a case of a screen misreading a strong operating quarter as a blow-up. This dynamic also explains the earnings-surprise record — the –81.8% Q1'26 and –82.8% Q4'25 "misses" against a +345% Q3'25 "beat" are almost entirely investment mark-to-market, not operating variance.

Second, Mobility revenue grew only 5% while Mobility Gross Bookings grew 25%. The gap is a UK business-model change effective January 2, 2026 that reclassified roughly $1.0B per quarter of driver payments from cost to a revenue contra. There is no profit impact; it is a presentation change tied to UK VAT law. Analysts unaware of this will misread Mobility as decelerating when the underlying volume grew 25%.

The balance sheet supports the buyback program. Uber holds $6.1B of unrestricted cash and short-term investments against $10.6B of total debt (well-laddered notes maturing 2028–2054, no revolver drawn, $5B undrawn facility). Total equity is $25.6B, though the accumulated deficit remains $10.4B and goodwill is ~$8.9B. The company carries roughly $11B in deferred tax assets from prior-loss carryforwards, with the valuation allowance largely released, a driver of low cash taxes near-term. Share count is declining (2.04B versus 2.09B a year earlier). We score Financial Health 7.5: strong FCF generation, appropriate leverage for a scaled platform, and disciplined capital structure, held below 8 by the still-large accumulated deficit and the >$10B of AV capital commitments that will pressure the asset-light framing.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is broad-based and durable: Trips +20%, MAPCs +17%, Gross Bookings +21% cc. Delivery advertising and the Uber One subscription continue to layer higher-margin revenue onto the transaction base. Estimate revisions are modestly constructive — current-quarter EPS +3.5% over 90 days, next-quarter +5.3%, next-year +4.4% — though current-year estimates were cut 9.9% over 90 days, largely reflecting the investment mark-to-market noise flowing through GAAP EPS. Consensus sees EPS moving from roughly $3.04 this year to $4.54 next year, implying continued operating-income compounding.

The revision picture does not reach the +30%-in-90-days threshold that would signal a systematic underestimation of a turnaround, and Uber is not at a cyclical trough — it is a mature-growth platform. So we treat the estimate trend as confirmation of a healthy business rather than a standalone BUY catalyst. Growth Prospects score 7.0, down from prior reports' 7.5–8.0. The downgrade reflects two things: the AV transition introduces uncertainty into the long-run Mobility take rate (the largest profit pool), and the growth is now partly being bought via M&A (SpotHero ~$600M, Blacklane ~$550M, Getir Türkiye $435M) rather than delivered purely organically.

## Valuation Assessment

At $70.74, Uber trades at ~15.6x the $4.54 next-year consensus EPS and ~17.5x the trailing/forward figure the profile reports. Against Industrials peers (median P/E 35.3 — Honeywell 35.3x, Eaton 38.5x, Deere 32.2x, Parker Hannifin 33.3x), Uber looks less than half as expensive. That comparison is not clean; those are diversified industrial compounders, not platform businesses, so the peer set overstates the discount. But even against a fair software/platform benchmark, ~17.5x forward earnings for a business compounding operating income at ~50%+ and growing bookings ~20% is undemanding.

The valuation math cuts in favor of the stock. If Uber delivers the ~$4.54 next-year EPS and the market applies a 20x multiple — reasonable for a platform growing bookings 20% with expanding margins — that is ~$91, roughly 28% above the current price. Analyst targets reinforce this: mean $103.89, median $105.90, with a low of $70 and a high of $150. The current price sits at the analyst floor.

We stop short of a high valuation score for two reasons. First, our own experience with "cheap" scores as value-trap flags counsels caution — a low multiple is a warning to demand an independent catalyst, not a buy signal by itself. Here the independent catalyst (the buyback and operating leverage) is real, which supports a 6.5, but the AV overhang is precisely the kind of structural risk that can keep a multiple compressed regardless of near-term earnings. Second, the analyst targets — while a floor on a cheap, growing stock — assume the AV endgame resolves favorably; if disintermediation risk is real, the targets are stale in the wrong direction. Valuation scores 6.5: asymmetric upside on the numbers, capped by an unresolved structural discount.

## Competitive Landscape

Uber is the global scale leader in ridesharing and a top-two U.S. food-delivery player. The defining industry dynamic is the transition to autonomous ridehailing, and this is where the competitive trajectory has weakened.

Waymo is the clear AV leader with ~4,000 vehicles, 500K+ trips per week, and expansion into ~20 new cities. The strategic question is binary: are AVs a new supply source that flows onto Uber's demand-aggregation platform (bull case), or a disintermediation threat where AV owners capture demand directly through their own apps (bear case)? In 2026 the evidence tilted toward tension, with the Phoenix exit and London competition. Waymo still runs exclusively on Uber in Austin and Atlanta, so the relationship is not severed, but the most capable AV operator is increasingly favoring its own consumer app, which is the specific mechanism the bear case requires.

Uber's response has been to secure its own AV supply: the Lucid commitment was raised from 20,000 to a minimum 35,000 vehicles, equipped with Nuro L4 systems over six years. This partially de-risks supply access but converts an asset-light aggregator into a capital-intensive fleet participant, and it signals that Uber itself does not believe pure aggregation is sufficient. Competition remains intense across the core business too (Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, Bolt, Didi, Delivery Hero) with structurally low switching costs.

Competitive Position scores 5.5, continuing the decline from 8.0 to 7.0 in prior reports. The trajectory, not the current level, drives the score. Uber remains a scaled leader today, but the moat that matters most for the next decade (control of, or preferred access to, autonomous supply) is contracting at the margin, and the most important partner is demonstrating it can route demand around the aggregator.

## Risk Assessment

**Autonomous-vehicle disintermediation — the central risk.** Uber does not own the AV technology. If Waymo, Tesla, or Zoox deploy at scale on their own apps and undercut Uber's pricing, the Mobility profit pool erodes. The Phoenix exit and London competition are early, concrete data points that this risk is materializing rather than hypothetical. This is the single variable that justifies the drawdown and caps our rating.

**Driver classification.** Global and unresolved. California's Prop 22 was upheld, but the state AG's pre-2020 suit is pending, and adverse rulings have accumulated in Switzerland, France (URSSAF), New Zealand (November 2025 Supreme Court ruled four drivers are employees), and Mexico (December 2024 reclassification law). Forced reclassification would fundamentally alter both the economics and the agent-basis revenue presentation.

**UK VAT dispute.** Roughly $1.8B (£1.4B) in HMRC assessments (March 2022–September 2024) paid under protest and booked as a receivable, with more assessments expected. Uber expects to prevail on appeal, but this is a material contingent exposure.

**Minority-stake volatility.** Didi, Grab, and Aurora holdings create large swings in reported net income; ~48% of the Aurora stake is pledged as collateral on exchangeable notes.

We score Risk Factors 5.0. The AV question is a genuine binary with asymmetric downside that is not fully priced — the market has begun to price it (hence the drawdown) but the outcome is unresolved and trending unfavorably. The classification and VAT exposures are real but more slowly-developing and partly priced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 46.6% against 34.4% one-year historical volatility — rich by 12.3 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (46.6% near-term versus 39.5% at six months), which signals the market expects an imminent catalyst, consistent with the earnings cadence and AV newsflow. Put/call open interest sits at 1.38 with a modest 2.9% put skew near-term that steepens to –4.5% at six months, indicating growing demand for downside protection over the medium term. Short interest is low at 2.6% of float (2.5 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short and there is no squeeze dynamic. The options market shares our caution on near-term direction and medium-term downside, without pricing a collapse, which confirms rather than contradicts the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$105–150).** Uber remains the indispensable demand aggregator. AVs flow onto its platform as incremental supply, Mobility take rates hold, Delivery advertising and Uber One compound margins, and the buyback shrinks the share count against rising operating income. Apply a 22–25x multiple to $4.54+ EPS growing into the high-$5s and the stock reaches the low-$100s; a fully favorable AV outcome supports the $150 high target. This is the case the current cheap multiple would reward.

**Bear case (~$50–60).** AV owners (Waymo, Tesla) disintermediate the aggregator, routing demand through their own apps and undercutting Uber's pricing. The Lucid/Nuro fleet commitment turns Uber into a capital-intensive operator competing on cost against better-funded technology owners. Mobility margins compress, the multiple derates toward the low teens on trough take rates, and driver-classification or VAT rulings add discrete hits. At 12–13x a pressured earnings base, the stock revisits the low-$50s.

The bull/bear spread is wide because the AV outcome is binary and unresolved. The current price and multiple are attractive if the bull case holds, but the trend in the evidence over the past two quarters has moved toward the bear mechanism, which is why we are not adding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** We see a positive 12-month operating thesis and a reasonable valuation, but the central strategic variable is deteriorating enough to withhold a BUY. This is a deliberate downgrade from four consecutive BUY ratings; the bar for continuing an unchanged BUY was high, and the AV evidence over the trailing two quarters did not clear it.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report and any material AV partnership news (Waymo economics, Lucid/Nuro deployment milestones, London launch dynamics). The 8.9% implied move on the one-month options straddle points to elevated event risk around the next print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence Uber is winning the aggregator role: a new exclusive AV supply agreement, expansion (not contraction) of Waymo trips on Uber's app, or a deepening of AV economics that preserves Mobility take rates.
- Sustained operating-margin expansion above 15% with Mobility Gross Bookings holding ~20% growth, confirming the leverage story survives the AV transition.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s that widens the margin of safety on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Additional AV partners pulling vehicles off Uber's platform, or Tesla/Waymo scaling own-app ridehailing that visibly compresses Uber's Mobility volumes.
- An adverse, enforceable driver-reclassification ruling in a major market (UK, EU-wide, or a binding U.S. federal standard) that forces the agent-basis model to unwind.
- Operating-margin reversal below ~11%, signaling the leverage story is breaking rather than pausing.

## Conclusion

Uber is a scaled, profitable, free-cash-flow-generative platform compounding operating income at high-double-digit rates, buying back stock aggressively, and trading at a low forward multiple after this year's decline — a setup that on operating fundamentals alone would support a BUY. We are stopping at HOLD because the question that will define the next five years — whether Uber remains the indispensable demand aggregator or gets disintermediated by AV owners who increasingly favor their own apps — is unresolved and trending against the bull case. The Waymo Phoenix exit, the London competition, and the Lucid/Nuro fleet commitment collectively signal that the asset-light aggregator thesis is under real pressure, and the market's drawdown reflects that repricing. The valuation and buyback provide downside support and genuine upside if the aggregator role holds, but we need to see the AV evidence stabilize or improve before returning to a BUY. Overall score 6.35, HOLD with a growth-hold rationale.
