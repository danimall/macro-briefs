## Executive Summary

Canadian National's second quarter was better operationally than the headline margin figures suggest, and less durable than the raised guidance suggests. Both statements can be true, and reconciling them is the entire investment question.

The operational reality: Q2 2026 revenue rose 11% to C$4,753M on 5% revenue-tonne-mile growth, while total operating expenses rose C$338M — of which fuel alone accounted for C$246M, or 73%. Non-fuel operating expenses rose just C$92M, or 4.1%, against 11.3% revenue growth. Management disclosed that higher year-over-year fuel prices diluted the operating ratio by 210 basis points while leaving EPS unaffected, because surcharges lift revenue and fuel expense in roughly equal dollar terms. Applying that disclosed drag to the 50bp adjusted OR deterioration (62.2% versus 61.7%) implies underlying ex-fuel margin improvement of roughly 160bp. Average fuel price went from C$3.55 to C$5.67 per gallon, a 60% increase. Any screen reading "operating margin compressed" on this quarter draws the wrong conclusion about CN's cost execution, which was among the better quarters the company has posted: fuel efficiency of 0.836 US gallons per 1,000 GTMs was a company record for both the quarter and the half, gross tonne-miles per average employee rose 9%, and headcount fell 4.8% on an average basis.

The durability question: on July 24 CN raised FY2026 guidance from "flattish" RTM growth and EPS growth "slightly exceeding volume" to low-single-digit RTM growth and mid-to-high single-digit adjusted EPS growth. On the C$7.63 FY2025 base that implies C$8.09-8.32. With H1 actual at C$3.88, implied H2 is C$4.21-4.44 against H2 2025 of C$3.92 — an increase of C$0.29 to C$0.52. The CFO guided a fuel tailwind of "close to $0.15" in Q3 and "around $0.10" in Q4, totaling C$0.25. At the low end of guidance the disclosed fuel tailwind covers roughly 86% of the required increase; at the high end, roughly 48%. This is a decomposition, not an accusation: management disclosed the sensitivity unprompted and conditioned it explicitly on prices holding, volume assumptions genuinely improved, and the Fast Track program has delivered about C$100M of realized benefit year to date. But CN's own WTI band widened from US$60-70 in January to US$80-110 by April and stayed there in July. A guidance raise substantially levered to oil falling is not a structural earnings step-up that should be capitalized into the multiple.

At US$126.60 (C$178.31), CN trades at 21.9x current-year consensus of C$8.14, 19.8x next-year C$9.00, roughly 14.8x LTM adjusted EBITDA, a 2.05% dividend yield and a 3.1-3.6% free cash flow yield. That is a fair-to-full price for a franchise growing carloads at zero, RTMs at low single digits, and EPS at high single digits with roughly 30% of that growth coming from share count. We rate the shares HOLD with a base case of US$134, a bull case of US$152 and a bear case of US$106.

## Company Overview

CN operates an approximately 18,900 route-mile network — 18,800 at end-2024, 18,900 at end-2025 and unchanged at Q2 2026 — making it a mature, physically non-expanding franchise where growth must come from density, pricing, length of haul and access rights rather than new track. It is the only Class I railroad reaching three coasts: Pacific at Vancouver and Prince Rupert, Atlantic at Halifax, and Gulf at New Orleans and Mobile via the former Illinois Central corridor. The company reports in Canadian dollars under U.S. GAAP and files a Form 40-F as a Canadian MJDS foreign private issuer rather than 10-K/10-Q.

The freight book is markedly more resource-weighted than U.S. peers. On FY2025 freight revenue of C$16,681M: intermodal 23.3% (+4% y/y), grain and fertilizers 21.9% (+7%), petroleum and chemicals 20.9% (+2%), metals and minerals 11.8% (-4%), forest products 11.0% (-5%), coal 5.8% (+3%), automotive 5.3% (flat). Grain/fertilizers, petroleum/chemicals, coal and metals/minerals together are roughly 60% of freight revenue. That composition insulates CN somewhat from trans-Pacific container cycles and truck competition but concentrates exposure in weather-dependent grain and in Canadian resource production.

**FY2025 was a cost year.** Revenue rose 2% on 1% RTM growth, yet adjusted OR improved 120bp and adjusted EPS rose 7%. The drivers were a fuel price decline from C$4.41 to C$3.91 per gallon (-11%), a 3.1% reduction in average headcount, and 4% growth in GTMs per employee. ROIC was flat at 12.9% and adjusted ROIC declined 10bp to 13.0%. The margin gain did not convert into better returns on capital, because CN simultaneously spent C$3,658M of gross property additions against C$17,304M of revenue — 21.1% capital intensity. For a business whose case rests on incremental volume over an already-capitalized network, flat ROIC through a 120bp margin improvement is the single most important disconfirming datapoint in the financials.

**H1 2026 free cash flow improvement is entirely capital allocation, not cash conversion.** FCF rose C$294M to C$1,842M (+19%). But cash from operations *fell* C$33M to C$2,876M. Investing outflows fell C$327M, of which lower gross property additions explain C$190M and roughly C$84M of proceeds from the Q1 sale of a portion of the Newmarket subdivision (Washago and Sundridge, Ontario, generating a C$66M pre-tax gain excluded from adjusted results) explain most of the balance. This is a deliberate outcome: CN cut its 2026 capital program to approximately C$2.8B net of customer reimbursements from roughly C$3.3B, and management attributed flat OCF to higher required tax payments, which is a legitimate timing explanation against C$62M higher H1 net income. The FCF *level* is robust; the *attribution* matters for anyone modeling forward cash conversion. Investing outflows are back-half weighted (Q4 2025 alone was C$1,232M), so H1 FCF must not be annualized. A normalized FY2026 range of roughly C$3.8-3.9B is a sensitivity, not a forecast.

**Balance sheet is investment-grade with modest deterioration.** Adjusted debt rose to C$23,056M from C$21,210M — debt alone up C$1,829M year over year — taking adjusted leverage to 2.61x from 2.50x against management's stated 2026 target of 2.7x. At current LTM adjusted EBITDA of C$8,832M, that target implies roughly C$790M of incremental debt headroom. Interest coverage on LTM operating income of approximately C$6,669M against LTM interest expense of C$936M is about 7.1x; Q2 interest expense rose 10% to C$241M on both the higher balance and refinancing at higher rates. For a railroad, 2.6x leverage and 7x coverage are unremarkable and appropriate. What the incremental debt bought was buybacks, not capacity: H1 repurchases of C$1,323M more than tripled the C$407M spent in H1 2025 while capex fell C$190M.

Two quality flags. First, "other components of net periodic benefit income" contributed C$517M over the LTM to June 30, 2026 (up 8% from C$478M) against LTM pre-tax income of roughly C$6,377M — 8.1% of pre-tax income from a below-the-operating-line, largely non-cash pension credit that is sensitive to discount rates and expected asset returns. This is standard U.S. GAAP treatment, transparently reconciled, and CN deducts it from adjusted EBITDA; it inflates EPS-based multiples relative to EV/EBITDA-based ones. Second, the C$337M "pension plans in deficiency" line in the leverage reconciliation covers only the underfunded subset of plans and does not establish an aggregate pension shortfall.

**Verification gaps that constrain the financial health score.** Cash, total assets, book equity, working capital, the debt maturity schedule, covenants and average cost of debt were not obtainable — CN's 40-F index returned HTTP 403 and the XBRL endpoint yielded only shares outstanding. No covenant analysis was possible and standard distress screens could not be computed. The derived credit metrics are consistent with investment grade, but they are not a substitute for the full balance sheet.

**Earnings delivery is consistent and modest.** Four consecutive beats: 2.2%, 3.8%, 2.5% and 4.9%. This is a company that guides conservatively and clears the bar by low single digits — supportive of confidence in consensus, but not a pattern that generates upside surprises large enough to re-rate the multiple.

## Growth Analysis

The composition of CN's growth is the crux. Q2 2026 total carloads were 1,409k versus 1,414k, down 0.4%, while RTMs rose 5% and freight revenue per carload rose 12%. Intermodal carloads fell 5% while intermodal revenue rose 8%. Growth is coming from heavier, longer, better-priced hauls, not from more shipments, and part of the revenue-per-RTM gain (+6%) is fuel surcharge recovery rather than price, which management conceded and which cannot be independently verified because CN does not break out surcharge revenue.

CN is also deliberately shedding volume. Overseas intermodal is guided weak in H2 "in part related to the demarketing of certain low profitability shipments through the Port of Vancouver." Metals and minerals saw fewer long-haul shipments into Alberta and more short-haul U.S. movements. Coal RTMs fell 6% in H1 on Canadian West Coast production challenges. Forest products remain weak with the CCO stating there is "no sign yet that housing starts will improve."

Against that, the growth vectors that management emphasizes are real and specific:
- **Grain**: a record June 2026 month of 2.67 million metric tonnes, beating the prior June record of 2.64Mt from 2020, with H1 RTMs +12%, achieved without incremental fleet additions and with 15% better cycle times. The catch: guidance assumes 2026/2027 crops merely in line with five-year averages after 2025/2026 came in above average, and both the CEO and CCO flagged a "tough comp on Q4."
- **Canadian energy egress**: Phase 2 of the Greater Toronto Area fuel terminal ramped in April 2026, driving roughly 30% RTM growth in refined products. NGL export partnerships with Keyera and AltaGas via Prince Rupert, with new fractionation capacity coming online in H2 2026 and an ACE Terminal agreement contributing "probably in the 2028 or so time frame."
- **Capacity projects at low incremental cost**: Zanardi Bridge near Prince Rupert and the Glen Valley/Abrahamson double-track near Vancouver, with more than 300 customer growth projects announced across the network.
- **Fast Track**: approximately C$100M of realized pre-tax benefit year to date, roughly C$0.12 per share after tax at a 25.5% rate on 606M shares.

Consensus has moved modestly higher: current-year estimates up 3.2% and next-year up 3.0% over 90 days, current-quarter up 4.8% over 90 days (up 4.0% over 30 days). This is positive earnings momentum but nowhere near the magnitude that signals a fundamental shift. Next-year consensus of C$9.00 against current-year C$8.14 implies 10.6% EPS growth, which requires the fuel tailwind to persist, buybacks to continue at pace, and grain to hold near average. The buyback contribution is quantifiable: Q2 diluted share count fell from approximately 626.7M to 606.3M, or 3.3%, contributing roughly 3.4 points of the 11% adjusted EPS growth. Roughly 30% of EPS growth was share count.

This is a mature compounder with low-single-digit volume growth, high-single-digit EPS growth, and a meaningful portion of near-term EPS momentum sourced from commodity price timing and share retirement. It is not a growth story, and the growth score reflects that.

The growth-adjusted picture is unflattering. On guidance growth of 6-9%, the forward P/E of roughly 22x implies a PEG of 2.4-3.7. On consensus growth from C$8.14 to C$9.00 (10.6%), PEG is approximately 2.1. Roughly 3.4 points of that growth is buyback-driven and roughly C$0.25 of the H2 step-up is fuel-driven; adjusting for both leaves organic earnings growth in the low-to-mid single digits, against which 22x forward earnings and 14.8x EBITDA are full.

The market is not obviously wrong about anything here, which is what keeps the valuation score below the midpoint. There is no compressed multiple concealing normalized earnings power, no identified catalyst that re-rates the franchise, and no absolute yield support at 2.05%. Two candidates for asymmetry exist and both fail the test: the Union Pacific settlement optionality is contingent on a merger CN cannot control and is currently under adversarial attack (see Risk Assessment), and the ex-fuel cost improvement of roughly 160bp is genuine but already embedded in the raised guidance.

Sell-side dispersion is unusually wide and internally inconsistent. The provided consensus is a mean of US$139.82 and a median of US$140.92, with a low of US$114.19 and a high of US$152.59 — the mean implying 10.4% upside. Separately reported Canadian-bank targets (RBC C$205, CIBC C$198, Scotiabank C$199) imply roughly 11-15% upside from C$178.31, while a June 25 Barclays target of US$109 sits about 14% below spot and appears to pre-date both the Q2 beat and the UP agreements. J.P. Morgan is reported at Hold. None of these were verified against primary research documents. The recommendation distribution has degraded: strong-buy plus buy fell from 16 to 15 over three months while hold rose from 12 to 13 and a strong-sell appeared, with the aggregator classifying sentiment as degrading.

These targets are not treated as a floor. CN is a fairly valued mature compounder at roughly 22x forward earnings where the target and the price largely reflect the same set of assumptions about fuel, grain and buyback pace.

CN's own repurchase prints provide the cleanest internal reference. Q2 2026 buybacks were C$454M for approximately 2.9M shares, an average of roughly C$156.6. FY2025 was approximately 15M shares for C$2,047M, or roughly C$136.5. The shares now sit about 14% above the most recent quarterly average repurchase price and about 31% above the FY2025 average. The CFO's language — "we will continue to be opportunistic on our current share buyback program" — combined with the 24M-share NCIB being a ceiling rather than a commitment (roughly 35% utilized in five months), is consistent with a slower pace at higher prices. That would erode the 3.4-point buyback contribution to EPS growth. Offsetting this, roughly C$790M of debt headroom to the 2.7x target, expanding with EBITDA, means buybacks could equally continue at pace.

## Competitive Landscape

CN's structural moat is among the widest in North American transportation and is not in dispute: sole Class I access to three coasts; exclusive rail service at Prince Rupert, the shortest great-circle Asia-North America routing; wholly owned EJ&E Chicago bypass; single-line haul from Western Canadian resource origins to the Gulf and East; and industry-leading fuel efficiency. The relevant question is trajectory, and the trajectory is genuinely two-sided because of the proposed Union Pacific acquisition of Norfolk Southern.

On July 22, 2026 CN announced two structurally distinct agreements with UP, frequently conflated in press coverage:

**A commercial agreement, not contingent on the merger.** CN receives new rights for Canada-Mexico volumes moving via the Memphis gateway with direct access to Ferromex. In exchange, CN grants UP rights to additional capacity over the EJ&E for U.S.-origin/destination traffic only, with UP funding any required expansion and CN retaining dispatch control. Management sized the Canada-Mexico truck market at roughly US$3B and immediately caveated it: "those that have gone after the truck-to-rail conversion, most haven't met the targets that they put in place. This is tough." No definitive agreement has been confirmed — the parties signed a binding MOU, with provisions effective "as soon as the definitive agreement is in place."

**A settlement agreement, fully contingent on STB approval and closing.** CN would acquire Norfolk Southern's ownership interests in Kansas City Terminal Railway and the Terminal Railroad Association of St. Louis, gain overhead rights between Tuscola and East St. Louis, Illinois, rights to serve customers between St. Louis and Kansas City, use of UP's Neff Yard, access to roughly five identified 2-to-1 shippers plus additional 3-to-2s where "commercially and operationally feasible," and a most-favored-remedy clause extending to CN any provisions the STB grants others. In return, CN will not oppose the merger. The CEO was explicit: "we don't have access to that market right now."

CN has therefore exchanged its regulatory veto, exercisable at the moment of maximum leverage, for a package in which the reliable leg is the one where it gives capacity to a competitor, and the valuable leg vests only if a contested merger closes with CN's remedies intact. A benign reading is equally available: CN faced the merger regardless, extracted the best available terms early, and the most-favored-remedy clause lets it inherit whatever concessions opponents win. We are not inferring that management acted against shareholder interest; the structure is consistent with prudent risk transfer. CN has already booked C$17M/C$34M/C$49M of advisory costs tied to the STB review.

If the merger closes, CN faces a single competitor spanning coast to coast with pricing and service scale it cannot match, partially offset by a Kansas City footprint it has never had, terminal railroad equity, and longer average length of haul. CPKC's CEO has publicly said the CN-UP deal "does little to assuage concerns around competition in the industry," and CPKC competes directly in the Canada-Mexico corridor CN is targeting.

One competitive indicator is moving the wrong way. Safety metrics have deteriorated for two consecutive periods: Q2 2026 accident rate 2.30 per million train miles versus 1.56 (+47%), injury frequency 1.01 versus 0.83; H1 2.26 versus 1.82; and FY2025 was already worse than FY2024 (1.84 versus 1.69). This sits in tension with the COO's statement that "we are encouraged by the progress we made over the last few years," and with the productivity narrative of 5% fewer employees moving 3% more GTMs on longer trains. These are unaudited FRA-reported estimates, volatile at quarterly granularity on small event counts, and CN separately disclosed a severe winter tail, wildfire-driven traffic bunching in Northern Ontario and record volumes — any of which can elevate incident rates without indicating systemic degradation. We are not asserting a causal link to the workforce reduction, but the trend is now two periods long and warrants monitoring.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel is now a first-order earnings variable.** The disclosed OR sensitivity is 210bp. Surcharge lag makes fuel a headwind on the way up and a tailwind on the way down. The guidance raise leans on prices holding or falling. An analyst asked directly whether CN can hedge the downside; management did not commit. If WTI moves toward the top of CN's own US$80-110 band, the C$0.25 guided tailwind reverses and the low end of guidance becomes difficult.

**The contingent UP leg may never vest, and the merger is under active attack.** The STB rejected an initial application as incomplete, unanimously accepted a revised version on May 28, 2026, then immediately paused its own review pending supplemental information due July 27. UP/NS completed that response on July 27 with added commitments including expanded gateway pricing. On August 7, 2026, BNSF, CSX and five shipper organizations — the American Chemistry Council, The Fertilizer Institute, National Industrial Transportation League, American Fuel & Petrochemical Manufacturers, and the Alliance for Chemical Distribution — petitioned the STB to reject or dismiss the application outright, arguing it fails to address requirements established in 2001. This is a live, adversarial, multi-year proceeding whose outcome CN neither controls nor can any longer oppose.

**Grain concentration into a hard comparison.** Grain and fertilizers is roughly 22% of freight revenue and was the largest single H1 growth driver at +12% RTMs. Guidance assumes 2026/2027 crops merely in line with five-year averages after above-average 2025/2026 crops, and management has flagged the Q4 comparison against a record grain quarter. This risk is weather-dependent and non-diversifiable.

**Trade policy is an unhedged guidance assumption.** Management disclosed significant steel and aluminum tariffs, a prior-year pull-forward of overseas intermodal on tariff uncertainty, and said guidance "embeds a tariff level that looks a lot like what it is right now" while referencing hopes for a constructive trilateral agreement. USMCA outcomes are therefore an assumption, not a hedge.

**Labor status is unresolved.** CN lists labor negotiations among its risk factors and the 2024 Canadian rail work stoppage is recent precedent. Current collective agreement expiry dates and negotiation status could not be verified. Headcount down 4.4% year over year following a Q4 2025 reduction program (C$34M charge) is a productivity positive that arguably raises the stakes in the next bargaining round.

**Governance is unassessed, not clean.** As a foreign private issuer CN files no DEF 14A and its insiders are exempt from Section 16, so there is no Form 4 record and no verified data on CEO compensation, pay-for-performance alignment, incentive metrics, board composition or related-party transactions. The absence of Form 4 data must not be read as absence of insider activity — CN insiders report through Canada's SEDI system, which was not queried. The one compensation datapoint available is a disclosed approximately C$40M year-over-year increase in Q2 incentive compensation, consistent with a strong operating year but tied to metrics we cannot verify.

**Currency.** In H1 2026 a stronger Canadian dollar (C$1.378/US$ versus C$1.411) was a headwind worth C$107M of revenue and C$26M of net income at constant currency. CN then cut its H2 assumption to US$0.71 from US$0.73. For a U.S.-dollar holder of CNI this cuts both ways: CAD weakness helps reported CAD earnings but reduces the USD value of earnings and dividends.

**Wildfire and climate.** An active wildfire season in Northern Ontario and British Columbia produced traffic bunching from mainline shutdowns this quarter, with minimal disclosed financial impact. CN maintains a dedicated firefighting fleet. This is a recurring seasonal exposure for a network traversing boreal forest.

### Options Market Signal

The options market prices no stress and no event. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 19.9% sits slightly below 21.8% one-year historical volatility (spread -1.8%, characterized as fair) and in the 16th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew is 2.2% and described as balanced; the term structure is flat (19.9% near versus 19.0% far), with uncertainty evenly distributed. Put/call open interest of 0.11 is heavily call-tilted, and put/call volume of 0.55 confirms limited demand for downside protection. The nearest expiry implies a ±2.9% move; the January 2027 tenor implies ±11.5%, with zero put volume in that tenor making its -6.5% skew reading unreliable. Short interest of 2.8M shares is roughly 0.46% of shares outstanding at 2.0 days to cover — negligible.

Positioning is complacent into a period where the two largest swing factors (oil direction and the STB proceeding) are both unresolved and neither is company-controlled. Cheap near-term volatility at the 16th percentile makes protection inexpensive for holders who want to own the franchise through the Q3 print without the fuel exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (US$152, +20%)

Ex-fuel cost execution is genuinely improving: non-fuel operating expenses grew 4.1% against 11.3% revenue growth in Q2, implying roughly 160bp of underlying adjusted OR improvement, and Fast Track has C$100M realized with the major-terminal review essentially complete and intermodal terminals and network operation centers still to come. Volume is layering onto an already-capitalized network at low incremental cost — record grain moved without incremental fleet additions, 15% better cycle times, car velocity/train speed/dwell each improved roughly 3%. The energy egress pipeline (GTA terminal Phase 2 driving 30% refined-products RTM growth, Keyera/AltaGas NGL exports via Prince Rupert, new fractionation in H2 2026, ACE Terminal in 2028) plus Canada's trade diversification agenda gives CN a multi-year volume source that US peers cannot replicate. If oil stays in the lower half of CN's US$80-110 band, FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches roughly C$9.40 and a 23x multiple — within CN's historical range for a quarter of improving margins and clearing regulatory uncertainty — yields C$216, or US$153. The STB approving the merger with CN's remedies intact adds a Kansas City footprint, terminal railroad equity and roughly five 2-to-1 shippers on top.

### Bear Case (US$106, -16%)

Oil rises toward the top of CN's own band, the guided C$0.25 H2 fuel tailwind reverses, and FY2026 lands at or below the C$8.09 low end of guidance. The Q4 grain comparison against a record quarter misses because 2026/2027 crops come in below the assumed five-year average. Forest products stay depressed with no housing recovery in sight, coal production challenges persist, and the deliberate demarketing of low-margin Vancouver intermodal keeps reported volume growth narrow. Buybacks slow at prices 31% above the FY2025 average repurchase level, removing roughly 3 points of EPS growth. The STB rejects the merger or strips CN's remedies, leaving CN having surrendered its regulatory veto for the EJ&E capacity grant alone. On FY2027 EPS of C$8.30 at 18x — the low end of CN's historical range — the shares are C$149, or US$106. Barclays' US$109 target sits in this neighborhood. Secondary risks that would deepen this case: a significant derailment against a two-period deteriorating accident-rate trend, or a labor disruption in a bargaining round whose timing we could not verify.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case: US$134 (+5.8% from US$126.60), 12 months.** Math: FY2027 adjusted EPS of C$9.00 (next-year consensus, itself revised up 3.0% over 90 days) at 21x = C$189, converted at US$0.71 = US$134.2. The 21x multiple is a modest discount to the current 21.9x on current-year earnings, reflecting the reversal of the fuel tailwind partially offset by continued Fast Track delivery and low-single-digit volume growth. Adding the 2.05% dividend yields a total return of roughly 8%.

**Bull case: US$152 (+20%).** C$9.40 FY2027 EPS at 23x = C$216 → US$153. Aligns with the provided analyst high of US$152.59.

**Bear case: US$106 (-16%).** C$8.30 FY2027 EPS at 18x = C$149 → US$106.

**Probability-weighted expected return:** approximately 55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear gives roughly +3.3% price return plus 2.05% dividend, or about +5.4%. That is a fair-to-slightly-below-fair risk-adjusted return, which is what a HOLD means.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price toward US$108-112 (roughly C$152-158, or 17-18x next-year consensus) with FY2026 guidance intact — this restores a margin of safety at a multiple where CN has historically been accumulable.
2. Q3 2026 (late October, historically) demonstrating ex-fuel adjusted OR improvement of 100bp or better *with* operating cash flow growing year over year, confirming that the H1 FCF gain converts from capex-driven to conversion-driven, alongside adjusted ROIC turning up from 13.0%.
3. STB approval of the UP-NS merger with CN's settlement remedies intact, plus execution of a definitive agreement on the non-contingent Mexico/EJ&E commercial leg — this converts a contingent option into a modelable earnings stream.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut or narrowed to the low end alongside WTI sustaining above US$100 — the specific configuration in which the guidance raise unwinds.
2. Q4 grain RTMs down more than 10% year over year against the record comparison, or CN revising its crop assumption below five-year averages.
3. Buyback pace falling below roughly C$400M per quarter for two consecutive quarters, removing the roughly 3-point EPS contribution while the multiple stays above 21x forward.
4. A reportable mainline derailment with material liability, or a third consecutive period of rising accident rate above 2.3 per million train miles.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results, historically late October, is the natural decision point. It carries the first clean read on the H2 fuel assumption, the Q4 grain setup, whether operating cash flow has resumed growth, and buyback pace at prices above the Q2 average print. ## Conclusion

CN is a high-quality, wide-moat franchise executing well on cost — the ex-fuel operating story in Q2 2026 was materially better than the reported 50bp adjusted OR deterioration implies, and any reading of margin compression here is an artifact of fuel pass-through inflating both the numerator and denominator of the ratio. Balance sheet strength is adequate and appropriate at 2.61x adjusted leverage and roughly 7x interest coverage, the dividend has risen for 30 consecutive years, and the share count has fallen 15.4% over seven years.

What the price does not offer is asymmetry. At US$126.60, roughly 3.8% below the 52-week high after a 37% twelve-month advance, the shares trade at approximately 21.9x current-year and 19.8x next-year consensus for guided mid-to-high single-digit EPS growth, of which management's own disclosed fuel tailwind covers roughly half to nearly all of the H2 increase and buybacks supply about 30%. Carloads are flat, ROIC has been flat for two years despite 120bp of FY2025 margin improvement, H1 free cash flow growth came entirely from lower capex and an asset sale while operating cash flow declined, and 8.1% of pre-tax income sits in a non-operating pension credit. The Union Pacific settlement is a contingent option on a merger CN can no longer oppose and that BNSF, CSX and five shipper associations are actively asking the STB to dismiss. Sell-side sentiment is classified as degrading, with a strong-sell appearing in the distribution over the past two months.

HOLD. Existing holders should own the franchise, collect the 2.05% dividend and reassess at the Q3 print in late October. New capital should wait for either US$108-112, or evidence that operating cash flow and ROIC are improving alongside the margin gain. The classification is a growth hold — there is a coherent path to a US$134 base case driven by identifiable catalysts — but the margin of safety at today's price is thin enough that we are not recommending purchases here.
