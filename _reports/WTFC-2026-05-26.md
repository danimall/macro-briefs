## Executive Summary

Wintrust Financial is a $71 billion-asset community banking franchise delivering 14%+ returns on tangible common equity while growing assets at 10%+ annually. The company reported record Q1 2026 net income of $227.4M ($3.22/share), beating consensus by 6.1%, extending a streak of four consecutive quarterly beats. Forward EPS estimates have been revised upward by 5.2% over 90 days, reflecting accelerating earnings momentum from stable net interest margins, improving efficiency, and continued organic loan growth.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a differentiated premium finance business representing 33% of average loans that provides national diversification, higher yields, and short duration unavailable to most regional peers; (2) consistent double-digit earnings growth driven by organic balance sheet expansion and operating leverage; and (3) a valuation at 12.6x trailing earnings (11.5x forward on FY2026 estimates of $13.03) that does not adequately reflect the company's superior growth profile relative to the peer median P/E of 11.6x on materially slower growth. The primary risk is CRE concentration at 26% of the loan portfolio, though office exposure is limited to 3.2% and net charge-offs improved to 0.14% in 2025.

## Company Overview

Wintrust Financial operates through 16 separately chartered community banks and 209 banking locations primarily in the Chicago metropolitan area, with expansion into southern Wisconsin, northwest Indiana, and west Michigan (via the 2024 Macatawa acquisition). The multi-charter structure, unusual in an industry that consolidated toward single-charter models, serves as both a competitive tool (expanded FDIC coverage through the MaxSafe® product) and an organizational discipline mechanism (local management accountability).

The company operates in three segments: Community Banking (74.7% of net revenue), Specialty Finance (18.4%), and Wealth Management (6.9%). The Specialty Finance segment houses the premium finance business — FIRST Insurance Funding and Wintrust Life Finance — which originated $21.8B in short-term loans to finance insurance premiums in 2025. This nationally diversified business generates higher yields than traditional banking with rapid portfolio turnover (average duration under 6 months) and historically superior credit performance (0.19% net charge-offs on P&C premium finance).

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power:** FY2025 net income reached $823.8M ($11.40/diluted share), up 19% from $695.0M in FY2024. Net interest income grew 13% to $2.22B on a net interest margin that held steady at 3.52% despite the Fed cutting rates during the year. This margin stability reflects successful deposit repricing — the company maintained NIM essentially flat versus 3.51% in 2024 even as the rate environment shifted.

**Efficiency:** The efficiency ratio improved to 55.64% from 57.17% in 2024, demonstrating operating leverage as revenue growth outpaced expense growth. Non-interest expense grew 7% versus 11% revenue growth.

**Credit Quality:** Non-performing loans sit at 0.35% of total loans. Net charge-offs declined to $72.3M (0.14% of average loans) from $94.4M (0.21%) in 2024. The allowance for credit losses stands at $460.2M (0.87% of total loans), providing adequate coverage. Provision expense declined to $95.6M from $101.0M.

**Capital:** CET1 ratio of 10.3% provides a 330bp buffer above the 7.0% well-capitalized minimum. Tangible book value per share grew 18% to $88.66. The stock trades at 1.70x tangible book, justified by the 14.43% ROTCE and growth trajectory.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $910.3M and free cash flow of $860.4M support the current $0.55/quarter dividend (17.5% payout ratio) with substantial retained earnings for growth.

## Growth Analysis

Wintrust has grown total assets from $56.3B to $71.1B over two years while maintaining nearly unchanged profitability metrics. Loan growth of 11% in 2025 ($48.1B to $53.1B) was predominantly organic, with the Macatawa acquisition contributing only $1.3B of the $5.0B increase.

Forward estimates reflect continued momentum: consensus projects $13.03 EPS for FY2026 (up 14% from $11.40 reported in FY2025) and $13.86 for FY2027 (+6% further). The 90-day upward revision of 5.2% for the current year signals that analysts are still catching up to the company's earnings power. The Q1 2026 annualized run-rate of $12.88 suggests consensus for the full year ($13.03) is achievable without further acceleration.

Growth drivers include: (1) continued premium finance origination growth leveraging the expanding insurance market, (2) organic commercial lending growth in the Chicago metro area, (3) full integration benefits from Macatawa (cost saves plus cross-selling), and (4) wealth management expansion through the LPL platform transition.

The dividend has grown from $0.50/quarter in 2025 to $0.55/quarter in 2026, a 10% increase providing a secondary return component.

## Valuation Assessment

At $149.89, WTFC trades at:
- 13.1x trailing EPS ($11.40)
- 11.5x current-year consensus ($13.03)
- 10.8x next-year consensus ($13.86)
- 1.69x tangible book value ($88.66)
- 1.47x book value ($102.03)

The peer median P/E is 11.6x. WTFC trades at a modest premium (13.1x trailing), but this premium is insufficient given the growth differential. Peers like SSB (10.1x), ZION (9.6x), and ONB (12.3x) generally deliver mid-single-digit earnings growth versus WTFC's 11-14% trajectory. On a PEG basis (using forward P/E / expected growth), WTFC at roughly 0.8x-0.9x is cheaper than most peers.

The analyst consensus target of $174.67 (median $178.00) implies 17-19% upside. The target range of $150-$190 suggests the low target is essentially current price, indicating limited downside even in analyst bear cases. At 13-14x the $13.03 forward EPS estimate — a reasonable multiple for a bank growing earnings at 10%+ with 14% ROTCE — fair value sits at $169-$182.

The 1.5% dividend yield is below the peer median of 2.5%, reflecting the company's preference for retaining capital to fund growth. This is appropriate given the growth trajectory and 18% tangible book value growth rate.

## Competitive Landscape

**Premium Finance Moat:** The insurance premium finance business is genuinely scarce among publicly traded banks. It requires specialized origination infrastructure, carrier relationships, and operational expertise. The business contributes disproportionately to profitability (higher yields, shorter duration, better credit metrics) while providing geographic diversification that pure community banks lack. Combined P&C and life insurance premium finance receivables of $17.2B represent 33% of average loans — a structural advantage that cannot be replicated quickly.

**Scale in Chicago Metro:** With 209 locations and 16 charters, Wintrust has established density advantages in the Chicago area that create deposit gathering efficiencies. The multi-charter model, while operationally complex, provides the MaxSafe® competitive product for deposits and maintains community accountability at the branch level.

**Growth vs. Peers:** Five-year TSR of 249% versus the peer group's 217% demonstrates sustained competitive outperformance. The company has grown assets 26% over two years while improving profitability — most peers this size are struggling to find organic growth at all.

The primary competitive threat is the gradual commoditization of community banking through fintech and larger bank digital offerings. Wintrust's $142M annual technology investment (+16% YoY) addresses this, but the company remains dependent on physical branch density for its core deposit franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (Primary Risk):** CRE loans total $13.9B (26% of the portfolio), concentrated in the Midwest. Management's own sensitivity analysis indicates a 10% decline in CRE prices would increase the construction loan allowance requirement by 120-125% and increase non-construction CRE reserves by 50-55%. In a severe CRE downturn, this could consume $150M+ of pre-tax earnings (roughly one quarter's worth). Mitigating factors: office exposure limited to 3.2% of total portfolio, largest CRE segment is multi-family (25% of CRE), and industrial (23%) remains well-supported by logistics demand.

**Interest Rate Risk:** With 62% variable-rate loans ($32.8B SOFR-indexed), further rate cuts would compress loan yields. The $6.15B hedging book provides partial protection, and deposit repricing success in 2025 demonstrates management's ability to manage through rate cycles. However, if the Fed pursues additional easing while deposit costs floor, NIM compression is possible.

**Geographic Concentration:** Heavy dependence on Chicago metro and Illinois more broadly exposes the franchise to regional economic weakness or Illinois fiscal deterioration. The Macatawa acquisition provided some Michigan diversification.

**Tariff/Trade Policy:** Explicitly flagged in the 10-K as a new risk factor. Given significant commercial lending to manufacturing-adjacent borrowers in the Midwest, tariff-driven supply chain disruption could impair borrower credit quality.

**Options Market Signal:** Near-term ATM IV of 25.0% is essentially in line with historical volatility of 26.1% — no unusual uncertainty priced. The put/call volume ratio of 11.0 appears elevated but the OI ratio of 0.24 suggests this is noise (potentially a single block trade) rather than informed positioning. The flat term structure confirms markets see no binary event approaching. Short interest at 3.2% of float with 4.7 days to cover is unremarkable.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175-190):** NIM stability continues through 2026 as deposit repricing offsets loan yield compression. Organic loan growth of 8-10% combines with operating leverage to deliver $13.50+ EPS. CRE concerns fade as credit metrics remain benign. Premium finance originations accelerate with insurance market hardening. Multiple expands toward 13-14x forward earnings as the market recognizes the growth premium versus peers. Price target: 14x $13.50 = $189.

**Bear Case ($120-130):** CRE deterioration materializes through 2H 2026, requiring $150M+ in additional reserves. NIM compresses 15-20bp as rate cuts accelerate. Midwest economic slowdown (tariff-related) slows loan growth to 3-4%. EPS falls to $10.50-$11.00. Multiple compresses to 11x. Price: 11x $11.50 = $127.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Bull case target:** $185-190. Requires sustained NIM + accelerating premium finance + CRE benign.

**Bear case target:** $125-130. Represents ~15-17% downside from current price.

**Upside/downside from current ($149.89):** Base case +13-17%; Bull case +23-27%; Bear case -13-17%.

**Timeframe:** 12 months. Key catalyst dates: Q2 2026 earnings (July), FOMC rate decisions (ongoing), CRE performance through year-end.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. NIM compression exceeding 20bp in any single quarter (below 3.30%) — would signal deposit repricing failure
2. CRE non-performing loans exceeding 1.0% of total CRE portfolio — would signal credit cycle turn
3. Two consecutive quarterly EPS misses versus consensus — would break the earnings momentum thesis

## Conclusion

Wintrust Financial represents an unusually well-positioned regional bank earning 14%+ ROTCE, growing earnings at double-digit rates, and trading at a modest premium to slower-growing peers that does not adequately price the growth differential. The premium finance business provides a structural competitive advantage unavailable to most community banks. With estimates being revised upward, four consecutive earnings beats, and the analyst target implying 17% upside, the risk/reward favors ownership at current levels. The primary risk — CRE concentration — is manageable given low office exposure, strong reserves, and a track record of prudent credit management. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $170-175.
