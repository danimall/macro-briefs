**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TSMC sits at the center of the AI hardware build-out as the world's largest dedicated foundry, holding roughly 70% of global foundry share against Samsung's ~7%. Its Q1 2026 results confirmed the demand thesis: revenue of NT$1.134 trillion (~US$35B), up 35% year over year, and net income of NT$572.48 billion, up 58%, with gross margin at an all-time high. Management raised guidance twice in 2026 — Q2 revenue to US$39–40.2B, full-year growth above 30% in USD, and 2026 capex to the high end of the US$52–56B range. Every 2nm wafer through end-2026 is already sold out, entirely to AI data-center customers, and the company is funding a historic capacity ramp internally while raising the dividend ~28%.

The valuation question is whether ~26x forward earnings (the consensus-based figure, against a trailing/forward P/E of ~38x on the ADR-EPS basis in the data feed) is reasonable for a business compounding earnings at 30%+ with 60% gross margins and the strongest demand visibility in its history. It is. EPS estimates have risen +9.6% (current year) and +9.0% (next year) over 90 days, and the company has beaten consensus for four straight quarters. The discount to AI-exposed growth peers is almost entirely a Taiwan geopolitical risk premium, not a fundamental quality discount. That premium is real and should be respected through position sizing rather than avoidance.

The recommendation is BUY, with explicitly bounded sizing (2–3% of a diversified book). The thesis carries one genuinely binary risk, a China-Taiwan military escalation that would impair the equity catastrophically, which caps conviction below where the fundamentals alone would place it. The stock has run ~130% over the trailing twelve months and now trades near its 52-week high of $449, so the margin of safety on multiple is thin; the case rests on earnings continuing to compound and consensus targets (mean $467.84, high $600) being revised upward as they have been all year. Near-term options are pricing elevated stress (IV 57.3% vs 35.3% realized, backwardation), consistent with the event-window character of June rather than a fundamental warning.

## Company Overview

TSMC is a pure-play contract semiconductor manufacturer. It does not design or sell its own chips; it fabricates silicon designed by its customers, most notably Nvidia (now its largest customer), Apple, AMD, Broadcom, Qualcomm, and MediaTek. The competitive moat is process leadership at the leading edge, reinforced by a flywheel: leading-edge process leadership attracts the highest-margin customers, which funds the industry's largest R&D and capex budget, which sustains process leadership.

As of Q1 2026, high-performance computing (HPC, including AI and 5G) was ~61% of revenue, up sharply and reflecting the AI buildout. Advanced nodes (7nm and below) were ~74% of total wafer revenue, with sub-3nm shipments alone at ~25%. TSMC is the sole or near-sole supplier of the most advanced AI accelerator chips, and holds the CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity essential to Nvidia and AMD AI GPUs. The customer base has structurally shifted: Nvidia has overtaken Apple as the largest customer, which means TSMC is now first an AI-infrastructure company and second a mobile-chip foundry, a change that concentrates its fortunes in the hyperscaler AI capex cycle and alters its cyclicality profile.

The company files a 20-F as a foreign private issuer rather than a 10-K, and does not file US-style proxies or Section 16 insider reports. Financial figures below are drawn from corroborated reporting of TSMC's official quarterly disclosures; a few data points (notably the Arizona first-year profit figure) are single-sourced and flagged accordingly.

## Financial Analysis

TSMC is in elite financial shape. FY2025 revenue surpassed US$100 billion for the first time. Q4 2025 alone delivered NT$1.4609 trillion (~US$46.2B) in revenue, up 20.5% sequentially, with net income of NT$505.74 billion, the eighth consecutive quarter of year-over-year profit growth.

Q1 2026 extended the trend: a fourth consecutive record quarter and a beat against ~NT$1.127T consensus, with net income beating ~NT$543B estimates. Gross margin built on a Q3 2025 figure already near 60%. The earnings-surprise record reinforces confidence in forward estimates: four straight beats of 3.7%, 8.0%, 5.2%, and 4.4%, a consistent pattern of conservative guidance.

The capital structure is the cleanest in the cyclical-capital-goods world. TSMC is net-cash and funds its capex internally. The 2026 capex guide (up as much as 37% YoY), now skewed to the high end, is the single most important demand signal in the company's disclosures; management does not commit that scale of capital without multi-year order visibility. Despite that, the company raised its 2026 dividend to at least TWD 23/share (from TWD 18) and approved up to US$20B in additional capital for the Arizona subsidiary. High capex coexists with rising returns, the financial signature of a business with genuine pricing power and demand outrunning supply.

The principal financial watch item is utilization. The historic capacity ramp pays off only if AI capex remains durable; a moderation in hyperscaler spending would leave TSMC carrying large depreciation on underutilized new fabs. There is no evidence of that today (2nm sold out, capex raised, estimates rising), but it is the channel through which a cyclical downturn would arrive.

The score-trajectory data shows Financial Health improving from 8.5 to 9.0. The Q1 print, the second capex raise, and the dividend increase all confirm rather than reverse that trend.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is accelerating off an already-large base, which is unusual. Full-year guidance is above 30% in USD. The demand visibility is the strongest in the company's history: every 2nm wafer through end-2026 is pre-sold, entirely to AI data-center customers, and 2nm capacity is guided to grow at a ~70% CAGR from 2026 to 2028. Management characterizes this as a multi-year AI megatrend with "extremely robust" demand.

The estimate-revision trend confirms the shift in consensus, not just in the company's own commentary. Current-year EPS estimates have risen to $15.61 and next-year estimates to $19.52, with smaller but still positive 30-day moves. The combination of revenue growth above 30% and estimate revisions approaching +10% over 90 days is the profile that historically precedes continued re-rating as consensus catches up to fundamentals. The signal is moderated here by the fact that the stock is no longer cheap on a trailing basis, so I weight it as confirmatory of growth durability rather than as a standalone trigger for further multiple expansion.

The growth thesis has high durability because it is broad-based, not a single-company story. Hyperscaler cloud capex guidance exceeding $725B, ASML's order book, and memory supply-gap commentary stretching beyond 2028 all point in the same direction. When multiple independent participants in the same supply chain confirm the same demand, the thesis carries more weight than any one company's guidance. The risk to growth is not whether AI demand is real; it is whether the magnitude is being extrapolated too far, which is a cyclical-timing question rather than a structural one.

## Valuation Assessment

On the consensus EPS basis the analyst community uses, TSMC trades at roughly 26x forward earnings (the data feed's ~38x reflects the ADR EPS of $10.43, a different share basis; the relevant figure for cross-checking against growth peers is the ~26x forward). For a business compounding earnings 30%+ with 60% gross margins, ~70% market share, and the cleanest balance sheet in its industry, that is not a demanding multiple. The implied PEG is well below 1.0 on current-year growth.

The peer comparison sharpens the point. AI-exposed growth names — Nvidia, application-layer software, and integrators — trade at materially higher multiples for lower-quality or more contestable economics. TSMC's discount to that cohort is almost entirely a Taiwan geopolitical risk premium. The stock fell ~7% in a single week on Xi Jinping's Taiwan rhetoric during the May 2026 Trump-Xi summit, then recovered to new highs, illustrating that the multiple swings on geopolitical headlines rather than on fundamental revisions, which have been uniformly positive.

The valuation score is constrained to 6.0 for two reasons that are about price level, not business quality. First, the stock has run ~130% over the trailing year and trades near its 52-week high, so the margin of safety on the multiple is thin; the bull case now requires earnings to keep compounding rather than the multiple to expand. Second, at this price the asymmetry is bounded: upside to the consensus high of $600 is real but contingent on continued beats, while the geopolitical tail introduces a downside that no DCF captures. Analyst targets (mean $467.84, median $472.50) sit above the current price and have been revised upward through the year, so on inflection logic they function as floors rather than ceilings, though I temper that here because the magnitude of the prior run means the easy re-rating has already occurred. The score trajectory shows Valuation improving from 6.0 to 7.0 over recent reports; I hold it at 6.0 now because the price has risen faster than the multiple compression that the rising estimates would otherwise produce, leaving valuation roughly where it was on a forward basis with less room.

## Competitive Landscape

TSMC's competitive position remains dominant but is no longer unchallenged at the bleeding edge, which is the reason the Competitive Position score has declined from 9.5 to 8.0 across recent reports. The ~70% foundry share and CoWoS packaging monopoly are intact, and the Arizona fab reportedly posted a ~$514M first-year profit (single-source, Tech Times, May 2026), which if accurate weakens the long-standing bear thesis that leading-edge fabs cannot be economical outside Taiwan.

The watch-worthy cracks are early but credible. AMD has reportedly sent some 2nm work to Samsung, and Samsung's chairman reportedly visited Taiwan to court MediaTek with preferential 2nm access. Intel Foundry is positioned as a third leading-edge option with US policy support. The key analytical question is whether these are genuine technology-share shifts or supply-diversification hedges driven by geopolitical risk and the sold-out environment. The evidence leans toward the latter: customers want a second source precisely because TSMC is sold out and concentrated in Taiwan. But for the first time in years, rivals are landing punches rather than simply trailing, which is the reason competitive position is a notch below its prior peak even as the absolute moat remains formidable.

## Risk Assessment

The defining risk is binary and not capturable in any earnings model. TSMC's most advanced fabs sit roughly 100 miles from mainland China. During the May 2026 Trump-Xi summit, Xi reportedly warned that mishandling Taiwan could produce an "extremely dangerous situation," and the US reportedly postponed a $14B arms sale to Taiwan, read by some as reduced deterrence. Polymarket-implied probability of a Chinese invasion by end-2026 was ~7.45%, a tail with catastrophic equity consequences. This single factor caps the Risk Factors score at 5.0 and bounds the position size regardless of how attractive the fundamentals are.

Secondary risks: (1) Extreme customer concentration: Nvidia is now the largest customer and HPC/AI is 61% of revenue, so a hyperscaler capex pullback or an Nvidia-specific stumble would hit the highest-margin segment, and Nvidia's guidance reportedly already excludes China data-center revenue. (2) Geographic concentration of the leading edge in Taiwan despite Arizona/Japan diversification, exposing the company to earthquakes, drought, and energy constraints. (3) Newly elevated supply-input risk from the Middle East conflict (helium and hydrogen used in fabrication), which management has acknowledged but downplayed citing multiple sourcing and safety inventories. (4) US trade/tariff policy now explicitly linked to Arizona expansion commitments, embedding political pressure in capital allocation.

The geopolitical risk is partly priced (that is what the discount to growth peers represents), but it is not a smooth, continuous risk; it is a step-function that headlines can trigger overnight. That asymmetry is the reason this is a BUY with bounded sizing rather than a high-conviction overweight.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 57.3% against 35.3% one-year realized volatility, rich by 22 points, at the 76th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (57.3% near-term vs 48.6% at six months). IV jumped 15.7 points in three sessions alongside a 6% price gain. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (0.0% near-term, modestly put-tilted at longer tenors), and put/call volume (1.05) and OI (1.03) ratios are neutral. This is the signature of an event window (ongoing Taiwan and Middle East headlines) rather than directional bearish positioning. Short interest is negligible at 0.6% of float (1.9 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short overhang. The elevated IV argues for patience on entry timing and against chasing the move into the 52-week high; it does not contradict the fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$540–600).** AI demand sustains above-30% revenue growth through 2026 and into 2027, 2nm ramps at the guided ~70% CAGR with margins holding near record highs, and consensus EPS estimates continue rising toward and past $20 next year. The multiple holds at ~28–30x forward as the geopolitical premium narrows on continued de-escalation, taking the stock to the consensus high of $600 and beyond. The Arizona profitability data point validates geographic diversification, removing a structural bear argument.

**Bear case (~$300–340, roughly -23% to -32% from current).** Either AI capex moderates and TSMC's new capacity sits underutilized, compressing margins on heavy depreciation, or a Taiwan geopolitical shock (escalating rhetoric, an arms-sale standoff, or worse) re-rates the equity sharply lower as the risk premium expands. A genuine military escalation would produce far larger losses than this range and is the uninsurable tail; the -23% to -32% bear reflects a severe-but-non-catastrophic combination of cyclical moderation and widening risk premium.

The base case favors the bull side because the demand evidence is broad-based and the estimate revisions are positive and accelerating, but the bounded position size acknowledges that the bear tail is binary and unhedgeable.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$510, 12 months.** This applies ~26x forward to next-year consensus EPS of ~$19.52 (the multiple held roughly constant as earnings grow), which is ~+15% from the current $443.31. This sits between the bear case (~$300–340) and bull case (~$540–600).

**Bull case target: $540–600** (+22% to +35%) — multiple expansion to ~28–30x on continued beats and a narrowing geopolitical premium.

**Bear case target: $300–340** (-23% to -32%) — cyclical AI-capex moderation plus a widening Taiwan risk premium. A military escalation would breach this range with far larger losses.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to quarterly earnings (next report mid-July 2026) and the cadence of capex/2nm capacity updates.

**Upside/downside from current ($443.31):** +15% to base, +22–35% to bull, -23–32% to bear (excluding the catastrophic geopolitical tail).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Revenue growth decelerates below 20% YoY in any quarter, or management cuts full-year revenue or capex guidance, signaling AI-capex moderation.
2. A confirmed leading-edge share shift (not a dual-source hedge), e.g., a major customer moving a flagship product line entirely to Samsung or Intel at 2nm.
3. A material escalation in Taiwan Strait tensions (sustained PLA activity, blockade rhetoric translating into action, or a deterioration in US deterrence posture) that converts the geopolitical tail from priced premium to active risk; exit on this regardless of fundamentals.

## Conclusion

TSMC is the indispensable manufacturing node of the AI hardware supply chain, and Q1 2026 confirmed the thesis with accelerating revenue and earnings growth, record margins, a sold-out 2nm book through end-2026, and a self-funded capex ramp alongside the dividend hike. Forward earnings near 26x are reasonable for 30%+ growth, and rising consensus estimates plus four consecutive beats support confidence in the forward numbers. The discount to AI-exposed growth peers is a Taiwan geopolitical risk premium rather than a quality discount.

The recommendation is BUY, with deliberately bounded sizing of 2–3% of a diversified portfolio. The single binary risk, China-Taiwan military escalation, caps conviction and is the reason this is not a maximum-conviction position despite fundamentals that would otherwise warrant it. The stock's ~130% trailing-year run means the easy re-rating has occurred and the case now depends on earnings continuing to compound; near-term options stress argues for patience on entry rather than chasing the 52-week high. Accumulate on weakness toward the high-$300s/low-$400s, size for the geopolitical tail, and monitor the leading-edge competitive threads as the first credible challenge to the moat in years.
