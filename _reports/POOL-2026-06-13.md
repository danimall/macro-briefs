**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pool Corporation is the world's largest wholesale distributor of swimming pool supplies, equipment, and outdoor-living products, operating 455 sales centers and serving roughly 125,000 mostly small, family-owned customers. The structural attraction is an installed-base annuity: 64% of 2025 revenue came from non-discretionary maintenance and minor repair tied to the ~5.5M in-ground U.S. pools that require lifelong upkeep. That recurring floor cushioned the post-COVID downturn, during which revenue fell ~14% from the 2022 peak of $6.18B to ~$5.29B in 2025 and operating margin compressed from 16.6% to 11.0%.

The central tension is the divergence between operating trends and the tape. Q1 2026 inflected positive (sales +6%, adjusted EPS +8% to $1.43, management citing "continued improvement in discretionary categories"), yet the stock has fallen ~34% over the trailing year and ~43% from its 52-week high of $345, and was just removed from the S&P 500 (effective ~June 2026), moving to the SmallCap 600. Index-fund forced selling is a near-term mechanical overhang. Against that, directors, including former CEO Manuel Perez de la Mesa, deployed roughly $5.8M of open-market purchases at progressively lower prices ($176–$218) across March–May 2026, the strongest insider signal available.

Two specific items prevent an upgrade despite a reasonable valuation and clean balance sheet. First, the CEO departed abruptly on May 4, 2026, resigning from the board as well, days after presiding over a strong quarter, with separation terms not yet public. Second, the growth thesis still depends on a discretionary recovery (remodel + new construction = 36% of revenue) playing out against a housing cycle. The valuation is fair-to-attractive, not deeply mispriced, and the catalysts are unresolved. This is a HOLD.

## Company Overview

POOL aggregates all sales centers into one reportable segment but operates five networks (SCP, Superior, Horizon, NPT, Sun Wholesale) across North America, Europe, and Australia, offering 200,000+ SKUs from a fragmented manufacturer base. The 2025 revenue mix:

- **64% recurring maintenance & minor repair** (non-discretionary)
- **22% remodel / renovation / upgrade** (semi-discretionary)
- **14% new pool construction** (most cyclical)

Geographic concentration is high: California, Florida, Texas, and Arizona generate ~53% of net sales; North America is ~95%. The moat rests on scale-driven purchasing power, branch density, vendor rebate programs, proprietary brands (Regal, E-Z Clor, SuperPro, PoolStyle, NPT), and a digital ecosystem (POOL360, Horizon 24/7, BlueStreak). No competitor approaches national scale, though management itself flags "low barriers to entry" at the local level.

## Financial Analysis

The defining pattern is post-COVID normalization off the 2022 peak, with early stabilization in 2026.

| Metric | 2022 (peak) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 vs Q1'25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $6.18B | $5.54B | $5.31B | $5.29B | +6% ($1.14B) |
| Gross margin | 31.3% | 30.0% | 29.7% | 29.7% | 29.0% (–20bps) |
| Operating income | $1,026M | $747M | $617M | $580M | +7% |
| Operating margin | 16.6% | 13.5% | 11.6% | 11.0% | 7.3% (Q1 seasonal) |
| Net income | $748M | $523M | $434M | $406M | ~flat |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $11.30 | $10.85 | $1.45 (+2%) |

Revenue has flattened (2024→2025 essentially unchanged at ~$5.3B). New in-ground construction fell from 62,000 units in 2024 to under 60,000 in 2025, a 3–5% decline that looks like a cyclical trough. The key concern is margin, not the top line: operating margin compressed ~560bps from 2022 to 2025 as the high-margin discretionary mix shrank and S&A grew ~4% in 2025 on technology investment, greenfields, and wage/facility inflation. Gross margin held steady (~29.7%) on pricing discipline and private-label growth.

Financial health is solid. Altman Z of 3.1 sits in the safe zone; current ratio 2.24; leverage ratio 1.73x against a 3.25x covenant; fixed-charge coverage 4.73x; ROE 34.3% and ROA 11.2% typical of an asset-light distributor. The headline 2.06 debt/equity overstates leverage; it reflects equity shrinkage from $3.2B of cumulative buybacks, not balance-sheet stress. Debt rose to ~$1.2B mainly to fund repurchases, with share count down from 39.4M (2022) to ~36.4M.

Cash flow warrants caution. 2025 operating cash flow of $366M fell sharply from $659M in 2024, of which ~$68.5M was an IRS tax-payment timing swing and the remainder largely an inventory build (+13–14% YoY, with turns slipping from 2.8x to 2.6x). FCF was ~$310M. The inventory build ahead of price increases is defensible if demand recovers and a liability if it stalls. Capital allocation continued: ~$185M dividends (raised to $1.25/quarter for 2026), $341M of 2025 buybacks, $64M in Q1 2026 with $271M remaining on authorization.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: 1 beat, 3 misses across the last four quarters, including a –15.7% miss in Q4 2025. That Q4 miss is partly explained by a non-repeating comp: Q4 2024 was flattered by Hurricane Helene/Milton repair demand. The pattern argues for measured rather than high confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is cyclical recovery, not secular acceleration. Estimate revisions are essentially flat: current-year EPS consensus of $11.08 is up only +0.7% over 90 days, next-year $12.04 up +1.2%. This is not the >30% revision velocity that signals a fundamental inflection; it is a stable, low-single-digit grind. Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance of revenue up low-single-digit, gross margin ~29.7%, and diluted EPS of $10.87–$11.17, with opex growth ~3%.

The structural longer-term supports are real: an aging installed base driving recurring replacement demand (variable-speed pumps, automation, robotic cleaners, energy efficiency upgrades), Sunbelt migration, and high mortgage rates discouraging housing turnover in favor of backyard investment. The constraint is that the 64% recurring base still depends on the new-construction engine continuing to expand the pool count over time, and new construction is the most rate-sensitive segment. The discretionary recovery is plausible but not yet confirmed across a full Q2–Q3 selling season (61% of sales and 78% of operating income fall in Q2–Q3).

The Q1 2026 print is genuine evidence of troughing (+7% equipment, +5% building materials), but one seasonally small quarter does not establish the recovery. The growth score reflects a probable cyclical bottom offset by flat revisions and a rate-sensitive discretionary backdrop.

## Valuation Assessment

At $195, POOL trades at ~17.6x trailing EPS of $10.85 and ~17.6x the midpoint of 2026 guidance (~$11.02), with a 2.74% dividend yield. The stock has de-rated meaningfully from its multi-year norm in the high-20s/low-30s.

Peer comparison is awkward because the "Distributors" cohort is heterogeneous: LKQ at 13.0x P/E, GPC at a distorted 238.1x (depressed trailing earnings), ARMK at 40.7x; peer median 40.7x is not a usable anchor here. POOL is a higher-quality, higher-return business than LKQ but trades only modestly above it, and far below the median distorted by GPC/ARMK. On its own history and return profile (34% ROE, asset-light, recurring-heavy), ~17.6x forward on trough-cycle earnings is reasonable rather than rich.

The cycle-adjustment matters. If discretionary recovers and operating margin normalizes back toward the mid-teens over a multi-year recovery, mid-cycle earnings power is well above the current ~$11. That is the bull's cycle-adjusted case. But this is not a sharp inflection stock; revisions are flat at +0.7–1.2%, so analyst targets here are not stale floors lagging a fast-rising estimate; they reflect a slow grind. Consensus mean target of ~$256 (range $210–$300) implies meaningful upside if the recovery materializes, but the targets are themselves cycle-dependent.

The valuation is fair-to-modestly-attractive: a quality compounder at a trough multiple, but not a deep mispricing demanding a buy. The market is paying a normal multiple on trough earnings, which is appropriate. A 6.5 reflects asymmetry skewed slightly to the upside, capped by the absence of a near-term re-rating catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

POOL sits at the top of a fragmented distribution industry with no national-scale peer. Its branch density, purchasing scale, vendor rebate economics, and private-label penetration create a structural cost and service advantage that strengthens with scale. The digital ecosystem raises switching costs for small contractor customers. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: POOL has continued opportunistic bolt-on M&A (Vegas Stone Brokers, Great Plains Supply) and plans 5–8 greenfields in 2026, gaining share in a consolidating industry.

The genuine competitive risk is supplier disintermediation. The top three suppliers — Pentair (20%), Zodiac (12%), Hayward (11%) — account for 43% of COGS, and management explicitly flags that these manufacturers could sell direct. To date this has not materialized at scale because POOL's last-mile distribution to 125,000 fragmented customers is hard to replicate, but it is the central long-term moat question. The competitive position is strong and durable, with the trajectory tilting slightly down from prior assessments given the disintermediation risk being flagged more prominently.

## Risk Assessment

Risks specific to POOL, not generic sector truisms:

1. **Abrupt CEO transition (most important governance development).** Peter Arvan stepped down as President & CEO and resigned from the board effective immediately on May 4, 2026, days after presiding over a strong Q1 call and the 10-Q. The full board resignation is more complete than typical retirement, the separation terms are not yet public, and analysts have cited "CEO uncertainty" as weighing on shares. Until the 8-K/proxy text clarifies whether this was planned succession or a board-driven exit, this is an unresolved overhang.

2. **S&P 500 removal.** The move to the SmallCap 600 forces mechanical index-fund selling unrelated to fundamentals. Seeking Alpha has framed this as a trailing indicator of the decline, but it removes the passive large-cap bid and creates a near-term technical drag.

3. **Discretionary cyclicality tied to housing/rates.** New construction (14%) and remodel (22%) are highly rate- and confidence-sensitive. The 2022–2025 downturn is the live example, and the discretionary recovery thesis carries real downside if rates stay high.

4. **Margin durability.** The operating-margin compression may be partly structural (mix shift toward lower-margin maintenance/equipment) rather than fully cyclical. If margins do not recover with discretionary demand, mid-cycle earnings power is lower than bulls assume.

5. **Inventory build.** The 2025 inventory build with declining turns is a liability if demand disappoints.

6. **Supplier concentration** (43% of COGS in three names) and **geographic concentration** (53% in four states, exposed to weather and water-use restrictions).

7. **Weather/comp distortion.** Q4 2024 hurricane repair demand did not repeat, depressing 2025 comps and complicating trend reads.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing fair-to-cheap volatility with a clear near-term bearish tilt. Near-term ATM IV of 34.7% sits essentially on top of 1-year historical volatility of 34.2% (spread +0.5%), and IV percentile within the chain is 2%; the market is not pricing an imminent large move. The near-term put/call skew of 18.8% is elevated, and the put/call OI ratio rose to 1.68 from 1.53, indicating institutions are accumulating downside protection. Short interest is elevated at 12.1% of float (4.1 days to cover), consistent with bearish positioning around the index removal and CEO uncertainty. The term structure is in contango (34.7% near vs 38.5% at six months), a normal/complacent shape rather than acute stress; the skew flattens to slightly negative at longer tenors, suggesting the put demand is concentrated near-term event-hedging (index removal, CEO clarity) rather than a durable directional bet. The signal supports caution near-term without indicating a structural break.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($255–$300).** The cycle has bottomed, and Q1 2026 is the first leg of a multi-year discretionary recovery. As remodel and new construction recover, operating leverage returns and margins climb back toward the mid-teens, driving mid-cycle EPS well above $11. Insider buying, led by the former CEO, signals the board views the stock as materially undervalued. The index removal is a trailing technical event that creates a temporary entry point. At a trough multiple on a 34%-ROE recurring-revenue franchise with no national-scale competitor, a re-rating toward 20–24x on recovering earnings supports ~$256 (consensus mean) to $300 (consensus high).

**Bear Case ($150–$170).** The CEO's abrupt full-board resignation signals problems not yet disclosed, and separation terms surprise to the downside. The discretionary recovery stalls as mortgage rates stay elevated, pressuring the 36% of revenue tied to remodel and new construction. Margin compression proves structural rather than cyclical, capping mid-cycle EPS below bull assumptions. Forced index selling and elevated short interest amplify downside. At a 14–15x multiple on stagnant ~$11 earnings, the stock retests the low-$170s 52-week low and below.

The base case is a slow cyclical recovery with the stock range-bound-to-modestly-higher as discretionary demand troughs but the housing backdrop limits the pace. The presence of both a credible bull (insider buying, troughing demand, fair valuation) and a credible bear (governance overhang, housing cyclicality, margin question) makes this genuinely two-sided.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The valuation is reasonable and insiders are buying, but the risk/reward is two-sided rather than asymmetric, with two unresolved overhangs (CEO transition terms, discretionary recovery confirmation) and a consumer-discretionary backdrop facing housing headwinds. I am not prepared to upgrade until at least one of these resolves favorably.

**Why not a BUY at a ~6.05 overall.** Three concrete, falsifiable concerns block an upgrade. (1) With separation terms still not public, governance carries unquantified downside risk until the 8-K/proxy clarifies whether this was orderly succession or a board-driven exit. (2) The growth case requires a discretionary recovery to play out across the Q2–Q3 selling season against a rate-sensitive housing backdrop; one seasonally small Q1 does not confirm the recovery, and estimate revisions are flat rather than the velocity that would signal a true inflection. (3) The index removal forces near-term mechanical selling, and elevated put skew plus high short interest confirm institutions are positioned for further downside. A BUY here would require asymmetric upside, and at a trough multiple with these unresolved catalysts, the upside is contingent and the downside is specific.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CEO separation terms disclosed as planned/orderly succession AND the successor reaffirms strategy and guidance on the next call.
- Q2 2026 (reported ~late July) showing discretionary categories (remodel/new construction) growing, not just maintenance, with operating margin expanding YoY.
- Estimate revisions turning positive (current-year EPS revisions exceeding +3% over 90 days), confirming the consensus is catching up.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 2026 sales decelerating below +2% YoY or guidance cut, indicating the discretionary recovery has stalled.
- Operating margin compressing further toward 10% or below, confirming structural rather than cyclical impairment.
- Adverse CEO-separation disclosure (e.g., material disagreement, restatement risk) or further senior departures.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is Q2 2026 earnings (~late July), the most operationally significant quarter and the first full read on the discretionary recovery, plus disclosure of the CEO-transition terms in the next proxy/8-K.

## Conclusion

POOL is a high-quality, dominant distributor with a non-discretionary recurring revenue base, a clean balance sheet (1.73x leverage, 4.7x coverage, Altman Z 3.1), and aggressive insider buying during a ~40% drawdown. Q1 2026 suggests the post-COVID cycle is bottoming, and the forward multiple on trough earnings is reasonable for a 34%-ROE franchise. Those are genuine positives.

But the setup is not asymmetric enough to recommend buying. An abrupt, complete CEO/board resignation with undisclosed terms, mechanical index-removal selling, operating-margin compression whose cyclical-versus-structural nature is unresolved, and a discretionary recovery dependent on a still-elevated rate environment leave the risk/reward balanced. The insider buying is the strongest argument for patience, but director purchases do not, by themselves, resolve the governance and demand questions. **HOLD**, with a clear path to upgrade on confirmed discretionary recovery in Q2 and benign CEO-transition disclosure, and a path to downgrade on stalled sales or further margin erosion.
