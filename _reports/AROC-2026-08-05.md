## Executive Summary

Archrock is a US-only natural gas compression lessor: it owns 5,319 compression units and 4.79 million available horsepower, charges customers a fixed monthly fee per horsepower, and passes fuel gas and electricity cost to the customer. The model carries no direct commodity price exposure on compressed volumes, generates 71-72% segment adjusted gross margins, and produced $622.1M of operating cash flow on $1,489.8M of FY2025 revenue. That is a genuinely good business.

The problem is what the second quarter of 2026 revealed about its growth rate. FY2022-2025 revenue compounded at ~20.7% ($845.6M → $1,489.8M), but essentially all of it was purchased — TOPS for $868.7M cash in August 2024 and NGCS for $349.4M in May 2025. Q2 2026 was the first quarter with a near-clean organic comparison, and consolidated revenue fell 3.1% year over year to $371.2M, with contract operations up only 3.4%. Adjusted EBITDA was flat at $212.6M. Spot utilization declined for a third consecutive period to 94.4% from 96.0% a year earlier, period-end operating horsepower fell 2.9%, and management narrowed FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $865-885M by cutting $30M off the top end. Adjusted EPS of $0.38 missed consensus of roughly $0.48, the second consecutive double-digit miss after a 13.4% shortfall in Q1.

Two offsets deserve weight. First, the headline FY2025-to-FY2026 EBITDA "decline" is largely an artifact: FY2025 included approximately $35.0M of net sales-and-use tax audit settlements and credits recorded as a reduction to cost of sales, so ex-benefit FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA was ~$866M against a FY2026 midpoint of $875M — roughly +1% rather than −2.9%. Second, the balance sheet is materially stronger: leverage fell to 2.6x from 3.3x a year earlier against a 5.25x covenant, liquidity is $631M, and quarterly interest expense declined from $41.7M to $37.0M. Archrock also signed an eight-year contract covering ~665,000 horsepower — about 14.7% of operating horsepower — which converts a large block of month-to-month rollover risk into contracted duration.

At $35.74 the equity is ~$6,264M and enterprise value ~$8,607M, or 9.84x the FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoint and roughly 21x normalized adjusted EPS of $1.60-1.70. For a lessor earning ~11% ROIC against a ~8% cost of capital, growing organically at 1-5%, and about to raise growth capital intensity by 40-50%, that multiple leaves no margin of safety. Hold what you own for a 2.6% dividend covered 3.1x by cash available for dividend; do not add here.

## Company Overview

Two segments. **Contract operations** (~88% of FY2025 revenue, ~96% of Q2 2026 adjusted gross margin) owns and operates the compression fleet under fixed monthly horsepower fees. Approximately 74% of operating horsepower sits in units above 1,000 hp, and roughly three-fourths of operating horsepower is in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford. About 60% serves midstream gathering, 40% wellhead gas lift. Average unit age is ~10 years. **Aftermarket services** (~12% of revenue, ~4% of adjusted gross margin) provides maintenance and parts to customers who own their own compression; it is low-margin at 23-24% and currently shrinking — Q2 revenue of $42.0M against $64.8M a year earlier.

The central structural feature is a duration mismatch that the 10-K states plainly: initial contract terms of 12-36 months (up to 60 for the largest units) are not long enough to recoup equipment cost, and terms convert to month-to-month cancellable on 30 days' notice. Remaining performance obligations for contract operations were $864.6M at 3/31/2026 ($441.0M in 2026, $288.0M in 2027, $92.3M in 2028) against ~$1.5B of annual revenue. This is contracted revenue, not total backlog — aftermarket obligations are excluded because no aftermarket contract exceeds one year. The mitigant is economic rather than contractual: the 10-K reports a compression package averages ~6 years at a single customer location. That gap between ~6-year economic life and ~2-year contractual commitment is both the risk and the resilience of the model.

## Financial Analysis

**Organic growth is now visible and modest.** Contract operations revenue was $318.3M (Q2 2025), $330.9M (Q1 2026), $329.3M (Q2 2026). Decomposing the year-over-year change using average operating horsepower: revenue per average operating horsepower per month was $24.32 in Q2 2026 versus $23.75 in Q2 2025, or +2.4% pricing/mix, on average operating horsepower up ~1.1%. Sequentially, pricing rose only 0.4% from $24.22. Management's own FY2026 guidance implies contract operations revenue of $1,325-1,335M, +4.2% to +5.0% over FY2025's $1,272.1M, and total revenue near $1,510M against $1,489.8M — ~1.4% consolidated growth.

Period-end operating horsepower fell 2.9% year over year. Part of this is deliberate high-grading: ~165,000 non-strategic operating horsepower sold in the last year, ~325,000 hp in FY2025 (~85% small horsepower) and ~175,000 hp in FY2024, with disposal proceeds of $191.8M in FY2025 against $67.6M in FY2024. Culling low-return small units in favor of large horsepower is defensible capital allocation, but it means reported horsepower, revenue growth and utilization optics all understate value creation while it continues, and it makes the growth deceleration harder to disentangle from the strategy.

**Margins are high and stable, but the FY2025 base is inflated.** Contract operations adjusted gross margin was 73% in FY2025 against 67% in FY2024. The 10-K and Q1 2026 10-Q disclose $27.8M of net sales-and-use tax audit settlements plus $8.0M of related credits — approximately $35.0M — recorded primarily as a decrease to cost of sales. Excluding it, FY2025 margin was ($928.9M − $35.0M) ÷ $1,272.1M = 70.3%, so genuine expansion was ~330bp rather than ~600bp. Current margins remain excellent: 71% in Q2 2026, 72% in Q1 2026, 70% in Q2 2025, guided to 71.0-71.5% for the year.

The same adjustment reframes the EBITDA comparison. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $901M ex-benefit is ~$866M; the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $875M is therefore approximately +1.0%, not −2.9%. A quarterly cross-check is consistent: implied 2H 2025 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$244M per quarter versus $221.0M in Q1 2026 and $212.6M in Q2 2026 suggests the benefit landed in the second half of 2025; ex-benefit, 2H 2025 ran ~$227M per quarter against ~$217M in 1H 2026, a real but modest ~4% step-down consistent with the cost pressures management cited. The timing inference rests on arithmetic, not on a direct disclosure.

**Cash flow quality is good, with two qualifications.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $622.1M was 1.93x net income of $322.3M; capex was $502.5M and free cash flow $119.6M. Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $185.9M should not be annualized — the 10-Q attributes the increase primarily to $35.4M of tax refund receipts as the sales/use tax receivable fell from $41.5M to $6.1M. The company's cash available for dividend framework is cleaner: $134.1M in Q1 and $127.2M in Q2, guided to $566M for FY2026.

Two items a screen would miss. First, cash taxes are running at ~4% of the book provision: FY2026 guidance is a $103.0M provision against $4.0M of cash tax expense, with Q1's deferred provision at $22.4M of a $23.4M total and the deferred tax liability rising from $198.3M to $219.4M in one quarter. Accelerated tax depreciation on a growing fleet is deferring nearly the entire bill, worth roughly $99M/year of cash flow support. This is a deferral that reverses when fleet growth slows, and the 10-K's Section 382 risk factor means an ownership change could impair the underlying attributes. Second, the gain-on-sale tailwind has stopped: $10.2M (FY2023) → $17.9M (FY2024) → $47.1M (FY2025) → $10.1M (Q1 2026) → $0.3M (Q2 2026). These gains are excluded from Adjusted EBITDA but included in Archrock's Adjusted EPS. The $9.8M sequential collapse is worth ~$0.043/share after tax on 174,744k diluted shares, which explains most of the $0.42 → $0.38 move.

**The balance sheet is stronger than screening ratios suggest.** Long-term debt of $2,347.8M against reconstructed TTM Adjusted EBITDA of ~$922M gives 2.55x, reconciling with the company's stated 2.6x, versus a 5.25x total debt/EBITDA covenant and a 2.5x minimum interest coverage against roughly 5.8x actual. Liquidity is $631M. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.16-1.29 flagged by screens is dominated by a −0.727 contribution from a $2.257B accumulated deficit, a legacy artifact of the 2015 Exterran spin-off and historical impairments; it is shrinking (−$2,438M at 12/31/2024 → −$2,223M at 3/31/2026) but will suppress the score for years. Solvency here should be judged on leverage, coverage and liquidity, all of which are comfortable.

The H1 2026 restructuring changed the risk profile. Archrock issued $800M of 6.0% notes due 2034 in January, then on April 1 redeemed all $800M of the 6.25% 2028 notes using revolver borrowings. Derived from the $2,347.8M carrying value less $1,500M of remaining notes, roughly $848M is drawn on the $1.5B revolver, corroborated by the $631M liquidity figure. Fixed-rate debt fell from ~96% to ~63%, and 100bp of rate movement is now worth roughly $8.5M annually against the $1.0M sensitivity disclosed at 3/31/2026. Economically this was rate arbitrage that is working — interest expense fell to $37.0M in Q2 from $41.7M — but the revolver matures May 2028, the same year as the retired bond, now in secured, floating form. Management termed out revolver balances opportunistically in January 2026 and will most plausibly do so again; until then, the exposure sits against a floating-rate market that has just risen materially in size.

**Capital returns.** The dividend has been raised five times in two years to $0.23 quarterly ($0.92 annualized), a 2.57% yield at $35.74, covered 3.1x by Q2 CAFD. Buybacks are paused: cumulative repurchases since April 2023 total 4,632,263 shares at an average $20.91, with $113.2M of capacity remaining. In Q1 2026 the company withheld 464,146 shares ($12.9M) for vesting taxes against 170,952 shares ($4.4M) repurchased, so compensation-related issuance currently exceeds buyback activity — immaterial to the share count, which was 174,744k diluted in Q2 versus 175,264k a year earlier, but it is not shrinking the base.

## Growth Analysis

Three inputs define the forward growth rate.

**Pricing and utilization.** Pricing is still rising but at 2.4% year over year and 0.4% sequentially, well off the double-digit repricing of 2023-2024. Utilization is going the other way: 96.0% (6/30/2025) → 95.0% (3/31/2026) → 94.4% (6/30/2026), with idle horsepower rising from ~237k to ~268k between March and June while available horsepower grew slightly. Idle unit retirements accelerated to 60 units / 24,000 hp in Q1 2026 from 20 units / 6,000 hp a year earlier, with impairment of $5.3M versus $1.0M. There is a real tension in management's account: utilization is falling while rising make-ready spend is cited as the reason for the guidance trim.

**The 665,000-horsepower contract.** An eight-year base term with a two-year extension on ~14.7% of operating horsepower is the most valuable disclosure in the Q2 release, because it directly addresses the duration mismatch and should produce a step-change in reported RPO when the Q2 10-Q is filed. A fleet-average scaling exercise (665,000 hp × $24.32 × 12) gives ~$194M/year, but no rate was disclosed and long-tenor large-horsepower units plausibly price below the fleet average, so treat that as a loose bound. The likely economic substance is re-contracting horsepower already deployed with an existing large customer — a duration upgrade rather than incremental revenue. It should not be described as diversification: the counterparty is an existing strategic customer, top-five concentration has been flat-to-worse (33% → 35% → 35% across FY2023-2025), and two customers were ~30% of trade receivables at 12/31/2025. Eight years of fixed pricing on 15% of the fleet is a hedge if the market softens and an opportunity cost if scarcity pricing persists.

**The 2027-2030 capital program.** Management guided cumulative growth capex of $1.4-1.6B for 2027-2030, or $350-400M/year against $250-275M in 2026 — a 40-50% step-up. At compression new-build economics of roughly 4.5-5x capex-to-EBITDA, $375M/year implies ~$75-83M of incremental EBITDA annually, or high-single-digit gross growth on an $875M base before base-fleet attrition. That is the credible path to re-accelerating growth. It also consumes essentially all of the ~$142M/year of discretionary cash remaining after the $161M dividend, and it lands in a rate environment where Archrock's floating exposure has just risen eightfold.

**Demand backdrop is more mixed than the framing suggests.** The 10-K cites AI data-center power demand, industrial use and LNG exports. The EIA outlook Archrock itself references projects 2026 dry gas production +1%, oil production 0%, domestic gas consumption −1%, LNG exports +9%; and for 2027 gas +1%, oil −3%, consumption +1%, LNG +10%. With ~75% of operating horsepower in the oil-directed Permian and Eagle Ford, where gas is largely associated production, flat-to-declining oil output is a direct headwind to horsepower demand, only partly offset by rising gas-to-oil ratios in mature Permian wells and LNG-driven gathering needs.

**Estimate revisions do not support an inflection.** Current-year consensus fell from $2.07 to $2.00 over 90 days (−3.4%); next year rose modestly from $2.35 to $2.40 (+2.2%). The current-year figure looks stale relative to guidance: 1H 2026 adjusted EPS was $0.79 against $0.95 estimated, and Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $865-885M with $433.6M delivered in 1H implies a roughly flat second half — consistent with full-year adjusted EPS nearer $1.57-1.65 than $2.00. I expect downward revisions rather than the +30% velocity that would flag an inflection.

## Competitive Landscape

Archrock is the largest US compression provider by fleet horsepower, serving ~300 contract operations customers across 41 states. Its 10-K risk factors nonetheless concede the business is "highly competitive, and there are low barriers to entry." That language sits in tension with physical reality: an aggregated account of the Q1 2026 earnings call reported Caterpillar prime-mover lead times near 160 weeks (~3.1 years). That figure is externally reported and not confirmed in a filing, though the 10-K's supplier-dependence and lead-time risk language is directionally consistent. If accurate, it is the most credible explanation for how incumbents have pushed rates and held 70-72% margins, and it also explains the strategy — unable to add horsepower organically at speed, Archrock bought it (TOPS, NGCS), culled small horsepower, and pushed price. The corollary is that when lead times normalize, both pricing power and the scarcity value of the installed fleet compress.

Direct competitors are Kodiak Gas Services and USA Compression, plus regional operators and customers who self-operate — the latter being both a competitor and the source of aftermarket revenue, which is currently declining ~35% year over year. A report that Kodiak has moved into behind-the-meter distributed power is unconfirmed and I have not verified terms or counterparties; if accurate, it marks a strategic divergence in which a peer takes direct exposure to the data-center power theme while Archrock's exposure remains indirect — it compresses gas that ultimately feeds power generation and LNG. Reasonable investors can read Archrock's returns-based discipline as the better choice; the point is that the AI-power narrative reaches Archrock two steps removed, which is relevant to how much thematic multiple it should command.

Electrification substitution is a mix risk rather than an existential one: the NGCS purchase allocation explicitly covers electric motor drive equipment, and Permian grid constraints currently limit electric deployment.

Trajectory assessment: margins stable, scale intact, pricing still rising, but operating horsepower down 2.9%, utilization down 160bp year over year, aftermarket contracting, and customer concentration flat-to-worse. Competitive position is stable-to-marginally-eroding rather than improving. Score: **5.8**.

## Risk Assessment

**Contract duration.** Approximately 85% of the fleet outside the new 665,000 hp agreement operates on initial terms of 12-36 months converting to month-to-month at 30 days' notice, against contracted RPO of $864.6M on ~$1.5B of annual revenue. In a demand downturn, repricing risk is realized quickly.

**Cost pass-through failure is realized, not hypothetical.** The guidance trim was attributed to higher make-ready costs, 2H lube oil pressure, weaker aftermarket demand, and higher SG&A from long-term incentive compensation driven by the stock price. That is the contract structure's weakness manifesting.

**Basin and customer concentration.** ~75% of operating horsepower in the Permian and Eagle Ford against EIA projections of 0% and −3% oil production growth in 2026 and 2027. Top-five customers at 35% of revenue with no improvement over three years; two customers at ~30% of trade receivables.

**Rate sensitivity and the 2028 wall.** ~$848M floating, secured, maturing May 2028; ~$8.5M of pre-tax income per 100bp. The revolver replaced an unsecured bond of the same maturity year with secured, floating-rate debt, and liquidity fell from ~$1.4B to $631M in a quarter — a live exposure rather than a theoretical one until it is termed out.

**Deferred tax reversal.** Cash taxes at ~4% of the book provision add ~$99M/year to cash flow. The $1.4-1.6B capital program should sustain the deferral through 2030, but it reverses eventually.

**Governance items.** No open-market insider purchases in the reviewed window. The departing CFO sold 294,550 shares for ~$12.56M beginning two days after his separation agreement was dated — ordinary liquidation, low signal. Two continuing officers sold near the highs: SVP Jason Ingersoll 33,000 shares at $38.19 and the Chief Accounting Officer 10,413 at $36.74. The CEO's transactions were gifts, which carry no valuation signal. A $3.6M limited liability agreement amendment fee paid to FGC Holdco, a 32%-owned VIE carried at $0.1M, is unexplained in the disclosures reviewed; the amount is immaterial (~1.1% of FY2025 net income) and I draw no inference about intent, but it is a disclosure gap. A board member affiliated with customer Hilcorp is a structural related-party feature; Q1 2025 included a $9.9M sale of used equipment to Hilcorp.

**Deregulation cuts both ways.** Federal methane rollbacks — NSPS OOOOb/OOOOc deadlines pushed to January 2027, the waste emissions charge delayed to 2034, the February 2026 rule overturning the 2009 endangerment finding — lower Archrock's compliance capex across 5,300+ units, a margin positive. They also weaken the commercial pull behind its ~$26.7M of emissions-technology investments (ECOTEC, Ionada, Shoreline AI, FGC Holdco), where purchases from two ventures were zero in Q1 2026. The aggregate carrying value is ~0.6% of assets and immaterial either way.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 45.1% against 31.9% trailing realized is rich, and the term structure is backwardated (45.1% at one month versus 40.6% at six months) — the market is pricing near-term event risk around the just-released print and the guidance reset. The nearest expiry implies a ±7.8% move. Positioning, however, is not bearish: put/call skew is call-heavy at −5.9% and put/call open interest is 0.19, among the most call-tilted readings one sees. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV rose 1.3pp while the price fell 3.8% and put/call OI rose from 0.06 to 0.19 — hedging demand increased off a very low base but the structure remains call-dominated. Short interest of 5.9% of float and 3.2 days to cover indicates an uncrowded book with no squeeze dynamic. The takeaway: rich near-term vol makes this a poor moment to buy optionality, and the absence of bearish positioning means the market is not treating the growth deceleration as a credit or solvency question, consistent with my read that this is a multiple problem, not a distress problem.

Score: **5.3**.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $44 (+23%)

Compression remains structurally tight; if lead times stay near three years, incumbents keep pricing power and utilization recovers toward 96% as make-ready spend converts idle units back to service. The 665,000 hp eight-year contract triggers a visible RPO step-up in the Q2 10-Q and re-rates the perceived duration of cash flows. The 2027-2030 program deploys $350-400M/year at ~4.5x capex-to-EBITDA build multiples, lifting Adjusted EBITDA to ~$960M and re-establishing high-single-digit growth. Leverage continues down toward 2.0x, the revolver is termed out at fixed rates, and the dividend compounds at ~10% off 3.1x coverage. At 10.5x EV/EBITDA on $960M, equity is $7,732M, or $44/share.

### Bear Case — $26 (−27%)

Permian oil production declines as the EIA projects, associated gas volumes flatten, and horsepower demand softens. Utilization slips through 93% as customers exercise 30-day cancellation on month-to-month units, and pricing rolls over from +2.4% toward flat. Make-ready and lube oil costs continue outrunning price, compressing contract operations margin below 69%. The 40-50% growth capex step-up lands into a weaker demand curve, consuming discretionary cash while the ~$848M floating revolver reprices higher and the May 2028 maturity approaches. Deferred taxes begin reversing as fleet growth slows. At 8.0x on $860M EBITDA, equity is $4,532M, or $26/share. The 52-week low of $21.17 defines a deeper tail.

### Which Is More Likely

Neither, decisively. The trajectory data show the score declining from 6.8 in May to 5.4 in July, driven by growth prospects (7.5 → 5.5), valuation (6.0 → 4.5) and competitive position (7.5 → 6.0). The Q2 print confirms the growth and competitive legs of that decline: revenue fell year over year, utilization eroded further, and a second consecutive double-digit miss landed. It also partially offsets it: leverage at 2.6x, a $30M-only guidance trim on a base inflated by a $35M one-timer, and a genuine duration upgrade on 15% of the fleet. The price fell 15.4% from the June 26 high of $42.23 on the authoritative basis and ~22% on the extended-hours print, which improves entry arithmetic, but the decline is accompanied by identifiable fundamental deceleration, so this is not a case of unchanged fundamentals at a lower price. The business is worth roughly what it trades for, which is a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** Two near checkpoints: the Q2 2026 10-Q filing (expected within weeks), which should disclose the RPO step-up from the 665,000 hp agreement and the post-redemption revolver balance; and the Q3 2026 print in early November, which tests whether the guidance narrowing holds and whether utilization stabilizes.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Spot utilization back above 95.5% for two consecutive quarters with period-end operating horsepower flat or growing — this would confirm that the current erosion is fleet high-grading rather than demand loss.
2. Contract operations RPO in the Q2 or Q3 10-Q stepping above ~$1.6B, confirming that the 665,000 hp agreement adds material contracted duration rather than merely re-papering existing units at flat economics.
3. Price toward $28-29, which on FY2026 guidance is ~8.5x EV/EBITDA and a ~6.5% FCF yield after growth capex — a level where the ~11% ROIC and 3x-covered dividend produce asymmetric upside.
4. The revolver termed out at a fixed rate ahead of the May 2028 maturity, removing both the floating exposure and the maturity concentration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $865M, or FY2027 guided below $875M, which would break the "no change in demand fundamentals" framing.
2. Spot utilization below 93%, or contract operations adjusted gross margin below 69% for two consecutive quarters.
3. CAFD dividend coverage falling below 2.0x, or growth capex for the 2027-2030 program funded with equity issuance.
4. A third consecutive double-digit adjusted EPS miss, which would establish a guidance-credibility problem rather than a cost-timing problem.

**Position guidance.** Hold existing positions for the 2.57% dividend and contracted cash flows. New capital should wait — either for one of the upgrade triggers above, or for the extended-hours price weakness to persist into a close below ~$32, at which point EV/EBITDA reaches ~9.0x and the base case offers double-digit upside.

## Conclusion

Archrock has finished its acquisition phase and is now visible as what it is: a well-run, high-margin, capital-intensive compression lessor with no direct commodity exposure, 2.6x leverage, a covered and rising dividend, and an organic growth rate of 1-5%. The Q2 2026 print did not damage the business — Adjusted EBITDA was flat, margins held at 71%, and the balance sheet improved — but it removed any remaining ambiguity about the growth rate, and the market repriced accordingly. Roughly two-thirds of the decline from the June peak occurred before the print, which I cannot attribute to company disclosure.

The obstacle to a constructive call is price rather than quality. At $35.74 the stock trades at 9.84x FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA, above the range where scaled compression assets normally clear, and at ~21x normalized adjusted EPS with current-year estimates falling and two consecutive misses behind it. A base case built on 2027 EBITDA of ~$920M at 9.5x lands near $36-37, essentially flat.
