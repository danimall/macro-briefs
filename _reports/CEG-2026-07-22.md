**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Constellation Energy is the largest private-sector power producer and largest nuclear operator in the United States, and following the January 2026 acquisition of Calpine it now controls a ~55 GW coast-to-coast fleet combining clean-nuclear baseload with the largest dispatchable gas fleet in the country. The strategic logic is coherent: hyperscalers need 24/7 carbon-free firm power, and CEG can assemble a product of nuclear baseload backed by gas peaking that few competitors can match. Q1 2026 adjusted operating EPS rose 28% to $2.74, FY2026 adjusted operating EPS is guided to $11.00–$12.00, and management targets 20%+ base EPS growth annually through 2029. Both rating agencies affirmed investment grade (BBB+/Baa1) after the debt-laden deal.

The stock has fallen roughly 36% from its October 2025 high of $412.70 to $262, and is down about 18% over the trailing year even as operations beat and guidance held. The drawdown traces to two identifiable causes: the December 2025 FERC draft order finding PJM's co-located-load tariff "unjust and unreasonable," which directly governs the economics of powering data centers behind the meter, and a broader derating of AI-capex-levered equities as the market questions the durability of the data-center power premium. The debate is about valuation and whether the data-center premium is durable and recoverable through the regulatory process, not about fundamentals or balance-sheet health.

We rate CEG a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.5, essentially stable versus the prior 5.9 read. The price decline on largely intact fundamentals has improved the risk/reward, and the forward multiple of ~22x is now at the low end of the merchant-nuclear peer set for a company guiding to 20%+ base EPS growth. We stop short of BUY because two binary swing factors remain unresolved — the final FERC co-located-load tariff, and NRC approval plus grid deliverability for the Crane/Microsoft restart — and because Calpine integration and its $11.5B of goodwill are early enough that management itself calls the acquired earnings "impracticable to determine." We want to own this business at the right entry; the entry is closer than it was, but the regulatory binaries argue for patience.

## Company Overview

Constellation operates two engines. The generation business spans six segments — Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, New York, ERCOT, Other Power Regions, and the new Calpine segment — selling merchant power into PJM, ERCOT, NYISO, ISO-NE, CAISO, and MISO. The competitive retail supply business is the nation's leading platform, serving roughly 2.5 million customer accounts including 80% of the Fortune 100, with Calpine adding approximately 62 TWh per year of retail load.

The competitive moat rests on the nuclear fleet. CEG provides about 10% of the nation's clean energy, and management estimates the new-build replacement cost of the combined fleet exceeds 3x current enterprise value, a credible claim given the near-impossibility of permitting new nuclear capacity in the US. Roughly 147M MWh of annual nuclear output is available for long-term contracting, which is the "inventory" hyperscalers are competing to secure. Policy is supportive: the IRA nuclear production tax credit runs through 2032, and federal posture favors "energy dominance."

The January 2026 Calpine acquisition (~$21.8B: 50M shares plus $4.5B cash, with ~$12.6B of assumed debt) was a strategic pivot toward firm-shaped reliability. Coupling the largest clean-nuclear fleet with the largest dispatchable gas fleet lets CEG offer round-the-clock firm power, but it also injects gas commodity exposure, carbon-policy risk (the Zion plant faces a 2030 Illinois shutdown mandate), and Winter Storm Uri tort litigation into what was previously a cleaner nuclear story.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 was the first quarter consolidating Calpine, and the headline numbers require careful interpretation. Revenue rose 63.8% YoY to $11,122M, with the Calpine segment contributing $2,395M and power prices up sharply. GAAP net income to common was $1,590M ($4.49 diluted EPS) versus $118M ($0.38) a year earlier, but this figure was inflated by roughly $1.3B of non-cash unrealized mark-to-market hedge gains plus a favorable nuclear asset-retirement-obligation remeasurement. GAAP earnings for this company are structurally noisy and not a reliable performance gauge.

The decision-useful figure is adjusted operating EPS of $2.74, up 28% from $2.14, against a diluted share count that rose to 354M from 314M on the Calpine issuance. The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats in the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter (March 2026) beating by 5.7%.

The GAAP-versus-reality gap cuts both ways. FY2025 GAAP net income fell to $2.32B from $3.75B in 2024, which a naive screen reads as deterioration. The actual driver was the nuclear PTC phase-out caused by high power prices, which is a positive. The PTC provides up to ~$15/MWh of support only when unit revenues fall between roughly $26 and $44.75/MWh and phases out above that band. With power prices high, most units are above the phase-out, so PTC revenue collapsed from ~$2.08B in 2024 to ~$0.32B in 2025 and was near-zero in Q1 2026. The PTC functions as a floor with uncapped upside; the risk is inverse, as a sustained collapse in power prices would drag earnings back toward that floor.

On the balance sheet at 3/31/2026: total assets $96.9B, total debt ~$22.5B ($17.0B long-term, $5.1B short-term, $0.4B current), equity $33.8B, goodwill $11.5B (nearly all from Calpine), cash $0.8B, and nuclear decommissioning trust funds of $19.5B. Credit ratings of BBB+/Baa1 were affirmed post-Calpine, which is a genuine competitive advantage in a collateral-intensive business.

Two caveats on cash flow and screening tools. Q1 operating cash flow was only $425M, depressed by working-capital and collateral swings plus Calpine integration noise; capex was $1,275M. This is not representative of run-rate. Management guides free cash flow before growth of $11.5–$13.0B cumulative for 2028–2029. Separately, the Altman Z-Score of ~1.3 flags "distress" but is a false positive here — it penalizes the large, low-turnover asset base and leverage inherent to capital-intensive merchant utilities without crediting the $19.5B in NDT funds or investment-grade market access. Covenant compliance is intact and both agencies affirmed IG after the deal.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is specific and quantified. Management affirmed FY2026 adjusted operating EPS of $11.00–$12.00 (base EPS $6.65–$6.75), targets base EPS growth of 20%+ annually from 2026 to 2029, reaching 2029 base EPS of $11.40–$11.90, and holds a long-term target of 10%+ rolling three-year growth. Earnings are framed as roughly 60–70% "base" (highly visible, PTC-floored, contracted) and 30–40% "enhanced" (market upside).

The demand backdrop is confirmed across multiple independent data points, which raises the durability of the growth thesis. PJM capacity prices surged — EMAAC to ~$269.92/MWday (+404% YoY), ComEd +833% — and PJM West power was up 81% YoY. The data-center demand is visible in CEG's own commercial wins. This breadth argues against a near-term deceleration of demand at the power layer.

CEG's commercial momentum is concrete. The Crane Clean Energy Center (restart of Three Mile Island Unit 1, 835 MW) carries a 20-year Microsoft PPA and a DOE loan guarantee of up to $1.0B. Recent ERCOT wins include CyrusOne (380 MW at Freestone plus a 380 MW Phase 2 exclusive) and 400 MW at Thad Hill, for 780 MW signed in "powered land." Pin Oak Creek (460 MW gas peaker) and Pastoria Solar (105 MW) reached commercial operation in April 2026, and CEG submitted 5,000 MW of new capacity to PJM. A Walmart PPA (per July 2026 news) shows demand broadening beyond hyperscalers. Management sizes the optionality: $0.20–$0.50 EPS per 1,000 MW of ERCOT powered-land deals, and $0.40–$1.00 EPS per 1 GW nuclear PPA, real upside layered on top of base guidance.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating, which tempers the growth score. Current-year EPS consensus is $11.72 (essentially flat over 30 and 90 days), and next-year is $13.58 (marginally lower over 90 days). The current-quarter estimate slipped 4.2%. This is a company delivering on a credible plan while the market withholds the multiple, without the revision velocity that would flag a BUY signal. Growth prospects are strong and well-supported, but the absence of upward revision momentum keeps the score at 6.8 rather than higher.

## Valuation Assessment

At $262, CEG trades at roughly 22.4x trailing earnings and ~22x forward on the $11.72 current-year consensus. Against the peer set — Duke 19.1x, Southern 23.8x, AEP 19.0x, Vistra 24.7x, Entergy 28.1x, peer median 23.8x — CEG is at or slightly below the median. That is a discount for a company guiding to 20%+ base EPS growth versus the mid-single-digit growth typical of regulated utilities. On a growth-adjusted basis, the PEG is well below 1.0 if the 20%+ base-growth guidance holds.

Prior appreciation is not a valuation input, and neither is the drawdown by itself, but at a decline of this magnitude the burden is on identifying what the market is repricing, and here it is identifiable. The FERC co-located-load ruling put a question mark over the behind-the-meter data-center model that underwrote much of the premium, and the broader AI-capex derating pulled sentiment-driven names down together. The multiple compressed from a peak that embedded aggressive data-center optionality back toward a level that prices the base business plus modest optionality.

Analyst targets frame the range: low $296, median $366, mean $358, high $441 — all above the current price, with the low target implying ~13% upside. With estimate revisions merely stable, we treat the target cluster as informative rather than a firm floor. The valuation score of 6.5 reflects genuine relative cheapness for the growth on offer, discounted by the fact that a meaningful piece of the upside (the data-center premium) is gated on unresolved regulatory outcomes. A compressed multiple can reflect a real, not phantom, discount when binary regulatory risk sits between the current business and the growth algorithm.

## Competitive Landscape

CEG's competitive position is improving and structurally advantaged. The differentiator versus peers is the largest deliverable carbon-free inventory in the country, uniquely valuable to buyers requiring 24/7 firm clean power. Nuclear cannot be replicated at scale given permitting constraints, and the Calpine gas fleet lets CEG bundle firm-shaped reliability that pure-play nuclear or pure-play gas operators cannot. The retail platform, at 80% of the Fortune 100, provides both a demand funnel and a hedge.

The industry backdrop is favorable: surging capacity prices, a PJM price collar of ~$175–$325/MWday accepted by FERC for 2028–2030 auctions, and supportive federal policy including DOE emergency orders keeping units online. The customer base is diversifying from hyperscalers alone toward non-tech buyers such as Walmart.

The trajectory qualifier is regulatory. The co-located-load tariff outcome will determine how much of the behind-the-meter economics CEG captures. Vistra is the closest merchant-fleet comparable and has drawn down more steeply (-28% from its 52-week high), which suggests the merchant-power derating is sector-wide rather than CEG-specific. We score competitive position 7.5 — a widening structural moat against a modest regulatory uncertainty on how fully that moat converts to contracted economics.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter here are regulatory and integration-related, not solvency-related.

**FERC/PJM co-located load** is the central binary. The draft order directs new interconnection and behind-the-meter rules, and the final compliance tariff is unresolved. An adverse or delayed outcome would compress the enhanced-earnings portion of the algorithm.

**Crane restart execution** is the second binary. NRC review is pending, and the FERC waiver (filed March 2026) to transfer Eddystone capacity interconnection rights aims to accelerate deliverability; without it, some grid upgrades slip to December 2030. The 20-year Microsoft PPA depends on these approvals.

**Calpine integration and goodwill** carry $11.5B of goodwill on a preliminary purchase-price allocation subject to one-year revision, with management stating it is impracticable to isolate Calpine's post-acquisition earnings. Early integration carries surprise risk.

**Credit-downgrade collateral cliff**: a below-IG downgrade (a three-notch move) would trigger ~$3.0B of incremental collateral, manageable against $6.7B of facility capacity but a real tail in a stress scenario.

**Other**: ~35% of 2026–2031 uranium concentrate needs come from three suppliers exposed to Russian import/export restrictions (mitigated by inventory builds); the Zion plant faces a January 2030 forced-shutdown risk under Illinois CEJA; Geysers geothermal is exposed to drought/wildfire/seismic risk; and Winter Storm Uri ERCOT tort litigation continues (Texas Supreme Court denied plaintiffs' mandamus in March 2026, rehearing sought). The DOJ-mandated divestitures (5 PJM gas plants, ~4.4 GW, to LS Power for $5.0B) must be under definitive agreement by September 4, 2026. A partial lock-up on Calpine shares (50% freed June 30, 2026; 50% June 30, 2027) is a modest overhang.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects two genuine binary regulatory outcomes plus early integration risk that are not yet resolved, balanced against a solvency profile that is sound and IG-affirmed. The prior trajectory dropped this dimension to 4.5; we hold at 5.0 because the co-located-load process is progressing and the balance-sheet risk is well-characterized and manageable.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning shows the market pricing near-term stress with resolving uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 55.7% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 46.9%, and the term structure is in backwardation (55.7% near-term versus 47.1% at six months), signaling an imminent-event premium consistent with the pending regulatory catalysts and next earnings. IV has collapsed from 119.9% to 55.7% over the past three weeks while the price rose 14.9%, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 3.35 to 1.29, a sharp unwind of bearish positioning. The put/call skew is now balanced at -3.0%. Short interest is low at 3.8% of float (2.7 days to cover), indicating this is not a crowded bearish trade. Prior acute stress has drained, positioning has normalized, and the market is pricing event risk into upcoming catalysts rather than a directional collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$360–$440).** The FERC co-located-load tariff resolves constructively, unlocking behind-the-meter data-center contracting. The Crane restart clears NRC and grid deliverability, validating the nuclear-PPA model. Powered-land and nuclear-PPA optionality ($0.20–$1.00 EPS per deal) layers onto affirmed base guidance, driving 20%+ base EPS growth to $11.40–$11.90 by 2029. Capacity prices stay elevated, Calpine integration delivers synergies, and the multiple re-rates back toward its prior premium as the data-center premium proves durable. Analyst high target of $441 embeds this scenario.

**Bear Case (~$200–$230).** The co-located-load ruling comes out restrictive or drags, compressing the enhanced-earnings piece; power prices normalize downward, pulling nuclear economics toward the PTC floor; Calpine integration surprises negatively against $11.5B of goodwill; and the AI-capex-sentiment derating deepens, taking the multiple to the low end of the utility range on base-only earnings. A three-notch downgrade collateral event would be the acute tail. The 52-week low of $228.63 (set July 1, 2026) marks the recent trough; a break of that on adverse regulatory news opens the low-$200s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $305.** This applies ~26x forward on the $11.72 current-year consensus, above the peer median for a company growing base EPS at 20%+, a modest re-rating from the current ~22x as regulatory clarity improves and Calpine integration de-risks. This sits between the bear and bull cases.

**Bull case target: $400+**, on constructive FERC resolution, Crane clearance, and multiple re-rating toward the prior premium (analyst high $441).

**Bear case target: ~$220** (roughly -16% from current), on adverse co-located-load ruling, power-price normalization, and derating on base-only earnings.

**Upside/downside from current ($262):** ~+16% to base, ~+53% to bull, ~-16% to bear — a roughly symmetric-to-slightly-favorable risk/reward that supports a growth-hold rather than an add.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, tied to the final FERC co-located-load tariff, NRC action on Crane, the September 4, 2026 DOJ divestiture deadline, and the next two earnings prints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) constructive final FERC co-located-load tariff that preserves behind-the-meter data-center economics; (2) NRC approval plus confirmed grid deliverability for the Crane/Microsoft restart; (3) positive estimate-revision velocity (current-year consensus rising above $12.00) confirming the optionality is flowing into numbers; or (4) a pullback toward the low-$230s / prior 52-week low on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) an adverse or materially delayed co-located-load ruling that guts the data-center premium; (2) a Calpine-related goodwill impairment or integration-driven guidance cut; (3) a credit-rating action toward the downgrade threshold that puts the $3.0B collateral cliff in play; or (4) sustained power-price collapse dragging earnings toward the PTC floor.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings print (early August), with the FERC compliance process and the September DOJ divestiture deadline as the intervening catalysts.

## Conclusion

Constellation Energy is a high-quality, structurally advantaged business — the largest US nuclear operator, now paired with the largest dispatchable gas fleet, sitting at the center of a confirmed data-center power demand cycle with a specific, quantified 20%+ base EPS growth plan and an IG-affirmed balance sheet. The drawdown from the October high has taken the multiple to ~22x forward, at or below the peer median for a company growing several times faster than a typical utility, which materially improves the risk/reward versus six months ago.

We rate CEG a HOLD (growth_hold) at an overall 6.5, stable versus the prior 5.9. The score history shows growth, management, and risk dimensions marked down over the spring; our current read stabilizes rather than extends that decline, because the fundamental deterioration those markdowns implied has not materialized — operations beat, guidance held, and the price weakness is sentiment- and regulation-driven rather than earnings-driven. We stop short of BUY for two reasons: the enhanced-earnings portion of the growth algorithm is gated on unresolved regulatory binaries (the FERC co-located-load tariff and the Crane restart), and estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating, so the BUY signal is absent. A constructive FERC outcome or a pullback toward the low-$230s would convert this to a BUY, while an adverse ruling or integration surprise would push it toward AVOID. For now, own it for the growth thesis, monitor the two regulatory catalysts, and reassess at the Q2 print.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the $305 base-case target implies ~26x forward earnings (not ~24x, which would be ~$281), sitting above the peer median rather than "near" it; the +16% base-case upside claim depends on this multiple assumption, so the "slightly-favorable" risk/reward framing may be less supported than stated.
