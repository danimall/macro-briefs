**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TransUnion is one of three global consumer credit bureaus, an oligopoly structure with high barriers built on proprietary, decades-deep consumer data files and embedded customer integrations. The stock has fallen roughly 24% over the trailing year to ~$66, sitting near its 52-week low of $63.37 against a high of $99.39. The drawdown is sector-wide (management notes the broader bureau industry fell ~25%) and rate-driven, but it has compressed TransUnion's forward P/E to roughly 13.8x consensus FY26 EPS of $4.78 against a Research & Consulting Services peer median near 26.5x. That gap is the core of any constructive case.

The operating story is solid but not clean. Q1 2026 revenue grew 13.7% YoY to $1.246B, though organic growth was only ~4% with the balance from the Trans Union de Mexico and Monevo acquisitions plus FX. Headline GAAP EPS of $2.04 is distorted by a one-time, non-cash $225.5M gain on the Mexico step-acquisition; the cleaner read is Adjusted EPS of $1.18, up 12.5% YoY. The company has beaten consensus in four straight quarters by 3.9-7.9%, which gives reasonable confidence in forward estimates, but those estimates have drifted lower over 90 days (current year -1.6%, next year -3.5%), and Adjusted EBITDA margins are compressing as FICO mortgage royalty price hikes flow through the P&L as a margin-dilutive pass-through.

We rate TRU a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The valuation discount is real and the FHFA approval of VantageScore 4.0 (jointly owned by the three bureaus) gives TransUnion a credible path to reclaim score economics it currently cedes to FICO. But the thesis is gated by three live drags: FICO royalty margin pressure, mortgage-cycle dependence on rates that the current macro keeps elevated, and an unresolved (paused, not cleared) CFPB enforcement matter. The combination of a structural discount and stalled-to-negative estimate revisions is more consistent with a value name awaiting a catalyst than an inflection trade.

## Company Overview

TransUnion collects, stores, and analyzes data on over one billion consumers across 30+ countries and reports in two segments. **U.S. Markets (~78% of revenue)** spans Financial Services (~37% of consolidated gross revenue — credit reporting and scoring across mortgage, auto, card/banking, and consumer lending), Emerging Verticals (insurance, tech, retail, telecom, tenant/employment screening, collections, public sector), and Consumer Interactive (~13% — direct-to-consumer credit monitoring and B2B breach response). **International (~22% of revenue)** covers Canada, Latin America (now including a controlling stake in Mexico), the UK, Africa, India, and Asia Pacific.

The competitive moat rests on the regulated oligopoly structure of the bureau industry: consumer data files that cannot be readily replicated, deep customer integrations, and switching costs. The company's strategic differentiator is OneTru, a cloud platform unifying credit, marketing, fraud, and identity data (anchored by an identity graph enhanced by the 2021 Neustar acquisition) with AI-powered analytics. The multi-year accelerated technology investment to migrate to OneTru and cloud was completed at the end of 2025 at a total cost of ~$373M from Q4 2023 through 2025, which should remove a recurring cost drag going forward.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is accelerating.** FY2022 ~$3.71B → FY2023 ~$3.83B → FY2024 ~$4.18B → FY2025 ~$4.58B (+9.4%).

**Profitability has recovered from the 2023 trough.** The business swung from a net loss of ~$206M in 2023 (driven largely by a $414M goodwill impairment in the UK reporting unit) to ~$284M net income in 2024 and ~$455M in 2025. Q1 2026 net income attributable to TRU was $397.1M, but $225.5M of that is the non-cash Mexico step-acquisition gain, and the 6.5% effective tax rate was a one-off from the non-taxable gain (adjusted tax rate ~25%). On a clean basis, Adjusted Net Income was $230.2M (+10.9%).

**Margins are the key nuance.** Q1 2026 consolidated Adjusted EBITDA margin fell ~1.0pp YoY to 35.2%, and U.S. Markets Adjusted EBITDA margin fell 0.8pp. Management attributes this explicitly to FICO mortgage royalties — a pass-through cost that grows the top line but dilutes margin percentage. This is a structural dynamic: as FICO raises wholesale mortgage score prices, the price flows through TransUnion's revenue line at near-zero incremental margin, so reported revenue growth overstates economic value capture.

**Balance sheet is leveraged but not distressed.** Net debt is ~$4.87B and the reported leverage ratio is 2.8x against a covenant ceiling of 5.5x senior secured net leverage — comfortable headroom. Total debt is ~$5.6B, essentially all variable-rate but ~68.5% hedged via interest-rate swaps fixing portions at 1.4%-3.9%. The remaining ~31% unhedged creates genuine rate sensitivity, and the revolver was drawn $520M to fund Mexico. The Altman Z-Score (~1.7) nominally reads "distress," but that is an artifact of an asset-light, goodwill/intangible-heavy data company (>70% of assets are goodwill and intangibles) carrying LBO-legacy leverage. Interest coverage and free cash flow are healthy. Capex discipline is improving — ~5.2% of revenue in Q1 2026, down from 6.2%, consistent with the tech program ending.

Financial health earns a 6.0. The cash generation, covenant headroom, and capex normalization support resilience, but the floating-rate exposure in a higher-for-longer environment and the margin compression keep this from a higher mark. Applying the downside-filter lens, nothing here flags TRU as a worst-in-class name, but the leverage plus rate exposure prevents a top-third score.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth profile is mid-single-digit organic with acquisition contribution layered on top. Street consensus carries ~10.4% revenue growth in 2026 and ~8.5% in 2027, with EPS growth ~11.9% in 2026. The four-quarter beat streak (6.5%, 3.9%, 4.5%, 7.9% surprises) supports confidence that management delivers against guidance.

The concern is estimate direction. Over the trailing 90 days, current-year EPS estimates have been cut to $4.78, next-year to $5.58, and the current quarter by 4.2%. The 30-day picture is flat. This is stable-to-deteriorating, not the kind of strong positive revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection. The deceleration ties directly to the macro: elevated 30-year mortgage rates continue to suppress housing activity, and Q1 mortgage strength was partly FICO-price-driven rather than volume-driven. The 10-Q references the February 2026 conflict pressuring energy prices, inflation, and the 10-year Treasury, which feeds back into mortgage rates and the volumes driving TRU's growth engine.

International is becoming a more material lever but is uneven. Latin America revenue jumped 64% in Q1 2026 on the Mexico consolidation, and the UK/Monevo and Africa businesses are growing double digits, but India fell 10.5% and Asia Pacific 18.2% on regulatory credit-tightening and prior-year one-time contracts.

Growth prospects earn 5.8. The structural demand for credit data is durable and the OneTru platform completion plus VantageScore optionality provide upside, but the near-term revision trend is the dominant signal, and it argues for moderation rather than acceleration. This is the dimension we weight most heavily, and the evidence here is mixed at best.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$66, TRU trades at roughly 13.8x consensus FY26 EPS and ~11.8x FY27 EPS. Against the Research & Consulting Services peer median P/E of 26.5x, that is a ~48% discount. The most relevant direct comp is Equifax at 28.1x and Verisk at 27.8x — both data-analytics oligopolists with similar moats. The dispersion in the peer set is wide (Booz Allen 11.2x, FTI 18.4x), so the median overstates the gap to the pure-play bureau comps, but even against the lower end of analytics peers TRU screens cheap.

The question the valuation score must answer is why the market is wrong, not simply that the multiple is low. A cheap multiple alone is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal. Here there are specific, falsifiable reasons for the discount: (a) margin compression from FICO royalties dilutes the quality of reported revenue growth, (b) mortgage cyclicality makes near-term earnings rate-dependent, and (c) the CFPB matter is an unpriced binary tail. The offsetting bull argument is the VantageScore option — FHFA approval of VantageScore 4.0 for GSE mortgages, with the three bureaus owning the JV, gives TransUnion a credible path to recapture score economics it currently cedes to FICO. If lenders migrate volume, this is both a revenue and margin tailwind that the market is not pricing.

Analyst targets span $72 low to $108 high, mean $89.76, median $90 — every target sits above the current price, with the low at +9%. On a stock that is cheap relative to its forward growth (PEG roughly 1.2 on ~12% EPS growth), these targets read more as floors than ceilings, and BofA's May cut to $80 (Neutral) still implies meaningful upside. We assign valuation a 6.5. The discount is real and supported by an identifiable catalyst, but it is not the kind of clean, high-conviction mispricing that warrants a 7+, because the same FICO/mortgage drags that created the discount are still live and the estimate trend is negative. The prior report's 7.0 valuation score was set before the additional 90-day estimate erosion; we trim modestly to reflect that the cheapness is partly justified by softening fundamentals.

## Competitive Landscape

The defining 2026 industry dynamic is the upheaval in mortgage credit scoring. FICO has been aggressively raising wholesale mortgage score prices, which flow through bureau P&Ls — boosting TransUnion's reported revenue but compressing margin percentage. FICO is capturing a growing share of mortgage credit-pull economics and is pushing a Mortgage Direct License Program to license scores directly to lenders, which would further erode bureau pricing leverage. The 10-K added this program to its forward-looking risk factors.

The structural offset is VantageScore. The FHFA approval for GSE-backed mortgages goes live ~May 2026, ending FICO's exclusive franchise. VantageScore is jointly owned by the three bureaus, so volume migration toward it lets TransUnion reclaim economics it currently passes through to FICO. FICO is countering with a Milliman study claiming FICO 10T is more predictive, signaling an active fight over the mortgage score standard. The outcome of that fight is the single most important idiosyncratic swing factor for TRU over the next one to two years, and its direction is genuinely uncertain.

Beyond mortgage, the bureau oligopoly remains structurally attractive — regulated, data-moated, high switching costs. The headwinds are pricing pressure in commoditized products, free-data disintermediation in direct-to-consumer (Consumer Interactive grew only 1.3% in Q1 with slowing demand for paid credit products), and end-customer consolidation as bank mergers reduce contract counts.

Competitive position earns 6.3. The moat is durable and the VantageScore stake is a real strategic asset, but the FICO royalty dynamic and the Direct License threat represent genuine competitive pressure on the most profitable vertical. The trajectory is roughly stable with a credible upside option, which supports a score modestly above neutral rather than a high mark.

## Risk Assessment

**FICO economics (most company-specific).** TransUnion distributes FICO scores and must pass through FICO's mortgage price hikes, diluting margins. The Mortgage Direct License Program could erode bureau pricing leverage further. This is the cleanest idiosyncratic risk and it is partially offset by VantageScore.

**Mortgage cyclicality.** ~37% of revenue is U.S. Financial Services, with mortgage highly rate-sensitive. The current macro keeps the 10-year and mortgage rates elevated, suppressing housing activity. A reversal in rates would be a meaningful tailwind; continued elevation caps the growth case.

**CFPB overhang (live, unpriced binary).** A March 2024 CFPB NORA letter alleges FCRA violations in dispute-handling practices; in July 2024 the Enforcement Division obtained authority to pursue injunctive relief and civil money penalties. Engagement is currently paused due to CFPB leadership changes — paused, not cleared, and it could resume. Penalties can reach $1M/day for knowing FCRA violations. This is the primary source of unpriced asymmetric downside. A partial offset: the 2017-Consent-Order suit was dismissed with prejudice in March 2025, allowing reversal of a $56M accrual.

**Cybersecurity.** A July 2025 social-engineering attack on a third-party customer-support application exposed personal data of 4.4M consumers. Management deemed it immaterial and not affecting the core credit database, but regulatory inquiries and class-action costs remain. For a company holding data on 1B+ consumers, breach is an existential category (cf. Equifax 2017).

**Goodwill impairment.** >$5.7B goodwill; the 2023 UK impairment ($414M) shows this is not theoretical if macro deteriorates.

Risk factors earn 5.0. The CFPB matter and breach exposure are specific catalysts for material downside that the current valuation does not fully price, and they sit alongside leverage and mortgage cyclicality. The improvement from the prior 5.5 is reversed modestly because the CFPB matter remains unresolved and the macro mortgage drag has intensified.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 44.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 40.1% — modestly rich (4.4pp spread), but the IV percentile within the chain is just 11%, so the market is not pricing acute near-term stress. The put/call skew is balanced at 1.5% and the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.25, indicating no concentrated downside hedging. The term structure is flat (44.5% near vs 44.1% far), with uncertainty evenly distributed rather than clustered around a specific catalyst. The put/call OI ratio collapsed from 1.01 to 0.41 over the prior month as the stock fell 3.6%, which reads as bearish positioning unwinding rather than building. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±9.2%. Net, the options market is not confirming an imminent downside catalyst, which is mildly supportive of the HOLD rather than a deteriorating signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** TRU is a moated bureau oligopolist trading at a ~48% discount to data-analytics peers after a sector-wide drawdown that overshot fundamentals. The OneTru platform is complete, removing a multi-year cost drag. The VantageScore stake — now FHFA-approved for GSE mortgages — is an underpriced option to reclaim score economics from FICO, a genuine margin and revenue tailwind if lenders migrate. Management is buying back stock ~17% cheaper than a year ago ($71 vs $85 average) and raised the authorization to $1.0B as the stock fell. The beat streak supports the ~12% EPS growth path. If rates ease and VantageScore gains traction, multiple re-rating toward 18-20x on $5.58 FY27 EPS gets the stock to $100-110.

**Bear Case.** The margin compression is structural — FICO's royalty pass-through and Direct License push permanently erode the most profitable vertical, and VantageScore migration is slow or contested by FICO's 10T counter-push. Mortgage stays suppressed on elevated rates, organic growth stays ~4%, and estimates continue drifting lower. The CFPB matter resumes and produces material penalties and an injunctive overhang. In that scenario the discount is justified and the stock holds the low-$60s or breaks toward the mid-$50s. The 3-year TSR already materially lags peers, a governance flag.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: a positive 12-month appreciation case exists but is gated by unresolved drags that prevent a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence of meaningful VantageScore 4.0 volume migration in GSE mortgage pulls, reflected in U.S. Financial Services margin stabilization or expansion in a quarterly print.
- Resumption of positive estimate revision velocity (current-year EPS revised up >2% over 90 days) alongside organic revenue growth re-accelerating above 5%.
- Favorable resolution or formal closure of the CFPB dispute-handling matter without material penalty.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CFPB enforcement resumes with a settlement or penalty materially above the existing $71.8M legal/regulatory accrual, or an injunctive remedy constraining the dispute-handling business.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin compression accelerates beyond ~1.5pp YoY in consecutive quarters, signaling the FICO royalty drag is overwhelming operating leverage.
- A material cybersecurity breach affecting the core credit database, or a leverage breach driven by rate moves on the unhedged ~31% of floating debt.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July), with particular attention to VantageScore mortgage traction, U.S. Markets margin trajectory, and any CFPB engagement update. The VantageScore/FICO competitive fight is the dominant 1-2 year swing factor and warrants monitoring each print.

## Conclusion

TransUnion presents a genuinely cheap moated business with a specific, identifiable catalyst in its VantageScore stake, set against equally specific drags: FICO margin compression, mortgage cyclicality on elevated rates, and a paused-but-live CFPB matter. The score trajectory has improved over the past two reports (5.7 → 6.5), but the additional 90-day estimate erosion and the still-unresolved CFPB tail argue for holding the line rather than upgrading. The beat streak and constructive buyback at lower prices are real positives; the negative revision trend and margin pressure are real offsets. The balance of evidence supports a HOLD at an overall 5.95, with the path to a BUY running through VantageScore traction and an easing of the mortgage-rate drag.

The business is cash-generative, covenant-compliant, and trades cheap, with analyst targets uniformly above current price and no options confirmation of imminent downside, so it is not a short candidate. For existing holders, the asymmetry is reasonable: ~12% downside to the bear-case mid-$50s against ~35%+ upside to the consensus mean target if the VantageScore option pays off and rates cooperate.
