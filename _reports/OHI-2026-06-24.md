**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Omega Healthcare is a triple-net-lease healthcare REIT concentrated in skilled nursing facilities (SNFs) and assisted living facilities (ALFs), the most reimbursement-dependent, lowest-margin corner of healthcare real estate that scaled peers like Welltower and Ventas have de-emphasized. The investment case rests on two pillars: a well-covered dividend yielding ~5.8% (~$2.68 annualized at $46.41), and the "silver tsunami" demand backdrop that supports long-run needs-based occupancy. Reported results have been strong: Q1'26 revenue rose 17% YoY to $323.0M, Nareit FFO grew 36% to $249.5M, and management raised 2026 AFFO guidance to $3.19–$3.25/share, implying an ~80–84% payout that leaves the dividend comfortably covered.

The complication is what sits beneath the GAAP headline. Roughly one-fifth of revenue (20 operators, 21.8% of total revenue, up from 18.6% a year earlier) is recognized on a cash basis from financially stressed tenants. Genesis Healthcare remains in Chapter 11 with an unresolved lease assumption/rejection decision and active clawback litigation from the unsecured creditors' committee. The Maplewood revolver ($263.6M amortized cost, 18.1% of all real-estate loans) earns zero cash interest, with PIK accrual at the borrower's election. These are specific, concentrated, partly binary exposures that the reported FFO growth understates.

The trajectory is improving: Q1'26 showed a net credit-loss recovery (+$3.3M vs a $5.1M provision a year prior), Genesis paying full DIP rent, a Maplewood lease restructuring, and the CommuniCare Maryland portfolio sold at ~2.6x book value, validating durable private-market SNF asset value. But near-term consensus EPS estimates are falling (-11% over 90 days for the current quarter, -3.3% for next year), the stock has roughly round-tripped through a 24% run off its 52-week low to $46.41, and the residual operator-credit tail is too live to underwrite multiple expansion. We rate OHI a HOLD on an income basis. The yield is the return; the catalyst path to a higher multiple is contingent on resolutions (Genesis lease, Maplewood) that are not yet in hand.

## Company Overview

Omega operates as a Maryland UPREIT through OHI Healthcare Properties LP (~95% owned). As of March 31, 2026, the portfolio spans 1,039 operating facilities (553 SNFs, 340 ALFs, plus independent living, specialty/rehab, and CCRC assets) across 42 states, Washington D.C., the United Kingdom, Jersey, and (newly) Canada, run by 89 third-party operators. About 98% of real estate investments relate to long-term care. The company reports as a single segment.

The business model provides capital to operators via three channels: long-term triple-net leases (operators bear taxes, insurance, maintenance), real estate loans (~$1.39B of mortgages), and non-real estate operator loans (~$355M). Growth has historically come from accretive-spread investing: acquiring facilities at cap rates above the cost of equity and debt, funded by recycling assets and issuing equity above NAV. In 2025, Omega acquired 71 facilities (~$690M) and sold 49 (~$283M, $67M gains).

The Q4 2025 deployment of the RIDEA/SHOP structure is the key strategic shift, under which Omega owns and operates senior housing through third-party managers and captures operating upside rather than fixed rent. Resident fees ($6.7M) now appear in revenue. This is a new growth lever tied to senior-housing demographics, but it moves Omega off the insulation of the triple-net model into direct operating exposure (occupancy, labor, fraud/abuse regulation applying directly to the company).

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded at a double-digit rate: $878M (2022) → $950M (2023) → $1,051M (2024) → $1,190M (2025), driven by acquisitions, contractual escalators, and recovering cash-basis rent. Q1'26 extended the trend with net income of $158.6M, EPS of $0.47 (vs $0.33, +42%), and operating cash flow of $215.5M (+18%). Net margins run ~49–51%.

The credit-loss line is the most informative quality signal: a net recovery of +$3.3M in Q1'26 versus a $5.1M provision a year earlier, consistent with operator credit stabilizing rather than deteriorating. This corroborates the qualitative narrative of a maturing distress cycle (Genesis DIP rent, Maplewood restructuring).

The dividend is $0.67/quarter, held flat for years. Q1'26 FFO covered the dividend ($198.4M) at roughly an 80% payout; on the AFFO guidance midpoint, payout sits at ~83–84%. Coverage is adequate but not generous, and the multi-year freeze reflects management's deliberate conservatism through the operator-distress cycle. Income investors should not expect dividend growth as a catalyst; they are being paid to wait.

The balance sheet is appropriate for an investment-grade REIT. Total assets $10.2B, equity $5.5B, total debt $4.5B (45.1% of total cap), weighted-average rate 4.2%, 90.5% fixed-rate. Maturities are laddered 2027–2033; the next is $700M of 4.5% notes in April 2027, manageable against ~$1.6B of revolver availability and $1.9B of remaining ATM capacity. Ratings are Baa3/BBB-/BBB- across all three agencies. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.52 that flags "distress" is a manufacturing-model artifact applied to a high-leverage asset-heavy REIT and should be disregarded.

Two financial-quality caveats temper the picture. First, capital allocation is heavily equity-funded: 16.3M shares (~$612M) issued via ATM/DRIP in 2025, plus $222M of OP units for the Saber JV, and a charter amendment increasing authorized shares from 350M to 700M. This is accretive only while the stock trades above NAV; it is dilutive in a rate shock or credit scare. Second, the Maplewood revolver earns zero cash interest, a non-earning loan dressed in accrual mechanics and a candidate for future write-down.

## Growth Analysis

The structural tailwind is real and durable. Needs-based SNF/ALF demand rises with an aging population, and Omega is one of few scaled, investment-grade capital providers to a capital-starved niche, a sourcing advantage. Near-term growth drivers include contractual escalators, the RIDEA build-out (capturing operating upside in senior housing), the Saber relationship (9.9% equity stake at an 8% minimum yield, complementing the 49% SHH JV of 64 facilities), and the Canada entry (a C$87.6M development loan at 10%, convertible into 34.9% equity).

The reported FFO growth overstates organic momentum, however. Much of it reflects acquisition volume, recovering cash-basis collections, and a swing from credit provision to recovery, components that are partly non-recurring or contingent on continued operator stabilization. The forward earnings signal is mixed-to-soft: consensus current-quarter EPS has been revised down 11% over 90 days, next-quarter down 10.1%, current-year down 3.3%, and next-year down 3.3%. This is negative earnings-revision momentum, and on the dimension that most reliably sorts winners from losers it argues against an aggressive growth score. The earnings-surprise record is favorable (four consecutive beats, including a 30.4% beat in Q3'25), which lends some confidence that the company is delivering against a lowered bar, but the bar itself is moving down.

Net assessment: durable demographic demand and accretive external growth support mid-single-digit AFFO compounding, but falling estimates and the equity-funded model cap the upside relative to a clean inflection story. The RIDEA pivot is the most interesting forward lever and the one most likely to surprise to the upside if senior-housing occupancy continues recovering, but it is early and unproven at scale.

## Valuation Assessment

At $46.41, OHI trades at ~14.4x the 2026 AFFO guidance midpoint ($3.22) and yields ~5.8%. The trailing P/E of 22.5x is not the right lens for a REIT; AFFO multiple and yield are the relevant anchors.

Peer context is unusually noisy. The health-care REIT median P/E of 64.3x is distorted by names like Healthpeak (64.3x), American Healthcare REIT (80.9x), and Healthcare Realty (negative), which carry different asset mixes and depreciation profiles. On dividend yield, OHI sits above the peer median of 4.7% and near Sabra's 6.4%, appropriate given that OHI's SNF concentration carries higher reimbursement and operator-credit risk than diversified or senior-housing-tilted peers. CareTrust, the closest SNF-focused comparable, trades at 23.4x earnings with a 4.1% yield; OHI's lower yield-implied multiple reflects its larger but more concentrated stressed-tenant book.

The valuation is fair rather than cheap, and the discount to a clean health-care REIT multiple is earned, not a mispricing. The CommuniCare Maryland sale at ~2.6x book is the strongest argument that NAV support sits above the current quote, and the analyst target range ($45 low, $63 high, $50.6 mean) implies modest upside. But proximity to the consensus mean does not signal a floor here: estimates are falling, not rising, so the conditions that make analyst targets lag reality are absent. We do not award a high valuation score. A compressed multiple that reflects genuine, concentrated tenant-credit overhang is the market pricing the risk correctly, not asymmetric upside. The yield support and NAV floor justify a mid-range score, not a buy-the-mispricing call.

## Competitive Landscape

Omega's moat is its scale and dedication to the SNF/ALF niche that mainstream REITs avoid. With 1,039 facilities and 89 operators, it has diversification within a concentrated sector, deep operator relationships that enable restructurings, and an investment-grade balance sheet that lets it provide capital where private lenders have retreated. The CommuniCare Maryland sale confirms durable private-market value for the underlying real estate, supporting both NAV and the franchise's ability to recycle capital accretively.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The operator-distress wave (Genesis, Maplewood, LaVie, CommuniCare) appears to be maturing rather than worsening, evidenced by the credit-loss recovery, full Genesis DIP rent, and the Maplewood restructuring. The CommuniCare relationship has been de-risked (concentration down from ~11% to 9.4%, with a high-multiple asset sale). Against this, the cash-basis revenue share ticked up to 21.8%, and the RIDEA pivot trades insulation for operating risk. The competitive position is solid but tied to the most structurally pressured segment of healthcare real estate; the moat is real but the sector backdrop limits how much it can widen.

## Risk Assessment

The defining risk is operator credit concentration. The 20 cash-basis operators are on the rise year-over-year. The largest live exposures:

- **Genesis Healthcare** — Chapter 11 (July 2025, 31 Omega facilities). Currently paying full contractual rent as a DIP condition ($13.3M rent + $7.0M interest recognized in Q1'26). The court-approved sale to WSSH is expected to close after June 30, 2026, and the buyer may elect to assume or reject the Omega lease, an unresolved binary. Omega also carries $134.5M of term loans (risk-rated "5") plus a $25M share of an $80M Super-Priority DIP loan.
- **Genesis clawback litigation** — The unsecured creditors' committee seeks to recharacterize Omega's term loans as partly unsecured and to claw back 90-day pre-petition lease/loan payments as preferences. Consideration is stayed until plan confirmation; an adverse outcome could require returning cash already collected. This tail risk is underappreciated.
- **Maplewood** — ~6.6% of revenue, >10% of total investments, cash-basis/non-accrual since 2023. The revolver earns zero cash interest (PIK at the borrower's election). A material write-down candidate.

Layered on top are reimbursement and regulatory risks: OBBBA's ~$920B of decade-long Medicaid cuts (indirect but pressuring state rate-setting), staffing mandates, and periodic political scrutiny of REIT/PE ownership of SNFs. Medicare's +3.2% FY26 SNF rate update is a partial offset. Operator-level labor and wage inflation continue to pressure coverage ratios, and the RIDEA model introduces direct operating exposure to occupancy, labor, and fraud/abuse regulation.

These are specific, concentrated, and partly binary, which is why the risk score sits below the midpoint. The mitigants are meaningful: improving collections, the credit-loss recovery, NAV support from the CommuniCare sale, an investment-grade balance sheet with 90.5% fixed-rate debt, and ample liquidity. But the Genesis lease decision and the clawback litigation are unresolved events that could move the stock, and they are not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contradiction to the fundamental read. Near-term ATM IV of 21.3% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 19.9% (a 1.5pp spread, "fair"), at the 8th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (2.4%), and the put/call volume ratio is very low (0.06). The term structure is in contango (21.3% near vs 24.5% long-dated), a normal/complacent shape. The change since the prior snapshot is the put/call OI ratio rising from 0.18 to 0.71 alongside a 3.8% price decline and a 1.9pp IV uptick, a modest increase in hedging/put interest but well short of stress. The longer-dated 6-month skew turning slightly negative (-5.6%) is consistent with the market pricing the unresolved Genesis/Maplewood events over a multi-quarter horizon. There is no informed-positioning signal pointing to an imminent dislocation in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($53–58):** Genesis emerges from Chapter 11 with the Omega lease assumed and clawback claims dismissed; Maplewood restructuring converts PIK to cash interest; cash-basis revenue share trends back below 18%; RIDEA occupancy recovery surprises to the upside; AFFO compounds toward $3.40+ in 2027. With credit overhang lifted, the AFFO multiple re-rates toward 16–17x and the stock revisits its 52-week high near $49, with NAV support suggesting further room. Yield compresses toward 5%.

**Bear Case ($38–41, ~12–18% downside):** The Genesis buyer rejects the Omega lease, the clawback litigation produces a meaningful cash return, Maplewood is written down, and OBBBA-driven state Medicaid cuts pressure operator coverage broadly, pushing cash-basis revenue higher. The equity-funded growth model stalls as the stock falls below NAV, forcing dilutive issuance or curtailed external growth. The multiple compresses to ~12x AFFO and yield rises toward 7%.

**Base Case ($46–49):** Operator credit continues stabilizing without a clean resolution; Genesis pays DIP rent through close with an uncertain lease outcome; estimates stabilize after the recent downward drift; AFFO lands within the guide. The stock trades on its yield with limited multiple movement, total return driven by the ~5.8% dividend plus low-single-digit AFFO growth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income hold. The total-return expectation is the ~5.8% dividend plus low-single-digit AFFO growth, roughly 7–9% absent a credit-resolution re-rating, with the dividend the durable component.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Genesis sale closes with the Omega lease assumed and the clawback litigation dismissed or settled immaterially.
- Cash-basis revenue share declines two consecutive quarters back toward/below 18%, with consensus EPS estimate revisions turning positive.
- Maplewood revolver converts from PIK to cash-paying, or is resolved at/above carrying value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Genesis buyer rejects the Omega lease, or the clawback litigation advances toward a material adverse outcome.
- Cash-basis revenue share climbs above ~25%, or a new top-10 operator moves to non-accrual.
- A Maplewood write-down, or dividend coverage (FFO payout) deteriorating above ~90%.
- The stock falls below NAV in a way that forces dilutive equity issuance to fund maturities.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Genesis sale close (expected after June 30, 2026) and the Q2'26 earnings release, which will update cash-basis revenue share, credit-loss trends, and AFFO guidance.

## Conclusion

Omega is a well-run, investment-grade REIT with a covered ~5.8% dividend, a durable demographic tailwind, and a maturing operator-credit cycle that is moving in the right direction. The reported FFO growth is strong but flattered by acquisitions and credit-loss recoveries, and the most important number, 21.8% of revenue on cash-basis recognition, sits in plain sight beneath the headline. With near-term estimates falling, the multiple fair rather than cheap, and binary Genesis/Maplewood resolutions unresolved, the appreciation case is contingent rather than in hand. The score trajectory has been improving (5.5 → 6.2 across prior reports, all HOLDs), and the underlying credit stabilization confirms that direction, but the residual tail risk and soft estimate momentum keep this from clearing the bar for an upgrade.

We rate OHI a **HOLD (income)**. Own it for the well-covered yield and the optionality on a clean credit resolution, but underwrite the dividend as the return rather than a re-rating, and watch the Genesis lease decision and cash-basis revenue share as the variables that would move this off neutral.
