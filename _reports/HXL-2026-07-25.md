**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hexcel is a vertically integrated leader in aerospace-grade advanced composites (carbon fiber, prepregs, honeycomb, resins, and engineered structures) with products on every Airbus and Boeing commercial platform and the F-35. The fundamental story has genuinely improved. Q1 2026 marked a clear operating inflection: revenue rose 9.9% YoY to $502M, Commercial Aerospace grew 18.8% on the A350/A320/787/737 MAX ramps, gross margin expanded 450bps to 26.9%, and operating income rose 30%. The company is a direct beneficiary of a near-record combined Airbus+Boeing backlog (15,474 aircraft, +3.8% YoY), where the binding constraint is supply-chain throughput rather than end demand. As OEM build rates normalize, Hexcel's operating leverage should compound, aided by the secular trend toward higher composite content per airframe.

The problem is price. The stock has appreciated 76.8% off its 52-week low of $58.20 to $110.28, sitting essentially at its 52-week high of $111.01. At a trailing P/E of 73x and roughly 48x on the current-year consensus of $2.28 and ~34x on the next-year $3.20, the market has already priced in a smooth, multi-year build-rate recovery. Peer-median P/E in the aerospace-composite/defense cohort is around 29x. Even crediting the earnings inflection and the appropriate cycle-adjusted framing, the forward multiple leaves little margin for the execution slips that characterize this supply chain (Boeing MAX turbulence, Airbus supplier constraints). Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current-year EPS is up only 2.5% over 90 days and next-quarter estimates have drifted down 5%.

We rate HXL a HOLD (growth_hold). The business quality and the recovery are real, and we expect the earnings trajectory to support the stock over 12 months. The entry point is unattractive: a stock at ~48x forward earnings, in the middle of a complete C-suite reset, with near-term implied volatility at 49.5% (vs. 31.9% historical) and a backwardated term structure pricing an imminent move ahead of the July 29 print. We would rather own the recovery on a pullback than chase it at record highs.

## Company Overview

Hexcel operates in a single industry, advanced composites, through two reporting segments. Composite Materials (~80% of 2025 sales, $1,516M) comprises carbon fiber, prepregs, honeycomb, and resins; the segment is vertically integrated, with Hexcel manufacturing its own aerospace-grade carbon fiber from PAN precursor and consuming 60–65% internally. Engineered Products (~20%, $378M) supplies finished composite structures, machined honeycomb, RF/EMI materials, and HexAM 3D-printed parts.

By end market, 2025 sales split Commercial Aerospace 61% and Defense/Space & Other 39%. Commercial exposure is highly concentrated: Airbus plus its subcontractors account for roughly 39% of total sales and Boeing roughly 13%, meaning about 77% of commercial revenue traces to two OEMs. The largest defense program is the F-35, representing under 25% of defense-market revenue.

The competitive moat is durable. Aerospace-grade composites require decade-long qualification databases, 100% material traceability, customer certifications, and heavy capital scale for carbon-fiber production. Qualified competitors are few, principally Toray and Solvay/Syensqo, plus metal/alternative substitutes. Each new aircraft generation uses more composites (the 787 and A350 are over 50% composite by weight), which structurally raises Hexcel's dollar content per airframe over time.

## Financial Analysis

Balance sheet health is solid. Altman Z-Score is 3.9 (safe zone), the current ratio is 2.26, and debt-to-equity is 0.44. Total debt is approximately $998M (3.95% notes due 2027, 5.875% notes due 2035, and $300M drawn on a $750M revolver refinanced to 2031 in March 2026).

The multi-year trajectory shows a soft 2025 followed by a sharp Q1 2026 inflection:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 1,578 | 1,789 | 1,903 | 1,894 | 502 (+9.9% YoY) |
| Operating income ($M) | 175 | 215 | 186 | 172 | 57.6 (+30%) |
| Net income ($M) | 126 | 106 | 132 | 109 | 37.2 (+29%) |
| Gross margin | 22.6% | 24.2% | 24.7% | 23.0% | 26.9% |
| FCF ($M) | — | — | — | 157 | (6.2) |

2025 was a transition year: revenue fell 0.5%, net income declined 17%, and operating margin compressed. Reported operating income of $172M trailed adjusted/segment EBIT of roughly $214M because of about $42M of restructuring, impairment, and divestiture charges tied to portfolio pruning (Austria industrial divestiture, Belgium and Hartford closures, Leicester UK shutdown). The negative EBIT-to-assets flag in raw screening data is an artifact of these one-time charges, not operating deterioration.

The Q1 2026 quarter is the key data point. The gross-margin jump reflects genuine volume leverage and mix as commercial-aero volumes recovered. Operating margin reached 11.5%. Q1 free cash flow was negative, but Q1 is seasonally negative on working-capital build; FY2025 FCF of $157M (an 8.3% margin) is the better run-rate gauge.

Equity fell from $1,528M (2024) to $1,251M (2025), not from losses but from aggressive buybacks: share count dropped from ~82.3M to ~75.9M, including a $350M accelerated share repurchase settling 4.52M shares at a $77.38 average. Interest expense rose 51% YoY in Q1 as rates repriced. The near-term watch item is the $400M 3.95% notes maturing February 2027, which will refinance at meaningfully higher coupons.

The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with Q1'26 beating by 34% ($0.59 vs. $0.44 estimate). The beats narrowed through 2025 before the Q1 pop, and the recovery's shape depends on OEM build-rate execution outside Hexcel's control.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on the commercial-aero build-rate recovery, which is materializing. Commercial Aerospace grew 18.8% in Q1'26, driven by A350, A320, 787, and 737 MAX rates. Demand is not the constraint, so the question is throughput normalization across the supply chain. As rates climb, Hexcel captures operating leverage on a largely fixed manufacturing base, which is what the Q1 margin expansion demonstrated.

Defense/Space was flat-to-down (-4.3% in Q1'26), but that is distorted by the Austrian industrial divestiture; underlying Defense & Space grew about 2%. Rising global defense budgets (European fighters, military helicopters, space) provide a secondary, slower tailwind.

Two structural drivers support durability. First, secular composite penetration: each aircraft generation uses more composite content, raising Hexcel's dollars-per-airframe over multi-decade cycles. Second, product qualification momentum: the HexPly M91 prepreg received NCAMP qualification in July 2026, and Farnborough brought new and extended long-term supply agreements with Boeing across commercial, defense, and space, reaffirming the relationship after years of 737 MAX disruption.

The estimate picture is constructive but not the >30% inflection that would flag a high-conviction growth signal. Next-year consensus rose 5.6% over 90 days to $3.20 and current-year rose 2.5% to $2.28, but current-quarter and next-quarter estimates drifted down 3.4% and 5.0% respectively. Consensus characterizes the trend as stable: the recovery is being priced in gradually, not with the accelerating revision velocity that precedes a re-rating. This tempers the growth score: the direction is right, the magnitude is moderate.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the binding constraint on the recommendation. At $110.28, HXL trades at roughly 73x trailing earnings (EPS $1.37 on 2025's depressed, charge-laden base), ~48x the current-year consensus, and ~34x the next-year consensus. Peer-median P/E in the aerospace-composite/defense cohort is about 29x (SARO 29.5x, AIR 28.2x, HII 19.6x; the KTOS 342x and AVAV negative figures are not comparable earnings-basis anchors).

We apply cycle-adjusted framing: trailing 2025 EPS understates normalized earnings power because it embeds ~$42M of one-time charges and trough build rates. On normalized earnings the multiple compresses, but even on the forward $3.20 the stock trades at ~34x for a business consensus expects to grow high-single-to-low-double-digit EPS at maturity. That is a full multiple. A rough PEG on next-year growth sits well above 1.5. The stock is not cheap on any reasonable normalized basis, and the guardrail against treating analyst targets as floors applies here in reverse: the consensus price-target mean of $98.93 and median of $97.50 both sit below the current price, with the high target at $116. The stock is trading above where the sell side sees fair value, which is unusual for a name I would classify as an inflection buy.

We deliberately avoid penalizing the stock merely for its run; price appreciation driven by fundamental improvement is not itself evidence of overvaluation. But the current price versus current and forward fundamentals is stretched. The recovery is real and largely known; the multiple prices it as a smooth, uninterrupted ramp. Given a supply chain that has repeatedly disappointed (MAX certification, Airbus supplier constraints) and estimate revisions that are stable rather than accelerating, the risk/reward at 48x forward is skewed against new entry. Valuation scores 4.0; the elevated multiple is the dominant offsetting factor against strong business quality.

## Competitive Landscape

Hexcel's competitive position is strong and, at the margin, improving. It is one of a handful of qualified aerospace-grade carbon-fiber producers globally, with positions on every Airbus and Boeing platform and the F-35. Barriers to entry are among the highest in industrials: qualification databases take a decade to build, traceability and certification requirements are exhaustive, and carbon-fiber production is capital-intensive at scale. The Farnborough Boeing long-term agreements and the M91 NCAMP qualification are recent evidence of the moat being reinforced rather than eroded, and the deliberate portfolio focus on high-margin aerospace composites (shedding wind/marine, Austria, Belgium, Hartford, Leicester) should be margin-accretive.

The competitive risks are relationship-level rather than share-loss: any Airbus or Boeing production cut, program delay, or in-sourcing decision is directly material given the ~77% commercial concentration in two customers. The pruning also reduces the industrial diversification that once cushioned aero cyclicality. On balance, trajectory is positive, with Hexcel gaining content per airframe through secular composite penetration, so competitive position scores 7.0, the highest of the six dimensions.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration** is the dominant idiosyncratic risk. Airbus (with subcontractors) is ~39% of sales and Boeing ~13%; roughly 77% of commercial revenue depends on two OEMs. This is not fully de-risked by valuation at a 48x forward multiple; the market is pricing a clean ramp, so a single OEM build-rate cut would be disproportionately punitive.

**Build-rate cyclicality and destocking** amplify volatility. Hexcel ships 4–6 months (up to 18) ahead of aircraft delivery, so supply-chain inventory swings magnify its results. Backlog is not a reliable indicator (mostly requirements contracts).

**Cost pass-through** is a recurring margin risk: management repeatedly flags an inability to fully pass raw-material, tariff, energy, and labor inflation through fixed-price, long-duration contracts. Sole/limited-source inputs (acrylonitrile, aramid paper, specialty resins) add supply risk. FX matters materially: 47% of production and 57% of sales are ex-US, and European operations sell in USD while incurring EUR/GBP costs.

**Leadership transition** is a live execution risk. The entire C-suite has turned over: new Chairman/CEO Tom Gentile (former Spirit AeroSystems CEO), new CFO Jamie Coogan (effective May 1, 2026; the Q1 10-Q was signed under an interim CFO), and new director Neal Keating. A near-total reset during a critical ramp raises the odds of a stumble.

**Legal:** the Lower Passaic River CERCLA matter carries a wide potential remedy range ($0.97B–$2.07B across ~120 PRPs), with a $150M multi-party consent decree under appeal. Hexcel's own reserve is only $0.1M, viewing its share as immaterial, but the appeal remains an open item.

**Refinancing:** the $400M 3.95% notes due February 2027 will refinance at higher rates; interest expense is already rising 51% YoY.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM implied volatility is 49.5% versus 31.9% historical (rich by 17.6 points, 83rd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (49.5% near-term vs. 38.4% at six months), the signature of the market pricing an imminent binary event, here the July 29 earnings print. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.7%. Put/call skew is balanced (0.3%) and put/call volume is negligible, so this is elevated uncertainty around the quarter rather than a directional bearish bet. ATM IV has jumped 14.3 points since late June as the stock rose 13.6%. For a prospective buyer, the message is to wait for the post-print volatility crush and a cleaner entry rather than buy the recovery into a rich-IV, record-high setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($130–140).** Commercial-aero build rates ramp smoothly through 2026–2027, driving continued double-digit revenue growth and operating leverage that lifts gross margin toward the high-20s. The Farnborough Boeing agreements and secular composite penetration compound content per airframe. Activist-influenced capital discipline (portfolio pruning plus the $600M buyback authorization) accretes EPS. Next-year consensus of $3.20 proves conservative, and the stock holds a ~40x forward multiple on a rising earnings base.

**Bear case ($78–85, ~23–29% downside).** A Boeing or Airbus build-rate cut, an F-35 budget disruption, or supply-chain destocking interrupts the ramp. Cost inflation the company cannot pass through compresses the margin gains. The new management team stumbles on execution, and the 48x forward multiple compresses toward the ~29x peer median on decelerating estimates. The Passaic appeal turns adverse. The stock reverts toward the low-$80s, where the analyst-target low sits ($81).

**Base case ($100–108).** The recovery proceeds with the normal lumpiness of this supply chain. EPS reaches consensus ($2.28 this year, $3.20 next year), the multiple compresses modestly from ~48x forward toward the low-40s as the earnings base grows, and the stock trades roughly sideways-to-modestly-lower over 12 months from the current $110, delivering total return roughly in line with the market. Applying ~33x to next-year $3.20 yields ~$106.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 29, 2026 Q2 print is the immediate catalyst; watch Commercial Aerospace growth, gross-margin trajectory, and management's build-rate commentary under the new CFO. The February 2027 notes maturity is the next balance-sheet milestone.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A pullback to the low-$90s or below, compressing the forward multiple toward the mid-30s while the recovery remains intact, which restores asymmetry.
2. Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +10% over 90 days on the forward year, confirming consensus is materially underestimating the ramp.
3. Two consecutive quarters of Commercial Aerospace growth above 15% with gross margin sustained above 26%, demonstrating the operating leverage is durable rather than a single-quarter mix benefit.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A Boeing or Airbus build-rate cut, or an F-35 budget disruption, that reverses the commercial ramp.
2. Gross margin retreating below 24% on cost inflation the company cannot pass through, signaling the Q1 margin jump was not durable.
3. An adverse Passaic River consent-decree ruling materially above Hexcel's $0.1M reserve, or a management execution stumble that cuts guidance.

## Conclusion

Hexcel is a high-quality, wide-moat aerospace-composites franchise experiencing a genuine and demonstrable earnings inflection, backed by a near-record OEM backlog and secular composite-penetration tailwinds. The business deserves a premium multiple, and the Q1'26 numbers validate the recovery. The trajectory of our scoring has been positive (financial health and growth improving as the recovery confirms), and nothing in the current data reverses that.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY because the price has outrun the near-term risk/reward. At $110.28 (77% above its 52-week low, at its 52-week high, above the consensus mean and median price targets, at ~48x forward earnings for high-single-to-low-double-digit steady-state EPS growth) the stock already discounts a smooth ramp. Estimate revisions are stable, not accelerating; the C-suite is in the middle of a complete reset; and near-term implied volatility is pricing a ±10.7% move around the July 29 print. This is a business worth owning through the recovery, but the entry point should be earned on a pullback or a confirmed acceleration in estimates rather than paid at record highs. We would become buyers in the low-$90s or on a demonstrated step-up in revision velocity.
