**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Baxter is a global medical-products company that has spent the past three years dismantling and reassembling its portfolio, selling Kidney Care (to Carlyle, now Vantive, for $3.8B) and BioPharma Solutions ($3.96B), integrating Hillrom, and using the proceeds to delever. The result is a leaner, less diversified enterprise with three segments — Medical Products & Therapies (~$5.3B), Healthcare Systems & Technologies (~$3.1B), and Pharmaceuticals (~$2.5B) — carrying roughly $9.48B of debt against a shrunken, currently unprofitable earnings base. Management concedes the company is now "smaller, less diversified… more vulnerable to changing market conditions." The core problem is that leverage did not fall as fast as the earnings base shrank, and the earnings base is still contracting.

The near-term trajectory is negative on the metrics that matter. Even stripping out $2.09B of FY2025 special items, gross margin fell approximately 4 points in FY2025 and 5 points year-over-year in Q1 2026, driven by tariffs, higher manufacturing and supply costs, and unfavorable mix. Q1 2026 organic sales were -1% (US -4%), and the headline non-GAAP beat ($0.36 vs $0.31) was flattered by FX and masked a GAAP loss. The flagship Novum IQ large-volume infusion pump has been under a Class I recall shipment-and-installation hold in the US and Canada since July 2025 with "no meaningful sales," handing share to ICU Medical and BD. Credit ratings sit at the bottom rung of investment grade (S&P BBB-, Moody's Baa3) with the outlook cut back to Negative in Q1 2026, and the covenant leverage ratio was amended upward in November 2025. The dividend was cut to $0.01/quarter.

We rate BAX AVOID with an overall score of 3.4. The stock is optically cheap — around 11.8x current-year consensus EPS of $1.92 — but this is the textbook value-trap configuration: a compressed multiple reflecting structural impairment rather than a temporary dislocation. The turnaround thesis rests entirely on new CEO Andrew Hider's "Baxter Growth and Performance System" delivering cost and margin recovery while hitting a ~3.0x net-leverage target by end-2026. That is an execution-dependent bet against deteriorating fundamentals, a management team in transition (interim CFO), active securities litigation, and a DOJ investigation. We see no near-term catalyst to reverse the decline and multiple pathways to further downside.

## Company Overview

Baxter operates across three segments following the portfolio transformation:

- **Medical Products & Therapies (~47% of sales, ~18% margin):** Sterile IV solutions, infusion pumps and administration sets, parenteral nutrition, and surgical hemostats/sealants. This is the highest-margin segment but also houses the two most acute problems — the Novum IQ pump under recall hold and the IV solutions business hit by Hurricane Helene.
- **Healthcare Systems & Technologies (~27% of sales, ~14% margin):** The Hillrom-derived franchise (smart beds, Welch Allyn patient monitoring, Front Line Care respiratory devices, OR integration). This is the weakest segment and the site of repeated goodwill and trade-name impairments.
- **Pharmaceuticals (~22% of sales, ~9% margin):** Specialty injectables, inhaled anesthetics, and drug compounding. Compounding is the bright spot, growing +10% in FY2025 and +30% in Q1 2026.

Baxter's competitive advantages are the breadth of its essential-product portfolio, deep group-purchasing-organization and integrated-delivery-network relationships, shared manufacturing scale, and installed-base dynamics where leased equipment pulls consumable revenue. Those moats are intact but no longer wide enough to offset the margin and organic-growth erosion the company is experiencing.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability.** Baxter posted a GAAP net loss of $(957)M in FY2025 ($(1.87)/share), its third loss in four years, driven by special items including $775M of goodwill and trade-name impairments, Hurricane and pump charges, and tax valuation allowances. Q1 2026 continuing operations swung to a $(17)M loss versus +$64M a year earlier. The trailing P/E of -12.2 is meaningless; the relevant metric is forward EPS, and even there the momentum is negative (see Valuation).

**Margins — the single most important trend.** FY2025 gross margin was 30.1% versus 37.5% in FY2024. The drivers — tariffs, higher manufacturing and supply costs, unfavorable mix, and Kidney Care manufacturing-and-supply-agreement (MSA) dilution — are structural and ongoing, not one-time. This is the metric that will determine whether the turnaround thesis has any support, and it is currently going the wrong way.

**Revenue.** FY2025 continuing-operations revenue was $11.24B (+6% reported, +3% operational). The apparent drop from $14.8B in 2023 reflects Kidney Care and BioPharma Solutions moving to discontinued operations, not organic collapse. The top line has stalled.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Debt of ~$9.48B against ~$6.0B equity yields a D/E of 2.28. The Altman Z-score of 1.52 sits in the distress zone. Kidney Care proceeds retired ~$3.81B of debt, but leverage remains high relative to a smaller earnings base. Credit ratings were cut in Q4 2025 (S&P BBB→BBB-, Moody's Baa2→Baa3). The net-leverage target of ~3.0x by end-2026 requires both debt reduction and EBITDA recovery — the latter is not currently cooperating.

**Cash flow — the lifeline.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $951M against $513M of capex, producing modest positive free cash flow of ~$438M. This keeps the story from being a credit crisis, but it is thin relative to the debt load and constrains the R&D and growth investment the turnaround requires. Liquidity is adequate: current ratio 2.31, $1.97B cash, and undrawn revolver capacity.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is weak and deteriorating. Estimate revisions confirm negative earnings momentum: current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut -16.9% over 90 days (to $0.37), and current-year EPS is essentially flat ($1.92) while next-year estimates have edged down -1.0%. This is the opposite of the estimate-revision pattern that would signal an undervalued turnaround.

The specific growth pockets that do exist are outweighed by the drags. Drug compounding (+30% in Q1'26) and advanced surgery (hemostats/sealants) are healthy niches, but they are small relative to the IV solutions business (structurally reset lower by post-Helene fluid-conservation practices), the infusion-pump franchise (Novum IQ under recall hold), and the Hillrom/Healthcare Systems segment (repeated impairments, soft hospital capital demand). US sales at -4% in Q1'26 indicate the largest market is contracting.

The Novum IQ hold is particularly damaging because it was designed to be the next-generation growth product; the hold since July 2025 both removes revenue and cedes share to ICU Medical and BD during a competitive window. We do not know when it resolves, and Spectrum IQ is only a fallback.

## Valuation Assessment

At $22.61, BAX trades at roughly 11.8x current-year consensus EPS of $1.92 and ~11.3x next-year $2.01. Against a Health Care Equipment peer set with a median P/E of 48.6 (RVTY 48.6, PEN 73.4, PODD 34.8, MASI 126.7, GMED 18.8), Baxter looks deeply discounted. That discount is warranted, not an opportunity.

The cheapness reflects three durable impairments rather than a mispricing: GAAP unprofitability with erosion visible even ex-special items; leverage at the top of the acceptable range with a Negative credit outlook; and negative organic growth. A low multiple that reflects structural impairment with no identifiable recovery catalyst is a value trap, and the estimate-revision trend confirms consensus is still marking the business down, not up. The inflection-stock exception — where a high trailing multiple masks tripling earnings — does not apply here; earnings are declining.

Analyst targets frame the range: consensus mean $21.95 (below current price), median $20.00, with a low of $17.00 and a high of $40.00. The mean and median sitting at or below the current price signals that analysts do not see asymmetric upside. The $40 high target is an outlier reflecting a successful-turnaround scenario that requires margin recovery, pump resolution, and deleveraging all to break right. We anchor to the median-to-low range as the more probable outcome given current trajectory.

We assign a valuation score of 4.0 — modestly above the floor because the free-cash-flow lifeline and bottom-of-investment-grade rating provide some downside support, but well below neutral because the compressed multiple is a warning, not a buy signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Baxter competes across fragmented med-tech and pharma niches, and no single competitor spans all its segments. That breadth is a modest moat, but its trajectory is deteriorating. In infusion pumps, the Novum IQ hold hands share to ICU Medical and BD. In smart beds and patient monitoring (Hillrom/Welch Allyn), soft hospital capital spending and competitive pressure have driven repeated impairments — Front Line Care goodwill was impaired $485M in FY25 and $425M in FY24, and the Welch Allyn trade name was written down $290M. Front Line Care still carries $1.51B of goodwill, where a 5% fair-value decline would trigger ~$160M of further impairment.

Industry dynamics compound the pressure: GPO/IDN buyer consolidation drives price concessions, long-term customer contracts limit tariff pass-through, and CMS competitive-bidding expansion adds reimbursement pressure. The healthiest positions — compounding and advanced surgery — are too small to move the overall trajectory. The competitive position is stable-to-eroding, warranting a score of 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

Baxter carries an unusually dense stack of idiosyncratic risks, several of which are not fully priced:

1. **Leverage against falling earnings.** Distress-zone Z-score (1.52), bottom-rung investment-grade ratings, and a Negative outlook. A downgrade to junk would raise borrowing costs and restrict capital access.
2. **Novum IQ recall hold.** Ongoing since July 2025 with resolution timing we cannot predict; $105M+ in charges while the hold persists.
3. **Structural IV-demand reset.** Post-Helene fluid-conservation practices have permanently lowered demand in the largest segment.
4. **Tariffs.** Section 232 pharma/device probe and Section 122 duties actively compressing margins with limited pass-through ability.
5. **Impairment risk not over.** Front Line Care and Welch Allyn remain flagged as sensitive.
6. **Tax.** US deferred-tax valuation allowance and an IRS transfer-pricing NOPA (Costa Rica/Puerto Rico, 2019–20) with ~$280M reserves.
7. **Litigation.** Securities class actions (Novum LVP, class period Feb 2022–Oct 2025), derivative suits, and DOJ CIDs on IV flexible containers and the Hillrom respiratory business.
8. **Management transition.** New CEO (July 2025) plus a CFO departure within a year (interim CFO by April 2026).

Each of these is specific, dated, and in several cases binary. The risk-factor score is 3.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm bordering on complacent. Near-term ATM IV is 38.6% against 1-year historical volatility of 49.7%, leaving IV cheap (-11.2 pp spread) and in the 2nd percentile of its own chain. The put/call skew is balanced (-1.2%), and the term structure is in contango (near-term 38.6% < long-term 45.2%), signaling no near-term event premium. ATM IV collapsed from 232.0% to 38.6% between the June and July snapshots as the Q1 earnings and near-term catalysts resolved. The 6-month tenor shows a more meaningful -4.0% put skew and an elevated 6.55 put/call volume ratio, indicating some positioning for downside over the medium term. Short interest is elevated at 11.8% of float (5.5 days to cover), consistent with institutional bearishness. The absence of near-term put demand reflects a slow-grind deterioration rather than an imminent binary, which is why this is an AVOID rather than an active short.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Hider's "Baxter Growth and Performance System" (a Danaher/ATS-style continuous-improvement model) delivers genuine cost takeout and margin recovery. Tariff headwinds ease or get passed through. The Novum IQ hold resolves and the pump re-launches into a receptive installed base. Compounding and advanced surgery continue double-digit growth. Deleveraging reaches ~3.0x by end-2026, the credit outlook stabilizes, and the market re-rates the stock toward peer multiples. In this scenario the $40 high target becomes achievable over 18-24 months, roughly +77% from current levels.

### Bear Case
Margins continue to erode on tariffs and supply costs, organic growth stays negative, the Novum IQ hold drags on, and a further goodwill impairment hits Front Line Care. EBITDA fails to recover, leverage stays elevated, and the credit rating slips to junk, raising borrowing costs and limiting capital access. Securities litigation and the DOJ CID produce material settlements. The stock retraces toward the $17 analyst low and its $15.73 52-week low, roughly -25% to -31% from current levels.

The base case sits between: a company grinding through an execution-dependent turnaround with the market unwilling to pay up until margin recovery is proven. We see the balance of probabilities skewed toward the bear side given that the margin and organic-growth trends are currently negative and the estimate revisions are still falling.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** There is no single dated catalyst likely to drive a sharp decline. The deterioration is a slow grind — margins eroding quarter by quarter, uncertain Novum IQ resolution timing, and an open-ended credit-downgrade risk. The next earnings report and any Front Line Care impairment or credit-rating action are the closest to catalysts, but none has a firm date or a clearly quantifiable downside gap.

**Timing window.** The relevant threats (a further downgrade, an impairment, a litigation development) are open-ended over the next 6-18 months, longer than the ideal short window.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is already elevated at 11.8% of float with 5.5 days to cover. This is not extreme, but combined with the modest ~$11.7B market cap and the possibility of a low-quality FX-aided earnings beat (as in Q1'26), a squeeze on any positive headline is a real risk. The Q1'26 non-GAAP beat and the improving analyst sentiment trend (one strong-buy upgrade, one sell removal) show the tape can rally on thin news.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market is not confirming an imminent bearish thesis — near-term IV is cheap and in the 2nd percentile, skew is balanced, and the term structure is in contango. Only the 6-month tenor shows meaningful put positioning. This neutral-to-complacent near-term positioning is a warning sign for shorts: the market is not pricing a near-term break.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear-case downside is roughly -25% to -31% (to $15.73-17). Upside squeeze/turnaround risk on any positive execution surprise is easily +20-30%. That is close to a 1:1 setup absent a dated catalyst, below the 2:1 asymmetry required to short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** This is a mediocre-and-deteriorating business in a difficult environment, not an actively collapsing one. The free-cash-flow lifeline, bottom-of-investment-grade rating, and calm near-term options positioning mean the downside is a grind rather than a cliff, while elevated short interest and a rally-prone tape create real squeeze risk. Avoid owning it; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria** — we would revisit the name constructively if any of the following materialize:
1. Ex-special gross margin stabilizes and turns positive year-over-year for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the "Baxter Growth and Performance System" is delivering.
2. The Novum IQ shipment-and-installation hold is lifted and the pump resumes meaningful sales, removing both a revenue drag and the associated litigation overhang.
3. Net leverage credibly tracks toward the ~3.0x target by end-2026 with the credit outlook restored to Stable, and organic growth returns to positive territory.
4. Insider conviction: clustered open-market purchases by the new CEO or CFO, which have been conspicuously absent.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — tariffs, margin compression, the pump hold, and deleveraging — are likely to persist through at least end-2026, the company's own stated leverage-target date. We would not expect a durable fundamental turn before mid-to-late 2026 at the earliest, and only if execution beats the current negative trajectory.

## Conclusion

Baxter is a highly leveraged, GAAP-unprofitable medical-products company whose transformation left it smaller, less diversified, and more exposed to the margin and organic-growth erosion now visible in its results. The cheapness relative to peers is real but earned: it reflects structural impairment — falling margins, negative organic growth, a stalled flagship product, bottom-rung credit with a Negative outlook, active litigation, and a management team in transition — rather than a mispricing. Estimate revisions are still falling, analyst targets sit at or below the current price, and there is no identifiable near-term catalyst to reverse the decline.

The turnaround is possible but requires nearly everything to break right, and the current data trends run the other way. We rate BAX **AVOID** with an overall score of 3.4, extending the prior string of AVOID calls. The score has stabilized in the 3.2-3.6 range over the past several reports, and the latest data — a low-quality FX-aided Q1 beat masking a 5-point margin decline, a credit outlook cut to Negative, and continued estimate cuts — confirms rather than reverses the negative trend. We would move to the sidelines constructively only on demonstrated margin stabilization, resolution of the Novum IQ hold, and credible deleveraging with a restored credit outlook.
