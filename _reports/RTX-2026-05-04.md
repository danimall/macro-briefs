## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation is trading at $173 after a 19% pullback from its March 2026 high of $214.50, triggered by the broader market selloff following the escalation of the US-Iran naval confrontation. The fundamental thesis remains intact: $268B backlog (+23% YoY), 11% organic revenue growth in 2025, expanding margins across all three segments, and strong defense bookings from active munitions demand. Q1 2026 delivered an approximately 18% earnings beat on 10% organic sales growth, with full-year guidance raised.

The stock trades at approximately 25x FY2026 adjusted EPS (guidance midpoint of $6.80), a premium to historical A&D multiples but justified by: (1) commercial aerospace aftermarket growth accelerating into a multi-year upcycle, (2) munitions replenishment creating decade-long demand visibility for Raytheon, and (3) the Powder Metal overhang improving with only $0.7B accrual remaining, though execution risks persist. The GTF Advantage EASA certification for the A320neo family in April 2026 is a significant positive milestone for the P&W segment.

At current levels, the stock offers 24% upside to the $216 mean analyst target and roughly 18% upside to my base case of $205-210, assuming margins continue their recovery trajectory and defense bookings sustain. The pullback creates a more favorable entry than the $200-210 range where risk/reward was less attractive.

## Company Overview

RTX is the world's largest aerospace and defense company by revenue ($88.6B in 2025) with approximately 185,000 employees. The company operates through three segments:

- **Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B revenue, 16.3% operating margin): Avionics, systems, and aircraft interiors for both commercial and military platforms. High-margin aftermarket is a significant contributor to segment revenue.
- **Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B revenue, 7.9% operating margin): The GTF engine family powers the Airbus A320neo fleet; the F135 engine is the sole powerplant for F-35. Long-term maintenance contracts create recurring revenue.
- **Raytheon** ($28.0B revenue, 11.5% operating margin): Missiles, integrated defense systems, radars. Patriot, AMRAAM, Stinger, Tomahawk, SM-3, SPY-6 — the largest munitions portfolio in the Western world.

Revenue mix is approximately 52-53% government/military and 48% commercial, providing a natural hedge across cycles. The $268B backlog represents approximately 3x annual revenue, providing exceptional forward visibility.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

Revenue grew 9.7% in 2025 to $88.6B, with organic growth at approximately 11% after adjusting for divestitures. The growth trajectory has been consistent: from the $67.1B base in 2022, RTX has grown revenue by 32% cumulatively. All three segments contributed:

- Collins: +7% reported growth (commercial aftermarket strength driving mix improvement)
- Pratt & Whitney: +17% reported growth (aftermarket and long-term service agreements ramping)
- Raytheon: +5% reported growth (bookings strength ahead of revenue recognition given long production cycles)

Q1 2026 delivered approximately 10% organic growth with guidance raised. RTX's updated FY2026 guidance calls for adjusted sales of $92.5–$93.5B and organic sales growth of 5–6%.

### Margin Expansion

Operating margin recovered from the 5.2% trough in 2023 (Powder Metal charge year) to 10.5% in 2025. The trajectory by segment:

| Segment | 2023 Margin | 2025 Margin | 2026E Target |
|---------|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| Collins | 14.6% | 16.3% | 17-18% |
| P&W | -8.0% | 7.9% | Estimated 10-11% (analyst assumption) |
| Raytheon | 9.0% | 11.5% | 12-13% |

P&W's margin recovery from negative territory to 7.9% has further room to expand as Powder Metal inspection costs wind down and the GTF installed base generates maintenance revenue. Q1 2026 adjusted P&W segment margin reached 8.7%, showing continued progress. Collins is approaching best-in-class avionics margins. Raytheon benefits from operating leverage on higher production volumes.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow of $7.9B in 2025 (9.0% FCF margin) represents a step-up driven by higher earnings and favorable working capital timing, with operating cash flow of $10.6B jumping $3.4B YoY. FCF conversion relative to net income exceeds 100% (using $7.9B FCF vs. $6.7B net income), signaling high-quality earnings. Capital allocation priorities are: (1) debt reduction ($3.4B repaid in 2025, $3.4B maturing in 2026), (2) dividends (~$3.7B annualized), and (3) future buyback resumption once leverage targets are achieved.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt stands at $37.9B with $7.4B cash, producing net debt of ~$30.5B. Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 2.6x — elevated but improving and manageable for an investment-grade credit (Baa1/Positive at Moody's, BBB+/Stable at S&P). Scheduled maturities of $3.4B (2026), $2.9B (2027), and $3.5B (2028) are easily serviceable from operating cash flow.

The $85.2B goodwill/intangibles balance (50% of assets) depresses traditional leverage metrics like Altman Z-Score but reflects the 2020 merger accounting, not operational distress. Annual amortization of ~$2.0B ($1.15/share after-tax impact) will gradually reduce this balance over the next decade.

## Growth Analysis

**Commercial Aerospace (48% of revenue):** Revenue passenger miles continue expanding globally, driving Collins and P&W aftermarket demand. Narrowbody OEM production remains constrained by Boeing and Airbus supply chains, which extends the duration of the aftermarket supercycle — older engines require more maintenance as fleets age while waiting for replacements. The GTF Advantage EASA certification opens the path for next-generation engine deliveries and commercial entry into service.

**Defense (~52% of revenue):** Multiple demand vectors are converging:
1. Active US-Iran conflict driving demand for Patriot, Tomahawk, SM-3, and AMRAAM munitions
2. Ukraine-related stockpile replenishment continuing for NATO allies
3. Golden Dome missile defense initiative ($24.4B allocated) where RTX states it is "well-positioned to play a role" with next-generation technologies across land, sea, and space
4. Foreign military sales growing from $5.0B (2023) to $6.7B (2025) as allied nations increase spending
5. $25.4B in supplemental munitions/supply chain resiliency funding

Defense bookings grew from $51B (2023) to $61B (2024 and 2025), and the $107B defense backlog provides multi-year revenue conversion visibility. Raytheon's revenue growth has lagged bookings growth because of production cycle length — this creates a multi-year tailwind as booked orders convert to revenue through 2027-2029.

**Total organic growth outlook:** RTX's updated FY2026 guidance calls for organic sales growth of 5–6%, with adjusted sales of $92.5–$93.5B. Q1 2026 organic growth of 10% ran ahead of this full-year pace, reflecting timing of defense deliveries. Estimate revisions are trending positive: current year EPS revised up 1.3% over 30 days, 1.8% over 90 days.

## Valuation Assessment

At $173, RTX trades at:
- ~25.4x FY2026 guidance midpoint adjusted EPS ($6.80)
- 22.9x FY2027E adjusted EPS ($7.55)
- 34.9x trailing GAAP EPS ($4.96)
- ~14x EV/EBITDA (estimated)
- 3.4% FCF yield on 2025 actual ($7.9B FCF / $233B market cap)
- 1.68% dividend yield

**Peer context:** The peer median P/E is 35.2x (dragged up by Boeing at 89.8x and Howmet at 63.8x). Among comparable defense primes, LMT trades at 24.7x and GD at 21.9x. RTX's ~25x forward P/E sits between these, appropriate given its higher growth rate and unique commercial/defense diversification, but below GE Aerospace's 35.2x given GE's higher margin profile.

**Why the market could be underpricing RTX at $173:**
1. The Powder Metal situation is improving ($0.7B accrual remaining vs. $2.8B peak) but the market may still embed excessive risk premium
2. P&W margins have meaningful expansion room (7.9% currently, with Q1 2026 at 8.7%) that isn't fully reflected in near-term estimates
3. Defense bookings-to-revenue conversion lag means 2027-2028 revenue growth is better supported than current estimates suggest
4. The 19% drawdown from March highs creates an entry discount that didn't exist 60 days ago

**Why valuation prevents a higher score:** At ~25x forward earnings, RTX is not cheap in absolute terms. The FCF yield of 3.4% provides moderate support but doesn't create a valuation floor. If defense spending deceleration or a diplomatic resolution in Iran materializes, the growth premium embedded in the multiple would compress. The stock needs continued execution to justify the current price — there's no margin of safety in the traditional sense.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX's competitive position remains strong and improving across all three segments:

**Collins Aerospace** holds dominant installed-base positions in avionics and aircraft systems. The aftermarket generates high margins with high switching costs (airframers don't change avionics mid-program). Collins competes against Honeywell and Parker Hannifin but holds sole-source positions on key military platforms.

**Pratt & Whitney** competes directly with CFM International (GE/Safran JV) in commercial narrowbody engines. The GTF vs. LEAP competition is the industry's defining rivalry. GTF holds a significant share of the A320neo market. The F135 is sole-source on F-35 with no competitive alternative. GTF Advantage certification strengthens the next-generation competitive position.

**Raytheon** holds leading positions in integrated air and missile defense, with Patriot being the dominant Western system at scale. For specific munitions categories (AMRAAM, SM-3, Stinger), sole-source or dual-source arrangements create strong competitive positions. The active conflict environment makes qualification of alternative suppliers prohibitively time-consuming. RTX's own filings note that competition remains significant across all of its businesses.

The score trajectory shows competitive position declining from 8.5 to 7.5. I maintain 7.5 as appropriate: the company's competitive moats are intact, but NATO fracture developments create longer-term uncertainty about European defense procurement autonomy. The near-term impact is negligible (backlogs are locked in), but the structural direction of EU procurement independence represents a headwind beginning FY2028+.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Powder Metal execution risk (improving but ongoing):** $0.7B accrual remaining with estimated ~$0.7B cash impact in 2026. Aircraft-on-ground levels expected to remain elevated through 2026. RTX explicitly warns that assumptions around shop visits, inspection results, turnaround time, parts availability, MRO capacity, and customer negotiations could materially affect results. If inspection findings worsen or new defects emerge in adjacent engine models, the charge could re-expand. Probability of material adverse surprise: 10-15%.

2. **Tariff exposure on fixed-price contracts:** RTX cannot pass through tariff costs on existing fixed-price defense contracts. Management states current tariffs will not have "material adverse effect" but acknowledges significant uncertainty. Incremental tariff escalation could pressure margins 50-100bps if defense supply chain imports face similar treatment.

3. **Iran conflict diplomatic resolution:** A diplomatic breakthrough would reduce near-term munitions urgency and remove some of the defense premium embedded in the stock. Oil price normalization would simultaneously pressure defense spending rationale. Downside: 10-15% if combined with broader A&D de-rating.

4. **Fixed-price development program overruns:** Net EAC adjustments were -$386M in 2025 (improving from -$648M in 2023), but these remain structurally negative and could reverse if specific programs encounter difficulties.

5. **Macro/stagflation impact on commercial aerospace:** If elevated oil prices trigger demand destruction in air travel, commercial aftermarket growth could decelerate from +20% to mid-single digits. At 48% commercial revenue mix, this would reduce organic growth by 3-5%.

6. **Regulatory/compliance risk:** RTX is under Deferred Prosecution Agreements with the DOJ and SEC for legacy Raytheon bribery and defective pricing issues, with an independent compliance monitor expected to be installed under a three-year term. A separate DOS Consent Agreement covers export control violations. Non-compliance could result in prosecution or contract debarment.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV at 33.4% is 10pp above 1-year historical volatility of 23.5% — the market is pricing elevated uncertainty following the post-earnings selloff and broader macro vol spike. The term structure is flat (33.4% near-term vs. 32.7% at 6-month), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a specific event. Put/call OI ratio of 0.69 and volume ratio of 0.30 are both below 1.0, suggesting positioning remains modestly bullish despite the drawdown. Notable unusual activity: 730 contracts on the $178 call expiring May 8 (4.6x volume/OI) suggests near-term upside positioning. (Note: Options data sourced from market data providers as of early May 2026 and is time-sensitive.)

The IV compression from 46.9% to 33.4% over the May 1-4 period (post-earnings normalization) confirms the market has digested the Q1 results and is not pricing any specific binary event. The remaining IV premium over HV likely reflects the broader macro vol regime.

## Investment Thesis

- P&W margins expand to 11%+ by FY2027 as Powder Metal costs normalize and GTF Advantage enters production
- Defense bookings convert to revenue at accelerating pace: Raytheon revenue grows 8-10% annually through 2028
- Golden Dome and supplemental munitions funding ($50B+ combined) extend the defense supercycle beyond current estimates
- EPS reaches $8.00+ by FY2027 (vs. current $7.55 consensus), stock re-rates to 27x forward = $216+

- P&W Powder Metal issue re-expands; new engine models affected

- EPS growth stalls at $6.80-7.00 level; stock de-rates to 21-22x = $145-155

## Conclusion

RTX at $173 offers a compelling entry point for a company with 3x revenue coverage in backlog, expanding margins, and structural demand tailwinds from both commercial aerospace recovery and defense spending acceleration. The 19% drawdown from March highs was driven by macro factors (market vol spike, broader risk-off) rather than fundamental deterioration — Q1 beat estimates by approximately 18% and guidance was raised.

The valuation at ~25x forward is full relative to historical A&D norms but justified by above-average organic growth and margin expansion potential. The Powder Metal situation is improving with only $0.7B accrual remaining, though RTX cautions that inspection results, MRO capacity, and customer negotiations could still materially affect outcomes. The GTF Advantage EASA certification for the A320neo family is a significant product milestone.

I maintain a BUY recommendation despite the declining score trajectory. The score decline from 6.8 to 6.4 in prior reports reflected correctly identified risk factor deterioration (tariffs, macro vol, post-earnings gap-down). However, the fundamental value proposition has strengthened: estimates are rising, the backlog is at record levels, and the entry price is 19% cheaper than 60 days ago. The specific impediment preventing a higher-conviction score is valuation — at ~25x forward, the stock requires continued execution and cannot absorb significant negative surprises. Position sizing at 2-3% reflects this calibration: strong thesis, full valuation, elevated macro uncertainty.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** FY2025 adjusted EPS corrected from $6.88 to $6.29, and FY2026 organic growth guidance corrected from 10–11% to 5–6% (company guidance of $92.5–$93.5B sales). These corrections reduce the near-term earnings growth rate implied in the thesis and increase the effective forward P/E multiple, which may affect the confidence level in the base-case target of $208.
