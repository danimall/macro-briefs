**Research Date: June 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Automatic Data Processing is a global leader in Human Capital Management that pays roughly one in six U.S. workers, serves over 1.1 million clients, and moves $3.3 trillion in client funds annually. The business is a high-quality recurring-revenue compounder: ~92% revenue retention, ~13-year average client tenure in Employer Services, ROE near 66%, an FCF margin around 23%, and top-tier (A-1+/P-1/F1+) commercial paper ratings. The most recent quarter (Q3 FY26, reported April 29, 2026) was a beat-and-raise: revenue +7% to $5.94B, adjusted EBIT margin +80bps to 30.2%, adjusted EPS $3.38, and the company raised full-year guidance. Margins are expanding, pricing power is intact, and capital returns stepped up with a new $6B buyback authorized in January 2026.

The stock, however, has fallen roughly 30% from its $315.98 52-week high to $214.60. The de-rating reflects sentiment rather than numbers: a market narrative that AI-native competitors and agentic automation will erode the payroll moat. So far the financial evidence runs the other way: retention is at record client-satisfaction levels, margins are accelerating to 30%+, and the company is deploying its own GenAI tooling (ADP Assist) on a proprietary 42-million-worker dataset that competitors cannot replicate. The dislocation between fear and delivered fundamentals is the central question, and it is partly answered by a director's open-market purchase of ~$746K at $206 in May 2026, right after the beat-and-raise.

We rate ADP a HOLD with a growth bias. The valuation has compressed to attractive territory (~20x forward, roughly in line with the 21.9x peer median for a higher-quality franchise), and the risk/reward has improved as the price fell on improving fundamentals. What keeps this short of a BUY is a genuine cyclical headwind that the AI debate has obscured: roughly $1.2B of FY25 revenue is float income on client funds, and a Fed easing cycle would directly compress the highest-margin, fastest-growing piece of recent results. Combined with only +1% same-store employment growth and ongoing PEO margin erosion, the forward earnings path is more two-sided than the "Dividend King quality at a discount" framing suggests.

## Company Overview

ADP operates two reportable segments. **Employer Services (ES)** is ~67% of revenue ($13.9B in FY25) and runs the cloud HCM platforms tiered by client size: RUN (940K+ small businesses), Workforce Now (90K+ mid/large), and the newer Lyric HCM for large global enterprises (launched September 2024). ES is the high-margin engine, with segment margin of 36.1% in FY25 expanding toward ~41% in the most recent quarter. **PEO Services (ADP TotalSource)** is ~33% of revenue ($6.7B), a co-employment model serving 750K+ worksite employees. Roughly 64% of PEO "revenue" is zero-margin benefits pass-throughs (wages, taxes, benefits for worksite employees). Excluding pass-throughs, PEO revenue is closer to $2.4B, and PEO margins are structurally lower (~13-14%) and compressing.

The moat rests on four pillars: extremely sticky recurring revenue (13-year ES tenure, ~92% retention); the industry's largest HCM dataset (1M+ organizations, 42M workers); hard-to-replicate compliance and regulatory infrastructure (GDPR Binding Corporate Rules, an OCC-chartered ADP Trust Bank holding client funds, Certified PEO status); and scale with a fortress balance sheet. No single client exceeds 2% of revenue.

Leadership is CEO Maria Black (since January 2023) and CFO Peter Hadley, who signs the latest 10-Q following a CFO transition from Don McGuire.

## Financial Analysis

The headline screen risk, an Altman Z-Score of 0.7 implying distress, is a false signal and should be disregarded. ADP's balance sheet is dominated by funds held for clients (~$31B–$46B), offset by an equal client-funds obligation: payroll money in transit held in a fiduciary capacity through a consolidated grantor trust. Stripping out the pass-through, the operating balance sheet is pristine: $3.2B cash, only ~$4.0B of fixed-rate senior notes, $10.6B in untapped committed credit facilities, and the highest commercial-paper ratings. The reported 7.6x debt/equity ratio is similarly an artifact of low book equity because ADP returns nearly all earnings to shareholders.

Revenue has compounded steadily: $16.5B (FY22) → $18.0B (FY23) → $19.2B (FY24) → $20.6B (FY25, +7%). FY26 is tracking +7% reported / +6% organic constant currency. FY25 EPS was $9.98 (+10%); nine-month FY26 diluted EPS rose ~10% to $8.49. Consolidated EBIT margin reached 25.8% in FY25 (+50bps) and Q3 FY26 hit 30.0% (+80bps), driven by operating efficiencies, lower contract-cost amortization, and float income. Capital intensity is minimal, with capex of only ~$170–250M on $20B+ of revenue.

The earnings-surprise record is consistent: four consecutive beats over the last four quarters, though the magnitude is modest (0.4% to 1.7%). This is a company that delivers reliably and narrowly rather than dramatically, appropriate for a low-volatility compounder.

The float-income engine deserves explicit attention because it is both a recent tailwind and the chief forward swing factor. Interest on funds held for clients was $1,189M in FY25 (up from $1,025M in FY24) and ~$999M in the first nine months of FY26. Average client balances of ~$37.6B (FY25), growing toward $40-48B in FY26, earn ~3.2-3.4%. This income carries very high incremental margins and has driven a disproportionate share of recent margin expansion. A 25bp parallel rate move is roughly $20-24M pre-tax. The portfolio is laddered out to 5-10 years to smooth rate cycles, and net unrealized AFS losses have shrunk to -$361M from -$1.5B in FY24 as rates moderated.

Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly. FY25 returned $3.7B ($2.4B dividends + $1.3B buybacks). The January 2026 authorization replaced the 2022 $5B plan, and nine-month FY26 buybacks of 5.7M shares (versus 3.4M prior year) show management leaning into the lower price. ADP is a Dividend King (50+ years of increases) with the quarterly dividend raised to $1.70 in FY26, supporting a current yield of ~3.1% that is comfortably covered.

## Growth Analysis

The honest read on growth is steady but unspectacular, with a cyclical asterisk. Revenue growth of ~7% reported and ~6% organic is durable and recurring, but the composition matters. Same-store U.S. employment ("pays per control") grew only +1% in both FY25 and FY26, and PEO worksite employee growth decelerated to +2%. Organic volume is therefore a minimal tailwind; growth is being carried by pricing, new bookings, float income, and FX.

Estimate revisions confirm this is not an inflection story. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $11.07 (revised +1.0% over 90 days), and next-year at $12.19 (+1.9% over 90 days). These are stable-to-marginally-positive revisions, not the +30% velocity that flags a fundamental re-rating. Growth prospects are real and reliable but not accelerating, which is why this dimension scores in the middle of the range despite the company's quality.

The forward growth swing factors cut both ways. On the upside, ADP Assist and the broader AI toolset are already showing up as a margin tailwind (service and sales productivity), and Lyric HCM plus the WorkForce Software acquisition (Oct 2024, $1.17B) extend reach into large global enterprise. On the downside, two cyclical levers point the same direction in a downturn: a softer labor market compresses per-employee fees and client-fund balances simultaneously, and a Fed easing cycle erodes float income that has been a primary margin driver. The latter is the most underappreciated risk in the current debate, which is dominated by competitive (AI) fears rather than macro sensitivity.

## Valuation Assessment

At $214.60, ADP trades at roughly 20x forward EPS, down from ~26-27x at the January 2026 highs. Against the peer median P/E of 21.9, ADP is now valued in line with or slightly below a group of structurally lower-quality businesses: Paychex (21.9x), Paycom (15.4x), Paylocity (23.2x), Korn Ferry (14.5x), and Robert Half (24.4x). For a franchise with 92% retention, expanding margins, and the cleanest balance sheet in the group, in-line-to-discounted multiples on a quality basis is reasonable support.

The 30% drawdown is material, and at that magnitude the burden of proof sits with anyone arguing the market is wrong. Our view is that this is a sentiment-driven de-rating rather than a fundamental break, because the de-rating occurred through a beat-and-raise quarter with margins expanding to 30%+, the opposite of what an actual competitive-share-loss thesis would produce. The specific mispricing is the gap between the AI-disruption narrative (which would predict decelerating retention and revenue) and the delivered numbers (record retention, accelerating margins, pricing power). We apply caution: a cheap multiple is not, by itself, a buy signal, and the genuine forward risk to float income means the "cheap quality compounder" framing is incomplete. A faster Fed easing cycle could pressure the very earnings the forward multiple is applied to.

Analyst targets span low $190, mean $246.80, median $240, and high $305, with the consensus mean ~15% above the current price. We treat these as informative but not as floors here, because ADP is not a high-momentum stock with rising estimates; the sentiment trend is flagged as degrading, and one analyst recently moved to Equal Weight at $214. We score valuation modestly above neutral: the price offers reasonable asymmetry given quality and capital returns, but not the deep mispricing that would justify a high conviction score, and the float-income overhang argues against treating the discount as a free option.

## Competitive Landscape

ADP competes across the HCM spectrum: traditional payroll/HRO (Paychex, Paycom, Paylocity, Dayforce), ERP-embedded HCM (Workday, Oracle, SAP SuccessFactors), AI-native SMB disruptors (Rippling, Deel, Gusto), and clients' in-house systems. The central debate is whether AI lowers entry barriers and commoditizes payroll, or whether ADP's compliance infrastructure, tenure, dataset, and scale make it the natural winner of the AI transition.

The financial evidence to date favors the incumbent. Retention is at record levels, margins are accelerating, and pricing power is evident, none of which is consistent with share loss. ADP can deploy AI agents on proprietary data competitors cannot access while harvesting its own service and sales productivity gains, which is already showing up in the margin line. The compliance and regulatory moat (multi-jurisdiction tax, GDPR, BSA/AML for the Trust Bank, ERISA, Certified PEO) is genuinely hard for nimble entrants to replicate at scale, and the absence of client concentration limits the risk of a single defection cascading.

The competitive-position score has drifted down across recent reports (from ~8.5 to ~7.5 historically, and we mark it 6.5 here) for a defensible reason: the trajectory is the relevant variable, and while ADP is not visibly losing share, the AI transition introduces a structural uncertainty that did not exist a few years ago. The disruptors are real, well-funded, and growing fast in SMB. ADP's position is still strong and arguably strengthening on margins, but the trajectory is stable with a widening fan of outcomes rather than unambiguously improving, which is why we no longer score it at the top of the range.

## Risk Assessment

The 10-Q states no material changes to the FY25 10-K risk factors. The risks that genuinely matter:

1. **Float-income / rate sensitivity.** The single biggest cyclical earnings swing factor. Interest on client funds and a disproportionate share of recent margin expansion sit here. A faster-than-expected Fed easing cycle compresses it directly. The medium-term path of rates is the principal threat to the forward earnings base. [unverified — specific Fed rate-decision probability not in sources]

2. **AI disruption / technological obsolescence.** Explicitly elevated in the FY25 10-K and the dominant market narrative. Not yet visible in the numbers, but a real, hard-to-falsify structural risk that justifies a degraded competitive and risk score relative to ADP's history.

3. **Employment-level sensitivity.** +1% pays-per-control means little organic volume cushion. A labor downturn pressures per-employee fees and float balances simultaneously.

4. **PEO margin compression.** PEO EBT margins are sliding -110 to -120bps in FY26 on workers' comp/SUI costs, a lower ADP Indemnity benefit, and higher selling costs. Ex-pass-through growth is a more modest ~5%.

5. **Cybersecurity.** ADP holds enormous volumes of PII and moves $3.3 trillion annually; a material breach would be severe. Past incidents have not been material per the company.

6. **Regulatory and legal.** Multi-jurisdiction exposure plus an outstanding ERISA class action (TotalSource Retirement Savings Plan, filed 2020), defended and not deemed material.

These risks are largely known and partly priced after the drawdown, but the float-income cyclicality and the unfalsifiable AI overhang keep this from being a low-risk name. We score risk factors mid-range, reflecting both the asymmetric recovery upside already priced and the genuine two-sided macro exposure.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 35.9% sits well above 1-year historical vol of 24.3% (an 11.6-point spread, "rich"), and the term structure is in backwardation (35.9% near-term vs 29.2% at six months), pricing an imminent catalyst, likely the upcoming FY26 year-end print and macro/rate uncertainty. Put/call skew of +4.7% at the front shows elevated put demand, and the put/call OI ratio of 1.05 leans modestly defensive. This diverges from a constructive fundamental read: the market is paying up for downside protection. Short interest is low at 4.3% of float (5.3 days to cover), so this is hedging and macro caution rather than a crowded bearish bet. The rich near-term IV argues against buying premium here and is consistent with our HOLD: the market sees a near-term resolution event that could move the stock either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** ADP is a wide-moat Dividend King de-rated on a narrative its own fundamentals contradict. Retention is at record highs, margins are expanding to 30%+, pricing power is intact, and the company is converting AI from a threat into a margin tailwind via ADP Assist on a proprietary 42M-worker dataset. A director's open-market purchase at $206 after a beat-and-raise is the strongest available insider signal. At ~20x forward and a peer-median multiple for a superior franchise, with a 3.1% covered dividend and a $6B buyback being executed into weakness, the stock re-rates as the AI fear fades and consensus targets (mean $246.80) catch up. Bull case: ~$280.

**Bear case.** The AI threat is real and early; SMB disruptors (Rippling, Deel, Gusto) keep taking share at the entry tier, and agentic automation eventually commoditizes payroll. More immediately, a Fed easing cycle compresses the ~$1.2B float-income engine that has driven recent margin gains, while +1% employment volume offers no offset and PEO margins keep eroding. Earnings stall, the multiple stays compressed, and the stock revisits the April low near $188. Bear case: ~$188 (-12% from current).

The realistic path sits between these. The fundamentals support the bull framing on competition, but the macro/float overhang is a genuine constraint the bull case understates, which is why we land on HOLD rather than BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** We see a positive 12-month appreciation case driven by multiple recovery as the AI-disruption fear is contradicted by continued margin expansion, but the float-income cyclicality and degrading sentiment keep conviction below a BUY threshold. The recommendation history shows HOLD → HOLD → BUY → HOLD, and the bar for a fresh BUY is appropriately higher; the current evidence (stable, not accelerating, estimates plus a real rate headwind) does not clear it.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY26 year-end / FY27 guidance confirming continued EBIT margin expansion (≥30%) AND organic constant-currency revenue growth holding ≥6%, demonstrating the AI fear is unfounded.
- Evidence that float-income compression is manageable — e.g., management guiding to flat-to-up client-funds interest despite rate cuts, or the rate path stabilizing higher-for-longer.
- A further pullback toward the high-$180s/low-$190s (the April low) on unchanged fundamentals, which would push forward P/E below ~18x and restore clear asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of decelerating revenue growth (<5% organic) or retention slipping below ~90%, which would validate the competitive-displacement thesis.
- PEO margin erosion accelerating beyond the current -110 to -120bps pace, or ES margins rolling over.
- A guidance cut tied to float income that the buyback cannot offset.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next FY26 fourth-quarter / full-year earnings report (typically late July/early August), which the options market is pricing as a near-term catalyst, and each subsequent Fed decision given the float-income sensitivity.

## Conclusion

ADP is a high-quality, wide-moat compounder whose pristine (and frequently mis-screened) balance sheet, expanding margins, and disciplined capital returns are being de-rated on an AI-disruption narrative that the latest fundamentals do not support. The Q3 FY26 beat-and-raise, record retention, and a director's open-market purchase near the lows all argue the selloff overshot the reality of competitive share loss. The valuation has compressed to an attractive level against a 21.9x peer median for a superior franchise, and the price decline on improving fundamentals genuinely improves the risk/reward.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is a specific, falsifiable concern the AI debate has crowded out: roughly $1.2B of revenue is rate-sensitive float income, and the Fed easing path threatens the exact line item that has driven recent margin expansion, against only +1% organic employment volume and an eroding PEO margin. The growth picture is stable, not accelerating, and estimate revisions confirm there is no inflection here to chase. We would upgrade on confirmation that margins hold through a rate-cut cycle or on a further pullback toward the April lows; we would downgrade only on actual fundamental deterioration in retention or organic growth. For now, ADP is a defensible hold for investors comfortable owning a quality franchise through cyclical macro uncertainty, with a covered 3.1% yield while they wait.
