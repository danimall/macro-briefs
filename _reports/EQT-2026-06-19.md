**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EQT is the largest natural gas producer in the United States, a pure-play Appalachian operator that became vertically integrated through the July 2024 all-stock merger with Equitrans Midstream. The story over the past 18 months has two clean threads. First, a substantial earnings and cash-flow recovery on rising NYMEX gas prices: FY2025 net income to EQT jumped to $2.04B ($3.31 diluted EPS) from $231M in 2024, and Q1 2026 alone delivered $1.49B ($2.36) on an average realized price of $5.08/Mcfe. Second, an aggressive and visibly executed deleveraging program: total debt fell from $7.86B at year-end 2025 to $6.04B by March 2026, prompting upgrades from S&P and Fitch to BBB (Moody's Baa3, all stable).

The forward case rests on two structural demand tailwinds: LNG export expansion (EQT signed 10-year leases on two LNG carriers in January 2026, commencing 2028) and AI/data-center baseload power demand pulling structural gas consumption into Appalachia. These are genuine optionality, but they are 2028+ events and, in the data-center case, strategic intent rather than booked revenue. The near-term picture has deteriorated: current-quarter consensus EPS is down 21.8% over 90 days, the stock has fallen roughly 26% from its $68.24 52-week high to $50.72, and the entire thesis is reflexively levered to a gas-price cycle that is itself tied to an unverified Iran/Hormuz energy backdrop.

We move EQT to a HOLD. The deleveraging is real and rewarded, the franchise is best-in-class, and the long-term demand optionality is credible. The combination of falling near-term estimates, hedges that cap near-term upside, a structural cash claim from the Blackstone midstream JV ahead of common holders, and a price that is cheap-on-trailing but not asymmetrically cheap-on-forward leaves the risk/reward roughly balanced. This is an uncertain hold, not a high-conviction one.

## Company Overview

EQT operates entirely in the Appalachian Basin (Marcellus and Utica shales), with approximately 93% of proved developed reserves in natural gas. Production runs ~6,500–6,860 MMcfe/d. Since the Equitrans merger, the company reports three segments:

- **Upstream**: E&P, the dominant earnings driver.
- **Gathering**: the gathering system overlapping its own acreage, generating substantial affiliate revenue.
- **Transmission**: FERC-regulated interstate pipelines and storage plus EQT's equity interest in the Mountain Valley Pipeline (MVP) joint venture.

The vertical-integration logic is the core competitive claim: by owning the midstream that serves its own wells, EQT converts third-party gathering costs into intra-company transactions that net out in consolidation, structurally lowering its all-in cost of supply versus Appalachian peers. Management points to a 30+ year drilling inventory. CEO Toby Z. Rice, whose Rice Energy reverse-merged into EQT in 2017, is a founder-operator; his brother Daniel Rice IV sits on the board.

The July 2025 Olympus Energy acquisition (~92 Bcfe of annual upstream volume on contiguous PA acreage) extended the integrated model as a bolt-on. The defining strategic pivots ahead are LNG export leverage and supplying gas to data-center load growth, both targeted at 2028 and beyond.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings recovery on commodity prices.** FY2025 revenue was $23.8B versus $15.1B in 2024, with net income to EQT of $2.04B ($3.31 diluted EPS), up roughly 9x year-over-year. The jump was driven by NYMEX gas rising from $2.30 to $3.42/MMBtu and a full year of Equitrans midstream earnings. GAAP revenue is noisy: the apparent 2023 "collapse" to $5.0B reflects unrealized derivative mark-to-market swings, not operational deterioration. Analysts should anchor on realized prices and Upstream adjusted revenue rather than the GAAP top line.

Q1 2026 was exceptionally strong: net income to EQT of $1.49B ($2.36 diluted EPS) versus $242M a year earlier, on an average realized price of $5.08/Mcfe (NYMEX $4.95). The Appalachian basis turned positive (+$0.38/Mcf) in Q1'26, a favorable reversal versus EQT's historically negative in-basin differential, plausibly linked to new MVP takeaway and rising regional demand. If sustained, that basis improvement is a structural positive for a producer long penalized for its location.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $5.13B, capex $2.29B, producing free cash flow of $2.84B at a 36.7% FCF margin. Q1'26 OCF alone was $3.06B. 2026 capex guidance is $2.65–2.85B, disciplined and largely discretionary given the high operated percentage. The dividend is $0.165/quarter (~1.3% yield). A $2B buyback authorization has $1.4B remaining (expires 12/31/26), but EQT repurchased zero shares in Q1'26, directing cash entirely to debt reduction.

**The deleveraging is the standout.** Total debt fell from $7.86B (12/31/25) to $6.04B (3/31/26) after a ~$1.73B Q1 tender/redemption, executing the stated Debt Retirement Plan. S&P and Fitch responded with upgrades from BBB– to BBB between February and April 2026. Liquidity ratios look thin (current ratio 0.76) but this is normal for E&P. Altman Z of 1.93 (grey zone) reflects the capital-intensive, low-asset-turnover model rather than distress; Piotroski 4/5 signals strengthening fundamentals. ROE ~9.8%, ROA ~5.6%, D/E ~0.61.

One nuance the headline cash flow obscures: the PipeBox midstream JV. Blackstone Credit (BXCI) paid $3.5B for a noncontrolling Class B interest, and EQT must distribute 60% of the JV's available cash to BXCI until a "Base Return" is achieved (~$3.47B remaining as of Q1'26). EQT consolidates the JV, which flatters consolidated cash-flow optics while diverting real cash away from common holders, a structural claim that sits ahead of equity.

**Earnings surprise track record.** EQT has beaten consensus for four straight quarters (Q1'26 +11.0%, Q4'25 +20.0%, Q3'25 +19.8%, Q2'25 +9.7%). The beat pattern supports confidence in near-term realization, but those beats occurred while estimates were broadly being cut; the level matters more than the beat.

## Growth Analysis

The 2025 earnings surge is largely a commodity-price recovery rather than organic volume growth, and the forward growth case is now facing a near-term headwind even as the long-term optionality remains intact.

**Estimate revisions have turned negative.** Current-quarter EPS consensus has fallen to $0.56 from $0.71 ninety days ago (−21.8%) and from $0.58 thirty days ago (−3.6%). Next-quarter estimates are down 15.9% over 90 days. Full-year 2026 is roughly stable (+1.3% over 90 days, −2.4% over 30 days), but next-year 2027 is down 8.9% over 90 days. The pattern is falling near-term momentum with a stable full-year anchor, which is negative earnings momentum rather than the signal that would justify aggressive scoring. The strongest forward indicator in the toolkit is rising estimates, and EQT's are falling at the front of the curve.

**Hedges cap near-term upside.** EQT carries ~850 Bcf of gas hedged through 2030, with short calls at ~$4.94–6.29. This protects the deleveraging plan against a price collapse but limits participation if gas spikes, relevant given the current energy backdrop where a Hormuz failure could send gas and LNG-linked prices materially higher. The hedge book trades volatility protection for upside compression.

**The durable growth drivers are 2028+ optionality:**
- **LNG export**: the 10-year vessel leases (commencing 2028) signal a move to capture the spread between low Appalachian in-basin prices and higher global LNG prices. Real intent, but offtake commitments and economics are not yet booked.
- **Data-center / AI power demand**: US data-center electricity consumption is approaching ~1,050 TWh in 2026, pulling baseload demand into Appalachia and the Gulf with gas as the marginal fuel. EQT lists data-center development among strategic initiatives, but specific supply contracts were not found in filings. This is strategic intent, not revenue.
- **MVP expansions**: Southgate (FERC construction authorization 3/23/2026) and MVP Boost (+0.6 Bcf/d), both targeting mid-2028, expand takeaway. The original MVP's multi-year delays and cost overruns are a cautionary precedent.

Net: genuine multi-year optionality exists, but the near-term growth signal is decelerating, and the long-term catalysts are 2-3 years out.

## Valuation Assessment

At $50.72, EQT trades at ~9.6x trailing P/E and ~10.7x full-year 2026 consensus EPS of $4.73. Against the E&P peer median P/E of 19.2x, that looks cheap on its face. But the discount is partly explained and partly structural.

The most relevant comparison is Expand Energy (EXE) at 6.5x, the other large gas-weighted Appalachian/Haynesville name, which trades at a discount to EQT, confirming that pure gas producers carry lower multiples than the oil-weighted Permian names (PR 22.4x, OVV 19.2x) that anchor the median up. EQT's 9.6x is reasonable for a gas-weighted producer, neither distressed nor obviously mispriced. Its dividend yield (1.3%) is the lowest in the group, reflecting capital directed to debt paydown rather than distributions; defensible but it removes the yield support that makes a cheap energy name attractive.

The valuation discipline that history rewards is skepticism toward "cheap" energy names without an independent catalyst. EQT's low trailing multiple is a function of a commodity-cycle business where trailing earnings reflect a favorable price period that the hedge book and falling forward estimates suggest may not repeat at the same level. The forward multiple (~10.7x) on falling near-term estimates does not screen as asymmetrically cheap. Analyst targets (mean $69.96, median $70, range $57–$83) imply meaningful upside, but those targets have been set against a falling estimate curve and one recent downgrade to the strongSell column; they are stale relative to the deteriorating front-end momentum rather than a forward-looking floor.

The case for a higher valuation score would require the LNG/data-center demand to begin converting to contracted revenue, or a sustained basis improvement, neither of which is yet in the numbers. The case against is a value-trap risk: cheap-looking gas producers stay cheap when the price cycle rolls over. We score valuation slightly below neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

EQT's competitive position is strong and largely stable, with a modestly favorable trajectory on structural demand but a question mark on commodity-cycle timing.

**Moat / cost position.** As the largest US gas producer with vertical integration into the gathering and transmission that serves its own acreage, EQT operates near the lowest cost of supply in Appalachia. The Equitrans merger structurally lowered its all-in cost by internalizing midstream margin. The 30+ year inventory provides durability that most E&Ps lack.

**Structural tailwinds the market may be underpricing.** The data-center load growth thesis is confirmed across the broader energy complex: regulated-utility data shows commercial KWh demand surging, and gas is the primary marginal fuel for new baseload. Appalachian gas is geographically positioned to serve both Mid-Atlantic data-center clusters and (via expanding takeaway) Gulf LNG terminals. If EQT converts even a fraction of this into long-term contracts, it widens the moat.

**Headwinds.** Geographic concentration in Appalachia exposes EQT to in-basin takeaway constraints and (historically) negative basis differentials, though Q1'26's positive basis is encouraging. ~95% of transmission capacity is under fixed "negotiated rate" contracts with no inflation adjustment, which caps midstream margin growth and creates "turnback" risk as contracts expire. Competition exists in both upstream (other Appalachian E&Ps) and midstream (interstate pipelines).

The trajectory is stable-to-improving on demand, offset by a commodity-price cycle that is two-sided. We hold competitive position high but slightly below the prior 7.0-7.5 range given the front-end estimate deceleration.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity-price dependence is the overriding risk**: ~93% gas, with earnings highly levered to NYMEX. The hedge book dampens both downside and upside. A 10% NYMEX decline raises derivative fair value ~$107M, partially offsetting physical exposure. The current macro backdrop is two-sided: a verified Hormuz reopening pulls global gas/LNG-linked prices lower (a headwind capped further by hedges), while a Hormuz failure spikes them (with hedges limiting EQT's participation).

**Securities Class Action.** EQT recorded a $134M loss-contingency charge in 2025 (up from $18M in 2024), a sizable, specific item warranting monitoring.

**Legacy Equitrans liabilities — Rager Mountain.** PHMSA civil penalty settled at $466,550 (April 2026), but a Pennsylvania grand jury returned four criminal misdemeanor charges against Equitrans, L.P. in July 2025. Management deems it financially immaterial, but criminal proceedings carry reputational tail risk.

**Blackstone JV cash claim.** The PipeBox JV distribution obligation to BXCI is a real claim ahead of common holders that consolidated cash-flow optics understate.

**Pipeline/regulatory execution.** MVP's history of delays and cost overruns is a cautionary precedent for Southgate and Boost (both mid-2028). Negotiated-rate transmission contracts (~95% of capacity) carry no inflation adjustment.

**Deleveraging dependent on monetizations and FCF.** The improving credit story relies on continued asset monetizations and sustained free cash flow, which in turn depend on gas prices.

Most of these risks are reasonably priced. The class action and the criminal proceeding are the most clearly idiosyncratic and not fully reflected. We score risk slightly below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly cautious but not alarmed. Near-term ATM IV is 30.4% versus 1-year HV of 32.5% (fair-to-slightly-cheap), and IV has fallen 6.8pp (from 37.2% to 30.4%) since June 1 even as the price dropped 8.1%, indicating resolved near-term uncertainty rather than rising fear. The near-dated put/call skew is modestly elevated at 3.2% (elevated put demand), but skew inverts to negative at the 1-month and 3-month tenors (−6.7% and −4.2%), suggesting hedgers are not pricing a sustained directional decline. The put/call OI ratio rose to 3.25 from 2.47, but the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.56; the OI skew likely reflects standing protective positions rather than fresh bearish flow. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.5%. Net read: the options market is consistent with a stock that has de-rated and is now in a wider, two-sided regime rather than one pricing an imminent break lower. Short interest is low at 3.9% of float (3.2 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$70, in line with analyst mean/median)
Gas prices hold or firm on a Hormuz-driven energy backdrop and rising LNG-linked demand; the positive Q1'26 basis differential proves structural rather than transient; deleveraging continues toward investment-grade-mid and frees cash for buybacks once the Blackstone Base Return is satisfied; and LNG offtake plus data-center supply contracts begin converting strategic intent into booked revenue ahead of the 2028 capacity additions. On normalized ~$5.00 EPS at a 14x multiple (a re-rate toward the gas-peer-plus level as the demand thesis crystallizes), the stock approaches $70. This requires both a favorable price environment and execution on the LNG/data-center conversion.

### Bear Case (~$42, ~17% downside)
A verified Hormuz reopening and softening global demand push gas prices lower; the hedge book limits any offsetting upside; the positive basis reverses to its historical negative; front-end estimate cuts continue; and the LNG/data-center catalysts remain unbooked optionality. At ~$4.00 normalized EPS and a 10-11x multiple appropriate for a gas producer in a soft-price environment, the stock falls toward $42. The Blackstone cash claim and the securities litigation add modest incremental drag.

### Base Case (~$54, ~6% upside)
Gas prices grind in a $100-120 oil-equivalent regime with the most-probable Hormuz outcome a prolonged stalemate rather than resolution or spike; deleveraging continues and the BBB rating holds; full-year 2026 EPS lands near consensus $4.73; and LNG/data-center remain promising but unrealized. At ~10.5-11.5x on $4.73-5.00, fair value sits in the low-to-mid $50s, close to the current price, which is why this is a hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (late July), with the gas-price/Hormuz macro and any LNG offtake or data-center contract announcements as ongoing inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimate revisions turn positive (reversing the −21.8% / −15.9% 90-day declines), signaling the front-end momentum has bottomed.
- A signed LNG offtake agreement or a named data-center supply contract that converts strategic intent into booked revenue.
- A sustained positive Appalachian basis differential (two-plus consecutive quarters above zero), confirming the Q1'26 +$0.38 was structural.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified, held Hormuz reopening that pulls gas/LNG-linked prices structurally lower while EQT's hedges cap any offset, combined with continued front-end estimate cuts.
- Deterioration in the deleveraging path: a ratings outlook revision back to negative, or debt rising rather than falling on weaker FCF.
- Material adverse development in the Securities Class Action or an escalation of the Rager Mountain criminal proceeding beyond management's "immaterial" framing.

## Conclusion

EQT is a high-quality franchise executing a credible balance-sheet repair with genuine long-term optionality in LNG export and data-center demand. Those are real positives, and the four-quarter earnings beat streak and Q1'26's positive basis differential reinforce the operational quality.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are equally concrete. Near-term earnings momentum has turned negative, the opposite of the signal that justifies leaning into a cheap-looking multiple. The forward P/E of ~10.7x is reasonable but not asymmetrically cheap for a gas-weighted producer, the dividend yield is the lowest in its peer group, the hedge book caps participation in any gas spike, and a structural Blackstone cash claim sits ahead of common holders. The entire thesis is reflexively levered to a gas-price cycle tied to an unresolved Hormuz backdrop that is itself two-sided. The trajectory also matters: this is the second consecutive HOLD after a string of BUYs, with growth prospects having declined as the front-end estimate curve weakened, and we are not seeing the fundamental catalyst that would reverse that.

We rate EQT a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The franchise is excellent and the long-term optionality is credible, but neither the current entry price nor the near-term estimate trend offers the asymmetric upside required for a buy. We would upgrade on a positive estimate-revision turn, a booked LNG/data-center contract, or a confirmed structural basis improvement.
