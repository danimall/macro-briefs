**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Range Resources is a pure-play Appalachian natural gas, NGL, and condensate producer with essentially all of its proved reserves in southwestern Pennsylvania's Marcellus. The investment case rests on two pillars: a materially de-risked balance sheet and a liquids-rich reserve mix that differentiates it from dry-gas peers. Both are real. The 2026 earnings surge, however, is almost entirely a function of higher realized gas prices against flat production — Q1 2026 net income more than tripled YoY to $342M and diluted EPS reached $1.44, but volumes rose only 0.3% while all-in realized prices climbed 27-29%. This makes the equity a direct, largely-hedged bet on Henry Hub and Appalachian basis rather than a growth or self-improvement story.

The driver of the strong quarter has reversed. Natural gas prices dropped sharply in early July 2026, analysts cut price targets, and consensus EPS estimates have fallen across every near-term window — current-quarter estimates down 9.5% over 30 days and next-year estimates down 8.8% over 30 days. The stock has retraced roughly 24% from its late-March high of $48.31 to $36.76. Insiders sold into the spring rally at $41-48 and none have bought on the open market since. That combination of falling estimates, negative price momentum, and insiders monetizing near the peak argues against reading the pullback as a clean buying opportunity.

We rate RRC a HOLD. The balance-sheet work supports a floor and the ~$800M projected 2026 free cash flow (third-party estimate) funds a growing dividend and an enlarged $1.5B buyback authorization. But with growth flat, estimates falling, hedges capping upside, and the valuation offering no identifiable mispricing versus a two-sided gas outlook, there is no asymmetric edge here at $36.76. We would become constructive on a durable turn in estimate-revision velocity or a pullback that restores a wider margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Range Resources operates as a single reportable segment, developing a large, low-decline Marcellus position in Pennsylvania. As of year-end 2025, proved reserves were approximately 65% natural gas, 34% NGLs, and 1% oil (condensate) — one of the most liquids-rich portfolios among Appalachian pure-plays. In Q1 2026, natural gas contributed $704M of sales, NGLs $259M, and oil $47M. The liquids exposure gives Range an incremental revenue stream tied to propane, ethane, butane, and condensate pricing, partly diversifying it away from pure Henry Hub exposure, though NGL prices were down YoY in the quarter (Mont Belvieu composite $0.53/gal vs. $0.64).

Production runs at roughly 2.2 Bcfe/day (Q1 2026 averaged 2.207 Bcfe/day), with management targeting a ~2.5 Bcfe/day exit rate by end of 2026 per external coverage. The strategy is returns-focused: fund a multi-decade drilling inventory from operating cash flow, maintain a hedge book and strong liquidity, and return capital through a modest dividend and buybacks. Range achieved and maintained net-zero Scope 1 and 2 emissions in 2024-2025 and ties executive compensation to emissions performance, a genuine ESG differentiator in the sector.

## Financial Analysis

The headline 2026 numbers are strong but require attribution. Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025:

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oil/gas/NGL sales | $791.9M | $1,010.3M | +28% |
| Net income | $97.1M | $341.6M | +252% |
| Diluted EPS | $0.40 | $1.44 | +260% |
| Operating cash flow | $330.1M | $619.1M | +88% |
| All-in realized price | $2.48/mcfe | $3.21/mcfe | +29% |
| NYMEX gas benchmark | $3.66/mcf | $4.97/mcf | +36% |

Production was flat (+0.3%). The entire improvement came from price. Appalachian basis flipped positive — Range realized $0.21 above NYMEX in Q1 2026 versus -$0.05 a year earlier, likely reflecting pull from LNG and demand centers. The concern is symmetry: what price gives, price takes away, and July gas weakness is already flowing into the estimate cuts.

The balance-sheet transformation is the more durable positive. Total debt fell to $834M in Q1 2026 from roughly $1.2B at year-end 2025 and ~$1.7B on average in Q1 2025. In January 2026, Range fully redeemed its $600M 8.25% senior notes due 2029 (at 101.375%, a $12.3M extinguishment loss) using low-cost revolver borrowings, leaving just $500M of 4.75% notes due 2030 plus $334M drawn on the revolver. Interest expense per mcfe fell 33% YoY. Liquidity stands at $1.5B against a $3.0B borrowing base and $2.0B in commitments (revolver amended October 2025, extended to 2030). The retained deficit narrowed from $909M at year-end 2025 to $592M in Q1 2026. The screening Altman Z of ~2.02 (grey zone) understates true resilience because it does not capture the January refinancing; for a capital-intensive E&P with $6.76B of net oil and gas properties, this is not a distress signal.

Cash flow quality is high. Q1 2026 OCF of $619M comfortably covered $167.7M of capex, $23.8M of dividends (raised 11% to $0.10/quarter), and $27.1M of buybacks (800K shares at $33.91). The board raised the buyback authorization to $1.5B in February 2026. One drag deserves emphasis: Range paid $49.3M in net cash derivative settlements in Q1 as gas rallied above its ~$4.06 swap prices, and it sold call swaptions that cap further upside. Hedging protects the downside but has become a headwind at current gas levels.

FCF conversion looks healthy on the third-party ~$800M 2026 estimate, but that figure originates from analyst coverage, not a Range filing, and is sensitive to the gas price path that has already softened.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the case is weakest. Underlying growth is minimal: production up 0.3% YoY, with the exit-rate target of 2.5 Bcfe/day implying modest volume gains funded by ~$167M/quarter of maintenance-plus capex. The 2026 earnings improvement is a price event, not a volume or structural-margin one, which matters for how the estimate-revision signal should be read.

The estimate-revision data is negative:

Estimates are falling across every window, and for the current quarter and next year the 30-day cuts are large relative to the 90-day moves, indicating the deterioration is not stabilizing. Growth prospects is the single most reliable winner-selection dimension in our framework, and here it points the wrong way. The earnings-beat track record is clean (four straight beats, including 19.1% in Q1 2026), which lends some confidence to near-term delivery, but management beating a falling bar is a weaker signal than beating a rising one.

Structural demand tailwinds are real: LNG export expansion and power demand from data centers and electrification support Appalachian gas over a multi-year horizon, and supply discipline (constrained takeaway, moderated reinvestment, inventory exhaustion in some basins) is constructive for prices. But those are industry-level, multi-year forces already broadly known. On a 12-month view, the driver that matters most for RRC's earnings is the near-term gas price, which is retreating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $36.76 the stock trades at ~9.7x trailing earnings and roughly 9.1x the current-year consensus of $4.02. That looks cheap in isolation, and the peer table shows a wide dispersion: SM Energy at 32.5x, Antero at 11.1x, Matador at 13.8x, Murphy at 65.8x, Chord negative. The nearest comparable, Antero (also a liquids-rich Appalachian name), trades at 11.1x, so RRC sits at a modest discount to its cleanest peer.

The problem is that a low multiple here reflects a two-sided commodity outlook and falling estimates, not an identifiable mispricing. Our framework treats a cheap valuation as a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst, and cheapness has been the most reliably backwards-sorting dimension. For RRC the specific catalyst that would unlock the discount — a sustained gas-price breakout — is precisely what the July decline and the estimate cuts argue against, and even if it arrived, more than 35% of remaining 2026 gas is hedged at ~$4.06 with call swaptions sold on top, so a large slice of the upside is contractually capped. This is not a cyclical-trough setup where trailing multiples understate normalized earnings power; if anything, the trailing quarter captured a price peak.

Analyst targets bracket the debate: mean $45.36, median $44.00, low $36.00, high $57.00. Because the estimates behind those targets are falling, we treat the mean less as a floor than as a level that will likely be revised down toward the current price if gas weakness persists. We do not extend "analyst targets are floors" treatment here; that principle applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates, and RRC's estimates are moving the other way.

Fair value on ~$4.02 current-year EPS at 10x (a small premium to Antero for the de-risked balance sheet and liquids mix) is roughly $40, modest upside from $36.76, but predicated on estimates holding, which they are not currently doing. The valuation offers a floor, not asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Range is one of the four largest U.S. pure-play gas producers, competing with EQT, Antero, and Expand Energy. Its differentiators are genuine: a large contiguous Marcellus position, a low unit-cost structure, a liquids-rich mix that provides NGL optionality, and a de-risked balance sheet that is now among the least levered in Appalachia. The net-zero Scope 1 and 2 achievement is a real ESG edge, reinforced by Pennsylvania's November 2025 exit from RGGI, which removes a cap-and-trade overhang and supports in-state gas-fired power demand.

Competitive trajectory is best described as stable rather than improving. Range is not visibly gaining share — production is flat — and its liquids advantage was a headwind in Q1 as NGL prices fell YoY. The positive basis differential is a favorable signal but is more a function of regional demand pull than a durable structural moat Range controls. The balance-sheet improvement genuinely strengthens its relative position within the Appalachian cohort, which supports a mid-range competitive score, but there is no evidence of moat widening or share capture that the market has not priced.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-basin concentration.** Essentially 100% of reserves are in Pennsylvania, concentrating exposure to PA-specific regulation, takeaway capacity, and processing. A disruption at a key third-party processing facility could force curtailment.

**Pennsylvania regulatory overhang.** Cecil Township adopted a 2,500-ft setback ordinance (from 500 ft) in November 2024, and a citizen petition to expand setbacks statewide was accepted for review by the PA Environmental Quality Board on December 9, 2025. Range calls it unlawful but is monitoring. Severance-tax proposals recur, and a fracking ban is a low-probability existential tail given the 100% Marcellus footprint. The RGGI exit is a partial offset.

**Hedging caps upside.** Beyond the 2026 hedges noted above, 270,000 Mmbtu/d is swapped at $4.05 for 2027. Investors betting on a gas breakout should note that much of the upside is hedged away, while downside is protected.

**Legacy divestiture liability.** The 2020 North Louisiana divestiture left Range with a ~$260M gathering/transport obligation ($69M current, $190M long-term) running through 2030, accreting ~$7M/quarter with no offsetting revenue.

**NGL/ethane takeaway.** Finite local ethane demand and infrastructure could force curtailment or ethane blending into residue gas if sales agreements lapse.

**Estimate momentum.** The most immediate risk is that falling consensus estimates continue if gas stays soft, pulling reported earnings and cash-return capacity down and validating the market's caution.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.8% sits rich to 1-year historical vol of 32.3% (70th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (38.8% near-term vs. 32.7% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress consistent with the next earnings report and continued gas-price sensitivity. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.2% near-term, steepening to -4.7% at three months), and put/call volume and OI ratios are very low, so there is no aggressive directional put positioning. The implied one-month move is ±9.2%. The rich near-term IV suggests option premium is expensive for buyers here and that the market expects a two-sided catalyst rather than a directional collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Gas prices firm on LNG export growth, power demand, and supply discipline; Appalachian basis stays positive. Range delivers ~$800M of 2026 FCF, funds a growing dividend and the $1.5B buyback (retiring shares at a low-9x multiple), and the de-risked balance sheet re-rates the equity toward the peer mean. Estimate revisions turn positive as gas stabilizes, and analysts revise targets up toward $45-57. Bull target ~$52-57.

**Bear case.** Gas weakness persists through H2 2026, estimates continue falling, and the hedge book prevents Range from capturing any relief rally while providing only partial downside protection. The July decline extends as the winter demand premium unwinds. PA setback rulemaking advances, adding a regulatory discount. Bear target ~$30-32, roughly 13-18% below current.

**Base case.** Gas chops in a range, estimates settle after the current round of cuts, and Range holds near current levels supported by buybacks and its balance sheet while lacking a catalyst to re-rate. Base target ~$40, ~9% upside, contingent on estimates stabilizing rather than continuing lower.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (uncertain). The balance-sheet de-risking and capital-return capacity support the position, but flat growth, falling estimates, capped upside, and insiders selling near the highs remove any near-term edge, and the driver of the recent strong quarter has reversed.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive months, signaling gas-price and cash-flow momentum has re-established.
- The stock pulls back toward the low-$30s (near the 52-week low of $32.60), restoring a margin of safety with the balance sheet intact.
- Evidence of an unhedged or lightly-hedged 2027-2028 volume ramp that would let Range capture a sustained gas move, converting the flat-production profile into genuine per-share growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The PA statewide setback rulemaking advances toward adoption in a form that would materially constrain core SW Pennsylvania well siting.
- Estimate cuts accelerate further (next-year revisions worse than -15% over 90 days) alongside a gas price sustained below hedge floors, pressuring FCF and the buyback pace.
- Cancellation or slowdown of the buyback program, or a dividend that stops growing, signaling deteriorating cash generation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings report and the accompanying updated 2026 guidance/hedge disclosure, plus the winter gas strip as it forms in Q4 2026. The PA Environmental Quality Board's handling of the setback petition is an ongoing monitoring item.

## Conclusion

Range Resources has done the hard work on its balance sheet — total debt cut to $834M, the 8.25% notes retired, $1.5B of liquidity, and only $500M of low-coupon fixed debt remaining. That transformation lowers distress risk and underpins a credible floor near current levels, supported by a growing dividend and an enlarged buyback. The liquids-rich mix and net-zero emissions profile are genuine differentiators within the Appalachian cohort.

But the equity is a largely-hedged bet on natural gas prices, and that bet is going the wrong way right now. The 2026 earnings surge was a price event on flat production; estimates are falling across every window; the stock has retraced 24% from its high; and insiders monetized into the spring rally with no offsetting open-market buying. At $36.76 the ~9.7x multiple is cheap in isolation but reflects a two-sided commodity outlook rather than an identifiable mispricing, and the hedge book caps the upside that a bull case would need. We hold, and we want to see estimate revisions turn or a deeper pullback before adding, and we would step aside if regulatory or cash-flow deterioration confirms the market's caution.
