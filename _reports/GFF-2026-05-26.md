## Executive Summary

Griffon Corporation is executing a strategic transformation from a diversified industrial conglomerate into a focused North American building products business centered on Clopay, the #1 garage door manufacturer in North America. The company is divesting its underperforming Consumer and Professional Products segment (AMES), which removes tariff exposure and simplifies the business to a high-margin, capital-light operation generating ~$458M in EBITDA on $1.8B of revenue (25%+ margins).

The investment case hinges on two dynamics: (1) a dominant competitive position with 40+ year exclusive retailer relationships, 31% segment EBITDA margins, and a 57-location distribution network that creates meaningful barriers to entry, and (2) aggressive capital return — 20% of shares retired since April 2023 at an average price of $53.21, well below the current $86.19. However, flat-to-declining residential volume, a $975M Senior Notes refinancing due 2028, and an 88x trailing P/E (distorted by a $244M Hunter Fan impairment) create near-term headwinds that limit immediate re-rating potential.

At ~11.4x EV/EBITDA on guided continuing operations, the stock is fairly valued relative to building products peers (median ~17.5x P/E suggests a different capital structure dynamic). The path to material upside requires housing volume recovery — which management acknowledges remains "soft" — or a credit-friendly refinancing outcome. The 7 Wall Street analysts covering the name are unanimously bullish (median target $115), providing a defined upside framework.

## Company Overview

Griffon Corporation operates through two primary businesses in its continuing operations:

**Clopay Corporation (Core Asset):** The #1 manufacturer of residential and commercial garage doors and rolling steel doors in North America. Operates 57 distribution centers with ~3,100 employees. Holds exclusive 40+ year relationships with Home Depot and 30+ year relationships with Menards. Residential garage doors account for approximately 85% of demand from repair/replacement activity, providing relative insulation from new construction cycles. FY2025 segment revenue was $1.58B with 31.2% adjusted EBITDA margins.

**Hunter Fan (Retained, Minor):** Commercial and residential ceiling fan business acquired in January 2022. Suffered $244M in goodwill/trademark impairment in FY2025 due to weak consumer demand and tariff disruption. Being integrated with Clopay's commercial distribution network for cross-selling potential. Generates limited revenue relative to Clopay.

**AMES (Divesting):** U.S. and Canadian operations being transferred to a JV with ONCAP (expected to close June 2026), generating $100M cash + $161M in 10% PIK notes. Australian operations under strategic review. UK operations being exited.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**

Consolidated revenue has declined from $2.85B (FY2022) to $2.52B (FY2025), driven entirely by CPP weakness. The core HBP segment has been stable at ~$1.59B for three consecutive years. FY2026 continuing operations guidance of $1.8B and $458M adjusted EBITDA implies a 25.4% margin profile.

Adjusted EPS has grown steadily: $4.54 (FY2023) → $5.12 (FY2024) → $5.65 (FY2025), driven by margin expansion and share count reduction offsetting flat revenue. The trailing GAAP EPS of $1.09 is distorted by the one-time $244M Hunter Fan impairment and should not be used for valuation purposes.

Q2 FY2026 (reported May 7, 2026): Revenue $422M (down 1% YoY), adjusted EBITDA $98M (23.2% margin, down 4% YoY), adjusted EPS $1.05 (beat consensus of $1.00 by 5.3%). Residential volume was down 6%, partially offset by the mid-single digit price increase implemented in late March 2026.

**Cash Flow Generation:**

FY2025 operating cash flow was $357M with free cash flow of $305M (12.1% FCF margin). Capex is minimal at ~$50M annually (~2.8% of revenue), making this a capital-light business. FCF conversion significantly exceeds net income. FY2026 guidance reiterates FCF > net income.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt of $1.42B ($975M Senior Notes at 5.75% due 2028; $449M Term Loan B due 2029). Net debt/EBITDA is 2.4x — appropriate leverage for a business with this cash flow stability. Stockholders' equity of only $74M (driven by $611M in buybacks since April 2023, not by losses) creates an optically alarming D/E ratio that is misleading. Available liquidity: $486M undrawn revolver + cash on hand.

**Capital Return:**

Since April 2023: $611M in repurchases (11.5M shares at average $53.21), reducing the share count by 20%. $247M remaining in authorization. Dividend recently raised to $0.22/quarter ($0.88 annually, ~1.0% yield). CEO has explicitly stated buybacks remain the priority over M&A.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are constrained in the near term but supported by structural factors over 2-3 years:

**Near-term headwinds:**
- Residential volume down 6% in Q2 FY2026; management expects continued softness
- Elevated mortgage rates suppressing existing home sales (lock-in effect)
- Estimate revisions slightly negative: current year EPS consensus down 0.7% over 90 days

**Medium-term catalysts:**
- Price realization from March 2026 mid-single digit increase (not yet fully annualized)
- Aging U.S. housing stock driving replacement demand
- Product innovation (VertiStack, C-Power technology — two consecutive "Best of IBS" awards)
- Commercial rolling steel growth for data centers and mission-critical infrastructure
- Post-AMES simplification may attract multiple expansion as market reclassifies GFF
- Share count reduction continues ($247M remaining authorization)

**Quantified growth path:** FY2026 consensus EPS of $5.22, FY2027 consensus of $5.73 implies ~10% growth, driven primarily by continued buybacks (~$200M annually at current prices removes ~4-5% of shares) plus modest operating improvement. Revenue growth requires volume recovery which is mortgage-rate dependent.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current valuation on continuing operations:**
- Market cap: $4.0B
- Enterprise value (market cap + net debt - JV cash proceeds): ~$5.2B
- EV/EBITDA on $458M guided: 11.4x
- Forward P/E on $5.22 FY2026 adjusted EPS: 16.5x
- Forward P/E on $5.73 FY2027 adjusted EPS: 15.0x
- FCF yield on ~$300M normalized FCF: 7.5%

**Peer comparison:**
- Building products peer median P/E: 17.5x
- TREX (composite decking, more cyclical): 21.6x
- FBIN (diversified building products): 16.1x
- UFPI (wood/composites): 17.5x

GFF's 16.5x forward P/E is roughly in line with peers, which is appropriate given its superior margins (31% vs. peers typically 15-20%) but offset by higher leverage and flat near-term volume. The FCF yield of 7.5% provides downside support.

**Why this prevents a BUY:** The analyst consensus target of $115 (median) implies 34% upside, which is attractive. However, the specific concerns preventing a BUY recommendation are: (1) negative estimate revision momentum (current year and next year estimates drifting lower), (2) residential volume declining 6% with no visibility on recovery timing (mortgage rates remain elevated and the housing market is rate-sensitive), and (3) the $975M refinancing cliff in 2028 which, in the current environment, could result in significantly higher interest expense ($975M at ~7% vs. current 5.75% = ~$12M additional annual interest = $0.20+ EPS headwind). These three factors — declining estimates, volume uncertainty, and refinancing risk — create a concrete barrier to higher conviction. The stock needs either estimate revisions to inflect positive (driven by volume recovery) or clarity on refinancing terms before the risk/reward supports a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Griffon's competitive position is exceptional and its strongest attribute:

**Market leadership:** Clopay is the #1 garage door manufacturer in North America across both residential and commercial segments. The combination of scale, distribution (57 centers), and exclusive retailer relationships creates a durable moat.

**Exclusive relationships:** 40+ year exclusive supplier to Home Depot; 30+ year exclusive relationship with Menards. These relationships are contractual and deeply embedded in retailer operations (inventory systems, installer networks, warranty fulfillment). Switching costs for retailers are high.

**Distribution advantage:** The 57-location network enables just-in-time delivery for a product (garage doors) that is bulky, fragile, and customized. Replicating this network would require hundreds of millions in capital and years of execution.

**Technology differentiation:** VertiStack (vertical-stacking garage door for ADUs/small lots), C-Power (electrical power through door panels), and the MyClopay digital platform for dealers represent genuine innovation in a traditionally staid category. Two consecutive "Best of IBS" awards confirm industry recognition.

**Repair/remodel focus:** 85% of garage door demand is replacement/repair, not new construction. This creates more predictable, less cyclical demand compared to peers more exposed to housing starts.

**Competitive trajectory:** Improving. Product innovation accelerating, pricing power demonstrated (mid-single digit increase implemented without volume loss beyond macro trend), commercial segment growing.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Refinancing Risk (Material, Timed):** $975M Senior Notes due 2028. If not refinanced by Dec 1, 2027, the revolver maturity accelerates to that date. In an environment where long-end yields remain elevated, refinancing at meaningfully higher rates is probable. At 7% (vs. current 5.75%), annual interest expense rises ~$12M, reducing EPS by $0.20+. Timeline: must be resolved by late 2027.

**2. Housing Volume Uncertainty:** Recovery requires mortgage rate decline (each 50bp decline in mortgage rates increases existing home sale activity). Current rate environment (7%+ mortgages) is hostile. No visibility on when this resolves.

**3. Hunter Fan Residual Risk:** $244M impairment already taken, but remaining tariff exposure on China-sourced product persists. Hunter is small relative to the whole but continues to drag. If China tariffs escalate further, additional writedowns are possible.

**4. Customer Concentration:** Home Depot represents 10% of consolidated revenue. Loss of the exclusive relationship (low probability given 40-year history, but non-zero) would be catastrophic.

**5. Steel Cost Inflation:** Primary raw material. 4-5 month lag between purchase and realization in COGS. Pricing power has historically offset this, but in a weak volume environment, passing through costs is harder.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110-115, +30-35%):**
- Housing volume recovers in H2 2026 / early 2027 as mortgage rates moderate
- AMES divestiture closes cleanly in June 2026; market reclassifies GFF as pure-play building products → multiple expansion to 13-14x EV/EBITDA
- Continued buybacks ($200M+ annually) at current prices reduce share count by another 5%+
- Commercial garage door growth accelerates (data center, infrastructure demand)
- Refinancing completed at favorable terms given strong cash flow coverage
- Price increases fully annualized without volume destruction

**Bear Case ($65-70, -20-25%):**
- Housing recession deepens; residential volume declines 10%+ as consumer confidence collapses
- Refinancing in 2027 at significantly higher rates (8%+) compresses earnings power
- Tariff escalation forces Hunter Fan write-off and disrupts Clopay's supply chain (steel)
- Home Depot relationship deteriorates or shifts to multi-source strategy
- Consumer spending cliff (H2 2026 per macro environment) reduces discretionary renovation spending

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Current quarter EPS estimate revisions inflect positive (+5% or more over 30 days), indicating volume recovery is beginning
2. Mortgage rates decline below 6.5%, creating clear housing activity catalyst
3. Refinancing of Senior Notes completed at ≤6.5%, removing the 2028 cliff risk and confirming earnings power durability

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Residential volume declines exceed 10% for two consecutive quarters, indicating structural demand impairment
2. Refinancing terms come in above 8%, compressing FY2028 EPS below $5.00
3. Home Depot exclusive relationship announcement changes (contract modification, multi-sourcing)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (August 2026 for Q3 FY2026 — seasonally strongest quarter). Volume trends and AMES JV closure confirmation will provide key data. Refinancing activity should become visible by late 2026/early 2027.

## Conclusion

Griffon Corporation is a high-quality business undergoing a value-creating transformation. The Clopay franchise — with 31%+ EBITDA margins, dominant market share, exclusive 40-year retailer relationships, and capital-light economics — is a rare asset in building products. Management has demonstrated exceptional capital allocation (474% 5-year TSR vs. 217% peer group) through aggressive, well-timed buybacks.

At $86.19, the stock trades at 16.5x forward earnings and 11.4x EV/EBITDA — fair for the quality of the business but not cheap enough to compensate for current headwinds: declining volume, negative estimate momentum, and a refinancing cliff approaching in a hostile rate environment. The unanimous analyst consensus (7/7 Buy, median target $115) provides a defined upside framework, but the catalyst for re-rating (volume recovery or refinancing clarity) has no specific timeline.

HOLD with a growth orientation. The business is improving structurally (simplification, innovation, capital return), but the stock requires either macro cooperation (lower rates → housing recovery) or a company-specific catalyst (refinancing completion) to justify upgrading to BUY. Current position holders should maintain exposure given the 7.5% FCF yield floor and ongoing buyback support; new entrants should wait for either an estimate inflection or a pullback toward the $75-78 range (where the buyback program provides a soft floor).
