**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PVH is a two-brand apparel company — Tommy Hilfiger and Calvin Klein generate more than 95% of revenue — trading at roughly 6.2x reaffirmed FY26 EPS guidance ($11.80–$12.10) and approximately 6% free-cash-flow yield. On a pure screen, the stock looks like deep value: $3.4B market cap against $538M of FY25 FCF, investment-grade credit (BBB stable), and a fundamentally sound balance sheet whose headline "distress" signals are artifacts of a non-cash $480M goodwill/intangible impairment taken in FY25. The earnings collapse from $598M to $25M of net income was almost entirely accounting, not operations.

The problem is that cheapness without a catalyst is a recurring trap in apparel, and PVH's catalysts are running the wrong way. Constant-currency revenue has been flat-to-declining for three years; the FY25 reported growth and the Q1 FY26 "beat" were both FX-flattered (Q1 constant-currency revenue was −2%). On June 3 the company beat on EPS but cut its full-year FY26 revenue outlook to roughly flat, citing the Middle East/Iran conflict's drag on EMEA, which is its profit engine. The stock fell ~23% on the print, Evercore downgraded it, and forward-year EPS estimates have been revised down 7–9% over 90 days. The valuation discount is genuine, but the business is not growing organically, and the consensus is no longer extrapolating margin recovery into next year.

Layered on top is an asymmetric tail: PVH sits on China's Unreliable Entity List over alleged Xinjiang-related conduct. China is only ~6% of revenue but ~20% of pre-impairment profit, so an imposed measure would hit earnings far harder than the revenue line suggests. Combined with an interim CFO during a multi-year SAP migration, a guidance cut, and falling forward estimates, the setup is a genuinely cheap stock with too many uncatalyzed overhangs to upgrade. We maintain HOLD.

## Company Overview

PVH is a 140-year-old global apparel company that has consolidated into a two-brand pure-play. Tommy Hilfiger and Calvin Klein, both fully owned, account for more than 95% of revenue, with a small residual Heritage Brands business licensing Van Heusen and Nike-branded categories. The company divested its Warner's/Olga/True&Co. intimates business in late 2023, completing a multi-year simplification.

The profit base is Europe-centric. More than 70% of revenue is generated outside the United States, mostly in euros. In FY25 PVH reorganized into four region-based segments: EMEA (largest and most profitable, $749M IBIT at a 17.5% margin), APAC (highest margin at 19.3%), Americas (lowest margin at 9.2%, and the most tariff-exposed), and Licensing (tiny revenue but roughly $357M of near-pure-profit IBIT). The company runs about 1,350 freestanding stores plus roughly 1,450 concessions, with wholesale and digital channels; digital is roughly 20% of revenue. Strategy is governed by the multi-year "PVH+ Plan," with brand-building and operating-model simplification as its pillars. Stefan Larsson is CEO.

The competitive position rests on global brand equity in Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger, which confers pricing power and a high-margin licensing annuity. That equity is also partly tied to the personal reputations of Tommy Hilfiger (still Principal Designer) and Calvin Klein, formalized as a key-man risk factor and underscored by a one-time $51M payment in FY24 to amend Mr. Hilfiger's employment agreement.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY25 (year ended Feb 1, 2026) revenue was $8,950M, up 3% reported, but $251M of that was FX translation — roughly flat in constant currency. FY24 was $8,653M and FY23 was $9,218M. The mechanism matters: a weak dollar translated euro revenue into more dollars, and the G-III license takeback brought previously licensed categories in-house. Strip both out and the business has been flat-to-declining in constant currency for three years.

**The earnings distortion.** Operating income (IBIT) fell to $231M (2.6% margin) from $772M (8.9%), and net income dropped to $25.3M from $598.5M. Roughly 98% of that collapse is a non-cash $480M goodwill/intangible impairment in Q1 FY25 ($426M of goodwill writing off the Americas and APAC reporting units entirely, plus $54M of Australia license rights) and $93M of restructuring severance. The impairment was driven by higher discount rates, not deteriorating operations. The 83.3% effective tax rate is an artifact, since the impairment was non-deductible. Underlying adjusted profitability held roughly stable.

**Margins.** Gross margin fell 190bps to 57.5%: roughly 80bps from tariffs, 50bps from the lower-margin wholesale mix in the G-III takeback, and 60bps from promotional pressure, freight, and CK delivery delays.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $680M and FCF was $538M (6% margin); cash generation far exceeds GAAP net income, which is the cleaner read on the business. Cash was $701M, long-term debt $2,291M, equity $4,792M, D/E 2.44, with roughly 80% fixed-rate debt. Goodwill and intangibles total $5.1B, 44% of assets, mostly euro-denominated, which keeps further impairment risk live if discount rates rise or the stock stays depressed.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.78 ("distress zone") is a false positive, depressed by the asset-heavy, goodwill-heavy balance sheet and the one-time impairment crushing the EBIT/assets term, not by insolvency risk. PVH is covenant-compliant, investment-grade (S&P upgraded to BBB stable per the Q1 10-Q), and FCF-positive. The Piotroski signals (positive net income, strong OCF, OCF above net income, current ratio 1.52) point to a sound if low-margin operation.

**Q1 FY26 (ended May 3, 2026).** A clean quarter on the surface: revenue $2,025M (+2% reported, −2% constant currency), net income $88M, EPS $1.90, IBIT $124M (6.1%). DTC grew 6% (digital +11%); wholesale was flat reported, down ex-FX in all regions. APAC was strong (+10%, helped by Lunar New Year timing) and EMEA was weak ex-FX (Middle East/Turkey). The headline beat was FX-assisted, and the underlying volume trend is negative.

**Earnings track record.** The company has beaten consensus four straight quarters, including a 24.4% surprise in the September 2025 quarter and 14.5% in March 2026. That history supports confidence that management can hit the reaffirmed FY26 EPS range. It does not address the top-line stagnation, since several beats were cost- and FX-driven rather than demand-driven.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks are the central return story, and they are aggressive. FY25 saw $561M repurchased, including a $500M accelerated share repurchase at an average $72.44, partly debt-funded, in the same year earnings cratered and the company took impairments. Share count fell from roughly 51M to roughly 46M. FY26 guidance is at least $300M of buybacks with capex rising sharply to ~$250M for the SAP S/4 build-out. The dividend is negligible (~$7M annually, 0.2% yield). At today's $74, the ASR is roughly break-even; the strategy magnifies both per-share upside and balance-sheet risk.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is the missing ingredient. Constant-currency revenue has stalled for three years and turned slightly negative in Q1 FY26. On June 3, management cut the FY26 revenue outlook to roughly flat (from "increase slightly"), explicitly citing the Middle East/Iran conflict's drag on EMEA consumer traffic in Turkey and broader European wholesale demand. That guidance cut is the decisive data point: while many apparel peers reported solid growth and raised outlooks this spring, PVH lowered its top-line view, making this a partly company- and region-specific weakness rather than a pure sector phenomenon.

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration where it matters. The current-quarter estimate jumped +23.9% over 30 days to $3.07, but next-quarter is down 11.8% over 30 days, current-year is roughly flat (+1.4% over 90 days), and next-year EPS has been revised down 8.6% over 30 days and 7.6% over 90 days to $12.53. The forward-looking estimate trend is negative — a cost-and-FX-supported earnings base with a softening demand backdrop, not the +30% revision pattern that signals a buy.

The growth that remains is margin-driven, not volume-driven: cost savings from operating-model simplification, expected IEEPA tariff refunds (PVH received $27M post-Q1, with ~$100M anticipated), and a transactional FX tailwind. Management guides FY26 gross margin up ~100bps net of tariffs. That can support the reaffirmed EPS range, but it is a quality-of-earnings story, not an expansion story. The reorganization, PVH+ Plan, and SAP migration are multi-year self-help levers rather than near-term growth catalysts.

## Valuation Assessment

At $74.21, PVH trades at roughly 6.2x the midpoint of reaffirmed FY26 EPS guidance and ~6% FCF yield. That is a deep discount to the apparel peer set: Columbia 21.2x, Kontoor 16.2x, Carter's 16.7x, G-III 12.5x, with a peer median P/E of 16.7x. Even Capri, a struggling comparable, trades at 32x. PVH sits at roughly 0.4x the peer median multiple.

The discount is partly justified and partly excessive, which is why the valuation score lands mid-range rather than high. Justifications: no organic growth, a guidance cut peers did not match, the China tail risk, elevated leverage (D/E 2.44 with goodwill at 44% of assets), and falling forward-year estimates. The excess: at ~6x forward earnings with $538M of FCF and investment-grade credit, the market is pricing a degree of structural impairment that the cash flows do not yet support.

The discipline here is to treat a cheap multiple as a warning, not a signal, absent an independent catalyst. Cheapness in apparel names has historically marked value traps more often than entry points, and PVH has no clear near-term catalyst to close the gap: the next earnings print is several months out, the China overhang is binary and unscheduled, and forward estimates are declining rather than rising. The trailing P/E of 21.9x reflects the impairment-distorted trailing EPS and is meaningless; the forward multiple on reaffirmed guidance is the right lens, and at 6x it is genuinely cheap but uncatalyzed.

Analyst targets span $70 (low) to $128 (high), with a $92 mean and $88.5 median, implying meaningful upside to consensus. But the consensus is itself in flux (Evercore downgraded to In Line on June 4, characterizing the quarter as a low-quality FX-driven beat), and the mean target predates full digestion of the guidance cut. We weight the median ($88.5) as a more reliable anchor than the high outlier. On a normalized basis, mid-cycle margin recovery toward an 8–9% operating margin on ~$9B revenue would support EPS in the $13–14 range and a markedly higher fair value, but that recovery requires the EMEA demand drag to lift and constant-currency revenue to inflect, neither of which is visible today.

## Competitive Landscape

The premium/affordable-luxury apparel sector is contending with soft North American demand, tariff-driven cost inflation, FX volatility, and now Middle East-driven European softness. PVH's relative strength is two globally scaled owned brands and a high-margin licensing annuity, with Europe and APAC as profit anchors. EMEA's 17.5% segment margin and APAC's 19.3% are genuinely strong; the Americas, at 9.2% and tariff-exposed, is the weakest region.

The competitive trajectory is the concern. PVH competes in a low-barrier, intensely promotional market against larger luxury houses, fast fashion, and digitally native brands. Its core challenge is reigniting constant-currency growth, which has stalled while peers generally grew and raised guidance this spring. Brand equity remains a real moat, but the moat is not translating into share gains, and the FY26 guidance cut suggests the franchise is losing relative momentum even where the brands remain desirable. Customer concentration is creeping up — top-5 wholesale customers reached 16.6% of revenue versus 13.3% in FY23 — a modest erosion of bargaining position. Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating: a strong brand portfolio that is not currently winning the growth contest.

## Risk Assessment

**China Unreliable Entity List (the defining idiosyncratic risk).** PVH was investigated in September 2024 over alleged discriminatory conduct toward Xinjiang-sourced goods and placed on the UEL in February 2025. No measures have been imposed, but potential outcomes include fines, import/export bans, investment bans, and personnel entry denial. The profit exposure is disproportionate to the ~6% revenue share. A forced exit would trigger inventory and receivable charges plus further impairments. This is binary, unscheduled, and largely outside management's control, the single biggest reason risk scores low.

**Tariffs.** FY25 gross hit was ~$69M (~80bps); FY26 is guided to ~$195M (~215bps), partly offset by mitigation and ~$100M of expected IEEPA refunds following the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unconstitutional. New Section 301 tariffs are expected in Q2/Q3 2026. Net FY26 gross margin is still guided up ~100bps, a manageable but volatile headwind.

**Middle East/Iran conflict.** The direct cause of the FY26 revenue guidance cut, depressing traffic in Turkey and broader EMEA wholesale. Direct Middle East ex-Turkey is ~1% of revenue but ~7% of IBIT; the larger risk is European spillover, since EMEA is the profit engine.

**FX translation.** More than 70% of revenue (mostly EUR) is translated, and euro-denominated goodwill/intangibles (46% of the $5.1B total) swing OCI and impairment-test inputs. The company hedges via €1.125B notes and cross-currency swaps designated as net-investment hedges.

**Other.** Goodwill/intangible re-impairment risk on $5.1B (44% of assets); G-III license takeback execution (~50bps annual gross-margin drag plus integration risk); a multi-year SAP S/4 migration following a just-remediated material weakness in EMEA ERP user-access controls; and 2027 refinancing of €600M notes plus a €408M term loan.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not confirming a near-term directional thesis in either direction. ATM IV has collapsed from 69.3% to 43.3% since June 1 (price down 20.4% over the same window), and near-term IV (43.3%) sits below 1-year historical volatility (48.1%): options are modestly cheap, and the term structure is in contango. Put/call skew is balanced at −1.2%, so there is no pronounced downside hedging premium. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±8.5%. The one notable shift is the put/call OI ratio rising from 0.50 to 1.39 as the stock fell, indicating more put positioning post-guidance-cut, but balanced skew suggests this is hedging rather than aggressive bearish conviction. Combined with 13.1% short interest and 4.6 days to cover, positioning is bearish-leaning but not extreme. Cheap IV would favor entry timing for a bullish view, but the fundamental case does not yet justify one.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($95–110)
The earnings "collapse" is an accounting illusion, and the market is mispricing a sound, FCF-generative business at ~6x forward earnings. If the Middle East drag lifts, EMEA demand stabilizes, tariff refunds flow ($100M expected), the FX transactional tailwind holds, and cost savings deliver the guided ~100bps of gross-margin expansion, FY26 EPS lands at the reaffirmed range and the stock re-rates toward the peer median. Even a partial re-rating to 8–9x on $12 EPS implies $96–108. The four-quarter beat streak and aggressive buybacks (shrinking the share count below 46M) amplify per-share earnings. If the China UEL listing resolves without imposed measures, the largest overhang clears and the discount narrows. The $88.5 median analyst target sits ~19% above current price.

### Bear Case ($55–62)
The cheapness is justified, not excessive. Constant-currency revenue keeps shrinking, the EMEA/Middle East drag persists or worsens, and forward-year estimates continue falling. The debt-funded buyback at $72.44 looks pro-cyclical and risky if a recessionary consumer cliff (a live H2 2026 scenario) hits discretionary apparel. The China UEL converts to imposed measures, taking out ~20% of profit and triggering further impairments on the $5.1B goodwill/intangible base. With an interim CFO during a multi-year SAP migration and a just-remediated material weakness, execution risk compounds. The stock de-rates toward its 52-week low of $59.60 or below, a 16–25% decline from current levels.

The asymmetry is closer to balanced than the headline multiple suggests, because the bull case depends on a demand inflection that is not visible and a binary China resolution that is unscheduled, while the bear case has multiple live triggers.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an `uncertain` rationale: the valuation is genuinely cheap, but there is insufficient evidence of a near-term catalyst to upgrade to BUY, and the cash-generative, investment-grade balance sheet argues against an AVOID. The score trajectory has drifted modestly (5.4 → 5.9 → 5.8 → 5.6), reflecting improving growth and risk perceptions earlier in the year that the June guidance cut has now partly reversed. The fourth consecutive HOLD is warranted given the unresolved China tail and falling forward estimates; we do not see enough to justify a stronger call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Constant-currency revenue inflects positive for two consecutive quarters, or management raises the FY26 revenue outlook back above flat.
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (currently −7.6% over 90 days); a return to rising forward estimates at a 6x multiple would be a compelling combination.
- The China UEL investigation resolves without imposed measures, removing the largest binary overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- China imposes concrete UEL measures (fines, import/export or investment bans), threatening the ~20% of profit at risk.
- FY26 EPS guidance is cut from the reaffirmed $11.80–$12.10 range, or a further goodwill impairment is taken.
- Constant-currency revenue decline accelerates beyond the −2% Q1 pace, or a recessionary consumer cliff materially impairs discretionary apparel demand alongside the debt-funded buyback posture.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY26 print (expected late August/early September 2026), which will test whether the EMEA/Middle East drag is stabilizing and whether the constant-currency trend is improving. The China UEL status and any Section 301 tariff developments should be monitored continuously between earnings.

## Conclusion

PVH is a genuinely cheap stock — ~6.2x reaffirmed forward EPS, ~6% FCF yield, investment-grade, with a headline earnings collapse that is almost entirely non-cash accounting. But cheapness without a catalyst is the defining hazard of apparel investing, and PVH's catalysts point the wrong way: three years of flat-to-declining constant-currency revenue, an FX-flattered beat masking a −2% organic quarter, a guidance cut peers did not match, forward-year estimates revised down 7–9%, and an asymmetric China tail risk that could remove a fifth of profit on an unscheduled regulatory decision. The interim CFO and in-flight SAP migration add execution overhang during a period of macro stress.

We maintain HOLD. The valuation discount is real and the balance sheet is sound enough to wait, but the burden of proof for an upgrade rests on a demand inflection and a China resolution, neither of which is visible today. For existing holders, the cash generation and depressed multiple limit downside absent a China measure or a consumer cliff; for new capital, we would want to see constant-currency revenue stabilize or forward estimates turn positive before stepping in. Overall score 4.7/10.
