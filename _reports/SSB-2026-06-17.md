**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SouthState is the fifth-largest regional bank headquartered in the South, with $67.2B in assets, $48.6B in loans, and $55.1B in deposits across an eight-state Sunbelt footprint. The January 1, 2025 all-stock acquisition of Independent Bank Group (IBTX) grew the bank from ~$46B to ~$67B in assets, added Texas and Colorado markets, and roughly doubled the share count. On adjusted metrics the franchise is top-tier: FY2025 adjusted ROTCE of 19.9%, adjusted ROAA of 1.48%, and an adjusted efficiency ratio near 50%. Capital is comfortably above well-capitalized (CET1 11.4%, Total Risk-Based 13.8%, TCE 8.8%). The Altman Z-Score of 0.29 that screens "distress" is a model-misapplication artifact for a deposit-funded bank and should be disregarded.

The investable question is earnings durability, not franchise quality, which is high. FY2025 net interest margin ran 3.48% (Q4'24) → 4.06% (Q3'25) → 3.86% (Q4'25), and the Q3 peak was inflated by purchase-accounting loan accretion. The unamortized discount on acquired IBTX loans fell from $457M at Q1'25 to $259M at Q4'25, meaning roughly $200M of accretion income has been recognized and is rolling off. Q4'25 NIM already declined 20bps sequentially and net interest income fell QoQ ($600M → $581M) even as loans grew. Reported 2025 earnings power overstates the core run-rate that will define 2026, and consensus 90-day EPS revisions are modestly negative across every horizon (Current Year -0.6%, Next Year -1.2%).

We rate SSB a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. At $95.83 (most recent print $97.63), the stock trades at ~12.2x trailing EPS and ~10.1x current-year consensus ($9.48), a multiple that reflects the accretion fade rather than a mispricing. Analyst targets ($105 low / $125 high, $115.77 mean) imply ~10-30% upside, and we view the low end as a reasonable floor given the franchise. With the dominant 2026 earnings driver fading and no offsetting near-term catalyst, the risk/reward does not clear our bar for a BUY. The path to upgrade is concrete: evidence that core (ex-accretion) NIM is stabilizing.

## Company Overview

SouthState operates ~342-343 branches across Florida, South Carolina, Georgia, North Carolina, Alabama, Virginia, Texas, and Colorado. Management's strategic thesis is geographic concentration in high-growth markets: it operates in 12 of the 15 fastest-growing U.S. MSAs, with projected 2026-2031 population growth led by Florida (7.5%), Texas (6.5%), South Carolina (6.1%), North Carolina (5.1%), and Colorado (2.7%). The bank positions itself as a "true alternative to the largest banks": large enough to invest in technology and capital markets, with local market leadership.

Two business lines differentiate SSB from a typical regional bank. First, the Correspondent Banking & Capital Markets division serves 1,319 financial-institution clients nationally with hedging (ARC), fixed-income sales, international, and clearing. This fee engine generated $27.5M in Q4'25, up from $13.6M a year earlier. Second, the loan book is granular and diversified: Investor CRE 37%, Consumer RE 21%, C&I 19%, Owner-occupied CRE 16%, Construction/Development/Land 5%, with the top 10 relationships representing only ~2% of loans.

The IBTX integration, with systems and people conversion completed in May 2025, is the source of both the bank's current scale advantage and its central earnings risk.

## Financial Analysis

The headline FY2025 numbers are strong, but require careful separation of merger-scale effects from organic performance.

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net interest income | $1.42B | $2.30B | +63% |
| Total revenue (adj., TE) | $1.72B | $2.68B | +56% |
| Net income (GAAP) | $535M | $799M | +49% |
| Adj. net income | $553M | $964M | +74% |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $6.95 | $7.87 | +13% |
| Adj. diluted EPS | $7.21 | $9.50 | +32% |
| Adj. ROAA | 1.21% | 1.48% | +27bps |
| Adj. ROTCE | 15.5% | 19.9% | +440bps |
| Efficiency ratio (adj.) | 54% | ~50% | improved |
| Tangible book value/share | ~$51 | $56.27 | +10% |

The 63% NII growth is largely IBTX scale; GAAP EPS rose only 13% because of the share dilution from the all-stock deal. The cleaner signals are the adjusted returns: 19.9% ROTCE and 1.48% ROAA are top-tier for a regional bank and reflect genuine operating quality, not just balance-sheet growth.

The central debate is NIM trajectory. Tax-equivalent NIM ran 3.48% (Q4'24) → 3.85% → 4.02% → 4.06% (Q3'25) → 3.86% (Q4'25). The mechanism is straightforward: acquired loans booked at a discount accrete that discount into interest income over time, front-loading yield; as the discount depletes, reported NIM converges toward the lower core rate. Q4'25 total loan yield declined 35bps QoQ to 6.13%. This is the single most important determinant of 2026 earnings.

Two offsets partially cushion the fade. Deposit costs are declining (total cost 1.82% in Q4, -9bps QoQ) as the Fed's path eases funding pressure, and fee income is growing (noninterest income $106M in Q4, +31% YoY, led by correspondent/capital markets). A subtle headwind is the deposit mix shift: DDA fell from 27% to 24% of deposits over the year, a gradual move toward interest-bearing funding that the falling-rate environment currently masks.

Capital management in 2025 was disciplined. The bank executed a sale-leaseback paired with a securities-portfolio restructuring: a ~$229M securities loss offset by a ~$229M sale-leaseback gain, EPS-neutral but improving go-forward securities yields (Q4 securities yield 3.40%). It raised the dividend 11%, repurchased 2.4% of shares, and authorized a new 5.56M-share buyback for 2026. Capital sits comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds.

The earnings-surprise record is strong: four consecutive beats (Q2'25 +13.6%, Q3'25 +16.1%, Q4'25 +7.5%, Q1'26 +2.0%). The surprise magnitude is compressing; the 2.0% Q1'26 beat is the smallest of the streak, consistent with the accretion tailwind fading and consensus catching up to the normalized run-rate.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth in 2025 was solid (Q4 loans grew ~8% annualized, deposits ~8% annualized) and the Sunbelt geographic tailwind is real and durable. The footprint's overweight to Florida, Texas, and the Carolinas gives SSB structurally higher market growth than a typical regional, and the IBTX addition of Dallas, Austin, Houston, and Denver deepens that exposure.

The headline growth rates, however, overstate forward momentum because they incorporate the IBTX consolidation. The cleaner forward read is the consensus estimate path: Current Year EPS $9.48, Next Year $10.45, implying roughly 10% EPS growth, healthy but well below the 32% adjusted EPS growth reported in 2025. Estimate revisions are modestly negative, not positive: Current Year -0.6% over 90 days, Next Year -1.2%, Next Quarter -2.4%. Consensus is gently trimming, consistent with the accretion roll-off, which does not trigger a positive momentum read.

The growth case rests on the bank successfully replacing fading accretion income with core balance-sheet growth, lower deposit costs, and fee expansion. The fee engine is the most differentiated growth lever: the correspondent/capital-markets business doubled its Q4 contribution YoY and is genuinely scaled in a way few similarly sized peers can match. If volatility in rates and credit markets persists, this fee stream provides a counter-cyclical buffer. The base case is single-quarter-to-quarter grinding through the NIM normalization, not acceleration.

On a trailing basis, SSB's ~12.2x multiple is in line with the 12.2x peer median for a bank earning a 19.9% adjusted ROTCE. On a forward basis (~10.1x current-year consensus), the multiple looks cheaper, but that discount is the market pricing the accretion fade, not a free mispricing. Adjusted EPS of $9.50 in 2025 was flattered by ~$200M of non-recurring accretion income; on a normalized basis the bank's true earnings power is closer to the $9.48 current-year consensus that already strips much of that benefit.

A cheap-looking forward multiple on a regional bank with a known, identified earnings headwind is the kind of value-trap setup that warrants skepticism rather than a high valuation score. The discount is earned, not anomalous. It is not a structural impairment either: the bank is high-quality, well-capitalized, and growing organically, which supports a HOLD-grade valuation score rather than a low one.

Analyst targets are $105 low / $115 median / $125 high (mean $115.77), implying 10-30% upside from $95.83. We treat the $105 low as a reasonable floor given franchise quality and the 2.5% dividend yield support. We do not treat these targets as a re-rating signal: JPMorgan cut its target to $110 from $120 on April 1, 2026 (while keeping Overweight), and the negative estimate-revision trend means targets are more likely to drift down than up over the next several quarters. This is a fairly valued, quality franchise rather than an asymmetric entry.

## Competitive Landscape

SouthState competes with super-regionals (Truist, Regions, Fifth Third), other Sunbelt regionals, credit unions, and fintechs. Its competitive position is stable-to-improving on two axes. First, the geographic concentration in the highest-growth U.S. markets is a structural advantage that compounds: deposit and loan growth in its footprint outpaces national averages. Second, the scaled national correspondent/capital-markets fee business gives it a revenue stream most $50-70B peers lack, and that business is growing fast.

The 10-K's forward-leaning posture on digital assets: SSB explicitly discusses the GENIUS Act (2025) and CLARITY Act and states it intends to position itself to "act as a reserve and issuer for stablecoins." This is unusually proactive for a traditional regional and could become a differentiated fee/deposit-gathering channel if the regulatory framework matures, though it is speculative today.

On credit, management presents a multi-year track record of below-peer net charge-offs (2015-2025), supporting a "resilient credit" narrative. The offsetting concern is CRE concentration (discussed below), which is the structural constraint on the competitive growth story; further aggressive CRE growth could draw regulatory scrutiny.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by one earnings risk and one structural credit-concentration risk, neither of which is fully resolved.

1. **Purchase-accounting accretion roll-off** — the single most important near-term earnings risk. The Q3'25 NIM peak is not sustainable; the 10-K explicitly flags that "recent results may not be indicative of future results" and that 2025 NIM was aided by the merger. The Q4 QoQ NIM and NII declines confirm the fade has begun. Where core NIM settles in 2026 is the open question capping the stock.

2. **CRE concentration** — 78.5% of loans carry real estate collateral; commercial + CRE loans are 77% of total ($37.2B). The bank's CRE concentration ratio is 272% of risk-based capital, approaching the 300% regulatory threshold that triggers heightened supervisory scrutiny. This limits CRE growth headroom and is the structural credit pressure point.

3. **Credit-quality inflection** — substandard assets rose steadily from 2.86% (Q4'24) to 3.68% (Q4'25). A Q3'25 commercial loan loss spiked net charge-offs to 27bps (vs. 4-9bps in other quarters); NCOs normalized to 9bps in Q4. ACL/loans declined to 1.35% by Q4 even as criticized assets rose, a reserve dynamic worth monitoring.

4. **IBTX integration/attrition risk** — newly emphasized risk factors around synergy realization, IBTX relationship-manager retention, and culture merger. Deposit/client attrition is specifically flagged.

5. **Regulatory** — crossing ~$66B in assets subjected SSB to OCC heightened standards. A potential offset: the OCC's December 23, 2025 proposal to lift the heightened-standards threshold from $50B to $700B would remove a compliance overhang SSB incurred specifically by crossing $66B via IBTX. Basel III "endgame" rules ($100B+ banks) loom longer-term.

6. **Cybersecurity** — a February 2024 cyber incident produced a putative class action pending in Polk County, FL. No material losses to date.

7. **Concentrated ownership** — two funds own ~20%, top 10 own ~42%.

### Options Market Signal

The options structure shows a sharp near-term spike that is event-specific, not a structural bearish signal. Near-term ATM IV is 101.0% against 24.8% one-year historical vol, but this is concentrated in the one-week (June 18) expiry with only 1 day to expiry, a known distortion. One-month IV is 30.9% and three-month is 27.8%, both close to historical norms. The term structure is in backwardation (101% near-term > 27.8% long-term), and ATM IV jumped from 28.3% to 66.0% since the prior snapshot, indicating the market is pricing a near-dated catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (0.3%) and OI put/call ratio is 0.50 (call-heavy), so there is no directional bearish positioning. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.9-6.2%. The options market is pricing event uncertainty rather than a company-specific bearish lean, which is consistent with a fundamentally driven HOLD.

Short interest is low at 2.8% of float (3.3 days to cover), indicating no informed bearish consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($115-125)
Core NIM stabilizes faster than feared as deposit costs continue falling and the loan book reprices up; the fee engine (correspondent/capital markets) continues its 30%+ growth and partially offsets accretion fade; organic loan/deposit growth in the high-growth Sunbelt footprint sustains mid-single-digit-plus balance-sheet expansion; the OCC threshold proposal is finalized, removing a compliance overhang; and the bank executes its buyback at attractive prices. In this scenario, normalized EPS grows toward $10.45+ (Next Year consensus) and the multiple holds near peer median (12x), supporting a $115-125 valuation. The 19.9% ROTCE and disciplined, shareholder-aligned management (pay tied to PPNR net of charge-offs and TBV/share) justify a premium-quality franchise multiple once the accretion noise clears.

### Bear Case ($80-85)
Core NIM settles lower than consensus as the accretion benefit fully unwinds and deposit-mix shift (DDA 27%→24%) continues to pressure funding costs; CRE concentration at 272% draws regulatory scrutiny or forces higher reserves as substandard assets continue their 2.86%→3.68% climb; IBTX relationship-manager attrition impairs the Texas growth thesis; and a broader credit cycle in CRE (a live macro concern given regional-bank exposure to commercial real estate) pushes charge-offs up. In this scenario, EPS stagnates near $9, estimates continue trimming, and the multiple compresses toward 9x (the WAL level), implying ~$80-85.

The base case sits between: NIM normalizes gradually, organic growth and fees largely offset the accretion fade, EPS grows toward $10, and the multiple holds near 11-12x for a fair value around $105-108, roughly the analyst low target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The franchise quality and ~10% upside to the analyst-target floor justify holding; the fading earnings tailwind and absence of a positive near-term catalyst prevent a BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2'26, expected late July 2026). The two quarters following Q1'26 are the critical window for observing where core, ex-accretion NIM settles.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Core (ex-accretion) NIM stabilizes or expands for two consecutive quarters, with management explicitly quantifying the residual accretion contribution as immaterial — this is the single most important condition.
- 90-day forward EPS revisions turn positive (currently -0.6% to -1.2%), signaling consensus has found the floor and is rebuilding.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $80s on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Substandard assets accelerate above ~4.5% of assets, or net charge-offs run above ~25bps for two consecutive quarters (signaling the Q3'25 commercial loss was not idiosyncratic).
- CRE concentration crosses 300% of risk-based capital and draws explicit regulatory remediation.
- A guidance cut on NII or NIM materially below consensus, or evidence of meaningful IBTX deposit/client attrition.

## Conclusion

SouthState is a high-quality, well-capitalized Sunbelt regional bank with genuine differentiation in its geographic footprint and its scaled correspondent/capital-markets fee business. The adjusted returns (19.9% ROTCE, 1.48% ROAA, ~50% efficiency) place it among the better-run regionals. The score trajectory (6.2 → 6.7 → 6.8) reflected improving confidence over the spring, and the franchise merits that confidence.

The reason we step back to HOLD from the prior BUY is specific and falsifiable: the dominant earnings driver of 2025, purchase-accounting accretion, is rolling off ($457M → $259M of unamortized discount), Q4'25 already showed NIM down 20bps QoQ and NII down sequentially, and consensus estimates are being trimmed across every horizon. This is the opposite of the estimate-revision inflection that would warrant pressing a BUY. The cheaper-looking ~10.1x forward multiple reflects the market correctly pricing a known, identified margin headwind, a setup that historically rewards skepticism over a reflexive "it's cheap" buy. Until core NIM demonstrably stabilizes, the re-rating case lacks a catalyst, and the stock is fairly valued near $105-108. We rate SSB HOLD with a clear, monitorable path to upgrade.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the trailing P/E was overstated as "cheap" at ~10.5x vs. peers when SSB's actual trailing P/E (~12.2x) is in line with the 12.2x peer median, weakening the "cheap multiple / value-trap discount" framing the HOLD partly rested on.
