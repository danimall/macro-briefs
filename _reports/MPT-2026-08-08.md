## Executive Summary

Medical Properties Trust has done what it said it would do operationally. The two tenant bankruptcies that destroyed roughly $3.2B of GAAP equity value between FY2023 and FY2025 are behind it: the Steward hospitals are re-tenanted with five replacement operators whose rent has progressed from 59% of contractual in 2025 to approximately 79% by Q2 2026 with 100% targeted by October 2026, and the six former Prospect California hospitals are leased to NOR Healthcare. Q1 2026 cash rent billed rose 19.6% year over year to $197.5M and total revenue rose 12.6% to $252.1M. Total-portfolio TTM EBITDARM coverage is reported at 2.5x. That is a genuine improvement over any point in the last three years.

The problem is that the recovery is largely pre-spent. MPT has $1,243.2M of debt maturing in 2026 and $1,600.0M in 2027, and the two largest pieces — €500M of notes at a 0.993% coupon and $1.4B of notes at 5.000%, $1,977.65M combined at a blended 3.83% — must be replaced at a marginal cost the company has already demonstrated in its own recent issuance: 6.877% on the 2034 GBP secured term loan, 7.000% on the 2032 secured euro notes, 8.500% on the 2032 secured USD notes. Refinancing that $1.98B at 7.5% adds $72.6M of annual interest, or about $0.121 per share; at 8.5% it adds $92.3M, or $0.154 per share. The disclosed rent ramp — roughly $42M annualized from the Steward replacements reaching full contractual rent, plus NOR moving from $0 of Q1 revenue toward approximately $45M annualized by December 2026 — is worth on the order of $0.14-0.15 per share. The two numbers cancel. Normalized FFO, $0.14 per diluted share in both Q1 2025 and Q1 2026 and $0.58 for FY2025, plausibly plateaus near $0.55-0.60 rather than recovering toward the $1.30-plus of 2022.

At $4.70 the equity trades at 0.62x the $7.60 book value and 8.4x annualized NFFO, with a 7.66% dividend yield that is roughly 64% covered on headline NFFO but approximately 85-100% covered once non-cash straight-line rent is removed. That combination — cheap on stated book, marginal on cash coverage, sitting behind $9.79B of debt at a CCC+/B3 rating with 10.2x net leverage — produces a roughly balanced expected return with a wide distribution. Base case $5.10 (+8.5%), bull $7.00 (+48.9%), bear $3.20 (-31.9%). We stay at HOLD and lower the overall score modestly from 4.5 to 4.15: the operational trajectory continues to improve, but the refinancing arithmetic is now quantified and it caps the earnings recovery the prior scores implicitly extrapolated.

## Company Overview

MPT is a self-advised REIT formed in 2003, headquartered in Birmingham, Alabama, that acquires and develops net-leased hospital real estate. It owns buildings and land; independent hospital operators are the tenants and pay rent under absolute-net master leases. MPT does not operate hospitals.

As of March 31, 2026 the portfolio comprised 378 properties, 51 operators and approximately 38,000 licensed beds across 30 U.S. states and nine countries, with total assets of $14,762.7M split almost exactly between the U.S. ($7,376.9M, 50.0%) and international ($7,385.8M, 50.0%). The United Kingdom is the largest single non-U.S. geography: 92 properties, $4,085.0M of assets (27.7%), generating $96.4M of Q1 revenue, or 38.2% of the total. By asset type, general acute care is 58.8% of assets and 62.0% of Q1 revenue; behavioral health 16.3% and 22.0%; post-acute 11.3% and 15.2%. A residual "other" category is 12.9% of assets and produced zero revenue — dominated by the straight-line rent receivable, cash, loans and other non-property items.

Beyond fee-owned property, MPT holds $1,390.4M of equity in five unconsolidated real estate joint ventures (MEDIAN/Germany, Swiss Medical Network/Infracore, CommonSpirit/Utah, Policlinico di Monza, HM Hospitales), $320.9M of passive minority equity in operating companies (Swiss Medical Network 8.9%, Aevis 4.6%, Priory 9.2%, Aspris 9.2%), and $362.5M of loans to tenants ($238.0M other loans plus $124.5M of mortgages). These positions are correlated with the rent roll rather than diversifying from it — MPT is a second-loss equity holder and an unsecured or lightly secured creditor to the same operators that pay it rent.

The weighted-average remaining initial lease term is 16.7 years and 85.9% of the $1,120.2M of annualized base rent and interest matures after 2035. Duration is a genuine asset. The constraint is capital cost.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Total revenue fell from $1,542.9M in FY2022 to $972.0M in FY2025, down 37%. Cumulative GAAP losses across FY2023-FY2025 total approximately $3.24B against $4.61B of year-end FY2025 equity; the retained deficit stood at $(4,157.4)M at March 31, 2026. Normalized FFO per share declined from $0.80 (FY2024) to $0.58 (FY2025). FY2025 operating cash flow was $230.8M.

**Q1 2026 requires two adjustments before it can be read.** Reported net income to common was $32.8M ($0.05 per share) against a $(118.3)M loss a year earlier. Pre-tax income was $285 thousand — statistically zero. Essentially all of the profit came from a $32.8M income tax benefit, and MPT's own NFFO reconciliation removes $(45,155) thousand of "tax rate changes and other" as non-core. Any screen reading MPT as having returned to profitability is reading a one-time item. Second, the $9.7M year-over-year decline in G&A is explained almost entirely by share-based compensation mark-to-market: GAAP SBC was $0.6M in Q1 2026 versus $17.7M in Q1 2025 because certain awards are cash-settled and remeasured each period, while on a grant-date fair-value basis SBC was $9.0M versus $8.1M — higher. NFFO of $82.2M, or $0.14 per diluted share, versus $81.1M and $0.14 a year earlier is the honest comparison, and it is flat.

**Leverage is the governing constraint.** Management reports Adjusted Net Debt of $8,693.6M against $934.7M of Adjusted Annualized EBITDAre — 9.3x — and adjusted interest coverage of 1.8x ($233.7M ÷ $128.5M, which reproduces at 1.82x). Three adjustments matter. Management's net debt deducts $544.0M of cash already spent on in-progress developments; that cash will generate rent but it cannot repay debt, and adding it back gives 9.9x. Using unadjusted EBITDAre ($226.6M × 4 = $906.3M) gives 10.2x. Consolidating MPT's pro-rata JV net debt ($1,145.2M of debt less $24.3M of JV cash) gives 11.4x. Management's presentation is internally consistent — it excludes JV debt and includes only the unlevered portion of JV EBITDAre — but an investor looking through to economic exposure arrives near 11x.

Coverage on a cash basis is thinner than the reported 1.8x. Adjusted EBITDAre of $233.7M includes $34.2M of non-cash straight-line rent; removing it gives $199.5M ÷ $128.5M = 1.55x, and further removing the $9.8M unlevered JV add-back gives 1.48x. Interest expense of $133.3M consumed 52.9% of Q1 2026 revenue.

**The secured ratchet.** Of $9,790.2M of total debt, $4,356.0M (44.5%) is secured. MPT has repeatedly pledged collateral to access capital, which subordinates the remaining $5,434.2M of unsecured notes and shrinks the unencumbered pool available for future secured borrowing. The weighted-average cost of debt has risen from 4.3% (FY2024) to 5.2% (FY2025) to 5.363% (Q1 2026) and will keep rising as sub-4% legacy paper rolls. S&P rates the company CCC+/negative; Moody's B3 corporate family, B2 secured.

**Liquidity and the wall.** Liquidity was approximately $1.0B at both February 23 and early May 2026. Cash fell from $540.9M at year-end to $425.0M at March 31. MPT gave notice to extend the revolver to June 30, 2027 subject to conditions and repaid $200M of the revolver on April 1, 2026. Against that, $1,243.2M matures in 2026 and $1,600.0M in 2027, and FY2025 contractual obligations including interest total roughly $1.59B in 2026 and $2.12B in 2027. The $500M ATM program is effectively unusable at 0.62x book (no shares were sold in 2025), leaving asset sales and JV recycling as the only equity-like funding sources.

**Dividend coverage.** The quarterly dividend was raised from $0.08 to $0.09 and reaffirmed at $0.09 on May 28, 2026 (paid July 16). Annualized $0.36 is 64% of $0.56 annualized NFFO. Adjusting NFFO of $82,217K by removing $36,479K of non-cash straight-line rent and adding back $7,547K of debt-cost amortization gives $53,285K per quarter, or $0.357 annualized — a 101% payout. Adding back $9,035K of grant-date SBC (generous, since MPT cash-settles some awards) gives $0.417, an 86% payout. FY2025 operating cash flow of $230.8M against a $215.2M annualized dividend is 93%. Three methods converge on 85-100%. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $14.3M on $174.1M of cash interest paid, but MPT's largest coupons cluster semi-annually and that quarter should not be annualized; the FY2025 figure is the right anchor and it leaves roughly $15.6M of cushion before $79.8M of remaining development commitments and continued tenant lending.

Because of the accumulated deficit, the REIT 90% distribution test is unlikely to bind — the dividend is discretionary. It is also not the main obstacle to deleveraging: eliminating it entirely would redirect ~$215M and move net debt/EBITDAre from roughly 10.2x to 10.0x in a year. The $9.2B of net debt against ~$930M of EBITDAre is the issue.

**Balance-sheet quality flags.** The straight-line rent receivable of $904.1M is 6.1% of total assets and 19.9% of the $4,544.7M of book equity — accrued but uncollected future rent, precisely the asset written off in a lease restructuring, as happened with Steward and Prospect. The credit loss reserve fell from $553M to $42.8M in Q1 2026, which was not a credit improvement: roughly $525M of Prospect-related reserves were charged off against the underlying assets. The deferred tax asset valuation allowance rose to $529M from $490M, which is management's own statement that it does not expect sufficient taxable income to use those attributes. Other loans grew $51.7M in one quarter to $238.0M; approximately $130M of working capital loans were made to the Steward-replacement operators and only about $3M had been repaid. Two hospitals under construction have no identified lessee, with $5-10M of further near-term cost.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is recovery of interrupted rent, not expansion. External acquisitions are uneconomic when the marginal cost of debt is 6.9-8.5% against portfolio cash yields near 8%, which is why 2025-2026 activity has been entirely workout, re-tenanting and maturity management.

The recovery is documented and on schedule. Quarterly re-tenanted rent from the Steward replacements progressed $3.4M (Q1 2025) → ~$11M → ~$12M → $26.1M (Q4 2025) → $24.5M (Q1 2026), against a disclosed path from 59% of contractual in 2025 to approximately 79% by Q2 2026 and 100% by October 2026. If $24.5M per quarter represents roughly 70% of contractual, full ramp adds about $42M annually, or $0.07 per share. NOR's six former Prospect California hospitals produced $0 of Q1 2026 revenue — rent was 100% deferred through mid-June 2026, then 50% deferred for six months, recognized on a cash basis — with the FY2025 10-K indicating a ramp toward approximately $45M annualized by December 2026, another $0.075 per share. Combined potential uplift is roughly $0.14-0.15.

Set against the $0.121-0.154 per share of incremental interest on the 2026-2027 refinancing, the net effect on NFFO is close to zero. Secondary retail commentary references a management target of roughly $1.0B of annualized cash rent collections by end-2026; that figure does not appear in any filing we reviewed and should be treated as directionally consistent with the 10-K ramp disclosure rather than established.

**On estimate revisions:** the data provided shows current-year consensus EPS of $0.11 (from $0.12 ninety days ago) and next-year $0.15 (from $0.14), covered by one analyst on near-term quarters and three on the out-year. On a near-zero GAAP EPS base for a REIT whose reported earnings are dominated by impairments and tax items, these revisions carry no information. The large-magnitude estimate-revision buy signal — 30%-plus upward revisions on a meaningful earnings base combined with accelerating revenue — is not present here. The reported "+37.5%" current-year revision reflects a swing of $0.03 in absolute terms.

The earnings surprise record (three beats, one large miss driven by Q3 2025 impairments) is similarly low-signal on a GAAP EPS basis.

## Valuation Assessment

At $4.70:
- **0.62x book value** ($7.60 per share). Equity issuance is book-dilutive at this level, which is why the ATM is dormant.
- **8.4x annualized NFFO** ($0.56), or 8.1-8.5x on the $0.55-0.60 plateau range.
- **7.66% dividend yield**, versus a health care REIT peer median of 6.1% (LTC 6.1%, UHT 7.4%, SBRA 6.4%, HR 4.7%, CTRE 4.1%). The 156bp premium to the peer median is the market's compensation for a CCC+ credit with 85-100% cash payout coverage.
- Peer median P/E of 29.3 is not usable — MPT's trailing EPS is negative.

The most informative cross-check is the enterprise. Adding $9.66B of debt (net of issuance costs) to $2.81B of equity and subtracting $425M of cash gives roughly $12.05B, about 13x annualized EBITDAre, implying a 7.7% capitalization rate against roughly 8% portfolio cash yields on gross investment. Marking the debt to the fair value indicated in the Q1 10-Q footnote — approximately $8.7B, a $1.0B discount to carrying, implying high-single-digit market yields — gives an enterprise value near $11.1B and an implied 8.4% cap rate, or roughly 68% of the $16,476M of gross assets. The market is capitalizing MPT's cash flow slightly above its own portfolio yield and discounting gross assets by about a third. That is a meaningful discount, consistent with the impairment history, but it is not the free option the 0.62x book multiple superficially suggests. Book equity itself embeds a $904M straight-line receivable and $1,711M of JV and operating-company equity carried at values that have proven mobile.

The bond market's $1.0B discount also creates a competing use of capital: accretive debt repurchase versus funding the 2027 maturity. Management has instead deployed $23.4M into share buybacks at an average of $5.20 — above today's price — and raised the dividend.

**Scenario values.**

- **Bear $3.20 (-31.9%):** refinancing at 8.5%, dividend cut to $0.18-0.20 to preserve liquidity, NFFO to $0.48-0.50, multiple compressing to 6.5x. Alternatively, a credit event at one of the newer, uncovered tenants or an adverse OBBBA-driven deterioration in behavioral coverage.
- **Base $5.10 (+8.5%):** rent ramp completes on the disclosed schedule, 2026-2027 maturities refinanced or partly retired via asset sales at a blended 7.0-7.5%, NFFO stabilizes at $0.56-0.60, dividend held at $0.36. Valued at 8.75x NFFO, cross-checked by a 7.0-7.5% yield support band of $4.80-$5.14. This sits close to the analyst median of $5.00 and below the mean of $5.71.
- **Bull $7.00 (+48.9%):** credit spreads compress, MPT refinances near 6.0-6.5% or sells assets/JV interests to retire the 2027 notes outright, cash rent reaches roughly $1.0B annualized, NFFO recovers to $0.68-0.72, and the multiple re-rates to 10x as the rating improves off CCC+. The 30.9% short interest and 19.4 days to cover mean any such repricing would be amplified mechanically.

Probability-weighting roughly 30% bear / 45% base / 25% bull gives an expected price return near +6%, plus a dividend that is intact in the base and bull cases and impaired in the bear. That is a fair, not asymmetric, expected return. Valuation scores 5.0 — cheap on stated book with an identifiable catalyst (the Q4 2026 ramp milestones), but the discount reflects a genuine and quantifiable cost-of-capital impairment rather than pure sentiment.

## Competitive Landscape

MPT's advantage is scarcity of scale: no other public vehicle offers global hospital net-lease exposure at this size, and hospital real estate is genuinely difficult to underwrite (licensure, certificate-of-need, seismic, payor mix). Its disadvantage is that hospital net-lease investing is a spread business and MPT's marginal funding cost of 6.9-8.5% sits at or above its asset yields, while Welltower, Ventas and Healthpeak fund at investment grade. The five-year proxy measurement window quantifies the consequence: $100 invested in MPT became $37.95 while the peer index reached $163.81.

Two trajectory items are negative. First, concentration has not improved. The top five operators by assets (Circle Health 14.0%, Priory 8.6%, Healthcare Systems of America 8.2%, Swiss Medical Network 5.9%, Lifepoint Behavioral 5.5%) total 42.2%, essentially unchanged from the FY2025 10-K's 42%. By revenue, concentration is higher: Circle Health alone is 21.8% of Q1 2026 revenue and Priory 10.9%, so two tenants are 32.7%. The apparent shift toward the UK (38.2% of revenue against 27.7% of assets) is a consequence of U.S. rents being deferred at NOR and ramping at HSA, not diversification.

Second, behavioral health — the segment MPT expanded into most aggressively at 16.3% of assets and 22.0% of revenue — shows the weakest coverage: Priory at 1.6x on $1,227.9M of net investment, and an anonymized "Domestic Operator 3" at 1.2x on $806,344K. That net-investment figure matches Lifepoint Behavioral Health exactly in the operator table, so the identification is a derived match rather than a disclosure. A footnote also discloses that MPT revised allocated central costs for Priory, which retrospectively reduces trailing coverage by 40bp for Priory and 20bp for the behavioral property type — prior-period coverage was overstated on the current method.

The genuinely new industry dynamic is political. The FY2025 10-K adds a risk factor on state-level action targeting REIT and private-capital involvement in healthcare facilities, including prohibitions on sale-leasebacks of acute-care hospitals and restrictions on issuing new hospital licenses for REIT-leased facilities. That attacks the transaction mechanism MPT depends on for both origination and exit liquidity. If such rules proliferate, both MPT's addressable market and the buyer pool for its U.S. assets shrink — which matters directly, because asset sales are the only fast deleveraging lever available.

Offsetting: roughly half of assets sit in the UK, Germany, Switzerland, Spain, Italy and Finland, in single-payor or heavily regulated systems with no exposure to OBBBA Medicaid reform or U.S. state REIT-ownership legislation. Circle Health's implied 2.6x coverage is currently carrying the portfolio while U.S. transitions season.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing.** $1,243.2M due 2026 and $1,600.0M due 2027 against ~$1.0B liquidity, 44.5% of debt already pledged, a dormant ATM, and a CCC+/negative rating. The revolver extension to June 30, 2027 is subject to conditions. This is the dominant binary.

**Coverage is undisclosed where risk is newest.** $3,031.4M of net investment (about 20.5% of assets) sits with tenants for whom property-level reporting is "not required and/or not available," including HSA ($1,115.3M) and NOR ($505.2M) — $1,620.5M, or 11.0% of assets, at the two least-seasoned operators. The reported 2.5x portfolio coverage does not speak to them.

**OBBBA Medicaid reform** is enumerated in both the 10-K risk factors and the Q1 supplemental's forward-looking statements. MPT's tenant base skews to community acute-care and behavioral hospitals with meaningful Medicaid mix. California coverage figures explicitly include expected Hospital Quality Assurance Fee Program 8 receipts, so coverage there is partly a function of a state supplemental-payment program.

**Prospect recovery depends on third-party litigation.** Remaining exposure is roughly $61M of investment plus up to $25M of further funding, with recovery dependent on Connecticut receivables and Prospect's pledged causes of action. MPT's PHP Holdings equity was written down $550M in 2024 and a further $147M in 2025.

**Litigation.** The Alabama securities class action was dismissed with prejudice on August 14, 2025; a New York action relating to Prospect disclosures had a motion to dismiss pending. No liability has been recorded because MPT cannot estimate reasonably possible losses.

**Execution capacity.** Approximately 121 employees manage 378-plus properties, 51 operators and nine countries while running two bankruptcy workouts, five operator transitions, a tenant lending book and a refinancing program.

**Positioning.** Short interest is 30.9% of float, 138.2M shares, 19.4 days to cover. This is two-sided: it creates real squeeze mechanics on any positive surprise, and it also means a large, patient cohort holds the leverage-and-dividend-sustainability view. The February 19, 2026 session — 24.0M shares, an intraday high of $6.47, a $6.02 close and a $5.66 close the next day — is a template for how violently this name can move on a single headline.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 61.9% against 35.3% one-year realized (a 26.5-point spread, 78th percentile within the chain) with the term structure in backwardation — 61.9% at one week, 46.5% at one month, 41.7% at three months, 36.6% at six months — prices a specific imminent event, consistent with a Q2 2026 report landing immediately after the observation date. The five-day expiry implies a ±7.4% move. ATM IV has risen from 58.0% to 61.9% since July 22 while the price fell 2.3%, which is event risk building rather than resolving.

Positioning does not corroborate the bearish view held by the short base. Near-term skew is call-heavy at -5.8%, the put/call open-interest ratio has fallen from 0.70 to 0.55, and the put/call volume ratio is 0.68. The one-month tenor is the exception (skew +1.6%, put/call volume 6.11), suggesting some downside hedging specifically around the print. The six-month tenor implies a ±20.2% distribution — wide for a 7.7% yielder, and a reasonable market estimate of the refinancing and dividend uncertainty. For a prospective buyer, options are expensive to use for entry; selling upside calls against an existing position is the better-priced expression, though it caps the squeeze optionality that constitutes much of the bull case.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($7.00, +48.9%).** Cash rent collections reach roughly $1.0B annualized by year-end 2026 as HSA hits full contractual rent in October and NOR ramps toward $45M. Credit spreads compress or MPT sells assets and JV interests to retire rather than refinance the $1.4B 2027 notes, holding the interest step-up well below the $72.6-92.3M modeled. NFFO recovers to $0.68-0.72, the rating moves off CCC+, and the multiple expands to 10x. Portfolio coverage at 2.5x, general acute care near 3.0x, a 16.7-year weighted-average initial lease term with 85.9% of base rent maturing after 2035, and 91% fixed-rate debt all support the durability of the cash flow once the maturities are cleared. With 30.9% short interest and 19.4 days to cover, a re-rating would be mechanically amplified. Aldag holds roughly 6.69M shares and Hamner 3.79M, so management economic exposure is aligned with this outcome, and the 2024 performance award only vests if the stock holds $7.00 for 20 consecutive days.

**Bear case ($3.20, -31.9%).** MPT refinances the 2026-2027 maturities at 8.5%, adding $92.3M of annual interest and pushing NFFO to $0.48-0.50 even with the full rent ramp. The dividend, already 85-100% covered on cash and running $215M against FY2025 operating cash flow of $230.8M, is cut to preserve liquidity for the 2027 wall, removing the yield support that anchors the current price. A credit event at one of the two operators with no disclosed coverage, or OBBBA-driven pressure on the 1.2-1.6x behavioral tenants, forces another round of straight-line receivable write-offs against a $904M balance that equals 19.9% of book equity. State legislation restricting REIT ownership of hospital real estate spreads, compressing the U.S. buyer pool and removing asset sales as a deleveraging lever.

**What resolves it.** The 2027 refinancing terms. Everything else is second-order.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders can maintain exposure into the Q4 2026 ramp milestones and collect the dividend, accepting that it is thinly covered. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $4.70: base case upside of 8.5% does not compensate for a 31.9% bear case in a capital structure where the equity is a $2.81B sliver behind $9.79B of debt.

**Base case $5.10 (+8.5%):** 8.75x NFFO of $0.58, cross-checked against a 7.0-7.5% yield on a maintained $0.36 dividend ($4.80-$5.14).
**Bull case $7.00 (+48.9%). Bear case $3.20 (-31.9%).**
**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, gated by three dated events — the October 2026 HSA full-contractual-rent milestone, the December 2026 NOR ramp to roughly $45M annualized, and the refinancing or repayment of the $1.4B 5.000% notes due 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Refinancing of the 2026 and/or 2027 maturities at a blended coupon at or below 6.5%, or retirement of the 2027 notes via asset or JV sales without a comparable new coupon. This is the single condition that unlocks the bull case.
2. Two consecutive quarters of cash rent billed above $215M per quarter (versus $197.5M in Q1 2026) with disclosed coverage published for HSA and NOR, converting the ramp from guidance into seasoned collections.
3. Trailing-twelve-month operating cash flow exceeding $300M, which would put the dividend at roughly 70% cash coverage and create real deleveraging capacity.
4. A price below $3.80 with the above operating metrics intact — that would restore genuine asymmetry versus the base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any dividend reduction, or a quarter in which the declared dividend exceeds adjusted cash FFO by more than 15%. A cut would confirm the cash-coverage analysis and remove the yield anchor.
2. New secured issuance that takes secured debt above 55% of total debt, or a further ratings action from S&P below CCC+. Both would signal the refinancing is being executed under duress.
3. Rent deferral, restructuring or bankruptcy at any operator representing more than 5% of revenue — specifically Circle Health (21.8% of Q1 revenue), Priory (10.9%), HSA or the 1.2x-coverage behavioral tenant.
4. Enacted state legislation in more than one additional state prohibiting hospital sale-leasebacks or restricting licensure for REIT-leased facilities, which would remove asset sales as a deleveraging path.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediately on the Q2 2026 report (imminent as of the information cutoff — the options term structure is pricing it) with attention to cash rent billed, the NOR deferral roll-off in mid-June, cash interest paid, and any refinancing announcement; then again at the Q3 2026 report for the October HSA milestone.

## Conclusion

MPT has moved from a solvency question to a refinancing question, which is progress. The Steward and Prospect workouts are substantially behind it, cash rent is growing 19.6% year over year, portfolio coverage is 2.5x, and the lease book is long. The score trajectory from 3.1 in March to 4.5 in July correctly captured that improvement.

The reason we now mark the score down slightly to 4.15 rather than continuing to extrapolate is arithmetic. The rent recovery investors are underwriting — roughly $0.14-0.15 per share from the HSA and NOR ramps — is very close in magnitude to the interest step-up MPT faces refinancing $1.98B of 2026-2027 maturities from a 3.83% blended coupon at a marginal cost its own recent issuance places at 6.9-8.5%, or $0.121-0.154 per share.
