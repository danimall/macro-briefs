## Executive Summary

British American Tobacco is a £25.6bn-revenue nicotine business that converts roughly 38% of revenue into operating cash flow in a normal year and distributes about half of it as dividends. At $59.33 the ADR yields approximately 5.4% on a trailing cash dividend of roughly $3.22 per share and trades at about 12.6x trailing adjusted earnings of $4.71. Seven covering analysts are unanimously above the current price, with a target range of $63 to $75 and a $70.20 mean. Short interest is negligible at 0.4% of float.

The investment question is whether the cash available to shareholders after litigation obligations, interest, and a stalled deleveraging program supports a return above the roughly 11% that the current yield plus mid-single-digit adjusted EPS growth would imply. Three findings from the FY2025 Form 20-F make that judgment harder than the headline multiple suggests. First, FY2025 operating cash flow fell 37.4% to £6,342m from £10,125m, and about 88% of that decline (£3,346m) is arithmetically matched by a reduction in the balance-sheet provision, most plausibly a cash settlement of Canadian tobacco litigation. Second, £3,769m of provisions still sit on the balance sheet against a 2021-2023 norm of £853m-£1,521m, implying £2.2-2.9bn of above-normal obligations whose payment schedule is not disclosed in anything I could verify. Third, net debt on a gross-borrowings-less-cash basis fell only £410m (1.3%) in 2025, versus £3,418m in 2024 and £4,622m in 2023, because £1,880m of gross debt reduction was offset by a £1,470m draw on cash, and an unquantified share of the gross decline is sterling translation of USD and EUR liabilities rather than repayment.

Operating momentum is genuinely better than the reported GBP financials show. H1 2026 delivered group revenue +2.9% at constant currency, New Categories revenue +18% cc, adjusted operating profit +3.5%, and adjusted diluted EPS +7.9%, with FY2026 guidance reaffirmed and a £1.3bn buyback in progress. But smokeless products were 19.8% of group revenue at the half, so roughly four-fifths of the revenue base remains combustible. Mid-teens growth on a one-fifth base contributes about three points of group revenue growth per year before combustible erosion, so the transition arithmetic constrains the outcome regardless of how well Velo and Vuse execute. **HOLD, income rationale.** Base case $64, bull $75, bear $48.

## Company Overview

BAT operates a two-pillar nicotine model. Combustibles — cigarettes sold in the US through the Reynolds American subsidiary — supply the overwhelming majority of revenue and effectively all of the funding for the second pillar. That second pillar, "New Categories," comprises vapour (Vuse), modern oral nicotine pouches (Velo, with Velo Plus and Velo Max as the newer US formats), and heated products (glo). Reporting runs through two geographic segments, AME (Americas, Middle East and Europe) and APMEA (Asia-Pacific, Middle East and Africa).

FY2025 revenue was £25,610m with profit for the year of £7,765m and total assets of £109,290m against £48,145m of equity. Asset turnover of 0.224x and a 30.3% reported net margin are the signature of a brand- and intangible-heavy business with modest operating capital intensity. Both of those ratios, however, sit on a denominator and numerator that have been reshaped by two consecutive years of large non-cash and non-recurring items, discussed below.

One structural note materially affects how BTI is priced. As a UK-domiciled foreign private issuer, BAT files a Form 20-F rather than a 10-K and Form 6-K rather than 8-K or 10-Q. It files no DEF 14A, and its officers are exempt from Section 16, so no Form 4 insider transaction record exists. Standard 10-K-keyed quantitative screens return nothing for BTI, which means much of the consensus quantitative dataset on this name is third-party imputation rather than SEC-derived. That creates real scope for differentiated fundamental work, and it also means data feeds should be cross-checked. The profile data supplied here illustrates the point: it reports both trailing and forward P/E at 15.39 against EPS of $4.706, which is internally inconsistent with any price near $57-59. At $59.33 and $4.706 of EPS the multiple is 12.6x, and that is the figure used throughout.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: three consecutive declines in reported sterling

| Year | Revenue (£m) | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 25,684 | — |
| 2022 | 27,655 | +7.7% |
| 2023 | 27,283 | -1.3% |
| 2024 | 25,867 | -5.2% |
| 2025 | 25,610 | -1.0% |

Revenue is 7.4% below the 2022 peak, a -2.53% compound annual rate over 2022-2025. Half-year reported revenue has declined in every period: £13,441m (H1 2023), £12,340m (H1 2024), £12,069m (H1 2025).

Against that, H1 2026 constant-currency revenue grew 2.9%. For a sterling reporter with a US-weighted profit pool, the gap between reported and constant-currency growth is one of the larger swing factors in the P&L, and comparing BAT's reported top line against dollar-reporting peers without an FX adjustment produces a misleading answer. Part of the 2024 step-down also reflects the Russia and Belarus exit rather than currency; the split is not quantified in the filings accessible for this work.

### Earnings: no clean reported year since 2022

| Year | Profit for year (£m) | Operating cash flow (£m) | OCF / Profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 6,846 | 10,394 | 1.52x |
| 2023 | (14,189) | 10,714 | n/m |
| 2024 | 3,181 | 10,125 | 3.18x |
| 2025 | 7,765 | 6,342 | 0.82x |

Three separate distortions sit inside this window. FY2023's £14,189m loss coincided with total assets falling £34,830m and equity falling £22,776m while operating cash flow hit a four-year high; the arithmetic alone establishes the charge as non-cash. FY2024's £3,181m profit accompanied a £6,116m increase in provisions (£999m to £7,115m), with the charge concentrated in H2: H1 2024 profit was £4,559m, implying an H2 2024 loss of £1,378m. FY2025's £7,765m is the mirror image, flattered by the absence of that charge; H1 2025 profit of £4,565m was up only 0.1% year on year, and the entire annual improvement came in H2.

The practical consequence is that reported FY2025 ROE of about 15.8% on average equity overstates normalized returns, because write-downs shrank the denominator while a non-repeating comparison lifted the numerator. Analysis has to run on adjusted figures, and the adjusted figures for H1 2026 (+3.5% operating profit, +7.9% diluted EPS) are the cleanest available read on trajectory.

### The FY2025 cash flow decline and the residual liability

Operating cash flow fell £3,783m in 2025. Provisions fell £3,346m over the same period. The unexplained residual is £437m. On the settlement reading, normalized FY2025 operating cash flow is approximately £9,688m, which puts the business on a £9.5-10.5bn annual operating cash generation run-rate with a slow downward drift from £10,714m in 2023.

Two caveats matter. A provision balance can also fall through P&L release, FX translation, or reclassification; if a material share of the £3,346m was a release rather than a cash payment, the normalization above is too generous and would also partly explain the FY2025 profit rebound, double-counting in the bull case. And the £3,769m still on the balance sheet, against a £853m-£1,521m historical norm, implies £2.2-2.9bn of above-normal obligations with an undisclosed payment schedule. That is the single most decision-relevant unresolved item in this name.

### Dividend coverage and funding mix

Cash dividends paid: £5,070m (2023), £5,208m (2024), £5,240m (2025). Coverage from operating cash flow was 2.11x, 1.94x, and 1.21x reported — or approximately 1.85x in 2025 on the normalized £9,688m base. Capital expenditure is not verified, so free-cash-flow coverage cannot be stated [unverified — actual capex: N/A].

What is verifiable is the 2025 funding mix. Reported operating cash flow of £6,342m less dividends of £5,240m leaves £1,102m before any capex, against a buyback program referenced at £1.3bn, while cash fell £1,470m (£5,297m to £3,827m). Distributions in 2025 were bridged from the balance sheet. Disposal proceeds — BAT has been monetizing its ITC Ltd. holding in recent years — could have funded part of the gap, so the mix is not fully established, but the direction is clear.

### Leverage

| Date | Gross borrowings (£m) | Cash (£m) | Net (derived) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 31 Dec 2023 | 39,730 | 4,659 | 35,071 |
| 31 Dec 2024 | 36,950 | 5,297 | 31,653 |
| 31 Dec 2025 | 35,070 | 3,827 | 31,243 |

Net debt on this measure fell 1.3% in 2025 versus 9.7% in 2024 and 11.6% in 2023. Deleveraging effectively paused. Net debt to normalized operating cash flow is 3.23x (4.93x on reported OCF), and gross borrowings to equity is 0.73x. This measure excludes lease liabilities and debt-related derivatives and is not management's "adjusted net debt," which I could not obtain; BAT's own metric and its leverage target range are verification gaps, and management's figure may show a better trajectory.

Equity has fallen 36.4% from the 2022 peak of £75,710m to £48,145m, and total assets 28.8% from £153,546m. The 2025 portion of that decline is concentrated in H1 (assets -£8,673m in H1 versus -£936m in H2; the reconciling "other movements" in equity were -£4,773m in H1 and +£398m in H2) even though the cash outflow was H2-weighted. A large, partially reversing, non-cash swing hitting both sides of the balance sheet in a single half is most consistent with adverse translation of USD-denominated net assets during sterling strength, though pension remeasurement, hedge reserves, or amortization phasing could each contribute. The relevant implication is that the -36% equity erosion since 2022 is not all fundamental value destruction, and that a single adverse half-year of FX can move reported gearing by roughly 10% of book equity with no operational change.

**Financial health: 5.5.** Cash generation and margin structure are genuinely strong; leverage sits within tobacco norms on normalized cash flow; but the residual provision, the paused net-debt reduction, and distributions funded from the balance sheet in 2025 are three concrete constraints on capital flexibility in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth arithmetic is tightly bounded. Smokeless products were 19.8% of H1 2026 group revenue growing at 18% constant currency, which contributes roughly 3.5 points of group revenue growth. Group constant-currency revenue grew 2.9%, so combustibles were roughly flat to marginally negative on a price/mix basis, with pricing and mix offsetting volume decline, as management describes. Management guides New Categories to mid-teens growth, which on a rising base contributes 3-4 points annually. For group revenue to accelerate materially, combustible pricing power would need to strengthen rather than merely hold, which excise escalation and downtrading pressures make unlikely.

Adjusted diluted EPS grew 7.9% in H1 2026 against a medium-term framework of 5-8%. This growth is partly manufactured: a £1.3bn buyback on roughly £97bn of market capitalization retires about 1.3% of shares annually, and the reported +7.9% is a non-GAAP measure that must not be compared with the IFRS profit figures above. A secondary-source discrepancy on guidance framing is worth flagging: one report describes management expecting results at the "lower end" of medium-term growth targets, another describes EPS growth "toward the middle" of the 5-8% range with second-half weighting. These may describe different frameworks (revenue/profit versus adjusted EPS), and neither was confirmed against BAT's own 6-K. Treat FY2026 guidance as reaffirmed in direction but not established in precision.

The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in consensus: three beats and one miss across four quarters, all within ±1.5%. This business lands close to expectations, which reduces the probability of a violent re-rating in either direction from the operating line.

**Growth prospects: 4.8.** Real constant-currency growth and a credible smokeless franchise, capped by mix arithmetic and three consecutive years of declining reported sterling revenue.

## Valuation Assessment

At $59.33 with trailing adjusted EPS of $4.706, the ADR trades at 12.6x. On FY2026 adjusted EPS of approximately $5.00 (applying the 5-8% guided range to the trailing base), the forward multiple is roughly 11.9x. The trailing cash dividend of about $3.22 per ADR (£5,240m paid across roughly 2.2bn shares, £2.38 per share, converted at approximately 1.34) yields 5.4% at the official close and 5.6% at the last extended-hours trade. Payout against adjusted EPS is approximately 68%, consistent with BAT's stated policy. The share count, ADR ratio, and GBP/USD rate underlying the per-share dividend are unverified, so the $3.22 and 5.4% figures should be treated as estimates dependent on those inputs.

Within the tobacco peer set, this is mid-pack rather than distressed: Philip Morris International trades at a substantial premium on the strength of IQOS and ZYN, while Altria and Imperial Brands trade at or below BAT's multiple. BAT's relative position reflects a defensible middle ground — better smokeless traction than Altria, more leverage and less momentum than PMI. The stock is 11.8% below its 52-week high of $67.30 set on 14 May 2026 and 19% above the $49.88 low reached in October 2025, and it has given back roughly 4% since the 30 July H1 print despite reaffirmed guidance, most plausibly on the reported-profit decline and the ambiguous guidance framing.

The specific case that the market is mispricing this asset rests on two mechanisms. The first is informational: FY2025 reported cash flow, net income, and cash-conversion ratios are all distorted, and BTI is invisible to 10-K-keyed screens, so accrual-quality and cash-conversion filters read FY2025's 0.82x OCF-to-net-income as deterioration when the correct reading is the opposite. The second is structural: normalized cash generation of £9.7bn against roughly £97bn of market capitalization is a 10% normalized operating cash yield before capex and interest.

Against that, three things restrain the valuation score. Terminal-decline discounts in tobacco have persisted for a decade and have repeatedly punished investors who treated a low multiple as sufficient. 12.6x is near the upper end of BAT's own recent trading range, not the lower end; the May 2026 peak was about 14x. And the residual £2.2-2.9bn provision liability is a claim on the same cash flow that supports the yield. Analyst targets are unanimously above spot with a $63 floor, but in a mature staple at a mid-teens-percentage discount to its own recent peak multiple, targets clustered near 14-15x forward earnings represent multiple-expansion assumptions rather than independent confirmation.

**Valuation: 6.0.** Reasonable absolute return support and an identifiable screening-driven mispricing mechanism, without the margin of safety that would justify a higher score at a price 12.6x trailing and near the top of the name's own recent multiple range.

## Competitive Landscape

BAT reports US modern-oral share gains driven by Velo Plus, and double-digit smokeless growth in both AME and APMEA. Gaining share in modern oral against a large, well-capitalized incumbent franchise is a meaningful signal, though I have no independent share data to size it. APMEA is described as a slower recovery, with US and AME strength offsetting.

The most consequential competitive dynamic is the US regulatory shift. Reuters reporting from May 2026 described the FDA loosening regulation of new vapes and nicotine pouches, potentially admitting hundreds of additional products. Management characterized recent US developments as supportive of a planned nationwide Velo Max and selected Vuse flavour rollout. Both readings are correct and they point in opposite directions. For roughly five years, US vapour market access was gated by premarket authorization, which advantaged incumbents able to fund the process while enforcement gaps let unauthorized imported disposables take share regardless. A permissive authorization regime helps BAT's own pipeline and simultaneously erodes the scarcity value of the authorizations BAT already holds. Which effect dominates is unresolved, and it is the most important open industry question for this name over the next 24 months.

A definitional caution on the transition narrative: a revenue mix shifting toward smokeless is a real change in mix, and it is not the same as reduced concentration risk. Roughly 80% of revenue remains combustible, and the US remains the dominant profit pool. Mix shift establishes that new categories are growing; it does not establish that dependence on the legacy business or on a single regulatory jurisdiction has diminished.

**Competitive position: 5.5.** Improving in smokeless, structurally eroding in the four-fifths of revenue that funds it, with a regulatory change that cuts both ways.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Residual litigation cash obligations.** £3,769m of provisions at 31 December 2025 against a £853m-£1,521m historical norm implies £2.2-2.9bn of above-normal obligations. FY2025 dividends of £5,240m already consumed 83% of reported operating cash flow. Further settlement tranches of this magnitude would compress buyback capacity, deleveraging, or dividend growth. The Canadian settlement's amount, structure, and payment schedule are unverified in every particular, and the link between the provision movement and Canada rests on a single secondary source.

**2. Regulatory two-sidedness in the US.** The FDA liberalization is a catalyst for BAT's product pipeline and a de-moating event for its existing authorizations. No data quantifying US illicit disposable vape share was obtainable.

**3. Currency translation.** The H1 2025 equity residual of -£4,773m demonstrates that one adverse half-year of FX can erase roughly 10% of book equity. With £35,070m of gross borrowings against £48,145m of equity, translation swings of that size move reported gearing meaningfully with no operational change.

**4. Political and reputational exposure via Reynolds.** Aggregated reporting in May and June 2026 alleged a $5m payment by a Reynolds American subsidiary to an independent political committee shortly before FDA flavoured-vape clearances, with critics alleging impropriety. Stated literally: the payer was a Reynolds subsidiary, the recipient was a political committee, and the regulator was not a party to the payment. This is an unverified tier-3 allegation, and temporal sequence is not evidence of causation. What it establishes is live headline risk and a plausible source of future congressional or state-AG attention.

**5. Governance and disclosure opacity.** No DEF 14A exists for a foreign private issuer, and the UK Directors' Remuneration Report was inaccessible, so there is no verified data on compensation structure, performance metrics, TSR versus peers, board composition, or related-party transactions. Twenty 6-K filings between 18 May and 6 August 2026 were catalogued but not read; their characterization as routine buyback and holdings notifications is an inference from filing cadence. Foreign private issuers are exempt from Section 16, so the absence of insider transaction data is a filing-regime artifact and carries no information in either direction.

**6. Screening misreads.** FY2025's inversion of OCF-to-net-income to 0.82x from 3.18x will be flagged as accrual deterioration by mechanical screens when it is a provision settlement. This affects cash-flow and accrual inputs specifically and does not invalidate leverage, liquidity, or margin ratios drawn from the same balance sheet.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 26.6% against 24.7% one-year realized is fairly priced, with the IV percentile at just 24% of the current chain. The term structure is mildly backwardated (26.6% at 10 days versus 24.0% at 129 days), which is consistent with a defined near-term event rather than a regime shift; with earnings already reported on 30 July, the ±4.9% implied move on the 21 August expiry looks generous relative to the realized post-print reaction. Put/call open-interest ratio of 0.39 is call-tilted, and the near-term skew is balanced at -0.2%, widening to -3.6% at the six-month tenor. Read together: no meaningful hedging pressure at the front, some willingness to pay for longer-dated downside protection. Short interest of 0.4% of float with 1.6 days to cover means there is essentially no bearish positioning in this name. None of this contradicts the fundamental view; it confirms that the market is not pricing an imminent dislocation, which is appropriate given that the identified provision-settlement risk has no disclosed date.

**Risk factors: 5.2.** Sector-level litigation and regulatory risk is permanently priced into the multiple. The unpriced idiosyncratic items are the undisclosed provision payment schedule, the two-sided US regulatory shift, and the governance blind spot.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $75 (+26.4% from $59.33, plus ~5.4% yield)

Normalized operating cash flow of £9.7bn is the true earnings base, and FY2025's reported figure is a one-off distortion that mechanical screens are misreading. The residual provision runs off over three or more years without forcing a capital-allocation change, net-debt reduction resumes in 2026-2027, and the buyback continues at 1.3% of shares annually. New Categories compound in the mid-to-high teens, US modern-oral share gains against the incumbent continue, and FDA liberalization proves net-positive because BAT's scale and distribution convert a permissive regime into share faster than new entrants can build channels. Adjusted EPS grows at the upper end of 5-8%, reaching approximately $5.10-5.20 for FY2027. A re-rating to about 14.5x — roughly where the stock traded in early 2026 and near the $75 top-end analyst target — produces $75 with the dividend on top for a total return near 32% over 18 months.

### Bear Case — $48 (-19.1% from $59.33)

The £3,346m provision reduction included a material P&L release rather than being purely cash settlement, meaning normalized operating cash flow is closer to £8.5bn than £9.7bn and the FY2025 profit rebound was partly the same item counted twice. Further settlement tranches of £1bn-plus per year force a choice: the buyback is suspended, deleveraging stalls a second and third year, and dividend growth goes to zero in nominal terms. FDA liberalization floods the US vapour market, Vuse loses share to authorized new entrants and grey-market disposables, and New Categories growth decelerates to high single digits, pulling group constant-currency growth to roughly 1%. Combustible volume decline accelerates past pricing power. Sterling strengthens, compressing reported sterling revenue further and eroding book equity. The multiple compresses to Imperial-like levels near 9.5x on approximately $5.00 of adjusted EPS, producing $48, close to the $49.88 52-week low.

### Base Case — $64 (+7.9% from $59.33, plus ~5.4% yield ≈ 13% total return)

FY2026 adjusted EPS reaches approximately $5.00 (midpoint of the 5-8% guided range on the trailing $4.71). Constant-currency revenue growth holds at 2-3%, New Categories deliver mid-teens growth, the residual provision is settled in tranches that consume free cash flow without forcing distribution cuts, and net debt resumes a slow decline. The multiple holds at 12.8x, roughly where it sits today and within the recent range. $64 is below the midpoint of the $63-$75 analyst target band, which I regard as appropriate: those targets require multiple expansion the operating trajectory does not obviously earn.

Probability-weighting 50% base, 25% bull, 25% bear gives a price-only expected return of approximately +5.7%, or roughly +11% including the dividend. That is a fair-to-marginally-positive risk-adjusted return, which places this at the HOLD-to-ACCUMULATE boundary and, given the unresolved provision schedule and the absence of any governance or insider-alignment evidence, on the HOLD side of it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income rationale).** The 5.4% dividend, covered approximately 1.8x by normalized operating cash flow and approximately 68% of adjusted EPS, is the reason to own the shares. Existing holders should keep the position and collect the yield; prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at 12.6x trailing near the upper end of the name's recent multiple range.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Disclosure in the FY2026 20-F or an interim 6-K that quantifies the residual provision's payment schedule at £1bn per year or less, or that reduces the provision balance toward £1.5bn without a corresponding drop in operating cash flow. This resolves the largest open item.
2. Net debt (management's adjusted measure) declining by £1.5bn or more in FY2026, demonstrating that the 2025 pause was settlement-driven rather than structural.
3. Price weakness to $50-53 with fundamentals intact, which would put the yield above 6.2% and the forward multiple near 10x — a level at which the bear case is substantially pre-paid.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any suspension or downsizing of the buyback, or a dividend increase below 1% in nominal terms, which would signal that litigation cash needs are constraining the distribution policy.
2. New Categories constant-currency revenue growth decelerating below 10% in any half-year, or a reported loss of US vapour share following the FDA liberalization.
3. FY2026 adjusted EPS growth coming in below 5% (the bottom of the medium-term framework), or a second consecutive year in which distributions exceed operating cash flow less capex.
4. Formal regulatory, congressional, or state-AG action arising from the political-donation allegations, as distinct from press coverage.

**Reassessment timeline:** The FY2026 preliminary results and 20-F, expected February 2027, are the natural decision point; that filing should disclose the FY2026 provision balance, capex, adjusted net debt, and full-year cash flow, which together resolve most of the open items. An interim check at the H1 2027 announcement (late July 2027) on New Categories growth and buyback continuity is warranted. In the nearer term, watch for any 6-K quantifying remaining litigation obligations.

## Conclusion

BAT is a high-margin, cash-generative consumer staple trading at 12.6x trailing adjusted earnings with a 5.4% dividend that is comfortably covered on normalized cash flow. Operating momentum is better than the reported sterling financials show: 2.9% constant-currency revenue growth, 18% New Categories growth, 7.9% adjusted EPS growth, and reaffirmed FY2026 guidance. Seven analysts are unanimously above the current price with a $63 floor, and short interest of 0.4% means there is no bearish positioning to squeeze.

The reasons this is a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE are specific and quantified. £3,769m of provisions remain against a £0.9-1.5bn norm, with no disclosed payment schedule, against a dividend that already absorbed 83% of reported FY2025 operating cash flow. Net debt reduction stalled to 1.3% in 2025 while cash fell £1,470m to fund distributions. Roughly 80% of revenue remains combustible, capping group growth near 3% constant currency regardless of smokeless execution. And there is no verified governance, compensation, or insider-alignment evidence, which for a co-primary scoring dimension means management's revealed preference — holding the full distribution profile through a settlement year by drawing on the balance sheet — is defensible but not confirmable as optimal.

At $59.33 the probability-weighted total return is approximately 11%, most of which is the dividend. That is fair compensation for the risks, not asymmetric upside. A move toward $50-53, or disclosure that bounds the residual litigation cash requirement, would change the call.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: several per-share/dividend figures (the $3.22 dividend, 5.4% yield, ~68% payout, ~2.2bn share count and 1.34 GBP/USD rate) rest on unverified share-count/FX inputs and are flagged as estimates; the normalized-FCF-coverage "~1.7x" claim was removed because capex is unverified; and the bull-case "14x May 2026 peak multiple" was demoted to "roughly where the stock traded in early 2026" since neither the report nor the sources establish a peak-multiple figure. None of these changes the direction of the HOLD, but the income rationale depends on dividend/yield inputs that a primary-source reviewer will not be able to confirm.
