## Executive Summary

Dorian LPG operates 27 Very Large Gas Carriers (VLGCs) almost exclusively in the spot market through the Helios Pool joint venture. The stock has doubled from its May 2025 low of ~$18 to $42.48, driven by a VLGC rate surge to record levels (~$170K/day on Houston-Chiba) caused by Strait of Hormuz disruption reshaping global LPG trade flows. At current rates, quarterly earnings power could reach $4-6/share versus $1.11 earned in Q3 FY2026 at $50K/day rates.

The rate environment's sustainability depends on the duration of the geopolitical disruption. Analyst consensus estimates are falling (-17% to -38% over 30 days for near-term periods), suggesting the sell-side has not fully incorporated the May 2026 rate spike. This creates a temporary information asymmetry that cuts both ways. If rates normalize toward $50-60K/day, the stock at 14.8x trailing earnings with a 7.8% yield offers reasonable value. If the Hormuz disruption resolves and rates collapse toward $25-35K/day, the stock is materially overvalued.

The specific concerns preventing a BUY recommendation: the stock is at its 52-week high after a 98% rally, EPS estimates for the next two quarters are declining at -17% to -33% rates, the company has missed earnings in 3 of 4 recent quarters, and options markets are pricing imminent stress (51.1% near-term IV vs. 40% long-term, classic backwardation structure). The rate spike is real and confirmed, but buying a pure spot-market shipper at 52-week highs during a geopolitical disruption with non-trivial probability of resolution represents poor risk/reward timing.

## Company Overview

Dorian LPG is one of the world's five largest independent VLGC operators, with 27 vessels (21 owned, 6 time-chartered-in) aggregating ~2.1 million cubic meters of capacity. The fleet averages ~8.9 years for owned vessels versus ~12 years for the global fleet. Sixteen vessels are scrubber-equipped, and five are dual-fuel ECO VLGCs capable of burning LPG as fuel.

The company operates almost entirely through the Helios Pool, a 50/50 joint venture with MOL Energia (a subsidiary of Mitsui OSK Lines). This pool structure exposes 97% of revenues to spot market rates, making Dorian among the most leveraged plays on VLGC freight rates available in public markets. The primary trade route is US Gulf → Asia via Panama Canal, with Middle East → Asia as the secondary corridor.

A newbuilding VLGC/AC (93,000 cbm, ammonia-capable) was expected for delivery in early-to-mid 2026, representing the company's first diversification beyond pure LPG transport.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings

FY2025 (ended March 2025) represented a cyclical trough: revenue $353M (-37% YoY), operating income $113M (-66%), net income $90M. TCE rates averaged $39,778/day versus $62,129/day in FY2024. Despite this, the company generated $154M in free cash flow (45% FCF margin) and maintained profitability throughout.

The recovery has been sharp. Q3 FY2026 (October-December 2025) showed TCE rates of $50,333/day (+39.5% YoY), revenue $120M (+49% YoY), and EPS of $1.11. Management noted rates "continued to be strong into the current quarter."

The May 2026 rate spike to $170K/day, if sustained even for one quarter, would generate quarterly earnings of roughly $4-6/share based on the company's cost structure (~$14-16K/day opex + G&A). This is not yet in consensus estimates.

### Balance Sheet

Cash: $294M (December 2025). Long-term debt: $415M (declining from $499M in March 2025). D/E ratio: 0.70x. Current ratio: 3.54x. Fleet market value exceeds book by $590M. The balance sheet is in excellent condition for a shipping company, with active deleveraging and substantial liquidity.

### Capital Returns

Total returned since IPO exceeds $960M. Dividends declined from $1.00/share quarterly (peak) to $0.50/share (May 2025), tracking the rate downturn. At current rates, dividend restoration toward $0.70-1.00/quarter is highly probable. Current annualized yield: ~4.7-6.6% depending on assumed distribution rate.

### Earnings Track Record

The 3-of-4 miss record is concerning: -67.2% miss (Q4 FY2025), -61.4% miss (Q1 FY2026), +1.2% beat (Q2 FY2026), -7.4% miss (Q3 FY2026). This pattern reflects the difficulty in forecasting spot-rate-dependent revenues, but also suggests analyst estimates may systematically lag rate movements in both directions.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Catalyst: Hormuz Disruption

With Middle East barrels rerouted or curtailed, US exports (Dorian's primary trade) become the marginal supply source for Asian buyers, lengthening average voyage distances and tightening vessel availability simultaneously.

### Medium-Term Demand Drivers

US LPG export growth remains structurally supported by shale gas production. Asian petrochemical demand (propane dehydrogenation plants) continues expanding. Q4 2025 saw a new VLGC US export record.

### Supply Headwinds

The VLGC orderbook stands at 111 vessels by 2029 (~27% of existing 402-ship fleet). This is a meaningful medium-term headwind that will pressure rates once geopolitical disruptions normalize. However, an aging fleet (12-year average) suggests accelerating scrapping, and many newbuilds are ammonia carriers that may not directly compete.

### Fleet Growth

The VLGC/AC newbuilding adds ~4% capacity. Two additional charter-ins expanded the fleet from 25 to 27 vessels. Modest, capital-efficient growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $42.48, LPG trades at 14.8x trailing EPS ($2.14 represents a blended period including trough earnings). Forward P/E is also 14.8x based on current FY consensus of $4.29. Against peer median P/E of 25.9x, LPG appears cheap on a relative basis.

However, the $4.29 FY2026 estimate is declining (-5% over 30 days, -8% over 90 days). If current record rates persist for even 2-3 months, FY2026 earnings could meaningfully exceed this estimate. If rates normalize to the $50-60K/day range that prevailed before the Hormuz disruption, the $4.29 estimate is approximately correct, putting the stock at ~10x earnings on a mid-cycle basis.

At $42.48, the market is pricing something between mid-cycle ($50-60K/day) and elevated rates. It is not fully pricing the $170K/day record. If the disruption persists, there's upside to $50-55. If it resolves within 2-3 months, the stock likely reverts to $30-35.

Fleet asset value provides a floor: estimated market value of the fleet (~$1.76B including the book-to-market premium) roughly equals the current market cap, meaning you're paying approximately net asset value for the operating business at current prices.

## Competitive Landscape

Dorian ranks among the top 5 independent VLGC operators globally. The Helios Pool with MOL Energia provides commercial scale and diversified customer relationships (ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, Glencore, Vitol, Indian Oil, SK Gas). The pool operates 28 VLGCs total.

Competitive advantages include: younger-than-average fleet (lower maintenance costs, longer useful life), scrubber equipment on 16 vessels (fuel cost savings of $2-4K/day when fuel spreads are wide), and dual-fuel capability on 5 ECO vessels (regulatory compliance advantage as IMO/EU ETS tightens).

The primary competitive concern is concentration: 97% of revenue through a single pool structure with a single JV partner. This is both a strength (commercial scale) and a vulnerability (counterparty and relationship risk).

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risk: Rate Reversion

VLGC rates could normalize rapidly if: (a) Hormuz disruption resolves, (b) Chinese tariffs reduce US-Asia LPG trade, or (c) newbuild deliveries overwhelm demand growth. A reversion to $35-40K/day rates (the FY2025 trough) would produce EPS of $1.50-2.50, putting the stock at 17-28x.

### Geopolitical Dual-Edged Sword

The same Hormuz disruption driving record rates also threatens Middle East LPG supply (a key cargo source for Asian buyers). If the conflict destroys production infrastructure rather than merely rerouting trade, long-term LPG supply contracts and shipping demand could decline even as spot rates spike temporarily.

### Orderbook Overhang

111 VLGCs delivering by 2029 represents meaningful supply growth. Even with scrapping and ammonia diversion, net fleet growth of 15-20% over 4 years will pressure rates in normalized environments.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV at 51.1% significantly exceeds 6-month IV of 40.0%, creating a steep backwardation structure pricing imminent event risk tied to the Hormuz situation's binary resolution potential. Put skew of 6.0% in the near term confirms elevated downside hedging demand. ATM IV rose 13.9pp in the last two weeks while the stock gained 8.8%; vol rising sharply alongside a rally suggests informed participants are buying protection.

### Falling Estimates

Current quarter EPS estimate: $1.21, down 16.5% in 30 days and 12.7% in 90 days. Next quarter: $0.66, down 32.6% in 30 days. These declines reflect analyst models that haven't fully incorporated the May rate spike, but also reflect the difficulty of estimating spot-market earnings given the company's recent pattern of misses.

## Conclusion

Dorian LPG is a high-quality operator in a record rate environment. The balance sheet is strong, the fleet is young, and the geopolitical backdrop supports elevated rates for an uncertain duration. At $42.48, the stock has already doubled and sits at its 52-week high.

Buying a pure spot-market shipper at 52-week highs during a geopolitical disruption with uncertain near-term resolution probability creates asymmetric downside. If Hormuz disruption persists, upside to $50-55 represents 18-30% gain. If it resolves or rates partially normalize, downside to $28-32 represents 25-33% loss. The risk/reward ratio is approximately 1:1.3 (unfavorable) at current prices.

The appropriate posture is to hold existing positions but not initiate new positions at this level. A pullback to $35-37 would restore favorable risk/reward. Alternatively, confirmation that rates are sustained at $100K+ for a full quarter (via earnings or forward booking data) would justify paying the current price for a stock earning $15-20+ annualized EPS.
