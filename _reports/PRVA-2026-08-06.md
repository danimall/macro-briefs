## Executive Summary

Privia Health operates a physician-enablement platform: it recruits independent physicians into single-TIN regional medical groups, holds the payer contracts, supplies technology and administrative services under management services agreements of five to twenty years, and retains a spread the company calls Care Margin. The operating engine works as advertised. FY2025 Care Margin grew 14.4% to $462.2M while cost of platform grew 11.3% and combined sales, marketing and G&A grew 8.3%, producing Adjusted EBITDA growth of 38.8%, roughly 2.7x operating leverage. Q1 2026 continued it: revenue $603.8M (+25.8%), Care Margin $128.7M (+22.2%), Adjusted EBITDA $36.7M (+36.4%), with Platform Contribution Margin improving to 52.1% of Care Margin from 49.1%.

The problem sits below Adjusted EBITDA. The reconciliation is arithmetically exact: FY2025's $125.5M of Adjusted EBITDA less $71.1M of stock-based compensation, $9.9M of D&A and $10.3M of other expense equals precisely the $34.2M of reported operating income. SBC consumed 56.6% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA and 59.7% of Q1 2026's, and unrecognized SBC of $138.7M with a 1.1-year weighted-average recognition period points toward pressure rather than relief. Federal and state NOLs are down to $26.9M and $24.7M, the Q1 2026 effective tax rate was 60.2% on §162(m) limits and equity-settlement shortfalls, and management now guides only 80% of Adjusted EBITDA converting to free cash flow versus the 130% realized in FY2025. Consensus adjusted EPS has been cut 11.5% for the current year and 8.3% for next year over 90 days even though every profit line of guidance was reiterated; the cuts are happening below the operating line, in tax and share count, not in the business.

That combination — a genuinely improving operating platform whose GAAP and cash economics are being progressively taxed by equity compensation, dilution and a normalizing tax rate — produces a fair-value band rather than a mispricing. At 17.2x EV to the $150M midpoint of guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA the shares are not demanding; at 47x EV to Adjusted EBITDA less SBC and a 4.0% free-cash-flow yield on guided FCF, they are not cheap. Base case $27.50 (+16%), bull $34 (+43%), bear $18.50 (-22%). With a Q2 print within days carrying a ±18% implied move, an 8.0% put skew, and near-term implied volatility of 72.8% against 31.9% realized, this is a position to hold rather than one to build. Fifth consecutive HOLD, with the overall score marked down from 6.0 to 5.65 on management/governance evidence and near-term event risk, not on operating deterioration.

## Company Overview

Privia is a management services organization, not a provider-employer and not an insurer. Physicians join a regional Privia Medical Group as member-owners while continuing to own their underlying affiliated practices, which supply the medical group with space, staff and equipment. Privia owns a majority of certain medical groups and none of the "Non-Owned" groups; in states whose corporate-practice-of-medicine statutes bar corporate ownership, the group is a "Friendly Medical Group" owned by physicians or a licensed physician in a Privia leadership role. Six such entities — West Texas, Tennessee, Washington, South Carolina, Indiana and Arizona — are consolidated as VIEs with combined carrying value of about $19.1M.

Scale as of Q1 2026: 5,535 implemented providers (from 4,305 at YE2023), 1,606,000 attributed lives (from 1,120,000), and Practice Collections of $914.8M in the quarter against $3,470.5M for FY2025. Ten owned ACOs. The May 2026 press release cites presence in 24 states plus DC, 1,300+ practices and 5.9M+ patients — a broader footprint definition than the medical-group market list, reflecting ACO and Care Partners participation beyond full-market builds.

Two structural features shape the financials. First, at-risk Medicare Advantage capitation is booked gross because Privia is the principal arranging care, which inflates reported revenue relative to company economics: capitated revenue grew 44.8% in FY2025 to $308.5M, which is the arithmetic reason Care Margin fell from 23.3% to 21.8% of revenue while Platform Contribution Margin against Care Margin rose from 48.4% to 50.8%. Second, the platform depends on athenahealth's EMR (Privia is the customer, not the owner), with practices converted from approximately 50 different source EMR systems. Net PP&E was $384 thousand at 3/31/26; the balance sheet is receivables, cash and intangibles.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2025 revenue $2,122.8M (+22.3%), operating income $34.2M, net income attributable to PRVA $22.9M, diluted EPS $0.178. Q1 2026: revenue $603.8M, operating income $7.4M, net income $3.1M against $4.2M a year earlier — GAAP net income fell year over year despite Adjusted EBITDA rising 36%, entirely because of the tax line ($5.6M provision on $9.3M pretax, a 60.2% rate, versus 25.8% in Q1 2025).

The FY2024 revenue growth figure of 4.7% is a presentation artifact, not a demand event. Management renegotiated capitation agreements in Q1 2024 following Medicare Advantage utilization headwinds, and the restructured contracts moved to net presentation under shared savings. Any model built on headline revenue CAGR misreads 2024 in both directions.

**Operating leverage.** The mechanic is measurable and repeated: FY2025 cost of platform fell from 56.2% to 54.7% of Care Margin; S&M grew 2.6%; G&A grew 9.5%. Q1 2026 broke the pattern in one place — G&A rose 30.7%, the fastest line, of which $13.4M was stock-based compensation. Adjusted EBITDA margin on Care Margin reached 27.2% for FY2025 and 28.5% in Q1 2026.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $163.4M equals 130% of Adjusted EBITDA and 7.1x GAAP net income, with de minimis capex, so FCF ≈ $163M. Composition matters: MD&A attributes the improvement largely to a $65.6M provider-liability build versus $32.9M in FY2024, a $32.7M swing that is a working-capital source rather than earnings. Q1 operating cash flow was negative $49.5M (2026) and negative $24.1M (2025), with accounts receivable up $112.8M in Q1 2026 alone — structural, because shared-savings receivables accrue through the measurement year and settle only after payer reconciliation. Do not annualize Q1 in either direction. Management's own 80%-of-Adjusted-EBITDA conversion guide implies $116-124M of FY2026 FCF, a step down from FY2025's realized rate, plausibly attributable to provider-liability normalization, rising cash taxes, or both; the filings do not adjudicate.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/26: cash $419.5M, no drawn debt, total liabilities $612.0M, total equity $805.9M including $52.8M non-controlling interest. Current ratio 1.61, down from 1.86 at YE2024, driven by the provider-liability build rather than borrowings. The revolver was upsized from $125M to $250M and extended to 2031 on May 6, 2026 at SOFR + 1.25-1.75%, giving roughly $670M of dry powder against covenants restricting additional debt, liens, investments, acquisitions and dividends. An Altman Z of approximately 4.8 sits in the safe zone but is dominated by the market-equity-to-liabilities and asset-turnover terms; the earnings terms contribute roughly 0.09, so the score would compress mechanically on a share-price decline without any business change.

**The number that governs valuation.** Adjusted EBITDA less SBC was $54.5M in FY2025. SBC equalled 15.4% of Care Margin and 30.3% of Platform Contribution. Contingent instruments at 3/31/26 — 7,400,912 options at a $10.38 weighted-average strike and essentially fully exercisable, 4,819,692 unvested RSUs, 2,796,723 PSUs at target with 0-200% payout — total 15,017,327 shares at target, 11.9% of shares outstanding and 10.7% post-issuance ownership dilution. The maximum PSU case (14.2% of shares, 12.4% ownership dilution) is conditional: performance targets for years two and three are set annually at subsequent grant, so it should be probability-weighted, not treated as current capitalization.

**Earnings delivery.** Four quarters: one beat (+96% in Q4 2025), one in-line, two misses (-60.9% in Q2 2025, -73.7% in Q1 2026). The Q1 2026 miss was $0.02 actual against $0.08 estimated and was tax-driven rather than operational. A record of two misses in four quarters on a small-denominator EPS line argues for anchoring on Adjusted EBITDA and Care Margin, and for discounting the precision of consensus EPS.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth rates coexist and they are not interchangeable.

*Reported revenue* grew 22.3% in FY2025 and 25.8% in Q1 2026, flattered by gross-basis capitation and by acquisition timing (the Evolent ACO book closed December 2025, PMG Arizona April 2025, so Q1 2026 carries a full quarter of both against roughly one month and nine months in the comparable base).

*Practice Collections* grew 16.9% in FY2025 and 14.6% in Q1 2026 — the cleanest volume-and-rate measure.

*Same-store proxy* — Practice Collections growth less implemented-provider growth — was +4.6pp for FY2025 (16.9% vs 12.3%) and +1.0pp in Q1 2026 (14.6% vs 13.6%). That deceleration in per-provider productivity, rate and VBC contribution is the most important growth datapoint in the file, though one quarter of a lumpy metric is weak evidence.

FY2026 guidance, reiterated on May 7 after a Q1 beat: implemented providers 5,900-6,000 (+9.7-11.5%), attributed lives raised to 1,600,000-1,625,000, Practice Collections $3,650-3,750M, revenue $2,350-2,450M, Care Margin $515-530M, Platform Contribution $260-270M, Adjusted EBITDA $145-155M (+15.5-23.5%), and no assumed new business development. Backing out Q1 leaves implied Q2-Q4 Adjusted EBITDA of $108-118M against $98.6M in the equivalent 2025 period, or +10-20% versus Q1's +36%. Management chose reiteration over a raise, which is consistent with the 10-Q's own disclosure that Q1's 56.5% shared-savings growth reflected "timing of certain shared savings accruals and settlements" — timing borrows from later quarters rather than adding to the year. Attributed lives at 1,606,000 already sit at the low end of the raised range, but MSSP and MA attribution is largely fixed at January 1, so that is mechanical, not upside.

Estimate revisions are negative across every period: current quarter -8.2%, next quarter -12.8%, current year -11.5% to $0.95, next year -8.3% to $1.10, all over 90 days, with 11 analysts. Profit-line guidance was unchanged, so the cuts are concentrated below the operating line — the 60.2% Q1 tax rate, NOL exhaustion, and rising share count. The operating thesis has not been cut; the equity's per-share earnings power is being eroded by items that will persist regardless of how well the platform executes.

Growth score 6.3: durable low-to-mid-teens Care Margin compounding with a real operating-leverage multiplier, offset by decelerating per-provider uplift, guidance implying second-half deceleration, and uniformly negative revisions.

## Competitive Landscape

The trajectory is favorable in a niche where several peers have retrenched. Privia bought Evolent Health's accountable care business in December 2025, adding over 120,000 attributed lives across MSSP, commercial and MA. Privia was the buyer; Evolent was the seller. Goodwill rose $68.2M and net intangibles $106.1M across FY2025, with gross intangibles at 3/31/26 dominated by payer contracts of $167.4M and an MSO service agreement of $51.8M on a 17.2-year weighted-average life — a long amortization tail that suppresses GAAP earnings while being added back to Adjusted EBITDA. Consideration for the Evolent transaction is not disclosed in the sections available, so acquisition-return discipline cannot yet be assessed; total FY2025 investing outflow was $181.6M covering both Evolent and PMG Arizona.

The moat is market-level scale in payer negotiation plus operational execution in EMR conversion, credentialing and VBC infrastructure — replicable by well-capitalized competitors, not proprietary. The 10-K is candid about a two-sided squeeze. Payers have developed their own managed-services tools and are vertically integrating with providers, gaining negotiating leverage and pricing-data access, while commercial rate contracts run only one to three years, and those same payers are 62% of revenue. On the physician side, Privia competes with health systems, independent practice-management companies, insurers and private-equity-backed platforms for the same doctors. CMS publishes ACO and clinician quality performance, so relative underperformance on metrics is publicly visible and directly competitive.

Two disclosed frictions deserve tracking. FFS-administrative services revenue declined 2.6% in Q1 2026 ($32.3M → $31.4M) while every other category grew double digits, and contractual rebates to MSA customers nearly doubled to $3.5M in FY2025 from $1.8M. The benign reading is conversion of Non-Owned groups into consolidated groups, reclassifying an administrative fee into gross patient-care revenue. The less benign reading is concession-giving to health-system MSA partners. The amount is immaterial in isolation ($0.85M); it matters only as a possible early indicator of pricing pressure in exactly the channel where the 10-K identifies its sharpest market-level cliff risk.

Competitive position 6.3: share gaining, footprint expanding, consolidating a distressed niche, but with worsening counterparty concentration and a moat resting on execution rather than technology.

## Risk Assessment

**Structural (CPOM/Stark).** The 10-K states that if state corporate-practice or fee-splitting doctrines are successfully applied, "agreements between the Company and physicians may be considered void and unenforceable, our MSAs and management fees could be adversely affected, and we may be required to restructure." Six consolidated Friendly Medical Group VIEs exist precisely because Privia cannot own those groups. The model also depends on Stark Law exceptions, and Stark is strict liability with refund obligations for amounts received on prohibited referrals. Low probability, high severity, and not obviously priced.

**Unquantified estimate change.** FY2025 MD&A attributes the 20.0% Platform Contribution increase to organic and inorganic growth "and a change in estimate related to our Shared Savings accrual," without quantifying it. Platform Contribution rose $39.2M and Adjusted EBITDA $35.1M year over year. If a material portion of that reflects a non-recurring accrual revision, underlying improvement was smaller than reported — and it would explain why guidance was reiterated rather than raised after a Q1 beat. The amount may be disclosed in the Item 8 revenue footnote, which was not retrieved. Treat the size as unknown, not as established materiality.

**Payer concentration, worsening.** Q1 2026: Payer A 25% of revenue (27% prior year), B 20% (17%), C 17% (15%) — top three 62%, up from 59%. On receivables: A 51% (up from 48%), B 18%, C 13% — 82% of AR. Accounts receivable of $513.7M is 36% of total assets. Revenue *mix* diversified (VBC 26.3% → 29.0%) while direct counterparty concentration deteriorated; these measure different things and mix diversification does not reduce counterparty risk. The mitigant is that provider liability ($518.6M) slightly exceeds AR, so most collections are pass-through and net working-capital exposure is far smaller than a 78-day DSO implies — but the offset is economic, not legal. Privia owes providers whether or not a payer pays.

**Capitation and MA cost trend.** Capitated revenue grew 44.8% in FY2025 to $308.5M against stop-loss coverage the company says it "generally ha[s] little ability to increase during the terms of our VBC arrangements." Q1 2026 prior-year claims development was +$0.435M unfavorable versus -$0.954M favorable in Q1 2025 — only about 0.6% of an ~$79M opening reserve, but a directional reversal in the right early-warning metric, in an industry that already forced Privia to renegotiate capitation contracts in Q1 2024.

**Regulatory.** RADV: CMS extrapolates audit findings across an entire contract, DOJ has intervened in FCA litigation over risk scores, and a plan "may seek repayment from us, our Medical Groups or our ACOs." The February 2023 RADV rule was vacated, replacing a known adverse methodology with an unknown one. Newly present or expanded in the FY2025 10-K: OBBBA changes to ACA subsidies and Medicaid eligibility; *Loper Bright* raising expected litigation against HHS/CMS guidance; CMS implementing prior authorization in Medicare starting 2026; an expanded AI risk block; tariffs on medical supplies; non-compete enforceability uncertainty. MSSP benchmark ratcheting — an ACO that lowers cost of care has its future benchmark reduced — remains the largest exogenous variable in the shared-savings line, and the 10-K concedes "it is unclear whether VBC arrangements will ultimately achieve their aims."

**Vendor and partner cliffs.** Single-EMR dependence on athenahealth is named explicitly, including future price increases. In Non-Owned markets, termination by a majority-owner health system "may restrict our ability to continue operations in that market," possibly with contractual limits on retaining providers — a market-level cliff invisible in the reported metrics.

**Tax and dilution.** Q1 2026 ETR 60.2% on §162(m) limits and equity-settlement shortfalls; NOLs down to $26.9M federal and $24.7M state with pre-2018 federal NOLs consumed in 2025. Cash taxes paid in Q1 were only $63K, so the near-term cash effect is muted, but book and cash taxes should converge from here. Contingent dilution of 10.7% ownership at target.

**Governance.** Classified board with staggered three-year terms, no stockholder written consent, no stockholder-called special meetings, removal only for cause, blank-check preferred, §203-equivalent provision — a complete anti-takeover package five years past IPO. All five incentive-plan performance measures (Practice Collections, Care Margin, Implemented Providers, Attributed Lives, Adjusted EBITDA) are non-GAAP or volume-based; the selected measure excludes the largest single cost. Proxy TSR index 68.23 versus peer group 95.45 at FY2025, having trailed the selected peer group in every year 2021-2025 and remaining below the 100 base. FY2025 PEO compensation actually paid rose to $12.4M from $8.20M in FY2024. A defensible counter-reading: because GAAP revenue is inflated by gross-basis capitation, GAAP metrics are genuinely poor proxies for company economics, and Care Margin and Platform Contribution are the nearest analogues to net revenue and segment profit. Both readings hold; the alignment gap is nonetheless quantifiable at 56.6% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA.

**Unresolved.** An 8-K dated 2026-07-07 under Item 5.02 (departure/election of directors or officers) could not be retrieved. Prior Item 5.02 filings on 2025-07-08 and 2025-03-07 suggest a possible annual cadence, which would make it routine, but the content is unknown and the CFO filed sales on July 2, 7 and 9. Flagged as a verification gap, not as evidence.

Risk score 4.2: the density of specific, non-boilerplate items — structural CPOM/Stark exposure, an unquantified accrual change, single-payer receivable concentration at 51%, capitation cost trend, RADV methodology vacuum, and an imminent binary print — exceeds what a 17x forward EV/EBITDA multiple compensates.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $34 (+43%)

Care Margin compounds at 12-15% as implemented providers reach 5,900-6,000 and attributed lives pass 1.6M, while in-market and corporate infrastructure grows at half that rate, delivering Adjusted EBITDA at or above the $155M guidance high and roughly $180-190M in FY2027. Privia is the consolidator in a fragmented, distressed niche: it bought Evolent's ACO book, has $670M of dry powder after the revolver upsize, and guidance explicitly excludes new business development, so any additional market entry or ACO acquisition is incremental. The asset-light model (net PP&E of $384K) means growth requires no capital, and 96% claimed provider retention under multi-year agreements plus 90%+ rolling-forward collections predictability underpin visibility. SBC intensity is already falling as a share of Adjusted EBITDA (62.7% in 2024 → 56.6% in 2025) and should continue to as the denominator grows. On $185M FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA at 22x EV, plus roughly $450M of net cash against 134M shares, the equity is worth about $34. Analyst mean of $31.89 and high of $40 bracket that.

### Bear Case — $18.50 (-22%)

The Q1 shared-savings acceleration (+56.5%, attributed by the company to accrual and settlement timing) reverses in Q2 or Q3, and the unquantified FY2025 shared-savings estimate change proves to have flattered the FY2025 base — meaning the repeatable rate of Adjusted EBITDA improvement is materially below 38.8%. Per-provider uplift, already down from +4.6pp to +1.0pp, goes flat as commercial rate inflators lapse and payers press their vertical-integration leverage. Medicare Advantage cost trend deteriorates again, and the +$0.435M unfavorable prior-year development becomes a pattern against limited stop-loss headroom. Cash taxes begin tracking a 30%+ book rate with NOLs gone, FCF conversion undershoots the 80% guide, and SBC recognition steps up toward the $138.7M/1.1-year disclosure, holding GAAP earnings near zero while share count grows 4-5% annually. Consensus, currently anchored on $1.10 for FY2027, converges toward the $24 analyst low. At 12x EV on $160M of FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA plus net cash, that is roughly $18.50 — just below the 52-week low of $18.92.

**Which side has the better evidence?** The operating platform is confirmed and improving; the per-share economics are confirmed and deteriorating. Neither dominates, and the single most decisive unknown — the magnitude of the FY2025 shared-savings estimate change — is not in the retrieved record. That is why this stays a HOLD rather than resolving in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold). Base case $27.50, +15.6% from $23.79, 12 months.**

*Base-case math:* FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of $177M (FY2026 midpoint $150M growing 18%, at the upper half of the guided FY2026 range extended one year), at 18x EV = $3,186M, plus roughly $500M of net cash (current $419.5M plus one year of guided FCF less NCI buyouts), divided by 134M shares (current 126.0M plus RSU/PSU/option settlement at roughly the observed 3-4% annual pace) = $27.50. The same figure is reached at 25x consensus FY2027 adjusted EPS of $1.10.

*Bull $34 (+43%)* and *bear $18.50 (-22%)* as derived above. Risk/reward from spot is roughly 0.7:1 to base and 2:1 to bull against bear — adequate to hold, insufficient to add ahead of a print priced for ±18%.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q2 2026 print showing Adjusted EBITDA guidance raised (not reiterated) with shared-savings revenue growth sustained on a normalized basis, confirming Q1 was not timing.
2. Quantification of the FY2025 shared-savings estimate change at an immaterial amount, plus a second consecutive quarter of Platform Contribution Margin above 52% of Care Margin.
3. Price in the $19-20 area with FY2026 guidance intact — approximately 13-14x EV to guided Adjusted EBITDA, at which point the SBC-adjusted multiple compresses toward 35x and the FCF yield exceeds 5%.
4. A buyback authorization funded from the cash position that meaningfully offsets the 10.7% contingent ownership dilution, combined with open-market insider purchases.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut, or Care Margin guidance ($515-530M) trimmed.
2. Prior-year unfavorable claims development exceeding roughly 3% of opening capitation reserves in any quarter, or renegotiation of capitation contracts on adverse terms as in Q1 2024.
3. A payer dispute, audit recoupment, or settlement delay affecting Payer A, which holds 51% of $513.7M of receivables.
4. Full-year 2026 operating cash flow tracking below roughly 60% of Adjusted EBITDA, indicating the provider-liability tailwind has reversed rather than normalized.
5. Adverse CPOM/fee-splitting or Stark development in a state hosting one of the six Friendly Medical Group VIEs.

**Next reassessment: the Q2 2026 print, due within days** (last year's Q2 landed August 7). Secondary checkpoints: quantification of the shared-savings estimate change and Evolent consideration in the Q2 10-Q, resolution of the July 7 Item 5.02 8-K, and Q3 evidence on whether Q1's shared-savings timing benefit reverses.

## Conclusion

Privia's operating model is working: Care Margin compounding at low-to-mid teens, cost of platform and overhead growing at half that rate, Adjusted EBITDA up 38.8% in FY2025 and 36.4% in Q1 2026, an asset-light balance sheet with $419.5M of cash, no drawn debt and a freshly upsized $250M revolver to 2031. The company is buying assets from retreating competitors rather than selling its own, and guidance excludes further acquisitions.

What keeps this a HOLD is the wedge between that operating result and shareholder economics. Stock-based compensation consumed 56.6% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA and 59.7% of Q1 2026's, with $138.7M unrecognized over a 1.1-year weighted average; contingent instruments represent 10.7% post-issuance ownership dilution; NOLs are nearly exhausted against a 60.2% Q1 effective tax rate; guided FCF conversion steps down from 130% to 80%; and consensus adjusted EPS has been cut 8-13% across all forward periods even as operating guidance held. Add worsening payer concentration (62% of revenue, 82% of receivables, one payer at 51% of AR), an unquantified FY2025 shared-savings estimate change embedded in the profit-growth base, five consecutive years of TSR below the company's own selected peer group alongside a complete anti-takeover package and non-GAAP-only incentive metrics, and dense insider selling by the CEO, CFO and largest option-holding director into July highs with no offsetting purchases.

The score moves from 6.0 to 5.65 on the management/governance evidence and the elevated near-term event risk, not on any deterioration in the platform. At 17.2x EV to guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA the shares are reasonably priced against the metric management is measured on; at 47x EV to Adjusted EBITDA less SBC and a 4.0% FCF yield, they are fully priced against the metric shareholders realize. Hold existing positions through the print, do not add ahead of an 18% implied move, and require either a guidance raise with the shared-savings question resolved, or a price near $19-20, before committing new capital.
