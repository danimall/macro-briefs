## Executive Summary

Schrödinger Inc. is a computational chemistry company that combines physics-based molecular simulation with machine learning to enable drug discovery. The company licenses its platform to all top 20 pharmaceutical companies and operates its own drug discovery pipeline through collaborations with Novartis, BMS, and Lilly. Despite strong scientific credentials and a validated platform (evidenced by the $150M Novartis upfront payment in late 2024), the business remains structurally unprofitable with an accumulated deficit of $629M and no clear path to sustained operating profitability within a reasonable investment horizon.

The stock has declined 44% over the past year, and the most recent Q1 2026 earnings report (May 5) showed an EPS miss of -$0.81 vs. consensus of -$0.65. Consensus FY2026 estimates have deteriorated 83% over the past 90 days, moving from -$1.23 to -$2.25. Net dollar retention fell from 113% to 100% in FY2025, removing the organic expansion narrative that previously supported the software valuation. With 26% short interest and the stock trading near all-time lows, the recent fundamental deterioration does not support the bull case.

## Company Overview

Schrödinger was founded in 1990 and operates two business segments. The **Software segment** (~78% of FY2025 revenue) licenses computational chemistry tools — including FEP+ for free energy calculations, Glide for virtual screening, LiveDesign for enterprise informatics, and AutoDesigner for molecule generation — to pharmaceutical and materials science companies. The **Drug Discovery segment** (~22% of revenue) provides computational services to collaborators and advances proprietary clinical candidates.

The platform's differentiation comes from physics-based simulation methods that do not require training data, unlike pure ML approaches. With 850 employees (363 holding Ph.D.s), the company maintains deep scientific expertise. Major collaborations include Novartis ($150M upfront + up to $2.27B in milestones), BMS ($55M upfront + $482M remaining milestones), and Lilly (up to $420M in milestones).

The proprietary pipeline includes SGR-1505 (MALT1 inhibitor, Phase 1 for B-cell malignancies with orphan drug and fast track designations) and SGR-3515 (Wee1/Myt1 inhibitor, Phase 1 with data expected Q2 2026). A third candidate, SGR-2921 (CDC7 inhibitor), was discontinued in August 2025 after contributing to two patient deaths.

**Profitability:**

Operating losses remain massive and persistent:
- FY2025: -$166.9M operating loss (-65% margin)
- FY2024: -$209.3M operating loss
- FY2022: -$149.2M operating loss

R&D spending of $173.1M in FY2025 (68% of revenue) reflects the capital-intensive nature of platform development and drug discovery. SG&A of $95.4M adds further drag. Gross margins of 55.7% are reasonable for software but far below peer levels for pure SaaS companies.

**Balance Sheet:**

Cash and marketable securities of $402.3M provide adequate runway. Current ratio of 2.75 and positive FCF of $12.5M in FY2025 reduce near-term liquidity risk. The company also maintains a $241M ATM equity facility. However, with $628.8M in accumulated deficit and no visibility on when operating losses cease, the cash balance is being depleted over time when adjusting for stock-based compensation (which the positive OCF obscures).

**Cash Flow Quality Concern:** The $12.5M positive FCF in FY2025 was likely boosted by the $150M Novartis cash receipt (January 2025) flowing through working capital while being recognized over a multi-year period in the income statement. Normalized FCF, absent such lumpy payments, is likely negative by $50-80M annually.

## Valuation Assessment

At $13.28 with 65.6M shares outstanding, the market cap is approximately $860M. With $402M in cash, the enterprise value is roughly $460M.

**Valuation approach considerations:**
- Trailing P/E: negative (not meaningful)
- Forward P/E: negative (not meaningful)
- EV/Revenue: 1.8x on FY2025 revenue of $255.9M
- EV/ACV: 2.3x on $198.5M ACV (4.3x including full market cap)

For a loss-making software company with 4-12% ACV growth, these multiples suggest the market is pricing in eventual profitability at a reasonable but not demanding level. The floor valuation is approximately the $402M cash balance + some multiple on the software business + option value on drug discovery milestones.

The problem is the path: at current burn rates (normalized $50-80M/year cash consumption), the company erodes $100-160M of cash over the next two years without a major milestone payment. The "cheap on cash" argument assumes either (a) profitability or (b) value-realization events (drug partnerships, milestone payments) that are uncertain.

Peers trading at positive earnings multiples (MD at 10.8x, ADUS at 17.8x) highlight that Schrödinger's negative earnings make direct comparison difficult. NEO, also loss-making, trades at a similar relative discount to its 52-week high (-34% vs. SDGR's -52%).

**The analyst consensus target of $21.13 (mean) implies 59% upside, with a low of $13.00 (essentially current price) and a high of $30.00.** The 6 Buy / 2 Hold consensus is bullish despite the stock's decline, which either represents a stale consensus that hasn't adjusted for Q1's miss or genuine conviction in the long-term platform.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- All top 20 pharma companies are customers (complete market penetration at the top)
- 96% gross retention rate demonstrates product stickiness
- 35 years of physics-based platform development creates deep scientific moat
- Novartis $2.27B potential deal validates platform at the highest level
- Physics-based methods don't require training data (advantage in novel chemical space)

**Weaknesses/Threats:**
- Net dollar retention declining (113% → 100%) suggests expansion limits
- AI/ML competitors (Insilico Medicine, Recursion, XtalPi, etc.) are rapidly improving
- Pure computational approaches may converge with physics-based methods as compute/data scale
- Dependence on Columbia University licensing for core IP
- Only 4% organic ACV growth in FY2025

The competitive position is stable but not improving. The company's moat is deep historically but faces pressure from AI-native competitors that may not need 35 years of physics development if empirical ML approaches reach sufficient accuracy with scale.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Perpetual Operating Losses:** Six years post-IPO with $629M accumulated deficit and no visibility on profitability. Annual cash consumption of $50-80M (normalized) erodes runway.

2. **Accelerating Estimate Deterioration:** -83% revision on FY2026 and -49% on FY2027 estimates over 90 days.

3. **Net Dollar Retention Decline:** 113% → 100% signals customers are not expanding usage. If this breaks below 100%, the software business is contracting organically.

4. **Clinical Pipeline Credibility:** SGR-2921's safety signal (two deaths) raises questions about the platform's predictive toxicology capabilities. The remaining two candidates are early-stage with high failure probability.

5. **AI Disruption Threat:** The company's own risk factors acknowledge increasing competition from AI-native drug discovery platforms. If ML approaches achieve parity without physics-based simulation, Schrödinger's moat narrows substantially.

6. **26% Short Interest (10.9M shares, 9.8 days to cover):** This level of bearish conviction from sophisticated investors is a structural negative signal. While it creates squeeze potential, the sustained high SI reflects informed skepticism about the business trajectory.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely August 2026) represents the next data point on ACV trajectory and cash burn rate. SGR-3515 data readout (Q2 2026) is binary — positive data would hurt the short, negative data would confirm pipeline risk. The broader catalyst is the ongoing deterioration in estimate revisions and the Q1 miss establishing a pattern after breaking a beat streak.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. The next earnings report (August 2026) will reveal whether Q1's miss was an anomaly or the start of accelerating losses. ACV trajectory will be critical.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 26.2% SI and 9.8 days to cover, this is already a crowded short. The stock is also near all-time lows ($10.95 52-week low). Squeeze risk is elevated — any positive surprise (partnership announcement, positive clinical data, large milestone payment) could trigger a violent short-covering rally. The June 2025 spike from $20 to $27.63 and subsequent crash to $21.43 in one day demonstrates this stock's capacity for sharp moves.

**Borrow and options signal:** High SI typically implies elevated borrow costs. The very low P/C OI ratio (0.13) suggests the short thesis is expressed through equity rather than options, meaning covering pressure comes through share purchases rather than options unwinds — this creates more mechanical squeeze potential.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** With the stock at $13.28 and the bull case at $20-25 (50-88% upside), vs. bear case of $7-9 (33-47% downside), the risk/reward for a new short entry is approximately 1:1.5 to 1:2.5 against the short (upside risk > downside reward from these levels). Combined with 26% SI creating squeeze risk, the asymmetry does not favor an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis directionally favors continued weakness (deteriorating estimates, Q1 miss, NDR decline, AI competition), but the stock is already near all-time lows, short interest is extreme at 26%, and the risk of a partnership announcement or positive clinical data readout generating a 30-50% squeeze from these levels makes the reward:risk unacceptable for new short initiation. The correct expression is avoidance rather than active shorting.

## Conclusion

Schrödinger possesses genuine scientific differentiation and validated platform value (Novartis $150M upfront, 96% gross retention, all top 20 pharma as customers). However, the investment case requires accepting persistent operating losses of $100-170M annually with uncertain path to profitability, deteriorating organic growth signals (NDR declining, ACV growing only 4% in FY2025), accelerating negative estimate revisions, and increasing AI/ML competitive pressure that threatens the company's core value proposition.

The stock's 44% decline from 52-week highs and proximity to all-time lows ($10.95) might suggest opportunity, but the fundamental trajectory supports the decline: Q1 2026 missed estimates, FY2026 EPS estimates have deteriorated 83% in 90 days, and the company is explicitly pivoting away from self-funded clinical development (reducing future optionality). At 26% short interest with 9.8 days to cover, sophisticated investors are expressing sustained conviction in continued deterioration.

The appropriate stance is avoidance. The company could be worth significantly more if (a) the software business reaccelerates, (b) clinical programs find partners, and (c) AI proves complementary rather than competitive — but these are conditional on multiple uncertain developments over a multi-year horizon. The current price does not offer sufficient margin of safety given ongoing $50-80M annual cash consumption and no profitability visibility.
