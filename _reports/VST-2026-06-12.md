**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vistra is the largest competitive (merchant) power generator in the United States, operating ~44,000 MW across five ISO/RTO markets, with the strategic asset being its 6,448 MW nuclear fleet (Comanche Peak in ERCOT; Perry, Davis-Besse, and Beaver Valley in PJM). The integrated retail-plus-generation model (roughly 5 million retail customers used as a natural hedging channel for owned generation) produces more stable cash flows than a standalone IPP. The company sits at the center of the AI-power demand thesis alongside Constellation and NRG, and has converted that thesis into contracted cash flow: ~3,800 MW of 20-year power purchase agreements with Meta (2,609 MW from PJM nuclear) and AWS (1,200 MW from Comanche Peak). On these sell-side contracts, Vistra owns the plants and supplies carbon-free baseload to creditworthy hyperscalers, de-risking a slice of output with long-dated off-take rather than exposing the company to equity-side AI speculation.

The fundamental story has not deteriorated, but the stock has. VST trades at $146.38, down 12.2% over the period covered here and roughly 33% below its $219.82 52-week high. Q1 2026 was operationally strong: revenue $5,640M (+43% YoY), Adjusted EBITDA $1,475M (+21%), operating cash flow $1,199M (doubled YoY). Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance and projects $10B+ cash generation through 2027. S&P and Fitch both granted investment-grade ratings (BBB-) in late 2025/early 2026, triggering full collateral release on secured notes in April 2026, a structural credit milestone that lowers cost of capital. The widely-circulated low Altman Z-Score (~0.76) is a false distress signal for a leveraged merchant generator and should not drive scoring.

We rate VST a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The structural case is real and the price decline on intact fundamentals improves the risk/reward versus three months ago. What keeps us from a BUY is the concentration of near-term binary risk against a thesis that is reflexively dependent on hyperscaler capex durability. The options market is pricing imminent stress (64% near-term IV in backwardation, rising put skew at 9.1%), the AI-financing complex broadly is being stress-tested for the first time this cycle, leverage sits near 3.4x debt/equity heading into the Cogentrix close, and the four-quarter earnings record (one beat, three misses on GAAP) reflects the MTM noise that makes any single print hard to underwrite. The combination of an unusually rich, backwardated vol surface and a thesis tied to AI-capex sentiment that is actively being repriced is the specific, falsifiable reason we will not chase the long here at a 6.3 composite.

## Company Overview

Vistra operates two integrated halves. **Generation** spans ~43,641 net MW across ERCOT (Texas, ~46% of capacity), PJM/ISO-NE/MISO/NYISO (East, ~51%), and CAISO (West, ~3%). The fuel mix is approximately 62% natural gas, 20% coal, 15% nuclear, and 3% renewable/battery, producing more than 230 TWh annually. **Retail** serves ~5 million customers, anchored by TXU Energy's 2.6 million Texas accounts plus Ambit, Dynegy, Energy Harbor, and other brands.

The competitive moat is the integrated model: the retail book provides a natural sales channel to hedge generation output, dampening commodity-price volatility relative to a pure-play generator or retailer. The nuclear fleet provides 24/7 carbon-free baseload that hyperscalers are now contracting for at scale, and it cannot be replicated on any near-term timeline given licensing and construction constraints. Three of the four nuclear sites (Perry, Davis-Besse, Beaver Valley) came via the 2024 Energy Harbor acquisition.

By Q1 2026 segment, the East has become the profit engine: Adjusted EBITDA of $801M (PJM capacity prices plus the Lotus acquisition), versus Texas $586M, Retail $68M, and West $56M. This shift toward PJM capacity and nuclear is where the data-center demand and the hyperscaler PPAs concentrate.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results (period ended 3/31/2026):** Net income $1,029M versus a $(268)M loss; diluted EPS $2.87 versus $(0.93).

GAAP earnings are mark-to-market-distorted noise. Vistra hedges close to 100% of near-term generation, so GAAP net income swings violently with unrealized MTM on commodity derivatives. Q1 2026 carried a net unrealized derivative gain of $739M versus a $615M loss in Q1 2025, a $1.35B swing that drove most of the headline EPS reversal. The same dynamic explains the apparent FY2025 GAAP operating-income decline (~$4.08B to ~$1.9B), which was MTM-driven while Adjusted EBITDA grew. The earnings-surprise record (one beat, three misses across four quarters, including a +112% surprise in Q1'26 and a -77% miss in Q4'25) is a function of this MTM volatility against GAAP estimates, not operational instability. The correct anchor for this company is Adjusted EBITDA and operating cash flow.

**Capital allocation runs aggressively at both ends.** Capex is ~$883M per quarter (including nuclear fuel); the company repurchased $372M of stock in Q1 and pays a growing dividend ($0.229/quarter). The cumulative buyback authorization is $7.75B with ~$1.62B remaining; treasury stock has reached 217.9M shares against ~337M outstanding, evidence of disciplined long-run share-count reduction. The nuclear production tax credit (IRA §45U, $15/MWh through 2032) contributed $220M in 2025, providing a meaningful earnings floor.

**Balance sheet:** ~$19.2B long-term debt against ~$5.6B total equity (including $2.476B preferred), with $41.3B total assets. The resulting ~3.4x debt/equity and the low Z-Score are normal for a leveraged merchant IPP and should not be read as distress. The credit story runs the other direction: S&P upgraded to BBB- in December 2025, Fitch to BBB- in March 2026, and collateral on secured notes and credit facilities was released in April 2026, making the notes effectively unsecured and pari passu. This lowers cost of capital and is a real structural improvement. Liquidity stood at $4.17B at 3/31/26. The April 2026 issuance of $4.0B senior unsecured notes (4.55%–5.55%) refinanced 2027 maturities and Term Loan B-3, removing a near-term refinancing question.

The one watch item is leverage trajectory through the Cogentrix close (2H 2026), which adds ~$1.5B of assumed debt plus 5M shares of dilution at $185. It is manageable, but it pushes the balance sheet in the wrong direction while the equity is being pressed on AI-capex durability concerns.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three pillars, two of which are contracted and one of which is structural.

**Contracted PPAs.** The hyperscaler off-take breaks down as Meta's 2,609 MW (Perry 1,268 MW + Davis-Besse 908 MW + 433 MW of uprates), with deliveries beginning late 2026 and full by YE2027 (uprate power by 2034); and AWS's 1,200 MW from Comanche Peak (delivery Q4 2027, ramping to full by 2032, extendable by an additional 20 years). These are long-dated contracts with creditworthy counterparties that convert merchant nuclear output into predictable, escalating cash flow. This is the durable component of the thesis and distinct from speculative AI sentiment.

**M&A-driven capacity addition.** The Lotus acquisition (closed October 2025, $1.231B, 2,600 MW of gas) is already contributing to East EBITDA. The pending Cogentrix acquisition (announced December 2025; ~$2.3B cash + ~$1.5B assumed debt + 5M shares) adds ~5,500 MW of modern gas across PJM/ISO-NE/ERCOT and closes in 2H 2026 pending FERC/HSR. Combined with planned ~2,000 MW of new dispatchable gas in ERCOT (W-TX peakers ~2028, Coleto Creek repower, upgrades), Vistra is adding the scarce dispatchable capacity that tightening reserve margins demand.

**Structural demand.** Surging load from hyperscale data centers, oilfield electrification, and EVs against tightening ERCOT and PJM reserve margins favors owners of nuclear and dispatchable gas. The May 2026 PJM acceleration of its data-center interconnection / reliability backstop auction to September 2026 is a near-term policy catalyst, and FERC's December 2025 clarification of co-location rules reduces regulatory uncertainty for nuclear-data-center deals.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed. Current-year EPS estimates have risen (+8.1% over 30 days, +10.4% over 90 days to $9.70), consistent with the operational momentum. But next-quarter estimates have fallen sharply (-24.9% over 90 days), and next-year estimates are roughly flat (+2.3%). This is a company whose forward numbers are being marked around by MTM and timing of PPA ramp, with the full-year picture intact but the quarterly path noisy. Growth durability is high on a multi-year horizon and choppy on a quarterly one.

## Valuation Assessment

VST trades at ~24.7x trailing/forward earnings on the reported $2.18 EPS figure, but trailing GAAP EPS is distorted by the MTM accounting described above and is not the right denominator. On consensus current-year EPS of $9.70, the stock trades at ~15.1x; on next-year consensus of $11.05, ~13.2x. Those forward multiples are at or below the peer median P/E of 19.1x (ETR 28.3x, EXC 16.7x, PEG 17.6x, WEC 22.5x, AEP 19.1x), despite Vistra carrying materially higher growth from contracted PPAs and capacity additions than regulated-utility peers.

The peer set is imperfect, since these are regulated utilities while Vistra is a merchant generator, which warrants some discount for commodity and earnings volatility. But a merchant generator with 20-year contracted nuclear off-take, investment-grade ratings, $10B+ projected FCF through 2027, and a substantial buyback trading at ~13-15x forward earnings is not stretched. The dividend yield (~0.66%) is well below peers because Vistra prioritizes buybacks over payout, so income is not the support here.

The analyst dispersion is wide: low target $99, high $320, mean $225, median $227. TD Cowen lowered its target to $230 (from $253) on May 4 while maintaining its rating, still ~55% above the current price. We treat the median $227 as optimistic and the low $99 as a genuine bear scenario (AI-capex disappointment plus commodity-margin compression), not as a tail to dismiss. The current price sits below recent insider/buyback prints of $157–167, meaning the company itself was repurchasing stock above where it trades today.

On balance the valuation is reasonable-to-attractive on forward earnings, with the asymmetry constrained by the binary risks below rather than by a stretched multiple. We score valuation moderately positive (6.5): there is a credible mispricing case relative to forward earnings and contracted cash flow, but it is not the deep, unambiguous discount that would override the near-term risk profile.

## Competitive Landscape

Vistra's competitive position is improving. It owns two scarce asset classes (nuclear baseload and dispatchable gas) at scale in the two markets (ERCOT, PJM) with the tightest reserve margins and the heaviest data-center interconnection queues. The integrated retail model provides a hedging advantage no pure generator can match. The Energy Harbor, Lotus, and pending Cogentrix acquisitions consolidate dispatchable capacity ahead of a demand wave, and the nuclear uprates (+433 MW) and Perry license renewal through 2046 extend the runway of the highest-value assets.

The principal competitive critique, voiced by GE Vernova bulls, is that GE Vernova represents a "purer" AI-power play on the equipment side, capturing turbine and grid demand without commodity-price exposure. That is a fair distinction, but it describes a different business model rather than a deterioration in Vistra's position. Against generator peers (Constellation, NRG), Vistra's mix of nuclear plus gas plus retail and its contracted hyperscaler off-take is competitive. The trajectory of gaining contracted output, consolidating scarce capacity, and achieving investment grade is positive, which supports a 7.5 here.

## Risk Assessment

The risks are specific and material, which is why this dimension scores low (4.8) and why the recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY.

1. **Hyperscaler/PPA execution and AI-capex durability.** The re-rating thesis depends on data-center demand persisting and PPA deliveries ramping on schedule (2027–2034). The broader AI-financing complex is being stress-tested this cycle through equity-side financing strain at multiple AI-adjacent names, debt-funded data-center buildouts, and emerging questions about end-demand durability. If hyperscaler capex slows, the sentiment premium in VST compresses faster than the contracted cash flow would justify, because the stock has become a heavily-followed retail AI-power momentum name.

2. **Commodity/MTM volatility.** Average VaR rose to $469M in Q1'26 from $224M in FY2025. MTM swings make GAAP results erratic quarter to quarter, and the one-beat/three-miss record against GAAP estimates means any single print is hard to underwrite in advance.

3. **Concentrated counterparty credit risk.** A single counterparty represented ~40% ($298M) of wholesale net derivative exposure.

4. **Moss Landing battery fire (Jan 2025).** Vistra wrote off the 300 MW facility (~$400M) plus a 100 MW impairment (~$155M); 350 MW restart is uncertain (mid-2026); EPA cleanup ~$110M; $500M insurance collected; multiple lawsuits outstanding. This is the genuine operational scar and a reminder that the Vistra Zero/battery leg carries real technology and liability risk.

5. **Leverage into Cogentrix.** ~3.4x debt/equity rises with the assumed debt at close, against an equity being pressed on AI-capex concerns.

6. **Litigation and regulatory overhang.** Dorrell antitrust (alleged nuclear-labor wage-fixing, with Constellation and others); Ohio HB6 (inherited, stayed); coal retirements (4,578 MW 2027–2028) and environmental compliance, partially offset by EPA rollbacks.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface is the clearest near-term warning and the proximate reason we hold. Near-term ATM IV is 64.0% against 49.0% one-year historical vol, rich by 15 points, at the 79th percentile within the current chain. The put/call skew is elevated at 9.1% (one-week tenor) with rising put demand, and the P/C OI ratio climbed from 0.92 to 1.10 over the prior nine sessions as the price fell 6.4%. The term structure is in backwardation (64.0% near-term versus 50.4% six-month), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress rather than steady-state uncertainty. This configuration of a rich, backwardated surface with rising put skew on a falling price typically precedes a binary resolution. It does not tell us direction, but it tells us the next few weeks carry concentrated event risk that an entry should respect. Short interest is moderate (5.4% of float, 2.7 days to cover), so this is not a squeeze setup; the options stress reflects genuine hedging demand.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($220–260).** AI-power demand proves durable, the Meta and AWS PPAs ramp on schedule, Cogentrix closes and is integrated, ERCOT/PJM reserve margins tighten further, and the contracted-cash-flow story re-rates the merchant generator toward a quasi-utility-with-growth multiple. On $11+ forward EPS at a ~20–22x multiple appropriate for the growth and contracted off-take, the stock reaches the low-$220s to $260. Investment-grade access and $10B+ FCF fund continued buybacks at accretive prices.

**Bear case ($99–115).** Hyperscaler capex slows or PPA timelines slip, the AI-power sentiment premium compresses, commodity margins normalize lower, and Cogentrix integration plus leverage weigh on the balance sheet. The MTM volatility produces a string of ugly GAAP prints that retail holders extrapolate negatively. On ~$9–10 EPS at a de-rated ~11–12x merchant multiple, the stock falls toward the analyst low of $99, roughly a 32% decline from current. The Moss Landing liabilities and litigation provide additional downside texture.

The base case sits between these. On consensus $9.70–11.05 forward EPS at a ~15–17x multiple that credits the contracted growth but discounts the binary risk and commodity volatility, fair value is ~$165–180, modestly above the current $146.38.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale and a 12-month horizon tied to the Cogentrix close (2H 2026), PPA delivery ramp (late 2026 onward), and the September 2026 PJM reliability-backstop auction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The options term structure normalizes (near-term IV falls back below the six-month tenor) and the AI-capex demand picture stabilizes, confirmed by hyperscaler capex guidance holding and no PPA timeline slippage, while the stock remains below ~$160. A de-rated entry with resolved near-term event risk would tip the risk/reward.
- Cogentrix closes on terms and management reaffirms or raises the $10B+ FCF-through-2027 projection with leverage trajectory intact (net-debt/EBITDA stable).
- Two consecutive quarters of Adjusted EBITDA growth with current-year estimate revisions holding positive and the next-quarter revision trend turning up from the current -24.9%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A hyperscaler defers, renegotiates, or cancels a portion of the Meta/AWS PPAs, or broader hyperscaler capex guidance is cut materially.
- Adjusted EBITDA (not GAAP) declines YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling operational rather than MTM deterioration.
- Cogentrix-related leverage pushes net-debt/EBITDA above the level consistent with the investment-grade ratings, or either rating agency signals a negative outlook.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings, the September 2026 PJM auction, and the Cogentrix close are the natural checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Vistra owns the scarce assets the AI-power demand wave requires and has converted a meaningful slice of that demand into 20-year contracted off-take with creditworthy counterparties. The operational fundamentals are strong on the metrics that matter for this business (Adjusted EBITDA +21%, operating cash flow doubled), the investment-grade upgrade and collateral release are genuine structural improvements, and the 33% drawdown from the 52-week high on intact fundamentals improves the entry relative to three months ago. On forward earnings of ~$9.70–11.05, the ~13-15x multiple is reasonable-to-attractive against a peer median of 19.1x.

We stop short of a BUY for concrete, falsifiable reasons rather than generic caution. The thesis is reflexively tied to hyperscaler capex durability while the AI-financing complex is being stress-tested; the options market is pricing imminent stress through a rich, backwardated vol surface with rising put skew; leverage rises into the Cogentrix close; and GAAP earnings volatility makes the next print hard to underwrite. If these conditions resolve favorably (vol normalizes, PPAs ramp on schedule, Cogentrix closes cleanly), we would tip to a BUY below ~$160. Until then, the asymmetry does not justify chasing the long, and the structural quality does not justify avoiding the name. HOLD, growth-hold, with the upgrade and downgrade triggers above as the monitoring framework.
