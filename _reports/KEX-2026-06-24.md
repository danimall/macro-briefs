**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kirby Corporation operates the largest domestic tank barge fleet in the United States, moving petrochemicals, refined products, and black oil across the inland river system and coastwise routes, complemented by an equipment distribution and manufacturing arm (KDS) that has become a meaningful supplier to the data-center and behind-the-meter power buildout. The company has compounded adjusted EBITDA from $306M in 2021 to $781M in 2025, a ~2.5x climb driven by the post-COVID inland marine recovery and a tightening barge supply that supports pricing power. Q1 2026 confirmed the trend: revenue $844M (+7.4% YoY), diluted EPS $1.50 (+12.8% YoY), with power-generation revenue up 45%. The balance sheet is underleveraged (debt-to-cap 22.3% against a 60% covenant ceiling), and management is reducing the share count by roughly 6% annually through buybacks.

Two developments dominate the current setup, and they pull in opposite directions. On the bull side, the KDS power line has shifted from an oil-services distribution business into a structural-growth picks-and-shovels supplier to the AI/data-center power theme, helping drive the stock +27.6% YTD and re-rating its earnings stream. On the bear side, the March 17, 2026 Jones Act waiver, issued at DoD request, tied to the Iran conflict, and extended at least twice to cover roughly 700 cargoes, erodes the regulatory moat that underpins coastal pricing. The waiver has had limited practical impact so far because foreign spot rates are elevated and inland routes are physically unreachable by blue-water vessels, but a broadening or permanent waiver would structurally impair the coastal franchise.

We rate KEX a HOLD. The fundamentals are solid and the growth story is real, but after a 27% YTD advance the stock trades at roughly 20x forward EPS with estimate revisions that have gone flat, a known near-term fuel-lag margin headwind in Q2, and an unresolved binary regulatory risk that the market cannot currently price. The risk/reward that justified an upgrade to BUY a month ago has narrowed; we step back to HOLD pending resolution of the waiver and confirmation that the Q2 air pocket is transient rather than structural.

## Company Overview

Kirby runs two segments. **Marine Transportation (KMT, ~59% of revenue)** operates 1,124 inland tank barges (25.1M barrels capacity, 1,093 owned), 27 coastal tank barges, and roughly 284 inland towboats plus coastal tugboats. Kirby controls about 28% of the entire U.S. inland tank barge fleet (~1,105 of ~4,004 barges). Cargo mix skews to petrochemicals (47%), black oil (27%), refined products (22%), and agricultural chemicals (4%). Q1 2026 KMT revenue was $497M (+4% YoY) at an 18.0% operating margin.

**Distribution & Services (KDS, ~41% of revenue)** distributes and services engines, transmissions, and power-generation systems, and manufactures specialized equipment including electric fracturing systems and power-generation/data-center gear. Q1 2026 sub-market mix was Commercial & Industrial 46%, Power Generation 44%, Oil & Gas 10%. KDS revenue was $347M (+12% YoY) at a 6.7% operating margin.

The competitive moat rests on scale plus the Jones Act, a cabotage law requiring domestic marine transport to use U.S.-built, U.S.-flagged, U.S.-crewed vessels, which insulates Kirby from cheaper foreign-flagged competition. Secondary advantages include a structurally tight inland barge supply (limited new construction; ~600 barges aged 30+ years and likely to retire), high safety/vetting standards, and exclusive OEM distributorships (EMD, MTU, Allison, Daimler, Thermo King) in KDS.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is the strongest part of the case. Adjusted EBITDA progression — $306M (2021), $410M (2022), $557M (2023), $708M (2024), $781M (2025) — reflects a genuine multi-year cycle recovery rather than a one-quarter spike.

Q1 2026 (filed 5/8/26):
- Total revenue **$844.1M** (+7.4% YoY)
- Net earnings to Kirby **$81.2M** (+6.9%); diluted EPS **$1.50** (vs. $1.33)
- Operating cash flow **$97.7M** (+167% YoY, partly working-capital timing)
- Segment operating margins: KMT 18.0%, KDS 6.7%, both modestly down YoY on fuel-lag and inflationary pressures

The balance sheet supports a high financial-health score. Total debt is ~$986M against $58M cash, with debt-to-cap of just 22.3% versus a 60% covenant ceiling and ample headroom on the ≥2.5x interest-coverage test. Maturities are laddered: $500M 4.2% notes (2028), $300M ~3.5% notes (2033), and a newly amended $750M revolver to March 2031 with $570M available. This is an investment-grade profile with latent capacity for larger buybacks or M&A.

Capital allocation has been disciplined. 2026 capex is guided to $220–260M (of which $170–210M is maintenance), capital-light against the asset base. Kirby repurchased 0.4M shares for $52.7M in Q1 2026 (~$354M in 2025), cutting weighted diluted share count from ~57.0M to ~53.7M, a ~6% reduction that directly lifts EPS. The company pays no dividend; returns flow through buybacks and counter-cyclical secondhand barge acquisitions ($95.8M for 23 barges + 3 towboats in March 2026; $97.3M for 14 barges in March 2025).

The earnings track record reinforces confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, with the most recent (Q1 2026) a 6.6% beat. FCF conversion is healthy when adjusted for working-capital timing. The one near-term wrinkle is management's pre-announced Q2 2026 inland margin compression as rising diesel costs outrun 30–120 day fuel escalators, a knowable, transient quality issue rather than structural deterioration.

## Growth Analysis

Two distinct growth drivers operate here. The marine cycle is supported by structural supply tightness: roughly 4,004 inland barges industry-wide, only ~60–70 new barges ordered for 2026 delivery against ~600 barges aged 30+ years that are retirement candidates, with new-build economics constrained by near-record steel costs. Term contracts are renewing at 0–2% on inland and 19–21% on coastal, and management characterizes coastal conditions as "very favorable." Inland utilization is steady in the low-90s%.

The higher-octane driver is KDS power generation, now ~44% of segment revenue and growing ~45% YoY on data-center, behind-the-meter, backup, and prime-power demand. This repositions KDS from a cyclical oil-services play toward a secular power-infrastructure supplier. Kirby participates as a picks-and-shovels equipment provider and manufacturer (gen-sets, switchgear, electrical distribution gear), not as an AI pure-play, and the OEM engine supply that gates these projects (MTU/EMD) is sourced from third parties. Management flagged delayed OEM engine deliveries pushing some power-gen projects from Q2 2026 into H2 2026, a timing constraint rather than a demand problem.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and tempers the growth score. Current-year EPS has been revised +3.7% over 90 days (to $7.01) and next-quarter +6.7%, but the current quarter has been cut 4.9% (fuel-lag effect) and the 30-day trend is essentially flat across all periods. This is stable-to-modestly-positive momentum, below the >30% revision velocity that would signal systematic underestimation. Growth is real and durable but already largely visible to the market.

## Valuation Assessment

KEX trades at ~$138, roughly 20x forward consensus EPS of $7.01 and ~16.7x next-year $8.28. On trailing EPS of $6.33 the P/E is ~21x. The stock has advanced 27.6% YTD and sits about 12% below its $157.69 52-week high.

Peer comparison is of limited use because the Industrials peer set provided is heterogeneous (P/E range 7.0 to 154.0, median 43.3), and none are direct marine-transport comparables. Against that distorted median Kirby looks cheap, but the more honest read is that KEX trades at a reasonable absolute multiple for a mid-single-digit organic grower with a high-single-digit EPS tailwind from buybacks. That is fair, not asymmetrically cheap.

Two principles temper the valuation score. First, a low or moderate multiple on a cyclical at a relatively elevated point in its cycle (EBITDA up 2.5x since 2021) carries value-trap risk; the trailing earnings power may not be the right anchor if the marine cycle softens or the waiver bites coastal pricing. Second, while estimate revisions are modestly positive, they are flat over 30 days and not running at the velocity that would let analyst targets function as floors. Consensus targets ($158 low, $167.5 median, $175 high) sit 14–27% above the current price and are constructive, but with revisions stalled, those targets function more as anchor than launchpad. A Seeking Alpha downgrade to Hold "on valuation" and Zacks' offsetting upgrade capture the standoff: strong fundamentals, but the easy re-rating money appears largely made. We score valuation modestly below neutral, reflecting that the asymmetry that existed at lower prices earlier this year has compressed.

## Competitive Landscape

Kirby's position within inland marine is dominant and stable, with ~28% fleet share and a structural supply backdrop that favors incumbents. The inland franchise is genuinely defended: foreign blue-water vessels physically cannot serve the river systems, so the bulk of Kirby's revenue is insulated from the Jones Act waiver regardless of its breadth. New coastal ATB construction is zero industry-wide, supporting coastal pricing.

The trajectory question, which is what drives this dimension, is two-sided. KDS power generation is a clear improving line; Kirby is widening exposure to a structural tailwind the market is only beginning to price into a marine-transport multiple. Against that, the Jones Act waiver represents the first credible erosion of the coastal regulatory moat in the company's history, and conventional oilfield (diesel frac) demand within KDS continues to decline as the market shifts to electric frac. Crude/condensate barge demand has structurally shrunk (from ~550 inland barges in 2015 to ~170–180 today). Net, the competitive position is improving in the growth-bearing parts of the business and facing a novel, unresolved threat in coastal: a balanced, mid-range read rather than a clear improvement.

## Risk Assessment

**Jones Act waiver (the dominant idiosyncratic risk).** The DHS waiver permits foreign-flagged vessels to move oil/fuel between U.S. ports, and the extensions (~April 24 and ~May 18, in 90-day increments) cover the roughly 700 cargoes. The 10-K frames the Jones Act as foundational and historically assumed waivers would be brief (7–18 days, disaster-specific). This one is unprecedented in breadth and duration. Mitigants: Kirby reports no material adverse impact yet, foreign spot rates are elevated so actual displacement has been small, and the waiver threatens coastal — not inland — trade. But a broadened or permanent waiver is a genuine structural threat to coastal pricing, and the outcome is binary and politically driven. This is the single highest-variance item in the file and the primary reason the risk score sits at 5.0 rather than higher; it is not yet reflected in the multiple because the market cannot currently handicap it.

**Cyclicality and energy exposure.** Roughly half of KMT revenue is petrochemicals, tied to refinery/petrochemical production volumes and ultimately to natural-gas and crude dynamics. EBITDA up 2.5x since 2021 means the comps are demanding.

**Fuel-cost lag.** Pre-announced Q2 2026 inland margin compression before escalators catch up; knowable and transient, but a near-term earnings-quality wrinkle.

**Operational/labor.** Aging inland lock-and-dam infrastructure, weather delays (3,264 delay days in Q1'26, down 19% YoY), and an acute mariner labor shortage pressuring wages.

**KDS supply gating and oilfield decline.** OEM engine delivery delays pushing power-gen projects into H2 2026; conventional oilfield demand falling.

**Legal.** The 2016 Nathan E. Stewart spill (Heiltsuk First Nation claim, Federal Court of Canada limitation action); long-tail, believed insured, not deemed material by management.

**Customer concentration.** Top 5 KMT customers ~17% of 2025 revenue; manageable.

### Options Market Signal

The options market shows no acute stress. Near-term ATM IV of 32.8% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 36.2% (a -3.4% spread, cheap), with IV in the 9th percentile of its own chain, indicating modest, well-distributed uncertainty rather than fear of an imminent binary. Put/call OI ratio is a very low 0.05 and short interest is light at 3.2% of float (2.0 days to cover), so positioning is not crowded. The one caveat is a 3.1% near-term put/call skew indicating somewhat elevated put demand, and a kink in the term structure: 3-month IV jumps to 39.9% (implied move ±12.3%) before easing to 32.5% at 6 months. That bulge points to the market pricing event risk around the late-summer window, plausibly the next 90-day waiver-extension decision and the Q2/Q3 earnings prints. Cheap near-term IV is mildly favorable for entry timing, but the 3-month bulge corroborates that the waiver is the variable the market is watching.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The marine cycle has multi-year legs given structurally constrained barge supply, with coastal term contracts renewing at 19–21% and inland utilization in the low-90s%. KDS power generation (+45% YoY, ~44% of segment) re-rates the company toward a data-center power-infrastructure supplier. The underleveraged balance sheet (22.3% debt-to-cap) funds continued ~6% annual share-count reduction and counter-cyclical barge M&A, compounding per-share value. The Jones Act waiver expires without broadening, restoring the full coastal moat. Bull target ~$175, consistent with the high analyst target, achievable on continued power-gen momentum and waiver resolution.

**Bear Case.** The Jones Act waiver is extended again or made permanent, structurally impairing coastal pricing, the highest-value contract book in the marine segment. The Q2 fuel-lag compression proves stickier than guided, and the marine cycle rolls over from an elevated EBITDA base. KDS power-gen growth is gated by persistent OEM engine delays, deferring the secular re-rating. In this scenario the stock de-rates toward the high-$90s/low-$100s where it traded as recently as October 2025, roughly 25–30% downside.

**Base Case.** The waiver remains a coastal-only, immaterial overhang that eventually lapses; the marine cycle holds with modest pricing gains; power-gen continues mid-double-digit growth with intermittent OEM delays; buybacks add ~6% to EPS annually. Forward EPS of ~$7.50–8.00 over the next 12 months at a ~19–20x multiple supports a base case around $150–155, modest upside from the current $138.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$152.** ~19x next-twelve-month EPS of ~$8.00 (blending the $7.01 current-year and $8.28 next-year consensus, plus the buyback tailwind), implying ~10% upside from $138.

**Bull case target: ~$175.** Matches the high analyst target; requires waiver resolution plus sustained power-gen acceleration.

**Bear case target: ~$100–105.** ~24–28% downside; triggered by a broadened/permanent waiver or a marine-cycle rollover.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, with the key checkpoints being each 90-day waiver-extension decision, the Q2 2026 print (fuel-lag confirmation), and the Q3 2026 print (power-gen project timing into H2).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. The Jones Act waiver lapses or is formally narrowed to exclude routes Kirby competes on, removing the coastal overhang.
2. Power-generation revenue sustains >30% YoY growth for a second consecutive quarter with OEM delivery delays resolving, alongside a pullback toward ~$120 that restores asymmetry.
3. Estimate-revision velocity turns clearly positive (current-year EPS revised up >5% over 30 days).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. The waiver is broadened to inland routes or made permanent, with management quantifying a material coastal revenue hit.
2. Inland utilization falls below the high-80s% or coastal pricing renewals turn negative, signaling a cycle rollover.
3. Q2/Q3 margin compression proves structural rather than fuel-lag-driven (KMT operating margin below ~15% for two quarters).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release and the next waiver-extension decision in the late-summer window flagged by the options term structure.

## Conclusion

Kirby is a high-quality, well-managed cyclical with a genuinely improving growth mix and a fortress balance sheet, and the prior upgrade to BUY a month ago was reasonable given the power-gen momentum and tight marine supply. New data since then does not reverse the thesis, but it narrows the margin of safety: the stock has run, estimate revisions have flattened, a known Q2 fuel-lag headwind sits ahead, and the Jones Act waiver remains an unresolved, binary threat to the coastal moat that the market cannot price. At ~20x forward EPS the asymmetry that supported a BUY has compressed. The track record of three beats in four quarters and the durable buyback engine keep the downside contained, but with the waiver outcome and the Q2 margin air pocket both unresolved, the classification is a hold rather than a conviction call in either direction. We rate KEX a **HOLD**, with a clear upgrade path on waiver resolution plus a better entry price, and a downgrade path if the waiver broadens or the marine cycle turns.
