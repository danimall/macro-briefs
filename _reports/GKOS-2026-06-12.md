**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Glaukos is an ophthalmic device-and-pharmaceutical company in the middle of a fundamental transition driven by iDose TR, its first-of-its-kind intracameral travoprost implant that delivers up to ~3 years of glaucoma drug therapy from a single procedure. Revenue grew 32% to $507.4M in 2025 and accelerated to a record $150.6M (+41%) in Q1 2026, with U.S. glaucoma up 58% and iDose TR alone contributing roughly $54M in the quarter. Management raised FY2026 guidance to $620-635M (~25% growth). The reported GAAP loss profile is misleading: 2025's headline gross margin decline to 56% from 75% was almost entirely a $112.9M non-cash impairment of the legacy Photrexa intangible booked through COGS upon Epioxa's approval; underlying non-GAAP gross margin runs 82-84% and is rising.

The combination that moves this from a watch-list name to an actionable long is accelerating revenue, declining cash burn (operating cash burn improved from $(61.3)M in 2024 to $(14.8)M in 2025), and a decisive turn in forward estimates. FY27 consensus EPS has flipped to positive $0.48 with +21.7% upward revision over 90 days, and FY26 estimates (current-year loss) narrowed +9.9% over the same window. This estimate-revision-plus-revenue-acceleration profile typically precedes continued re-rating as consensus catches up to a launch curve. The company beat on three of its last four quarters and carries a clean balance sheet (Altman Z 3.02, current ratio 4.69, D/E 0.36, ~$280M cash, converts fully retired).

The recommendation is BUY with disciplined sizing. The thesis is gated by a specific, trackable catalyst: the pace at which the remaining Medicare Administrative Contractors publish professional fees for iDose's temporary CPT codes, which directly throttles utilization. Four of seven MACs had published as of year-end 2025, Palmetto added in March 2026, and the January 2026 FDA approval of unlimited iDose re-administration materially expands the recurring-revenue case. The main offsets are real: near-term options markets are pricing elevated stress (64.5% IV in backwardation, 21.3% put skew), executives sold into Q1 strength, and legacy MIGS volumes face CMS reimbursement headwinds. These cap the score but do not break the thesis.

## Company Overview

Glaukos operates as one reportable segment across three commercial franchises. Glaucoma is ~83% of net sales and includes the iStent family of micro-invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) devices (the category Glaukos created in 2012) plus iDose TR, the central growth driver (FDA-approved December 2023, controlled launch February 2024). Corneal Health is ~17% of sales, anchored by the iLink bio-activated cross-linking platform for keratoconus, where legacy Photrexa ("epi-off") is being superseded by Epioxa ("epi-on," incision-free, FDA-approved October 2025, launched March 2026). The pipeline spans iLution (transdermal), Retina XR (sustained-release intravitreal, GLK-401 in first-in-human), next-gen iDose (TREX, TRIO), and the licensed PRESERFLO MicroShunt.

The strategic frame is "Interventional Glaucoma": shifting treatment earlier and replacing daily eye-drops, where non-compliance exceeds 50%, with durable procedural therapy. iDose TR's principal competitor is AbbVie's Durysta (bimatoprost implant); iDose's multi-year duration and now-approved re-administration are the differentiators. The company holds 500+ owned/licensed patents, employs 1,094 people, and manufactures implantable devices at a single San Clemente, CA campus. U.S. sales are 74% of the total; no customer exceeds 10%.

## Financial Analysis

The top line is the cleanest part of the story. Revenue progressed $282.9M (2022) → $314.7M (2023) → $383.5M (2024) → $507.4M (2025), then accelerated to $150.6M in Q1 2026, +41% reported and +39% constant currency. U.S. glaucoma drove the gains, rising 50% in 2025 to $298.6M and 58% in Q1 2026 to $93.5M, with iDose TR the swing factor at ~$54M in the quarter. International glaucoma grew 18% in 2025 and Corneal Health 8%, both accelerating in Q1 2026 (international glaucoma +23% reported, Corneal Health +15%).

Profitability requires careful reading. GAAP net loss widened to $187.7M in 2025 from $146.4M in 2024, and accumulated deficit reached $933.1M. The gross margin decline was triggered when Epioxa's approval rendered Photrexa's developed technology not fully recoverable; stripping out the impairment, underlying non-GAAP gross margin is ~82-84% (Q1 2026 came in at 83.6% versus 82.4% a year earlier). Operating losses reflect deliberate launch investment: 2025 SG&A of $331.7M (+27%, including ~$11.9M of performance-tied stock comp) and R&D of $150.6M (+10%); total stock-based comp was $63.2M. The trajectory toward breakeven is visible in Q1 2026, where the non-GAAP operating loss narrowed to $(10.5)M from $(15.2)M and non-GAAP EPS improved to $(0.18) from $(0.22). As iDose gross-profit dollars compound against a more stable opex base, the operating-leverage cross-over approaches.

The balance sheet is healthy for a growth-stage medtech. Equity/assets is 0.73; FCF was $(22.5)M and cash rose modestly in Q1 2026. Liquidity is ~$280M in cash and short-term investments; the 2.75% convertible notes were fully retired/exchanged in 2024, leaving long-term "debt" largely a finance lease. Two items bear monitoring. Receivables nearly doubled in 2025 (from $60.7M to $108.6M, then $119.7M in Q1 2026) because Glaukos is offering extended payment terms to seed the iDose launch, a deliberate tactic that flatters reported demand and required a $7.4M doubtful-account allowance. And capex steps up for the >$80M multi-year Huntsville, AL facility beginning construction in 2026. Neither threatens solvency over the next 12+ months absent large M&A, but both consume cash.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three layers. iDose TR is in the early innings of a category-defining launch, and the January 2026 FDA approval permitting unlimited re-administration in patients with a healthy cornea converts a one-time procedure into a recurring-revenue franchise. The legacy iStent MIGS base provides a still-meaningful installed surgeon network and international runway (EU MDR and UK certification for the iStent family in June 2025, with European launches begun). The corneal franchise is upgrading its own standard of care from painful epi-off Photrexa to incision-free Epioxa, launched March 2026 with bio-activation systems already covering ~65% of the U.S. population and a path to ~95%, plus access pathways for >100M commercial lives.

The forward estimate picture is the strongest single confirmation. Pairing 90-day estimate revisions of this magnitude with revenue growth above 40% is the profile that historically precedes further re-rating, because consensus tends to lag a launch ramp rather than lead it. The beat record reinforces confidence: three beats in four quarters (Q1 2026 surprise +35.3%, Q3 2025 +39.1%), with the lone Q4 2025 miss attributable to launch-timing noise rather than demand softness.

Pipeline optionality is not in the base case but is real: iDose TREX Phase 2a showed IOP reductions of 8.6-10.8 mmHg, the iStent infinite mild-to-moderate PMA pivotal completed enrollment in Q4 2025, and Retina XR GLK-401 completed first-in-human enrollment, opening a posterior-segment opportunity that would put Glaukos against pharma giants.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$7.5B market cap on $507M FY2025 sales, GKOS trades at ~15x trailing and ~12x 2026E sales. Trailing P/E is meaningless here: the company is at a cyclical/launch trough with deliberate loss-generating investment, and FY27 EPS only just turned positive at $0.48. The relevant lens is forward revenue multiple against growth and the embedded operating-leverage step-up, plus the trajectory toward GAAP profitability.

Peer comparison is awkward because the Health Care Equipment cohort is heterogeneous and several comps carry distorted or negative earnings (BRKR -235x, BAX -10x, TFX 4,229x). The peer median P/E of 44.3 is not a usable anchor for a pre-profitability name. The better framing: Glaukos is a category-creating ophthalmic platform growing 40%+ with 82-84% underlying gross margins and a clean balance sheet, which supports a premium revenue multiple. ~12x forward sales is full but not extreme for that profile, and it is justified if the iDose ramp delivers the revenue acceleration the guidance and revisions imply.

I do not treat the stock's prior appreciation (+25% over twelve months, recovered from a $73 low) as a valuation negative; that move tracks fundamental improvement (revenue acceleration, margin clarity post-impairment, FY27 EPS turning positive). The relevant question is price versus forward fundamentals today. The average analyst target is ~$156 (median $161), with a $136 low and $170 high, all above the current $124-128. On a name with rising estimates and a forward sales multiple in the low-teens (well below the >50x forward P/E zone where I'd discount targets as circular), I treat consensus targets as a floor that lags reality rather than a ceiling. Valuation scores 5.5, full but not stretched, with the multiple gated by execution on the reimbursement curve.

## Competitive Landscape

Glaukos holds genuinely differentiated positions. It created the MIGS category in 2012 and is the only player with an FDA-approved long-duration intracameral drug-eluting glaucoma implant. In keratoconus it holds the only FDA-approved bio-activated CXL therapy and is upgrading the standard of care to a less painful epi-on procedure. The competitive trajectory is improving in the franchises that matter for growth (iDose, Epioxa) even as the legacy MIGS device base faces reimbursement-driven volume pressure.

That pressure is concrete: Local Coverage Determinations finalized by five of seven MACs in late 2024 confirmed non-coverage of MIGS-on-MIGS combinations, and 2026 Final Rules cut physician fees for cataract/surgical-MIGS Category I codes, producing single-digit declines in non-iDose iStent volumes and pushing some surgeons toward competitor stents (Alcon, Sight Sciences, AbbVie, New World Medical, Iantrek). This is why iDose TR, which carries its own reimbursement pathway, is strategically central. The September 2025 trade-secret suit against SpyGlass Pharma signals both a competitive moat and intensity in next-gen sustained-release glaucoma; the court already granted Glaukos a preliminary injunction (December 2025) finding likely success on the merits, with trial set for October 27, 2026. Competitive position scores 8.0 on the strength of the category leadership and the improving trajectory in the growth franchises, tempered by the legacy MIGS drag.

## Risk Assessment

**iDose reimbursement build-out is the dominant near-term risk and catalyst.** Physician fees for iDose's temporary CPT codes (0660T/0661T) are set MAC-by-MAC (Palmetto added 0660T in late March 2026). Delayed or inconsistent fees throttle utilization directly. This is monitorable: each MAC publication is a discrete, observable event, and the pace is accelerating.

**Legacy MIGS reimbursement headwinds** (MIGS-on-MIGS non-coverage LCDs, 2026 physician-fee cuts) are a structural drag on the cash-cow franchise and have already pushed share to competitors.

**Working-capital and credit quality.** Receivables nearly doubled on extended iDose payment terms; the $7.4M doubtful-account allowance is small but bears watching as the launch scales.

**Other risks:** single-site San Clemente manufacturing (earthquake/fire/sole-source supplier exposure); lapsed Ivantis/Hydrus royalty (high-margin income stream expired April 2025); Medicaid Drug Rebate Program and 340B pricing pressure; Photrexa→Epioxa transition headwinds with Epioxa's permanent J-code (J2789) only effective July 1, 2026 and coverage largely commercial-payer driven; ~$546.9M federal NOLs subject to Section 382 limitation; and new FY2025 10-K risk language on AI/ML regulation, government-shutdown/FDA-funding, and tariffs.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress that diverges from the fundamental thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 64.5% versus 51.5% one-year historical vol (rich by 13 points, 76th percentile within the current chain), the term structure is in backwardation (64.5% near-term vs. 61.7% six-month), and the one-week put/call skew is elevated at 21.3% with an implied ±7.0% move. IV rose 7.1 points since late May even as the price climbed 15%, which indicates the market is pricing an imminent catalyst or hedging event rather than complacency. The likely focal points are the next reimbursement data points and the upcoming earnings cadence. Short interest is modest at 6.4% of float (2.6 days to cover), so this is hedging demand, not a crowded short. For a BUY, the practical implication is to scale in rather than chase, and to size for a near-term drawdown of the implied magnitude.

Risk Factors scores 5.0: the reimbursement uncertainty and legacy-MIGS pressure are specific and partly binary, though they are increasingly priced and the asymmetry tilts favorable as MAC fees publish.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($170-185).** The remaining MACs publish professional fees through 2026, broad commercial/Medicare Advantage coverage solidifies, and iDose re-administration drives recurring volume. Revenue compounds above guidance into FY27, the non-GAAP operating loss flips positive, and GAAP profitability comes into view. Epioxa converts the keratoconus standard of care with its J-code live mid-2026. Pipeline (TREX, iStent infinite mild-to-moderate, Retina XR) provides optionality the market begins to credit. The stock re-rates toward and through the high analyst target of $170 as consensus chases rising estimates. (Note: the $170 high target is the upper bound of disclosed consensus; the $185 figure is an analyst extrapolation beyond published targets.)

**Bear Case ($90-100, ~22-28% downside).** MAC fee publication stalls or commercial coverage lags, capping iDose utilization. Legacy MIGS volumes erode faster than iDose offsets. Extended-payment-term receivables produce a collection problem, and the cash burn re-widens with Huntsville capex. The launch ramp disappoints against the ~25% guided growth, estimate revisions reverse, and the ~12x forward sales multiple compresses. A bear print near $90-100 would mark roughly the late-May 2026 trough.

**Base Case ($150).** iDose reimbursement continues building at the current accelerating pace, FY26 lands within or modestly above the $620-635M guide, the operating-leverage transition plays out as gross-profit dollars outgrow opex, and FY27 turns profitable. The stock works toward the analyst median around $150-161 as the loss narrows and the recurring-revenue case strengthens.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $150** (~18% upside from $127.62). Derivation: ~12x forward sales held flat on FY27 revenue of roughly $775-795M (25% growth off the FY26 guide midpoint of ~$627.5M), supported by the FY27 EPS turn to positive $0.48 and the analyst median of $161. The multiple holds rather than expands because execution risk on reimbursement remains.

**Bull case target: $170-185** (~33-45% upside). Above-guidance revenue, GAAP-profitability visibility, and a re-rate to a higher forward sales multiple as recurring iDose revenue compounds.

**Bear case target: $90-100** (~22-28% downside). Reimbursement stall, faster legacy-MIGS erosion, and multiple compression.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months**, tied to the cadence of MAC fee publications through 2026, the July 1, 2026 Epioxa J-code activation, quarterly iDose revenue prints, and progress toward the operating-leverage cross-over.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. iDose TR quarterly revenue growth decelerates below ~20% sequentially-annualized, or the remaining MACs fail to publish professional fees by year-end 2026, signaling a reimbursement bottleneck rather than a build.
2. FY26/FY27 EPS estimate revisions turn negative for two consecutive months, reversing the current +21.7% FY27 momentum.
3. Operating cash burn re-widens beyond $(40)M annualized, or the doubtful-account allowance materially increases, indicating the extended-payment-term receivables are a collection problem rather than a launch tactic.

## Conclusion

Glaukos clears the bar for a BUY because it pairs a genuine earnings inflection (40%+ revenue growth, declining cash burn, FY27 EPS turning positive, +21.7% 90-day upward revisions) with a specific, trackable catalyst in the iDose Medicare reimbursement build-out rather than an open-ended hope. The headline GAAP losses overstate the problem; underlying gross margins of 82-84% and a clean balance sheet give the company room to fund the launch toward profitability. The validation concern about a high-growth name being held back without a concrete reason is resolved by recognizing that the gating risk (MAC-by-MAC fee publication) is itself the catalyst, and it is resolving favorably and observably.

The offsets are real and explain the moderate overall score rather than negating the call: elevated near-term options-implied stress, executive selling into Q1 strength with no open-market buying, and structural pressure on legacy MIGS volumes. These argue for scaling in rather than chasing, and for sizing modestly to absorb the implied ±7% near-term move. On balance, with the stock at ~12x forward sales below a $136-170 analyst target range and estimate momentum still building, the risk/reward favors accumulation. **BUY.**
