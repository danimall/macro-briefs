## Executive Summary

BlackRock delivered its best operating quarter in nearly five years in Q2 2026. Assets under management crossed $15.3 trillion — the first asset manager ever above $15 trillion — on $192 billion of net inflows, revenue rose 31% year over year to roughly $7.1 billion, the adjusted operating margin reached 45.9% against 43.3% a year earlier, and adjusted diluted EPS of $13.91 beat the $12.72 Zacks consensus by 9.4%. That was the fourth consecutive beat, and the sell side responded by lifting FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS 5.6% in thirty days to $56.23 and FY2027 by 5.7% to $64.11. Every one of the seventeen published price targets sits above the current share price; the low is $1,190 and the mean $1,313.50.

Two things keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY at $1,130.34. First, the quality of the earnings base. AUM rose $1.4 trillion in a single quarter against $192 billion of inflows, meaning roughly 86% of the increase was market appreciation and currency. Base fees are charged on AUM, so the mechanism runs symmetrically. FY2027 consensus of $64.11 is therefore built on an asset base that is itself near a cyclical peak. Second, the alignment and capital-return record. The company's own proxy shows BlackRock trailing its selected peer group in cumulative TSR in every disclosed year from 2021 to 2024, with the gap widening from roughly 8 points to 44 points; CEO compensation rose 23% to $37.7 million in a year GAAP net income fell 12.8%; the FY2026 buyback target of $2 billion equals 1.1% of market capitalization; and there were no open-market insider purchases in the period reviewed.

The valuation discount to alternatives peers is real and identifiable: 17.6x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS versus a 31x peer median, with trailing GAAP P/E of 32x distorted by roughly $7.3 billion of intangibles added in one year plus a share-settled earn-out that moves GAAP earnings inversely to the stock price. That discount is the credible re-rating case. But the price is 7.0% higher than at our last review while the forward multiple is essentially unchanged (19.8x then, 20.1x now), and the incremental fundamental news, while good, was substantially market-driven. Base case $1,282 (+13.4%), bull $1,475 (+30.5%), bear $884 (-21.8%). Hold existing positions; new capital is better deployed toward $1,020-1,060 or after a flat-market quarter demonstrates that the margin inflection is operating leverage rather than market leverage.

## Company Overview

BlackRock manages $15.3 trillion across four businesses that have changed materially in composition over three years.

**iShares ETFs** crossed $6 trillion in Q2 2026, roughly doubling in three years, and contributed $178 billion of the quarter's net inflows. Fixed income was the largest asset-class contributor at $92 billion, the category where BlackRock's secondary-market liquidity advantage is most pronounced and where fee compression has been least severe.

**Institutional index and active strategies** span a very large low-fee institutional index book alongside higher-fee active equity, active fixed income and liquid alternatives. Active strategies took $53 billion in Q2, including a record $7 billion into liquid alternatives.

**Private markets** was rebuilt through three transactions in roughly fifteen months: Global Infrastructure Partners (infrastructure equity, closed 2024), HPS Investment Partners (private credit, closed 2025), and Preqin (private-markets data, closed March 2025). The balance-sheet consequence is unambiguous: goodwill rose from approximately $25.9 billion to $35.3 billion and acquired intangibles from approximately $20.7 billion to $28.0 billion during 2025. Roughly $63 billion of the balance sheet, the majority of total equity, is now goodwill and intangibles from these deals. Management targets approximately $400 billion of private-markets AUM by 2030.

**Technology (Aladdin, eFront, Aladdin Wealth, Preqin)** generated $530 million of revenue in Q1 2026, up 21.6% year over year, of which approximately $65 million was the full-quarter Preqin effect. Q2 technology revenue grew 13% with annual contract value up 15%, meaning contracted revenue is growing faster than recognized revenue, a constructive leading indicator.

The firm also runs a large securities-lending operation: approximately $397 billion on loan against $422 billion of collateral at March 31, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue growth is real; GAAP earnings are not the right lens

FY2025 revenue rose 18.7% to $24,216 million while net income attributable to BlackRock fell 12.8% to $5,553 million. That divergence is the single most common source of analytical error on this name, and it resolves into two mechanical drivers.

The first is amortization. Adding roughly $7.3 billion of acquired intangibles in one year creates a large non-cash charge that suppresses GAAP net income, GAAP EPS and any net-income-derived return metric without touching operating cash flow, base-fee generation, dividend capacity or buyback capacity.

The second is more unusual. The contingent-consideration liability stood at $8,429 million at December 31, 2025, with approximately $720 million of FY2025 expense from fair-value changes. By March 31, 2026 the liability had fallen to $7,865 million and the quarter recorded an approximately $549 million benefit. A portion of that consideration is share-settled, so it is remeasured against BlackRock's own share price, which closed at $962 on March 31, 2026, below the year-end level. That is why Q1 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $14.06 *exceeded* adjusted EPS of $12.53, inverting the usual relationship. Discount-rate changes and revisions to probability-weighted earn-out achievement also drive the mark, and the relative contribution of each input is not disclosed, so the share-price linkage is an inference rather than an established decomposition. The practical implication holds either way: GAAP earnings now contain a counter-cyclical component that has nothing to do with economic performance, and Q2 2026, when the shares rose from $962 to roughly $1,100, likely carried a substantial charge in the opposite direction. Q2 GAAP EPS is not available; the $13.91 result and the $12.72 consensus are both non-GAAP.

The adjusted operating-margin series is internally consistent and is the more usable comparable: 44.5% (FY2024) → 44.1% (FY2025) → 44.5% (Q1 2026) → 45.9% (Q2 2026, versus 43.3% in Q2 2025). The FY2025 dip is consistent with absorbing the HPS/GIP/Preqin cost base ahead of full revenue realization; the H1 2026 recovery to a near-five-year high suggests integration dilution is passing. One caveat: an XBRL-tagged FY2025 operating income of $7,045 million on $24,216 million of revenue implies a 29.1% GAAP operating margin, which does not reconcile with the 42.0% GAAP figure BlackRock itself reported for Q1 2026. That 29.1% should not be treated as BlackRock's reported GAAP operating margin.

### The fee rate rose, which cuts against the standard bear case

Base fees plus securities lending divided by average AUM:
- FY2024: $16,100M ÷ $10,804,007M = **14.90 bps**
- FY2025: $19,179M ÷ $12,603,633M = **15.22 bps**

The realized fee rate rose approximately 0.31 basis points, or 2.1%, in a year when BlackRock also shed approximately $119 billion of institutional index assets. The most plausible reading is mix shift: HPS private credit and GIP infrastructure carry materially higher fee rates than index products, and their inclusion more than offset ETF price competition plus the loss of cheap institutional index mandates. Institutional index assets often price in the 1-3 basis-point range, so $119 billion of outflows may represent only $10-35 million of annual base fees.

This calculation establishes that revenue *mix* improved. It says nothing about client concentration, and the Q1 2026 10-Q discloses that the outflows include a single client's approximately $52 billion partial redemption in Q2 2025. Institutional index outflows continued at $34.7 billion in Q1 2026, bringing the trailing-twelve-month figure to approximately $108.5 billion. The filings do not identify the client or the reason, so in-sourcing cannot be distinguished from relationship loss. If large sovereign funds, national pensions or insurers are systematically bringing index management in-house, the pattern recurs and eventually reaches assets that do carry meaningful fees.

### Cash flow and balance sheet

Q1 2026 GAAP operating cash flow was negative $980 million; excluding consolidated investment products it was positive $583 million. The dominant driver was a $2.26 billion decrease in accrued compensation, the annual incentive payout, which BlackRock settles in Q1. This is a seasonality artifact and must not be annualized; adding back the working-capital reversal that accrues over the remaining three quarters puts Q1 ex-CIP operating cash flow near $2.8 billion on a working-capital-neutral basis. That is a sensitivity using a single disclosed input, not a forecast, and it does not adjust for cash taxes, performance-fee timing or CIP effects.

A financial-health screen returning an Altman Z-Score near 1.05 is not interpretable here. Total assets are inflated by consolidated investment products whose assets and liabilities belong to third-party fund investors, roughly $63 billion of assets are goodwill and intangibles that contribute nothing to any numerator, and asset managers have no working-capital cycle. BlackRock is investment grade, generates several billion dollars of annual free cash flow, and covers a 2.03% dividend. There is no near-term solvency question.

Two balance-sheet items do warrant attention. An 8-K filed April 3, 2026 discloses Items 1.01 and 2.03 (the standard signature of a note issuance) with size, tenor, coupon and covenants not obtained. And the $8.4 billion contingent-consideration liability is a claim on future equity, not just cash. **Financial health: 7.0** — strong, durable, fee-based cash generation and investment-grade access, marked down from the top band for the goodwill load, the unresolved debt terms, and the contingent equity claim.

## Valuation Assessment

The gap between bases is unusually wide and determines the answer.

**Trailing GAAP.** FY2025 net income of $5,553 million over approximately 158.7 million diluted shares gives GAAP diluted EPS near $35.00, and the market-data feed reports trailing EPS of $35.31. At $1,130.34 that is **32.0x trailing GAAP**. The same feed reports a trailing and forward P/E of 27.04, which does not reconcile with its own EPS field ($1,130.34 ÷ $35.31 = 32.0x); the 27.04 figure appears to rest on a different earnings base and should not be used.

**Forward adjusted.** Consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of $56.23 and FY2027 of $64.11 give **20.1x** and **17.6x** respectively. H1 2026 actuals of $26.44 ($12.53 + $13.91) imply H2 of $29.79 to reach FY2026 consensus — 12.7% above H1, supportable given H2 opens with AUM at $15.3 trillion against a lower Q2 average base and given Q4's seasonally heavy performance fees.

Against peers, the discount is stark on the forward basis:

Peer median P/E is 31.0. BlackRock at 32x trailing GAAP screens in line with the alternatives managers; at 17.6x FY2027 adjusted it screens closer to the custody banks (BK 16.9, STT 16.7) despite a materially better growth and margin profile than either. The specific mispricing mechanism is identifiable: any screen running on GAAP net income sees a company whose earnings fell 12.8% in a year revenue rose 18.7%, and whose reported earnings now move inversely to its own share price. That is an accounting artifact of the GIP/HPS structure, not an economic result.

Two things prevent a higher valuation score. First, the adjusted numbers exclude real costs — the intangibles being amortized were paid for in cash and stock, and there is a further contingent equity claim outstanding. True earnings power sits between the two bases, closer to adjusted. Second, and more important, the forward estimate base is cyclically elevated. Base fees are a direct function of AUM, AUM is at a record, and 86% of the most recent increase was market-driven. A 15% equity drawdown compresses AUM, base fees and performance fees simultaneously and could take FY2027 adjusted EPS toward $52, at which point 17.6x becomes 22x on falling estimates.

The analyst target set — low $1,190, mean $1,313.50, high $1,488, with every target above spot and the strong-buy count rising from four to five — is corroborating rather than decisive. At 20x forward the stock is not at a premium multiple where targets simply echo price extrapolation, so the target floor carries some information. **Valuation: 6.5** — a real, mechanically explicable discount, offset by a cyclically peak earnings base.

## Competitive Landscape

**Indexing and ETFs.** Vanguard competes at cost and can do so indefinitely; State Street, Fidelity, Invesco, Schwab and JPMorgan all contest the category on price. BlackRock's defense is scale, secondary liquidity and tracking quality rather than price leadership, and the evidence says it is working: iShares reached $6 trillion and captured $178 billion of inflows in a single quarter while not being the cheapest option. The $92 billion of fixed-income inflows is the most defensible slice — bond ETF liquidity is a genuine network effect and pricing pressure there has been milder than in equity index.

**Private markets.** Here BlackRock is the challenger. Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Ares, Brookfield, Blue Owl and Carlyle have decades of relationships, track records and established carried-interest economics. BlackRock's differentiated pitch — bundling private credit and infrastructure with public beta, wealth-channel distribution and Aladdin/Preqin underwriting data — is coherent and unproven at scale. The industry backdrop cuts both ways: the secular institutional and wealth allocation shift to private markets is multi-year and real, but private-credit spreads have compressed and capital formation has been heavy. HPS was bought into a strong asset class at a strong moment.

**Technology.** Aladdin competes with MSCI, SimCorp, State Street's Charles River, FactSet and Bloomberg AIM. Revenue +13% with ACV +15% indicates continued share gains, and Preqin makes Aladdin the system of record for private-markets data as well as public, deepening switching costs precisely where clients are adding allocations.

**Digital assets.** BlackRock is the clear leader in spot crypto ETPs, which is why the drawdown is so visible. The category has behaved as a high-beta expression of the underlying asset rather than a durable annuity.

Trajectory: improving in ETFs and technology, unproven in private markets, deteriorating in digital assets, with the realized fee rate rising. **Competitive position: 7.5.**

## Risk Assessment

**1. Market beta dominates revenue and cannot be diversified away.** With roughly 86% of the latest quarter's AUM growth market-driven, revenue is levered to asset prices in both directions. The 52-week range of $917.39-$1,219.94 — a 33% spread — reflects that sensitivity. This is the largest single risk and it is not company-specific.

**2. Goodwill and impairment on ~$63 billion of intangibles.** GIP, HPS and Preqin were acquired at private-markets valuations near a cyclical peak for private credit. No impairment has been disclosed. If HPS fee-earning AUM growth or realized carry disappoints, the carrying value becomes vulnerable. Key-person risk within acquired investment teams is acute — earn-out structures exist precisely to retain them.

**3. Contingent consideration and dilution.** At March 31, 2026: 155.4 million common shares, 7.6 million exchangeable Subco Units (HPS-related, carried as non-controlling interests), 163.0 million total economic units, 165.0 million reported diluted. Subco Units are 4.9% of current common shares, and full exchange would leave existing holders owning 4.7% less of the resulting count. Remaining maximum contingent issuance — approximately 5.3 million HPS residual plus up to 5.2 million GIP — totals roughly 10.5 million shares, or 6.8% of current common shares; if fully issued on top of the 163.0 million economic base, existing holders would own 6.1% less. These are earn-out-conditional maximums, not current fully diluted capitalization; the correct current denominator remains 165.0 million diluted shares. The earn-outs vest most fully in the scenario where the acquisitions perform best.

**4. Antitrust litigation attacking the stewardship function.** Thirteen state attorneys general allege that BlackRock and other large index managers coordinated to suppress coal output through climate-related stewardship. The motion to dismiss was largely denied in 2025, so the case proceeds to discovery. Denial of a motion to dismiss is a low bar and says nothing about ultimate merits. But the theory of the case attacks the proxy-voting and stewardship mechanism of a $15 trillion index manager. Direct damages are speculative; a broad injunction requiring pass-through voting infrastructure at scale would be an operational and industry-wide event. Note 16 of the FY2025 10-K, containing BlackRock's own reasonably-possible-loss characterization, was not read.

**5. Fee compression in the core.** The FY2025 realized fee rate rose only because of acquisition mix. Absent continued mix shift, underlying index pricing trends down. Durability of the mix-shift offset is the medium-term question.

**6. Digital asset demand deceleration** — net inflows from a ~$8.7 billion quarterly average in FY2025 to $935 million in Q1 2026, on above-index fee rates.

**7. Securities-lending indemnification** — $397 billion on loan against $422 billion of collateral. Over-collateralized and remote, but a correlated-default tail.

**8. Fink succession.** Fink remains founder, Chairman, CEO and principal client-relationship anchor with no designated successor. Two 8-Ks under Item 5.02 (January 16 and January 27, 2026) were not read, and their relevance to succession is unknown.

**9. Named verification gaps.** All Q2 2026 figures are externally reported from the earnings release across five outlets rather than filing-verified (the Q2 10-Q was not yet filed). Q2 GAAP EPS is unknown. An 8-K filed June 30, 2026 under Item 8.01 (Other Events) with an exhibit was not retrieved; subject matter unknown. The April 3, 2026 debt terms were not obtained.

**Risk factors: 5.5** — the antitrust case, the impairment exposure on peak-cycle private-credit goodwill, the contingent equity overhang, and succession are all live and only partially priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $1,475 (+30.5%)

The private-markets pivot re-rates BlackRock toward the alternatives-manager multiple it has been denied. The proxy's own pay-versus-performance table shows BLK cumulative TSR of 167.24 against peer-group 211.25 at FY2024, trailing in every disclosed year with the gap widening from ~8 to ~44 points — consistent with a market paying more for Blackstone, Apollo and KKR than for an index-weighted mix. GIP/HPS/Preqin were the deliberate response. The evidence that it is working: the realized fee rate rose to 15.22 bps from 14.90, adjusted operating margin hit 45.9% (near a five-year high) after absorbing three integrations, technology ACV grew 15% against 13% revenue, and organic base fee growth ran 8% in the quarter against a 10% TTM rate. On that path FY2027 adjusted EPS of $64.11 proves conservative, the market applies 23x — still a discount to the 31x peer median — and the stock reaches $1,475, matching the high analyst target of $1,488.

### Bear case — $884 (-21.8%)

A 15% equity drawdown does the work. Average AUM falls, base fees fall roughly proportionally, performance fees fall more, and FY2027 adjusted EPS compresses toward $52. The multiple contracts to 17x on falling estimates and the stock trades to $884 — below the 52-week low of $917.39. Q1 2026 demonstrated the mechanism at smaller scale: $216 billion of market depreciation plus $50 billion of adverse FX overwhelmed $130 billion of inflows and AUM declined. Layered on top: private-credit spread compression exposes HPS as a peak-cycle purchase and triggers the first impairment against $63 billion of goodwill and intangibles; the state-AG antitrust case advances toward a stewardship constraint; institutional index outflows continue past the low-fee book into mandates that carry real revenue; and the 6.8% contingent-issuance overhang lands in a scenario where the earn-outs vest.

## Conclusion

BlackRock is executing well against a strategy that was, on the company's own disclosure, a response to four consecutive years of TSR underperformance versus its selected peer group. The evidence that the strategy is working is concrete: the realized fee rate rose to 15.22 basis points from 14.90 despite $119 billion of institutional index outflows, adjusted operating margin reached 45.9% after absorbing three integrations, Aladdin's contracted revenue is growing faster than recognized revenue, iShares crossed $6 trillion, and consensus adjusted EPS has been revised up 5.6-6.3% across every forward period on the back of four consecutive beats. At 20.1x FY2026 and 17.6x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS against a 31x peer median, with trailing GAAP P/E of 32x distorted by acquisition accounting that also makes reported earnings move inversely to the share price, the discount has an identifiable mechanism and is not simply a low multiple.

What holds the recommendation at HOLD is the composition of the earnings base and the quality of shareholder alignment. Roughly 86% of last quarter's $1.4 trillion AUM increase was market appreciation, so FY2027 consensus rests on an asset base near a cyclical high. The margin inflection has not yet been tested in a flat-market quarter. Capital return is thin — a $2 billion buyback is 1.1% of market capitalization against a 6.8% maximum contingent-issuance overhang — CEO compensation rose 23% in a year GAAP net income fell 12.8%, and there were no open-market insider purchases in the reviewed period. The antitrust case attacking the stewardship function has survived dismissal and is in discovery, and $63 billion of goodwill sits on private-markets assets bought into a strong tape.

Since our last review the price has risen 7.0% while the forward multiple is essentially unchanged, meaning the incremental return has been earned by estimate revisions rather than multiple expansion, a pattern that leaves base-case upside at 13.4% against 21.8% bear-case downside. Hold what you own; new capital is better committed toward $1,020-1,060, or after a flat-market quarter proves the 45%+ margin is structural.
