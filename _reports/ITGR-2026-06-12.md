**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Integer Holdings is one of the largest independent medical-device contract development and manufacturing organizations (CDMOs), supplying components and finished implantable devices to large cardiac and neuro OEMs. The investment case has shifted from a steady ~10% compounder to a catalyst-driven special situation. Within roughly twelve months the company has cut 2026 guidance on three simultaneous new-product adoption misses, watched its stock fall from ~$144 to the low-$60s, absorbed a securities class action, settled an activist campaign, refreshed essentially its entire C-suite under a new CEO, and launched a Board strategic review explicitly considering "a sale, merger, or strategic business combination." The stock now sits at ~$92, mid-range of its 52-week band of $62–$124.

The central debate is whether the current trough is cyclical or structural. The bull case rests on CDMO scarcity value — there are few large, independent, publicly traded pure-play medical CDMOs — and a strategic review that could surface a premium take-out for a high-quality asset trading at a depressed multiple. The bear case is that the new-product weakness reflects customer/end-market adoption risk Integer cannot control, that the review yields nothing, and that the combination of 57% intangible assets, a 2028 maturity wall, thin cash, and litigation weighs on the multiple. Estimate revisions are negative (current-year EPS cut 6.3% in 30 days, current-quarter down 10.3%), and the options market is pricing imminent stress with near-term IV in backwardation at 59.9%.

We rate ITGR a HOLD. The strategic review is a specific catalyst that distinguishes this from a generic "bad business in a bad tape" avoid, and the asset has genuine scarcity value at a multiple characterized by third-party commentary as ~10x EBITDA. But we cannot underwrite a confident 12-month upside thesis when consensus estimates are falling across every horizon, Q1 margins compressed 270bps, and the review has no defined timeline or outcome. The catalyst keeps us from avoiding; the absence of fundamental improvement keeps us from buying.

## Company Overview

Integer designs and manufactures medical devices and components for multinational medical-device OEMs, operating as one reportable segment with three product lines:

- **Cardio & Vascular (C&V)** — $1.11B, 60% of 2025 sales, +16.6% in 2025. The growth engine, levered to electrophysiology, neurovascular, and structural heart, and the focus of M&A and capacity investment.
- **Cardiac Rhythm Management & Neuromodulation (CRM&N)** — $669M, 36%, +1.2% in 2025. A mature "grow-at-market" business dragged modestly by the planned wind-down of a legacy spinal-cord-stimulation IPG customer.
- **Other Markets** — $78M, 4%, -27%, deliberately shrinking via the multi-year Portable Medical exit.

The model is B2B: Integer manufactures its OEM customers' devices and does not own the finished branded products sold to patients. Competitive advantages center on end-to-end engineering plus manufacturing scale, a regulatory/quality track record, and switching costs in highly regulated implantables. The company has built a tuck-in acquisition machine adding specialized coating and processing technologies (Pulse in 2024; Precision, VSi, Biocoat in 2025) and divested its non-medical Electrochem business in October 2024 to become a pure-play medical CDMO.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily: $1,376M (2022) → $1,597M (2023) → $1,717M (2024) → $1,854M (2025), roughly a 10% CAGR. Profitability, however, deteriorated in 2025 and again in Q1 2026.

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,597M | $1,717M | $1,854M |
| Gross margin | 26.2% | 26.7% | 27.0% |
| Operating income | $167M | $208M | $221M |
| Operating margin | 10.5% | 12.1% | 11.9% |
| Net income (cont. ops) | $91M | $121M | $103M |
| Diluted EPS | — | $3.40 | $2.89 |

2025 net income fell 15% despite 8% revenue growth, almost entirely from a $46.7M non-cash "induced conversion" expense tied to the March 2025 convertible-note exchange. Stripping that out, underlying operations held up — gross profit rose 9% and operating income 6%. The optical EPS decline overstates fundamental deterioration in 2025.

The more concerning data point is Q1 2026. Sales were essentially flat (+0.5% to $440M), but gross margin compressed 270bps to 24.9% (from 27.5%) on poor fixed-cost absorption from lower production volumes. Operating income fell 36% to $31.9M, and operating margin dropped to 7.2% from 11.3%. The mechanism is straightforward: a largely fixed manufacturing cost base means underutilization flows directly to gross margin. The three new-product adoption misses reduced volumes, which reduced absorption, which compressed margins. This is the figure to watch, not the 2025 conversion charge.

Cash flow and balance sheet: 2025 operating cash flow was $196M and FCF $105M (FCF margin ~5.7%), with capex normalizing to $91M from $120M in 2023. Cash is thin at $17M (Dec 2025) and $8M (Q1 2026); the company runs lean and relies on its $800M revolver (~$730M available). Debt principal was $1.27B at Q1 2026, with reported total net leverage ~3.0x against a 5.0x covenant max and ~14x interest coverage — comfortable covenant headroom. The March 2025 refinancing converted variable-rate bank debt to low-coupon fixed converts (1.875% 2030 notes, 2.125% 2028 notes), cutting interest expense 23% in 2025 and ~34% in Q1 2026, with 88–92% of debt now fixed.

Two balance-sheet items deserve emphasis. Goodwill and intangibles are $1.9B, or 57% of total assets, a structural overhang even though management's impairment tests show fair value "significantly in excess" of carrying value. And the 2028 notes ($116M), revolver, and term loan all mature within roughly February 2028, a maturity wall that converges with thin cash if volumes deteriorate further or a convert is put. Altman Z of 2.29 sits in the grey zone, reflecting the leverage and intangible-heavy structure rather than operational distress; Piotroski signals are healthy (positive net income, OCF above net income, current ratio 3.3x).

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has cracked at the point where it matters most. New-product ramps in C&V are the core of the growth story, and both the 10-K and Q1 10-Q flag that 2026 growth will be hurt by "lower sales related to three new products due to lower than anticipated market adoption," with management explicitly calling the simultaneity "highly unusual." Because Integer is a CDMO, its growth is downstream of its OEM customers' product cycles, so weak adoption is partly an end-market and customer issue Integer cannot fully control.

The estimate revision data confirms negative earnings momentum across every horizon: current quarter -10.3% (30d) / -9.6% (90d), next quarter -8.2% / -6.0%, current year -6.3% / -5.9%, next year -5.8% / -5.5%. Consensus is systematically marking down forward earnings, not raising them. The 30-day cuts being deeper than the 90-day cuts for the near-term quarters indicates the deterioration is accelerating, not stabilizing. We weight this heavily and negatively in the growth score.

The earnings track record is mixed (2 beats, 2 misses over the last four quarters), with the most recent quarter a narrow miss (-0.8%). The secular tailwind in electrophysiology, structural heart, neurovascular, heart pumps, and leadless pacing remains intact, and OEM outsourcing of complex manufacturing is a durable driver. But the proximate question — whether the three new products recover adoption in 2026 — has no answer in the current data, and management's own framing offers no confidence that it resolves this year.

## Valuation Assessment

ITGR trades at ~23x trailing/forward GAAP EPS of $2.89 and, on management's 2026 adjusted EPS guide of $5.83–$6.40, at roughly 14–16x adjusted earnings. On the ~$92 price and the company's EBITDA base, external commentary frames the multiple at roughly 10x EBITDA — depressed for a scaled, differentiated CDMO. (This multiple characterization is third-party Seeking Alpha opinion, not company disclosure.)

Peer multiples are not directly comparable but are informative. The Health Care Equipment peer median P/E is 34.7, with the set ranging from TMDX at 15.6 to LIVN at 37.2. On adjusted earnings ITGR screens cheap versus this cohort, though the GAAP-to-adjusted gap is wide given heavy intangible amortization, which is real economic cost embedded in the acquisition-driven model. The analyst target band is $84 low to $115 high, mean ~$97.6, median ~$97 — modest upside to the mean from the current price.

The valuation score reflects genuine asymmetry from the strategic review. A scarce public CDMO asset in an active sale process can command a takeout premium that the standalone DCF does not capture; the negative CEO "compensation actually paid" (-$5.38M in 2025), the activist settlement, and an aggressive buyback (including a $50M ASR completed in April at ~$84.80) all signal the board and management view the shares as undervalued. That said, this is not a re-rating case driven by rising estimates — estimates are falling, so we do not treat analyst targets as floors here. The valuation upside is contingent on a transaction or a volume recovery, not on consensus catching up to rising estimates. We score valuation moderately above neutral to capture the optionality without over-crediting an uncertain catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Integer's moat is real but two-sided. On the positive side, end-to-end engineering plus manufacturing scale, quality/regulatory track record, and high switching costs in implantables create stickiness, and the coating/surface-treatment and micro-machining acquisitions deepen differentiated, high-value process capabilities that raise pull-through. CDMO scarcity — few large, independent, publicly traded pure-plays — is the crux of the strategic-buyer thesis.

The structural tension is that the customer is also the competitor. OEMs can in-source or dual-source, many supply agreements lack minimum-purchase requirements, and the 10-K explicitly notes ongoing customer-driven price reductions. Healthcare consolidation increases customer bargaining power. Customer concentration is severe: top three customers ~49% of 2025 revenue (Q1 2026: A 19%, B 18%, C 15%). A single OEM's destocking or program delay materially moves results, and the three new-product misses are partly a reflection of that downstream dependence. The competitive position is stable rather than improving on a 12-month view: the moat holds, but the trajectory is flat-to-soft while volumes are weak.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is elevated and partly idiosyncratic:

- **The "three new products" problem.** The proximate cause of the volume and margin miss; multiple simultaneous adoption failures in the growth engine is a genuine thesis risk and may be structural rather than transient.
- **Customer concentration.** ~49% from three customers, many agreements without minimum-purchase commitments.
- **Securities class action.** *Pembroke Pines Pension Fund v. Integer Holdings* (SDNY, filed Dec 2025, amended April 2026), almost certainly tied to the guidance shortfall and stock collapse; motion-to-dismiss deadline was May 1, 2026. Outcome and cost are uncertain.
- **Leverage, intangibles, and the 2028 maturity wall.** $1.27B debt, 57% intangibles, thin cash ($8M), with the revolver, term loan, and 2028 notes converging near February 2028. Convertible-note conversion/dilution risk exists (capped calls partially offset; 2028 cap $108.59, 2030 cap $189.44).
- **Strategic-review uncertainty.** A new risk factor flags distraction, cost, and no assurance of a transaction.
- **Tariff/geopolitical exposure.** Manufacturing in Mexico, Ireland, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic; 47% of sales ex-U.S.; explicit flag on new U.S. tariffs.
- **Intangible impairment risk** given the asset mix.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 59.9% versus 48.0% historical volatility (rich by 11.9pp), at the 66th percentile of the current chain, with a put/call skew of 10.3% indicating elevated put demand. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 59.9% above the six-month 52.6%), which the market uses to signal a near-dated catalyst or stress event, consistent with the upcoming earnings cadence and the unresolved strategic review. ATM IV jumped from 49.4% to 59.9% since late May while the price rose 2.2%, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 1.60 to 0.82. Short interest is 16.1% of float with 6.6 days to cover — meaningful bearish institutional positioning. The options and short-interest data corroborate, rather than challenge, the cautious fundamental read: the market is bracing for a downside resolution. This is confirmation that the binary outcome of the strategic review and the next print carries two-sided, near-term risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$115–$130).** The strategic review surfaces a strategic or financial buyer who pays a premium for a scarce, high-quality public CDMO at a trough multiple (characterized by third-party commentary as ~10x EBITDA). Alternatively, the three new products regain adoption through 2026, volumes recover, fixed-cost absorption normalizes, gross margin returns toward 27%, and the multiple re-rates toward the adjusted-earnings peer cohort. Management's buyback at ~$84.80 and negative comp-actually-paid signal conviction that the shares are undervalued.

**Bear Case (~$62–$70).** The review concludes with no transaction; the new-product weakness proves structural and customer-driven; Q1 margin compression persists or worsens; the securities suit and integration of seven years of acquisitions weigh on the multiple; and the 2028 maturity wall meets thin cash. Estimates continue to fall, and the stock re-rates back toward its 52-week low of $62.

The setup is genuinely two-sided. The decisive variable is whether the trough is temporary and whether the board can monetize scarcity value before the maturity wall and litigation become binding constraints; the secular med-tech tailwind is intact.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August) and any disclosed update on the strategic review, whichever comes first. The review is the dominant near-term swing factor and has no defined timeline, so material 8-K disclosures should be monitored continuously.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A definitive transaction announcement (sale/merger) at a premium to current price, or credible disclosure of advanced negotiations.
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing gross margin (back above ~26.5%) with evidence the three new products are regaining adoption, accompanied by a halt-and-reversal in EPS estimate revisions (positive 30-day revisions).
- Organic revenue growth reaccelerating above mid-single digits with operating margin recovering toward 11%+.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Strategic review concludes with no transaction and management reaffirms standalone execution while estimates continue to fall.
- Further gross-margin compression in Q2 (below ~24%) or a 2026 adjusted-EPS guidance cut below the $5.83 low end.
- Adverse development in the *Pembroke Pines* litigation (denial of motion to dismiss combined with a quantified exposure), or any goodwill/intangible impairment charge.
- Cash deterioration or a convert put/conversion event that strains liquidity into the 2028 maturity wall.

## Conclusion

ITGR is a scarce, scaled medical CDMO trading at a depressed multiple after a self-inflicted and partly customer-driven growth stumble. The Board's strategic review is a specific catalyst that elevates this above a generic avoid, and management's buyback and negative comp-actually-paid indicate genuine conviction that the shares are undervalued. Against that, every measurable forward indicator is pointed the wrong way: estimate revisions are negative across all horizons, Q1 margins compressed 270bps, the options market is in backwardation pricing near-term stress, short interest is 16.1%, and the review has no timeline or assured outcome.

We rate ITGR a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. We cannot construct a confident 12-month appreciation thesis on falling estimates and an undefined catalyst, but the asymmetry of a potential premium takeout and the scarcity value of the asset keep us from avoiding. The position is to wait for resolution — either a transaction that crystallizes value, or two quarters of margin stabilization and positive estimate revisions that demonstrate the trough is cyclical. Until one of those arrives, the risk/reward is balanced and the right action is patience.
