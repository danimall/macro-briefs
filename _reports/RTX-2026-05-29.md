**Research Date: May 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RTX is the world's largest pure-play aerospace and defense company by revenue, sitting at the intersection of two strong demand cycles: a rising global defense-spending environment and a still-recovering commercial aftermarket. The operational story is good. Revenue grew 9.7% in FY2025 to $88.6 billion, almost entirely organic, and operating margin expanded from 5.2% (FY23) to 8.1% (FY24) to 10.5% (FY25), then to 11.6% in Q1 2026. Free cash flow reached $7.94 billion (~9% margin), debt fell to $37.9 billion, and Moody's upgraded RTX to Baa1 (positive) in February 2026. The $271 billion remaining performance obligation — roughly 60% commercial, 40% defense, with ~45% tied to long-dated Pratt maintenance contracts — gives the franchise exceptional revenue visibility.

The investment question is whether the current price offers an attractive entry. At ~$179, the stock trades at roughly 26x consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.90 and ~24x FY2027's $7.56, a PEG near 2.5 for a company growing revenue ~9%. Consensus estimates are stable, not rising — current-year EPS has been revised up just 1.3% over 90 days, and next-quarter estimates were trimmed 2.7%. The dual-cycle recovery, margin expansion, and defense tailwinds are widely understood and reflected in the multiple. There is no identifiable mispricing where the market is missing something material to the upside.

What the market may be underpricing is a specific downside: a January 7, 2026 Executive Order, reinforced by a bipartisan Hawley-Warren bill (March 2026), that would empower the government to restrict dividends, buybacks, and executive pay at defense contractors deemed to "underperform" on federal contracts. RTX's buybacks have already collapsed from ~$12.9 billion (FY23) to ~$50 million (FY25). Combined with an open SEC investigation into the 2023 GTF disclosures and a securities class action on appeal to the Second Circuit, this creates asymmetric tail risk that is not in trailing fundamentals. We rate RTX HOLD with a constructive 12-month bias.

## Company Overview

RTX (formerly Raytheon Technologies, renamed in 2023) was formed from the 2020 merger of United Technologies and Raytheon. It operates three roughly co-equal segments:

- **Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B FY25 sales, 16.3% operating margin): avionics, interiors, mechanical and electrical systems, mission systems. The highest-margin segment, levered to commercial aftermarket.
- **Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B FY25 sales, 7.9% operating margin): commercial geared-turbofan (GTF) engines powering the A320neo family, plus military engines (F135 for the F-35). Largest by revenue, lowest margin, weighed down by the GTF powder-metal issue.
- **Raytheon** ($28.0B FY25 sales, 11.5% operating margin): integrated air and missile defense (Patriot, LTAMDS, NASAMS, SM-3/SM-6), effectors and munitions, sensors, and command-and-control. The primary beneficiary of rising defense budgets.

Customer mix in FY2025 was approximately 38% US Government, 8% foreign military sales, 7% foreign direct, and 48% commercial, a dual-cycle profile that most defense primes lack.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue moved from $68.9B (FY23) to $80.7B (FY24) to $88.6B (FY25), with the FY25 increase almost entirely organic (+$8.9B). Net income was $6.73B in FY25 versus $4.77B in FY24; diluted EPS of $4.96 versus $3.55. Operating margin doubled over two years. Q1 2026 continued the trend: revenue of $22.08B (+8.7% YoY), operating margin of 11.6% (up from 10.0%), and EPS of $1.51 versus $1.14, with all three segments posting double-digit operating profit growth. Raytheon bookings surged 51% to $6.6 billion.

A structural distortion to flag: roughly $2.0 billion per year of operating profit is consumed by acquisition-accounting intangible amortization from the UTC/Raytheon merger. GAAP EPS therefore understates underlying earnings power, which is why consensus and the market work off adjusted figures (FY26 ~$6.90).

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow was $10.57B against $2.6B of capex, producing $7.94B of free cash flow. Total debt fell to $37.9B from $41.3B; debt-to-capital is ~36%; cash is $7.4B; equity is $65.2B; average debt maturity is ~12 years at ~4.5%. S&P holds BBB+/stable.

The balance sheet carries ~$85B of goodwill and intangibles against ~$171B of total assets, which causes the Altman Z-Score to screen in the "distress" zone (~1.6). This is a false signal driven by merger purchase accounting, not by any liquidity or solvency stress. With ~$8B FCF, the backlog, and investment-grade ratings, default risk is negligible.

**Earnings quality.** RTX has beaten consensus four straight quarters (16.1%, 4.6%, 19.5%, 7.0%), supporting confidence in forward estimates. FCF conversion is healthy. The combination of margin recovery, deleveraging, and the rating upgrade justifies a strong Financial Health score.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has two engines. On defense, the policy backdrop is unusually favorable for RTX's product mix: the "Golden Dome for America" initiative (January 2025 EO) funds ~$24.4B as part of a $156.2B Department of War supplemental through 2029, plus ~$25.4B in munitions and supply-chain funding. RTX dominates integrated air and missile defense (Patriot, LTAMDS, SM-3, NASAMS) and is a leading effectors/munitions producer, positioning it for this spend. A reported ~$1.5T FY2027 defense budget reinforces the multiyear demand picture.

On commercial, the post-pandemic air-traffic recovery and elevated OEM build rates drive Collins and Pratt aftermarket revenue. Roughly 45% of RPO is long-dated Pratt maintenance (contracts up to 20 years), giving multi-decade visibility on a high-margin revenue stream.

The constraint on the growth score is that this is high-single-digit revenue growth, with double-digit EPS growth coming primarily from margin expansion that is now well advanced. Consensus estimates are stable rather than accelerating — next year +0.7%. This is not the earnings-inflection profile (estimate revisions >30%, revenue acceleration from a trough) that signals systematic underestimation. The growth is real and durable, but largely visible to the market.

RTX's ~26x forward sits well below the peer median (distorted by BA, HWM, and GE) but at a premium to the pure defense primes, Lockheed (25.8x) and General Dynamics (21.6x). RTX commands a premium to those names because of its commercial aftermarket leverage, while growing revenue at a similar high-single-digit pace. The dividend yield of 1.65% is below GD and LMT.

The analyst distribution frames the asymmetry: mean target $215, median $220 (~20% upside to the mean), but a low target of $180 — essentially the current price. That spread of $180 to $242 reflects the bifurcation in this name: the bulls underwrite continued dual-cycle execution; the floor reflects the view that the recovery is priced.

A BUY requires identifying something specific the market is mispricing. The margin recovery, defense tailwinds, and backlog are all well-understood and reflected in the multiple. With estimates stable rather than rising, there is no consensus catch-up mechanism to drive re-rating. The specific item the market may be missing is to the downside — the regulatory payout risk discussed below — which argues for a full, not cheap, valuation read. We score Valuation 5.5: not stretched, but not offering the asymmetric upside required for a higher call.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX's moat is among the strongest in the sector. It holds sole-source positions on key defense programs, a massive installed base on commercial platforms generating decades of high-margin aftermarket revenue, and a backlog with embedded long-term maintenance contracts. The dual-cycle exposure is a structural advantage over pure defense primes (LMT, NOC, GD) and pure-commercial peers.

The competitive trajectory has softened modestly. GE Aerospace is widely viewed as the cleaner, higher-margin engine story, and Pratt's GTF powder-metal issue has been a multi-year drag on the segment that competes most directly with GE. Collins faces OEM-margin pressure from tariffs and titanium re-sourcing. These are reasons the competitive position score has come down from prior reports rather than evidence of share loss. The franchise remains a clear leader; the trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the Pratt level, offset by Raytheon's bookings strength.

## Risk Assessment

**Capital-return regulatory risk (novel, material).** The January 7, 2026 Executive Order empowers the Secretary of War to restrict a defense contractor's dividends, buybacks, and executive compensation if it "underperforms" on US government contracts, and the Hawley-Warren bill would codify similar limits. RTX explicitly warns this could pressure its stock. Only ~$0.6B remains under buyback authorization. Whether the collapse in buybacks is deleveraging discipline or pre-emptive caution, the capital-return component of the bull thesis is exposed to a development not in any trailing financial.

**GTF powder-metal matter and disclosure litigation.** The 2023 manufacturing defect in powder-metal turbine disks (PW1100 GTF) drove a ~$2.9B pre-tax charge. The remaining accrual is winding down ($0.7B → $0.5B), with ~$0.7B of cash outflow expected in 2026 and AOG counts normalizing. The residual legal tail is the open SEC investigation into the 2023 disclosures and a securities class action dismissed in September 2025 but on appeal to the Second Circuit, plus continuing derivative suits in Delaware.

**Legal/compliance overhang.** Two Deferred Prosecution Agreements, an SEC Administrative Order, and a False Claims Act settlement (FCPA bribery and defective-pricing matters) cost ~$384M and ~$580M. An independent compliance monitor was engaged in April 2026 for a three-year term, and an ITAR Consent Agreement carries a $200M penalty ($100M suspended). The DPAs restrict RTX's ability to pursue divestitures, limiting portfolio optionality (e.g., separating lower-margin Pratt commercial) for ~3 years.

**Government pricing claims.** DCMA claims of ~$1.7B (cost-accounting standards), ~$1.1B (IR&D allocation), and collaborator-parts claims are contested; a 2021 partial-favorable ASBCA ruling was remanded by the Federal Circuit in December 2025 — unresolved.

**Tariffs and geopolitics.** RTX paid ~$0.5B in IEEPA tariffs; the Supreme Court ruled IEEPA tariffs unauthorized (February 2026) and ordered a refund, though recovery is uncertain and not yet booked. China sanctions over Taiwan arms sales and Russian counter-sanctions on leadership add background friction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options pricing carries an unusual reading: 1-week ATM IV of 97.8% against 1-year historical volatility of 23.4%, an IV percentile of 88%, and a put/call skew of 8.4% (elevated put demand), with the term structure in steep backwardation. The 1-week figure is internally inconsistent (paired with an implied move of only ±1.0%) and is likely a data artifact; the more reliable 1-to-6-month tenors show normal 25–27% IV with mild near-term put skew (5.5% at 1m, 1.6% at 3m) flipping to slight call skew (–3.6%) at 6 months. The takeaway is modest near-term hedging demand rather than a clean directional signal, and the elevated short-dated put bias is consistent with caution into upcoming catalysts. Short interest is negligible (1.1% of float, 2.4 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$220–242, +23% to +35%)

RTX executes on the dual-cycle recovery: defense bookings continue at the Q1 pace as Golden Dome and munitions funding flow through, the commercial aftermarket sustains high margins, and operating margin pushes toward 12%+ across all segments. The GTF accrual fully winds down without further charges, the SEC investigation closes without material penalty, and the EO/Hawley-Warren payout risk is resolved favorably (RTX certified as a strong performer). FY2027 EPS re-rates to ~28–30x as estimate revisions turn positive, producing $215–242. The backlog and Baa1 upgrade underpin durability.

### Bear Case (~$150–160, –11% to –17%)

The EO/Hawley-Warren framework restricts RTX's dividends and buybacks, removing the capital-return leg of the thesis and triggering multiple compression toward the pure-defense-prime range (21–25x). The SEC investigation produces a penalty or adverse appellate ruling, supply-chain constraints cap revenue (the soft FY26 ~$93B guide hinted at this), and GTF or CAS claims require incremental accruals. The multiple compresses to ~22x FY27 EPS, producing ~$150–166.

### Why HOLD rather than BUY

The validation question is direct: at a ~6.5 composite the stock is a quality franchise, so what specifically prevents a BUY? The answer is concrete and falsifiable. First, this is an Industrials/A&D name trading at ~26x forward (PEG ~2.5) for ~9% revenue growth, with consensus estimates that are stable rather than rising. There is no earnings-inflection signal and no consensus catch-up mechanism; the dual-cycle recovery is already in the multiple, so the upside path depends on continued execution rather than mispricing the market hasn't recognized. Second, the January 2026 Executive Order plus the Hawley-Warren bill create a novel, asymmetric downside to the capital-return component of the thesis that is absent from all trailing fundamentals and that RTX itself flags as a stock-price risk. Third, the open SEC investigation into the 2023 GTF disclosures and the Second Circuit appeal are binary legal items with negative-skew payoffs. The analyst low target of $180 sitting at the current price quantifies this: the downside scenarios are real and dated, while the upside requires the recovery to keep compounding. That balance is a constructive hold, not a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings report (~late July 2026), the regulatory trajectory of the Hawley-Warren bill, and any update on the SEC GTF investigation or the Second Circuit appeal.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Consensus FY2027 EPS revisions turn meaningfully positive (>10% over 90 days) on accelerating Raytheon bookings or sustained margin gains above 12%, signaling the market is catching up to a stronger trajectory than priced.
2. Favorable resolution or clear exemption from the EO/Hawley-Warren payout framework, plus a resumption of buybacks at scale (>$2B annualized), restoring the capital-return leg of the thesis.
3. A pullback toward $150–160 (the bear-case range) on no fundamental deterioration, which would restore the upside/downside asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. The SEC GTF investigation produces a material penalty or the Second Circuit reinstates the securities class action.
2. Operating margin reverses below 10% or a new GTF/CAS accrual materializes, breaking the margin-recovery narrative.
3. The government formally invokes payout restrictions against RTX, forcing a dividend cut or freeze.

## Conclusion

RTX is a high-quality, dual-cycle aerospace and defense franchise with a broad-based margin recovery, strong free cash flow, a deleveraged balance sheet, a fresh credit upgrade, and a backlog levered to global defense spending and the commercial aftermarket. These attributes support a strong Financial Health and Competitive Position read and a constructive 12-month bias.

At ~26x forward EPS for ~9% revenue growth with stable rather than rising estimates, the dual-cycle recovery is largely priced. The case for waiting rests on three specific, falsifiable items: the absence of an estimate-revision inflection that would drive re-rating, a novel regulatory threat to the capital-return thesis (the January 2026 Executive Order and Hawley-Warren bill), and an open SEC GTF-disclosure investigation with an appellate tail. The analyst range ($180 low, $215 mean, $242 high) captures this: meaningful upside if execution continues, but a floor at the current price reflecting the dated downside catalysts. We rate RTX **HOLD** (growth hold), with a path to BUY on a pullback toward $150–160 or a clean resolution of the payout-restriction overhang alongside resumed buybacks.
