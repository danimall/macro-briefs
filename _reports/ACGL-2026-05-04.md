## Executive Summary

Arch Capital Group is a Bermuda-domiciled specialty insurer, reinsurer, and mortgage insurer with $26.9 billion in capital, delivering a 20.1% net income ROAE in FY2025 on $19.9 billion in revenue. The company generated $6.2 billion in operating cash flow, repurchased $1.9 billion in stock, and grew book value per share by 22.6% while absorbing the California wildfire losses and a near-doubling of its effective tax rate from Bermuda's new 15% CIT.

The investment thesis hinges on whether the market is adequately pricing Arch's cycle management capability during a softening P&C market. The stock trades at ~1.4x book and ~10x FY2026 consensus EPS. For a franchise consistently delivering 17-20% ROE with proven discipline to shrink when pricing deteriorates, this represents a reasonable entry point. The Q1 2026 results (81.7% combined ratio, 75% underwriting income growth YoY) demonstrate earnings quality remains intact even as management signals a $250M premium headwind from walking away from underpriced business.

The primary risk is that the softening cycle extends and compresses returns toward 12-14% ROE, at which point the current P/BV premium becomes less justified. Partially offsetting this: the mortgage segment continues to deliver near-zero combined ratios, the investment portfolio ($47.4B, AA-quality) benefits from sustained higher rates, and catastrophe exposure remains disciplined at 25% of tangible equity.

## Company Overview

Arch Capital operates three segments: Insurance (47% of NPW) writing specialty P&C globally, Reinsurance (46% of NPW) providing property and casualty capacity from Bermuda and multiple international platforms, and Mortgage (6% of NPW) offering U.S. primary mortgage insurance and international mortgage reinsurance. The August 2024 MCE Acquisition (Allianz's U.S. MidCorp/Entertainment business) materially expanded the insurance segment.

The company's structural differentiation is the three-segment model itself. The mortgage segment has produced $1B+ in annual underwriting income for four consecutive years at combined ratios of 12-15%. This provides a low-volatility earnings floor that pure P&C peers lack, enabling more aggressive cycle management in the property and casualty books without threatening overall ROE.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated. Revenue growth has been exceptional ($9.6B → $19.9B in three years), driven by hard market pricing and the MCE Acquisition. But the forward outlook is decelerating:

- Management explicitly stated the market is "clearly more competitive" heading into 2026
- Property catastrophe rates fell 10-20% at January 1, 2026 renewals
- Signaled ~$250M net premium headwind for 2026 from underwriting discipline
- Reinsurance NPW declined 1.7% in FY2025 and 6% in Q1 2026
- Q1 2026 GPW declined 0.6%, NPW declined 3.7%

Consensus estimates reflect this: FY2026 EPS at $9.31 (down from ~$11.60 trailing), FY2027 at $10.02. Estimates have drifted lower over 90 days (current quarter -3.0%, next year -1.5%).

Arch's revenue decline is voluntary, not operational. The company is shrinking books where pricing is inadequate. This historically precedes outperformance when the cycle turns. The insurance segment still grew NPW 13.4% in FY2025 driven by the MCE integration. Casualty rates in North America are tracking above loss trends, providing selective growth opportunities.

Growth vectors: (1) MCE integration ramp (still early, $193M in intangible amortization flowing through), (2) casualty pricing adequacy enabling selective expansion, (3) investment income growth as portfolio rolls into higher yields, (4) mortgage volume stability with housing activity.

## Competitive Landscape

Arch operates in markets with high barriers to entry (regulatory capital requirements, rating agency oversight, distribution relationships, underwriting expertise). Its competitive position is stable-to-improving:

**Improving factors:**
- MCE Acquisition added North American specialty scale and distribution
- Cycle management discipline is a competitive advantage as market softens — peers chasing volume at inadequate rates will suffer later
- Investment portfolio size ($47.4B) generates meaningful income regardless of underwriting environment
- Mortgage segment dominance providing earnings stability peers lack

**Stable factors:**
- Financial strength ratings enable access to business
- Diversified geographic and product platform limits concentration risk
- Deep analytics capabilities in specialty lines

**Deteriorating factors:**
- Bermuda tax advantage erosion (though affects all domiciled peers equally)
- Alternative capital in property cat reinsurance compresses margins
- International casualty pricing "tracking slightly below loss trends"

The company's willingness to shrink proactively is the single most important competitive differentiation in (re)insurance. When peers maintain volume at inadequate rates, they generate losses that materialize 2-4 years later. Arch's history of avoiding these vintage years and then deploying capital when rates harden has driven its multi-cycle outperformance.

## Risk Assessment

**1. P&C Cycle Softening (High probability, moderate impact):** If softening extends beyond property into casualty, ROE could compress to 13-15% range. At that ROE level, the current 1.4x P/BV becomes harder to justify.

**2. Reserve Adequacy ($24.5B net reserves):** The 90th percentile simulation shows reserves could be $4.5B higher than booked. Casualty reserves grew 24% YoY in reinsurance. Industry-wide social inflation could force reserve strengthening. $4.5B adverse development would represent ~$12/share pre-tax impact, though this is a tail scenario.

**3. Catastrophe Concentration:** Peak zone PML of $1.9B (1-in-250 year) represents ~8% of equity. An uncorrelated catastrophe on top of portfolio volatility could strain capital. Management limits cat exposure to 25% of tangible equity.

**4. Mortgage Housing Downturn:** Delinquency rate edging up (2.09% → 2.17%). PMIER sufficiency declining (186% → 179%). Realistic Disaster Scenario estimates $931M maximum loss. Given the current rate/affordability environment, mortgage credit deterioration is a plausible medium-term risk.

**5. Estimate Revision Momentum:** Negative across all periods — current quarter estimates down 3.0% over 90 days, next year down 1.5%. This represents a mild headwind for near-term price action.

## Conclusion

ACGL remains a BUY at $93.82. The stock trades at 10x forward earnings for a franchise delivering 17-20% ROE with $6.2B in annual operating cash flow, conservative leverage (10.1% debt-to-capital), and a proven history of outperforming through P&C cycles. The softening market creates headline risk but is the environment where Arch's cycle management discipline generates its widest competitive advantage versus less disciplined peers.

The score trajectory shows a gradual decline (8.1 → 7.9) primarily driven by weakening growth prospects as the P&C market softens. This is reflected in the reduced Growth Prospects score (6.5 vs. prior 7.0-7.5). However, the decline in forward premiums is voluntary and intentional — management is executing exactly the discipline that justified owning this company in the first place. Director Houston's $499K open-market purchase at $94.09 on April 30, 2026 provides contemporaneous insider confirmation of the value case near current prices.

The 2:1+ risk/reward to base case ($110), backed by aggressive buybacks compounding book value growth and investment income benefiting from the rate environment, supports maintaining the BUY recommendation. Position sizing at 2-3% remains appropriate given moderate macro sensitivity (catastrophe exposure, housing cycle) and negative near-term estimate revisions.
