## Executive Summary

Renasant Corporation completed a transformative merger with The First Bancshares on April 1, 2025, increasing total assets by 48% to $26.8B and expanding its Southeastern footprint to 277 offices across six states. The integration is tracking well: core systems conversion was completed in August 2025, the adjusted efficiency ratio improved to 57.46% (from 66.30%), and NIM expanded to 3.79%. Run-rate adjusted EPS of $3.06 implies the stock trades at approximately 12.9x normalized earnings — a reasonable multiple for a well-run community bank, but above the 11.7x peer median.

The bull case centers on the first clean quarter (Q1 2026 reporting April 28) demonstrating true earnings power, continued NIM stability, and the remaining ~$137M buyback authorization providing a price floor. The bear case focuses on a disclosed material weakness in internal controls, concentrated C-suite selling in January-February 2026, elevated CRE concentration (33% of loans, 283% of bank capital), and purchase accounting accretion that will fade over time. The stock has rallied 27% from its April 2025 low and sits just 6% below its 52-week high, limiting near-term asymmetry.

## Company Overview

Renasant Corporation operates through Renasant Bank, providing full-service commercial and retail banking across Mississippi, Louisiana, Alabama, Georgia, Florida, and Tennessee. The bank uses a decentralized model: local community bank presidents run their markets while centralized functions (IT, legal, risk, audit) provide infrastructure. Post-merger, the company has over 3,000 employees and $19.05B in loans.

The loan portfolio is tilted toward real estate: non-owner occupied CRE (32.8%), 1-4 family residential (24.3%), owner-occupied CRE (17.5%), C&I (14.8%), and construction/land development (10.0%). Revenue is primarily net interest income, supplemented by mortgage banking, wealth management ($6.87B AUM/AUA), SBA lending, and Republic Business Credit (factoring/ABL).

## Financial Analysis

**Balance Sheet:** Total assets of $26.75B, deposits of $21.47B (23.5% noninterest-bearing, zero brokered), and shareholders' equity of $3.88B. Goodwill of $1.41B (36% of equity) reflects the acquisition-driven growth strategy. Tangible book value per share is approximately $25.95, meaning the stock trades at 1.52x tangible book. Total capital ratio of 14.78% and CET1 of 11.24% provide adequate buffers above well-capitalized minimums.

**Profitability:** GAAP EPS of $2.07 was depressed by $49.3M in merger expenses and a $66.6M Day 1 CECL provision (covering both loans and unfunded commitments). Adjusted EPS of $3.06 better reflects ongoing earning power. Net interest income grew 57% to $804M. The NIM of 3.79% includes 22bps of purchase accounting accretion ($48.9M); adjusted NIM of 3.57% is still a meaningful improvement from 3.31% in 2024, driven by deposit cost reductions (2.10% from 2.42%).

**Credit Quality:** Net charge-offs of 0.15% remain low. ACL coverage at 1.54% of loans and 167% of nonperforming loans is adequate. The concerning data point is the 124% increase in 30-89 day past-due loans to $89.2M and elevated NPL rates in non-medical office (6.72%) and senior housing (3.71%) sub-segments.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $271.5M and free cash flow of $237.8M support the dividend (~2.3% yield) and buyback program comfortably. FCF yield on market cap is approximately 6.5%.

**Efficiency:** The adjusted efficiency ratio of 57.46% represents a roughly 9-point improvement over the prior year and suggests merger synergies are being captured. For a bank this size, sub-58% efficiency is competitive.

## Growth Analysis

Forward growth is driven by three vectors:

1. **Merger cost synergies still accruing.** The efficiency ratio likely improves further as residual integration costs wash out. Full-year 2026 should represent the first truly clean period.

2. **Organic loan growth.** Management has significant capacity — FHLB availability of $5.57B and Fed Discount Window access of $657M. The Southeastern markets served tend to grow faster than the national average, supporting mid-single-digit loan growth.

3. **Earnings trajectory.** Consensus estimates project $3.58 EPS in FY2026 and $3.85 in FY2027 — representing 17% and 7.5% growth respectively. Estimate revisions have been modestly positive: +3.3% on current quarter over 90 days, +1.8% on full-year estimates.

The primary growth headwind is purchase accounting accretion fade. The $48.9M contribution in 2025 will decline each year, creating a 22bps NIM headwind that must be offset by organic NIM management or volume growth.

- 19.0x trailing GAAP EPS ($2.07) — inflated by merger charges
- 12.9x adjusted 2025 EPS ($3.06)
- 11.0x FY2026 consensus ($3.58)
- 10.2x FY2027 consensus ($3.85)
- 0.96x stated book value ($41.05)
- 1.52x tangible book value (~$25.95)

**Peer comparison:** The regional bank peer median P/E is 11.7x. RNST's 11.0x on FY2026E is roughly in-line, not discounted despite the integration risk and material weakness disclosure. The 2.3% dividend yield is below the 2.9% peer median. The 1.52x tangible book multiple is justified if the bank can sustain mid-teens ROTCE, which the adjusted earnings power implies.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus mean of $43.86 with a range of $43-$45, implying 9-14% upside. This is a relatively tight range with limited dispersion, suggesting limited debate among the seven covering analysts. Current consensus is skewed bullish with 2 Strong Buy, 4 Buy, and 1 Hold.

The valuation is fair-to-slightly-rich relative to peers. The market is pricing in successful integration and earnings growth, leaving limited margin of safety if credit quality deteriorates or synergy realization disappoints.

## Competitive Landscape

RNST operates in moderately competitive Southeastern markets against national banks (JPM, BAC), super-regionals (Regions, Hancock Whitney), and numerous community banks. Its competitive position is stable: the decentralized model and long-standing community relationships provide natural customer retention, while the doubled asset base creates economies of scale in compliance, technology, and treasury management that smaller competitors cannot match.

The zero-brokered-deposit funding base is a structural advantage, signaling genuine customer franchise value rather than rate-chasing hot money. The 23.5% noninterest-bearing mix, while likely to decline as depositors remain rate-aware, is still healthy.

The primary competitive vulnerability is that mid-size banks ($20-50B) are too small to compete on technology with the largest banks and too large to differentiate purely on relationship banking. RNST must continue investing in digital capabilities while maintaining its community presence.

## Risk Assessment

**Material Weakness (High Impact, Moderate Probability of Negative Outcome):** The disclosed failure in segregation of duties for manual journal entries is a governance red flag. While management states no misstatements occurred, this disclosure creates headline risk, could delay future acquisitions, and must be remediated before the next annual audit. It is likely an integration artifact (two accounting systems merged), but it signals that the integration was more operationally difficult than the earnings suggest.

**CRE Concentration (High Impact, Low-Moderate Probability):** Non-owner occupied CRE at 283% of bank capital approaches the 300% regulatory threshold. A 5-10% decline in CRE values across the portfolio would create meaningful charge-off pressure. The non-medical office sub-segment (6.72% NPL rate) is the most vulnerable.

**Insider Selling Pattern (Moderate Signal):** Four senior executives sold shares in January-February 2026 (CFO, General Counsel, SEVP, and CAO) with zero offsetting open-market purchases. The concentration is worth flagging at a price level ($37-41) that the stock is now trading through.

**Purchase Accounting Accretion Fade:** Assuming a 5-7 year amortization schedule, the declining accretion creates an annual headwind of roughly $7-10M in pre-tax income.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** Asset sensitivity means a 200bp rate cut would reduce NII by approximately 10.5% over 13-24 months. In the current macro environment where rates are more likely to remain elevated or rise, this is actually a positive factor.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 18.4% sits well below the 27.3% historical volatility — a -8.9pp spread indicating the options market is pricing minimal near-term risk despite Q1 earnings reporting imminently (April 28). The term structure is in steep contango (18.4% near-term vs. 35.4% at 6 months), which is atypical ahead of a major catalyst. The elevated near-term put/call skew (92.8%) diverges from the low IV level — someone is paying for downside protection despite cheap overall vol. This pattern suggests informed hedging activity ahead of the earnings report. With only 24 contracts across the chain, liquidity is minimal and the signal should be interpreted cautiously. For fundamental investors, the cheap IV creates a favorable environment to establish positions or add upside calls at below-realized-vol cost.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($44-45, +12-14%):** Q1 2026 earnings demonstrate clean run-rate earnings power of $0.90+/quarter. Efficiency ratio remains below 58%. Credit quality stabilizes. The market rerated RNST upward in February (to $42.11) when early Q4 results indicated merger success; a similar catalyst from Q1 results is plausible. Buyback authorization provides a floor. At 10x forward earnings with mid-teens ROTCE, the stock could rerate to $44-46 on peer convergence.

**Bear Case ($33-34, -14-16%):** Material weakness remediation takes longer than expected, creating audit delays or disclosure concerns. CRE losses accelerate as early-stage delinquencies cascade into charge-offs. Purchase accounting accretion fades faster than expected. The stock reverts to the $33-34 level where it traded in October-November 2025.

## Conclusion

Renasant is executing a major bank merger integration that appears on track operationally, with strong efficiency gains and NIM expansion. The fundamental trajectory supports mid-teens earnings growth over the next 12 months. However, the stock has already rallied substantially and trades at a slight premium to peer medians, leaving limited asymmetry at current levels. The material weakness disclosure, insider selling cluster, and CRE concentration add idiosyncratic risks not fully reflected in the consensus view. At $39.35, the risk/reward is approximately balanced. A strong, clean Q1 quarter with no governance surprises would support an upgrade. Until then, HOLD with a growth orientation.
