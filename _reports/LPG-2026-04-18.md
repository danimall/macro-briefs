## Executive Summary

Dorian LPG is a pure-play VLGC (Very Large Gas Carrier) operator with 27 vessels generating nearly all revenue through the Helios Pool, a spot-market-oriented joint venture with Mitsui OSK Lines. The stock has rallied 91% from its April 2025 lows near $19 to $36.26, driven by a recovery in VLGC freight rates from the tariff-induced trough of ~$15,000/day to $50,333/day in the December 2025 quarter. Despite the rally, shares trade at 12.4x trailing earnings and roughly 8x forward estimates, with a current annualized dividend yield near 7.7% ($2.80/share at the most recent quarterly rate of $0.70).

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a recovering freight rate environment with management noting continued demand strength into Q4 FY2026; (2) fleet market value exceeding book value by $590M, providing substantial downside protection; and (3) aggressive insider buying by the founder/CEO at $17.75 and $27.30 during periods of maximum uncertainty. The primary risk is the company's near-total exposure to spot VLGC rates, which collapsed 63% in a single move when China imposed 84% tariffs on US LPG in early 2025. A 111-vessel orderbook through 2029 (~27% of existing fleet) represents a medium-term supply overhang. At current valuation, the risk-reward favors ownership with a base case target of $42-44.

## Company Overview

Dorian LPG, incorporated in the Marshall Islands and headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut, operates one of the largest independent VLGC fleets globally. The company's 27 vessels (21 owned, 6 time-chartered-in) have an aggregate capacity of approximately 2.1 million cubic meters and an average owned-fleet age of ~10 years, younger than the ~12-year global average.

All vessels operate through the Helios Pool, a 50/50 joint venture with MOL Energia, which commercially manages 28 VLGCs. The pool accounted for 97% of FY2025 revenues. This structure provides operational scale and scheduling efficiency but means Dorian has effectively zero fixed-rate revenue visibility beyond the current quarter.

The fleet includes 20 ECO-design vessels, 5 dual-fuel capable ships (can burn LPG as fuel), and 16 scrubber-equipped vessels. A VLGC/AC newbuilding (93,000 cbm, capable of transporting LPG or ammonia) is scheduled for delivery from Hanwha Ocean in Q2 2026, providing first-mover exposure to the nascent ammonia transport market.

### Cash Flow and Capital Return

FY2025 FCF was $154M ($173M operating cash flow less $19M capex), representing a 45% FCF margin even in a weak rate year. Since IPO, the company has returned over $960M to shareholders through variable dividends and buybacks. The dividend has tracked freight rates: $1.00/quarter in FY2024 peak, cut to $0.50/quarter in May 2025 during the tariff trough, and recovering to $0.70/quarter by November 2025 and February 2026.

Capital allocation has been disciplined. Management issued 2M shares at $44.50 near the FY2024 cycle peak, raising $84.4M, and then the CEO personally bought shares at $17.75 and $27.30 during the trough. The newbuilding commitment ($86.4M remaining) is the only significant capex obligation ahead.

## Growth Analysis

Growth for Dorian is a function of three variables: fleet size, freight rates, and fleet utilization.

**Fleet:** Adding 1 vessel (the VLGC/AC newbuilding) in Q2 2026, growing the fleet from 27 to 28 — roughly 3.7% capacity growth. No additional newbuilding orders have been placed.

**Rate environment:** VLGC rates have recovered to the $50K+/day range, supported by continued US LPG export growth (>5% YoY), PDH plant expansions in Asia, and partial normalization of US-China trade flows following the tariff escalation. Management noted on the Q3 call that "a new export record" was set and demand remained strong into Q4 FY2026.

**Estimate trajectory:** Current-year EPS consensus of $4.51 and next-year of $3.44 imply a modest rate moderation. The 30-day revision trend is mixed — current-year estimates dipped 3% while next-year estimates rose 16%. This divergence likely reflects near-term seasonal softness expectations offset by structural demand improvement.

**Ammonia optionality:** The VLGC/AC vessel arriving March 2026 can transport ammonia, positioning Dorian for the emerging clean ammonia trade. The market is nascent, with uncertain demand timing, but the option value is not reflected in the current share price. If ammonia-as-fuel gains traction for maritime decarbonization, this vessel class could command meaningful rate premiums.

The 111-vessel VLGC orderbook through 2029 (27% of existing fleet) is the key structural headwind. If global LPG trade growth doesn't absorb this supply — particularly if tariff disruptions persist — the freight rate recovery could stall or reverse.

## Competitive Landscape

Dorian operates in a fragmented but concentrated VLGC market. Key competitors include BW LPG (largest VLGC owner-operator with ~40 vessels), NYK Line, and Petredec. With 27 vessels, Dorian is the second-largest pure-play VLGC company.

**Advantages:**
- Younger fleet (10 vs. 12 years average) provides lower fuel costs and longer remaining economic life
- Helios Pool scale (28 vessels) improves scheduling efficiency and commercial positioning
- Scrubber penetration (16 of 27 vessels) delivers fuel cost savings in a widening high/low-sulfur spread environment
- Dual-fuel capability on 5 vessels positions for tightening IMO emissions regulations

**Disadvantages:**
- 97% spot exposure vs. BW LPG's more balanced charter profile
- Single vessel-type concentration vs. diversified shipping peers
- Underperformed BW LPG's 5-year TSR (525% vs. 593%)
- Marshall Islands incorporation with combined CEO/Chairman role raises governance questions vs. listed peers

The moat is narrow. VLGC shipping is capital-intensive but commoditized — no operator has meaningful pricing power. Competitive advantage derives from fleet efficiency, scale within pools, and capital allocation through the cycle. Dorian scores adequately on the first two and well on the third.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. US-China Tariff Escalation (HIGH).** China's 84% tariff on US LPG in early 2025 caused rates to collapse 63% in a single move. While the situation has partially normalized (reduced to 10% for 12 months), any re-escalation would hit Dorian's earnings immediately given full spot exposure. The US-China LPG trade represented 14% of global seaborne LPG volumes in 2024.

**2. VLGC Supply Overhang (MEDIUM-HIGH).** 111 vessels on order through 2029 represent 27% of the existing fleet. If US LPG export growth slows or tariffs structurally redirect trade flows, the supply influx could depress rates for an extended period.

**3. Spot Concentration (HIGH).** With 97% spot revenue, earnings visibility is approximately 0-3 months. The dividend, while generous at current rates, has already been cut twice in the past year. Investors treating the 7.7% yield as stable income will be disappointed when rates soften.

**4. Panama Canal Constraints.** Over 90% of US-to-Asia LPG voyages transit the Panama Canal. Toll restructuring, slot limitations, and potential drought conditions add cost and scheduling uncertainty.

**5. USTR Chinese-Vessel Fees.** Two time-chartered-in vessels are owned by a Chinese financial institution. The proposed USTR port service fees on Chinese-connected vessels are suspended until November 2026 but could create a cost headwind if implemented.

**6. Earnings Beat/Miss Record.** Dorian missed consensus estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters, including a 61% miss in June 2025 and a 67% miss in March 2025. The June and March misses were during the tariff-driven rate collapse, so context matters, but this record warrants skepticism on forward consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($44-48)

VLGC freight rates sustain at $50K+/day through FY2026 and into FY2027 as US LPG export growth continues at 5%+ annually, Chinese tariffs remain at the current 10% level, and the VLGC orderbook is partially absorbed by scrapping of older vessels and delays. At $50K/day rates, EPS runs at $5.00+ annually, putting the stock at 7x earnings at current prices. A 10-12x P/E on $4.50-5.00 EPS yields $45-60. The ammonia VLGC/AC delivery creates option value. The dividend stabilizes at $0.70-1.00/quarter ($2.80-4.00 annualized).

### Bear Case ($22-26)

US-China tariffs re-escalate beyond 10%, disrupting trade flows and collapsing rates below $30K/day. The 111-vessel orderbook begins delivering into a weak demand environment, structurally depressing freight rates for 2-3 years. EPS declines to $1.50-2.00, the dividend is cut to $0.25/quarter or suspended, and the stock de-rates to 12-14x trough earnings. This scenario implies 30-40% downside from current levels, partially offset by asset-backed value near book plus fleet premium.

## Conclusion

Dorian LPG offers an asymmetric setup at ~8x forward earnings with 7.7% current dividend yield, a balance sheet carrying $590M in hidden fleet value, and a CEO who bought shares with personal funds at $17.75 during the tariff crisis. The positive estimate revision momentum aligns with the recovering freight rate environment. The stock is priced for rates meaningfully below current levels, creating a margin of safety if the rate recovery merely sustains rather than accelerates.

The risks are concentrated: full spot exposure means earnings can collapse as quickly as they recovered, and the orderbook creates a medium-term supply overhang. The 3-of-4 earnings miss record warrants calibrated expectations on forward consensus.

At $36.26, the risk-reward skews favorably. The asset-backed floor limits downside to roughly 8-10% before reaching fleet premium-adjusted book value, while the base case offers 16-21% upside plus yield. BUY with 2-3% position sizing given the inherent cyclical volatility.
