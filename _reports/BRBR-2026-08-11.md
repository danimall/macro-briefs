## Executive Summary

BellRing's revenue is at an all-time high and its profit dollars have fallen by roughly 40%. Nine-month FY2026 net sales of $1,706.4M grew 2.3%, while gross margin compressed 640bp to 28.5% and adjusted diluted EPS fell 51% to $0.81. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA is guided to $275-295M against a derived FY2025 figure of approximately $467-482M — an unusual outcome for a branded staples company whose category (RTD protein) is growing 11%. Four mechanisms account for the compression: whey and non-fat dry milk inflation (expected to persist through FY2027, hedged only ~6 months forward), freight (~140bp of second-half headwind), tariffs (~80bp), and a $28M demand-planning failure in which lower-priced Tetra Pak cartons cannibalized bottled shakes faster than the supply chain could flex.

This is the fourth consecutive quarter of assessment on this name and the first upgrade. The prior AVOID rested on two planks: falling earnings power into a levered balance sheet, and an unfunded ~$86M litigation obligation arriving as leverage rose. The first plank stands — estimates continue to fall, with FY2026 consensus cut 16.1% over 90 days to $1.04. The second has resolved: the New York and multistate Joint Juice settlements were payable on or before August 10, 2026 and were fully accrued at June 30; the $840M of 7.00% notes do not mature until March 2030 (revolver springing to December 2029); the total net leverage covenant is 6.00x against management-stated 3.2x at Q3, going to ~4.0x at fiscal year end. The notes traded at $840.2M against $840.0M principal at June 30 — essentially par. Credit holders are pricing an earnings reset, not a solvency event, and the arithmetic supports them: EBITDA would need to fall roughly another one-third at constant net debt to approach the covenant.

At $11.53 the equity is 8.8x FY2026E EV/EBITDA (including the settlement as debt-like) and 8.7x FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.32. Our scenario work puts base case at $14.00 (+21%), bull at $18.50 (+61%) and bear at $8.50 (-26%), for a probability-weighted $13.70, or +19%. That is a positive but unremarkable expected return with wide dispersion, resting on an unverified assumption: that a double-digit list price increase on Premier shakes effective Q1 FY2027 sticks with elasticity "slightly greater than one." Management's own Tetra Pak experience is evidence that this consumer base is more price-sensitive than modeled. We move to HOLD with low confidence and no urgency to buy ahead of November FY2027 guidance.

## Company Overview

BellRing operates two brands in ready-to-drink protein and sports nutrition powders. Premier Protein — the #1 RTD protein brand by the company's account, with the highest household penetration and repeat rate in the category — plus Dymatize powders account for essentially all revenue. RTD shakes were 81.7% of FY2025 net sales and 80.5% of nine-month FY2026 net sales ($1,374.4M of $1,706.4M).

The model is radically asset-light: approximately 530 employees run a $2.3B revenue business, net property was $19.0M at September 30, 2025, and FY2025 capex was $4.7M (under 0.3% of sales). This produces exceptional returns on the asset base (FY2025 ROA 23.0%, asset turnover 2.46x) and concentrates the vulnerabilities outside the company. BellRing owns no plants. Its largest third-party contract manufacturer supplies 46.3% of Premier RTD shake volume, with 28.0% from a single facility; 11oz packaging comes from one supplier across three locations, and the contract manufacturers use that supplier's equipment; one supplier provides the majority of milk-based protein. Walmart (including Sam's Club), Costco and Amazon together were 74.0% of FY2025 net sales.

BellRing was carved out of Post Holdings (IPO 2019, separation 2022). Chairman Robert V. Vitale is simultaneously an officer and director of Post. The Master Services Agreement expired in March 2026, so FY2026 is the first year with a fully standalone cost base. A co-packing arrangement with Comet Processing, a wholly-owned Post subsidiary in which BellRing is the paying customer, nearly doubled to $80.6M for the nine months from $44.2M.

Michael C. Axelrod became President and CEO effective July 29, 2026, replacing Darcy Horn Davenport, who led the business for more than a decade. Axelrod was on his seventh day at the company during the August 4 earnings call. CFO Paul Rode remains.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth has decelerated from 16-20% to low single digits, and the composition is unfavorable.** Nine-month net sales rose 2.3% on volume +4.7% and price/mix −2.4%. Within Premier Protein RTD shakes — four-fifths of the company — nine-month net sales grew 0.5% on volume +5.2% and price/mix −4.7%. The flagship moved 5% more units for essentially no incremental revenue. Dymatize is the offset: Q3 net sales +26.7% on volume +6.0% and price/mix +20.7%.

**Margin compression, quarter by quarter.** Q3 FY2026 gross margin was 28.6% versus 35.4%, a 680bp decline. Q3 adjusted EBITDA was $78.3M (13.7% of sales) versus $120.3M (22.0%), a 35% decline and 830bp of compression. Nine-month adjusted EBITDA was $222.4M. Implied Q4: $52.6-72.6M on ~$648.6M of sales, a ~9.7% margin at the midpoint, consistent with management's "~10%" statement.

A useful derived observation: nine-month FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was $364.2M (21.8%). Against a derived FY2025 full-year figure of $467-482M, that implies Q4 FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $103-118M on $648.7M of sales — a 16-18% margin. The margin break therefore began in the September 2025 quarter, before FY2026 and before the CEO change, which is corroborated independently by the FY2025 10-K's disclosure of $72.1M of higher net product costs. That sequencing matters: input cost inflation, not the Tetra Pak error, is the larger driver. (Caveat: the FY2025 full-year adjusted EBITDA figure is derived from GAAP operating profit of $357.4M plus $18.6M D&A plus the $69.0M legal provision plus an estimated $22-24M of stock compensation; it is not a filed number.)

**Working capital is the acute problem.** Inventories rose from $330.4M to $480.6M (+45.5%), with finished products up 69.4% to $409.9M, against 2.3% sales growth. On nine-month COGS of $1,220.6M annualized, that is roughly 108 days of inventory versus ~78 days on the FY2025 basis — a 30-day build. Raw materials actually declined ($88.3M to $70.4M), confirming this is finished goods.

**Cash flow followed.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $65.0M versus $91.5M, with $8.1M of capex, so free cash flow of $56.9M. The inventory build consumed $150.7M, partly offset by a $78.4M increase in payables. Management stated Q3 alone generated $79M of operating cash flow, which implies the six months to March 31, 2026 consumed roughly $14M — a striking figure for a branded staples business with $1.14B of half-year sales, though it is arithmetic on a management-stated quarterly number rather than a filed six-month statement, and BellRing's quarters are seasonal.

**Balance sheet.** Debt at June 30 was $1,140.0M ($840M notes at 7.00%, $300M revolver at 5.62%) against $50.4M cash, so net debt of $1,089.6M. Revolver availability was $197.6M on the $500M facility. Post the ~$86M settlement payment, net debt is approximately $1,176M and net leverage on guided FY2026 EBITDA of $285M is ~4.1x, matching management's ~4.0x exit guidance. Covenant headroom: at 6.00x, permitted net debt is $1,710M, or equivalently EBITDA could fall to ~$196M at constant net debt. Nine-month interest expense of $60.0M was up 21.7%; coverage on nine-month adjusted EBITDA is 3.7x.

Two structural features require correct interpretation. The $(467.2)M stockholders' deficit is an artifact of the Post separation plus $910.1M of cumulative treasury stock — retained earnings are positive $272.6M. Separately, total assets of $1,052.1M are less than total debt of $1,140.0M; lender recovery depends entirely on brand cash flows. The FY2025-based Altman Z of 4.2 is stale and structurally distorted; re-running the EBIT/assets term on annualized nine-month FY2026 operating profit reduces it by roughly 0.38 on its own.

**Normalized cash generation is the strongest part of the bull case.** At $320M of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA, $85M of cash interest, $10M of capex and ~$45-50M of cash taxes, free cash flow lands around $130-160M with no working capital release assumed — a 10-12% yield on the current market capitalization. That cash currently goes to the revolver, which transfers value to equity at constant enterprise value at roughly $1.10-1.35 per share per year. The $506.85M of remaining buyback authorization is paused.

## Growth Analysis

The category is expanding faster than the company. Per company-cited Circana data for the 13 weeks ended June 28, 2026, the wellness category grew 8% and RTD within it grew 11%, while Premier Protein RTD dollar consumption grew 6.0% and Premier powders declined 4.2%.

The velocity math is the central issue. In Q3, Premier RTD total distribution points rose 27% and household penetration rose 21.9%, yet dollar consumption grew 6.0%. Dollars per point of distribution and per buying household both declined. Three alternative explanations deserve weight: new distribution ramps to mature velocity over several quarters, not within the same 13 weeks; Circana MULO+C under-measures club, where Premier over-indexes with Costco and Sam's; and the dollar figure is mechanically depressed by discounting, so unit share loss is smaller than dollar share loss (nine-month Premier RTD volume did grow 5.2%). Granting all three, the pattern is a leader defending with price and shelf space in a market growing faster than it is.

**The forward growth levers are specific and dated.** A double-digit price increase on Premier shakes plus a third Dymatize powder increase, both effective Q1 FY2027, with a price umbrella in place — management noted another major RTD player took a double-digit increase earlier in 2026 and a peer announced a high-single-digit increase. Innovation: Premier Protein "Ultimate" (42g) and Premier Protein Sparkling Protein Soda, both rolling into mass, food and eCommerce in Q4 FY2026. Convenience channel entry via regional DSD partnerships beginning FY2027, addressing a channel that is ~10% of the RTD category where BellRing has minimal presence. The June 2026 realignment carries $10-12M of annualized run-rate savings, mostly landing in FY2027, partly offset by a bonus reset. And $28M of FY2026 inventory-related impacts (~120bp of margin) should not recur.

**Estimate revisions cut both ways, and the split is informative.** Current-quarter consensus fell 37.7% over 30 days to $0.23 — the fiscal Q4 trough. Next-quarter consensus rose 7.6% to $0.36 — the quarter in which pricing lands. FY2026 fell 16.1% over 90 days to $1.04; FY2027 fell 9.5% to $1.32, but only 2.4% over the last 30 days. Analysts are marking down the trough and marking up the recovery quarter, which is coherent with the announced pricing calendar. Against that, the company has missed three of the last four quarters, including by 56.5% and 20.1%, so confidence in the $1.32 should be modest.

FY2027 consensus of $1.32 versus FY2026's $1.04 embeds 27% earnings growth off a trough. Whether that materializes is entirely a function of realized elasticity.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market cap | $1,340M |
| Net debt (post-settlement) | ~$1,176M |
| Enterprise value | ~$2,516M |
| EV / FY2026E adj. EBITDA ($285M mid) | 8.8x |
| EV / derived FY2025 adj. EBITDA (~$467M) | 5.4x |
| P/E on FY2026 consensus $1.04 | 11.1x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $1.32 | 8.7x |
| Normalized FCF yield (FY2027E $130-160M) | 9.7-11.9% |
| Debt as share of EV | ~47% |

The peer set is of limited use. Packaged foods comparables trade at a 31.2x median P/E with a 4.0% median yield (FDP 19.9x, DMC 43.6x, FLO 21.5x, JJSF 35.6x, TR 31.2x), but several of those multiples reflect depressed earnings rather than premium quality, and every one of them pays a dividend while BellRing pays none. The relevant benchmark is BellRing's own normalized earnings power and its leverage.

Because debt is 47% of enterprise value, a 20% change in EV produces roughly a 36% change in equity value. That gearing defines the scenario spread. Holding the current 8.5x EV/EBITDA multiple:

- **$290M FY2027 EBITDA at 7.5x** (pricing largely offset by elasticity and continued promotion; multiple compresses on confirmed share loss) → EV $2,175M − ~$1,180M net debt = $995M = **$8.56/share**
- **$320M at 8.5x** (non-recurring items reverse, realignment savings land, pricing roughly offsets mid-single-digit input inflation) → EV $2,720M − ~$1,120M = $1,600M = **$13.76/share**
- **$385M at 8.5x** (pricing recovers roughly half the ~800bp margin gap, elasticity at or below 1.0) → EV $3,272M − ~$1,100M = $2,172M = **$18.68/share**

The current price sits close to the low end of that band, which is the substance of the value case: at $11.53 the market is paying approximately nothing for the pricing action working. Street targets bracket this — mean $14.71, median $14.50, low $10, high $20, with DA Davidson at $15 and Deutsche Bank at $13. The low target being only slightly below spot indicates the sell side sees limited downside; we think that understates the gearing.

We score valuation 5.5 rather than higher. Cheapness on FY2027 consensus is real, but the multiple rests on estimates that have been cut four times in a company that has missed three of four quarters, and the low multiple partly reflects genuine competitive erosion rather than pure sentiment. A compressed multiple on a business losing category share is not automatically a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

BellRing's structural advantages are intact: the #1 RTD protein brand, highest household penetration and repeat rate, ~23% household penetration, distribution in over 90 countries, and an asset-light model producing 23% ROA on sub-1%-of-sales capex.

The competitive set is widening on three fronts. PepsiCo relaunched Muscle Milk with a new Pro lineup in what trade press called its biggest transformation. Clean-label and plant-based entrants such as Elmhurst 1925 have extended into RTD protein as the category broadens beyond fitness users. And management's own term, "insurgent brands," drove unusually heavy promotion in Q2 FY2026 — though the CFO noted they pulled back significantly in Q3, suggesting that promotional intensity is at least partly episodic. Management cited 17% of RTD shake category volume sold on price promotion in Q3.

Two pieces of evidence cut in opposite directions on pricing power. Negatively, Premier RTD price/mix of −4.7% over nine months, concentrated in Club, Mass and eCommerce — the same three accounts that are 74% of revenue. Positively, Dymatize realized +20.7% price/mix in Q3, and competitors moved on price first, which is what a functioning category price structure looks like.

The Tetra Pak episode deserves reading as an elasticity data point rather than only as an execution failure. BellRing introduced cartons at a lower price point in eCommerce, and value-conscious consumers switched out of bottles far faster than modeled. That is a revealed preference measurement on the company's own base, in the channel with the highest price transparency, and it argues that elasticity may exceed the "slightly greater than one" assumption embedded in FY2027 planning. The counterargument is that within-brand format switching at a visible price gap is a different event from a category-wide list price increase under which competitors are also raising — the substitution set is narrower.

One additional disclosure: a Club item representing a low-single-digit percentage of this year's net sales rotates out of the FY2027 assortment. Small in isolation, but it demonstrates that shelf decisions at three accounts move the whole company.

## Risk Assessment

**Elasticity on the Q1 FY2027 price increase.** The single binary that determines whether FY2027 EBITDA is $290M or $385M. First read arrives with the December quarter results in February 2027.

**Securities litigation, unquantified.** *In re: BellRing Brands, Inc. Securities Litigation* (S.D.N.Y.), filed January 22, 2026 against the company, former CEO Davenport and CFO Rode. The amended complaint of May 8, 2026 added Chairman Vitale and expanded the class period to August 6, 2024 – May 4, 2026. On July 28, 2026 the court granted in part and denied in part the motion to dismiss, so claims survive. The company cannot estimate a loss and we found no disclosure of D&O insurance limits. Consolidated derivative actions (D. Del.) were stayed pending that ruling. A partial denial establishes only adequate pleading, not merits, and securities cases frequently settle well below demands — but arriving immediately after an $86M settlement payment, this is a real unaccrued claim on a levered balance sheet.

**Supply chain single points of failure.** 46.3% of Premier RTD shake volume from one contract manufacturer, 28.0% from a single facility, sole-source 11oz packaging, sole-source majority of milk protein, and $1,362.9M of purchase commitments running through FY2033 ($602.7M due in FY2026). With no owned plants and 530 employees, there is no internal fallback.

**Input costs and hedging.** Whey elevated through FY2027, mid-single-digit input inflation guided into FY2027, hedged only ~6 months forward ($168.1M notional, principally non-fat dry milk). Freight rose sharply after the May call on fuel and driver supply. Management guided to ~140bp of incremental freight headwind to second-half margins, and expects these pressures to persist rather than resolve in a single quarter.

**Refinancing.** $840M of 7.00% notes mature March 2030, with the revolver springing to December 2029. Not urgent, but it caps how long deleveraging can be deferred.

**Governance and capital allocation.** Six Item 5.02 officer/director change 8-Ks in twelve months. The related-party co-packing relationship with a Post subsidiary nearly doubled while the Chairman sits on both boards; pricing terms and approval process are not disclosed in what we reviewed. We allege nothing and infer no motive — it is a monitoring item.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 80.5% sits 4.4pp above one-year realized volatility of 76.1%, with a ±13.7% implied move on the nine-day tenor and a flat term structure (near 80.5% versus far 82.4%). Near-term put/call skew is characterized as balanced at −1.1%, and put/call volume is 1.00. The more informative change is positioning: put/call open interest fell from 1.12 on June 18 to 0.43 alongside a 21.8% price gain, so the visible options book has shifted away from downside protection. Short interest is 14.5% of float but only 2.1 days to cover, which means the position is easily unwound and squeeze pressure is limited by ample liquidity — consistent with the August 4 session, when the stock gapped to $10.16 intraday and closed at $12.75. Options are pricing genuine uncertainty at a cost slightly above realized, and the flat term structure indicates the market sees that uncertainty distributed across tenors rather than concentrated in a single event — appropriate given that the decisive information (November guidance, February elasticity read) sits beyond the near-dated expiries.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $18.50 (+61%)

Roughly 400bp of the ~800bp margin loss is demonstrably transitory: $28M of inventory-related impacts (~120bp) do not recur, $10-12M of realignment savings land in FY2027, and the double-digit shake price increase arrives under a category price umbrella that competitors established first. If elasticity comes in at or below 1.0, FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches $380-400M against $285M in FY2026. Volume infrastructure is in place — distribution points up 27%, household penetration up ~22%, two new platforms (Ultimate, Sparkling Soda) launching, convenience channel entry beginning FY2027 in a channel that is 10% of category volume where BellRing has almost no presence. Normalized free cash flow of $130-160M on a $1,340M market capitalization deleverages toward 3.0x within eighteen months and transfers roughly $1.10-1.35 per share per year to equity holders at constant enterprise value. The credit market's par pricing on the 2030 notes is consistent with this reading. At 8.5x, the equity reaches $18.50; a re-rate to 9.5-10x on confirmed margin recovery reaches $20-23, matching the street high.

### Bear Case — $8.50 (−26%)

The velocity data says share is being lost, not just discounted: 27% more distribution and 22% more households produced 6% dollar consumption growth in a category growing 11%. A double-digit list increase into that setup, with cheaper insurgent brands and a relaunched PepsiCo entry on shelf, produces elasticity materially worse than 1.0 — as the Tetra Pak cannibalization already demonstrated on BellRing's own consumer base. Volumes fall, the pricing benefit is partly given back in trade spend at Club, Mass and eCommerce, and FY2027 EBITDA stalls at $290-310M against mid-single-digit input inflation and elevated freight. Net leverage stays near 4.0x, the securities case settles for a material unaccrued amount, and the multiple compresses to 7.5x on a business no longer growing with its category. That is $8.50, close to the June low of $7.82. The capital allocation record does not inspire confidence in the recovery execution: $611.1M of buybacks at an average $44.09 against a current price of $11.53, funded in FY2025 by a $250M net revolver draw that exceeded free cash flow by $220.7M, followed by zero repurchases in May and June 2026 with $506.85M of authorization remaining.

### Base Case — $14.00 (+21%)

Non-recurring items reverse and realignment savings land, but pricing roughly offsets input inflation rather than recovering the margin gap, and Premier continues to trail category growth. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of $320M at 8.5x, less ~$1,120M of net debt after a year of paydown, equals $13.76.

Probability-weighted at 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear: $13.70, or +19% over twelve months, against a −26% bear outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders have adequate compensation for waiting through the trough; new capital has no reason to commit ahead of November guidance. The prior AVOID no longer applies because the specific funding risk it identified — an $86M cash obligation into rising leverage with no maturity buffer — has resolved: the settlements were payable by August 10 and fully accrued, revolver capacity plus Q4 seasonal cash generation covers it, and there is no maturity before December 2029. What remains is a business-quality problem at a price that discounts most of it.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q4 FY2026 results and initial FY2027 guidance in November 2026 (management explicitly deferred FY2027 guidance to that call). Second checkpoint: Q1 FY2027 results in February 2027, the first quarter containing the price increase.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following three:**
1. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $330M or better with gross margin guided above 31%.
2. Q1 FY2027 Premier RTD price/mix positive with volume decline of 5% or less — realized elasticity at or below 1.0.
3. Inventory days back below 90 and net leverage below 3.5x, with either resumed repurchases or revolver paydown of $100M+.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA guidance below $300M, or a guidance range that again embeds new non-recurring charges.
2. Premier RTD volume down more than 8% post-pricing, or dollar consumption growth below 3% while the category grows high single digits — confirmation that the pricing action bought margin at the cost of share.
3. Net leverage above 4.5x, or a fourth consecutive quarter of inventory days above 100.
4. A disclosed securities litigation accrual above roughly $75M, or the loss of a top-three customer item beyond the disclosed Club rotation.

**Downgrade to AVOID** if two of the above coincide with revolver availability falling below $100M.

## Conclusion

BellRing is a genuinely good asset — the #1 brand in a category compounding at 8-11% — that is currently being run at a 12% EBITDA margin instead of 20%, for reasons that are roughly half external (whey, freight, tariffs) and half self-inflicted (a demand-planning failure that tied up $150.7M of working capital and cost $28M in charges and trade spend). The score trajectory of 4.4 → 4.0 → 3.4 tracked a deteriorating operating reality accurately; the move to 4.05 reflects two changes in information rather than any improvement in the business. First, the trough is now identified, quantified and funded: Q4 FY2026 is the low point on margin (~10%), leverage (~4.0x) and free cash flow simultaneously, with the $86M litigation payment behind and no maturity wall until 2029. Second, the recovery mechanics now have dates and magnitudes attached — $28M of non-recurring items, $10-12M of realignment savings, and a double-digit price increase effective in the December quarter under a price umbrella competitors established first.

Against that, the dimensions that matter most for forward returns remain weak. Management quality scores 3.0 on a capital allocation record that deployed $611M of borrowed money into buybacks at an average $44.09 and then stopped at $8-12, plus six officer changes in twelve months and a new CEO with seven days of tenure at the last print. Competitive position scores 3.5 because distribution up 27% and households up 22% delivering 6% consumption growth in an 11% category is share loss, however much of it is discounting rather than defection. And the central assumption — elasticity slightly greater than one on a double-digit increase — is contradicted by the company's own Tetra Pak experience and cannot be verified until February 2027.

Expected return of roughly +19% against a −26% bear case, in an equity where debt is 47% of enterprise value, is approximately fair rather than compelling. Hold what you own through November; commit new capital only on guidance that puts FY2027 EBITDA above $330M or on a price near $9 that restores the margin of safety the current level does not provide.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the covenant-headroom cushion was overstated (report claimed EBITDA "would need to fall roughly another 31%" and permitted EBITDA of ~$196M; management's stated 3.2x implies covenant-basis EBITDA of ~$340M, so at the 6.00x covenant EBITDA could fall to ~$181M — the cushion framing is directionally intact but the specific figures were not derivable as stated); the FY2026 consensus 90-day revision is 16.1% (not 16.6%).
