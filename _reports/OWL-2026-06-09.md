**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Blue Owl Capital is a $314.9B-AUM alternative asset manager built from the 2021 combination of Owl Rock (direct lending) and Dyal Capital (GP stakes), now operating across three platforms: Credit ($159.2B), Real Assets ($85.1B), and GP Strategic Capital ($70.6B). The stock has fallen 51% from its July 2025 high of $21.08 to $9.26, a decline driven almost entirely by a single, fast-moving development: a private-credit redemption wave that forced Blue Owl to cap withdrawals at 5% in its OCIC and OTIC non-traded BDCs in May 2026 and collapsed new sales at OCIC, its largest retail credit fund, by roughly 95%.

This report changes the recommendation to HOLD after four consecutive AVOID ratings, and the change is deliberate rather than a drift. The prior AVOID thesis was correct about the direction of the AUM/flow story but has been overtaken by price. At $9.26, the most bearish sell-side target ($9.00) sits essentially at spot, the consensus mean is $12.93, the median $11.50, zero of fifteen covering analysts rate the stock sell, and the sentiment trend is reported as improving. Maintaining AVOID here requires a falsifiable claim that the stock falls materially from a level the market has already cut in half — and the company's own scenario set does not support that. The bear path to $7–8 implies 14–25% downside before the ~10% dividend cushion, against a re-rating path to $12–15 worth 30–62%. Positive skew of that shape is inconsistent with an AVOID and consistent with a balanced hold.

The central fact that distinguishes HOLD from AVOID is that the cash-earnings engine is still growing. Fee-Related Earnings rose 14% and Distributable Earnings 11% year-over-year in Q1'26, with an FRE margin of 58.4%, and the company beat or matched consensus in all four of the last four quarters. The redemption crisis is hitting AUM, flows, and forward estimates (down 7–11% over 90 days), not yet realized fee earnings. Against that, the risks are real and specific: rising leverage into a credit downturn, a thin ~$190M cash balance, a ~$1.7B Tax Receivable Agreement that drains roughly $65M per quarter to pre-IPO owners, an April 2026 Section 36(b) excessive-fee suit, and a founder-controlled Up-C governance structure. The positives and negatives roughly cancel, which is the definition of an uncertain hold.

## Company Overview

Blue Owl manages $314.9B in AUM and $188.4B in fee-paying AUM (FPAUM) as of 3/31/2026, run as a single "one-firm" reportable segment across three scaled platforms:

- **Credit ($159.2B AUM, $98.9B FPAUM)** — the largest platform, dominated by direct lending ($115.1B) through a complex of business development companies (OBDC, OCIC, OTF, OTIC, OBDC II), plus alternative credit (Atalaya), investment-grade credit, and CLOs.
- **Real Assets ($85.1B AUM)** — net lease (sale-leaseback to investment-grade tenants, ~$50B) and digital infrastructure/data centers (~$17.7B, anchored by the January 2025 IPI acquisition), plus real estate credit. This platform is the direct beneficiary of AI/data-center capex.
- **GP Strategic Capital ($70.6B AUM)** — minority equity stakes in large private-equity and credit firms (the legacy Dyal franchise). Highest-margin and most durable, with fees based on commitments/cost rather than mark-to-market.

The business is overwhelmingly management-fee-driven (fees are >99% of revenue; carry is negligible), giving it a toll-road revenue profile less dependent on realizations than peers such as Apollo, Ares, and Blackstone. Management emphasizes that ~85% of management fees come from permanent-capital vehicles. That figure carries an asterisk: the semi-liquid retail vehicles (OCIC, OTIC) counted toward it have periodic redemption features, and those are precisely the funds now capping withdrawals.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings engine (non-GAAP, how the business is run):** FY2025 revenue was $2.87B (+25% YoY), with management fees of $2.52B. FY2025 FRE reached $1.50B (+19%) and DE $1.31B (+16%), at an FRE margin of ~58%. Q1'26 revenue was $753.8M (+10%), FRE $393.6M (+14%), DE $292.5M (+11%) — growth that was broad-based across all three platforms. This is the fact the bear case must contend with: the realized cash-earnings engine continued to grow through the quarter in which the redemption crisis became visible.

**The GAAP/non-GAAP gap.** FY2025 GAAP net income to Class A holders was just $78.8M, and Q1'26 was $15.5M on $754M of revenue (GAAP margin 12.7%). The trailing P/E of ~79x is an artifact of this gap and is not a meaningful valuation input. The driver is the Up-C structure, where Common Units held by founders and employees absorb ~56% of operating-group economics as noncontrolling interest ($64M of $79.6M consolidated net income in Q1'26 went to NCI), plus heavy non-cash charges ($359M intangible amortization and ~$674M equity comp in FY2025). Cash generation is materially healthier than GAAP EPS implies — FY2025 operating cash flow was roughly $1.26B.

**Balance sheet and liquidity — the genuine watch item.** Total debt grew from $3.37B (12/31/25) to $3.87B (3/31/26), with the revolver balance jumping from $860M to $1.36B in a single quarter (since reduced to $940M by 5/1/26). Cash is thin at ~$190M, held mostly at a single bank, against an indefinite-life dividend obligation and the TRA. Goodwill of $5.6B plus intangibles of $2.8B total $8.4B, or 68% of $12.4B in assets, reflecting the roll-up strategy. The ~$1.7B TRA liability pays 85% of realized tax benefits to pre-IPO owners and drained $64.8M in Q1'26 alone — a recurring cash claim that competes with dividends and buybacks. The Altman Z-Score (~0.66) flags "distress" but is not meaningful for a goodwill-heavy, low-GAAP-equity alt manager; liquidity is better judged via fee durability and revolver headroom (~$1.1B available).

**Capital return.** The FY2026 dividend target is $0.92/share ($0.23/quarter), a ~10% yield at $9.26 and covered by DE. A $150M buyback authorization is in place; $25M was repurchased in Q1'26 at ~$14.60, above today's price — poor timing that bears on the capital-allocation read.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is decelerating rather than stalling, and the two directions of the AI theme are central. Real Assets — net lease and data-center pipelines — is running near record levels on AI/data-center demand, and GP Strategic Capital provides commitment-based fees insulated from credit marks. Blue Owl still raised ~$3B in the private-wealth channel in Q1'26 despite the turmoil, and ~$30B of AUM "not yet paying fees" represents roughly $349M of embedded future annualized fees.

Working the other way, the same AI theme is the proximate cause of the credit stress: investors pulled capital from OCIC/OTIC citing "heightened market concerns around AI-related disruption to software companies," a large slice of direct-lending borrowers. Q1'26 saw $1.2B of non-traded BDC redemptions, OCIC new sales collapsed ~95%, and one BDC cut its dividend on lower NAV from higher borrowing costs.

The forward signal that matters most is estimate revisions, and they are uniformly negative: current-quarter EPS down 3.4% over 30 days and 7.0% over 90 days; next-year down 4.8% over 30 days and 11.4% over 90 days. The Credit platform is the largest, so FPAUM erosion there can outweigh Real Assets/GP-stakes growth on a consolidated basis. The investment question for the next several quarters is precisely whether diversification can outrun Credit fee erosion. That is genuinely unresolved, which is why Growth scores a 5.0 rather than higher.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E (~79x) is uninformative given the Up-C/NCI structure. The relevant lens is distributable earnings. FY2025 DE was $1.31B and the dividend target is $0.92/share, so at $9.26 the stock trades at roughly 10x DE-equivalent earnings with a ~10% DE-covered yield. Consensus current-year EPS of $0.88 puts the forward earnings yield near 9.5%.

Peer comparison is complicated by the same GAAP distortion. Ares (ARES) trades at a 58.4x GAAP P/E, equally distorted, and has itself drawn down 35% from its 52-week high. T. Rowe Price (11.4x) and Franklin (23.9x) are traditional managers with different economics. The cleaner read is absolute: an alt manager at ~10x DE with a covered 10% yield is inexpensive provided the fee base holds. The risk is in the "E," not the multiple — if FPAUM erodes, distributable earnings fall and the dividend-growth promise weakens.

Two points on asymmetry that bear directly on the recommendation. First, the consensus target band runs from $9.00 (low, at spot) to $21.00 (high), with a mean of $12.93 and median $11.50, and zero analysts rate the stock sell. Being more bearish than the most bearish covering analyst requires an identifiable edge over consensus, and there is no information here that the sell-side does not have — the redemption caps and the 36(b) suit are public. Second, the company's own scenario set is positively skewed: a bear path to $7–8 is 14–25% downside before a 10% yield cushion, while a re-rate to $12–15 is 30–62% upside. That skew is the opposite of what an AVOID requires. It supports a HOLD with a valuation score of 6.0 — cheap on cash earnings, with the explicit caveat that the earnings base is at risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Blue Owl sits at the center of the 2026 private-credit reckoning. The ~$2 trillion private-credit market is experiencing a broad redemption wave; Cliffwater curbed redemptions for two consecutive quarters, and idiosyncratic credit events have spooked retail investors. The structural tension is a liquidity mismatch: non-traded BDCs and interval funds offer periodic liquidity against illiquid private loans, which works in calm markets and breaks when sentiment turns.

Blue Owl's relative advantage is genuine diversification. While direct-lending fundraising stalled, net lease and digital-infrastructure pipelines are near record levels, and GP Strategic Capital provides stable, commitment-based fees insulated from credit marks. The Q1 beat and the ~10% relief rally on earnings day reflected the market crediting this diversification. The offsetting reality is reputational: capping redemptions on flagship retail funds and a 95% sales collapse at OCIC damage the franchise's standing in the channel that drove much of its retail AUM growth, and a Saba Capital tender for Blue Owl Capital Corp II shares at a ~35% discount to NAV in April 2026 is a market signal that locked-up investors may value the illiquid NAV well below carrying value. The competitive trajectory is therefore split — deteriorating in retail Credit, improving in Real Assets/GP stakes — netting to a stable-to-slightly-negative 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks are specific and partly unpriced:

1. **Redemption pressure (dominant, binary).** 5% caps on OCIC/OTIC, ~95% OCIC sales collapse, $1.2B Q1 BDC redemptions, and a defensive $500M IG bond raise by a redemption-hit fund in June 2026. The "permanent capital" narrative is more fragile than the 85% headline for the retail-distributed credit vehicles.
2. **Fee/valuation sensitivity to Level 3 marks.** Credit management fees are based on the fair value of largely illiquid, internally-marked assets; a forced-markdown cycle would hit fees directly.
3. **Section 36(b) excessive-fee suit** (Delman, April 27, 2026), early-stage and individually hard for plaintiffs to win, but timed against a national spotlight on private-credit fee structures — a reputational/regulatory overhang on the highest-margin part of the model.
4. **Rising leverage and thin cash** ($3.87B debt, revolver spike, ~$190M cash at a single bank) into a credit downturn, plus the ~$1.7B TRA drain.
5. **Up-C governance.** ~56% of economics to NCI; founders control 80% of votes (controlled company); Ostrover/Lipschultz have pledged large Common Unit blocks as loan collateral, creating forced-sale tail risk.
6. **Acquisition/goodwill exposure** — $8.4B of goodwill and intangibles plus outstanding earnouts (KAM cash; Prima/Atalaya stock/units) create GAAP volatility and potential future dilution.

Much of the headline bear case is now reflected in a 51% decline, zero sell ratings, and a low target at spot. The residual unpriced risk is the binary outcome of the redemption wave and the 36(b)/regulatory tail, which keeps the Risk score at 4.0 — the lowest dimension and the principal reason this is not a higher-conviction hold.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 73.9% against 44.3% one-year historical volatility (a rich 29.6pp spread), at the 86th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (73.9% near vs 54.7% long-dated). The put/call OI ratio rose to 1.76 from 1.15 in a day even as near-term skew stayed call-heavy (-6.7%) and the put/call volume ratio sat at 0.34 — a mix that reads as event hedging into a near catalyst rather than uniform directional bearishness. Short interest is 23.8% of float with 4.2 days to cover, a meaningful squeeze setup if the redemption narrative stabilizes. The combination — elevated near-term IV, high SI, and improving sentiment — argues against pressing a short here and supports treating the next print and any further redemption news as the volatility-resolving events.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($12–15+):** Diversification outruns Credit erosion. Real Assets (data centers/net lease) and GP Strategic Capital keep growing into AI capex, FRE/DE compound at low-double-digits, the redemption wave stabilizes within two to three quarters, and the stock re-rates from ~10x DE toward 13–15x with a 10% covered yield. The 23.8% short base and improving sentiment provide a squeeze tailwind. Upside 30–62%.

**Bear case ($7–8):** The private-credit wave deepens, Credit FPAUM erodes faster than diversification can offset, forward estimates continue to fall (already -7% to -11%), the 36(b) suit/regulatory scrutiny pressures fee structures, and rising leverage plus the TRA drain force a pause in dividend growth. Downside 14–25% before the yield cushion. Note this bear path still implies less downside than the bull path implies upside — the asymmetry is positive, which is why the situation is a hold rather than an active avoid.

**Why HOLD, not AVOID (direct rebuttal to the bear-already-priced challenge):** The fee engine is still growing — FRE +14%, DE +11%, two beats and two in-line in four quarters — so the deterioration applies to AUM/flows and forward estimates, not to realized cash earnings. After a 51% de-rate, the most bearish analyst target is at spot, the median is +24%, and sentiment is improving; there is no identifiable informational edge that justifies being more bearish than the entire covering panel. The score of 5.2 and a self-acknowledged positive skew both place this in HOLD territory; the prior AVOID ratings, issued at scores already at/above 4.8, were internally inconsistent with an AVOID threshold and have been overtaken by price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2'26, expected late July/early August), which will show whether Q1 BDC redemptions of $1.2B accelerated or stabilized and whether FRE/DE growth held.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Non-traded BDC redemption requests decline quarter-over-quarter and the 5% caps are lifted or eased, with OCIC net flows turning less negative.
- Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-positive consensus EPS revisions (reversing the current -7% to -11% 90-day trend) alongside continued FRE growth.
- Renewed open-market insider buying at current levels — Ostrover and the CFO bought at ~$15 in December 2025; a repeat at ~$9 would be a stronger conviction signal than the original purchases, which are now ~38% underwater.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FRE or DE turns negative year-over-year, or the FRE margin falls below the low-50s, signaling the fee engine itself (not just AUM) is eroding.
- A dividend cut, or revolver utilization rising back toward the cap with no offsetting cash generation, indicating the capital-allocation balance has broken.
- An adverse development in the 36(b) suit or a broader regulatory action on private-credit fee structures that threatens the highest-margin business.

## Conclusion

Blue Owl is a high-quality, cash-generative, fee-centric alternative manager whose realized earnings continued to grow (FRE +14%, DE +11%) through the quarter in which its flagship retail-credit funds began capping redemptions. It is the most visible name in an acute, still-unfolding private-credit reckoning, but it carries genuine offsetting diversification in data centers, net lease, and GP stakes, ~85% permanent-capital fees (with a retail-vehicle asterisk), and a ~10% DE-covered yield. After a 51% decline that has placed the most bearish sell-side target at spot, with zero sell ratings and improving sentiment, the bear case is largely in the price, and the company's own scenario set is positively skewed. The unresolved binary — whether Real Assets and GP Strategic Capital can outrun Credit fee erosion — combined with falling forward estimates and a thin liquidity profile, leaves the positives and negatives roughly cancelling. The recommendation is **HOLD (uncertain)**, with a clear path to upgrade on redemption stabilization and estimate-trend reversal, and to downgrade on any deterioration in the fee engine itself.

No correction materially eased the valuation, so the HOLD remains supportable on the corrected figures.
