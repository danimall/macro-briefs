## Executive Summary

StepStone Group is a global private markets solutions provider managing $220B in AUM across private equity, infrastructure, private debt, and real estate. The business is demonstrating strong momentum: fee-earning AUM grew 21% YoY to $138.6B, fee-related earnings (FRE) expanded 64% in FY2025 to $312M, and the company holds $32.7B of undeployed fee-earning capital representing ~$213M in annualized fees waiting to activate. The stock trades at $52.59, down 32% from its January 2026 high of $77.80, which would ordinarily represent an improving risk/reward setup on unchanged fundamentals.

However, a specific, imminent binary risk dominates the near-term outlook: the SPW (private wealth) management team holds a put option exercisable as early as June 30, 2026 — approximately five weeks from now — with an estimated settlement range of $165M to $661M in cash or stock. This liability has already generated $1.54B in equity-based compensation charges through the first nine months of FY2026, distorting GAAP results and creating ongoing investor confusion. The resolution of this put option will either: (a) trigger significant dilution or debt issuance, pressuring the stock in the near-term, or (b) be deferred/restructured, removing the overhang. Until this binary event resolves, the stock's risk profile is fundamentally different from what its growth metrics alone would suggest.

The macro backdrop compounds the challenge. With elevated rates, private equity exit activity faces headwinds from higher discount rates and constrained deal markets. StepStone's performance fee realization cycle — which hit $253M in Q3 FY2026 — depends on continued GP realizations that become harder in a rising-rate environment.

## Company Overview

StepStone Group, founded in 2007 and publicly listed since September 2020, operates as a fully integrated private markets investment firm. It deploys capital through three strategies (primaries, secondaries, co-investments) across four asset classes (PE, infrastructure, debt, real estate) for approximately 460 revenue-generating client programs.

**Scale metrics as of December 31, 2025:**
- Total capital responsibility: $811B
- AUM: $220B
- Fee-earning AUM: $138.6B
- Undeployed fee-earning capital: $32.7B
- ~1,130 employees across 28 cities in 16 countries
- Advisory client retention: 95% since inception

Revenue comes from three sources: management and advisory fees ($767M in FY2025, 27% CAGR since IPO), performance fees/carried interest ($408M in FY2025, highly variable), and investment income on co-invested capital. The business generates recurring, predictable management fees supplemented by episodic and currently accelerating carried interest realizations.

## Financial Analysis

**The GAAP vs. Economic Reality:** StepStone's reported GAAP net loss of $180M in FY2025 and negative trailing P/E (-7.49x) reflect accounting distortions rather than economic impairment. The primary distortion is $629M in equity-based compensation from the mark-to-market of SPW profits interest liability, plus carried interest that accrues entirely to non-controlling interest holders. For analytical purposes, Adjusted Net Income (ANI) and Fee-Related Earnings (FRE) are the appropriate measures.

## Growth Analysis

StepStone's growth profile is among the strongest in public alternative asset management:

1. **FEAUM growth of 21% YoY** (to $138.6B) driven by fundraising across commingled vehicles and separate accounts
2. **Undeployed fee-earning capital of $32.7B** (+51% YoY) — committed capital awaiting deployment that will generate fees once invested, providing 12-24 months of embedded revenue growth at current deployment pace
3. **Private wealth expansion** through SPW (evergreen funds, SPRING vehicle) represents the fastest-growing revenue stream, with FRE contribution growing from $3M/quarter to $14M/quarter in one year
4. **New distribution channels**: LSEG Digital Markets Infrastructure platform (international wealth managers), U.S. Defined Contribution initiative (hired Taylor Benson, May 2026), PitchBook data partnership
5. **Secondaries market growth**: As LPs seek liquidity in a higher-rate environment, secondaries volumes benefit StepStone's deployment capabilities

The $32.7B undeployed pipeline at ~0.65% fee rate implies ~$213M in latent annualized fees — roughly 28% incremental to the current fee run-rate — with high visibility.

**Estimate Revision Context:** Current-quarter EPS estimate declined 5.8% over 30 days (from $0.53 to $0.50), and next-year estimates fell 4.0%. This negative revision trend, while modest, indicates the market is trimming expectations, likely reflecting macro uncertainty around fundraising pace and realization timing. This is the opposite of the strong positive revision signal that would support a high-conviction entry.

StepStone commands a premium to the peer median, partially justified by its faster growth (FEAUM +21% vs. peer single-digit AUM growth) and private wealth optionality. However, the premium has compressed significantly from the 35-40x levels seen at the January highs.

**Analyst targets:** Low $54, mean $71.38, high $91. The current price sits below the analyst low target, with 36% upside to mean. Analyst sentiment is degrading — one strong buy downgraded to hold in recent months.

The valuation is reasonable for the growth profile but not clearly cheap given: (a) the SPW dilution overhang, (b) negative estimate revision momentum, and (c) the complex structure that suppresses multiples. A simple-structure company growing FRE 30%+ would trade at 30-35x; the complexity discount explains the gap to ~21-25x.

**Competitive advantages:**

- Multi-asset-class integration allows cross-selling and holistic portfolio construction
- Scale ($811B capital responsibility) generates deal flow and manager access advantages

**Competitive trajectory — improving:**

- New distribution partnerships (LSEG, PitchBook) expanding addressable market

**Key competitor:** Hamilton Lane (HLNE) is the most direct public comparable. Both benefit from identical structural tailwinds. StepStone's larger AUM base ($220B vs. HLNE's ~$125B) and broader asset class coverage provide modest advantages.

The private wealth channel — which created the SPW option problem — is simultaneously the company's most powerful competitive differentiator. The ability to offer institutional-quality private markets access to high-net-worth individuals through evergreen structures positions StepStone for the largest fundraising opportunity in alternatives over the next decade.

## Risk Assessment

**1. SPW Put Option (Critical — Imminent):** Exercisable June 30, 2026. Settlement range $165M-$661M. StepStone can settle up to 75% in Partnership units (dilutive) but may need significant cash or new debt. At the high end ($661M), this exceeds current cash ($289M) and would require either substantial equity dilution or ~$400M+ of incremental debt. The outcome depends on private wealth business valuation at exercise date, making it partially market-dependent.

**2. Negative Estimate Revisions:** Current-quarter EPS estimates down 5.8% in 30 days; next-year estimates down 4.0%. Modest deterioration but directionally negative. This eliminates the strong positive revision signal that would support buying into the drawdown.

**3. Macro/Rate Environment:** Elevated rates directly impair private equity exit multiples, slow realization pace, and compress GP fundraising. The $253M Q3 realization quarter may prove difficult to repeat.

**4. Structural Complexity:** Multi-class shares, Tax Receivable Agreements, ongoing asset class entity exchanges (~5% annual acquisitions in stock/cash), and GAAP distortions suppress the multiple. The post-Sunset simplification helps over time but doesn't resolve near-term.

**5. Short Interest:** 6.8% of float, 4.8 days to cover. Moderate — not at crowded-short levels but suggests organized bearish positioning, likely targeting the SPW resolution.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 63.9% vs. 42.6% historical volatility implies the market is pricing a 15% move in the next month. The backwardation structure (63.9% near-term vs. 53.8% at 6 months) confirms the market views the imminent SPW catalyst window as generating outsized uncertainty. The put/call volume ratio of 61:1 in the near-term is extreme, though this could reflect a single large institutional hedge rather than broad bearish consensus. The balanced put/call skew (-2.5%) suggests participants are buying insurance against the binary outcome rather than decisively betting on direction.

## Conclusion

StepStone Group is a high-quality growth business in an expanding addressable market, demonstrating 20%+ AUM growth, expanding fee rates, and accelerating carried interest realizations. Under normal circumstances, a 32% drawdown from highs on unchanged growth fundamentals would present a compelling entry point. The SPW put option exercisable in approximately five weeks creates a genuine binary risk that could result in significant dilution or leverage. The options market confirms this with extreme near-term IV and backwardation.

The specific reason this cannot be a BUY despite the above-threshold overall score: the SPW put option is a dated, quantifiable, binary catalyst with downside asymmetry (resolution at the high end = 15-20% dilution; resolution at the low end merely removes the overhang without creating upside). Buying into a binary event where the adverse outcome is significantly more damaging than the favorable outcome requires a price discount that compensates for this asymmetry. At $52.59, the stock is priced for a moderate outcome but does not adequately compensate for the worst case. Negative estimate revisions confirm the market is still adjusting to deteriorating forward visibility rather than stabilizing.

The correct posture is to wait for the SPW resolution and then reassess whether the growth story justifies entry at the post-event price. If the put is resolved favorably and the stock remains in the low-$50s, it becomes a clear BUY. If the put requires a large equity raise, the stock likely revisits $40-45, where the growth premium on the surviving business may again become attractive.
