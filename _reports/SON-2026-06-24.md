**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sonoco has completed the largest portfolio reshaping in its history, pivoting from a diversified fiber/flexibles packager into a metal-and-fiber business with roughly 52% of sales now outside the U.S. The December 2024 $3.8B Eviosys acquisition (Europe's leading food can/ends/closures maker) was financed with $1.8B of senior notes and $2.2B of term loans, leaving total debt of $4.69B against ~$3.59B of equity at Q1 2026. Concurrent divestitures of TFP/Trident ($1.8B net to Toppan) and ThermoSafe ($0.66B net to Arsenal) funded part of the deleveraging plan, but the company has explicitly acknowledged it now has less optionality to raise cash from asset sales going forward.

The numbers underneath the transformation are soft. Q1 2026 net sales fell 1.9% YoY to $1.68B, Adjusted EBITDA dropped 18% to $276.5M, and both reportable segments lost 150–170bp of margin on weak volumes. Adjusted diluted EPS declined to $1.20 from $1.38. The headline FY2025 net income near $1.0B is inflated by one-time divestiture gains, and underlying operating momentum is negative. Consensus estimates are falling across all forward periods (current quarter -5.8% over 90 days, current year -2.3%), and the company missed its most recent quarter. A management-flagged near-term impairment trigger exists in the Metal Packaging EMEA reporting unit ($1.37B goodwill marked to fair value only ~15 months ago), where weak European volumes could force a large non-cash writedown.

This is an income hold. The ~4%+ dividend is well covered and backed by a multi-decade growth record, and the stock at ~8.3x trailing earnings is not demanding. Cheapness without a catalyst is a weak basis for a buy, organic trends are deteriorating, and the deleveraging-plus-integration turnaround is unproven. The score trajectory has been declining (6.0 → 5.5 → 5.5 since March), and the new data confirms rather than reverses that drift. We maintain HOLD.

## Company Overview

Sonoco is a ~$7.5B-revenue global packaging manufacturer operating roughly 265 locations across 37 countries. Following its transformation, it reports two segments:

- **Consumer Packaging (~65% of sales):** rigid paper composite cans, steel and plastic containers, and the newly dominant metal food cans, ends, and closures (the Eviosys business). Q1 2026 segment sales were $1,097M at an 11.5% operating margin, down from 13.2% a year earlier.
- **Industrial Paper Packaging (~35% of sales):** paperboard tubes, cones, cores, uncoated recycled paperboard, protective packaging, and the realigned industrial/specialty plastics business. Q1 2026 segment sales were $579M at a 12.0% margin, down from 13.0%.

Management states the multi-year transformation is complete as of late 2025, with the strategic goal of becoming the global leader in value-added, sustainable metal and fiber packaging. The two-segment structure is intended to enable margin expansion, deleveraging, and capital return. The pivot concentrated the business heavily in European metal packaging and away from flexibles and temperature-assurance, a bet that consumer-staples-skewed metal food packaging delivers defensive demand through cycles.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue path is distorted by deal/divestiture timing.** Reported revenue moved 2022 $7.25B → 2023 $6.78B → 2024 $5.31B → 2025 $7.52B. The 2024 dip and 2025 surge reflect divestiture/acquisition accounting and discontinued-ops reclassification rather than organic dynamics. The cleaner read is the Q1 2026 organic picture: net sales fell 1.9% YoY, with volume declines of $97.8M (macro/geopolitical customer pressure) plus the ThermoSafe divestiture (-$55.8M), partly offset by FX (+$87.1M) and price (+$38.7M). Underlying volume is the problem, and it is showing up in both segments.

**Margins are compressing.** Adjusted EBITDA fell to $276.5M from $337.8M, with Adjusted Operating Profit down 5.6%. Gross margin held around 20.6% sequentially, but the EBITDA-to-EPS bridge weakened because the TFP sale removed earnings that the lower share count and Eviosys contribution did not fully replace.

**FY2025 net income is not what the headline suggests.** The near-$1.0B GAAP net income is inflated by divestiture gains on TFP and ThermoSafe. Underlying GAAP operating income was $1.02B. A naive screen reading "net income up roughly 6x" would badly mischaracterize a business with negative organic operating momentum.

**Cash flow quality is the near-term watch item.** Operating activities used $367.9M in Q1 2026, versus using $208.1M in Q1 2025. Roughly $122M was tax payments on 2025 divestiture gains, $72M was TSA payments to Toppan (partly reimbursable), with the balance seasonal working-capital builds (tinplate steel inventory and advance steel payments to China). Q1 is seasonally weak, and the one-time items are explainable, but the YoY deterioration and the working-capital intensity of the new metal business deserve scrutiny when Q2/Q3 prints arrive. FY2025 OCF was $689.8M with FCF of ~$346M.

**Leverage is the central financial issue.** Net debt was ~$4.46B against ~$3.59B equity. Goodwill plus intangibles of ~$5.1B exceed total equity, leaving the balance sheet heavily intangible. The Altman Z-Score sits in the grey/distress zone (~1.7), driven by leverage rather than operating distress; covenants (interest coverage, net worth) are reported "substantially above" minimums. $1.2B of debt matures within 12 months. Liquidity is bridged by a new $300M delayed-draw term loan (Wells Fargo, March 2026), $906M of available revolver capacity, and management's stated intent to apply divestiture proceeds to debt reduction; $344M of commercial paper is outstanding. The deleveraging plan is credible but now operates with reduced asset-sale optionality, since the company has consolidated into two core segments with little left to divest.

**Capital allocation favors income.** The dividend was raised to $0.54/quarter in April 2026, a ~4%+ yield at current prices, backed by a multi-decade growth record. There are no meaningful buybacks; the $138M authorization has been untouched since 2021. Capital priorities are high-return growth/margin projects, deleveraging, and the dividend, in that order.

In the higher-for-longer rate environment, the financing model is a clear headwind: the $1.2B of near-term maturities will likely be refinanced at higher coupons than the legacy debt they replace, and the company's ability to lean on asset sales has narrowed.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is weak on current evidence. Organic volumes are declining in both segments, management attributes it to macro/geopolitical pressure on customers, and consensus estimates are falling across every forward period:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Revision |
|--------|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Current Quarter | $1.49 | $1.58 | -5.8% |
| Next Quarter | $1.95 | $2.03 | -3.8% |
| Current Year | $5.82 | $5.96 | -2.3% |
| Next Year | $6.41 | $6.47 | -0.9% |

Falling estimates are among the more reliable forward indicators, and the direction here is negative across the board. This is the opposite of the signal (>30% upward revisions plus accelerating revenue) that would warrant a growth bet. The most recent quarter was a small miss (-1.4%), and the four-quarter record is one beat against three misses.

The forward earnings story, to the extent there is one, rests on three execution items rather than market expansion: (1) integrating Eviosys and realizing synergies; (2) re-accelerating organic volume as the macro backdrop normalizes; and (3) margin recovery in both segments off the depressed Q1 levels. Each is plausible but unproven, and none is a near-term catalyst.

There are two small structural positives. The new paper-can plant in Nong Yai, Thailand, addresses the stacked-chip snack market, and a $20M expansion at Hartselle, AL produces nailed wood reels serving AI data-center wire/cable demand, a modest tilt toward the infrastructure buildout. The consumer-staples skew of the metal food business (food, beverage, household, personal, pharma) provides defensive demand characteristics that management cites as the rationale for the pivot. These support stability more than growth.

Sonoco trades at ~8.3x trailing earnings and a forward multiple in the same vicinity, with a ~4%+ dividend yield. Against the peer set, it screens cheap:

| **Peer median** | | **15.5** | **2.6%** |

SON trades at roughly half the peer-median P/E. The discount reflects the leverage profile, the flagged EMEA goodwill-impairment risk, negative estimate revisions, soft organic volumes, and a documented five-year shareholder-return underperformance (cumulative TSR 88.39 vs. peer group 141.6). The trailing P/E is not a trough-earnings distortion that forward inflection would correct; estimates are falling, not rising, so cycle-adjustment does not rescue the multiple.

A low multiple absent a catalyst is a value-trap signal more often than an opportunity, and the evidence here is consistent with that pattern: cheap names without an independent re-rating trigger have tended to stay cheap. The dividend yield provides absolute support and is the strongest valuation pillar; at ~4%+ with solid coverage, the income floor is real. The equity re-rating case requires deleveraging proof points and synergy delivery that have not yet arrived. We score valuation modestly above neutral on yield support alone, not on an asymmetric upside thesis.

Analyst targets (low $54, mean ~$60.9, median $63, high $65) sit above the current $50.79 and imply roughly 6–28% upside. Given falling estimates and a quality discount that is fundamentally justified rather than a mispricing, we treat these targets as optimistic anchors rather than reliable floors; the floors-not-ceilings logic applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates, which SON is not.

## Competitive Landscape

Sonoco competes in fragmented, cost-driven packaging markets where being a responsive, high-quality, low-cost producer is the differentiator. Post-Eviosys it is a global leader in metal food cans, ends, and closures, particularly in Europe, competing with Crown Holdings, Ardagh, and Silgan in metal and a range of fiber peers in paperboard. The competitive trajectory is roughly stable rather than improving:

- **Customer and supplier consolidation** squeezes pricing on both ends of the chain.
- **Consumer-staples skew** gives the Consumer segment defensive demand characteristics across cycles, which is the structural argument for the metal pivot.
- **Substitution risk** runs in both directions; sustainability-driven format shifts (PFAS rules, single-use plastic regulation) can favor or disfavor specific formats.
- **AI as a competitive threat:** the 10-K explicitly warns that AI could lower barriers to entry if competitors adopt it faster, while Sonoco's own AI adoption is described as "minimal."

The five-year TSR underperformance (declining every year from 2021's 99.75) frames the transformation as a turnaround attempt from a position of relative weakness, not a leader pressing an advantage. Whether the metal pivot improves the trajectory depends entirely on Eviosys integration and European volume recovery, neither of which is yet evident in the numbers. We score competitive position at neutral.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is skewed toward specific, identifiable downside that is not fully priced:

1. **Leverage and goodwill impairment (most material).** Management has twice singled out Metal Packaging EMEA as at risk of near-term impairment because the Eviosys assets were marked to fair value only ~15 months ago, leaving little cushion. Weak European volumes could trigger a large non-cash writedown. A separate APAC reporting unit ($27M goodwill) is also flagged. This is unusually specific disclosure and a concrete, monitorable trigger.

2. **Refinancing risk.** The $1.2B of near-term maturities comes due in a higher-for-longer rate environment, with reduced asset-sale optionality after portfolio simplification. Liquidity is bridged but the cost of refinancing will weigh on earnings.

3. **Eviosys integration execution.** A larger, more complex European metal business now drives the bulk of consolidated sales from outside the U.S. Integration, synergy realization, and FX translation are repeatedly cited risks. Q1 2026 saw a $61M translation loss in OCI with AOCI swinging negative; cross-currency swaps (~$2.3B notional) partially hedge.

4. **Volume softness.** Broad-based volume declines in Q1 2026 with both segments missing prior-year margins.

5. **Section 232 tariffs.** By pivoting into metal, Sonoco took on direct 25–50% steel/aluminum duty exposure it largely lacked before. Management cites contractual pass-through and a local-for-local model, and does not expect material direct 2026 impact, but this is a newly prominent exposure.

6. **Russia operations** (inherited via Eviosys, ~1% of sales) carry sanctions/asset-seizure risk; the filing notes Russia has seized Western can manufacturers' assets.

7. **Management-turnover thread (unresolved).** A cadence of Item 5.02 (director/officer departure or election) 8-Ks across Feb 2025, Jun 2025, Jan 2026, Feb 2026, and May 11, 2026 is unusual over ~16 months. The content of the May 11, 2026 filing could not be verified. This could be benign post-acquisition reshuffling or a sign of instability/CEO succession. It is the single most important open item and a governance watch flag.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly cautious but not alarming. Near-term ATM IV is 27.2% versus 1-year HV of 32.0%, so near-dated options are cheap relative to realized volatility (IV percentile 6% within the current chain), and the term structure is in contango (27.2% near vs. 32.5% long-term). There is modest elevated near-term put skew (3.0%), but the put/call volume ratio is light at 0.22 and the P/C OI ratio of 0.39, while rising from 0.27 a month ago, remains call-tilted overall. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.2%.

Short interest is elevated at 11.1% of float with 6.6 days to cover, indicating meaningful institutional bearish positioning consistent with the leverage and impairment concerns but also a source of squeeze risk on any positive surprise. The combination reads as a market that sees moderate downside risk without pricing an imminent binary event. Cheap near-term IV would favor option-based expression of a directional view for those inclined.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Eviosys integration delivers synergies, European volumes stabilize, and the metal food business proves its defensive demand thesis. Divestiture proceeds and FCF reduce leverage on schedule, the EMEA goodwill cushion holds, and the dividend plus a re-rating toward even a discounted slice of the peer median lifts the stock toward the $63–65 analyst target range. The cheap multiple converts to a genuine opportunity as the turnaround proof points arrive.

**Bear case.** European volumes remain weak, forcing a large non-cash goodwill impairment in Metal Packaging EMEA. Refinancing at higher rates compresses earnings, estimate revisions keep falling, and the management-turnover thread resolves as instability or succession disruption. The stock derates further toward the low-$40s (near the 52-week low of $38.65), with the dividend the only support. The five-year TSR underperformance continues.

**Base case.** Sonoco muddles through: leverage grinds down slowly, margins recover partially off the Q1 trough, no impairment is triggered but the EMEA risk persists as an overhang, and the stock trades in a range anchored by yield. Forward EPS settles around the falling consensus. At ~8.5–9x that base, fair value sits roughly $52–56, modestly above the current price, with total return driven primarily by the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q2 2026) and the resolution of the EMEA impairment question over the next two quarters.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or positive organic volume in either segment, accompanied by estimate revisions turning positive.
- Demonstrated deleveraging — net debt falling meaningfully toward 3x EBITDA — combined with the company clearing the near-term maturity wall without margin-eroding refinancing cost, and explicit confirmation that the EMEA goodwill is not impaired.
- Clarification of the management-turnover pattern as orderly board/executive refreshment rather than instability.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material goodwill impairment in Metal Packaging EMEA, or guidance acknowledging EMEA underperformance versus the acquisition plan.
- Operating cash flow failing to recover off the seasonally weak Q1 into Q2/Q3, signaling working-capital problems beyond seasonal builds.
- Any sign the dividend is at risk (coverage deteriorating, or capital priorities reordered away from the payout), or a refinancing executed at punitive rates.
- The May 2026 leadership change confirmed as a destabilizing succession or unexpected senior departure.

The stock has recovered from its April 2026 post-earnings drop (to $47.58) back to $50.79, but the recovery reflects market beta and yield support rather than improving fundamentals. We are not lowering scores because of price action; we are holding them down because estimate revisions, organic volumes, and the impairment overhang have all deteriorated since the prior review, confirming the declining score trajectory.

## Conclusion

Sonoco is a deleveraging-and-integration turnaround dressed up by a misleading 2025 headline net income figure. The Eviosys pivot remade the company into a European-metal-heavy packager carrying high debt, a flagged EMEA goodwill-impairment risk, falling estimates, soft organic volumes, and a documented five-year shareholder-return underperformance. The trailing multiple looks cheap against the peer median, but the discount is fundamentally justified rather than a mispricing, and a cheap multiple without a re-rating catalyst is a value-trap signal more often than an opportunity.

The investment case that holds up is income: a ~4%+ dividend with solid coverage and a multi-decade growth record. That justifies holding the position for yield, not for appreciation. We maintain **HOLD** with an overall score of 4.65, reflecting the continued decline in financial health, growth prospects, and risk that the latest data confirms. An upgrade requires evidence of deleveraging progress, EMEA impairment clearance, and stabilizing volumes; a downgrade follows a writedown, a refinancing at punitive rates, or dividend stress.
