## Executive Summary

Addus HomeCare is a $1.8B market cap provider of personal care, hospice, and home health services operating across approximately 262 offices in 23 states. The company generates 77% of revenue from Medicaid-funded personal care services, with the balance from Medicare hospice (18%) and home health (5%). The stock trades at $99.57 (as of the date of this analysis), down approximately 20% from its October 2025 highs near $124, primarily on concerns around Medicaid spending restrictions in the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" and broader healthcare policy uncertainty.

The fundamental picture remains solid: the company produced $104M in free cash flow in FY2025 (7.3% of revenue, 108% FCF conversion), has deleveraged from approximately $223M in revolving credit outstanding at the end of 2024 to $94M by March 2026—approximately $129M in deleveraging over 15 months—and operates with expanding margins (operating margin from 7.2% in 2022 to 9.7% in 2025). At ~14x FY2026E earnings of $7.00, the stock offers a meaningful discount to its growth rate and historical trading range, with analyst consensus implying significant upside.

The key risks center on government payor concentration (Illinois alone represents 32% of revenue), potential Medicaid funding restrictions, and the labor-intensive nature of the business in a tight employment market. However, secured rate increases of ~$35M annualized, the structural shift toward managed care organizations, and an aging population provide multi-year visibility into mid-to-high single digit revenue growth.

## Company Overview

Addus HomeCare provides in-home care services across three segments: Personal Care (77% of revenue), Hospice (18%), and Home Health (5%). The personal care business provides non-medical assistance with daily living activities for elderly and disabled individuals, funded primarily through state Medicaid programs and managed care organizations. The hospice segment provides end-of-life care (94% Medicare), while home health delivers skilled nursing and therapy (61% Medicare).

The company completed the approximately $353.6M Gentiva acquisition in December 2024 (net adjusted purchase price of approximately $350.6M after a $2.9M purchase-price adjustment received in 2025), adding approximately $280M in annualized personal care revenue concentrated in Texas. This significantly diversified the company's geographic footprint away from Illinois dependence and expanded the total platform to a $1.4B+ annual revenue run rate.

The dual-eligible patient population (qualifying for both Medicare and Medicaid) creates a natural continuum-of-care opportunity across all three segments. The ongoing managed care shift—MCO revenue grew to 47.6% of personal care from 45.3% a year prior—aligns with coordinated care initiatives and may provide more predictable reimbursement over time, advantaging larger, multi-state providers like Addus who can serve MCOs at scale.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** FY2025 revenue reached $1.42B, up 23.2% YoY driven primarily by the Gentiva acquisition. Organic growth was approximately 7-8%. Q1 2026 revenue of $363.6M (+7.7% YoY) reflects the post-acquisition run rate with personal care same-store organic growth of 6.5%.

**Margin Expansion:** Operating margins improved from 7.2% (2022) to 9.7% (2025), demonstrating operating leverage and disciplined cost management. G&A as a percentage of revenue declined to 21.4% in Q1 2026 from 21.7% prior year. Personal care segment margins expanded to 18.8% from 18.4%, while home health margins improved to 18.9% from 16.8% (the latter through deliberate payor mix improvement).

**Free Cash Flow:** FY2025 FCF was $103.8M on minimal capex of $7.7M—the business is extremely capital-light. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $52.4M (vs. $18.9M prior year), reflecting improved collections and earnings power. FCF/net income of 108% indicates high earnings quality.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt is approximately negative $9M (essentially net cash, calculated as $103.1M cash less $94.3M revolving loans outstanding as of March 31, 2026). Available credit facility capacity is $548M on a $650M facility maturing July 2028. Debt-to-equity is 0.08x. The balance sheet provides substantial capacity for additional tuck-in acquisitions.

**Goodwill:** At $997M (69% of total assets), goodwill reflects the company's acquisition-driven growth history. While this creates impairment risk if acquired businesses underperform, the consistent margin expansion and organic growth post-integration suggest the acquisitions have been performing well to date, though goodwill remains a significant balance-sheet risk that warrants ongoing monitoring.

**Key Metrics:**
- FCF Yield: ~5.6% (on approximately $1.85B enterprise value)
- ROE: 9.3% (FY2025)
- Current Ratio: 1.83x
- Shares Outstanding: 18.3M (minimal dilution)

## Competitive Landscape

Addus operates in a highly fragmented market with structural advantages favoring consolidation. The personal care industry has thousands of small operators, many of which lack the scale, compliance infrastructure, and technology systems to contract with MCOs or manage complex state Medicaid programs efficiently.

**Competitive Advantages:**
- Scale: One of the largest publicly traded personal care providers, enabling MCO relationships that smaller operators cannot access
- Multi-service platform: Personal care + hospice + home health enables cross-selling and continuum-of-care positioning
- Geographic diversification: 23 states reduces single-state policy risk (though Illinois remains concentrated at 32%)
- Regulatory compliance: Scale enables investment in compliance infrastructure that smaller operators cannot maintain
- MCO relationships: 47.6% of personal care revenue from MCOs (growing), which prefer fewer, larger vendors

**Competitive Threats:**
- UnitedHealth/Optum's acquisition of Amedisys and LHC Group creates a vertically integrated competitor with payor-provider alignment (though UnitedHealth divested certain home health/hospice assets to Pennant as part of the Amedisys transaction)
- BrightSpring Health Services (public) is a credible multi-service competitor
- Help at Home (private) is the largest personal care pure-play
- Labor competition remains intense, though Addus benefits from scale in recruitment

MCO penetration growth naturally advantages larger providers, and the fragmented market provides an extended runway for accretive acquisitions.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Medicaid Policy Risk (HIGH):** The OBBBA (enacted July 2025) includes Medicaid spending restrictions. While specific personal care cuts have not been implemented, the legislative environment creates ongoing uncertainty. Illinois representing 32% of revenue with a single state agency at 17.8% creates concentration vulnerability. Any material rate reduction or payment delay would directly impact profitability.

2. **Regulatory Risk — CMS 80/20 Rule:** The finalized rule requiring 80% of Medicaid personal care payments go to direct worker compensation could compress margins. Management expects repeal under the current administration, but this is not guaranteed. Timeline extends to mid-2030 for full compliance.

3. **Labor Supply Constraints:** In a tight unemployment environment, recruiting low-wage caregivers remains challenging. Minimum wage increases in key states (Illinois) require corresponding Medicaid rate increases that may lag. The ARPA funding cliff in September 2026 removes $14.6M in recruitment/retention support.

4. **Goodwill Impairment:** $997M in goodwill (69% of assets) creates write-down risk if acquired businesses—particularly Gentiva—underperform expectations. A goodwill charge would not affect cash flow but would significantly impact book value and potentially credit metrics.

5. **Client Concentration:** Illinois Department on Aging at 17.8% of total revenue creates binary risk around Illinois fiscal dynamics. The state has historically experienced budget crises and payment delays.

**Mitigating Factors:**
- Government preference for home-based care (dramatically cheaper than institutional alternatives—personal care averages approximately $26/hour vs. significantly higher costs for institutional settings)
- Bipartisan support for HCBS spending in principle
- Geographic diversification improving post-Gentiva
- Conservative balance sheet provides buffer against policy disruptions

## Conclusion

Addus HomeCare offers an attractive entry point at $99.57. The stock is down 20% from its 52-week high while fundamental performance has been consistent: margins are expanding, the balance sheet is near net-cash with $548M available credit, and the Gentiva acquisition is integrating successfully. The forward multiple of 14.2x is well below the company's historical range and does not adequately reflect the structural demographic tailwinds supporting multi-year growth in home-based care services.

The primary risks—Medicaid policy uncertainty and Illinois concentration—are legitimate but quantifiable. The company has sufficient liquidity to weather payment delays, and geographic diversification is improving through Texas expansion. At a ~5.6% FCF yield with mid-single-digit earnings growth, the margin of safety is adequate even under modestly adverse policy scenarios.

Analyst consensus (as of the date of this analysis) reflects approximately 12 of 14 Buy/Strong Buy ratings with a median target of approximately $135. Options pricing—with IV below realized volatility and call-heavy skew—is directionally consistent with the bullish fundamental thesis, though options positioning alone should not be treated as confirmation of the investment case.
