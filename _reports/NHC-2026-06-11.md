**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

National HealthCare Corporation operates 80 skilled nursing facilities (10,329 beds), 26 assisted living facilities, 9 independent living facilities, 3 behavioral hospitals, plus homecare, hospice, and pharmacy operations across nine Southeastern and Midwestern states. The investment case has two distinct components. The operating business is performing well: net patient revenue grew 17.4% to $1,470M in 2025, operating margin expanded from 5.1% (2023) to 8.4% (2025) on the near-elimination of agency nurse staffing and 4.0% composite per-diem rate increases, and occupancy reached 90.0% in Q1 2026. The corporate-action layer is more consequential: NHC has agreed to buy the real estate underneath 32 SNFs and 3 ILFs from its related-party REIT landlord, National Health Investors (NHI), for $560M, resolving a lease-default dispute that had threatened its right to operate a large block of facilities.

The deal is strategically sound. It converts roughly $32M/year of base rent into owned assets, removes an existential operating risk, and dissolves the long-criticized NHC–NHI board overlap. It also re-leverages a balance sheet that entered 2026 debt-free. The May 27, 2026 financing 8-K confirms NHC has arranged new debt to fund the purchase, shifting the company from under-levered to moderately levered. Against $185M of annual operating cash flow and a large base of unencumbered real estate, the leverage is manageable, but the risk profile changes from pristine to leveraged.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable. The CMS FY2027 SNF proposed rule (April 2026) is considering a blanket ~4.3% case-mix reduction to offset post-PDPM "coding creep." Management explicitly states that against the proposed +2.4% market-basket increase, a net ~3.6% system-wide reduction "could represent a net negative reimbursement outcome." With Medicare at 31% of net patient revenue, that directly threatens the margin expansion driving the equity. The stock trades at roughly 24x trailing and forward earnings after an 84% twelve-month run, leaving little cushion if the final rule (typically released in July) confirms the cut. The asymmetry is not attractive enough at this price to underwrite a BUY ahead of that binary.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1971 and headquartered in Murfreesboro, Tennessee, NHC is a post-acute and senior-care operator. Skilled nursing is the core, generating roughly 87% of net patient revenue through inpatient services. The company differentiates on clinical quality: as of Q1 2026, 65% of its SNFs carried a 4- or 5-star CMS rating versus 39% industry-wide, with an average rating of 3.85 versus 2.98 for the industry. In skilled nursing, star ratings drive census, payor mix (higher-acuity Medicare and managed-care patients carry better reimbursement), referral flow from hospitals and physicians, and value-based purchasing incentives.

NHC pairs SNFs with adjacent assisted living, independent living, homecare (34 agencies), hospice (33 agencies, operating as Caris Healthcare), behavioral health, regional pharmacies, two captive insurers, and an Institutional Special Needs Plan (NHC Advantage). The company owns approximately 54% of its SNF beds outright, making it asset-heavier and lower-leverage than typical operator/REIT-dependent peers. Roughly 15,200 employees are employed mainly through a related-party Administrative Services Contractor, National Health Corporation, a legacy structure dating to the company's reorganization.

The 2024 White Oak acquisition (15 SNFs, 2 ALFs, 4 ILFs, and a pharmacy in NC/SC, closed August 2024) contributed $227.5M of revenue in 2025 and was accretive to earnings. Same-facility revenue growth was 8.4% in 2025, so the top line is not purely acquisition-driven.

## Financial Analysis

NHC's financial position is strong by any standard, with the caveat that it is in the process of changing.

**Revenue and margins.** Net patient revenue progressed $1,029M (2022) → $1,088M (2023) → $1,252M (2024) → $1,470M (2025). The 2025 increase combined the White Oak full-year contribution with same-facility growth. The more important development is margin: operating margin moved through 6.9% in 2024. Two mechanisms drove it. Agency nurse staffing expense fell roughly 66% in each of 2024 and 2025, down to $1.06M in Q1 2026, effectively eliminated, which removes a high-cost premium labor line. And per-diem rates rose: Medicare +5.1% and private pay +6.8%. Salaries, wages, and benefits as a share of revenue improved to 60.7% (2025) from 62.4% (2023). Occupancy reached 89.7% in 2025.

**Earnings and cash flow.** 2025 GAAP net income was $120.0M (diluted EPS $7.67), but this is inflated by $22.3M of unrealized mark-to-market gains on the equity portfolio (mostly the NHI stock holding; the comparable 2024 figure was $31.0M). The cleaner number is adjusted net income of $104.1M, or non-GAAP EPS of $6.65, up 35% year-over-year. Operating cash flow was $185.1M (up 72%), and free cash flow was $148.6M (roughly 10% FCF margin). Q1 2026 net patient revenue rose 2.3% organically, a deceleration explained by lapping White Oak's full-year contribution and a one-time prior-year Missouri Medicaid catch-up, while adjusted net income grew 21.1%.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $1.53B; stockholders' equity $1.07B (equity/assets 70%). Debt/equity 0.42; current ratio 1.82; Altman Z-Score 3.53 (safe zone). The notable trajectory: long-term debt fell from $137M post-White-Oak (2024) to $32.5M at end-2025 and to $0 by Q1 2026, with the credit facility fully repaid. Funding the $560M NHI purchase will re-leverage the balance sheet meaningfully. The relevant judgment is that the annual operating cash flow plus substantial unencumbered real estate makes the new debt serviceable, but investors should anchor to the post-deal capital structure rather than the Q1 2026 zero-debt snapshot.

Capital allocation has been disciplined: a growing quarterly dividend ($0.64/share in 2026, up from $0.61), share repurchases largely offsetting option dilution, and self-funded growth. Shares outstanding are roughly 15.6M.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth drivers are intact and one is at risk.

Intact: (1) the demographic tailwind of an aging population sustaining demand for post-acute and senior care; (2) the continued shift toward value-based purchasing, ACOs, and bundled payments, which structurally favors high-quality operators like NHC; and (3) the death of the CMS federal minimum-staffing mandate — barred from enforcement until October 2034 under the July 2025 reconciliation law, with CMS rescinding the hours-per-resident-day requirement in December 2025. The staffing-mandate relief is a genuine positive versus prior expectations and protects the margin gains from agency-staffing elimination.

The NHI purchase adds an inorganic growth lever by removing the lease-renewal/default overhang. State Medicaid rate increases in Tennessee (+$3M/year) and South Carolina (+$4.2M/year) are additional FY2026 tailwinds.

At risk: the FY2027 CMS SNF proposed rule. If the case-mix recalibration is finalized as proposed, the per-diem rate increases that drove 2025 margin expansion partly reverse in FY2027. Home health faces a separate 1.3% FY2026 cut. Organic growth has already decelerated on tougher comps. The growth thesis depends materially on the final rule, which is the central reason to wait.

The earnings beat record is thin (two recent quarters, both beats; older 2011 data is not decision-relevant), which limits confidence in extrapolating consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

NHC trades at roughly 24.4x trailing and forward earnings on $7.67 GAAP EPS, or closer to 29x on the cleaner adjusted EPS of $6.65. Market cap is approximately $3.06B at $195.62.

Peer comparison in Health Care Facilities is unusually noisy. Reported peer P/Es range from 15.4x (Select Medical) and 16.0x (Option Care) to 62.4x (Astrana) and 125.6x (U.S. Physical Therapy), with Acadia negative. The peer median of 62.4x is distorted by the high-multiple outliers and is not a usable benchmark. The more relevant comparables — lower-multiple facility operators (SEM 15.4x, OPCH 16.0x) — trade well below NHC. NHC's premium to those names is defensible on quality grounds: best-in-class star ratings, owned real estate, a fortress balance sheet, and same-facility growth. But a 24x multiple on a skilled-nursing operator is full, not cheap, particularly with a binary reimbursement event pending.

This is not a cyclical-trough situation where trailing P/E understates earnings power. Margins are at multi-year highs, not depressed. The GAAP EPS is, if anything, flattered by unrealized equity gains, so the true operating multiple is higher than 24x. The cycle-adjusted argument cuts the wrong way here: 2025 earnings reflect peak-margin conditions (agency staffing eliminated, per-diems rising), and the FY2027 rule introduces explicit downside to that margin level. Paying 24x for peak-cycle margins ahead of a potential reimbursement cut is the opposite of buying a trough.

The stock has appreciated 84% over twelve months. Prior appreciation alone is not a valuation argument; the run reflects genuine fundamental improvement (margin expansion, deal resolution). But at the current price, the multiple already capitalizes most of that improvement, and the asymmetry no longer favors the buyer. A valuation score of 6.0 reflects fair value with limited margin of safety rather than a clear mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The SNF/post-acute industry is fragmented and local; no operator dominates. Competition is for both patients (via referral quality and reputation) and scarce labor. NHC's competitive position is improving on the metrics that matter. Its clinical quality lead is wide and durable, and in a value-based-purchasing regime that quality lead translates into better payor mix and referral flow, and it compounds — high ratings attract higher-acuity patients, which support the ratings.

The owned-real-estate base (~54% of SNF beds, rising materially after the NHI purchase) differentiates NHC from thinly-capitalized, lease-dependent operators who are exposed to REIT rent escalators and renewal risk. The NHI transaction itself reflects a broader REIT trend of rotating away from skilled nursing toward private-pay senior housing, which is what gave NHC the opportunity to buy its own dirt at a negotiated price. The death of the federal staffing mandate removes a cost burden that would have hit smaller operators harder, on a relative basis a modest competitive positive for the better-capitalized NHC.

The competitive trajectory is positive, supporting a 7.5 score. The main qualifier is that the entire industry is reimbursement-dependent, so even a structurally advantaged operator cannot fully offset a system-wide rate cut.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile carries more specific, near-term downside than is typical for a stable healthcare operator, which is why this dimension scores 5.0.

1. **FY2027 CMS SNF case-mix reduction (the binding risk).** The final rule typically lands in July. This is a dated, falsifiable, asymmetric event that directly threatens peak margins.

2. **NHI deal execution and re-leveraging.** The purchase is subject to HSR clearance with an expected Q3 2026 close. Execution risk on close is modest, but the balance sheet shifts from zero debt to materially levered. Interest expense will rise in an environment where rates have stayed elevated.

3. **Self-insurance / professional liability.** NHC self-insures through two captives; accrued risk reserves rose to $126.5M (Q1 2026) from $103.6M (2024), with $17.6M of unfavorable claims activity booked in 2025. There is no cap on claims, and punitive damages may be uninsurable in some states. The long-term-care sector faces rising wrongful-death litigation frequency and severity.

4. **NHI investment concentration.** Roughly 70% of the $189M equity portfolio (~$131.9M) is a single NHI common-stock holding carrying $107M of unrealized gains. This injects equity-market volatility into GAAP earnings and concentrates risk in the same related party NHC is buying real estate from.

5. **Governance / related-party web.** Non-executive Chairman Robert G. Adams sits on both the NHC and NHI boards, and the Flatt family is prominent in management. The NHI deal is constructive here — the press release notes Adams and Charlotte Swafford are departing NHI's board, eliminating conflicts — but the legacy structure (Administrative Services Contractor, related-party management agreements) remains a standing watch-item.

The staffing-mandate repeal and the favorable NHI resolution are genuine risk reductions that offset some of the above, but the combination of a dated reimbursement cut and a full valuation keeps the net risk elevated.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is mildly cautious near-term. Near-term ATM IV of 34.8% sits slightly above 1-year historical volatility of 31.9% (a fair 2.9-point spread), with the term structure in backwardation (34.8% near-term versus 26.5% at six months), pricing some imminent, scheduled stress, consistent with the June 18 short-dated expiry and the pending reimbursement-rule timeline. Skew is call-heavy at the front (positive 2.5% at one week, -3.2% at one month), and the put/call OI ratio is a benign 0.60, up from 0.16 a month ago as positioning normalized after the 15.5% price rise. Short interest is light at 4.7% of float (5.8 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The net read: no acute bearish positioning, but the backwardated term structure aligns with the view that the CMS final rule is the swing factor.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The CMS final rule comes in milder than proposed (component-by-component cuts rather than a blanket 4.3%, or a delayed implementation), preserving the FY2027 Medicare per-diem trajectory. The NHI purchase closes on schedule, lease expense converts to owned assets accretively, and the conflict-of-interest cleanup re-rates the governance discount. Margins hold near 8.4%, same-facility growth normalizes toward 5-6%, and adjusted EPS approaches $7.50-8.00 in FY2026-27. On a 24-26x multiple, that supports a price of roughly $215-235.

**Bear case.** The CMS rule is finalized near the proposed 4.3% case-mix cut, netting negative on Medicare reimbursement and reversing part of the margin gains. The re-leveraged balance sheet now carries meaningful interest expense into a peak-margin reversal. Adjusted EPS stalls or declines, and the 24x multiple compresses toward the 15-16x where lower-quality facility peers trade. That implies a price in the $130-150 range, a 25-35% decline from current levels.

The base case sits between these: the rule is partially mitigated, the deal closes, and earnings grow modestly while the multiple holds, putting fair value near current levels with limited upside until the reimbursement question resolves.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-oriented HOLD. The operating business and the deal resolution justify owning the shares for existing holders, but the pending reimbursement binary and full valuation prevent adding at current prices.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The CMS FY2027 SNF final rule (expected ~July 2026) lands materially milder than the proposed 4.3% case-mix reduction, or is delayed/phased, removing the principal margin threat.
- A pullback toward $150-160 (roughly 20x adjusted EPS) that restores margin of safety against the reimbursement risk.
- Confirmation that the NHI purchase closes on terms that are clearly accretive to adjusted EPS, with the new debt structure disclosed and serviceable within FCF.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The final CMS rule confirms a net-negative Medicare reimbursement outcome and adjusted EPS revisions turn negative.
- Operating margin reverses below ~7% on a sustained basis.
- The NHI deal collapses or closes on materially worse terms, or post-deal leverage exceeds a level that pressures the dividend or growth funding.
- A material adverse claims-reserve development beyond the recent $17.6M unfavorable activity.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the CMS FY2027 SNF final rule in July 2026, followed by the Q3 2026 deal close. Both should resolve within roughly four to six months.

## Conclusion

NHC is a genuinely high-quality skilled-nursing operator that has executed well — eliminating agency staffing, expanding margins, growing same-facility revenue, and converting an existential lease dispute into a strategically de-risking real-estate purchase that also cleans up its governance. These are real positives, and the staffing-mandate repeal is a tailwind that protects the cost gains.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY for a concrete, falsifiable reason: the CMS FY2027 SNF proposed rule embeds a ~4.3% case-mix reduction that management itself states could net negative on reimbursement, directly threatening the peak margins that drove the twelve-month run, with the stock trading at 24x trailing/29x adjusted earnings that already capitalize the improvement. The asymmetry at this price is unattractive ahead of a binary rule that resolves in July. Existing holders should hold through the deal close and the rule; new capital should wait for either a milder final rule or a pullback toward $150-160. The thesis upgrades on rule clarity, not on the strength of the operating story, which is already in the price.
