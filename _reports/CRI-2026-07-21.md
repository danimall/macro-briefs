**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carter's is North America's largest branded marketer of baby and young-children's apparel, with the Carter's and OshKosh B'gosh nameplates distributed through ~19,500 wholesale doors, 804 U.S. stores, and eCommerce. The investment case has narrowed to a contest between two forces. On the positive side, the top line has inflected: Q1 FY2026 net sales rose 8.1% to $681M, and U.S. Retail comparable sales rose 10.5%, the fourth consecutive positive quarter. On the negative side, profitability has collapsed. Operating margin fell from 11.8% in FY2022 to 5.0% in FY2025, net income fell to $92M, and diluted EPS fell to $2.53 from $5.12 the prior year. The dominant swing factor is tariffs: roughly $110M paid in FY2025 and ~$50M in Q1 FY2026 alone, a quarterly figure that now exceeds the company's quarterly operating income of ~$28M.

The equity is a collection of offsetting binaries rather than a clean directional bet. Carter's filed a ~$130M IEEPA tariff-refund claim in April 2026 following the Supreme Court's ruling that those tariffs were unlawful, an unrecognized gain contingency that, if realized, would be larger than a full year of net income, but which carries genuine timing and collection risk plus the threat of replacement Section 122 tariffs. A limited-duration poison pill adopted in September 2025 confirms a live activist/control situation whose one-year sunset (September 2026) is a near-term catalyst window. Management is in disarray: the company has cycled through three CEO arrangements in roughly fifteen months, with Sharon Price John (former Build-A-Bear CEO) taking the seat effective June 15, 2026.

We move to a HOLD rating with a 4.6 overall score, continuing the modest upward trajectory in scores (4.3 → 4.4 → 4.8 prior reads) that reflects the top-line inflection and the emergence of the tariff-refund and activist optionality. We stop short of a BUY because the earnings engine remains broken: tariffs are structurally impairing margins faster than pricing and cost actions can offset them, and none of the three catalysts (refund, activist, new CEO) has a dated resolution we can underwrite. This is an uncertain hold.

## Company Overview

Carter's has operated since 1865 and is the category leader in apparel for newborns to size 14. Its brand portfolio spans owned brands (Carter's, OshKosh B'gosh, Skip Hop baby gear, Little Planet organics, and the 2025-launched Otter Avenue) and exclusive private-label lines produced for mass retailers: Child of Mine (Walmart), Just One You (Target), and Simple Joys (Amazon).

Three reporting segments comprise FY2025 revenue of $2.90B:
- **U.S. Retail** — $1.47B (50.6%), 804 stores plus eCommerce/app
- **U.S. Wholesale** — $1.00B (34.5%)
- **International** — $431M (14.9%), primarily Canada (191 stores) and Mexico (73 stores), with licensees in 90+ countries

The moat rests on brand ubiquity and trust across a broad wholesale footprint. That moat is compressing under several structural forces: declining U.S. birth rates that directly shrink the addressable market, secular decline in mall traffic, intensifying competition from mass retailers' private brands (some of which are Carter's own wholesale customers), and heavy tariff exposure on a supply chain that is essentially 100% offshore in Asia.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year earnings decline predates the tariff shock and was then compounded by it:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.21B | $2.95B | $2.84B | $2.90B |
| Operating income | $379M | $323M | $255M | $144M |
| Operating margin | 11.8% | 11.0% | 9.0% | 5.0% |
| Net income | $250M | $233M | $186M | $92M |
| Diluted EPS | ~$6.42 | ~$6.29 | $5.12 | $2.53 |

FY2025 net income fell 50.5% even as revenue rose 1.9% (helped by a 53rd week worth ~$37M). The margin structure deteriorated on both sides: gross margin fell 260bps to 45.4% while SG&A rose 230bps to 41.0%. Tariffs drove the gross-margin damage; of the ~$110M of incremental tariffs paid in FY2025, ~$60M hit product costs (~$50M capitalized into inventory). Sourcing concentrates in Vietnam, Bangladesh, Cambodia, and India (~75% of spend); finished goods from China are under 3%, though China supplies roughly 60% of fabric.

Q1 FY2026 crystallized the tension. Gross margin fell another 310bps to 43.1% as tariffs added to COGS, and net income slipped to $14.3M from $15.5M. The demand recovery is real; the margin recovery is not yet visible.

The balance sheet is adequate but not fortress-grade. Cash stood at $473M in Q1'26, current ratio at 2.51, with no ABL borrowings and ~$605M of availability. The Altman Z-score of 2.52 sits in the grey zone. Debt is a single $575M 7.375% senior note due 2031, refinanced in November 2025 from a 5.625% note due 2027, a move that added more than $30M in annual interest (Q1 interest expense rose ~50% YoY) and pushed the maturity out. D/E is ~1.77.

Cash generation weakened materially. FY2025 operating cash flow fell 59% to $122M on a tariff-driven inventory build, leaving FCF of $69M. Q1'26 OCF recovered to +$6.4M (from –$48.6M a year earlier) as inventory sold through. Management has reset capital allocation defensively: the dividend was cut 69% (from $0.80 to $0.25 per quarter) beginning Q2 FY2025 "to realign with current profitability," and buybacks have been suspended since Q3 FY2024 with ~$599M of authorization untouched. These are prudent decisions given 5% operating margins and 7.375% debt, but they signal a company in balance-sheet-defense mode rather than one confident in its earnings trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line story has genuinely turned. Four consecutive quarters of positive U.S. Retail comps, culminating in +10.5% in Q1'26, indicate the store-fleet segmentation, marketing investment, and product mix shift (premium plus opening-price-point) are gaining traction with consumers. International returned to profitability in Q1'26. These are the first tangible signs that the strategic reset is working on volume.

The problem is that top-line growth alone does not fix the business when tariffs of ~$50M per quarter exceed quarterly operating income. Whether Carter's can convert the comp recovery into earnings recovery depends almost entirely on factors adjacent to demand: tariff resolution (refund and/or rollback), pricing pass-through (average unit retail is rising), and the ~$35M in annual savings from a 15% office headcount cut plus SKU rationalization of 20–30%.

Structural headwinds cap the durable growth ceiling. Declining U.S. birth rates directly shrink the addressable market, which management explicitly cites. The Amazon partnership is being restructured toward flagship brands, and Simple Joys demand is declining and expected to fall further in FY2026. The 150-store closure program (FY25–28; 35 closed in FY25, ~60 planned FY26) is a rationalization exercise that shrinks the footprint even as comps improve.

The earnings-surprise pattern reflects the volatility of a company mid-transition: Q1 FY2026 beat by 207.6% (a low-bar $0.13 estimate vs. $0.39 actual), Q4 beat by 7.9%, Q3 was in-line, and Q2 FY2025 missed by 57%. The wide dispersion signals low estimate visibility rather than a reliable beat cadence. Estimate-revision data was not available in the provided sources; the growth here is a demand recovery, not yet an earnings recovery.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.68, CRI trades at ~15x trailing EPS of $2.53 and carries a ~2.6% dividend yield (post-cut). On the surface, a 15x multiple on a category leader looks undemanding. But the trailing EPS figure sits on tariff-crushed earnings, and the forward path is genuinely uncertain.

The core difficulty is that "cheap" here does not equal "asymmetric upside." The 15x multiple applies to earnings that could go either direction by a wide margin. If the refund is realized and/or IEEPA tariffs are permanently rolled back without Section 122 replacement, normalized operating income could recover toward FY2024's $255M or beyond, and the stock is materially undervalued. If tariffs persist at current levels and pricing pass-through stalls, ~5% operating margins are the run-rate, and $2.53 EPS is closer to a ceiling than a floor. The market cap of ~$1.4B on ~$2.9B in sales already embeds meaningful skepticism.

We treat the apparent cheapness as a warning flag rather than a buy signal. A compressed multiple on a business with an eroding structural franchise (birth rates, mass-retailer competition) and an unresolved tariff overhang has the profile of a value trap absent a specific catalyst. The catalysts do exist — the refund claim, the activist/poison-pill situation, and the new CEO — but none has a dated resolution we can underwrite into a base case. The activist situation is the most interesting valuation lever: a live control contest with a September 2026 pill sunset introduces a plausible path to a strategic transaction or accelerated turnaround that could re-rate a cheap stock. We assign a 5.0 valuation score, reflecting genuine two-sidedness rather than clear undervaluation.

## Competitive Landscape

Carter's competes in a fragmented, highly promotional children's apparel market against both branded and private-label players, including some of its own wholesale customers (Walmart, Target, Amazon private brands). The 10-K flags larger competitors' superior resources and faster adoption of data and AI capabilities. Industry-wide structural headwinds — falling U.S. birth rates, secular mall-traffic decline, and post-2022 cost inflation — are shared across the sector, but Carter's is disproportionately exposed to tariffs because it sources essentially 100% offshore from Asia and sells value-priced goods where price elasticity is high.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating despite the comp recovery. The strategic pivot toward a premium plus opening-price-point mix, marketing investment, and store fleet segmentation is a defensive response to share loss, not an offensive expansion. The Amazon/Simple Joys erosion is a direct example of a distribution channel contracting. Customer concentration is a structural vulnerability: the top five wholesale customers are ~30% of sales, the two largest are 11.0% and 10.3% individually, and ~83% of gross receivables sit with the top ten customers, none under long-term contract. The comp recovery is a positive for the trajectory, but it does not yet outweigh the structural erosion, and it is occurring within a shrinking store fleet.

## Risk Assessment

The specific, idiosyncratic risks that matter most for CRI:

1. **Tariff exposure and refund uncertainty** — The single largest earnings variable. The refund claim (filed April 2026 via CBP's CAPE process following the Supreme Court IEEPA ruling) is treated as a gain contingency, not yet recognized, with genuine timing and amount uncertainty. Replacement Section 122 tariffs are a live risk that could neutralize any rollback benefit.

2. **CEO instability** — Three CEO arrangements in ~15 months (Westenberger interim → Palladini → Westenberger interim again → Sharon Price John effective June 15, 2026). Palladini's ~one-year tenure suggests either strategy clash or activist pressure. This is the risk a standard screen misses.

3. **Live activist/control situation** — The September 2025 poison pill (15% trigger, 20% for passive filers, expires September 21, 2026) was adopted explicitly in response to rapid share accumulation by an unnamed third party. This is both a risk and an optionality; the sunset is a near-term catalyst window.

4. **Customer concentration** — ~30% of sales in top five, ~83% of receivables in top ten, no long-term contracts.

5. **Structural demand headwind** — Declining U.S. birth rates permanently shrink the addressable market.

6. **Leverage and cost of debt** — 7.375% coupon locked to 2031; ABL carries springing fixed-charge-coverage and dividend-restriction covenants.

7. **Canada goodwill** — $38M carrying value with fair value only ~8% above carrying, impairment-sensitive.

Insider activity is a mild negative: only routine grants and tax-withholding dispositions, with no open-market buying despite the stock near multi-year lows. The absence of insider conviction at $35–38 stands out given the tariff-refund optionality that management understands better than anyone.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The comp recovery (four straight positive quarters, +10.5% in Q1'26) proves the turnaround has traction on demand. The tariff refund is realized, larger than a full year of net income, and IEEPA tariffs are permanently rolled back without Section 122 replacement; normalized operating income recovers toward FY2024's $255M, and EPS re-rates back toward $4–5. The activist situation forces a strategic transaction or accelerated value creation ahead of the September 2026 pill sunset. New CEO Sharon Price John, with turnaround credentials from Build-A-Bear, executes the pricing pass-through and cost program. On recovered earnings power of ~$4.50 EPS at a 14x multiple, the stock approaches $60. Bull case: ~$58 (roughly +54% from current).

### Bear Case
Tariffs persist at ~$50M/quarter, the refund claim collects little or slowly, and Section 122 replacements neutralize any IEEPA rollback. Pricing pass-through stalls against elastic value consumers, and operating margins stay near 5%. Declining birth rates and mass-retailer share loss keep the top line structurally flat-to-down beyond the near-term comp bounce. Management churn continues, the activist exits without a transaction, and the Canada goodwill impairs. On trough earnings and a de-rating to 11x, the stock revisits the low-$20s (near the 52-week low of $23.38). Bear case: ~$24 (roughly –36% from current).

### Base Case
Demand recovery persists modestly, tariffs partially pass through and partially refund, and margins recover toward the 6–7% range but not to prior peaks. EPS recovers toward ~$3.25–3.50 over 12–18 months. At ~12x, that supports a $40–42 range, modest upside from current with wide dispersion around it. Base case: ~$41 (roughly +9% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the next earnings release under new CEO Sharon Price John, which will carry the first guided view of FY2026 tariff-pass-through assumptions and any commentary on the refund claim. The September 21, 2026 poison-pill sunset is the second dated checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The tariff refund is recognized or credibly quantified with near-term collection timing, materially de-risking normalized earnings power.
- Gross margin stabilizes or expands YoY for two consecutive quarters (i.e., tariff pass-through demonstrably working), pushing operating margin back above 6.5%.
- A disclosed activist SC 13D with a constructive value-creation plan, or a strategic transaction emerges from the poison-pill situation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- U.S. Retail comps turn negative, signaling the demand recovery has stalled.
- Section 122 replacement tariffs are imposed, or the refund claim is denied/materially reduced.
- Canada goodwill impairment, dividend elimination, or covenant pressure emerges.
- Further CEO/CFO departure signaling the fourth leadership change in under two years.

## Conclusion

Carter's is a category leader whose demand has inflected positive but whose earnings engine remains broken by a tariff burden that currently exceeds operating income. The stock is a cluster of offsetting binaries — a large unrecognized refund claim, a live activist/poison-pill situation, and a brand-new CEO — none of which resolves on a schedule we can underwrite into a base case. The valuation is superficially cheap at ~15x trailing but sits on distorted earnings, and cheapness here reads as a warning about a structurally challenged franchise rather than a clear signal of asymmetric upside.

We rate CRI a **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale and an overall score of 4.6. The score trajectory has drifted higher on the top-line recovery and the emergence of the refund/activist optionality, and current data confirms rather than reverses that drift. But the improvement is not yet in the earnings, and the specific catalysts that could turn this into a BUY are exactly the ones we cannot date. We would move to BUY on evidence of tariff resolution plus two quarters of margin stabilization, and to AVOID on negative comps, Section 122 replacement tariffs, or continued leadership disintegration.
