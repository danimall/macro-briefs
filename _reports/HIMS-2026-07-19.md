**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hims & Hers is a direct-to-consumer digital health platform that scaled from $527M of revenue in 2022 to $2.35B in 2025, riding the GLP-1/obesity wave and the regulatory window that permitted mass-marketed compounded semaglutide during the FDA-declared shortage. That window closed. The FDA declared the semaglutide shortage resolved in February 2025, and in February 2026 the FDA named Hims directly in a statement on restricting GLP-1 APIs used in non-FDA-approved compounded drugs, while HHS stated it referred the company to the DOJ for investigation of potential FDCA and Title 18 (criminal) violations. The profit engine that drove the 2024–2025 growth and margins was substantially regulatory arbitrage, and the company is now being forced into a lower-margin branded-distribution model under active legal duress.

The Q1 2026 print quantified the reset. Revenue grew only 4% YoY to $608M (US revenue declined 8%, with total growth propped up by acquired international revenue), gross margin fell from 73% to 65%, adjusted EBITDA halved to $44.3M (7% margin vs. 16% a year earlier), and the company swung to a $92.1M net loss versus a $49.5M profit. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised from +$0.55 ninety days ago to -$0.08, a swing of more than 100%. Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $2.8–3.0B but cut adjusted EBITDA to $275–350M, an explicit acknowledgment that the branded GLP-1 pivot carries structurally worse unit economics.

We rate HIMS a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bear case is specific and severe: a DOJ referral for potential criminal violations is a binary overhang no fundamental model can price with confidence, and the core domestic business is already contracting. The bull case is also concrete: a large cash-pay TAM as employers drop GLP-1 coverage, a détente-turned-partnership with Novo Nordisk, international expansion, diagnostics and peptides optionality, and a genuine open-market director purchase near the February lows. When the dominant variable is an undetermined regulatory outcome and the fundamentals are mid-transition, we lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call.

## Company Overview

Hims & Hers operates a subscription-based telehealth platform. Consumers access licensed providers contracted through Affiliated Medical Groups (variable interest entities the company does not own, structured around corporate-practice-of-medicine laws) who prescribe medications fulfilled through owned and partner pharmacies. Core specialties span sexual health, hair loss, weight loss (GLP-1s), dermatology, mental health, and hormone health, with newly launched laboratory testing (November 2025) and a peptide manufacturing business.

The business carries roughly 2.5–2.6M subscribers, with the majority of US revenue recurring. Personalized offerings (provider-customized plans, often compounded formulations) grew to over 70% of US revenue in 2025 from about 50% in 2024. This concentration is both the margin engine and the regulatory vulnerability. The Hers brand represents about 40% of US revenue, driven by GLP-1s and dermatology.

The company has been vertically integrating aggressively, bringing pharmacies (Apostrophe, XeCare) in-house from VIE status in 2025, operating a 503B outsourcing facility, and acquiring peptide manufacturing (C S Bio) and blood-sampling technology (YourBio). It reports as a single operating segment but began disaggregating US versus Rest-of-World reporting in 2025 as international M&A scaled, culminating in the announced $1.15B Eucalyptus acquisition expected to close mid-2026.

CEO, Chairman, and co-founder Andrew Dudum controls approximately 90% of voting power through Class V super-voting shares (175 votes per share). This is a controlled company; public Class A holders have minimal influence over strategy and capital allocation.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue trajectory is impressive in absolute terms but decelerating sharply. Revenue moved from $527M (2022) to $872M (2023), $1,476M (2024, +69%), and $2,348M (2025, +59%). Q1 2026 broke the pattern at $608M, with international revenue up 969% off a tiny base.

Profitability is inflecting negatively. FY2025 net income of $128.4M (5.5% margin) overstates underlying quality — FY2024's $126M included a one-time ~$68M tax-valuation-allowance release, and 2025 operating income was $105.6M (4.5% margin). Adjusted EBITDA of $318M (14% margin) is the cleaner 2025 metric. The Q1 2026 loss was driven by the gross-margin decline plus $33.5M of restructuring charges (mostly inventory write-downs) and $15M of legal settlement costs tied to the US weight-loss strategic shift.

The gross-margin story is the crux. Gross margin has fallen structurally: 82% (2023) → 79% (2024) → 74% (2025) → 65% (Q1'26, 70% adjusted). Two forces drive this: the growing mix of low-margin, high-shipping GLP-1/weight-loss and international revenue, and the 2026 pivot to branded GLP-1s, which carry far higher product cost than the compounded product they replace. Branded distribution is a fundamentally worse-margin business than the compounding it substitutes for.

Cash flow and balance sheet remain adequate but weakening. Operating cash flow was $300M in FY2025 and $89M in Q1'26, but free cash flow fell to $57M in 2025 from $198M in 2024 as capex reached $226M for the pharmacy, lab, and peptide buildout. Liquidity was roughly $929M (cash plus investments) at year-end 2025, bolstered by $1.0B of 0% convertible notes due 2030 (raised May 2025) and a $175M JPM revolver. A July 2026 JPM master receivables purchase agreement unlocked ~$400M of working capital as receivables ballooned to $150M in Q1'26 amid changing branded-drug/wholesale dynamics. Debt/equity sits at 2.98 (elevated by the convert), Altman Z at 2.01 (grey zone), and the accumulated deficit widened to $205.9M by Q1'26.

Two earnings-quality flags warrant attention. The company has repeatedly revised its adjusted EBITDA definition — adding payroll tax on SBC in Q2'25 and restructuring add-backs in Q1'26 — with each revision flattering the adjusted number. Stock-based compensation ran $135M in 2025, about 6% of revenue. Investors should anchor on GAAP operating income and free cash flow rather than adjusted figures.

FY2026 guidance frames the transition, with adjusted EBITDA implying a 10–12% margin. The 2030 targets of $6.5B revenue and $1.3B adjusted EBITDA depend on the branded/peptides/diagnostics model successfully replacing the compounding profit engine.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth rate masks a bifurcated reality. The compounding-driven US core is contracting, and reported growth is now sustained by international M&A rather than organic domestic momentum. This is a company buying growth to offset a shrinking core during a forced transition.

The forward growth case rests on several unproven vectors. First, a cash-pay TAM tailwind: as employers increasingly drop GLP-1 coverage, patients may migrate to cash-pay telehealth, with Hims' branded GLP-1 priced at roughly $39 for the first month and ~$149 ongoing. Reuters flagged this as a potential 2027 demand catalyst in June 2026. Second, the Novo Nordisk relationship, which pivoted in about 30 days from a patent-infringement suit (February 9, 2026) to a branded-distribution partnership (March 2026), with Novo publicly calling Hims one of its most voluminous telehealth partners. Third, international expansion via Eucalyptus and prior acquisitions. Fourth, diagnostics (lab testing launched November 2025) and peptides.

The peptides optionality took a hit: in July 2026 an FDA advisory committee recommended against adding seven peptides to the 503A bulk list, tempering that thesis. The estimate revision data is unambiguous: current-quarter, next-quarter, and current-year consensus have all been revised down 68% to 139% over 90 days as analysts absorbed the Q1 reset. This is a downward reset toward lower-margin reality rather than an accelerating-revision signal that would support a growth-inflection thesis. We weight growth prospects heavily as a selection dimension, and here the signal is deteriorating: revenue growth has collapsed to single digits organically, margins are compressing, and the growth that remains is acquired and dilutive.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are meaningless here — the company is loss-making on a GAAP forward basis, producing the -562x figure in the profile data. The relevant lenses are EV/EBITDA on guided adjusted EBITDA, EV/revenue, and free cash flow.

At $32.84 the market cap is roughly $7.3B. On the midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA (~$312M), the enterprise trades at roughly 22–23x adjusted EBITDA — a full multiple for a business whose margins are compressing and whose core is contracting. On revenue (~$2.9B guided), the ~2.5x EV/sales is not extreme for digital health, but the quality of that revenue is deteriorating toward lower-margin branded and international mix. The FCF yield is under 1%, offering no valuation support.

Peer comparison is imperfect given HIMS's unique regulatory profile, but the Health Care Services median P/E of ~40x on profitable peers (CHE 23x, SHC 40x, VCYT 44x, CON 20x) is not a usable anchor for a company mid-transition to losses. The current price already embeds a recovery of the branded model to durable profitability — the market is not pricing HIMS as a broken business.

The estimate-revision picture argues against treating this as a mispriced growth stock. When consensus swings from +$0.55 to -$0.08 in 90 days, the multiple is being applied to a moving and shrinking earnings base. There is no independent catalyst that makes the market's current pricing clearly wrong in the bullish direction; if anything, the unresolved DOJ referral is a downside catalyst the price only partially reflects. The stock has fallen 34% over the trailing period and sits well below its $70.43 high, but the decline tracks genuine fundamental deterioration — an 8-point gross-margin collapse, a swing to losses, and a downward estimate reset — not sentiment overshoot on intact fundamentals. Cheapness relative to the prior peak is not a valuation input, and there is no compelling case that the current multiple undervalues the transitioning business. We score valuation below neutral: the multiple prices in a successful pivot that is unproven, with a binary regulatory tail unaccounted for.

## Competitive Landscape

Hims' competitive advantages are real but shallow: brand strength built on heavy marketing ($919M, 39% of revenue in 2025, including Super Bowl ads), scale, closed-loop vertically integrated fulfillment, and a large recurring subscriber base. The company competes with Ro, LifeMD, Amazon Pharmacy, traditional telehealth, retailers, Eli Lilly's LillyDirect, and Novo's own direct channels for the same cash-pay consumer.

The competitive trajectory is the concern. The differentiation that drove 2024–2025 — aggressive compounded GLP-1 offerings at low cash-pay prices — was a regulatory-arbitrage advantage that has closed. In the branded model, Hims is a distribution channel for Novo's product, dependent on a supplier and former litigant it does not control, with worse unit economics and less pricing differentiation. The vertical integration (owned pharmacies, 503B facility, peptides) is a genuine structural asset, but its value depends on regulatory pathways (compounding, peptides, LDT lab testing) that are all under active scrutiny.

The 39% marketing spend also makes the model sensitive to ad-platform policy — Meta's 2025 health-data ad-targeting restrictions raised customer acquisition costs. Rising CAC in a business whose organic core is contracting compresses the reinvestment economics. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-eroding: a strong brand and integrated infrastructure offset by a lost arbitrage edge, intensifying entry from Amazon/Lilly/Novo, and dependence on a supplier relationship for its largest growth category.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and, in one case, potentially existential.

**Regulatory/legal (dominant):** There are September 2025 FDA warning letters on compounded semaglutide website claims, a December 2025 warning letter to the outsourcing facility on adverse-event reporting, a Novo Nordisk patent-infringement suit (Delaware, February 2026), and an FTC Civil Investigative Demand outstanding since October 2023. The DOJ criminal referral is a binary overhang that cannot be modeled with confidence.

**Business-model transition:** The forced pivot from high-margin compounded GLP-1s to low-margin branded product is already visible in the Q1'26 results. Whether branded economics plus peptides and diagnostics can rebuild the profit engine is the central open question, and the July 2026 peptide advisory-committee setback narrows one of the offsets.

**M&A/dilution:** Aggressive acquisitions (Zava, Medici, YourBio, C S Bio) culminate in the Eucalyptus deal, where roughly 60% of deferred/earn-out consideration can be settled in Class A stock — potential dilution atop multi-jurisdiction integration complexity.

**Governance:** Dudum's voting control leaves public holders with minimal influence. The shift from quarterly to annual shareholder letters, announced as the business enters regulatory scrutiny and margin deterioration, reduces transparency.

**VIE structure:** Revenue depends on Affiliated Medical Groups the company does not own; adverse reinterpretation of corporate-practice-of-medicine rules could force restructuring.

These risks are the reason the risk score sits below 4. The market prices some regulatory overhang, but a DOJ referral for potential criminal violations carries a left tail that is difficult to argue is fully reflected in a stock trading at 22x adjusted EBITDA.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 132.7% sits far above 1-year historical vol of 88.1%, with IV at the 97th percentile within the current chain and the term structure in backwardation (near-term 132.7% > long-term 104.8%). Put/call skew of 9.7% at the 1-week tenor reflects elevated put demand, and the ±14.5% implied move signals the market expects a large near-term swing, consistent with headline-driven regulatory risk. There is heavy short-dated call activity ($36 and $34 strikes expiring July 24 with volume/OI ratios of 11–34x), suggesting some speculative upside positioning alongside the put demand. The rich IV means options are expensive to buy for hedging, and the elevated near-term vol confirms the market treats this as a high-variance, catalyst-sensitive name. Short interest of 31.8% of float (3.3 days to cover) adds squeeze potential on any positive regulatory or partnership headline, but also signals that a large cohort of investors shares the bearish structural view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Employers dropping GLP-1 coverage push a large cash-pay population toward Hims' branded offering, driving a 2027 demand inflection. The Novo partnership stabilizes GLP-1 supply and legitimizes the model, the DOJ referral resolves without material penalty, international expansion via Eucalyptus scales, and diagnostics/peptides add optionality. Vertical integration lets margins recover toward the 2030 target. Director Wells' $1.17M open-market purchase at $24.235 in May 2026 marks a floor, and the short interest fuels a squeeze on positive catalysts. Bull target: ~$40 (the high analyst target), roughly 22% above current.

### Bear Case
The DOJ referral escalates to enforcement action or a material settlement, the branded model fails to replace compounding profitability, US revenue continues contracting, Eucalyptus integration disappoints while diluting shareholders, and adjusted EBITDA margins settle in the low double digits or below. The reduced disclosure cadence obscures deterioration until it is severe. Bear target: ~$21 (the low analyst target), roughly 36% below current, with tail risk lower on an adverse DOJ outcome.

The base case sits between: the transition muddles through, revenue reaches the guided range with adjusted EBITDA around the guided range, the DOJ overhang persists unresolved, and the stock trades in a wide, headline-driven band. Base-case fair value approximates the current price to the mid-$30s (broadly consistent with the ~$28.42 mean and $25.00 median analyst targets), offering no compelling asymmetry in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The recommendation history (HOLD → AVOID → HOLD → HOLD) and declining score trajectory (5.6 in March to 5.0 in June) reflect a business that has deteriorated fundamentally, not merely fallen in price. The Q1'26 loss, margin collapse, and downward estimate reset are genuine fundamental deterioration that justifies the lower scores. The risk-factor score improved modestly (4.0 → 4.8 across prior reports) as the Novo relationship pivoted from adversarial to commercial, but the DOJ referral keeps the tail severe.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):** (1) DOJ referral resolves without material financial penalty or with a clear, contained settlement; (2) gross margin stabilizes at or above ~68% and adjusted EBITDA margin recovers toward the mid-teens across two consecutive quarters, demonstrating branded/diagnostics economics can rebuild the profit engine; (3) US organic revenue returns to positive YoY growth, confirming the cash-pay tailwind is real.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):** (1) DOJ investigation escalates to a formal enforcement action or indictment; (2) adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below the current range, or a fourth consecutive quarter of gross-margin decline; (3) Eucalyptus close triggers dilution beyond expectations, or further senior finance departures compound the July 2026 CAO exit amid the DOJ overhang.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next scheduled reassessment is the FY2026 annual shareholder letter and Q2/Q3 disclosures, though the shift to annual letters means the market may go dark on granular data — any 8-K on the DOJ matter, Eucalyptus close terms, or executive changes should trigger interim review. The DOJ outcome is the dominant unscheduled catalyst.

## Conclusion

Hims & Hers is a strong-brand digital-health platform in the middle of a forced, regulatory-driven business-model transition, with an unresolved DOJ criminal referral as the dominant variable. The financial evidence is clear that the reset is underway: a Q1'26 loss, a gross-margin collapse, contracting US revenue, and a downward estimate revision. The forward case depends on a cash-pay tailwind, a Novo partnership the company does not control, and diagnostics/peptides optionality that recent regulatory actions have partly clouded. Against that, a genuine open-market director purchase near the lows and a large short base offer upside optionality on positive headlines.

We do not have sufficient conviction for a directional call. The valuation prices in a successful pivot with no allowance for the binary regulatory tail, the fundamentals are mid-transition with deteriorating quality and disclosure, and the options market is pricing imminent stress. HOLD, uncertain, with the DOJ resolution as the pivot point. Position sizing, if held, should reflect the high variance and binary regulatory risk.
