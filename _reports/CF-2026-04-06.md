## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer, operating a network of eight manufacturing complexes across North America, the U.K., and Trinidad. The company's competitive position rests on a structural feedstock cost advantage: North American natural gas at ~$3.31/MMBtu versus European and Asian marginal producers paying multiples of that cost. CF does not set nitrogen prices — it takes them from global markets set by high-cost producers. When those prices rise, CF's margins expand disproportionately because its costs remain anchored to Henry Hub.

The stock has risen ~63% since early April 2025, driven by the Iran/Middle East conflict disrupting the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb, which together route ~30% of global fertilizer trade. Urea prices are up 77% per UBS. CF trades at ~14.5x forward earnings — a fraction of the 33.2x Materials sector peer median — while generating $1.8B in free cash flow and returning 95% of it to shareholders through aggressive buybacks (share count down 20% in three years). Non-discretionary agricultural demand, supply destruction with multi-year physical rebuild timelines, a $100-110/barrel oil floor supporting elevated energy input costs for competitors, and insurance-driven shipping disruption create a structural earnings tailwind that persists under any scenario short of full Hormuz reopening with P&I club reinsurance restoration.

The primary risks are insider selling breadth (five executives sold $28M+ during the March spike), commodity cyclicality if geopolitical tensions de-escalate materially, and execution risk on the $3.7B Blue Point JV. The Yazoo City outage removes ~600K tons of ammonia capacity through Q4 2026. At 14.5x forward earnings with a $100-110 oil floor supporting competitor cost disadvantages, the valuation compensates for these risks.

## Company Overview

CF Industries manufactures nitrogen-based products — anhydrous ammonia, granular urea, UAN solution, ammonium nitrate, and diesel exhaust fluid — for agricultural and industrial applications. Revenue in 2025 was $7.08B across five product segments: Ammonia (31%), UAN (31%), Granular Urea (25%), AN (6%), and Other (8%).

The company operates six U.S. manufacturing facilities (anchored by Donaldsonville, Louisiana — the world's largest ammonia plant), two Canadian facilities (Medicine Hat, Alberta and Courtright, Ontario), one U.K. facility (Billingham), and a 50% JV in Trinidad (PLNL). A $3.7B greenfield low-carbon ammonia plant (Blue Point JV, 40% CF ownership) is under construction in Louisiana targeting 2029 production.

CF's North American terminal network adds logistics advantages at scale. The company is the first major nitrogen producer to operationalize carbon capture and storage (CCS), with Donaldsonville CCS running since July 2025 and generating $42M in 45Q tax credits.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** Revenue recovered to $7.08B in 2025 (+19% YoY), driven almost entirely by pricing — average selling prices rose 19% to $372/ton while volumes held flat at ~19.1M tons. The operational leverage is direct: volume is relatively stable (agriculture is non-discretionary), but pricing swings drive large margin changes. Gross margins expanded to 38.5% from 34.6%, and operating income rose to $2.30B (+31%). Net income reached $1.46B, up from $1.22B.

Natural gas costs rose 38% to $3.31/MMBtu, absorbing $316M of margin. CF's advantage held because European gas prices rose more sharply, maintaining the cost spread that sets CF's margin.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $2.75B in 2025, with capex of $950M yielding $1.80B in free cash flow (25.4% FCF margin). FCF conversion quality is high: OCF exceeds net income, indicating clean accrual accounting. The company returned $1.70B to shareholders ($1.37B buybacks + $0.33B dividends), consuming 94% of FCF.

**Balance Sheet:** $1.98B cash, $3.25B long-term debt (all fixed-rate), $750M undrawn revolver. No maturities until 2034 after successfully refinancing $750M of 2026 notes. Current ratio of 3.37x. Net leverage is approximately 0.5x EBITDA. The Altman Z-Score of 1.37 is misleading, driven by buyback-reduced equity rather than operational distress. A company generating $2.75B in operating cash flow with $2.7B in available liquidity and no near-term maturities has no refinancing risk.

**Capital Intensity Increasing:** Capex is guided to $1.3B for 2026 (up from ~$500M run-rate), driven by Blue Point JV construction and scalable infrastructure. Elevated spend persists through 2029. FCF will compress in the near term, but the investment creates optionality in low-carbon ammonia, a market with potential pricing premiums and regulatory tailwinds (EU CBAM, 45Q credits).

**Earnings Surprises:** Three beats in the last four quarters, including a 24.9% beat in Q4 2025 ($3.20 vs. $2.56 estimate) and a 28.5% beat in Q1 2025. The single miss (Q2 2025, -10.3%) appears attributable to seasonal timing. Forward estimates look achievable on this basis.

## Growth Analysis

CF's growth profile has two components: cyclical upside from nitrogen pricing and structural investment in low-carbon ammonia.

**Cyclical:** The dual-chokepoint disruption drives the near-term thesis. 30% of global fertilizer trade transits Hormuz. 86% of fertilizer shipments from the Gulf to East Africa have ceased (Windward data). India's urea stocks are 35% below year-ago levels. FAO warns of food price escalation and "record levels of hunger" as sub-Saharan African farmers enter planting season unable to treat crops. This demand is seasonal and non-discretionary — farmers must fertilize or lose yield. CF captures this pricing tailwind without exposure to the supply disruption because its manufacturing is in North America.

Even a 45-day ceasefire leaves Hormuz insurance unreopened. Seven of twelve P&I clubs have cancelled Gulf war risk coverage entirely. Historical precedent for insurance market re-entry after comparable disruptions: 1-3 years. Iran's toll corridor is institutional — parliamentary legislation, fee schedules, IRGC screening protocols. Physical shipping insurance, not just military access, determines whether fertilizer flows resume through the Strait.

**Structural:** The Blue Point JV ($3.7B, 2029 target) adds ~1.9M tons of low-carbon ammonia capacity using ATR technology with CCS. EU CBAM, effective January 2026, creates a pricing premium for verified low-carbon ammonia entering Europe. Donaldsonville CCS (operational July 2025) validates the technology pathway and already generates 45Q tax credits. If low-carbon ammonia markets develop as projected by IRENA and the IEA for shipping fuel and industrial decarbonization, Blue Point positions CF as the dominant Western hemisphere supplier.

**Volume outlook:** 2026 ammonia production guided at ~9.5M tons (down from 10.1M in 2025) due to the Yazoo City outage. This is a temporary headwind — restart expected Q4 2026. Global demand grows ~1.5% annually on population and dietary trends.

## Valuation Assessment

CF trades at ~14.5x forward earnings versus a Materials sector peer median of 33.2x. The discount partly reflects CF's commodity-cyclical nature — the market assigns lower multiples to earnings perceived as peak or temporary. Several factors argue the discount is excessive:

1. **The $100-110 oil floor is structural, not cyclical.** Supply response failure at $116 (no drilling response, capital discipline confirmed by three independent sources), bypass pipeline capacity covering only 28% of Hormuz flows, and destroyed Gulf industrial capacity with multi-year rebuild timelines mean competitor energy costs remain elevated regardless of ceasefire outcomes.

2. **FCF yield is ~9% at current prices** ($1.80B FCF / $20.1B market cap). Even with capex rising to $1.3B in 2026, operating cash flow at current nitrogen pricing levels would sustain $1.5B+ FCF.

3. **Share count compression accelerates EPS growth.** With 20% fewer shares than 2022 and another $1.7B remaining on the buyback authorization, CF converts flat-to-modest revenue growth into meaningful per-share growth.

4. **The stock trades 22% above analyst consensus** ($131 vs. median PT of $103), but consensus targets lag the macro regime shift. UBS raised to $140 after the Hormuz disruption; BofA remains at $103 with Underperform. The analyst distribution (15 Hold, 3 Buy, 3 Sell) reflects consensus anchored to pre-crisis nitrogen pricing. Several targets likely predate the March escalation.

The 1.5% dividend yield is modest but supported by a conservative 22% payout ratio. Buybacks drive the capital return thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

CF is the world's largest ammonia producer with an unmatched North American manufacturing and distribution network. Key competitors:

- **Nutrien (NTR):** Larger by revenue but more diversified (retail, potash, phosphate). CF is a pure-play nitrogen producer, providing cleaner exposure to nitrogen pricing.
- **Yara International:** Global #1 in nitrogen overall, but European manufacturing base faces structurally higher gas costs, carbon pricing, and now Hormuz-related LNG disruptions.
- **OCI Global:** Exposed to Middle East operations and European gas costs.
- **Koch Fertilizer / LSB Industries:** Smaller-scale U.S. producers that benefit from the same tailwinds but lack CF's scale.

CF's advantages are threefold: (1) the largest and lowest-cost ammonia network in the Western hemisphere, (2) proximity to the largest nitrogen-consuming market (U.S. Corn Belt), and (3) first-mover advantage in commercially validated low-carbon ammonia production. The distribution network creates switching costs — farmers and industrial buyers depend on reliable supply during narrow application windows.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Cyclicality:** CF's earnings are a leveraged bet on the nitrogen price cycle. A return to 2024 pricing ($313/ton) from current levels ($372+) would reduce EBITDA by approximately $1B+. The 10-K warns of historical price declines of 34%+. The current geopolitical premium, while structurally supported, could partially unwind on ceasefire progress.

**Blue Point Execution Risk:** $3.7B project using ATR technology at unprecedented scale for ammonia production. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges the technology is unproven at this scale. Louisiana's moratorium on new Class VI CCS well applications adds regulatory risk. Total CF exposure exceeds $2B. Construction cost overruns on a project of this magnitude would consume substantial FCF.

**Yazoo City Outage:** 600K ton production shortfall through at least Q4 2026. Management recorded a $25M impairment; further charges possible if restart is delayed.

**Natural Gas Linkage Risk:** LNG exports are increasingly linking Henry Hub to global gas markets. U.S. gas hit $7.72/MMBtu in January 2026 before retreating. Sustained U.S. gas prices above $5/MMBtu would compress CF's cost advantage versus global producers.

**IRS Audit / Tax Reserve:** $357M in unrecognized tax benefits represent potential cash exposure. This is material — approximately 2% of market cap.

**Insider Selling:** Five executives sold $28M+ in stock during March 2026. This is broad, heavy, and concentrated at prices between $108-$136. No open-market purchases were observed. When five insiders sell simultaneously during a geopolitically-driven price spike, it indicates internal assessment that current prices reflect temporary factors. This is the most bearish signal in the dataset.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

CF is the best-positioned company in the global fertilizer complex to benefit from the dual-chokepoint crisis. The causal chain: Hormuz/Bab el-Mandeb disruption → global nitrogen supply contraction → fertilizer price surge → CF margins expand because its costs (North American gas) are decoupled from the disrupted supply routes. The demand side is non-discretionary and seasonal — farmers must fertilize during planting windows regardless of price.

Insurance market re-entry takes 1-3 years. Iran's toll corridor has institutional infrastructure. Destroyed Gulf capacity (smelters, refineries, petrochemical complexes) has multi-year physical rebuild timelines. Even a rapid ceasefire does not restore fertilizer trade flows through the Strait because insurance, not military access, is the binding constraint on commercial shipping.

At ~14.5x forward earnings with 9% FCF yield and a 20% share count reduction over three years, CF offers commodity upside at a valuation that already discounts cyclical risk. The Blue Point JV, while risky, creates optionality in low-carbon ammonia at a time when EU CBAM is creating a verified pricing premium.

### Bear Case

CF has rallied 63% in three months on a geopolitical event that could partially reverse. Insiders sold $28M+ at current levels — the broadest insider selling pattern in the company's recent history. Analyst consensus ($103 median) is 21% below the current price, and two firms (BofA, Mizuho) rate the stock Underperform with targets at $103. A ceasefire acceptance at Tuesday's deadline could send nitrogen prices down 10-15% immediately, compressing CF's geopolitical premium.

The Yazoo City outage removes 600K tons from 2026 production. Capex is stepping up to $1.3B, compressing near-term FCF. Blue Point's $2B+ CF commitment uses unproven technology at scale, with regulatory risk from Louisiana's CCS moratorium. Natural gas costs spiked to $7.72/MMBtu in January 2026, and LNG export growth is eroding CF's structural cost advantage.

If nitrogen prices revert to 2024 levels ($313/ton), EPS could fall to $5-6, putting the stock at 22-26x trough earnings — no longer cheap for a cyclical commodity producer.

## Conclusion

The insider selling is the strongest counter-argument and cannot be dismissed. Five executives selling $28M in a three-week window is a credible negative signal. However, the pattern is consistent with executives monetizing a geopolitical windfall at prices they may not see again if the crisis de-escalates — rational behavior that does not invalidate the structural thesis.

The structural thesis does not depend on continued escalation. It depends on three conditions that persist under any scenario short of full Hormuz reopening with insurance restoration: (1) disrupted global fertilizer supply routes, (2) non-discretionary seasonal demand, and (3) CF's cost insulation as a North American producer. The probability of full Hormuz reopening with P&I club re-entry within the next 12 months is low (10-15% per the macro framework). At 14.5x forward earnings with 9% FCF yield, the stock prices in meaningful downside risk already.

Recommendation: BUY. Specific catalysts and favorable valuation are both present. The catalyst is the dual-chokepoint disruption with multi-year resolution timelines. The valuation is less than half the sector median P/E with best-in-class FCF generation. The primary risk is rapid geopolitical de-escalation that normalizes nitrogen prices, which would compress earnings toward $6-7 and likely bring the stock to $90-100. Position sizing should reflect this downside scenario.
