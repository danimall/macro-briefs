**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hilltop Holdings is a Dallas-based diversified financial holding company that the "Regional Banks" label understates. It operates through three segments — PlainsCapital Bank (94% of Q1 2026 pre-tax income), Hilltop Securities (a top-tier national municipal-finance franchise), and PrimeLending (a chronically loss-making residential mortgage originator). The investment case rests less on loan growth than on capital return: management retired roughly 10% of shares outstanding in 16 months at prices between $32 and $38, near or below tangible book value of $31.97, funded by one of the most overcapitalized balance sheets in the sector (CET1 ~19.08%, roughly double peer norms).

The operating story is improving underneath a flattering FY2025 headline. Reported EPS jumped to $2.64 from $1.74, but that comparison is distorted by a $27.8M pre-tax merchant-bank gain (Moser/Atlas Energy sale) plus ~$16M of legal and settlement recoveries. Stripping those out, the Q1 2026 print shows the underlying franchise strengthening: consolidated NIM expanded to 3.15% from 2.86% year-over-year (banking-segment NIM to 3.39% from 2.97%), broker-dealer pre-tax income rose 60%, and the mortgage loss narrowed 72%. Forward estimates confirm the momentum: current-year consensus EPS has been revised up 13.9% over 90 days to $2.38, and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters, twice by 30-64%.

The offsets are specific. The bank remains asset-sensitive into a Fed cutting cycle, so continued rate cuts pressure net interest income (a -200 bps consolidated shock is modeled at -$15.3M NII). Credit is deteriorating from a low base: non-accruals rose $7.8M to $61M and potential problem loans jumped $21.9M in a single quarter, concentrated in non-owner-occupied CRE and C&I auto-note financing. The mortgage unit carries a standing goodwill-impairment warning, and an unresolved DOJ grand-jury inquiry into PrimeLending's "float-down option" remains an open tail. We rate HTH a HOLD. The durable return comes from buybacks below book and a covered ~2.1% dividend, not from a re-rating catalyst, and the stock at ~1.2x tangible book already reflects the capital-return story.

## Company Overview

Hilltop Holdings is organized under two primary units, PlainsCapital Corporation and Hilltop Securities Holdings, with three reportable segments:

- **Banking (PlainsCapital Bank):** The earnings engine. Texas-focused commercial and consumer bank with ~$12.5B in segment assets and ~$8.4B in loans held for investment, heavily weighted to commercial real estate. It contributed $47.1M of the $50.4M consolidated pre-tax income in Q1 2026.
- **Broker-Dealer (Hilltop Securities):** A national municipal and public-finance franchise offering municipal advisory, underwriting, fixed-income sales and trading, securities lending, clearing, structured finance, and retail wealth management. Q1 2026 pre-tax income of $14.8M, with 190 issues and $16.4B of aggregate offerings in the quarter.
- **Mortgage Origination (PrimeLending):** A nationwide residential originator that has lost money for three consecutive years: pre-tax losses of $17.5M (2025), $33.7M (2024), and $62.8M (2023), narrowing to $2.4M in Q1 2026.

The company also runs a merchant-banking arm, Hilltop Opportunity Partners (~$90M carrying value), with holdings in power generation, youth sports, dental, industrial, and aerospace/defense. This portfolio produces episodic gains that add earnings lumpiness, as the 2025 Moser sale demonstrated.

The diversified structure differentiates HTH from plain-vanilla regional banks: the bank provides stable spread income, the broker-dealer is a genuine national franchise in municipal finance, and the mortgage unit is a cyclical drag. This mix drives more earnings volatility than a comparably sized single-charter bank.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability.** FY2025 net income to Hilltop was $165.6M ($2.64 diluted EPS), up from $113.2M ($1.74) in 2024. The quality caveat matters: the year benefited from a $27.8M pre-tax ($21.6M net) merchant-bank gain, a $6.5M bank legal-fee reimbursement, and a $9.5M PrimeLending settlement. Core earnings excluding those items are materially lower, so the headline growth rate overstates the underlying trajectory. Q1 2026 net income was $37.8M ($0.64), down from $42.1M ($0.65) a year prior, but the prior-year quarter contained the Moser gain. Adjusting for that, the underlying business improved across all three segments.

**Net interest margin.** Consolidated NIM expanded to 3.15% (Q1 2026, tax-equivalent) from 2.86% a year ago; banking-segment NIM rose to 3.39% from 2.97%. The driver is deposit repricing: interest-bearing deposit cost fell 67 bps to 2.56% following the Fed's 175 bps of cuts since September 2024, with cumulative deposit beta around 74%. This is the clearest fundamental positive in the story.

The complication is asset-sensitivity. The bank runs a positive GAP position (cumulative +31.8% of interest-sensitive assets beyond five years). A falling-rate environment therefore pressures NII: -100 bps is modeled at roughly -$1.1M of bank NII, and -200 bps at roughly -$15.3M consolidated. Management has explicitly stated it may reposition toward a more neutral or liability-sensitive balance sheet. Given a two-sided rate path, the near-term direction of NIM is uncertain, which caps how much of the recent expansion should be extrapolated.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Total assets $15.7B, deposits $10.5B, loans HFI $8.4B. CET1 stands at 19.08%, total capital 21.5%, and leverage 12.82% at the Hilltop level, well above well-capitalized thresholds and among the highest in the sector. Book value per share is $36.63, tangible book value $31.97. At $38.82, the stock trades ~1.06x book and ~1.21x tangible book. Leverage is minimal: a single $150M tranche of 2035 sub-notes (6.125%) remains after redeeming $150M senior notes (January 2025) and $50M sub-notes (May 2025).

**Capital allocation.** The buyback is the central story. FY2025 repurchases totaled $184M / 5.7M shares (~9% of the count) at an average ~$32.26; Q1 2026 added $47.5M / 1.24M shares at $38.40. A new $125M authorization was approved in January 2026 (~$77.5M remaining after Q1). The dividend was raised 11% to $0.20/quarter with a payout ratio around 31%. Share count fell from 65.0M (December 2024) to 58.5M (April 2026), a ~10% reduction that is materially accretive to per-share metrics and is the core of the equity thesis. With CET1 at roughly double peers, HTH has ample excess capital to sustain this pace.

**Deposit dynamics.** Total deposits declined ~$347M in Q1 2026 to $10.5B, partly a deliberate runoff of high-cost balances, funded via short-term borrowings (+$314M). Uninsured deposits are ~$5.9B (56% of total, ~46% excluding collateralized and internal accounts), with top-15 depositors at 16.5%. Mitigants include $6.2B of available secured liquidity, no FHLB usage, and a captive FDIC-sweep program from Hilltop Securities.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is modest and cyclical rather than structural. Revenue and earnings power are recovering on funding-cost relief and a broker-dealer rebound, not on durable balance-sheet expansion; the loan book is roughly flat and deposits are being deliberately trimmed.

The forward estimate trend is the more useful signal. The current-quarter estimate is up 16.5% to $0.49. The revision velocity has stalled in the most recent 30 days (0.0% to +0.5% across periods), so the momentum is real but decelerating. Combined with the three-of-four beat record — including 63.7% and 49.5% surprises in recent quarters — the estimate path warrants reasonable confidence in the near-term earnings floor. This falls short of the +30% inflection threshold that would flag a strong buy signal, and the underlying business is a mature financial rather than a company recovering from a genuine trough, so we treat the revisions as supportive but not decisive.

The three growth levers, weighted:
- **Broker-dealer (positive):** Public-finance activity rebounded 60% in Q1, a genuine franchise strength. It is transaction-dependent and rate-sensitive, so it will not compound smoothly.
- **NIM (two-sided):** Expanded sharply on deposit repricing, but asset-sensitivity means further cuts reverse the tailwind.
- **Mortgage (structural drag turning cyclical):** Losses narrowed 72% and volumes rose 16% YoY as rates eased, but three straight loss years and a standing goodwill-impairment warning cap the upside here.

The dominant per-share growth engine is the buyback, which mechanically lifts EPS by ~10% over 16 months independent of operating performance. That is real but not a source of multiple expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.82, HTH trades at ~14.8x trailing EPS of $2.64 and ~16.3x current-year consensus of $2.38. On tangible book of $31.97, the stock is at ~1.21x TBV. Against the Regional Banks peer set (BANR 11.3x, MBIN 12.1x, TBBK 11.0x, NWBI 15.6x, FCF 12.9x; peer median P/E 12.1x), HTH trades at a premium on earnings.

Two things justify some premium but not a re-rating. First, the trailing $2.64 is inflated by ~$0.35+ of non-recurring gains ($21.6M net after tax on ~58.5M shares), so the "true" trailing multiple on core earnings is higher than 14.8x — this argues against the stock being cheap. Second, the CET1 cushion at ~19% is roughly double peers, which supports a higher TBV multiple because the excess capital is being returned rather than sitting idle. On balance, ~1.2x tangible book for a franchise with a top-tier broker-dealer, a fortress balance sheet, and aggressive buybacks is fair rather than cheap.

The dividend yield of ~2.1% sits below the peer median of 2.9%, so the stock does not screen as a yield play. The forward P/E of ~16.3x is at a premium to the ~12-13x peer cluster; the premium is defensible given the diversified model and capital return, but it does not create the asymmetric mispricing that would warrant a high valuation score. We resist reflexively rewarding a low absolute multiple here: the earnings quality is mixed and the multiple is above, not below, its peer group. There is no independent catalyst (spin, tender, forced re-rating) that would close a valuation gap, because there is no clear gap to close. The stock is priced approximately where a diversified, over-capitalized Texas financial should be.

## Competitive Landscape

Hilltop's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving, with one genuine moat and one structural weakness.

**Broker-dealer moat.** Hilltop Securities is a top-tier national municipal advisor and underwriter. This is a durable, fee-generating franchise that differentiates HTH from single-charter regional banks and diversifies revenue away from spread income. Its pre-tax gain in Q1 reflects both cyclical rate relief and share retention.

**Texas banking franchise.** PlainsCapital operates in high-growth Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin, Houston, and San Antonio markets, with a low-cost deposit base augmented by the captive broker-dealer sweep. This funding advantage is what drove the NIM recovery. The offset is CRE and office concentration in those same markets plus ~$119M of energy loans.

**Mortgage weakness.** PrimeLending is a sector-wide casualty of elevated rates since 2022 rather than a company-specific failure, but three straight loss years, recurring capital infusions ($25M in 2025 to maintain GNMA/FNMA minimums), and a goodwill-impairment warning make it a persistent liability. It improves as rates ease but remains structurally challenged in the current environment.

Net, the trajectory is stable. The broker-dealer is gaining, the bank is holding funding advantage, and the mortgage unit is recovering cyclically without a clear path to sustained profitability. Competitive-position scores have shown decaying predictive value recently, so we weight this dimension modestly and pair it with the more reliable financial-health and growth reads.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit migration (primary near-term risk).** Net charge-offs rose to ~0.21% of average loans (from 0.14% in 2024, 0.03% in 2023). Non-accruals reached 0.66% of loans, driven by non-owner-occupied CRE (+$11.4M) and owner-occupied CRE (+$4.6M), and potential problem loans jumped to $146.8M from $124.9M. Non-owner-occupied office exposure is ~$538M. The Q1 provision was only $1.8M despite a $4.0M specific-reserve build, because collectively-evaluated reserves were released $2.3M — a benign provision that could reverse if stress broadens. The downside CECL scenario implies +$59M to the allowance. This is early-stage deterioration off a low base, not distress, but it is the item most likely to move the stock.

**Asset-sensitivity in a cutting cycle.** The positive GAP means continued Fed cuts pressure NII. Management is contemplating a shift to neutral/liability-sensitive positioning, a strategic change worth tracking.

**Mortgage goodwill.** MD&A repeatedly warns the mortgage reporting unit's fair value may require a goodwill impairment ($13.1M mortgage goodwill; $267M total). A write-down would be non-cash and capital-neutral but headline-negative.

**DOJ inquiry.** PrimeLending has cooperated since September 2020 with a U.S. Attorney (W.D. Virginia) grand-jury investigation into its "float-down option." Unresolved, low-probability, but a genuine tail.

**Deposit concentration/liquidity.** Mitigated by secured liquidity and captive sweep deposits, but a relevant vulnerability in any deposit-flight scenario.

**Governance churn.** A cluster of Item 5.02 8-K filings across four quarters (August 2025, December 2025, February 2026, April 2026) suggests board/executive turnover worth monitoring, alongside steady small director selling.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing acute, event-specific stress. ATM IV is 93.1% versus 23.7% one-year historical volatility (a 69.3pp spread), with the term structure in steep backwardation (93.1% near-term versus 27.1% at six months) and an implied move of ±8.9% into the nearest expiry. IV surged from 36.5% to 93.1% since late May while the price moved only +2.7%, which signals the market is bracing for an imminent binary. The skew is call-heavy (-38.5%), so positioning is not aggressively bearish; the elevated near-term vol most plausibly reflects an approaching earnings print rather than a directional put bet. This tempers conviction on any short-term entry, but the call-heavy skew argues against a fundamental deterioration thesis. Short interest is moderate at 6.2% of float with 7.7 days to cover, uncrowded and not squeeze-prone.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$46, ~+18%)
Credit migration stabilizes without a material provision build; the broker-dealer sustains its municipal-finance momentum; NIM holds as management repositions ahead of further cuts; and the buyback continues to retire ~5-9% of shares annually below tangible book. Forward EPS revisions continue upward, the mortgage unit turns marginally profitable on easing rates, and the stock re-rates modestly toward 1.4x tangible book (~$45) as excess capital is recognized. This implies roughly $46 on ~18x forward EPS approaching $2.55.

### Base Case (~$41, ~+6%)
The franchise grinds forward: NIM stable-to-slightly-lower on cuts, broker-dealer solid, credit costs rising gradually but contained, buyback continuing. EPS lands near consensus $2.38-2.40, and the stock holds ~1.25x tangible book with per-share metrics lifted mechanically by repurchases. This is the most likely outcome, and it sits near the top of the analyst target band.

### Bear Case (~$31, ~-20%)
CRE and C&I credit deterioration broadens, forcing a provision build toward the modeled +$59M allowance scenario; a mortgage goodwill impairment adds a headline-negative charge; continued Fed cuts compress NIM on the asset-sensitive book; and the DOJ inquiry resolves adversely. The stock derates to ~1.0x tangible book (~$32) or below as the earnings-quality caveats become the market narrative. Downside is cushioned by the fortress capital position, which limits solvency risk but not multiple compression.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: a positive but modest 12-month appreciation case anchored on capital return and rising estimates, without a catalyst strong enough to warrant a BUY at ~1.2x tangible book near the top of the analyst target range.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July 2026) is the natural checkpoint; it will show whether Q1 credit migration was a blip or a trend, and how NIM tracks against the Fed path. The near-term options backwardation suggests the market shares this focus.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Non-accruals and potential problem loans stabilize or decline for two consecutive quarters while the buyback pace holds, confirming the capital-return story without a credit offset.
- The stock pulls back toward tangible book (~$32-34) on no fundamental change, restoring asymmetric upside on the buyback-accretion math.
- Estimate revision velocity re-accelerates (current-year EPS revised up >5% in a 30-day window) alongside sustained broker-dealer growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2/Q3 provision expense jumps materially (e.g., >$15M in a quarter) or non-accruals exceed ~1.0% of loans, signaling CRE stress is broadening.
- A mortgage goodwill impairment is recorded, or PrimeLending losses re-widen despite easing rates.
- An adverse DOJ resolution, or a deposit outflow that forces reliance on higher-cost wholesale funding and reverses NIM gains.

## Conclusion

Hilltop is a well-run, over-capitalized diversified Texas financial whose equity story is capital return rather than growth. The fortress balance sheet, a genuine municipal-finance moat, a NIM expansion on funding-cost relief, and a ~10% share-count reduction in 16 months at prices near tangible book are all real positives, and rising forward estimates support the near-term earnings floor. Against those sit an asset-sensitive book heading into a Fed cutting cycle, early CRE and C&I credit migration, a chronically loss-making mortgage unit with a standing goodwill warning, and an unresolved DOJ inquiry. The FY2025 headline is inflated by non-recurring gains, so the true earnings power is more modest than $2.64 implies.

At ~1.2x tangible book and ~16.3x forward earnings, a premium to the peer P/E cluster, the stock is fairly priced for what it is. The durable return comes from buybacks below book and a covered dividend, not from a re-rating. Financials as a sector have historically delivered negative selection alpha absent a distinct company catalyst, and HTH does not yet present one. We rate HTH a HOLD, with the base case pointing to modest single-digit appreciation and the credit trend the single most important variable to monitor at the July print.
