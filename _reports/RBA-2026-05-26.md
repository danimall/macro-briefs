## Executive Summary

RB Global (formerly Ritchie Bros.) operates as the world's largest omnichannel marketplace for commercial assets and salvage vehicles, combining the legacy Ritchie Bros. heavy equipment auction franchise with IAA's auto salvage marketplace acquired in 2023. The company generates $4.6B in annual revenue across $16.2B in Gross Transaction Value, with strong unit economics ($719M FCF on an asset-light model) and a structural deleveraging trajectory (1.4x net debt/EBITDA, down from 1.6x a year ago).

Q1 2026 results were encouraging: revenue grew 11.4% with CC&T GTV up 27% (+16% organic) as pent-up equipment supply returned to auction. Management raised full-year guidance to 6-9% GTV growth and ~8% EBITDA growth. The BigIron agricultural equipment acquisition (closed May 2026) adds an adjacency not yet in guidance.

The challenge is valuation. At ~49x trailing EPS ($2.04) and ~24x consensus FY26E ($4.40), RBA trades at a substantial premium to diversified services peers (median 20x). The premium is partially justified by the marketplace business model's capital efficiency and recurring demand drivers, but requires sustained execution on integration synergies, CC&T volume recovery, and IAA international expansion to deliver returns from current levels. With 7.7% short interest (10 days to cover) and limited near-term estimate revisions (+1.1% over 90 days for FY26), the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling.

## Company Overview

RB Global operates three core businesses under a unified marketplace platform:

**Automotive (53% of GTV):** The IAA segment processes total-loss and salvage vehicles from insurance companies, connecting them with a global buyer base of dismantlers, rebuilders, and exporters. This operates as a duopoly with Copart (the #1 player). Key demand driver: total loss rates (23.6% in Q1 2026, up 70bps YoY) which are structurally rising as repair costs inflate faster than used vehicle values.

**CC&T (35% of GTV):** The Ritchie Bros. brand conducts live and online auctions of construction equipment, commercial trucks, agricultural machinery, and industrial assets. This is the global leader in a highly fragmented market. Volume declined in FY2025 but rebounded sharply in Q1 2026.

**Other (12% of GTV):** Includes Rouse (data/analytics), SmartEquip (equipment lifecycle management), VeriTread (transportation), and other value-added services.

Revenue is primarily service-fee based (~76%), with buyer and seller commissions generating predictable transaction economics. The remaining ~24% comes from inventory sales (buying and reselling equipment directly) at lower margins.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**
- FY2025 revenue: $4.59B (+7% YoY), with organic growth in both segments
- FY2025 adjusted EBITDA: $1.4B (+7%), representing a 30.5% margin
- FY2025 adjusted EPS: $4.00 (+15%), reflecting operational leverage and debt paydown
- GAAP net income: $428M, but only $382M attributable to common shareholders due to $482M in preferred stock taking priority on distributions

**Q1 2026 results confirmed acceleration:**
- Revenue: $1.23B (+11.4% YoY)
- GTV: $4.3B (+13%, +9% organic)
- Adjusted EPS: $1.01 vs. $1.00 consensus (0.7% beat)

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:**
- Operating cash flow: $978M (FY2025)
- Free cash flow: $719M (15.7% FCF margin)
- Net debt: $1.94B (down from $2.12B)
- Adjusted net debt/EBITDA: 1.4x
- Available liquidity: $1.2B+ under revolving credit facility (extended to 2030 at 85bps tighter spread)

The Altman Z-Score of 1.78 is mechanically driven by $7.1B in goodwill/intangibles on $12.1B total assets — a consequence of acquisition accounting, not operational distress. FCF covers all obligations comfortably.

**Key concern:** $53.7M in executive transition costs in FY2025 (former CEO arbitration) depressed GAAP earnings and signals governance friction. This is non-recurring but reflects poorly on the leadership transition.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term drivers (6-12 months):**
1. CC&T volume recovery: Q1 2026 showed +16% organic GTV growth as sellers who deferred in 2025 return. Management expects continued tailwinds from mega-project demand (data centers, infrastructure, reshoring).
2. Buyer fee optimization: 8% transactional buyer revenue growth in FY2025 from fee restructuring, with further optimization underway.
3. BigIron integration: Agricultural equipment auctions (U.S.) add a new vertical not reflected in 2026 guidance.

**Medium-term drivers (1-3 years):**
1. IAA international expansion: Suncorp partnership in Australia (65,000 units/year) validates the IAA model's exportability. If successful, opens Japan, South Korea, and broader European markets.
2. Total loss rate secular trend: Rising repair costs and parts inflation structurally push more vehicles into salvage, expanding IAA's addressable units.
3. Data monetization: Rouse analytics and vehicle scoring create embedded switching costs and incremental revenue streams.

**Growth constraints:**
- Estimate revisions are modestly positive but unremarkable (+1.1% over 90 days for FY26, +0.8% for FY27)
- GTV guidance of 6-9% growth is solid but not explosive
- CC&T is inherently cyclical — the 2025 downturn could repeat in any economic slowdown

Consensus FY26 EPS of $4.40 implies ~10% growth from adjusted FY25 EPS of $4.00. FY27 consensus at $4.90 implies further 11% growth. This is a steady mid-teens earnings compounder.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current multiples:**
- Trailing P/E: ~49x (on GAAP EPS of $2.04, depressed by non-recurring items)
- Forward P/E (FY26E): ~23.8x ($4.40 consensus)
- Forward P/E (FY27E): ~21.4x ($4.90 consensus)
- EV/EBITDA: ~15.5x (on ~$1.4B adjusted EBITDA)
- FCF yield: ~3.7% (on $719M FCF / $19.5B market cap)

**Peer comparison:**
The peer median P/E is 20.2x. RBA trades at an ~18% premium on forward earnings. Copart (CPRT), the closest pure comp in auto salvage, trades at 21.6x — below RBA despite being the dominant #1 player with superior margins. LDOS and SAIC trade at 11-12x but are government services companies with different characteristics.

**Premium justification:**
RBA's marketplace model warrants some premium over services peers due to:
- Higher capital efficiency (asset-light)
- Network effects (buyer base creates competitive moat)
- Counter-cyclical automotive segment (accidents are recession-resistant)
- Mid-teens earnings growth trajectory

However, the premium appears full at current levels. At 24x forward earnings on 10% growth, PEG is ~2.4x. Copart achieves similar growth rates with a more dominant market position and higher margins at a lower multiple. The analyst consensus target of $128 (median $130) implies 22-24% upside, but this requires execution on growth guidance and continued multiple support in a rising-rate environment.

The current price offers limited margin of safety. A reasonable fair value range is $100-115 based on 22-26x FY26 EPS, with the stock currently at the midpoint.

## Competitive Landscape

**Automotive salvage (duopoly):**
RBA/IAA is the clear #2 behind Copart. Copart has longer customer relationships, higher margins, and superior operational metrics. RBA's competitive response has focused on: (1) buyer fee optimization, (2) international buyer network expansion (UAE, Panama, Guatemala), and (3) technology investment in vehicle scoring and damage assessment. The relationship with top 3 insurance customers (~23% of revenue) is both a strength (steady volume) and a vulnerability (cancellable on 30-90 days notice).

**CC&T (fragmented leader):**
Ritchie Bros. is the global leader in a fragmented market that includes regional auctioneers, OEM dealers, and private brokers. Competitive advantages include: global buyer reach (170 countries), data/analytics moat (Rouse), omnichannel flexibility, and brand trust for high-value assets. The BigIron acquisition extends into agricultural equipment, and Smith Broughton adds Australian coverage.

**Competitive trajectory:** Stable to slightly improving. The international expansion of IAA (Australia, potentially more markets) and CC&T organic recovery suggest market share maintenance or modest gains. Copart's operational superiority in auto salvage limits upside in the highest-value segment.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Customer concentration (HIGH):** Three insurance customers represent ~23% of revenue, cancellable on 30-90 days notice. Loss of a major supplier would immediately crater automotive GTV.

2. **Cyclical CC&T exposure (MODERATE):** Construction equipment volumes declined materially in FY2025 and could do so again in a recession. The current recovery depends on sustained infrastructure spending and mega-project activity.

3. **CRA tax dispute (MODERATE):** C$79.1M assessed for 2010-2015 with additional exposure for 2016-2020. If RBA loses, the total liability could reach C$150-200M+ including interest. Company is contesting vigorously.

4. **Integration execution (MODERATE):** The IAA integration is progressing but the combined entity is still relatively early in realizing synergies. Leadership transition adds friction.

5. **Preferred stock dilution (LOW-MODERATE):** $482M in participating preferred shares absorb ~11% of earnings before common shareholders participate. This is an ongoing structural drag.

6. **Macroeconomic sensitivity:** In an uncertain macro environment, equipment asset values and transaction volumes face headwinds. The auto salvage business provides partial offset.

**Options Market Signal:**
Options activity is extremely thin (96 contracts across 3 expirations), precluding meaningful sentiment analysis. Implied moves of ±5.9% (1-month) and ±12.9% (6-month) are consistent with historical volatility of 25.8%, suggesting no unusual risk pricing. The absence of meaningful options activity means institutional hedging is likely done through other means or simply isn't elevated.

**Short interest** at 7.7% with 10 days to cover is elevated but not extreme, suggesting meaningful skepticism about the valuation premium without reaching squeeze-risk levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$130-140, +24-34%):**
- CC&T volume recovery sustains through FY26-27, driving mid-teens revenue growth
- BigIron integration adds 2-3% revenue upside not in current guidance
- IAA Australia success catalyzes further international expansion announcements
- Earnings reach $5.00+ by FY27; market re-rates to 26-28x on marketplace premium → $130-140
- Multiple catalysts: debt paydown → potential dividend increase, preferred buyback

**Bear Case (~$85-90, -14-19%):**
- CC&T volume recovery proves transient as macro deteriorates (recession scenario)
- Insurance customer loss or contract repricing compresses automotive margins
- CRA tax case decided against RBA, triggering C$150M+ liability
- Multiple compression → stock de-rates to 18-20x forward earnings
- At 20x $4.40 FY26E → $88; at 18x → $79

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $90-95, offering 22x FY26 EPS — creating a margin of safety adequate for the growth profile
2. FY26 estimate revisions accelerate to +10%+ within 30 days, signaling fundamental acceleration above current trajectory
3. New major insurance customer win or IAA international market announcement providing clear incremental growth visibility

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. CC&T GTV growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters, signaling cyclical downturn
2. Loss of a top-3 insurance customer (would be disclosed in 8-K)
3. Forward P/E expands above 30x without corresponding estimate acceleration — signaling speculative premium

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected early August 2026). The CC&T volume trajectory and guidance maintenance/raise will be the key signal.

## Conclusion

RB Global is a high-quality marketplace business with structural tailwinds in auto salvage (rising total loss rates), improving CC&T dynamics (volume recovery, BigIron expansion), and strong financial characteristics (asset-light model, $719M FCF, deleveraging balance sheet). The IAA integration is progressing, leadership appears stabilized, and recent results beat expectations.

The stock's challenge is its valuation at ~24x forward earnings for a ~10% grower in an uncertain macro environment. The premium to peers (including the structurally superior Copart) is difficult to justify without acceleration in estimate revisions. Near-term catalysts (CC&T recovery, BigIron contribution, Australia ramp) could drive that acceleration, but the current risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

HOLD with a positive lean. The business quality and growth trajectory merit continued ownership, but the valuation does not justify new capital deployment at current levels. A pullback toward $90-95 would create a more compelling entry point.
