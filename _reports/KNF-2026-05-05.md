## Executive Summary

Knife River Corporation is a vertically integrated construction materials and contracting services company with 1.3 billion tons of permitted aggregate reserves across 14 U.S. states, spun off from MDU Resources in May 2023. The business enjoys structural competitive advantages from transportation cost barriers and permitting moats, with approximately 81% of contracting revenue derived from public-sector projects. Several near-term catalysts exist: a record $1.03B backlog (+38% YoY), a potential doubling of North Dakota DOT bid lettings in 2026 ($745-810M vs. $345M in 2025), and Oregon's $4.3B transportation bill beginning to flow into projects.

However, the company's aggressive acquisition strategy ($612M deployed across 5 acquisitions in 2025 alone) has produced a leveraged balance sheet (Debt/EBITDA ~2.4x, debt up 75% to $1.18B), negative free cash flow (-$70M in 2025), and declining net income ($245M → $202M → $157M over 2023-2025). EPS estimates are falling across all time horizons (current year -5.7% over 90 days, next year -6.5%), and the stock trades at 32.7x trailing earnings — a premium multiple for a business experiencing margin compression in contracting services and energy services segments. With elevated long-term rates and construction materials exposed to both rate sensitivity (private construction) and cost pressures, the risk/reward at current levels is unfavorable for new capital deployment.

## Company Overview

Knife River operates through four segments: **West** (Alaska, California, Hawaii, Oregon, Washington), **Mountain** (Idaho, Montana, Wyoming), **Central** (Iowa, Minnesota, North Dakota, South Dakota, Texas), and **Energy Services** (liquid asphalt terminals across 8 states). The company controls 208 active aggregate sites, 135 ready-mix concrete plants, and 55 asphalt plants. Approximately 35% of aggregates are consumed internally, providing vertical integration benefits. Revenue in 2025 reached $3.15B (+9% YoY), though organic growth contributed only mid-single-digit pricing gains; the balance came from acquisitions.

The March 2025 Strata acquisition ($454M) added 24 ready-mix plants, 3 asphalt plants, and 30 years of aggregate reserves in North Dakota/Minnesota. Strata's margin trajectory improved rapidly from 1.6% net margin in its first 7 months to 9.8% in Q3 alone, suggesting acquisition accounting drag is dissipating. The company's EDGE (Efficiency, Discipline, Growth, Excellence) strategy targets margin expansion through pricing discipline and operational standardization.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability divergence:** Revenue grew 9% to $3.15B in 2025, but net income fell 22% to $157M. The divergence stems from three compounding factors: (1) D&A surging to $194M from acquired asset bases, (2) interest expense rising to $82M from the $500M Term Loan B, and (3) SGA climbing 15% on acquisition-related costs. EBITDA grew 7% to $484M, demonstrating the core business generates cash, but the capital structure now absorbs a larger share.

**Margin compression is broad-based:**
- Gross margin: 19.7% → 18.4% (contracting services fell 180bps to 11.2%)
- Operating margin: 10.9% → 9.1%
- Net margin: 7.0% → 5.0%
- Energy Services EBITDA margin collapsed: 26.7% (2023) → 21.8% (2024) → 16.2% (2025)

**Balance sheet stress:** Total debt rose from $675M to $1.18B. Variable-rate exposure of $779M at average ~5.9% creates sensitivity: each 100bps increase adds ~$7.8M annual interest expense. The $500M undrawn revolver provides liquidity but signals management intends to pursue further acquisitions.

**Cash flow deterioration:** Operating cash flow declined for the second consecutive year ($336M → $322M → $279M). CapEx surged to $348M (vs. $124M in 2023). Free cash flow was negative $70M in 2025, before $610M in acquisition spending. Cash on hand dropped from $281M to $74M.

**Q1 2026 seasonal loss:** The $79.2M Q1 loss compares to -$68.7M in Q1 2025 and -$47.6M in Q1 2024, reflecting the growing fixed cost base from acquisitions during the off-season.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term catalysts are genuine but partially priced:**
- North Dakota DOT budget: $745-810M in 2026 vs. $345M in 2025 — KNF is the dominant player in its home market
- Oregon transportation bill: $4.3B with ~50% to cities/counties, funding flowing in 2026
- IIJA: ~46% of formula funding still undistributed in KNF's 14 states
- Record backlog of $1.03B (+38% YoY)
- Central segment backlog surged 147%

**Growth concerns:**
- Backlog quality: management explicitly states margins are "lower" in the current backlog
- Estimate revisions are uniformly negative: next quarter -15.3% over 90 days, current year -5.7%, next year -6.5%
- Earnings surprise history is mixed: 2 beats, 2 misses over 4 quarters, with a -37.8% miss in Q2 2025
- Organic growth requires continued heavy CapEx ($300-365M guided for 2026)
- Strata integration execution remains a variable

**Aggregates pricing power:** The company achieved 9% YoY aggregate pricing ($18.99/ton) and 6% on ready-mix ($199/cy). These increases demonstrate the transportation and permitting moat. However, asphalt pricing was slightly negative, and contracting margins compressed, suggesting pricing power exists in materials but not in labor-intensive services.

## Valuation Assessment

At $90.30, KNF trades at:
- **P/E trailing:** 32.7x (on $2.76 EPS)
- **P/E forward (FY2026E):** 28.2x (on $3.21 consensus)
- **P/E next year:** 22.6x (on $4.00 consensus)
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~12.8x (on 2025 EBITDA of $484M; EV ≈ $5.1B market cap + $1.18B debt - $74M cash ≈ $6.2B)

**Peer context:** Vulcan Materials (VMC) trades at 36.3x and Martin Marietta (MLM) at 37.5x — but both are pure-play aggregates companies with higher margins, lower leverage, and more consistent earnings profiles. Eagle Materials (EXP) at 15.5x is the closest comp by market cap and offers a meaningful discount, though with a different product mix. CRH at 20.6x offers scale and diversification at a discount to VMC/MLM.

**Why the market might be wrong (bull case for valuation):** If Strata fully integrates and EBITDA reaches $540-560M in FY2026 (consensus implies ~$520M), EV/EBITDA compresses to ~11-12x — genuinely cheap vs. VMC/MLM. The North Dakota DOT surge is a temporary but large demand accelerant that could lift 2026-2027 earnings above current estimates.

**Why the market might be right (bear case for valuation):** At 32.7x trailing earnings with falling estimates, negative FCF, rising leverage, and margin compression in two segments, the current multiple reflects hope for acquisition-driven earnings recovery rather than demonstrated operational leverage. In an elevated long-rate environment, the appropriate discount rate for a mid-cap materials company with 2.4x leverage argues for lower multiples.

**Net assessment:** The stock is neither cheap enough to provide margin of safety nor expensive enough to warrant a short. EV/EBITDA at ~12.8x offers reasonable value relative to pure-play aggregates peers if execution delivers, but execution must be proven, not assumed.

## Competitive Landscape

KNF's competitive position is structurally sound and potentially improving:

**Moat characteristics:**
- 1.3 billion tons of permitted reserves create a 30+ year production life
- Aggregates are uneconomical to transport beyond ~50 miles by truck (regional monopoly characteristics)
- New aggregate permits require years of environmental review — limited new entry
- Vertical integration (mining → processing → paving) captures margin that competitors leave on the table

**Share trajectory:** The Strata acquisition meaningfully expanded KNF's position in North Dakota/Minnesota, where the DOT budget is surging. The company is gaining share through M&A rather than organic market-share theft — this is durable because acquired reserves cannot be replicated.

**Vulnerability:** Contracting services (37% of gross revenue) operates in a highly competitive, bid-driven market where margins are compressing. The 180bps contracting margin decline and management's acknowledgment of "lower expected margins" in backlog suggest this segment may be losing pricing power.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Rate environment:** $779M variable-rate debt at ~5.9%. Each 100bps = ~$7.8M additional annual expense. Higher rates also elevate refinancing risk for the broader construction value chain.

2. **Integration execution:** $612M in acquisitions in a single year. Strata excluded from Sarbanes-Oxley assessment in year one. Integration failure would compress margins further and burden the elevated debt load.

3. **Estimate momentum is negative:** All revision windows show declining estimates. Next quarter EPS estimate fell 15.3% over 90 days ($1.27 → $1.07). This typically precedes further downside.

4. **Cost inflation:** Rising diesel costs (a material input for aggregates mining, trucking, and paving operations) and labor cost pressures could compress margins further. A recession would pressure private construction volumes.

5. **IIJA cliff risk:** Federal funding authorized through 2026; reauthorization uncertainty beyond that horizon could reduce the public-sector pipeline.

6. **Energy Services structural decline:** EBITDA margin has fallen 1,050bps over two years (26.7% → 16.2%). If this segment continues deteriorating, it becomes a drag on consolidated margins.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-115):**
- Strata integration delivers $38-40M annual EBITDA contribution at normalized margins
- North Dakota DOT budget surge drives Central segment revenue well above 2025 levels
- Oregon $4.3B bill generates meaningful West segment recovery
- Aggregate pricing continues at 6-9% annual increases, expanding materials margins
- EBITDA reaches $540-560M in 2026, compressing EV/EBITDA to 11x
- Multiple re-rates toward 14-15x as leverage declines and earnings visibility improves

**Bear Case ($65-72):**
- Contracting margin compression continues as competitive bidding intensifies
- Elevated long rates raise interest expense further and depress private construction
- Acquisition integration underperforms (Strata Q3 margin improvement proves seasonal rather than structural)
- Recession materializes, delaying public-sector projects as state tax revenues decline
- FCF remains negative in 2026 as CapEx stays elevated and acquisitions continue
- Stock re-rates to 10x EV/EBITDA on $480M EBITDA = EV ~$4.8B = equity value ~$3.6B = ~$64/share

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 earnings demonstrate contracting margins stabilizing at ≥12% with explicit backlog margin improvement
2. Stock pulls back to $75-78 range (EV/EBITDA ~11x on current EBITDA) where margin of safety exists
3. Estimate revisions turn positive across current and next year consensus, demonstrating the acquisition-driven growth is translating to bottom-line results

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 miss on EBITDA with continued margin compression in contracting AND materials segments
2. Management announces additional large-scale acquisition (>$200M) before demonstrating positive FCF
3. Debt/EBITDA exceeds 3.0x or interest coverage falls below 3.5x

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (August 2026) — the first seasonally strong quarter that will reveal whether North Dakota DOT budget surge and Strata integration are translating to margin recovery.

## Conclusion

Knife River possesses a durable competitive franchise built on irreplaceable aggregate reserves and public-sector demand visibility. The IIJA tailwind, North Dakota budget surge, and Oregon transportation bill provide genuine multi-year growth vectors. However, the company is currently in the most capital-intensive and leveraged phase of its post-spinoff life, with negative FCF, declining estimates, and compressed margins in two segments.

The appropriate posture is HOLD — recognizing the structural quality of the franchise while acknowledging that the current price already reflects optimistic assumptions about acquisition integration and margin recovery that have yet to be demonstrated in reported earnings. The next clear decision point is Q2 2026 results, which will reveal whether the record backlog and North Dakota surge are generating profitable growth or margin-dilutive volume.
