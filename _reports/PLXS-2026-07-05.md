**Research Date: July 5, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Plexus Corp is a contract electronics manufacturing services (EMS) company that competes in a deliberately narrow niche: highly complex, low-to-mid-volume products in regulated end markets (aerospace/defense, healthcare/life sciences, industrial including semiconductor capital equipment). It avoids the commodity consumer-electronics battleground where Foxconn, Jabil, and Flex operate at scale. After two flattish years, the business inflected sharply in FY2026: Q2 (ended April 4, 2026) revenue grew 18.7% year-over-year to $1.16 billion, with H1 revenue up 14.2%, operating margin expanding to 5.3%, and EPS of $1.82 versus $1.41. Estimate revisions are positive across all horizons (current-year EPS +6.9% over 90 days, next-year +9.8%), and the company has beaten consensus in three of the last four quarters.

The problem is price. The stock has risen roughly 98% over twelve months to $275, carrying a trailing P/E near 40x and a forward P/E of ~29x on a business that generates only ~5% operating margins and is capital-intensive by design. Two fundamental issues temper the growth story. First, the FY2026 growth is cash-consumptive: free cash flow swung to negative $34.6 million in H1 as inventory built ~$145 million to fund program ramps, forcing revolver draws (average daily borrowing tripled to $104.8 million). This is the textbook EMS ramp pattern rather than a deterioration (cash-cycle days actually improved to 64), but reported EPS growth currently overstates cash generation. Second, the Malaysian tax holiday that contributed ~$1.56 per diluted share (roughly a quarter of FY2025 EPS) is being eroded in real time by the OECD global minimum tax; the effective tax rate is guided to roughly double, from ~8% in FY2025 to 16–18% in FY2026, a structural multi-year earnings headwind that partially offsets operating gains.

We rate PLXS a HOLD (growth_hold). The operating inflection is real and broad-based across all three segments and all three regions, which reduces the risk that the acceleration is a single-program mirage. But at ~40x trailing / ~29x forward earnings with negative near-term free cash flow, a tax headwind ramping through FY2026, and uniform insider selling into strength (no open-market buying while the stock doubled), the risk/reward at current levels is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would revisit constructively on a pullback that restores a growth-adjusted entry, or on confirmation that margins and free cash flow are converting through the ramp.

## Company Overview

Plexus, founded in 1979 and headquartered in Neenah, Wisconsin, employs roughly 20,000 people and generated $4.03 billion of revenue in FY2025. Its value proposition is full product-lifecycle EMS (design and development, supply-chain management, new product introduction (NPI), turnkey manufacturing where Plexus buys the materials, and sustaining/aftermarket services) for products where regulatory complexity, quality requirements, and production flexibility create switching costs.

End-market mix (FY2025): Industrial ~43%, Healthcare/Life Sciences ~40%, Aerospace/Defense ~17%. Geographic reporting segments: APAC ~59% of sales (concentrated in Penang, Malaysia, and the primary profit engine), AMER ~30%, EMEA ~11%. The company serves ~190 customers with no single customer exceeding 10% of sales; the top 10 represent ~49%. Plexus owns no proprietary products.

The moat is operational and relational rather than IP-based: FDA, AS9100, NADCAP, ITAR, ISO 13485 and related certifications, painful regulatory requalification for customers who would switch, and lifecycle integration. Management explicitly acknowledges that manufacturing processes carry "no significant proprietary protection" and that customers can in-source, which caps the moat's durability.

## Financial Analysis

Financial health is solid rather than elite. The Altman Z-Score of 3.0 sits at the edge of the safe zone; the current ratio is 1.58, ROE 11.9%, ROA 5.5%, and debt/equity 1.16. This is appropriate leverage for a working-capital-intensive contract manufacturer.

The FY2025-to-FY2026 story splits cleanly into two phases:

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 FY2026 | Q2 FY2026 YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.96B | $4.03B (+1.8%) | $2.23B (+14.2%) | +18.7% |
| Gross margin | 9.6% | 10.1% | 10.1% | 10.2% |
| Operating margin | 4.2% | 5.0% | 5.2% | 5.3% |
| Diluted EPS | $4.01 | $6.26 | $3.32 | $1.82 (vs $1.41) |
| ROIC (non-GAAP) | 11.8% | 14.6% | 13.8% | — |

FY2025 was an earnings story on a flat top line: revenue rose only 1.8% but net income jumped 54.7% to $172.9 million and EPS 56%, driven by +50bps gross margin (favorable customer mix plus prior restructuring benefits), a $15.6 million restructuring charge, and $17.3 million lower interest expense from deleveraging.

FY2026 became a revenue story. Q2 growth was broad-based (AMER +34.5%, Aerospace/Defense +23%, Healthcare +15%, Industrial +21%), driven overwhelmingly by production ramps of new products for existing customers and new customer wins, not pricing.

The cost of that growth is cash. FY2024 free cash flow of $341 million was flattered by a one-time inventory drawdown; it fell to $154 million in FY2025 as that tailwind faded, then turned negative as inventory built to support ramps (raw materials up to $1.19 billion). The key mitigant: cash-cycle days improved to 64 (days-in-inventory down to 120 from 132 YoY), meaning the inventory build is volume-driven, not an efficiency deterioration. The company is drawing its revolver to fund the ramp (total debt/finance leases ~$234 million at Q2 FY2026, up from $138 million at fiscal year-end).

Liquidity is adequate: cash of $303 million (83% offshore, concentrated in Malaysia/China), a $500 million revolver expandable to $750 million maturing June 2027, and $50 million of 4.22% notes due 2028. Covenants are comfortably met. Capital allocation is buyback-only (no dividend), with a $100 million 2026 program authorized in May 2025 and ~$42 million remaining. Average repurchase prices have climbed from ~$95 (2023) to ~$139 (2025) to ~$189 (Q2 FY2026): capital return is happening at progressively richer valuations rather than at accretive lows.

## Growth Analysis

The growth acceleration is genuine and diversified, spanning all three end markets and all three regions simultaneously, which lowers the probability that the surge depends on a single vulnerable program. The Industrial segment includes semiconductor capital equipment, which management and industry context associate with the broader capital-equipment demand environment; the filings do not attribute the ramp to a specific end-market vertical (growth is described generically as "production ramps of new products for existing customers" and "new customers").

Estimate revisions support the momentum thesis. Consensus current-year EPS has risen to $8.23, next-year EPS to $9.47, and next-quarter estimates 10.2%. These are meaningful but fall below the +30% velocity that would flag an unambiguous inflection-driven BUY signal; the revision pace is a positive supporting factor, not a standalone trigger. The earnings-surprise record (three beats of 6.8–12.3% and one narrow miss over four quarters) lends confidence to forward estimates.

Two structural offsets constrain the earnings translation. First, the tax step-up erodes the Malaysian tax holiday, putting roughly $1.56 per share of prior-year benefit at risk. Second, capex guidance was raised to $100–120 million (from ~$95 million) to fund ramps and a 27th facility (additional Penang capacity), which combines with the working-capital build to keep near-term free cash flow suppressed. Net: revenue and operating-income growth are strong, but per-share cash earnings growth is materially slower than the top line implies.

## Valuation Assessment

At $275, PLXS trades at ~40x trailing EPS and ~29x forward EPS ($8.23 current-year consensus, $9.47 next-year). For a ~5% operating-margin, capital-intensive contract manufacturer, this is a full growth multiple that prices in continued double-digit revenue growth and further margin expansion.

Peer comparison is complicated by the electronic-components cohort's distorted multiples (VSH at 5,518x, LFUS and ROG showing negative trailing P/Es from cyclical trough earnings, peer median P/E of 55.4x is not meaningful). More instructive is the direct high-mix EMS peer set (Celestica, Benchmark, Sanmina), where Plexus's forward multiple sits at a premium justified only if its current growth rate and margin trajectory persist.

Applying the cycle-adjusted lens: this is not a trough-earnings situation where trailing P/E overstates expensiveness. Margins are near cyclical highs (5.3% operating is the best in the recent data set), EPS is at record levels, and the tax rate is moving against the company. The forward P/E of 29x against a ~10–15% forward EPS growth rate (net of the tax headwind) yields a PEG near 2.0, which is not compelling. Analyst targets (mean $293, median $293, range $258–330) imply roughly 7% upside to the mean and 20% to the high, but the stock trades near consensus with only a narrow gap, and on a premium-multiple name analyst targets are not an independent floor; they reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price.

We score valuation 4.5. The multiple has run ahead of the cash-earnings profile, the tax headwind compresses the earnings power the multiple capitalizes, and no identifiable near-term catalyst suggests the market is under-pricing the stock at these levels. Consistent with the broader pattern that a "cheap" screen is anti-predictive, the inverse holds here: PLXS is not cheap on any adjusted metric, and the burden of proof for further re-rating sits with the bulls.

## Competitive Landscape

Plexus occupies a defensible middle ground in EMS. It sidesteps commodity high-volume competition and instead sells complexity, regulatory certification, and lifecycle integration to customers for whom requalification is costly. Its competitive position is stable-to-improving on trajectory: it is winning new programs across A&D, healthcare, and industrial simultaneously, and gross margin has expanded ~50bps year-over-year, evidence of favorable mix and operating discipline rather than commodity pricing pressure.

The moat's ceiling is honest. Management concedes manufacturing processes have no significant proprietary protection, and customers can in-source. The ~5% operating margin leaves limited cushion, and a reduction in higher-margin programs would hurt disproportionately. Direct high-mix peers (Celestica, Benchmark, Sanmina) compete for the same regulated, low-volume work. We score competitive position 6.3: a genuine niche advantage with improving share momentum, capped by the operational (not IP-based) nature of the moat and thin structural margins.

## Risk Assessment

**Malaysia concentration and tax-holiday erosion.** APAC generates ~59% of revenue and the majority of operating income and cash, concentrated in Penang. The Malaysian tax holiday runs to December 31, 2034 and cut taxes by $43.1 million in FY2025. The OECD global minimum tax is now actively eroding this benefit, with the effective rate rising to 13.9% in Q2. This is a confirmed, multi-year earnings headwind, not a theoretical risk. Penang facilities are also at/near sea level, adding physical/climate concentration risk.

**Working-capital and inventory risk during ramp.** Plexus finances customer inventory up front under turnkey arrangements. Customers give no firm long-term purchase commitments and can cancel or delay; "disengagements" have cost tens of millions in prior years. A mid-ramp customer disengagement would strand working capital and risk write-offs.

**Trade/China exposure.** Export controls on advanced semiconductors and chip-making equipment, plus tariff regimes, could restrict production/shipment in China and raise component costs, directly relevant given semiconductor-capital-equipment exposure in Industrial.

**Cyclicality understated by beta.** The 0.88 beta reflects the defensive healthcare/A&D mix and buyback-supported float, but semiconductor-cap-equipment exposure and demand volatility make the underlying business more cyclical than the beta implies.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-complacent. Near-term ATM IV of 41.1% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 40.9% (fair, spread 0.1%), with IV in the 5th percentile of its own chain and a balanced put/call skew of 2.6%. The term structure is in contango (near-term 41.1% below 6-month 46.3%), a normal/complacent shape, and ATM IV has drifted down 4.2 points since late May even as the price rose 2.8%. There is no elevated put demand or hedging pressure that would contradict the fundamental view; the market is not pricing an imminent binary event. Implied one-month move is ±6.7%. Short interest is low at 4.8% of float (3.1 days to cover), consistent with no crowded bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($330+).** The FY2026 inflection proves durable: broad-based program ramps across all three segments continue into FY2027, revenue sustains low-double-digit growth, and operating margin pushes toward 6%. New-program mix drives further gross-margin gains, the semiconductor-cap-equipment cycle extends on demand, and the working-capital build reverses as ramps mature, restoring free cash flow. Estimates keep rising (next-year $9.47 and climbing), and the market rewards a scaled, diversified high-mix EMS franchise with a premium multiple. At ~35x a $9.50+ forward number, the stock reaches the low-$330s.

**Bear Case (~$200, roughly -27%).** The tax headwind bites harder than modeled (effective rate at the top of the 16–18% range), materially compressing per-share earnings growth despite operating gains. A customer disengagement mid-ramp strands inventory and triggers write-offs, exposing the working-capital risk that negative FCF has been flagging. The semiconductor-cap-equipment cycle rolls over, growth decelerates back toward mid-single digits, and the ~40x trailing multiple compresses toward the mid-20s on a ~$8 EPS base, taking the stock toward $200.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $300 (12 months).** ~30x forward on a blended ~$9.50–10.00 FY2027 EPS trajectory, discounted for the tax-rate headwind and negative near-term FCF, yields roughly $300, about 9% above the current $275. This sits between the bear ($200) and bull ($330) scenarios and near the analyst mean of $293.

- **Bull case:** $330 (+20% from current)
- **Bear case:** $200 (−27% from current)
- **Upside to base:** +9%; **upside to bull:** +20%; **downside to bear:** −27%

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable at current levels, which drives the HOLD rather than BUY.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the FY2026 Q3 and Q4 earnings releases, where the market will test whether margins hold and free cash flow begins converting through the ramp, and to visibility on the FY2026 full-year effective tax rate.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Free cash flow turns positive in H2 FY2026 while revenue growth stays above 10% YoY (confirming the ramp is self-funding).
- A pullback toward the low-$220s / mid-$220s that restores a forward P/E below ~25x on rising estimates (growth-adjusted entry).
- Estimate revisions accelerate above +30% over 90 days combined with continued margin expansion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A customer disengagement or ramp reversal producing inventory write-offs and a further FCF deterioration.
- Effective tax rate guided above 18%, or evidence the Malaysian holiday is being clawed back faster than expected.
- Operating margin compresses back toward 4.5% on unfavorable mix.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next FY2026 Q3 earnings release.

## Conclusion

Plexus is a well-run, differentiated high-mix EMS franchise experiencing a genuine and broad-based revenue inflection, supported by rising consensus estimates and a credible beat history. Financial health is solid, the competitive niche is defensible, and the diversification across end markets and regions reduces single-program risk. Those are the reasons to hold through the inflection.

The reasons not to add at $275 are equally concrete: a ~40x trailing / ~29x forward multiple on a ~5% operating-margin business, near-term free cash flow that has gone negative as the ramp consumes working capital, a structural tax headwind that halves the earnings benefit the market is capitalizing, and uniform insider selling into strength with no offsetting open-market buying. The score trajectory has been gently improving (6.2 → 6.3), and this read holds that trend flat at 5.75: the operating fundamentals continue to strengthen, but the valuation has kept pace, leaving the risk/reward balanced. We rate PLXS a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale, and would turn constructive on a pullback that restores a growth-adjusted entry or on confirmation that cash conversion improves as the ramp matures.
