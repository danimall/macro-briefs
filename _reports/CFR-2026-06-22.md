**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cullen/Frost Bankers is a $52.7 billion-asset, Texas-only commercial bank built on one durable advantage: an exceptionally low-cost, sticky deposit franchise. Non-interest-bearing demand deposits run $14.2 billion, about 33% of total deposits, and total cost of deposits was 1.04% in Q1 2026. That funding base produced the central dynamic in this story: Frost expanded its taxable-equivalent net interest margin 14bps YoY to 3.74% while the broader regional-bank sector reported margin compression. As the Fed eases (funds target 3.50–3.75% at quarter-end), Frost's deposit costs are repricing down faster than its asset yields, and the bank is redeploying ~$6B+ of excess Fed cash into higher-yielding loans and Texas tax-exempt securities. Q1 2026 net income to common was $169.3M ($2.65 EPS), up 13.4% YoY, with ROA at 1.32% and ROE at 15.15%.

The balance sheet is exceptionally strong. CET1 sits at 14.07%, reserve coverage of non-accruals is 396%, non-accruals are just 0.32% of loans, and annualized net charge-offs are 0.11%. The company grows organically through de novo branch expansion (the "Dallas 2.0" push, 27 of a planned wave of new centers opened) rather than acquisitions, avoiding integration and goodwill risk while accepting near-term expense drag. Management has raised the dividend to $1.00/quarter (37.8% payout) and authorized a fresh $300M buyback, repurchasing $70M at ~$138 in Q1.

The constraint is price. At ~$145.66, CFR trades near its 52-week high ($148.97) at roughly 14.7x trailing EPS and ~13.2x next-year consensus ($10.62/$11.01), a meaningful premium to the regional-bank peer median of ~10.5x. The franchise quality justifies a premium, and estimate revisions are modestly positive (+3.0% current-year, +3.8% next-year over 90 days), but the gentle NII guide (3–5% for 2026), the AOCI overhang (-$923.5M), the CRE concentration (47% of loans), and a construction/land past-due spike to 1.79% combine to cap the upside from here. We maintain HOLD with a growth bias; this is a high-quality compounder to accumulate on weakness rather than chase at the top of its range.

## Company Overview

Frost Bank, the principal subsidiary, operates exclusively across Texas — the four major metros (Austin, Dallas/Ft. Worth, Houston, San Antonio) plus the Gulf Coast, Permian Basin, and Rio Grande Valley. The business is a pure-play bet on the Texas economy, with the loan portfolio and deposit base almost entirely intra-state. It runs in two segments: Banking (~96% of net income) covering commercial and consumer lending, treasury management, and capital markets/public finance; and Frost Wealth Advisors, with $50.4 billion in trust assets ($26.4B managed, $24.1B custody).

The defining feature is the deposit franchise. Frost declined to chase high-fee overdraft economics and built a service-led, "flight-to-safety" brand that gained share after both 2008 and the 2023 SVB episode. The result is a funding base that lets the bank win on cost rather than rate, which is the engine behind its margin resilience.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability is strong and accelerating. Q1 2026 net income available to common was $169.3M ($2.65 diluted EPS), up 13.4% from $149.3M ($2.30) a year earlier.

- **Net interest income** of $438.5M, up 5.4% YoY, with taxable-equivalent NIM expanding 14bps to 3.74% from 3.60%. The mechanism: in a falling-rate environment, Frost's deposit costs reprice down faster than asset yields while the bank shifts excess Fed cash into loans and munis. Average loans grew $1.2B (+5.9%) YoY.
- **Returns:** ROA 1.32% (up from 1.19%), ROE 15.15%.
- **Non-interest income** of $136.3M, up 9.9% YoY, led by trust/investment fees (+11.7%) and deposit service charges (+12.4%).
- **Expenses:** non-interest expense rose 5.1% to $365.7M on organic-expansion staffing, cloud services (+$1.8M), advertising (+$1.9M), and a $2.4M jump in deposit fraud losses. The de novo growth model front-loads cost; the efficiency drag is a deliberate trade.

A quiet earnings lever sits in the tax-exempt securities book, almost entirely Texas issuers, ~70% PSF-guaranteed or pre-refunded. Tax-exempt securities grew $608M and their tax-equivalent yield rose 35bps to 4.73%, pushing the effective tax rate down to 15.5%. This boosts after-tax returns more than headline pre-tax metrics suggest.

**Capital and liquidity are exceptionally strong.** CET1 14.07%, Total Capital 15.89%, well above well-capitalized minimums. Liquidity is abundant: ~$6.4B at the Fed, $7.1B of unused FHLB capacity, $12.8B in pledgeable securities. The earnings-beat track record is consistent, with four straight beats (Q1 2026 +6.2%, Q3 2025 +11.0%), supporting confidence in forward consensus.

**The AOCI overhang is the key watch item.** Accumulated other comprehensive loss widened to -$923.5M (from -$843.0M at YE2025) as long rates rose. The AFS book carries ~$1.2B in gross unrealized losses, with $8.7B underwater for 12+ months. Frost opted out of including AOCI in regulatory capital, so this does not impair regulatory ratios, but it suppresses GAAP tangible book and represents real economic interest-rate risk. The investor's view hinges on whether these are pull-to-par (management's position, supported by no intent to sell, massive surplus liquidity, and sticky deposits) or a latent impairment if funding ever forced sales. Given the liquidity profile, the forced-sale scenario is remote.

Standard distress screens (Altman Z, many "health" ratios) are mechanically uninformative for banks and should be disregarded; CET1, ROA, reserve coverage, and NIM trend are the correct lenses, and all read favorably.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three components, each modest but reliable:

1. **NII growth of 3–5% for 2026** (management guide), driven by loan growth and higher earning assets, with continued deposit-cost repricing as the swing factor. The bank is now only slightly asset-sensitive (a 200bp drop cuts NII ~2.7%), so further Fed cuts are a mild headwind to asset yields offset by lower funding costs.
2. **Organic Texas expansion.** The "Dallas 2.0" program (27 of a planned new wave of centers opened) follows successful Houston (50+ branches) and original Dallas programs. De novo branches generate near-term expense drag in exchange for long-term low-cost deposit and loan share in the fastest-growing large-state economy. This avoids integration and goodwill risk that plague M&A-led peers.
3. **Fee income momentum.** Trust/investment fees (+11.7%) and deposit service charges (+12.4%) are growing double digits, diversifying away from rate-dependent NII.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year EPS revised +3.0% and next-year +3.8% over 90 days, with next-quarter +2.4%. This is positive earnings momentum, but it falls well short of the velocity (>30% in 90 days) that would warrant treating analyst targets as floors. CFR is a steady mid-single-digit compounder; the growth-prospects score reflects durability rather than acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$145.66, CFR trades at ~14.7x trailing EPS ($9.92) and ~13.2x next-year consensus ($11.01), near its 52-week high of $148.97. The regional-bank peer set is materially cheaper: SSB 10.5x, WAL 9.6x, ONB 12.7x, COLB 12.2x, ZION 10.3x, for a peer median ~10.5x. Frost commands roughly a 40% P/E premium to the peer median on a trailing basis.

The premium is defensible on franchise quality: Frost's NIM expanded while peers compressed, its deposit base is among the best in the sector, its credit book is pristine, and its capital is strong. A bank that bucks sector-wide margin compression and grows organically without goodwill warrants a higher multiple. The dividend yield (~2.8%) sits near the peer median (~2.5%) with a comfortable 37.8% payout.

What it does not offer is asymmetric upside. The stock is up ~17.9% over the trailing window and trades at the top of its range. Analyst targets bracket the current price (low $130, mean ~$150, median $155, high $165), and the sentiment trend is flagged "degrading" with three sell ratings persisting in the mix. With a 3–5% NII guide and mid-single-digit EPS growth, the math points to a base case only modestly above current levels. The valuation score is below the franchise quality because the price has already captured much of the quality premium; a "cheap" multiple here is not on offer, and the premium multiple is fairly earned rather than mispriced in either direction.

We score valuation 5.5: neither a value trap nor an asymmetric bargain. The improvement from 5.5 to 6.5 in the prior report reflected rising estimates, but the stock has since climbed back to its high, re-compressing the forward upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Frost competes against money-center banks (JPMorgan, BofA, Wells), super-regionals, and other Texas banks. Its differentiation is service-led relationship banking anchored by a low-cost deposit base, allowing it to compete on funding cost rather than promotional rates. The competitive position is improving on trajectory: while the FDIC reports sector-wide margin compression and rising unrealized losses, Frost expanded NIM and continues to take share organically in the highest-growth large-state economy.

The de novo expansion model is distinctive. Most regionals grow via acquisition, carrying integration and goodwill risk; Frost grows by building branches, deepening its brand and minimizing goodwill on the balance sheet. The trade-off is the visible expense drag in salaries, occupancy, and advertising. Trust/wealth ($50.4B assets) adds a fee-based, relationship-deepening leg. The competitive-position score of 7.5 reflects a widening funding moat and share gains the market only partially prices.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Texas and sector concentration.** Essentially all loans and deposits are Texan. Any Texas-specific shock (energy, real estate, in-migration reversal) hits the whole book. The bank explicitly tracks the Texas Leading Index.
2. **Energy exposure.** The largest industry concentration at 5.4% of loans ($1.2B), up 10.4% QoQ, plus $1.1B unfunded commitments. The 10-Q references US-Iran conflict and oil-price volatility in its downside-scenario reserve overlay.
3. **CRE/office.** CRE is $10.6B (47% of loans). Management runs substantial qualitative office-building and minimum-reserve overlays (of the $286M ACL, $160.9M / 56% is qualitative overlay, including ~$100.9M of CRE overlays). The clearest deterioration signal: **construction & land past-dues spiked to 1.79%** (up ~$38M QoQ). This is the one soft spot in an otherwise clean book and is the single most important credit metric to monitor at the next print.
4. **Interest-rate / AOCI risk.** Regulatory capital is insulated by the AOCI opt-out, but tangible book is suppressed. Mitigated by surplus liquidity and sticky deposits; a forced sale is the tail risk.
5. **Deposit competition / fintech** threatening the low-cost funding advantage over time.
6. **Interchange regulation.** A proposed Fed rule could cut debit interchange ~30%; litigation is unresolved.
7. **Third-party cyber incident (new).** On April 22, 2026, vendor Sefas Innovation notified Frost of an incident likely involving Frost customer data. Frost's own systems were unaffected and it deems the impact not reasonably likely to be material; the investigation is ongoing.

The Q1 10-Q reports no material change to risk factors versus the 2025 10-K. The risk score of 5.5 reflects that concentration and CRE risks are largely known and priced, but the construction/land past-due deterioration and the energy/oil tail are live, monitorable, and not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign with a mild bearish tilt. Near-term ATM IV is 26.6% versus 22.1% trailing historical vol, modestly rich, with the market pricing a ±5.5% move into July expiry. The term structure is in contango (26.6% near, 29.1% at six months), a normal/complacent structure. Put/call skew is elevated at 6.6% near-term (easing to negative at six months), and the put/call OI ratio is very low at 0.03. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float, 13,547 shares). The picture: no informed bearish positioning of consequence, modest near-term hedging demand, and richer IV further out consistent with macro (rate-path) uncertainty rather than company-specific concern. Nothing here challenges the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$165, +13%)
The Fed eases ~25–50bp through 2026-27, deposit costs continue repricing down faster than assets, and NIM expansion persists into 2027. Loan growth runs at the high end on Dallas/Houston de novo maturation, fee income compounds double digits, and the construction/land past-due tick proves idiosyncratic. EPS reaches the high end of consensus (~$11+) with the premium multiple holding at ~15x, supporting the $165 analyst high.

### Bear Case (~$120-130, -11% to -18%)
A Texas-specific downturn — an energy shock, or CRE/office stress migrating from construction & land into the broader book — forces reserve builds and charge-offs above the current pristine run-rate. Simultaneously, deeper Fed cuts compress NIM faster than deposit repricing can offset. The premium multiple compresses toward the peer median (~10.5x), taking the stock toward $120-130, near the analyst low of $130 and the 52-week low of $119.

### Base Case (~$152, +4%)
NII grows 3–5% as guided, EPS lands near consensus ~$10.62-11.01, credit stays clean ex the watched construction line, and the premium multiple holds at ~13.5-14x. That supports a base case modestly above current levels, roughly the analyst mean of ~$150. The total return including the ~2.8% dividend is attractive for a defensive holding but does not clear the bar for a fresh BUY at the top of the range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings (late July).**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$130s or below (toward the analyst low and prior repurchase zone ~$138), restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above the current ~3-4% pace alongside evidence the NIM-expansion-in-a-cutting-cycle dynamic extends into 2027.
- Construction & land past-dues normalizing back below ~1.0% at the next print, removing the one credit soft spot.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Construction/land and broader CRE/office past-dues continuing to climb (e.g., construction & land >3%, or non-accruals breaching ~0.75% of loans), signaling the qualitative overlays are anticipating real losses.
- A verified, sustained energy shock materially impairing the energy book and triggering reserve builds.
- NIM reversing into compression as Fed cuts outrun deposit repricing, with NII guidance cut below 3%.

**Reassessment point:** Q2 2026 earnings, with focus on the construction/land past-due trend, NIM trajectory, and any update on the Sefas cyber investigation.

## Conclusion

Cullen/Frost is among the highest-quality regional banks in the country: a fortress balance sheet, a deposit franchise that let it expand margin while the sector compressed, a pristine credit book, and a disciplined organic-growth model in the best large-state economy in the US. The score trajectory (6.4 → 6.9) correctly captured improving fundamentals, and the latest data confirms the franchise strength. The financial-health (8.0) and competitive-position (7.5) scores reflect genuine, durable advantages.

The binding constraint is price. Near a 52-week high at a roughly 40% trailing premium to peers, the market has already paid for the quality. With a 3–5% NII guide, mid-single-digit EPS growth, and a base case only modestly above current levels, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, and the construction/land past-due spike plus the energy/oil tail are live reasons not to chase. We maintain HOLD with a growth bias: a name to accumulate on weakness toward the low-$130s, not to add at the top of its range. For investors already holding, the franchise quality and ~2.8% yield support continued ownership.
