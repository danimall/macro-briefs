**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Helix is an offshore energy services company in the middle of a transformational corporate event. On April 22, 2026 it agreed to an all-stock merger with Hornbeck Offshore Services in which Helix shareholders will own approximately 45% of a combined company renamed "Hornbeck Offshore Services, Inc." The exchange ratio is fixed (10.27167 Helix shares per Hornbeck share), Helix reincorporates from Minnesota to Delaware, and the deal is expected to close in the second half of 2026, subject to a Helix shareholder vote and HSR antitrust clearance. The buyback was suspended concurrently. Every forward-looking judgment about HLX now depends on the economics of this combination, and those economics cannot be assessed until Hornbeck's financials and the joint Form S-4 are filed.

The standalone business softened into the deal. FY2025 revenue fell 5% to $1,291M, net income dropped about 45% to $30.8M, and Well Intervention gross margin collapsed from 13% to 6% as the Seawell sat warm-stacked for the full year and three flagship vessels absorbed 131 combined docking days. Q1 2026, the seasonally weakest quarter, printed a net loss of $(13.4)M / $(0.09) per share. Against this, the balance sheet is a genuine asset: $501M of cash, a net-cash position of roughly $197M (about 14% of market cap), free cash flow of $120M for FY2025, and $59M in Q1 2026.

The right posture is HOLD with low conviction. A more confident bearish call is not supportable: the stock trades near 52-week highs, up 42% over the trailing year, sits below the entire Street target range (mean $12.5, median $13.5 versus $9.54), carries net cash, and has near-unanimous bullish coverage (three strong-buy, one buy, one hold, zero sell). A more confident bullish call is equally unsupportable, because the merger converts HLX holders into a 45% minority of an OSV-weighted entity whose pro-forma leverage and accretion I cannot evaluate without the S-4. The combination of downside protection against real operating deterioration and an unresolved binary merger outcome warrants holding and waiting for the disclosure that resolves it.

## Company Overview

Helix provides specialty services across the offshore well life cycle through four segments (FY2025 revenue):

- **Well Intervention (~$729M, 57% of revenue):** the core franchise, operating seven purpose-built intervention vessels (Q4000, Q5000, Q7000, Seawell, Well Enhancer, plus two chartered Brazil vessels on long-term Petrobras contracts) and 12 intervention systems. Helix positions these vessels as a lower-cost, more mobile alternative to drilling rigs for intervention and plug-and-abandonment work, and runs a Subsea Services Alliance with SLB (jointly developed 15K IRS and ROAM; Amendment 2 to the alliance was filed February 13, 2026).
- **Robotics (~$323M, +9%):** trenching, seabed clearance, and ROV services. This is the renewables-exposed segment — roughly 49% of Robotics revenue came from offshore wind (cable burial, boulder/UXO clearance), concentrated in Europe and Asia Pacific.
- **Shallow Water Abandonment (~$200M, +7%):** Gulf shelf decommissioning through Helix Alliance (acquired July 2022); turned profitable in 2025.
- **Production Facilities (~$73M, -18%):** the HP I floating production vessel, the Helix Fast Response System, and mature properties (Thunder Hawk, Droshky).

The company is mid-tier in a fragmented, competitive market alongside Oceaneering, TechnipFMC, Expro, DOF Subsea, and Fugro. It employs 2,212 people, and year-end backlog was $1.3B, with 82% concentrated in six customers (Shell, Subsea 7, Petrobras, Talos, NKT, CNR). Shell alone was 18% of 2025 revenue and Petrobras 10%.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was a year of margin compression on a strong balance sheet. Gross profit fell 28% to $159M, taking gross margin from 16% to 12%. Adjusted EBITDA declined to $272M from $303M. The decline was concentrated in Well Intervention, where gross margin fell from 13% to 6% and gross profit dropped about $70M — driven by the Seawell being warm-stacked for all of 2025 (versus 86% utilization in 2024) and 131 combined docking days on the Q4000/Q5000/Q7000. The prior year also included $14M of contract-cancellation fees that did not recur, and 2025 absorbed an $18.1M non-cash impairment of the Thunder Hawk field. Partly offsetting, Shallow Water Abandonment swung to a positive gross profit and Robotics grew on trenching demand.

The balance sheet is the standout. Net debt was -$137M at year-end 2025, improving to -$197M at Q1 2026 (cash $501M against $304M total debt). Current ratio is 2.75, debt/equity 0.66, and liquidity ran from $553M to $612M. Debt consists of the $300M 2029 Notes at a 9.75% coupon — expensive, and a natural refinancing candidate — plus a $9.9M MARAD note (4.93%, matures February 2027). Altman Z of 2.58 sits in the grey zone, driven by thin EBIT/assets rather than leverage or liquidity; Piotroski is 5/9.

Q1 2026, structurally Helix's weakest quarter, deteriorated to a net loss. Revenue rose 4% to $288M, but gross profit fell 68% to $8.8M. Adjusted EBITDA was $32M against $52M. The shortfall came from a Production Facilities gross loss of $(7.5)M (Thunder Hawk workover costs) and a seasonal Shallow Water Abandonment loss of $(8.9)M. Free cash flow was nonetheless healthy on accounts-receivable collections. An $11.6M FX translation loss (GBP/BRL) flowed through OCI, a recurring swing factor given North Sea and Brazil exposure.

Capital allocation has historically combined low net debt with buybacks ($30M repurchased in 2025; $128.4M remained authorized). The board suspended the buyback on April 22, 2026 concurrent with the merger, reallocating capital priorities toward the combination.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis the growth picture is weak. Revenue declined in FY2025, the largest segment saw its margins halve, and Q1 2026 produced a net loss. Backlog of $1.2B at Q1 2026 is moderately reassuring but the near-term portion attributable to 2026 declined from $694M to $551M within the quarter. Estimate revisions are mixed rather than directional: current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 25% over 90 days and next-year estimates 19.2%, but next-quarter and current-year revisions were roughly flat (+1.2% and -4.6%), which the consensus tracker characterizes as stable. There is no estimate-revision pattern of the magnitude that signals a fundamental turn.

The secular case management makes rests on three pillars it frames as the energy transition: production maximization of existing wells (intervention), decommissioning of end-of-life fields (a regulation-driven backlog as Gulf properties revert after bankruptcies and U.K. abandonment enforcement tightens), and offshore wind support (robotics/trenching). New 2025 awards are consistent with that case — a four-year NKT trenching deal, ~300 days of Seaway 7 renewables trenching, a three-year ExxonMobil Gulf decommissioning framework, and multi-year North Sea riserless P&A on up to 34 wells. But two of the three pillars face active policy headwinds: the U.K. Energy Profits Levy was raised to 38% and extended to March 2030, depressing North Sea customer capex, and the January 2025 Presidential Memorandum withdrew OCS wind leasing, hurting U.S. renewables (international wind remains robust).

The dominant growth variable is now the merger itself, which shifts the combined asset mix toward offshore supply vessels — a more spot-exposed, more commoditized sub-sector than Helix's intervention/robotics franchise. Whether that combination is accretive to per-share value cannot be determined without Hornbeck's financials and the S-4.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of 98x is meaningless at a cyclical trough and should be disregarded. More informative is enterprise value relative to cash earnings. Backing out net cash, enterprise value is about $1.24B against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, or roughly 4.6x EV/EBITDA — undemanding for an offshore services name with a net-cash balance sheet. The peer set trades at a median P/E of 37.3x (RES 75.6x, INVX 37.3x, CLB 22.1x, VTOL 11.0x), though peer comparability is weak given the heterogeneity of the group and HLX's trough-earnings denominator.

Street targets sit well above the current price: mean $12.5 (about 31% above the $9.54 current price), median $13.5 (about 42% above and above the 52-week high of $10.75), high $14, low $9. These targets are partly framed by the merger and the implied combined-company value, so I would not treat them as independent fundamental anchors, but the breadth and direction of the coverage are inconsistent with a structural-impairment view.

The net-cash position provides a meaningful floor: roughly 14% of the current market cap is cash net of debt, and the fixed-ratio all-stock structure means HLX holders are not being cashed out at a depressed price — they retain 45% of a combined entity that includes their own cash. The valuation is therefore better described as floored and reasonable on cash earnings than as asymmetrically attractive, because declining standalone earnings, a Q1 loss, and an unquantifiable merger outcome cap the upside case. That balance argues for a middling valuation score rather than a low one.

## Competitive Landscape

Helix occupies a defensible but mid-tier position. In Gulf shelf decommissioning it claims to be the only provider of all facets of the work, which is a genuine niche advantage as decommissioning obligations accumulate. Its intervention vessels compete against drilling rigs that, when over-supplied, pressure Helix's day rates directly. Robotics is gaining on international offshore-wind trenching demand, offsetting the U.S. wind-policy reversal. The SLB Subsea Services Alliance provides a technology and channel partnership that smaller competitors lack.

The trajectory is stable-to-soft rather than clearly improving or deteriorating. Well Intervention spot exposure (North Sea, Q4000, Q7000) faces weak rates and rig overhang, and the U.K. fiscal regime is suppressing a key customer base. The Hornbeck combination tilts the combined company toward OSVs, a sub-sector with lower differentiation and higher spot sensitivity, which on the margin dilutes the specialty-services character that has been Helix's competitive identity. The renaming to "Hornbeck" and the Delaware reincorporation suggest Hornbeck's management and strategy will lead the combined entity, which adds uncertainty about how the combined competitive position evolves.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is now merger-related and partly unquantifiable. HLX holders become the ~45% minority in a company in which former Hornbeck shareholders hold ~55%; integration, HSR clearance, and the shareholder vote all carry execution risk; merger-related securities/derivative litigation is flagged as a new risk factor in the Q1 10-Q; and a termination fee is payable under certain scenarios. The central open question is whether Helix's clean, net-cash balance sheet effectively subsidizes a more leveraged OSV operator (Hornbeck went through Chapter 11 in 2020). That question cannot be answered without Hornbeck's financials and the S-4, and it is the single most important diligence item before this stock can be re-rated in either direction.

Standalone risks are concentrated and real: six customers represent 82–83% of backlog (Shell 18%), much of which is cancelable; spot-market and rig-overhang exposure on intervention assets; the U.K. Energy Profits Levy and U.S. wind ban pressuring two of three strategic pillars; roughly $747M of fixed charter/operating-lease commitments that must be paid regardless of utilization (operating leverage cuts both ways); ~$118M of undiscounted decommissioning liabilities (partly offset by a $30M Marathon receivable); and recurring FX swings on GBP/BRL.

A brief oil-price tailwind appeared in Q1 2026 as crude spiked on the Strait of Hormuz disruption, but offshore services revenue is backlog-driven and lags spot prices, and that tailwind would reverse if the chokepoint reopens. The net-cash position and the fixed-ratio deal anchor materially limit downside, which is why the risk profile, while genuinely uncertain, is not skewed sharply to the downside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 39.3% against one-year historical volatility of 47.6%, leaving IV cheap (a -8.2 percentage point spread) and in the 14th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in contango (39.3% near-term versus 47.1% at six months), a complacent shape. Near-term put/call skew is elevated at 9.6%, indicating some demand for downside protection into the next month, but open interest is overwhelmingly call-tilted (P/C OI 0.08), and the six-month skew is actually negative (-2.3%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.4%. The net read is a market pricing low near-term volatility and modest directional conviction, consistent with a stock waiting on a binary merger disclosure rather than one pricing imminent distress. Cheap IV makes long-dated optionality a reasonable way to express the binary if one wished to, but it does not point to a directional edge.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The merger proves accretive. The combined Hornbeck/Helix entity scales the OSV and intervention/robotics platform, Helix's ~$500M cash and net-cash position de-risk the combined balance sheet and enable refinancing of the 9.75% 2029 Notes, and synergies plus a stronger backlog re-rate the stock toward the Street's $13.5 median / $14 high targets. Decommissioning demand and international offshore-wind trenching provide durable mid-cycle volume, and oil-price strength supports intervention day rates. In this case HLX is worth $13–14, roughly 36–47% above the current price.

### Bear Case
The S-4 reveals that Helix is contributing a disproportionate share of the combined balance-sheet quality for a 45% economic interest, Hornbeck's leverage and lower returns dilute combined per-share value, integration disappoints, and standalone Well Intervention margins remain compressed amid rig overhang and U.K. fiscal drag. The net-cash floor and merger-arb anchor nonetheless cap realistic downside; absent the deal collapsing, the stock likely holds near its net-cash-supported range. Bear case is approximately $7, around -27%.

The bull and bear cases hinge on the same undisclosed information, which is why a confident directional call is not warranted today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The decisive event is the filing of the joint Form S-4 / proxy ahead of the Helix shareholder vote, expected in advance of a 2H 2026 close. The next earnings report and any merger-update 8-Ks are interim checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The S-4 shows the combined entity is accretive to per-share value — pro-forma net leverage at or below Helix's standalone level and credible synergy/free-cash-flow accretion — confirming HLX's cash is not subsidizing a weaker balance sheet.
- Standalone Well Intervention gross margin recovers toward double digits over the next two quarters (Seawell reactivation and reduced docking days), with backlog stabilizing or rising.
- Evidence of a 9.75% 2029 Notes refinancing at a materially lower coupon post-close.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The S-4 discloses pro-forma leverage and returns that demonstrate clear value transfer from HLX holders to Hornbeck holders, with no offsetting synergy case.
- The merger terminates with a termination fee payable and standalone fundamentals continuing to deteriorate (further margin compression, sustained operating losses beyond Q1 seasonality), pushing the stock below its 50- and 200-day moving averages.
- Backlog erosion accelerates (the 2026-attributable portion falling materially below the current $551M) or a top-three customer is lost.

## Conclusion

HLX is a net-cash offshore services operator whose forward value is now governed by a fixed-ratio all-stock merger into Hornbeck that I cannot evaluate without Hornbeck's financials and the forthcoming S-4. The standalone business is genuinely softening, but the downside is anchored by net cash, a fixed-ratio deal structure that retains HLX holders' interest in their own cash, and near-unanimous bullish sell-side coverage. The stock trades near 52-week highs, which forecloses a credible short and is inconsistent with a structural-impairment call.

Real operating deterioration is offset by tangible downside protection and an outcome that cannot yet be scored. The appropriate action is to hold existing positions and wait for the S-4 to resolve the pro-forma economics, treating that filing as the trigger to move to BUY or AVOID. Issuing a confident directional rating ahead of the disclosure that determines the answer would be premature. Overall score 5.0; recommendation HOLD (uncertain).
