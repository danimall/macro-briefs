## Executive Summary

Arcosa is executing a well-timed portfolio transformation, divesting its cyclical barge business ($450M, closed April 2026) and pivoting wind tower manufacturing capacity toward utility structures — a business where record backlog ($558M, +28% from year-end) and record margins (21.1% EBITDA in Q1 2026) confirm secular demand from grid modernization. Combined with a durable aggregates franchise in Texas and the NY-NJ MSA, the company's continuing operations generated 18.0% EBITDA margins in Q1 2026 and guided full-year adjusted EBITDA of $545-585M, a $22.5M midpoint raise from prior guidance.

The fundamental concern is that the stock at $126.47 (~30x trailing, 29.7x consensus FY2026 EPS of $4.26) already prices in the operational improvement, while EPS estimates are declining meaningfully: FY2026 consensus has fallen 12% in 30 days and 10.5% over 90 days. The wind tower business faces a structural policy cliff (AMP credits expire post-2027 under OBBBA, PTC eligibility narrows), GE Vernova concentration at 12.2% of FY2025 revenue creates near-term earnings volatility, and $1.5B in debt from the Stavola acquisition carries $109M in annual interest expense. The stock has rallied 58% from its 52-week low. The growth story is real, but the valuation demands patience for a better entry point.

## Company Overview

Arcosa, Inc. is a Dallas-based infrastructure products company operating two continuing segments after the April 2026 barge divestiture: **Construction Products** (~50% of revenue) and **Engineered Structures** (~45%). Construction Products includes natural aggregates from a quarry network spanning Texas, the Southeast, and the NY-NJ MSA (via the $1.2B Stavola acquisition, October 2024), plus specialty materials, asphalt, and recycled aggregates. Engineered Structures manufactures utility poles (steel and concrete), wind towers, and traffic/lighting structures.

The company was spun off in 2018 and has since pursued an aggressive acquisition strategy, completing over $1.5B in deals including Stavola and Ameron ($180M, April 2024). The strategic direction is clear: reduce cyclicality, concentrate on infrastructure-driven end markets, and exploit the secular tailwinds from U.S. grid modernization and construction spending.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 consolidated revenue reached $2.88B (+12.2% YoY), driven by the Stavola contribution ($219M inorganic) and organic growth in utility structures. On a continuing operations basis, Q1 2026 revenue was $571.7M (+4% YoY), with adjusted EBITDA of $102.9M (+10% YoY) at an 18.0% margin (+100bps YoY).

Operating income grew 73% in FY2025 to $341.9M, with operating margin expanding from 7.7% to 11.9%. SG&A declined from 12.5% to 10.7% of revenue as acquisition-related transaction costs ($44.4M reduction) fell away and the fixed cost base scaled. Engineered Structures hit a record 21.1% EBITDA margin in Q1 2026, driven by operating leverage on utility structure volumes.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

FY2025 operating cash flow was $341M, with $176M in free cash flow (6.1% FCF margin). Working capital consumed $155M due to acquisition integration normalization. Q1 2026 FCF from continuing operations was $21.2M, a significant improvement from ($49.1M) in the prior year quarter.

The balance sheet carries $1.5B in debt: $400M of 4.375% notes (2029), $600M of 6.875% notes (2032), and ~$542M on a refinanced term loan (SOFR + 200bps). Cash stood at $215M at year-end 2025. Pro forma for the barge sale ($370M net after-tax proceeds, $83M already applied to term loan prepayment), net debt/EBITDA is 1.9x — below the company's target range and providing clear capacity for additional bolt-on acquisitions.

Interest expense of $108.8M in FY2025 (up $38M YoY) is the cost of the transformation. At ~19% of adjusted EBITDA, this is manageable but not trivial. The blended cost of debt is approximately 5.5-6.0%.

### Earnings Quality

The 4-for-4 earnings beat record reflects an average surprise of +58% over four quarters, though this partly reflects low initial bars during the integration period. More importantly, the effective tax rate dropped to 13.6% in FY2025 from 27.9% due to AMP (Advanced Manufacturing Production) tax credits from wind tower manufacturing. This benefit is at risk post-2027 under OBBBA, which would mechanically increase the effective rate by ~14 percentage points and compress EPS by roughly $0.75-1.00, all else equal.

## Growth Analysis

### Secular Drivers

Two structural tailwinds support mid-to-high single-digit organic growth:

1. **Grid modernization:** AI-driven electricity demand, grid hardening, and electrification are driving unprecedented utility capex. Arcosa's utility structure backlog hit a record $558M in Q1 2026 (+28% from year-end), with management converting two idled wind tower facilities to utility structure production for H2 2026.

2. **Construction materials demand:** The IIJA provides approximately $350B for highway programs through 2026, with reauthorization or extension expected. Arcosa's aggregates network in Texas and the NY-NJ MSA benefits from population growth and public infrastructure spending. Q1 2026 showed 7% unit profitability improvement on only 2% pricing, confirming operational leverage.

### Headwinds

The wind tower business represents a meaningful near-term drag. Backlog declined 19% YoY to $628M at year-end 2025 ($600M by Q1 2026), and OBBBA's termination of AMP credits and PTC eligibility creates a structural cliff after 2027. Management's facility conversion strategy mitigates this, but the transition creates a 12-18 month earnings air pocket as wind tower volumes decline before utility structure capacity fully ramps.

### Estimate Revisions Signal Caution

Current FY2026 EPS consensus is $4.26, down from $4.83 just 30 days ago (-11.8%) and from $4.76 ninety days ago (-10.5%). FY2027 estimates fell 9.5% in 30 days to $5.07. The current quarter estimate declined 36.5% over 90 days to $0.36. These are significant downward revisions across every time horizon, and they accelerated in the most recent 30-day window. This is the clearest quantitative risk signal in the data: positive operational results are not translating to positive estimate momentum, likely because analysts are pricing in the wind tower cliff and tariff uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

At $126.47, ACA trades at approximately 29.7x FY2026E EPS ($4.26) and 24.9x FY2027E EPS ($5.07). Against construction and engineering peers with a median P/E of 33.9x, the stock appears modestly discounted on a headline basis. However, the peer comparison requires adjustment: Arcosa's 4-12% revenue growth is below the peer median, and its 21% EBITDA margin guidance, while improving, is not yet at the levels of pure-play aggregates companies like Vulcan or Martin Marietta.

On an EV/EBITDA basis, using the $565M midpoint of FY2026 guidance and enterprise value of ~$7.4B (market cap $6.1B + net debt ~$1.3B pro forma), the stock trades at 13.1x — reasonable for an infrastructure industrial but not cheap given the wind tower overhang and negative estimate revisions.

The $128.4 mean analyst price target (range $115-$135) implies only 1.5% upside from the current price. With the stock having rallied 58% from its 52-week low of $77.90, much of the transformation premium appears captured.

A fair value range based on 12-14x FY2026 EBITDA produces $108-$128. The current price sits at the upper end. A pullback to $105-110 (approximately 11-12x) would create a more favorable risk-reward entry.

## Competitive Landscape

Arcosa's competitive position is improving on both fronts. In aggregates, the Stavola acquisition gave it access to the supply-constrained NY-NJ MSA, where permitting barriers create near-permanent competitive advantages. Texas remains a strong organic market. Aggregates earn local geographic monopoly economics — the product is too heavy and low-value to ship economically over long distances, so proximity to demand is the moat.

In engineered structures, the pivot from wind towers to utility structures is strategically correct. Grid modernization demand is less policy-dependent than wind (utilities must invest regardless of energy policy) and the customer base is more diversified. The record utility backlog and margin expansion validate early execution. The wind tower-to-utility conversion of two facilities is operationally feasible because the manufacturing processes share common capabilities.

The risk is that Arcosa is a mid-cap competing against larger, better-capitalized aggregates operators (Vulcan, Martin Marietta) and specialized utility pole manufacturers (Valmont). Scale advantages in procurement and logistics could limit margin expansion over time.

## Risk Assessment

### Wind Tower Policy Cliff (High Impact, Medium Probability)

OBBBA terminates AMP tax credits after 2027. Loss of these credits could add 10-14pp to the effective tax rate. Simultaneously, PTC eligibility for new wind projects narrows significantly. Wind tower backlog is already declining. The conversion strategy is the mitigant, but timing and execution risk remain.

### Negative Estimate Revisions (High Impact, Occurring Now)

If estimates continue falling, the stock's current multiple becomes increasingly stretched. The Q2 2026 estimate ($1.27) has declined 8.3% in 30 days, suggesting analysts see further pressure.

### Leverage and Interest Burden

While deleveraging is on track (1.9x pro forma), $109M in annual interest expense consumes 19% of adjusted EBITDA. In a rising-rate environment, refinancing the 2029 maturities could occur at higher rates than the current 4.375% coupon.

### Tariff and Trade Uncertainty

Steel and aluminum tariffs, USMCA renegotiation in July 2026, and DOC wind tower import investigations create cost and competitive uncertainty across both segments. Management flagged these as material risks.

### GE Vernova Concentration

GE Vernova's share of revenue declines as wind tower volumes shrink, but the transition period creates vulnerability to order timing disruptions.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.6% versus 28.9% long-term creates a pronounced backwardation term structure, suggesting the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew of 17.6% indicates elevated put demand, though the very low OI P/C ratio of 0.04 suggests limited institutional hedging activity. The implied move of ±5.9% on the nearest expiry is consistent with post-earnings positioning decay. Overall, options are pricing modestly elevated near-term risk but nothing extreme, consistent with a stock digesting a large post-beat rally.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($140-145, +11-15%)

Grid modernization accelerates, driving utility structure backlog above $600M and supporting 20%+ segment EBITDA margins through FY2027. Aggregates pricing improves to 4-5% in H2 2026 as IIJA spending ramps. Wind tower conversion executes on schedule, and remaining wind backlog provides earnings bridge. Successful bolt-on acquisitions (Florida aggregates deal is the template) at attractive multiples extend the growth runway. Deleveraging to 1.5x enables return of capital. At 14x FY2026 EBITDA, the stock reaches $140.

### Bear Case ($95-105, -17-25%)

Wind tower demand collapse arrives faster than utility structure ramp, creating a 2-3 quarter earnings gap in H2 2027. OBBBA tax credit loss adds $40-50M to annual tax expense. Estimate revisions continue downward, with FY2026 EPS ultimately settling at $3.80-4.00. Construction spending softens as GDP decelerates. At 11x FY2026 EBITDA, the stock retreats to $95-100. Tariff disruption on steel inputs compresses engineered structures margins by 100-200bps.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $105-110 (~11-12x FY2026 EBITDA), providing 15%+ upside to base case target of $128-132.
2. FY2026 EPS estimates stabilize or reverse higher for two consecutive 30-day periods, indicating the market has absorbed wind tower and tariff impacts.
3. Utility structure backlog exceeds $600M with visibility on H2 2026 conversion completion, confirming the wind-to-utility transition is tracking.

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS estimates fall below $3.80, which would push the stock to 33x+ earnings — unsustainable for an industrial without acceleration.
2. Utility structure backlog declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters, invalidating the grid modernization demand thesis.
3. Net Debt/EBITDA rises above 2.5x, indicating the deleveraging plan is stalling or acquisition discipline is weakening.

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August) will provide the next data point on utility structure conversion progress, aggregates pricing, and wind tower backlog trajectory.

## Conclusion

Arcosa's strategic transformation is well-conceived and showing early results. The pivot from wind towers to utility structures, the Stavola aggregates acquisition, and the barge divestiture collectively reposition the company toward durable, infrastructure-driven end markets with meaningful competitive advantages.

The specific concern that prevents a BUY recommendation at $126.47 is the convergence of three quantitative signals: (1) FY2026 EPS estimates have declined 12% in the last 30 days across all forecast periods, indicating analyst consensus is actively deteriorating despite the operational beat — this suggests the market is re-pricing wind tower cliff risk and tariff uncertainty; (2) at 29.7x FY2026E EPS, the stock trades at the upper bound of its fair value range (12-14x EBITDA yields $108-$128), offering only 1.5% upside to the $128 mean analyst target; and (3) the AMP tax credit cliff post-2027 creates a quantifiable EPS headwind of $0.75-1.00 that is difficult to model with precision but directionally compresses forward earnings power. A 15% pullback to $105-110 would provide the margin of safety appropriate for these risks and warrant an upgrade to BUY.
