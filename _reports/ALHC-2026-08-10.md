## Executive Summary

Alignment Healthcare is a pure-play Medicare Advantage insurer that has done the hard part of the growth-insurer playbook: it scaled premium revenue ~47% annually for two years, drove SG&A from 16.9% of revenue in FY2023 to 9.8% in H1 2026, and converted a $127.8M FY2023 operating loss into $57.6M of H1 2026 operating income. Membership reached 294,100 at June 30, 2026, up 31.5% year over year, across 45 markets in five states, with 100% of members in plans rated 4.0 Stars or better for the 2026 rating year. On the guided FY2026 numbers — revenue $5,195-5,225M and adjusted EBITDA $145-163M — the stock trades at roughly 0.49x revenue and 16.6x adjusted EBITDA crediting all consolidated cash, or 0.60x and 20.2x crediting only the $135.4M of cash actually held outside the regulated insurance subsidiaries. That is not an expensive multiple for a business compounding revenue at 32%.

The reason the stock is 43.9% below its July 7 high of $25.12, and why we are not upgrading it, is that the earnings side of the growth story pauses at exactly the moment the risk side thickens. Backing H1 actuals out of raised full-year guidance produces implied second-half revenue growth of ~31.5% against adjusted EBITDA growth of ~9.6% (versus +60.4% in H1) and an implied second-half medical benefits ratio near 88.2% against 87.42% in 2H 2025. Guidance midpoints therefore contract the adjusted gross profit margin ~25bps for the full year while expanding the EBITDA margin only ~18bps, entirely on SG&A leverage. Management calls this deliberate pre-AEP reinvestment, which is consistent with the company's own disclosed seasonality. The alternative reading is that a book 31.5% larger is heavily weighted to first-year members whose acuity is not yet priced or risk-scored; this is equally consistent with the data and cannot be distinguished from outside. The market took the second reading, marking the stock down 14.8% on a guidance raise.

Layered on that are two binaries that public disclosure cannot size. CMS notified the company on June 25, 2025 that its California HMO — the plan covering the majority of an ~84%-California member base — was selected for a contract-level RADV audit for payment year 2019, with no accrual recorded; whether the exposure is a sampling-level clawback or a contract-wide extrapolation depends on a federal appeal of the September 2025 Texas decision vacating the extrapolation rule. And on July 7, 2026 a former executive officer sued alleging he had reported "concerns of accounting irregularities," externally reported as the misclassification of operating expenses into capital expenditures to inflate adjusted EBITDA — the primary metric in the executive incentive plan. The Audit Committee, outside counsel and a national accounting firm reviewed the concerns and cleared the accounting, and Deloitte issued unqualified opinions on both the FY2025 statements and internal control, with IBNR rather than software capitalization as the sole critical audit matter. We do not treat the allegation as established. We do treat it as a live overhang: nine plaintiffs' firms announced investigations between July 8 and August 7, 2026, and no securities class action had been filed as of the latest news reviewed. HOLD, classified as uncertain.

## Company Overview

Alignment is a single-segment Medicare Advantage HMO/PPO operator headquartered in Orange, California. Earned premiums were $3,911.7M of $3,948.7M FY2025 revenue (99.1%), essentially all monthly capitated payments from CMS. Third-party capitation has been wound down to $5.1M from $132.8M in FY2023, and the company exited the CMS ACO REACH model effective December 31, 2025 (FY2025 ACO revenue $1.2M). Revenue is therefore a single government payer, priced annually through the bid process, with per-member economics set by county benchmarks, risk scores and Star Ratings.

Three elements define the operating model. AVA, a proprietary data platform, stratifies members into four clinical tiers and routes interventions; the 10-K discloses no patent protection on AVA. "Care Anywhere" is an employed clinical workforce delivering in-home and virtual care to the highest-acuity tier, the mechanism by which management argues it runs a lower MBR than peers on identical CMS premium. Star Ratings drive both the ~5% benchmark bonus and the share of rebate dollars retained, which funds benefit richness — the company's California HMO has held ≥4 Stars for nine consecutive years.

Footprint: 45 markets across California (22), North Carolina (16), Arizona (3), Nevada (2) and Texas (2). The FY2025 10-K puts ~84% of members in California; no updated split as of June 30, 2026 was located, and faster non-California growth does not by itself establish diversification. Management frames the runway as ~8.5M Medicare-eligible lives in its markets at ~6% MA share versus 10-30% in its most mature markets — a company estimate, not an audited figure, but directionally consistent with a 31.5% membership growth rate against national carriers that have been trimming county footprints.

This structure is ordinary for an insurance holding company and is not a solvency issue on the current trajectory. It is a valuation-denominator issue, and it is why we carry two enterprise values rather than one.

**Reserve adequacy — the first crack.** The development record is genuinely clean: accident-year 2024 incurred claims were re-estimated down 2.8% after a year, 2023 down 2.4% cumulatively, with ex-PAD favorable development of $20.2M/$7.1M/$11.0M in FY2025/24/23. Against that, Q2 2026 disclosed $6.5M of unfavorable prior-year development (H1 still net favorable $1.8M), attributed to claims-recovery timing. At 0.5% of quarterly revenue this is inside actuarial noise; it matters because it lands in the same quarter that guidance conceded second-half MBR deterioration. Those two mutually reinforcing datapoints raise the priority of Q3 development disclosure. Separately, the notes carry an allowance against substantially all of the IPA risk-sharing receivable — delegated provider deficits are being written down essentially in full, which means the "shared risk" model transfers less downside than it appears to.

**Quality of earnings.** Equity compensation was $62.1M in FY2025 (56% of adjusted EBITDA) and $32.2M in H1 2026 (30%), excluded entirely from the adjusted measure. Unrecognized compensation stepped up to $95.6M over 1.89 years from $46.5M over 1.51 years. Holding H1 run rates for equity comp, D&A and interest, guidance-midpoint second-half adjusted EBITDA of $48.0M implies roughly -$9M of second-half GAAP pre-tax income and FY2026 diluted EPS near $0.17 on ~216M shares — approximately 84x. The point is not the precise number but the gap: 17-20x on adjusted EBITDA and ~84x on GAAP describe the same business, and the difference is mostly stock issued to employees.

The supplied profile shows trailing EPS of -$0.00366 alongside a 72.7x P/E; those cannot both hold, and neither is a usable valuation anchor. We rely on guided adjusted EBITDA and the derived GAAP sensitivity.

## Growth Analysis

The membership bridge is mechanical and largely locked for 2026: 236,300 at December 31, 2025 → 275,300 at January 1, 2026 (a 16.5% AEP step-up) → 294,100 at June 30. Year-end guidance of 298,000-301,000 implies only 4,000-7,000 further net adds, consistent with MA's January-1 enrollment concentration. That means 2027 revenue is decided by two events in the next five months: the 2027 Star Ratings (expected October 2026) and the AEP running October 15 to December 7, 2026.

Three structural growth constraints deserve weight. First, the dual-eligible channel — among the highest revenue-per-member segments — is being closed in the core state: CMS D-SNP look-alike thresholds tighten from 80% to 70% then 60% in 2026, and California DHCS restricts new D-SNP contracts to Medi-Cal-affiliated plans and bars unaffiliated existing D-SNPs from enrolling new members from CY2025. Alignment is not a Medi-Cal plan. Second, Part D redesign under the IRA moved the CMS catastrophic share from 80% to 20% and introduces a $2,100 out-of-pocket cap plus negotiated Maximum Fair Price drugs in 2026; a bid-year mispricing runs for a full plan year with no repricing. Third, Star Ratings at the top of the scale have compressed sharply — 18 MA-PD contracts earned 5 Stars for 2026 versus 57 for 2023 — and CMS has proposed removing the Health Equity Index reward for 2027, a measure whose loss disproportionately affects a plan with above-average minority and socioeconomically disadvantaged membership.

Against those, the share-gain thesis is credible. National carriers have been trimming MA footprints and benefit richness to repair margins; a 4+ Star plan pressing benefit richness into that vacuum should keep taking share, and 6% share in markets where mature vintages reach 10-30% supports several more years of double-digit membership growth. The earnings question is whether those members arrive at an MBR that clears the cost of acquiring them. Guidance's implied ~88.2% second-half MBR reads as management already conceding that they do not, at least initially.

The track record supports confidence in near-term delivery: four consecutive EPS beats, including $0.17 versus $0.13 in Q2 and $0.05 versus $0.01 in Q1, and H1 results that beat the high end of prior guidance.

## Valuation Assessment

At $14.085 and 207.4M shares, equity value is $2,921.7M.

- Crediting all cash: EV = $2,921.7 + $330 − $701.7 = **$2,550M** → **0.49x** FY2026E revenue and **16.6x** guided adjusted EBITDA ($154M mid); **17.0x** TTM adjusted EBITDA of $149.8M.
- Crediting only unregulated cash ($135.4M): EV = **$3,116M** → **0.60x** revenue and **20.2x** adjusted EBITDA.

The honest number sits between them. The regulated cash is not worthless — it supports the required capital of a book growing 31% and would be released if growth stopped — but it is not available for debt service or buybacks, and only $72.9M was distributable without regulatory approval at year-end. Call it ~18x guided adjusted EBITDA on an EV of roughly $2.8B.

Peer P/E comparison is not usable here. The Health Care Services cohort supplied (PRVA 102.4x, CRVL 27.8x, PGNY 31.0x, ADUS 21.0x, MD 12.9x, median 27.8x) mixes asset-light services businesses with an insurer, and ALHC's GAAP earnings are too thin to support a meaningful multiple. The relevant comparison is against MA insurers on EV/revenue and EV/EBITDA, where 0.49-0.60x revenue is a growth premium to mature national carriers and a discount to where high-growth insurtech traded in 2021.

Building forward: assume FY2027 revenue of ~$6.2B (~19% growth, consistent with an AEP cohort roughly in line with 2026's) and adjusted EBITDA of $205-220M (3.3-3.5% margin, resuming modest expansion once first-year members season). At 14x EV/EBITDA that is an EV of ~$2.9-3.1B; adding back a conservatively credited $250M of net cash and dividing by ~218M shares gives roughly $14.5-15.5 — essentially the current price. At 16x the same estimates produce $16.5-17.5. Reaching the mean analyst target of $24.08 requires either FY2027 adjusted EBITDA near $280M (a 4.5% margin, well above anything delivered) or a multiple above 20x on $220M.

That arithmetic is why we do not treat the analyst distribution — low $16.00, high $30.00, mean $24.08, median $25.00 — as a floor. On a stock at ~84x GAAP where the adjusted multiple depends on excluding stock compensation equal to a third to half of the measure, targets are not independent confirmation. The sell-side has been cutting into the reset while maintaining ratings (JPMorgan $26→$22 Buy, TD Cowen $25→$21 Buy, Raymond James Strong Buy→Outperform, all per secondary sourcing), which reads as multiple reduction rather than thesis change and means the published mean is stale.

One unpriced positive exists on the other side. Alignment reportedly filed suit against CMS in D.C. federal court on July 10, 2026, arguing that CMS's upward-only, partial application of the Clover Star Ratings ruling deprives it of more than $50 million — roughly 32% of FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance. We could not locate this in the Q2 10-Q legal note, which is unsurprising since gain contingencies are generally not recognized until realized, and the figure is a litigant's assertion contingent on CMS's July 21 Eleventh Circuit appeal. It belongs in the bull case, not the base case.

Valuation score 5.3: the 43.9% drawdown has removed froth, and 0.49-0.60x revenue on 32% growth is not demanding, but on stock-compensation-inclusive economics the company is near breakeven, second-half earnings growth is single-digit, and two of the largest swing factors (RADV, 2027 Stars) cannot be sized. There is no clean margin of safety at $14.09.

## Competitive Landscape

Alignment competes against UnitedHealth, Humana, CVS/Aetna and Elevance nationally, and against SCAN, Blue Shield of California and Kaiser in its core market. Its advantages are regulatory and operational rather than structural: a Star rating that funds richer benefits per premium dollar, and a clinical model management argues lowers MBR on retained high-acuity members. Both are replicable by better-capitalized competitors, and AVA carries no patent protection.

The trajectory is favorable on share — 31.5% membership growth in a market where nationals are selectively retrenching — and unfavorable on moat durability. The Star Ratings litigation cycle makes this explicit: multiple methodology changes, plans suing with mixed results, Clover's May 27, 2026 win invalidating twenty measures, CMS recalculating only upward and only under half the ruling, then Elevance ($115M claim), SCAN and Alignment suing. If courts eventually force a broad upward recalculation, Alignment's absolute rating improves while its relative advantage narrows, which is why its complaint reportedly cites competitive harm alongside the dollar amount.

The scale disadvantage is real: no owned PBM, no provider assets at scale, no diversified services segment to smooth a bad medical-cost year. Pharmacy rebate receivables of $139.8M at June 30 (from $179.2M) are a third-party-dependent asset, and Part D economics are set by rules the company cannot influence. Score 5.9 — improving share against a thin and partly regulatory moat.

## Risk Assessment

**RADV (the largest unquantifiable item).** CMS selected the California HMO — covering the majority of members — for a contract-level PY2019 RADV audit on June 25, 2025; records were still being assembled at June 30, 2026, and no accrual is recorded. The 2023 rule authorizing extrapolation without a fee-for-service adjuster was vacated in Texas on September 25, 2025 on procedural grounds and is on appeal. Without extrapolation the exposure is sampling-level; with it reinstated, contract-wide. CMS has stated it intends to audit all eligible MA contracts annually and clear the PY2018-2024 backlog. This risk is not sizeable from public disclosure and is not accrued.

**Whistleblower suit and derivative litigation risk.** The 10-Q confirms a former executive officer sued on July 7, 2026 alleging retaliation after reporting "concerns of accounting irregularities," with no accrual. Externally reported detail alleges opex-to-capex misclassification that inflated FY2024 adjusted EBITDA — a year in which reported adjusted EBITDA was only $1.3M, small enough that "millions" of misclassification would be arithmetically consequential. Counterweights: the Audit Committee retained outside counsel and a national accounting firm and concluded the concerns were unfounded; Deloitte's unqualified ICFR opinion flagged IBNR, not capitalization, as the sole critical audit matter; and the capex pattern ($36.0M/$41.4M/$26.8M FY2023-25) is equally consistent with project completion, since $13.1M of construction-in-progress converted to placed-in-service software in 2025. We cannot distinguish the two explanations and do not infer motive. The tradeable risk is procedural: nine plaintiffs' firms announced investigations between July 8 and August 7 with no class action filed yet, and a filing would extend the overhang and legal spend regardless of merit.

**California concentration.** ~84% of members per the FY2025 10-K, meaning one regulator (DMHC), one provider market and one Star rating drive most of the P&L. No mid-2026 update was located.

**Second-half execution.** Guidance already concedes ~79bps of MBR deterioration; the Q3 print is the first check on whether that was conservatism or the leading edge. The $6.5M of unfavorable Q2 prior-year development is the corroborating datapoint to watch.

**Regulatory/structural.** FCA per-claim penalties rose to $14,308-$28,619 effective July 3, 2025 — for a plan submitting millions of risk-adjustment records, per-claim exposure is the tail that matters. California and Oregon strengthened corporate-practice-of-medicine statutes, and the 10-K concedes that equity-transfer restrictions used to control affiliated physician entities may be unenforceable in several states, a structural risk to VIE consolidation and to the Care Anywhere delivery arm. The CMS broker compensation rule was vacated in August 2025, and DOJ has an active FCA interest in MA marketing — material for a company whose growth depends on broker-driven AEP enrollment.

**Dilution.** The economically correct if-converted count on the $330M notes is 20.59M shares ($330M ÷ $16.04), a 9.9% increase to share count and 9.0% ownership dilution to 91.0%. (The 10-K antidilutive table shows 25.72M, which equals $330M ÷ $12.83 — the November 14, 2024 reference price rather than the conversion price; the discrepancy is immaterial to valuation but means published fully diluted counts may be overstated.) On top sit 19.4M unvested awards (~9.4% of shares), a December 22, 2025 registration of an additional 27,915,561 plan shares (~13.5% of the count), and a 2021 Plan evergreen of up to 4% annually. The CEO's February 2026 award of 1,000,000 market-condition PSUs requires 30-day VWAP hurdles of $33.75/$44.00/$55.25 and service through February 24, 2029 — the first is 140% above the current price, so it belongs only in a bull scenario.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 67.6% sits 5.7 points above 61.9% one-year realized, so the near-dated market is pricing event risk at a modest premium rather than distress; the 41st percentile IV reading within the chain and a contango term structure (67.6% near versus 71.0% at six months) point the uncertainty at events beyond the next month — the October Star Ratings release, the AEP, and the late-October Q3 print, all captured by the November 20 tenor pricing a ±29.8% move. The nearest expiry implies ±10.5%. Open-interest put/call of 2.21 is the heaviest structural reading in the chain, while the volume-based skew is labeled call-heavy at -6.5%: standing downside protection combined with more recent upside speculation, which describes a name 43.9% off its high and 7.9% above its 52-week low. Short interest of 12.3% of float at 4.7 days to cover is elevated but not squeeze-grade, and matches the fundamental debate rather than resolving it. The options structure argues that the risk is concentrated in the fourth quarter rather than the next few weeks.

**Management Quality.** This is the weakest dimension. Across 2026, eight officers sold in the open market against one purchase. CEO John Kao's reported holdings fell from ~2,354,641 shares (March 18) to 1,088,766 (July 10), a ~54% reduction in under four months at $16.85-$21.40, with the final tranche executing two days after the whistleblower story broke; he then adopted a new 10b5-1 plan on May 27 for up to 800,000 additional shares, effective September 10, 2026 through February 26, 2027, so programmatic selling resumes into the Star Ratings and AEP window. President Dawn Maroney, EVP Konowiecki (25,000 shares weekly at $21-$24 in June/July, after a 122,807-share grant on June 3), the CFO, CMO, CLO, CIO, CHRO and COO-MSO all sold. The sole open-market purchase was Mark Kent's 14,848 shares at $13.31 on June 2, one day before receiving an 87,719-share grant.

Much of this is mechanical: 11.3M RSU shares and 6.7M PSUs vested in FY2025, 10b5-1 sales execute without discretion, and this is a 2021 IPO where insiders remain concentrated. On whether the selling is clustered C-suite activity alongside deteriorating fundamentals, the answer here is partially yes: the fundamentals did deteriorate at the margin (2H guidance reset, first unfavorable development), the selling is broad rather than isolated, and the CEO's reduction exceeds 50% of holdings. The absence of any meaningful open-market purchase at $13-$14 after a 38% drawdown is the more informative fact.

Governance adds to the concern. The primary incentive metric is adjusted EBITDA, a measure that excludes ~$62M of annual equity compensation and that capitalizing operating costs would mechanically increase — the structural fact that makes the whistleblower allegation coherent enough to warrant the investigation it received. A partial offset: the 2023-granted PSUs weighted "Adjusted EBITDA less capital expenditures" at 20%, which penalizes exactly that shift; 2024 and 2025 PSUs are 50% revenue / 50% adjusted EBITDA. Compensation actually paid to the CEO was $52.29M in FY2025 against a $11.28M summary-table total, and the five-year actually-paid series (-$2.7M in 2023, $52.3M in 2025) is highly procyclical. Four senior employment agreements were signed or amended within six weeks in May-June 2026 alongside a new COO and a CFO transition, and the available evidence points to the CEO having assumed the chairmanship while the former chairman moved into a paid executive role — an inference from the related-party note, 2026 Form 4 titles and the May 12, 2026 8-K (Items 5.03/7.01), which was not read. If correct, it combines the CEO and Chair roles and converts a board leader into an executive who is also a steady seller. Score 3.2.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($22, +56%).** The second-half MBR guidance proves conservative, as H1 already did relative to prior guidance, and Q3 delivers toward the high end of the $20-30M adjusted EBITDA range with clean prior-year development. October Star Ratings hold 4+ across the book, the AEP produces another 15%+ January-1 membership step-up, and the CMS suit or the Eleventh Circuit appeal converts some portion of the reported >$50M claim into 2027 revenue. First-year members season, MBR reverts toward 87%, FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches $250-270M on ~$6.3B of revenue, and the market pays 17-18x — with the whistleblower suit resolved or settled quietly and no class action filed. National carriers continue retrenching, extending the share-gain runway.

**Bear case ($9.50, -33%).** The second-half MBR deterioration is member-mix rather than reinvestment, Q3 shows further unfavorable development, and FY2026 lands near the $145M low end. The 2027 Star Ratings slip on Health Equity Index removal or measure recalibration, cutting 2027 rebate dollars and forcing benefit reductions into the AEP just as growth was supposed to compound. A securities class action is filed, adding legal spend and an unquantified accrual. RADV extrapolation is reinstated on appeal, putting a contract-wide clawback on the California HMO with no reserve. Adjusted EBITDA stalls near $150M, the multiple compresses to 12x, and the equity — with stock compensation running at a third of adjusted EBITDA and the parent holding no cash — trades toward $9-10.

**Base case ($16.50, +17%).** Guidance holds at the midpoint, FY2027 revenue reaches ~$6.2B with adjusted EBITDA of $205-220M as first-year members partially season, the market pays ~15-16x on an EV of roughly $3.0-3.3B, and net cash of ~$250M credited conservatively yields $16-17 per share on ~218M shares. RADV and the whistleblower matter remain open but unresolved, capping the multiple.

Probability-weighted at 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear: (0.25 × 56%) + (0.45 × 17%) + (0.30 × -33%) ≈ **+12%**. Positive, but with a 33% left tail, no insider support, and two unquantifiable binaries, this does not clear the ACCUMULATE bar.

## Conclusion

Alignment has built a genuine 32%-growth Medicare Advantage franchise and demonstrated the SG&A leverage that a scaling insurer needs, moving from a $128M operating loss two years ago to $57.6M of half-year operating income and ~$150M of trailing adjusted EBITDA. At 0.49-0.60x FY2026E revenue and 17-20x guided adjusted EBITDA, the price is not demanding for that growth rate, and the 43.9% decline from the July high has removed the multiple that made the stock vulnerable.

What keeps this a HOLD is the conjunction of a pause in earnings growth with a thickening of unquantifiable risk. Guidance itself embeds ~9.6% second-half adjusted EBITDA growth on ~31% revenue growth and ~79bps of MBR deterioration; adjusted EBITDA excludes stock compensation equal to 30-56% of the measure, so on an economic basis the company is close to breakeven and the GAAP multiple is near 84x; the first unfavorable prior-year development in the disclosed history landed in the same quarter; the RADV audit of the plan covering most members carries no accrual and an outcome range that spans sampling clawback to contract-wide extrapolation; and the whistleblower suit — unproven, reviewed and cleared internally, with an unqualified ICFR opinion behind it — has nonetheless drawn nine plaintiffs' firms and targets the exact metric on which management is compensated. Against all of that, eight officers sold in 2026 against one small purchase, the CEO's stake fell 54% in four months, and programmatic selling resumes September 10.

The probability-weighted return of roughly +12% from $14.09 is positive but does not compensate for a 33% bear case in a name where the two largest swing factors resolve in October and cannot be sized in advance. Own it if you own it; wait for the Q3 development disclosure, the Star Ratings release, or a price closer to $12 before adding.
