## Executive Summary

Crane Company is a 170-year-old manufacturer of mission-critical engineered components for aerospace, defense, and process industries. The company completed a set of acquisitions on January 1, 2026—purchasing Druck, Panametrics, and Reuter-Stokes from Baker Hughes, plus Optek-Danulat—funded by $1.15B in new variable-rate debt. This capital deployment, combined with six prior acquisitions in two years, positions Crane to reach management's $4B revenue / 20%+ margin target by 2028, with 2026 guidance calling for low-to-mid 20% revenue growth.

The underlying business is performing well. Aerospace & Advanced Technologies (AAT) margins reached 25.0% in 2025 on 12.5% organic growth, with backlog up 25% YoY to $1.08B. Process Flow Technologies (PFT) is slower organically (1.5% growth) but benefits from pricing power and margin expansion to 21.0%. Across the company, Crane generated $341M in free cash flow on $2.3B of revenue (15% FCF margin). The consistent earnings beat record (4/4 quarters, average surprise +5.8%) supports confidence in management's execution.

The primary risk is integration execution on the Baker Hughes carve-out, the largest transaction in Crane's modern history, carrying typical carve-out complexity (standalone infrastructure buildout, transition services). The $1.15B in variable-rate debt creates meaningful interest rate sensitivity (~$11.5M per 100bps). At 26.8x FY2026E consensus EPS of $6.69, the stock trades at a substantial discount to the peer median P/E of 42.5x, reflecting appropriate integration risk but underweighting the quality of the aerospace franchise.

## Company Overview

Crane Company operates two segments:

**Aerospace & Advanced Technologies (AAT)** — $1,049M revenue (45% of total). Supplies pressure sensors, braking systems, power conversion, microwave/RF systems, and lubrication systems for commercial and military aerospace, defense, and space applications. Split 61% commercial / 39% military, 66% OEM / 34% aftermarket. Backlog: $1.08B.

**Process Flow Technologies (PFT)** — $1,256M revenue (55% of total). Manufactures process valves, pumps, and commercial valves for pharmaceutical, chemical, nuclear, cryogenic, water/wastewater, and general industrial end markets. Backlog: $360M.

The company was separated from Crane Holdings in April 2023, with the Payment & Merchandising Technologies business becoming Crane NXT. Crane divested its Engineered Materials segment on January 1, 2025, further sharpening focus.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue grew from $2,086M (2023) to $2,305M (2025), a 5.1% CAGR. Core organic growth was 6.2% in 2025 and 8.4% in 2024. Adjusted EPS compounded at 21.3% annually ($4.16 → $5.32 → $6.12).

Margin expansion has been the defining trend:
- AAT operating margin: 20.1% → 22.4% → 25.0% (2023–2025), a 490bps improvement
- PFT operating margin: 19.4% → 20.1% → 21.0% (2023–2025)
- Company-wide operating margin: 13.4% → 16.7% → 18.4%

2025 revenue growth was driven primarily by pricing, with PFT achieving 1.5% organic growth on essentially flat volumes. This pricing-ahead-of-cost dynamic, combined with $53.7M in productivity gains offsetting $110.7M in tariff-related cost increases, demonstrates genuine pricing power from sole-source and specification-driven positions.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow reached $395M in 2025 (+53% YoY), with capex of $54M producing ~$341M of free cash flow (15% FCF margin). FCF conversion on $367M net income is approximately 93%, indicating high earnings quality.

The balance sheet changed materially in late 2025. Net debt went from a modest $247M to $1,148M as Crane drew $900M on a term loan and $250M on a revolver to pre-fund the January 2026 acquisitions. All $1,150M is at variable rates, maturing September 2030. At year-end, cash was $507M, inflated by the pre-acquisition staging.

Post-acquisition, net debt is approximately $650-700M (after deploying the $507M cash plus any additional draws). Debt/EBITDA on a pro forma 2026 basis (assuming ~$600M EBITDA on ~$2.85B revenue at ~21% EBITDA margins) would be approximately 1.1-1.2x — manageable but a meaningful step-up from the near-zero leverage of 2024.

Interest expense will increase substantially from the $11.3M paid in 2025. At current SOFR levels, annual interest on $1.15B of variable-rate debt runs approximately $50-60M, reducing EPS by roughly $0.60-0.70 relative to a debt-free baseline.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

Crane beat consensus EPS in all four quarters of 2025, with surprises ranging from 3.3% to 7.8% (average 5.8%). This consistent beat pattern reflects management's tendency to guide conservatively and over-deliver, supporting confidence in current FY2026E consensus of $6.69.

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year EPS estimates are essentially flat over 90 days ($6.69 vs. $6.69), with a marginal +0.1% revision in the past 30 days. Next-year estimates have drifted down 3.1% over 90 days ($7.54 vs. $7.78), likely reflecting tariff uncertainty and concerns about integration-related margin dilution. The stability in current-year estimates, despite macro headwinds, suggests the base business is performing at or above expectations.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term (2026)

Management guides for low-to-mid 20% revenue growth, implying approximately $2.8B-$2.9B. The Baker Hughes and Optek acquisitions contribute the majority of this growth. Organic growth is guided at mid-single digits, consistent with the 6.2% achieved in 2025.

AAT growth is underpinned by the $1.08B backlog (+25% YoY), rising global defense budgets (military aftermarket grew 16.2% in 2025), and commercial aerospace production ramps. Management noted aftermarket growth rates will decelerate from increasingly challenging comps, but the base demand remains strong.

PFT's organic trajectory is more uncertain. Chemical market sluggishness and a declining backlog ($360M, -4% YoY) constrain near-term visibility. Growth here depends on pharmaceutical/biopharma demand (targeted by Optek), nuclear instrumentation (Reuter-Stokes), and cryogenic/hydrogen applications (CryoWorks, Technifab).

### Medium-Term (2027-2028)

Management's long-term target is $4B revenue with 20%+ adjusted EBITDA margins in both segments by 2028. At a ~$2.85B 2026 run rate, reaching $4B requires a further 18-20% growth over two years, achievable via continued M&A plus mid-single-digit organic growth.

The acquisitions position Crane in higher-growth subsegments: sensors/instrumentation (Druck, Panametrics), nuclear monitoring (Reuter-Stokes), biopharma inline measurement (Optek), and hydrogen/cryogenic infrastructure (CryoWorks, Technifab). These are secular growth markets that should deliver above-GDP organic growth rates.

### Margin Trajectory

Management explicitly guided that operating margins will decline modestly in 2026 due to the dilutive impact of acquired businesses, which enter with lower initial margins. The Crane Business System—which drove $53.7M in productivity gains in 2025—will be applied to these acquired businesses, likely restoring and expanding margins over 12-24 months. The track record of margin expansion from 13.4% to 18.4% over two years provides credibility.

## Competitive Landscape

Crane's competitive moat derives from five sources:

1. **Regulatory qualification barriers.** Aerospace components require multi-year certification by OEMs and aviation authorities. Once qualified on a platform, Crane maintains the position for the life of the program.

2. **Sole-source positions.** Explicitly noted for several high-volume commercial and military platforms. These create monopolistic pricing dynamics on installed base aftermarket.

3. **Engineering depth.** Crane invented several foundational technologies in fluid dynamics and sensing. Technical Paper 410 remains an industry standard reference. This engineering heritage translates into specification authority.

4. **Switching costs.** Component suppliers in aerospace and nuclear are rarely switched mid-program due to requalification costs and risk aversion.

5. **The Crane Business System.** A formalized operational excellence framework that consistently drives 2-3% annual productivity gains, enabling margin expansion while investing in growth.

The acquisition of Druck, Panametrics, and Reuter-Stokes from Baker Hughes adds sensor/instrumentation technology that deepens Crane's position in higher-margin, technology-differentiated product categories. These businesses were non-core within Baker Hughes but align directly with Crane's core competencies in mission-critical measurement and control.

## Risk Assessment

### Integration Execution (HIGH)
The Baker Hughes carve-out requires building standalone IT, HR, and finance infrastructure, negotiating transition services agreements, and managing employee retention during ownership transition. Crane has no recent track record of executing at this scale. Failure to integrate efficiently could delay margin recovery and absorb management bandwidth.

### Variable-Rate Debt Sensitivity (MODERATE-HIGH)
Each 100bps rate increase on the $1,150M variable-rate balance reduces pretax earnings by ~$11.5M ($0.15/share after tax). The Fed is holding at 3.64% with 35-45% probability of a hike in 2026. If rates move materially higher on inflation persistence, this could offset 2-3% of expected EPS growth.

### Tariff Exposure (MODERATE-HIGH)
With 41% of revenue international and manufacturing in the UK, France, Germany, and Taiwan, additional tariff escalation or trade disruption represents a direct margin risk. Management demonstrated pricing-through ability in 2025 (absorbing $110.7M in tariff costs via $53.7M in productivity and pricing), but the offset is not 1:1.

### Aerospace Cycle Deceleration (MODERATE)
AAT aftermarket growth was 12.9% in 2025. Management guided for deceleration in 2026 on harder comps. A broader aerospace downturn—whether from rising fuel costs dampening air travel demand or OEM production disruptions—would directly impact the highest-margin segment.

### PFT Organic Softness (MODERATE)
PFT organic growth of 1.5% with declining backlog signals demand weakness in chemical and general industrial markets. If this deteriorates further, the segment's growth will depend entirely on acquisitions.

### Options Market Signal
Options are pricing significant near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 54.4% is sharply above 1-year historical volatility of 30.6% (23.8pp spread), placing IV at the 84th percentile. The term structure is in steep backwardation (54.4% 1-month → 37.8% 6-month), indicating the market expects near-term uncertainty to resolve rather than persist. OI put/call ratio of 1.29 shows mild put demand, consistent with hedging rather than aggressive directional positioning. Short interest is low at 1.4% of float with 1.6 days to cover. The elevated near-term IV suggests the market is pricing an event window around upcoming earnings or macro developments. For a fundamental buyer with a 12-month horizon, the near-term IV overshoot relative to realized vol suggests the stock is carrying a temporary risk premium that should fade as acquisition integration proceeds.

## Conclusion

Crane Company is a high-quality industrial franchise executing an aggressive but strategically coherent acquisition strategy. The AAT segment's 25% margins, sole-source positions on critical aerospace platforms, and $1.08B backlog provide a durable earnings base. The Baker Hughes carve-out adds sensor/instrumentation technology across both segments and accelerates the path toward $4B revenue by 2028.

The primary tension is between excellent long-term positioning and near-term execution risk. The $1.15B in variable-rate debt, integration complexity, and guided margin dilution in 2026 create legitimate uncertainty. However, at 26.8x FY2026E EPS—a 23% discount to the peer median—the market is pricing meaningful integration risk while underweighting the consistent earnings beat record, the 490bps of AAT margin expansion over two years, and the secular tailwinds in aerospace, defense, nuclear, and hydrogen/cryogenic applications.

The rating is BUY with a 12-18 month base case target of $205, representing approximately 14.5% upside from the current price. Elevated options-implied volatility suggests a favorable entry point for fundamental investors willing to absorb near-term uncertainty around integration execution.
