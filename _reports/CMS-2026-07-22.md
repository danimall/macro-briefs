**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CMS Energy is a Michigan-focused regulated-utility holding company whose value rests almost entirely on Consumers Energy, an electric and gas monopoly serving ~6.8 million residents in the Lower Peninsula. The investment case is the standard regulated-utility algorithm: deploy capital into rate base, earn an authorized ~9.8–10.25% return, and compound EPS at a mid-single-digit rate. Management guides to >8% annual rate-base growth funded by a $24.1B five-year (2026–2030) Consumers capital plan. Layered on top is an incremental catalyst: a disclosed >1 GW data-center load agreement in Consumers' service territory, de-risked by an MPSC-approved large-load tariff (15-year minimum contracts, 80% minimum demand billing, upfront fees, collateral and exit protections) that shifts stranded-asset risk to the customer.

The offsets are equally concrete. The funding model is structurally free-cash-flow negative — 2025 operating cash flow of $2.24B against $3.82B of capex produced roughly –$1.59B of FCF — and the gap is closed with ongoing debt and equity issuance. Long-term debt rose to $17.8B in 2025 from $15.2B in 2024, and the ATM equity program dilutes per-share growth. In a higher-for-longer rate regime, both the cost of that debt and the dilution matter more than they did two years ago. On top of the base algorithm sit three idiosyncratic overhangs a standard screen would miss: the J.H. Campbell coal plant being force-run by DOE emergency order with no approved cost-recovery mechanism, bipartisan Michigan legislative proposals for a data-center moratorium through April 2027, and an unresolved cluster of executive/board-change 8-K filings.

At $72.50 the stock trades near 18.7x current-year consensus EPS ($3.88) and ~17.3x next-year ($4.18), yielding ~3.1%. That is roughly fair-to-slightly-full for the peer group and offers no obvious mispricing. Current-quarter estimates were cut 39.5% over 30 days, though full-year estimates held flat, indicating a timing/weather shift rather than a thesis change. We rate CMS a HOLD (growth hold): a stable mid-single-digit total-return compounder with limited asymmetric upside at today's multiple, consistent with the three prior HOLDs and the gradually declining score trajectory (6.9 → 6.5 → 6.1).

## Company Overview

CMS Energy operates through three segments:

- **Electric utility (Consumers):** ~37.4 billion kWh delivered in 2025; segment net income $719M. The largest earnings contributor.
- **Gas utility (Consumers):** 311 Bcf delivered in 2025; segment net income $409M.
- **NorthStar Clean Energy:** non-utility IPP/renewable developer with ~1,665 MW of capacity; segment net income $71M — small but growing.

The competitive moat is regulatory and legal: a service-territory monopoly under municipal franchises, rate-of-return regulation by the Michigan Public Service Commission, and a constructive Michigan framework. The economic model is mechanical: invest capital, grow rate base, earn a regulated return on it, subject to MPSC approval. Michigan's 2023 Energy Law (60% renewable by 2035, 100% clean by 2040) provides a multi-decade capital-deployment runway, with statewide plans for up to 9,000 MW solar, 4,000 MW wind, and 2,500 MW storage.

EPS grew ~6% in 2025, in line with the utility algorithm. Revenue swings largely reflect pass-through commodity costs and weather rather than earnings power. Operating margin expanded to ~20% and net margin to ~11.7%. The 2025 earnings bridge (+$68M vs 2024) was driven by an electric rate increase (+$210M) and a gas sales/weather rebound (+$151M gas sales, +$71M gas rate), partly offset by higher depreciation (–$63M), interest (–$43M), property taxes (–$29M), higher income taxes (–$87M), and ERP/IT implementation costs. A meaningful portion of the improvement was favorable weather, so weather-normalized growth is more modest than the headline suggests.

The cash-flow profile is the crux. Negative FCF of roughly –$1.59B represents a –18.6% FCF margin. For a regulated utility in a heavy build cycle, this is structural and expected; the company funds the gap with debt and equity, not a sign of distress. Debt/equity is ~4.5x, which is normal for the model. Total debt/capital at the parent is 0.56 (covenant <0.70) and at Consumers 0.51 (covenant <0.65), comfortably compliant. Liquidity includes a $1.4B revolver and an undrawn $500M CP program. The Altman Z-Score of 0.8 ("distress zone") is a model artifact: it penalizes the high leverage, low asset turnover (0.21x), and thin working capital inherent to utilities, and the company holds investment-grade ratings backed by regulated cash flows.

Two forward-looking financial points deserve emphasis. First, capex is guided up to $4.4B (CMS) in 2026 and peaks at ~$6.2B in 2028, extending the negative-FCF, external-financing dependence for years. Second, only $8M of forward equity contracts remain under the $1B ATM program (maturing Nov 2026), meaning further equity issuance to fund the plan is likely, diluting per-share growth. The central financial tension is the interaction of rising financing needs with a higher-for-longer rate path: every incremental dollar of rate base is funded at a higher marginal cost of capital than the plan was originally underwritten against.

## Growth Analysis

The base algorithm is credible and well-supported: >8% rate-base growth on the Consumers plan translating to mid-single-digit EPS growth (consensus $3.88 current year, $4.18 next year — roughly +10% and +8% respectively over the $3.53 base). This is the durable, low-variance component of the story.

The incremental catalyst is data-center load. The MPSC's November 2025 large-load tariff (≥100 MW customers) materially de-risks the >1 GW agreement by preventing cost-shifting to existing ratepayers. Additional generation moves support the runway: a 10-year, up-to-1,240 MW PPA with MCV Partnership (effective June 2030), 850 MW of contracted battery storage online through 2028, and Muskegon Solar (250 MW) operational in 2025.

Two caveats temper the growth score. First, the estimate-revision picture is mixed: current-quarter EPS was cut 39.5% over 30 days (to $0.47 from $0.78), though next-quarter revised +4.7% and full-year held roughly flat (–0.1% over 30 days). The current-quarter cut reflects seasonal/weather timing and rate-case order timing rather than a structural downgrade; the flat full-year and next-year estimates confirm the annual trajectory is intact. This is not the >30% positive-revision-velocity signal that flags a strong BUY; it is a stable-estimates utility. Second, the data-center opportunity is genuinely double-edged: Michigan legislators from both parties have proposed a moratorium on data-center development through April 2027, New York enacted a statewide moratorium in July 2026, and a FERC advance NOPR on large-load interconnection could let hyperscalers bypass the utility by buying directly from wholesale markets. The Google/Van Buren Township 1-GW project frequently cited in coverage is served by DTE, not Consumers, so it does not accrue to CMS.

## Competitive Landscape

Consumers holds a legal monopoly in its service territory, which is the moat. The trajectory question is whether that position is widening, stable, or eroding. On balance it is stable with two-sided pressure. Positives: the constructive MPSC framework, the proactive large-load tariff that lets CMS capture data-center rate base while insulating ratepayers, and Governor Whitmer's voluntary "Affordable and Responsible Growth" pledge (data centers cover their own costs, no residential rate increases), all of which support franchise growth.

Erosive pressures are also real. Retail Open Access deliveries are at or over the 10% statutory cap with a waiting list, and advocacy for an "ROA-like community solar" program could chip at the generation franchise. A January 2025 Sixth Circuit ruling found the MPSC's local-clearing-requirement discriminates against interstate commerce (remanded and unresolved), creating capacity-market uncertainty. The FERC large-load-interconnection NOPR is the most significant structural threat: if hyperscalers can interconnect directly to wholesale markets, the utility's ability to monetize data-center load through rate base is undermined. The competitive-position score of 6.0 reflects a durable but not-widening moat facing identifiable regulatory challenges, consistent with the trajectory's decline from 7.5 to 6.5 in prior reports.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for this specific name, beyond generic utility exposure:

1. **J.H. Campbell forced-run cost recovery (idiosyncratic).** The 1,407 MW coal plant, approved for retirement in May 2025, is being force-run by successive DOE §202(c) emergency orders (through at least Feb 17, 2026, with more possible). There is no MPSC rate provision and no finalized FERC-approved MISO tariff to recover these costs. FERC granted Consumers' complaint in August 2025 directing MISO to revise its tariff, but final approval is pending and third parties (Michigan AG, environmental groups) are litigating. The dollar amounts (~$4M per 90-day retention plus operating costs) are modest, but the recovery uncertainty, litigation exposure, and complication of the coal-retirement narrative are real.

2. **Rate-case / regulatory-lag risk.** The growth thesis depends entirely on MPSC approvals. The 2025 electric case ($447M requested, order due ~April 2026) and 2025 gas case ($240M requested, order by Oct 2026) determine authorized ROE and equity layer. Election-year affordability pressure could compress outcomes.

3. **Data-center moratorium risk.** Bipartisan Michigan moratorium bills through April 2027 could freeze or dilute the >1 GW opportunity; the FERC NOPR is the structural tail risk.

4. **Funding-model / rate-path risk.** Persistent negative FCF and ongoing equity issuance into a higher-for-longer rate environment raise the marginal cost of the plan and drag per-share growth.

5. **Governance uncertainty.** A cluster of Item 5.02 executive/board-change 8-Ks (June 2025, Feb 2026, June 2026) is unresolved: potentially routine rotation, potentially a leadership transition. Combined with unretrieved proxy/compensation data, this is a monitoring flag rather than a confirmed red flag.

6. **Clean-energy execution and environmental tail:** large multi-year renewable buildout with interconnection/supply-chain/tariff exposure; 23 former MGP sites, coal-ash, and PFAS obligations (recoverable in rates but not guaranteed).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($82–88).** The base algorithm compounds EPS at mid-single digits with the reliability of regulated cash flows. The >1 GW data-center agreement, protected by the large-load tariff, adds incremental load-growth upside the market has not fully capitalized. Moratorium bills fail or exempt existing contracts; the FERC NOPR does not enable bypass; J.H. Campbell cost recovery is granted; rate cases land near request. In that scenario CMS re-rates higher toward the mean/median target zone of $81–82 with upside to $88.

**Bear Case ($66).** A higher-for-longer rate path raises financing costs on the plan while ongoing equity issuance dilutes per-share growth. A Michigan data-center moratorium freezes the load-growth catalyst, or the FERC NOPR enables hyperscaler bypass. Rate cases come in below request on affordability grounds, and J.H. Campbell costs go partially unrecovered. The multiple compresses toward the low end and the stock revisits the analyst low target of $66, roughly –9% from current.

The most-likely path sits between: mid-single-digit EPS growth plus a ~3.1% dividend, with the data-center optionality providing modest upside skew offset by rate-path and political risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Own for the ~8% EPS-growth-plus-dividend total return, not for asymmetric appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Positive estimate-revision velocity on full-year/next-year EPS (currently flat) confirming the data-center load is converting to earnings, OR
- A constructive 2025 electric rate-case order (near the $447M request with authorized ROE ≥9.9%) that de-risks the near-term trajectory, OR
- Pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s (near the $66 analyst low), where the ~3.1% yield and mid-single-digit growth would offer a more asymmetric entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Michigan data-center moratorium enacted that freezes the >1 GW opportunity, or a FERC large-load ruling enabling utility bypass, OR
- A rate-case order materially below request (authorized ROE compression or reduced equity layer) that breaks the >8% rate-base-to-EPS bridge, OR
- Confirmation that the Item 5.02 cluster reflects disruptive leadership turnover, or a dividend-coverage deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the electric rate-case order (~April 2026, which may already be resolving given the current-quarter IV backwardation) and the next quarterly earnings release, which will show whether data-center load and rate outcomes are flowing into full-year estimate revisions.

## Conclusion

CMS Energy is a competently managed regulated utility with a credible, low-variance growth algorithm and an incremental catalyst in contracted data-center load, backed by a well-designed protective tariff. Those strengths are balanced by a structurally negative-FCF, equity-diluting funding model that faces rising costs in a higher-for-longer rate regime, a valuation (~17–19x forward, ~3.1% yield) that is fair-to-slightly-full rather than mispriced, and three specific overhangs: J.H. Campbell cost recovery, Michigan data-center moratorium risk, and unresolved governance changes. The declining score trajectory (6.9 → 6.5 → 6.1) reflects the accumulation of these overhangs against a stable but unexciting core, and the current data does not reverse it. We reiterate HOLD, would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s or a positive estimate-revision turn, and would downgrade on a moratorium enactment, an adverse FERC ruling, or a below-request rate-case order.
