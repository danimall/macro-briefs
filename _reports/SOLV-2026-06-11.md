**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Solventum is the former Health Care division of 3M, spun off on April 1, 2024, now operating as a standalone ~$8.3B-revenue medtech and health-IT company across three segments: MedSurg (58% of sales), Dental Solutions (16%), and Health Information Systems (16%). The investment case is an orphaned-division turnaround. A neglected unit with low-single-digit organic growth is being re-energized by a medtech-veteran management team, with organic growth re-accelerating from roughly +1% post-spin to +2-3% currently, a deleveraged balance sheet following the ~$4B sale of Purification & Filtration to Thermo Fisher, and a four-year cost program targeting ~$500M in annual savings.

The financials require careful reading because GAAP results are heavily distorted. FY2025 net income of $1,556M was inflated by a $1.5B pre-tax gain on the P&F sale plus a one-time 9.2% tax rate; underlying operations are barely profitable, as Q1 2026 net income of just $13M (EPS $0.07) makes clear. The crux of the story is cash conversion: operating cash flow has collapsed from $1,915M in 2023 to $1,185M in 2024 to $369M in 2025, and was negative $189M in Q1 2026. Some of this is one-time (separation costs, TSA drag, incentive-comp timing), but FCF must normalize sharply for the leverage profile (~$5B debt, Altman Z of 1.97) to look comfortable. MedSurg's operating margin fell to 13.1% in Q1 2026 from 17.8% a year earlier on tariffs, freight, and 3M supply mark-ups.

At ~12x forward EPS on $6.55 consensus CY earnings, the valuation is undemanding relative to a 37.7x peer median, and the 4-for-4 beat record plus +13.7%/30d current-quarter estimate revisions support the near-term momentum. The next-quarter estimate was cut -15.0% over 30 days, free cash flow is currently negative, and the structural 3M sole-source supply dependency touches ~$2-3B of revenue with no identified substitute. We rate SOLV a **HOLD** on a 12-month growth thesis: the catalysts (cost-program execution, separation-cost roll-off, tariff-refund optionality, HIS margin expansion) are real and dated but unproven, and the bear-case downside exceeds the threshold for an active long.

## Company Overview

Solventum inherited 3M's 70+ year Health Care operating history and now runs three reportable segments after divesting Purification & Filtration in September 2025:

- **MedSurg (57.9% of FY2025 sales, $4,817M):** Advanced Wound Care (negative-pressure wound therapy, dressings, skin care) and Infection Prevention & Surgical Solutions (Tegaderm I.V. site management, sterilization assurance, surgical supplies, medical tapes, stethoscopes, OEM). This is the volume growth engine and the margin pressure point. Operating income of $810M implies a 16.8% margin, down from 19.1% in 2024. [unverified — actual: 23.9% 2023 margin not in sources]
- **Dental Solutions (16.2%, $1,349M):** Brackets, aligners, restorative cements, bonding agents. Operating margin 25.6%, competing against Dentsply Sirona, Envista, Straumann, Align, and Ivoclar.
- **Health Information Systems / HIS (16.3%, $1,360M):** Healthcare SaaS — computer-assisted physician documentation, revenue-cycle and coding automation, the Solventum 360 Encompass platform. The highest-quality segment at a 36.5% FY2025 margin (rising to 38.1% in Q1 2026), competing against Optum, Microsoft/Nuance, Epic, Oracle/Cerner, and Waystar.

The competitive advantages are entrenched hospital and clinic relationships, recognized brands (Tegaderm, Coban, Bair Hugger), material-science manufacturing know-how, and recurring software and rental revenue in HIS. In most categories Solventum is a #2-4 player rather than a dominant leader.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Low-growth but improving organically. FY2025 reported revenue rose +0.9% to $8,325M (organic +3.3%, versus +1.2% organic in 2024). Q1 2026 reported revenue fell 3.0% to $2,007M because of the P&F divestiture, but organic growth was +2.1% with all three segments positive in the U.S. Organic growth has roughly tripled versus the immediate post-spin baseline, which is the central data point supporting the turnaround.

**Profitability — GAAP is distorted.** Operating income and net income were inflated by the P&F gain. The segment margins tell the real story: MedSurg fell on tariffs (~$55M), logistics, and 3M supply-agreement mark-ups, while HIS expanded on software mix. Q1 2026 lays it bare: operating income of $81M (4.0% margin) and net income of $13M, with SG&A at 41.2% of sales on standalone stand-up and separation costs.

**Cash flow — the variable that matters.** FY2025 FCF was roughly zero (-$10M after $379M capex). The drivers are partly one-time (separation costs, P&F transaction and TSA drag, incentive-comp timing), but the magnitude of the deterioration is the single most concerning item in the financials. The mechanism for recovery is concrete: separation and TSA costs roll off through the 24-month transition window, and the $500M cost program begins delivering. Whether that converts into normalized FCF over the next 12-18 months determines whether the leverage and zero-FCF concerns dissolve.

**Balance sheet.** Debt was cut from ~$8B at spin to ~$5.0B using P&F proceeds (notes tender plus term-loan repayment), with maturities laddered 2027-2064 at 5.0-6.0% coupons. Leverage remains material: D/E 1.83, Altman Z 1.97 (grey zone). Goodwill and intangibles of $8.3B exceed equity of $5.0B. Of $878M year-end cash, only ~$78M was domestic (~$73M at Q1 2026), so repatriation friction limits flexibility for U.S. debt service and buybacks. No dividend; a first-ever $1B buyback was authorized in November 2025, of which only $67M was used in Q1 2026 at an average $72.53.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three legs. First, organic re-acceleration across all segments versus a sub-2% post-spin baseline. Second, HIS margin expansion, a high-quality recurring-revenue mix benefiting from the administrative-waste reduction theme in U.S. healthcare. Third, the "Transform for the Future" restructuring (November 2025), targeting the cost savings noted above by ~2029 for ~$500M pre-tax cost, layered on top of the wind-down of separation and TSA expenses.

Estimate revisions are mixed in a way that flags low visibility. Current-quarter EPS consensus rose +13.7% over 30 days and +16.8% over 90 days, and the company has beaten in all four post-spin quarters (by 2.7% to 15.1%). But the next-quarter estimate was cut -15.0% over 30 days, and full-year revisions are only modestly positive (+1.5% CY, +0.9% next year). This is a lumpy, low-visibility earnings profile where the front quarter looks good and the next does not, rather than the clean, broad-based estimate inflection that signals a durable earnings re-rating. The Acera Surgical acquisition ($696M plus $125M contingent, closed December 2025) deepens the higher-growth regenerative wound-care franchise within MedSurg and is a sensible bolt-on, but it does not change the segment's near-term margin trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

At $80.94, SOLV trades at ~12x forward EPS on $6.55 CY consensus and ~11.5x on $7.04 next-year consensus. Against a Health Care Technology peer median of 37.7x, this is a steep discount, but the peer set is heterogeneous and skewed (Omnicell 97.7x, Certara negative, Doximity 21.0x) and reflects faster-growing or asset-light software businesses. The more honest comparison is to medtech and diversified health peers growing low-single-digit organically, where mid-teens multiples are normal — placing SOLV at a justified but not screamingly cheap valuation.

The valuation deserves a high score for a specific reason: the trailing P/E of ~9.1x is computed on GAAP EPS of $8.88 that is inflated by the P&F gain and therefore meaningless, while the forward multiple sits on consensus that already incorporates depressed standalone-cost-burdened earnings. There is identifiable mispricing optionality: an unrecorded tariff refund (Solventum paid ~$55M under IEEPA; the February 20, 2026 SCOTUS ruling and March 4, 2026 Court of International Trade refund order create a potential cash benefit that has zero recorded value), plus the cost program that, if delivered, lifts normalized earnings power well above the current run-rate. The absolute multiple provides downside support: at 10x the CY estimate, the stock would sit near $66, close to the 52-week low, implying limited multiple downside absent a fundamental break.

We score Valuation at 7.0. The catalysts are real and the multiple is undemanding, but the negative current free cash flow and the unproven nature of the FCF recovery cap the score below the level that would support an active long.

## Competitive Landscape

Solventum competes in fragmented, intensely competitive markets where it is generally a #2-4 player. In wound care it faces Smith & Nephew, Mölnlycke, and Convatec; in dental, Dentsply Sirona, Envista, and Align; in HIS, Optum, Epic, Oracle/Cerner, and Waystar. Secular tailwinds support the franchise: aging demographics, the roughly 25% of U.S. healthcare spend that is administrative waste (HIS opportunity), and the shift to lower-cost care sites.

The competitive trajectory is mixed, which warrants a neutral score. HIS margin expansion and recurring software revenue are genuine moat indicators, and organic growth turning positive across all segments suggests share is at least stabilizing. Two structural concerns weigh against the trajectory: the AI wave is both an opportunity for HIS (coding and RCM automation) and a threat from AI-native disruptors, with clinician-productivity revenue already declining within the segment; and MedSurg's margin erosion reflects an inability to fully pass through tariff and input costs in a competitive category. The 3M sole-source dependency is also a competitive vulnerability rather than a strength: ~$2-3B of revenue relies on 3M-proprietary inputs Solventum cannot manufacture itself.

## Risk Assessment

1. **3M sole-source supply dependency (the dominant structural risk).** 3M is the sole supplier of certain materials used in products representing ~$3B of revenue, including a proprietary material tied to ~$2B (transparent I.V. film dressings, sterilization biological indicators, dental composites). No substitute has been identified. If the supply agreement lapses or is renewed on unfavorable terms, a material revenue block is at risk.
2. **Margin compression from tariffs and 3M cost pass-throughs.** The IEEPA tariff situation is fluid: a potential refund offsets prior payments, but new Section 122 tariffs maintain go-forward cost uncertainty.
3. **Leverage plus weak near-term cash conversion.** ~$5B debt against negative Q1 operating cash flow and a grey-zone Altman Z of 1.97. Only ~$73M of cash is domestic.
4. **3M stock overhang.** 3M retains ~14.8% and intends to dispose within five years, a persistent supply-of-stock pressure. The Tax Matters Agreement also restricts M&A, equity issuance, and buybacks to protect tax-free status.
5. **Bair Hugger litigation.** Solventum indemnifies 3M for uninsured liabilities across 8,500+ surgical-site-infection suits, with trials anticipated in 2026. Accrued litigation is only $31M; an adverse outcome could exceed reserves.
6. **PFAS.** 3M's indemnification covered products sold only through 2025, and 3M stopped supplying PFAS components at end-2025. Post-2025 PFAS-containing products are Solventum's own liability.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign. Near-term ATM IV of 29.8% is roughly fair to 27.8% historical volatility (a 2.0% spread), and IV sits in the 3rd percentile of its own chain, so the market is pricing low near-term event risk. The term structure is in contango (29.8% near, 35.8% at six months), a normal configuration. The put/call OI ratio fell from 0.64 to 0.34 since mid-May, indicating reduced hedging demand into the rally. There is a modest near-term put skew of 4.0% but it inverts to -3.8% at six months, where calls are bid. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.5%. The options market sees a low-drama, range-bound stock, consistent with a slow-developing turnaround rather than a binary catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Organic growth continues re-accelerating toward +3-4%, the $500M cost program and the roll-off of separation/TSA costs convert into normalized FCF, HIS margin holds near 38%, the tariff refund materializes as a cash and earnings surprise, and the stock re-rates toward 15x normalized EPS. Target ~$100, matching the high analyst estimate.

**Bear case.** The 3M supply agreement renewal turns adverse or MedSurg margins fail to recover from tariff and input pressure, operating cash flow stays depressed, the grey-zone Z-score becomes a live credit concern, Bair Hugger trials produce reserve-exceeding liabilities, and the 3M overhang caps the multiple. The stock retests the low-$60s, near the 52-week low and a 10x-on-depressed-earnings floor. Target ~$62, roughly -23% from current.

The base case sits between: a slow grind higher as organic growth holds and cost savings begin to show, but with FCF normalization unproven and lumpy quarterly earnings keeping the multiple range-bound. The key swing factors are FCF normalization, tariff resolution, and restructuring execution.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is the self-help margin-and-FCF recovery, which is specific and dated but not yet evidenced in cash flow. The stock does not clear our bar for an active long because free cash flow is currently negative and the bear-case downside (~23%) exceeds the threshold for a BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August) is the key checkpoint for FCF normalization and MedSurg margin recovery.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating cash flow turns durably positive (two consecutive quarters of positive FCF, trending toward a $600M+ annualized run-rate).
- MedSurg operating margin recovers above 16% as tariff and separation costs roll off.
- Tangible evidence of the $500M cost program landing (a quantified savings figure in management commentary) and/or a recorded tariff refund.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The 3M supply agreement is not renewed on reasonable terms, or management discloses renewal uncertainty on the ~$2-3B exposed revenue.
- A Bair Hugger trial outcome materially exceeds the $31M reserve.
- Organic growth decelerates back below +1% with continued negative operating cash flow.

## Conclusion

Solventum is a leveraged, low-growth medtech spin-off in the early innings of a credible self-help turnaround. The positives are real: organic growth has re-accelerated to +2-3%, the HIS segment is a genuine high-margin asset expanding toward 38%, the balance sheet was deleveraged from ~$8B to ~$5B post-P&F sale, and the forward multiple of ~12x is undemanding with embedded tariff-refund and cost-savings optionality. The negatives are equally real and unresolved: free cash flow is currently negative, MedSurg margins have compressed to 13.1%, the next-quarter estimate was cut -15%, and the 3M sole-source dependency is a structural vulnerability the market under-appreciates.

A HOLD requires a specific 12-month growth thesis, which is satisfied here: FCF and margin normalization as separation costs roll off and the $500M cost program lands, with a dated reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings. That thesis is unproven in the cash-flow statement, and until it is, the asymmetry does not support an active long. We rate SOLV a **HOLD** with a constructive lean, and would upgrade on confirmed positive free cash flow and MedSurg margin recovery.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the FY2023 MedSurg margin (23.9%) is not supported by the sources and was removed; the trailing P/E was corrected from ~10x to ~9.1x (consistent with $78.19 / $8.88, though current price is now $80.94); and the bull-case normalized FCF figure ("approaching $700M-$1B") was removed as unsupported by the sources — none of these change the core HOLD thesis but the valuation framing relies partly on unverified normalized-FCF assumptions.
