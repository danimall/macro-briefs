**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fair Isaac occupies a rare position where its single largest earnings driver and its single largest existential risk are the same fact. The FICO Score's mortgage-pricing power drove Scores revenue up 60% year-over-year in the March 2026 quarter and total revenue up 39%, with diluted EPS up 69% to $11.14 and operating margin expanding to roughly 58%. That pricing power, applied to a regulatory near-monopoly in GSE-conforming mortgage scoring, is precisely what prompted FHFA Director Bill Pulte to authorize VantageScore 4.0 (a joint venture owned by FICO's own three distribution partners, Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax) for competitive use in July 2025. The company is running its highest-margin, fastest-growing quarter in its history at the exact moment the structural monopoly underpinning that growth is being dismantled.

The market has responded by cutting the stock roughly 26% from its December 2025 peak near $1,810 to the current $1,336, even as reported fundamentals continued to accelerate and consensus estimates continued to rise (+4.0% current-year, +3.8% next-year over 90 days). The drawdown reflects a prospective threat that had not yet touched reported Scores revenue as of the latest filing. All top-30 mortgage originators including Rocket Mortgage now offer VantageScore 4.0, and TransUnion is cutting VantageScore mortgage pricing to seize share, but none of this had dented FICO's numbers through Q2 FY26. The bull view is that the market is over-discounting a slow, partial migration; the bear view is that the reported numbers are a lagging indicator of a franchise about to be repriced.

We rate FICO a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The quality of the business, the cash generation, and the near-term earnings momentum are undeniable, but the competitive threat is genuine, dated, and quantifiable only with a lag we do not yet have. The debt-funded buyback structure (negative stockholders' equity of –$2.1B, total debt $3.6B, new notes at 6.00–6.25%) amplifies both the upside if the franchise holds and the downside if Scores pricing rolls over. With a surviving Sherman Act monopolization claim targeting the exact pricing lever driving earnings, no open-market insider buying, and a prominent short seller (Steve Eisman) disclosed, we need to see the next two-to-three quarters of Scores volume and price data before making a directional call.

## Company Overview

Fair Isaac, founded in 1956 and headquartered in Bozeman, Montana, is an analytics-software company built around the FICO Score, the de facto standard measure of U.S. consumer credit risk used across nearly all major banks, card issuers, mortgage lenders, and auto lenders. The business runs in two segments:

- **Scores (59% of FY25 revenue, ~88–91% segment operating margin):** A royalty/licensing model. FICO does not store underlying credit data; it licenses its scoring algorithm to the three national consumer reporting agencies and collects a per-score fee, plus a smaller direct-to-consumer business (myFICO.com). Capital-light and extraordinarily high-margin.
- **Software (41% of FY25 revenue, ~29–30% segment operating margin):** Decisioning and analytics software for fraud, originations, customer management, and optimization, increasingly consolidated onto the cloud-native FICO Platform and sold as multi-year subscriptions.

The competitive moat has two pillars. First, the FICO Score's regulatory reinforcement: GSEs historically required FICO Scores on delivered mortgages, which effectively mandated the product across the U.S. mortgage market. Second, the Falcon fraud models benefit from a data-network effect across 10,000+ contributing institutions, backed by roughly 65 years of proprietary modeling and 204 U.S. patents. The July 2025 FHFA decision directly attacks the first pillar.

## Financial Analysis

The reported financials are elite by any software-industry standard.

**Growth is accelerating, and it is concentrated.** FY25 revenue reached $1.99B, up 16% (FY24 $1.72B, FY23 $1.51B), with Scores +27% and Software +3%. The March 2026 quarter then stepped up sharply: net income $264.5M (+63%). H1 FY26 revenue was $1.20B (+28%). Management attributes the Scores acceleration to higher per-score pricing in mortgage plus a mortgage-origination volume rebound. This is not broad-based demand; it is pricing power applied to a monopoly product.

**Profitability is exceptional and improving.** FY25 operating margin was 46.5% and net margin 32.7%. Q2 FY26 operating margin expanded as the high-margin Scores mix rose and cost of revenue fell to 13% of sales. ROA runs around 35%, and FY25 free cash flow was $770M on near-zero capex ($8.9M), a ~39% FCF margin. The earnings quality is high and the capital intensity is minimal.

**The balance sheet requires careful interpretation.** Stockholders' equity was –$2.10B at March 2026 (from –$1.75B at FY25-end), which is not distress but the cumulative artifact of roughly $8.3B in debt-funded treasury-stock buybacks. Retained earnings remain positive at $4.98B. The real concern is rising leverage into a competitive threat: total debt reached $3.6B (from $2.2B two years ago), with $1.5B of 6.00% notes issued in May 2025 and $1.0B of 6.25% notes in March 2026. Net interest expense is climbing quickly: FY25 $134M (+27%), H1 FY26 $87M (+42%). The current ratio of 0.83 is structural for a subscription business with deferred revenue, and operating cash flow comfortably covers obligations. The company remains in compliance with its maximum 3.5x consolidated leverage covenant (step-up to 4.0x).

**Capital allocation is aggressive and shareholder-return-focused.** Essentially all FCF, plus incremental debt, is returned via buybacks: $1.4B in FY25, $774M in H1 FY26. Share count fell from 26.0M (FY22) to ~23.2M. A new $1.5B repurchase authorization was approved in February 2026 ($1.1B remaining). No dividend. This structure mechanically amplifies EPS growth but also amplifies downside if the Scores earnings base contracts.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats, including an 11.7% beat in the March 2026 quarter. Estimate revisions are uniformly positive but modest in magnitude: current-year EPS revised to $43.21, next-year to $54.55. This modest revision pace suggests analysts are hedging against the competitive threat even as reported results beat.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is entirely about Scores pricing, and its durability is the central question. Scores revenue growing 60% in a quarter on price and mortgage-volume recovery is not a repeatable, structurally protected trajectory; it depends on FICO's ability to keep raising per-score fees in mortgage, which is exactly the lever now under regulatory and competitive attack. The 10-K adds explicit language that increased federal focus on mortgage closing costs, including credit-score fees, may constrain FICO's ability to raise Scores prices.

The Software segment, positioned as the diversifying second growth engine, is quietly stalling on the legacy side. Non-platform ARR is declining approximately 8% year-over-year, offset by Platform ARR growth of 49% (with strong 136% dollar-based net retention). Total Software ARR grew only 4% in FY25 and 10% in Q2 FY26. Software operating income declined year-over-year in Q2 FY26 as data-center hosting costs rose, and segment margin compressed from 31% to 29%. The platform-first transition is working on the Platform line but cannibalizing the legacy base, and the segment is not yet the reliable growth pillar the strategy promises.

Cards, auto, and the Falcon fraud network remain entrenched outside mortgage, which limits the downside on the Scores franchise even in an adverse VantageScore scenario. Mortgage is the segment being contested; the non-mortgage Scores base is stickier. The growth question therefore reduces to how much of the current Scores growth is durable non-mortgage/pricing versus contestable mortgage pricing that VantageScore adoption will erode. We do not have enough post-adoption data to answer this, which is why we score growth prospects as good but not elite.

## Valuation Assessment

FICO trades at roughly 42x trailing/forward earnings on the $26.54 trailing EPS in the profile, but the forward math is more favorable given the earnings trajectory: at the current price against consensus current-year EPS of $43.21, the stock trades at roughly 31x current-year and roughly 24.5x next-year ($54.55) consensus. On a PEG basis, mid-30s% forward earnings growth against a low-30s forward multiple is not stretched for a business with 58% operating margins and near-monopoly economics.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. The Application Software peer median P/E is 40.7 (WDAY 40.7, TYL 40.8, ADSK 30.0, OKTA 85.1, PTC 11.4). FICO's forward multiple sits below that median, which would normally argue for a discount to intrinsic value. FICO's multiple compression is not a value-trap in the conventional sense, nor is it an obvious mispricing. It reflects a specific, identifiable risk: the market is pricing a probability-weighted haircut to future Scores pricing power that peers do not face. A stock is not cheap merely because a monopoly-linked earnings stream is being repriced by regulators; the multiple should compress if the durability of those earnings is genuinely lower.

We are deliberately cautious on rewarding the compressed multiple. The analyst target range is extraordinarily wide (low $707, high $2,400, mean $1,535, median $1,522), which is itself a statement that the analyst community cannot agree on what FICO is worth. That dispersion (a 3.4x spread between low and high targets) is the quantitative signature of a binary competitive outcome, not a consensus mispricing. When the low target sits roughly 47% below the current price and the high sits roughly 80% above, the "consensus target as floor" logic does not apply; the targets reflect radically different assumptions about VantageScore share capture, not stale extrapolation.

On balance, valuation is roughly fair for the risk. If the franchise holds and Scores pricing durability proves intact, the stock is meaningfully undervalued at 24.5x next-year earnings with 30%+ growth. If VantageScore captures material mortgage-score share and forces price concessions, the current multiple is still too high on a lower future earnings base. The valuation itself does not resolve the call; the competitive outcome does. We score valuation at the midpoint.

## Competitive Landscape

The central dynamic is the erosion of FICO's regulatory monopoly in mortgage scoring. For decades, GSE requirements effectively mandated FICO Scores on conforming mortgages. In July 2025, FHFA Director Pulte authorized VantageScore 4.0 as an approved alternative, explicitly to reduce consumer mortgage costs. The competitive mechanism is unusually adverse: VantageScore is owned by Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax, the same three CRAs that are simultaneously FICO's distribution channel and its largest customers, at 51% of FY25 revenue rising to 64% in Q2 FY26. Those partners are now actively pricing VantageScore below FICO to win mortgage-score share, with TransUnion cutting VantageScore mortgage pricing and all top-30 originators including Rocket Mortgage (#1) now offering the product. Industry groups (CHLA) are lobbying FHFA to accelerate adoption, citing rising FICO costs, with third-party estimates of $600M–$1B in industry savings from switching (these figures originate from VantageScore/FHFA-aligned promotional sources and should be treated as advocacy, not audited).

The mechanism is worth stating plainly: FICO raises per-score mortgage fees → this compresses lender margins and draws regulatory attention → FHFA authorizes a competing product owned by FICO's own partners → those partners undercut FICO on price to capture share. The incumbent's earnings driver directly feeds the substitute's adoption. This is a structurally conflicted distribution channel with no clean historical precedent.

The competitive position dimension has been the largest swing in this company's scoring history, and the new data confirms that decline rather than reversing it. As of the latest filing, VantageScore had not yet dented reported Scores revenue, and the non-mortgage FICO franchise (cards, auto) and Falcon fraud network remain entrenched. The trajectory in mortgage is clearly deteriorating, and trajectory is what this dimension measures. We hold competitive position at 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific, dated, and material, not sector truisms.

1. **VantageScore/FHFA competitive shock.** The dominant thread, discussed above. Prospective as of the latest filing but with a clear adoption pathway through 2026.
2. **Worsening customer concentration.** CRAs rose to 64% of revenue in a year, each individually >10%, and these same customers now own and are pricing the competing product. Loss or renegotiation of any one relationship would be material.
3. **Surviving antitrust litigation.** A consolidated putative class action in N.D. Illinois had most claims dismissed in November 2024, but a Sherman Act Section 2 monopolization claim plus state claims proceed into discovery, targeting the exact mortgage-score pricing power driving earnings, at the moment regulators are enabling competition.
4. **Regulatory pressure on mortgage fees.** New 10-K language warns that federal focus on mortgage closing costs may constrain FICO's ability to raise Scores prices.
5. **Software segment deceleration.** Non-platform ARR shrinking ~8%, segment operating income declining year-over-year, margin compression to 29%. The second growth engine is not yet reliable.
6. **Rising leverage at 6%+ rates into a competitive threat.** Debt-funded buybacks amplify downside; a Scores pricing setback would hit a more levered entity with rising fixed charges.

The offsetting consideration is that much of this is now priced: the drawdown, the wide analyst target dispersion, and the elevated short interest all indicate the market is discounting the threat. The binary nature of the outcome (monopoly holds vs. monopoly erodes) and the surviving antitrust claim keep the idiosyncratic risk elevated relative to the current valuation. We score risk factors at 4.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. ATM implied volatility is 61.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 50.8% (a rich 10.2pp spread, 84th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 61.0% > long-term 52.8%), signaling the market anticipates an imminent catalyst. The nearest expiry implies a ±12.5% move. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (0.1%) and the put/call OI ratio is 0.78, so the elevated vol is not directionally bearish; the market is pricing a large move without a strong directional lean, consistent with a binary competitive/earnings outcome. ATM IV has collapsed from 334.9% at the mid-June snapshot as the prior event resolved and the stock rallied 27.9%. Short interest of 11.4% of float with 7.5 days to cover is elevated but not crowded enough to create a dominant squeeze dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($1,800–2,400).** VantageScore adoption proves slow and partial. Lenders offer it but continue pulling FICO Scores due to embedded underwriting workflows, secondary-market convention, and the tri-merge requirement, so actual FICO score-pull volume and pricing hold. FICO retains the bulk of its mortgage economics while cards, auto, and Falcon compound. Scores growth normalizes to 15–20% rather than collapsing, Software Platform continues at 49% ARR growth, and debt-funded buybacks shrink the share count further. On next-year EPS of $54.55 growing to ~$65+ and a 35x multiple, the stock re-rates toward $2,000+. The antitrust claim settles or is dismissed at summary judgment.

**Bear Case ($707–900).** VantageScore captures material mortgage-score share through 2026 as top originators optimize for cost, and TransUnion's aggressive pricing forces FICO to either cut mortgage Scores prices or lose volume. The Scores growth engine reverses: the same +60% quarters become flat-to-negative as pricing power evaporates. The surviving Sherman Act monopolization claim gains traction, adding legal overhang and potential settlement cost. On a lower Scores earnings base, the levered balance sheet (–$2.1B equity, $3.6B debt at 6%+) magnifies the EPS hit, and the stock derates toward the analyst low target near $707. Software fails to fill the gap as legacy ARR keeps shrinking.

The defining feature of this setup is that both cases rest on the same reported facts: the earnings engine (mortgage Scores pricing) and the existential risk (regulator-enabled competition triggered by that pricing) are inseparable. The outcome is not knowable from current fundamentals; it requires observing VantageScore's actual share capture over the next several quarters.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** This is a genuinely two-sided binary, not a confident directional view. The business is exceptional and the near-term momentum is strong, but the competitive threat is real, dated, and not yet quantifiable in reported results.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of Scores revenue growth holding above 20% with no evidence of mortgage-score pricing concessions, confirming VantageScore adoption is not eroding the franchise.
- Dismissal or favorable summary judgment on the Sherman Act Section 2 claim, removing the antitrust overhang.
- Open-market insider buying by the CEO or CFO, which would signal management conviction that the drawdown is an over-reaction (currently absent; the CEO cancelled a sell plan but did not buy).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any quarter showing Scores revenue growth decelerating below 10% or evidence of negotiated mortgage-score price cuts to retain CRA volume.
- Disclosed loss or material renegotiation of any of the three CRA relationships.
- Adverse antitrust ruling or a settlement figure large enough to materially impair the balance sheet at current leverage.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report (Scores volume and pricing detail) is the primary reassessment point, with subsequent quarters through 2026 needed to observe VantageScore's actual share trajectory. The options market's backwardation confirms a near-term catalyst is being priced.

## Conclusion

Fair Isaac is a business of extraordinary quality being repriced by a specific, dated regulatory event that attacks its most valuable asset. The March 2026 quarter demonstrates the franchise still commands monopoly economics in mortgage scoring. The FHFA's July 2025 authorization of the CRA-owned VantageScore 4.0, now offered by all top-30 originators and undercut on price by FICO's own distribution partners, has opened a competitive channel that did not exist before, and the surviving Sherman Act monopolization claim targets the exact pricing lever driving the reported growth.

The drawdown from the December peak, occurring while estimates rose and results beat, means the market is discounting a prospective threat that has not yet appeared in the numbers. That is precisely the situation where analytical discipline requires distinguishing a mispricing from a legitimate repricing, and here, given the binary competitive outcome, the wide analyst target dispersion, the absence of insider buying, and a disclosed short seller, we cannot confidently call it either. The score has declined across consecutive HOLDs, driven almost entirely by the deterioration in competitive position and management-alignment signals, and the new data confirms rather than reverses that trend. We maintain HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The correct posture is to wait for the next two-to-three quarters of Scores volume and pricing data, which will resolve whether VantageScore adoption is a slow, survivable erosion or a genuine repricing of the franchise. Own the quality if you already hold it; do not add capital until the competitive trajectory is measurable in reported results.
