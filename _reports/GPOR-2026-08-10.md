## Executive Summary

Gulfport Energy is a 1.0 Bcfe/d, natural-gas-weighted Appalachian producer with a secondary SCOOP position, and until roughly two quarters ago it was one of the cleanest per-share compounding stories in small-cap energy. Since March 2022 the company retired approximately 8.6 million shares — around a third of its post-emergence equity — for about $1.2 billion at a weighted average of $135.09, against a current price of $164.84. Cumulative total return since the 2021 bankruptcy emergence indexed to 306 versus a peer group at 148. That is a real accomplishment and it is why the stock trades where it does.

The problem is that the mechanism has broken down in 2026. Adjusted free cash flow — the company's own selected performance measure, which ran $199M to $361M annually from 2021 through 2025 ($325M in 2025) — fell to **$6.4M in Q2 2026**. In the same quarter the company repurchased $70.0M of stock and drew the revolver to $280.0M, taking funded debt to $930.0M against $1.1M of cash. First-half repurchases totaled $242.8M at an implied average near $193/share, roughly 15% above today's price. Base capital guidance was raised to ~$430M while Q2 volumes ran 4.3% below the prior year and roughly 7% below the annual guide, and management layered on a new $140M discretionary acreage program plus an Ohio state land lease (~4,700 net acres, ~16 net locations, 2027 operations). Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 8.7% in 30 days and 10.7% in 90 days; the company has missed the quarterly EPS consensus four times in a row, by 12.5% most recently.

Layered on top is a leadership discontinuity that a screen would not capture. CEO John Reinhart departed March 6, 2026 with no successor in place; an "Office of the Chairman" governed until May 28. Domenic Dell'Osso, formerly CEO of Chesapeake and then Expand Energy, was appointed effective May 28, an unusually senior hire for a $2.9B company. CFO Michael Hodges then resigned July 31, effective August 5, and **Dell'Osso is currently serving as interim principal financial officer** while reaffirming a full-year production guide that requires a 12-15% second-half volume increase off the Q2 base. Our conclusion is HOLD on genuine uncertainty rather than on conviction: proved reserve value net of debt roughly covers the market capitalization, leverage is low at about 1.2x, and short interest and options positioning show no crowding, but probability-weighted expected return from $164.84 is close to zero, and the next two quarters are a clean binary test the buyer has no reason to front-run.

## Company Overview

Gulfport operates two asset areas. Utica/Marcellus in eastern Ohio comprises roughly 223,000 net Utica reservoir acres plus ~35,000 net acres identified for Marcellus development, and produced ~841 MMcfe/d in 2025, or 81% of total volumes. SCOOP in central Oklahoma comprises ~74,000 net acres and produced ~197 MMcfe/d, 19% of the total. Total proved reserves at year-end 2025 were 4,253 Bcfe with PV-10 of $3,622M and a standardized measure of $3,403M, computed at SEC 12-month averages of $66.01/Bbl WTI and $3.39/MMBtu Henry Hub. The company employs 245 people, pays no dividend, and its stated strategy is maintenance-level development funded by operating cash flow with surplus directed to share repurchases and incremental leasehold.

Two features of the capital structure follow directly from the November 2020 Chapter 11 and May 2021 emergence: a very small share count (17.68M shares, hence a $165 price on a $2.9B cap), and concentrated legacy creditor holders. Silver Point Capital, the most visible of these, sold 759,740 shares in early March 2026 at approximately $204.22 and a further 84,416 shares directly to the company for about $17.2M, leaving roughly 2.61M shares — approximately 14.7% of outstanding — as a disclosed overhang.

A legacy 24.5% interest in Grizzly Oil Sands (Alberta) has been suspended since 2015, carries no proved reserves, and has been diluting since the company stopped funding capital calls in 2019. Value it at zero.

## Financial Analysis

**Volumes have not grown in three years.** Production was 384.8 Bcfe (2023), 385.8 Bcfe (2024) and 379.2 Bcfe (2025) — down 1.7% last year. The 43% revenue increase in 2025 to $1,323.5M was entirely price: realized gas rose from $2.02/Mcf to $3.12/Mcf. Reported GAAP net income (+$1,471M in 2023, −$261M in 2024, +$428M in 2025) is dominated by deferred-tax and derivative fair-value movements and carries little information about operations; the useful series is adjusted free cash flow and unit costs.

**Q2 2026 is the pivot.** Production of 962.8 MMcfe/d was down 4.3% year over year, with oil down 46.4% to 4,203 Bbl/d and NGLs down 12.8%. Realized gas ex-hedge fell 16.5% to $2.48/Mcf, roughly $0.41 below the quarter's Henry Hub benchmark — a ~14% locational discount consistent with Appalachian takeaway constraints. Adjusted EBITDA was $179.1M and operating cash flow $149.9M against $148.6M of base capital plus $40.3M of discretionary acreage, producing $6.4M of adjusted free cash flow. Roughly $40M of the compression is the discretionary land program, which is reversible; the balance is price and volume, which is not.

**The funding of shareholder returns has changed character.** First-half capital incurred was $270.4M base plus $79.7M of discretionary acreage; first-half buybacks were $242.8M. Funded debt went from $650M of 2029 notes plus a modest revolver draw at year-end 2025 to $930.0M at June 30, 2026, with cash at $1.1M. Net debt is therefore approximately $928.9M. Putting full-year 2026 together — ~$430M base capex, the $140M discretionary program, and ~$39.5M of prior-year land carryover — implies roughly $610M of total capital against an estimated FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $740-790M, a reinvestment rate of roughly 77-82% before cash interest of about $67M (≈$0.18/Mcfe on ~375 Bcfe) and cash taxes. There is very little left for repurchases. The revolver is the plug.

We want to be precise about what this is and is not. Net debt to estimated FY2026 EBITDA of roughly **1.2x** against a credit facility ceiling we read as 3.5x maximum net funded leverage is not a solvency problem, and liquidity of $772.4M is ample. The GAAP current ratio of approximately 0.58 at June 30 is an artifact of accrued capital and revenue-payable balances normal to the E&P model, and the facility's current-ratio test is defined to include unused commitments, under which the company reports compliance. What has changed is discipline: Gulfport spent above cash flow in both 2025 and the first half of 2026, and it did so partly to buy stock at prices ~15% above the current market.

**Buyback arithmetic.** The program-to-date average of $135.09 is 22% below today's price. The marginal purchases are worse: 1.8M shares at $188.65 in 2025 and ~1.3M shares at ~$192.91 in H1 2026 — roughly 3.1M shares for ~$579.1M now worth about $511M, an unrealized shortfall near $69M or 11.9%. The charitable reading is that mark-to-market against one quote is not the correct test of a repurchase; the disciplined reading is that buying stock at $193 while drawing a revolver is a materially higher-risk version of the strategy that worked at $110. The authorization has $336.8M remaining and **expires December 31, 2026**, making re-authorization an observable near-term decision point.

**Cost stack is creeping.** FY2025 unit costs: LOE $0.22/Mcfe, production taxes $0.08, transportation/gathering/processing/compression $0.95, total cash field cost $1.25. Q2 2026: LOE $0.23, taxes $0.08, TGP&C $0.97, recurring cash G&A $0.13, interest $0.18. TGP&C at $0.97 against realized revenue of $2.99/Mcfe means **32.4% of every revenue dollar goes to moving the molecule**, and because firm transport is contractual and largely volume-insensitive, that cost is close to fixed per unit in a downturn — negative operating leverage. TGP&C has risen from $0.91 (2023-24) to $0.95 (2025) to $0.97; LOE from $0.18 to $0.23.

**Reserves.** Headline 2025 replacement was 185% (3,969 opening → +701 extensions, −38 net revisions, −379 production → 4,253). Composition matters: +255 Bcfe came from the higher SEC gas price and +161 Bcfe from working-interest gains earned through leasing, while −185 Bcfe came from development schedule changes and −129 Bcfe from PUD well-design changes. Stripping the price benefit, replacement was roughly 118%. PUDs are 43.5% of proved reserves with a 28% conversion rate in 2025, and nine Utica plus four SCOOP PUD locations were moved beyond the five-year SEC window. The disclosed ±10% price sensitivity moves PV-10 by ±$0.77B — about 21% of the total for a 10% price change, which swamps any of the valuation premiums discussed below.

## Growth Analysis

There is no volume growth thesis here, and the near-term question is whether volumes hold. Management reaffirmed FY2026 production of 1.030-1.055 Bcfe/d in the August 3 release while reporting 962.8 MMcfe/d for Q2. Working from the Q2 actual (962.8 MMcfe/d × 91 days = 87,610 MMcfe) and a first-half average near 979.7 MMcfe/d, the low end of guidance requires second-half volumes averaging roughly **1,079 MMcfe/d — up 12.1% versus Q2 actual** — and the midpoint requires ~1,104 MMcfe/d, up 14.7%.

The stated mechanism is specific and mechanically credible: 12 Utica/Marcellus wells were completed in Q2 but only 8 turned to sales, and two recently completed wet-gas Utica pads near the Ohio state land are expected to drive a meaningful liquids increase in the second half. Completion-to-sales lags do produce step-function volumes. But a required 12-15% half-over-half ramp with the year more than half elapsed, delivered by a CEO three months into the job who is also acting CFO, is a demanding bar, and the base capex guide was raised to the top of the range while volumes ran below plan, meaning first-half capital efficiency came in worse than budgeted.

The forward-looking growth Gulfport is actually buying is inventory, not volumes. The combined Ohio state land plus discretionary program is claimed to add more than 20% to total Utica net inventory and extend the development runway by more than 2.5 years — approximately 56 net locations for roughly $220M of land capital, or about $3.9M per net location before drilling and completion. That is a market-clearing price for core Utica wet gas, and it implicitly discloses that the pre-existing runway was shorter than investors assumed, a reading corroborated by the 2025 reserve report pushing 13 PUD locations outside the five-year window.

Consensus is moving the wrong way. Current-year EPS of $23.15 is down 8.7% in 30 days and 10.7% in 90 days; next year at $27.99 is down 4.1% and 8.8%. Four consecutive misses (−2.9%, −0.9%, −4.5%, −12.5%) argue against treating current estimates as conservative. The estimate-revision signal here is negative, which also disqualifies the wide gap to consensus price targets (mean $227.25, median $232.50, low $186) from being read as a floor. When estimates are falling and the company has missed four straight quarters, target gaps of that size historically close through target cuts rather than price appreciation.

## Valuation Assessment

Reconciling the inputs: 17,683,866 shares × $164.84 = **$2,915M** market cap. Net debt of $928.9M gives an enterprise value of approximately **$3,844M**. Note that the supplied profile shows a trailing and forward P/E of 6.21 that does not reconcile with reported EPS of $21.48 at this price (which implies 7.67x); we use our own arithmetic throughout. On consensus, the stock trades at **7.1x** current-year EPS and **5.9x** next-year EPS, on estimates that are still being cut.

**Against reserves:** EV ÷ PV-10 of $3,622M = **1.06x**. Equivalently, PDP PV-10 of $2,291M plus PUD PV-10 of $1,331M less net debt of $928.9M gives $2,693M of proved-reserve equity value against a $2,915M market cap — an **8% premium**, meaning the market is assigning approximately zero net value to unproved inventory at the SEC deck of $66.01 WTI / $3.39 Henry Hub. That framing cuts both ways: it means the ~56 net locations being acquired are not being credited, but it also means there is no proved-value cushion at that deck, and Q2 realized gas of $2.48 sat well below the $3.39 deck used to compute it.

**Against EBITDA:** annualizing Q2's $179.1M gives $716M and 5.4x, which we label a sensitivity rather than a forecast — Q2 is the seasonally weakest realized-gas quarter and management guides higher liquids volumes in the second half. A normalized derivation from FY2025 (commodity sales $1,323.5M less $474.0M field cash costs less ~$49.3M cash G&A plus ~$56.9M settled hedge gains) yields roughly $857M and 4.5x; applying realizations closer to Q2's $3.39/Mcfe including hedges on 370-375 Bcfe yields $740-790M and **4.9-5.2x**. The range is therefore roughly 4.5-5.4x depending on deck.

**Against peers**, the supplied comparison set is close to unusable on P/E — TALO (−6.0), NOG (−4.4), CRC (−9.7) are loss-making and CRGY at 62.4x skews the median. The one clean read is Comstock Resources at 7.8x, a similarly gas-weighted producer, versus GPOR at 7.1x current-year and 5.9x next-year. GPOR is in line to modestly cheaper, with a better balance sheet and worse volume trajectory. Peer 52-week drawdowns of 16-52% confirm that the entire gas-weighted cohort has de-rated; GPOR's 27% decline from the $225.78 November high is mid-pack, not idiosyncratic distress.

Our scenario math, anchored on $164.84:

- **Base $175 (+6.2%)**: 5.1x on ~$790M of normalized EBITDA = $4,029M EV, less ~$950M of year-end net debt, over ~17.5M shares.
- **Bull $225 (+36.5%)**: 5.75x on ~$860M (winter gas strip recovery plus a delivered H2 liquids ramp), less ~$920M net debt, over ~17.3M shares after modest buybacks.
- **Bear $118 (−28.4%)**: 4.5x on ~$680M (sustained sub-$2.50 realized gas, guidance miss, continued outspend), less ~$1,000M net debt.

Weighting base 45%, bull 25%, bear 30% gives an expected return near +3%. For a 33%-volatility gas equity with no dividend, that is not a return that justifies new capital. The valuation is undemanding but not asymmetric, and the cheapness reflects the risk in a business with falling estimates and a rising cost stack rather than compensating for it.

## Competitive Landscape

Gulfport is a price-taker with a narrow moat. Its own 10-K concedes that it competes with companies possessing greater resources, many vertically integrated into midstream and marketing. At ~1.0 Bcfe/d it sits well below Expand Energy (which Dell'Osso ran), EQT (now integrated with Equitrans midstream), Ascent and Antero. The practical consequence is visible in the $0.97/Mcfe TGP&C line: Gulfport rents takeaway rather than owning it, and roughly a third of realized revenue per Mcfe leaves through that line.

Two genuine differentiators. First, lateral length: Q2 2026 Utica/Marcellus wells drilled averaged 17,300 feet and wells turned to sales averaged 17,800 feet, at the leading edge of the basin, and longer laterals per unit of surface capital are the primary remaining source of Appalachian capital-efficiency gain. Second, the liquids-rich wet-gas window, where NGL and condensate realizations lift revenue per Mcfe above a dry-gas peer. Utica LOE of $0.20/Mcfe in 2025 is genuinely low-cost.

The offsetting evidence is that the liquids window is where the volume problem originates. Utica oil grew from 255 MBbl (2023) to 847 MBbl (2024) to 1,729 MBbl (2025), and Q2 2026 oil volumes then fell 46% year over year — consistent with the steep first-year declines characteristic of that window. Because oil near $86/Bbl is worth roughly $14/Mcfe against gas at $2.48/Mcf, liquids mix drives revenue per Mcfe disproportionately, and it is precisely the liquids-led ramp on which second-half guidance depends.

SCOOP is the strategic question mark: 19% of production, 22% of proved reserves, total cash field cost of $1.36/Mcfe versus $1.21 in Appalachia, LOE $0.31 versus $0.20, volumes down 27% in two years (98.7 → 77.8 → 72.0 Bcfe), and low working interests (20.86% NRI / 25.82% WI) implying limited operational control. It looks like a harvest asset and a plausible divestiture candidate under a new CEO funding Appalachian inventory, though the alternative reading — that SCOOP's ~2.4 MMBbl of annual NGL production diversifies away from Appalachian basis — is defensible.

One clarification on the narrative sometimes attached to Gulfport: the 2025 shift in revenue mix toward liquids is commodity-mix change, not customer diversification. Direct concentration disclosure shows a single purchaser at 12%, 15% and 14% of sales across 2023-2025 — essentially flat, and unnamed in the filings.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity price and Appalachian basis.** With 91% of Q2 volumes as gas, the equity is a levered claim on Henry Hub net of a $0.40+/Mcf discount. The disclosed ±$0.77B PV-10 sensitivity for a ±10% price move is the cleanest quantification available; at the low deck only 132 PUDs remain PV-10 economic. Hedging cuts both ways: in Q2, gas hedges added $0.52/Mcf while **oil hedges cost $13.50/Bbl**, so Gulfport realized $72.36/Bbl including hedges against $85.86 unhedged, surrendering most of a geopolitically driven oil spike. The current macro backdrop — Hormuz disruption lifting crude while US gas realizations fell 16.5% year over year — is close to the worst configuration for this particular hedge book.

**The second-half volume ramp.** A required 12-15% increase off the Q2 base is the cleanest falsifiable near-term test and the single largest driver of Q3/Q4 estimates.

**Royalty post-production-deduction litigation.** A January 2025 putative class action in the Southern District of Ohio alleges royalty underpayment, and an April 2025 Sixth Circuit ruling went against a different Ohio operator on post-production-cost deductions under a market-enhancement clause — precedent in the same circuit. Scaling the exposure: FY2025 Utica/Marcellus TGP&C of $0.96/Mcfe on 307.1 Bcfe is $294.8M, and the implied Utica royalty share of ~18.4% (1 − 50.25% NRI ÷ 61.58% WI) puts order-of-magnitude annual exposure near $50-55M with multi-year retroactive damages potentially a multiple of that. This is a scale check on disclosed averages, **not a liability estimate** — class definition, lease-language mix, affected vintages and any accrual are all undisclosed, and the precedent involved different lease language. Separately disclosed "deep rights" trespass claims seek damages for periods after May 2021, and are therefore not discharged by the bankruptcy.

**Governance and disclosure risk.** No permanent CFO; the CEO is acting as principal financial officer three months into the role while reaffirming a demanding guide and launching a $140M land program. The March 2026 8-K describing the prior CEO's departure was not obtained, so its characterization and any severance terms are unverified. We also flag two disclosure ambiguities that materially affect the 2026 capital estimate: whether the $140M discretionary program is inclusive of or incremental to the $40.3M deployed in Q2, and whether the ~$83M Ohio state land lease sits inside or outside that $140M.

**Section 382 tension.** Gulfport carries large federal NOLs (we read approximately $1.5B, with a ~$398M net deferred tax asset and ~$82M valuation allowance — figures to re-verify) and discloses an ownership-change risk factor. Large buybacks mechanically raise the ownership percentages of remaining 5% holders, one input to a 382 calculation. We assert no proximity to a limitation; Silver Point's selling has been reducing concentration in the other direction. It is a tension a standard model would miss, not an identified impairment.

**Other.** Silver Point's residual ~14.7% stake is a disclosed overhang, sold down at ~$204 in March. Well-control insurance limits of $25M single-well / $37.5M multi-well pad are modest for an operator running 18,000-foot laterals, and the 10-K concedes coverage may be inadequate.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 33.6% against 33.1% one-year realized is fair — the options market is not charging a premium for the Q3 catalyst, and the 34th-percentile IV reading within the current chain suggests no unusual demand for protection. Put/call skew is balanced (−0.4% at three months, though −2.6% at six months), and put/call open interest of 0.33 is call-tilted, which does not corroborate a bearish thesis. The term structure is in mild backwardation (33.6% near versus 30.6% at six months), consistent with a dated near-term event rather than a regime shift; the nearest expiry prices a ±4.9% move and the September tenor ±8.3%. Short interest of 8.3% of float with 2.2 days to cover is uncrowded. Positioning is neutral-to-mildly-constructive and cheap relative to realized volatility, which argues against an active bearish stance and supports HOLD rather than REDUCE. Anyone holding through the Q3 print can buy protection at close to fair value.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $225 (+36.5%)

A proven large-cap operator is rebuilding inventory depth at a price the market is crediting at zero. Dell'Osso ran a company an order of magnitude larger; his arrival plausibly widens the strategic aperture to include a SCOOP divestiture funding Appalachian inventory, larger-scale consolidation, or a sale of the company. The second-half ramp lands as described — 12 Q2 completions with only 8 turned to sales plus two wet-gas pads is a mechanically sound explanation — restoring volumes above 1.05 Bcfe/d and lifting realized revenue per Mcfe through liquids mix. Winter gas strip recovery on top of that pushes FY2027 EBITDA toward $860M, and at 5.75x with net debt reduced toward $900M the equity is worth about $225. The $220M of land capital converts into 56 net locations at ~$3.9M apiece, and the December buyback authorization is renewed with the stock 15% below where the company was buying in the first half. Balance-sheet risk is genuinely low at 1.2x against a 3.5x covenant with $772M of liquidity, and analyst targets ($186 low, $263 high) sit entirely above spot.

### Bear Case — $118 (−28.4%)

A maintenance-capital business has been distributing capital it could not spare, and is now paying market-clearing prices to buy back a runway it had already shortened. The evidence is on the page: volumes flat for three years and down 4.3% in Q2; oil down 46% as the liquids window declines hard; TGP&C up from $0.91 to $0.97 and LOE from $0.18 to $0.23; 43.5% of proved reserves undeveloped at a 28% conversion rate with 13 PUD locations pushed beyond five years; ex-price reserve replacement of ~118%; adjusted FCF of $6.4M against a $325M 2025 comparison; funded debt up to $930M with $1.1M of cash; four consecutive EPS misses and current-year consensus down 10.7% in 90 days. If the second-half ramp lands short of 1.03 Bcfe/d, estimates get cut again, the buyback authorization lapses or is not renewed at December 31, and the revolver keeps absorbing the gap. On $680M of EBITDA at 4.5x with net debt near $1.0B, the equity is $118. Royalty litigation against adverse circuit precedent is an unquantified overlay, and the market pays an 8% premium to proved value at a deck above current realized gas — there is no asset cushion at spot prices.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (hold rationale: uncertain). Probability-weighted expected return from $164.84 is roughly +3%, approximately fair. Existing owners are compensated for waiting by low leverage, ample liquidity, and proved-reserve backing near the market capitalization; prospective buyers have no entry signal until the H2 volume ramp, the CFO appointment, and the buyback re-authorization resolve. This is not a REDUCE because the downside asymmetry is bounded by the balance sheet and by uncrowded positioning, and not an ACCUMULATE because the estimate-revision trend, the four-quarter miss streak, and the CFO vacancy remove any margin of safety at a mid-single-digit EBITDA multiple.

**Scenario anchors from $164.84:** base $175 (+6.2%); bull $225 (+36.5%); bear $118 (−28.4%).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 production at or above ~1,050 MMcfe/d with liquids volumes recovering — confirming the completion-to-sales mechanism and putting the low end of the FY guide in reach.
2. A permanent CFO appointed and the December 31, 2026 buyback authorization renewed with repurchases resuming below $170, funded by operating cash flow rather than revolver draws (i.e., adjusted FCF back above ~$50M/quarter).
3. Strategic action that monetizes the harvest asset — a SCOOP divestiture at or above a ~$400M implied value, redeployed into Appalachian inventory or debt reduction.
4. A price near $135-140 (roughly 4.4x normalized EBITDA and a discount to proved PV-10 net of debt) would create the margin of safety absent today, independent of operating news.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Q3 volumes below ~1,000 MMcfe/d with the FY guide cut, or the FY guide reaffirmed a second time on arithmetic that no longer works — either outcome damages the new team's disclosure credibility.
2. Revolver drawn above ~$400M, or two consecutive quarters of adjusted FCF below $25M while capital guidance rises again.
3. A disclosed accrual or class certification in the S.D. Ohio royalty case implying exposure above ~$150M.
4. Buyback authorization allowed to lapse at December 31, 2026 without replacement, which removes the last leg of the per-share compounding thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (early November) is the decisive checkpoint, with the CFO announcement and any acreage-program disclosure as interim reads. A December reassessment around the buyback authorization expiry is the second scheduled decision point.

## Conclusion

Gulfport at $164.84 trades at roughly 4.9-5.4x EV/EBITDA, 7.1x current-year consensus EPS, and about 1.06x proved PV-10 — an unexceptional multiple for a sub-scale Appalachian gas producer with modest leverage. Nothing in the valuation is stretched; nothing in it is compelling either, because the equity story that generated a 306 TSR index since emergence was per-share compounding funded by free cash flow, and in 2026 that funding disappeared. Adjusted free cash flow of $6.4M in a quarter that also saw $70M of repurchases and a revolver draw to $280M is the fact that matters most, and it arrived alongside a 10.7% ninety-day cut to current-year consensus, a fourth consecutive earnings miss, the departure of both the CEO and CFO within five months, and a reaffirmed volume guide requiring a 12-15% second-half increase.

The counterweight is real. Leverage near 1.2x against a 3.5x covenant with $772M of liquidity means this is a capital-allocation question rather than a solvency question, and Dell'Osso's arrival raises the ceiling on strategic outcomes for a company that had been running a narrow playbook. The market is paying nothing for unproved inventory precisely as the company spends ~$220M acquiring roughly 56 net locations. If the second-half ramp lands and the winter strip cooperates, $225 is reachable.

We are not willing to underwrite that ahead of the evidence with estimates falling, no permanent CFO, and an oil hedge book that gave back $13.50/Bbl into the current crude environment. Hold what you own, buy protection at close to fair implied volatility if you need it, and let the Q3 print and the CFO hire decide whether this becomes a $135 accumulation or a $225 re-rating.
