## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer, operating from a structural cost advantage via North American natural gas. The stock has appreciated 73% over the past year, with the most violent leg occurring in March 2026 as the Iran conflict physically removed significant Middle Eastern nitrogen supply from the global market. The prior BUY recommendation at $130.98 on April 6 has experienced a -7.4% drawdown to $121.32, driven by ceasefire-related volatility and a single-session oil crash that temporarily depressed fertilizer equities.

The thesis has changed in the favorable direction. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised upward 83% over 90 days (from $7.13 to $13.06), and next-quarter estimates have surged 72%. On the revised $13.06 estimate, CF trades at ~9.3x current-year earnings, cheaper than the ~14.5x implied at the prior entry using the old consensus. The supply destruction driving these revisions — Hormuz transit reduction, Iran's petrochemical infrastructure damage, and China's continued fertilizer export restrictions — involves physical infrastructure that takes 3-5 years to rebuild, providing a multi-year pricing floor beneath CF's earnings power.

The thesis carries real risks. CEO Will sold $20M+ in stock at $106-$131, insiders are net sellers across the C-suite, Yazoo City production is offline until Q4 2026 at earliest, and Henry Hub gas averaged $6.32/MMBtu in early 2026 versus $3.53 in 2025. These factors constrain the upside and explain the score decline in Management Quality. But the magnitude of the supply shock, the durability of the infrastructure destruction, and the non-discretionary nature of fertilizer demand make this one of the clearest commodity beneficiary setups available.

## Company Overview

CF Industries operates nine nitrogen manufacturing complexes across the U.S., Canada, and the UK. Its Donaldsonville, Louisiana facility is the world's largest ammonia production complex, representing approximately 40% of the company's total ammonia capacity. The product mix includes anhydrous ammonia, granular urea, UAN solutions, ammonium nitrate, and diesel exhaust fluid.

The company produced 10.1 million gross ammonia-equivalent tons in 2025 and shipped 19.1 million product tons. Revenue is split between agricultural applications (the majority) and industrial uses (emissions control, explosives, chemicals). A strategic partnership with CHS Inc., which holds an ~11% noncontrolling interest in CF Industries Nitrogen (covering 5 of 6 U.S. plants), provides downstream distribution advantages.

CF's clean energy pivot is anchored by two projects: the completed Donaldsonville CCS unit (capturing up to 2M metric tons of CO2 annually, enabling ~1.9M tons of low-carbon ammonia) and the Blue Point joint venture with JERA (35%) and Mitsui (25%), a $3.7B low-carbon ATR ammonia plant targeting 2029 production.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins recovered strongly in 2025.** Revenue rose 19% to $7.1B, driven entirely by pricing — average selling prices increased 19% to $372/ton while volume grew only 1%. Gross margin expanded to 38.5% from 34.6%, and operating income reached $2.3B. EPS of $8.97 represented 33% growth, amplified by a 10% reduction in diluted share count from buybacks.

**Cash generation is exceptional.** Operating cash flow of $2.75B (+21% YoY) and free cash flow of $1.8B (25.4% FCF margin) fund both growth investments and aggressive shareholder returns. The company returned $1.67B to shareholders in 2025 ($1.34B buybacks + $326M dividends), with $1.72B remaining under the 2025 buyback authorization.

**The balance sheet is strong despite optically high leverage.** Net debt of ~$1.27B against $2.75B OCF yields a net debt/OCF ratio of 0.46x. The 2.91x debt/equity ratio reflects accumulated buyback-driven equity reduction, not financial distress. No debt matures until 2034. Cash on hand of $1.98B plus a $750M undrawn revolver provides $2.7B of liquidity. The Altman Z-Score of 1.37 is a formula artifact of compressed equity; the company's Piotroski partial score (5/5) and 3.37x current ratio confirm robust health.

**Cost headwinds are real but partially self-hedging.** Henry Hub gas averaged $6.32/MMBtu in January-February 2026 versus $3.53 in 2025. Each $1/MMBtu increase adds ~$300M to annual production costs. However, global nitrogen prices are set by marginal producers (European plants paying 3-4x NA gas prices), meaning higher gas tends to correlate with higher fertilizer prices. The margin compression is real but partial: CF's cost structure remains far below the global marginal cost of production.

**Capex is elevated.** 2026 guidance of ~$1.3B ($550M existing operations + $600M Blue Point JV + $150M scalable infrastructure) is well above the ~$500M maintenance baseline. Blue Point construction costs total $3.7B, of which CF's 40% share is ~$1.48B over the construction period plus $550M of owned infrastructure. At current FCF levels, this is fundable without incremental debt.

## Growth Analysis

**The estimate revision trajectory is the most important near-term signal.** Current-year EPS consensus has risen from $7.13 to $13.06 over 90 days (+83%), with the next-quarter estimate surging from $2.30 to $3.96 (+72%). These are among the most aggressive upward revisions in the entire Materials sector. The 30-day acceleration (current-year +51% in the last 30 days alone) indicates the revision cycle is still gaining momentum, not plateauing.

**Earnings beat history supports confidence in these estimates.** Over the last four quarters, CF beat consensus three times (by 25% and 29% in two quarters) with one miss of 10%. The pattern suggests management and the street have historically underestimated CF's earnings power in strong pricing environments.

**The supply disruption driving revisions is structural.** Iran's petrochemical infrastructure (including facilities at Asaluyeh and Lavan) has sustained damage that requires 3-5 years to rebuild. Hormuz transit has been reduced to approximately 15 ships per day under an Iranian crypto-toll system that prevents insurance normalization for 1-3 years minimum. China maintains fertilizer export restrictions. Egypt and Russia experienced production outages throughout 2025. The FAO has issued famine warnings linked to fertilizer supply disruption, and 86% of Gulf-to-East Africa nitrogen flows have ceased.

**Nitrogen demand is non-discretionary.** Farmers must fertilize to maintain yields. Unlike discretionary commodities where demand destruction can occur at high prices, fertilizer demand is highly inelastic over 1-3 year horizons. Reduced application rates eventually cause yield declines that create their own price feedback loop through food inflation.

**Clean energy provides a multi-year optionality layer.** The Donaldsonville CCS unit is operational and generating 45Q tax credits (~$42M in H2 2025). Blue Point targets 2029 production. JERA's committed offtake for Japanese power generation and Mitsui's steel production volumes represent genuinely new demand verticals for ammonia outside agriculture. However, ATR technology has not been deployed at this scale, and execution risk is meaningful.

**Yazoo City offline until Q4 2026.** Management guided 2026 gross ammonia production at ~9.5M tons versus 10.1M in 2025, reflecting the November 2025 incident. This reduces volume but does not impair the pricing thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

**On revised estimates, CF is cheap.** At $121.32 with current-year EPS consensus of $13.06, the stock trades at 9.3x. The forward P/E using the data provider's trailing figures shows 13.3x, but the relevant metric is the dramatically revised consensus. The peer median P/E of 28.3x is not directly comparable (packaging and specialty chemicals have different margin and cyclicality profiles), but even against its own historical range, sub-10x on peak-cycle estimates with multi-year supply disruption support is not expensive.

**The dividend yield of 1.6% is modest but supplemented by aggressive buybacks.** The combined shareholder yield (buybacks + dividends) was 9.1% in 2025. If buybacks continue at the 2025 pace, total shareholder return from capital allocation alone would be ~8-9% annualized.

**The next-year EPS estimate of $9.50 implies the market expects normalization.** This is the critical bear-case number: if the Iran supply disruption resolves faster than expected, 2027 earnings revert toward $9-10 range, putting the stock at ~12.8x. That is still reasonable for a well-capitalized commodity producer at the low end of the global cost curve.

**Analyst targets reflect deep disagreement.** The consensus mean of $116 (below current price) with BofA at $103 and UBS at $140 represents a 47-point range — unusual for a $18.5B market cap company. This wide dispersion reflects genuine uncertainty about the duration and magnitude of the supply disruption.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's competitive position is the strongest element of the thesis. The company's advantages are structural and durable:

1. **Cost position:** North American natural gas at $3-6/MMBtu versus European gas at $10-15/MMBtu and Asian LNG at $12-18/MMBtu places CF firmly in the first quartile of the global cost curve. Even at $6.32 Henry Hub, CF's production cost per ton is a fraction of the marginal producer's.

2. **Scale:** The world's largest ammonia production network provides operating leverage and purchasing power. The Donaldsonville complex alone exceeds the total capacity of most competitors.

3. **Distribution:** Extensive terminal, pipeline, and transportation infrastructure across the U.S. Midwest creates a logistics moat that new entrants cannot easily replicate.

4. **Import dependency:** North America is a net nitrogen importer. Domestic producers sell into a market where import parity sets the floor price, meaning CF captures the spread between its low NA gas cost and the higher delivered cost of imports.

5. **First-mover in low-carbon ammonia:** The Donaldsonville CCS unit and Blue Point JV position CF ahead of competitors in the emerging low-carbon nitrogen market, with committed offtake from JERA and Mitsui.

Key competitors include Nutrien (NTR, diversified across potash/phosphate/nitrogen), LSB Industries (smaller scale), OCI (European-listed, Middle Eastern operations under geopolitical pressure), and Koch Fertilizer (private).

## Risk Assessment

**Natural gas cost spike (HIGH).** Each $1/MMBtu above the 2025 average compresses annual EBITDA by ~$200-250M net of pricing offsets. While partially offset by higher global fertilizer prices (as marginal producers face worse cost inflation), the magnitude at current Henry Hub levels represents a meaningful margin headwind.

**Ceasefire/de-escalation risk (HIGH).** The Iran ceasefire, while currently fraying, represents the most immediate threat to CF's elevated pricing. A durable peace settlement with full Hormuz normalization would reduce global nitrogen prices toward 2024 levels over 6-12 months, cutting current-year EPS estimates by 30-40%. However, structural barriers to normalization (crypto-toll, insurance market closure, infrastructure destruction) make a rapid return to pre-conflict pricing unlikely even under a peace scenario.

**Blue Point execution risk (MEDIUM).** The $3.7B project uses ATR technology at unprecedented scale. Cost overruns, tariff impacts on $1.2B of imported materials, and the Louisiana moratorium on new Class VI carbon sequestration wells all represent execution risks. If low-carbon ammonia demand fails to develop as expected, the investment becomes partially stranded.

**Insider selling (MEDIUM-HIGH).** The breadth and magnitude of C-suite selling suggest management views the stock as fairly to fully valued at recent levels. No insider purchases were observed. While some selling reflects vest-and-sell patterns, this is a genuine negative signal.

**Yazoo City production loss (MEDIUM).** The revenue impact from reduced 2026 production is partially offset by pricing strength, but it reduces CF's ability to capitalize on the supply-constrained environment.

**Cyclicality (MEDIUM).** CF's earnings can decline 60%+ in trough years (2024 EPS of $6.74 versus 2022 peak). Current pricing reflects supply disruption, not permanent demand growth. If global capacity additions materialize as announced, the next trough could be sharper than consensus expects.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($150-160)

Global nitrogen supply remains structurally constrained through 2027 as Hormuz normalization requires 1-3 years for insurance markets alone, and destroyed production infrastructure takes 3-5 years to rebuild. China maintains fertilizer export restrictions. CF's current-year EPS of $13+ proves conservative as fertilizer prices continue rising through H2 2026. Blue Point construction proceeds on schedule. Low-carbon ammonia premiums are validated by commercial sales. At 12x $13 EPS, the stock reaches $156.

### Bear Case ($85-95)

A comprehensive Middle East peace settlement restores Hormuz shipping within 3-6 months. Global nitrogen prices revert toward 2024 levels. China relaxes fertilizer export restrictions. Henry Hub gas remains above $6/MMBtu, compressing margins from both the cost and revenue sides. 2027 EPS estimates converge on $6-7, putting the stock at 13-14x normalized earnings — approximately $90. Blue Point faces cost overruns.

The 11x multiple is justified by CF's position as the lowest-cost global producer with structural supply tailwinds lasting 2-3 years. Upside from current: +20%.

**Bull case target: $160.** 12x $13.06 current-year EPS with upside from further estimate revisions as H2 2026 pricing captures full war-period passthrough. Upside from current: +32%.

**Bear case target: $90.** 13x normalized $7 EPS in a peace/normalization scenario. Downside from current: -26%.

**Risk/reward ratio: 1.23:1 base case, approximately even bear/bull.**

**Timeframe: 6-12 months.** The thesis resolves as: (1) Q1 2026 earnings report (expected late April/early May) confirms elevated pricing and provides updated 2026 guidance; (2) the Iran ceasefire either stabilizes or collapses, establishing the pricing regime for H2 2026; (3) 2026 natural gas costs are embedded in actuals versus estimates.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. **Hormuz shipping normalizes above 30 ships/day** (current: ~15), indicating insurance markets are reopening and supply disruption is abating.
2. **Current-year EPS consensus declines below $10.00** (from current $13.06), indicating the pricing environment is deteriorating faster than the structural thesis implies.
3. **Global urea benchmark prices decline below $350/ton for two consecutive months**, indicating the supply disruption is no longer supporting elevated pricing.

### What Has Materially Changed Since Prior BUY

The validation system correctly flagged the -6.7% drawdown since the April 6 BUY at $130.98. Three material changes justify maintaining the BUY:

1. **Estimates have surged.** Current-year EPS consensus rose from $8.66 to $13.06 in 30 days (+51%). The stock is cheaper on forward earnings at $121 than it was at $131 using the old consensus. The prior entry was at ~15x the then-consensus; the current price is at ~9.3x the current consensus.

2. **The ceasefire is fraying.** The April 8-9 oil crash that hit CF's stock (-7.4%) was driven by a ceasefire announcement, but within 48 hours three destabilizing events occurred (Saudi pipeline attack, Iran breach accusations, Qassem killing). The structural barriers to Hormuz normalization remain fully intact.

3. **Physical crude divergence confirms supply constraints are real.** European and African physical crude benchmarks hit records while futures traded at ~$97. This physical-futures gap validates that the supply disruption is not a financial market artifact — actual buyers of physical commodities are paying record premiums because barrels and cargoes cannot transit Hormuz normally.

The -6.7% drawdown reflects ceasefire-driven volatility in a structurally favorable setup, not thesis deterioration.

## Conclusion

CF Industries combines the world's best cost position in nitrogen production with a multi-year supply disruption that has driven the most aggressive estimate revisions in the Materials sector. At 9.3x revised current-year estimates with $1.8B in annual free cash flow, no debt maturities until 2034, and non-discretionary demand characteristics, the risk/reward favors continued ownership.

The thesis is constrained by meaningful risks: heavy insider selling, Yazoo City offline for most of 2026, elevated natural gas costs, and the possibility that geopolitical de-escalation reverses the pricing environment. The 20pp IV premium in options confirms the market recognizes these risks.

On balance, the structural supply thesis — grounded in physical infrastructure destruction and insurance market barriers that operate on multi-year timelines regardless of diplomatic outcomes — dominates the near-term risks. BUY is maintained with a $145 base case target and a $90 bear case floor, representing approximately 1.2:1 upside/downside from current levels.
