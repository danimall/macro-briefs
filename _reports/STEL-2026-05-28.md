## Executive Summary

Stellar Bancorp is a $1.9 billion market cap Texas-based regional bank operating 52 branches primarily in Houston and Beaumont. The company was formed through the 2022 merger of Allegiance Bancshares and CBTX and has since delivered consistent operating performance: 4.24% net interest margin, 35.7% noninterest-bearing deposits, net charge-offs of just 7 basis points, and regulatory capital ratios well above minimums.

The dominant fact for any STEL investment thesis is the pending acquisition by Prosperity Bancshares (NYSE: PB), announced January 27, 2026, for 0.3803 PB shares plus $11.36 cash per STEL share. Regulatory approvals were received April 22, 2026, and the shareholder vote occurred May 27, 2026. Expected close is approximately July 1, 2026. At $37.29, STEL trades at an implied ~4-6% discount to deal value (which fluctuates with PB's stock price around $72-74, implying ~$38.7-$39.5 per STEL share).

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation despite a score above 6.2: the merger-arb spread of 4-6% over ~5 weeks, while annualizing attractively at 40-60%+, carries asymmetric downside risk in the current macro regime. With the exchange ratio of 0.3803 meaning STEL holders bear PB equity risk for the ~5-week interim period, any macro shock that drives Prosperity's stock below ~$68 would compress the deal consideration below current STEL levels. A standard merger-arb fund would size this at 2-5% with offsetting PB hedges — but as a standalone long equity position, the risk/reward is insufficient for a conviction BUY given the narrow absolute spread and macro vulnerability.

## Company Overview

Stellar Bancorp operates Stellar Bank, a Houston-headquartered commercial bank focused on small-to-medium businesses and professionals. The bank holds $10.9 billion in total assets, $7.59 billion in loans (50.7% CRE, 20.6% C&I), and $8.98 billion in deposits. The company employs approximately 1,174 people across 52 banking centers concentrated in the Houston MSA (35 locations) and Beaumont MSA (16 locations).

The bank's franchise quality is demonstrated by its deposit composition: 35.7% noninterest-bearing deposits with essentially zero brokered deposits and a cost of deposits of just 1.66% in Q1 2026 (declining from 1.90% a year earlier). These metrics reflect deep commercial banking relationships that produce sticky, low-cost funding.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current Metrics:**
- Price: $37.29
- Trailing P/E: 18.2x (using EPS of $1.99 per Phase 1 data)
- Price/Tangible Book: 1.73x ($37.29 / $21.62)
- Dividend yield: ~1.6%

**Peer Comparison:**
- Peer median P/E: 13.7x
- Peer median yield: 3.0%
- STEL premium to peers: 33% on P/E, 47% lower yield

The premium to peers is entirely explained by the acquisition — Prosperity is paying approximately 1.8x tangible book and ~18x earnings for a high-quality Houston franchise. Absent the deal, STEL would likely trade at 13-15x earnings ($27-$30), consistent with peer valuations.

**Merger-Arb Math:**
- Deal terms: 0.3803 × PB price + $11.36 per STEL share
- At PB = $72: deal value = $27.38 + $11.36 = $38.74 → 3.9% gross spread
- At PB = $74: deal value = $28.14 + $11.36 = $39.50 → 5.9% gross spread
- At PB = $68: deal value = $25.86 + $11.36 = $37.22 → -0.2% (breakeven)

The break-even PB price is approximately $68.20. If PB trades down 5-8% from current levels (plausible in a macro selloff), the spread evaporates.

## Competitive Landscape

Stellar possesses genuine competitive advantages in its Houston franchise:

1. **Deposit quality**: 35.7% noninterest-bearing deposits exceeds most community bank peers (typically 25-30%)
2. **Cost of funds**: 1.69% total cost of funds reflects relationship-based banking vs. rate-dependent deposit gathering
3. **NIM superiority**: 4.24% tax-equivalent NIM is well above the ~3.3-3.5% industry average
4. **Market density**: 35 branches in Houston provides meaningful commercial banking coverage

These attributes explain why Prosperity was willing to pay a premium to STEL's pre-announcement price. The combined entity will be Texas's second-largest bank by deposits with ~$54B in assets.

The risk in the competitive positioning is geographic concentration: virtually all revenue from Houston and Beaumont, creating energy sector cyclicality and Gulf Coast catastrophe exposure.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk — Merger Execution (Low probability, moderate impact):**
All regulatory approvals are received. Shareholder vote was held May 27, 2026. The remaining risk is limited to material adverse change ("MAC") clauses or previously undisclosed issues. Probability of deal failure at this stage is historically <3% for approved transactions with all conditions substantially met.

**Secondary Risk — PB Stock Price Decline During Close Period:**
Since approximately 70% of consideration is PB stock (0.3803 shares at ~$72-74), STEL holders are effectively long PB for the ~5-week close period. A 10% decline in PB would reduce deal value from ~$39 to ~$36.6, below current STEL price. In the current environment — where regional bank sentiment is fragile and rate volatility pressures bank equity valuations — this is a non-trivial risk for the 5-week holding period.

**CRE Concentration (Relevant for ongoing holders converting to PB shares):**
61.5% CRE/CRE construction concentration, including $365M in office loans (9.5% of CRE portfolio). In a stagflationary macro with CRE refinancing stress building, this concentration could weigh on the combined entity post-close.

**Credit Quality Trajectory:**
NPAs up 17% in Q1 2026 ($60M → $70M). While starting from low levels and well-covered by reserves, the direction is unfavorable in a rising-rate environment with elevated geopolitical uncertainty.

**Short Interest:** At 2.0% of float with 3.1 days to cover, short interest is minimal — consistent with a near-closing acquisition where downside is structurally limited.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:**
Deal closes on schedule (~July 1, 2026), Prosperity trades at or above current levels, and STEL holders receive ~$38.7-$39.5 in mixed consideration. The 4-6% gross spread over 5 weeks annualizes at 40-60%+. Post-close, the combined Prosperity entity benefits from Stellar's high-quality deposit base and strong Houston relationships, potentially re-rating higher as integration synergies materialize.

**Bear Case:**
A macro shock (rate signal from FOMC, geopolitical escalation impacting energy equities, or broader CRE stress narrative) drives PB stock down 10-15% before close. Deal consideration compresses to $36-37, and STEL trades flat-to-down through close. Alternatively (very low probability <3%), a previously undisclosed issue triggers a MAC clause or other closing condition failure, sending STEL back to standalone valuation of $27-30.

## Conclusion

Stellar Bancorp is a high-quality Texas community bank franchise that is being absorbed into Prosperity Bancshares in a transaction that has cleared all substantive hurdles. The standalone business is sound: strong NIM, excellent deposit quality, controlled credit costs, and fortress capital levels. The acquisition premium appropriately reflects these attributes.

At $37.29, the stock offers a modest 4-6% merger-arb spread over ~5 weeks. This is attractive on an annualized basis but carries meaningful equity risk through the Prosperity exchange ratio in a macro environment characterized by rate volatility, geopolitical uncertainty, and regional bank sentiment fragility. The spread is too narrow to justify a BUY recommendation for new capital entering a position without a corresponding PB hedge. For existing holders, the position is worth maintaining through close to capture the remaining spread — hence a HOLD for income/spread capture. The 1.6% dividend yield during the holding period adds modestly to total return.

The critical variable is Prosperity's stock price over the next 5 weeks. If PB trades above $72, holders are rewarded. If PB trades below $68.20, the spread turns negative. A 5-8% equity decline over 5 weeks for a regional bank is well within normal variance in the current macro regime.
