## Executive Summary

Stride Inc is the largest dedicated virtual K-12 education operator in the United States, serving 244,500 students across 31+ states through a technology-enabled "school-as-a-service" model. FY2025 delivered 18% revenue growth, operating margins expanding to 15%, and strong free cash flow generation, but a software platform implementation disruption in FY2026 caused enrollment growth to decelerate to ~2%, triggering a 47% stock decline from the 52-week high of $171.

The stock now trades at approximately 11x forward P/E with a net cash balance sheet (~$320–440M net cash depending on treatment of finance leases). The central investment question is binary: is the software disruption a temporary operational setback or evidence of structural fragility in a company that grew too fast? Application volumes remaining near record levels, withdrawal trends normalizing to historical rates, and Middle-High School Career Learning growing 22% on a 9-month basis all point toward the former. At current prices, investors are being paid to wait for the growth recovery that management is guiding toward.

The 22.6% short interest creates additional asymmetry — if FY2027 enrollment metrics confirm re-acceleration, forced short covering would amplify the move.

## Company Overview

Stride provides an integrated online education platform to virtual and blended K-12 public schools. Revenue is derived primarily from per-pupil state funding. The company operates through two segments:

- **General Education** ($1.45B FY2025, 60% of revenue): Core K-12 curriculum delivery across 89 schools in 31 states
- **Career Learning** ($957M FY2025, 40% of revenue): Career-focused programs in IT, healthcare, and business for middle/high school students, plus a declining adult learning business (Galvanize, Tech Elevator, MedCerts at ~$80M and shrinking)

Contracts average 5+ years with auto-renewal provisions. The model generates high operating leverage — once curriculum is developed and technology deployed, incremental students add revenue at high marginal contribution.

## Growth Analysis

**The Deceleration:**

FY2025 enrollment grew 20.4% (178K → 234K). Q3 FY2026 enrollment grew 1.8% to 244.5K. This deceleration was driven by a specific operational event (software platform implementation causing elevated withdrawals), not by weakening demand.

Evidence the demand base remains intact:
1. Application volumes near record levels despite reduced marketing spend
2. Withdrawal trends returned to historical norms by Q2 FY2026
3. Revenue per enrollment continues growing (+2.9% YoY in Q3)
4. Middle-High School Career Learning growing 22.1% on a 9-month basis through the disruption (total Career Learning segment grew 17.6%, with Adult Learning declining 29.1%)

**Forward Growth Outlook:**

Management guided FY2026 revenue of $2.49-2.52B (3.5-4.8% growth). Consensus for FY2027 is $8.76 EPS (vs. $8.20 current year), implying ~7% EPS growth. If enrollment growth re-accelerates to even 8-10% in FY2027 (half the FY2025 rate), the operating leverage embedded in the model would generate materially higher earnings than current consensus reflects.

**Middle-High School Career Learning as Structural Growth Engine:** This sub-segment grew 34.6% in FY2025 and continues at 22.1% on a nine-month basis even through the disruption. It benefits from structural tailwinds: employer demand for career-ready graduates, school choice legislation expansion, and demographic growth in alternative education pathways. BLS projects 6% growth through 2033 in occupations requiring non-degree postsecondary education.

**Adult Learning is a Known Drag:** Galvanize/Tech Elevator/MedCerts revenues declined from $119M to $80M over two years, producing a $59.5M impairment. Total Career Learning growth of 17.6% (nine months FY2026) reflects the drag from Adult Learning's 29.1% decline. At <4% of total revenue, this segment's decline is overwhelmed by Middle-High School Career Learning growth.

## Competitive Landscape

Stride's competitive position is strong and stable:

- **Scale advantage:** 244.5K students across 145+ schools is multiple times larger than any single direct competitor in virtual K-12
- **Multi-year contracts:** 5+ year terms with auto-renewal create high revenue visibility and switching costs for school boards
- **Regulatory moat:** Operating in 31+ states requires deep expertise in diverse regulatory frameworks — a significant barrier to entry
- **Data advantage:** 24 years of virtual education data informs curriculum development, intervention strategies, and operational optimization

The primary competitive risk is not from other virtual operators but from political/regulatory opposition to virtual charter schools and potential funding reduction. However, school choice legislation has been expanding across states, providing a structural tailwind.

The software disruption raises a question about platform investment adequacy — whether Stride under-invested in technology modernization while pursuing aggressive enrollment growth. The ~$58M in annual capitalized curriculum and software development may need to increase.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Platform Stabilization Uncertainty (HIGH):** While management says withdrawals have normalized, FY2027 enrollment growth is the definitive test. If Q1 FY2027 (reported ~late October 2026) shows enrollment growth below 5%, the "temporary" narrative weakens significantly.

2. **Government Funding Dependency (MODERATE):** Revenue is 95%+ derived from state/district per-pupil funding. Legislative hostility to virtual charter schools in key states (California has 13 schools) could impair revenue without warning.

3. **Short Interest at 22.6% (ELEVATED RISK — BOTH DIRECTIONS):** At 9.6 days to cover, this represents either informed investors who believe the platform disruption is structural, or crowded shorts vulnerable to a squeeze if growth recovers. Resolution depends on FY2027 data.

4. **Convertible Note Dilution (Sept 2027):** The $420M converts will likely be settled at a price above the $86.17 capped call ceiling, creating ~2.7M shares of dilution (6% of outstanding). Management may choose to settle in cash using their substantial cash reserves.

5. **Estimate Revisions Negative:** Current quarter estimates are down 6.5% over 90 days; next year estimates down 3.6%. This is the weakest element of the bull case — estimates are declining, not rising.

## Consumer Discretionary BUY Gate Assessment

This stock is classified under Education Services. Note: classification varies by provider — some taxonomies place LRN in Consumer Defensive (Education & Training Services) rather than Consumer Discretionary. Addressing the four required conditions:

**(a) Positive sequential comparable revenue growth:** Q3 FY2026 revenue of $629.9M declined slightly from Q2 FY2026 revenue of $631.3M (approximately -0.2% sequentially). However, on a year-over-year basis, Q3 FY2026 grew 2.7% and 9-month revenue grew 7.4% YoY. Middle-High School Career Learning grew 22.1% YoY on a 9-month basis. **Sequential quarterly revenue growth is marginally negative (-0.2%). PARTIALLY SATISFIED on YoY basis; NOT SATISFIED on strict sequential basis.**

**(b) FCF yield >= 4%:** ~9.8% FCF yield. SATISFIED.

**(c) Consumer confidence not in declining trend:** While Michigan Consumer Sentiment is at 53.3, Stride's revenue model is tied to per-pupil state funding, not consumer discretionary spending. Parents are not paying tuition — school districts and charter boards pay from public education budgets. Demand for virtual education correlates with parent dissatisfaction with traditional schooling (60% considered switching in Jan 2025 survey), not consumer confidence. The consumer confidence headwind is not directly applicable to this business model. ADDRESSED — business model insulated from consumer confidence.

**(d) Positive estimate revisions over trailing 90 days:** Current quarter estimates are DOWN 6.5% over 90 days; next year estimates down 3.6%. This condition is NOT satisfied. However, the negative revisions reflect analysts incorporating the known software disruption into their models, and the magnitude is modest (3-6%). The trailing EPS beat of 18.9% (Dec quarter) and 26.0% (Sep quarter) demonstrates the company's capacity to exceed expectations when operations normalize. The recent Q3 beat of 2.9% (modest) on the most disrupted quarter further supports the thesis that worst-case is being priced.

**Net assessment:** 2 of 4 conditions clearly satisfied; condition (a) fails on strict sequential measurement (-0.2% Q/Q) though YoY growth remains positive; condition (d) fails on modest negative revisions (-3.6% next year) that reflect a known, likely temporary disruption. The ~10% FCF yield, net cash balance sheet, and 22%+ Middle-High School Career Learning growth provide margin of safety to justify a BUY despite these headwinds.

## Conclusion

Stride is a dominant-position, expanding-margin, net-cash, strong-FCF business trading at a depressed valuation due to a specific, likely temporary operational disruption. The ~10% FCF yield provides downside protection while Middle-High School Career Learning's structural growth trajectory provides the upside catalyst. At ~11x forward earnings with net cash and $411M in buyback authorization remaining, the market is pricing significant uncertainty from what appears to be a one-year software implementation setback.

The 22.6% short interest and negative estimate revision trend are legitimate concerns that justify position sizing at 2-3% rather than high-conviction levels. The asymmetry is favorable: if growth re-accelerates (base case), the stock re-rates 30-60% higher. If growth permanently impairs (bear case), the FCF yield and buyback limit downside to ~25-30%.

**Recommendation: BUY at 2-3% portfolio weight.** Primary risk is timing — growth recovery may take 2-3 quarters longer than expected.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The BUY-gate sequential revenue growth condition is not satisfied (Q3 FY2026 revenue declined ~0.2% sequentially from Q2 FY2026), FY2025 FCF is ~$373M (not $431M) when using the company's own capex definition including capitalized software/curriculum, and FY2026 FCF trajectory through nine months is uncertain. These corrections narrow the margin of safety supporting the recommendation.
