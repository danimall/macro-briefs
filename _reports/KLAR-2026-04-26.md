## Executive Summary

Klarna is a Swedish-founded, UK-domiciled digital bank and BNPL platform that IPO'd on the NYSE in September 2025 at $40/share and now trades at ~$14, a 75% decline from its 52-week high of $57.20. The collapse was driven by two events: a 102% YoY spike in credit loss provisions disclosed in Q3 2025 (weeks after IPO), and a Q4 2025 earnings report on February 19, 2026 that showed full-year losses (EPS -$0.79) despite record revenue of $3.5B (+25% YoY). The stock fell 27% on earnings day on 45.4 million shares — approximately 10x average daily volume.

The bull case rests on structural advantages: a banking license enabling 94% deposit-funded lending ($9.5B deposits), 118 million active consumers, 966,000 merchants, AI-driven cost efficiency that tripled revenue per employee since 2022, and a banking pivot where banking consumers generate 3.5x the revenue of average users. At 1.5x trailing revenue, the stock is cheap relative to peer Affirm at 7x+. The bear case centers on credit losses growing faster than revenue in the highest-growth segments, material weaknesses in internal controls (unaudited until 2026), a $302M gap between adjusted and IFRS operating profit, an active securities class action, and management guidance pointing to slower growth with higher provisions. The credit loss estimation weakness is the most consequential: it is the single most important accounting judgment a lending company makes, and Klarna has admitted the systems governing that estimation are materially weak.

The stock has been rated AVOID in three consecutive assessments. Valuation has improved meaningfully (the stock is 75% cheaper than at IPO), but the fundamental risks that drove each prior downgrade have intensified rather than resolved. Upgrading to HOLD requires at minimum two consecutive quarters of declining credit loss ratios and remediation of the material weakness. Neither has occurred.

## Company Overview

Klarna operates a two-sided network connecting 118 million active consumers and 966,000 merchants across 45 markets. The core business model charges merchants approximately 3% of transaction value for facilitating payments through BNPL, installment plans, and increasingly, traditional banking products. Klarna processes ~3.4 million transactions per day with $127.9B in GMV for FY2025.

Revenue streams include merchant fees (primary), consumer financing revenue from installment plans and "Fair Financing" products, advertising ($180M in 2024, 6% of revenue), and banking services (savings accounts, Klarna Card with 4.2M users, debit cards). The company holds a Swedish banking license (now operating under UK regulatory framework), which allows it to fund 94% of BNPL loans through consumer deposits, a structural cost-of-capital advantage over debt-funded competitors like Affirm.

Klarna re-domiciled from Sweden to the UK in May 2024 and listed on the NYSE in September 2025. It reports under IFRS, which creates comparability challenges for U.S.-focused institutional investors.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is strong and accelerating.** FY2025 revenue was $3.5B (+25% YoY), with Q4 delivering the first billion-dollar quarter at $1.082B (+38% YoY). U.S. revenue grew 58% YoY in Q4, confirming the U.S. as the primary growth engine. GMV reached $127.9B (+22% FY, +32% Q4). Take rate expanded from 2.3% in 2022 to approximately 2.7% in 2024-2025.

**Profitability reversed.** After briefly achieving a $21M net profit in 2024 (the first in company history), Klarna swung back to losses in 2025 with EPS of -$0.79. Adjusted operating profit declined from $181M (2024) to $65M (2025) despite 25% revenue growth, representing adjusted margin compression from ~6.5% to 1.9%. The IFRS operating loss was -$121M in 2024 versus the $181M adjusted profit — a $302M gap largely attributable to stock-based compensation and restructuring.

**Credit losses are the critical variable.** Consumer credit losses rose from $353M (2023) to $495M (2024), then spiked 102% YoY in Q3 2025. Q4 showed sequential improvement with credit loss provisions declining from 0.72% to 0.65% of GMV. However, credit losses as a percentage of revenue (~17.6% in 2024) already exceed Sales & Marketing spend. The banking expansion into Fair Financing (165% GMV growth, longer duration) deepens credit cycle sensitivity.

**Deposit costs are eroding the funding advantage.** Deposit interest costs surged from $63M (2022) to $343M (2024), a 5.4x increase. In a sustained higher-rate environment, this trend compresses the margin benefit of deposit-funded lending versus ABS-funded competitors.

**Estimate revisions tell a clear story.** Current-year EPS estimates have collapsed from $0.61 (90 days ago) to $0.07 — an 88.6% downward revision. Next-year estimates fell from $1.13 to $0.68 (-39.7%). The 30-day trend shows modest stabilization (+11.5% current year, -3.6% next year), but this follows massive markdowns. Five analysts cover the current quarter, expecting -$0.14 EPS versus the -$0.01 estimate from 90 days ago.

**Earnings track record is limited and poor.** In two quarters as a public company, Klarna has one in-line result and one massive miss (Q4 FY2025: -$0.12 vs -$0.02 expected, -424% surprise). This is a single-digit-quarter dataset, but the magnitude of the miss undermines confidence in management's ability to forecast its own credit dynamics.

## Growth Analysis

Klarna's topline growth is unambiguously strong. 25% FY revenue growth, 38% Q4, 58% U.S., with active consumers up 28% and merchants up 42%. Banking consumers doubled to 15.8M, generating $107 in revenue per user versus $30 for average consumers.

The growth quality is the issue. Revenue growth is partly driven by take-rate expansion (higher fees and financing charges), which draws regulatory scrutiny. The fastest-growing product, Fair Financing, is the most credit-intensive. The banking pivot, which is the core long-term thesis, inherently transforms Klarna from a tech-adjacent payment platform (high multiples) into a lending-heavy digital bank (lower multiples). Investors must decide which growth trajectory the business is actually on.

AI-driven cost efficiency is real. Revenue per employee reached $1.24M, up 3.6x since 2022, after headcount was cut 49% while revenue doubled. AI handles 62% of customer service inquiries and saves $7M/quarter in marketing. These are genuine operational improvements, but the easy gains (customer service automation, CRM optimization) may already be captured. The harder gains (credit underwriting AI, fraud detection) carry higher stakes if they fail.

Consensus estimates imply Klarna reaches approximately breakeven in the current fiscal year ($0.07 EPS) and $0.68 EPS in the following year. If achieved, that would imply ~20.7x forward P/E on the out-year, reasonable for a high-growth financial services company. But the 89% downward revision in current-year estimates over 90 days suggests the market has limited visibility into the path to profitability.

## Valuation Assessment

At $14.07 and ~$5.3B market cap, Klarna trades at approximately 1.5x trailing revenue. Peer Affirm trades at 7x+ revenue. A comparable BNPL platform at one-fifth the revenue multiple is the core bull case.

The discount is warranted by measurable factors. Affirm has higher gross margins and a cleaner credit quality narrative despite also being unprofitable. Klarna's IFRS reporting, material weakness in internal controls, and securities class action all justify a structural discount. The $302M adjusted-to-IFRS gap raises legitimate questions about whether the adjusted numbers are meaningful.

The analyst consensus price target is $22.67 mean / $20.00 median, with a range of $14.00 to $45.00. The low target equals the current price, suggesting the most bearish analyst sees no further downside. The consensus implies 60%+ upside. Sell-side coverage is 13 BUY/Strong BUY versus 6 HOLD and 0 SELL, a distribution that likely reflects pre-Q4 positioning that hasn't fully adjusted.

On a forward basis, if Klarna achieves $0.68 EPS next year, the stock trades at ~20.7x. But that estimate has been cut 40% in 90 days, and the company's only full-quarter earnings report missed by 424%. The confidence interval around $0.68 is wide.

Valuation has improved meaningfully from prior assessments as the stock dropped from the mid-$40s to $14. The stock is no longer expensive on a revenue basis. But "cheap on revenue" for a lending company with rising credit losses and unaudited internal controls differs materially from "cheap on revenue" for a high-margin SaaS company.

## Competitive Landscape

On the advantage side: 118M consumers and 966K merchants create significant network effects; the banking license enables deposit-funded lending at lower cost than ABS-funded competitors; AI-driven operational efficiency is measurable; and the advertising/commerce platform ($180M, high-margin) is a differentiated revenue stream with no credit risk.

On the vulnerability side: traditional banks and credit card companies are launching their own installment products, compressing BNPL margins over time. Apple discontinued Apple Pay Later but retains the option to re-enter through partnerships with massive distribution advantages. Affirm is growing faster with a better credit quality perception. The European regulatory environment (Basel III, PSD2, evolving BNPL regulation) is tightening, while U.S. CFPB enforcement has loosened under the current administration, a temporary dynamic that could reverse.

The competitive position is stable to slightly deteriorating. The network is large and growing, but the moat (merchant switching costs, consumer stickiness) is not as deep as payment networks like Visa or Mastercard. BNPL remains a feature that can be replicated, and Klarna's differentiation increasingly rests on the banking pivot, which is credit-intensive and carries different competitive dynamics.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit Quality (Critical).** The class action allegation that Klarna was financing fast-food purchases for financially unsophisticated consumers, if substantiated, would fundamentally undermine the "responsible alternative to credit cards" narrative. Q4 sequential improvement (0.72% → 0.65% of GMV) provides one data point of stabilization, but absolute credit losses continue growing with the loan book.

**Material Weakness (High).** The S-1 disclosed material weaknesses in internal controls related to user access, system changes, revenue recognition, and credit loss estimates. External audit of internal controls is not expected until 2026. Institutional investors face elevated model risk when relying on reported numbers.

**Securities Class Action (Moderate-High).** Hagens Berman filed suit alleging IPO documents misled investors about credit quality and lending practices. Lead plaintiff deadline passed February 20, 2026. Discovery could surface damaging internal communications about underwriting standards. Even without liability, the overhang and management distraction are real costs.

**Off-Balance Sheet Exposure (Moderate).** $867M in securitized loans do not appear on the balance sheet. Synthetic securitization transfers credit risk while keeping loans on books. This obscures the full extent of credit exposure and creates analytical uncertainty.

**Insider Alignment (Moderate).** Pre-IPO insiders sold $800M+ in secondary transactions. CEO sold $56M, co-founder sold $73M, Sequoia sold $135M. SBC was $50M+ in 2024 and rising. For a company that has been public for approximately 7 months and lost 75% of its value, the pre-IPO selling pattern and ongoing dilution create an alignment question.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($22-25, ~60% upside):** Q4 revenue beat and sequential credit improvement mark the trough. Banking consumers continue doubling, Fair Financing takes share from revolving credit, and advertising revenue scales as a high-margin overlay. AI efficiency gains continue compressing the cost base. Credit losses stabilize at 0.60-0.65% of GMV through 2026. Klarna reaches sustainable profitability by H2 2026 (consistent with the $0.68 next-year EPS estimate). The valuation discount to Affirm narrows from 5x to 2-3x. Material weakness is remediated, and the class action settles for immaterial amounts.

**Bear Case ($8-10, ~30-40% downside):** Credit losses re-accelerate as the U.S. consumer weakens under sustained high inflation and interest rates. Fair Financing and Klarna Card expose the company to credit-cycle losses that BNPL's short-duration structure previously avoided. The class action survives a motion to dismiss, and discovery surfaces aggressive underwriting practices. Material weakness is not remediated on schedule, triggering institutional selling and potential regulatory action. Deposit costs continue rising, eroding the funding advantage. The stock trades to 1.0x revenue or below ($9-10), in line with troubled lending companies.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The next identifiable catalyst is the Q1 FY2026 earnings report (likely May 2026). If credit loss provisions re-accelerate after one quarter of sequential improvement, the stabilization narrative collapses. The class action discovery process could also surface material information, though the timeline is less predictable.

**Timing window:** 4-8 weeks to next earnings. Carrying costs are moderate given the stock's high borrow cost environment (10.6% SI, 5 days to cover).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 10.6% of float with 5 days to cover is elevated but not extreme. Squeeze risk is moderate given the stock is already down 75%, which limits the magnitude of short-covering rallies. However, 13 of 19 covering analysts rate the stock BUY/Strong BUY, so any positive catalyst could trigger a violent short squeeze on a stock with 5 days to cover.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options IV is cheap relative to HV (67.7% vs 73.4%), and the contango structure suggests no imminent catalyst is priced. The call-heavy OI (0.62 put/call ratio) is a mild warning for shorts. Recent put buying (1.41 put/call volume ratio) is modest versus the call-heavy OI base.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $8-10 versus current $14.07 implies 30-40% downside. Squeeze risk to $18-20 (prior consolidation zone in February) implies 30-40% upside risk. Risk/reward is approximately 1:1, which does not meet the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The stock has already declined 75% from its 52-week high, the valuation is not demanding on revenue multiples, and the risk/reward ratio on the short side is approximately 1:1. The bearish thesis is correct on fundamentals — credit quality, governance, and profitability are all problematic — but the price already reflects significant impairment. The 10.6% short interest, 5 days to cover, and overwhelmingly bullish analyst consensus create meaningful squeeze risk. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (for upgrade from AVOID to HOLD/BUY):**

1. **Two consecutive quarters of declining credit loss provisions as a percentage of GMV** (Q4 showed one quarter of improvement from 0.72% to 0.65%; a second quarter at or below 0.65% would establish a trend).
2. **Remediation of material weakness in internal controls**, confirmed by external audit or management disclosure in SEC filings.
3. **IFRS operating profit in any quarter**, demonstrating that profitability is achievable on the accounting basis that reflects actual economics, not adjusted metrics excluding SBC and credit losses.

**Timeline:** These criteria are unlikely to be satisfied before the Q2 FY2026 report (approximately August 2026 at the earliest). The material weakness remediation could take through calendar year 2026.

**Price level re-entry consideration:** If the stock trades below $10 (0.9-1.0x trailing revenue) with stable credit metrics, the risk/reward improves sufficiently to warrant HOLD consideration even without full satisfaction of the criteria above, given that extreme valuation compression can compensate for elevated fundamental risk.

## Conclusion

Klarna has genuine structural assets — a banking license, 118M consumers, 966K merchants, measurable AI efficiency gains, and a promising banking pivot. Revenue growth at 25-38% is strong. At 1.5x trailing revenue, the stock is statistically cheap relative to peers.

The stock has been rated AVOID for three consecutive assessments. Each time, valuation improved (the stock got cheaper), but the fundamental risks driving the rating intensified. Credit losses spiked 102% in Q3, the only full-quarter earnings report missed by 424%, full-year profitability reversed from positive to EPS -$0.79, and the adjusted-to-IFRS gap widened. A securities class action was filed. The material weakness remains unremediated.

The bull case requires trusting management's credit loss projections at a time when (a) management's only public forecast missed by a factor of 5, (b) the internal controls governing credit loss estimation are materially weak, and (c) the company is aggressively expanding into higher-duration, higher-balance lending products during a period of rising consumer stress. The rating remains AVOID. The valuation score has improved, reflecting the 75% price decline, and if credit quality stabilizes over two consecutive quarters with concurrent internal controls remediation, the thesis changes. Until then, the data does not support taking the risk.
