## Executive Summary

Cheniere Energy is the largest U.S. LNG exporter, operating ~45 mtpa of liquefaction capacity across Sabine Pass (Louisiana) and Corpus Christi (Texas), with a contracted revenue backlog of $290.6 billion. The business model is structured around long-term, take-or-pay SPAs, with approximately 90% of anticipated production contracted through the mid-2030s. FY2025 revenue grew 27% to $20.0 billion, operating income rose 49% to $9.1 billion, and operating cash flow was $5.5 billion.

The stock has moved from ~$193 in late December 2025 to ~$266 today, driven by geopolitical disruption to global energy supply chains and structural tightening of global LNG markets. This analysis addresses the prior BUY recommendation issued on March 20 at ~$281, which is currently down ~5.6%. The material change since that call is threefold: (1) the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports creates a structurally tighter LNG supply environment than the earlier ceasefire implied, strengthening the demand thesis; (2) Stage 3 construction continues ahead of schedule with Train 5 producing first LNG in February 2026; and (3) next-year EPS estimates have been revised up 11% over 90 days ($14.71 → $16.34), confirming underlying earnings momentum independent of geopolitical premium. The stock's pullback from $300+ to $266 provides a better entry than the prior recommendation.

Cheniere's competitive position — first-mover U.S. LNG infrastructure, Bechtel EPC execution, Gulf Coast feedstock access, and 44+ mtpa of expansion optionality — remains unmatched domestically. The primary risks are GAAP earnings volatility from derivative mark-to-market accounting, an approaching global LNG supply glut in 2027-2028, and elevated leverage (4.4x D/E) inherent to project finance. Insider selling across multiple C-suite officers in late March 2026 near $291-300 warrants monitoring but appears opportunistic given the 43% YTD run at the time.

## Company Overview

Cheniere Energy operates two LNG liquefaction and export terminals: Sabine Pass (6 operational trains) and Corpus Christi (multiple operational trains plus Stage 3 expansion). In FY2025, the company loaded a record 670 LNG cargoes totaling 2,439 TBtu. The company's revenue is generated primarily through two contract types:

- **Fixed-fee SPAs**: Customers pay a fixed liquefaction fee per MMBtu regardless of whether they lift cargo ($107.7 billion contracted).
- **Variable-fee SPAs**: Typically 115% of Henry Hub, passing gas procurement costs to buyers ($182.9 billion contracted at forward curves).

This structure provides base-load cash flow stability while retaining upside from widening international-to-domestic gas price spreads. Counterparties include major investment-grade utilities, national oil companies, and trading houses across Europe, Asia, and Latin America.

**Growth Pipeline:**
- Corpus Christi Stage 3 (7 midscale trains, >10 mtpa): Trains 1-4 completed in 2025; Train 5 producing LNG as of Feb 2026; Trains 6-7 expected in 2026. Ahead of schedule, on budget.
- CCL Trains 8 & 9 (FID June 2025, ~5 mtpa): Bechtel EPC at ~$2.9 billion fixed price.
- SPL Expansion (~20 mtpa) and CCL Expansion (~24 mtpa): FERC applications filed; pre-FID. These would roughly double total capacity to 75+ mtpa over the next decade.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue of $20.0 billion represented a 27% increase from $15.7 billion in FY2024, driven by $2.9 billion in higher pricing (Henry Hub increased), $1.2 billion in higher volumes from Stage 3 ramp, and $417 million in derivative gains. Operating income reached $9.1 billion (45.6% margin), up 49% from $6.1 billion.

Net income attributable to Cheniere was $5.3 billion ($24.13 diluted EPS), substantially above the $3.3 billion earned in FY2024. The Q4 2025 EPS of $10.68 against a $3.94 consensus estimate represented a 171% surprise — the largest in recent history.

A critical nuance: $3.6 billion of FY2025 pre-tax income came from derivative fair value changes on IPM agreements. These are non-cash and subject to reversal. Operating cash flow of $5.5 billion — essentially flat with 2024's $5.4 billion — is a cleaner measure of underlying economics than reported earnings.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

Total debt stands at $22.8 billion (down from $24.1 billion in 2022), producing a debt-to-equity ratio of 4.4x and a current ratio of 0.94. The Altman Z-Score of 1.41 technically sits in the "distress zone," but this metric is functionally inapplicable to project-financed LNG infrastructure backed by $290 billion in contracted revenue. S&P upgraded Cheniere to BBB+ and Fitch to BBB in 2025-2026, both with stable-to-positive outlooks. The company maintains $8.8 billion in available liquidity and targets ~4x long-term leverage, paying down $652 million in debt during FY2025 at a weighted average cost of 4.65%.

### Capital Allocation

Management's capital return program is aggressive and well-executed:
- **Buybacks**: $2.7 billion repurchased in FY2025 (12.1 million shares at avg $221.55), $2.3 billion in FY2024. A new $10 billion authorization through 2030 was approved in February 2026 — roughly 18% of current market capitalization.
- **Dividends**: $2.22/share annualized (10%+ increase), yielding ~0.84%.
- **Share count reduction**: 251 million (2022) → 220 million (2025), a 12.4% reduction in three years.

As Stage 3 capex rolls off through 2026, FCF should expand materially from the $2.5 billion reported in FY2025 (which included $3.1 billion of growth capex). Management's run-rate DCF target of ~$30/share implies $6.6 billion in annual distributable cash flow on the current share count.

### Tax Tailwinds

The OBBBA (July 2025) reinstated 100% bonus depreciation and expanded FDDEI export deductions (14% effective rate on eligible income). Cheniere will also recognize $370 million in alternative fuel excise tax credits in Q1 2026.

## Growth Analysis

### Volume Growth: Stage 3 and Beyond

Stage 3 is the near-term growth engine. The seven midscale trains add >10 mtpa of capacity, with the final trains expected online by year-end 2026. This project has executed flawlessly under Bechtel's fixed-price EPC contract.

CCL Trains 8 & 9 (FID June 2025) add another ~5 mtpa for ~$2.9 billion. The SPL Expansion (~20 mtpa) and CCL Expansion (~24 mtpa) are pre-FID but would bring total potential capacity toward 75+ mtpa.

### Estimate Revisions: Positive Momentum

Next-year EPS estimates have been revised up 11.0% over 90 days ($14.71 → $16.34) and 7.8% over 30 days. Current-year estimates are slightly weaker (-3.7% over 30 days to $14.35), likely reflecting LNG price curve movements and non-cash derivative volatility. The next-year acceleration is more significant — it reflects growing confidence in Stage 3 volume contribution and sustained LNG pricing.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

The 3-of-4 quarterly beat record includes two massive surprises (171% and 186%). The Q1 2025 miss (-42.4%) coincided with a derivative mark-to-market loss, reinforcing that GAAP earnings are noisy while underlying operational performance has been consistently strong.

### Demand Catalysts

The European Parliament's vote to ban Russian gas/LNG by 2027 structurally locks in European import demand. The Iran conflict and U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports has further tightened global LNG supply expectations — Qatar's facilities sustained damage during March 2026 strikes, directly removing competing supply. Japan's JERA and Taiwan's CPC both signed new long-term SPAs extending through 2050, validating sustained Asian demand.

## Valuation Assessment

At $266, Cheniere trades at approximately 10.5x trailing EPS ($24.13) and 10.5x forward P/E. Against the peer median P/E of 21.7x (OKE 13.6x, FANG 26.2x, TRGP 21.7x, OXY 30.5x, BKR 17.5x), Cheniere trades at a **52% discount** to the energy peer median.

This discount partially reflects the unique nature of LNG infrastructure (toll-model economics with lower commodity beta should trade at tighter multiples than E&P names) and the market's recognition of derivative-inflated GAAP earnings. However, even on management's run-rate DCF target of ~$30/share, the stock trades at ~8.9x distributable cash flow — cheap for an investment-grade infrastructure business with 90% contracted capacity and a decade of growth ahead.

Analyst consensus reflects this undervaluation: the mean target is $301 (13.2% upside), the median is $308.50 (16.0% upside), and the high target is $340 (27.8% upside). The lowest target of $255 represents only 4.1% downside — the tightest bull-bear spread for a large-cap energy name, reflecting near-universal conviction.

21 of 23 analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy (91%), with zero Sell ratings. Recent changes show Strong Buy increasing from 5 to 7 and Hold decreasing from 3 to 1.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's competitive position is strong across multiple dimensions:

1. **First-mover scale**: Largest U.S. LNG exporter by a wide margin, with the deepest operational track record (4,610+ cumulative cargoes).
2. **Contract quality**: ~90% contracted through mid-2030s. The take-or-pay structure means customers pay regardless of whether they lift LNG.
3. **Feedstock advantage**: Gulf Coast proximity to Permian and Haynesville basins gives Cheniere access to the lowest-cost pipeline gas in the world.
4. **EPC execution**: The Bechtel relationship has delivered Stage 3 ahead of schedule and on budget, giving investors confidence in future expansion execution.
5. **Expansion optionality**: 44+ mtpa of potential expansion capacity at existing sites, requiring no new greenfield permitting. This is years ahead of competitors like Venture Global, NextDecade, or Driftwood LNG, several of which face construction delays and financing challenges.

The primary competitive risk is the global LNG supply wave: ~20 mtpa of new capacity came online in 2025 alone, and Qatar's North Field expansion adds substantial volumes through 2027-2028. However, Cheniere's contracted model means spot market oversupply primarily compresses re-contracting terms in the mid-2030s rather than near-term cash flows.

## Risk Assessment

### Derivative Accounting Volatility (HIGH)
$3.6 billion in FY2025 pre-tax derivative gains created a massive gap between reported and cash earnings. This volatility works both ways — a reversal of LNG price expectations could produce multi-billion dollar non-cash losses. Investors must evaluate Cheniere on operating cash flow rather than GAAP net income.

### Global LNG Supply Glut (MODERATE-HIGH)
The company itself acknowledges the market is "transitioning from tight conditions into rapid growth." China's LNG imports dropped 16% in 2025. If new global supply outpaces demand growth by 2027-2028, spot LNG margins compress. Cheniere is partially protected by its contract book, but the residual ~10% uncontracted volumes and eventual contract rollovers are exposed.

### Leverage (MODERATE)
Investment-grade ratings and the contracted revenue backlog provide comfort, but in a severe recession or prolonged LNG price collapse, the debt load limits flexibility. Property tax incentive expirations at both facilities (beginning 2026-2027) add incremental cost pressure of uncertain magnitude.

### Insider Selling (MODERATE — MONITORING)
The CFO, CCO, CLO, and two directors all sold shares in late March 2026 at $249-$300. Total insider sales exceeded $29 million. While this appears to be opportunistic profit-taking after the YTD surge, the breadth across multiple senior officers warrants attention. No open-market purchases have been reported.

### Section 301 Vessel Requirements (LOW-MODERATE, LONG-DATED)
Starting April 2029, U.S. LNG exports face escalating requirements for U.S.-built vessel carriage. With essentially zero U.S.-built LNG carriers in existence, this creates a structural regulatory overhang that could increase long-term shipping costs or constrain export volumes.

### Geopolitical Concentration (MODERATE)
Europe's record ~125 mtpa of LNG imports in 2025 was driven by Russian gas substitution. A diplomatic resolution to the Russia-Ukraine conflict (however unlikely in the near term) could reduce European LNG demand. Conversely, further Gulf escalation could damage additional LNG infrastructure (as occurred with Qatar's facilities in March 2026).

### Options Market Signal
Short interest is minimal at 2.1% of float with 1.0 day to cover. The stock's beta of 0.098 is remarkably low for an energy name, reflecting the toll-model insulation from commodity swings. Low short interest and low beta together suggest the market views LNG as a defensive energy holding, consistent with the fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($310-340)
Stage 3 completion ahead of schedule drives 15-20% volume growth in 2026. The naval blockade and Gulf infrastructure damage sustain elevated LNG pricing through 2026, pushing operating cash flow above $7 billion. The $10 billion buyback accelerates share count reduction. FID on SPL Expansion validates the 75+ mtpa long-term capacity path. At 18x the $16.34 next-year EPS estimate, the stock reaches $294; at 20x (still below peer median), $327. Analysts targeting $330-340 are pricing this scenario.

### Bear Case ($200-220)
The global LNG supply glut materializes faster than expected, compressing spot margins. A negotiated resolution to the Gulf conflict removes the geopolitical premium (the stock was at $193-200 in December 2025 before the Iran escalation). Derivative mark-to-market losses in 2026 create headline earnings declines that spook retail investors. China LNG demand continues to weaken. At 12x a compressed $16 EPS, the stock trades at $192; at 14x, $224.

### Addressing Prior BUY at $281

The prior BUY issued March 20 at ~$281 is currently down 5.6%. Three material changes justify maintaining a BUY rather than downgrading:

1. **Geopolitical regime shift**: The ceasefire collapsed and a U.S. naval blockade was announced, creating a structurally tighter LNG supply environment. Blockade continuation scenarios (25-60% combined probability) support sustained elevated LNG pricing.

2. **Estimate revisions accelerating**: Next-year EPS revised up 11% over 90 days. Stage 3 volumes are real and increasing.

3. **Better entry point**: At $266 vs. the prior $281, the risk/reward has improved by 5.6% while the fundamental thesis has strengthened. The stock is now 11.6% below the 52-week high rather than 6.4%.

The weakness since the prior call was driven by the ceasefire announcement on April 8-9 (which briefly crashed energy prices), not by any deterioration in Cheniere's fundamentals. The ceasefire's collapse and replacement with a blockade validates the original thesis that Gulf energy disruption has structural staying power.

## Conclusion

Cheniere Energy at $266 offers a toll-road infrastructure business with $290 billion in contracted revenue visibility, trading at a 52% discount to its energy sector peers. The ~10.5x P/E is cheap for a business with 90% contracted capacity, investment-grade credit ratings, and a clear path to doubling output over the next decade. The $10 billion buyback authorization (18% of market cap) provides a concrete mechanism for price support and value creation.

The geopolitical environment — Gulf infrastructure damage, the U.S. naval blockade, and European pipeline gas replacement — creates sustained demand for U.S. LNG that Cheniere is uniquely positioned to serve. The approaching global supply wave is a genuine medium-term concern, but Cheniere's contract book provides 5-10 years of cash flow protection that most energy names lack.

The pullback from $300+ to $266 since the prior BUY recommendation provides a more attractive entry point while the underlying thesis — accelerating estimates, Stage 3 completion, and structural LNG demand — has strengthened. Insider selling breadth is the most significant monitoring item. BUY with a $300 base case target.
