**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Allegiant Travel Company is a leisure-focused ultra-low-cost carrier (ULCC) in the middle of a genuine turnaround. The 2024 collapse (a ~$308M pretax loss driven by the Sunseeker Resort venture and MAX delivery delays) has given way to a clean recovery: Sunseeker was sold in September 2025, the company returned to a pure-play airline, and Q1 2026 delivered net income of $42.5M ($2.30 diluted EPS) with a 14.9% operating margin, the highest of any US carrier that quarter. The margin recovery reflects the flexible-capacity model working as designed: the airline cut capacity 5.9% while pushing yield up 20.8% and load factor up 3.9 points, letting pricing power more than offset the unit-cost drag of shrinking ASMs.

Current-year consensus EPS has been revised from $4.22 to $7.56 over 90 days (+79%), and the current-quarter estimate swung from a small loss to a $1.27 profit. Management raised Q2 2026 guidance from a ~50¢ adjusted loss to ≥$1.25 adjusted EPS for the combined company, driven by moderating fuel (June ~$2.87/gal versus a Q2 average near $4.20) and resilient leisure demand. The Sun Country merger closed May 13, 2026, adding MSP hub scale, international route authorities, and Amazon cargo flying, broadening Allegiant's historically narrow leisure niche.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because of the balance of the setup at the current price. The stock has roughly doubled from its August 2025 low of $42.56, trades below analyst target floors ($103 low, $137 mean), and carries real forward momentum. But the merger synergies driving the re-rating remain unquantified by management ("future financial impacts are not yet estimable"), a five-year-stale pilot contract sits alongside a $256M lump-sum retention liability that grows ~$20M/quarter and converts to cash on ratification, a $403M note matures in 2027, and the founder sold ~$34M of stock near the highs in February. Near-term options pricing (77.8% ATM IV, backwardation, elevated put skew) signals the market expects volatility around the August 4 earnings call. The earnings momentum is real and the growth signal is strong, but execution and balance-sheet risk that the current valuation does not fully discount mute the asymmetry that would justify a BUY.

## Company Overview

Allegiant operates a point-to-point leisure model connecting underserved small and mid-size US cities to leisure destinations (Florida, Las Vegas, Phoenix). The strategic core is low aircraft utilization with flexible, seasonal capacity, flying aircraft hard during peak demand periods and parking them off-peak. Management credits this model for its survival while pure ULCC peers Spirit (bankrupt) and Frontier struggled. The carrier identifies 1,400+ incremental domestic routes, more than 75% with no current nonstop service, which lets it fly monopoly or near-monopoly routes that avoid head-to-head competition with legacy carriers' segmented basic-economy products.

As of Q1 2026 the fleet stood at 123 aircraft (78 Airbus A320, 28 A319, and 17 Boeing 737-8200 MAX), with the company transitioning from aging Airbus to owned MAX (50 firm on order, options for 80 more, 33 on firm commitment through 2028). Revenue is diversified across scheduled passenger fares, very high ancillary charges (~$71/passenger air-related versus ~$82 base fare), fixed-fee charter (including "sports flying" during March Madness), and a high-margin co-brand credit card. The card generated $139.6M of remuneration in 2025 (+3.6%) across ~21M Allways Rewards members, a capital-light profit stream that has been named Best Airline Credit Card by USA Today readers for seven consecutive years.

The May 2026 Sun Country acquisition ($4.10 cash + 0.1557 ALGT shares per Sun Country share, ~$1.5B) creates one of the largest US leisure-focused airlines. The two carriers will operate as separate brands for 18-24 months before the Sun Country brand is retired under a single FAA operating certificate.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory frames the turnaround thesis:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2,138M | $2,324M | $2,217M | $2,324M |
| Operating income | $92M | $221M | –$240M | $37M |
| Pretax income | $5M | $159M | –$308M | –$55M |
| Interest expense | $116M | $153M | $156M | $150M |
| Stockholders' equity | $1,221M | $1,329M | $1,089M | $1,053M |
| Long-term debt | $1,944M | $1,820M | $1,612M | $1,682M |

The 2024 loss was largely self-inflicted (Sunseeker resort hurricane damage, MAX delivery delays, heavy interest burden). On an airline-only basis, 2025 revenue was a record $2.5B (+4.3%) with an adjusted airline operating margin of 7.4%; the underlying business was healthier than the consolidated figures, which carry discontinued-operations noise.

Q1 2026 is the clean recovery quarter. Total revenue of $732.4M (+9.6% ex-Sunseeker) produced net income of $42.5M and diluted EPS of $2.30, up from $32.1M and $1.73 a year earlier. TRASM rose 16.4%, yield 20.8%, and load factor climbed 3.9 points to 84.4%, all on 5.9% less capacity. The cost of shrinking is visible: airline CASM ex-fuel and special charges rose 7.1% to 8.64¢ as fewer ASMs absorbed fixed costs. This is the model's tradeoff, and in the current fare environment the yield gains dominated.

Cash flow and the balance sheet are moving in the right direction. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $268M (versus $191M); full-year 2025 OCF was $390M with $75M FCF after $315M of fleet capex. Liquidity was ~$933.6M (cash plus investments) at Q1 2026 plus a $250M undrawn revolver. Net debt fell to ~$0.9B (down $103M sequentially), with net debt/EBITDA of 2.33x, beating the 2.40x stretch comp target. Total debt of ~$1.8B carries a weighted-average rate near 6.6% fixed and 5.7% variable, 59% fixed. The near-term refinancing item is $403M of 7.25% Senior Secured Notes due 2027; the revolver was upsized to $150M and extended to 2030 in December 2025. The dividend is indefinitely suspended to preserve cash for fleet and merger needs.

The earnings beat pattern supports confidence in current estimates: over the last four quarters Allegiant beat by 10.7%, 40.2%, and 59.2%, with a single 12.8% miss in the seasonally weak Q3 2025. The beat magnitude on the profitable quarters is large enough to give weight to the upwardly revised consensus. Interest expense of ~$150M annually still consumes most of operating income, which is the central financial-health caveat: this is a leveraged, cyclical business where the improvement is real but the margin of safety is moderate rather than fortress-like.

Financial Health scores 5.5: improving profitability, ample liquidity, declining net leverage, and beat-target deleveraging, offset by ~$1.8B of debt, ~$150M annual interest that absorbs most operating income, the 2027 refinancing wall, and the post-merger addition of aircraft debt (April 2026: $115M plus up to $176M in facilities).

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth signal is one of the strongest in the airline group. Estimate revisions exceed +30% over 90 days across every window: current-year consensus rose +79.2% (to $7.56), current quarter +1,359.3% (from a loss to $1.27), and next year +21.5% (to $12.12). This is the recovery profile (earnings recovering from a self-inflicted trough with revenue reaccelerating) where estimate-revision velocity of this magnitude tends to precede continued upward re-rating as consensus catches up. The signal is credible here because it is grounded in specific catalysts: the raised Q2 guide, moderating fuel, resilient leisure demand, and Sun Country's profitable contribution.

The organic growth engine has three legs. First, the 1,400+ route runway with 75%+ lacking nonstop service gives Allegiant a long pipeline of monopoly leisure markets. Second, the credit-card and ancillary ecosystem is a high-margin, capital-light stream growing faster than the underlying passenger base. Third, the fleet transition to owned MAX aircraft lowers unit costs as aging Airbus airframes retire.

The Sun Country merger is the largest single growth lever and the largest uncertainty. It diversifies Allegiant into international leisure/charter (DOT transferred Sun Country's international route authorities in July 2026) and Amazon cargo flying, revenue streams uncorrelated with Allegiant's seasonal leisure peaks. Management has not quantified synergies, so the upside is real in direction but unmeasured in magnitude. The Q2 guidance reversal is partly Sun Country's profitable contribution plus fuel relief, which means the near-term earnings jump overstates the organic improvement.

Growth Prospects scores 6.5: the estimate-revision velocity and revenue reacceleration are a genuine BUY-signal recovery, but the growth is partly acquisition-driven with unquantified synergies and depends on the flexible-capacity model continuing to hold pricing power in a fare-sensitive leisure market.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are meaningless here: the trailing EPS of –$2.48 reflects the 2024/2025 trough distorted by Sunseeker and discontinued-operations accounting. On forward earnings the picture is more supportive: current-year consensus of $7.56 implies ~13x forward, and next-year $12.12 implies ~8.1x. For a business posting the best operating margin among US carriers, deleveraging ahead of target, and reaccelerating revenue, single-digit forward earnings on the out-year is not demanding.

Peer context is heterogeneous. Median passenger-airline P/E is 22.1x, but the group ranges from JBLU (–2.6x, unprofitable) to ALK (75.6x, depressed earnings) to SKYW (8.6x) and AL (7.0x, an aircraft lessor). Allegiant's ~8x on next-year earnings and ~13x on current-year sits comfortably below the profitable-peer median, and the analyst target set treats the current price as a floor: low $103, mean $137, median $138.50, high $160; the entire target range sits above the $98.08 current price, and sentiment is improving (three strong-buys up from one). On a recovery stock with rising estimates trading below its target floor, those targets are more likely stale on the low side than a ceiling.

The offsetting reality is that the re-rating leans heavily on unquantified merger synergies and a fuel tailwind that could reverse; Allegiant does not hedge, so the same June fuel decline that lifted the Q2 guide can reverse the P&L if crude spikes. The fuel beta is a live two-sided risk. The stock has also doubled off its low; that prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, but it does mean the easy re-rating from distressed levels is behind it and the remaining upside depends on synergy delivery that management has explicitly declined to quantify.

A compressed multiple is only attractive with an independent catalyst, which here is the estimate-revision recovery plus merger synergies. That catalyst exists but is partially priced. Valuation scores 5.5: forward multiples are genuinely undemanding relative to the recovery, but the asymmetry is capped by unquantified synergies, fuel beta, and the fact that consensus already embeds a large upward revision.

## Competitive Landscape

Allegiant's competitive position is improving within a brutal ULCC segment. Spirit went bankrupt; Frontier has been pressured. Allegiant differentiated by avoiding head-to-head legacy competition (flying monopoly/underserved routes rather than fighting over segmented basic economy) and by pairing low utilization/flexible capacity with a high-margin ancillary and credit-card ecosystem. The Q1 2026 result validated the model: the operating margin led all US carriers, and the airline grew revenue while shrinking capacity, which no pure fare-cutting ULCC could replicate. The WSJ ranked Allegiant #2 among major US carriers for 2025.

The Sun Country merger strengthens the position further, adding scale, an MSP hub, international authorities, and Amazon cargo diversification. This broadens Allegiant from a narrow seasonal-leisure carrier into a more diversified leisure/charter/cargo platform, reducing dependence on any single demand pocket. The near-term cost is integration complexity (separate brands for 18-24 months, dual labor groups, a single-operating-certificate process, and IT/fleet harmonization) which temporarily dilutes the clean competitive story until the entity operates as one.

The structural moat (monopoly leisure routes, ancillary/card ecosystem, flexible capacity) is durable and improving, but the trajectory is complicated by integration risk over the next two years. Competitive Position scores 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Sun Country integration is now the dominant risk.** The carriers operate as separate brands for 18-24 months, requiring fleet, labor, IT, and single-FAA-certificate integration. Management concedes financial impacts are "not yet estimable"; the re-rating rests on synergies that are directional but unmeasured, and integration cost overruns are a real downside vector.

**The pilot contract is a five-year-stale liability.** Amendable since 2021 and in NMB mediation since January 2023, Allegiant has accrued a $256M pilot retention bonus (a current liability growing ~$20M/quarter) payable in a lump sum only on CBA ratification. Ratification triggers a large cash outflow and permanently higher pilot pay, and the merger adds Sun Country's separate pilot group to the labor complexity. A standard screen misses this lumpy future cash-and-margin event.

**No fuel hedging.** Fuel was 27.7% of operating expenses; a 10% move is ~$18M/quarter. The Iran/Middle East conflict pushed Q1 2026 fuel to $3.04/gal (+16.5% YoY). The June fuel decline helped the raised Q2 guide, but the full fuel beta cuts both ways.

**2027 refinancing and leverage.** The $403M of 7.25% notes mature in 2027; ~$150M annual interest still consumes most operating income; post-merger aircraft debt was added in April-May 2026. A higher-for-longer rate path raises the refinancing cost.

**Boeing delivery dependence and a $9.9M TSA dispute** (pending 9th Circuit) round out the specific items. Growth timing hinges on Boeing's production stability as aging Airbus airframes retire.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV is 77.8% versus 62.3% historical volatility (rich by 15.5 points, 79th percentile within the chain), the term structure is in backwardation (77.8% near-term versus 63.8% long-term), and put/call skew is elevated at 6.0% with an implied ±17.1% move into the nearest expiry. The put/call OI ratio spiked from 0.26 to 26.80 since mid-June, signaling heavy protective positioning ahead of the August 4 earnings call. ATM IV fell 22 points from 100.2% to 77.8% over the same window as price rose 2.3%, so some event premium has drained, but the residual richness and backwardation indicate the market expects a large binary move on the combined-company results and first synergy quantification. For a fundamentally constructive but not-yet-BUY view, this argues for letting the event resolve rather than chasing into rich near-term volatility.

Risk Factors scores 4.5: the merger integration, stale pilot contract with the lump-sum liability, unhedged fuel beta, and 2027 refinancing are specific, material, and partially unpriced; several are binary or lumpy in a way the current valuation does not fully discount.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($137-160):** The flexible-capacity model continues to hold pricing power, Sun Country synergies materialize and are quantified favorably on the August 4 call, fuel stays moderate, and next-year EPS delivers toward the $12.12 consensus. At a modest 11-13x on $12+ EPS, the stock reaches the analyst target range of $137-160, roughly 40-63% upside. The credit-card/ancillary ecosystem and cargo diversification add durable, capital-light earnings, and the 2027 note refinances smoothly given improving leverage.

**Bear Case ($70-80):** Integration costs overrun, the pilot CBA ratifies and triggers the cash outflow plus permanently higher labor costs, fuel spikes on a supply disruption (no hedge to cushion it), and leisure demand softens into an H2 consumer cliff. Estimates reverse, the merger-synergy premium unwinds, and the stock retraces toward the low-$70s where it traded in spring 2026, roughly 20-29% downside.

**Base Case ($105-115):** Earnings momentum continues but synergy delivery is gradual and integration frictions cap the pace of margin expansion. The stock re-rates modestly toward the low end of the analyst target range as consensus catches up to the raised guidance, offset by fuel and refinancing uncertainty. This implies ~7-17% upside from the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: a positive 12-month thesis with specific catalysts, but a risk/reward at the current price that is balanced rather than asymmetric enough to justify a BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** The August 4, 2026 earnings call is the decisive near-term event. It should provide the first quantification of Sun Country synergies, integration costs, the single-operating-certificate timeline, and combined labor economics, the items management has declined to estimate. The rich near-term options pricing confirms the market views this as a binary catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Management quantifies Sun Country synergies at a level that supports next-year EPS toward or above $12.12 with a credible integration-cost envelope.
- The stock pulls back toward the low-to-mid $80s on event volatility without any fundamental deterioration, restoring asymmetry to the entry.
- Fuel stabilizes below ~$2.90/gal and the 2027 note is addressed (refinanced or termed out) at a manageable rate.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The pilot CBA ratifies on terms that materially raise the go-forward cost structure alongside the $256M lump-sum outflow, and estimate revisions turn negative.
- Integration cost overruns or a single-certificate delay push combined-entity margins below the 7-8% adjusted airline level.
- A severe fuel spike with no hedge, combined with softening leisure demand, drives current-year estimates back below $6.

## Conclusion

Allegiant is a credible turnaround with a strong forward-earnings recovery: best-in-class Q1 operating margins, sharply rising consensus estimates, falling net leverage, and a raised Q2 guide. The flexible-capacity model has proven it can grow revenue and expand margins while shrinking capacity, the differentiator that separated Allegiant from the failed pure ULCCs. Forward valuation at ~8x next-year earnings sits below the profitable-peer median, and the entire analyst target range ($103-160) sits above the current price.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are specific and partially unpriced: the re-rating leans on Sun Country synergies management has explicitly declined to quantify, a five-year-stale pilot contract carries a growing $256M lump-sum liability that converts to cash on ratification, fuel is fully unhedged into a two-sided oil regime, and a $403M note matures in 2027. The stock has doubled off its low, and rich, backwardated options pricing signals the market expects a large move around the August 4 call. The prudent course is to let the August catalyst resolve the synergy and integration questions before committing fresh capital, upgrading on either favorable synergy quantification or a pullback that restores the asymmetry.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold), overall score 5.65.** This continues the improving trajectory (5.1 → 5.7 → 5.65) and the prior AVOID → HOLD upgrade; the new data confirms the fundamental improvement but does not yet clear the bar for a BUY given execution and balance-sheet risk that the doubled price now partly discounts.
