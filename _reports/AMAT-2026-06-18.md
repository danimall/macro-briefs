**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Applied Materials is the world's largest supplier of materials-engineering equipment to the semiconductor industry, and growth is accelerating. After a pedestrian FY2025 (+4% revenue), Q2 FY2026 delivered record revenue of $7.91B (+11% YoY) and EPS of $3.51, and management guided to roughly 23% total revenue growth for 2026, with semiconductor equipment growing more than 30% and advanced packaging more than 50%. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 11% in the last 90 days and next-year EPS up 17.5%. The demand driver — AI/data-center capex, HBM/advanced-packaging intensity, and a developing DRAM/NAND memory upturn — is broad-based and corroborated across the wafer-fab equipment cohort.

The problem is price. The stock has appreciated approximately 240% over twelve months, from ~$174 to ~$593, and roughly doubled since February 2026 alone. At ~$593 it trades at ~48x current-year consensus EPS ($12.27) and ~36x next-year ($16.26), a re-rating that has compressed the gap to AI-momentum peers but leaves little margin for error. The fundamental thesis is real and improving; the risk/reward at this entry is the issue, not the business.

We rate AMAT a HOLD with a growth bias. Fundamentals are accelerating, so this is not a thesis-deterioration call; it is a discipline call on entry point. Three specific factors prevent a BUY at $593: (1) the multiple already discounts the >30% equipment growth guidance, so the asymmetry has narrowed to roughly balanced; (2) the near-term options market is pricing imminent stress (124% near-term ATM IV, backwardation), signaling the AI-capex narrative is fragile to any digestion signal; and (3) every named executive has sold into the rally with zero open-market buying, which is routine diversification but removes any insider-conviction support at the all-time high. We would add on a pullback toward the low-$500s or on confirmation that the FY2026 acceleration is sustaining into FY2027.

## Company Overview

Applied Materials makes the capital equipment chipmakers use to fabricate semiconductors: etch, deposition, CMP, rapid thermal processing, ion implantation, metrology/inspection, and advanced packaging. It does not make chips and does not serve lithography (ASML's domain), a structural gap that means customer capital allocated to litho does not accrue to AMAT.

The business runs two reportable segments. Semiconductor Systems generated $20.8B in FY2025 (73% of revenue) at a 35.5% operating margin, with a FY2025 mix of foundry/logic/other 67%, DRAM 26%, NAND 7%. Applied Global Services (AGS) generated $6.4B (23%) at 28.1% margin from services, spares, and factory-automation software on a large installed base, and is being shifted toward a subscription model for more predictable revenue. The de-emphasized Display business no longer exists as a separate reportable segment as of October 2025, a diminished strategic importance reinforced by a small $41M goodwill impairment in Q4 FY2025. Effective Q1 FY2026, the 200mm equipment business moved from AGS into Semiconductor Systems and corporate costs are now fully allocated to segments (prior periods recast).

The moat rests on breadth across materials-engineering process steps, deep customer co-development relationships that secure "tool-of-record" positions, and a large installed base feeding recurring service revenue. AMAT's leadership in advanced packaging — HBM stacking and heterogeneous integration — is increasingly central to AI compute economics.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet and profitability are strong. Altman Z-Score of 3.52 sits in the safe zone; current ratio 2.61; debt/equity 0.78; ROA 19.3%; ROE 34.3%; operating margin 29.2%; net margin 24.7%. Total cash and investments of $13.4B exceed $6.5B of well-laddered, investment-grade senior notes (2027–2050), so the company is net-cash positive.

Revenue progression has been steady: $25.8B (FY2022) → $26.5B (FY2023) → $27.2B (FY2024) → $28.4B (FY2025, +4%). FY2025 gross margin expanded 1.2 points to 48.7% on favorable mix and lower input costs, and operating income rose 5% to $8.3B. Net income fell slightly to $7.0B from $7.2B despite higher operating income, driven entirely by tax: the effective rate jumped to 24.5% from 12.0% on a $659M deferred-tax remeasurement (Singapore incentives) and a $407M valuation allowance against CAMT credits invalidated by the OBBBA legislation. Diluted EPS held flat at $8.66 (vs $8.61) on buybacks.

Two earnings-quality flags warrant emphasis. First, in H1 FY2026 net income of $4.8B (up from $3.3B) was heavily flattered by ~$1.14B of largely unrealized gains on equity investments; the company carries publicly traded equity securities at $2.25B fair value vs $0.97B cost. H1 operating income was essentially flat YoY ($4,354M vs $4,344M), held back by a $253M legal-settlement charge and higher RD&E. The headline H1 EPS surge overstates operating momentum; the genuine operating acceleration shows up in the Q2-standalone result (+11% revenue, 49.9% gross margin) and in forward guidance, not in H1 net income. Forward models should strip the mark-to-market gains, which are volatile and non-operating.

Second, the tax tailwind is reversing. The FY2024 12% rate was a one-off; the global minimum tax in Singapore materially raises foreign taxes beginning Q1 FY2026, and CAMT credits are gone. A normalized forward rate in the ~13–25% range (depending on geographic mix) is the right assumption, not the historical low, and is a structural EPS headwind partially offsetting the revenue acceleration.

Cash flow remains robust. FY2025 operating cash flow was $7.96B (down from $8.68B on higher tax and inventory payments); capex stepped up to $2.26B (from $1.2B) for the new EPIC innovation center, with construction-in-progress at $1.86B by Q2 FY2026. Capital return is aggressive: ~$6.3B in FY2025 ($4.9B buybacks + $1.4B dividends), with $13.2B remaining on the buyback authorization and the dividend raised to $0.53/quarter. Diluted share count has fallen from 845M (FY23) to 799M (Q2 FY26).

## Growth Analysis

The earnings-revision signal is meaningful: current-quarter EPS revised +16.5% over 30 days and +17.8% over 90 days; current-year EPS +10.8%/+11.0%; next-year +13.2%/+17.5%. The 30-day revisions are accelerating relative to the 90-day on the near-term quarters, which indicates positive earnings momentum the Street is still catching up to. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (1.6% to 5.9% surprises), so confidence in the rising estimates is reasonable.

Management's FY2026 guidance represents a step-change from FY2025's +4%. The drivers are credible and broad-based: AI/data-center capex at leading-edge foundry/logic customers, HBM and advanced-packaging intensity, and a developing memory upturn. AGS grew 16–17% in H1, demonstrating the durability of the recurring services franchise independent of the equipment cycle. AMAT's NAND revenue rose from 4% to 7% of Semi Systems in FY2025, and DRAM strengthened in H1 FY2026, consistent with the memory-recovery thesis.

The infrastructure-demand evidence is corroborated across the equipment cohort and downstream: hyperscaler capex acceleration, advanced-packaging confirmation, and a DRAM supply gap projected through 2028 (per Citi, mid-June 2026 — single-analyst view, flagged as tier-3). The breadth of confirming signals raises the durability of the growth thesis relative to a single-company story.

The caveat AMAT discloses itself: AI investment timing and magnitude "can change significantly" and is "difficult to forecast." The company could over- or under-build to a demand signal that proves lumpy. The revenue acceleration is heavily levered to AI-capex durability, which is the single largest swing factor in the model.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing EPS of $8.66 the multiple is ~68x. The PEG on next-year growth (~30%+ guided) is roughly 1.2 — not absurd for a structural-demand beneficiary, but no longer cheap.

Against peers, the comparison is supportive but not a buy signal on its own. Lam Research trades at 68.5x, Teradyne 70.8x, KLA 68.3x, Qnity 48.1x; peer median P/E is 68.5. On that basis AMAT at 48x current-year (or 68x trailing) appears to trade at a discount to the WFE cohort on a forward basis. But the peer multiples are trailing P/Es inflated by the same cyclical-trough dynamic, and the cohort as a whole has re-rated on the identical AI/memory narrative. When an entire group re-rates on one thesis, peer multiples reflect shared optimism rather than independent confirmation; they are not a reliable floor.

The governing constraint is that the stock has already priced the acceleration. The 240% twelve-month move and the doubling since February embed the >30% equipment growth into the current multiple. Estimate revisions of this magnitude (+11% current-year, +17.5% next-year) typically precede further re-rating when a stock is cheap relative to the rising estimates, but AMAT is no longer cheap on that test. At ~48x current-year, the risk is asymmetric in the wrong direction: a beat is expected and produces little re-rating, while any AI-capex digestion signal hits a richly-priced cyclical hard. Prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark down valuation, but the current price vs. current/forward earnings power, adjusted for the cyclical nature of WFE demand, does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify a high valuation score.

The analyst target structure shows the dispersion: mean $517, median $525, high $650, low $358. The current price of ~$593 sits above both the mean and median consensus and below the high. The Citi $710 target (mid-June) reflects one analyst's bull case on NAND/DRAM/AI, and exceeds the high target in the Phase 1 consensus distribution ($650). When a stock trades above consensus mean and near the high target while the low target ($358) implies ~40% downside, the distribution is skewed against new buyers at this level.

## Competitive Landscape

AMAT is one of the big-four WFE leaders (with Lam, KLA, and ASML), differentiated by breadth across materials-engineering steps and leadership in advanced packaging via HBM and heterogeneous integration. The competitive position is strong but the trajectory has two crosscurrents.

Positive: the geographic re-mix is improving revenue quality. China fell from 37% of revenue in FY2024 ($10.1B) to 30% in FY2025 ($8.5B, –16%) as export rules bit, but Taiwan surged +71% to 24% of revenue and Korea +25%. The rotation is toward leading-edge foundry/memory customers tied directly to AI, arguably higher-quality revenue. China then rebounded +18% in Q2 FY2026, so the story is controlled rotation rather than collapse.

Negative: US export controls are inadvertently incentivizing Chinese domestic equipment makers to gain share at trailing-edge nodes, a structural long-term threat AMAT repeatedly flags. Customer concentration is also material: two customers were ~21% and ~15% of H1 FY2026 revenue, and configured-to-spec orders carry non-recoverable cancellation costs, so a single customer's deferral can swing results. The lithography gap means AMAT does not capture the full WFE wallet. The competitive score reflects a strong but not improving moat: leadership in advanced packaging is the bright spot, offset by the China-self-sufficiency threat and concentration risk.

## Risk Assessment

The most company-specific risk is the export-controls compliance overhang. On 2/11/2026 AMAT settled with Commerce/BIS for $253M (paid in Q2 FY2026), but the settlement includes a suspended denial order waived only after three years contingent on AMAT completing required internal audits and compliance obligations. This is a multi-year compliance probation: any slip over three years could reinstate export prohibitions on certain products, a genuine tail risk. The DOJ and SEC matters were not described as resolved, leaving parallel inquiries open.

China revenue concentration (30% of revenue) remains exposed to further export-rule tightening, and China's own rare-earth export controls affect AMAT's inputs. The AI demand-forecasting risk is structural to the thesis: AMAT could over- or under-build to a lumpy signal. Tax risk is newly elevated (Singapore global minimum tax from Q1 FY2026; CAMT credit loss; Singapore conditional rates begin expiring FY2030). And the broader AI-capex deceleration question — whether recent equipment-vendor warnings elsewhere in the chain are idiosyncratic or the leading edge of a digestion phase — is the macro risk that would hit AMAT hardest at its current multiple.

The risk score reflects that several of these (export-controls probation, customer concentration, AI-capex durability) are not fully priced at a ~48x multiple where the market is discounting flawless execution.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing near-term caution that diverges from the bullish fundamental narrative. Near-term ATM IV is 124.2% against 49.3% one-year historical volatility — a 75-point spread, in the 92nd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (1-week IV 124.2% vs 6-month 71.5%), which prices imminent stress. The 1-week put/call skew is sharply negative (–45.7%), indicating concentrated demand for near-dated downside protection even as longer-dated skew is balanced. ATM IV rose 18.7 points (105.5% → 124.2%) while the price rose 46.3% since mid-May, and the put/call OI ratio fell to 0.27: positioning is net-long and complacent on a multi-month view, but the front-week is pricing a specific event. Unusual activity in far-OTM calls ($750 strike, 11.2x vol/OI) reflects speculative upside chasing. The market is pricing the AI/memory narrative as both euphoric (cheap longer-dated puts, OTM call chasing) and fragile (rich near-term IV, steep front-week put skew). For a long at the all-time high, this is a reason to wait for the near-term vol event to resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($700+):** AI-capex durability holds through 2027, the DRAM/NAND upturn plays out as Citi projects (supply gap through 2028), advanced packaging grows >50% as guided, and AMAT compounds the +17.5% next-year EPS revision into a clean FY2027. Apply ~40x to $16.26 next-year EPS = ~$650; with continued upward revisions toward $18+ and a sustained-momentum multiple, $700+ is reachable. This requires the AI-capex cycle to extend rather than digest.

**Bear case ($358–420):** An AI-capex digestion phase arrives, equipment orders soften (the lumpy-demand risk AMAT discloses), and the rich cyclical multiple compresses hard. The low analyst target of $358 implies ~40% downside, consistent with a ~25–28x multiple on a normalized ~$13–14 EPS if FY2027 growth disappoints. A compliance slip triggering the suspended denial order, or escalation of the open DOJ/SEC matters, would amplify the downside.

**Base case (~$560):** FY2026 growth lands near the ~23% guide, FY2027 estimates stabilize around $16–17, and the multiple normalizes modestly from ~48x toward ~38–40x current-year as the cycle matures. ~38x on $14.50 (blended forward) ≈ $560 — roughly flat to slightly below the current price, reflecting a fairly-valued name with the acceleration already in the price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 FY2026, reporting ~August 2026) is the key checkpoint; it will confirm or challenge the >30% equipment and >50% advanced-packaging guidance.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback to the low-$500s or below (~$500–520), restoring a current-year multiple near ~40x and asymmetric upside to the $650+ bull case.
- Q3 FY2026 confirming the FY2026 acceleration with FY2027 equipment-growth visibility intact, demonstrating the acceleration sustains beyond a single up-year.
- Resolution of the open DOJ/SEC matters without material penalty, removing the legal/regulatory tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut or order-softening signal indicating AI-capex digestion (e.g., Q3 equipment growth guided below ~15%).
- Any compliance development reinstating or threatening the suspended BIS denial order.
- A break of the next-year EPS revision trend to negative (estimates falling alongside a stalling price), which would mark the cycle rolling over at a still-rich multiple.

## Conclusion

Applied Materials is a high-quality business in a genuine fundamental acceleration, supported by accelerating estimate revisions, a credible AI/advanced-packaging/memory demand thesis, a fortress balance sheet, and disciplined capital return. The trajectory of the underlying business is positive, and the prior BUY ratings were correct as the stock tripled.

The recommendation moderates to HOLD at ~$593 for a specific, falsifiable reason: the ~48x current-year multiple now prices the >30% equipment growth guidance, compressing the upside-to-base-case to roughly flat while the bear case implies ~40% downside (the $358 low target). The near-term options market is pricing imminent stress (124% front-week IV, backwardation, –45.7% put skew), and every named executive has sold into the rally with zero open-market buying. This is an entry-point discipline problem at an all-time high, not fundamental deterioration. We would move back to BUY on a pullback toward the low-$500s or on Q3 confirmation that the acceleration sustains into FY2027. For existing holders, the growth thesis remains intact and the position is worth holding; for new capital, the asymmetry does not favor adding at this level.
