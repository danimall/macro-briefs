## Executive Summary

CMS Energy is a Michigan-regulated utility delivering consistent mid-single-digit EPS growth through an expanding rate base funded by clean energy mandates and grid investment. The company's 23-year track record of adjusted EPS growth is genuinely rare among regulated utilities, and the emerging data center opportunity (9+ GW pipeline, commercial terms reached with one hyperscaler) provides meaningful upside optionality beyond the base plan.

The core tension is between growth visibility and balance sheet strain. The $25.8B five-year capex plan drives >8% rate base growth, but it has already triggered a Moody's negative outlook on Consumers Energy, requires ~$700-750M annual equity issuance (~3% dilution), and faces execution risk across solar, gas generation, and distribution buildout — all while interest expense has grown 57% in three years. At 20.7x forward earnings, the stock trades at peer median valuation without a premium or discount. The combination of a 3% dividend yield and 6-8% EPS growth provides an attractive total return proposition for income-oriented investors, but the stock requires either multiple expansion (via data center conversion confirmation) or a pullback to $68-70 to offer asymmetric upside.

## Company Overview

CMS Energy operates through Consumers Energy, Michigan's largest combination gas and electric utility, serving ~1.9 million electric customers and ~1.8 million gas customers across the state's Lower Peninsula. The third segment, NorthStar Clean Energy, contributes ~5% of earnings through independent power production (1,665 MW) and renewable development.

Michigan's regulatory framework is among the most constructive in the country, featuring forward-looking test years, investment recovery mechanisms, and the 2023 Energy Law mandating 50% renewable generation by 2030 and 100% clean energy by 2040. This effectively legislates multi-decade rate base growth with regulatory pre-approval of the investment program.

## Competitive Landscape

CMS operates as a regulated monopoly in Michigan's Lower Peninsula, which provides the fundamental competitive moat. Several factors strengthen this position:

1. **Regulatory quality:** Michigan's 2023 Energy Law creates legislative mandate for decades of clean energy investment. Forward test years and cost recovery mechanisms reduce regulatory lag.
2. **Affordability headroom:** Electric bills 14th lowest nationally, providing regulators room to approve capital recovery without consumer backlash.
3. **Data center positioning:** Michigan's engineering workforce density, existing infrastructure, and CMS's large-load tariff (approved November 2025) create differentiation vs. competing states.
4. **Competitive protection:** Michigan's 10% cap on Retail Open Access limits competitive entry.

The competitive position is improving: load growth is accelerating, data center negotiations are advancing, and the large-load tariff provides a framework for hyperscaler service that didn't exist a year ago.

## Risk Assessment

**Moody's Negative Outlook (Material):** The negative outlook on Consumers Energy reflects genuine credit tension. A $25.8B five-year capex plan against a regulated entity that can only recover costs through periodic rate cases creates cash flow timing mismatches. If rate outcomes disappoint or construction costs overrun, a downgrade is plausible, raising the cost of the remaining $15-20B of planned debt issuance.

**Interest Rate Exposure (Significant):** With $798M in 2025 interest expense growing at 16% annually, every 50bps increase in the company's cost of new debt adds roughly $50-75M in annual interest by 2028 (on projected debt of $22-25B). In the current environment with elevated long-term rates, this is an ongoing headwind.

**Data Center Conversion Risk (Moderate):** The 9+ GW pipeline sounds transformative, but local zoning challenges (Gaines Township tabled April 2026), multi-state competition for hyperscaler facilities, and the inherent gap between "pipeline" and "contracted load" create execution uncertainty. If the market is pricing any data center premium into CMS, disappointment could pressure the multiple.

**Political/Regulatory Risk (Low-Moderate):** Michigan's 2026 gubernatorial election introduces uncertainty. While both parties have historically supported utility investment, populist pressure on rate increases during a period of elevated inflation could complicate future rate cases.

**Campbell Coal Plant (Near-term):** The DOE's emergency order forcing continued operation of the 1,407 MW plant creates unplanned costs with uncertain recovery. FERC proceedings are underway but no tariff mechanism exists. Exposure estimated at tens of millions annually in unrecovered costs until resolved.

## Conclusion

CMS Energy is a well-run regulated utility with above-average growth visibility, a constructive regulatory framework, and meaningful optionality from data center demand. The 23-year track record of consistent EPS growth and the 3% dividend yield make it an appropriate income holding. However, at ~19-21x forward earnings — essentially peer median — the stock does not offer the mispricing required for a BUY recommendation. The specific concern preventing an upgrade: Moody's negative outlook on the operating subsidiary, reflecting genuine credit strain from a $25.8B capex plan that requires both constructive rate outcomes AND manageable interest costs to deliver the 6-8% EPS growth target. Until either the stock pulls back to create valuation cushion ($68-70 range) or the data center pipeline converts to contracted load growth that would justify premium multiple expansion, the appropriate posture is to hold for the ~9-11% total annual return (6-8% EPS growth + 3% yield) that the base plan delivers.
