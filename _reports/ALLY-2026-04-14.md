## Executive Summary

Ally Financial is the nation's largest all-digital bank and one of the largest independent auto finance companies, with $196B in total assets, $151.6B in deposits, and ~21,400 active dealer relationships. The company has undergone meaningful strategic simplification over the past 18 months, exiting credit cards, ceasing mortgage originations, and selling its point-of-sale lending business to concentrate capital on its core auto finance and deposit franchise. These moves have improved credit metrics and positioned the company for NIM expansion as higher-yielding auto originations replace maturing lower-rate loans.

At ~$42, ALLY trades at 17.7x trailing EPS of $2.37 and roughly 11x the $3.81 adjusted EPS management reported for FY2025, a discount to both the peer median P/E of 12.3x on a trailing basis and the 14x implied by sell-side consensus targets. The earnings trajectory is positive: four consecutive quarterly beats (averaging +17.6% surprise), NIM guided to 3.60-3.80% for 2026 (vs. 3.43% in 2025), and a $689M decline in provision expense in 2025 that signals the consumer auto credit cycle has peaked. The CEO and CFO together purchased ~$1.5M in open-market stock within days of Q4 results.

The key concern is macro exposure. Ally's consumer auto portfolio is directly sensitive to unemployment, vehicle affordability, and gasoline prices. With consumer sentiment at record lows, a naval blockade driving oil above $100, tariff uncertainty threatening vehicle prices, and recession probability at 45-50%, the next 6-12 months carry meaningful credit risk. The company's downside stress scenario (unemployment at 8.4%) would require an additional ~$628M in reserves, equivalent to roughly $1.60/share after-tax. This risk is partially priced — ALLY sits 11% below its 52-week high — but not fully discounted for a recession scenario.

## Company Overview

Ally Financial operates four segments: **Automotive Finance** ($115.8B in assets, $5.6B net revenue), which purchases retail installment contracts and operating leases from dealers across the credit spectrum; **Insurance** ($9.9B in assets, $1.7B revenue), providing dealer-centric F&I products; **Corporate Finance** ($13.0B in assets, $538M revenue), offering senior-secured loans to PE-sponsored middle-market companies; and the **Ally Bank** digital deposit platform ($151.6B deposits, 3.5 million customers).

The company's transformation from GM's captive finance arm (GMAC) to an independent, digital-first financial services company is now largely complete. The credit card sale (April 2025), mortgage origination cessation (Q2 2025), and Ally Lending divestiture (March 2024) leave a focused auto finance + digital banking + insurance core.

Consumer auto originations grew to $43.7B in 2025 (from $39.2B in 2024), with used vehicles comprising ~62% of volume. The portfolio yield expanded to 9.18% (from 8.88%) as higher-rate originations roll on. Average deposit cost declined to 3.56% (from 4.18%), creating a widening spread.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability Recovery Underway:**
FY2025 net income reached $852M (up 27% from $669M in 2024), with adjusted EPS of $3.81 representing 62% YoY growth. Core ROTCE improved to 10.4% (from 7.1%). NIM expanded to 3.43% (from 3.27%).

The NIM expansion mechanics are clear: (1) consumer auto portfolio yield is repricing upward as 9%+ yielding originations replace sub-8% maturing loans, (2) deposit costs are declining as the rate environment normalizes, and (3) the securities portfolio repositioning in Q1 2025 (which crystallized a $495M pre-tax loss) improved the book's run-rate yield.

**Credit Quality — Peak Stress Appears Past:**
Consumer auto NCO rate declined to 2.0% (from 2.2%). 30+ day delinquencies fell $84M YoY. Provision for credit losses dropped $689M, the single largest contributor to earnings improvement. The allowance stands at $3.49B (2.5% of total finance receivables), with $3.2B specifically covering consumer auto (3.7% coverage).

Commercial credit quality is excellent, with net recoveries of $2M and criticized exposures declining to 8.2% (from 10.5%).

**Capital Position — Returning to Shareholders:**
CET1 ratio of 10.23% (up from 9.82%) provides a 320bp buffer above the 7.0% minimum including the stress capital buffer. Unrealized securities losses improved to $2.8B after-tax (from $3.9B peak). The $2B share repurchase authorization (December 2025) is the first significant buyback in years. At current prices, this represents roughly 15% of the float.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow was $3.7B (down from $4.5B due to timing items). The dividend payout ratio is 50% ($1.20/share annually, ~2.9% yield at current price).

## Growth Analysis

The growth story rests on three mechanical drivers:

**1. NIM Expansion (3.60-3.80% guided for 2026):** This is the most visible and highest-confidence growth lever. The auto portfolio's weighted average yield is repricing upward by roughly 20-30bp annually as the 2021-2022 low-rate vintage matures and is replaced by 9%+ originations. Deposit costs have further room to decline if rates stay at current levels or fall.

**2. Volume Recovery:** Consumer auto originations of $43.7B (up 12% YoY) are growing, and management reported record application volumes. New vehicle SAAR of 15.7M remains below the pre-pandemic 17M pace, suggesting pent-up demand. However, tariff-driven price increases could choke this channel.

**3. Operating Leverage from Simplification:** Exiting credit cards (11.2% NCO rate) and mortgage originations removes high-cost, subscale businesses. The remaining franchise has higher returns on capital. Management has not given explicit efficiency ratio guidance, but the trend toward fewer, better-performing business lines should support positive operating leverage.

**Countervailing Factor — Revenue Attrition:** Total net revenue has declined from $8.43B (2022) to $7.91B (2025), reflecting the divestiture of credit cards, lending, and mortgage. This creates a headwind to topline growth that NIM expansion must overcome.

Consensus expects EPS improvement toward $2.37 trailing (which is GAAP-depressed by the securities loss and goodwill impairment) to roughly $2.40-2.50 on a forward basis, implying a forward P/E of ~17x. However, the $3.81 adjusted EPS for 2025 suggests meaningful gap between GAAP and economic earnings. If 2026 adjusted EPS reaches $4.00-4.50 (consistent with 3.60-3.80% NIM and stable credit), the stock trades at only 9-10.5x adjusted earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Ally operates in a competitive market with captive finance arms (GM Financial, Chrysler Capital/Santander), bank auto lenders (JPMorgan Chase, Capital One), and credit unions. Key differentiators:

**Scale and Dealer Relationships:** 21,400 active dealer relationships provide origination breadth. Ally's ability to finance the full credit spectrum (prime through nonprime) makes it a one-stop-shop for dealers, increasing wallet share.

**Digital Banking Moat:** As the largest online-only bank by retail deposits ($143.5B), Ally benefits from lower cost-to-serve than branch-based competitors. Online/direct banking deposits grew from 11% to 14% of the broader market from 2020-2025, a structural tailwind.

**Insurance Cross-Sell:** The integrated insurance platform (VSCs, GAP, inventory coverage) creates sticky dealer relationships and diversified revenue. Few auto finance competitors offer a comparable suite.

**Emerging Threats:** The 10-K explicitly flags industrial loan companies (ILCs), often fintech-backed entities offering bank-like products without full BHC supervision, as a growing competitive concern. Additionally, Carvana's growing share of originations (now 10.4% of consumer auto receivables) introduces concentration risk and structural dependence on a single counterparty.

## Risk Assessment

### Consumer Credit Deterioration

This is the primary risk. Ally's consumer auto portfolio is directly exposed to unemployment, vehicle affordability, and energy costs. With gasoline prices rising due to the active naval blockade (oil >$100), consumer sentiment at record lows, and tariff uncertainty threatening 10-20% vehicle price increases, the probability of a credit cycle deepening is meaningful.

The severe stress scenario (~$838M additional reserves) would materially impair earnings. At 45-50% recession probability, the expected value of additional credit losses is approximately $250-350M, equivalent to $0.65-0.90/share after-tax.

Nonprime exposure has edged up to 10.1% (from 9.7%), and the extension/modification rate of 14.6% indicates ongoing borrower stress even as headline NCOs improve. The Carvana concentration (10.4% of receivables, $6B committed facility) adds single-name risk.

### EV/PHEV Residual Value Pressure

Operating lease remarketing swung from +$132M gains (2024) to -$28M losses (2025). With $4.2B in EV/PHEV lease exposure and Stellantis discontinuing certain PHEV models, this headwind could persist. The offset: 39% of leases now carry OEM residual guarantees (up from 24%), and BEV leases have ~50% guaranteed residual coverage.

### Interest Rate Sensitivity

The balance sheet is modestly asset-sensitive near-term (beneficial at current rates) but liability-sensitive over the medium term. An instantaneous +200bp shock would reduce NIM by $221M. With rate hike probability at 35-45% and the Fed unlikely to cut, this introduces two-way risk depending on the rate path.

### Tariff and Auto Industry Risk

Tariffs on automobiles could increase vehicle prices, reduce consumer purchasing power, impair creditworthiness, and disrupt dealer inventory levels. This affects both origination volume (fewer sales) and credit quality (higher monthly payments → higher defaults). The auto finance franchise is the 80%+ majority of Ally's earnings power, making this an existential-grade risk at extreme tariff levels.

### Deposit Stability

As an all-digital bank, Ally faces social-media-driven run risk. Only 8% of deposits are uninsured (excluding affiliates), and total available liquidity of $66.1B exceeds uninsured retail deposits by $54.2B. Retail deposit growth was essentially flat in 2025 ($99M increase), raising questions about organic deposit gathering at higher rate levels.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 64.2% is elevated relative to 29.8% 1-year realized volatility, with the term structure in sharp backwardation (64.2% near-term vs. 37.2% 6-month). This pricing reflects imminent stress expectations, likely tied to the broader geopolitical/macro environment rather than company-specific catalysts. The put/call skew of 13.5% at the nearest expiry indicates elevated put demand. The 1-month tenor normalizes to 38.9% IV with only 1.8% skew, suggesting the market views this as a short-duration risk event rather than a fundamental reassessment. For a buyer with a 12-month horizon, the implied move of ±16.5% at 6 months brackets the bull and bear scenarios reasonably well.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($52-55, +24-31%)
NIM expansion to 3.70%+ in 2026 drives adjusted EPS toward $4.20-4.50. Consumer auto credit continues improving as the 2022-2023 vintage seasons without recession materializing. The $2B buyback program reduces share count by 5-7% over 2026-2027. Used vehicle values hold up as tariffs push new car prices higher, supporting collateral values and remarketing gains. At 12x adjusted EPS of $4.50, the stock reaches $54.

### Bear Case ($30-33, -21-28%)
Recession materializes (unemployment >6%) as combined effect of $100+ oil, tariff-driven inflation, and elevated rates crushes consumer spending. Auto NCOs rise above 2.5%, requiring $600M+ in additional provisions. NIM expansion stalls as deposit competition intensifies. Used vehicle values decline 10-15% on weakening demand and EV oversupply. At 8x depressed adjusted EPS of $3.00, the stock falls to $24-28; at 0.7x TBV the floor is approximately $30-33.

**Bull case target: $55.** 12.5x FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.40, with buyback accretion and multiple expansion as the market recognizes simplification progress.

**Upside/downside:** +24% to base case, +31% to bull case, -21% to bear case. Reward-to-risk ratio approximately 1.1-1.5:1.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Consumer auto NCO rate exceeds 2.5% for two consecutive quarters (signals the credit cycle did not peak in 2024)
2. Management reduces or eliminates 2026 NIM guidance below 3.50% (signals the core earnings driver is impaired)
3. Retail deposit outflows exceed 3% of balances in any quarter (signals digital bank franchise under stress)

## Conclusion

Ally Financial is a simplifying franchise with improving credit metrics, expanding margins, and constructive insider buying, trading at a discount to both adjusted earnings and tangible book value. The 2.9% dividend yield, 4-quarter earnings beat streak, and $2B buyback provide income support and capital return upside. The risk profile is materially elevated by the macro environment: a consumer finance company entering a potential stagflationary recession with oil above $100 and tariff uncertainty is inherently vulnerable. However, the stock already sits 11% below its 52-week high, the CET1 capital cushion of 320bp above minimums provides loss-absorption capacity, and management's ~$1.5M in open-market purchases signals informed confidence. On balance, the risk-reward favors ownership at current levels with a 12-18 month horizon, provided the thesis invalidation triggers are monitored closely.

**Recommendation: BUY** at $41.94, with position sizing reflecting the elevated macro risk (standard sizing, not maximum conviction).
