## Executive Summary

Kinder Morgan reported a decisive Q1 2026 earnings beat (adjusted EPS $0.48 vs. consensus) and indicated full-year 2026 EBITDA is tracking more than 3% above its $8.6B budget, implying approximately $250M+ of upside. The company's $10.1B expansion backlog — 92% weighted toward natural gas projects, with roughly 60% associated with power generation and LDC demand — positions KMI as a direct infrastructure beneficiary of AI-driven electricity demand growth, a theme with multiple independent confirming data points across the sector.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) structural natural gas demand growth (KMI projects 27% U.S. gas demand increase by 2031, corroborated by utilities doubling their gas-fired generation capacity plans), (2) improving financial health evidenced by dual credit upgrades to BBB+/Baa1 while simultaneously growing the backlog, and (3) a fee-based model that largely insulates EBITDA from commodity price swings. At $31.65, the stock trades at 21.8x forward P/E with a 3.7% dividend yield — below peer median multiples (29.5x) despite comparable or superior growth visibility. The primary constraint on further multiple expansion is the elevated long-term rate environment, where 30Y Treasuries around 5% compress the valuation premium traditionally commanded by utility-like infrastructure names.

## Company Overview

Kinder Morgan operates approximately 78,000 miles of pipelines, 136 terminals, and 706 Bcf of working natural gas storage capacity across North America. The business generates approximately 67% of adjusted segment EBDA from Natural Gas Pipelines (~$5.9B), ~13% from Products Pipelines (~$1.16B), ~13% from Terminals (~$1.14B), and ~7% from CO2 operations (~$608M). The company's weighted average remaining contract life is approximately 7 years for gas transportation and 12 years for LNG contracts, providing substantial cash flow visibility.

The strategic focus has shifted toward servicing natural gas demand growth from power generation (including data centers), LNG exports, and local distribution companies. Of the $10.1B expansion backlog, approximately 60% is associated with projects supporting power generation and LDC demand — a structural departure from the company's historical commodity-transportation orientation.

## Growth Analysis

KMI's growth profile has undergone a fundamental transformation. The $10.1B backlog (average in-service Q1 2028, average multiple below 6x) provides 2-3 years of visible expansion capital deployment with attractive returns. Key growth vectors:

- Management forecasts U.S. gas demand at 150 Bcf/day by 2031 (27% above 2026 levels)
- S&P Global reports utilities planning 153 GW of gas-fired generation additions, double the estimate from one year ago
- Data center and power generation demand driving new pipeline deals, including Q1 2026 backlog additions
- LNG export authorization at Elba Island was increased approximately 22% via DOE approval in April 2026

**Major Projects — Subject to Permitting Timelines:**

- Mississippi Crossing: $1.7B, interstate connectivity
- Note: SSE4 and Mississippi Crossing remain subject to timely receipt of FERC certificate orders (expected July 2026) and continued effectiveness of necessary permits and approvals

**Estimate Revisions:**

- Next-year EPS: +3.4% over 90 days (from $1.46 to $1.51)
- Positive but not explosive — reflects gradual consensus catch-up to raised guidance

The growth story is credible: fee-based contracts with investment-grade counterparties (utilities, LDCs), underpinned by physical constraints (permitting, right-of-way) that limit competitive supply response. The average backlog multiple below 6x implies EBITDA accretion as projects enter service in 2027-2028.

## Competitive Landscape

KMI's competitive position is improving along measurable dimensions:

**Scale advantage:** 78,000 miles of pipelines creates network effects — incremental connections to existing infrastructure cost less per unit capacity than greenfield competitors. The $10.1B backlog represents expansion of existing systems, not speculative new construction.

**Market position in structural growth:** KMI's positioning along the Gulf Coast LNG corridor, TVA service territory, and Texas intrastate gas systems places it directly in the path of the highest-growth demand vectors. The Elba Island LNG export volume increase demonstrates regulatory favorability.

**Competitive dynamics:** Williams Companies (WMB) and Energy Transfer (ET) compete for similar projects. WMB trades at 34.3x forward earnings vs. KMI's 21.8x, suggesting the market assigns a meaningful premium to WMB's Transco system despite KMI's broader diversification. TRGP at 29.5x has more NGL/commodity exposure. The valuation gap between KMI and WMB is the most interesting relative value opportunity within the peer set.

**Power generation and data center servicing:** KMI's Q1 2026 backlog additions include projects designed to serve growing power generation, industrial, and data center demand (e.g., the Creekside Lateral). Pipeline infrastructure to serve data center power needs has multi-decade demand visibility — these are not cyclical contracts.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest Rate Sensitivity (HIGH):** With 3.7% dividend yield competing against ~5% risk-free rates, multiple expansion is structurally limited until rates normalize. A further move higher in 30Y rates would compress the stock. This is the dominant near-term risk.

**Execution Risk on $10.1B Backlog (MODERATE-HIGH):** Three projects exceeding $1.5B each carry material construction/permitting risk. SSE4 and Mississippi Crossing specifically depend on timely FERC certificate orders expected in July 2026. Tariff-driven steel cost increases could compress returns if not passed through. KMI added a new risk factor on tariffs in its FY2025 10-K.

**Geopolitical Oil/Gas Price Volatility (LOW-MODERATE):** While KMI's fee-based model limits direct commodity exposure, prolonged oil prices below $70 would reduce producer drilling activity and potentially lower pipeline utilization rates over 12-18 months. Natural gas pricing dynamics are more domestically driven.

**CO2 Segment Decline (LOW):** The CO2 segment is declining ~11% annually on volume attrition. This is fully expected and offset by growth in other segments, but it represents a ~$60-70M annual EBITDA headwind.

**Leverage in Rising Rate Environment:** $3.5B in variable-rate debt plus $23.6B in long-term fixed debt. Short-term debt of $2.2B is manageable given approximately $3.4B of available credit facility capacity, but refinancing at current rates is incrementally costly relative to historical norms.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38-43, +20-36%):**
- Natural gas demand growth accelerates beyond current consensus as data center buildout intensifies
- Backlog projects deliver on time at or below 6x multiples, with additional $2-3B in new project FIDs annually
- Rate environment normalizes toward 4.5% on 30Y, driving yield-seeking capital back into infrastructure
- Multiple re-rates toward 25-27x forward earnings (still below WMB at 34x)
- Dividend grows 3-5% annually for next 3 years

**Bear Case ($26-28, -12-18%):**
- 30Y rates persist above 5.5%, compressing utility/infrastructure multiples further
- Major project delays or cost overruns on SSE4/Trident/Mississippi Crossing reduce return expectations
- General energy infrastructure sells off on geopolitical market stress despite KMI's fee-based insulation
- CO2 segment decline accelerates; gas demand growth disappoints if economic recession materializes
- Multiple compresses to 18-19x as market reprices growth duration

**Base Case ($35-37, +10-17%):**
- Backlog executes on schedule; FY2027 EBITDA reaches ~$9.0-9.5B
- Dividend grows to $1.23-1.25/share
- Multiple holds at 22-23x as rates stabilize
- Total return: 10-14% annualized (yield + growth + modest re-rating)

## Conclusion

KMI warrants a BUY recommendation based on: (1) decisive Q1 2026 earnings beat and guidance tracking well above budget, (2) $10.1B expansion backlog providing 2-3 years of visible growth with below-6x project multiples, (3) structural natural gas demand tailwind from AI/data center power needs and LNG exports, (4) 26% P/E discount to peer median despite comparable or superior growth visibility, and (5) dual credit upgrades confirming improving financial health.

The specific risk that limits conviction to moderate (rather than maximum) is the rate environment: at 30Y around 5%, KMI's 3.7% dividend yield has less appeal relative to risk-free alternatives, structurally capping multiple expansion. This is a measurable, falsifiable concern — if 30Y rates fall below 4.7% sustained, the stock likely re-rates toward $38-40. If rates rise above 5.5%, the bear case activates.

Position sizing: 2% allocation appropriate given moderate total return profile (10-14% annualized base case) and rate sensitivity. The stock functions as a defensive growth position — lower beta than E&P peers, higher income, with emerging power generation/data center optionality that the market has not fully priced at current multiples.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The corrected EV/EBITDA (~11.9x vs. originally stated 13.5x) suggests KMI is cheaper than initially presented, which supports the BUY thesis; however, the data-center-specific backlog claims and "guidance raised" framing were stronger than SEC-filed disclosures support, and major backlog projects remain subject to permitting milestones not yet achieved.
