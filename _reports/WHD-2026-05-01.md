## Executive Summary

Cactus Inc. is a well-run oilfield equipment company trading at ~23x trailing earnings ($55) after a 47% rally from October 2025 lows. The company's balance sheet is exceptional (current ratio 5.8x, net cash position, 20% FCF margin), and the January 2026 acquisition of a 65% controlling stake in Baker Hughes's Surface Pressure Control business adds meaningful international diversification at a reasonable purchase price. However, the stock is now priced near the consensus analyst mean target ($58.22), estimate revisions are uniformly negative (-2% to -5% across all periods), and CEO/President Scott and Joel Bender sold ~$20M in combined shares in early March 2026.

Cactus is navigating its most complex integration ever (Baker Hughes JV spanning Middle East and Sub-Saharan Africa) while its domestic business faces declining rig counts for the third consecutive year (667 → 580 → 545). The first consolidated quarter reports May 6, 2026, which will provide the first real visibility into international revenue contribution, integration costs, and management's ability to deliver synergies. Until that data arrives, the risk/reward from current levels is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Cactus designs, manufactures, sells, and rents pressure control equipment (wellheads, frac trees, production trees) and spoolable pipe technologies for the onshore unconventional oil and gas industry. The company operates through two segments:

- **Pressure Control (~66% of FY2025 revenue):** Wellhead and pressure control equipment under the Cactus Wellhead brand, with manufacturing in Bossier City, LA (high-value/rapid turnaround), Suzhou, China, and Hai Duong, Vietnam. Post-January 2026, this segment now consolidates Cactus International (the Baker Hughes JV) serving international markets.

- **Spoolable Technologies (~34% of FY2025 revenue):** FlexSteel-branded spoolable pipe and end fittings used as production and gathering pipelines, manufactured in Baytown, TX. Acquired via the $658.6M FlexSteel/HighRidge merger in February 2023.

The Baker Hughes SPC acquisition (closed January 1, 2026) moves the company from a primarily U.S. land-focused operator to one with meaningful Middle Eastern and African exposure, hedging against domestic cyclicality.

**Balance sheet is a clear strength:** Total debt of only ~$17M against $124M in cash (post-escrowing $371M for the Baker Hughes deal), current ratio of 5.81, and an Altman Z-Score of 4.12. **Cash flow quality is excellent:** FY2025 operating cash flow of $258M exceeds net income of $166M by a wide margin. FCF/Net Income conversion of 132% indicates high-quality earnings with limited accrual dependency.

**Capital allocation concern:** Despite a $150M buyback authorization, only $3.7M was repurchased in FY2025. The company preserved cash for the Baker Hughes transaction, which is rational, but the buyback program remains essentially unexercised with $146.3M remaining.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth faces headwinds but medium-term prospects are structurally improved:

**Negative factors:**
- U.S. rig counts declining for 3 consecutive years; management explicitly warns of continued weakness in 2026
- Estimate revisions uniformly negative: current year EPS revised down 3.0% over 30 days, next year down 3.9%
- Spoolable Technologies revenue declined 9.5% in FY2025 — the segment most tied to domestic pipeline activity
- Tariffs (50% Section 232 on steel, China-specific tariffs) are compressing margins on imported components

**Positive factors:**
- Baker Hughes JV adds international revenue (Middle East, Sub-Saharan Africa) starting Q1 2026 — consensus expects higher revenues in FY2026 despite domestic weakness
- Management targets margin improvement synergies from the JV beginning 2027
- Natural gas demand from AI infrastructure buildout provides a medium-term tailwind that management specifically flagged in the 10-K
- Elevated Brent oil prices create a structural demand floor for international pressure control equipment — Middle Eastern national oil companies are likely accelerating activity at these prices

The earnings beat record (3 of 4 quarters, averaging +10.8% when beating) suggests conservative guidance practices, but the Q2 2025 miss (-9.2%) demonstrates that when domestic activity deteriorates faster than expected, the company cannot avoid it.

FY2026 consensus EPS of $2.96 implies 23% growth from FY2025's $2.41, but this growth is almost entirely attributable to Baker Hughes JV consolidation rather than organic improvement. FY2027 consensus of $3.41 (+15% YoY) depends on synergy delivery and international ramp.

## Competitive Landscape

Cactus holds a strong position as one of the largest U.S. wellhead suppliers, competing against Vault, SLB divisions, and TechnipFMC. The company's competitive advantages include:

1. **Manufacturing speed:** Bossier City plant handles rapid-turnaround, high-value work while Asian facilities (Vietnam, China) handle longer lead-time production
2. **Vertical integration:** Designs, manufactures, sells AND rents equipment — capturing value across the well lifecycle
3. **FlexSteel differentiation:** Spoolable pipe technology offers installation cost savings versus traditional rigid steel pipe
4. **International scale** through the Baker Hughes JV, gaining access to NOC relationships and service infrastructure that would take years to build organically

The competitive trajectory is improving because the Baker Hughes JV immediately provides infrastructure, customer relationships, and equipment in international markets where Cactus previously had no presence. Execution must still be proven.

Baker Hughes is simultaneously a JV partner in pressure control AND a competitor in spoolable pipe. This creates a complex commercial relationship that could either create synergies or strategic friction.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Baker Hughes integration execution (HIGH):** Cactus has never operated at this international scale. Baker Hughes transition services are time-limited, the JV has concentrated customer risk, and operational challenges in the Middle East differ fundamentally from U.S. land operations. Stephen Tadlock spans three roles (EVP, CEO of Spoolable Technologies, AND CEO of Cactus International) — a potentially overstretched leadership structure for a critical growth initiative.

2. **Baker Hughes put right (MODERATE-HIGH):** Starting January 2028, Baker Hughes can force Cactus to acquire their remaining 35% at up to $660M enterprise value. At 6x TTM EBITDA, if the JV generates $100M EBITDA, the put price for 35% would be ~$210M. This is within Cactus's financial capacity but could require drawing on credit facilities or foregoing shareholder returns.

3. **Tariff exposure (MODERATE):** 50% Section 232 steel tariffs and China-specific tariffs directly increase costs at facilities manufacturing with imported inputs. Partial cost recovery through pricing has been achieved, but in a declining activity environment, full pass-through is uncertain.

4. **Domestic activity decline (MODERATE):** Both segments are directly correlated to U.S. drilling/completion levels, which management expects to weaken further in 2026.

5. **Customer concentration (MODERATE):** One customer represented 17% of FY2025 revenue, up from 10% in FY2023 — a rapid increase.

- Sustained international oil prices drive NOC activity acceleration, benefiting Cactus International disproportionately

## Conclusion

Cactus Inc. is a high-quality OFS company executing a strategically sound international expansion at a time when its domestic business faces cyclical headwinds. The balance sheet can absorb integration costs and the eventual Baker Hughes put obligation, and the FCF profile provides substantial flexibility. However, at $55 the stock is priced near consensus targets with uniformly negative estimate revisions, founding family selling into strength, and the most important strategic question (can Cactus execute internationally?) still unanswered by reported financial data.

The May 6 earnings report will provide the first consolidated quarter including the Baker Hughes JV and is the key clearing event for upgrading conviction. Until then, the stock offers balanced risk/reward with modest upside to consensus and meaningful downside if integration disappoints. HOLD with a growth-contingent upgrade path.

The score trajectory showing improvement from 6.0 → 6.3 reflects the genuine strategic improvement from the Baker Hughes JV, but the prior improvement in Competitive Position (to 7.0) was warranted while Management Quality decline (to 5.5) deserves further downgrade given the $20M insider selling pattern.
