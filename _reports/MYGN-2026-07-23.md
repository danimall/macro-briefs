**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Myriad Genetics is a molecular diagnostics company with genuine pioneer status in hereditary cancer and pharmacogenomic testing, operating at a $538M market cap against ~$825M of trailing revenue that has been flat-to-declining for two years. The FY2025 headline is a $365.9M net loss, but roughly 87% of that is non-cash goodwill and intangible impairment ($319.4M) triggered by the company's own depressed stock price. Stripping out the impairment, the underlying operating loss was approximately $68M — a business that is loss-making but far less broken than the headline implies. The distinction matters, because it separates the accounting doom loop (low price → impairment → lower equity → reinforced distress signal) from the operating question, which is whether a slow-growth diagnostics franchise can grow into a positive-EBITDA turnaround.

The evidence for a fragile inflection is real. Q1 2026 revenue grew 2.3% YoY to $200.4M; Mental Health, the epicenter of the 2024 UnitedHealthcare GeneSight coverage shock, rebounded +15% on revenue per test as that shock annualizes; Cancer Care Continuum volume grew +13%; the Precise MRD molecular-residual-disease test launched in March 2026 and drove a +9.2% single-session move on June 24 when it expanded into breast, colorectal, and renal cancers. Management guides 2026 revenue to $860–880M (~6% growth) and adjusted EBITDA to $37–49M (positive, versus effectively breakeven in 2025). The earnings track record is mixed but not deteriorating — three beats and one miss over four quarters.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID is a change in setup, not conviction. The prior calls (AVOID at 4.3 in May, 3.8 in June) were made when the stock traded near $4 into a washed-out MRD-uncertainty base. Since then MYGN has rallied ~23% to $5.66 as the MRD catalyst played out and the GeneSight recovery became visible, moving the stock to the middle of the analyst range ($4.00 low / $6.25 mean / $10.00 high). The fundamentals have modestly improved and the price has caught up to them; the asymmetry that made this an obvious avoid is gone. What remains is a two-sided situation gated by an escalating debt covenant and unproven management, which does not support a confident directional call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Myriad (HQ Salt Lake City) develops and commercializes genetic tests billed on a per-test, reimbursement-dependent basis. It reports as a single operating segment but organizes revenue into three product families:

- **Cancer Care Continuum (~60% of revenue)** — the flagship MyRisk hereditary cancer test (expanded to 63 genes in November 2025), FDA-approved companion diagnostics BRACAnalysis CDx and MyChoice CDx, Prolaris (prostate), Precise Tumor, and the newly launched Precise MRD. This is the designated growth engine.
- **Prenatal Health (~21%)** — Foresight carrier screen, Prequel NIPT, SneakPeek consumer gender test, and the pipeline FirstGene multi-modal prenatal screen.
- **Mental Health (~19%)** — the GeneSight pharmacogenomic test for psychiatric medication selection, which took the largest demonstrated demand shock of the last two years.

Most tests are laboratory-developed tests run in single-site CLIA labs (Salt Lake City; South San Francisco; Mason, OH; San Diego). The business model has a structural feature worth naming: tests are largely single-use per patient, so revenue requires perpetual new-patient acquisition or new-product development rather than recurring subscription-like billing.

**The impairment layer.** The FY2025 operating loss of $387.2M contains ~$319.4M of goodwill and long-lived-asset impairment ($234.7M goodwill, $82M+ intangibles), concentrated in the Prenatal (Women's Health) and Mental Health reporting units. The trigger was a sustained decline in market capitalization combined with downward forecast revisions — a self-reinforcing loop rather than a fresh operating deterioration, in which a low stock price forces the write-down, which depresses reported equity and reinforces the distress signal. A further $5.4M impairment in Q1 2026 zeroed out Women's Health goodwill. Goodwill has fallen from $286M to $47M, and book equity from $886M (2022) to $337M. Most of the remaining goodwill risk is now written off, so the doom loop has limited further fuel.

**The operating layer.** Ex-impairment, the FY2025 operating loss was approximately $68M, with a GAAP operating margin around –47% on the reported basis but far narrower on an adjusted basis. Revenue is the core problem — flat-to-declining, up only ~1% on volume in FY2025, with Hereditary Cancer (+2%) and Prenatal (+5%) offset by Mental Health (–15%) and Tumor Profiling (–3%). The Q1 2026 quarter is where the turnaround thesis gets its evidence: Mental Health up $7.3M (+15% revenue per test, +7% volume) as the UnitedHealthcare shock annualizes, partly offset by Prenatal down $7.4M (–12% volume). The direction is positive but the magnitude is modest and the mix is uneven.

**The balance-sheet clock.** Cash-flow quality is poor. FY2025 operating cash flow was barely positive (+$1.8M) with FCF of –$13.8M. Q1 2026 burned $15.7M of operating cash, and cash fell $25M to $124.4M. The company is not self-funding. Altman Z-Score of 1.08 sits firmly in the distress zone, driven by deeply negative EBIT/assets (–0.548) rather than leverage — the income statement, not the debt stack, is the immediate problem, but the July 2025 OrbiMed term loan changes that calculus (see Risk Assessment).

Financial Health scores a 3.5. Negative FCF, a distress-zone Z-score, an expensive secured term loan, and dependence on hitting a 2026 turnaround to preserve going-concern comfort are all real. The offset is a still-meaningful $124.4M cash cushion and a genuine near-breakeven adjusted-EBITDA trajectory, which keeps this from a lower score.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the most genuinely two-sided dimension here. The bull evidence is the fragile inflection: 2026 revenue guidance of ~6% growth (the first meaningful growth after a decline), adjusted EBITDA guided positive versus effectively breakeven in 2025, CCC volume +13% in Q1, the GeneSight recovery, and a new-product pipeline (Precise MRD, FirstGene, MyChoice CDx label expansions).

Precise MRD is the swing factor. It launched in March 2026, expanded into breast/colorectal/renal in June with positive clinical data, and is the single asset most capable of re-accelerating CCC revenue. But Myriad enters MRD as a follower against entrenched leaders — Natera, Guardant, and others — in a market defined by clinical-evidence competition and reimbursement decisions. The upside is real but not yet quantifiable in the numbers, and the same is true of FirstGene, which is still working through the CONNECTOR multi-site study.

The estimate-revision picture argues against treating this as a strong-momentum inflection. Consensus current-year EPS is stable at $0.05 (no 90-day movement), next-quarter revisions are positive (+77% over 90 days off a tiny base), but current-quarter revisions swung sharply negative (–520% over 90 days, from $0.01 to –$0.06) and next-year drifted down 3%. This is stabilization, not the +30% velocity that would flag a durable inflection. The earnings record supports moderate confidence in the forward guide — three beats and one miss — but the one miss was the most recent quarter (Q1'26, –28.9%), which tempers enthusiasm.

Growth Prospects scores 4.5. There is a credible path to mid-single-digit revenue growth and positive adjusted EBITDA, but the base is a washed-out slow-growth diagnostics franchise, the growth bets are unproven against scaled competitors, and estimate revisions are flat rather than accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiples are uninformative here — trailing and forward P/E are negative on GAAP losses, so peer P/E comparison (biotech median 23.5x) does not transfer. The relevant frame is EV and adjusted EBITDA. At $5.66, market cap is ~$538M; adding the $125M OrbiMed term loan and netting $124M cash gives an enterprise value around $539M. Against the 2026 adjusted-EBITDA guide midpoint of ~$43M, that is ~12.5x EV/EBITDA on aspirational, non-GAAP, first-year-positive earnings. That is not obviously cheap for a company still generating GAAP losses and burning cash.

On a price-to-sales basis, ~0.62x EV/revenue on the $870M 2026 guide midpoint is below diagnostics peers, which reflects the real reasons — no GAAP profitability, distress-zone balance sheet, covenant risk, and a five-year record of value destruction. This is the profile the calibration framework flags as a value trap: cheapness that reflects structural impairment rather than mispricing. A low sales multiple is not a buy signal absent a specific catalyst the market is missing.

The catalyst question is where the prior AVOID and the current HOLD diverge. In May–June, at ~$4, the stock arguably underpriced the GeneSight recovery and the MRD optionality, but that gap has closed. The stock now sits near the analyst median ($5.75) and below the mean ($6.25), within a wide range ($4.00 to $10.00) consistent with a binary turnaround. The specific mispricing that would justify a high valuation score — the market ignoring a visible inflection — has been substantially arbitraged away by the recent move.

Valuation scores 4.5. It is not stretched (EV/sales is genuinely low), but the discount reflects real structural impairment, and the recent rally has removed the asymmetric-upside window that existed at $4. This is a fair-to-slightly-cheap stock on a distressed balance sheet, not a clear mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Myriad is a genuine pioneer and brand leader in hereditary cancer and pharmacogenomic testing, with deep community-medicine commercial reach and a strong clinical-evidence base (peer-reviewed publications drive both adoption and reimbursement). That is the level. The trajectory is more concerning. The 10-K acknowledges competitors are "numerous" and often have "considerably greater financial, technical, marketing and other resources" — Labcorp, Quest, Natera, Guardant, Exact Sciences, and direct-to-consumer genomics players. Revenue was flat-to-declining while the diagnostics market grew, which is prima facie share erosion or at best share stagnation.

The competitive dynamics that matter most are reimbursement-driven. The GeneSight/UnitedHealthcare episode showed that a single payor decision can erase $26M of revenue in a year. PAMA reform (delayed again to a May–July 2026 reporting period, with the 15% Medicare cut phase-in pushed to 2027) is an unresolved industry-wide compression risk. Myriad's growth bets (MRD, companion diagnostics) place it as a follower in markets with entrenched, better-capitalized leaders. Its differentiation rests on brand, evidence, and reach — durable but not moat-like against scaled competitors who can outspend on clinical trials and undercut on price.

Competitive Position scores 4.0. Strong legacy franchise, but the trajectory is stable-to-eroding in the core and follower-status in the growth markets, with reimbursement as a structural vulnerability the market has already seen materialize.

## Risk Assessment

**OrbiMed covenant is the fulcrum.** In July 2025 Myriad drew a $125M secured term loan from OrbiMed (option for +$75M) at ~10.2–10.4% (SOFR floor 2.5% + 6.5% margin), secured by substantially all assets. The 10%+ cost signals conventional financing was unavailable on better terms. The binding feature is a minimum trailing-twelve-month revenue covenant tested monthly, starting at $615M (December 2025) and escalating to $974M by December 2029. Against FY2025 revenue of $824.5M and 2026 guidance of $860–880M, the near-term $615M floor has comfortable headroom. But the escalation to $974M requires ~18% cumulative revenue growth versus the FY2025 base that the recent track record does not support. This converts a slow-growth problem into a potential default risk over the medium term, with cross-default and change-of-control provisions attached, and the market appears not to have priced it.

**GeneSight reimbursement erosion** remains the largest demonstrated demand shock — the UnitedHealthcare coverage drop cut FY2025 Mental Health revenue by $26.1M. Management warns the impact continues into 2026 and beyond. The Q1'26 rebound is encouraging but the payor-concentration exposure is structural (one third-party payor is 13% of A/R).

**Continued cash burn against a finite balance sheet** ($124.4M cash, negative FCF, expensive debt) means going-concern comfort depends on the 2026 turnaround. Any equity raise at $5–6/share would be highly dilutive.

**Legal/False Claims Act exposure** includes an unsealed qui tam matter (January 2025) alleging kickbacks re: Carolina Urology referrals, against a history of large settlements ($77.5M securities class action 2023, $45.25M qui tam 2022, $12.75M Ravgen patent 2023). D&O insurance was depleted by the 2023 settlement — a material overhang if new claims escalate.

**Regulatory:** the FDA LDT final rule was vacated by a Texas court in March 2025 (FDA did not appeal), a near-term positive, but Congress could legislate and FDA has separately scrutinized pharmacogenomic tests.

Risk Factors scores 3.5. The covenant-escalation cliff, cash burn, depleted D&O insurance against active litigation, and payor concentration are all specific, and several are not fully priced into a stock trading mid-analyst-range on a turnaround narrative.

The options market is pricing imminent, high-magnitude uncertainty with a mildly bullish tilt. Near-term ATM IV is 110.3% versus 74.7% one-year historical vol — rich by 35.6 points, at the 81st percentile of the current chain. The nearest expiry implies a ±25.9% move, and the term structure is in backwardation (110.3% near-term versus 97.2% at six months), which signals the market expects a near-term catalyst or stress event, likely the next earnings print. The rich IV argues against buying premium here; for a fundamental holder, the backwardation flags the next quarter as a genuine binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($8–10).** The 2026 turnaround is real: revenue grows to the guide, adjusted EBITDA turns positive, GeneSight fully recovers, and Precise MRD gains commercial traction in a large MRD market, re-accelerating CCC. Positive EBITDA and improving cash flow relieve covenant pressure, the balance-sheet distress signal fades, and the stock re-rates toward the $10 analyst high on turnaround-plus-optionality. The impairment cycle is behind it (goodwill nearly fully written off), so reported results improve mechanically as the operating business stabilizes.

**Bear Case ($3–3.50).** Guidance proves aspirational, revenue stalls near flat, MRD underdelivers against Natera/Guardant, and cash burn continues against the OrbiMed clock. The escalating revenue covenant becomes a visible default risk, forcing a dilutive equity raise at a depressed price or a distressed refinancing. Another payor coverage decision (the GeneSight risk repeated in a different product) erases tens of millions. The stock retests its $3.53 52-week low.

The two cases are roughly symmetric from $5.66, which is why this is not a directional call. The prior AVOID at $4 carried a favorable asymmetry to the downside; the ~23% rally has neutralized it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the next quarterly earnings print is the natural checkpoint (the last reported date was 2026-05-05; the next is expected early August 2026). The backwardated options structure confirms the market views this quarter as a binary. The 2026 full-year revenue and adjusted-EBITDA delivery, reported through year-end, is the medium-term test.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of revenue growth ≥5% YoY with CCC volume growth sustained above 10%, confirming the MRD/GeneSight-recovery thesis is durable rather than a one-quarter bounce.
- Adjusted EBITDA tracking to or above the guide with operating cash flow turning consistently positive, relieving covenant and going-concern pressure.
- A pullback toward the low-$4s on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore the downside-asymmetry that justified the prior favorable-avoid setup as a value entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Revenue growth stalling below 3% YoY or a guidance cut, which would put the escalating OrbiMed covenant path in jeopardy.
- A cash balance falling below ~$90M without a visible path to positive FCF, raising the probability of a dilutive raise.
- Any new material payor coverage loss (a GeneSight-style shock in another product) or an adverse development in the qui tam matter.

## Conclusion

Myriad is a slow-growth molecular diagnostics franchise whose reported financials were dominated by a non-cash impairment doom loop that obscured a less-broken operating business with a near-breakeven-to-positive adjusted-EBITDA trajectory. A fragile inflection is genuinely underway: Q1'26 revenue growth, GeneSight recovery, CCC volume strength, the Precise MRD launch, and a positive adjusted-EBITDA guide. Against that, the OrbiMed term loan's revenue covenant converts chronic slow growth into a medium-term default risk, the balance sheet is in the distress zone, and management is a fully reconstituted, unproven team following five years of shareholder-value destruction (TSR of 31 versus peer 125).

The recommendation moves from AVOID to HOLD, with an honest "uncertain" rationale. This is not a conviction upgrade — the fundamentals have only modestly improved. It reflects that the ~23% rally to $5.66 has arbitraged away the downside asymmetry that made the prior AVOID clean at ~$4. The stock now sits mid-analyst-range in a two-sided situation: real turnaround optionality against real covenant and cash risk. The right posture is to wait for the August print. A durable two-quarter revenue re-acceleration with positive cash flow would justify a BUY; a stall or guidance cut, or a pullback to the low-$4s, would flip the call in the respective direction. At the current price with a binary near-term catalyst priced by 110% implied vol, there is insufficient edge to lean either way.
