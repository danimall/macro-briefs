**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Booking Holdings is the world's largest online travel company, an asset-light marketplace connecting travelers with roughly 4.5 million bookable properties and earning commission, payment-facilitation, and advertising revenue. The business is highly cash-generative: FY2025 revenue was $26.9B (+13.4%), operating income $8.8B at a 32.8% margin, and free cash flow $9.1B at a 33.8% margin. At $177.46 (post the April 2026 25-for-1 split), the stock sits near the middle of its 52-week range ($150.14–$231.80) and trades at roughly 17x consensus current-year EPS of $10.46 and 14x next-year $12.30, a wide discount to hotel and OTA peers (MAR 41.6x, HLT 52.1x, ABNB 32.1x) and to its own historical multiple.

The valuation gap is the central question. On forward metrics BKNG screens cheap, and the current price sits at the very bottom of the analyst target range ($175 low, $220 median, $224 mean, $298 high). But the discount reflects a specific, escalating concern rather than mispricing alone. The FY2025 10-K materially rewrote its risk factors around generative-AI disintermediation, naming Google/Gemini and AI-native agents as threats to route travel intent away from OTCs. That risk has begun showing up in data: the Q3'25 $457M KAYAK impairment explicitly cited rising customer-acquisition costs in meta-search, and the Q1'26 10-Q states management expects SEO/organic-search traffic to decline over the short-to-medium term, pushing more volume into paid channels where marketing already runs $8.2B (30% of revenue).

We rate BKNG a HOLD (growth-hold). The operating engine is intact and improving: operating income rose 17% in FY2025, the shift to merchant/payment facilitation is now net-accretive, and the Transformation Program adds ~$550M of run-rate savings. There is a credible path to a re-rating toward peer multiples, supported by ~3-4%/year share shrinkage. But estimate revisions are drifting modestly negative, growth is decelerating to a high-single-digit 2026 guide, and the moat's leading indicators (CAC, organic traffic) are the exact metrics deteriorating. The cheapness is real; whether it is opportunity or value trap depends on a structural debate the trailing numbers cannot yet settle.

## Company Overview

Booking Holdings operates five consumer brands: Booking.com (the core asset, ~89% of revenue from online accommodation reservations), Priceline, Agoda (Asia-focused), KAYAK (meta-search), and OpenTable (restaurant reservations). The company does not own hotels; it monetizes an inventory marketplace of roughly 4.0 million alternative-accommodation listings and ~500,000 hotels/resorts. FY2025 gross bookings reached $186.1B on 1,235 million room nights (+8% YoY). Substantially all financial results are generated outside the U.S., principally in Euro and Sterling, with Booking.com domiciled in the Netherlands.

The competitive moat rests on three pillars. First, global inventory breadth. Second, a growing direct/app channel: app room-night mix reached a high-fifties percentage by Q1 2026, with the majority of app bookings direct and repeat, which reduces dependence on paid acquisition. Third, the "Connected Trip" strategy that bundles flights, cars, accommodations, payments, and loyalty into an AI-assisted platform.

Management flags a structural tension: the Connected Trip's non-accommodation verticals (flights grew +29% in FY25 gross bookings) and alternative accommodations (38% of Booking.com room nights in Q1'26) carry lower margins than core hotel commissions. Their growth is mix-dilutive over time, a headwind that partly offsets the accretive shift toward merchant/payment revenue.

The financial profile is strong and cash-generative, with a balance sheet structure that is easy to misread.

Three observations drive the assessment.

First, the FY2025 net-income decline to $5.4B from $5.9B is optical, not operational. It was caused by a $457M KAYAK impairment, roughly $1.3B of negative non-operating "Other income" (dominated by unrealized FX losses on Euro-denominated debt), and higher interest expense ($1.6B). Operating income rose 17%. The FX line is volatile in both directions and swung to a +$302M gain in Q1'26. A screen on net-income trend would misread the underlying trajectory.

Second, the revenue take-rate is rising. Total revenue equaled 14.5% of gross bookings in FY25 (up from 14.3%), driven by the merchant/payment mix climbing from 63% to 70% of gross bookings (72% in Q1'26). Payment facilitation is now net-accretive, with incremental revenue exceeding incremental variable cost. Margin expansion also reflects the Transformation Program (~$250M realized in 2025, ~$550M run-rate by end-2026) and a 17% drop in G&A on roughly flat headcount (~24,300 in 2025, ~24,900 by Q1'26).

Third, stockholders' equity is negative $5.6B (FY25), deepening to −$8.7B at Q1'26. This is a buyback artifact, not distress: the cumulative effect of ~$58.3B of treasury stock against $41.4B retained earnings. It explains why the Altman Z-Score sits in the grey zone (2.14) despite 18.5% ROA and a 33% operating margin. The Piotroski profile is strong. Long-term debt of $16.9B plus $3.0B short-term (~$19.9B senior notes, ~$17.1B Euro-denominated) sits against $16.5B cash and investments; net leverage is modest against ~$9B FCF, and the $2B revolver is undrawn and in covenant compliance. The one caution: debt has risen from $12.2B (2023) to ~$20B to fund buybacks, and in a higher-for-longer rate environment the $1.6B interest bill is a real, growing drag on the FX-noisy bottom line.

Capital allocation is return-heavy: FY25 saw $6.4B of buybacks plus $1.2B of dividends, and Q1'26 alone $3.8B of buybacks (22.8M shares at ~$170–207) plus $343M of dividends. The $18.2B repurchase authorization remaining and a raised dividend indicate the company is returning essentially all FCF-plus-incremental-debt to holders while shrinking the count 3-4% annually.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth has been robust (13-16%), but the forward path decelerates. Management guided full-year 2026 to high-single-digit growth, framed around a maturing base and Middle East softness. Room nights grew +6% in Q1'26, dampened roughly two percentage points by the Middle East conflict (a new Q1'26 risk factor); ex-conflict growth was around +8%, and management characterizes the drag as transitory.

Consensus estimate revisions confirm the deceleration rather than an acceleration. Current-year EPS ($10.46) is down 2.1% over 90 days, next-year ($12.30) down 1.1%, with the current quarter cut 6.6% over 90 days. Over 30 days estimates are essentially flat: stabilizing, but not turning up. This is the opposite of the accelerating-revision signal that would support a high-conviction growth call. The earnings track record is respectable (three beats and one small miss in the last four quarters, most recently a +3.6% beat in Q1'26), which supports confidence in the consensus number but does not by itself argue for upward revisions.

Underneath the top line, the growth mix is shifting toward lower-margin verticals (flights, cars, alternative accommodations), which pressures blended margins even as merchant/payment accretion works the other way. R&D and product investment centers on the Connected Trip and AI-driven internal efficiencies, which management cites as already delivering cost savings. The durable growth story is share of a large, structurally-expanding global travel market combined with buyback-driven per-share compounding; the near-term tempo is slowing.

## Valuation Assessment

On forward earnings, BKNG looks inexpensive. At $177.46 against consensus EPS of $10.46 (current year) and $12.30 (next year), the forward multiples are roughly 17x and 14x. That compares with peer medians of 32.1x P/E and specific comps at 41.6x (MAR), 52.1x (HLT), and 32.1x (ABNB). The current price sits at the very floor of the analyst target range (low $175, median $220, mean $224, high $298), implying ~26% upside to the mean if the discount narrows.

The case for the discount being an opportunity: BKNG is the highest-quality operator in the group, with a 34% FCF margin, 33% operating margin, negligible capital intensity, and a share count falling 3-4%/year. A high-single-digit revenue grower converting to mid-teens EPS growth via buybacks, at 14-17x forward, prices in either meaningful margin erosion or terminal-value impairment.

The case for caution, and the reason we do not score this a 7-plus, is that the discount is explained, not mysterious. The market is applying a structural haircut for AI disintermediation risk, and that risk has concrete supporting evidence (the KAYAK impairment on rising CAC, the forecast SEO decline). Believing "the market is wrong" requires believing the disintermediation fear is overblown; the leading indicators suggest the fear has some substance. Cheap multiples that reflect a real structural overhang have a poor record of converting into returns, and this sits in the sector where selection has historically added the least. Estimate revisions drifting down while the multiple is compressed is a warning, not a green light. We view the valuation as attractive on paper but discounted for a legitimate reason: a balanced, not asymmetric, setup.

## Competitive Landscape

BKNG remains the dominant OTC by inventory breadth, room-night volume, and direct/app engagement, and the transactional layer of inventory plus payments plus fulfillment remains a genuine barrier. A July 2026 industry note (TechTimes) captured the point that AI agents "cannot confirm a ticket": the answer/recommendation layer is being commoditized, but the booking-and-payment layer where BKNG's assets live is harder to disintermediate. Oakmark's Q2'26 letter argues that global supply, loyalty, and the high direct/app mix insulate the franchise, and that AI is already improving BKNG's own cost structure.

The bear case is where trajectory matters more than level. Google's Gemini integration into Search and Maps, plus AI-native agents (Navan launched "Navan Anywhere" to book travel via Gemini Enterprise agents in June 2026), threaten to satisfy travel intent, and increasingly complete transactions, without a visit to Booking.com. Platforms may favor their own offerings, raising acquisition costs. KAYAK's meta-search unit was impaired specifically on rising CAC, and management now openly forecasts declining organic/SEO traffic, which mechanically shifts volume into paid marketing. Marketing held at 4.4% of gross bookings in FY25; the key monitorable is whether that efficiency deteriorates as free organic traffic erodes. Direct competition from Expedia/Vrbo, Airbnb, Trip.com, and hotel/airline direct-booking pushes remains, but the AI-platform vector is the one that has moved our competitive-position assessment lower over the past two quarters. The moat is not broken, but its leading indicators are pointing the wrong way, which caps the score.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk load is heavier than for a typical mega-cap and only partly priced.

- **AI disintermediation** is the defining strategic risk, elevated in the FY25 10-K and now visible in the KAYAK impairment and SEO-decline commentary. This is the swing factor on the entire thesis.
- **Regulatory tail** is long and multi-jurisdictional, unique to BKNG's status as an EU DMA "gatekeeper" and Booking.com's DSA "Very Large Online Platform" designation. Active matters include a $476M Spanish CNMC liability accrued (under appeal), a Swiss commission order, a French DGCCRF matter (Agoda order due by Oct 2026), newly opened Italian (April 2026), Greek (June 2025), and Hungarian (Aug 2025) probes, and parity class actions across five European jurisdictions (Netherlands, Germany, Spain, France, and UK).
- **Data security**: April 2026 incidents at both Booking.com and OpenTable were reported to EU regulators, with follow-on GDPR exposure of up to 4% of global revenue.
- **Tax**: heavy reliance on the Netherlands Innovation Box regime (9% vs. 25.8%), plus digital services taxes and U.S. interest-limitation pressure; Q1'26 effective rate rose to 22.6% from 15.8%.
- **Middle East conflict**: quantified at ~2 points off Q1'26 room-night growth and the reason for cautious FY26 guidance; management frames it as transitory.
- **FX and rate sensitivity**: Euro/Sterling exposure drives large non-operating swings on Euro debt, and the rising debt load raises interest expense in a higher-for-longer environment.

Many of these are known and partly reflected in the compressed multiple, but the AI, regulatory, and data-security items each carry an open-ended, hard-to-quantify tail. The negative-equity balance sheet is not a risk (it is a buyback artifact), but standardized screens will keep flagging it.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning does not corroborate a bearish structural thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 37.6% against 34.2% one-year historical vol (only modestly rich), and the IV percentile within the current chain is a low 6%, having collapsed from an inflated 145% reading in mid-June (the June figure was distorted by the stock-split mechanics). The skew is call-heavy (-4.2%), put/call volume 0.90 and OI 0.70, and the term structure is flat. In plain terms: the market is pricing a roughly ±4% near-term move with no elevated demand for downside protection. Short interest at 3.0% of float with 2.7 days to cover confirms this is not a crowded bearish name. Cheap, non-fearful options make for a reasonable entry cost if one wants to establish a position, but they also mean the market is not pricing an imminent negative catalyst; this is a slow-burn structural debate, not a binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$260–298):** AI disintermediation fear proves overblown; the transactional/payments moat holds, the direct/app mix (high-50s%) keeps CAC contained, and BKNG re-rates toward peer multiples. High-single-digit revenue growth plus margin expansion (Transformation Program fully realized) plus 3-4% share shrinkage compounds EPS toward mid-teens growth. On $12.30+ next-year EPS at a 22-24x multiple (a modest premium to today, still below peers), the stock reaches the high-$260s to $298 analyst high.

**Bear case (~$130–150):** SEO decline and rising CAC erode marketing efficiency; blended margins compress under the mix shift to flights/cars/alt-accommodations; regulatory fines and business-practice restrictions accumulate; growth stalls into the high-single digits and then lower. The multiple de-rates further as the market treats OTC economics as structurally impaired. On flat-to-declining EPS at 12-14x, the stock revisits the low-to-mid $130s, roughly 15-25% below current.

**Base case (~$205):** Fundamentals hold near guidance (high-single-digit revenue growth, stable-to-slightly-lower margins, continued buybacks), the AI overhang persists without decisive resolution, and the multiple recovers partway toward peers. ~20x forward on ~$10.50 current-year EPS moving toward $12.30 supports roughly $205, ~15% above current and modestly below the $220 median target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Primary reassessment at the next quarterly print (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August 2026; the options term structure shows heightened positioning into the early-August expiry), and again at FY26 full-year results for confirmation of the high-single-digit growth guide and marketing-efficiency trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that marketing efficiency is holding, with marketing as a percentage of gross bookings stable at or below ~4.4% even as organic/SEO traffic declines, demonstrating the AI/CAC threat is contained.
- A turn in estimate-revision velocity to positive over 90 days (current-year EPS revised upward, reversing the −2.1% trend) alongside a re-acceleration in room-night growth as the Middle East drag rolls off.
- A pullback toward the $150–160 range (52-week low area) that widens the forward-multiple discount to a level where the risk/reward turns asymmetric even under the structural-overhang assumption.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Marketing spend rising materially as a share of gross bookings with room-night growth still decelerating, the concrete signal that AI disintermediation is eroding unit economics.
- A guidance cut below high-single-digit growth, or a second impairment beyond KAYAK signaling broader channel-cost pressure.
- A material adverse regulatory ruling (beyond the $476M Spain accrual) that imposes business-practice restrictions on parity or commission structures across major EU markets.

## Conclusion

Booking Holdings is a high-quality, cash-rich franchise trading at a genuine discount to its peer group and its own history: roughly 14-17x forward earnings on ~$9B of annual free cash flow, with a share count shrinking 3-4% a year. The operating momentum is intact, with operating income up 17% in FY2025, an accretive merchant/payment shift, and Transformation Program savings still flowing through. The current price sits at the very floor of the analyst target range, which under normal circumstances would tilt the risk/reward favorably.

The circumstances are not entirely normal. The discount encodes a specific structural fear, gen-AI disintermediation, that has moved from risk-factor language into observable data via the KAYAK CAC impairment and the forecast SEO decline. Growth is decelerating to a high-single-digit 2026 guide, estimate revisions are drifting down rather than up, and the sector is one where security selection has historically added little. The cheapness is real, but so is the reason for it, and the numbers cannot yet resolve whether this is an opportunity or a value trap.

We rate BKNG a **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale. There is a credible 12-month path to a re-rating toward the ~$205 base case and the $220 median target, supported by buyback compounding and forward-multiple normalization. But that path is contingent on the AI overhang not deteriorating, and the leading indicators are pointing the wrong way. We would move to a BUY on evidence that marketing efficiency is holding as organic traffic erodes, on a positive turn in estimate revisions, or on a pullback toward the low-$150s. We would move to AVOID on rising marketing intensity into decelerating volumes or a guidance cut. Until one of those resolves, the balanced setup argues for holding rather than pressing.
