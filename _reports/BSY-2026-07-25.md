**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bentley Systems is a pure-play infrastructure-engineering software vendor with one of the higher-quality recurring-revenue profiles in application software: 93% recurring revenue, 99% account retention, ~109% net dollar retention, an ~82% gross margin, and a ~34% free-cash-flow margin. The operations are sound; the multiple has been the problem for shareholders. The stock has fallen 47% from its 52-week high of $59.25 to ~$31.40, cutting the forward P/E from the mid-40s into the low-20s (~23x on the current-year consensus EPS of $1.38) even as constant-currency revenue growth re-accelerated to +11.9% in Q1 2026 from ~10% in FY2025. The company's own proxy quantifies the de-rating: cumulative TSR of 96 versus a peer-group index of 183 through FY2025, despite steady fundamentals. This is a quality business with a de-rated stock rather than an operational-deterioration story.

The offsetting facts are concrete. The repayment of the ultra-low-coupon (0.125%) 2026 convertible notes and refinancing into 5%+ floating-rate debt roughly doubled net interest expense year over year (Q1'26 net interest $8.2M vs. $3.8M), a permanent EPS headwind that did not exist when the stock traded at 45x. Consensus EPS estimates have gone flat: the current quarter and next quarter are essentially unchanged over 30 and 90 days, and full-year revisions are only +0.8% over 90 days. The estimate-revision signal that would justify chasing a re-rating is absent. Dual-class governance concentrates ~53.5% of voting power in the Bentley family and hard-wires a 20%-family-voting-power floor into the credit agreement as an event of default, while two family directors adopted 10b5-1 sale plans in March 2026 for up to ~1.85M shares combined.

We rate BSY a HOLD (growth_hold) with a base-case target of $38, ~21% above the current price. The recommendation has now been HOLD across four consecutive reports; it remains HOLD rather than upgrading to BUY because the two conditions that would confirm the mispricing thesis — a second consecutive quarter of constant-currency re-acceleration and a positive turn in estimate revisions — have not both arrived. The valuation is attractive on quality-adjusted forward metrics, but application software as a category has demonstrated through 2025-2026 that correct fundamental theses do not protect against extended multiple compression when estimates stall. We would upgrade on confirmed revision momentum or a further pullback toward the high-$20s.

## Company Overview

Bentley Systems (founded 1984, IPO September 2020) develops infrastructure-engineering software serving ~42,000 accounts across 189 countries, split roughly 52% engineering/construction firms and 48% infrastructure owner-operators. It operates as a single reportable segment. Sector-attributable ARR is concentrated in Public Works/Utilities (~59%) and Resources (~27%, spanning mining, upstream oil & gas, and renewables), with the balance in Industrial (~9%) and Commercial/Facilities (~5%). Management's structural argument is that these end-markets are partly countercyclical: private-sector downturns in resources and industrial tend to be offset by government public-works spending.

The product portfolio spans the project-to-asset lifecycle. Bentley Open Applications (OpenRoads, OpenBridge, OpenRail, OpenPlant, STAAD, built on MicroStation) are the primary growth engine. Seequent (acquired 2021) covers subsurface/geoprofessional modeling. Bentley Infrastructure Cloud handles project collaboration and asset lifecycle (ProjectWise, SYNCHRO, AssetWise). Bentley Asset Analytics delivers AI-driven O&M insights, and the iTwin/Cesium platform (Cesium acquired September 2024) is the company's cloud-native digital-twin and 3D-geospatial developer stack.

The competitive edge is comprehensiveness across disciplines, lifecycle stages, sectors, and geographies, combined with openness/interoperability. Over 70% of revenue comes from accounts that have been customers for more than 10 years. Principal competitors are Autodesk, Trimble, Hexagon, AVEVA (Schneider Electric), Nemetschek, Dassault, Oracle, Esri, and GE Vernova.

## Financial Analysis

Bentley is a high-quality recurring-revenue compounder with rising leverage and rising interest cost.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $1,228M | $1,353M | $1,502M (+11.0%; +10.1% cc) | $424M (+14.5%; +11.9% cc) |
| Subscriptions | $1,080M | $1,223M | $1,377M (+12.5%) | $392M (+14.7%) |
| ARR (period-end) | $1,175M | $1,283M | $1,462M | $1,495M |
| Operating income | $231M | $302M | $363M | $126M |
| Net income | $327M* | $235M | $278M | $95M |
| Operating cash flow | $417M | $435M | $538M | $193M |

*FY2023 net income was inflated by a $143M deferred-tax benefit (effective rate −78%), so FY2024→FY2025 is the cleaner comparison.

The recurring-revenue quality is genuine, with E365 (consumption-based enterprise) at 46% of ARR and consumption-based contracts totaling 51% of ARR. Margins are strong and stable: operating margin ~24%, net margin ~18.5%, and FCF margin ~34.6% (FY2025 FCF ~$520M on ~$18M capex). Capital returns are modest but disciplined: a growing dividend ($0.07/quarter in 2025, up from $0.06), $125M of buybacks in FY2025 plus $40M in Q1'26, and a board authorization expanded to $500M through December 2028.

The interest-cost step-up is the key financial development. The $678M 2026 convertible notes (0.125% coupon) matured January 15, 2026 and were repaid via revolver plus cash, shifting near-free debt onto floating-rate borrowings. On April 23, 2026 the company added a $550M senior secured term loan (SOFR + 100-200 bps), expanding the total facility to $1.85B and terming out the balance sheet at a weighted-average rate of ~5.28%. A $200M interest-rate swap (through 2030) hedges a portion. Interest coverage remains comfortable (FY2025 operating income $363M vs. net interest $12M), but this is a permanent EPS drag that was absent when the stock carried a premium multiple.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.39 that a mechanical screen would flag as "distress" is a false positive. The SaaS structure — negative working capital, current ratio 0.56, large deferred revenue ($284M) and cloud-services deposits ($526M) collected in advance — mechanically depresses the Z-Score, which was designed for asset-heavy manufacturers. The Piotroski F-Score (positive net income, ROA, OCF, and OCF > net income) and the 34.6% FCF margin confirm this is a leveraged but highly cash-generative business, not a stressed one. Financial health scores well above the danger zone but tempered by the convertible cliff (the $575M 2027 notes mature July 2027, out-of-the-money at a $83.23 conversion price and therefore a cash refi obligation) and rising floating-rate exposure.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is quietly re-accelerating on a constant-currency basis, which is the metric that matters given ~33% non-USD-denominated revenue. Q1'26 posted +12.2% cc subscriptions, with services rebounding +30% on Maximo-related work. Growth is almost entirely organic — programmatic acquisitions add less than 1% of ARR per year — supported by new-account growth of ~2.5-3% (mostly SMBs via the Virtuosity e-store) and the 109% net dollar retention.

The earnings-surprise record is respectable but not exceptional: 3 beats and 1 miss over the last four quarters, with beats of 5.9%, 2.5%, and 11.5% and one 2.5% miss. The rising consumption mix improves alignment with customer project activity but adds intra-year revenue variability.

The reason growth prospects score moderately rather than highly is that estimate revisions have stalled. Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimates are flat over both 30 and 90 days (current quarter -0.9% over 90 days, next quarter -1.1%). Full-year revisions are only +0.2% over 30 days and +0.8% over 90 days. Q1'26 delivered a real constant-currency acceleration, but consensus has not yet extrapolated it into higher forward numbers, so the market is treating one strong quarter as noise rather than trend. A second quarter of confirmed re-acceleration would change that. The structural tailwinds — global infrastructure investment, engineering-workflow digitization, and the convergence of project-design and asset-operations markets via infrastructure digital twins — are durable, but durability is different from acceleration, and the growth score reflects the difference.

Bentley grows constant-currency revenue in the low double digits with a ~34% FCF margin and 109% net retention. On a growth-and-quality-adjusted basis, trading at roughly half the peer-median multiple is difficult to justify on fundamentals alone; the TSR of 96 vs. a peer index of 183 through FY2025 is prima facie evidence of multiple compression against unchanged operational quality. This is the core of the constructive case, and it is why the valuation score sits above neutral.

Two disciplines temper the enthusiasm. First, cheapness in application software has repeatedly been a value trap through the 2025-2026 SaaS derating; a low multiple relative to peers is a necessary but not sufficient condition for outperformance, and the absence of rising estimates removes the catalyst that typically re-rates a cheap software name. Second, the interest-cost step-up structurally lowers the earnings that the multiple is applied to, so a mechanical "re-rate to peers" argument overstates the upside. Analyst targets — consensus mean $44.8, median $43.0, range $40-61 — sit 27-94% above the current price and are properly read as floors on a name this cheap relative to its growth, but they have not moved the stock, which underscores that the market is waiting for confirmation rather than a valuation argument. We assign a valuation score that recognizes genuine relative cheapness while withholding the higher score that a clean, revision-supported re-rating would warrant.

## Competitive Landscape

Bentley's competitive position is stable-to-improving. Its moat is the most comprehensive infrastructure-specific software stack in the market, spanning design through asset operations, versus broader-based rivals (Autodesk in AEC, Trimble, Hexagon, Dassault). The 99% account retention and >70%-of-revenue-from-decade-plus customers quantify the switching costs. The digital-twin convergence — merging previously siloed project-design and asset-operations markets — is a structural tailwind that plays directly to Bentley's cross-lifecycle breadth, and the Cesium acquisition positions iTwin as a developer platform for 3D geospatial.

Two competitive-position risks bear watching. China (part of the ~18% APAC revenue base) is essentially flat, with management flagging geopolitical friction, cloud-deployment obstacles, and a customer preference for perpetual licenses over subscriptions — a structural headwind in one region rather than a share loss globally. Longer term, AI is both an opportunity (embedded engineering intelligence, asset analytics) and a risk (product-liability exposure if AI-enabled software produces faulty engineering output, plus evolving AI regulation). On balance, the trajectory is favorable: constant-currency growth is accelerating and net retention is holding, the signature of a widening rather than compressing position. The competitive-position score reflects this improving trajectory, weighted with the recognition — consistent with the pattern that competitive-position scores have been weak recent predictors — that we anchor conviction on the harder financial-health and growth evidence.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risks are financial-structural and governance-related rather than operational.

**Leverage and refinancing.** The convertible-to-floating-rate transition has locked in ~5% funding. The $575M 2027 notes (July 2027) are out-of-the-money and become a cash/refi obligation. Covenants require net senior secured leverage ≤3.0x and fixed-charge coverage ≥3.0x — comfortable today but tighter with rising rates.

**Dual-class control wired into debt.** Class A shares carry 29 votes each; BSY is a Nasdaq "controlled company." The credit agreement contains an unusual event of default triggered if the family drops below 20% of aggregate voting power. The March 2026 10b5-1 plans (Keith Bentley up to 350,000 shares; Raymond Bentley up to 1,500,000 shares) leave the stakes far above the covenant threshold, but the interplay between programmatic family selling and the covenant deserves monitoring.

**FX and China.** ~59% of revenue is non-U.S., ~33% non-USD-denominated, with no hedging beyond natural matching; a stronger dollar is a headwind. China is flat with structural cloud/licensing friction.

**Cyclicality.** Demand is tied to long-timeline infrastructure projects and government budgets, with tariff/trade-war language recently emphasized as a monitored risk. The 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K, and there is no material litigation pending.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that fundamentals do not obviously support. Near-term ATM IV is 55.2% versus 1-year historical volatility of 38.1% — rich by a 17-point spread — with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 55.2% > 6-month 48.5%), the profile of a market bracing for an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is elevated at 7.0% (28.4% at the 1-month tenor), signaling downside demand into the next print. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±14.3%. For a HOLD, this argues against establishing a position immediately ahead of the earnings binary; the rich IV and backwardation suggest better entry after the event-driven volatility crush and after the market sees whether Q1's constant-currency acceleration extends. Short interest is moderate at 8.2% of float (4.7 days to cover) — not a crowded short, and not a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Bentley is a 93%-recurring, 109%-net-retention, 34%-FCF-margin infrastructure-software franchise trading at ~23x forward EPS, roughly half the peer-median multiple, after a 47% derating that reflects multiple compression rather than operational decline (TSR 96 vs. peer index 183). If a second quarter confirms the acceleration and estimates begin rising, the multiple re-rates toward the mid-30s peer range on a higher earnings base, and analyst targets ($43-61) prove to be floors. Bull-case value ~$48, roughly the analyst median-to-high zone.

**Bear case.** Estimates stay flat or drift down; the interest-cost step-up compresses EPS growth; the SaaS-derating regime keeps the multiple depressed regardless of quality (the value-trap outcome that cheap application-software names have repeatedly delivered through 2025-2026); China stays flat and FX turns unfavorable; family 10b5-1 selling adds a persistent overhang. In that scenario the stock holds a low-20s forward multiple on ~$1.40 EPS and trades toward $26-28.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment at the next earnings report.**

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) a second consecutive quarter of constant-currency revenue growth ≥11%, confirming the Q1'26 acceleration is trend not noise; (2) full-year or next-year EPS estimate revisions turning positive by >3% over a 90-day window; or (3) a pullback toward the high-$20s that widens the risk/reward on unchanged fundamentals.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) constant-currency growth decelerating below 8%; (2) net dollar retention slipping below ~105%; (3) full-year EPS estimates cut by >5% over 90 days; or (4) any covenant/refinancing complication on the 2027 notes or the term loan.
- **Reassessment timeline:** the next quarterly print (the options market is pricing a ±14.3% move around it), with the July 2027 note maturity as the longer-dated structural checkpoint.
- **Reference targets:** base case $38 (~24x the $1.58 next-year EPS, a modest re-rate above the current ~20x forward and still a wide discount to the 40.8 peer median, ~+21% from $31.40); bull case ~$48 (~+53%); bear case ~$27 (~-14%).

## Conclusion

Bentley is the higher-quality end of application software trading at a discounted multiple, an attractive quality-and-price combination that has nonetheless failed to work because the catalyst that re-rates cheap software, rising estimates, is not yet present. The Q1'26 constant-currency acceleration is encouraging and the FCF machine is intact, but the doubled interest expense is a permanent drag on the earnings the multiple compounds, and consensus is treating the strong quarter as noise until proven otherwise. The dual-class control structure and the family sell plans are manageable but real overhangs. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it is met because the fundamental case has improved (financial health, valuation) while the confirmation (estimate revisions, growth durability) has not. We would move to BUY on a second quarter of confirmed re-acceleration or a pullback into the high-$20s, and we would let the rich near-term implied volatility resolve before adding. For now, own the quality if already held; wait for confirmation before pressing.
