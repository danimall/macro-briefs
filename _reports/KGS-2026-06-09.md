**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kodiak Gas Services is the largest US provider of large-horsepower contract gas compression, running a ~4.48 million horsepower fleet that is effectively sold out at 98.0% utilization (Q1 2026). The core business has the characteristics of contracted infrastructure: 3-5 year fixed-fee, take-or-pay-style contracts with annual inflation escalators, an investment-grade Permian/Eagle Ford customer base (top four = ~32% of revenue, none over 15%), and pricing power that pushed Contract Services adjusted gross margin to 70.6% in Q1 2026 from 65.7%. Revenue grew to $1,308M in 2025 (+12.8%), operating income to $340M (+36%), and adjusted EPS to $1.54 from $1.05. This is a high-quality cash generator.

The thesis changed materially on April 1, 2026, when Kodiak closed its ~$675M acquisition of Distributed Power Solutions, rebranded Kodiak Power Solutions, entering turnkey behind-the-meter gas-fired power generation targeting data centers and microgrids. Management raised FY2026 guidance to $820-860M adjusted EBITDA and $520-570M discretionary cash flow, disclosed >260 MW already procured, and set targets of 300-500 MW/yr of power additions plus 750,000 new compression horsepower through 2030. The stock rose ~23% in early February and has compounded into a ~93% twelve-month gain, re-rating from the low-$30s to ~$66. The market is now pricing a data-center power-demand growth story on top of a compression utility.

Kodiak carries ~$2.85B of debt against ~$1.18B of equity, interest expense of roughly $198M consumes a large share of operating income, and the company has committed $244.8M of incremental power-equipment purchases over four years to fund a capital-hungry new business whose demand durability depends on the AI/data-center capex cycle. At ~89x trailing and ~24x forward consensus EPS the cash-cow valuation has already absorbed most of the optionality, while near-term estimate revisions have rolled over (-8.7% to next-year EPS over 30 days) and the options market is pricing imminent stress. The forward growth is genuine and already in the price. We rate KGS a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Kodiak operates ~4,700 compression units totaling ~4.48 million horsepower, roughly 80% of it "large" (>1,000 hp/unit), with ~82.8% of assets deployed in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale. Compression is mission-critical: unconventional wells produce at low pressure and require compression from first production through the life of the well, which ties demand to long-life basins rather than short-cycle drilling activity. Only ~9% of revenue-generating horsepower was month-to-month at year-end 2025; the rest sits on multi-year contracts with escalators, with customers supplying fuel gas free and reimbursing property and sales taxes.

The 2024 CSI Compressco acquisition roughly transformed scale and Permian density. The April 2026 DPS acquisition adds a second engine. From Q2 2026, Kodiak reports three segments: Compression Infrastructure (the legacy contract-services franchise), Power Infrastructure (the new distributed-power platform), and Other Services. The investment question now blends a stable, sold-out compression utility with a capital-intensive, higher-growth power business whose end-market is the same AI/data-center electricity demand wave driving capex across the broader market.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue progressed $850M (2023) → $1,159M (2024) → $1,308M (2025), the 2023-24 step-up reflecting the CSI Compressco close. Net income rose to $80.5M from $49.9M, with a ~26% operating margin. Adjusted EBITDA margin ran ~54.7%. The margin trajectory is the cleanest fundamental positive: Contract Services adjusted gross margin climbed to 68.4% in 2025, reflecting a sold-out market that confers pricing power and inflation pass-through. Adjusted diluted EPS of $1.54 versus a $0.89 trailing GAAP figure illustrates how heavily one-time items have depressed reported earnings.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow surged to $599.7M in 2025 from $328M, aided by an ~$87M working-capital benefit, with company-defined FCF of $229.6M. Maintenance capex is modest (~$76M); growth capex of $199.5M reflects fleet expansion. The cash generation comfortably covers the dividend.

**Balance sheet — the central tension.** Long-term debt was $2.56B at year-end 2025, rising to ~$2.79B net / ~$2.85B face by Q1 2026, against equity of ~$1.18B (D/E ~2.58x; equity ~28% of assets). The Altman Z-Score of 1.17 places the company in the "distress zone," but that reading is an artifact of applying a manufacturing-oriented model to a contracted-cashflow infrastructure business with 98% utilization and investment-grade customers; the formula penalizes leverage and low asset turnover while ignoring recurring revenue quality. Interest coverage sat well above the 2.5x covenant minimum, and the ABL max leverage covenant of 5.25x was met. This is a deliberately leveraged structure now being stretched further to fund a power buildout.

The capital structure was reworked aggressively across 2025-26: $770M of 6.50% 2033 notes plus $630M of 6.75% 2035 notes (September 2025) to term out the ABL, then $1.0B of 5.875% 2031 notes (March 2026) to defease the higher-cost 7.25% 2029 notes, incurring a one-time $36.5M extinguishment loss (including a $27.2M call premium) in Q1 2026. The net effect extends maturities (nothing due before 2030) and lowers the average coupon, while adding gross debt. Liquidity is solid at ~$1.6B (Q1 2026: $94M cash + $1.5B ABL availability). The floating-rate exposure is partly hedged: only $325M of the SOFR-based ABL is swapped to fixed, leaving interest-cost sensitivity to the rate path.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.49/share (~$172M annualized), well-covered against guided 2026 discretionary cash flow. A $100M buyback ($31.7M remaining at Q1 2026) was used largely to absorb sponsor stock in 2025.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue of $345.8M (+4.9% YoY), GAAP net income of $17.8M / $0.20 EPS (depressed by the $36.5M debt-extinguishment loss), adjusted EPS of $0.59 versus $0.35 YoY, and adjusted EBITDA of $190M (55% margin). Revenue and adjusted EPS beat consensus.

## Growth Analysis

Two distinct growth vectors are at work. The compression franchise grows through price escalators, fleet high-grading, and the secular outsourcing trend in large-horsepower units. EIA projections of rising US gas production led by Permian associated gas, Gulf Coast LNG export buildout, and flaring-reduction mandates all support compression-intensive demand. This is durable, moderate-growth, cash-generative.

The DPS-driven Power Infrastructure segment is the higher-growth, higher-risk leg, riding the same data-center electricity-demand wave that is driving the broader AI buildout. The FY2026 guidance step-up partly reflects this segment. The strategic logic is coherent: a cash-cow compression business funding a distributed-power platform with a structural demand tailwind.

The estimate-revision picture warrants caution. Ninety-day EPS revisions are strongly positive (+58.5% current quarter, +41.2% current year, +36.0% next year), which on the surface resembles a classic upgrade signal. But the 30-day picture has rolled over: current-year roughly flat (-0.3%), next-quarter -4.7%, and next-year -8.7%. The 90-day strength largely captures the DPS-driven re-rating that has already happened; the more recent 30-day softening suggests consensus is trimming after the initial enthusiasm. The earnings track record is mixed — two beats and two misses across the last four quarters, including a -29.6% miss in Q3 2025 — which limits confidence that current estimates are conservative. The deceleration in near-term revisions outweighs the stale 90-day strength, and it keeps the growth score from rising into the high range despite the genuine demand story.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$66 the stock trades at ~89x trailing GAAP EPS and ~24x forward consensus EPS of $2.70 (current year) / $3.02 (next year). The trailing figure is distorted by the $36.5M debt-extinguishment loss and other one-offs; on adjusted EPS the trailing multiple is ~43x, and on adjusted run-rate the forward picture compresses toward the low-20s. Even on the cleaner forward basis, this is no longer a value situation.

Against peers in oil & gas equipment and services, the picture is mixed rather than cheap. Peer median P/E is 31.3x; KGS sits well above on trailing but in line to slightly below on a normalized-forward basis. Archrock (AROC), the closest pure compression comparable, trades at 18.6x. Kodiak's premium to AROC is justifiable on scale leadership, superior margins, and the power-growth optionality, but it is a premium, not a discount. The dividend yield of ~3.0% provides absolute support and exceeds the peer median 1.3%, a genuine valuation anchor for an income-oriented holder.

The re-rating from the low-$30s twelve months ago to ~$66 has been driven by real fundamental improvement — margin expansion, the CSI scale-up, the clean sponsor exit, and the DPS narrative — so prior appreciation alone is not a reason to call the stock expensive. The reason to be cautious on valuation is that the forward multiple already capitalizes the power-growth story before the Power Infrastructure segment has demonstrated a single quarter of standalone results. Analyst targets (mean $82.21, median $84.00, high $94.00, low $62.00) imply ~25% upside to the mean and reflect the power optionality. The 30-day estimate softening argues that the consensus target may now function more as a ceiling than a floor until the Power segment proves itself. We score valuation below neutral: the asymmetry is no longer favorable, though the yield and the forward multiple prevent a low score.

## Competitive Landscape

Kodiak holds the leading scale position in large-horsepower Permian compression within a consolidated industry (USA Compression, Archrock, Flowco/CSI-legacy assets). Its edge rests on fleet standardization, a high mechanical-availability reputation, the BEARS Academy technician pipeline (new Midland facility opening summer 2026), and the density gained from CSI Compressco. The 98% utilization rate and 70.6% adjusted gross margin are direct evidence of a tightening market where Kodiak captures pricing. High switching costs (large-hp units are costly to install and relocate) plus the producer trend toward outsourcing compression to preserve capital reinforce the moat.

The competitive trajectory is improving in the core business. The power pivot positions Kodiak alongside a fast-growing peer trend of energy-services firms monetizing gas-fired distributed power for data centers, but here the company is a new entrant against established players, and the moat that protects the compression franchise does not yet exist in power. The competitive-position score reflects a strong and widening moat in compression tempered by an unproven position in the new segment.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** ~$198M annual interest expense reduced 2025 pretax income to $113M. The maturity wall has been pushed past 2030 and coupons lowered, but the company is stretching into a capital-intensive power buildout from an already leveraged base. The unswapped portion of the floating ABL leaves exposure to the rate path.

**Power-generation execution and funding.** The 10-Q added explicit risk language around integrating DPS, sourcing and funding power equipment, and demand for electricity. Kodiak has $244.8M of post-close power purchase commitments (total commitments $276.4M). This is a new business with different demand drivers and competitive dynamics; the 300-500 MW/yr target is the key variable, and a shortfall in data-center demand or financing capacity would impair the growth thesis the stock has already priced.

**Concentration.** ~32% of revenue from the top four customers and ~83% of assets in two basins tie results to sustained Permian/Eagle Ford production economics. A 2024 $9.9M impairment tied to a customer bankruptcy illustrates the tail.

**Texas sales/use tax contingency.** Accrued liability of $102.3M at year-end 2025, reduced to $66.0M in Q1 2026 after a partial settlement (which booked a $28M interest/penalty charge in 2025). A real, quantified cash and earnings overhang, though now smaller.

**Mexico FCPA/sanctions matter.** Payments through the CSI-acquired Mexico business (pre-Kodiak ownership) were "likely made to persons associated with" an FTO/SDGT-designated organization. Kodiak self-reported to DOJ, OFAC, and SEC and sold the Mexico operations on 9/30/2025 at a $33.3M loss. Management calls the amounts immaterial; the investigation is open, a low-probability but binary regulatory tail.

**Regulatory/climate.** Methane and GHG rules cut both ways — compliance cost versus electric-compression demand. The current administration's rollbacks reduce near-term pressure but add policy uncertainty.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 51.5% versus 34.6% one-year historical volatility — rich, at the 69th percentile within the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (51.5% near-term vs 43.5% long-term), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress. The nearest expiry implies a ±7.3% move. Put/call OI ratio rose to 1.19 from effectively zero in the prior snapshot as the stock fell 10.5% (from $73.71 to $65.97), indicating fresh downside hedging into the decline. The near-term skew is positive (puts bid) while the 3-month skew is negative (-7.0%), a configuration consistent with hedging an imminent event rather than a durable directional bearish tilt. The backwardation and rich front-end vol reinforce a wait-and-see posture: the market expects a near-term catalyst to resolve before the stock trends. For a HOLD this corroborates the view that the next earnings print — the first to report the Power segment standalone — is the decisive reassessment point.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Kodiak is a sold-out compression franchise with expanding margins and pricing power, now layered with a distributed-power platform riding the durable data-center electricity-demand wave. FY2026 guidance funds a well-covered ~3% dividend while self-funding much of the growth. The clean EQT exit from ~43% to zero removed the float overhang. If the Power segment delivers 300-500 MW/yr and the data-center demand proves durable, the company re-rates as a two-engine infrastructure compounder toward the $84-94 analyst range, roughly +27% to +42%.

**Bear case.** The ~$2.85B debt load leaves little margin for error. If data-center power demand disappoints, financing tightens, or rates stay elevated, the $244.8M power commitments become a capital drag against a business whose interest expense already consumes most of operating income. Near-term estimate revisions have already rolled over, and the mixed earnings record signals execution variability. A power-execution stumble plus multiple compression toward the AROC 18.6x level on normalized forward EPS would take the stock toward $48-52, roughly -21% to -27%.

The base case sits between: the compression cash cow holds, the dividend is safe, the Power segment ramps but with the typical first-year noise of a new capital-intensive business, and the stock consolidates near current levels as the market waits for proof. A reasonable base-case anchor is ~22x normalized forward EPS of ~$3.00, or roughly $66, essentially flat to current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q2 2026 earnings report — the first full quarter reporting the Power Infrastructure segment as a standalone unit — is the decisive event. The options market is pricing a near-term move, and the segment data will determine whether the power-growth re-rating is supported.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Power Infrastructure segment delivers visible MW additions tracking the 300-500 MW/yr target with contracted, investment-grade-quality offtake, alongside FY2026 EBITDA guidance reaffirmed or raised.
- 30-day estimate revisions turn positive again on the forward year, reversing the current softening.
- A pullback toward the mid-$50s (the level of the March 2026 director open-market buys at ~$54.8) that restores forward-multiple-to-growth asymmetry while fundamentals stay intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net leverage rising materially above current levels without commensurate EBITDA growth, or any covenant-headroom compression.
- A Power segment shortfall — procured MW failing to convert to contracted revenue, or power purchase commitments funded with incremental debt against weak demand.
- An adverse resolution of the Mexico FCPA/sanctions matter or a material adverse Texas sales-tax development beyond the $66M accrual.

## Conclusion

Kodiak is a high-quality compression franchise with a credible, well-funded growth pivot into data-center power. The core business is sold out, margins are expanding, the dividend is covered, and the sponsor overhang is gone. What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is valuation and timing: the ~93% twelve-month run has capitalized the power-growth story before it has produced a quarter of standalone results, the forward multiple is no longer cheap relative to the AROC-anchored peer set, near-term estimate revisions have turned negative, the balance sheet is stretched into a capital-intensive new business, and the options market is pricing imminent stress. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would become buyers on either proof of power-segment execution at reaffirmed guidance or a pullback toward the mid-$50s that restores the asymmetry. The trajectory of improving scores (5.3 in April to 6.1 in May) reflects the improving competitive position and financial structure, and our overall score of 5.9 confirms that trend has slowed as the price has caught up to the fundamentals. HOLD, with the classification of uncertain — the evidence does not yet support a confident directional call ahead of the next print.
