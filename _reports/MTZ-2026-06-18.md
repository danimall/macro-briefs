**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MasTec is a top-five North American infrastructure engineering and construction contractor experiencing a sharp operating acceleration. Q1 2026 revenue grew 34.5% YoY to $3.83B (organic +29%), operating income rose 292% to $141.8M, and adjusted diluted EPS reached $1.39 versus $0.51, up 174%. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters, raised 2026 guidance, and carries a record 18-month backlog of $20.3B, up 28% YoY. The business sits directly in the electrification, grid-modernization, and AI-data-center power-buildout cycle, with diversified exposure across communications, clean energy, power delivery, and pipeline. On fundamentals, this is one of the better operating stories in the Industrials complex.

The debate is entirely about price. The stock has appreciated roughly 130% over twelve months and trades around $380, at approximately 43x consensus FY26 EPS of $8.84 and 32x next-year EPS of $11.72. That places it near its peer median P/E of 44.3 and below the mean analyst price target of $473, so the multiple is not an outlier within its re-rating cohort. But it does fully capitalize the power/data-center demand thesis, and the quality of the Q1 result depended heavily on a Pipeline segment EBITDA margin of 21.2% (up from 12.5%) that management attributes to project mix and that should not be extrapolated. The largest segment, Clean Energy, faces a genuine two-sided regulatory fork from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

We rate MTZ HOLD with a constructive growth bias. The prior BUY at $433 has since declined to ~$380, which on its own improves risk/reward; estimates have continued to rise modestly over that window, so we do not read the decline as thesis deterioration. What stops us short of repeating the BUY is the combination of a demanding multiple that already prices the tailwind, the fragility of the Pipeline margin that carried the quarter, the absence of any insider conviction buying, and an estimate-revision pace (current-year +4.0% over 90 days) that is positive but below the velocity that would force a higher-conviction call.

## Company Overview

MasTec is a labor-based infrastructure E&C contractor with a ~95-year operating history, roughly 37,000 employees, and ~790 locations as of Q1 2026. It ranks among ENR's Top 400 contractors and operates five reportable segments:

- **Clean Energy & Infrastructure (~35% of Q1'26 revenue):** renewable generation (wind/solar/battery), heavy civil, and data-center infrastructure.
- **Power Delivery (~27%):** electric transmission and distribution, grid hardening, substations, storm restoration.
- **Communications (~21%):** wireless/wireline/fiber, data-center interconnection, install-to-home.
- **Pipeline Infrastructure (~18%):** natural gas/midstream, water, carbon-capture pipelines, integrity work.
- **Other:** equity investees (notably the Waha JVs).

Approximately 40% of revenue is recurring/repeatable work under multi-year Master Service Agreements; the remainder is discrete project work under fixed-price, unit-price, time-and-materials, and cost-plus contracts. Revenue is recognized over time using cost-to-cost percentage-of-completion, which makes reported results sensitive to estimate revisions and change orders. The core competitive advantage is end-market diversification: MasTec can shift crews and capital across communications, power, renewables, and pipelines as customer capex cycles rotate. Its closest comparable is Quanta Services (PWR), which carries a substantially larger backlog ($48.5B vs. MTZ's $20.3B).

Within the AI/power theme, MasTec is the builder of power-delivery, generation, and data-center infrastructure for utility and developer customers. It does not own data centers or AI workloads. The demand pull is real but indirect, flowing through utility and developer capex budgets.

## Financial Analysis

The recovery-and-acceleration arc is clear. FY2025 revenue grew ~16% to ~$13.6B, net income rose ~145% to ~$399M, and Adjusted EBITDA reached ~$1.15B. Q1 2026 then accelerated:

- Revenue $3.83B, +34.5% YoY (organic +29%, acquisitions +$169M).
- Operating income $141.8M vs. $36.2M (+292%).
- GAAP diluted EPS $0.77 vs. $0.13; adjusted diluted EPS $1.39 vs. $0.51 (+174%).
- Adjusted EBITDA $283.6M, a 7.4% margin, up from 5.7%.

The segment composition of that quarter is the most important detail. Pipeline Infrastructure revenue grew 91.5% to $682.5M, and its EBITDA margin jumped 870 bps to 21.2% on "improved efficiencies" and project mix, contributing $144.9M of the $337.1M total segment EBITDA. Clean Energy revenue grew 45% (organic +32%) with margin up 50 bps. Power Delivery grew 16% with margin up 120 bps. Communications grew 18% but margin fell 100 bps on costs to exit certain install-to-home markets. The disproportionate Pipeline contribution is the central caveat on the quarter's quality: large midstream projects are lumpy, and a 21.2% margin is not a run-rate.

Balance sheet and cash flow: total debt is ~$2.55B (4.5% notes due '28, 5.9% and 6.625% notes due '29, $600M term loan due '28, ~$304M revolver). The $1.9B revolver matures in 2030 with ~$1.54B availability, and the company is in covenant compliance. Total equity is $3.43B against total assets of $10.4B; goodwill ($2.35B) plus intangibles ($0.76B) represent ~30% of assets, reflecting an acquisition-heavy build.

The cash-flow tell is working capital. Q1'26 operating cash flow was $98.9M, up from $78.4M but well below the revenue and earnings ramp. Days sales outstanding rose to 72 from 65, and contract assets surged (+$255M use of cash) as rapid revenue growth outran collections. This is typical for percentage-of-completion contractors in acceleration, but it bears watching alongside $244M of unresolved change orders and claims being negotiated or arbitrated. Capital intensity is high: $97M of capex plus $59M of finance-lease equipment plus $267M for the McKee acquisition in the quarter, with FY26 gross capex guided to ~$290M.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, the most recent a 38.9% upside ($1.39 vs. $1.00) in Q1'26, preceded by 5.3%, 6.6%, and 5.5% beats. A company beating this consistently warrants above-average confidence that current consensus is achievable, though the magnitude of the Q1 beat was itself partly a function of the fragile Pipeline margin.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest dimension of this story and the primary reason to remain constructive. Three independent demand vectors are converging: grid modernization and electrification (Power Delivery margin expanding), the AI/data-center power buildout (driving both Clean Energy data-center work and Power Delivery), and a Pipeline/midstream upcycle that OBBBA's oil-and-gas incentives reinforce. The backlog and raised guidance corroborate the demand picture, and this is a broad-based sector shift confirmed across multiple infrastructure contractors rather than a single-company narrative.

Estimate revisions are positive but not extreme. Current-year EPS consensus has risen to $8.84 from $8.50 ninety days ago (+4.0%), and next-year to $11.72 from $10.62 (+10.4%). Current-quarter estimates rose +3.8% over 30 days. The one yellow flag is the next-quarter estimate, which fell 5.0% over 30 days, a reminder that the segment mix that powered Q1 is lumpy. The revision pace is healthy and consistent with continued positive momentum, but it falls short of the +30% velocity that would mark a stock the market is systematically underestimating.

Two qualifiers on growth durability. First, 44% of the record backlog sits under MSAs with no minimum purchase commitment, cancellable on short or no notice; management explicitly describes backlog as "an uncertain indicator of future revenue." Second, the OBBBA fork cuts directly through the largest segment: the act accelerates the phaseout of IRA clean-energy tax credits (a headwind to the renewable pipeline within Clean Energy) while incentivizing oil and gas, carbon capture, and energy storage (a tailwind to Pipeline and parts of Clean Energy). The net effect is genuinely unresolved and is under-discussed in bullish coverage.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$380, MTZ trades at approximately 43x consensus FY26 EPS of $8.84 and ~32x next-year EPS of $11.72. Trailing P/E (~65x on $5.07 TTM EPS) is not the relevant metric given the earnings ramp; forward and normalized figures are appropriate. On a PEG basis, ~43x forward against ~33% expected next-year growth yields roughly 1.3 — full, but not absurd for a quality compounder mid-cycle.

The peer set supports a parity rather than premium read. Construction & engineering peers trade at a median P/E of 44.3 (Sterling Infrastructure 75.0, Dycom 44.3, Everus 32.9, AECOM 14.5). MasTec at ~43x forward sits right at that median. Analyst targets are above the current price: mean $473, median $494.5, range $256–$545, implying ~25% upside to the mean. Because the stock trades below consensus targets, unlike a name pinned at its target, those targets retain some independent informational value rather than simply echoing the price.

Where the valuation argument loses force is on what the multiple already embeds. The market is paying ~43x for a contractor whose best recent quarter leaned on a Pipeline margin that management does not represent as sustainable, and whose largest segment faces an unresolved policy fork. The absence of any buyback activity at these prices ($250M authorized, none used in Q1) and director selling into strength are consistent with a stock that insiders view as fully valued. We score valuation 4.5: not cheap, with stretched downside if either Pipeline normalizes or estimates wobble, partially offset by peer-relative parity and below-target pricing. A compressed multiple is no longer available here, and we do not award a high valuation score to a price that requires the demand thesis to keep compounding flawlessly.

## Competitive Landscape

MasTec's moat rests on scale, a national crew/fleet footprint, multi-decade utility and developer relationships, and the diversification that lets it rotate capital across segments. The trajectory is improving in the most attractive end markets — power delivery margins expanding, clean-energy and data-center work growing — which matters more than absolute position. Customer concentration is easing: no customer exceeded 10% of revenue in Q1'26 (AT&T was ~10% a year earlier), though the top ten customers still represent ~41% and government ~12%.

Against Quanta Services, MasTec is the smaller player by backlog but offers Pipeline/midstream exposure Quanta lacks, an advantage now that OBBBA favors oil, gas, and carbon capture. The proxy notes a competitive caveat: MasTec's multi-year TSR of 318.8 has lagged its peer group's 355.8, so this has been a strong-but-not-best performer relative to comparables. The competitive trajectory is favorable in power and data-center work, which supports a score above neutral, but the lagging relative TSR and smaller scale versus the segment leader keep it from a high-conviction rating.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter most are specific and at least partly unpriced:

1. **Pipeline margin reversion.** The 21.2% Q1 EBITDA margin drove a disproportionate share of profit and is project-mix-driven. Normalization toward mid-teens would meaningfully compress consolidated margins and pressure the multiple.
2. **OBBBA two-sided fork.** Accelerated IRA credit phaseout is a headwind to the renewable pipeline within the largest segment; offsets from oil/gas, carbon capture, and storage are real but the net is unresolved.
3. **Backlog cancellability.** 44% of the record backlog is under MSAs with no minimum commitment, cancellable on short notice. A capex pause among utility/developer customers would convert "record backlog" into a weaker revenue indicator quickly.
4. **Working-capital and estimate risk.** DSO at 72 days, contract assets surging $255M, and $244M of unresolved change orders/claims; adverse resolution would reverse recognized revenue.
5. **Tariffs/input costs.** Section 232 tariffs on steel, aluminum, and copper, plus shifting IEEPA tariff regimes, could pressure customer project economics.
6. **Valuation derating.** At ~43x forward, any miss or guidance trim invites disproportionate multiple compression; the asymmetry at this level is unfavorable.

Governance is a watch item rather than a red flag: founder/family control (Jorge Mas ~4.56M shares and José Mas ~3.25M shares, ~7.8M combined) aligns interests, but recurring related-party dealings — Inter Miami CF construction ($30.3M Q1 revenue, $65.7M receivable), an aircraft lease (~$1.4M/quarter), and family-affiliated subcontracting — are a persistent feature investors should monitor even though each is small relative to a $30B enterprise.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 53.5% against 39.3% one-year historical volatility, so options are rich (a ~14pp spread), pricing elevated uncertainty rather than a cheap-entry setup. The skew is balanced (2.8% near-term), put/call volume ratio 0.67, and put/call OI ratio a low 0.26; positioning is not bearishly tilted. The term structure is in contango (53.5% near vs. 57.1% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. The implied one-month move of ±12.3% is consistent with a high-multiple name where earnings and segment mix can swing results. Short interest is low at 5.4% of float (2.6 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The options market is not contradicting the fundamental view; it is pricing the volatility appropriate to a richly valued, momentum-driven stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($475–$545):** The power/grid/data-center supercycle proves durable and broad, backlog converts at or above plan, Power Delivery and Clean Energy margins continue to expand, and Pipeline activity stays elevated through 2026–2027. Estimate revisions keep grinding higher, the four-beat streak extends, and analysts move targets up as consensus catches reality. At 40–45x a rising FY27 number, the stock reaches the $475–$545 analyst high band, roughly +25% to +43% from current levels.

**Bear Case ($256–$300):** Pipeline margin normalizes toward mid-teens, removing the largest piece of the Q1 upside; OBBBA's clean-energy headwind bites the largest segment faster than the oil/gas tailwind offsets; cancellable MSA backlog softens as customer capex pauses; and a quarter that merely meets rather than beats triggers multiple compression from ~43x toward ~28–32x. That combination takes the stock to the $256–$300 zone, roughly -21% to -32% from current.

**Base Case (~$420):** Growth decelerates from the Q1 pace but stays strong (high-teens to low-20s%), margins hold near current levels with Pipeline reverting modestly, and FY26 EPS lands around $8.84 with FY27 near $11.72. Applying ~36x to the FY27 number 12 months out yields roughly $420, about +11% from current. This sits between the bear and bull cases and reflects a good business growing into a full, but not unjustifiable, multiple.

The central reason we hold rather than buy: the base-case upside is moderate, the bear case is a real -20%-plus, and the upside depends on a Pipeline margin and policy environment that management itself frames as uncertain. Within the Industrials complex specifically, contractor BUYs at premium multiples have a poor track record of generating returns above the market unless a distinct, fresh catalyst is present, and the catalyst here (the supercycle) is already in the price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July/early August), which will test whether Pipeline margins hold and whether the -5% next-quarter estimate revision proves accurate.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$300s or below (≤32x next-year EPS / ~28x FY27), which would restore a margin of safety and a clearly positive risk/reward, OR
- 90-day estimate revisions accelerating past +15–20% on the full-year number alongside evidence that Pipeline margins (or Power Delivery margins) are structurally higher rather than mix-driven, OR
- Management initiating meaningful buyback activity (none of the $250M authorization used to date) or clustered open-market insider purchases, signaling that insiders see value at the current price.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Pipeline EBITDA margin reverting toward mid-teens combined with consolidated margin compression in two consecutive quarters, OR
- A guidance cut or first earnings miss after the four-beat streak, particularly if accompanied by backlog cancellations, OR
- A clear net-negative OBBBA outcome that materially shrinks the Clean Energy renewable pipeline without offsetting Pipeline/storage gains.

## Conclusion

MasTec is a high-quality, well-run infrastructure contractor with genuine exposure to durable, multi-segment demand and an enviable recent operating record — four straight beats, 34.5% revenue growth, a 174% jump in adjusted EPS, and a record backlog. We do not lower our view because the stock fell ~12% from the prior level; on rising estimates, that decline modestly improves risk/reward and is not evidence of thesis deterioration. The reason we stop at HOLD is the valuation and the quality of the recent print. At ~43x forward earnings, the price already capitalizes the power/data-center supercycle; the quarter that drove the latest enthusiasm leaned on a Pipeline margin that management does not represent as sustainable; the largest segment faces an unresolved policy fork; and insiders are trimming into strength rather than buying or repurchasing stock. Analyst targets imply ~25% upside, which keeps the stock holdable for investors already positioned, but the asymmetry at this multiple does not support adding. We rate MTZ **HOLD (growth_hold)** and would turn more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$300s or on evidence that the Q1 margin strength is structural rather than mix-driven.
