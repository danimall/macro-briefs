**Research Date: June 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Adeia is a pure-play intellectual-property licensing platform, spun out of Xperi in October 2022, that monetizes roughly 13,750 media and semiconductor patents with only about 150 employees. The economics are exceptional in the abstract: FY2025 operating margin of 39.5%, net margin of 25.1%, and a free-cash-flow margin around 35%. The company is misclassified in the sector data as "IT Consulting"; it is closer to a levered royalty stream than a services business, which materially changes how the peer set and valuation should be read.

The investment question is whether the semiconductor and OTT growth engines can outrun the secular decay of the Pay-TV royalty base. FY2025 revenue rose 18% to $443.4M, but the composition is the story: recurring revenue grew only 3% while non-recurring revenue surged 166% on the Disney settlement. Q1 2026 sharpened the divergence: recurring revenue fell 21.4% while semiconductor revenue jumped to $33.5M from $3.7M a year earlier, driven by newly signed AMD, Microsoft, and a third semiconductor license. The March 2026 AMD settlement, which extended Adeia's hybrid-bonding/DBI monetization from memory into leading-edge logic (3D V-Cache in Ryzen X3D), is a legitimate strategic win and validates the AI-adjacent packaging thesis.

The offsetting reality is that two of the largest historical Pay-TV licensees, DISH (unlicensed as of April 1, 2026) and DIRECTV (in declaratory-judgment litigation), have moved from renewal disputes into active litigation, and the recurring base beneath the headline growth is contracting. After a ~2.4x re-rating off the November low to ~$33, the stock trades at roughly 30x trailing and ~23x forward earnings on estimates that are falling in the near term. The setup is balanced: a durable cash machine with real semiconductor optionality against genuine base erosion, concentration risk, and a 2028 refinancing wall. We rate ADEA a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Adeia ("to license," from Greek) inherits the Tessera, Rovi, and TiVo lineage and retained the IP-licensing operation when Xperi Holding separated its product business. The portfolio splits into two engines:

- **Media IP** (the larger, mature base): fundamental patents on program guides, search and discovery, recommendation, personalization, multi-screen, and DVR/content storage. Licensees include MVPDs/Pay-TV (Charter, Comcast/Sky, Cox, Verizon, Google/YouTube TV), OTT platforms (Amazon, Disney+, Hulu, Peacock, and newly Microsoft), and consumer-electronics makers (Samsung, LG, Roku, TCL).
- **Semiconductor IP** (the growth engine): hybrid bonding / Direct Bond Interconnect (DBI), advanced packaging, advanced process nodes, and RapidCool direct-to-chip liquid cooling. Licensees include Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix, Kioxia, Sony, SanDisk, UMC, and AMD.

The moat is the portfolio itself, backed by a demonstrated willingness to litigate. Roughly 80% of patents are internally generated. The last issued patent expires in March 2045. The strategic direction is to pivot monetization toward semiconductor and OTT to offset the structural decline of the per-subscriber Pay-TV royalty base.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue is lumpy by design, and treating any single year as a run-rate is a mistake. The multi-year path (2022 $438.9M, 2023 $388.8M, 2024 $376.0M, 2025 $443.4M) masks the underlying split. In FY2025, recurring revenue grew 3% ($341.5M to $351.3M) while non-recurring surged 166% ($34.6M to $92.0M) on the Q4 2025 Disney settlement recognized upfront. The prior-year comparison had been flattered by a Q4 2024 Amazon deal.

Profitability is strong. FY2025 operating income was $175M, and net income rose to $111.1M from $64.6M. Operating cash flow was $158.1M, down 26% from $212.5M in 2024 on working-capital timing (much settlement revenue sits in unbilled/deferred balances), with FCF of $156.3M. Cash conversion is high, which is the durable feature of this model.

Leverage is the key risk vector. The Term Loan B stood at $426.7M at year-end 2025 (down from $561M), reduced to $398.6M by Q1 2026, at a variable rate of roughly 7.3% (SOFR + 2.5% after two repricings), maturing June 8, 2028 with a ~$356M balloon. D/E is ~1.16, and the Altman Z of 2.4 (grey zone) reflects this leverage rather than operational distress; Piotroski signals are strong. A 1% rate move is roughly $4.2M of interest expense. Cash plus securities was $115.8M at Q1 2026. The higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of both the floating rate today and the 2028 refinancing, though rapid paydown ($60M in 2025, $28M in Q1 2026) is de-risking that wall.

Q1 2026 confirmed the pivot and the tension within it. Revenue rose 19.5% to $104.8M, net income roughly doubled to $22.8M (from $11.8M), and diluted EPS was $0.20. Recurring revenue fell 21.4% to $66.3M on Pay-TV declines, while non-recurring jumped to $38.5M. Remaining performance obligations (contracted backlog) stood at ~$437M, a better forward-visibility metric than any single quarter's reported revenue.

The earnings surprise record is mixed: two beats (+13.5% in Q1 2026, +29.6% in Q4 2025), one in-line (+1.7% in Q2 2025), and one miss (−23.5% in Q3 2025). This is consistent with a settlement-driven model where quarterly outcomes hinge on deal timing rather than steady accrual.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three legs, of which one is proven, one is promising, and one is decaying.

**Semiconductor (the proof point).** The AMD settlement is the single most important development in the story. Adeia sued AMD in November 2025 over 10 patents covering hybrid bonding / 3D V-Cache used in Ryzen X3D processors, plus a parallel ITC case naming Lenovo and Super Micro. The settlement (March 9–10, 2026) produced a new multi-year AMD semiconductor license and extended Adeia's monetization from memory into leading-edge logic. As Moore's Law slows, hybrid bonding and advanced packaging become more essential, and the DBI portfolio reading on advanced logic is a durable, structurally supported tailwind that aligns with the broader AI-infrastructure capex cycle. RapidCool liquid cooling targets the AI-server thermal bottleneck but is not yet a material contributor.

**OTT (promising).** Microsoft (media) and a third semiconductor customer signed in Q1 2026; Amazon and Disney were prior wins. OTT penetration is the natural offset to Pay-TV decline, but these deals tend to arrive as episodic settlements rather than steady recurring streams.

**Pay-TV (decaying).** The recurring base is anchored to per-subscriber Pay-TV fees in secular decline, and the renewal environment has turned adversarial. DISH became unlicensed on April 1, 2026 (Adeia suing in Colorado over 5 patents; DISH filed a DJ action in NDCA over 10 patents), and DIRECTV filed a declaratory-judgment action in December 2025, with Adeia countersuing in SDNY for breach of contract and trade-secret misappropriation. Two of the three largest historical Pay-TV licensees are now in active litigation. This is the clearest reason the 21% Q1 recurring decline should not be dismissed.

Estimate revisions confirm near-term caution. Current-quarter EPS was revised down 13.1% over 30 days; next-quarter down 13.9% over 90 days. Current-year estimates are roughly flat (+0.5% over 90 days) and next-year is modestly positive (+2.8% over 90 days). This is the opposite of the >30% upward-revision signal that would flag a high-conviction growth entry; consensus is trimming the near term even as it holds the outer years. Growth durability here is real but lumpy, and heavily dependent on converting litigation into licenses.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$33, ADEA trades at ~30x trailing EPS ($0.99) and ~23x the $1.42 current-year consensus, or ~21x the $1.55 next-year estimate. The dividend yield is a token ~0.6%. The reported peer set (Teradata 7.5x, EPAM 13.3x, Kyndryl 13.3x, DXC 88.2x, NCR Voyix 23.8x, median 13.3x) is not a clean comparison; those are IT-services businesses, not IP-licensing platforms. On a business-model basis, Adeia is closer to a levered royalty stream, which supports a premium to services peers but not an open-ended one.

The valuation is not obviously cheap and not obviously stretched. The bull case for the multiple is that trailing earnings understate normalized power if semiconductor licensing compounds and OTT offsets Pay-TV decay; the bear case is that a meaningful share of reported earnings comes from one-time settlements recognized upfront, so a 23x forward multiple may be paying peak-ish, lumpy earnings a growth premium the recurring base does not support. The falling near-term estimate-revision trend argues against treating the current consensus targets ($30 low, $37.5 median, $43 high) as floors; on this name they read more like a fair-value band than a launchpad, because the earnings momentum is not accelerating.

Applying the discipline that a compressed multiple is not itself a buy and, symmetrically, that a re-rated multiple is not itself a sell: the prior 2.4x run reflects genuine fundamental improvement (Disney and AMD settlements, semiconductor growth), so price history is not the basis for the score. The basis is that at ~23x forward on flat-to-slightly-declining near-term revisions, with roughly a quarter of revenue from episodic settlements, there is no identifiable mispricing large enough to justify a high valuation score. We score valuation modestly below neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

Because patent rights are exclusionary, Adeia does not compete for licensees conventionally; its position is defined by portfolio breadth and litigation credibility. On that basis the trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. The semiconductor franchise is strengthening: the AMD win demonstrates the DBI/hybrid-bonding portfolio reads on leading-edge logic, and advanced packaging is a structural beneficiary as chip scaling shifts from transistor density to interconnect and 3D integration. This is a genuine moat-widening event in a growing market.

Against that, the media franchise is weakening in the sense that matters most for cash flow: the willingness of large MVPDs to renew is deteriorating. DISH and DIRECTV moving to litigation signals that the pricing power of the media portfolio against squeezed Pay-TV distributors is being tested. Adeia has a track record of prevailing (Videotron permanent injunction October 2025, Disney global settlement), and litigation leverage is real, but each contested renewal converts a predictable recurring stream into a binary, lumpy outcome. The net competitive trajectory is roughly stable, with semiconductor gains offsetting media erosion, which is why we score this near neutral rather than as an improving-moat story.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and not fully priced:

1. **Revenue lumpiness and recognition timing.** A large share of upside is one-time settlements recognized upfront, making any single quarter or year a poor predictor. The 21% recurring decline in Q1 is the concerning underlying signal beneath 19.5% headline growth.
2. **Deteriorating MVPD renewals.** DISH is unlicensed and DIRECTV is in litigation; both are among the largest historical licensees. This is active, ongoing base erosion rather than a hypothetical.
3. **Customer concentration.** Five customers were 55.7% of FY2025 revenue; in Q1 2026 two customers each exceeded 10% (21.5% and 13.8%), and one customer represented 68% of accounts receivable. A single non-renewal or credit event is material.
4. **Litigation and IPR dependency.** The model requires enforcement litigation (expense up 81% in 2025 to $24.7M). Defendants routinely file Inter Partes Review petitions, which carry meaningful invalidation rates and could impair portfolio value.
5. **Leverage and refinancing.** The ~$356M 2028 balloon at ~7.3% floating rate constrains flexibility and drives the grey-zone Z-score in a higher-for-longer rate environment.
6. **Governance changes.** Two Item 5.02 officer/director filings (January and May 2026) warrant monitoring; details were not fully assessed.
7. **Foreign royalty taxation.** The Q4 2025 Korea Supreme Court ruling overturning territorial royalty sourcing led Adeia to derecognize a withholding-tax receivable, a negative read-through for foreign royalty taxation more broadly.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is call-heavy and complacent, not confirming the elevated short interest. Near-term ATM IV of 87.3% is rich versus 61.2% one-year historical volatility (a 26pp spread), the put/call OI ratio is a low 0.15, near-term skew is −5.0% (call-tilted), and the term structure is in contango. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±16.2%, consistent with a settlement-driven name where any quarter can swing on deal timing. Short interest of 13.7% of float (3.7 days to cover) reflects a real bearish institutional lean on the recurring-revenue erosion, but the options market is not pricing imminent downside. The rich IV means options-based expression (rather than outright equity) is expensive here; it also flags that the market expects a large move around the next print without a directional consensus.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Semiconductor licensing compounds off the AMD proof point as hybrid bonding and advanced packaging become standard in AI-adjacent logic and memory; OTT deals (Microsoft, Amazon, Disney) offset Pay-TV decay; and the DISH/DIRECTV disputes resolve into new licenses the way Disney and AMD did, converting litigation overhang into revenue. FCF near 35% funds continued debt paydown, de-risking the 2028 balloon, and normalized earnings power moves well above the trailing figure. In that scenario the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range around $43.

**Bear Case.** The recurring base keeps eroding faster than settlements can replace it, DISH/DIRECTV litigation drags without favorable resolution (or produces adverse IPR outcomes), and the market discounts the settlement-driven earnings as low-quality and lumpy. The multiple compresses toward the low-$20s on de-rating plus a soft settlement year. Combined with the 2028 refinancing overhang, that puts the stock around $24, roughly 27% below current levels.

The base case sits between these: semiconductor and OTT roughly offset Pay-TV decline, earnings hold in the $1.42–1.55 range, and the stock tracks the middle of the analyst band around $37.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction after the re-rating. The recurring-revenue erosion and near-term negative estimate revisions offset the genuine semiconductor optionality and durable FCF.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Recurring revenue stabilizes or returns to growth for two consecutive quarters, or the DISH/DIRECTV disputes resolve into new multi-year licenses (as Disney and AMD did).
- Consensus EPS revisions turn decisively positive (>+10% over 90 days) alongside a second marquee logic/AI semiconductor licensee beyond AMD.
- Forward multiple compresses below ~18x on stable-to-rising estimates, restoring asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse IPR ruling or court decision that materially impairs a core portfolio (media or DBI).
- Loss of a >10% customer without replacement, or accounts-receivable concentration converting into a credit/write-off event.
- Semiconductor revenue proving a one-time AMD/Microsoft slug rather than a compounding stream, with recurring revenue continuing to decline double-digits.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, with particular attention to recurring-revenue trajectory, RPO backlog, and any development in the DISH and DIRECTV proceedings. The June 2028 debt maturity is the longer-dated structural checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Adeia is a high-margin, cash-generative IP-licensing platform in the middle of a real strategic transition. The AMD hybrid-bonding settlement validates that its semiconductor portfolio reads on leading-edge logic, and the OTT wins provide a credible offset to Pay-TV decay. But the recurring base is contracting, two of its largest MVPD licensees are now in litigation, and the earnings that drove the re-rating are heavily settlement-dependent and lumpy. At ~23x forward earnings on falling near-term estimates, the price already reflects much of the good news, and there is no identifiable mispricing large enough to underwrite a BUY. The 2028 debt balloon and customer concentration keep the risk score below neutral.

We recommend **HOLD**. The business quality and semiconductor optionality are real, and continued debt paydown de-risks the balance sheet, but the risk/reward is roughly balanced after the run. We would turn constructive on evidence that the recurring base has stabilized or that the DISH/DIRECTV disputes convert into licenses, and cautious if IPR/litigation outcomes impair the portfolio or the recurring erosion accelerates.
