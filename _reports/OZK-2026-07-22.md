**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bank OZK is a $5.6bn-market-cap regional bank holding company built around an unusual model for its size: a national commercial real estate construction lending franchise (the Real Estate Specialties Group, or RESG) funded by a Southern deposit-gathering network of roughly $34bn. For most of the last decade, RESG's low-LTV, high-spread construction lending was the bull case. It is now the center of the bear case, and management has responded by deliberately shrinking it. Real estate fell to 52% of total loans in Q1 2026 from 54% the prior quarter, RESG origination in Q1 was the slowest first quarter in five years (~$1bn), and repayments surged to a five-year Q1 high of $1.6bn as private-credit funds refinance OZK's best borrowers away. This produces a self-inflicted earnings squeeze: net income fell 6.8% YoY in Q1 2026 to $159.3M, expenses rose 12% QoQ on the Corporate & Institutional Banking (CIB) build-out, and Q2 2026 net income of $163.3M merely met estimates.

The stock trades at roughly 8.25x trailing and forward earnings, a discount to a regional-bank peer median around 11.5x, with a 3.7% dividend yield. That looks cheap, but the discount is doing work: net charge-offs doubled YoY to 0.57%, nonperforming assets doubled YoY to 1.08% of the portfolio, and five loans concentrated in office and life sciences account for $409.5M of past-dues. Management doubled its allowance for credit losses ahead of expected charge-offs, which is prudent but also signals it sees more pain coming. Next-year consensus EPS has been revised down 1.2% over 90 days even as current-quarter estimates edged up, so there is no clean earnings signal to justify treating the low multiple as a mispricing.

We rate OZK a HOLD. The franchise is solidly profitable with a growing low-cost deposit base and a well-reserved, concentrated credit book, but the investment case hinges on an unresolved question: whether CIB and other diversified verticals ramp revenue fast enough to offset RESG runoff before credit costs and the shrinking high-margin book compress returns further. Until that turn is visible in the numbers (reaccelerating loan growth in the new verticals, stabilizing NPAs, or an estimate-revision turn), the low multiple is a fair price for a bank managing through a transition.

## Company Overview

Bank OZK, headquartered in Little Rock, Arkansas, operates an Arkansas state-chartered bank subsidiary regulated by the Federal Reserve at the holding-company level and the FDIC at the bank level. Its distinguishing feature is RESG, one of the largest construction lenders in the United States, with loan production offices in Dallas, New York, Los Angeles, Atlanta, Austin, Houston, and San Francisco. The bank gathers relatively low-cost deposits across a Southern footprint (Arkansas, Georgia, Florida, Texas, the Carolinas) and deploys that funding into large, complex, national CRE construction loans at attractive spreads. Historically low loan-to-value ratios (around 80% on commercial construction, 65% on raw land) and low losses through prior cycles underpinned the model. The bank grew through 15 acquisitions between 2010 and 2016, including FDIC-assisted deals, and has been organically driven since.

The current strategic narrative is diversification. Management is intentionally reducing CRE concentration, building record deposits, and standing up a fast-growing CIB unit. This is a voluntary de-risking, not a forced deleveraging, and it is the crux of the investment debate: whether OZK is prudently reducing an extreme concentration or surrendering the structural edge that made it a top-returning bank for a decade.

A data note is warranted. Automated regulatory-filing pulls for this ticker map to a stale entity terminating in 2017 (the former "Bank of the Ozarks, Inc."), returning FY2013–2016 XBRL data and a mechanical Altman Z-Score of 0.31 that flags "distress." That Z-Score is not meaningful for a bank; the metric was designed for industrial firms and treats deposits as debt, and it is based on eight-year-old data. It should be disregarded entirely. Current-period figures in this report are sourced from mid-2026 earnings coverage; the FY2016 10-K is used only for franchise structure.

## Financial Analysis

OZK remains solidly profitable at roughly $160M of net income per quarter. Trailing EPS is $6.18, putting the shares at about 8.25x. The deposit franchise is healthy and growing, with record deposits near $34bn, up about $500M YoY. This funding base is the durable asset in the story.

The earnings pressure is a deliberate trade-off. Three forces are compressing near-term profitability simultaneously:

1. **RESG runoff.** Q1 2026 origination fell from $1.2bn in Q4 2025, and FY2026 origination is guided to more than $5bn, roughly flat with 2024–2025. As the highest-yielding book shrinks, net interest income faces a headwind.
2. **Elevated repayments.** Q1 2026 repayments were up $850M YoY. Abundant private-credit liquidity is refinancing OZK's best borrowers away, shrinking the RE book faster than management intended and compressing net interest income beyond the planned runoff.
3. **Expense growth from the CIB build-out.** Operating expenses rose on higher compensation and the cost of standing up new verticals, ahead of the revenue those verticals will eventually produce.

Credit quality is deteriorating from a low base and is well-reserved. Net charge-offs rose from 0.25% a year earlier. Nonperforming assets rose from 0.99% QoQ. The stress is concentrated: the five loans represent 124 of 141 basis points of past-due loans, with two in a sale process, one in sale negotiation, and two in recapitalization. Problem exposure skews to office and life sciences, both structurally challenged sectors.

The earnings-surprise record is telling: over the last four quarters OZK posted two in-line results and two misses (Q3 2025 missed by 5.3%, Q4 2025 by 1.6%, Q1 and Q2 2026 essentially in line). The bank is not beating; it is meeting a lowered bar. This is consistent with a transition year in which visibility is modest and management is managing expectations down rather than surprising to the upside.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story here in the near term, by design. The core RESG engine is contracting, and origination is guided roughly flat. The growth thesis rests entirely on whether CIB and other new verticals can replace runoff at acceptable spreads and credit quality. Management framed Q2 2026 as CIB growth "offsetting" elevated RESG repayments, but no verified CIB balance or yield metrics were available to size that ramp.

Estimate revisions confirm the muted picture. Current-quarter and current-year consensus have edged up marginally (+0.7% and +0.4% over 30 days), but next-year EPS has been revised down 0.9% over 30 days, to $6.36. This is the opposite of the sharp positive turn that would flag a mispriced recovery. The absence of positive estimate momentum is the single most important reason we do not treat the low multiple as an opportunity. Growth is being deliberately deferred, and the market has no reason yet to underwrite a reacceleration.

The macro backdrop for the core business is unfavorable. Construction backlogs are at a four-year low, tariff-driven materials costs are rising, and restrictive monetary policy delays project starts, structurally limiting the origination engine and reinforcing the diversification rationale. Private credit is the swing factor cutting both ways: it out-competes OZK for new deals and accelerates repayment of existing good loans. Management has flagged that this dynamic will persist.

## Valuation Assessment

OZK trades at approximately 8.25x forward and trailing earnings, versus a Regional Banks peer median P/E of 11.5x. Peer comparables cluster in a range: HOMB at 11.5x (3.0% yield), ASB at 10.1x, AX at 10.9x, HWC at 14.7x, AUB at 16.8x. OZK sits below the entire visible peer set on earnings multiple, and its 3.7% dividend yield exceeds the 3.0% peer median.

A low P/E is not a buy signal on its own, and in this case the discount is well-earned rather than a mispricing. The discount reflects three specific concerns the market is correctly pricing: rising credit costs (charge-offs and NPAs both doubled YoY), a shrinking high-margin book whose runoff is accelerating beyond management's control, and construction-lending concentration that carries lumpy, idiosyncratic loss potential. There is no identified catalyst to close the gap: no earnings signal, no estimate-revision turn, no dated event that reprices the stock. The mid-cycle earnings power argument that would justify a higher score cuts the other way here: current earnings are likely near a normal-to-good level for a bank, not a trough, and next-year estimates are drifting down.

Analyst price targets bracket a wide range (low $40, high $61, mean $53.56, median $53) against a current price around $50.78. The stock sits modestly below the mean/median, offering roughly 4-5% to consensus. Because OZK is not a high-momentum stock with rising estimates, we do not treat consensus targets as a floor; they are a reasonable read of a fairly valued transition-year bank. The sentiment trend is degrading, with one buy rating moving to hold in the most recent period, and the analyst distribution (1 strong buy, 2 buy, 6 hold, 1 sell) is centered on hold. This is consistent with a bank trading near fair value given its risk profile. We score valuation at 5.0: cheap on the screen, but the compression reflects genuine uncertainty rather than a correctable error.

## Competitive Landscape

OZK competes on two fronts. For deposits, it faces community and larger regional banks across the Southeast and Southwest, a stable, healthy franchise where record deposits demonstrate durable competitiveness. For CRE construction assets, it competes nationally against money-center banks, debt funds, and increasingly private credit. The competitive position on the asset side is deteriorating in a way the diversification pivot acknowledges: private credit is simultaneously winning new deals and refinancing OZK's existing borrowers away, evidenced by the five-year-high repayment surge. This is a moat compression in the franchise's highest-margin business.

Relative positioning within the sector's credit cycle is unfavorable. The largest banks have been reducing problem CRE aggressively (Bank of America nonperforming commercial loans down 44% YoY, PNC down 26%, Wells cutting). OZK's NPAs are rising, not falling. This is not necessarily a sign of worse underwriting; OZK's construction-heavy, longer-duration book naturally sits at a later point in the credit cycle than stabilized-CRE megabank portfolios, but it means OZK's credit costs are climbing while peers' are normalizing. The trajectory, which matters more than the level, is negative on the asset side even as the deposit franchise remains solid. We score competitive position at 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE construction concentration** is the defining risk. Even at 52% of loans, this is an extraordinary concentration dominated by construction lending, which is higher-risk than stabilized CRE. The bank has historically exceeded regulatory CRE-concentration guidance thresholds, triggering heightened risk-management requirements. This is structural and permanent to the model until diversification materially advances.

**Idiosyncratic large-loan risk** is inherent to RESG. Because the bank makes large, complex loans, credit stress concentrates in a handful of names; five loans drive the bulk of past-dues. A single large deal souring moves the P&L materially, and lumpy charge-offs are a feature, not a bug, of this model.

**Office and life-sciences exposure** drives current NPAs, and both sectors remain structurally challenged with further markdown potential.

**Runoff-to-earnings risk** from private-credit-driven repayments compresses net interest income faster than intended.

**Execution risk on diversification** is meaningful: building CIB means lending where OZK has less through-cycle track record than in RESG, plus a rising expense base. Diversification could import new credit risk rather than reduce it.

**Key-person and succession risk** is significant and easily missed by standardized data. George Gleason has been Chairman and CEO since roughly 1979 and is now in his early 70s. He is the OZK credit culture. The prior departure of the longtime RESG head (Dan Thomas) was a material transition for the core business line. Succession planning is a priority follow-up item that could not be verified from current filings.

Many of these risks are at least partially priced; the sub-peer multiple reflects concentration and credit concerns. But the combination of a binary large-loan structure, a later-cycle credit position than peers, and unresolved succession keeps the idiosyncratic risk that is *not* fully reflected in valuation elevated. We score risk factors at 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly cautious but not alarming. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 28.3% against 25.0% one-year historical volatility, a modest 3.4-point richness that prices some event uncertainty without extreme fear. The put/call skew is balanced at 2.7%, and the term structure is in contango (28.3% near-term vs. 30.6% long-term), a normal, non-stressed configuration. The put/call volume ratio of 2.76 and OI ratio of 1.06 lean bearish but are within normal ranges for a regional bank under scrutiny. The ±6.4% implied move on the nearest expiry is consistent with a bank that meets rather than surprises. Short interest is elevated at 14.7% of float with 13.5 days to cover, indicating meaningful institutional bearish positioning; the high days-to-cover would amplify any positive surprise but also signals that informed money is skeptical of the diversification story. None of this overrides the fundamental read, and the combination of balanced skew and high short interest reinforces a two-sided, uncertain setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** OZK is a proven, disciplined lender voluntarily reducing an extreme concentration before it becomes a forced problem. The deposit franchise is healthy and growing to record levels, providing durable low-cost funding. The credit deterioration is concentrated in five well-identified loans, most already in active resolution, and reserves were doubled preemptively; this is idiosyncratic large-loan lumpiness, not broad portfolio rot. If CIB and new verticals ramp as management intends, OZK emerges as a more diversified, more resilient bank with the same funding advantage and less concentration risk, deserving a re-rating toward the peer 11.5x multiple. On $6.36 of next-year EPS at 10x, that implies roughly $64, with the top of the analyst range at $61. Bull case: $60-64.

**Bear case.** The diversification pivot surrenders the franchise's only structural edge (high-spread national construction lending) for lower-return, less-differentiated CIB assets where OZK lacks a through-cycle track record. RESG runoff accelerates faster than new verticals can replace it, net interest income compresses, and expense growth outpaces revenue. Credit costs keep rising as office/life-sciences stress broadens and the construction book seasons at a later point in the cycle than peers. Next-year estimates continue drifting down. A single large problem loan or a broadening of NPAs takes charge-offs materially higher. In that scenario the multiple compresses further toward the low end of the analyst range near $40, roughly 21% below current levels. Bear case: $40-42.

Neither case is clearly more probable, and the decisive variable, the pace of CIB revenue ramp versus RESG runoff and credit cost escalation, is not yet visible in verified numbers. This is why the recommendation is a hold with an "uncertain" rationale rather than a conviction call in either direction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The yield (3.7%, covered) provides support while the diversification thesis resolves, but there is insufficient evidence for a stronger call in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (currently -1.2% over 90 days) alongside evidence of CIB-driven loan growth reaccelerating total loan balances.
- Net charge-offs and NPAs stabilize or decline for two consecutive quarters (charge-offs back below ~0.40%, NPAs flat-to-down from 1.08%), confirming the credit deterioration was concentrated and self-limiting.
- Verified metrics from the Q2 2026 10-Q (CET1, NIM stability, efficiency ratio) confirming the transition is not eroding core profitability faster than modeled.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- Charge-offs accelerate beyond ~0.80% or NPAs broaden beyond the five identified loans into a wider portfolio problem.
- Net interest income declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters as RESG runoff outpaces CIB growth with no offset.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration or a cut, which would break the income-support anchor.
- Adverse succession developments or further senior-lending departures.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release, with the verified Q2 2026 10-Q, current proxy (for succession and insider activity), and CIB ramp metrics as priority inputs. The private-credit repayment dynamic and construction-backlog data are the key macro variables to monitor between quarters.

## Conclusion

Bank OZK is a well-run, deposit-rich regional bank in the middle of a deliberate transition that trades OFF near-term earnings for reduced concentration risk. It is genuinely cheap on trailing metrics (8.25x earnings, a 3.7% yield, below every visible peer), but that discount is earned, not mistaken. Charge-offs and NPAs have doubled YoY, the highest-margin book is shrinking faster than management intended as private credit refinances its best borrowers away, and next-year estimates are drifting lower with no positive signal. The diversification bet may prove prudent, but its payoff is not yet visible in the numbers, and the earnings-surprise record shows a bank meeting a lowered bar rather than beating.

We recommend HOLD. The overall score of 4.95 reflects a solid funding franchise and proactive reserving offset by a deteriorating asset-side competitive position, rising credit costs, elevated concentration and key-person risk, and the absence of a catalyst to close the valuation gap. The yield supports patience, but this is a wait-for-evidence situation. We would turn constructive on a confirmed estimate-revision turn plus credit stabilization, and cautious on any broadening of the NPA problem or a sequential decline in net interest income. The declining score trajectory (6.5 → 5.7 → 5.1 → 4.95) reflects the accumulating weight of these transition-year headwinds; the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend.
