**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Korn Ferry is a global organizational and talent consultancy that has moved past the FY2024 hiring-slowdown trough and delivered a clean cyclical recovery: FY2026 fee revenue of ~$2.91B (+7% YoY, roughly 5% ex-FX), net income of $277.4M (9.5% margin), and adjusted EBITDA of $497.8M (17.1% margin). The business is asset-light, cash-generative (FCF $324M, ~11% margin, exceeding net income), and carries effectively negative net debt against a single $400M note due December 2027. Management is returning capital aggressively: the quarterly dividend has risen from $0.10 (2021) to $0.55 (March 2026), and FY26 returned $116M of buybacks plus $105M of dividends. The Q4 FY26 print (reported June 23) beat, and the ~$1.9B backlog (+~10% YoY) supports near-term revenue visibility.

The issue is the entry point and the unresolved strategic question. The stock has appreciated roughly 33% from its February low near $59 to $78.25, back within ~2% of its 52-week high of $79.97. At ~15x trailing/forward earnings the multiple looks undemanding versus the HR-services peer median of ~23x, but that gap partly reflects a genuine debate: whether AI compresses the "scope, value, or pricing" of engagements at a labor-intermediation firm, or whether Korn Ferry's Digital platform turns AI into a product. The FY2026 10-K materially expanded its AI risk factor and named LinkedIn, Indeed, Eightfold, HireVue, Paradox, Phenom, iCIMS, and Google for Jobs as disintermediation threats. That is a live, structural overhang the market is pricing, not a boilerplate cyclicality warning.

We rate KFY a HOLD (growth-lean) with an overall score of 5.85. The prior HOLD calls (three consecutive) and the modest score decline (6.1 → 5.9) reflect a stock that has re-rated on the cyclical recovery while the AI question remains open. Financial health is genuinely strong and improving; the remaining upside is capped by full re-rating relative to the recovery and by the absence of a catalyst that resolves the AI debate favorably. The near-term risk/reward is balanced.

## Company Overview

Korn Ferry (fiscal year ending April 30) is a Los Angeles-based diversified people-and-organization consultancy operating 98 offices in 51 countries with 8,965 employees. Despite its GICS classification under Industrials / HR & Employment Services, it is better understood as an IP-and-data-led advisory firm built on proprietary behavioral-science assets: 12B+ data points, 115M+ assessments, and compensation data on 29M professionals.

Fee revenue splits across five solution areas:

- **Executive Search** — $924.1M (32%), ~26% EBITDA margin. ~80% of searches are C-suite/board-level. Top-tier globally against Heidrick & Struggles, Russell Reynolds, Spencer Stuart, and Egon Zehnder.
- **Consulting** — $691.7M (24%), 17% margin. Org strategy, assessment/succession, leadership development, rewards.
- **Professional Search & Interim** — $561.1M (19%), 21.6% margin. Fastest grower (+11%), aided by the Trilogy International acquisition (Nov 2024).
- **Digital** — $363.5M (12.5%), 31% margin (highest). The Talent Suite platform; subscription/license revenue $148.6M (+7.9%). The recurring-revenue engine.
- **RPO** — $367.1M (13%), 15.7% margin; highest cross-referral (66% referred from other solutions).

The differentiation is the integrated "We Are Korn Ferry" model: cross-solution referrals rose from 14% (2018) to 27% of fee revenue in FY2026, >75% of revenue comes from multi-solution clients, ~82% of assignments are repeat clients, and the 350-account Marquee & Diamond program drives ~40% of revenue (growing 8%). The client roster is elite: 94% of the S&P 100, 82% of the S&P 500.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Fee revenue moved from $2.86B (FY23) to a $2.76B trough (FY24, when a hiring slowdown forced a $68.6M restructuring) to $2.73B (FY25) and up to $2.91B (FY26). Roughly two points of FY26 growth were FX tailwind. This is a credible early-cycle recovery led by Professional Search & Interim, Executive Search NA/EMEA, and Consulting.

**Profitability, strong and expanding off the trough.** Net income improved from $169M (FY24, 6.1% margin) to $246M (FY25, 9.0%) to $277.4M (FY26, 9.5%), with diluted EPS of $5.22. Adjusted EBITDA rose from $408M (14.8%) to $464M (17.0%) to $497.8M (17.1%). The 230bps expansion off FY24 owes much to the absence of the FY24 restructuring charge and the cyclical rebound. FY26 margin was essentially flat versus FY25 — the easy margin expansion is behind, and further gains likely require Digital/AI operating leverage rather than cyclical tailwind. Operating margin was 12.8%, ROE 14.2%, ROA 6.9%.

**Cash flow quality is high.** Operating cash flow rose to $414M (from $364M); FCF was $324M (~11% margin), comfortably above net income, a sign of clean earnings. Capex of $90M was elevated by ~$27M YoY due to Talent Suite/Digital platform investment.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $4.06B; stockholders' equity $1.97B. Debt is a single $400M 4.625% senior note due December 2027 plus $72M of COLI borrowings; the new $850M Wells Fargo revolver (July 2025) is essentially undrawn ($845.7M available). Net debt is effectively negative. Of the $1.38B gross cash and securities, only ~$729M is truly unencumbered — the rest sits in trust against deferred-comp/bonus liabilities. Analysts using headline cash for a net-cash valuation overstate liquidity by roughly half. Current ratio 1.94; D/E 1.06 on a book basis but modest on an EBITDA basis. The Altman Z of 2.55 (grey zone) is structurally depressed for an asset-light services firm carrying 23% of assets in goodwill and is not a credible distress signal given negative net debt and strong FCF.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, with beats in the low-single-digit percentage range — a company that meets or modestly exceeds consensus, supporting confidence in forward estimates but not signaling a step-change in visibility.

Financial health is the standout dimension and merits a 7.2 — the same level as the prior report, confirming the improving trend (up from 6.5). Cash generation, negative net debt, an easily refinanceable maturity wall, and disciplined working capital in an asset-light model justify the score.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is constructive but not spectacular. Q1 FY2027 guidance (fee revenue $725–745M, ~17% adjusted EBITDA margin, adjusted EPS $1.32–1.38) points to continued mid-single-digit organic growth. Professional Search & Interim and the Digital subscription base are the structural growth vectors; Executive Search is more cyclical and levered to corporate hiring confidence.

The longer-term growth thesis hinges on two things. First, whether the Digital segment — 31% EBITDA margins and rising subscription revenue — can scale enough to shift the mix toward recurring, higher-margin, less cyclical revenue. Second, whether AI is a net product opportunity (embedding AI into Talent Suite and RPO to expand engagement value) rather than a net disintermediation threat (clients and candidates using AI to bypass the intermediary). The expanded AI risk factor signals management views this as the central long-term question, and it is not yet resolved in the numbers.

We do not observe the estimate-revision velocity or the revenue acceleration that would flag a strong buy signal. This is a steady mid-single-digit compounder in cyclical recovery, not a step-change growth story. Growth Prospects scores 5.8 — directionally positive, supported by backlog and Digital, but tempered by the flat sequential margin and the AI overhang.

## Valuation Assessment

At $78.25, KFY trades at ~15x trailing and forward earnings, a ~2.8% dividend yield, and roughly ~8x EV/EBITDA (adjusting for the ~$729M truly free cash, not the headline gross balance). Against the HR & Employment Services peer median P/E of ~23x, KFY looks cheap. But the peer set is heterogeneous and instructive: the SaaS-heavy names (PCTY ~23x, PAYC ~15x) command recurring-revenue multiples, staffing pure-plays (MAN, NSP) are loss-making on trailing EPS, and RHI trades at ~24x with a much higher (7.6%) yield.

The peer discount is not a free lunch. Two factors explain it. First, roughly two-thirds of KFY's revenue (Search, Consulting, Professional Search) is project-based and cyclical rather than recurring, which the market rationally discounts versus subscription software. Second, the AI disintermediation debate specifically threatens the labor-intermediation core. A "cheap" multiple on a business facing a credible structural question is the value-trap signature that warrants skepticism; cheapness alone is not a buy thesis without a catalyst that closes the gap.

We do not treat the recent run-up as a reason to mark valuation down; price appreciation driven by the recovery is legitimate. Nor do we treat proximity to the 52-week high as expensive per se. The issue is that the cyclical re-rating (multiple recovering as earnings recovered off the FY24 trough) is largely captured. From here, the re-rating that would push the multiple toward peers requires proof that Digital/AI mix-shift is structural and margin-accretive — a multi-quarter proof point, not a near-term catalyst. The valuation score of 5.5 (roughly flat versus the prior 5.8, down from 7.2 in March) reflects a fair-but-not-asymmetric setup: modest absolute cheapness and yield support, offset by the cyclical/structural discount being at least partly deserved.

## Competitive Landscape

Korn Ferry's moat is the integrated model, not any single segment. No competitor replicates the full talent lifecycle from executive search through consulting, professional/interim staffing, RPO, and IP-driven Digital. The evidence of moat durability is in the cross-referral trend, multi-solution penetration, and repeat-client rate. Consultant portability — the classic search-industry risk — is well-mitigated: the top 10 consultants account for only ~4% of revenue.

The competitive trajectory is the harder call. Executive Search holds top-tier position against the other three global firms, and Digital's 31% margin with growing subscriptions suggests a genuine IP advantage. But the FY2026 10-K names a widening field of AI-native talent-tech players (Eightfold, HireVue, Paradox, Phenom, iCIMS) plus scale platforms (LinkedIn, Indeed, Google for Jobs) as disintermediation threats. In consulting and staffing, KFY competes against Aon, Deloitte, McKinsey, Mercer, WTW, Randstad, Robert Half, and Michael Page. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding on trajectory: the integrated model is defensible today, but the direction of the AI threat is genuinely uncertain and the market is pricing that uncertainty. We score Competitive Position 5.5 — a solid position with a real, unresolved structural question about its durability, matching the prior report's downgrade from 6.5.

Korn Ferry was named Founding Partner and Official Talent & Organizational Consulting Partner of the LA28 Olympic & Paralympic Games, building the 5,000+ person Games workforce. This is a marquee credential rather than a material revenue driver.

## Risk Assessment

1. **AI disintermediation — the defining risk.** The expanded AI risk factor explicitly warns that clients, candidates, and competitors using AI could compress "the scope, value, or pricing of our engagements," and flags reliance on a limited number of third-party AI providers with pricing power (margin risk). KFY is investing in embedding AI into Talent Suite, but this remains a genuine two-sided risk for a labor-intermediation business and is not yet resolved in the data.

2. **Cyclicality / macro sensitivity.** Demand tracks corporate hiring confidence. FY2024 showed how fast this turns. A hiring air pocket would hit Search and Professional Search first. 48% of fee revenue is non-U.S., adding FX and geopolitical exposure.

3. **Goodwill impairment.** $951M goodwill (23% of assets) from the acquisition-led strategy (Trilogy, Infinity, Salo). A demand shock could trigger write-downs.

4. **Reduced transparency from FY27 reporting change.** Effective Q1 FY2027, KFY moves from solution-based to regional reporting (Americas, EMEA, APAC). This aligns disclosure to the integrated model but removes visibility into the solution-level margins that underpin the mix-shift thesis. Analysts lose the ability to track the Digital scaling story cleanly.

5. **Debt maturity (minor).** Refinancing the $400M note at higher rates is plausible but easily managed given the undrawn revolver and negative net debt.

6. **Insider signal neutral-to-soft.** July 2026 activity is dominated by routine RSU grants and tax-withholding dispositions (noise). But CEO Burnison made ~$5.4M of open-market sales in Oct 2025 at ~$70, and a few executives made small open-market sales in July 2026 at $74–79. These are modest relative to holdings and appear to be diversification, but there is no open-market buying.

The risk profile is dominated by a structural, hard-to-quantify AI question layered on ordinary cyclicality, with a transparency reduction arriving just as investors need to track the offsetting Digital story. There is no imminent binary catastrophe risk, but the AI overhang is not fully resolvable with current data. Risk Factors scores 5.3.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 32.0% versus 1-year historical volatility of 29.0% — roughly fair (2.9pp spread), sitting at the 7th percentile within the current chain, so options are not pricing elevated near-term stress. IV has compressed sharply from 43.9% to 32.0% since June 22 as the stock rose ~11%, consistent with the post-earnings uncertainty being resolved favorably. The one caution: the 1-month put/call skew is elevated at 13.6% (versus flat-to-slightly-negative skew at 3–6 months), indicating some near-term put demand and hedging into the current level. The term structure is in contango (32.0% near vs. 36.4% long), a normal structure. The options market corroborates a stock that has digested its earnings beat, with mild near-term downside hedging but no signal of an imminent large move.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($90–95).** Cyclical recovery has legs; the backlog converts to sustained mid-single-digit-plus organic growth. Digital scales its 31%-margin subscription base and AI proves accretive (embedded in Talent Suite/RPO, expanding engagement value), driving mix-shift toward recurring revenue and pushing the multiple toward the ~20x+ peer range. Aggressive capital return (dividend up 5.5x since 2021, $227.7M buyback authorization remaining) compounds per-share value on a slowly declining share count. A re-rating to ~18x on ~$5.60 forward EPS supports ~$100.

**Bear case ($58–62).** A hiring air pocket drives a repeat of FY24 — declining fee revenue and another restructuring charge. AI disintermediation begins compressing Search and staffing engagement pricing, the market extends the structural discount, and the multiple stays stuck at ~13–14x on flat-to-declining earnings. This maps to the February low near $59, roughly 25% below the current price.

**Base case ($80–85).** Continued mid-single-digit growth, roughly flat ~17% EBITDA margin, and steady capital return, with the multiple holding near ~15x as the AI question remains unresolved. The stock grinds modestly higher on earnings growth and dividends without meaningful multiple expansion — a total return roughly in line with mid-single-digit EPS growth plus the ~2.8% yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-lean).** The business quality and balance sheet support ownership, and the backlog gives near-term visibility, but the stock near $78 has captured the cyclical re-rating and the AI question caps asymmetric upside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Digital/subscription revenue accelerates to double-digit growth with evidence AI is expanding (not compressing) engagement value, which would justify a multiple toward peers.
- A pullback to the low-$60s (roughly the February range), where the ~13x multiple and higher yield would restore asymmetric downside protection against unchanged fundamentals.
- Backlog growth sustains above 10% for two consecutive quarters alongside positive estimate-revision velocity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Fee revenue growth turns negative YoY or management announces a restructuring charge, signaling a cyclical rollover.
- Concrete evidence of AI-driven pricing/scope compression in Search or Professional Search (declining revenue-per-engagement, client attrition to AI-native platforms).
- A goodwill impairment on the $951M balance, indicating a demand-shock reassessment.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q1 FY2027 print (against the $725–745M / $1.32–1.38 EPS guide), the first report under the new regional segment structure — watch for whether the reduced disclosure obscures the Digital-mix story. A subsequent read at the Q2 FY2027 print should confirm or refute backlog conversion.

## Conclusion

Korn Ferry is a high-quality, asset-light consultancy with genuinely strong finances — negative net debt, $324M FCF, a comfortably covered and rapidly growing dividend, and an easily managed 2027 maturity. FY2026 confirmed the cyclical recovery, and the backlog underpins near-term visibility. The financial-health improvement is real.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is entry point and unresolved structure. The stock has run ~33% off its February low back to within ~2% of its 52-week high, capturing most of the recovery re-rating, while the multiple discount to peers is at least partly deserved given cyclicality and the live AI disintermediation question that management elevated in the FY2026 10-K. This is the third consecutive HOLD, and the bar for a change is appropriately higher; nothing in the current data resolves the AI debate or restores the asymmetric upside that a lower entry would provide. Hold for the backlog visibility, shareholder returns, and Digital optionality; wait for either a pullback into the low-$60s or concrete evidence that AI is a product rather than a threat before upgrading. The risk/reward near $78 is balanced.
