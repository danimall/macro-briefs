**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BlackRock crossed $15 trillion in assets under management in Q2 2026 ($15.345T at June 30, up 22.5% year-over-year), the first firm to reach that milestone. The signal underneath the headline is that organic base-fee growth ran ~8% — real client flows rather than market appreciation. Trailing-twelve-month net inflows of ~$868B and $191.7B of net inflows in Q2 alone confirm that clients are actively adding capital across the iShares ETF franchise (fixed-income ETFs drew ~$92B in the quarter), private markets, and cash. The firm has beaten consensus for four straight quarters, most recently by 9.4% in Q2, and consensus EPS estimates have been revised up ~6% across every forward window over the past 90 days.

Despite this, the stock trades at ~$1,038, roughly 15% below its October 2025 high of $1,219.94 and near the middle of its 52-week range. On the raised current-year consensus of $56.33, that is ~18.4x forward earnings. The peer median for asset managers and custody banks sits near 31x. Some of that gap is justified — BlackRock's index-heavy revenue mix carries structurally lower fee rates than the private-capital peers (BX, KKR, APO) that anchor the peer multiple — but the discount also reflects two concerns the market is over-weighting: secular fee compression, which BlackRock is actively out-running via its mix shift, and integration risk from the GIP/HPS/Preqin acquisitions, which is a bounded execution question rather than a thesis-breaker.

We move the rating to BUY. The score trajectory has drifted lower over the past four reports (7.7 → 6.6), and the prior two calls were HOLDs, so the bar for changing direction is appropriately high. What clears it here is the combination of a lower price on improving fundamentals: estimates rising, a fourth consecutive beat, and a specific re-rating path as private-markets fee accretion converts to reported margin. The management-quality dimension remains the drag: the Larry Fink concentration/succession question and the shift away from a buyback-centric capital allocation toward large M&A are genuine, and we score that dimension accordingly. The price/fundamental disconnect is the dominant fact.

## Company Overview

BlackRock is the world's largest asset manager, built on several reinforcing franchises:

- **iShares ETFs** — the dominant global ETF platform and the primary organic-growth engine. The platform also houses the iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT), now at ~$47B, making BlackRock the leading institutional gateway for crypto exposure.
- **Active and index equity/fixed income** across institutional and retail channels.
- **Private markets / alternatives** — the strategic growth thrust, expanded through the 2024–2025 acquisitions of Global Infrastructure Partners (GIP), HPS Investment Partners (private credit), and Preqin (private-markets data). These carry meaningfully higher fee rates than index products and represent stickier, longer-duration capital.
- **Aladdin** — BlackRock's technology and risk-management platform, licensed to third parties. This is a high-margin, recurring-revenue software business that also creates deep client lock-in and differentiates BlackRock from pure-play managers. The Aladdin + Preqin combination extends that data/technology edge across public and private assets.
- **Cash management and advisory.**

The core competitive advantage is scale-driven cost leadership — enabling ultra-low-fee index products competitors cannot profitably match — combined with product breadth (one-stop coverage across index, active, private, and technology). Revenue is monetized primarily through recurring AUM-linked base fees, supplemented by performance fees and technology-services revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale and growth.** Q2 2026 revenue was ~$7B. Organic base-fee growth is the number that matters most for an asset manager: it isolates growth driven by client flows and fee mix from growth that is merely a function of the S&P 500 rising.

**Revenue quality.** The bulk of revenue is recurring, AUM-linked base fees — an annuity-like stream. Performance fees rose in Q2, and Aladdin technology services add a higher-margin, diversifying, recurring layer. The mix shift toward private markets is margin-accretive because those products carry materially higher fee rates than index.

**Profitability and earnings power.** The four consecutive EPS beats were Q3 2025 +1.7%, Q4 2025 +6.7%, Q1 2026 +7.5%, and Q2 2026 +9.4%. Q2 2026 non-GAAP EPS was $13.91 versus a $12.72 estimate (a $1.19 beat). Trailing EPS is ~$35.31 and next-year consensus is $64.14, implying continued double-digit earnings growth. The beat cadence is accelerating, which supports confidence in those forward estimates rather than treating them as aspirational.

**Balance sheet — the Altman Z-Score is a false signal here.** A low Z-Score reading (~1.0) for BlackRock does not indicate distress. The Z-Score was designed for industrial firms; BlackRock's balance sheet is inflated by consolidated investment products and separate-account assets that carry equal and offsetting liabilities. These are client assets, not corporate leverage. On a corporate-only basis, BlackRock is conservatively capitalized with manageable debt and strong interest coverage. Any screen flagging BlackRock as distressed on leverage is misfiring on this name. The one genuine balance-sheet development is the growth in goodwill and intangibles following GIP/HPS/Preqin, which raises impairment risk if the deals underperform.

**Capital allocation.** BlackRock returns capital via a ~2.1% dividend yield and buybacks, but 2024–2025 marked a deliberate pivot toward large debt- and equity-funded M&A to build the private-markets franchise, a departure from its historically buyback-heavy posture. This is the single most consequential financial development, and it cuts both ways: it accelerates the mix shift into higher-fee assets but introduces integration and dilution considerations that were absent from the prior model.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth vectors are working simultaneously, which is unusual for a firm this size:

1. **Organic base-fee growth of ~8%** demonstrates the record AUM is not purely a market-beta artifact. For a $15T manager, sustained high-single-digit organic fee growth is a strong result, driven by iShares fixed-income flows (~$92B in Q2) and active/alternatives demand.

2. **The private-markets pivot** re-rates BlackRock's revenue mix toward higher-fee, longer-duration capital. GIP (infrastructure), HPS (private credit), and Preqin (data) reposition the firm as a top-tier alternatives manager rather than an index house. This is margin-accretive by design; the question is execution, not direction.

3. **Crypto and technology optionality.** IBIT is now a needle-moving flow contributor, and Aladdin's third-party licensing plus Preqin's private-markets data give the firm a recurring, high-margin software layer that competitors cannot easily replicate.

The estimate-revision picture confirms momentum: current-year EPS was revised up 6.0% over both 30 and 90 days, next-quarter +6.3%/+6.4%, and next-year +5.7%/+5.8%. This is not the +30% velocity that flags a trough-earnings recovery — BlackRock is a large-cap compounder, not a turnaround — but consistent mid-single-digit upward revisions across every window on a stock that has fallen ~15% is a favorable divergence. Estimates are rising while the price has come down.

The prior report's downgrade of Growth Prospects (from 8.5 to 6.8) reflected worry about fee compression and market-driven AUM. The Q2 print partly reverses that concern: 8% organic fee growth and accelerating beats argue the growth engine is intact. We set Growth Prospects at 7.0 — improved from the prior read but held below the peak given the structural fee-compression headwind and the fact that a meaningful slice of AUM growth still rides equity-market levels.

The peer set is heterogeneous, which matters. The high-multiple names (BX, KKR, APO) are pure private-capital managers whose fee-related earnings and carried-interest streams command premium multiples. The low-multiple names (BK, AMP) are custody/advisory businesses closer to BlackRock's index core. BlackRock straddles both: its index-heavy base is priced like a scaled, lower-fee franchise, but the point of the GIP/HPS/Preqin pivot is to shift the earnings mix toward the private-capital cohort that trades at 30x+. As that mix shift converts to reported private-markets fee-related earnings, the appropriate multiple should migrate upward. That is the re-rating mechanism, and it is specific rather than a hope for generic multiple expansion.

On our lessons about valuation as an anti-predictive signal: cheapness alone is not a buy thesis, and we do not treat it as one here. The high valuation score requires an identified catalyst for the market being wrong. That catalyst is the private-markets fee-accretion story showing up in reported segment margin over the next several quarters, combined with continued upward estimate revisions on a de-rated stock. The stock is not cheap in absolute terms; it is cheap relative to its own improving fee mix and relative to a peer group it is deliberately moving toward.

Analyst targets support the asymmetry: mean $1,313.5, median $1,310, low $1,190, high $1,488 — all above the current price. On a name with a forward P/E well under 30x and rising estimates, consensus targets tend to lag and get revised upward as the fundamentals confirm. We set Valuation at 6.8.

## Competitive Landscape

BlackRock competes with Vanguard and State Street at the passive/index end (a scale-driven price war it is structurally positioned to win) and increasingly with Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Brookfield, and Ares in private markets following the GIP/HPS deals. The industry's defining dynamic is a barbell: relentless fee compression at the passive end and a land-grab for higher-fee private capital at the other. BlackRock is one of the few firms with the distribution scale to play both ends, and the Aladdin + Preqin combination gives it a data/technology edge spanning public and private assets that competitors cannot easily assemble.

The competitive question is whether it can retain acquired private-markets talent and compete on investment performance against entrenched alternatives specialists. This is where the prior report's downgrade of Competitive Position (9.5 → 7.5) was warranted: BlackRock is a challenger in private markets, not the incumbent, and its moat in that arena is being built rather than defended. Its position in ETFs and technology is widening, and the record AUM plus 8% organic fee growth argue the overall competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than eroding. We hold Competitive Position at 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

- **Market-driven revenue sensitivity.** Fees are AUM-linked, so a sustained equity/bond drawdown compresses revenue directly. A material share of the recent AUM record rode S&P 500 gains; this cuts both ways and is the dominant systematic risk. It is largely priced.
- **Structural fee compression.** The secular shift to passive and ongoing price competition pressures blended fee rates. The mix shift to private markets and Aladdin is the answer; execution is critical.
- **M&A integration and intangibles.** GIP, HPS, and Preqin are large, recent, and central to the thesis. Talent retention and margin realization carry execution risk, and goodwill/intangibles have grown materially, creating impairment exposure if the deals underperform.
- **Regulatory and political exposure.** As a systemically visible $15T manager, BlackRock faces potential systemic-risk regulation, ESG-related political backlash in the U.S. balanced against ESG demands elsewhere, and proxy-voting scrutiny given its outsized voting power.
- **Crypto/IBIT.** The IBIT position sits in a volatile, still-evolving regulatory asset class, tying a growing slice of flows to crypto price action and regulatory outcomes.
- **Key-person/succession.** Long-standing dependence on CEO/Chairman Larry Fink, whose combined role concentrates leadership. Succession is a genuine, under-priced qualitative risk.

None of these are new binary catalysts likely to move the stock sharply in the next quarter; they are known, largely-priced structural exposures, with the integration and succession threads warranting the most monitoring. We set Risk Factors at 6.0.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 32.8% against 26.7% one-year historical volatility — rich, at the 60th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (32.8% near-term vs 26.2% at six months), which typically prices imminent event stress; here that reflects the July 15 earnings print and its immediate aftermath rather than a durable stress signal, and the six-month tenor sitting below realized vol suggests the market does not expect sustained turbulence. Put/call skew is elevated at 4.6% near-term with a put/call volume ratio of 1.09, indicating some downside hedging demand — worth flagging against a BUY, though the six-month skew turns negative (-3.5%), consistent with balanced-to-constructive longer-dated positioning. Short interest is negligible at 1.4% of float (2.4 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded bearish base nor squeeze dynamics. The net read: near-term hedging around the print, no structural bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($1,488).** Private-markets integration delivers the projected fee/margin uplift, organic base-fee growth stays near or above 8%, IBIT and Aladdin/Preqin compound as high-margin recurring revenue, and the earnings mix shift drives multiple re-rating toward the private-capital peer cohort. On next-year consensus of $64.14 at ~23x, the stock reaches the high analyst target.

**Bear case ($900, ~-13%).** A sustained equity/bond drawdown compresses AUM-linked revenue, fee compression outruns the mix shift, one or more of the GIP/HPS/Preqin deals underperforms and triggers goodwill impairment plus talent departures, and succession uncertainty around Fink weighs on the multiple. On flat-to-lower earnings at a de-rated ~16x, the stock revisits the low end of its range.

**Base case ($1,220).** Estimates continue their mid-single-digit upward drift, private-markets accretion shows in reported segment margin, and the forward multiple normalizes modestly upward from ~18.4x. This is ~21.6x current-year consensus of $56.33, or ~19x next-year $64.14 — a re-rating that recovers the October high without requiring full convergence to the private-capital peer multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $1,220.** ~21.6x current-year consensus EPS (≈19x next-year). This sits between the bear and bull scenarios and recovers the 52-week high on modest multiple normalization plus estimate follow-through.

**Bull case target: $1,488** — ~23x next-year EPS as the mix shift drives convergence toward the private-capital peer cohort.

**Bear case target: $900** (~-13% from current) — sustained market drawdown plus integration disappointment and multiple de-rating to ~16x.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the Q3 2026 (October) and Q4 2026 (January) earnings prints, which will show whether organic base-fee growth holds near 8% and whether private-markets segment margin is expanding.

**Upside/downside from current (~$1,038):** +17.5% to base case, +43% to bull case, -13% to bear case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Organic base-fee growth falls below ~4% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the mix shift is not out-running fee compression.
2. Consensus current-year EPS estimates reverse and fall more than 5% from the current $56.33, reversing the positive revision trend.
3. A goodwill impairment charge on GIP/HPS/Preqin or disclosed material talent departures from the acquired private-markets teams.

## Conclusion

**Recommendation: BUY.** BlackRock is the world's largest asset manager, compounding on 8% organic base-fee growth rather than market beta alone, in the middle of a deliberate pivot toward higher-fee private markets and data that should lift both its margin profile and its warranted multiple. The stock has fallen ~15% from its October high while consensus estimates rose ~6% over 90 days and the firm delivered a fourth straight beat — a lower price on improving fundamentals, which strengthens the buy case. At ~18.4x forward earnings against a 31x peer median, with every analyst target above the current price and a specific re-rating catalyst in the private-markets accretion story, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside.

The rating change from HOLD reverses a four-report downward drift, so we have set a high bar. The remaining reservation is management quality: the Fink succession/concentration question and the shift from buybacks to large M&A are legitimate, and we score that dimension at 5.5 rather than papering over it. The dominant fact is the price/fundamental disconnect, and it favors ownership. We are on the right side of the two dimensions our own record trusts most — financial health is strong and the fee-compression concern that pressures valuation is being actively out-run.
