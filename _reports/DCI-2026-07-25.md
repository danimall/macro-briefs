**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Donaldson is a global filtration franchise built on a razor/razor-blade dynamic: engineered filtration systems get specified into OEM equipment (construction, mining, agriculture, transportation, gas turbines), which locks in a long, high-margin stream of replacement-filter demand. Aftermarket alone is roughly half of company sales and is the most resilient, profitable business unit; it grew through FY2025 even as new-equipment sales fell. This is a durable, cash-generative compounder with ROE of 25.2%, an Altman Z-Score of 3.33, and a dividend-aristocrat cash-return record.

The near-term story is a recovery obscured by prior-year noise. FY2025 GAAP net income fell 11.3% to $367M, but $62M of that was a pre-tax intangible impairment in bioprocessing (Univercells and Solaris write-downs), not operational deterioration. Nine-month FY2026 data through April confirms the underlying health: sales +4.3% to $2,827M and diluted EPS +31.9% to $2.75, with Mobile Solutions margins pushing toward 20% and Life Sciences turning solidly profitable. Q3 was management's self-described strongest quarter to date.

This remains a HOLD rather than a BUY because of the changed risk profile layered on top of a full valuation. Donaldson closed its largest-ever acquisition, the ~$820M all-cash Facet deal, on May 4, 2026, funded almost entirely with new variable-rate debt that roughly doubles long-term debt to ~$1.4B. It did so with a C-suite that is largely 18 months old or newer (new CEO, new CFO, new CHRO) and a mixed inorganic track record (three of four bioprocessing deals underdelivered). A self-inflicted Power Generation production relocation also drove Industrial Solutions segment margins down ~400bps. At ~$92.79, roughly 23-24x forward EPS against tightened 3-5% organic growth guidance and a sell-side consensus mean of $96.8 (median $95.0), the quality is real but the margin of safety is thin. Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, not the +30% inflection that would justify paying up.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1915, Donaldson is a technology-led filtration company operating ~150 locations (77 manufacturing/distribution) across six continents with ~15,000 employees. It reports three segments:

- **Mobile Solutions (62% of FY2025 sales, $2,291M):** Air, fuel, lube, hydraulic, and emissions filtration for off-road and on-road equipment. The critical sub-unit is Aftermarket (~$1,821M, ~50% of total company sales): recurring, high-margin replacement demand. Segment EBT margin ~18%.
- **Industrial Solutions (30%, $1,104M):** Industrial Air Filtration (dust/fume/mist collectors), Industrial Gases/Hydraulics, Power Generation (gas-turbine inlet systems), and a growing Aerospace & Defense unit.
- **Life Sciences (8%, $296M):** Food & Beverage, Disk Drive (data-center HDD filters), vehicle electrification/medical venting, microelectronics, and bioprocessing (bioreactors, chromatography for cell/gene therapy).

Its moat rests on proprietary media (Ultra-Web, PowerCore, Synteq XP), more than 3,100 patents, deep OEM design-in relationships, and global scale. No single customer exceeds 10% of sales. Geographic mix is US/Canada 44%, EMEA 28%, APAC 17%, LATAM 11%. A long-standing 50/50 JV with Caterpillar (AFSI, since 1986) contributes ~$10M/yr in equity earnings.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet and returns (pre-Facet):** Strong. ROE 25.2%, ROA 12.3%, current ratio 1.93, Altman Z 3.33 (safe zone). Historical leverage was modest at D/E ~1.05 with total debt at 31.5% of capitalization.

**Revenue:** Steady, low-single-digit organic. FY2022 $3,307M → FY2023 $3,431M → FY2024 $3,586M → FY2025 $3,691M (+2.9%). Growth is driven by pricing and aftermarket volume/share gains; new-equipment (off-road/on-road) is cyclical and declined in FY2025.

**FY2025 earnings decline was mostly optical.** EPS was $3.05 vs $3.38; the $62M pre-tax bioprocessing impairment accounted for the bulk. Operating margin compressed to 13.4% (from 15.2%) and gross margin to 34.8% (from 35.5%) on footprint-optimization costs and tariff-driven LIFO inflation.

**FY2026 recovery is confirmed in the 9-month numbers.** Net income rose +28.4% to $324.5M. Q3 net earnings were $118.1M (EPS $1.00), Mobile Solutions margins expanded toward ~20%, and Life Sciences turned to an 8.9% 9M EBT margin from breakeven. The headline +32% YTD EPS growth flatters the underlying rate because the prior-year comparison carried the impairment; the clean operating improvement is real but more modest than the headline.

**Earnings quality and delivery:** The four-quarter surprise record is mixed: one beat, two in-line, one miss (the March 2026 quarter actual $0.83 vs $0.90 estimate, a 7.5% miss). This is not a company that reliably clears consensus by wide margins, which tempers confidence in aggressive forward estimates.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** FY2025 FCF was $340M (9.2% margin); operating cash flow dipped to $419M from $493M on working-capital and tax timing. The company returned ~$463M in FY2025 (dividends $132M + buybacks $331M); buybacks slightly exceeded FCF and were partly debt-funded. Buybacks were deliberately throttled to $111M in 9M FY2026 to conserve capacity for Facet. The dividend was raised again to $0.32/quarter (declared May 2026), extending a multi-decade record. Current yield is ~1.4%, roughly in line with the peer median (1.1%).

**The balance sheet is being transformed.** The May 4 Facet close was funded with ~$820M of new variable-rate debt (Term SOFR + spread, initial ~4.6%), alongside a $400M delayed-draw term loan and a revolver upsized to $600M (maturity extended to 2030). The company remained covenant-compliant, but the equity story now depends on Facet integration and deleveraging in a still-elevated rate environment, a higher-risk profile than DCI historically carried. This drives the Financial Health score to 6.0: the underlying cash engine is strong and covenants are intact, but the deliberate shift from a lightly-levered compounder to ~$1.4B of mostly-floating debt is a real derating of the balance-sheet resilience the market once priced.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is the durable but unspectacular core: management tightened FY2026 organic guidance to ~3-5%, and Stifel cut its target to $91 on that revision. The aftermarket annuity supplies resilience, not acceleration.

The growth levers that could lift the trajectory are inorganic and mix-shift driven:

1. **Facet ($828.7M):** Adds mission-critical fuel/fluid filtration for aerospace & defense and power generation (Tulsa + Spain, ~250 employees). Management guided $25-30M in Q4 FY2026 sales and a ~$10M annualized benefit target in FY2027. This deepens two secularly-favored, higher-margin end markets and reduces reliance on cyclical diesel equipment.

2. **Power generation demand:** Gas-turbine inlet systems benefit from the data-center and grid buildout. Donaldson participates as a filtration pick-and-shovel, though the current constraint is execution (see below), not demand.

3. **Life Sciences:** Food & Beverage and Disk Drive are growing, and Life Sciences turned profitable in FY2026. Bioprocessing remains the long-term secular bet on biologics and cell/gene therapy, but the track record here is poor.

Estimate revisions do not support an inflection thesis. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $3.95 (down 0.7% over 90 days), next-year $4.38 (up 1.0%). This is stability, not the +30%/90-day velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection. Growth Prospects score 5.5, reflecting a healthy, diversifying franchise with two secular tailwinds (A&D, power gen) offset by low organic growth, a decelerated guidance range, and no revision momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

At $92.79, DCI trades at ~24x forward EPS (24.5x on the reported forward P/E, ~23.5x on the $3.95 current-year consensus) and ~30x trailing FY2025 GAAP EPS. On a growth-adjusted basis this is not cheap: paying ~24x for 3-5% organic growth (mid-single-digit total with Facet) implies a PEG well above 2, and the quality premium is already embedded.

Peer context supports a "full, not stretched" read. The Industrial Machinery peer median P/E is 31.2 (FLS 28.7, TKR 31.2, PNR 18.2, SPXC 44.7, GTLS negative). DCI's ~24x sits below the peer median, but the peer set is heterogeneous and several trade on higher near-term growth or cyclical recovery. The discount to peers is partly justified by Donaldson's lower organic growth rate and the newly-added leverage.

The valuation dimension has been anti-predictive across recent cohorts: cheap-looking stocks have tended to underperform, and a modest discount to a scattered peer group is not a mispricing catalyst. DCI's discount to peers is not a specific reason the market is wrong; it reflects lower growth and higher post-Facet risk. Analyst consensus mean is $96.8, median $95.0, with a $79-123 range; the stock trades essentially at/below consensus, and Stifel's $91 Hold sits below the current price. On a premium-quality name at a full multiple, proximity to consensus is not a floor; it reflects the same quality-premium optimism baked into the price.

There is one unmodeled upside: the Q3 10-Q disclosed the Supreme Court's Feb 20, 2026 ruling that IEEPA does not authorize the President's tariffs, with the Court of International Trade ordering CBP to begin refunds. Donaldson has conservatively not booked a refund asset. If realized against its ~$35M/yr tariff cost run-rate, this is a genuine, quantifiable tailwind to margins and cash not in current numbers.

Netting these, Valuation scores 5.0: a full multiple against modest growth, a scattered peer group offering no clean mispricing signal, and one real but uncertain tariff-refund option.

## Competitive Landscape

Donaldson competes in fragmented, highly competitive filtration markets against Parker Hannifin, MANN+HUMMEL, Cummins Filtration/Atmus, Camfil, and Pall/Danaher in life sciences. Its durable edge is the OEM design-in dynamic: original filtration systems specified into equipment generate a long, high-margin aftermarket replacement stream. Aftermarket's status as the largest, most profitable, and most resilient unit is the clearest evidence the moat is intact.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on mix. Facet deliberately tilts the portfolio toward secularly-favored A&D and power generation and away from over-reliance on cyclical diesel. The offsets: bioprocessing M&A has stumbled (impairments on Univercells and Solaris signal misjudged capital allocation), and 5-year TSR of 161 lags the peer group's 184. The Industrial Solutions margin decline is execution-driven, not a share loss, but it is a competitive-position drag until proven transitory. The secular electrification risk to diesel-engine filtration is real but long-tailed, and Donaldson is diversifying against it. Competitive Position scores 6.3: a wide, durable moat with an improving end-market mix, tempered by TSR underperformance and self-inflicted execution issues. This is slightly above the prior 6.2 read; Facet's strategic direction is sound even if execution is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Facet integration and leverage (most material, newly disclosed).** The largest deal in company history, funded with variable-rate debt. Integration risk, undisclosed liabilities, and floating interest expense in an elevated-rate environment. The higher-for-longer rate path directly raises the cost of the financing model.

2. **Concentrated leadership turnover running the biggest-ever deal.** CEO Carpenter stepped down March 2, 2026 (to Executive Chairman); COO Richard Lewis became President & CEO. CFO Pogalz (Nov 2024) and CHRO (Oct 2024) are also new. Orderly internal succession is reassuring, but a largely 18-month-old C-suite is integrating Facet at the start of its tenure. A July 24, 2026 8-K flags further officer changes to monitor.

3. **Industrial Solutions margin problem.** Segment EBT margin fell ~400bps to 12.6% (9M) on Power Generation production-relocation inefficiencies and A&D project/supply-chain timing. The issue is execution, and whether it is transitory or structural is unresolved.

4. **Bioprocessing execution.** Three of four bioprocessing acquisitions have underdelivered (Univercells/Solaris impaired, Purilogics contingent liability being reduced). This is the clearest capital-allocation misjudgment and tempers confidence as the far-larger Facet integration begins.

5. **Cyclical end markets.** Construction, ag, mining, transportation, oil & gas. On-road/off-road new equipment is volatile (on-road down sharply in FY2025); the aftermarket cushions but does not eliminate this.

6. **Tariffs and geopolitics.** ~$35M/yr tariff cost (<1% of sales), largely offset by pricing. The Feb 28, 2026 Middle East conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption raise raw-material cost and demand risk. Offset: the potential IEEPA tariff refund is unbooked upside.

Risk Factors scores 5.5. The idiosyncratic risks not fully priced (Facet integration on doubled leverage, a fresh C-suite, and an unexplained Industrial margin decline) are specific and could move the stock, but none is a hard-dated binary and the balance sheet remains covenant-compliant.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-mildly constructive. Near-term ATM IV of 26.6% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical vol of 27.0% (spread -0.4%), at the 6th percentile of the current chain; the market is not pricing a near-term catalyst. Put/call skew is -3.6% (call-heavy) and the put/call volume ratio is 0.40, both mildly bullish. ATM IV collapsed from 45.5% to 26.6% as the stock recovered from $81.87 to $92.79 since June 1, reflecting resolved earnings uncertainty rather than complacency. The contango term structure (26.6% near vs 31.8% at 6 months) is normal. Short interest is low at 4.1% of float (5.1 days to cover). None of this challenges the fundamental HOLD; it confirms the market sees DCI as a stable quality name without an imminent binary.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($108-115)
The aftermarket annuity holds, Mobile Solutions margins stay near 20%, and Life Sciences continues its recovery. Facet integrates cleanly, delivers its FY2027 ~$10M benefit target and beyond, and re-rates the portfolio toward higher-margin A&D and power-gen. The Industrial Solutions relocation completes and margins revert toward the prior ~16-17%. The IEEPA tariff refund is realized as unmodeled cash/margin upside. FY2027 EPS toward $4.60-4.75 at ~24x supports $110-114. Continued dividend growth and resumed buybacks compound returns.

### Bear Case ($75-80)
Facet integration proves harder than guided, deleveraging stalls with floating rates elevated, and interest expense weighs on EPS. Industrial Solutions margin weakness proves structural rather than transitory. A cyclical downturn in construction/ag/mining hits new-equipment demand harder than the aftermarket cushion offsets. Bioprocessing requires further impairment. Organic growth slips below the 3% guidance floor. FY2026 EPS near $3.80 at a de-rated ~20x lands around $76-79, roughly the analyst low target and ~15% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it unchanged is appropriately high. The FY2026 recovery confirms the underlying franchise is healthy, but the combination of a full multiple, tightened organic guidance, doubled leverage, a fresh C-suite, and an unresolved Industrial margin issue keeps the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. Own it for the aftermarket annuity and dividend-aristocrat cash return; the appreciation case requires Facet execution not yet demonstrated.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Facet integration tracks to or ahead of the ~$10M FY2027 benefit target with no undisclosed liabilities, and net debt/EBITDA begins declining on schedule.
- Industrial Solutions segment EBT margin recovers toward 15%+, confirming the Power Generation relocation was transitory.
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $70s (near the $79 analyst low / bear case), which would restore a genuine margin of safety at ~19-20x forward on a quality compounder.
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive (+5% or more over 90 days), signaling consensus is catching up to a real inflection.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Facet requires an impairment or the deal materially misses its sales/margin contribution guidance.
- Organic growth falls below the 3% floor for two consecutive quarters.
- A dividend-coverage or covenant stress signal emerges as floating-rate interest expense compounds.
- Further bioprocessing write-downs, confirming a structural capital-allocation problem.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q4/FY2026 print (early-mid September 2026), which will provide the first full quarter of Facet contribution, updated FY2027 organic guidance, and a read on whether Industrial Solutions margins have stabilized.

## Conclusion

Donaldson is a genuinely high-quality filtration franchise (wide moat, recurring aftermarket cash flows, 25% ROE, and a dividend-aristocrat cash-return record) trading at a full ~24x forward multiple that already prices that quality. The FY2026 recovery validates that the FY2025 earnings dip was an optical bioprocessing impairment rather than operational decay, and the strategic direction (Facet's tilt toward A&D and power generation) is sound. But the equity story has meaningfully changed: a lightly-levered compounder has taken on ~$1.4B of mostly-floating debt to fund its largest-ever acquisition, run by a C-suite that is almost entirely new, while a self-inflicted Industrial Solutions margin problem lingers and the bioprocessing M&A track record cautions against overconfidence in integration. With flat-to-negative estimate revisions, tightened 3-5% organic guidance, and the stock trading near consensus, the setup is balanced. HOLD, own it for the annuity and the dividend, and wait for either demonstrated Facet execution or a pullback into the mid-$70s before adding.
