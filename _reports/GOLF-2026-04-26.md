## Executive Summary

Acushnet Holdings is the global leader in premium golf products through its Titleist and FootJoy brands, generating $2.56B in FY2025 revenue with dominant market share in golf balls, golf shoes, and golf gloves. The company's "pyramid of influence" strategy—building brand equity through professional tour dominance to drive aspirational demand—has produced consistent 3-5% annual revenue growth and a 211% cumulative total shareholder return over five years. Golf participation remains at multi-year highs, with U.S. rounds played up ~25% versus 2019.

The investment case is complicated by several offsetting factors. On the positive side, estimate revisions are trending upward (+4.7% for FY2026, +7.1% for the next quarter), the consumable product mix (~35% of revenue from balls and gloves) provides revenue durability, and prior buybacks at ~$67.50/share have been materially accretive. Against these, the stock trades at 24.2x forward earnings—a 73% premium to peer median of 13.9x—while operating cash flow has declined 48% over two years ($372M to $194M), capital returns ($268M) are running at more than 2x free cash flow ($120M), and 3 of the last 4 quarterly earnings reports missed consensus. The $70M tariff assumption embedded in FY2026 guidance could prove conservative if Vietnam/Thailand tariff pauses expire.

At $98.10, GOLF trades precisely at the consensus median target of $98.00. All 8 covering analysts rate the stock Hold. The options market is pricing elevated uncertainty (38.5% near-term IV vs. 26.4% HV), and short interest sits at 13.6% of float. The risk/reward from here is approximately balanced—insufficient evidence exists for either a conviction buy or conviction sell.

## Company Overview

Acushnet Holdings operates four reporting segments:

- **Titleist Golf Equipment** ($1,596M, 62% of FY2025 revenue): Golf balls ($821M) and golf clubs ($775M). Golf balls are consumable, generating recurring demand tied to rounds played.
- **FootJoy Golf Wear** ($570M, 22%): Footwear, gloves, and apparel. Revenue declined 0.8% in FY2025, with weakness in Japan (-2.2%) and Korea (-5.3%).
- **Golf Gear** ($245M, 10%): Bags, headwear, travel gear. The fastest-growing segment at +5.5%.
- **Other** (~$148M, 6%): Premium apparel (KJUS, Club Glove).

Geographically, the U.S. represents ~60% of revenue. Key international markets include Korea ($276M), EMEA ($357M), Rest of World ($272M), and Japan ($131M). Over 40% of revenue is denominated in foreign currencies while ~80% of international COGS is in USD, creating a structural margin headwind when the dollar strengthens.

Magnus Holdings (a Fila Holdings subsidiary) owns 50.6% of outstanding shares. Acushnet has been systematically repurchasing Magnus shares alongside open-market buybacks, reducing the share count from 72M in 2022 to 58.6M currently.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory
Revenue growth has been consistent but moderate: +4.9% (2023), +3.1% (2024), +4.1% (2025). FY2026 guidance of $2.625-2.675B implies 2.5-4.5% growth—a slight deceleration reflecting tariff uncertainty and international softness.

### Profitability Under Pressure
Gross margin compressed 60bps to 47.7% in FY2025, driven by tariff costs and higher manufacturing expenses, partially offset by pricing. Operating margin fell 70bps to 11.7% as SG&A rose 4.0% (fitting network expansion, advertising for product launches, R&D). Net income declined 12% to $189M despite 4% revenue growth, with additional drag from a $17M debt extinguishment loss and higher effective tax rate (21.9% vs. 19.2%).

Adjusted EBITDA of $410M grew 1.5%, with margin at 16.0%—adequate but not expanding.

### Cash Flow Deterioration
This is the most concerning trend:

| Year | Operating CF | CapEx | FCF | FCF Margin |
|------|-------------|-------|-----|------------|
| 2023 | $372M | $101M | $271M | 11.4% |
| 2024 | $245M | $75M | $170M | 6.9% |
| 2025 | $194M | $74M | $120M | 4.7% |

Operating cash flow has declined 48% over two years. Drivers include ERP implementation costs and working capital investments. FY2026 guided capex of ~$95M plus ~$25M in capitalized ERP costs suggests continued capital intensity.

### Balance Sheet
Total debt stands at $950M (D/E of 2.0x), consisting of $500M in 5.625% senior notes due 2033 and ~$450M in revolver/other. Net debt/EBITDA is ~2.2x. Cash is only $49M, with 95% held internationally. Current ratio of 2.38x is healthy. Altman Z-Score of 2.59 sits in the "grey zone."

The November 2025 refinancing extended maturities and reduced coupon by ~175bps on the refinanced portion, but added $150M of incremental debt.

### Capital Returns vs. Cash Generation
Acushnet returned $268M in FY2025 ($212M buybacks + $56M dividends) against only $120M in FCF. The $148M gap was funded by debt. The buyback program has been highly accretive—average repurchase price of ~$67.50 vs. current $98.10—but this pace is unsustainable absent a cash flow recovery. The Magnus exit dynamic partially explains the elevated buyback pace.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.05 (up 4.7% over 90 days from $3.87) implies roughly 30% growth from FY2025's $3.11. This recovery is predicated on: (1) absence of the $17M debt extinguishment charge, (2) full-year interest savings from the refinancing, (3) new product launches (GT hybrids, T-Series irons, accelerated driver launches), and (4) pricing actions to offset tariffs.

The estimate revision trajectory is the strongest positive signal in the data. Current-quarter estimates are up 0.5%, next-quarter up 7.1%, and full-year up 4.7% over 90 days. This rising trend is inconsistent with a deteriorating fundamental picture and suggests the near-term earnings trajectory is improving.

Structural growth drivers remain intact: golf participation at multi-year highs (U.S. on-course participants up for the 8th consecutive year), demographic broadening, and the consumable product mix providing rounds-driven recurring revenue. However, the 3-5% organic revenue growth ceiling is well-established, and the golf ball rollback proposal (USGA/R&A ODS rule change) introduces medium-term uncertainty for the company's most profitable product line.

GOLF's 73% premium to peer median P/E reflects the market's recognition of superior brand positioning, consumable revenue mix, and consistent profitability. Some premium is justified—Titleist's dominant golf ball franchise has genuinely differentiated characteristics compared to more cyclical peers like Brunswick (marine), Thor (RVs), or Polaris (powersports). The appropriate comparison is less to these leisure peers and more to premium consumer branded goods.

However, the magnitude of the premium is stretched given: (1) 3-5% organic revenue growth is not a premium-growth profile, (2) operating cash flow has declined for two consecutive years, (3) 3 of 4 recent quarters missed EPS estimates, and (4) the $70M tariff assumption in FY2026 guidance could prove conservative. The consensus median target of $98.00—precisely the current price—reflects the view that GOLF is fairly valued here.

On a DCF basis, assuming $4.05 FY2026 EPS growing at 5% with a 25x terminal multiple and 10% discount rate, fair value is approximately $95-105—consistent with the consensus range.

## Competitive Landscape

Acushnet's competitive position is its strongest dimension. The "pyramid of influence" model—where Titleist dominates professional tour usage (Pro V1 is the #1 ball on every major tour) to drive aspirational demand among dedicated amateurs—creates a self-reinforcing brand advantage that competitors have failed to replicate over decades.

Key competitive strengths:
- **Golf balls**: Dominant global share. Consumable product with recurring demand tied to rounds played. R&D intensity creates meaningful technical moats (dimple patterns, multi-layer construction, urethane formulations).
- **Golf shoes/gloves**: FootJoy holds #1 position globally in both categories.
- **Fitting network expansion**: Acushnet has invested in custom fitting infrastructure, deepening customer relationships and creating switching costs.

The competitive position is stable to slightly improving, with the caveat that the 10-K explicitly acknowledges "limited opportunities for incremental market share gains" in core categories. Growth is participation-driven (more rounds = more ball consumption) rather than share-driven.

The golf ball rollback (ODS rule change) could paradoxically benefit Acushnet if implemented, given its dominant R&D capabilities in ball design. However, the transition period would create uncertainty and could invite smaller competitors to gain share during a product reset.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Tariff Escalation ($70M+ embedded cost).** FY2026 guidance assumes ~$70M in tariff costs, offset through pricing and supply chain adjustments. Golf balls manufactured in Thailand and FootJoy footwear in Vietnam are both exposed to elevated tariffs. If paused higher tariffs on Vietnam/Thailand are implemented, the impact could materially exceed $70M. Management described keeping the tariff assumption even after a Supreme Court ruling because "timing and implementation remain uncertain."

**2. Golf Ball Rollback (ODS Rule Change).** Augusta National's chairman stated in April 2026 that "failure is not an option" on the distance rollback. This is the highest-profile endorsement yet and increases implementation probability. While timelines remain unclear and any change would affect all manufacturers, the redesign costs and transition period create genuine uncertainty for Acushnet's profit engine.

**3. Cash Flow vs. Capital Returns Mismatch.** The $148M gap between capital returns and FCF in FY2025 is unsustainable without cash flow recovery. Continued deficit would require additional borrowing at a time when D/E is already 2.0x.

**4. Magnus Overhang.** At 50.6%, Magnus is right at the controlled company threshold. A disorderly block sale could pressure shares. The ongoing systematic buyback arrangement mitigates this but does not eliminate it. Acushnet has chosen not to rely on any controlled company exemptions from NYSE independent director requirements—a positive governance signal.

**5. Consumer Discretionary Sensitivity.** Golf equipment is discretionary spending. The consumable ball franchise provides partial insulation, but club purchases (30% of revenue) are highly discretionary.

**6. ERP Implementation Risk.** The multi-year global ERP migration has consumed $50.8M in capitalized costs (2024-2025) with another ~$25M guided for 2026. Implementation risks include business disruption and cost overruns.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.5% is 12.0 percentage points above historical volatility of 26.4%, with the term structure in backwardation (38.5% near-term vs. 32.8% at 6-month). This pricing pattern points to a specific near-term event—likely Q1 2026 earnings on May 6, 2026. The call-heavy skew (-9.0%) suggests options participants are positioned for upside around the event. However, the rich IV means option buyers are paying a significant premium relative to realized volatility, which should be factored into any options-based positioning.

Short interest at 13.6% of float with 10.7 days to cover is elevated and indicates meaningful institutional bearish positioning. This creates squeeze risk on any positive catalyst but also reflects informed concerns about the valuation/growth profile.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110-115)
- Tariff costs come in below the $70M assumption due to supply chain adjustments or policy relief, supporting margin recovery.
- Rising estimate revisions accelerate as Q1 2026 earnings demonstrate pricing power and volume resilience.
- Golf participation continues expanding, driving ball consumption growth above 5%.
- Magnus overhang reduces as systematic buyback continues, removing the stock's discount and supporting P/E expansion toward 27-28x FY2026E EPS.
- FY2026 EPS approaches $4.20+, and at 27x forward that implies $113-115.

### Bear Case ($78-85)
- Tariff costs escalate beyond the $70M assumption, compressing gross margin below 46%.
- Consumer spending weakness reduces golf equipment demand, particularly club purchases, leading to inventory buildup and promotional activity.
- Cash flow continues declining, forcing a reduction in buybacks or dividend growth.
- Golf ball rollback proposal advances toward implementation, creating product cycle uncertainty.
- At 19-20x a reduced FY2026E EPS of $3.80, the stock trades to $72-76.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of EPS beats with operating cash flow exceeding $250M annualized.
2. Tariff clarity—either policy relief reducing the ~$70M assumption or demonstrated pricing pass-through with no volume degradation, confirmed in Q1 or Q2 2026 results.
3. Stock pullback to $85-90 range (21-22x FY2026E EPS), compressing the peer premium to a more reasonable level.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS estimate revisions turn negative (below $3.85) as tariff costs exceed guidance or volumes weaken.
2. Operating cash flow declines below $150M annualized, raising sustainability concerns for the capital return program.
3. Golf ball rollback receives a formal implementation timeline from USGA/R&A with mandatory compliance within 24 months.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 2026 earnings (May 6, 2026) is the immediate checkpoint—tariff cost trajectory, pricing realization, and cash flow trends will all be visible. FY2026 full-year results in February 2027 provide the next major data point on margin recovery.

## Conclusion

Acushnet is a high-quality franchise with dominant brand positioning in its core categories and a recurring-revenue consumable product mix that distinguishes it from cyclical leisure peers. These attributes justify a premium to the peer group. At 24x forward earnings—a 73% premium to the 13.9x peer median—with declining operating cash flow, recent quarterly misses, and tariff costs that could prove conservative, the upside from current levels is constrained. Conversely, rising estimate revisions, structural golf participation tailwinds, and the accretive buyback program argue against positioning for significant downside. The consensus view (8 Holds, $98 median target) accurately captures this balanced risk/reward.

The rating is **HOLD**. The earnings trajectory is improving, but the valuation leaves limited margin of safety if tariff or consumer spending headwinds intensify. Monitor Q1 2026 earnings on May 6 for tariff cost realization and cash flow trends as the primary catalyst for a directional view change.
