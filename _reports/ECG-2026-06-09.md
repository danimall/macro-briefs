**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Everus Construction Group is a specialty construction contractor spun off from MDU Resources on October 31, 2024, with two segments: Electrical & Mechanical (E&M, ~78% of revenue) and Transmission & Distribution (T&D, ~22%). The company has emerged as a direct beneficiary of AI/data-center construction capex, posting FY2025 revenue of $3.75B (+31.5% YoY) and net income of $201.8M (+40.7%), with Q1 2026 accelerating further (revenue +25.4%, net income +58.9%, EPS $1.14). It has beaten consensus EPS in all four quarters as a public company, by margins of 39% to 78%. The balance sheet is a genuine strength: roughly zero net debt after the April 2026 SCNM acquisition, ~0.5x pro forma leverage, and 32% ROE.

The fundamental story is real, but the investment case is balanced rather than asymmetric. The growth is increasingly concentrated: the top 10 customers rose to 43% of revenue in 2025 from 33% in 2024, and a single data-center customer climbed from under 10% to ~17% in 2025 and ~19% in Q1 2026 (~24% of the E&M segment). ECG added a dedicated risk factor for this dependence. A material portion of reported earnings comes from favorable changes in percentage-of-completion estimates: +$1.64 of FY2025 diluted EPS and +$0.81 of the $1.14 Q1 2026 EPS came from prior-period project closeouts, which makes headline EPS more volatile and estimate-dependent than the clean growth rate suggests. Management stated that Q1's $131.9M free cash flow reflected favorable working-capital timing and will normalize lower.

At $141.47, ECG trades at ~35x trailing and ~30.6x current-year consensus EPS of $4.62, a premium to the construction & engineering peer median P/E of 26.9 that is broadly justified by faster growth and a cleaner balance sheet, but which leaves little margin for error. The stock has appreciated ~150% from its 52-week low and now sits ~18% below its May 5 peak of $171.58 following the Q1 beat. We rate ECG a HOLD with a growth bias. The earnings momentum and estimate revisions (+11.5% current-year over 90 days) are strong, but the combination of single-customer/single-theme concentration, estimate-driven earnings quality, and a full multiple prevents a higher-conviction call. The bar to upgrade is concrete: evidence that customer concentration is broadening alongside continued beats.

## Company Overview

Everus operates a decentralized model of roughly 16 operating companies under ~19 local and regional brands, employing approximately 9,400 people (~85% unionized). It ranks #12 on the ENR Top 600 Specialty Contractors list and is headquartered in Bismarck, North Dakota.

The **E&M segment** (+43.8% in FY2025) provides electrical, mechanical, fire-protection, low-voltage and communications services across commercial, industrial, institutional and high-tech end markets. Data-center construction is the dominant marginal growth driver. The **T&D segment** (+1.4% in FY2025) builds electric transmission lines, substations, distribution and undergrounding work for utilities, with ~55-60% of revenue from recurring master service agreements. On the Q1 call, management noted that data-center power demand is pulling through into T&D (transmission and undergrounding to feed large loads), giving ECG two ways to monetize the same AI-infrastructure theme.

Leadership consists of CEO Jeffrey Thiede, CFO Maximillian Marcy and COO Thomas Nosbusch. The stated competitive differentiation is disciplined project selection, execution consistency, early involvement in projects before design finalization, and deep general-contractor relationships.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025:** Diluted EPS ~$3.95. Adjusted EBITDA $319.8M (8.5% margin). Operating cash flow $156.8M (down slightly from $163.4M), with FCF ~$100M versus $128.8M the prior year, reflecting a working-capital drag from rapid growth. Cash stood at $170.5M against a term loan of ~$285M; net leverage was well under the 3.0x covenant. The Altman Z-Score of 3.92 places the company in the safe zone; ROA was ~11.7%.

**Q1 2026:** EBITDA $88.9M (+44%) at an 8.6% margin, up 110 bps. E&M EBITDA margin 9.0%; T&D 13.3%. Operating cash flow surged to $143.7M from $7.1M, with FCF of $131.9M. Cash rose to $275M against virtually zero net debt. Backlog rebounded to a record $3.68B (+20% YoY), recovering from a year-end 2025 dip and growing across both segments.

Two earnings-quality items warrant explicit treatment. First, favorable changes in cost-to-cost percentage-of-completion estimates added +$113.7M to gross profit, +$83.7M to net income, and +$1.64 to diluted EPS in FY2025, and +$55.9M revenue / +$41.5M net income / +$0.81 EPS in Q1 2026. Management frames this as conservative bidding plus disciplined closeouts, and the stability of margins supports that interpretation, but the mechanism makes reported EPS more volatile than the headline growth rate and capable of reversing in a quarter where estimates move against the company. Second, full-year cash conversion will normalize lower as growth capex (~$90-100M guided for 2026) and working capital build. The Q1 cash figure is not a run-rate.

**Raised FY2026 guidance:** Revenue $4.3-4.4B; EBITDA $345-360M (~8.1% margin at the midpoint), incorporating the SCNM acquisition. The guidance is conservative relative to the data-center tailwind; management raised primarily to reflect Q1 actuals plus SCNM, holding legacy-business margins reverting toward ~8% for the balance of the year. A Wolfe analyst pushed back on the conservatism; management cited it being "early in the year."

Net assessment: financially healthy with a strong balance sheet, strong returns on capital, and a clean covenant position. The qualifiers are earnings-quality dependence on estimate revisions and non-extrapolatable Q1 cash generation, which is why financial health scores 7.5 rather than higher.

## Growth Analysis

The growth is genuine, broad within the relevant end market, and confirmed by an estimate-revision pattern that is among the stronger forward indicators available. Current-year consensus EPS has risen to $4.62 from $4.14 ninety days ago (+11.5%) and next-year to $5.11 from $4.62 (+10.9%). Revenue growth of 25-31% over the trailing periods sits alongside a four-quarter beat streak averaging well above 40% surprises. The combination of double-digit estimate revisions and accelerating revenue is a constructive signal.

The durability case rests on the AI/data-center construction theme, which has multiple independent confirmations from hyperscaler capex guidance and is the dominant capital-markets theme of this cycle. ECG's E&M segment is a direct beneficiary, and the pull-through into T&D (power infrastructure for large loads) provides a second monetization path. Record backlog supports near-term visibility. The SCNM acquisition (closed April 1, 2026; ~$158M cash plus earnout; ~$109M of 2025 revenue at high-teens EBITDA margin) adds the Southeast region, pharma/healthcare end-market diversification, and a stable service/retrofit base (>60% of SCNM revenue is service work). It is accretive and represents the consolidation template for a fragmented industry.

One caution applies to the growth score: the revision velocity, while positive, is +11.5% over 90 days, below the +30% threshold that signals a genuine upturn the market is systematically underestimating. ECG's growth is strong but is being recognized by consensus in real time rather than lagging it. Combined with the fact that the marginal growth is increasingly tied to a single end market and a single customer, the durability is good but not bulletproof. Growth Prospects scores 7.5, consistent with the modest step-down in the trajectory data (8.0 → 7.0), which reflected the recognition that the growth is concentrating.

## Valuation Assessment

ECG trades at ~30.6x current-year consensus, falling to ~27.7x next-year $5.11. Against the construction & engineering peer median P/E of 26.9, ECG carries a premium that is broadly justified by faster top-line growth and a materially cleaner balance sheet (near-zero net debt versus levered peers). On a growth-adjusted basis, ECG is not egregiously priced: ~30.6x current-year against ~25%+ revenue growth and double-digit estimate revisions yields a PEG comfortably under 1.5.

Peer dispersion is wide and instructive. TTEK trades at 16.6x, FLR at 21.6x, PRIM at 26.9x, GVA at 38.2x, and MYRG at 49.1x. ECG sits in the upper-middle of this range, which is appropriate for its growth and balance-sheet profile but reflects that the data-center premium is recognized, not latent. Analyst price targets (low $157, mean $169.6, median $172, high $185 from Oppenheimer) imply ~11-30% upside from current levels and serve as a useful reference, though the sentiment trend has degraded (one buy moved to hold over the past month, leaving a 3 buy / 3 hold split).

The valuation score is 5.0 rather than higher because this is not a mispriced stock where consensus targets are stale floors. The forward multiple already capitalizes the growth, the estimate-driven component of EPS introduces quality risk into the denominator, and the single-customer concentration introduces a discrete downside path the multiple does not appear to discount. A clean reason the market is wrong on the upside is hard to articulate at 30.6x forward when the growth is being recognized concurrently. The multiple is not stretched to the point of being a clear short either. The valuation is fair, which caps but does not destroy the case. The trajectory data shows valuation improving from 3.5 to 5.5 as the stock pulled back from its peak; we hold it at 5.0, reflecting that the ~18% decline from the May high has improved entry but not created a discount to intrinsic value.

## Competitive Landscape

ECG's competitive position is stable-to-improving but not dominant. The Q1 2026 beat-and-raise contrasted with peer Primoris, which missed and sold off the same week, suggesting relatively strong execution discipline. Management describes competitive intensity as roughly unchanged over the past couple of years. The moat is rooted in long-standing local brand relationships, a skilled (largely union) labor force, early project involvement, and deep GC relationships that produce repeat work.

The improving element is the company's positioning at the center of the data-center construction wave with two monetization paths (E&M and T&D pull-through), plus a balance sheet that enables consolidation of a fragmented specialty-contractor market. The constraint on the trajectory is that the marginal share gains are concentrated in the data-center submarket and increasingly in a single customer relationship, which is a less durable form of competitive advantage than diversified share gains across end markets. Skilled-labor availability is a perennial constraint that could cap the ability to scale into record demand. The trajectory data's step-down in competitive position (7.5 → 6.5) reflects this concentration dynamic; we hold the score at 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer/theme concentration (dominant idiosyncratic risk).** The single data-center customer represents ~24% of E&M and ~20% of trade receivables. A pullback in AI/data-center capex or the loss of one hyperscaler relationship would disproportionately hit results. This is the single most important risk and the primary reason the risk score is 4.5. It is not fully priced into a 30.6x forward multiple.

**Earnings-quality dependence.** The favorable changes in percentage-of-completion estimates detailed above can reverse, making reported EPS more volatile than headline growth implies.

**Cash-flow normalization.** Full-year cash conversion will be materially lower than Q1's working-capital-aided figure.

**Other specific items.** A $31.3M payment has been withheld since October 2023 on a time-and-materials project, with ECG continuing to recognize the revenue as collection-probable. A tax matters agreement restricts certain M&A/equity issuance until ~October 2026 to preserve tax-free spin status. Roughly 50% of work is fixed-price, carrying cost-overrun exposure. An executive-departure 8-K (Item 5.02) was filed in July 2025 and an auditor change (Item 4.01, January 2026, with a March amendment); neither surfaced any disagreement, restatement, or going-concern issue, and both appear consistent with routine post-spin transitions, but the auditor change merits monitoring as a newly independent company settles its controls environment.

The skew is call-heavy (-11.0% put/call skew at the 1-week tenor), and the term structure is flat (near 63.0% ≈ far 62.8%), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly rather than concentrated around a near-term binary. The rich IV suggests elevated general uncertainty (appropriate given a thin standalone trading history and concentration risk) rather than directional pessimism. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** ECG is a pure-play on AI/data-center construction capex with two monetization paths (E&M and T&D). Revenue is growing 25-31%, margins are expanding (Q1 EBITDA margin +110 bps), the balance sheet is near-net-cash at ~0.5x pro forma leverage, and the four-quarter beat streak plus +11.5% current-year estimate revisions suggest management is guiding conservatively. The SCNM deal is the first of a consolidation playbook that activates fully when the post-spin M&A restriction lapses around October 2026. If data-center demand holds and concentration broadens as new geographies and end markets (pharma/healthcare via SCNM) scale, the company beats guidance again and re-rates toward the Oppenheimer $180 target. Bull case ~$185.

**Bear case.** The growth is concentrated in one end market and increasingly one customer (~19% of revenue). A moderation in AI capex or the loss of one hyperscaler relationship would disproportionately hit E&M. Layered on top is earnings-quality risk: a large share of reported EPS comes from estimate revisions that can reverse, and Q1's strong cash flow was flagged as non-recurring. At 30.6x forward with the growth already recognized, a single disappointing quarter de-rates the stock sharply. Bear case ~$105-115 (a return toward the ~25x forward level on a quarter that misses or shows concentration-driven volatility).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias).** The reason this is not a BUY despite a solid overall profile is specific and falsifiable: customer concentration is rising, not falling (one customer from <10% to ~19% in roughly a year), a material portion of EPS is estimate-revision-driven and reversible, and the stock trades at ~30.6x forward where the growth is already priced. The asymmetry that a BUY requires (downside contained, catalyst within 60 days, growth under-recognized by consensus) is absent: bear-case downside is ~20-25%, exceeding the threshold for a high-conviction long, and consensus is revising estimates concurrently rather than lagging them.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Evidence over the next one to two quarters that customer concentration is broadening — the single-customer share declining below ~15% while revenue growth holds above 20%, demonstrating diversification rather than deepening dependence.
2. A pullback toward ~$120-125 (~25x forward) on no fundamental deterioration, which would restore asymmetric upside to the $169-185 analyst range.
3. A second beat-and-raise confirming the conservative-guidance read, with EPS quality shifting toward operations rather than estimate revisions (changes-in-estimates contribution falling below ~30% of incremental EPS).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Loss or material reduction of the largest data-center customer, or explicit hyperscaler capex guidance cuts that would compress E&M backlog.
2. A reversal of percentage-of-completion estimates producing a negative EPS adjustment, or a quarterly miss after the four-quarter beat streak.
3. Backlog declining for two consecutive quarters, or gross margin compression signaling cost-overrun problems on fixed-price work.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August) is the natural checkpoint, with particular attention to the customer-concentration disclosure and the changes-in-estimates contribution to EPS.

## Conclusion

Everus is a high-quality specialty contractor with a strong balance sheet, strong returns on capital, a four-quarter beat streak, and direct exposure to the most powerful capex theme in the market. The fundamentals are improving and the growth is real. The constraint is that the investment case is balanced rather than asymmetric: the growth is concentrating into a single end market and a single customer, a material slice of reported earnings depends on reversible estimate revisions, Q1 cash flow was non-recurring, and the ~30.6x forward multiple already capitalizes the visible growth. The ~18% pullback from the May peak improves entry but does not create a discount to intrinsic value. We rate ECG a HOLD with a growth bias, with a clear path to upgrade on evidence of diversification, a multiple reset toward 25x, or a second beat-and-raise with improving earnings quality. The stock is worth owning for the growth thesis, but the risk/reward does not yet support adding aggressively at current levels.
