**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Chart Industries is now valued on deal terms rather than fundamentals. On July 28, 2025, Chart agreed to be acquired by Baker Hughes (BKR) for **$210.00 per share in all cash**, abandoning an earlier all-stock merger with Flowserve and paying a $266M termination fee in the process (of which Baker Hughes funded $258M). Stockholders approved the deal on October 6, 2025; the U.S. HSR waiting period expired November 6, 2025. As of mid-June 2026 the transaction awaits EU antitrust clearance, with a Phase 1 decision deadline of June 26, 2026 and Baker Hughes already having offered remedies to address bundling concerns. At the current price of $207.99, GTLS trades at roughly a 1% gross spread to the $210 deal price. The entire investment question reduces to the probability and timing of EU approval.

Underneath the deal, the standalone business is deteriorating. Q1 2026 revenue fell 11.7% year-over-year across all four segments, gross margin compressed 550 basis points to 28.4% on lower volume and tariff-driven materials inflation, operating income dropped 65.5%, and the company posted a $17.1M net loss. Consensus EPS estimates have collapsed — current-year estimates are down 45.2% over 90 days, and the four-quarter earnings track record is four straight misses, including a 117% miss in Q1 2026. This deterioration defines the break case: if the deal fails, GTLS reverts to a leveraged (D/E ~2.0x), slowing industrial with high-cost debt, and the stock likely revisits the $130–160 range.

The payoff structure is a late-stage merger arb: capped upside of ~1% to $210, against larger tail downside on a regulatory break, partially cushioned by a $500M reverse-termination fee Baker Hughes owes on an antitrust failure (~$10/share of recovery value) and a record $6.3B backlog. For holders already positioned in the arbitrage, this is a hold-to-close. For new capital, the asymmetry does not compensate for the binary regulatory risk concentrated in a single late-June decision. We rate GTLS **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale: the outcome is binary and resolves on a regulator's calendar, not on anything the company controls.

## Company Overview

Chart Industries manufactures process technologies and equipment for gas and liquid molecule handling, branded *Nexus of Clean™*, spanning LNG, hydrogen/helium, biogas, CO2 capture, and water treatment. It operates 62–63 manufacturing sites and 50+ service centers globally, serving more than 10,000 customers (top-10 = 27% of sales).

The company reports four segments (FY2025 sales):
- **Repair, Service & Leasing** — $1,303.7M (30.6% of sales), the highest-margin segment at 44.3% gross margin, with recurring aftermarket revenue.
- **Heat Transfer Systems** — $1,237.7M (29.0%), the FY2025 standout (sales +19.5%, operating margin 29.4%), levered to LNG, data-center cooling, and traditional energy.
- **Specialty Products** — $1,098.4M (25.8%), spanning hydrogen, carbon capture, space, nuclear, marine, and water.
- **Cryo Tank Solutions** — $624.2M (14.6%), industrial-gas storage and distribution.

The current footprint is the product of the $4.4B March 2023 Howden acquisition, which more than doubled revenue from $1.6B (2022) to $3.4B (2023) and loaded roughly $3.7B of goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles onto the balance sheet along with high-coupon debt. Chart's competitive position rests on engineering know-how, proprietary processes (such as the IPSMR® liquefaction technology), and breadth/global footprint rather than a patent wall.

## Financial Analysis

The reported FY2025 financials look weak but are distorted by deal-related charges. Revenue was $4,264.0M (+2.5% YoY), driven by Heat Transfer Systems backlog conversion while the other three segments declined. Operating income fell to $358.4M (8.4% margin) from $647.5M (15.6%), but $266M of that decline is the one-time Flowserve termination fee; adjusting it out, operating income was roughly $624M at a ~14.6% margin. FY2025 net income of $40.7M (vs. $218.5M in 2024) reflects the same termination fee plus ~$22.8M of deal costs. Full-year FCF was positive at $203M.

Q1 2026 shows deterioration that is not deal-related and is therefore the more important read for the break scenario:
- Sales of $884.8M, down 11.7% YoY, with declines in all four segments.
- Gross margin of 28.4% (−550bps) on lower volume, unfavorable mix, and explicit tariff impacts on aluminum, stainless, and carbon steel.
- Operating income of $52.6M, down 65.5% YoY; net loss of $(17.1)M.
- Operating cash flow of **negative $248M** (vs. −$60M in Q1'25) on working-capital timing.

The offsetting positive is backlog, which grew to $6,283M at Q1 2026 from $5,886M at YE2025 (+21% YoY), driven by Specialty Products (carbon capture, nuclear, space, marine). Roughly 42% is expected to convert within 12 months, providing revenue visibility that partly offsets the near-term softness.

The balance sheet is the real fundamental risk if the deal breaks. Total debt is ~$3.79B against $3.16B of equity (D/E ~1.99x). The debt is high-cost: 7.500% secured notes due 2030, 9.500% unsecured notes due 2031, term loans, and a revolver, generating ~$308M of FY2025 interest expense. The Altman Z-Score of 1.41 nominally signals distress, but that reading is a false positive for an intangible-heavy engineering company — $3.7B of acquisition goodwill mechanically penalizes the score. Chart was in compliance with all covenants as of Q1 2026, with ~$524M of unused revolver capacity. The HTEC put option is a further consideration: BDT&MSD can require Chart to buy HTEC shares for ~$323M ($51.20/share, accreting at 11.25%), and a change of control — the Baker Hughes merger — is an explicit put trigger.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis, the growth story has inverted over the past two quarters. Revenue turned negative in Q1 2026 after modest FY2025 growth, and estimate revisions confirm the deterioration: current-quarter EPS estimates are down 23.4% over 30 days and 35.8% over 90 days; current-year estimates have fallen 45.2% over 90 days to $7.05 from $12.88; next-year estimates are down 29.1% to $10.18. Estimate revisions of this magnitude in the wrong direction, combined with negative revenue growth, are a clear negative signal.

The single durable bright spot is backlog, with Specialty Products strength in carbon capture, nuclear, space, and marine, plus data-center cooling demand within Heat Transfer Systems. That backlog gives the eventual owner (most likely Baker Hughes) multi-year revenue visibility. But for an equity holder today, the growth question is moot: the stock converges to $210 on close regardless of the backlog trajectory, and on a break the negative near-term momentum is what defines downside.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard valuation multiples are not the operative framework here. Trailing P/E is negative on near-zero trailing EPS; forward multiples are unstable because estimates are collapsing. The peer set (Donaldson 23.0x, Flowserve 28.7x, Timken 31.2x, Valmont 29.4x, SPX 44.7x; median 29.4x) is informative only for the break case — it tells you what a slowing, leveraged Chart might fetch as a standalone, which is well below $210.

The relevant valuation is the merger-arb math. At $207.99 against a $210 cash deal, the gross spread is ~1% ($2.01/share). Annualized over the expected resolution window (EU decision expected around late June 2026, with an Outside Date of July 28, 2026 that is auto-extendable by two six-month periods if only regulatory approvals remain outstanding), the spread implies a high market-assigned probability of close — but not complacency, since the spread plus the risk of a Phase 2 delay reflects residual EU risk.

Expected value is best framed as: P(EU clearance) × $210 + P(break) × (standalone ~$130–160 + ~$10 reverse-fee benefit). With the market pricing close at high probability, the position offers ~1% defined upside against a tail loss of roughly 23–37% on a break. We assign a valuation score of 5.0 — neither cheap nor expensive in any conventional sense, because the price is anchored to the deal terms rather than to fundamentals. The score reflects a balanced, fully-priced arb spread, not an asymmetric opportunity. We are deliberately skeptical of treating the depressed standalone metrics as cheap; a compressed multiple here would reflect genuine impairment (leverage plus falling revenue), the textbook value trap.

## Competitive Landscape

Chart's competitive moat is breadth and engineering depth across LNG, industrial gas, hydrogen, carbon capture, and increasingly data-center cooling, rather than narrow patent protection. Competitors are largely regional or product-specific, and Chart's full-stack offering is its differentiator. The strategic logic of the Baker Hughes acquisition (~$13.6B enterprise value) is to combine Chart's process/heat-transfer/cryogenic equipment with Baker Hughes's energy-technology portfolio, and the EU's bundling concern is itself evidence that the two portfolios overlap and complement in LNG and gas processing.

The trajectory is mixed. Heat Transfer Systems is gaining in data-center cooling and LNG, and the backlog signals customers continue to commit. But Q1 2026 saw declines across all four segments and tariff-driven margin compression, indicating near-term competitive and cyclical pressure on pricing and volume. We score competitive position 5.5 — a structurally well-positioned franchise facing cyclical and cost headwinds, with trajectory neutral-to-slightly-negative in the most recent data.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger-specific risks dominate:**
1. **EU antitrust clearance is the live binary.** Baker Hughes offered remedies during the EU Phase 1 review, with concerns centered on product bundling. A move to Phase 2 would delay closing and raise break risk. The stock now trades tick-for-tick on regulatory headlines (the June 11, 2026 dip on EU-bundling news demonstrates this).
2. **Asymmetric break downside.** On termination, GTLS reverts to a leveraged industrial with falling sales and high-cost debt, likely revisiting $130–160. The reverse-termination fee (payable on antitrust/foreign-investment failure) provides a partial floor and signals Baker Hughes negotiated meaningful but bounded regulatory commitment.
3. **Interim operating restrictions** limit Chart's ability to refinance, repurchase, acquire, or make major capex without Baker Hughes consent, constraining any response to the Q1 slowdown.

**Standalone risks (relevant only on a break):** high leverage at 7.5%–9.5% coupons into a slowing top line; $3.7B of goodwill/intangibles (38% of assets) carrying impairment risk; tariffs now materially hitting margins; cyclicality and customer concentration in energy/industrial-gas capex; 57.8% international sales with FX and geopolitical exposure; and the ~$323M HTEC put triggered by the change of control.

The risk score of 5.5 reflects that the dominant risk is binary and well-understood by the market — it is largely priced — with a partial cushion on the downside from the reverse-termination fee and backlog. This is not a risk-free arb (EU Phase 2 is a real possibility), but neither is it an unpriced governance time bomb.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress around the regulatory decision. Near-term ATM IV jumped from ~4.4% to ~19.3% since the prior snapshot, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (1-month IV 15.3% vs. 6-month 2.7%), consistent with the market expecting volatility to resolve quickly around the late-June binary event. Implied moves are ~10% on the nearest expiries despite the ~1% spread, signaling that traders price a meaningful gap risk on an adverse EU outcome. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.15 to 0.77, showing increased downside hedging into the decision. Short interest is moderate at 9.6% of float (3.8 days to cover). None of this changes the fundamental conclusion; it confirms the binary, near-term nature of the catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($210, ~+1%):** The EU grants Phase 1 clearance on the offered remedies by late June 2026 (or after a contained Phase 2), the deal closes, and holders receive $210.00 cash. This is the high-probability base outcome that the spread implies.

**Bear Case ($130–160, ~−23% to −37%):** EU concerns escalate to a prolonged Phase 2 or the deal collapses. GTLS reverts to standalone valuation on deteriorating fundamentals and high leverage. The reverse-termination fee cushions but does not offset the decline. A peer-multiple framework on depressed forward earnings supports a sub-$160 standalone value.

**Base Case ($210 on close):** We weight EU clearance as the most likely outcome and treat $210 as the modal value, with the timing potentially slipping into late summer if a brief Phase 2 occurs.

The thesis is a probability-weighted bet on a regulator's decision rather than a fundamental equity view. The expected value is modestly positive only if you assign a high clearance probability and tolerate the tail.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The decisive event is the EU Phase 1 decision, with the merger Outside Date (auto-extendable). The position should resolve within weeks, not quarters.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- EU grants unconditional or remedy-conditioned Phase 1 clearance, removing the last material condition and widening the certainty of $210, at which point any residual spread becomes a low-risk arb worth capturing.
- A clear, near-dated closing date is announced post-clearance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- EU opens a full Phase 2 investigation, pushing resolution out 6+ months and raising break risk, at which point the carry and tail risk would no longer justify holding the spread.
- Any signal Baker Hughes seeks to renegotiate price or walk, or evidence the offered remedies are rejected.
- A definitive deal termination, after which the standalone story governs and the stock should be avoided absent a multiple reset toward the low-$130s.

For investors not already in the position, there is no compelling reason to initiate: ~1% upside does not compensate for the EU tail risk, and the standalone fundamentals are weak enough that a break is genuinely punishing.

## Conclusion

GTLS is a merger-arbitrage instrument trading at a ~1% spread to a $210.00 all-cash Baker Hughes acquisition, with the outcome gated almost entirely on EU antitrust clearance. The standalone business is deteriorating, which defines a punishing break scenario ($130–160) partly cushioned by the reverse-termination fee and the backlog.

The score trajectory (4.2 → 4.5 → 4.8) has drifted up as the deal has progressed toward closing without breaking, and the prior two AVOID calls reflected the unattractiveness of the standalone business. We move to **HOLD (uncertain)** rather than AVOID because the dominant fact today is a high-probability cash takeout at $210, not a fundamental short thesis: holders in the arb should hold to close, while the deteriorating standalone fundamentals and binary EU risk keep us from a BUY. The recommendation should flip mechanically on the EU decision: clearance argues for capturing the residual spread; a Phase 2 escalation or break argues for exiting into the standalone risk.
