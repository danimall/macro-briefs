## Executive Summary

Cardinal Health has executed an impressive operational transformation over the past three years, growing non-GAAP EPS from $5.07 to a guided $10.70-$10.80 in FY2026 (ending June 2026), a 17% CAGR. The Q3 FY2026 report (April 30, 2026) delivered $3.17 EPS against $2.82 consensus, extended a streak of four consecutive beats, and prompted a guidance raise. The strategic pivot toward specialty physician practice management ("The Specialty Alliance") and the at-Home Solutions segment represents a structural improvement in Cardinal's earnings quality and margin profile.

At $187.13 (current price), the stock has pulled back 20% from its February 2026 high of $233.60 following a mixed Q3 report where revenue missed by ~$800M and GMPD segment profit declined 35% due to tariff impacts. The selloff creates a more attractive entry point, but the valuation remains elevated relative to distributor peers (29x trailing vs. 21.9x peer median), and several specific risks — GMPD tariff exposure, CVS concentration (30% of revenue), regulatory risk to the MSO model (DOJ investigation + Oregon legislation), and $4.9B in remaining opioid liabilities — prevent the risk/reward from being asymmetrically positive at current levels.

The stock offers approximately 31% upside to the consensus median target of $245, with analyst estimates broadly rising (+7.5% for current year over 90 days). I rate this a growth HOLD with identifiable upgrade triggers: resolution of GMPD tariff headwind, clean DOJ investigation outcome, or pullback to $170-175 (16x FY2026 EPS) would each improve the asymmetry sufficiently for a BUY.

## Company Overview

Cardinal Health operates as one of the "Big Three" U.S. pharmaceutical distributors (alongside McKesson and Cencora), collectively handling approximately 90% of U.S. pharmaceutical distribution volume. The company generates $222.6B in annual revenue across three segments:

**Pharmaceutical and Specialty Solutions (92% of revenue, $204.6B):** Core drug distribution to hospitals, pharmacies, and physician offices, plus the newly built Specialty Alliance MSO platform managing 1,900+ physicians across gastroenterology, oncology, and urology.

**Global Medical Products and Distribution (6%, $12.6B):** Manufacturing and distribution of Cardinal-branded medical/surgical products. Currently the weakest segment due to tariff exposure on China-sourced products.

**Other (2%, $5.4B):** Nuclear/precision health (radiopharmaceuticals), at-Home Solutions (direct-to-patient supplies), and OptiFreight logistics. Generates 18% of segment profit at 9.6% margins — the highest-margin and fastest-growing component.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:** Non-GAAP EPS has compounded at approximately 17% over three years: $5.07 (FY2022) → $5.85 (FY2023) → $7.53 (FY2024) → $8.24 (FY2025) → $10.70-10.80 guided (FY2026). The FY2026 acceleration reflects specialty platform integration, the OptumRx contract loss being fully lapped, and margin mix improvement.

**Margin Dynamics:** Gross margin improved 40bps in FY2025 to 3.7%, driven by the departure of low-margin OptumRx volume and growing specialty/branded pharma contribution. The OptumRx loss (17% of FY2024 revenue) was materially positive for profitability despite the revenue decline.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $2.4B in FY2025, depressed by $798M in opioid payments and OptumRx working capital unwind. FY2026 guidance of $3.3-3.7B adjusted FCF represents a meaningful step-up. At the midpoint ($3.5B), FCF yield on $43.8B market cap is approximately 8%.

**Balance Sheet:** Negative stockholders' equity (-$2.8B) is structural for pharma distributors (trade payables of $34.7B effectively finance operations). Long-term debt increased to $8.0B following $2.9B in bond issuance and an $800M term loan to fund $5.3B in acquisitions. Altman Z-Score of 4.27 indicates no distress. Net leverage is within the company's 3.75x covenant limit.

**Capital Allocation:** $765M in buybacks (FY2025), $494M in dividends (1.1% yield), $5.3B in acquisitions. The prioritization of growth investment over returns is appropriate given the specialty platform opportunity, though it has temporarily elevated leverage.

## Growth Analysis

- Consensus estimates are rising: current-year EPS revised +7.5% over 90 days, next year +6.1%

**Secular tailwinds:**
- Specialty pharma distribution is structurally higher-margin than traditional distribution
- The at-Home healthcare shift benefits the Other segment (ADS acquisition)

**Headwinds to growth:**

- Of the 50-cent FY2026 EPS guidance raise, only 13 cents came from operational improvement; the remainder from taxes, buybacks, and interest — suggesting underlying momentum may be peaking

## Valuation Assessment

At $187.13, Cardinal trades at:
- 29x trailing EPS ($6.45)
- ~17.4x FY2026 guided EPS ($10.75 midpoint)
- ~15.6x FY2027 consensus EPS ($11.98)

**Peer comparison:** MCK trades at 21.4x, COR at 30.3x, HSIC at 21.9x. Peer median is 21.9x. Cardinal's 29x trailing multiple sits well above MCK/HSIC but below COR. On forward earnings, the 17.4x multiple is more reasonable and approaches peer territory.

**The bull case for paying a premium:** Cardinal's growth rate (30% EPS growth FY2026, guided 6-12% thereafter from specialty platform maturation) exceeds MCK's trajectory, the specialty MSO strategy is genuinely differentiated, and ~8% prospective FCF yield provides valuation support.

**The bear case:** The premium to MCK (which trades at 21.4x with a larger, more diversified business and lower customer concentration) is difficult to justify when Cardinal carries DOJ investigation risk, significant tariff exposure in GMPD, and 30% revenue concentration with CVS. The 29x trailing multiple prices in continued execution without margin for error.

**Fair value estimate:** 18-20x FY2026 EPS of $10.75 = $193-$215. Current price of $187 is slightly below the low end of this range on trailing but approximately fair on forward estimates. Upside exists but requires continued execution.

## Competitive Landscape

Cardinal's competitive position is stable-to-improving within the Big Three oligopoly:

**Structural advantages:** Red Oak Sourcing JV with CVS (through June 2029) provides procurement scale in generics. 90% market share collectively across Big Three creates nearly impenetrable barriers to entry. IT infrastructure and regulatory compliance requirements make new entry virtually impossible.

**Differentiation through specialty:** The MSO strategy is unique among the Big Three — neither McKesson nor Cencora directly own/manage physician practices at this scale. If execution succeeds, this creates a vertically integrated specialty drug distribution model that captures both the distribution margin and practice management economics. GI Alliance (345 locations), ION (oncology), and Urology America create a multi-specialty platform that is difficult to replicate.

**GMPD competitive risk:** In medical products, Cardinal competes against Medline Industries and Owens & Minor. Tariff exposure creates an uneven playing field if competitors have different sourcing footprints. The company's 10-K explicitly warns that competitive pricing pressure may prevent full tariff pass-through.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CVS Concentration (30% of revenue):** The single largest risk factor. Any contract renegotiation, pricing pressure, or relationship deterioration would be material. The Red Oak Sourcing JV (extending to 2029) provides structural linkage but does not guarantee distribution contract continuity.

**2. Tariff Impact on GMPD:** Management's admission that it "does not expect to be able to establish alternative sources of supply or otherwise mitigate the potential impact of tariffs on all products" is a direct earnings headwind with uncertain duration.

**3. DOJ Investigation of MSO Practices:** The November 2023 Civil Investigative Demand regarding Anti-Kickback Statute violations in a 2022 rheumatology MSO transaction directly threatens the $5.3B+ MSO investment thesis. Adverse findings could create both financial penalties and operational constraints.

**4. Regulatory Risk to MSO Model:** Oregon has adopted legislation limiting corporate ownership of physician practices. If other states follow, Cardinal's entire specialty platform strategy faces existential risk. The company has invested $5.3B in one year building this capability.

**5. Opioid Liability Cash Drain:** $4.9B accrued, with $798M paid in FY2025. Payments extend through 2038. While provisioned, this reduces capital available for growth investment and shareholder returns by $600-800M annually.

## Conclusion

Cardinal Health has genuinely improved its business quality through the specialty MSO buildout and margin-accretive segment mix shift. The 30% EPS growth in FY2026 and rising consensus estimates confirm positive operational momentum. However, the stock's 20% decline from its February high primarily reflects legitimate fundamental concerns: a 35% GMPD profit decline from tariffs, revenue misses across all segments in Q3, and the realization that only 13 cents of a 50-cent guidance raise came from operations.

At $187 and ~17.4x FY2026 forward EPS, the valuation is reasonable but not compellingly cheap. With CVS concentration risk, active DOJ investigation, tariff uncertainty, and opioid obligations, the stock requires continued flawless execution to justify its valuation — and Q3's GMPD miss demonstrated that execution risk is real.

I maintain a HOLD recommendation: the growth trajectory is positive and the business is genuinely improving, but specific identifiable risks prevent the risk/reward from being sufficiently asymmetric at current prices to warrant a BUY. A pullback to $170-175 or resolution of the DOJ/tariff headwinds would represent clear upgrade triggers.
