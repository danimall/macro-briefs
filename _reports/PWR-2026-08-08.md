## Executive Summary

Quanta's Q2 2026 print, released July 30, was the strongest operating quarter in the company's history and it changed the shape of the earnings model. Revenue grew 41.1% to $9,557.0M, consolidated operating margin expanded 180 basis points to 7.3%, and both reportable segments set margin records — Electric at 11.5% versus 10.1%, Underground and Infrastructure at 9.1% versus 6.9%. Roughly 67% of the revenue increase was organic. Consensus responded: FY2026 adjusted EPS moved from $14.01 to $16.71 (+19.2%) in thirty days and FY2027 from $16.46 to $19.19 (+16.6%), with 21 and 25 contributing analysts respectively. Total backlog reached $53.44B at June 30, up 21.5% in six months, and remaining performance obligations — the GAAP measure — rose 41.2% to $33.55B, with about 70% expected to convert inside twelve months.

The mechanism behind the margin move is worth stating precisely, because it was the central bear argument a year ago. Through FY2025, segment operating income grew strongly (Electric +20.5%, Underground +50.3%) while consolidated operating margin stayed flat at 5.7% and net margin actually fell from 3.8% to 3.6%, because corporate and non-allocated costs rose 30.8% to $1,147.0M — including $115.8M of incremental intangible amortization from the Cupertino Electric and Dynamic Systems purchases. In H1 2026 corporate costs grew 32.4% against 34.0% revenue growth, so the ratio fell from 4.0% to 3.9% and consolidated margin converged toward segment margin for the first time in the cycle. Revenue is now scaling past the fixed amortization step-up.

The stock is not cheap on any basis we can construct. At $671.86 Quanta trades at 40.2x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $16.71, 35.0x FY2027 consensus of $19.19, roughly 77x trailing twelve-month GAAP diluted EPS of $8.71, and 31.1x TTM adjusted EBITDA on an enterprise value of $107.5B. Trailing free cash flow of $2,391.2M is flattered by a $700.2M contract-liability build and an $884.0M payables build in H1; a working-capital-neutral estimate puts normalized FY2026 FCF near $1.9B, a 1.9% yield. The reported H1 tax rate of 20.9% versus 24.6% a year earlier reflects a $35.9M equity-vesting windfall that is a function of the share price and not repeatable — worth about $0.24 of H1 EPS. Against a probability-weighted expected return of roughly +8% over twelve months (base $745, bull $930, bear $470), this is a fourth consecutive HOLD, but issued on materially better fundamentals and a 20% lower forward multiple than the prior three. Our upgrade level is explicit: $590-620.

## Company Overview

Quanta provides engineering, procurement, construction and maintenance services for electric transmission and distribution networks, substations, power generation (gas, wind, solar, storage), gas utility systems, pipelines, communications networks, and — increasingly — low-voltage electrical, mechanical and process infrastructure for data centers and advanced manufacturing. Operations are principally US, Canada and Australia. Notably, the business is becoming more US-concentrated, not less: the US share of quarterly revenue rose to 95.2% in Q2 2026 from 92.8% a year earlier, with Canada at 2.3% and Australia at 2.2%.

In Q1 2025 management collapsed the former Electric Power and Renewable Energy segments into a single Electric Infrastructure Solutions segment. FY2025 segment detail: Electric $23,001.5M revenue (80.8% of total) at 10.3% operating margin; Underground and Infrastructure $5,478.2M (19.2%) at 7.3%; corporate and non-allocated costs of $(1,147.0)M reduced consolidated operating margin to 5.7% on $28,479.7M of revenue. The recast removes investors' ability to isolate renewables execution — the source of the 2024 solar-project margin damage — from utility T&D execution.

Two structural features matter for the competitive assessment. First, scale of self-perform craft labor and bonding: outstanding performance bonds grew from ~$14.9B at December 31, 2025 to ~$21.5B at June 30, 2026, a capacity level that few competitors can match, though Quanta's own disclosure notes sureties issue bonds project-by-project and may decline. Second, vertical integration into the equipment bottleneck. Quanta manufactures power transformers and mobile energy storage, acquired a 49% interest in a high-voltage dead-tank circuit-breaker manufacturing joint venture in H1 2026, and bought an Australian producer of transportable substation buildings and switchgear. Its own risk factors identify transformer availability as constrained "by the inability of manufacturers to meet current market demand." Controlling supply of the long-lead equipment that gates customer schedules is a different and more durable advantage than labor scale.

The LUMA Energy joint venture, which operates Puerto Rico's transmission and distribution system, generates revenue at cost — $26.0M in Q2 2026 and $53.3M in H1, down from $45.8M and $96.9M. Receivables from unconsolidated affiliates were $157.1M, primarily LUMA. The economics are immaterial; the political and reputational exposure is not, and Quanta names LUMA in its own reputational-risk factor.

Three-year revenue CAGR is 18.6%. Consolidated operating margin expanded 60 basis points over that span against 67% revenue growth — the amortization drag described above.

**H1 2026 inflection:** revenue $17,431.8M (+34.0%), gross margin 15.2% (+100bp), operating income $1,033.6M (+69.6%), net income to common $672.0M (+79.9%), GAAP diluted EPS $4.41 (+78.5%), adjusted EBITDA $1,753.0M (+49.5%). Approximately $930M of the $2,784M Q2 consolidated revenue increase came from acquired businesses, leaving about 67% organic.

**Basis discipline.** The reported earnings-surprise series ($4.24 actual versus $3.33 estimate for Q2; $2.68 versus $2.08 for Q1) is on an adjusted, non-GAAP basis. Q2 GAAP diluted EPS was $2.96 and H1 GAAP $4.41. The gap versus adjusted is principally acquisition intangible amortization ($498.8M annualized in FY2025, roughly $3.30 per share pretax), acquisition and integration costs, and contingent-consideration fair-value movements. Consensus of $16.71 for FY2026 and $19.19 for FY2027 is on that adjusted basis, and every forward multiple in this report is computed accordingly. The vendor profile field showing forward P/E identical to trailing P/E at 76.4x is a stale duplicate of the trailing figure and is not used.

**Cash flow — the item requiring the most care.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $2,230.0M against $1,028.4M of net income (2.2x conversion, driven by $411.5M depreciation and $498.8M amortization); capex $609.2M; free cash flow $1,620.8M, a 5.7% margin. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $1,487.2M versus $538.9M, which management characterizes as a 176% increase. The disclosed drivers are: contract liabilities +$700.2M (versus −$32.9M), payables and accrued/other non-current liabilities +$884.0M (versus +$64.5M), receivables −$1,127.1M (versus −$69.5M) as AR grew from $6,847.1M to $8,532.3M, and a prior-year drag from a ~$109.1M deferred federal tax payment. Netting the disclosed lines, working capital was a modest net source in H1 2026 against a net use in H1 2025. H1 free cash flow was $1,036.2M ($1,487.2M less $451.0M capex). FY2026 capex is guided to $750-800M.

The contract-liability position deserves explicit treatment. The balance grew from $3,258.5M to $4,241.9M and the filing attributes the increase to "favorable billing terms on certain large projects." That is cash received ahead of work performed — real cash, but a liability that must be discharged through future performance and one that reverses into a cash drag if new advance-billed awards slow. Quanta did recognize $2.53B of H1 revenue from prior-year-end contract liabilities, so the balance turns over. Annualizing H1 operating cash flow is not defensible given disclosed seasonality (working capital builds in summer and fall and converts in winter) plus the billing-terms effect.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $28,291.8M, goodwill $7,868.9M, other intangibles $3,216.1M, stockholders' equity $9,637.9M, total long-term debt obligations $6,096.2M, cash $506.4M. Net debt of $5,589.8M against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $3,456.7M (FY2025 $2,876.3M less H1 2025 $1,172.6M plus H1 2026 $1,753.0M) gives net leverage of 1.62x, down from 1.93x at year-end. Funded debt to equity is 0.67x. Liquidity was $2,766.1M. In July the revolver was increased from $2.80B to $2.98B and extended from July 2030 to July 2031, with the commercial paper ceiling raised to $2.98B effective August 8. Covenant compliance is confirmed. The senior notes carry a $4.48B fair value against $4.71B carrying value, reflecting legacy low coupons (2.35% 2032, 2.90% 2030, 3.05% 2041) marked against current rates; the revolver ran at a 4.91% weighted average in Q2 and commercial paper at 4.11%.

Goodwill plus intangibles of $11,085.0M exceeds equity of $9,637.9M, so tangible book value is negative by roughly $1.45B. For an acquisitive asset-light services business this is not a solvency concern at 1.62x leverage with $2.77B of liquidity, but it does mean the equity cushion depends on acquired businesses performing. Management performed only qualitative goodwill assessments in 2024 and 2025. With market capitalization of $101.0B against $9.6B of book equity, consolidated-level headroom is enormous and the qualitative election is defensible; the exposure is a reporting-unit question we cannot size from disclosure. H1 2026 customer-relationship valuations used 15-17% discount rates and 10-20% attrition assumptions — the sensitivity to watch.

An Altman Z-Score of 2.25 has been cited on this name. It is structurally depressed here: the working-capital and EBIT/assets terms are penalized by advance billings and by $11.1B of acquired intangibles that inflate the asset denominator without generating segment EBIT, and the implementation uses book rather than market equity. We do not read it as a distress signal.

## Growth Analysis

The forward evidence is unusually strong on three independent measures.

**Order book.** Total backlog $53.44B at June 30 (+21.5% in six months, following +27.3% in 2025). Remaining performance obligations $33.55B (+41.2% in six months, following +41.8% in 2025). A definitional caution: Quanta's backlog is a non-GAAP measure broader than RPO, including estimated orders and renewals under master service agreements that customers are not contractually committed to — $19.89B, or 37% of the total, at June 30. RPO is the contractual measure and it is the faster-growing one, which improves order-book quality even as it raises fixed-price exposure. MSA share of total backlog fell from 44% to 41%, and of twelve-month backlog from 37% to 33%.

**Estimate revisions.** FY2026 consensus rose 19.2% in thirty days and 19.3% in ninety; FY2027 rose 16.6% and 17.0%. Q3 2026 moved from $4.24 to $4.97 (+17.2%) and Q4 from $3.81 to $4.71 (+23.6%). Revision velocity of this magnitude, combined with 34% first-half revenue growth, is among the more reliable forward signals available. We weight it, with one qualification that matters: the revision-driven buy signal is strongest when the stock is cheap relative to the improving estimates. At 35x FY2027 consensus and a PEG of roughly 2.4 on FY2027 earnings growth of 14.8%, Quanta is not in that category. The revisions have compressed a very high multiple to a merely high one.

**Delivery record.** Four consecutive beats, with the last two at 27.2% and 28.7% — a step change from the 3.5% and 1.2% surprises in the two prior quarters. That pattern raises confidence in FY2027 consensus but also means consensus is now chasing rather than anchoring.

**Demand drivers.** Management attributes Electric demand to utility grid modernization, reliability and hardening programs, plus "increasing demand for electricity associated with, among other things, data centers and other technology-related dynamics, domestic manufacturing reshoring initiatives and overall electrification trends." Breadth across generation, transmission, equipment and construction is consistent with demand that is broad-based rather than a single-company story, which supports the durability of the growth thesis; management's own filings frame these as multi-year drivers.

Two offsets. First, Quanta names electricity affordability as a demand risk in its own filings: if utilities cannot meet technology-sector load "while maintaining affordability of electric power to end consumers, it could alter existing operating models and could result in a reduction in demand for our services." Given the political salience of retail rate increases attributed to data-center load, this is a specific rather than boilerplate risk. Second, the company lists availability of renewable energy tax credits among its demand drivers and warns that legislative initiatives may not be implemented or extended.

**Growth prospects: 8.0.** Order-book acceleration, revision velocity, organic contribution of two-thirds, and management's stated end-market drivers support a high score. We hold it below 8.5 because a meaningful share of reported growth is acquired (roughly a third of the Q2 increase), and because the backlog measure that grew fastest is the one carrying the most estimation risk.

Peer comparables in Construction & Engineering trade at a median trailing P/E of 62.7 (FIX 53.2, EME 27.2, MTZ 62.7, STRL 75.0, APG negative). Quanta at roughly 77x trailing GAAP sits at a ~22% premium to that median. But the cohort as a whole has already re-rated on the same electrification and data-center theme, so peer comparison establishes that Quanta is priced consistently with a re-rated group — not that either the group or Quanta is cheap.

We anchor the base case on twelve-month forward earnings power rather than trailing multiples, and we credit the beat cadence rather than taking consensus at face value. Assume FY2027 adjusted EPS of $20.50 — about 7% above the $19.19 consensus, well short of the 27-29% recent surprise rate — and FY2028 growth of 16% to roughly $23.75. The bull case does not require a multiple expansion; it requires the beat cadence to persist. FY2027 at $22.50 and FY2028 at $27.00 at 34.5x gives **$930**, +38.4%, near the $976 high target.

FY2027 flat at approximately $17.50 with the multiple at 27x gives **$470**, −30.0%, above the $410 low target.

Probability-weighting 50% base, 25% bull, 25% bear yields roughly **+7.6%** expected return over twelve months. For a name with realized volatility of 42.3% and an implied six-month move of 21.3%, that is approximately fair compensation and does not clear a BUY or ACCUMULATE bar at this price.

**Valuation: 4.5.** The score is up from where it sat in June — at $702 against a then-current-year consensus near $14.00 the stock was on roughly 50x, versus 40.2x today, a genuine 20% multiple compression achieved through estimate revisions rather than price weakness. Two behavioral data points on price. Management took zero open-market repurchases in H1 2026 despite a fresh $1.00B authorization effective May 21, and simultaneously issued 182,733 shares ($128.3M) in June and 64,889 shares ($45.0M) in July as acquisition consideration. Issuing equity near $700 rather than retiring it is internally consistent capital allocation, and it is consistent with management not viewing the stock as undervalued.

## Competitive Landscape

Quanta's own filings describe an industry "served by numerous companies, from small, owner-operated private companies to large multi-national, public companies," with "relatively few barriers... to entry into some areas of our business." Two competitive pressures are disclosed with specificity: "more competitive pricing for smaller scale projects," and subcontractors that "may develop into a competitor to us on prime contracts with our customers," with subcontracting requirements having increased in recent years (subcontracted work is now described as approximately 15-20% of the total, widened from "approximately 20%"). The company also names customer self-perform capability and the possibility that customers face regulatory pressure to self-perform.

The Q2 evidence is inconsistent with broad pricing pressure in Quanta's core work. Electric margin of 11.5% and Underground margin of 9.1% are the highest in disclosed history, achieved while revenue grew 41%. The disclosed pricing pressure is located specifically where barriers are lowest — small projects. The coherent reading is that Quanta is migrating up-market into large, complex fixed-price EPC work where credible bidders are few, scarce craft labor and $21.5B of bonding capacity function as barriers, and vertical integration into transformers, breakers and switchgear relieves the equipment constraint that gates customer schedules. Underground margin expanding from 5.7% (FY2024) to 7.3% (FY2025) to 9.1% (Q2 2026) is evidence that the acquired mechanical and civil businesses are absorbing fixed cost rather than diluting returns.

The trajectory is improving. **Competitive position: 7.3.** We hold it below 8 because the vertical-integration positions are minority or small-scale (the circuit-breaker JV is 49%-owned and equity-accounted, contributing no measured revenue yet), because the moat is weakest in the segment where pricing pressure is already disclosed, and because 30% top-ten customer concentration shows no evidence of improvement — the FY2025 10-K gives the level but no comparable prior-year figure, so no trend claim is supportable. A shifting end-market mix toward data centers and mechanical work is not evidence of reduced customer concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**Fixed-price concentration, rising each quarter.** Percentage-of-completion revenue was 56.5% (2023), 60.0% (2024), 63.8% (2025), and 68.8% in Q2 2026. Fixed-price contracts by type rose from 58.3% of Q2 2025 revenue to 63.7% of Q2 2026. Quanta states that fixed-price contracts "are more common in connection with our larger and more complex projects that typically involve greater performance risk" and that it has "strategically expanded these service offerings in recent years, including with respect to power generation projects, and the size and scope of these projects continues to increase." This is the primary mechanism by which a demand boom converts into an earnings disappointment. Net changes in estimates were favorable by 0.9% of revenue in Q2 2026 — helpful now, and a reminder that the same line can turn negative. At 40x forward earnings, a single large charge de-rates the stock disproportionately.

**Wildfire liability with an acknowledged coverage gap.** The FY2025 10-K states that insurers "have reduced coverage availability and increased the cost of insurance coverage for such events," and that "Quanta's current level of insurance coverage for wildfire events may not be sufficient to cover potential losses." The captive insurer can bear up to $100.0M per occurrence in certain general and auto liability circumstances. On the Silverado Fire: since 2022 two Quanta subsidiaries have received tenders of defense and evidence-preservation demands from Southern California Edison relating to an October 2020 Orange County fire alleged to have damaged ~13,000 acres; one subsidiary replaced two utility poles and reattached electrical and telecom equipment in March 2019, about 19 months before ignition. The relationship matters for sizing: SCE is the customer, Quanta subsidiaries agreed under master service agreements to defend and indemnify SCE, plaintiffs are property owners and subrogated insurers suing SCE and T-Mobile, and T-Mobile has cross-complained against SCE alleging the ignition site encompassed poles replaced by SCE or a third party and that equipment including T-Mobile's lashing wire was not sufficiently re-secured after the pole replacements. Quanta is not disclosed as a named defendant; it faces contractual indemnity tenders. Management assesses material loss as not probable and expects insurance to be adequate above the deductible. No damages figure, attachment point or reserve is disclosed, so the exposure is unquantified. Gross accruals for employer's liability, workers' comp, auto, general liability and group health grew from $521.4M to $648.2M in six months — slightly slower than the 34% revenue growth, which argues scale rather than deterioration — against only $5.7M of recorded recoveries.

**Acquisition accounting and goodwill.** Consideration for businesses acquired between June 2025 and June 2026 allocated to $693.6M net tangible assets, $1.88B identifiable intangibles and $2.15B goodwill — roughly 85% to intangibles and goodwill. Goodwill has grown from $3,586.7M (FY2022) to $7,868.9M. Contingent consideration carries at $814.4M against a $921.4M maximum. If the electrification and data-center capex cycle decelerates, impairment exposure is large relative to $9.6B of book equity, and the 10-20% customer attrition assumptions are the key sensitivity.

**Cash conversion quality.** The H1 operating cash flow figure is supported by a $700.2M contract-liability build and $884.0M of payables growth. A stalling advance-billing pipeline reverses both.

**Tax rate.** The 20.9% H1 rate versus 24.6% reflects a $35.9M equity-vesting windfall driven by the share price. Normalizing to 25% on H1 pretax income of $873.5M removes roughly $35.8M of net income, about $0.24 per H1 diluted share. This benefit is pro-cyclical and reverses if the stock falls.

**Rate and macro sensitivity.** The company funds acquisitions and working capital with revolver and commercial paper drawings at 4.91% and 4.11%. A higher-rate environment is an unfavorable backdrop for a long-duration compounder at 35-40x forward earnings, raising the cost of the borrowings funding the acquisition program. Separately, Quanta's data-center revenue stream is exposed to whatever correction risk attaches to technology-sector capex; the equity multiple is exposed to a sentiment de-rating even if physical demand holds.

**Risk that has been removed.** Unapproved change orders and claims recognized as revenue fell from $983.6M at December 31, 2025 to $411.1M at June 30, 2026, with the largest component being customer approval of the balance on a large Canadian renewable transmission project completed in 2024. That converts roughly $570M from disputed to agreed, removing a specific and quantified revenue-reversal risk that had been dragging DSO (60 days to 57) and cash flow for at least two years. It does not confirm collection — approved change orders still flow through billing — and $411.1M of unapproved claims remains at risk.

**Risk factors: 5.2**, up marginally from 5.0. The Canadian claim resolution and lower leverage are real improvements; rising fixed-price share and an unquantified wildfire tail against a 40x multiple keep the score below 6.

With IV near realized and at a low chain percentile, options are a cheap way to express a view here — which is more relevant to position construction than to the fundamental call. Short interest is only 2.4% of float at 2.2 days to cover, so there is no crowded bearish position and no squeeze dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $930 (+38.4%)

The operating inflection is real and self-reinforcing. Consolidated margin converges toward segment margin as revenue scales past the fixed amortization step-up from the Cupertino Electric and Dynamic Systems deals: FY2025 corporate costs at 4.0% of revenue, H1 2026 at 3.9%, with 180 basis points of consolidated margin expansion in Q2. Electric holds above 11% and Underground above 9% as acquired mechanical and civil businesses absorb fixed cost. RPO of $33.55B converts roughly 70% inside twelve months, giving unusual near-term revenue visibility, and total backlog is compounding above 20% semi-annually. The beat cadence (27.2% and 28.7% in the last two quarters) continues, taking FY2027 adjusted EPS to $22.50 against $19.19 consensus and FY2028 to $27.00. Vertical integration into transformers, circuit breakers and switchgear wins awards competitors cannot schedule. The Canadian claim resolution demonstrates that large disputed balances resolve in Quanta's favor. At 34.5x forward-year earnings — no expansion from today — the stock reaches $930.

### Bear Case — $470 (−30.0%)

Fixed-price revenue at 68.8% and rising, on projects whose "size and scope continues to increase," produces the 2024 outcome at larger scale: an unfavorable change in estimate on one or two large power-generation or data-center jobs turns the +0.9% estimate benefit negative and stalls Electric margin. Simultaneously, the flattering items reverse: the 20.9% tax rate normalizes to 25% (−$0.24 per half-year), the contract-liability build turns into a drag as advance-billed awards slow, and receivables of $8.53B compound working-capital demands. Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of the commercial paper and revolver borrowings funding the acquisition program, while electricity-affordability backlash — a risk management itself names — slows utility capex approvals. FY2027 adjusted EPS holds flat near $17.50 and the multiple compresses to 27x, giving $470. Secondary risks that would deepen this: a reporting-unit goodwill impairment against $11.1B of goodwill and intangibles exceeding $9.6B of equity, or an adverse turn in the Silverado wildfire indemnity matter where the company concedes coverage may be insufficient.

### Our view

The bull case requires no multiple expansion and the bear case requires no macro shock — only a repeat of a mistake this company has made before, at larger project scale. That symmetry, combined with a base case offering roughly 11% over twelve months, is the definition of fair value rather than opportunity. We note explicitly that this is the fourth consecutive HOLD on PWR (6.7 in February, 6.7 in May, 6.3 in June, 6.3 now) and that the composition has changed: growth prospects and valuation have both improved since June (the forward multiple compressed from roughly 50x to 40x), while risk factors improved only marginally and financial health improved modestly on lower leverage and the claim resolution. The price is 4% lower than at the June review while FY2026 consensus is 19% higher — that is improving risk/reward, and it is the reason we now name a specific upgrade level rather than a generic wait.

## Conclusion

Quanta earned its re-rating on operations: 41.1% Q2 revenue growth with two-thirds organic, 180 basis points of consolidated margin expansion, record margins in both segments, RPO up 41.2% in six months, and a $570M disputed claim converted to approved. The amortization drag that suppressed consolidated margin through FY2025 is now being outrun by revenue scale, and consensus has moved 19% higher on the current year in thirty days. Financial condition is sound: 1.62x net leverage, investment grade, $2.77B of liquidity, revolver extended to 2031.

What we cannot get to is a margin of safety. At $671.86 the stock is 40.2x FY2026 and 35.0x FY2027 adjusted consensus, 31.1x TTM EV/adjusted EBITDA, and under 2% on a normalized free-cash-flow yield, with a PEG of 2.4 on out-year growth. The base case is +11% and the bear case is −30%, and the bear case runs through a mechanism — fixed-price estimation error on large projects, now 68.8% of revenue and rising — that this company has already experienced once in this cycle. Management's own capital allocation reads the price the same way: zero repurchases against a fresh $1.00B authorization, while issuing equity near $700 as acquisition currency.

Existing holders should stay long; the operating trajectory does not warrant trimming. Prospective buyers have a clear, quantified level at which the arithmetic works — $590-620, or roughly 31x FY2027 consensus — and a clear set of operating conditions that would justify paying up before then.

**Recommendation: HOLD. Overall score 6.3.**
