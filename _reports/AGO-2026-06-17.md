**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Assured Guaranty is the world's leading financial guaranty insurer, operating an effective duopoly in U.S. public finance (against Build America Mutual) and a near-monopoly in structured finance. The investment case rests on a single, durable mechanism: the stock trades at a steep discount to its per-share intrinsic value (book value $125.32, adjusted book value $186.43 against a $77.25 price), and management has converted that discount into shareholder value by repurchasing 81% of shares outstanding since 2013 at prices well below ABV. This is a "shrink-to-grow" compounder where the per-share value lever is buybacks, not revenue growth.

The business is structurally low-growth. Muni insurance penetration sits around 7.5% of par, the in-force book amortizes faster than new production replaces it ($3.6B of net deferred premium remains to be earned), and reported GAAP earnings are lumpy. FY2025 net income of $503M (diluted EPS $10.26) was flattered by roughly $220M of non-recurring items: a $103M LBIE litigation gain, $96M of FX remeasurement gains, and a $23M building-sale gain. The cleaner Insurance-segment adjusted operating income declined modestly year-over-year ($508M vs $525M), so the trailing EPS overstates run-rate earnings power. Consensus reflects this: current-quarter estimates have been cut 11.4% over 30 days and next-quarter 13.9%, even as full-year estimates edged up slightly.

We rate AGO a HOLD with a constructive long-term lean. The discount-plus-buyback engine is real and the balance sheet is well-capitalized (S&P assesses "AAA" capital adequacy under its model), but two factors temper enthusiasm for new capital here: the $2.4B net par exposure to Thames Water (internally rated B, in active senior-creditor restructuring as of June 2026), and the absence of an identifiable catalyst to close the book-value discount, which has persisted for years. The repurchase program is the realized-return mechanism; the discount closing is optionality, not a base case.

## Company Overview

Assured Guaranty Ltd is a Bermuda-domiciled, UK tax-resident holding company. Its core product is an unconditional, irrevocable guaranty of scheduled principal and interest on debt obligations. A structural feature central to its risk profile is that it generally cannot be forced to pay on an accelerated basis, only the periodic shortfall when due. This "pay-as-it-comes" structure underpins its liquidity management and explains why even severe credit events (Puerto Rico, Detroit) have been workouts rather than liquidity shocks.

The company runs three reportable activities:

1. **Insurance** — the earnings engine, generating $508M of adjusted operating income in 2025 across U.S. public finance (munis/infrastructure), non-U.S. public finance (heavily UK), and structured finance (fund finance, insurance reserve financing, legacy RMBS, CLOs).
2. **Asset Management** — a ~30% non-controlling interest in Sound Point Capital (>$44B AUM), contributing $20M of adjusted operating income in 2025, up sharply from $5M and $3M in prior years. Small but a growing fee-based diversifier.
3. **Life & Annuity Reinsurance** — newly established via the January 2026 acquisition of Assured Life Re (formerly Warwick Re) for $158M, covering UK bulk purchase annuities/pension risk transfers and U.S. MYGA.

In U.S. public finance, AGO is the only pre-2008 guarantor still continuously writing, holding ~58% of insured par in 2025 against BAM's ~42%. Its advantages are a larger capital base, capacity to insure larger and more diverse deals, and multiple AA-category ratings (AA from S&P, AA+ from KBRA, A1 from Moody's) versus BAM's single AA. In structured finance, AGO is effectively the only financial guaranty insurer writing new business. This is a moaty but mature industry with a structurally capped total addressable market.

The per-share value framework is the right lens. Adjusted operating shareholders' equity is $126.78/share. At $77.25, the stock trades at roughly 0.62x book and 0.41x ABV. This discount has historically defined the thesis and the management response: buy below book, harvest via buybacks.

Earnings quality requires care. The $503M GAAP result reflected the non-recurring items detailed above. Adjusted operating income of $445M is the better run-rate figure, with Insurance-segment adjusted operating income falling on lower refunding-driven premium acceleration and higher public-finance loss expense. Trailing EPS of $10.26 should not be extrapolated. The earnings-surprise record reinforces the lumpiness: three beats (47.8%, 37.0%, 60.4%) and one large miss (-34.2%) over the last four quarters reflect mark-to-market and loss-development swings, not a clean visibility trend.

Capital allocation is the dominant story. Since 2013, AGO has repurchased 157M shares for $5.9B, equal to 81% of shares outstanding at program inception. In 2025 alone, shares fell from 50.5M to 45.2M ($500M repurchased at an average $85.92). Management estimates cumulative buybacks added approximately $68.77/share to book equity and $116/share to ABV. Because repurchases occurred well below ABV, they were heavily accretive to per-share value. $204M remained authorized as of February 2026, and management targets ~$500M of repurchases in 2026. In a flat-revenue business, this is the primary per-share growth lever, and it has been executed consistently. The mechanism: company buys shares at ~0.41x ABV → each retired share transfers ABV to remaining holders → per-share ABV rises even when aggregate ABV is flat or falling.

The balance sheet is sound. The $8.5B investment portfolio is 87% fixed-maturity, high grade, with a 4.76% book yield. Holding-company long-term debt of $1.7B is well-laddered (2028–2066). Insurance subsidiaries can upstream roughly $245M (AG) plus $213M (AG Re) of ordinary dividends in 2026. New liquidity backstops, FHLBNY membership ($300M capacity) and $400M of committed capital securities, add cushion. The Altman Z-Score of 1.18 ("distress") and any "revenue = 0" screen readings are artifacts of applying industrial-company models to an insurer and should be disregarded; AGO carries S&P "AAA" capital adequacy.

ROE was 9.6% and ROA 4.5%, with debt-to-equity of 1.13, appropriate for the business model. The dividend yield is modest at ~2.0%, with the return profile weighted heavily toward buybacks rather than distributions.

## Growth Analysis

This is the weakest dimension and the principal reason the stock is not a BUY. Bond insurance penetration is roughly 7.5% of muni issuance by par, down from 8.3% and 8.8% in prior years, in a mature, consolidated industry. Revenue is essentially flat-to-declining as the in-force book amortizes faster than new production replaces it. The $3.6B of net deferred premium remaining to be earned provides visibility into a declining premium stream, not a growing one.

The estimate-revision picture confirms negative near-term earnings momentum: current-quarter EPS estimates fell 11.7% over 90 days; next-quarter fell 14.2%. Full-year estimates rose modestly (+4.1% over 30 days, +2.4% over 90 days), and next-year fell 3.9%. The mixed signal reflects the difficulty of modeling a business whose reported earnings depend on FX, mark-to-market, refunding activity, and loss development. The quarterly trend is down.

The growth optionality, such as it is, comes from diversification away from the cyclical guaranty stream. Sound Point fee income grew to $20M of adjusted operating income, and Assured Life Re introduces spread and longevity income. Both leverage AGO's stated core competency in credit, but Sound Point is a minority stake with limited control and Assured Life Re is a brand-new, unproven platform introducing a different underwriting discipline. Modest bond-insurance market growth (+4% in 2025 to ~$42.8B insured par on record muni supply) is a small tailwind. There is also a genuine counter-cyclical feature: AGO's product becomes more valuable when credit spreads widen, so a recession or volatility spike could lift demand even as it raises default risk, a partial natural hedge. None of this changes the structural reality that top-line growth is not the equity story.

The valuation case is built on the discount to per-share value rather than on an earnings multiple.

Against peers (PLMR 16.1x, RDN 7.9x, HCI 7.1x, STC 14.6x, RLI 12.6x), AGO's 8.9x trailing P/E sits below the 12.6x median. But the trailing P/E is distorted by the non-recurring 2025 gains; on cleaner adjusted operating EPS of $9.08, the multiple is ~8.5x, and forward consensus EPS of $7.26 (current year) puts the forward P/E near 10.6x. The earnings-based comparison is therefore less favorable than the headline 8.9x implies, and bond insurers are not directly comparable to mortgage insurers (RDN) or specialty/title names (RLI, STC).

The genuine valuation support is the book and ABV discount, combined with management's demonstrated ability to convert it into per-share value via buybacks. There is a specific mechanism (sub-ABV repurchases) that has added a documented ~$116/share of ABV cumulatively. That said, the discount has persisted for years without closing, so the realized return has come from per-share accretion, not multiple re-rating. A buyer at 0.41x ABV is paying for the floor and the repurchase machine; the discount narrowing is optionality, not a dated catalyst.

The analyst price-target distribution (low $80, high $103, mean $92.33, median $94) implies the consensus sees the discount as too wide, with a median ~22% above the current price. We treat these as reasonable but not as a reliable floor, because near-term estimates are falling, which is when consensus targets tend to drift down rather than up.

We score valuation 6.5: the asymmetry is favorable on a multi-year view given the discount and buyback cadence, but the absence of a re-rating catalyst and the falling near-term estimates cap it short of a high-conviction valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

AGO occupies an unusually defensible position for a financial company. Post-2008, the U.S. public finance guaranty field collapsed from more than a dozen players to two, AGO and BAM, and to one in structured finance. AGO's larger capital base and superior ratings stack give it pricing and deal-size advantages. The barriers to entry are formidable: ratings, capital, and a multi-decade track record that issuers and investors rely on.

The trajectory is stable-to-mildly-positive on share within a structurally flat-to-shrinking market. AGO's ability to insure larger, more complex deals supports the case that it is not losing ground. The deliberate pivot toward fee income (Sound Point) and spread/longevity income (Assured Life Re) is a credible attempt to widen the franchise beyond the cyclical guaranty premium stream, leveraging the same credit-analysis core competency.

The competitive risk is not displacement but ratings dependency. Demand for the product is a direct function of AA-category financial strength ratings. S&P and Moody's apply subjective downward adjustments (largest-obligor test, competitive profile) that cap ratings below model-implied capital adequacy, meaning a downgrade may not be curable simply by raising capital. A downgrade below AA would pressure pricing; a downgrade below single-A would cripple new business. This is the structural vulnerability that offsets the moat. We score competitive position 6.5: a strong, durable franchise with a capped TAM and a ratings-dependency overhang.

## Risk Assessment

**UK water utility concentration — the standout idiosyncratic risk.** AGO's largest non-U.S. exposures are dominated by UK regulated water: Southern Water $2,866M (BBB-), Thames Water $2,400M (internally rated B, distressed), Dwr Cymru $2,060M (A-), Anglian $1,923M (A-), Yorkshire $1,384M (BBB). Thames Water reported a £1.65bn FY loss, warned its turnaround would take "at least a decade," and is in an active June 2026 senior-creditor takeover process that could involve ~£749M ($1B) in fees. AGO typically wraps senior-secured (Class A) tranches, which have fared better than junior debt in restructurings, and the company has an elite workout record (Puerto Rico, Detroit). UK regulated-utility and student-accommodation exposures drove $33M of non-U.S. loss development in 2025. Total UK net par is ~$24.5B, with additional sensitivity to UK inflation since index-linked debt accretes par. This is a live workout, not a tail risk, and it is the single most important credit story to monitor.

**PREPA / Puerto Rico.** The last unresolved Puerto Rico payment default: $464M net par, $537M debt service, driving ~$64M of U.S. public finance loss development in 2025, with scheduled amortization to 2037.

**Ratings dependency** (discussed above) is the structural risk to the franchise.

**Assured Life Re — new, unproven line.** The first dedicated life/annuity platform introduces longevity, mortality, and lapse assumptions plus integration risk to a credit-risk company. The 10-K added multiple new Strategic Risk factors specifically for this entry: a genuine change in risk profile, with diversification upside but unproven underwriting.

**Sound Point / alternatives.** The ~30% non-controlling stake provides no control, sits alongside a $1.5B alternatives commitment ($490M unfunded) and a reinvestment lock-up to 2038, and adds mark-to-market volatility (a $29M increase in credit-loss allowance on CLO equity/loss-mitigation securities in 2025).

The net BIG (below-investment-grade) exposure fell to $8.77B from $10.19B, a positive trend that partially offsets the named concentrations. On balance, the idiosyncratic risk set is real and specific (Thames Water in active restructuring being the live wire), which is why we score risk factors 5.0 rather than higher despite the strong capital position.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 61.3% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 21.9% (a 39.4-point spread), in the 76th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (61.3% near-term versus 24.5% longer-dated), the put/call volume ratio is an elevated 10.0, and the put/call OI ratio rose to 2.30 from 1.88 since early May. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.6%. The skew itself is balanced (-1.4%), so this is broad near-term event-hedging rather than a directional crash-hedge, likely tied to the active Thames Water restructuring timeline and the post-earnings drawdown (the stock gapped from $82.43 to $75.05 on May 8 following the May 7 print). The 6-month skew of -3.5% indicates modest downside positioning further out but not structural-collapse pricing. The signal supports the cautious read on near-term risk without contradicting the longer-term thesis. For existing holders, the rich near-term IV makes covered-call overwriting attractive.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

AGO is a per-share value compounder hiding behind a flat top line. At 0.41x ABV and 0.62x book, the downside is cushioned by tangible per-share value, and the buyback machine mechanically transfers that value to remaining holders, with another ~$500M targeted in 2026 against a $3.4B market cap (roughly 15% of shares at current prices). Management's compensation is tied to the relevant metrics (Core ABV/share, Core Operating income/share, PVP, relative TSR), and 5-year TSR of 311 versus a peer group at 201 evidences execution. The Thames Water exposure is senior-secured and AGO's workout track record is elite; resolution at acceptable recoveries removes an overhang. Diversification into fee (Sound Point) and longevity (Assured Life Re) income reduces dependence on the cyclical guaranty stream. If credit spreads widen in a recession, demand for the product rises, a counter-cyclical kicker. A bull case of ~$110–120 reflects partial closure of the book-value discount toward 0.85–0.95x book combined with continued buyback accretion.

### Bear Case

The discount has persisted for years and may never close; the realized return is buybacks plus a 2% dividend, not re-rating. Trailing EPS overstates run-rate earnings, and Insurance-segment adjusted income is declining. Near-term estimates are falling sharply. Thames Water is in active distress, and the broader stressed UK water sector (Southern BBB-, Yorkshire BBB) is a correlated concern subject to Ofwat price-review outcomes. A ratings downgrade, not curable simply by raising capital given subjective rating-agency adjustments, would impair new business demand. Assured Life Re introduces unfamiliar longevity risk. The bear case of ~$62–66 reflects elevated UK water loss development, a soft new-business environment in a tight-spread market, and continued discount persistence, roughly -15% to -20% from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income/value hold with constructive long-term lean).**

The thesis is balanced. The deep discount-to-ABV and the proven sub-ABV buyback machine make this a reasonable long-term per-share compounder for existing holders, supported by a ~2% dividend. But new capital lacks an asymmetric near-term catalyst: there is no earnings inflection (estimates are falling near-term), the discount-closing is optionality rather than a dated event, the Thames Water restructuring is an unresolved live risk, and sector selection in Financials has historically delivered negative relative returns, raising the bar for a fresh BUY.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and any update on the Thames Water senior-creditor takeover (progressing June 2026); the March 2026 Item 5.02 director/officer change as a succession watch item.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Thames Water restructuring resolves with recoveries at or above carrying value on the wrapped senior tranches, removing the largest below-investment-grade overhang.
- Adjusted operating EPS stabilizes or estimate revisions turn positive (current-quarter consensus rising rather than falling), confirming run-rate earnings power above ~$7.50.
- A re-rating catalyst emerges: accelerated buyback above the $500M target, a special distribution, or evidence the book-value discount is narrowing toward 0.75x+.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A ratings agency places AGO on negative outlook or downgrades below AA, threatening new-business demand.
- Material adverse loss development across the UK water book (Thames recovery below senior-tranche carrying value, or contagion to Southern/Yorkshire) that erodes ABV.
- Evidence Assured Life Re or Sound Point alternatives are generating outsized mark-to-market losses that overwhelm buyback accretion.

## Conclusion

Assured Guaranty is a high-quality, well-capitalized franchise with a genuine moat (effective duopoly in U.S. public finance, monopoly in structured finance) trading at a steep discount to per-share value, with a disciplined and accretive buyback program as the realized-return engine. For existing holders, the combination of a tangible-value floor, a ~2% dividend, and ~15%-of-shares annual repurchases supports holding through the cycle, and we lean constructive over a multi-year horizon.

We stop short of a BUY for new capital. The growth profile is structurally flat, near-term earnings momentum is negative, the trailing EPS overstates run-rate power, and the $2.4B Thames Water exposure is an unresolved live risk in active restructuring. The book-value discount that anchors the thesis has persisted for years without closing; the return has come from per-share accretion, not re-rating, and there is no dated catalyst to change that. HOLD, with reassessment at the next earnings print and on resolution of the Thames Water workout.
