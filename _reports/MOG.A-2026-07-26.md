**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Moog Inc. is a worldwide designer and systems integrator of high-performance precision motion and fluid controls, with roughly 75–76% of revenue tied to aerospace and defense. The operating story is strong and accelerating: first-half FY2026 (ended March 28, 2026) delivered net sales of $2,152M (+17% YoY), net earnings of $161M (+43%), and diluted EPS of $5.01 (+44%), with margin expansion across all four segments. The 12-month backlog jumped to $3.31B (+33% YoY), driven by the Textron Bell MV-75 (FLRAA) program, broad Space & Defense capture, and a differentiated data-center liquid-cooling exposure inside the Industrial segment. The earnings-beat streak is intact: four consecutive beats, most recently +8.5% in the March 2026 quarter.

The complicating factor is governance. During the FY2025 close, management identified misstatements in long-term aftermarket service contracts in the Commercial Aircraft segment — inaccurate inputs to total-cost-at-completion estimates used in cost-to-cost revenue recognition, plus a warranty understatement — that accumulated over several years and forced a revision of FY2023, FY2024, and interim FY2024/FY2025 financials. Management concluded that both internal control over financial reporting and disclosure controls were not effective as of September 27, 2025, and the material weakness still existed as of March 28, 2026. The failure sits in the same over-time, fixed-price accounting that underpins roughly 65% of revenue, and it coincided with two Item 4.01 auditor-change filings (August and November 2025). That combination is a yellow-flag pattern.

The result is a tension between an excellent, largely organic operating inflection and an unresolved control failure in the mechanism that produces the reported numbers. We rate MOG.A a HOLD (growth_hold). The fundamentals support continued appreciation and would justify a higher score on their own; the unremediated material weakness, the auditor change we could not fully diagnose, and the jump in favorable prior-period revenue catch-ups ($27.3M in H1 FY26 vs. $11.3M prior year) in the very estimation area that broke keep us from a BUY until remediation is confirmed and the auditor situation is clarified.

## Company Overview

Moog operates through four reporting segments, each carrying operating margins in the 11–13% range:

- **Space & Defense** (FY25 sales $1,113M): satellite components, missile steering controls, launch-vehicle thrust controls, turrets.
- **Military Aircraft** ($888M): F-35 flight controls, Textron Bell MV-75 (FLRAA), V-22 aftermarket.
- **Commercial Aircraft** ($904M): Boeing 787 and Airbus A350 flight controls, plus aftermarket spares and repair.
- **Industrial** ($956M): industrial automation, flight simulation, energy, medical devices, and data-center liquid-cooling pumps.

Defense alone is ~52% of revenue. The moat rests on being an embedded, sole- or limited-source supplier on long-life aircraft and defense platforms. Once designed into a platform such as the F-35, 787, or A350, Moog captures decades of high-margin aftermarket revenue. Manufacturing is globally diversified across the US, Philippines, UK, Germany, Italy, Costa Rica, China, Netherlands, Japan, Canada, India, and Lithuania. Management strategy centers on an 80/20 "simplification" program (portfolio shaping, footprint rationalization, divestitures) and a pricing-for-value initiative.

The company carries a dual-class structure — Class A (MOG.A, ~28.4M shares, limited voting) and Class B (MOG.B, ~3.2M shares, full voting) — that concentrates voting control with the Moog family and insiders, supplemented by SECT and SERP trusts that hold and vote Moog shares. This reduces external shareholder influence over governance outcomes.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings growth is strong and accelerating.** FY2025 net sales reached $3,861M (+7% YoY), following $3,609M in FY2024 and $3,316M in FY2023, a steady mid-to-high-single-digit organic trajectory. FY2025 net earnings were $235M ($7.33 diluted EPS), up from $209M (FY24, revised) and $175M (FY23, revised). Q2 FY26 EPS reached $2.55, up 49%. All four segments grew, and operating margins expanded: Space & Defense to 13.8%, Military Aircraft to 13.7%, and Industrial to 12.9% in Q2.

**Backlog underwrites the runway.** Total backlog reached $6.0B at FY25-end (+19%). Remaining performance obligations stood at $7.23B. This is a visible, contracted growth base rather than a demand forecast.

**Margins.** Consolidated gross margin runs ~27% (27.4% FY25; 27.0% H1 FY26). The near-term pressure is tariffs, which compressed Commercial Aircraft margins; management is offsetting with pricing. The FY24 gross margin benefited from a one-time $14M CARES Act Employee Retention Credit, which creates an unfavorable optical comparison in FY25 but does not reflect operating deterioration.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow improved to $273M (from $198M), aided by $123M of customer advances on defense programs. H1 FY26 operating cash flow swung to +$85M (from −$93M a year earlier), and cash rose to $308M at March 2026 (from $62M), largely on financing activity. Leverage is moderate: total debt ~$1.25B against ~$2.1B equity. Covenants require interest coverage ≥3.0x and net-debt/EBITDA ≤4.0x, and the company is comfortably compliant. The business is capital-intensive: capex ~$145M FY25, with heavy finance-lease use ($153M finance-lease liabilities).

**Liability management.** In H1 FY26, Moog extended its $1.1B revolver and $250M term loan to 2031, issued $500M of 5.50% senior notes due 2034, and redeemed the $500M 4.25% notes due 2027 (in April 2026). This terms out the maturity wall at a modestly higher coupon and removes near-term refinancing risk. The receivables purchase agreement was amended in January 2026 (new agent Truist, maturity to February 2028, $125M capacity fully utilized).

**Capital allocation.** Balanced: FY25 included $100M of buybacks (1.7M shares remain authorized) and $36M of dividends, with the quarterly dividend raised to $0.30/share. No open-market buybacks occurred in Q2 FY26, plausibly reflecting caution during the restatement period, and worth monitoring as a tell on management's own conviction while controls remain unremediated.

The financial-health picture is solid but not pristine: strong cash generation and a termed-out maturity profile against a capital-intensive model, moderate leverage, and $874M of goodwill plus $61M of intangibles against ~$4.9B total assets, where the Medical Devices reporting unit already carries a $38.2M accumulated impairment. We score financial health 6.3 — above average on cash flow and leverage discipline, tempered by capital intensity and the accounting-quality overhang that sits directly in the revenue line.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has multiple independent legs, which raises its durability:

1. **Defense.** Elevated US and European defense spending, with content growth on next-gen programs. The MV-75 ramp is a multi-year growth engine, complemented by missile/turret and satellite demand. This aligns with a broadly confirmed multi-front defense demand backdrop.
2. **Commercial aircraft.** Boeing 787 and Airbus A350 widebody rate recovery drives OEM growth, while high fleet utilization drives lucrative aftermarket (Commercial aftermarket +$68M in FY25). The aftermarket franchise is the higher-margin, stickier component.
3. **Space.** Growing satellite and launch-vehicle component demand.
4. **Industrial — data-center liquid cooling.** Moog's liquid-cooling pumps for data centers are a differentiated, secular-growth exposure cited repeatedly as a backlog driver. This ties into the data-center buildout and AI-driven cooling demand. It is under-appreciated because it sits inside a segment the market tends to discount as cyclical.

The estimate and results momentum is meaningful. Four consecutive earnings beats (6.8%, 11.8%, 15.4%, 8.5%) together indicate the market has been systematically underestimating the near-term trajectory. Revenue growth is high-single-digit organic accelerating toward high-teens, with margin expansion — the kind of profile that typically precedes upward estimate revisions. We weight this positively but note the growth is not a trough-to-peak inflection off depressed earnings; it is a well-run mid-teens grower with expanding margins, which does not trigger the strongest single-signal inflection thesis but does support a durable growth_hold. Growth prospects score 7.2.

## Valuation Assessment

The provided data set lacks a company profile, market cap, P/E, and analyst targets, so valuation must be built from the price and earnings inputs available. At $410.13 and FY2025 diluted EPS of $7.33, the trailing P/E is ~56x. That headline number overstates the true multiple because it uses trailing earnings that materially understate current run-rate profitability. H1 FY26 EPS of $5.01 annualizes to roughly $10 on a simple doubling, and with Q2 momentum, a FY26 EPS in the $10.50–$11.00 range is reasonable. On ~$10.75, the forward P/E is ~38x.

That is a full multiple for a mid-teens-growth A&D supplier. This is not a cyclical-trough situation where trailing P/E is misleading in the way a semiconductor or memory name would be; Moog's earnings are compounding off already-healthy levels rather than recovering from a depressed base. Aerospace and defense primes and suppliers with visible backlogs have re-rated on the multi-front defense demand and aftermarket recovery, so 38x forward is within the re-rated A&D cohort but toward the richer end for the growth rate delivered. The PEG on ~40% near-term EPS growth is below 1.0, which flatters the picture, but that growth rate is a step-up phase (tariff-offset pricing, MV-75 ramp, favorable catch-ups) rather than a steady-state 40% grower — normalizing to a mid-teens algorithm, the PEG is closer to 2.5x.

We are deliberately skeptical of treating the compressed forward multiple as a green light. The valuation dimension has been a reliable value-trap warning across the broader tape, and here the "cheap on forward EPS" argument rests partly on catch-up revenue in the exact estimation area under a live material weakness. That is a reason to discount, not extrapolate, the forward number. The stock has also re-rated ~53% YTD into the mid-2026 high, so much of the good news is priced.

Balancing the durable growth (which argues the forward multiple can be sustained) against the accounting-quality discount (which argues for a haircut on reported earnings power), we score valuation 5.3 — modestly better than the prior report's 4.5. The improvement reflects that a higher share price on stronger-than-expected earnings is not, by itself, a reason to mark valuation down further; the forward multiple has not expanded proportionally to the earnings beat. But we do not reach a high valuation score because there is no clean catalyst for a re-rating beyond continued execution, and the earnings base carries a governance asterisk.

## Competitive Landscape

Moog occupies niche, high-barrier positions in precision motion control where it is frequently a sole or limited-source supplier. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: content growth on next-gen platforms (MV-75), aftermarket expansion on widebody fleets, and a differentiated data-center cooling product all point to share and content gains rather than erosion. Being designed into long-life platforms converts a single design win into decades of aftermarket annuity, the structural feature that makes A&D suppliers with embedded positions durable.

Offsetting factors: the company depends on OEM production rates (Boeing/Airbus) it does not control; some industrial and medical competitors are larger and better-resourced; and A&D supplier consolidation is an ongoing dynamic that cuts both ways. Cyclicality in the Industrial segment is real but partially masked by the secular cooling exposure.

The prior report trimmed competitive position from 8.0 to 7.0. We hold at 7.0. The moat is genuine and the trajectory favorable, but "improving" is the right characterization rather than "widening decisively" — the growth is coming from favorable end-market tailwinds Moog participates in, not from Moog taking dramatic share from named competitors. A 7.0 reflects a strong, stable-to-improving position without over-crediting a level that is already priced.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Unremediated material weakness in ICFR (the headline risk).** Remediation is ongoing but incomplete. The weakness sits in the same over-time, fixed-price accounting (~64–65% of revenue, ~90% of over-time sales fixed-price) that produces the reported numbers investors are paying 38x forward for.

**2. Auditor change coincident with the restatement.** Two Item 4.01 filings (August 8, 2025 and an 8-K/A on November 26, 2025) indicate a change of certifying accountant during the same window as the restatement. The identity of the predecessor/successor auditors and whether the change was routine or disagreement-related could not be confirmed. An auditor change alongside a restatement and a live material weakness carries binary downside if the change proves to be disagreement-related. This is the single most important unresolved item before a BUY.

**3. Favorable prior-period catch-ups jumped.** The H1 FY26 favorable catch-ups fall in the precise estimation area that just failed control testing. This is not necessarily improper, but the timing — large favorable catch-ups flowing through earnings during an unremediated control failure in that same account — warrants scrutiny and tempers confidence in the reported EPS momentum.

**4. Customer/government concentration.** US Government (direct plus prime/sub) is ~38% of sales; Boeing is ~10%; foreign governments ~9%. A Boeing production disruption or a defense-budget/continuing-resolution event would be material.

**5. Tariffs.** Moog both sources from and sells into China, exposing it to bilateral trade-policy risk.

**6. Backlog realization and goodwill.** A large portion of backlog depends on Boeing/Airbus rates and cancellable government programs. The goodwill and intangibles against ~$4.9B assets carry impairment risk; Medical Devices already carries the $38.2M accumulated impairment.

We score risk factors 4.5, matching the prior report's reset from 6.0. The dominant, unpriced-if-it-worsens risks — an unremediated control failure in the core revenue mechanism, a coincident and undiagnosed auditor change, and outsized favorable catch-ups in that same account — are specific, live, and asymmetric. The market appears to be pricing the operating strength while discounting the governance tail; a re-restatement, a disagreement-driven auditor change disclosure, or a delayed 10-K would all be negative surprises relative to a stock trading near highs.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Moog is a quiet A&D compounder with an accelerating, largely organic inflection anchored by the multi-year MV-75 ramp, widebody aftermarket recovery, and a differentiated data-center liquid-cooling franchise. The maturity wall is termed out to 2031/2034, cash flow inflected positive, and the earnings-beat streak suggests consensus continues to lag. If remediation completes cleanly and the auditor change proves routine, the governance discount evaporates and the stock re-rates on ~$10.75 FY26 EPS toward a mid-40s multiple. Bull case ~$480–$500 (roughly 17–22% upside).

**Bear case.** The material weakness proves broader than a "distinct group" of aftermarket contracts, the outsized favorable catch-ups reverse, or the auditor change surfaces as disagreement-related — any of which triggers a further restatement, a delayed filing, or a sentiment reset for a stock trading near highs at 38x forward. Layer in tariff-driven margin compression, a Boeing production stumble, or a continuing-resolution disruption to ~38% government revenue, and the multiple compresses to the low-30s on flat-to-down earnings. Bear case ~$300–$320 (roughly 22–27% downside).

**Base case.** Operating execution continues, remediation progresses without a further restatement, and the auditor change turns out to be manageable. The stock holds its re-rated multiple on rising earnings, appreciating in line with EPS growth net of a persistent governance discount. Base case ~$430–$450.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is intact and supported by contracted backlog and segment-wide margin expansion, but the unremediated control failure and undiagnosed auditor change prevent a BUY at 38x forward near all-time highs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmation that the material weakness has been remediated, with an unqualified ICFR opinion in a subsequent 10-K/10-Q.
- Clarification that the August/November 2025 auditor change was routine (not disagreement-related), ideally with resumption of open-market buybacks signaling management's own confidence.
- Continued backlog and EPS momentum (12-month backlog growth sustained >20%, another double-digit earnings beat) with no reversal of the favorable prior-period catch-ups.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any further restatement, a delayed periodic filing, or disclosure that the auditor change stemmed from a disagreement.
- Material reversal of the favorable revenue catch-ups, or evidence the material weakness extends beyond Commercial Aircraft aftermarket.
- A Boeing production cut or defense-budget disruption that impairs the 12-month backlog realization.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly report (Q3 FY26) and the FY2026 10-K are the natural checkpoints; the 10-K will disclose whether the material weakness has been remediated and will carry the new auditor's opinion.

## Conclusion

Moog is a genuinely good business hitting an accelerating stretch: mid-teens organic growth, expanding margins across all four segments, a growing backlog, and multiple durable demand vectors including an under-appreciated data-center cooling franchise. On the operating merits alone the stock would warrant a higher score. The problem is that the reported numbers rest on an over-time, fixed-price revenue mechanism that just failed control testing, the material weakness remains unremediated a full quarter later, an auditor change coincided with the restatement in a way we could not fully diagnose, and favorable prior-period catch-ups in that same account jumped sharply. At ~38x forward earnings near all-time highs, the market is pricing the strength and discounting the tail. We rate MOG.A a HOLD, with a clear path to a BUY once remediation and the auditor situation are resolved, and equally clear downgrade triggers if the governance issue proves broader than disclosed.
