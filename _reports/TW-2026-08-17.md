## Executive Summary

Tradeweb operates the leading electronic marketplace franchise in institutional fixed income — U.S. Treasuries, high-grade credit, TBA MBS, European government bonds, global interest rate swaps and European ETFs — with 3,000+ clients across 85+ countries, $2.6 trillion of FY2025 average daily volume and FY2025 revenue of $2,052.4M, up 18.9%. The balance sheet is strong: $2,058M of cash at 6/30/26, no drawn debt, an undrawn $500M revolver, and free cash flow of $1,064.6M in FY2025 equal to 51.9% of revenue. Nothing in the current data set threatens solvency, liquidity or the going concern.

What has changed is the earnings-conversion mechanism. Q2 2026 revenue of $558.9M grew 9.0%, roughly half the ~21.3% pace of Q1 2026 (derived: H1 2026 of $1,176.7M less Q2 gives Q1 of $617.8M against a reconstructed Q1 2025 of $509.3M). Adjusted EBIT margin was 50.6% against 50.7%, down 20bps reported and 74bps in constant currency, while GAAP operating margin rose 490bps to 43.9% on lapping and normalization effects. Management guided FY2026 adjusted expenses to the top half of $1,100–1,160M against FY2025's $1,018.4M, implying roughly 11–14% cost growth. Nine percent revenue growth against 11–14% expense growth is negative operating leverage, and it explains a roughly 10–12% single-day decline on results that management's own adjusted figures show as an 11.5% EPS advance.

Underneath the margin arithmetic sits a harder question. Q4 2025 disclosed blended average variable fees per million of $2.04 versus $2.28, down 10.2%, with credit down 20.4% and cash credit at $126.83/mm, down 14.3%. Credit ADV grew 22.9% while credit revenue grew 4.3% (+2.5% constant currency), and U.S. high-grade total TRACE share fell 110bps to 25.7% with fully electronic share flat at 18.3%. Roughly eleven percentage points of volume growth did not convert to revenue. Public disclosure does not permit separating mix (low-fee short-tenor swaps at ~$0.21/mm, wholesale Treasuries, all-to-all odd lots) from price. That is the central unresolved variable, and it determines whether a ~30x multiple on adjusted earnings is defensible. We rate the shares HOLD: high-quality franchise, adequate but unexciting risk/reward at $105.49, and no catalyst we can date that resolves the fee-conversion question before the Q3 print.

## Company Overview

Tradeweb Markets Inc. is an Up-C holding company whose only material asset is its interest in Tradeweb Markets LLC. The operating business runs electronic marketplaces across rates, credit, equities and money markets, organized by client sector rather than product:

| Client sector | Q2 2026 revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional | $355.4M | +10.4% |
| Wholesale | $109.6M | +8.3% |
| Retail | $33.1M | **−12.0%** |
| Corporates (ICD Portal) | $23.6M | +8.5% |
| Market data | $37.3M | +22.6% |
| **Total** | **$558.9M** | **+9.0%** |

Approximately 53% of FY2025 revenue came from the Rates asset class; credit asset-class revenue was $488.0M; 42% of revenue was international.

Two capital-structure facts materially affect any valuation work. First, LSEG/Refinitiv held ~90.0% of combined voting power and ~51.2% of economics at 6/30/26; public Class A holders have ~9.9% of votes. Tradeweb relies on Nasdaq controlled-company exemptions from majority-independent-board and fully-independent-committee requirements. Second, Class A shares outstanding were 114.0M at 6/30/26, but total LLC Interests outstanding were 234.1M. The supplied market capitalization of $12.05B corresponds to Class A only (114.0M × $105.66). At the frozen basis of $105.485, the correct equity value is 234.1M × $105.485 ≈ **$24.69B**, and enterprise value is approximately $24.69B plus the $317.5M tax receivable agreement liability less $2,058M of cash ≈ **$22.95B**. Any screen anchored on the $12.0B figure understates Tradeweb's valuation by roughly half. Approximately 120M Class A shares are issuable on exchange of LLC Interests/Class B, a persistent supply overhang above the float.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2022 $1,188.8M → FY2023 $1,338.2M (+12.6%) → FY2024 $1,725.9M (+29.0%) → FY2025 $2,052.4M (+18.9%, +17.5% constant currency). H1 2026 revenue was $1,176.7M, +15.1%. The quarterly reconstruction is instructive: 2025 quarters ran $509.3M / $513.0M / $508.9M / $521.2M — nearly flat sequentially — while 2026 delivered $617.8M then $558.9M. Q2 2026 therefore fell 9.5% sequentially from an exceptional Q1 but remains 7.2% above Q4 2025. The deceleration from ~21% to 9% is substantially a volatility and comparison effect rather than a demand break; management characterized Q2 as "more normalized volatility."

**Margins and expenses.** FY2025 operating income was $835.3M, a 40.7% margin, versus roughly $678M in FY2024 and $505M in FY2023 — genuine multi-year operating leverage. Q2 2026 GAAP operating income of $245.3M produced a 43.9% margin against 39.0%. On the measures management itself uses for incentive compensation, the direction reverses: adjusted EBITDA margin 54.4% versus 54.2%, and adjusted EBIT margin 50.6% versus 50.7%, down 74bps in constant currency. The GAAP improvement is largely acquisition-related D&A lapping and other normalized items; the adjusted figures are the cleaner read on underlying leverage.

The cost lines driving this are specific and identifiable:
- Technology and communications: $42.0M versus $30.2M, **+39.1%**
- Occupancy: $8.4M versus $6.0M, **+40%** (new headquarters; $122.4M of "thereafter" lease payments disclosed)
- Related-party technology paid to LSEG: $7.1M versus $1.9M in Q2; $14.1M versus $4.0M in H1 — a ~$10M H1 step-up
- Headcount grew 11.1% in 2025 (1,412 → 1,569)

FY2026 guidance: acquisition-related D&A ~$160M, non-GAAP tax 23.5–24.5%, capex plus capitalized software $107–117M, LSEG market data revenue ~$105M.

**Fee-per-million — the dominant financial dynamic.** Q4 2025 ADV of $2.827 trillion grew 23.3% while revenue grew 12.5%. Blended average variable fees per million fell to $2.04 from $2.28 (−10.2%): credit −20.4%, cash credit $126.83/mm (−14.3%), rates −11.5%, cash equities −10.4%; derivatives ex-"other" $2.51 versus $2.59 (−3.2%). Short-tenor swaps carry roughly $0.21/mm, so volume growth concentrated there mechanically dilutes the blend without any per-product price cut. The 10-K also warns explicitly that clients "may become more price sensitive and exert pricing pressure on us" in weaker volume periods. Both mix and price are plausible contributors; the disclosure does not allow a decomposition. This is the single most important open question in the name, because a fixed cost base plus a structurally falling take rate is a different business model from a fixed cost base plus a stable one.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 FCF grew 31.6% — high-quality conversion with no accrual concerns. Goodwill of $3,150.1M plus ~$0.3B indefinite-lived intangibles is unchanged year over year; the 10-K names market-capitalization declines among impairment triggers, and the stock has de-rated 20–25% from 2025 levels, which puts the annual test in view without making impairment probable given the cash-flow base. The TRA liability obliges payment of 50% of realized tax savings, accelerates on change of control on full-utilization assumptions, and is not reimbursable if the IRS disallows the benefits.

The dividend was raised 16.7% to $0.14 quarterly ($0.56 annualized, a 0.53% yield at $105.485); coverage is not a question at this payout. Buybacks totaled $240.5M in H1 2026: 706,028 shares at an average $104.76 completing the 2022 program, then 1,684,901 shares at an average $98.35 under the new $500M authorization, leaving $334.3M. Both averages sit below the current price, meaning management repurchased into weakness rather than strength.

**Earnings quality caveat.** FY2025 consolidated net income rose 61.7% to $921.5M against 18.9% revenue growth, because MD&A discloses $270.9M of net realized and unrealized gains on Canton Coin holdings, $205.4M of which fell in Q4 2025. FY2025 pretax income of $1,175.0M against operating income of $835.3M reflects roughly $340M of non-operating contribution. Any net-income-derived ratio (net margin, ROE, ROA) is flattered; operating income, adjusted EBITDA and operating cash flow are not materially affected. In H1 2026 the mark reversed: unrealized losses of $18.0M, with holdings falling to $230.0M from $242.7M. The accounting is mandatory once a quoted price exists, so no inference about management intent is warranted, but ±$15–200M of quarterly earnings swing from a single illiquid token (cost basis $16.8M, ~81% of coins locked under commitments running to mid-2029, self-custodied private keys) degrades the earnings predictability that a 30x multiple assumes.

## Growth Analysis

The secular case is intact and quantifiable. Estimated ADV across Tradeweb's asset classes (ex-ICD) grew from ~$4.2 trillion in 2016 to ~$10.0 trillion in 2025, while Tradeweb's own ADV rose to $2.6 trillion, +213% versus FY2020. AiEX automated execution reached over 40% of institutional trades in 2025 from 30% in 2021 — the clearest quantified evidence of workflow embedding. The platform processes over 150,000 trades daily, feeding Ai-Price reference pricing (~30,000 U.S. corporate bonds, ~1 million municipals) and the FTSE Russell partnership, where FTSE incorporated Tradeweb closing prices for U.S. Treasuries, EGBs and Gilts into global fixed income indices including WGBI. Data embedded in third-party benchmarks is durable revenue.

Against that, near-term growth momentum has softened and consensus reflects it. Estimates have drifted lower across every window: current quarter $0.99 (−1.1% over 30 days), next quarter $1.01 (−1.3%), current year $4.06 (−0.5%), next year $4.59 (−0.8%). The 90-day picture is roughly flat (+0.2% current year, +0.3% next year). The earnings record shows one beat, one in-line and two misses in four quarters, with Q1 2026 missing by 2.1% and Q2 by 0.8%. This is a modest negative drift on a business still compounding revenue in the low-to-mid teens, not an estimate-revision inflection that would support aggressive positioning.

Management stated on the Q2 call that July 2026 revenue was tracking up in the low teens percent year over year with two month-end trading days remaining. That is externally reported commentary rather than a filed figure, and it is the most important near-term data point: at low-teens revenue growth against 11–14% expense growth, operating leverage is roughly neutral to slightly positive and the Q2 margin scare becomes a one-quarter event. At 9% revenue growth it does not.

The segment-level picture is mixed. Market data (+22.6%) and Institutional (+10.4%) are healthy; Corporates (+8.5%) adds a fourth client sector and a rate-sensitive fee stream; Retail is contracting 12.0%. Credit — the highest fee-per-million business and the one the 10-K names as "one of our biggest areas of focus" with "a long runway" — grew ADV 22.9% while producing 4.3% revenue growth and losing 110bps of total U.S. high-grade TRACE share. Fully electronic share was flat at 18.3% and high-yield electronic share rose 20bps, so this is deteriorating revenue conversion rather than demonstrated share loss to a named rival. Either way, the growth engine the equity story leans on did not advance in the most recent full-year data.

A genuine two-sided catalyst arrives on schedule: U.S. Treasury central clearing takes effect for certain cash transactions on December 31, 2026 and for repo/reverse repo on June 30, 2027. The 10-K describes it as potentially accelerating electronification or reducing trading activity and liquidity through added clearing cost. Given Rates is 53% of revenue, this is the largest dated uncertainty in the model.

## Valuation Assessment

Working from the frozen basis of $105.485 and the correct 234.1M LLC interest denominator:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Equity value (234.1M × $105.485) | ~$24.69B |
| Enterprise value (+$0.32B TRA − $2.06B cash) | ~$22.95B |
| P/E on FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS $3.47 | **30.4x** |
| P/E on current-year consensus $4.06 | **26.0x** |
| P/E on next-year consensus $4.59 | **23.0x** |
| EV / FY2025 FCF ($1,064.6M) | ~21.6x |
| EV / FY2025 adjusted EBITDA (~$1,108M, derived) | ~20.7x |
| Dividend yield | 0.53% |

The supplied forward and trailing P/E of 25.07 sits between the adjusted and consensus figures and is consistent with a ~$3.78 GAAP diluted EPS on a ~215M share denominator; we treat 26.0x current-year and 23.0x next-year consensus as the working anchors.

The stock has de-rated meaningfully. The FY2025 average share-withholding price disclosed in the 10-K was $137.13; insiders sold at ~$125 in March and $102.00 on June 1; the company repurchased at $104.76 and $98.35; the 52-week range is $91.42–$132.13. Current price is 20.2% below the 52-week high. This de-rating occurred while revenue grew 18.9% in FY2025 and 15.1% in H1 2026; it is multiple compression, not an earnings decline. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table quantifies it: Tradeweb's cumulative TSR index ended 2025 at 175.73 against a peer index of 194.20, implying roughly −17.5% total return versus +15.7% for peers in a year of 18.9% revenue growth.

The bull argument is that 23x next-year earnings for a debt-free, 54% adjusted-EBITDA-margin network business with 50%+ FCF conversion and secular electronification is a reasonable price, and that the derating has already discounted the fee-compression concern. The bear argument is that 23x forward is not obviously cheap for a business whose take rate is falling 10% a year, whose highest-value segment converted a 23% volume gain into 4% revenue growth, whose expense base is guided to grow faster than revenue, and whose Rates concentration faces a dated regulatory change in December 2026.

We do not find a specific, identifiable mispricing at $105.49. Consensus targets span $98–$178 with a mean of $123.86 and median $120.00 — an 80-point high-to-median gap that signals genuine analyst disagreement rather than a stale consensus. The estimate-revision guidance about analyst targets acting as floors applies to stocks with rising estimates; here estimates are drifting down across all four horizons, so the targets carry no such asymmetry. Nor is this a cycle-adjusted-trough situation: margins are near peak (50.6% adjusted EBIT), volumes are near record, and the pressure is on the take rate rather than on volume. A valuation score of 5.0 reflects fair value with roughly symmetric distribution; cheapness relative to the 2025 multiple is not itself a buy signal when the reason for the compression remains unresolved.

Our base case is **$118**, derived as 24.5x current-year consensus of $4.06 plus modest credit for the low-teens July revenue commentary, or equivalently ~25.7x on the $4.59 next-year figure discounted for the fee-conversion uncertainty. That is +11.9% from $105.485, close to the analyst median of $120.

## Competitive Landscape

Tradeweb's moat as the filings describe it is protocol and workflow breadth rather than price leadership: RFQ (pioneered 1998), RFM, request-for-stream, list trading, compression, blast all-to-all, click-to-trade, portfolio trading, session-based Sweep, CLOB, bilateral firm streams, inventory-based, Rematch, and voice. A client trading Treasuries via RFQ can be cross-sold swaps compression, ETF portfolio trades and repo through a single integration. Management claims electronic-trading leadership across Treasuries, U.S. high-grade credit, TBA MBS, EGBs, global rate swaps, European ETFs and U.S. institutional money market funds — a self-assessment, not third-party audited.

Genuine advances are documented: first platform to offer corporate bond portfolio trading (2019), extended to European government bonds in 2025; the first fully electronic RFM swaption package trade; a dealer algorithmic execution suite for U.S. Treasuries with J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley supplying algorithms and Tradeweb supplying distribution.

The competitive map is unusually specific and shows a different attacker per product. Credit and municipals: MarketAxess, Bloomberg, ICE (BondPoint, TMC Bonds, Creditex), Trumid, TP ICAP (Liquidnet). Rates and derivatives: Bloomberg, Euronext (MTS), CME (NEX), BGC (Fenics), MarketAxess (LiquidityEdge), GLMX. Money market portals: BNY Mellon, State Street, J.P. Morgan Morgan Money, Goldman Sachs — several of whom are also Tradeweb liquidity providers. The 10-K identifies the most credible structural threat itself: "Bloomberg and ICE have trading platforms that compete with ours and also have data and analytics relationships with the vast majority of institutional, wholesale and retail market participants." A competitor bundling execution with the terminal or data feed does not need to win on execution economics.

Two structural dependencies cut against the moat narrative. First, dealer clients are simultaneously suppliers, customers and competitors: the 10-K states certain dealers account for a significant portion of total volume, that they are non-exclusive with termination rights, that reliance "may give that client a degree of leverage against us," and that several operate or invest in competing platforms. No concentration percentages are disclosed. Second, the 10-K warns that industry consolidation "may lead to increased revenue concentration among our dealer clients... and reduce our ability to negotiate pricing." Client-sector and geographic mix are genuinely diversifying (ICD added Corporates in 2024; 42% international); counterparty concentration is described as flat-to-worsening. These are different things and should not be conflated.

Trajectory matters more than level: flat electronic credit share, −110bps total high-grade share, and a 20.4% credit fee decline point to a competitive position that is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the revenue-capture level even as volumes grow. Regulatory relief helps at the margin — the SEC withdrew proposed Regulation ATS and SCI amendments in June 2025 that would have swept government-securities ATSs and RFQ-style systems into those regimes, and the CFTC did not advance several SEF governance proposals. Score: 5.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Fee-per-million compression as a structural feature (highest-impact).** The 10-K pairs client price sensitivity with a largely fixed cost base: "if use of our platform and demand for our solutions decline for any reason or if we are forced to reduce fees, we may not be able to adjust our cost structure to counteract the associated decline in revenues." With adjusted expenses guided above revenue growth, this warning is currently operative.

**Rates concentration and Treasury clearing.** The asset class most levered to central-bank policy contains the lowest fee-per-million instruments, and central clearing is explicitly two-sided per the 10-K. The risk is that volume growth again fails to convert at the take rate.

**ICD rate sensitivity.** ICD earns basis-point commissions on average daily money-market fund balances. The 10-K: "If the federal funds rates fall to near-zero levels, the amount of cash held by our corporate clients that is available for investment through the ICD Portal may significantly decline." This is a direct short-rate option written against the Corporates segment.

**Digital-asset earnings and operational risk.** Canton Coin holdings of $230.0M (cost $16.8M) with ~$181.6M locked; validator revenue is itself Canton-Coin-price-sensitive, so token weakness hits revenue as well as non-operating income; Tradeweb self-custodies wallets and is "directly responsible for maintaining the security and integrity of the private keys." The exchange of ~161M coins for 8.1M pre-funded warrants of Canton Strategic Holdings (formerly Tharimmune; CNTN) booked $24.9M and $14.8M of realized gains, then $16.8M of H1 unrealized losses — converting illiquid token exposure into nano-cap equity exposure to the same token. Amounts are immaterial to a $23B EV; the signal is about risk appetite and disclosure complexity.

**Goodwill.** $3.15B goodwill plus ~$0.3B indefinite-lived intangibles against a stock down 20–25% from 2025 levels, with market-cap decline named as a trigger. Cash flow of $1.06B makes impairment unlikely near term but the test is live.

**Governance.** LSEG holds ~90% of votes and ~51% of economics; controlled-company exemptions apply; the charter waives any obligation of LSEG or non-employee directors to refrain from competing lines of business or from taking acquisition opportunities Tradeweb might want. No control premium is available to Class A holders, and ~120M exchangeable shares sit above the float. The iAltA Capital 50/50 joint venture, whose other half is held by an entity affiliated with a Tradeweb board member who is also CEO of both iAltA entities, is a related-party item warranting independent scrutiny (carrying value $3.6M — small, but structurally noteworthy). A further $40.2M of "other equity investments" over which Tradeweb exerts significant influence is disclosed without counterparty itemization.

**Unresolved disclosure items.** The May 20, 2026 8-K carrying Item 3.03 (Material Modification to Rights of Security Holders) alongside Items 5.03 and 5.07 was not retrievable. The most probable reading, given the 10-Q's statement that class rights and privileges did not change in H1 2026 and given co-filing with annual-meeting results, is a non-economic charter restatement. A substantive class-right change cannot be excluded. Similarly, Item 1.01 material agreements filed October 30, 2025 and January 6, 2026 were not read; attribution of the October filing to the LSEG data amendment is timing inference only. We carry these as verification gaps, not as facts in either direction.

**Consensus characterization gap.** Secondary coverage of the Q2 reaction conflicts — one outlet reported results "met Wall Street's revenue expectations and exceeded non-GAAP profit estimates," another headlined a "revenue miss." Only the filed figures ($558.9M revenue, $0.85 GAAP diluted EPS, $0.97 adjusted diluted EPS) and the direction of the move (−10% to −12%) are established. Phase 1 earnings-surprise data does record Q2 adjusted EPS of $0.97 versus a $0.98 estimate (−0.8%), a marginal miss.

Put/call skew is call-heavy at −12.6%, the put/call open-interest ratio has fallen from 0.38 to 0.15 since early June, and put/call volume is 0.75. None of this contradicts our HOLD; the absence of protective demand is a mild argument against the bear case rather than support for the bull case.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $138 (+30.8%)

July revenue tracking up low teens proves Q2 was a comparison artifact. FY2026 revenue lands near $2.35–2.40B against adjusted expenses at $1.13–1.16B, restoring positive operating leverage and delivering adjusted EPS near $4.05–4.15, with FY2027 reaching $4.59 or better. Fee-per-million compression proves to be mix (low-fee swaps and all-to-all odd lots growing fastest) rather than repricing, which the Q3 disclosure supports by showing stable per-product rates. Credit electronic share resumes advancing as portfolio trading and Rematch scale. Treasury central clearing in December 2026 accelerates electronification of the largest asset class. Market data compounds at 20%+ off the amended LSEG agreement toward the guided ~$105M and beyond, with FTSE benchmark embedding adding durability. The multiple recovers toward 30x on $4.59, or $138. The remaining $334.3M of buyback authorization, executed below current prices, supports the path.

### Bear Case — $85 (−19.4%)

The take-rate decline is price, not mix, and continues at high-single to low-double digits annually. Volume growth of 20%+ keeps converting to single-digit revenue growth while a largely fixed cost base grows 11–14%, compressing adjusted EBIT margin from 50.6% toward the mid-40s. Credit share erodes further as Bloomberg and ICE bundle execution with data and terminals. Rates volatility normalizes lower, removing the volume tailwind that has masked the fee problem. Treasury central clearing adds cost and reduces liquidity rather than accelerating electronification. Consensus for FY2027 falls from $4.59 toward $4.20, and the multiple compresses to 20x, giving $85 — near the 52-week low of $91.42 and consistent with the low analyst target of $98 being breached.

### Base Case — $118 (+11.9%)

Revenue grows 12–14% in FY2026 to roughly $2.32–2.36B; adjusted expenses land in the top half of guidance at ~$1.14B; adjusted EBIT margin holds roughly flat near 50%; adjusted EPS reaches approximately $4.05–4.10, consistent with consensus $4.06. Fee compression continues at a moderated pace, partially mix-driven. Credit share stabilizes without materially advancing. The market pays 24.5–26x, giving $118 — close to the analyst median of $120 and well below the $123.86 mean.

Probability-weighted at roughly 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear: 0.25(+30.8%) + 0.50(+11.9%) + 0.25(−19.4%) ≈ **+8.8%** expected return over twelve months, before a 0.53% dividend. That is a positive but unremarkable expected return with roughly 1.6:1 upside-to-downside asymmetry — insufficient for BUY, and short of the margin of safety we require for ACCUMULATE given the unresolved fee-conversion question and the December 2026 clearing binary.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders should maintain exposure; new capital has no urgency at $105.49.

**Base case: $118** (24.5–26x FY2026 adjusted EPS of ~$4.06–4.10), +11.9% from $105.485.
**Bull case: $138** (30x FY2027 consensus $4.59), +30.8%.
**Bear case: $85** (20x a reduced ~$4.20 FY2027 estimate), −19.4%.
**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the Q3 2026 print (late October) as the decisive interim checkpoint and the December 31, 2026 Treasury cash-clearing effective date as the second.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 revenue growth of 12% or better with adjusted EBIT margin flat or up in constant currency — this resolves the negative-operating-leverage concern directly.
2. Blended fee-per-million decline moderating to single digits, or company disclosure that separates mix from price and shows per-product rates stable. This is the single most valuable piece of new information available.
3. U.S. high-grade fully electronic TRACE share advancing above 18.3% for two consecutive quarters alongside credit revenue growth of 10%+.
4. A price near $92–95 (the 52-week low area and 20–21x next-year consensus) without fundamental deterioration would restore adequate margin of safety and move us to ACCUMULATE.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Q3 revenue growth below 8% with adjusted expenses tracking the top half of guidance — that is confirmed negative leverage over two consecutive quarters.
2. Credit fee-per-million declining more than 15% year over year again, or total U.S. high-grade TRACE share falling below 25%.
3. FY2027 consensus falling below $4.35 (a further ~5% cut from $4.59), which would break the 23x forward support.
4. Any disclosure that the May 20, 2026 charter restatement materially modified Class A rights, or a substantive adverse change in the LSEG relationship.

## Conclusion

Tradeweb is a high-quality business trading at a fair price with an unresolved question at its center. The balance sheet requires no defense: no drawn debt, an undrawn $500M revolver, and free cash flow equal to 51.9% of revenue. The network and workflow position is real and quantified — AiEX at over 40% of institutional trades from 30% in 2021, 150,000 daily trades feeding Ai-Price and the FTSE benchmark partnership, portfolio trading and swaption package firsts, dealer-supplied Treasury algorithms distributed through Tradeweb.

The problem is conversion. Q4 2025 turned 23.3% ADV growth into 12.5% revenue growth; credit turned 22.9% volume growth into 4.3% revenue growth while total U.S. high-grade TRACE share fell 110bps. Q2 2026 delivered 9.0% revenue growth against FY2026 adjusted expense guidance of 11–14%, and adjusted EBIT margin fell 74bps in constant currency even as GAAP margin rose 490bps on lapping effects. Estimates are drifting lower across every horizon, and the earnings record shows two misses in four quarters. Management repurchased $240.5M of stock at averages of $104.76 and $98.35 — below today's price — while every open-market insider transaction across the 25 most recent Form 4s was a sale, with the CTO reducing holdings roughly 74%. The June sales fall under 10b5-1 plans adopted May 15, 2026 and carry little signal; the more defensible observation is the absence of offsetting open-market buying while the stock fell from ~$137 to ~$100.

At $105.485 the shares are 26.0x current-year and 23.0x next-year consensus on a correctly sized $24.7B equity base, roughly 21.6x EV/FCF. That is neither the premium the market paid in 2025 nor a margin of safety. Probability-weighted expected return is approximately +9% over twelve months with 1.6:1 asymmetry. The Q3 print in late October is the event that resolves whether July's low-teens revenue commentary restores operating leverage or whether 9% growth against 12% cost growth is the new run rate. We would rather pay $92–95 for that answer than $105. **HOLD.**
