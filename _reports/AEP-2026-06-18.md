**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

American Electric Power is a regulated electric utility holding company serving ~5.6 million customers across 11 states, with the largest electricity transmission network in the United States. The investment case rests on a demand signal visible in current operating data rather than forward projection: data-center and large-commercial load is driving Ohio Power commercial KWh volumes up 41% year-over-year, I&M commercial volumes up 71%, and the T&D segment overall up 33%. AEP's largest growth states — Ohio, Texas, Indiana, Oklahoma — overlap with the densest U.S. data-center buildout. This load is funding a $78 billion 2026-2030 capital plan and a stated 7-9% long-term EPS growth target, with 2026 guidance of $6.15-$6.45.

The stock trades at 18.7x forward earnings against a Utilities peer median of 24.7x (peers range from Xcel at 22.6x to Sempra at 31.1x). Part of that discount is earned: AEP carries higher leverage (60.8% debt-to-total-cap), heavier equity-funding needs than peers, a 10,200 MW coal stranded-asset tail, and a regulatory track record of approved ROEs (9.5-9.84%) consistently below request. But a ~22% discount to the median for a utility with confirmed superior load growth and the nation's premier transmission footprint is wider than those factors fully justify. At 18.7x with mid-to-high-single-digit EPS growth, the relative setup favors AEP versus peers paying 24-31x for the same secular demand.

The case is moderate-conviction rather than high-alpha. EPS estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative across every horizon, so the earnings-inflection signal is absent; this is rate-base compounding, not a trough recovery. The defining risk is structural: AEP runs a perpetual external-financing model, with capex far exceeding operating cash flow, debt rising $2.3B in a single quarter, and share count up from 534M to 545M year-over-year. A higher-for-longer rate path directly compresses returns on a financing-heavy model. We rate AEP a BUY with disciplined sizing, recognizing the low beta (0.52) caps relative upside.

## Company Overview

AEP operates through four reportable segments:

- **Vertically Integrated Utilities (VIU)** — generation plus transmission and distribution in regulated states (APCo, I&M, PSO, SWEPCo, KPCo). The largest earnings contributor at $462M of $874M in Q1 2026.
- **Transmission & Distribution Utilities (T&D)** — AEP Texas and Ohio Power, wires-only operations with no generation. $237M in Q1 2026.
- **AEP Transmission Holdco (AEPTHCo)** — FERC-regulated transmission build-out earning forward-looking formula rates with CWIP recovery and incentive ROEs around 10.5-10.8%. $209M in Q1 2026.
- **Generation & Marketing (G&M)** — competitive generation and trading in PJM/ERCOT/SPP/MISO. $75M in Q1 2026.

The company owns ~26,300 MW of generating capacity, of which ~10,200 MW remains coal-fired, operates approximately 252,000 circuit miles of distribution lines and 38,000 circuit miles of transmission lines. AEP was incorporated in 1906 and is headquartered in Columbus, Ohio. The competitive advantage is a regulated monopoly franchise plus the largest U.S. transmission footprint. FERC formula rates with forward-looking cost recovery insulate transmission from the regulatory lag that affects state-regulated generation and distribution.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results (vs. Q1 2025):**
- Earnings to common shareholders: $874M ($1.60 diluted EPS) vs. $800M ($1.50), up 9.3%.
- Operating (non-GAAP) earnings: $891M vs. $823M.
- Total revenues: $6.02B vs. $5.46B, up 10%.
- Q1 2026 reported EPS of $1.64 beat the $1.58 consensus by 3.5%. Over the trailing four quarters, AEP posted three beats and one miss (Q3 2025 missed by 1.4%), with an 11.2% beat in Q2 2025.

The earnings improvement was driven by transmission investment growth, favorable rate cases, and new data-center load in commercial and industrial classes, partially offset by mild weather and a $31M Pirkey Plant disallowance.

**Demand mechanics.** Commercial KWh sales are the engine. In the T&D segment, commercial volumes rose to 12,777M KWh from 9,588M (+33%); at Ohio Power, commercial volumes jumped to 7,719M from 5,489M (+41%); I&M commercial rose to 2,181M from 1,272M (+71%). Residential and weather-driven volumes declined, but C&I and data-center growth more than offset it. The causal chain is direct: AI/data-center load → higher C&I KWh sales → rate-base expansion to serve that load → regulated return on incremental capital → EPS growth.

**Capital plan and allocation.** The $77.94B 2026-2030 plan ($12.8B budgeted for 2026) allocates VIU $40.9B, T&D $22.5B, and Transmission Holdco $12.9B. Funding draws on a multi-pronged program: debt issuance, a $3.5B ATM equity program (filed Nov 2025, ~$264M issued in Q1 2026), forward equity sale agreements (~$2.1B of expected proceeds remaining), securitizations (West Virginia $2.6B, Virginia $1.4B), tax-credit monetization ($14M transferred for cash in Q1), and minority-interest sales.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt of $51.1B puts debt-to-total-cap at 60.8% (up from 60.3%), high but normal for a regulated utility and within the 67.5% credit covenant (the contractually-defined ratio was 55.5%). Long-term debt grew to $49.6B from $47.3B in one quarter to fund the capital plan; interest expense rose to $552M from $495M. Liquidity stands at ~$5.7B net available, and revolvers were upsized in April 2026 to $6.5B + $1.5B with extended maturities to secure capital-markets access for the capex ramp.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $1.52B in Q1, against an investing outflow of $3.57B (including a $965M Oregon plant acquisition and $2.83B of construction). Financing inflow was $2.12B. AEP is firmly in outspend mode: capex far exceeds operating cash flow and requires continuous external financing. FCF conversion is structurally negative during the build phase, which is normal for a utility in a capex supercycle but elevates sensitivity to financing conditions. The dividend was raised to $0.95/quarter from $0.93, supporting a 2.93% yield.

The Altman Z-Score reads as distressed for AEP, which is a false signal: the model penalizes the high-leverage, asset-heavy regulated utility model. AEP's consistent ~$1B+ quarterly net income, regulated cash flows, investment-grade credit, and covenant headroom are the relevant facts. The metrics to watch are FFO/debt and credit ratings, not the Z-Score.

I score Financial Health 6.0. The franchise generates resilient regulated cash flow, but the perpetual external-financing requirement, rising leverage, and equity dilution are genuine structural weaknesses relative to lower-leverage peers, and they cap the score.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest dimension. Three elements support it:

1. **Confirmed load growth.** The KWh-by-class data shows demand inflecting now, not as a forecast. AEP's growth states are the data-center centers of gravity.

2. **Rate-base compounding.** The capital plan (raised ~8% in May 2026) translates directly into rate base, which at allowed ROEs of ~9.5-10.8% drives the 7-9% EPS growth target. Transmission Holdco's FERC formula rates with CWIP recovery minimize lag on the highest-quality slice of the build.

3. **Large-load tariffs as a structural innovation.** AEP has built 20-year, take-or-pay tariffs covering 80-90% of contracted demand to protect ratepayers if data centers fail to materialize. Eight jurisdictions have filed; four are approved. Each approval is a discrete catalyst that de-risks the load thesis and is dated to the regulatory docket over the next several quarters.

The constraint on growth is twofold. Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative across every horizon (current-year EPS $6.36, down 0.1% over 90 days; next-year $6.86, down 0.1%), so the earnings-inflection signal that would justify aggressive scoring is absent. And regulatory ROE outcomes are running below ask, which is the structural governor on whether the 7-9% target is met at the high or low end. I score Growth Prospects 7.5: the demand durability is high and multi-confirmed across segments, but the absence of positive revision momentum and the below-ask regulatory pattern keep it short of a higher mark.

AEP trades at a ~22% discount to the peer median while delivering the most clearly confirmed load growth in the group. Part of that discount is earned: higher leverage, heavier reliance on equity funding than peers, a coal stranded-asset tail with disallowance risk, and approved ROEs below request. Dominion, the only peer below AEP's multiple, carries its own well-known restructuring history.

On an absolute basis, 18.7x forward with 7-9% EPS growth implies a PEG near 2.3 — not cheap for a utility in isolation, and consistent with the lesson that a low multiple alone is not a buy signal. The case for the valuation score rests on the relative mispricing: the market is paying 24-31x for SRE, ETR, and VST to access the same data-center demand boom that AEP is capturing with visible KWh growth and a superior transmission franchise. The discount is wider than AEP's higher leverage and below-ask ROEs fully justify. Consensus targets (low $129, median $145, high $173) sit 0.6% to 35% above the current price; the median implies ~13% upside plus the ~2.9% yield. I score Valuation 6.5: a defensible discount to peers with a confirmed-demand catalyst, tempered by an unremarkable absolute multiple and the earned portion of the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

AEP's moat is its regulated monopoly franchise across 11 states plus the largest U.S. transmission network. Transmission, earning FERC formula rates with incentive ROEs, is the differentiator versus state-regulated peers. The multi-state diversification spreads regulatory and weather risk. New transmission JVs extend the footprint: Grid Growth Ventures (with FirstEnergy, ~$600M AEP share of PJM RTEP awards) and Midcontinent Grid Solutions (with Berkshire Hathaway Energy, ~$500M AEP share of MISO awards), both with favorable FERC treatment.

The competitive trajectory is improving. The large-load tariff model is industry-leading and being watched by regulators and peers. AEP's growth states position it ahead of the demand curve. Against that, peers (Duke, NextEra, Southern, Xcel) face the same boom, and AEP's relative weaknesses are real: higher leverage and equity-funding needs versus the group, and a structural tension with PJM. Tier 2-3 outlets (DuckDuckGo News) report AEP publicly questioning continued PJM membership amid rising prices and interconnection delays (ERCOT's queue is up ~300% to 233 GW); this is a developing situation rather than a confirmed decision, but it reflects genuine cost-allocation friction. I score Competitive Position 7.5: share-of-load is structurally rising and the moat is widening through transmission scale and the tariff innovation, partially offset by the financing disadvantage and PJM friction.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Financing / capital-markets dependence.** The capital plan requires uninterrupted access to both debt and equity markets. The 10-Q explicitly flags risk if tax-credit sales and securitizations do not materialize. Higher-for-longer rates directly compress returns on the financing-heavy model, and equity dilution is a live headwind.

2. **Large-load concentration.** The data-center thesis is the central bet. The 20-year take-or-pay tariffs mitigate the downside, but a material slowdown in AI buildout would undermine the load-growth engine.

3. **Regulatory recovery / disallowance.** A long docket of pending matters: SWEPCo Pirkey ($31M recorded, more possible), West Virginia ENEC remand ($232M disallowance under appeal), AEP Texas UTM partial disallowance recommended by an ALJ, and the PSO Oklahoma case where intervenors recommend as low as $10M (vs. $299M requested) with ROE down to 8.3%. Outcomes have consistently come in below request.

4. **Coal transition.** Coal capacity remains, with retirement/conversion decisions at Welsh, Northeastern, and Pirkey carrying unrecovered balances subject to disallowance. The $674M Legacy CCR ARO could grow as groundwater monitoring data comes in.

5. **Wildfire** — newly emphasized as a standalone risk; insurance may be insufficient.

6. **PJM structural friction** — interconnection bottlenecks and cost-allocation disputes could reshape transmission economics.

I score Risk Factors 5.5. The financing dependence into a higher-for-longer rate environment and the cluster of below-ask regulatory rulings are specific, near-term, and not fully priced into the discount. This is a one-notch decline from prior framing, reflecting that the rate path has hardened while the capex ramp has accelerated.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($173 / +35%).** Data-center load continues compounding at the rates seen in Q1; the remaining four large-load tariffs are approved on favorable terms; rate cases land closer to request; the financing program executes without a downgrade; and AEP re-rates partway toward the peer median multiple. EPS grows at the high end of 7-9%, reaching ~$6.86 next year and compounding from there, with the multiple expanding toward 22x.

**Base Case (~$148 / +15%).** Load growth moderates but stays robust; EPS grows in the middle of the 7-9% range; the multiple holds near 18.7x on $6.86 next-year EPS (~$128) plus continued re-rating to ~21.5x as the demand thesis matures, supporting ~$148. The 2.9% dividend adds to total return.

**Bear Case (~$108 / -16%).** A higher-for-longer rate path raises financing costs and dilution; regulatory rulings come in at the low end of intervenor recommendations (ROEs toward 8.3%); a coal disallowance or CCR ARO expansion hits; and the multiple compresses to ~16x on flat-to-modest EPS growth. The low beta limits the downside relative to higher-risk peers, but the financing model amplifies rate sensitivity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $148** (12-18 months). Derivation: ~21.5x on $6.86 next-year EPS reflects partial re-rating toward the peer median as the data-center thesis is validated by continued KWh growth and tariff approvals; +15% from $128.27 plus a 2.9% yield.

**Bull case target: $173** (+35%) — full re-rating toward 22x on high-end EPS execution.

**Bear case target: $108** (-16%) — multiple compression to ~16x on low-end regulatory and financing outcomes.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, tied to the regulatory docket (remaining large-load tariff approvals, OK/TX rate-case resolutions), quarterly KWh data, and the rate path.

**Upside/downside from current ($128.27):** +15% to base, +35% to bull, -16% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- Commercial KWh growth at Ohio Power or I&M decelerates below ~15% YoY in two consecutive quarters, signaling the data-center load thesis is fading.
- A credit-rating downgrade or evidence the ATM/forward-equity program is forcing dilution beyond the ~2% annual share-count growth currently embedded.
- A material adverse rate-case outcome (PSO Oklahoma ROE approved near the 8.3% intervenor floor, or a large coal disallowance) that cuts the 7-9% EPS growth target.

## Conclusion

AEP offers a confirmed, multi-segment data-center load-growth engine funding a $78B rate-base plan with a 7-9% EPS target, while trading at 18.7x forward — a ~22% discount to the 24.7x peer median that exceeds what its leverage and below-ask regulatory ROEs fully justify. The specific, dated catalyst is the rolling approval of large-load take-or-pay tariffs (four of eight jurisdictions approved, four pending) plus rate-base compounding. The principal risk is the perpetual external-financing model (60.8% debt-to-cap, rising share count) into a higher-for-longer rate path, and the absence of positive estimate-revision momentum keeps this a moderate-conviction call. The low beta (0.52) caps relative upside but also limits downside. We rate AEP a **BUY** with disciplined sizing, recognizing it is a steady regulated compounder rather than a high-alpha name.
