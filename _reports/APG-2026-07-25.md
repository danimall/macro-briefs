**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

APi Group is a global provider of fire & life safety, security, elevator/escalator, and specialty contracting services, run as a disciplined acquisition roll-up under a founder platform originally sponsored by Sir Martin Franklin and Ian Ashken. The business has a differentiated model: it leads with statutorily mandated inspection work, which is recurring and sticky, and pulls through higher-margin service, monitoring, and project revenue. FY2025 revenue reached $7,911M (+12.7% YoY) with adjusted EBITDA of $1,041M (13.2% margin), and the company generated $663M of free cash flow on capex under 1.5% of revenue. Gross margin has expanded ~530bps since 2022, net income has more than quadrupled, and first-lien net leverage sits at just 1.1x–1.3x. This is a higher-quality franchise than the "Construction & Engineering" sector label implies.

The current setup is favorable on several fronts. The stock trades at $39.57, roughly 21% below its April 52-week high of $49.99, while forward earnings estimates are rising (next-year EPS revised +4.0% over 90 days to $1.98, and current-year EPS jumped +30.1% over 30 days to $1.21). The company has completed the WTech Fire Group acquisition (July 1) and is closing Onyx-Fire (~C$725M from Blackstone Tactical Opportunities), raising 2026 guidance twice. Analyst targets cluster at $49–$55, well above spot. The earnings track record is clean — four consecutive beats.

The reason this is a hold rather than a buy is a combination of an under-appreciated dilution engine and valuation that is not cheap. The Series A Founder Preferred structure transfers 20% of the year-over-year share-price appreciation, applied to a ~211.8M reference-share base, to co-chair Martin Franklin's entity annually; the January 2026 dividend alone cost 15.2M shares, four times the prior year, and 6M more common shares convert at year-end 2026. The founders and affiliated directors sold over $250M of stock in 2026. Adjusted-EBITDA add-backs for ERP implementation are large and growing ($96M in 2025). The stock trades at ~20x next-year EPS ($1.98), reasonable for the quality but a premium to the depressed peer group, so the margin of safety is thin. We rate APG a HOLD (growth_hold) with a 12-month base case of $47.

## Company Overview

APi operates 500+ locations across 20+ countries with roughly 29,000 employees, organized into two reportable segments:

- **Safety Services** (~69% of revenue, $5,456M in 2025; 16.8% segment margin): fire protection, electronic security, and elevator/escalator design, installation, inspection, service, and monitoring. This is the strategic core — higher margin and more recurring.
- **Specialty Services** (~31%, $2,460M; 10.7% segment margin): specialty contracting, fabrication & distribution, and infrastructure/utility services, concentrated in North America.

The inspection-first model is the crux of the business. Fire codes and insurance mandates require periodic inspections; APi leads with that mandated recurring work and uses it to capture downstream service, monitoring, and project revenue. Customer concentration is low (no customer exceeds 5% of revenue), and the company reports an OSHA recordable rate of 0.87 versus a 2.2 industry average, a real differentiator on large commercial and institutional bids.

APG is a Franklin platform company, assembled through a 2019 SPAC-style vehicle that acquired the operating APi Group and later Chubb Fire & Security (2022). It has completed 140 acquisitions since 2005 in a fragmented industry with few businesses of scale, the structural setup a well-capitalized acquirer wants.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year record shows consistent top-line growth and steady margin expansion:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 6,558 | 6,928 | 7,018 | 7,911 |
| Gross margin | 26.1% | 28.0% | 31.0% | 31.4% |
| Net income ($M) | 73 | 153 | 250 | 302 |
| Adj. EBITDA ($M) | 673 | 782 | 893 | 1,041 |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | ~10% | ~11% | 12.7% | 13.2% |

Margin expansion has been driven by disciplined customer/project selection, procurement scale, pricing, and completion of the post-Chubb restructuring in mid-2025. Management's long-term 13%+ adjusted EBITDA margin target has now been met.

Q1 2026 (seasonally the weakest quarter) delivered revenue of $1,982M (+15.3% YoY), net income of $57M (up from $35M), and adjusted EBITDA of $235M (+21.8%, 11.9% margin). Specialty Services grew 25.6% with favorable fixed-cost absorption lifting its margin to 6.9%. Gross margin dipped 20bps on mix — worth watching, not alarming.

The balance sheet and cash flow are the strongest part of the story. Capex is under 1.5% of revenue ($96M in 2025), producing operating cash flow of $759M and FCF of $663M (8.4% margin), a ~3.8% FCF yield on the ~$17.2B market cap. Long-term debt is ~$2.75B with first-lien net leverage of just 1.1x–1.3x against a 3.75x covenant. Debt is well-laddered (maturities in 2029), and ~$1.84B is hedged via interest-rate swaps at a weighted-average fixed ~3.52%. Interest expense fell in 2025 despite higher debt, a positive in a rate environment that is pressuring more leveraged construction and utility names. The Altman Z-Score of 2.14 ("grey zone") is a false signal here: it penalizes the goodwill-heavy, low-fixed-asset balance sheet of a services roll-up; the 1.3x net leverage and strong FCF are the relevant credit indicators.

The key balance-sheet caveat is that goodwill ($3.17B) plus intangibles ($1.58B) equal ~53% of total assets, an inevitable consequence of the roll-up and the primary impairment risk. Capital allocation is M&A-first: the $1B buyback authorization is essentially untouched ($0 in Q1 2026), and there is no common dividend, with all incremental capital going to CertaSite, WTech, and Onyx-Fire.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has three legs. First, organic momentum in the recurring inspection base, supported by increasingly stringent fire and building codes and aging U.S. infrastructure (IIJA). Second, the acquisition machine — 14 deals for $235M in 2025, plus CertaSite ($271M), WTech (~$324M), and Onyx-Fire (~C$725M) in 2026 — which management folds into a business with a proven integration track record. Third, an emerging AI/high-performance-computing data-center demand vector that management explicitly cites across both segments, consistent with the broad data-center buildout confirmed across the industrial complex (power, electrical equipment, structural steel, networking). Fire suppression and electronic security for hyperscale facilities is a credible incremental driver, though APi's exposure is a modest slice of a diversified book rather than a pure play.

Forward estimates are moving in the right direction. Next-quarter EPS was revised +3.9% over 90 days, next-year EPS +4.0%, and current-year EPS +30.1% over the last 30 days (the 90-day current-year figure is noisier at -5.9%, reflecting timing of acquisition accretion and split-adjusted mechanics). The +30% 30-day current-year revision is not a trough-earnings recovery: APi's earnings have compounded steadily, and the revision reflects incremental M&A accretion layered onto a growing base rather than a cyclical rebound. The clean beat record (four straight, 2.5%–5.4% surprises) supports confidence that current consensus is achievable-to-conservative.

Management raised 2026 guidance twice — after the WTech close (July 2) and the Onyx-Fire announcement — signaling confidence into the July 30 print.

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.57 with next-year consensus EPS of $1.98, APG trades at ~20x forward earnings. On a headline basis the trailing multiple is meaningless (GAAP EPS of -$0.69 reflects the non-cash Founder Preferred dividend mechanics and other below-the-line items), so forward and cash-based metrics are the correct anchors. The stock yields ~3.8% on FCF, reasonable but not compelling for a company reinvesting all of it into M&A. Adjusted EBITDA against roughly $19.3B enterprise value (equity + ~$2.75B long-term debt less ~$0.9B cash, plus preferred) puts EV/EBITDA near 18x.

Against peers, APi is expensive relative to the depressed E&C group but justifiably so given its recurring-revenue quality and asset-light margins. Peer P/Es span a wide range: Aecom 14.5x, Fluor 22.8x, Jacobs 36.8x, Dycom 44.3x, Sterling 75.0x, with a median of 36.8x. On that median, APi's ~20x forward looks cheap, but the peer set is heterogeneous: Aecom and Fluor are lower-quality, lower-margin project shops, while the higher multiples reflect data-center and infrastructure momentum names. APi's recurring inspection base and 13% EBITDA margins warrant a premium to Aecom/Fluor and a discount to Sterling's 75x. Fair value on that logic is a mid-20s multiple.

Applying 24x to next-year EPS of $1.98 yields ~$47.5, below the analyst low target of $49, the mean of $52.3, and high of $55. The analyst range sits meaningfully above spot, and on a rising-estimate, sub-30x-forward name, consensus targets tend to lag rather than lead. That argues the stock has room to the low-$50s if guidance is raised again.

The offsetting consideration is dilution. The Founder Preferred structure and end-2026 conversion add several million shares annually that a per-share DCF must absorb, and the ~53% intangible asset base leaves latent impairment risk if a large deal disappoints. Cheapness alone is not a sufficient reason to own this name; here the quality and rising estimates support a constructive-but-not-cheap valuation score. We assign 5.8: the multiple is defensible for the quality, but there is no clear mispricing, and the dilution mechanism is a real per-share drag the screen misses.

## Competitive Landscape

The life-safety and specialty-services markets are highly fragmented with low barriers to entry and few players of scale, the ideal structure for a roll-up. APi's competitive edge comes from scale (procurement, shared back office, leadership development), a recurring inspection-first revenue base that is stickier than typical project work, and a repeatable acquisition engine that lets it consolidate at attractive multiples relative to its own. Competition ranges from local operators to global players (Johnson Controls in fire/security, EMCOR and Comfort Systems in adjacent mechanical/electrical). Price matters on commoditized project work, but APi differentiates on scale, technical capability, and safety record.

The trajectory is improving rather than merely stable: gross margin is climbing, the higher-margin Safety Services mix is growing, and the company is extending internationally (WTech in Europe, Onyx-Fire in Canada) while adding data-center exposure. The secular tailwinds — stricter codes, mandated inspection demand, infrastructure spending — favor the recurring base. We score competitive position 7.0, consistent with the prior report's assessment and supported by continued margin gains.

## Risk Assessment

**Acquisition and integration risk is the central risk.** The equity story depends on continued accretive M&A. A misstep on a large deal (Onyx-Fire at ~C$725M) or overpaying in a competitive market could impair returns and trigger goodwill write-downs against a $3.17B goodwill base.

**ERP implementation and control history.** The multi-year global ERP migration (started 2024) is flagged prominently, including internal-control risk, and the company has a history of remediated material weaknesses in ICFR. The related "systems and business enablement" add-backs to adjusted EBITDA are large and growing ($27M in Q1 2026 alone versus $12M a year earlier), which widens the GAAP-to-adjusted gap and deserves scrutiny; these have persisted for years and are not obviously non-recurring.

**Founder-structure dilution.** The Series A Founder Preferred (held by Martin Franklin's Mariposa entity) pays an annual dividend equal to 20% of the YoY share-price increase multiplied by ~211.8M reference shares, payable in stock or cash. This transfers a fifth of appreciation on a large reference base to the founders each year, a structural per-share headwind that partly offsets the unused buyback. Mariposa also collects a $1M/quarter advisory fee.

**Other risks:** ~53% intangible assets (impairment); ~50% U.S. union workforce with multiemployer pension exposure and a pending UK defined-benefit wind-up (non-cash); over-time revenue recognition estimate reversal on fixed-price contracts amid copper/steel/tariff cost inflation; and ~1/3 of revenue in project-based construction with macro cyclicality and Q1/Q2 seasonal weakness.

Most of these are priced or manageable, but the founder-dilution mechanism and growing add-backs are the two items the market appears to under-weight. We score risk factors 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 42.2% is rich versus 29.0% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (42.2% near-term vs 36.3% at six months), pricing imminent stress around the July 30 earnings date; the market implies a ±9.7% move. The one-month put/call skew of ~12–15% shows elevated put demand into the print. This is consistent with a market hedging a binary earnings event rather than expressing a structural bearish view; the three- and six-month skews are near flat-to-slightly-negative. The prior IV reading (957%) was a corrupted thin-options artifact and is non-informative. Short interest is low at 2.9% of float (3.2 days to cover), indicating no crowded bearish positioning. Net read: elevated event risk into July 30, but no evidence of informed structural shorting.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($55):** APi compounds high-single-digit organic revenue with continued margin expansion toward the mid-teens, executes WTech and Onyx-Fire cleanly, and raises 2026 guidance again on July 30. Data-center demand accelerates the Safety and Specialty books. Next-year EPS beats the $1.98 consensus, the market pays ~26x for a recurring-revenue, asset-light, low-leverage compounder, and the stock re-rates to the analyst high of $55 (+39%).

**Bear Case ($33):** A second-half construction slowdown pressures the ~1/3 project-based book, integration friction on the large 2026 deals compresses margins, and a goodwill impairment or a control issue tied to the ERP migration surfaces. Combined with founder-preferred dilution and continued heavy insider selling, sentiment sours and the stock re-tests its 52-week low near $33.4 (-16%).

**Base Case ($47):** Steady mid-teens revenue growth (organic plus M&A), stable-to-expanding margins, and a modestly raised 2026 guide. Next-year EPS lands near $1.98–$2.05, the market applies ~24x, and the stock works toward the mid-$40s to low-$50s (+19%).

The balance tilts modestly positive, but the dilution drag and premium-to-peers valuation, against a two-sided macro backdrop, keep this a hold rather than a buy.

## Conclusion

APi Group is a high-quality business carrying a low-quality sector label: recurring, mandated inspection revenue; asset-light margins; expanding profitability; strong free cash flow; and low, well-hedged leverage. The forward setup is attractive on the surface — a ~21% drawdown from the April high, rising estimates, a clean beat record, and analyst targets clustered $9–$15 above spot. That justifies a constructive stance and improving scores across financial health, valuation, and competitive position, consistent with the recent trajectory.

What holds us back from a BUY is specific and structural, not a reaction to the price decline. The Founder Preferred dividend transfers a fifth of share-price appreciation on a large reference base to the founders each year and grows with the stock, the end-2026 conversion adds more shares, founders and affiliated directors have sold over $250M in 2026, and the adjusted-EBITDA add-backs are large and growing. The valuation, while defensible for the quality, sits at a premium to the depressed peer group and offers no clear mispricing. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the fundamentals continue to improve, but the same overhangs that capped conviction before remain in place. We maintain HOLD with a growth-hold rationale and a 12-month base-case target of $47, and we would turn more constructive on a guidance-driven Q2 beat or a pullback toward the low-$30s.
