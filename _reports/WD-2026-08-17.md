## Executive Summary

Walker & Dunlop is two businesses moving in opposite directions inside one income statement. The origination franchise is recovering with force: 1H26 debt financing volume rose 44% to $24.285B, and Capital Markets pre-tax income rose 58.1% to $79.0M on 20.3% segment revenue growth. Against that, consolidated 1H26 revenue rose only 9.2% and Q2 GAAP diluted EPS was $0.09 against $0.99 a year earlier. Two mechanisms explain the gap, and they need to be separated because they have very different durations. The first is a bounded credit event: fraudulent borrower activity plus origination-policy breaches by a departed banking team produced $193.3M of repurchased or indemnified loans and $35.752M of net 1H26 P&L charges versus $2.040M in 1H25. Management says Freddie Mac's review is complete, Fannie Mae's is nearly complete, and one further $12-16M charge in Q3 2026 finalizes the matter. The second mechanism is structural and has nothing to do with fraud: combined origination-fee plus MSR revenue per dollar originated fell from 206bp in FY2024 to 190bp in 1H25 to 170bp in 1H26, as brokered lending rose to 58% of debt financing volume (61% in Q2) from 54%, and Fannie Mae fell to 19% of volume from 28%.

That second mechanism is why we do not treat the 52% decline from the $90 high as a straightforward mispricing. Brokered volume earns an origination fee and essentially no mortgage servicing right. In 1H26, MSR additions of $100.438M failed to cover $115.232M of amortization and prepayments, so the capitalized MSR balance fell to $793.351M even as the servicing portfolio hit a record $145.8B. The weighted-average servicing fee rate slipped to 23.4bp from 24.2bp in FY2024, and the weighted-average remaining term shortened to 7.1 years from 7.7. The company is growing the size of its annuity while reducing its yield and duration. A cyclical earnings recovery therefore arrives capped well below the 2021-22 earnings base, and any valuation anchored to prior-cycle EPS of $6-8 is anchored to a mix that no longer exists.

We upgrade to REDUCE from AVOID. The upgrade is a function of price, not of improved fundamentals: at 0.81x reported book, 1.19x book adjusted for the after-tax MSR fair-value excess, and 8.7x consensus adjusted core FY2026 EPS of $4.75, a substantial amount of impairment is already discounted, and the credit event has moved from open-ended to quantified with $80M of repurchased assets sold or being prepared for sale at prices close to carrying estimates. What keeps us below HOLD is the arithmetic: our probability-weighted 12-month total return is roughly +7%, against a bear case of $30 (-27%) and a base case of $44 (+6.5%). That does not clear a 10-12% cost of equity, and it is exposed to a $2.72 dividend running at approximately 250% of GAAP earnings while unrestricted cash has fallen 46% in six months.

## Company Overview

Walker & Dunlop operates a commercial real estate finance platform through three reportable segments: Capital Markets, Servicing & Asset Management, and Corporate. The economic core is a government-agency multifamily lending franchise built on licenses that are granted rather than purchased. WD is one of 25 approved Fannie Mae DUS lenders and one of 23 approved Freddie Mac Optigo lenders, plus an approved HUD MAP/LEAN lender and Ginnie Mae issuer. It is the sixth-largest CRE loan servicer in the United States, with a $145.8B servicing portfolio, $18.675B of AUM, $3.1B of escrow deposits, and 1,466 employees.

The risk profile differs sharply by channel, which is central to the current situation. Under Fannie Mae DUS, WD sells the loan to Fannie Mae, retains servicing, and bears first-loss credit risk: 100% of losses on the first 5% of UPB, sharing above that, capped at 20% of original UPB, with the cap subject to doubling or tripling if underwriting criteria were not met or if the loan defaults within twelve months of sale. WD is the guarantor; Fannie Mae is the beneficiary. WD receives a higher servicing fee in exchange. Freddie Mac underwrites its own loans and WD rarely shares risk. HUD reimburses approximately 99% of principal losses, though WD must advance principal, interest and escrow shortfalls. In debt brokerage, a third-party lender funds the loan; WD earns a fee, takes no credit risk, and receives little or no MSR.

Adjacent businesses include investment sales brokerage, Apprise (appraisal technology), Zelman & Associates (housing research and investment banking), WDIP (a registered adviser with $2.7B of AUM), and Walker & Dunlop Affordable Equity, which ranks ninth among LIHTC syndicators. The legacy Interim Program JV with a Blackstone Mortgage Trust affiliate has originated nothing since 2022 and is winding down; the on-balance-sheet interim loan program held zero loans at 12/31/25.

## Financial Analysis

**The five-year earnings record is the starting point.** FY2025 revenue of $1,234.3M was within 2% of the 2021-22 peak, yet net income of $56.25M was 79% below 2021 and diluted EPS of $1.64 was 80% below the $8.15 of 2021. Revenue recovered; earnings did not.

Four causes, in rough order of magnitude:

**Revenue per unit of volume.** Origination fee rate fell from 92bp (FY2024) to 83bp (FY2025) to 75bp (1H26). Agency MSR rate fell from 114bp to 96bp to 95bp. Combined: 206bp → 179bp → 170bp. On 1H26's $24.285B of debt financing volume, the 20bp year-over-year decline in combined take rate (190bp in 1H25 to 170bp in 1H26) is worth roughly $49M of gross revenue relative to 1H25 rates, larger than the entire 1H26 credit charge. This is the most consequential number in the file and it is not a fraud artifact.

**MSR erosion.** 1H26 MSR roll-forward: $808.145M opening, $100.438M additions, $(107.343)M amortization, $(7.889)M prepayments and write-offs, $793.351M closing. Additions covered 87% of runoff. Year over year, Fannie servicing UPB rose 6% and Freddie 15%, but brokered servicing fell 16% from $16.865B to $14.234B, and brokered UPB carries materially less economic value per dollar. The offsetting positive is real: MSRs are carried at amortized cost of $793.4M against disclosed fair value of approximately $1.4B, roughly $607M of unrecognized pre-tax value, or about $13.50 per share after tax at a 26% rate.

**Credit charges.** 1H26 net P&L impact of $35.752M versus $2.040M in 1H25, comprising initial repurchase costs $0.797M, operating costs $7.534M, expected principal losses $8.614M, loan-loss provision $13.058M, risk-sharing provision $5.752M, and OREO impairment $1.538M, less $1.541M of other interest income. After tax at 26% on 33.343M shares, that is approximately $0.79 per share. One definitional point matters: the allowance on repurchased and indemnified loans rose $34.225M in six months, but only $13.058M of that was a P&L provision; $21.167M was a purchased-credit-deteriorated gross-up that increases both loan balance and allowance without touching earnings. Treating the balance change as an expense would overstate the damage by more than 60%.

**Cost base.** Personnel expense rose 11.5% in 1H26 to $315.738M against 9.2% revenue growth. Headcount grew 4.8% in 2025. Total Q2 expenses rose 11.6% while Q2 revenue fell 3.9%.

**Segment attribution makes the diagnosis unusually clean.** 1H26 versus 1H25: Capital Markets revenue $331.2M (+20.3%) with pre-tax income $79.0M (+58.1%); SAM revenue $271.9M (-0.3%) with pre-tax income $41.7M (-47.6%); Corporate pre-tax loss widened 18.8% to $(92.7)M. SAM pre-tax income fell $38.0M against $35.8M of credit charges. Essentially the entire consolidated decline sits in SAM and is credit-driven, with a widening corporate loss on top. That supports management's framing that the core business performed well. The uncomfortable corollary is that SAM is where the recurring, capitalized earnings are supposed to live, and SAM revenue was flat while its unit economics deteriorated.

**Cash flow requires an adjustment before it means anything.** Reported operating cash flow was +$25.572M in 1H26 versus $(519.560)M in 1H25, an apparent $545M swing. Net loans-held-for-sale flows were +$52.611M in 1H26 (originations of $(8,380.007)M against transfer proceeds of $8,432.618M) and $(567.620)M in 1H25. These are warehouse-funded positions that reverse within roughly 60 days. Excluding them, operating cash flow was approximately $(27.0)M in 1H26 versus +$48.1M in 1H25, a deterioration of about $75M, the opposite of the headline. This adjustment is crude: it does not isolate a $57.5M receivables build or a $62.2M decline in other liabilities, both of which can reverse, and one half-year is not a run rate. We deliberately do not annualize it. It is presented as a sensitivity establishing that the reported figure is uninformative. The same accounting explains why screening tools produce an Altman Z-Score near 1.0 for WD; that is a mortgage-banking artifact, not a solvency signal.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/26: total assets $4,893.6M, total liabilities $3,172.9M, WD stockholders' equity $1,707.042M (down from $1,735.034M). Corporate debt carrying value $820.948M: a $450M term loan ($444.4M drawn), $400M senior notes due 2033, and an undrawn $50M revolver. The fixed-rate notes are swapped to floating SOFR under a fair value hedge, so all corporate debt is effectively floating. Net corporate debt is $660.1M against cash of $160.858M, which fell 46% from $299.315M at 12/31/25. Identified uses: dividends $47.155M, buybacks and withholding $19.398M, contingent consideration $8.646M, investing outflows $44.977M, net warehouse repayments $42.432M. Cash paid for interest rose 83% to $64.201M. Capex was $2.487M, genuinely asset-light.

Warehouse capacity is $6.05B, of which only $1.6B is committed; $2.95B is uncommitted and a further $1.5B is an uncommitted Fannie Mae repo. Roughly 73% of stated capacity is uncommitted, which is conventional for a mortgage banker but is a genuine funding fragility in a stress scenario. Outstanding was $1,384.7M at SOFR+120 to 145bp. A new $750M uncommitted Facility #6 closed 5/29/26; Facility #3 matured August 13, 2026, one week after the 10-Q filing.

Agency capital cushions are comfortable: Fannie Mae required net worth of $357.4M against $941.0M actual at Walker & Dunlop, LLC (2.6x), and operational liquidity of $71.0M against $128.5M (1.8x). An additional $89.3M of restricted liquidity must be funded over 48 months.

**Dividend coverage is the clearest capital-allocation tension.** In FY2025, retained earnings fell $35.555M against $56.247M of net income, implying roughly $91.8M of distributions and other retained-earnings charges, about 163% of GAAP net income. In 1H26, dividends declared of $47.154M against net income of $18.877M is a payout near 250%. The board nonetheless raised the quarterly rate to $0.68 from $0.67 for Q1 2026 and declared $0.68 again on 8/5/26. On management's adjusted core basis, Q2 EPS of $1.19 against $0.68 is a 57% payout. The judgment is whether adjusted core is the right coverage test when it excludes credit provisions that have now recurred for three consecutive quarters and are guided to recur in Q3. Separately, the $75M buyback authorized 2/26/26 was used for $13.3M in Q1 (283K shares at a weighted-average $47.13) and nothing in Q2, despite the shares trading at $44-51, leaving $61.7M unused. Liquidity preservation is the most plausible explanation given the $138.5M cash decline; blackout timing around the credit review is equally consistent with the facts.

## Growth Analysis

The volume backdrop is the strongest part of the case and it is externally corroborated. Approximately 17% of outstanding commercial mortgage balances, roughly $875B, mature during 2026. FHFA raised GSE multifamily caps to $88.0B each for 2026, $176B combined, from $73.0B each ($146B) in 2025, a 20.5% increase. The GSEs deployed $62.5B in 1H26, leaving roughly $114B of capacity for the second half. Multifamily fundamentals are inflecting: 187,000+ units absorbed in Q2 against roughly 77,700 delivered, occupancy at 95.5%, and a sixth consecutive quarter of declining deliveries. CRED iQ data confirm that 2026 volume is refinancing-led at 62.8% of Fannie Mae YTD volume.

Two qualifications matter. Effective asking rents rose 1.4% in the quarter but remained 0.2% below year-ago levels, so borrower NOI growth, the input to loan sizing, has not yet returned. And a Berkadia survey reported 61% of investors holding a negative multifamily outlook for 2026, a single secondary datapoint but a reminder that the supply-absorption improvement is not universally read as bullish.

The conversion problem is the binding constraint. 1H26 volume grew 44% and produced 9.2% revenue growth. Q2 transaction volume grew 3% and revenue fell 3.9%. Within Q2, HUD originations rose 43% and brokered rose 17% while property sales fell 18%. Until the mix stabilizes, WD's growth translates into earnings at roughly 83% of the FY2024 rate per dollar (170bp versus 206bp).

The strategic-execution record argues for discount rather than extrapolation. The company published its own "Drive to '25" scorecard in the FY2025 10-K: revenue $1,234.3M against a $2,000M target (62% attainment), debt financing $41.48B against $60B (69%), property sales $13.35B against $25B (53%), servicing $143.98B against $160B (90%), and small-balance lending $870.2M against $5,000M (17%). AUM of $18.63B exceeded a $10B target that had already been surpassed at $16.44B in 2021. Publishing the miss is creditable governance; the miss itself, particularly the 17% attainment on the technology-enabled small-balance engine, is evidence against paying for management's next multi-year growth plan.

Consensus adjusted core estimates are $4.75 for FY2026 (revised +3.3% over 30 days, on two analysts) and $5.61 for FY2027 (revised -3.6%, on four analysts). Current-quarter estimates were cut 12.9% to $1.22. This is drift in both directions on very thin coverage, not the greater than 30% upward revision velocity that would signal a systematic underestimate.

## Valuation Assessment

Book value per share is $1,707.042M ÷ 33.269M shares = $51.31, so $41.32 is **0.81x reported book**. Removing $868.710M of goodwill and $134.369M of other intangibles leaves tangible book of $703.963M, or $21.16 per share, **1.95x tangible book**. Adding the after-tax MSR fair-value excess (($1,400M − $793.351M) × 0.74 = $449M, or $13.50 per share) gives an economic book of $34.66, so the stock trades at **1.19x** that measure. Framed differently, the market is paying roughly $6.66 per share above tangible-book-plus-MSR-mark against $30.15 per share of goodwill and intangibles on the balance sheet, ascribing value to roughly 22% of the acquired intangible base. Goodwill is $868.7M net of $95.0M of accumulated impairment (all in Capital Markets) and equals 51% of stockholders' equity.

On earnings, the picture depends entirely on which earnings. Trailing four-quarter GAAP EPS is $1.12 ($0.98 + $(0.41) + $0.46 + $0.09), so the stock is roughly 37x TTM GAAP, consistent with the 38.2x P/E carried in the market data, though the same data set separately reports EPS of $1.64, the FY2025 figure, which implies 25.2x. On consensus adjusted core, $41.32 is 8.7x FY2026 ($4.75) and 7.4x FY2027 ($5.61).

Our own normalization sits between these. FY2025 GAAP EPS of $1.64 included roughly $64.8M pre-tax of credit charges, about $1.42 per share after tax; adding that back gives roughly $3.06, before considering $26.1M of LIHTC impairments that may or may not recur. 1H26 GAAP EPS of $0.55 plus the $0.79 credit drag gives roughly $1.34 for the half. A defensible normalized GAAP EPS range for a clean year at current mix is **$3.00-3.50**, putting the stock at **12-14x normalized GAAP**. That is the honest multiple, and it is not distressed-cheap.

The peer table requires care. NMIH (8.8x), ESNT (9.6x) and ACT (10.3x) are mortgage insurers, capital-heavy underwriters earning low-to-mid-teens ROE, with 2.0% median yields. WD is a fee-and-servicing business earning 3.25% ROE in FY2025 and 2% in 1H26. Saying WD trades near the peer median on adjusted core EPS conflates a non-GAAP metric with peers' GAAP earnings and different economics. On GAAP, WD is at a large premium to these peers, and its 6.58% dividend yield (at $2.72 annualized) is more than triple theirs, a yield that reflects payout risk as much as generosity.

The 0.81x price-to-book is internally consistent with a market expectation of steady-state ROE around 8-10% against a 10-12% cost of equity. That is a substantial recovery from 3% but nowhere near the mid-teens ROE of 2021. We think that is approximately the right expectation, which is why we score valuation 5.5 rather than 7+: cheapness is present but it is explained by identifiable impairments (mix compression, uncovered dividend, 51%-of-equity goodwill) rather than mispriced.

The published analyst targets, low $57, high $70, mean $62, median $59, sit 38% to 69% above spot on four analysts with no recorded revisions since the Q2 print. We treat these as stale rather than as a floor. The logic that analyst targets lag on inflection names applies when estimates are rising into a cheap multiple; here next-year estimates were cut 3.6% and the current quarter 12.9%.

## Competitive Landscape

WD leads the largest agency channel. Per CRED iQ data covering 1,071 Fannie Mae multifamily loans totaling $16.5B from January through mid-May 2026, WD originated $2.18B across 110 loans, 13.2% of that volume, ahead of CBRE Multifamily Capital ($1.88B), PGIM ($1.56B), Newmark ($1.39B), JLL ($1.22B) and Berkadia ($1.22B). The top ten captured roughly 78% of volume. Management separately cited combined Fannie plus Freddie share up 350bp to nearly 15% YTD; that is a different scope from the CRED iQ figure and the two should not be blended.

The share evidence is genuinely good. The FY2025 10-K discloses that 72% of refinancing volume came from loans new to WD (72% in 2024, 69% in 2023), direct evidence of winning contested refinancings from incumbent servicers. Against that, new customers fell to 19% of total transaction volume from 20% and 22% in the two prior years, suggesting the client-acquisition funnel is narrowing slightly. These measure different things and neither establishes revenue diversification.

The moat is narrower than the share data implies, for three reasons. First, the DUS and Optigo approvals are the moat, and they are revocable; the 10-K states plainly that the agencies may terminate or withdraw approval, with material adverse impact. A fraud event involving WD-originated loans and departed employees engages that risk directly, even if the probability remains low. Second, the growth is coming from the channel with no moat: brokerage competes on relationships and price against Wells Fargo, CBRE, JLL, Eastdil, Northmarq, Newmark and Berkadia, several of which the 10-K concedes enjoy greater name recognition, financial resources and lower-cost capital. Third, in LIHTC, WD ranks ninth against Boston Financial, Raymond James, Enterprise, Richman, National Equity Fund and PNC, and that platform produced $26.1M of FY2025 impairments plus a decision to sell asset-management contracts below carrying value.

Trajectory: share improving, unit economics deteriorating. That combination scores 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit and repurchase exposure — the dominant near-term risk.** WD repurchased, agreed to repurchase, or indemnified the GSEs on $221.6M of loans over 2024-25, recognizing $33.5M of loan-related losses plus $31.3M of additional costs in FY2025. An internal investigation into two portfolios with $100.0M of aggregate UPB found fraudulent borrower activity including undisclosed flip transactions, and that certain employees had not adhered to origination policies; those employees have departed. They originated $194.6M of the $221.6M repurchased, 87.8%. At 6/30/26, $193.3M remained repurchased or indemnified against $54.3M of valuation adjustments, with a $50.6M collateral escrow funded with the GSEs and a financing fee payable on the uncollateralized portion. Post-quarter, that fell to $153.8M UPB with $41.7M of adjustments, and a $34.8M loan was disposed at proceeds approximating its $22.5M carrying value.

The bounding evidence is real but management-asserted. The Q2 charge arose precisely because loans WD had already indemnified in Q4 2025 subsequently defaulted, forcing a $11.8M increase in loss estimates after updated property inspections. Loss estimates on this portfolio have proven unstable, and residual valuation adjustments of $41.7M on $153.8M UPB (27%) leave room for further marks. Separately, Fannie Mae raised underwriting concerns on two already-defaulted loans totaling $15.9M and WD elected to increase loss sharing rather than repurchase, adding $5.8M of provision, a precedent worth watching.

**Broader credit deterioration continues.** Defaulted loans rose from $41.7M (12/31/24) to $108.5M to $158.8M to $198.6M (6/30/26); loan count from 8 to 16; defaults as a share of the at-risk portfolio from 0.07% to 0.28%; the risk-sharing allowance from $33.191M to $49.081M. The at-risk Fannie portfolio was $70.5B with maximum quantifiable exposure of $14.433B. Mitigants: management cited weighted-average DSCR of 2.0x and underwritten LTV of 61%, though underwritten LTV reflects origination-date values. A technical tailwind to watch: the at-risk portfolio's weighted-average remaining life shortened to 4.8 years from 5.1, which mechanically reduces the CECL requirement for reasons unrelated to credit quality.

**Dividend risk.** $2.72 annualized against $1.12 TTM GAAP EPS, unrestricted cash down 46% to $160.9M, buyback halted with $61.7M unused, a term loan containing direct dividend restrictions, and $89.3M of restricted liquidity to fund over 48 months. A cut is not imminent, since adjusted core covers it at 57% and the revolver is undrawn, but the margin is thinner than a 6.6% yield implies.

**Rate exposure is closer to neutral than commonly assumed, with unfavorable composition.** Disclosed sensitivities: +100bp EFFR adds $30.9M of placement-fee revenue on $3.1B of escrows; +100bp SOFR costs $14.0M of net warehouse interest income and $8.4M of pre-tax income on corporate debt (all effectively floating post-swap). A parallel +100bp shift is roughly +$8.5M pre-tax; -100bp roughly -$8.5M, partially offset by higher origination volume. The composition matters because placement fees are near-zero-cost income and have been falling: $152.6M in FY2025 (-9%) and $65.144M in 1H26 (-5.9%) as EFFR fell from 433bp to 363bp. Fed cuts would erode the highest-margin line while helping volume; the current stagflationary hold, with CPI at +4.2% year over year, protects it.

**Governance.** CEO summary-table compensation rose 105% to $15.50M in FY2025, the year net income fell 48% and EPS fell 49%, against five-year indexed TSR of 74.68 versus a peer group at 149.81, roughly 75 points of underperformance. ISS recommended against say-on-pay over a one-time Value Creation Award (maximum $50M, equity-only, requiring both 100bp of annualized relative TSR outperformance and a 12% absolute TSR CAGR); Glass Lewis recommended for. Counter-evidence within the same program: the 2025 cash incentive funded at 44% of target, the 2023-25 performance units paid zero, "compensation actually paid" fell to $4.25M, and roughly 91% of CEO target pay is variable. The award is deeply out of the money at $41.32; maximum funding requires the stock to more than double from 12/31/25, so probability-weighted dilution is well under 1% of the 33.269M shares outstanding. One item to monitor: Q4 2025 profit interests entitling certain non-executive Capital Markets employees to 15% of one wholly owned subsidiary's pre-tax income ($0.909M of temporary equity; $0.903M attributed in 1H26). Immaterial today, but if that subsidiary houses the fast-growing brokerage business, common shareholders permanently forgo 15% of the segment currently driving growth.

**Resolved risk.** The Corporation for Better Housing litigation filed 12/15/25 was dismissed with prejudice on June 29, 2026.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 61.8% against 44.3% one-year historical volatility, a 17.5-point premium at the 71st percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in pronounced backwardation (61.8% at three days, 43.0% at one month, 37.9% at six months). The market is pricing near-term stress and then normalization. The nearest expiry implies a ±9.3% move and the one-month tenor ±10.5%, consistent with the guided Q3 credit charge. Put/call open interest of 5.43 is heavy protection in size; the reported skew of -9.6% is characterized as call-heavy by the data source, so we do not draw a directional conclusion from the two together; the reliable read is that near-term uncertainty is expensive and longer-dated vol is not. Short interest of 4.2% of float and 4.7 days to cover means this is not a crowded bear position, so there is no squeeze cushion supporting the stock, and no confirmation that sophisticated bears have concentrated here either.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $56 (+35% from $41.32)

The credit event is genuinely bounded and nearly complete. Freddie Mac's review is finished with no further repurchase requests expected; Fannie Mae's is nearly complete and finalizes with the guided Q3 charge and no additional loan repurchases. Management states 95% of losses recognized to date relate to identified fraudulent sponsors and loans originated by the departed team. Asset disposition is validating the marks: $40M sold since quarter-end at prices close to estimates, another $41M being prepared, with all repurchased assets expected sold by early 2027. Once the charges stop, GAAP earnings converge toward adjusted core, and the market re-rates a business earning $5.00-5.60 of adjusted core EPS with 13-15% agency share.

The volume setup is the best since 2021. $875B of 2026 maturities, FHFA caps up to $176B combined with $114B of capacity remaining for the second half, occupancy at 95.5% after six quarters of declining deliveries, and 52% of WD's own servicing portfolio maturing within five years form a captive refinancing pipeline. Capital Markets pre-tax income up 58% in 1H26 demonstrates the operating leverage is intact. If agency volume surges in 2H, the brokered mix mechanically reverses and take rates recover toward 190bp.

Hidden asset value supports the downside: $607M of unrecognized pre-tax MSR fair value ($13.50 per share after tax), a 2.6x Fannie net worth cushion, and negligible capex. Insiders bought: four open-market purchases totaling roughly $790K in a 17-day window after the FY2025 disclosure, including $474,632 by the CEO, with no open-market sales among the 30 most recent Form 4 transactions. At 15x $3.75 of recovered GAAP EPS, or at analysts' low target of $57, the stock returns 36-38% plus a 6.6% dividend.

### Bear Case — $30 (-27% from $41.32)

The mix shift is structural, not cyclical. Brokered share rose from 54% to 61% over four quarters while Fannie fell from 28% to 19% of volume, and management itself notes brokered volume could keep rising as non-multifamily loans mature. Every point of brokered share converts a capitalizable, annuity-like MSR into a one-time fee. MSR additions already cover only 87% of runoff; the servicing fee rate and remaining term are both declining. Volume growth of 44% delivering revenue growth of 9.2% is the steady state, not an aberration, which means normalized EPS is $3.00-3.25 rather than $5.00, and at 10x that is $30-33.

Credit is not contained. Defaults rose from $158.8M to $198.6M and the risk-sharing allowance from $37.5M to $49.1M in six months, and the Q2 charge came from re-marking loans WD had already indemnified. Residual repurchased UPB of $153.8M carries only 27% valuation adjustments. If commercial property values or the 2.0x-DSCR assumption weaken, the at-risk book of $70.5B with $14.4B of maximum quantifiable exposure produces charges that dwarf the fraud event.

The dividend is the pressure valve. At roughly 250% of GAAP earnings, with unrestricted cash down 46% to $160.9M, term-loan dividend restrictions, $89.3M of restricted liquidity to fund, and a buyback halted at prices above today's, a cut becomes the rational choice, and a cut removes the yield support that is holding the shares near 0.81x book, taking the stock toward the economic book value of $34.66 and potentially to 0.55-0.60x reported book. Governance provides no reassurance: pay was reset upward 105% in the worst operating year, ISS objected, and the prior five-year plan missed five of six targets.

The score trajectory on this name has declined from 6.6 to 6.0 to 5.0 across three prior reviews, and the recommendation moved BUY to HOLD to AVOID. Two things prevent a HOLD. First, the credit event has one more scheduled charge, guided at $12-16M, which after tax is roughly $0.27-0.36 per share and could put Q3 GAAP EPS near zero again; the options market is pricing that with 61.8% near-term implied volatility against 44.3% realized in steep backwardation. Second, and more durable, the take rate on originated volume has fallen from 206bp to 170bp in six quarters because the growth is coming from brokerage, which produces fees without servicing rights. That caps the earnings recovery at roughly $3.00-3.50 of normalized GAAP EPS rather than the $5.00-plus that the adjusted core metric and stale $57-70 analyst targets imply. A 6.58% dividend running at 250% of GAAP earnings, funded from cash that fell 46% in six months while the buyback was halted, is the mechanism through which a disappointment would transmit to the share price.

The franchise is real: the largest Fannie Mae originator, 13.2% share of that channel, scarce and non-purchasable licenses, $607M of unrecognized MSR value, and a refinancing wave the company is well positioned to intermediate. Owners with a multi-year horizon and tolerance for a dividend cut have a defensible reason to hold a reduced position. Prospective buyers should wait for the Q3 print to close the Fannie Mae review, or for a price in the low-to-mid $30s that prices the downside case directly.
