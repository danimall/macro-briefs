**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Scotts Miracle-Gro is the dominant North American consumer lawn and garden company with #1 market positions across multiple categories (lawn fertilizer, garden soil, consumer pest control). The company has executed a credible operational turnaround from the Hawthorne cannabis-supply debacle — gross margins recovered from 18.5% (FY2022) to 30.6% (FY2025), the business returned to profitability ($145M net income vs. -$437M two years prior), and $777M of debt has been paid down over three years. The April 2026 Hawthorne divestiture removes the final overhang.

The core tension is between a strong franchise at a reasonable valuation (~13.5x forward earnings, 4.5% dividend yield, 8% FCF yield) and persistent structural risks that justify a discount: $2.1B of debt (Z-Score 1.76, distress territory), 52% sales concentration in two retailers, $250M notes due December 2026 requiring refinancing, and negative near-term estimate revisions. The Street is unanimously constructive (zero sell ratings, mean target $74 implying 26% upside), and Q2 FY2026 just beat by 11.5%. However, the absence of insider buying, elevated short interest (10.8%), and the current macro environment for levered consumer businesses create enough uncertainty that the asymmetry is unclear.

The stock warrants holding for investors with existing positions given the identifiable deleveraging path and valuation support, but the leverage profile and concentration risks prevent a conviction buy signal absent further confirmation of the credit trajectory.

## Company Overview

Scotts Miracle-Gro operates as the leading marketer of branded consumer lawn and garden products in North America, generating approximately $3.4B in annual revenue (FY2025). Following the April 2026 Hawthorne divestiture, the company is a pure-play consumer lawn and garden business.

**Segment Breakdown (FY2025):**
- U.S. Consumer: $2,994M (88%) — Scotts® Turf Builder®, Miracle-Gro®, Ortho®, Tomcat®, plus exclusive Roundup® marketing
- Other (Canada): $254M (7%)
- Hawthorne (divested April 2026): $166M (5%)

The company employs approximately 5,200 people and maintains deep retailer integration, including ~2,100 in-store merchandising associates at Home Depot and Lowe's — its two largest customers comprising 52% of total sales.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability Recovery:**
The turnaround trajectory is quantifiable and substantial:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $3,924M | $3,551M | $3,553M | $3,413M |
| Gross Margin | 18.5% | 23.9% | — | 30.6% |
| Operating Income | -$382M | -$174M | $209M | $359M |
| Net Income | -$437M | -$380M | -$35M | $145M |
| FCF | N/A | N/A | $668M | $274M |

The gross margin recovery from 18.5% to 30.6% was driven by lower materials costs, reduced warehousing/transportation expenses, and restructuring benefits. FY2024's elevated FCF ($668M) benefited from one-time receivable sale program timing; $274M in FY2025 better represents normalized cash generation.

**Balance Sheet — The Binding Constraint:**
- Total debt: $2,049M (reduced from $2,826M peak)
- Leverage ratio: 4.10x
- Interest expense: $129M (36% of operating income)
- Cash: $36.6M
- Altman Z-Score: 1.76 (distress zone)
- Credit ratings: B+ (S&P, positive outlook), B1 stable (Moody's)

The November 2025 Seventh A&R Credit Agreement ($1.5B revolver + $500M term loan, maturity November 2030) provides breathing room but the $250M of 5.250% Senior Notes due December 2026 requires near-term refinancing in an elevated rate environment.

**Most Recent Quarter (Q2 FY2026, April 29, 2026):**
- Adjusted EPS: $4.53 vs. consensus $4.06 (11.5% beat)
- Revenue beat expectations
- Hawthorne reclassified to discontinued operations
- Full-year FY2026 guidance reaffirmed

The quarterly earnings beat pattern (3 consecutive: +2.6%, +21.6%, +11.5%) provides confidence that the operational execution is real.

## Growth Analysis

**Revenue Outlook:** The top line remains pressured. Revenue declined from $3,924M (FY2022, COVID-inflated) to $3,413M (FY2025). The core lawn and garden market is mature with low-single-digit organic growth potential. Management's $1B incremental sales target by 2030 implies approximately 5-6% CAGR from the current base — ambitious but plausible if new product innovation (live goods, innovative fertilizer formats) and e-commerce penetration gain traction.

**Estimate Revisions — Mixed Signal:**
- Full-year FY2026: $4.37 (up 2.0% over 30 days)
- Full-year FY2027: $4.74 (down 2.5% over 90 days)
- Current quarter: $2.50 (down 12.2% from $2.85 over 30 days)
- Next quarter: -$1.94 (down 9.5%)

The quarterly estimate declines likely reflect reclassification effects from Hawthorne's move to discontinued operations rather than fundamental deterioration, given that the full-year number actually rose. However, the absence of accelerating growth makes this a maintenance story dependent on margin stability and capital allocation rather than organic expansion.

**Growth Drivers:**
1. Post-Hawthorne simplification → analyst visibility improves → potential multiple re-rating
2. Continued margin normalization (normalized tax rate of ~25-27% vs. 34.5% in FY2025)
3. Capital return program (1/3 share buyback target)
4. E-commerce expansion and new product categories

**Growth Constraints:**
- Mature end market with limited organic pricing power
- Consumer spending headwinds in current macro environment
- Weather sensitivity creates inherent unpredictability

## Valuation Assessment

At $58.88, SMG trades at:
- ~13.5x FY2026E earnings ($4.37)
- ~12.4x FY2027E earnings ($4.74)
- ~8.0% FCF yield (on $274M normalized)
- ~4.5% dividend yield
- ~10.1x Adjusted EBITDA ($584M)

**Peer Comparison:**
Peer median P/E is 20.0x. SMG at 13.5x represents a 33% discount. H.B. Fuller (FUL) and Avient (AVNT) trade at 20x with lower dividend yields and weaker brand positions. Silgan (SLGN) at 14.2x is the closest comp — also a consumer-facing materials company with leverage.

**Why the Discount Exists:**
The discount is partially justified: 4.1x leverage vs. typical peer leverage of 2-3x, sub-investment-grade ratings, extreme customer concentration, and recent history of massive impairment charges. However, the discount has likely overcorrected given the confirmed turnaround, Q2 beat, and S&P positive outlook.

**Street Consensus:**
Zero sell ratings (1 strong buy, 4 buy, 3 hold). Mean target $74 (26% upside). Low target $67 (14% above current). The entire analyst community sees material upside, though consensus has been wrong before on highly levered businesses.

The valuation provides meaningful support at current levels. The stock is trading at a discount that assumes limited upside to the deleveraging trajectory, while a successful path to investment-grade credit would likely trigger a re-rating toward 18-20x forward earnings ($79-95).

## Competitive Landscape

**Competitive Strengths:**
- Clear #1 position in consumer lawn fertilizer, garden soil/plant food, consumer pest control, and rodent control
- Iconic brands with decades of consumer trust (Scotts, Miracle-Gro, Ortho, Tomcat)
- Exclusive Roundup® marketing agreement — barrier to entry
- Deep retailer integration (2,100 in-store associates) creates switching costs for HD/Lowe's
- Scale advantages in manufacturing, distribution, and R&D

**Competitive Risks:**
- Private label competition at lower price points during consumer belt-tightening
- No long-term sales agreements with retailers — relationships are leveraged but contractually insecure
- Roundup® agreement has termination triggers (program EBIT below $50M, control changes)

**Market Position Trajectory:** Stable. SMG's brands remain dominant and the lawn/garden category has limited disruption risk. The competitive position is the strongest element of the investment case — this is a genuine franchise with pricing power and consumer loyalty.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Leverage / Refinancing (HIGH):** $2.1B debt with December 2026 Senior Notes maturity ($250M). In the current rate environment, refinancing costs will be higher. Default remains unlikely given $274M FCF and the new credit facility, but covenant compliance leaves limited cushion. A material earnings miss during peak season (Q2/Q3) could compress margins on covenants.

**2. Customer Concentration (HIGH):** 52% of sales through Home Depot and Lowe's with no long-term contracts. Any shift in retailer strategy (private label emphasis, shelf-space reallocation) would be immediately material. Both retailers are facing their own consumer spending headwinds.

**3. Macro/Consumer Sensitivity (MODERATE-HIGH):** Garden spending is semi-discretionary. The company's spring selling season (Q2/Q3 FY2026) has already passed, limiting near-term revenue risk, but FY2027 faces this headwind fully.

**4. Tariff Exposure (MODERATE):** The 10-K explicitly flags China sourcing risk. Current trade policy uncertainty creates cost variability that cannot be easily passed through given retailer bargaining power.

**5. Weather/Seasonality (PERMANENT):** 76%+ of sales in Q2/Q3. A single bad spring season can impair an entire year's results.

**6. Hagedorn Family Control:** ~23% ownership with effective board control. Minority shareholders have limited governance rights.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV at 45.1% vs. 35.4% historical volatility represents a rich 9.7pp premium — the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (45.1% near vs. 41.7% far), which typically signals imminent stress rather than long-term concern. Put/call volume ratio at 1.72 confirms moderately bearish positioning. The 3-month P/C volume ratio at 46.25x is extreme — concentrated put activity at the July expiry likely reflects institutional hedging around the earnings/refinancing timeline.

Combined with 10.8% short interest (4.8M shares, 4.9 days to cover), institutional positioning is meaningfully bearish despite unanimous Street buy/hold consensus.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($74-80; 26-36% upside):**
- Continued operational execution drives FY2027 earnings toward $5.00+
- Successful December 2026 refinancing at reasonable terms
- S&P upgrades to BB- within 12 months, triggering institutional buying
- Share buyback program launches and reduces share count 5-10%
- Multiple re-rates from 13.5x toward peer-level 18-20x
- Tax rate normalization adds $0.30-0.50 EPS uplift

**Bear Case ($45-48; 20-24% downside):**
- Consumer spending weakness during FY2027 spring season compresses revenue 5-8%
- Tariff costs can't be passed through, compressing gross margins 200-300bp
- December 2026 refinancing occurs at punitive terms (B+ rated in rising rate environment)
- One retailer reduces shelf space or increases private label push
- Leverage ratio creeps back toward covenant limits, restricting capital allocation

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Successful refinancing of December 2026 notes at ≤6.5% yield with IG upgrade path confirmed
2. FY2027 estimate revisions turning positive (+5% or more over 30 days)
3. Leverage declining below 3.5x with S&P upgrade to BB-

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Quarterly revenue miss exceeding 5% of consensus during peak selling season (Q2/Q3)
2. Covenant violation or disclosure of waiver requirement
3. Loss of material shelf space at Home Depot or Lowe's (confirmable via channel checks or management disclosure)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely July-August 2026 for Q3 FY2026) will provide the critical data point on spring selling season execution. The December 2026 refinancing is the second key milestone.

## Conclusion

Scotts Miracle-Gro presents a genuine tension between a strong franchise executing a real turnaround and structural balance sheet risk that constrains the investment case. The operational metrics confirm improvement (gross margins restored, three consecutive earnings beats, $777M debt reduction), and the valuation at 13.5x forward earnings provides a margin of safety relative to peers at 20x.

The HOLD recommendation reflects specific unresolved uncertainties: (1) the December 2026 refinancing hasn't been completed and represents binary risk at B+ credit ratings in this rate environment, (2) no insider buying despite the stock trading near 52-week lows, and (3) the options market and short interest are meaningfully more bearish than Street consensus, suggesting informed participants see risk that the buy-side consensus does not.

The Street's $74 mean target is plausible on a 12-18 month horizon if the deleveraging path continues and refinancing succeeds. The stock is likely near fair value at $58-61 on a risk-adjusted basis — the premium that a BB-rated business would command on 14-15x forward earnings once credit risk diminishes. Investors with existing positions should hold and monitor the December 2026 refinancing and Q3 FY2026 results. New capital requires higher conviction on the refinancing outcome before deployment.
