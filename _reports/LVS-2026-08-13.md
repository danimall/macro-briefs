## Executive Summary

Las Vegas Sands is now a two-market Asian integrated-resort operator: 74.80% of Sands China (five Macao properties plus the Four Seasons/Plaza) and 100% of Marina Bay Sands in Singapore. FY2025 EBITDA split 44% Macao / 56% Singapore. The Las Vegas assets are gone, the New York license bid was abandoned in April 2025, and the $1.26B seller-financing note from the Las Vegas sale was repaid in May 2026.

The Q2 2026 print on July 22 was the second consecutive quarter of the same underlying story. Reported adjusted property EBITDA fell 16.1% to $1,119M on revenue of $3,154M (−0.7%), and adjusted EPS of $0.59 missed the $0.76 consensus by 21.9% after three straight beats. Roughly 60% of the EBITDA decline was rolling-chip hold: Macao VIP hold of 1.35% against a 3.3% expectation, an $87M drag that reconciles to the segment tables at ~$88M when the 40% Macao gaming levy burden is applied. Adjusting both periods for disclosed hold, EBITDA fell 6.3% ($1,169M versus $1,247M), not 16.1%. The remaining 40% is real: Macao segment margin of 24.0% versus 31.5%, driven by +$228M of volume-linked gaming taxes, +$60M payroll and +$27M casino marketing in the first half, and MBS margin of 49.9% versus 55.3% because Singapore's premium gaming tax tier stepped from 8% to 12% in June 2026 versus July 2025.

Two things have changed materially since the last review at a nearly identical share price. First, consensus has been cut hard and the cut is entirely post-print: FY2026 EPS went from $3.38 thirty days ago to $3.09 (−8.6%), FY2027 from $3.69 to $3.53 (−4.2%). Second, Macao market-wide GGR fell 8.4% year over year in July, a second consecutive monthly decline. Against that, the price already reflects a lot: at $45.445 the stock is 14.7x FY2026 and 12.9x FY2027 consensus, roughly 8x EV/EBITDA on a $5.0–5.2B run-rate, against a casino peer median P/E of 24.6x. The market is not pricing a Macao recovery. Our base case of $53 offers +17%, the bear case of $34 costs 25%, and probability-weighted expected total return including the 2.6% dividend is around +14% with low confidence. That is approximately fair rather than compelling. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

**Macao (Sands China, 74.80% owned, HKEX-listed).** Six properties: The Venetian Macao (~659 tables, 2,905 suites, ~1.2M sq ft of convention space), The Londoner Macao (~500 tables, four hotel towers including the 2,405-room Londoner Grand), The Parisian (~255 tables), The Plaza Macao & Four Seasons (~106 tables, 19 invitation-only Paiza Mansions), Sands Macao (~160 tables), plus the Cotai Water Jet ferry business. The concession runs to December 31, 2032, at which point gaming assets — casinos, gaming areas, supporting areas and gaming equipment acquired during the term — revert to the Macao government without compensation. The government may redeem the concession from January 1, 2029 with one year's notice.

**Singapore (Marina Bay Sands, 100% owned).** 1,844 rooms including 775 suites post-renovation (from 135 previously), ~568 tables, ~157,000 sq ft of gaming space, 794,000 sq ft of retail at 100% occupancy, ~1.2M sq ft of meeting space. Only two casino licenses exist and the regulator is barred from exceeding two until January 1, 2031. The casino concession runs to August 2036; the three-year operating license expires April 2028.

The retail portfolio is a genuinely differentiated asset: Q2 2026 mall revenue of $198M, with Shoppes at Four Seasons at $624 base rent and $4,650 tenant sales per square foot, and Shoppes at MBS at 100% occupancy, $401 base rent and $3,279 tenant sales per square foot.

Patrick Dumont became Chairman and CEO in the February–March 2026 window, succeeding Robert Goldstein. Dumont is the son-in-law of Miriam Adelson, whose family group holds approximately 57% of the common stock. The combined chair/CEO role removes the independent-chair separation that existed previously.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (10-K, filed February 6, 2026).** Net revenues $13.017B (+15.2%), operating income $2.818B (+17.3%), net income attributable to LVS $1.627B, consolidated adjusted property EBITDA $5.232B (+19.5%). MBS EBITDA $2.922B (+42.4%); Macao $2.310B (−0.7%). Total carrying debt $15.784B against $3.841B of cash; LVS stockholders' equity fell to $1.590B from $2.884B after $2.27B of buybacks.

**Q2 and 1H 2026 (10-Q, filed July 24, 2026).** Q2 revenue $3,154M, operating income $618M (−21.1%), net income attributable $346M (−24.9%), GAAP diluted EPS $0.53. First half: revenue $6,739M (+11.6%), operating income $1,522M (+9.3%), EBITDA $2,540M (+2.7%), diluted EPS $1.38 (+20.0%). The half is fine; the second quarter broke.

**The hold adjustment holds up, and it cuts both ways.** Applying the 3.3% Macao expectation to disclosed Q2 rolling volumes produces roughly $147M of gross revenue shortfall (Four Seasons/Plaza alone posted a *negative* 1.15% rolling win on $2,824M of volume, collapsing that property's EBITDA to $20M from $66M); at the 40% combined gaming levy that flows to ~$88M of EBITDA, essentially management's disclosed $87M. Q2 2025's disclosed adjustment was −$87M, so the honest year-over-year comparison is $1,169M versus $1,247M, a 6.3% decline.

The same discipline applied in reverse damages the FY2025 base most models anchor on. MBS FY2025 rolling win was 4.54% on $39.4B of volume against a company-stated smart-table theoretical band of 3.6–4.2%, and non-rolling win was 23.4% versus 20.1% the prior year. Normalizing both plausibly removes $350–450M of EBITDA from MBS's reported $870M increase. Management counters that the premium clientele's side-bet mix legitimately raises theoretical hold, which is why the company revised its Singapore expected-win methodology to a smart-table basis in Q3 2025. That dispute is unresolved and is the single largest uncertainty in normalizing Singapore earnings power. Anyone extrapolating a ~$2.9B annualized MBS EBITDA base is probably extrapolating several hundred million dollars of good fortune, and anyone treating the Q2 Macao result as a run-rate is making the mirror-image error.

**Cash flow: the reported improvement is a timing artifact.** First-half operating cash flow of $1,413M versus $704M looks like a $709M improvement. Both periods contain a land-premium payment classified inside operating activities: −$137M in 1H26 (Additional Gross Floor Area, March 2026) and −$848M in 1H25 (Additional Gaming Area, April 2025). Normalized, the two halves are $1,550M and $1,552M — flat, on +11.6% revenue. First-half free cash flow was $887M ($1,413M less $526M capex) against $1,542M of buybacks, $400M of LVSC dividends and $130M of minority distributions, totaling $2,072M. Cash fell $465M despite receiving the $1.26B Las Vegas note repayment. We do not annualize a single half — Q2 is management's stated seasonal trough and working-capital items swing materially — but returns currently exceed internal generation.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $3,376M, total debt $15,262M (net debt $11,886M, roughly 2.3x on a $5.1B EBITDA run-rate), LVS stockholders' equity $581M against treasury stock of $10,570M. Equity fell $1,009M in six months while $913M of income was earned, because buybacks plus dividends exceeded it. Book equity will go negative within one to two quarters at the Q2 pace. That is an arithmetic consequence of repurchasing above book value, not a solvency signal, but it does invalidate book-based screens on this name and removes any tangible-net-worth cushion in rating conversations.

The better solvency lens is the covenants: U.S. leverage 1.53x against a 4.00x maximum, Sands China 3.18x against 4.00x, Singapore 1.42x against 4.50x, with $3.38B of unrestricted cash, $4.26B of revolver availability and SGD 6.05B (~$4.68B) undrawn on the 2025 Singapore delayed-draw term loan. Sands China is the tight one, and Macao EBITDA is the denominator's driver while it declines. Holding SCL net debt constant, a further ~20% decline in SCL EBITDA would approach the ceiling. The credit agreements' definitions are not disclosed, so treat that as a mechanism rather than a forecast; it is the channel through which a prolonged Macao margin squeeze becomes a financing problem rather than an earnings problem.

**Interest cost has troughed.** May 2026's $1.0B dual-tranche issuance (5.300% 2031, 5.650% 2033) refinanced $1.0B of 3.500% 2026 notes. Weighted average rate 4.6% on a $16.06B average balance in Q2, versus 4.8% prior; the average is still falling as older paper amortizes, but new money is being priced ~180bp above what it replaces, which matters given the higher-for-longer rate backdrop.

**Credit extension is inflecting.** Provision for credit losses was $65M in 1H26 versus $21M (+209.5%), write-offs $52M versus $26M, allowance $237M versus $225M. FY2025's provision had already jumped to $85M from $19M. Credit play is 11.7% of Macao and 12.2% of Singapore table volume. Offsetting: settlements of previously reserved accounts rose ($27M at MBS, $6M in Macao), and casino receivables actually *fell* to $767M from $828M. This is not yet a credit break, but part of the documented VIP share gain appears credit-financed, and the direction is consistent across FY2025 and 1H26.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative and consensus has recognized it. Current-quarter estimates fell 6.1% in thirty days, current-year 8.6%, next-year 4.2%. Because the 30-day cut exceeds the 90-day cut for the current year, essentially the entire revision happened after the July 22 print. The estimate reset is fresh, not gradual, and there is no evidence yet that it has finished.

What consensus now embeds: FY2026 adjusted EPS of $3.09 against $1.50 delivered in the first half ($0.91 + $0.59), requiring $0.75 and $0.86 in the back half. Given that hold normalization alone would have delivered roughly $0.75 in Q2, the sequential path is not heroic, but July Macao GGR of MOP 20.26B (−8.4% year over year) is a real headwind, and management declined to quantify how much of June's World Cup drag persisted into July.

The medium-term drivers are identifiable and mostly 2028-and-later:

- **Venetian renovation.** Roughly 400 keys were out of inventory on average in Q2, fluctuating 400–500 per quarter into 2027, with all 2,900 rooms and new premium salons reintroduced by Chinese New Year 2028. This suppresses reported Macao results for six more quarters and then reverses.
- **MBS Expansion.** $8.0B total estimated cost, ~$3.0B incurred through June 2026, construction complete June 2030, opening January 2031. Real capacity addition, but no earnings contribution inside any reasonable investment horizon.
- **Share gains.** Sands China rolling volume grew 73% year over year to a market-leading 26% share, from fourth in the segment a year earlier; non-rolling drop +15%, slot handle +30%, mass GGR +8% against a market at +4%. May 2026 was an all-time monthly mass-GGR record for SCL.
- **Singapore mix.** Q2 rooms revenue +12.7%, F&B +14.3%, mall +8.1%; ADR $982 versus $888; RevPAR $939 versus $844. Non-gaming carries no gaming levy, which partly offsets the tax-tier ratchet.

Two structural brakes on growth deserve more weight than they typically get. Singapore's tax is tiered — premium play steps 8%→12% above SGD 2.4B of GGR, mass 18%→22% above SGD 3.1B — so each year MBS grows, it crosses into the higher tier earlier and the blended rate mechanically ratchets up. The incremental EBITDA margin on Singapore growth is structurally below the reported average, and the 2031 expansion accelerates the crossing date. Second, the Macao EBITDA target ladder has quietly slipped: a long-standing $700M quarterly goal, then "$600 came into the mix" last quarter, and now roughly $500M on a hold-adjusted basis — a progression a Goldman analyst put on the record on the call, to which Dumont responded "our target is still the 700 million… I think we have some work to do to get there." Hold-adjusted Q2 Macao EBITDA was $517M. The gap to target is $183M per quarter, or $732M annualized.

## Valuation Assessment

The supplied feed reports trailing and forward P/E identically at 17.72, which is internally inconsistent; we use consensus estimates directly.

| Metric | LVS at $45.445 | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026E ($3.09) | 14.7x | — |
| P/E on FY2027E ($3.53) | 12.9x | 24.6x |
| EV/EBITDA (FY26E $5.0–5.2B) | 7.9–8.3x | — |
| Dividend yield ($1.20) | 2.64% | 0.5% |

Enterprise value: $29.43B equity plus $11.89B net debt ≈ $41.3B, before adjusting the Sands China minority to market value. Peers: MGM 27.8x, WYNN 24.6x, CHDN 15.0x, BYD 3.6x (an outlier likely reflecting a one-time gain), CZR loss-making. The group is heterogeneous enough that the 24.6x median is a weak anchor, but LVS at 12.9x forward is the cheapest large-cap Asian-exposed operator on the screen while carrying the strongest asset base.

The cheapness has identifiable causes, which is why we score valuation 6.0 rather than 8.0:

1. **Roughly 44% of FY2025 EBITDA sits on an asset base with a hard December 2032 legal terminus** and a government redemption right from January 1, 2029. Concession renewal is the overwhelming historical base case, but a leasehold-like right should not be capitalized at a freehold multiple.
2. **The reported 16.1% first-half effective tax rate is conditional.** VML's shareholder dividend tax agreement expired December 31, 2025; an extension covering 2026–2027 was requested on January 19, 2026 and the 10-Q states plainly there is no assurance it will be granted. Separately, VML's gaming income tax exemption expires December 31, 2027.
3. **Estimates are still being cut,** and the cut is only three weeks old.
4. **The multiple has to absorb competing capital claims:** ~$5.0B remaining on MBS Expansion, ~$3.4B remaining on the Macao concession Investment Plan (of 35.84B patacas committed, 33.39B non-gaming, all by December 2032), ~$780M of LVSC dividends, ~$720M of cash interest, and a $6.0B buyback authorization that Dumont said "our goal is to use."

That last point is the crux. Against a ~$5.0–5.2B consolidated EBITDA run-rate, subtracting corporate (~$310M annualized), development expense (~$170M), cash interest (~$720M), cash taxes (~$420M) and maintenance capex leaves roughly $2.5–2.9B before growth capex. MBS Expansion ramping toward $1.2–1.5B annually in 2027–2029 plus ~$0.5B of annual Macao concession spend plus ~$910M of total dividends plausibly absorbs all of it. Executing the full $6.0B authorization on top mathematically requires rising consolidated leverage, a buyback pace well below the authorization, or a materially better Macao recovery than 1H26 delivered. The $4.68B Singapore delayed-draw is dedicated project financing: it funds the expansion and frees MBS operating cash for upstreaming, so the gap is smaller than a naive comparison implies, but it is not a general-purpose buyback facility. Authorizations are ceilings: June 2026 repurchases were 831,496 shares versus 10.5M in May, and average prices stepped $53.89 → $52.24 → $47.30 across the quarter.

**What the current price actually discounts.** Assume no Macao margin recovery and full hold normalization at MBS: Macao at ~$2.0–2.1B annualized, MBS at ~$2.6–2.8B, consolidated ~$4.7–4.9B. At 8.5x, that is $40–41.6B of EV less ~$11.9B of net debt, or $44–46.4 per share on ~640M shares. At $45.445 the market is paying essentially nothing for a Macao recovery, for the Venetian rooms returning by CNY 2028, or for the 26% rolling-share position converting to sustainable margin. That is the reason this is a HOLD rather than a REDUCE.

**Base case $53.** 15x FY2027 consensus $3.53 = $52.95. Cross-check: $5.3B FY2027 EBITDA at 9.0x = $47.7B EV less ~$11.9B net debt = $35.8B on ~635M shares = $56.4. We use the lower of the two given the live estimate-cut risk. Upside +16.6%.

The price sits *below* the analyst low target of $47.00, with a $59.07 mean and $60 median. We do not read this as upside confirmation. Sentiment is flagged as degrading and the ratings mix has drifted (hold count 5→6→7→6 over four months); the more likely near-term development is target cuts catching up to estimate cuts, not the reverse.

## Competitive Landscape

**Macao** is a six-concessionaire oligopoly (SJM, Wynn Macau, Galaxy, MGM China, Melco, Sands China) all operating under 10-year concessions expiring December 2032, all paying a 35% special gaming tax plus 5% mandated contributions, and all carrying non-gaming investment obligations. The battleground has moved to premium mass and VIP and is being fought with reinvestment and suite product. Dumont: "competition in that segment remains intense, and luxurious suite product coupled with outstanding service levels are critical to success." Grant Chum dated LVS's more aggressive posture to May/June 2025.

The evidence establishes that LVS is winning share and losing margin. Share gains are large and documented (26% rolling volume share, first from fourth; mass GGR +8% versus market +4%). The margin cost is equally documented (24.0% versus 31.5%; +$60M payroll and +$27M casino marketing in 1H26). The unresolved question, and the one that determines whether the base case or the bear case is right, is whether the share is sticky at a lower reinvestment ratio in 2027. Management asserts it will be; the 1H26 numbers do not prove it. A Wynn read-across (1Q26 EBITDAR missing by 17%, margins compressed across all geographies) suggests the compression is at least partly sector-wide reinvestment competition rather than LVS execution failure, which is mildly reassuring on relative position and unhelpful on absolute margins.

**Singapore** is a genuinely superior structural position: a two-license duopoly with LVS and Genting's Resorts World Sentosa, capped until January 1, 2031, against 16.9M international visitors in 2025 (+2.3%) and 70M Changi passengers (+3.4%), with a fifth terminal under construction. Two caveats. The regulator does not publish market gaming revenue, so no official market-share denominator exists; anyone claiming to track MBS share is estimating. And the license-count guarantee lapses in January 2031, the same month MBS Expansion opens.

An adjacent threat surfaced on the call worth tracking: prediction markets as a competitor for wagering dollars during the World Cup, alongside LVS's own stated reason for abandoning New York — "the impact of the potential legalization of online gaming on the New York market." That is a coherent read of digital as a genuine competitive vector, and it explains the sustained if now-trimmed digital gaming development spend ($193M in FY2025, $69M in 1H26, with a $5M impairment on discontinued activities).

Regional supply expansion (Thailand, Japan/Osaka) is frequently cited as a medium-term threat but is not named in the FY2025 risk factors and we have no sourced 2026 datapoint. Treat it as an unquantified open item rather than a dated risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Macao tax conditionality (highest priority, nearest-dated).** The shareholder dividend tax agreement expired December 31, 2025; the extension request dates to January 19, 2026 and the 10-Q states there is no assurance it will be granted, yet the reported 16.1% first-half effective rate anticipates one. VML's gaming income tax exemption separately expires December 31, 2027. Macao granted the prior arrangements to all six concessionaires, so denial is low-probability, but it would be a step-change in Macao cash taxes and reduce upstreamable cash to a holding company that depends entirely on subsidiary distributions.

**Concession horizon.** December 31, 2032 expiry with gaming assets reverting without compensation, plus a government redemption right from January 1, 2029 with one year's notice. VML is prohibited from listing and needs government approval to raise debt or equity or grant liens.

**Sands China covenant path.** 3.18x against a 4.00x ceiling while Macao EBITDA declines. Directional only, but the mechanism is real.

**Premium concentration and hold variance.** LVS's deliberate top-end share grab has increased earnings variance. Dumont: "the play is very concentrated… some of those people weren't in the building this quarter." A single quarter's luck moved reported EBITDA by $87M in one segment. Expect continued quarter-to-quarter noise in both directions.

**MBS Expansion contractual deadline.** MBS agreed with the Singapore government to complete construction by July 8, 2029; the company guides to June 2030 completion and January 2031 opening, and states any extension beyond July 8, 2029 requires Singapore government approval. Three prior extensions (March 2022, March 2023, January 2025) are genuine precedent and a routine extension is the likely outcome, but it is an open contractual exposure with default provisions attached.

**Governance concentration.** Family group at ~57% on a shrinking share count, combined chair/CEO role held by a family member, incentive metrics limited to Adjusted Property EBITDA, Liquidity and ESG with no ROIC or per-share measure while $6B of buybacks and $8B of greenfield capex are deployed simultaneously. Adjusted Property EBITDA is not hold-adjusted in the company's own definition, so a lucky quarter mechanically inflates the incentive metric in a business whose CEO describes hold as random.

**Resolved favorably.** The Asian American Entertainment claim — inflated by the plaintiff to 96.45B patacas (~$11.94B) — reached final judgment for the LVS defendants on March 4, 2026, certified March 13. That removes a nominally enormous if always low-probability contingent liability.

**Verification gaps we are not treating as facts.** The effective date and stated reason for the Goldstein-to-Dumont transition (the relevant 8-K text was not retrievable); whether the pause in Sands China share purchases reflects an HKEX minimum-float constraint at 74.80% (an unverified hypothesis); the precise magnitude of the FY2025 MBS hold overstatement; and the exact covenant definitions underlying the Sands China sensitivity.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term ATM IV of 45.9% against 35.1% one-year realized vol overstates the stress: the one-day expiry carries a 5.11 put/call volume ratio and a 14.6% skew, which is same-day pinning mechanics rather than positioning. The informative points are the one-month tenor at 31.3% IV with a benign 1.8% skew, and the three-month tenor at 36.9% with an 8.6% put skew and a 9.8% implied move — the September expiry, which spans the Q3 print and two monthly Macao GGR releases, is where downside protection is being bought. ATM IV collapsed from 80.5% to 45.9% since July 20, and the put/call open-interest ratio eased from 1.06 to 0.97, so the event premium around the Q2 print has been released without leaving a directional bearish structure behind. Short interest of 11.6% of float at 2.9 days to cover is elevated and confirms institutional skepticism, but is not crowded enough to create squeeze mechanics. The options market prices ordinary event risk around Q3 earnings, neither confirming a collapse nor pricing a recovery, consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $70 (+54%)

Q2 was the trough: management's stated seasonal low, with 400+ Venetian keys offline, the largest Macao hold adjustment in company history, and a World Cup drag concentrated in June. The underlying volume story was strong: rolling volume +73%, mass GGR +8% against a flat market, record May mass GGR, a jump to first place in rolling volume share. Chum guided operating-expense growth to be "slower" in the second half after the step-change investment "largely happened." If reinvestment normalizes while share is retained, Macao rebuilds toward the reaffirmed $700M quarterly target as the Venetian's 2,900 rooms return by Chinese New Year 2028. Singapore's suite-driven step-change is real (775 suites, ADR $982, mall at 100% occupancy). Consolidated EBITDA of $5.8B at 10x on a shrinking share count gets to roughly $75; the analyst high of $71.50 is a reasonable cap.

### Bear Case — $34 (−25%)

The margin compression is structural, not cyclical. Macao reinvestment does not normalize because competitors do not let it, and the 750bp of segment margin loss is the new equilibrium price of a 26% rolling share partly financed with house credit (provisions +209%, write-offs doubled). Singapore's tax tier ratchets earlier each year, so the reported 53–55% margin is not repeatable on rising revenue. MBS's FY2025 hold windfall of $350–450M unwinds. Consolidated normalized EBITDA settles near $4.5B; Sands China leverage approaches its covenant; the buyback slows to fund $8.4B of committed capex; and the Macao dividend tax agreement is not renewed, stepping up cash taxes into 2028 when the VML income-tax exemption also lapses. At 7.5x on $4.5B less rising net debt, the equity is worth $32–34.

### Base Case — $53 (+17%)

Hold normalizes in Q3–Q4 and Macao stabilizes near a hold-adjusted $520–570M per quarter through 2027, improving in 2028 as the Venetian completes. MBS delivers $2.6–2.8B on normalized hold with non-gaming mix partly offsetting the tax ratchet. FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $3.53, helped by 5–6% annual share-count reduction, and the stock re-rates modestly to 15x. Probability-weighted at 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear, expected price return is approximately +11.5%, or roughly +14% including the 2.64% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** The overall score steps down from 5.4 to 5.1. The trajectory (6.5 → 6.2 → 5.8 → 5.4 → 5.1) reflects genuine, identifiable fundamental deterioration rather than price action: since the July 20 review the shares are roughly flat while FY2026 consensus fell 8.6%, Macao segment margin contracted 750bp, and Macao market GGR posted a second consecutive year-over-year decline. Risk/reward is not better than it was; it is differently distributed, with more of the bad news now in the price.

**Targets:** base $53 (+16.6%), bull $70 (+54.0%), bear $34 (−25.2%). Timeframe 12 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (late October), the Q4/FY print (early February 2027), and monthly Macao DICJ GGR releases.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Hold-adjusted Macao EBITDA of $570M or better in a quarter with Venetian keys still offline, with reinvestment as a percentage of revenue flat or lower sequentially.
2. Macao market GGR returning to year-over-year growth for two consecutive months (Seaport expects August/September; that is a forecast, not data).
3. Confirmation that the VML shareholder dividend tax agreement has been granted for 2026–2027, removing the conditionality from the effective tax rate.
4. A price below $40 with FY2027 consensus holding above $3.40, which would put the stock under 12x on an intact estimate base.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following:**
1. FY2027 consensus falls below $3.20 (a further ~9% cut), which would eliminate the base-case upside at an unchanged multiple.
2. Sands China covenant leverage rises above 3.6x, or the company discloses any covenant amendment or waiver request.
3. Provision for credit losses exceeds $50M in a single quarter, or the allowance rises above $280M, indicating the credit-financed share gain is souring.
4. Macao segment margin falls below 23% in a quarter with normal rolling hold, confirming the compression is structural.
5. Denial or non-renewal of the Macao dividend tax agreement.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 report, expected late October 2026. Two hold-normalized quarters at or above $570M in Macao, with the operating-expense moderation management promised, would convert this to a constructive call. Two more quarters near $500M would convert it to REDUCE.

**For existing holders:** maintain. The 2.64% dividend is covered by earnings, management has stated the $0.30 quarterly rate continues through 2026, and the price already discounts a no-recovery Macao. **For new capital:** no urgency. The estimate reset is three weeks old and unfinished, and the stock trades below the lowest published analyst target, which argues target cuts are the next development rather than upgrades.

## Conclusion

LVS is a good asset base at a reasonable price with a deteriorating near-term earnings trajectory and an unusually dense stack of dated regulatory items. The quality is real: a Singapore duopoly position with a genuinely superior suite and retail product, a first-place rolling-volume share in Macao's premium segment, and 2.9M square feet of retail generating $3,279–4,650 of tenant sales per square foot. The problems are also real: Macao segment margin down 750bp on reinvestment and payroll that management describes as largely complete but has not yet proven is complete; a Singapore tax structure that mechanically ratchets the blended rate higher as MBS grows; a $6.0B buyback authorization competing directly with $8.4B of committed capital programs; book equity about to go negative; and an effective tax rate that assumes a government agreement which does not yet exist.

Expected total return of roughly 14% against a 25% bear case, with low confidence in the normalized Macao run-rate, is approximately fair compensation. Own it if you own it; wait for the October print, a confirmed tax agreement, or a price nearer $40 before adding.
