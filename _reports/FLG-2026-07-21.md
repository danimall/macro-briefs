**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Flagstar Bank, N.A. is the surviving entity of what was New York Community Bancorp, the ~$87B-asset regional lender that nearly failed in early 2024 and was rescued by a ~$1.05B capital injection led by Steven Mnuchin's Liberty Strategic Capital. Under CEO Joseph Otting, the former Comptroller of the Currency, the company is executing a deliberate transformation from a monoline NYC multi-family/CRE lender toward a diversified commercial bank targeting a roughly one-third CRE, one-third C&I, one-third consumer loan mix. Two straight profitable quarters (Q4 2025 +$0.06 GAAP EPS, Q1 2026 +$0.03), a $0 credit provision in Q1 2026, a top-quartile 13.24% CET1 ratio, and investment-grade deposit ratings from Moody's and Fitch confirm the turnaround is real and the balance sheet is materially de-risked from the 2024 run.

The problem is timing and earnings power. Management cut both 2026 and 2027 guidance in April 2026 because CRE and multi-family loans are paying off faster than expected, shrinking earning assets before the C&I engine is large enough to replace the lost net interest income. 2026 adjusted EPS guidance dropped to $0.60-0.65 from $0.65-0.70, and NII guidance fell to $1.95-2.05B from $2.15-2.20B. Consensus EPS estimates have followed downward: current-year estimates are down 17.1% over 90 days and current-quarter estimates down 29.5%. The entire equity case rests on a steep ramp from ~$0.60 EPS in 2026 to $1.80-1.90 in 2027, and the near-term revision trend runs against that thesis even as the structural story improves.

At ~$14.84 the stock trades below fully-diluted tangible book value (~$15.70) and below undiluted TBV (~$17.42), which provides a valuation floor and a plausible re-rating path if capital returns begin in 2H 2026 and the C&I remix converts to earnings. This is a growth-oriented HOLD: the trajectory has improved for three consecutive reports, but the earnings ramp is unproven, the NYC rent-regulated book remains a live credit hazard, and falling estimates argue against reaching for a BUY here.

## Company Overview

Flagstar operates roughly 340 retail branches across nine states plus about 20 private-banking offices, headquartered in Hicksville, NY with a regional hub in Troy, MI. It reports as a single business segment. The key structural fact that standard data feeds miss: in October 2025 the company collapsed its bank holding company structure, so the SEC registrant is now the operating bank itself, Flagstar Bank, National Association, primarily regulated by the OCC rather than the Federal Reserve. The Oct 20, 2025 8-K carrying delisting, change-in-control, and disposition items reflects this reorganization, not a distress event. The bank now files with the SEC voluntarily, a genuine transparency consideration for equity holders since it could theoretically curtail public disclosure.

Historically the balance sheet was dominated by NYC multi-family lending, much of it rent-regulated. The transformation thesis is to shrink that legacy book while building Commercial & Industrial and consumer franchises. Total assets have contracted from $114B (2023) to $87.1B (Q1 2026), with management now guiding assets back up to ~$94B by end-2026 and ~$102B by end-2027 as the new lines scale.

## Financial Analysis

The turnaround is legitimate but early. Net income moved from -$1,118M (2024, driven by a $1.09B credit-loss provision and goodwill impairment) to -$177M (2025) to +$21M in Q1 2026, the second consecutive profitable quarter after eight straight losses. Quarterly GAAP diluted EPS traced -$0.23 (1Q25) → -$0.14 → -$0.07 → +$0.06 (4Q25) → +$0.03 (1Q26). Q1 2026 revenue was $498M (+2% YoY), with NII of $443M (+8% YoY) offsetting fee income of $55M (-31% YoY, largely the mortgage-servicing sale).

Margin recovery drives the story. Net interest margin fell from ~2.99% (2023) as funding costs spiked, bottomed at 1.74% in 1Q25, and recovered to 2.15% in 1Q26 as interest-bearing deposit costs fell 72bps YoY. Guidance calls for 2.30-2.40% in 2026 and 2.70-2.80% in 2027. The mechanism is straightforward: high-cost brokered deposits (slashed from $12B+ to ~$2.3B) and elevated funding costs are rolling off while the loan book remixes toward higher-yielding C&I. Whether NIM reaches the 2027 target depends on redeploying CRE runoff into new loans faster than the runoff itself compresses interest income.

Credit trajectory is improving. The provision collapsed from $1.09B (2024) to $184M (2025) to $0 in Q1 2026. Net charge-offs ran 0.52% in Q1 2026, or 0.29% excluding one resolved relationship. Non-accruals fell 11% QoQ to $2.68B, and criticized/classified loans dropped $2.4B (-16%) YoY. The $0 provision is not sustainable — guidance calls for $100-150M of provision in 2026 — and the allowance/total loans ratio at ~1.67% looks thin given that roughly a third of non-accruals are still classified as performing.

Capital is the clearest strength. CET1 stood at 13.24% in Q1 2026, described as top-quartile versus peers, with about $1.6B of excess capital above the 10.5% low end of the target range. Tangible book value is ~$17.42/share undiluted and ~$15.70/share fully diluted for warrants, both above the current ~$14.84 price. The common dividend is a token $0.01/quarter, and there are no buybacks yet; management has signaled the board will likely consider capital distributions in 2H 2026 once earnings consistency is established.

Deposit stability has improved markedly: insured deposits are now ~80% of the base versus a peer average near 56%, directly addressing the 2024 run risk. Core deposits grew $1.1B QoQ in Q1 2026.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a remix story, not an expansion story. C&I loans rose 9% QoQ to $16.6B in Q1 2026, the third straight quarter of C&I growth, led by Specialized Industries and Corporate/Regional Commercial Banking. Multi-family has declined to ~$26-28B and CRE to ~$8.8B. Management wants to grow total assets back toward $102B by end-2027, funded by C&I and consumer origination.

The near-term earnings momentum is negative, which is the central tension. Estimate revisions are broadly falling: next-year estimates are down 5.9% over 90 days. This is the opposite of the positive-revision signal that would support an aggressive call. The revisions fell for a defensible reason — faster CRE payoffs shrink earning assets and push NII out — but a falling-estimate bank in the middle of a transformation does not clear the bar for a growth-momentum BUY. The 2027 target of $1.80-1.90 EPS (ROATCE 10.75-11.25%) is a large step up from 2026's ~$0.60-0.65 (ROATCE ~4%), and the whole equity thesis depends on that ramp materializing on schedule.

The earnings surprise record is mixed and improving: two beats, one in-line, one miss over four quarters, with the two most recent quarters (Q4 2025 +176.5% surprise off a tiny base, Q1 2026 +22.3%) both positive. Small absolute EPS numbers make percentage surprises noisy, so I weight the direction (turning profitable) more than the magnitude.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$14.84 FLG trades at roughly 0.85x undiluted TBV and ~0.95x fully-diluted TBV. On a trailing basis the P/E is negative and meaningless. On forward consensus of $0.44 (2026) and $1.51 (2027), the stock trades at ~34x 2026 and ~9.8x 2027 EPS. The forward multiple is the relevant lens for a bank in earnings recovery, but 2027 is far enough out and dependent enough on an unproven ramp that I discount it.

Peer regional banks trade at a median P/E of 13.7 (ABCB 13.7, HWC 14.7, UBSI 12.6, ASB 10.1, GBCI 22.8). FLG's ~9.8x on 2027 estimates screens cheap against that median, but only if 2027 estimates hold, and the recent revision trend is down. The more defensible anchor is TBV: the stock below diluted book provides downside support, and management explicitly targets a 1.0x TBV multiple in its own dilution math. Buybacks below TBV, if approved in 2H 2026, would be accretive to book value per share.

I resist treating the cheapness-to-TBV as a clean buy signal. Discounted-book bank turnarounds screen cheap precisely because the market is uncertain whether book value itself is intact, and here the $4.25B criticized rent-regulated sub-book with a 1.01x DSCR is exactly the kind of exposure that can erode book if credit re-deteriorates. The cheapness is a conditional floor contingent on the credit book holding, not an independent catalyst. That warrants a moderate valuation score rather than a high one: there is genuine asymmetry to the upside if the turnaround sticks, but the discount reflects real, identifiable risk rather than a clear mispricing.

The relevant sector caution reinforces this: Financials BUYs have historically produced negative alpha as a group, so a positive raw view here needs a distinct company-specific catalyst, which the 2H 2026 capital-return decision partially supplies but does not yet confirm.

## Competitive Landscape

FLG benchmarks against a mid-cap regional peer group (Citizens, Fifth Third, First Citizens, First Horizon, Huntington, KeyCorp, M&T, Pinnacle, Regions, Valley National, Webster, Western Alliance, Zions). Against these it is a "show-me" turnaround rather than a steady compounder. Its multi-family allowance ratio (~1.8%) sits well above peers (0.3-1.3%), reflecting both a riskier book and more conservative reserving.

The competitive position is improving in trajectory even if the absolute level remains below peers. Investment-grade deposit ratings from Moody's and Fitch restore eligibility for custodial and institutional deposits and lower funding costs, a concrete moat-repair. (Issuer-level ratings remain sub-investment-grade at most agencies; the upgrades apply specifically to deposit ratings.) The C&I build demonstrates the franchise can originate outside its legacy niche, though the base is small and the durability of these relationships through a full credit cycle is untested. RBC's Jon Arfstrom characterized the balance-sheet remix as "fundamental progress" with "strong" C&I growth, a cautiously constructive external read.

The industry backdrop is mixed. The March 2026 proposal to rescind the 2023 Basel III "endgame" is a tailwind — management estimates +60-80bps CET1 benefit — though Category III/IV banks would eventually run AOCI through capital, a modest offset given FLG's unrealized securities losses. Regional-bank CRE stress remains a live sector theme, and FLG is arguably the most CRE-concentrated survivor and the most exposed to NYC rent-regulated multi-family specifically. The picture is one of improving trajectory against still-elevated concentration and sub-peer profitability.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is the NYC rent-regulated multi-family book. Of ~$14.1B in NYC multi-family, ~$8.8B is at least 50% rent-regulated, and within that $4.25B is criticized/classified with an amortizing DSCR of just 1.01x and current LTV of 77%. The 2019 HSTPA rent caps combined with higher rates and inflated operating costs structurally impair these borrowers. A rate or NOI shock would immediately generate new charge-offs and force the provision back up. Part of a $3.7B repricing wave hits in 2026, with more in 2027.

Payoffs cut both ways. In Q1 2026 CRE/multi-family fell $1.6B, which de-risks the balance sheet but pressures near-term earnings and directly forced the guidance cut. CFO Lee Smith called payoffs "both good news and bad news." Earnings power depends on redeploying runoff into C&I/consumer fast enough, and the current revision trend suggests the market doubts the pace.

Additional risks: thin non-accrual coverage (ACL/loans ~1.67%, ~33% of non-accruals still "performing"); reduced disclosure transparency post-reorganization (OCC-regulated bank filing with the SEC voluntarily); dilution overhang from ~46M warrant shares plus options (fully-diluted count ~463M vs. ~417M basic) and ~$503M of preferred ahead of the common; and a litigation tail from the 2023-24 crisis (In re NYCB Securities Litigation, derivative suits, cyber-breach class actions, a lost 9th Circuit escrow-interest preemption case, and an open Signature Bridge Bank FDIC settlement). The previously disclosed material weakness in internal controls was reported as remediated as of Q1 2026, a positive worth confirming against clean auditor attestation.

These risks are partially, but not fully, reflected in the discount to book. The rent-regulated book and the unproven earnings ramp are the two items that could move the stock in ways the current price does not fully capture, which keeps the risk score below neutral.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 37.0% sits rich to 1-year historical vol of 30.6% (a 6.4pp spread) and in the 71st percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (37.0% near-term vs. 29.8% at six months). This signals the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with an approaching earnings print and the 2H 2026 capital-return decision. Put/call skew is balanced (0.7%) and the put/call volume ratio is very low (0.17), so there is no concentrated directional bearish bet; the elevated IV reads as broad uncertainty rather than a specific short thesis. The P/C OI ratio rose from 0.08 to 0.57 over the past month, indicating some hedging accumulation but still call-heavy overall. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float), confirming the bear case is uncrowded. The rich near-term IV means options-based entry is expensive today; a patient buyer of the stock might prefer to let the near-term vol resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The turnaround sticks. NIM climbs toward the 2026 and 2027 targets as high-cost funding rolls off and the C&I book scales. Credit stays contained, non-accruals keep falling, and the rent-regulated criticized book resolves through payoffs rather than charge-offs. The board initiates capital returns in 2H 2026, and buybacks below TBV compound book value per share. 2027 EPS reaches the $1.80-1.90 target and the stock re-rates from ~0.9x diluted TBV toward 1.2-1.4x, or ~10-11x 2027 EPS in line with peers. That path implies a stock in the $18-20 range, consistent with the $18 analyst high target.

**Bear Case.** CRE/NOI stress on the 1.01x-DSCR rent-regulated book generates fresh charge-offs, the provision snaps back above the $100-150M guide, and reserves prove inadequate. Payoffs continue to outrun C&I growth, NII disappoints again, and 2027 EPS lands well short of $1.80. Estimates keep falling, capital returns are deferred, and the stock breaks below diluted TBV toward the $13 analyst low and potentially lower if book value itself erodes. A regional-bank CRE scare or a rate re-acceleration would amplify this path.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a 12-18 month growth thesis anchored to the 2H 2026 capital-return catalyst and the 2027 earnings ramp.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Board approves a common-stock buyback or meaningful capital-return program in 2H 2026, executed below tangible book value.
- Two consecutive quarters of C&I/consumer growth clearly outpacing CRE runoff, with NII and consensus estimates stabilizing or turning up (reversal of the current -17% CY revision trend).
- Non-accruals and criticized loans continue declining while net charge-offs (ex one-offs) stay below ~0.35%, confirming the rent-regulated book is resolving through payoffs rather than losses.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Provision snaps back materially above the $100-150M 2026 guide, or new charge-offs emerge in the $4.25B criticized rent-regulated book.
- 2027 EPS guidance is cut again, or a quarterly loss recurs, signaling the ramp is failing.
- Tangible book value per share declines QoQ on credit deterioration, removing the valuation floor.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and any 2H 2026 board announcement on capital distributions are the natural checkpoints. The capital-return decision is the single most concrete near-term catalyst.

## Conclusion

Flagstar has genuinely de-risked: 13.24% CET1, ~$1.6B of excess capital, ~80% insured deposits, investment-grade deposit ratings, a collapsed provision, and two consecutive profitable quarters. The score trajectory (4.3 → 4.7 → 5.3) reflects real fundamental repair, and the current data confirm rather than reverse that improvement — financial health and competitive position have both strengthened as the balance sheet stabilized and funding costs fell.

What holds the rating at HOLD rather than BUY is the earnings engine. Management cut 2026-27 guidance for a defensible reason (faster CRE payoffs), but consensus estimates are falling across every window, the 2027 ramp to $1.80-1.90 EPS is steep and unproven, and the rent-regulated multi-family book at a 1.01x DSCR remains a live threat to the book value that currently supports the stock. The negative revision trend argues against a momentum BUY, and Financials as a group carry a negative-alpha history that raises the bar for a positive call. The stock below diluted TBV offers a reasonable floor and a credible re-rating case if the 2H 2026 capital-return decision materializes and the C&I remix converts to earnings, but the evidence is not yet there to reach for it. Hold for the catalyst; reassess at the next print and the capital-return announcement.
