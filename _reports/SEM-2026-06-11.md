**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

On March 2, 2026, Select Medical Holdings signed a definitive merger agreement to be taken private at **$16.50 per share in cash** (~$3.9B enterprise value) by a consortium led by Executive Chairman and Co-Founder Robert Ortenzio, Senior EVP Martin Jackson, and private-equity sponsor Welsh, Carson, Anderson & Stowe. Management is expected to roll into the private entity, the deal carries a majority-of-the-minority shareholder vote condition, and the targeted close is mid-2026. The investment question has shifted from whether the post-acute care business is good to whether this insider-led buyout closes at $16.50, gets bumped, or breaks.

At the current price of $16.63, the stock trades roughly 0.8% *above* the announced cash consideration. That premium implies the market assigns some probability to a price bump, plausible given the deal price sits below the pre-announcement average analyst target of $17.70 and several plaintiff firms have opened fiduciary-duty investigations into whether $16.50 is fair to public holders. The negative gross spread also means a holder buying today is, in the base case, locking in a small *loss* to the deal price while bearing full deal-break downside.

The setup is symmetric and capped. Upside in the base case is essentially zero (the stock converts to $16.50 cash at close), with a low-probability bump scenario toward $17. Downside if the deal breaks is to the pre-announcement trading level of roughly $13-14, a 16-22% decline, exacerbated by deteriorating fundamentals that the announcement masked. This is a HOLD for any existing arbitrage allocation, but the absence of a directional appreciation thesis and the negative gross spread make it an uncompelling entry for a 12-month equity investor. We classify the hold as uncertain: the outcome is binary and event-driven, not a function of operating performance we can forecast with confidence.

## Company Overview

Select Medical, founded in 1997, is one of the largest U.S. operators of post-acute care facilities. As of December 31, 2025, it operated across 39 states plus D.C. through three reportable segments:

- **Critical Illness Recovery Hospitals (LTCHs):** 104 long-term acute care hospitals in 28 states, ~45% of revenue ($2,477.8M). Patients recovering from complex critical illness, typically admitted from acute-care ICUs. Average length of stay ~31 days, occupancy 69%, revenue per patient-day $2,230.
- **Rehabilitation Hospitals (IRFs):** 38 inpatient rehab hospitals (26 consolidated, 12 managed minority-owned) in 15 states, ~24% of revenue ($1,289.0M). This is the growth engine, with revenue +16.1% in 2025 on new builds and health-system JVs, and the highest-margin segment at 21.6% adjusted EBITDA margin.
- **Outpatient Rehabilitation:** 1,917 PT/OT/speech clinics, ~24% of revenue ($1,284.9M). The weakest segment, with margins compressed to 7.0% (from 8.7%) and flat revenue per visit at $100.

A structural change frames the recent financials. On November 25, 2024, SEM completed a tax-free distribution of Concentra Group Holdings (occupational health) to shareholders. Concentra is now reported as discontinued operations across all periods, which explains the apparent revenue drop from $6.66B (2023) to $5.19B (2024) in standardized data. SEM is now a pure-play post-acute and rehab operator, smaller and less diversified than before the spin.

The company is the scale leader in LTCH and a major IRF and outpatient player. Principal competitors include Encompass Health (IRF), Kindred/ScionHealth (LTCH), and fragmented local outpatient providers.

| Free cash flow | — | — | $117M |
| Long-term debt | $3,588M | $1,692M | $1,804M |

Revenue grew 5.1% in 2025, but profitability inflected down. Adjusted EBITDA fell 3.4% despite the revenue gain, a margin-compression story driven by labor cost inflation (nurses, therapists, agency staffing). Reported net income rose to $215M, but the quality of that number is poor: it was driven primarily by a one-time ~$86M reduction in stock compensation tied to RSU modifications at the Concentra spin, which cut G&A stock comp from $99M to $13M. Underlying operating performance deteriorated.

Segment divergence is the operating story. Rehab hospitals were strong (adjusted EBITDA +13.4%); LTCHs weakened (−12.0% on higher operating costs); outpatient deteriorated (−17.0%, personnel-cost driven). The mix of a healthy growth segment offset by a structurally pressured LTCH segment and a low-barrier outpatient segment leaves blended margins flat-to-down.

Operating cash flow fell from $518M to $346M, largely because 2024 still included Concentra's cash generation; on a continuing-ops basis the trend is less alarming, but FCF of $117M against ~$1.8B net debt is modest.

Leverage was materially de-risked post-spin. Long-term debt fell from $3.6B to ~$1.8B as Concentra IPO and spin proceeds paid down term loans. The credit-agreement leverage ratio was 3.67x against a 7.00x covenant, comfortable headroom. Liquidity is adequate at $26.5M cash plus $469M revolver availability, though the current ratio of 1.04 reflects thin working capital ($40.8M). Goodwill of $2.36B is ~40% of assets.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.62 ("distress zone") is a false signal here. It is depressed by the capital-intensive, high-operating-lease, goodwill-heavy hospital model, where the EBIT/assets input reads near zero on an operating-income classification quirk. Covenant leverage of 3.67x, positive FCF, and a creditworthy Medicare/insurer payor base argue against a genuine bankruptcy signal. ROE of 12.6% and the partial Piotroski 5/5 indicate a functioning, profitable operator. The financial-health score reflects a deleveraged, cash-generative business with the operating margin trend as the principal offset.

## Growth Analysis

The standalone growth case is modest. FY2026 is guided to ~$5.7B, in line with consensus and implying mid-single-digit top-line growth. Within that, the IRF segment is the genuine bright spot, with a favorable FY2026 IRF conversion factor (~+2.5% gross), supported by health-system JV demand. The LTCH segment, at ~45% of revenue, faces structural headwinds that cap blended growth.

Estimate revisions are mixed and net negative on the figures that matter most. Current-year EPS consensus has fallen 6.1% over 90 days to $1.23, and next-year consensus has fallen 8.5% over 90 days to $1.37. The near-term quarterly estimates ticked up modestly (next quarter +6.8% over 30 days), but the full-year revision trend is downward. This is a business with decelerating estimate momentum and margin compression, not an earnings-inflection profile. The positive-90-day-revision signal that would support a growth thesis is absent.

In practical terms, the deal removes the relevance of the standalone growth profile. If the merger closes, future operating growth accrues to the private buyers, not public shareholders. The growth score reflects the weak underlying trajectory rather than any equity-relevant upside.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation here collapses to deal mechanics. Trailing P/E of 15.5x and a 1.5% dividend yield are largely irrelevant; the stock is priced to the announced transaction, not to fundamentals.

The peer set illustrates why a fundamental multiple framework is unhelpful here. Health Care Facilities comps show extreme dispersion: Astrana 62.4x, Acadia negative, NHC 24.4x, Option Care 16.0x, USPH 125.6x, with a peer median P/E of 62.4x that is distorted by trough or loss-making earnings across the group. SEM's 15.5x trailing multiple is below that median, but the discount reflects the LTCH reimbursement overhang and margin compression, not a mispricing the market has missed. Absent the deal, a low-double-digit to mid-teens multiple on a flat-to-declining-margin post-acute operator with regulatory tail risk would be defensible, putting unaffected fair value in the low-to-mid teens, broadly consistent with the pre-announcement trading range.

The bookends are clear. Upside is capped near $16.50 at close, with a low-probability bump toward the ~$17 analyst high if the Special Committee process or fairness litigation forces a price improvement. Downside on a deal break is to roughly $13-14, the pre-announcement level, and potentially lower given the FY2025 margin deterioration the announcement masked. The valuation score reflects an essentially symmetric, capped payoff with a negative entry spread today.

## Competitive Landscape

SEM is the scale leader in LTCH and a meaningful IRF and outpatient operator, but the competitive trajectory is mixed. The IRF segment is gaining share through health-system joint ventures and new builds, with margins (21.6%) well above the corporate average. The outpatient segment faces low barriers to entry and intense local competition from physician-owned and hospital-affiliated clinics, which explains flat revenue per visit and margin erosion. The LTCH segment, the largest, is structurally challenged by the dual site-neutral payment structure and escalating CMS outlier thresholds.

The broader industry is in a synchronized squeeze of reimbursement pressure plus cost inflation. Peer read-throughs confirm the pattern: Universal Health Services flagged weaker admissions and higher costs in Q4'25, and Tenet guided 2026 EBITDA margins roughly flat-to-down at ~21%. SEM's competitive position is best characterized as stable-to-modestly-improving in IRF, stable in LTCH share but pressured on reimbursement, and eroding in outpatient. The blended trajectory is roughly neutral, which the competitive score reflects.

## Risk Assessment

The transaction itself is now the dominant risk. The principal scenarios are: (1) deal closes at $16.50 (most likely), converting the stock to cash near current levels; (2) deal is bumped modestly toward $17 under Special Committee or litigation pressure; (3) deal breaks, sending the stock back toward $13-14.

Several SEM-specific fundamental risks underpin the bear case and inform whether $16.50 is fair:

- **LTCH outlier fixed-loss escalation.** CMS raised the LTCH-PPS fixed-loss threshold to record levels: $59,873 (FY24) → $77,048 (FY25) → $78,936 (FY26), having proposed higher ($91k+). Higher thresholds mean fewer outlier cases qualify and lower payments on those that do, directly pressuring the LTCH segment. The 10-K warns FY27 will likely rise again.
- **Outlier reconciliation rule change.** Effective October 1, 2025, CMS loosened the recoupment trigger (CCR ±20% vs prior 10 points), which will increase Medicare recoupments and delay cost-report settlements.
- **OBBBA Medicaid cuts.** The act signed July 4, 2025 cuts ~$1 trillion of federal Medicaid/CHIP funding over 10 years; SEM cannot yet quantify the impact but expects reduced reimbursement.
- **IRF Review Choice Demonstration** expanding to PA/TX/CA, with 100% pre-claim/post-payment review; PA IRF Medicare reimbursement reportedly fell from $33M to $29M/month after RCD began.
- **Labor cost inflation** remains the primary driver of 2025 margin compression.
- **Short-term LTCH leases** create clustered non-renewal risk on facilities held on sub-10-year terms.
- **Conflict-of-interest governance.** The Executive Chairman buying the company he runs, at a price below the average street target, with management rolling over, is an inherent conflict. The independent Special Committee and active plaintiff-firm investigations are the appropriate checks but raise a modest probability of a price bump or extended process.

The biggest fundamental threats (LTCH outlier escalation, OBBBA Medicaid cuts) land in FY27, after a mid-2026 close. The insiders may be buying ahead of a tougher reimbursement environment, which cuts against deal fairness for public holders but also reduces the attractiveness of owning the stock if the deal breaks. The risk-factor score balances the deleveraged balance sheet and low deal-break probability against the binary, event-driven nature of the situation.

### Options Market Signal

This is consistent with a name that has converted to a merger-arbitrage situation, where directional optionality has collapsed to the deal outcome. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($17.00):** The Special Committee process or active fiduciary-duty litigation forces a price improvement above $16.50, toward the $17 analyst high or the broader pre-announcement target range. The insiders are buying ahead of FY27 reimbursement headwinds at a price below the street average, which strengthens the fairness challenge. A bump to $17 represents ~2% upside from current levels.

**Bear Case ($13.50):** The deal breaks (financing, regulatory, or a failed majority-of-the-minority vote) and the stock reverts to its pre-announcement trading level, compounded by FY2025 margin compression, the LTCH outlier and OBBBA overhangs, and the weak quality of 2025 net income. This represents roughly 16-19% downside from $16.63.

**Base Case ($16.50):** The merger closes at $16.50 cash in mid-2026. A holder at $16.63 realizes a small loss to the deal price. Future operating performance accrues to the private buyers.

The asymmetry runs against a new long position: ~2% upside in the bump scenario against ~16-19% downside in the break scenario, with the most-likely outcome a marginal loss to the deal price. For an existing holder, the deal provides a near-certain cash exit close to current value with limited need to act.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD, classified as uncertain because the outcome is binary and event-driven rather than a function of forecastable operating performance.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A definitive proxy (DEFM14A) and fairness opinion that materially raise the consideration above $16.50, or a credible competing bid emerging (low probability given ~16% insider ownership and anti-takeover provisions).
- The gross spread widening to a level (e.g., the stock falling below ~$16.00 on deal-process noise while the deal probability remains high) that creates positive arbitrage carry to the $16.50 floor.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material adverse developments in the regulatory financing or antitrust review path, or signs the majority-of-the-minority vote will fail, that raise the probability of a deal break toward the ~$13-14 reversion level.
- Withdrawal or renegotiation of the merger agreement downward.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the definitive proxy filing (DEFM14A with projections and fairness opinion), the unaffiliated-shareholder vote, and the targeted mid-2026 close. The situation should resolve within roughly six months.

## Conclusion

SEM has converted from an operating equity into a merger-arbitrage position priced to a $16.50 cash take-private led by its own Executive Chairman and Welsh Carson. At $16.63 the stock trades above the deal price, reflecting a modest bump probability against deal-break downside. The underlying business is deleveraged and cash-generative but shows margin compression, negative full-year estimate revisions, and significant back-loaded regulatory risk that lands after the targeted close, which strengthens the fairness challenge but also weakens the equity if the deal fails.

For existing holders, the deal offers a near-certain cash exit close to current value; there is no compelling reason to sell ahead of close absent a deterioration in deal probability. For a new directional investor, the payoff is capped near $16.50 with negative carry from the current premium and asymmetric downside on a break. There is no 12-month appreciation thesis here, and the risk/reward does not support adding. We rate SEM a HOLD.
