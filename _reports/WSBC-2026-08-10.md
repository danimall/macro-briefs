## Executive Summary

WesBanco has completed the largest transaction in its history and the reported financials now look transformed. The February 28, 2025 acquisition of Premier Financial Corp. added $7.9B of assets and $5.9B of loans, took the company to $27.8B in assets, and lifted net interest margin from 2.96% (FY2024) to 3.53% (FY2025) and 3.63% in Q2 2026. First-half 2026 diluted EPS of $1.79 compares with $0.50 in 1H25, and the company-defined efficiency ratio improved from 63.52% to 52.87% as branch count fell from 251 to 216 in six months. The market has paid for it: the stock is up 41% over twelve months and sits within 2% of its 52-week high.

Three things prevent this from being an attractive entry. First, the earnings improvement is less durable than it looks. Dividing net interest income by reported NIM implies average earning assets near $23.07B in FY2025; against $55.3M of disclosed loan accretion plus roughly $10.3M of liability-side accretion, purchase-accounting accretion contributed approximately 28bp of the 57bp margin expansion — so core NIM improved about 31bp, from ~2.94% to ~3.25%, with $274.2M of unaccreted discount left to amortize on a front-loaded schedule. Second, credit is deteriorating in the two quarters *after* acquisition accounting closed, which removes the cleanest benign explanation. Non-accrual loans went $39.8M → $91.6M → $146.1M (YE24, YE25, 6/30/26); criticized-plus-classified commercial credits rose from 2.80% to 3.15% to 3.74% of loans; allowance coverage of non-performing loans fell from 3.49x to 2.39x to 1.49x while ACL/loans was flat at 1.12%. Third, and most unusual for a bank of this size, management and Ernst & Young both concluded internal control over financial reporting was not effective at 12/31/2025, with the weakness located precisely in "the design and operating effectiveness of controls related to the fair value of assets acquired" in the Premier deal — the same estimates that generate the accretion income and determine whether acquired-loan deterioration hits provision expense. Disclosure controls were still ineffective at 6/30/2026.

The valuation does not compensate. Forward P/E of 11.6x on 2026 consensus of $3.58 screens 24% below the regional-bank peer median of 15.2x, but price-to-tangible-book of 1.81x (on the CFO's ~$23 TBV) is a premium to peers on the metric that matters when 45% of common equity is goodwill and intangibles. Consensus for next year has been cut 2.4% over 90 days, the analyst mix has shifted (one strong buy from two; four holds from three), and the mean target of $44.25 sits 6.2% above spot. A probability-weighted outcome of roughly $41.4 plus a 3.64% dividend produces a total return near 3% against a bear case 25% lower. That is a trim, not a hold.

## Company Overview

WesBanco is a Wheeling, West Virginia bank holding company whose subsidiary, Wesbanco Bank, is a state non-member bank supervised by the FDIC and the West Virginia Division of Financial Institutions. At 6/30/2026 it operated 216 branches across West Virginia, Ohio, western Pennsylvania, Kentucky, southern Indiana, Maryland and Michigan, plus loan-production offices in Virginia, Tennessee and Florida. Two reportable segments: Community Banking (FY2025 segment profit $242.6M on $892.2M of revenue) and Trust and Investment Services (segment profit $8.9M on $31.6M of revenue, with trust assets under administration up to $7.9B from $6.0B).

The Premier Financial transaction was funded entirely in stock: 28,738,104 shares valued at $1.008B, taking average diluted shares from 62.65M (FY2024) to 91.03M (FY2025). Existing holders were diluted to roughly 70% of the combined company. WesBanco booked $485.5M of goodwill and $142.9M of intangibles, including a $136.0M core deposit intangible amortized over ten years on an accelerated basis. Crossing $15B in assets also reclassified $161.9M of trust preferred securities from Tier 1 to Tier 2 capital.

Functionally this is a commercial-real-estate-weighted community bank with a good deposit franchise (non-interest-bearing demand 24.8% of deposits at YE2025) operating in slow-growth Appalachian and Midwestern markets. The fee businesses are genuine but small: non-interest income actually *fell* as a share of total revenue, from 21.1% to 17.0%, because net interest income grew faster. WesBanco became more spread-dependent in 2025, not less.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported earnings.** FY2025 net income to common of $202.6M was up 43.3% year over year, and diluted EPS still declined to $2.23 from $2.26 and $2.51 in 2023 — the second consecutive annual decline, because the share count rose faster than earnings. Roughly $0.06 of the FY2025 shortfall came from a one-time $5.5M redemption premium on the Series A preferred embedded in the EPS numerator. Management's core EPS of $3.40 adds back $75.9M of restructuring and merger expense (of which $56.8M was Premier-specific) and is the figure used in the compensation plan.

**Margin quality.** The reported 57bp NIM expansion decomposes to roughly 31bp of core improvement and 28bp of accretion. Q2 2026 NIM of 3.63% included 3bp of *accelerated* mark accretion per management; total accretion contribution was likely near 23bp (annualized ~$56M on ~$24.5B of earning assets), implying core margin near 3.40%. The unaccreted discount fell from $302.4M at YE2025 to $274.2M at 6/30/26 — $28.2M recognized in six months with no acquisitions to replenish it. Management guides to ~3.60% reported NIM for the balance of 2026, which is credible, but the accretion component decays mechanically under level-yield accounting.

**Expenses.** The company-defined 52.87% efficiency ratio is adjusted; a GAAP cross-check (non-interest expense ÷ NII + fees) gives 63.7% for 2025 versus 66.3% for 2024. Excluding restructuring and intangible amortization reproduces the ~53% figure. The ~11-point improvement is real operating leverage from scale and 37 branch closures year-to-date 2026, with a "phase 3" optimization planned for Q4 2026. Two fixed costs to note: FDIC insurance rose to $20.9M from $14.2M, and the core banking software contract commits a minimum ~$16.6M per year through 2033.

**Capital and book value.** Holdco CET1 fell 170bp to 10.37% at YE2025 and recovered to 10.70% at 6/30/26; Tier 1 leverage 9.83%; total capital 14.18%. Bank-level CET1 of 12.06% versus holdco 10.37% at YE2025 reflects 169bp of double leverage. Holding-company cash fell to $161.9M from $321.8M while dividends taken from the Bank rose to $111.0M from $42.0M; Bank dividend capacity without prior approval is $331.3M, so near-term liquidity at the parent is adequate.

Tangible common equity works out to roughly $2.08B at YE2025, or $21.70/share against the company-reported $22.01 (the gap likely a deferred-tax add-back on intangibles). Either way, tangible book *declined* from $22.83 at YE2024 — the acquisition was tangible-book dilutive. Intangibles are 45.3% of common equity, and goodwill alone equals about 40% of market capitalization. Separately, $104.0M of HTM unrealized losses at 6/30/26 are excluded from AOCI and regulatory capital; marking them would cost roughly $0.82/share of tangible book. Economic, not regulatory.

**Cash flow and taxes.** Operating cash flow of $290.4M in FY2025 (versus $211.0M and $169.3M) is genuine growth but includes a $44.4M negative other-accretion adjustment and a $42.0M deferred-tax swing. Current federal tax expense fell to $11.7M from $30.3M while deferred swung to +$36.0M — purchase-accounting mechanics, not a cash-tax windfall (cash taxes paid $31.7M versus $26.8M).

**Capital allocation.** No common repurchases in FY2025. A new 4.0M-share authorization was approved May 20, 2026, under which 290,135 shares were bought in 1H26; management described buybacks as "muted" for the next couple of quarters. Dividends of $1.49/share in 2025 were a 66.8% GAAP payout, raised to $0.38 quarterly in 2026 — a 42.5% payout on 1H26 GAAP EPS and roughly 42% of 2026 consensus. The preferred restructuring (issued $230M of 7.375% Series B, redeemed $150M of 6.75% Series A, retired $50M of Premier sub debt) raises the annual preferred coupon from $10.13M to $16.96M, about $0.07/share of ongoing dilution to common.

**Earnings delivery.** Three beats in four quarters: +5.7% (Q2 26), +3.2% (Q1 26), −3.1% (Q4 25), +4.6% (Q3 25). The pattern supports modest confidence in near-term consensus but the magnitude is small, and consensus itself is drifting down.

## Growth Analysis

Strip out the acquisition and the organic engine is slow. Portfolio loans grew 1.3% year-to-date to $19.48B and 3.5% year over year. Deposits fell slightly to $21.59B, with CDs down 9.9% in 1H26, pushing loan-to-deposit from 88.7% to approximately 90.2%; management said incremental growth will be funded wholesale. Net interest income rose only 2.5% year over year in Q2 despite the balance-sheet step-up, because the year-ago quarter already included Premier for a full quarter.

Consensus embeds $3.58 for 2026 and $3.87 for 2027, roughly 8% earnings growth, and both have been revised down (−0.2% and −2.3% over 30 days; +0.1% and −2.4% over 90 days). Current- and next-quarter estimates were each cut about 2-3% in the last 30 days. This is the opposite of the estimate-revision pattern that would justify paying up.

The identifiable growth levers are: (1) remaining cost take-out from the Q4 2026 branch phase, (2) the finite accretion tailwind, (3) trust and insurance fee growth (AUA +31.7%), and (4) the Florida loan-production push — $200M booked in 90 days against a $2B multi-year target. The Florida initiative is the one genuinely incremental revenue source, and it is also the least de-risked: out-of-footprint CRE-adjacent origination from a bank already above both interagency CRE supervisory criteria, into an environment where trade reporting describes banks competing on rate, leverage and recourse. Property types, LTVs and sponsor profiles are undisclosed.

Management has explicitly ruled out near-term M&A, which is consistent with the capital position and with the practical reality that an unremediated adverse ICFR opinion makes regulatory approval of another deal unlikely. That removes the mechanism that produced most of WesBanco's historical growth.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | WSBC at $41.65 | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E, FY2025 GAAP EPS $2.23 | 18.7x | — |
| P/E, FY2025 core EPS $3.40 | 12.3x | — |
| P/E, 2026 consensus $3.58 | 11.6x | 15.2x |
| P/E, 2027 consensus $3.87 | 10.8x | — |
| Price / tangible book (~$23) | 1.81x | — |
| Price / book (~$3.81B common equity) | 1.05x | — |
| Dividend yield | 3.64% | 2.2% |
| Distance to 52-week high | −2.0% | — |
| Upside to mean target $44.25 | +6.2% | — |

The forward P/E discount to peers is the bull's best number, and it is largely an artifact of where the intangibles sit. On price-to-book WSBC looks cheap at 1.05x; on price-to-tangible-book it trades at a premium at 1.81x, because 45% of common equity is goodwill and core deposit intangibles. For a bank, tangible book is the loss-absorbing anchor and the correct denominator. Any screen using P/B, Altman Z, or margin ratios will misclassify this company as cheaper and safer than the tangible metrics support.

Is 1.81x tangible book defensible? Annualizing 1H26 net income to common (~$172M) gives roughly $344M against average tangible common equity near $2.15B, or ~16% ROTCE. A Gordon-style implied multiple at 16% ROTCE, 5% growth and a 10% cost of equity is near 2.2x, which would make 1.81x look inexpensive. Two adjustments matter. Stripping accretion (roughly $56M pre-tax, ~$43M after tax) takes core ROTCE to ~14.3%, and normalizing provisioning toward a mid-cycle 0.30-0.35% of loans from the current ~0.09% run rate removes another $24M after tax, taking core ROTCE closer to 13%. At 13% ROTCE the implied fair multiple drops to roughly 1.6x, which is *below* where the stock trades. The bull and bear cases on this stock are almost entirely a debate about which of those ROTCE numbers is the run rate, and the credit data is currently moving toward the lower one.

The CFO's own framing on the Q2 call — TBV compounding $0.70-0.80 per quarter, at a constant 1.8x multiple, implies about $5 of annual price appreciation — is arithmetically correct and rests entirely on the multiple staying at 1.8x. The multiple is the variable most exposed to the credit trend.

On analyst targets: the mean of $44.25 is 6.2% above spot and the *low* target of $41.00 is below the current price. This is not a stock where consensus targets function as a stale floor — estimates are being cut, the rating mix has degraded, and the price has already closed most of the gap.

Valuation score 4.8: cheap on a headline forward multiple that is contradicted by the tangible-book premium and by falling estimates. The discount to the peer P/E is explained by identifiable impairments rather than mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Peer comparables at similar scale trade at 12.8x (RNST) to 15.8x (CVBF), median 15.2x, with a median 2.2% yield. WSBC's higher yield and lower P/E come with a materially different risk profile.

**Genuine strengths.** Office exposure is only $507M, 2.8% of loans — a real differentiator versus most CRE-heavy regionals. Non-interest-bearing deposits at 24.8% of the base are a durable funding advantage in slow-growth markets where branch density still matters. The $7.9B trust business is larger than most $25-30B banks carry. Management preemptively disclosed NDFI exposure under $55M and no direct technology, software or data-center lending, which addresses the two credit themes currently in focus across the sector. No customer exceeds 10% of segment revenue.

**Genuine weaknesses.** Total CRE is $10.94B, 56.9% of loans, with the non-owner-occupied CRE-to-capital ratio at 300.3% (above the 300% interagency criterion) and 36-month CRE growth of 66.9% (above the 50% criterion) — both screens tripped simultaneously, the condition for heightened supervisory attention to CRE risk management. Multi-family alone is 80.1% of risk-based capital. Item 1 confirms that CRE concentrations above supervisory thresholds disqualify the holding company from expedited Federal Reserve consultation on dividends exceeding earnings; WesBanco pays out ~42% currently, so this is a contingent rather than an active constraint. Five-year TSR of 138.23 versus a peer group at 145.48 (2021=100) shows the franchise has lagged. And an adverse ICFR opinion is uncommon among $25B+ banks; it practically forecloses the M&A channel until remediated.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable rather than improving: scale and cost efficiency up, organic loan and deposit growth minimal, credit metrics moving the wrong way, and the acquisition engine paused.

## Risk Assessment

**Internal control (highest-signal item).** Management concluded ICFR was not effective at 12/31/2025 and Ernst & Young issued an adverse ICFR opinion dated March 2, 2026, citing "a lack of precision and evidence of reviews of the assumptions supporting the fair value of acquired assets." Disclosure controls remained ineffective at 6/30/2026, with remediation in progress and third-party advisors to be engaged. The weakness sits on the estimates that (a) generate accretion income and (b) determine PCD versus non-PCD classification of acquired loans, which in turn determines whether subsequent deterioration flows through provision expense or was pre-funded at closing. Note 2's measurement-period revisions netted to only +$2.1M of goodwill but comprised roughly $100M of gross, partly offsetting line-item changes (net loans +$38.3M, loans held for sale −$29.4M, intangibles −$8.6M, DTAs +$11.4M, accrued liabilities −$14.2M).

The fair counterweight: E&Y issued an *unqualified* opinion on the financial statements, expanded substantive testing, and deployed valuation specialists to build independent ranges. Evidence-of-review deficiencies in a first-time integration of a $7.9B target are frequently documentation failures rather than valuation errors. There is no restatement and no identified misstatement. This is a control-quality and execution finding. It is also, notably, one that received essentially no external press coverage — which argues it is not fully priced.

**Credit.** The clearest hard-data deterioration in the file. Non-accruals up 267% since YE2024 and 59.5% in 1H26 alone; criticized-plus-classified up $122.9M organically in 1H26 with no acquisitions to explain it; ACL/NPL coverage more than halved to 1.49x while ACL/loans was flat. Ten individually evaluated relationships totaled $79.5M at 6/30/26. The most striking disclosure is in the modification tables: loans previously modified for borrowers in financial difficulty that defaulted within twelve months rose to $49.0M in 2025 from $3.9M in 2024, of which $46.3M was CRE improved property extended by term — largely a legacy, pre-Premier cohort. Of $118.5M modified during 2025, 19% was already 90+ days past due at year end, versus 7% a year earlier.

The counterweight is real: net charge-offs were just $18.6M in FY2025 (0.10%) and $8.5M in 1H26 (~0.09% annualized), and reported NPLs of $91.6M carried $112.2M of unpaid principal, meaning $20.6M of loss content had already been absorbed via charge-offs and acquisition marks. Management said on the Q2 call that criticized/classified had already fallen 11bp to 3.61% post-quarter, expected "low 3s" by end of Q3, and that all three newly added CRE non-accruals (none office) were likely to resolve within the quarter. That commentary is unaudited and intra-quarter. The Q3 and Q4 2026 prints are the decisive evidence, and the asymmetry of that test is unfavorable at a 1.8x tangible-book multiple.

**Funding and rate path.** FHLB borrowings of $1.4B with 89% maturing during 2026 at an average ~3.90% is a repricing event whose sign depends on the curve. Uninsured deposits are $7.0B (33%), or $4.7B (22%) excluding collateralized public funds. The balance sheet is asset-sensitive (+300bp shock → +4.7% NII) and management's internal model assumes one Fed *hike* in December 2026. That assumption is not consensus, and a dovish resolution would pressure the 3.60% NIM guide.

**Governance.** CEO compensation rose 76% to $4.68M in a year when GAAP diluted EPS declined and the company reported a material weakness — substantially explained by the scale step-up and a peer-group reset to $18.6B-$49.9B institutions, but the compensation and shareholder outcomes diverged. The plan pays on core EPS ($3.40), which adds back the merger costs that caused the GAAP decline. Offsetting features are genuine: the plan includes NPA and net charge-off ratios, and the 2023-2025 TSR award paid *zero* because WesBanco failed to reach the 50th percentile of its peer group. A Chief Risk Officer transition effective June 2026, arriving alongside a 59.5% six-month rise in non-accruals, is a datapoint; the "transition and consulting" structure is also the standard form for a planned retirement, and no motive should be inferred.

**Insider activity.** Low signal in both directions. One 400-share open-market purchase at $34.08 (May 8, 2026) by an EVP is too small to count as conviction; a 12,500-share sale at $41.80 on July 29, 2026 is routine wealth management near a 52-week high. There is no clustered C-suite buying — the signal that would most support paying this multiple.

**Positioning.** Short interest is 7.3% of float at 3.3 days to cover — elevated for a $4B regional bank and consistent with a visible bear case, but not crowded enough to create squeeze risk on any good news.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($50, +20%).** Management's credit commentary proves out: criticized/classified falls into the low 3s by Q3 and non-accruals resolve at minimal loss, validating the re-grading interpretation. The material weakness is remediated by year end. NIM holds near 3.60% as the Q4 branch phase delivers further cost saves, the 4M-share authorization is executed, and TBV compounds $0.70-0.80 per quarter toward ~$25 by mid-2027. Consensus stabilizes at $3.95-4.00 and the market pays 12.5-13x, or ~2.0x tangible book. Roughly 16% ROTCE at a $4B market cap with a 3.64% covered yield would deserve a peer-comparable multiple.

**Bear case ($31, −25%).** The 1H26 credit trend continues rather than reverses. Provisioning normalizes from ~0.09% of loans toward 0.35%, costing roughly $31M pre-tax and $0.25 of EPS, while accretion decay removes another $0.10-0.15 as the $274.2M discount amortizes on a front-loaded schedule. EPS settles near $3.10-3.25 rather than $3.87, and the market applies 10x — or 1.35x a tangible book that is no longer compounding — as the unremediated control weakness and the 300.3% CRE ratio invite supervisory friction on capital actions. Florida originations season badly in a market entered from a standing start against competitors cutting terms.

**Base case ($42, roughly flat).** Credit deterioration slows but does not fully reverse; provisioning drifts to 0.25% of loans; accretion contributes less each quarter; EPS lands $3.55-3.70 for 2027 rather than $3.87. TBV reaches ~$24.50 by mid-2027 and the multiple compresses modestly to ~1.70x as the market discounts the accretion tail. $42 is 11.4x $3.70 and 1.71x $24.50 — two independent routes to approximately the current price.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives roughly $41.4, or about −0.6% in price plus a 3.64% dividend: a ~3% total return against a required return closer to 9-10% for a CRE-concentrated regional with an open control finding. The downside is 25% and the upside 20%, with the base case at spot. That combination argues for reducing exposure.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim now, into a price within 2% of the 52-week high and 6% below a mean target whose low end ($41.00) is already beneath spot. Existing holders wanting continued regional-bank exposure should cut position size by roughly half ahead of the Q3 2026 report in October, which is the first audited-quality test of management's "low 3s" criticized/classified claim. Income-oriented holders can retain a reduced position for the 3.64% yield at a 42% payout, recognizing that the dividend is well covered even in the bear case ($1.52 against ~$3.15 of EPS) — the risk here is capital, not the distribution.

**Upgrade triggers (back to HOLD or better).**
1. Q3 2026 criticized-plus-classified confirmed at or below 3.61% of loans *and* non-accruals below $130M, with ACL/NPL coverage stabilizing at or above 1.5x.
2. A filing (10-Q Item 4 or 10-K Item 9A) confirming remediation of the material weakness and effective disclosure controls.
3. Price toward $35-36 with the above credit metrics intact, which would put the stock near 1.45-1.50x tangible book and under 9.5x 2027 consensus — a level at which the accretion tail and normalized provisioning are already discounted.

**Avoid triggers (full exit).**
1. Q3 or Q4 2026 non-accruals above $175M, or criticized-plus-classified above 4.0% of loans.
2. Net charge-offs annualizing above 0.30% of loans in any quarter, or a provision build that takes ACL/loans above 1.30% (the signal that non-performing status is converting to realized loss).
3. Any regulatory action or informal agreement tied to CRE concentration or internal controls, or a formal restriction on capital distributions.
4. A third consecutive quarter with disclosure controls reported ineffective (i.e., still ineffective at 12/31/2026).

**Reassessment timeline.** Mid-to-late October 2026 with the Q3 10-Q, which should carry both the credit prints and an updated Item 4 control assessment. The Q4 2026 report (late January 2027) is the second checkpoint, adding the phase-3 branch savings and the FY2026 ICFR opinion.

## Conclusion

WesBanco delivered on the mechanics of the Premier Financial transaction: assets up 48%, NIM up 57bp reported, efficiency improved roughly eleven points, capital rebuilt to 10.70% CET1, and first-half EPS more than tripled off a merger-charge-depressed base. Three beats in four quarters support the near-term consensus.

The problem is the quality and durability of that improvement measured against the price. Roughly 46% of the margin expansion came from a finite, front-loaded accretion of acquisition marks with $274.2M remaining. Non-accruals have risen 267% in eighteen months and 59.5% in the last six alone, with no acquisition to explain the organic increase, while allowance coverage of non-performing loans fell from 3.49x to 1.49x. Both interagency CRE supervisory criteria are tripped, and management is adding CRE-adjacent exposure in Florida from a standing start. Sitting on top of all of it is an adverse internal-control opinion, unremediated at 6/30/2026, located precisely at the assumptions behind the acquired-asset fair values that drive reported earnings — a disclosure that has received almost no external attention and therefore is unlikely to be fully in the price.

At 1.81x tangible book, 11.6x consensus that is being cut, 2% from the 52-week high, and 6% from a mean target whose low end sits below spot, the setup offers roughly 20% upside against 25% downside with the base case at today's price. The forward P/E discount to the 15.2x peer median is explained by identifiable impairments rather than mispricing. **REDUCE.** Revisit at $35-36, or earlier on confirmed credit stabilization and control remediation.
