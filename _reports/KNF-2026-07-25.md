**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Knife River is a vertically integrated, aggregates-based construction materials and contracting-services company spun tax-free from MDU Resources in May 2023. Its economic foundation is 1.3 billion tons of permitted aggregate reserves feeding downstream ready-mix concrete, asphalt, and heavy-civil contracting across 15 states. The business benefits from two structural advantages: aggregates are uneconomical to transport far, so quality reserves near mid-sized growth markets carry local pricing power, and roughly 81% of contracting revenue is public-sector, which cushions the cycle. Since separation, total shareholder return of ~201 has outrun the construction-materials peer group's ~122, and the market has rewarded the growth-through-acquisition strategy despite a decline in reported earnings.

The central tension is that revenue is rising while net income is falling. 2025 revenue grew 9% to $3,146M and EBITDA rose to $484M, but net income dropped 22% to $157M. The entire earnings gap sits below the operating line: interest expense rose 48% to $82M on the $500M Term Loan B used to fund the Strata acquisition, and D&A jumped from $137M to $194M (roughly 42%) from new PP&E and purchase-accounting intangibles. Layered on top is a multi-year margin erosion in Energy Services (EBITDA margin 26.7% in 2023 to 16.2% in 2025) and a growing backlog that management repeatedly flags as carrying "lower expected margins." The investment question reduces to whether the acquired assets earn returns above the 5.5–7.75% cost of the debt used to buy them, a payoff that has not yet shown up in consolidated net income.

We rate KNF a HOLD. The near-term public-funding tailwind (11 of 15 states with record 2026 DOT budgets, ~43% of IIJA formula funding still unspent in KNF's markets) is real and supports elevated backlog. But at ~28x trailing earnings on forward estimates that are broadly falling, with an IIJA reauthorization cliff after 2026, seasonally tight liquidity ($13.3M unrestricted cash and $270M drawn on the revolver at Q1 2026), and an M&A model that is amplifying interest and D&A faster than it is compounding net income, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. This is our fourth consecutive HOLD; the decline in Financial Health and Management Quality scores over the trailing period reflects the leverage step-up and the earnings-vs-EBITDA divergence, not a change in the underlying franchise quality.

## Company Overview

Knife River operates a vertically integrated model in which roughly 35% of its aggregate production is consumed internally to feed ready-mix concrete and asphalt, which in turn supply contracting services (paving, site development, precast/prestressed concrete, heavy-civil). Physical assets include 208 active aggregate sites, 135 ready-mix plants, 55 asphalt plants, 9 liquid asphalt terminals, and ~1,150 mixer trucks. The workforce is ~5,298 (peaking near 6,900 in construction season), 11% unionized under 39 collective-bargaining agreements.

The company reports four segments. West (~37% of revenue: AK, CA, HI, OR, WA) is the highest-margin unit at 19.3% EBITDA margin in 2025. Central (~32%: IA, MN, ND, SD, TX) is the fastest-growing, driven by the Strata acquisition. Mountain (~20%: ID, MT, UT, WY) recently added Utah via the Morgan Asphalt deal. Energy Services (~11%) produces and supplies liquid asphalt across 8 states.

No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue; the top 15 customers are ~21% of revenue, nine of which are state DOTs. The competitive set includes Vulcan Materials, Martin Marietta, CRH, Heidelberg Materials, Eagle Materials, Construction Partners, Granite, Cemex, and Amrize, though the industry is highly fragmented and KNF's most common competitors are small private local operators.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue grew from $2,830M (2023) to $2,899M (2024, +2%) to $3,146M (2025, +9%), with the 2025 acceleration largely inorganic (Strata plus four smaller deals) plus mid-single-digit pricing on aggregates and ready-mix. Net income moved the other way: $182.9M (2023, on MD&A's cleaner comparable) to $202M (2024) to $157M (2025), a 22% drop. Operating income fell 10% to $286M and net margin compressed to 5.0% from 7.0%. EBITDA, by contrast, grew from $422M to $454M to $484M, with Adjusted EBITDA margin holding roughly 15.8–16.0%.

**Why the earnings and EBITDA lines diverged.** The gap is almost entirely below operating income. Interest expense rose 48% to $82M following the March 2025 $500M Term Loan B. D&A rose from $137M to $194M (roughly 42%) on new PP&E and acquired intangibles. Purchase accounting added a $3.7M inventory fair-value markup, $12.9M of acquired-intangible amortization in SG&A, and $4.4M of higher transaction costs. Separately, Energy Services EBITDA margin fell from 26.7% (2023) to 21.8% (2024) to 16.2% (2025) as liquid asphalt pricing normalized, roughly halving the margin of what was the crown-jewel segment at spin-off.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt was ~$1,181M at YE2025 (versus $667M long-term debt at YE2024), comprising $425M of 7.75% senior notes due 2031, $756M of term loans (A+B), and an undrawn $500M revolver at year-end. D/E stood at 1.22, current ratio a healthy 2.54, and the Altman Z-Score at 2.43 (grey zone), moderate risk appropriate to a capital-intensive, cyclical model carrying fresh acquisition leverage. ROA was 4.3% and ROE 9.6%, both modest and reflecting the earnings dip against an asset-heavy base.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow declined from $336M (2023) to $322M (2024) to $278M (2025) on acquisition-related working capital. Capex was $348M in 2025, split roughly $170M maintenance / $178M growth. Reported FCF was −$70M, but this is distorted by growth capex and $610M of acquisitions; on a maintenance-capex basis the business remains comfortably cash-generative. The negative headline reflects deliberate, debt-and-cash-funded expansion rather than operational distress.

**Q1 2026.** Q1 revenue was $410M (+16% YoY) with a seasonal net loss of $79M, structural for a northern-weather construction company, where Q1 is always a loss. The gross loss narrowed to $(2.8)M from $(9.6)M, every segment's EBITDA loss improved YoY, and ready-mix gross margin swung positive to 10.7%. The revolver was drawn $270M to fund seasonal working capital and Q1 acquisitions, lifting total debt to ~$1.45B and dropping unrestricted cash to $13.3M (plus $62M restricted at the captive insurer). A 1% rate increase adds roughly $10.2M of annual interest on ~$1.0B of variable-rate debt, a live sensitivity given the revolver draw.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** The track record over four quarters is mixed: two beats, two misses. A large Q2 2025 miss (−37.8%) and a Q3 2025 miss (−3.4%) bracket a strong Q4 2025 beat (+33.2%) and an essentially in-line Q1 2026 (+1.1%). Visibility into any single quarter is limited by weather and DOT-letting timing, which argues for weighting full-year and EBITDA trends over quarterly prints.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term demand backdrop is favorable. Roughly 43% of IIJA formula funding remained unspent in KNF's markets as of early 2026, and 11 of 15 states carry record 2026 DOT budgets (+15% combined), with North Dakota bid lettings roughly doubling to $745–810M. Backlog reached $1.17B at Q1 2026, up ~24% YoY. ASCE's 2025 "D+" road grade and a cited $2.2T roadway funding gap through 2033 underpin a long structural demand case. A new private-sector vector emerged in Q1 2026: Central-segment aggregate volumes rose partly on data-center projects, a modest diversifier away from the public-infrastructure core.

Organic projects add measured growth: a Sioux Falls aggregate expansion online in 2027, a Twin Falls greenfield ready-mix operational in Q2 2026, and a new South Dakota polymer-modified asphalt plant. Management reiterated 2026 maintenance/improvement capex of $170–235M plus ~$130M of organic growth capex.

Two caveats temper the growth thesis. First, backlog is up but management explicitly flags "lower expected margins" on the current book; volume is not the concern, pricing and mix discipline is. Second, and more important for forward estimates, consensus is broadly falling. Current-year EPS has been cut 2.7% over 30 days (to $3.29) even as it rose 2.4% over 90 days, and next-year EPS has been cut 3.4% over 30 days and 1.8% over 90 days (to $3.96). The near-term revision trend is negative. That, combined with revenue growth that is more acquisition-driven than organic, keeps the growth score in the middle of the range.

## Valuation Assessment

At $78.06 the stock trades at ~28x trailing earnings (EPS $2.76) and ~20–24x forward on consensus current-year/next-year estimates of $3.29/$3.96. Against Construction Materials peers, the picture is nuanced. Peer median P/E is 33.2 (MLM 35.4, VMC 33.2, CRH 19.4, EXP 16.5), so KNF's trailing multiple is not a premium to the large-cap aggregates names. But those large-cap multiples reflect metro-market scale, higher through-cycle margins, and cleaner balance sheets. KNF's forward multiple compresses meaningfully because forward EPS is expected to recover from the 2025 dip, but those forward estimates are being revised down, which is the crux of the valuation problem.

The "cheap" signal deserves skepticism. On trailing metrics KNF screens no worse than peers, and the forward multiple looks reasonable if the acquisition payoff materializes. But a low or in-line multiple set against falling forward estimates, a structurally eroding Energy Services segment, a lower-margin backlog, and a post-2026 funding cliff is the profile of a value-trap risk rather than an asymmetric opportunity. The estimate-revision signal sorts the wrong way for a bull case.

Analyst price targets span $81 low to $119 high, mean $103, median $105, well above the current $78. On a stock with rising estimates, we would treat consensus targets as stale floors. Here the opposite applies: estimates are falling, so the wide gap between price and target more likely reflects either lagging targets that will be trimmed or a market that is discounting the leverage and margin risks the sell-side has not yet fully modeled. The stock's ~19% decline from its June $96.28 52-week high, into a period of negative near-term revisions, is consistent with the second interpretation.

We assign a mid-range valuation score. The multiple is not stretched, and the public-funding tailwind supports the base, but there is no identified catalyst forcing an upward re-rating and the forward-estimate direction is unfavorable.

## Competitive Landscape

KNF's competitive position is a genuine strength and the one dimension whose trajectory has improved. The aggregates business is structurally local: transportation economics create regional moats around quality reserves, and KNF's permitted reserves are strategically located near mid-sized, higher-growth markets rather than contested major metros. Vertical integration and multimodal transport (truck/rail/barge) reinforce the position. The public-sector-heavy contracting mix provides revenue that is less cyclical than private construction.

The acquisition strategy, whatever its balance-sheet cost, is expanding this footprint: entry into Utah via Morgan Asphalt, ND/MN scale via Strata, and Montana aggregates/ready-mix/precast additions. Each deal adds reserves and local density in fragmented markets where KNF's most common competitor is a small private operator, not a large public. Gross margin and per-segment EBITDA loss narrowing in Q1 2026 suggests integration and pricing execution are progressing at the operating level even as the consolidated bottom line is weighed down by financing costs. The competitive-position score of 6.8 reflects a franchise that is widening its geographic moat, tempered by the reality that the durability of its largest end-market (public infrastructure) hinges on a federal reauthorization outside the company's control.

## Risk Assessment

**Acquisition-integration and leverage risk (dominant thesis risk).** KNF has acquired 90+ businesses since 1992 and is in an aggressive phase — 5 deals in 2025 ($611M including Strata at $454M) plus 3 more in Q1 2026 ($174M). Goodwill has nearly doubled to $573M. The strategy layers on debt, interest, D&A, and integration execution risk. The 10-K flags that many acquired targets had unaudited financials and could carry undisclosed liabilities and impairment risk. Whether acquired assets earn returns above the debt cost is not yet answered in reported results.

**Public-funding dependency and the IIJA cliff.** The near-term tailwind is the flip side of a concentration risk. The IIJA authorizes through 2026; a delayed or smaller successor bill would reverse the tailwind. This is the single biggest macro variable for a public-sector-heavy model.

**Energy Services margin erosion.** Three consecutive years of liquid-asphalt margin compression is a structural drag partly masked by acquisition-driven revenue growth. Whether pricing stabilizes is a real swing factor for consolidated profitability.

**Seasonality, weather, and liquidity.** Q1 losses are structural; weather is a genuine swing factor (Hawaii flooding, ND weather/DOT timing). Thin unrestricted cash against the revolver draw leaves limited cushion and likely explains the May 18, 2026 financing action flagged in the 8-K index. Roughly $178M of revolver capacity remained.

**Interest-rate/refinancing risk.** ~$1.0B of variable-rate debt with heavy first-half revolver reliance; $10.2M of additional annual interest per 100bps. A higher-for-longer rate path directly raises the cost of the negative-headline-FCF financing model, and any credit downgrade would compound it.

**Backlog margin quality.** Backlog is up but at management-acknowledged lower expected margins, a recurring tension between the "quality-over-quantity" strategy and the reported book.

**Standard sector risks.** Commodity/input inflation (diesel, liquid asphalt, cement, steel — partly pass-through), permitting difficulty, environmental/reclamation liabilities including the Portland Harbor Superfund site (a KNF-Northwest subsidiary is a named PRP), multiemployer pension withdrawal exposure, and California CARB Scope 1&2 reporting beginning 2026 (Scope 3 in 2027).

**Unverified items.** The specifics of the May 18, 2026 material agreement/new financial obligation and the May 21, 2026 officer change could not be retrieved and should be confirmed when filings become accessible.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 60.6% sits well above 1-year historical vol of 48.3% (a rich 12.4pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (60.6% near-term vs 52.7% at 6 months), so the market expects a resolving event, most plausibly the next earnings print into the seasonally strong construction quarter. ATM IV has jumped from 42.7% to 60.6% since late June as the stock fell 15.5% from $92.39 to $78.06. Skew is modestly call-heavy at −3.7% near-term, so positioning reads as elevated uncertainty rather than directional bearishness. For a prospective buyer, rich IV argues against paying up for optionality and favors waiting for the post-print vol crush. Short interest at 7.5% of float (5.3 days to cover) is uncrowded and not a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$115–120).** IIJA reauthorization passes at similar scale, extending the public-funding tailwind past 2026. The 2025–2026 acquisitions deliver the "meaningful volume and margin growth" management projects, driving returns above the debt cost and letting net income re-converge with the rising EBITDA line. Energy Services margins stabilize, data-center private demand diversifies the revenue base, and the leverage built for expansion begins to delever as acquired assets season. Multiple holds near the peer median on recovering EPS. This is roughly the sell-side high target of $119.

**Bear case (~$58–62).** IIJA reauthorization stalls or shrinks, DOT budgets normalize off record levels, and the lower-margin backlog converts to disappointing profitability. Energy Services margins keep eroding, higher-for-longer rates lift variable-debt interest, and one or more acquisitions underperform or trigger goodwill impairment against the goodwill balance. Net income stays depressed, forward estimates continue falling, and the stock retraces toward the 52-week low. A ~20–25% decline from current levels.

**Base case (~$85–90).** The public-funding tailwind carries 2026 backlog conversion, EPS recovers toward the current-year/next-year consensus (which may itself be trimmed), and the acquisition integration progresses at the operating level even as financing costs cap net-income growth. The multiple holds in a 21–23x forward range. Modest upside from the current $78, but not the asymmetric setup a full-conviction buy requires.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The franchise quality and near-term demand support the position, but the combination of falling forward estimates, leverage-driven earnings dilution, and an unresolved IIJA cliff prevents a stronger call in either direction. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the recommendation is unchanged, but the trajectory of declining Financial Health (6.0 to 5.0) and Management Quality (5.5 to 5.2) scores warrants explicit acknowledgment — those reflect the real leverage step-up and the net-income-vs-EBITDA divergence, offset by an improving competitive position (6.5 to 6.8).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the natural checkpoint, testing whether the seasonally strong quarter converts the elevated backlog into margin recovery. The IIJA reauthorization progress through late 2026 is the larger structural checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward EPS estimate-revision trend turns clearly positive (current-year and next-year revised up in consecutive months), signaling the acquisition payoff is reaching net income.
- Evidence that acquired-asset returns exceed the ~6% blended debt cost, visible in consolidated ROIC improvement or Energy Services margin stabilization.
- A credible, similar-scale IIJA successor bill advances, de-risking the post-2026 funding cliff.
- A pullback into the low-to-mid $60s that widens the discount to intrinsic value while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- IIJA reauthorization stalls or a materially smaller bill emerges, undermining the ~81% public-sector revenue base.
- Goodwill impairment against the $573M balance, or a deal that closes/underperforms visibly.
- Backlog conversion at margins below the already-lowered guidance, or Energy Services EBITDA margin falling below ~14%.
- A credit-rating downgrade or a financing action on unfavorable terms that raises the ~$1.0B variable-rate interest burden.

## Conclusion

Knife River owns a real asset base and a local moat in aggregates, and it sits in front of a strong near-term public-infrastructure demand cycle. Since spin-off, the market has rewarded the EBITDA and cash-generation story over reported net income, and TSR has outrun peers. But the equity today is a debt-funded roll-up whose consolidated earnings are being suppressed by the financing used to grow it: 2025 net income fell 22% while revenue rose 9% and EBITDA rose 7%, with the entire gap in +48% interest and higher D&A. Forward estimates are being trimmed, Energy Services margins have halved from spin-off levels, backlog is growing at management-acknowledged lower margins, and the largest end-market faces a reauthorization cliff after 2026. At ~28x trailing and ~20–24x forward on falling estimates, with seasonally thin liquidity and rich near-term implied volatility, the setup is balanced. We maintain a HOLD and would look for a decisive turn in forward estimate revisions, evidence that acquired assets are earning their cost of capital, or a more attractive entry in the low-to-mid $60s before moving more constructive.
