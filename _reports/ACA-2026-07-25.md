**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Arcosa is no longer a fundamental equity story. On June 22, 2026, CRH plc agreed to acquire the company for $150.00 per share in all-cash consideration, an enterprise value of roughly $8.5 billion, with both boards approving the transaction. At the current price of $145.00, the stock trades at a ~3.4% gross discount to the deal price. The investment question has narrowed to deal-completion probability, antitrust clearance (CRH is a direct competitor in aggregates and building materials), the shareholder vote, and time-to-close. Standalone earnings trajectory, estimate revisions, and forward guidance are now secondary; they matter mostly as the definition of the downside if the deal breaks.

The price action confirms this. The stock has been pinned in a tight $144–146 band since June 22 on volumes 4–7x its historical average (the June 22 session traded 4.46 million shares versus a ~353K daily average), the classic signature of merger arbitrage funds establishing positions and index/passive holders exiting. Near-term implied volatility collapsed to 14.6% against a 36.1% trailing realized, and the option chain's backwardation (near-term 14.6% versus 4.9% six-month) reflects the market pricing the binary regulatory/closing outcome into the front end. Short interest at 2.4% of float with 0.9 days to cover is arbitrage-related mechanics, not directional bearishness.

The remaining return profile is asymmetric against the buyer: roughly +3.4% to the deal price versus roughly −10% to the pre-bid standalone value (~$130) if antitrust blocks the transaction. Merger-arb desks with a cost of capital and a view on regulatory timing can underwrite that spread; a fundamental portfolio cannot add value here beyond the arb math. We rate ACA HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the outcome is binary and gated by an antitrust review whose duration and remedy requirements are not yet knowable. Existing holders should hold to close or exit into the arb bid; new capital finds better risk/reward elsewhere.

## Company Overview

Arcosa, spun out of Trinity Industries in 2018 and headquartered in Dallas, is a North American infrastructure-products company. Following a multi-year portfolio reshaping, culminating in the April 1, 2026 close of the barge-business sale to Wynnchurch Capital for ~$450M, it operates two reportable segments:

- **Construction Products** (~$1.31B 2025 revenue, 46% of total, highest-margin segment): natural and recycled aggregates, specialty materials, asphalt, and trench-shoring. The #1 U.S. recycled-aggregates producer with ~1.3 billion tons of proven/probable reserves. End markets skew ~45% infrastructure, ~25% non-residential, ~20% residential; Texas (~35%) and New Jersey (~20%, from the 2024 Stavola deal) dominate.
- **Engineered Structures** (~$1.19B 2025 revenue, 41%): steel/concrete utility transmission and distribution structures, structural wind towers, traffic/lighting structures, and telecom towers, positioned for grid hardening, electrification, and data-center-driven electricity demand.

The strategic thesis Arcosa executed — buy aggregates (Stavola, Ameron, a $60M Florida bolt-on in March 2026), shed cyclical/commoditized units (steel components in August 2024, barges in April 2026), and de-lever with proceeds — is precisely what CRH is paying a premium to acquire. CRH cited the intent to build "an aggregates-led portfolio aligned with infrastructure megatrends."

## Financial Analysis

Arcosa's standalone financials matter as the break scenario, and they show a business that inflected favorably in 2025. Revenue grew from $2.57B (2024) to $2.88B (2025), up 12.2%, driven by the Stavola acquisition (Construction Products +18.6%) and utility/wind volumes (Engineered Structures +13.6%). Profitability accelerated faster than the top line:

- Operating profit rose to $341.9M (+73%) from $197.6M in 2024, though the prior year was depressed by ~$44M of deal/divestiture costs and a steel-components loss.
- Net income reached $208.4M versus $93.7M, a 7.2% net margin.
- Record adjusted EBITDA of $583M (+30%) at a 20.2% margin (+280bps), reflecting the mix shift toward aggregates and utility structures.

The 2025 effective tax rate of 13.6% (versus 27.9% in 2024) was flattered by AMP wind-tower tax credits and lower state taxes, a tailwind that OBBBA partially sunsets after 2027, so normalized standalone earnings power sits somewhat below the reported figure.

The balance sheet is the key standalone risk, and it explains why the aggregates strategy needed a well-capitalized acquirer to fully monetize. The $1.2B Stavola acquisition pushed long-term debt from $562M (2023) to $1.68B (2024), now ~$1.51B. Interest expense nearly quadrupled from $28M (2023) to $109M (2025). The Altman Z-Score of 2.14 sits in the grey zone and D/E is ~0.89. Liquidity is nonetheless solid: current ratio 2.2, a $700M undrawn revolver, no near-term maturities (term loan 2031, notes 2029/2032), and compliance with all covenants (max net leverage 4.0x). Management is de-levering deliberately: $156.5M of term-loan prepayment in 2025, another $83M in April 2026 from barge proceeds.

Cash generation is adequate but not the strength: 2025 operating cash flow of $341M and FCF of $175.5M (6.1% margin). OCF fell from $502M in 2024 on a working-capital build (higher receivables/inventory, lower advance billings). Q1 2026 continuing-operations revenue was $571.7M (+4.4%) with operating profit of $47.1M (+14.9%), utility structures (+15% revenue, +28% operating profit) offsetting a planned wind-tower decline (−21%).

## Growth Analysis

For a standalone Arcosa, the growth picture is a barbell of a genuine secular tailwind against a policy-driven structural decline. The utility/T&D structures business is the engine: backlog grew 35% YoY to $557.6M by Q1 2026, funded by grid hardening, electrification, and data-center electricity demand, with IIJA reauthorization expected by end-2026. Arcosa supplies infrastructure to the AI/data-center theme; it is not itself an AI beneficiary, but the demand pull for transmission structures and non-residential aggregates is real and confirmed across the utility complex.

The offsetting drag is wind towers. OBBBA (enacted July 4, 2025) terminates the AMP tax credit for wind towers sold after 2027 and phases out the wind PTC for projects not under construction by July 4, 2026 or in service by end-2027. Wind-tower backlog already fell 19% YoY to $627.8M (Dec 2025) and to $600M by Q1 2026, reversing a one-time ~$1.1B IRA-driven order surge. Arcosa is mitigating by converting an idled wind-tower plant to utility-structures production (operational H2 2026), which shifts capacity toward the growing end market but does not fully offset the demand cliff.

Estimate revisions reflect this: current-quarter EPS consensus fell 17.4% over 90 days and current-year fell 10.7%, though the 30-day trend has stabilized (+1.8% current year). The four-quarter beat streak (all four beats, including a 116% surprise in Q1 2026) indicates conservative guidance, but the medium-term revision direction is negative on the wind cliff. In a merger-arb frame, these revisions are essentially moot — the deal fixes value at $150 regardless of the standalone earnings path. They matter only in the break scenario, where they modestly pressure the standalone re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is now a merger-arbitrage calculation, not a multiples exercise. Annualizing the 3.4% gross spread depends entirely on the close date; if the deal clears in ~9 months (mid-2027), the annualized gross return is ~4.6%, thin for the antitrust risk given CRH is a direct competitor. Analyst price targets have compressed to a $140–150 range around the deal price ($145.50 mean, $146 median), which is what one expects once a definitive cash agreement is announced — targets converge on the deal price, not on standalone fair value.

The standalone valuation matters as the downside anchor. Pre-bid, ACA traded around $130 in April 2026 with analyst targets clustered near that level. The 32.5x trailing P/E is roughly in line with the peer median of 32.9x; on the peer set (FLR 22.8x, GVA 37.5x, MYRG 46.8x, ECG 32.9x, TTEK 16.6x), the aggregates-led mix would warrant a premium to a pure engineering-services comp, but the leverage (Z-score 2.14) and wind exposure temper it. The break-scenario standalone value sits near the pre-deal $130, roughly 10% below the current price.

We score valuation 5.0. The 3.4% capped upside to the deal price is not asymmetric enough to reward, and the standalone downside is real. The price efficiently reflects a deal-completion probability net of a competitor-antitrust discount rather than a mispricing of intrinsic value. Treating the compressed spread as "cheap" would be a category error; the spread is thin precisely because the market assesses meaningful regulatory risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Arcosa's competitive position is genuinely strong in its core, which is exactly why CRH bid. Aggregates is a fragmented industry (>5,000 U.S. producers) with natural moats: the high transport-to-value ratio localizes competition and makes reserve density in specific geographies durable. Arcosa's Texas and New Jersey positions, plus 1.3 billion tons of reserves, are scale assets that consolidate well into CRH's platform. The utility-structures franchise is riding a confirmed secular tailwind (backlog +35%), and the strategic pivot toward less-cyclical, infrastructure-aligned products has strengthened the business mix over the past several years.

The wind-tower position is structurally impaired by policy, and GE Vernova concentration (12.2% of 2025 revenue, up from 8.1% in 2023, concentrated in the policy-exposed wind business) is a specific vulnerability. On balance, the trajectory of the core aggregates and utility franchises is improving while wind deteriorates — a mix that a strategic acquirer values more than the public market did, evidenced by Arcosa's persistent TSR lag (cumulative 196 versus peer group 239).

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is now deal-specific. Antitrust/HSR review is the gating item — CRH and Arcosa overlap in aggregates and building materials, and a competitor acquisition of this scale invites scrutiny that could require divestitures or extend the timeline into 2027. The July 15, 2026 8-K (Item 1.04) is consistent with the HSR process being underway. Secondary deal risks are the shareholder vote and customary closing conditions, both lower-probability obstacles given board approval.

If the deal breaks, the standalone risk stack reasserts: floating-rate leverage (term loan at SOFR + 2.00%), the OBBBA-driven wind cliff, GE Vernova concentration, $1.35B of goodwill exposed to a wind-down in the Engineered Structures unit, steel-input and tariff uncertainty (Commerce Department wind-tower-component investigation, USMCA review affecting ~1,225 Mexican employees), and Construction Products cyclicality against a softening Texas housing market (permits −8% in 2025). Downside in that scenario is toward ~$130, roughly −10%.

### Options Market Signal

The options market corroborates the merger-arb read. The collapsed near-term IV and front-end backwardation leave only the binary close/break outcome priced. IV jumped 9.7pp since June 22 on a flat price, consistent with the market pricing an imminent catalyst (HSR developments). Balanced skew (−2.1%) and no meaningful put demand indicate the market is not pricing a deal break as the base case. The implied ±3.5% near-term move brackets the gap to the $150 deal price on the upside and a modest partial break on the downside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($150, +3.4%).** The CRH acquisition clears antitrust — potentially with modest aggregates divestitures — and closes at $150/share in cash in late 2026 or 2027. Arb holders capture the full spread plus any small dividend accrual. The strategic logic is clean, both boards approved, financing is not a constraint for CRH, and the shareholder vote at a ~15% premium to pre-deal levels is highly likely to pass.

**Bear case (~$130, −10%).** Antitrust review blocks the deal or CRH walks after remedy demands prove uneconomic. The stock re-rates to its standalone value — a levered, two-segment company with a strong aggregates and utility core but a structurally shrinking wind business, GE Vernova concentration, and negative near-term estimate revisions. Pre-deal analyst targets clustered near $130, defining the floor.

The asymmetry runs against the buyer at $145: ~3.4% up versus ~10% down. That is a professional arb trade, not a fundamental long. The probability-weighted expected value is positive only if one assigns a high (>80%) close probability, which is a judgment about antitrust outcomes for a competitor merger that we do not have sufficient visibility to underwrite with confidence.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the outcome is binary and gated by a regulatory review of unknown duration and remedy scope.

**Reassessment timeline:** HSR clearance or second-request news (near-term, watch for 8-K filings), the shareholder-vote record date and meeting, and any CRH commentary on expected close (management guidance points to late 2026/2027).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- HSR clears or a second request resolves with no material divestitures required, raising close probability above ~90% and making the ~4.6% annualized spread attractive for the remaining timeline.
- The spread widens materially (stock drifts to $140 or below) on a temporary regulatory scare without a genuine deterioration in close probability, improving the risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRH signals it will walk, or antitrust authorities issue a formal challenge/complaint.
- Remedy demands emerge that materially reduce the deal price or introduce a re-cut of terms below $150.

**Exit criteria for existing holders:** Hold to close to capture the spread, or sell into the arb bid at $144–145 if the opportunity cost of capital exceeds the ~4.6% annualized return for the expected timeline. New capital should wait for either a clearer regulatory path (upgrade) or a wider spread that improves the asymmetry.

## Conclusion

Arcosa built a strong aggregates-led infrastructure platform that a strategic acquirer valued at $150/share, crystallizing value the public market had persistently under-awarded (TSR 196 versus peer 239). For shareholders, the CRH bid is the right outcome. For a prospective investor at $145, the trade is now merger arbitrage: ~3.4% gross upside to the deal price against ~10% downside to standalone value if antitrust blocks a competitor acquisition. The price efficiently reflects a close probability net of that regulatory discount, the options market confirms the binary framing (collapsed IV, front-end backwardation, balanced skew), and the volume signature shows arb desks and passive exiters have already repriced the stock.

We rate ACA HOLD. The standalone business is respectable but levered and facing a wind-tower policy cliff, so the break scenario is not benign. The remaining return is thin and capped, the risk is binary and regulatory, and there is no fundamental edge to be added beyond the arb math. Existing holders hold to close; new capital should look elsewhere unless the spread widens or the antitrust path clarifies favorably.
