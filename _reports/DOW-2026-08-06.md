## Executive Summary

Dow reported a large second-quarter earnings recovery: net sales of $12,092M (+20% year over year), Operating EBIT of $1,648M against negative $21M in Q2 2025, GAAP diluted EPS of $0.99 and adjusted EPS of $1.44 versus a $1.28 non-GAAP consensus. The composition is the whole story: sales growth decomposed as local price +20%, currency +1%, volume **-1%**. Within Packaging & Specialty Plastics, price rose 30% while volume fell 4%; polyethylene local price rose more than 40% per the CFO. Asia Pacific volume fell 9% and EMEAI 4%. Dow captured a supply-shock margin windfall created by Strait of Hormuz disruption that removed or delayed competing Middle Eastern supply while its ethane-advantaged U.S. Gulf Coast and Latin American assets kept running. Dow's own updated risk factor concedes there is "no guarantee any such increases will continue."

Management's guidance and the sell side agree the windfall fades. Q3 Operating EBITDA is guided to approximately $1.7B against $2.3B in Q2, a 26% sequential decline built on roughly $0.10/lb of polyethylene margin compression from the June North American settlement. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut **-33.0% over 30 days for the current quarter (to $0.78) and -41.9% for the next quarter (to $0.40)**, with full-year 2026 down 13.3% to $2.55 and 2027 down 13.9% to $1.84. Consensus 2027 sits below the implied H2 2026 run rate, meaning the sell side is modeling a plateau at a depressed level rather than either a collapse or a recovery. That is the mirror image of a positive earnings-momentum signal.

The balance sheet cannot absorb much disappointment. Net debt is $13,579M, or approximately $14.9B including the $1,294M of liabilities carried against the contingently repayable Nova judgment. S&P cut TDCC to BBB-/A-3 on February 18, 2026 and Moody's to Baa3/P-3 on February 27, 2026, both with negative outlooks, one notch above speculative grade at two of three agencies. Excluding the ~$1.0B Nova receipt that ran through operating cash flow in Q1, H1 2026 free cash flow was approximately **$313M against $505M of common dividends and $158M of distributions to noncontrolling interests**. Roughly $1.4B of restructuring cash costs and ~$2.5B of FY2026 capex, including the Fort Saskatchewan Path2Zero build, sit ahead. Against that: $13.5B of liquidity, $8.2B of committed facilities entirely undrawn, a covenant ratio of 0.48 against a 0.70 limit, and no substantive maturities until 2029. Near-term solvency is not the question; earnings durability and distribution coverage are. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Dow Inc. is a Delaware holding company whose sole operating subsidiary is The Dow Chemical Company. It runs manufacturing sites in 29 countries with approximately 32,800 permanent employees at June 30, 2026, down from 34,600 at December 31, 2025. FY2025 net sales were $39,968M.

Six businesses report in three segments. Q2 2026 segment detail:

| Segment | Q2'26 net sales | Q2'26 Operating EBIT | Derived margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Packaging & Specialty Plastics | $6,385M | $1,278M | 20.0% |
| Industrial Intermediates & Infrastructure | $3,166M | $246M | 7.8% |
| Performance Materials & Coatings | $2,361M | $133M | 5.6% |
| Corporate | $180M | $(9)M | — |

P&SP generated 77% of segment Operating EBIT on 54% of sales, holds $32,724M of Dow's $61,585M total assets (53%), and absorbed $548M of $632M Q2 capex (87%). Dow should be modeled as a scale ethylene-to-polyethylene producer with two smaller derivative franchises attached, not as a balanced three-legged specialty chemicals company. The 10-K grounds the competitive claim in "extensive low-cost feedstock positions" and full ethylene-to-polyethylene chain integration, a cost-curve position rather than a differentiation position.

No single customer represented a significant portion of 2025 sales, which is a real structural strength. End-market exposure is nonetheless concentrated in packaging, construction/infrastructure, mobility and consumer durables, all cyclically correlated. Dow also carries meaningful equity-method exposure it does not control, including Sadara (35%, Saudi Arabia) and three Kuwaiti affiliates at 42.5% each; investment in nonconsolidated affiliates was $1,121M at June 30, 2026, against negative $861M carried in other noncurrent obligations, a net investment of $260M.

Karen S. Carter became CEO on July 1, 2026, succeeding Jim Fitterling, who moved to Executive Chair. Carter was previously President of Packaging & Specialty Plastics and COO from December 2024. This was an internal, pre-planned succession; strategy continuity is high, since the Transform to Outperform program was Board-approved on January 26, 2026 while Carter was COO. CFO Jeff Tate remains in seat.

## Financial Analysis

**The downcycle.** Revenue fell 29.8% from $56,902M (2022) to $39,968M (2025). FY2025 pretax income was negative $2,511M and net income negative $2,444M, a -6.1% net margin, driven in part by a $690M goodwill impairment on the Polyurethanes & Construction Chemicals reporting unit and a $303M Latin American asset impairment. Across the same period long-term debt rose $3,151M (+21%) to $17,849M while stockholders' equity fell $4,710M (-23%) to $16,008M. Both sides of the capital structure deteriorated.

**The Q2 reversal, and its cash content.** H1 2026 cash from continuing operations was $2,448M against negative $366M in H1 2025, capex $1,135M, Dow-defined free cash flow $1,313M. The MD&A attributes H1 operating cash flow partly to "a cash receipt from Nova related to a judgment on the ethylene asset matter": CAD $1.4B (≈USD $1.0B) received in March 2026. Excluding it, H1 free cash flow was approximately **$313M**, against $505M of dividends and $158M of NCI distributions: roughly a $350M shortfall on recurring generation.

Q2 in isolation is cleaner, because the Nova money arrived in Q1. Q2 operating cash flow of $1,324M less $632M capex is $692M of free cash flow; less $253M of dividends and $89M of NCI distributions leaves approximately $350M of surplus. Annualizing that quarter would produce ~$2.8B of free cash flow, which is not a defensible forecast: Q2 is seasonally the strongest quarter, working capital was a $1,052M net use in H1 with receivables ballooning from $4,762M to $6,430M on the price spike, Q2 included an unrepeated Taiwan land sale, and restructuring cash outflows are front-loaded ahead.

The more useful frame uses management's own guide. At ~$1.7B of Q3 Operating EBITDA and the H1 cash conversion rate of 76.9%, quarterly operating cash flow lands near $0.9B against ~$0.65B of capex, leaving roughly $0.25B before the ~$253M dividend and ~$85M of NCI distributions: approximately breakeven to modestly negative discretionary free cash flow at the guided run rate. This is a sensitivity, not a projection, but it is built from Dow's guidance and Dow's disclosed conversion rate.

**Distributions to non-shareholders are growing.** The 2025 sale of 49% of Dow InfraCo to a Macquarie-managed fund raised $2,433M of non-dilutive capital, but NCI net income rose to $169M in H1 2026 from $51M, and NCI distributions rose to $171M from $48M. Annualizing gives roughly **$340M/year of consolidated cash flow that belongs to the minority holder**, about one-third of the $1,011M common dividend run rate. Consolidated Operating EBITDA overstates cash available to common shareholders by approximately that amount.

**Liquidity and covenants are the genuine strength.** Cash and committed available liquidity of $13.5B at June 30, 2026. All $8.2B of committed facilities undrawn, including a $5.0B revolver to June 2031. Zero receivables sold under $900M/€400M committed facilities, an entirely unused working-capital lever. Maturities are laddered and small: $107M (2026), $781M (2027), $765M (2028). The binding covenant is indebtedness-to-capitalization ≤ 0.70, tested only above $500M of revolver draws; actual was 0.48.

**Screening scores.** Altman Z recalculated on the June 30, 2026 balance sheet with trailing inputs is 1.50; substituting annualized H1 2026 Operating EBIT lifts it only to ~1.70, still below the 1.81 distress threshold. The binding constraints are structural: asset turnover of 0.67, a $44.2B liability base against $21.4B of market capitalization, and a legacy stack of $4.5B pension/OPEB, $1.0B environmental and $0.66B asbestos. Given $13.5B of undrawn liquidity and no maturities until 2029, this is not a near-term insolvency signal. The sharper warning is the ratings: BBB-/Baa3, both negative. A one-notch downgrade would push Dow's index-eligible paper into high yield and force a mandate-driven repricing of both debt and equity.

**Financial Health: 4.0.** Ample liquidity and covenant headroom against recurring free cash flow that does not cover distributions, junk-adjacent ratings on negative outlook, ~$1.4B of committed restructuring cash outflows, and an off-balance-sheet guarantee now paying cash.

## Growth Analysis

There is essentially no volume growth in this business today. Q2 volume was -1% consolidated, -4% in P&SP, -9% in Asia Pacific and -4% in EMEAI. Growth in Latin America (+9%) and U.S. & Canada (+3%) did not offset it. Revenue also faces a disclosed structural headwind: under the 2021 Aramco agreement, Sadara marketing rights are transitioning toward each partner's equity ownership, which Dow says will reduce reported sales of Sadara products while leaving equity earnings unaffected.

The earnings recovery off the FY2025 loss is real but has two distinct components. The exogenous component, polyethylene price, is the larger one and is already reversing per the June North American settlement. The endogenous component is the self-help stack: the completed $1B 2025 cost program, Transform to Outperform (4,500 role reductions, ~$2B near-term Operating EBITDA target, substantially complete by end-2027), the Barry siloxanes closure, and the Terneuzen restart. Realized T2O benefit was $190M in H1 2026, with roughly 55% of targeted role reductions implemented and total 2026 self-help expected to exceed $1.3B. Headcount corroborates: down ~1,800 in six months. Against $190M of realized T2O benefit, cumulative announced cash costs of roughly $1.4B lie ahead across 2026-2028. The program is credible, front-loaded on cash out and back-loaded on benefit.

The estimate revision picture is decisive and negative. Current quarter -33.0%, next quarter -41.9%, current year -13.3%, next year -13.9%, all on 14-17 analyst coverage. Sequencing $1.30 of H1 2026 adjusted EPS plus $0.78 (Q3) plus $0.40 (Q4) reconciles to approximately the $2.55 current-year figure; the 2027 estimate of $1.84 implies roughly $0.46 per quarter, essentially flat with the Q4 2026 trough. Consensus is not modeling a cyclical recovery in 2027.

One genuine offset: Dow has beaten in each of the last four quarters, by 11.6%, 51.8%, 27.5% and 35.7%, and the Q3 bar has just been cut a third. If that pattern holds against a lowered estimate, Q3 adjusted EPS could print above the $0.78 consensus. The beats have been against sharply reduced expectations rather than against rising ones, which limits how much they should raise confidence in the multi-year path.

Path2Zero at Fort Saskatchewan will add 2 million metric tons of ethylene capacity and decarbonize 20% of Dow's global ethylene base. It is first-quartile cost and carbon-advantaged, which matters as EU carbon and anti-dumping regimes tighten. It also commissions into a chain whose current profitability rests on a supply disruption, and capex/depreciation of 0.82 in H1 2026 implies the non-Alberta maintenance and growth base is being compressed to fund it. RBC's July 1, 2026 downgrade (Outperform to Sector Perform, target $51 to $28) cited exactly this "outsized capex commitment."

**Growth Prospects: 4.0**, down from 5.5. The downgrade reflects the guided sequential EBITDA fade and the revision collapse, both of which post-date the prior assessment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $29.67, market capitalization is $21.4B. Enterprise value is $21.4B + $17,995M gross debt − $3,973M cash − $443M marketable securities ≈ **$35.0B**.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026 consensus $2.55 | 11.6x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $1.84 | 16.1x |
| EV / annualized H1'26 Operating EBITDA ($6,370M) | 5.5x |
| EV / implied FY2026 Op. EBITDA if Q4≈Q3 ($6,585M) | 5.3x |
| EV / FY2025 unadjusted EBITDA proxy ($1,188M) | ~29.5x |
| Price / book ($21.96/share) | 1.35x |
| Forward dividend yield ($1.40) | 4.7% |

The 5.3x-to-29.5x spread compresses the entire debate: the multiple depends almost wholly on whether 2026 or 2025 is representative. Neither is. Both are extremes of a cycle whose swing factor is a geopolitical supply shock.

The disciplined approach is mid-cycle. Give Dow $5.3B of normalized Operating EBITDA, above the FY2024 level, crediting perhaps half the $2B T2O target and the European closures but well below the 2026 windfall run rate. At 6.75x that is $35.8B of EV; subtracting $14.0B of net debt inclusive of the Nova contingency leaves $21.8B of equity, or roughly **$30/share**. At 6.0x the same EBITDA the answer is $24.6; at 7.5x it is $35. The stock is trading within the fair-value band, not below it.

Peer context is of limited use here. Median Materials P/E of 26.8 is distorted by two negative-earnings comparables (IP -7.0x, LYB -29.9x) and two packaging names on different cycles. The relevant comparison is LyondellBasell, the closest structural analogue, which is also loss-making on a trailing basis and 28% below its 52-week high; the polyolefin chain, not Dow specifically, is what the market is derating.

The 4.7% forward yield is the strongest support, and it is not fully secure. The $1.40 annual run rate costs $1,011M; adding ~$340M of NCI distributions gives ~$1,351M of cash out against approximately breakeven discretionary free cash flow at the guided Q3 run rate. Dow has $931M of buyback authorization remaining and repurchased nothing in H1 2026 or H1 2025, while share count rose from ~708.2M to ~722.4M weighted average; the company is not covering dilution, let alone returning the targeted ~65% of Operating Net Income.

Consensus targets: low $29.00, mean $35.69, median $35.00, high $48.00. The low target sits at the current price and RBC's $28 is below it. Analyst sentiment is degrading, with the recommendation distribution shifting to 2 strong buy / 5 buy / 10 hold / 1 strong sell from 3/5/9/1 two months ago. On a name where estimates are falling 13-14% and the multiple is not compressed relative to normalized earnings, targets are not floors.

**Valuation: 4.5.** The stock screens cheap on windfall earnings and roughly fair on normalized earnings, with real downside if the price component fully reverts. Cheapness on non-repeatable earnings is the classic value trap configuration.

## Competitive Landscape

Dow's cost position is improving and its financial position relative to competitors is deteriorating. Named competitors in the ethylene and polyethylene chains include ExxonMobil, Shell, SABIC, Sinopec, CNPC, Chevron Phillips, INEOS, LyondellBasell and Nova; three of four segment competitor lists are dominated by integrated majors with materially larger balance sheets than a BBB-/Baa3 issuer funding a mega-project.

On the improving side: announced closures of the Böhlen ethylene cracker and Schkopau CAV assets by end-2027, the completed Barry U.K. siloxanes shutdown (~25% of European siloxane industry capacity), and the June 2026 restart of Terneuzen, described by the CFO as Dow's lowest-cost, most flexible European cracker. EMEAI long-lived assets are $2,600M of $22,208M and fell $150M in six months. Dow is shrinking its structurally impaired footprint and concentrating on cost-advantaged geographies. Management also cited emerging EU anti-dumping and anti-subsidy support, which would help remaining European derivative assets by restricting imports.

Working the other way: Asian volume fell 9%, management noted Chinese refinery normalization improving regional energy availability and accelerating Asian industrial production, both point toward returning supply. Sadara's outages constrained Dow's own polyurethanes volumes, so the joint-venture impairment is now an operating constraint as well as a financial one. And Dow appears simultaneously as a competitor to, joint-venture partner with, and litigation adversary of Nova Chemicals at the Joffre, Alberta asset, a durable structural conflict.

Commercial progress cited on the Q2 call involves long-term low-carbon agreements with P&G (an end customer) and Univar Solutions (a distributor that resells Dow product). Neither is disclosed as material to revenue.

**Competitive Position: 4.5**, unchanged. Cost-curve trajectory improving; relative financial flexibility and volume share trajectory not.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Polyethylene price reversion.** Every point of Q2's 20% sales growth was price. Management's Q3 guide already embeds ~$0.10/lb of margin compression, and the 10-Q says projecting a quarter ahead "is difficult." Dow explicitly warns near-term price gains carry no guarantee of continuation. The driver, Hormuz disruption, remains ongoing per management commentary, with Hormuz traffic "below historical levels"; [unverified] de-escalation is not established in the sources.

**2. Sadara has become a cash-paying guarantee.** Dow suspended recognition of its share of Sadara equity losses in Q1 2026 because carrying liabilities reached the total of its relevant obligations; the II&I segment's swing to positive $246M is partly attributable to the *absence* of losses previously booked, which the 10-Q states directly. The fair value of TDCC's guarantee of Sadara project financing rose $298M in Q1 2026 to a $450M recorded liability, attributed to adverse conditions "including the ongoing conflicts in the Middle East." TDCC guarantees ~$1.2B of Sadara project-debt principal plus interest and states there is "a more than remote probability that some future performance under the project financing guarantee may be required." Dow remitted cash to satisfy an $80M guarantee obligation in Q2 2026 and issued an $86M letter of credit. Total guarantees: $1,340M maximum future payments against $536M reserved, so incremental unreserved exposure is bounded near $0.8B.

**3. The Nova cash is a liability, not income.** Nova paid ~USD $1.0B in March 2026 plus a ~USD $318M CRA withholding refund received July 15, 2026. Dow recognized only a $40M pretax gain and carries $1,294M of liabilities ($119M current + $1,175M noncurrent) against the disputed damages judgment and fees while Nova's damages appeal is pending. Net debt inclusive of that liability is ~$14.9B rather than the $13.6B headline. Management's stated intent to deploy litigation proceeds toward deleveraging is spending money that may have to be returned; gross debt fell only $166M in six months, so this has not yet appeared in the balances.

**4. Securities litigation targeting dividend statements.** A putative class action filed August 29, 2025 in E.D. Michigan, amended March 31, 2026, names the Company, its then-CEO and its CFO for allegedly false statements "regarding the Company's ability to sustain its dividend." Five derivative actions followed, naming board members, all stayed pending the motion to dismiss. Damages unspecified. The practical consequence is that the current $0.35 quarterly rate is likely to be defended more stubbornly than cash math alone would dictate.

**5. Asbestos claim counts rising against a falling accrual.** Unresolved Union Carbide/Amchem claims rose to 7,490 at June 30, 2026 from 6,552 a year earlier (+14.3%), with H1 filings of 2,131 against 1,799 resolutions, yet the recorded liability fell from $708M to $663M with no adjustment deemed required. This may reflect a shifting severity mix; it may also reflect an actuarial estimate lagging an inflecting filing trend. The disclosure does not distinguish them.

**6. Other legacy items.** Environmental accrual of $1,005M with reasonably possible exposure up to approximately 2.5x that amount. A Texas state complaint filed February 13, 2026 over Seadrift wastewater discharges. A February 2026 Dutch settlement (~$1.2M) for Terneuzen process-safety non-compliance, small in dollars, relevant as a process-safety datapoint. Pension and OPEB of $4,462M.

**7. Ratings.** BBB-/A-3 negative at S&P and Baa3/P-3 negative at Moody's. The short-term downgrades materially narrow the practical commercial-paper investor base, consistent with zero CP outstanding.

**Risk Factors: 3.5.** Individual exposures are bounded and liquidity is ample, but the stack of dated, quantified binaries (a guarantee already paying cash, $1.29B of contingently repayable proceeds, dividend litigation, a junk threshold one notch away) is dense and only partly reflected at 1.35x book.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 56.5% against 44.6% one-year realized is rich by 11.9 points, with the chain in backwardation (56.5% near versus 48.4% long-dated). The 1-week tenor should be discounted as expiry mechanics; the informative readings are the 3-month tenor pricing a ±12.0% move by mid-September and the 6-month at ±19.9%. Implied volatility collapsed from 94.3% on July 23 to 56.5%, a normal post-event crush. Put/call open interest rose from 1.21 to 1.35, the highest of the recent snapshots, while the quoted skew is balanced.

The read: the market prices genuine two-sided uncertainty over the next two quarters, with somewhat more downside protection held in size, and near-term vol expensive relative to realized. For an existing holder, that combination makes covered-call or collar overwrites relatively attractive financing for the position. Short interest of 5.3% of float and 2.7 days to cover means the bearish view is not crowded, so there is little squeeze fuel in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $42 (+41.6%)

Hormuz disruption persists or deepens; Gulf exports stay well below pre-war levels; global polyethylene supply remains constrained through 2027 while Dow's ethane-advantaged U.S. and Latin American assets run above 90% after the Q2 turnaround. Self-help delivers the full $2B T2O target on top of the completed $1B program, and the European closures plus EU anti-dumping measures shift Dow permanently down the cost curve. Operating EBITDA holds near $7.5B; at 6.5x that is $48.8B of EV and roughly $48/share of equity, which is where the analyst high target sits. Dow deleverages with retained Nova proceeds, ratings outlooks stabilize, the dividend is unambiguously covered and the buyback restarts against the $931M remaining authorization. A disciplined bull anchor is the March 31, 2026 high of $42.74.

### Bear Case — $21 (-29.2%)

Verified Hormuz normalization returns Middle Eastern supply; Chinese refinery normalization and recovering Asian industrial production add more; polyethylene margins revert toward 2025 conditions. 2027 Operating EBITDA lands near $4.5B despite self-help. At 6x that is $27B of EV against ~$14B of net debt, leaving roughly $18.50/share; a dividend reduction to preserve the ratings adds sentiment damage before it adds balance-sheet credit. Working capital releases cash on the way down, which cushions liquidity but confirms the earnings quality problem. Nova wins its damages appeal and Dow returns up to $1.29B. Sadara requires further guarantee performance. A downgrade to high yield forces IG-mandate selling of Dow paper and compresses the equity multiple. The 52-week low of $20.40 was printed one year ago on far worse trailing earnings, so $21 is not an extreme.

### Base Case — $31 (+4.5%)

Prices give back most of the windfall over 2026-2027 but land above 2025 troughs; self-help delivers roughly half the T2O target; normalized Operating EBITDA settles near $5.3B. At 6.75x that is $35.8B of EV and ~$21.8B of equity, or approximately $30-31/share. The dividend is maintained at $1.40 and consumes essentially all discretionary free cash flow. Deleveraging is nominal. Total return is the yield.

Probability-weighted, the distribution is roughly symmetric in magnitude and skewed negative in likelihood, because the guided path and the revision trend both point down while the bull case requires the persistence of an event neither Dow nor anyone else forecasts.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in late October 2026, which tests the ~$1.7B Operating EBITDA guide, the >90% Hydrocarbons & Energy operating-rate target, and the H2 self-help uplift of >$200M from role reductions plus ~$50M from site transformations. Secondary checkpoints: any 8-K on ratings actions, the motion-to-dismiss ruling in the E.D. Michigan class action, and quarterly Sadara guarantee-liability disclosure.

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of **volume-led** growth — consolidated volume +2% or better with P&SP volume positive — confirming demand rather than price is driving results.
2. Recurring free cash flow, excluding litigation receipts, covering the ~$1,011M dividend plus ~$340M of NCI distributions across two consecutive quarters, together with resumption of buybacks sufficient to at least offset dilution.
3. A ratings outlook revision to stable at S&P or Moody's, or an upgrade off the BBB-/Baa3 threshold, combined with gross debt reduction of $1B or more.
4. Price toward $23-24 with forward estimates stabilized, which would put the stock near 5x normalized EV/EBITDA and restore genuine asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. A dividend reduction, or a Q3/Q4 print in which discretionary free cash flow after NCI distributions is negative with no offsetting working-capital release.
2. A downgrade to speculative grade at S&P or Moody's, which would force index-mandate selling of Dow paper and re-rate the equity.
3. Q3 Operating EBITDA below approximately $1.5B against the ~$1.7B guide, or a further 10%+ cut to 2027 consensus below $1.65.
4. Sadara guarantee performance exceeding the $536M already reserved, or an adverse Nova appeal outcome requiring return of the ~$1.0B received.

**For existing holders:** the 4.7% yield plus rich near-term implied volatility makes overwriting near $34-36 an efficient way to hold the position through the Q3 print. New capital should wait for either volume confirmation or a materially better entry.

## Conclusion

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on Dow, and the overall score moves from 4.7 to 4.15. The reduction is not a response to price — the stock is down only about 5% since the last review. It reflects two specific pieces of new fundamental information. First, management guided Q3 Operating EBITDA to approximately $1.7B against $2.3B delivered in Q2, quantifying the fade in the price windfall that produced the entire Q2 beat. Second, consensus EPS was cut 33.0% for the current quarter and 41.9% for the next in the space of thirty days, with 2027 marked down to $1.84 — below the implied H2 2026 run rate. Estimate momentum of that magnitude and direction has consistently mattered more than a single beat, particularly when the beat came from an exogenous, non-repeatable source and against an estimate that had already been reduced.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than an AVOID is the two-sidedness of the dominant variable and the strength of the liquidity position. Hormuz normalization is not verified; Gulf exports remain below pre-conflict levels; if the disruption persists into 2027 Dow prints $2B+ EBITDA quarters and the stock re-rates toward the March high. Meanwhile $13.5B of liquidity, $8.2B of entirely undrawn facilities, a 0.48 covenant ratio against a 0.70 limit, and no substantive maturities until 2029 mean there is no near-term forced event. The self-help program is real, is Dow-controlled, and has already removed ~1,800 positions with more than $1.3B of 2026 savings expected.

What prevents a BUY is that at $29.67 the stock is priced at roughly mid-cycle fair value — 16.1x next-year consensus, 1.35x book, ~6.75x a $5.3B normalized EBITDA — while carrying BBB-/Baa3 ratings on negative outlook, a dividend that recurring cash flow did not cover in the first half once the Nova receipt is excluded, ~$1.4B of restructuring cash costs and ~$2.5B of capex ahead, a guarantee obligation that has begun paying cash, and $1.29B of proceeds subject to a pending appeal. Own it for the yield if you already do, hedged if you can be paid for it. There is no asymmetry here for new capital.
