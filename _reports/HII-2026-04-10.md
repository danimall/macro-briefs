## Executive Summary

Huntington Ingalls Industries is the largest military shipbuilder in the United States and the sole manufacturer of nuclear-powered aircraft carriers. No competitor can replicate its nuclear shipbuilding capabilities within any reasonable timeframe. HII's $53.1B backlog (up 9% YoY), combined with a FY2027 defense budget request of $65B for shipbuilding and $29B+ in mandatory maritime industrial base funding, establishes a demand floor that extends well into the 2030s.

The investment case turns on the tension between an extraordinary competitive moat and persistent execution challenges. Newport News Shipbuilding has experienced significant cost overruns on pre-COVID aircraft carrier and submarine contracts, producing -$126M in net cumulative catch-up adjustments in 2024 and -$28M in 2025. These adjustments, combined with labor throughput issues and percentage-of-completion accounting opacity, create earnings volatility that the market penalizes. The stock dropped 11% in February 2026 after management guided Q1 2026 free cash flow to negative $600M — a timing issue, not a structural one, as FY2026 FCF guidance remains $500-600M.

At 26x trailing earnings and ~23x FY2026 consensus ($17.39), HII trades at a discount to peer median P/E of 42x and below its own 52-week high of $460. With four consecutive earnings beats, improving throughput metrics (14% improvement in 2025, targeting 15% in 2026), and the most supportive shipbuilding budget environment in decades, the risk/reward favors buyers willing to tolerate quarterly cash flow lumpiness.

## Company Overview

HII operates through three segments:

**Newport News Shipbuilding** ($6.5B revenue, 52% of total) is the sole designer, builder, and refueler of U.S. Navy nuclear aircraft carriers and one of two builders of nuclear submarines (Virginia-class SSN-774 and Columbia-class SSBN-826). Active programs include CVN-80 (Enterprise) and CVN-81 (Doris Miller) carriers, plus Virginia/Columbia submarine construction modules.

**Ingalls Shipbuilding** ($3.1B revenue, 25%) builds surface combatants (Arleigh Burke-class DDG-51 destroyers) and amphibious assault ships (America-class LHA-6, San Antonio-class LPD-17), with 11+ destroyers under contract.

**Mission Technologies** ($3.0B revenue, 24%) provides IT services, C5ISR, cyber operations, and unmanned systems — primarily service revenue (~91%) with improving margins (3.7% → 5.0% over two years).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory

Revenue grew 8.2% in 2025 to $12.5B after a flat 2024 ($11.5B), with Q4 2025 at $3.48B representing a 15.7% YoY acceleration. Management's medium-term target of $16B+ by 2030 implies a ~6% CAGR from the 2025 base, underpinned by the backlog — of which approximately $11.2B (21%) is expected to convert to revenue in 2026.

### Margin Profile

Percentage-of-completion accounting on multi-year ship programs produces significant volatility via cumulative catch-up adjustments:

| Year | Net Catch-Ups | Seg. Op. Margin |
|------|--------------|-----------------|
| 2023 | +$118M | 7.3% |
| 2024 | -$126M | 5.0% |
| 2025 | -$28M | 5.7% |

Newport News margins collapsed from 6.2% (2023) to 4.1% (2024) before recovering to 5.1% (2025). Ingalls fell from 13.2% to 7.6% and held. Mission Technologies improved steadily to 5.0%. Management guides 2026 shipbuilding margins at 5.5-6.5%, implying continued recovery. The key question is whether "challenged pre-COVID contracts" — management's own language — are largely flushed. The trajectory from -$126M to -$28M in catch-ups is encouraging but not yet conclusive.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow swung from near-zero ($40M) in 2024 to $800M in 2025 — a 20x improvement driven by favorable working capital timing ($170M source vs. $260M use) and higher collections. The 6.4% FCF margin exceeds the 4.8% net margin, supported by non-cash pension benefits ($190M in 2025, projected $213M in 2026) and depreciation exceeding maintenance capex.

The Q1 2026 negative $600M FCF guidance reflects Q4 2025 cash collections pulled forward. FY2026 FCF guidance of $500-600M implies a step-down from 2025's $800M as growth capex rises to $500-600M (from $402M in 2025) for the Charleston facility and capacity expansion.

### Balance Sheet

Debt/equity of 1.51x is elevated but manageable against the backlog. Long-term debt of $2.7B includes $500M at 5.353% due 2030 and $500M at 5.749% due 2035, issued November 2024. The Altman Z-score of 2.14 (grey zone) reflects the capital-intensive nature of shipbuilding rather than acute financial distress. Pension plans are well-funded at 126%, generating a $190M annual non-operating benefit. Goodwill and intangibles of $3.3B (26% of assets) are concentrated in Mission Technologies acquisitions.

### Capital Allocation

No share repurchases occurred in 2025, despite $3.8B of remaining authorization through 2028, signaling management is prioritizing capacity investment over buybacks — strategically sound given the backlog, but it limits near-term capital return. The dividend ($5.43/share, ~1.4% yield) has been consistently increased.

## Growth Analysis

### Demand Environment

The demand backdrop for naval shipbuilding is the strongest in a generation:

- FY2027 defense budget requests $65B for shipbuilding: 2 Virginia-class submarines, 1 Columbia-class submarine, 34 total ships (double the FY2026 request)
- FY2026 NDAA authorized $900.6B in national security funding with $26B for shipbuilding
- $29B+ in mandatory shipbuilding and maritime industrial base funding
- $1.5B specifically appropriated for the Maritime Industrial Base

The Columbia-class submarine — carrying ~70% of the nation's deployed nuclear warheads — is the Pentagon's #1 acquisition program, providing a programmatic floor beneath HII's revenue that is essentially immune to budget fluctuations.

### Throughput and Capacity

HII achieved 14% throughput improvement in 2025 and targets 15% in 2026. Outsourced hours doubled in 2025 with 30% additional growth targeted for 2026. The new Charleston facility is expanding. The USS Boise overhaul cancellation (April 2026), while a contract loss, is margin-accretive — it reallocates scarce submarine-qualified workers from a cost-overrun legacy program to new construction with better economics.

### Revenue Growth Path

Consensus FY2026 EPS of $17.39 and FY2027 of $20.35 imply 13-17% annual EPS growth, driven by revenue scale-up toward the $16B target and margin normalization as pre-COVID contracts roll off. The four consecutive quarterly beats (average 14.3% surprise) provide confidence in forward estimates, though 90-day revisions for the current quarter show a -4.5% decline, suggesting some analyst caution.

## Competitive Landscape

**Sole-source capabilities:** No other company in the world builds or refuels nuclear aircraft carriers. No new entrant could replicate this capability in less than a decade at a cost of tens of billions. For submarine construction, HII shares the work with General Dynamics' Electric Boat division in a teaming arrangement mandated by national security requirements.

**Industrial base fragility benefits the incumbent:** The 10-K describes "a fragile supplier base that has trended toward exclusive providers." This cuts both ways — it creates supply chain risk but also means the government has strong incentives to support HII's financial health and capacity. Congress has repeatedly demonstrated this through supplemental funding and industrial base appropriations.

**Limited surface ship competition:** Bath Iron Works (GD) competes for DDG-51 destroyers, but Ingalls also has 11+ destroyers under contract and holds exclusive positions on amphibious ships.

**Mission Technologies differentiation:** While this segment faces more competition, it benefits from integration with shipbuilding customers and holds classified technology positions in C5ISR and unmanned systems.

## Risk Assessment

### Execution Risk on Legacy Contracts (HIGH)
The -$126M catch-up adjustment in 2024 wiped out substantial shareholder value. While the -$28M in 2025 shows improvement, management acknowledges "ongoing performance challenges" at Newport News. Another large negative adjustment would reset the margin recovery timeline and likely send the stock back toward the $300-350 range.

### Cash Flow Lumpiness (MODERATE-HIGH)
The negative $600M Q1 2026 FCF guidance illustrates the working capital volatility inherent in billion-dollar ship deliveries. The February 2026 earnings reaction (-11%) demonstrates this risk is real and poorly tolerated by the market.

### Labor and Throughput (MODERATE)
~45% unionized workforce with 13 collective bargaining agreements. Hiring, training, and retaining skilled nuclear shipbuilders remains the binding constraint on revenue growth. Inexperienced workers have contributed to rework and cost overruns.

### DOGE / Government Efficiency (LOW-MODERATE)
Mission Technologies has experienced some contract renegotiations. Core shipbuilding programs, as the nation's nuclear deterrent infrastructure, are effectively insulated from budget-cutting exercises.

### Inflationary Environment (MODERATE)
With 49% of revenue on fixed-price contracts in an environment of elevated inflation, HII bears cost risk on active programs. Diesel and material costs are rising. The company has filed requests for equitable adjustments, but recovery is uncertain and slow.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 34.2% sits only 1.7pp above 1-year historical volatility of 32.5%, indicating the market views current uncertainty as fairly priced. The IV percentile at 2% suggests options are cheap relative to recent ranges. Put/call skew of 11% indicates moderately elevated put demand, consistent with lingering concern from the Q1 2026 FCF guidance shock. The contango term structure (34.2% near → 39.7% long-term) reflects no imminent catalyst anxiety. With only 263 contracts across expirations, liquidity is thin, limiting the informativeness of the options signal. The relatively low put/call OI ratio (0.06) suggests institutional hedging has not increased materially. Short interest at 3.5% of float with 2.6 days to cover is unremarkable.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($460-500)

If shipbuilding margins reach the high end of guidance (6.5%) and Mission Technologies continues improving, FY2027 EPS could approach $22-23. At 22x forward earnings, the stock reaches $484-506. The USS Boise cancellation frees scarce workers for higher-margin new construction. The pending FY2027 budget request ($65B for shipbuilding, double the prior year) provides a multi-year demand catalyst not yet fully priced. A return to share repurchases from the $3.8B authorization would provide additional support.

### Bear Case ($300-330)

Another large negative cumulative catch-up adjustment ($100M+) on submarine or carrier programs at Newport News would collapse margins back toward 2024 levels (5.0% segment operating margin). If throughput improvements stall — plausible given the tight labor market and complexity of nuclear shipbuilding — revenue growth could disappoint despite the backlog. Rising capex ($500-600M in 2026) without corresponding buybacks depresses capital returns. Government efficiency initiatives could pressure Mission Technologies margins. In this scenario, FY2026 EPS would come in at $15-16, and the stock at 20x would trade at $300-320.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, gating on: (a) Q1 2026 earnings (April/May 2026) — confirms or refutes FCF timing thesis, (b) FY2027 NDAA passage (expected fall 2026) — locks in shipbuilding appropriations, (c) mid-year 2026 margin trajectory — demonstrates whether pre-COVID contracts have largely rolled off.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Cumulative catch-up adjustments exceed -$100M in any single quarter, indicating material new cost overruns on legacy programs
2. FY2026 full-year FCF comes in below $400M (well below the $500-600M guidance range), suggesting structural rather than timing-related cash flow weakness
3. Management reduces the 2026 shipbuilding margin guidance range below 5.5% on the low end

## Conclusion

HII combines an irreplaceable competitive position, extraordinary demand visibility, and a valuation discount driven by near-term execution concerns. The stock trades at 23x FY2026E earnings vs. a peer median of 42x, a gap that overstates the margin risk given the improving catch-up adjustment trajectory and the strongest shipbuilding budget environment in decades. Elevated defense spending driven by the Iran conflict, Indo-Pacific naval competition, and bipartisan support for shipbuilding provides a demand floor extending well beyond 2030.

The primary risk is execution: can HII train enough workers, improve throughput, and flush legacy contract problems without additional large write-downs? The 2024-to-2025 trajectory (catch-ups improving from -$126M to -$28M, FCF from $40M to $800M, revenue growth accelerating to 8.2%) suggests yes, though confirmation is required over the next two to three quarters.

At $394, with a base case target of $450-460 and a well-defined invalidation framework, HII warrants accumulation for portfolios positioned for the multi-year defense spending cycle. The thin 1.4% dividend yield and quarterly FCF lumpiness make this an appreciation play rather than an income holding. Size conservatively (2-3% of portfolio) given the earnings opacity and monitor Q1 2026 results closely.
