**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Elbit Systems is delivering one of the cleanest fundamental stories in global defense. Q1 2026 revenue grew 15.5% YoY to $2.19B, GAAP net income rose 50% to $160.8M, and non-GAAP operating margin crossed 10% for the first time in the company's history. The order book reached a record $30.2B (up from $28.1B at year-end 2025), equal to roughly 4.4x annual revenue, with 71% sourced outside Israel. This backlog converts the European rearmament cycle into multi-year revenue visibility and marks the company's transition from an Israel-centric supplier into a globally diversified defense provider. The company has beaten EPS estimates by 15-24% in each of the last four quarters, and consensus EPS for the current year has been revised up 12.4% over 90 days.

The constraint is price. At ~$830 against trailing GAAP EPS of $11.39, the stock trades at ~67x trailing earnings, ~53x the current-year consensus of $15.59, and ~46x the next-year consensus of $18.06. These are premium multiples even for a high-quality compounder growing earnings 25-40%; they price in continued flawless backlog conversion, sustained margin expansion, and an uninterrupted defense-spending cycle. The stock has risen ~97% over the trailing twelve months and sits roughly 18% below its 52-week high of $1,016.06 (reached March 17, 2026). The fundamental momentum is real and broad-based, but the valuation removes the asymmetry that would justify a high-conviction long at the current entry.

We rate ESLT a HOLD with a growth bias. The business quality, backlog visibility, and estimate-revision velocity are all consistent with continued appreciation, and we would not be short. The absence of valuation support and the concentration of execution, geopolitical, and currency risk into a multiple that prices perfection prevent a BUY at ~$830. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$700s, where the next-year multiple compresses toward 40x and the risk/reward turns asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Elbit Systems is Israel's largest defense company and one of the world's leading defense-electronics suppliers, operating as an integrated provider across land, sea, and air domains. Its portfolio spans aerospace (military aircraft systems, UAVs, electro-optics, training and simulation), C4ISR/ISTAR (command, control, communications, intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance), land systems (artillery, armored-vehicle systems, munitions, soldier systems), electro-optics (Elop — night vision and targeting), electronic warfare, naval systems, and a substantial U.S. subsidiary (Elbit Systems of America) serving the Pentagon.

The company carries a ~$39B market capitalization on ~46.4M shares and generated FY2025 revenue of $6.83B. As a foreign private issuer, Elbit files Form 20-F annual reports and Form 6-K interim reports rather than the standard 10-K/10-Q structure. Its competitive positioning rests on deep in-house R&D, combat-proven systems validated in active Israeli operations, and a stated strategy of anticipating customer needs rather than waiting for requirements to be defined. The most recent FY2025 20-F was filed March 17, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

The profitability trajectory has inflected sharply. Net income progressed from $215M (2023) to $321M (FY2024) to $534M (FY2025), more than doubling in two years. Operating income rose from $369M to $489M to $671M over the same period, and gross margin expanded from ~24.8% in 2023 to ~28.3% in the latest full year. Diluted EPS moved from $4.82 (2023) to $7.18 to $11.39.

Q1 2026 extended the acceleration. Revenue of $2,188.8M grew 15.5% YoY and beat the ~$2.14B consensus. GAAP net income of $160.8M (+50%) carried a 7.3% margin; non-GAAP net income of $186.4M (+59%) carried 8.5%. GAAP operating margin reached 9.4% (up from 7.9%), and non-GAAP operating margin surpassed 10% for the first time. GAAP diluted EPS of $3.34 rose 42%; non-GAAP EPS of $3.87 (vs. $2.57 prior year, +51%). Operating cash flow of $281.0M (vs. $183.6M YoY) exceeded net income, confirming that earnings are cash-backed, a meaningful quality marker for a long-cycle contractor where percentage-of-completion accounting can otherwise inflate reported profit. Financial expenses fell to $32.2M from $39.0M on lower average debt.

The balance sheet is sound and strengthening. Total assets are ~$12.7B against stockholders' equity of ~$4.13B (up from $3.28B a year prior). Cash rose to ~$635M from ~$265M. Long-term debt is modest for a contractor of this scale, and management notes average debt is declining. Capex runs ~$226M (~3% of revenue) while net R&D is ~$600M (~8.8% of revenue) and rising, a deliberate investment to fund next-generation offerings. FY2026 guidance calls for mid-teens revenue growth, capex of ~3% of revenue, and continued margin expansion under "strict capital discipline."

The earnings-surprise record reinforces confidence in forward estimates: beats of 23.7%, 20.5%, 16.2%, and 15.7% across the last four quarters. A four-for-four beat streak of this magnitude justifies treating consensus as conservative rather than aspirational.

*Data note:* The structured XBRL feed double-counts FY revenue ($6,827,871,000 for both 2024 and 2025). The correct read is FY2025 net income ~$534M / diluted EPS $11.39 as the latest full year. The company is growing double digits, not flat.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is anchored in the backlog, equal to ~4.4x annual revenue, with ~49% scheduled for delivery in 2026-2027. This is the single most important forward indicator and de-risks the mid-teens FY2026 revenue guidance. The backlog grew $2.1B sequentially in a single quarter, and the company added a $1.4B five-year European modernization contract on the same day it reported Q1 results, alongside a U.S. night-vision binocular development win.

Estimate revisions confirm broad-based momentum: current-year EPS consensus rose 12.4% over 90 days, next-year rose 14.7%, and current-quarter rose 10.7% (to $3.68). Revisions are positive across every tenor. Under the framework where +30% 90-day revisions plus >30% revenue growth flags a strong inflection signal, ESLT does not fully clear that bar: revenue growth is mid-teens rather than >30%, and revision velocity is ~12-15% rather than >30%. The signal here is solid positive momentum, not the rare hyper-inflection that would override valuation concerns.

The structural driver is durable. Global defense spending is rising across multiple independent vectors: European rearmament post-Ukraine, NATO budget increases, and sustained Middle East demand. Elbit's 71% international backlog demonstrates it is capturing this beyond its home market, transforming a previously Israel-concentrated franchise into a diversified global supplier. Management characterizes demand as "well above historical levels." The combination of margin expansion (operating leverage as production scales), deleveraging (falling interest expense), and revenue growth is compounding EPS at a 40%+ GAAP rate.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is the binding constraint and the reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY. At ~$830:
- ~67x trailing GAAP EPS ($11.39)
- ~53x current-year consensus ($15.59)
- ~46x next-year consensus ($18.06)

Even adjusting for the cyclical inflection (ESLT genuinely warrants forward rather than trailing analysis given rising estimates), the forward multiples sit at a clear premium to the U.S. defense primes. Elbit's faster growth (mid-teens revenue, 25-40% EPS) justifies a premium, but a 46x next-year multiple against ~16% revenue growth implies a PEG well above 1.5 on revenue terms and roughly 1.2-1.5 even on the faster EPS line. The premium is defensible but not cheap. [Peer forward multiples for Lockheed, Northrop, General Dynamics, and RTX are not available in the provided data — unverified.]

The estimate-revision-as-floor logic applies to inflection stocks trading below ~30x forward with PEG <1.5. ESLT trades at 46-53x forward, which sits firmly in the premium-multiple guardrail zone. At these levels, analyst targets and the share price reflect the same growth extrapolation rather than independent confirmation: the mean target of $880 and median of $950 embed continued backlog conversion, and the high target of $1,075 prices a return toward the all-time high. At premium valuations the risk is asymmetric: beats are expected (limited re-rating), while any miss or cooling of the spending cycle produces sharp de-rating.

There is no absolute-yield support (dividend yield ~0.5%) and no specific identifiable mispricing. The stock is not a value trap, as fundamentals fully justify the direction of travel, but it offers no margin of safety at the current entry. A valuation score of 5.0 reflects a fairly-to-fully-priced stock where the market is approximately right, not wrong.

## Competitive Landscape

Elbit competes with U.S. primes (RTX, Lockheed, Northrop, L3Harris) in electro-optics and C4ISR, and with European players (Leonardo, Rheinmetall, Thales, Saab, BAE) across land and air systems. Its differentiated strengths are UAVs, electro-optics, soldier and land systems, and cost-competitive, combat-proven integration. The "battle-tested" positioning is a genuine moat in a market where validated systems command procurement preference, and the company's stated use of automation, robotics, and AI to scale production is a tangible competitive lever as it converts backlog.

The competitive trajectory is improving, not merely stable. The shift to 71% international backlog demonstrates share gains outside the home market, and the $1.4B European modernization win plus the U.S. night-vision contract show penetration into both NATO-Europe and Pentagon programs. Gross margin expansion against a backdrop of rapid revenue growth indicates pricing power and operating leverage rather than competitive compression. The primary competitive risk is reputational: European arms-export scrutiny and divestment campaigns targeting suppliers of weapons used in active conflict could constrain access to certain markets.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic and geopolitical concentration.** As Israel's largest defense firm, ESLT is exposed to the regional conflict as both a demand tailwind and an operational risk: reservist mobilization of its workforce, supply-chain disruption, and reputational scrutiny. A de-escalation that cools the regional demand surge, or an escalation that disrupts operations, both cut against the stock.

**Defense-cycle dependency.** The 46x next-year multiple prices an uninterrupted European rearmament cycle. Any sign that NATO budget momentum is cooling, or that procurement timelines are slipping, would compress the multiple regardless of current backlog.

**Execution and scaling risk.** Converting a record backlog requires rapid production scaling. Labor constraints, supply-chain bottlenecks, or fixed-price contract cost overruns (a structural feature of long-duration defense programs amid inflation) could pressure margins or delay deliveries, and at this multiple, an execution stumble would be punished.

**Currency.** Costs are substantially shekel-denominated while a large share of revenue is USD/EUR; a strong shekel compresses margins.

**Reputational/export-control.** Arms-export restrictions by foreign governments, divestment campaigns, and contract cancellations in certain European markets are live risks for a supplier of systems used in active conflict.

These risks are partly but not fully priced. The valuation embeds flawless execution, so the idiosyncratic downside catalysts carry more weight than they would in a cheaper stock, hence a risk score of 5.5.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$1,075).** Backlog converts on schedule, non-GAAP operating margin continues climbing past 10%, and the European rearmament cycle sustains double-digit revenue growth through 2027. Next-year EPS of $18.06 proves conservative (consistent with the four-quarter beat streak), and the market sustains a 55-60x multiple on a unique combat-proven, internationally diversified franchise. The stock retests its $1,016 high and pushes toward the $1,075 high target, ~29% above current levels.

**Bear Case (~$520-560).** Defense-spending momentum cools, or an execution stumble (production scaling, fixed-price overrun, currency drag) produces a missed quarter for the first time in the streak. The premium multiple compresses toward the U.S.-prime range. On $15.59 current-year EPS at ~33x, the stock falls toward $515 (the analyst low target of $518), roughly 38% downside. A reputational/export shock in Europe would accelerate this.

**Base Case (~$880-950).** Backlog converts at guidance, mid-teens revenue growth and margin expansion continue, and estimates drift higher as analysts catch up. The multiple holds roughly where it is. The stock tracks toward the $880 mean / $950 median targets over 12 months, ~6-14% upside, positive but not asymmetric given the ~38% bear-case downside.

The asymmetry favors patience: ~6-14% base-case upside against ~38% bear-case downside is a roughly 1:3 to 1:6 risk/reward at the current entry. That ratio improves materially on any pullback.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (expected late August 2026), with the backlog figure and margin trajectory as the key data points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price pullback toward the low-$700s, compressing the next-year multiple toward ~40x and restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
2. Backlog continuing to grow sequentially (above $30.2B) with non-GAAP operating margin sustaining above 10% for a second consecutive quarter, confirming the margin inflection is structural rather than a single-quarter mix effect.
3. Next-year EPS consensus revised above $19 on continued European contract wins, which would lower the effective forward multiple even at the current price.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A quarterly revenue or EPS miss that breaks the four-quarter beat streak, particularly if driven by execution (production delays, fixed-price overruns) rather than timing.
2. Backlog declining sequentially, or evidence that European procurement timelines are slipping, the leading indicator that the demand cycle is cooling.
3. A material reputational/export-control event (a major European contract cancellation or government-imposed export restriction) that impairs the 71% international book.

## Conclusion

Elbit Systems is executing at a high level: record backlog, accelerating margins, four consecutive double-digit earnings beats, and rising estimates across every tenor. The business quality and growth durability are not in question, and the transformation into a globally diversified defense provider (71% international backlog) genuinely mitigates the historical Israel-concentration risk. We would not be short and would not avoid the name.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: at ~46x next-year and ~53x current-year GAAP EPS, the stock prices in flawless multi-year backlog conversion and an uninterrupted European rearmament cycle, with no margin of safety. The base case offers ~6-14% upside to the $880-950 analyst targets, while the bear case (a single execution stumble or a cooling defense cycle de-rating the multiple) implies ~38% downside to ~$515. That 1:3-to-1:6 risk/reward is unattractive at the current entry. The estimate-revision momentum (~12-15% over 90 days) is real but does not reach the hyper-inflection threshold that would override premium-multiple valuation concerns, and the stock sits firmly in the >30x forward zone where rising estimates alone do not constitute a buy signal. We rate ESLT a growth-oriented HOLD and would upgrade to BUY on a pullback toward the low-$700s or on confirmation that the margin inflection is structural over a second quarter.
