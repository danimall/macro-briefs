**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Columbia Sportswear presents a divided picture. The headline financials show a multi-year operating-margin collapse from 11.3% in FY2022 to 6.1% in FY2025, with net income falling from $311M to $177M over the same span. Beneath that, two offsetting dynamics are at work: the U.S. core business (58% of revenue) is structurally weakening, declining 4% in FY25 and 10% in Q1'26, while international segments are growing rapidly and at higher margins: EMEA up 13% in FY25 and 35% in Q1'26, LAAP up 9% and 5% respectively. The investment question is whether the ACCELERATE brand-elevation strategy can arrest the U.S. decline before international growth runs out of offset.

The forward earnings picture has improved enough to justify holding rather than avoiding. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 13.1% over 90 days to $3.85, and next-year up 10.5% to $4.22, against a forward P/E of 15.9x, which is not a premium multiple. The company has beaten estimates in three of the last four quarters, including an 80.7% beat in Q1'26 and a 42.2% beat in Q4'25, though these were partly aided by tariff and working-capital timing rather than clean operating acceleration. The balance sheet is a genuine differentiator: zero funded debt, ~$791M of cash and short-term investments at year-end, and an Altman Z-score of 3.39. Management is returning capital aggressively, repurchasing $267M of stock in FY25 and accelerating to $150M in Q1'26 alone, shrinking the share count from 61.2M (2023) to 54.7M.

The case for caution is equally concrete. Three of four brands are shrinking, with Mountain Hardwear goodwill written to zero and prAna's trade name impaired; management's own models now imply minimal growth from the smaller brands, leaving this effectively a single-brand (Columbia) story. The U.S. consumer faces tariff-driven price increases for Spring/Fall 2026, tariff exposure remains live (Section 122 tariffs running through ~July 2026), and the structural brand-relevance problem with younger consumers is acknowledged in the company's own filings. We rate COLM a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale, recognizing improving risk/reward against unresolved structural headwinds.

## Company Overview

Columbia Sportswear designs, markets, and distributes outdoor, active, and lifestyle apparel, footwear, accessories, and equipment in 115 countries under four brands: Columbia (~$2.97B, ~88% of FY25 revenue), SOREL (~$222M footwear), prAna (~$103M yoga/lifestyle), and Mountain Hardwear (~$100M technical/alpine). The company operates across four geographic segments: U.S., LAAP (Latin America/Asia-Pacific), EMEA, and Canada.

Revenue is split roughly evenly between wholesale (~52%) and direct-to-consumer (~48%), and between apparel/accessories/equipment (~80%) and footwear (~20%). The business is highly seasonal, weighted to Q3/Q4 fall/winter, and weather-sensitive, with warm winters dampening outerwear and cold-weather footwear demand. The company is founder-controlled: CEO/Chairman Timothy Boyle and related family shareholders hold more than 50% of shares. Headquartered in Portland, Oregon, the company employs about 9,620 people.

## Financial Analysis

The central financial story is operating-margin compression that is not occurring at the gross line. Gross margin expanded 30bps to 50.5% in FY25 on cleaner inventory and reduced promotion, even while absorbing roughly $50M of unmitigated tariffs. The damage is below gross profit.

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3,464M | $3,487M | $3,369M | $3,397M (+1%) |
| Operating income | $393M | $310M | $271M | $207M |
| Operating margin | 11.3% | 8.9% | 8.0% | 6.1% |
| Net income | $311M | $251M | $223M | $177M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $3.82 | $3.24 |

FY25 operating income fell $64M year-on-year despite 1% revenue growth, driven by SG&A up $59M (+130bps from demand-creation spend on ACCELERATE, DTC store costs, and retail impairments), a $29M goodwill/intangible impairment, and unmitigated tariffs. The SG&A deleverage and impairments are partly strategic and self-inflicted, which makes them more reversible than a structural gross-margin problem would be. The executed Profit Improvement Program has delivered more than $150M in cumulative annualized cost savings (2024-25), exceeding its original $125-150M target, demonstrating that cost levers exist.

Cash flow remains solid. FY25 free cash flow was $217M (operating cash flow $283M less $66M capex), down sharply from FY24's $491M operating cash flow, though most of the decline reflects working-capital timing (the reversal of FY24 vendor payment-term extensions) rather than earnings deterioration. The balance sheet carries zero borrowings; the reported D/E of 1.0 reflects operating-lease liabilities. A new $500M revolving facility was put in place in March 2026 (maturing 2031, undrawn), providing liquidity insurance amid tariff-driven cash volatility.

Q1'26 is the most timely data point and it sharpens the bifurcation. Revenue was flat at $779M, but U.S. fell 10% ($471M → $422M) with U.S. segment operating income down $17M and a 580bps unmitigated tariff hit to U.S. gross margin. Diluted EPS was $0.65 versus $0.75. International again offset: EMEA +35% (+21% constant currency), LAAP +5%. The company repurchased $150M of stock in the quarter against $101M in Q1'25.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is essentially absent; revenue has been flat to slightly down for three years. The growth thesis is not a revenue-acceleration story; it is an earnings-recovery-and-capital-return story layered on a geographic divergence.

Estimate revisions are the strongest forward signal. Eight to nine analysts contribute, and the revisions are broadly positive across horizons. These are meaningful but fall short of the magnitude (>30% over 90 days combined with >30% revenue acceleration) that would mark a powerful turn. The recent beats are partly explained by tariff and working-capital timing rather than durable operating leverage, so I weight the revision signal as a genuine positive but not high-conviction enough to override the sector and structural concerns.

International growth is the durable component. EMEA is gaining share and commanding 21% segment margins; LAAP gross margin runs around 57%, with China and Japan driving growth. This suggests the brand-relevance problem is more U.S.-specific than global. The ACCELERATE strategy, targeting younger, active consumers through the "Engineered for Whatever" campaign and refreshed product such as "Amaze Puff," is the U.S. turnaround bet, but it is increased demand-creation spend ahead of proven payback, and the company explicitly warns margins may decline "as is currently occurring."

The unrecognized ~$80M tariff-refund option is a real, asymmetric upside not in consensus models. Columbia has paid roughly $80M in IEEPA tariffs, expensed ~$55M through COGS, and recognized $0 of refund receivable following the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that found those tariffs unconstitutional. If the CBP refund program crystallizes, this flows back through gross margin plus interest as a one-time windfall. Timing and amount are uncertain, and management flags possible "accommodations to third-party vendors" that could dilute the benefit.

## Valuation Assessment

At $66.99, COLM trades at 15.9x forward EPS and 21.4x trailing. The forward multiple is undemanding for a net-cash company, and the trailing multiple overstates expense because FY25 earnings were depressed by impairments and unmitigated tariffs.

The peer context matters: premium outdoor/active brands command much higher multiples, but those are growth names (On, Hoka via Deckers, Arc'teryx via Amer) capturing share Columbia is ceding. Columbia is a flat-revenue, value-positioned incumbent, so a discount to that cohort is appropriate. Against broader apparel peers and given its balance sheet, 15.9x forward is closer to fair than cheap.

The 1.79% dividend yield provides modest support, and the buyback (54.7M shares outstanding against $276.5M of remaining authorization at Q1'26) provides ongoing EPS tailwind. Analyst targets span $47 to $92 with a $70.7 mean and $69 median; the stock sits just below the median target, implying limited consensus upside from here. I do not treat the median target as a hard ceiling given rising estimates, but at a near-52-week-high price with structural U.S. weakness, the asymmetry is more balanced than compelling. The $80M tariff-refund option is the cleanest source of unmodeled upside.

## Competitive Landscape

The outdoor/active apparel category has bifurcated. Premium technical-lifestyle brands (Arc'teryx, On, Hoka, Salomon) and athleisure players are capturing the younger, style-driven consumer, while Columbia's "high quality at exceptional value" function-first positioning has become less differentiated. Management's own filings concede this casualization and style shift is the structural headwind, and that the Columbia brand has lost relevance with younger consumers.

This justifies a low competitive-position score. Three of four brands are shrinking: SOREL -7% in FY25 and -12% in Q1'26, Mountain Hardwear -8% (goodwill written to zero), prAna -1% (trade name and goodwill impaired). The portfolio is effectively a single-brand story, and the impairments signal management has lowered its own forward expectations.

The counter-signal is international. EMEA and Asia-Pacific are gaining share at higher margins, suggesting the moat erosion is concentrated in the U.S. rather than global. The ACCELERATE response is a brand-elevation bet against far better-funded competitors with stronger youth resonance, which raises execution risk on the most important segment.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs — the dominant, evolving risk.** Columbia has recognized $0 refund receivable because recovery is not yet "probable." This cuts both ways: an asymmetric upside if refunds crystallize, and a continued drag from new Section 122 tariffs (10%, effective February 24, 2026 for 150 days, assumed in the FY26 outlook to run through ~July 2026). China remains material in the supply chain (~27% of U.S. footwear raw materials, ~21% of U.S. apparel).

**U.S. brand erosion and turnaround execution.** The entire bull case rests on ACCELERATE arresting the U.S. decline, which requires elevated spend before proven payback against well-funded competitors.

**Consumer macro.** Low-to-middle-income consumer pressure, compounded by tariff-driven price increases, risks further demand erosion in a discretionary, cancellable-order wholesale model with no long-term contracts.

**New Middle East conflict risk** (added in the Q1'26 10-Q) is already causing order cancellations in Middle East distributor markets and raising freight/energy costs, with potential input-cost exposure beginning Spring 2027.

**Family-control estate overhang.** Boyle-family holdings exceeding 50% could be "significantly diminished" to satisfy estate or unrealized-gains tax obligations, a concrete, disclosed potential supply overhang.

**Weather** remains a perennial swing factor; a warm Q4 already dampened China cold-weather sales in FY25.

### Options Market Signal

The options data shows an anomalous ATM IV reading jumping from 32.3% to 642.2% between early and mid-May 2026, which I treat as a data artifact rather than a genuine volatility signal; a 642% implied vol on a flat-revenue apparel name with a 0.93 beta is not credible and almost certainly reflects a feed error or an illiquid quote. Setting that aside, short interest at 12.9% of float with 5.1 days to cover indicates meaningful institutional skepticism, consistent with the structural U.S. brand concerns, but is below the >20% level that would create acute squeeze risk. The elevated short interest tempers the upside case rather than supporting a contrarian long.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Rising consensus EPS at 15.9x forward is undemanding for a net-cash business. International growth sustains the offset while ACCELERATE gains U.S. traction. The ~$80M tariff refund crystallizes as a one-time gross-margin windfall. The Profit Improvement Program ($150M+ realized) plus reversal of strategic SG&A spend rebuilds operating margin back toward 8-10%. Aggressive buybacks compound EPS off a shrinking 54.7M share base. Bull case earnings power of roughly $4.50-5.00 at a 17-18x multiple supports ~$85-90.

**Bear Case.** ACCELERATE fails to arrest U.S. decline, international growth decelerates and runs out of offset capacity, Section 122 tariffs and consumer-demand pressure compress margins further, and the tariff refund is delayed or diluted by vendor accommodations. The single-brand reality and ongoing portfolio impairments cap the multiple. Earnings stall near $3.25-3.50 at a 13-14x multiple, with the family-estate overhang weighing on supply. Bear case ~$47-50.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD (growth_hold)**. The 12-month thesis is improving earnings momentum into a reasonable forward multiple, supported by an unmodeled ~$80M tariff-refund option, executed cost savings, and aggressive buybacks against a net-cash balance sheet, sufficient to hold rather than avoid, but capped by structural U.S. brand erosion and consumer-cycle risk that prevent a stronger call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- U.S. segment revenue returns to flat or positive growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming ACCELERATE traction (versus -10% in Q1'26).
- CBP tariff-refund recovery becomes "probable" and is recognized, adding ~$80M plus interest to earnings/cash.
- Operating margin recovers above 8% on a trailing-twelve-month basis with continued positive estimate revisions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn negative, or a quarter misses materially after the recent beat streak.
- International growth (EMEA/LAAP) decelerates to low single digits while the U.S. continues double-digit declines, removing the offset.
- Gross margin (the resilient line) compresses below ~49%, signaling the problem has moved from SG&A to structural pricing/cost.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2'26), with particular attention to U.S. trajectory, the Section 122 tariff rollover after July 2026, and any update on refund-recovery probability.

## Conclusion

Columbia Sportswear is a financially sound, founder-controlled apparel company navigating a genuine brand-relevance problem in its largest market while international segments and aggressive capital return cushion the transition. The improving earnings picture is enough to justify holding through the turnaround attempt, and the net-cash balance sheet limits downside risk. Flat revenue, a single-brand reality, acknowledged structural competition, live tariff exposure, and tariff-driven consumer pricing pressure cap the upside and prevent a stronger endorsement. With the stock near its 52-week high and just below the consensus median target, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling. HOLD, with a growth-oriented rationale anchored on the international offset and earnings recovery, and a clear path to upgrade if U.S. trends stabilize or the tariff refund crystallizes.
