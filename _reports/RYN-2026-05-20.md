## Executive Summary

Between late 2023 and January 2026, Rayonier sold its entire New Zealand operations (~$688M), divested ~$737M of U.S. timberland, and completed a merger-of-equals with PotlatchDeltic that nearly doubled the share count to ~305M shares. The combined entity now owns approximately 3.5M+ acres across the U.S. South, Pacific Northwest, and Idaho, with the addition of ~1.1B board feet of annual lumber manufacturing capacity.

The investment case hinges on whether (a) the $40M synergy target is achievable by January 2028, (b) Southern pine stumpage prices stabilize after a 16% two-year decline, and (c) wood products earnings contribute meaningfully without introducing unacceptable volatility. At ~$20/share with a market cap of ~$6.1B, the stock trades well below its $26.10 year-end 2024 price, reflecting integration uncertainty, soft lumber markets, and the overhang of elevated long-term Treasury yields pressuring all duration-sensitive REITs.

The balance sheet is strong (0.8x net debt/EBITDA, $843M cash pre-merger), the real estate segment is underappreciated ($127M EBITDA on growing development communities), and the company has investment-grade ratings. However, with no meaningful insider buying, Q1 2026 showing a net loss, and multiple integration unknowns, the risk/reward does not support a high-conviction position in either direction.

## Company Overview

Rayonier operates as a timberland REIT through an UPREIT structure, generating revenue from stumpage sales, delivered logs, wood products manufacturing (new via PotlatchDeltic), real estate development, and land-based solutions (carbon capture/storage, solar leases). Post-merger segments include:

- **Southern Timber** (~1.69M acres pre-merger Rayonier): Loblolly/slash pine; stumpage and delivered log sales
- **Pacific Northwest Timber** (~307K acres): Western hemlock and Douglas-fir; domestic sawtimber and Pacific Rim export logs
- **Idaho Timber** (new via PotlatchDeltic): Sawlog prices indexed to lumber pricing on a 4-week lag
- **Wood Products** (new via PotlatchDeltic): ~1.1B board feet annual lumber shipments from multiple sawmills
- **Real Estate**: Improved development (Wildlight, FL; Heartwood, GA), rural/recreational, and non-strategic dispositions

The company is the second-largest publicly traded timberland REIT behind Weyerhaeuser (~12M acres). The merger narrows that gap and adds vertical integration comparable to Weyerhaeuser's model.

**Core Earnings Power (Pre-Merger Rayonier):**

The business generated stable ~$230-248M Adjusted EBITDA annually despite shrinking its asset base through dispositions, indicating improving per-acre productivity. CAD grew meaningfully in 2025 ($199M vs. $137M in 2023) as debt service declined following $250M in repayments.

**Balance Sheet (Dec 31, 2025):**
- Cash: $843M
- Total Debt: $1.05B
- Net Debt/Adjusted EBITDA: 0.8x (down from 4.6x in 2023)
- Credit Ratings: BBB/Baa3 (Stable)
- Current Ratio: 3.26

This is an exceptionally clean balance sheet for a REIT. The NZ sale proceeds funded the deleveraging and positioned the company for the PotlatchDeltic merger without requiring external equity issuance.

**Q1 2026 (Combined, Partial Quarter):**
- Revenue: $276.8M (includes only ~2 months PotlatchDeltic contribution)
- Net Loss: -$12.4M (-$0.05/share)
- Adjusted EPS: $0.07

The reported loss reflects merger costs, not underlying economics. However, it provides limited visibility into combined run-rate earnings.

**Post-Merger Pro Forma Considerations:**
- Share count: ~305M (nearly doubled from ~161M)
- New $1.81B credit facility
- 2026 dividend: ~$317M ($1.04/share annualized at ~$0.26/quarter)
- At $20/share: dividend yield ~5.2%
- 2026 capex: $104-108M + $40-44M real estate development

**Key Concern:** At ~305M shares and $20/share, market cap is ~$6.1B. For the stock to return to $26 (year-end 2024 levels), the combined company needs to demonstrate ~$400M+ Adjusted EBITDA to support a ~15x EV/EBITDA multiple typical for timberland REITs. This requires full synergy realization plus stable/recovering timber pricing plus positive Wood Products contribution.

## Growth Analysis

**Growth Drivers:**
1. **Synergy realization**: $40M annual run-rate by January 2028 from operational efficiencies, overhead elimination, and procurement savings. Credible but unproven.
2. **Real Estate development**: Improved Development sales grew from $30.8M (2024) to $47.2M (2025) with attractive pricing ($79,351/acre). Wildlight and Heartwood have multi-decade runways benefiting from Southeast migration.
3. **Land-based solutions**: CCS and solar leases contributed $11.2M in 2025 (down from $14.5M in 2024). Small but potentially scalable as carbon markets develop.
4. **Wood Products earnings**: At normalized lumber prices, PotlatchDeltic's ~1.1B board feet shipments could contribute meaningfully. Idaho sawlog pricing indexed to lumber creates leveraged upside in housing recoveries.

**Growth Headwinds:**
1. **Southern stumpage pricing decline**: Weighted-average realizations fell from $22.49/ton (2023) to $18.83 (2025), a 16% decline. Pulpwood specifically fell 22% YoY to $13.14/ton. This reflects structural oversupply from planted pine maturation, salvage timber from storms, and mill closures/curtailments.
2. **Housing starts below trend**: Elevated mortgage rates constrain lumber demand and new construction activity.
3. **Integration execution**: History shows merger synergies often arrive late and below target. The 24-month timeline pushes full realization to early 2028.
4. **Federal timber supply risk**: Executive Order 14225 (March 2025) targets increased federal harvests in PNW/Idaho, which would pressure private timberland values and log pricing if implemented.

Net assessment: Growth depends on (a) cyclical timber/lumber recovery timing and (b) integration execution. Neither is within management's control near-term, and the macro backdrop (elevated rates, soft housing) does not favor an imminent recovery.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$20/share with ~305M shares outstanding:
- Market Cap: ~$6.1B
- Enterprise Value: ~$7.3B (adding ~$1.2B net debt post-merger, rough estimate given cash deployment)
- EV/2025 Adjusted EBITDA (standalone): ~29x on $248M — expensive
- Dividend Yield: ~5.2% ($1.04 annualized)

The valuation challenge is that we lack a clean combined Adjusted EBITDA figure. If the combined entity can generate $350-400M EBITDA (Rayonier's $248M + PotlatchDeltic contribution + synergies), then EV/EBITDA is 18-21x, which is reasonable but not cheap for a timberland REIT in a declining pricing environment. Weyerhaeuser typically trades at 15-18x EV/EBITDA.

The 5.2% dividend yield provides some support, but barely exceeds risk-free rates in the current environment. For a yield buyer, the proposition is marginal unless you believe in share price appreciation from synergy realization.

The stock at $20 is down 40% from its $33.41 year-end 2023 level. However, the share count has nearly doubled, so the market cap decline is less severe (~$5.4B then vs. ~$6.1B now). Much of the price decline reflects per-share dilution from the merger, not pure value destruction.

The stock does not screen as obviously mispriced on readily available metrics. No specific non-consensus catalyst with a near-term timeline would drive re-rating. The synergy story requires 12-24 months to prove out.

## Competitive Landscape

Rayonier's competitive position has strengthened through the merger. The combined entity closes the gap with Weyerhaeuser in both acreage and vertical integration. Key differentiators:

1. **Scale advantage**: ~3.5M+ combined acres creates procurement and operational efficiencies
2. **Geographic diversification**: Southeast, Pacific Northwest, and Idaho — reducing regional weather/market concentration
3. **Real estate optionality**: Wildlight and Heartwood communities are unique assets with multi-decade development potential; Weyerhaeuser lacks comparable development capabilities
4. **Land-based solutions**: Early-mover in CCS/solar leasing on timberlands
5. **Vertical integration**: Wood products manufacturing creates supply chain flexibility

Weaknesses relative to Weyerhaeuser include smaller scale (~3.5M vs. ~12M acres), less proven manufacturing operations, and integration execution risk. The addition of collective bargaining agreements and pension liabilities (90.9% funded) introduces obligations that pure timberland holdings avoid.

Southern timber market dynamics remain unfavorable: oversupply from planted pine maturation, salvage volumes from recent storms, and mill closures in containerboard/pulp. Recovery requires either demand inflection (new mill starts, housing recovery) or supply rationalization (further mill closures reducing log demand, counterintuitively).

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Integration execution (HIGH)**: The PotlatchDeltic merger creates a substantially more complex organization. Dual leadership (CEO McHugh + Executive Chairman Cremers) could create tension. Different corporate cultures, union relationships, and systems require harmonization. The $40M synergy target is achievable but failure would materially impair the investment case.

2. **Lumber market cyclicality (HIGH)**: The Wood Products segment exposes RYN to amplified lumber price sensitivity through Idaho's 4-week lag indexing. At current housing starts levels with depressed lumber demand, a further downturn would generate segment losses offsetting stable timber income.

3. **Interest rate sensitivity (MEDIUM-HIGH)**: At elevated long-term rates, all yield-sensitive assets face headwinds. Timberland NAV valuations use discount rates that rise with long-term rates. The 5.2% dividend yield offers thin spread over risk-free. Further rate increases would compress relative yield attractiveness.

4. **Southern stumpage pricing (MEDIUM)**: The combined company's larger Southern footprint amplifies exposure to continued pricing weakness.

5. **Federal timber supply (LOW-MEDIUM)**: Executive Order 14225 could increase federal harvests, pressuring private timberland values. Implementation faces legal and logistical challenges, limiting near-term impact, but the medium-term risk is real.

6. **Dividend sustainability (LOW-MEDIUM)**: $317M in projected 2026 dividends requires the combined entity to generate sufficient cash flow through the integration period. While manageable given the strong balance sheet, any shortfall would damage REIT investor confidence disproportionately.

**Altman Z-Score Note:** The 1.65 Z-Score is structurally misleading for timberland REITs. Very low asset turnover (0.14x) is inherent to the model — massive timberland assets generate modest annual harvest revenue by design. Investment-grade ratings and 0.8x net leverage confirm no actual distress.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$26-28, +30-40%):**
- $40M synergies achieved on time or early, demonstrating combined EBITDA of $380-420M by 2027
- Southern stumpage prices stabilize as mill capacity expands (announced projects come online 2026-2027)
- Housing starts recover modestly as rates plateau, benefiting Wood Products
- Real estate development continues strong growth trajectory ($50M+ Improved Development sales)
- Market re-rates combined entity closer to Weyerhaeuser multiples (15-17x EV/EBITDA)

**Bear Case (~$14-16, -20-30%):**
- Integration proves more costly/slower than expected; synergies delayed or reduced
- Lumber pricing weakens further on continued housing weakness, generating Wood Products losses
- Southern stumpage continues declining on structural oversupply
- Further rate increases compress yield-sensitive REITs
- Dividend cut becomes necessary, triggering forced selling from income-oriented holders

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Evidence of combined Adjusted EBITDA exceeding $350M on an annualized basis (would need Q2-Q3 2026 reporting)
2. Southern pine stumpage realizations stabilizing or recovering above $20/ton for two consecutive quarters
3. Stock declining to $16-17 range while fundamentals remain intact (would push yield to ~6.5% and EV/EBITDA to more attractive levels)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Management reduces or delays the $40M synergy target
2. Dividend is cut or covered by less than 1.0x CAD
3. Southern stumpage realizations fall below $17/ton with no recovery catalyst identified

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (August 2026) will be the first full quarter of combined operations and the first opportunity to evaluate integrated financial performance.

## Conclusion

Rayonier is in the early stages of proving its post-merger thesis. The balance sheet is strong, the real estate segment is undervalued, and the strategic logic of the PotlatchDeltic combination is sound. However, the stock at $20 does not offer sufficient margin of safety given: (a) unproven synergy realization, (b) cyclical headwinds in both timber pricing and housing, (c) a rate environment that pressures timberland valuations and reduces the relative attractiveness of the 5.2% yield, and (d) 12-24 months before normalized combined earnings power becomes visible.

The thesis becomes investable either on a price decline to the $16-17 range (creating genuine yield support and margin of safety) or on operating evidence that combined EBITDA is tracking above $350M with visible synergy progress. Neither condition is met today.

**Recommendation: HOLD (Uncertain)**
