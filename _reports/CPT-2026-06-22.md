**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Camden Property Trust is a high-quality, Sunbelt-concentrated multifamily REIT with one of the strongest balance sheets in the sector (A-/A3/A- ratings, ~6x interest coverage, 90.6% of properties unencumbered, 5.4-year weighted-average debt maturity). The defensive characteristics are real, and the ~3.9% dividend at a ~71% AFFO payout ratio is well covered. The problem is the operating line. Same-store revenue growth has decelerated from +0.8% in FY2025 to +0.2% in Q1 2026, same-store NOI flipped to -0.7%, and blended lease rates printed -1.4%, direct evidence of impaired pricing power as the post-2021 Sunbelt construction wave hits absorption in Camden's core markets (Austin, Phoenix, Nashville, Dallas, Charlotte, Raleigh, Orlando).

The 2025 GAAP net income surge to $384.5M (from $163.3M) is misleading: roughly $260.9M of it came from property-sale gains, not operating improvement. On the metric that matters for a REIT, Core FFO, the business is flat-to-declining ($6.88/share FY2025; Q1 2026 Core FFO of $1.70 was down 1.2% year-over-year even though it beat consensus). Management's thesis is that supply is peaking and moderating while demand stays firm given favorable rent-vs-buy economics, setting up a recovery as the delivery wave is absorbed across 2026-2027. That thesis is plausible and consistent with the view that housing affordability keeps renters renting, but it has not yet appeared in the operating numbers, and next-quarter FFO estimates were revised down 69% over 90 days.

We maintain a HOLD with an income-hold rationale. The stock at $109 trades at ~30x trailing P/E (a depressed/non-comparable figure for a REIT given the disposition-gain distortion) and ~3.9% yield, below the peer-median 4.4%. The capital-return posture is disciplined (aggressive buybacks below NAV, a modest dividend raise), but the Executive Chairman sold $3.4M of stock near a local high in June, there is no open-market insider buying, and the DOJ/state-AG RealPage actions remain open and unquantified after the $53M class-action settlement. Own this name for the dividend and balance-sheet quality, not for near-term appreciation.

## Company Overview

Camden Property Trust (formed 1993, Houston-headquartered Texas REIT) owns, operates, develops, and manages multifamily apartment communities. As of Q1 2026 it held interests in, operated, or was developing 174 properties comprising 59,416 apartment homes across 15 U.S. markets, heavily tilted toward the Sunbelt. Top markets by home count: Houston (8,207), Washington D.C. Metro (6,194), Dallas/Fort Worth (5,424), Orlando (4,276), Atlanta (4,270), and Phoenix (4,094), with growing positions in Raleigh, Austin, Charlotte, Tampa, and Nashville.

The competitive position rests on three pillars: operating scale and density within core markets (property-management economies of scale), an A-rated balance sheet that lowers cost of capital versus most peers, and vertical integration through a wholly owned general-contracting subsidiary that develops for Camden's own account and for third parties (generating fee income). The portfolio is well diversified at the asset level, with no single community exceeding 1.5% of revenue. Camden operates as a single reportable segment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and NOI.** FY2025 total property revenues were $1,573.5M (+1.9% y/y) and total property NOI was $1,006.8M (+2.2%). The headline masks weak organic performance: FY2025 same-store revenue grew only +0.8% and same-store NOI just +0.3%. By Q1 2026 the deceleration was clear: same-store revenue +0.2%, same-store NOI -0.7% as operating expenses (+1.9%) outpaced revenue. Total property revenues declined 0.5% y/y in Q1 2026 on dispositions. Apartment leases reprice on roughly 14-month terms, so soft market rents pass through to the income statement quickly.

**FFO — the metric that matters.** FY2025 Core FFO was $757.2M ($6.88 per share-equivalent) and Core AFFO was $649.1M. Net income to common rose to $384.5M from $163.3M, but the swing was driven by a $260.9M gain on property sales (vs. $43.8M in 2024) and lower impairments. Q1 2026 Core FFO of $1.70/share was down 1.2% y/y while beating consensus ($1.67). FFO has plateaued and is modestly declining, consistent with a late-cycle, oversupplied apartment market.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was solid at $826.6M in FY2025 (up from $774.9M). The dividend is well covered: FY2025 dividends of ~$461M against Core AFFO of $649M is a ~71% payout, and the 2026 distribution was raised to $4.24 annualized. Buybacks have been aggressive and value-oriented: $270.7M repurchased in 2025 at an average of $106.92, then 2.63M shares in Q1 2026 (including March at $97.62) plus 1.43M more in April at $100.78. A new $600M authorization was set in February 2026, with ~$297.8M remaining. Repurchasing below estimated NAV signals management prefers its own equity to new development or acquisitions in a soft market.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt was ~$4.25B at Q1 2026 with ~90.6% of properties unencumbered, a weighted-average maturity extended to 5.4 years, and interest coverage near 6.0x. Ratings are A- (Fitch/S&P) and A3 (Moody's), all stable. Recent financing has been healthy: $600M of 4.90% senior notes due 2036 (February 2026), the $1.2B revolver extended to March 2030, a $600M commercial paper program, and a new $500M ATM (April 2026). Floating-rate debt is ~$900M (~21% of total). The reported Altman Z-Score of 1.39 ("distress zone") is a model artifact that does not apply to structurally leveraged REITs; the A-rating, 6x coverage, and laddered maturities are the correct read on solvency.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the case and the principal reason for the HOLD. Organic growth has stalled, and negative blended lease rates confirm that Camden cannot push rents in markets absorbing record deliveries. Earnings surprises have been strong on a headline basis (43.4% beat in Q1 2026; 309% and 243% in the two prior quarters), but those outsized beats reflect property-sale gains rather than operating outperformance, and the underlying same-store trend is what drives durable FFO.

The estimate-revision data is mixed and best read with caution given the low analyst counts. Current-year EPS estimates rose +75.4% over 90 days and next-year +27.6%, which on the surface looks like a recovery signal. But the GAAP EPS line is heavily distorted by disposition gains and the RealPage contingency, so these revisions reflect transaction timing more than operating momentum. The more telling figure is the next-quarter estimate, revised down 69% over 90 days, consistent with the same-store NOI rolling over. We do not treat the headline +30%-plus revisions as a clean BUY signal here, because the numerator is gain-driven rather than NOI-driven.

The bull thesis for growth is sequencing: management argues supply is "manageable and moderating" while demand stays firm. With home values and mortgage affordability pressuring the buy decision, the rent-vs-buy math favors renting, which should support occupancy and eventually pricing as new supply is absorbed in 2026-2027. The April acquisitions (Atlanta 269 homes, Orlando 288 homes for $171.3M) show Camden leaning into Sunbelt at trough pricing. The thesis is credible, but it requires patience: there is no evidence in the Q1 numbers that the turn has begun, and the trajectory of same-store NOI is the variable to watch.

## Valuation Assessment

At $109, Camden carries a ~30x trailing P/E and a ~3.9% dividend yield. The trailing P/E is not a useful comparable for a REIT (FFO, not EPS, is the relevant earnings measure, and the EPS line is distorted by gains and contingencies). On a Core FFO basis ($6.88 FY2025), the stock trades at roughly 15.9x FFO, fair-to-slightly-rich for a no-growth apartment REIT, justified by the A-rated balance sheet.

Against peers, Camden's reported P/E of 30x sits below the peer median of 41.9x (MAA), but that comparison is noisy across the group given varying gain/impairment distortions (UDR 26.5x, ESS 31.6x, IRT 84x, CSR 123x). More meaningful is the dividend yield: Camden's 3.9% is below the peer median of 4.4% (UDR and MAA both 4.4%), so the market is not pricing Camden cheaper than the group; if anything, it carries a quality premium that compresses its yield relative to peers. Camden's 52-week discount is in line with the group.

The analyst consensus offers a low target of $102, a mean of ~$114, and a high of $134, implying modest upside to mean and ~23% to the high. Morgan Stanley sits Equal-Weight with a $117 target (cut from $119), and Scotiabank recently downgraded to Sector Underperform, a neutral-to-cautious sell-side stance. We do not treat these targets as floors that consensus will chase upward, because this is a fairly valued income vehicle with stalled organic growth rather than a cheap stock with rising operating estimates. The buyback evidence is the closest thing to a positive valuation tell (management bought stock down to $97.62), but that signals NAV support around the high-$90s/low-$100s rather than a catalyst for re-rating. There is no specific, identifiable mispricing to reward with a high valuation score, so we score it mid-range and lean against rewarding the optically "low" trailing multiple, which is a value-trap-style artifact rather than a buy signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Camden competes against public multifamily peers (AvalonBay, Equity Residential, MAA, UDR, Essex, Independence Realty) and against single-family rentals, condos, for-sale housing, and short-term rentals. Its durable advantages (scale density in core markets, A-rated cost of capital, and vertically integrated development) remain intact and differentiate it from smaller, more levered peers.

The competitive trajectory, however, is stable-to-soft rather than improving. The defining industry dynamic is the Sunbelt supply glut: record deliveries across Camden's core markets have suppressed rent growth sector-wide, and Camden's negative blended lease rates and flat-to-negative same-store NOI show it is not immune. The vertically integrated development engine, an advantage in a tight market, becomes a liability in a soft one: Camden recorded a $12.9M land impairment in 2025 (after $41.0M in 2024) and a $4.9M technology-investment impairment in Q1 2026. The reported marketing of 11 California communities suggests a deliberate retreat from coastal rent-control exposure toward lower-regulation Sunbelt markets, a sensible portfolio tilt but one that does not move the competitive needle near-term. The moat is wide and stable, but the cyclical headwind dominates the next several quarters.

## Risk Assessment

**Sunbelt oversupply / weak pricing power (dominant risk).** Short lease terms mean revenue reprices quickly when market rents fall. This risk is partly priced (the stock has drifted lower through 2026) but the timing of absorption is uncertain.

**RealPage antitrust litigation (material, partially unresolved).** Camden agreed in April 2026 to a binding term sheet to settle the consolidated class action for $53.0M (two $26.5M installments) and recorded $58.8M in aggregate loss contingencies in Q1 2026. That caps the class-action exposure, but DOJ and multiple state-AG matters (DC, Arizona, a DOJ/10-state civil action) remain open and unquantified, and the settlement carries prospective commitments on the use of revenue-management software and non-public data, a structural constraint on the industry's pricing toolkit rather than a one-time cost.

**Rent control / rent stabilization.** Camden flags expanding rent-control advocacy, particularly its California exposure (LA/OC, San Diego). The reported California disposition marketing reduces this over time.

**Development and refinancing.** $492M of construction in progress (~$177M cost to complete) plus a development pipeline carry markdown risk in a softening market. ~$900M floating-rate debt and ~$663.8M of maturities over the next 12 months pressure FFO at higher refi rates, though the A-rating and revolver capacity make this manageable.

**Options Market Signal.** The options market is benign and complacent rather than fearful. Near-term ATM IV is 24.9% versus 19.4% historical vol, modestly rich but typical for an event-light income name. The put/call skew is balanced (0.0%), the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.38, and the term structure is in contango (24.9% near vs. 27.4% long-term), the normal/complacent shape. The implied one-month move is ±5.3%. The put/call OI ratio did rise from 0.29 to 0.76 over the past month, indicating some incremental hedging, but nothing that signals informed bearish positioning ahead of a catalyst. Short interest is moderate at 8.1% of float with 5.4 days to cover, not a crowded short, consistent with a "no strong view" market posture. The options tape neither confirms nor challenges the income-hold thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($120-134).** Supply peaks and moderates through 2026-2027; with the rent-vs-buy math remaining favorable, occupancy holds and blended lease rates turn positive in 2H 2026. Same-store NOI re-accelerates toward +2-3%, Core FFO returns to growth, and the A-rated balance sheet lets Camden acquire at trough pricing while peers are constrained. Buybacks below NAV compound per-share value. A re-rating toward the high end of the analyst range ($134) plus the ~3.9% dividend delivers a low-double-digit total return.

**Bear Case ($95-100).** Supply absorption takes longer than management expects; same-store NOI stays negative through 2026, blended lease rates remain negative, and Core FFO declines further. DOJ/state-AG RealPage actions produce additional settlements and force changes to revenue-management practices that structurally cap pricing. Higher-for-longer rates pressure refinancing costs on the $900M floating book and the $663.8M of near-term maturities. The stock retests the buyback floor in the high-$90s, where NAV support (management's demonstrated buying zone) should hold.

The skew is roughly symmetric. The balance-sheet quality and dividend coverage cap the downside near the high-$90s, while the absence of an operating turn caps the near-term upside. This is a range-bound income holding rather than an asymmetric long.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on an income-hold rationale. The ~3.9% well-covered dividend and A-rated balance sheet justify continued ownership for income-oriented investors, but the absence of operating momentum and the lack of a specific mispricing argue against adding for appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Same-store NOI returns to positive growth (>+1% y/y) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the supply-absorption recovery has begun.
- Blended lease rates turn positive, signaling restored pricing power.
- A pullback toward the high-$90s (management's demonstrated buyback zone), which would restore a margin of safety and improve the yield toward/above the 4.4% peer median.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store NOI deteriorates further (toward -2% or worse) with no sign of absorption.
- DOJ/state-AG RealPage matters escalate into materially larger settlements or binding restrictions on revenue-management software that structurally impair pricing.
- A dividend that ceases to be comfortably covered by AFFO (payout rising toward 90%+), or a halt to buybacks signaling capital stress.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August) for the same-store NOI and blended-lease-rate trajectory, and any 10-Q/8-K disclosures on the open RealPage AG/DOJ matters and the California disposition program.

## Conclusion

Camden is a well-run, financially fortress-like apartment REIT trading at a fair price for a business whose organic growth has stalled. The bull case (Sunbelt supply moderating into a 2026-2027 recovery) is credible but unproven in the operating numbers, and the next-quarter FFO estimate cut of 69% over 90 days argues the trough is not yet behind it. The capital-return discipline (buybacks below NAV, a covered and rising dividend) supports the floor, but the Executive Chairman's $3.4M June sale, the absence of any open-market insider buying, the below-peer dividend yield, and the unresolved DOJ/state-AG RealPage overhang collectively remove any asymmetric entry. We rate CPT HOLD (income-hold), appropriate for investors seeking a defensive ~3.9% yield from a top-quality balance sheet, with a reassessment at the next earnings print and an upgrade contingent on a confirmed turn in same-store NOI or a pullback into the high-$90s.
