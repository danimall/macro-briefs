**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

REX American Resources is a pure-play corn-ethanol producer that carries a misleading "Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing" sector label. The company operates one reportable segment (ethanol and by-products), holds a fortress balance sheet with roughly $376M in cash and Treasuries against essentially zero funded debt, and has delivered a sharp earnings improvement over the last four quarters. FY2025 net income to shareholders rose 43% to $83.0M, Q1 FY2026 net income more than doubled to $18.5M ($0.56/share), and management characterized the quarter as its "most profitable first quarter ever."

This earnings acceleration is largely a tax-credit and by-product story, not a margin story. Effective February 1, 2026, REX early-adopted ASU 2025-10, reclassifying 45Z clean-fuel production tax credits from a tax-line benefit into "Production tax credit income" within gross profit. Under the new presentation, FY2025 gross profit jumps from $93.7M to $125.4M, and $31.7M of 45Z credits ($28.1M attributable to REX) become the visible earnings driver. In Q1 FY2026, ethanol selling prices fell 6% year-over-year ($1.66 vs $1.76/gal); the profit surge came from $7.5M of 45Z credits and stronger distillers corn oil and DDG pricing. Investors should not extrapolate the crush spread from these results.

The result is a balanced setup. The balance sheet floors downside risk in a way few commodity processors can match, and management's capital allocation is disciplined (buybacks at self-defined discounts to intrinsic value, self-funded high-return growth capex, no dilutive M&A). But the earnings engine now depends on unfinalized Treasury rules governing 45Z, on carbon-capture permitting facing hostile Midwest pipeline politics, and on a structurally overcapacitied industry with a flat federal mandate. At ~16.7x trailing earnings inflated by tax credits, no dividend, and chairman selling into recent highs, we rate REX a HOLD. This is our fifth consecutive HOLD, consistent with a declining score trajectory (6.2 in late May to 5.7 in mid-June).

## Company Overview

Despite its sector classification, REX has no oil and gas refining operations. The former retail electronics business (Rex Radio and Television) and the refined-coal operation (ceased November 2021) are legacy. REX entered ethanol in 2006 and today holds interests in three producing entities:

| Entity | Location | REX Ownership | Consolidation |
|---|---|---|---|
| One Earth Energy | Gibson City, IL | 76.1% | Consolidated |
| NuGen Energy | Marion, SD | 99.7% | Consolidated |
| Big River Resources (4 plants) | IA/IL/WI | 5.7%–10.3% | Equity method |

Across all six facilities, roughly 722M gallons of ethanol shipped in the trailing twelve months; REX's effective ownership is approximately 294M gallons per year. Products are ethanol (~77% of revenue), dried distillers grains (DDG), distillers corn oil (DCO), and modified distillers grains. Corn is roughly 74–75% of cost of goods sold; natural gas is 5–6%. This is fundamentally a crush-spread business with thin, volatile core margins, currently supercharged by federal tax credits.

ADM, POET, Valero, and Green Plains are larger; REX is not the low-cost giant. Its differentiation is operational efficiency and capital structure: it can self-fund $220–230M of growth capex (One Earth expansion from 150 to 200M gal/yr plus carbon capture) entirely from cash while competitors carry debt.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet (FY ended January 31, 2026).** Cash and short-term investments totaled approximately $375.8M (of which ~$336.5M sits at One Earth and NuGen). Total debt was about $3.2M, essentially all finance and operating leases with no meaningful funded borrowings. Stockholders' equity was $610.7M against total assets of $797.7M. The current ratio was 5.9x (6.8x as of April 30, 2026) with working capital of $372M. The screening artifacts (an Altman Z of 2.91 "grey zone" and D/E of 1.31) are misleading: both are dragged by noncontrolling interests and lease accounting, not leverage risk. REX is effectively debt-free, with roughly 25% of its $1.5B market capitalization held in cash and Treasuries yielding ~3.7%.

**Earnings, adjusted for the accounting change.** As originally reported, FY2025 revenue was $650.5M (+1% YoY). Under the ASU 2025-10 presentation, pretax income was $120.3M. A standardized data pull reading only the as-originally-filed income statement understates true economic earnings by roughly $32M and misattributes the source of the growth.

**Q1 FY2026 (ended April 30, 2026).** Revenue was $156.5M (-1% YoY). Production tax credit income of $7.5M (vs $0 a year earlier) drove gross profit to $29.1M, double the prior-year $14.3M. Net income to shareholders was $18.5M ($0.56/share) vs $8.7M ($0.26), +113%. The company beat consensus of $0.34 by 63%, continuing a pattern of three beats and one in-line print over the last four reported quarters.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $117.8M (up from $64.2M), capex $68.4M, and free cash flow roughly $49.4M. REX repurchased 1.65M shares for $32.9M in FY2025, with 2.36M shares remaining authorized, and pays no dividend. A 2-for-1 stock split effected September 15, 2025 explains the share count roughly doubling from ~17.6M to ~33.2M.

Applying the financial-health lens that most reliably discriminates outcomes, REX scores well: strong operating cash generation, a debt-free structure appropriate for a volatile commodity processor, and working-capital discipline. This is the anchor of our thesis and the dimension we weight most heavily. It earns a 7.5 (down from a prior 8.2, reflecting that a meaningful slice of the earnings base is now tax-credit-dependent rather than operationally durable).

## Growth Analysis

The growth question splits cleanly into two components with very different durability.

**The transient component: 45Z tax credits and by-product pricing.** These drove essentially all of the recent earnings acceleration. DCO pricing (+17–23%) is a genuinely improving stream tied to renewable diesel and biodiesel feedstock demand, and it is likely to persist. But the 45Z credit stream rests on proposed Treasury rules that are not yet final (public hearings held in 2026), requires meeting prevailing-wage/apprenticeship and carbon-intensity thresholds, and expires December 31, 2029 (extended by OBBBA). REX has not yet decided whether or how to monetize its 2026 credits. This is a policy-dependent windfall with a hard sunset, not a durable compounding growth vector.

**The structural component: capacity expansion and CCS.** The One Earth expansion (150 to 175 to 200M gal/yr) is accretive volume growth, self-funded, targeted for completion by end-2026. The carbon-capture project is both offensive (lower carbon intensity boosts 45Z eligibility) and defensive (producers who cannot lower CI scores become structurally disadvantaged). But the CCS timeline has slipped, with the EPA Class VI draft permit expected around July 2026 and a final decision around November 2026, and it faces a hostile Midwest regulatory backdrop discussed below.

The core industry backdrop is unfavorable for organic growth: roughly 18.5B gallons of U.S. ethanol capacity against a flat 15.0B-gallon RFS conventional mandate through 2027 caps pricing power. EV adoption and ICE phase-outs are a long-dated demand headwind. Growth prospects therefore score a 5.2 — the durable pieces (expansion volumes, DCO) are real but modest, while the large recent earnings gains are policy-driven and non-recurring.

## Valuation Assessment

REX trades at approximately 16.7x trailing earnings. The peer group in this classification is heterogeneous and largely distressed (WKC -3.0x, CVI -72.7x, IEP -13.3x on a 27.8% yield, PARR 6.2x, PBF 10.8x), with a peer median P/E of 10.8x. On that basis REX trades at a premium, but the comparison is not clean: the peers are actual oil refiners, not ethanol producers, and REX's debt-free balance sheet justifies a valuation premium to leveraged refiners.

The central valuation problem is that the "E" in the P/E is inflated by non-recurring tax credits. Stripping the ~$28M of attributable 45Z credits from FY2025 net income leaves core earnings closer to $55M, against which the ~$1.5B market cap implies a normalized multiple well above the headline 16.7x. The calibration lesson that cheap valuation scores mark value traps rather than opportunities is directly relevant: REX does not screen as cheap, and where it appears reasonably priced it is because trough-cycle metrics are being flattered by a policy windfall.

Netting out the cash provides some support. With cash and Treasuries netted against a $1.5B market cap, the enterprise value is roughly $1.13B, so the operating business trades at a lower effective multiple than the headline suggests. That cash cushion is the primary reason this is not an AVOID. But cash-heavy balance sheets without a clear deployment path or dividend are priced by the market as capital inefficiency, and REX pays no dividend.

There is no analyst target coverage in the provided data, so we cannot triangulate against consensus. The estimate-revision signal that would flag an inflection is absent; this is not a case of rapidly rising forward estimates on a cheap multiple. We assign a valuation score of 5.0: the balance sheet supports the floor, but there is no identified mispricing and the earnings base is of lower quality than the multiple implies.

## Competitive Landscape

REX's competitive trajectory is modestly improving, which is why this dimension has risen from 4.5 to 5.5 over prior reports. The decisive industry dynamic is 45Z, which rewards low-carbon-intensity producers with $0.02–$1.00/gal. REX's efficiency plus its CCS investment position it to capture higher per-gallon credits than producers who cannot sequester carbon. If One Earth CCS succeeds, REX moves into a structurally advantaged CI tier while carbon-disadvantaged competitors are penalized.

The offsetting reality is that carbon-pipeline politics have turned hostile across the Midwest, threatening the entire industry's CCS thesis and REX's specifically. South Dakota banned eminent domain for CO2 pipelines (March 2025), a North Dakota court voided Summit Carbon Solutions' storage permits (March 2026) as unconstitutional, and Illinois SB 1289 imposed a CO2-pipeline permitting moratorium that led the ICC to dismiss REX's pipeline application. NuGen's CCS economics depend entirely on Summit, whose Midwest pipeline is now legally imperiled. One Earth's project is self-controlled and further along, so the market may be conflating two bets with materially different odds.

Structural overcapacity caps pricing power industry-wide, and REX competes against far larger integrated players. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving on the CI/45Z axis, but the improvement is contingent on permitting outcomes outside the company's control. Score: 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by three factors not fully priced.

1. **Crush-spread volatility.** REX explicitly states it may operate plants at "negative or minimally positive operating margins" and can hedge only ~4 months forward. A 10% adverse ethanol move implies a ~$51.6M pre-tax hit; corn ~$38.3M. Core earnings are inherently unpredictable.

2. **45Z dependency.** A large and growing share of profit now comes from 45Z credits. These hinge on Treasury rules that are still proposed, on meeting wage/apprenticeship and CI thresholds, and on a 2029 sunset. Unfavorable CI modeling or rule changes could materially cut this earnings stream, the single most important open variable.

3. **CCS permitting and pipeline politics.** REX has spent ~$58.7M on the One Earth CCS project with no operating permit yet. The Class VI permit timeline has slipped. NuGen's dependence on the imperiled Summit pipeline compounds the risk. Failure could force a write-off and leave the plants with competitively higher CI scores.

Additional risks include trade/tariff exposure (~36% of U.S. ethanol exports went to Canada, ~20% of DDG exports to Mexico in 2025), long-term EV/demand erosion, complete lack of business diversification (management flags this itself), and cash concentration above FDIC limits (mitigated by Treasury holdings).

Because several of these are binary or policy-contingent (45Z finalization, EPA permit decision) and only partially priced, the risk score is 5.0. The fortress balance sheet prevents a lower score: there is no refinancing cliff, covenant risk, or cash-burn problem.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** REX is a debt-free processor with ~$376M cash trading at a modest enterprise multiple. If Treasury finalizes 45Z favorably and the One Earth CCS project secures its EPA Class VI permit by late 2026, REX moves into a structurally advantaged CI tier, the ~$28M+ annual credit stream becomes durable through 2029, and the completed One Earth expansion adds accretive volume. DCO pricing continues to strengthen on renewable-diesel demand. Continued buybacks at discounts to intrinsic value compound per-share value. TSR of 265% vs a 48% peer group over the measurement window validates the capital-allocation approach. Upside scenario roughly $58–62 (~25–35% above current), driven by CCS de-risking and durable credits.

**Bear case.** 45Z rules are finalized unfavorably or REX fails to meet CI thresholds, cutting the credit stream that drove the entire recent earnings surge. The One Earth EPA permit slips further or is denied, forcing a write-off of ~$59M spent to date. NuGen's Summit-dependent CCS collapses entirely given the ND court ruling and SD eminent-domain ban. The crush spread, already showing falling ethanol prices, compresses further into a structurally overcapacitied market. Normalized earnings revert toward ~$55M ex-credits, and the multiple derates. Downside roughly $30–33 (~28–35% below current), floored by the cash balance.

The base case sits between: modest core-margin volatility, DCO strength persisting, expansion completing on schedule, but 45Z durability and CCS permitting remaining unresolved through most of 2026. That keeps the stock range-bound around current levels absent a catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The balance-sheet floor and disciplined capital allocation prevent an AVOID; the tax-credit-dependent, policy-contingent earnings base and absence of an identified mispricing or dividend prevent a BUY. This marks the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the declining score trajectory (6.2 in late May to 5.7 in mid-June to 5.6 here) reflects growing recognition that the earnings quality is lower than the headline growth implies. The price is essentially unchanged from prior reports (~$45–47), so this is an earnings-quality reassessment rather than a price-driven downgrade.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- EPA issues a favorable Class VI draft permit (~July 2026) or final approval (~November 2026) for One Earth CCS, materially de-risking the CI/45Z advantage.
- Treasury finalizes 45Z rules confirming REX's eligibility and credit magnitude through 2029, converting the windfall into a durable earnings stream.
- Core ethanol crush spread inflects positively (ethanol prices turning up rather than down) alongside continued DCO strength, demonstrating the earnings base is not solely credit-driven.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Unfavorable 45Z rule finalization or a failure to meet CI/wage thresholds that cuts the credit stream by >50%.
- EPA permit denial or further multi-quarter slippage, raising write-off risk on the ~$59M CCS spend.
- A crush-spread collapse driving core (ex-credit) operating margins negative for two consecutive quarters.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 print (roughly late August 2026), followed by the EPA Class VI permit milestones in July and November 2026. The September options term structure suggests the market is pricing these same windows.

## Conclusion

REX American Resources is a well-run, exceptionally well-capitalized ethanol producer whose recent earnings acceleration is substantially a 45Z tax-credit and by-product story rather than a durable crush-spread improvement; core ethanol prices fell even as reported profit doubled. The ~$376M cash balance (roughly a quarter of market cap), zero funded debt, and shareholder-friendly buyback discipline provide a genuine downside floor and justify avoiding a bearish stance. The earnings engine depends on unfinalized Treasury rules, on carbon-capture permitting facing hostile Midwest pipeline politics, and on a structurally overcapacitied industry with a flat federal mandate. At ~16.7x trailing earnings inflated by non-recurring credits, no dividend, chairman selling into recent highs with no offsetting open-market buying, and no identifiable near-term re-rating catalyst, the risk/reward is balanced. We maintain HOLD with an overall score of 5.6, and would revisit constructively on favorable EPA/CCS permitting progress or confirmation that the 45Z credit stream is durable through 2029.
