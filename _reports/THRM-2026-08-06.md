## Executive Summary

Gentherm's operating story in the first half of 2026 was better than the auto-supplier tape it sits in. Product revenue grew 11.1% to $809.9M while light-vehicle production in the company's key markets fell 2.0%, per the S&P Global Mobility data cited in the 10-Q. The Automotive Climate and Comfort Solutions line grew 14.1% (12.7% ex-FX), an outperformance the company quantifies at roughly 14 percentage points. The growth is concentrated in content expansion rather than volume: Lumbar and Massage grew 38.2% and Climate and Comfort Electronics 48.1%, both built on the shape-memory-alloy valve architecture that eliminates discrete sensors. Q2 adjusted diluted EPS of $0.75 beat the $0.57 non-GAAP consensus by 31%, the third beat in four quarters, and consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS has been revised up 9.7% over 90 days to $2.92.

Three things keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. First, the equity is primarily a bet on the pending Modine Performance Technologies reverse Morris Trust rather than a clean bet on content growth. Gentherm will issue approximately 21M shares, a 68.4% increase in share count that transfers roughly 40 percentage points of ownership, to acquire a business whose revenue has declined from $1,321M (FY2024) to $1,132M (FY2026) per Modine's own 10-K, at 8.1x pre-synergy LTM adjusted EBITDA versus Gentherm's own 7.1x. Management states the deal is accretive to adjusted EPS "by year two," which is an explicit acknowledgment of year-one dilution.

Second, the quality of the reported beat is mixed. Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin fell 50bp year over year to 11.7% despite 11% revenue growth; gross margin fell 70bp on higher material costs and warranty. All of the 1H margin improvement occurred in Q1 (implied 12.5%) and reversed in Q2. The warranty accrual rose 75% in six months to $13.646M on $5.040M of prior-period estimate revisions. Third, cash conversion is weak and the guide is demanding: unadjusted 1H free cash flow was negative $11.9M, adjusted FCF conversion was 16.1% versus 24.3% a year earlier, and FY guidance of $85-100M requires 2H adjusted FCF of $69-84M, a 4.4-5.3x step-up. FY revenue guidance of $1.55-1.65B implies 2H growth of roughly +2.6% against 1H's +11.1%.

Base case $44 (+5.6%), bull $53 (+27%), bear $32 (-23%). Skew does not support new capital at $41.67, 13.8% below the July 24 high of $48.35 and 54% above the March low of $27.00.

## Company Overview

Gentherm designs and manufactures thermal management and pneumatic comfort systems, organized into Automotive (~97% of product revenue in each of 2023-2025) and Medical. The technology base is resistive heating (wire, carbon fiber including proprietary Carbotex, PTC elements), air-moving blowers, shape-memory-alloy pneumatic valves, and injection-molded valves. Product lines in Q2 2026: Climate Control Seats $217.5M (52% of revenue), Lumbar and Massage $72.6M (+38.2%), Climate Control Interiors $52.5M, Climate and Comfort Electronics $8.7M (+48.1%), Valve Systems $25.1M (-0.2%), Other Automotive $28.4M (-7.5%), Medical $11.4M (+1.0%).

Two structural features of the business model matter for how any forecast should be read. The 10-K states plainly that Gentherm "typically does not have a backlog of firm orders at any point in time" — purchase orders are issued close to production against third-party volume forecasts. The $690M of Q2 "new business awards" and $2.2B for FY2025 are lifetime-revenue estimates on awarded platforms, not contracted backlog, and the 10-K carries a risk factor noting that record awards *increase* forecasting risk. Separately, customer contracts "generally provide annual price reductions over the vehicle production life"; pricing and other reduced 1H26 revenue by $6.1M against $67.5M of volume contribution. Growth here requires continuous cost-down to hold margin.

Direct customer concentration sits with two Tier 1 seat makers: Lear at $234M (16% of FY2025 product revenue) and Adient at $164M (11%). By ultimate OEM destination, Volkswagen and GM were each 12%, BMW 9% (up from 8%), Ford 8%. The 10-K's competition section notes that seat manufacturers are among the parties capable of manufacturing certain products Gentherm makes, a live insourcing risk in the same counterparties that represent 27% of revenue.

Manufacturing is heavily weighted to low-cost geographies: Mexico 5,546 employees, China 1,852, North Macedonia 1,850, Ukraine 1,302, Vietnam 1,195, with Morocco growing from 52 to 764 in a single year. Ukraine's Vynohradiv facility represented roughly 5% of 2025 revenue against only $5.5M of PP&E, a small book exposure with a disproportionate revenue dependency and disclosed power-interruption risk.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 optics versus underlying.** Reported net income collapsed 72% to $18.285M (verified against the retained-earnings roll: accumulated earnings $695.801M → $714.086M with no dividends). But operating income fell only to $82.7M from $107.0M, and the $46.9M gap between operating and pre-tax income was roughly $12.6M of interest and approximately $34M of foreign-currency loss, of which $28.484M was an unrealized remeasurement on a non-current intercompany dollar receivable. The effective tax rate was punitive at roughly 49%. The clean read on 2025 underlying deterioration is adjusted EBITDA margin compressing 90bp to 11.7% on 2.9% revenue growth — real, driven by restructuring, price givebacks, warranty and footprint costs, but far smaller than the GAAP headline. The trailing GAAP P/E of 48.6x on $0.59 TTM EPS is an artifact of these non-operating items and should not be used as a valuation input.

**1H 2026.** Revenue $809.9M (+11.1%); gross margin 23.9% versus 24.2%; SG&A 13.7% of revenue versus 10.9%, inflated by $5.135M of bridge/backstop/facility fees, $21.052M of cash M&A costs and $9.0M of restructuring; GAAP diluted EPS $0.28 versus $0.01; adjusted diluted EPS $1.58 versus $1.05.

The gap between $0.28 GAAP and $1.58 adjusted — $1.30 per share in a single half — deserves emphasis when applying a "14x forward" multiple. Restructuring has been an adjustment in every period reviewed, at escalating size ($6.6M → $12.7M in 1H), across four overlapping plans (2026 salaried plan, 2025 manufacturing footprint plan running through 2027, EMEA Plzeň closure, Asia Shanghai-to-Tianjin move). Restructuring that recurs annually for four consecutive years at rising amounts is closer to a cost of doing business than a one-time item.

**Margin trajectory within the half.** Q2 adjusted EBITDA was $48.785M on $416.166M (11.7%) versus $45.897M on $375.090M (12.2%). Deriving Q1 from the 1H total gives $49.3M on $393.7M, or 12.5%. So the 40bp of 1H margin improvement was entirely Q1, and Q2 gave 50bp back year over year. The 39% year-over-year rise in adjusted EPS was also flattered by the base: Q2 2025 carried an $18.877M unrealized currency loss that reversed to a small gain. Management's "strong operating leverage" language holds at the gross-profit-dollar level (+$6.7M) but not at the margin level.

**Warranty.** The accrual rose from $7.805M to $13.646M (+75%) in six months, driven by $5.040M of adjustments to prior-period estimates versus $1.462M in 1H25, plus $5.443M of current-period expense against $4.546M of claims paid. Two recalls are disclosed. The VW/Alfmeier suction jet pump matter covers 261,257 vehicles (MY2015-2020); Gentherm has not accepted financial responsibility, no accrual or estimate is recorded, and insurance is **not** expected to apply because the production period falls outside coverage, leaving only partial seller indemnification from the 2022 Alfmeier acquisition. The VW/Porsche seat-heating-mat matter (13,508 vehicles) is expected to be insured.

**Cash flow — the weakest element.** 1H26 operating cash flow was $2.272M versus $31.701M; capex $14.203M; unadjusted FCF negative $11.861M versus positive $11.718M. Management attributed the decline to restructuring and M&A expense, but cash M&A ($21.1M) plus cash restructuring ($6.6M) accounted for only a $17.3M delta against a $29.4M OCF decline. The larger driver was receivables, which consumed $58.121M versus $23.690M; AR rose 20.6% to $338.851M against 11% revenue growth, implying DSO of roughly 74 days on the Q2 run rate versus roughly 68.5 days on FY2025 revenue — a five-to-six day deterioration worth about $25M. Some is euro translation, some growth timing; the split is not disclosable from the filings. On the credit side, inventory *fell* from $252.702M to $244.538M on 11% revenue growth, genuine discipline. The supplier finance program was terminated in Q2 with no balance outstanding, removing one working-capital lever.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $213.173M ($165.6M offshore), total debt $273.258M (revolver $260.0M at 4.76% plus $13.3M finance leases), net debt $60.085M, net leverage 0.3x on TTM adjusted EBITDA of $187.712M, liquidity $502.3M. A new Third Amended and Restated $550M secured revolver matures 6/29/2031 with minimum interest coverage and maximum net leverage covenants. Hedges include $100M notional floating-to-fixed swaps (100bp = $2.6M annual sensitivity) and MXN forwards stepped up from $35.1M to $103.7M notional at an average 17.86.

Two corrections worth carrying: screening data labeling 0.94 as debt-to-equity is total liabilities over equity; interest-bearing debt/equity was 0.26 at 12/31/25 and 0.38 at 6/30/26. And the Altman Z of 2.95 (grey zone) is driven almost entirely by EBIT/assets of 0.059, itself depressed by restructuring and price givebacks, not by leverage or liquidity, both of which are healthy (current ratio 1.92). The screen speaks to earnings power, not solvency.

Equity of $723.118M was essentially flat versus 12/31/25 despite $8.638M of net income because AOCI moved from -$0.964M to -$11.905M. In FY2025 the reverse happened: equity rose roughly $103M on $18.3M of earnings, driven by roughly $84M of translation gain. Book value here is dominated by currency translation, which limits the usefulness of P/B as an anchor. Deferred tax assets of $92.957M (with an $11.4M valuation allowance at year-end) are a disclosed write-down candidate.

## Growth Analysis

The core growth engine is credible and measurable. With light-vehicle production in Gentherm's markets down 1.3% in Q2 and S&P Global Mobility forecasting 75.5M units for FY2026 (-2.6% versus 2025), Climate and Comfort Solutions grew 14.1%. The composition matters: heated/ventilated seats are a mature, high-penetration product growing 8-9%, while the incremental dollars come from Lumbar and Massage (+38.2%) and seat-position/hands-on-detection electronics (+48.1%). Content per vehicle expansion in massage, PilotSense and IPS modules is the durable part of the story, and the SMA-valve architecture is the clearest technical differentiation the 10-K describes. China revenue grew 50.8% to $82.3M in Q2; South Korea fell 17.7%.

Against this, the FY guide implies a marked second-half slowdown. Revenue of $1.55-1.65B against 1H's $809.9M leaves 2H at $740-840M, midpoint $790M versus $769.7M in 2H25 — +2.6%. Some of that is conservatism (guidance assumes EUR/USD at 1.16 and a low-single-digit LVP decline, and explicitly excludes any Modine contribution), and management has beaten three of four quarters. But the guide as constructed does not extrapolate 11% growth.

Estimate revisions are positive but not extreme: current year +7.7% over 30 days and +9.7% over 90 days to $2.92; next year +7.3%/+7.6% to $3.28; next quarter +7.5%. This is constructive earnings momentum, not the >30% revision velocity that historically marks a turn worth paying up for. I am explicitly not treating this as a strong-buy revision signal.

New end markets are real but small. Two leading North American furniture brands were selected in Q2 (the fourth consecutive quarter of new home-and-office customers), with management citing visibility to $50-100M of home-and-office revenue by 2028 — an aspiration sourced from a call summary rather than a contracted figure. FDA 510(k) clearance for ThermAffyx (conductive air-free patient warming with securement for robotic surgery) is confirmed in company materials, with initial sales expected in Q3 2026.

Medical is the segment that argues against paying a growth multiple. Revenue of $11.351M in Q2 (+1.0% reported, -0.2% ex-FX) came with a $2.5M segment operating loss versus -$0.3M a year earlier, and -$3.3M in 1H versus roughly break-even. Cumulative goodwill impairment of $19.5M already sits against the Medical unit with $28.1M remaining. Management then paid $34.0M plus a $1.0M earn-out for Innovative Medical Equipment (ThermaZone) on 7/1/2026. The strategic logic — automotive thermal IP transferring into higher-margin medical — is sound, and the capital at risk is modest against a $1.28B market cap, but on current numbers Medical is a widening drag being invested into rather than fixed.

The Modine Performance Technologies acquisition would add roughly $1.1B of revenue and $123M of LTM adjusted EBITDA (after standalone costs, per the issuer footnote), taking pro-forma revenue to $2.6B with roughly one-third from end markets outside light vehicles and net leverage near 1.0x. That is genuine scale and cyclical diversification. It is also, on Modine's own primary-source disclosure, a business with three consecutive years of revenue decline (-14.3% cumulatively) and 18.1% gross margin versus Gentherm's 23-24%. Segment operating margin has improved every year (8.5% → 9.3% → 9.7%), achieved largely by cutting segment SG&A from $106M to $79M (-23% in FY2026 alone), which means the easy cost is already out and the $25M of identified synergies must come from somewhere else.

## Valuation Assessment

At $41.67: market cap $1.279B, EV $1.340B, EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA 7.14x, EV/FY26E adjusted EBITDA midpoint ($192.5M) 6.96x. On consensus adjusted EPS, 14.3x FY2026 ($2.92) and 12.7x FY2027 ($3.28). Trailing GAAP P/E of 48.6x is not usable given the FX and restructuring distortion described above.

Peer context is mixed and the sector is heterogeneous: median peer P/E 24.1 (XPEL 24.1, ADNT 30.9, VC 20.1, SMP 10.5, FOXF negative). On P/E, THRM at 14.3x forward adjusted looks discounted. On EV/EBITDA, 7.1x sits within the normal 5-7x band for Tier-2 auto suppliers rather than below it, and the two measures disagree because THRM's adjusted EPS strips $1.30 per half-year of charges that keep recurring. Adjusting for that, the "cheap on P/E" case weakens materially. Median sell-side target of $50 (mean $47.71, high $59, low $34) sits 20% above spot, but three of four recent target increases followed the stock rather than led it, and one broker downgraded to Hold. Analyst targets are a reasonable corroborating input here, not independent confirmation.

The more important valuation question is what the pro-forma entity is worth, since the share issuance is expected in early Q4 2026. Building it from the disclosed inputs: FY26E Gentherm adjusted EBITDA $192.5M + PT $123M + $25M synergies ≈ $340M. Post-close net debt: $60.1M today, plus $34M for IME (paid 7/1), plus the $250M SpinCo delayed-draw term loan funding the $210M cash distribution, less 2H free cash flow — roughly $300-340M, consistent with the company's stated ~1.0x pro-forma leverage. Shares ≈ 51.7M.

- At 7.5x: EV $2.55B − $320M = $2.23B equity → **$43.1/share**
- At 8.0x: EV $2.72B − $320M = $2.40B → $46.4
- At 6.5x: EV $2.21B − $340M = $1.87B → $36.2

So the current price already embeds roughly 7.4x on synergized pro-forma EBITDA. A re-rating case requires the market to award a genuine multiple premium for one-third non-light-vehicle revenue and a path to mid-teens EBITDA margin. That is plausible but unearned; PT's off-highway end markets (agriculture, construction) may be near a cyclical trough, in which case the acquired EBITDA base is understated and the deal is cheaper than 8.1x on normalized earnings. That is the strongest bull argument and I give it real weight, but it is a claim about a cycle turn, not a verifiable fact in the filings.

The $400M three-year repurchase authorization (31.3% of current market cap, 18.6% of the roughly $2.15B pro-forma equity value) is the clearest identified catalyst for multiple support. Two caveats keep me from underwriting it. Gentherm repurchased **zero** shares in 1H26 with roughly $110M available under the prior program, at prices of $26-29 — a period when the stock was demonstrably cheaper than today. And RMT structures commonly constrain post-closing repurchases because buybacks can affect the ownership-continuity analysis underpinning tax-free treatment; the Tax Matters Agreement is executed at closing and its repurchase constraints are unverified. How much of the $400M is actually deployable in the two years after the Distribution is unresolved and material.

Valuation score 5.6: not expensive on adjusted metrics, but the discount narrows sharply once recurring "one-time" charges are normalized, and the pro-forma arithmetic shows the current price is already at a fair multiple on synergized earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory in the core is improving. The 14-point outperformance versus light-vehicle production is content capture in massage, seat electronics and hands-on detection, not sector beta, and it has persisted for multiple quarters. Gentherm's platform-agnostic position — selling to any Tier 1 seat maker at OEM direction — is a structural advantage over a captive supplier, and 382 issued patents plus the sensor-eliminating SMA valve design support pricing on the differentiated lines.

Four offsets keep this from a high score. Chinese component suppliers are flagged in the 10-K as building positions with Chinese OEMs and expanding into Europe and North America with aggressive pricing that spills into global negotiations, and China is now over 15% of revenue and growing 50.8%, so Gentherm is both beneficiary and target. Contractual annual price givebacks are a permanent margin headwind. Lear and Adient, at 27% of revenue combined, are identified in the 10-K as capable of manufacturing certain Gentherm products. And the softer lines are not small: Valve Systems flat, Other Automotive -7.5%, Medical loss-making.

On the pro-forma view, the mix argument cuts both ways. End-market diversification genuinely improves — one-third of revenue outside light vehicles, including cooling modules for mission-critical stationary power. But direct customer concentration does not improve, and the acquired asset carries structurally lower gross margin (18.1%) in a metal-intensive, more commoditized heat-exchanger market exposed to aluminium, copper and steel. Modine is retaining Climate Solutions (FY2026 sales $2,062M, +43%, 15.6% segment operating margin, driven by data-center cooling) and targeting 50-70% annual data-center growth. Gentherm is buying the vehicular asset that Modine is selling to fund the secular one. That does not make the purchase wrong — cyclical assets bought near troughs at 8x can work — but the buyer is not obviously acquiring competitive advantage. Score 5.6, effectively unchanged from the prior 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal completion and dilution mechanics.** Two separate shareholder approvals are required — the share issuance and a charter amendment raising authorized shares from 55,000,000 (post-merger outstanding of roughly 51.7M would leave only about 3.3M for all equity plans). A customary IRS ruling on tax-free treatment, SpinCo financing completion and regulatory approvals are also conditions. Outside date 3/31/2027, extendable to 6/30/2027. A $45M termination fee is payable by Gentherm in specified circumstances. The 40/60 ownership split is "subject to adjustment": per the company's summary of the Merger Agreement, the $210M cash distribution *decreases* if additional Gentherm shares must be issued to support tax-free treatment, an embedded equity-for-cash swap that could push existing holders below 60%. The formula and any caps are unverified; I have not read the merger agreement or the S-4/Form 10 carve-out financials, which is the highest-value remaining document (PT segment D&A is undisclosed, so my year-one dilution sensitivity rests on a ~$45M assumption).

**Year-one adjusted EPS dilution.** Testing management's "accretive by year two" language: neutrality on a 68.4% share-count increase requires adjusted net income to rise 68.4%, or roughly $65-68M of PT contribution against FY2026E adjusted net income of $95-100M. PT at $123M adjusted EBITDA less roughly $45M assumed D&A less roughly $15M incremental interest ($250M at ~6%) gives $63M pre-tax, or $44-47M after tax, plus roughly $18M after-tax from full synergies = $62-65M. That reconciles with year-one dilution and gives a clean monitoring framework: the math depends on delivering $25M of synergies and on PT revenue stabilizing after a 14.3% three-year decline. Purchase-accounting amortization (including on the acquired brand) will worsen GAAP results further.

**Unquantified recall exposure.** The VW/Alfmeier suction jet pump matter covers 261,257 vehicles with no accrual, no estimate, no accepted responsibility, no expected insurance coverage for the production period, and only partial seller indemnification. There is no basis in the filings for sizing this.

**Warranty escalation.** $5.040M of prior-period true-ups in 1H26 versus $1.462M in 1H25, with the accrual up 75%. Non-recurring by definition, but it has now recurred at escalating size.

**Cash-flow guide.** 2H26 adjusted FCF of $69-84M against $15.8M in 1H is the single most demanding line in guidance. Seasonality supports a stronger second half (FY2025 2H OCF was roughly $74M versus $31.7M in 1H) and the $58.1M AR build should partially reverse, but adjusted FCF conversion is one of five named executive compensation measures, which puts both an operating and an incentive spotlight on it.

**Other.** No firm backlog; awards are lifetime estimates. Ukraine roughly 5% of revenue. Tariffs: the February 2026 Supreme Court decision struck certain IEEPA tariffs and refund claims were filed with immaterial receipts, but the administration has invoked other authorities and FY guidance is conditioned on "tariffs currently in effect." Deferred tax assets of $93.0M carry write-down risk. Sector-level: this is a Consumer Discretionary cyclical facing a light-vehicle market forecast to shrink 2.6% — conditions under which the marginal dollar of vehicle content spend is the first to be deferred.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 65.8% sits 21.7 points above 1-year realized volatility of 44.1%, with the term structure in backwardation (65.8% at 14 days, 59.2% at 42 days, 44.1% at 133 days) and a put/call skew of 19.4% characterized by the data as elevated put demand. The nearest expiry prices a ±13.8% move within 14 days despite no scheduled earnings (Q2 reported 7/23; Q3 due late October). The most likely referents are deal-process events — S-4 effectiveness, proxy timing, the IRS ruling — or simply thin liquidity: only 30 contracts across three expirations, so the reading is low-confidence. Put/call open interest of 0.80 is call-tilted, which cuts against reading the skew as directional conviction.

Short interest of 6.3% of float with 4.1 days to cover is moderate and probably partly technical: in a reverse Morris Trust, arbitrageurs typically go long the distributing company against short the issuer of the stock consideration, and both the elevated put demand and the short base are consistent with hedging of stock-consideration exposure rather than a fundamental bear thesis. The options market is pricing event risk around the transaction, not directional deterioration. It corroborates the case for waiting rather than adding.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $53 (+27%)

Content per vehicle keeps compounding at double digits against a declining production base; Lumbar/Massage and seat electronics sustain 30-40% growth as attach rates rise across mainstream platforms. 2H26 guidance proves conservative (the +2.6% implied growth is a low bar against 1H's +11.1%), and adjusted FCF converts to the high end at $100M, validating the 46-50% full-year conversion. PT's off-highway end markets prove to be near a cyclical trough, so the $123M acquired EBITDA base is understated and 8.1x pre-synergy is cheap on normalized earnings; the $25M of synergies land on schedule and management's "clear path to mid-teens adjusted EBITDA margin" becomes credible with a year of evidence. The combined entity re-rates from 7.4x toward 8.5x on roughly $340M of synergized EBITDA, and a partially deployable $400M authorization absorbs Modine-shareholder flowback and shrinks the count from 51.7M toward 50.5M. That path gets to $53-54.

### Bear Case — $32 (-23%)

Q2's 50bp adjusted EBITDA margin decline was the start of compression rather than warranty noise: price givebacks, tariff-adjacent material costs and a fifth consecutive year of restructuring keep incremental margins below revenue growth. 2H FCF misses the $69-84M requirement as receivables stay elevated, and the market re-reads adjusted EPS given the $1.30-per-half gap to GAAP. PT revenue declines a fourth year on North American heavy-duty and agricultural weakness, synergies slip, and purchase-accounting amortization makes GAAP results unattractive to generalist buyers. Post-closing tax-continuity constraints prove to bar most of the buyback in the two years that matter. The combined entity trades at 6.0-6.5x on $310M of EBITDA against $340-360M of net debt: $29-36, call it $32. A deal break is a milder version — standalone at 6.0x FY26E EBITDA of $185M against roughly $95M of net debt is about $33, less any termination fee.

The bear case does not require a recession; it requires only that margin and cash conversion behave as they did in Q2 rather than as guided.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the shareholder vote and charter amendment (proxy expected ahead of a targeted early-Q4 2026 close), then the Q3 print in late October, which is the first test of both the implied 2H revenue deceleration and the required FCF step-up.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin at or above 12.2% (reversing the Q2 year-over-year decline) with gross margin stabilized at or above 23.9% and no further prior-period warranty true-ups above $1.5M.
2. Q3 adjusted free cash flow of $35M or more, putting the $85-100M full-year guide on a straight-line path, together with DSO recovering below 70 days.
3. Disclosure — in the S-4, Form 10 or Tax Matters Agreement — that at least $200M of the $400M authorization is deployable within two years post-Distribution, combined with actual repurchases commencing. Alternatively, a price near $34-35 (roughly 6.5x pro-forma synergized EBITDA) that restores 25%+ upside to the $44 base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 revenue or adjusted EBITDA guidance cut, or adjusted FCF guidance reduced below $85M.
2. PT revenue disclosed in the S-4/Form 10 as declining again in the September or December quarters, or synergy guidance reduced below $25M.
3. Any accrual established for the VW/Alfmeier recall above roughly $25M, or an ownership-adjustment disclosure that pushes existing Gentherm holders materially below 59%.
4. Deal termination in circumstances triggering the $45M fee, absent an offsetting capital-return commitment.

**On the score trajectory:** the sequence 5.3 (AVOID) → 6.0 → 5.7 → 5.5 reflects a business whose financial health and growth genuinely improved (net leverage 0.3x, $550M revolver to 2031, 11% growth against a shrinking market, +9.7% 90-day estimate revisions) while competitive position and now risk absorbed the offsets. This report marks a modest decline, and the reasons are specific rather than price-driven: the Q2 year-over-year adjusted EBITDA margin decline, warranty true-ups tripling, 1H unadjusted FCF turning negative, and the 2H revenue guide implying +2.6%. The 54% rally off the March low is not itself a reason to mark the stock down, but it has consumed most of the gap to a defensible base case, which changes the risk/reward rather than the thesis.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Gentherm is executing well on the part of the business it controls: content per vehicle is compounding at double digits against a light-vehicle market forecast to shrink 2.6%, three of the last four quarters beat consensus, guidance was raised on all three metrics, and the balance sheet at 0.3x net leverage with $502.3M of liquidity gives management room. At 7.1x EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA and 14.3x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS, nothing about the standalone valuation is stretched.

The reason not to buy is that the standalone valuation is about to stop being the relevant one. A 68.4% increase in share count to acquire a business with three straight years of revenue decline and 18.1% gross margin, at 8.1x pre-synergy versus Gentherm's own 7.1x, dilutive to adjusted EPS in year one, is an outcome that depends on synergy delivery and a cycle turn in off-highway equipment.
