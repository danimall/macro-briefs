## Executive Summary

TSMC remains the single most important company in the global AI infrastructure buildout. Q1 2026 delivered NT$1,134B in revenue (+35% YoY), 66.2% gross margins (+740 bps YoY), and diluted EPS of NT$22.08 (US$3.49 per ADR), beating consensus by approximately 4.5%. Management raised full-year revenue growth guidance to "above 30%" and guided Q2 revenue to $39.0-40.2B with gross margins of 65.5-67.5%. Leading-edge demand remains exceptionally strong, with management indicating robust capacity utilization across advanced nodes.

The stock trades at approximately 26x current-year forward earnings against 9-10% upward estimate revisions over the past 90 days, a consistent beat track record (4/4 quarters), and strong demand visibility from major customers including Nvidia, Apple, AMD, and hyperscalers. The structural geopolitical discount persists, but TSMC Arizona posting approximately NT$18.81B (~$600M) in net income in Q1 2026 provides tangible evidence that geographic diversification can be economically viable. At current prices, the stock sits approximately 5% below Morningstar's $428 fair value estimate.

The primary risk remains Taiwan Strait concentration, which is structural and cannot be fully hedged in the near term. Secondary risks include customer diversification pressure (Apple-Intel preliminary agreement, AMD-Samsung MoU), though these are driven by TSMC's capacity constraints rather than competitive weakness. Position sizing at 2-3% reflects the asymmetric upside potential constrained by irreducible geopolitical tail risk.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, holding an estimated ~72% share of the global foundry market. The company operates a pure-play model: it does not design its own chips, which eliminates customer competition and has enabled it to capture virtually every major fabless semiconductor designer as a client. Revenue is concentrated in High-Performance Computing (61%) and Smartphones (26%), with advanced nodes (7nm and below) constituting 74% of wafer revenue.

The company is executing an unprecedented geographic expansion: a planned U.S. investment expected to reach $165B (including three new fabs, two advanced packaging facilities, and an R&D center), Japan JASM joint venture (first reported profit in Q1 2026), and planned European operations. This expansion directly addresses the primary criticism of TSMC — geographic concentration in Taiwan — while simultaneously positioning the company for government-adjacent demand.

## Growth Analysis

TSMC's growth trajectory is underpinned by multiple converging demand drivers:

1. **AI Infrastructure Demand:** Management disclosed an AI accelerator revenue CAGR target of 54-56% through 2029. Nvidia disclosed $95.2B in total outstanding inventory purchase and long-term supply/capacity obligations in its FY2026 10-K, with TSMC as the primary advanced foundry supplier.

2. **2nm Technology Ramp:** TSMC is aggressively building 2nm capacity in Kaohsiung, Taiwan, with management indicating strong customer demand for the node. This drives both revenue growth (higher wafer prices) and margin expansion (technology premium).

3. **Advanced Packaging (CoWoS):** Demand for advanced packaging substantially exceeds current supply. TSMC has engaged outside partners for interposer production to supplement capacity. This is a higher-margin, higher-value revenue stream that compounds with leading-edge wafer revenue.

4. **Global Semiconductor TAM:** Management projects the industry reaching $1.5 trillion by 2030. TSMC's market position suggests it captures a disproportionate share of this growth.

Full-year 2026 guidance of "above 30%" revenue growth, following 8 consecutive quarters of double-digit growth, establishes a trajectory that supports the current multiple and potentially justifies expansion as visibility improves.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $408, TSM trades at approximately 26x current-year forward earnings ($15.61 consensus). Using next-year estimates ($19.52), the forward P/E compresses to ~21x.

**Relative to peers:** The PHLX Semiconductor Index trades at ~26x forward earnings. TSMC on current-year forward earnings trades roughly in line with the index, though on next-year estimates it trades at a discount reflecting expected earnings growth.

**Absolute valuation:**
- Morningstar fair value: $428 (5% upside)
- Yahoo Finance 1-year target estimate: ~$463 (~13% upside)
- Analyst high target: $600.00 (47% upside)

The discount on next-year estimates reflects geopolitical risk — specifically Taiwan Strait concentration. This risk is real and structural, but it is being actively mitigated (Arizona profitability represents a positive data point for diversification economics). The current discount may overcompensate given improving diversification evidence, though the tail risk remains unquantifiable.

At ~21x next-year P/E on earnings growing approximately 25% (from $15.61 to $19.52), the PEG ratio is approximately 0.8x — below the 1.5x threshold that typically characterizes expensive growth stocks. The valuation is not cheap in absolute terms, but when normalized for growth trajectory and quality, it represents a reasonable entry point with more upside than downside to consensus estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market share:** Approximately 72% of the global foundry market. The company maintains a dominant position at leading-edge nodes where few competitors operate at scale.

**Technology lead:** 3nm in volume production, 2nm ramping 2026, A16 and A13 in development. TSMC's historical yield track record at advanced nodes remains industry-leading.

**Customer lock-in dynamics:** Nvidia's substantial supply obligations, hyperscaler custom silicon commitments, Apple's multi-year roadmap — these represent contracted, multi-year relationships that cannot be replicated by competitors for years even if technology parity were achieved.

**Capacity constraint dynamics:** Apple exploring Intel, AMD signing an MoU with Samsung to discuss foundry partnership opportunities — these represent customer diversification driven by TSMC being unable to produce enough, not by competitive weakness. A company whose customers are forced to seek alternatives because its capacity is fully sold retains pricing power and can justify aggressive capex. This demand overhang supports continued pricing power.

**Intel as emerging threat:** Under Lip-Bu Tan, Intel is pursuing foundry customers with EMIB packaging technology. The Apple preliminary agreement is symbolically significant. However, Intel remains behind on yields, volume capability, and customer trust. TSMC's response (aggressive 2nm investment, CoWoS expansion, overseas expansion) suggests management is not complacent.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical — Taiwan Strait (Critical):** ~90% of the world's most advanced chip production remains in Taiwan. China's military capability continues expanding. TSMC Chairman Liu has stated explicitly that invasion would render Taiwan factories non-operable. No investment thesis can eliminate this risk — it can only be sized for.

**Mitigation evidence:** TSMC Arizona posting ~$600M net income in Q1 2026 is a positive data point for geographic diversification economics. Japan JASM reporting its first quarterly profit (NT$951M) adds a second proof point. However, one profitable quarter does not prove that multi-fab overseas economics will be sustainably margin-accretive at scale. TSMC itself has previously cautioned that overseas ramps can dilute margins during early phases.

**Customer concentration:** TSMC's 2025 20-F discloses that its top 10 customers accounted for approximately 78% of net revenue, with its largest customer at 19% and second-largest at 17%. Apple's preliminary Intel agreement signals intent to diversify. If 5-10% of Apple volume shifts to Intel over 2-3 years, revenue impact would be modest (~2-4% of total) given overall growth, but it removes some pricing leverage.

**Capex execution:** $52-56B in 2026, multiple new fabs and advanced packaging facilities simultaneously. Yield problems, construction delays, or demand softening could impair returns. History suggests TSMC executes technology transitions with higher-than-industry success rates, but the current cycle is unprecedented in scale.

**Macro/inflation:** Rising commodity costs from global supply disruptions add incremental cost pressure. Management acknowledged financial impact secondary to ramp costs but any additional margin headwinds matter at a 66% gross margin business (there's limited room for error on the upside).

## Conclusion

TSMC at $408 represents a high-quality compounder executing at historically exceptional levels, priced at a meaningful discount to peers on next-year estimates given geopolitical risk that is being actively mitigated. The combination of 30%+ revenue growth, 66% gross margins, strong capacity utilization across advanced nodes, rising estimates, consistent beat pattern, and positive early evidence on diversification economics supports continued accumulation at 2-3% portfolio sizing.

The primary constraint on sizing is irreducible tail risk — Taiwan Strait concentration remains structural regardless of Arizona/Japan progress. This is a position to hold and add to on weakness, not to over-concentrate regardless of fundamental quality. The risk/reward profile favors continued ownership with the understanding that geopolitical events could impair the position by 20%+ in ways that fundamental analysis cannot predict or prevent.
