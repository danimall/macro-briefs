## Executive Summary

Liberty Energy is a leading North American hydraulic fracturing services provider operating approximately 40 active fleets, primarily in the Permian, Appalachian, and DJ basins. The company is simultaneously executing a strategic pivot into distributed power generation for data centers through its Liberty Power Innovations (LPI) subsidiary, which has signed a marquee 1 GW partnership with Vantage Data Centers but has generated effectively zero revenue to date.

The financial trajectory of the core business is severe: revenue declined from $4.75B (2023) to $4.01B (2025), operating income collapsed 90% from $761M to $73M, and operating margins compressed from 16% to 1.8%. FY2025 net income of $148M was materially supported by $162.6M in unrealized gains on Oklo equity securities. Free cash flow has effectively disappeared ($14M in FY2025 vs. robust prior years). However, three consecutive earnings beats (Q3 2025 through Q1 2026), massive positive estimate revisions (current-year EPS swinging from -$0.46 to +$0.31 in 90 days), management commentary on equipment cannibalization creating supply-side tightening, and a sophisticated director's open-market purchase collectively suggest the earnings trough may be forming. At $33.79 and ~37x trailing earnings (which include unrealized investment gains), the stock has already rallied 194% off its lows, pricing in meaningful recovery that remains uncertain.

The balance of evidence — genuine cyclical inflection signals offset by stretched valuation on thin earnings, pre-revenue LPI execution risk, and elevated short interest — points to a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Company Overview

Liberty Energy is one of the largest integrated completions services providers in North America, offering hydraulic fracturing, wireline, proppant delivery, field gas processing, and data analytics to onshore E&P operators. The company operates proprietary technology including digiFrac electric/hybrid pumps (~25% lower CO₂ emissions vs. Tier IV diesel), Liberty Quiet Fleet noise reduction systems, and a vertically integrated supply chain encompassing Permian Basin sand mines.

In early 2025, founder/CEO Christopher Wright departed to become U.S. Secretary of Energy, with COO/President Ron Gusek succeeding as CEO. The company simultaneously launched Liberty Power Innovations (LPI), targeting distributed power generation for data centers, mining, and industrial customers. LPI signed a strategic partnership with Vantage Data Centers in January 2026 for up to 1 GW of power capacity over five years. In March 2026, LBRT issued $475M in zero-coupon convertible notes due 2032 to fund LPI's buildout, with management targeting 3 GW of deployment by 2029.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

Revenue peaked at $4.75B in FY2023 and has declined 16% to $4.01B in FY2025, driven by completions pricing pressure from equipment oversupply and moderating E&P activity. The profit decline is disproportionately steep:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $4.75B | $4.32B | $4.01B |
| Operating Income | $761M | $389M | $73M |
| Operating Margin | 16.0% | 9.0% | 1.8% |
| Net Income | $556M | $316M | $148M |

FY2025 net income of $148M includes $162.6M in gains on investments (primarily Oklo mark-to-market). The core operating business generated only $73M in operating income on $4B in revenue. Adjusted ROCE declined from 42% (FY2023) to 19% (FY2024) to 8% (FY2025).

Q1 2026 showed early stabilization: revenue of $1.02B (+4.5% YoY), non-GAAP EPS of $0.06 (well above consensus of -$0.13), and record pumping efficiencies. This was the third consecutive earnings beat following a Q2 2025 miss.

### Cash Flow & Capital Intensity

Operating cash flow remained healthy at $610M in FY2025, but $595M in capital expenditure (fleet upgrades to digiFrac electric, maintenance, and LPI investment) consumed nearly all of it, leaving free cash flow of just $14M. The company is in a heavy reinvestment phase. Share repurchases collapsed from $128M (FY2024) to $24M (FY2025), confirming the capital allocation pivot toward LPI.

### Balance Sheet

Cash stands at just $27.6M. Total debt is approximately $307M (short-term $94M, long-term $213M), plus $230M drawn on the $750M revolving credit facility. The March 2026 convertible offering adds $475M of debt (zero coupon, due 2032). After spending $77M on capped call transactions, net proceeds of ~$511M are earmarked for LPI. The company is also seeking a credit amendment permitting up to $600M in additional bridge financing.

Debt-to-equity sits at 0.71x, moderate for OFS but rising. The Z-Score of 2.91 places LBRT in the "grey zone." The Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 is positive but based partly on net income that includes unrealized investment gains.

### Estimate Revisions

Consensus estimates have swung dramatically upward across all periods:

|--------|-------------|---------|----------|
| Current Year | -$0.46 | +$0.31 | +168% |
| Next Year | -$0.09 | +$0.35 | +516% |

This momentum, combined with three consecutive beats (Q3 2025: +1,603%, Q4 2025: +151%, Q1 2026: +206%), signals that the earnings trough is likely forming. However, absolute earnings levels remain thin: consensus expects $0.31 EPS in FY2026 and $0.35 in FY2027. At $33.79, the stock trades at 109x FY2026E and 97x FY2027E earnings. Even assuming these estimates rise further as completions pricing stabilizes, the stock is pricing in substantial earnings normalization that hasn't materialized in reported results.

## Growth Analysis

### Completions Recovery — Supply Destruction as Catalyst

Management's MD&A provides a specific, named recovery mechanism: "pricing pressures on completions services, combined with the slowdown in activity, have driven an acceleration in equipment cannibalization and attrition" while "underinvestment in next generation technology has limited replacement of lost capacity." This supply destruction dynamic — aging fleets being retired faster than replacements are built — is the fundamental case for margin recovery in completions services.

The rig count declined to 709 (Q4 2025 vs. 765 Q4 2024), but completions demand is projected "flat to firm" in 2026. WTI averaged $65.45/bbl in 2025 (down from $76.63 in 2024), and global oil markets are balancing a structural oil surplus with OPEC+ production increases. Oil prices remaining near or above FY2025 levels should support E&P activity and create conditions for completions pricing recovery. However, this pricing recovery is a multi-quarter process and the magnitude is uncertain — the oversupply of equipment built during 2022-2023 has not been fully absorbed.

### LPI — The Data Center Power Option

LPI represents the highest-beta growth opportunity and the highest-risk component. The Vantage Data Centers partnership (1 GW over 5 years, 400 MW reserved for 2027) provides commercial validation, and the convergence of AI-driven power demand with multi-year grid interconnection queues creates a genuine structural opportunity.

However, LPI is pre-revenue. The 3 GW target by 2029 is aspirational with no binding contracts beyond the Vantage development agreement, and final power purchase contracts with end-user data center tenants remain to be executed. The 330 MW Texas preliminary reservation requires a binding contract. Management has no direct prior experience in distributed power. The power industry has established competitors (Enchanted Rock, Scale Microgrids, major industrial gas turbine OEMs) with deeper capital access and customer relationships.

The $475M convertible + potential $600M bridge financing represents substantial financial commitment to a business line with zero revenue. If LPI fails to generate meaningful revenue by 2028, LBRT will carry significant incremental debt with minimal return.

## Valuation Assessment

LBRT trades at ~37x trailing P/E, but this figure is misleading because FY2025 EPS of $0.89 includes $162.6M (~$1.00/share) in unrealized Oklo investment gains. Stripping these out, core operating EPS was approximately -$0.11, making the trailing P/E effectively undefined (negative earnings on core operations).

Against consensus estimates:
- FY2026E: $0.31 → 109x forward P/E
- FY2027E: $0.35 → 97x forward P/E

Peer medians: P/E of 20.8x. TDW trades at 13.3x, OII at 10.7x, WHD at 23.1x. LBRT's premium is 4-5x peer median, implicitly pricing in the LPI option value and a substantial completions recovery.

The consensus analyst price target range is $21-$40, with a mean of $32.77 and median of $32. The current price of $33.79 is above the consensus mean. The analyst sentiment trend is degrading: strong buys have decreased from 3 to 2 and holds have increased from 5 to 7 over the past three months.

The stock has rallied 194% from its 52-week low of $9.90. The 52-week high of $34.42 is only 2% above the current price. For an OFS company generating $0.31 in estimated EPS, this pricing embeds aggressive recovery assumptions.

## Competitive Landscape

In completions, Liberty is a Tier 1 operator competing with Halliburton, Patterson-UTI (which merged with NexTier), ProPetro, and RPC. Liberty's competitive advantages include proprietary digiFrac electric technology, vertical integration (sand mines, logistics, fuel delivery), and a strong technology reputation. The supply destruction dynamic should preferentially benefit operators with newer, more efficient fleets like Liberty.

However, the completions market is intensely cyclical and pricing-sensitive. Liberty's operating margin compression from 16% to 1.8% demonstrates that even Tier 1 technology does not insulate against industry-wide overcapacity. Market share stability is positive, but it hasn't prevented severe margin deterioration.

In distributed power, LPI is entering a competitive market against established players with advantages in capital access, existing customer bases, and vendor relationships (per the company's own 10-K risk factors). Liberty's advantages — expertise in modular construction, natural gas logistics, and engineering from oil & gas operations — are plausible but unproven in the power context.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks (Not Fully Priced)

1. **LPI Execution Risk:** The market is assigning meaningful option value to LPI (justifying the premium over completions peers), but LPI has zero revenue, no binding power purchase agreements with end users, and faces experienced competitors. Failure would leave LBRT with substantial incremental debt and no growth offset to a mature completions business.

2. **Completions Pricing Recovery Timing:** Supply destruction is a real dynamic, but the timeline is uncertain. If pricing remains depressed through FY2026-2027, the company will continue generating negligible earnings while servicing increased debt. Current consensus ($0.31/$0.35 EPS) barely covers the dividend ($0.35/share annualized).

3. **Oil Price Sensitivity:** WTI averaged $65.45/bbl in 2025 amid a structural oil surplus with OPEC+ production increases. Further declines would reduce E&P capital spending and push completions pricing recovery further out. At 109x forward earnings, the stock has limited margin of safety for a downturn scenario.

4. **Convertible Dilution:** The $475M of zero-coupon converts create ~9-10% dilution at the ~$41-42 conversion price. Capped calls mitigate dilution up to ~$72, but between $42-$72, shareholders bear full dilutive impact.

5. **Customer Concentration:** Top 5 customers represent 39% of revenue, up from 34% in 2023. Concentration is increasing as the market contracts.

### Options Market Signal

IV at 55.1% sits below 1-year historical vol of 60.1% — options are modestly cheap to realized volatility. Since April 8, put/call OI ratio collapsed from 1.36 to 0.18, and ATM IV fell by 24.3pp alongside a 16% price increase. Institutional put holders have aggressively unwound downside hedges. Near-term put skew of 5.8% indicates residual caution, but the overall positioning shift is bullish, suggesting informed participants view the post-earnings inflection as durable.

Short interest at 11.6% of float (2.8 days to cover) remains elevated, indicating meaningful institutional bearish positioning persists despite the rally. A continued positive fundamental trajectory could trigger short covering, providing further upside pressure.

### Insider Activity

Director Arjun Murti made an open-market purchase of approximately $250K worth of shares at $25.79 in February 2026. Murti is a former Goldman Sachs senior energy analyst and one of the most respected voices in energy markets. This purchase at an above-average price (not a grant or automatic plan) carries significant informational weight. CFO Michael Stock and CLO R. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$40-42, +18-24%)

Completions pricing inflects in H2 2026 as supply destruction accelerates (fleet retirements exceed replacements). Oil prices remaining supportive sustain elevated E&P capex. LPI secures 1-2 binding power purchase agreements, de-risking the growth story. FY2027 EPS normalizes to $1.50-2.00 as margins recover toward 8-10% (vs. 1.8% currently). At 20x normalized EPS, fair value reaches $30-40. LPI option value adds $5-10/share on a DCF basis if 1 GW is contracted by 2028.

### Bear Case (~$16-20, -41-53%)

Completions pricing remains depressed through 2027 as equipment oversupply persists longer than expected. Oil prices decline further on surplus conditions, reducing E&P activity. LPI fails to convert the Vantage development agreement into binding PPAs, and the $1B+ capital investment generates minimal return. Oklo investment reverses, removing the mark-to-market earnings support. EPS remains below $0.50. At 20x peer-average P/E on $0.50 EPS = $10. With LPI option value at $5-8, stock reaches $15-18.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. Completions operating margin recovers above 6% for two consecutive quarters, confirming pricing inflection
2. LPI secures a binding power purchase agreement with a creditworthy end user for ≥200 MW
3. Stock pulls back to $20-22 (~65x FY2026E EPS), providing margin of safety for the cyclical recovery

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. Q2 or Q3 2026 operating margins remain below 3% despite elevated oil prices, indicating structural rather than cyclical impairment
2. Vantage partnership is terminated or materially scaled back
3. LPI capital requirements expand beyond $1.2B cumulative without revenue generation

### Reassessment Timeline
Next earnings (likely July 2026) is the natural reassessment point. By Q2 2026, completions pricing trends for the summer season should be visible, and any LPI contract developments should provide more clarity on the power pivot.

## Conclusion

Liberty Energy presents a genuinely split narrative: a mature completions business at cyclical trough with credible supply-destruction dynamics that should eventually drive margin recovery, layered with a pre-revenue data center power bet that could be transformational or value-destructive. The positive estimate revision momentum is real — estimates have swung from materially negative to positive across all forward periods, and three consecutive earnings beats confirm improving operational execution.

However, at $33.79 the stock has already priced a significant portion of this recovery, trading at 109x FY2026E EPS and above the consensus analyst mean target. The premium to completions peers (4-5x the sector median P/E) embeds LPI option value that has not been commercially validated through binding contracts. Insider buying from Murti at $25.79 and the collapse in institutional put positioning are genuine positive signals, but they were established at prices 20-30% below current levels.

The evidence does not support an AVOID — the cyclical inflection signals are too numerous and concrete to dismiss, and the stock is not structurally impaired. Equally, the evidence does not support a BUY — the valuation on current and near-term earnings is extreme, LPI is pre-revenue with substantial execution risk, and the 194% rally has compressed the risk/reward. HOLD is the appropriate call, with clear upgrade criteria tied to margin recovery evidence and LPI commercial milestones.
