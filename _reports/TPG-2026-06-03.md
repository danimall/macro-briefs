**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TPG is a global alternative asset manager with $306.2B in AUM as of 3/31/2026, nearly tripled from $113.6B in 2021 through organic growth and two transformational deals (Angelo Gordon in late 2023, Peppertree in mid-2025). The investable story is the shift in the firm's earnings mix toward fee-related earnings (FRE), which compounded 36% YoY in Q1 2026 to $246.9M at a ~44% margin, pushing LTM FRE above $1B for the first time. Management has guided to more than $50B of 2026 capital raising and a 47% FRE margin target. The market that misreads TPG's GAAP results is making a real error: Q1 2026 printed a $(0.05) GAAP basic loss driven by $461.8M of non-cash unrealized performance-allocation markdowns, while realized carry was positive at $323.4M and adjusted distributable EPS was $0.70, beating ~$0.61 consensus.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is concrete and falsifiable. First, near-term earnings momentum is negative: the single covering analyst's current-quarter EPS estimate was cut 9.6% in 30 days and 13.5% in 90 days, and next-quarter and next-year estimates are also drifting lower. This is the opposite of the positive-revision profile that would justify a high-conviction long. Second, the carry engine is structurally lumpy: a single quarter of PE markdowns flipped the firm to a GAAP loss, and worst-case clawback exposure rose to $2.66B with $6.8B of cumulative performance allocations subject to reversal. Third, governance overhangs are unresolved and dated: a controlled-company dual-class structure, a $782M Tax Receivable Agreement that accelerates on change of control, and a "Sunset" mechanism whose timing is unsettled. Fourth, the options market is pricing elevated put demand (8.9% skew) and rich near-term IV (46.7% vs 37.1% historical vol) into a stock down roughly 42% from its January 2026 high of $70.38.

The bull and bear cases are both live and roughly balanced from $42.39. FRE durability and the Jackson insurance optionality argue for upside toward the high-$50s consensus; the falling estimates, defensive realization environment, and governance friction argue the stock can stay range-bound or retest the high-$30s. We are constructive on the fee-earnings trajectory but want positive estimate stabilization and concrete evidence of insurance-AUM ramp before upgrading.

## Company Overview

TPG operates as a single reportable segment marketed through six platforms: Credit ($95.2B AUM, built on Angelo Gordon), Capital ($89.7B, flagship control PE), Real Estate ($39.2B), Growth ($32.4B), Impact ($31.6B, among the largest climate/impact franchises globally with Rise Climate at $16.2B raised), and Market Solutions ($18.1B, GP-led secondaries plus an in-house capital-markets group and the Peppertree digital-infrastructure business). Fee-paying AUM is roughly $175B across the platforms.

The structural distinction from a typical operating company is the Up-C arrangement: TPG Inc. holds only ~42% of the TPG Operating Group, with the remainder flowing to non-controlling interests via ~224M Class B/Common Units that are economically equivalent and exchangeable 1:1 into Class A. The ~$6.3B headline market cap reflects only the ~154–160M Class A float; on a fully-exchanged basis, equity value is roughly $15B+. Any valuation must use the fully-exchanged share count, and corporate-level leverage screens (Z-score, debt/equity) are not meaningful because the balance sheet is dominated by consolidated VIE assets with no recourse to TPG.

The firm's differentiation is a diversified, growth-tilted platform mix combined with multi-decade fund track records (TGS I net 66% IRR, Rise Climate I net 14% IRR, flagship funds in the 1.3x–2.6x net MoM range). It is pursuing the three durable-fee vectors the entire industry is chasing: insurance balance-sheet capital (Jackson), perpetual/retail vehicles (T-POP, evergreen credit), and real assets/digital infrastructure (Peppertree).

## Financial Analysis

The right lens is FRE and distributable earnings (DE), not GAAP, because GAAP is distorted by consolidated funds and mark-to-market on unrealized carry.

**Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025:**
- Fee-related revenue $557.2M, +17%; management fees $475.1M, +15%.
- FRE $246.9M, +36%; FRE margin ~44% and rising; LTM FRE above $1B for the first time.
- After-tax distributable earnings $281.6M, +51%; adjusted EPS $0.70 vs ~$0.61 consensus.
- GAAP net loss attributable to TPG of $(1.5)M, $(0.05) basic / $(0.22) diluted, driven by $461.8M of unrealized performance-allocation losses (TPG VIII –$149M, TPG IX –$79M, Growth –$88M, Impact –$34M), partly offset by Real Estate +$150M and Credit +$88M. Realized performance allocations were positive at $323.4M, so the GAAP loss is a non-cash valuation swing, not a cash event.
- Capital raised $10.3B (vs $5.9B Q1'25); capital invested $14.4B; realizations $8.7B.
- Net accrued performance (unrealized carry receivable) $1.19B; dry powder $72.8B.

**Balance sheet and cash flow:** Total assets $13.3B. Debt obligations rose to $2.34B from $1.72B at YE2025 after a $500M 4.875% 2031 senior note in February 2026 plus revolver draws; total liquidity is $2.8B with a $1.75B revolver maturing 2030. Operating cash flow was $176.5M; the $516M investing outflow is almost entirely the $500M Jackson common-stock purchase. The TRA liability stands at $782.2M (85% of tax benefits owed to pre-IPO holders), with $29.9M paid in Q1. The dividend policy targets ≥85% of TPG's share of after-tax DE; $0.59/share was declared for Q1 2026, against $2.06 declared across the 2025 dividend year. At the current price the trailing yield is roughly 5.5%.

Durable, high-margin fee revenue is supplanting lumpy carry as the earnings base, which is the structural reason FRE compounding matters more than the GAAP headline. Corporate leverage of ~$2.3B against $1B+ LTM FRE and $2.8B liquidity is modest for the model.

## Growth Analysis

AUM grew 22% YoY to $306.2B, and management's stated targets are aggressive but supported by an identifiable fund-activation pipeline: TPG X activated Q3 2025, with Rise IV, Rise Climate II ($6.8B), THP III, TPG Sports, and MMDL VI all activating. The >$50B 2026 capital-raise target (against $10.3B already raised in Q1) and the 47% FRE-margin goal would extend the FRE step-up that drove the +36% Q1 print.

The earnings-surprise record is constructive at the DE level — three beats and one miss over four quarters, including a 14.1% beat last quarter and a 54% beat in Q2 2025. But the forward signal is mixed and currently negative. The covering analyst's current-quarter EPS estimate fell to $0.64 from $0.71 (30d ago) and $0.74 (90d ago) — cuts of 9.6% and 13.5%. Next-quarter ($0.71, –6.8%/30d) and next-year ($3.68, –1.9%/30d, –3.9%/90d) estimates are also drifting lower. Only the current-year figure ($2.89) ticked up modestly (+1.2%/30d). This negative revision momentum on the near-term line is the single most important reason not to extend a BUY. It contradicts the rising-estimates profile that would otherwise warrant high conviction, and it reflects a realization environment management itself characterized as defensive (hawkish Fed pause, geopolitical risk, AI disruption to legacy business models pressuring exit valuations).

The Jackson Financial mandate (February 2026) is the highest-optionality growth thread. TPG became investment manager for select Jackson general-account assets, bought ~4.7M JXN shares ($500M), and issued ~2.3M Class A shares to a Jackson subsidiary (amortized as a reduction of future fee revenue). This is TPG's catch-up entry into the insurance permanent-capital model that re-rated Apollo, KKR, and Blackstone. It is small today; the AUM ramp and fee economics over the next several quarters will determine whether it materially closes the permanent-capital gap.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of ~120x is a function of the GAAP distortion and is not the right metric. On adjusted/distributable earnings, the picture is materially cheaper: the current-year consensus of $2.89 implies roughly 14.7x adjusted EPS at $42.39, and next-year $3.68 implies roughly 11.5x. That is a discount to the larger alternative managers, consistent with TPG's smaller scale, later move into insurance, and lumpier carry mix.

The valuation does not clear the bar for a high score for two specific reasons. First, the forward estimates underpinning that "cheap" multiple are falling, which means the apparent discount is partly a function of estimates that may keep drifting lower in a soft realization environment — a discount that prices a real concern rather than a clean mispricing. Second, the governance structure (controlled company, dual-class, TRA, unresolved Sunset) is a persistent multiple cap relative to peers with cleaner structures. The stock sits below the consensus mean ($56) and median ($58) targets, with a low target of $45 and a high of $75. We treat the consensus targets cautiously here: they are not independent confirmation of upside given the negative near-term revisions and the wide low-to-high dispersion ($45–$75) that reflects genuine disagreement about realization timing and insurance-ramp credit.

A 5.5/10 reflects a stock that is reasonably valued on durable FRE but not asymmetrically mispriced: the absolute yield (~5.5%) provides support, the FRE multiple is defensible, but there is no specific near-term catalyst the market is demonstrably mis-pricing, and the falling estimates argue against treating the current discount as a coiled spring.

## Competitive Landscape

TPG competes with Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Ares, Carlyle, Brookfield, and Blue Owl. Its competitive position is improving on the metrics that matter for an alt manager: AUM up 22% YoY, fundraising more than doubling year-over-year, an internal capital-markets group generating $83.2M of transaction revenue (+35% YoY) that monetizes its own deal flow, and an industry-leading Impact/Climate franchise. Per StockStory's Q1 2026 coverage, TPG led asset-management Q1 2026 earnings on relative fundraising strength in a defensive quarter.

The clear competitive gap is insurance permanent capital, where TPG is a later mover than Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, and Blackstone. The Jackson mandate is the catch-up move; its eventual scale determines whether TPG narrows the durable-fee gap that has driven peers' multiple expansion. Net trajectory is positive (share gains, platform diversification, new durable-fee vectors), which supports a 6.5, but the position is not yet dominant in the highest-multiple part of the industry.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Carry lumpiness.** Q1 2026 demonstrated that a single quarter of PE markdowns flips GAAP to a loss. Carry is cumulative and mark-to-market; future losses can reverse prior gains and create volatile reported results even when fee earnings compound.
2. **Clawback exposure.** Worst-case clawback rose to $2.66B (from $2.46B at YE2025), with $6.8B of cumulative performance allocations subject to reversal. The realistic current-fair-value clawback is $6.2M, but the tail is large and grows with markdowns.
3. **Governance / Up-C / Sunset.** Class B carries 10 votes/share; founders and GP LLC control the vote, and TPG is an exempt controlled company. A future Sunset event collapses Class B to 1 vote and could trigger an Investment Advisers Act change-of-control question. The timing is unresolved and is a standing overhang.
4. **TRA.** $782M liability that accelerates on change of control, acting as both a cash drain and a takeover deterrent.
5. **Realization environment.** Management's own MD&A flagged a hawkish Fed pause (rates 3.50–3.75%, no cuts now expected), geopolitical risk, and AI disruption to legacy business models — all of which pressure portfolio marks and exit windows, and which align with the negative near-term estimate revisions.
6. **Integration/dilution.** Angelo Gordon and Peppertree integration continues; large equity grants (43.8M Common Units to AG partners, 5.4M to Peppertree) and a Peppertree earnout up to $300M create dilution and retention dependency. Q1 equity comp was $255M, with ~$945M unrecognized RSU expense and ~$1.1B of unit awards still to amortize.
7. **Legal/regulatory.** Ongoing Hellas/TIM litigation (since 2011), industry-wide SEC scrutiny, and a January 2026 Executive Order targeting Wall Street homebuyers that could affect single-family real-estate exposure.

A 5.0 on risk reflects the combination of binary/structural overhangs (Sunset, TRA, clawback tail) and a near-term realization environment that the estimate cuts confirm is actively pressuring marks. These are not fully priced sector truisms; several are TPG-specific and currently moving in the wrong direction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 46.7% is rich versus 37.1% trailing realized vol (a 9.6% spread), and put/call skew of 8.9% indicates elevated demand for downside protection into the June expiry. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.7%. The put/call OI ratio of 10.06 signals heavy standing put positioning, though the put/call volume ratio of 0.84 is more balanced, suggesting the OI skew is partly legacy hedging rather than fresh directional bets. The term structure is flat (near 46.7% ≈ far 48.4%), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated on a single dated catalyst. The options market is pricing meaningful two-sided uncertainty and a downside tilt, which is consistent with a HOLD and argues against pre-positioning for a sharp re-rating in either direction. Short interest is modest at 5.5% of float (3.0 days to cover), so squeeze dynamics are not a material factor.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** FRE compounding is the durable engine, with a fund-activation pipeline supporting the 2026 raise target. The Jackson insurance mandate scales into a permanent-capital franchise, and the market re-rates TPG toward the Apollo/KKR durable-fee multiple. Realizations recover as the rate environment eases, unrealized markdowns reverse into positive carry, and adjusted EPS pushes toward and beyond the $3.68 next-year figure. The stock reverts to consensus in the high-$50s. Bull target ~$58–62.

**Bear case.** The defensive realization environment persists, near-term estimates keep falling, and unrealized markdowns continue to weigh on GAAP and sentiment. Insurance scale disappoints or ramps slowly. Governance overhangs keep the multiple capped below peers. Equity-comp dilution and integration costs drag DE growth. The stock retests the high-$30s. Bear target ~$36–38, roughly 10–15% downside.

The two cases are roughly symmetric from $42.39, which is the definition of a HOLD. The deciding variable is estimate-revision direction: until the near-term cuts stabilize and reverse, the bear path has the near-term edge even though the fee-earnings trajectory favors the bull over a longer horizon.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026) is the natural reassessment point, with the FRE trajectory, 2026 capital-raise pace against the target, and any Jackson AUM disclosure as the key data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Near-term EPS estimate revisions turn positive — specifically, current-quarter and next-quarter consensus stop declining and revise up by mid-single digits over a 30-day window.
2. Jackson insurance AUM/fee economics disclosed at a scale that credibly accelerates FRE (e.g., a multi-billion-dollar managed general-account mandate ramp), supporting a permanent-capital re-rating.
3. A clean resolution or de-risking of the Sunset/governance overhang, which would remove a structural multiple cap.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FRE growth decelerates below ~15% YoY or the FRE margin contracts, signaling the durable engine is faltering.
2. Capital raising falls materially short of the 2026 target (e.g., tracking below ~$30B annualized by mid-year), indicating fundraising momentum has broken.
3. A dividend cut, or sustained DE coverage below the ≥85% payout policy, signaling distributable cash strain.
4. A material clawback event or a further large step-up in the worst-case clawback figure alongside continued markdowns.

## Conclusion

TPG is a genuinely improving alternative asset manager whose fee-related earnings are compounding at 36% with a rising 44% margin, whose AUM has nearly tripled since 2021, and whose Jackson insurance mandate provides credible optionality toward a permanent-capital re-rating. The GAAP loss that some screens flag is a non-cash artifact of unrealized PE markdowns against positive realized carry, and the right metrics (FRE, DE, adjusted EPS) tell a constructive story.

The reasons this stops short of a BUY are specific and checkable, not generic: the covering analyst's near-term EPS estimates are falling — negative momentum that directly contradicts a high-conviction long; the carry mix just produced a GAAP loss and carries a $2.66B worst-case clawback tail; the dual-class controlled-company structure, $782M TRA, and unresolved Sunset are standing multiple caps; and the options market is pricing rich IV and elevated put demand into a stock down ~42% from its January high. We rate TPG a HOLD (growth_hold) and would upgrade on stabilizing/positive estimate revisions plus concrete evidence of insurance-AUM scaling. Current consensus targets ($45 low, $58 median, $75 high) bracket a wide range that reflects the realization-timing and insurance-ramp uncertainty this report identifies.
