## Executive Summary

News Corp trades at $25.44 with a market capitalization of approximately $14.1 billion (based on approximately 555 million total Class A and Class B shares outstanding). The stock presents a sum-of-the-parts value opportunity: News Corp's 61.4% stake in REA Group is worth an estimated $8-9 billion USD, representing a substantial portion of NWSA's equity value. The remaining businesses (Dow Jones, HarperCollins, Move, News Media) collectively generate $571 million in free cash flow and appear undervalued relative to comparable standalone companies.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) the persistent but potentially narrowing conglomerate discount, estimated at 30-40% to sum-of-parts; (2) growing AI content licensing revenue (approximately $100M/year from OpenAI + Meta with additional deals under negotiation) representing a genuinely new business line with high incremental margins; and (3) meaningful capital return capacity ($1.044B remaining buyback authorization as of December 2025, covering approximately 7% share retirement at current prices). The primary risks are Murdoch family governance/dual-class structure limiting shareholder activism, secular decline in News Media, and binary AI exposure (content licensing revenue vs. traffic cannibalization). At 24x forward FY2026 EPS ($1.08) with estimates rising +4.3% over 90 days, the valuation is reasonable given the asset base and growth trajectory.

## Company Overview

News Corp operates across five segments following the April 2025 sale of Foxtel to DAZN:

- **Dow Jones** (28% of revenue, $2.33B): Wall Street Journal, Barron's, and a fast-growing professional information business (Risk & Compliance +15%, Energy +11%)
- **Digital Real Estate** (21%, $1.80B): 61.4% stake in REA Group (Australia's dominant property portal) and 80% of Move (Realtor.com)
- **Book Publishing** (25%, $2.15B): HarperCollins, the world's #2 consumer publisher
- **News Media** (26%, $2.17B): The Times (UK), The Sun, New York Post, Sky News Australia, Herald Sun
- **Other**: Corporate overhead

The Foxtel divestiture eliminated a capital-intensive, declining pay-TV business at >7x EBITDA and generated A$592M in loan repayments plus a 6% equity stake in DAZN. The remaining portfolio is cleaner, more digital, and more defensible.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins (FY2025, ended June 2025):**
- Total revenue (continuing operations): $8.45B (+2% YoY)
- Total Segment EBITDA: $1.415B (+14% YoY) with margin expansion across all segments
- EBITDA margins improved: Dow Jones 25.2% (from 24.3%), Digital Real Estate 33.4% (from 30.6%), Book Publishing 13.8% (from 12.9%), News Media 7.1% (from 5.9%)
- Headline GAAP profitability understates underlying performance: Total Segment EBITDA less D&A ($459M), restructuring ($132M), and equity losses ($15M) implies adjusted operating income of approximately $809M, or roughly 9.6% of revenue. The disconnect with Segment EBITDA margins reflects heavy non-cash and restructuring charges rather than weak operations.

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:**

- Cash & equivalents: $2.05B as of December 31, 2025 (down from $2.4B at FY2025 year-end, reflecting buyback activity and working capital timing; note that $321M of consolidated cash is held at REA Group and not readily accessible to the parent)

- Net cash position: approximately $100M as of December 2025 (versus ~$440M at FY2025 year-end)

**FCF yield at current market cap:** $571M / $14.1B = approximately 4.0%. This is reasonable for a media company with growing digital segments and emerging AI licensing streams.

## Growth Analysis

**Growth drivers by segment:**
1. **Dow Jones Professional Information** (+15% Risk & Compliance, +11% Energy): Mission-critical enterprise data with high retention. This is the highest-quality growth engine, competing with RELX and S&P Global in regulatory compliance and energy pricing data.

2. **Digital Real Estate** (+9%): REA Group's Australian dominance provides pricing power and international optionality (India). U.S. Move/Realtor.com remains challenged by interest rates and NAR settlement disruption but represents cyclical recovery upside when housing markets normalize.

3. **AI Content Licensing** (approximately $100M/year identified): OpenAI (~$50M/yr based on reported $250M+ over five years including cash and credits) + Meta (up to $50M/yr) with additional deals under negotiation (including an expanded Bloomberg partnership). Management has characterized these as high-margin revenue streams given minimal incremental cost of licensing existing content, though specific margin disclosure has not been provided.

4. **Book Publishing** (+3%): Backlist revenue (64% of consumer sales) provides stability. Audiobook growth via Spotify partnership and digital sales (+5%) supplement.

**Growth headwinds:**
- News Media (-4%): Structural print decline, algorithm-driven traffic volatility. Management is optimizing costs (combined UK printing with DMG Media) but this segment will continue shrinking.
- Move lead volumes down 9% in FY2025: Rate-sensitive business requiring material mortgage rate improvement.

**Consolidated growth trajectory:** Mid-single-digit revenue growth (4-6%) with high-single-digit to low-double-digit EBITDA growth (driven by margin expansion in digital segments and AI licensing contribution). Consistent improvement from a low base with potential for positive surprises from AI monetization. Q2 FY2026 results (reported February 5, 2026) showed revenue of $2.36B (+6%) and Segment EBITDA of $521M (+9%), confirming the growth trajectory is intact.

## Competitive Landscape

**Dow Jones Professional Information:** Competes with RELX, S&P Global, and Reuters/Refinitiv. Risk & Compliance growth of 15% suggests market share gains in a regulatory-driven spending cycle. The WSJ brand provides content leverage that pure-data competitors lack.

**Digital Real Estate:** REA Group is the clear Australian leader with deep moat (network effects, brand, data). Move/Realtor.com is distant #2 to Zillow in the U.S., a structural weakness that limits re-rating potential for this segment. The NAR operating agreement creates key-man risk unique to Move.

**Book Publishing:** HarperCollins is #2 globally with a durable backlist and diversified catalog. Scale advantages in distribution and marketing. The audiobook/digital transition is being managed adequately.

**AI Content Licensing:** News Corp is among the most valuable content libraries globally for AI training. Only NYT, Reuters, and a handful of other publishers have comparable scale and quality. Early-mover advantage in licensing deals (OpenAI May 2024, Meta March 2026) establishes precedent pricing.

**Trajectory:** Improving in professional information and AI licensing; stable in book publishing and REA Group; deteriorating in News Media and challenged at Move. Net: modestly positive direction.

## Risk Assessment

**1. AI Content Cannibalization (High Impact, Medium Probability):** The same AI systems licensing News Corp content could reduce traffic to digital properties and substitute for subscriptions. Court decisions finding AI training as "fair use" threaten the licensing framework. This is the primary long-term existential risk.

**2. Governance / Dual-Class Structure (Medium Impact, Ongoing):** The Murdoch family trust litigation was resolved on September 8, 2025, resulting in new trusts for Lachlan, Grace, and Chloe Murdoch, while Prudence, Elisabeth, and James Murdoch ceased to be beneficiaries of trusts holding News Corp/Fox shares. A new stockholders agreement and voting-power cap arrangement were established. While the succession question is now formally resolved in Lachlan's favor, governance risks persist: Lachlan Murdoch serves as Chair of NWSA and CEO of Fox Corp, creating explicit conflicts of interest. The amended by-laws allowing directors to divert opportunities to Fox Corp remain a concern for minority shareholders. The conglomerate discount is partially a governance discount and may not close without structural change.

**3. U.S. Real Estate Market / NAR Disruption (Medium Impact, Elevated Near-Term):** Move faces both cyclical pressure (elevated mortgage rates) and structural disruption (NAR settlement changing buyer agent dynamics). The NAR operating agreement termination risk adds tail exposure. Recovery depends on rate normalization.

**4. News Media Secular Decline (Low-Medium Impact, Certain):** Manageable as long as restructuring continues reducing the cost base, but this segment will not contribute growth and may eventually require write-downs.

**5. Foreign Currency (Medium Impact, Variable):** Significant AUD and GBP exposure creates translation volatility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($33-38, +30% to +49%):**
- Conglomerate discount narrows from ~38% to ~20% as portfolio simplification continues
- AI licensing revenue scales to $150-200M/year as additional LLM providers sign deals
- Aggressive buyback execution at depressed levels (~7% annual share count reduction)
- U.S. real estate market recovers, re-rating Move from trough multiples
- Dow Jones Professional Information sustains double-digit growth, eventually driving spin-off or re-rating
- FY2027 EPS achieves $1.30+ driving P/E compression to <20x on current price

**Bear Case ($20-22, -13% to -21%):**
- AI content licensing revenue proves temporary or commoditized as more publishers sign deals
- Traffic cannibalization from AI tools accelerates, reducing digital advertising and subscription growth
- U.S. real estate remains depressed through 2027, Move continues losing share to Zillow
- News Media decline accelerates faster than cost cuts can offset
- Murdoch governance actions (related-party transactions, value extraction) damage minority shareholders
- Macro recession reduces advertising spend across segments

## Conclusion

NWSA remains a BUY at $25.44. The score trajectory (6.3 → 6.4 → 6.5) reflects ongoing modest improvement as portfolio simplification, deleveraging, and AI licensing continue. The fundamental thesis is reinforced by positive estimate revisions, consistent earnings beats (3 of 4 quarters), and meaningful buyback authorization.

The ~38% conglomerate discount remains wide following the October-February selloff. Governance risks provide a structural explanation for some discount, but the current magnitude appears excessive given accelerating shareholder returns, simplified portfolio, and emerging AI licensing monetization. The primary near-term risk is the upcoming earnings event (suggested by options pricing), but the ~4% FCF yield and balanced balance sheet provide downside support.

Position sizing should be moderate (2-3%) given governance constraints that may prevent full discount closure, with willingness to add on any post-earnings pullback below $23.
