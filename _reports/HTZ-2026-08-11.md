## Executive Summary

Hertz has two coherent stories running simultaneously, and they lead to opposite conclusions. The operating story improved for a third consecutive quarter: Q2 2026 revenue rose 10% to $2,396M, total revenue per day reached $61.98 (+9%, the strongest second-quarter RPD on record excluding the 2022 peak), the spread between RPD and direct operating expense per day widened 17% year over year, and Adjusted Corporate EBITDA of $81M came in above the $50–80M range management guided down to on June 23. Management quantified an exogenous drag: recall activity roughly 300% above prior year immobilizing ~15,000 vehicles, worth about $30M of EBITDA, which implies an underlying quarter closer to $111M. Against Avis Budget's Q2 revenue decline of 1.3%, Hertz's top line looks like a genuine recovery.

The financing story deteriorated in the same period, and it is the one that determines what the equity is worth. Total debt rose $1,693M in six months to $18,747M. Corporate liquidity fell 34% to $984M, with revolver availability down to $356M from $924M and no remaining letter-of-credit capacity under the Term C loan. Stockholders' equity is $(628)M; tangible book value is approximately $(4,550)M against $3,922M of goodwill and intangibles. The company reports its own Net Corporate Leverage as "NM" because LTM Adjusted Corporate EBITDA is $(59)M. In June it raised $350M (plus a $30M July greenshoe) of first-lien exchangeable PIK notes at a stated effective interest rate of ~17.7%, and concurrently lent 37,037,037 shares to J.P. Morgan for a nominal fee, booked as an $85M issuance cost, to supply the borrow that convertible hedgers required. Hertz's own fair-value tables mark $6,303M of nominal non-vehicle debt at $4,297M (68 cents), versus 87 cents at December 31. The plain non-convertible portion, which carries no equity optionality, is marked at 71 cents. A 32-cent widening of the discount to face over six months is a company-specific distress signal rather than a rates artifact.

At $2.455 the equity is worth roughly $783M on the 318.9M shares excluding the borrowed shares, or $875M on the full 356.45M share count from the 10-Q cover. Either figure is about 4-5% of an $18.8B enterprise value, and it sits beneath $6.3B of non-vehicle claims that creditors mark $2.0B below face. That is an out-of-the-money option on a residual-value and pricing recovery, purchased after a 57% rally from the August 5 close of $1.56 and at a price 7.9% above the consensus mean target. We reiterate AVOID for the third consecutive review; 73.8% short interest and 186.8% near-term implied volatility make the short side equally unattractive.

## Company Overview

Hertz Global Holdings operates the Hertz, Dollar, Thrifty and Firefly rental brands across approximately 11,000 locations in 160 countries, plus Hertz Car Sales and Hertz 24/7 car-sharing in Europe. Average vehicles in Q2 2026 were 539,118. Revenue is overwhelmingly ASC 842 operating-lease income ($2,143M fixed plus $187M variable in Q2), with roughly $66M from franchise royalties and retail vehicle sales.

Two reportable segments: Americas RAC produced $1,918M of Q2 revenue (+10%) at a 5% Adjusted EBITDA margin ($88M vs. $43M); International RAC produced $478M (+7%) at 10% ($47M vs. $38M). Americas remains 80% of revenue and carries essentially all of the residual-value exposure that matters — program vehicles were only 11% of the Americas fleet at year-end 2025.

Management now frames the business as Rent-a-Car, Service, Fleet and Mobility. The Mobility leg is Oro Mobility, described in company releases as an "operating affiliate." It runs a driver-led managed fleet supporting rideshare platforms in four markets and has a fleet-services role in Uber's robotaxi program supporting Lucid vehicles with Nuro autonomy, expected to begin Bay Area operations later in 2026. The economic substance is depot services — charging, maintenance, cleaning, repairs — paid for by Uber. Hertz owns neither the autonomy stack nor the robotaxi platform. No contract term, minimum volume, margin structure or revenue figure for Oro appears in any filing, and there is no Oro segment. Avis is pursuing comparable AV fleet arrangements. This is an option on a services contract, not a proprietary technology position, and it is currently unvalued because it is unquantified.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue declined three consecutive years: $9,371M (2023) → $9,049M (2024) → $8,504M (2025), the last down 6.0%. Adjusted Corporate EBITDA went $2,305M (2022) → $561M (2023) → $(1,541)M (2024) → $(339)M (2025). Net loss attributable was $(2,862)M in 2024 and $(747)M in 2025. Total shareholder return indexed to 100 finished 2025 at 16 against a peer index at 140.

**2026 to date.** H1 revenue of $4,400M was up 10.1%, but transaction days were flat in Q2 (38,646 vs. 38,695) and up only 1% in H1. All growth is price and mix. Part of the Q2 pricing strength was attributed by management to a World Cup bonus, which does not recur beyond early Q3. H1 Adjusted Corporate EBITDA improved $204M year over year, from $(284)M to $(80)M.

**Q2 GAAP earnings quality is poor and the mechanism matters.** The $64M GAAP profit contains +$98M of Public Warrant fair-value gain, +$51M of net gains on financial instruments, and +$64M of gain on a real-estate sale-leaseback, $213M in total. The first and third of those arise mechanically because the share price fell from $4.61 on March 31 to $2.27 on June 30; the collapse in the equity manufactured the accounting gain. The Hertz Corporation, which does not carry the Public Warrants, reported a Q2 net loss of $(34)M. Management's own Adjusted Net Loss was $(47)M, or $(0.11) per diluted share. If the stock recovers, these marks reverse into losses, exactly as they did in Q2 2025 when warrant and derivative marks cost $(222)M combined.

**Cash flow.** GAAP operating cash flow of $401M in H1 was down from $597M, largely because of the $346M Wells Fargo make-whole payment on January 27 following the Supreme Court's certiorari denial. Company-defined Adjusted Free Cash Flow was $(304)M in H1 versus $(251)M, and that figure already includes a $359M add-back for bankruptcy-related payments; without it, the number is approximately $(663)M. Gross revenue-earning-vehicle expenditures rose 22% to $7,217M on a flat-to-slightly-smaller fleet: this is rotation, not growth, and it produced "the youngest fleet in a dozen years" with 94% of the U.S. core fleet model-year 2025 or 2026. The rotation was funded with debt. We have not annualized any single quarter given severe seasonality (Q1 2026 net loss $(333)M; Q3 2025 net income +$184M).

**Balance sheet and the interest wall.** Net debt of $17,962M rose $1,754M in six months. The covenant structure requires minimum liquidity of $400M at Q2/Q3 month-ends but $500M at Q1/Q4 month-ends, and a first-lien ratio of ≤3.5x (Q2/Q3) or ≤3.0x (Q1/Q4). Hertz asserts compliance at June 30. Compliance with a leverage test while LTM Adjusted Corporate EBITDA is negative is only possible because the credit agreement's "Consolidated EBITDA" definition differs materially from the reported non-GAAP measure; the FY2025 10-K says so explicitly. The practical consequence is that outside investors cannot compute covenant headroom. What they can compute is that $984M of liquidity against a $500M year-end test leaves $484M of nominal room, with revolver availability of only $356M and drawings already at $816M.

The single most concrete forward drag: HVF III Series 2021-2, $2,000M at a 2.12% coupon, matures in December 2026. New HVF III paper priced in May 2026 at 5.74% and 6.12%. Refinancing $2.0B from 2.1% to ~6.0% adds roughly $78M of annual vehicle interest, and vehicle interest is charged against Adjusted Corporate EBITDA. Separately, in April HVF III issued $221M of Class E notes — the most junior ABS tranche — across eleven existing series at coupons of 10.67% to 12.54%. Whether that reflects enhancement pressure from softer vehicle values or an optimization of retained tranches is not disclosed; either reading involves paying 11-12% for vehicle-backed subordination.

Below the vehicle line, non-vehicle cash interest was $219M in H1 and the 10-Q shows $560M of total contractual interest obligations for 2026. Even at management's aspirational ~$1B of 2027 EBITDA, net non-vehicle debt of $5,584M implies ~5.6x corporate leverage and non-vehicle interest would consume roughly half of the EBITDA. At any Adjusted Corporate EBITDA below roughly $500M, corporate interest exceeds corporate cash generation and the shortfall is funded with new debt, which transfers value from equity to creditors every quarter it persists.

The 2028 maturity wall is $7,378M ($4,621M vehicle, $2,757M non-vehicle), with the revolver (March 2028) and the Term B, Incremental Term B and Term C loans (all June 2028) maturing together.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 10% is real but its composition limits what it is worth. Transaction days were flat; the entire gain is RPD. RPD strength came partly from the World Cup and partly from an easy comparison — Hertz's own FY2025 Americas RPD fell 5% following the EV and fleet missteps. This is recovery from a self-inflicted trough rather than share capture.

Depreciation per unit is the offsetting variable and it moved the wrong way: consolidated DPU rose 18% to $302/month and Americas DPU rose 22% to $304. The realized result on disposals swung from a $38M gain in Q2 2025 to a $5M loss in Q2 2026, a $43M adverse move that management attributed to prioritizing older vehicles and certain models and a higher mix of sales through "less favorable disposition channels." Management targets full-year Net DPU at or below $300, which requires second-half per-unit depreciation below the Q2 rate at the same time the 2.12% ABS tranche reprices.

The industry backdrop does not corroborate a residual-value crash but does not help Hertz's mix. The Manheim index read 210 in July, +1.3% year over year and about 2.5% below the March peak, with the EV sub-index +10.5% flattering a non-EV sub-index up only 0.4% and pickups and SUVs "down notably." High-value rental units are in the weak part of the mix.

Consensus is moving against the story. Current-quarter EPS estimates fell from $0.46 to $0.29 over 90 days (-37.2%) and from $0.44 over 30 days (-33.8%). Next-quarter estimates fell 21.5%. Next-year consensus swung from +$0.24 to $(0.10). The earnings-surprise record (3 beats in 4 quarters, including a 55.5% beat on the June quarter) argues that consensus may again be beatable on a quarterly basis, but the direction of full-year revisions is the more important signal, and it is negative on every horizon.

Testing the reported FY2026 target: H1 Adjusted Corporate EBITDA was $(80)M and the H1 improvement was $204M. Repeating that rate of improvement in H2, against a comp that was already positive (+$21M derived for H2 2025), gets FY2026 to roughly $145M — about 1.5% of an estimated $9,357M of revenue, against a reported 3-6% margin target that would require $281M to $561M. Reaching the low end demands an H2 improvement 67% larger than H1's while absorbing the December refinancing. That target's definitional basis is unverified: if it refers to segment-level Adjusted EBITDA (H1 2026: +$30M) rather than Adjusted Corporate EBITDA, the gap narrows materially. The ambiguity itself is a problem for underwriting the equity.

Avis, the closest comparable, said travel demand softened relative to earlier expectations and guided Q3 RPD roughly flat. Hertz guides full-year RPU above its $1,500 "North Star." One of those H2 pricing views is wrong, and Hertz's is the optimistic one. Airport leisure rental volume is a levered function of air travel and discretionary spending, and Hertz's transaction days are already flat.

## Valuation Assessment

At $2.455, equity value is $783M (318.9M shares excluding the borrowed shares) to $875M (356.45M shares per the 10-Q cover). Adding company-calculated net debt of $17,962M gives an enterprise value of roughly $18.7-18.8B, of which the common stock is 4.2-4.7%. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative; the company's own leverage metric is "NM." Conventional multiples cannot be constructed, so the equity must be valued as a residual claim under scenarios.

**Peer context.** Avis (CAR) also carries a negative P/E and sits 84% below its 52-week high, worse than Hertz's 70% drawdown. Uber trades at 16.4x and Lyft at 2.3x, but neither is a fleet-owning lessor and neither is comparable on capital structure. The relevant peer comparison is operational: Avis produced a 9.5% Q2 Adjusted EBITDA margin against Hertz's 3.4%, and Avis's FY2026 guide of $850M-$1B implies roughly 7.5-8.5%. A fully delivered Hertz turnaround at 3-6% still lands 400-500bp below its closest competitor, which is why the enterprise cannot support the leverage it carries at current profitability.

**Scenarios (all from the $2.455 basis):**

- **Bear — $0.40 (-84%).** Corporate capital structure is restructured or exchanged with impairment to unsecured and second-lien claims. This is what a 68-cent blended mark on non-vehicle debt (71 cents on the non-convertible portion) implies. Common equity recovery in that outcome is typically nominal.
- **Base — $2.20 (-10%).** Muddle-through. FY2026 Adjusted Corporate EBITDA of roughly $145-200M, the December ABS tranche refinanced at ~6% adding ~$78M of annual interest, liquidity held above the $500M year-end test with continued asset sales and sale-leasebacks, 2027 EBITDA in the $400-500M range. Corporate interest still exceeds corporate EBITDA, funded with incremental debt. Fair value approximates the $2.275 consensus mean.
- **Bull — $7.00 (+185%).** RPD holds above $60, DPU falls to the $300 target with realized disposal gains, recalls normalize, and 2027 Adjusted Corporate EBITDA reaches $900M. Deducting ~$475M of non-vehicle interest and ~$50M of non-vehicle capex and cash taxes leaves ~$375M of equity free cash flow; at 8x on roughly 400M diluted shares (the $3.58 first-lien exchangeable converts in this scenario, adding up to 63.5M capped shares) that is about $7.50. The 2028 wall must also be refinanced.

Weighting bear 45%, base 35%, bull 20% gives $2.29. At bear 40% / base 40% / bull 20% it gives $2.44. Either way, expected value is at or modestly below the current price with a distribution whose modal loss is near-total. There is no margin of safety at $2.455, and the price sits above both the mean ($2.275) and median ($2.25) analyst target after a 57% rally off the August 5 close of $1.56.

Two structural points argue against paying up for the optionality. First, the option has negative carry: roughly 14M shares per year of equity-award vesting at depressed prices, PIK accretion on the 2029 and new first-lien notes, and quarterly corporate cash burn funded with 6-18% money. Second, the ATM program sold only ~524,000 shares for ~$3M in Q2 with ~$247M of capacity unused, while management instead issued first-lien PIK paper at a ~17.7% effective rate and stated the notes "used capacity created through expiring revolving commitments as well as from term loan amortization." That sizing was constrained by debt baskets rather than set by need, which is what a capital structure near its secured capacity looks like.

A $783M equity stub on $9.4B of revenue is the pattern that most often marks a value trap rather than an entry point. Score: 3.2.

## Competitive Landscape

Hertz's durable advantages are physical: airport concession positions, a national depot and service network, one of the larger U.S. used-vehicle disposition channels, and brand recognition across four nameplates. Those support the Oro depot-services concept and the franchise-conversion initiative.

What Hertz does not have is a cost-of-capital advantage, and in a business where the asset is financed vehicles, that is the decisive variable. Avis funds a structurally more profitable operation; Hertz funds a less profitable one at 68 cents on the dollar in the non-vehicle stack and 10.67-12.54% on junior ABS. The vehicle securitization market continues to fund Hertz essentially at par because vehicles are ring-fenced, which keeps the operating business viable while leaving the corporate entity as the stressed layer.

Trajectory is genuinely improving from a trough: the RPD-to-DOE spread has widened for three consecutive quarters, International RAC is growing at a higher margin (10% vs. 5%), and the fleet is the youngest in twelve years. But the mix shift toward International does not reduce the risk that threatens solvency — approximately 89% of Americas residual risk sits with Hertz on non-program vehicles, unchanged in scope. And the growth versus Avis is best explained by comparison base rather than share gain, given flat transaction days.

Autonomy is not a Hertz-specific option. Avis has publicly engaged with Waymo; both operators are competing for the same fleet-management role. Depot services are a labor-and-real-estate business with no disclosed contract economics here.

## Risk Assessment

**Residual values (dominant).** DPU is an accrual reflecting management's forward residual assumptions, not realized outcomes. It rose 18% consolidated and 22% in the Americas in Q2 while the Manheim non-EV sub-index rose 0.4%, and the disposal line swung $43M adversely. The full-year sub-$300 DPU target requires improvement in H2.

**Liquidity and covenants.** $984M against a $500M year-end test, revolver availability $356M, no remaining Term C letter-of-credit capacity, and $2,636M of unused vehicle-debt capacity as the offset. The $100M covenant step-up coincides with the December $2.0B ABS maturity.

**Structural subordination.** Substantially all revenue-earning vehicles are pledged to the securitizations. Non-vehicle creditors reach that value only after vehicle secured parties are paid in full; equity is below $6.3B of non-vehicle claims marked $2.0B under face.

**Litigation.** The Wells Fargo make-whole is resolved in cash ($346M paid January 27). A new securities class action (*Schweitzer*, M.D. Fla., filed July 24, 2026) names Hertz Global, CEO Gil West and CFO Scott Haralson under §10(b)/20(a), class period May 7 – June 23, 2026, alleging misrepresentation of liquidity sufficiency and the transitory nature of used-car softness. Lead-plaintiff deadline September 22, 2026. Hertz states the matter is not material at this stage.

**Sale-leaseback cost.** $64M of Q2 non-vehicle asset-sale gains on ~$122M of H1 proceeds converts owned real estate into $4,992M of undiscounted lease payments at a 12.58% weighted-average discount rate over 11.6 years, with $2,847M payable after 2030. Management cited higher sale-leaseback expense as a driver of the DOE-per-day increase.

**Other.** Receivables provision rose 58% to $84M in H1. Goodwill of $1,045M and intangibles of $2,877M sit against negative book equity; an impairment would be non-cash but would deepen the deficit and could interact with net-worth-linked provisions. Item 1A of the Q2 10-Q reports "no material changes" to risk factors despite a guidance cut, a >40% one-day decline, an emergency first-lien financing, a 34% liquidity reduction and a new class action.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 186.8% against 97.6% one-year realized, a 90th-percentile reading within the chain, with a term structure in backwardation (186.8% at one week, 147.1% at one month, 135.2% at six months). Backwardation of that magnitude prices imminent event risk. The near-dated flow is call-heavy per the supplied classification, with a put/call volume ratio of 0.26 and a ±14.5% implied move on the two-day expiry, consistent with post-earnings speculative and squeeze-related positioning rather than hedging. The six-month tenor tells the opposite story: a put/call volume ratio of 3.85. Put/call open interest has fallen from 4.80 in June to 1.14, indicating the June put-heavy positioning has largely been monetized or rolled. Read together: short-dated speculation on a squeeze, longer-dated accumulation of downside protection. Options premium is expensive on both sides, which removes cheap expression of either view.

Short interest is 73.8% of float (97.5M shares) at 3.3 days to cover. Roughly 37M of that is likely the convertible-arbitrage hedge tied to the June share-lending agreement, leaving perhaps 60M shares of directional short. Both figures are extreme enough to make sharp two-sided moves the base case regardless of fundamentals.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The turnaround is working on the metrics management controls: RPD at a record for the quarter, the RPD-to-DOE spread widening three quarters running, utilization 79% (81% ex-recalls), the youngest fleet in twelve years, and Q2 Adjusted Corporate EBITDA above the revised range. Recalls cost ~$30M and are exogenous. Vehicle ABS funds at par, so the operating business is financeable. If DPU falls to $300, RPD holds, and 2027 EBITDA approaches $900M-$1B, the equity stub is worth several times the current price, and the first-lien exchangeable's $3.58 exchange price becomes the marker for where the credit market re-rates the equity. Optionality is asymmetric on the upside because the stub is small relative to the enterprise.

**Bear case.** Corporate EBITDA cannot service corporate interest. LTM Adjusted Corporate EBITDA is $(59)M, H1 non-vehicle cash interest was $219M, and 2026 contractual interest is $560M. The December refinancing adds ~$78M. Every quarter of that shortfall is funded with new debt, and the debt is getting more expensive: 5.74-6.12% on new ABS, 10.67-12.54% on Class E subordination, ~17.7% effective on the first-lien PIK notes, and a 12.58% implied rate on sale-leasebacks. Credit markets have already marked the outcome: 68 cents blended, 71 cents on the non-convertible stack, with the company's own credit-spread input at 40.90%. Simply repeating H1's improvement rate lands FY2026 EBITDA at roughly half the low end of the reported margin target, and Avis's flat Q3 RPD guide suggests the pricing tailwind is fading. A restructuring or coercive exchange would leave common equity with nominal recovery.

**What decides it.** Whether second-half per-unit depreciation falls while RPD holds, and whether the December ABS maturity is refinanced without further junior-tranche subordination. Neither is knowable today, and the price does not compensate for the uncertainty.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Three dated items: the Q3 print in early November, which must demonstrate an H2 improvement 67% larger than H1's to validate the 3% low end; the December 2026 maturity of $2,000M of HVF III 2021-2 at 2.12%, refinanceable only at roughly triple that coupon; and the Q4 month-end liquidity covenant stepping to $500M against $984M of corporate liquidity with $356M of revolver availability. The September 22 lead-plaintiff deadline is a minor incremental item.

**Timing window.** Three to five months. That is a workable horizon, but the carrying cost is the problem.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 73.8% of float short, 97.5M shares, 3.3 days to cover. Even netting out ~37M shares of likely convertible-arb hedging, the directional short position is roughly 60M shares — extraordinary crowding. The stock rose 57% in three sessions after the August 6 print, with 227M shares traded on August 7 against a 13.3M average, and at least one outlet attributed part of the move to short covering.

**Borrow and options signal.** Near-term implied volatility of 186.8% versus 97.6% realized makes put protection expensive; the two-day expiry prices a ±14.5% move and the one-month ±25.2%. Near-dated flow is call-heavy at a 0.26 put/call volume ratio, which is squeeze fuel rather than confirmation of the bear case. Longer-dated puts are being accumulated (six-month put/call volume 3.85), meaning the cheap part of the bearish trade has already been taken.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $0.40 gives 84% reward. Upside risk is a repeat of this year's pattern in reverse: the stock traded $1.45 to $8.18 within twelve months and moved +57% in three sessions this month. A move to $4.00 on a Q3 beat plus continued covering is a 63% loss on the short. Reward-to-risk of roughly 1.3:1 before borrow cost and volatility drag is inadequate.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis is well supported by the credit marks and the interest arithmetic, but at 73.8% short interest, 3.3 days to cover and 186.8% near-term implied volatility, the position is already consensus and the mechanics are hostile. Avoid on the long side; do not initiate a short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is the third consecutive AVOID (3.7 in May, 3.7 in June, 3.2 now), and the stock has fallen 52% since the June review. The price decline is accompanied by identifiable fundamental deterioration rather than sentiment alone: corporate liquidity down 34% ($1,489M → $984M), total debt up $1,693M, the non-vehicle debt mark down 19 points (87c → 68c), the company's own credit-spread input up from 11.26% to 40.90%, a delivered guidance cut, an emergency financing at a ~17.7% effective rate, and current-quarter consensus EPS down 37% in 90 days. The Q2 operating beat is a genuine positive and is why growth prospects only fell from 5.0 to 4.3 rather than further. The score decline is concentrated in financial health (2.0 → 1.8) and valuation (3.5 → 3.2), the latter because the stock has rallied 57% off its August low to a price 7.9% above the consensus mean target, which removes the discount that a 52% decline had briefly created.

**Scenario targets from the $2.455 basis:** bear $0.40 (-84%), base $2.20 (-10%), bull $7.00 (+185%). Probability-weighted expected value $2.29-2.44, i.e., zero to modestly negative, with a bimodal distribution.

**Re-entry criteria — all four required, not any one:**
1. HVF III Series 2021-2 refinanced at or below ~6% with no incremental Class E subordination and no reduction in the aggregate vehicle-debt borrowing base.
2. Two consecutive quarters of positive Adjusted Free Cash Flow excluding the bankruptcy-payment add-back and excluding non-vehicle asset-sale proceeds.
3. Net DPU at or below $300 with realized gains (not losses) on vehicle disposals for two consecutive quarters, confirming the accrual assumption rather than restating it.
4. Corporate liquidity rebuilt above $1.2B with revolver availability above $600M, and the non-vehicle debt fair-value mark recovering above 85 cents.

**Preferred entry if those conditions develop:** $1.30-1.50, where the option premium is roughly 40% below current levels for the same payoff structure.

**Timeline.** The headwinds persist through at least the 2028 maturity wall ($7,378M, of which $2,757M non-vehicle). The nearest decision points are the Q3 print in early November and the December ABS refinancing.

**What would move us to AVOID with a short recommendation:** short interest falling below 30% of float while liquidity breaches $600M or the December refinancing is completed only through additional junior subordination. Under current positioning, the short side is not available at acceptable risk/reward.

**What would move us to HOLD:** the base case confirmed with the stock at or below $1.60, giving roughly 35% upside to a $2.20 base value against the same bear case.

## Conclusion

Hertz is executing better than it was a year ago and financing itself worse. Both statements are supported by the company's own disclosures: Adjusted Corporate EBITDA improved $63M year over year in the quarter and $204M in the half, while corporate liquidity fell 34%, total debt rose $1,693M in six months, and the non-vehicle capital structure repriced from 87 to 68 cents. The company publishes its corporate leverage as "not meaningful" because LTM Adjusted Corporate EBITDA is negative, and the arithmetic below that line is unforgiving: roughly $450-500M of annual non-vehicle interest against a business that, if it simply repeats its first-half rate of improvement, generates about $145M of Adjusted Corporate EBITDA this year, with $78M of that consumed by the December refinancing of a 2.12% ABS tranche.

At $2.455 the equity is a small residual claim on a large, expensively financed enterprise, priced above the consensus mean target after a 57% two-week rally, in a name where 73.8% of the float is short and near-term implied volatility is 186.8%. The upside scenario is real and would be worth multiples of the current price, but it requires second-half per-unit depreciation to fall while pricing holds against a peer guiding flat, and it requires refinancing $7.4B in 2028. Probability-weighted expected value is at or below the current price with a modal downside close to total loss. We maintain AVOID; the short side is a crowded trade with inadequate asymmetry.
