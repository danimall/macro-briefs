**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

KKR is a global alternative-asset manager that has deliberately diversified away from its leveraged-buyout roots into a three-segment platform spanning Asset Management ($744B total AUM), Insurance (Global Atlantic, $219B), and Strategic Holdings. The investable franchise is the recurring fee engine: Fee Related Earnings (FRE) grew 14% in FY2025 to $3.71B at a ~75% margin, accelerated to +23.6% YoY in Q1 2026, and is supported by fee-paying AUM of $604-615B that carries roughly 92% duration of eight years or more. This is a higher-quality, more durable earnings stream than the carried-interest-heavy model of a decade ago, and it sits at the center of four structural tailwinds the firm is leaning into: private credit and asset-based finance (ABF grew from $7B to $85B in five years), insurance permanent capital, infrastructure ($17B to $100B), and private-wealth democratization.

The stock has fallen roughly 37% from its July 2025 high of $153.87 to $97.17, and that decline coincides with two developments that prevent a clean buy. First, consensus EPS estimates are falling broadly: current-year down 7.8% and next-year down 6.8% over 90 days, with management explicitly warning in May 2026 that tariff-driven market volatility has dimmed the growth outlook. Falling estimates against record headline results signal that the Street is repricing the realization and capital-markets cyclicality embedded in the model. Second, KKR's signature growth vector, the $34B K-Series private-wealth franchise built on semi-liquid evergreen vehicles, sits directly in the path of an actively materializing sector risk. On June 3, 2026, KKR fell alongside Ares and Blackstone after Partners Group capped private-equity fund withdrawals, a renewed reminder of redemption-pressure concerns in semi-liquid retail private-markets vehicles.

The counterweight is genuine and explains why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID: a clustered open-market insider-buying signal in February-March 2026, with both Co-CEOs and three directors purchasing at prices around and above the current quote, and a forward multiple near 16x that is undemanding for a franchise compounding FRE in the high teens. We rate KKR HOLD (growth_hold) with an upgrade path that hinges on estimate stabilization and confirmation that retail redemption pressure does not impair the K-Series flywheel.

## Company Overview

KKR (founded 1976) manages $744B of AUM as of 12/31/2025 across three reporting segments built on one integrated platform with ~4,200 core employees and 36 offices. The de-concentration from its buyout origins is the defining structural shift: traditional private equity, once over 70% of AUM, is now under 25%.

**Asset Management** spans five lines. Private Equity ($229B AUM / $151B FPAUM) includes traditional ($167B), core PE ($41B, holds up to 25 years), and growth ($21B). Real Assets ($192B / $163B) is led by infrastructure ($100B), real estate ($86B), and energy ($6B). Credit & Liquid Strategies ($322B / $289B) covers leveraged credit ($145B), alternative/private credit ($135B including ABF $85B), and hedge funds ($34B, mainly the 39.6% Marshall Wace stake). Capital Markets generated $930M of transaction fees in 2025. Principal Activities deploys ~$15B of KKR's own balance-sheet capital alongside clients.

**Insurance (Global Atlantic)** is 100%-owned ($219B AUM, 3.5M+ policyholders), earning the spread between investment income and policyholder cost of funds across annuities, life, reinsurance, pension risk transfer, and funding agreements, with A-tier financial-strength ratings.

**Strategic Holdings** (new since Q1 2024) holds 19 core-PE operating companies long-term for dividends, contributing pro-rata ~$4.4B revenue / $1.1B EBITDA.

The competitive architecture rests on scale across asset classes, long-duration and increasingly perpetual capital that produces recurring fees, ~$30B of firm and employee capital co-invested alongside clients, and an integrated insurance balance sheet that supplies long-dated capital for the credit and real-asset engines to invest, compounding both fee and spread income.

## Financial Analysis

KKR must be assessed on segment and non-GAAP metrics rather than consolidated GAAP, because consolidating Global Atlantic's policy liabilities and third-party fund/CLO assets grosses the balance sheet up to $410B against $33.1B of book value. Those liabilities are non-recourse to KKR. The headline screening metrics, an Altman Z-Score of 0.17 and debt/equity of 10.63, are artifacts of this consolidation and do not describe a distressed business; FRE is $3.7B and growing, and credit ratings are A-tier.

**Earnings power (FY2025 vs FY2024):**
- FRE: $3.71B, +14%, at a ~75% margin on $4.94B of fee-related revenue — the most durable stream.
- Total Operating Earnings: $4.99B, +14% (FRE + Insurance Operating Earnings $1.11B + Strategic Holdings $162M).
- Adjusted Net Income: $4.38B, +4%; Adjusted EBITDA $5.96B.
- Management fees: $4.1B, +18% (PE $1.53B, Real Assets $1.30B, Credit $1.27B) — broad-based, not concentrated.
- Total Investing Earnings fell to $905M (from $1.15B), dragged by the Asian Fund II clawback.

GAAP net income to KKR common fell to $2.25B (from $3.08B), driven by insurance realized losses on available-for-sale fixed-maturity securities from portfolio repositioning, a rate/accounting artifact rather than core deterioration, given Insurance Operating Earnings actually rose to $1.11B. A naïve EPS read therefore understates the franchise.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation:** KKR book value is $33.1B with $4.8B cash and short-term investments. The dividend was raised to $0.78/yr ($0.195/qtr) from Q1 2026. The firm issued $2.5B of Series D Mandatory Convertible Preferred in Q1 2025 (converting to ~20.8M shares; $118.6M dividend in 2025) to fund growth. Buybacks have been modest relative to size — 94.2M shares / $2.8B retired since 2015 at an average of $29.36 — as capital is reinvested into Insurance, Strategic Holdings, and M&A rather than returned.

**Earnings track record and momentum:** KKR has beaten consensus four straight quarters (Q1 2026 +6.6%, Q4 2025 +10.7%, Q3 2025 +7.7%, Q2 2025 +2.7%). Q1 2026 delivered adjusted EPS of $1.39 vs ~$1.27 estimate (up from ~$1.13 a year prior), FRE +23.6% YoY, total operating earnings +19.1%, and fee-earning AUM $614.8B (+16.9% YoY).

The fee engine is high quality, broad-based, and growing at a faster rate than total earnings, a constructive mix shift. The clawback variability (KKR repaid $344M on Asian Fund II in Q4 2025, a net $207M hit to realized performance income, with ~$150M of distributed carry still subject to clawback and a $417M netting hole at North America Fund XI) is the reason investing earnings cannot be modeled as smooth. We score Financial Health 7.0: the recurring base is resilient and capital structure is appropriate for an AM/insurance holding company, but the insurance investment portfolio carries genuine credit and rate exposure (including ~21% office/retail CRE within commercial mortgages) and the preferred issuance reflects a capital-intensive growth model.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is real but momentum-divergent. AUM grew 17% YoY ($637B → $744B); FPAUM grew 18% ($512B → $604B); capital invested was $94.6B (+13%); and dry powder stands at $118.4B, providing visible fee runway as new vintages (North America Fund XIV, Global Infrastructure V, Asia Pacific Infrastructure III) commence fees. The structural drivers — ABF, infrastructure, insurance permanent capital, and private wealth — are confirmed across multiple peers, which lends the thesis durability beyond a single-company story.

The complication is that consensus EPS estimates are falling, not rising, despite the record headline numbers. Current-quarter consensus dropped 10.9% over 90 days ($1.56 → $1.39), next-quarter fell 7.1%, current-year fell 7.8% ($6.55 → $6.04), and next-year fell 6.8% ($7.94 → $7.40). The 30-day moves are smaller and flatter (roughly -0.2% to -0.8%), so the bulk of the negative revision occurred earlier in the window and has decelerated, but the trend has not turned positive. This is the inverse of the signal that would justify treating analyst targets as floors. Management's May 2026 warning that volatility dimmed the growth outlook is consistent with the Street marking down realization and capital-markets income, where Capital Markets fees fell from ~$1.0B on smaller deal sizes.

The mechanism matters: the recurring FRE base is more insulated than the carried-interest and capital-markets lines, which are macro- and realization-dependent. If exit markets stay choppy and tariff-driven volatility persists, the variable lines remain pressured even as fees compound, which is precisely the mix the falling estimates reflect. We score Growth Prospects 6.5 — the FRE growth is durable and high-quality, but broadly falling consensus estimates against a management volatility warning cap the score below the level the headline AUM and FRE growth would otherwise support. Growth_prospects is the dimension we weight most heavily for winner selection, and a name with falling estimates does not earn a high mark here regardless of the structural story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $97.17, KKR carries a market cap of ~$88.9B. Trailing P/E is 33.8x on $2.34 GAAP EPS, but GAAP is depressed by the insurance AFS realized losses and is the wrong lens. On consensus current-year adjusted EPS of $6.04, the forward multiple is ~16.1x; on next-year $7.40, ~13.1x. Against peer medians of 16.9x (the comparable set includes BX 31.0x, APO 84.8x, BK 16.9x, STT 16.7x, AMP 11.2x), KKR's forward multiple is roughly in line with the median and well below the alternatives-manager peers most comparable to its model (BX, APO). For a franchise compounding FRE in the high teens to mid-twenties, a low-to-mid-teens forward multiple is not stretched.

The reason valuation does not score higher is that the multiple is reasonable but the catalyst to re-rate is absent and the estimate trend works against it. A "cheap" multiple is anti-predictive on its own and tends to mark a value trap unless paired with an independent catalyst and rising estimates, and here estimates are falling. The Sunset Date governance simplification (one-share-one-vote, carry pool brought under KKR, Series I preferred redeemed, by year-end 2026) is a legitimate structural positive that could broaden the investor base, but it is not an earnings catalyst and its timing is diffuse. The K-Series and Capital Group distribution expansion is a genuine growth lever, but it is the same lever currently exposed to the redemption-gating risk hitting peers.

Analyst price targets bracket the range at $104 low / $153 high, mean $125.5, median $125 — implying ~29% upside to the mean. We treat these as informative but note that on a high-momentum-but-falling-estimate name, consensus targets lag the downward revision rather than the upward one. The valuation supports holding, not an upgrade. We score Valuation 6.0 — undemanding on forward earnings, but lacking the asymmetric mispricing and positive revision momentum that would warrant a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

KKR competes with Blackstone, Apollo, Ares, Brookfield, Carlyle, and TPG, and its competitive position is improving on trajectory. The integrated model — Global Atlantic supplying long-dated permanent capital that KKR's credit and real-asset engines invest — is a structural edge that compounds both fee and spread income, and the firm is gaining share in the fastest-growing alternative segments (ABF and infrastructure). Long-term fund returns remain strong (all-funds 25.5% gross / 18.6% net IRR since inception), which sustains fundraising velocity. Roughly 92% of AUM has duration of eight years or more, much of it perpetual, which converts the platform into a durable recurring-fee machine rather than a campaign-to-campaign fundraiser.

The competitive risk is concentrated in the same place as the growth opportunity: private wealth. The democratization of alternatives via evergreen vehicles is the industry's most contested frontier, and the liquidity mismatch inherent in semi-liquid retail products is now being tested across the complex. KKR's K-Series and Capital Group partnership position it well for the inflows, but they also expose it to the redemption-pressure sentiment that the Partners Group cap crystallized. On balance the trajectory is positive — KKR is gaining share in structurally growing markets with a differentiated integrated model — and we score Competitive Position 7.5. We weight this as a secondary discriminator and confirm it against the durable FRE growth rather than treating the moat narrative alone as conclusive.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks not fully reflected in the current valuation cluster around three vectors.

**Retail/evergreen redemption risk (the live catalyst).** The K-Series and Capital Group partnership introduce semi-liquid vehicles whose liability side can gate. The risk is that retail redemption pressure forces NAV markdowns or impairs the inflow flywheel that underpins the private-wealth growth thesis. This is the single most material, actively-materializing risk and is not fully priced.

**Mark and clawback sensitivity.** Carried interest and investment income depend on fair-value marks of illiquid assets (PE leans 55% on DCF, 38% comps; real assets 91% DCF). A 10% decline in fund values disproportionately hits carried interest. The clawback is materialized, not theoretical, as shown by the Asian Fund II repayment and the North America Fund XI netting hole.

**Insurance/Global Atlantic.** Spread compression, rate sensitivity, AFS mark-to-market losses, policy-liability assumption unlocking, and embedded-derivative volatility on fixed-indexed annuities. The $200B+ investment portfolio carries credit risk, including the office/retail CRE exposure within commercial mortgages.

A higher-for-longer rate path compresses alternatives-manager equity multiples and pressures the insurance spread book simultaneously, a double exposure. The Sunset Date governance unwind is a net positive but introduces transition complexity. We score Risk Factors 5.0 — the redemption catalyst is specific and unfolding, and the mark/clawback variability is demonstrated rather than hypothetical, which outweighs the absence of any near-term binary blow-up risk.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options structure flags imminent event stress. One-week ATM IV spiked to 169.5% against one-year historical vol of 37.5% (a 132pp premium, 92nd percentile within the chain), with the term structure in steep backwardation (169.5% near vs 38.5% at three months) — the market is pricing a near-dated catalyst that resolves quickly. The one-week reading is dominated by a single expiry; the one-month and three-month IVs of ~39% are far more representative of the durable vol regime. Put/call skew is balanced (1.1%) and put/call volume modestly elevated at 1.35, so the positioning is event-hedging rather than a directional bearish bet. ATM IV jumped from 55.4% to 145.1% over the prior month while price was roughly flat (+1.4%) and put/call OI ratio fell from 1.98 to 1.23 — rising vol with falling put-heaviness suggests hedging of an upcoming event rather than accumulating bearish conviction. Short interest is low at 2.1% of float (3.0 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short overhang and no squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$135-153).** FRE compounds in the high teens to low twenties as new fund vintages commence fees and K-Series/Capital Group inflows scale. Estimate revisions stabilize and turn positive as tariff volatility fades and exit markets reopen, restoring realization and capital-markets income. The Sunset Date completes cleanly, collapsing the dual-class structure into one-share-one-vote and broadening the investor base. The redemption-pressure scare proves transient, as the institutional-vs-wealth bifurcation favors scaled managers with perpetual capital. At a re-rated ~20x on next-year EPS of $7.40, the stock reaches ~$148, consistent with the $153 analyst high and the insider buying near today's level.

**Bear Case (~$78-85).** Tariff-driven volatility persists, exit markets stay frozen, and realization/carry income remains depressed, driving further estimate cuts. The evergreen-redemption scare reaches KKR's K-Series directly, forcing NAV markdowns or a flagship gate that impairs the private-wealth flywheel and chills the inflow narrative. Insurance AFS losses and CRE credit deterioration compound a higher-for-longer rate path. At ~12x on cut next-year estimates near $6.80, the stock falls toward the lower bound of where insiders bought (~$80), roughly 15-18% below current.

The clustered open-market insider buying — both Co-CEOs (Nuttall ~175K shares, Bae ~175K) plus directors Barakett (100K), Cohler (43.9K), and Dillon (22.2K) in February-March 2026 at $87-105, around and below today's $97 — is the strongest single piece of evidence for the bull case and the reason this is not an AVOID. The much larger entity "sales" in the data are Sunset-restructuring plumbing, not market selling, and should not be misread as bearish.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is constructive — a high-quality compounding franchise at a reasonable forward multiple with an insider-conviction signal — but the combination of broadly falling consensus estimates and an actively-materializing redemption-pressure catalyst on KKR's signature growth vector removes the asymmetry required to upgrade to BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Consensus current-year and next-year EPS estimates stabilize and turn positive over a 30-60 day window (current-year above $6.04 and rising), confirming the negative revision trend has ended.
2. K-Series/evergreen redemption requests remain orderly through the next reporting cycle — no fund gating, no flagged NAV markdown — demonstrating the private-wealth flywheel is intact despite the sector scare.
3. A clean Sunset Date completion with a broadening institutional shareholder base, or a clear re-acceleration in realization/carry income as exit markets reopen.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. KKR or its K-Series vehicles gate redemptions, or a peer gate forces a visible KKR NAV markdown.
2. FRE growth decelerates below ~10% YoY, signaling the durable base is cracking, not just the variable lines.
3. Insurance AFS losses or CRE credit deterioration force a material reserve build or capital action beyond routine debt issuance.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026) for the FRE-growth and redemption-flow read, with the Sunset Date completion (by Dec 31, 2026) as the structural milestone. Monitor the alternatives-complex redemption flow continuously, as that catalyst is exogenous to KKR's own reporting calendar.

For reference, a base-case fair value of ~$115 (15.5x next-year $7.40, between the bear and bull scenarios and below the $125 analyst median) implies ~18% upside, but the path to realizing it requires the estimate trend to turn, which has not yet occurred.

## Conclusion

KKR is a structurally advantaged alternative-asset franchise with a high-quality, broad-based fee engine compounding at 14-24%, durable perpetual-capital tailwinds, and a forward multiple near 16x that is undemanding for the growth on offer. The stock's ~37% drawdown from its high, combined with clustered open-market insider buying by both Co-CEOs and three directors at current levels, makes the risk/reward more attractive than it was at $153 and supports holding the position.

The reasons this stops short of a BUY are specific and falsifiable. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across every horizon, management has warned that volatility dimmed the growth outlook, and the firm's marquee growth vector, the K-Series retail-wealth franchise, sits directly in the path of evergreen-redemption-pressure concerns that resurfaced at peers (Partners Group). A reasonable multiple without rising estimates and with an active downside catalyst is a hold, not a buy. The financials sector carries a structurally negative selection record on a market-relative basis, which raises the bar for any BUY here beyond what beta alone provides; KKR does not clear it today. We rate KKR HOLD with a clear upgrade path: stabilizing estimates and confirmation that retail redemptions stay orderly would flip the call.
