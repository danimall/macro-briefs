**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

WillScot is the largest North American provider of turnkey temporary space (modular offices, classrooms, and complexes, plus portable storage), operating a lease-first specialty-rental model rather than the "office supplies" business its sector tag implies. The company carries ~304,000 units with a fleet net book value of ~$3.1B, generated $971M of adjusted EBITDA (a ~43% margin) in 2025, and produced ~$489M of adjusted free cash flow. Headline optics are misleading in both directions: the 2025 GAAP net loss of $(53.0)M is dominated by $302M of non-cash accelerated depreciation from a strategic Network Optimization Plan, and the reported Altman Z-Score of 0.38 is a false distress signal for an asset-backed rental roll-up funded on secured debt with no maturities until 2028. The trailing P/E of −70x and the negative EPS in the profile data reflect that non-cash charge, not an operating collapse.

The core investment debate is rate-versus-volume. Three consecutive years of declining US non-residential construction starts, driven by higher rates, have cut total average units on rent by ~11% (modular −5.5%, portable storage −15.6%) and pushed utilization down (modular 61.9%→59.9%, storage 60.0%→51.5%). WSC has offset this with pricing power: modular average monthly rates rose 4.9% and storage 7.5% in 2025, and both kept climbing in Q1 2026. The bull case is operating leverage: when starts recover, volume returns on top of a higher rate base and a leaner cost structure. The bear case is that pricing power eventually exhausts if volume keeps falling and utilization has room to drop further from the low-to-mid 50s.

We rate WSC a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 5.55, confirming the improving trajectory from prior reviews (4.6 in March, 5.5 in May). Q1 2026 showed genuine stabilization: revenue decline narrowed to −2.0% from FY2025's −4.8%, net income returned to $28.1M, and delivery/installation revenue rose 12.3% on large complex projects. But near-term estimate revisions are still slightly negative, the company has missed EPS in four consecutive quarters, and a $63.5M surge in AR write-offs signals real customer stress in the construction base. The stock has declined ~15% from where it was priced two months ago and sits ~17% below its 52-week high, but the fundamentals have not deteriorated enough to justify an AVOID, nor inflected enough to justify a BUY. The August 6 Q2 print is the natural reassessment point.

## Company Overview

WillScot serves 85,000+ customers across 15 end markets through ~250 branches in the US, Canada, and Mexico. The business splits between modular space (offices, classrooms, complexes; ~128k units) and portable storage (~176k units), with a fast-growing Value-Added Products (VAPS) attachment business layering furniture, HVAC, power, security, climate control, and perimeter solutions onto base rentals.

The economics are attractive at the unit level. Modular units have 20+ year economic lives with >50% residual values, and management targets >25% unit-level IRRs including VAPS. The average effective lease duration runs ~40–42 months, far beyond the sub-12-month initial contractual terms, creating a sticky, recurring revenue base. Customer diversification is strong: no single customer exceeds 2% of revenue, the top 10 account for ~6%, and the top 50 for ~15%.

The company's history is a consolidation story: ~40 acquisitions and ~$4.9B of enterprise value deployed since 2017, including the transformational 2020 Mobile Mini merger (integrated and rebranded away by 2024). Two strategic pivots now define the forward story. First, the Network Optimization Plan approved in December 2025 will exit ~108 locations (~665 acres, ~25% of leased acreage) and abandon ~53,000 units through 2029, targeting $25–30M/year in avoided real estate cost increases. Second, a capital-allocation shift toward large complex projects (data centers, power generation, reshoring), where Q1 2026 delivery/installation revenue strength provides early evidence of traction.

## Financial Analysis

**Full-year 2025 versus 2024:** Total revenue fell 4.8% to $2,281.4M, with leasing revenue (the recurring core) down 4.9% to $1,749.0M. Gross margin compressed 330 bps to 51.0%, and adjusted EBITDA declined 8.7% to $971.0M. The GAAP net loss of $(53.0)M compares to $28.1M of net income in 2024, but both years carry heavy one-time distortions: 2025 includes $301.9M of restructuring (accelerated depreciation) plus ~$41M of accelerated depreciation for earlier real estate exits, while 2024 absorbed a $180M McGrath merger-termination fee and a $132.5M Mobile Mini trade-name impairment. Adjusted EBITDA is the cleaner read of the operating business, and it declined ~9%.

Cash generation held up. Operating cash flow rose ~$200M to $762.0M, and adjusted free cash flow was $488.8M (down 11.8%), comfortably covering interest, the ~$51M dividend, and $97.5M of buybacks (3.9M shares). The counter-cyclical FCF profile is a genuine structural feature: in a downturn, WSC can slash growth capex and sell fleet, which is why cash flow stayed robust even as volumes fell.

**Q1 2026 stabilization:** Revenue was $548.6M, down only 2.0%, a narrower decline than FY2025. Net income returned to $28.1M and adjusted EBITDA was $211.0M (−7.8%). Modular utilization jumped to 68.0% (from 59.3%) and storage to 55.9% (from 52.6%), though this improvement is partly optical: the Network Optimization Plan shrank the fleet denominator by removing units. Underlying average units on rent still fell ~7.3% YoY. Rates continued climbing (modular +2.6%, storage +6.4%), and delivery/installation revenue rose 12.3% on large complex projects. Capex rose 41% (net capex +44%) to fund FLEX/complex units, a pivot from countercyclical restraint toward selective growth investment.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt is ~$3.5B (down from $3.6B). The optically alarming ~4x debt-to-equity reflects a small equity base ($870M) eroded by an accumulated deficit of $(772)M from historical impairments and buybacks, standard for an asset-heavy rental roll-up. The maturity wall is benign: no maturities until 2028, then $500M 2028 notes at 4.625%, $500M 2029 at 6.625%, $500M 2030 at 6.625%, $450M 2031 at 7.375%, plus a $1.4B ABL draw against a $3.0B facility (~4.95% after swaps, ~$1.5B availability). The company is in compliance with all covenants. The one rate-timing risk: $1.25B notional of swaps (at ~3.44%/3.70%) mature June 30, 2027, after which unhedged variable-rate exposure rises unless renewed; +100 bps adds roughly $1.8M/quarter to interest.

The $63.5M increase in AR write-offs of aged receivables is the clearest financial yellow flag. The allowance fell from $101.7M (end-2024) to ~$62M (Q1 2026) as write-offs were taken, and the Q1 2026 credit provision ($17.8M) was still up YoY. Management frames this as an order-to-cash cleanup, which is plausible, but the magnitude points to prior under-reserving and/or genuine customer financial stress in a weak construction market.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is one of decline moderating rather than acceleration. Revenue fell 4.8% in 2025 and 2.0% in Q1 2026, and consensus current-year EPS sits at $1.10 (up 4.1% over 90 days) with next-year at $1.35 (up 5.4% over 90 days). The multi-quarter EPS revisions are positive, but the near-term signal is soft: current-quarter estimates are down 0.2% over 30 days and 2.8% over 90 days, and next-quarter is down 0.3%/1.7%. This is negative earnings momentum at the quarterly level even as full-year figures firm, a pattern consistent with a business bottoming but not yet re-accelerating.

The estimate-revision threshold that would flag a strong buy signal (+30% over 90 days combined with >30% revenue growth) is not present here. WSC is a moderating-decline story on the numbers. The growth case rests on two forward drivers management has repeatedly named. First, large complex projects (data centers, power generation, and reshoring), where Q1 D&I revenue up 12.3% and the 41% capex increase into FLEX/complex units are early confirmation of a demand pivot. This aligns with the broadly confirmed data-center and power-infrastructure buildout. Second, VAPS cross-sell and adjacency expansion (climate-controlled storage via the 2025 acquisition, clearspan structures, perimeter solutions) that lift revenue-per-unit independent of the base-unit cycle.

The structural growth engine is operating leverage on recovery. WSC has shed ~11% of units on rent while pushing rates up mid-single-digits and stripping out ~$25–30M/year of real estate cost. When non-residential construction starts recover, volume returns on top of a higher rate base and a leaner cost structure. The timing of that recovery is rate-dependent and unresolved, which is why growth prospects score in the middle of the range.

## Valuation Assessment

WSC's trailing P/E of −70x is meaningless given the non-cash restructuring charge. On forward earnings, the stock trades at ~24x current-year EPS ($1.10) and ~19.6x next-year EPS ($1.35) at the $26.50 price. On EBITDA, an enterprise value of roughly $8.3B ($4.8B equity + ~$3.5B total debt) against $971M adjusted EBITDA implies ~8.6x, a reasonable multiple for a market-leading specialty rental franchise with high recurring revenue and strong FCF conversion.

Peer comparison is unhelpful here because the "Office Services & Supplies" peer set (HNI, MSA, PBI, TILE, MLKN) is heterogeneous and not economically comparable to a lease-first rental roll-up: the peer median P/E of 21.5 conflates furniture manufacturers, safety-equipment makers, and mailing-services businesses. The more relevant comparable is McGrath RentCorp, and WSC's ~8.6x EV/EBITDA is within the normal band for the modular-space rental industry.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $24, mean $28.8, median $29.0, high $37.0. The mean implies ~8.7% upside and the median ~9.4%. On our valuation framework, a modestly-below-forward-multiple stock with positive full-year revision trends but negative near-quarter momentum and no identified catalyst for immediate re-rating does not qualify for a high valuation score. Cheapness on forward EBITDA is offset by the absence of an inflection signal and by the AR/credit overhang. The recovery optionality is real but rate-contingent. The market is pricing a business that is stabilizing, not one that is inflecting, and we agree with that read. This is a fairly-valued name with asymmetric upside only if construction starts recover and large-project demand compounds, which supports a mid-range valuation score rather than a high one.

## Competitive Landscape

WillScot's moat is structural: the broadest North American branch network, in-house logistics and delivery, a unified SAP ERP plus CRM platform, and VAPS cross-sell capability that smaller local competitors cannot match. The industry is highly fragmented and local, with an estimated ~$20B addressable market inside a ~$2 trillion North American commercial space market, which underpins the roll-up thesis.

The competitive position is stable-to-improving even through the downturn. WSC has held and raised pricing (rate up mid-single-digits) while volume fell, which demonstrates pricing power rather than share loss to discounters. The pivot into climate-controlled storage, clearspan structures, and perimeter solutions expands the addressable wallet per customer. VAPS attachment continues to lift revenue-per-unit economics.

The abandoned McGrath acquisition is critical context. WillScot agreed to acquire McGrath in early 2024, then terminated in September 2024, paying a $180M break fee. The reported (though not independently re-confirmed here) antitrust scrutiny signals a ceiling on further large-scale modular consolidation, pushing WSC toward smaller tuck-ins and adjacencies. This constrains the historical inorganic-growth playbook but does not erode the existing scale advantage. Net, the trajectory is favorable: pricing power intact, adjacency expansion underway, and the leading position in a fragmented market that will consolidate around scale players over time.

## Risk Assessment

**Demand and rate sensitivity.** ~85–87% of revenue is Commercial & Industrial (~43%) plus Construction & Infrastructure (~42%). Three straight years of falling construction starts drove the volume decline; a prolonged high-rate environment or recession would pressure units-on-rent and utilization further. In the current higher-for-longer rate regime, this headwind persists.

**Credit quality.** The AR write-off surge and still-elevated Q1 2026 credit provision suggest genuine customer stress in the construction base. This is the risk a standardized screen misses and the one most likely to surprise negatively if the construction downturn extends.

**Network Optimization execution.** Abandoning ~53,000 units ($312M NBV) and exiting 108 locations through 2029 carries service-disruption and disposal-cost risk (~$30M disposal + ~$20M relocation remaining). ~21,000 units were disposed as of Q1 2026. Execution upside is $25–30M/year in avoided costs.

**Swap maturity.** $1.25B of interest-rate swaps mature June 30, 2027, after which unhedged variable-rate ABL exposure rises unless renewed.

**Goodwill/intangibles.** $1.26B goodwill plus $224M intangibles (~26% of assets) create impairment risk in a sustained downturn, as the $132.5M Mobile Mini trade-name write-down in 2024 already demonstrated.

**Activist involvement and governance turnover.** The 10-K contains a dedicated activist-investor risk factor. Combined with the Executive Chairman appointment (Worthing Jackman), a Lead Independent Director holding ~2.4M shares (Jeff Sagansky), the CEO change (Tim Boswell effective January 2026), and a high cadence of officer turnover, this points to an activist-influenced governance reset. This can be a positive catalyst (cost discipline, capital returns) or a source of strategic uncertainty.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is flashing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 80.3% versus 1-year historical volatility of 51.6% (rich, at the 97th percentile within the current chain), with a term structure in backwardation (80.3% near vs 60.2% long-dated), signaling the market is pricing an imminent catalyst, almost certainly the August 6 Q2 earnings print. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±18.1%. Skew is call-heavy (−16.4%) with a put/call volume ratio of just 0.02, a bullish-leaning tilt in near-term positioning rather than defensive put-buying. The offsetting signal is short interest at 16.5% of float with 9.5 days to cover, meaningfully elevated institutional bearish positioning. The combination of call-heavy options flow and high short interest suggests a two-sided setup into earnings: elevated squeeze potential if Q2 confirms stabilization, meaningful downside if the volume trend re-weakens or the AR provision worsens.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($33–37):** Q2 2026 confirms the stabilization seen in Q1: revenue decline narrows further toward flat, rates keep rising mid-single-digits, and large-complex-project demand (data centers, power, reshoring) accelerates D&I revenue. The Network Optimization Plan delivers $25–30M/year in cost savings, expanding EBITDA margin. Construction starts stabilize or begin recovering in late 2026/2027, and volume returns on a higher rate base and leaner cost structure, driving strong operating leverage. On ~$1.05B normalized EBITDA and re-rating to ~9.5–10x EV/EBITDA, the equity reaches $33–37, matching the analyst high target. The 16.5% short interest amplifies upside via a squeeze if the earnings confirm the turn.

**Bear Case ($20–22):** Construction starts fall for a fourth year, pricing power exhausts as customers push back on rate increases, and utilization drops further from the mid-50s. The AR write-offs prove to be the leading edge of broader customer defaults, forcing a larger credit provision. Adjusted EBITDA slides below $900M, and the market de-rates the stock on impaired growth visibility toward $20–22, roughly 17–25% below current levels. Goodwill impairment risk resurfaces if the share price stays depressed.

**Base Case ($27–29):** The construction downturn moderates but does not sharply recover in the next 12 months. Revenue stabilizes near flat, rates continue climbing, the cost-reduction program delivers, and large-project demand offsets soft small-project volume. EPS reaches ~$1.10 this year and ~$1.35 next year, and the stock trades in line with fair value at ~20x forward, landing around $27–29, consistent with the consensus mean/median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 (August 6) shows revenue growth turning flat-to-positive YoY combined with continued rate increases and units-on-rent stabilizing, confirming the volume bottom.
- Consensus current-quarter and next-quarter EPS revisions turn positive alongside evidence that large-complex-project demand is compounding (D&I revenue growth sustained above 10%).
- AR write-offs normalize and the credit provision declines YoY, removing the customer-stress overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2 2026 revenue decline re-accelerates beyond FY2025's −4.8% pace, or utilization falls despite the shrunken fleet denominator, signaling the volume trend is worsening not stabilizing.
- A materially larger credit provision or a second AR write-off surge, indicating broad-based customer defaults in the construction base.
- Goodwill impairment or covenant pressure if EBITDA slides below ~$900M.

**Reassessment timeline:** The August 6, 2026 Q2 earnings release is the decisive near-term catalyst; the options market is pricing a ±18% move around it. Secondary checkpoints are the pace of Network Optimization disposals and the June 2027 swap maturity as it approaches.

## Conclusion

WillScot is a high-quality, cash-generative market leader that a naïve screen would misclassify as distressed: the negative EPS and 0.38 Altman Z-Score are artifacts of a strategic non-cash restructuring charge, not operating failure. The business funds itself on secured asset-backed debt with no maturities until 2028 and holds pricing power through a three-year construction downturn. Those attributes support the improving score trajectory from 4.6 in March to 5.55 now.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the absence of confirmed inflection. Volume is still eroding, near-quarter estimates are drifting lower, the company has missed EPS four consecutive quarters, and the AR write-off signals real credit stress in the customer base. As an Industrials name, the stock carries a structural negative-alpha tilt that requires a distinct company catalyst to overcome, and the large-complex-project pivot, while promising, is early. The ~15% decline from where the stock traded two months ago improves the risk/reward at the margin, but the fundamentals have not deteriorated to warrant AVOID nor inflected to warrant BUY. We rate WSC a HOLD (growth_hold) with a base case of $27–29, and we would revisit the rating after the August 6 Q2 print, which will resolve whether the Q1 stabilization was durable or transitory.
