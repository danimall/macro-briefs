**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Simmons First National Corporation is a $24.7B-asset Mid-South regional bank whose 2025-2026 story is dominated by a deliberate balance-sheet repositioning rather than operating deterioration. In Q3 2025 management reclassified $3.59B of held-to-maturity securities to available-for-sale and sold $3.16B of low-yield bonds, realizing a $625.6M after-tax loss. The proceeds retired high-cost wholesale funding and rotated into higher-yielding assets, driving net interest margin (FTE) from 2.95% in Q1 2025 to 3.84% in Q1 2026, an 89bp jump that lifted Q1 2026 EPS to $0.47 from $0.26 a year earlier. The headline GAAP data (a ~$380M FY2025 net loss and a negative trailing P/E) is a misleading artifact of a one-time strategic reset, not a distressed bank.

The trade is working on the income statement, but the terms were unfavorable to legacy holders. The $327.4M equity raise at $18.50 (below today's $23.18) covered only ~52% of the realized loss and diluted share count ~15%, so the higher run-rate EPS is spread across permanently more shares while tangible book took a real hit. Tangible book value sits at $14.03/share against a stock at $23.18, roughly 1.65x TBV. The recovery in reported earnings is genuine, but the second-derivative is now flattening: Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.50 missed consensus (~$0.53), revenue slightly missed, and consensus current-year and current-quarter estimates are drifting modestly lower.

We maintain a HOLD (income rationale). The ~3.7% dividend yield is well-covered at roughly a 45% payout, capital is solid (CET1 11.58%), and the NIM re-rating gives the P&L momentum through 2026 as management guides 9-11% net interest income growth. But three things cap the appreciation case: deposit-cost competition is eroding the funding tailwind, the balance sheet is liability-sensitive to the Fed cuts the market expects (−100bp = −2.08% NII), and there is no open-market insider buying or buyback execution to signal management conviction. The stock is a fairly-valued yield holding.

## Company Overview

Simmons First National is a financial holding company headquartered in Pine Bluff, Arkansas, and parent of Simmons Bank, an Arkansas state-chartered institution operating since 1903. As of Q1 2026 it carried ~$24.7B in consolidated assets, $17.9B in loans, $20.2B in deposits, and 221 financial centers across six states: Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Texas. It offers consumer, real estate, and commercial lending plus specialty products including credit cards, trust/wealth, agricultural finance, equipment lending, insurance, and SBA lending, run as a single reportable segment.

The company's identity is an acquisitive roll-up. A long series of deals (Citizens National 2016, Landmark, Triumph, Spirit of Texas, and others) built the current footprint and left $1.32B of goodwill plus $81M of other intangibles on the balance sheet. That is why GAAP book value of $23.70/share sits so far above tangible book of $14.03/share. The loan book is heavily real-estate weighted: real estate is 77.8% of loans (commercial real estate $8.76B, single-family residential $2.57B, construction & development $2.62B), with commercial at 15.9% and small consumer/credit-card books. This is a traditional community/commercial bank with branch density and scale in its region, but no differentiated moat beyond that footprint.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 repositioning is the pivot around which everything turns. The FY2025 net loss was driven entirely by the securities loss; retained earnings fell from $1.377B at YE'24 to $864M at YE'25. To absorb the capital hit, the company raised $327.4M net on July 23, 2025 via 18.65M shares at $18.50, expanding share count from ~125.8M to ~145M. The raise covered barely half the loss, with the rest coming out of retained earnings and AOCI capacity.

On the P&L, the reset is delivering. Selling low-yield bonds and paying down high-cost brokered deposits and wholesale funding drove NIM (FTE) through 3.81% in Q4'25. Net interest income (FTE) rose 17.9% YoY to $200.2M in Q1'26. Q1 2026 net income was $68.5M versus $32.4M a year earlier, though down sequentially from Q4'25's $78.1M ($0.54). Q2 2026 adjusted EPS was up from $0.44 a year earlier, with revenue of roughly $249-252M also a slight miss.

Capital and liquidity are solid. CET1 stands at 11.58%, total risk-based capital 14.36%, Tier 1 leverage 10.14%, and TCE/TA 8.74%, comfortably well-capitalized. Core deposits are 83.5% of total, with $1.91B brokered deposits, $1.42B of jumbo CDs, and $431.6M of FHLB advances. In September 2025 the company issued $325M of 6.25% fixed-to-floating subordinated notes (Tier 2 capital, maturing 2035, hedged with step-down swaps).

Two balance-sheet overhangs warrant emphasis. Despite the repositioning, the remaining $3.15B AFS book still carries $397.9M of gross unrealized losses (82.6% of AFS underwater), and AOCI is −$314.4M. Any further rise in rates deepens this. Separately, the earnings track record has softened: the four-quarter surprise record is 1 beat and 3 misses, with the two most recent quarters missing by −5.7% and −1.8%. Consensus estimates are drifting lower (current-quarter −1.2% and current-year −0.9% over 90 days), which is negative earnings momentum rather than positive revision velocity.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is mechanical NIM re-expansion off a depressed base rather than franchise expansion. Management guides tracking the top end of the NII range, and the +89bp YoY NIM move is the engine. This is real and gives the P&L momentum: a full year at ~3.84% NIM against a prior-year base near 3.0% supports mid-teens core earnings growth as the trade fully seasons.

Three factors question the durability. First, deposit competition is an active headwind flagged on the Q2 call; funding costs are eating into the margin tailwind, which is why Q2 missed. Second, the bank is liability-sensitive to Fed cuts: the interest-rate model shows −100bp = −2.08% NII and −200bp = −3.70% NII. In a base case where the Fed eases through 2026-2027, the margin recovery partially reverses. Third, loan growth is muted: this is a mature branch bank in six Mid-South/Great Plains states with no structural demand tailwind. The higher-for-longer rate environment currently helps the margin, but the market is positioned for cuts, which would work against SFNC. Management is investing in deposit-gathering marketing and funding it with expense/efficiency actions, a defensive maneuver to protect the funding side rather than a top-line growth catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is uninformative here; the negative figure reflects the one-time FY2025 loss. On forward earnings the picture is a fairly-valued yield stock. Consensus current-year EPS is $2.05 and next-year $2.20, putting SFNC at ~11.3x current-year and ~10.5x next-year earnings. That is a discount to the regional-bank peer median of 15.9x (PRK 16.3x, BANC 16.0x, BOH 15.9x, FHB 12.3x, CBU 15.9x). The discount reflects SFNC's multi-year TSR underperformance (5-year TSR of 105 vs peer group 153), its still-negative estimate revisions, liability sensitivity, and the residual AOCI/embedded-loss overhang.

On book, the stock trades at ~0.98x GAAP book but ~1.65x tangible book. The gap between the two is the $1.40B of goodwill/intangibles from the acquisition history, real capital that a peer buyer would not pay full price for. The dividend at $0.86 annualized yields ~3.7%, in line with the higher-yielding peers (BOH 3.6%, FHB 3.7%) and above the 2.9% median, providing absolute-yield support.

The cheapness relative to peers is the classic value-trap configuration our discipline flags: a compressed multiple that reflects structural underperformance and a liability-sensitive balance sheet, not an identified mispricing. There is no clean catalyst for a re-rating. The re-rating that would close the peer gap requires demonstrated, sustained EPS growth without margin give-back, and Q2's miss plus falling estimates argue that has not yet been de-risked. Analyst targets ($24 low, $27 high, $25 mean) sit only modestly above the current price, implying ~8% upside to the mean plus the dividend. Because estimates are falling rather than rising, we do not treat those targets as a floor with upside re-rating potential; they are a fair reflection of a bank whose recovery is priced.

## Competitive Landscape

SFNC competes against national banks, larger regionals, community banks, credit unions, and increasingly fintechs, with no differentiated moat beyond branch density and scale in its six-state footprint. Management explicitly cites intense competition and the need for costly technology/AI investment to keep pace, and it has added expanded AI/agentic-AI and cyber risk language to its 2026 filings.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable-to-slightly-negative. The 2025 repositioning is a playbook that many regional banks ran post-2022 to escape the low-yield securities they accumulated in 2021-22; SFNC itself transferred $2.5B AFS→HTM at the peak, then reversed it at a loss. Because SFNC ran this trade later than many peers, its NIM is normalizing off a lower base, which is both why the recent NIM gain looks dramatic and why its TSR has trailed. Deposit competition is currently pressuring the funding side, and there is no evidence of share gains or moat widening. Credit is quietly softening even as headline earnings recover: NPLs rose to $141.9M (0.79% of loans) from 0.64% at YE'25, ACL/NPL coverage fell from 199% to 162%, and nonaccruals rose $29.4M driven by a single ~$26.9M construction relationship. Given 78% real-estate loan concentration, this trajectory bears watching.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit and concentration.** Credit metrics are deteriorating at the margin, and FY2025 included two problem-credit charge-offs (a pre-pandemic St. Louis hotel at $26.7M and a commercial/fast-food relationship), with full-year 2025 NCOs of $70M. This is not distress, but the trend runs the wrong direction against a heavily real-estate-weighted book.

**Rate/AOCI overhang.** Rising rates deepen the embedded AFS losses; falling rates compress the newly-won margin (−100bp = −2.08% NII). The balance sheet is exposed on both sides of the rate debate.

**IRS tax-treatment tail risk.** The company treated the securities loss as an ordinary loss. The 10-K explicitly warns the IRS could recharacterize it, which given the magnitude would create "material amounts of taxes." This is a specific, company-level tail risk a generic screen would miss.

**Goodwill impairment.** The goodwill balance sits against a stock trading near book. A sustained price decline could force an impairment charge, which while non-cash would dent GAAP capital optics.

**Governance in transition.** A genuine CEO succession is underway (George Makris Jr. to Executive Chairman, James Brogdon to President & CEO) alongside an unusually dense cluster of Item 5.02 officer/director filings across 2025-26 and new change-in-control agreements for incoming executives. Management turnover during a strategic reset adds execution risk.

**Capital-allocation signal.** Despite a $175M buyback authorization in January 2026, zero shares were repurchased in Q1'26 and the dividend was raised only 1%. This is prudent post-reset capital preservation but signals no management conviction that the stock is cheap.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Front-month ATM IV of 84.2% sits far above 1-year historical volatility of 26.3% and at the 92nd percentile within the current chain, with an elevated 41.5% put skew and an implied move of ±21.4%. The term structure is in steep backwardation (84.2% near-term vs 41.0% at six months), consistent with a specific near-dated catalyst; this snapshot is anchored around the mid-July earnings event, and the June-to-July delta shows IV collapsing from an anomalous prior reading. The elevated near-term put demand aligns with the fundamental cautions: a soft Q2 print, falling estimates, and deposit-cost pressure. The options market is not confirming an upside thesis. Short interest is modest at 5.0% of float with 3.8 days to cover, so there is neither a squeeze setup nor heavy informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$27, +16%).** The NIM re-rating fully seasons, 2026 NII growth lands at the top of the guide, deposit competition eases as the Fed holds or the funding mix improves, and credit softening proves idiosyncratic (the single construction credit resolves without loss). EPS reaches the ~$2.20 next-year consensus and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median as SFNC demonstrates sustained beats. The buyback gets deployed, and the ~3.7% yield plus modest re-rating delivers low-double-digit total return. This requires the estimate-revision trend to turn positive, which it has not.

**Bear case (~$18, −22%).** The Fed eases, compressing the liability-sensitive margin (−200bp = −3.70% NII), deposit competition worsens, and CRE/construction credit deterioration accelerates against the real-estate concentration, forcing higher provisions. The IRS challenges the ordinary-loss treatment, creating a material tax charge, and/or a sustained price decline triggers a goodwill impairment. Estimates continue to fall and the stock re-rates back toward tangible-book-plus territory near the low-$18s where the equity raise was priced.

**Base case (~$25, +8%).** NII growth lands in the guided range, but deposit-cost competition holds margin gains near current levels rather than expanding them further. Credit softening is contained but coverage stays under pressure. EPS approaches ~$2.10-2.15, the multiple holds around 11-12x, and the total return is driven mostly by the ~3.7% dividend plus modest earnings growth, landing near the analyst mean of $25.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to quarterly earnings (next print ~mid-October 2026) and the Fed rate path.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS estimate revisions combined with NIM holding above ~3.80% despite deposit competition.
- Meaningful buyback execution (material use of the authorization) at prices below tangible-book-plus, signaling management conviction.
- Credit stabilization — NPLs returning below ~0.65% and ACL/NPL coverage rebuilding toward ~190%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A confirmed Fed easing cycle compressing NII toward the −3.7% (−200bp) modeled sensitivity with no offsetting deposit-cost relief.
- Accelerating credit deterioration (NPLs above ~1.2% of loans or additional large CRE/construction charge-offs).
- An adverse IRS ruling on the ordinary-loss treatment, or a goodwill impairment charge.
- A dividend cut or coverage deteriorating toward the point where the ~45% payout is threatened.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release (~mid-October 2026) and each subsequent quarter, with particular attention to the NIM trajectory, deposit-cost trend, and credit metrics.

## Conclusion

SFNC is a well-capitalized regional bank whose reported financials mislead on the downside; the negative trailing P/E and FY2025 loss are artifacts of a deliberate securities reset, not operating distress. The reset is working: core earnings have roughly doubled off the trough. But the recovery came at a real cost to legacy holders (15% dilution, permanent tangible-book impairment), the second-derivative is flattening (Q2 miss, falling estimates), the bank is exposed to the Fed cuts the market expects, and credit is quietly softening against the real-estate loan book. Management's defensive posture (zero buybacks despite an authorization, a token 1% dividend hike, no open-market insider buying) signals capital caution rather than conviction.

The stock at ~11x forward earnings and ~3.7% yield is fairly priced for a mid-cycle recovery bank with a covered dividend and no identified upside catalyst. The peer-relative discount reflects genuine underperformance and rate sensitivity, not a mispricing. We rate SFNC a HOLD on an income basis: own it for the yield and the modest earnings-recovery tailwind, but the risk/reward does not support adding here, given the falling estimates plus near-term options stress. This is consistent with the declining score trajectory; nothing in the current data reverses the drift, and the negative revision momentum modestly reinforces it.
