## Executive Summary

DoorDash is the dominant US food delivery platform (~65%+ share) that has transformed itself through the $3.7B Deliveroo acquisition into a global local commerce platform operating in 40+ countries. The company has achieved sustained profitability: from -$1.4B net income in 2022 to +$935M in 2025, with free cash flow reaching $2.17B (15.8% FCF margin). Q1 2026 revenue grew 33% YoY to $4.04B with 27% order growth, demonstrating continued execution.

The fundamental quality of this business is high and improving. The three-sided network (merchants, consumers, Dashers) creates genuine moat, the advertising business is scaling as a high-margin revenue stream, and international expansion via Deliveroo provides years of integration-driven growth. The $6.4B cash position and $4.8B remaining buyback authorization provide capital flexibility.

The specific reason this is not a BUY at $161: consensus EPS estimates for the current quarter have collapsed 35% in 90 days (from $0.71 to $0.46), and the company has missed estimates in 2 of the last 4 quarters (including a 22% miss in Q3 2025 and 20% miss in Q4 2025). At 76x trailing earnings and ~63x FY2026 consensus ($2.57), the stock requires accelerating earnings delivery to justify its multiple, and the estimate trajectory is moving in the wrong direction. Until the negative revision cycle stabilizes and the Deliveroo margin dilution is better understood, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

DoorDash operates a three-segment local commerce platform:

1. **Marketplaces** — DoorDash (US), Wolt (Europe/Nordics), and Deliveroo (UK/Europe). These connect consumers with merchants via delivery and pickup services.
2. **Commerce Platform** — White-label delivery (Drive), online ordering, restaurant SaaS (SevenRooms), and branded apps for merchant's own digital channels.
3. **Advertising** — Sponsored listings and offsite media for merchants and CPG companies.

Key operating metrics (Q1 2026): 933M total orders (+27% YoY), $31.6B Marketplace GOV (+37% YoY), $754M Adjusted EBITDA (+28% YoY). The Deliveroo acquisition (October 2025) added the UK and broader European presence, driving international revenue from $376M to $932M in a single year.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
Revenue has re-accelerated: $10.7B (2024) → $13.7B (2025, +28%) → ~$16.1B annualized run-rate in Q1 2026. The operating margin inflected from -17% (2022) to +5.3% (2025). Net income of $935M in 2025 represents a substantial transformation from the loss-making years.

**Cash Flow:**
FCF of $2.17B in 2025 (15.8% margin) positions DoorDash among the strongest cash generators in consumer internet. Q1 2026 FCF of $420M annualizes to ~$1.7B, slightly below 2025 due to Deliveroo integration costs and higher capex. With $6.4B in cash/investments, the balance sheet is fortress-quality for a growth company.

**Balance Sheet:**
Debt consists primarily of $2.75B in 0% convertible notes due 2030 with a $292 conversion price (81% above current). This is exceptionally accretive financing—zero cash interest with conversion only triggered by significant stock appreciation. D/E of 0.96 is reasonable given the cash position.

**Margin Trajectory Concern:**
Net Revenue Margin compressed from 13.1% to 12.8% YoY. This reflects Deliveroo's lower take rate structure and competitive pressure to reduce consumer fees. While contribution margin improved (33.6% → 34.2%), the take rate compression is a headwind to earnings growth unless offset by advertising scale.

**SBC and Dilution:**
Stock-based compensation remains significant: $1.6B in unrecognized SBC expense to be amortized. Total potentially dilutive securities of 30.4M shares (~7% of outstanding) create ongoing dilution headwind to per-share economics.

**The Negative Estimate Revision Problem:**
Despite strong top-line growth, EPS estimates have deteriorated materially:
- Current quarter: $0.71 → $0.46 (−35% over 90 days)
- Next quarter: $0.91 → $0.75 (−18%)
- FY2026: $3.17 → $2.57 (−19%)
- FY2027: $4.98 → $4.39 (−12%)

This decline reflects analysts absorbing Deliveroo integration costs, margin dilution from lower-take-rate international markets, elevated D&A from acquired intangibles (+77% YoY to $269M quarterly), and restructuring charges. The Q3 2025 (-22%) and Q4 2025 (-20%) EPS misses validate that the earnings path is less predictable than the revenue path.

**Growth Quality Assessment:**
Revenue growth is genuine and broad-based. Order growth of 27% with GOV growth of 37% (implying higher average order values) is healthy. The advertising business appears to be scaling as a pure-margin incremental revenue stream. However, translating this top-line momentum into proportional earnings growth has proven difficult, and this is what the market is correctly scrutinizing.

## Competitive Landscape

**Dominant US Position:** DoorDash's ~65%+ US food delivery market share continues to widen. Network effects are self-reinforcing: more restaurants attract more consumers attract more Dashers improve delivery speed attract more consumers.

**Advertising Flywheel:** The emerging advertising business (similar to Amazon's evolution from retail to advertising platform) adds high-margin revenue without requiring additional order volume. Symbiosys acquisition adds offsite capabilities.

**International Challenge:** Post-Deliveroo, DoorDash must compete against deeply entrenched incumbents (Delivery Hero in Germany, Glovo in Southern Europe, Grab in Southeast Asia). The Q1 2026 restructuring (exiting certain countries) acknowledges that not all international markets are worth pursuing.

**Uber Antitrust Lawsuit:** Uber's February 2025 antitrust suit against DoorDash introduces competitive litigation risk. The outcome is unpredictable but creates management distraction and legal costs.

**Structural Moat:** The platform's moat is real but not unassailable. Switching costs for consumers are low (most have multiple delivery apps). Merchant exclusivity is limited. The moat lies primarily in logistics efficiency at scale and DashPass subscription lock-in.

## Risk Assessment

**Worker Classification (Critical):** The independent contractor model underpins the entire unit economics. The EU Platform Work Directive (effective Dec 2024), Italy's judicial administration of Deliveroo, and ongoing US litigation (California EDD audit, PAGA claims) all threaten to reclassify workers as employees. This would increase costs by an estimated 20-30% per delivery and fundamentally alter the business model. The risk is structural and recurring.

**Deliveroo Integration Execution:** A $3.7B acquisition closed 6 months ago with $102M in contingent liabilities and ongoing country exits. Integration complexity is real—the company is simultaneously re-platforming its global technology stack while absorbing a business that was unprofitable under Deliveroo's ownership.

**Negative Estimate Momentum:** Two consecutive earnings misses (Q3 and Q4 2025) eroded analyst confidence. If Q2 2026 also disappoints, the stock will face further derating pressure despite the operational progress.

**Consumer Spending Sensitivity:** In a weakening macro environment, delivery services face demand pressure as consumers trade down. DoorDash has shown resilience historically, but order growth could decelerate if the consumer weakens further.

## Conclusion

DoorDash is a high-quality business at a premium valuation with deteriorating near-term earnings visibility. The company's competitive position is among the strongest in consumer internet, its cash generation is excellent, and the Deliveroo acquisition creates a genuine global platform. These factors support the improving score trajectory (6.2 → 6.4 → 6.7).

The specific impediment to a BUY recommendation is the combination of: (1) 35% negative EPS revision in 90 days for the current quarter, (2) two earnings misses in the last four quarters establishing a pattern of reduced forecast reliability, and (3) a 63x forward multiple that requires earnings acceleration which the data does not currently confirm. The stock has declined 44% from its high, but the estimates have declined proportionally, meaning the P/E has compressed only marginally.

Substantial upside exists if Deliveroo integration succeeds and advertising scales, but the negative revision cycle must stabilize before the market will reward the growth. At $135-140 (35x FY2027 EPS of ~$4), or with two months of positive estimate revisions, this becomes a compelling BUY. At $161 with falling estimates, it is a well-positioned HOLD for investors who already own it, with clear upgrade triggers ahead.
