**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Landstar is a technology-enabled, asset-light freight broker that has lived through a deep, multi-year freight recession and is now showing a credible cyclical recovery. Revenue fell roughly 36% from the 2022 boom ($7.44B) to a near-trough $4.74B in FY25, and operating income collapsed about 73% over the same window on fixed-cost deleverage. Q1 FY2026 reversed that trend: revenue rose 2% to $1.171B, operating income climbed 35% to $53.2M, and diluted EPS rose 36% to $1.16. Operating income as a percent of gross profit expanded to 47.3% from 40.1%, confirming that operating leverage, which worked violently against the company on the way down, is now working in its favor. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised up 6-10% over 90 days, and the next-year figure sits at $7.09, implying the market expects a return toward prior-cycle earnings power.

The quality of the business is not in question. Landstar runs the largest independent agent network and the largest owner-operator fleet in U.S. trucking, carries essentially no funded debt (only ~$69M of trailer finance leases against $353M cash and $147M investments), converts earnings to free cash flow at a high rate on minimal capex, and returns cash aggressively through buybacks, regular dividends, and a third consecutive $2.00 special dividend. The share count has shrunk from 37.6M in 2022 to ~34.0M today. This is a well-run, shareholder-friendly franchise.

The difficulty is price and tail risk. The stock has rallied roughly 50% off its September 2025 low of $119 to ~$207, trading at ~57x trailing EPS of $3.31 and ~36x the current-year consensus of $5.67. The trailing multiple overstates the case because FY25 earnings were depressed by ~$32M of non-cash impairments and an unusually large ~$32M of adverse prior-year insurance reserve development, but even on a normalized basis the entry no longer offers asymmetry. The recovery is price/mix-led — loads were still down ~3% YoY in Q1 — and an asymmetric broker-liability legal exposure (a $22.8M 100%-liability judgment in the Cabral matter despite a 15% fault finding, plus a pending Supreme Court FAAAA preemption case) sits underneath the asset-light model. We rate the shares HOLD, with the recovery durable enough to own through a true volume recovery but not cheap enough to add at current levels.

## Company Overview

Landstar generates freight through a network of approximately 980 independent commission sales agents and moves it via more than 70,000 third-party capacity providers: roughly 8,500 owner-operator "BCO Independent Contractors" on exclusive lease and 62,000+ non-exclusive Truck Brokerage Carriers. The company owns almost no trucks. This network model produces high returns on invested capital, very low capex, and a variable cost structure (purchased transportation ~78% of revenue plus agent commissions ~8%, so ~86% of costs flex with volume).

Revenue is essentially all Transportation Logistics (~99%), with a small captive insurance segment (Signature Insurance) reinsuring BCO risk. By mode, BCO Independent Contractors represent 38% of FY25 revenue, Truck Brokerage Carriers 53%, rail 2%, and air/ocean 5%. Truck transportation is ~91% of the total. Van and unsided/platform (flatbed and heavy-haul) are the two largest equipment types, with heavy-haul/specialized freight a higher-revenue-per-load growth focus.

Concentration runs through the agent base rather than the customer base. In FY25, 457 "Million Dollar Agents" generated 95% of revenue and 77 agencies (>$10M each) drove ~68% of consolidated revenue. No single customer exceeds 8% of revenue, and the top 100 customers are only ~46%; customer diversification is excellent, but the dependence on a small set of large agents is a structural feature of the model.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant financial fact is the trough-to-recovery arc. Revenue fell from $7.44B (FY22 peak) to $5.30B (FY23), $4.82B (FY24), and $4.74B (FY25). Operating income fell from $571M to $344M to $249M to $152M. Diluted EPS dropped from $7.36 (FY23) to $5.51 (FY24) to $3.31 (FY25).

FY25 earnings were further depressed by roughly $32.2M of non-cash impairments ($0.71/share): goodwill/held-for-sale on Landstar Metro ($18.2M), wind-down of Landstar Blue's redundant TMS ($9.0M), and a write-down of a 2022 Cavnue equity investment ($5.0M). Excluding these, FY25 EPS would have been approximately $4.02. A second, partly non-recurring drag came from insurance and claims, which rose ~$45.5M, driven by $32.1M of adverse prior-year claim development, two fatal Q4 accidents (~$11M), and higher reserves. The underlying earnings power of the business is therefore somewhat above the $3.31 headline.

The Q1'26 recovery drivers are concrete: revenue per load up roughly 4%, heavy-haul/platform revenue per load up ~11%, BCO share rising to 41% from 37%, and a $6.5M reduction in unfavorable prior-year claims development versus Q1'25. Variable contribution margin has been stable through the cycle (14.1-14.7%); the swing in profitability comes from fixed-cost leverage, which is why operating income/gross profit jumped to 47.3% on only a 2% revenue increase. Load count was still down ~3% YoY, so the recovery is currently price/mix-led, not volume-led.

The balance sheet is strong. The reported D/E of ~2.06 reflects insurance claim reserves and payables, not borrowings. Funded debt is only ~$69M of trailer finance leases. Cash is $353M, investments $147M, with a $265M undrawn revolver plus a $300M accordion. Altman Z-Score is 4.56 (safe zone) and the current ratio is 1.9x. Free cash flow was ~$215M in FY25 against minimal cash capex (~$10M; trailers are largely lease-financed). Capital return has been substantial and consistent: FY25 returned $180.9M of buybacks, $54M of regular dividends, and a $68M special dividend, with the regular dividend raised to $0.40/quarter from $0.36.

The earnings surprise track record is a mild yellow flag: over the last four quarters, one beat and three misses, including a sharp -43.8% miss in the December 2025 quarter (EPS $0.70 vs $1.25 estimate). The most recent quarter (March 2026) was an in-line beat (+1.2%). Forward visibility in a freight broker is inherently limited by spot-rate volatility, so the recent miss history argues for modest confidence in any single forward print even as the directional trend improves.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is cyclical recovery layered on a structurally sound network. Estimate revisions are broadly positive: current-quarter EPS revised +10.1% over 90 days to $1.47, next-quarter +6.2% to $1.50, current-year +6.2% to $5.67, and next-year +6.2% to $7.09. These revisions fall short of the >+30% velocity that would flag a genuine BUY signal; this is a steady upward grind, not a step-change.

The mechanism underneath the recovery is supportive. Total available truck capacity fell roughly 18% YoY in Q1 (88.5K to 72.7K capacity providers) as carriers exited during the downturn. Tighter supply supports the rate recovery Landstar is now beginning to capture, a constructive structural signal for the next 12-18 months. Management has described "encouraging signs" of a strengthening rate environment and a freight upcycle taking shape. Landstar's growing mix of higher-revenue-per-load heavy-haul/platform freight is a genuine differentiator and a stated growth priority.

The limit on the growth thesis is that volumes have not turned. A price/mix-led recovery can carry earnings a long way given the operating leverage, but the highest-confidence growth scenario — rates firming and loads recovering simultaneously — has not yet materialized. The path from $3.31 (FY25) toward the implied $5.67 (FY26) and $7.09 (FY27) is plausible against the prior-cycle peaks of $5.51 and $7.36, but it requires the upcycle to broaden from price into volume.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$207 on trailing EPS of $3.31, the headline P/E is ~57-62x, a trough-earnings multiple that overstates expensiveness. The more relevant frame is forward: ~36x the current-year consensus and ~29x the next-year consensus. Even adjusting for the cyclical trough, this is not a cheap stock.

The peer set frames the dilemma. In Cargo Ground Transportation, the peer median P/E is 68.2x, with ARCB at 70.1x and SNDR at 68.2x, while several names (RXO -45.8x, WERN -298.2x) carry negative or distressed earnings, and Ryder — the more asset-heavy, less cyclically depressed comp — trades at 23.2x. The peer multiples are themselves distorted by the same freight-cycle trough, so they do not establish that Landstar is cheap; they establish that the whole group is being valued on recovery earnings. On forward earnings, Landstar at ~29x next-year is in line with the asset-light brokers and at a premium to Ryder.

Analyst targets center below the current price: mean $181.29, median $180.00, range $145-$240. The stock at $207 trades above mean and median, with only the high-target analyst above spot. The shares are not at a clearly cheap multiple relative to growth (29-36x forward versus a ~+6% revision pace and a recovery that is not yet volume-confirmed), so the "analyst targets are floors" principle does not apply here: at these multiples the targets and the price reflect similar recovery optimism, and the consensus target sitting below the price is a caution, not a setup.

Cheapness is not the case for owning Landstar, and we treat the elevated trailing multiple as a signal that the recovery is already substantially priced rather than as a value-trap. The valuation score reflects that the asymmetry that existed at $119-$140 has largely closed after the ~50% run. We assign a 4.5: neither a value trap nor an attractive entry, with the multiple fairly reflecting a recovery that still has to prove its volume leg.

## Competitive Landscape

Landstar's moat is the scale of its agent and owner-operator network combined with balance-sheet strength. It runs the largest independent agent network and the largest owner-operator fleet in the U.S., and its capitalization lets it assume customer credit risk and pay agents and carriers promptly, an edge over thinly capitalized brokers, especially through a downturn that has thinned the field. The company has invested ~$220M since 2016 in its digital platform (~$28M in FY25), including an internal AI task force layering pricing, retention, and fraud-detection tools onto third-party infrastructure. These tools are Landstar-owned, with no indication of third-party ownership of the company's technology.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. The freight recession forced capacity out of the market, and digital-broker disruptors from the Convoy/Uber Freight era have been culled by the same downcycle that pressured Landstar. The Million Dollar Agent count fell from 524 (2023) to 457 (2025), but management attributes this primarily to agents slipping below the $1M threshold in the soft market rather than terminations (historical attrition <3%/year), so the network remains intact. Heavy-haul/specialized freight, where Landstar holds a relative advantage, is a higher-margin growth pocket.

The market is "extremely competitive and fragmented," and the asset-light model is structurally replicable by anyone with capital and a carrier network, which caps the moat. The trajectory is positive within the cycle, but this is not a widening structural moat on the order of an IT-platform franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**Broker liability — the asymmetric tail.** In the Cabral matter, a jury assigned Landstar Ranger 15% fault ($3.42M) acting as a broker, but in January 2026 the trial court entered judgment holding Landstar 100% liable for the full $22.8M plus interest. Landstar is appealing and recorded a $5.7M charge. The systemic question matters more: FAAAA federal preemption of broker negligent-selection claims is before the U.S. Supreme Court (Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II). An adverse ruling would expand broker-liability exposure across the entire asset-light brokerage industry and raise the structural cost of the model. This is a genuine, partly unpriced tail risk.

**Insurance and claims severity.** Excess coverage premiums (above the $10M layer) are up ~400% / ~$22M since 2020. Landstar self-insures up to $5M per occurrence and could bear ~$36M on a single $65M claim. "Nuclear verdict" inflation is a structural margin headwind, not a one-off.

**Cyclicality and the unconfirmed volume turn.** Earnings are highly geared to freight demand and spot rates. If the upcycle stalls before volumes turn, the forward estimates are at risk. The December 2025 EPS miss is a recent reminder of how quickly the quarterly picture can deteriorate.

**Supply-chain fraud and cargo theft.** Q1'25 fraud in international freight-forwarding cost $4.8M directly, lost an ocean customer, and elevated cargo-claim development into Q1'26. Cargo theft is an industry-wide and rising problem; management has deployed AI fraud detection in response.

**Agent and geopolitical concentration.** The model depends on a small set of large agents, and the second-largest agent by revenue is exposed to the Russia/Ukraine conflict, a specific, repeatedly disclosed risk.

**Contractor classification and regulation.** Ongoing legal risk to the owner-operator model plus FMCSA changes (English-proficiency enforcement, non-domiciled CDL restrictions), not yet materially affecting capacity sourcing.

These are largely company-specific and not fully priced — particularly the FAAAA Supreme Court binary — which supports a below-neutral risk score of 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contrarian signal. Near-term ATM IV is 37.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 35.4% — fair, with the IV sitting at the 4th percentile of its own chain. Put/call skew is -1.0% (balanced), the put/call OI ratio is a low 0.11, and the term structure is in normal contango (37.3% near vs 42.1% long-term). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.8%. The market is pricing no acute near-term stress or directional fear, consistent with a stock that has been grinding higher; it neither confirms nor challenges the fundamental view, and the modest rise in P/C OI from 0.06 to 0.11 alongside a -1.3% price move is noise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The freight upcycle broadens from price into volume over the next 12-18 months. Tighter capacity sustains rate gains, heavy-haul mix lifts revenue per load, and Landstar's operating leverage drives earnings back toward the prior-cycle range. FY26 lands near the $5.67 consensus and FY27 toward $7.09, at the prior peak of $7.36. The balance sheet keeps returning cash through buybacks, regular dividends, and special dividends, and the share count keeps shrinking. The stock re-rates toward $240 (the high analyst target) as recovery earnings prove durable.

**Bear Case.** The recovery stalls as a price/mix illusion: loads never turn, rates plateau, and the freight cycle disappoints. An adverse Supreme Court FAAAA ruling expands broker liability and raises the structural cost of the asset-light model, while nuclear-verdict inflation continues to erode insurance economics. The miss history repeats, estimates roll over, and the ~36x forward multiple compresses hard. The stock retraces toward the low analyst target of $145, roughly -30% from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed volume turn: load count growth turning positive YoY for two consecutive quarters, validating the recovery beyond price/mix.
- A pullback toward the high-$160s to low-$170s, restoring asymmetry on FY26-27 recovery earnings (roughly 24-26x next-year EPS).
- Favorable resolution of the FAAAA preemption question at the Supreme Court, removing the most asymmetric tail and de-risking the model.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse Supreme Court FAAAA ruling that materially expands broker liability, structurally raising the cost base.
- Estimate revisions turning negative alongside a renewed quarterly miss (>10% below consensus), signaling the upcycle has stalled.
- A second large adverse insurance reserve development (>$25M) confirming a structural rather than cyclical claims problem.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 FY2026, late July/early August) for the volume-versus-price read, plus any Supreme Court action on Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II. The freight-cycle trajectory over the next two prints is the central variable.

## Conclusion

Landstar is a high-quality, asset-light franchise with a strong balance sheet, exemplary capital discipline, and a genuine cyclical recovery underway. The latest data confirms that direction: Q1 operating income +35%, steadily rising estimates, and capacity exits supporting the rate recovery. The improvement is real, and the business merits being held through the upcycle.

The recovery is now substantially priced. The stock has run ~50% off its September lows to ~36x current-year and ~29x next-year earnings, the recovery remains price/mix-led, the December quarter posted a -43.8% miss, and the broker-liability tail — Cabral and the pending FAAAA Supreme Court case — sits underneath the model as an asymmetric, partly unpriced risk. Analyst consensus targets sit below the current price, and there is no insider open-market buying to signal conviction (instead, a director and the CFO sold into post-earnings strength). The combination of a fully recovery-priced multiple and an unconfirmed volume turn means the asymmetry that existed in the $119-$140 zone has closed.

We rate Landstar **HOLD (growth_hold)**. The thesis is constructive — own it for the cyclical recovery and the durable, cash-returning franchise — but we would wait for either a true volume turn or a pullback toward the high-$160s before adding.
