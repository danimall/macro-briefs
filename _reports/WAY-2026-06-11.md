**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Waystar is a cloud-based healthcare revenue-cycle management (RCM) software platform that sits between providers, payers, and patients, automating eligibility, claims submission, denial management, remittance, and patient payment collection. The business is high quality: more than 99% of revenue is recurring, adjusted EBITDA margins run at 43.1%, operating cash flow grew 32% year-over-year in Q1 2026, and the company swung from a FY2024 GAAP net loss of ($19.1M) to FY2025 net income of $112.1M. The franchise processed over $2.4 trillion in gross claims and 7.5 billion transactions in 2025, touching roughly 60% of U.S. patients and one in three hospital discharges.

The stock has fallen roughly 55% from its 52-week high of $41.47 to $18.80, and trades below its $21.50 IPO price. The decline is attributable to two identifiable, non-fundamental forces: (1) relentless secondary supply from private-equity sponsors (EQT, CPPIB, Bain executed three offerings in 2025 totaling ~55.4M shares), and (2) the broad 2026 "SaaS-pocalypse / AI software loser" repricing that swept up the entire healthcare-tech and application-software complex. Operating performance moved the opposite direction over the same window: revenue +22.4% YoY in Q1 2026, margins expanding, EPS beats in three of the last four quarters, and a swing to GAAP profitability.

At ~$18.80, WAY trades at approximately 11.5x forward non-GAAP EPS ($1.64 FY26 consensus) and roughly 11x adjusted cash flow against low-teens cash-flow growth. That is a sizeable discount to its Health Care Technology peer median P/E of 37.7x, and it sits well below the company's own growth and margin profile. The catalyst that converts this from a value observation into a timed thesis is the $200M buyback authorized May 19, 2026, which mechanically absorbs sponsor supply at depressed prices and signals management's own read on intrinsic value. I rate WAY a BUY with a base-case target of $26 (about 16x FY26 EPS), bull case $33 (consensus median), and bear case $14.

## Company Overview

Waystar (NASDAQ: WAY), headquartered in Lehi, Utah, operates a single-segment cloud software platform addressing healthcare administrative cost, an area carrying roughly $440B of annual U.S. administrative spending and ~60M claims denied each year for administrative errors. The platform spans the full RCM workflow: pre-service eligibility verification, claims submission, denial prevention and management, payer remittance, and patient payment collection.

Scale is the moat. Waystar serves about 30,000 clients representing over 1 million distinct providers, including 16 of the top 20 U.S. News Best Hospitals (17 of 20 pro forma with the Iodine acquisition). Revenue splits between subscription (54.9% of Q1 2026 revenue) and volume-based fees (44.4%), with the remainder professional services. Contracts run two to three years with automatic one-year renewals. The structure produces natural operating leverage and two embedded growth vectors: volume revenue rises as existing clients see more patients, and cross-sell adds modules over time. The count of clients generating over $100K of annual revenue grew from 1,244 to 1,433 year-over-year.

The competitive differentiation rests on a data network effect. Every transaction trains "Waystar AltitudeAI," the company's proprietary multi-model engine combining machine learning, LLMs, and agentic AI. The October 2025 Iodine acquisition adds a proprietary clinical-intelligence dataset covering over one-third of U.S. inpatient discharges. Both AltitudeAI and Iodine's "IodineIQ" are owned models and datasets, not licensed third-party products; the board's Google Cloud connection (director Aashima Gupta) is an infrastructure relationship, not a product dependency.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and growth.** FY2024 revenue of $943.5M rose to $1,099.3M in FY2025 (+16.5%). Q1 2026 revenue reached $313.9M, up 22.4% YoY, though the headline includes roughly one quarter of Iodine. The composition matters: subscription revenue grew 37.7% (Iodine plus cross-sell), while volume-based revenue grew 7.3% organically. Stripping out Iodine, organic growth runs in the mid-teens, which is the number to model forward.

**Profitability and margins.** The company moved from a FY2024 net loss of ($19.1M) to FY2025 net income of $112.1M. Q1 2026 GAAP net income was $43.3M (13.8% margin) versus $29.3M (11.4%) a year prior. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 43.1% from 42.0%. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $0.42 versus $0.32. The gap between GAAP net income ($43.3M) and non-GAAP ($81.2M) is almost entirely intangible amortization (~$34.5M/quarter) and stock-based compensation, a legacy of the private-equity roll-up that assembled the platform through ten acquisitions since 2018. Any valuation lens anchored on trailing GAAP EPS will misjudge this business; cash flow is the correct metric.

**Cash flow quality.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $84.9M, up 32% YoY, on $43.3M of GAAP net income. Cash conversion runs well above reported earnings because of the D&A add-back and favorable working-capital dynamics inherent in a payments-adjacent model. FCF yield on a ~$3.6B market cap is robust and supports both deleveraging and the buyback.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is approximately $1.48B (First Lien $1.38B plus a $100M receivables facility), with net leverage around 3.5x following the Iodine close. Cash is $34M plus $125M in investment securities. The effective First Lien rate is 5.84% (SOFR + 2.00%), partially hedged via swaps (~$550M fixed at ~3.3–3.6%); a 100bp rate move equates to roughly $3.7M of quarterly interest impact, modest against $85M of quarterly OCF. The asset base is intangible-heavy: $4.0B goodwill plus $1.26B intangibles equal roughly 90% of $5.8B total assets, which is what places the Altman Z-Score in the grey zone. That reflects acquisition accounting, not operating distress. Management is actively deleveraging: $20M First Lien repaid in February 2026, and the receivables facility was repriced (SOFR+1.61% to +1.10%) and extended to 2029. Leverage at 3.5x against 43% EBITDA margins and high cash conversion is serviceable for this business model.

## Growth Analysis

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss across the last four quarters (Q1 2026 +7.8%, Q4 2025 -9.5%, Q3 2025 +4.4%, Q2 2025 +6.3%). Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising: current-year EPS consensus moved +0.9% over 90 days to $1.64, next year +0.2% to $1.85. This is a steady, low-teens grower whose estimates are holding firm while the multiple has collapsed.

Three growth drivers carry the forward case. First, organic volume growth tracks U.S. healthcare transaction volumes, which expand with utilization and an aging population. Second, cross-sell continues to lift per-client revenue, evidenced by the >$100K-client count growing 15% YoY. Third, Iodine extends the addressable market by more than 15% into clinical documentation and utilization management, with $15M+ of run-rate synergies targeted over 18–24 months. Iodine is not accretive to non-GAAP EPS until 2027, so near-term it inflates the revenue optic while diluting EPS; the real earnings contribution arrives in 2027.

The single genuine growth yellow flag is net revenue retention, which decelerated from 113.5% to 110.5% YoY. This almost certainly reflects the maturing Change Healthcare cohort: the February 2024 cyberattack on competitor Change Healthcare drove 30,000+ providers to Waystar, adding ~$34M of incremental revenue in 2024 and ~$11M in Q1 2025. As that windfall anniversaries, NRR is normalizing. If NRR stabilizes in the low-110s, the durability case holds. If it slips toward ~105%, the "one-time benefit" bear case gains real traction. This is the metric to monitor each quarter.

## Valuation Assessment

WAY trades at approximately 11.5x FY26 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.64 and roughly 11x adjusted cash flow, against low-teens cash-flow growth. The peer set in Health Care Technology shows wide dispersion: DOCS at 21.0x, OMCL at 97.7x, HSTM at 37.7x, SOLV at 9.9x, CERT unprofitable. The peer median P/E is 37.7x. Adjusting for WAY's superior margin profile (43% EBITDA) and recurring-revenue quality, an 11x adjusted cash-flow multiple is a clear discount to where this franchise should clear.

The trailing GAAP P/E of ~27.9x looks unremarkable, but it is depressed by ~$138M of annual intangible amortization that does not reflect economic earnings. On the metric that matters, cash flow, the stock is cheap. This is not a low-multiple value trap masking structural decline: the business is growing revenue 16–22%, expanding margins, and converting earnings to cash above 100%. The compression is sentiment-driven (the "AI software loser" repricing) and supply-driven (sponsor secondaries), neither of which reflects deteriorating economics.

The >50% drawdown raises the burden of proof, and I will meet it specifically. What sophisticated sellers have been doing is mechanical, not informational: EQT, CPPIB, and Bain sold ~55.4M shares across three 2025 offerings to monetize their pre-IPO positions. That supply, layered onto the broad SaaS derating, explains the price action without requiring any fundamental thesis about hidden deterioration. The one falsifiable concern a bear can hold is that the Change Healthcare cohort proves transient and NRR collapses, which would mean trailing revenue overstates run-rate. The NRR trend (113.5%→110.5%, decelerating but still well above 100%) does not yet support that view; retention above 110% means the installed base is still net-expanding even as the windfall fades.

At $18.80, a re-rating to a still-modest 16x FY26 EPS implies ~$26, roughly 39% upside. The analyst consensus median target of $33 implies ~75% upside and a high of $44. I treat consensus as a reasonable bull-case marker given the cheapness relative to growth, while anchoring my base case more conservatively at 16x to reflect the NRR risk and sponsor-overhang technicals.

## Competitive Landscape

Waystar's primary competitor, Change Healthcare (now part of UnitedHealth/Optum), was crippled by the 2024 cyberattack and remains a diminished force in the independent RCM channel. Other competition spans Optum, R1 RCM, Experian Health, and provider in-house systems. Waystar's differentiation is end-to-end breadth, a data network effect that strengthens with scale, and an AI automation layer trained on proprietary transaction data.

The competitive trajectory is improving, not merely stable. Share gains from the Change Healthcare disruption appear largely sticky (implementations are permanent and switching costs in RCM are high), the >$100K-client base is expanding, and Iodine adds a differentiated clinical-intelligence dataset that competitors cannot easily replicate. Customer concentration is low, with the top 10 clients representing ~10.8% of revenue, which removes a common failure mode in software franchises. The structural tailwind of automating $440B of administrative cost with AI is real and broad-based, and Waystar is among the better-positioned to monetize it given its data scale.

## Risk Assessment

**Net revenue retention deceleration.** The most important fundamental risk. A slide toward 105% would validate the one-time-benefit bear case and compress the growth multiple further.

**Sponsor share overhang.** The 2025 secondaries have pressured the stock. Advent holds 16.6M shares from the Iodine deal, locked up until ~April 2027. Continued sponsor selling remains a technical headwind, partially offset by the $200M buyback.

**Iodine integration and near-term dilution.** The $1.26B deal (50/50 cash/stock) added ~$639M cash outlay, 16.6M shares, and incremental debt. Integration execution and synergy capture carry execution risk.

**Leverage and rates.** Partial hedging caps the rate sensitivity, and the model's cash generation comfortably services the debt, but the intangible-heavy balance sheet and grey-zone Z-Score warrant monitoring.

**Regulatory/policy exposure.** Revenue depends on U.S. healthcare transaction volumes and reimbursement rules; HIPAA/cybersecurity obligations are heavy, and Waystar is itself a high-value cyber target given the data it processes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($33).** NRR stabilizes in the low-110s, confirming the Change Healthcare wins are durable. Iodine synergies land and turn accretive in 2027. The $200M buyback absorbs sponsor supply, and as the overhang clears, the multiple re-rates from ~11x cash flow toward the high-teens that a 43%-margin, mid-teens grower deserves. Consensus median target of $33 (and high of $44) reflects this path. Roughly 75% upside.

**Bear case ($14).** The Change Healthcare cohort proves more transient than management frames it; NRR slides toward 105%, organic growth decelerates to high-single-digits, and the market concludes trailing revenue overstated run-rate. Continued sponsor selling and a persistent SaaS-derating regime keep the multiple compressed at ~8–9x cash flow. Roughly 26% downside.

The asymmetry favors the long. The franchise quality (99% recurring, 43% EBITDA, low customer concentration, expanding cash flow) is not in dispute; the debate is entirely about how much of the recent growth is durable and how long the technical overhang persists. With the stock below its IPO price, management buying back stock, and estimates holding firm, the price is discounting a bear case that the data does not yet support.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $26.** 16x FY26 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.64 = $26.24. A 16x multiple is conservative for a mid-teens grower at 43% EBITDA margins and remains a discount to the peer median; it reflects appropriate haircuts for the NRR risk and sponsor overhang. Implies ~39% upside from $18.80.

**Bull case target: $33.** Consensus median target, ~20x FY26 EPS, achieved if NRR stabilizes and the overhang clears. ~75% upside.

**Bear case target: $14.** ~8.5x FY26 EPS, achieved if NRR deteriorates toward 105% and the SaaS-derating regime persists. ~26% downside.

**Timeframe: 12–18 months,** tied to: quarterly NRR prints, Iodine accretion confirmation in 2027 guidance, and progress on the $200M buyback offsetting sponsor supply.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Net revenue retention falls below 108% in either of the next two quarters, signaling the Change Healthcare cohort is rolling off faster than management frames.
2. Adjusted EBITDA margin contracts below 41% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the operating-leverage story is breaking.
3. Management abandons or fails to execute the $200M buyback, or sponsors announce a further large secondary (>15M shares) that overwhelms the repurchase, signaling the overhang persists indefinitely.

## Conclusion

Waystar is a high-quality, recurring-revenue healthcare-payments software franchise priced as if it were structurally impaired, when the evidence points to the opposite: revenue growing 16–22%, EBITDA margins expanding to 43%, operating cash flow up 32%, EPS beating in three of four quarters, and a swing to GAAP profitability. The ~55% drawdown reflects sponsor secondary supply and the indiscriminate "AI software loser" sentiment trade, neither of which reflects operating deterioration. The valuation is the catalyst, and the $200M buyback authorized at depressed prices provides the timing trigger and a credible signal of management's own conviction.

The honest counterweight is net revenue retention, which is decelerating as the Change Healthcare windfall matures. That is the one metric capable of breaking the thesis, and it sits at the top of the monitoring list. At 110.5% the installed base is still net-expanding, the franchise economics are intact, and the asymmetry of ~39% upside to base case against ~26% downside, in a name trading below its IPO price with stable estimates and insider/corporate capital coming in, supports a BUY. I rate WAY **BUY**, base-case target $26, 12–18 month horizon.
