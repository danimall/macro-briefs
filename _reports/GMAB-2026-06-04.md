**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Genmab is a Copenhagen-based antibody-engineering company in the middle of a transition from a high-margin royalty collector into a self-commercializing oncology firm. The economic engine is daratumumab, which Genmab invented and licensed to Johnson & Johnson; J&J sells it as DARZALEX/DARZALEX FASPRO ($3,964M in Q1 2026 net sales, +22% YoY), and Genmab collects a royalty. Royalty revenue was $742M in Q1 2026 (+26% YoY), roughly 83% of total revenue of $896M (+25% YoY). The company is GAAP-profitable, which is rare for a biotech of this size and is genuinely favorable, but the quality of that profit is shifting: reported operating profit declined to $180M in Q1 2026 from $188M a year earlier, with growth visible only on an adjusted basis ($237M, +24%) after stripping out Merus integration charges and rising intangible amortization that will persist for years.

The investment debate reduces to a single question: can Genmab's late-stage pipeline (petosemtamab, acquired in the ~$8B Merus deal closed December 2025; Rina-S from ProfoundBio; and continued EPKINLY/TEPKINLY expansion with AbbVie) replace DARZALEX royalty economics before biosimilar erosion arrives. To fund that bet, Genmab abandoned its historical net-cash posture and took on meaningful leverage, with the Merus tender (~75.9M shares at $97.00, ~$7.36B settled Dec 29, 2025) cited by The Globe and Mail/TipRanks as a sharp 2025 leverage increase. The strategic logic of diversifying out of hematology and away from partner-controlled royalties is sound, but it converts a clean, cash-generative balance sheet into an execution story dependent on solid-tumor clinical outcomes.

The ADR has fallen from a January 2026 high of $35.43 to roughly $23.88, a ~33% drawdown that places it near the 52-week low of $20.23 and well below the analyst target range of $32–$41.50 (mean ~$36.82). At that magnitude of decline the burden is on the bull case to identify what sellers are missing. The market is pricing two real developments: near-term EPS estimates have been cut across every forward period over the past 30 days, and an April DLBCL readout disappointed. Against that, current-year and next-year estimates are still up over 90 days, the royalty base is compounding, and an FDA label improvement (removing the 24-hour hospitalization recommendation for 3L+ DLBCL EPKINLY) helps adoption. The signals are genuinely two-sided, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Company Overview

Genmab operates a hybrid model with three economic layers:

- **Royalty engine.** Genmab invented daratumumab (DARZALEX) and ofatumumab (Kesimpta, marketed by Novartis in multiple sclerosis), and collects royalties on partner net sales. This is the cash cow and the largest single risk, since Genmab does not control pricing, manufacturing, label strategy, or litigation for these products. The 2022 arbitration loss to J&J on two DARZALEX license issues already demonstrated that the royalty economics are contractual and contestable.
- **Commercial product.** EPKINLY/TEPKINLY (epcoritamab), a CD3xCD20 bispecific T-cell engager for DLBCL and follicular lymphoma, co-developed and profit-shared with AbbVie. Because the product is profit-shared, EPKINLY contributes less to Genmab economics than headline sales imply. Cost of product sales rose 55% YoY in Q1 2026 largely because of this share.
- **Proprietary platforms and pipeline.** DuoBody, HexaBody, DuoHexaBody, and HexElect underpin internal and partnered programs. The growth bet rests on petosemtamab (EGFRxLGR5 bispecific for head and neck cancer, from Merus) and Rina-S (an ADC from ProfoundBio), plus Tivdak in cervical cancer.

As a Danish foreign private issuer, Genmab files Form 20-F annually (FY2025 filed February 17, 2026) and reports interim and material events on Form 6-K. It does not file 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, or proxy statements, so several standard quantitative inputs (Altman Z, Piotroski F, XBRL trend pipelines, US-style proxy compensation) are unavailable. This is a structural transparency difference rather than a red flag, and the absence of those filings should not be scored as a deficiency.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** FY2025 revenue was approximately $3.72B. Q1 2026 revenue of $896M was driven by DARZALEX and Kesimpta royalties plus rising EPKINLY product sales. The headline profit story is more nuanced than the revenue growth suggests. Reported GAAP operating profit fell despite 25% revenue growth, because of $45M in Merus severance and retention charges and $12M of acquired-intangible amortization. Operating expenses excluding deal charges were $606M (+25%), reflecting pipeline advancement and global commercial buildout ahead of launches.

**Earnings quality.** The wedge between reported and adjusted profit is the key financial development. M&A integration charges and intangible amortization from both ProfoundBio and Merus will run through the P&L for several years, so the gap between adjusted and GAAP profit is not a one-quarter artifact. The trailing P/E of ~1.8x reflects a $15.37 FY2025 EPS figure on the ordinary share against an ADR price that represents a fraction of an ordinary share; the trailing multiple is not directly comparable across the ADR structure and is not a usable valuation signal on its own.

**Balance sheet.** Genmab was historically net-cash and self-funding. The Merus acquisition materially increased leverage, and third-party analysis flags weaker earnings-quality signals tied to the deal. This is the single largest change to the financial profile and the reason a once-pristine balance sheet now carries integration and capital-deployment risk.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** The recent record is mixed and recently negative: +25.1% (quarter ended June 2025) and +36.4% (quarter ended September 2025) beats gave way to a -39.1% miss (quarter ended December 2025) and a -41.0% miss (quarter ended March 2026). The two misses coincide with the Merus close and the charges flowing through; the magnitude of the misses warrants caution on the reliability of near-term consensus.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is real and royalty-led. The DARZALEX net sales compound a high-margin royalty base, and Kesimpta adds a second royalty stream. Royalty growth is the durable part of the story until the patent cliff arrives.

The forward growth thesis depends on pipeline execution. Supporting catalysts: positive EPCORE FL-1 Phase 3 data (December 2025) for fixed-duration EPKINLY plus R² in relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma, with an August 2025 readout showing a 79% reduction in progression/death risk, supporting earlier-line expansion; the FDA label change, a concrete adoption improvement; and the addition of solid-tumor optionality through petosemtamab (head and neck) and Rina-S (gynecologic and other).

The estimate-revision picture is split by horizon. Over 90 days, current-year EPS is up +25.4% and next-year +10.3% — revision velocity that, in isolation, would be a bullish signal. But over the most recent 30 days every forward period has been cut: current quarter -3.6%, next quarter -8.3%, current year -12.0%, next year -4.1%. The 30-day deterioration is the more recent and more relevant signal, and it reflects the April DLBCL disappointment and the persistent drag from acquisition charges. A genuine inflection BUY signal requires rising estimates and a cheap multiple on those estimates; here the most recent revision momentum is negative, which removes the inflection setup.

## Valuation Assessment

The ADR trades near $23.88 against the analyst target range (low $32, high $41.50, mean ~$36.82, median $38.00), implying roughly 34–74% upside to the target band, and a recent Buy rating carries a $38 target. The composition of the analyst panel (9 buy, 2–3 hold, no sells) skews constructive, though sentiment has been classified as degrading after one buy moved to hold.

Two things temper the apparent discount. First, the analyst targets were largely set before the most recent 30-day estimate cuts and the April DLBCL reaction, so they are stale to a degree. Second, current-year consensus EPS of $1.04 (ADR basis) and next-year $1.83 imply a forward P/E in the low-to-mid teens to low-20s depending on which year is used — not the deep-value multiple the 1.8x trailing figure suggests once the ADR ratio is normalized. Morningstar's narrow-moat, roughly-fairly-valued framing is consistent with this: the stock is not obviously mispriced on forward fundamentals, and the discount to targets compensates for genuine pipeline and cliff risk rather than representing a clean error.

The valuation supports a 6.5: there is identifiable upside to a constructive analyst band and a real royalty base providing downside support, but the absence of asymmetry (the discount is the price of binary risk, and near-term estimates are falling) keeps it out of high-conviction territory.

## Competitive Landscape

Genmab's moat rests on a validated antibody-discovery platform (DuoBody/HexaBody) that has produced multiple commercial molecules, plus a recurring royalty stream few biotechs of its size possess. That is a defensible base.

The competitive trajectory in the growth segments is harder. In DLBCL and follicular lymphoma, EPKINLY competes against Roche's CD20xCD3 bispecifics (glofitamab, mosunetuzumab) and CAR-T therapies — among the most contested arenas in oncology. In multiple myeloma, the DARZALEX franchise faces an evolving competitive field and eventual biosimilar erosion, which is the entire basis of the patent-cliff thesis. The Merus and ProfoundBio deals are a deliberate move to diversify into solid tumors and reduce hematology and royalty concentration; whether that repositioning improves or merely defends the competitive position depends on petosemtamab and Rina-S clinical outcomes that are not yet resolved. The trajectory is "in transition" rather than clearly improving, supporting a 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is concentrated and partly binary, which is the primary reason the overall score is anchored below 6.

1. **DARZALEX patent cliff.** The royalty stream funding everything faces eventual biosimilar erosion. This is the dominant long-term risk and the axis of the entire bull/bear debate.
2. **Partner concentration and limited control.** The bulk of revenue is partner-controlled royalties, dominated by J&J. Genmab does not control pricing, manufacturing, or label strategy and already lost royalty economics in the 2022 J&J arbitration.
3. **Capital deployment and leverage.** Back-to-back acquisitions converted a net-cash balance sheet into a leveraged one. Returns depend on petosemtamab and Rina-S delivering; integration execution risk is now live.
4. **Pipeline binary risk.** The stock fell after the April 2026 EPKINLY DLBCL readout, and upcoming readouts (EPCORE FL-1 expansion, petosemtamab head-and-neck, Rina-S) are material in both directions.
5. **Shared EPKINLY economics.** Rising cost of product sales reflects the AbbVie profit-share; EPKINLY is less profitable to Genmab than headline sales imply.
6. **FX/ADR structure.** USD-reporting Danish company; ADR holders bear translation and ADR-ratio effects.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 49.1% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 34.2% — the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty (~14.9pp premium), consistent with binary pipeline-catalyst risk rather than complacency. The term structure is in contango (49.1% near-term vs 58.0% at six months), with the six-month tenor showing a -7.4% put skew and a put/call volume ratio of 13.2, signaling demand for downside protection on the catalyst-heavy horizon. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±7.7%. Near-term put/call OI is only 0.26 (call-heavy on open interest) even as recent put/call volume spiked to 7.08 — a mix that reads as fresh hedging into upcoming events rather than entrenched bearish positioning. The market expects a large move and is paying up for longer-dated protection, but is not uniformly positioned for a decline.

Short interest is low at 1.7% of float (5.4 days to cover), so this is not a crowded bearish trade; informed sellers are not heavily present, which cuts both ways.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Royalty revenue compounds at 20%+ on the back of DARZALEX and Kesimpta, providing high-margin cash flow that funds the pipeline without dilution. EPKINLY expands into earlier lines (the 79% progression/death-risk reduction in follicular lymphoma and the FDA hospitalization-label change support adoption), and at least one of petosemtamab or Rina-S delivers a solid-tumor franchise that begins to replace DARZALEX economics. In that scenario the ADR re-rates toward the $36–$41.50 analyst band as the market gains confidence the cliff will be bridged. Bull target ~$38–$40 (~59–67% upside).

**Bear case.** The April DLBCL disappointment proves a leading indicator; pipeline readouts underwhelm; integration charges keep GAAP profit flat-to-down even as revenue grows; and the market increasingly discounts the DARZALEX cliff without a credible offset. Leverage that funded the Merus deal becomes a liability if pipeline value does not materialize. The ADR retests the 52-week low of $20.23 and could trade lower toward $18–$19 (~20–25% downside).

**Base case.** Royalties continue to grow, the pipeline produces incremental but not transformative wins, near-term estimates stay soft as charges persist, and the stock works back toward the low end of the analyst range over 12 months as the discount to targets partially closes. Base target ~$28–$30 (~17–26% upside).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are quarterly results (next print expected roughly August 2026) and any interim pipeline readouts for petosemtamab, Rina-S, and EPCORE FL-1 expansion.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Positive pivotal data from petosemtamab (head and neck) or Rina-S that establishes a credible solid-tumor revenue offset to the DARZALEX cliff.
- Reversal of the 30-day negative estimate trend — current-year and next-year EPS revisions turning positive over a 30-day window alongside reaffirmed or raised FY2026 guidance.
- Evidence that GAAP operating profit re-accelerates as Merus integration charges roll off, narrowing the gap to adjusted profit.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second negative late-stage readout following the April DLBCL miss, undermining the pipeline-replacement thesis.
- Concrete DARZALEX biosimilar timing or adverse J&J royalty development that pulls forward the cliff.
- Material guidance cut or evidence the leverage taken on for Merus is constraining R&D or commercial investment.

## Conclusion

Genmab is a genuinely profitable, growing biotech with a high-quality royalty base and a credible platform, but it has deliberately converted a clean balance sheet into a leveraged, execution-dependent pipeline bet at the same moment its core royalty stream faces an eventual cliff. The ~33% drawdown from the January high reflects real developments — falling near-term estimates, a Q1 GAAP profit decline, post-acquisition leverage, and a disappointing April DLBCL readout — not merely volatility. The 90-day estimate strength that might have signaled an inflection has reversed over the most recent 30 days, removing the cleanest bull setup, while the analyst target band is constructive but partly stale and conditioned on pipeline outcomes that remain unresolved.

The recommendation is **HOLD (uncertain)**. The downside is supported by a compounding royalty base and the upside is real if petosemtamab or Rina-S delivers, but the path runs through binary clinical events with negative near-term revision momentum and elevated implied volatility pricing exactly that uncertainty. There is not enough confirmed evidence to justify a directional call in either direction until the next data points clarify whether the pipeline can bridge the DARZALEX cliff. Existing holders with a multi-year horizon and tolerance for binary readouts can hold; new capital is better deployed after a confirming pipeline catalyst or a stabilization in near-term estimates.
