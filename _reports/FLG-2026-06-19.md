**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Flagstar Bank, N.A. is the rebuilt institution that emerged from the near-collapse of New York Community Bancorp in early 2024. The bank has now posted two consecutive profitable quarters (Q4 2025 net income recovering and Q1 2026 at +$21M after eight straight loss quarters), restored its deposit ratings to investment grade at both Fitch and Moody's, and built a top-quartile capital position (CET1 13.24% at Q1 2026). The turnaround is measurable: net loss narrowed from -$1,118M in 2024 to -$177M in 2025, the provision for credit losses fell from $1,092M to $184M and then to $0 in Q1 2026, and net interest margin recovered from a 1.74% trough in Q1 2025 to 2.15% in Q1 2026.

The investment tension is the gap between distressed screening optics and improving fundamentals. The stock at $14.65 trades roughly at tangible book value (~$15.70/share including warrant dilution) and at approximately 8x management's FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance of $1.80-1.90. If management delivers the guided NIM expansion (2.30-2.40% in 2026, 2.70-2.80% in 2027) and the ~11% ROATCE target for 2027, a bank trading below tangible book would warrant a meaningful re-rating. That is the bull case in compact form.

We rate FLG HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis rather than BUY. The path to re-rating exists and is supported by specific operational catalysts, but three factors restrain conviction: near-term estimates are falling (current-year EPS cut 12.5% over 90 days, current quarter down 32.8%), the rent-regulated NYC multi-family book (47.7% of loans) faces an interest-only amortization wall through end-2026 with elevated non-accruals, and the $0 provision plus declining reserve coverage (ALLL/loans down to 1.67%) means recent earnings are partly flattered by credit normalization that bears can credibly question.

## Company Overview

Flagstar Bank, N.A. is a national bank headquartered in Hicksville, NY, with $87.5B in assets, $61.0B in loans, $66.0B in deposits, and $8.1B in equity at year-end 2025. It operates roughly 340 branches across nine states, concentrated in the NY/NJ metro area and the upper Midwest, with additional presence in Florida and the West Coast, plus around 20 private-bank branches. The bank reports as a single segment.

The franchise was assembled through serial acquisitions: the Flagstar Bancorp merger in December 2022 and the FDIC-assisted acquisition of substantial portions of the failed Signature Bank in March 2023. Its legacy specialty, and the source of its 2024 crisis, is rent-regulated NYC multi-family lending.

The most consequential structural change occurred October 17, 2025, when the company eliminated its holding company. Flagstar Financial, Inc. merged into the Bank, which is now the top-level public entity. The practical consequences are material: the bank is no longer subject to Federal Reserve examination or bank-holding-company Enhanced Prudential Standards, its primary regulator is now the OCC, and its SEC filings are now voluntary. The voluntary-filing status is flagged in the risk factors as a potential transparency degradation, since investors could in principle lose EDGAR access if the bank stops filing.

## Financial Analysis

The trajectory across the income statement shows a bank that has turned the corner on credit and is rebuilding earnings power from a depressed base.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net income | -$79M | -$1,118M | -$177M | +$21M |
| Diluted EPS (to common) | — | -$3.49 | -$0.50 | +$0.03 |
| Provision for credit losses | $833M | $1,092M | $184M | $0 |
| Net interest income | $3,077M | $2,152M | $1,721M | $443M |
| Net interest margin | 2.99% | 1.95% | 1.89% | 2.15% |
| Net charge-offs | — | $892M (1.16%) | $351M (0.55%) | 0.52% |

The mechanism behind the recovery is straightforward. The 2024 crisis drove a $1.09B provision as multi-family and CRE credit deteriorated; as that book stabilized, the provision collapsed to $184M in 2025 and $0 in Q1 2026. Net charge-offs fell from 1.16% to 0.55% to an annualized 0.52% (which management notes would be 0.29% excluding one resolved relationship). Net interest income compressed sharply through the crisis as the bank shed assets and funding costs rose, but NIM bottomed in Q1 2025 and has expanded for several quarters since.

Capital is now a genuine strength. CET1 rose from 12.83% at year-end 2025 to 13.24% in Q1 2026, the leverage ratio is 9.22%, and the bank is well-capitalized on all regulatory measures. This was made possible by the $1.05B capital raise of March 2024 led by Steven Mnuchin's Liberty Strategic Capital, which recapitalized the bank at the depth of the crisis.

The balance sheet has been deliberately shrunk and de-risked. Total assets fell from $114B (2023) to $100B (2024) to $87.5B (2025) as management ran off multi-family (-$5.1B in 2025) and CRE (-$2.5B) to reduce concentration, while growing C&I loans ($5.8B of originations in 2025, with C&I now 25% of the book; a further $1.4B or +9% in Q1 2026, the third consecutive quarter of net C&I growth). Funding has been cleaned up: brokered deposits cut from $10.2B to $2.4B, FHLB advances reduced with $2.5B prepaid in December 2025, and insured deposits now approximately 80% of total versus a roughly 56% peer average, a meaningful de-risking from the 2024 deposit-flight scare.

The earnings quality caveat is real. The $0 provision in Q1 2026 and the decline in ALLL/loans from 1.79% to 1.67% mean the bank is releasing reserve cushion into earnings as credit improves. This is normal in a recovery, but it is not a repeatable earnings source, and if reserves prove inadequate against the multi-family amortization wall, the provision line will reverse course.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is an earnings-recovery story rather than a revenue-expansion story. The bank is deliberately shrinking its balance sheet while shifting mix toward higher-yielding C&I lending. The growth lever is margin, not volume.

Management's guidance frames the recovery: FY2026 net income of $275-325M and adjusted diluted EPS of $0.60-0.65, then FY2027 net income of $850-950M, adjusted EPS of $1.80-1.90, ROATCE of 10.75-11.25%, and ROAA approaching 1%. The NIM ramp from 2.15% currently to 2.30-2.40% (2026) and 2.70-2.80% (2027) is the central driver, achieved as the low-yielding legacy multi-family book runs off and higher-yielding C&I balances grow, alongside continued funding-cost reductions.

The earnings surprise record is constructive but small-sample: two beats, one in-line, one miss across the last four quarters, with the most recent (Q1 2026) a 22.3% beat on a $0.03 estimate. The estimate revision trend is the most important near-term signal, and it is negative. Current-quarter consensus has been cut 32.8% over 90 days, current-year EPS cut 12.5%, next-year cut 5.6%. Estimates are broadly falling even as the bank posts profitable quarters, which indicates the Street is marking down the pace of the recovery rather than abandoning it. This negative revision momentum is the single biggest reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY: the strongest forward indicator in our framework is moving against the position.

The C&I pivot has genuine durability. The bank is hiring senior bankers across Specialized Industries (power/renewables, sponsor finance, healthcare, funds finance) and corporate/regional commercial banking, and the CRE concentration ratio has fallen from 501% (2023) to 367% (Q1 2026), reducing the regulatory-scrutiny flag attached to the 300% threshold. The risk is execution: three quarters of C&I growth is encouraging but not yet proof that a thrift-style monoline can become a diversified relationship bank at the margins management is guiding to.

## Valuation Assessment

FLG trades at $14.65 against tangible book value of approximately $15.70/share (with warrant dilution) at Q1 2026, so the stock sits slightly below tangible book. On management's FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance, the stock trades at roughly 8x guided 2027 earnings. The trailing P/E is meaningless here (negative trailing EPS of -$0.50 reflects a loss year), and the forward multiples on near-term consensus are distorted by the recovery's low base.

Against the regional-bank peer set, the comparison is instructive. Peers trade at a median P/E of 13.7x with a median dividend yield of 3.0%: HWC 14.7x, UBSI 12.6x, ABCB 13.7x, AUB 16.8x, HOMB 11.5x. These are stable, profitably-operating banks with normal NIMs near 3%+. FLG's near-tangible-book valuation reflects the market's discount for a bank that is still mid-turnaround, carries a concentrated multi-family book under credit pressure, and yields essentially nothing (the common dividend was slashed to $0.01/quarter from $0.17 pre-crisis).

The asymmetry case: if FLG delivers FY2027 guidance of ~$1.85 EPS and ~11% ROATCE, a bank earning a low-double-digit return on tangible equity should trade at or above 1.0x tangible book, and likely 1.1-1.3x as the franchise normalizes. That implies a stock in the high-$17s to low-$20s on a successful recovery, versus the current $14.65. The discount to tangible book is the source of upside, and it is a specific, identifiable reason the market may be under-pricing the recovery, not a generic "cheap" screen.

The discipline required: a low multiple on guidance the company may not hit is a value trap, not a bargain. The falling estimates argue the market is correctly skeptical of the 2027 ramp. We assign a moderately favorable valuation score because the sub-tangible-book entry provides a margin of safety on a recapitalized bank, but we explicitly decline to treat analyst targets (median $16.00, high $17.00) as a floor here: at near-tangible-book on a turnaround with falling estimates, those targets reflect appropriate caution rather than lagging optimism.

## Competitive Landscape

FLG is repositioning from a thrift-style monoline multi-family/CRE lender into a diversified relationship C&I bank, benchmarked against mid-cap regionals including Citizens, Fifth Third, Huntington, KeyCorp, M&T, Regions, Webster, Western Alliance, Zions, and Valley National. Its NIM remains well below peers, a direct function of the low-yielding legacy multi-family book; the gap should narrow as multi-family runs off and C&I grows, but the bank starts from a structural disadvantage.

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than stable: the CRE concentration ratio dropped to 367%, insured deposits rose to ~80%, deposit ratings returned to investment grade (restoring custodial-deposit eligibility the bank had lost), and the bank is actively poaching senior commercial bankers from larger institutions. The constraint is that FLG is competing for C&I relationships against well-capitalized incumbents with deeper product sets and longer client tenure, and it is doing so while still working through a problem loan book. The competitive position is improving but remains below peer norms in both margin and diversification.

Regulatory tailwinds cited in the 10-K could help: a proposed OCC rule (December 2025) to raise the Heightened Standards asset threshold to $700B would, if finalized, remove FLG from Heightened Standards entirely, while the proposed Basel III Endgame would add 60-80 bps to CET1.

## Risk Assessment

**Rent-regulated NYC multi-family concentration is the core risk.** Multi-family is 47.7% of loans ($29B); 55% is NY-secured and 88% of that is rent-regulated to some degree. The 2019 NY Housing Stability & Tenant Protection Act severely caps rent increases, pressuring borrower debt-service coverage as loans reprice higher. Non-accrual multi-family rose 29% to $2.26B in 2025 (partly a single borrower in bankruptcy). The NYC ≥50% rent-regulated criticized/classified cohort carries 1.01x amortizing DSCR and 77% LTV, barely covering debt service. ALLL on this sub-segment is 3.20-3.37%, among the highest versus peers.

**The interest-only amortization wall is the concrete near-term stress point.** Roughly 51% of $6.5B in interest-only multi-family loans begin amortizing by end-2026, raising borrower cash-flow burdens precisely on the weakest cohort. This is the most monitorable risk and should feature in every upcoming quarterly review.

**Credit quality is still elevated though improving.** Non-accruals were $2.98B (4.90% of loans) at year-end 2025, falling 11% to $2.68B in Q1 2026. Reserve releases are flattering earnings; this reverses quickly if office values weaken (CRE office is $2.0B with ALLL cut from 4.90% to 3.04% in Q1 2026, which could prove optimistic).

**Execution and profitability risk.** Retained earnings are -$988M. The 2026-2027 guidance requires flawless execution of the C&I pivot, continued funding-cost reductions, and benign credit simultaneously.

**Governance and oversight degradation (new in FY2025).** Post-reorganization there are no Fed exams and SEC filing is voluntary. Combined with heavy executive compensation during loss years and Liberty Strategic Capital's board influence, the structure is increasingly oriented around the rescue investors, which minority common holders should weigh.

**Litigation overhang.** Stockholder class and derivative actions tied to the 2024 disclosure events remain active, though the ICFR material weaknesses first disclosed in 2023 were remediated in 2025.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent on FLG, which neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental thesis but does suggest little near-term informed positioning for a sharp move. Near-term ATM IV is 28.0% versus 31.4% trailing historical volatility, so implied vol is cheap (spread -3.4%) and sits at the 4th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (0.8%), the put/call OI ratio has collapsed to 0.08 (from 0.43 a month ago), and the term structure is in contango. Short interest is negligible at 0.0% of float (19,030 shares). The combination indicates the market is pricing a calm, low-uncertainty path, neither a setup for a squeeze nor evidence of bearish positioning ahead of a catalyst. The cheap IV makes options a relatively inexpensive way to express a directional view into the next earnings print, but the signal itself is muted.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($18-21)

FLG delivers its FY2027 guidance: ~$1.85 adjusted EPS, ~11% ROATCE, NIM at 2.70-2.80%. Multi-family runs off without a credit event, the amortization wall passes with manageable losses, and C&I growth continues to lift the asset yield. A bank earning low-double-digit ROATCE re-rates from below tangible book toward 1.1-1.3x, putting the stock in the high-$17s to low-$20s. Regulatory relief (OCC Heightened Standards threshold) removes a cost and scrutiny layer. The restored investment-grade ratings lower funding costs and reopen institutional deposit channels.

### Bear Case ($10-11)

The multi-family amortization wall triggers a renewed wave of non-accruals as borrowers at 1.01x DSCR cannot absorb amortizing payments. The $0 provision proves premature; reserves rebuild and earnings turn negative again, deferring the 2027 ramp. Falling estimates prove to be the leading edge of a guidance cut. The stock retests its 52-week low of $10.38 and trades back toward a distressed multiple of tangible book. NYC office weakness compounds the multi-family problem.

The base case sits between these: a recovery that proceeds but more slowly than guided, with the stock range-bound near tangible book until the 2027 earnings power becomes visible in reported results rather than guidance.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-to-18-month growth-hold thesis tied to the earnings-recovery trajectory, with the key catalysts being quarterly NIM prints, C&I growth continuity, and clean passage of the interest-only amortization wall through end-2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions stabilize and turn positive — specifically, current-year consensus stops falling and the FY2027 EPS estimate holds at or above $1.70, confirming the Street believes the ramp.
- NIM prints at or above the 2026 guidance midpoint (2.35%) for two consecutive quarters with continued net C&I growth.
- The interest-only amortization wall passes through 2026 with non-accruals continuing to decline and no reserve rebuild.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A return to a net quarterly loss, or a provision rebuild that signals the $0 provision was premature (multi-family non-accruals re-accelerating above the year-end 2025 level of $2.26B).
- A guidance cut to FY2027 net income below $700M, which would break the re-rating math.
- CET1 falling below 11% or any indication the warrant dilution or further capital actions materially increase the share count.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the bank reports roughly quarterly; Q1 2026 was reported April 24). The amortization-wall progression through the second half of 2026 is the decisive monitorable.

## Conclusion

FLG is a credible mid-execution turnaround that has converted catastrophic 2024 losses into two consecutive profitable quarters, restored investment-grade ratings, built top-quartile capital, and begun a genuine mix shift from rent-regulated multi-family toward C&I. The stock trades near tangible book and at roughly 8x management's FY2027 guided EPS, which sets up a real re-rating if guidance is delivered. That is a specific, falsifiable 12-month growth thesis, not a generic value screen.

We rate FLG HOLD rather than BUY for three reasons that the bull case must overcome: near-term estimates are falling, the rent-regulated multi-family concentration faces a concrete interest-only amortization wall through end-2026 with non-accruals still elevated, and recent earnings are partly flattered by reserve release that is not repeatable. As a regional bank in a sector where selection has historically added little excess return, the burden of proof for a BUY is higher than the current evidence supports. The recovery is real and the entry near tangible book provides a margin of safety, but the path requires clean execution against a credit overhang that is improving rather than resolved. We would upgrade on stabilizing estimates and a clean amortization-wall passage; we would downgrade on any provision rebuild or guidance cut.
