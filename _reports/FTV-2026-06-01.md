**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fortive completed the tax-free spin-off of its Precision Technologies segment as Ralliant Corporation on June 28, 2025, leaving a simpler two-segment company built around Intelligent Operating Solutions (69% of sales, 25.8% operating margin) and Advanced Healthcare Solutions (31% of sales, 10.6% margin). The headline revenue drop from $6.23B (2024) to $4.16B (2025) is an accounting artifact of the spin, not organic deterioration. On continuing operations, the company grew GAAP revenue modestly and generated $978M of free cash flow on $4.16B of revenue, a ~23.5% FCF margin that places it among the higher-quality industrials by cash conversion.

The investment debate is whether a smaller, faster-growing, more software-weighted Fortive plus a fresh management team can close a persistent valuation and total-return gap versus peers. The cumulative TSR disclosed in the proxy (105.69 vs. peer group 189.72) quantifies a multi-year underperformance that is the entire reason the spin and the aggressive buyback exist. Q1 CY2026 is the first concrete evidence the thesis may be working: sales rose 7.7% YoY to $1.07B, core revenue grew roughly 5%, adjusted EBITDA rose 13%, adjusted EPS rose 25%+, and the company beat consensus by 8.0% while reaffirming FY26 adjusted EPS guidance of $2.90–$3.00 and trending toward the upper half. Management cited data center and AI-related demand for Fluke power and electrical test instrumentation as a growth driver.

At $58.86 the stock trades at roughly 19.6x the FY26 adjusted EPS midpoint (~$3.00) and ~18.3x the FY27 consensus ($3.22), in line with where a low-single-to-mid-single-digit organic grower with strong cash conversion should trade. That is neither cheap enough to underwrite asymmetric upside nor expensive enough to short. The combination of a one-year-only reacceleration, a near-total C-suite turnover during the most important transformation in the company's history, and a fair multiple supports a HOLD with a bullish lean. The path to a BUY runs through one or two more quarters confirming that core growth has durably stepped up.

## Company Overview

Fortive is a diversified industrial technology company headquartered in Everett, Washington, run on the Fortive Business System (FBS), a Danaher-derived continuous-improvement operating model. Fortive itself was spun from Danaher in 2016, and the June 2025 Ralliant separation continues the deconglomeration of that lineage.

Post-spin, the company operates two segments (FY2025, continuing operations):

- **Intelligent Operating Solutions (IOS)** — $2,856M sales (69%), 25.8% operating margin. Professional instrumentation, facility and asset lifecycle software, and connected-worker safety. Brands include Fluke, ServiceChannel, Gordian, Accruent, Industrial Scientific, and Intelex. This is the growth and margin engine.
- **Advanced Healthcare Solutions (AHS)** — $1,303M sales (31%), 10.6% operating margin. Sterilization (ASP), instrument tracking, biomedical test, radiation dosimetry, and clinical software. Brands include ASP, Censis, Fluke Biomedical, Landauer, and Provation. Lower margin and heavily regulated under FDA medical-device frameworks.

The portfolio is asset-light, brand-driven, and increasingly software and recurring-revenue oriented. Roughly 44% of sales are international. The competitive advantages are leading positions in niche end markets, FBS operating discipline, durable brands, and a growing software attach rate that raises switching costs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and growth (continuing ops).** Core (organic) revenue grew +1.7% in 2025, decelerating from +4.3% in 2024. The composition matters: pricing contributed +2.2% while volume declined -0.6%. IOS grew core +2.1%; AHS only +0.8%, reflecting sterilization and biomedical-test softness tied to healthcare-policy changes. Q1 CY2026 delivered core revenue growth of roughly 5%, a step up from the 2025 organic trough. Whether this is durable or partly a favorable post-spin comparison and mix effect is the central open question.

**Margins.** Consolidated operating margin was 17.3% in 2025 versus 17.6% in 2024. The 30bp decline was driven by transition items: roughly 100bp of incremental separation-related stock comp and ~55bp of restructuring, against +105bp of underlying pricing and FBS productivity, with tariffs fully offset by countermeasures. Gross margin is strong at 63.5%. Underlying operating performance is healthier than the GAAP margin line suggests.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow from continuing ops was $1.036B in 2025. Capex is light at ~$105M (2.5% of sales), confirming the asset-light model. On the current $17.9B market cap, the FCF represents a ~5.5% FCF yield, which provides reasonable valuation support without being a screaming bargain.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets were $11.7B, of which goodwill ($7.3B) plus intangibles ($2.2B) equal $9.5B, or 81% of assets, a legacy of the acquisitive roll-up. Long-term debt was $2.3B and total debt ~$3.2B, with D/E around 1.82. Net debt is modest against $1B+ annual FCF. The widely-cited Altman Z-Score of 1.59 ("distress zone") is a false signal here: the model penalizes negative working capital (current ratio 0.71, normal for a deferred-revenue-heavy software/subscription mix) and a heavy intangible base, neither of which reflects solvency risk for a company with investment-grade access to a revolver and commercial paper. The Piotroski reading (positive net income, ROA, OCF, and OCF above net income) is the more accurate signal.

**Capital allocation.** Fortive repurchased 30M shares for ~$1.61B in 2025 (versus $890M in 2024), partly funded by the $1.15B cash dividend received from Ralliant at the spin. It used the Ralliant proceeds to repay ~$715M of euro/yen-denominated debt. The dividend remains small (~$92M, low payout, ~0.4% yield). Basic share count fell from 349M to 332M, a ~5% reduction. The March and May 2026 debt agreements (~$1.1B in May) are liability-management refinancings of existing debt, not new leverage for M&A.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one miss over the last four reported quarters, with the most recent two beats at 6.5% and 8.0%, plus a 17.9% beat in Q3 2025. The single miss (-3.2% in Q2 2025) coincided with the spin disruption. The pattern supports moderate confidence in consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two pillars. First, post-spin Fortive is structurally higher-margin and more software-weighted than the prior conglomerate, which should support both faster organic growth and higher-quality recurring revenue. Second, an emerging data center and AI-buildout tailwind benefits Fluke power and electrical test instrumentation through electrification and capacity expansion, a demand vector management called out explicitly on the Q1 print.

The evidence is encouraging but thin. The 2025 full year showed only +1.7% core growth, with volume actually negative. The reacceleration to ~5% core growth and 25%+ adjusted EPS growth in Q1 CY2026 is one quarter, sourced partly from press summaries rather than a retrievable transcript. Estimate revisions corroborate a constructive but undramatic picture: current-year EPS revised +1.0% over 90 days to $2.99, next-year +1.2% to $3.22, with the current quarter +2.3%. These are positive but modest revisions, well short of the velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection. The next-quarter estimate slipped -1.4% over 90 days, a mild caution flag.

AHS remains a drag. Sterilization and biomedical-test volumes declined in 2025 on healthcare-policy changes, and the filing flags OBBBA-driven reductions in U.S. government spending as a 2026 headwind, with ~$417M of 2025 sales exposed to the federal government. Until AHS stabilizes, blended organic growth will lean heavily on IOS and the data center vector.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing GAAP P/E of ~34x overstates the multiple because GAAP EPS ($1.59) is depressed by separation costs and amortization; the forward adjusted basis is the right anchor.

Against peers in industrial machinery, the picture is mixed. The peer median P/E is 30.7x (Nordson 30.7x, IDEX 31.2x, Stanley Black & Decker 32.6x), but those figures are not consistently adjusted, and Snap-on (19.2x) and Pentair (17.8x) trade well below. On a forward-adjusted basis, FTV's ~19.6x sits below the higher-quality compounders (NDSN, IEX) and roughly in line with the cheaper, more cyclical names. The differential is defensible: Fortive's organic growth does not yet justify the 30x+ multiples that IDEX and Nordson command, and the persistent peer-TSR underperformance argues against assuming a premium re-rating before the execution is proven.

The ~5.5% FCF yield provides a floor. To re-rate materially higher, the market needs sustained evidence that core growth has stepped up to mid-single digits with margin expansion, at which point a multiple toward 22-23x forward becomes defensible. Today the stock is roughly fairly valued: the price embeds the early reacceleration but not a durable inflection. This is a 5.0 valuation score: not a value trap, but no identifiable asymmetric mispricing at the current multiple.

The analyst consensus (mean $63.56, median $64.00, high $72, low $51) implies ~8% upside to the midpoint. Given the modest estimate revisions and a sub-30x multiple, these targets are credible anchors rather than stale floors; the magnitude of revision velocity here does not support treating consensus as systematically lagging.

## Competitive Landscape

Fortive competes across fragmented niche markets rather than as a single-industry pure-play, against a mix of large diversified industrials, specialized regional players, and low-cost-manufacturing entrants. Its moat rests on entrenched brands (Fluke is iconic in electrical and electronic test), high software switching costs (Accruent, ServiceChannel, Provation, Censis), and FBS operating discipline. The growing software and recurring-revenue mix is the most durable element of the competitive position and supports the 63.5% gross margin.

The trajectory is improving at the margin: the simplified portfolio concentrates on the higher-growth, higher-margin IOS franchise, and the data center / AI buildout is an emerging structural tailwind for the test-and-measurement business that the market has only partially priced. Offsetting this, AHS faces genuine policy-driven demand headwinds, and the overall portfolio delivered only low-single-digit organic growth in 2025 before the early-2026 reacceleration. The competitive position is stable-to-improving rather than clearly winning share, which supports a 6.5 score.

## Risk Assessment

**Leadership transition (the dominant idiosyncratic risk).** Fortive transitioned its CEO, CFO, and Chief People Officer roles in 2025/early 2026. CEO Olumide Soroye (previously head of IOS) and CFO Mark Okerstrom (former Expedia CEO) are both new in role. A near-total C-suite turnover during the most consequential corporate transformation in the company's history is a real execution risk and the reason the management-quality score sits at 5.5 despite sensible incentive design.

**Spin-related indemnification and tax exposure.** Fortive retains potential indemnification liabilities to both Ralliant and Vontier under separation agreements, plus shared tax exposure. A taxable-transaction determination on any of the Danaher/Vontier/Ralliant spins would trigger significant U.S. federal income tax liabilities.

**Tariff / IEEPA volatility.** The filing repeatedly flags the Supreme Court IEEPA ruling invalidating certain tariffs, creating uncertainty over refunds, re-imposition, and counter-tariffs. Management offset 2025 tariff costs via pricing and FBS, but this is a live, fluid 2026 swing factor.

**Healthcare and government-spending demand.** AHS volume already declined in 2025 on healthcare-policy changes, and OBBBA-driven federal spending cuts are flagged as a 2026 headwind.

**Goodwill impairment cushion.** With $9.5B of goodwill plus intangibles, some reporting units carry thin fair-value-to-carrying multiples, disclosed as low as 1.5x (1.3x under a 10% haircut). A downturn could force impairments, though these would be non-cash.

**Open tax matters.** Examinations span 2011–2024, including pre-spin Danaher returns, with $159M of unrecognized tax benefits and added Pillar Two uncertainty.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 25.4%, essentially equal to one-year historical volatility of 25.5%, with IV in the 10th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in contango (25.4% near-term rising to 30.6% at six months), a normal, complacent shape with no near-term event premium priced. The put/call skew of 5.9% reflects modestly elevated put demand, and the put/call OI ratio of 1.38 leans hedged but is unremarkable. The put/call OI ratio collapsed from 6.68 to 1.38 since early May, which removed a prior extreme hedging signal. The options market is not flagging an imminent catalyst or stress, consistent with a stock between earnings reports. Short interest is moderate at 5.7% of float, 3.9 days to cover, neither a squeeze setup nor a confident-bear signal.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$72)

The post-spin simplification works as designed: a higher-margin, software-weighted Fortive sustains mid-single-digit core growth, AHS stabilizes, and the data center / AI tailwind for Fluke instrumentation compounds. New management executes, the ~5% annual share-count reduction continues, and the persistent peer-TSR gap begins to close. On a re-rated forward multiple, the stock reaches the high analyst target near $72, roughly +22% from current.

### Bear Case (~$48)

The Q1 reacceleration proves to be a post-spin comparison artifact and mix effect rather than a durable inflection; core growth reverts toward 1-2%. AHS deteriorates further on healthcare-policy and OBBBA spending cuts, tariff/IEEPA uncertainty pressures pricing, and the new C-suite stumbles on execution. A goodwill impairment in a thin-cushion reporting unit dents sentiment. The multiple compresses toward the cheaper peer cohort (~16x) on ~$3.00 EPS, returning the stock to roughly $48, near the 52-week low of $46.34, about -18% from current.

The base case sits between these: organic growth settles in the 3-4% range, EPS lands at the upper half of the $2.90–$3.00 FY26 guide and grows toward $3.22 in FY27, and the multiple holds near 19-20x forward, supporting a fair value around $62-64, modestly above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a bullish lean (growth_hold). The thesis is that the post-spin entity plus new management can sustain the early-2026 reacceleration, but one quarter of data and a fair multiple do not yet justify a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters confirming core revenue growth at or above 5% with stable-to-expanding adjusted operating margins (validating the inflection rather than a one-quarter comparison effect).
- Forward EPS estimate revisions accelerating beyond +5% over a 90-day window alongside AHS volume stabilization (sterilization/biomedical returning to growth).
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s that compresses the forward multiple toward ~17x while fundamentals hold, improving the risk/reward to clearly asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Core organic growth reverting below 2% on a reported quarter, confirming the reacceleration was transitory.
- A guidance cut to FY26 adjusted EPS below the $2.90–$3.00 range, or a material goodwill impairment.
- Evidence of new-management execution failure (unexpected further C-suite departures, FBS productivity reversal, or sustained margin compression beyond transition costs).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings report (expected late July 2026) is the natural checkpoint to test the durability of the Q1 reacceleration and AHS trajectory.

## Conclusion

Fortive is a high-quality, cash-generative industrial whose reported numbers have been obscured by the Ralliant spin and whose Z-Score "distress" flag is a mechanical false alarm. The post-spin company is structurally more attractive than its predecessor, and Q1 CY2026 delivered the first concrete evidence the simplification thesis is working. The data center / AI tailwind for Fluke is a genuine, partially-priced growth vector.

What holds the recommendation at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of a fair valuation against organic growth that has only just inflected, modest estimate revisions, and a near-total new C-suite tasked with closing a documented multi-year peer-TSR gap. The downside to the bear case (~-18%) is roughly comparable to the upside to the bull case (~+22%), and the base case offers only mid-single-digit upside. That symmetry, combined with execution risk that one quarter of data cannot resolve, argues for patience. **Recommendation: HOLD with a bullish lean.** Become a buyer on confirmation of durable mid-single-digit core growth or on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s.
