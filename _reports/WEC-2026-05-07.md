## Executive Summary

WEC Energy Group is a $37B regulated utility holding company operating primarily in Wisconsin with a transformational growth opportunity in AI/data center load. Microsoft's $20B+ commitment and Vantage's potential 3.5 GW in Port Washington create a pathway to 40-50% load growth in WEC's Wisconsin territory through 2030, differentiated from most utility peers growing rate base at 5-8% annually. The Wisconsin PSCW's April 2026 approval of the Very Large Customer tariff — requiring data centers to cover full generation and distribution costs at premium ROEs of 10.48-10.98% — removes the primary regulatory risk to this thesis.

However, at ~24x forward earnings ($115.22 / $4.81 trailing, or ~21.9x on adjusted $5.27 FY25 EPS), WEC already trades at a meaningful premium to regulated utility peers (typical range 17-20x). The $37.5B five-year capital plan (2026-2030) requires approximately $10-12B in equity issuance, implying 25-30% dilution to shares outstanding. This dilution mathematically constrains EPS growth to 6-7% even as rate base grows faster, which is precisely what consensus estimates reflect ($5.59 FY26E, $6.01 FY27E — 7-8% growth). With estimate revisions essentially flat over 90 days (+0.1% for both FY26 and FY27) and negative over 30 days, there is no positive earnings momentum to drive re-rating from current levels.

The stock is a quality compounder with above-average utility growth, a 3.35% dividend yield, and genuine structural tailwinds — but the current price requires data center execution to proceed on schedule with minimal construction delays, tariff impacts on solar panels, or regulatory setbacks. This is a growth HOLD: own it for the compounding, but the entry point offers insufficient asymmetry for a new position.

## Company Overview

WEC Energy Group serves approximately 4.75 million electric and natural gas customers across Wisconsin, Illinois, Michigan, and Minnesota through regulated subsidiaries including We Energies, Wisconsin Public Service, Peoples Gas Light & Coke, and others. The company owns 8,375 MW of generation capacity (31% coal, 33% gas, 8% renewables plus nuclear PPAs) and holds a ~60% equity interest in American Transmission Company (ATC).

The business generates ~$9.8B in annual revenue across six reportable segments, with Wisconsin contributing the majority of earnings ($1.05B net income in 2025). The non-utility energy infrastructure segment ($411M net income) operates 12 wind/solar facilities generating production tax credits. Illinois operations ($122M net income in 2025, down from $252M in 2024 due to a $205M settlement charge) face a more adversarial regulatory environment.

The transformational development is Microsoft's data center commitment in southern Wisconsin, with WEC expecting up to 2.6 GW of load growth through 2030 in the Milwaukee-to-Chicago corridor, plus Vantage Data Centers potentially adding 3.5 GW in Port Washington.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Quality:** Adjusted EPS has grown at a ~7% CAGR from $4.11 (2021) to approximately $5.27 (2025, excluding the Illinois QIP settlement charge of $0.46/share). Q1 2026 delivered $2.45 vs. $2.30 consensus (+6.7% beat), and full-year guidance is $5.51-$5.61. The Q4 2025 miss (-31.3% vs. consensus) was entirely attributable to the one-time Illinois settlement — underlying operations remain on track.

**Cash Flow Position:** Operating cash flow of $3.38B (2025) against capital expenditures of $4.40B produces negative free cash flow of -$1.02B. This deficit will widen: the 5-year capex plan averages $7.5B annually (2027-2028 at $7.0B each), far exceeding operating cash generation. The utility is structurally cash-flow-negative during its investment phase.

**Capital Structure:** Total debt of $21.9B (debt/total-cap 61.7%) is within regulated utility norms but leaves limited flexibility. Moody's changed WPS's outlook to negative in November 2025 citing growing leverage. The effective tax rate guidance of 5.5-6.5% (vs. 21% statutory) reflects heavy reliance on production tax credits — any legislative change to renewable credits would increase tax burden materially.

**Dilution Trajectory:** Shares outstanding grew from 315.4M (2022) to 323.8M (2025). Funding ~$37.5B in capex with ~$3.4B annual OCF implies ~$25-30B in external financing over 5 years. At 60% debt funding, this requires $10-12B in equity issuance — roughly 25-30% dilution from current share count. This is the critical constraint on EPS growth despite rapid rate-base expansion.

**Dividend:** $3.81/share annualized, yielding 3.35% at current price. Payout ratio ~72% on adjusted earnings, providing adequate coverage. Consecutive quarterly dividends since 1942.

## Growth Analysis

**Rate Base Growth:** The $37.5B capital plan (2026-2030) breaks down as: $12.6B in renewables, $5.4B thermal generation, $2.9B battery storage, $4.7B electric distribution, $7.1B gas distribution, $4.1B ATC transmission contributions. This represents a rate-base growth rate likely exceeding 10% annually.

**Data Center Load:** 4-6 GW of potential new load (40-50% above current peak demand) is extraordinary for a regulated utility. The VLC tariff at 10.48-10.98% ROE with 57% equity layer creates above-average earnings per dollar of investment. Microsoft, Vantage, and potentially a third data center customer (expected announcement by fall 2026) provide multi-year visibility.

**Growth vs. Dilution Tension:** Rate base growing at 10%+ but EPS growing at 6-7% reflects the dilution headwind. Consensus estimates ($5.59 FY26, $6.01 FY27) embed this — precisely what's achievable given financing requirements. The market correctly understands the math; there is no hidden upside.

**Estimate Revisions:** FY26 estimates essentially unchanged over 90 days (+0.1%). FY27 similarly flat (+0.1%). Near-term quarterly estimates revised downward (-4.5% to -4.9% over 30 days). This is not the estimate momentum profile of a stock poised for re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $115.22, WEC trades at:
- ~24x trailing EPS ($4.81, depressed by Illinois charge)
- ~21.9x adjusted FY25 EPS ($5.27)
- ~20.6x FY26 consensus ($5.59)
- ~19.2x FY27 consensus ($6.01)

Regulated utility peers (DTE, CMS, XEL) typically trade at 17-20x forward earnings. WEC's premium is justified by the data center growth differential, but at ~20.6x FY26E the premium is already substantial. The analyst consensus target of $125.50 (median) implies 8.9% upside — reasonable but not compelling given execution risks.

The dividend yield of 3.35% is adequate but not premium within the utility sector (peers range 3.0-4.5%). Total return potential of ~11-12% annually (3.3% yield + 7% EPS growth + modest multiple compression) is solid for a utility but doesn't meet the threshold for active BUY given the capital plan execution risks.

The specific valuation concern: at 20.6x FY26E, WEC is priced for successful execution. If data center timelines slip 12-18 months (construction delays, tariff impacts on solar panels, permitting issues from Wisconsin ozone nonattainment), the premium compresses toward peer levels (18x), implying downside to $100-101.

## Competitive Landscape

**Regulatory Quality:** Wisconsin's constructive regulatory framework (forward-looking test years, earnings sharing mechanisms, timely rate adjustments) provides high visibility on capital recovery. The VLC tariff approval confirms regulators' willingness to accommodate data center growth without cross-subsidizing existing ratepayers.

**Geographic Positioning:** The Milwaukee-to-Chicago corridor sits at the intersection of fiber infrastructure, available land, water resources, and proximity to major population centers — structural advantages for hyperscaler site selection that cannot be replicated.

**ATC Transmission:** The ~60% stake in American Transmission Company provides exposure to MISO's Long Range Transmission Plan (Tranches 1 and 2.1), adding ~$1.1B in WEC's share of investment through 2030 at a FERC-regulated 9.98% base ROE plus incentives.

**Competitive Risk:** The exclusive franchise model eliminates retail competition, but behind-the-meter solutions (on-site generation, microgrids) represent a long-term threat to load growth assumptions. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges that advances in technology could reduce data center electricity demand.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution Risk (PRIMARY):** The $37.5B capex program requires flawless execution across multiple dimensions simultaneously: solar panel procurement amid AD/CVD tariffs and UFLPA compliance, construction labor in a tight market, battery storage technology at scale, and coordination with data center customer construction timelines. Any 12-18 month delay compresses earnings growth below guidance.

**Financing/Dilution Risk:** Continuous equity issuance at prices below $115-120 would be dilutive to current shareholders beyond plan assumptions. A credit downgrade would increase borrowing costs across the $21.9B debt stack.

**Illinois Regulatory Risk:** The QIP settlement removed near-term overhang but the ICC's "Future of Gas" proceeding and Chicago's CABO ordinance create structural headwinds. Illinois contributes only ~8% of net income post-settlement but commands disproportionate management attention and capital.

**Data Center Concentration:** Dependence on a small number of customers (Microsoft, Vantage) in an emerging technology sector creates binary risk. Cancellation provisions require cost reimbursement but don't compensate for delayed earnings growth.

**Tax Credit Vulnerability:** The 5.5-6.5% effective tax rate relies on PTCs/ITCs that are being phased out under OBBBA. Battery storage credits are exempt, but wind/solar projects must begin construction by July 2026.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** With $21.9B in debt and continuous new issuance, WEC is directly exposed to the elevated rate environment. Higher long-term rates increase the cost of funding the capital plan and compress utility valuations broadly.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($130-140, 13-22% upside):** Data center load materializes on schedule, additional hyperscaler customers announced by fall 2026, rate base growth accelerates to 12%+ annually, equity issuance completed at premium prices minimizing dilution, and WEC achieves the high end of the 6.5-7% long-term EPS growth range. At 22x a $6.00+ FY27 EPS scenario, the stock reaches $132-140. Illinois settlement approval removes final regulatory overhang.

**Bear Case ($100-105, 9-13% downside):** Data center construction timelines slip 12-18 months due to solar tariffs or permitting delays, equity issuance at depressed prices creates a dilution spiral, Illinois regulatory environment deteriorates further, and long-term rates remain elevated compressing utility multiples sector-wide. At 17-18x FY26E ($5.50), the stock falls to $93-99. Dividend support provides a floor near $100.

**Base Case ($120-125, 4-8% upside):** Capital plan proceeds with modest delays, data center load grows as expected but on a slightly extended timeline, EPS reaches $5.55-5.60 in FY26 and $5.95-6.00 in FY27, and the stock maintains its current 20-21x forward multiple. Total return of ~7-11% annually (price appreciation + dividend).

4. Equity issuance at prices below $100/share (dilutive spiral risk)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August) will provide the first update on data center construction progress, capital plan spending trajectory, and financing costs.

## Conclusion

WEC Energy Group is a high-quality regulated utility with a genuinely differentiated growth catalyst in AI/data center demand. The Wisconsin regulatory construct, VLC tariff approval, and Microsoft/Vantage commitments provide multi-year visibility on rate-base growth that most peers cannot match. Management has delivered consistent 7% EPS growth and maintains credible guidance for continuation.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation at $115.22 is quantifiable: consensus EPS estimates have revised +0.1% over 90 days and are declining over 30 days for near-term quarters. The market has already incorporated the data center narrative into the ~20.6x forward multiple, and required equity issuance mathematically constrains EPS growth to 6-7% regardless of how fast rate base grows. At 20.6x forward earnings with flat estimate momentum, upside to consensus target ($125.50) is 8.9% while downside to bear case ($100-105) is 9-13%. For a new position, the entry point needs to be below $108 (19x forward) to create attractive risk/reward. For existing holders, the combination of 3.35% yield plus mid-single-digit EPS growth constitutes an adequate total return that justifies continued ownership.
