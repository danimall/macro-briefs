## Executive Summary

NVIDIA's operating results have not deteriorated in any respect. Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended April 26, 2026) revenue was $81,615M, up 85% year over year and 20% sequentially, with a 74.9% gross margin and a 65.6% operating margin. Data Center revenue reached $75,246M (+92%), with the Hyperscale sub-segment at $37,869M (+115%) and the AI Clouds/Industrial/Enterprise sub-segment at $37,377M (+74%). Consensus EPS for the current year has risen 6.9% over 90 days to $8.96 and next-year consensus 12.0% to $12.80. At $225.65 that is 25.2x FY27 and 17.6x FY28, a PEG of roughly 0.59 on the 42.9% implied FY27→FY28 earnings growth. Four consecutive quarterly beats (2.0% to 4.3%) support reasonable confidence in those estimates.

Two things changed materially since our last review. First, price: the stock is up roughly 10% from the early-August basis, compressing the distance to our base case. Second, and more consequentially, the 8-K filed the morning of August 17 (accession 0001045810-26-000069) converted previously reported but unfiled arrangements into a filed, quantified contingent obligation: residual value guaranties covering approximately 4.25 GW of IT load at the Portsmouth, Ohio site, with aggregate payment obligation "cumulatively capped at $105 billion," plus discretionary capacity to add credit support for approximately 3.8 GW more. The tenant is an OpenAI affiliate on a 20-year lease; NVIDIA is guarantor of the shortfall between a guaranteed minimum lease value and recovery from re-lease or sale; the trigger is OpenAI insolvency or payment failure; the stated mitigant is an OpenAI indemnity, which is a promise from the entity whose failure activates the obligation. The $105B cap equals 108.6% of FY26 free cash flow, 53.7% of April-2026 shareholders' equity, and 208.6% of cash plus marketable debt securities.

Set against that, the filed figure is materially smaller and better structured than the up-to-$250B backstop reported in July, and it is a shortfall guarantee rather than a full lease guarantee; loss requires both an OpenAI default and a collapse in the residual value of powered, grid-interconnected AI data-center capacity. We are downgrading from BUY to ACCUMULATE on entry arithmetic and risk composition, not on business deterioration: base case $270 (+19.7%), bull $330 (+46.2%), bear $160 (−29.1%), probability-weighted expected return roughly +14% over twelve months.

## Company Overview

NVIDIA describes itself in both the FY26 10-K and the Q1 FY27 10-Q as "a data center scale AI infrastructure company." That framing is reflected in the accounting: management attributes the FY26 gross-margin decline to the transition "from offering Hopper HGX systems to Blackwell full-scale datacenter solutions," a shift from selling boards to selling racks, fabrics and power-adjacent systems.

FY2026 (ended January 25, 2026) revenue was $215,938M, up 65%. Compute & Networking contributed $193,479M (+67%) and $130,141M of segment operating income; Graphics contributed $22,459M (+57%) and $9,156M. Compute & Networking is therefore 89.6% of revenue and 93.4% of segment operating profit. Within it, FY26 Data Center compute grew 59% while Data Center networking grew 142% — NVLink compute fabric for GB200/GB300, Spectrum-X Ethernet and InfiniBand. The interconnect is the fastest-growing line, which moves switching costs from the die to the cluster.

Effective Q1 FY27 the company recast disclosure into Data Center (Hyperscale and ACIE) and Edge Computing ($6,369M, +29%). Product cadence remains annual: Blackwell → Blackwell Ultra/GB300 → Rubin, expected to begin shipping in H2 FY2027.

The Q1 FY27 recovery in gross margin to 74.9% from 71.1% in FY26 is mostly the non-repeat of a charge rather than a reversal of the rack-scale mix shift. FY26 inventory and excess-purchase-obligation provisions totalled $7.2B with $1.5B of releases — a 2.6-point gross-margin drag, including the $4.5B H20 charge. Q1 FY27 provisions were $1.1B with $103M released, a 1.2-point drag versus 11.0 points in the year-ago quarter. Sequentially, gross margin was 74.9% versus 75.0%: essentially flat, which is the cleaner read. We cannot separate Blackwell yield improvement from mix effects with the disclosure provided.

**Earnings quality requires an explicit adjustment.** Q1 FY27 pretax income was $69,903M, of which other income (expense), net was $15,929M — attributed by the 10-Q primarily to $13.4B of unrealized gains on publicly-held equity securities and $2.6B on non-marketable securities. That is 22.8% of pretax income. Stripping all other income and applying the reported 16.6% effective rate on $53,536M of operating income gives roughly $44.6B of net income and about $1.83 of diluted EPS on 24,391M diluted shares, versus $2.39 reported. Roughly 23% of reported GAAP EPS was non-operating and largely unrealized. FY26 showed the same pattern in smaller size: total other income, net of $11,063M, or 7.8% of $141,450M pretax income.

These marks are GAAP-mandated under ASU 2016-01 and are not discretionary, and the non-marketable portion uses the observable-price-change measurement alternative. We infer nothing about management intent. The practical consequences are two. First, GAAP EPS growth now contains a pro-cyclical amplifier correlated with the same AI funding cycle driving revenue; the 10-Q discloses that a hypothetical 10% decline in publicly-held equities would reduce fair value by $3.9B, more than double the $1.8B sensitivity at January 25, 2026. Second, consensus EPS of $8.96/$12.80 is an operating-basis estimate; any GAAP "beat" driven by investment marks is not a beat on operating terms, and comparisons must be kept on matching bases.

**Cash flow.** Q1 FY27 operating cash flow was $50,344M and free cash flow $48,587M. This should not be annualized. Taxes payable rose from $2,669M to $10,638M because, per the filing, "We made no federal income tax payments in the first quarter of fiscal year 2027, whereas our second quarter of fiscal year 2027 is scheduled to include two payments." Accrued and other current liabilities rose $7,763M. Removing only the tax-timing benefit gives roughly $42.4B for the quarter. As a labeled sensitivity, a $42–50B quarterly range implies $170–200B of annual operating cash flow if revenue holds at or above Q1 levels — against a $5.47T market capitalization, a 3.1–3.5% cash-flow yield. FY27 is a 53-week year with a 14-week Q4, so quarterly cash flow will be lumpy for reasons unrelated to demand.

**Balance sheet (April 26, 2026).** Cash and equivalents $13,237M; marketable debt securities $37,098M; marketable equity securities $30,237M; non-marketable securities $43,364M; inventories $25,797M; receivables $40,710M; total assets $259,474M; total liabilities $64,000M; equity $195,474M. Debt is $8,470M across seven tranches with non-financial covenants only and full compliance; a $25.0B commercial paper program is undrawn. Altman Z of 6.64, current ratio 3.91.

One definitional point matters for liquidity. Management's own paragraph separates "$50.3 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable debt securities" from "$30.2 billion of marketable equity securities." Of the equity, $27.4B was subject to short-term lock-up at April 26 and $8.9B is locked through December 2027 and classified in Other assets. The price-insensitive liquid buffer is $50.3B, not the ~$80B headline. Aggregate equity investment exposure — $39.1B publicly-held, $42.3B non-marketable, ~$1.0B equity-method fund interests — is approximately $82.5B, or 31.8% of total assets. Non-marketable equity carries cumulative gross unrealized gains of $5.3B against $199M of cumulative losses and impairments; that portfolio grew from $3.4B to $42.3B in five quarters and has never been tested through a funding downturn.

**Working capital.** FY26 inventory grew 112% against 65% revenue growth, with days of inventory rising from 113 to 125. Q1 FY27 inventory grew 20.5% sequentially against 20% revenue growth — back in line, with raw materials nearly doubling to $6,647M, consistent with Rubin pre-build. DSO improved from ~51 days to ~45. The Piotroski-style flag that FY26 operating cash flow ($102,718M) was below net income ($120,067M) is mechanically explained by $9.0B of non-cash investment gains plus inventory (+$11.3B) and receivables (+$15.4B) builds; the same year produced $96,676M of free cash flow. It is a legitimate accrual-quality flag, not a distress signal.

**Capital allocation is the strongest management evidence.** FY26 buybacks were 282M shares for $40.4B. Q1 FY27 alone repurchased 108.3M shares for $20.2B at approximately $185 — about 18% below today's price. Authorization was $38.5B at April 26, plus $80.0B approved May 18, 2026, for roughly $118.5B available. The quarterly dividend was raised from $0.01 to $0.25 on May 18, roughly $6.06B annualized, or about 6% of FY26 free cash flow (0.44% current yield). Diluted shares declined from 24,804M (FY25) to 24,514M (FY26) to 24,391M (Q1 FY27). Unvested equity awards of 200M shares represent 0.83% of shares outstanding and 0.82% post-issuance ownership dilution — modest for a company of this size.

**Revenue visibility is thinner than headlines suggest.** Deferred revenue was $3,117M and remaining performance obligations from contracts longer than one year were $2.6B. The 10-K is explicit that "most of our sales are made on a purchase order basis, our customers can generally cancel, change, or delay product purchase commitments with little notice to us and without penalty." The $119B of manufacturing and supply commitments ($95B payable in the remainder of FY27) is NVIDIA's own forward *supply* bet, not customer-contracted demand.

Growth durability is supported by company-specific drivers: networking growth of 142%, the Rubin ramp in H2 FY27, and consensus estimates that continue to rise across every forward horizon. ## Competitive Landscape

The competitive threat NVIDIA now names is its own customers. The Q1 FY27 10-Q added: "Some of our customers are developing their own ASICs and other products, including designs optimized for certain workloads that may not require all of the features and functionality our data center systems provide." That is a sharper concession than the 10-K's generic reference to in-house expertise, and it lands precisely where concentration is highest — the parties with the volume and capital to justify custom silicon are the same three direct customers that are 54% of revenue. For stable, high-volume inference, a customer accelerator can be adequate without paying for NVIDIA's generality and fabric. [unverified — the specific "$6–11B per gigawatt versus roughly $19B for NVIDIA systems" figures do not appear in the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

NVIDIA's response is visible in capital allocation. The Groq non-exclusive IP license — subject of a dedicated new risk factor disclosing "significant, nonrefundable payments" and conceding NVIDIA "may be unable to recover the associated costs" — is an inference-specific technology acquisition, with $3,957M of accrued purchase consideration on the April balance sheet. Trade press reports the arrangement near $20B including an acquihire of most of Groq's development team; those figures are not confirmed in the filings we read. Related and unexplained: FY26 goodwill rose from $5,188M to $20,832M, roughly $15.6B, against only a $2.5B increase in net intangibles, with no business combination discussed in the MD&A. If part of the Groq arrangement met the definition of a business combination, goodwill would arise, and most of the value would have been attributed to workforce and expected synergies. This remains an open verification gap rather than an established fact, and the next 10-Q should close it.

Where the moat is widening is the fabric. Networking at +142% growth, sold as NVLink/InfiniBand/Spectrum-X inside rack-scale systems, raises the cost of substituting a single accelerator because the customer must replace a cluster architecture, not a die. The same logic explains the Ohio structure: both filings state that "the availability of data centers, energy, and capital to support the buildout of NVIDIA AI infrastructure by our customers and partners is crucial," and that expanding energy capacity is "a complex, multi-year process." NVIDIA is spending contingent balance-sheet capacity to remove non-silicon bottlenecks that cap its own revenue. Whether that is prudent vertical de-risking or risk transfer to shareholders is the live debate; sell-side opinion split cleanly, with BofA framing it as guaranteeing "asset quality, not the debt" and Mizuho arguing it "doesn't fundamentally answer the question of how much end-user demand and economic return sits underneath all of this spending."

Regulatory attention is broad. The 10-K discloses information requests from competition regulators in the EU, U.S., UK, China and South Korea covering supply allocation, foundation models, and "investments, partnerships and other agreements with companies developing foundation models." On September 15, 2025 Chinese antitrust regulators published a preliminary finding that NVIDIA's compliance with U.S. export controls violated conditions attached to the Mellanox approval — a proceeding aimed at the fastest-growing segment.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The $105B residual value guaranty.** The cap is on cumulative payments, and the obligation is a shortfall guarantee, so loss requires both OpenAI default and a collapse in the re-lease/resale value of a 4.25 GW powered campus with grid interconnection. Both conditions are meaningfully harder than default alone. Against that: the tenor runs 20 years from a 2028 ready-for-service date, far beyond any credible demand forecast; the indemnity is conditional on the defaulting party; and the precedent $3.5B facility guarantees are accounted for as credit derivatives with fair-value changes running through other income. If the new guaranties receive comparable treatment, mark-to-market movement on $105B of notional could inject material non-cash volatility into earnings once leases commence. The accounting treatment is not yet disclosed.

**2. Customer concentration is worsening.** Direct customers at ≥10% of revenue: 13% (FY24) → 34% across three (FY25) → 36% across two (FY26) → **54% across three (Q1 FY27: 21%, 17%, 16%)**. Receivables concentration is worse: 30%, 18% and 16% across three customers, or 64%, versus 56% in January. Management's emphasis that hyperscalers are "approximately 50% of Data Center revenue" with the balance from diversified AI clouds, sovereign and enterprise buyers describes end-market breadth; it does not offset billing and credit concentration, which moved the other way. Both readings should be carried, since rack-scale sales flow through a narrow set of ODMs and integrators.

**3. Single-entity linkage.** Both filings disclose that "one AI research and deployment company contributed to a meaningful amount of our revenue by purchasing cloud services from our customers." The same entity is a prospective equity investee ("finalizing an investment and partnership agreement with OpenAI") and now the credit behind the largest contingent obligation in company history.

**4. Investment-portfolio concentration.** $82.5B, or 31.8% of assets, in equities concentrated in the sector NVIDIA supplies, with a disclosed $3.9B fair-value hit from a 10% decline and an explicit new 10-K statement that "our investment portfolio contains industry sector concentration risks."

**5. Litigation.** *In Re NVIDIA Corporation Securities Litigation* had class certification granted March 25, 2026 for purchasers from August 10, 2017 to November 15, 2018; NVIDIA petitioned the Ninth Circuit April 8, 2026 and has accrued nothing, judging loss reasonably possible but not probable. Certification raises settlement expectancy, though the class period predates most of the current float.

**6. Tax and policy.** IRS examination for FY23–FY24 with unrecognized tax benefits of $4.5B (including $439M interest and penalties), up from $4.0B. USG officials have expressed an expectation of receiving "15% or more of the revenue" from licensed China sales with no codifying regulation published. RASA and the GAIN AI Act are new export-control vectors.

**7. Governance detail.** Five Item 5.02 filings in under eighteen months, a new Principal Accounting Officer (Scott Gawel, grants June 8, 2026) during a phased ERP upgrade, and an unusual change-of-control impediment: a Microsoft Xbox agreement granting Microsoft first and last rights of refusal if a third party offers to buy at least 30% of NVIDIA's shares.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 40.2% against 36.7% one-year realized is modestly rich (3.5pp), with a backwardated term structure (40.2% near vs 36.2% at six months) consistent with hedging the August 26 print rather than a directional regime shift. Implied moves are ±6.6% at one month and ±9.9% at three months; NVIDIA's last four beats were 2.0–4.3%, so the market is pricing a wider distribution than recent delivery. Open-interest put/call of 0.57 and volume ratio of 0.83 are call-tilted, offering no confirmation of a bearish thesis, and short interest is 1.3% of float at 2.2 days to cover — no crowding either way. The very large same-day put volume at $225/$228 against negligible open interest is expiry pinning mechanics and should be discounted. The most informative datapoint is the change since August 3: ATM IV collapsed from 67.4% to 40.2% while price rose 10.1%, and put/call open interest fell from 0.70 to 0.57. Uncertainty has been priced out into a print that now carries a newly filed $105B contingent obligation. That combination argues for staged entry rather than sizing up ahead of the event.

## SEC Trigger Review

**Accession 0001045810-26-000069 — Form 8-K filed 2026-08-17, Items 1.01, 2.03 and 7.01, primary document nvda-20260817.htm.** NVIDIA disclosed a multi-year partnership with SB Energy for the PORTS Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio, and entry into multiple residual value guaranties covering leases for approximately 4.25 GW of IT load, with "aggregate payment obligation… cumulatively capped at $105 billion for its initial commitment." An OpenAI affiliate is tenant and will deploy NVIDIA's DSX AI factory platform; NVIDIA "will pay an amount generally equal to any shortfall between the guaranteed minimum value of a lease… and amounts recovered through a replacement lease or sale"; obligations become effective generally at lease commencement with ready-for-service expected beginning in 2028; triggers are OpenAI insolvency or payment failure; OpenAI "has agreed to reimburse and indemnify NVIDIA for any and all amounts actually paid"; termination occurs at the 20th lease anniversary, lease termination by OpenAI, OpenAI achieving a satisfactory credit rating, or other customary events; NVIDIA "can provide credit support to secure approximately an additional 3.8 gigawatts at the Portsmouth Site, exercisable in its sole discretion." The form of the agreements will be filed with the 10-Q for the quarter ended July 26, 2026.

**Rating and thesis impact.** This is the proximate cause of our downgrade from BUY to ACCUMULATE. It converts an unfiled, reported arrangement into a filed, quantified off-balance-sheet exposure, concentrated on one non-rated counterparty, on a tenor extending two decades past any forecastable demand horizon. It also cuts partly the other way: the filed cap of $105B for 4.25 GW is materially smaller and more structured than the up-to-$250B backstop for 10 GW reported in late July (the WSJ report of which triggered a sector-wide selloff on July 27), and the structure secures land, power and shell capacity that both filings identify as the binding constraint on NVIDIA's own revenue. We reduced risk_factors to 4.5 and financial_health to 7.0, held management_quality at 6.0, and applied a multiple haircut in the base case (21.1x FY28). Position sizing should be staged until the July-quarter 10-Q discloses both the agreement forms and the accounting classification of the guaranties.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($330, +46.2%)

Consensus FY28 EPS of $12.80 proves conservative as Rubin ramps in H2 FY27 into demand that is supply-constrained by power and shells rather than by silicon — the constraint the Ohio structure addresses. The $500B third-party financing framework announced August 11 with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR (memoranda of understanding, non-binding, no corresponding 8-K, so not committed capital) moves the marginal financing of customer purchases off NVIDIA's balance sheet, and BofA's read — NVIDIA guarantees asset quality rather than debt — becomes the market's read. Networking compounds above 100% and raises cluster-level switching costs faster than customer ASIC programs can qualify. The $118.5B buyback authorization retires shares at multiples below 20x forward. [unverified — the specific hyperscaler capex confirmation figures (Amazon +69%, Azure +43% cc, Caterpillar's raise) do not appear in the sources.] The sell-side median target of $300 may prove a floor if the stock trades at 25x forward against ~43% expected earnings growth.

### Bear Case ($160, −29.1%)

The three customers at 54% of revenue qualify internal accelerators for steady-state inference, and the substitution shows up first as slower Hyperscale growth, then as multiple compression from 25x to 18x. Memory and component inflation squeezes gross margin below 70% while rack-scale content raises the cost basis. A frontier-lab funding stumble — the credit event the Ohio guaranty is written against — reprices the $82.5B equity portfolio (a 10% decline costs $3.9B of fair value and, through other income, a meaningful slice of GAAP EPS) at the same time as it raises the market-implied probability of guaranty draw. Chinese remedies from the Mellanox conditions proceeding hit the networking line specifically. [unverified — the CoreWeave $2.6bn loan repricing detail and the Fed/long-end characterization do not appear in the sources.] In this case the multiple, not the earnings, drives the drawdown.

## Conclusion

NVIDIA's operating business is performing at the top of its range: 85% revenue growth, a 65.6% operating margin, $48.6B of quarterly free cash flow before timing adjustments, share count shrinking on $20.2B of quarterly buybacks executed 18% below the current price, and consensus EPS rising 6.9% and 12.0% for the current and next fiscal years. At 25.2x FY27 and 17.6x FY28 consensus, with a PEG near 0.6, the stock is not expensive against the growth it is delivering.

What has changed is the composition of the risk. The August 17 8-K puts a filed, quantified number on an exposure that until now existed only in press reports: $105B, capped, shortfall-based, single-counterparty, 20-year, with an optional 3.8 GW extension at NVIDIA's discretion. Direct-customer concentration rose to 54% of revenue and 64% of receivables in a single year, while management's own risk language now concedes that those same customers are building substitutes. Roughly a quarter of the most recent quarter's GAAP EPS came from unrealized marks on a $82.5B portfolio concentrated in the sector NVIDIA sells into, with sensitivity to a 10% decline more than doubling in one quarter. Each of these is a reason to own less than a full position, and none is a reason to sell a franchise generating $130B of annual operating income.

The prior four reports rated NVDA a BUY at prices below today's. The stock has risen roughly 10% since the last, and the newly filed contingent obligation raises the discount we apply to the terminal multiple. Probability-weighted expected return of approximately +14% against a −29% bear case, with a dated catalyst nine days out priced for a ±9.9% move, supports ACCUMULATE rather than BUY. Buy in tranches; add on the print or into the $195–205 range.
