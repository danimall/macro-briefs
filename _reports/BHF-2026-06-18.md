**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

On November 6, 2025, Brighthouse Financial agreed to be acquired by Aquarian Holdings for $70.00 per share in all cash, valuing the equity near $4.1 billion. Shareholders approved the transaction on February 12, 2026, the HSR antitrust waiting period has expired, and the remaining gating items are state insurance regulatory approvals (Delaware, New York, Massachusetts), FINRA Rule 1017 approval, and customary closing conditions. At $63.22, the stock trades roughly 10.7% below the $70.00 cash price. That spread is the investment thesis. Every conventional valuation input — earnings, book value, RBC ratio — matters now only as a guide to whether the deal closes and where the stock lands if it does not.

The headline financials would mislead anyone screening BHF mechanically. Q1 2026 produced a GAAP net loss available to common of $(792)M, or $(13.82) per share, the trailing P/E reads negative, and the Altman Z-Score is near zero. None of this signals operating distress. The GAAP loss is driven by non-cash mark-to-market on variable-annuity hedges and the change in market risk benefits — $(509)M of net derivative losses and a $(748)M MRB swing from equity and rate moves. Stripping that volatility out, adjusted earnings were $239M in Q1 2026, up from $235M a year earlier, with net investment spread expanding. The Z-Score is built for industrial firms; an insurer's $231B of "liabilities" are policyholder reserves and separate accounts, not distress debt.

We rate BHF a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale tied to the merger arbitrage. The spread offers ~10.7% gross upside over a closing timeline that should resolve within 2026, a respectable annualized return for an event with shareholder approval and HSR clearance already in hand. The reason this is not a clean BUY is that the downside on a deal break is asymmetric. The ~35% premium embedded in the deal price implies the stock re-rates toward the low-$50s or below if regulators block the transaction or buyer financing falls through, and the financing syndicate (Mubadala Capital's ~$3 billion commitment, being syndicated by Aquarian) was still being assembled as recently as late May 2026. The reward-to-risk is roughly 1:1.5 against the buyer, which justifies holding existing positions but not aggressively adding.

## Company Overview

Brighthouse is one of the largest U.S. providers of annuities and life insurance, spun off from MetLife in 2017. It distributes through independent channels and operates four segments:

- **Annuities** — the core business, roughly $163B of $237B total assets. Variable, fixed, index-linked (the "Shield" registered index-linked annuity, the growth product), and income annuities. This segment generates essentially all adjusted earnings ($324M in Q1 2026).
- **Life** — term, universal, whole, and variable life. Small and roughly breakeven (Q1 2026 adjusted loss of $6M).
- **Run-off** — discontinued blocks including universal life with secondary guarantees (ULSG), structured settlements, pension risk transfer, and COLI. Persistently loss-making on an adjusted basis (–$48M in Q1 2026).
- **Corporate & Other** — institutional spread/funding-agreement business, excess capital, debt interest, and reinsured long-term care.

The defining structural feature is a very large, in-force variable annuity book with rich guarantees (GMIB/GMWB/GMAB/GMDB). GAAP requires those guarantees to be carried as market risk benefits at fair value and Shield features as embedded derivatives, while the offsetting hedges are also marked to market. Because the two legs do not move in lockstep, reported earnings swing violently; the "annuity guaranteed benefits and Shield" line was $(1.27)B in Q1 2026. Full-year net income has whipsawed: +$1.55B (2021), +$3.78B (2022), –$1.21B (2023), +$286M (2024), +$331M (2025). This volatility, combined with subscale economics and ratings pressure, made BHF a perennially undervalued public company and a logical target inside a private platform.

## Financial Analysis

The economic earnings power is more stable than the GAAP statements suggest. Adjusted earnings reflect a profitable annuity franchise, a roughly breakeven life book, and a loss-making run-off block. Net investment spread expanded, consistent with the higher-rate environment supporting fixed-annuity economics.

The balance sheet carries the marks of the rate cycle:
- Total equity fell to $5.6B from $6.8B at YE2025, pressured by widening unrealized losses on the available-for-sale bond portfolio (AOCI –$4.16B; fixed-maturity net unrealized loss of $5.9B from higher rates). These are non-cash and reverse as bonds pull to par or rates fall.
- Long-term debt is $3.15B. The investment portfolio is high quality: 97.2% investment grade; commercial mortgage loans 99% performing with average LTV of 68% and DSCR of 2.2x.
- The company targets a combined RBC ratio of 400–450%, and all insurance subsidiaries exceeded required RBC at last filing.
- Holding-company liquidity is thin relative to the enterprise — the parent held roughly $827M of liquid assets and depends on subsidiary dividends, which require Delaware DOI approval in 2026.

The earnings surprise record reinforces the GAAP-volatility point rather than signaling deterioration: over four quarters, BHF posted three misses (–7.5%, –29.8%, –23.9%) and one enormous beat (+230.5% in Q3 2025). These are noise generated by hedge and MRB marks, not operating cadence. For a merger-arb position the relevant question is solvency and RBC adequacy, both of which support the deal closing, not quarterly EPS precision.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth is not the thesis and would not, on its own, justify ownership. Shield index-linked annuity sales remain the organic growth lever, but the Fed's late-2025 rate cuts (to 3.50–3.75%) modestly pressure spread economics and new annuity demand. Estimate revisions are mixed: current-quarter EPS revised +8.5% over 30 days but current-year down 1.9% and next-year down 7.1% over 90 days, consistent with analysts modeling a deal close and adjusting for rate-driven spread compression rather than a fundamental change.

The growth that matters here is the realized return from spread capture: $63.22 to $70.00 is +10.7% gross, plus the absence of any thesis dependence on revenue acceleration. That is the entire forward-return case, and it is dated: a close in 2026 implies an attractive annualized return for an event already through shareholder vote and antitrust review. This is a transaction-driven return, not an operating-growth story.

## Valuation Assessment

The only valuation that matters is the deal arithmetic. The merger consideration is a fixed $70.00 cash per share. At $63.22, the gross spread is 10.7%. Against the analyst price-target range (low $55, mean $65, median $66, high $70), the high target is effectively the deal price and the mean sits between the current price and the consideration, reflecting probability-weighted deal-close odds rather than a fundamental fair value.

The standalone peer comparison is informative only for the break scenario. Life & health peers trade at a median P/E of 20.0 (CNO 20.0, UNM 19.9, PRI 11.7), but BHF's GAAP earnings are too volatile to anchor a multiple — Jackson Financial, the closest VA-heavy comparable, also screens with a negative P/E (–18.2) for the same MRB-mark reasons. The pre-deal undisturbed price was in the mid-$40s to low-$50s through summer and early fall 2025 (the stock traded $42–48 in September 2025 before the deal leaked/announced and gapped to ~$58 on September 19 and then to ~$66 on the November 6 announcement). A deal break therefore implies downside to roughly $48–52, or –18% to –24% from current levels.

This frames the asymmetry precisely: ~$6.78/share upside to $70 (+10.7%) versus roughly $11–15/share downside on a break (–18% to –24%). We assign a moderately favorable valuation score because the spread is genuinely wide for a shareholder-approved, HSR-cleared transaction and the implied close probability (using a ~$50 break price) is around 75–78%, arguably conservative for a deal at this stage. But the score is not high, because the residual risk is real and the downside dominates the upside in magnitude.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. annuity and life industry is in a multi-year wave of private-capital and alternative-asset managers acquiring annuity writers — Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, Brookfield, Sixth Street — to gather long-duration liabilities and redeploy assets into higher-yielding private credit. Aquarian's bid, backed by Mubadala Capital, fits this pattern squarely. BHF's hard-to-hedge VA block, GAAP volatility, and ratings pressure made it subscale and unloved as a public equity but valuable inside a platform with a different capital structure and asset-management model.

Standalone, BHF's competitive position is stable-to-weakening: it competes on scale, product features, and financial-strength ratings against larger peers, and insurer M&A by PE-style buyers draws ratings-agency scrutiny that has already weighed on BHF's ratings. The five-year TSR (178.95 indexed) lagged its peer group (201.0), which explains the board's receptivity to a ~35% premium. The competitive trajectory does not improve the equity case; it explains why the company is being taken private.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-completion risk is the dominant factor.** Closing requires insurance regulatory approvals in Delaware, New York, and Massachusetts that are largely outside the parties' control, plus FINRA Rule 1017 sign-off. The agreement contains no "Burdensome Condition" acceptance obligation and a Material Adverse Effect out; the termination fee is approximately $144M. A break sends the stock toward the undisturbed price.

**Deal-financing risk is the live, primary-source-confirmed concern.** Bloomberg reported on May 21, 2026, that Aquarian was approaching new investors to syndicate the ~$3 billion committed by Mubadala Capital, confirming the financing was not fully locked at signing. A subsequent late-May report indicated new institutional investors had joined, which is incrementally positive but unsettled. Mubadala Capital is a backer of the buyer; Aquarian is the contractual acquirer. We have not seen primary confirmation that financing is fully committed; that is the single item most worth tracking.

**Ratings overhang** is a standard concern in PE-led insurance acquisitions and is explicitly flagged as a risk factor; regulators scrutinize affiliated-asset strategies, leverage, and investment policy.

**Standalone tail risks (relevant only if the deal breaks):** VA guarantee and hedging volatility under equity stress; ULSG run-off sensitivity to rates; $12.1B of CRE mortgage loans (22% office, with nonaccruals ticking up to $347M from $333M); and AOCI/unrealized bond losses if rates stay elevated. Litigation (cost-of-insurance class actions, MOVEit data breach) carries disclosed reasonably-possible losses of only ~$10M plus up to ~$100M on tax/reinsurance matters, not deal-relevant.

**Capital is locked.** No common dividend and no buyback during pendency (despite $441M of remaining authorization), and a $328M MetLife Tax Receivables Agreement liability accelerates at close. Preferred dividends continue at ~$26M/quarter. Holders capture value only through the $70 cash.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sending a mixed but generally calm signal. Near-term ATM IV of 23.1% sits far below the one-year historical volatility of 49.5% (a –26.4% spread), and the term structure is flat (near 23.1%, far 24.9%), consistent with a market that expects the stock to be pinned by the deal price rather than to move on operating news. That is typical of a high-probability arb. The cautionary note is positioning: the put/call open-interest ratio rose from 3.67 to 5.35 over the past month, and put/call skew of 7.6% indicates elevated put demand. This is consistent with arbitrageurs hedging the deal-break tail (buying downside protection against the ~20% air-pocket) rather than a directional bearish view on closing. The 11.7% short interest (11.6 days to cover) similarly reflects merger-arb mechanics and hedging more than a fundamental short thesis. The depressed IV makes downside protection relatively inexpensive for holders who want to cap break risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($70.00, +10.7%):** Insurance regulators in DE/NY/MA approve, FINRA clears Rule 1017, Aquarian completes the Mubadala financing syndicate, and the deal closes in 2026 at $70.00 cash. Holders capture the full spread plus time-value decay narrowing as the close approaches. Given shareholder approval and HSR clearance already secured, this is the base-case path.

**Bear Case (~$50, –20%):** Regulators impose burdensome conditions or block the transaction over the PE-style affiliated-asset/leverage model, or Aquarian fails to finalize financing. The stock re-rates to roughly the undisturbed pre-deal price in the low-$50s, and investors are left holding a subscale, GAAP-volatile VA writer with rate-driven unrealized bond losses, office CRE exposure, a loss-making run-off block, and ratings pressure — the very profile that made it unattractive standalone.

The probability-weighted expected value modestly favors holding: at roughly 75–78% close odds, the blended expected return is slightly positive, but the magnitude of the downside relative to the upside keeps this from being a high-conviction add.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold (event-driven) rationale.

- **Reassessment timeline:** Continuous through the expected 2026 close. The next natural checkpoints are any 8-K announcing state regulatory approvals (DE/NY/MA), FINRA Rule 1017 clearance, and confirmation that Aquarian's financing syndicate is fully committed.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) Primary confirmation that buyer financing is fully committed and closed, removing the largest residual risk; (2) two of the three state insurance approvals secured, which would compress the spread and tighten close odds toward 90%+; (3) the spread widening materially (e.g., stock toward $58–60 on a non-fundamental wobble) while the regulatory path remains intact, which would improve reward-to-risk back above 2:1.

- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) A regulator signals it will impose a burdensome condition or deny the change-of-control application; (2) Aquarian/Mubadala financing is reported as falling apart or being repriced; (3) a ratings action or MAE-relevant development that gives the buyer a credible walk-away path. Any of these would shift the expected value sharply negative given the ~20% break downside.

For holders, buying inexpensive downside puts is a sensible way to cap the break tail while retaining the spread.

## Conclusion

BHF is a merger-arbitrage position trading at a 10.7% gross spread to a $70.00 all-cash deal that shareholders have approved and that has cleared antitrust review. The standalone financials — a $(792)M GAAP quarterly loss, negative trailing P/E, near-zero Altman Z-Score — are accounting artifacts of variable-annuity hedge and MRB marks and should not be read as distress; adjusted earnings are stable at ~$239M per quarter and RBC remains within the 400–450% target. The return case is entirely the spread, and the risk is a regulatory or financing failure that re-rates the stock ~20% lower toward its undisturbed level. With the financing syndicate still being assembled as of late May 2026 and three state insurance approvals outstanding, the asymmetry (1:1.5 against the buyer) supports holding existing positions and capturing the spread, but not aggressively adding. We rate BHF HOLD, and we would upgrade to BUY on confirmation of fully committed financing or two of three state approvals.
