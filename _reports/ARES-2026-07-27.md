**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ares Management is one of the largest global alternative asset managers, with $644.3B of AUM as of March 31, 2026, weighted ~66% to a private-credit franchise that ranks #1/#2 in direct lending. The business generates predominantly recurring management fees, with 93% of Q1 2026 management fees sourced from perpetual capital or long-dated funds. Fee Related Earnings grew 26% YoY to $685.3M in Q1 2026, management fees rose 21% to $989.5M, and the dividend was raised 20% to $1.35/quarter. On the reported fundamentals through Q1, this is a fast-compounding, high-margin franchise.

The stock trades at $126.51, down ~30% from its January 2026 high near $176 and ~35% below the August 2025 peak of $195. The decline is a private-credit sentiment event rather than a fundamentals collapse in the reported quarter. The proximate cause: Ares Strategic Income Fund (ASIF, ~$22.6B), the flagship perpetual wealth private-credit vehicle, capped redemptions for a second consecutive quarter in Q2 2026 after requests reached 14.4% (from 11.6%), exceeding the quarterly gate. This is the central debate for the entire perpetual-capital cohort: whether semi-liquid wealth vehicles are truly "permanent" when the retail channel tests liquidity in unison.

We rate ARES a HOLD. The bull case (durable compounder on sale, ~22% embedded fee growth, insider buying near the lows) and the bear case (ASIF gating as the leading edge of a broader liquidity unwind that undermines the fee-durability thesis) are both credible, and the near-term evidence is genuinely two-sided: FRE and management fees grew strongly through Q1, but estimate revisions are broadly negative, the company has missed EPS in three of the last four quarters, and near-term implied volatility is pricing imminent stress. We would want either a stabilization in ASIF redemption trends or a lower entry before moving to a constructive stance.

## Company Overview

Ares operates through four reportable segments plus an "Other" bucket:

- **Credit Group** ($422.6B AUM, ~66% of total) — the dominant franchise, spanning liquid credit, alternative credit, opportunistic credit, and direct lending. U.S. direct lending ($193B) plus European ($87B) is the single largest exposure. This is a private-credit-centric manager.
- **Real Assets Group** ($143.4B AUM) — real estate and infrastructure equity/debt. AUM roughly doubled from ~$75B (12/2024) largely via the GCP International acquisition (global logistics/industrial real estate and data centers, ex-Greater China).
- **Secondaries Group** ($42.6B AUM) — PE, real estate, infrastructure, and credit secondaries.
- **Private Equity Group** ($24.7B AUM) — the smallest and slowest-growing segment.
- **Other** — Ares Insurance Solutions (Aspida), SPACs, an applied-AI venture, and structured financing vehicles.

The moat rests on recurring management fees from a heavily institutional and long-dated base. Permanent-capital vehicles (the publicly-traded BDC Ares Capital Corp (ARCC) and non-traded perpetual wealth funds) anchor fee durability. Roughly 83% of debt assets and 51% of total assets are floating rate, so Part I fees rise with net investment income in a higher-for-longer rate environment. Total AUM grew from $484B (12/2024) to $622B (12/2025) to $644B (3/2026), driven by both record organic fundraising and the GCP acquisition.

## Financial Analysis

**Fee Related Earnings**, the primary value metric, has compounded consistently: FY2023 $1.164B → FY2024 $1.362B → FY2025 $1.775B (+30% YoY), with Q1 2026 FRE at $685.3M vs $542.2M a year earlier (+26%). Realized Income reached $722.9M in Q1 2026 vs $580.2M (+25%). Consolidated GAAP revenue (which grosses up Consolidated Funds) was $1.396B in Q1 2026 vs $1.089B (+28%), and FY2025 revenue was ~$5.60B vs $3.88B in FY2024.

Management fees of $989.5M in Q1 2026 (+21% YoY) were boosted by a full quarter of GCP ($34.6M), ASIF and European direct-lending Part I fees (+$29.2M), and organic deployment. Incentive fees jumped to $161.9M from $32.0M, but this is not clean run-rate: $138.5M came from a one-time SDL I continuation-vehicle sale. The underlying recurring fee engine is strong, but the headline incentive-fee number should not be extrapolated.

FRE margin remains robust at approximately 53% (segment FRE $685M on ~$1.30B segment revenue), holding up despite 24% YoY headcount growth to 4,297 FTEs (~511 from GCP). This is genuine operating leverage on the committed-capital base.

The balance sheet: debt obligations rose to $4.39B (3/2026) from $3.94B (12/2025), including a new $400M Term Loan (SOFR+1.00%, due 2029) used to pay down the revolver. Debt is well-laddered across senior notes maturing 2028/2030/2052/2054, subordinated notes due 2051, and a credit facility due 2030, all covenants in compliance. Standard distress metrics like the Altman Z-Score are not meaningful for an asset manager whose balance sheet is grossed up by ~$13.7B of non-recourse Consolidated Fund/CLO debt; ROE near 25% and strong FRE coverage are the relevant reads.

A Series B mandatory convertible preferred (30M shares, 6.75% dividend, ~$25.3M/quarter) auto-converts to Class A on 10/1/2027 at 0.2717–0.3260 shares each. This adds 8–10M shares at current prices but removes the annual preferred dividend (roughly $101M annualized), a modest net structural tailwind to common EPS post-conversion.

On capital allocation, a $750M buyback was renewed in February 2026, but zero shares were repurchased in Q1 2026 — management is prioritizing the dividend and growth investment over buybacks. The dividend yields ~4.3% at the current price.

The single most bullish forward data point sits in the filings: as of 3/31/2026, $79.4B of AUM was not yet paying fees, plus $4.2B of development assets, which could generate ~$715.9M of incremental annual management fees — a 22% embedded growth rate on the base. This committed-but-unactivated capital is what distinguishes a durable-fee compounder from a name that lives on lumpy incentive fees.

**A note on share count for valuation.** The provided $28.5B market cap reflects only the ~222M Class A shares. Fully-diluted economic ownership includes ~104M Class C/AOG units held by insiders (~31.6% of the operating group), plus the preferred conversion; true economic enterprise value is materially larger than the Class-A float implies, and forward P/E should be computed on the fully-diluted base.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth engine remains intact through the reported quarter. AUM is up ~18% YoY, and external reporting cites ~$30B raised in Q1 2026 (+45% YoY) with an $8.5B Pathfinder raise in June 2026 — institutional demand has not cracked even as the wealth channel wobbles. The embedded fee-growth pipeline provides visibility that most financials lack; this capital is committed and will activate as it deploys, largely independent of near-term fundraising.

Against this, the estimate-revision picture is negative and worth weighting. Current-quarter consensus fell 6.5% over 90 days to $1.27, next-quarter down 4.8% to $1.42, current-year down 3.4% to $5.89, and next-year down 2.7% to $7.22. The company has missed EPS in three of its last four quarters, including a 16% miss in Q4 2025 and a 9% miss in Q1 2026. Falling estimates paired with a real-time redemption scare mean the market is not extrapolating the +26% FRE headline. The strong-BUY signal that would normally attach to a growth acceleration (>30% estimate revisions plus >30% revenue growth) is absent here; estimates are moving the wrong direction.

Fee-rate compression is the secondary growth drag. Both Credit and Real Assets effective fee rates declined YoY — Credit due to lower-fee liquid credit growth (<0.50%), Real Assets due to GCP funds priced on NOI/GAV rather than commitment. AUM is growing partly in lower-quality, lower-fee-rate mix, so headline AUM growth overstates fee growth. Real Assets, tied to logistics, data centers, and Japanese/Asian real estate, is the more interesting structural bet given AI-driven digital-infrastructure demand, but it comes with GCP integration cost (Real Assets headcount +66%) and contingent earnout dilution.

Net: the growth thesis is real and above-average in durability given the embedded fee pipeline, but decelerating estimate momentum and fee-rate mix argue against scoring this as accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $126.51 and a trailing P/E near 59x on Class-A-only earnings, the headline multiple looks stretched, but that figure is distorted by GAAP items and the wrong share count. The more meaningful anchor is forward economic earnings: current-year consensus of $5.89 and next-year $7.22 imply roughly 21x current-year and 17.5x next-year on the correct denominator. Against alternative-manager peers, that is not obviously cheap — Carlyle trades ~30x with a -35% 52-week discount, and the broader Asset Management & Custody Banks peer median P/E is 24.3x. But those peers are lower-quality or lower-growth on average, and Ares' durable fee base and embedded fee growth justify a premium to the traditional asset managers (TROW at 11.6x, NTRS at 17.9x) rather than a discount.

The valuation dimension warrants skepticism precisely because the stock screens as "having pulled back." A 30% decline from the highs is not, by itself, a valuation input, and cheapness relative to prior price is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal. The correct question is price versus forward economic value today. On next-year earnings of $7.22, the ~17.5x multiple is reasonable but not asymmetric — it prices a good compounder fairly given the live overhang. The ~4.3% dividend yield provides absolute support, well-covered by FRE.

Analyst targets bracket the range: low $122, mean $141.4, median $140, high $162 — implying ~12% upside to the mean. Because the stock trades below 30x forward with rising committed AUM, these targets would normally function as floors that lag reality. Here, the negative estimate-revision trend and the unresolved ASIF gating temper that logic; targets are more likely to be revised sideways-to-down until redemption trends stabilize.

The valuation is fair, not compelling. We score it 5.5 — the multiple is defensible against quality peers and the yield supports downside, but there is no identified catalyst forcing a near-term re-rate, and the cheapness-versus-prior-price framing is a trap to be resisted.

## Competitive Landscape

Ares competes in the ~$1.8T private-credit industry alongside Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Blue Owl, and Brookfield. Its differentiation: scale and #1/#2 positioning in direct lending; a heavily institutional and long-dated fee base; the GCP-driven expansion into global logistics, data centers, and Japanese/Asian real estate (an explicit bet on AI-driven digital infrastructure); and a growing insurance platform (Aspida) supplying sticky, long-duration capital. Flagship direct-lending funds (ACE V/VI, SDL series, PCS II) show net IRRs generally 7–15% and net MoICs of 1.1–1.5x.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the institutional side and stressed on the wealth side. The ASIF gating is industry-wide, reflecting coordinated redemption pressure across semi-liquid private-credit vehicles rather than an Ares-specific problem, which limits relative competitive damage but does not eliminate franchise risk. Ares' institutional weighting is a relative advantage versus managers more dependent on the retail/wealth channel, and continued record institutional fundraising shows the core franchise gaining, not losing, share. The concern is that the entire cohort's "perpetual capital" narrative is being stress-tested at once, which compresses the multiple sector-wide regardless of individual execution. We score competitive position 6.0 — a genuine leader with improving institutional share, offset by a sector-wide liquidity question that dents the perpetual-capital moat.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Private-credit redemption pressure (dominant risk).** Management frames the ASIF gating as <1% of the shareholder base and largely non-US, and ASIF AUM was still growing in the 10-Q ($26.3B by Q1). But this is a direct test of the model underpinning 93% of fee durability. Persistent redemptions could pressure fundraising momentum and fee growth in the highest-margin segment. This is not yet priced as a fundamental impairment — the market is pricing sentiment, and a genuine perpetual-capital unwind would carry further downside.

2. **Single-fund/customer concentration.** ARCC contributed ~29% of Credit Group management fees in Q1 2026, and other significant Credit funds another ~45%. ARCC is a permanent-capital anchor, but a large share of the most important segment's fees sits in one publicly-traded vehicle.

3. **GCP integration and earnout dilution.** Two contingent earnouts remain — DC Earnout (≤$1.0B) and Japan Earnout (≤$0.5B) — settling by June 2028 (≥15% cash, rest equity). Contingent-consideration fair value is ~$777M and rising ($14.3M non-cash charge in Q1). This creates a multi-year contingent equity-dilution and comp overhang up to $1.5B combined.

4. **Fee-rate compression.** Effective fee rates declined YoY in both Credit and Real Assets; AUM growth is partly lower-fee mix.

5. **Structural governance items.** A $581.7M (and rising) Tax Receivable Agreement liability pays 85% of tax savings to Holdco insiders on AOG exchanges. The controlled-company structure gives public Class A holders limited governance say.

The risk score of 4.5 reflects that the ASIF gating is a specific, live, asymmetric catalyst that is not fully priced as a fundamental (versus sentiment) event, layered on concentration and multi-year dilution overhangs.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 78.9% versus 43.1% one-year historical vol — rich, at the 82nd percentile of the current chain, with a ±7.0% implied move on the nearest expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (78.9% near-term vs 46.2% at six months), signaling an anticipated near-term catalyst. Put/call skew is elevated at 9.5% and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.66 to 1.06 since early June, confirming bearish positioning building into the next print. Short interest is elevated at 15.8% of float with 6.6 days to cover. This positioning is consistent with the ASIF overhang and argues against chasing here; it also means a stabilization in redemption trends could produce a sharp relief move given the crowded bearish setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($160–176).** The ASIF gating proves to be idiosyncratic wealth-channel noise (<1% of shareholders, non-US skew), institutional fundraising continues at record pace, and the $79.4B of unactivated AUM converts to ~$716M of incremental fees, driving 20%+ FRE growth. On next-year EPS re-rating toward the high analyst target of $162 as estimates turn positive, with the ~4.3% dividend supporting total return. Insider director buying near the lows ($124–127 in February) confirms the compounder-on-sale view.

**Bear Case ($95–100).** ASIF gating is the leading edge of a coordinated perpetual-capital liquidity unwind. Redemptions persist and spread to other semi-liquid vehicles, forced asset sales pressure NAV marks, fundraising momentum breaks, and the 93% fee-durability thesis is repriced structurally lower. Estimates continue falling, GCP earnout dilution compounds, and the multiple compresses toward the 52-week low near $96 as the sector-wide private-credit cascade develops.

**Base Case (~$140).** ASIF stress remains contained but unresolved for another quarter or two, institutional fundraising offsets wealth-channel softness, FRE grows in the high teens, and the stock recovers toward the analyst mean of ~$141 as sentiment stabilizes without a full re-rate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence is genuinely two-sided: a strong reported fee engine and embedded pipeline against a live, unpriced liquidity catalyst and negative estimate momentum. We lack sufficient conviction for a BUY at $126.51 given the near-term overhang, and the fundamentals are too strong to AVOID.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- ASIF redemption requests decline below the quarterly gate for two consecutive quarters, removing the gating overhang.
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive over a 30–90 day window.
- A pullback toward the low-$110s or below (near book/trough sentiment) that widens the margin of safety while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Redemption gating spreads to additional Ares perpetual/wealth vehicles, or ASIF requests rise materially above 14.4%.
- Evidence of forced asset sales or NAV markdowns in the credit funds.
- FRE growth decelerates below ~10% YoY or management fees turn sequentially negative.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly earnings release (following the May 1 report) is the natural checkpoint, with ASIF redemption disclosures and any commentary on wealth-channel flows the decisive data. The rich near-term IV and backwardation suggest the market expects that catalyst imminently.

## Conclusion

Ares is a high-quality alternative manager compounding FRE at ~26% with an unusually durable fee base and ~22% of embedded fee growth already committed, trading ~30% below its January highs on a sector-wide private-credit liquidity scare. That combination is attractive on paper, but the ASIF redemption gating is a specific, unresolved, asymmetric risk that the market is pricing as sentiment rather than as a fundamental impairment; if it proves fundamental, downside runs to the mid-$90s. Falling estimates across every window, three EPS misses in four quarters, and near-term IV pricing imminent stress reinforce patience.

We rate ARES a HOLD with an overall score of 5.8. The score trajectory has been range-bound (5.2–6.1) with a declining Management Quality and Growth Prospects trend as the redemption overhang emerged, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that stall. We would become buyers on either a stabilization in ASIF redemption trends with an estimate-revision turn, or a lower price in the low-$110s that restores asymmetry. Financials carry a structural negative-alpha caution, and cheapness relative to prior price is not a reason to buy; we require the redemption catalyst to resolve before rewarding the compounder thesis.
