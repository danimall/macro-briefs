**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kinetik is a pure-play Permian/Delaware Basin midstream operator providing gathering, processing, treating, NGL transport, crude gathering, and produced-water disposal, ranking as the fourth-largest gas processor in both the Delaware Basin and the broader Permian. The investment case is two-sided. On the constructive side, the company carries a ~7% dividend yield, a New Mexico/Eddy County growth program scheduled to convert capex into EBITDA across 2026–2027, and a live takeover approach from Western Midstream (backed by Occidental) that has been reported twice by the Financial Times, with Kinetik reportedly preparing a sale process. The entire published analyst target range (low $48, mean $52.4, high $61) sits above the current $45.53.

On the cautious side, the underlying business is not growing. FY2025 revenue rose 19% to $1.764B, but Adjusted EBITDA grew only 2% to $987.7M, and 2026 guidance is flat at the midpoint (~$1.0B). Consensus EPS estimates have been cut sharply: current-quarter estimates are down 32% over 30 days and 43% over 90 days, with next-year estimates down 16%/30% over the same windows. Q1'26 produced a small net loss driven by a non-cash hedge mark, Waha gas-price weakness is forcing producer curtailments of ~220 MMcf/d, and the sponsor (I Squared Capital) is steadily selling shares into the market.

We rate KNTK a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale anchored on the M&A optionality and the New Mexico ramp, supported by yield. We would not short this name into a live sale process given 10.3% short interest and the squeeze risk on any deal confirmation, and we would not chase it as a BUY given flat fundamentals and an unconfirmed catalyst.

## Company Overview

Kinetik is an integrated, single-basin Permian (Delaware) midstream company headquartered in Houston with operational headquarters in Midland. Its asset base includes 2.4+ Bcf/d of cryogenic processing across eight complexes, 4,200+ miles of gathering pipe, 825,000 HP of compression, ~290 miles of crude gathering with 90 MBbl of storage, and ~370 miles of water gathering with 580 MBbl/d of disposal capacity.

The company reports two segments. **Midstream Logistics** is the operating core, accounting for more than 99% of consolidated revenue and cost of sales (gas gathering and processing, crude gathering, water disposal). **Pipeline Transportation** consists primarily of equity-method investments in long-haul pipes: Permian Highway Pipeline (55.5%, operated by Kinder Morgan), Breviloba/Shin Oak NGL (33%, operated by Enterprise), plus wholly owned Kinetik NGL and Delaware Link assets. These equity-method investments generate roughly 89%+ of the segment's EBITDA through equity income and distributions rather than consolidated revenue, a structural feature worth bearing in mind: a meaningful share of cash flow comes from assets Kinetik does not operate or control.

A structural point frames the entire financial picture. Kinetik is an Up-C holding company. Kinetik Inc. owns the non-economic general partner interest plus a ~42–45% LP stake in the operating partnership. The ~93.6M sponsor-held Common Units are classified as redeemable noncontrolling interest (temporary equity, $4.51B at Q1'26), which produces negative book equity (–$1.67B at Q1'26 after a redemption-value remeasurement) and a distressed-looking Altman Z-Score of 0.11. Both readings are artifacts of the corporate structure, not signals of insolvency. The genuine financial-risk gauge is leverage and distribution coverage.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and EBITDA.** FY2025 revenue reached $1.764B (+19% YoY), up from $1.483B in 2024 and $1.256B in 2023, with growth driven by a full year of Durango, the Barilla Draw addition, and higher NGL/condensate volumes. The revenue mix is approximately 74% product (commodity-exposed) and 25% fee-based service, more commodity-sensitive than fee-heavy peers, though much product margin is structurally hedged via percentage-of-proceeds contracts and swaps. Adjusted EBITDA grew only 2% to $987.7M despite the 19% revenue gain, because incremental product revenue carries pass-through cost of sales and because equity-method EBITDA fell following the GCX and EPIC divestitures. Segment EBITDA split: Midstream Logistics $636M, Pipeline Transportation $370M.

**Profitability and cash flow.** FY2025 total net income including NCI was $526M, boosted by a $415M gain on the EPIC sale; income attributable to Class A holders was only ~$178M. Q1'26 posted a net loss of –$5.1M including NCI, driven by a $47M non-cash unrealized commodity-hedge mark and lower equity-method earnings post-EPIC. FY2025 operating cash flow was $604M; free cash flow after ~$492M of capex was ~$112M.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt was ~$3.67B at Q1'26: $1.05B 6.625% notes (2028), $1.0B 5.875% notes (2030), a $1.15B term loan at 5.39% (2028), a $468M revolver (2030), and a $187M A/R facility. Approximately $1.81B is floating-rate (a 1% rate move equals ~$18M of annual interest), partly hedged with $1.0B of swaps. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA is approximately 3.7x, elevated but typical for the midstream model, and the company is covenant-compliant with ~$1.16B of liquidity. The 2028 maturity cluster (the $1.05B notes plus the $1.15B term loan) will require refinancing in a higher-rate environment than when the debt was issued.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend is $0.81/quarter ($3.24 annualized, ~7% yield), with $500M paid in 2025. The buyback authorization ($500M, $318M remaining) saw $176M of repurchases in 2025 but zero in Q1'26; management has stated buybacks will not meaningfully ramp until gross debt falls below internal thresholds. That is a disciplined signal, but it also confirms that deleveraging takes priority over returns, and it underscores that the FY2025 dividend of $500M against FCF of $112M was funded partly through divestiture proceeds and the balance sheet rather than organic free cash flow. On a midstream distributable-cash-flow basis (which adds back growth capex), coverage is more comfortable than the FCF figure implies, but the gap is worth flagging.

## Growth Analysis

EBITDA is flat-lining. Despite a 19% revenue increase and active M&A (Durango, Barilla Draw), 2025 Adjusted EBITDA grew only 2%, and 2026 guidance ($950M–$1.05B) is again flat at the midpoint. Growth is being consumed by pass-through commodity costs, equity-method divestitures (GCX in 2024, EPIC in 2025), and Waha gas-price weakness that is forcing producer curtailments of ~220 MMcf/d. The mechanism: record Permian associated-gas production is overwhelming regional takeaway capacity, periodically driving Waha spot prices to or below zero, which incentivizes producers to curtail volumes that would otherwise flow through Kinetik's processing complexes.

The forward growth story rests on the New Mexico/Eddy County buildout. The ECCC pipeline (~150 MMcf/d, Eddy County to Culberson County) is targeted for Q2'26 in-service, and the Kings Landing AGI project is expected to reach service by year-end 2026 after the complex itself came online in late September 2025 (+200 MMcf/d). With ~70% of the 2026 capex budget of $450–510M directed to this region, the bet is that these projects convert into EBITDA growth in 2026–2027 and offset the Waha drag.

The estimate-revision picture is the most important near-term signal, and it is negative. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 32% over 30 days and 43% over 90 days; next-quarter is down 36%/48%; next-year is down 16%/30%. Only the current-year figure is marginally positive over 30 days (+3.2%, though –18.6% over 90 days). This is broad-based negative earnings momentum reflecting the Q1 miss, Waha curtailments, and lost equity-method income, the opposite of the positive-revision trend that would warrant a more constructive growth score. This combination of flat reported EBITDA and falling estimates is why Growth Prospects scores 4.5.

## Valuation Assessment

KNTK trades at 18.6x trailing earnings against a peer-median P/E of 24.4 for oil & gas storage and transportation. On an enterprise basis, ~$3.4B of Class A equity plus ~$3.67B of debt yields roughly $7.1B of EV against ~$1.0B of EBITDA, or ~7.1x EV/EBITDA, a discount to larger fee-based peers (DT Midstream at 31x P/E, Antero Midstream at 24.4x) that reflects Kinetik's single-basin concentration, higher commodity exposure, sponsor overhang, and flat growth. The ~7% dividend yield is above the peer-median 4.3% and provides a valuation floor, though investors should note the thin FCF coverage discussed above.

The case that the market is mispricing KNTK rests on two specific items rather than on the low multiple alone. First, the entire published analyst target range sits above the current price (low $48, median $52, mean $52.4, high $61), implying a 5–34% gap to the current $45.53, with 11 of 15 covering analysts at buy or strong-buy. Second, the takeover optionality: a sale to Western Midstream/Occidental, if it materialized, would likely clear at a control premium to public-market levels. Against those, the discount is partly justified: falling estimates mean the forward multiple is not as cheap as the trailing figure suggests, and the sponsor's open-market selling at $44–51 is hard to reconcile with an imminent premium takeout. Net, we score valuation 5.5: there is identifiable peer-discount and yield support plus a real upside catalyst, but the gap is not a clean mispricing because the structural and earnings-momentum headwinds are genuine.

## Competitive Landscape

Kinetik competes in a fragmented, consolidating Permian midstream market against much larger diversified players: Energy Transfer, Targa, Enterprise, ONEOK, Kinder Morgan, and Western Midstream. Its edge is dense Delaware Basin processing scale with multiple residue-gas and NGL takeaway interconnects, plus a growing, less-contested New Mexico position management characterizes as early-mover. Competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving: the New Mexico expansion adds defensible scale, while the EPIC and GCX divestitures reduce exposure to non-controlled long-haul equity stakes in favor of owned Delaware Basin gathering and processing.

The same single-basin concentration that gives Kinetik gathering density also concentrates its risk in one region's takeaway constraints, producer activity, seismicity-driven water-disposal regulation, and Waha pricing. The midstream consolidation makes a single-basin, sponsor-controlled, ~$3.4B-equity operator a logical acquisition target, and Western Midstream's Delaware Basin focus makes Kinetik strategically complementary. Competitive Position scores 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Waha pricing and curtailments.** The most immediate operating headwind. Producer curtailments tied to weak/negative Waha gas prices directly pressure processing volumes and 2026 EBITDA; new Permian gas takeaway capacity is the swing factor.

**Energy Transfer (Lone Star) litigation.** Two consolidated NGL-purchase-agreement lawsuits (2021 and 2025) went to bench trial in March–April 2026, with a decision pending. Reserves are characterized as immaterial, but an adverse specific-performance or damages ruling is a binary tail risk, and litigation costs are already meaningful ($19.7M in 2025, $11.6M in Q1'26 alone).

**Sponsor overhang.** Blackstone and I Squared Capital retain majority voting power and director-designation rights while each owns ≥10%. I Squared is methodically selling: ~4.0M shares at $44.85 (~$179M) on Feb 26, 2026, and ~1.5M+ more in late April at $48–51, creating a persistent technical overhang. Other large holders have exited (Brave Warrior sold its entire ~$69M position in February). A full sponsor exit could clear the path for an outright sale, but in the interim it caps the share price.

**Leverage and refinancing.** ~3.7x net leverage with the 2028 maturity cluster and ~$1.81B of floating-rate debt is manageable but not trivial in a higher-for-longer rate regime.

**Commodity exposure.** ~74% product revenue creates direct sensitivity to NGL and residue gas prices; hedge marks generate GAAP earnings noise, as in the Q1'26 loss. If the recent de-escalation in the oil complex holds and crude/NGL prices compress, product margins face pressure.

**M&A uncertainty.** The takeover is press-sourced and unconfirmed by SEC filing. A "no deal" outcome could unwind the optionality premium embedded in the stock.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing an event. ATM implied volatility jumped from 45% to 57% intraday on June 2 while the stock was essentially flat, and the put/call open-interest ratio is extremely call-tilted at 0.04 (down from an already low 0.08). Rising IV alongside a stable price and call-heavy positioning is consistent with informed positioning ahead of a potential catalyst, most plausibly the reported sale process. This supports the M&A optionality thesis but also signals elevated near-term uncertainty: the market is paying up for upside exposure, which means a "no deal" resolution would carry asymmetric downside as that premium deflates. Combined with 10.3% short interest and 5.4 days to cover, any confirmed deal would create squeeze conditions, reinforcing why this is not a short.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
A takeover by Western Midstream/Occidental clears at a control premium toward the high end of the analyst range ($61), or the standalone story re-rates as the New Mexico buildout (ECCC in Q2'26, Kings Landing AGI by YE2026) converts capex into EBITDA growth in 2026–2027, lifting the company off its flat ~$1.0B EBITDA plateau. The sponsors complete an orderly exit, removing the overhang and governance discount, and the ~7% yield underpins total return while investors wait. Bull case ~$61 (+34%).

### Bear Case
No deal materializes and the optionality premium deflates. Waha curtailments worsen and persist, the New Mexico projects ramp slower than guided, and flat-to-down EBITDA combined with continued sponsor selling pressures the shares. An adverse Energy Transfer ruling adds a discrete cost. Falling estimates continue, the forward multiple proves not cheap, and the stock derates toward the low-$40s/high-$30s. Bear case ~$38 (–17%).

### Base Case
No confirmed deal in the near term; the stock trades on yield plus optionality, with the New Mexico ramp gradually offsetting the Waha drag and keeping EBITDA roughly flat-to-slightly-up. The ~7% dividend anchors total return around the current level, with modest appreciation if the sale process advances or estimates stabilize. Base case ~$48 (+5%), in line with the low end of the analyst range.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2'26 earnings release (which should reveal whether the ECCC in-service contributes as guided and whether Waha curtailments are easing) and any 8-K disclosure confirming or terminating the reported sale process. The Energy Transfer litigation decision is also pending and could land at any time.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmation via 8-K or definitive agreement of a Western Midstream/Occidental transaction at a premium, with manageable squeeze-adjusted entry.
- Stabilization or reversal of the negative estimate-revision trend (current-quarter and next-year EPS revisions turning flat-to-positive over 30 days) alongside evidence that New Mexico projects are adding EBITDA on schedule.
- Net debt/EBITDA falling toward 3.0x with buybacks resuming, signaling the balance sheet has cleared management's internal threshold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The reported sale process is publicly terminated with no other strategic alternative, and estimate revisions continue falling.
- Waha curtailments expand materially beyond ~220 MMcf/d, or 2026 EBITDA guidance is cut below the $950M floor.
- An adverse Energy Transfer ruling with material damages or specific-performance obligations.
- Accelerated, distress-flavored sponsor selling or a dividend coverage shortfall forcing a distribution cut.

## Conclusion

Kinetik is a flat-growth, single-basin midstream operator whose near-term earnings momentum is negative: estimates have been cut double-digits across most periods, EBITDA is stuck near $1.0B, and Waha curtailments are a live headwind. What keeps this from being an AVOID is the combination of three specific, identifiable supports: a credible (if unconfirmed) takeover approach from Western Midstream/Occidental with the entire analyst target range above spot, a New Mexico growth program scheduled to add EBITDA through 2026–2027, and a ~7% dividend from a covenant-compliant balance sheet with $1.16B of liquidity. The Up-C structure makes the headline distress metrics meaningless; the real risk is ~3.7x leverage with a 2028 maturity wall, which is manageable.

We rate KNTK a HOLD on a 12-month horizon, with the thesis resting on the sale-process catalyst and the New Mexico ramp providing potential upside while the yield limits downside. We would not buy aggressively into falling estimates and an unconfirmed deal, and we would not short into a live M&A process with elevated short interest and call-heavy options positioning. The posture is patience: monitor Q2 results, the sale-process disclosures, and the litigation decision, and act on the upgrade or downgrade triggers as they resolve.
