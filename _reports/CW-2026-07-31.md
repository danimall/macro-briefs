## Executive Summary

Curtiss-Wright has done almost everything an industrial operator can do well. FY2025 revenue rose 12% to $3,498.4M, of which 9 points were organic; operating income rose 20% to $633.5M with margin expanding to 18.1%; free cash flow of $553.7M converted at 114% of net income. Q1 2026 extended the pattern — sales +13.4% to $913.7M, GAAP diluted EPS $3.46 (+29%), orders of $1.2B for roughly 1.3x book-to-bill with mid-teens order growth in all three segments — and management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $14.90–15.30. The balance sheet is conservatively financed: $957.5M of debt, all fixed-rate at a 3.8% weighted average coupon whose fair value ($909.2M) sits below carrying value, net debt/EBITDA near 0.73x, $721M of an undrawn revolver, and roughly $2.9B of stated covenant borrowing capacity.

The problem is price. At $716.66 the stock trades at 55.7x trailing GAAP EPS of $12.87, roughly 47x the 2026 consensus of $15.24, and 41.9x the 2027 consensus of $17.11, against a 2.2% free-cash-flow yield and a 0.13% dividend. That multiple is being paid for a nuclear new-build narrative that, by the company's own end-market disclosure, is currently about 1.5% of revenue: Power & Process is 19% of 2026E sales, commercial nuclear roughly 65% of that, and new-build (Gen III/Gen IV including SMRs) only 12% of commercial nuclear. The Q1 deck explicitly excludes an AP1000 order from guidance. The 10-K places SMR steady-state production in the middle of the *next* decade. The earning nuclear business today is a high-quality license-extension aftermarket franchise; the valuation reflects an option with a long-dated strike.

Two pieces of evidence sharpen the caution. First, decomposing the proxy's own disclosures, adjusted EPS grew 1.83x from 2021 to 2025 while an assumed $100 investment became $483.25 — implying roughly 2.6x of the ~4.8x return came from multiple expansion rather than earnings. Second, management has structured its repurchase program not to transact at prevailing prices: FY2025 saw 934,000 shares bought for $465M at roughly $498 average with zero acquisitions, while Q1 2026 saw only $14.5M at $662.80 under a $60M plan described as a "minimum… to offset dilution," with a separate $100M tranche gated behind an undisclosed price limit the company says may never trigger. That is defensible stewardship and simultaneously a revealed preference against buying the stock in the $630–685 range. Combined with no open-market insider purchases by any officer or director since at least March 2026, the internal signal argues for holding rather than adding.

## Company Overview

Curtiss-Wright supplies highly engineered products and services across three reportable segments. **Naval & Power** (FY25 sales $1,503.0M, 43% of total, 15.4% margin) is the largest and fastest-growing: main coolant pumps, compact motors, generators, valves and secondary propulsion for U.S. Navy nuclear propulsion (Virginia- and Columbia-class submarines, Ford-class carriers, SSN(X) development), plus ship repair through three coastal service centers; and commercial nuclear content including reactor coolant pumps and control rod drive mechanisms supplied to Westinghouse for the AP1000, alongside aftermarket hardware, valves, fastening systems, containment doors, spent-fuel management and simulation. The 10-K states CW has content on every reactor operating in the U.S., Canada and U.K., with significant South Korean exposure.

**Defense Electronics** ($1,018.6M, 29%, **27.3% margin**) is the most profitable segment: COTS/COTS+ embedded computing, data acquisition and flight-test instrumentation, tactical communications, turret drive stabilization, commercial avionics and flight data recorders, positioned around Modular Open Systems Approach architectures. **Aerospace & Industrial** ($976.8M, 28%, 17.0% margin) covers sensors, controls, electro-mechanical actuation, surface treatment services (shot/laser peening, coatings), and industrial vehicle power management and control systems.

Customer structure: no single customer exceeded 10% of net sales in 2023, 2024 or 2025. U.S. Government direct and indirect sales were 47% of 2025 revenue (48% in 2024, 46% in 2023); including foreign governments, 58% (57%, 56%). Non-U.S. sales were 27% of revenue and foreign operations generated 41% of pre-tax earnings. While the revenue mix is broadening toward commercial nuclear, the government-dependence measure is flat to rising; mix change is not the same as reduced concentration. Roughly 9,100 employees across 20+ countries, ~6% unionized.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory (SEC/XBRL):**

| FY | Revenue | Op. income | Op. margin | Net income | Diluted EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $2,557.0M | $423.4M | 16.6% | $294.3M | $7.62 |
| 2023 | $2,845.4M | $484.6M | 17.0% | $354.5M | $9.20 |
| 2024 | $3,121.2M | $528.6M | 16.9% | $405.0M | $10.55 |
| 2025 | $3,498.4M | $633.5M | 18.1% | $484.2M | $12.87 |

Three-year revenue CAGR is 11.0%; operating income CAGR 14.4%; margin expanded roughly 150bps. FY2025's decomposition per MD&A — sales +12% comprising +9% organic and +3% acquisition, operating income +20% comprising +17% organic — is high-quality growth, not roll-up arithmetic.

**Segment quality is uneven in an important way.** Defense Electronics lifted margin 260bps to 27.3% and contributed $53M of the $105M consolidated operating income increase from a base of only 29% of sales. Naval & Power margin *declined* 20bps to 15.4% despite 18% sales growth, on first-year purchase accounting for the I&C Solutions acquisition, mix, and higher R&D.

**Orders and backlog.** FY2025 orders of $4,053.7M against $3,498.4M of sales is 1.16x book-to-bill; backlog closed at $4,076.5M, +18%. By segment: Naval & Power 1.36x (orders +23%), Aerospace & Industrial 1.06x, and **Defense Electronics 0.96x** with orders down 8% and backlog flat at $991.4M. Q1 2026 partially repaired this — DE orders +18% to $278M on $256M of sales (~1.09x) and margin 28.1% — but FY2026 DE guidance of +4–6% revenue with 0–20bps of margin expansion makes the highest-margin business the slowest contributor to the 2026 plan.

**Backlog coverage deserves precision.** CW defines backlog as remaining performance obligations including deferred revenue — unusually clean disclosure, since backlog and RPO are the same measure. Roughly 90% of the ~$4.3B backlog at 3/31/26 converts over 36 months, or about $1.29B per year, against FY2026 revenue guidance of $3.74–3.80B. Backlog therefore underwrites roughly one-third of a forward year, consistent with 49% of FY2025 revenue (48% in Q1'26) being recognized point-in-time. Backlog is cancellable: the U.S. Government may unilaterally modify or cancel, and commercial customers may terminate for convenience. This is normal for a half book-and-ship business with a recurring aftermarket, but it does mean backlog is a weaker downside cushion than the "record backlog" framing implies.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow $643.4M against capex of $89.7M gives FCF of $553.7M (15.8% of revenue, 114% of net income). Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $5.7M, an improvement from negative $38.8M a year earlier; Q1 is structurally cash-consumptive (receivables consumed $67.4M, inventory $28.9M, payables/accruals $60.9M), and annualizing it is meaningless. FY2026 guidance is $580–600M of FCF at ~105% conversion on capex of $110–120M, roughly 30% above 2025. As a sensitivity rather than a forecast: FY2025 FCF plus mid-single-digit earnings growth less the guided capex step-up lands near the low end of the range, so guidance requires working-capital improvement beyond the Q1 pace.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/26: cash $343.4M, total debt $957.5M all fixed-rate at 3.8% weighted average, net debt $614.1M. Against FY2026E EBITDA of roughly $837M (guided operating income ~$720M plus ~$117M D&A), net leverage is about 0.73x. The nearest maturity is $200M of 4.24% notes due December 2026 — immaterial against $721M of undrawn revolver. Goodwill and intangibles of ~$2.2B are 43% of total assets; the October 2025 impairment test found all reporting units' fair values substantially in excess of carrying amounts. The Altman Z-Score of 2.46 reflects asset-turnover of 0.67 and low equity/liabilities mechanically depressed by that goodwill balance, not solvency stress; on cash and coverage measures the company is conservatively financed. Pension is a net benefit ($340.2M prepaid asset, no 2026 contributions expected), though the expected return assumption was cut to 7.0% from 7.25% and the discount rate to 5.35% from 5.55%, modest future EPS headwinds.

**Earnings quality.** Q1'26 GAAP diluted EPS $3.46 versus adjusted $3.48 — a $0.02 gap. There is essentially no non-GAAP inflation here, which is unusual for a serial acquirer and a genuine positive. The 10-K and 10-Q both state no significant changes in estimated contract costs across 2023, 2024, 2025 or Q1'26. Effective tax rate of 18.7% in Q1 benefited from stock-comp tax benefits, a price-dependent and low-quality tailwind against guided FY2026 tax of 21.5%.

**Financial Health: 7.8/10** — confirms the prior trajectory. Deducted for the goodwill concentration, the unverified May 20, 2026 8-K creating a direct financial obligation, and a capex step-up that consumes part of the FCF growth.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance (raised May 6): sales $3,740–3,795M (+7–8%), operating margin 19.0–19.2% (+40–60bps), adjusted diluted EPS $14.90–15.30 (+13–16%), FCF $580–600M, diluted shares 37.1M.

End-market guidance is more mixed than the headline: Aerospace Defense +11–13%, Commercial Aerospace +10–12%, Power & Process +13–15%, Naval Defense +6–8%, **Ground Defense −4 to −6%**, General Industrial flat. General Industrial was flat in FY2025 ($412.1M versus $410.5M) and is guided flat again — 11% of revenue contributing nothing. Two of six end markets are flat-to-declining, and the highest-margin segment carries the slowest plan.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current quarter +1.5% over 90 days, current year +1.2%, next year +1.8%, with seven covering analysts. This is not an inflection signal; it is a well-guided company delivering tight, low-magnitude beats — the surprise record over four quarters is +0.3%, +0.3%, −0.2%, +2.2%. That pattern raises confidence in the $15.24/$17.11 consensus path and simultaneously argues against expecting upside surprises large enough to re-rate a 47x stock.

The medium-term growth case rests on three legs. Naval shipbuilding has bipartisan support, supplemental maritime industrial base funding, and CW content on Virginia, Columbia, Ford and SSN(X) — the most visible and contractually anchored piece. Allied defense budgets offer real runway given direct foreign military sales are only ~10% of revenue against NATO's ten-year 5.0%-of-GDP commitment; CW under-indexes to that spending today. Commercial nuclear aftermarket benefits from a structural dynamic the 10-K names explicitly: competitors abandoning the nuclear-grade market as qualification costs rise, combined with 80-year subsequent license renewals extending original 40-year lives.

Nuclear new-build is the fourth leg and the one the market is capitalizing most aggressively. The policy stack is real — May 2025 executive orders targeting 400GW by 2050, NRC licensing reform, 5GW of uprates, 10 new large reactors by 2030 — and CW cites potential for 20–25 Central and Eastern European reactors plus 10 proposed U.S. reactors. But CW participates only as a component supplier to reactor vendors; the $80B U.S. government–Westinghouse arrangement CW describes in its 10-K is one to which CW is not a party, and no CW revenue is attributed to it. Data-center electricity demand from hyperscalers is a demand driver for utilities and developers, not a CW customer relationship. Converting this into revenue requires reactors to actually be ordered and built, with SMR steady-state production placed in the middle of the next decade.

**Growth Prospects: 7.2/10** (from 7.5). Mid-teens EPS growth with a broadening order book is solid, but revisions are modest, two end markets are flat-to-down, and the segment with the best economics has the weakest plan.

## Valuation Assessment

Reproducing the arithmetic at $716.66 with 37.1M guided diluted shares:

- Market cap ≈ $26.86B; EV = 26.86 + 0.958 − 0.343 = **$27.48B**
- Trailing P/E on FY25 GAAP EPS $12.87 = **55.7x** (the 52.5x field in the profile data implies a stale reference price near $675 and should be disregarded)
- Forward P/E on FY26 consensus $15.24 = **47.0x**; on guided adjusted midpoint $15.10 = 47.5x
- P/E on FY27 consensus $17.11 = **41.9x**
- EV/FY26E EBITDA (~$837M) = **32.8x**
- FCF yield on guided $590M = **2.20%**
- Dividend $0.96 annualized = **0.13%**; guided FY26 dividends ~$37M = 6.3% of FCF

PEG on forward earnings: 47.0x divided by the guided 13–16% EPS growth is roughly 3.1–3.6x. On next-year consensus growth of 12.3% ($15.24 → $17.11), PEG is about 3.8x. There is no reading of this that is cheap.

Peer context cuts partly the other way. The A&D comparables — ATI 65.9x, ESLT 73.9x, BWXT 51.9x, FTAI 48.1x, TXT 17.9x — carry a median trailing P/E of 51.9x, so CW's 55.7x trailing sits close to cohort median. But that cohort is heterogeneous and itself re-rated on nuclear and defense-budget enthusiasm; taking a re-rated group as the fair-value anchor for a member of the same group is circular. The more informative internal benchmark is CW's own history: roughly 16x adjusted EPS in 2021 versus ~48x today.

The re-rating is quantifiable from the proxy. Company-selected adjusted EPS went $7.23 (2021) → $13.23 (2025), 1.83x, a 16.3% CAGR. Total shareholder return on an assumed $100 reached $483.25 versus $220.22 for the peer group. Dividing, roughly 2.6x of the ~4.8x return came from multiple expansion. (TSR includes dividends, which at a 0.1–0.3% yield makes the distortion de minimis.) Operating execution has been excellent; the share price has additionally embedded a different terminal multiple.

The valuation is therefore best understood as paying roughly 47x forward earnings for a business with a 2.2% cash yield, 7–8% revenue growth, and an embedded call option on nuclear new-build that is presently about 1.5% of revenue with a mid-2030s production strike. The arithmetic of reversion frames the asymmetry: 30x forward on unchanged FY2026 earnings implies roughly $455; 35x FY2027 consensus implies roughly $600. Neither is a forecast, but both are well inside the range of ordinary multiple movement for a stock that expanded from 16x to 48x in five years.

Analyst targets do not resolve this. The distribution — low $768, mean $822, median $821, high $870 — sits entirely above spot, implying 7% to 21% upside. At a 47x forward multiple, however, targets and price reflect the same growth extrapolation rather than independent confirmation, and the cluster was largely set before the July 29 selloff. Treating a consensus target as a floor is appropriate for cheap inflection stocks; it is not appropriate here.

**Valuation: 3.5/10** (from ~4.5). The reduction is not a function of price action — the stock is down about 3.5% since the last review — but of three pieces of newly quantified evidence: the re-rating decomposition, the ~1.5%-of-revenue sizing of the new-build option, and the thinner-than-advertised backlog coverage that weakens the visibility premium.

## Competitive Landscape

CW's moat is program-specific incumbency: qualification barriers, sole-source designations, nuclear-grade certification, and content on "over 400 platforms and 3,000 programs." The 10-K's own framing is candid — it competes "primarily based on technology and pricing" against companies with "higher sales volumes and greater financial, technological, research and development, human, and marketing resources." That is a description of narrow, defensible positions rather than structural scale advantage.

Trajectory is the more useful lens, and it is mixed-to-improving. Positive: consolidated book-to-bill of 1.16x in FY2025 rising to ~1.3x in Q1'26 with mid-teens order growth across all three segments; Naval & Power orders +23% and backlog +27%; DE margin at 28.1% in Q1; nuclear aftermarket strengthened by competitor exit from the qualified supplier base; a testable 10-K claim that CW has outgrown the base U.S. defense budget over 20 years, aided by absorbing work primes outsource in restrained budget cycles. Negative: DE orders fell 8% in FY2025 to 0.96x book-to-bill with backlog flat, and DE is guided to the slowest growth and least margin expansion in 2026; Ground Defense revenue guided down 4–6%; General Industrial flat for a second year; Process pressured by tariffs and reduced capital spending. Consolidated margin expansion to 19%+ therefore leans on the two lower-margin segments (A&I +100–120bps, N&P +40–60bps) while the 27–28% business plateaus. If DE margin has peaked, the consolidated margin story gets harder after 2026.

Commercial aerospace (13% of 2026E sales, ~90% OEM, roughly 60/40 narrowbody/widebody) is levered to Boeing and Airbus rates, with growing Airbus content in actuation and high-temperature engine sensors and FAA 25-hour flight-data-recorder mandates as product-specific drivers. Boeing labor stoppages are named as a realized risk in the 10-K.

**Competitive Position: 7.5/10** (from 8.2). The trim reflects the DE order and margin-plateau evidence and the recognition that the nuclear franchise's durable component is aftermarket, not new-build, rather than any deterioration in CW's realized positions.

## Risk Assessment

**Valuation compression is the dominant risk.** At 47x forward and a 2.2% FCF yield, the equity carries no cushion for disappointment. CW fell 6.8% on July 29 without company news, which is the kind of mechanism that operates in real time when the growth extrapolation is challenged.

**Nuclear timeline slippage.** A dedicated risk factor warns that delays in SMR development could adversely affect results. The 10-K places SMR prototypes from 2026, initial production orders by the end of this decade, steady state mid-next-decade; subsea pumping production orders mid-next-decade; AP1000 construction in Poland, Bulgaria and the U.S. before the end of the decade. Timeline extension does not damage current earnings but directly attacks the multiple.

**Fixed-price contract exposure.** Defense revenues are primarily fixed-price, with CW absorbing the majority of cost overruns; 51% of FY2025 revenue (52% in Q1'26) is recognized over time on cost-to-cost input measures with cumulative catch-up. No significant estimate changes have been recorded through Q1'26 — this is latent rather than manifesting — but it is the mechanism by which a naval production ramp could surprise negatively.

**Defense Electronics goodwill.** $711.7M of goodwill sits in the segment with the weakest order trend. A genuine deterioration would produce a large non-cash charge affecting net income, book equity and covenant headroom (debt-to-capitalization based), though not operating cash flow.

**Government budget process.** FY2025 ran under a historic full-year continuing resolution and the longest shutdown in modern history; FY2026 began under a CR before February 2026 appropriations at levels consistent with FY25. The upside case leans on $150B of OBBBA-approved funding ($113B anticipated for FY2026). The Department of War has not released a future-year defense plan through FY30, reducing long-term visibility.

**USMCA renewal in 2026 and tariffs** — a named, dated risk with manufacturing in Mexico and Canada. A&I 2026 margin guidance explicitly cites restructuring savings outpacing China tariff impacts, so tariffs are a quantified offset rather than an abstraction.

**Other:** a $40M surety bond posted to the NRC for eventual Cheswick, PA decommissioning; Price-Anderson indemnification may not cover all liabilities; pension assumption cuts as modest EPS headwinds; hedge-fund holder count down from 59 to 52 portfolios between Q4'25 and Q1'26 (a count, not a dollar-weighted measure).

**Unresolved:** the 8-K filed May 20, 2026 carries Items 1.01, 1.02, 2.03, 8.01 and 9.01. The combination is most consistent with a replacement or refinancing of the ~$750M revolver, but the document was not retrievable. Nothing about post-Q1 capital structure or M&A intent should be asserted from it.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (291 contracts across four expirations), so read it directionally rather than precisely. Near-term ATM IV of 47.5% against 35% one-year realized vol is rich by 12.5 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (47.5% at 20 days versus 42.8% at six months) with an implied move of ±9.3% on the August 21 expiry. That configuration is consistent with an imminent Q2 earnings event — CW reported Q1 on May 6 and has historically printed Q2 in early August. Put/call skew is 4.1% at one month (elevated put demand), flattens to 0.5% at three months and inverts modestly at six. Put/call open interest is 1.00 (down from 1.09) while put/call volume is 0.04, so the standing structure is balanced and current flow is negligible.

Interpretation: the options market is pricing a near-dated event with elevated but not extreme downside demand, and is not pricing sustained stress beyond it. For a holder, that argues against adding size ahead of the print; for a buyer, waiting for the post-print volatility crush is the better-timed entry. Short interest of 2.2% of float and 2.4 days to cover means no crowded bearish positioning to squeeze.

**Risk Factors: 5.5/10** (from 6.5). The balance sheet is not the risk. The reduction reflects a near-dated binary event on a multiple that leaves no room for error, the DE goodwill/order combination, and the unverified May 20 financing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $875 (+22%)

Naval shipbuilding and allied defense spending compound at the guided pace while CW converts its under-indexed ~10% direct foreign military sales exposure into NATO's ten-year budget ramp. Defense Electronics' 2025 order weakness proves to be timing, as Q1's +18% orders and 28.1% margin suggest, and DE resumes double-digit growth in 2027 with margin above 28%. An AP1000 order — excluded from 2026 guidance — is booked with disclosed content value, converting the nuclear option from narrative into orders and validating the multiple through a step-change in growth rate and duration rather than requiring compression. Management deploys the $2.9B of covenant capacity into an accretive nuclear or defense-electronics acquisition after two years of restraint. FY2027 EPS reaches $17.50–18.00 and the market pays 49–50x: $860–900.

### Bear Case — $520 (−27%)

The market re-rates long-duration industrial optionality lower. AP1000 orders slip past the decade, SMR production stays mid-2030s, and investors mark the nuclear option closer to its current ~1.5%-of-revenue contribution. Defense Electronics book-to-bill falls back below 1.0x, Ground Defense declines beyond the guided 4–6%, and consolidated margin expansion stalls in 2027 once the two lower-margin segments have delivered their catch-up. A single adverse cumulative catch-up adjustment on a fixed-price naval program breaks the clean contract-estimate record. Multiple compresses to roughly 30x FY2027 consensus: $515–550. The mechanism does not require earnings to fall — 30x on $17.11 is $513 — which is what makes the asymmetry unfavorable at 47x.

### Base Case — $765 (+7%)

FY2026 delivers within guidance ($15.10 adjusted EPS, 19.1% margin, $590M FCF), FY2027 approaches the $17.11 consensus, and the multiple compresses modestly from 47x to roughly 45x forward. 44.7x on $17.11 = $765. The stock earns most of its earnings growth and gives back a little multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Own the position; do not add at $716.66. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the score has moved from 6.7 to 6.2 — a decline driven by valuation, management-signal, and risk-cushion dimensions, not by fundamental deterioration. Fundamentals confirmed positively this cycle (Q1 beat-and-raise, 1.3x book-to-bill, backlog to $4.3B, guidance raised on all four metrics). The price is 3.5% lower than at the prior review, which improves risk/reward at the margin but not enough to change the call at 47x forward.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q2 2026 print (expected within days, priced for ±9.3%) and FY2027 guidance in February 2027.

**Targets:** Base $765 (+6.8%). Bull $875 (+22.1%). Bear $520 (−27.4%). Analyst range $768–870 with a mean of $822 sits above base case and was largely set before the July 29 derating.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
1. Forward multiple compresses below 35x on FY2027 consensus — roughly $600 or lower — with book-to-bill still above 1.1x and FCF conversion above 100%. That is where the growth is paid for rather than extrapolated.
2. A firm AP1000 or SMR production order booked with disclosed CW content value, converting the option to contracted revenue.
3. Execution of the $100M price-limited repurchase tranche. Management transacting where it has declined to transact is the cleanest available signal on internal fair-value assessment.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
1. Defense Electronics book-to-bill below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters, particularly with any softening in goodwill-test language against the $711.7M balance.
2. Any disclosed significant change in estimated contract costs on a naval or ground fixed-price program — breaking a four-year clean record.
3. FY2026 FCF guidance cut below $580M or conversion guided under 100%, or loss of the 19.0–19.2% margin guide.

**Reassessment:** immediately following the Q2 2026 release, with particular attention to segment orders, whether the $100M tranche transacted in Q2, and the contents of the May 20 financing 8-K.

## Conclusion

The operating record is close to unimpeachable: 11% three-year revenue CAGR with 9 points organic in the latest year, 150bps of margin expansion to 18.1%, 114% FCF conversion, a $0.02 gap between GAAP and adjusted quarterly EPS, no contract-cost estimate changes in four years, 0.73x net leverage on all-fixed 3.8% debt whose fair value is below carrying value, and a raised guide across every metric. The nuclear aftermarket franchise — protected by qualification barriers and strengthened by competitor exit — is a genuinely attractive, currently-earning asset.

Price is the issue. Roughly 47x forward earnings and a 2.2% FCF yield for 7–8% revenue growth requires the nuclear new-build cycle to arrive on schedule, and that cycle is about 1.5% of revenue today with its production strike in the middle of the next decade. Roughly 2.6x of the last five years' 4.8x return came from multiple expansion. Management's own behavior — a $60M anti-dilution buyback minimum, a $100M opportunistic tranche gated behind a price limit that has not triggered in the $630–685 range, no open-market insider purchases since at least March, and the CEO's full 5,000-share plan executed within four days of eligibility at $722–758 — is consistent with a team that believes in the business and does not believe the shares are cheap. That is the correct posture for a shareholder: hold the compounding, and wait for either a materially better price or a booked new-build order before paying more.
