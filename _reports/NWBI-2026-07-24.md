**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Northwest Bancshares is the $2.3 billion holding company for Northwest Bank, a 130-year-old Pennsylvania-chartered savings bank now headquartered in Columbus, Ohio, operating 161 community-banking offices across Pennsylvania, Western New York, Northeastern Ohio, and Indiana. The investment case rests on two features that distinguish NWBI from most regional peers: a genuinely granular, low-cost deposit base (average account size under $20,000, cost of total deposits 1.48% in Q1 2026, only ~14.5% uninsured), and net interest margin that is expanding rather than compressing (3.42% in Q4 2024 to 3.70% in Q1 2026). The bank posted a record 2025 and a clean Q1 2026 (EPS $0.34, ROA 1.22%, ROTCE 13.1%).

The central analytical question is whether the 2025 credit-quality scare — classified loans rising to $498M (3.81% of loans) from $279M (2.49%) a year earlier, provision expense more than doubling to $56.8M — represents fresh organic deterioration or an accounting artifact of the July 2025 Penns Woods acquisition. The Q1 2026 10-Q supports the benign reading: nonperforming loans fell 15% to $91.5M (0.70% of loans), 90+ day delinquencies dropped to 0.34% from 0.52%, and net charge-offs eased to 0.16% annualized. Much of the 2025 spike was one-time CECL Day-1 provisioning (~$20.6M) plus acquired classified loans. Open-market purchases in spring 2026 by the CFO, a director, and the Chief Risk Officer — the executive closest to the loan book — reinforce the view that the credit stress is contained.

The problem for a fresh position is price. NWBI has appreciated 16.5% over the trailing three months to $15.29, sits above the $15.00 consensus mean and median target, and trades within reach of its $15.72 52-week high. On normalized earnings of roughly $1.36–1.38, the stock trades near 11x forward — reasonable for a bank showing margin expansion, but no longer cheap, and the estimate-revision trend has flattened (current-quarter revised -0.4% over 30 days). The ~5.2% dividend, backed by a 30-year streak and a normalized 58.8% payout, is the durable return here. We rate NWBI a HOLD on income grounds; the appreciation case largely played out over the last quarter.

## Company Overview

Northwest Bank, founded in 1896, is a traditional community/commercial bank operating one reportable segment. Total assets stand at $16.9 billion with a ~$13.1B loan book split roughly 55% personal banking (residential mortgages $3.0B, home equity $1.5B, vehicle $2.5B, consumer) and 45% commercial (commercial real estate $3.2B, CRE owner-occupied $0.4B, C&I $2.7B). Revenue is ~81% net interest income, with the balance from service charges, trust/wealth management, and SBA/mortgage banking.

Management is executing a deliberate mix shift toward higher-yielding C&I lending (yields ~6.6–7%) and building out a de novo consumer franchise in Columbus, Ohio, a faster-growth metro relative to its slow-growth Rust Belt core. The July 2025 all-stock acquisition of Penns Woods ($234M, 18.2M shares, adding $2.2B assets and $1.8B loans) provided scale. The deposit franchise ($14.2B in deposits, 22% noninterest-bearing, average balance $19,259) is the clearest competitive asset and the reason the bank funds itself so cheaply.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings power.** FY2025 GAAP net income was $126M ($0.92 diluted EPS), up from $100M ($0.79) in 2024, but the GAAP figure was suppressed by $42.8M of merger/restructuring charges and the $20.6M CECL Day-1 provision. Adjusted 2025 EPS was $1.26. Q1 2026's clean $0.34 annualizes to roughly $1.36, which is the honest read on run-rate earnings power. On that basis the stock trades near 11x, versus the 16.4x trailing P/E the depressed GAAP number implies.

**Margin trajectory.** Net interest margin (FTE) expanded from 3.42% (Q4 2024) to 3.69% (Q4 2025) to 3.70% (Q1 2026), driven by the loan-mix shift to commercial, Penns Woods purchase-accounting accretion (~$4.6M/quarter), and declining interest-bearing deposit costs (2.27% to 1.89%). FY2025 net interest income of $525M was up 21% YoY on total revenue of ~$655M (+17%). Expanding NIM is unusual in the current regional-bank environment and is the strongest single positive in the numbers. Two caveats attach: the accretion tailwind fades over the coming quarters, and the bank is modestly asset-sensitive, so Fed cuts pressure NIM from the other direction.

**Capital and balance sheet.** Regulatory capital is solid: CET1 12.32%, total capital 15.33%, Tier 1 leverage 9.25% at the holding company. Tangible common equity/tangible assets ~8.6%, with tangible book value of $9.63/share (Dec 2025). AOCI carries a ~$79M unrealized securities loss, plus another ~$79M of unrealized losses in the HTM portfolio not marked through equity, manageable given a 21.9% liquidity ratio and $4.4B FHLB plus $1.7B Fed borrowing capacity. There is no forced-sale dynamic here.

A note on standardized screens: NWBI's Altman Z-Score near 0.2 and D/E ~8x will trip a distress flag. This is a false positive. The Altman model was built for industrial firms; banks are structurally leveraged, and NWBI is well-capitalized on every regulatory measure. This should be discounted entirely.

**Capital allocation.** Operating cash flow is solid. The $0.20/quarter dividend marks the 126th consecutive quarterly payout (a 30+ year streak) and yields ~5.2%. Management authorized a new $50M buyback in April 2026 — 2% of market cap, and the first meaningful repurchase authorization in over a decade, replacing a dormant 2012 plan. Combined with the dividend, this signals confidence that the post-acquisition balance sheet is de-risked enough to return capital.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth story is modest and cyclically dependent. The bank's slow-growth footprint (PA/NY/OH/IN) limits underlying loan demand, so growth is being manufactured through three levers: the C&I mix shift, the Columbus consumer buildout, and M&A. Estimate revisions have been positive over 90 days but flat over 30 days — current-year consensus moved from $1.31 to $1.38 (+4.8%) over 90 days but is unchanged (-0.1%) over the past month. This is directionally favorable but well short of the >30% revision velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection warranting a growth premium.

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in consensus: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter (Q1 2026) beating by 14.7%. Management guides to "another year of record revenue" for FY2026 with a 23–25% effective tax rate. Next-year consensus of $1.44 implies mid-single-digit EPS growth off the run rate — reasonable, not exciting, and vulnerable to Fed cuts given the bank's asset-sensitivity (+5.3% net income for +100bp, -4.8% for -100bp).

The growth thesis is bank-arithmetic durable but faces two known fades: purchase-accounting accretion rolling off and a possible rate-cut headwind. We score growth prospects at 5.8, above midpoint on the NIM-expansion and record-revenue trajectory but capped by the flat 30-day revisions and structural footprint limits.

## Valuation Assessment

The peer set for a $2.3B Rust Belt community bank trades broadly at 10–13x forward earnings and 1.0–1.4x tangible book. NWBI at ~11x normalized EPS and ~1.6x tangible book is fairly valued to slightly rich on book, fair on earnings. The dividend yield is a genuine support and sits above most peers, reflecting the elevated payout.

The valuation discipline here is important. The stock now trades above the consensus mean/median target and sits within 3% of its 52-week high. Analyst targets on a bank showing single-digit earnings growth and flattening revisions are not "floors" to be dismissed — that guardrail applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and sub-30x multiples where consensus lags. NWBI has neither the revision velocity nor the trough-earnings profile that would make current consensus stale. The high target of $16.00 implies only ~5% upside; the mean implies the stock has slightly overshot.

Cheapness in banks has been a poor standalone signal, and there is no specific catalyst here to argue the market is mispricing NWBI upward. The re-rating from the low-$12s (where the stock traded through much of 2025) has already captured the credit-inflection narrative and the buyback announcement. What remains is a fully-valued regional bank with an attractive yield. We score valuation at 5.2 — no asymmetric upside at this price, but the dividend and normalized-earnings support prevent a lower mark.

## Competitive Landscape

NWBI's moat is its funding base. A granular, low-cost liability structure became distinctly more valuable after the 2023 regional-bank deposit runs, which punished banks with concentrated, uninsured, rate-sensitive deposits. A 1.48% blended deposit cost while still expanding margin is evidence this franchise is holding up.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving. The bank is gaining share in Columbus (a new-market bet, unproven) and shifting toward higher-yielding commercial lending, which improves revenue mix at the cost of a structurally higher credit-loss profile. The strategy mirrors the standard community-bank playbook — M&A for scale, C&I yield reach, metro expansion — and the risk is that the yield-chasing arrives late in the credit cycle, which is what the 2025 classified-loan surge reflected. The margin expansion is partly durable (mix shift, deposit repricing) and partly transient (Penns Woods accretion). We score competitive position at 5.8: the deposit franchise is a real advantage, but the growth levers are common to the peer group and the credit-cycle timing is a genuine concern.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial credit quality** is the central thread. Classified loans still rose ~$44M QoQ in Q1 2026 even as NPLs fell 15% — a mixed signal. The bull read is that the classified increase reflects acquired Penns Woods loans and the CECL Day-1 mechanics, while the forward-looking metrics (NPLs, delinquencies, charge-offs) are all improving. The bear read is that the C&I push structurally raises loss content and classified loans are a leading indicator that hasn't yet peaked. The next two quarters of classified-loan trend are the key data point.

**CRE concentration** is ~$3.2B, geographically concentrated in Pennsylvania (47.5%), New York (24.3%), and Ohio (14.1%). The property mix includes cyclically sensitive segments: non-owner-occupied office (8.1%), nursing homes (7.8%), retail (12.6%), and multifamily 5+ (11.8%). Office and nursing-home exposure in slow-growth markets warrant monitoring; CRE nonaccruals were the largest single problem category ($46M at Q1 2026, down from $56M).

**Rate sensitivity** cuts against the current environment. The bank is modestly asset-sensitive, so the Fed easing path is a swing factor. A meaningful cutting cycle would pressure NIM alongside the fading accretion tailwind.

**Integration and accretion.** Penns Woods is fully integrated with goodwill finalized at $63.3M, but goodwill/intangibles total ~$484M against $1.9B equity, and the accretion that is currently flattering NIM will fade.

**Governance.** Total shareholder return lagged the peer group over the proxy measurement window (~129 vs. ~167), a pay-for-performance concern, and there was a recent accounting-leadership change (new Principal Accounting Officer signing the Q1 2026 10-Q). Neither is a red flag on its own.

We score risk factors at 5.3: credit is improving but not resolved, the classified-loan QoQ rise is a live watch item, and the asset-sensitivity/accretion-fade combination is an underappreciated earnings headwind if rates fall.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning offers little edge here. ATM implied volatility collapsed from an anomalous 558.4% (a thin-options data artifact, not informative) to a benign 21.8% as of late July, with a negligible put/call open-interest ratio of 0.03. This is a quiet, low-conviction options market with no evidence of directional positioning ahead of a catalyst, consistent with a stable, income-oriented regional bank. Short interest is moderate at 6.1% of float (5.2 days to cover), neither a squeeze setup nor a strong bearish signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($17–18).** Credit continues to normalize — the classified-loan QoQ rise reverses next quarter, NPLs stay below 0.75%, and charge-offs remain benign. NIM holds near 3.70% as the deposit-cost tailwind and C&I mix offset accretion fade. The Columbus consumer buildout and continued C&I growth push EPS toward $1.45–1.50, and the $50M buyback plus 5%+ dividend deliver a mid-teens total return. Multiple re-rates toward 12–13x and 1.7–1.8x tangible book on a cleaner credit story.

**Bear Case ($12–13, ~15–20% downside).** Classified loans prove to be a leading indicator; office and nursing-home CRE deteriorate; provision expense re-accelerates and clips the $1.36 run rate. The Fed cuts more than expected, compressing the asset-sensitive NIM just as Penns Woods accretion fades, and normalized EPS drifts toward $1.20. The stock de-rates back toward tangible book and the low-$12s where it traded for much of 2025, with the dividend providing the only support.

**Base Case ($15–16).** Credit stays contained but not pristine, NIM eases modestly as accretion fades and offsets deposit-cost improvement, and EPS lands around $1.38–1.44. The stock trades sideways-to-modestly-higher, delivering primarily its ~5.2% yield plus low-single-digit price appreciation. This is the most likely outcome and is roughly where consensus targets sit.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income-oriented HOLD with a 12-month horizon. The recurring return is the dividend, well-covered at a normalized 58.8% payout and backed by a 30-year streak; price appreciation from current levels is likely limited given the stock sits above consensus targets.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- A pullback toward the low-$13s / tangible book that restores a meaningful yield-plus-appreciation setup.
- Two consecutive quarters of falling classified loans (not just NPLs), confirming the credit inflection is complete rather than partial.
- Estimate revisions re-accelerating (current-year consensus moving above $1.45 with 30-day momentum turning positive).

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Classified loans breaching ~$550M or NPLs turning back above 1.0% of loans, signaling the C&I/CRE credit push is deteriorating organically.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration where normalized payout exceeds ~75%, or provision expense re-accelerating toward the 2025 pace.
- Confirmation of an aggressive Fed cutting cycle compressing NIM below ~3.50% with accretion exhausted.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (late July 2026 print), with particular attention to the classified-loan trend and NIM trajectory as accretion begins to fade.

## Conclusion

Northwest Bancshares is a well-run community bank with a genuinely valuable deposit franchise, rare NIM expansion, and a credit picture that inflected favorably in Q1 2026 — a shift a static data pull would miss, and one that insiders including the Chief Risk Officer validated with open-market buying near $14. The trajectory of the underlying business is stable-to-improving. But the equity has already re-rated to a level above the consensus target and near its 52-week high, and offers limited appreciation upside on normalized ~11x earnings that are modestly exposed to Fed cuts and a fading accretion tailwind. The classified-loan QoQ rise remains an unresolved watch item.

The prior two reports scored NWBI 5.9 and 5.8 with a HOLD; our 5.75 continues that trajectory, with the small step down reflecting the price appreciation into a full valuation rather than any fundamental deterioration. We rate NWBI a **HOLD on income grounds** — own it for the covered yield and the credit-normalization optionality, not for price appreciation at this level. New capital is better deployed on a pullback toward tangible book.
