## Executive Summary

Ralph Lauren has delivered one of the cleanest execution records in apparel: FY2026 (ended March 28, 2026) net revenue of $8,114.5M, up 14.6% reported and 11.8% in constant currency, with gross margin of 69.9% (+130bp), reported diluted EPS of $15.11 (+30.1%) and adjusted diluted EPS of $16.59 (+34.6%). Q1 FY2027 extended the run — revenue $1,959.8M (+14.0%), operating margin 17.5% (+160bp), adjusted EPS $4.59 (+21.8%) — and management raised the FY2027 constant-currency margin-expansion outlook to +60-80bp. The stock has compounded 32% over twelve months. The mechanism is straightforward and verifiable in the filings: average unit retail up 15% across the direct-to-consumer network with lower-than-planned promotions, mix shift toward Asia (26% of revenue, 33.5% Q1 segment margin), and a licensing stub earning an 86.9% operating margin.

Three things argue against paying up here. First, management guides FY2027 constant-currency revenue growth of 5-6% on a comparable-week basis versus FY2026's 11.8% — roughly a halving, with only ~1 point of 53rd-week help and a 50-100bp FX drag. Second, the quality of the Q1 beat is lower than the headline: the 10-Q discloses ~15 points of the 22% North America wholesale growth came from a resumed luxury account plus a Q4 FY2026 shipment shift, and ~5 points of Europe's wholesale growth was pulled forward from Q2 FY2027. Adjusting for both puts underlying growth nearer 11.5-12%, and the Europe piece mechanically reverses this quarter. Third, Europe — 31% of revenue at a 27.8% FY2026 operating margin — has decelerated from +6% comps in FY2026 to +1% in Q1, with brick-and-mortar flat, AUR growth down from high- to low-single-digit, and segment margin flat only because of a 40bp FX tailwind.

At $387.46 on 59.6M total shares (37.7M Class A plus 21.9M Class B), equity value is ~$23.1B, not the $14.6B implied by Class A alone. That works out to 24.4x trailing reported EPS of $15.87, 22.3x trailing adjusted EPS of $17.41, 20.5x FY2027 consensus of $18.90 and 18.6x FY2028 consensus of $20.88 — against a peer median trailing P/E of 15.5x on a peer set where LULU sits 47% below its high and VFC 33% below. The premium is earned; it is not a mispricing. Base case $425 (+9.7% plus a 1.0% dividend) against a bear case of $290 (-25%) produces a probability-weighted return in the mid-single digits. HOLD, with a defined add zone in the low $340s.

## Company Overview

Ralph Lauren designs and distributes luxury lifestyle products across apparel, handbags, footwear and accessories, fragrances, home and hospitality. The brand architecture is tiered: Ralph Lauren Collection, Purple Label, Double RL and Home at the luxury end (Collection and Purple Label made predominantly in Italy); Polo Ralph Lauren as the volume core; Lauren Ralph Lauren at accessible price points through department stores; and Chaps licensed out to 5 Star Apparel.

The company owns no manufacturing. It contracts with roughly 300 suppliers, none exceeding 5% of products by dollar value, with 96% of production outside the U.S. — 21% Vietnam, 16% Cambodia, 11% India. As of March 28, 2026 the footprint comprised 594 directly-operated stores (287 full-price Ralph Lauren, 307 outlets, ~4.1M sq ft), 644 concession shop-within-shops of which 615 are in Asia, ~9,500 full-price wholesale doors, ~13,800 shop-within-shops inside wholesale partners and ~110 third-party digital partners.

One structural detail drives the segment margin picture: in concession shop-within-shops Ralph Lauren retains inventory ownership until the sale to the consumer and the salespeople are generally its own employees. Asia's 615 concessions therefore book retail-like gross economics, which explains a meaningful part of Asia's 27.4% FY2026 and 33.5% Q1 FY2027 segment margin.

FY2026 segment mix: North America 41% of revenue ($3,329.6M, 21.8% margin), Europe 31% ($2,538.9M, 27.8%), Asia 26% ($2,103.5M, 27.4%), licensing and other 2% ($142.5M, 86.9%). Approximately 59% of revenue is earned outside the U.S. Corporate expense of $832.5M sits unallocated and rose $77.1M year over year.

Governance is concentrated. Ralph Lauren and family entities hold ~85% of voting power through Class B shares carrying ten votes each; RL is a controlled company. Ralph Lauren, 86, remains Executive Chairman and Chief Creative Officer. Patrice Louvet has been CEO since July 2017; Justin Picicci CFO since May 2024; the COO (Robert Ranftl) and Chief Product & Merchandising Officer (Halide Alagöz) roles were both filled in spring 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** Revenue has compounded from $6,443.6M (FY2023) to $8,114.5M (FY2026), with the acceleration concentrated in the last two years: +6.7% then +14.6%. FX contributed $201.9M of FY2026 revenue (~2.9 points of the 14.6%), $163.4M of gross profit and $81.8M of operating income — approximately 6.9% of FY2026 operating income came from currency translation, and FY2027 guidance flips that to a 50-100bp revenue headwind with roughly neutral margin impact.

FY2026 gross margin of 69.9% (+130bp) breaks down as ~30bp FX and ~100bp from AUR and product elevation, net of tariffs and higher non-cotton input costs. SG&A improved 70bp to 53.9% of revenue. Restructuring and other charges were $118.1M versus $57.8M, of which $83.9M was the Next Generation Transformation (NGT) program. Adding those charges back gives an adjusted operating margin of 16.0% versus ~14.0% in FY2025 — about 200bp of expansion. Note the proxy's company-selected "Adjusted Operating Profit Margin" is 15.5% for FY2026 and 14.3% for FY2025; the ~50bp gap is a definitional difference that has not been reconciled, so the two figures should not be treated as the same metric.

Q1 FY2027 confirmed the trend: gross margin 73.7% (+140bp), SG&A 55.0% (-20bp), operating margin 17.5% (+160bp), adjusted operating margin 18.7% (+170bp). Comps +12% (digital +13%, brick-and-mortar +12%). But the regional split is where the information is: North America comps +9% on high-teens AUR, Asia +23% on mid-teens AUR with China revenue up more than 40%, and Europe +1% with brick-and-mortar flat.

**Earnings quality — the cash flow divergence.** FY2026 operating cash flow fell 6.5% to $1,154.2M while net income rose 26.7% to $941.1M. Conversion dropped from 1.66x to 1.23x. Management attributes the gap to working capital and tax timing, including cash settlement of uncertain tax positions — the non-current unrecognized tax benefit liability declined from $217.3M (6/28/25) to $168.7M (3/28/26) — plus accounts payable timing and a higher bonus payout. The explanation is credible and the balance moved as described, so this reads as timing rather than accrual aggressiveness. It nonetheless deserves monitoring: two consecutive years of earnings outpacing cash generation is the pattern that usually precedes an accrual reversal, and supplier finance program obligations rose from $172.1M at fiscal year end to $268.4M at 6/27/26.

**Capex is structurally higher.** FY2026 capex nearly doubled to $408.1M (5.0% of revenue) from $216.2M (3.1%), "primarily related to the strategic purchase of real estate" — Q1 FY2026 North America capex of $129.6M locates most of it in the June 2025 quarter. This should not be normalized away: FY2027 guidance is 4-5% of revenue, roughly $340-430M. Free cash flow of $746.1M in FY2026 (versus $1,018.9M in FY2025) implies a 3.2% FCF yield on ~$23.1B of equity value, and on the FY2027 scenario below, FCF conversion stays meaningfully below 100% of adjusted earnings.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and short-term investments of $1,941.6M against total debt of $1,239.4M at 6/27/26 gives net cash of $702.2M — up from $639.8M a year earlier despite heavy buybacks, because $400M of 3.750% notes were repaid in September 2025 and $500M of 5.000% notes due 2032 issued in June 2025. The other maturity is $750M of 2.950% notes due June 2030. The $750M revolver runs to June 2028 with a 4.25x maximum Adjusted Debt/EBITDAR covenant and was undrawn apart from $10.4M of letters of credit. Interest coverage was 21.8x and net interest was approximately zero ($54.2M expense against $53.7M income). Current ratio 2.04x. ROIC of 23.1% on debt-plus-equity, or 16.8% including ~$1,537M of operating lease liabilities.

**Capital allocation.** FY2026 returned $840.3M — $216.5M dividends and $623.8M of repurchases including $123.6M of shares surrendered for tax withholding — against $746.1M of FCF, or 113%. Q1 FY2027 returned $379.9M against $285.9M of FCF (133%), though the 10-K is explicit that operating cash flow is weighted to the second half, so Q1 ratios cannot be annualized. The quarterly dividend went from $0.825 to $0.9125 (May 2025) to $1.00 (May 2026), +21% over two steps. Buyback authorization remaining fell from $1.352B at fiscal year end to $1.102B at 6/27/26 — roughly one year of runway at the Q1 pace. Weighted-average diluted shares fell 1.9% year over year to 61.3M.

**Share count note.** The supplied market capitalization of $14.56B divided by the quoted price equals exactly the 37.7M Class A count and excludes the 21.9M Class B shares. Every multiple in this report uses 59.6M total shares outstanding (61.3M weighted diluted for per-share earnings), giving ~$23.1B of equity value and ~$22.4B enterprise value ($23.9B including operating leases). The supplied trailing and forward P/E of 24.63x also does not reconcile to $387.46 on any of the EPS series available; the multiples below are computed from the filings.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is price, not volume. FY2026 comps of +13% came on mid-teens AUR in North America and Asia and high-single-digit in Europe; Q1 FY2027 comps of +12% came on AUR of +15%. That arithmetic raises the question of whether comparable-store unit volumes are flat to modestly down. The subtraction is not clean — AUR is measured across the full DTC network including new stores while comps exclude them, and category mix affects the two differently — and management reported higher North America traffic in FY2026 plus 1.5 million new DTC consumers in Q1. But the honest read is that this is a franchise monetizing a broadly stable customer base at rising prices, which is a durable strategy while it lasts and a fragile one if any competitor returns to promotion.

Category and geographic breadth are the constructive offsets. High-potential categories — Women's Apparel, Outerwear, Handbags — grew more than 20% in constant currency in Q1, ahead of the total company. China grew more than 40%. Asia grew 25% cc in Q1 at a 33.5% margin, the group's highest, which is a favorable consolidated mix dynamic and looks like share capture from higher-priced European houses that have struggled with the Chinese consumer. Twenty-two new owned and partnered stores opened in the quarter, including The Grove and Stanford Shopping Center in California, Istanbul, and Polo stores in Sydney and Perth. Some of Asia's growth is distribution expansion rather than pure share gain, but +23% comps argue expansion is not the whole story.

Against that, FY2027 guidance is the binding constraint on the growth score: constant-currency revenue growth of 5-6% on a 52-week comparable basis, plus ~1 point from the 53rd week, less 50-100bp of FX; operating margin expansion of 60-80bp cc, explicitly weighted to the first half because of marketing timing and "a lower prevailing tariff rate through the first half of the fiscal year." That last clause is management telling investors the tariff rate steps up in 2H FY2027. Q2 guidance is +5-6% cc revenue with a 100-150bp FX headwind and +80-100bp cc margin — against a Europe wholesale base that lost ~5 points of Q2 revenue to the Q1 pull-forward.

Estimate revisions are positive but not of inflection magnitude: current-year consensus rose 2.6% in 30 days and 4.8% in 90 days to $18.90; next year rose 2.3%/4.4% to $20.88. Next-quarter estimates actually fell 2.7% over 90 days to $6.87, consistent with the timing reversal. Four consecutive beats (7.0%, 3.9%, 6.6%, 3.1%) support confidence in consensus being achievable rather than aspirational. But +5% revision velocity is not the +30% signature that precedes systematic re-rating; it is a well-run company beating modestly and analysts catching up.

Implied EPS growth: from $17.41 trailing adjusted to $18.90 FY2027 consensus is +8.6%; to $20.88 FY2028 is a further +10.5%. With ~1.5-2% annual share shrinkage embedded, that is a low-double-digit compounder decelerating from a mid-thirties percent year.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's competition discussion names conditions rather than rivals: brand strength, timeless style, quality, value and service; the ability to price competitively, secure desirable retail floor space and withstand prolonged adverse economic conditions. It concedes some competitors are significantly larger with greater resources and that internet-only sellers are intensifying.

Three dynamics in RL's own numbers matter more than the boilerplate.

**Asia is running counter to the broader luxury slowdown.** Asia grew 21.5% cc in FY2026 with 320bp of margin improvement and 25% cc in Q1 FY2027 with China above +40%. RL's positioning as accessible/aspirational luxury with a heavy concession footprint and +32% digital growth appears to be capturing trade-down from European houses. This is the single most valuable piece of the competitive story and the least dependent on the U.S. consumer.

**North American wholesale is contracting structurally, and RL is on the correct side of it with concentrated counterparty exposure.** The FY2026 risk factors specifically cite Saks Global's bankruptcy filing and Macy's plan to close ~150 stores through calendar 2028. RL's answer is the DTC pivot — Q1 retail revenue of $1,369.3M versus $554.7M wholesale, roughly 71/29 — and NGT is explicitly framed as enabling it. But mix shift does not reduce counterparty concentration: the three largest wholesale customers were ~11% of FY2026 revenue and ~29% of gross trade receivables at fiscal year end (23% at 6/27/26, plausibly a shipment-timing artifact rather than diversification). RL neither factors nor insures receivables and extends credit without collateral. The Q1 "resumed shipments to a luxury wholesale account" may be Saks Global — the filings never name it, so treat that as inference — and if so, RL has re-extended trade credit to a recently reorganized counterparty.

**Outlets are both a growth channel and a pressure valve.** RL operates 307 outlets versus 287 full-price stores, and the 10-K describes them as serving additional consumer groups *and* as a secondary channel for excess and out-of-season product. Industry peers including Tapestry's Coach are expanding outlet capacity to reach lower-income shoppers. Heavier outlet reliance would work against the AUR narrative over time — worth monitoring in the full-price/outlet store-count split at each fiscal year end.

Licensing provides high-margin category optionality: $142.5M of FY2026 revenue at 86.9% margin, with contractually guaranteed minimum royalties of $248.9M remaining ($76.6M for the rest of FY2027, then $78.4M, $52.8M, $38.8M and $2.3M). That is a floor on contracted minimums with declining coverage as contracts reach expiry — not a backlog, not total expected royalties, and not evidence of declining licensing revenue.

Competitive trajectory is mixed rather than uniformly improving: Asia clearly strengthening, North America stable-to-better, Europe deteriorating on both comps and ex-FX margin. That mix supports a 6.5 rather than a 7+.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs, two-sided.** With 96% of production outside the U.S. and concentration in Vietnam, Cambodia and India, RL is directly exposed. Management has told investors the prevailing rate steps up in 2H FY2027, which is why margin expansion is guided first-half-weighted. Against that sits an unquantified refund claim. Neither side is in any consensus number in a quantified way.

**Europe.** 31% of revenue at a 27.8% FY2026 margin, decelerating to +1% comps with flat brick-and-mortar and AUR growth cut to low-single-digit. Europe's Q1 segment margin was flat including a 40bp FX tailwind, implying ~40bp of ex-FX contraction, and Q2 faces the mechanical wholesale reversal. Management flagged Europe demand risk on the Q1 call. This is the most probable source of a negative surprise in the next two prints.

**Key-person risk, named first.** Ralph Lauren is 86 and remains Chief Creative Officer, with products designed "by, and under the direction of, Mr. Ralph Lauren and our design teams." The company's own forward-looking-statements language leads with "the loss of key personnel, including Mr. Ralph Lauren." For a franchise whose entire pricing power rests on a personal aesthetic, this is a genuinely binary, unhedgeable risk that no multiple can price cleanly.

**NGT execution.** Charges escalated $5.1M (FY2024) → $25.2M (FY2025) → $83.9M (FY2026), with $20.8M in Q1 FY2027 alone versus $11.0M a year earlier. The program includes a global ERP replacement, distribution-center automation and merchandise allocation/demand-planning rollouts phased "over the next several years." The cost curve has not inflected down, and ERP cutovers at multi-channel retailers carry real inventory-disruption risk.

**AUR dependence.** The margin story requires continued double-digit AUR growth with reduced promotion. The 10-K itself cites "increasing pressure on our margins in a highly promotional retail environment." A trade-down by the aspirational North American customer, or a competitor returning to promotional depth, attacks the mechanism directly.

**Governance.** ~85% family voting power means public Class A holders have minimal influence over board composition or strategy. Related-party items are disclosed and mostly benign — Mr. Lauren's royalty-free perpetual rights to use the marks for beef and living animals, film projects through RRL Productions, and $24.2M of interchange-litigation income donated in matching amount to the family foundation of which David Lauren is President.

**FX.** A tailwind of $81.8M to FY2026 operating income becomes a guided 50-100bp revenue drag in FY2027. A 10% dollar move would change the derivative portfolio's fair value by ~$141M.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 32.9% sits marginally below one-year realized volatility of 34.5% and in the 27th percentile of the current chain — options are fairly to slightly cheaply priced, with the post-earnings crush from 45.8% (July 22) to 32.9% having removed the event premium. The term structure is flat (32.9% near, 31.4% at six months), implying uncertainty spread evenly rather than concentrated in a single catalyst. Put/call volume of 3.10 and a put/call open-interest ratio that rose from 1.16 to 1.31 since late July indicate accumulating protection alongside 8.9% short interest and 5.3 days to cover. The near-dated skew reads as balanced by the data provider's own characterization; the wider readings at the three- and six-month tenors coincide with the Q2 and Q3 FY2027 prints, which is where the Europe wholesale reversal and the guided 2H tariff step-up land. Positioning is modestly defensive without being extreme, and it neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view — it is consistent with a stock priced for continued delivery with hedges laid on around the second-half guidance risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $490 (+26.5%)

The elevation flywheel has further to run. AUR growth of 15% with lower-than-planned promotion, 73.7% gross margin and 18.7% adjusted operating margin in Q1 show the strategy compounding, not maturing. Asia at 26% of revenue growing 25% cc at a 33.5% margin drives consolidated mix upward without requiring anything from the U.S. or European consumer; China above +40% suggests share capture from European luxury that could persist for several years. High-potential categories (Women's, Outerwear, Handbags) growing 20%+ diversify away from the men's Polo core. Management has beaten four consecutive quarters and raised FY2027 margin guidance, and its 5-6% cc revenue guide is plausibly conservative after a large Q1 beat. Net cash of $702M, 21.8x interest coverage and $1.102B of buyback authorization support ~2% annual share shrinkage; a further authorization is a reasonable expectation. The IEEPA refund is a free option of unquantified size. On 23.5x FY2028 consensus of $20.88, $490.

### Bear Case — $290 (-25.1%)

Growth is price-led and price-led growth ends. FY2027 guidance already halves the constant-currency rate to 5-6%; Europe has arrived there first, with +1% comps, flat brick-and-mortar and ex-FX margin contraction in a region carrying 31% of revenue at a 27.8% margin. Roughly 2-2.5 points of the Q1 "beat" was wholesale timing, part of which reverses in Q2. The 2H FY2027 tariff step-up is disclosed by management and unquantified. Free cash flow is structurally lower than earnings — capex now guided at 4-5% of revenue versus 3.1% in FY2025 — while return of capital ran at 113% of FY2026 FCF, funded partly from the balance sheet. Wholesale counterparty risk is real and uninsured, with Saks Global freshly reorganized and Macy's closing ~150 doors. NGT charges are still rising, and a botched ERP cutover would hit inventory and margin simultaneously. Add a U.S. consumer under pressure and a European slowdown, and a derating to the 15.5x peer median on $18.90 puts the stock at $290 — roughly the 52-week low.

### Base Case — $425 (+9.7%)

Guidance is delivered with modest upside: FY2027 revenue ~$8.6B, adjusted operating margin ~16.7%, adjusted EPS in the $18.40-18.90 range against consensus of $18.90, with Asia offsetting European softness and the 2H tariff step-up absorbing part of the 1H margin gain. The multiple holds near 22x trailing adjusted. Add the 1.0% dividend and total return is roughly 10-11% over twelve months, with a wide distribution around it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (fifth consecutive). Rationale: growth_hold.**

This is the fifth HOLD in a row and the score has drifted from 6.8 (February) to 6.0. The relevant new information since the July 22 report is that the stock is essentially unchanged ($387.14 → $387.46) while FY2027 consensus rose 2.6% and FY2028 rose 2.3%, and management raised the constant-currency margin outlook. Risk/reward is therefore modestly better than a month ago — forward P/E compressed from ~21.0x to ~20.5x on flat price — which is why the score is not falling further despite the guided growth deceleration. It is not enough to justify ACCUMULATE: the base case offers under 10% price upside against 25% bear-case downside, the Q2 print carries a disclosed mechanical revenue headwind, and Consumer Discretionary names of this profile have historically required a clearer company-specific catalyst to outperform.

- **Base case target: $425** — 22.5x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $18.90, holding the current 22.3x trailing adjusted multiple flat. **+9.7%** from $387.46, plus a 1.0% dividend.
- **Bull case target: $490** — 23.5x FY2028 consensus of $20.88. **+26.5%.**
- **Bear case target: $290** — 15.5x (peer median) on FY2027 $18.90, near the $283.50 52-week low. **-25.1%.**
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with two decision points — the Q2 FY2027 print (early November 2026, first clean read on the Europe wholesale reversal and Europe comps) and the Q3 print (early February 2027, first quarter of the guided higher tariff rate).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Price in the **$330-345** range with FY2027/FY2028 estimates intact — approximately 17.5-18x FY2027 consensus, which restores a double-digit margin of safety to the $425 base case.
2. Europe retail comps reaccelerating to mid-single digits for two consecutive quarters while Asia holds above +20% cc.
3. A quantified IEEPA tariff refund recognized in results or disclosed as a specific receivable, which would convert an unmodeled option into earnings and cash.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Europe retail comps turning negative, or Europe segment operating margin declining ex-FX for two consecutive quarters.
2. Consolidated DTC AUR growth falling below mid-single digits, or explicit commentary that promotional depth is increasing — this is the load-bearing element of the entire margin thesis.
3. A cut to the FY2027 +60-80bp constant-currency margin guide, or NGT charges continuing above the ~$80M annual pace accompanied by any inventory-availability or allocation disruption.
4. A material receivable reserve or charge tied to a wholesale counterparty.

**Reassessment date:** Q2 FY2027 earnings, early November 2026.

## Conclusion

Ralph Lauren is a well-run, financially fortress-like business executing a strategy that is visibly working: 400bp of gross-margin expansion over two years, adjusted operating margin near 16% and heading to ~16.7%, net cash of $702M, 21.8x interest coverage, ROIC above 23%, and an Asia franchise growing 25% in constant currency at the highest segment margin in the group. Nothing in the filings suggests deterioration in the core mechanism.

What the price does not offer is asymmetry. At $387.46 — 22.3x trailing adjusted, 20.5x FY2027 consensus, 18.6x FY2028 — the market pays a full and largely deserved premium to a distressed peer cohort for a company whose own guidance halves the constant-currency growth rate to 5-6%, whose largest high-margin region has decelerated to +1% comps with ex-FX margin contraction, whose Q1 headline included roughly 2-2.5 points of wholesale timing that partly reverses this quarter, and whose management has disclosed a tariff cost step-up in the second half. Set against a genuinely binary key-person risk in an 86-year-old chief creative officer and 85% family voting control, a mid-single-digit probability-weighted expected return does not justify new capital.

Own it if you own it — the four-beat record, rising estimates and buyback support argue against selling a compounder at 20.5x forward. Add in the low $340s, or on evidence that Europe has stabilized and the tariff refund has been quantified.

### Valuation and Price Discipline

- Current price: $387.46
- Fair value: $425.00

- Rationale: Base case 22.5x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $18.90, holding the current trailing adjusted multiple flat; under a 10% discount, below the threshold for new capital.

**Thesis:** Ralph Lauren is monetizing brand elevation through 15% AUR growth, 73.7% gross margin and an Asia segment compounding 25% cc at a 33.5% margin, funded by net cash of $702M and ~2% annual share shrinkage. At 20.5x FY2027 and 18.6x FY2028 consensus, that quality is priced; a decelerating guided growth rate, a disclosed 2H tariff step-up and a Europe wholesale timing reversal cap twelve-month upside near the mid-single digits on a probability-weighted basis.

**Variant view:** Consensus treats the Q1 beat as clean; filings attribute roughly 2-2.5 points of the 14% growth to wholesale timing and a resumed luxury account, part reversing in Q2. Underlying growth is nearer 11.5-12% and heading to guided 5-6% cc, so the earnings path is flatter than the headline suggests.

### Catalysts

- **Medium — Nov 2026:** Q2 FY2027 print (early November) gives the first clean read on Europe comps and the disclosed wholesale pull-forward reversal.
- **Medium — 6-12 months:** Quantification or recognition of IEEPA tariff refund claims would convert an unmodeled contingent asset into earnings and cash.
- **Low — next 2 quarters:** Asia/China momentum plus buyback: China revenue up over 40%, $1.102B authorization remaining supporting ~2% share shrinkage.

### Thesis Invalidators

- **next 2 quarters:** Europe retail comps turn negative or Europe segment margin declines ex-FX for two consecutive quarters.
- **next 2-3 quarters:** Consolidated DTC AUR growth falls below mid-single digits or management signals increasing promotional depth.
- **FY2027:** Cut to the FY2027 +60-80bp constant-currency margin guide, or NGT charges above ~$80M annually with inventory disruption.
