## Executive Summary

Credo is a fabless supplier of high-speed interconnect for AI data centers. In fiscal 2026 (year ended May 2, 2026) revenue rose 205.7% to $1,335.1M, gross margin expanded 320bp to 68.0%, operating margin went from 8.5% to 33.3%, and net income reached $472.3M on GAAP diluted EPS of $2.51. Free cash flow was $407.0M against $57.3M of capex. The balance sheet holds $1,165.0M of cash, no funded debt, a 10.2x current ratio and $2,063.6M of equity. On any conventional financial-strength screen this is a pristine company.

The composition of that growth is the entire investment question. Management discloses that active electrical cables (AECs) sold into hyperscale data centers "contributed over 99% of the increase in revenue." Three end customers were 33%, 32% and 19% of FY2026 revenue — 84% combined, up from roughly 63% concentrated in a single name a year earlier. The mix rotated away from one dominant buyer, which reduces single-program variance, but aggregate concentration worsened. Twelve-month remaining performance obligations were $31.8M at January 31, 2026 against quarterly revenue of $407.0M, so essentially all demand runs on cancellable purchase orders. Against that, Credo carries roughly $272M of near-term supply-side obligations ($157.5M of capacity-reservation purchase levels, $147.3M of it in FY2027, plus $114.5M of non-cancelable foundry and subcontractor POs) and $250.8M of inventory, up from $90.0M, with finished goods roughly tripling.

At $206.99 the stock is 33% below the June 22 high of $308.67 while consensus estimates have moved the other way: FY2027 EPS is up 12.5% and FY2028 up 20.8% over 90 days. That combination — lower price, higher estimates — improves risk/reward, and it maps to a sector-wide multiple reset in late July rather than any company-specific disappointment. It does not make the stock cheap. Trailing GAAP P/E is 82.5x, EV/sales 28.0x, and price/FCF 94.8x; forward multiples of 33.8x FY2027 and 22.8x FY2028 rest on non-GAAP estimates that exclude $182.6M of stock compensation (13.7% of revenue) and on an effective tax rate of 0.7% that the company itself expects may reset toward the OECD 15% minimum in fiscal 2028. Recommendation: **HOLD** — a growth hold, owned through the ramp, not added to at this price.

## Company Overview

Credo is a Cayman-incorporated holding company operating through subsidiaries in the US, Canada, mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore and Taiwan. It sells the electrical and optical link layer inside and between data-center racks:

- **ZeroFlap AECs** — copper cables with Credo SerDes/DSP retimer silicon in the connector heads. This is the revenue engine.
- **ZeroFlap optical transceivers**, developed in collaboration with Oracle to address "link flap" failures.
- **OmniConnect** memory solutions, Ethernet/PCIe retimers and DSPs, the **PILOT** telemetry software layer, and **SerDes IP licensing**.

Per-lane speeds span 32G to 200G, up to 1.6T aggregate. The FY2026 10-K adds UALink, ESUN and SUE — open scale-up accelerator fabrics — to the addressable protocol list, language absent from the Q3 10-Q. No revenue is attributed to those protocols yet.

Go-to-market is two-pronged: Credo wins the design directly with the hyperscaler or NeoCloud, then the end customer's contract manufacturer places the order. This means the counterparty in the financial-statement footnote is frequently a contract manufacturer rather than the end user, which is why the company publishes two concentration tables. All IC manufacturing is at TSMC, with no long-term foundry contract; AEC assembly is concentrated at BizLink under the company's only long-term supply agreement. Management estimates a foundry transition would take 9 to 12 months plus customer requalification. One reportable segment.

Intra-year cadence matters more than the annual print. Nine-month revenue was $898.1M, of which Q3 (ended January 31, 2026) was $407.0M, +201.5% YoY. Q4 is therefore **$437.0M** (derived: $1,335.1M − $898.1M), or **+7.4% sequential**. Management had guided in the February 9 8-K to "mid-single digit" sequential growth after pre-announcing a Q3 beat of roughly 18-20% above the $335-345M guidance midpoint. Actual Q4 modestly exceeded that framing. The deceleration from triple-digit YoY to a mid-single-digit sequential run rate is real, disclosed by management, and the correct base for forward modeling.

**Operating leverage.** R&D fell from 33.6% to 20.9% of revenue while growing 90.2% in dollars to $279.4M; SG&A fell from 22.6% to 13.8% while growing 86.0% to $184.0M. Of the $132.5M R&D increase, $60.9M (46%) was stock compensation; of the $85.0M SG&A increase, $44.4M (52%) was. Total SBC of $182.6M equals 13.7% of revenue and 41% of GAAP operating income. Cash operating leverage is therefore stronger than GAAP shows, and shareholder dilution is a real cost. Both are true and should be carried simultaneously, which is why GAAP and non-GAAP earnings must not be mixed in a single multiple.

**Gross margin quality is unresolved.** Margin expanded 320bp to 68.0% (68.5% in Q3) while the mix shifted toward a cable product carrying copper, PCB, connector and BizLink assembly content, normally dilutive to a fabless margin. Management attributes it entirely to "improved economies of scale." Three explanations are consistent with the disclosure: genuine unit-cost leverage and yield gains, favorable pricing and richer high-speed SKU mix during a supply-tight buildout, or the arithmetic of mask-set and IP-license amortization spread over a revenue base that tripled. The filings do not disaggregate product-level margin. I would not underwrite 68% as a floor.

**Cash flow and working capital.** Operating cash flow of $464.3M against $472.3M of net income (0.98x) decomposes into $234.2M of non-cash add-backs (SBC $182.6M, D&A $34.6M, inventory write-down $15.1M) offset by a **$242.3M working-capital drag**: receivables +$70.8M, inventory +$174.0M, other assets +$71.3M (largely refundable supplier deposits for reserved capacity), less payables +$48.8M and accruals +$25.1M. FCF of $407.0M is a 30.5% margin, and capex of $57.3M (mask sets, CAD tools) is genuinely fabless-light. This is a business funding its growth through inventory and supplier deposits rather than debt.

Ending inventory was $250.8M versus $90.0M a year earlier; at Q3, finished goods were $169.1M against $52.7M. Against FY2026 cost of revenue of $426.8M that is roughly 214 days; against Q4-annualized COGS (~$556M) it is roughly 165 days. Either figure is elevated, and the company already took a $15.1M excess-and-obsolete charge in FY2026, so part of the build has proven imperfectly matched to demand. Management ties the build to "unfulfilled backlog and related new product ramps," which is credible with a 7.4% sequential quarter, but it is the single line item most exposed to a program pause.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $1,165.0M, total assets $2,295.6M, current liabilities $197.1M, equity $2,063.6M (89.9% of assets), debt/equity 0.11 consisting of lease and technology-license obligations. Retained earnings flipped from a $83.2M deficit to +$389.1M. Altman Z of 8.76. ROA 20.6%, ROE 22.9% — both depressed by the cash pile; returns on capital employed ex-cash are materially higher. Solvency is not the question here.

**The tax line is the least-discussed earnings risk.** FY2026 tax was $3.156M on $475.4M of pretax income — a 0.7% effective rate arising from the Cayman/Hong Kong/Singapore income mix. The 10-K states the company "may be subject to legislation based on the OECD's 15% global minimum tax regime in our fiscal year 2028." Holding FY2026 pretax income constant, a 15% rate reduces net income by roughly $67M, or about 14%, lifting trailing GAAP P/E from 82.5x to about 96x. The company hedges with "may," incidence depends on jurisdictional implementation and substance carve-outs, and a larger FY2028 revenue base dilutes the proportional impact. But any model that run-rates a 0.7% tax rate overstates sustainable EPS.

**Capital allocation.** An October 2025 $750M ATM with Goldman Sachs was fully drawn within the same fiscal year: 4.8M shares for $736.3M net at a blended ~$153-156, in two tranches. That is 2.8% dilution against the 171.2M pre-raise count, equivalent to new holders owning 2.73% of the post-issuance base — modest, and struck well below today's price, so accretive to remaining holders relative to $206.99. Uses were not obviously capital-constrained: FY2026 FCF alone was $407M, acquisitions took $112.9M, and $393.3M went into certificates of deposit. The straightforward reading is opportunistic balance-sheet building for capacity deposits, M&A and ramp working capital. An equally consistent reading is that management views $272M of supply commitments, escalating deposits and purchase-order-only demand as requiring a larger liquidity buffer than the reported balance sheet implies. The filings state no specific use of proceeds; I do not infer motive.

Acquisitions were Hyperlume (September 29, 2025, $92.0M total consideration; microLED chip-to-chip optical interconnect) and CoMira Solutions (closed in Q4, terms not broken out; ~$30M implied residual, unverified). Hyperlume allocated $69.1M — 75% of consideration — to goodwill, with a $17.2M indefinite-lived IPR&D intangible. Founder retention equity of 264k shares measured at $146.01 adds roughly $38.5M of post-close compensation expense on top of the purchase price.

One clean positive: the Amazon customer warrant is fully exercised and retired (3.8M net shares issued cashless). Warrant contra-revenue was $13.2M across the first nine months of FY2025 and zero in FY2026, meaning the reported +205.7% growth is very slightly understated rather than flattered, and roughly 2% of pending dilution is now settled and permanently gone.

## Growth Analysis

The forward case rests on three things: the reach limit of passive copper, the AEC design-win pipeline, and whether anything outside AECs inflects.

The structural driver is physical. As per-lane speeds move from 100G to 200G, the usable reach of passive direct-attach copper shrinks, while optics remain more expensive and more power-hungry at rack scale. That widens the window for active copper. Credo's differentiation within that window is reliability rather than raw speed: ZeroFlap targets link-flap failures that delay AI cluster turn-on, and PILOT provides telemetry. Selling "faster time to first revenue on a multi-billion-dollar GPU cluster" is a stronger proposition than component price/performance, and it is a plausible explanation for how a sub-$40B company wins slots against Broadcom and Marvell.

Consensus reflects continued strong growth. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS stands at $6.13 (up 12.5% in 90 days, +0.9% in 30) and FY2028 at $9.08 (+20.8% in 90 days, +4.4% in 30). Summing the four reported FY2026 non-GAAP quarters ($0.52, $0.67, $1.07, $1.16) gives $3.42, so consensus embeds roughly +79% EPS growth in FY2027 and +48% in FY2028. One caveat on precision: the quarterly panel ($1.16 current quarter, $1.25 next) does not sum consistently with the $6.13 annual figure, implying a back-half of $3.72 — a steeper ramp than the recent sequential cadence supports. Provider panels aggregate from different contributor sets; I use the annual figures and treat the quarterly detail as indicative only.

The earnings-surprise record is four consecutive beats: +40.7%, +33.5%, +9.8%, +10.5%. Surprise magnitude is compressing as analyst coverage catches up. That raises confidence in the level of consensus while reducing the odds of another 30%+ upside shock.

Two constraints on the growth score. First, the revision velocity, while clearly positive, is +12.5%/+20.8% over 90 days, not the +30% threshold that historically marks a genuine mispricing. Second, growth is single-vector: optical, OmniConnect memory, PCIe retimers, chiplets and IP licensing together contributed under 1% of the FY2026 increase. The Hyperlume and CoMira acquisitions, the Oracle optics collaboration and the UALink/ESUN/SUE positioning are investments in the next vector, and with two-to-three-year design cycles they would not show revenue yet. That is a reasonable defense of the timing; it is not evidence of diversification.

Sector context supports the demand side. Hyperscaler capex continues to expand and funds the AI compute load that AECs connect. [unverified — specific hyperscaler capex figures not in the research brief] The offsetting reality is that the marginal buyer class is broadening toward NeoClouds, which Credo added to its own customer taxonomy this year, and which the 10-K itself flags as a class that "may be unable to pay for their required AI infrastructure."

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors in the 10-K are Broadcom, Marvell and Astera Labs, plus cable and optical transceiver suppliers. The filing concedes competitors are "substantially larger" with greater financial, technical, marketing and government resources.

Three competitive vectors matter:

1. **The n-1 node claim cuts both ways.** Management asserts it achieves "similar performance to leading competitors' products but at a lower cost and more highly available legacy node." If accurate, that means lower wafer cost and better TSMC capacity access than competitors bidding for leading-edge allocation — a real advantage in a supply-constrained market. It also means Credo is not protected by scarce leading-edge access. Its moat is DSP/SerDes design skill plus system software, not process privilege. The claim is management's and is unverified against competitor cost structures.

2. **Customer vertical integration is a named risk in the filing itself.** The same buyers that are 84% of revenue design their own accelerators, NICs and networking silicon. Credo's defense is co-development and standards adjacency — the HiWire Switch AEC partnership with Microsoft including an open-source implementation for dual-top-of-rack, and the Oracle ZeroFlap Optics collaboration. Read literally these are joint-development relationships in which Credo supplies hardware and the partner contributes architecture; they do not establish which lettered customers correspond to which companies, and no such mapping is verified anywhere in the filings.

3. **Standards optionality is real but unquantified.** Adding UALink, ESUN and SUE positions Credo for open scale-up fabrics, where SerDes and retimer content per accelerator would rise substantially if those standards take share from proprietary approaches. The 10-K also warns that a shift toward a standard Credo does not support "could significantly decrease demand," and that standards are typically driven by larger incumbents.

Trajectory assessment: improving. Revenue tripled, gross margin expanded 320bp, and two new end customers scaled from immaterial to 32% and 19% while the former dominant customer's absolute dollars still roughly doubled. That is share capture in a product category expanding for physical reasons. The offsets — ASP erosion disclosed as historically normal, sticky wins but equally durable losses given 7+ year product lives, and a competitor set with substantially greater resources — cap how high this dimension can score. **6.8.**

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, precisely stated.** FY2026 end-customer revenue: D 33%, B 32%, E 19% — 84% from three; top ten ~90%. MD&A says concentration should decrease as adoption broadens; FY2026 data shows a rotation, not a broadening. Single-name dependence improved (D fell from 63% to 33%); aggregate concentration worsened. On the contracting-party basis at Q3, one counterparty was 48% of quarterly revenue and 57% of the $243.2M receivable balance, and the company records **no reserve for credit losses**. That is defensible with hyperscaler counterparties and provides zero cushion if one program unwinds.

**Demand is contractual only in Credo's direction.** Twelve-month RPO was $31.8M at Q3 — small relative to a $437M quarter — and sales are on purchase orders cancellable on short notice without penalty. Against that sit $157.5M of capacity-reservation purchase levels ($147.3M in FY2027), $114.5M of one-year non-cancelable foundry/subcontractor POs disclosed outside the commitments table, $17.5M of technology licenses, $28.8M of refundable supplier deposits and $250.8M of mostly finished-goods inventory. A single program pause hits revenue, inventory reserves and shortfall fees in the same quarter. The important mitigant: the capacity-reservation penalty is contractually capped at the value of the refundable deposits, and $1.165B of cash can absorb a very large inventory writedown.

**Single foundry, single geography.** All ICs at TSMC in Taiwan, no long-term contract, no contractual capacity assurance from TSMC or any assembly partner, 9-12 month transition estimate plus requalification. Because customers rarely change a design mid-generation, a two-to-three-quarter supply interruption likely means permanent loss of the slot. This exposure is near-universal among fabless AI-infrastructure suppliers and is partly offset by the n-1 node strategy and the new assembly-capacity reservations.

**Geographic and regulatory.** North America went from 15% to 58% of revenue; 28% of FY2026 revenue was generated in Hong Kong against 2% of assets by book value — largely a contract-manufacturer/shipment-destination artifact, but exposed to the Hong Kong National Security Law, the Hong Kong Autonomy Act and EO 13936. Mainland China shipments collapsed from $25.3M to $3.7M in a single quarter, unexplained in the filings. The US Outbound Investment Rule creates "increased regulatory burden" on investments in Credo's own Chinese subsidiaries, with revisions anticipated in 2027. PRC Order No. 835 (April 2026) was newly added. The 10-K also acknowledges that certain past exports "may have been made in violation of Trade Controls laws," with remediation described and no enforcement action, penalty or litigation disclosed — an unquantified compliance history that should not be assumed either way.

**New risk language chosen by management.** The FY2026 10-K introduces an explicit AI-bubble risk factor, a statement that the semiconductor upturn "may not be sustainable," language that some AI customers "may be unable to pay for their required AI infrastructure and/or seek alternative financings or novel or deferred payment models from their vendors," and the addition of NeoClouds to the customer taxonomy. The Q3 10-Q had reported no material risk-factor changes, so these are annual-report additions. Risk-factor language is cheap and often reflects counsel's posture rather than operating experience, and nothing in the reported financials — no bad-debt provision, no disclosed receivable deterioration — indicates a payment problem has occurred. The specificity of the deferred-payment language, read alongside a $243M receivable at 57% single-name concentration with no credit reserve, is worth monitoring.

**Governance and structure.** Cayman holding company, classified board with staggered terms, 50M authorized blank-check preferred usable for a rights plan, shareholders unable to expand the board or remove directors without cause, articles amendable only by two-thirds vote, and difficulty enforcing US judgments. PFIC status is management's belief for FY2026 but not assured going forward given the $1.165B cash balance. Disclosure controls were concluded effective; no restatements or material weaknesses.

**Management alignment.** Form 4s from June 3 to July 27, 2026 — the eight weeks after the 10-K — show sales by the CEO, CFO, COO/director, CTO, Chief Legal Officer and three directors at prices from roughly $206 to $281, with **no open-market purchases by anyone**. The CTO sold ~220,000 shares from a 6.02M-share position; the CEO reduced from 422,311 to 367,327 and separately gifted 157,500 shares; the COO sold ~106,000 across two accounts. Directors Hosein, Acevedo and Danesh reduced to 16,850, 16,337 and 7,017 shares respectively — small in dollars, large as fractions of already-small positions. Meaningful offsets: two documented 10b5-1 plans (Lam adopted December 15, 2025; Fleming re-adopted January 12, 2026) pre-date the 10-K; several transactions are tax withholding or trust/charitable transfers rather than discretionary sales; and the CTO retains ~5.8M shares. This pattern is clustered enough across C-suite and board, within a narrow window, to weigh negatively on alignment. I draw no inference about business fundamentals from it. What it establishes is that no insider found $206-$281 attractive enough to buy. Insiders and affiliates held ~9% as of May 2, 2026.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $320

The physics are on Credo's side: passive copper reach shrinks with every speed doubling, expanding the AEC window, and Credo has established itself as the reliability-differentiated supplier in that window with three of the largest AI infrastructure buyers. FY2028 consensus of $9.08 at a 35x multiple gives $320 (+54.6%), consistent with the 52-week high of $308.67 and below the $350 street high. Getting there requires: sequential revenue growth sustained near or above the mid-single digits through FY2027; gross margin holding near 68%; at least one non-AEC vector (optical, PCIe, UALink-related content, or the Hyperlume microLED path) beginning to contribute; and Pillar Two incidence landing well below 15%. The 90-day estimate revisions and four consecutive beats say consensus has been chasing this business upward, and at 33.8x forward against ~48% out-year growth the multiple does not require heroic assumptions.

### Bear Case — $110

The bear case is a single-quarter triple debit. Three customers, no contractual commitments, $250.8M of mostly finished-goods inventory and $272M of near-term supply obligations mean one program pause simultaneously reduces revenue, forces inventory reserves and triggers shortfall fees (capped at deposits, but real). Layer on a 320bp gross-margin expansion whose durability is unverified — if part of it reflects scarcity pricing or mask amortization arithmetic rather than structural cost advantage, margins normalize toward the low 60s as the buildout matures. Add the tax reset: 15% instead of 0.7% removes roughly 14% of net income. In that scenario FY2028 estimates reset toward $4.50-5.00 and the multiple compresses to 22-24x on lower confidence, giving $110 (-46.9%), still well above the 52-week low of $86.49. The macro channel is credible: the marginal customer class is shifting toward less creditworthy NeoClouds, which management flagged in its own risk factors alongside language that some AI customers "may be unable to pay."

### Base Case — $240

Growth decelerates from triple-digit YoY toward 45-65%, gross margin holds in the 65-68% range, one non-AEC vector shows early revenue, and the tax rate moves up but not fully to 15%. FY2028 non-GAAP EPS lands near consensus $9.08; a 26.4x multiple — a modest premium reflecting faster growth discounted for concentration — gives **$240**, or 39x FY2027 consensus. That is +16.0% from $206.99, below the $279.29 street mean, and it is the return profile that supports holding rather than buying.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is intact and specific — AEC content expansion at three scaling hyperscale customers with rising consensus behind it — but base-case upside of 16% against 47% bear-case downside is not the asymmetry that justifies new capital at 33.8x forward non-GAAP, ~42x forward GAAP-equivalent, and 28x trailing EV/sales.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q1 FY2027 report, expected late August or early September 2026 (the first disclosure of FY2027 guidance in evidence; no Q4 call or FY2027 guidance was obtained). The one-month options tenor prices ±19.6% around it.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. FY2027 revenue guidance implying ≥50% growth **with** gross margin guided at 66% or better, plus explicit disclosure that the top-three end-customer share is falling below 80%.
2. Non-AEC revenue (optical, PCIe retimers, OmniConnect, IP licensing) disclosed at ≥10% of quarterly revenue — the single cleanest refutation of single-product risk.
3. Price at $155-170 on unchanged or rising FY2028 estimates, which would put the stock at 17-19x FY2028 consensus and restore genuine asymmetry. Note that the ATM was executed at a blended ~$153-156, a level management found acceptable to issue at.
4. Clustered open-market insider purchases — any C-suite buying would materially change the alignment read.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Sequential revenue growth turning negative, or gross margin falling below 64%, in either of the next two quarters.
2. An inventory write-down materially above the $15.1M FY2026 charge, or a first-ever credit-loss reserve against the receivable book.
3. Disclosure that a top-three end customer has paused, cancelled or in-sourced a program, or that a capacity-reservation shortfall fee has been incurred.
4. Evidence that Pillar Two will apply at or near the full 15% from FY2028 without offsetting carve-outs, alongside decelerating revenue — the combination that compresses both earnings and the multiple at once.

**Position management for existing holders:** hold through the print rather than adding. With near-term IV at 129.6% versus 91.5% realized, selling upside calls against a portion of the position is better compensated than buying stock here.

## Conclusion

Credo delivered one of the cleanest fundamental years in the AI infrastructure complex, with strong execution: four consecutive beats, a pre-announced 18-20% guidance beat, and consensus EPS revised up for FY2027 and FY2028. The 33% drawdown from the June 22 high occurred while those estimates rose, so it reflects multiple compression across high-beta AI semis, not company-specific deterioration.

What keeps this a hold rather than a buy is the concentration of everything that produced the result. Over 99% of the $898M revenue increase came from one product line sold to hyperscale buyers; three end customers are 84% of revenue with only $31.8M of twelve-month contracted obligations against them; all ICs come from one foundry in Taiwan with no long-term contract; the gross-margin expansion is unexplained at the product level; and the earnings base carries a 0.7% tax rate the company itself expects may reset toward 15% in fiscal 2028. Management chose to add an AI-bubble risk factor and customer-payment-capacity language to its own 10-K this year, and in the eight weeks after filing it, every senior executive and three directors sold shares between roughly $206 and $281 with no one buying.

At $206.99 the base case of $240 offers 16% upside against 47% downside if the ramp stalls. That is a reasonable position to own through a continuing product cycle and a poor one to build into at these multiples. Hold; revisit on the Q1 FY2027 print, on evidence that revenue is broadening beyond AECs, or on a price in the $155-170 range.
