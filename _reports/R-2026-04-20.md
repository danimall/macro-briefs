## Executive Summary

Ryder System operates a $12.7B fleet leasing, supply chain, and dedicated transportation business that benefits from a secular outsourcing trend — only $234B of $1.5T in U.S. logistics spending is currently outsourced. The company generated $946M in free cash flow in fiscal 2025 (ending December), improved comparable EPS 8% to $12.92, and executed a well-planned CEO succession. Supply Chain Solutions, the highest-growth segment at 43% of revenue, posted 7% earnings growth on record new business wins.

The stock has appreciated 64% over the past twelve months, moving from $138 to $228, and now trades at 19x trailing EPS and 16.3x the $14.01 FY2026 consensus estimate. That consensus is declining: current-quarter EPS has been cut 15% over 90 days (from $2.68 to $2.27), and full-year 2026 estimates have fallen 4.2%. The freight downturn persists, used vehicle values continue deteriorating (tractors -11%, trucks -15% YoY), and rental utilization sits at 70%, well below the 75% level reached in 2023. Options markets are pricing near-term stress with IV in backwardation and elevated put demand.

Ryder occupies a strong competitive position in a structurally growing market, and the valuation is not unreasonable relative to history. But with the stock at 99% of its 52-week high, negative earnings revision momentum, and multiple cyclical headwinds still unresolved, the risk-reward skews toward waiting for a better entry point.

## Company Overview

Ryder System provides outsourced fleet management, supply chain logistics, and dedicated transportation services across North America. The business operates through three segments:

**Fleet Management Solutions (FMS, 38% of revenue):** Full-service leasing (ChoiceLease), commercial rental, and vehicle maintenance across 789 locations, managing ~185,000 vehicles for ~11,700 lease customers. This is the capital-intensive foundation, generating $501M in pre-tax earnings in 2025.

**Supply Chain Solutions (SCS, 43% of revenue):** Warehousing (319 facilities, 105M sq ft), distribution, transportation management, brokerage, and e-commerce fulfillment organized by industry vertical. The fastest-growing segment at $355M pre-tax earnings (+7% YoY) serving 722 accounts.

**Dedicated Transportation Solutions (DTS, 18% of revenue):** Turnkey fleet operations with vehicles, drivers, and administration for 210 customers. Expanded significantly through the 2024 Cardinal Logistics acquisition ($302M), which nearly doubled the DTS fleet to 19,100 vehicles. Generated $140M in pre-tax earnings (+12% YoY).

The total addressable market includes approximately 5 million privately-owned commercial vehicles in the U.S. that represent Ryder's conversion opportunity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Consolidated revenue of $12.7B in 2025 was essentially flat versus 2024 ($12.6B), despite the Cardinal acquisition contributing to DTS. Operating revenue (excluding subcontracted transportation and fuel) reached $10.4B (+1%). The freight downturn is suppressing organic growth across all segments.

**Earnings:** Comparable EPS of $12.92 grew 8% YoY, recovering toward but still below the 2022/2023 cycle peak. GAAP EPS was $11.99. Segment profitability diverged meaningfully: FMS margins compressed to 9.8% (from 13.2% in 2023) on weaker rental demand and used vehicle losses, while SCS and DTS margins expanded on operating leverage and Cardinal synergies.

**Free Cash Flow:** $946M in 2025, a dramatic improvement from $133M in 2024, driven by reduced capex ($2.1B vs. $2.7B) and stronger operating cash flow ($2.6B). Management guides $800M FCF for 2026 with higher ChoiceLease investment (~$2.4B capex).

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $7.6B against $3.1B in equity (debt/equity 250%). This leverage ratio reflects the fleet financing business model, not operational distress — the debt is matched to vehicle asset lives. Investment-grade ratings (BBB+/Baa2) with Moody's on positive outlook, $936M in available liquidity, and consistent capital market access (issued $900M in new medium-term notes in 2025 at 4.3%-5.0%) confirm adequate financial flexibility. Interest expense of $404M (5.2% effective rate) grew 5% YoY and represents an ongoing margin headwind in the current rate environment.

**Shareholder Returns:** $664M returned in 2025 ($519M buybacks + $145M dividends). The annualized dividend was raised 12% to $3.64/share, yielding approximately 1.6% at the current price.

**Earnings Quality Concern:** The last four quarters show a mixed beat/miss record — two narrow beats (Q1 and Q2 2025) and two narrow misses (Q3 and Q4 2025). The misses coincide with the period of deepening freight market weakness, suggesting management's visibility into near-term demand has been declining.

## Growth Analysis

**Secular Tailwinds:** The outsourcing thesis is structurally sound. Increasing vehicle complexity (electrification, emissions regulations, advanced safety systems), chronic labor shortages (drivers and technicians), and growing supply chain complexity (reshoring, e-commerce, regulatory requirements) all push privately-operated fleets toward specialist providers. Ryder's 789-location maintenance network and integrated three-segment model represent genuine competitive advantages in capturing this conversion.

**SCS as Growth Engine:** Record new business wins in 2025, with SCS organized by industry vertical to deepen customer relationships. E-commerce and last-mile fulfillment capabilities position the segment for continued above-average growth. The omnichannel retail vertical specifically benefits from increasing demand for flexible fulfillment networks.

**DTS Acquisition Strategy:** The Cardinal Logistics deal demonstrates management's willingness to use acquisitions to accelerate growth in its least capital-intensive segment. CEO Sanchez indicated in March 2025 that Ryder is "in the market for more dedicated acquisitions." With DTS margins improving and synergies tracking ahead of plan, further M&A appears likely.

**Cyclical Recovery Optionality:** When freight markets normalize, Ryder would benefit from three compounding effects: higher rental utilization (currently 70%, historically 75%+), used vehicle value recovery, and accelerated contractual sales as customers gain confidence. This represents meaningful embedded earnings power not reflected in current results. Timing remains uncertain.

**Estimate Trajectory:** This is the key concern. Current-quarter EPS consensus has fallen 15.2% over 90 days ($2.68 to $2.27), and FY2026 consensus has declined 4.2% ($14.62 to $14.01). FY2027 estimates at $16.73 are also falling, though more modestly (-1.3% over 90 days). The negative revision trend suggests the market may be too optimistic on the timing of freight market recovery.

## Valuation Assessment

At $228, Ryder trades at:
- 19.0x trailing EPS ($11.99)
- 16.3x FY2026E EPS ($14.01)
- 13.6x FY2027E EPS ($16.73)
- ~9.5x EV/EBITDA (on ~$2.87B comparable EBITDA)

**Relative to consensus targets:** The median analyst target is $225, and the mean is $228.56 — both effectively at the current price. The stock is already at fair value by sell-side consensus. The high target of $263 represents 15% upside; the low target of $202 represents 11% downside.

**Historical context:** Ryder has historically traded in a 10-15x trailing earnings range. The current 19x trailing multiple is elevated by historical standards and reflects the market pricing in cyclical recovery that has not yet materialized in the financial results.

**Peer context:** Without direct peer multiple data, Ryder's forward P/E of 16.3x appears reasonable for a transportation/logistics company with an asset-heavy model, particularly one generating $800M+ in guided FCF. However, the 8.5% premium to the trailing multiple (19x vs. the typical 12-14x range) prices in meaningful growth acceleration.

The valuation is not expensive on 2027 estimates, but it is fully valued on 2026 estimates, and those 2026 estimates are declining. At 16.3x on falling numbers, the stock needs either estimates to stabilize or the freight cycle to turn to justify the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Ryder's competitive position is its strongest attribute. The company operates the largest full-service commercial vehicle leasing network in North America with 789 locations — a scale advantage that creates switching costs for customers who depend on geographically dispersed maintenance coverage. The integrated three-segment model (lease, supply chain, dedicated) allows cross-selling that smaller competitors cannot replicate.

Key competitive dynamics:
- **FMS:** Competes against finance lessors, OEM dealers, and managed maintenance providers. Scale of maintenance network is primary differentiator.
- **SCS:** Competes against large 3PLs and specialized providers. Industry vertical organization and technology platform (RyderShare, COOP) provide differentiation.
- **DTS:** Competes against other dedicated carriers and truckload carriers. Cardinal acquisition significantly improved scale.

Ryder's total shareholder return of 353% over the measurement period (vs. 149% for peers) demonstrates management's ability to translate competitive position into value creation.

Threats include zero-emission vehicles (which could reduce demand for Ryder's diesel maintenance services), autonomous driving technology (which could alter the dedicated transportation model), and warehouse automation (which could reduce labor-intensive SCS services). Ryder is investing in all three areas but remains primarily a diesel-era fleet operator.

## Risk Assessment

**Freight Market Duration (High):** The extended freight downturn has persisted beyond expectations, and management's own language — "extended freight downturn and overall economic uncertainty" — does not provide a timeline for recovery. Rental utilization at 70% and above-target used vehicle inventory (9,500 vs. 7,000-9,000 target) indicate significant slack. Rising energy costs ($95+ oil) add cost pressure to Ryder's customers, potentially delaying the freight recovery further.

**Used Vehicle Residual Risk (High):** A 10% reduction in residual values would increase depreciation by approximately $340M over the remaining fleet life. Used vehicle values have been declining (tractors -11%, trucks -15% YoY), and effective January 2026, management is reducing residual estimates for certain tractors — a proactive but earnings-negative action.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity (Moderate-High):** With $7.6B in debt and a 5.2% effective rate (up from 4.4% in 2023), Ryder is directly exposed to the current rate environment. Rates remaining elevated through 2026 would continue pressuring net margins.

**Tariff Impact (Emerging):** The 10-K explicitly flags tariffs on imported vehicles, parts, and industrial goods as increasing maintenance costs. Secondary effects — tariff-driven demand reduction in freight volumes — could compound the cyclical headwind.

**Customer Concentration:** The top 10 customers in SCS and DTS each represent approximately one-third of their respective segment revenues. Automotive plant shutdowns in Q4 2025 already impacted SCS earnings, demonstrating concentration risk in practice.

**CEO Transition:** While orderly and well-planned (Diez has deep operational experience across all three segments), any leadership transition introduces execution risk. Sanchez remaining as Executive Chair mitigates this.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 42% is 8.5pp above 1-year historical volatility of 33.5%, and the term structure is in backwardation (42% near-term vs. 37.9% at 6 months), indicating the market prices an imminent catalyst or resolution. The put/call OI ratio of 2.54 shows heavy protective positioning, and near-term put/call skew of 3.9% reflects elevated demand for downside protection. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.7% ($208-$248 range). This options positioning is consistent with institutional hedging ahead of earnings uncertainty in a stock near its highs with negative estimate revisions. It does not override the fundamental case but reinforces caution on timing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($260-$270, +14-18%):** Freight markets inflect in H2 2026, driving rental utilization back toward 75%, used vehicle values stabilize, and SCS continues winning new business at record rates. FY2027 EPS of $17+ becomes achievable, and the stock re-rates to 15-16x on recovery earnings. The new CEO executes additional DTS acquisitions at accretive multiples. Tariff-driven reshoring accelerates the outsourcing trend. At 15.5x $17 EPS = $264.

**Bear Case ($170-$180, -21-25%):** Freight downturn extends through 2027, used vehicle residual values decline another 10% (adding ~$340M in depreciation over fleet life), rising diesel costs compress customer margins and delay outsourcing decisions, and interest expense continues rising. FY2026 EPS falls to $12-$13, and the stock de-rates toward 14x on disappointed expectations. At 14x $12.50 = $175.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. A pullback to $195-200 (14x FY2026E) would provide adequate margin of safety for the cyclical recovery thesis
2. FY2026 consensus estimates stabilize and begin rising — specifically, if the current quarter estimate stops declining and the 30-day revision trend turns positive across all periods
3. Rental utilization improves above 73% for two consecutive quarters, signaling freight market recovery

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS estimates fall below $12.50 (indicating the freight downturn is deepening and the SCS growth engine is stalling)
2. Used vehicle residual value write-downs accelerate beyond the January 2026 adjustment, particularly if management signals further reductions on upcoming calls
3. Credit rating downgrade or Moody's removing positive outlook — would signal balance sheet stress in a way the Z-Score currently overstates

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late April or early May 2026 for Q1). Key metrics to monitor: rental utilization, used vehicle pricing trends, SCS new business wins, and management's updated freight market commentary.

## Conclusion

Ryder System is a well-run company with a strong competitive moat in a structurally growing market. The CEO succession is orderly, free cash flow generation is strong, and the secular outsourcing thesis remains intact. However, the stock has already priced much of this — at $228, it trades at consensus fair value, with negative estimate revision momentum and unresolved cyclical headwinds. The freight downturn, deteriorating used vehicle values, elevated interest costs, and oil prices above $95 all argue for patience. Insider activity shows no open-market buying. The right approach is to wait for either a price pullback to the $195-200 range or for evidence that freight markets are turning, as indicated by stabilizing estimates and improving rental utilization.
