**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Associated Banc-Corp is the largest bank holding company headquartered in Wisconsin, running a ~$45.6B-asset Midwest commercial-and-consumer franchise that grew to roughly $50B with the April 2026 close of the American National acquisition. The investment story is a credible self-help turnaround: management absorbed a depressed FY2024 (net income $123.1M) by selling low-yielding mortgages and securities at a loss, then reinvested into higher-yielding C&I and auto loans. That repositioning has lifted net interest margin to 3.03% (from 2.97%), pushed the efficiency ratio to ~56% (from ~60%), and produced a 13.03% ROATCE. Consensus EPS estimates are rising across all forward periods, and the bank has beaten in each of the last four quarters.

The complication is price and timing. ASB trades at ~$29, within 3% of its 52-week high of $29.96, at ~10x forward earnings and below the consensus mean target of $31. The franchise is structurally asset-sensitive into a Fed easing cycle, so continued rate cuts pressure the NIM gains that drive the bull case (EAR -1.4% for a 100bp gradual decline). The American National deal adds scale and deposit funding but dilutes the share count by roughly 14% and will drag reported Q2 2026 earnings through a Day-2 CECL provision and integration costs. A $449.7M gross unrealized loss in the held-to-maturity book sits outside CET1, so economic capital is thinner than the headline 10.47% ratio suggests.

The fundamental trajectory is improving and the prior HOLD reads have correctly captured a slow-grind re-rating (score 6.2 → 6.5). New data confirms the operating improvement but does not create asymmetric upside at this price: the stock has already moved 28% off its 52-week low, trades modestly below consensus, and the analyst mix has degraded modestly (strong-buy count cut). We maintain a HOLD with a growth tilt: own it for the turnaround compounding and the ~3.3% yield, reassess on the Q2 print that will reveal pro-forma earnings power.

## Company Overview

Associated Banc-Corp operates through Associated Bank, N.A., with roughly 200 legacy branches across Wisconsin, Illinois, Minnesota, and the upper Midwest, now extended into Greater Omaha and a deepened Minneapolis/St. Paul presence post-acquisition. The business runs in three reportable segments:

- **Corporate & Commercial Specialty** — large corporate, middle-market, small business, asset-based lending, equipment finance, and capital markets. Largest earning-asset base (~$18.2B average loans, ~$58.6M Q1 net income).
- **Community, Consumer & Business** — branch banking, residential mortgage, auto, home equity, and wealth/private banking (~$12.7B average loans, ~$72.2M Q1 net income, ~$22.2B deposits).
- **Risk Management & Shared Services** — treasury, the investment portfolio, and corporate functions.

The $31.8B core loan book (pre-acquisition) is 66% commercial / 34% consumer. Within commercial, the largest concentrations are C&I (39% of total loans), CRE-investor (16%), and construction (7%). Sub-sector exposures include utilities (9% of total loan exposure, 70% renewable energy) and real estate/REIT lines (9%, 68% REIT lines). Wealth management contributes a meaningful fee stream (~$15.7B AUM, fees +12% YoY). No single concentration exceeds 10% of loan exposure, which gives the book reasonable granularity for its size.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings recovery is the headline, and it is partly real and partly an easy comp.** Net income moved $183.0M (FY2023) → $123.1M (FY2024 trough) → $474.8M (FY2025). The 286% FY2025 jump is flattered by the FY2024 repositioning losses, so the right lens is the underlying run-rate, not the YoY change. On that basis the improvement is genuine: NIM up to 3.03%, efficiency ratio down to ~56%, ROA at 1.08%, ROATCE at 13.03%.

**Quarterly trajectory (net income / diluted EPS):** Q1'25 $101.7M / $0.59 → Q2'25 $111.2M / $0.65 → Q3'25 $124.7M / $0.73 → Q4'25 $137.1M / $0.80 → Q1'26 $119.6M / $0.70. The sequential Q4-to-Q1 decline looks like deterioration but is largely tax and day-count noise: pre-tax income actually rose 26% YoY ($121M → $153M), while the effective tax rate jumped to 21.75% from a year-ago 16.03% that benefited from a valuation-allowance release. The prior-year quarter carried an artificially low tax burden, so the YoY EPS comparison understates operating improvement.

**Q1 2026 detail vs Q1 2025:**
- Net interest income +7% to $307.2M; NIM 3.03% (from 2.97%), driven by the asset-mix shift toward C&I and a 39bp drop in funding costs as rates fell.
- Noninterest income +29% to $75.9M (wealth +12%, mortgage banking +60%, plus the absence of the prior-year repositioning loss).
- Noninterest expense +4% to $219.2M.
- Average loans +4% YoY (intentional C&I and auto growth, residential mortgage run-off).

**Capital and liquidity (Q1 2026):**
- CET1 10.47%, Total capital 13.02%, TCE/TA 8.27%: adequate, middling for the peer group, not a fortress.
- Loans/deposits 89%. Uninsured & uncollateralized deposits 25.7% of total (down from 26.5%), with $17.5B available liquidity covering uninsured deposits 191%, a materially stronger position than the 2023 regional-bank stress baseline.
- Tangible book value per share rising: $20.25 (Q1'25) → $22.23 (Q1'26).

**The quiet vulnerability is the HTM book.** Held-to-maturity securities carry $449.7M of gross unrealized losses (fair value $3.12B vs amortized cost $3.57B, marked at 87.4%). Because ASB opts out of AOCI inclusion in CET1, reported capital looks healthier than economic capital. This is common across regionals and not an acute solvency issue given the strengthened liquidity, but it constrains repositioning flexibility and should be netted against tangible book in any stress view. AOCI widened to -$44.5M in Q1'26 (from -$7.6M at YE'25) on rate moves.

**Credit is benign and improving.** NPAs/total assets 0.32%, nonaccruals 0.35% of loans, ACLL/nonaccruals a robust 384%, net charge-offs only 7bp annualized in Q1'26. The mid-2025 CRE-investor charge-off spike (-67bp/-61bp annualized in Q3/Q2'25) has cleared. The risk here is rate/margin and integration, not credit.

## Growth Analysis

Forward growth rests on three legs, two organic and one inorganic.

**NIM and mix.** The deliberate pivot toward higher-yielding C&I and auto while running off residential mortgage is lifting asset yields, and funding costs fell 39bp YoY. The catch is asset sensitivity: ASB benefits from higher/steeper rates and is pressured by cuts. With the Fed easing cycle underway and the rate path two-sided, the NIM tailwind is conditional. A continued easing cycle is the single largest threat to the earnings-recovery thesis.

**Estimate revisions are positive but modest.** Current-quarter EPS revised +5.8% over 90 days, current year +3.7%, next year +1.6%. This is steady upward momentum, not the +30% velocity that signals a systematic re-rating. The bank has beaten four straight quarters (1.9% to 13.1% surprises), which supports confidence in the forward estimates ($2.98 current year, $3.24 next year).

**The American National acquisition adds scale and funding.** The all-stock deal (total consideration $594.1M, 22.98M ASB shares, closed April 1, 2026) brought $5.2B assets, $3.8B loans, and $4.5B deposits, a ~12% balance-sheet expansion into Omaha (new market) and the Twin Cities (deepening). The target's 84% loan/deposit ratio means it adds funding, not just assets, which is strategically sound for an asset-sensitive bank. The follow-through hire of a franchise-banking vertical in Minneapolis (May 2026) signals intent to build organically on the expanded footprint. The near-term cost: ~14% share-count dilution that EPS accretion must overcome, plus a Day-2 CECL provision and integration expenses depressing reported Q2 2026 GAAP earnings. Management early-adopts ASU 2025-08 (credit-loss gross-up for acquired seasoned loans) in Q2, which produces a reserve double-count.

The net read: a mid-single-digit organic compounder with a credible self-help margin story and an accretive but near-term-noisy deal. This is durable but not high-velocity growth.

## Valuation Assessment

ASB trades at ~$29.20 (last close $28.94), ~10x trailing/forward EPS of ~$2.77–2.98, a ~3.3% dividend yield, and ~1.3x tangible book ($22.23 TBVPS). Against the regional-bank peer set, the picture is mixed:

| Metric | ASB | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E | ~10.0 | 11.3 |
| Dividend yield | 3.3% | 1.9% |
| 52-week discount | ~3% | varies |

ASB screens slightly cheap on P/E versus the peer median (FFIN 18.1, IBOC 11.3, FULT 11.0, TCBI 13.5, AX 10.9) and carries a clearly higher yield. That argues for a modest discount being unwarranted given the improving ROATCE. But the case against rewarding the cheapness with a high score is stronger than the screen suggests. A low P/E in a regional bank facing an easing cycle, sitting on ~$450M of off-capital HTM losses, and digesting a 14% dilution is the kind of "cheap for a reason" setup that tends to stay cheap until the catalysts (Q2 pro-forma earnings, rate-path clarity) resolve.

On forward fundamentals, the stock sits modestly below the consensus mean and median target of $31 with a high of $34 and a low of $27. At ~1.3x tangible book with 13% ROATCE, the franchise is fairly priced, not mispriced. The analyst-targets-are-floors logic applies to cheap inflection names with rapid estimate revisions; here the revisions are modest (+3.7% current year) and the stock already trades close to consensus, so the targets read as a ceiling reflecting the same operating optimism embedded in the price rather than independent upside. Applying ~10x to the $3.24 next-year consensus supports a ~$32 base case, about 10% above current, before netting the dilution and rate risk. That is a respectable but unexceptional return for a Financials name.

## Competitive Landscape

ASB occupies the $45–50B regional tier, competing against larger super-regionals (US Bancorp, Fifth Third, Huntington) for commercial relationships and against community banks for deposits. Its edge is deep Midwest deposit relationships and a granular, diversified loan book. The competitive trajectory is improving rather than deteriorating: the balance-sheet repositioning structurally lifted returns, the C&I pivot moves the book up the yield curve, wealth fees are growing double-digits, and the acquisition adds scale to absorb the rising technology and regulatory cost base that pressures sub-$50B banks. Five-year cumulative TSR of 184 versus a peer-group 153 supports the view that management's strategy is working relative to the group.

The competitive position is solid-and-improving but not a wide moat. ASB is a price-taker on deposit costs, lacks the scale advantages of the super-regionals, and its renewable-energy/utilities concentration (9% of loans, tied to tax-credit economics) is exposed to shifting federal clean-energy incentives. The Omaha/Twin Cities expansion is an opportunity but execution against entrenched local competitors is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Interest-rate / asset-sensitivity (primary).** The NIM expansion that drives the bull case reverses in a sustained easing cycle. EAR -1.4% for a 100bp gradual decline, -3.1% for 200bp. The rate path is the dominant swing factor.
2. **HTM unrealized losses (~$450M).** Off-CET1 economic loss limiting securities-book flexibility; the same 2023-vintage dynamic that hurt regionals. Not acute given 191% liquidity coverage of uninsured deposits, but a real drag on economic capital.
3. **Acquisition integration.** Day-2 CECL provision and integration costs will depress reported Q2 2026 earnings and obscure pro-forma run-rate; ~14% dilution must be overcome by accretion; success hinges on retaining American National's deposits and Omaha/Twin Cities relationships.
4. **CRE/construction concentration.** CRE-investor + construction ~23% of loans; construction carries a 3.30% reserve ratio; multi-family is the largest property type in both buckets, a nationally pressured segment. Charge-offs have normalized but bear watching.
5. **Wholesale funding reliance.** FHLB advances grew to $3.4B (avg +68% YoY); brokered CDs ~10% of deposits: margin and liquidity sensitivity.
6. **Renewable-energy concentration (9% of loans).** Tax-credit and policy dependent.

Most of these are at least partially priced: the ~10x multiple and modest premium-to-tangible-book reflect a market aware of the rate and CRE risks. The unpriced edge is the asymmetry around Q2: the dilution and CECL noise could spook investors who read GAAP EPS at face value, while a clean pro-forma print could resolve the overhang. That two-sidedness keeps the risk score balanced rather than high.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$34):** The repositioning continues to compound: NIM holds or expands as the C&I/auto mix builds, the efficiency ratio drops below 55%, and ROATCE pushes toward 14%. American National integrates cleanly, deposits are retained, and Omaha/Twin Cities deliver organic loan growth that overcomes the dilution within a year. The Fed holds rather than cuts aggressively, protecting asset-sensitive NIM. EPS reaches the upper end of the $3.24+ next-year range, and the stock re-rates toward 11x on a higher ROATCE, reaching ~$34 (consensus high). ~17% upside.

**Bear Case (~$24):** The Fed eases meaningfully, compressing NIM (EAR -1.4% to -3.1%). The American National integration runs over budget, deposit attrition disappoints, and the dilution plus Day-2 CECL provision produce a soft Q2 that shakes confidence. CRE/multi-family charge-offs re-accelerate, and the ~$450M HTM mark constrains flexibility. The stock derates toward 8.5x and tangible book, falling to ~$24. ~17% downside.

**Base Case (~$32):** Mid-single-digit organic growth, NIM modestly pressured by rate cuts but offset by mix, accretion roughly offsetting dilution by year-end. ~10x on $3.24 next-year EPS = ~$32. ~10% upside plus a 3.3% yield.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric (17% up to bull, 17% down to bear) with a modestly positive base case. That balance, at a stock sitting near its 52-week high and modestly below consensus, supports a HOLD rather than a BUY. The fundamentals justify ownership; the entry point does not offer the asymmetry a BUY requires.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July 2026), which will reveal pro-forma earnings power net of the American National Day-2 CECL provision and integration costs. This is the decisive print for the thesis, the first reporting quarter to include American National, which closed April 1, 2026 (after Q1 ended).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 confirms NIM holding at or above 3.03% despite Fed easing, demonstrating the mix shift is overpowering rate pressure.
- Pro-forma EPS accretion from American National visible by Q3 2026 (run-rate EPS clearing the ~$0.80 Q4'25 mark on the larger share count), confirming the deal overcomes its ~14% dilution.
- A pullback toward the high-$25s/low-$26s that restores a clearer discount to the $31–32 fair value while fundamentals stay intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NIM compresses below ~2.90% on accelerating rate cuts with no offsetting mix benefit.
- CRE-investor or construction net charge-offs re-accelerate above the mid-2025 peak (>65bp annualized) or the ACLL/nonaccrual coverage deteriorates materially.
- Deposit attrition at American National or an integration-cost overrun that materially impairs the accretion math.
- A dividend cut or capital action signaling stress against the ~$450M HTM overhang.

## Conclusion

Associated Banc-Corp is a competently run regional bank executing a credible balance-sheet repositioning that has genuinely improved margin, efficiency, and returns, supported by rising estimates and a strategically sound, scale-additive acquisition. The improving trajectory (score 6.2 → 6.5 over prior reads) is confirmed by the latest data. But at ~$29, within 3% of its 52-week high, modestly below the consensus target, with asset sensitivity into a potential easing cycle, ~14% dilution to digest, and ~$450M of off-capital HTM losses, the stock is fairly valued rather than mispriced. The risk/reward is balanced, and the analyst sentiment mix has softened at the margin.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth tilt).** Own it for the turnaround compounding and the ~3.3% yield; the bar for upgrading to BUY is a Q2 print confirming pro-forma earnings power or a pullback that restores asymmetry. There is no case for shorting: short interest is negligible, credit is benign, and the franchise is improving.
