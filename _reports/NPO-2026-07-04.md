**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Enpro has repositioned itself over the past several years from a diversified industrial into a proprietary-content specialist with two segments, Sealing Technologies (~64% of FY25 sales) and Advanced Surface Technologies (AST, ~36%), where semiconductors are now the single largest end market at 32% of revenue. The result is a business with structurally higher margins (FY25 adjusted EBITDA $277.6M, ~24.3% margin), high-quality cash conversion (FCF $159.2M, 13.9% margin), and a secular-growth tilt toward semis/AI capital equipment, single-use biopharma, and process analytics.

The dominant analytical point is that the FY25 GAAP earnings figure is misleading. Reported net income fell to $40.5M ($1.91 diluted EPS) from $72.9M ($3.45), which drives the headline 163x trailing P/E. That decline is almost entirely a $67.2M non-cash pension settlement loss from terminating the frozen U.S. defined-benefit plan. On an operating basis the business improved: adjusted EPS rose to $7.91 from $6.96 (+14%), and management guides FY26 to $8.85–$9.50 adjusted EPS on 10–14% sales growth. Screening on trailing GAAP or the tool-reported operating margin of –0.2% misreads a company that is accelerating and has just extinguished a source of future volatility.

The recommendation is HOLD (growth_hold). The trajectory is positive: scores have climbed from 5.2 (March) to 6.4 (May), and the Q1'26 print plus a raised guide confirm rather than reverse that trend. But at ~$335 the stock trades at roughly 36x the FY26 adjusted EPS midpoint after a 66% move off its 52-week low, and the same AST customer concentration (~24% of consolidated revenue in one account) that powers the bull case is the central downside risk. Added to that is a fast-materializing, unreserved Water Valley litigation tail. The quality is real, but the entry point is not asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Enpro (spun from Goodrich in 2002) operates two segments:

- **Sealing Technologies (~$732M FY25, ~64%):** Garlock (gaskets, seals, compression packing), Technetics (metal/mechanical seals plus the new "Process Analytics" group — AMI and AlpHa gas/liquid sensing), and STEMCO (commercial-vehicle wheel-end/suspension). Roughly two-thirds of segment revenue is recurring/aftermarket. Segment adjusted EBITDA margin was 32.9% in FY25 and rising.
- **Advanced Surface Technologies (~$412M FY25, ~36%):** NxEdge, Technetics Semi, LeanTeq (Taiwan-based cleaning/refurb for advanced-node semiconductor equipment), and Alluxa (optical filters). This is the growth engine, tied to wafer-fab-equipment (WFE) capex. Segment adjusted EBITDA margin was ~20.4% FY25, recovering to 23.3% in Q1'26.

FY25 end-market mix: semiconductor 32%, general industrial 26%, commercial vehicle 15%, aerospace 8%, food/biopharma 7%, power generation 6%, oil & gas 6%. About 43% of sales are international. The competitive moat rests on qualification barriers, proprietary process know-how, and specified positions on critical applications where the cost of product failure vastly exceeds the cost of the product itself.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $1,059M (2023) → $1,049M (2024) → $1,143M (2025, +9.0%, of which 7.6% organic and 1.1% acquisitions). Q1'26 accelerated to +10.9% (3.5% organic, 5.3% acquisition, 2.1% FX), with sales of $303M.

**The GAAP-versus-adjusted disconnect:** The 163x trailing P/E and –0.2% operating margin in the data feed are artifacts of the one-time pension charge, not the operating business. The pension liability is now off the balance sheet, removing future mark-to-market volatility.

**Margins and cash flow:** Gross margin ~42.6% FY25, ~42.9% in Q1'26. Operating cash flow was $201.2M FY25 (up from $162.9M). Q1'26 OCF of $39.6M nearly doubled year-over-year. FCF/net income conversion is distorted by the pension charge on the denominator, but against adjusted earnings the conversion is high-quality, which supports the Financial Health score.

**Balance sheet and leverage:** Total debt ~$660M ($450M 6.125% Senior Notes due 2033 plus revolver). The company refinanced in 2025 — issued the $450M notes, redeemed the $350M 5.75% 2026 notes, and repaid the term loan, clearing the near-term maturity wall. Net leverage sits comfortably inside the 4.0x covenant and interest coverage well above the 2.5x minimum. Current ratio 2.32. In Q1'26 the company paid down $50M of revolver with repatriated foreign cash. The Altman Z of 2.18 ("grey zone") is distorted by the pension-charge-depressed EBIT and by a goodwill/intangible-heavy balance sheet ($1.06B goodwill + $0.82B intangibles ≈ 71% of assets) that is structural for a serial acquirer, not a distress signal.

**Earnings track record:** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters (Q1'26 +1.7%, Q4'25 +3.0%, Q3'25 +4.1%, Q2'25 –4.1%). The magnitude of beats is modest, which argues for taking the raised FY26 guide at roughly face value rather than assuming large upside surprises.

**Capital allocation:** Dividend has grown steadily ($0.29 → $0.32/qtr). A $50M buyback authorization exists but zero shares have ever been repurchased across two authorizations. Management is an M&A compounder; capital-allocation discipline should be judged on deal ROIC, not on shareholder returns.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, and I weight growth heavily as the most reliable discriminator of forward returns:

1. **Semiconductor/AI capex through AST.** AST sales rose 11.1% in Q1'26 with segment margin expanding to 23.3%. Enpro sits inside the WFE supply chain (named customers include Applied Materials and ASML), benefiting from leading-edge chip capex without owning fabs. The world-model read of the AI-infrastructure cycle argues the demand at the equipment layer is broad-based and durable; regulated-utility and electrical-equipment data corroborate that data-center buildout is not decelerating at the infrastructure tier.

2. **The process-analytics pivot.** Three acquisitions in roughly two years — AMI (2024, $209.4M), Overlook and AlpHa (2025, $273.9M combined) — are assembling a gas-plus-liquid-phase sensing and single-use biopharma instrumentation platform inside Sealing. This diversifies away from cyclical semis toward recurring, secular-growth instrumentation. The cost is that Sealing growth is increasingly acquisition-fueled (organic –0.4% in Q1'26 as pricing/mix offset weak commercial-vehicle and international-industrial demand).

3. **Guidance and estimate momentum.** Management raised FY26 guidance. Estimate revisions are positive but moderate: current-year consensus +5.0% over 90 days ($8.77 → $9.21), next quarter +2.8%, current quarter +4.2%. This is steady positive momentum rather than the >30% velocity that would flag a strong standalone buy signal. Backlog rose to $357.7M at Q1'26 from $256.7M at year-end, though management notes backlog is not highly predictive given short aftermarket lead times.

The growth thesis is credible and confirmed by the latest print, but it is a mid-teens-EPS-grower story that matters for how the valuation is anchored.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$335, Enpro trades at roughly 36x the FY26 adjusted EPS midpoint ($9.18) and ~32x the FY27 consensus of $10.36. Trailing GAAP P/E (163x) is meaningless here given the pension charge, so forward adjusted earnings are the right anchor.

Against sector peers (median P/E 28.2 across GTES, ESAB, MIDD, JBTM, ESE), Enpro carries a premium on forward multiples. Part of that premium is justified — higher margins, higher recurring-aftermarket mix, and a semiconductor-growth engine the mechanical-machinery peers lack. But 36x forward for a company guiding to low-double-digit sales growth and mid-teens adjusted EPS growth is a full price, not a discount. The PEG on FY26 EPS growth (~15–17%) sits above 2.0, which does not clear the threshold where estimate-revision momentum alone would justify treating analyst targets as floors.

The analyst target set reinforces the balanced read: mean $327, median $331, high $345, low $305 — the current price sits above the mean and just below the median and high. The stock is trading at consensus, and unlike a cheap name where targets lag reality, here the multiple already embeds continued execution. Treating cheapness as a value signal has been reliably wrong in the current regime, and the inverse holds: a full multiple on a good business is not a reason to chase, but prior appreciation is not a reason to sell. The 66% run since the 52-week low reflects genuine fundamental improvement (adjusted EPS +14%, guide raised, margins up), so it is not evidence against further appreciation; it means the easy re-rating is behind us and forward returns now depend on earnings delivery rather than multiple expansion.

Valuation score of 5.0: the multiple is defensible on quality but offers no margin of safety and no identifiable mispricing. A pullback toward the high-$200s (roughly 30x FY26) would restore asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Enpro's moat is trajectory-positive, which matters more than absolute level. In AST, the fields are fragmented — precision machining has no dominant provider, semi cleaning is contested by Taiwan/Japan/Korea/U.S. players, and coatings (Alluxa) is highly fragmented — but Enpro's advantage is the qualification barrier and proximity to advanced-node processes. Once qualified inside a WFE customer's process, switching costs are high and margins expand with utilization.

In Sealing, the company competes on engineering reputation, breadth, and aftermarket lock-in, which supports premium pricing. Two-thirds recurring/aftermarket mix provides ballast against cyclicality. Five-year TSR indexed to 296.9 versus a peer group at 232.8 confirms the company has outperformed its comparison set, a direct read on relative competitive strength.

The weak spot is commercial vehicle (STEMCO), where North American OEM demand has been soft through FY25 into Q1'26, though this is the smaller, more cyclical piece the portfolio strategy is deliberately de-emphasizing. Net trajectory is improving share and widening moat in the growth segments, supporting a competitive-position score of 7.5. I note the calibration caution that competitive position has recently been a weaker standalone predictor, so I lean on growth and financial health as the primary winner-selection dimensions and treat this as confirming rather than driving.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks not fully reflected in a full valuation are what cap the risk score at 5.0:

1. **AST customer concentration.** One unnamed semiconductor-equipment customer was ~24% of consolidated 2025 revenue. The same account that powers the bull case is the single largest downside vector; a pullback would hit the highest-growth, highest-margin part of the story. The customer base is also concentrated in Taiwan, Singapore, and the U.S., layering Taiwan geopolitical risk (low probability, extreme impact) onto the concentration.

2. **Water Valley, MS TCE litigation (new and escalating).** Between the 10-K and the Q1 10-Q, plaintiff counts rose from a dismissed 8-person federal case to ~531 plaintiffs across 13 Mississippi state suits (two of which, ~104 plaintiffs, name Enpro/EnPro Holdings directly) plus ~150 workers'-comp petitions. No reserve is accrued and no range is estimable. This is an open-ended tail that did not exist in this form a year ago.

3. **Other legacy/environmental exposure.** Lower Passaic River (Crucible legacy) settled for $5.9M but under appeal, with separate ~$165M-remedy cost-recovery litigation stayed; Arizona uranium mines reserved at $11.3M (range up to ~$15.9M+, non-priority mines unbounded); total environmental reserves ~$41.3M across 19 sites.

4. **Semiconductor cyclicality.** 32% of revenue is tied to WFE, which is notoriously cyclical. The current upcycle is a tailwind; a downturn would compress the highest-multiple segment.

5. **Supply and regulatory.** Rare-earth/China dependency in AST; PFAS/PTFE regulatory risk (EU REACH), where PTFE is a critical Sealing raw material.

6. **Labor and governance.** The Palmyra, NY (Garlock) CBA expired Feb 15, 2026 and union employees launched a work stoppage Feb 16, 2026 (resolution unconfirmed). Two Item 5.02 8-Ks in 2026 plus a Q1 10-Q signed by the CAO/GC and Chief Accounting Officer rather than a CFO suggest a possible unresolved CFO transition — worth verifying before relying on continuity of the finance function.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and does not challenge a constructive fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 41.1% versus 36.2% one-year historical vol — mildly rich (4.9pp spread) but at only the 10th percentile of the current chain, so the market is not pricing acute near-term stress. Put/call skew is balanced (0.4%), the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.22, and IV fell 3.2pp since late May even as the stock rose 5.5%, indicating resolving rather than building uncertainty. The one nuance: the longer-dated (6-month) term shows a –5.1% put skew, so hedging demand is concentrated further out, consistent with a market comfortable near-term but pricing tail risk (litigation, semi-cycle) over the coming two-to-three quarters. Short interest is negligible at 2.4% of float (2.0 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$430–$475):** The AI/semiconductor upcycle drives AST above the guided range with continued margin expansion toward the mid-20s%; the process-analytics platform (AMI/Overlook/AlpHa) delivers recurring, secular-growth revenue that de-risks the semi concentration; adjusted EPS compounds mid-teens to ~$10.50+ in FY27, and the market rewards the de-cyclicalized mix with a sustained ~40x-plus multiple. Water Valley resolves without a material accrual. This is roughly 45x FY27 EPS.

**Bear case (~$225–$250):** WFE capex rolls over and the ~24% customer pulls back, taking AST volume and margin with it; the Water Valley plaintiff count converts into a material, unreserved liability; commercial-vehicle weakness persists; and the multiple compresses to ~25x on decelerating growth. That is roughly 25% below the current price.

**Base case (~$355):** FY26 lands within the guided adjusted EPS range, semi demand stays firm, the analytics build-out contributes, and the multiple holds around 32–33x forward with modest EPS growth carrying the stock. This is ~6% above the current price and consistent with the analyst median of $331 drifting higher as FY27 estimates ($10.36) come into view.

The bull and bear cases run through the same AST account, which is why the risk/reward is symmetric rather than skewed. The business quality is high; the entry price is full.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is intact and confirmed by the Q1'26 beat and raised guide, but the current price already reflects it.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback toward the high-$200s (roughly 30x FY26 adjusted EPS), restoring asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +10% over 90 days on current-year adjusted EPS, signaling the semi upcycle is running ahead of consensus.
- Evidence the AST customer concentration is diminishing (a named second large WFE customer, or concentration falling below ~18% of revenue) while total AST growth is maintained.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The ~24% AST customer reduces orders, or WFE capex commentary from Applied Materials/ASML turns negative for two consecutive quarters.
- Water Valley litigation produces a material accrual or an estimable range that is large relative to the ~$41.3M total environmental reserve.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin compresses below ~22% or organic Sealing growth stays negative for another two quarters, indicating the M&A machine is masking core stagnation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled quarterly print (roughly early August 2026), with interim monitoring of Water Valley filings and any 8-K confirming the CFO situation.

## Conclusion

Enpro is a quality compounder: rising adjusted earnings, ~14% FCF margin, a widening moat in semiconductor-adjacent surface technologies, a deliberate and well-executed pivot into recurring process analytics, and a balance sheet that just shed its pension overhang. The FY25 GAAP decline is a non-cash pension artifact, and anyone screening on trailing metrics will misprice a business that grew adjusted EPS 14% and raised guidance.

Against that, at ~$335 the stock trades at ~36x forward adjusted EPS at the top of its analyst range after a 66% twelve-month run, with a ~24% single-customer concentration and an unreserved, fast-escalating Water Valley litigation tail. The multiple is defensible on quality but offers no margin of safety, and the primary bull and bear vectors run through the same account. That combination supports a HOLD with a clear bias to accumulate on weakness rather than a chase at consensus. The improving score trajectory (5.2 → 6.4) is confirmed, not reversed, and the bar for a fresh entry is a better price, not a better business.
