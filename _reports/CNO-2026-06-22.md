**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CNO Financial Group is a Carmel, Indiana specialty insurer serving middle-income pre-retirees and retired Americans through the Bankers Life, Washington National, Colonial Penn, and Optavise (now exiting) brands. The investment case rests on a defensible demographic niche, a deliberate de-risking of the long-term-care book that has historically been the industry's weak point, and spread income benefiting from new-money yields above 6% for thirteen consecutive quarters. Operating earnings momentum is genuine: Q1 2026 net operating income rose 25% YoY to $101.3M ($1.05/share), and management reaffirmed full-year operating EPS guidance of $4.25–$4.45. On that base the stock trades at roughly 11.7x the midpoint of current-year operating earnings guidance, reasonable for a 12%+ ROE compounder that is shrinking its share count and raising its dividend.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is price. CNO has appreciated about 37% over the trailing twelve months, from the mid-$30s to $50.83, and now sits at or slightly above the consensus analyst target range ($47 low, $53 high, $49.50 mean). The multiple re-rating that rewarded earlier accumulation, when the stock was a clean value compounder in the high-$30s, has largely played out. At current levels the equity is priced close to fair value on forward operating earnings, with the upside dependent on continued estimate beats and ROE expansion rather than a mispricing the market has missed. The trajectory of prior reports (6.1 in April, 6.5 in May) reflected an improving fundamental story; the fundamentals remain intact, but the entry edge has narrowed as the price caught up.

We rate CNO a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The business is improving and worth owning for the durable operating-ROE and capital-return story, but the risk/reward at the current price is roughly balanced. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $40s, where the forward multiple and the dividend-plus-buyback yield re-establish a margin of safety, or on confirmation that the Medicare supplement and LTC product tailwinds are converting to sustained margin recovery.

## Company Overview

CNO is a holding company for a group of insurance subsidiaries focused on a single demographic: middle-income Americans approaching or in retirement. The company organizes around three product-line segments plus investment and fee income.

- **Health** is the largest margin contributor. It spans supplemental health (specified-disease/cancer, accident, hospital indemnity), Medicare supplement, and long-term care. Q1 2026 health margin was $132.6M, roughly 31% of policy income.
- **Annuity** is predominantly fixed indexed annuities (~$11.9B account value) plus fixed-interest annuities. Q1 2026 annuity margin was $58.5M.
- **Life** covers traditional and interest-sensitive life. Q1 2026 life margin was $65.8M.

Distribution runs through two divisions: a Consumer Division built on exclusive captive agents plus one of the largest direct-to-consumer insurance operations (the Colonial Penn brand, web/digital, and call centers); and a Worksite Division selling voluntary benefits under the Optavise brand. CNO announced in November 2025 that it is exiting the Worksite fee-services business, a move substantially complete by mid-2026.

Management's preferred profitability metric is insurance product margin and net operating income (non-GAAP), which strip out investment gains/losses, embedded-derivative and market-risk-benefit fair-value swings, and TechMod modernization costs. Given the size of GAAP volatility from rate-driven mark-to-market on the bond portfolio, operating earnings are the more meaningful lens for this business.

## Financial Analysis

**Operating momentum versus GAAP noise.** Q1 2026 GAAP net income was $37.7M ($0.39 diluted), up from $21.5M ($0.21) a year earlier. The more representative figure is net operating income of $101.3M ($1.05/diluted share) versus $81.1M ($0.79), a 25% increase. Total revenues were $1,029.6M (vs. $1,004.1M), total insurance product margin $256.9M (+3%), and total net investment income $315.2M (+6%). The investment-income growth is driven by the sustained 6%+ new-money yields and by portfolio book growth.

**Segment dynamics.** Supplemental health margin improved to 37% of policy income (from 35%) on lower morbidity and block growth. Medicare supplement margin compressed to 14% (from 18%) on higher benefit ratios, but volumes are surging as consumers shift back from Medicare Advantage to Medicare supplement, a reversal of a decade-long trend, and rate-increase filings are pending to restore margin. Long-term care margin expanded to 51% (from 46%), a strong result in a product line that has damaged many legacy carriers. CNO has pivoted to a short-duration "LTC Fundamental" product (average benefit period 13 months; 99% of new policies with ≤2 years of benefits) and, effective October 2024, stopped ceding 25% of new LTC business, retaining 100%.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Total assets were $39.0B; shareholders' equity $2.50B, down from $2.64B at year-end 2025 on a $102.6M OCI loss from rising rates widening unrealized bond losses. AOCI sits at a deeply negative $1.22B, almost entirely rate-driven unrealized losses on the available-for-sale portfolio ($2.27B gross unrealized losses against $116.8M gross gains). GAAP book value per share is $26.64; ex-AOCI it is $39.62. The consolidated RBC ratio is approximately 375% (within the 360–390% target), and debt/total capital ex-AOCI is 26.4% (target 25–28%) against a 35% covenant. The investment portfolio is $30.0B, with fixed maturities 96.9% investment grade (NAIC 1–2) and only ~3% below investment grade; structured securities are 32% of bonds, with no commercial mortgage loans non-current or in foreclosure.

A standardized credit screen will flag a very low Altman Z-Score and distorted margins for CNO. These are false signals: the Altman model was built for industrial companies and is meaningless for an insurer whose balance sheet is dominated by investment assets and policyholder liabilities. The actionable solvency read is the 375% RBC ratio, which is healthy.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $148.8M in Q1. The company repurchased $60M (1.4M shares) in the quarter with $360.4M remaining on authorization, and raised the dividend 5.9% to $0.18/quarter in May 2026 (yield ~1.4%). Trailing-twelve-month holding-company free cash flow is approximately $377M, with 2026 guidance of $200–250M. Share count is steadily shrinking, from 101.6M at year-end 2024 to 93.8M as of March 2026, a roughly 8% reduction that directly supports per-share earnings growth.

**Earnings track record.** CNO has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters: +11.0% (Q1 2026), +20.6% (Q4 2025), +39.3% (Q3 2025), and +1.5% (Q2 2025). A four-for-four beat record lends confidence to current-year estimates and suggests management guidance has been conservative.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has three components, and growth prospects earn a moderate-positive score here, neither a standout nor a drag.

First, **spread income**. Higher-for-longer rates are net-positive for CNO's spread-based earnings, lifting net investment income against a maturing book. The Q1 portfolio repositioning — selling $1.66B of lower-yielding bonds (realizing $56.3M of gross losses, largely offset by $52.2M of gains) and reinvesting at higher rates — is a deliberate trade of current GAAP pain for future spread income, not distress selling.

Second, **the Medicare supplement tailwind**. The consumer shift from Medicare Advantage back to Medicare supplement is an emerging demand vector for a traditional Med-supp writer like CNO. Strong Q4 2025 sales (effective January 2026) confirm the trend is materializing. The near-term margin compression is a function of growing benefit ratios, with pending rate filings the mechanism to restore profitability. The growth is real; the margin recovery is the variable to monitor.

Third, **ROE expansion and per-share leverage**. Management targets a +200bps operating ROE improvement through 2027 off a ~10% 2024 run-rate; trailing operating ROE was 12.2% as of Q1 2026. Combined with the share-count reduction, this drives operating EPS growth even with modest top-line gains. The Worksite fee-services exit removes ~$30M of annual revenue (under 1% of total) but adds ~$20M of annual pre-tax income, a margin-accretive cleanup.

Consensus revision data is roughly stable rather than accelerating: current-year EPS estimates have risen +1.6% over 90 days to $4.48 and next-year to $4.95 (+1.5%), while the current-quarter estimate slipped -3.5%. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a trough-to-recovery story — CNO is a steady compounder. That distinction matters for the valuation framing below: this is a quality business growing operating EPS in the high single to low double digits, not a mispriced cyclical.

## Valuation Assessment

At $50.83, CNO trades at roughly 11.7x the midpoint of 2026 operating EPS guidance ($4.35) and about 10.3x the 2027 consensus of $4.95. On trailing GAAP EPS of $2.30 the reported P/E of ~20x overstates the multiple because GAAP earnings are depressed by mark-to-market noise; operating earnings are the correct denominator.

Against peers in Life & Health Insurance, the picture is mixed. The peer median P/E is 11.7x (Primerica 11.7x, Globe Life 11.4x), with Jackson Financial and Brighthouse showing negative GAAP P/Es from their own MRB/derivative volatility. On operating earnings CNO is squarely in line with the Primerica/Globe Life cluster — appropriately so, given a comparable 12% ROE profile. There is no meaningful discount to exploit here; the stock is priced like a well-run mid-cap specialty insurer, which is what it is.

The valuation score sits at the midpoint rather than higher because the market is not obviously wrong about CNO at this price. A cheap multiple paired with an identifiable catalyst the market has missed is not present here. The stock re-rated from the high-$30s to $50 over twelve months as operating momentum confirmed, and it now sits at or just above the consensus target range. The earlier mispricing has been corrected. Cheapness as a standalone signal has been an unreliable, often anti-predictive indicator in this regime, and CNO at 11.7x is not even cheap — it is fairly priced.

We are explicit that prior appreciation is not, by itself, a reason to mark a stock down. The 37% move was driven by genuine fundamental improvement: rising operating EPS, share-count reduction, and the LTC de-risking. The issue is not that the stock has run, but that at the current price the forward return is anchored to earnings growth and capital return (call it ~10% operating EPS growth plus a ~1.4% dividend and buyback) rather than to any multiple re-rating. That is a respectable total-return profile for a hold, but it is no longer the asymmetric setup that justified accumulation in the low-$40s.

## Competitive Landscape

CNO occupies a defensible niche in the middle-income senior and pre-retiree market, served through an integrated captive-agent and direct-to-consumer model. It competes against Globe Life and Primerica in life and supplemental, Aflac in supplemental health, and Mutual of Omaha and others in Medicare supplement and LTC. The differentiation is twofold: the combined agent-plus-DTC distribution, and a deliberate avoidance of long-tail LTC risk through short-duration product design.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving. The LTC pivot to a 13-month average benefit period structurally de-risks new writings in a category that has impaired competitors with decades-long liabilities, widening the moat relative to legacy carriers. Supplemental health margin expansion and the Medicare supplement volume surge both point to share gains in CNO's core demographic. Working against the company: NAIC scrutiny of offshore/Bermuda reinsurance and proposed new C-1 CLO capital factors is an industry-wide tightening that specifically targets CNO's capital-efficiency playbook, which could erode a structural advantage. On balance, the competitive position is improving modestly, with the LTC differentiation the most durable edge and the regulatory capital tightening the main offset.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and identifiable rather than generic sector truisms.

1. **Interest-rate / AOCI sensitivity.** The negative AOCI and gross unrealized bond losses are real exposures. Held-to-recovery intent limits realized losses, and the high new-money yields are a partial offset, but sustained high rates pressure GAAP book value and could constrain statutory capital flexibility. This is largely priced — investors understand the rate sensitivity — but it caps how much GAAP book-value growth supports the equity in a higher-for-longer regime.

2. **Long-duration health reserve adequacy.** LTC (10.8-year liability duration) and supplemental health carry reserve assumptions on morbidity, persistency, and discount rates. Adverse experience or an inability to obtain timely rate increases, particularly on Medicare supplement and LTC, is a recurring and explicitly flagged risk. The current Med-supp margin compression makes the pending rate filings a near-term watch item.

3. **Bermuda reinsurance and regulatory capital.** CNO Bermuda Re reinsures large FIA and supplemental-health blocks offshore for capital efficiency. The NAIC's new AG 55 reinsurance asset-adequacy testing (adopted August 2025) and proposed C-1 CLO factors (year-end 2026) could erode the capital benefit and require the holding company to inject capital or recapture business. Bermuda Re cannot pay dividends to the parent for five years post-2023 without BMA approval.

4. **Dividend-blocked subsidiaries.** Several key subsidiaries (Washington National, CLTX, Colonial Penn, and Bankers Life) have negative statutory earned surplus, requiring regulatory approval for dividends to the holding company. The company has consistently obtained approvals — it received $35.5M in Q1 and contributed $35.5M back — but this is a structural liquidity constraint.

5. **Deferred tax asset realization and Section 382.** CNO carries $731.8M of net deferred tax assets and $1,032.3M of federal NOLs. Realization depends on future taxable income, and Section 382 ownership-change limitations could be triggered by aggressive buybacks or 5%+ holder activity, a genuine tension given the active repurchase program.

6. **Mid-tier ratings.** Subsidiaries are rated "A" (AM Best/Fitch), "A-" (S&P), "A3" (Moody's); holding-company debt is BBB-/Baa3/bbb. Solid but mid-tier; downgrades would raise funding costs and risk policy surrenders.

7. **Modeling-control signal.** An immaterial $10.1M overstatement of the FIA embedded derivative (Q1 2025 figures revised) was disclosed. Management deemed it immaterial and controls effective, but it remains a controls signal to monitor.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 34.5% against 22.1% one-year historical vol, a 12.4-point spread that prices uncertainty rich relative to realized. The term structure is in backwardation (34.5% near-term vs. 21.9% at six months), which typically signals the market pricing an imminent event. ATM IV has risen from 19.4% to 27.1% over the past month even as the price climbed 8.8%, consistent with positioning ahead of a catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced at -3.0%, and the put/call OI ratio collapsed to 0.01, indicating no meaningful bearish positioning. The signal is benign: elevated near-term vol with no directional bear tilt. Short interest is negligible (2,735 shares), so there is neither a squeeze setup nor an informed-short overhang. For a fundamental holder, the rich near-term IV is a modest argument against chasing the stock here and a mild tailwind to anyone writing covered calls into the elevated premium.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Operating EPS compounds at low-double-digit rates as spread income benefits from the sustained 6%+ new-money yields, the Medicare supplement volume surge converts to margin recovery once rate filings are approved, the LTC book continues to outperform on its short-duration design, and the ~8% share-count reduction plus a rising dividend amplify per-share growth. Operating ROE reaches the +200bps target through 2027, supporting a re-rating toward the higher end of the peer range. In this scenario the stock works toward the high-$50s to low-$60s, roughly 13x 2027 operating EPS of $4.95.

**Bear case.** Higher-for-longer rates deepen the AOCI hole and constrain statutory capital, NAIC tightening of Bermuda reinsurance and CLO factors forces capital injections or recaptures that erode the capital-efficiency benefit, Medicare supplement margin compression persists because rate filings lag claims inflation, and Section 382 limits the buyback that has been a primary per-share growth lever. The stock de-rates toward 9x on flat-to-soft operating EPS, implying the low-$40s, roughly 15–20% downside from the current price.

The base case, and our view, sits between: a quality compounder delivering high-single-digit to low-double-digit operating EPS growth, a fair-value multiple already reflected in the price, and a total return driven by earnings and capital return rather than multiple expansion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the business is improving and worth owning, but the entry asymmetry has been realized at the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $40s (roughly 9.5–10x forward operating EPS), which would re-establish a margin of safety and restore the asymmetry that existed earlier in the year.
- Approval of Medicare supplement rate increases that visibly restore segment margin toward the prior 18%+ range, confirming the volume surge converts to profit rather than dilutive growth.
- Operating ROE crossing 13%+ on a sustained basis, ahead of the +200bps-through-2027 target.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NAIC AG 55 or new C-1 CLO factors forcing a material holding-company capital injection or large block recapture that meaningfully reduces free cash flow available for buybacks and dividends.
- A subsidiary financial-strength downgrade (loss of the "A" AM Best/Fitch rating), which would raise funding costs and risk surrenders.
- Evidence the embedded-derivative restatement reflects a broader controls weakness, or a Section 382 ownership-change event that caps the buyback.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July / early August), which will show whether the four-quarter beat streak continues and whether Med-supp margin is stabilizing. The year-end 2026 NAIC C-1 CLO factor adoption is the key medium-term regulatory checkpoint.

## Conclusion

CNO Financial is a well-run, well-capitalized specialty insurer with a defensible middle-income niche, genuine operating-earnings momentum, a structurally de-risked LTC book, and disciplined capital return through buybacks and a rising dividend. The 375% RBC ratio and 26% debt/cap ex-AOCI confirm a healthy balance sheet that standardized industrial screens badly misrepresent. The fundamental trajectory that lifted prior scores remains intact.

The constraint is price. After a 37% twelve-month re-rating to a level at or just above the consensus target range, the stock is fairly valued at ~11.7x current-year operating EPS, in line with its closest peers and offering a total return anchored to earnings growth and capital return rather than to a mispricing the market has overlooked. We maintain a HOLD and would look to accumulate on weakness toward the low-to-mid $40s or on confirmation that the Med-supp and ROE-expansion tailwinds are converting to sustained margin recovery. The business merits ownership; the current entry point does not offer the asymmetry that would justify a BUY.
