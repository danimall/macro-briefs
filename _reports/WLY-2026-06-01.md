**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

John Wiley & Sons is a 200-year-old academic and scientific publisher that has executed a multi-year portfolio cleanup, divesting low-margin education-services businesses (University Services, Wiley Edge, CrossKnowledge) to refocus on two segments: a high-margin, recurring Research business (~65% of revenue, ~32% adjusted EBITDA margin) and a structurally challenged Learning business (~35%, declining print/courseware). The reported revenue decline from $2,083M (FY22) to $1,678M (FY25) is overwhelmingly deliberate shrinkage; on a like-for-like basis the core grew ~3% in FY25 with adjusted operating income +29% and adjusted EPS +31% to $3.64. The most recent quarter (Q3 FY26, ended Jan 31, 2026) showed Research Publishing +1% constant currency (+4% ex-AI) and Learning -2%, with adjusted EPS of $0.97 (+19%) and a raised cost-savings target of ~$125M annualized.

At $42.83, WLY trades at roughly 11.8x trailing adjusted EPS ($3.64) and ~12x forward, against a publishing peer median of ~32x. The discount is partly justified: Wiley is a slower-growth, more cyclically and politically exposed name than NYT or News Corp, but the gap is wide enough that modest execution on margin and capital returns supports the current price. The dividend yields 3.4% (a multi-decade grower), a $250M buyback ($237M remaining) is being executed below recent averages, and a potential Anthropic copyright settlement recovery offers undetermined upside.

The reasons this is a HOLD rather than a BUY are specific and concentrated. First, ~$800M of variable-rate debt matures November 2027 at a weighted ~5.3% rate; refinancing into a higher-rate or tighter-credit environment is the key balance-sheet watch item. Second, AI is simultaneously the company's most-touted growth line (~$40M FY25, lumpy and ~46% pass-through reselling of others' content) and an explicit threat to its content moat and discovery-driven subscription model; the industry itself is openly skeptical of AI licensing durability. Third, proposed cuts to NIH and Department of Education funding directly threaten the high-margin Research customer base. Fourth, the options market is pricing near-term stress (56.9% near-term IV vs 34.6% HV, backwardation, 16.8% put skew). The fundamentals are improving; the risk overlay caps conviction.

## Company Overview

Wiley operates two core reportable segments after a completed divestiture program:

- **Research (~65% of revenue; $1,075M FY25):** Peer-reviewed STM journals, the Atypon publishing platform, and research solutions sold to academic, corporate, and government libraries, learned societies, and individual researchers. ~88% of Research Publishing revenue comes from journal subscriptions, transformational ("read-and-publish") agreements, and open-access pay-to-publish fees. Recurring, high-margin, and defended by decades of copyrighted content plus entrenched society and institutional relationships.
- **Learning (~35%; $585M FY25):** Academic ($334M) and Professional ($251M) — print/digital books, courseware, assessments, and the "For Dummies" brand. Higher segment EBITDA margin (~37%) but in structural decline from print contraction, used/rental textbook competition, and the higher-education enrollment demographic cliff.

The company is ~83% digital, ~48% recurring revenue, with geographic diversity (~51% USD, 29% GBP, 11% EUR). Fiscal year ends April 30. A dual-class structure (Class A public ~42.8M shares; Class B convertible ~8.8M shares) preserves founding-family influence.

The ~$195M YoY revenue drop is almost entirely lost revenue from divested units. Margin expansion is genuine and cost-driven: the Global Restructuring Program cut real estate footprint ~35%, with O&A expenses down ~6-7% YoY (~$110M of the ~$125M target realized in FY26).

Cash quality is solid: OCF/net income consistently above 1, with 9M FY26 OCF jumping to $103M (from $52M). The company collected $119.5M in June 2025 from monetizing the remaining University Services seller note/earnouts, removing a receivable-risk overhang.

The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss across the last four quarters, with the two most recent quarters beating by 11.7% and 12.3%. The single miss (Q2 FY26, -3.0%) coincides with the seasonally smaller September quarter.

The one genuinely cautionary area is the balance sheet, and it requires careful interpretation. The Altman Z-Score of 1.35 ("distress zone") is largely a structural artifact: the current ratio of 0.54 and negative working capital reflect ~$293-463M of deferred subscription revenue (cash collected in advance from libraries), which is a negative-working-capital float feature of the subscription model, not a liquidity problem. The more real concern is leverage: D/E ~2.58, ~$800M of variable-rate debt under a $1.115B revolver and term loan maturing November 2027, partially hedged ($450M swaps), weighted-average rate ~5.3%, in covenant compliance. Goodwill and intangibles of ~$1.7B sit against ~$750M of equity, though core reporting units passed FY25 impairment tests.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is modest but real, concentrated in the Research segment. Research Publishing grew +1% constant currency in Q3 FY26 (+4% ex-AI), riding secular tailwinds from rising global R&D spend and research output (open-access article output grew ~16-24% YoY). Learning is the offsetting drag (-2% CC, Professional -5%), and is structurally challenged.

The defining growth question is AI licensing. Management leans heavily on this narrative: AI license revenue grew from $23M (FY24) to ~$40M (FY25), with $42.3M in 9M FY26. Three factors temper the story:

1. **Lumpiness** — Q3 FY26 AI revenue was $7.3M vs ~$9M in the prior-year quarter, so the line is not a smooth ramp.
2. **Quality** — ~$19.4M of 9M FY26 AI revenue is pass-through content licensed from other publishers and resold via Wiley's Research Solutions, meaning roughly 46% of recent AI revenue is intermediary margin on others' content, not monetization of Wiley's own assets.
3. **Industry skepticism** — external reporting (Nieman Lab, Digiday, Publishers Weekly, late 2025-early 2026) reflects growing doubt that AI licensing becomes a durable, meaningful revenue stream for publishers, with one analysis describing the market as "effectively stalled."

There is no estimate-revision data provided in the available sources that would flag a forward acceleration. The reported +31% FY25 adjusted EPS growth was driven primarily by cost-out and the comparison against an impairment-laden prior year, not a sustainable top-line acceleration. Forward growth is more plausibly low-single-digit revenue with continued margin and buyback-driven EPS leverage in the high-single-digits.

The Anthropic copyright settlement is a discrete potential upside catalyst: the August 2025 authors' class action settled for $1.5B (preliminarily approved September 2025), Wiley confirmed its content is among the pirated works, and it will file claims for an as-yet-undetermined recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

Wiley's Research segment sits in an oligopolistic STM publishing industry alongside Elsevier (RELX), Springer Nature, and Taylor & Francis (Informa). The moat is durable: brand journals, learned-society relationships, switching costs, and decades of copyrighted content. Wiley is well-positioned within secular tailwinds of global R&D growth and open-access expansion, and the segment trajectory is stable-to-improving.

The competitive risk is AI-driven disintermediation. The same high-quality, citable scientific content that makes Wiley an attractive AI training/grounding partner is also the content most exposed to unlicensed scraping and to AI-powered discovery tools that could erode the search-and-subscribe behaviors underpinning journal revenue. The direction of the AI effect on Wiley's moat is not yet resolved.

The Learning segment competes with Pearson, McGraw Hill, and Cengage in a structurally declining print/courseware market facing the U.S. enrollment demographic cliff. This is the deteriorating leg of the franchise.

Net competitive trajectory: Research stable-to-improving, Learning declining, AI an unresolved two-sided force.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Debt refinancing wall (Nov 2027).** Refinancing the ~$800M variable-rate maturity into a higher-rate or tighter-credit environment would raise interest expense and is the primary balance-sheet risk. [unverified] macro claims about specific long-end yield levels and credit-spread conditions in the 2026-2027 window are not supported by the provided sources; the maturity nonetheless remains the key refinancing watch item.
2. **AI as cannibalization and IP risk.** Beyond the upside narrative, generative AI could disrupt existing products, enable mass copyright infringement, and add compliance costs.
3. **U.S. federal research/education funding cuts (NEW, elevated).** The 10-K explicitly cites proposed NIH and Department of Education cuts. Sustained reductions would directly hit the high-margin Research customer base — the single most company-specific macro risk.
4. **China concentration.** ~30% of CY2024 articles had China-based authors; trade tensions, foreign-journal warning lists, and APC caps could pressure volumes.
5. **Open-access transition risk.** Funder mandates to make research free could erode the subscription model; transformational agreements mitigate but pricing terms remain an overhang.
6. **Learning secular decline** and the enrollment demographic cliff.
7. **Customer/agent concentration:** agents ~18% of revenue; top-10 book customers ~36% of receivables.
8. **ERP/enterprise modernization execution risk**, explicitly flagged as a potential disruption to order management and financial reporting.
9. **Leadership instability.** A recurring series of Item 5.02 (director/officer departure or election) 8-Ks since late 2024 (Nov-2024, Dec-2024, Mar-2025 ×2, Jun-2025 ×2, and May-2026) signals an extended period of board and C-suite churn, alongside cumulative TSR materially lagging peers (137.1 vs 205.4).

The 10-Q (March 2026) noted no material changes to risk factors since the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress that contrasts with the constructive fundamental story. Near-term ATM IV is 56.9% against 34.6% trailing realized vol (a rich 22.3pp spread), the term structure is in backwardation (56.9% near-term vs 48.6% six-month), and the 1-month put/call skew is elevated at +16.8% with an implied move of ±10.9% into the June 18 expiry. This configuration typically signals an upcoming binary event or hedging into a catalyst (likely the next earnings print) rather than steady-state conditions. Short interest is also elevated at 12.2% of float with 10.1 days to cover — meaningful bearish institutional positioning. For a HOLD this is a flag to be patient on entry; the market is pricing the possibility of a near-term disappointment, and the high days-to-cover does create modest squeeze potential on any positive surprise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$58-66):** Cost-out delivers the full ~$125M run-rate, Research continues compounding low-to-mid single digits on R&D tailwinds, the multiple re-rates toward the low end of the peer range (14-15x) as the turnaround is recognized, the buyback shrinks the share count, and an Anthropic settlement recovery plus durable AI licensing add upside. At 16x on ~$4.10 adjusted EPS, the stock approaches the $66 target.

**Bear Case (~$30-33):** The Nov 2027 debt refinances at materially higher rates, NIH/education funding cuts hit Research volumes, AI licensing proves to be a stalled, low-quality revenue line while AI discovery tools erode subscriptions, Learning's decline accelerates with the demographic cliff, and the multiple stays at ~10x on flat-to-down EPS. This maps to the December 2025-February 2026 lows around $30-32, roughly 25-30% downside.

**Base Case (~$50-54):** Mid-single-digit adjusted EPS growth (~$3.85-4.00), a modest re-rating to ~13-14x as the cost story is digested, continued dividend growth and buyback. This implies ~20-25% upside over 12-18 months, with a 3.4% yield paid while waiting.

The risk/reward is favorable but not asymmetric enough for a high-conviction call. The downside (-25-30%) is wide given the debt wall and funding risk, and the upside lacks a single near-term forcing catalyst beyond continued execution and a contingent Anthropic recovery.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean), 12-18 month horizon.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clear progress on refinancing the November 2027 debt at terms that do not materially raise interest expense, removing the largest balance-sheet overhang.
- Two consecutive quarters of Research Publishing growth ex-AI accelerating to ≥5% CC, confirming the core compounds independent of the AI narrative.
- A quantified Anthropic settlement recovery of meaningful size (e.g., >$50M), or AI licensing revenue showing a durable, own-content-driven ramp rather than lumpy pass-through.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Confirmation of material NIH/Department of Education funding cuts flowing through to declining library/institutional subscription revenue.
- A guidance cut to the cost-savings target or adjusted EPS, or a return to GAAP-adjusted operating margin compression.
- Refinancing pushed close to the 2027 maturity with no plan, or covenant pressure emerging as rates stay elevated.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q4/FY26 full-year print, expected June 2026) is the natural checkpoint, particularly given the options market is pricing a ±10.9% move into the June 18 expiry. The November 2027 debt maturity is the dominant medium-term gate.

## Conclusion

Wiley is a completed-turnaround STM publisher whose underlying economics are healthier than the headline declining-revenue and distress-zone Z-score screens suggest: a high-margin recurring Research business, ~$126M FCF after product development, +31% adjusted EPS growth in FY25, an active buyback executed below recent averages, and a 3.4% growing dividend. At ~12x forward adjusted EPS against a ~32x peer median, the relative valuation is genuinely cheap, and the base case offers ~20-25% upside while paying yield.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the concentration of unresolved risks against the absence of a single forcing catalyst: a November 2027 variable-rate debt wall; an AI dynamic that is both the headline growth line and a threat to the content moat, with the line itself lumpy and ~46% pass-through; proposed federal research-funding cuts aimed at the core customer base; and an options market pricing near-term stress (rich IV, backwardation, +16.8% put skew, 12.2% short interest). The fundamentals support a constructive growth lean; the risk overlay caps conviction. I would own this for the yield and turnaround optionality, add on weakness toward the low-$30s if the debt and funding risks clarify favorably, and upgrade only on refinancing visibility or confirmed core-Research acceleration.
