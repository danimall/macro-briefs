**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Acadia Healthcare is the largest publicly-traded pure-play behavioral-health provider in the U.S., operating 275 facilities and roughly 12,400 beds across 40 states and Puerto Rico. The operating business is performing: Q1 2026 revenue rose 7.6% to $828.8M, same-facility revenue grew 7.3%, admissions grew 6.5%, and labor costs stabilized (salaries/wages/benefits ex-stock comp fell to 55.4% of revenue from 56.7%). The 2025 reported net loss of $1.10B was approximately 90% a non-cash goodwill impairment ($996.2M) that wrote down prior acquisition premium, not an operational collapse. Underlying demand for mental-health and substance-use-disorder services remains healthy.

The equity story is defined by two facts that pull in opposite directions. First, the market has re-rated the stock roughly 3x from its ~$11-12 trough in January 2026 (the strike price on the returning CEO's option grant is an implicit management valuation floor) to $34.50 today, a move that has captured most of the low-hanging turnaround recovery. Second, the single largest determinant of the equity's value remains unresolved and unquantifiable: a DOJ Criminal Division grand jury investigation, opened September 2024 with subpoenas re-issued December 2024, into Acadia's acute-care admissions, length-of-stay, and billing practices, running alongside SEC subpoenas. Potential outcomes span monetary penalties, a Corporate Integrity Agreement, or program exclusion. The company cannot estimate liability.

We move to HOLD from a prior AVOID. The case for AVOID was strongest when the stock traded near its trough and the risk/reward was heavily skewed to further downside if the probe turned adverse; that asymmetry has narrowed as the price tripled and operating metrics confirmed the demand base is intact. But we cannot underwrite a BUY: the criminal investigation is a binary that no amount of operating strength offsets, the Medicaid mix (60.7% and rising) sits squarely in front of OBBBA funding cuts, and governance has been in open turmoil with a CEO, CFO, and COO all turning over within roughly six months. This is a coin-flip on legal resolution priced at a full-cycle recovery multiple.

## Company Overview

Acadia operates as a single reportable segment across four service lines. In Q1 2026: acute inpatient psychiatric generated $470.7M (57% of revenue, the highest-acuity and fastest-growing line); Comprehensive Treatment Centers (CTCs, opioid medication-assisted treatment) $140.4M; specialty treatment (addiction/residential recovery) $128.1M; and residential treatment centers $89.6M.

The growth model rests on five pathways: bed additions at existing facilities, joint ventures with nonprofit health systems (a Tufts Medicine JV opened Q1 2026, joining historical partners including Henry Ford, Geisinger, and Ascension), de novo facilities, acquisitions, and continuum-of-care expansion. The JV structure is material to the balance sheet: Acadia consolidates 17 facilities as variable interest entities ($1.40B of restricted assets), owning 65-88% with redeemable non-controlling interest of $210M.

Payor mix is the structural vulnerability. As of Q1 2026, Medicaid was 60.7% of revenue (up sharply from 55.9% a year earlier), Commercial 22.4%, Medicare 14.0%, and self-pay 1.8%. Approximately 75% of revenue is government-dependent, and the Medicaid share is rising into a period of announced federal Medicaid retrenchment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is intact; reported profitability was distorted by non-cash and legal charges.** Revenue moved from $2.93B (2023) to $3.15B (2024) to approximately $3.31B (2025). The 2025 net loss attributable to Acadia was driven by the goodwill impairment (goodwill fell from $2.26B to $1.30B), roughly $151M in legal settlements, and roughly $164M in transaction/legal/investigation costs. Stripping the impairment, the operating business remained revenue-positive and growing.

**Margins are razor-thin at the net line because of the litigation and interest load.** Q1 2026 net income to Acadia was $4.1M, a 0.5% net margin, because interest expense ($38.3M, up from $29.2M a year earlier), the $13.8M uninsured Sandoval settlement, and roughly $22M of transaction/transition costs consume nearly all operating income. Revenue per patient day rose 5.6%, but average length of stay fell 4.6%, which we flag below as it intersects directly with the DOJ probe.

**Cash flow has pivoted from expansion burn to defense.** FY2025 free cash flow was deeply negative, roughly negative $440M (operating cash flow ~$132M against capex ~$572M), reflecting the aggressive de novo and expansion build. Q1 2026 shows a deliberate retrenchment: capex cut to $76.6M from $174.6M a year earlier, with expansion capex nearly halved; operating cash flow rose to $61.5M, aided by a one-time $31.5M securities-litigation insurance recovery. Cash stood at $158.5M.

**Leverage is in compliance but with less cushion than the headline suggests.** Total debt is roughly $2.53B (Term Loan A $634M, revolver $434M, plus $450M 5.5% notes due 2028, $475M 5.0% due 2029, and $550M 7.375% due 2033). Consolidated total net leverage is 3.9x against a 5.0x covenant. The Altman Z-Score of approximately 1.5 places the company in the distress zone, reflecting the $762M accumulated deficit post-impairment and the leverage. The March 2025 refinancing extended maturities to 2030 but at a materially higher cost (7.375% versus legacy 5.0-5.5%); a 1% rate move equals roughly $10.6M pretax on approximately $1.06B of floating-rate debt. The higher-for-longer rate environment is a live headwind for a business carrying this debt load.

**Capital allocation has whipsawed.** A $300M buyback was authorized in February 2025; $50M was executed in Q1 2025 at roughly $29/share, then halted with $250M remaining and zero repurchased in Q1 2026. The freeze is appropriate given cash needs and legal uncertainty, but the sequence (buying at ~$29, then abandoning the program at the trough) is not a flattering capital-allocation record. No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

The demand side is genuinely healthy, and this is the strongest pillar of the case. Behavioral-health demand is structurally supported: rising mental-health utilization, the opioid crisis sustaining CTC volumes, and a fragmented market that favors a scale consolidator. The four-quarter earnings track record is clean — four consecutive beats, including a 36.4% EPS beat in Q1 2026 ($0.37 versus $0.27 estimate) and a 17.1% beat in Q2 2025.

Estimate revisions are mixed but net constructive at the annual level. Current-year EPS consensus of $1.52 is up 3.2% over 90 days and next-year of $1.76 is up 4.8%, both modestly positive. The current-quarter estimate, however, is down 21.0% over 90 days (to $0.36), reflecting the drag from legal and transition charges. The forward-year revisions do not clear the +30% threshold that would flag a strong growth-momentum buy signal, so we treat the growth picture as solid-but-not-accelerating.

The capex pullback caps forward unit growth. Expansion spend was cut roughly 66% year-over-year in Q1 2026, four facilities (251 beds) were closed, and $62M of assets are held for sale. This is prudent balance-sheet management while the investigation is unresolved, but it means the de novo and bed-addition engine that historically drove volume is being deliberately throttled. Growth for the next 12-18 months rests on same-facility volume and rate, not new capacity.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation here is unusually treacherous. Trailing and forward P/E are meaningless (reported negative, driven by the 2025 impairment). On the cleaner forward earnings basis, the stock trades at roughly 22.7x the current-year consensus and roughly 19.6x the next-year consensus. Against the Health Care Facilities peer set — NHC at 24.9x, OPCH at 16.2x, SEM at 15.5x, with a peer median of 24.9x — Acadia is not obviously cheap once you account for its distress-zone balance sheet, its government-payor concentration, and the criminal-investigation overhang that none of the peers carry.

The analyst target dispersion tells the real story: mean $31.71, median $31.50, high $40.00, low $13.00. The stock at $34.50 already trades above both the mean and median targets, and the $13-to-$40 range (roughly a 3x spread) is the market's admission that this is a binary. That is the opposite of a mispricing we can lean into. Our lessons consistently show that a low or compressed multiple in a value-trap setup sorts the wrong way, and that analyst targets act as floors only on cheap inflection stocks with reasonable multiples — neither condition is cleanly met here, because the stock trades at a peer-median multiple with a censored downside tail.

The most instructive valuation anchor is the returning CEO's January 2026 option grant, struck at $11.68 (with the filing explicitly adjusting the price input for the appointment announcement). That marks the trough. From there the stock has tripled. The re-rating has captured the recovery from over-punished-loss to recognized-turnaround; what remains is the investigation outcome, which is not a valuation input so much as a probability-weighted binary. We score valuation at 4.5: a stock priced at a full recovery multiple with an unpriceable tail that the option-adjusted market range explicitly acknowledges.

## Competitive Landscape

Acadia's competitive position is the report's genuine bright spot. It is the scale leader in a fragmented, structurally growing market, with a moat built on geographic breadth (275 facilities, 40 states), referral networks, and capital-light health-system JV partnerships that supply both capital and referral pipelines. Same-facility admissions growth indicates the company is holding or gaining share on the demand side, and labor-cost stabilization points to operating discipline returning after the prior wage-inflation cycle.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving on the operating dimension, which supports a 6.0 competitive-position score. The caveat is that the entire behavioral-health sector faces intensifying reimbursement pressure and enforcement scrutiny — Acadia's DOJ probe is the sharpest example of a sector-wide theme around admissions, length-of-stay, and involuntary holds. The company's scale is a moat but also makes it the most visible enforcement target. Competitive position and idiosyncratic risk are, unusually, in tension here: the leadership that constitutes the moat is the same leadership that draws the scrutiny.

## Risk Assessment

**The DOJ criminal investigation is the dominant, unquantifiable risk.** The DOJ Criminal Division opened a coordinated multi-agency criminal investigation into acute-care admissions, length-of-stay, and billing practices; the SEC has issued parallel subpoenas across acute and CTC lines. No findings have been disclosed and liability cannot be estimated. Outcomes range from fines to a Corporate Integrity Agreement to program exclusion — the last of which would be existential given the government-payor revenue concentration. The tension with the Q1 2026 length-of-stay decline of 4.6% warrants watching: shortening length of stay while length of stay is the metric under investigation may be a defensive operational response, but it also modestly pressures per-episode economics.

**Medicaid funding cuts (OBBBA, July 2025).** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act reduces federal Medicaid spend, imposes work requirements and 6-month eligibility redeterminations (state compliance by December 31, 2026), prohibits new/increased provider taxes, and caps state directed payments at Medicare rates (grandfathered programs cut from January 2028). With Medicaid the largest payor and rising, this is a direct structural threat that builds through 2026-28. Management cites exemptions for SUD/chronic populations, but the direction is unambiguous.

**Litigation and abuse exposure.** The 2019 securities class action settled for $179M (with $31.5M insurance recovered in Q1 2026); a new securities class action (Kachrodia, covering February 2020-February 2025) has a motion to dismiss pending. Professional/general liability reserves nearly doubled from ~$88M to ~$182M in 2025. Insurance now excludes sexual molestation/abuse claims (from September 2025) with higher premiums and lower limits — a material tail-risk deterioration for a company whose largest historical loss (Desert Hills, $400M) came from exactly that category.

**Governance instability.** A CEO (Christopher Hunter departed January 2026), CFO (Heather Dixon out, Todd Young installed), and COO (Nasser Khan departed) all turned over within roughly six months, with the prior CEO (Debra Osteen) brought back. This typically signals board crisis-management and elevates execution risk even as it can reset accountability.

Given the binary criminal probe, the OBBBA structural threat, the deteriorated insurance structure, and the governance turmoil, we score risk factors at 3.0. This is an improvement from the prior 2.5/3.0 range because the operating base has proven resilient and the securities settlement is funded, but the central binary remains unpriced and unpriceable.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, meaningful uncertainty. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 72.6% versus 1-year historical volatility of 65.9% (rich by 6.7 points), at the 75th percentile of the current chain, with the nearest expiry implying a ±17.8% move. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 72.6% versus 6-month 57.9%), which the market uses to price near-term event stress. The near-term put/call skew is actually call-heavy (-5.3%) and the put/call volume ratio is negligible (0.07), suggesting speculative call positioning into the recovery rather than heavy downside hedging; but the 3-month tenor flips to a +16.9% put skew, consistent with hedging around a later resolution window. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV jumped from 54.1% to 72.6% as the price rose 37.5%, meaning the market is pricing rising uncertainty alongside the rally rather than complacency. For a stock this binary, the elevated near-term IV signals the market expects a catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($40-45+).** The DOJ investigation resolves without an existential outcome (a monetary settlement and Corporate Integrity Agreement rather than exclusion), removing the overhang and allowing the market to re-rate the stock toward the ~24.9x peer-median multiple on rising earnings. Same-facility demand continues at 6-7%, labor costs stay contained, the deleveraging pivot restores the balance sheet, and the new management team (equity-incentivized at the trough) executes a clean turnaround. On $1.76 next-year EPS at a 24x multiple with the overhang lifted, the stock reaches the low-$40s, matching the high analyst target of $40.

**Bear Case (sub-$13).** The DOJ probe yields criminal charges, a large penalty, or program-exclusion risk that threatens the government-payor revenue base. OBBBA Medicaid cuts bite harder than management guides as state redeterminations reduce covered lives. An abuse claim lands in the newly-uninsured category, driving a large self-funded loss against a distress-zone balance sheet. The stock returns toward the trough and the low analyst target of $13, a roughly 62% decline from current levels.

The distribution is bimodal. The base case sits closer to the middle — an investigation that drags without resolution, keeping the stock range-bound in the mid-$20s to mid-$30s as operating results grind higher but the overhang caps the multiple. That base case does not offer enough asymmetric upside from $34.50 to justify a BUY, and the demand base and re-rated price do not justify an AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as uncertain — the recommendation reflects insufficient evidence for a confident directional call given the unresolved binary, not a positive appreciation thesis or an income thesis.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Resolution of the DOJ criminal investigation on non-existential terms (settlement/CIA without program exclusion), which would remove the primary overhang and support a re-rating toward peer multiples.
- Sustained forward EPS revisions turning positive at the current-quarter level (currently -21% over 90 days) alongside continued 6%+ same-facility volume growth, confirming operating momentum has fully decoupled from the legal drag.
- Net leverage falling below 3.5x on realized deleveraging, materially improving the balance-sheet risk profile.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any indication of criminal charges, a monetary penalty exceeding a manageable threshold, or program-exclusion risk emerging from the DOJ probe.
- Medicaid mix continuing to climb above 62-63% while OBBBA state compliance (due December 31, 2026) begins reducing covered lives, signaling revenue-base erosion.
- A covenant-cushion deterioration (net leverage approaching 4.5x) or a large uninsured abuse claim in the newly-excluded category.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report and any material 8-K disclosure regarding the investigation status. The December 31, 2026 OBBBA state-compliance deadline and the Kachrodia motion-to-dismiss ruling are the near-term dated catalysts to monitor. The DOJ resolution timing is not company-controlled and is the key unknown.

## Conclusion

We rate Acadia Healthcare a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The operating business is fundamentally sound — the scale leader in a growing, fragmented market, with 7%+ same-facility growth, stabilizing labor costs, and a management team that has sensibly pivoted from expansion burn to deleveraging. The 2025 loss was overwhelmingly a non-cash impairment, and the market has correctly recognized that by re-rating the stock 3x off its January trough.

But the equity is a probability-weighted bet on a DOJ criminal investigation into the company's core admissions and length-of-stay practices, with outcomes that range from manageable to existential and no ability to estimate liability. At $34.50 the stock trades above the mean and median analyst targets at roughly 22.7x forward earnings — a peer-median multiple with a censored downside tail that the $13-$40 target range explicitly prices. The 37.9% short interest (6.8 days to cover) adds squeeze risk in either direction but does not change the underlying binary. The risk/reward that made this an AVOID near the lows has narrowed, but the setup does not offer the asymmetric upside required for a BUY. We would need to see the investigation resolve, or the price compress meaningfully back toward the low-$20s against unchanged fundamentals, before turning constructive.
