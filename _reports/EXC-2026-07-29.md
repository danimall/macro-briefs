**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Exelon is the largest pure-play regulated transmission and distribution utility in the United States, serving roughly 10.7 million electric and 4 million gas customers across six operating utilities in seven jurisdictions. Since spinning off Constellation in February 2022, Exelon owns no generation. It buys power on behalf of customers and passes the cost through with little or no markup, which makes reported earnings essentially immune to commodity prices and, through revenue decoupling at most of its utilities, largely immune to weather and volume. The investment case rests on a capital plan: $41.7B of capex from 2026-2029 driving rate base from $64.6B to $87.4B (7.9% CAGR), which management translates into 5-7% adjusted operating EPS growth (guided to the top end) plus a ~3.65% dividend for a 9-11% total return target.

The wires business is one of the most insulated in the sector, yet the stock's multiple is exposed to a risk the company does not control. Wholesale supply costs in Exelon's PJM footprint rose more than 70% year-over-year in 2025, and roughly 75% of customer bill increases are driven by supply, not by Exelon's T&D charges. Those bill shocks generate political pressure that lands on the regulated wires utility regardless of where the cost originates. Maryland's Utility RELIEF Act (April 2026), which restricts forecast test years, scales back EmPOWER, caps executive-comp recovery, and mandates refunds for multi-year rate plan underspend, is the first concrete manifestation. ComEd's appeal of an 8.905% allowed distribution ROE is another. This affordability dynamic, not the durability of the earnings stream, is the swing variable for the valuation.

At $47.31, roughly 6.6% below the 52-week high of $50.65, EXC trades at ~17.3x forward EPS ($2.86 consensus current-year) versus a peer median near 22.5x, with a yield above the peer median of 3.3%. The discount is partly justified by higher leverage (D/E ~3.05) and continuous equity dilution to fund a deeply negative-FCF growth model, and partly by the regulatory overhang. Near-term estimate revisions have drifted negative (current-quarter -9.3% over 90 days), which is inconsistent with a re-rating catalyst. We rate EXC a HOLD on an income basis: the total-return math is intact and the balance sheet is sound, but there is no asymmetric entry here and the affordability/regulatory tail caps upside.

## Company Overview

Exelon operates six regulated utilities with no single jurisdiction accounting for more than ~35% of rate base:
- **ComEd** (Northern Illinois/Chicago) — largest at ~35% of rate base, electric only
- **PECO** (SE Pennsylvania/Philadelphia) — ~22%, electric and gas
- **BGE** (Central Maryland/Baltimore) — ~18%, electric and gas
- **Pepco** (DC and suburban Maryland) — ~13%, electric only
- **DPL** (Delaware/Maryland) — ~7%, electric and gas
- **ACE** (Southern New Jersey) — ~6%, electric only

The structural distinction that matters is the wires-only model. Revenue decoupling at ComEd, BGE, Pepco, DPL-Maryland, and ACE neutralizes weather and usage volatility. Only PECO and DPL-Delaware retain meaningful volume and weather exposure. Combined with commodity pass-through, this produces one of the most predictable earnings streams among large-cap utilities. The company's operating advantages are real and measurable: O&M has grown near 2% annually since 2016, well below inflation; reliability metrics (SAIFI/SAIDI) have been top-quartile for ten consecutive years with ComEd top-decile; and Exelon is one of only four U.S. operators of 765kV transmission with 11,197 circuit miles.

The company sits inside the PJM interconnection, the U.S. grid region under the most acute capacity stress. PJM is forecasting roughly a 6 GW supply shortfall by 2027 and saw prices spike above $2,000/MWh in the summer 2026 heat. This positions Exelon at the center of the AI/data-center demand super-cycle, with roughly 18 GW of committed data-center pipeline (about 45% secured via industry-first Transmission Security Agreements that shift stranded-cost risk to hyperscalers) and Northern Illinois data-center demand growing at roughly a 26% CAGR.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue grew from $16.9B in 2022 to $25.0B in 2025 (+8.1% YoY in 2025), though a large portion of the top-line figure reflects pass-through supply costs that carry no margin. Net income to common rose from $2,460M in 2024 to $2,768M in 2025 (+12.5%). GAAP diluted EPS moved from $2.45 to $2.73; adjusted operating EPS from $2.50 to $2.77. The 2025 gains came from rate increases at ComEd, PECO, BGE, and PHI, higher return on regulatory assets, and favorable weather at PECO.

**Earnings quality and delivery.** Exelon has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (Q3 2025 by 9.6%, Q4 2025 by 6.8%, Q2 2025 by 5.0%, Q1 2026 by 1.8%), which supports confidence in the guidance framework. Q1 2026 adjusted operating EPS was $0.91 versus $0.92 a year earlier, roughly flat, driven by timing of distribution earnings and tax items. Full-year guidance of $2.81-$2.91 was reaffirmed, so the flat first quarter is a timing artifact rather than deterioration, though the year is back-half weighted.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Operating cash flow of $6.25B in 2025 against $8.53B of capex produced negative free cash flow of -$2.28B. This is structural, not a warning sign: a utility investing at a 7.9% rate-base CAGR funds the gap with a planned mix of debt and equity. Long-term debt is $42.8B and total debt roughly $51.2B (Q1 2026), with D/E ~3.05, normal for a T&D utility. The Altman Z-score of 0.89 is a false distress signal; the model penalizes the high leverage and low asset turnover inherent to regulated utilities. Exelon is solidly investment grade (ExCorp Baa2/BBB+ stable, ComEd A1/A), targeting ~14% FFO/debt with 100-200 bps of cushion above downgrade thresholds. Two watch items surfaced in April 2026: Moody's placed PECO under review for downgrade and S&P cut BGE senior unsecured from A to A-. Neither is thesis-changing at the consolidated level, but both bear monitoring in a higher-for-longer rate environment where the negative-FCF financing model reprices.

**Financing.** Equity needs are $3.4B through 2029 (~$850M per year, under 2% of market cap annually), executed via ATM forward contracts (~37% already priced). About 43% of 2026 debt has been issued. The dilution is modest and pre-planned, but it is a persistent drag on per-share growth and, together with debt costs, is the mechanism through which higher rates erode the value of the rate-base build.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is the capital plan. The capex program (revised up from $41.3B, adding $1.5B to transmission and trimming $1.1B from distribution) drives the rate-base CAGR cited above. Management guides adjusted operating EPS to the top end of a 5-7% CAGR through 2029, with a ~60% payout ratio funding roughly 5% annual dividend growth (raised to $0.42/quarter for 2026 from $0.40).

Transmission is the underappreciated lever. Transmission rate base is growing roughly 16% per year, earns higher FERC-regulated ROEs (10.35-11.5% including the RTO adder) than distribution, and is less politicized than state-level distribution rate cases. Management cites $12-17B of incremental transmission opportunity beyond the plan (MISO Tranche 2.1, PJM RTEP competitive windows where Exelon won $1.2B, Brandon Shores ~$1.5B, Tri-County $1B+). No single project exceeds ~3% of the plan, limiting execution concentration.

Demand support comes from the data-center pipeline, where the Transmission Security Agreements contractually protect existing customers and Exelon's balance sheet from stranded-cost risk, a more de-risked structure than most peers offer. ComEd announced 6.5+ GW of new TSAs in January 2026.

The tension is that estimate revisions do not confirm accelerating momentum. Current-quarter consensus fell 9.3% over 90 days and next-quarter fell 5.9%, while full-year and next-year estimates were essentially flat. This is a stable-compounder profile. The load-growth narrative is real, but its per-share benefit is absorbed by dilution, higher financing costs, and the possibility of unfavorable regulatory outcomes on allowed ROE and test-year mechanics. Growth prospects merit a mid-6 score: durable and well-articulated, but not accelerating in the numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.31 and ~$2.86 current-year consensus EPS, EXC trades at ~17.3x forward earnings. The peer set (Electric Utilities) shows a median P/E of 22.5x: ETR 28.1x, VST 24.7x, WEC 22.5x, PEG 17.4x, EIX 7.9x. EXC's discount to the median is meaningful but explicable. Exelon carries higher leverage than most peers, funds a deeply negative-FCF plan with continuous equity issuance that dilutes per-share growth, and faces a more acute affordability/regulatory overhang given its PJM supply-cost exposure. PEG at 17.4x is the closest comparable in profile and multiple, suggesting the market is pricing the two similarly-situated wires-heavy names in line.

On a growth-adjusted basis, the picture is fair rather than cheap. A 5-7% EPS CAGR against ~17.3x forward gives a PEG near 2.5-3.5x, which does not screen as inexpensive for a regulated utility. The ~3.65% yield provides real support, but the absolute yield is not high enough to constitute a standalone value case in a 3.50-3.75% policy-rate environment. Analyst targets — low $41, mean $49.56, median $49.50, high $58 — bracket the current price with roughly 5% upside to the mean.

We treat the cheapness relative to peers with appropriate skepticism. A below-median multiple in this sector more often reflects a structural discount (leverage, dilution, regulatory risk) than a mispricing, and cheap utility multiples have historically been poor predictors of forward outperformance absent a specific catalyst. There is no identifiable near-term re-rating trigger here; the ComEd Grid Plan order is not expected until December 15, 2026, and near-term estimates are falling. The valuation is fair for the risk and growth on offer, warranting a 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Exelon's competitive position within regulated T&D is strong and stable to modestly improving. Scale, executional discipline, a decade of top-quartile reliability, and jurisdictional diversification give it a durable operating advantage. Its 765kV transmission franchise and FERC-regulated transmission growth are genuine moats less exposed to state political risk.

The data-center opportunity is the principal source of upside to competitive position, and Exelon's Transmission Security Agreement structure is a differentiated response that protects both the balance sheet and existing ratepayers, reducing regulatory backlash risk relative to peers pursuing large-load growth without such protections. Against this, the score trajectory shows competitive position declining from 7.5 to 6.5 across prior reports, reflecting the affordability backlash and a new distributed-energy entrant. Base Power launched in Chicagoland in June 2026 offering supply 25% below ComEd's rate. That threat is to supply, not to ComEd's regulated wires revenue, but it reinforces the affordability narrative that is pressuring the regulatory environment. On balance the wires competitive position is stable-strong; we hold the score at 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is not the wires business but supply-cost-driven affordability politics. The resulting political pressure lands on the regulated utility even though it does not set commodity prices. Maryland's Utility RELIEF Act is the first concrete outcome; its forecast test-year prohibition, pending a PSC study, already pushed Pepco-Maryland from a forecast to a traditional test-year rate case. This mechanism directly compresses the timeliness and certainty of cost recovery that underpins utility ROE.

Additional risks:
- **Allowed ROE pressure.** ComEd's 8.905% distribution ROE is under appeal (along with a 50% equity ratio and denial of pension asset return) to the Illinois Appellate Court. An adverse outcome would weigh on the largest rate base.
- **PJM generation adequacy.** The forecast ~6 GW shortfall by 2027 could constrain load growth and stress reliability, indirectly limiting rate-base expansion.
- **Goodwill.** $6.6B ($2.6B ComEd, $4.0B PHI) is exposed to impairment on adverse regulatory outcomes.
- **ComEd Chicago franchise.** Terminable on one-year notice since 2020, with a municipalization option held by the city. No notice issued, but flagged as a potential material adverse event.
- **Higher-for-longer rates.** With the Fed at 3.50-3.75% and a December-hike probability that has moved around, the negative-FCF, equity-dilutive model reprices at higher cost of capital, the same headwind visible in the April 2026 PECO/BGE rating actions.
- **DOE grant terminations** under federal energy-policy shifts (no stated material financial impact, but a funding headwind).

We score risk at 5.0. The regulatory/affordability catalysts are specific and near-term, and the two April credit-watch items are recent surfacings not fully priced. This is consistent with the prior report's downgrade of the risk dimension.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term event stress. ATM one-month IV is 24.2% against one-year historical volatility of 19.0% (rich by 5.2 points), the term structure is in backwardation (24.2% near versus 18.4% at six months), and put/call skew is elevated at 3.4% with unusual October $43 put activity (13.8x volume/OI). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.1%. The prior snapshot showed a corrupted 391.7% ATM IV that has normalized to 24.2%, so the level is now informative. The backwardation and put skew signal hedging demand into a catalyst, most likely the next earnings print and the ongoing regulatory docket flow, rather than a directional collapse thesis. Short interest is low at 4.0% of float (3.8 days to cover), so this is not a crowded bearish position. The signal is consistent with our HOLD: the market is pricing modest downside protection, not conviction in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($55-58).** Data-center load in PJM converts to approved, recoverable rate base faster and larger than modeled; transmission opportunity beyond the plan ($12-17B) materializes at FERC ROEs; affordability legislation proves a one-year adjustment rather than a structural re-rating of allowed returns; and the stock re-rates toward the peer median as the growth durability is recognized. On $3.04 next-year EPS at ~19-20x plus dividend, the stock reaches the mid-to-high $50s. This is the analyst high target of $58.

**Base case ($49-50).** The company delivers top-end EPS growth and ~5% dividend growth, generating a ~9-11% total return, but the multiple holds near 17-18x because the affordability overhang and dilution cap re-rating. Roughly consensus mean of $49.56, about 5% above current price plus the yield.

**Bear case ($41-42).** ComEd's ROE appeal fails, additional states follow Maryland with test-year restrictions and refund mechanisms, allowed returns compress, and higher-for-longer rates raise financing costs on the negative-FCF model. Estimate revisions continue to fall, and the stock de-rates to the low end of the analyst range and the 52-week low ($42.58), roughly -11% to -13% from current.

The distribution is roughly symmetric with a mild dividend-supported skew, which is why this is a hold rather than a buy or an avoid.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an income rationale. The thesis for holding is the ~9-11% total-return math backed by a sound balance sheet and a well-executed capital plan, not an expectation of near-term re-rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A constructive resolution of ComEd's Illinois Appellate Court ROE appeal, or a favorable ComEd Grid Plan order (ICC Docket 26-0047, order expected December 15, 2026) that reaffirms recovery certainty.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity turning current-quarter and next-quarter consensus from negative to positive, confirming the load-growth narrative is flowing into per-share earnings.
- A pullback toward the low-$40s (near the $42.58 52-week low or the $41 analyst floor), which would restore an asymmetric total-return entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Additional states adopting Maryland-style test-year restrictions or refund mechanisms that structurally compress allowed ROE across multiple jurisdictions.
- A consolidated-level credit downgrade (beyond the subsidiary-level PECO/BGE watch items), signaling the financing model is stressed at higher rates.
- Goodwill impairment at ComEd or PHI, which would indicate a genuinely adverse regulatory turn.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings print (guidance reaffirmation and estimate direction), the Maryland PSC test-year study outcome, and the December 15, 2026 ComEd Grid Plan order.

## Conclusion

Exelon is a high-quality, well-managed pure-play regulated wires utility with a durable growth plan and an insulated earnings stream. The score trajectory has declined from 6.4 to 5.8 across three prior reports, all HOLDs, driven by falling risk and management-quality dimensions as the affordability/regulatory overhang crystallized. Current data neither reverses nor materially worsens that trend: fundamentals are intact, the balance sheet is sound, and guidance was reaffirmed, but near-term estimate revisions are negative and the specific regulatory catalysts (ComEd ROE appeal, Maryland RELIEF Act, potential contagion) remain unresolved. We assign an overall score of 5.7.

The stock at $47.31 is fairly priced, a justified discount to peers rather than a mispricing, offering roughly 5% upside to the consensus mean plus a ~3.65% yield, against roughly 11-13% downside in the bear case. The recommendation is HOLD on an income basis. We would become buyers on a pullback into the low-$40s or on a positive resolution of the Illinois ROE appeal and an estimate-revision turn. We would move to AVOID on multi-state regulatory contagion or a consolidated credit downgrade.
