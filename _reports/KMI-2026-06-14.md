**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kinder Morgan operates one of the largest fee-based energy infrastructure franchises in North America: roughly 78,000 miles of pipelines, 136 terminals, ~706 Bcf of working gas storage, and an estimated ~40% of U.S. natural gas production moving across its network. The business is a regulated, take-or-pay toll road. KMI is paid for reserved capacity regardless of utilization, no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and the gas transport book carries roughly a seven-year weighted-average contract life. This cash-flow profile holds up across commodity cycles, and the FY2025 and Q1 2026 results confirm a genuine re-acceleration: FY2025 revenue rose 12% to $16.9B, net income to KMI rose 17% to $3.06B, and Q1 2026 delivered adjusted EPS of $0.48 (a ~19% beat) with adjusted EBITDA up 18% YoY.

The thesis sits at the intersection of two durable demand drivers: U.S. LNG export growth (KMI projects feed Plaquemines, Port Arthur, and the broader Gulf corridor) and natural-gas-fired power demand from AI and data centers (the TVA Cumberland project among them). A ~$10B contracted project backlog, anchored by long-term take-or-pay agreements and visible in ~$35B of remaining performance obligations, supports mid-single-digit-plus EBITDA growth that is self-funded with no need to issue equity. Two investment-grade upgrades in early 2026 (S&P to BBB+, Moody's to Baa1) validate the balance sheet, and the standout governance signal is Executive Chairman Richard Kinder's $26M open-market purchase of 1,000,000 shares at ~$25.96 in October 2025.

We rate KMI a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale and an overall score of 6.5. The business quality and the founder buy are real, but the entry is no longer asymmetric. The stock has gained ~15% over twelve months to $31.94, trades at ~21.5x earnings and a ~3.8% yield, and sits below the analyst mean target of $35.33 but only ~3% above the consensus low of $31. The bulk of the backlog revenue lands in 2028 and beyond, the highest-conviction insider entry was at ~$26 (roughly 19% below the current price), and the energy sector's return profile in this regime argues for owning a quality compounder at the right price rather than adding at the top of its recent range. We would upgrade on a pullback toward the high-$20s or on confirmation that the 2026 EBITDA-above-budget guidance is being raised through the year.

## Company Overview

Kinder Morgan runs four reporting segments:

- **Natural Gas Pipelines (~65% of segment EBDA)** — the dominant and fastest-growing franchise: ~42,000 miles wholly owned plus equity interests in roughly 25,000 more (TGP, NGPL, SNG, Florida Gas/Citrus). Q1 2026 segment EBDA was $1,711M, up 18% YoY, led by Midstream gathering/processing (+45%) on colder weather, higher volumes, and the Outrigger acquisition.
- **Products Pipelines** — refined products, crude, and condensate under FERC/state tariffs. Q1 2026 EBDA $320M. Stable and rate-regulated.
- **Terminals** — liquids and bulk terminals plus 16 Jones Act tankers. Q1 2026 EBDA $329M.
- **CO2 / Energy Transition Ventures** — enhanced oil recovery, oil & gas production, and RNG. The only commodity-exposed segment (hedged through 2028 at ~$64/bbl) and the only one currently declining. Q1 2026 EBDA $168M.

The moat is the asset base itself: hard-to-permit, effectively irreplaceable pipeline corridors carrying long-term contracts. New gas pipelines are still being built, so competition exists, but replicating KMI's installed footprint and permitting position is a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar undertaking. The business is run by an "Office of the Chairman" structure (Executive Chairman Richard Kinder, CEO Kim Dang, and the President), with founder influence still central.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings trajectory.** Revenue moved from $19.2B (2022) to $15.3B (2023) to $15.1B (2024) and back up to $16.9B in 2025 (+12%). The 2022-2023 decline was a function of lower commodity pass-through prices, not volume loss; gas-sales revenue is largely offset by cost of sales. Net income to KMI rose from $2.39B (2023) to $2.61B (2024) to $3.06B (2025, +17%), with FY2025 EPS of $1.37. Adjusted EBITDA grew from $7.94B to $8.39B (+6%).

**Q1 2026 acceleration.** Revenue $4.83B (+14% YoY), net income to KMI $976M (+36%), GAAP EPS $0.44 (+38%), GAAP EPS $0.48, and adjusted EBITDA $2.54B. The earnings-surprise record supports the forward estimates: two beats and two in-line over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating by 19.3%.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $5.92B against $3.03B of capex, generating ~$2.89B of free cash flow (~17% FCF margin). The 2026 plan calls for ~$3.9B total capex, including ~$3.0-3.3B of discretionary/expansion spend, funded internally while still covering the dividend. The dividend was raised 2% to $1.19/share for 2026 (Q1 declared $0.2975), implying a ~3.8% yield. The $1.5B remaining buyback authorization has been used sparingly (~86M shares since 2017 at an average of $17.09), which is reasonable given the better return profile of the growth backlog.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$32.0B with net debt/adjusted EBITDA in the ~3.9-4.0x range, within management's ~3.5-4.0x target. Maturities are laddered at roughly $1.2B/year near-term, and the company carries a ~$3.4B undrawn revolver. The credit-rating direction is the relevant solvency signal: S&P upgraded to BBB+ (January 2026) and Moody's to Baa1/stable (March 2026), joining Fitch at BBB+.

The Altman Z-Score of ~1.48 ("distress zone") is a false positive here. The model is calibrated to manufacturers and penalizes the deliberately negative working capital (current ratio 0.64) and high goodwill ($20B) that are normal for a capital-intensive regulated midstream funding short-term needs with commercial paper and operating cash. The two investment-grade upgrades, covenant compliance, and stable fee-based cash flows are the better read. We score Financial Health 6.8 — strong cash generation and improving credit offset by genuinely elevated absolute leverage at a time when refinancing is continuous.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is contract-backed rather than speculative. Remaining performance obligations disclosed in the Q1 10-Q total ~$35B ($4B remaining in 2026, $5B in 2027, $26B in 2028 and beyond), giving multi-year visibility into future revenue. The ~$10B project backlog (Trident, Mississippi Crossing, SSE4, FGT) is anchored by long-term take-or-pay agreements tied to LNG and power demand, with in-service dates stretching to 2030. Management guides 2026 adjusted EBITDA more than 3% above budget.

The two secular drivers are both confirmed at the sector level. U.S. LNG export capacity continues to ramp, and several KMI assets feed it directly — the Evangeline Pass project serving Venture Global's Plaquemines LNG (2 Bcf/d) came fully into service in July 2025. Gas-fired power demand from data centers is the near-term winner of AI electrification, and the TVA Cumberland project feeds a 1,450 MW facility. Management forecasts U.S. gas demand reaching ~150 Bcf/d by 2031, roughly 27% growth. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year EPS estimates have risen 7.9% over 90 days to $1.48, with smaller upticks across other periods.

Two caveats temper the growth score. First, the revision velocity (current-year +7.9%/90d, next-year +2.2%) is positive but well below the >30% threshold that would flag a systematic underestimate — this is steady mid-single-digit growth, not a step-change. Second, the heavy concentration of contracted revenue in 2028+ means the near-term earnings ramp is more gradual than the backlog headline implies. We score Growth Prospects 7.0, consistent with the trajectory's gradual decline from 7.5 as the growth story matured from "re-accelerating" to "confirmed but priced."

## Valuation Assessment

KMI trades at ~21.5x trailing/forward earnings (the two are equal here given flat near-term EPS) and a ~3.8% yield. Against the peer set in Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation, the comparison is mixed:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| TRGP (Targa) | 27.5 | 1.8% |
| OKE (Oneok) | 15.9 | 4.7% |
| WMB (Williams) | 31.4 | 2.9% |
| **KMI** | **21.5** | **3.8%** |
| Peer median | 27.5 | 2.9% |

KMI sits below the peer median P/E of 27.5 and offers a higher yield than the median, but it is well above OKE at 15.9x. The discount to WMB (31.4x) is partly earned: Williams is pivoting ~$7B into power generation, a bolder strategy the market is awarding a premium, while KMI is the more conservative, higher-yield, transport-focused way to play the same gas-demand theme. The relevant question is whether $31.94 offers asymmetric upside today. On a ~3.8% yield growing ~2%/year plus mid-single-digit EBITDA growth, the total-return math points to high-single-digit/low-double-digit annual returns — attractive but not a mispricing.

The analyst price targets frame the asymmetry: mean $35.33, median $35.00, high $43.00, low $31.00. The current price sits only ~3% above the consensus low and ~11% below the mean. Given the stock has already re-rated from the high-$20s to $31.94 (a ~15% twelve-month gain), and given the most informed buyer in the file — the founder — entered at ~$26, the current entry lacks the margin of safety that would justify a high valuation score. We score Valuation 5.5: a fair price for a quality asset, not a discounted one.

## Competitive Landscape

KMI's competitive position is stable-to-improving. The installed footprint and permitting moat are difficult to replicate, the gas franchise is gaining EBDA share within the company's own mix, and the bolt-on M&A has been disciplined and on-strategy: Outrigger Energy ($648M, North Dakota gathering/processing), the EagleHawk divestiture ($382M with a $123M gain), and the announced Monument Pipeline acquisition ($505M cash, Houston-area gas, expected to close Q2 2026). All are natural-gas-centric, consistent with concentrating capital where demand is growing.

Relative to peers, KMI is the lower-multiple, higher-yield incumbent versus Williams's higher-multiple power-generation pivot. Both are valid expressions of the gas-demand thesis; KMI carries less strategic execution risk but also less optionality. The CO2/Energy Transition segment is the soft spot — declining EBDA, residual commodity exposure (hedged), and ongoing modest divestitures — but it is small relative to the gas franchise. We score Competitive Position 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Contract roll-off.** The seven-year weighted-average gas transport contract life means recurring re-contracting exposure at uncertain future rates. This is the single biggest structural risk to the toll-road model, though take-or-pay structures and the demand backdrop mitigate near-term concern.

**Leverage and rates.** ~$32B of debt with continuous refinancing and ~$1.8B/year of interest expense. The recent direction is favorable, but absolute leverage near 4x EBITDA leaves limited cushion if rates stay elevated or the gas-demand thesis slips.

**Regulatory.** FERC/CPUC/RCT rate review and complaint risk, plus PHMSA pipeline-safety mandates (MAOP reconfirmation by 2035, expanding integrity management) that add capital costs. The policy backdrop shifted favorably in February 2026 with the EPA's announced rescission of the GHG endangerment finding, reducing a long-standing compliance overhang.

**Project execution.** A >$10B multi-year backlog carries permitting, cost-overrun, and timing risk, with in-service dates to 2030.

**Litigation tail.** Portland Harbor Superfund (final remedy >$2.8B across ~90 PRPs, KMI's share to be allocated by end-2026), Lower Passaic River, Louisiana coastal-zone suits, the Freeport LNG Winter Storm Uri suit (~$104M, reversed and remanded April 2025), and an ERISA pension class action (potential >$100M, remedies pending). None is individually deemed material by management, but the cluster is a watch-item and the Portland Harbor allocation is a 2026 event.

We score Risk Factors 6.0: the leverage and litigation cluster are real and partly idiosyncratic, but the improving credit profile and the favorable regulatory shift offset some of the downside.

### Options Market Signal

The options market shows no stress and no directional conviction. The put/call OI ratio is 0.48 and the put/call skew is essentially balanced (-1.0% near-term). The term structure is flat (20.6% near, 22.3% far), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±2.1%. Short interest is low at 2.3% of float with 4.2 days to cover. The market is pricing KMI as a low-volatility income compounder with no near-term binary catalyst — consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Gas demand rises on LNG exports and AI/data-center power, the backlog converts on schedule, and 2026 EBITDA beats the above-budget guide. Continued deleveraging into BBB+/Baa1 territory and steady ~2% dividend growth support a re-rating toward the peer-median multiple. The founder's $26M buy proves prescient. Target $40-43.

**Bear case.** Rates stay elevated, refinancing costs climb on the debt load, a project or two slips, the Portland Harbor allocation lands at the high end, and gas-demand growth disappoints relative to the 150 Bcf/d-by-2031 thesis. The stock de-rates toward the consensus low and CO2-segment weakness drags. Target $27-28 (roughly -12% to -15%).

The base case is steady compounding: ~3.8% yield growing ~2%/year plus mid-single-digit EBITDA growth, with the multiple roughly held. That produces high-single-digit total returns from $31.94 without a catalyst to force the multiple higher near-term.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: we expect appreciation, but the current entry is not asymmetric enough to add aggressively.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which should confirm the Monument Pipeline close and provide an updated read on the 2026 EBITDA-above-budget trajectory. The Portland Harbor allocation (by end-2026) is the next discrete risk event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$20s (≤$29), restoring the margin of safety the founder captured at ~$26, with fundamentals intact.
- Management raises 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance through the year, or current-year EPS revisions accelerate beyond +10%/90d on a contract win or LNG/data-center FID.
- Net debt/EBITDA confirmed trending below 3.7x alongside a third agency action, lowering the leverage discount.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material project cancellation or backlog write-down, or a Portland Harbor allocation that materially exceeds management's framing.
- A guidance cut, a dividend-coverage deterioration, or net debt/EBITDA breaking above ~4.3x.
- Estimate revisions turning negative for two consecutive quarters alongside slowing gas-volume data.

## Conclusion

Kinder Morgan is a high-quality, fee-based gas infrastructure franchise levered to two durable demand drivers, with a strengthening balance sheet, disciplined on-strategy capital allocation, and the founder's $26M open-market purchase as a governance signal. The growth re-acceleration is real and contract-backed. The constraint is price: at $31.94, the stock has already re-rated ~15% over the past year, sits only ~3% above the consensus low target, and offers steady-compounding rather than asymmetric upside. The most informed buyer in the file entered at ~$26, and the bulk of contracted backlog revenue does not land until 2028+. We rate KMI a HOLD (overall 6.5), preferring to own the business on weakness toward the high-$20s or on confirmation that the 2026 guide is being raised, rather than adding at the top of its recent range.

The validation note flags the 6.5 score against a HOLD recommendation. The specific, falsifiable reason this is not a BUY: the entry lacks a margin of safety and a near-term catalyst. The stock trades ~3% above the analyst consensus low, ~23% above the founder's October buy price, with a contracted-revenue profile weighted to 2028+ (only $4B of $35B RPO falls in 2026) and EPS revisions of +7.9%/90d that fall well short of the >30% threshold that would justify chasing the price. The options market confirms no near-term binary catalyst (IV at the 4th percentile, balanced skew, ±2.1% implied move). Energy-sector longs in this environment have systematically underperformed when bought after a multi-month rally rather than on a pullback, which compounds the case for patience. A BUY here would require either a price reset toward the high-$20s or a guidance raise/FID catalyst — both of which are spelled out as upgrade triggers above.
