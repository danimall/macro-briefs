**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ryan Specialty is the second-largest U.S. P&C wholesale insurance broker and the largest U.S. P&C managing underwriter by premium volume, operating exclusively on the wholesale and delegated-underwriting side of the distribution chain. The deliberate absence of retail operations eliminates channel conflict with the retail brokers who are its customers, which is why RYAN sits on the wholesale panels of nearly every top-100 retail broker. The core secular thesis is intact: 78% of premiums flow through the Excess & Surplus market, which has grown at a 10.6% CAGR since 2010 versus 4.4% for the admitted market as risks become larger, more complex, and higher-hazard.

The stock has fallen 37% from its 52-week high of $66.27 to $41.75, and the central debate is not solvency or business quality but growth durability. Organic growth has stepped down three consecutive years — 15.4% in 2023, 12.8% in 2024, 10.1% in 2025 — driven specifically by softening property rates and increased carrier capacity that lets retailers place some property coverage directly. Goldman Sachs' June 8 downgrade to Neutral ($35 target) is a bet that P&C pricing moderation persists and that consensus re-acceleration forecasts are too optimistic, while conceding the business is high quality. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut 7-8% across the current-year and next-year windows over 90 days, though they have stabilized over the trailing 30 days.

Two facts prevent this from being an AVOID and keep it a HOLD with a modest positive lean. First, GAAP earnings weakness is largely optical: net income fell 6.9% to $214.2M in 2025 on rising acquisition amortization (+73.9% to $274M), higher interest (+40.4% to $222M), and a one-time deferred-tax item, while adjusted net income rose 11% and operating cash flow grew to $643.7M. Second, insiders are backing the stock aggressively into weakness — founder Patrick Ryan bought ~$3.9M on the open market at $32.50, joined by the CFO, general counsel, and two directors, all below the current price. That cluster is the strongest signal in the file. At ~52x trailing / ~20x forward adjusted EPS with decelerating revisions, the risk/reward is roughly balanced; we would become more constructive on evidence that organic growth stabilizes or on a re-entry toward the mid-$30s where the insider cohort was buying.

## Company Overview

Founded in 2010 by Patrick G. Ryan (previously founder and 41-year leader of Aon), Ryan Specialty is an international specialty insurance intermediary that operates only on the wholesale and underwriting side of the distribution chain. It intermediates between roughly 35,000 retail brokerage firms and 350+ carriers. The business runs through three specialties, with FY2025 net commissions and fees as follows:

- **Wholesale Brokerage** (RT Specialty) — $1,600M, 53.4% of revenue, +7.5% YoY. Places hard-to-place and complex risks.
- **Underwriting Management** — $1,024M, 34.2% of revenue, +58.5% YoY. The fastest-growing and most acquisition-fueled segment; 39 MGAs/MGUs plus National Programs underwriting and binding on delegated carrier authority.
- **Binding Authority** — $370M, 12.4% of revenue, +15.5% YoY. High-volume, small-premium policies.

The structural tailwind is the migration of risk into the E&S market, whose share of commercial premium rose from 13.5% in 2010 to 25.7% in 2024. Catastrophe exposure, cyber, and social inflation drive complex risks into E&S, where only scaled specialists can compete. RYAN's competitive edge rests on scale, a 300+ product breadth, top-tier producer talent (96% producer retention), and the absence of channel conflict.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue rose from $2,077M in 2023 to $2,516M in 2024 to $3,051M in 2025 (+21.3%), split roughly half organic, half M&A. The key trend to monitor is organic growth, which decelerated from 15.4% to 12.8% to 10.1% over three years as property rates softened.

GAAP net income declined 6.9% to $214.2M in 2025 despite the 21% revenue gain, with GAAP diluted EPS falling to $0.47 from $0.71. The decline is attributable to non-cash and financing items rather than operating deterioration: amortization of acquisition intangibles rose 73.9% to $274M, interest expense rose 40.4% to $222M on acquisition debt, and a one-time non-cash deferred-tax charge from a Common Control Reorganization hit the bottom line. On an adjusted basis, EBITDAC was $966.7M (margin 31.7%, down slightly from 32.2%), adjusted net income rose 11% to $548M, and adjusted diluted EPS rose to $1.96 from $1.79. The compensation-and-benefits ratio improved to 59.1% from 63.2%, while the G&A ratio rose to 14.9%.

Cash generation is healthy. Operating cash flow reached $643.7M (+$128.8M). The cash balance fell sharply to $158M from $540M after $746M was deployed on 2025 acquisitions. Total debt stands at $3,356M (4.375% notes due 2030, 5.875% notes due 2032, a SOFR+2.00% term loan, and revolver). Moody's upgraded RYAN from B1 to Ba3 in August 2025 (an upgrade deeper within the sub-investment-grade tier, not a move to investment grade), lowering the term-loan spread. Net debt to adjusted EBITDAC runs approximately 3.3-3.4x, typical for a broker roll-up.

A note on screening artifacts: the Altman Z-Score of 0.55 and reported D/E of 14.4x badly misrepresent this business model. Insurance brokers carry large fiduciary premium balances (~$1.4B fiduciary cash plus $2.9B fiduciary receivables, offset by fiduciary liabilities) that inflate reported liabilities and current ratios; the business is asset-light by design. Liquidity is adequate, with a $1.33B undrawn revolver, strong operating cash flow, and all covenants met. This is not a distressed balance sheet.

The earnings surprise record is mixed — one beat, one in-line, and two misses over the last four quarters, including a Q4 2025 miss of 11.3%. That cadence is consistent with a company navigating a decelerating top line rather than one delivering the reliable serial beats that would justify a premium multiple on optimism.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two layers. The secular layer remains attractive: E&S premium share continues to expand, retail-broker consolidation favors scaled wholesale partners, and Underwriting Management (delegated authority) grew 58.5% in 2025. The cyclical layer is the problem. Property capacity increased through H2 2024 and into 2025, pushing property rates down and enabling retailers to place some coverage directly, pulling flow out of the wholesale channel. Property pulled back while casualty grew, and the net effect drove organic growth to 10.1% for the full year, with FY2025 organic at 10.1% after Q3 organic of 15%.

Estimate revisions capture the tension. Consensus has cut current-year EPS to $2.06 (from $2.22 ninety days ago, -7.3%) and next-year EPS to $2.35 (from $2.56, -8.3%). The current-quarter estimate was cut sharply, from $0.73 to $0.60 (-17.3%). Revisions have stabilized over the trailing 30 days (current year +0.4%, next year +0.3%), suggesting the initial downgrade wave may be nearing a bottom. This is a deceleration that appears to be flattening rather than the revision velocity that would flag a clear buy. Management deferred its 35% adjusted EBITDAC margin target on the Q3 2025 call while maintaining double-digit organic growth guidance through 2026, acknowledging that near-term margin expansion is on hold.

The Empower Program, announced in Q1 2026, is the offsetting internal lever: a three-year restructuring targeting roughly $80M in annual savings by 2029 against ~$160M in cumulative one-time charges through 2028, alongside data/tech investment including AI submission intake. If executed, it rebuilds margin even if the top line grows in the low double digits. Execution risk is real, and the savings are years out.

## Valuation Assessment

At $41.75 the stock trades at ~52x trailing GAAP EPS ($0.465), which overstates the multiple because trailing GAAP is depressed by non-cash amortization and the one-time tax charge. On adjusted forward earnings the picture is more reasonable: at consensus current-year EPS of $2.06 the forward multiple is ~20.3x, and on next-year $2.35 it is ~17.8x. Against peer medians (P/E 20.7, dividend yield 1.3%), RYAN screens roughly in line to modestly below the group on forward earnings while carrying the fastest growth profile among the pure wholesale/underwriting specialists.

The peer set is heterogeneous. AJG trades at 35.7x and GSHD at 34.0x (both high-growth), while WTW sits at 15.2x and BRO at 19.2x as more mature, retail-heavy franchises. RYAN's forward multiple of ~18-20x on a business still compounding revenue at 20%+ (albeit with a moderating organic component) does not look stretched, but it also does not offer the clear asymmetric mispricing that would justify a high valuation score. The bull argument rests on organic growth re-accelerating back toward the low-to-mid teens as casualty strength offsets property softness; the bear argument, articulated by Goldman, is that property moderation persists and consensus re-acceleration is too optimistic.

The valuation dimension warrants some skepticism here rather than reward. A compressed-looking trailing multiple is not, by itself, a buy signal, and the forward multiple prices in continued double-digit growth that the estimate revisions have been trimming. Analyst targets bracket the debate widely: low $34, mean $45.9, median $43, high $76. With the mean only ~10% above spot and the median roughly at spot, the consensus does not signal a clear discount. We score valuation at 5.5 — fair, not compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

The wholesale/delegated-underwriting segment is fragmented, with a handful of national players: RYAN, CRC/Truist, Amwins, and Brown & Brown's wholesale arm. RYAN's structural advantage is the no-retail model, which keeps it conflict-free on the wholesale panels of top retail brokers, reinforced by scale, 300+ products, and 96% producer retention. The industry tailwinds — E&S secular growth, retail-broker consolidation favoring scaled partners, carrier consolidation — remain in place.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving in delegated authority and stable-to-slightly-weaker in wholesale brokerage. The 7.5% growth in Wholesale Brokerage against 58.5% in Underwriting Management reflects both the property-cycle drag and the deliberate acquisition-led buildout of the MGA/MGU platform. The direct-placement risk in property is a genuine, cycle-driven headwind to the wholesale channel, but it is cyclical rather than structural — as property rates firm again, flow historically returns to the wholesale channel. We score competitive position at 6.5: a durable moat with a currently mixed trajectory across segments.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant near-term risk is property-rate softening and organic deceleration, the direct cause of the slowdown and the basis for Goldman's downgrade.

Beyond the cycle, several company-specific risks matter:
- **MGA/MGU carrier-contract dependence.** Underwriting Management (34% of revenue) runs on delegated-authority contracts that are terminable on short notice, with carriers able to cut commissions at renewal. Contingent commissions (~5% of revenue) are volatile and depend on underwriting profitability outside RYAN's control.
- **Acquisition/goodwill risk.** With $3.2B goodwill and $1.6B intangibles from 60+ deals, impairment exposure grows if growth slows or market cap declines materially, and rising amortization is masking GAAP earnings.
- **Leverage and rate exposure.** $3.36B debt with a floating-rate term-loan component; interest expense already up 40%. The Ba3 upgrade helps, and the higher-for-longer rate path remains a headwind to a financing-heavy roll-up.
- **Tax Receivable Agreement.** A $459M liability owed to LLC unitholders (85% of tax savings) is a structural cash drain that reduces takeover appeal and could accelerate on a change of control.
- **Dual-class control.** The Ryan Parties control ~77% of voting power (Class B carries 10 votes/share), leaving Class A holders with limited say and no takeover-premium optionality. Board declassification is underway (phased 2026-2028), a modest improvement.
- **Empower execution.** A new risk factor flags that the restructuring may not achieve intended savings.

These risks are largely known and partly priced after a 37% drawdown, but the property-cycle overhang has no dated resolution, and the multiple still assumes double-digit growth. We score risk at 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 57.3% sits rich to 43.5% one-year historical vol (13.8-point spread), the term structure is in backwardation (57.3% near-term vs 43.0% long-term), and put/call open interest has risen sharply to 1.70 (from 0.70 a month ago) with a 6.8% put skew. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±12.4%. This is consistent with a market bracing for the next earnings print and skeptical of a fast growth re-acceleration. Short interest is elevated at 14.8% of float (7.0 days to cover), confirming meaningful institutional bearish positioning. The divergence between this cautious options/short setup and the aggressive insider buying is the crux of the debate: insiders with the most information are betting the cycle concern is temporary, while the derivatives market prices continued near-term pressure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($55-66).** Property rates stabilize and firm, casualty strength continues, and organic growth re-accelerates back toward the low-to-mid teens. The Empower Program delivers $80M of annual savings by 2029, rebuilding the path to the deferred 35% adjusted EBITDAC margin target. M&A continues to add ~10 points of growth, adjusted EPS compounds toward $2.75-3.00 within two years, and the stock re-rates to the mid-20s forward multiple its growth and moat warrant. The insider cohort buying at $32.50 is proven right. Upside to $55-66 (mid-20s multiple on normalized adjusted EPS).

**Bear Case ($30-35).** Property moderation persists longer than expected, organic growth settles in the high single digits, and the market re-rates RYAN toward the more mature broker multiple (15-16x forward, à la WTW). Rising amortization and interest keep GAAP earnings weak, goodwill impairment risk surfaces if growth disappoints, and the estimate-cut cycle continues. Downside to $30-35, roughly matching Goldman's target and the low end of the analyst range, about 16-28% below current.

**Base Case (~$45).** Organic growth stabilizes in the 9-11% range, M&A adds high-single-digit points, adjusted EPS reaches ~$2.35-2.50, and the stock holds a ~18-19x forward multiple. Base case ~$45, roughly 8% above current and near consensus mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (growth_hold). The 12-month thesis is positive but not yet high-conviction; the near-term risk/reward is roughly balanced pending evidence that organic growth has stopped decelerating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic growth stabilizes or re-accelerates to ≥11% for two consecutive quarters, or property rates show firming that reverses the disintermediation drag.
- Consensus current-year/next-year EPS estimates turn positive on a 90-day basis (currently -7% to -8%), confirming the revision-cut cycle has ended.
- A pullback toward the mid-$30s (the insider-buying zone), which would restore clear asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic growth falls below 8% or management withdraws double-digit organic guidance.
- A goodwill-impairment charge, or a material MGA/MGU carrier-contract loss in Underwriting Management.
- Insider activity reverses to open-market selling by the founder or C-suite.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings report, and the trajectory of estimate revisions between now and then. The Empower Program's savings realization is a multi-year monitor.

## Conclusion

Ryan Specialty is a structurally advantaged, cash-generative specialty intermediary caught in a cyclical property-rate downturn that has slowed its organic growth and compressed forward estimates. The GAAP earnings decline is largely optical, driven by acquisition amortization and interest rather than operating deterioration, and the balance sheet is healthy once fiduciary-balance distortions are stripped out. The strongest signal in the file is the contrarian insider-buying cluster — founder Patrick Ryan plus the CFO, general counsel, and two directors buying with real cash at $31-35, below the current $41.75 — which argues the property concern is cyclical rather than structural.

Against that, the forward multiple of ~18-20x still prices in continued double-digit growth that estimate revisions have been trimming, the options and short-interest setup signals near-term skepticism, and there is no dated catalyst to resolve the property-cycle debate. The score trajectory (5.7 → 6.8 → 6.5) reflects a company whose quality improved but whose growth momentum stalled; the current data confirms stalled momentum rather than a fresh inflection. We maintain a HOLD with a modest positive lean, and would move to BUY on stabilizing organic growth or a re-entry toward the mid-$30s.
