**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rexford Industrial Realty is a pure-play owner of infill Southern California industrial real estate, a strategy that produced years of sector-leading FFO growth as the company acquired functionally obsolete buildings and re-leased them far above expiring rents. That engine has stalled. Greater Los Angeles industrial asking rents have fallen roughly 8.3% year-over-year and more than 30% from the 2023 peak (CBRE), vacancy has risen to ~5.4%, and net absorption is negative. The legacy below-market leases that were a structural tailwind are now re-pricing flat-to-negative, illustrated by the Q1 2026 renewal of tire distributor Tireco at a ~30% negative rent spread, a single deal that represented roughly a quarter of reported quarterly leasing volume.

Governance now matters as much as real estate. Elliott Investment Management built a stake in August 2025; the co-founder Co-CEOs departed late that year; and Laura Clark, a former Green Street analyst with a capital-allocation background, became CEO on April 1, 2026. The strategy has reversed 180 degrees: from $2B+/year of acquisitions to no acquisitions under contract, ~$500M of planned dispositions, six halted development projects, and share repurchases as a primary use of capital. New management bought stock in the open market near the transition at prices above today's $33.68, a credible signal of conviction in the recycle-capital-into-buybacks thesis.

The investment case hinges entirely on whether SoCal industrial has bottomed. With ~15.1% of rentable square footage expiring in 2026 and 14.0% in 2027, a large slug of leases will re-price into the current soft market before any recovery materializes. Consensus EPS estimates are broadly falling, and the stock trades near its 52-week low after an ~8.9% decline since the prior review. We rate REXR a HOLD. The long-term supply-constraint thesis is intact and the assets are genuinely irreplaceable, but the near-term mark-to-market headwind, the lease-roll exposure, and disposition-execution risk leave the 12-month risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Rexford is an internally managed REIT that owns and operates exclusively industrial properties in infill Southern California submarkets (South Bay/Torrance, San Fernando Valley, San Gabriel Valley, and Inland Empire West, among others). Management explicitly avoids the bulk-logistics Inland Empire East. The portfolio is concentrated in smaller, multi-tenant space, with average tenant size around 28,000 square feet and leases concentrated in tenants under 50,000 square feet. Management argues this small-bay segment is the most supply-constrained and most resilient slice of the market.

The tenant base spans Manufacturing (22.5% of annualized base rent), Wholesale Trade (21.7%), and Transportation & Warehousing (21.6%), with a substantial majority of rent coming from non-investment-grade tenants. Historically, the company sourced ~77% of acquisitions off-market or through lightly-marketed channels, then repositioned or developed obsolete product into Class A space, a value-add engine that depended on rising market rents to monetize.

That model has been paused. The company now holds no acquisitions under contract, targets ~$500M of dispositions in 2026, and is redirecting capital toward buybacks of stock trading below net asset value. This is a deliberate capital-recycling stance: sell into still-firm private-market valuations, buy back equity the public market has marked down.

## Financial Analysis

The headline GAAP earnings figures understate cash economics, as is typical for a REIT carrying heavy real-estate depreciation. The trailing P/E of 35.7x on $0.86 EPS reflects this distortion and is not a meaningful valuation anchor. The operating picture is better read through NOI, occupancy, and net effective rent trends.

Q1 2026 captured the current squeeze precisely. The company reported record leasing volume and ~96% occupancy, but the leasing was renewal-heavy, NOI fell roughly 4%, and net effective rents declined approximately 10%. The Tireco renewal at a ~30% negative spread shows the mechanism: legacy leases signed at trough rents years ago are now re-pricing against a market where asking rents have fallen sharply, so what was once a mark-to-market tailwind is now a headwind. Occupancy is being preserved at the cost of rate.

The earnings-surprise record is volatile and partly an artifact of REIT accounting: beats of 39.0% (Q1 2026), 33.1% (Q3 2025), and 81.0% (Q2 2025) bracket a deeply negative Q4 2025 print (-$0.30 actual vs. $0.23 estimate, a -230.7% miss) that likely reflects impairment or one-time items rather than operating collapse. The pattern argues for using FFO-based normalized earnings power rather than GAAP EPS, but it also signals that GAAP visibility is poor and that impairment risk on the halted development projects is live.

The balance sheet is sound and a clear positive. The $1.25B unsecured revolver was undrawn at year-end 2025; three term loans ($400M, $300M, $60M) are fully drawn; and $760M of variable-rate debt is fully swapped to fixed at effective rates of ~2.82%–3.71%, insulating near-term interest expense from higher rates. The company is investment-grade rated, leverage is moderate relative to the asset base, and access to capital is adequate to fund the dividend (5.25% yield) and the disposition-funded buyback. The principal balance-sheet caveat is the lease-expiration ladder: just over 29% of the portfolio re-prices into the soft market over 2026-27.

One non-recurring cost: 2025 CEO total compensation spiked to ~$25.9M, driven by ~$60.2M of accelerated equity awarded to the departing co-founders. This inflated G&A and represents a real cash and dilution cost of the transition, though it does not recur.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative, and the company will not call a market bottom. Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration: current-quarter consensus EPS has been trimmed 2.1% over 90 days, next-quarter estimates are down 8.0% over 30 days, and the current-year figure is down 6.8% over 90 days. This is the opposite of the positive revision velocity that would flag an earnings inflection; the trend is broadly downward, and growth prospects score accordingly.

The growth optionality is real but back-loaded. Management cites ~$50M of embedded NOI still to be captured from leasing up the existing development and repositioning pipeline, growth within the existing portfolio that does not depend on new acquisitions. The longer-term bull case rests on supply: SoCal industrial construction sits near multi-decade lows, and California's AB 98 and SB 415 regulations (effective January 1, 2026) sharply raise the bar for new infill development. If those constraints hold while demand recovers, existing well-located infill assets become more valuable on a multi-year horizon.

The mechanism connecting these to per-share growth is dispositions plus buybacks: selling assets at premium private valuations and retiring shares trading below NAV is accretive to FFO per share if execution holds. The accretion math depends on dispositions actually closing at the implied premiums, which management itself newly flagged as an execution risk. Management has ruled out the data-center pivot that has supported some industrial peers, citing insufficient SoCal power supply, so this demand vector is closed to REXR specifically.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E is uninformative given REIT depreciation and the Q4 2025 GAAP distortion. On a peer basis, REXR's headline P/E of 35.7x sits above the Industrial REIT peer median of 29.2x, but the more relevant comparison is on FFO and on price relative to NAV, where the bull case is that the stock trades below the private-market value of its assets, precisely the gap the buyback program is designed to exploit.

Among peers, the dispersion is wide: TRNO at 16.3x, FR at 24.3x, CUBE at 29.2x, STAG at 29.5x, and EGP at 36.8x. REXR's 5.25% dividend yield is well above the 3.2% peer median, providing meaningful absolute yield support, though a yield that elevated relative to peers also reflects the market's discounting of forward growth.

The valuation is not cheap on forward fundamentals, and the cheapness that does exist (price below NAV) requires a catalyst to close. The catalyst here, disposition-funded buybacks, is credible but unproven, and the dominant near-term force is falling estimates against a stalled rent engine. Analyst targets range from $35 (low) to $44 (high), with a median of $40 and mean of $39.19, all above the current price, but the sentiment trend is degrading and Scotiabank cut its target to $39 from $44 in March while still rating it Sector Outperform. We treat the current price as roughly fair: the asset quality and yield support the downside, but there is no specific reason to believe the market is materially mispricing forward earnings power today. A higher valuation score would require either a confirmed market bottom or evidence that dispositions are closing at the premiums implied.

## Competitive Landscape

REXR's differentiation is genuine: a pure infill-SoCal, small-bay, value-add model in one of the most supply-constrained industrial markets in the world, versus the bulk-logistics big-box exposure that has been hardest hit in the downturn. The smaller spaces (<50,000 sq ft) that are Rexford's sweet spot have held up better than large-box product. Off-market sourcing relationships and deep local expertise are durable advantages on the acquisition side, though acquisitions are currently paused.

The competitive trajectory has shifted from improving to defensive. The company's historical edge, buying obsolete infill cheaply and re-leasing far above expiring rents, depended on rising market rents that have now reversed. Occupancy at ~96% remains strong, demonstrating that the locations retain demand, but the company is holding occupancy by conceding on rate, which is a sign of pricing-power compression rather than expansion. Demand bright spots persist in advanced manufacturing, defense/aerospace, food & beverage, medical, and 3PL, but these have not been enough to offset the broad rent reset.

Direct competitors include Prologis, Terreno (also infill-focused), EastGroup, and private owners. Terreno's lower multiple and First Industrial's discount illustrate that the market is pricing the SoCal-specific deterioration into REXR more heavily than into more geographically diversified peers. The structural supply-constraint thesis is the durable competitive asset; the cyclical rent reset is the near-term drag. On balance, the competitive position is stable-leaning-defensive: irreplaceable assets, but a moat that is being tested on pricing.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic and product concentration.** The entire company is one bet on infill Southern California, exposing it to region-specific shocks: earthquakes (with material deductibles and some catastrophe risk uninsured), wildfires (explicitly not insured), California regulatory and tax burden, and port volume risk. The 10-K specifically flags that reduced imports at the Ports of L.A./Long Beach, via tariffs, trade policy, or labor disruption, would directly reduce tenant demand.

**Negative mark-to-market and lease roll.** With ~29% of square footage rolling in 2026-27, the single biggest swing factor for FFO is whether re-leasing spreads stabilize. Tireco at -30% is the cautionary data point. This risk is partly priced (the stock is near its 52-week low) but the magnitude of the roll means the downside is not fully resolved.

**Activist and strategy-execution risk.** The 10-K added a brand-new "Stockholder activism and related public campaigns" risk factor and expanded its disposition-execution risk language, both directly reflecting the Elliott situation. The disposition target underpins the buyback accretion math; if dispositions slip on price or timing, the per-share thesis weakens. Six halted development projects raise impairment risk, which the 10-K describes as highly subjective and dependent on holding-period and rent assumptions that could deteriorate further.

**Non-investment-grade tenant base.** A majority of rent comes from smaller, non-IG tenants more vulnerable to bankruptcy in a downturn.

**Tax Matters Agreements.** OP-unit structures limit the company's ability to sell certain contributed properties without triggering tax-indemnity obligations, constraining the disposition flexibility the new strategy depends on.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is cautious but not alarming. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 29.9% against a 1-year historical volatility of 24.8%, leaving IV modestly rich (5.1% spread), so the market is paying up slightly for protection, though IV sits at only the 16th percentile of the current chain, indicating no acute event premium. The put/call skew of 7.0% and elevated put/call volume (6.00) and OI (3.70) ratios point to defensive hedging demand, consistent with the falling-estimate backdrop and the near-52-week-low price. The term structure is in contango (near-term 29.9% vs. 6-month 34.6%), a normal, non-panicked shape. The options market is positioned defensively around a stock whose fundamentals are under pressure, confirming the cautious fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** SoCal industrial is at or near a cyclical bottom after a 30%+ rent reset. Construction near multi-decade lows plus AB 98/SB 415 structurally constrain future supply, making existing infill assets more valuable over a 10-year horizon. New CEO Laura Clark, with a capital-allocation pedigree, executes the disposition-funded buyback at a discount to NAV, accreting FFO per share. The ~$50M of embedded NOI from the existing pipeline leases up. Occupancy holds at ~96% and re-leasing spreads turn positive as the market firms. Insider buying by the new team near the transition proves prescient. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the $40-44 analyst range over 18-24 months.

**Bear case.** The SoCal market has not bottomed; rents fall further as the portfolio rolls in 2026-27 at negative spreads. NOI continues to decline, dispositions slip on price or timing, impairment charges hit the halted development projects, and the buyback fails to offset the FFO erosion. A consumer/industrial downturn drives non-IG tenant defaults and a softer port-volume environment compounds the demand shortfall. In this scenario the stock revisits and breaks the low-$30s toward the high-$20s.

The current setup sits between these. The asset quality and 5.25% yield provide a floor, and the activist-driven discipline plus insider buying are genuine positives. But the dominant near-term force is falling estimates against an unresolved rent reset, and the company itself will not call an inflection. That is the profile of a balanced hold rather than an asymmetric buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence does not support a confident call in either direction. The long-term supply thesis and aligned management argue against an AVOID; the near-term negative mark-to-market, heavy lease roll, and falling estimates argue against a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or positive re-leasing spreads, confirming SoCal rents have bottomed.
- Dispositions closing at or above the implied premiums (toward the ~$500M target), validating the buyback accretion math.
- A reversal in estimate-revision trend to positive over a 90-day window, combined with NOI returning to growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Re-leasing spreads deteriorating below the Tireco-level (-30%) on a meaningful share of the 2026-27 roll.
- Material impairment charges on the halted development pipeline that exceed one-time levels.
- Dispositions stalling or repricing materially lower, undermining the buyback strategy and forcing reliance on equity or incremental leverage.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings release. The Q2 2026 print is the key checkpoint for whether occupancy is still being held at the cost of rate, whether dispositions are closing, and whether the embedded NOI pipeline is leasing up.

The 12-month target reference range is the $35-40 analyst zone, roughly 4-19% above the current price, against a bear-case downside toward the high-$20s (~15-20% below current). That distribution is too symmetric to justify a BUY and the downside is too well-cushioned by asset quality and yield to justify an AVOID.

## Conclusion

REXR is a high-quality, irreplaceable-asset REIT caught in a severe Southern California rent reset and undergoing an activist-driven leadership and strategy overhaul. The fundamentals tell two stories that have not yet resolved: a credible long-term supply-constraint thesis with aligned new-management insider buying, set against near-term negative mark-to-market, a heavy 2026-27 lease roll, halted development, and execution risk on the disposition-funded buyback. The current 5.4 reflects a modest pullback as the price decline since the prior review coincides with falling estimates rather than improving fundamentals. A lower price on weaker estimates does not create a stronger buy; the company will not call a market bottom.

We maintain a HOLD. The reasonable course is to monitor the Q2 print for evidence that SoCal rents are stabilizing and that dispositions are closing at the premiums the buyback math requires. Until one of those confirms, the risk/reward is balanced, and the dividend yield plus asset quality provide adequate reason to hold without adding.
