## Executive Summary

ONE Gas is a 100% regulated natural gas distribution utility serving approximately 2.3 million customers across Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas. The company holds dominant market positions (89% of Oklahoma, 72% of Kansas gas distribution customers) and benefits from constructive regulatory frameworks that include interim capital recovery mechanisms. Operating income grew 15% in FY2025 to $457M, and management guides FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.83-$4.95, representing ~8-10% growth.

The investment case hinges on two competing dynamics. On the positive side, ONE Gas is positioned to benefit from a natural gas demand renaissance driven by data center power needs, with 6 projects representing approximately 3 GW of generation in late-stage discussions. Kansas regulatory amendments and Texas HB 4384 structurally reduce regulatory lag. On the negative side, the company generates persistently negative free cash flow (~$800M capex vs. ~$580M operating cash flow), requiring continuous equity issuance that has diluted share count by ~12% over three years. Recent earnings execution has been weak — 2 misses and 1 in-line result out of the last 4 quarters — and the stock's 5-year TSR has underperformed peers by approximately 39 percentage points.

At ~16.4x forward adjusted EPS with a 3.3% dividend yield, OGS trades roughly in-line with gas utility peers. The data center opportunity introduces meaningful optionality but remains speculative at this stage, while the dilution and execution headwinds are present and quantifiable.

## Company Overview

ONE Gas operates through three divisions: Oklahoma Natural Gas (largest natural gas distributor in Oklahoma), Kansas Gas Service (largest in Kansas), and Texas Gas Service (third largest in Texas). The company was separated from ONEOK in January 2014.

The business model is straightforward: OGS earns returns on invested capital (rate base) as determined by state regulators. The company does not profit from the underlying commodity — gas costs are passed through at cost. In Oklahoma, approximately 91% of revenues from sales customers (excluding gas cost) derive from fixed charges, providing substantial volumetric insulation.

Major markets include Oklahoma City, Tulsa, Kansas City, Wichita, Austin, and El Paso. The company operates a single reportable segment.

**Income Statement:**

|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|

| Net Income ($M) | $222 | $231 | $223 | $264 |
| Diluted EPS | $4.08 | $4.14 | $3.91 | $4.37 |

FY2025 operating income grew 15% year-over-year, driven by $116M from new rates and $6.6M from customer growth. Adjusted EPS (non-GAAP) was $4.48, up from $3.94 in FY2024.

Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.11 grew 6% despite one of the warmest winters in service territory history (25% warmer than prior year in Oklahoma and Texas), demonstrating the buffering power of fixed-charge revenue structures.

**Balance Sheet:**

- Total debt-to-capital: 47.6% (excluding securitized debt)
- Long-term debt: $2.13B in Senior Notes (no maturities within 12 months)
- Short-term debt (commercial paper): $737M at 3.94% weighted average rate
- Credit ratings: A3/A- (Moody's/S&P) with stable outlook
- Debt-to-equity: 2.57x

The low current ratio of 0.6 is structurally normal for utilities that use revolving credit facilities for working capital management. The $1.5B credit facility (extended to October 2030) provides adequate liquidity.

**Cash Flow & Capital Intensity:**

Operating cash flow of $579M in FY2025 (up from $368M in FY2024 due to working capital recovery) is insufficient to fund ~$760-800M in annual capital expenditures. This creates a persistent ~$200M+ annual funding gap addressed through equity issuance. Share count has grown from 54.2M to 60.5M diluted shares over 2022-2025 — approximately 12% dilution in three years, or roughly 3-4% annually. This dilution is the primary constraint on per-share earnings growth.

**Dividend:**

Annualized dividend of $2.72/share yields approximately 3.4% at the current price. The payout ratio on FY2025 GAAP EPS was 62%, which is sustainable but leaves limited room for meaningful dividend growth given the funding gap.

## Growth Analysis

**Rate Base Growth (Core Driver):**

OGS's earnings growth engine is expanding its rate base through capital investment. The company is deploying approximately $800M annually, primarily in system integrity (pipeline replacement), customer growth connections, and large-load infrastructure.

**Data Center / Large-Load Opportunity (Emerging Catalyst):**

Management has identified 6 projects in late-stage discussions representing approximately 3 GW of generation capacity and up to 1 Bcf/day of potential demand across all three states. A 20 MMcf/day transportation agreement has already been signed for an Oklahoma data center. The Western Farmers project ($120M, 43-mile pipeline) is expected to complete in Q3 2028.

This opportunity is meaningful because data center power demand is growing rapidly, natural gas provides reliable baseload generation, and OGS's service territories in Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas are politically favorable for gas infrastructure. However, these projects are mostly transportation (not sales) arrangements — lower margin but capital-efficient — and only one has reached signed-agreement stage.

**Regulatory Tailwinds:**

- Kansas GSRS amendment (effective July 2026) expands eligible investments to all state-specific utility plant and increases the maximum monthly surcharge from $0.80 to $1.35 (+69%)
- Texas HB 4384 allows deferral and recovery of costs for PP&E placed in service but not yet in base rates
- Texas rate consolidation into a single statewide division simplifies future filings

**Guidance:** FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.83-$4.95, implying ~8-10% growth. Consensus current-year estimate is $4.87.

**Dilution Offset:** Even with 15% operating income growth in FY2025, diluted EPS grew only from $3.91 to $4.37 (~12%). The persistent 3-4% annual share count growth compresses the conversion of rate base growth into per-share value creation.

## Competitive Landscape

ONE Gas's competitive position is defined by its regulatory monopoly status in served territories. Key advantages:

- **Dominant market share:** 89% of Oklahoma, 72% of Kansas gas distribution customers
- **Established regulatory relationships:** Decades of operating history in all three states
- **Fixed-charge revenue structure:** 91% of Oklahoma residential revenue from fixed charges minimizes volumetric risk
- **Infrastructure advantage:** Existing pipeline networks create natural barriers to alternative energy penetration in the near term
- **Favorable state regulatory environments:** Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas are all politically supportive of natural gas

Competitive threats are structural rather than direct:
- Electrification of heating (heat pumps) and cooking (induction) represents long-term displacement risk
- However, this risk is meaningfully lower in OGS's three-state footprint than in coastal states with aggressive decarbonization mandates
- Data center demand for gas-fired generation partially offsets residential electrification risk

The company is gaining competitive ground through operational efficiency (12,000+ hours of annualized labor savings from AI/automation, in-sourced line locating) and large-load infrastructure positioning.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Equity Dilution (Ongoing):** The ~$200M+ annual funding gap requires persistent share issuance. At 3-4% annual dilution, an investor effectively loses 3-4% of their ownership each year. This is structural and unlikely to change until operating cash flows catch up to capex needs — which requires further rate base growth to generate higher allowed earnings.

**2. Weather Sensitivity:** Q1 2026 demonstrated resilience (6% adjusted EPS growth despite warmest winter since 1895), but weather normalization mechanisms are imperfect. Climate warming trends could structurally reduce heating demand over time.

**3. Interest Rate Exposure:** $737M in commercial paper at variable rates (currently 3.94%) and a $250M variable-rate term loan create cost sensitivity. If authorized ROEs (9.4-9.8%) don't keep pace with rising capital costs, earned returns compress.

**4. Regulatory Risk:** Oklahoma rate case mandated by June 2027. Any adverse outcome could impair earnings. The Kansas GSRS expansion and Texas HB 4384 are positive, but regulatory environments can shift.

**5. Data Center Execution Uncertainty:** Of 6 large-load projects in late-stage discussion, only 1 has reached signed agreement. Power plant development timelines are long, and project cancellations are common. The market is right to not yet price this optionality.

**6. Earnings Execution:** Track record of 1 beat, 1 in-line, and 2 misses in the last 4 quarters is concerning for a regulated utility where earnings should be predictable.

**Options Market Signal:**

Near-term ATM IV of 21.4% sits materially above 1-year historical volatility of 16.5% (4.8pp spread), indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. Put/call skew of 19.6% reflects elevated put demand, consistent with the stock's decline from its $90.78 52-week high. The put/call OI ratio of 0.17 is low, suggesting limited structural hedging in place. The flat term structure (21.4% near vs. 19.4% far) indicates uncertainty is not concentrated around any specific near-term event. The options market is mildly cautious but not pricing acute stress.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95-103):**

- 2-3 of the 6 data center/large-load projects advance to signed agreements in 2026-2027, validating accelerated rate base growth
- Kansas GSRS expansion and Texas HB 4384 drive faster capital recovery, reducing regulatory lag
- FY2026 earnings come in at the high end of guidance ($4.95+), and FY2027 estimates move toward $5.25+
- Multiple re-rates from 16.5x to 19-20x as market recognizes growth acceleration
- Total return: 15-20% capital appreciation + 3.3% yield

**Bear Case ($70-74):**

- Earnings continue to miss consensus as weather normalization disappoints
- Data center projects fail to materialize or are pushed beyond 2030
- Rising interest rates compress earned returns below authorized ROEs
- Equity dilution accelerates if capex requirements grow without corresponding rate relief
- Stock de-rates to 14-15x forward P/E on execution concerns
- Downside: 8-13% capital loss, partially offset by dividends

**Base Case ($86-90):**

- Earnings track guidance (FY2026 adjusted EPS ~$4.87-4.90)
- 1-2 additional large-load contracts signed but not yet contributing materially
- Regulatory outcomes remain constructive
- Stock re-rates modestly toward 18x forward as data center narrative builds
- Total return: ~10-14% including dividends over 12 months

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**

1. Two or more additional signed large-load transportation agreements announced, validating the data center growth thesis
2. FY2026 earnings beat guidance high-end ($4.95) by 3%+ and FY2027 estimates rise above $5.20
3. Stock pulls back to $72-74 range (15x forward P/E) creating more attractive risk/reward while fundamentals remain intact

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**

1. Two consecutive quarterly EPS misses exceeding 5% of consensus
2. Share count growth accelerates above 5% annually without corresponding EPS growth acceleration
3. Any of the three state regulatory commissions issues an adverse rate case order that materially reduces authorized ROE

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (late July/early August 2026 for Q2 results). The Oklahoma PBRC filing and Texas GRIP filing outcomes (expected mid-2026) will provide regulatory visibility. Any data center contract announcements would warrant immediate reassessment.

## Conclusion

ONE Gas is a fundamentally sound regulated utility with emerging growth optionality from data center demand. The stock's current valuation (~16.5x forward, 3.3% yield) reflects the market's skepticism about execution following recent earnings misses and TSR underperformance. The data center thesis is legitimate — the signed Oklahoma contract proves demand exists — but early-stage enough that it should not drive a conviction BUY recommendation today.

The equity dilution dynamic is the key structural headwind: investors are effectively paying for rate base growth while seeing ~3-4% of their ownership eroded annually through share issuance. Until operating cash flows catch up to capex (which requires the growth investments to flow through rate cases into higher earnings), this drag persists.

For income-oriented investors, the 3.3% yield with 6-8% EPS growth potential provides a reasonable total return proposition. For capital appreciation investors, the stock needs visible progress on large-load projects or a more attractive entry point. HOLD with income orientation, monitoring for data center contract announcements and regulatory outcomes through 2026.
