**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

ONE Gas is a 100% regulated natural gas distribution utility serving 2.3 million customers across Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas. The company earns regulated returns on its growing rate base, currently investing ~$800M annually in pipeline infrastructure. Full-year 2026 guidance of $4.83-$4.95 adjusted EPS implies 10-13% growth over 2025, supported by $116M in approved new rates and an emerging pipeline of data center/large-load interconnection projects representing up to 1 Bcf/day of incremental demand.

The investment case has two offsetting dynamics. On the positive side: constructive regulatory frameworks across all three states, a data center demand catalyst that could accelerate rate base growth, and a stock trading at ~16.5x forward earnings versus the peer median of 17.2x. On the negative side: structural equity dilution of ~3% per year to fund capex, a deteriorating earnings surprise pattern (2 misses and 1 in-line in the last 4 quarters), and cumulative TSR underperformance of ~39 percentage points versus the peer group. At $80-81 per share with a 3.3% yield, the stock offers fair compensation for a regulated utility with moderate growth, but lacks a specific catalyst to close the peer valuation gap in the near term.

## Company Overview

ONE Gas operates as a single-segment regulated natural gas distribution company, having been spun off from ONEOK in 2014. The business is straightforward: the company invests capital in pipeline infrastructure, earns a state-approved rate of return on that invested capital, and passes through commodity costs at no profit. Revenue drivers include: (1) authorized return on rate base, (2) fixed customer charges, (3) variable volumetric charges, and (4) net customer additions (~23,000/year).

The three operating divisions—Oklahoma Natural Gas (931K customers), Kansas Gas Service (653K customers), and Texas Gas Service (711K customers)—each operate under distinct regulatory frameworks that collectively provide multiple rate adjustment mechanisms to reduce regulatory lag.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:**
- 2025 EPS: $4.37 (up from $3.91 in 2024, +12%)
- 2026 Guidance: $4.83-$4.95 adjusted EPS (midpoint $4.89, +12% YoY)
- Consensus 2027E: $5.00 (+2% growth)
- Q1 2026: Adjusted EPS $2.11, slight miss vs. $2.18 consensus despite warmest winter since 1895

**Operating Performance:**
Operating income grew 15% in 2025 to $457M, driven by $116M in new rates across all jurisdictions. Net income grew 19% to $264M. The disconnect between net income growth (19%) and EPS growth (12%) reflects ongoing share dilution.

**Capital Structure:**
- Total debt: ~$2.87B (including $737M commercial paper)
- Debt-to-capital: 47.6% (excluding securitization)
- Credit ratings: A3/A- (stable outlooks)
- $1.5B credit facility extended to 2030
- No long-term debt maturities within the next year
- Commercial paper rate: 3.94% weighted average

The Altman Z-Score of 1.14 is a false signal for regulated utilities—the model penalizes structural features (low asset turnover, high leverage) that are normal for capital-intensive regulated businesses earning predictable returns.

**Cash Flow & Dilution:**
Operating cash flows improved to $579M in 2025 (vs. $368M in 2024), but capex of $707M produces structurally negative free cash flow. The company funds the gap through equity issuance: shares outstanding grew from 54.3M (2022) to 60.5M (2025)—12% dilution in three years, or approximately 3% per year. This is the primary mechanism limiting per-share value creation despite absolute earnings growth.

**Dividend:**
- $2.72 annualized ($0.68/quarter)
- Yield: 3.3% at current price
- Payout ratio: ~62% of GAAP EPS (sustainable; average for gas utilities)

## Growth Analysis

**Rate Base Investment:**
The 5-year capital plan calls for ~$800M annually, growing rate base from approximately $5.9B (2025) toward $7-8B by 2028-2029. At authorized ROEs of 9.5-10.0%, each $100M of incremental rate base adds roughly $5-6M in after-tax earnings, or $0.08-0.10 per share.

**Data Center/Large-Load Pipeline:**
Management disclosed 6 projects in late-stage discussions supporting approximately 3 GW of power generation and up to 1 Bcf/day of incremental gas demand. Current total system throughput is ~374 Bcf/year, so 1 Bcf/day would represent a ~98% increase in throughput if fully realized. These are primarily transportation customers (capital-light, immediately accretive), though the Western Farmers pipeline ($120M, 43 miles, Q3 2028 completion) is a construction project.

The challenge: management won't quantify EPS contributions from individual projects, and "late-stage discussions" is not a binding commitment. The timeline for materialization is 2027-2029, creating a gap between the current narrative and earnings visibility.

**Regulatory Tailwinds:**
- Oklahoma performance-based ratemaking (annual filings without full rate cases)
- Kansas GSRS expansion (July 2026): broadens qualifying investments, raises monthly surcharge cap from $0.80 to $1.35
- Texas HB 4384: allows deferral and recovery of infrastructure costs placed in service but not yet in rates
- Full Oklahoma rate case required in 2027 (could be a positive reset or a risk)

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current quarter estimates revised up +15.3% over 90 days; current year up +3.3%; next year up +1.6%. The revisions are positive but modest. The pattern reflects seasonal adjustments and guidance normalization rather than a fundamental re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

ONE Gas operates as a regulated monopoly within its service territories—there is no direct competition for existing customers. The relevant competitive question is whether the company can attract incremental large-load customers (data centers, manufacturing) versus competing utilities or alternative energy sources.

**Relative positioning:**
- Pure-play gas distribution (unlike diversified peers like National Fuel Gas or Southwest Gas Holdings)
- Service territories in Oklahoma and Texas benefit from favorable gas infrastructure, pro-business regulatory environments, and growing power demand
- TSR underperformance versus proxy peer group suggests the market views OGS as a below-average operator or growth story within the gas utility space

**Electrification risk:** Long-term structural headwind as policy preferences shift toward electrification of heating. Current mitigation is strong—data center and power generation demand is growing faster than residential gas demand may be declining. But over a 10-20 year horizon, this remains a legitimate overhang on terminal growth assumptions.

## Risk Assessment

**Quantified Risks:**
1. **Weather sensitivity:** Q1 2026 demonstrated that normalization mechanisms don't fully offset extreme weather. The warmest winter since 1895 still resulted in an earnings miss despite cost flexibility.
2. **Equity dilution:** ~3% annual share growth mathematically reduces per-share compounding by 300bp relative to asset growth.
3. **Regulatory haircuts:** Texas 2025 rate case approved only $14.5M of $41.1M requested (65% rejection rate). If Oklahoma's 2027 full rate case produces a similar outcome, the 5-year plan assumptions would need revision.
4. **Rising O&M costs:** 7% growth in 2025, 8.6% in Q1 2026—outpacing the company's 3-4% target. If this persists, it compresses earned returns between rate cases.
5. **Interest rate reversal risk:** $987M in variable-rate debt (CP + term loan). Each 100bp increase in short rates costs ~$10M pre-tax.

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term ATM IV of 21.5% versus 16.5% historical volatility (5pp spread) suggests the market is pricing modestly elevated uncertainty. Put/call skew of 19.5% indicates elevated put demand, consistent with the recent earnings miss pattern and stock decline from the $90.78 high. The OI put/call ratio of 0.17 is very low (more calls outstanding than puts in absolute terms), suggesting the elevated skew is recent put buying rather than structural bearishness. Overall, the options market confirms moderate uncertainty but not acute stress.

**Short interest:** 6.1% of float with 6 days to cover—above average for a utility, suggesting active skepticism about the growth thesis or concern about near-term headwinds.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90-95, +12-18%):**
- Data center/large-load pipeline materializes: even 2-3 of the 6 projects connecting by 2028 could add $0.15-0.25 in EPS
- Kansas GSRS expansion (July 2026) and Texas HB 4384 reduce regulatory lag, improving earned ROE
- Oklahoma 2027 rate case resets allowed ROE higher, reflecting current interest rate environment
- Interest rate declines reduce variable-rate debt costs (~$5-10M benefit per 50bp cut)
- Valuation re-rates toward peer median of 17-18x on improved growth visibility

**Bear Case ($70-73, -10-14%):**
- Data center pipeline stalls (permitting delays, customer decisions deferred, competing locations chosen)
- Oklahoma 2027 rate case produces a negative outcome (ROE compression, cost disallowances)
- O&M cost growth continues at 7-9% vs. 3-4% plan, compressing margins
- Interest rates rise, increasing debt costs and reducing utility relative attractiveness
- Continued equity dilution without offsetting growth acceleration leads to peer multiple compression below 16x

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Signed, binding agreements for 2+ large-load/data center projects with quantified rate base additions totaling >$200M
2. Oklahoma 2027 rate case filing reveals materially higher ROE request (>10%) with supportive commission signals
3. Stock declines below $73 (15x forward EPS) while guidance remains intact—creating a yield >3.7% with unchanged growth

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. 2026 EPS comes in below $4.70 (missing the low end of guidance by >$0.13), indicating structural cost overruns or regulatory shortfalls
2. Management withdraws or materially reduces the data center/large-load project pipeline from 6 projects to <3 without replacement
3. Oklahoma rate case decision results in earned ROE below 9.0%

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next decision points are (1) Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August), (2) any large-load contract announcements, and (3) the Oklahoma rate case filing (expected 2027). Reassess quarterly.

## Conclusion

ONE Gas is a fundamentally sound regulated utility trading at fair value. The 3.3% dividend yield plus 5-8% EPS growth potential produces a reasonable 8-11% total return expectation, appropriate for investors seeking regulated utility exposure with moderate income. The data center demand catalyst is real but unquantified and 2-3 years from full materialization. The persistent dilution problem, recent earnings miss pattern, and peer TSR underperformance collectively argue against upgrading to BUY at current prices. The stock is appropriately held for income, with the data center pipeline representing a potential upgrade catalyst if it converts to binding commitments.

The score trajectory from 5.7 to 6.2 reflects genuinely improving regulatory mechanics (Kansas GSRS expansion, Texas HB 4384) and the clarification of the data center pipeline. However, the Q1 2026 earnings miss (-6.5% surprise on the March quarter) and the appearance of a Sell rating in the current analyst consensus temper further upward movement in scores. The thesis remains: hold for income, monitor for catalysts.
