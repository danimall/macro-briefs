**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

U.S. Physical Therapy operates 783 outpatient physical therapy clinics across 44 states plus an industrial injury prevention (IIP) services arm, growing primarily by acquiring local multi-clinic practices into "Clinic Partnerships" in which the founding therapist retains a minority equity stake. The model aligns clinician-owners with the business and locks in referral relationships, but it produces a complex capital structure with a large redeemable non-controlling interest (NCI) liability that distorts GAAP earnings and creates recurring involuntary cash claims.

The stock has fallen from a 52-week high of $93.50 to $65.79, a 30% drawdown, driven by a Q1 2026 operating miss (non-GAAP Operating Results of $0.46 vs ~$0.55 consensus, an 11% shortfall) and core PT gross-margin compression to 15.8% from 16.6%. The headline GAAP loss of $(0.12) per share is largely an accounting artifact: a $9.4M non-cash revaluation of redeemable NCI that rises when subsidiaries perform well. The operating shortfall underneath it was real, and it reflects the central tension in the story: growth has become increasingly acquisition-led (mature-clinic revenue grew only 2.5%) and is now being funded by an expanding revolver as operating cash flow thins.

The reason this is a hold rather than an avoid is a specific, dated growth thesis. USPH has signed two 10-year hospital-system alliances covering 70 clinics with quantified accretion of ≥$7.3M combined annualized EBITDA to USPH at full ramp, with contribution phased into 2H 2026 and embedded in reaffirmed FY26 guidance of $102–106M adjusted EBITDA (versus $95.0M in 2025, implying 7–12% growth). This is layered on the first positive Medicare fee-schedule update in five years (+1.75% for 2026) and a +13.1% 90-day upward revision to next-quarter EPS estimates. These are concrete catalysts a 12-month holder can monitor against earnings releases. The offsetting concerns — the ~$313M redeemable NCI, a ~$54M IIP put exercisable January 2027, rising leverage, a CFO departure, and heavy insider selling near the highs — cap the upside and prevent a stronger call.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1990 and headquartered in Houston, USPH runs two reportable segments:

- **Physical Therapy Operations (~85% of revenue):** Outpatient clinics treating orthopedic disorders, sports injuries, post-operative rehabilitation, neurological injuries, and injured-worker rehab, plus a growing home-health therapy business.
- **Industrial Injury Prevention Services / IIP (~15% of revenue):** Onsite injury prevention, ergonomic assessments, post-offer employment testing, and functional capacity evaluations contracted directly with employers, including numerous Fortune 500 companies.

The distinctive feature is the partnership structure. USPH typically takes a 1% general-partner interest plus a 50–90% limited-partner interest in each acquired practice, with the managing therapist retaining a minority stake and a multi-year employment plus non-compete agreement. This secures the local referral network and clinical talent that drive a PT practice, which is the company's competitive advantage in a fragmented industry. The cost is structural complexity: the retained minority interests carry put/call rights priced at a multiple of trailing EBITDA, generating the large redeemable NCI liability that dominates the balance sheet and EPS optics.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025:**
- Net revenue $198.3M, +7.9% YoY (a record). PT revenue $167.7M (+7.2%); IIP $30.6M (+11.8%, +8.2% same-store).
- Volume metrics were healthy: patient visits 1,543,144 (+6.9%); average daily visits per clinic 31.8 vs 31.2; net rate per visit $106.49 (+0.8%).
- Growth quality was weaker than the headline: mature-clinic revenue grew only **2.5%**, with clinic additions contributing roughly $9.7M of the $11.8M net-patient-revenue increase.
- Core PT gross margin compressed to **15.8% from 16.6%** (adjusted 16.1% vs 16.8%); salaries per visit rose to $64.20 from $63.53, operating cost per visit to $90.31 from $88.77. Management attributed part of the pressure to severe winter weather. IIP margin improved to 20.4% from 18.6%.
- GAAP loss per share of $(0.12) vs +$0.80, distorted by the $9.4M non-cash redeemable-NCI revaluation charged to retained earnings (excluded from net income but included in the EPS calculation under GAAP). Net income to USPH shareholders was $5.0M vs $9.9M.
- **Non-GAAP Operating Results of $0.46 missed ~$0.55 consensus by approximately 11%.** Adjusted EBITDA was $20.2M vs $19.5M (+$0.7M). Two additional drags: a $6.8M swing in contingent earn-out revaluation ($2.0M loss vs $4.8M gain) and a higher effective tax rate (32.3% vs 28.1% on discrete items).

**Balance sheet and cash flow:** Total assets are $1.24B, of which goodwill ($715.9M) plus intangibles ($179.8M) total roughly $896M, or 72% of assets. Debt rose to $203.9M (term loan $129.4M plus revolver $74.5M) from $161.8M at year-end 2025, against cash of $28.4M. Q1 operating cash flow was only $3.8M while investing used $39.8M for acquisitions; the gap was funded by drawing the revolver, producing negative free cash flow for the quarter. A new $450M credit facility (April 14, 2026 — $175M term plus $275M revolver, maturing 2031) expands acquisition capacity. The dividend was raised to $0.46/quarter; it is covered by adjusted EBITDA, though GAAP coverage looks thin in weak quarters.

The earnings-surprise record is deteriorating: three misses in the last four quarters (-17.3%, -1.5%, -3.4%) after one beat (+11.1%). This reduces confidence that consensus estimates have stabilized and is the single most concrete reason the Financial Health and Growth scores sit at the midpoint rather than higher.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm has two engines, only one of which is firing well organically. Volume growth (+6.9% visits) is solid, and demand for outpatient PT remains structurally supported by an aging population and a shift toward lower-cost outpatient care. But mature-clinic revenue at +2.5% shows the organic base is barely outgrowing pricing, and acquisitions are now doing the heavy lifting while being funded with debt rather than internally generated cash.

The forward case rests on three identifiable, dated catalysts:

1. **Hospital-system alliances.** Two signed 10-year agreements place 70 clinics (60 of Metro's NY clinics into a prominent NY hospital network, plus 10 clinics of another subsidiary partner into a local system) into hospital clinical networks. Management quantifies ≥$6M and ≥$1.3M of annualized EBITDA accretion to USPH respectively at full ramp, beginning May 2026 and weighted to 2H 2026. If replicated across the broader clinic base, this would shift the payer mix, referral volume, and margin trajectory. This is the differentiated piece of the thesis and is monitorable on the Q3 and Q4 2026 prints.

2. **Medicare rate increase.** After cuts in most years from 2021 to 2025 (-2.9% in 2025), the 2026 fee schedule brought a +1.75% increase, the first positive update in five years. Modest, but it removes a recurring headwind and is embedded in guidance.

3. **Estimate revision momentum on forward periods.** Next-quarter EPS estimates rose +13.1% over 90 days and +4.5% over 30 days; next-year estimates rose +4.8% (90d) and +3.0% (30d). Current-year estimates have fallen (-6.3% over 90 days), reflecting the Q1 miss, but the forward curve is rising; consensus expects the back-half ramp to materialize.

Reaffirmed FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $102–106M implies 7–12% growth over 2025's $95.0M. The growth thesis is credible but unproven; the 2H weighting means the next two quarters are the real test.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of 130x is not the relevant metric: it reflects the GAAP earnings distortion from the redeemable-NCI revaluation, not the operating business. On forward consensus EPS of $2.81 (current year) and $3.41 (next year), USPH trades at roughly 23x and 19x respectively. Against the Health Care Facilities peer median P/E of 24.4, the next-year multiple is a modest discount, but the peer set is heterogeneous (SEM at 15.4x, NHC at 24.4x, OPCH at 16.0x, ASTH at 62.4x, ACHC negative). On a quality- and growth-adjusted basis, USPH at ~19x next-year earnings for a high-single-digit EBITDA grower with rising leverage and a complex cap structure is fairly priced, not obviously cheap.

The dividend yield of 2.9% provides some support and exceeds the peer median, but the GAAP payout ratio looks stretched in weak quarters and the dividend competes with M&A and put-right obligations for the credit facility's capacity. The 30% drawdown from the high partly reflects a genuine operating miss and a deteriorating beat/miss record, not pure sentiment, so the lower price does not automatically constitute a stronger buy. What the market is debating — growth quality and the durability of margins — is a legitimate concern, not a mispricing I can confidently say the sellers have wrong. The valuation is reasonable but offers limited asymmetric upside until the hospital-alliance ramp demonstrates margin accretion in reported numbers.

Analyst price targets (low $88, high $100, mean $94) sit well above the current $65.79, implying 34–52% upside. These targets predate or have not fully absorbed the Q1 margin shortfall and the CFO transition; I treat them as optimistic rather than as a reliable floor, given that this is not a clean inflection story trading below its growth rate.

## Competitive Landscape

USPH is one of the larger pure-play outpatient PT operators in a highly fragmented industry. Its partnership/clinician-equity model differentiates it from corporate roll-ups by retaining local owner-operators, which protects referral relationships and clinical retention. The competitive position is broadly stable rather than clearly improving: volume share is holding, but core margins compressed in Q1, and growth depends on continued acquisition at sensible multiples in a market where the company is also bidding for the same multi-clinic practices as competitors.

Industry dynamics cut both ways. PT labor scarcity and wage inflation pressure margins sector-wide (salaries are roughly 60% of revenue). Medicare fee-schedule pressure has been the recurring headwind, now modestly relieved in 2026. The structural shift toward hospital-system affiliation is the most differentiated strategic move USPH is making; if the two initial alliances scale beyond 70 clinics, it could improve payer mix and re-rate the growth story. That outcome is plausible but not yet demonstrated, which is why the competitive score sits at the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

- **Redeemable NCI and put-right obligations (the defining financial risk).** Redeemable NCI (temporary equity) is $313.4M, up from $293.3M, of which ~$82.5M is past its contractual period and potentially puttable. Partner retirements or terminations trigger USPH purchase obligations at a multiple of trailing EBITDA, a recurring and partly involuntary cash drain. Separately, a specific IIP put-right becomes exercisable January 2027; if exercised at March 31, 2026 values it would cost ~$53.9M for an 80% stake. These claims compete directly with M&A and the dividend for the new facility's capacity and are largely outside the company's control.
- **GAAP earnings volatility.** Strong subsidiary performance increases the NCI revaluation, which reduces GAAP EPS. This will continue to confuse the headline numbers even when the operating business performs.
- **Margin compression and labor.** Core PT margin fell ~80bps amid wage inflation and a constrained PT labor supply. If this proves structural rather than weather-related, the back-half EBITDA ramp is at risk.
- **Acquisition dependence and leverage.** With organic mature-clinic growth at 2.5%, growth depends on continued M&A funded by rising debt as operating cash flow thins. Integration and capital-allocation discipline matter more each quarter.
- **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** 72% of assets are intangible. A January 2026 regional realignment triggered an impairment test (none found), but the risk persists if reporting-unit performance deteriorates.
- **Management transition.** CFO Carey Hendrickson resigned effective April 24, 2026, with an interim CFO in place pending a permanent search, against a cluster of COO/President title changes and "planned leadership transitions." TSR materially lags peers (3-year indexed 65 vs 134).
- **Regulatory/legal.** Corporate-practice-of-medicine rules require a VIE structure (VIE assets $278.3M); ongoing qui tam / False Claims Act exposure is typical of healthcare providers.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is positioned more constructively than the equity drawdown would suggest. Near-term ATM IV is 44.9% versus 42.0% historical volatility, roughly fair (2.9pp spread), not a fear premium. Put/call skew is -34.6% at the 1-week tenor (call-heavy), and the put/call open-interest ratio of 23.47 reflects structural positioning rather than acute hedging demand. ATM IV fell from 48.0% to 44.9% as the stock rose 11% off the May lows, consistent with resolving rather than building uncertainty. The one caution: the term structure is in backwardation (44.9% near-term vs 41.6% at 3 months), with the nearest expiry pricing a ±7.5% move, which suggests the market expects an imminent event, consistent with positioning into the next quarterly print where the hospital-alliance ramp must begin to show. On balance, options sentiment does not contradict the hold thesis and leans modestly supportive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($88–100):** The two hospital-system alliances ramp on schedule in 2H 2026, delivering the ≥$7.3M combined EBITDA accretion to USPH and validating a replicable model that improves payer mix and margins. Core PT margin compression proves weather-driven and reverses. The +1.75% Medicare update and rising forward estimates (+13.1% next-quarter, +4.8% next-year over 90 days) confirm a back-half acceleration. FY26 EBITDA lands at the top of the $102–106M range and 2027 estimates of $3.41 EPS prove conservative. A permanent CFO with M&A credentials restores confidence. The stock re-rates toward the analyst target range of $88–100 (34–52% upside).

**Bear Case (~$50, -24%):** Margin compression proves structural as wage inflation persists and the PT labor shortage continues. The hospital-alliance ramp slips or under-delivers, and the back-half guidance proves too optimistic, producing a fourth consecutive miss. Rising leverage collides with the January 2027 IIP put (~$54M) and accelerating NCI redemptions, forcing the company to choose between M&A, the dividend, and deleveraging. A goodwill impairment on the intangible-heavy balance sheet crystallizes. The CFO vacancy lingers and the management-change cluster proves to signal deeper issues. The stock de-rates toward ~17x a stagnant ~$2.90 EPS.

The base case is closer to the bear in probability weighting than the bull, given three misses in four quarters and a 2H-weighted, unproven catalyst. The reasonable forward multiple plus the dividend and the dated, monitorable catalysts keep this from being an avoid, but the asymmetry is not compelling enough for a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is a back-half-2026 operating acceleration driven by the hospital-system alliances, the Medicare rate tailwind, and rising forward estimates. Reassessment timeline: the Q2 2026 print (early August) and especially the Q3 2026 print, where alliance contribution must begin appearing in reported EBITDA.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q3 2026 reported adjusted EBITDA tracking toward the upper half of the $102–106M FY guidance with visible hospital-alliance contribution, AND core PT gross margin recovering above 16.5%.
- Announcement of additional hospital-system alliances beyond the initial 70 clinics, demonstrating replicability.
- A permanent CFO hire with credible M&A/capital-allocation experience, combined with leverage stabilizing below ~2.5x net debt/EBITDA.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fourth consecutive non-GAAP operating miss, or FY26 EBITDA guidance cut below $100M.
- Core PT gross margin compressing further below ~15.5%, confirming structural rather than weather-driven pressure.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or leverage rising above ~3.0x while NCI redemptions and the January 2027 IIP put pressure liquidity and force a dividend or M&A pullback.

## Conclusion

USPH is a fairly valued operator with a real but unproven near-term catalyst. The Q1 miss and 30% drawdown reflect genuine operating concerns — slowing organic growth, core margin compression, and a deteriorating beat/miss record — not a clean mispricing the market has wrong. The redeemable NCI structure, the January 2027 IIP put, rising acquisition-funded leverage, and the CFO transition cap the upside and warrant caution. Against that, the two signed 10-year hospital alliances with quantified accretion ramping in 2H 2026, the first positive Medicare update in five years, rising forward estimates, and a reasonable ~19x next-year multiple with a 2.9% dividend provide a specific 12-month growth thesis worth holding through the next two quarterly prints. The appropriate posture is to hold and watch the Q3 2026 print for confirmation that the alliance ramp is converting into reported EBITDA, with explicit upgrade and downgrade triggers defined above.
