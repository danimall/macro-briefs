**Research Date: August 5, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Golden Entertainment is no longer a public company. On April 30, 2026 the company completed a two-part transaction in which VICI Properties acquired 100% of the land, buildings and improvements of seven casino real estate assets for $1.16 billion, while Chairman and CEO Blake L. Sartini and affiliated entities acquired the operating business and took it private under family ownership. Public holders received 0.902 shares of VICI common stock plus a $2.75 per-share cash distribution funded by Sartini, and GDEN was delisted from Nasdaq. The transaction closed after the Nevada Gaming Commission granted final approval on April 23, 2026, following a special-meeting vote of 20,430,245 for, 208,131 against and 20,158 abstaining.

This resolves the question the prior three reports on this name were circling. The recommendation history reads AVOID at 4.4, 4.1 and 4.3, with risk factors improving from 3.0 to 5.0 as deal certainty rose and competitive position and management quality each declining by one point. The current overall score of 3.63 is lower than the June reading, and the reason is mechanical rather than fundamental: there is no security, no authoritative quote (the canonical valuation snapshot returns `schwab_quote_unavailable` and `missing_authoritative_price`), no analyst coverage, and consequently no upside to measure. Every valuation, target and margin-of-safety field in this report is therefore either null or expressed against transaction terms rather than a market price. Readers should treat any residual GDEN price series, forward P/E, or earnings-surprise feed as terminal historical data, not a live input.

Two substantive conclusions survive the delisting. First, the deal was a full price for what the business had become: implied enterprise value of roughly $1.21 billion against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $140.0 million is about 8.7x, versus roughly 6.7x on the pre-announcement price, and the entire premium is explained by the arbitrage between an operator multiple and a 7.50% real estate cap rate ($87 million rent divided by $1.16 billion). Second, the risk did not disappear; it moved. VICI now owns a 30-year triple-net master lease at $87 million of initial rent with 2% escalators from year three, against a tenant whose consolidated Adjusted EBITDA fell 9.9% organically in FY2025 and whose recent capital spending ($47.5 million) nearly consumes post-rent EBITDA of roughly $53 million. Investors interested in this asset base should look at VICI, not at a delisted ticker.

## Company Overview

At its final 10-K (filed February 27, 2026, for FY2025), Golden operated eight casino properties in Nevada and 72 branded taverns concentrated in greater Las Vegas — approximately 5,600 slots, 80 table games, 6,000 hotel rooms and roughly 4,800–5,000 employees. Three reporting segments:

| Segment | FY2025 revenue | FY2025 Adj. EBITDA | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nevada Casino Resorts (The STRAT, Aquarius, Edgewater) | $375.6M | $92.4M | 25% |
| Nevada Locals Casinos (Arizona Charlie's ×2, Pahrump) | $150.9M | $67.9M | 45% |
| Nevada Taverns (PT's, Sierra Gold, Sean Patrick's) | $107.2M | $25.2M | 24% |
| Corporate and Other | — | $(45.5)M | — |
| **Total** | **$634.9M** | **$140.0M** | **22.1%** |

The genuine structural asset was the tavern network. Nevada's restricted-gaming license regime caps slot machines per food-and-beverage location, which limits entry and cannot be replicated by out-of-state operators; Golden built the largest such network in the state. The casinos sat at the value end of the market — The STRAT competes on the north Strip against far better-capitalized resorts, and Arizona Charlie's competes in the locals market against Red Rock Resorts and Boyd Gaming, both of which the 10-K risk factors acknowledge have "greater brand recognition and significantly greater resources."

Between 2023 and 2024 the company deliberately shrank into a pure Southern Nevada operator, selling Rocky Gap Casino Resort ($260.0 million, July 2023), Montana distributed gaming ($109.0 million, September 2023) and Nevada distributed gaming ($213.5 million, January 2024), for roughly $582.5 million of headline consideration and disclosed cash receipts of $362.4 million in 2023 and $204.4 million in 2024. Two small tavern bolt-ons (Lucky's, $10.0 million; GAP, $7.3 million) were the only additions.

## Financial Analysis

**A data-integrity correction that should be applied before any screening.** The standardized XBRL extraction returns FY2025 revenue of $584.192 million and FY2024 revenue of $610.757 million, against 10-K MD&A total revenues of $634.911 million and $666.818 million. The gaps of $50.719 million and $56.061 million equal the "Other" revenue line exactly; the FY2023 XBRL figure ($1,053.149 million) does match MD&A, so the tagging is inconsistent across years rather than systematically offset. Corrected on the audited denominator: operating margin 3.39% (not 3.7%), FCF margin 5.60% (not 6.1%), asset turnover 0.62 (not 0.57). The growth rate is barely affected (−4.8% versus −4.4%) because the error is present in both years, which is why it is easy to miss. The MD&A presentation governs below.

**Revenue.**

| ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaming | 674.3 | 319.3 | 316.1 |
| Food & beverage | 182.4 | 171.9 | 162.9 |
| Rooms | 124.6 | 119.6 | 105.1 |
| Other | 71.8 | 56.1 | 50.7 |
| **Total** | **1,053.1** | **666.8** | **634.9** |

The 2023→2024 collapse is divestiture mechanics. The FY2025 decline of $31.9 million (−4.8%) is nearly all organic, and its composition matters: rooms revenue fell $14.5 million, or 12.1%, which is a hotel-occupancy problem at The STRAT and Laughlin rather than a gaming problem. Gaming fell only 1.0%, but that figure is flattered by a presentation change — certain taverns moved from a space-lease arrangement (fixed rent booked in Other revenue) to a participation agreement (booked in Gaming), moving $2.9 million into gaming and $3.6 million out of Other. Underlying gaming was weaker than reported and Other weaker than reported by less. Because participation economics differ from fixed rent, a clean adjusted gaming growth rate cannot be computed from the disclosure, and the shift should not be read as evidence of changed business quality in either direction.

**Profitability.** Total Adjusted EBITDA fell 37.1% from $222.5 million (FY2023) to $140.0 million (FY2025). Excluding divested units, the FY2024→FY2025 decline was $15.3 million, or 9.9%. Three features are more informative than the headline:

1. The deterioration was tourist-facing. Nevada Casino Resorts revenue fell 6% and Adjusted EBITDA 11%, with margin compressing from 26% to 25%. Nevada Locals Casinos grew Adjusted EBITDA 2% with margin expanding from 44% to 45%, on higher slot and bingo revenues and lower operating expense. Within one company and one state, the locals business outperformed the tourist business.
2. Tavern margins eroded two consecutive years — 30% (2023) to 25% (2024) to 24% (2025) — attributed in MD&A to higher labor costs and cost of goods. The licensing moat protected the right to operate but not unit economics, which are exposed to Nevada wage and food inflation like any restaurant chain. Given that the taverns were the differentiated asset, this is the most concerning line in the segment data.
3. Stranded corporate cost is real and quantified. Corporate and Other drag was $(51.5)M, $(42.1)M and $(45.5)M in 2023–2025 — 4.89%, 6.31% and 7.17% of revenue. The company shed roughly 40% of its revenue base and did not shed proportionate overhead; the dollar drag then rose in 2025. Part of the increase is investment (MD&A identifies $0.638 million of ERP/finance/payroll implementation costs as an add-back) and part is transaction activity that cannot be isolated from the disclosure, but the direction is unambiguous and is the most defensible economic argument for private ownership.

**GAAP earnings.** FY2025 produced a net loss of $(6.0) million, the first in four years, against net income of $50.7 million (FY2024) and $255.8 million (FY2023). The prior-year comparisons are dominated by non-recurring gains on sale of businesses ($303.2 million in 2023, $69.2 million in 2024). Within FY2025, $10.2 million of the deterioration was a loss on disposal of retired property in the Locals and Casino Resorts segments; absent that item pre-tax income was roughly breakeven. The structural point is thin GAAP earnings cushion: $140.0 million of Adjusted EBITDA was almost entirely consumed by $90.3 million of depreciation and $30.7 million of net interest. The quarterly record corroborates the deterioration — the four reported quarters of 2025 each missed consensus, with the December quarter at $(0.33) against a $0.17 estimate.

**Cash flow.**

| ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 119.2 | 92.3 | 83.1 |
| Capital expenditures | 85.9 | 49.9 | 47.5 |
| **Free cash flow** | **33.3** | **42.4** | **35.6** |

Operating cash flow of $83.1 million against a $(6.0) million net loss confirms the GAAP loss was a depreciation-and-retirement artifact rather than a cash problem. Management's stated explanation for the $9.2 million year-over-year OCF decline — "primarily related to a $10.0 million loss on disposal of assets" — does not hold up analytically, since a disposal loss is a non-cash add-back in the operating section; the true driver is more likely working-capital timing and lower EBITDA, and the filing does not establish it. No maintenance-versus-growth split of the $47.5 million capex is disclosed, which is the binding gap for any normalized free cash flow or rent-coverage estimate.

**Balance sheet at December 31, 2025.** Cash $55.328 million; senior debt $435 million ($390 million Term Loan B-1 plus $45 million revolver drawn) plus $1.343 million of notes; total assets $1,018.1 million; equity $420.9 million; debt/equity 1.42x; net PP&E $699.6 million; goodwill $86.5 million; no FY2025 impairment; current ratio 1.17. Net debt of $381.0 million against $140.0 million of Adjusted EBITDA is 2.72x — comfortable for a gaming operator. Maturities: $4 million annual term amortization with a $374 million balloon in 2030, revolver due 2028; 2026 contractual interest of $26.8 million. The credit facility carried a springing maximum net leverage covenant triggered only above 30% revolver utilization ($72 million of the $240 million commitment); at $45 million drawn (18.75%) the company was effectively covenant-lite, and an $8 million January 28, 2026 revolver repayment took the drawn balance to $37 million with $203 million of availability, or roughly $258 million of total liquidity. All borrowings were floating and unhedged, with each 25 basis points worth about $1.0 million of annual interest.

**On the Altman Z-Score of 1.66 ("distress zone").** The binding component is EBIT/assets of 0.021, a direct function of $90.3 million of depreciation on a $699.6 million net PP&E base plus the $10.2 million disposal loss. An owner-operator of casino real estate will score poorly on both EBIT/assets and revenue/assets by construction. Against that: 2.72x net leverage, $83.1 million of operating cash flow versus $26.8 million of 2026 cash interest, $258 million of liquidity, a springing-only covenant not triggered, and no maturity before 2028. The screen identified the right economic condition — thin operating earnings against a large, undermonetized asset base — and drew the wrong conclusion from it. That condition is precisely what the sale-leaseback exploited. No Piotroski F-Score was returned, so one leg of the screen is absent rather than favorable.

**Share count discipline.** FY2024 basic shares were 28.184 million versus diluted 29.699 million. FY2025 reports both at 26.283 million because the net loss renders potential shares antidilutive; the convergence is an accounting consequence, not evidence dilution disappeared. The reduction in basic shares reflects buybacks, and MD&A notes a $69.3 million year-over-year decrease in repurchase spending, meaning 2024 buybacks substantially exceeded 2025's.

Financial health scores 5.0. Leverage, liquidity and covenant structure were sound; thin GAAP earnings power, unhedged floating debt, two years of tavern margin compression and rising overhead against a shrinking revenue base cap it at average.

## Growth Analysis

The company-selected compensation metric series tells the story directly: Adjusted EBITDA of $194.8 million (2020), $300.7 million (2021), $271.3 million (2022), $226.8 million (2023), $155.4 million (2024) and $140.0 million (2025). Most of the 2021–2024 decline is divestiture mechanics — distributed gaming and Maryland contributed $47.2 million of segment EBITDA in 2023 and essentially nothing by 2025 — so the series should not be read as five years of operating decay. The genuinely organic deterioration is the FY2025 −9.9%, driven by a 12.1% decline in rooms revenue and lower STRAT tower visitation, plus two consecutive years of tavern margin erosion.

There was no visible reacceleration path in the disclosure. Company-wide FY2025 capex of $47.5 million against $699.6 million of net PP&E is a reinvestment rate of roughly 6.8% per year, and the 10-K risk factors concede that failure to renovate "may put us at a competitive disadvantage." Over the same window Red Rock Resorts was spending $385 million expanding Durango and renovating Green Valley Ranch and Sunset Station, and reported (August 4, 2026) its second-highest second-quarter gaming revenue and profitability in company history. That comparison is directional rather than like-for-like — Red Rock competes at a different price point with newer product, and its Q2 2026 results postdate Golden's FY2025 by up to eighteen months — but it argues against a "whole market rolled over" reading and toward asset-specific weakness at the north-Strip and Laughlin properties.

Growth prospects score 2.5. Declining organic EBITDA, an underfunded reinvestment rate against a better-capitalized locals competitor, and zero forward participation for any public investor.

What can be reconstructed is the transaction arithmetic, which is the only valuation question that remains.

- Implied EV paid: 26.2M × $30.00 = **$786M** equity, plus up to **$426M** of debt assumed and repaid by VICI = **$1,212M**, or **8.66x** FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $140.033M.
- Implied EV pre-announcement: 26.2M × $21.28 = **$558M**, plus net debt of $381.0M = **$939M**, or **6.70x**.
- Implied real estate cap rate: $87M initial rent / $1,160M = **7.50%**.

The entire 41% premium is explained by the spread between a ~6.7x operator multiple (a 14.9% earnings yield on EV) and a 7.50% cap rate on the same real estate. That is the mature gaming sale-leaseback arbitrage VICI and its peers built over the last decade, and it required no assumption about mistimed selling or private information.

## Competitive Landscape

The tavern licensing regime was a genuine barrier: a capped slot count per food-and-beverage location, requiring Nevada restricted licensure, with Golden holding the largest network in the state at more than 70 PT's pubs. That moat protected access but not economics, and tavern margins fell from 30% to 24% over two years on labor and cost of goods inflation.

The casino portfolio was structurally disadvantaged. The STRAT is a north-Strip asset dependent on tourist visitation, which is where the FY2025 damage concentrated (rooms revenue −12.1%, Casino Resorts EBITDA −11%). Aquarius and Edgewater in Laughlin depend entirely on drive-in Arizona and California customers, exposing them to the specific channels the 10-K names: expansion of California and Arizona tribal gaming closer to that customer base, flight frequency into Las Vegas, and interstate highway capacity. In the locals market Golden competed against Red Rock and Boyd with roughly 6.8% annual reinvestment of net PP&E, while Red Rock deployed $385 million on one expansion. Longer term, the 10-K notes internet gaming has been approved in various forms in Nevada, which is a real substitution threat to a convenience-slot tavern product.

Sartini's own account to the Nevada Gaming Control Board in April 2026 was internally consistent with the segment data: the Las Vegas Valley economy "stable to strong" on population growth and business diversification, an explicit acknowledgment of "a disconnect between lower visitation and rising gaming revenue on the Strip," and a claim that the local market is less dependent on Strip trends than in the past.

Competitive position scores 4.0, continuing the prior decline. The differentiated asset was compressing on margin; the largest segment was losing to better-capitalized competition it could not out-invest.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that mattered, drawn from Item 1A of the final 10-K, now attach to either the private operator or, through the lease, to VICI:

- **Total geographic concentration.** All casinos in Nevada, taverns almost entirely in greater Las Vegas, with named channel risks (tribal gaming expansion, flight capacity, interstate highway capacity).
- **Tenant credit / rent coverage.** Initial rent of $87 million against FY2025 consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $140.033 million is **1.61x** coverage, leaving post-rent EBITDA of **$53.0 million** against recent capex of $47.5 million — before acquisition-debt interest under the Santander financing, before cash taxes, and before the 2% escalator beginning in year three. Measured only against the two casino segments ($92.4M + $67.9M = $160.3M), coverage is **1.84x**, but that denominator excludes the ~$45.5 million of corporate overhead the operator must still fund. Neither figure is a company disclosure; VICI has published no coverage ratio for this lease in anything reviewed. Sensitivities, not forecasts: a further 10% EBITDA decline to ~$126 million puts coverage at 1.45x; a 20% decline to ~$112 million puts it at 1.29x. Offsets are real — a private owner can strip a meaningful share of public-company overhead, maintenance capex is likely well below $47.5 million given prior-year growth spending at The STRAT, and the Sartini family holds a large equity interest in the operator's survival.
- **Floating, unhedged debt with a covenant-lite structure.** $435 million at variable rates, no swap requirement, $1.0 million of annual interest per 25 basis points, and a leverage covenant that springs only above 30% revolver utilization. Favorable in benign conditions, unbuffered otherwise.
- **Labor.** Roughly 1,350 employees, or 28% of the workforce, under collective bargaining agreements. Culinary renewals in Las Vegas are step-function cost events, and tavern margins were already compressing on labor.
- **Water and energy scarcity.** The 10-K states plainly that potable water in Nevada will become "an increasingly scarce commodity at an increasing price." For 6,000 hotel rooms this is an operating cost line.
- **Gaming-license suitability mechanics.** Authorities can compel any securityholder to be found suitable, and the articles permitted redemption of securities held by persons jeopardizing licenses at the trailing 30-day average price, which the company warned could "divert our cash resources from other productive uses."
- **Legal mechanics gap.** Golden was a Minnesota corporation and remained subject to MBCA §302A.673, which generally prohibits business combinations with an interested shareholder for four years following the acquisition date. How that statute interacted with a purchase by a ~25% holder is not explained in any source reviewed and remains unresolved.
- **Prior cyber incident.** A 2021 ransomware attack, disclosed as fully remediated, in a market where the 2023 MGM and Caesars incidents established that Las Vegas operators are actively targeted.

**Verification gaps material to confidence.** The DEFM14A filed March 6, 2026 could not be retrieved, so Macquarie Capital's fairness opinion and its underlying valuation ranges, the Background of the Transaction, golden-parachute amounts, and any majority-of-the-unaffiliated-shareholders condition are all unverified — the single largest limitation on any judgment about whether $30.00 was full value. The closing 8-K was likewise unretrievable, so closing rests on two independent secondary sources plus the filing's existence and date. No Form 4 insider data was obtainable for the deregistered entity, so no claim is made about insider transactions. VICI's April 30 closing price is unverified. The XBRL pull returned an entity name of "NEW ROYAL HOLDCO I INC.", plausibly related to the disclosed pre-closing restructuring but equally consistent with a stale data mapping; either way, a pipeline keyed to entity names would have mislabeled these final financials.

Risk factors score 4.5. Deal-completion risk, the dominant binary in the prior report, is fully resolved, which argues for improvement, but that resolution came with total loss of forward participation, and the residual GDEN-linked risk set is unmeasurable rather than benign.

## Management Quality

Compensation (DEF 14A filed April 9, 2025, FY2024 data): CEO Sartini total compensation $5,404,137, compensation actually paid $2,711,157; NEO average $2,006,108 and $795,598. Five-year CEO totals were remarkably stable at $5.23M, $8.78M, $6.16M, $6.06M and $5.40M. The **sole disclosed performance measure was Adjusted EBITDA**, with MD&A confirming the annual plan is tied to it. A single-metric EBITDA plan contains no capital-efficiency, leverage or return-on-capital component, which is one plausible reason a large real estate base sat unmonetized while EBIT/assets ran at 2%. The counterargument is legitimate: Adjusted EBITDA is the conventional gaming-industry metric and the primary measure used by the chief operating decision maker, and indexed five-year TSR of 178.40 against a peer group at 74.15 is genuine outperformance. Golden entered 2025 roughly 32% below its 2021 indexed TSR peak.

The governance question is the conflict: the Chairman and CEO bought the operating business. Disclosed protections were real — an Independent Committee that approved unanimously and retained its own advisors (Macquarie Capital, Latham & Watkins) separate from Sartini's (Santander, Greenberg Traurig), a go-shop through December 5, 2025 with a right to terminate for a superior proposal, and a shareholder vote. They were not maximal. A post-signing go-shop is weaker than a pre-signing auction, the company said it would not update the market on that process unless required, and the family's ~25% voting agreement meant a plain majority-of-outstanding condition required only about 25.1% additional support from the remaining ~75%. Whether an unaffiliated-majority condition existed is unverified. In the event the point was academic: 98.99% of votes cast were in favor, and no topping bid emerged during the go-shop.

Two other threads. Halper Sadeh announced a fairness investigation the day of announcement — routine plaintiff-firm activity on any take-private, carrying essentially no informational content. Everbay Capital published a **follow-up** letter to the board on November 13, 2025 expressing "significant concerns" about both legs of the transaction; the wording implies pre-announcement engagement, but the letter's substance, Everbay's stake and any board response could not be retrieved and are unverified. Given the 98.99% vote, whatever was argued did not gain traction.

Management quality scores 4.5, marginally above the prior 4.0. The conflicted structure and weaker-than-maximal process protections keep it below average, but the delivered outcome is a real datapoint: a 41% premium, an 8.7x exit against a 6.7x market multiple, a tax-deferred stock leg (the §368(a) opinion was a substantive closing condition), and a coherent multi-year sequence of divesting non-core assets, repaying debt (including the April 2024 redemption of the 7.625% notes for $276.5 million) and returning capital.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (retrospective only).** A former holder who owned GDEN at the pre-announcement reference of $21.28 realized between $27.10 and $30.00 of value plus roughly $0.50 of dividends over about six months — a gain of at least 27% — and now holds VICI shares acquired on a tax-deferred basis, a 6.8%-yielding diversified experiential REIT with 93 experiential assets, 54 gaming properties and Q2 2026 AFFO of $0.62 per share (+4.6%) on revenue of $1.06 billion (+5.7%), with 2026 AFFO guidance of $2.45–$2.47 and the low end raised. The board monetized an undermonetized real estate base at a 7.50% cap rate rather than continuing to run a value-tier operator with a 6.8% reinvestment rate against a competitor spending $385 million on one property.

**Bear case (retrospective and forward, for VICI holders).** Holders were paid roughly 91% in another company's stock at a fixed ratio with no disclosed collar, and appear to have realized meaningfully less than the $30.00 headline. A holder who bought near the FY2025 high of $34.09 never recovered cost. The buyer was the Chairman and CEO, the go-shop came after signing, and the fairness opinion supporting the price could not be examined. Forward, the tenant is a business whose organic Adjusted EBITDA fell 9.9% in its final reported year, whose differentiated tavern asset has compressed margin two years running, and which retains roughly $53 million of post-rent EBITDA against $47.5 million of recent capex, before acquisition-debt interest and before a 2% annual escalator that runs for 30 years regardless of performance.

**Neither case is actionable in GDEN.** There is no listed security, no residual GDEN-linked instrument identified, and no price basis. The recommendation is AVOID because there is nothing to buy or hold.

## Short Candidate Assessment

There is no future earnings report, refinancing date, regulatory ruling or contract renewal attached to GDEN, because the registrant no longer files and the equity no longer trades.

**Timing window.** Not applicable. The event has passed.

**Borrow and options signal.** No options data was available and no borrow exists. A delisted security cannot be located for short sale.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Undefined. With no price basis (the valuation snapshot returns no quote, currency or market date) there is no entry, no target and no ratio to express.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The instrument does not exist. Any bearish view on the underlying assets would have to be expressed through VICI, where the relevant exposure is landlord credit against a tenant covered roughly 1.6x on FY2025 consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, offset by a 30-year triple-net lease, escalators, and a diversified 54-property gaming portfolio. That is a low-conviction short at best and is outside the scope of this report.

2. **The exposure has migrated to VICI.** Anyone who wants this asset base should evaluate VICI directly, monitoring: (a) whether VICI discloses a reported rent-coverage ratio for the Golden master lease, and whether it holds above roughly 1.5x on its own EBITDAR methodology; (b) whether the private operator's post-rent cash flow supports maintenance capex once public-company overhead (a $45.5 million FY2025 drag) is stripped; (c) VICI AFFO guidance revisions from the current $2.45–$2.47.
3. **Remove GDEN from the coverage universe** pending confirmation of deregistration, on the same basis applied to other closed transactions. Any GDEN price series, trailing multiple, or earnings-surprise record appearing in a data feed should be flagged as terminal historical data. The XBRL entity-name anomaly ("NEW ROYAL HOLDCO I INC.") is an additional reason to sever the mapping rather than continue ingesting it.

**Timeline.** Permanent for GDEN as a listed equity. The tenant-credit question at VICI is a multi-year monitoring item, with the first material checkpoint being the year-three onset of the 2% rent escalator and any VICI disclosure of lease-level coverage before then.

**Trajectory note.** Prior reports scored 4.4, 4.1 and 4.3, all AVOID, with the June reading crediting improved risk as deal certainty rose. The current 3.63 is lower, and the mechanism should be stated plainly: the decline is driven by valuation and growth dimensions collapsing to reflect the absence of a security and of forward participation, not by newly discovered fundamental deterioration. The FY2025 fundamentals are essentially as prior reports described them — 2.72x net leverage, $83.1 million of operating cash flow, $35.6 million of free cash flow, a 9.9% organic EBITDA decline and compressing tavern margins.

## Conclusion

**AVOID.** Golden Entertainment ceased to be a public company on April 30, 2026. VICI Properties acquired seven casino real estate assets for $1.16 billion and assumed and repaid up to $426 million of debt; Blake Sartini and affiliates acquired the operating business with Santander financing; public holders received 0.902 VICI shares plus $2.75 in cash and the shares were delisted from Nasdaq. No authoritative price exists, no analyst coverage exists, and no analyst targets can be reported.

Retrospectively the outcome was defensible for holders who owned the stock before announcement: roughly 8.7x trailing Adjusted EBITDA paid against a market multiple near 6.7x, with the full premium explained by the 7.50% cap rate on the real estate, and realized value of roughly $27 to $30 per share against a $21.28 pre-announcement reference. It was less flattering for anyone who bought near the FY2025 high of $34.09. The governance structure was conflicted and the process protections were real but not maximal, and the fairness opinion that would settle the value question could not be examined.

The forward question belongs to VICI. Initial rent of $87 million is covered roughly 1.6x by FY2025 consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $140.0 million, leaving about $53 million of post-rent EBITDA against $47.5 million of recent capital spending, before acquisition-debt interest, in a business whose organic EBITDA declined 9.9% in its final reported year and whose only genuinely differentiated asset — the largest restricted-license tavern network in Nevada — has been losing margin for two consecutive years. That is a monitoring item for landlord credit, not a prediction of distress, and it is not expressible through a ticker that no longer trades.
