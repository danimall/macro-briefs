## Executive Summary

Broadcom is pivoting from a diversified chip/software conglomerate toward the dominant provider of custom AI accelerators (XPUs) for hyperscale data centers. AI semiconductor revenue hit $8.4B in Q1 FY2026 (ended February 2026), up 106% YoY, and management guided Q2 revenue to $22.0B (+47% YoY) with AI revenue accelerating to $10.7B. Six hyperscaler customers — Google, Meta, ByteDance, OpenAI, and two undisclosed — are locked in with multi-year, multi-billion-dollar XPU engagements. CEO Hock Tan has stated line-of-sight to $100B in AI chip revenue by 2027, roughly a doubling from the implied current run rate.

The stock trades at ~21x forward earnings on FY2026E consensus of $11.41, revised up 12.5% over 90 days. Next-year estimates of $17.84 have been revised up 27% over the same period. At ~$373, AVGO sits 10% below its 52-week high of $415 and roughly 130% above its 52-week low. The forward multiple of 21x represents a discount to AMD (82x) and a modest premium to TSM (4.7x trailing, reflecting Taiwanese accounting conventions). The primary risks are customer concentration (one distributor at 32% of revenue), TSMC single-source dependency, $67B in debt, and the sustainability of hyperscaler AI capex.

The prior score trajectory showed a decline from 7.2 to 6.7, with a downgrade from BUY to HOLD in early March. Since that downgrade at approximately $318, the stock has rallied 17%. Q1 FY2026 results and Q2 guidance exceeded expectations, and the 90-day estimate revision cycle confirms accelerating earnings momentum. The data supports re-upgrading to BUY.

## Company Overview

Broadcom operates two segments: **Semiconductor Solutions** (58% of FY2025 revenue, $36.9B) and **Infrastructure Software** (42%, $27.0B). The semiconductor business designs custom AI accelerators, network switching ASICs (Tomahawk series), SerDes interconnects, wireless components (FBAR filters for Apple), storage controllers, and broadband products. Manufacturing is fabless, with ~95% of wafers produced by TSMC.

The infrastructure software segment, anchored by VMware Cloud Foundation (VCF), also includes mainframe software (CA Technologies), cybersecurity (Symantec enterprise), and Fiber Channel SAN management. Broadcom's $84B VMware acquisition, closed November 2023, transformed the company into a roughly half-chip, half-software entity. The VMware integration is now substantially complete, with the subscription conversion driving annualized recurring revenue up 19% YoY.

Broadcom employs ~33,000 people and is headquartered in Palo Alto, California.

### Profitability

FY2025 gross margin recovered to 67.8% from 63.1% in FY2024, reflecting the completion of VMware integration charges and favorable AI semiconductor mix. A structural margin concern exists: Broadcom's expansion into selling and leasing integrated AI rack systems (rather than just chips) will compress gross margins. The 10-K explicitly warns of this transition. The impact is likely gradual through FY2026-2027 but could shift the margin profile downward by 200-400bp over time while potentially improving operating margins through volume leverage.

### Balance Sheet

The Altman Z-Score of 2.16 falls in the "grey zone" but is misleading for acquisition-heavy balance sheets with large goodwill. Broadcom's actual liquidity position — $27B+ annual FCF, $16B cash, investment-grade credit, and declining debt — is strong. Debt service coverage (FCF/interest) is ~8.4x.

## Growth Analysis

4. **OpenAI** — First-generation XPU, volume deployment in 2027, 1GW+

The $100B AI chip revenue target for 2027 is ambitious. Q2 FY2026 AI revenue of $10.7B annualizes to ~$43B. Reaching $100B in calendar 2027 (FY2027, ending October 2027) requires roughly a 130% increase in 18 months. This is aggressive but directionally consistent with the ramp profiles described — particularly Meta (scaling to multi-GW), OpenAI (new customer at scale), and two unnamed customers doubling.

## Competitive Landscape

**vs. Nvidia:** Broadcom's custom XPU model is complementary to Nvidia's GPU approach. Hyperscalers deploy both. Broadcom's advantage is workload-specific optimization — custom silicon designed for a specific model architecture can deliver better performance/watt than general-purpose GPUs. Nvidia's advantage is ecosystem, software stack (CUDA), and the ability to serve the broader AI market beyond the 6 hyperscalers. The competitive risk is that Nvidia's product cadence continues to narrow the performance gap, reducing the ROI of custom silicon.

**AI Networking:** Broadcom's Tomahawk 6 (100 Tbps) and forthcoming Tomahawk 7 (200 Tbps) are industry-leading switch ASICs. The 200G SerDes technology enables cheaper copper interconnects for AI clusters, displacing optical. Marvell competes in some segments, but Broadcom's scale and customer relationships provide meaningful advantages.

**VMware:** The deliberate up-market strategy is working by revenue metrics but has created a competitive opening for Nutanix, Proxmox, and hypervisor alternatives in the SMB/mid-market. The risk is that VMware's moat weakens over a multi-year period as alternatives mature. Gartner forecasts 35% of VMware workloads migrated elsewhere by 2028. Large enterprise VCF adoption rates (~90%+ of top customers) are holding for now.

**TSMC Dependency:** 95% of Broadcom's wafers come from TSMC. This is the single most critical supplier relationship in the company. TSMC's pricing power, capacity allocation decisions, and geopolitical risk (Taiwan) are existential factors. Broadcom's supply chain is "secured through 2028" per management, but the structural dependency remains.

## Risk Assessment

### Customer Concentration (HIGH)
A single distributor accounts for 32% of total revenue (up from 28%), and the top 5 end customers represent ~40%. Loss of a single hyperscaler XPU customer would reduce AI revenue by 15-20%.

### TSMC Single-Source Risk (HIGH)
95% wafer dependency on one fabricator creates supply, pricing, and geopolitical risk. TSMC price increases directly compress semiconductor margins. Taiwan Strait escalation (estimated at 12-18% probability over the medium term) would be catastrophic.

### AI Capex Sustainability (MEDIUM-HIGH)
The $100B revenue target requires hyperscalers to collectively invest hundreds of billions in AI infrastructure through 2027. Any material pullback — driven by disappointing AI ROI, recession, or capital constraints — would directly impact Broadcom's growth trajectory. The shift toward AI rack leasing and alternative financing models suggests some customers already face capital constraints.

### Debt Load (MEDIUM)
$67.1B in debt is manageable at current FCF levels (8.4x coverage) but creates vulnerability if growth decelerates. The current rate environment (Fed funds 3.64%, unlikely to be cut) means refinancing costs stay elevated.

### Global Minimum Tax (MEDIUM)
Management explicitly guided a "material impact" from global minimum tax laws in FY2026. Singapore's 15% minimum takes effect this fiscal year. Broadcom saved $2.7B in taxes through offshore incentives in FY2025 — even a partial recapture would reduce EPS by $0.50-$0.60.

### VMware Churn (LOW-MEDIUM)
Nutanix's 30,000 customer migration claim and Gartner's 35% workload migration forecast by 2028 present a slow bleed rather than an acute risk. Revenue growth is overcoming churn for now.

### Options Market Signal
The broader semiconductor space has seen elevated implied volatility given the geopolitical backdrop (US-China tensions, trade restrictions). AVGO's beta of 1.25 is moderate for semis, but the stock's 54% range between 52-week high and low ($162-$415) indicates the market prices significant outcome dispersion. The move from ~$300 to ~$373 in two weeks (April 7-13) on AI deal reports suggests significant event-driven optionality.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($475-535)
AI semiconductor revenue reaches or exceeds the $100B target by FY2027. All six XPU customers scale as guided, with potential for 7th and 8th customers. AI networking captures 40%+ of AI revenue. VMware VCF becomes the standard private AI cloud platform. Broadcom achieves $17-19 in FY2027 EPS, and the market assigns a 28-30x multiple reflecting durable growth. Stock reaches $475-535 within 12-18 months.

### Bear Case ($260-290)
Hyperscaler AI capex peaks in late 2026, with 2-3 customers delaying or reducing XPU orders. VMware churn accelerates as Nutanix and alternatives mature. Global minimum tax reduces EPS by $0.50+. TSMC raises pricing aggressively. FY2027 EPS comes in at $14-15 rather than $17.84 consensus. Stock trades down to 18-20x trough estimates. Downside: 22-30% from current levels.

### Base Case ($445-475)
AI revenue grows 60-80% in FY2027 (below the $100B stretch target but still exceptional). VMware stabilizes at current growth rates. Estimate revisions moderate but remain positive. FY2027 EPS of $16-17.50. Market assigns 26-28x forward multiple. Stock reaches $445-475 within 12 months.

## Conclusion

Broadcom is a $1.77 trillion company with 100%+ growth in its largest business driver, trading at 21x forward earnings against a 27% 90-day upward revision to next-year estimates. The prior downgrade to HOLD in March at ~$318 was defensible given valuation uncertainty at the time, but the subsequent Q1 earnings report, Q2 guidance, and estimate revision cycle have materially strengthened the fundamental case. The growth trajectory is confirmed by six hyperscaler customers with quantified deployment plans.

The risks are concentrated and serious: 32% revenue dependency on a single distributor, 95% wafer dependency on TSMC, $67B in debt, and the binary question of whether hyperscaler AI capex sustains through 2027. These risks constrain the valuation score but do not override the growth-adjusted value proposition at current prices.

Upgrading to BUY with a base case target of $460 and a 12-month investment horizon.
