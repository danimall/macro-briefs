## Executive Summary

Choice Hotels delivered its first genuinely encouraging operating quarter in three years on August 5, 2026, and simultaneously cut GAAP earnings guidance by 13%. Both facts are real, and reconciling them is the whole analysis. U.S. room openings rose 27% to the highest second-quarter level since 2019, exits fell to the lowest second-quarter level since 2020, U.S. franchise agreements awarded rose 30%, the conversion pipeline grew 24% to 24,100 rooms, and U.S. RevPAR turned positive at +1.3% after falling 3.1% in FY2025. U.S. net rooms improved for a second consecutive quarter to -0.3% year over year, against -2.9% for full-year 2025. Management raised guidance on U.S. RevPAR, global net rooms, royalty rate, and adjusted EBITDA.

The cost of that turn is visible in three places the adjusted metrics do not capture. First, the net reimbursable deficit — marketing and reservation-system spending in excess of the fees Choice collects from franchisees for those programs — has gone from $18.2M in FY2024 to $47.1M in FY2025 to $99.9M implied by FY2026 guidance ($695.6M expense less $595.7M revenue). Choice excludes 100% of it from adjusted EBITDA, adjusted net income, and adjusted EPS on the basis that the programs are managed to break even over time. Second, cash franchise-agreement acquisition costs (key money) net of reimbursements rose 74.0% in H1 to $72.2M from $41.5M, against only $12.5M of amortization added back in adjusted EBITDA. Third, interest expense, a higher tax rate, and $7.5M of equity-method losses pushed H1 adjusted diluted EPS *down* 4.9% to $3.09 from $3.25 even as adjusted EBITDA rose 2.2%. H1 operating cash flow was $67.4M against $301.1M of adjusted EBITDA; derived H1 free cash flow was approximately $22.2M.

We are upgrading from AVOID to REDUCE while nudging the composite score to 4.15 from 4.0. The unit-contraction plank of the prior bearish thesis has partially reversed, and at 30.4% short interest with 10.4 days to cover, an actively negative stance carries poor mechanics. But the case for owning is not there either: consensus estimates are falling across every horizon (next-year EPS -3.8% over 90 days), the base case values the equity at roughly the current price, the bear case is -23%, capital returns are running at about twice operating cash flow on borrowed money with the repurchase authorization nearly exhausted, and the company has an interim chief executive with no announced successor. Trim into strength; revisit on evidence the reimbursable deficit has peaked.

## Company Overview

Choice is a franchisor, not an operator. At December 31, 2025 the system comprised 7,575 open hotels and 656,825 rooms across 49 U.S. states and 50 countries and territories, plus a pipeline of 825 hotels and 77,862 rooms. U.S. franchise agreements typically run 10 to 30 years and carry a royalty of 5%–6% of gross room revenue plus a marketing and reservation fee of 3%–4%. The marketing and reservation fee is contractually restricted: the 10-K states Choice is required to use those collections for system-wide marketing and reservation activities. That restriction creates the reimbursable revenue and expense lines that dominate the GAAP earnings picture.

The portfolio's center of gravity is midscale and upper midscale: 4,223 U.S. hotels and 322,296 rooms across Comfort, Quality, Sleep, Country, Clarion, and Park Inn. Comfort (178,818 global rooms) and Quality (148,452) alone approach half the global system. Economy (Econo Lodge, Rodeway) is 1,000 U.S. hotels and 56,550 rooms. Extended stay (WoodSpring, MainStay, Suburban, Everhome) is 598 hotels and 60,121 rooms, and is the growth engine. Upscale and above (Cambria, Ascend, Radisson family) is 374 hotels and 60,259 rooms.

Two structural features matter for interpreting headline growth. Of 161,863 international rooms, 93,354 are master-franchised, and the 10-K states master franchising "typically provide[s] lower revenues to the Company." FY2025 international royalty fees of $41.3M on 159,846 rooms works out to roughly $258 per room against U.S. royalty fees of $439.8M on 496,979 rooms, or roughly $885 per room, a 3.4x yield gap. Second, Choice owned 19 operating hotels as of August 5, 2026 (plus one under construction) as a brand-incubation tool, with the 10-K stating it does not intend to own hotels permanently. The company has now announced first dispositions in H1 2027, subject to market conditions.

Headcount is 1,562 U.S. and 192 international associates, a lean base against 656,825 rooms. Choice Privileges had more than 74 million members at year-end 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 established the problem.** Reported revenue was approximately $1.60B with net income near $370M, GAAP operating income of $448.4M, and operating cash flow of $270.4M, down from $319.4M. The net income figure requires adjustment: it included an approximately $100M non-taxable gain on remeasurement of the Choice Hotels Canada equity investment upon acquiring control, leaving roughly $270M of clean FY2025 net income. (The exact gain and consolidation date are not independently confirmed and the FY2025-to-FY2026 bridge below is sensitive to that figure.)

The domestic engine went into reverse. U.S. properties fell from 6,328 to 6,187 (-141), rooms from 511,739 to 496,979 (-2.9%), and U.S. royalty fees from $454.7M to $439.8M (-3.3%) despite the average royalty rate rising 8bps to 5.14%. U.S. RevPAR fell 3.1% to $52.85 on occupancy of 55.6% (from 56.4%) and ADR of $95.05 (from $96.67). Pricing gains were more than offset by unit and RevPAR losses, and unit attrition concentrated in economy brands where franchisee balance sheets are thinnest.

**Q2 and H1 2026 show revenue growth and GAAP earnings decline simultaneously.** Q2 total revenue was $440.8M (+3.4%); revenue excluding reimbursables was $277M against $259M (+7.0%). Yet GAAP operating income fell 16.4% to $104.1M and net income fell 21% to $64.3M, with diluted EPS of $1.41 against $1.75. For H1, operating income fell 19.7% and net income 33%. Adjusted EBITDA rose 6.3% in Q2 to $175.4M and 2.2% in H1 to $301.1M.

The reconciling item is the reimbursable deficit: $72.2M in H1 2026 against $29.4M in H1 2025. Reimbursable revenue is declining (H1 $287.2M vs $290.8M) while reimbursable expense rose 12.3% in H1 to $359.5M. The deficit is widening from both sides.

**The guidance split is the clearest expression.** In one release:

| FY2026 guidance | Current | Prior |
|---|---|---|
| Net income | $230–241M | $265–275M |
| Adjusted net income | $312–323M | $320–330M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $635–650M | $632–647M |
| Diluted EPS | $5.07–5.31 | $5.72–5.94 |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $6.86–7.10 | $6.92–7.14 |
| Effective tax rate | 26% | 25% |

A reproducible bridge: FY2025 net income ~$370M less the ~$100M Canada gain = ~$270M; less the ~$53M incremental reimbursable deficit taxed at 26% (-$39M) = ~$231M; less roughly $5M after-tax from higher interest expense (FY2026 guide $96.2–96.4M vs ~$89M in FY2025) and the swing to a guided $10.6M equity-method loss, partly offset by fee growth. That lands inside the $230–241M range. Substantially all of the ~$130M GAAP earnings decline is the absent one-time gain plus the reimbursable ramp.

**Adjusted EBITDA is not approximating cash.** H1 adjusted EBITDA of $301.1M produced $67.4M of operating cash flow (from $116.1M). Three disclosed items account for most of the gap: the $72.2M reimbursable deficit (real cash, added back), $72.2M of net cash key money against $12.5M of amortization added back, and $48.2M of interest. Derived H1 free cash flow: $67.362M less $17.900M other property and equipment less $27.292M owned-hotel investment = **$22.2M**, against $32.1M on the same basis in H1 2025.

We are deliberately not annualizing. H1 is seasonally weaker for a franchisor whose fees track franchisee room revenue, and key money is lumpy and tied to opening timing. As a labeled sensitivity, H2 key money at the H1 pace would push FY2026 key money toward ~$145M. As a normalized offset, management guides FY2026 net capital outlays for hotel-development activities to $20–45M against $103.4M in 2025, and H1 already absorbed $45.2M of the two capex lines, so H2 investing should be materially lighter. FY2026 free cash flow is likely positive but well below adjusted EBITDA less interest and cash taxes.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $42.8M, receivables $279.8M (from $207.5M), total assets $2.989B, goodwill and intangibles $1.409B, long-term debt $2.002B (from $1.906B), shareholders' equity $142.2M (from $181.2M). Tangible book equity is negative. Net debt of $1.959B against management-reported net debt/adjusted EBITDA of 3.1x, inside the stated 3.0x–4.0x target and well inside the 4.5x credit-agreement covenant (the 10-Q reported 3.02x on the covenant definition). Interest coverage is approximately 6.7x on FY2026E adjusted EBITDA and roughly 5.0x on FY2025 GAAP operating income. Total available liquidity of $475M reconciles as ~$434M undrawn revolver plus $42.8M cash; cash on hand is $42.8M, not $475M.

**Capital returns are debt-funded and nearly out of runway.** H1 2026: dividends $26.3M plus treasury purchases $107.5M (issuer cites $139M including tax-withholding repurchases) against $67.4M of operating cash flow, with $96.3M of net revolver borrowings. Only 1.8 million shares remain authorized, roughly 4% of shares outstanding.

The accretion math on that trade is thin. Approximately $107.5M repurchased near $110 retires about 977K shares, roughly 2.2% of count, worth roughly $0.15 on $6.98 of adjusted EPS. Incremental interest on $96.3M of borrowings, at the implied cost inside the $96.2–96.4M FY2026 interest guide, costs roughly $0.10 per share after tax. (Debt tranche coupons are unverified, so treat this as a sensitivity.) That is why H1 adjusted EPS fell 4.9% to $3.09 while adjusted EBITDA rose 2.2%: interest, a higher tax rate, and equity-method losses consumed the operating gain, and the share count reduction did not bridge it.

**Earnings track record.** Four quarters: two beats, two misses, with the misses larger. Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.07 missed the $1.33 estimate by 19.7%. Q2's $2.02 beat $1.98 by 2.1%. Visibility on a franchisor's quarterly cadence is weaker than the model's stability implies, which argues for discounting rather than extrapolating the guided range.

## Growth Analysis

The Q2 volume data is the strongest set of operating datapoints Choice has produced since 2019, and it is multi-metric rather than a single line item: U.S. room openings +27% (~6,400 rooms), U.S. franchise agreements awarded +30% (~9,400 rooms), global awards +20%, U.S. conversion pipeline +24% to 24,100 rooms and +6% sequentially, U.S. extended-stay net rooms +13.0% marking a twelfth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth, U.S. royalty rate +11bps to 5.23%, U.S. RevPAR +1.3% (rate +0.7%, occupancy +40bps), international RevPAR +2.1% currency-neutral. Guidance was raised on U.S. RevPAR (0% to +1.25%, from -2% to +1%), global net rooms (~1.5% from ~1%), royalty rate (+7 to 9bps), and adjusted EBITDA.

The conversion pipeline is the most structurally credible element. Choice's own framing in the 10-K is that conversion brands benefit from lodging downturns as independents and other-chain affiliates convert in. When new construction is expensive, new-build economics are impaired and conversion economics improve. Conversion rooms are 26,400 of the 77,300 global pipeline. This is a genuine counter-cyclical asset and it is beginning to show up in the awards data.

Three qualifications keep growth prospects at a below-average score.

First, the translation to earnings is weak. Derived FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of approximately $625M (backed out of the issuer's 3.1x TTM net leverage and $1.959B net debt, then rolled through H1 periods) implies FY2026 growth of roughly 2.7% at the guided midpoint, below the +6.3% Q2 optic and below the +2.2% H1 print's flattering comparison. That derivation depends on the issuer's net-debt definition matching ours.

Second, global net rooms growth of ~1.5% is a lower-yield metric than it reads. U.S. rooms are -0.3% while international is +12.5%, and one U.S. room carries roughly 3.4x the royalty of one international room. A meaningful portion of the 2025 international step-up appears attributable to consolidating Choice Hotels Canada rather than organic franchise wins: international rooms were 143,838 at June 30, 2025 against 142,071 at December 31, 2024, implying most of the year's addition landed in H2. Consolidating an acquired joint venture is not organic expansion, and the two should be tracked separately.

Third, and most important for the score: **consensus estimates are falling on every horizon.** Current quarter -3.5% over 30 days and -3.9% over 90 days; next quarter -1.1% and -2.9%; current year -1.0% and -2.3% to $7.03; next year -2.3% and -3.8% to $7.49. The pattern of large positive ninety-day revisions alongside accelerating revenue that would signal an underestimated inflection is absent here; the sign is negative. Sixteen analysts cover the next-year estimate and the recommendation distribution is 1 strong buy, 2 buy, 9 hold, 2 sell, 2 strong sell.

On the cost side: adjusted SG&A rose 10.3% in H1 to $156.4M against 2.2% adjusted EBITDA growth. Full-year guidance for mid-single-digit adjusted SG&A growth arithmetically requires roughly flat H2 spending year over year. Management attributed part of the Q2 shortfall to expense timing, so some relief is plausible, but the H1-to-guide gap is a specific and dated execution test.

## Valuation Assessment

At $104.44 with 45.1M shares and $1.959B of net debt, enterprise value is approximately $6.67B.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2026E adjusted EBITDA ($642.5M mid) | 10.4x |
| EV / FY2026E EBITDA including reimbursable deficit ($542.6M) | 12.3x |
| Price / FY2026E adjusted diluted EPS ($6.98 mid) | 15.0x |
| Price / FY2026E GAAP diluted EPS ($5.19 mid) | 20.1x |
| Price / consensus current-year EPS ($7.03) | 14.9x |
| Price / consensus next-year EPS ($7.49) | 13.9x |
| Dividend yield (~$1.17 annualized) | ~1.1% |

Against peer medians of 27.1x P/E and 2.6% yield, with Wyndham, the closest franchisor comparable, at 27.1x, CHH screens cheap on the adjusted line. That discount is the crux, and it is not obviously a mispricing.

The 10.4x-versus-12.3x spread is the valuation expression of the reimbursable question. If the deficit is a recoverable timing difference, with Choice front-loading technology and guest-delivery investment and recovering it through the marketing-fee assessments the contracts permit, then 10.4x on a stabilizing franchisor is inexpensive and GAAP will converge upward toward adjusted over two to three years. If the deficit has become a structural subsidy sustaining system size, then roughly $100M per year of reported adjusted EBITDA is not economic, the right multiple is 12.3x on a business growing adjusted EBITDA ~2.7%, and 20.1x GAAP earnings is full. Three consecutive years of widening deficits sits uneasily with "manages to break even over time," though three years is short against 10-to-30-year franchise agreements and the exclusion is a standard, fully disclosed franchisor convention. We do not have evidence on management intent and decline to infer it from the accounting.

The second reason to withhold a high valuation score is cash. Free cash flow of $22.2M in H1 against a $4.71B equity value is not a support level, and the gap to adjusted EBITDA is not a one-quarter artifact: it is the reimbursable deficit plus key money plus interest, two of which are growth spending that should persist while the company is buying unit growth back.

**Scenario framework** (12 months, from $104.44):

- **Bear — $80 (-23%).** The U.S. consumer weakens, RevPAR returns to negative territory, economy-brand attrition resumes, and the reimbursable deficit holds above $100M with no franchisee-fee recovery. Adjusted EPS drifts to ~$6.30 and the multiple compresses to ~12.5x. Reference points: analyst low target $86; 52-week low $84.04.
- **Base — $106 (+1.5%).** Net rooms cross to flat-to-slightly-positive, royalty rate adds 7–9bps, RevPAR runs 0% to +1%, and the deficit plateaus without inverting. FY2027 adjusted EPS ~$7.20 at 14.5–15x, a discount to franchisor peers for slower growth, negative tangible equity, weak cash conversion, and interim leadership.
- **Bull — $130 (+24%).** The deficit peaks in 2026 and begins inverting in 2027 so GAAP converges toward adjusted; owned-hotel dispositions execute near the externally reported ~$450M (Skift, unconfirmed by Choice), taking net leverage toward ~2.3x; the board re-authorizes repurchases; a permanent CEO is named with a credible plan; FY2027 adjusted EPS ~$7.60 at 17x. Consistent with the $131 analyst high.

Probability-weighting 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear gives roughly +1.8% expected return over twelve months, below any reasonable required return on equity risk. Analyst mean of $114.93 and median of $119 sit above our base case; on a stock with negative estimate revisions, falling GAAP earnings, and a 20x GAAP multiple, those targets embed the same adjusted-metric lens whose validity is the open question, so they are not independent confirmation of upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Choice competes in the least defensible part of the branded-lodging structure. The 10-K names Hampton by Hilton, Holiday Inn Express, and Fairfield by Marriott as the principal competitors to Comfort, Choice's largest brand. Those three sit inside systems with substantially larger loyalty programs, deeper direct-booking share, and stronger corporate account access. Choice's counter is franchisee return on investment, and 141 U.S. net property losses in 2025 indicate a segment of franchisees was not persuaded.

Truist's Patrick Scholes, reported by Skift on August 5, observed that Choice's segment-level Q2 RevPAR growth trailed the corresponding U.S. industry segments — upscale +1.3% against industry ~5%, midscale +1.1% against ~4%, economy -0.7% against ~+1% — concluding continued share loss. Those industry comparison figures are secondary and unconfirmed by primary sources, but the direction is corroborated by Choice's own disclosures of -3.1% U.S. RevPAR and -2.9% U.S. rooms in FY2025.

The internal barbell is stark. Extended stay works: 598 U.S. hotels, net rooms +13.0%, Q2 RevPAR +3.7% on ADR +3.5%, and 29,900 rooms or 39% of the global pipeline. But the competitor set the 10-K names for that category now includes Marriott's StudioRes, Hilton's LivSmart, and Wyndham's Echo Suites; the majors have pointed new-construction capital directly at Choice's best-performing segment. Economy is in structural decline: 1,000 U.S. hotels with Q2 RevPAR -0.7% and H1 RevPAR -4.4% on ADR -2.6% and occupancy -80bps, and this is where unit attrition and franchisee credit stress concentrate.

Choice's route to competitive scale through consolidation closed when it abandoned the Wyndham pursuit in 2024 (background, not verified in the filings read). The current strategy is organic: royalty-rate increases, extended-stay new build, master-franchised international expansion, conversions, and partnership revenue ($28.7M in Q2, +5.9%). Conversion-brand breadth across Quality, Clarion Pointe, Ascend, Park Inn, and Radisson is the most credible structural asset, and the 24% pipeline growth is the first evidence in several years that it is re-engaging.

## Risk Assessment

**Franchisee distress and system attrition.** The dominant risk, and it materialized in 2025: 141 net U.S. property losses and 14,760 net room losses concentrated in economy. It transmits through three channels at once: lost royalties, credit losses on receivables, and a shrinking base over which to spread marketing and reservation costs, which worsens the reimbursable deficit. Any weakening of the U.S. consumer would land squarely on midscale and economy RevPAR.

**Franchisee credit quality.** Receivables rose from $207.5M to $279.8M between December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026. Part is normal seasonality, but the 10-Q also disclosed a higher allowance for credit losses and a meaningful balance of non-accrual notes, with H1 provisions running near the full-year 2025 level. The specific allowance and non-accrual figures were flagged for re-verification and we treat the direction, not the magnitude, as established.

**The reimbursable program.** The risk is asymmetric: Choice bears the cost of underfunding brand investment (weaker system, more defections) but recovering an accumulated deficit means assessing higher fees on franchisees already choosing to leave. The company-selected performance measure in the incentive plan is operating income — the GAAP line the deficit depresses — so management is not insulated the way adjusted-EBITDA-focused investors are.

**Governance and leadership.** Four Item 5.02 filings between January and July 2026. CEO Patrick Pacious stepped down effective May 20, 2026 after nine years, with Dominic Dragisich appointed **interim** CEO. Expected post-employment benefits of approximately $2.7M through August 31, 2026 suggest a short, defined transition rather than a change-in-control package, but severance size establishes nothing about cause and no company statement characterized the departure. The material fact is the absence of a permanent CEO while strategy, the owned-hotel disposition program, and capital allocation are all live. Five-year cumulative TSR of $92.81 against a peer group at $129.35 — roughly 36 percentage points of underperformance — is the context in which the board acted. The Board's Human Capital and Compensation Committee oversees succession planning for the top 60 leadership roles, with the talent landscape reviewed with the Board semi-annually.

**Balance-sheet flexibility.** Not a solvency question at 3.1x net leverage against a 4.5x covenant and ~6.7x interest coverage. It is an optionality question: $42.8M of cash and ~$434M of revolver capacity, with capital returns already running at twice operating cash flow, leaves limited capacity to absorb a demand shock while sustaining the dividend and buyback. A standard Altman Z-Score will screen CHH as distressed; that is largely an artifact of a decade of buybacks pushing book equity to $142M in an asset-light model, and covenant headroom and coverage are the informative measures.

**Owned-hotel disposition execution.** Property and equipment net is $653.5M and Choice intends to begin selling 19 owned hotels in H1 2027, subject to market conditions. Cambria is an upscale, urban, select-service product whose transaction market depends on debt availability and cap rates neither the company nor investors control. The externally reported ~$450M figure is not a company estimate.

**Insider activity.** Dispositions only, no open-market purchases in the reviewed window. Interim CEO Dragisich sold roughly 22,600 shares at ~$113–115 in late May, partly option-exercise funded, retaining ~81,600 shares; CFO Oaksmith sold 4,000 shares in June. Option-exercise-and-sell is routine and the retained position is substantial, so we weight this lightly. The more informative observation is the absence of any open-market buying at $105 after 36 percentage points of five-year TSR underperformance, with an interim CEO publicly stating "this business has significantly more potential."

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 54.1% against 37.6% one-year realized, a 16.6-point premium, at the 70th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (54.1% at three days, 35.9% at one month, 38.1% at six months). ATM IV rose from 49.0% to 54.1% since July 23 while the price fell 2.0%. The market is paying up for near-dated protection or exposure without pricing a durable regime change six months out; the nearest expiry implies ±4.5% and the December tenor ±18.8%. Reported put/call skew is -10.5% near-dated, characterized by the data source as call-heavy, with a modest -3.3%/-3.1% at the one- and six-month tenors. Call-heavy positioning alongside 30.4% short interest and 10.4 days to cover is consistent with squeeze hedging.

That short interest is the single most important mechanical fact here. At 30.4% of float, the bearish case is thoroughly consensus. It caps the attractiveness of an actively negative stance: a single quarter confirming the unit inflection, a permanent CEO appointment, or an owned-hotel sale at or above the reported ~$450M could force a sharp move higher on flow rather than fundamentals. That risk argues for trimming exposure rather than shorting it, and it is the primary reason we are moving off AVOID.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $130 (+24%)

The 2025 contraction was cyclical rather than terminal, and Q2 2026 is the turn. Openings +27%, awards +30%, exits at a six-year low, and the conversion pipeline +24% reflect a franchisor whose brand breadth is designed for exactly this environment: new construction is uneconomic at current financing costs, so independents and other-chain affiliates convert into Quality, Clarion Pointe, and Ascend. The royalty rate has risen 8bps in 2025 and 11bps in Q2 2026 and is guided up another 7–9bps, so revenue per room is compounding even at flat unit counts. Extended stay has grown double-digits for twelve straight quarters.

On this reading, the reimbursable deficit and the 74% key-money increase are counter-cyclical investment with identifiable payback: key money amortizes against a 10-to-30-year royalty stream, and $99.9M of system marketing on ~657,000 rooms is roughly $150 per room per year. The deficit peaks in 2026, inverts in 2027 as collections catch the spend and the system grows, and GAAP earnings converge toward the $6.86–7.10 adjusted line. Owned-hotel proceeds near $450M cut net leverage toward 2.3x, funding a re-authorized buyback without additional borrowing. At 17x on $7.60 of FY2027 adjusted EPS, the equity is worth $130.

### Bear Case → $80 (-23%)

The adjusted metrics are describing a business that no longer exists in cash terms. H1 2026 produced $22.2M of free cash flow against $301.1M of adjusted EBITDA, and the gap is structural: a reimbursable deficit widening from both sides (revenue declining, expense +12.3%), key money up 74%, and interest expense rising as buybacks are funded on the revolver. Adjusted EPS itself fell 4.9% in H1 despite adjusted EBITDA growth, because interest, tax, and equity-method losses consumed the gain.

The competitive picture supports the pessimistic reading of the spend. Segment RevPAR trailed the industry in all three chain scales in Q2. Comfort and Quality compete against Hampton, Holiday Inn Express, and Fairfield inside far larger loyalty ecosystems, and the majors have now launched competing economy and midscale extended-stay new-build brands aimed at Choice's one strong category. Economy H1 RevPAR of -4.4% points to continued attrition where franchisee balance sheets are weakest, and rising credit provisions corroborate it. If the U.S. consumer weakens into 2027, RevPAR returns negative, the deficit cannot be recovered from a shrinking franchisee base, and the market re-rates the stock on GAAP earnings of roughly $5.20 rather than adjusted $6.98. At 15x GAAP that is $78; the 52-week low is $84.04 and the lowest analyst target is $86.

The bear case does not require a crisis. It requires the deficit not to invert and estimates to keep drifting the way they have drifted for 90 days.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE. Twelve-month base case $106 (+1.5%), bull $130 (+24%), bear $80 (-23%). Probability-weighted expected return approximately +2%.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength rather than selling indiscriminately. The stock has traded between $102.63 and $115.65 over the past six weeks; scale exposure down on moves above $112–115, which is where the risk/reward is most clearly negative against a $106 base case. Retain no more than a token position pending resolution of the two open items below. Do not short: at 30.4% short interest, 10.4 days to cover, and call-heavy near-dated skew, the mechanics are unfavorable and the squeeze tail is live.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or ACCUMULATE** (any two would move us):
1. **The reimbursable deficit peaks.** Track reimbursable revenue and reimbursable expense every quarter. Q3 and Q4 2026 must show the year-over-year deficit expansion decelerating, with a credible path to inversion in 2027. This is the single falsifiable test of the entire adjusted-metric framework.
2. **U.S. net rooms cross to positive** (from -0.3% at June 30) with U.S. royalty fees growing year over year — the dollar metric, not the room count.
3. **A permanent CEO is named** with a stated capital-allocation framework, and the board re-authorizes repurchases beyond the remaining 1.8M shares.
4. **H2 2026 adjusted SG&A roughly flat year over year**, validating the mid-single-digit full-year guide after +10.3% in H1.
5. **Owned-hotel dispositions signed at or above ~$450M** with proceeds applied to debt reduction rather than incremental buybacks.
6. **Price below $88–90**, which would place the stock near 12.5x current-year adjusted consensus and inside the bear-case band, restoring asymmetry independent of the operating questions.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. FY2026 reimbursable expense guidance raised again, or the FY2027 deficit guided at or above $100M, which would convert the "timing difference" reading into a structural subsidy.
2. U.S. net rooms growth deteriorating back below -1.0%, or U.S. RevPAR turning negative for two consecutive quarters.

4. A material increase in the credit-loss allowance or non-accrual notes receivable alongside accelerating economy-brand exits.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results (early November) are the first hard test: reimbursable line trajectory, H2 SG&A, U.S. net rooms, and any CEO announcement. The second is the FY2026 10-K (February 2027), which will confirm full-year key money, the FY2027 reimbursable guide, and disposition timing. The H1 2027 asset-sale window is the third.

## Conclusion

Choice Hotels is a genuinely good business model — 5%–6% royalties on 657,000 rooms run by roughly 1,750 employees — attached to a franchise system that shrank 2.9% in the United States last year and is now being rebuilt with cash the adjusted income statement does not show. The Q2 volume data is real and better than we expected: openings +27%, awards +30%, exits at a six-year low, RevPAR positive, and a conversion pipeline up 24% in exactly the financing environment where Choice's conversion brands should win. That partially reverses the unit-contraction plank on which the prior AVOID rested, and it is why we are upgrading one notch.

The reasons not to own are unchanged and quantified. The reimbursable deficit has gone $18.2M → $47.1M → $99.9M guided, entirely excluded from the metrics management guides to, and GAAP net income guidance was cut 13% in the same release that raised adjusted EBITDA. H1 free cash flow was $22.2M against $301.1M of adjusted EBITDA. H1 adjusted EPS fell 4.9% year over year despite adjusted EBITDA growth, because debt-funded buybacks, a higher tax rate, and equity-method losses consumed the operating gain. Consensus estimates are falling on all four horizons. The company has $42.8M of cash, negative tangible equity, 1.8M shares left on its repurchase authorization, and an interim chief executive.

At $104.44 the base case is roughly the current price, the bull case requires the deficit to invert and asset sales to execute into an uncertain cap-rate environment, and the bear case is 23% lower. That is below-fair expected return. Trim. The trade becomes interesting again either near $88–90, where the multiple compresses inside the bear band, or on two consecutive quarters showing the reimbursable deficit has peaked, whichever arrives first.
