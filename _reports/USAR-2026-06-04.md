**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

USA Rare Earth is a development-stage, vertically integrated "mine-to-magnet" rare earth company that went public via SPAC merger in March 2025. Its purpose is to build a U.S./allied rare earth value chain spanning mining (Round Top, Texas), separation (Colorado), metal/alloy production (Less Common Metals in the UK), and sintered NdFeB permanent magnets (Stillwater, Oklahoma), in order to displace Chinese dominance of a supply chain in which China controls roughly 90% of rare-earth processing and 99% of heavy-rare-earth processing. The current price (~$30.70 as of the latest session, up ~225% over twelve months) supports a market capitalization near $6.8 billion against trailing-twelve-month revenue of single-digit millions and no commercial magnet production.

Two June 2026 developments materially changed the risk profile. First, the definitive agreements with the Department of Commerce were finalized (Reuters-confirmed June 3), unlocking access to up to $1.6 billion (up to $277M in CHIPS direct funding plus up to $1.3B in senior secured loan capacity). Second, the April 19 definitive agreement to acquire Serra Verde would, if it closes, convert USAR from a pre-revenue developer into an operator of a producing Brazilian mine that supplies all four magnet rare earths and carries a 15-year offtake backed by a U.S.-government-capitalized SPV with price floors on 100% of Phase 1 output. These two items are the substance of the bull thesis. Against them sit a $6.8B valuation on negligible revenue, no proven reserves, no committed magnet offtake, an estimated $4.1B long-term capex requirement, and dilution that has already taken share count from ~28.6M (2024) to ~218M (March 2026) with another ~126.8M Serra Verde shares pending.

The honest call is HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The government and Serra Verde catalysts create real, dated optionality that separates USAR from a generic story stock, and the recent definitive agreements de-risk the funding side of the thesis. But the equity is priced for flawless execution across mining permitting, magnet commissioning, three-continent integration, and multiple future capital raises. There is no fundamental basis for a long recommendation at this valuation, and the binary, dilution-heavy structure does not support a short either. This is a position to hold and monitor against a specific milestone calendar, not to accumulate.

## Company Overview

USA Rare Earth operates four links of an intended end-to-end rare earth value chain:

1. **Round Top Deposit (Sierra Blanca, TX)** — held through the RTMD joint venture (USAR ~81.6%, with Texas Mineral Resources Corp. holding the remainder). The deposit is described as one of North America's largest recoverable heavy-rare-earth resources, with a ~72% HREE basket including dysprosium, terbium, yttrium, plus gallium and hafnium. It remains at the **exploration stage with no proven or probable reserves** under Reg S-K 1300. A Pre-Feasibility Study is expected Q3 2026, a Definitive Feasibility Study Q1 2027, and commercial production is targeted for late 2028 under an accelerated mining plan. Fluor and WSP have been named EPCM partners.

2. **Colorado Facility (Wheat Ridge)** — R&D and demonstration for solvent-extraction separation, with a DOE National Energy Technology Laboratory collaboration LOI.

3. **Less Common Metals (LCM)** — acquired November 18, 2025; a Cheshire, UK metal/alloy maker at ~2,500 MTPA capacity (expanding to 3,000 by end-2026). This is the company's only current revenue source. It achieved its first commercial yttrium-metal pour in April 2026 and is building a 3,750 MTPA plant in Lacq, France.

4. **Stillwater Facility (OK)** — sintered NdFeB magnet plant. Phase 1a was commissioned in Q1 2026; first customer orders were expected Q2 2026; the target ramp is 600 MTPA by Q4 2026, 1,200 MTPA by Q1 2027, and 10,000 MTPA by 2029.

The investment proposition is a national-security reshoring play, anchored by DFARS 225.7018 (effective January 1, 2027), which bars Department of War procurement of China-origin NdFeB and samarium-cobalt magnets. Management's argument is that this creates a bifurcated market in which DFARS-compliant, non-China magnets command premium pricing. The company employed 132 people as of December 2025 and is headquartered in Stillwater, Oklahoma.

## Financial Analysis

USA Rare Earth is pre-revenue in everything except the recently acquired UK metals business. The financials are best understood as a cash-rich balance sheet funding a multi-year build-out, with reported losses distorted by non-cash mark-to-market on SPAC-legacy liabilities.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story. After the January 2026 $1.5B PIPE (69.8M shares), cash stood at $1.75 billion at March 31, 2026, against total liabilities of only $246M and stockholders' equity of $1.88B. The accumulated deficit was $387M. Standard distress screens (Altman Z 2.86 grey zone, current ratio 10.2, debt/equity 0.39) are not meaningful for a pre-revenue developer; they reflect a large cash pile against minimal liabilities, not operating durability.

Reported net losses substantially overstate cash burn. The FY2025 loss included roughly $245M of non-cash fair-value movement on warrant and earnout liabilities (earnout liability $145M, warrant liability $26.5M, both Level 3). Underlying operating cash use was approximately $18.5M in Q1 2026, plus roughly $38.6M of capex. The relevant financial constraint is not near-term solvency but the magnitude of the future capital program: management cites approximately $4.1 billion of long-term capex, a requirement to raise at least $600M more equity by year-end 2027, and a $250M revolver targeted for year-end 2026.

Share dilution is the dominant financial fact. Shares outstanding moved from ~28.6M (2024) to 148M (December 2025) to ~218M (March 2026). Pending issuances include ~16.1M government shares plus ~17.5M government warrants (each at $17.17), ~3.8M for the TMRC consolidation, ~126.8M for Serra Verde (roughly 58% of the current count), and 5M earnout shares in tranche 2 (triggers above $20). Series A Preferred and Preferred Investor Warrants carry full-ratchet anti-dilution, with the conversion price already cut from $10 to $7.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed and of limited use at this stage: two beats and two misses across the last four quarters, with the misses driven largely by the non-cash fair-value items (–278% and –216% surprises in Q4 2025 and Q3 2025). The Q1 2026 print (–$0.12 vs. –$0.14 estimate) was a modest beat.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is entirely forward and capacity-driven, not extrapolated from a revenue base. The estimate-revision pattern is positive on the surface — next-year EPS estimates were revised from –$1.34 to +$0.03 over 90 days (+102.5% / +105.1%), and current-quarter estimates rose +42.7% over 30 days. Under normal circumstances, revisions of this magnitude combined with a revenue inflection would constitute a strong buy signal. The qualification is that these revisions are driven by the changing corporate structure (the Serra Verde producing-asset acquisition and finalized government funding) rather than organic operating leverage on an existing product. A swing to breakeven EPS next year is contingent on Serra Verde closing and contributing; it is not a clean operating-trough recovery.

The substantive growth drivers are:

- **Stillwater magnet ramp**: from Phase 1a commissioning (Q1 2026) to 1,200 MTPA (Q1 2027) to 10,000 MTPA (2029). Each step depends on commissioning execution and securing magnet offtake, of which there is currently none under definitive contract.
- **Serra Verde**: a producing Brazilian mine supplying all four magnet rare earths with U.S.-government-backed price floors on 100% of Phase 1 output. This is the single largest near-term step-change in the revenue and cash-flow profile, if it closes.
- **LCM expansion**: 2,500 → 3,000 MTPA by end-2026, plus the 3,750 MTPA Lacq, France plant.
- **DFARS demand cliff** (January 2027): the structural demand catalyst for premium-priced non-China magnets, contingent on the rule being enforced as scheduled and translating into orders.

The thematic case is real. Rare-earth supply-chain reshoring carries multiple independent confirming signals — finalized federal CHIPS funding, state grants (Texas TSIF up to $14.2M), French C3IV credits, and the DFARS rule. The demand backdrop is policy-driven and broad-based. What is unproven is USAR's ability to convert that demand into commercial production and contracted revenue on its stated timeline.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation is the binding constraint on this report. On a pro-forma basis including the 126.8M Serra Verde shares, the fully diluted equity value approaches $10 billion before counting government warrants, earnouts, and ratchet effects.

There is no conventional multiple framework that supports this price. Forward P/E is meaningless (negative trailing, breakeven-at-best next year contingent on the acquisition). EV/EBITDA is not calculable. The relevant valuation question is whether the optionality — government-backed funding, producing-asset offtake with price floors, and a regulatory demand cliff — justifies a $6.8–10B enterprise value before any of the core mining or magnet assets reach commercial scale.

The cycle-adjusted and inflection guidance matters here but does not rescue the score. The estimate-revision velocity is positive, but the company is not a cyclical trough recovery trading at a depressed multiple of normalized earnings; it is a venture-stage developer whose entire value rests on multi-year execution and continued capital access. The "analyst targets are floors" principle applies to cheap, inflecting stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples — USAR does not qualify because there is no earnings base against which to call the multiple cheap. Analyst price targets (low $32, high $45, mean $39.50, median $40, with Cantor at $35) sit above the current price, and the analyst tally is unusually bullish (1 strong buy, 7 buys, 0 holds, 0 sells), but these targets are built on the same forward-optionality extrapolation as the share price, not on independent fundamental support. They are not a margin of safety.

The valuation score is 3.5. The price embeds successful permitting of Round Top, successful magnet commissioning, successful three-continent integration, successful closing of Serra Verde, and successful execution of billions in future raises. Any single failure removes a meaningful fraction of the value, and the dilution structure means even success is shared with a growing share count. This is a stretched valuation with no current earnings base to anchor it.

## Competitive Landscape

The structural thesis is sound: China dominates every midstream and downstream step of the rare-earth chain, and U.S. defense procurement faces a 2027 regulatory cliff requiring non-China magnets that barely exist outside China today. USAR's genuine differentiators are its HREE-heavy Round Top basket (most domestic peers are light-rare-earth focused) and the LCM metal/alloy capability, which is a scarce non-China midstream asset.

Against this, USAR is earlier-stage than its most credible domestic competitor. MP Materials is the most advanced U.S. mine-to-magnet operator with real production, albeit LREE-focused. Lynas is the established international player, also LREE-weighted. Energy Fuels and Noveon are building positions. USAR's current revenue is tiny and entirely from an acquired UK metals business; its mining and magnet operations are not yet commercial. The Serra Verde acquisition would close much of this gap by adding a producing mine, which is why it is the pivotal transaction in the story.

The competitive trajectory is improving — USAR is assembling a credible, government-backed, multi-continent platform faster than peers — but the level remains that of a challenger that has not yet proven it can produce at scale. Competitive position scores 6.0: improving trajectory, unproven execution.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are large and only partially priced, which is why this dimension scores low (3.0):

1. **No commercial production, no proven reserves, no committed magnet offtake.** The company has never sold a neodymium magnet, Round Top has no declared reserves, and there are no definitive magnet sales contracts — only MOUs and discussions.
2. **Severe, compounding dilution.** Beyond the share-count explosion already realized, the pending Serra Verde issuance alone is ~58% of current shares. Government equity/warrants, earnouts, and full-ratchet preferred layer on top.
3. **Government-deal execution and constraint risk.** Funding is milestone-tied and clawback-eligible; the government retains its equity even if funding is withheld; covenants preclude additional non-government debt, a real constraint for an acquisition-heavy company.
4. **Capital dependence.** The plan requires billions more in equity and debt; failure to raise forces scope reduction.
5. **Customer concentration at LCM** (Customer 1 = 49% of Q1 2026 revenue).
6. **China dumping / export-control risk** and dependence on third-party feedstock until Round Top produces.
7. **DFARS demand tailwind could be delayed, waived, or fail to convert into orders.**
8. **Three-continent integration risk** and goodwill/intangible impairment exposure ($135M goodwill + $67M intangibles from LCM).

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in backwardation (near-term IV 122.2% vs. long-term 108.8%), with implied volatility roughly fair to one-year historical vol (119.5%). This is the market pricing imminent event stress around the cluster of June catalysts (government deal finalization, South Carolina facility, Serra Verde progress). Positioning is call-heavy: put/call skew of –33% near-term, put/call volume ratio 0.38, and the most unusual single line is a $28 put with 20.9x volume-to-open-interest that, against the call-tilted skew, reads more as event hedging than directional bearishness. Short interest at 11.7% of float with only 1.2 days to cover indicates elevated but easily-covered bearish positioning, not a crowded short and not enough to drive a sustained squeeze. The net options read is consistent with a binary, catalyst-driven name where the market is bracing for large moves in either direction without a strong directional consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$45, in line with the high analyst target)
The finalized CHIPS package de-risks funding; Serra Verde closes and delivers government-floor-protected cash flow from a producing mine supplying all four magnet rare earths; Stillwater ramps to 1,200 MTPA on schedule; DFARS enforcement in January 2027 creates a premium-priced compliant-magnet market; and USAR establishes itself as the leading Western mine-to-magnet platform. In this scenario the optionality converts to real cash flows and the equity re-rates on a producing-asset basis. Cantor's "go-to rare earth stock in the West" framing captures the upside narrative.

### Bear Case (~$14, roughly –55% from current)
Serra Verde closing slips or is renegotiated; the ~58% dilution lands without commensurate cash flow; Round Top permitting and feasibility studies disappoint or push beyond 2028; magnet offtake fails to materialize into definitive contracts; the next equity raise comes at a depressed price; and DFARS demand proves slower or smaller than the bull case assumes. With no earnings base, the stock reverts toward a financing-and-cash valuation closer to its 2025 trading range.

The base case sits between these, with execution milestones over the next 12–18 months determining which way the equity resolves. The risk/reward is genuinely two-sided, which is the core reason for the neutral call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next 12–18 months are dense with binary milestones. Natural checkpoints are the Q3 2026 PFS, the Stillwater ramp to 1,200 MTPA (Q1 2027), the Serra Verde close, CHIPS funding tranche releases, and the $250M revolver (due end-2026) and $600M equity raise (due end-2027).

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Serra Verde closes on stated terms and begins contributing government-floor-protected revenue, with the dilution offset by demonstrable cash flow, moving the company off a pure-optionality valuation.
- Definitive magnet offtake contracts signed at Stillwater (not MOUs), with the Phase 1 ramp on schedule.
- The PFS/DFS confirms commercially viable Round Top economics and a credible path to declared reserves.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- Serra Verde fails to close or is materially renegotiated, leaving the company pre-revenue at a ~$6.8B valuation.
- An equity raise priced well below market that triggers full-ratchet preferred dilution.
- CHIPS funding clawback or milestone failure, or a covenant-driven constraint that forces strategy contraction.
- Stillwater commissioning delays that push first commercial magnet revenue beyond 2026.

## Conclusion

USA Rare Earth is a policy-backed venture bet that has been partially de-risked by the June 2026 finalization of government funding and by the pending acquisition of a producing, offtake-protected mine in Serra Verde. These are substantive, dated catalysts that distinguish USAR from a generic pre-revenue developer and give the equity asymmetric optionality. The growth thesis is real — reshoring demand is broad-based, government-capitalized, and reinforced by the 2027 DFARS cliff — and the estimate-revision trend is positive.

What prevents a long recommendation is valuation and structure. At ~$6.8B (closer to $10B pro-forma for Serra Verde shares), the market is pricing successful execution across mining permitting, magnet commissioning, three-continent integration, and billions in future raises, with compounding dilution shared even in success. There is no earnings base to anchor the price, and the bear case carries roughly 55% downside. The recommendation is HOLD with an uncertain rationale: hold the position against the specific milestone calendar above, do not add at this valuation, and reassess at each catalyst. A confirmed Serra Verde close with floor-protected revenue, or definitive magnet offtake, would justify revisiting a more constructive stance.
