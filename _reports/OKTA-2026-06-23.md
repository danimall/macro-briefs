**Research Date: June 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Okta has completed one of the cleaner profitability turnarounds in software. GAAP operating income swung from –$516M in FY24 to +$149M in FY26, net income moved from –$355M to +$235M, and free cash flow reached $875M (a ~30% FCF margin) on negligible capex. This was driven by spend discipline rather than one-offs: sales and marketing fell from 46% to 35% of revenue and R&D from 29% to 22% over three years. The balance sheet is strong: $2.59B in cash and short-term investments, D/E of 0.39, and a sole $350M convertible note maturing June 2026 that will be cash-settled without strain. Management initiated the company's first-ever $1B buyback in January 2026 and has already deployed ~$320M near 52-week lows.

The offsetting reality is that the top line has decelerated steadily — +22% (FY24), +15% (FY25), +12% (FY26), +11% in Q1 FY27 — with Q2 guidance implying ~9-10%. Dollar-based net retention has compressed from 111% to 106-107%, meaning expansion within the installed base is thin and the model is increasingly dependent on new-logo wins. The story has re-rated from "unprofitable hypergrowth" to "profitable, cash-generative, slowing growth." The central tension is whether the agentic-AI / non-human-identity (NHI) opportunity — which management says drove roughly 30% of Q4 bookings — can reaccelerate growth before the human-seat IAM market saturates and Microsoft's bundled Entra identity compresses pricing.

We rate OKTA a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 5.9. The financial-health and cash-generation profile is now genuinely strong, and the Q1 beat-and-raise — which diverged from a same-day SentinelOne miss — suggests the operational narrative is improving faster than the lagging share price has rewarded. But with growth in the low double digits, net retention flat, dual-class control and a dense cluster of executive departures, and no insider-conviction buying, we lack the evidence for a BUY. The AI/NHI TAM expansion is the swing variable; until it shows up as reaccelerating subscription growth, the stock sits in fair-value territory.

## Company Overview

Okta is a pure-play cloud identity and access management vendor that positions itself as the "leading independent identity partner." It operates two platforms: the Okta Platform (Workforce Identity), governing access for employees, contractors, and increasingly non-human identities to applications, servers, APIs, and infrastructure; and the Auth0 Platform (Customer Identity / CIAM), a developer-focused product embedded into customer-facing applications. Auth0 was acquired in 2021 for ~$6.5B and is the source of Okta's $5.49B goodwill balance.

The business is ~98% subscription revenue, with customers typically paying annually in advance. That generates predictable revenue and a substantial deferred-revenue float ($1.9B at FY26 year-end). Scale metrics: 5,180 customers with >$100K ACV (Q1 FY27), >20,000 total customers, and >7,000 integrations in the Okta Integration Network. International is ~20% of revenue, and the company reports a single segment.

The differentiating claim is neutrality. Okta's competitive advantage is being the independent identity layer that interoperates across all clouds, applications, and even rival identity providers, distinct from Microsoft, which bundles Entra identity into its broader Office/Azure stack. In May 2026 Okta leaned into this by opening "Okta for AI Agents" to any identity provider and integrating with AWS Bedrock. (For attribution: Okta hosts on AWS and integrates with Bedrock as a partner; the AI-agent identity products are Okta's own IP, not AWS's.)

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory — double-digit but decelerating:**

| Fiscal Year | Revenue | Growth |
|----|---------|--------|
| FY23 | $1,858M | — |
| FY24 | $2,263M | +22% |
| FY25 | $2,610M | +15% |
| FY26 | $2,919M | +12% |
| Q1 FY27 | $765M | +11% YoY |

Management attributes the deceleration to longer sales cycles and a tougher macro. Q2 FY27 guidance implies ~$792M, roughly 9-10% growth — the deceleration is continuing, not stabilizing yet.

**Profitability — a real GAAP inflection.** GAAP operating income moved from –$516M (FY24) to –$74M (FY25) to +$149M (FY26), with Q1 FY27 at $56M versus $39M a year prior. Net income went from –$355M to +$28M to +$235M, with Q1 FY27 net income of $74M. The mechanism is operating leverage from cost discipline, not revenue acceleration. The key caveat is stock-based comp, which at $544M (FY26) is ~19% of revenue — large, though trending down from $684M (FY24) and $565M (FY25).

**Cash flow is the standout.** Operating cash flow rose from $512M (FY24) to $750M (FY25) to $884M (FY26), with Q1 FY27 OCF of $277M. FCF was $875M in FY26 on ~$9M of capex, well above GAAP margins because of the deferred-revenue float and the large non-cash SBC add-back. FCF conversion above net income confirms high-quality earnings.

**Balance sheet.** Cash plus short-term investments of $2.59B (Q1 FY27), an Altman Z-Score of 3.04 (safe zone), current ratio 1.43, and D/E of 0.39. The only debt is the $350M 2026 convertible note (0.375% coupon, maturing June 15, 2026), which Okta has elected to settle in cash, trivially covered by liquidity. The one structural caveat: $5.49B of goodwill is ~56% of total assets, entirely legacy Auth0. No impairment to date, but it dominates the asset base and would be a latent risk if CIAM growth disappoints. The accumulated deficit of ~$2.49-2.57B reflects the prior loss-funded growth era.

**Capital allocation pivot.** Okta repurchased $79M in FY26 and $241M (3.0M shares) in Q1 FY27 at ~$80/share, with $680M remaining as of April 30, 2026. Repurchases were executed near the low end of the FY26 trading range ($75-$128), so the timing looks reasonable. Net-share-settlement of RSU tax withholding ($192M in FY26) is a recurring, partly offsetting cash cost tied to vesting and stock price.

**Tax.** A full valuation allowance is still maintained on U.S. deferred tax assets, but management has twice flagged a potential release "in the foreseeable future" given sustained profitability. A release would produce a one-time non-cash earnings boost and a structurally lower go-forward effective rate — a headline EPS event that would not reflect operating performance. The R&D-expensing provision of recent tax law is a modest cash-tax tailwind.

The financial-health picture supports a high score (7.5): strong and growing FCF, an asset-light model, minimal leverage, an easily covered near-term maturity, and improving (if still elevated) SBC. The downside filter — covenant risk, refinancing cliffs, cash burn — does not apply here.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the dimension that holds OKTA back from a higher rating, and it is the most reliable discriminator in our framework. Three signals point in different directions:

**Negative:** Revenue growth has decelerated for three consecutive years. Dollar-based net retention fell from 111% (FY24) to 107% (FY25) to 106% (FY26), recovering slightly to 107% in Q1 FY27. At 106-107%, in-base expansion is thin, and the model leans heavily on new-logo acquisition in a maturing human-seat IAM market.

**Encouraging:** Remaining performance obligations grew 16% to $4.72B in Q1 FY27 (current RPO $2.5B), faster than reported revenue. Backlog growing ahead of recognized revenue is a modestly positive forward signal that suggests bookings are healthier than the headline growth rate implies.

**The swing variable:** The agentic-AI / NHI opportunity. Management describes "acute" demand for AI-agent identity security, with the President quoted that interest has been unlike anything seen for prior launches, and AI security products cited as driving the bulk of Q4 bookings momentum. The logic is sound: every AI agent and non-human identity needs to be authenticated and governed, and identity becomes a control point as enterprises deploy agents at scale. If this converts to reaccelerating subscription revenue, it expands the TAM beyond the saturating human-seat market. The "Okta for AI Agents" expansion in May 2026 to any agent ecosystem, any identity provider, and AWS Bedrock reinforces the neutrality positioning that differentiates Okta from hyperscaler-bundled identity.

The R&D investment supporting this (~22% of revenue, or ~$642M based on FY26 revenue) is substantial, and the product velocity is real. But the thesis is unproven at the level of reported financials — bookings mix is encouraging, yet net retention and revenue growth have not inflected. We score growth 5.5: real bookings momentum and a credible TAM expander, offset by a decelerating top line and flat retention. This is the dimension to watch most closely on the next two prints.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$92, OKTA trades at roughly 25x forward earnings on consensus non-GAAP EPS (Q1 FY27 delivered ~$0.91 vs. ~$0.85 expected), with FCF yield in the mid-single digits on the ~$15-16B market cap. This is a moderate multiple for a profitable, cash-generative software franchise — neither stretched nor obviously cheap.

The case against rewarding the valuation: revenue growth is decelerating, not accelerating from a trough, and estimate revisions could not be verified in the available data. The earnings inflection has already occurred (FY26), so the easy operating-leverage gains are largely in the numbers. PEG on ~11% growth and ~25x forward is above 2 — full for the growth being delivered today. A compressed multiple relative to historical SaaS peaks reflects the structural reality of slowing growth and Microsoft bundling, not a mispricing to be arbitraged.

The case for some upside optionality: the valuation-allowance release would deliver a one-time EPS boost and a lower run-rate tax rate; the remaining buyback at low-$80s/low-$90s prices supports per-share metrics; and if AI/NHI bookings convert to reaccelerating revenue, the multiple re-rates on a higher growth trajectory. Barclays raised its target above $100 in May 2026 anticipating a possible FY27 guidance raise. Company TSR (indexed 33) has badly lagged the software peer set (256), reflecting the 2021-22 SaaS de-rating and breach overhang, which means sentiment is depressed, not euphoric, leaving room if execution holds.

We treat the cheap-relative-to-peak framing with skepticism: a low multiple here is mostly a fair discount for decelerating growth and competitive pressure, not a value-trap signal in the impairment sense, but also not an independent buy catalyst. We score valuation 5.8: balanced, with identifiable but not yet realized upside catalysts (VA release, AI conversion) against full PEG on current growth. The analyst targets [unverified — structured price-target feed unavailable; only a Barclays target above $100 is sourced in the brief] bracket a fair-value stock rather than one with asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Okta's defensible niche is vendor neutrality — large enterprises that want identity independent of any single cloud or application vendor, and developers (Auth0) who need embeddable CIAM. The Okta Integration Network and the switching costs inherent in an identity layer wired across an enterprise's app estate are real moats.

The principal threat, named explicitly in the 10-K as Okta's "principal competitor," is Microsoft. Entra ID is bundled into E3/E5 licenses, can be sold at zero or negative margins, and benefits from incumbency in nearly every enterprise. This is a structural pricing and bundling pressure on a standalone vendor, and it is the most likely explanation for the net-retention compression — customers can satisfy "good enough" identity within an existing Microsoft license. The same risk extends to the AI-agent opportunity: Microsoft and hyperscalers could embed equivalent agent-identity features for free, a risk the 10-K calls out directly ("competitors give an equivalent feature away for free").

The competitive trajectory is the reason this dimension declined from 7.0 to 5.5 across prior reports, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that. The neutrality positioning is genuinely differentiating in the agentic-AI context, and the same-day divergence from SentinelOne suggests Okta-specific strength rather than a falling tide. But net retention near 106-107% indicates the moat is holding share more than gaining it, and the bundling threat is the structural headwind. We score competitive position 5.5 — a quality franchise with a real moat, but a flat-to-modestly-eroding trajectory rather than an improving one. We confirm this against the growth and retention data rather than treating the neutrality narrative alone as conclusive.

## Risk Assessment

**Cybersecurity incident history.** Okta has had two high-profile incidents — a January 2022 third-party compromise and an October 2023 breach of its third-party customer support system. Both the 10-K and 10-Q carry a dedicated "Impact of Cybersecurity Incidents" section stating these "harmed our reputation and customer relations and adversely impacted our financial results" and that the company expects the impact "to adversely affect our future financial performance." For a vendor that secures the security stack, any breach is disproportionately damaging, a brand risk unique to this business. This is partly priced (it explains some of the multiple discount), but a future incident would be an asymmetric downside event.

**Microsoft bundling.** The dominant structural competitive risk and the most likely driver of net-retention compression, discussed above.

**Growth deceleration and thin retention.** NRR at 106-107% and revenue growth heading toward ~9-10% leave little margin for error. If the AI/NHI narrative does not convert, the stock is a low-double-digit grower with a ~25x multiple, vulnerable to derating.

**Governance and management churn.** Dual-class structure gives directors, officers, and affiliates 32% of voting power, with co-founders McKinnon and Kerrest retaining outsized control. The high-vote structure sunsets April 12, 2027, after which control normalizes — a positive on the horizon. More immediately concerning is a dense cluster of Item 5.02 management-departure 8-Ks (Dec 2025, March 11, March 23, April 22, 2026) and a Transition and Separation Agreement dated April 21, 2026 with a senior officer (Larissa Schwartz, historically legal leadership), consistent with substantial Form 4 selling in May-June 2026. Combined with the February 2025 go-to-market re-specialization, this signals meaningful organizational churn during the transition to a profit-focused operating model.

**Goodwill concentration.** The $5.49B Auth0 goodwill could trigger impairment if CIAM underperforms.

**Non-risks.** The June 2026 convertible maturity is a non-event (cash-settled, fully covered). No material legal proceedings as of April 30, 2026, and no material changes to risk factors in the Q1 FY27 10-Q.

We score risk factors 5.5. The breach-recurrence tail and the management-churn cluster are the genuine idiosyncratic risks not fully priced; most other risks (Microsoft, cyclicality, goodwill) are well-understood and at least partly embedded in the discounted multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and modestly constructive. The put/call open-interest ratio sits at 0.37, up slightly from 0.32 but still well below 1.0, indicating call-skewed positioning with no elevated put demand or hedging fear ahead of catalysts. Price was flat ($92.24) across the two snapshots. There is no options-market divergence to flag against our fundamental view — positioning is consistent with a stock the market views as fairly valued rather than one priced for a near-term shock.

### Insider Signal

Insider activity is informational noise rather than a directional signal. The June 2026 Form 4 cluster is overwhelmingly RSU vesting plus automatic tax-withholding (code F) and option exercises (code M). The standout open-market sales — CFO Tighe (~$7.6M) and departing officer Schwartz (multiple $100K-$1M+ sales) — are diversification/departure-driven, not bearish conviction. There is no open-market insider buying, so there is no conviction-buy signal to lean on either. We weight management quality at 5.5: smart, well-timed buyback execution and demonstrated comp-performance alignment (CEO comp-actually-paid went negative ~$21M in FY23 when the stock fell), offset by the absence of insider buying, the dual-class structure, and the unusual density of executive departures.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$130-150):** The agentic-AI / NHI thesis converts. AI security bookings translate into reaccelerating subscription revenue, net retention inflects back above 110%, and RPO growth flows through to recognized revenue. The valuation-allowance release delivers a headline EPS boost and a lower run-rate tax rate, the buyback compounds per-share metrics, and the multiple re-rates from ~25x toward the high-20s on a faster growth trajectory. The dual-class sunset in April 2027 removes a governance discount. With FCF margins at ~30% and growth reaccelerating to the mid-teens, the stock re-rates meaningfully from depressed sentiment (TSR index 33 vs. peers 256).

**Base case (~$100-110):** Growth stabilizes in the low double digits, the AI narrative provides incremental but not transformational bookings, FCF margins hold near 30%, and the buyback supports modest per-share growth. The stock compounds with earnings at roughly 25x forward, plus the optionality of the VA release. Modest upside from current ~$92.

**Bear case (~$70-75, ~20-24% downside):** Growth continues decelerating toward high single digits, net retention slips below 106%, Microsoft bundling accelerates the erosion, and the AI/NHI opportunity is captured by hyperscalers giving equivalent features away. A breach recurrence, a goodwill impairment on Auth0, or evidence that management churn is destabilizing execution compounds the downside. The multiple compresses toward 18-20x on a sub-10% grower.

The risk/reward from ~$92 is roughly balanced: meaningful upside if AI converts, comparable downside if growth keeps fading. That symmetry, combined with the unproven nature of the swing variable, supports a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next two earnings prints (Q2 FY27 in late summer 2026, Q3 FY27 in late 2026) are the decisive tests of whether AI/NHI bookings convert to reaccelerating revenue and retention. The April 2027 dual-class sunset and any valuation-allowance release are secondary catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Revenue growth reaccelerates above ~13% YoY or net retention recovers above 110% — direct evidence the AI/NHI TAM expansion is converting.
- Valuation-allowance release announced alongside continued operating-margin expansion, providing both an EPS catalyst and a structurally lower tax rate.
- Open-market insider buying by the CEO or CFO, signaling conviction at current levels (currently absent).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Revenue growth falls below ~8% YoY with net retention slipping below 105%, confirming structural saturation and Microsoft displacement.
- A new material cybersecurity incident, given the disproportionate brand damage to an identity vendor.
- A goodwill impairment on Auth0 or evidence that the management-departure cluster is disrupting go-to-market execution (e.g., a bookings or guidance miss attributed to sales-org turnover).

**Reassessment point:** Q2 FY27 earnings. The HOLD has now been issued three consecutive times; the bar for continuing it is whether the AI/NHI narrative shows up in the numbers. If two more prints pass without revenue reacceleration, the stock is a sub-10% grower at ~25x and the rating should move toward the lower end of the range.

## Conclusion

Okta is a higher-quality business today than its lagging share price reflects. The GAAP profitability inflection is real and recent, FCF margins of ~30% are excellent, the balance sheet is a fortress, and management has pivoted to shareholder returns with well-timed buybacks. The Q1 FY27 beat-and-raise, diverging from a same-day SentinelOne miss, indicates Okta-specific operational strength. These factors anchor a strong financial-health score and an improving overall trajectory (5.7 → 5.9).

The investment case turns on growth, and growth is decelerating with net retention flat near 106-107%. The agentic-AI / non-human-identity opportunity is a credible and genuinely differentiated TAM expander — management says it drove the bulk of Q4 bookings momentum and RPO is growing faster than revenue — but it has not yet converted to reaccelerating subscription growth. Until it does, OKTA is a profitable, cash-generative software franchise growing in the low double digits at ~25x forward earnings, with structural Microsoft bundling pressure, recurring breach-overhang risk, and an unusual cluster of executive departures during its operating-model transition. The valuation is fair, not cheap, and the risk/reward is balanced.

We rate OKTA a **HOLD** (growth_hold), overall score 5.9. We are watching the next two prints for evidence that AI/NHI bookings flow through to the top line. A revenue reacceleration above ~13% or net retention back above 110% would move this to a BUY; continued deceleration below 8% with slipping retention would move it toward AVOID.
