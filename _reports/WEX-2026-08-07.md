## Executive Summary

WEX has re-rated hard. The stock is up 51.3% from its June 18 low of $125.29 and 41.4% from the $134.48 basis of our last review seven weeks ago, and now sits 1.2% below its 52-week high. The proximate cause was the July 22 Q2 print: revenue $753.5M (+14.2%), adjusted diluted EPS $5.35 versus $5.10 consensus, and full-year adjusted EPS guidance raised to $19.68-20.08 from $18.95-19.55. Four consecutive beats, consensus revised up 3.7-3.9% over 90 days, and Credit Agreement leverage down to 2.9x from 3.1x.

The company itself tells you not to extrapolate the quarter. Exhibit 2 of the release quantifies the year-over-year price-per-gallon effect at +$63.8M of revenue and +$35.2M of adjusted net income, which is 18.9% of the quarter's $186.5M adjusted net income. Average U.S. retail fuel was $4.70/gal versus $3.28. Strip the disclosed fuel and FX effects and Q2 revenue grew 4.2%, adjusted EPS roughly 9.8%. Gallons fell 0.8%, transactions rose 0.1%, and Corporate Payments purchase volume fell 3.6%. Guidance assumes $3.91/gal for the full year — implying roughly $3.66/gal in H2 against Q2's $4.70 — and management guides below the $21.40 that annualizing Q2 would produce.

What makes the stock interesting at $190 is the arithmetic of the capital structure rather than the fuel windfall. At 9.6x guided adjusted EPS and 9.0x FY2027 consensus, with $940M of remaining repurchase authorization that would retire 14.5% of the share count at current prices and which guidance explicitly excludes (35.0M diluted shares assumed, no incremental buybacks), per-share compounding of mid-to-high teens requires no multiple expansion. The CFO committed on July 22 to directing "the vast majority of adjusted free cash flow to share repurchases." The board that authorized the program on May 14 includes four new directors seated the same day as the annual meeting following a cooperation agreement with Impactive Capital, whose ~5% stake now sits on the board. That is a specific, monitorable catalyst, which is what a single-digit multiple requires before it can be treated as opportunity rather than a warning. We move to ACCUMULATE, staged rather than full, because the entry is poor and the fuel dependency is genuine.

## Company Overview

WEX operates three segments. **Mobility** ($422.4M Q2 revenue, 56%) provides fleet payment processing and software to more than 600,000 customers across North American, Over-the-Road, and International books. Economics are a net payment processing rate (a percentage of fuel dollars purchased on WEX cards, less discounts and network fees) plus late fees and freight-invoice factoring. WEX funds the entire receivable, paying merchants in roughly ten days and collecting from cardholders in roughly thirty.

**Benefits** ($206.0M, 27%) administers HSAs, FSAs, HRAs and COBRA on SaaS platforms with integrated cards. Average SaaS accounts were 21.7 million. The structural asset here is that WEX Inc. is an IRS-designated non-bank HSA custodian while WEX Bank, a Utah industrial bank, supplies the depository. Of approximately $5.2B of custodial HSA cash at June 30, $4.758B now sits on WEX Bank's balance sheet at a weighted-average deposit cost of **0.11%** against a 4.93% HSA yield. Only $477.1M remains at unaffiliated banks.

**Corporate Payments** ($125.1M, 17%) issues virtual and physical commercial cards through Embedded Payments (including online travel) and Direct-to-Corporate AP automation, earning a net interchange rate on purchase volume (0.53% in Q2).

The bank charter is the differentiating asset: it funds receivables with $6.55B of deposits and $1.53B of FHLB advances rather than wholesale corporate debt, and it captures the custodial spread rather than paying it away as a program fee to third-party depositories.

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 baseline was weak.** Revenue $2,660.8M (+1.2%), segment adjusted operating income $1,095.9M (−5.7%), adjusted net income $578.0M (−8.4%), adjusted EPS $16.10 (+5.4%). Dollar earnings shrank while per-share earnings grew because roughly 13% of shares were retired, funded by $550M of 6.500% notes due 2033 and a $450M Term Loan B-3, deployed through a March 2025 Dutch auction. H1 2025 repurchases totaled 5.1 million shares for $801.7M including excise tax and fees, an implied all-in cost near $157/share, which looks accretive against today's $190.165.

**2026 has inflected, and the composition matters.** Q2: revenue $753.5M (+14.2%), operating income $203.2M (+29.6%), GAAP diluted EPS $3.11 (+57.1%), adjusted diluted EPS $5.35 (+35.4%), adjusted operating margin 39.6% (+280bp), total volume $68.9B (+15.7%). Mobility's segment adjusted operating margin rose 450bp to 43.2% because, as the 10-Q states, fuel price is accretive when most costs are unaffected by it.

Removing the disclosed fuel and FX contributions:

|---|---|---|
| Q2 revenue growth | +14.2% | +4.2% |
| Q2 adjusted EPS growth | +35.4% | ~+9.8% |
| H1 revenue growth | ~+13% | +4.8% |
| H1 adjusted net income growth | ~+21% | +8.8% |

Underlying unit activity is flat. The five-quarter gallon series (3,625.4 / 3,639.8 / 3,496.5 / 3,428.3 / 3,595.9 million) shows no trend. Mobility's net payment processing rate fell to 1.23% from 1.31%, which is mechanical: cents-per-gallon components and tiered rebates do not scale with price. Corporate Payments purchase volume has fallen from $23.18B in Q3 2025 to $19.76B.

**Credit is deteriorating, and guidance requires it to stop.** Consolidated provision for credit losses was $33.0M in Q2 (vs $21.5M) and $62.3M in H1 (vs $37.4M, +67%); H1 charge-offs $67.9M vs $49.4M. Mobility credit losses ran 17.4bp in H1 and 16.1bp in Q2 against a Q3 guide of 8-13bp and a full-year guide of 12-17bp. Meeting Q3 requires a material sequential improvement. The counterweight: allowance rose only to $93.2M from $84.3M while gross receivables grew far faster, so coverage fell from roughly 2.44% to 1.92%. The delinquency table supports the benign reading, with 99% of trade receivables under 30 days past due versus 98% at year-end, consistent with receivable growth being price rather than new credit exposure. The Q3 print settles it.

**Cash flow requires reading the right measure.** GAAP operating cash flow was −$408.4M in H1 (vs −$217.0M) because receivables grew from $3,362.6M to $4,761.0M on the fuel spike, with the offsetting funding appearing in financing activities. Adjusted free cash flow was $219.0M in Q2 and $268.5M in H1, a presentation that adds back $1,638.8M of H1 net funding activity and deducts $81.0M of capex. For a deposit-funded lender this is legitimate, but it is a non-GAAP measure that should be judged over full years. Annualizing H1 gives roughly $537M against a $6.48B market cap, an 8.3% adjusted FCF yield, offered as a sensitivity rather than a forecast given the working-capital swings embedded in it.

**Capital structure.** Total assets $16,498.8M, liabilities $15,154.3M, equity $1,344.6M. Corporate debt, the figure relevant to equity value since bank funding is receivable-matched, totals $3,607.5M: $798.8M Term A-1, $1,367.5M Term B-2 due April 2028, $444.4M Term B-3 due March 2032, $446.8M drawn revolver, and $550.0M of 6.500% notes. Roughly $3.06B is floating. Weighted-average cost on Credit Agreement borrowings is 5.4%. Corporate Cash is only $123.3M. Enterprise value is therefore approximately $6,482M + $3,607M − $123M = **$10.0B**.

Company-reported Credit Agreement leverage is 2.9x against a 4.75x maximum and a 3.00x minimum interest-coverage covenant, in compliance. Backing EBITDA out of the 2.9x ratio implies roughly $1.20B and **EV/EBITDA near 8.3x**, approximate since the covenant definitions of net debt and EBITDA were not reproduced.

Three balance-sheet items warrant weight. Goodwill of $3,022.6M plus $1,054.0M of other intangibles against $1,344.6M of book equity leaves tangible equity around **negative $2.73B**. WEX Bank's total capital ratio fell from 15.94% at December 31 to **13.48%** at June 30 as risk-weighted assets grew roughly 41% (approximately $5.10B to $7.18B, derived from disclosed 8% threshold dollars) while total capital grew 19%. FHLB capacity is nearly exhausted: $1,532.0M drawn against $118.3M remaining, leaving the revolver ($1,108.6M undrawn) and discount window ($221.9M) as the marginal sources.

Standard distress screens return an Altman Z near 0.55 and debt/equity above 10x. Both are artifacts of counting $6.55B of deposits and $1.53B of FHLB advances as liabilities against receivables and securities in current assets; Altman was calibrated on manufacturers. The direction of high financial leverage and thin tangible equity is real; the absolute values are not interpretable.

## Growth Analysis

Guided FY2026: revenue $2.86-2.90B (+7.5-9.0%), adjusted net income $689-703M (+19-22%), adjusted EPS $19.68-20.08 (+22-25%). With diluted shares guided at 35.0M against roughly 35.9M in FY2025, only about 2.5 points of the ~23% adjusted EPS growth is share-count driven, a change in character from FY2025, when buybacks were the entire source. Most of the improvement is operating leverage on fuel-driven revenue.

Underneath, the business is a mid-single-digit revenue grower. Three forward items are genuine and unpriced in the current volume data:

**The bp program.** WEX became co-branded issuer for BP Products North America's Commercial Fleet Card program in July 2025, signed an asset purchase agreement with the incumbent third-party issuer in October, advanced $58.6M as a fair-market-value premium, converted the portfolio onto WEX's platform in Q1 2026, and paid a further $23.0M in Q2 for receivable balances. The premium is now a customer-relationship intangible amortized over the 10-year bp contract term. This is a new-logo win in a segment with otherwise flat volume. Its size was not disclosed, which weakens the inference that legacy gallons are shrinking, but total gallons fell 0.8% in a quarter that should have carried bp incrementally.

**Freight capacity.** Mobility factoring fee revenue rose 34% to $18.9M on higher average invoice sizes from increased spot rates, attributed to decreased carrier and driver supply. After a multi-year freight recession, tightening capacity is a forward positive that has not yet appeared in gallons or Over-the-Road transactions.

**Benefits custody internalization.** Moving HSA cash onto WEX Bank converts a program fee into a 4.93%-versus-0.11% spread. The accounting cost is that Benefits account servicing revenue fell 2.8% while other revenue rose 20.3%, which a revenue-line screen would misread as deterioration. Average custodial cash grew 11.1%. Benefits was, however, the only segment whose adjusted operating income declined in Q2 (−1.6%, margin −300bp) on "increased nonrecurring operating costs" that the company neither sizes nor describes.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current year $20.03 (+3.7% in 30 days, +3.8% in 90), next year $21.09 (+3.9%/+3.8%). This is ordinary upward drift following a beat, not the >30% revision velocity that signals a systematic underestimation of an inflection. Growth here is durable mid-single-digit organic with a commodity kicker and a large buyback lever, not a secular acceleration.

The long-run structural question is electrification. Mobility's percentage-of-gallon economics do not translate cleanly to electricity, where WEX has no closed-loop merchant position comparable to fuel; FY2025 included a $9.9M EV-related write-off. This is a decade-scale risk, not a 2027 one, but it caps the terminal multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is stable-to-slightly-negative with one genuinely durable structural asset.

**Mobility.** The economics are levered to fuel price while the take rate is inversely levered, with revenue winning. Competitive pressure shows in flat-to-declining gallons across five quarters despite the bp conversion, and in the 10-Q's reference to "the net impact of pricing initiatives" as a partial offset to rate compression, which reads as pushing price to defend the rate. Whether the legacy book is losing share or simply riding a freight cycle is unresolved; the factoring commentary about contracting carrier and driver supply supports the cyclical reading, and the bp portfolio's undisclosed size prevents a conclusion.

**Benefits.** Deliberate vertical integration is the story. Capturing a 4.93% asset yield against 0.11% deposit cost beats collecting a program fee from a third-party bank, but it concentrates duration and rate risk on WEX's own balance sheet: of $5,061.4M of amortized cost in the securities book, $2,770.3M matures beyond ten years and only $110.7M within one year, with $81.0M of gross unrealized losses across 831 underwater positions and AOCI at −$192.5M. SaaS account growth of 2.2% is not a share-gain signal.

**Corporate Payments.** Volume is falling and rate is the offset. Q2 segment adjusted operating income rose $8.9M on $6.8M of revenue growth, with $6.5M of the improvement coming from the credit provision swinging from +$6.4M to −$0.1M on "lower collection risk." The net interchange rate rose to 0.53% from 0.48% "substantially due to an increase in network incentives" — volume-based rebates from the networks rather than pricing to customers. The reported 46.7% margin overstates operating momentum. The alternative reading is that network incentive arrangements are contractual and recurring and the provision reversal reflects a genuinely de-risked book after the 2024 travel-customer renegotiation, in which case both drivers persist. Guidance implicitly requires the "travel volumes timing between the first half and second half" to reverse.

The moat that survives scrutiny is the WEX Bank charter: deposit funding at 0.11% for HSA cash, self-funded receivables, and captured custodial spread are not replicable by a non-bank processor. Closed-loop, gallon-level fuel data and owned issuing technology in Corporate Payments are secondary but real.

Read the receivables-concentration disclosure carefully: no individual customer represented 10% or more of outstanding receivables at June 30 or December 31. That establishes receivables concentration only. The FY2025 10-K separately disclosed that a large travel customer renegotiated its Corporate Payments contract in Q2 2024, materially reducing segment purchase volume. Revenue concentration in online travel is real and should not be inferred away.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel price is the largest single earnings variable and cuts both ways.** WEX quantifies it: $35.2M of adjusted net income in one quarter from a $1.42/gal move. Because guidance already assumes $3.91/gal, the risk is not that the guide is wrong on fuel but that fuel falls *below* $3.66/gal in H2. Separately, the 10-Q flags European fuel price spreads as a distinct exposure that hurt Q1 and reverted in Q2, and guidance excludes any future impact from them.

The 10-Q attributes the Q2 fuel spike to "high volatility and rapid increase in the price of fuel as a result of the war in the Middle East." Whether the current oil regime supports or undercuts the guided H2 fuel path cannot be established from the provided sources; the skew on the fuel assumption is therefore not independently assessable here. [unverified — no current oil-price macro data in sources] The offset is imperfect regardless: high fuel simultaneously raises Mobility credit losses (17.4bp H1 versus a 12-17bp full-year guide) and inflates receivables, which inflates bank risk-weighted assets.

**The bank-capital constraint is the least-appreciated risk.** WEX Bank total capital fell from 15.94% to 13.48% as RWA grew ~41% on fuel-inflated receivables. The bank remains comfortably well capitalized (13.48% versus a 10.00% threshold; Tier 1 leverage 8.93% versus 5.00%), but sustained high fuel requires capital to be downstreamed from parent to bank, a claim that competes directly with the $940M repurchase authorization when Corporate Cash is $123.3M and FHLB capacity is $118.3M. This is the mechanism by which the fuel tailwind partially cancels the buyback catalyst. If fuel normalizes as guided, RWA contracts and the ratio recovers without an injection, so the two upside drivers are partly mutually exclusive, which caps the bull case.

**The FDIC consent order of September 20, 2023 remains open.** The $650 thousand civil money penalty assessed December 2024 was paid and the company states no material effect. The live risk is not cost but constraint: an open order can limit new activities, bank-to-parent dividends, or charter flexibility. Three years without escalation suggests remediation is progressing.

**Rates are two-sided.** Roughly $3.06B of corporate debt is floating and reprices immediately; the $5.06B custodial securities book is long-dated and reprices slowly. A 100bp move in short rates changes interest expense by roughly $30.6M pre-tax annually before any custodial-yield offset arrives. Benefits interest income was $58.5M in Q2 on $5.23B of average custodial cash; financing interest expense was $52.2M. A 100bp decline in reinvestment yields across the whole custodial book would be roughly $52M of pre-tax revenue at current balances, but the long-dated portfolio means repricing is slow and partial. [unverified — no current FOMC-path or rate-expectation data in sources]

**Other items:** interchange regulation is listed prominently in the forward-looking factors and Corporate Payments' 0.53% net interchange rate is the direct exposure; International Mobility carries approximately $93.5M of allocated goodwill that received a quantitative impairment test in FY2025; refinancing calendar risk concentrates in April 2028; and the newly added forward-looking factors include activism costs and AI adoption. The 10-Q states no material legal proceedings and internal controls were effective at June 30.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 30.0% sits well below 40.2% trailing realized volatility (spread −10.3%) at the 6th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in contango (30.0% at 1m rising to 35.9% at 6m). Put/call open interest fell from 1.40 to 0.63 alongside the 41.7% price move since June 24, unwinding bearish positioning into the rally. The 1-month skew of 5.0% is described as elevated put demand and the nearest expiry prices a ±4.9% move; 3-month and 6-month skew readings are negative. Short interest is 9.5% of float at 3.8 days to cover, moderate and likely reduced by the squeeze.

Two takeaways. Cheap near-term vol against a business with quantified commodity beta and a Q3 print that must validate an 8-13bp credit-loss guide makes long optionality inexpensive relative to the event set, which favors defined-risk exposure over outright stock for entry timing. And the collapse in put/call open interest means the rally has consumed the hedging cushion; there is less short-covering fuel left and less protective structure beneath the price than in June.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($280, +47%)

Fuel stays above the guided $3.66/gal H2 assumption and refining stays tight, driving a fifth consecutive beat and FY2026 adjusted EPS above $20.50. Mobility credit losses revert toward the 12-13bp guide, validating management's read that receivable growth is price rather than new exposure. Corporate Payments travel volume recovers in H2 as guidance implies, and freight capacity tightening converts into Over-the-Road gallon growth. The board directs $700-900M to repurchases over 18 months, retiring 11-14% of shares; FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $24-25 on roughly 30-31M shares. The multiple re-rates to 11.5x as the market accepts that per-share compounding is durable and activist-supervised. $280.

### Bear Case ($152, −20%)

Retail fuel falls below $3.40/gal as the Middle East disruption eases. The $35.2M-per-quarter fuel contribution reverses; FY2027 adjusted EPS falls to $17-18 despite buybacks. Mobility credit losses stay at 17-20bp rather than improving to 8-13bp, confirming that allowance coverage falling from 2.44% to 1.92% was reserve-build lag rather than benign price-driven receivable growth. Benefits' unquantified "nonrecurring" costs prove recurring; Corporate Payments volume does not recover. The multiple holds at 8.5x on lower earnings as the April 2028 maturity approaches with $3.06B of floating debt in a higher-for-longer environment. $152.

### What decides it

The Q3 print on three axes: realized fuel versus the $3.90/gal assumption, Mobility credit losses against the 8-13bp guide, and the pace of repurchases against the $940M authorization and the bank's 13.48% capital ratio. Two of the three are largely outside management's control.

The base-to-bear ratio of 0.78 is why this is ACCUMULATE rather than BUY; the entry at 1.2% below the 52-week high, after a 51% run off the June low, does not provide an adequate margin of safety for a full position.

**Staged entry plan.** Establish a partial position now; add on weakness toward **$170-175** (roughly 8.6x FY2026 guidance) or on the Q3 print delivering credit losses inside the 8-13bp guide with disclosed repurchases above $200M for the quarter.

**Upgrade to BUY if:** (1) Q3 discloses cumulative repurchases above $300M since May with WEX Bank total capital stabilizing at or above 13.5%, demonstrating that the buyback and the bank's RWA growth are not mutually exclusive; or (2) Mobility credit losses print inside 8-13bp while realized fuel exceeds $4.10/gal, showing the fuel tailwind is not being consumed by credit; or (3) the stock trades below $170 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $21.

The April AVOID and the subsequent decline to $125.29 were vindicated; the June HOLD at $134.48 understated the setup, since the stock has since risen 41.4% on a genuine beat-and-raise. Today's upgrade to ACCUMULATE rests on new evidence rather than momentum: guidance raised, leverage down to 2.9x, an explicit capital-return commitment, and four new directors seated. ## Conclusion

WEX at $190.165 is a levered, flat-volume payments business with one genuine structural asset, the bank charter that funds its own receivables and captures a 4.93%-versus-0.11% custodial spread, trading at 9.6x guided adjusted earnings and roughly 11.3x once stock compensation is charged against them. The bear case is straightforward and quantified by the company: strip the $35.2M quarterly fuel contribution and revenue grows 4.2%, gallons decline 0.8%, and Corporate Payments volume falls 3.6%.

The bull case rests on arithmetic rather than narrative. A $940M authorization retires 14.5% of the share count at current prices, guidance explicitly excludes it, the CFO has committed to it, and the board that authorized it now includes the activist whose 5% stake forced the change. Mid-to-high-teens per-share compounding requires only that the multiple hold. The constraint on that arithmetic is WEX Bank's capital ratio, which fell 246 basis points in six months as fuel-inflated receivables grew risk-weighted assets 41%: the same tailwind that produced the beat also produced a competing claim on capital.
