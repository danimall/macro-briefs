**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

FirstCash operates the largest pawn store network in the world (3,334 locations spanning the U.S., Mexico/Latin America, and, since August 2025, the U.K.) plus American First Finance (AFF), a point-of-sale lease-to-own and installment-finance business. The pawn franchise is the engine: it generates over 90% of net revenue and segment pre-tax income, carries no credit-loss allowance because loans are over-collateralized (≈75% gold jewelry), and behaves counter-cyclically, with demand strengthening in periods of elevated inflation and rates. Same-store pawn receivables, the leading indicator of future fee revenue, accelerated in Q1 2026 to +19% in the U.S. (an eleventh consecutive double-digit quarter), +30% in Latin America, and +29% in the U.K. Management raised 2026 guidance across all three pawn segments.

The fundamentals are clean. FY2025 delivered revenue of $3.66B (+8%), net income of $330.4M (+28%), and diluted EPS of $7.42 (+29%); Q1 2026 was a record at $1.05B revenue (+26%) and $2.43 diluted EPS (+30%). The company beat consensus in each of the last four quarters and is deleveraging — net-debt/adjusted-EBITDA fell to 2.9x (2.6x pro-forma) even after funding the ~$392M H&T acquisition entirely with debt. Operating cash flow runs near $600M annually, with a FCF margin around 32%.

The tension is price. At ~$220, FCFS trades at roughly 20x forward current-year consensus EPS ($10.92) and 27.6x trailing — about twice the consumer-finance peer median P/E of 10.3x and above the closest pure-play comp, EZCorp, at 16.8x. Consensus targets (mean $241.40, median $250) imply only ~9-13% upside, while a credible bear case (peso reversal, gold pullback, continued AFF shrinkage, and multiple compression) points to ~20% downside. These are record earnings, not trough earnings, and the estimate-revision velocity (+4.3% for the current year over 90 days) is too modest to signal the kind of earnings acceleration that would justify chasing the stock here. We rate FCFS a HOLD (growth bias), with a clear path to upgrade on a pullback toward the mid-$180s or an acceleration in revision velocity.

## Company Overview

FirstCash runs four reportable lines:

- **U.S. Pawn** — 1,207 stores across 29 states plus D.C., concentrated (~80%) in high-growth Texas, the Southeast, and the Mountain West. This is the legacy core.
- **Latin America Pawn** — 1,838 large-format, full-service stores (1,733 in Mexico, plus Guatemala, El Salvador, Colombia) serving a heavily unbanked population. ~24% of consolidated revenue.
- **U.K. Pawn** — 289 stores acquired via H&T in August 2025; the U.K.'s largest pawnbroker, ~98-99% gold-jewelry collateral.
- **Retail POS Payment Solutions / AFF** — lease-to-own, retail installment sales, and bank-partner installment loans across ~16,600 merchant doors.

The pawn model is structurally resilient. Loans are non-recourse and collateralized; when a borrower forfeits, the collateral becomes inventory at the loan principal and resells at roughly 42% (U.S.) / 35% (LatAm) margins. There is no credit-loss reserve on pawn receivables. The industry is fragmented (12,000-14,000 U.S. shops, 8,000-9,000 in Mexico), with licensing barriers to entry and only two U.S.-listed public operators (FirstCash and EZCorp), which makes scale, proprietary loan-management technology, and owned real estate (458 properties) durable advantages in a roll-up business.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $3.66B (+8%) with net income of $330.4M (+28%), diluted EPS of $7.42 (+29%), and adjusted EPS of $8.76 (+31%). Adjusted EBITDA reached $698.4M (+25%). Q1 2026 set records: revenue $1.05B (+26%), net income $108M (+29%), diluted EPS $2.43 (+30%). Trailing-twelve-month revenue is approximately $3.9B with adjusted EBITDA of $746M.

**Segment dynamics.** The growth is pawn-driven. Same-store pawn receivables (the forward indicator for fee income) are running at +19% (U.S.), +30% (LatAm, local currency), and +29% (U.K.). U.S. retail margins hit a record 44% in Q1 2026, aided by elevated gold prices lifting scrap-jewelry sales. AFF moves the other way: leased-merchandise income fell ~27% in 2025 after furniture partners Conn's and American Freight went bankrupt in late 2024, and management guides AFF revenue down mid-to-high single digits and net revenue down 15-20% in 2026. AFF segment margin actually improved (pre-tax margin 13%→19%) on lower loss provisions even as the top line shrank, and the business is diversifying away from furniture (48% of volume in 2023 to 23% in 2025).

**Returns and cash flow.** Net margin is ~20%, FCF margin ~32%, ROE ~14.5% (adjusted ~19%), ROA ~6% (adjusted ~8%). FY2025 operating cash flow was $585.9M (+9%), funding earning-asset growth, store and real-estate acquisitions, dividends ($70.9M; $1.68/share annualized), and buybacks (a $200M program completed, a new $150M authorized in October 2025 with ~$100M remaining).

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt rose to ~$2.24B principal after H&T was funded entirely with the credit facility, with no equity issued. Net-debt/adjusted-EBITDA stood at 2.9x at 3/31/26 (2.6x including pro-forma acquisition EBITDA) and is declining. The current ratio is 4.6x and equity is $2.3B. Interest expense is guided up 10-15% in 2026 given the partly floating-rate debt. Goodwill is $2.02B, up from $1.79B on the H&T deal.

A note on the Altman Z-Score of 1.7, which screens as "distress": this is a false signal for FirstCash. The model penalizes the acquisition-driven goodwill, the asset-heavy debt-funded roll-up structure, and the low EBIT/assets ratio that results from pawn-receivable funding flowing through investing activities. Positive net income, operating cash flow exceeding net income, and a 4.6x current ratio describe a healthy, cash-generative business. The leverage and goodwill are real considerations; bankruptcy risk is not the issue the Z-Score implies.

## Growth Analysis

The central growth case is the eleven-quarter streak of double-digit U.S. same-store pawn receivable growth, accelerating rather than fading, joined by +30% LatAm and +29% U.K. comps. Because pawn receivables lead fee revenue by roughly a quarter or two, this points to durable forward momentum and is the reason management raised 2026 guidance across all three pawn segments.

Several mechanisms underpin the durability. Pawn demand strengthens in periods of consumer financial stress; the current environment of elevated inflation and rates is supportive, and the deep-value retail side is relatively insulated from tariffs. In Latin America, management attributes part of the strength to reverse migration from the U.S. and reduced remittances pushing more Mexican consumers toward local pawn credit. The H&T integration is tracking ahead of plan: Q1 2026 U.K. segment income was $39M at a 39% margin, and 2026 U.K. segment-income guidance was raised to $125-135M, with POS-system migration to FirstCash's platform expected to unlock further synergies.

The counterweight is AFF, which is shrinking and being managed down rather than grown. The headline consolidated growth is entirely pawn-driven. Investors should also normalize for currency: in Q1 2026, LatAm grew 40% in USD terms versus 23% in local currency, so roughly half of the reported LatAm growth was FX-driven, a tailwind that can reverse.

On forward estimates, consensus current-year EPS is $10.92 (next year $12.42), with the revision trend positive but moderate: +0.2% over 30 days and +4.3% over 90 days for the current year. This is steady upward drift, not the >30% revision velocity that flags an earnings acceleration. The four-quarter beat record (14.3%, 3.1%, 14.1%, 5.0%) supports confidence in the consensus path but does not, on its own, argue that estimates are systematically too low.

## Valuation Assessment

FCFS trades at 27.6x trailing EPS and roughly 20x forward current-year consensus ($10.92), or ~17.7x next-year ($12.42). On adjusted EPS (FY2025 $8.76), the multiple is lower, but the company is being valued on record, not trough, earnings, so cycle-adjusted normalization does not lower the effective multiple here.

Against consumer-finance peers, the premium is wide: the peer median P/E is 10.3x (ALLY 10.3, SLM 6.2, ENVA 13.3, BFH 7.5, EZPW 16.8). Most of these are different businesses (auto, student, card lending); the most relevant comparable is pawn peer EZCorp at 16.8x trailing. FCFS at ~20x forward sits above EZCorp's trailing multiple, a premium that is defensible given FirstCash's larger scale, faster same-store growth, UK platform, and superior return profile. PEG is roughly 1.0-1.3 on mid-teens to high-teens EPS growth — reasonable, not cheap.

The issue is margin of safety. Consensus targets are mean $241.40, median $250, high $252, low $220 — implying ~9-13% upside to the median and essentially no downside cushion at the low. A bear case combining a peso reversal, a gold-price pullback, continued AFF erosion, and modest multiple compression toward EZCorp's 16.8x would take EPS estimates and the multiple down together, plausibly to the $170-180 range, or roughly 18-23% below current levels. That asymmetry (single-digit-to-low-teens upside against ~20% downside) is closer to balanced than to the asymmetric setup a high-conviction long requires.

The valuation score reflects a quality compounder at a full multiple with limited identifiable mispricing. A pullback toward the mid-$180s (~15-16x forward) on unchanged fundamentals would shift the risk/reward decisively in the buyer's favor.

## Competitive Landscape

FirstCash's competitive position is strong and improving on the pawn side. It is the largest pawn operator globally in a fragmented industry where licensing creates entry barriers and where only two U.S.-listed public operators exist. Scale advantages, proprietary POS and loan-management technology, owned real estate, and a demonstrated ability to source and integrate acquisitions are durable in a roll-up sector. The H&T deal extended this moat to the U.K. and is integrating ahead of plan. Management is concentrating capital on the pawn segments (now 90%+ of profit), which de-emphasizes the lower-moat, faster-changing AFF/POS-finance arena where it competes against BNPL providers, fintechs, virtual LTO operators, and banks.

The competitive vulnerability is concentrated in AFF, where merchant concentration (the Conn's/American Freight bankruptcies) and a more disrupted competitive set have driven the segment into decline. Management is diversifying the AFF merchant base toward automotive and elective medical, but this remains the structurally weaker side of the franchise.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the primary reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY, because several of these exposures are binary or concentrated and are not fully reflected at a ~20x forward multiple.

- **Regulatory / rate-cap risk (existential).** The entire pawn model depends on pawn-fee and interest regulations (4-25%/month). A spreading 36% APR cap at the federal or state level is the single largest structural threat. FirstCash recorded an $11.0M CFPB litigation settlement in 2025 ($9.4M after tax) tied to alleged Military Lending Act violations and launched an MLA-compliant military product, resolving the most prominent overhang while signaling ongoing regulatory friction. The CFPB's current operational turmoil reduces near-term enforcement risk while raising long-term uncertainty.

- **Bank-partner ("rent-a-bank") risk.** AFF's bank-originated loans (45% of AFF volume, ~6% of consolidated net revenue) rely on a Utah-chartered bank; the program agreement expires August 2028 and is terminable on certain events. A successful "true lender"/valid-when-made challenge could void loans or trigger usury/licensing liability. A new off-balance-sheet loan program (launched Q3 2025) shifts loans off the balance sheet but creates a credit-exposure liability ($13.8M reserve) on 90+ day delinquencies.

- **Mexico FX and political risk.** LatAm (mostly Mexico) is ~24% of revenue and a major profit driver. Each one-point move in USD/MXN swings EPS by ~$0.10-0.12. FY2025 carried a 5% unfavorable rate headwind; Q1 2026 enjoyed a 14% favorable swing, so a meaningful share of recent reported LatAm growth is FX-driven and can reverse. Mexico's 2024 judicial reform, aggressive tax audits, minimum-wage/profit-sharing increases, and U.S.-Mexico trade tension add to the exposure.

- **Gold price dependence.** ~75% of pawn collateral and ~62-67% of inventory is gold jewelry. Current high prices are a tailwind (larger loans, strong scrap margins); a sharp decline would compress loan balances, scrap sales, and collateral coverage simultaneously.

- **Leverage and goodwill.** $2.24B debt (partly floating, interest +10-15% guided 2026) and $2.02B goodwill create refinancing and impairment risk if performance deteriorates. The May 2026 8-K disclosing a new material debt agreement is consistent with the disciplined growth/refinancing narrative but adds floating-rate sensitivity.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term (1-month) ATM implied volatility is 37.8% versus 28.6% trailing realized, a rich 9.2-point premium, and the term structure is in backwardation (37.8% near-term vs 32.6% six-month), which typically reflects an anticipated catalyst rather than a steady-state regime. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.1%. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.6%) and put/call OI is low at 0.16, so the positioning is not directionally bearish; the elevated, front-loaded vol and the recent jump (ATM IV rose 4.6 points to 37.8% in a single session) signal the market expects volatility around the next event window. Short interest is modest at 3.8% of float (3.9 days to cover), so squeeze dynamics are not a factor in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (toward $250+).** The pawn demand acceleration proves durable: eleven straight quarters of double-digit U.S. same-store receivable growth extends, LatAm and U.K. comps hold near 30%, and the H&T integration drives U.K. segment income above the raised $125-135M guide. Continued estimate beats push current-year EPS above $11 and next-year above $12.50, while deleveraging toward 2.0-2.5x and continued buybacks support a premium ~22-24x multiple. The stock re-rates toward the $250 median target and beyond.

**Bear Case (toward $170-180).** The peso reverses, removing the ~14% FX tailwind that flattered Q1; gold prices pull back, compressing loan sizes and scrap margins; AFF erosion accelerates beyond the guided 15-20% net-revenue decline; and a regulatory development (rate-cap momentum or a true-lender challenge) introduces a discrete overhang. Estimates flatten and the multiple compresses toward EZCorp's 16.8x, implying roughly 18-23% downside.

**Base Case (toward $235-245).** Pawn momentum moderates but stays healthy (low-double-digit same-store growth), AFF continues its managed decline, FX normalizes, and EPS lands near consensus ($10.92 CY / $12.42 NY). The stock holds a ~19-20x forward multiple and drifts toward the consensus mean of $241, roughly 9% above current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected late July 2026), which will test whether same-store pawn receivable growth holds its double-digit pace and whether AFF stabilizes. The June 18 options expiry and the macro rate path are secondary near-term markers.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A pullback to the mid-$180s or lower (~15-16x forward CY EPS) on unchanged or improving fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.
2. Revision velocity accelerating above ~15-20% over 90 days (currently +4.3%), signaling the consensus is materially underestimating the trajectory.
3. Concrete de-risking on the regulatory front — favorable resolution of the bank-partner/true-lender exposure or clarity that rate-cap momentum has stalled — while same-store receivable growth stays double-digit.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. U.S. same-store pawn receivable growth decelerating to single digits for two consecutive quarters.
2. A material adverse regulatory development (a 36% APR cap advancing federally or in a major operating state, or an unfavorable true-lender ruling affecting the AFF bank-partner program).
3. Net-debt/adjusted-EBITDA rising back above ~3.2x, or a goodwill impairment, signaling the roll-up economics are deteriorating.

## Conclusion

FirstCash is a high-quality, counter-cyclical compounder running one of the most consistent same-store growth records in consumer finance, with a collateral-backed model that carries no pawn credit risk and a U.K. acquisition integrating ahead of plan. The business deserves a premium to its disparate consumer-finance peers. The constraint is price: at ~20x forward current-year EPS, roughly twice the peer median and above the closest pawn comp, with only ~9-13% upside to consensus targets and a bear case that exceeds 20% downside, the risk/reward is closer to balanced than asymmetric. The estimate-revision velocity is positive but well short of earnings acceleration, the options term structure is pricing near-term stress, and a cluster of binary regulatory, FX, and gold-price exposures is not fully reflected in the multiple.

These are concrete, falsifiable reasons that prevent a BUY at current levels: the absence of asymmetric upside (mid-single-digit-to-low-teens upside versus ~20% downside), the absence of a near-term earnings-acceleration catalyst (modest revisions, next print not until late July), and identifiable binary risks (rate-cap, bank-partner true-lender, peso/gold). We rate FCFS a **HOLD** with a growth bias, and would move to BUY on a pullback toward the mid-$180s or a clear step-up in revision velocity.
