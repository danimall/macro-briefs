**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

OFG Bancorp is the parent of Oriental Bank, one of three major banks in a consolidated Puerto Rico market. It runs the most profitability-efficient balance sheet of the three, generating a 5.45% net interest margin (Q2 FY2026), 1.78% ROA, and 16.4% return on tangible common equity — metrics roughly 200 basis points ahead of mainland regional peers on NIM and well above the group on returns. The engine is a high-yield asset mix (auto 32% of loans, consumer 8%, commercial 43%) funded by sticky, low-cost core deposits at a 1.40% weighted cost. The company beat consensus by ~18% in Q2 (EPS $1.39), raised its second-half NIM guidance to 5.25%–5.35%, and consensus current-year EPS estimates have moved up 7.6% over 90 days to $5.06.

The investment debate concerns whether the current price adequately compensates for the risks. The stock has appreciated ~17% year-to-date and now trades at roughly 1.7x tangible book value ($30.14/share) and ~10.3x trailing/forward earnings. That is a discount to the regional-bank peer median of ~12.9x on a raw P/E basis, but the comparison is misleading given OFG's superior returns; the more relevant framing is that the market has already awarded OFG a fair premium for its 16.4% ROTCE. The remaining upside to the analyst mean target of $55 is ~6%, and to the high target of $58 is ~11%. Options markets are pricing a ±10% move with near-term implied volatility of 58.4% against 25.9% realized; elevated put skew (34.8%) and term-structure backwardation signal the market is bracing for near-term stress even after the Q2 print.

We move OFG to a HOLD (growth_hold), consistent with the prior report's rating, at an overall score of 5.95. The fundamentals confirm the underlying franchise is strong and earnings momentum is positive, which argues against downgrading. But the risk/reward at $52 is roughly balanced: the durable high-ROTCE-plus-buyback story is largely reflected in the multiple, while the offsets (single-market concentration, structurally high charge-offs, an asset-sensitive NIM facing Fed cuts, and imported Commercial US credit risk) cap the asymmetry. This is a quality compounder we would add on weakness rather than chase at a fresh high.

## Company Overview

OFG Bancorp is a San Juan–based financial holding company operating through Oriental Bank across 42 branches in Puerto Rico and 2 in the U.S. Virgin Islands, with roughly 2,181 employees and approximately $12 billion in total assets. The company reports three segments:

- **Banking** (~$27M of $54M Q1'26 net income): commercial, auto, mortgage, and consumer lending plus deposit gathering — the core franchise.
- **Wealth Management** ($4.1M): trust, brokerage, insurance, and a Cayman reinsurance captive, with ~$5.4B in assets under management/administration.
- **Treasury** ($22.8M): asset-liability management, the investment securities portfolio, and borrowings.

The strategic thrust is a transition to a "digital bank with personal connections," with retail (Libre, Elite) and commercial (My Biz) products designed to drive low-cost deposit growth. The company also uses Puerto Rico tax-advantaged international banking entities and a private equity fund vehicle under the PR Incentives Code.

The competitive advantage is a structurally very high net interest margin driven by the high-yield consumer/auto asset mix funded by low-cost core deposits. This produces best-in-class regional-bank profitability, but it embeds a corollary of elevated net charge-offs that defines the central risk-reward tension in the story.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Net income has compounded steadily: $166.2M (2022) → $181.9M (2023) → $198.2M (2024) → $205.1M (2025). Diluted EPS on the company-selected metric rose from $3.46 (2022) to $4.58 (2025). Momentum accelerated in FY2026: Q1'26 net income of $53.9M produced diluted EPS of $1.26 (+26% YoY), and Q2'26 EPS reached $1.39. The earnings-surprise record is strong: three beats in the last four quarters (Q2 +18.1%, Q1 +25.4%, Dec'25 +9.3%, with a single -1.6% miss in Sep'25), which supports confidence in the rising consensus estimates.

**Profitability and efficiency.** Q1'26 metrics were elite for a regional bank: NIM 5.36%, ROA 1.78%, ROTCE 16.43%, efficiency ratio 50.97%. Q2 NIM widened further to 5.45%. These returns are a direct function of the high-yield PR consumer/auto book rather than any pricing edge available to mainland banks.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets were $12.05B at Q1'26, down from $12.47B at year-end 2025, driven by a $500M government deposit relocation (discussed below), not franchise erosion. Loans held for investment stood at $8.24B (commercial 43.1%, auto 32.0%, mortgage 16.7%, consumer 8.2%). Deposits were $9.85B at a 1.40% cost. The $2.79B securities book is almost entirely agency MBS, carrying minimal credit risk.

**AOCI cleanliness.** AOCI stood at only −$33.6M against $1.37B of equity, with roughly $44.7M of unrecognized HTM losses off balance sheet. Many mainland regionals still carry large unrealized AFS marks from the 2022 rate move; OFG's short-duration agency portfolio limited that damage, protecting tangible book value and capital flexibility.

**Capital.** Ratios are strong: CET1 13.75%, total risk-based capital 15.01%, leverage 10.88%, TCE/TA 10.66%. Book value is $32.35/share and tangible book value $30.14/share, placing the stock at ~1.7x TBV.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend was raised 17% to $0.35 in January 2026 (current yield ~2.6%). Two active buybacks ($100M April 2025, $200M January 2026) drove $44.5M of repurchases in Q1'26 at ~$40.51, with ~$193.6M of authorization remaining. Diluted share count has fallen from 48.4M (2022) to ~42.3M, roughly a 12% reduction. Operating cash flow was $217.7M and free cash flow ~$199M in 2025, comfortably funding the return program.

Financial health is the strongest dimension here (7.5): the combination of elite returns, fortress capital, clean AOCI, and self-funded shareholder returns is durable and reliable, and it is the single most predictive dimension in our framework.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth setup is positive. Consensus EPS estimates have risen across every window: current-year up 7.6% (to $5.06), next-year up 6.5% (to $5.01), and next-quarter up 5.6% over 90 days. Q2 revenue grew 4.5% YoY to $190.3M. Management raised its second-half FY2026 NIM guidance to 5.25%–5.35% from an initial full-year 5.10%–5.20%, citing deposit growth and the government-deposit relocation. Expenses are guided to ~$380–385M.

Several qualifiers temper the growth durability. First, the estimate-revision magnitude (+7.6% current year over 90 days) is meaningful but below the +30% threshold that flags a genuine earnings inflection; this is steady compounding, not a re-rating catalyst. Second, the raised NIM guidance still points to a lower second-half margin than Q2's 5.45%, reflecting the bank's asset sensitivity into a Fed-easing environment (+200bp → +$32.6M NII; −200bp → −$31.2M). Third, revenue growth in the mid-single digits is respectable but not the kind of top-line acceleration that would justify multiple expansion from here. The Commercial US book ($872M of purchased mainland participations) is the deliberate diversification growth vehicle, but it is also where the largest recent charge-offs originated, so growth in that segment imports credit risk outside management's home-market underwriting edge.

The Puerto Rico macro backdrop is currently a genuine tailwind: the PR Economic Activity Index was 127.2 in January 2026 (+0.3% YoY), employment sits near ~951,600 jobs, and federal reconstruction funding plus pharma/manufacturing onshoring support loan demand and credit quality. That tailwind is cyclically and politically dependent on continued U.S. federal transfers, which management explicitly flags as at risk from federal spending cuts and tariffs.

Growth prospects score 6.0: directionally positive and confirmed by rising estimates, but with a thinner separation than the profitability metrics and a margin trajectory that has likely peaked for the cycle.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.12, OFG trades at ~10.3x trailing/forward EPS and ~1.7x tangible book value. The raw P/E is below the regional-bank peer median of 12.9x (MBIN 12.1x, HTH 14.4x, NWBI 15.6x, BANR 11.3x, FCF 12.9x), which superficially reads as cheap. That comparison is misleading. OFG earns a 16.4% ROTCE against a peer group generating materially lower returns, so a 1.7x TBV multiple is the appropriate premium expression, and it is already awarded. The dividend yield of 2.6% is slightly below the peer median of 2.9%.

Our framework treats a low headline multiple as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal unless there is an independent, identifiable catalyst for re-rating. Here, there is no such catalyst. The earnings-inflection exception (which would justify looking past a compressed multiple on cycle-adjusted power) does not fully apply: estimate revisions are positive but modest (+7.6%, not >30%), revenue growth is mid-single-digit (not >40%), and the NIM has peaked for this cycle rather than inflecting upward. This is a high-quality bank fairly priced for its quality, not a mispricing.

Analyst targets frame the remaining opportunity: mean $55 (+5.5%), median $56.5 (+8.4%), high $58 (+11.3%), low $49 (−6.0%). The stock currently sits above the low target and roughly 5–8% below the central targets. On a stock trading near a 52-week high with a peaked margin and no re-rating catalyst, we do not treat consensus targets as floors; that framing applies to cheap inflection names, and OFG is neither cheap on quality-adjusted terms nor at a genuine trough. The valuation trajectory in prior reports (7.5 → 6.5) reflected the same recognition that the re-rating from the low-$40s has largely closed the discount.

We score valuation 5.0. The business is worth its multiple, but there is no evidence the market is systematically mispricing it, and the asymmetry has narrowed after the ~17% year-to-date advance.

## Competitive Landscape

OFG operates in a rational three-bank Puerto Rico oligopoly with Popular (BPOP) and First BanCorp (FBP). It is the smallest of the three but the most profitability-efficient on ROA and ROTCE. The consolidated market structure supports strong margins and disciplined pricing.

The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than expanding. The digital-bank transition and low-cost deposit gathering are executing well (deposit cost of 1.40% is a genuine funding advantage), and the wealth-management pivot — including converting the $500M government deposit into fee-generating AUM — deepens client relationships while reducing public-fund concentration. Against that, the 5.4% NIM is an outlier that reflects a higher-risk lending posture, not a defensible pricing moat; competitors could pursue similar high-yield lending if credit conditions warranted. The Commercial US expansion diversifies away from single-market dependence, but it puts OFG into mainland middle-market competition where it lacks a structural edge.

Competitive position scores 6.0, in line with the prior report's downward drift from 7.2. The franchise is solid and share is stable in a favorable oligopoly, but the trajectory is one of maintaining a strong position rather than widening a moat. Our framework flags competitive_position as a low-reliability recent signal, so we weight it modestly and lean on financial health for confirmation.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific and material, which is why this dimension does not benefit from OFG's otherwise clean profile:

1. **Puerto Rico geographic concentration** — the dominant risk. Substantially all credit exposure sits on the island, vulnerable to hurricanes (Maria, Fiona), earthquakes, and the PR government's fiscal fragility (PROMESA/PREPA). The current federal-reconstruction tailwind is politically dependent and could reverse with U.S. spending cuts or tariffs, explicitly flagged in the filings including reference to the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act."

2. **Structurally high net charge-offs** — the flip side of the high NIM. NCOs run ~1.05% of average loans (auto 1.52%, consumer 4.40%), well above mainland peers. A PR downturn would pressure the consumer/auto book hardest, and this tradeoff is precisely why the stock has not re-rated higher despite the profitability.

3. **Deposit concentration** — uninsured deposits are ~52% of total ($5.12B), and PR government deposits were $1.26B at Q1'26 (down from $1.68B). The wind-down of the $1.2B government deposit is a modest ongoing funding headwind, partially offset by the fee-income conversion.

4. **Asset-sensitive NIM facing Fed cuts** — a −200bp rate move reduces NII by ~$31.2M, and management has already guided the second-half NIM down from Q2's peak. This is the most predictable near-term earnings headwind.

5. **Imported Commercial US credit risk** — the $872M mainland participation book generated the quarter's largest charge-off ($3.9M) and carries concentrated substandard/doubtful credits, plus a $44.1M PR telecom loan on non-accrual.

None of these is systemic, but several are concentrated and some are binary (a hurricane season, a shift in federal policy). This warrants a risk score of 5.0: the risks are largely known and partly priced, but the concentration and macro-dependency mean they are not the kind of low-magnitude, asymmetric-recovery profile that earns a high score.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing a near-term caution signal that diverges from the benign fundamental read. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 58.4% against 25.9% realized (a 32.4-point premium), the put/call skew is elevated at 34.8%, and the term structure is in backwardation (58.4% near-term vs 29.3% at three and six months), the classic signature of the market pricing imminent, resolvable stress. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.1%. The 6-month skew, by contrast, flips negative (−8.9%), suggesting the fear is concentrated in the near term rather than reflecting a structural bearish view. The prior options snapshot showed an anomalous 798.3% ATM IV that has since normalized to 58.4%, so the current read should be treated as the credible one. For a fundamental holder, this argues for patience on entry timing: the near-term vol premium suggests better risk-adjusted entry points may emerge, consistent with our preference to add on weakness.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** OFG is a durable high-ROTCE compounder in a rational PR banking oligopoly. It sustains a 16.4% ROTCE and 5.4%+ NIM, funded by 1.40%-cost sticky deposits, while returning capital aggressively (12% share-count reduction since 2022, 17% dividend hike, ~$194M of buyback remaining). Clean AOCI protects tangible book. Positive estimate revisions (+7.6% current year) and a three-of-four beat record support continued earnings growth. The favorable PR macro — reconstruction funding, onshoring, employment near record — extends the credit-quality runway. If the local economy holds and the bank keeps compounding TBV at mid-teens returns while buying back stock, the multiple can support a move toward the $58 high target (~11% upside) plus the dividend.

**Bear case.** The 5.4% NIM has peaked and compresses as the Fed cuts, given the bank's asset sensitivity (−$31.2M NII on a −200bp move). Structurally high consumer/auto charge-offs (~1.05% blended, 4.4% consumer) turn from manageable to painful if the PR economy weakens or federal transfers are cut. The Commercial US book keeps generating out-of-market credit surprises. At 1.7x TBV and near a 52-week high, the market has fully paid for the quality, so any credit deterioration or NIM disappointment derates the stock toward tangible book (~$30, roughly −42%) or the analyst low of $49 (−6%) in a milder scenario.

The most-likely path sits between these: continued mid-single-digit earnings growth, gradual NIM normalization off the Q2 peak, and steady buyback support, producing a total return roughly in line with the dividend plus modest multiple stability — a base case around $55 (~6% upside plus 2.6% yield).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is one of continued compounding rather than re-rating, with catalysts in the earnings cadence (next report ties to the Q3 FY2026 print) and buyback execution.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $40s (approaching ~1.4x TBV), restoring the asymmetry that existed earlier in 2026.
- Evidence that NIM is holding above 5.35% into 2027 despite Fed cuts (deposit-cost discipline offsetting asset repricing), which would materially raise normalized earnings power.
- A step-up in estimate-revision velocity (current-year consensus rising above ~$5.30) combined with stable-to-improving charge-off trends.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Consumer/auto NCOs breaking materially above ~1.3% of average loans, signaling PR consumer-credit deterioration.
- A concrete reversal in federal reconstruction funding or a PR fiscal shock that impairs the macro tailwind.
- NIM compressing below ~5.0% faster than guided, cutting into the profitability that justifies the TBV premium.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings release, with the second-half NIM guidance as the key metric to validate. Monitor Fed rate-path signals given the bank's asset sensitivity.

## Conclusion

OFG is a genuinely high-quality regional bank: elite profitability, fortress capital, clean AOCI, disciplined shareholder returns, and positive earnings momentum in a favorable local economy. The prior score trajectory (7.0 → 6.9 → 6.2) reflected a franchise that continued to execute even as the valuation caught up to the fundamentals, and the latest data confirms rather than reverses that read: the Q2 beat and raised NIM guidance are constructive, but the stock's ~17% year-to-date advance to a fresh high has closed most of the discount that justified the earlier BUY calls.

At $52 and ~1.7x tangible book, the market is paying a fair premium for the 16.4% ROTCE. The remaining upside to central analyst targets is modest (~6–8%), the near-term options market is pricing outsized stress, and the offsets — a peaking asset-sensitive NIM, structurally high charge-offs, single-market concentration, and imported Commercial US credit risk — cap the asymmetry. We rate OFG a HOLD with an overall score of 5.95, and would look to accumulate on weakness toward the mid-$40s where the risk/reward turns clearly favorable again.
