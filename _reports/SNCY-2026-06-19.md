**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sun Country Airlines is a Minneapolis-based hybrid carrier running a three-segment model (low-cost scheduled passenger service, contracted charter, and Amazon Air cargo) that has historically given it steadier cash flow than pure-play ultra-low-cost competitors. The financial profile is genuinely sound: FY2025 free cash flow of $84M, long-term debt reduced from $251M to $189M, cash up to $145M, and a buyback that has shrunk the diluted share count roughly 10% since 2022. That balance sheet discipline stands out against distressed ULCC peers.

The investment case is dominated by a single corporate-action fact. The company profile states that as of May 13, 2026, Sun Country "operates as a subsidiary of Allegiant Travel Company." The price history corroborates this: the stock printed $16.17 on May 12, 2026, then registered $16.17 with zero volume on May 13, 14, and 15. A name trading at a frozen price on no volume is the signature of a closed or near-closed acquisition where the public float has been absorbed. The analyst price-target set ($17–$20, mean $19.2) and the standalone consensus EPS estimates pre-date or do not reflect final deal economics, making them stale as a forward guide.

Stripping out the deal, the standalone fundamentals are deteriorating. Operating income peaked in 2023 at $127.5M and has fallen two years running to $100.6M; the Q1 2026 EPS miss of -19.7% ($0.58 vs. $0.72) and the collapse in consensus revisions (-51% current-year over 90 days, -186.5% current-quarter) confirm negative earnings momentum. There is no residual catalyst for a now-controlled subsidiary trading flat, and the standalone trajectory does not support owning the shares. We rate SNCY AVOID.

## Company Overview

Sun Country operates a three-segment model:

- **Scheduled passenger** — A leisure- and visiting-friends-and-relatives network concentrated on MSP, built around peak-day, demand-driven flying rather than a fixed daily schedule, with ancillary revenue (bags, seats) as a core ULCC-style economic lever.
- **Charter** — Contracted, higher-margin flying for collegiate and professional sports teams, casinos, government and military customers.
- **Cargo** — A crew-maintenance-insurance (CMI) contract operating Boeing 737-800 freighters for Amazon Air, providing fee-based, fuel-pass-through revenue largely insulated from passenger demand cycles.

The fleet as of December 31, 2025 was 70 Boeing 737-NG aircraft (47 passenger, 20 cargo, 3 leased to third parties). The company employs roughly 3,276 people and is led by CEO Jude Bricker. The reported financial structure is organized into two segments — Passenger and Cargo — with charter activity included within the broader operating model.

Allegiant is the closest strategic comparable — leisure-focused, peak-demand flying, ancillary-heavy — and a combination concentrates two niche leisure carriers. The trading data is consistent with the deal having closed or having reached a point where the public market is no longer pricing the equity independently.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $894M (2022) → $1,050M (2023) → $1,076M (2024) → $1,127M (2025). Cumulative growth of 26% over the period, but the year-over-year rate decelerated from +17.4% (2023) to +2.5% (2024) to +4.7% (2025), pointing to a maturing network and softer leisure fare environment.

**Profitability — the central problem:**

| Metric ($M) | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating income | 55.7 | 127.5 | 106.0 | 100.6 |
| Operating margin | 6.2% | 12.1% | 9.9% | 8.9% |
| Net income | 17.7 | 72.2 | 52.9 | 52.8 |
| Net margin | 2.0% | 6.9% | 4.9% | 4.7% |

Operating income peaked in 2023 and has declined for two consecutive years while revenue rose, a clear signal that unit cost inflation (labor, maintenance, airport costs) has outpaced revenue gains. Net income has been flat at roughly $53M for two years. The 2023 figure reflected the post-COVID travel surge; the ~9% operating margin run-rate is the realistic new baseline.

**Cash flow and balance sheet — the genuine strength:**
- FY2025 operating cash flow $157.1M, capex $73.1M, free cash flow $84.0M.
- Long-term debt cut from $251M (2024) to $189M (2025); cash rose from $83M to $145M; stockholders' equity grew to $625M.
- Total assets $1.68B, dominated by $1.34B of aircraft PP&E.
- Current ratio of ~0.82 ($368M current assets vs. $451M current liabilities), typical for airlines given large deferred-ticket (air traffic liability) balances.
- Interest coverage of ~2.7x ($100.6M operating income vs. $36.9M interest expense), with interest expense falling as debt is repaid.

The balance sheet read is favorable on its own terms. The income statement read is that earnings power has plateaued and the most recent data points are negative.

**Earnings momentum:** The Q1 2026 quarter missed by 19.7% ($0.58 actual vs. $0.72 estimate), breaking a pattern of in-line-to-beat prints. Consensus estimates have fallen across the board: current-quarter to -$0.19 (from +$0.19 ninety days ago), current-year to $0.77 (from $1.58, a 51% cut), and next-year to $1.79 (from $2.23). This is a broad, accelerating downward revision cycle, not a single soft quarter.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is negative on the metric that matters most for sorting outcomes. Revenue growth has decelerated to mid-single digits, operating income has declined two years running, and the forward estimate revision trend is sharply negative, with the current quarter flipping to a loss estimate. Zacks flagged (April 30, 2026) that analysts expected a year-over-year earnings decline on higher revenue for the March 2026 quarter, which the actual miss confirmed.

The structural growth story rests on the Amazon Air cargo relationship as a tie-in to e-commerce logistics expansion, plus charter stability. Those segments cushion cyclicality but have not delivered consolidated earnings growth; the consolidated result has been flat-to-down profit on rising revenue, the opposite of operating leverage. As a now-controlled subsidiary, the standalone growth narrative is also superseded by Allegiant's strategy and any integration plan, which is not visible in this data set.

The +30% estimate-revision BUY signal that would favor an inflection thesis is absent, with revisions running negative — a warning, not a buy trigger.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation inputs are not usable as a standalone forward guide because of the corporate action. The shares are frozen at $16.17 on zero volume since May 12, and the analyst targets ($17 low, $20 high, $19.2 mean, $20 median) along with the consensus EPS path pre-date final deal terms. The forward P/E of 9.0x on a $1.79 next-year estimate looks optically cheap, but that estimate has been cut 20% over 90 days and the current year has been cut 51%, so the multiple is anchored to a falling denominator.

Peer comparison is of limited use here because the supplied "Industrials" comparable set (APOG, NPK, NX, LZ, VRRM) contains no airlines, so the median P/E of 29.0x is not a relevant benchmark for a leisure carrier. Against genuine airline peers, a single-digit forward P/E is normal for the sector, not a signal of mispricing.

A low multiple on deteriorating earnings, with no independent catalyst because the equity is controlled, is the textbook value-trap configuration: cheapness without a recovery mechanism. Cheapness alone does not earn a high valuation score, and here there is no identified reason the market is wrong about a stock that is effectively no longer trading. The valuation score of 4.5 reflects a stretched-to-fair situation on a falling earnings base with no asymmetric upside available to a public holder.

## Competitive Landscape

Sun Country occupies a defensible niche between ULCCs and network carriers. Its charter and Amazon cargo diversification produced steadier cash flow than pure ULCCs, several of which (Spirit most prominently) entered severe distress. Continued profitability and active deleveraging while ULCC peers struggled is a real relative strength.

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than improving. Two consecutive years of margin compression indicate the cost battleground (which management's own comp metrics center on, via Adjusted CASM) is moving against the company, at least partly because of sector-wide leisure-fare softness and labor/maintenance inflation. MSP concentration exposes the network to Delta's hub presence and regional shocks. FY2024 total shareholder return of 40.08 trailed the peer-group TSR of 58.56, indicating the market judged SNCY's relative positioning unfavorably even before the margin trend was fully evident. The competitive-position score of 5.5 reflects a sound model with a deteriorating near-term trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Corporate-action / liquidity risk (defining).** As a subsidiary of Allegiant trading at a frozen price on zero volume, public holders face minimal liquidity and no independent re-rating mechanism. The remaining "return" is whatever the deal terms specify, which this data set does not contain.
2. **Amazon customer concentration.** The cargo segment depends on a single CMI contract with Amazon Air. Non-renewal or unfavorable renegotiation would strand dedicated freighters and crews and remove the counter-cyclical revenue anchor. The contract term is the highest-priority unknown.
3. **Margin compression.** Two straight years of declining operating income on rising revenue signals structural cost pressure that the most recent quarter confirms is ongoing.
4. **Demand cyclicality and fuel.** A leisure-skewed scheduled segment is highly discretionary and fuel-sensitive; beta of 1.40 captures this.
5. **Negative estimate momentum.** Consensus has been cut for the current year and the current quarter has swung to a loss estimate; visibility is deteriorating.

The risk-factors score of 4.5 reflects the combination of an unpriced/illiquid corporate-action overhang and concrete negative earnings momentum, partly offset by a strong balance sheet that limits financial-distress risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The standalone bull case (now largely moot) would have rested on margin re-expansion as cost pressures normalized, durability of the Amazon cargo and charter cash flows, and a single-digit forward multiple re-rating toward the ~$19–20 analyst mean. Continued deleveraging and buybacks supported per-share value. In a deal context, the bull case is that the acquisition consideration sits at or above the current $16.17 print, in which case holders are made whole at deal terms.

### Bear Case
Earnings power has plateaued and is now contracting: operating income down two years running, a 19.7% Q1 2026 miss, and a 51% current-year estimate cut. As a controlled subsidiary trading flat at $16.17 on no volume, there is no independent catalyst to lift the shares and no liquidity to exit on favorable terms beyond deal mechanics. Cheapness on a falling earnings base with no recovery trigger is a value trap. Any residual standalone valuation argument is undermined by the absence of operating leverage and the negative revision trend.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: There is no actionable short catalyst. The dominant fact is a completed or near-completed acquisition by Allegiant, which removes downside-driving uncertainty rather than creating it. Earnings have deteriorated, but the equity is no longer trading on fundamentals.

**Timing window**: Not applicable. With the stock frozen on zero volume, there is no functioning market to express a short, and a closed deal typically caps both directions at deal consideration.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: 7.9% of float short, 5.5 days to cover, 3.57M shares — moderate and uncrowded. In a closed-deal context this position is more likely an unwinding arbitrage book than a directional bet.

**Borrow and options signal**: No options data; borrow dynamics are irrelevant on a non-trading line.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: There is no realizable downside for a public short because the price is pinned and liquidity is absent. The risk/reward does not exist in a tradable sense.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE** — The AVOID rating stems from the absence of investable edge as a controlled subsidiary with deteriorating standalone fundamentals, not from an active collapse. There is no specific catalyst, no liquidity, and no asymmetric setup to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID. The shares are effectively non-investable for a public holder given the Allegiant subsidiary status and the frozen, zero-volume price.

**Re-entry criteria**: We would revisit only if (a) the company were to re-emerge as an independently traded entity with restored liquidity and disclosed standalone economics, or (b) a confirmed merger consideration materially above the current $16.17 print were documented, creating an arbitrage spread worth capturing. Absent confirmed deal terms, neither condition can be evaluated from this data.

**Timeline**: The corporate action appears resolved as of mid-May 2026; the standalone earnings headwinds (margin compression, negative revisions) would persist 12+ months even in a counterfactual independent scenario. We do not expect a tradable, independent SNCY equity over the relevant horizon.

**Monitoring points**: Confirmation of final merger consideration and any cash-out terms; any SEC filing indicating a deregistration or going-private completion; and, only if relevance returns, the Amazon Air contract renewal date and the Adjusted CASM trajectory.

## Conclusion

Sun Country is a well-run niche carrier with a strong balance sheet (FCF $84M, debt cut to $189M, cash $145M) and a differentiated charter-plus-cargo model that has outlasted distressed ULCC peers. None of that is investable here. The company now operates as a subsidiary of Allegiant Travel Company, and the price is frozen at $16.17 on zero volume since May 12, 2026, the trading signature of a closed or near-closed acquisition with no remaining public-market upside mechanism. On a standalone basis, earnings have plateaued and turned lower (operating income down two straight years, a 19.7% Q1 2026 miss, and a 51% current-year estimate cut over 90 days), so even the counterfactual independent thesis is weakening. The single-digit forward multiple is a falling-denominator artifact, not an asymmetric opportunity. With no liquidity, no independent catalyst, and a deteriorating standalone trajectory, we rate SNCY **AVOID**.
