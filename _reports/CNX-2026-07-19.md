**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CNX Resources is a pure-play Appalachian natural gas producer that runs one of the most distinctive capital-return programs in the E&P universe: no dividend since 2016, and a buyback authorization of $2.37B remaining that exceeds 50% of the company's ~$4.7B market cap. The entire equity thesis rests on shrinking the share count through free-cash-flow-funded repurchases, with a heavily hedged production base providing downside protection in a soft gas-price environment. The mechanism works when free cash flow holds up; the same hedge book that protects the equity also caps upside and is currently a large cash drag: CNX paid $288M net in cash to settle derivatives in Q1 2026, a direct reduction in buyback capacity.

The reported financials are deeply misleading without adjustment. The swing from a $198M net loss in Q1'25 to a $348M profit in Q1'26 is almost entirely a mark-to-market derivative effect: a $226M unrealized gain this year against a $418M unrealized loss last year. The optically cheap ~4.6x trailing P/E is an artifact of these non-cash gains, not a durable earnings multiple. The operational reality is more modest and genuinely improving: production margin rose ~19% to $1.56/Mcfe, and sales including cash settlements grew to $500M from $441M. But the growth story is structurally constrained: management has held production flat in "maintenance mode" for roughly six years because of Appalachian takeaway limits, core SW PA Marcellus inventory has only an end-of-decade runway, and the growth lever (Deep Utica) is unproven at scale.

We maintain a HOLD. The setup is genuinely two-sided and lacks a near-term catalyst in either direction. Estimate revisions are broadly negative (next-quarter consensus down 31.5% over 90 days), which offsets the four-quarter beat streak; the valuation looks cheap only if you accept mark-to-market noise as recurring; and the AI/data-center demand tailwind that could re-rate the equity is, by management's own admission, "a few years out." This is a disciplined, well-run FCF-per-share compounder trading with a free option on Appalachian power demand, worth holding for patient capital, but not a name where the current evidence supports a confident directional call.

## Company Overview

CNX is the successor to CONSOL Energy, having spun off its coal business in November 2017. It operates in the Marcellus and Utica shales and coalbed methane (CBM) deposits across Pennsylvania, West Virginia, Ohio, and Virginia. Production is ~92% natural gas, making the equity a leveraged bet on Henry Hub and Appalachian basis.

The business runs across three segments. The Shale segment is the core (~143 Bcfe/quarter), driven by Southwest PA Marcellus with an emerging Deep Utica program, and includes the wholly-owned CNX Midstream gathering business. The CBM segment (~9 Bcf/quarter) is mature and declining but commands higher realized prices on higher-Btu gas; its production margin swung from -$0.21/Mcf in Q1'25 to +$0.76/Mcf in Q1'26. The "Other" segment houses a distinctive environmental-attributes business: sales of carbon/methane/renewable credits, Remediated Mine Gas, and the 45Z clean-fuel tax credit (~$30M/yr run-rate), a revenue stream few E&P peers have (~$15M in Q1'26, down from $23M a year earlier).

CNX's structural advantages are vertical integration (owning its midstream gathering, compression, and water infrastructure keeps Shale LOE near $0.11/Mcfe), the environmental-attributes optionality, and a low-decline, long-life inventory base. The offsetting reality is that the core inventory is running thin, with management indicating roughly 40,000–50,000 core SW PA Marcellus acres remaining and an end-of-decade runway, which makes the still-nascent Deep Utica the key determinant of whether the company has a growth future beyond the next several years.

## Financial Analysis

The single most important interpretive point is that CNX's GAAP revenue and net income are distorted by mark-to-market swings on a large hedge book. The reported ~99% net margins and headline "revenue growth" are derivative noise. The right lens is the company's non-GAAP "Sales including cash settlements" and production margin.

**Q1 2026 (most recent):**
- GAAP net income of $348M ($2.18 diluted EPS) versus a $198M net loss in Q1'25. The ~$546M swing is essentially the unrealized derivative reversal noted above: accounting, not operations.
- Operational reality: sales of gas/NGL/oil including cash settlements of $500M; production margin of $1.56/Mcfe (vs. $1.31), a real ~19% improvement.
- Realized natural gas price of $4.74/Mcf (up 29.5% YoY), but hedges cost $1.59/Mcf, netting ~$3.15. Cash settlement losses on hedges were $222M in Q1'26 (vs. $110M in Q1'25), the direct cost of downside protection in a rising-price environment.
- Operating cash flow of $278M against capex of $170M: positive free cash flow.
- Volumes of 152.4 Bcfe, up 3% YoY on Apex wells and 2025 TILs.

Full-year 2025 net income was ~$633M versus a ~$90M loss in 2024, driven by higher gas prices (~$2.99 vs. $1.98/Mcf) and Apex volumes.

**Balance sheet (3/31/26):** Total debt of $2.38B against equity of $4.63B and total assets of $9.13B. Net leverage sits well within the 3.50x covenant, with ~$1.3B revolver availability. Debt is largely fixed-rate (~$2.2B at 5.875%–7.375%), with only ~$181M variable, so the higher-for-longer rate path is a modest incremental headwind rather than an acute one. Cash is negligible ($3.7M), which is normal for CNX given its revolver-plus-FCF operating model. A standardized distress screen (e.g., Altman Z) will flag CNX in the distress zone, but that is a false positive reflecting capital intensity, not genuine stress: the company is covenant-compliant, FCF-positive, and liquid.

The company managed its maturities constructively in Q1 2026: it issued $500M of 5.875% notes due 2034 and retired $500M of 6.00% notes due 2029 (a $12M extinguishment loss), pushing out the maturity wall at a lower coupon. The $208M 2.25% convertible notes due May 2026 are being settled in stock (conversion price $12.84, deeply in-the-money at ~$33), conserving cash but adding roughly 16–26M shares to the diluted count, a quiet offset to buyback-driven share reduction worth flagging.

The defining financial story is capital allocation. With no dividend, 100% of return-of-capital flows through buybacks. The board raised the authorization by $2.0B in January 2026 to $2.37B remaining. CNX bought 4.2M shares in Q1'25 and 1.4M in Q1'26 at ~$37. Financial health is solid but not fortress-grade: the earnings quality is genuinely obscured by hedge accounting, and the hedge cash drag is a real constraint on the buyback engine that anchors the thesis.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest part of the CNX story, and it is structural rather than cyclical. Management has run the company in maintenance production for roughly six years, attributing it directly to takeaway constraints — the "unwillingness of regulators to allow additional pipelines." Without new egress or in-basin demand, volume growth is capped and in-basin gas remains subject to weak basis. The 2026 guidance confirms this posture: capex of $556–586M for essentially flat production of 605–620 Bcfe.

The core inventory picture reinforces the caution. SW PA Marcellus runway is only end-of-decade, and the growth lever, Deep Utica, is unproven at scale (only 3 TILs in Q1'26, ~5 laterals planned for 2026, with spacing tests ongoing at 1,300–1,500 ft). The AI/data-center power-demand thesis that could unlock a growth acceleration is real but, per management, "a few years out." CEO Shepard has been candid that in-basin power/AI projects are longer-lead, that there is "no reason to build those volumes yet," and that CNX would only ramp against long-term contracts tied to new power infrastructure, not spot pricing. Westbound/brownfield takeaway expansions to serve Midwest/Ohio data centers exist on paper but haven't been greenlit.

The estimate-revision data cuts against any growth optimism. Current-quarter consensus is down 15.9% over 90 days, next-quarter down 31.5%, current-year down 3.7%, and next-year down 4.1%. This is broadly negative earnings momentum, driven by weaker forward gas-price assumptions and the hedge drag. That negative revision trend materially outweighs the four-quarter earnings-beat streak, because the beats have been flattered by production-margin improvement and, in the GAAP figures, mark-to-market gains, while the forward path is being marked lower. Growth prospects score low as a result: the company is deliberately not chasing growth, and the one lever that could change that is years away and not yet contracted.

## Valuation Assessment

The ~4.6x trailing P/E is the first thing a screen sees, and it is misleading. That multiple is calculated on GAAP EPS inflated by non-cash derivative gains; on the company's underlying cash-earnings power it is materially higher. The peer set underscores how noisy the group's optical multiples are — CRC at -10.6x, MGY at 15.8x, MUR at 65.8x, CRK at 6.2x, CRGY at -11.1x — with a peer-median P/E of 15.8x. CNX's "discount" to that median is largely a function of which quarter's mark-to-market swing lands in the trailing window, not a clean statement of relative cheapness.

On forward consensus, the picture is more sober. Current-year EPS consensus is $2.91 and next-year is $4.09 (both revised down over the past 90 days), putting the stock at roughly 11x current-year and ~8x next-year — reasonable for a hedged, low-growth gas producer, but not the deep bargain the trailing figure implies. The analyst price-target range is $32 (low) to $48 (high), with a mean of $38 and median of $36; at $33.28, the stock sits below the mean but the analyst mix has been drifting more bearish (sell ratings rose from 2 to 3, strong-sell from 1 to 2 over the past three months), and Truist's July upgrade was only to Hold at a $35 PT.

Cheapness here should be treated as a caution flag, not a buy signal. In this sector specifically, low-multiple energy names have repeatedly sorted the wrong way: the discount reflects capped volume growth, hedge-suppressed upside, and inventory depletion rather than a mispricing the market has overlooked. There is no identified catalyst that forces a re-rating: the AI-demand option is years out, the hedge book caps participation in any gas rally through 2028, and forward estimates are falling. We do not see a specific reason the market is wrong at the current price. Valuation scores in the middle: the absolute forward multiple is undemanding and offers some support, but there is no asymmetric upside case, and the trailing "cheapness" is largely an accounting artifact.

## Competitive Landscape

CNX competes with larger Appalachian gas peers — EQT, Antero, Range, Coterra — but is smaller (~$4.7B cap) and more midstream-integrated. Its vertical integration is a genuine cost advantage (Shale LOE ~$0.11/Mcfe), and its environmental-attributes business (45Z credit, RMG, PA RECs) is a differentiator that supports an ultra-low-carbon-intensity gas branding few peers can match.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable rather than clearly improving. On the positive side, the integrated cost structure and hedge book let CNX weather weak-gas environments better than unhedged peers, and the environmental-attributes optionality is a real (if modest and politically exposed) edge. On the negative side, the structural takeaway constraint means CNX cannot grow share in a rising-demand environment the way a peer with more egress could, and its core inventory is depleting faster than the larger operators'. The AI/power-demand tailwind, if it materializes in-basin, would benefit CNX as a fuel supplier positioned "right behind" hyperscalers, but that is a demand-side option, not a business line, and it accrues to the whole basin, not to CNX uniquely. Net trajectory is stable with a differentiated-but-narrow moat, warranting a middling score.

## Risk Assessment

**Hedging is a double-edged sword.** CNX is ~80% hedged for 2027 (~$4/Mcf), heavily hedged for 2026 (346 Bcf at ~$2.74), and hedged at ~$3.25 for 2028. This protects downside and underpins Truist's equity-support thesis, but it caps participation in a gas rally and generated $222M in cash settlement losses in Q1'26 alone. The bull case and bear case are the same fact viewed from opposite gas-price assumptions.

**Structural volume cap.** Six years of maintenance production driven by pipeline/takeaway constraints means growth is capped absent new egress or in-basin demand, and in-basin gas is subject to weak basis.

**Inventory depletion.** Core SW PA Marcellus runway ends around end-of-decade; the Deep Utica growth lever is unproven at scale.

**Customer concentration.** In Q1'26, NRG Business Marketing ($156M) and DTE Energy Trading ($83M) each exceeded 10% of revenue, and the concentration shifts year-to-year (Citadel was the >10% customer in Q1'25).

**Legacy coal indemnity dependence.** CNX remains exposed to UMWA Coal Act and pension claims and relies on Core Natural Resources to indemnify it — a counterparty that has already once repudiated an indemnity obligation (CNX won partial summary judgment in Nov 2024). Not currently material, but a genuine tail risk.

**Environmental-attribute policy risk.** The 45Z credit and PA REC/AEC markets are exposed to Trump-administration policy shifts; management noted PA REC prices softened after the administration change.

**Commodity/impairment leverage.** With 92% gas exposure, a sustained price decline triggers write-downs and borrowing-base cuts. This is partly mitigated by the hedge book near-term but re-emerges beyond 2028.

Many of these risks are known and at least partially priced, which is why the risk score sits in the middle rather than the bottom. The idiosyncratic items the market may be underweighting are the customer-concentration reversal risk and the Core indemnity dependence; the more visible risks (hedge drag, volume cap) are broadly understood.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 34.8% is rich to 29.1% one-year historical vol, the term structure is in backwardation (34.8% near vs. 31.9% long), and put/call skew of 4.7% shows elevated put demand, consistent with an implied move of ±8.9% into the nearest expiry. ATM IV has risen sharply from 26.8% to 34.8% since mid-June while the price barely moved, and the P/C OI ratio collapsed from 1.92 to 0.89. Rising near-term vol on a flat price signals the market is bracing for a catalyst, most plausibly the next earnings print and summer gas-price direction. Combined with 16.4% short interest (7.7 days to cover), the positioning is defensively bearish, reinforcing the case for patience rather than a directional bet. This confirms our HOLD stance rather than challenging it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$44–48):** Summer heat and any firming in the forward gas curve lift realized prices; the hedge cash drag eases into 2027–2028 as legacy low-price hedges roll off; the buyback compounds FCF-per-share aggressively at depressed prices; and the Appalachian AI/data-center demand option begins to convert into contracted volume with greenlit takeaway expansions, providing a growth catalyst the market isn't pricing. Deep Utica delineation proves the inventory runway extends well beyond end-of-decade. In this scenario the equity re-rates toward the high analyst target.

**Bear Case (~$26–28):** Gas prices soften into the hedged years (where CNX has sold most of its upside), production stays capped by takeaway, the AI-demand thesis stays perpetually "a few years out," and falling estimate momentum continues. The buyback partially offsets weak fundamentals but is itself constrained by hedge settlement cash outflows, and the convertible-note dilution quietly counteracts the share-count reduction. Estimates keep drifting lower and the stock retests the 52-week low near $27.72.

**Base Case (~$35):** The most likely outcome is range-bound trading. The hedge book supports the downside, the buyback provides a floor and slow per-share compounding, but capped volumes, depleting core inventory, negative near-term revisions, and the absence of a dated catalyst prevent a sustained re-rating. This aligns with Truist's $35 target and the analyst median of $36.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A durable turn in estimate-revision momentum — current-year and next-year consensus stabilizing and turning positive over a 60–90 day window, reversing the current -3.7%/-4.1% trend.
- Concrete progress on the AI/power-demand option: a signed long-term supply contract tied to a new in-basin power plant/data center, or a greenlit takeaway expansion — evidence the growth lever is converting from optionality to bookable volume.
- Deep Utica results (more than the current 3 TILs) demonstrating repeatable, economic laterals that meaningfully extend the inventory runway beyond end-of-decade.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sustained decline in forward gas prices that pushes realized prices below the hedge floor and forces a borrowing-base cut or impairment.
- A covenant-relevant deterioration in net leverage (approaching the 3.50x limit) or a meaningful pullback in buyback pace, signaling the FCF-per-share engine is stalling.
- Confirmation that the Core Natural Resources indemnity obligations are being repudiated in a way that puts material legacy coal liabilities back on CNX's balance sheet.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July/early August 2026) is the natural checkpoint — watch the realized-vs-hedge price spread, cash settlement outflows, buyback pace, Deep Utica commentary, and any update on in-basin demand contracting. The options market's backwardation flags this print as the near-term catalyst.

## Conclusion

CNX is a well-run, disciplined FCF-per-share compounding vehicle: hedged to protect the downside, buying back stock aggressively with no dividend, and holding a free option on Appalachian AI/power demand. The trajectory of our scoring has drifted higher over the past several months (5.9 in April to a peak of 6.7 in May, easing to 6.1 by mid-June) on genuine improvements in financial resilience and risk framing. This read pulls back modestly, not because the price fell (the decline from the low-$40s to ~$33 on largely intact fundamentals is not itself a thesis-negative), but because the forward estimate momentum has turned clearly negative, the growth lever remains years out and uncontracted, and the "cheap" trailing multiple is a hedge-accounting artifact rather than a genuine mispricing.

The setup is honestly two-sided. The hedge book, the buyback floor, and the value-manager endorsement (Longleaf) support the downside; the capped volumes, depleting core inventory, negative revisions, rich near-term implied vol, and 16.4% short interest cap the upside and remove the case for a confident bullish call. We maintain **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale: this is a name to own for patient capital comfortable with a levered, hedged gas-price bet and a long-dated demand option, but the current evidence does not support a stronger directional stance in either direction. We would turn constructive on a durable revision turn or a signed in-basin demand contract, and cautious on a gas-price break through the hedge floor.
