**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Chemours is a global performance-chemicals company operating three franchises with sharply divergent trajectories: a booming refrigerant business (Thermal & Specialized Solutions) that is currently carrying the entire company, a titanium-dioxide pigment business (Titanium Technologies) in a deep cyclical trough, and a fluoropolymers business (Advanced Performance Materials) that is both cyclically weak and facing the most acute regulatory tail risk in the chemicals industry. The operating story — regulatory-mandated HFC phasedown driving Opteon volumes and legacy Freon pricing — is real, but it is happening on top of a balance sheet and litigation profile that dominate the investment case.

The central fact is the capital structure. Total debt of roughly $4.18B sits against book equity of just $216M, down from $572M at the start of 2025, after a FY2025 net loss of $386M. That produces a debt-to-equity ratio above 19x and an Altman Z-Score of approximately 1.1, squarely in the distress zone. Layered on top are $491M of accrued litigation reserves and $617M of environmental reserves, with management's own disclosures indicating losses could exceed the environmental accrual by roughly $630M and PFAS exposure that is repeatedly described as "not estimable." The equity is a thin sliver that behaves like a levered call option on PFAS liabilities being successfully sequenced into manageable settlements under the DuPont/Corteva 50/50 cost-share, of which only about $1.3B of the $4B headroom remains.

We rate CC AVOID. The near-term stock path will be driven by legal and permit outcomes — the New Jersey Judicial Consent Order approval, the finalization of the ~$450M federal PFAS settlement announced June 24, 2026, and the Washington Works NPDES permit renewal — rather than by operating fundamentals. An active SEC and SDNY investigation into 2024 accounting/ethics violations by the prior CEO, CFO, and Controller adds a governance scar that a distressed balance sheet cannot afford. The score trajectory (4.2 → 4.4 → 4.3) has been essentially flat across three reports; nothing in the current data supports upgrading the base case, and the June federal settlement and continued negative estimate revisions argue for reaffirming the cautious view.

## Company Overview

Chemours was spun off from DuPont on July 1, 2015. It reports three primary segments (plus a small "Other" segment):

- **Thermal & Specialized Solutions (TSS)** — refrigerants (Opteon low-GWP and legacy Freon), propellants, and blowing agents. Q1'26 sales of $568M (+22% YoY) with Adjusted EBITDA of $190M at a 33% margin. Growth is driven by the AIM Act HFC phasedown in the US and EU F-Gas regulation favoring higher-value Opteon, plus a spike in Freon pricing (+67% YoY to $162M revenue) as legacy refrigerant supply tightens.
- **Titanium Technologies (TT)** — Ti-Pure TiO₂ white pigment. Q1'26 sales of $559M (-6%), with Adjusted EBITDA collapsing to $18M (3% margin) from $50M (8%) a year earlier on weak volumes, unfavorable mix, and higher input costs including sulfur inflation.
- **Advanced Performance Materials (APM)** — fluoropolymers (Nafion, PFA) for semiconductors, hydrogen, and autos. Q1'26 sales of $243M (-17%), with Adjusted EBITDA cratering to $5M (2% margin) from $32M (11%), hurt by a Washington Works outage, the SPS Capstone exit, and weak cyclical/hydrogen demand.

The competitive moat is real in refrigerants (regulation-driven leadership in low-GWP refuse-and-replace products) and durable in chloride-process TiO₂ and specialty fluoropolymer know-how. But the fluoropolymer franchise sits at the center of the PFAS regulatory storm, which converts an asset into a contingent liability.

## Financial Analysis

Total revenue is roughly flat at ~$1.4B per quarter (~$5.6B annualized), but the composition is the story: the refrigerant segment is masking severe deterioration elsewhere. FY2025 swung to a $386M net loss from +$69M in 2024 and -$252M in 2023, a volatile and deteriorating earnings trajectory.

Q1'26 net sales were $1,381M (+1%), with gross margin compressing to 15.4% from 17.3% and a net loss of $(29)M, or $(0.19)/share, versus $(5)M a year prior. Operating cash flow was negative $(44)M, a seasonal first-half working-capital drain, though improved from $(112)M in Q1'25.

The balance sheet is the defining concern:

- Total equity fell to $216M from $250M at year-end 2025. Equity has been eroded almost entirely by losses, dividends, and buybacks.
- Liquidity of ~$1.5B (cash $563M, of which $357M is offshore, plus a $953M undrawn revolver). Management asserts sufficient liquidity "through at least the end of May 2027," a rolling ~12-month runway statement conditioned on legal/environmental outcomes not turning adverse.
- A maturity wall of ~$1,101M in 2028 and $644M in 2029. Management has been terming out aggressively: a March 2026 issue of $700M in 7.875% notes due 2034 (up sharply from prior 4.6–5.75% coupons), redemption of the 2027 notes and part of the 2028 notes, extension of the USD term loan to 2032, and a €140M paydown of the 2028 Euro loan in April 2026. Interest expense of $69M per quarter (~$276M/year) is rising roughly $9M annually from refinancing.
- Ratings are solidly non-investment-grade with a negative bias: Moody's Ba3 (negative, 8/2025) and S&P BB- (negative, 4/2025).

The one clear liquidity bright spot is the Kuan Yin, Taiwan land sale: ~$287M net cash received in April 2026, with a ~$265M gain to be booked in Q2'26 and a further ~$55M parcel expected by year-end. This is a one-time asset monetization, not recurring earnings power.

The dividend was cut ~65% (from $0.25 to $0.0875/quarter), a prudent move but a signal of balance-sheet stress. The 2022 $750M buyback expired in December 2025 having repurchased $309M at an average $29.90/share, well above the current mid-$17 price. That capital was destroyed.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is narrow and cyclical. TSS is the only segment growing, and it is doing so on a combination of durable regulatory tailwind (HFC phasedown) and transitory pricing (Freon +67% YoY as legacy supply tightens). The Freon component in particular is not a durable growth driver; it is a supply-constraint price spike that will normalize.

Estimate revisions confirm negative earnings momentum, not recovery. Current-year EPS consensus has fallen 12.3% over 90 days (to $1.28), and the current-quarter estimate is down 34.3% over the same window. Next-quarter revisions are also negative (-6.2% over 90 days / -4.0% over 30 days). This is the opposite of the positive revision velocity that would signal a genuine trough-to-recovery turn. The only stable line is next-year EPS at $2.24 (+0.5% over 90 days), which embeds a TT/APM recovery that is not yet visible in the run-rate.

Segment concentration risk is acute. With TT and APM Adjusted EBITDA down to $18M and $5M respectively, essentially the entire company's near-term earnings power rests on TSS at $190M. Any reversal in Freon pricing or Opteon demand would remove the one pillar holding up the story. The DeLisle, Mississippi chlor-alkali project with PCC Group was terminated in March 2026 (now in arbitration), removing a growth vector. We score growth prospects at 4.5: the regulatory refrigerant tailwind is genuine and durable, but it is offset by two segments in trough conditions, falling consensus estimates, and a transitory pricing component that flatters the current print.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing and forward P/E are both negative (-6.4x) on GAAP losses, so headline multiples are uninformative. On forward consensus of $1.28 for the current year and $2.24 for next year, CC trades at roughly 13.7x current-year and 7.8x next-year EPS. On its face, the next-year figure looks cheap. It is not a high-conviction value opportunity for three reasons.

First, the "cheap" multiple reflects structural risk, not mispricing. Enterprise value of roughly $6.4B (equity market cap ~$2.7B plus net debt) against a business generating volatile, currently-negative net income and negative operating cash flow is a distressed-credit valuation, not a discounted quality compounder. A low multiple that reflects a genuine impairment or a value-trap dynamic sorts the wrong way. Here the compression is driven by an unquantifiable PFAS liability tail, a 19x-leveraged balance sheet, and an active federal accounting investigation, none of which is a catalyst for re-rating.

Second, the next-year EPS on which the sub-8x multiple rests embeds a TT and APM recovery that consensus itself is cutting on the near-term lines. Buying a stock on a distant recovery estimate while the near-term estimate falls 34% in a quarter is buying hope.

Third, peers offer no support for a premium. TiO₂ comparables (Tronox, Kronos, Venator) are in the same trough, and the market applies a distress discount to Chemours specifically because of the litigation and leverage overhang. The analyst price target range of $21–$30 (mean $25.33) sits well above the current $17.52, but on a name with negative earnings, a distress Z-Score, and an active DOJ investigation, we do not treat those targets as reliable floors; they lag the deteriorating legal and balance-sheet reality. We score valuation at 3.5. The apparent cheapness is a warning flag, not a buy signal.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Refrigerants (TSS):** Structurally advantaged. The global HFC phasedown is a multi-year regulatory tailwind favoring low-GWP Opteon, and legacy Freon supply constraints drive outsized pricing. Honeywell (Solstice) is the primary low-GWP competitor. This is the strongest competitive position in the portfolio and the reason the equity has any near-term earnings support.
- **TiO₂ (TT):** A global commodity cyclical in a demand trough (weak coatings and construction) with input-cost pressure and low-cost Chinese competition. A 3% Adjusted EBITDA margin signals near-trough conditions. Competitive position is stable at the technology level (chloride process) but the business has no pricing power at this point in the cycle.
- **Fluoropolymers (APM):** Long-term secular demand (semiconductors, hydrogen, EV batteries) but presently weak and facing the most acute regulatory tail risk from PFAS bans. 3M's stated exit from PFAS manufacturing could ease competition but also underscores how toxic the category has become to incumbents. The EU REACH PFAS restriction (potential 2027 entry) could restrict fluoropolymers and F-gases outright.

On balance the competitive position is mixed: a genuine moat in refrigerants offset by a commodity trough in pigments and an existential regulatory threat in fluoropolymers. We score competitive position at 5.0, with the trajectory two-sided: improving in TSS, deteriorating in TT and APM on both cyclical and regulatory grounds.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by binary, largely unpriced liabilities that make this a special situation rather than an operating investment.

1. **PFAS litigation — the defining risk.** Chemours is a defendant/indemnitor in a vast web of PFAS litigation: the AFFF MDL (~12,900 matters), hundreds of opt-out water-system suits, state natural-resource-damage claims, and turnout-gear personal-injury suits. Management repeatedly states losses "could be material" and are "not estimable"; the reserve is a floor, not a ceiling.

2. **New Jersey JCO.** The August 2025 Judicial Consent Order requires settling companies to pay $875M over 25 years (Chemours' 50% share NPV ~$266M accrued at 8%). The JCO was not yet final, with an approval hearing scheduled for June 24, 2026, after objector challenges. It also triggers Remediation Funding Source requirements ranging from $177M to $1,048M in year one, a wide and potentially very large range.

3. **First-ever federal PFAS settlement.** Chemours agreed to the ~$450M settlement (a $22.5M civil penalty plus $370M+ in mitigation/clean-water programs) over PFAS discharges in NC, WV, and NJ. North Carolina's Attorney General publicly opposed it as a "backroom deal," so implementation may face state-level friction.

4. **Finite MOU cost-share.** The January 2021 MOU splits legacy PFAS costs 50/50 with DuPont/Corteva up to $4B, with only ~$1.3B of headroom remaining after the Ohio, Delaware, and NJ settlements. Once exhausted (or past 2040), Chemours bears costs alone.

5. **Environmental remediation.** The $617M accrual comprises Fayetteville $316M, Chambers Works $73M, and Pompton Lakes $53M, with disclosed upside of up to ~$630M above the accrual. Fayetteville carries ongoing GenX consent-order obligations and 2026 trials.

6. **Washington Works single-point-of-failure.** A core APM site is simultaneously subject to a federal discharge injunction, awaiting NPDES permit renewal (July 2026), and recovering from a January 2026 outage that cost $25M in Q1 EBITDA. The 10-Q explicitly warns that failure to renew "could impact our ability to operate the site" and could trigger long-lived asset impairment.

7. **Governance/accounting scar.** The 2024 Audit Committee review found the then-CEO, CFO, and Controller violated the Code of Ethics regarding working-capital/incentive metrics. This triggered SEC subpoenas, an ongoing SDNY investigation, two securities class actions, three consolidated derivative suits, and a 2025 financial-statement revision. New management (Dignam/Hostetter) inherits a credibility-repair mandate. Insider Form 4 activity shows only routine grants and tax-withholding sales, with no open-market buying at depressed prices.

Sector risk (chemicals cyclicality) is priced; the idiosyncratic litigation, permit, and governance risks are only partly priced and skew asymmetrically to the downside. We score risk factors at 2.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market corroborates the near-term stress view. Near-term ATM IV is 76.5% versus 1-year historical volatility of 64.3% (rich by 12.2 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (76.5% near-term versus 69.9% at six months), pricing imminent, event-driven volatility consistent with the cluster of legal/permit catalysts. Longer-dated skew is meaningfully negative (-6.6% at six months), indicating demand for downside protection. Put/call OI ratio is low at 0.09, but the backwardation and rising longer-dated put skew are the more informative signals. IV has fallen from 90.5% to 76.5% since the prior snapshot even as the stock dropped 20.4%, consistent with a stock grinding lower rather than gapping, with the market still pricing elevated forward uncertainty. Short interest of 11.8% of float (4.8 days to cover) reflects institutional bearishness without extreme squeeze risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$28–30):** The refrigerant supercycle proves durable — the HFC phasedown continues to lift Opteon volumes and Freon pricing holds — while TiO₂ and APM recover off trough as coatings/construction demand normalizes and the Washington Works site returns to full capacity with permits renewed. PFAS liabilities are successfully sequenced into scheduled, NPV-manageable settlements cushioned by the DuPont/Corteva cost-share, the NJ JCO is approved on manageable terms, and the federal settlement finalizes without material state add-ons. The Kuan Yin land proceeds and tariff refunds buttress liquidity, refinancing removes near-term default risk, and the equity re-rates as a deleveraging cyclical toward analyst targets in the high-$20s to $30.

**Bear Case (~$8–11):** PFAS and environmental costs exceed reserves (the ~$630M disclosed environmental upside plus unquantifiable PFAS amounts), consuming the remaining MOU headroom faster than expected. The NJ JCO faces adverse revision, the federal settlement expands under NC opposition, or Washington Works loses its NPDES permit and takes an APM impairment. Freon pricing normalizes, removing the one earnings pillar, while TT stays in trough. The SEC/DOJ investigation produces charges or penalties. With only $216M of equity beneath $4.2B of debt, any of these outcomes compresses the thin equity cushion sharply, and the stock retests the 52-week low near $10.44 or below.

The asymmetry favors the downside: the bear case is driven by binary, non-estimable liabilities against a razor-thin equity base, while the bull case depends on a cyclical recovery plus a favorable resolution of every legal and permit catalyst simultaneously.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** There are multiple dated catalysts — the NJ JCO approval hearing, the finalization of the federal settlement with active NC AG opposition, the Washington Works NPDES permit renewal, and Q2'26 earnings (which will book the ~$265M Kuan Yin gain, potentially flattering the headline and complicating a clean short entry). The problem for a short is that the two clearest liability events are near-term binary in both directions: the JCO and federal settlement could resolve favorably as easily as adversely.

**Timing window:** The permit and settlement catalysts cluster in mid-2026, but resolution timing is controlled by courts and regulators, not the calendar. The Q2 land-sale gain risks producing a positive GAAP surprise in the near term, working against a short.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI is elevated but not extreme. This is a moderately crowded bearish name, which limits incremental room and creates modest squeeze risk on any favorable settlement headline or refrigerant-pricing surprise.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options confirm the bearish tilt (backwardation, negative six-month skew, rich near-term IV), but the IV level makes puts expensive, and the balanced near-term put/call skew (0.2%) shows no acute panic. High realized volatility (64% HV) makes a directional short costly to hold through the Q2 gain and event noise.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** A bear-case retest of ~$10 from $17.52 is ~43% downside; the upside risk on favorable JCO/settlement resolution plus the Q2 gain could take the stock back toward the low-$20s (~+20%), roughly a 2:1 reward/risk. The timing is muddied by the near-term positive Kuan Yin gain and the possibility that both major liability events resolve within-reserve.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamentals justify AVOID, but the setup does not clear the bar for an active short. The liability catalysts are two-sided rather than one-way, a near-term GAAP-flattering land-sale gain works against timing, moderately elevated short interest limits room, and high IV raises the cost of expressing the view via options. This is a distressed equity to avoid owning, not one to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Definitive resolution of the PFAS liability tail — the NJ JCO approved on within-reserve terms, the federal settlement finalized without material state-level expansion, and clear evidence that remaining MOU headroom is adequate — such that the equity is no longer a levered option on non-estimable liabilities.
- Balance-sheet repair: book equity rebuilt materially above $216M and D/E moving back below single digits, with sustained positive operating cash flow and a rising Altman Z-Score out of the distress zone.
- Broadening of the earnings base beyond TSS — a demonstrated TiO₂ and APM recovery visible in the run-rate (not just distant consensus), confirmed by positive estimate revisions rather than the current 12–34% cuts.
- Resolution of the SEC/SDNY investigation without material charges, plus open-market insider buying signaling management conviction.

**Timeline:** Headwinds are likely to persist 12–24 months. The legal and permit catalysts resolve over the next several quarters, but balance-sheet repair and a broad cyclical recovery in TiO₂ and fluoropolymers are multi-year processes. We would reassess after the NJ JCO hearing outcome, the federal settlement finalization, the Washington Works NPDES decision, and Q2'26 earnings (stripping out the Kuan Yin gain to judge underlying trends).

## Conclusion

Chemours has a genuinely attractive refrigerant franchise riding a durable regulatory tailwind, and management has capably termed out its near-term maturities and monetized non-core assets. Those positives are dwarfed by the structure of the situation: $216M of book equity beneath $4.18B of debt and over $1.1B of accrued litigation and environmental reserves, with management's own disclosures pointing to hundreds of millions in potential excess and PFAS exposure that is "not estimable." A single cyclical segment carries the company's earnings, near-term consensus estimates are falling double digits, the cost of capital has risen to ~7.9% coupons, and an active federal accounting investigation sits atop a razor-thin equity cushion. The stock will trade on litigation and permit outcomes rather than fundamentals, and the risk/reward from the current mid-$17 price is asymmetric to the downside.

We rate CC **AVOID** with an overall score of 3.5. This is a high-risk special situation for distressed-credit specialists, not a general-equity holding. The apparent cheapness on next-year EPS is a value-trap flag, not a buy signal, and there is no catalyst on the horizon likely to re-rate the equity favorably against its liability tail.
