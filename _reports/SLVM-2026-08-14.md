## Executive Summary

Sylvamo has moved from a cheap, cash-generative spin-off into a company where the earnings base has narrowed to a single geography. North America produced $75M of segment operating profit in the first half of 2026; Europe lost $64M and Latin America lost $12M, leaving company-wide business segment operating profit at negative $1M versus $74M in 1H 2025. Adjusted EBITDA fell to $89M in the first half from $172M, and last-twelve-month free cash flow on the company's own definition is approximately negative $11M against a dividend that costs $71.6M annually. Net debt rose $123M in six months to $841M. Management has already halted the buyback, repurchasing zero shares in 1H 2026 despite $150M remaining on the authorization, $150M of which the Board added in Q3 2025.

The offsetting evidence is real. The May 2026 issuance of a $357M Term Loan F-3 due 2032 at SOFR+205bp fully retired the $257M 2027 term loan and $100M of revolver, eliminating what had been a $370M 2027 maturity wall; the receivables securitization was extended to 2029. Net leverage of 2.3x on LTM adjusted EBITDA and $443M of available liquidity against a $275M restricted-payments condition are not distress metrics for a commodity paper producer. The first half is structurally the weaker half (2H25 FCF was $71M against 1H25's negative $27M), and an $85M inventory build explicitly tied to an extended Eastover outage should release in the second half. Eastover's woodyard and machine work adds 60,000 tons and a stated $50M incremental annual adjusted EBITDA benefit, $30–40M of it in 2027.

Every element of the recovery case is prospective while every element of the deterioration is realized. Consensus FY2026 EPS has been cut from $2.34 to $1.46 (−37.8%) over 90 days and FY2027 from $5.58 to $5.08. The company has missed consensus in four consecutive quarters, the last two by 82% and 89%. Consensus implies 2H26 EPS of $2.04 (Q3 $1.21 + Q4 $0.83) against an adjusted 1H26 result of approximately negative $0.50. An earnings recovery of that magnitude, in a quarter that also carries the extended Eastover outage and a Q4 Nymölla outage, is not something a four-for-four misser has earned the benefit of the doubt on. At $38.52 the base case sits roughly 6% above spot with a credible bear case 30% below. We move from AVOID to REDUCE on the improved maturity profile and the balance sheet's genuine headroom, but the asymmetry does not support owning the stock into the Q3 report.

## Company Overview

Sylvamo was spun out of International Paper on October 1, 2021 as a pure-play global uncoated freesheet (UFS) producer — copy paper, offset and commercial printing grades, converting grades — with a secondary market pulp business. Segments are geographic and their economics diverge sharply.

| Segment | FY2025 Sales | FY2025 Segment Op. Profit | 1H26 Op. Profit | Assets (6/30/26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North America | $1,754M | $263M | $75M | $973M |
| Latin America | $904M | $100M | $(12)M | $1,279M |
| Europe | $741M | $(112)M | $(64)M | $482M |
| **Total** | **$3,351M** | **$251M** | **$(1)M** | **$2,875M** |

North America runs owned mills at Eastover, South Carolina and Ticonderoga, New York, with Hammermill, Springhill and Accent brands plus a licensed right to produce HP-branded copy paper across nearly all geographies. Latin America comprises three Brazilian mills with more than 1.1 million short tons of capacity supported by roughly 250,000 acres of owned and managed eucalyptus forestland carried at $393M on a sub-seven-year rotation. Europe holds Saillat, France and Nymölla, Sweden (acquired January 2023, ~500,000 tons).

The 10-K asserts that North and Latin American mills "predominantly rank in the lowest quartile on global and regional UFS cost curves." Europe is excluded from that claim, which is consistent with its realized losses. The claim is management's assertion, not independently verified.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings trajectory

| ($M) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 1H25 | 1H26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 3,721 | 3,773 | 3,351 | 1,615 | 1,561 |
| Net income | 253 | 302 | 132 | 42 | (14) |
| Adj. EBITDA | — | 632 | 448 | 172 | 89 |
| Adj. EBITDA margin | — | 16.8% | 13.4% | 10.7% | 5.7% |
| Diluted EPS | — | $7.18 | $3.24 | $1.02 | $(0.35) |
| Op. cash flow | — | 469 | 268 | 87 | 28 |
| Capex | — | 221 | 224 | 114 | 110 |
| FCF (co. def.) | — | 248 | 44 | (27) | (82) |

The FY2025 revenue decline of $422M was mostly deliberate. North American sales fell $275M "driven by lower volumes ($292 million) primarily due to the termination of the Georgetown mill offtake agreement" — tons Sylvamo purchased from International Paper and resold rather than manufactured. A second such arrangement, the Riverdale (Selma, Alabama) offtake, ended at the end of April 2026. Management quantifies the combined 2026 effect at $85M of unfavorable adjusted EBITDA.

Europe's losses are operational rather than outage-driven. The 1H26 loss of $64M came despite $37M lower planned maintenance outage costs year over year; the entire benefit was consumed by $30M of adverse price/mix and $6M of higher energy input costs. Annualized, Europe is running roughly negative $110–130M on approximately $780M of sales, before the $11M France goodwill write-off, which the company reports outside segment profit. All $13M of European goodwill is now impaired.

The newer and less-discussed deterioration is Latin America, which swung $40M to a $12M loss on "higher operating ($16 million) and input costs ($9 million), primarily for purchased fiber." That connects to a disclosed operating problem: Brazilian forestlands have been "producing sub-optimal yields, partly due to weather events," forcing third-party wood purchases. The vertically integrated segment holding $1,279M of the $2,875M asset base is losing money because it is buying fiber it was designed to grow. Suzano won an appeal on August 6, 2026 revoking Sylvamo's injunction, enabling it to invoice Brazilian VAT on pulp supplied to Três Lagoas "potentially totaling $15 to $20 million annually," with no liability recorded.

### Cash flow and the dividend gap

LTM free cash flow is approximately negative $11M, derived as FY2025's $44M less 1H25's negative $27M (giving 2H25 of $71M), plus 1H26's negative $82M. Against that, Sylvamo paid $36M of dividends in the first half and runs a $0.45 quarterly dividend — $1.80 annually, $71.6M on 39.8M shares, a 4.67% yield at $38.52. The dividend is not currently earned in cash.

Two documented offsets argue against annualizing 1H26. Inventories rose $85M to $503M, with finished paper and pulp up $82M, which the 10-Q attributes to building stock ahead of the extended Eastover outage; that build should release. The seasonal pattern is pronounced: 2H25 adjusted EBITDA was $276M against 1H25's $172M. Working the other way, the Nymölla planned outage falls in Q4 2026, the extended Eastover outage also lands in the second half, and $120–140M of the $230–250M FY2026 capex guidance remains to be spent.

Capital returns have outrun cash generation. In 2025 Sylvamo returned $155M against $44M of FCF — 352% — financed with $178M drawn on the revolver and $47M on the securitization. In 1H26 it returned $36M against negative $82M of FCF, with the buyback at zero.

### Balance sheet and covenant arithmetic

At June 30, 2026: term loans and securitization of $851M, finance leases $14M, other financing $19M, revolver $80M = $964M total debt; less $123M cash = $841M net debt. That is up from $718M at December 31, 2025. LTM adjusted EBITDA of $365M (FY25 $448M − 1H25 $172M + 1H26 $89M) gives net leverage of 2.30x and gross leverage of 2.64x against a maximum consolidated total leverage covenant of 3.75x, with the company confirming compliance.

Holding debt at $964M, the 3.75x test requires LTM adjusted EBITDA of at least $257M. With $89M booked in the first half, that implies second-half adjusted EBITDA of at least $168M, or 61% of 2H25's $276M. Two caveats matter: the credit agreement's EBITDA definition and permitted add-backs are not disclosed in the filings, so this uses reported adjusted EBITDA as a proxy rather than the actual covenant test, and inventory release should reduce debt and lower the required EBITDA. This is a headroom question to monitor, not a solvency question today. Interest coverage on LTM adjusted EBITDA is approximately 9.1x, down from 11.5x in FY2025; the first half alone ran at 4.5x.

The maturity work deserves credit. The May 2026 Term Loan F-3 ($357M, 2032, SOFR+205bp, effective ~4.79% net of roughly 90bp of Farm Credit patronage credits) retired the $257M 2027 term loan and $100M of revolver at a $2M extinguishment cost. The securitization now runs to 2029 with $110M capacity; the $400M revolver ($320M available) matures 2029. There is no meaningful maturity before 2029.

Book equity of $955M implies P/B of about 1.6x, but accumulated other comprehensive loss of $1,316M — almost entirely cumulative Brazilian translation — makes book value a poor anchor here.

## Growth Analysis

Sylvamo does not have a growth story; it has a mix-improvement story inside a slowly declining end market, and the arithmetic of the swap is incomplete.

Global UFS demand declined at a 2.1% CAGR from 2019–2025 and a 1.1% CAGR from 2021–2025 per RISI data cited in the 10-K. Roughly 1% annual erosion is manageable if supply discipline holds. The strategy is to replace thin-margin purchased tonnage with owned low-cost capacity: Georgetown's termination removed $292M of 2025 North American volume and $71M of segment operating profit (implying roughly a 24% contribution margin on lost tons), Riverdale ended in April 2026, and the offset is Eastover's 60,000 incremental tons.

Sixty thousand tons is far below the tonnage forgone — Georgetown alone implied roughly 225,000–265,000 tons at plausible realizations — so revenue is permanently lower and the bet is entirely on margin per ton. The critical unresolved number is the split of the $85M FY2026 transition headwind between the transitory extended Eastover outage and the permanent Riverdale exit. Management has not disclosed it. If the outage dominates, 2027 North American profit could exceed 2025's once the Eastover benefit arrives. If Riverdale dominates, the $50M benefit merely partially offsets a permanent loss. This is not resolvable by assumption in either direction, and it is the single most important variable in the equity story.

Consensus has been marking the near term down hard while holding the 2027 recovery mostly intact:

| Period | Current | 90d ago | Revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 2026 | $1.21 | $1.58 | −23.6% |
| Q4 2026 | $0.83 | $0.76 | +9.2% |
| FY2026 | $1.46 | $2.34 | −37.8% |

A 37.8% cut to the current year is a strong negative earnings-momentum signal, and the shape of the estimates — 2H26 EPS of $2.04 against 1H26's roughly negative $0.50 — places nearly all of the FY2026 number in two quarters that contain two planned outages. A secondary summary of the Q2 call cites a $75–85M second-half price/mix tailwind and a $55M Eastover benefit; neither figure appears in a company document we could verify, and the 10-Q states $50M for Eastover. If the price/mix tailwind is real it resolves much of the covenant-headroom concern; treating it as fact would be an unforced error.

At $38.52, market capitalization is $1,531M (39.736M shares) and enterprise value is $2,372M including $841M of net debt.

The sector peer table supplied is not usable for relative valuation — three of five comparables carry negative or triple-digit P/Es (SCL −543.7x, FMC −0.5x, AMR −42.3x, WS 225.1x) and a 225.1x median tells us only that the Materials small-cap cohort is at a cyclical earnings trough. The relevant anchor is the commodity-paper EV/EBITDA range, historically 5–7x for a business with mid-single-digit secular volume decline, modest leverage and heavy maintenance capex.

On that basis, Sylvamo prices roughly at the midpoint. A normalized adjusted EBITDA of $420M — FY2025's $448M less the permanent portion of the offtake exits, plus partial Eastover contribution — at 5.8x gives EV of $2,436M, and net of $820M of net debt yields $40.6 per share. Eight times FY2027 consensus of $5.08 gives $40.6. Two independent methods converge near $41, roughly 6% above spot.

The 7.6x FY2027 multiple is the crux of the bull case, but the FY2027 number has itself been cut 8.8%, the company has missed four consecutive quarters, and two of three segments are loss-making with a new $15–20M annual Brazilian VAT cost potentially layering onto the weaker one. A low multiple on estimates that are falling and unearned is the classic value-trap configuration, not evidence of mispricing. To score valuation highly we would need to identify what the marginal seller is missing; on this evidence the market is pricing unresolved earnings power appropriately.

Analyst targets ($45 low, $47.50 median, $51.25 mean, $65 high) sit 17% to 69% above spot with sentiment characterized as degrading and only three to four contributors. On stocks where estimates are rising, consensus targets lag reality and function as floors. Here estimates are falling and the buy-side count has drifted from two buys to one over three months; these targets are stale in the unfavorable direction and should not be treated as independent confirmation. The stock is 32% below its February 12 intraday high of $56.80 and 8.4% above its May 20 low of $35.53; a drawdown of that size means many informed holders sold, and on the evidence available their reasons — segment losses spreading, uncovered dividend, rising net debt — are visible rather than mysterious.

## Competitive Landscape

The investable industry question is whether UFS supply discipline continues to offset roughly 1% annual demand erosion. In North America the answer has been yes: the four largest producers, Sylvamo included, hold approximately 80% of capacity, competitors have converted machines to linerboard, pulp and boxboard, and FY2025 North American price/mix was favorable by $19M even as volumes collapsed. First-half 2026 North American price/mix was slightly positive at +$1M despite the Riverdale exit.

Outside North America the mechanism runs the other way, and the 10-K names it: producers "to avoid substantial cash costs in connection with idling or closing a mill, may choose to continue operating at a loss, which could prolong weak pricing environments due to oversupply." Europe's $30M first-half price/mix decline and Latin America's $8M are consistent with exactly that behavior, and Sylvamo — running Europe at roughly $120M of annualized losses without closing it — is plausibly one instance of the dynamic it describes.

Two structural asymmetries define the position. From 2023–2025, Europe and Latin America averaged 48% of revenue but 28% of segment operating profit; both are now loss-making, leaving North America carrying the company. And Latin America's moat — 250,000 acres of eucalyptus on a sub-seven-year rotation — is temporarily impaired rather than absent: the segment is losing money because of purchased fiber costs caused by sub-optimal yields on its own plantations. The $116M of 2025 and $49M of 1H26 Latin American capex, much of it reforestation, is the repair spend, and on a multi-year view the weather-driven yield shortfall should reverse.

Two additional dependencies belong in a competitive assessment. Sylvamo produces HP-branded copy paper under license across nearly all geographies, and the 10-K flags loss of that license as a specific risk. Top-ten customers represent about 41% of sales with one at roughly 15% (~$503M of 2025 revenue), and customers "are not contractually required to purchase any product minimums." The 10-K describes tariffs as a two-way mechanical problem — they have "introduced additional competing products into some countries where we sell, putting downward pressure on the pricing of our products" and have "constrained our ability to import and sell in the United States various products that we produce outside the United States." For a manufacturer with Brazilian and European mills selling globally, that is a direct explanation for the non-US price/mix weakness rather than a generic macro caveat.

## Risk Assessment

**Contingent liabilities, correctly bounded.** The Brazil Tax Dispute headline is $434M ($113M tax plus $321M interest and penalties) but the Tax Matters Agreement caps Sylvamo at 40% of the first $300M, with International Paper paying 100% above that — maximum exposure $120M, about 7.8% of market cap. The structural weakness is that IP controls all litigation and settlement decisions, so Sylvamo carries economic exposure without decision rights, and its broader Tax Matters indemnity obligations to IP are explicitly uncapped. Separately, Brazilian VAT matters assessed as not probable and therefore unreserved total roughly $103M (São Paulo ~$57M, 2009–2010 credits $27M, 2011–2013 credits $19M), and the 10-Q notes these grow with currency moves and accrued interest and that concurrent settlement could be material.

**Newly adverse and unreserved.** The August 6, 2026 Suzano appeal enables VAT invoicing of $15–20M annually at Três Lagoas against a segment that just posted a first-half loss. If Sylvamo prevails in arbitration it recovers amounts collected, and Suzano's right expires in 2032 under Brazil's VAT reform even on an adverse outcome, but the cash cost lands now.

**Unquantified and disclosed as potentially material.** Mogi Guaçu legacy wastewater basins (mercury and other metals): the June 15, 2026 CETESB Agreement was procedurally favorable, eliminating the pilot intervention plan, but a future-use and remediation plan is due March 2027 and the company "is unable to estimate its ultimate potential liability, which may be material." The UK pension plan faces *Virgin Media* exposure, where late-1990s amendments could be deemed void and require backdated benefits, unquantified. The spin-off's tax-free treatment remains open to IRS challenge until October 1, 2026 or later.

**Financial.** The dividend is funded by debt at present. The buyback is already at zero. Covenant headroom requires second-half adjusted EBITDA of roughly $168M on our proxy calculation. The restricted-payments condition requires $275M of available liquidity against approximately $443M today — a $168M buffer — and the 10-K flags a harsher variant tied to an adverse Brazil tax ruling combined with failure to deposit $60M into escrow.

**Governance.** A shareholder rights plan was adopted November 10, 2025 with a Series B preferred certificate of designations, layered on blank-check preferred, no action by written consent, board-only vacancy filling, advance notice and Delaware exclusive forum. Two Schedule 13D holders own approximately 16% and 15%, but their filings date from 2022–2023, so these are longstanding rather than newly arrived positions. The October 2025 First Amendment to the Brazil Payment Agreement reduces the maximum payment owed to IP on a transfer of the eucalyptus forestlands or a change of control from a $100M maximum, "reduced in increments over time starting September 1, 2026." That makes both a forestland monetization and a change of control progressively cheaper from September 2026. The amendment is equally consistent with routine housekeeping as the post-spin period closes, but a timberland sale or sale-leaseback (an $11M version was executed in Q1 2026) is the most obvious deleveraging lever available and worth monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (43 contracts across three expirations) and the term structure is the informative part. Near-term ATM IV of 30.0% sits below 38.0% one-year realized volatility and at the 5th percentile within the chain, with a ±3.5% implied move on the six-day expiry, reflecting a post-earnings vol trough rather than a directional view. The one-month tenor jumps to 41.5% (±10.9%) and the three-month to 44.5% (±18.8%), so the market is pricing meaningful risk into the window containing the Q3 print rather than the next week. Put/call open interest of 2.33 indicates protection is held in size, while near-term skew reads call-heavy at −10.7% and flattens to −3.8% at three months. The prior snapshot's 1566.8% ATM IV reading is an illiquid-chain artifact and carries no information. There is no bearish options confirmation of our fundamental caution in the near term, but the term structure prices a genuine event in the autumn, consistent with the four-for-four miss record and the concentration of FY2026 earnings into 2H.

The mechanics also argue against shorting rather than trimming: 8.4% of float short with 7.9 days to cover, a 4.67% dividend to carry, a net-debt-driven sensitivity to any asset-monetization announcement, and a forestland/change-of-control cost structure that gets cheaper from September 1.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($55, +43%)

Second-half 2026 delivers on the reported $75–85M price/mix tailwind; the $85M North American transition headwind proves mostly transitory outage cost rather than permanent Riverdale volume; the inventory build releases and second-half FCF returns to a 2H25-like $71M or better, covering the dividend on a full-year basis; Brazilian forestland yields normalize as reforestation capex works through, eliminating purchased-fiber cost and restoring Latin America to a $100M+ profit run rate; Eastover's 60,000 tons ramp from early 2027 delivering $30–40M of the stated $50M benefit; Europe is restructured, sold or closed, removing a $110–130M annualized drag. On $470M of normalized adjusted EBITDA at 6.5x, less $780M of net debt, equity is worth roughly $57; 11x FY2027 consensus of $5.08 gives $56. A forestland monetization at a fraction of the $393M carrying value would accelerate deleveraging and is structurally cheaper to execute after September 1, 2026.

### Bear Case ($27, −30%)

The Riverdale exit proves the dominant component of the $85M headwind, making the North American profit loss permanent and the $50M Eastover benefit a partial offset rather than incremental growth. Europe continues at a $120M annualized loss because closure costs exceed the cash bleed. Suzano's $15–20M annual VAT lands unmitigated on a Latin American segment that has not recovered its fiber position. Second-half adjusted EBITDA comes in near $140–150M rather than $168M+, pushing gross leverage toward or through 3.5x and forcing a dividend cut to preserve headroom. On $350M of trough EBITDA at 5.5x, less $880M of net debt, equity is worth roughly $26. A dividend cut would remove the yield support that has held the stock in the $37–41 range since May and would likely take it through the $35.53 low.

### Base Case ($41, +6%)

Second half improves materially from an abnormally weak first half but falls short of consensus, as it has in each of the last four quarters. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands near $260–290M, covenant compliance is maintained with modest headroom, the dividend is held but not fully earned in cash for the full year, and 2027 shows real improvement from Eastover without Europe being resolved. Normalized EBITDA of $420M at 5.8x, less $820M of net debt, gives $40.6; 8x FY2027 consensus gives $40.6.

Probability weighting the three at roughly 30/40/30 gives an expected value near $41, approximately 6% above the $38.52 basis, before a 4.67% dividend whose full-year cash coverage is itself contingent. That is a flat-to-slightly-positive expected return with a 30% downside tail, achieved on a stock in a sector where selection has historically not been rewarded.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** The rating change from AVOID reflects two specific developments: the May 2026 refinancing eliminated the 2027 maturity wall and pushed the nearest meaningful maturity to 2029, and net leverage of 2.30x with $443M of liquidity does not constitute the unacceptable tail risk that an AVOID requires. It is not an endorsement — the earnings trajectory has worsened since our June review, with Latin America flipping to a loss, the Suzano injunction revoked, and FY2026 consensus cut 37.8%.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength ahead of the Q3 report rather than after it. The one-month tenor prices a ±10.9% move and the three-month ±18.8%; with four consecutive misses and consensus requiring $2.04 of 2H EPS against negative $0.50 in 1H, the distribution around that print is skewed unfavorably. Holders wanting residual exposure should size it as a yield position no larger than they would hold through a dividend suspension, and should not add on weakness before the covenant path is visible.

**Targets and math from the $38.52 basis:** base $41 (+6%), bull $55 (+43%), bear $27 (−30%). Reward-to-risk to the base case is approximately 0.2:1 against the bear; to the bull case approximately 1.4:1.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $110M or better, confirming that the reported second-half price/mix tailwind is real and putting FY2026 on a path above the ~$257M implied covenant floor.
2. Second-half free cash flow positive with the inventory build released and net debt down from $841M — the specific evidence that the dividend is covered on a full-year basis.
3. Latin American segment operating profit back to breakeven or better, demonstrating that purchased-fiber cost is cyclical rather than structural.
4. Disclosure of the permanent-versus-transitory split of the $85M transition headwind showing the transitory portion at more than half.
5. Clustered open-market insider purchases — none have occurred in the reviewed window despite the stock near multi-year lows, and management framing 2026 as a transition trough.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE would additionally require** a resolution of Europe (closure, sale or restructuring with quantified cost) or a forestland monetization that takes net debt below $650M, plus confirmed Eastover ramp on schedule.

**Downgrade back to AVOID (any one):**
1. Dividend reduced or suspended.
2. Gross leverage on reported adjusted EBITDA above 3.3x at any quarter end, or a covenant amendment sought.
3. Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA below $80M, which would make the ~$257M implied floor unreachable without asset sales.
4. An adverse Brazil Tax Dispute ruling requiring the $60M escrow deposit while liquidity is near the $275M condition.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 report (early November) is the decisive checkpoint — it is the first period free of the offtake transition noise on a full-quarter basis and will reveal whether the second-half tailwind is materializing. Secondary dates: the March 2027 Mogi Guaçu future-use plan submission, the Q4 Nymölla outage impact in the FY2026 report, and any 8-K on forestland or European asset action after September 1, 2026.

## Conclusion

Sylvamo's equity now depends on a single geography carrying the company while two others lose money, on a second-half recovery whose largest component we could not verify against a company document, and on a 2027 self-help program smaller in tonnage than the volume it replaces. Against that, the balance sheet has been competently termed out, leverage at 2.3x net is normal for the industry, and the shares at 6.5x LTM EV/EBITDA and 7.6x FY2027 consensus are not expensive on any measure of what the business might earn in a better year.

The distinction that matters is between cheap and mispriced. The evidence available — four consecutive misses, a 37.8% cut to current-year consensus, negative LTM free cash flow against the dividend, net debt up $123M in six months, a buyback taken to zero, and a newly adverse and unreserved $15–20M annual Brazilian VAT cost — supports the view that the multiple compressed for identifiable reasons rather than in error. A base case 6% above spot against a 30% bear tail is inadequate compensation for that dispersion. We would revisit constructively on a Q3 print above $110M of adjusted EBITDA with positive second-half free cash flow, or at a price in the high $20s where the trough is priced rather than assumed away.
