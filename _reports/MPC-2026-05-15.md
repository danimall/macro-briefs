## Executive Summary

Marathon Petroleum is experiencing an earnings uplift driven by geopolitical supply disruption. The stock trades at a compressed multiple for a company with the largest U.S. refining footprint, an integrated midstream business generating $6.75B in stable EBITDA, and an aggressive capital return program that has retired 40% of outstanding shares since 2022.

The Q1 2026 beat ($1.65 vs. approximately $0.72–$0.75 estimate, a surprise exceeding 119%) confirmed MPC's operational leverage to the current margin environment. With approximately 94% utilization guided for Q2 and crack spreads sustained at elevated levels, the upward estimate revision cycle has further to run. The $5B buyback authorization expansion in May signals management confidence in sustaining capital returns. The primary risks are crack spread mean-reversion if geopolitical tensions de-escalate and California regulatory overhang, though the latter was substantially deferred in August 2025.

## Company Overview

Marathon Petroleum operates the largest independent refining system in the U.S. at approximately 3.0 million barrels per day across 13 refineries in three regions (Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent, West Coast). The company's approximately 64% ownership of MPLX LP provides stable midstream cash flows ($6.75B segment EBITDA, $2.56B in distributions to MPC in 2025). A small Renewable Diesel segment (-$110M EBITDA) is currently a drag but immaterial to the thesis.

Key structural differentiators include ~50% sour crude processing capability (providing access to discounted feedstock), geographic diversification with inter-refinery pipeline connectivity, and the industry's most aggressive share repurchase program (approximately $56.4B retired since 2012).

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Inflection:** The trailing P/E of approximately 16x (on $13.22 FY2025 EPS) reflects normalized refining margins. FY2026 consensus estimates have risen significantly, driven by the geopolitical windfall. The Q1 2026 blended 3-2-1 crack spread of $15.34/bbl (up from $11.31/bbl in Q1 2025) and R&M margin of $17.74/bbl (up from $13.38/bbl) confirm the uplift, though estimates remain subject to commodity volatility.

**Q1 2026 Performance:** Adjusted EPS of $1.65 significantly beat consensus (Zacks consensus was approximately $0.72). The beat was driven by elevated refining margins and disciplined turnaround execution, partially offset by derivative losses related to MPC's economic hedging program (net commodity derivative losses of $909M in Q1). Q1 utilization was 89% (depressed by planned maintenance, with approximately 40% of 2026 planned turnaround activity completed), with Q2 throughput guidance of 2,990 mbpd total, implying approximately 94% crude utilization.

**Cash Generation & Returns:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $8.3B with approximately $4.8B in FCF. Under sustained elevated margin conditions, FY2026 FCF could meaningfully exceed FY2025 levels, though this is highly dependent on crack spread durability. The approximately $8.6B in remaining buyback authorization (approximately $3.6B remaining after Q1 2026 repurchases plus the new $5.0B authorization) represents meaningful capital return capacity.

**Leverage Context:** Consolidated debt of $33.3B as of December 31, 2025 (approximately $32.4B as of March 31, 2026) appears high (D/E 3.46x), but approximately $26B sits at MPLX (non-recourse to MPC). MPC standalone carries investment-grade ratings (Baa2/BBB/BBB) and approximately $5.6B in liquidity as of Q1 2026. Interest expense rose to $1.276B in 2025 due to MPLX acquisition financing, which is a drag on near-term earnings but funds long-duration fee-based assets.

**Margin Sensitivity:** Every $1/bbl change in crack spreads impacts annual Refining segment EBITDA by ~$1.125B, or approximately $2.75/share after tax. The current geopolitical premium in cracks is worth an estimated $8-12/share versus mid-cycle levels.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term Estimate Momentum:** Analyst estimates for MPC have risen substantially over recent months, reflecting the geopolitical disruption to global refining supply. The velocity of revision is among the strongest in large-cap energy, though the durability of these estimates is tied to the persistence of elevated crack spreads.

**MPLX Growth Engine:** Midstream EBITDA grew from $6.54B (2024) to $6.75B (2025), with a $2.4B organic growth capital plan for 2026 focused on Permian-to-Gulf Coast value chain expansion (approximately 90% directed toward natural gas and NGL infrastructure). MPLX's Permian growth strategy is expected to support 12.5% annual distribution growth to MPC in 2026 and 2027, meaning MPC's $2.56B annual distribution receipt should grow to approximately $2.9B by 2026–2027.

**Share Count Reduction as EPS Growth:** From 512M shares (2022) to ~292M today (43% reduction). At the current buyback pace and authorization (~$8.6B), MPC could retire a meaningful number of additional shares over the next 18 months, providing significant EPS accretion independent of operating performance.

**Volume Growth:** Refined product sales volumes increased from 3,510 mbpd (2023) to 3,718 mbpd (2025), indicating the refining system is running at higher throughput as turnarounds are completed and capacity optimization continues.

MPC's superior capital return program and MPLX ownership provide differentiation versus pure refining peers.

**Why the Market May Be Undervaluing MPC:** The MPLX stake alone is worth ~$34.6B (approximately 47% of current market cap), implying the refining business trades at ~$39B for $6.14B+ in segment EBITDA — approximately 6.3x, below historical mid-cycle valuations of 7-8x for quality refining assets.

## Competitive Landscape

MPC's competitive position remains strong and improving:

1. **Scale advantage:** ~3.0M bpd capacity provides procurement leverage, inter-refinery optimization, and cost amortization unmatched by smaller refiners.
2. **Heavy crude processing:** ~50% sour crude capability creates a structural cost advantage when heavy/sour differentials are wide. While the sour differential narrowed from -$6.31 (2023) to -$2.76 (2025), access to Venezuelan crude through exempted flows partially offsets.
3. **Integrated logistics:** The MPLX network eliminates third-party transportation costs and provides operational flexibility that standalone refiners cannot replicate.
4. **Geographic positioning:** Gulf Coast and Mid-Continent refineries have structural advantages vs. European competitors facing higher gas costs, older assets, and tighter environmental regulation.

The sour differential narrowing is the primary competitive concern — it reduces MPC's relative advantage vs. peers processing lighter crudes. However, at current geopolitical premiums, the absolute margin environment more than compensates.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Crack Spread Mean-Reversion (Primary Risk):** If geopolitical tensions de-escalate and Hormuz normalizes, crack spreads could compress from current elevated levels toward mid-cycle (~$14-15/bbl). Even modest de-escalation could significantly reduce forward earnings estimates.

**2. California Regulation:** SB X1-2 margin caps were deferred 5 years (CEC adopted resolutions in August 2025 indicating it would not take further action for at least five years), substantially reducing near-term risk. AB X2-1 minimum inventory requirements and SCAQMD Rule 1109.1 remain cost headwinds for the 365 mbpcd Los Angeles refinery, but are manageable and partially priced.

**3. Leverage:** Consolidated interest expense of $1.276B annually is a meaningful drag. If crack spreads normalize while debt remains elevated, FCF compression could limit buyback capacity. Mitigated by MPLX's growing distributions and fee-based earnings.

**4. Sour Crude Differential Compression:** From -$6.31 to -$2.76 over two years — reduces MPC's relative feedstock advantage. If OPEC+ compliance loosens or Iranian sanctions are eased, this differential could narrow further.

**5. Long-term Demand Risk:** EV penetration creates structural headwind to refined product demand over 5-10+ year horizon. Not relevant to 12-18 month thesis but limits terminal multiple.

## Conclusion

Marathon Petroleum at its current compressed forward multiple represents a compelling risk/reward for the current geopolitical environment. The estimate revision momentum combined with structural advantages in heavy crude processing, the largest U.S. refining footprint, and aggressive capital returns via an approximately $8.6B buyback authorization create multiple paths to value realization. The MPLX midstream platform provides a $34.6B value anchor and growing distributions that partially insulate the company from refining cycle volatility.

The primary risk — crack spread normalization — requires a geopolitical resolution. Even in a moderate de-escalation scenario, MPC's peer-leading position, buyback support, and midstream diversification limit downside to the $185-200 range. At current prices, the stock offers asymmetric upside as the market gradually prices in the duration of elevated refining margins.

**Recommendation: BUY** with 3-4% portfolio sizing, acknowledging inherent commodity cyclicality and the stock's 52% appreciation over 12 months. The forward multiple discount and accelerating estimate revisions provide sufficient margin of safety for entry at current levels.
