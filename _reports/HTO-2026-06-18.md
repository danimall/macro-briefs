**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

H2O America (formerly SJW Group, renamed May 2025) is a regulated water and wastewater utility holding company operating across California, Connecticut, Maine, and Texas through four subsidiaries serving roughly 409,000 connections and 1.6 million people. The core attraction is durable: a statutorily protected monopoly franchise, decoupling mechanisms that protect revenue when usage falls, and a dividend with 58 consecutive years of increases and 330 consecutive quarterly payments. Revenue grew 7% to $800.6M in 2025 and net income rose 9% to $102.6M, both driven by authorized rate increases rather than volume — usage is actually declining.

The problem is per-share value creation. Net income grew 9% in 2025 and 15% in Q1 2026, yet diluted EPS was flat in Q1 because share count rose roughly 24% (from ~33.6M in December 2024 to 41.8M by April 2026). The company is funding a ~$2.57B five-year capex program (including ~$400M of mandated PFAS treatment) and a pending ~$540M Quadvest acquisition in the Houston region, and it runs a chronic free-cash-flow deficit that must be met perpetually with new debt and equity. The March 2026 raise of $290.2M plus forward sale agreements for an additional 7.5M shares (settling by March 2028) pre-funds the Texas bet, but at the cost of an S&P outlook cut to Negative and continued dilution before the deal even closes. Total shareholder return has badly lagged the water-utility peer group — indexed TSR of 80 versus a peer 152.99 in 2025.

We rate HTO a HOLD on an income basis. The forward P/E of ~19.5x sits at a discount to the 22.1x peer median, but that discount is warranted by higher relative leverage, a higher cost of incremental debt (new 2025 issuances priced 5.83%–6.70% vs. a 4.38% book), acute dilution, and California supply/regulatory overhangs. The 3.1% yield is well covered (57% payout) and the franchise is genuinely defensive, which supports holding for income. We do not see a path to meaningful appreciation over the next 12 months until Quadvest closes accretively and the dilution overhang clears.

## Company Overview

H2O America is a single-segment ("Water Utility Services") regulated utility holding company. Its operating subsidiaries are:

- **San Jose Water Company (SJWC)** — California; ~232,000 connections serving ~1 million people in the San Jose metro. The largest entity, with an estimated authorized rate base of ~$1.46B at year-end 2025.
- **Connecticut Water Company (CWC)** and **Maine Water Company (MWC)** — acquired in the 2019 CTWS merger; ~143,000 connections, ~465,000 people. CWC rate base ~$878M, MWC ~$204M.
- **The Texas Water Company (TWC)** — the fast-growing San Antonio–Austin corridor; ~30,000 connections, ~90,000 people, rate base ~$212M and rising.

The business model is conventional regulated water: capital invested in infrastructure earns an authorized return on rate base, with periodic general rate cases re-setting allowed revenue. The moat is the regulated monopoly; statutory protection prevents a competing investor-owned utility from operating in the same territory. Growth comes from rate-base investment plus tuck-in acquisitions of small or municipal systems, a fragmentation-driven consolidation thesis shared with peers American Water, Essential Utilities, California Water, and American States Water.

The defining strategic event is the pending Quadvest acquisition, a 50-year family-run Texas operator in the Houston region. PUCT confirmed a $483.6M purchase price as ratemaking rate base under the fair-market-value process; the statutory STM application was administratively complete on April 14, 2026, with closing expected in 2H 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue progressed from $620.7M (2022) to $670.4M (2023), $748.4M (2024), and $800.6M (2025), with the 2025 gain driven by $44.1M of authorized rate increases and pass-through water-cost recovery. Q1 2026 revenue was $183.3M, up 9% YoY. Volume is a headwind, not a tailwind: SJWC residential usage ran 14.6% below authorized levels, and earnings growth comes from rate design and decoupling, not consumption. Net income rose from $73.8M (2022) to $102.6M (2025), with Q1 2026 net income of $19.0M, up 15% YoY.

**Margins and returns.** Operating margin is ~22%, net margin ~12.8%. ROE of ~6.7% and ROA of ~2.0% are modest and sit below the 9.3%–10.88% authorized ROEs across the four states, reflecting regulatory lag and below-authorized usage. This is typical for a capital-heavy, leveraged water utility, but the gap to authorized returns is wider than at better-positioned peers.

**Cash flow and capital intensity — the central tension.** Operating cash flow was $244.8M in 2025, but company-funded capital improvements were $489.6M, up from $353M in 2024. The 2026 capex budget alone is $458M before Quadvest, against the five-year plan. The structural free-cash-flow deficit means HTO must continuously raise external capital, which makes the equity highly sensitive to interest rates and capital-market access.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$1.95B against $1.54B of equity at year-end 2025. Goodwill of $640.3M (12% of assets) traces to the 2019 CTWS merger. The current ratio of 0.7 is normal for a utility funding short-term needs with revolvers. The weighted-average cost of long-term debt rose to 4.38% in 2025 from 4.12% in 2024, and new 2025 issuances priced at 5.83%–6.70%. As debt rolls over in a higher-for-longer rate environment, this is a persistent earnings headwind, because rate cases recover higher financing costs with a lag. The relevant credit anchor is the S&P A-/Negative rating, cut to Negative in July 2025 on Quadvest leverage.

**Earnings track record.** The company beat in three of the last four quarters (Q1 2026 +5.3%, Q3 2025 +7.9%, Q2 2025 +7.6%) with one miss (Q4 2025 −5.8%). The beats are modest and consistent with a regulated utility's limited variance.

**Estimate revisions.** Consensus is drifting lower. Current-year EPS estimates fell 7.0% over 90 days (to $2.74 from $2.95) and current-quarter estimates fell 2.5%. Next-year estimates are marginally higher (+2.8% over 90 days, to $2.75), but the near-term trend is negative. This reinforces a cautious read on growth.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is rate-base-driven and reasonably visible, but per-share growth is the binding constraint. The rate-case pipeline is active: a new CWC general rate case filed March 2026 (~$26M increase, decision expected February 2027); an MWC general rate case (~$9.5M) filed April 2026; MWC's unification of its 10 rate divisions in February 2026; and TWC's second system improvement charge amendment pending in mid-2026. Texas HB 2712, effective September 2026, will allow future test years, a genuine regulatory tailwind for the capex-heavy TWC and Quadvest footprint, since it lets the utility recover anticipated rather than only historical investment.

Quadvest is the swing factor. If it closes in 2H 2026 at the confirmed $483.6M rate base and integrates cleanly, it adds a fast-growing Houston-area platform and demonstrates the M&A engine the new management team was hired to run. But accretion is entirely prospective, and the company has already issued substantial equity (and committed to 7.5M more forward shares settling by March 2028 at ~$51.28) to fund a deal that has not closed. The investor must underwrite whether Quadvest's accretion outpaces the dilution already booked, a question that will not be answered for several quarters.

The per-share dynamic is the heart of the issue: net income growth has produced essentially flat diluted EPS because share count rose ~24% in 16 months. With negative near-term estimate revisions on top, the growth case for the equity is muted even though the underlying enterprise is expanding. We score growth prospects at 5.5: the rate-base and acquisition engine is real, but per-share earnings power is being diluted at a pace that offsets it, and consensus is trimming.

The ~2.6-turn discount to the peer median reflects identifiable factors: higher relative leverage and a higher marginal cost of debt, the acute and ongoing dilution, a Negative S&P outlook tied to Quadvest, and California water-supply and inverse-condemnation overhangs. The 3.1% dividend yield is above the peer median and well covered, which provides yield support and anchors a fair value near current levels.

The forward multiple is fair for a utility whose per-share growth is being eroded by share issuance and whose estimates are being revised down. The discount to peers is largely warranted and is not, on its own, a reason the market is wrong. Analyst price targets (low $60, high $67, median $64) sit above the current price, but on a stock with falling estimates and full dilution, we treat those targets as aspirational rather than a confirming floor. We score valuation at 5.0: fairly priced, with the yield offsetting the absence of an appreciation catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

HTO's competitive position is its strongest dimension. The regulated monopoly franchise is durable, and the U.S. water industry remains highly fragmented and municipally dominated, which provides a structural runway for investor-owned consolidators. State fair-market-value statutes (California §2720, Texas PUCT FMV) let acquirers place fair value — often above book — into rate base, directly incentivizing the kind of M&A the Quadvest deal represents. HTO's edge is a management team with deep capital-markets and M&A pedigree and demonstrated acquisition capability.

The disadvantages are relative. HTO carries a heavier financing burden than larger peers like American Water and Essential Utilities, its stock has materially lagged the peer group on TSR (with the gap widening since 2023), and it is concentrated in challenging jurisdictions — California conservation calls and Connecticut streamflow rules requiring reservoir releases by February 2028. The Quadvest acquisition shifts the geographic mix toward higher-growth Texas, a modest improvement to the long-term trajectory. On balance, the franchise is improving at the margin via consolidation, supporting a 6.5 — above peer-average competitive durability but constrained by relative financial scale.

## Risk Assessment

**Quadvest execution and financing.** The deal is pending PUCT approval (expected mid-2026), carries a $21M termination fee under certain circumstances, an outside date of January 7, 2027 (extendable up to 18 months), and the integration risk of a 50-year family-run operator. The deal triggered the S&P outlook cut. If approval slips or fails, HTO holds expensively raised capital and a potential break fee with management bandwidth diverted.

**Perpetual external financing dependence.** The capex plan against a structural FCF deficit forces continuous debt and equity issuance. Rising rates raise the cost of capital faster than rate cases recover it. Any capital-markets disruption or further downgrade directly threatens the capex program and the dividend. In a higher-for-longer rate regime, this is the dominant macro risk for the name.

**Dilution.** Share count rose ~24% in 16 months, and 7.5M forward shares remain to settle by March 2028. This is the most important under-the-hood dynamic; it has converted enterprise growth into flat per-share results.

**California supply and liability.** SJWC operates under a Valley Water 15% voluntary conservation call and a 23% cut to its contractual treated-water delivery schedule through June 2026 while the Anderson reservoir is drained for seismic retrofit. Decoupling (WCMA) largely protects revenue but carries a 20-bp ROE reduction. Inverse-condemnation and wildfire liability in the Santa Cruz Mountains watershed are tail risks where cost recovery is not guaranteed and pollution liability is excluded from general liability insurance.

**PFAS — two-sided.** Roughly $400M of mandated treatment capex by 2029 is the cost side. The offset: HTO is a plaintiff and has collected $25.1M in manufacturer settlements (3M, DuPont, Chemours, Tyco, BASF) with another $5.3M in April 2026 and multi-year tails, booked as regulatory liabilities that offset future costs.

**Litigation.** CWC faces an October 2023 putative class action alleging contaminants in its water (loss not estimable).

**Management turnover.** The entire C-suite turned over within ~12 months, and an additional Item 5.02 officer/director departure was filed June 12, 2026, worth monitoring given the cadence of leadership changes.

### Options Market Signal

Options data carries an unusual configuration that should be read with care. Near-term (1-week) ATM IV of 134.4% sits in steep backwardation versus ~32.7% at one- and three-month tenors, and far above 23.3% historical volatility. This is almost certainly a thin-options expiry artifact rather than a genuine signal of imminent fundamental stress for a low-beta water utility; the one-week reading is an outlier against a flat ~33% term structure further out. The put/call OI ratio of 0.07 is call-heavy, and the three-month skew of −14.3% is also call-tilted, which is mildly constructive and inconsistent with a market bracing for a downside event. The more durable signal is the 15.3% short interest with 10 days to cover, elevated for a utility and consistent with the bearish institutional view on dilution and leverage. We treat the near-term IV spike as non-informative and weight the moderate short interest as confirmation of the cautious fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Quadvest closes in 2H 2026 and proves accretive, validating the consolidation playbook and the finance-heavy management team. Texas HB 2712's future-test-year mechanism accelerates rate recovery in the fastest-growing footprint. The rate-case pipeline (CWC ~$26M, MWC ~$9.5M, TWC SIC) lifts authorized revenue, ROE closes toward the authorized band as regulatory lag normalizes, and the dilution overhang clears once forward shares settle. The dividend streak continues, and a >10% infrastructure holder (GIP/Atlas) that bought ~$3.0M at $59.06 in April 2026 — above today's price — is proven right. Re-rating toward the peer 22.1x multiple on normalized EPS supports the mid-$60s, in line with the $67 analyst high.

**Bear case.** Quadvest approval slips or attaches onerous conditions; HTO is left holding expensive capital and a break-fee risk. Higher-for-longer rates push the marginal cost of debt further above the recovered book, ROE stays stuck near 6.7%, and continued dilution keeps per-share earnings flat. The S&P Negative outlook converts to a downgrade, raising financing costs further. Estimate revisions continue downward, and the stock drifts toward the low-$50s, with the 52-week low of $43.75 as a downside reference in a stress scenario. The market continues to discount rate-base growth for dilution, leverage, and California overhangs, and the TSR underperformance persists.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The reason to own HTO over the next 12 months is its income profile, not appreciation. The dividend is covered at a 57% payout, supported by decoupled, recession-resistant regulated cash flows and a 58-year increase streak — characteristics that hold up well in the stagflationary, high-rate environment that pressures more cyclical names. We do not see a catalyst for material capital appreciation until Quadvest closes accretively and the forward-share dilution clears.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Quadvest closes on schedule (2H 2026) with management quantifying near-term EPS accretion that demonstrably outpaces the share-count increase.
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive on a sustained basis (reversing the −7.0% 90-day trend), and reported ROE moves above ~8% toward authorized levels.
- S&P returns the outlook to Stable, removing the financing-cost overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Quadvest approval fails or is delayed past the outside date, triggering the break-fee and leaving HTO over-capitalized with idle, dilutive equity.
- S&P downgrades from A-, materially raising the marginal cost of debt against an already structural FCF deficit.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration (payout sustained above ~70%) or any signal the increase streak is at risk.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next reassessment at the 2H 2026 Quadvest closing milestone and the Q2/Q3 2026 earnings reports, with the CWC rate-case decision (February 2027) and forward-share settlement (by March 2028) as longer-dated checkpoints.

## Conclusion

H2O America is a defensible regulated water franchise with a rare 58-year dividend-increase record and a credible consolidation strategy backed by a new, M&A-oriented management team. The enterprise is growing, but the equity case is held back by a structural free-cash-flow deficit, ~24% dilution in 16 months that has flattened per-share earnings, rising marginal debt costs, falling near-term estimates, and an S&P Negative outlook tied to an acquisition that has not closed. The forward multiple's discount to peers is largely warranted by these factors rather than evidence of mispricing. We rate HTO a HOLD for income: own it for the covered, recession-resistant yield, but do not expect appreciation until Quadvest demonstrates accretion and the dilution overhang clears. An upgrade to BUY requires evidence that the Texas bet adds more per-share earnings than the equity it consumed.
