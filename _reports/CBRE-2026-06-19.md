**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CBRE is the world's largest commercial real estate (CRE) services and investment firm, with FY2025 revenue of $40.6B (+13.4%), Core EPS of $6.38 (+25%), and Q1 2026 revenue of $10.5B (+18.6% YoY). The investment case rests on a growth driver that has shifted from cyclical to structural: CBRE generated more than $3B of AI/data-center infrastructure-related revenue in 2025 (~$950M in Q1 2026), its critical-infrastructure line is guided to grow 60%+ in 2026, and data-center leasing revenue more than tripled year over year in Q1. This sits on top of a cyclical CRE recovery (improving leasing and capital-markets volumes) and an 82%-resilient revenue base ($33.1B of the $40.6B total).

The valuation is the crux of the upgrade. The headline trailing P/E of 30x is computed on GAAP EPS ($3.85), which is depressed by a wide and persistent stack of M&A integration, wind-down, and Telford add-backs. On Core EPS — the metric management and the Street actually run the company on — the stock trades at roughly 17x the FY26 guidance midpoint ($7.70) against >20% Core EPS growth, a PEG below 1.0. At $131.55, the stock trades below the lowest published sell-side target ($137), roughly 27% below the median ($180.5), with 12 of 13 covering analysts at buy or strong-buy. Management is repurchasing shares at ~$148, about 13% above the current price, explicitly because they view the price as not reflecting the long-term growth trajectory.

I am moving to BUY from a prior HOLD. The growth/valuation combination — a cheap absolute multiple, sub-1 PEG, a confirmed and broad secular demand catalyst, and an asset-light, investment-grade, FCF-generative #1-in-industry compounder — warrants a buy. The legitimate offsets (a Telford UK remediation liability that grew 57% to $321M, front-loaded 2026 earnings creating tough H2 comps, rate sensitivity, and a 4% FX tailwind that could reverse) are real but none is a structural impairment. They argue for disciplined position sizing, not for sitting out.

## Company Overview

CBRE operates in 100+ countries with 155,000+ employees and serves roughly 90% of the Fortune 100. As of January 2025 it reorganized into four reportable segments:

- **Advisory Services** (~$8.7B FY25 revenue): leasing, capital markets (property sales plus mortgage origination), valuation, and loan servicing. The cyclical, transactional core, with segment operating margin back to ~16%.
- **Building Operations & Experience / BOE** (~$23.5B recast): enterprise and local facilities management, property management, the new **Critical Infrastructure** line, and **Industrious** (flexible workspace, fully acquired January 2025). The resilient, outsourcing-driven engine.
- **Project Management** (~$7.4B): delivered through Turner & Townsend (CBRE owns 70%).
- **Real Estate Investments / REI** (~$0.9B): CBRE Investment Management ($155.5B AUM) plus Trammell Crow Company development (~$29.5B in-process/pipeline; ~$900M of embedded gains to monetize).

The strategic logic is diversification across geography, client type, property type, and service line, with a deliberate tilt toward resilient revenue (now ~82% of total) and secularly favored sectors — data centers, logistics, and infrastructure. The moat is scale, an integrated cross-segment offering, a data/research platform, and a balance sheet capable of transformational M&A.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $30.8B (2022) → $31.9B (2023) → $35.8B (2024) → $40.6B (2025). Q1 2026 growth was broad-based — Advisory +22%, BOE +20%, Project Management +15% — and only REI declined on lumpy incentive-fee and development timing. A ~4% FX tailwind (GBP/EUR strength) flattered the Q1 figure and bears watching.

**Profitability:** FY25 net income attributable to CBRE was $1.157B (+19.5%), and Core EBITDA $3.31B (+22.3%). Q1 2026 net income $318M (nearly 2x YoY) and Core EBITDA $831M (+60%). The reported operating margin of ~4.3% looks thin but is structural: roughly 41% of revenue is reimbursable pass-through cost and ~61% of employee costs are client-reimbursed, so the relevant profitability lens is return on capital, not operating margin. ROE is ~14.4% and asset turnover 1.31x.

**Earnings quality caveat:** the gap between GAAP EPS ($3.85) and Core EPS ($6.38) is wide and recurring, driven by integration, business-transformation, wind-down, pension, and Telford add-backs. Many are genuinely non-operating, but the volume of adjustments warrants scrutiny, and intangible amortization from serial M&A is a real economic cost. Cash earnings power sits between the two figures, closer to Core, which still supports a high-teens/low-20s forward multiple rather than the 30x trailing GAAP figure.

**Cash flow:** FY25 FCF was $1.19B (OCF $1.56B less $366M capex); TTM FCF ~$1.7B at 78% conversion (within the 75–85% target). Q1 OCF is seasonally negative on prior-year bonus payouts.

**Balance sheet:** Total assets $30.9B, equity $8.9B, total debt ~$7.6B (LT notes/term loans $5.05B plus commercial paper/ST $2.4B). D/E 2.39, current ratio 1.09. The Altman Z of 2.2 ("grey zone") penalizes the asset-light, high-turnover model and overstates distress risk. Net-debt/EBITDA sits well within the 4.25x covenant. Debt is termed out via investment-grade senior notes (2.5%–5.95% coupons, 2029–2035 maturities) and a $3.5B commercial-paper program backed by a $3.5B revolver. Goodwill ($7.0B) plus intangibles ($2.9B) total ~112% of equity, swollen by Pearce and Industrious — an overhang if those deals underperform, though no impairments have been booked.

**Capital allocation:** ~$2.7B deployed in 2025 — $1.2B Pearce, $468M Industrious, $956M buybacks. Diluted shares fell from 327.7M (2022) to ~297M (Q1 2026), and $4.3B of buyback capacity remains (authorized to 2029). Q1 2026 repurchases averaged ~$148/share.

## Growth Analysis

The growth driver has shifted from a cyclical CRE recovery to a structural AI/infrastructure tailwind, and that shift is the core of the upgrade.

**The AI/data-center infrastructure engine.** The infrastructure-related revenue spans all four segments. The critical-infrastructure line within BOE was $1.7B in 2025 ($580M in Q1 2026). CBRE works on 1,300+ data centers globally. The Meta relationship is a recurring service arrangement — CBRE recruits, trains, and deploys technical staff for Meta's data centers — not a one-time land deal. Management likens this to its 1990s–2000s move into outsourcing, "but much faster." CBRE is a services beneficiary of hyperscaler capex, asset-light and not carrying the construction or obsolescence risk on the underlying infrastructure.

**The pull-forward question, addressed.** The most credible bear objection is that Q1's strength was partly accelerated data-center land-development profits, and that ~40% of full-year EPS is front-loaded into H1. Management's rebuttal is specific: REI full-year guidance was unchanged (the gain is timing, not magnitude), and only ~1/3 of the guidance raise reflects the Q1 beat — the other 2/3 is improved outlook. If the growth were a one-time pull-forward, management would have raised on the beat alone and left the outlook flat. They did the opposite. That cuts against the pull-forward thesis and supports treating the infrastructure-services growth as structural.

**Guidance and estimate revisions.** 2026 Core EPS guidance was raised to $7.60–$7.80 (>20% growth at midpoint) from $7.30–$7.60. The earnings-surprise record is strong — three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, including a 39.8% Q1 beat. On revisions, the 90-day trend for the figures that matter is positive: current-year +2.0% and next-year +1.9%. The 30-day prints on the two near quarters (-0.3% and -0.7%) are noise-level and should not override the annual direction or the +60% infrastructure guide. The prior HOLD leaned heavily on those 30-day flattening figures, which was internally inconsistent with the positive annual revisions it also cited.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation case is built on Core EPS, not trailing GAAP, because GAAP EPS is depressed by recurring M&A and Telford add-backs and is not the metric on which the company or the Street values the business.

- **Forward P/E ~17x** on the FY26 Core EPS midpoint of $7.70.
- **PEG <1.0** against >20% Core EPS growth.
- Even haircutting Core EPS for intangible amortization (a real cost), the cash-earnings multiple lands in the high-teens to low-20s — comfortably below the 30x trailing screen.

**Peer context.** The Real Estate Services peer set is heterogeneous and the medians are distorted: CoStar trades at 545x and Marcus & Millichap is unprofitable, while JLL sits at 16x. CBRE at ~17x forward Core EPS is in line with JLL on a much larger, more diversified, more resilient-revenue base, and it carries the cleanest AI-infrastructure exposure in the group. The peer screen does not flag CBRE as expensive.

**The analyst-target reconciliation.** I do not adopt the median $180.5 as my base case — on a name where I want to anchor to my own normalized-earnings work, the median implies ~23.4x forward Core EPS, which prices in continued beats. My base case is ~$165–170, roughly 21–22x the FY26 Core EPS midpoint, which I view as a fair multiple for >20% growth with a structural catalyst and IG credit. That base case sits above the lowest target ($137) and below the median, which I think is the honest place to be: more constructive than the prior report's $145–150 (which I now view as too conservative given the structural growth confirmation), but not as aggressive as the highest targets ($200) that fully extrapolate the data-center cycle.

The price/value disconnect is reinforced by management's own action: repurchasing at ~$148, with the explicit statement that the price "does not reflect the sustained long-term growth trajectory." A genuinely cheap multiple here is paired with an identified catalyst (the infrastructure-services growth plus the CRE capital-markets recovery), which distinguishes this from a value trap.

## Competitive Landscape

The global CRE services market is consolidating around a handful of diversified leaders — CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, Newmark — and CBRE is the largest by revenue. As occupiers consolidate vendors and demand integrated, cross-border solutions, scale advantages compound. CBRE's moat is its global platform, data/research depth, and balance-sheet capacity for transformational M&A.

The competitive trajectory is improving rather than merely stable. CBRE is positioning at the front of the AI/data-center services buildout, with the Pearce acquisition adding telecom/power/critical-infrastructure technical services. The trajectory points up.

The longer-term competitive debate is AI disintermediation — whether proptech could compress brokerage data/analysis work and bypass brokers on smaller transactions. Management argues the human, strategic element of brokerage protects the transactional businesses and that AI is a net tailwind via data-center demand. This is the key bear thesis to monitor, but on current evidence AI is additive to CBRE's revenue, not subtractive. I score competitive position at 8.0, a notch below the prior 8.5 to reflect that the disintermediation debate is unresolved even as near-term positioning improves.

## Risk Assessment

- **Telford fire-safety remediation (UK):** A legally binding 2023 obligation to remediate life-critical fire-safety issues on Telford Homes–built buildings. The liability grew ~57% in one year — $204M (2024) → $321M (Dec 2025), $303M as of Q1 2026 — and management is winding down the Telford construction self-delivery business. Cost estimates are "subjective, highly complex" and could rise. Not a solvency threat, but a recurring drag on GAAP results and the cleanest tail risk to monitor.
- **Cyclicality / rate sensitivity:** Advisory is tied to capital-markets conditions, and guidance explicitly assumes the 10-yr Treasury stays in the 4–4.5% band. A spike above that band would slow transactions. The resilient revenue base cushions but does not eliminate this risk.
- **Front-loaded earnings / tough comps:** ~40% of 2026 EPS is expected in H1, so H2 prints will optically decelerate against tough comps even if the business stays healthy.
- **FX:** ~43% of revenue is non-USD (GBP ~14%, EUR ~10%); the 4% Q1 tailwind could reverse.
- **Turner & Townsend put option:** Minority partners can require CBRE to buy additional shares at fair value between 2027–2030 ($447M mezzanine equity) — a future cash call.
- **Goodwill/intangibles:** ~112% of equity, swollen by serial M&A; impairment risk if deals underperform.
- **GSE loss-sharing:** $47.8B of Fannie Mae DUS loans subject to one-third loss sharing ($81M reserve).
- **Regional softness:** A noted slowdown in Middle East investment-management fundraising and softer non-data-center corporate capital investment.

Risk factors were unchanged in the Q1 10-Q versus the 10-K, and there were no material legal-proceedings changes. The 5.5 risk score reflects the Telford liability trajectory and rate sensitivity as the two specific, partly-unpriced overhangs; neither is a structural impairment.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-mildly-constructive. Near-term ATM IV is 35.0% vs. 1-year HV of 31.0% (modestly rich, +3.9pp spread), but the IV percentile within the current chain is just 2% — vol is cheap by its own recent history, consistent with a complacent setup rather than a market pricing imminent downside. Put/call skew is balanced (1.3%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.51, and the OI ratio collapsed to 0.06 from 1.17 a month ago, indicating the prior put-heavy positioning has unwound. The term structure is in contango (35.0% near vs. 37.9% three-month), normal and non-stressed. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±8.1%. The absence of elevated put demand on a stock trading below the lowest analyst target suggests the bearish case is not crowded into options.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($180–200).** The CRE capital-markets recovery continues, Advisory delivers high-teens SOP growth, and the AI/data-center infrastructure-services engine compounds — critical infrastructure sustains a high growth rate into 2027 as the hyperscaler buildout broadens. Core EPS reaches the top of guidance and beats into 2027 ($8.86 consensus next year), the multiple expands toward 24x on durable >15% growth, and the ~$900M of Trammell Crow embedded gains monetize. The buyback continues to shrink the share count below 290M.

**Bear case ($110–120, roughly -8% to -16% from current).** Rates spike above 4.5%, slowing capital-markets and leasing activity; the FX tailwind reverses into a headwind; H2 comps disappoint as the data-center land-development gains prove to be more pull-forward than management acknowledged; and the Telford liability rises further, adding to the GAAP/Core gap. The multiple compresses toward JLL's ~16x on slowing growth.

The asymmetry favors the long. The bear case is a moderate drawdown to a still-defensible valuation; the base and bull cases offer 25%+ and 35%+ upside. The downside is bounded by an IG balance sheet, 82% resilient revenue, and management buying stock above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $165** — ~21.4x the FY26 Core EPS midpoint of $7.70. I anchor below the consensus median ($180.5, ~23.4x) to avoid fully pricing in continued beats, and above the prior report's $145–150, which underweighted the structural growth confirmation. This represents ~25% upside from $131.55.

**Bull case target: $190** — ~24.7x FY26 Core EPS on durable >15% growth into 2027 (~44% upside).

**Bear case target: $115** — ~15x FY26 Core EPS on a rate-driven slowdown and FX reversal (~-13% from current).

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, tied to the Q2/Q3 2026 prints (which will test the H2-comp and pull-forward debate), the 2026 Core EPS delivery against guidance, and the trajectory of the critical-infrastructure line.

**Upside/downside from current ($131.55):** +25% to base, +44% to bull, -13% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Critical-infrastructure revenue growth decelerating materially below the guided 60% in 2026, or data-center leasing growth stalling — this would undercut the structural-growth premise.
2. 2026 Core EPS guidance cut below $7.60, or two consecutive quarters of negative current-year estimate revisions (not noise-level), signaling the growth has reversed.
3. Telford remediation liability rising past ~$450M, or a goodwill impairment on Pearce/Industrious, indicating the M&A-funded growth is destroying rather than creating value.

## Conclusion

CBRE is the #1 player in a consolidating global CRE services market, trading at ~17x forward Core EPS against >20% Core EPS growth, with a structural AI/data-center infrastructure-services tailwind layered on a cyclical capital-markets recovery. The stock sits below the lowest published sell-side target and ~27% below the median, with management repurchasing shares ~13% above the current price. The combination of a cheap multiple, a sub-1 PEG, a confirmed and broad secular catalyst, and an asset-light, IG-rated, FCF-generative business warrants a buy.

The real offsets — the Telford liability's 57% increase to $321M, front-loaded 2026 earnings and tough H2 comps, rate sensitivity, and a reversible FX tailwind — are legitimate and argue for disciplined position sizing rather than for sitting out. None is a structural impairment, and the options market is not pricing imminent stress. I rate CBRE a **BUY**, sized with discipline, base-case target $165 (~25% upside) over 12–18 months.
