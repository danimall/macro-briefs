**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dycom has remade itself over roughly six months. What was a pure-play U.S. specialty contractor for telecom carriers is now a two-segment business with a debt-funded data-center building-systems arm, built through the December 2025 acquisition of Power Solutions ($2.0B) and the announced May 2026 acquisition of National Technology Integrators ($275M). The strategic logic is to diversify away from increasingly consolidated telecom-carrier capex and to capture hyperscaler electrical/low-voltage demand at structurally higher margins (Building Systems posted a 17.7% EBITDA margin in Q1 FY2027 versus 12.3% for the legacy Communications segment). The results so far validate the pivot operationally: Q1 FY2027 revenue rose 56% to $1.96B (organic Communications still grew ~25%), adjusted EPS jumped ~85% to $4.42 against a $2.81 consensus, total backlog climbed to $11.9B from $7.8B a year earlier, and management raised full-year guidance.

The growth thesis is intact and well-supported. EPS estimate revisions are running +17% (current year) and +16.7% (next year) over 30 days, the company has beaten consensus in four consecutive quarters, and the data-center demand theme is corroborated across the broader infrastructure layer. The stock has responded: DY has nearly doubled over the past twelve months and trades near $465, off a post-earnings high of $566. At ~28x forward earnings (against a current-year estimate of $16.68 and next-year $20.04), the valuation has caught up with the fundamentals, and the burden of proof now sits with continued flawless execution.

The case against owning fresh shares is concentration and balance-sheet transformation. AT&T was 25.4% of FY2026 revenue and is rising further as it absorbs Lumen's fiber business; the top four customers are roughly 58% of revenue, and most master service agreements are cancellable for convenience with no volume commitments. Long-term debt rose from $933M to $2.81B to fund the pivot, ~$2.34B of it variable-rate, with goodwill and intangibles now concentrated 77-81% in an unproven six-month-old segment. We rate DY a HOLD on a growth thesis: own it for the demonstrated revenue and earnings momentum, but the current price already embeds successful integration, leaving limited margin of safety against a customer-concentration event, an AI-capex deceleration, or an integration stumble.

## Company Overview

Dycom provides specialty contracting services (labor, tools, and equipment) for digital infrastructure, telecom, and utilities. The fiscal year ends the last Saturday in January. As of late FY2026 the company reports two segments:

- **Communications (~80% of revenue):** The legacy business: fiber-to-the-home deployments, rural and federal fiber programs, wireless modernization, and underground facility locating. FY2026 segment revenue was $5.45B.
- **Building Systems (~20% of recent quarterly revenue):** Created by the Power Solutions acquisition. Provides electrical, energy-management, security, and fire-safety systems for data centers and critical facilities, concentrated in the DC/Maryland/Virginia data-center corridor.

Dycom is a contractor to telecom carriers and to general contractors building data centers. It does not own networks or operate data centers; AT&T, Verizon, Lumen, Comcast, and Charter are customers whose networks Dycom builds and maintains. Its data-center exposure is the provision of electrical/low-voltage building infrastructure and supporting fiber. The competitive set is Quanta Services (larger, more diversified) and MasTec, with a long tail of regional contractors. The 10-K acknowledges "relatively few barriers to entry" and competition from customers' in-house crews, a structurally low-moat industry where national footprint, scale, and carrier relationships are the primary advantages.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is growing and accelerating.** FY2023 through FY2026 revenue moved $3.81B → $4.18B → $4.70B → $5.55B (FY2026 +17.9% YoY, roughly +8% excluding acquisitions and the 53rd week). Q1 FY2027 revenue of $1.96B included ~$395M from Building Systems, but the legacy Communications business still grew 24.7% organically, a strong reading independent of the acquisition.

**Profitability is improving on operating leverage.** FY2026 net income was $281M (5.1% margin); adjusted EBITDA was $738M, or 13.3% of revenue, up from 12.3%. Q1 FY2027 adjusted EBITDA margin held at 13.4%. The mix shift toward the higher-margin segment is a genuine, durable improvement to consolidated margins if the data-center demand holds. ROE is roughly 15%; ROA roughly 4.7%.

**The balance sheet was transformed.** The debt increase was structured as a $1.54B Term Loan A, an $800M Term Loan B, and $500M of 4.50% senior notes due 2029. Goodwill rose from $330M to $1.44B and intangibles from $220M to $926M, with Building Systems now holding 77% of goodwill and 81.5% of intangibles. The Altman Z-Score of 1.99 sits in the grey zone and D/E of 2.22 is elevated for the sector, but both largely reflect deliberate, deal-driven leverage rather than operating deterioration. The company was in covenant compliance (net-leverage covenant 4.50x, interest-coverage minimum 2.50x), held $709M of cash at year-end with $746M of revolver availability, and the debt funded a cash-generative, higher-margin business.

**Cash generation is solid but working-capital intensive.** FY2026 operating cash flow was $642M (up from $349M) and free cash flow was $402M (7.2% margin), with OCF exceeding net income. The business consumes working capital as it grows because costs are paid before billing and collection; Q1 is seasonally cash-negative (operating cash flow of –$24.6M in Q1 FY2027). DSO improved to 96 days from 111.

**Quality-of-earnings items to monitor.** Dycom uses a non-recourse accounts-receivable sale program and a $349M supplier-finance (reverse-factoring) obligation, both of which flatter reported DSO and working capital. Cost-to-cost (percentage-of-completion) revenue jumped to 22% of Q1 FY2027 revenue from roughly 1%, reflecting Building Systems' fixed-price work and introducing more estimate-driven revenue and earnings volatility. Recurring equipment-sale gains ($27M in FY2026) are a real but non-core EBITDA contributor that management adds back. None of these are alarming, but they mean reported metrics run modestly ahead of pure organic cash conversion.

**Capital allocation** is oriented to M&A: no dividend, modest buybacks ($30M in FY2026, $36M in Q1 FY2027 at ~$360/share), and FY2027 capex guided to $210-220M. The clear priority is debt-funded acquisitions in data-center building systems.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, all currently positive.

**Backlog momentum.** Total backlog rose to $11.9B at Q1 FY2027 from $9.5B at FY2026 year-end, with $6.4B expected within twelve months and Building Systems contributing ~$1.1B. Backlog is non-binding (customers have no volume commitments and can cancel MSAs for convenience), so it is a directional indicator rather than a contractual guarantee, but the trajectory is unambiguous.

**Estimate revisions.** This is the strongest forward signal. Current-year EPS estimates have risen +22.6% over 90 days, and next-year estimates +19.2% over 90 days, with the 30-day moves consistent with the longer trend. Combined with revenue growth above 50% in Q1 and a four-quarter streak of beats (the most recent a 57.4% upside surprise), this revision velocity is among the more reliable predictors of continued earnings momentum as consensus catches up to the post-acquisition reality. The signal applies with full force here because the stock trades below 30x forward earnings; the revisions are not simply ratifying a premium multiple.

**Structural demand.** The data-center / AI-infrastructure buildout is corroborated across the broader infrastructure layer (power generation, transmission, electrical equipment, and structural materials all show confirming demand signals), which lends the data-center building-systems thesis more durability than a single-company story. The legacy fiber business also benefits from multi-year FTTH deployments and federal digital-divide programs, though the latter carries government-funding risk.

The principal caveat is that a meaningful share of the headline growth is acquired rather than organic, and the higher-margin segment that is driving the mix improvement is six months old with no track record through a demand downturn. We score Growth Prospects 8.0, the single most reliable dimension and the one where DY's evidence is strongest, but with explicit acknowledgment that the durability of the data-center leg is unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$465, DY trades at roughly 28x the current-year EPS estimate of $16.68 and ~23x the next-year estimate of $20.04. On trailing EPS of $9.56 the multiple is ~44x, but that trailing figure predates the acquisition-driven earnings step-up and is the wrong anchor; forward and next-year metrics are the appropriate lens.

Against peers in Construction & Engineering, the picture is mixed. The peer-median P/E is 32.9 (Jacobs 36.8, Everus 32.9, Fluor 22.8, Aecom 14.5), so DY's ~28x current-year / ~23x next-year sits below the median and is not obviously stretched relative to the cohort. On a growth-adjusted basis, with next-year EPS growth of ~20% and a forward P/E in the low-to-mid 20s, the PEG is in the 1.2-1.4 range, reasonable but not cheap.

The analyst consensus targets (low $610, high $700, mean $637) sit well above the current price, implying ~30%+ upside. On a high-momentum stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x forward multiple, consensus targets tend to lag and act more as floors than ceilings, and the revision pattern supports that interpretation. We weight this constructively but not decisively, because the same targets embed full credit for the data-center integration.

The reasons to hold valuation at a middling score rather than rate it high: the multiple has fully caught up to the fundamental improvement; the industry is structurally low-moat; a non-trivial share of earnings power now runs through estimate-driven percentage-of-completion accounting in an unproven segment; and the stock has compressed ~18% from its $566 post-earnings high, which signals that sophisticated holders are not extrapolating the Q1 surge linearly. A low trailing P/E is never sufficient for a high valuation score, and here the inverse caution applies: the cheapness case is incomplete because the durability of the improvement is not yet proven. We score Valuation 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Dycom's moat is shallow by the industry's own description, with "relatively few barriers to entry" and competition from customers' in-house crews. The durable advantages are national footprint, the ability to manage large, complex multi-year programs, and long-standing relationships with the largest carriers. Against Quanta Services and MasTec, Dycom is the more telecom-concentrated of the premier listed digital-infrastructure contractors; Quanta is larger and more diversified.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin. The Building Systems pivot moves Dycom into a higher-margin, structurally-growing data-center niche where it is establishing a platform via Power Solutions and NTI in the DC/Maryland/Virginia corridor. Organic Communications growth of ~25% suggests the company is at minimum holding, likely gaining, share within its core. Set against that, customer consolidation (AT&T absorbing Lumen, Verizon absorbing Frontier) reduces Dycom's negotiating leverage and concentrates its exposure, and the data-center customer base is itself "highly concentrated" per the filing, trading one concentration risk for another. The net trajectory is modestly positive but not a wide or widening moat. We score Competitive Position 6.0, applying the standard caution that the competitive-position signal warrants confirmation rather than standalone weight.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is the central, intensifying risk.** AT&T was 25.4% of FY2026 revenue, up from 16.9% in FY2024; Verizon 14.0%; Lumen 10.8%; Comcast 7.4% — the top four roughly 58%. Carrier consolidation is concentrating exposure further, not diversifying it: AT&T's acquisition of Lumen's mass-markets fiber business (closed February 2026) pushes pro-forma AT&T exposure above 25%. Most MSAs are cancellable for convenience with little or no notice and carry no volume commitments. A single customer's capex decision or a post-merger contractor switch could materially impair revenue.

**Integration and impairment risk on the pivot.** Two acquisitions in roughly six months created a Building Systems segment carrying ~$1.1B of goodwill and ~$755M of intangibles. The 10-K flags that hyperscaler/data-center customers are also highly concentrated and may bring "less advantageous contractual requirements." An AI-capex slowdown or a botched integration could trigger impairment against an unproven unit.

**Leverage and rate exposure.** Roughly $2.34B of variable-rate term loans price at Term SOFR plus spread (~5.4%); Q1 interest expense more than doubled YoY to $35.5M. Covenants tighten from 4.50x to 4.00x net leverage after roughly Q1 FY2028. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, the variable-rate exposure is a recurring earnings headwind.

**Contract and cost risk.** Most work is per-unit or per-task priced, with Dycom bearing cost overruns on labor, tariff-exposed materials (steel, aluminum, fiber), and fuel with limited pass-through. The jump in cost-to-cost contracts to 22% of revenue increases estimate-driven earnings volatility.

**Other.** Rural-fiber growth depends on federal program funding vulnerable to budget reform; the work is inherently dangerous with self-insurance and multiemployer-pension exposure (now including Power Solutions' union plans); a multi-year ERP implementation adds execution/control risk. Governance carries entrenchment features (classified board, blank-check preferred, restricted special-meeting rights).

These are mostly specific, partially unpriced risks attached to a high-expectations stock, which is why we score Risk Factors 4.5, consistent with the prior report's reading and reflecting that concentration is worsening, not resolving.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 50.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 44.1%, a modestly rich 5.8-point spread, with IV sitting at only the 6th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in contango (50.0% near-term vs. 58.7% at six months), a normal-to-complacent shape. Put/call skew is +4.0% at the front month (elevated put demand) with a 1.68 put/call open-interest ratio, indicating hedging interest, while the put/call volume ratio of 0.20 is call-heavy on flow. The nearest expiry implies a ±10.2% move. The signal is consistent with a stock that has run hard and where holders are buying downside protection rather than pricing an imminent collapse: the contango and low IV percentile argue against acute near-term stress, while the front-month put skew flags the concentration/integration tail that the fundamental analysis identifies.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The data-center pivot succeeds. Building Systems compounds at a 17.7%+ EBITDA margin, NTI closes and extends the platform, and the legacy fiber business sustains ~20%+ organic growth on FTTH and federal programs. Estimate revisions continue and consensus marches toward and past the next-year $20.04 EPS. Backlog conversion holds, leverage de-risks as cash flow services the term loans, and the stock re-rates toward analyst targets. Bull case ~$640-700, in line with consensus high.

### Bear Case
A customer-concentration event materializes (AT&T cuts fiber capex post-Lumen integration or switches contractors) and Communications revenue contracts. Simultaneously, AI/data-center capex decelerates, the Building Systems thesis cracks, and a goodwill/intangible impairment hits the six-month-old segment. Variable-rate interest expense compounds the earnings pressure. A high-expectations stock de-rates sharply. Bear case ~$330-360, roughly the FY2026 pre-spike trading range, ~25-30% below current levels.

### Base Case
The pivot largely works but at a measured pace; organic Communications growth normalizes from the Q1 ~25% toward mid-to-high single digits as the 53rd-week and acquisition tailwinds lap; Building Systems integrates without major incident; estimates hold near current levels. The stock tracks earnings growth at a roughly flat forward multiple, drifting toward ~$520-560 over twelve months, meaningful but not the consensus 30%+ upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth thesis is real and supported by the strongest forward signals (estimate revisions, backlog, beat streak), but the current price embeds successful execution and offers limited margin of safety against well-identified concentration and integration risks.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it is appropriately higher. The new data since the prior reports — the record Q1 print, raised guidance, the strong current-year revision velocity, and backlog at $11.9B — strengthens the growth leg materially and arrests the prior score decline. The offsets that have kept this from a BUY are unchanged or worsening: AT&T concentration is rising, the balance sheet carries ~$2.8B of mostly variable-rate debt, and the stock now sits ~28x forward after nearly doubling. The stock's ~18% pullback from its $566 high is not, in itself, thesis deterioration (fundamentals improved over the same window) but it does signal that the market is not extrapolating the Q1 surge, and we do not see a specific mispricing large enough to upgrade.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A second clean quarter (Q2 FY2027) confirming Building Systems margins ≥17% and organic Communications growth ≥15%, with FY2027 guidance reaffirmed or raised.
- Evidence of customer diversification — top-customer concentration declining below ~22% of revenue, or material new hyperscaler/non-AT&T contract wins — that reduces the single-point-of-failure risk.
- A pullback toward the low-$400s (~20x next-year EPS) that restores a margin of safety while estimates remain on their current upward trajectory.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut, a missed quarter, or a quarter where organic Communications growth turns negative, particularly any sign of AT&T capex reduction or a contractor switch post-Lumen integration.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment in Building Systems, or evidence of a botched integration (margin compression below the Communications level, customer attrition).
- A breach of, or material tightening toward, the net-leverage covenant as it steps down to 4.00x, or a sharp deterioration in free cash flow conversion.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2027 earnings release (expected late August 2026), which should confirm whether the NTI deal closed on schedule and whether the Building Systems margin and backlog trajectory hold for a second quarter.

## Conclusion

Dycom has executed a credible, well-timed strategic pivot into data-center building systems, and the early operating results are strong: 56% revenue growth, an 85% jump in adjusted EPS, a four-quarter beat streak, backlog expansion to $11.9B, and a strongly rising current-year estimate-revision cycle. The growth thesis is the most defensible part of the story and is corroborated by broad-based infrastructure-demand signals. The valuation has caught up (~28x current-year and ~23x next-year earnings, a PEG near 1.3, below the peer median but no longer cheap) and the company's risk profile has intensified through rising customer concentration, ~$2.8B of mostly variable-rate debt, and concentrated impairment risk in a six-month-old segment. On balance the risk/reward is fairly priced. We rate DY a HOLD on the growth thesis: appropriate to own for the demonstrated earnings momentum, but we would wait for a second quarter of Building Systems confirmation or a pullback toward the low-$400s before committing fresh capital, and we would exit on any sign of AT&T capex retrenchment or data-center integration failure.
