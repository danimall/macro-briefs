**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ESAB is a global fabrication-technology manufacturer (welding and cutting equipment, consumables, gas control, and cutting robotics) that became an independent public company in April 2022 via spin-off from Colfax. It runs a Danaher-pedigree operating model (EBXai) under Executive Chairman Mitchell Rales, pairing operational self-help with serial bolt-on acquisitions. The model is delivering on the margin line: FY25 adjusted EBITDA margin reached 19.7% (Core ex-Russia 20.0%), up from ~16.8% at the time of the spin. It is not yet delivering on organic demand: organic revenue was −1.1% in FY25 and −1.9% in Q1 2026, with reported growth coming almost entirely from acquisitions and currency.

The defining recent event is the ~$1.45B acquisition of Eddyfi Technologies (non-destructive inspection/monitoring), which closed June 1, 2026. This step up the fabrication value chain into a higher-margin, higher-growth adjacency nearly doubled gross debt to ~$2.06B, required ~$318M of equity (including a dilutive 6.5% mandatory convertible preferred), and pushed the Altman Z-score into the grey zone. Eddyfi's standalone and pro-forma financials are not yet public, with required SEC filings due by roughly mid-August 2026; investors are underwriting limited disclosed data on the largest deal in ESAB's standalone history.

The stock has fallen from a February high of $137.42 to the high-$90s/low-$100s, a drawdown exceeding 25%. Forward earnings power (~$5.80 current-year, $6.86 next-year consensus) implies roughly 17x next-year EPS, which is undemanding versus the peer median and versus ESAB's own history. But the combination of negative organic volume, tariff-driven margin dilution, leverage step-up, a C-suite in transition, and an information gap on the transformational deal leaves the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate ESAB a HOLD.

## Company Overview

ESAB traces its brand to 1904 and the invention of the coated welding electrode. It operates in ~150 countries with ~10,300 employees across two reporting segments: Americas (~40% of sales) and EMEA & APAC (~60%), plus a Gas Control Equipment reporting unit used for goodwill testing. Product mix runs roughly 66% consumables (lower-margin, recurring) and 34% equipment (higher-margin, more cyclical). About 80% of revenue is generated outside the U.S., and management estimates ~52% comes from high-growth markets (South America, Eastern Europe, India, Asia-Pacific, Middle East) it expects to grow at more than twice the developed-market rate. The company sizes its addressable market at ~$45B by 2028.

The competitive set is fragmented and niche-segmented. The principal large rivals are Lincoln Electric and the welding business of Illinois Tool Works; no single competitor spans ESAB's full product range. The moat rests on brand, distribution reach, application engineering, and the EBXai operating discipline. The investment thesis depends less on industry growth (mature and cyclical in developed markets) than on emerging-market exposure plus the compounder engine of margin self-help and accretive M&A.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025.** Revenue was $2.843B, up 3.7% YoY. The composition is the key story: organic −1.1% (volume −$60.8M on Russia weakness and Americas tariff drag, partly offset by +$29.4M pricing), acquisitions +4.2%, FX +0.6%. GAAP operating income was $412.2M (14.5% margin) and net income $226.8M, down from $264.8M in FY24 on higher interest, restructuring, and acquisition amortization. Adjusted EBITDA was $559.7M (19.7% margin, +40 bps), with Core Adjusted EBITDA ex-Russia at 20.0%. Gross margin declined 100 bps to 36.9%, primarily because tariff price pass-through inflates the revenue denominator without proportional profit. Free cash flow was $213.3M (7.5% margin); operating cash flow fell to $260.6M from $355.4M on working-capital build, higher interest, and deal costs.

The multi-year revenue trend ($2.59B in 2022, $2.77B in 2023, $2.74B in 2024, $2.84B in 2025) shows a flattish organic base with M&A layered on. The margin trajectory is upward, which is what the compounder model is designed to produce.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue rose 9.9% to $745.6M, but again organic was −1.9%, with acquisitions +6.3% and FX +5.5%. GAAP net income from continuing operations fell to $51.8M from $72.6M (6.9% margin vs 10.7%); diluted EPS dropped to $0.82 from $1.14. Non-GAAP EPS of ~$1.31 came in roughly 4.3% below consensus (Phase 1: $1.37 estimate vs $1.31 actual). Adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to 18.3% from 19.7%, with management citing EWM acquisition dilution, tariff pass-through, and higher freight/oil costs tied to Middle East logistics and the Strait of Hormuz. Interest expense jumped to $25.6M from $16.8M, including a $4.8M bridge-loan termination charge.

**Balance sheet.** Pre-Eddyfi (12/31/25), long-term debt was ~$1.2B with D/E of 1.18 and a 5.40% weighted-average rate. After the March 2026 issuance of $1.0B of 5.625% notes due 2031 to pre-fund Eddyfi, total debt reached ~$2.06B and cash rose to ~$1.0B (subsequently deployed). The Altman Z-score of 2.18 sits in the grey zone, driven by $1.95B of goodwill and $673M of intangibles inflating the asset base relative to equity. The Piotroski score of 5/5 on available signals indicates the operation is fundamentally sound: positive net income, ROA, and operating cash flow, with OCF exceeding net income and a current ratio of 1.9. ROA is 4.9% and ROE 10.8%, solid but not exceptional given the acquisitive, goodwill-heavy structure.

The earnings-surprise record is a mild caution: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter missing by 4.3%. This company has not been comfortably clearing the bar.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth rates overstate the underlying business. Both FY25 (+3.7%) and Q1'26 (+9.9%) reported growth came entirely from acquisitions and currency; organic volume has been negative across multiple periods. ESAB is buying growth and engineering margins, not riding end-market demand. That is a legitimate model (it is the Danaher template), but it raises the bar on capital allocation and integration, and it makes the company more sensitive to leverage and rate costs than an organic grower would be.

Estimate revisions are mixed. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $5.80 (roughly flat over 90 days, −0.4%), and next-year consensus rose to $6.86 from $6.50, a +5.4% to +5.7% revision over 90/30 days that may reflect expected Eddyfi accretion. Next-quarter estimates also ticked up (+0.8% over 30 days, +2.8% over 90 days). This is a modest upward drift on the out-year rather than a velocity that signals a fundamental turn. The current-quarter estimate has been cut 5.5% over 90 days, consistent with the soft industrial backdrop.

The Eddyfi deal is the principal growth lever. At ~$240M FY25 revenue and ~$270M projected 2026, it adds roughly 8-9% to the top line and pushes ESAB into NDT/asset-integrity inspection, a structurally higher-growth, higher-margin space adjacent to its joining business. Strategically this builds a broader fabrication-lifecycle platform (joining → inspection/monitoring) and reduces dependence on welding-consumable cyclicality. The execution and accretion case rests on management estimates because target financials are not yet public.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing EPS of $4.10 the stock trades at ~26x, but trailing earnings are depressed by acquisition amortization, restructuring, and the bridge-loan charge. The more relevant frame is forward: at ~$99-106 against $5.80 current-year and $6.86 next-year consensus, ESAB trades at roughly 17-18x current-year and ~15x next-year, undemanding relative to the peer median.

Peer comparables in industrial machinery show wide dispersion: JBT Marel 38.2x, Gates 28.2x, Enpro 163.1x, Middleby 21.5x, Esco 62.2x, with a peer median P/E of 38.2x. ESAB's ~26x trailing, and meaningfully lower forward multiple, sits well below that median. The discount is partly justified: organic growth is negative, leverage has stepped up, and a transformational deal carries integration risk. But a ~15x next-year multiple for a business expanding adjusted EBITDA margins toward 20%, with a credible diversification into a higher-growth adjacency, is not stretched.

I am cautious about over-weighting the cheapness. The valuation discount reflects real concerns (stalled organics, tariff drag, leverage), and a low forward multiple absent an organic re-acceleration catalyst can persist. The analyst price-target range ($117 low, $135.4 mean, $137.5 median, $154 high) implies meaningful upside from current levels, but analyst sentiment is degrading (one strong-buy downgrade and a new strong-sell appeared in the trailing window), so I treat those targets as aspirational rather than as a near-term floor. The most likely path is that the stock re-rates only once Eddyfi financials are disclosed (mid-August) and organic volumes stabilize. A score of 6.0 reflects reasonable value without a clear, dated catalyst to close the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

ESAB holds leading positions across its niches in a fragmented market, with Lincoln Electric and ITW as the principal large competitors. The competitive trajectory is roughly stable: ESAB is not visibly losing share, and the Eddyfi move expands its addressable footprint into NDT/inspection (where Baker Hughes' Waygate and Mistras operate). Margin expansion toward 20% suggests pricing and operational discipline are holding even amid tariff cost inflation.

The relative-performance record is a genuine positive: TSR of 252.2 versus a peer-group index of 165.7 since the 2022 spin indicates the market has rewarded the compounder execution. The structural emerging-market tilt (~52% of sales) is a long-term differentiator versus more developed-market-concentrated peers, assuming those markets grow as management projects. The competitive position is improving at the edges (Eddyfi adjacency, margin gains) but constrained by negative organic volume and the maturity of developed-market welding demand. A 6.0 reflects a solid, stable-to-slightly-improving position rather than a widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Organic stagnation masked by M&A and FX** is the central risk. If end-markets stay soft and the company cannot reignite organic volume, the model depends entirely on continued accretive dealmaking and margin self-help, a higher-risk dependence in a higher-rate environment.

**Leverage and financing risk** stepped up materially. Total debt with a Z-score in the grey zone and a ~5.45% weighted-average rate means interest expense is now a meaningful drag. The higher-for-longer rate path is a direct headwind to a debt-funded compounder.

**Integration and goodwill risk** is elevated by the $1.95B goodwill balance and the Eddyfi pivot. EWM is already explicitly diluting margins during integration. A sustained end-market decline could pressure goodwill.

**Information gap on Eddyfi.** Standalone and pro-forma financials are not public until ~mid-August 2026, so the accretion/leverage math is management-estimated only.

**Tariff exposure** cuts both ways. Tariffs have reduced Americas volumes and diluted gross margin. A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize the tariffs creates potential refunds (not booked), but the administration plans new tariffs under alternative authority. The outcome is genuinely uncertain.

**Legacy and stranded-asset items.** Inherited Colfax asbestos liabilities ($263M, partly offset by $232M insurance asset) show rising claim counts (1,498 filed in Q1'26 vs 887 YoY), recorded only on a 15-year horizon. Russia exposure includes ~$42M trapped cash and a ~$108M translation loss that would crystallize on exit.

**Governance items.** The Rales Holders' registration rights are a stock-overhang risk. The C-suite is in transition: the CEO doubled as interim CFO into Q1'26, the head of Fabrication Technology resigned in May 2026, and the company is hiring Danaher/Veralto/GE alumni. Concentrated leadership change during a transformational acquisition adds execution risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 50.0% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 43.2%, and the term structure is in backwardation (50.0% near-term vs 43.6% long-term), which typically signals an imminent catalyst: here, the mid-August Eddyfi financial disclosure and the Q2 print. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (−1.3%), and the put/call OI ratio of 1.11 is not extreme, so positioning is not aggressively bearish. The implied one-month move of ~11% frames the near-term uncertainty. The reported ATM IV spike to 213.5% in the delta snapshot appears to be a thin-options data artifact and is not treated as informative. The market expects volatility around upcoming events without a strong directional bias, consistent with our balanced HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$135-154).** Eddyfi closes the disclosure gap with strong standalone financials and proves accretive; the NDT/inspection adjacency re-rates ESAB toward the higher-growth-platform multiple its peers carry. Organic volumes stabilize and turn modestly positive as the industrial cycle firms, tariff refunds materialize as upside, and margin self-help carries adjusted EBITDA above 20%. Next-year EPS of $6.86 at a ~20-22x multiple supports $137-150, broadly matching the analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$80-85).** Organic volume stays negative, end-markets soften further, and Eddyfi integration disappoints or its disclosed financials underwhelm. Leverage and interest costs compress earnings, tariffs are re-imposed under new authority, and a goodwill impairment crystallizes. The stock retraces toward its May low near $84-88, roughly 12-15% below current levels.

**Base Case (~$110-118).** Eddyfi proves modestly accretive and the platform story holds, but organic demand remains soft and the multiple stays compressed without a clean catalyst. EPS of ~$6.50-6.86 at ~16-18x supports $110-118 over twelve months, modest upside from current levels with the risk skewed by leverage and the information gap.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a 12-month reassessment horizon, anchored to the mid-August 2026 Eddyfi financial disclosure and the Q2 2026 print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Eddyfi standalone/pro-forma financials disclosed (by ~mid-August) confirming margin and growth profile at or above the ~$270M 2026 revenue estimate with clear accretion, AND organic volume turning positive (>0% YoY) for two consecutive quarters.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin re-expanding back toward/above 20% with leverage trending below ~3x net debt/EBITDA on integration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut or organic volume deteriorating below −4% YoY, signaling end-market weakness beyond the current soft patch.
- A goodwill impairment charge, a covenant-pressure disclosure, or evidence that Eddyfi is materially dilutive to FCF.
- Continued EPS misses (a fourth miss in five quarters) alongside falling next-year revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the mid-August Eddyfi 8-K/financials and the Q2 2026 earnings release. The degrading analyst sentiment and the options backwardation both point to that window as the resolution point.

## Conclusion

ESAB is a well-run niche industrial compounder whose margin engine is working but whose organic top line is stalled, now executing the largest and most strategic acquisition in its standalone history with leverage stepped up and target financials not yet public. The valuation is reasonable on forward earnings (roughly 15x next-year EPS versus a peer median far higher), and the drawdown of roughly 25%+ from the February high has improved the entry math. But the discount reflects real concerns rather than a clean mispricing: negative organic volume, tariff-driven margin dilution, a higher interest burden, a C-suite in transition, and an information gap on Eddyfi that will not close until mid-August.

There is neither a confident bull catalyst nor evidence of fundamental collapse that would justify an AVOID. The recommendation is HOLD, classified as uncertain; the next two months of disclosure (Eddyfi financials, Q2 results) will determine whether the platform re-rates higher or the leverage-plus-soft-demand combination weighs on the stock. We would become buyers on confirmed Eddyfi accretion plus organic stabilization, and sellers on a guidance cut or impairment.
