**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Otter Tail Corporation is misclassified by standard sector screens as an electric utility. It is a hybrid holding company in which a small, capital-light PVC pipe manufacturer (the Plastics segment) generated roughly 61% of total reportable-segment net income in FY2025 — $170.4M of $279.5M — while the regulated Electric utility contributed the minority. The commodity business funds the corporate story. Any model treating OTTR as a stable regulated utility will misjudge both its earnings volatility and the appropriate multiple.

The central dynamic is an intentional, guided earnings decline. Consolidated diluted EPS peaked at $7.17 in FY2024, fell to $6.55 in FY2025, and management has reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $5.22–$5.62, a midpoint roughly 17% below FY2025. This reflects the planned normalization of PVC pipe margins from a 2021–2024 supercycle back toward pre-2021 levels, a process management says continues through 2027. Q1 2026 confirmed the trend: Plastics net income fell 24% YoY on a 19% drop in average PVC sales prices (the thirteenth consecutive quarter of decline from the late-2022 peak), partially offset by a 43% jump in Electric net income from interim rate increases and wind production tax credits.

The swing factor that surface screens miss is a multi-front antitrust complex centered on the profit engine: a federal price-fixing class action seeking treble damages, a DOJ Antitrust Division grand jury subpoena, a Canadian class action, and a shareholder derivative demand, all concerning PVC pipe pricing. Management states it cannot estimate a loss range and warns of potential "material" impact on financial position, results, and liquidity. With the stock at ~13x trailing earnings against falling EPS, fairly compensated for the step-down but carrying an unquantifiable legal tail, the risk/reward is two-sided. We rate OTTR HOLD.

## Company Overview

Otter Tail runs three reportable segments:

1. **Electric (Otter Tail Power Company):** A vertically integrated regulated utility serving western Minnesota, eastern North Dakota, and northeastern South Dakota. FY2025: ~$567M revenue, $97.6M net income. Generation includes jointly-owned coal at Big Stone Plant (53.9% interest) and Coyote Station (35%), with a growing renewables build-out (Abercrombie Solar, wind repowering).

2. **Plastics (Northern Pipe Products, Vinyltech):** PVC pipe manufacturing at plants in North Dakota and Arizona, sold primarily into western U.S./Canada municipal water, wastewater, and rural water markets. FY2025: ~$423M revenue, $170.4M net income, the dominant profit contributor on a fraction of the asset base.

3. **Manufacturing (BTD Manufacturing, T.O. Plastics):** Metal fabrication and thermoformed plastics for agriculture, construction, powersports, and other end markets. FY2025: ~$315M revenue, $11.5M net income; currently recovering off a soft 2024–25 trough.

Consolidated FY2025 revenue was $1,304M, down from $1,331M (2024), $1,349M (2023), and $1,460M (2022), a multi-year decline driven by normalizing PVC pricing and volumes. The company has ~42.0M shares outstanding and a ~$3.7B market capitalization.

## Financial Analysis

**Headline FY2025 metrics:**
- Revenue $1,304M; net income $275.9M; diluted EPS $6.55
- Operating margin 26.5%, net margin 21.2%, entirely a function of Plastics
- ROE 14.8%, ROA 7.0%
- Operating cash flow $386M; capex $288M; free cash flow ~$98M
- Cash $386M; current ratio 2.28

**Quarterly trajectory.** Q1 2026 net income rose 6.6% YoY with EPS of $1.73, but the composition is diverging sharply:
- **Plastics:** net income −24% YoY ($32.9M vs $43.4M); average PVC sales prices −19% YoY. Volumes were +7%, partly reflecting buyer pull-forward ahead of feared resin price increases, a near-term tailwind that can reverse into H2 2026.
- **Electric:** net income +43% YoY ($35.3M vs $24.7M), driven by interim rate increases (MN, SD), new rate base, and a sharp jump in wind PTCs (effective tax rate fell to 6.3% from 12.9%).
- **Manufacturing:** net income +180% off a low base ($4.3M) on recovering construction/RV demand.

**Balance sheet quality is sound despite a misleading screen.** Reported D/E of 2.13 and an Altman Z-Score of 1.69 ("distress zone") are artifacts of applying a manufacturing-oriented bankruptcy model to a regulated-utility holding company; we disregard them. Actual covenant metrics are healthy: holding-company debt/total-cap is 0.40x against a covenant maximum of 0.60–0.65x, with interest-plus-dividend coverage of 7.76x; the utility subsidiary runs ~0.48–0.50x debt/cap with 3.13x coverage. Credit ratings are solid investment grade (holding co Baa2/BBB/BBB; utility Baa1/BBB+/BBB+), with a positive S&P outlook on the holding company.

**Capital allocation** is disciplined. The dividend has grown from $1.75 to $1.87 to $2.10 per share over 2023–2025, with Q1 2026 at $0.5775/quarter, a ~2.6% yield. There is no buyback program. Capex is ramping hard into the regulated utility: Q1 2026 capex was $185M versus $58M a year earlier, with Electric pulling forward solar panel procurement to beat tariffs. Management states the plan can be funded without external equity through at least 2030, financed via utility senior notes ($170M issued March 2026 at 5.33%/6.04%). FCF conversion (~$98M FCF against $276M net income) is low, reflecting the heavy capex cycle, which is normal for a utility entering a rate-base expansion phase but limits near-term cash return flexibility.

## Growth Analysis

OTTR is in a guided earnings *decline*, not growth. Consolidated EPS is rolling over from a 2024 peak. Estimate revisions corroborate the deceleration: next-quarter consensus has been cut 9.7% over 30 days and 11.9% over 90 days, and next-year EPS has been trimmed 2.1% over 90 days. The current-year estimate ticked up 3.4% over 30 days on the Q1 Electric beat, but the multi-quarter direction is down. This is the opposite of the rising-estimate profile that would support an aggressive growth score.

Two offsetting forces define the forward path:
- **Drag:** Plastics earnings are explicitly guided to decline through 2027 toward pre-2021 margin levels. As the largest profit contributor with prices falling for roughly thirteen consecutive quarters, this is the dominant near-term driver.
- **Offset:** the Electric segment targets a ~10% rate-base CAGR, funded internally, with three concurrent rate cases (ND settled +$13.1M; SD settled +$3.3M with a moratorium to Dec 2029; Minnesota pending on a $44.8M / 17.7% request, interim +$28.6M effective Jan 2026 subject to refund). Wind repowering restarted a 10-year PTC clock, supporting near-term effective tax rates.

The bull case is that "normalized" PVC margins settle above the pre-2021 baseline, leaving consolidated earnings power higher than guidance implies while the utility compounds rate base. The bear case is that PVC margins overshoot to the downside and the rate-base growth (a mid-single-digit EPS contributor at best) cannot offset a Plastics segment that has been earning at multiples of its historical norm. The weight of current evidence — falling prices, falling forward estimates, a guided multi-year decline — keeps the growth score low.

## Valuation Assessment

At $86.73, OTTR trades at ~13.2x trailing EPS, or ~16.0x the FY2026 guidance midpoint (~$5.42), and ~14.9x current-year consensus of $5.83. Against electric-utility peers (median P/E 19.7, yield 2.9% — MGEE 19.7x, POR 22.4x, UTL 15.9x, HE 17.9x, TXNM 42.1x), OTTR screens cheap on trailing earnings.

That apparent discount is the trap. The peer multiples are applied to stable, growing regulated earnings; OTTR's trailing earnings include a cyclical PVC peak that is mean-reverting. On forward earnings the discount narrows materially, and the right comparison is a sum-of-the-parts blend of a regulated utility (deserving a utility multiple) and a cyclical commodity manufacturer at a cycle peak (deserving a trough-cycle, below-market multiple). Applying a mid-teens multiple to a blended forward earnings stream that is declining produces a fair value close to the current price. The low headline P/E reflects the market correctly discounting a cyclical peak plus an unquantifiable legal overhang, not a mispricing to exploit.

The analyst consensus offers limited upside support: target mean and median of $90.50, low $86.00, high $95.00, implying roughly 4% upside to the mean from current levels, with all four covering analysts at HOLD. There is no identified catalyst that reprices the stock higher; the binary events (Minnesota rate order, antitrust motion-to-dismiss ruling) are as likely to cap as to lift the multiple. We score valuation at fair, neither offering asymmetric upside nor signaling clear overvaluation.

## Competitive Landscape

**Electric:** A small, rural, vertically integrated utility operating in a constructive multi-state regulatory environment with allowed ROEs of ~10.1–10.8% and equity ratios around 53.5%. The competitive position is stable; the growth lever is rate-base expansion (solar, wind repowering, transmission), funded internally. The coal wind-down — MPUC ordered an exit from serving Minnesota customers via Coyote Station by Dec 2031 — is a transition headwind being managed through accelerated cost-recovery requests, with residual VIE coal-supply exposure (up to ~$35M maximum loss) running to 2040.

**Plastics:** A cyclical commodity business that rode an extraordinary 2021–2024 supercycle on constrained resin supply and infrastructure demand. The industry is consolidated around four U.S. resin suppliers (mostly Gulf Coast, hurricane-exposed), and that concentrated pricing behavior is now the subject of antitrust scrutiny. The key analytical question — where normalized margins settle, and whether the antitrust matter is a one-time cost or a structural change to industry pricing transparency — is unresolved and bears directly on competitive durability. The thirteen-quarter price decline indicates the supercycle pricing power is eroding.

**Manufacturing:** Cyclical, tied to construction/agriculture/RV demand, currently recovering off a trough. No durable moat; a price-taker on its end markets.

The blended competitive trajectory is stable-to-eroding: the utility's position holds, but the segment driving the majority of profit faces both cyclical margin compression and legal/structural pressure on its pricing model.

## Risk Assessment

**1. PVC antitrust complex (dominant idiosyncratic risk).** Since August 2024, the company and its plastics subsidiaries are defendants in *In re: PVC Pipe Antitrust Litigation* (N.D. Ill.) alongside 20+ manufacturers and a pricing-data provider, alleging price-fixing via information exchange dating to 2017/2020. Plaintiffs seek treble damages. There is also a DOJ Antitrust Division grand jury subpoena (Aug 2024), a Canadian class action (Sept 2025), and a shareholder derivative demand (May 2025). Motions to dismiss were briefed in early 2026 with no ruling yet. The company cannot estimate a range of loss and states resolution could be material. Because Plastics is the profit engine, this is asymmetric downside not captured by any financial ratio.

**2. Plastics earnings normalization.** How far and fast PVC margins fall is the central earnings debate.

**3. Regulatory/rate-case risk.** The pending Minnesota case (+$44.8M requested; +$28.6M interim subject to refund) materially affects Electric earnings; an adverse final order or large refund obligation is a real risk.

**4. Resin supply concentration.** Four U.S. resin suppliers, mostly Gulf Coast; interruption would hit Plastics margins.

**5. Coal transition.** Coyote Station exit by Dec 2031 with accelerated recovery still being sought; stranded-asset and VIE exposure remain.

**6. Leadership transition.** A CFO handoff (Todd Wahlund to Tyler Nelson between the Feb 2026 10-K/A and the May 2026 10-Q) and a President title change (Timothy Rogelstad) are in progress. These appear orderly but warrant monitoring during an earnings-transition period.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 33.0% sits rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 21.8% (an 11.2-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (33.0% near-term vs 24.5% at six months), indicating the market expects an imminent event rather than persistent elevated vol. Put/call skew is balanced (0.1%) and put/call OI is low at 0.08, so the positioning is not directionally bearish through options; it reads as event-hedging into the next earnings print and pending legal/rate catalysts. Separately, short interest is elevated at 18.4% of float with 21.9 days to cover, reflecting institutional skepticism about the earnings step-down and antitrust tail; the high days-to-cover creates some squeeze potential on any benign resolution, but also signals a well-informed bearish constituency. The IV reading flagged at 144.6% in the delta-change feed is treated as a thin-options data artifact and disregarded.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$95–100):** PVC margins normalize above the pre-2021 baseline rather than collapsing to it, leaving consolidated earnings power higher than guidance implies. The Minnesota rate case settles near the interim level, the utility rate-base CAGR compounds, the antitrust motions to dismiss succeed or the matter settles for an immaterial sum, and the short interest covers into the relief. A blended forward multiple expands toward the peer mid-teens on a higher-confidence earnings base. Roughly +9–15% from current levels.

**Bear Case (~$70–75):** PVC prices continue falling and Plastics margins overshoot toward the pre-2021 trough, pulling FY2026–27 EPS below guidance. The antitrust complex produces a material adverse ruling or a costly settlement (treble damages exposure), the DOJ probe escalates, and the Minnesota case delivers a refund obligation. The market reprices the cyclical earnings at a trough multiple plus a litigation discount. Roughly −14% to −19% from current levels, approaching the 52-week low of $74.15.

This is a HOLD rather than a directional call because the offsetting forces are genuinely balanced. The utility's funded rate-base growth provides a floor, the dividend (well-covered) pays the carry, and the stock is fairly valued on forward earnings. But the largest profit contributor is declining by design, forward estimates are being cut, and an unquantifiable legal tail sits on top. There is no asymmetric upside catalyst to justify a BUY, and the business is too sound (investment-grade, internally funded, growing dividend) to justify an AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoints are the MPUC final order on the Minnesota rate request, any ruling on the antitrust motions to dismiss, and the next two quarterly prints to gauge the pace of PVC price decline and whether Q1 volume pull-forward reverses.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A benign resolution of the antitrust matter (motions to dismiss granted, or settlement disclosed at an immaterial sum), removing the unquantifiable tail.
- PVC prices stabilizing for two consecutive quarters with Plastics segment net income flat-to-up YoY, indicating normalized margins are settling above the bearish baseline, combined with a Minnesota rate order at or near the interim level.
- A pullback toward the low-$70s on unchanged fundamentals, which would shift the forward multiple to a clear discount and improve the risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse antitrust ruling, an escalation of the DOJ criminal probe (e.g., indictment), or disclosure of a settlement large enough to impair the balance sheet or dividend.
- FY2026 EPS guidance cut below the $5.22 low end, or Plastics net income declining more than ~30% YoY in consecutive quarters.
- A Minnesota rate order materially below interim levels with a significant refund obligation, combined with continued Plastics deterioration.

## Conclusion

Otter Tail is a well-run, investment-grade hybrid holding company that the market correctly understands better than a standard utility screen does. The trailing ~13x P/E is not a bargain; it reflects cyclical PVC earnings at a peak that management has explicitly guided to unwind through 2027, layered with an unquantifiable antitrust tail on the segment that drives the majority of profit. The regulated utility's ~10% rate-base growth and a growing ~2.6% dividend provide a floor and pay investors to wait, but forward estimates are falling, all covering analysts sit at HOLD with ~4% implied upside to the mean target, and there is no identifiable catalyst to reprice the stock higher in the next twelve months. We rate OTTR **HOLD**: the offsetting forces — funded utility growth and a covered dividend against a guided earnings decline and binary legal/regulatory events — are balanced enough that neither a constructive nor a negative directional case is supported by the current evidence. Investors should reassess on the Minnesota rate order, any antitrust ruling, and the next two quarters of PVC pricing data.
