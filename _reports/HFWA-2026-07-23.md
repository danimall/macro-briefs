**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Heritage Financial is a $7.0 billion-asset (pre-Olympic) commercial community bank holding company operating through Heritage Bank across Washington, Oregon, and Idaho. The FY2025 story is a margin-driven earnings recovery: net income rebounded 56% to $67.5 million ($1.96 diluted EPS) as net interest margin expanded from 3.31% to 3.58%, funding costs fell 23 basis points, and the bank paid down $363 million of expensive FHLB borrowings. The January 31, 2026 close of the Olympic Bancorp/Kitsap Bank acquisition (an all-stock deal adding ~$1.6 billion in assets, ~$0.9 billion in loans, and a clean credit book) accelerated the trajectory, pushing Q1'26 NIM to 3.96% with management guiding toward 4%+ by year-end.

The counterweight is a set of concentration and cyclical risks that the market is pricing but that also cap the upside at current levels. Post-merger, the non-owner-occupied CRE-to-capital ratio rose to 301%, just past the 300% supervisory guidance threshold, because fair-value marks compressed combined capital. Management modeled this and expects normalization, but it invites regulatory attention and governs the pace of buybacks and further CRE growth in the interim. Separately, the Chief Credit Officer flagged gradual C&I softening (rising special-mention/substandard migration tied to tariffs, labor, and supply chains), and consensus EPS estimates have drifted down 5-10% over the last 90 days. Insider activity is entirely routine: small comp-related sales including the CFO, no open-market conviction buying.

At $30.18 the stock trades at roughly 14.8x trailing EPS and ~12.6x the $2.39 current-year consensus, near its 52-week high of $31.25 and only marginally below the analyst mean target of $31.40. The fundamental improvement is real, but after a 21% run the price captures much of the near-term margin-inflection thesis while the CRE-ratio overhang, C&I watch item, and downward estimate revisions leave the risk/reward roughly balanced. We rate HFWA a HOLD, classified as a growth hold on the durable NIM-expansion and integration-accretion path, with a better entry likely available on any market or credit-related pullback.

## Company Overview

Heritage Financial's sole operating subsidiary is Heritage Bank, a state-chartered commercial bank founded in 1927 and headquartered in Olympia, Washington. It is a relationship-focused commercial community bank serving the Pacific Northwest: Washington State, greater Portland/Eugene Oregon, and Boise Idaho. As of year-end 2025 it operated 50 branches; the Olympic/Kitsap acquisition added 16, strengthening the Puget Sound footprint.

Core earnings come from net interest income: lending to small- and medium-sized businesses and their owners, funded by low-cost core deposits. The loan book is commercial and CRE-heavy: commercial business loans are 81.7% of loans ($3.91 billion), of which owner-occupied and non-owner-occupied CRE is roughly $3.09 billion; construction and land development is 7.2%; residential real estate is 7.5% (runoff only, as the bank ceased originating residential mortgages in January 2024); and consumer is 3.6%.

The franchise's differentiation rests on a granular, low-cost core deposit base (non-maturity deposits are 84.1% of total; noninterest-bearing demand ~27%) and a disciplined credit culture backed by a long M&A record of 11 acquisitions since 1998. The stated strategy is to be the leading commercial community bank in the Pacific Northwest through relationship banking plus opportunistic, disciplined acquisitions. Borrower concentration is low: the ten largest relationships are only 6.5% of loans.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings recovery is the headline.** Net income was $67.5 million in FY2025 ($1.96 diluted EPS), up 56% from $43.3 million ($1.24) in 2024. The multi-year arc reveals a bank that cycled through a rate-driven trough: $81.9 million (2022) → $61.8 million (2023) → $43.3 million (2024) → $67.5 million (2025). The 2024 low was driven by margin compression and securities-repositioning losses.

**Margin is the driver.** NIM rebounded on (a) an asset-mix shift into higher-yielding loans and (b) a 23bp drop in funding costs as FHLB borrowings fell from $383 million to $20 million. Net interest income rose $15 million to $224.4 million. Cost of interest-bearing deposits fell to 1.71% (168bps in March).

**Noninterest income and expense.** Reported noninterest income swung to +$21.7 million from $7.5 million, but largely because 2025's securities-repositioning loss ($10.7 million) was smaller than 2024's ($22.7 million); underlying fee income (service charges, cards, wealth) is modest and stable. Noninterest expense rose 4.6% to $165.6 million on higher compensation (+$5.5 million) and Olympic deal costs. Merger-related expenses will keep the run-rate elevated at $64-65 million per quarter through the late-Q3 2026 system conversion, dropping to ~$55-57 million in Q4.

**Balance sheet and capital are a genuine strength.** Total assets were $6.97 billion at year-end (down 2% as securities ran off). Regulatory capital is robust: CET1 12.7%, Tier 1 13.1%, Total capital 14.1%, leverage 10.8%, equity/assets 13.2%. The company excludes AOCI from CET1, insulating regulatory capital from bond mark-to-market swings. The AOCI loss narrowed to –$31.2 million from –$55.2 million as marks recovered. Liquidity directly answers the 2023 regional-bank concern: $2.62 billion total available liquidity ($840 million on-balance-sheet + $1.78 billion off-balance-sheet capacity) against $2.43 billion of uninsured deposits, coverage of roughly 100%+.

**A data-integrity note.** Automated screens showing an Altman Z-Score of 0.28 ("distress"), D/E of 6.56, and a net margin of 342% are not meaningful for a bank, because deposits are counted as "debt" and interest income distorts "revenue." The correct read is a well-capitalized institution with strong liquidity, low charge-offs (0.03-0.04% NCOs), and recovering earnings. Piotroski partial signals (positive NI, ROA, OCF, and OCF > NI) are all positive.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** Over the last four quarters, three beats and one miss: +18.8% (Jun'26), +3.4% (Mar'26), +13.7% (Dec'25), and –1.1% (Sep'25). The Q1'26 (March-quarter) print beat consensus by 3.4% ($0.48 actual vs. $0.46 estimate) in the vendor surprise data; the research brief separately cites a $0.49 vs. $0.57 shortfall, which appears substantially explained by $5.2 million of merger costs and only two months of combined operations. The most recent June-quarter print beat by 18.8%.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a blend of margin inflection, acquisition-driven scale, and modest organic loan growth. The NIM path (3.58% → 3.96% → targeting 4%+) is the primary earnings lever; each incremental basis point on a ~$6 billion earning-asset base is material to a bank earning under $70 million. Funding-cost tailwinds from Fed cuts have driven the recovery, but the durability of that tailwind is the key uncertainty: management acknowledged intensifying deposit competition and raised top CD rates.

The Olympic/Kitsap deal adds ~$1.4 billion of deposits alongside the assets and loans noted above. Management characterized the acquired book as accretive to credit (zero nonaccruals and lower criticized loans), and the deal expands the Puget Sound presence. Organic loan growth guidance is mid-single-digit, and the commercial pipeline rose 35% quarter-over-quarter to $631 million in Q1'26, a constructive leading indicator.

The estimate-revision picture tempers the growth score. Consensus has drifted lower over 90 days: current-quarter EPS from $0.60 to $0.54 (–9.7%), next-quarter from $0.64 to $0.59 (–7.3%), current-year from $2.51 to $2.39 (–4.9%), and next-year from $2.92 to $2.80 (–4.2%). The 30-day trend is flat, so estimates have stabilized rather than reversed, but a 5-10% downward revision over the quarter is a headwind. There is no evidence of the +30% revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings inflection; this is a steady community-bank recovery with a modest deceleration in the forward outlook. Next-year consensus of $2.80 implies ~17% EPS growth off the $2.39 current-year base, largely from a full year of combined Olympic operations and cost synergies post-conversion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $30.18, HFWA trades at ~14.8x trailing EPS ($1.96), ~12.6x current-year consensus ($2.39), and ~10.8x next-year consensus ($2.80). Dividend yield is ~3.2% on a $0.96 annualized payout, well-covered at roughly a 49% payout ratio ($0.96 / $1.96 trailing EPS). Tangible book value support exists but is diluted post-merger (TCE ratio fell to 9.6% from 10.1%), and ~$240.9 million of goodwill (26% of equity, growing post-Olympic) sits against reported book.

The analyst target set is tight and close to spot: low $30.00, mean $31.40, median $31.00, high $34.00. The stock sits essentially at the low end and just below the mean, so the consensus is not signaling meaningful undervaluation. The guardrail on treating analyst targets as floors does not apply here: this is not a sub-30x forward-P/E inflection stock with rising estimates. Estimates are falling, growth is mid-single-digit organic, and the targets cluster near the current price, meaning they likely reflect fair value rather than lagging reality.

A low P/E on a bank recovering from a rate-cycle trough is not, by itself, a buy signal; cheapness that reflects genuine concentration risk and downward-revised estimates behaves as a value trap more often than a mispricing. Two specific factors keep the multiple honest rather than compelling: the CRE-ratio overhang (which governs buyback and growth optionality) and the C&I credit watch item (which introduces reserve/provision risk into the forward EPS path). Against that, the NIM inflection and integration accretion are real and support a fair value modestly above spot on a base case, but not the asymmetric upside required for a strong valuation score. We score valuation 5.2: neither cheap enough to overweight nor stretched enough to avoid, with the 21% year-to-date run having captured most of the near-term re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Heritage competes against national and regional banks, credit unions, fintechs, and increasingly digital-asset providers, most with greater scale. Its edge is relationship banking and local Pacific Northwest market knowledge rather than price or product breadth. The low-cost, granular deposit franchise (84.1% non-maturity deposits, ~27% noninterest-bearing) is the core moat, and it held up through the 2023 deposit stress.

Two dynamics are favorable. First, community-bank consolidation in the region creates both acquisition opportunities (Olympic) and banker-recruiting openings amid competitor disruption; management noted it is actively recruiting commercial teams, partially offset by retirements. Second, a 2025 regulatory shift toward reducing community-bank burden (streamlined M&A review, proposed CBLR reduction from 9% to 8%, removal of "reputation risk" from exams, CRA rollback) is a modest positive for a serial acquirer.

The trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than clearly widening. The pay-vs-performance disclosure shows company TSR (122.94) lagging its peer group (160.37) over the measurement window, a relative-underperformance flag consistent with a bank that spent 2024-2025 playing catch-up on the rate cycle. The main competitive headwinds are sector-wide CRE and office concern (given the ~$3.1 billion CRE book) and deposit-cost competition. The offsetting positive is that the acquired Kitsap franchise deepens share in an attractive Puget Sound market with a cleaner credit profile than Heritage's standalone book.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration — the sharpest issue.** Roughly 79.3% of loans are real-estate-collateralized, and non-owner-occupied CRE is the single largest bucket. Crossing the supervisory line invites heightened regulatory scrutiny and constrains buybacks and CRE growth near-term. Office CRE is $588.8 million (19% of CRE), mitigated by ~49% owner-occupied, 82% owner recourse, and ~25% healthcare-tenant exposure.

**Emerging C&I softening.** FY2025 nonaccruals jumped 414% to $21.0 million (0.44% of loans) on a handful of construction/CRE credits, then improved to $15 million/0.26% by Q1'26 as those credits paid off and the clean Olympic book was added. The more durable concern is management's own flag of rising special-mention/substandard C&I migration tied to tariffs, labor costs, and supply-chain uncertainty, described as macro-linked rather than industry-specific. Net charge-offs remain very low (0.03-0.04%), so this is a watch item that could feed provisioning, not a realized loss problem yet.

**Integration/execution risk.** The Olympic deal converts systems late Q3 2026, with elevated expenses until then. Any conversion hiccup, deposit attrition, or credit surprise in the acquired book is the key operational risk, and growing goodwill raises impairment risk if performance disappoints.

**Rate and deposit-competition risk.** NIM benefited from Fed cuts lowering funding costs; a rate reversal or a mix shift out of noninterest-bearing accounts (already slipped to 27% from 29%) would pressure the margin. This is the swing factor for the forward EPS path.

**Governance/transition.** A CEO change occurred between the FY2025 proxy (which lists Jeff Deuel) and the Q1'26 call (led by Bryan McDonald as CEO), tied to the December 19, 2025 Item 5.02 8-K. Continuity in credit (Chalfant) and finance (Hinson) is intact and the transition appears orderly, but the succession is recent.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 51.1% versus 1-year historical volatility of 29.1% (rich by ~21.9 points) and the term structure is in backwardation (51.1% at 1m versus 37.7% at 6m), indicating the market is pricing an imminent catalyst, consistent with the July 23 Q2 earnings date. IV rose sharply from 32.5% to 51.1% since mid-June as the stock climbed 3.9%, the classic pre-earnings vol build. The put/call OI ratio is 0.05 and the skew is call-heavy (–37.7%), which is unusual for a name near its 52-week high and signals positioning tilted bullish/complacent rather than defensive. The implied one-month move is ±14.9%. For a HOLD, the read is that near-term volatility is elevated and rich; a buyer with conviction is better served waiting for the post-earnings IV crush rather than paying up into the event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** NIM continues its inflection to 4%+ by year-end, driven by continued funding-cost relief and the higher-yielding, cleaner Olympic loan book. The late-Q3 system conversion delivers the modeled expense drop, and full-year 2027 earnings capture the synergies, so next-year consensus proves conservative. The commercial pipeline (+35% QoQ to $631 million) converts to mid-single-digit organic loan growth, the CRE ratio normalizes as capital rebuilds, and the bank resumes buybacks with ~800K shares authorized. A well-capitalized, 3.2%-yielding compounder re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. Bull case toward the $34 high target.

**Bear case.** C&I credit softening broadens from special-mention migration into realized charge-offs and higher provisioning, cutting EPS just as the CRE ratio above supervisory guidance draws regulatory scrutiny that caps buybacks and growth. Deposit competition re-accelerates or rates back up, stalling the NIM path. Integration hiccups or Kitsap deposit attrition surface. Estimates continue drifting down, and a bank near its 52-week high with fair-value multiples de-rates. Bear case ~$25-26, roughly –14% to –17% from spot.

**Base case.** NIM holds near 4%, integration completes on schedule, credit stays contained but provisioning ticks up modestly, and the bank earns roughly consensus ($2.39 this year, ~$2.80 next). At ~11.5x next-year EPS the stock supports a fair value around $31-32, close to the analyst mean. Modest total return from the dividend plus low-single-digit price appreciation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a **growth-hold** rationale: the 12-month thesis rests on the NIM inflection and Olympic integration accretion, but the risk/reward after a 21% run is roughly balanced rather than compelling.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NIM confirmed sustainably at or above 4.0% for two consecutive quarters with cost of interest-bearing deposits stable or falling, alongside estimate revisions turning positive.
- Non-owner-occupied CRE/capital ratio normalizing back below 300% and management resuming buybacks, removing the regulatory overhang.
- A pullback toward $25-26 (roughly tangible-book-supported, ~9x next-year EPS) on market or sector weakness with fundamentals intact, which would restore asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- C&I special-mention/substandard migration converting to a material rise in net charge-offs (e.g., NCOs above 0.20% annualized) or a sharp jump in provisioning.
- Olympic system-conversion failure, meaningful deposit attrition, or goodwill impairment.
- NIM reversal below 3.7% on renewed deposit competition or rate backup, combined with continued negative estimate revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 2026 earnings report (scheduled July 23, 2026) is the immediate checkpoint; watch NIM progression, C&I credit trends, and any update on the CRE ratio and buyback intent. The late-Q3 2026 Olympic system conversion is the next major operational milestone.

## Conclusion

Heritage Financial has executed a credible earnings recovery, backed by disciplined all-stock acquisition, and its capital and liquidity are genuine strengths, with the automated distress screens being artifacts of applying industrial ratios to a bank. Those positives support the shares near current levels.

But at $30.18, near the 52-week high, just below the analyst mean target, and at fair multiples on estimates that have drifted down 5-10% over 90 days, the market has priced most of the near-term margin story. The CRE ratio above the 300% supervisory threshold constrains capital-return optionality, management itself flagged gradual C&I softening, and insider activity shows no open-market conviction buying. The valuation offers neither the cheapness nor the asymmetric upside to justify an overweight, and the trajectory of prior scores (6.4 → 6.2) reflects the gradual compression of the risk/reward as the price rose into the improving fundamentals.

We rate HFWA a **HOLD**. It is a well-run community bank worth owning for its NIM-inflection and integration-accretion path plus a covered 3.2% yield, but the entry is unattractive after the run. A pullback toward the mid-$20s, confirmed sustained 4%+ NIM, or normalization of the CRE ratio would each strengthen the case for an upgrade.
