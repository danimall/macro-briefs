## Executive Summary

Neogen's FY2026 report, delivered July 30, was the best operational print the company has produced since the 3M Food Safety Division integration went wrong. Q4 core revenue grew 4.3%, the highest rate of the fiscal year, with core Food Safety at 5.8% — described by management as the fastest since FY2023. GAAP operating income turned positive at $3.1M for the quarter, GAAP gross margin recovered to 47.8% from 41.2%, the sample-collection supply problems that plagued Animal Safety were largely resolved (that segment grew over 7% sequentially), total debt was cut $100.4M, and the material weaknesses in internal control were remediated as of May 31, 2026. The market rewarded it: the stock moved from $9.40 to $11.53 on the print and touched $12.85 on August 3.

The problem is what the equity now costs. At $11.30 the enterprise value is approximately $3,073M against FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $177.8M (17.3x) and FY2027 guided adjusted EBITDA of $180-182M (17.0x). That multiple is being paid for a business whose adjusted EBITDA peaked at $213.2M in FY2024, fell to $184.2M and then $177.8M, and is guided flat for a fourth year; whose core growth rate implied by its own guidance is roughly 3%; whose reported FY2026 free cash flow of $31.9M was more than fully explained by a $52.1M working-capital release; and which carries $608.2M of net debt (3.42x), negative tangible common equity of $272.3M, and $1,047.2M of residual goodwill after a $1,059.3M write-down. Normalized free cash flow is approximately zero, which is why deleveraging depends on selling assets rather than generating cash.

Two developments since the prior review cut against the shares. The $160M Genomics sale to Zoetis — the source of the ~$140M net proceeds earmarked for debt reduction — entered second-phase review at both the ACCC and the New Zealand Commerce Commission in July 2026. And consensus next-year EPS was cut 9.5% over 30 days to $0.38, even as current-year estimates edged up. Meanwhile the Petrifilm manufacturing transition, the single largest swing factor in the story, does not begin until November 2026, and Neogen's one prior attempt at the same kind of in-sourcing generated $27.4M of combined FY2025-FY2026 costs plus acknowledged product shortages. We move from HOLD to **REDUCE**: base-case value of roughly $10.25 sits 9% below spot, the bear case is −42% against a bull case of +33%, and probability-weighted expected return is approximately −10%.

## Company Overview

Neogen manufactures and sells diagnostic test kits, consumables, culture media and instruments for food safety testing, plus a smaller animal safety portfolio (veterinary instruments, animal care, insect and rodent control, genomics services). It operates in over 140 countries across two reported segments.

FY2026 revenue was $870.4M, down 2.7% from $894.7M. The composition matters more than the total:

| Line | FY2026 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food Safety | $641.1M | $638.1M | +0.5% |
| — Indicator Testing, Culture Media | $332.7M | $312.2M | +6.6% |
| — Bacterial & General Sanitation | $175.4M | $164.8M | +6.4% |
| — Natural Toxins & Allergens | $77.5M | $77.0M | +0.6% |
| Animal Safety | $229.3M | $256.6M | −10.6% |
| Combined Rodent/Insect Control & Disinfectants | $87.1M | $135.1M | −35.5% |
| **Total** | **$870.4M** | **$894.7M** | **−2.7%** |

The $48.0M decline in the combined rodent/insect control and disinfectants category — the Cleaners & Disinfectants divestiture plus discontinued lines — more than accounts for the entire $24.3M consolidated decline. Excluding it, revenue grew from $759.6M to $783.3M, or 3.1%. Management's own core revenue measure grew 4.3% in Q4.

The two consumable lines that constitute the economic core — Indicator Testing/Culture Media (which contains Petrifilm) and Bacterial & General Sanitation — represent $508.1M, 58.4% of FY2026 revenue, growing 6.5% for the year and 9.6% in Q4. Geographic split was $425.1M domestic (−4.7%) and $445.3M international (−0.8%), so 51.2% of revenue is now non-US.

Everything in the balance sheet traces to the 2022 Reverse Morris Trust acquisition of 3M's Food Safety Division: additional paid-in capital of $2,616.0M, an accumulated deficit of $544.3M, $1,047.2M of remaining goodwill and $1,318.0M of amortizable intangibles, together 70.7% of total assets.

## Financial Analysis

**Margins.** GAAP gross margin fell from 50.2% (FY2024) to 47.1% to 46.9%, but adjusted gross margin was essentially unchanged at 50.2% then 50.3%. The FY2026 bridge is $29.3M of add-backs, dominated by sample-collection transition and ramp-up costs of $12.4M and Petrifilm duplicate manufacturing of $11.9M. Q4 adjusted gross margin was 49.7% versus 46.4%, which management attributes to favorable mix and pricing. Underlying unit economics were never impaired; the reported deterioration was the cash cost of separating from 3M's manufacturing.

That distinction has a limit. The full FY2026 reconciliation from EBITDA to adjusted EBITDA totals $89.3M of add-backs, of which recurring operational items are approximately $72.4M — 8.3% of revenue. Two of the largest categories grew: transformation initiatives (ERP implementation, severance) more than doubled to $22.3M from $9.8M, and transaction costs nearly doubled to $10.6M from $5.7M. FY2027 guidance of $180-182M implies management does not expect that cost base to roll off next year.

**Operating expenses.** Sales and marketing fell 9.4% to $166.6M, but G&A rose 12.3% to $245.1M on revenue that declined 2.7%. R&D fell 12.8% to $18.4M, or 2.1% of revenue — thin for a company positioning itself as a diagnostics innovator. Management has signalled a substantial R&D step-up in FY2027 (a roughly 50% increase was reported from the earnings call but is not quantified in the release we can verify), which would imply roughly $9M of incremental spend and largely explains why FY2027 adjusted EBITDA is guided flat despite improving core growth.

**The adjusted EBITDA series is the cleanest read on whether the 3M deal created value.** Using the proxy's company-selected performance measure, which reconciles exactly to reported adjusted EBITDA at the FY2025 value of $184.2M: $205.1M (FY2023), $213.2M (FY2024), $184.2M (FY2025), $177.8M (FY2026), $180-182M guided (FY2027). Peak-to-current is −16.6%, with a fourth consecutive year of no growth guided. No revenue screen or GAAP earnings screen surfaces this.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $83.2M versus $58.2M and $35.3M in the two prior years; capex fell to $51.3M from $104.6M and $111.4M. Reported free cash flow of $31.9M is a 1.3% yield on market cap. Summing the FY2026 working-capital lines gives a net release of approximately $52.1M (+$6.8M receivables, +$38.2M inventories, −$6.7M prepaids, +$19.8M payables and accruals, −$6.0M other non-current). Excluding it, operating cash flow was approximately $31.1M and free cash flow approximately −$20.2M. Inventory days fell from roughly 147 to 114 as the 3M-transition safety stock unwound, and accrued compensation rose from $14.1M to $26.8M — a cash outflow that lands in FY2027.

Two offsetting readings deserve equal weight. The inventory reduction may be a permanent structural gain rather than a reversing benefit, in which case the level holds and simply does not repeat. And capex now runs at 0.44x depreciation and amortization of $116.3M following completion of the Lansing facility (construction in progress fell from $186.2M to $69.8M while buildings rose from $108.7M to $234.2M), so maintenance-level free cash generation should improve mechanically. Both readings support the same conclusion: FY2026 free cash flow should not be annualized or capitalized. Normalized free cash generation is currently near zero, and GAAP depreciation steps up in FY2027.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $185.5M; total debt $793.7M, all non-current, down $100.4M year over year; net debt $608.2M, or 3.42x FY2026 adjusted EBITDA. Current ratio 3.82x. Net interest expense $57.6M (cash interest $58.3M), giving adjusted EBITDA interest coverage of 3.09x and a blended cash cost of debt near 6.9%. Negative tangible common equity of $272.3M.

Reconstructing the classic Altman Z on FY2026 inputs gives approximately 1.37, below the 1.81 distress threshold; the Z″ variant on the same inputs gives approximately 2.39, in that variant's grey zone. The distress reading is driven by two non-cash terms — the accumulated deficit created by the FY2025 goodwill impairment and the GAAP operating loss of $21.6M, which reflects $89.1M of acquisition intangible amortization. Actual near-term solvency is better than the score implies: no current maturities, 3.8x current ratio, 3.1x coverage, $185.5M cash. But negative tangible equity means there is no asset cushion beneath $793.7M of debt, and the impairment that produced the deficit reflects economic value that was genuinely destroyed.

Financial health scores 4.2 — a marginal improvement on the prior review, reflecting the $100.4M debt paydown, the completed capex cycle, and ICFR remediation, held back by near-zero normalized free cash flow, 3.42x leverage, and dependence on an unclosed divestiture for further deleveraging.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture separates cleanly into a healthy core and a shrinking whole.

The core is genuine. Indicator Testing/Culture Media grew 6.6% for the year and 9.5% in Q4; Bacterial & General Sanitation grew 6.4%. Combined, 58.4% of revenue grew 6.5% for the year and accelerated to 9.6% in Q4 — through a period that included executive turnover, supply shortages, a $1.06B write-down and a manufacturing separation. That is meaningful evidence of workflow lock-in in validated test methods.

The whole is not growing. FY2027 guidance of $880-885M is +1.1% to +1.7% on reported FY2026 revenue and includes $92M of Genomics revenue that will be removed on closing. Implied core growth is approximately 3%, with a 1% FX headwind embedded. Adjusted EBITDA is guided flat.

Estimate revisions are mixed and, on the metric that matters most for a multi-year thesis, negative. Current-quarter estimates rose 6.7% over 30 days and 18.5% over 90 days; current-year rose 3.3% and 4.5% to $0.31. But next-year consensus was cut 9.5% over 30 days and 7.3% over 90 days, to $0.38 from $0.42. Three analysts cover the out-year. This is not the estimate-revision profile that supports paying up — the near-term bar was reset lower and is now being cleared, while the out-year earnings power that justifies a 17x EBITDA multiple is being marked down.

The earnings surprise record shows four consecutive beats, but the bases are inconsistent and the bar had been cut. Q4 FY2026 was a loss of $0.05 against a $0.07 estimated loss; the three prior quarters were beats of 96%, 78% and 53% (Q1 through Q3 FY2026, against estimates that had roughly halved). Full-year adjusted EPS still declined, to $0.32 from $0.33. The beats measure expectation reset as much as operational improvement, which limits how much confidence they should add to forward estimates.

Two identified upside vectors exist beyond the base plan. Petrifilm in-sourcing should deliver both the roll-off of $12.0M of duplicate manufacturing cost and the elimination of a contract-manufacturing margin. And Neogen received expanded FIFRA Section 2(ee) recommendations for two Prozap products tied to the US New World Screwworm response — real but unquantified, and not modellable.

Growth prospects score 4.5: a durable, sticky, mid-single-digit consumables core inside a corporate structure that is not compounding earnings.

| Enterprise value ($2,457.8M + $793.7M − $185.5M) | $3,066M |

| EV / FY2027 guided adjusted EBITDA ($181M) | 16.9x |
| EV / FY2024 peak adjusted EBITDA ($213.2M) | 14.4x |
| EV / FY2026 revenue | 3.52x |

| Price / consensus next-year EPS ($0.38) | 29.7x |
| Reported FCF yield | 1.30% |
| Normalized (ex-working-capital) FCF yield | ≈ −0.8% |
| Net debt / adjusted EBITDA | 3.42x |

Peer benchmarks are of limited use here: four of five listed comparables carry negative P/Es (XRAY −3.2, BLFS −109.7, QDEL −0.8, AVNS −15.6), leaving HAE at 38.9x as the only positive reading and making the 38.9x "median" an artifact of loss-making comparables rather than a usable anchor. Neogen's GAAP earnings are likewise negative (trailing EPS −$0.04), so the entire valuation case has to rest on EV/EBITDA and normalized earnings power.

On that basis, 16.9x forward EBITDA for approximately 3% core growth, flat guided EBITDA, 3.4x leverage and zero normalized free cash flow embeds substantial execution success already. Mid-teens EV/EBITDA is a reasonable range for a high-quality food-safety consumables franchise with mid-single-digit growth and modest leverage; Neogen has the franchise but not yet the growth, the leverage profile, or the cash conversion. Applying 15.5x to FY2027 guided EBITDA of $181M gives an EV of $2,806M, less net debt of $608M, or $2,198M of equity — approximately **$10.10 per share**. Blending in some credit for the pending deleveraging brings base case to roughly **$10.25**, about 9% below spot.

The medium-term bull arithmetic is legitimate and should be stated plainly. If the $72.4M of recurring add-backs partially rolls off, if Petrifilm in-sourcing removes a contract-manufacturing margin, and if the core compounds at 5%, adjusted EBITDA could reach $210-220M by FY2029 on a post-Genomics base — the level Neogen achieved in FY2024. At 16x on $210M with net debt reduced to roughly $400M, that is $2,960M of equity, or **$13.60**; at 17x on $220M, **$15.30**. That is the bull case, and it requires three separate things to work over three years.

The Genomics transaction is roughly EV-multiple-neutral and meaningfully deleveraging. At ~$140M net proceeds against $13M of embedded adjusted EBITDA, Neogen is selling at 10.8x, below its own ~17x trading multiple, so the deal is modestly dilutive to EV/EBITDA (pro forma approximately 17.6x) while cutting net leverage from 3.42x to about 2.79x and lifting corporate margin mix from 20.4% toward 21.2%. The case rests on the balance sheet and focus, not on multiple arbitrage.

Analyst targets ($12.00 low, $14.00 high, $13.00 mean, four contributing analysts) sit 6% to 24% above spot. We do not treat them as a floor here: the guardrail for that treatment is a cheap multiple against rising estimates, and Neogen has neither — 16.9x forward EBITDA with the out-year EPS estimate cut 9.5% in 30 days.

Valuation scores 3.8. The multiple is not distressed and not cheap; the asymmetry at spot is unfavorable.

## Competitive Landscape

Food safety testing is a structurally attractive end market. Demand is driven by regulation, retailer specifications and recall-avoidance economics rather than discretionary spending, and the consumables are inexpensive relative to the cost of a failure. Neogen's own numbers evidence customer stickiness: the two core consumable lines grew 6.5% for the year and 9.6% in Q4 through the worst internal disruption in the company's history. If switching were easy, FY2025-FY2026 was the window. Q4 gross margin commentary attributes part of the gain to pricing benefits, indicating the company held price rather than discounting to defend volume.

The competitive set includes bioMérieux, Hygiena, Thermo Fisher, Merck KGaA/Millipore and Romer Labs. We are not making a quantitative share claim; the filings retain competition, potential customer loss and distributor dependence as standalone risk factors, and no customer- or distributor-concentration figures are disclosed. Improving revenue mix (higher-margin consumables rising as a share of the total) is evidenced; reduced customer or channel concentration is not.

The competitive vulnerability sits on the supply side. 3M still manufactures Petrifilm under transition manufacturing agreements; Neogen owns the product, sells it, and bears the customer-facing quality and supply obligation. Dependence on key suppliers and potential disruption in manufacturing and service operations are both explicit risk factors. In-sourcing to Lansing is the correct strategic response and should be a structural gross-margin tailwind beyond the $12.0M duplicate-cost roll-off, but it converts a supplier dependency into an execution risk on the largest and fastest-growing product line.

The offsetting structural concern is scale. Pro forma for Genomics, Neogen is roughly a $790M-revenue food-safety pure-play at approximately 21% adjusted EBITDA margin with about $470M of net debt — cleaner and more coherent than the FY2024 portfolio, but smaller, with less absorptive capacity against a $57.6M annual interest burden and an R&D budget that ran at 2.1% of revenue and needs to rise.

Competitive position scores 4.8, an improvement from the prior review's 4.3 on the consumables acceleration and demonstrated pricing power, capped by supplier dependence, sub-scale R&D and unquantified share position.

## Risk Assessment

**Petrifilm transition (highest stakes, not yet begun).** Per the July 30 release, the first fully validated SKU is expected by end of August 2026 and the multi-quarter transition to Lansing begins in November 2026. Duplicate manufacturing costs ran $12.0M in FY2026 against $2.2M in FY2025 — ramping, not winding down. Petrifilm sits inside a $332.7M line growing 6.6%, so a yield problem, validation delay or customer requalification friction damages the growth engine rather than a peripheral product. The internal base rate is unhelpful: the sample-collection transition off 3M produced $15.0M (FY2025) and $12.4M (FY2026) of transition and ramp-up costs plus acknowledged shortages. The counter-evidence is that the Q4 sample-collection add-back was a −$1.0M credit and Animal Safety grew over 7% sequentially, so the organization did eventually solve the earlier problem.

**Deleveraging depends on a deal under antitrust review.** The $160M Genomics sale to Zoetis, with ~$140M net proceeds earmarked primarily for debt reduction, is targeted to close by the end of H1 FY2027. In July 2026 both the ACCC and the New Zealand Commerce Commission moved to second-phase review, with reported concerns in animal genomic testing. A block or material delay leaves net leverage near 3.4x with no alternative deleveraging lever given approximately zero normalized free cash flow, and leaves Neogen holding a sub-scale, ~14%-margin business it has already restructured. Break-fee and remedy provisions are unverified.

**Further impairment.** $1,047.2M of goodwill and $1,318.0M of intangibles remain after the $1,059.3M FY2025 write-down, together 70.7% of assets, against negative tangible common equity of $272.3M. Potential asset impairments is an explicit risk factor. A second write-down would be non-cash and would not affect covenant compliance, but it would deepen the accumulated deficit and confirm that the FSD purchase price is unrecoverable.

**Governance and continuity.** Fourteen Item 5.02 filings in roughly 22 months document a near-complete C-suite replacement: former CEO, CFO, COO and Chief Legal Officer all replaced, plus two new directors. FY2027 is this team's first full year and Q4 FY2026 was their first clean quarter. An additional Item 5.02 8-K was filed August 7, 2026, eight days after the 10-K, and its content is unread — a named verification gap that could be routine or adverse.

**Internal controls.** Material weaknesses were remediated as of May 31, 2026, but the Q3 FY2026 10-Q (quarter ended February 28, 2026) still concluded disclosure controls were not effective. One clean quarter after a multi-year deficiency is thin evidence of durable control quality, and identified material weaknesses remain in the forward-looking risk list.

**Litigation.** A putative securities class action and related derivative actions concerning 3M FSD integration disclosures remain outstanding. Combined litigation and recall add-backs were $4.2M in FY2026 versus $2.3M, corroborating a veterinary product recall and associated product liability claims.

**Tariffs, FX, and end-market cyclicality.** With 51.2% of revenue international and manufacturing concentrated in the US and UK, two-way tariff exposure is explicit; FY2027 guidance embeds a 1% negative FX impact. Animal Safety demand tracks protein-producer capex and herd cycles.

Risk factors score 3.5: a stack of live binary items — Petrifilm start, antitrust second phase, impairment, unread 8-K — that the current 17x multiple does not appear to discount.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 67.0% on July 22 to 97.4% on August 7, a 30.5-point increase, while the stock rose 15.9%. Implied volatility normally compresses after an earnings event; here it expanded sharply afterward. The market is pricing materially higher uncertainty over the next several weeks than it was before the print, which is consistent with the Petrifilm validation milestone (end of August), the ACCC/NZCC second-phase reviews, and the unread August 7 Item 5.02 filing. A 97% ATM implied volatility on a $2.5B healthcare-supplies name is an unusually wide distribution and cuts against the view that the July 30 print resolved the story. Short interest of 6.6% of float at 5.4 days to cover is moderate and neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view; we do not read direction into it. For a holder, elevated implied volatility makes covered call overwriting or collar structures relatively attractive versus outright exposure, and it argues against adding at spot.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $15.00 (+33%)

The consumables core is a genuine franchise: 58.4% of revenue growing 6.5% for the year and 9.6% in Q4, with pricing power demonstrated through the worst possible operating conditions and workflow lock-in from validated test methods and AOAC certifications. Adjusted gross margin never deteriorated (50.2% to 50.3%), proving the GAAP compression was transition cost. Capex has fallen to 0.44x D&A following completion of Lansing, so maintenance free cash flow improves mechanically. The new management team, fully installed with equity struck at $6.29-$9.53, executed decisive portfolio surgery: Cleaners & Disinfectants sold for $121.7M cash and a $76.4M gain, Genomics agreed at $160M, and $100.4M of debt repaid. If Genomics closes, leverage falls to approximately 2.79x. If Petrifilm in-sources successfully, $12.0M of duplicate cost rolls off and a contract-manufacturing margin is captured. If transformation and transaction costs — $32.9M combined in FY2026 — recede as the ERP and portfolio work finish, adjusted EBITDA reaches $210-220M by FY2029 on a post-Genomics base. At 16-17x with net debt near $400M, that is $13.60-$15.30. FY2027's flat guide is self-inflicted, absorbing a substantial R&D step-up and commercial reinvestment, which makes FY2028 the inflection year.

### Bear Case — $6.50 (−42%)

The Petrifilm transition repeats the sample-collection experience: validation slips, duplicate costs run above $12M into FY2028, and requalification friction slows the one line that is growing. The Genomics sale is blocked or remedied down in Australia and New Zealand, leaving leverage at 3.4x with no cash-generative deleveraging path — normalized free cash flow is approximately −$20M and the FY2027 reversal of the $12.7M accrued-compensation build plus higher depreciation makes GAAP results worse before they get better. Adjusted EBITDA slips to $160M as R&D and commercial investment land without revenue response, and the multiple compresses to 12x on a levered, sub-scale, no-growth asset: $1,920M EV less $608M net debt is $1,312M of equity, or $6.03. A second goodwill impairment against $1,047.2M of remaining goodwill confirms the FSD thesis failure and reinforces the derating. The August 7 Item 5.02 filing turns out to be another senior departure, restarting the continuity clock.

### Base Case — $10.25 (−9%)

FY2027 delivers approximately as guided: revenue $880-885M, adjusted EBITDA $181M, core growth near 3%. Genomics closes late, leverage settles near 2.8-3.0x. Petrifilm transition begins on schedule with the customary friction and neither breaks the growth line nor delivers the full margin benefit within twelve months. The transformation cost base declines slowly rather than rolling off. Fair value at 15.5x forward EBITDA is roughly $10.10, or $10.25 with credit for deleveraging progress.

Probability weighting 20% bull / 50% base / 30% bear gives an expected return of approximately −10%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** This is a valuation and asymmetry call. The core franchise evidence improved in FY2026; the price improved faster. The shares are 143% above their August 2025 low of $4.63 and 14% above the level at our July 22 review, while forward adjusted EBITDA guidance is flat and out-year consensus EPS has been cut 9.5% in 30 days. Prior reviews carried HOLD at meaningfully lower prices; at $11.30 the base case sits below spot and the downside case is 1.3x the magnitude of the upside case.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into current strength rather than waiting for a catalyst — implied volatility at 97.4% and the stock 12% below its August 3 intraday high of $12.85 indicate a wide near-term distribution around a base case below spot. Holders who want to retain franchise optionality should reduce to a residual position and consider overwriting against elevated implied volatility. Do not add at spot. The natural decision window is before the Petrifilm transition start in November 2026, because that is the point at which the company takes on execution risk in its largest growing product line.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. Genomics closes with net proceeds of at least $135M applied to debt, taking reported net debt below $480M and net leverage below 2.9x on FY2027 adjusted EBITDA.
2. Two consecutive quarters of core revenue growth at 5% or better with adjusted EBITDA margin at 21% or above, evidencing that the FY2027 reinvestment is converting.
3. Petrifilm transition milestones met — first validated SKU confirmed shipping, Lansing transition started in November 2026 without a duplicate-cost increase above the FY2026 $12.0M run rate.
4. Price at or below $9.00, which is roughly 14.5x FY2027 guided EBITDA and restores a margin of safety to the base case.

**Avoid triggers (full thesis impairment):**
1. Genomics sale blocked or abandoned in Australia/New Zealand without an alternative deleveraging transaction, leaving leverage at or above 3.4x.
2. Petrifilm validation slips past calendar Q1 2027 or duplicate manufacturing costs exceed $18M annualized.
3. Any FY2027 quarter in which core Food Safety growth falls below 2%, or adjusted EBITDA guidance is cut below $170M.
4. A new material weakness in internal control, or a further senior departure among the four executives installed since November 2025.

**Reassessment timeline.** Three dated checkpoints: the first fully validated Petrifilm SKU by end of August 2026; the Q1 FY2027 print (guided revenue $207-209M, adjusted EBITDA approximately $37M), expected early October 2026; and the Genomics closing deadline at the end of H1 FY2027 (November 30, 2026). The content of the August 7, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K should be reviewed as soon as it is accessible, since it bears directly on management continuity.

## Conclusion

Neogen is a better business than its GAAP financials show and a worse investment than its recent share price implies. The core food-safety consumables franchise — 58.4% of revenue, growing 6.5% for the year and 9.6% in the fourth quarter, with adjusted gross margin flat at 50.3% and pricing power demonstrated through severe internal disruption — is real and worth owning at the right price. Portfolio surgery has been decisive: $121.7M of cash from Cleaners & Disinfectants at a $76.4M gain, $160M agreed for Genomics, $100.4M of debt repaid, capex normalized to 0.44x D&A, and internal control weaknesses remediated.

But adjusted EBITDA is 16.6% below its FY2024 peak and guided flat for a fourth year; normalized free cash flow is approximately zero once the $52.1M working-capital release is removed from FY2026's reported $31.9M; net leverage is 3.42x against negative tangible common equity of $272.3M; the deleveraging plan hinges on a transaction now in second-phase antitrust review in two jurisdictions; and the Petrifilm in-sourcing that underwrites the bull case begins in November 2026 against an unhelpful internal precedent. Paying 16.9x forward adjusted EBITDA and 35x adjusted EPS for that combination leaves no margin for the execution the story requires. Base case $10.25, bull $15.00, bear $6.50, probability-weighted expected return approximately −10%. **REDUCE.**
