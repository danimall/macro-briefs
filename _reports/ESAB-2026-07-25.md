**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ESAB is a ~120-year-old global manufacturer of welding/cutting equipment, consumables, and gas control equipment, spun out of Colfax in April 2022 and run on a Danaher-lineage "premier industrial compounder" model: serial acquisitions plus a lean operating system (EBXai). The business is asset-light (~66% consumables, ~1.7% capex/sales), globally diversified (~80% of sales outside the U.S., ~52% from high-growth markets), and has genuine operating discipline: adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 19.7% in FY2025 even as GAAP earnings fell. The compounder engine is running on M&A, not organic momentum. FY2025 net sales rose 3.7% but organic sales fell 1.1%, and GAAP net income declined from $264.8M to $226.8M. Q1 2026 continuing-ops net income fell 29% year-over-year.

The stock has fallen 36% from its 52-week high of $137.42 to $84.44, and the central question is whether that decline reflects genuine deterioration or an overshoot. The evidence points to real, if bounded, fundamental softening rather than pure sentiment: consensus current-year EPS has been revised down 1.3% over 90 days and current-quarter estimates down 8.3%, three of the last four quarters were EPS misses, and margins are compressing under tariff-driven input inflation and dilutive pass-through pricing. Overlaid on this is the largest deal in company history, the ~$1.45B Eddyfi acquisition (closed June 2, 2026), a strategic pivot into non-destructive inspection funded with ~$1B new debt plus $318M dilutive equity, closed before investors saw target financials. Debt roughly doubled to ~$2.06B in a single quarter.

The offsetting positive is a high-conviction insider signal: Executive Chair Mitchell Rales personally bought $100M of the mandatory convertible preferred issued to fund Eddyfi (brother Steven added $25M), a rare instance of a controlling-family sponsor funding the pivot alongside shareholders. At ~14.8x forward earnings on $5.70 FY26 consensus and near the low end of the analyst target range ($117–$154), the valuation is no longer demanding. But with organic growth flat, estimates still drifting lower, integration and leverage risk elevated, and the first pro-forma Eddyfi disclosure not yet in hand, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate ESAB a HOLD and would upgrade on evidence that organic growth is reaccelerating or that Eddyfi is accretive on a de-levered basis.

## Company Overview

ESAB (founded 1904) is a global fabrication-technology company serving customers in ~150 countries with manufacturing in 16+ countries and ~10,300 employees. It became independent in April 2022 via the Colfax/Enovis separation. The product portfolio spans welding and cutting equipment, consumables, robotics, and digital solutions, plus gas control equipment (including medical/specialty gas). The revenue mix is roughly 66% consumables and 34% equipment, with consumables providing recurring, aftermarket-like demand at lower margins than equipment.

The company reports two segments: Americas (~40% of sales) and EMEA & APAC (~60%). Its stated strategy is to operate as a "focused premier industrial compounder," combining global scale, a high-growth-market weighting (~52% of sales in South America, Eastern Europe, India, APAC, and the Middle East), the EBXai operating system, and a disciplined M&A engine. Named competitors are Lincoln Electric (the pure-play welding leader) and the welding unit of Illinois Tool Works, alongside fragmented regional players.

The June 2026 Eddyfi acquisition broadens ESAB beyond welding into non-destructive testing/inspection and structural-health monitoring, an adjacent, higher-margin, more technology- and software-intensive space intended to diversify away from mature, cyclical welding demand.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and growth composition.** Net sales progressed from $2.59B (2022) to $2.77B (2023), $2.74B (2024), and $2.84B (2025). The FY2025 +3.7% headline decomposes into organic −1.1% (−$31.4M), acquisitions +4.2% (+$115.9M), and FX +0.6%. Ex-Russia core organic was roughly flat (−0.2%). Q1 2026 sales rose 9.9% to $745.6M, but organic was −1.9%, with M&A (+6.3%) and FX (+5.5%) driving the gain. The growth engine is acquisition-led, and the underlying business is not currently expanding.

**Margins are compressing at the GAAP line but holding on an adjusted basis.** Gross margin fell from 37.9% (FY2024) to 36.9% (FY2025), and Q1 2026 gross margin was 36.9% versus 37.6% a year earlier. Q1 2026 continuing-ops net income was $51.8M versus $72.6M. The drivers are specific and identifiable: 50% U.S. steel/aluminum tariffs raising input costs, dilutive tariff pass-through pricing in the Americas (raising revenue dollars while lowering margin percentage), and, in Q1 2026, higher freight/oil costs from Middle East disruption in the Strait of Hormuz. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded (19.7% vs 19.3%; core 20.0%), so much of the GAAP decline reflects rising acquisition amortization, transaction costs, and restructuring rather than operating deterioration. This gap between adjusted and GAAP earnings is characteristic of the roll-up model; it requires judging the company on adjusted metrics while scrutinizing whether the M&A actually clears its cost of capital.

**Cash flow and capital intensity.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $260.6M (down from $355.4M in 2024 on working-capital build, higher interest, and deal costs), with FCF of ~$213M (7.5% margin). Capex is light at ~$47M (~1.7% of sales), consistent with the asset-light consumables model. The dividend is small ($0.10/quarter) and well-covered; a 5M-share buyback authorized in August 2024 remains entirely unused.

**Balance sheet — the key watch item.** Goodwill of $1.95B plus intangibles of $0.67B represented ~55% of the pre-Eddyfi $4.77B asset base. The Altman Z-Score of 2.18 sits in the grey zone, reflecting heavy goodwill and leverage rather than distress, while the Piotroski score of 5 signals healthy underlying profitability and cash generation. Post-Eddyfi financing dramatically re-levered the company: in Q1 2026 ESAB issued $1.0B of 5.625% senior notes due 2031 on top of $700M of 6.25% notes due 2029 and a $350M term loan, pushing total debt to ~$2.06B (with cash near $1.0B pre-deployment). Long-term debt jumped from $1.23B to $2.03B in one quarter. FY2025 interest expense was $66.6M at a ~5.4% weighted average rate, and both leverage and interest cost will step up further with the Eddyfi financing. Covenants are EBITDA-leverage and interest-coverage based, so a margin miss or downturn would tighten headroom.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two distinct layers, and they are diverging. The organic layer is stalled, with ex-Russia core organic essentially flat over the past year. Welding and cutting is a mature, cyclical business in developed markets tied to industrial capex, and the high-growth-market exposure that should offset this has not yet lifted the organic line back into positive territory.

The acquisition layer is where all reported growth is coming from. ESAB completed four deals in 2025 (Bavaria, DeltaP, Aktiv, EWM), plus Sager, Bangladesh, and SUMIG in 2024, and now Eddyfi, its largest ever, adding a ~$240–270M revenue non-destructive inspection business. Each deal adds amortization and integration cost that suppresses GAAP earnings; EWM integration is explicitly cited as diluting EMEA margins. The strategic logic of Eddyfi is sound (higher-margin, tech/software content, diversification away from cyclical welding) but the execution and financial-accretion evidence is not yet available. Investors have very limited data: the 10-Q disclosed that Eddyfi's historical and pro-forma financials were not available pre-close and are due within 75 days.

Estimate revisions confirm the near-term deceleration. Current-quarter EPS consensus fell to $1.37 from $1.50 ninety days ago (−8.3%); current-year fell to $5.70 (−1.3%); and the beat/miss record is 1 beat, 3 misses over the last four quarters. The one bright spot is next-year consensus, revised up 4.0% over 90 days to $6.67, likely reflecting expected Eddyfi contribution and margin recovery. The estimate-revision picture does not support a growth-inflection thesis; the trend is broadly downward for the near term, with the out-year improvement dependent on M&A execution the market cannot yet verify. This falls well short of the revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

At $84.44, ESAB trades at ~14.8x FY26 consensus of $5.70 and ~12.7x next-year $6.67. On a trailing basis, at $4.10 EPS, the P/E is ~20.6x. Against the peer group in industrial machinery — FELE 30.7x, MWA 19.4x, SXI 37.0x, GTES 28.2x, NPO 163.1x, with a peer median of 30.7x — ESAB screens as inexpensive on forward earnings, at roughly half the peer median multiple.

The discount is partly justified and partly a potential opportunity. The justification: organic growth is flat versus peers that are growing, leverage roughly doubled to fund a large deal, and near-term estimates are falling. The compounder model dilutes GAAP earnings quality through amortization, which the market appears to be penalizing. The offset: the business generates real free cash flow (~$213M, 7.5% margin), adjusted EBITDA margins are expanding, the model has created shareholder value since the spin (TSR 252 vs peer index 166), and the stock now sits near the low end of the analyst target range ($117–$154, mean $135.40). The 36% drawdown has moved the stock from a demanding multiple at the highs to a below-peer level.

The prudent read is that this is a fairly-valued industrial roll-up in a soft patch, not an obvious mispricing. A low forward multiple on falling estimates is not a buy signal by itself; cheapness without a catalyst frequently persists, and roll-ups can trade at structural discounts when organic growth is absent and integration risk is elevated. The re-rating case rests on Eddyfi proving accretive and organic growth turning positive; neither is yet in the numbers. We assign a moderate valuation score: the price is reasonable and offers some downside support near analyst floors, but there is no identified catalyst to close the gap in the near term, and the "cheap" screen must be treated with the skepticism a value-trap setup warrants.

## Competitive Landscape

ESAB operates in fragmented, niche-driven markets where it claims leadership in its served segments; no single competitor spans all its markets. Against Lincoln Electric and ITW's welding unit, ESAB differentiates on global scale, high-growth-market weighting, the EBXai operating system, and its M&A engine. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving on strategy but ambiguous on execution: flat organic growth suggests it is not gaining meaningful share in its core welding markets, while the Eddyfi pivot could improve the mix toward higher-margin, more differentiated inspection/monitoring where competition is different and the technology content higher.

The sector-wide margin pressure from tariffs and trade-policy volatility is not ESAB-specific; peers are absorbing the same input inflation. That limits the read-through of margin compression as a competitive-position signal. The genuine trajectory question is whether Eddyfi adds durable, defensible content (positive for the moat) or becomes an expensive distraction (negative). Given ~5–6x revenue paid and no visible standalone financials, that verdict is pending. Competitive position is roughly stable, with the pivot representing an option on improvement that is not yet demonstrated.

## Risk Assessment

**Eddyfi execution, leverage, and impairment.** The single most important item. The deal closed before investors saw target financials, concentrating execution, leverage, and impairment risk. With goodwill and intangibles already ~55% of the asset base pre-deal, Eddyfi materially raises that share and the sensitivity of impairment tests to discount-rate and growth assumptions.

**Acquisition-dependent growth.** With organic growth flat, the model relies on continual M&A. If deals fail to earn their cost of capital, GAAP returns erode even as adjusted metrics look healthy.

**Tariffs and trade policy.** The dominant near-term earnings driver, with pass-through pricing margin-dilutive. There is unbooked optionality: a February 2026 Supreme Court ruling found IEEPA tariffs unauthorized with refunds ordered then suspended; ESAB conservatively recorded nothing, an uncertain upside not in current numbers.

**Legacy asbestos liabilities.** Inherited from Colfax: ~$307M accrued liability versus ~$248M insurance asset, with claims rising — unresolved claims grew 14,836 to 15,712 in Q1 2026, and new filings jumped to 1,498 from 887 year-over-year. This runs through discontinued operations at ~$15M annual cash outflow, a slow-burn liability worth tracking against the finite accrual window and insurer solvency.

**Russia exposure (stranded).** ~4–5% of sales/net assets, ~$42M cash trapped, and a ~$108M cumulative translation loss that would crystallize on exit. The business is effectively in run-off/limbo.

**FX translation.** With ~80% international sales, a 10% USD appreciation would reduce equity by ~$244M, driving reported results materially in both directions.

**Leadership churn.** The CFO seat was vacant/interim (CEO doubling as interim principal financial officer) through early 2026 during the largest deal in company history, now filled by R. Brent Jones, combined with the Fabrication Technology president's May 2026 departure. Elevated turnover to monitor, partially offset by the Danaher-pedigree hire of Ranjana Bhagwakar.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is flashing near-term caution. ATM IV of 63.4% sits rich versus 1-year historical vol of 46.5% (a 16.9pp spread) and at the 85th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (63.4% near-term versus 45.7% at six months), pricing imminent stress consistent with an approaching Q2 print that will carry the first Eddyfi contribution and pro-forma disclosure. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±13.7%. ATM IV has collapsed from 213.5% in mid-June (a distorted reading) to 63.4%, and price fell 20.5% over that window. Near-term skew is roughly balanced (2.0%), turning modestly put-heavy at three months (−7.9%). The rich near-term IV argues against chasing entry ahead of the print; a better-informed view is available after earnings and the 75-day pro-forma filing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$135–$154).** Eddyfi proves accretive, diversifies ESAB toward higher-margin inspection/monitoring with software content, and the 75-day pro-forma filing validates the ~5–6x revenue price. Organic growth turns positive as high-growth-market demand recovers and tariff pass-through normalizes. Adjusted EBITDA margin continues expanding, GAAP earnings recover as amortization is absorbed against growing revenue, and the multiple re-rates from ~15x toward the peer median. The Rales family's $125M personal capital and TSR outperformance prove prescient. Unbooked tariff-refund optionality provides a cash windfall. At the bull case, ~13x on ~$6.70+ next-year EPS supports the analyst high of $154.

**Bear Case (~$70).** Organic growth stays negative, Eddyfi's premium multiple proves hard to justify against soft welding demand, and integration costs plus rising interest expense compress GAAP earnings further. A goodwill/intangible impairment surfaces given the elevated intangible share of assets. Covenant headroom tightens in a cyclical downturn. Asbestos claims accelerate and Russia crystallizes losses. The multiple stays depressed or compresses further on the roll-up discount, taking the stock toward $70 (~12x depressed forward earnings).

**Base Case (~$100).** Eddyfi contributes as expected but takes several quarters to demonstrate clear accretion; organic growth stabilizes near flat-to-slightly-positive; margins bottom and adjusted metrics continue to expand modestly. The multiple recovers partway toward peers as leverage normalizes, supporting ~16x on ~$6.30 blended forward earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; we lack sufficient evidence for a stronger call in either direction until the Eddyfi financials and Q2 results are in hand.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The 75-day Eddyfi pro-forma filing shows the business is accretive to adjusted EPS and margins, with a leverage path back below ~2.5x net debt/EBITDA within 12–18 months.
- Organic sales growth turns positive for two consecutive quarters (currently −1.9% in Q1 2026).
- Current-year and current-quarter EPS estimate revisions turn positive after the recent current-quarter cut.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill or intangible impairment charge, or covenant-headroom disclosure indicating leverage stress.
- Organic sales decline accelerating beyond ~−3%, signaling deeper cyclical or share loss.
- Eddyfi pro-forma revealing materially worse standalone economics than the ~5–6x revenue price implies (e.g., low/negative margins requiring a re-marking of the deal).
- A booked adverse revision to the asbestos accrual or Russia crystallization.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 2026 earnings call (announced July 16, 2026) is the next natural checkpoint; it carries the first Eddyfi contribution and should coincide with the pro-forma disclosure window. Reassess promptly after that release.

## Conclusion

ESAB is a well-run industrial compounder trading at a below-peer forward multiple after a 36% drawdown, with a genuinely strong insider-alignment signal in the Rales family's $125M personal capital into the Eddyfi financing. Those are real positives. Against them sit flat organic growth, falling near-term estimates, a three-of-four-quarter miss record, a balance sheet that roughly doubled its debt for a premium-priced pivot closed before investors saw target financials, and margin compression that, while partly tariff-driven and sector-wide, is not yet reversing. The adjusted-versus-GAAP earnings gap is characteristic of the roll-up model and demands scrutiny of whether the M&A clears its cost of capital, a question Eddyfi has not yet answered.

The score trajectory (5.3 → 6.0 → 5.6, three consecutive HOLDs) reflects a company oscillating around fair value as financial-health and risk readings improved modestly while growth prospects softened. The current data confirms rather than reverses that pattern: financial health is pressured by re-leveraging, growth remains M&A-dependent with flat organics, and the primary catalyst (Eddyfi accretion) is unverifiable until the pro-forma filing. The valuation is reasonable and near analyst floors, but "cheap" on falling estimates without a proven catalyst is a balanced setup, not an asymmetric one. We maintain HOLD, with the Q2 print and Eddyfi disclosure as the decisive near-term inputs. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
