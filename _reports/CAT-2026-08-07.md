## Executive Summary

Caterpillar delivered the strongest quarter in its history on August 4, 2026: revenue of $20.543B (+24.0%), the first $20B quarter the company has reported, with operating margin of 20.9% against 17.3% a year earlier and adjusted EPS of $8.17 versus consensus near $6.18. Backlog reached $72.1B, up roughly 92% year over year and equal to 0.96x trailing-twelve-month revenue versus 0.46x at the end of 2024. Management raised full-year revenue guidance from low-double-digit to mid-to-high-teens growth. Consensus has followed: FY2026 EPS is now $26.85, up 10.7% in 90 days, and FY2027 is $31.97, up 9.1%. Four consecutive beats have averaged 16.7% upside to estimates.

The single most important fundamental change is not the AI-power narrative but the restoration of pricing power. FY2025 saw enterprise price realization of **-$817M**, with Construction Industries at **-$1.136B** — Caterpillar absorbed roughly $1.8B of tariff cost while discounting machines. In H1 2026 enterprise price realization was **+$1.021B**, with CI at **+$665M**, against FY2026 tariff cost now guided at ~$2.2B (down from ~$2.6B in the February 10-K). Price is now more than covering the incremental duty burden. CI segment margin expanded to 23.3% from 20.1% and Power & Energy to 24.6% from 22.1%.

Three offsets keep this a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE. First, total dealer inventory rose $2.6B in H1 2026 versus $0.2B in H1 2025 (CI: +$1.9B versus -$0.4B); that ~$2.4B swing equals roughly 34% of the $7.140B H1 revenue increase, and management has explicitly guided to a CI dealer inventory decrease of over $1.0B in Q4 2026 with an unfavorable dealer-inventory contribution to CI volume in H2. Guidance itself implies H2 revenue growth of +8% to +15.5%, decelerating from H1's +23.2%. Second, $392M of duty recoveries credited to cost of goods sold contributed ~$0.65 to Q2 adjusted EPS, and management assumes no further recoveries in H2. Third, at 31.4x FY2026 and 26.4x FY2027 consensus the market is capitalizing the current earnings level as durable in a business whose five-year net income series ($6.489B, $6.705B, $10.335B, $10.792B, $8.884B) has not compounded. The stock surged 11-12% intraday on the print, closed +5.6%, and has since given back the entire move — the marginal buyer is already looking past Q2 to Q4.

## Company Overview

Caterpillar, incorporated in Delaware since 1986, manufactures construction and mining equipment, off-highway diesel and natural gas engines, industrial gas turbines and diesel-electric locomotives. Four reportable segments plus All Other:

- **Construction Industries** — excavators, dozers, loaders, pavers, compact equipment. Q2 2026 total segment sales $8.346B (+35%), profit $1.947B (+57%), margin 23.3%. North America external CI revenue +50%.
- **Power & Energy** — reciprocating engines, gensets, integrated power systems, turbines and turbine services, marine, oil and gas, remanufacturing. Q2 total sales $8.238B (+17%), profit $2.027B (+30%), margin 24.6%. Within P&E: Power Generation $3.098B (+29%), Oil & Gas $2.044B (+9%), Industrial $1.653B (+9%). H1 Power Generation $5.915B (+34%).
- **Resource Industries** — surface and underground mining equipment, autonomy and fleet software; now includes Rail following a January 1, 2026 recast. Q2 total sales $4.648B (+20%), profit $693M (+23%), margin 14.9%.
- **Financial Products** — Cat Financial and captive insurance. Q2 revenue $1.145B (+10%), profit $328M (+32%).

Distribution runs through 41 U.S. and 109 non-U.S. independent dealers serving 190 countries, plus 86 Perkins distributors and 108 FG Wilson distributors; turbines and locomotives are sold directly. Revenue on most machine and engine sales is recognized on sale **to the dealer**, which is the mechanical reason dealer inventory swings matter to the reported top line. Cat Financial's subsidized-rate merchandising programs — where Caterpillar or the dealer funds an amount at inception that Cat Financial recognizes over the financing term — are described in the 10-K as a significant competitive advantage; competing captives (John Deere Capital, Komatsu Financial, Volvo Financial Services) run the same mechanism.

Headcount reached 118,000 at year-end 2025, up 5,100, with essentially all growth outside the United States (non-U.S. 66,400 from 61,400; U.S. 51,600 from 51,500). Capacity expansion is being executed predominantly offshore, though acquisitions, local-content requirements and service-center growth could each explain part of the mix.

### FY2026: the inflection

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales & revenues | $20.543B | $16.569B | $37.958B | $30.818B |

Q2 profit drivers: volume +$3.1B, price realization +$595M, currency +$199M.

**Cleaning the print.** Excluding the $392M duty-recovery credit, Q2 operating margin was $3.903B / $20.543B = **19.0%**, still 170bp above Q2 2025. Applying the 23.1% effective tax rate and 462.5M diluted shares, the credit contributed ~$0.65, leaving clean adjusted EPS of **$7.52**, up 59% year over year. The recovery flattered the quarter without creating it.

**Resource Industries is going backwards.** H1 RI profit fell 9.7% to $1.071B from $1.186B and margin compressed 300bp to 12.7% from 15.7%, on segment sales up roughly 12% (derived from margin: ~$8.43B versus ~$7.55B). The 10-Q attributes this to $376M of unfavorable manufacturing costs, "largely tariffs," plus $113M of higher SG&A/R&D. RI absorbs roughly 25% of the ~$2.2B FY2026 tariff burden (CI ~50%, P&E ~25%). Q2 recovered to 14.9% from 14.5%, so the trend is improving, but mining capex is on a separate cycle and is not currently contributing to the enterprise story.

**Geography.** H1 external revenue: North America $22.324B (+35%), EAME $6.633B (+17%), Latin America $3.413B (+8%), Asia/Pacific $5.588B (+4%). This is a U.S. capex boom with a flat Asian book.

### Backlog and order quality

Backlog "believed to be firm" moved $30.0B (12/31/24) → $51.2B (12/31/25) → ~$62.7B (3/31/26, derived) → $72.1B (6/30/26), with ~$29.2B beyond twelve months and ~$42.9B near-term convertible. Two definitional cautions matter. First, this is a company-defined, unaudited operational measure with no disclosed cancellation, deposit or price-adjustment terms. Second, the ASC 606 disclosure is a different and smaller figure: unsatisfied performance obligations on contracts with original duration exceeding one year were **$44.1B**, roughly one-third recognizable within twelve months, with a practical expedient elected for shorter contracts. The two are not interchangeable. Separately, one widely circulated report characterized the $9.4B sequential backlog increase as quarterly orders booked; gross order intake would approximate revenue recognized plus backlog growth, materially higher.

The harder corroboration is cash. Contract liabilities rose from $2.745B (12/31/24) to $4.678B (12/31/25) to **$7.280B (6/30/26)**, with current customer advances of $4.777B versus $3.314B at year-end. Customers are prepaying at an accelerating rate; that money has actually changed hands.

### The dealer-inventory question

Total dealer inventory rose **$2.6B in H1 2026** versus **$0.2B in H1 2025**; CI dealer inventory rose **$1.9B** versus a **$0.4B decrease**. The ~$2.4B year-over-year swing equals ~34% of the $7.140B H1 revenue increase. A dealer-inventory change is not a revenue reconciliation — dealer purchases and dealer retail sales are separate flows and Caterpillar discloses no bridge — so 34% is an upper-bound framing rather than a measured contribution. But because revenue is recognized on sale to the dealer, it is the closest disclosed proxy for shipments not yet absorbed by an end user.

Management's own guidance settles the direction: a CI dealer inventory decrease of over $1.0B in Q4 2026, CI dealer inventory finishing 2026 above 2025, and an unfavorable dealer-inventory effect on CI volume in H2. The benign reading — dealers rationally restocking after 2025 destocking and lengthening lead times, with Creed citing non-residential investment in infrastructure, heavy construction and data centers — is plausible and not distinguishable from over-shipment on the disclosure. Either way, Q4 carries above-average risk of an optical revenue and margin miss even if retail demand holds.

### Tariffs and the duty recovery

The Supreme Court ruled the IEEPA tariffs unauthorized on February 20, 2026, and CBP ceased collection during 2026. Total IEEPA cost across 2025-2026 was approximately $1.0B; in Q2, following CBP's CAPE process launch, $392M was deemed probable and credited to cost of goods sold. FY2026 tariff cost guidance fell to ~$2.2B from ~$2.6B, with Q3 guided near $600M. Non-IEEPA duty exposure of roughly $2.2B persists — about 2.8% of implied FY2026 revenue.

One disclosure item is unresolved and should not be built into forward earnings: the 10-Q places $1.2B of import-overpayment recoveries in long-term receivables (from $377M) plus $130M in current receivables (from nil), yet states the $392M was recorded in current receivables. Either the amount was split across classifications or the balance includes non-IEEPA duty claims. The gross ~$1.33B receivable exceeds what has been recognized in income, so additional recoveries are possible upside — but management has explicitly assumed none in H2, and neither should a valuation.

### Cash flow, balance sheet, capital allocation

H1 2026 consolidated operating cash flow was $6.241B (from $4.411B). On the Machinery, Power & Energy basis management uses, OCF was $7.011B against capex of $1.313B, giving **MP&E free cash flow of $5.698B**, up 121% from $2.575B. Working capital consumed heavily: receivables -$3.182B, inventories -$2.553B, offset by payables +$1.528B and customer advances +$2.576B. Inventory composition supports a production ramp rather than finished-goods accumulation — raw materials $8.831B from $7.434B, WIP $2.087B from $1.598B, finished goods $9.298B from $8.725B.

Capital returns ran ahead of in-period cash generation. H1 repurchases consumed **$6.522B** (a $4.50B Q1 accelerated share repurchase and a $1.00B Q2 ASR terminated early in July), dividends paid $1.399B, RPMGlobal $802M — roughly $3.0B beyond MP&E FCF, funded by drawing cash from $9.980B to $6.713B. The dividend was raised 8% to $1.63 quarterly in June; the annualized $6.52 yields 0.77%. Shares outstanding fell 1.88% to 459.7M from 468.5M, with $1.60B of unsettled forwards recorded as a reduction of common stock (contracted future share reduction, not contingent dilution). $8.415B remains under the June 2024 authorization. Q2 average repurchase price was **$880.26** — the current $843.12 is 4.2% below it.

Shareholders' equity **declined** $1.924B to $19.394B despite $6.142B of H1 profit, as treasury stock rose $4.994B and common stock fell $1.527B. Equity/assets fell to 18.9% from 21.6%.

**On leverage.** Screening tools return an Altman Z of ~1.72 and debt/equity near 3.6x on consolidated FY2025 data. That is a captive-finance artifact. Of $45.146B total debt at 6/30/26, approximately **$34.5B (76%)** sits at Financial Products ($29.410B long-term plus $5.046B short-term) against $25.448B of finance receivables and a $43.777B asset base. **Industrial debt is $10.690B against $19.394B of equity** — roughly 0.55x. Ratings are high-A (Fitch, Moody's) and mid-A (S&P), with $10.014B of available credit and no Credit Facility borrowings. A related screener output of ~0.2% net margin is a data error; FY2025 net margin was $8.884B / $67.589B = **13.1%**.

The real constraint is Cat Financial's covenant: leverage not exceeding 10:1 (actual 7.96:1 at 6/30/26, improved from 8.21:1) and interest coverage of at least 1.15:1 (actual 1.54:1). Roughly 20% headroom — comfortable, and the metric to watch if a downturn simultaneously grew floorplan financing of unsold dealer inventory and impaired equity. Portfolio quality is mixed on the margin: past dues fell to 1.31% from 1.62%, but H1 gross write-offs rose to $88M from $57M and non-accruals to $178M from $163M. Supplier finance obligations within accounts payable rose to $1.15B from $936M.

## Growth Analysis

Order visibility roughly doubled relative to revenue in eighteen months. That is the durable part of the story. Backlog at 0.96x TTM revenue with $42.9B convertible inside twelve months, plus contract liabilities up 165% since year-end 2024, gives Caterpillar a demand cushion it has not carried in this cycle.

Estimate revisions confirm the inflection and are accelerating: current-quarter EPS $6.89 (+7.4% in 30 days, +8.4% in 90), current-year $26.85 (+8.6% / +10.7%), next-year $31.97 (+5.4% / +9.1%). Notably, the bulk of the FY2026 revision — $2.12 of $2.59 — landed in the last 30 days, i.e. after the Q2 print. Twenty-six to twenty-seven analysts contribute, so this is broad rather than one house moving. Revision velocity of ~10% over 90 days is meaningful but sits well below the threshold where estimate momentum alone becomes a dominant buy signal.

Guidance arithmetic tempers the growth read. Mid-to-high-teens growth on $67.589B implies $77.7-80.4B. Subtracting H1's $37.958B leaves H2 of $39.8-42.5B against H2 2025's $36.771B — **+8.0% to +15.5%**, decelerating from +23.2%. That is consistent with the guided Q4 CI destock and with Q3 tariff cost near $600M. Management does expect to increase throughput in H2 and for services revenue to grow faster than previously outlooked, with capex rising to ~$3.5B from $2.794B in FY2025 to support the ramp.

The demand driver worth being precise about: grid interconnection queues have become the binding constraint on data-center deployment, and hyperscalers are increasingly pursuing off-grid and behind-the-meter generation using arrays of reciprocating engines to reach scale. That mechanism is what re-rated Caterpillar from an infrastructure-adjacent cyclical into an AI-adjacent growth name. It is corroborated at the industry level and by 34% H1 growth in Power Generation. It also has no dedicated risk factor in the 10-K, which means investors have no company-provided basis for sizing a reversal.

The most encouraging detail in Q2 was that **Construction Industries, not Power & Energy, led** — CI total sales +35% versus P&E +17%, with North America CI external revenue +50%. That partially decouples the growth from the AI-power narrative, which is exactly what the equity needed. The qualification is that the same segment is where the dealer build concentrated.

**Derived multiples at $843.12:**

|---|---|---|

Enterprise value, consolidated: $388.0B equity + $45.146B debt − $6.713B cash ≈ **$426.4B**. Excluding the ~$34.5B of Financial Products debt, industrial EV is roughly $392-399B depending on cash attribution.

Peer medians in the group are P/E 34.1 and yield 1.2% (CMI 34.1, PCAR 25.0, WAB 37.1, OSK 14.9, TEX 43.3). On trailing earnings Caterpillar sits at the median; on FY2027 consensus it screens below every peer's trailing multiple except Oshkosh and Paccar. FY2026 EPS growth of ~41% off the adjusted FY2025 base gives a PEG under 1.0; FY2027 growth of 19.1% gives a PEG of ~1.4. Neither number is stretched on its face.

The problem is what earnings base is being capitalized. Caterpillar's five-year net income series is $6.489B (2021), $6.705B (2022), $10.335B (2023), $10.792B (2024), $8.884B (2025) — cyclical and non-compounding. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $26.85 on ~460M shares implies profit near $12.3B, an all-time high, and FY2027 implies ~$14.7B. A 26x multiple on a record earnings level for a machinery manufacturer requires the market's implicit assumption — that infrastructure, power generation and data-center demand have converted a cyclical series into a secular one — to be right. Three years ago the same business earned $6.7B.

Adjusting for the pieces we can identify: strip the ~$0.65 IEEPA benefit and the H1 run-rate falls to $13.05, and a cycle-conservative full-year sits nearer $25.45 (assuming H2 runs ~10% below clean H1 to reflect the Q4 destock, Q3 tariffs and $300-350M of full-year restructuring), which is **33.1x**. That is the honest bear-side arithmetic on current-year earnings.

Scenario framework:

- **Bear $620 (-26.5%)** — 20x FY2027 consensus $31.97, or roughly 24x a cycle-normalized $26. Triggered by a Q4 destock that lands harder than guided, AI-capex order deferral from spreading siting restrictions, or continued RI margin drag. Consensus low target is $575.
- **Base $900 (+6.7%)** — 28x FY2027 consensus $31.97 = $895. Assumes backlog converts, price realization holds, and the multiple stays near the peer median as the services and power mix supports a premium to Caterpillar's own history.
- **Bull $1,150 (+36.4%)** — 30x an FY2027 upside case near $38, or 36x consensus. Requires H2 to beat the guided deceleration, additional duty recoveries becoming probable, and RI margins recovering toward 16%.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives an expected return of roughly +6%. Consensus mean of $972.95 implies +15.4%, and on inflection stocks with rising estimates analyst targets often lag; here, though, the stock trades at a premium multiple where targets and price reflect the same growth extrapolation, so I do not treat $973 as an independent floor. Sell-side composition has also softened: 1 strong buy / 13 buy / 12 hold / 1 sell / 1 strong sell today versus 1 / 14 / 11 / 2 / 0 three months ago, with Baird cutting to Neutral and lowering its target by $300 on July 29.

Valuation score of 4.5 reflects a premium multiple on a record earnings base with modest upside to a defensible base case, partly offset by rising estimates, a below-peer forward multiple and a PEG under 1 on FY2026 growth.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is real and, in the largest market, widening. CI margin at 23.3% (from 20.1%) alongside 50% North American revenue growth and a price-realization swing of roughly $1.8B year over year at the segment level indicates Caterpillar is taking price in a market where it was discounting twelve months earlier. Dealer distribution — 41 U.S. and 109 non-U.S. dealers, 190 countries — plus Cat Financial's merchandising economics remain the structural advantages competitors have not replicated at scale. The 90-day termination clause in dealer agreements is legal form, not live risk, given multi-generational franchise economics.

The trajectory is uneven across the portfolio. Asia/Pacific revenue grew only 4% in H1, and the 10-K acknowledges that developing-market customers "often prioritize purchase price," which drove creation of the lower-cost SEM brand. Against LiuGong, Sany, XCMG, SDLG and Shantui, Caterpillar is defending share rather than gaining it while harvesting the U.S. premium market.

In Power & Energy — where the equity narrative now lives — the competitive set is more crowded than the "AI winner" framing implies. Named global competitors include Cummins, Deutz, Rolls-Royce Power Systems and Siemens Energy, with INNIO, GE Vernova, Kawasaki, Everllence, Volvo Penta, FPT and Weichai in specific markets, plus a packager tier (Aggreko, Generac, Rehlko, Baker Hughes) that sometimes sources Caterpillar engines and components. Caterpillar is not a monopolist in data-center prime or backup power, and the packager relationships make direct share estimation difficult. Cummins trades at 34.1x trailing, so the market is pricing the same theme across the class.

Resource Industries is a tighter oligopoly (Komatsu, Liebherr, Hitachi, Epiroc, Sandvik), and the February 2026 RPMGlobal acquisition (~$786M gross, $546M goodwill, $200M intangibles) deepens mine-planning and fleet-optimization software against Komatsu, Epiroc and Sandvik. At ~0.2% of market cap it is strategically coherent and financially immaterial.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Regulatory siting restrictions on hyperscale data centers — verified, sign ambiguous.** On July 14, 2026, New York's governor signed an executive order imposing a one-year moratorium on new environmental permits for hyperscale data centers consuming 50 MW or more, the first statewide measure of its kind, confirmed across multiple tier-1 outlets. Baird downgraded Caterpillar to Neutral on July 29 citing spreading state and local restrictions; shares fell 4-6%. The counter-consideration is material: the order restricts environmental permits for *facilities*, not engine purchases, and permitting friction on grid-connected sites plausibly increases demand for behind-the-meter reciprocating generation — Caterpillar's product. The net sign is not determinable from available evidence, which is itself the risk: the FY2025 10-K contains **no dedicated risk factor** for data-center or AI-related demand, and the Q2 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors. Investors have no company-provided framework for sizing a reversal in the segment that has driven the re-rating.

**2. The Q4 destock is pre-announced.** CI dealer inventory decrease of over $1.0B guided for Q4, unfavorable dealer-inventory contribution to CI volume in H2, and implied H2 revenue growth of +8-15.5%. This is the most probable source of a near-term negative print.

**3. Tariff exposure of ~$2.2B persists and is administratively contingent.** Roughly 2.8% of implied FY2026 revenue, split ~50% CI / ~25% P&E / ~25% RI. Further recoveries depend on CBP's CAPE process and are not assumed by management.

**4. Resource Industries.** H1 profit -9.7% with margin -300bp. If the data-center thesis deflates, RI is not positioned to cushion it. RI also carries $2.100B of *historical* accumulated goodwill impairments — nothing in current filings indicates a new impairment, but it evidences prior overpayment for mining assets.

**5. Cat Financial covenant and portfolio.** 7.96:1 against a 10:1 maximum leaves ~20% headroom; Financial Products debt grew $1.839B in the half while industrial debt was flat. Gross write-offs rose to $88M from $57M.

**6. Capital structure posture.** Equity/assets fell to 18.9% from 21.6% as buybacks exceeded retained earnings; cash fell $3.267B in the half. Not a solvency issue at high-A ratings, but a reduction in cushion entering a capex ramp to ~$3.5B.

**7. Execution on the throughput ramp.** Management expects higher H2 throughput; the 10-K acknowledges parts and components constraints across some products.

**8. Sentiment.** Michael Burry disclosed a first-ever short on June 30, the local price peak, calling Caterpillar among the market's most overvalued AI beneficiaries. Size, instrument and current status are unverified; this is a sentiment data point, not evidence. Short interest is only 1.9% of float with 2.1 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short cushion beneath the stock.

### Options Market Signal

The headline reading — 64.5% near-term ATM IV against 39.2% one-year historical vol, 94th percentile, backwardation to 42.6% at six months — overstates the stress. The near-term tenor is a same-day (August 7) expiry with an implied move of only ±0.9%; that IV is expiry mechanics, not a directional signal. The one-month tenor at 38.9% sits essentially on top of realized vol at 39.2%, and its skew is balanced at +0.6%. So the market is not pricing near-term dislocation.

The informative points are further out. The six-month tenor carries 42.6% IV with a -3.4% skew and a put/call volume ratio of 3.17, indicating longer-dated put accumulation around the Q4 destock and the policy overhang. Since the July 22 snapshot, ATM IV rose from 54.9% to 64.5% while price fell 5.3% and put/call open interest moved from 0.96 to 1.05 — a modest hedging build rather than a regime change. Balanced against that, a $920 August 14 call traded 4.5x open interest at 40.3% IV, so upside speculation is present too. Net: the options market corroborates a two-sided Q4 event rather than either leg of the thesis, and one-month vol is not cheap enough to argue for entry timing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($1,150, +36.4%)

Backlog at $72.1B and 0.96x TTM revenue, plus contract liabilities of $7.280B versus $2.745B eighteen months ago, gives eighteen-plus months of demand visibility with customer cash already collected. The pricing reversal — CI from -$1.136B in FY2025 to +$665M in H1 2026 — demonstrates recovered list-price authority in the most competitive segment, more than offsetting the ~$400M incremental tariff burden. Q2 leadership came from Construction Industries rather than Power & Energy, diversifying growth away from the AI-power narrative. MP&E free cash flow more than doubled to $5.698B in H1 while share count fell 1.88% with $1.60B of contracted further reduction pending settlement. Estimate revisions of +10.7% (FY2026) and +9.1% (FY2027) over 90 days, concentrated in the last 30 days, plus four consecutive beats averaging 16.7%, argue consensus is still catching up. Over $1.3B of duty-recovery receivables sit on the balance sheet with zero assumed in guidance. At 26.4x FY2027 consensus against a 34.1x peer median trailing multiple, a re-rating toward peers on a 2027 beat supports $1,150.

### Bear Case ($620, -26.5%)

The market is paying 31x current-year and 26x next-year earnings for a manufacturer whose profit was $6.7B three years ago and $8.9B last year. Roughly 34% of the H1 revenue increase corresponds to dealer channel fill that management has pre-announced will reverse by over $1.0B in Q4, and $0.65 of Q2 adjusted EPS came from a duty recovery that will not repeat. Guidance itself implies H2 growth decelerating to +8-15.5%. Resource Industries — a quarter of the company — saw profit fall 9.7% with 300bp of margin compression. Asia/Pacific grew 4%. The demand driver carrying the multiple is data-center power, which now faces a verified statewide permitting moratorium in New York and a spreading patchwork of local restrictions, against which the company's own risk disclosure offers no sizing framework. Management deployed $6.522B on buybacks at a Q2 average of $880.26 — above today's price — funded partly by drawing cash from $9.980B to $6.713B, while equity/assets fell to 18.9%. A single Q4 miss on guided destocking, combined with any AI-capex order deferral, takes the multiple back toward 20x and the stock to $620.

## Conclusion

Caterpillar's operating inflection is real and larger than the market's initial framing suggested: 24% revenue growth, 170bp of clean year-over-year margin expansion excluding the duty credit, and a pricing reversal worth roughly $1.8B at the CI segment level in twelve months. Backlog nearly doubled relative to revenue, and customer prepayments of $7.280B provide cash confirmation that the orders exist. Consensus has raised FY2026 EPS 10.7% and FY2027 9.1% in ninety days, mostly in the last thirty, and four consecutive beats support confidence in those estimates.

What prevents an upgrade is the combination of price and disclosed near-term reversals. At $843.12 the stock trades at 31.4x FY2026 and 26.4x FY2027 consensus — a record earnings base capitalized at a premium multiple in a business that earned $6.7B in 2022. Management has pre-announced a Q4 CI dealer inventory decrease of over $1.0B against an H1 build of $1.9B, guidance implies H2 growth halving from H1, $0.65 of Q2 adjusted EPS will not repeat, and Resource Industries is contracting on profit. The verified New York permitting moratorium and the spreading local-restriction patchwork introduce a policy variable the company's own risk disclosure does not frame, with a genuinely ambiguous sign. The market gave back the entire 12% earnings-day gain within three sessions, which reads as the print being absorbed rather than dismissed.

The score moves to 6.1 from 6.0, stabilizing a four-report decline: the Q2 print and estimate revisions justify holding growth prospects at 7.5 and financial health at 7.0, while valuation (4.5), management quality (5.5 — no open-market insider purchases, $6.522B of buybacks executed above the current price at 35x trailing partly funded by cash drawdown) and risk factors (4.5 — Q4 destock plus unframed policy exposure) cap the composite. This is a fifth consecutive HOLD, and it is a hold with a positive twelve-month growth thesis rather than an uncertain one: existing owners should stay, and new capital should wait for either the Q3 confirmation described above or a price in the $720-750 range.
