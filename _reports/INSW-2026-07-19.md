**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Seaways is one of the largest publicly-traded crude and product tanker operators, currently reporting extraordinary results driven by the Strait of Hormuz disruption that began in late February 2026. Q1 2026 net income of $286M ($5.75 diluted) versus $50M a year earlier reflects VLCC spot rates of $86,693/day (against $33,531 in Q1'25). The company is a genuine fortress operationally (net loan-to-value below 7%, ~$918M liquidity, Altman Z-score 3.79) and is returning cash aggressively, declaring a record $4.55/share dividend for June 2026 at an 85% adjusted-payout ratio, on top of more than $1B returned since 2020.

The central problem is that both the earnings and the ~8x trailing multiple rest on a spot-rate spike that management itself does not treat as durable. About 82% of TCE revenue is spot-market exposed, so earnings swing violently with charter rates; the company posted a $134.7M net loss as recently as 2021. Management's actions (selling 12 vessels in 2025 and 7 more in Q1'26 into strength, deleveraging to sub-7% LTV, and raising the payout to 85%) are textbook late-cycle harvesting behavior. The truer run-rate is Q1'26 adjusted net income of $194M ($3.90/share), which strips out the $88M of vessel-sale gains embedded in the $286M GAAP figure the trailing P/E is built on.

We maintain HOLD. The balance sheet and capital-return program provide a hard floor that rules out AVOID; the +142% 90-day current-year estimate revision is a mechanical function of the Hormuz spike rather than a structural change, and a verified reopening reverses the very rate premium inflating the numbers, which rules out BUY. The setup is symmetric, and the score has drifted lower as the spot-rate windfall matures toward its most-probable reversal point.

## Company Overview

INSW, incorporated in the Marshall Islands (with vessel-owning subsidiaries redomiciled to Bermuda as of December 2025), operates an International Flag fleet of 67 vessels (~7.9M dwt; 59 owned, 8 chartered-in) across two segments:

- **Crude Tankers** (~52% of 2025 TCE revenue): VLCCs, Suezmaxes, Aframaxes, plus an asset-light Ship-to-Ship Lightering business.
- **Product Carriers** (~48%): MRs, LR1s, and one LR2. INSW opportunistically deploys LR1s into crude cargoes via the Panamax International pool.

The business model deploys roughly 82% of TCE revenue in the spot market via commercial pools (Tankers International, Panamax International). This gives high torque to spot cycles in both directions. The structural advantage over peers is diversification across all major tanker classes, allowing capital redeployment between crude and product sectors, combined with pool-derived scale. The competitive moat is modest — tanker shipping is fragmented and largely a price-taker business — and INSW's genuine edge is disciplined counter-cyclical capital allocation and balance-sheet strength rather than any durable cost or share advantage.

In January 2026, INSW acquired the remaining 50% of Tankers (UK) Agencies (the commercial manager of the Tankers International VLCC pool) from its former partner CMB.Tech, then launched a new Suezmax pool. This builds an asset-light commercial-management franchise, a modest diversification away from pure vessel ownership.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is cyclical, not secular.** Annual revenue moved from $864.7M (2022) to $1,071.8M (2023 peak), then $951.6M (2024) and $843.3M (2025). The 2023-2025 decline reflects normalizing tanker rates (Product segment TCE fell 20% in 2025), not franchise deterioration.

**FY2025 metrics:** net income $309.3M (36.7% net margin), operating income $345.4M (41% margin), Adjusted EBITDA $474.7M, operating cash flow $380.1M, ROE 15.3%, ROA 11.6%. FY2025 profit included $42.5M of vessel-sale gains.

**The Q1 2026 spike:** Suezmax spot ran at $68,027/day alongside the VLCC rate. Adjusted EBITDA was $244M versus $91M a year earlier. Q1'26 net income included a $3.9M holding gain on the Tankers International stake in addition to the vessel-sale gains. Extrapolating GAAP EPS off this base overstates the run-rate materially.

**The balance sheet is a genuine fortress:**
- Altman Z-score 3.79 (safe zone); D/E 0.32; current ratio 3.71.
- FY2025 net debt/total cap 16.5% (down from 22.2%); Q1'26 net LTV below 7%.
- Liquidity ~$918M ($377M cash and short-term investments + $541M undrawn revolver); 31 unencumbered vessels at YE2025.
- Debt laddered 2030-2037. In 2025 the company refinanced expensive lease debt, issued $250M of 7.125% 2030 unsecured bonds, exercised purchase options on six VLCCs (eliminating ~$22M/yr of mandatory principal), and set a 12-year ECA facility at SOFR+125bps for LR1 newbuilds.

**Capital return is the story.** Management runs a counter-cyclical playbook: selling old tonnage at peak prices (12 vessels for $246M in 2025; 7 more for $216M in Q1'26), deleveraging, and returning the rest. Dividends escalate with the cycle: $2.93/share in FY2025 ($144.6M), then $2.15 (March '26), then the record $4.55/share for June '26 plus discretionary top-up.

Financial health earns a 7.5. It is the most reliable dimension in the analytical framework as a downside filter, and INSW clears it comfortably on cash generation, leverage, and liquidity. It does not reach 8.5+ because a meaningful portion of reported earnings is non-recurring vessel-sale gains, and the underlying earnings engine is a violently cyclical spot exposure that can swing to losses.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth figures are eye-catching and misleading. Current-year consensus EPS has been revised +142.3% over 90 days ($5.83 to $14.14); next-quarter estimates +176%; the company has beaten four straight quarters (Q1'26 by 47.6%). Under normal circumstances, +30% 90-day revisions combined with revenue acceleration would be a strong BUY signal.

This is the guardrail case where the signal does not apply. The revision velocity is not an earnings recovery from a trough; it is a mechanical repricing of an ongoing geopolitical spot-rate spike. VLCC rates rose because ~90-95% of Strait of Hormuz traffic was disrupted, lengthening voyages and tightening effective capacity. Analysts are simply marking estimates to the current spot environment. The revisions do not reflect durable market-share gains, new contracted revenue, or structural demand growth; they reflect the persistence of a disruption that management explicitly characterizes as unsustainable.

Two forces work directly against the growth durability:

1. **The rate premium reverses on a reopening.** The macro backdrop assigns meaningful probability to Hormuz normalization — the most-probable path being a prolonged grind, but with a verified transit-fee reopening a live scenario. A durable reopening compresses ton-miles and rates rapidly, reversing the driver behind the +142% revisions. For a spot-heavy operator, trade-route normalization is bearish.

2. **Supply is building.** INSW's own 10-K notes the total tanker orderbook grew 23.6M dwt YoY (VLCC +19M dwt, Suezmax +6.4M dwt). Rising capacity delivering 2027+ is the classic setup for a next-cycle rate downturn.

The commercial-management franchise (full Tankers International ownership, new Suezmax pool) offers modest structural growth, but it is not material to the earnings base. Growth prospects score 4.5 — the reported momentum is real but not durable, and the most-probable path is a decline in earnings power from the current spike.

## Valuation Assessment

At $86.75, INSW trades at ~8.0x trailing P/E and 8.03x forward P/E on the framework's reported figures. Against the peer group (Kinetik, Dorian LPG, Viper, Antero Midstream, DT Midstream), median P/E is 24.9x — so INSW looks like a ~68% discount. That comparison is largely meaningless: the peers are stable-cash-flow midstream/storage names, while INSW is a spot-cyclical tanker operator whose trailing earnings sit at a geopolitically-inflated peak. The correct read is that the discount reflects the market's (accurate) view that current earnings are not the run-rate.

Cheapness here is a warning flag, not a buy signal. A low trailing multiple on peak-cycle earnings is the definition of a cyclical value trap. If normalized annual earnings power sits closer to the adjusted run-rate stripped of vessel-sale gains and the Hormuz premium — plausibly $6-9 in a mid-cycle year rather than the $14+ current-year consensus — the "cheap" 8x becomes a mid-teens multiple on normalized earnings, which is fair-to-full for a price-taker cyclical.

The scenario framework:
- **Bull ($110-120):** Hormuz disruption persists through 2026, rates stay elevated, the 85% payout compounds cash return, and the stock re-rates modestly on sustained cash generation.
- **Base ($84-90):** disruption partially normalizes over 6-12 months, rates ease from the peak but stay above mid-cycle, and the fortress balance sheet plus dividend support the price near current levels.
- **Bear ($56-62):** a verified, durable Hormuz reopening compresses rates, the orderbook overhang begins to bite, and the stock re-rates toward normalized earnings power. Analyst low target is $56.

Consensus mean is $93, median $97, high $120, low $56 — a dispersion wide enough that the market cannot converge on a normalized value. The absolute dividend yield support at an 85% payout is genuine near-term, but a payout that high is itself late-cycle and will shrink as rates normalize. Valuation scores 4.5: the multiple is optically cheap for the wrong reason.

## Competitive Landscape

INSW competes against Frontline, DHT, Scorpio, Teekay, and state-owned fleets. Its differentiation is balance-sheet strength and multi-class flexibility rather than cost leadership or scale dominance. The proxy shows strong relative execution — cumulative TSR of 470.75 versus a peer group at 196.0 — which is real evidence of superior counter-cyclical capital allocation over the cycle.

The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving at the margin: the full acquisition of Tankers International's commercial manager and the launch of a Suezmax pool extend INSW's cargo access and pool scale, and continued fleet renewal (average VLCC age ~8.5 years) keeps the fleet competitive. But the moat remains modest in an inherently fragmented, price-taking industry. Competitive position scores 5.5 — solid execution and a slightly widening commercial platform, but no durable structural advantage the market hasn't priced. Consistent with the framework, we do not lean on this dimension alone; its recent predictive signal has been weak.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Extreme spot-rate cyclicality (core risk).** ~82% spot exposure means earnings swing violently. The current windfall is geopolitically driven and could reverse quickly.

2. **Strait of Hormuz / Iran conflict (new Q1'26 10-Q risk factor).** This is bilateral for INSW: it inflates rates dramatically (upside) while raising bunker costs, war-risk insurance, and volatility. Management (CEO Zabrocky) warned that the world cannot substitute more than 20M bbl/day and that a prolonged disruption would strain global markets, meaning the same event boosting rates could ultimately depress oil demand and tanker volumes. A verified reopening is the primary downside catalyst.

3. **Newbuild orderbook overhang.** The rising orderbook threatens the 2027+ rate structure.

4. **Vessel carrying-value / impairment risk.** At YE2025, nine MRs carried at $327.2M were ~$43.3M above appraised market value; a rate downturn could force write-downs.

5. **Tax-structure fragility.** Reliance on the Section 883 exemption; the December 2025 Bermuda redomiciliation manages OECD Pillar Two exposure but adds a moving part.

6. **Governance flag — renewed poison pill.** The April 2026 Second Amended Rights Agreement most plausibly protects Section 883 status but also raises the takeover bar, given the CMB.Tech relationship.

Risk factors score 4.5. The dominant risk — a Hormuz reopening reversing the rate premium — is a specific, catalyst-linked downside that is only partially priced, and the earnings are levered directly to it. The fortress balance sheet caps how far the equity can fall, but the setup is genuinely two-sided.

The rich near-term IV means options-based downside protection is expensive here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110-120):** The Hormuz disruption persists through 2026. VLCC and Suezmax rates stay elevated, driving continued record cash generation. The 85% adjusted-payout dividend compounds shareholder return (the $4.55/share June declaration annualizes to a very high yield if sustained), the sub-7% LTV balance sheet allows opportunistic buybacks or accretive vessel purchases, and the stock re-rates modestly on demonstrated cash-return durability. INSW's four-quarter beat streak and rising estimates carry the narrative.

**Bear Case ($56-62):** A verified, held Hormuz reopening compresses spot rates rapidly. Voyage lengths normalize, effective capacity loosens, and the +142% estimate revisions unwind as consensus marks back down. The swelling VLCC/Suezmax orderbook compounds the pressure into 2027. Trailing GAAP earnings — inflated by $88M of Q1 vessel-sale gains and a peak rate premium — re-rate the stock toward normalized earnings power. The analyst low target of $56 anchors this scenario. The dividend, tied to an 85% payout of adjusted earnings, shrinks mechanically as those earnings fall.

The base case ($84-90) is a partial normalization over 6-12 months, with rates easing from the peak but staying above mid-cycle, and balance-sheet strength plus the dividend holding the price near current levels. The most-probable macro path (a prolonged grind rather than a clean reopening) supports the base case near-term but does not resolve the two-sidedness.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence supports neither a confident BUY nor an AVOID. The fortress balance sheet and aggressive capital return provide a hard floor that rules out AVOID; the fact that both earnings and the multiple are levered to an unsustainable, reversing spot-rate spike rules out BUY. The score trajectory (6.1 in late May to 5.6 in late June, now 5.4) reflects the maturing of this windfall toward its most-probable reversal — new data confirms rather than reverses that drift.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $60s that restores genuine asymmetry against normalized earnings power (this is the cleanest path — a lower price on the same fundamentals improves risk/reward).
- Evidence that the commercial-management franchise (Tankers International, Suezmax pool) is scaling into a material, less-cyclical earnings contributor.
- A structural tightening in the tanker supply/demand balance (orderbook slippage, accelerated scrapping) independent of the Hormuz premium.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified, durable Hormuz reopening (72+ hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial transit) with the stock still trading at a peak-earnings multiple — the rate premium reverses while the multiple is priced for continuation.
- A vessel-value impairment (the nine MRs carried ~$43.3M above appraised value are the tell) signaling the downturn is arriving.
- A dividend cut signaling management sees the cycle rolling over.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (August 2026) and ongoing Hormuz transit verification are the natural checkpoints. The August print will reveal how much of the Q2 rate spike converted to cash and whether management's payout posture is holding.

## Conclusion

International Seaways is a pristine, exceptionally well-managed cyclical operator harvesting a geopolitically-driven rate windfall. The balance sheet is a fortress, capital return is disciplined and generous, and management's counter-cyclical execution has outperformed peers over the cycle. None of that changes the core issue: at ~8x trailing earnings, the stock is optically cheap on peak-cycle, partly-non-recurring earnings that a Hormuz reopening reverses quickly, against a supply overhang that threatens the next cycle. The +142% 90-day estimate revisions look like a strong buy signal but are a mechanical artifact of the spot spike, the classic cyclical value-trap configuration where cheapness is a warning rather than an opportunity. The risk/reward is symmetric: sub-7% LTV and an 85% payout cap the downside, but the upside requires a disruption management itself does not treat as sustainable. HOLD, with a genuine upgrade path on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s.
