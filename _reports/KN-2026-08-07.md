## Executive Summary

Knowles has completed a genuine transformation. The December 2024 divestiture of Consumer MEMS Microphones removed a commoditized, capital-destroying franchise and left a two-segment specialty components company with a 20.2% adjusted EBIT margin (FY2025), 0.5x net leverage, and demonstrated pricing power. Q2 2026 revenue of $166.8M grew 14.3% year over year with gross margin expanding 320 basis points to 44.7%; non-GAAP diluted EPS of $0.33 beat consensus by 5.0% and rose 37.5%. Management raised FY2026 revenue growth guidance to 10–12% from an Investor Day organic target of 4–6%, and adjusted EBITDA growth guidance to 20–24%. Precision Devices grew 25.2% in the quarter with book-to-bill of 1.4x, the seventh consecutive quarter above 1.0. Consensus EPS estimates have risen 4.3% for the current year and 5.5% for next year over 90 days.

The problem is price relative to what has actually been contracted. At $39.48 the stock trades at 30.6x TTM non-GAAP EPS of $1.29, 24.4x FY2027 consensus of $1.62, 21.9x TTM EV/adjusted EBITDA of $157.9M, and roughly 46x TTM GAAP earnings. Trailing adjusted free cash flow is approximately $76.3M — a 2.3% yield — because first-half adjusted FCF fell to $19.8M (6.2% of revenue) from $57.9M (20.8%) as inventory built ahead of a guided second-half ramp and capex stepped to a structural ~5% of revenue. The FY2025 comparison base was itself flattered by $19.8M of disclosed customer prepayments and by adding back $32.5M of discontinued-operations cash outflows.

Two things must be held simultaneously. Decomposing the raised guidance: FY2025 revenue was $593.2M, 1H 2026 actual was $319.9M, and Q3 guidance midpoint is $172M, which leaves implied Q4 revenue of $160.6M–$172.5M against Q4 2025 of $162.2M — roughly −1% to +6% year over year. The cause is segment-specific: MSA grew 13.9% in Q1 2026 then 1.6% in Q2, and full-year MSA guidance of +2–4% implies second-half MSA of about $135.7M versus $137.2M a year earlier. The segment that generates about 62% of combined segment adjusted EBIT is guided to shrink slightly, with management attributing first-half strength partly to customer inventory building. Meanwhile the large defense upside management describes — missile-program volumes potentially 2–4x, allied rearmament flowing through existing primes, a larger US budget — is dated by management to "one- to two-year timeframe" and "late '27 into '28." The only contracted increment is a $15M+ radar order shipping over 36 months from 2027, roughly $5M per year.

## Company Overview

Knowles designs and manufactures specialty electronic components from Itasca, Illinois, with roughly 5,200 employees across operations in 11 countries. Two segments:

**Precision Devices (PD)** — FY2025 revenue $328.9M (55% of total), segment adjusted EBIT $63.3M (19.2% margin, up from 16.7% in FY2024). High-performance capacitors and RF/microwave filters sold into defense, industrial, MedTech, and electrification/energy. This is where growth sits: +9.6% in FY2025, +21.5% in 1H 2026, +25.2% in Q2 2026. Cornell Dubilier, acquired November 1, 2023, anchors the film-capacitor and energy platform.

**MedTech & Specialty Audio (MSA)** — FY2025 revenue $264.3M (45%), segment adjusted EBIT $102.4M at a 38.7% margin. Balanced-armature receivers and microphones for hearing aids and premium audio, effectively a duopoly with Sonion. Mature, high-margin, structurally 2–4% growth with disclosed price erosion offset by bill-of-materials cost reduction.

The profit pool does not align with the growth story. MSA produced $102.4M of the $165.7M combined segment adjusted EBIT — 62% of segment profit on 45% of revenue — while growing 4.3% in FY2025. PD delivered 38% of segment profit on 55% of revenue. Unallocated corporate expense is therefore roughly $45.6M (segment EBIT $165.7M versus consolidated adjusted EBIT $120.1M), about 7.7% of revenue.

A comparability warning matters for anyone screening this name: as-reported revenue reads $707.6M (FY2023) declining to $593.2M (FY2025), which looks like a multi-year contraction. Restated for continuing operations, FY2023 revenue was $456.8M. FY2024's apparent 21% growth was substantially inorganic (first full year of Cornell Dubilier). FY2025's +7.2% and 1H 2026's +15.0% are organic.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Q2 2026: revenue $166.8M (+14.3%), gross margin 44.7% versus 41.5%, adjusted EBIT $36.1M (21.6% margin) versus $27.7M (19.0%), GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations $0.21, non-GAAP diluted EPS $0.33. 1H 2026: revenue $319.9M (+15.0%), adjusted EBIT $66.2M (20.7%) versus $49.4M (17.8%), non-GAAP EPS $0.60 versus $0.42. Segment margins: PD 20.7%, MSA 40.3%. PD gross margin reached 40.1%, guided to "low 40s" in the second half.

The quarterly revenue sequence is $145.9M (Q2'25) → $152.9M → $162.2M → $153.1M (Q1'26) → $166.8M.

**Balance sheet — genuinely strong.** At June 30, 2026: cash $49.6M, total debt $131.0M (all revolver), total assets $1,076.0M, total equity $795.5M. Total liabilities are therefore $280.5M, giving debt/equity of 0.165x. Current ratio 3.20x, up from 2.75x at year end. Net debt $81.4M equals 0.5x TTM adjusted EBITDA of $157.9M; interest coverage on that basis is 21.6x ($157.9M / $7.3M), 12.7x on TTM EBIT. Covenants require minimum 3.00x EBITDA/interest and maximum 3.75x net leverage — very wide headroom. The $400M revolver matures February 8, 2028, with no principal due before then and liquidity above $315M.

Distress screens on this company are unreliable and should be discounted. An Altman Z-Score computed off FY2025 data reads 2.47, in the "grey zone," but the dominant negative term is the −$569.4M accumulated deficit, which records sunk write-offs of the divested consumer-microphone franchise rather than any current solvency condition. Substituting current market equity for book equity in the market-value-to-liabilities term produces a Z near 7.6. Balance-sheet distress is not a live risk. That said, the accumulated deficit against $1,747.2M of paid-in capital is a legitimate record of historical capital destruction relevant to judging long-run allocation.

**Cash flow — the weak link.** 1H 2026 operating cash flow was $27.5M versus $37.7M; Q2 OCF was $28.2M versus $36.4M. Adjusted FCF fell to $19.8M (6.2% of revenue) from $57.9M (20.8%). Drivers: inventories −$23.3M, receivables −$14.0M, capex $17.3M versus roughly $9.1M. Capex has stepped from 2.7% of revenue (FY2023) to 5.4% (FY2025) and is guided at ~5% ongoing off a growing base, with management signaling PD capacity additions for 2027.

Derived TTM adjusted FCF: $114.4M (FY2025) − $57.9M (1H 2025) + $19.8M (1H 2026) = $76.3M, a 2.26% yield on $3.372B. Management guides Q3 OCF to $35–45M, which would substantially repair the half-year figure. I deliberately do not annualize any single quarter: MSA has real Q4 seasonality, the inventory build is intended to reverse, and 1H included $4.7M of non-recurring discrete tax benefits. Treat the TTM figure as the conservative anchor and the guided Q3 as the sensitivity.

**Two disclosed tailwinds are ending together.** Foreign tax credits were fully utilized as of December 31, 2025, and the company states its non-GAAP effective tax rate will rise: 11.8% (FY2025) → 16.8% (1H 2026), with Q3 guided at 15–19%. Separately, 1H 2026 GAAP ETR of 11.6% included $4.7M of discrete stock-compensation benefits; excluding them the rate was 25.6%, and those benefits exist because the share price rose. A move from ~12% toward ~20% non-GAAP ETR is roughly a 9% mechanical headwind to non-GAAP EPS growth. Stock-based compensation of $28.4M in FY2025 equals 4.8% of revenue and 23.6% of adjusted EBIT, and is excluded from every guided non-GAAP metric; EV/(adjusted EBITDA less SBC) is $3,453M / $129.4M = 26.7x.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is concentrated and identifiable. Defense is roughly $125M of revenue — approximately $80M+ of RF filters at about 90% defense plus $40–50M of defense capacitors — or about 21% of FY2025 revenue and 38% of PD. Management describes the vast majority of the filter business as sole-sourced. These figures were given verbally and approximately on the Q2 call, not in audited segment disclosure, and should carry that caveat.

Order intake supports the near term: PD orders of about $140M in Q2 versus a little over $100M in Q1, book-to-bill of 1.4x for the seventh straight quarter above 1.0, and July bookings described as strong. Management stated this was core-product driven, since the energy order booked approximately nothing in the quarter. Pricing is the second leg — annual increases in PD, with management stating "we don't really see our prices go down" and noting a lag because 20-week lead times push some 2026 price actions into 2027 P&L. The medium-term target is adjusted EBITDA margin above 25% this year and near 30% within two to three years.

Three qualifications discipline the enthusiasm.

First, the order book is not contracted revenue, and the company says so plainly: "our total remaining performance obligations were immaterial" at both June 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025. Deferred revenue of $21.9M relates to prepayments, not backlog. Book-to-bill measures cancellable purchase orders; company materials caution that cancellations, extensions, and amendments may reduce orders. Management's own capacity discipline concedes the point: they will not add capacity "for what I would call transient orders where we get orders for commoditized product because the other guys' lead times go really long." That is credible behavior, and it implicitly acknowledges some cycle-peak order inflation.

Second, the Q4 arithmetic. Guidance of 10–12% implies FY2026 revenue of $652.5–664.4M; subtracting 1H actual of $319.9M and the $172M Q3 midpoint leaves Q4 at $160.6–172.5M against $162.2M last year. Susquehanna raised this on the call; management's answer was MSA-specific first-half inventory building, which the segment data corroborate (MSA Q1 +13.9%, Q2 +1.6%). Management also volunteered that fresh hearing-aid industry data "could be closer to the 4% range," so conservatism is a live alternative reading. Either way, the guidance as issued embeds deceleration in the high-margin half.

Third, timing. Management's three defense acceleration drivers carry explicit dates: budget growth "probably till late '27 into '28," missile stock replenishment with volumes potentially 2–4x on a "one- to two-year timeframe," and allied spending "not yet reflected." Knowles is a sub-tier component supplier; European rearmament reaches it through increased orders from primes it already serves, not direct contracts. The energy platform shipped more than $5M in Q2 and is fully ramped with better-than-planned yields, but no follow-on order has been disclosed, and the 10-K warns this expansion depends on "one or a small number of customers." The 10-K language on power demand is generic and does not name AI or data centers; underwriting this as a data-center power story extrapolates beyond disclosure.

Consensus reflects the profile rather than the ambition: FY2026 EPS $1.33, FY2027 $1.62, implying 21.8% growth. Revisions are positive but moderate — +5.9%/+5.2% (30d/90d) for the current quarter and +3.3%/+4.3% (30d/90d) for the current year, +3.9%/+5.5% for next year, across only four analysts. This is not the +30% revision velocity that historically precedes sustained re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

In PD, the named competitors are Kyocera AVX, Yageo/Kemet, and Vishay plus smaller specialty firms, and the company characterizes its end markets as facing less pricing pressure than commodity passives. The evidence supports the claim: annual price increases, no observed price declines, and 140bp of PD gross margin expansion year over year against a group where peers' prices move with demand. The sole-source design-in position on defense RF filters is the durable asset here — it explains both the pricing behavior and the 1.4x book-to-bill persistence.

In MSA the dynamic inverts. Sonion is the primary competitor in balanced-armature hearing-aid transducers, making this a duopoly with structural price erosion offset by bill-of-materials cost reduction. MSA gross margin stepped to 53.0–53.2% from 45.1% in Q2 2025 on factory productivity, which is impressive execution, but the segment is a share-defense franchise with a 2–4% structural growth rate. Management's ambition to raise content per device and add the Micro Solutions Group platform is an aspiration, not guidance.

Two positional negatives deserve weight. China's policy of reducing dependence on foreign suppliers has already adversely affected PD revenue — stated in the present tense in the 10-K, not as a hypothetical. And concentration is shifting rather than diminishing: WS Audiology fell from 16% of total revenue (FY2023) to 11% (FY2025), but remains 24% of MSA revenue with MSA's top five at roughly 76% of segment revenue, while distributor TTI newly crossed 10% of total revenue and is 19% of PD. The declining WSA percentage is arithmetic from PD growth and the CMM divestiture. Combined top-two exposure is about 21% of revenue, and the growth segment is becoming more distributor-concentrated — which converts channel concentration into inventory-cycle risk given the 10-K's own overstocking warning.

Trajectory is improving in PD and stable-to-slowly-eroding in MSA. Score: 6.5, unchanged.

## Risk Assessment

**Q4 print risk is the near-term binary.** Guidance implies −1% to +6% Q4 revenue growth against a stock at 24x forward earnings. If MSA's second-half decline materializes as guided and PD does not overshoot, the print itself will not surprise, but any PD order digestion on top of it produces a miss the multiple is not built to absorb.

**MSA channel fill.** Management identified first-half customer inventory building as the reason for the Q1-to-Q2 deceleration. That means part of first-half strength in the segment producing 62% of segment profit was not end demand.

**Cornell Dubilier goodwill.** At the October 1, 2025 annual test, CD was the only one of four reporting units requiring a quantitative test; the other three were tested qualitatively. Goodwill of $270.4M plus intangibles of $133.1M equals 37.5% of total assets and 51% of book equity, leaving tangible book equity near $392M. Requiring quantitative work is not evidence of impending impairment — a November 2023 purchase-price allocation can leave minimal headroom by construction — but CD anchors the energy narrative, so a demand disappointment there would hit earnings and carrying value together. FY2025 disclosures reference lower-than-expected yields during the specialty film ramp, with $1.7M of production-transfer costs continuing in 1H 2026.

**Syntiant exposure is unmarked.** Knowles holds Series D-2 preferred in the CMM acquirer at an $83.4M cost basis plus a $5.9M subordinated seller note — roughly $89.3M, equal to 8.3% of total assets, 11.2% of book equity, 2.6% of market cap — carried at cost and therefore not marked to market, so deterioration would surface as a discrete impairment. The note's rate was reduced from 13.0% to 8.0% effective May 11, 2026; principal has not amortized; FY2025's $6.2M dividend was paid in additional shares rather than cash. Benign readings exist (a completed financing round justifying a market rate reset; PIK dividends are standard for venture-stage preferred), and I have no information on Syntiant's actual condition. What is established: terms moved toward the borrower, and Knowles' exposure is unmarked. Related MSA revenue is declining ($8.3M in 1H 2025 to $7.1M in 1H 2026).

**Currency runs the wrong way.** A sustained 10% weakening of the dollar reduces pretax earnings by roughly $14.8M — about 12% of FY2025 adjusted EBIT — because manufacturing sits in China, Malaysia, the Philippines, and Mexico while sales are largely dollar-denominated. Most screens assume the opposite sign for a US-listed exporter, and the current regime is one in which dollar weakness is a live scenario.

**Governance and management alignment.** Across all Form 4 activity from February to July 2026 there were no open-market purchases by any officer or director. The CEO exercised 142,857 options at $16.77 and sold all of them at $33.25 in May; the General Counsel and one director took holdings to zero; the CFO, COO, CHRO, and Controller sold. Much of this is mechanically forced — only 32,803 options remained outstanding company-wide at June 30 after 380,855 were exercised in 1H at an average $18.04, and an expiring pool struck near $17 against a $33–39 stock compels exercise-and-sell. But the May 13, 2026 adoption of a new 10b5-1 plan covering 200,000 CEO shares and 40,000 CFO shares was a forward-looking discretionary decision at roughly $36. Set against a five-year TSR index of 116.28 versus a peer group at 236.57 — with the gap widening most in 2025 — and FY2025 CEO total compensation of $8.67M reaching a five-year high, the alignment picture is mixed. The plan does have teeth (comp actually paid was negative $145,717 in 2022), and the company-selected metric of adjusted FCF margin is a reasonable choice, though the FY2025 value of 19.3% was flattered by $19.8M of prepayments and add-backs and fell to 6.2% in 1H 2026.

Capital allocation partially offsets this. Buybacks ran at roughly $16.66, $17.97, and $18.20 average prices in FY2023–FY2025 and were throttled from a $65M annual pace to about $45M annualized as the price doubled — genuine discipline, with $106.5M remaining on authorization. Debt reduction has been real: the $122.9M interest-free CD seller note was fully retired by October 31, 2025. But share-count reduction has stalled: diluted shares went 90.1M (FY2024) → 88.0M (FY2025) → 87.9M (Q2 2026), as treasury-method dilution on RSUs and PSUs jumped from 0.7M to 2.2M. Buybacks now largely offset equity compensation rather than shrinking the base. Unvested awards at target (1.66M RSUs, 1.11M PSUs) represent roughly 3.2% of shares outstanding, leaving existing holders with about 96.9% of the enlarged base if all vested at target.

**Unresolved item.** An 8-K/A filed July 28, 2026 amending Item 5.07 annual-meeting vote results three months after the meeting could not be retrieved, nor could the February 20, 2026 Item 5.02 filing. The most likely explanation is a tabulation correction. Flagged as a verification gap, not a finding.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 53.0% sits 14.2 points above one-year realized volatility of 38.8%, in backwardation against 46.6% at six months — the market is pricing event risk in the next several weeks, with an implied move of ±8.3% on the August 21 expiry. IV has collapsed from 100.5% on June 18 to 53.0% now, so the extreme uncertainty priced two months ago has resolved. Put/call skew is balanced at −2.2% near-term and open interest is overwhelmingly call-weighted at a 0.06 put/call ratio, meaning almost no downside protection is in place. Short interest is 8.2% of float at four days to cover. The combination — rich near-term vol, no put protection, moderate short base near 52-week highs — describes a positioning setup with an air pocket beneath it if the Q4 guidance path disappoints. This corroborates the Q4 print as the key event and argues against adding into it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $49 (+24%)

PD growth proves structural rather than cyclical. Book-to-bill stays above 1.0 through 2026, the $15M+ radar order is followed by additional multi-year defense awards that management has committed to announcing, and missile stock replenishment lands at the near end of its one-to-two-year window. Fresh hearing-aid industry data proves out at the 4% end, so MSA grows in the second half rather than declining, and Q4 revenue comes in near $175M rather than $165M. PD gross margin reaches the low 40s and holds, 2026 price increases flow into 2027 P&L on the 20-week lead-time lag, and adjusted EBITDA margin exits 2027 at 27–28% on the way to management's ~30% target. Free cash flow normalizes as the inventory build converts, restoring a 15%+ FCF margin. FY2027 EPS reaches $1.75–1.80 and the market pays 28x for a sole-sourced defense component franchise with 0.5x leverage. Approximately $49.

### Bear Case — $28 (−29%)

The MSA second-half decline is end-demand rather than channel timing, and PD order intake proves partly cycle-peak — management's own "transient orders" caveat. Q4 revenue prints near $158–160M, below the guidance floor, and FY2027 guidance comes in at high-single-digit growth. The tax rate normalizes toward 20%+ while the discrete stock-compensation benefits that flattered 1H GAAP earnings do not recur, so non-GAAP EPS growth compresses to low double digits. Capex holds at 5% of revenue and free cash flow yield stays near 2%. Cornell Dubilier's film-capacitor ramp disappoints and the quantitative goodwill test produces a charge. With no put protection in the options market and 8.2% short interest, a multiple reset to 19x on FY2027 EPS of $1.45–1.50 takes the stock to roughly $28.

### Base Case — $42 (+6.4%)

Guidance is delivered close to the midpoint: FY2026 revenue near $658M, adjusted EBITDA up 22%, non-GAAP EPS near $1.34. Q4 growth lands in the low single digits as guided, PD carries the year, and MSA is roughly flat. FY2027 tracks consensus at $1.62 as PD holds mid-to-high-teens growth and margins expand toward 26% EBITDA, offset by an 800bp tax-rate normalization. The market pays 26x — a premium to the group's growth-adjusted average, warranted by the sole-source defense position and balance sheet, but below the current 24x-plus-multiple-expansion required for meaningful upside. $42.

Probability-weighting 55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear produces an expected return near +1%. That is a fair-value outcome, not an asymmetric one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (growth hold). Existing owners should maintain exposure — the business is improving, estimates are rising, and there is a specific 2027 catalyst set. New capital has no margin of safety at $39.48.

- **Base case target: $42** — 26x FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.62. Upside +6.4%.
- **Bull case target: $49** — 28x FY2027 EPS of $1.75. Upside +24.1%.
- **Bear case target: $28** — 19x FY2027 EPS of $1.47. Downside −29.1%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 print (late October 2026), the Q4/FY2026 print with FY2027 guidance (February 2027), and the planned 1H 2027 Investor Day detailing the energy, Micro Solutions Group, inductor, and downhole platforms.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price at $32–34** with fundamentals intact — approximately 20–21x FY2027 consensus, which would restore genuine asymmetry against the $42 base case.
2. **Q3 operating cash flow of $35M+ delivered as guided, with inventories declining sequentially** and full-year adjusted FCF margin recovering above 13%, confirming that first-half cash deterioration was working-capital timing rather than a structural change.
3. **MSA revenue growth of 3%+ in Q3 or Q4** combined with PD book-to-bill above 1.0, which would break the second-half decline embedded in guidance and validate the 10–12% consolidated guide as conservative.
4. **A second contracted multi-year defense order of $15M+**, which would convert management's 2027–2028 framing into visible revenue and justify paying a premium multiple on FY2028 rather than FY2027 earnings.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Q4 revenue below $160M** (below the guidance floor), or FY2027 revenue guidance below +8%, which would confirm the deceleration thesis and invite multiple compression toward the bear case.
2. **A Cornell Dubilier goodwill impairment**, or disclosure that the film-capacitor ramp is missing yield or volume targets — this would hit the energy narrative and book equity simultaneously.
3. **A Syntiant impairment or restructuring of the seller note**, which would crystallize the $89.3M unmarked exposure.
4. **PD book-to-bill below 1.0 for two consecutive quarters**, which would indicate the seven-quarter order run reflected lead-time-driven double ordering rather than end demand.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report in late October is the next decision point, with the FCF recovery and MSA trajectory the two variables that matter most.

## Conclusion

Three prior reviews scored this company 6.8, 6.5, and 6.2 with a HOLD each time. The Q2 print does not overturn that call, and it does not warrant repeating it uncritically either. The genuine changes since June are that the price fell 4.6% while FY2027 consensus rose 5.5%, compressing the forward multiple from roughly 27x to 24.4x — an improvement in risk/reward that I have reflected by raising the valuation score to 5.0 — and that the Q2 disclosure revealed two new items with the opposite sign: MSA decelerated from 13.9% to 1.6% on first-half customer inventory building, and first-half adjusted free cash flow fell to 6.2% of revenue from 20.8%. Net, the overall score moves to 5.9.

What is not in dispute: the balance sheet is strong (0.5x net leverage, 21.6x coverage, 3.20x current ratio, nothing due until February 2028), the distress screens flagging this name are artifacts of a divested business's sunk write-offs, PD is compounding at 20%+ with real pricing power, and the sole-sourced defense filter franchise is a durable asset in an environment of management-described multi-front defense demand.
