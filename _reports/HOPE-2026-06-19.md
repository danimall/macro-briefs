**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hope Bancorp is the $18.7 billion-asset holding company for Bank of Hope, the only regional Korean-American bank in the United States and, after its April 2025 Territorial Bancorp acquisition, the largest regional bank serving multicultural communities across the continental U.S. and Hawaii. The investment story turns on a single distinction: trailing financials look distressed, but the current run-rate shows a real recovery. Net income fell roughly 72% from a $218M peak in 2022 to $62M in 2025 as net interest margin compressed through the high-rate cycle and credit costs rose. Q1 2026 reversed the trend: net income of $29.5M and EPS of $0.23 (up 40% YoY), net interest income up 23% to $124.1M, and NIM expanding 36bp YoY to 2.90%.

The mechanism behind the recovery is straightforward and sector-wide. Fed rate cuts plus the repricing of high-cost time deposits drove the cost of interest-bearing deposits down 77bp to 3.37%, outpacing the 19bp decline in loan yields to 5.69%. HOPE benefits disproportionately because 40% of its deposits are time deposits that reprice as rates fall, and the Territorial deal added a block of low-cost Hawaii deposits. The recovery is genuine but not accelerating: forward consensus estimates are being trimmed, not raised, with the lone positive revision (next-year EPS +17.8% over 30 days) reflecting modeled accretion from the pending SMBC MANUBANK acquisition rather than organic momentum.

We rate HOPE a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. At $12.84 the stock trades at roughly tangible book value ($13.73 TBV/share) and about 12x the $1.11 current-year consensus: fair, not cheap, for a bank running 0.64% ROA and 6.66% ROTCE, well below the low-teens ROTCE that defines a healthy bank. The 12-month thesis rests on continued deposit-cost relief, integration of two acquisitions, and management's >20% loan-growth guide carrying the stock toward the $14.00–$15.00 analyst target band. Concentration in Southern California CRE, crystallizing C&I charge-offs, and a wholesale-tilted funding mix cap the upside and keep this out of BUY territory.

## Company Overview

Bank of Hope operates 45 full-service Korean/Asian-American-focused branches across California, New York, New Jersey, Washington, Texas, Illinois, Georgia, and Alabama, plus 28 Hawaii branches under the Territorial Savings banner acquired in April 2025. It runs SBA, commercial, and mortgage loan production offices nationwide and a Seoul representative office. As of March 31, 2026, total assets were $18.66B.

The business is a fairly traditional spread lender. The core franchise is commercial real estate and C&I lending to small and medium businesses with Korean and Asian-American ties, supplemented by SBA lending (it is an SBA Preferred Lender selling roughly $53M of guaranteed loans per quarter), residential mortgage (boosted by Territorial), trade finance, FX, and treasury management. The company reports as a single segment.

The moat is cultural and relationship-based: serving a Korean-American and increasingly broader Asian-American business community that money-center banks underserve. That drives a low-cost, sticky deposit base and trade-finance/FX fee streams tied to Asia commerce. The cost is concentration: geographic (heavily Southern California), demographic (Korea/Asia macro exposure), and asset-class (58% CRE).

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Net income moved $218M (2022) to $134M (2023) to $100M (2024) to $62M (2025), with ROE bottoming near 2.7% and FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS around $0.49. This was a rate-cycle problem (NIM compression plus elevated credit costs), not a franchise collapse.

**Q1 2026 recovery.** The current quarter is the most important datapoint and tells the recovery story:
- Net income $29.5M, EPS $0.23, versus $21.1M / $0.17 a year ago (+40% YoY), beating the ~$0.22 consensus.
- NII $124.1M, +23% YoY; NIM expanded to 2.90% from 2.54%.
- Cost of interest-bearing deposits fell 77bp to 3.37% on Fed cuts, time-deposit repricing, and low-cost Territorial deposits, against a 19bp decline in loan yields to 5.69%.
- Efficiency ratio improved to ~67% from ~72%.
- ROA 0.64%, ROE 5.14%, ROTCE 6.66%: improving but still well below the ~1% ROA and low-to-mid-teens ROTCE of a healthy bank.

**Balance sheet and funding.** Loans were $14.64B (CRE 58%, C&I 25%, residential mortgage 17%) and shrank 0.4% QoQ as management resolved problem credits. Deposits were $15.73B with a healthy 22% noninterest-bearing, but time deposits are high at 40% and brokered deposits rose to $1.04B from $902M, a modestly wholesale-tilted profile. Uninsured deposits are ~39% ($6.09B). Liquidity is strong: $8.38B of total borrowing capacity plus cash and unpledged securities, with only $285M of FHLB borrowings outstanding. Capital carries cushion: holdco CET1 12.36%, Bank leverage 10.84%, total capital 14.07%.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend held flat at $0.14/quarter ($0.56 annualized, ~4.3% yield). The FY2025 GAAP payout ratio exceeded 100%, a yellow flag, though core earnings covered it and Q1 2026 dividends of $17.9M were comfortably covered by $29.5M net income. Buybacks resumed modestly: 604K shares (~$6.7M) in Q1 2026 at ~$11.10, with $28.6M remaining on the authorization.

**AOCI.** An accumulated other comprehensive loss of -$152M (against $2.28B equity) sits in underwater AFS securities (~$199M gross unrealized losses, mostly agency MBS/CMOs). This is a rate-driven, recoverable mark, but it depresses tangible book so the stock trades right around TBV. Practically, the position is a leveraged bet on continued Fed easing: lower rates help both NIM and the AFS mark; higher rates pressure both.

One mechanical support: the Q1 2026 effective tax rate dropped to 22.1% from 24.2% via new renewable-energy tax-credit investments ($8.1M of credits). Legitimate, but an increasingly relied-upon lever supporting reported net income.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is M&A-led rather than organic. Organic loans declined slightly in Q1. Management guides to >20% loan growth in 2026, driven substantially by the pending all-cash acquisition of SMBC MANUBANK's California commercial banking unit (announced March 31, 2026; ~$2.5B of assets per press reports, structure confirmed in the 10-Q but dollar terms not SEC-disclosed; expected to close 2H 2026 subject to regulatory approval). The deal carries a collaboration agreement under which Bank of Hope becomes the U.S. banking provider for SMBC's Japanese commercial and retail clients, extending the multicultural-niche model from Korean to Japanese-American communities and adding a referral channel.

This follows the April 2025 all-stock Territorial acquisition (6.98M shares / $73.3M), which added 28 Hawaii branches, $1.67B of low-cost deposits, $1.07B of residential mortgages, and a new geography.

On forward earnings, the Street is trimming. Estimate revisions are negative across the near term: current quarter -4.4% (90d), next quarter -11.0% (90d), current year -2.4% (90d). The one positive, next-year EPS +17.8% (30d) to $1.60, reflects MANUBANK accretion entering models, not organic acceleration. The earnings record is mixed: two in-line, two misses over the last four quarters, with the Q2 and Q3 2025 misses (-10.3%, -4.8%) at the trough. This is a cyclical NIM recovery the market is modeling cautiously, not the rising-estimate, accelerating-revenue pattern that would warrant aggressive treatment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $12.84, HOPE trades at:
- ~12x the $1.11 current-year consensus, against a regional-bank peer median of 14.2x (DCOM 15.0, STBA 13.2, LKFN 14.2, CASH 9.9, TFIN 63.1 as an outlier).
- ~0.94x tangible book ($13.73 TBV/share).
- ~8x the $1.60 next-year estimate, if MANUBANK accretion materializes as modeled.

The forward multiple sits slightly below peer-median, and the price-to-tangible-book is appropriate for a sub-peer profitability profile. There is no identifiable asymmetric mispricing. Trading at TBV would be cheap for a bank earning a healthy low-teens ROTCE; for one earning 6.66%, it is fair. The MANUBANK accretion creates upside to the $1.60 next-year number, but execution and capital consumption from the cash deal are the offsets, and that accretion is already in consensus.

Annualizing Q1 ($0.23 × 4 ≈ $0.92) implies the Street's $1.11 already builds in sequential improvement, so the recovery is partly priced. The dividend yield provides absolute support, but the GAAP coverage history tempers how much weight that earns. We score valuation neutral-to-slightly-low: the stock is fairly valued, not a value opportunity, and a roughly peer-median multiple on a below-peer franchise is not a reason to buy.

## Competitive Landscape

HOPE competes with other Asian-American-focused banks (East West, Cathay General, Hanmi, Preferred Bank) and broadly with California and regional banks. Its ~$18.7B asset base gives it scale advantages over smaller ethnic-community banks while keeping it a focused niche play; crossing the $10B threshold subjects it to enhanced CFPB oversight and stress testing.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The Territorial and MANUBANK deals broaden geography (Hawaii) and demographic reach (Japanese-American channel), and the deposit franchise is sticky and culturally differentiated. Working against it: TSR indexed at 127 vs the peer group's 153 in FY2025 indicates shareholders have underperformed the regional-bank set, and sub-peer profitability suggests the market is discounting concentration risk and lower returns. The franchise is durable within its niche but is not visibly gaining share against the broader regional-bank field.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE concentration.** CRE is 58% of loans, with 53% of CRE in Southern California and exposure to specialized property types: multi-tenant retail (19%), industrial warehouse (15%), multifamily (14%), gas stations and car washes (14%), and hotels/motels (9%). Weighted-average LTV is conservative (~47%), but regulators scrutinize CRE-heavy banks and this is the single largest credit exposure.

**Credit normalization, bifurcated.** Nonaccruals improved to $109.5M (0.75% of loans) in Q1 2026 from a $131.7M (0.90%) peak at YE2025 after a large CRE nonaccrual payoff. The pain is in C&I: Q1 net charge-offs of $9.9M were almost entirely C&I. ACL coverage is 1.06% (142% of nonaccruals), adequate but thin if conditions worsen. Watch C&I migration ($29.8M doubtful/loss at quarter-end).

**Funding mix.** 40% time deposits plus rising brokered deposits and 39% uninsured deposits make the funding profile more wholesale-tilted than a deposit-rich peer.

**Korea/Asia and trade policy.** A substantial customer base has economic and cultural ties to South Korea; the 10-K flags transfer and deposit-outflow risk from Korean macro shocks and added language on tariffs/trade policy directly relevant to trade-finance customers.

**Integration and capital.** Back-to-back deals raise integration risk, and the all-cash MANUBANK transaction will consume part of the capital cushion.

**Dividend.** Explicitly flagged as a risk; the >100% GAAP payout in 2025 required attention, though Q1 2026 coverage was comfortable.

The Q1 2026 10-Q reported no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Options activity is unremarkable. The available data shows only a price move ($12.41 → $12.84, +3.5%) between snapshots with no meaningful change in positioning, and short interest is modest at 5.0% of float (5.7 days to cover). There is no elevated put demand or unusual positioning to confirm or contradict the fundamental view. The absence of a bearish options signal is consistent with a fairly-valued, low-drama name rather than one pricing a binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($15.00).** Fed easing continues, deposit costs keep falling faster than asset yields, and NIM grinds toward 3.0%+. The MANUBANK deal closes cleanly in 2H 2026, delivering the >20% loan-growth target and accretion toward the $1.60 next-year EPS. ROA recovers toward the high-0.7% / 0.8% range, ROTCE pushes into double digits, and the AOCI mark recovers as rates fall, lifting tangible book. The stock re-rates modestly above tangible book toward the high analyst target.

**Bear case (~$10.50, roughly -18%).** C&I charge-offs broaden, CRE deterioration resumes in Southern California, and the credit normalization that was supposed to be ending instead extends. Integration of two acquisitions strains management, the cash MANUBANK deal pressures capital, and deposit-cost relief stalls if the rate path reverses. Sub-peer profitability persists, the dividend coverage question re-emerges, and the stock drifts back below tangible book toward its 52-week range midpoint.

**Base case ($14.00, ~+9%).** The NIM recovery holds, MANUBANK closes and contributes, loan growth materializes, but profitability normalizes slowly and concentration keeps a lid on the multiple. The stock works toward the low end of the analyst target band over 12 months, supported by the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is appreciation toward the $14.00–$15.00 analyst target band, driven by continued deposit-cost relief, the MANUBANK acquisition closing and delivering the >20% loan-growth guide, and a gradual return toward normalized profitability. This is a genuine recovery with dated catalysts, but it lacks the asymmetric mispricing that would justify a BUY, particularly in a sector where bank BUYs have historically delivered negative relative performance.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July 2026) for Q2 NIM trajectory and C&I credit trend; the 2H 2026 MANUBANK close for accretion confirmation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NIM expands toward 3.0%+ with two consecutive quarters of positive forward EPS revisions (reversing the current trimming), confirming the recovery is accelerating rather than stabilizing.
- MANUBANK closes on accretive terms with C&I net charge-offs declining below 0.20% annualized, demonstrating credit normalization is complete.
- ROTCE moves sustainably above 9–10%, which would justify a re-rating above tangible book.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- C&I or CRE nonaccruals reverse higher (above the YE2025 0.90% peak) or ACL coverage falls below 1.0%.
- Deposit-cost relief stalls or reverses on a hawkish rate path, compressing NIM back below ~2.7%.
- The dividend is cut or GAAP coverage again falls below 100% on a sustained basis, or MANUBANK integration shows signs of materially straining capital (CET1 falling toward 11%).

## Conclusion

Hope Bancorp is a niche multicultural regional bank climbing out of a rate-cycle earnings trough. The Q1 2026 recovery is real and driven by a durable, sector-wide deposit-cost mechanism that HOPE captures disproportionately given its time-deposit-heavy funding base. The M&A path (Territorial closed, MANUBANK pending) converts a shrinking-loan-book story into a >20%-loan-growth story for 2026.

The offsets keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. The stock trades at a roughly peer-median forward P/E and roughly tangible book, fair for the franchise's profitability profile, with no asymmetric mispricing to exploit. Forward estimates are being trimmed, not raised. Concentration in Southern California CRE and Korea/Asia exposure, crystallizing C&I charge-offs, a wholesale-tilted funding mix, integration risk from back-to-back deals, and a GAAP dividend that was barely covered in 2025 all cap the upside. We would hold for the recovery toward the $14–15 target band and the dividend, and would need accelerating estimate revisions plus completed credit normalization to turn constructive.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the forward P/E was overstated at ~14x (peer-median) when $12.84 / $1.11 ≈ 11.6x, modestly below the 14.2x peer median — a slightly more favorable valuation setup than the HOLD narrative implies, though still not asymmetric.
