**Research Date: June 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

National Beverage Corp. is one of the more financially pristine businesses in consumer staples (no long-term debt, a 42% ROE, 37%+ gross margins, and roughly $170M in annual free cash flow), but its operating story has quietly deteriorated in a way the headline revenue line obscures. FY2025 net sales rose 0.8%, and that gain was entirely price plus a 53rd week, masking a 0.9% volume decline. The Q3 FY2026 10-Q sharpens the picture: case volume fell 4.8% year over year in the quarter and 4.9% over nine months, offset by price/mix of roughly +4.4% to +4.7%. The company is holding revenue flat by pushing price on a shrinking case base, a dynamic that expands margins today at the cost of borrowing from future volume.

The stock has fallen 34% from its July 2025 high of $47.89 to $31.73, and roughly 27% over the trailing twelve months. Some of that is a repricing of a formerly premium-multiple staple down toward peer levels, and some reflects the market absorbing the volume trajectory. At ~15.9x trailing earnings and ~15.9x forward, FIZZ now trades at a meaningful discount to the peer median P/E of 25.3, but that discount is defensible given zero organic unit growth, four consecutive quarterly earnings misses, and a governance structure that diverts economics to the controlling family through a 1%-of-net-sales management fee (~$12M in FY2025).

We rate FIZZ a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The balance sheet and cash generation are strong enough to rule out distress and provide a floor, and the recurring pattern of large special dividends offers episodic upside. But there is no identifiable catalyst to reverse volume declines, estimate revisions are flat, and insiders are net sellers with no conviction buying. This is a high-quality, no-growth cash machine whose per-share economics are partly captured by the controlling shareholder.

## Company Overview

National Beverage, celebrating its 40th anniversary in November 2025, is a vertically integrated U.S. beverage maker positioned around "Better For You" branding. The portfolio splits into two groups:

- **Power+ Brands** (the growth/margin engine): LaCroix sparkling water (the flagship, marketed as the #1 premium domestic sparkling water, zero calorie/sweetener/sodium, 26 flavors), Clear Fruit flavored water, Rip It energy drinks, and Everfresh/Mr. Pure juices.
- **Carbonated Soft Drinks**: legacy regional brands Shasta (135+ years) and Faygo (115+ years), positioned on value and regional loyalty.

The competitive model rests on three pillars: vertical integration (12 production facilities near major metros, in-house flavor and concentrate crafting, company-owned bottling versus rivals' third-party bottlers); speed to market on flavors and packaging; and low-cost marketing through social media, brand ambassadors, and sports sponsorships (including the LaCroix logo on the Florida Panthers) rather than expensive national advertising. FIZZ reports as a single segment, is almost entirely U.S.-focused, and employs roughly 1,681 people (384 unionized).

Its competitors (Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Keurig Dr Pepper, and Nestlé) are dramatically larger and better capitalized, a structural disadvantage in a category (flavored sparkling water) that LaCroix helped create but which is now crowded.

## Financial Analysis

The financials, taken in isolation, are excellent. The four-year trend:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 1,138.0 | 1,172.9 | 1,191.7 | 1,201.4 |
| Gross margin | 36.7% | 33.8% | 36.0% | 37.0% |
| Operating income ($M) | 207.9 | 186.7 | 218.5 | 235.5 |
| Net income ($M) | 158.5 | 142.2 | 176.7 | 186.8 |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | — | 197.9 | 206.7 |

FY2025 delivered ROE of 42.1%, ROA of 27.8%, an operating margin of 19.6%, and FCF of $170.4M (14.2% margin). The Altman Z-Score of 5.5 sits deep in the safe zone. Long-term debt was eliminated by FY2023 (down from $30M in FY2022), interest expense is a trivial $116K, and the current ratio is 2.9x. This is a fortress balance sheet by any staples standard, and it justifies a high Financial Health score.

The central issue sits underneath the profit line. Revenue growth has stalled, and the composition is deteriorating:

- FY2025 net sales +0.8%, comprising price and a 53rd week, masking volume of −0.9%.
- Q3 FY2026 net sales −0.9% YoY: case volume −4.8%, offset by price/mix +4.4%.
- Nine-month FY2026 net sales −0.5%: case volume −4.9%, offset by price +4.7%.

Volume declines hit both Power+ Brands and CSDs. Margins continue to expand (Q3 FY26 gross margin 37.6%, nine-month 37.8%), but that expansion is being manufactured through price on a shrinking case base. This is a classic late-cycle staples dynamic: pricing holds revenue and lifts margins until elasticity bites and the volume decline accelerates. Retailers and consumers accept a certain amount of price, but past a threshold, unit demand contracts faster than price can offset, and the pricing lever reverses into a headwind. A modest and temporary aluminum-hedge tailwind ($11.4M unrealized gain to flow into earnings over roughly 12 months on $30.9M notional) flatters near-term COGS but does not change this trajectory.

The earnings surprise record reinforces the concern: FIZZ has missed consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters (−2.0%, −5.8%, −2.0%, −2.0%). These are small misses, but the consistency signals that the pricing-for-volume trade is running out of room to beat.

Capital allocation is defined by episodic special dividends rather than a regular dividend or buybacks. FY2025 was dominated by a $304.1M special cash dividend (paid July 24, 2024), which cut cash from $327M to $194M and equity from $560M to $444M despite record profits. Cash has since rebuilt to $314M by January 2026, setting up another potential special dividend on the historical pattern. The buyback authorization (1.87M shares remaining) is used only tokenally (20,000 shares / $0.7M in nine months of FY26). Capex is modest at ~$36M for capacity, efficiency, and sustainability. Investors are effectively buying an unpredictable special-dividend stream layered on a slow-growth, high-margin core.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic unit growth thesis here. The company has grown revenue by less than 1% annually while shedding roughly 5% of case volume per year, replacing that with price. Estimate revisions confirm the stagnation: current-year EPS consensus is $2.00, unchanged over both 30 and 90 days; next-year consensus is $2.01, also flat. There is no earnings inflection, no positive revision velocity, and no accelerating revenue line.

The volume erosion is the growth story in reverse. LaCroix created the mass-market flavored sparkling water category but now faces well-capitalized entrants (Coca-Cola with AHA and Topo Chico Sabores, PepsiCo with Bubly, Keurig Dr Pepper, Nestlé) plus adjacent functional/prebiotic soda brands pulling incremental consumer occasions. The volume declines across both Power+ and CSD brands are consistent with share loss and/or category maturation. A beverage maker leaning entirely on price faces a demand environment where the pricing lever is more likely to become a headwind than a tailwind over the next 12 months.

Growth Prospects score low. The prior report's decline from 4.5 to 3.5 on this dimension is confirmed and extends further given the widening volume decline and four-quarter miss streak.

## Valuation Assessment

At $31.73, FIZZ carries a ~$2.9B market cap, ~15.9x forward P/E, and ~15.9x trailing net income. Against a peer median P/E of 25.3, that is a substantial discount:

| CELH | Celsius | $7B | 64.5 | — |
| COKE | Coca-Cola Consolidated | $11B | 25.3 | 0.5% |
| KDP | Keurig Dr Pepper | $42B | 23.2 | 2.9% |
| MNST | Monster Beverage | $89B | 44.5 | — |
| PEP | PepsiCo | $194B | 22.6 | 4.1% |
| **FIZZ** | **National Beverage** | **$2.9B** | **~15.9** | **0.0%** |

The discount is warranted, not a mispricing. FIZZ's peers, even the slow-growing ones (KDP, PEP), generate positive volume growth and pay a regular dividend. FIZZ has negative volume, no regular yield, and diverts roughly 1% of net sales to a controlling-shareholder-owned entity before minority holders see the economics. Our calibration work consistently shows that a low P/E in the absence of an independent catalyst is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, and that cheapness has been reliably anti-predictive. FIZZ fits the pattern: cheap versus peers, but for identifiable reasons.

There is no earnings inflection to trigger the cycle-adjusted valuation treatment; estimates are flat, not rising. The prior-appreciation-is-not-a-valuation-input principle cuts the other way here: the 34% drawdown is not evidence the stock is cheap, but neither is it evidence of a buying opportunity, because the fundamentals (volume, misses, governance leakage) genuinely support the derating. The analyst price target of $33 (single covering analyst, a Sell rating) sits ~4% above spot; this is not a case where consensus targets are stale floors lagging an accelerating business, since the single analyst carries a Sell.

A defensible fair-value range: the market is likely correct to price FIZZ around 15-16x on a no-growth base with governance leakage. The optionality on a special dividend and the balance-sheet floor argue against pricing it much below ~13x ($26-27), while any sustained volume stabilization or a large special dividend could support ~17x ($33-34). Valuation scores modestly below neutral: cheap on the multiple, but for real reasons and without a catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

FIZZ's structural edge (vertical integration, in-house flavor development, company-owned bottling, and agile low-cost marketing) is real and protects margins. The 37%+ gross margin sustained through a volume decline is the evidence: the company can defend profitability per case. What it cannot do, on current evidence, is defend cases sold. Volume is declining across both brand groups, which points to share loss to better-capitalized incumbents (Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Keurig Dr Pepper, Nestlé) and to newer functional-beverage entrants capturing the incremental occasions that once flowed to LaCroix.

The competitive trajectory is the key input for scoring, and it is deteriorating. A category pioneer losing units in the category it created scores lower than a challenger gaining share, regardless of absolute margin quality. External scanner data on LaCroix trends and Poppi/Olipop share gains could not be independently verified this session, but the internal volume data is consistent with erosion rather than stability. Competitive Position scores low on trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that matter for FIZZ specifically, weighted for what the market may not be fully pricing:

1. **Volume erosion / consumer preference shift (most important).** The case declines are the defining risk. If price elasticity bites, the pricing offset reverses and revenue turns negative on both volume and price, compressing the margin story that has held the equity up.

2. **Governance concentration and related-party leakage.** FIZZ is a controlled company run by Nick Caporella (Chairman/CEO) with son Joseph as President; the family controls the vast majority of shares. Corporate Management Advisors, Inc., a CEO-owned entity, receives a fee equal to 1% of consolidated net sales ($12.0M in FY2025, $8.8M in nine months of FY2026), a structural, growing cash transfer to the controlling shareholder that sits outside normal compensation. Reported CEO comp of $12.0M shows "compensation actually paid" of $0 across five years, a reporting artifact of the CMA structure. Capital return is lumpy and family-driven, not policy-based.

3. **Transparency by design.** No earnings calls, idiosyncratic press releases, an auditor change in November 2024 (Item 4.01, no disclosed dispute), and an October 2025 "Other Events" 8-K that could not be retrieved. Low disclosure raises the required scrutiny bar even where audited financials look clean.

4. **Input cost / tariff exposure.** Over 80% of products are in aluminum cans; the 10-K flags tariffs and commodity volatility (aluminum, resin, corn, linerboard, juice). Partial hedging ($30.9M notional) provides a modest near-term buffer only.

5. **Regulatory — SNAP and synthetic dyes.** Proposals to remove sweetened products from SNAP and phase out synthetic dyes could affect Shasta/Faygo/Rip It (management states LaCroix is unaffected by dye rules).

6. **Key-person dependence.** Explicitly tied to a small group of long-tenured executives in a concentrated family structure.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 46.4% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 27.8% (an 18.6-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (46.4% near-term versus 33.6% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent event, most likely the next earnings release or a special-dividend announcement. The 1-month put/call volume ratio of 3.15 shows heavy near-term put buying, though the put/call OI ratio of 0.62 and a call-heavy skew (−8.1% at the front) send a more mixed signal; the 3-month skew of −23.2% is put-heavy. Short interest is elevated at 14.0% of float (10.4 days to cover), confirming institutional bearish positioning. The implied ±9.4% move on the nearest expiry frames the binary. The market is bracing for a catalyst and leans bearish, consistent with the volume-erosion and miss-streak concerns; it does not support adding on the long side ahead of the event.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
FIZZ is a debt-free, 42%-ROE, 37%-gross-margin cash machine trading at ~15.9x forward, a discount of roughly a third to the peer median. Cash has rebuilt to $314M, setting up another large special dividend on the multi-year historical pattern (the FY2025 special was $304M, roughly 10% of the current market cap). Vertical integration protects margins even as volumes decline, and the aluminum hedge provides a near-term COGS tailwind. If volume stabilizes (LaCroix category maturation flattens rather than accelerating), the stock re-rates modestly toward 17x on stable ~$2.00 EPS, with a special dividend providing episodic total-return kicker. Bull case ~$36-37.

### Bear Case
Price elasticity bites: the volume decline that has been offset by price accelerates as consumers and retailers resist further increases in a softening discretionary environment. Revenue turns negative on both volume and price, margin expansion stalls, and the four-quarter miss streak extends into outright EPS declines. The market prices a shrinking no-growth staple at ~13x on eroding earnings, and the governance discount (CMA leakage, no regular dividend, minimal transparency) widens. Bear case ~$26-27, roughly 15-18% below spot.

### Base Case
Volume continues to decline in the low-to-mid single digits, price offsets most of it, revenue is roughly flat, and margins hold near 37-38% aided temporarily by the aluminum hedge. EPS stays near $2.00. The stock trades in a $30-34 range, with a potential special-dividend announcement the main upside swing factor and a volume-decline acceleration the main downside risk. Fair value ~$31-32, essentially at spot.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD; the reassessment framework:

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Case volume declines narrow to flat-to-positive for two consecutive quarters, confirming category stabilization rather than continued share loss.
- The stock compresses to ~13x forward (~$26-27) while EPS holds near $2.00, restoring a genuine margin of safety with balance-sheet support (a valuation floor rather than a value trap).
- Governance improvement: elimination or capping of the CMA related-party fee, or institution of a regular dividend/systematic buyback that returns capital on policy rather than family discretion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Case volume decline accelerates beyond −5% while price/mix offset shrinks below +4%, signaling elasticity has broken the pricing lever and revenue is turning negative.
- Gross margin rolls over below ~36% as the aluminum-hedge tailwind lapses and input costs rise, removing the last pillar supporting the equity.
- A further EPS miss combined with negative estimate revisions, or any deterioration in disclosure/governance surfacing from the unretrieved October 2025 "Other Events" 8-K or the auditor change.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings release (Q4 FY2026, expected mid-2026), which will confirm whether the volume/price dynamic is stabilizing or breaking. A special-dividend announcement, if it comes on the historical pattern, would also warrant immediate reassessment of total-return math.

## Conclusion

National Beverage is a genuinely excellent business on the metrics that measure balance-sheet resilience and profitability: no debt, 42% ROE, 37%+ gross margins, strong free cash flow, and a Z-Score deep in the safe zone. Those attributes justify a high Financial Health score and rule out any distress or shorting thesis; the stock is not actively collapsing.

The quality is offset by a growth engine that has stalled and reversed. Revenue is flat only because the annual volume decline is being papered over with price, a trade that expands margins now while borrowing from future demand, and four consecutive earnings misses suggest the room to keep beating on price is narrowing. Layer on a controlled-company structure that diverts ~1% of net sales to a CEO-owned entity, no regular dividend, minimal disclosure, and net insider selling, and the discount to peers looks earned rather than mispriced. At ~15.9x forward with flat estimates and no identifiable catalyst, there is no asymmetric edge in either direction. We rate FIZZ a **HOLD** (uncertain rationale): own it for the balance-sheet floor and the optionality of a lumpy special dividend, but do not mistake cheapness for opportunity absent evidence that the volume decline is stabilizing.
