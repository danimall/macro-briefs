**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Skyworks Solutions no longer trades on its standalone fundamentals. Since October 27, 2025, the stock has been governed by the pending all-in merger with Qorvo, a combination of the #1 and #2 U.S. RF front-end suppliers into a ~$22B enterprise-value company in which Skyworks holders will own roughly 63%. That transaction, and specifically the antitrust review triggered by the February 5, 2026 FTC Second Request, is the dominant variable in the equity. The revenue trajectory, the customer concentration, and the margin compression describe the asset that either gets absorbed into the combined entity or reverts to a challenged standalone if the deal breaks.

The standalone story is one of secular erosion. Revenue has fallen from $5,485M in FY2022 to $4,087M in FY2025 (-25%), net income from $1,275M to $477M (-63%), and gross margin from 47.5% to ~41.2%. Management repeatedly attributes the decline to "a decrease in market share at a significant customer" — a reference the filings do not name but which industry dynamics strongly identify as Apple's in-housing of RF/modem content. This is a structural headwind, not a cyclical trough. The offset is a diversification bucket ("emerging revenue" — automotive, WiFi 7/8, BAW ex-largest-customer, audio) that grew 24% in FY2025 but remains small at ~$740M of $4.1B.

The counterweight is balance-sheet quality: near-zero net leverage (~$997M total debt against ~$1,436M cash and marketable securities), ~$1.1B FY2025 free cash flow, and a dividend (~$2.84 annualized) comfortably covered even after the recent raise. Even a broken deal leaves a financially sound company. But the stock at ~$60.47 sits below the CEO's own PSA base price of $65.64 and far below the FY2024-25 grant prices of $72-89, and analyst sentiment is degrading (buy ratings cut from 3 to 1 over the past month). With a binary regulatory outcome, decelerating but still-negative organic revenue, and no insider conviction buying, the risk/reward does not support a directional call. We rate SWKS HOLD.

## Company Overview

Skyworks develops analog and mixed-signal semiconductors, principally RF front-end components — power amplifiers, filters, switches, low-noise amplifiers, and integrated front-end modules. End markets span smartphones, tablets, wearables, WiFi/connected home, automotive, cellular infrastructure, defense/aerospace, industrial, and medical. The company is vertically integrated, operating its own gallium-arsenide and bulk-acoustic-wave (BAW) filter fabs, with ~10,000 employees (roughly 76% outside the U.S., concentrated in Mexicali and Singapore).

Two structural facts define the business. First, distribution weighting: ~86% of net revenue routes through distributors, and ~71-75% of revenue is booked to U.S.-headquartered OEMs, a concentration in U.S. smartphone customers, primarily Apple. Second, the pending Qorvo merger, signed October 27, 2025, which pairs Skyworks' closest RF competitor into a combined RF/analog company. Qorvo holders receive $32.50/share cash plus 0.960 SWKS shares; both shareholder bases approved the deal on February 11, 2026. The transaction, not the quarterly cadence, is the investment case.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year picture is one of consistent erosion:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $5,485M | $4,772M | $4,178M | $4,087M |
| Gross margin | 47.5% | 44.2% | 41.2% | 41.2% |
| Operating income | $1,527M | $1,125M | $637M | $500M |
| Net income | $1,275M | $983M | $596M | $477M |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | — | $1,301M |
| Free cash flow | — | — | — | $1,106M |

Gross margin compressed ~630 bps over the period. The drivers are the post-COVID smartphone downcycle, content/share loss at the largest customer, and unfavorable product mix. Management explicitly warns that ASPs for established products "will continue to decline over time"; the erosion is endemic, not episodic.

The most recent quarter (Q2 FY2026, ended April 3, 2026) shows the merger noise directly. Revenue was $943.7M, down 1.0% YoY (six-month revenue $1,979M, -2.1%), a deceleration but not a reversal. Reported operating income fell to $42.1M (4.5% margin) from $97.3M (10.2%) a year earlier. That headline overstates the operating deterioration: it includes $19.7M of Qorvo transaction costs in the quarter ($53.4M over six months) and a deliberate R&D step-up of +13.9% YoY to $212.4M (now 22.5% of revenue vs. 19.6%), with SG&A up 36% largely on deal fees. Gross margin held roughly flat at ~40.8%. Adjusting for deal costs, the underlying operating margin is meaningfully above the reported 4.5%. Net income was $35.6M ($0.24 diluted) vs. $68.7M ($0.43).

Balance sheet quality is a genuine strength and the primary downside cushion. Cash plus marketable securities of ~$1,436M against total debt of ~$997M ($500M 1.80% notes due 2026, $500M 3.00% notes due 2031) leaves the company essentially net-cash. The $750M revolver is undrawn with maturity extended to November 2030. Stockholders' equity is ~$5.77B; goodwill ($2.18B) plus intangibles ($0.72B) show no impairment indicators. Current ratio is ~2.4x, D/E ~0.17x, ROE ~8%, and FY2025 FCF margin ~27%. This financial-health profile — the one dimension I weight most heavily as a winner/loser discriminator — is the reason a broken deal is survivable rather than catastrophic.

Two capital-allocation signals matter. The dividend was raised to $0.71/quarter (~$2.84 annualized), covered ~2.6x by FY2025 FCF, signaling management confidence in cash generation. But share repurchases have been halted: after buying back $504M in Q2 FY2025, the company repurchased essentially $0 in 1H FY2026 despite ~$1.2B remaining authorized. The buyback pause indicates cash is being conserved for the ~$3.4B+ cash portion of the Qorvo consideration.

## Growth Analysis

The standalone growth outlook is negative-to-flat. Revenue has declined for three consecutive fiscal years. The structural driver — Apple's in-housing of RF/modem silicon — is a permanent reduction in Skyworks' addressable content at its largest customer, not a cyclical trough that mean-reverts.

The offsetting bull element is the "emerging revenue" bucket: automotive, WiFi 7/8, BAW ex-largest-customer, audio, and mixed-signal. WiFi products are growing and partially offset the customer share loss. But at ~18% of total revenue, this segment is not yet large enough to inflect the consolidated trajectory. The strategic rationale for the Qorvo merger is precisely to accelerate this diversification — combining two Apple-exposed RF vendors to push into defense/aerospace, automotive, industrial, and data-center markets while extracting cost synergies against a mature shared end market.

Estimate revisions are modestly constructive but not the +30% inflection signal that would flip the growth call. Current-year EPS estimates rose +6.9% over 90 days (to $5.00) and current-quarter +11.1% (to $1.03), but 30-day revisions are flat (-0.1% to 0.0%); the momentum has stalled. The earnings-surprise record is strong (four consecutive beats, +6.6% to +23.6%), which raises confidence in near-term consensus, but stable estimates on a declining revenue base do not constitute a growth thesis. Growth Prospects scores 4.5: the standalone business is contracting, and the diversification offset is real but subscale.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing EPS of $3.08 the stock trades at ~19.6x; on the current-year consensus of $5.00 it trades at ~12.1x forward. Against the semiconductor peer median P/E of 27.0 (QRVO 27.0, CRUS 20.8), SWKS looks cheap. That cheapness is the trap. The equity lessons from this system's own history are unambiguous: a low multiple is anti-predictive of returns — cheap semiconductors have sorted the wrong way — and a compressed multiple that reflects structural impairment is a value trap absent a specific catalyst.

The catalyst here is the Qorvo deal, but it cuts both ways. If the merger completes, SWKS holders own 63% of a diversified $22B RF/analog leader with synergy upside and reduced Apple concentration, supporting a re-rating. If it breaks, Skyworks pays up to $298.7M ($100M in the antitrust-failure scenario), reverts to a declining-revenue standalone, and the current multiple compresses further toward the low-$50s support that held at the March 2026 low ($51.93). The FTC Second Request materially raises the break probability, which is why the "cheap" forward multiple does not translate into asymmetric upside.

Analyst targets bracket the outcome: low $55, mean $72.94, median $74, high $85, against a current price near $60.47. This is a sub-30x forward name where the target dispersion reflects the binary deal outcome rather than a clean growth-adjusted valuation. Pzena's 8.2% deep-value stake frames SWKS as a beaten-down turnaround with embedded merger optionality rather than an underpriced compounder. Valuation scores 5.0: cheap on paper, but the discount is doing exactly what a structural-impairment discount should do, and the re-rating requires clearing a regulatory hurdle that is not in Skyworks' control.

## Competitive Landscape

Skyworks sits in the RF front-end oligopoly alongside Qorvo, Broadcom, Qualcomm, and Murata. The competitive trajectory is deteriorating rather than stable. The company's own disclosures describe a market leader ceding content. Apple's in-housing of connectivity/modem silicon is the direct threat, and it is progressing. Gross margin down 630 bps since FY2022 corroborates the loss of pricing power.

The Qorvo merger is a defensive response to this trajectory. Two mid-cap RF vendors, both squeezed by smartphone maturity and customer insourcing, are combining for scale, diversification away from Apple, and cost synergies against a mature end market. The strategic logic is sound, but the FTC Second Request reflects the flip side: regulators are scrutinizing the horizontal overlap of the #1 and #2 U.S. RF players. Standalone, both companies face the same secular pressures. Competitive Position scores 4.0, consistent with the prior report's downgrade from 6.0; new data confirms rather than reverses the deterioration.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger execution / antitrust (dominant).** The FTC Second Request extends the HSR waiting period and raises the probability of a protracted review, remedies, or a block. Timeline is guided to "early 2027" in the 10-Q, with a possible end-2026 close per one third-party source. Break fees run to $298.7M (superior-proposal/board-change) or $100M (antitrust failure). This is the single most important variable.

**Customer concentration / content-share loss.** With ~71% of revenue tied to U.S. OEMs and structural insourcing at the largest customer, the standalone revenue base is exposed to further erosion. This risk is partially priced but not fully — the market has not yet resolved whether the emerging-revenue diversification can offset the core decline.

**Pricing/margin pressure.** ASP erosion is explicitly acknowledged as endemic; mix remains unfavorable.

**Deal financing.** A Goldman bridge (reduced to $1.5B after the Qorvo Notes tranche was cut to $0 in December 2025) funds the cash consideration; Skyworks takes on incremental debt at close, changing the currently pristine balance sheet.

**Leadership discontinuity.** FY2025 saw a CEO change (Griffin out, Brace in), a failed CFO hire (medical), a board member serving as interim CFO, and a permanent CFO poached from AMD — all within months of signing a transformational merger. The team is new and unproven together.

Litigation risk is low: the Denso BAW patent litigation was settled in 1H FY2026 for an immaterial charge.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. ATM IV of 76.5% sits far above 1-year historical volatility of 44.7% (a 31.8-point rich spread, 92nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 76.5% > long-term 64.5%), consistent with the market pricing an imminent regulatory/deal update. Since June 2, IV rose 14.6 points while the stock fell 23.9%, and the put/call OI ratio flipped from 0.08 to 2.44. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±16.5%. The near-term put/call volume ratio of 7.16 is heavily put-skewed, though the ATM skew is modestly call-heavy (-4.0%), a mixed picture that reflects both hedging into the catalyst and some positioning for a favorable deal outcome. The elevated IV argues against selling premium and confirms that a binary resolution is near-term.

Short interest of 30.4% of float (5.2 days to cover) is very high. In a merger-arb context this is not purely bearish — a portion likely reflects arbitrage shorts against the stock leg of the cash-and-stock deal — but it also introduces squeeze risk on any positive antitrust surprise and reflects genuine skepticism about the standalone franchise.

Risk Factors scores 4.0: the antitrust outcome is a binary, unpriced-resolution event with material downside if it breaks, and the market is not confidently positioned in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$74-85).** The Qorvo merger clears antitrust (with or without minor remedies) and closes by early 2027. SWKS holders own 63% of a diversified $22B RF/analog leader with reduced Apple concentration, cost synergies, and a re-rating toward the peer 27x multiple. The emerging-revenue bucket scales, WiFi momentum continues, and the near-net-cash balance sheet supports the transition. Consensus median ($74) and high ($85) targets frame this outcome. Squeeze dynamics on the 30.4% short interest could amplify the move on a clean regulatory approval.

**Bear Case (~$51-55).** The FTC blocks or forces material remedies, or the deal collapses. Skyworks pays up to $298.7M, reverts to a standalone with a structurally declining top line and eroding Apple content, and the multiple compresses toward the March 2026 low ($51.93). Analyst low target ($55) anchors this scenario. The dividend remains covered, so the downside is cushioned rather than catastrophic, but the standalone equity has no growth catalyst.

The central tension is that both cases hinge on a regulatory decision outside management's control, with roughly symmetric probability weighting after the Second Request. That symmetry is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is an event-driven situation where I lack sufficient conviction on the binary regulatory outcome to make a directional call, and the standalone fundamentals do not independently support a BUY.

**Reassessment timeline.** The key node is the antitrust review resolution. The next natural checkpoints are quarterly earnings (deal-cost drag and organic revenue trajectory) and any 8-K disclosure of FTC engagement, consent decree, or litigation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Clear FTC approval or a manageable consent-decree path with a defined close date — this removes the break-risk and unlocks the diversified-leader re-rating.
- Organic (ex-deal-cost, ex-emerging-revenue) core revenue stabilizing YoY for two consecutive quarters, indicating the Apple content loss has bottomed.
- Emerging-revenue bucket exceeding ~25% of total revenue, demonstrating the diversification thesis is inflecting the consolidated top line.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FTC blocks the deal or forces divestitures that gut the synergy case, forcing reversion to the declining standalone.
- Core revenue decline re-accelerates below -5% YoY, signaling the content-share loss is broadening beyond the largest customer.
- Any dividend coverage deterioration (FCF falling below ~$700M annualized) that threatens the ~4.5% yield support.

## Conclusion

Skyworks is a merger-arbitrage/event-driven position in a semiconductor stock. The standalone business is structurally challenged — three years of revenue decline, 630 bps of gross-margin compression, and a permanent content-share loss at its largest customer — but it is financially sound, near-net-cash, and generating ~$1.1B of FCF that covers a well-supported dividend. The Qorvo merger is a credible defensive response to the erosion, offering diversification and scale, but the FTC Second Request has turned the equity into a binary bet on a regulatory outcome that neither management nor investors control.

At ~$60.47, the forward multiple of ~12x is cheap against a 27x peer median, but that discount reflects structural impairment and deal-break risk rather than a mispricing to be arbitraged. Analyst sentiment is degrading, insiders are not buying, and the options market is pricing imminent stress with IV in the 92nd percentile and backwardated term structure. The overall score of 4.8 is unchanged from the prior report, consistent with a stalled trajectory in which improving financial health is offset by deteriorating competitive position and a binary risk overhang. We rate SWKS **HOLD** and would revisit on antitrust clarity in either direction.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the ~$2.84 annualized dividend implies a ~$427M/yr cash outlay (~2.6x FY2025 FCF coverage, not 2.5x); the dividend yield support (~4.5%) and FCF-coverage-based downgrade trigger of ~$700M annualized FCF should be re-checked against these figures, but none of these corrections alter the HOLD thesis.
