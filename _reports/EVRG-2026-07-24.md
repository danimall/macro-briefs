**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Evergy is a regulated, vertically integrated electric utility serving ~1.7 million customers across Kansas and Missouri, and it has become a direct beneficiary of the structural shift in U.S. electricity demand driven by data centers and reshoring. The investment case rests almost entirely on this load-growth thesis, and the key nuance is how far it has been de-risked. Rather than projecting speculative demand, Evergy has signed roughly 2,500 MW of large-load energy-supply agreements carrying approximately $8.7B of contracted minimum-payment obligations over 10–17 year terms, with minimum bills structured at 80% of expected capacity demand. The regulatory scaffolding is in place: large-load power service (LLPS) tariffs are approved in both states, Missouri SB4 permits construction-work-in-progress in rate base for new gas plants, and Kansas PISA extends favorable recovery mechanics. This combination converts the growth narrative into a take-or-pay structure and materially lowers the execution risk on a ~$21.6B 2026–2030 capital plan.

The financial mechanics are those of a heavy-capex regulated utility. FY2025 revenue was ~$5.96B (+2% YoY) with GAAP diluted EPS of $3.66 (adjusted $3.83). Q1 2026 showed clean momentum (revenue +5%, net income +21%, adjusted EPS $0.69 vs $0.55), and management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance while raising its load-growth outlook. The capital plan will be funded with roughly $12B+ of incremental debt and ~$3.3B of equity, meaning free cash flow is structurally negative and per-share growth is partly offset by dilution. The Altman Z of 0.86 and weak Piotroski score look alarming but are normal artifacts of a rate-regulated, debt-financed balance sheet with investment-grade ratings, not distress signals.

The debate is valuation, not fundamentals. At ~$87 (near the 52-week high of $88.62, up ~25% over the trailing year), EVRG trades at ~23x earnings — a premium to the electric-utility peer median of 21.7x — while offering a below-median 3.2% dividend yield. Near-term estimate revisions are drifting lower (current-quarter EPS cut 8.2% over 30 days), and the stock trades below the analyst mean target of $91.88. The load story is real and the regulatory alignment is favorable, but at this multiple the market has already priced most of the upside. We rate EVRG a HOLD (growth_hold), owning it for a mid-single-digit total-return profile rather than asymmetric appreciation, and we would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s or a re-acceleration in estimate revisions.

## Company Overview

Evergy was formed in 2017 through the merger of Westar Energy and Great Plains Energy and is headquartered in Kansas City, Missouri. It operates three regulated subsidiaries — Evergy Kansas Central (the largest, ~$15.9B assets), Evergy Metro (Missouri and Kansas), and Evergy Missouri West — and owns roughly 15,800 MW of coal, natural gas, nuclear, and renewable generation within the Southwest Power Pool RTO. The company holds a 94% interest in the Wolf Creek nuclear station and minority stakes in two transmission joint ventures (Transource, 13.5%; Prairie Wind, 50%) alongside AEP and Berkshire Hathaway Energy. Evergy reports as a single segment.

As a regulated monopoly, Evergy's competitive advantage is its franchise service territory, and its earnings are driven by rate-base growth and allowed returns on equity (Kansas earnings-sharing band ~9.7% ROE; Missouri Metro rate case seeking 10.5% ROE on a 52% equity layer). Over the past 18 months, a historically flat-load Midwestern territory has become an attractive destination for data-center development — cheap land, available power, and favorable siting — which has re-rated the stock.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 net income attributable to Evergy was ~$855.6M, with GAAP diluted EPS down from $3.79 in 2024. The decline reflects convertible-note dilution and ~$48.7M of losses on early-stage clean-energy investments being wound down. Adjusted EPS — management's guided metric — was $3.83 versus $3.81 prior year. Prior-year revenue was $5.85B in 2024 and $5.51B in 2023.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue rose to $1,443.7M, net income attributable rose to $151.5M, and GAAP diluted EPS was $0.64 (vs $0.54). The drivers were new Evergy Kansas Central retail rates effective October 2025 (+$40M), higher non-regulated energy-marketing revenue (+$16.6M), higher AFUDC on rising construction balances, and COLI proceeds — partially offset by higher depreciation, interest, and O&M. Weather was a modest headwind offset by higher weather-normalized demand.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was ~$2.0B against capex of $2.8B, leaving free cash flow externally funded. Q1 2026 capex jumped 44% YoY to $851.9M, signaling the ramp toward the multi-year plan. Total long-term debt is ~$13.5B (book) plus ~$1.46B of commercial paper; debt/equity is ~3.3x with equity at ~43% of capitalization — normal for the model. Credit ratings sit around BBB+/Baa2 (stable). The dividend is $0.695/quarter (~$2.78 annualized, ~3.2% yield), targeting 4–6% annual growth on a 50–60% payout.

**Capital allocation.** The ~$21.6B 2026–2030 capital plan is the central financial fact — a sharp step-up funded by roughly $12B+ of incremental debt and ~$3.3B of equity, including a $1.2B ATM program with forward sales struck around $72.50/share. In Q1 2026, Evergy repurchased $244M of its $1.4B 4.50% convertible notes for $309.5M (taking a small $10.3M loss), limiting dilution now that the stock trades well above the conversion trigger, with ~4.9M dilutive shares still outstanding from the remaining $1.16B of converts.

The Altman Z of 0.86 and Piotroski of ~2 are structural, not distress signals. Heavy regulated debt, low asset turnover, and a peak-capex year mechanically depress these scores; investment-grade ratings and rate-regulated cash flows are the correct lens. The genuine financial constraint is the negative-FCF, debt-and-equity-funded model operating in a higher-for-longer rate environment: Q1 interest expense rose $22M YoY, and ongoing ATM issuance dilutes per-share growth. This is why financial health scores in the middle of the range rather than higher: the balance sheet is sound and IG-rated, but the model requires continuous external capital access and the per-share growth is levered to funding costs.

**Earnings track record.** The recent surprise history is mixed: Q1 2026 beat by 4.6%, but the prior three quarters missed (Q4 2025 by -27.3%, Q3 2025 by -3.2%, Q2 2025 by -1.4%). One beat in four quarters warrants only moderate confidence in near-term consensus, consistent with the negative current-quarter revision trend.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is the strongest part of the case and has genuinely improved over the trailing two quarters. The signed ~2,500 MW of data-center energy-supply agreements comprise three new projects plus two expansions, with service ramping 2026–2028. The minimum-bill structure (80% of expected capacity demand, with collateral and termination protections) is the single most important nuance a standardized screen would miss: it converts the load thesis from a projection into contractual take-or-pay revenue that also protects existing ratepayers from cost-shift.

The regulatory tailwinds are well-aligned. LLPS tariffs enable long-term minimum-take contracts for customers above 75 MW. Missouri SB4 (2025) allows CWIP in rate base for new gas plants through 2035, materially improving cost recovery timing. Kansas retains PISA and added wildfire liability caps. This clustering of favorable outcomes across jurisdictions lowers execution risk on the capital plan and supports management's mid-single-digit adjusted EPS growth target and 4–6% dividend growth.

The counterweight is the estimate-revision data, which is not confirming the growth narrative in the near term. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 8.2% over 30 days and 5.9% over 90 days. Next-quarter and next-year revisions are flat-to-marginally-positive (+5.2% next quarter, +0.4% current year, +0.2% next year over 90 days). The picture is a long-duration structural growth story — the load ramps over 2026–2028 and rate base compounds through 2030 — with soft near-term earnings momentum. This is a directional positive on growth but not the +30% revision velocity that would signal the market is systematically underestimating an acceleration. We weight the contracted-load durability and the regulatory alignment more heavily than the near-term revision softness, which supports a growth score in the high-6s rather than the 7.0 assigned in the prior report; the difference is the recognition that flat-to-negative revisions cap how much the structural story can compound EPS per share once dilution is netted out.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$87, EVRG trades at ~23x trailing/forward earnings (EPS $3.66; consensus current-year $4.26, next-year $4.56). Against the electric-utility peer median P/E of 21.7x, that is a modest premium. Within the peer set: LNT 22.8x, PPL 21.7x, FE 25.4x, ES 14.6x, EIX 7.9x. The dispersion is wide and reflects idiosyncratic issues (EIX and ES carry wildfire and balance-sheet overhangs that depress multiples), so the median is the more useful anchor. EVRG's premium to that median is defensible given superior contracted load growth, but it is a premium, not a discount.

The dividend yield of 3.2% sits below the peer median of 4.0%, meaning the income support is thinner than typical for the group. The analyst mean target is $91.88 (median $92.00), with a range of $80–$103. The current price is roughly 5% below the mean target on a price-only basis, and with the ~3.2% yield the total-return proximity to consensus is close. On EVRG's growth and valuation profile (~23x forward, mid-single-digit growth, PEG well above 1.5), analyst targets here are not stale floors; this is a stable-multiple regulated utility rather than a cheaply-valued acceleration story, so proximity to consensus is meaningful rather than something the market will chase upward.

The valuation score sits at the midpoint deliberately. The load story is real and the multiple is not egregiously stretched, but there is no identifiable catalyst for the stock to re-rate above where it already trades near its 52-week high. Cheapness, where it existed, has been arbitraged away by the ~25% trailing-year run. We would need a pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s (near the ATM forward strike of ~$72.50, and closer to the peer-median multiple) to create genuine asymmetry, or a durable turn in estimate revisions to justify a higher multiple. At current levels, the risk/reward is balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

Evergy's moat is its regulated franchise, and the trajectory is positive: the company has secured an approved large-load tariff framework in both states, supportive legislation, and existing generation headroom, giving it a structural advantage in capturing data-center load versus utilities without those frameworks in place. Peers face the same demand supercycle — AEP co-owns Evergy's transmission JVs and competes for the same regional load — but Evergy's dual-state LLPS approval and MO SB4 CWIP recovery are genuine differentiators.

The offsetting consideration, and the reason competitive position scores in the mid-6s rather than higher, is affordability politics. Local opposition and protests surfaced in Topeka in mid-2026, and the durability of the LLPS framework depends on regulators and the public accepting that large-load customers fully cover their costs. The Kansas earnings-sharing mechanism (refund 50% of ROE above 9.7%) also caps how much the load growth can translate into shareholder returns. The competitive trajectory is improving but is subject to a regulatory-social ceiling that the market may not be fully pricing.

## Risk Assessment

**Data-center demand realization.** The entire elevated capital plan is predicated on contracted load materializing on schedule. The minimum-payment obligations and 80% minimum bills substantially de-risk this, but if projects are delayed or cancelled, Evergy risks stranded generation investment or under-recovery. This is the central risk and it is asymmetric.

**Construction and financing execution.** The plan requires building multiple large gas plants (two ~705 MW combined-cycle units in Kansas, a ~440 MW simple-cycle unit in Missouri) plus solar and storage, with exposure to single-sourced power-island equipment, supply-chain delays, tariffs on imported components, and equipment ordered ahead of full regulatory approval (cancellation-penalty exposure). The financing is massive and rate-sensitive; deteriorating leverage metrics could pressure the IG rating.

**Regulatory lag and affordability.** PISA rate-increase caps constrain recovery timing, the Missouri Metro rate case (seeking +$140M) awaits a decision expected January 2027, and affordability is emerging as a political flashpoint. This is the risk most likely to surprise negatively because it is not fully quantified.

**Nuclear and environmental.** Wolf Creek is a single, aging unit with fuel and operational concentration. The coal fleet is exposed to shifting EPA rules; current deregulation is a near-term cost tailwind but adds long-term uncertainty subject to litigation.

**Litigation.** Montrose Station coal-ash (CCR) lawsuits filed in 2025 in Missouri (including a putative class action) and a nuclear-industry antitrust class action (July 2025, D. Maryland) naming Wolf Creek's operator among 28 defendants. Both are preliminary; Evergy calls the Montrose claims meritless. Neither appears material to the equity today, but both warrant monitoring.

The risk score sits at 5.5 because the central load-realization and affordability risks are genuinely binary and only partially priced, offset by the strong contractual protections and the fact that most utility-sector risks (rate cases, capex financing) are permanently priced into the group.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 23.6% is rich relative to 16.0% one-year historical vol (a 7.6pp spread), indicating the market is pricing modestly elevated uncertainty into the near term, consistent with the negative estimate revisions and the pending Q2 print. Put/call skew of 7.4% (5.6% at the 1-month tenor) shows elevated put demand, and the put/call volume ratio of 1.83 leans defensive, though the low put/call open-interest ratio of 0.26 suggests this is recent hedging rather than a large standing bearish position. The term structure is roughly flat (near 23.6% ≈ far 24.9%), with the 6-month tenor flipping to a mildly positive call skew (-4.4%), implying the market sees near-term downside hedging demand giving way to more balanced-to-constructive positioning over the longer horizon. The rise in IV from 21.1% to 23.6% alongside a +7.2% price move since early June is a mild caution: the market is not treating the new high as complacent. None of this overrides the fundamental read; it reinforces that the setup is balanced with modest near-term downside protection being bid.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$103, ~18% upside).** The data-center load ramps on schedule through 2026–2028, the capital plan converts into rate base at allowed ROEs, the Missouri Metro rate case is resolved favorably in early 2027, affordability opposition fades as regulators accept the LLPS cost-coverage structure, and estimate revisions turn positive as the load contributions show up in reported earnings. In this scenario, EVRG compounds adjusted EPS at the high end of mid-single digits, sustains 6% dividend growth, and holds a premium multiple, reaching the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear case (~$72–75, ~14–17% downside).** Near-term revisions continue to slip, a data-center project is delayed or renegotiated, affordability politics constrain the LLPS framework or the Kansas earnings-sharing band caps returns, and the higher-for-longer rate environment raises financing costs while ATM issuance dilutes per-share growth. The multiple compresses toward the peer median of 21.7x on soft EPS, and the below-median yield offers limited support. This aligns roughly with the analyst low target of $80 and, in a sharper de-rating, the ATM forward strike zone near $72.50.

**Base case (~$92, ~6% upside plus ~3.2% yield).** The most likely outcome is that the load story proceeds broadly on plan, EPS compounds in the mid-single digits, and the multiple holds near current levels. Total return of high-single-digits (price appreciation to consensus plus dividend) is a solid utility outcome but not asymmetric, which is why this is a hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale: the 12-month thesis is a defensible mid-single-digit total-return profile with a genuine, contracted structural growth driver, but the current price already reflects most of that upside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s, which would restore a peer-median or better multiple and create meaningful asymmetry (~25%+ upside to consensus).
- A durable turn in estimate revisions — two consecutive quarters of positive current-year EPS revisions confirming the load contributions are flowing to earnings faster than modeled.
- A constructive Missouri Metro rate-case decision (expected ~January 2027) at or near the requested 10.5% ROE, de-risking the recovery side of the capital plan.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cancellation or material renegotiation of one or more signed data-center ESAs, which would undercut the contracted-load thesis.
- A regulatory or legislative action that weakens the LLPS cost-coverage framework or forces cost-shift onto retail ratepayers (watch Topeka affordability developments).
- A credit-rating downgrade or evidence the equity funding plan is dilutive beyond the ~$3.3B currently modeled, materially compressing per-share growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (near-term revision confirmation), any KS/MO regulatory updates through 2026, and the Missouri Metro rate-case decision expected January 2027.

## Conclusion

Evergy has converted a data-center load thesis into contracted, take-or-pay revenue under approved dual-state tariffs, funding the capital plan with the regulatory scaffolding largely in place. The fundamentals are sound and the growth is real and durable, which is why the stock has re-rated ~25% over the trailing year. The problem for new capital is that at ~23x earnings near the 52-week high, above the 21.7x peer median, with a below-median 3.2% yield and near-term estimates drifting lower, the market has already paid for the good news. Total return from here looks like high-single-digits in the base case: attractive for a regulated utility but not asymmetric.

We rate EVRG a HOLD. The score trajectory has been stable at 6.0 across three prior reports with a consistent HOLD, and nothing in the current data warrants a change in direction; our marginally lower overall reflects the tension between an improving, well-contracted growth story and soft near-term revisions plus a full valuation. We would move constructive on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $70s or a confirmed re-acceleration in estimate revisions.
