**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Deluxe Corporation is a 110-year-old check printer executing a deliberate, debt-financed transformation into a payments and data company. The legacy Print segment (53% of FY25 revenue, including checks at 32% of total) is a high-margin cash engine in structural decline, and management's explicit strategy is to harvest that cash flow to fund faster-growing Merchant Services, Data Solutions, and B2B Payments. In Q1 2026, payments plus data revenue trajectory now rivals Print, with Print falling to 48.7% of quarterly revenue from 54.3% a year earlier. The June 18, 2026 announcement of the $625M all-cash Celero Commerce acquisition is the defining event: it moves pro forma payments/data to 57% of 2026 revenue and makes Deluxe a top-10 U.S. non-bank merchant acquirer, while re-levering the balance sheet to roughly 3.9x net.

The investment question is whether the operating improvement outruns the balance-sheet and secular risk. The operating side is genuinely improving: FY25 net income rose 55% to $82.1M, adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 20.2% (21.9% in Q1 2026), free cash flow grew 75% to $175.3M (about a 16% FCF yield on the current $1.07B market cap), and the company has beaten consensus EPS by double digits for four consecutive quarters (+16.6%, +14.8%, +14.1%, +20.0%). The risk side is equally real: total debt of roughly $1.41B at a ~7.3% weighted-average rate, with $1.27B (90% of debt) maturing in 2029 at coupons near 8%, goodwill at 49.7% of assets, and an Altman Z-score in the distress zone (driven by leverage and goodwill weighting rather than operating weakness).

We rate DLX a HOLD. The stock at $22.94 trades at roughly 10x trailing earnings and a ~16% FCF yield, sits 28% below its $32.07 52-week high, and rests 35%+ below the lowest of three sell-side targets ($31) with a 1 buy / 1 hold / 0 sell consensus. The bear case (refinancing wall, secular Print erosion, re-levering into a higher-for-longer rate environment) and the bull case (FCF-funded deleveraging, accretive Celero, structural margin gains) roughly offset. The recent estimate re-baseline is widely misread as deteriorating momentum, and the valuation floor is supported by cash generation. Neither side is clearly dominant, which is the definition of a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Deluxe operates four reportable segments:

- **Print (53.3% of FY25 revenue, ~$1.14B):** Personal and business checks (32.4% of total company revenue), business forms, accessories, and promotional products. The legacy profit engine, declining in revenue but expanding adjusted EBITDA margin to 32-33% through cost discipline.
- **Merchant Services (18.7%, ~$399M):** Card authorization and payment processing for SMBs, built largely from the 2021 First American Payment Systems acquisition ($960M).
- **Data Solutions (14.4%, ~$307M):** Data-driven marketing, especially customer-acquisition marketing for financial institutions. The fastest grower (+31.3% in FY25, +26% in Q1 2026).
- **B2B Payments (13.6%, ~$291M):** Treasury management, lockbox/remittance processing, and eChecks (Deluxe Payment Network/Exchange).

CEO Barry McCarthy (since 2018) architected the fintech pivot and has pruned non-core units — web hosting (2023), payroll/HR (2024), and the Safeguard distributor channel (early 2026) — while concentrating capital on payments and data. The stated model is to leverage Print's cash flows, customer relationships, and brand to fund organic growth elsewhere. CFO is William (Chip) Zint.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is flat-to-declining at the consolidated level, but the mix is shifting favorably.** FY22 $2,238M → FY23 $2,192M → FY24 $2,122M → FY25 $2,133M (+0.5%). The headline flatness masks a faster-decaying Print business (–5.7% in FY25, –10.0% in Q1 2026) offset by Data Solutions (+31%) and Merchant Services (+4-7%). In Q1 2026, payments plus data grew 12.5% YoY with 22.8% adjusted EBITDA growth in those segments.

**Profitability is improving through cost discipline, and the improvement is partly structural.** FY25 net income was $82.1M, up 55% YoY, helped by lower amortization, a 60% reduction in restructuring expense to $19.3M, and a $36M SG&A reduction. FY25 adjusted EBITDA was $431.5M (+4.7%) at a 20.2% margin, up from 19.4%. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin reached 21.9% (from 18.7%). Adjusted diluted EPS was $3.61 in FY25 (+9.7%) and $1.05 in Q1 2026 (+40%). The North Star cost program (completed end-2025, ~$114M total spend) is delivering durable savings, with ~$22M of additional savings flagged for 2026. Print expanded its adjusted EBITDA margin to 32-33% even as revenue fell, evidence management can manage the secular decline profitably rather than watching it erode margins.

The gap between GAAP net margin (3.9%) and adjusted EBITDA margin (20.2%) is driven by ~$122M of interest expense and ~$138M of D&A. The interest burden is the price of the leveraged-transformation model.

**Cash flow is the strongest part of the story.** FY25 operating cash flow was $270.6M (+$76.3M) and free cash flow was $175.3M, up from $100M, a 75% increase. Operating cash flow exceeds net income, a positive earnings-quality signal. Capex is steady at ~$95M, guided to $90-100M for 2026. On the current $1.07B market cap, the ~$175M FCF is roughly a 16% yield, which is the foundation of the valuation support.

**The balance sheet is the central vulnerability.** Total debt is $1.41-1.44B, net debt ~$1.37-1.39B, D/E 4.21x, and goodwill is 49.7% of total assets ($1.42B, of which Merchant Services carries $727.7M). The Altman Z-score of 1.5 sits in the "distress zone," but this is largely an artifact of high leverage on a goodwill-heavy, capital-light balance sheet rather than imminent insolvency: FCF is robust, covenants (interest coverage ≥3.0x) are met, and $380.9M of revolver capacity is available. The covenant total-leverage cap tightens to 4.00x from June 30, 2026.

The more concrete risk is the cost and concentration of debt. About 64-66% of debt is fixed at a weighted-average 8.1% (8.0% senior unsecured notes due 2029; 8.125% senior secured notes due 2029), with a total weighted-average rate of ~7.3%. The $1,274.5M (90% of debt) maturing in 2029 is the single biggest financial risk: in a higher-for-longer rate environment, refinancing that wall would be expensive. Each 100bps move adds ~$4M of interest expense on variable debt.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis depends on the payments/data segments outrunning Print erosion, and recent data supports that the race is being won at the segment level even as consolidated revenue stays flat. Data Solutions is the standout, driven by FI customer-acquisition marketing. Management flagged that FY26 will not repeat the 2025 volume-based rebate margin benefit, and Q1 Data Solutions margin already compressed 2.1 points on client/campaign mix, so the segment's profit growth will moderate even if revenue growth holds.

**Celero is the defining growth catalyst.** The $625M all-cash acquisition of a Nashville-based integrated payment processor (>$200M revenue, 28% adjusted EBITDA margin, 90% unlevered FCF conversion, 375 active partners) moves payments plus data to 57% of pro forma 2026 revenue versus 31% in 2020, and creates a combined ~$70B transaction-volume platform ranking top-10 among U.S. non-bank acquirers. Management frames it as accretive to adjusted EPS in year one with >$15M of cost synergies over 24 months. It is debt-funded via a $375M incremental Term Loan A (BofA-led syndicate) plus a revolver draw, pushing net leverage to ~3.9x at close with a stated path to <3.0x within 24 months. Expected close is Q3 2026, subject to regulatory approval.

**On estimate revisions, the data requires careful reading.** The headline cuts are large — current quarter –21.5%, next quarter –15.4%, current year –7.2%, next year –4.3%. But the 30-day-ago and 90-day-ago figures are identical (current quarter: $1.04 → $0.81 in both columns), meaning the entire revision occurred in a single 30-day window. This is a one-time re-baseline tied to management reaffirming ex-Celero guidance in early May, not a sustained 90-day deterioration trend. Treating it as ongoing momentum overstates the bearish signal. The reaffirmed ex-Celero 2026 guidance of $1.99-2.05B implies a slight revenue decline versus FY25, reflecting the Safeguard exit and Print erosion; the standalone picture is flat-to-down until Celero closes.

The company has beaten consensus by double digits for four straight quarters (Q1 2026 actual $1.05 vs. $0.88 estimate). A re-baseline of sell-side estimates for a company that has out-earned consensus every quarter is more plausibly conservative modeling of guidance than evidence the business is rolling over. The growth thesis is intact but contingent on Celero closing on schedule and the payments/data momentum continuing.

## Valuation Assessment

DLX trades at $22.94, a 10.3x trailing P/E, with a 5.1% dividend yield and roughly a 16% FCF yield. The Industrials peer set provided (UPWK 10.6x, MLKN 105.5x, LZ 97.6x, LQDT 40.8x, ROCK 18.8x; median P/E 40.8x) is not a clean comparable group; Deluxe's profile is closer to a leveraged financial-technology/business-services hybrid than to commercial printing peers. Against the peer median, DLX is conspicuously cheap, but a 10x multiple on a business with secular revenue headwinds and 4x leverage is not automatically a buy; cheap multiples on structurally challenged businesses frequently stay cheap.

The case for the multiple being too low rests on three supports. First, the ~16% FCF yield is hard to reconcile with a business that grew net income 55%, expanded margins, and is covenant-compliant — the market is pricing a meaningful probability of either secular collapse or a refinancing accident. Second, every one of the three sell-side targets sits 35-53% above the current price (low $31, mean $32.67, high $35), reflecting analysts modeling material upside, though the timing of these targets relative to the May guidance reset is not established in the available data. Third, the 5.1% dividend yield provides an absolute-return floor, and the payout (~$55-60M/yr) is well covered by FCF.

The case against rewarding the cheapness: the value is contingent on a successful 2029 refinancing and on Celero delivering accretion without integration mishap, and the business is being re-levered just as the deleveraging story was gaining credibility. A low multiple here reflects genuine balance-sheet risk rather than pure mispricing. On balance, the valuation is mildly favorable — the FCF yield, dividend support, and street-target gap argue for asymmetric upside — but not enough to overcome the offsetting risks into a BUY. We score valuation modestly above neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

Deluxe sits between a structurally declining business (checks/forms) and competitive growth markets (merchant acquiring, treasury, marketing data). Its differentiated assets are scale, a 110-year trusted brand, and deep distribution into financial institutions, which it leverages to cross-sell payments and data. The Celero deal is a bet on operating leverage in merchant acquiring — combining volumes to spread fixed processing costs and reach top-10 non-bank acquirer status, where scale economics matter most.

The competitive risk is real. Merchant acquiring is crowded and price-competitive (Fiserv, Global Payments, FIS, Stripe, Block, plus bank in-house capabilities), and Deluxe is a sub-scale entrant buying toward relevance with borrowed money. In Print, it faces another major check printer and online check sellers; in Data Solutions, data brokers and agencies. The 10-K newly emphasizes generative AI and "agentic search" reducing Deluxe's visibility in customer discovery and automation potentially reducing demand — a forward-looking competitive threat that is difficult to quantify but credible for a business dependent on FI marketing relationships. The trajectory is mixed: Data Solutions and merchant volumes are gaining, but the core Print franchise is structurally eroding and the company is competing from a position of scale disadvantage in its growth markets.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Secular decline of the core profit engine.** Checks are the primary cash generator funding the transformation. Volumes have declined since the 1990s, and FedNow, RTP, Zelle, Venmo, and rising check-fraud publicity accelerate the shift. The entire strategy is a race to grow payments/data faster than Print erodes.

2. **Leverage and refinancing concentration.** Celero pushes net leverage to ~3.9x. A higher-for-longer rate environment makes the 2029 refinancing expensive — this is the most concrete downside catalyst, though it is three years out.

3. **Goodwill impairment risk.** $1.42B goodwill (49.7% of assets); Merchant Services alone carries $727.7M. Data Solutions and Print already carry accumulated impairments. A sustained price decline, margin compression, or higher discount rates could trigger fresh non-cash impairments.

4. **Integration/M&A execution risk**, heightened by Celero, the largest deal since First American.

5. **Macro/SMB sensitivity.** Heavy SMB exposure; management has flagged soft consumer confidence and softening small-business sentiment, hurting discretionary categories such as promotional products.

6. **AI/search disruption** to customer discovery and demand, newly emphasized in the 10-K.

The Q1 2026 10-Q states there were no significant changes to risk factors since the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 29.2% versus 43.2% trailing realized volatility, so front-month options are cheap (IV in the 10th percentile of its own chain); the market is not pricing near-term event risk despite the pending Celero close. The 1-month put/call skew is –4.6% (call-heavy), and the put/call OI ratio fell from 1.09 to 0.00 over the last six weeks, both modestly constructive. The term structure is in contango (1-month 29.2% vs. 6-month 45.6%), with the longer-dated tenors carrying higher skew (+9.2% at six months), consistent with the market pricing more downside uncertainty around the Q3 2026 deal close and beyond. Short interest is elevated at 10.2% of float with 6.1 days to cover, signaling meaningful institutional bearishness, but the near-term options positioning does not confirm an imminent break lower. The cheap near-term vol favors patient option-based entry for those inclined.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$31-35):** Celero closes on schedule, proves accretive, and the payments/data mix reaches 57% of revenue, re-rating the stock toward a payments-company multiple. FCF funds deleveraging back below 3.0x within 24 months, de-risking the 2029 maturity wall. The four-quarter beat streak continues, and analysts revise targets that already sit 35-53% above the current price. The dividend and FCF yield provide downside support while the transformation compounds. This reaches the $31-35 street-target range.

**Bear case (~$15-17):** Celero is delayed or its synergies disappoint, leaving a flat-to-declining standalone business carrying ~3.9x leverage into a higher-for-longer rate environment. Print erosion accelerates, SMB softness compresses Merchant Services and promotional categories, and a goodwill impairment in Merchant Services hits reported equity. The 2029 refinancing looms at coupons near or above today's 8%, compressing net income further. The stock retests its 52-week low of $14.59, roughly 36% below the current price.

The two cases roughly offset. The bull case depends on flawless execution of a debt-funded acquisition and continued momentum; the bear case depends on the secular and refinancing risks crystallizing earlier than the FCF can deleverage. With the FCF yield and a covered dividend providing a floor, and 3.9x leverage plus a 2029 wall capping the upside until deleveraging is demonstrated, neither side has a decisive edge.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an `uncertain` rationale — the forces are genuinely two-sided rather than reflecting a confident appreciation thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment is the Q3 2026 Celero close (expected) and the accompanying updated full-year guidance, followed by Q2 and Q3 2026 earnings. The Celero regulatory approval and close is the single most important near-term checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Celero closes on schedule with updated guidance confirming year-one EPS accretion, and management reaffirms the path to <3.0x leverage within 24 months with visible progress.
- A specific price-based entry: a pullback toward the high-$17s to $19 (a ~16-20% decline from current levels) on no fundamental deterioration would push the FCF yield toward 20% and offer the asymmetric risk/reward a BUY requires.
- Evidence of debt reduction toward the <3.0x target, or an early/extended refinancing of the 2029 notes at a manageable rate, which would remove the dominant overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Celero is terminated or materially delayed past 2026, leaving flat-to-down standalone revenue with rising leverage and no offsetting catalyst.
- A goodwill impairment is taken in Merchant Services or Data Solutions, or covenant headroom narrows toward the tightening 4.00x cap.
- Payments/data segment growth decelerates below high-single digits, breaking the core thesis that growth outruns Print erosion.
- Free cash flow falls below ~$120M, threatening both the deleveraging path and dividend coverage.

## Conclusion

We rate Deluxe Corporation a HOLD. The earlier bearish read leaned heavily on falling estimate revisions as the "cleanest forward warning," but those cuts were a single discrete re-baseline in one 30-day window tied to the reaffirmed ex-Celero guidance, not a sustained deterioration trend; the four-quarter double-digit beat streak argues the sell-side is modeling conservatively rather than catching a rolling-over business. The underlying fundamentals are improving: net income +55%, FCF +75% to $175M, margin expansion to 20.2%, and payments/data +12.5%.

Those improvements are offset by real balance-sheet risk: 3.9x pro forma leverage, a $1.27B 2029 maturity wall at ~8%, secular Print decline, and goodwill at half of assets. The result is a genuinely two-sided setup. At 10x earnings, a ~16% FCF yield, and 35%+ below the lowest of three street targets with zero sell ratings, the stock is not a compelling short, and the valuation floor argues against avoiding it outright. The contingency on Celero execution and the 2029 refinancing risk prevent a confident BUY. The call is HOLD: own it for the yield and optionality on the transformation, reassess at the Celero close and updated guidance, and look to add nearer the high-$17s to $19 where the FCF yield would offer clearer asymmetry.
