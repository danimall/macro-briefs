## Executive Summary

Amphenol reported Q2 2026 net sales of $8,758.1M, up 55% reported and 30% organic, with adjusted diluted EPS of $1.35 against a $1.19 consensus — a 13.0% beat and the fourth consecutive beat in four quarters. Operating margin expanded in all three segments year over year, with Communications Solutions reaching 33.6% from 30.6%. Consensus responded: FY2027 EPS moved from $5.73 thirty days ago to $6.45 today (+12.6%), and FY2026 from $4.82 to $5.32 (+10.2%). The share price has not followed. At $163.34 the stock is where it closed on June 18, 8.5% below the June 30 high of $178.52, and below the lowest published analyst target of $170.

The arithmetic that drives the recommendation: $163.34 divided by FY2027 consensus of $6.45 is 25.3x, against implied FY2026-to-FY2027 earnings growth of 21.2% — a PEG of 1.20. Trailing GAAP P/E is 40.8x on TTM diluted EPS of roughly $4.00, but the trailing period contains only one quarter of the CommScope Connectivity and Cable Solutions business, $329.0M of discrete tax charges ($0.26 per share) and $132.0M of acquisition inventory step-up in cost of sales. Trailing multiples are the wrong lens on a business that grew reported revenue 51.7% in FY2025 and 57% in 1H 2026.

The reasons this is a sized position rather than a core one are specific. Communications Solutions is now 62% of consolidated sales and grew 42% organically in Q2 with management repeatedly citing AI-related applications; Amphenol does not disclose AI revenue, so the exposure is unquantifiable from filings and cannot be assumed to decelerate gracefully. China holds 37% of long-lived assets, up from 29% a year earlier, with the 10-K explicitly attributing the increase to AI-related capacity investment — the asset concentration is rising even as China's revenue share optically falls to 12.5% of 1H sales through denominator growth. Goodwill plus intangibles equal 51.0% of total assets and tangible book equity is negative $7,352.0M. The adjusted effective tax rate has been raised permanently to 27.0% from 24.5%, a roughly 2.5-percentage-point structural rate headwind. And roughly 90 basis points of the reported 29.8% Q2 adjusted operating margin came from an $80.0M IEEPA tariff recovery that management left inside the adjusted figure.

## Company Overview

Amphenol manufactures electrical, electronic and fiber optic connectors, interconnect systems, antennas, sensors and coaxial, high-speed and fiber optic cable, across three reportable segments run by segment presidents with substantial P&L autonomy. FY2025 end-market mix was IT datacom 36%, industrial 19%, automotive 15%, communications networks 10%, defense 9%, mobile devices 6% and commercial aerospace 5%. Distributors were approximately 19% of FY2025 sales; that channel rose sharply to 28.7% of Q2 2026 sales from 18.9% a year earlier, reflecting CommScope's building-infrastructure and communications-networks products.

The operating model matters to the thesis. Roughly 140 general managers run individual businesses, which is the mechanism by which the company absorbed eight acquisitions across 2025 and 1H 2026 without a central integration bottleneck while expanding margins. FY2025 revenue of $23,089.3M against a company-estimated $500B addressable interconnect and sensor market implies mid-single-digit share. Approximately 170,000 employees, of which about 15,000 are in the United States; roughly 79% of long-lived assets are outside the U.S.

The transformational event was the closing of the CommScope Connectivity and Cable Solutions business on January 9, 2026 — a carve-out from CommScope Holding Company (which renamed itself Vistance Networks, Inc.), not a whole-company acquisition. Total 2026 acquisition spend was $10,684.0M net of cash across three deals, with CommScope dominant.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** Q2 2026 net sales were $8,758.1M versus $5,650.3M, +55% reported, decomposing across 1H into 32% organic, 24% acquisition and 1% currency. GAAP operating margin reached 29.5% from 25.1%; adjusted operating margin 29.8% from 25.6%. All three segments expanded:

| Segment (Q2 2026) | Sales ($M) | % total | Op. margin | Q2 2025 | Organic growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Communications Solutions | 5,383.6 | 62% | 33.6% | 30.6% | +42% |
| Harsh Environment Solutions | 1,856.8 | 21% | 30.1% | 25.2% | +22% |
| Interconnect & Sensor Systems | 1,517.7 | 17% | 21.0% | 19.5% | +13% |

Management attributes expansion to operating leverage on volume and cost control, partially offset by acquisitions running below company-average margin. That last point is the important one: the acquisitions are currently a margin drag, so the expansion is organic. Adjusting out the $80.0M IEEPA recovery gives a cleaner Q2 adjusted operating margin of approximately 28.9% ($2,528.1M / $8,758.1M), still roughly 330bp of year-over-year expansion.

**Cash flow.** 1H 2026 operating cash flow was $2,678.7M versus $2,181.7M; capex $647.1M versus $485.7M; company-defined free cash flow $2,036.7M versus $1,701.7M. FCF-to-net-income conversion fell to 75.4% from 93.0%, caused by a $1,176.4M working-capital consumption versus $374.3M — receivables up $1,310.7M and inventories up $579.0M excluding acquisition and translation effects, partly offset by payables up $804.1M. The working-capital metrics argue this is mechanical rather than qualitative deterioration: DSO rose only to 69 days from 66, inventory days improved to 76 from 77, payable days rose to 69 from 60. Capex intensity fell to 3.95% of sales from 4.64%.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt was $18,811.3M at June 30 against cash and short-term investments of $5,419.1M, giving net debt of $13,392.2M. Using Q2 operating income of $2,584.6M plus D&A of $341.4M, annualized, and stripping the IEEPA benefit, EBITDA proxy is $11,384.0M and net leverage approximately 1.18x. A more conservative 1H-based normalization gives 1.28x. These are sensitivities on a single quarter of CommScope consolidation, not forecasts. Interest coverage was 12.1x in Q2 and 10.5x for 1H. Commercial paper outstanding was zero at both quarter-ends and the $3.0B revolver is undrawn.

The debt term-out has been executed well. The November 2025 issuance was $6.5B across seven tranches spanning 2027–2055; 1H 2026 added €500M 3.625% 2031, €600M 3.375% 2029 and €500M 3.875% 2034 notes, designated as net investment hedges of foreign subsidiaries — a sensible match given the offshore asset base. The 364-day term loan has been paid down from $1,534.1M drawn on January 9 to $400.0M as of the July filing date via $600.0M on May 11, $300.0M on July 2 and $234.1M on July 27. That pace of deleveraging while growing 30% organically is the single clearest execution datapoint in the filings.

**What the balance sheet does not support.** Tangible book equity is negative $7,352.0M (equity attributable to Amphenol $15,491.6M less goodwill $17,554.7M less net intangibles $5,288.9M). Goodwill plus intangibles is 51.0% of total assets. For a serial acquirer this is structural rather than distress, and the 12.1x coverage plus 1.2x leverage are the informative solvency evidence — a mechanical Altman Z-Score reading in the grey zone on the FY2025 balance sheet is largely an artifact of $11.4B of pre-funded acquisition cash sitting alongside debt raised in November 2025 for an asset that arrived in January 2026. But it does mean impairment is the primary balance-sheet tail, and there is no hard-asset cushion beneath the equity.

**Interest and tax are now structural costs.** Q2 interest expense was $213.7M versus $80.9M; 1H $421.6M versus $157.4M, against FY2026 guidance of approximately $800M net. Separately, the China tax matter has repriced the rate permanently: $100.0M accrued Q4 2025, $130.0M more in Q1 2026, the full $230.0M paid in cash in Q2 2026, plus $160.0M in Q1 2026 from reassessing rate assumptions on prior periods *not* under inquiry, plus $39.0M in Q2 for other foreign matters. 1H discrete tax items totalled $329.0M or $0.26 per diluted share, with a 1H GAAP effective rate of 32.4% versus 20.2%. Management raised the adjusted effective rate to 27.0% from 24.5%, citing the China matter and a continued shift of income to higher-tax jurisdictions. The $160.0M spillover to unchallenged periods is the more concerning detail — it indicates the company concluded its historical rate assumptions were wrong more broadly than the specific positions at issue.

**Capital returns.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.25 from $0.165 (+51.5%) effective with Q4 2025 declarations; 1H dividends paid were $614.0M, annualizing to roughly $1.23B against annualized FCF near $4.07B — covered 3.3x. Buybacks were $386.0M in 1H plus $74.0M in July, leaving only $411.1M of the 2024 authorization as of July 29. Shares outstanding nonetheless *rose* from 1,228.9M to 1,235.1M despite the repurchases, because 7.7M options were exercised. The repurchase program functions as dilution offset, not share-count reduction, and the 10-Q explicitly describes sizing it around option-exercise volume.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth of 30-32% is the number that matters, because it excludes CommScope, Andrew and Trexon entirely. The FY2025 MD&A identified IT datacom as the largest dollar contributor with net sales up $4,593.7M year over year on AI-related applications. On a pro forma basis, as if CommScope had been owned since January 1, 2025, Q2 combined sales grew 34.2% and 1H grew 36.5% — so the like-for-like combined entity is compounding in the mid-30s, not just optically inflated by the deal.

Consensus is chasing. Estimate revisions:

| Period | Current | 30d ago | 90d ago | 30d rev. | 90d rev. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 2026 | $1.44 | $1.26 | $1.26 | +13.8% | +14.3% |
| Q4 2026 | $1.47 | $1.30 | $1.30 | +12.9% | +13.6% |
| FY2026 | $5.32 | $4.82 | $4.81 | +10.2% | +10.5% |
| FY2027 | $6.45 | $5.73 | $5.63 | +12.6% | +14.6% |

Almost the entire 90-day revision occurred in the last thirty days, meaning the print did the work and the sell-side has one revision cycle behind it. FY2026 consensus of $5.32 against 1H adjusted EPS of $2.42 implies 2H of $2.90, consistent with the quarterly estimates and therefore internally coherent rather than aspirational. FY2027 of $6.45 is 21.2% above FY2026.

Externally reported Q3 revenue guidance is $9.35B at the midpoint, described as above expectations. I could not verify this against a filing or press release — the earnings release 8-K and the call transcript were not obtainable — so treat it as an unverified figure. If accurate, it implies 6.8% sequential growth off Q2.

Two growth drivers remain unpriced in consensus in my view. First, CommScope contributed $2,100.9M of revenue and $190.2M of net income over its first 173 days, a 9.1% net margin rising to roughly 15.6% adding back $137.1M of after-tax step-up and backlog charges — still about 460bp below Amphenol's 20.2% consolidated Q2 net margin. On an annualized revenue run-rate near $4.4B, closing that gap is worth roughly $200M of net income, about $0.16 per diluted share. First-period carve-out margins are a floor, not a run-rate. Second, the fiber capability acquired is strategically timed: Amphenol's historic strength was copper high-speed interconnect for short-reach in-rack connections, and as AI clusters scale beyond the rack the medium shifts toward fiber. Owning that transition rather than being displaced by it is the legible logic, though the filings state the capability addition without stating the competitive motive.

The counterweight: backlog of $8.9B at December 31, 2025 (up 46% from $6.1B) is stale, cancellable prior to shipment, and not an RPO disclosure — the company expressly does not disclose remaining performance obligations because contracts generally run one year or less. There is no updated backlog in the Q2 10-Q. Growth visibility rests on end-market demand, not on a contracted order book.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $163.34 official close on 1,232.98M shares outstanding, basic market cap is $201.4B; on the 1,289.2M reported diluted share count, $210.6B. Adding net debt of $13,392.2M gives EV of approximately $214.8B to $224.0B.

|---|---|---|
| Trailing GAAP P/E | 40.8x | TTM diluted EPS ~$4.00 |
| FY2026 consensus P/E | 30.7x | $5.32, 15 analysts |
| FY2027 consensus P/E | 25.3x | $6.45, 17 analysts |
| PEG (FY26→FY27) | 1.20 | 25.3x / 21.2% EPS growth |
| EV / annualized Q2 EBITDA (ex-IEEPA) | 18.9x basic | $214.8B / $11.38B |
| FCF yield | ~2.0% | 1H FCF ×2 / basic market cap |
| Dividend yield | 0.61% | $1.00 annualized |

The published peer set is not usable as a benchmark: Corning at 85.0x, Littelfuse at negative earnings, Vishay at 5,518x. The 85.0x "peer median" reflects trough or negative earnings across the comparable group rather than a sector multiple, and Amphenol at 25.3x FY2027 is the cheapest coherent number in the cohort. The relevant comparison is Amphenol against its own growth: 25.3x forward for a business compounding organic revenue at 30%+ with margin expansion in all three segments and rising estimates.

Two things support the mispricing claim rather than a simple cheap-multiple screen. The stock at $163.34 trades below the lowest published analyst target of $170, against a mean of $198.06 and a high of $230 — an unusual configuration that occurs when price action lags a revision cycle. And the CommScope margin gap provides a specific, quantifiable accretion path independent of AI demand growth.

The bear side of valuation is equally concrete. Free cash flow yield of 2.0% is thin, and that 1H free cash flow is understated by a working-capital build that would partially reverse only if growth decelerates — meaning the FCF yield improves precisely in the scenario that compresses the multiple. Roughly $0.04 per quarter of adjusted EPS is a non-recurring tariff recovery. The 250bp adjusted tax-rate increase is a permanent drag. And at 30.7x current-year earnings, a deceleration from 30% organic growth toward the high single digits would compress the multiple toward 20x on a lower base — the mid-May 2026 test of $119 is the empirical precedent, when the stock traded there on AI-capex concern with fundamentals materially weaker than they are now.

I score valuation 6.0 — reasonably priced against growth with an identifiable revision catalyst, but not a bargain, and with asymmetry that favors the bull case only modestly.

## Competitive Landscape

Amphenol competes against TE Connectivity, Molex, Aptiv, Corning, Luxshare, Foxconn Interconnect Technology, Rosenberger, JAE, Hirose and Sensata in a fragmented market. The trajectory is improving: 42% organic growth in Communications Solutions at a 33.6% segment operating margin is direct evidence of pricing power in high-speed interconnect, where signal integrity at 224G and 1.6T is an engineering barrier rather than a stamping commodity. Margins expanded simultaneously with share gains, which is the harder combination.

Three constraints temper the score. Amphenol supplies components into systems designed by others — it captures rising per-rack dollar content but does not own the silicon roadmap or influence deployment pace. Pricing power at the current data-rate node does not automatically transfer to the next, and competitors are investing against the same opportunity; Corning becomes a more direct competitor now that Amphenol owns fiber connectivity. And the non-AI portfolio is growing far more slowly than the headline: automotive at 15% of FY2025 sales was conspicuously absent from consolidated Q2 strength commentary, and Interconnect & Sensor Systems grew 13% organically against Communications Solutions' 42%.

The geographic decomposition also complicates the "U.S. reshoring beneficiary" reading. U.S. sales rose 76% in Q2 with a 52-percentage-point acquisition contribution and 23% organic; foreign sales rose 44% with 33% organic. The U.S. surge is largely CommScope arriving; the organic engine is disproportionately non-U.S.

## Risk Assessment

**AI single-theme concentration.** IT datacom was 36% of FY2025 sales and Communications Solutions is 62% of consolidated sales growing 42% organically. Amphenol does not disclose AI revenue as a line item, so exposure is unquantifiable from filings. The 10-K confirms no single customer exceeded 10% of sales in 2025, 2024 or 2023 — which reduces single-customer risk but says nothing about top-5 or top-10 concentration, which is not disclosed. Concentration below the 10% line should be treated as unknown, not as demonstrated diversification. A 30% organic growth rate does not glide gently to 8% if hyperscaler capex growth stalls.

**China asset concentration rising.** 37% of long-lived assets are in China, up from 29%, with the 10-K attributing the increase to AI-related product investment. China revenue fell to 12.5% of 1H sales from 16.0% — but China revenue *grew* 22.4% in dollars; the percentage fell because the denominator grew faster. Under export-control, tariff or expropriation escalation, the stranded exposure is the asset, not the revenue percentage.

**CommScope integration and impairment tail.** The preliminary allocation assigned $6,958.0M to goodwill and $3,289.0M to intangibles out of $10,735.8M of net assets — 95.4% intangible, with only $317.0M of PP&E. None of the goodwill is tax-deductible. Amortization steps to roughly $388.7M in 2027 and stays near $360-380M annually through 2031. The allocation is explicitly preliminary. CommScope is excluded from the current scope of management's ICFR assessment, as permitted — meaning a business generating 14% of Q2 revenue is not yet inside the tested control environment. Against negative tangible book equity of $7,352.0M, an impairment would hit reported equity hard.

**Distribution channel visibility.** Distributors and resellers jumped to 28.7% of Q2 sales from 18.9%, introducing a sell-in versus sell-through gap that did not previously exist at this scale. Channel inventory is now a monitorable that was not material before.

**Buyback authorization near exhaustion.** $411.1M remained as of July 29 against $460.0M deployed in seven months. A new authorization is the base case given 12.1x coverage, but its absence or downsizing would signal management's leverage discomfort.

**Newly added defense risk factor.** The FY2025 10-K added a risk factor referencing the January 2026 executive order "Prioritizing the Warfighter in Defense Contracting," under which contractors designated as underperforming could be barred from repurchases and dividends. Defense is 9% of sales. No evidence in filings that Amphenol has been designated; treat as tail risk.

**Insider activity.** Norwitt, Lampo and D'Amico executed same-day exercise-and-sell transactions on options struck near $22.00-22.55 across February, May and July 2026, totalling well over $200M gross — including 686,104 options exercised and sold at $161.57-162.27 on July 31, two days after the print. On decade-old, roughly 85%-in-the-money options this is liquidity management rather than directional selling, and Norwitt retains substantial reported holdings plus a new 464,989-option grant dated May 22. The clustering of sales into each price high is also consistent with a valuation view; the data cannot distinguish. The one unambiguous directional signal is a buy: director Robert Livingston purchased 10,000 shares on the open market at $128.508 on February 5, 2026, holding 135,720 after — a price 24% below spot.

**Option overhang.** 86,011,251 options outstanding at a $44.82 weighted-average strike, of which 61,262,658 are exercisable at $30.70. Reported diluted share count is 1,289.2M. Maximum contingent issuance of 86.0M equals 7.0% of the 1,235.1M shares issued, which corresponds to 6.5% ownership dilution if fully exercised (1,235.1 / 1,321.1 = 93.5%). Full exercise would deliver roughly $3,854M of cash to the company. 24.7M options remain unvested with $522.4M of unrecognized cost, and 41.3M shares remain available for grant, so the overhang is replenishing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 53.3% against one-year realized volatility of 44.4%, a rich 8.8-point spread, with a flat term structure (53.3% near versus 52.7% far) and an implied move of ±9.3% on the August 21 expiry. IV percentile within the chain is 42%, so the richness is relative to realized rather than to the option surface's own history. Put/call volume ratio is 0.30 and open-interest ratio 0.74 — the flow skews toward calls. Near-term put/call skew is characterized as balanced at 0.8%. Short interest is 1.5% of float with 2.0 days to cover, so there is no crowded bearish position and no squeeze mechanic.

The read: the options market prices meaningful two-sided uncertainty distributed evenly across tenors, with no concentration of downside protection buying. That is consistent with the fundamental picture — a business whose earnings direction is confirmed but whose multiple is hostage to the AI capex cycle. Rich IV argues against paying for optionality here; own the equity rather than the calls.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $230 (+40.8%).** Organic growth holds in the 20-30% range through 2027 as AI cluster deployment continues and per-rack interconnect content rises with data rates. CommScope's net margin closes toward the corporate 20% average, adding roughly $0.16 per share of accretion independent of demand growth, and 2027 amortization of $388.7M becomes a shrinking headwind against a growing base. Deleveraging continues at the 1H pace, interest expense falls below the $800M FY2026 guide in 2027, and a new buyback authorization arrives. FY2027 EPS reaches $7.00 on continued revision momentum; at 33x that is $231. This case requires no multiple expansion beyond the current FY2026 level applied to a higher base.

**Bear case — $118 (-27.8%).** Hyperscaler capex growth decelerates in 2027 and Communications Solutions organic growth falls from 42% toward high single digits. The IEEPA benefit does not recur, the 27.0% tax rate compounds, and the working-capital build reverses into a revenue decline that flatters cash flow while destroying earnings. FY2027 EPS lands near $5.90 rather than $6.45; the multiple compresses to 20x on the growth reset. $118 is roughly where the shares traded in mid-May 2026 on precisely this concern. A CommScope goodwill impairment against negative tangible equity, or an export-control action affecting the 37% China asset base, would take the bear case lower.

**Base case — $192 (+17.5%).** Organic growth moderates to the high teens through 2027 as the AI base effect builds, margins hold near 28-29% adjusted ex-tariff items, and CommScope accretion partially offsets the higher tax rate. FY2027 EPS of $6.85, roughly 6% above current consensus and consistent with the 30-day revision pace decaying, at 28x gives $192. Equivalently, 29.8x current FY2027 consensus of $6.45 gives $192 — the same result, and close to the $198 street mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $192 (+17.5% from $163.34).** 28x FY2027 EPS of $6.85, or 29.8x current FY2027 consensus of $6.45. Both routes converge at $192.

**Bull case target: $230 (+40.8%).** 33x FY2027 EPS of $7.00; matches the published street high.

**Bear case target: $118 (-27.8%).** 20x FY2027 EPS of $5.90 on an AI-capex deceleration; anchored to the mid-May 2026 trading range.

**Timeframe: 12 months.** Catalysts are the Q3 2026 print in late October 2026, which tests whether the externally reported $9.35B guide converts and whether Communications Solutions margin holds above 30% without a tariff benefit; the Q4 print and FY2027 guide in late January 2027; and the CommScope purchase-accounting finalization, which will fix the goodwill and amortization schedule.

**Positioning.** Enter in size no larger than a normal single-name weight given the AI-theme correlation with existing infrastructure exposure. The stock's own risk profile — 53% implied volatility, ±9.3% on the next monthly expiry — argues for scaling rather than a single entry. Adding toward $148-152 (roughly 23x FY2027 consensus) would materially improve the asymmetry; the last trade near $170.77 in extended hours, if it holds, argues for patience on incremental capital rather than chasing.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. **Consolidated organic growth below 15% in any quarter**, or Communications Solutions organic growth below 20%. That would mark the transition from AI acceleration to normalization and invalidate the multiple.
2. **Communications Solutions operating margin below 30%** excluding tariff recoveries, or consolidated adjusted operating margin below 27%. Either would indicate pricing pressure at the current node or CommScope drag exceeding the accretion thesis.
3. **FY2027 consensus revised down more than 5% from $6.45, or two consecutive EPS misses.** Given four straight beats averaging 10.7%, a single miss would break the pattern that underwrites the forward multiple.

Secondary monitorables that would not alone invalidate the thesis but warrant reassessment: failure to authorize a new buyback while net leverage rises above 2.0x; any CommScope goodwill or intangible impairment charge; DSO above 75 days; and an increase in China long-lived assets beyond 40% of the total.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report was declining — 7.5 in February to 6.5 in June — driven by management quality falling from 8.0 to 5.5 and risk factors from 6.5 to 5.0 as the CommScope leverage step-up, the China tax escalation and the option-monetization pattern accumulated. The Q2 print does not resolve those concerns, but it does break the trend on the dimensions that matter most. Organic growth of 30%, margin expansion in all three segments, a fourth consecutive double-digit beat, term-loan paydown from $1,534.1M to $400.0M in under seven months, and consensus FY2027 EPS up 12.6% in thirty days constitute fundamental improvement against a share price that is flat since mid-June. That is a better risk/reward at the same price, and I am raising the overall score to 6.7 from 6.5 and reaffirming BUY.

Two dimensions remain below average and set the position size. Risk factors at 5.3 reflect genuinely unpriced idiosyncratic exposure: an unquantifiable AI concentration inside a segment that is 62% of sales, 37% of long-lived assets in China and rising, negative tangible book equity beneath goodwill and intangibles equal to half the balance sheet, and CommScope operating outside the tested control environment. Valuation at 6.0 reflects 30.7x current-year earnings and a 2.0% free cash flow yield — reasonable against 30% organic growth and a PEG of 1.20, but offering only modest asymmetry, with roughly 17% to base case against 28% to bear case.

The falsifiable claim is narrow: consensus of $6.45 for FY2027 is too low, because it was set before the CommScope margin gap of roughly 460 basis points on a $4.4B revenue base has closed and because the sell-side has completed only one revision cycle since the print. A stock trading below its lowest published analyst target with estimates rising 12.6% in a month is the configuration that revision cycles resolve upward. Buy, size for the AI correlation, and add on weakness toward $150 rather than into strength.
