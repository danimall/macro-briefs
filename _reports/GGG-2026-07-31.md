**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Graco is one of the highest-margin businesses in industrial machinery — 52.5% FY2025 gross margin, 28.1% LTM operating margin, 19.7% ROE — built on ROI-justified capital equipment sold through specialized distributor networks into niche fluid- and powder-handling applications. The balance sheet is close to unlevered: $29.2M of total debt against $507.6M of cash at June 26, 2026, a 3.0x current ratio, and $1,279M of available liquidity. On quality metrics, this is a top-decile industrial.

The problem is volume. Graco has produced zero cumulative organic revenue growth since 2023. FY2025's 5.8% headline increase decomposed to 0% organic, +5% acquisitions, +1% currency; H1 FY2026 decomposed to −3% organic, +4% acquisitions, +2% currency. FY2025 segment operating earnings of $646.4M remain 5.6% below the FY2023 peak of $684.7M even after roughly $110M of acquired revenue was layered in, and Contractor's operating dollars were flat year over year ($270.1M → $270.3M) on an 8.4% sales increase. Against stated long-term targets of 10% revenue and 12% earnings growth, delivery has been a ~1.4% revenue CAGR since FY2022.

Q2 FY2026 (reported July 22) was received well — the shares rose 5.2% the next day and are up roughly 8% from the July 21 close — but the print requires unpacking. Adjusted EPS of $0.91 versus $0.81 consensus included approximately $0.04 from a same-quarter change in the non-GAAP definition (now excluding acquired-intangible amortization and acquisition costs) and roughly $0.05–0.06 from $9M of IEEPA tariff refunds recognized on receipt. Excluding the refund, adjusted operating earnings grew about 6% rather than the reported 11%, and organic revenue still declined 1%. Revenue of $590.6M missed the $608M consensus by 2.9%. The genuinely encouraging item is Contractor Americas at +3% organic, described as broad-based across protective coatings, spray foam, professional paint and home-center channels — the first clean organic inflection in the largest segment in roughly two years. At 22.7x next-year consensus EPS of $3.50 for roughly 6% EPS growth, the shares price that inflection as probable rather than possible. Fifth consecutive HOLD.

## Company Overview

Graco (Minneapolis, incorporated 1926, ~4,400 employees) designs and manufactures equipment that moves, measures, mixes, controls, dispenses and sprays fluid and powder materials, deliberately targeting applications involving high-viscosity, abrasive, corrosive or multi-component materials requiring precise ratio control. Sales run primarily through independent third-party distributors, plus home-center retailers in Contractor and some direct sales.

Effective January 1, 2025 the company reports three segments. Contractor ($1,071.9M FY2025 sales, 48% of total, 25.2% operating margin) sells paint and texture sprayers, line striping, two-component spray-foam proportioners, and now volumetric/gravimetric dispense and mixing equipment. Industrial ($996.8M, 45%, 33.6% margin) covers liquid finishing, advanced fluid dispensing, lubrication, vehicle service, and Gema/ColorService powder coating. Expansion Markets ($167.9M, 7%, 24.7% margin) includes semiconductor pumps, high-pressure oil and gas valves, environmental monitoring, and high-torque electric motor design/licensing. Geographic mix: Americas 60%, EMEA 24%, Asia Pacific 16%; roughly 48% of sales are outside the U.S. against a majority-U.S. cost base.

Management describes the moat as manufacturing and engineering capability in focused factories, co-location of production with product development, ~4% of sales reinvested in product development ($82M in 2025), and distributor depth, and explicitly states the business is not materially dependent on any single patent or license.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory.** Net sales went $2,143.5M (2022) → $2,195.6M (2023) → $2,113.3M (2024) → $2,236.6M (2025). LTM revenue computes to $2,267.2M ($2,236.6 + $1,130.7 − $1,100.1). FY2025 gross margin fell about 100bp to 52.5% as price realization failed to offset product cost inflation, including $14M of incremental tariff cost and the lower margin rates of acquired operations.

Reported FY2025 operating margin rose to 27.9% from 27.0%, but the primary driver was a $14.1M non-cash gain from reducing acquisition-related contingent consideration recognized in Q3 2025: the Corob earn-out, whose first twelve-month performance period produced no payment (liability fell from $14.647M to $1.649M). On the company's adjusted basis, operating earnings rose 5.7% to $610.7M and adjusted diluted EPS rose 6.5% to $2.95, versus reported EPS growth of 9.2% to $3.08. The disclosure is transparent; a reader working only from GAAP operating margin would misread FY2025 momentum.

**Q2 FY2026 quality.** Operating margin of 29.6% versus 27.5% and gross margin of 53.7% versus 52.4% both benefited from the $9M net tariff refund ($5M Contractor, $4M Industrial). Operating earnings rose $17.6M to $175.1M; excluding the refund, the increase is roughly $8.6M (+5.5%). Contractor's 4pp margin expansion to 30% is attributed by the company to lower operating expenses plus the $5M refund — cost control and a recovery of prior-period cost, not volume leverage. Other income also carried $5M of lower FX losses.

**Cash generation and the capex question.** Operating cash flow: $651.0M (2023), $621.7M (2024), $683.6M (2025), $298.0M (H1 2026). Capex: $184.8M → $106.7M → $45.7M → $28.5M. FY2025 FCF of $637.9M is a 28.5% margin, but capex was only 43% of D&A ($107.4M), reflecting the completion of expansion projects in the U.S., Switzerland and Romania that Item 1 calls "the culmination of a period of significant investment." Management guides FY2026 capex to ~$100M against $28.5M spent in H1.

LTM FCF computes to roughly $629.5M (LTM OCF $673.5M less LTM capex $44.0M), a 4.9% yield on the $12.9B market capitalization. Substituting management's own $100M capex plan at unchanged OCF gives roughly $574M, a 4.4% yield. FY2025 OCF also included a $55.2M working-capital release from a deliberate inventory reduction program, the third consecutive year of such releases ($55.8M in 2024, $42.7M in 2023), a finite source. H1 2026 OCF fell $10M year over year despite higher net earnings, as receivables consumed $35.1M (versus $9.4M) and incentive accruals drained $16.9M. These are sensitivities, not forecasts; single quarters of Graco cash flow should not be annualized.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $3,130.5M, total liabilities $607.8M, equity $2,522.7M at June 26, 2026. Interest-bearing debt to equity is approximately 0.01x; total liabilities to equity approximately 0.24x. Covenants (≤3.50x cash-flow leverage, ≥3.00x interest coverage) are irrelevant against roughly $743M of LTM EBITDA. The Valco Melton acquisition ($447M cash, closing expected Q3 FY2026) will consume essentially the entire net cash cushion, moving Graco to roughly net-cash-neutral with $771M of undrawn committed facilities remaining.

**Capital returns.** Buybacks totaled $423.1M in FY2025 (5.155M shares at ~$82.08) and $331.1M in H1 2026 (4.203M shares at ~$78.78), a combined 18-month average of ~$80.60 against today's $79.38 — mark-to-market breakeven, showing neither timing skill nor value destruction. Share count fell from 169.394M (Dec 2024) to 161.948M (June 2026), a 4.4% net reduction. Dividends were raised 7% in December 2025 to $0.295/quarter ($1.18 annualized, ~1.5% forward yield, ~37% of LTM GAAP EPS), the 24th consecutive annual increase. Board authorization leaves 18.79M shares available, 11.6% of outstanding. The 10-Q language softened to Graco "may make opportunistic share repurchases going forward," from the 10-K's "has made and may continue to make."

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth arithmetic is straightforward and modest. FY2026 guidance was reaffirmed at low-single-digit organic constant-currency growth and mid-single-digit including acquisitions, with Q3 sales of $580–600M explicitly excluding Valco Melton. Consensus EPS is $3.30 for the current year (+7.1% on FY2025's $3.08) and $3.50 for next year (+6.1%), with revisions of +6.4% and +3.7% over 30 days and +6.5%/+3.8% over 90 days. Revisions are positive and consistent, which matters, but they are an order of magnitude below the threshold at which revision velocity becomes a standalone signal — this is a mid-single-digit grower having estimates nudged up after a beat, not an earnings inflection.

Three sources of growth are identifiable. First, Valco Melton adds ~$145M of revenue (6.4% of LTM sales) and ~$32M of implied EBITDA (4.3% of LTM EBITDA) into the Industrial division from Q3 2026. Netting foregone interest on $447M of deployed cash against incremental acquired-intangible amortization, year-one EPS accretion is likely to be small — a rough sensitivity puts it near $0.02–0.05, though management provided no accretion guidance and the purchase accounting is prospective. Second, semiconductor demand is improving: Expansion Markets Asia Pacific grew 23% organically in Q2 on better order rates, the one end market where the AI/data-center capex cycle touches Graco, though at 7% of sales the mix effect is limited. Third, and most important, Contractor Americas turned +3% organic in Q2 across professional paint, home-center, protective coating and spray-foam categories.

Working against these: residential and non-residential construction softness has been cited in every filing through FY2025; Expansion Markets EMEA fell 24% in Q2 2026; Industrial Asia Pacific fell 20% in 2024 and was flat in 2025. Management's reported reference to a "stronger backlog" on the earnings call is not corroborated by any filed figure; Graco does not disclose backlog, and the $98.4M "customer advances and deferred revenue" balance is not a backlog measure given payment terms under one year.

## Valuation Assessment

At $79.38 (with the July 30 close at $79.99), the shares trade at:

- 25.0x LTM GAAP diluted EPS of $3.18
- 24.1x current-year consensus EPS of $3.30
- 22.7x next-year consensus EPS of $3.50
- 16.7x LTM EBITDA on EV of $12.42B ($12.9B market cap less $478M net cash); pro forma for Valco, roughly 16.6x
- 5.5x LTM revenue
- 4.9% trailing FCF yield, ~4.4% on normalized $100M capex
- 1.5% forward dividend yield

Against the peer set, the median P/E of 34.1 flatters Graco's relative position. The dispersion is wide and driven by denominators: SPXC at 44.7x, CR at 37.4x, SWK at 34.1x (on depressed earnings), LECO at 26.6x, MLI at 17.7x. Graco at 25x trailing sits close to LECO, the most comparable high-quality compounder in the group, and above MLI. There is no clean discount to argue.

The more informative comparison is multiple against delivered growth. Paying 22.7x forward for roughly 6% EPS growth — where the revenue component is majority-purchased and the per-share component is assisted by a 4.4% share-count reduction — implies a PEG above 3. Graco's historical premium multiple was earned by a growth rate it is no longer producing, and the market has already partially adjusted: FY2025 total return was −28.7% against a peer group at −4.0%, and five-year cumulative TSR is +22% versus +70% for peers. The de-rating from 30x+ to 23–25x is a rational response to flat organic volumes, and it is not obvious the process is complete.

What supports the price: the absolute FCF yield of 4.4–4.9% against near-zero leverage is genuine cash support; buyback authorization covers 11.6% of shares; a 24-year dividend growth record at a 37% payout leaves ample room; and 52%+ gross margins with ~20% ROE are not commodity economics. What is absent is an identified catalyst for re-rating. The published analyst range is unusual — low $84, mean $91.29, median $94, high $100, with every target above spot — while 8 of 11 ratings are Hold and sentiment is degrading (one Buy moved to Hold in the past month). On a stock at 23x forward earnings for 6% growth, that target range reads as stale downward-lagging estimates rather than independent confirmation of upside. I score valuation at 5.0: fairly priced for the quality, with no asymmetry identified in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

Graco's competitive economics remain strong in level terms and flat-to-slightly-negative in trajectory, which is what the score reflects.

The moat mechanisms are real and specific. Distribution is the primary one: distributors hold inventory, provide application expertise and service equipment, which is genuinely hard for low-cost entrants to replicate. In Industrial, Graco co-develops equipment with material suppliers for hard-to-handle materials, creating switching costs at the application level. Manufacturing is largely in-house — critical machining, assembly and testing in focused factories co-located with product development.

Two filed facts indicate that pricing power is being tested. FY2025 gross margin fell about 100bp because price realization was insufficient to offset product cost inflation. The IP risk factor is unusually specific: competitors copying Graco products "are prevalent in Asia, and they are increasingly offering their low-cost copies outside of Asia, including in Europe and North America." For a business whose thesis rests on differentiated niche products, low-cost copies migrating into the core Americas and EMEA markets is a direct margin threat, and Graco's stated response is product-line refresh, deeper distribution and increased enforcement rather than any structural barrier.

The acquisition program is also mix-dilutive by construction. Note 12 pro formas imply Corob contributed ~$106M of revenue at ~$3.0M of net earnings (~2.8% net margin, against Graco's 23.0%). Valco Melton brings ~$145M of revenue at ~22% EBITDA margin versus Graco's ~33%, at 14x EBITDA (about 12.8x net of the $40M present value of tax benefits) and 3.1x sales versus Graco's own 5.5x. The multiple is accretive; the margin is dilutive. Contractor's flat FY2025 operating dollars on 8.4% sales growth is the clearest evidence that the margin lift Graco expects to apply to acquired assets has not yet arrived. The counterargument is legitimate: year-one-to-three margin dilution is the normal shape of a successful industrial bolt-on, Corob closed only in November 2024, and Gema is now a core franchise built the same way.

Graco names no competitors in the 10-K and I have no independent market-share data, which is a real gap in assessing whether niche positions are holding.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration (unquantified, persistent).** The FY2025 segment note discloses that one customer in the Contractor and Industrial segments individually represented over 10% of consolidated sales in 2025, 2024 and 2023. The customer is unnamed. The Contractor risk factor adds that the segment derives significant revenue from a few large channel partners whose own businesses depend on building and remodeling. Geographic mix has shifted (Americas from 63% to 60% of sales), but that does not evidence reduced customer concentration — the disclosure language is unchanged across three years.

**Goodwill and intangibles.** Goodwill grew from $370.2M (Dec 2023) to $487.5M (Dec 2024) to $585.3M (Dec 2025); other intangibles from $126.3M to $303.9M. Combined $889.2M equals 33% of equity, and post-Valco the combined balance rises materially against ~$2.5B of equity. No impairment was recorded in the Q4 2025 test. The Corob earn-out non-payment is an early indicator that at least one recent acquisition is running below the revenue trajectory assumed at underwriting, and FY2023 included both an $8.6M contingent-consideration gain and a $7.8M impairment on a 2020 acquisition.

**Tariffs, two-sided.** Graco absorbed $14M of incremental tariff cost in FY2025 and $6M more in H1 2026, against $9M recovered following the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court invalidation of certain IEEPA tariffs. Refunds are recognized only upon receipt. The 10-K flags explicitly that recovery of previously paid tariffs is uncertain and that the government may attempt to reimpose duties under another authority. Both the remaining ~$11M of recovery and the durability of the relief are unresolved.

**Insurance asymmetry.** The 10-K states Graco does "not currently maintain specific cyber insurance coverage" and that it has increased self-insured retentions and deductibles while procuring lower coverage limits under certain policies. Accrued self-insurance retentions were only $8.0M, so reserved exposure is small, but for a manufacturer with operating-technology assets across multiple countries this is a genuine tail asymmetry.

**Governance and turnover.** Four Item 5.02 filings occurred between October 30, 2025 and June 15, 2026; the substance of the June 15 filing could not be retrieved. A new CFO (Sanjiv Gupta) received initial grants in April 2026. Executive officers averaged nearly 22 years of tenure as of December 2025, so the cadence may reflect planned succession into a long-tenured bench — I cannot confirm either reading. On compensation, CEO total pay rose 13.5% to $9.49M in a fiscal year when shareholders lost 28.7% and the peer group lost 4.0%. Disclosed performance measures are Net Sales, Incentive EPS, Divisional Net Sales and Divisional Incentive EPS, with no return-on-capital or organic-growth measure surfaced in the structured proxy extract. A metric set of sales and EPS is satisfiable through acquisitions and buybacks even with flat organic volume, which describes FY2023–FY2025 reasonably well. I have not read the full CD&A narrative and cannot rule out modifiers inside the divisional definitions; compensation actually paid did mark to market downward ($4.76M in FY2024 on $8.36M granted), so the equity component is exposed to the stock.

**Insider activity.** The only open-market purchase in the reviewed window is Director Andrea Simon's 1,240 shares at $80.53 on April 27, 2026 (~$100K), establishing a from-zero position — most plausibly satisfying ownership guidelines rather than a valuation call. No CEO or CFO open-market purchases, and no open-market sales. No 10b5-1 adoptions or modifications in Q2.

**Cyclical exposure.** Construction, automotive, electronics, aerospace, semiconductor and agricultural/construction equipment. This is permanently priced for an industrial and is not a novel risk; the relevant point is that the cycle is currently at or below trend in most named markets, with residential and non-residential construction softness cited repeatedly as pressuring the Contractor end market specifically.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 21.7% against 20.5% one-year historical volatility is fair (1.2pp spread), and IV sits at the 11th percentile within the current chain — no event premium is being priced, consistent with the earnings catalyst having passed on July 22. Term structure is flat (21.7% near, 20.3% at six months), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated. The three-month tenor prices a ±6.6% move. Put/call volume ratio of 2.50 looks defensive, but with only 37 contracts across three expirations the sample is too thin to carry information, and skew is balanced at 2.2%. Short interest of 5.2% of float and 4.8 days to cover is unremarkable. The options market is expressing no view that contradicts or confirms the fundamental picture; the cheap absolute IV is mildly favorable for anyone wanting to write covered calls against a position with limited base-case upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($100–105, +26% to +32%).** Contractor Americas' +3% organic turn broadens through H2 as protective coating, spray foam and home-center channels sustain; semiconductor order strength lifts Expansion Markets; Valco Melton integrates on Graco's normal playbook and its 22% EBITDA margin converges toward the Industrial division's 33% over three years; the remaining ~$11M of IEEPA refunds arrive and tariffs are not reimposed; capex settles at $50–70M rather than $100M, sustaining a 4.7%+ FCF yield that funds continued repurchase of an 11.6% authorization. Organic growth returns to 3–4%, FY2027 EPS reaches $3.70–3.80, and the multiple re-rates toward 28x as the market re-underwrites Graco as a compounder. That is $103–106.

**Bear case ($62, −22%).** Construction softness in residential and non-residential end markets compresses remodel and repaint activity into 2027; Contractor organic growth relapses; the Q3 guide of $580–600M excluding Valco proves to be the high end; tariff refunds stop and duties are reimposed under alternative authority, restoring a $20M+ annual cost headwind that price realization has already failed to offset; Corob and Valco underperform underwriting and a goodwill charge lands against a materially larger intangible balance; and the FCF yield compresses as capex normalizes to $100M while the inventory-release tailwind exhausts. EPS holds flat near $3.10–3.20 and the multiple compresses to 19–20x, the low end of the quality-industrial range. That is $60–64.

**Base case ($84, +5.8% from $79.38).** 24x on FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.50. The multiple assumption holds Graco near its current de-rated level rather than restoring the historical premium, which requires organic growth Graco has not delivered for three years. Total return including the 1.5% dividend is roughly 7% over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $84 (+5.8%), bull $103 (+30%), bear $62 (−22%) over twelve months. The distribution is roughly symmetric with a thin positive expected value, which is not sufficient to justify adding capital to an Industrials-sector position absent a company-specific catalyst, and no such catalyst is identifiable here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive consolidated organic revenue growth, with Contractor organic growth at +3% or better in both — this is the single variable that would justify restoring a premium multiple.
2. Evidence that acquired margins are converging: Industrial segment operating margin above 34% with Valco consolidated, or Contractor operating dollars growing faster than Contractor sales in a clean quarter (no tariff refund, no earn-out write-back).
3. Price below $68–70 (roughly 19.5–20x FY2027 consensus $3.50, a ~5.5%+ FCF yield on normalized $100M capex) with FY2026 guidance intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 guidance cut below low-single-digit organic constant-currency growth, or a Q3 result below the $580M low end of guidance excluding Valco.
2. A goodwill or intangible impairment charge, or a second contingent-consideration write-back — either would confirm that acquired revenue is not delivering underwritten returns against a growing post-Valco intangible balance.
3. FY2026 capex confirmed near $100M while operating cash flow declines year over year, taking normalized FCF yield below 4.0% and constraining the buyback that supports per-share growth.
4. Disclosure of a change in the >10% channel-partner relationship, or Contractor organic growth returning to negative territory.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 results (late October 2026) are the natural checkpoint. Three items resolve there: whether Contractor organic growth was a one-quarter artifact or a trend; the Valco Melton purchase accounting, goodwill allocation and any earn-out structure; and H2 capex against the $100M plan, which determines the normalized FCF base. The FY2026 10-K (February 2027) will disclose the annual goodwill test and any change to the customer-concentration language.

## Conclusion

Graco earns its quality reputation on margins, returns and balance-sheet strength: 28.1% LTM operating margin, ~19.7% ROE, $29.2M of debt against $507.6M of cash, and $629M of LTM free cash flow. Those attributes justify the 8.0 financial health score and are the reason the stock has de-rated rather than collapsed.

What the shares do not offer at $79.38 is asymmetry. Twenty-two point seven times next-year earnings for roughly 6% EPS growth, where the revenue component is purchased at margins below the consolidated average and the per-share component leans on a 4.4% share-count reduction that just consumed $754M of cash, is a fair price rather than a mispriced one. Strip the $9M tariff refund and the ~$0.04 from the non-GAAP redefinition and underlying adjusted operating earnings grew about 6% on organic revenue that fell 1%. Contractor Americas' +3% organic turn is the first real evidence of inflection in two years and deserves respect, but it arrives against a backdrop of continued residential and non-residential construction softness that works directly against the remodel and repaint demand that drives professional sprayer replacement.

Management's incentive structure — Net Sales and Incentive EPS, with no return-on-capital or organic-growth measure surfaced — is satisfiable by exactly the strategy Graco has run, and CEO pay rose 13.5% in a year of 28.7% shareholder loss against a peer group down 4.0%. The buyback has been executed at an $80.60 average against a $79.99 stock, neither creating nor destroying value. That combination supports a 5.0 management score.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, with the overall score easing from 5.9 to 5.7 as a 6% higher share price meets fundamentals that improved at the margin rather than materially. The classification is honestly uncertain: the deciding variable — whether organic growth returns — has one quarter of favorable evidence in one segment, contaminated by one-time items elsewhere in the print. Own it for the balance sheet and the 24-year dividend record; wait for either two clean quarters of organic growth or a price in the high $60s before adding.
