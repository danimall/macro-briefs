## Executive Summary

The Andersons is a mid-cap North American agribusiness and renewable fuels company undergoing a material earnings transformation driven by two catalysts: the July 2025 acquisition of the remaining 49.9% of its TAMH ethanol joint venture for $425M, and the ramp of Section 45Z clean fuel production tax credits from $35M in FY2025 to a guided $90–100M in FY2026. These two factors combined could add roughly $3.50–$4.00/share in pretax earnings power versus the company's FY2025 base of $2.79 EPS. Consensus FY2026 EPS has been revised upward 12.4% over the past 90 days to $4.78, with management targeting $7.00 as the company exits 2028.

The stock has appreciated 89% from its May 2025 lows of $31.03 to $72.11 currently, reflecting early recognition of this earnings inflection. At ~15.1x FY2026E EPS and approximately 5.5x forward EV/EBITDA (assuming $450M+ EBITDA in 2026), ANDE still trades at a meaningful discount to food distribution peers, whose median P/E sits at 37.6x. The discount is partly warranted given commodity exposure and policy risk, but the magnitude appears excessive relative to the visibility on near-term earnings growth. The key risk is binary dependence on 45Z tax credit eligibility: if IRS/Treasury guidance changes GREET emissions modeling or prevailing wage requirements, the credit could shrink to one-fifth of its enhanced rate, eliminating approximately $2.00/share in earnings power.

## Company Overview

The Andersons operates in two reportable segments following a January 2025 reorganization. **Agribusiness** (~75% of revenue) merchandises, stores, and transports agricultural commodities across ~180 locations, manufactures and distributes fertilizers, and operates a growing specialty ingredients business. The segment added significant Western Grain Belt and Houston port export capacity through the November 2024 Skyland Grain acquisition. **Renewables** (~25% of revenue) produces ethanol and co-products through four fully-owned plants with ~500M gallons of nameplate capacity. Despite its smaller revenue share, Renewables generated 2x the pretax income attributable to shareholders versus Agribusiness in FY2025.

The company's strategic positioning centers on vertical integration: grain origination feeds directly into ethanol production, creating a closed value chain with natural hedging characteristics. The complementary east-west grain asset footprint provides diversified origination and multiple export channels, differentiating ANDE from purely regional operators.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability.** Reported revenue has declined from $17.3B (2022) to $11.0B (2025), but this reflects lower commodity prices rather than volume deterioration. Gross profit has been resilient, ranging $684M–$745M across four years; FY2025 gross profit of $714M rose 3% YoY. Net income was $96M ($2.79/share), down from $114M ($3.32/share) in FY2024, but this includes $23.6M in noncontrolling interest distributions to Marathon Petroleum that will not recur. Adjusted EBITDA was $337M.

Renewables segment profitability is the key driver. In just five months of full TAMH ownership (August–December 2025), Renewables pretax income attributable to ANDE reached $116M, exceeding FY2024's full-year $83M. Full annualization in 2026, combined with the 45Z credit ramp and Clymers plant expansion (adding 30M gallons capacity by 2027), should drive Renewables pretax income materially higher.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet.** Operating cash flow was $177M in FY2025, down from $332M in FY2024 due to working capital timing. CapEx of $233M drove negative free cash flow of approximately -$56M, though this level reflects peak strategic investment (Clymers expansion, Houston port upgrades, mineral processing). Cash declined $464M to $98M, primarily reflecting the $425M TAMH acquisition funded from cash on hand. Leverage remains conservative at 1.8x LT debt/EBITDA against a management target of <2.5x. Total liquidity is $1.9B including unused credit facilities on a $2.05B borrowing facility. The Altman Z-Score of 4.04 is firmly in the safe zone.

**Earnings Surprise Record.** ANDE beat consensus EPS in three of the last four quarters, including a 28.5% beat in Q4 2025 ($2.04 actual vs. $1.59 estimated) and a 79% beat in Q3 2025. The single miss was Q2 2025 (-54.2%), which predated full TAMH ownership. This pattern of upside surprises supports confidence in current forward estimates.

**Estimate Revisions.** Current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised up 288% over the past 30 days (from $0.17 to $0.66), and FY2026 estimates up 12.4% over 90 days to $4.78. FY2027 consensus is $5.62. The magnitude and breadth of revisions (all upward, across all periods, from all three covering analysts) constitute a strong positive earnings momentum signal.

## Growth Analysis

ANDE's growth profile is driven by three distinct engines, each with a different timeline and risk profile:

**1. TAMH Full Ownership Annualization (2026).** Elimination of ~$57M/year in NCI distributions plus full capture of ethanol plant economics. Five months of full ownership added ~$40M in incremental pretax income. Full-year annualization should contribute an incremental $25–35M pretax in 2026 versus 2025, depending on ethanol market conditions.

**2. 45Z Clean Fuel Tax Credits (2026–2029).** From $35M in FY2025 to a guided $90–100M in FY2026, representing ~$2.60–$2.90/share pretax. Credits are extended through 2029 under current legislation. This is the single largest earnings driver and the single largest risk factor (see Risk Assessment).

**3. Organic Capacity Expansion (2026–2028).** Clymers ethanol plant expansion (+30M gallons, 2027 completion). Houston port grain elevator upgrades (Q2 2026) and soybean meal export capacity (late Q3 2026). Ulysses, KS renewable feedstock facility (Q1 2026). Skyland Grain EBITDA guided to $25–35M in 2026, up from ~$20M in 2025. Corn and wheat light processing buildout across the asset footprint.

Management's long-range EPS target of $7.00 as the company exits 2028 (upgraded from $4.30) implies roughly 13% annual EPS growth from the FY2026 base. The intermediate target of $4.30 as the company exits 2026 is essentially in line with the $4.78 consensus, suggesting the near-term guide is achievable given current visibility on 45Z credits and TAMH contribution.

## Competitive Landscape

ANDE occupies a mid-cap niche between the agricultural commodity giants (ADM, Bunge, Cargill) and regional cooperatives. Its competitive advantages include:

- **Vertical integration** from grain origination through ethanol production, creating natural hedging and margin capture across the value chain
- **Complementary east-west asset footprint** following Skyland acquisition, reducing concentration risk and enabling diversified export channels including Houston port access
- **Operational efficiency** in ethanol production, with plants consistently exceeding nameplate capacity
- **Growing specialty ingredients business** offering higher margins than commodity merchandising

Disadvantages include significantly smaller scale than the major traders, concentration in the Eastern Grain Belt (despite recent western expansion), and limited pricing power in commodity markets. The ethanol segment faces long-term structural risk from EV adoption, though the timeline remains uncertain and biofuels should retain relevance in heavy transport and aviation for decades.

## Risk Assessment

**45Z Policy Dependency (HIGH).** Eligibility requires meeting lifecycle emissions thresholds and prevailing wage/apprenticeship requirements. IRS/Treasury guidance is still evolving. Failure to qualify at the enhanced rate reduces credits to one-fifth of face value. The risk factor disclosure on 45Z was detailed and appears newly enhanced, suggesting management itself views this as a material uncertainty.

**Commodity Price and Basis Risk (MODERATE).** Corn basis directly impacts ethanol profitability. Eastern corn basis was elevated in FY2025, compressing margins. The company hedges futures but cannot fully hedge basis. Oversupplied grain markets and low commodity prices continue to challenge Agribusiness merchandising margins and farmer nutrient demand.

**Trade Policy Risk (MODERATE-HIGH).** The current tariff environment creates uncertainty for grain trade flows and basis levels. Strong ethanol export demand (record levels in 2025) and E15 year-round blending approval provide partial offsets. Elevated oil prices support ethanol blending economics.

**Capital Intensity (MODERATE).** Guided CapEx for 2026 of $200–225M remains elevated. The Clymers expansion and Houston port upgrades are strategic but consume capital during a period of reduced cash reserves post-TAMH acquisition.

**Insider Selling (LOW-MODERATE).** Former CEO/Director Patrick Bowe sold ~$3.3M in open market transactions at $70–74 in March–April 2026. No insiders have been buying. This is a modestly negative signal, partially offset by Bowe's retention of ~130K shares and the fact that the selling occurred near 52-week highs.

**EV Transition Risk (LOW, long-term).** Electric vehicle adoption could structurally reduce biofuels demand over a multi-decade horizon. Management acknowledges this risk explicitly. Timeline uncertainty and continued biofuels relevance in heavy transport mitigate near-term impact.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90–$100; +25–39%).** 45Z credits fully materialize at $90–100M in FY2026. TAMH annualization drives Renewables pretax income above $170M. Agribusiness stabilizes as Skyland integration matures ($25–35M EBITDA). Ethanol export demand remains strong amid elevated global energy prices. FY2026 EPS reaches $5.00+ and the market re-rates toward 18–20x forward earnings as confidence grows in the $7.00 exit-2028 target. Oil prices above $90 support ethanol blending economics and margin spreads.

**Bear Case ($50–$55; -24–31%).** 45Z guidance is revised down due to IRS/Treasury rule changes or emissions modeling shifts, reducing credits to $30–40M. Agribusiness continues deteriorating as global grain oversupply persists. Ethanol margins compress on rising corn basis and weak domestic gasoline demand. FY2026 EPS comes in at $3.00–$3.50, and the market applies a 15–16x multiple given loss of growth credibility. Trade disruptions impact grain export flows.

**Base Case ($82–$88; +14–22%).** 45Z credits reach $75–90M (slightly below high end of guide). TAMH annualization and Skyland contribute as expected. FY2026 EPS of $4.50–$4.80. Market applies 17–18x multiple as earnings trajectory provides visibility toward $7 target.

## Conclusion

The Andersons presents a compelling asymmetric setup driven by two quantifiable earnings catalysts (TAMH annualization + 45Z credits) that should deliver a 70%+ EPS increase from FY2025 to FY2026. The stock trades at a substantial discount to food distribution peers, and rising estimate revisions across all periods confirm positive earnings momentum. The primary risk — binary dependence on 45Z tax credit policy — is real but bounded by the 2029 legislative extension and management's early-stage realization in FY2025. Insider selling near 52-week highs warrants monitoring but does not override the fundamental case. At ~15x forward earnings with a credible path to $7.00 EPS by 2028, ANDE offers mid-teens annual return potential with defined downside parameters.
