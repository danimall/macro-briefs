**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Expand Energy is the largest independent natural gas producer in the United States by net daily production (~7.2–7.4 Bcfe/d), created in October 2024 by the all-stock merger of Chesapeake Energy and Southwestern Energy. The company carries a triple-investment-grade balance sheet (S&P/Fitch BBB-, Moody's Baa3), an unusual distinction among gas E&Ps, and was added to the S&P 500 in March 2025. It generated $1.84B of free cash flow in 2025 (7.9% FCF margin) and an exceptional Q1 2026 ($1.16B net income, $2.4B operating cash flow on Winter Storm Fern pricing), building cash to $2.2B.

The stock has fallen ~19% from its December high of $126.62 to $92.04, with the bulk of the decline tracing to a February 6, 2026 leadership shock: CEO Nick Dell'Osso was replaced "effective immediately" by Chairman Michael Wichterich as Interim CEO and simultaneously resigned from the board. The evidence points away from a performance-driven exit. The proxy shows total shareholder return of 308 versus a 193 peer benchmark, and Dell'Osso resurfaced as CEO of smaller rival Gulfport Energy within 90 days. This pattern is consistent with a board/strategic disagreement rather than operational deterioration. The fundamentals (production, FCF generation, deleveraging pace, hedge coverage) did not change.

I am upgrading EXE to BUY from HOLD. At $92, the stock trades at roughly 10x conservative current-year consensus EPS ($9.08) and ~7x trailing EPS ($7.57), below closest comp EQT (10.4x) and well under the peer median (12.3x), for the largest-scale, lowest-cost, investment-grade name in the group. The entire analyst target range (low $100, median $133, high $160) sits above the current price. Three insiders, including a brand-new CFO, made open-market purchases at or above current levels during the selloff. The combination of a non-fundamental drawdown, a constructive valuation gap on a quality leader, clean insider alignment, and a near-dated catalyst (permanent-CEO appointment) tilts the risk/reward toward BUY.

## Company Overview

Expand operates three core onshore U.S. shale plays:
- **Haynesville** (LA/TX): ~3.0–3.1 Bcf/d premium dry gas adjacent to Gulf Coast LNG demand; ~23% of proved reserves.
- **Northeast Appalachia** (Marcellus, PA): ~2.6–2.8 Bcf/d dry gas; ~42% of reserves.
- **Southwest Appalachia** (Marcellus/Utica, WV/OH): ~1.5 Bcf/d, the only liquids-bearing area; ~35% of reserves.

Total proved reserves are 25,880 Bcfe (28% undeveloped) across ~6,600 gross wells, with ~99% operated production. The company has a vertically integrated oilfield-service arm inherited from Southwestern and a 35% stake in the NG3 Haynesville gathering pipeline and carbon-capture JV with Momentum, which began delivering CO2 to ExxonMobil for sequestration in February 2026.

The strategy is a maintenance-mode, through-cycle model: low-cost basins, disciplined flat capex ($2.75–2.95B guided for 2026, 11–12 rigs), and return of capital, with leverage to LNG export, AI/data-center power demand, and industrial reshoring. From a 2021 Chapter 11 emergence to S&P 500 / triple-IG status in roughly four years, this is a rare balance-sheet turnaround.

The 2024 loss reflected trough gas prices ($2.03/Mcf realized) and merger costs. 2025 was a clean recovery: realized gas rose to $3.08/Mcf, production roughly doubled with the full-year Southwestern contribution, and the company produced $4.58B operating cash flow and $1.84B FCF. Q1 2026 was outsized: realized gas $4.92/Mcf, EPS $3.83.

The balance sheet is the standout. Long-term debt fell to $4.13B as of 3/31/26 after redeeming ~$1.3B of 2029 notes with cash in April 2026. D/E is 0.52, well under the 65% covenant. Liquidity is $5.7B ($2.2B cash + $3.5B undrawn revolver extended to 2030 and upsized in September 2025). The 2026 capital-return framework prioritizes the base dividend ($2.30/yr), ~$1B annual net debt reduction, and 75% of remaining FCF to buybacks/variable dividends. Of the $1B buyback authorization, only ~$250M had been deployed through April 2026, leaving substantial dry powder undeployed at depressed prices.

Earnings quality requires looking through GAAP. Large unrealized derivative swings drive reported volatility (e.g., a –$1.0B derivative loss in the Q1 2025 merger period), so cash earnings and FCF are the better lens than headline net income.

## Growth Analysis

This is a FCF-and-demand story, not a volume-growth story. 2026 capex is flat/maintenance-mode, so the growth thesis depends on the structural demand backdrop tightening the market and lifting realized prices, plus the company's deliberate move down the value chain into LNG.

Estimate revisions are constructive at the annual level. Current-year consensus EPS sits at $9.08, up 9.9% over 30 days and roughly flat (+0.7%) over 90 days; next-year consensus is $9.64, up 4.0% over 90 days. The near-term quarterly picture is noisier: current-quarter estimate of $1.16 is down 19.9% over 90 days though up 10.0% over the last 30, reflecting softer Henry Hub on mild weather and production discipline. The annual numbers are the right anchor for a maintenance-mode producer, and they are rising. The earnings track record is mixed (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters), so I am not assuming consistent upside to consensus, but the Q1 beat (+4.1%) and Q4 beat (+5.1%) show the operating model delivers when prices cooperate.

The LNG strategy is being actively re-engineered rather than merely announced. In April 2026, Expand executed a Henry Hub-priced SPA with Delfin FLNG for ~1.15 Mtpa (targeted 2031 start, subject to FID), replacing prior Delfin and Gunvor SPAs with cleaner HH-linked, lower-basis-risk volumes. The February 2025 hire of an ex-ExxonMobil global LNG trader (Dan Turco) as EVP–Marketing & Commercial signals a serious commercial build-out aimed at capturing downstream margin.

The qualifier on growth: the bull case is back-half-loaded and depends on LNG export and data-center power demand materializing on schedule. Near-term prices are pressured by associated-gas supply and weather. This is why Growth Prospects scores 6.5 rather than higher: the demand thesis is real and broad-based, but its timing is the binding uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

Expand sits at the favorable end of the U.S. gas E&P spectrum: largest scale, lowest-cost basins, IG balance sheet, and direct leverage to the strongest structural demand thesis in U.S. energy. Gulf Coast LNG export build-out is adjacent to Haynesville; surging AI/data-center power demand and industrial reshoring tighten the broader gas market. Management explicitly cites these as tightening through 2027.

Its edge over peers (EQT, Antero, Coterra, Range, Comstock) is the combination of scale plus IG rating plus a maintenance-mode capital program that throws off FCF rather than chasing volume. The IG balance sheet is a genuine competitive weapon (a cheap revolver, no secured debt, no near-term refinancing pressure) that lowers cost of capital relative to single-B and BB gas peers. The NG3 carbon-capture JV and "responsibly sourced gas" positioning may help differentiate Haynesville volumes for LNG buyers with emissions-conscious procurement. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving, supporting a 7.3.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Commodity price leverage** is the dominant risk. Each 10% move in gas swings quarterly gas revenue ~$306M; 2024's loss demonstrates downside sensitivity. Hedges cover >65% of 2026 gas volumes via upside-preserving collars, but 2027+ is largely open. This sector-level cyclicality is permanently priced into gas E&P multiples and is not a novel risk.

2. **PUD reserve revision.** 2025 proved-undeveloped reserves jumped from 3,842 to 7,304 Bcfe, including a +4,998 Bcfe upward revision (5,430 Bcfe from "new PUDs with improved economics in areas previously classified as proved"). This nearly doubled the PUD book in one year and commits ~$4.2B of development capex over five years. It is legitimate under SEC rules but is a judgment-driven boost that flatters PV-10 and depends on prices holding. A monitoring item, not an impairment.

3. **Leadership transition.** The Interim CEO title has persisted for months. The permanent-CEO appointment is the binary near-term catalyst: positive on resolution, negative if it reveals deeper boardroom discord.

4. **Midstream/take-away dependence** ($9.2B undiscounted GP&T commitments; reliance on third-party and the 35%-owned NG3 JV it does not control), **LNG value-chain execution** (new Delfin offtake introduces long-dated counterparty and JKM/TTF-vs-HH basis risk), and **customer concentration** (one purchaser = 11% of 2025 revenue).

5. **Tax/regulatory:** Section 382 limits NOL use and the company is now a cash taxpayer (15% CAMT); OBBBA's restored bonus depreciation reduces near-term cash taxes. Methane/regulatory backdrop is currently favorable but reversible.

Risk Factors scores 6.0: the binary leadership event is near-dated and resolvable, the PUD item is a flag rather than a fault, and the balance sheet removes the financial-distress tail that would otherwise weigh on a cyclical E&P.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 32.5% against 31.8% historical vol, fairly priced and not signaling an outsized catalyst beyond normal commodity volatility. The term structure is flat (32.5% near ≈ 30.8% far). The put/call OI ratio has risen from 1.59 to 3.44 alongside the 8.2% price decline since mid-May, with put/call skew still balanced (-2.1% near-term). This reads as defensive hedging into the leadership uncertainty and softer Henry Hub rather than directional informed bearishness: skew is not steeply negative and IV is not bid above realized. Short interest is low at 2.5% of float (2.0 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The options market is pricing event uncertainty, not conviction of decline.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$133–160)
Henry Hub firms in the back half as LNG export capacity ramps and data-center power demand accelerates. The 2027+ open hedge book becomes a tailwind. A permanent CEO is named and the strategic direction clarifies, removing the governance discount. The multiple re-rates toward the peer median and beyond on a quality premium, and FCF compounds into continued deleveraging plus accelerated buybacks of the undeployed ~$750M. Analyst median $133 (+45%) to high $160 (+74%).

### Bear Case (~$76–80, –13% to –17%)
Mild weather and robust associated-gas supply keep Henry Hub depressed through 2026; the back-half demand thesis slips. The leadership vacuum persists or worsens, and the PUD revision proves optimistic if prices fail to support the development plan. The multiple compresses toward the harshest comp (APA 8.5x) on ~$9 EPS, implying roughly $76–80. This bear case requires the highest-quality, best-capitalized name in the group to trade below its weakest comp, the less probable tail.

### Base Case (~$107, +16%)
Gas prices normalize off the Q1 storm spike but the structural demand backdrop holds. Annual estimates continue their upward drift, a permanent CEO is named within two quarters, and the multiple re-rates modestly toward EQT-plus (~11x on $9.50–9.64). FCF funds continued debt reduction and a step-up in buybacks. ~$107.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $107** (+16% from $92.04). Math: ~11.2x normalized EPS of ~$9.55, a slight premium to EQT (10.4x) justified by scale and IG rating, against a peer median of 12.3x.
- **Bull case target: $133–160** (+45% to +74%). Re-rate to/above peer median on demand acceleration and governance resolution.
- **Bear case target: $76–80** (–13% to –17%). Multiple compresses to APA-level 8.5x on a stalled back-half thesis.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, with the permanent-CEO 8-K, Q2/Q3 2026 earnings (Henry Hub realization, FCF, buyback pace), and the cadence of annual estimate revisions as the catalysts.
- **Upside/downside:** +16% to base, +45–74% to bull, –13% to –17% to bear. The asymmetry is favorable: roughly 16% to base and 45%+ to bull against a 13–17% downside that itself requires an improbable derating of the group leader.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Annual current-year consensus EPS revised down below ~$8.00 (a >12% cut from the current $9.08), indicating genuine fundamental deterioration rather than weather noise.
2. Net debt rises or the buyback authorization stalls further while FCF is generated, signaling capital-allocation breakdown or a balance-sheet problem.
3. The leadership situation deteriorates: a second senior departure, a strategic reversal, or evidence the rift extends beyond a single succession dispute.

## Conclusion

The prior HOLD hedged a constructive call the evidence supports. The drawdown is leadership-driven and non-fundamental: the departed CEO outperformed (TSR 308 vs 193) and landed at a smaller rival, the pattern of a board/strategic rift rather than operational failure. Against that transient discount, the fundamentals are intact and improving: falling net debt, $5.7B liquidity, rising annual estimates, and a maintenance-mode model throwing off FCF. The valuation gap is the thesis: the largest-scale, triple-IG U.S. gas producer trades below its closest comp and well under the peer median, with the entire analyst target range above the price and the stock below even the $100 low target. Three insiders (a brand-new CFO at $96.43, the interim CEO at $107–108, and a director at $100.66) bought in the open market during the selloff with no abnormal selling, a clean alignment signal when sentiment was worst.

The two genuine overhangs (the PUD revision and back-half demand timing) are monitoring items, not impairments, and do not offset a ~10x conservative multiple on a quality leader. The bear case requires the best-capitalized name in the group to derate below its weakest comp. I rate EXE a **BUY** with a 12-month base-case target of $107 (+16%), bull case to the analyst median/high of $133–160, and a bear case of $76–80 (–13% to –17%).

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: Q1 2026 EPS is $3.83 (not $4.83), so Q1 net income did not "nearly match" full-year 2025; and the 2025 FCF margin is ~7.9% ($1.84B/$23.3B), not 15.8% — neither changes the valuation math underpinning the call but both were stated more favorably than the sources support.
