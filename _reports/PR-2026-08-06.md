## Executive Summary

Permian Resources reported a second quarter on August 5, 2026 that improved the fundamental case in three specific ways: full-year oil guidance rose to 197–201 MBbl/d, an increase of 10.0 MBbl/d from the February initial guide; adjusted free cash flow of $751M came in above management's own prior expectation; and the redemption of $325M of legacy 9.875% notes on July 15 took total debt to roughly $2.7B from $4.2B at year-end 2024. The stock fell from $21.31 on July 31 to $19.79 on August 5, a 7.1% decline driven by crude retreating on reported Iran–Oman talks toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz rather than by anything in the print.

For an E&P, that distinction matters less than it would elsewhere. Oil price is not sentiment; it is the revenue line. Q2 revenue of $1,858.0M rose 55.1% year over year while total Boe production *fell* 2.3%, because realized oil went from $62.71 to $97.81/Bbl. Share count and debt reconcile to confirm no acquisition contributed to the step-up: Class A shares issued rose 84.5M in H1 2026, almost exactly matching the 84,378,125 Class C shares that converted to zero in the January reorganization, and total debt declined over the same period. The earnings surge is a war premium on flat-to-lower volumes.

The useful observation is that the equity already discounts most of that premium. At $19.79 the stock trades at 10.2x FY2026 consensus EPS of $1.95 and 9.5x FY2027 consensus of $2.08, against a peer median of 10.1x. Normalizing Q2 EBITDAX to $66/Bbl realized oil produces roughly $4.15B annualized and $1.8–1.9B of pre-acquisition free cash flow, or about $2.15/diluted share — an 11% yield at the current price. Q2's own $0.93 GAAP diluted EPS annualizes to a 5.3x multiple, which no market assigns to a durable earnings stream from an investment-grade producer. The market is pricing $66–75 oil, not $98. That caps the downside from a Hormuz settlement but also means the upside case requires the war premium to persist or the multiple to expand, neither of which is a company-controlled variable. I keep the rating at HOLD with a $24 base case, a fifth consecutive hold, on a modestly higher overall score than the July review because the balance sheet, guidance and cost performance all improved while the price fell.

## Company Overview

Permian Resources is a Midland-headquartered independent producer operating exclusively in the Permian Basin, with its acreage concentrated in the core of the Delaware Basin across West Texas and southeast New Mexico. Per the FY2025 10-K, 100% of estimated proved reserves at December 31, 2025 were attributable to Permian properties. The company reported approximately 535,000 net acres as of the Q2 2026 release, with more than 96% held by production.

Scale and reserve metrics: FY2025 production of 392,633 Boe/d (+14% YoY) including 181,819 Bbl/d of oil; Q2 2026 production of 376,409 Boe/d including 198,071 Bbl/d of oil, a 53% oil cut versus 46% for FY2025. Proved reserves of 1,116 MMBoe at year-end 2025, 29% proved undeveloped, with PV-10 of $9.44B at the SEC trailing deck of $66.01/Bbl WTI and $3.39/MMBtu Henry Hub, down from $10.83B a year earlier on lower prices. Dividing reserves by annualized Q2 production gives an 8.1-year reserve life.

Two mechanisms define the business. The first is unit-cost leadership: Q2 total controllable cash costs (LOE plus gathering, processing and transportation plus cash G&A) were $7.49/Boe, below the midpoint of the $7.15–$8.15 full-year guidance range even though Boe volumes fell 8.8% sequentially, which normally inflates per-unit costs. Management attributes the resilience to power and compression optimization. The second is a high-frequency acquisition program: roughly 190 separate transactions year to date in 2026 added 54,000 net leasehold acres, 20,000 net royalty acres and about 5,000 Boe/d for $1.05B, priced at approximately $13,000 per net acre and $2.5M per net 10,000-foot location, with around 330 net locations acquired plus 200 identified upside locations. Management states the weighted-average front-month WTI at execution across those deals was $72.50/Bbl, materially below the $97.81 realized in the quarter.

The January 7, 2026 reorganization created a new holding company, converted all Class C shares to Class A, and eliminated the noncontrolling interest (NCI income fell from $99.1M in H1 2025 to $6.8M in H1 2026 and $0 in Q2). PR now has a single class of common stock.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is price-driven.** FY2025 oil and gas sales of approximately $5,065M grew only 1% despite 14% production growth, because realized oil fell from $74.87 to $64.06/Bbl. In Q2 2026, sales of $1,858.0M grew 55.1% on a 2.3% year-over-year *decline* in Boe. The Q1 10-Q records WTI at $102.88 on March 30, 2026 during the Hormuz disruption.

**GAAP earnings are unusable quarter to quarter.** Q1 2026 diluted EPS of $0.05 absorbed a $369.3M non-cash mark-to-market derivative loss as the hedge book repriced against the oil spike; adjusted EBITDAX in the same quarter was $1,047.7M. Q2's $0.93 GAAP diluted EPS was flattered by a $256.6M non-cash derivative gain. The reported adjusted figure for Q2 was $0.69 against a $0.60 estimate, a 15.2% beat. Trailing GAAP EPS of $1.279 and the resulting 23.0x trailing multiple carried in the market data are artifacts of these marks plus the $270.1M convertible-note extinguishment loss booked in Q3 2025; the same data field also reports a forward P/E of 23.0, which cannot be reconciled with consensus FY2026 EPS of $1.95. Forward valuation work here must use consensus, not the reported multiple.

**Cash generation and the free cash flow definition.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $3,607.5M against $1,965.9M of drilling and development capital, giving $1,641.6M — matching the company's own adjusted free cash flow measure of $1,643.6M. For H1 2026: operating cash flow $2,320.8M, D&C capex $987.7M, cash acquisitions $534.3M, other PP&E $3.7M, asset sale proceeds $9.4M. All-in cash generation after every investment line is therefore $804.5M against $269.1M of dividends paid, or 3.0x coverage; on the company's convention, which deducts only D&C, coverage is roughly 5x. Both are comfortable. The distinction matters because the widely-quoted claim of ~10% oil growth in 2026 "with less capital than we spent in 2025" is accurate on D&C capital and excludes over $1B of separately acquired inventory and working interest. Growth here is partly purchased.

**Balance sheet is the most improved element.** Total debt fell from roughly $4.2B at year-end 2024 to $3,575M at year-end 2025, $3,025M at June 30, 2026, and approximately $2,700M after the July 15 redemption of $325M of 9.875% legacy Earthstone notes. The April 15 redemption of $550M of 8.000% 2027 notes preceded it. Management quantifies the combined annual cash interest saving at ~$75M; interest expense has already fallen from $72.8M in Q2 2025 to $59.6M in Q2 2026. Net debt of $2,893M at June 30 equals 0.5x last-quarter-annualized EBITDAX.

Two structural upgrades materially change the risk profile and are not reflected in the FY2025 10-K risk factors, which still describe a secured reserve-based facility maturing February 2028 with semiannual borrowing-base redetermination and foreclosure risk. First, investment-grade ratings from Fitch (July 2025), S&P (Q1 2026) and Moody's (April 1, 2026). Second, a $3.0B senior *unsecured* revolver entered April 30, 2026 maturing 2031, with a single financial covenant of debt-to-capitalization ≤65%. At June 30, the ratio was $3,025M ÷ ($3,025M + $12,005M) = 20.1%, roughly a third of the limit, with liquidity of about $3.1B. Borrowing-base redetermination risk in a price crash — historically the most dangerous feature of a levered shale balance sheet — no longer applies.

**Where the balance sheet is genuinely tight.** The current ratio is 0.78 and working capital was negative $365.7M at year-end 2025. Accounts payable of $1,453.6M includes drilling and completion accruals on a roughly $2B annual program, so a sub-1.0 current ratio is normal for a fast-cycle shale operator; but the position means PR depends on continuous cash generation and revolver availability to fund its payables cycle. The Altman Z-Score of 2.30 in the grey zone reflects a revenue/assets term of 0.283 calibrated on manufacturers, a retained-earnings penalty from the recently-cleared legacy accumulated deficit (retained earnings have since grown from $1,567.5M to $2,131.7M), and an equity term computed on Class A equity excluding the now-eliminated NCI. Solvency risk here is price-driven rather than structure-driven.

**Financial Health: 7.3.** Top-quartile for the peer group on leverage, covenant headroom, liquidity and cash conversion (FY2025 FCF margin 32.4%), with real offsets in negative working capital and $1.22B of firm transportation commitments carrying pay-regardless language.

## Growth Analysis

Oil volumes are the growth engine and they are working. Q2 oil production rose 3% sequentially to 198,071 Bbl/d on a 7% increase in average working interest for Q2 completions (a direct output of the ground-game acreage capture) and a more-than-50% sequential increase in high-return workover projects. Full-year oil guidance rose to 197–201 MBbl/d, up 10.0 MBbl/d from February, with H2 guided above 200 MBbl/d on less than $1B of capital. Capex was raised to $1.9–2.0B from $1.75–1.95B, attributed to higher working interest and roughly $25M for the Ward County bolt-on closed July 31. Full-year working interest is now expected above 80%. The company drilled its first four-mile laterals in Q2.

Total Boe growth is going the other way by design. Sequentially, gas fell from 703,200 Mcf/d to 552,885 Mcf/d (−21%) and NGL from 103,333 Bbl/d to 86,191 Bbl/d (−17%), taking total Boe down 8.8%. Management states it curtailed high-GOR production with Waha exposure to maximize free cash flow. The arithmetic supports the decision: Q2 natural gas sales were *negative* $120.7M, with Waha averaging −$3.14/Mcf and trading as low as −$9.52/Mcf. Forgone NGL revenue alone was roughly $36M in the quarter (17,142 Bbl/d × 91 days × $23.28), so the avoided gas losses plus processing and transport cost exceeded that. Gathering, processing and transportation expense fell from $1.59/Boe to $1.07/Boe. Part of the decline could reflect third-party processing constraints or natural decline on high-GOR wells rather than purely elective curtailment; the release does not decompose it.

Consensus growth is modest. FY2026 EPS of $1.95 and FY2027 of $2.08 imply 6.7% earnings growth. Revision detail is mixed in a way that matters: the current quarter has been marked up 12.6% in 30 days and 21.4% in 90 days on the oil spike, but next quarter is down 7.7%/5.8%, current year is flat (−0.1% over 30 days, +5.0% over 90), and next year is down 2.0% over 30 days and up only 1.4% over 90. This is not the >30% revision inflection that reliably precedes further re-rating; the aggregate signal is flat-to-slightly-negative for the periods that drive valuation. The earnings track record is three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the miss (−86.7% in Q1) attributable to the derivative mark rather than operations.

The longer-run growth question is inventory. EV of roughly $19.5B against $9.44B of proved PV-10 means about half of enterprise value rests on resource not booked as proved. The 10-K's sensitivity shows a +10% price move lifts PV-10 by $2.2B, so even at ~$73 WTI proved PV-10 would be ~$11.6B. That gap is normal for shale, where SEC rules capture roughly five years of scheduled development, but it makes the ground game's 330 net locations added year to date the mechanism by which the unproved half of value gets replenished, with an 8.1-year R/P as the clock.

**Growth Prospects: 5.8.** Real double-digit oil growth with a raised guide, offset by declining total volumes, flat consensus earnings growth into 2027, partly-purchased growth, and a short reserve life.

## Valuation Assessment

Reproducing the inputs: $19.79 × 837.288M basic shares = $16.57B market cap; on 855.2M diluted weighted-average shares, $16.92B. Adding net debt of $2.893B at June 30 gives EV of roughly $19.5B, or closer to $19.3B after the July note redemption.

| Basis | EBITDAX | EV/EBITDAX |
|---|---|---|
| Q2 2026 annualized (realized oil $97.81) | $5,374M | ~3.6x |
| Q1 2026 annualized (realized oil $70.91) | $4,191M | ~4.7x |
| Normalized at $66 realized oil (derived) | ~$4,150M | ~4.7x |

The normalization: Q2 oil volume was 18,024 MBbls. Moving realized oil from $97.81 to $66.00 removes $573.4M of quarterly revenue; severance and ad valorem at the actual Q2 rate of 7.7% returns ~$44.1M; and hedge settlements, which cost $12.44/Bbl ($224.2M) in Q2, fall to approximately zero near the ~$67–68 swap strikes. Net EBITDAX impact of roughly −$305M gives ~$1,039M quarterly, or ~$4.15B annualized — which cross-checks against Q1's actual $1,047.7M at $70.91 realized. From there: $4.15B less ~$210M run-rate cash interest, less modest cash taxes (H1 tax expense of $236.9M was $230.6M deferred), less $1.95B capex leaves $1.8–1.9B, or $2.11–$2.22 per diluted share. That is a 10.7–11.2% free cash flow yield at $19.79 before acquisition spending, and roughly 5% all-in if $1B of annual ground-game spend is deducted.

Against consensus: 10.2x FY2026 EPS of $1.95 and 9.5x FY2027 of $2.08, versus the peer median P/E of 10.1x on a group spanning gas-weighted names (EXE at 6.5x, AR at 11.1x, RRC at 10.1x) and oilier peers (APA at 8.6x, OVV at 19.2x). PR yields 3.23% ($0.64 annualized on $19.79) against a 2.1% peer median, and the dividend is covered 3.0x on an all-in H1 basis and ~3.5x on the normalized bridge.

So the multiple is in line with peers rather than below them, and the identified support for the position is absolute rather than relative: an 11% normalized free cash flow yield at $66 oil from a 0.5x-levered, investment-grade, lowest-quartile-cost Delaware operator. Capitalizing $1.85B of normalized pre-acquisition FCF at a 9% yield gives $24/share; at 8%, $27; at the current 11%, $19.6. The market is already assigning PR the higher end of the sector's normalized yield range, which is a genuine argument that the war premium is not in the price. It is also a reminder that E&P terminal-value discounts have been persistent, and the cheapness resolves only if oil holds above the mid-$70s or if the sector's required yield compresses.

Analyst positioning is uniformly constructive — 3 strong buy, 16 buy, 2 hold, with targets from $22.00 to $30.00, mean $25.05, median $25.00 — and every published target sits above the current price. On a stock at roughly 10x forward earnings with a raised guide, those targets are more likely floors than ceilings. But the targets themselves are a function of the deck each analyst carries, and the deck is the open question.

**Valuation: 5.9**, a modest improvement from the prior 5.2 review. The price fell 5.7% since July 21 while guidance was raised, $325M more of debt was retired, and Q2 free cash flow beat management's expectation. That is better risk/reward on identical fundamentals. What prevents a higher score is that the multiple is in line with peers, the out-year revisions are drifting down, and the entire discount-to-normalized-value case rests on an oil deck subject to a named settlement window.

## Competitive Landscape

PR's positioning has improved on the dimensions that matter in this specific price environment. The defining 2026 industry dynamic is the divergence between crude, priced off a war-disrupted global market, and Permian associated gas, priced off a landlocked hub where Waha averaged −$3.14/Mcf in Q2. That environment structurally favors the oiliest, lowest-GOR, lowest-cost Delaware operators. PR's oil cut rose from 46% for FY2025 to 53% in Q2, partly by choice through curtailment, and its controllable cash costs of $7.49/Boe held below guidance midpoint on an 8.8% volume decline — evidence of genuine cost control rather than volume-driven unit dilution.

The ground game is the harder-to-replicate asset. Executing 190 transactions in six months at sub-$10M average size is structurally difficult for a super-major or a multi-basin operator headquartered elsewhere; the deal sizes fall below internal approval thresholds at scale. The 10-K is candid about the reverse: PR "competes with other companies that have greater resources than us, particularly following recent consolidation within the industry," and competitors "may be able to pay more for oil and natural gas properties." Post-consolidation Permian has fewer, larger, better-capitalized bidders (ExxonMobil/Pioneer, Diamondback/Endeavor, ConocoPhillips/Marathon, Occidental/CrownRock). PR's answer is to compete where those buyers do not.

One underappreciated differentiator: the 10-K states federal-lands leasing restrictions are expected to have "no more than a minimal impact" given limited federal lease exposure, which separates PR from New Mexico Delaware peers with heavy BLM footprints.

Weakening elements: purchaser concentration moved the wrong way, with Enterprise Crude Oil at 34% of total net revenues in 2025 against 19% in 2024, while Shell Trading fell from 31% to below 10% and BP America from 11% to below 10%. Three >10% purchasers became one taking a third of revenue. Crude is fungible and Enterprise is a large investment-grade midstream counterparty, so the practical exposure is credit and short-term remarketing friction rather than demand loss — but this is concentration increasing, and the changed revenue mix should not be read as diversification.

**Competitive Position: 6.8**, unchanged from the prior review. Cost trajectory, oil mix and inventory capture are all improving; single-basin concentration, an 8.1-year R/P and worsening purchaser concentration cap it.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Hormuz is the dominant variable and it is two-sided.** Reuters reporting on August 5–6 describes an active U.S.–Iran conflict with the Strait blockaded and crude trading on headlines about Iran–Oman talks. The normalized bridge above quantifies the exposure: roughly $305M of quarterly EBITDAX, or ~$1.2B annualized, sits between $66 and $98 realized oil. The mitigant is that the equity already prices a $66–75 deck. The residual risk is multiple compression on top of earnings compression, which is how E&Ps typically de-rate.

**2. The Waha hedge roll-off is the most distinctive and least-discussed risk.** Q2 natural gas sales were negative $120.7M and H1 negative $139.2M. PR's unhedged realization was −$1.74/Mcf, a $1.40/Mcf premium to Waha; gas hedges added $2.12/Mcf and purchased-gas margin $0.66/Mcf for an all-in netback of +$0.38/Mcf. The hedge book is the load-bearing element neutralizing roughly $120M per quarter of gas losses, and the 10-K discloses derivative coverage "through 2027," with new Houston Ship Channel basis hedges beginning 2027–2028. Relief capacity is arriving on a similar clock: Kinder Morgan's GCX expansion added 570 MMcf/d in late May 2026, and Hugh Brinson plus Blackcomb are expected to add over 4 Bcf/d by late 2026, with Waha turning briefly positive in mid-June. Against that, producers reportedly expect takeaway constraints to persist into 2027. The direction is favorable; the timing is not resolved, and the hedge expiry and the capacity arrival land in the same window.

**3. Firm transportation demand charges ramp while volumes are curtailed.** Aggregate contractual obligations of $1.5B at year-end 2025 include roughly $1.22B of firm transportation, escalating from ~$28.9M in 2026 to ~$118M/year in 2029–2030 with ~$769M in the "thereafter" period. The 10-K risk factor is explicit: PR may be "obligated to pay fixed amounts on minimum volumes regardless of actual volume throughput." Curtailing gas while owing demand charges on reserved capacity is the scenario the risk factor contemplates. The steep back-end ramp and the 2027–2028 HSC hedges suggest contracted Gulf Coast egress, which would be strategically sound and would explain the $1.40/Mcf premium to Waha, but the counterparty and project are not confirmed in the filings reviewed.

**4. Development obligations and permitting.** 29% of proved reserves are PUD and subject to the five-year development rule. Produced-water disposal and induced seismicity carry concrete regulatory teeth: the Texas Railroad Commission has restricted disposal wells linked to seismic activity, and New Mexico limits injection rates near recent events. For a Delaware operator with high water cuts, this is a live cost and permitting risk.

**5. Unresolved disclosure items.** A January 21, 2026 8-K reporting an Item 5.02 officer or director change is unexplained in the evidence base. Reported PEO total compensation of $0 for FY2023 and FY2024 in the proxy pay table is likewise unexplained; the plausible reading is that founder co-CEOs took little new annual pay given legacy equity, but that is inference. Neither is treated as established here.

### Options Market Signal

The options market prices an imminent binary and positions for upside. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 57.5% against 33.8% one-year realized — a 23.6-point spread at the 84th percentile of the current chain — with clear backwardation (57.5% at 14 days versus 44.6% at 42 days and 36.6% at 161 days). The nearest expiry implies a ±9.5% move. ATM IV rose from 39.3% to 57.5% between July 21 and August 6 while the price fell 4.9%, which is consistent with the market repricing the Hormuz deal window rather than a company-specific catalyst.

Two features are notable for a fundamental holder. Skew is call-heavy at −6.7% with a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.21, meaning the crowd is positioned for an oil spike, not hedged against a settlement. Short interest is only 3.0% of float at 2.1 days to cover, so there is no bearish institutional positioning either. Rich near-term volatility argues against buying the stock outright here — the option market will pay for the uncertainty — and the call-heavy structure creates the possibility of a disorderly unwind if the settlement verifies. Existing holders can monetize the volatility by writing covered calls into the 14-day expiry.

**Risk Factors: 5.5**, unchanged. The removal of borrowing-base redetermination risk and the investment-grade ratings are genuine improvements, offset by an imminent oil binary to which earnings are roughly 2x levered, a hedge-dependent negative gas line rolling off into 2027, and demand-charge obligations ramping against curtailed volumes.

## Management Quality

Capital allocation is the strongest part of this story. The compensation plan measures Relative and Absolute TSR, All-in Rate of Return, (LOE + cash G&A)/Boe, adjusted free cash flow per share, D&C cost per foot and NAV per share — with no production-growth metric. The Q2 decision to sacrifice 8.8% of Boe volumes to protect free cash flow is exactly what that plan incentivizes. FY2025 buybacks of $73.7M were executed at a weighted-average price of roughly $11.57, about 42% below the current price, and the absence of repurchases in H1 2026 at $20 is consistent with the same discipline rather than a policy change. Acquisitions were struck at a $72.50 weighted-average front-month WTI while the quarter realized $97.81. The proxy's TSR table shows $100 invested growing to $1,015 through FY2025 against a peer group at $243. Pay levels are modest for a $16.6B company: co-CEO Hickey's FY2025 total compensation was $11.43M and the CEO-to-average-NEO ratio was 2.3x.

The offset is insider selling, and it is clustered rather than isolated. On January 5–6, 2026 at $13.63–$13.76, five insiders sold: Hickey 777,705 shares (~$10.6M), Walter 777,703 (~$10.6M), CFO Oliphint 301,741 (~$4.1M), CAO Shannon 176,659, and GC Bell 176,648. On March 3–4 at $18.28–$18.71, Hickey sold 898,423 (~$16.5M), Walter 673,425 (~$12.4M) and Bell 162,513, with smaller sales by Oliphint and Shannon. Director Quinn sold ~$25.4M in March. No open-market purchases appear in the 25 most recent transactions. Separately, the large January 7 "Grant/Award" acquisitions reported for Hickey, Walter, Quinn, Bell and Shannon are each paired with a same-date disposition of an identical number of OpCo units — the mechanical reorganization exchange, not new equity awards; a naive screen would read roughly half a billion dollars of insider grants that did not occur.

The mitigating context is real: this was the first liquidity available to founders whose net worth had been locked in illiquid OpCo units until January 7, retained stakes remain large at 9,389,405 shares each for Hickey and Walter (~$186M each, ~2.2% of the company combined), and Quinn's sales are consistent with sponsor monetization under the January 7 registration rights agreement. The Q1 10-Q disclosed no 10b5-1 plan adoptions or terminations during the quarter, weakly implying any plans predate it. I do not infer that management believes the stock is overvalued. But the pattern meets the conditions under which selling carries signal — three or more C-suite insiders inside a 30-day window, twice — and it is not offset by rapidly rising out-year estimates, which are flat to slightly lower.

**Management Quality: 6.4.** Excellent, incentive-consistent capital allocation and a top-decile long-run return record, discounted for a clustered sale pattern with no offsetting purchases and two unresolved governance disclosures.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $30 (+51.6%).** Hormuz disruption persists or escalates, keeping realized oil above $90. H2 delivers above 200 MBbl/d of oil on less than $1B of capital, with full-year working interest above 80%. Waha basis compresses toward zero as more than 4 Bcf/d of takeaway arrives by late 2026, restoring the ~$36M/quarter of curtailed NGL revenue and converting the negative gas line to positive without hedge support. Net debt falls below $2.5B and management resumes buybacks. On sustained $95 oil, EBITDAX runs near $5.4B; at 5.2x EV/EBITDAX that supports roughly $30/share, equal to 14.4x FY2027 consensus and the top of the analyst range.

**Bear case — $14 (−29.3%).** A verified Hormuz reopening removes the war premium, taking WTI toward $58–60. Realized oil falls to roughly $58; hedges partially offset but the swap strikes at $67–68 limit protection. EBITDAX compresses toward $3.5B even after a capital-program cut. The market simultaneously de-rates the group to 4.0–4.5x EV/EBITDAX, giving $13.4–$15.4/share. The negative gas line becomes visible cash drag as 2027 hedges roll off before basis relief is confirmed, and firm-transport demand charges are owed on curtailed volumes. The 0.78 current ratio and negative working capital become constraints if the revolver is drawn. The 52-week low of $11.92, set in October 2025 at a $64 realized-oil deck, is the reference point — though the balance sheet is roughly $1.5B better and oil volumes 10% higher than at that time, which is why the bear case sits above it.

**Base case — $24 (+21.3%).** Partial de-escalation leaves a residual risk premium with WTI in the mid-to-high $70s. Realized oil averages $74–78, EBITDAX runs $4.6–4.9B, and pre-acquisition free cash flow of roughly $2.2–2.4/share supports a 9–10% normalized yield. Mechanically: 11.5x FY2027 consensus EPS of $2.08 = $23.9; alternatively 5.0x EV/EBITDAX on $4.6B = $23.0B EV less $2.7B net debt over ~845M shares = $24.0. The two methods agree, and the figure sits just below the $25.05 analyst mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive twelve-month path with identifiable catalysts and a base case 21% above spot, but the dominant variable is an oil deck subject to a named settlement window, and the asymmetry of +21% to base against −29% to bear does not clear the bar for new capital in a sector where my selection record has been persistently below market.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. **Waha basis confirmation.** Two consecutive months of positive Waha spot pricing, or PR disclosing its long-haul Gulf Coast transportation counterparty and firm capacity. This would convert the negative gas line from hedge-dependent to structurally positive, restore the curtailed ~150 MMcf/d and ~17 MBbl/d of NGL, and remove the 2027 hedge-roll cliff.

## Conclusion

Permian Resources is a well-run, low-cost Delaware Basin operator that has used the 2026 oil spike for exactly the right purposes: retiring $875M of high-coupon legacy notes, earning investment-grade ratings from all three agencies, replacing a secured borrowing-base facility with a $3.0B unsecured revolver maturing 2031, buying $1.05B of inventory at a $72.50 WTI execution price while realizing $97.81, and raising full-year oil guidance by 10 MBbl/d. The compensation plan rewards cash returns per unit rather than volume growth, and the Q2 decision to curtail 8.8% of Boe volumes to protect $751M of free cash flow demonstrates the incentive working.

The valuation is defensible rather than compelling. At 10.2x FY2026 consensus and roughly an 11% normalized free cash flow yield at $66/Bbl realized oil, the market is capitalizing a post-war deck, which limits the damage from a Hormuz settlement. But the multiple is in line with the 10.1x peer median, FY2026 and FY2027 consensus have been flat to slightly lower over 30 days, and the identified upside requires either a persistent war premium or a compression in the sector's required yield. Layered on top: a natural gas line running at negative $120.7M per quarter held to break-even by a hedge book that rolls off into 2027, firm transportation demand charges ramping toward $118M/year while volumes are curtailed, purchaser concentration that nearly doubled to 34%, and clustered insider selling with no offsetting purchases.

Overall score 6.3, a modest improvement on the July review because guidance, the balance sheet and unit costs all moved forward while the price moved back 5.7%. Recommendation HOLD, with a $24 base case, $30 bull case and $14 bear case. This becomes a BUY at $16.50–17.50, or on confirmed Waha basis relief that removes the 2027 gas cliff.
