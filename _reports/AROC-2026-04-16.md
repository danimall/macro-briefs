## Executive Summary

Archrock Inc. is the largest U.S. contract natural gas compression provider by fleet horsepower, operating approximately 4,571 thousand HP at 95-96% utilization across major producing basins. The company has delivered exceptional financial results over the past three years: revenue nearly doubled from $846M (2022) to $1,490M (2025), net income grew 7.3x to $322M, and contract operations adjusted gross margin expanded from 67% to 73%. Once a compressor is deployed, incremental revenue flows largely to the bottom line, producing strong operating leverage.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) structural demand growth from record U.S. natural gas production and expanding LNG export capacity; (2) pricing power derived from industry-wide utilization above 95%; and (3) scale advantages from two major acquisitions (TOPS for $869M, NGCS for $349M) that have consolidated Archrock's market position. The stock has rallied 54% over the past year, reflecting this thesis, and now trades at ~20x trailing earnings, in line with the peer median of 20.7x. All 8 covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy with a consensus target of ~$40.

The primary risks are leverage ($2.4B debt, though at a comfortable 2.7x EBITDA), capital intensity (capex consumed 81% of operating cash flow in 2025), a CFO transition, and the stock's proximity to its 52-week high ($37.73) after a sustained rally. A $35M non-recurring tax benefit in 2025 flatters the margin expansion narrative and should be adjusted for when modeling forward profitability. Despite the reduced margin of safety from the rally, forward earnings growth (consensus $2.04 current year, $2.29 next year) and the structural demand backdrop support a BUY at current levels with a 12-month base case target of $40.

## Company Overview

Archrock operates through two segments. **Contract Operations** (~85% of revenue) involves designing, owning, installing, operating, and maintaining natural gas compressors at customer sites under service contracts with typical initial terms of 12-36 months (up to 60 months for larger units), cancelable on 30 days' notice thereafter. **Aftermarket Services** (~15% of revenue) provides maintenance, overhaul, parts sales, and reconfiguration services to customer-owned compression equipment.

Compression is essential infrastructure for natural gas production and transport. Approximately 60% of Archrock's fleet supports midstream gathering operations and 40% supports gas lift for oil production. Revenue correlates more closely with production volumes than commodity prices, providing partial insulation from oil and gas price swings. Operations are entirely U.S.-based, concentrated in the Permian/Delaware, Eagle Ford, Mid-Continent, Marcellus, Utica, and Haynesville basins.

Two transformative acquisitions reshaped the company: TOPS ($869M, September 2024) and NGCS ($349M, May 2025), together adding over 600,000 HP to the fleet. The NGCS deal included 78,000 HP of electric motor-drive compression capacity, positioning Archrock for the regulatory shift away from gas-fired units.

Revenue growth was 17% organic in both 2023 and 2024, accelerating to 29% in 2025 (partly acquisition-driven). Net margin quadrupled from 5.2% to 21.6% in three years, reflecting the operating leverage of the contract compression model. Contract operations adjusted gross margin expanded from 67% to 73% year-over-year, with revenue growth of 30% versus cost growth of only 6%.

One important adjustment: a $35M net benefit from sales and use tax audit settlements in 2025 reduced cost of sales. Excluding this non-recurring item, the margin expansion is still significant but less dramatic. Investors should model 2026 margins excluding this tailwind.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $622M in 2025, up 45% from $430M in 2024. However, the business is extremely capital-intensive: total capex was $502M (growth: $348M, maintenance: $111M), leaving free cash flow of $120M (8.0% FCF margin). Capex consumed 81% of operating cash flow.

Management guided 2026 capex at $400-445M ($250-275M growth, $125-135M maintenance). If growth spending were curtailed entirely, free cash flow would approximate $500M — a useful stress test for the downside scenario. At current levels, the company generates enough free cash flow to cover its $154M annual dividend obligation ($0.88/share × 175M shares) with some surplus.

An underappreciated cash flow source: Archrock generated $45.3M in gains on compression asset sales in 2025 (up from $17.6M in 2024), with total proceeds of $192M. This secondary market activity provides liquidity optionality and establishes a floor on fleet valuation.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt stands at $2.4B against stockholders' equity of $1.5B, producing a D/E ratio of 1.62x (improving from 1.66x). Key debt components: an $800M 6.0% senior notes due 2034 (issued January 2026), and a $1.5B revolving credit facility. The $300M 2027 Notes were retired in November 2025, eliminating near-term maturities.

Leverage relative to EBITDA is approximately 2.7x against a 5.25x covenant limit — substantial headroom. The credit facility was expanded and repriced at favorable terms (margin reduced by 25bp), reflecting improving creditworthiness.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 superficially signals distress but is a structural artifact of the capital-intensive model. PPE of $3.7B on $1.5B revenue produces a 0.34x asset turnover, which mechanically depresses the Z-Score regardless of actual financial health. The Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 on available signals (profitable, positive ROA, cash flow exceeding net income, adequate current ratio) is the better health indicator.

Cash on hand is minimal ($2M), reflecting a strategy of using cash flow for capex and relying on the credit facility for liquidity.

## Growth Analysis

Archrock's growth is driven by three vectors:

**1. Production Volume Growth.** U.S. dry natural gas production hit 107.4 Bcf/d in 2025 (record) with EIA forecasting continued growth at ~1% annually. LNG export capacity expansion (9-10% annual growth) requires compression infrastructure throughout the midstream chain. Associated gas production in the Permian creates incremental demand independent of gas prices.

**2. Pricing Power from Tight Utilization.** At 95-96% utilization, the industry has minimal spare capacity. This supports rate increases — Archrock's revenue per HP has been rising consistently, driving organic growth independent of fleet additions.

**3. Acquisition-Driven Consolidation.** The TOPS and NGCS acquisitions added 600,000+ HP and consolidated Archrock's market-leading position. Management has demonstrated ability to acquire at reasonable multiples and integrate effectively. The aftermarket services segment also grew 23% in 2025.

Consensus forward estimates project EPS of $2.04 for the current year and $2.29 for next year, representing 11% and 12% growth respectively. The 90-day revision trend has been positive (+8.6% for current year, +2.7% for next year), though the 30-day trend shows modest downticks (-1.5% and -1.9%). This slight deceleration in estimate momentum warrants monitoring but does not invalidate the growth trajectory.

The most recent quarterly earnings (Q4 2025) showed a 61.3% upside surprise ($0.67 actual vs $0.42 estimate), indicating consensus estimates may still understate the company's operating leverage.

## Competitive Landscape

Archrock's competitive advantages rest on several factors:

**Scale.** At ~4.8M available HP, Archrock is the largest U.S. contract compression provider, with purchasing power over OEMs, geographic coverage across all major basins, and the ability to cross-sell aftermarket services. Scale enables the company to serve large E&P and midstream customers requiring fleet-wide compression solutions.

**Switching costs.** Compression equipment is installed at customer sites and integrated into production operations. Replacing a compression provider requires physical removal, new installation, and operational downtime. The 30-day cancellation provision after initial terms is offset by the practical cost of switching.

**Industry consolidation.** The TOPS and NGCS acquisitions have reduced the number of significant contract compression providers. USA Compression Partners (USAC) and Kodiak Gas Services (KGS) are the primary remaining competitors. Fewer competitors supports pricing discipline.

**Electric drive capability.** The NGCS acquisition added 78,000 HP of electric motor-drive compression, positioning Archrock ahead of competitors in serving customers facing tightening emissions regulations — particularly in Colorado, where the Midstream Rule mandates GHG limits by 2030.

**Vulnerabilities.** Barriers to entry are moderate — new compression equipment can be fabricated, and private operators can compete on price in specific basins. Customer concentration is notable (top 5 = 35% of revenue). The shift to electric drive, while a long-term opportunity, requires additional capital spending. Long equipment lead times (12-18 months for new builds) create capital deployment risk.

## Risk Assessment

### Financial Risks
- **Leverage:** Annual interest expense of ~$151M consumes a meaningful portion of cash flow, with limited room for error if compression rates decline or utilization softens.
- **Capital intensity:** Maintenance capex of $125-135M/yr plus growth capex of $250-275M creates persistent capital demands. The business converts relatively little operating cash flow to free cash flow.
- **Acquisition integration:** Two major acquisitions in 13 months ($1.2B combined) carry integration risk. Transaction costs of $12.7M in 2025 alone, plus the Flowco disposition ($9.6M impairment), suggest fleet rationalization is still ongoing.

### Operational Risks
- **Customer concentration:** Top 5 customers = 35% of revenue, with contracts cancelable on 30 days' notice after initial terms.
- **CFO transition:** Doug Aron retiring by year-end 2026 during a critical post-acquisition period. His $6.0M in stock sales (March 2026) are consistent with retirement planning but reduce skin-in-the-game during the transition.
- **Tariff exposure:** Imported steel and components face tariff risk. The 10-K explicitly flags this as a concern for parts costs and new equipment fabrication.

### Macro Risks
- **Energy demand cyclicality:** A U.S. recession would reduce natural gas production volumes and compression demand. The current macro environment shows elevated recession probability (45-50%), though energy demand has historically been more resilient than broader economic activity.
- **Oil price sensitivity:** While compression revenue is more production-correlated than price-correlated, a sustained oil price decline below $70 would reduce Permian Basin activity and associated gas production, directly impacting compression demand.
- **Regulatory uncertainty:** Methane emissions rules (EPA OOOOb/OOOOc pushed to 2027, IRA methane charge delayed to 2034) create a favorable near-term window but long-term compliance costs are unknown.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility of 108.6% is extreme — nearly 4x the 1-year historical volatility of 29.3%. This is driven by the imminent April 17 expiration (1 day), where the implied move is ±14.7%. The term structure is in steep backwardation (108.6% near-term → 35.8% at 6 months), consistent with an upcoming catalyst or event-driven positioning rather than structural stress. Beyond the near-term expiry, IV normalizes to 35-40%, still elevated relative to historical vol but more consistent with current market volatility levels. The put/call OI ratio of 0.20 (heavily call-skewed) and balanced put/call skew across tenors indicate positioning for continued upside, consistent with the bullish analyst consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($42-44, +16-22%)
- Compression utilization stays above 95%, enabling continued pricing power and margin expansion
- LNG export growth and Permian associated gas production drive organic revenue growth of 8-10%
- Full integration of TOPS and NGCS yields cost synergies and fleet optimization benefits exceeding management projections
- EPS reaches $2.20-2.30 in current year, with the market re-rating the stock toward KGS-like multiples (25-30x) given the infrastructure-like revenue profile
- Electric motor-drive capability becomes a competitive differentiator as emissions regulations tighten

### Bear Case ($28-30, -17-22%)
- U.S. natural gas production growth stalls or declines due to lower commodity prices or reduced drilling activity
- Compression utilization drops below 90%, eliminating pricing power and compressing margins
- Integration of recent acquisitions proves more costly than projected, with additional impairments
- Rising interest rates increase the cost of the $1.5B floating-rate credit facility, pressuring free cash flow
- The non-recurring 2025 tax benefit distorts baseline margins, leading to negative year-over-year margin comparisons in 2026

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**

## Conclusion

Archrock occupies a strong structural position as the dominant U.S. contract compression provider in an environment of record natural gas production, expanding LNG exports, and industry-wide utilization above 95%. The financial trajectory is compelling, with the Q4 2025 earnings beat suggesting consensus estimates may still understate operating leverage.

The stock's 54% rally over the past year and proximity to its 52-week high reduce the margin of safety. Leverage, capital intensity, and a CFO transition add risk. However, the combination of structural demand drivers, pricing power from tight utilization, strategic positioning in electric motor-drive compression, and a reasonable valuation (~20x trailing, in line with peer median) support continued ownership and new positions at current levels.

BUY with a 12-month base case target of $40 (11% upside plus 2.4% dividend yield). Position sizing should reflect the capital-intensive, leveraged nature of the business — this is a mid-conviction energy infrastructure position, appropriate at 3-5% of a diversified portfolio.
