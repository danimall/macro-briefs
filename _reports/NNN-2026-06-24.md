**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NNN REIT is a $8.8B internally-managed triple-net retail REIT with one of the cleaner business models in the sector: 3,711 freestanding single-tenant properties, 98.6% leased, a 10.1-year weighted-average remaining lease term, and an entire ~$9.4B portfolio run by 85 employees. The core attraction is durability: 36 consecutive years of dividend increases (third-longest streak of any public REIT), a ~69% FFO payout ratio that retains cash for self-funded acquisitions, and a balance sheet that is 100% unsecured, investment-grade, and laddered to a sector-leading ~10.5-year weighted-average maturity. For an income investor, this is close to a textbook compounder.

The investment debate is about growth, not survival. NNN's per-share FFO has compounded at ~4.5% (2021–2025, $2.86 → $3.41), but the engine is slowing. Interest expense rose to $204M in FY25 from $184M as low-coupon legacy notes (2.50%–3.10% coupons maturing 2030–2052) roll into a 4.1–4.6% cost-of-debt environment, and acquisition spreads compress as cap rates and funding costs converge. Management has responded with discipline, throttling Q1 2026 acquisitions to 41 properties/$145M from 82/$232M a year prior, which is the correct posture but mechanically caps near-term external growth. Consensus EPS estimates have drifted down ~3% over the past 90 days, and the stock trades near its consensus mean target of $46.23 against the current $46.02.

The score trajectory (5.7 → 6.6) reflects genuine improvement in balance-sheet visibility and competitive positioning, and the data here largely confirms that. It also surfaces a thread worth more weight than the headline suggests: tenant credit is deteriorating at the margin. Impairments jumped ~4x in FY25 to $28.6M and ran $10.7M in Q1 2026 versus $1.5M a year prior; cash-basis tenants grew to 14 (4.1% of ABR) from 10 (3.4%), and cash-basis GLA nearly doubled to 6.9%. None of this has dented FFO yet, but it is the metric to watch, and it tempers the case for an upgrade. We rate NNN a HOLD on income merit, with the recommendation classified as an income hold.

## Company Overview

NNN REIT (formerly National Retail Properties), founded 1984 and headquartered in Orlando, runs a classic triple-net leasing model: tenants bear taxes, insurance, maintenance, and capex, leaving NNN with low operating overhead and a predictable, escalating rent stream. The portfolio spans all 50 states, D.C., and Puerto Rico: ~39.6M sq ft across 3,711 properties.

The portfolio composition is deliberately tilted toward e-commerce-resistant, service-oriented retail. By annualized base rent (ABR), the largest lines of trade are automotive service (18.6%), convenience stores (16.3%), restaurants (14.3%), entertainment (7.2%), and dealerships (6.6%). The strategic distinction versus peers like Realty Income and Agree Realty is NNN's lean into unrated, internally-underwritten middle-market tenants, which lets it capture higher acquisition cap rates (~7.4% in FY25) than the investment-grade-heavy strategies of competitors. That is the source of NNN's above-market yield-on-cost and also the source of its incremental credit risk.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and FFO trajectory.** Revenue grew from $773M (2022) to $926M (2025), with net income roughly flat at $390M as interest costs rose. Core FFO/share climbed every year: $3.14 (2022), $3.26 (2023), $3.32 (2024), $3.41 (2025), a ~4.5% CAGR that is the hallmark of a steady net-lease compounder. The 2025 dividend of $2.36 against $3.41 Core FFO implies a ~69% payout ratio, leaving meaningful retained cash to self-fund acquisitions.

**Margins and cash flow.** Operating margin runs 63.6%, net margin 42.1%, ROE 8.8%. Operating cash flow of $667M in 2025 covered $443M in dividends roughly 1.5x, comfortable coverage that underpins the dividend-growth streak.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue of $240.4M (+4.1% YoY), net income $94.0M, diluted EPS $0.50 versus a $0.51 estimate (a 2.2% miss, the first in four quarters after a streak of beats). Occupancy improved to 98.6% from 97.7% a year earlier. The acquisition pace slowed sharply, consistent with management's higher-rate discipline.

**Balance sheet — the standout strength.** Total debt ~$4.85B, D/E 1.13x, debt/gross assets ~42%, conservative for a net-lease REIT. The asset base is 100% unsecured and fully unencumbered, which provides covenant headroom and financing flexibility. The maturity ladder is sector-leading at ~10.5 years weighted-average, with notes spanning 2026–2052 at a 4.2% weighted-average rate. Near-term maturities are modest ($350M in 2026, $400M in 2027), liquidity is ample ($1.12B on the revolver plus ~$74M unsettled forward equity), and management has been proactive: it issued $500M 4.60% notes in July 2025, redeemed $400M 4.00% notes in November, and added a $300M term loan in December swapped to a 4.10% all-in fixed rate.

**The structural FFO headwind.** Interest expense rises as low-coupon legacy debt rolls off. This is the mechanism that will keep per-share FFO growth in the mid-single digits even with disciplined acquisitions: each refinanced 2.5–3.0% note that resets to ~4.5% is a direct drag on the spread that drives the model. The Altman Z-Score of 1.5 ("distress") is a false signal: the model penalizes the asset-heavy, debt-financed REIT structure and low asset turnover; NNN is investment-grade and fully covenant-compliant.

## Growth Analysis

NNN's growth is spread-driven: FFO/share rises when NNN deploys capital at cap rates above its cost of capital and grows organically through contractual rent escalators on a 10.1-year lease book. Both levers are working, but both are compressing.

On external growth, FY25 acquisitions of 239 properties/$931M at a 7.4% cap rate (funded partly by dispositions at a 6.4% cap rate) generated a positive recycling spread. But the Q1 2026 throttling signals that management does not see enough accretive deals at current funding costs to maintain pace. Internal growth from escalators is steady but modest: net-lease bumps are typically 1.5–2.0% annually.

The estimate-revision data confirms deceleration. Current-year consensus EPS has fallen to $2.03 from $2.09 ninety days ago (-3.0%), next-year to $2.10 from $2.12. There is no positive revision momentum here, the opposite of the >30% revision velocity that would flag an earnings acceleration. Management's prior guidance raise (2025 Core FFO to $3.34–$3.39, actual $3.41) shows credibility on delivery, but the forward trajectory is mid-single-digit FFO growth at best, with the rising-rate refinancing drag as the persistent governor. We score growth prospects a 5.0: positive and durable, but unexciting and decelerating.

NNN trades at 22.4x trailing P/E and a ~5.3% dividend yield. Against the Retail REIT peer set, this is squarely fair-to-slightly-rich on P/E (peer median 22.5x) and attractive on yield (peer median 3.9%):

On a P/FFO basis (~13.5x on $3.41 Core FFO), NNN trades at a discount to faster-growing net-lease names like Agree Realty, and the higher yield captures the market's discount for NNN's middle-market/unrated tenant mix and slower external growth. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 102.1 versus a peer group at 119.5 quantifies that discount; NNN has lagged peers in four of the last five years.

The valuation question is whether that discount is a mispricing or a fair reflection of risk. We see it as fair. The higher yield is real income support, but there is no catalyst to close the discount: growth is decelerating, estimates are drifting down, and the tenant-credit thread argues the discount is justified. The stock sits at $46.02 against a consensus mean target of $46.23 and median of $46.00, with a low/high range of $44–$50. The analyst panel is overwhelmingly neutral (11 holds, 3 strong sells, only 3 buys — 1 strong buy and 2 buys). Treating proximity to consensus targets as a ceiling is appropriate here because this is a stable-to-decelerating income name where consensus targets are unlikely to ratchet up. We score valuation a 5.0: fairly valued, with the yield providing downside support but no identifiable mispricing to reward.

## Competitive Landscape

NNN's moat is real but narrow. Its differentiation is relationship-driven sale-leaseback origination with developer/operator partners rather than competing in cap-rate auctions, which lets it source deals at wider spreads than IG-focused peers. The occupancy and lease term reflect a well-managed book, and the e-commerce-resistant tenant mix (auto service, c-stores, QSR) is structurally defensible against the secular threat that has hurt other retail formats.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving on operational metrics: occupancy rose 90bps YoY, the portfolio is fully unencumbered, and the dividend streak anchors a loyal income-investor base. But the competitive position is constrained by the cost-of-capital regime. In a higher-for-longer environment, NNN's spread advantage narrows, and faster-growing peers with cheaper equity capital (trading at higher multiples) can out-acquire it. The middle-market tenant focus that drives the yield premium is also the source of the rising impairments. We score competitive position a 6.5: a solid, defensible niche with a real origination edge, modestly below the trajectory-driven 7.5 implied by the prior report, because the rising tenant-credit stress is beginning to show the cost of the unrated-tenant strategy.

## Risk Assessment

**Tenant credit deterioration — the central, underappreciated risk.** Three independent indicators point the same direction: real estate impairments, cash-basis tenant count, and cash-basis GLA are all rising. This suggests a handful of larger-box tenants are struggling. It has not yet hit FFO, and occupancy actually improved, but it is the metric that would most likely break the thesis if it accelerates.

**Tenant concentration in discretionary/cyclical sectors.** Top-5 tenants are 17.8% of ABR: 7-Eleven (4.3%), Mister Car Wash (3.8%), Dave & Buster's (3.6%), Camping World (3.5%), Kent Distributors (2.6%). Dave & Buster's (entertainment) and Camping World (RV retail) are directly exposed to the consumer discretionary cycle. The 7.2% entertainment exposure stacks on this.

**Interest-rate/refinancing risk.** As a spread business, NNN's FFO growth depends on acquisition cap rates exceeding cost of capital. The legacy sub-3% notes refinancing at materially higher rates is a known, persistent drag. The sector-leading maturity ladder substantially de-risks the near-term timing, but the direction of the cost-of-debt resets is unfavorable.

**Geographic concentration.** Texas (18.4%) and Florida (8.7%) create Sun Belt and hurricane/climate exposure.

These are largely known and partly priced; the discount to peers reflects the market's awareness of the tenant-credit and slower-growth profile. We do not score risk higher because the impairment/cash-basis trend is accelerating and is the kind of thing that often surfaces in trailing data only after it has already pressured forward FFO. We score risk factors a 5.5, below the prior report's 7.0, because the credit-deterioration thread has strengthened and is not yet fully reflected in consensus estimates.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is modestly cautious. Near-term ATM IV of 18.6% sits just above 1-year HV of 16.7% (fair, +1.9% spread), but the term structure is in backwardation (18.6% near-term versus 15.1% at six months), which signals the market is pricing some imminent near-term uncertainty, likely around the next earnings print and dividend declaration. Put/call skew is balanced (0.8%), and the P/C OI ratio rose to 0.13 from zero, a low absolute level that does not indicate aggressive bearish positioning. Short interest is modest at 5.5% of float with 3.9 days to cover. Nothing in the options data overrides the fundamental view; the backwardation is consistent with a stable income name approaching a routine catalyst rather than a stock under acute stress.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** NNN is a fortress-balance-sheet income compounder trading at a justified-but-closeable discount. The yield is well-covered (1.5x OCF coverage, ~69% FFO payout), the dividend-growth streak is a genuine differentiator, and the debt ladder de-risks refinancing better than nearly any peer. If rates ease in H2 2026, NNN's cost of capital improves, acquisition spreads widen, and the TSR discount to peers compresses. The impairment trend resolves as a normal late-cycle blip, occupancy holds near current levels, and FFO/share reaccelerates toward 5%. In that scenario the stock re-rates toward $50+ on multiple expansion plus the growing dividend.

**Bear case.** The consumer cliff arrives, and NNN's discretionary/cyclical tenant exposure (Dave & Buster's, Camping World, entertainment) drives the impairment and cash-basis trend higher. Cash-basis ABR climbs from 4.1% toward 6–7%, occupancy slips below 98%, and the unrated middle-market strategy that powered the yield premium reveals its credit cost. Simultaneously, higher-for-longer rates keep refinancing the legacy sub-3% notes upward, compressing FFO growth toward flat. The stock derates toward the low end of its 52-week range ($38.90) on yield expansion, a ~15% decline.

The base case sits between: mid-single-digit FFO growth, a stable and growing dividend, contained-but-elevated impairments, and a stock that earns its yield plus low-single-digit price appreciation, for a total return in the high-single digits, market-neutral-to-modestly-positive.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as an income hold; the thesis rests on yield and dividend durability, not capital appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A decisive easing of the cost-of-capital regime (Fed pivot or 10Y back below ~4%) that visibly widens acquisition spreads and reaccelerates FFO/share guidance above 5%.
- Stabilization or reversal of the tenant-credit trend: impairments returning toward the FY24 ~$6.6M run-rate and cash-basis ABR holding below 4%.
- A pullback toward the high-$30s to low-$40s that pushes the yield above ~5.75% with fundamentals intact, restoring asymmetric income-plus-upside.
- Clustered open-market insider buying (absent in the current Form 4 data, which shows only routine post-vest sales).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cash-basis ABR accelerating above ~6%, or a top-5 tenant (Dave & Buster's, Camping World) entering bankruptcy or major restructuring.
- A dividend-growth-streak break or a payout ratio rising above ~80% of FFO.
- Occupancy falling below ~97.5% alongside continued impairment escalation.
- Two or more consecutive quarters of FFO/share declines as refinancing drag overwhelms acquisition spreads.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July 2026) for the impairment/cash-basis trend and FFO trajectory; the June 23, 2026 8-K (new material financing agreement) is worth monitoring for terms. The H2 2026 rate path and consumer data are the macro variables that most directly move the thesis.

## Conclusion

NNN REIT is a high-quality, conservatively-financed net-lease compounder with a best-in-class debt maturity ladder, a well-covered yield, and a dividend-growth streak that anchors a durable income story. The score trajectory (5.7 → 6.6) correctly captured improving balance-sheet and competitive visibility, but the current data argues for stabilizing rather than extending that climb. Growth is decelerating as legacy low-coupon debt refinances at higher rates and acquisition spreads compress; consensus estimates have drifted down; and the tenant-credit thread is the metric most likely to break the thesis if the consumer weakens.

The stock is fairly valued near its consensus mean target, with the yield providing downside support but no identifiable catalyst to close the peer TSR discount. Our experience is that "cheap-looking" REITs without an independent re-rating catalyst tend to stay cheap, and NNN's discount appears justified rather than mispriced. We rate NNN a HOLD for income investors who value the dividend durability and balance-sheet quality, with an overall score of 5.8. Total returns should approximate the yield plus low-single-digit appreciation: adequate for income, insufficient for a capital-gains thesis. We would turn more constructive on a meaningful pullback that lifts the yield above ~5.75%, or on evidence the tenant-credit trend has stabilized.
