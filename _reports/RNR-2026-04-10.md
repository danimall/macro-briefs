## Executive Summary

RenaissanceRe is a Bermuda-domiciled global reinsurer with $53.8 billion in total assets, writing $11.7 billion in gross premiums across Property and Casualty & Specialty segments. The company compounded tangible book value per share by 27% in 2025, its third consecutive year of exceptional capital generation after a $1.1 billion loss year in 2022 (Hurricane Ian). At $303 per share, RNR trades at approximately 5.5x trailing earnings and 1.25x book value per share ($247), one of the cheapest quality reinsurers in the market despite delivering a 25.9% ROE.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) a structural earnings floor from $1.7 billion in net investment income that persists regardless of catastrophe outcomes, (2) a capital-light fee income stream ($329 million) from managed reinsurance vehicles that competitors have failed to replicate, and (3) aggressive capital return via $1.6 billion in share repurchases in 2025 alone (12.4% of shares retired). The critical risk is Casualty & Specialty reserve adequacy, where the segment's 104.4% combined ratio and steady upward revisions to 2017–2019 accident year reserves signal social inflation pressure. Management is actively de-risking this exposure, reducing general casualty premiums by 5.9% and increasing retrocession coverage by 22.7%. The valuation provides meaningful cushion against this tail risk.

The reinsurance pricing cycle is transitioning from hard to stable, with competitive pressures beginning to emerge. The current rate environment remains profitable, and RNR's superior risk selection through proprietary catastrophe modeling provides a compounding advantage that widens over time. With estimate revisions running positive across all time horizons (next-year EPS up 18% over 90 days), the fundamental trajectory supports accumulation at current levels.

## Company Overview

RenaissanceRe was founded in 1993 and has built one of the most respected franchises in global reinsurance. The company operates through two segments:

**Property Segment** ($4.9B gross premiums written, 61.4% combined ratio in 2025): Includes catastrophe reinsurance and other property lines. This is RNR's legacy franchise, where its proprietary catastrophe modeling platform, operated by the dedicated RenaissanceRe Risk Sciences team, provides a durable pricing and selection edge. The segment absorbed $1.1 billion from the California Wildfires and still generated substantial underwriting profit.

**Casualty & Specialty Segment** ($6.8B gross premiums written, 104.4% combined ratio in 2025): General casualty, professional liability, credit, and specialty lines. This segment expanded materially through the November 2023 Validus acquisition, which roughly doubled RNR's total premium base. The segment is currently unprofitable on an underwriting basis.

**Third-Party Capital Platform**: RNR manages approximately $10 billion in capital across DaVinci, Fontana, Medici, Vermeer, and other vehicles. These generate $329 million in fee income. The March 2025 launch of Medici UCITS ($342 million initial capital) extends this platform into European institutional markets.

Three brokers — Aon, Marsh & McLennan, and Arthur J. Gallagher — account for 81.3% of gross premiums, a significant concentration.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Power

RNR's three-driver profit model generated $2.6 billion in net income in 2025, translating to $56.03 in diluted EPS:

| Component | 2025 | 2024 |

| Underwriting Income | $1.27B | $1.62B |
| Fee Income | $329M | $370M |
| Net Investment Income | $1.70B | $1.68B |

| Tax & Other | ($1.87B) | ($1.76B) |
| **Net Income** | **$2.60B** | **$1.80B** |

The $1.70 billion in net investment income represents a structural floor: this income streams from the float generated by $22.3 billion in claim reserves, invested in an AA-rated fixed income portfolio yielding 4.2% with 2.8-year duration. At current rate levels, and with the Fed likely to hold or even raise rates, this income source is highly durable.

### Balance Sheet

Shareholders' equity stands at $11.6 billion, up $1.0 billion year-over-year despite returning $1.8 billion to shareholders (buybacks + dividends). Debt totals $2.33 billion (debt-to-equity of 0.20x), conservative for a reinsurer. Operating cash flow was $3.7 billion, providing ample coverage of all capital allocation priorities.

Book value per share grew from $195.77 to $247.00 (26.2%), a continuation of the 33% CAGR over the past three years from $104.65. This pace of book value compounding is among the highest in the global reinsurance industry.

### Capital Return

RNR repurchased $1.6 billion in shares during 2025, reducing shares outstanding from 50.2 million to 44.0 million (12.4% reduction). Combined with 2024's $667 million buyback, approximately 17% of shares have been retired in two years. The dividend yields 0.5%, modest by sector standards but consistent with the company's preference for buybacks at what management considers attractive valuations.

### Tax Headwind

Bermuda's new 15% corporate income tax, effective January 2025, increased RNR's tax expense from $33 million to $396 million — a $364 million permanent headwind reducing after-tax earnings by approximately 10%. A $640 million deferred tax asset partially offsets this near-term, but OECD Pillar II administrative guidance may limit its reversal benefit after 2026.

### Earnings Surprises

RNR has beaten consensus EPS estimates by 30.1%, 58.1%, and 23.7% in the last three reported quarters (Q4 2025, Q3 2025, Q2 2025). The Q1 2025 miss was driven by the California Wildfires — a catastrophe loss, not an operational shortfall. This consistent beat pattern suggests analyst models systematically underestimate RNR's earnings capacity, likely because catastrophe modeling is inherently conservative and because fee income and investment income receive less granular consensus coverage.

### Estimate Revisions

Accelerating 30-day revisions in the current quarter (+1.5%) and next quarter (+3.2%) suggest the fundamental reassessment is ongoing.

## Growth Analysis

RNR's growth profile is best understood through book value per share compounding rather than premium volume. Gross premiums were flat in 2025 ($11.74B vs. $11.73B in 2024), reflecting a deliberate choice to prioritize margin over top-line growth as the pricing cycle matures.

**Growth Drivers:**

1. **Investment income expansion**: With $36.1 billion in total investments growing as new premiums are collected and invested at current yields, the investment income run rate has upside even with flat premiums.

2. **Third-party capital platform scaling**: The Medici UCITS launch expands addressable capital into European institutions. Fee income can grow independently of RNR's own balance sheet utilization.

3. **Share count reduction**: At the current buyback pace, EPS growth runs 8-12 percentage points above net income growth. If management maintains $1.5-2.0B annual repurchases, the share count declines to approximately 38-40 million by end of 2027.

4. **Cycle optionality**: A return to hard pricing (triggered by a major loss event or market exit) would allow premium growth to re-accelerate from current levels.

**Growth Constraints:**

- Casualty & Specialty premium is declining (-1.6% in 2025) as management actively reduces exposure in challenged lines
- Competitive pressure from new capital entering the reinsurance market after several years of strong returns
- Bermuda CIT reduces the compounding rate by approximately 10% relative to the historical regime

Consensus expects $37.97 EPS in FY2026 and $43.97 in FY2027, implying 16% year-over-year growth. Given the consistent earnings beat pattern, actual delivery could exceed these estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

RenaissanceRe competes with Swiss Re, Munich Re, Hannover Re, Berkshire Hathaway, and Everest Re among major global reinsurers. Its competitive advantages are durable and compounding:

**Proprietary Catastrophe Modeling**: RenaissanceRe Risk Sciences operates independently within the company, building probabilistic models for natural catastrophe risk that are widely considered best-in-class. This advantage compounds because the models improve with each year of claims data, and competitors cannot easily replicate decades of calibrated experience.

**Third-Party Capital Platform**: The DaVinci/Fontana/Medici/Vermeer suite manages approximately $10 billion in external capital. RNR earns management and performance fees while sharing catastrophe risk with capital partners. Competitors have struggled to build comparable platforms because they require the same modeling credibility and track record that took decades to establish.

**Gross-to-Net Flexibility**: The ability to write large gross lines and then cede portions to managed vehicles and retrocessionaires gives RNR unique flexibility to scale risk up or down without turning away clients. This preserves client relationships during soft markets while protecting capital during hard markets.

**Scale from Validus Integration**: The 2023 Validus acquisition roughly doubled RNR's premium base, improving diversification and giving the company greater relevance to clients and brokers.

Management explicitly notes that "favorable results have increased competition and supply of reinsurance capacity, putting pressure on rates and margins." Property catastrophe rates are no longer hardening, and new capital from insurance-linked securities (ILS) and sidecars adds supply. RNR's response — prioritizing margin over volume and reducing challenged casualty lines — is the correct strategic posture.

## Risk Assessment

### Casualty Reserve Adequacy (Primary Risk)

The Casualty & Specialty segment is the most significant risk to the investment thesis. The reserve development table shows concerning trends:

- Accident Year 2018 reserves: initial $1.67B → current $1.97B (+18.0% adverse)
- Accident Year 2019 reserves: initial $1.55B → current $1.81B (+16.4% adverse)

The sensitivity analysis discloses that a 10% increase in expected loss ratios with slower reporting could add $2.7 billion to reserves, reducing equity by 23.4%. This is the tail risk the market is partially pricing.

Mitigating factors: Management is actively reducing casualty exposure (general casualty GPW -5.9%, professional liability -11.2%) and increasing retrocession coverage (+22.7%). The investment income stream provides buffer against underwriting losses. Even a $1.0 billion reserve charge would reduce EPS by approximately $22, and the stock would still trade at under 10x adjusted earnings at current prices.

### Catastrophe Concentration

RNR absorbed $1.4 billion in net large loss events in 2025 (California Wildfires + Hurricane Melissa + others) and still delivered $2.6 billion in net income. A multi-event year (major hurricane + earthquake, for example) could exceed this capacity. Climate change is expected to increase both frequency and severity of weather events, expanding the tail of the catastrophe loss distribution.

### Broker Concentration

81.3% of premiums flowing through three brokers creates relationship risk. Any material dispute, broker M&A, or competitive shift could impact premium volume.

### Bermuda Tax Regime

The new 15% CIT is permanent. OECD Pillar II rules may further limit the benefit of the deferred tax asset after 2026. Additional regulatory proposals targeting intercompany reinsurance transactions add further tax uncertainty.

### Reinsurance Cycle Softening

While rates remain adequate, the direction is no longer favorable. New capital entering the market after years of strong returns puts downward pressure on pricing. A sustained softening cycle would compress margins below current levels.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 27.8% trades 3.8 percentage points above one-year historical volatility of 24.0%, with the term structure in backwardation (27.8% near-term vs. 24.3% at 3-month). This pricing is consistent with near-term uncertainty from the approaching hurricane season and ongoing geopolitical risk affecting reinsurance pricing. Elevated put skew of 8.6% at the near-term expiry confirms institutional demand for downside protection, though the 3-month put/call skew flips to -1.6% (slightly call-favoring), suggesting this concern is concentrated in the near term. Short interest at only 3.2% of float and 2.7 days to cover indicates no crowded short thesis. The options signal is mildly cautionary for very short-term positioning but consistent with the intermediate-term fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($370–$445, +22% to +47%)

- Casualty reserves prove adequate or require only modest strengthening, and the segment's combined ratio improves toward 100% as management's underwriting actions take effect
- Property catastrophe pricing holds or re-hardens following a loss event, expanding underwriting margins
- Share buybacks continue at $1.5B+ annually, reducing share count to ~38M by end of 2027
- Book value per share reaches $280-310 by year-end 2026, and the market re-rates to 1.4-1.5x book (justified by sustained 15-20%+ ROE)
- Third-party capital platform grows to $15B+, with fee income exceeding $400M
- The current rate environment, combined with elevated interest rates supporting investment income, sustains an EPS run rate of $40-50+ for several years

### Bear Case ($230–$255, -16% to -24%)

- Casualty reserve development accelerates, requiring a $1.5-2.5B charge that reduces book value and raises questions about Validus acquisition underwriting
- A benign catastrophe year paradoxically hurts RNR by accelerating market softening and new capital entry, compressing property margins
- Investment income declines if a severe recession forces rate cuts (though the current macro environment makes this unlikely in the near term)
- Social inflation trends worsen beyond management's assumptions, requiring ongoing reserve additions for multiple years
- The stock de-rates to 6-7x forward earnings (below peer median) on execution concerns

## Conclusion

RenaissanceRe is a high-quality reinsurance franchise trading at a significant discount to peers (8.0x forward vs. 9.3x peer median) while delivering superior returns (25.9% ROE vs. peer average in the low-to-mid teens). The three-profit-driver model — underwriting income, fee income, and investment income — provides earnings resilience that single-driver reinsurers lack.

The Casualty reserve question is real and warrants monitoring, but management's active de-risking and the stock's low valuation provide meaningful cushion. At 1.23x book value, the market is pricing modest ROE sustainability; if RNR sustains even mid-teens ROE (well below its trailing 25.9%), the stock is undervalued.

Elevated interest rates supporting float income and geopolitical risk maintaining reinsurance pricing discipline provide additional tailwinds. RNR warrants accumulation at current levels, with conviction rising on any pullback toward the $280 analyst target floor.

**Recommendation: BUY**
