**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CNX is a pure-play Appalachian natural gas producer with an integrated midstream business, a low-cost asset base concentrated in the Marcellus and Utica shales, and a differentiated environmental-attribute monetization franchise. The stock trades at $32.11, near the low end of its 52-week range ($27.72–$43.62), having retraced roughly 26% from its high. Headline trailing P/E of 4.4x is misleading: GAAP earnings are heavily distorted by non-cash derivative mark-to-market swings (a +$226M unrealized gain in Q1 2026 against a –$418M unrealized loss in Q1 2025). On normalized operating earnings, consensus has CY26 EPS at $3.07 and CY27 at $4.23, implying ~10.5x and ~7.6x forward — reasonable but not deeply cheap for a gas E&P with capped near-term upside.

The investment tension is concentrated in the hedge book. CNX's signature long-dated hedging program protected cash flow through the 2024–25 downturn but now caps upside in a recovering gas market: the company is hedged ~346 Bcf for 2026 at ~$2.74/Mcf against ~$4.74 realized spot, producing a –$222M cash hedge loss in Q1 alone (–$1.59/Mcf drag). The in-basin data-center demand story is real and management is participating in supply RFPs, but no binding contract is disclosed in filings, and CNX has already sold forward much of its 2026–27 production at sub-$3.50 prices. Bulls underwriting an AI-driven gas upcycle should recognize that CNX participates in that upside on a multi-year lag, not immediately.

We rate CNX a HOLD. The bull case rests on a genuinely undervalued FCF generator with a $2.37B undeployed buyback authorization and optionality on >10 Bcf/d of forecast in-basin Appalachian demand by decade-end. The bear case rests on hedge-capped upside, ~21M cumulative shares of convertible dilution (9.5M in December 2025, ~12M in May 2026) that partially offsets the buyback, a fresh CEO, and analyst sentiment that has been mixed (a new strong-sell appeared this cycle, though sell-side ratings have modestly consolidated over the prior three months). The setup is balanced rather than asymmetric, and the AI tailwind is an option, not booked revenue.

## Company Overview

CNX Resources is a Pittsburgh-based Appalachian natural gas producer operating across three reportable segments: Shale (the engine), Coalbed Methane (small but recently profitable), and Other (purchased gas, environmental-attribute sales, derivatives, corporate). Production is ~92%+ dry gas, with FY2026 guided to 605–620 Bcfe; Q1 2026 ran 152.4 Bcfe (~617 Bcfe annualized). The Shale segment generated $663M of the $722M gas/NGL/oil revenue in Q1 and $249M of $428M pre-tax income.

The company differentiates from Appalachian peers (EQT, Antero, Range, Coterra) along three axes. First, a "New Technologies" / environmental-attributes franchise that monetizes methane capture, remediated mine gas, and carbon/methane-performance credits (~$15M in Q1 2026, down from ~$23M YoY), with upside tied to pending 45Z clean-fuel tax-credit guidance. Second, a long-dated, opportunistic hedging program extending to 2028–2029, further out than most peers. Third, a vertically integrated midstream (CNX Midstream, consolidated with non-recourse parent debt) that lowers unit costs.

Leadership has just turned over. Former CFO Alan Shepard is now President & CEO; founder-CEO Nicholas DeIuliis transitioned to Chairman/Director; Everett Good is now CFO and Navneet Behl is COO. Shepard signs the Q1 2026 10-Q as principal executive officer, confirming the handover is recent. The promotion is internal, which supports continuity.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings quality.** Q1 2026 net income was $348M ($2.18 diluted EPS) versus a $198M loss a year earlier, but ~$644M of that $546M swing is derivative mark-to-market noise. The cleaner view: "sales of gas/NGL/oil including cash settlements" of $500M (up from $441M) and production margin of $238M ($1.56/Mcfe, up from $194M). Operating EPS of ~$1.20 beat consensus of ~$0.93. Realized gas price was $4.74/Mcf (+29.5% YoY), cut to ~$3.28/Mcfe after the hedge drag. Underwriting CNX requires normalizing through GAAP to cash-settled realizations and FCF.

**Earnings momentum.** The beat track record is strong and consistent: four straight beats (27.0%, 94.8%, 47.6%, 30.2%). This supports confidence in forward operating estimates. Estimate revisions are mixed: current-year EPS revised to $3.07 (+4.2% over 30 days, +8.3% over 90 days), but next-quarter estimates have been cut –23.9% over 90 days and next-year revised –3.8% over 30 days. The near-term cuts reflect the hedge cash drag working through realizations, not a demand problem. This is not the >+30% revision velocity that would force a re-rating.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $278M in Q1 against $170M capex; FY2026 capex is guided $556–586M, leaving meaningful FCF at current strip. No dividend since 2016 — the entire return policy is buybacks. The board raised the authorization to $4.9B in January 2026 with $2.37B still available. Q1 buybacks were modest ($51M at ~$37) as cash funded the debt refinancing.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt ~$2.38B against $4.63B equity and $9.13B assets. The maturity profile is well-laddered following the February 2026 refinancing (issued $500M 5.875% notes due 2034, retired $500M 6.00% notes due 2029, $12M extinguishment loss for a lower coupon and longer tenor). Net leverage covenant ≤3.5x and current-ratio covenant ≥1.0x are both met, with ~$1.30B unused on the revolver (borrowing base $2.4B). The screen-level sub-1.0 current ratio is an artifact of the current-classified convertible and derivative liabilities, not distress.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is modest and acquisition-supported. The ~$518M Apex Energy II acquisition (closed January 2025) expanded central-PA leasehold and Utica rights and is contributing to the ~617 Bcfe annualized run-rate. Organic growth in a pure-play gas producer ultimately tracks the commodity and the drilling program rather than a secular volume story.

The growth optionality that matters is in-basin AI/data-center gas demand. On the Q1 call, Shepard shared peers' optimism on >10 Bcf/d of in-basin Appalachian demand growth by decade-end, cited named projects (NextEra's planned Mon Valley site, the Homer City redevelopment, Ohio/Clarington interconnects), and confirmed CNX is participating in gas-supply RFPs, leveraging resource depth and creditworthiness to pursue long-term supply deals. This is consistent with the broader confirmation that natural gas is the fast-deployable power source for AI compute.

Two qualifications keep this in the "option" rather than "growth driver" column. First, management explicitly flagged timing as the open question — "3, 5 or 7 years?" — and no binding large supply contract appears in the filings. CNX would be a gas supplier to power plants serving hyperscalers; it owns neither the data centers nor the power plants. Second, the hedge book means the 2026–27 portion of any near-term gas-price strength is already sold forward at ~$2.74–$3.31/Mcf. The tangible early benefit is tightening out-year basis differentials helping Cal-2028 realized pricing, which is real but incremental. Environmental-attribute revenue and the pending 45Z guidance add a smaller, policy-dependent growth vector.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing GAAP, CNX screens at 4.4x — meaningless given the derivative distortion. On normalized consensus, the stock trades at ~10.5x CY26 and ~7.6x CY27. Against the peer set, the median P/E is 16.0x (MGY 16.0x, CRK 6.2x, MUR 66.1x, with CRC and CRGY at negative trailing multiples reflecting their own distortions). CNX's forward multiple sits below the peer median, which is partly justified by the hedge-capped upside that other producers don't carry to the same degree — CNX gives up more of a rising-gas tape than peers, so a lower multiple on near-term earnings is rational.

The analyst price-target range is $34 (low) to $49 (high), mean $38.82, median $38.00 — implying ~21% upside to mean from the current price. The valuation case rests on FCF yield and the buyback authorization (roughly half the $4.7B market cap) rather than on a re-rating catalyst. At ~10x forward with a recovering gas market and a large undeployed buyback, the downside looks reasonably supported; the upside is capped by the hedges and by the absence of a near-term re-rating signal. The score reflects a stock that is cheap-to-fair on normalized earnings with asymmetric protection from the buyback, but without the identified mispricing catalyst that would justify a higher mark.

We deliberately do not treat the recovered prior valuation score (8.0 in the trajectory) as a standing call. At the current price the risk/reward has improved on a lower price with intact fundamentals, but the convertible dilution that landed in May 2026 is a real headwind to per-share metrics that argues against pushing the valuation score back toward 8.

## Competitive Landscape

CNX is a mid-cap player in the largest U.S. gas basin, competing with EQT, Antero, Range, and Coterra. Its competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving. The integrated midstream and low-cost structure are durable advantages; the environmental-attributes franchise is a genuine differentiator that supports the low-carbon-gas brand (methane intensity is a formal compensation metric). Resource depth and balance-sheet creditworthiness position CNX credibly to bid for long-term data-center supply contracts.

Two competitive cautions. First, the five-year cumulative TSR (340.8) materially lagged the peer group (531.8) — strong absolute returns but underperformance versus gas peers, indicating CNX has not been the basin's best capital compounder. Second, customer concentration rotates: in Q1 2026 NRG Business Marketing (~$156M) and DTE Energy Trading (~$83M) each exceeded 10% of revenue, where a year earlier it was Citadel. The rotation limits single-name dependence but bears monitoring.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity and hedge-timing risk (dominant).** As a near-pure gas producer, results swing with Henry Hub and Appalachian basis. The hedge book protects downside but caps upside — ~346 Bcf for 2026 at ~$2.74, 402 Bcf for 2027 at $3.31, 200 Bcf for 2028 at $3.25. In a rising-gas world this is the single largest drag on realized value.

**Convertible dilution (concrete, recent).** The 2.25% converts ($209M, $12.84 strike) matured May 1, 2026 and settled in stock — ~12M net new shares after the $18.19 capped call, on top of ~9.5M issued in a December 2025 private exchange. Roughly 21M cumulative shares against a 142M base is meaningful; net share-count direction now depends on whether buyback pace offsets it.

**Coal-legacy contingencies.** Despite the 2017 coal spin-off, CNX retains exposure to Coal Act retiree-benefit litigation (UMWA 1992 Plan, ~2,159 retirees) and a $75M UMWA 1974 Pension settlement. CNX won partial summary judgment forcing Core Natural Resources to indemnify, and Core has reimbursed to date — but this is a credit dependency on a third party's solvency.

**Other.** Environmental-attribute revenue is fragile and policy-dependent (45Z still pending); inflation in steel/diesel/labor pressures costs; and the fresh CEO introduces execution-continuity risk, partly mitigated by Shepard's internal promotion.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is balanced-to-mildly-cautious rather than alarming. Near-term ATM IV of 26.8% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 30.1% (a –3.2% spread, IV in the 5th percentile of the current chain), indicating the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst — potentially favorable entry timing if the fundamental case were stronger. The put/call OI ratio rose to 1.92 (from 1.01 over three sessions) and the 6-month skew is negative (–5.2%), pointing to accumulated downside hedging at longer tenors. Combined with 14.8% short interest and 8.2 days to cover, institutional positioning leans bearish. The elevated short interest is a two-sided signal: it confirms the cautious view but creates squeeze potential if a supply contract or gas-price move surprises to the upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** CNX is a low-cost, FCF-generative Appalachian gas pure-play trading at ~10x forward earnings with a buyback authorization equal to roughly half the market cap. The balance sheet is well-laddered with no near maturity towers and ample liquidity. In-basin AI/data-center demand (named projects, active RFP participation) provides genuine multi-year optionality, and tightening out-year basis is already improving Cal-2028 realizations. A signed long-term supply contract would re-rate the equity. Bull target ~$49 (analyst high), supported by gas-price upside flowing through as hedges roll off into 2028+ and aggressive buyback below intrinsic value.

**Bear case.** The hedge book caps 2026–27 upside in exactly the rising-gas scenario the bulls want, so CNX participates in the AI tailwind on a multi-year lag. Convertible dilution offsets the buyback's per-share benefit. The AI-demand thesis is an option with uncertain timing ("3, 5, or 7 years?") and no contracted backlog. Analyst sentiment carries a recent strong-sell addition (3 sell + 1 strong-sell of 12 ratings this cycle). Five-year TSR has lagged peers. Bear target ~$28 (near the 52-week low), reached if gas weakens, the data-center catalysts slip, and dilution continues.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as **uncertain** — the evidence does not support a confident directional call. The recommendation has been HOLD for four consecutive reports; the bar for changing it is a specific catalyst, which has not yet arrived.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A signed, disclosed long-term gas-supply contract tied to an in-basin data-center / power project (removes the "option vs. backlog" discount).
- Evidence the 2026–27 hedge cash drag is rolling off (e.g., 2027+ realized pricing converging toward strip, or hedge resets at materially higher prices), restoring near-term upside participation.
- Buyback pace demonstrably outrunning convertible dilution, with net share count declining over two quarters.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A break in the operating-earnings beat streak combined with falling current-year revisions (the inverse of the current pattern).
- Gas prices weakening materially while CNX remains hedged below market, or covenant headroom (≤3.5x net leverage) compressing.
- An adverse development in the Coal Act litigation or a deterioration in Core Natural Resources' ability to indemnify.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August), and any 8-K disclosing a supply contract or further capital-structure action.

## Conclusion

CNX is a financially sound, FCF-generative Appalachian gas producer with a credible low-carbon differentiator, a well-laddered balance sheet, and genuine optionality on in-basin AI/data-center demand. On normalized earnings it is fairly-to-modestly cheap (~10x forward), and the buyback provides downside support. The reasons to wait are concrete: the long-dated hedge book caps the very upside the AI thesis implies, convertible dilution offsets the buyback, the data-center demand is an option without contracted backlog, the CEO seat just turned over, and sell-side sentiment carries a recent strong-sell addition. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate CNX a HOLD and would upgrade on a signed supply contract or clear evidence the hedge drag is rolling off.
