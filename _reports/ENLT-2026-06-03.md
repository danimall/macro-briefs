**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Enlight Renewable Energy is an Israel-domiciled developer, owner, and operator of utility-scale solar, wind, and battery-storage assets across Israel, the United States (via its Clēnera platform), and Europe. The operating business is growing quickly: Q1 2026 revenue rose 54% year-over-year to roughly $200M and adjusted EBITDA rose 58% to roughly $154M, with full-year guidance of $755M–$785M reaffirmed and a stated ambition to reach a ~$2.1B annual revenue run-rate by the end of 2028. The May 26 announcement of a 15-year, 200 MWac power-purchase agreement to supply Google's Oklahoma data centers from the Solstice project places Enlight directly in the strongest secular demand theme in power markets (hyperscaler/AI electricity load) and upgrades the quality of its U.S. contracted revenue.

The problem is price. The stock has appreciated roughly 437% over the past year, from a 52-week low of $18.22 to a recent ~$104–108, and now trades at approximately 232x trailing and forward earnings. At ~$105 it sits roughly 27% above the analyst mean ($82.43) and roughly 29% above the median ($81.00) targets, and well above Deutsche Bank's $65 Hold target set in April. The market is already capitalizing flawless execution of a pipeline that management itself frames as a roughly three-fold revenue expansion over three years. That is a demanding bar in an environment of elevated long-end yields (a leveraged IPP's cost of capital), unresolved U.S. solar/storage policy, and active regional conflict around the company's domicile.

The growth and the catalyst are genuine; we treat the data-center demand confirmation as real. Confirming a demand thesis is not the same as a buy signal on a stock that has run this far this fast. With three consecutive earnings misses, an unverified high-leverage balance sheet, broad insider selling into the highs, and an options market in backwardation pricing imminent downside, the near-term risk/reward is unfavorable while the long-term franchise remains intact. We rate ENLT a **HOLD**.

## Company Overview

Enlight (NASDAQ/TASE: ENLT) runs an integrated, vertically-managed independent power producer model: it originates, develops, finances, builds, and then owns and operates renewable generation and storage assets that produce long-term contracted (PPA-based) revenue. The asset mix spans solar PV, onshore wind, and a growing battery-storage book, reflecting the industry's shift toward firmed, dispatchable renewable supply.

The company operates across three core regions: Israel, the United States, and Central/Eastern and Southern Europe. U.S. development runs primarily through the Clēnera Holdings subsidiary, the entity that signed the Google PPA. The structural advantages management points to are (a) a multi-market pipeline that diversifies regulatory and merchant risk, (b) an integrated developer-operator model that captures margin across the value chain, and (c) growing access to large corporate offtakers, now including hyperscaler data centers.

A practical note on disclosure: as a foreign private issuer, Enlight files Form 20-F (annual) and Form 6-K (interim) rather than the domestic 10-K/10-Q/8-K set. This limits the granularity of independently verifiable balance-sheet, leverage, and cash-flow data in our analysis, and it materially raises the importance of the 20-F for any credit assessment. Several conclusions below rely on management commentary and news sources that should be reconciled against that filing.

## Financial Analysis

The top line and operating profitability are the strongest part of the story. The Q1 2026 EBITDA margin of ~77% is typical of contracted IPP assets, where revenue is largely fixed-price and operating costs are low once assets are commissioned. The guidance and run-rate target imply management expects roughly to triple revenue as a large pipeline comes online.

The bottom line is noisy and weak. Reported EPS collapsed year-over-year from $0.75 to a range of $0.08–$0.16 (sources differ), and the company has missed consensus EPS in each of the last three quarters by margins of 75% to 98%:

| Quarter | EPS Estimate | EPS Actual | Surprise % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-06-30 | $0.60 | $0.01 | -98.3% |
| 2025-09-30 | $0.64 | $0.16 | -75.0% |
| 2025-12-31 | $0.75 | $0.10 | -86.7% |
| 2026-03-31 | — | $0.08 | — |

Part of this divergence is explainable: the sell-down of the "Sunlight cluster" and the lumpiness of capital-recycling gains distort year-over-year EPS comparisons, while financing costs weigh on net income during the build-out phase. EBITDA and contracted-revenue growth are the more honest read on operating momentum. But a three-quarter streak of large misses still signals limited earnings visibility and a bottom line that is being shaped as much by capital-markets activity as by operations.

The central gap in our work is the balance sheet. Renewable IPP developers are capital-intensive and typically carry substantial project-level (often non-recourse) debt, and Enlight funds growth partly through asset sell-downs (the Sunlight transaction), a common but execution- and market-timing-dependent strategy. We could not independently verify leverage ratios, the refinancing schedule, liquidity, or cash flow. With long-end yields elevated, the cost of capital and refinancing terms are central to project economics and to the credit picture. Until the 20-F leverage detail is in hand, we treat financing risk as a material unquantified overhang and score financial health at a middling 5.0: strong EBITDA generation offset by leverage opacity, rate sensitivity, and noisy reported earnings.

## Growth Analysis

This is the dimension that justifies owning the franchise. Revenue growth of 54% and EBITDA growth of 58% are not artifacts of a low base; they reflect a sizeable pipeline transitioning into operation. The $2.1B-by-2028 run-rate target, if delivered, would roughly triple the business over three years.

Through Clēnera, Enlight will supply 200 MWac of solar to Google's Oklahoma data center operations under a 15-year contract, sourced from the 250 MWdc Solstice project. Enlight is the generator and seller; Google is the offtaker. This expands Enlight's U.S. business into large hyperscaler counterparties and lengthens the duration and improves the credit quality of its contracted revenue. The demand backdrop is broad and confirmed by multiple counterparties (Reuters reported OG&E is also supplying Google's Oklahoma buildout), which raises the durability of the growth thesis beyond a single-company story.

Estimate revisions are modestly supportive but not a clean inflection signal: current-year EPS estimates are up 13.8% over 90 days, next-year up 10.7% over 90 days, but the 30-day trend has flattened (next year -1.3%, current quarter -2.6%), and only two-to-four analysts populate the near-term estimates. Revenue growth comfortably exceeds 40%, but the stock's valuation (discussed below) is far outside the range where rising estimates alone support a long. We score growth prospects 7.0: genuinely strong and theme-supported, capped by execution dependence on a large, multi-year, capital-hungry pipeline.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is where the thesis breaks down at the current price. At ~$105 the stock carries a trailing and forward P/E of roughly 232x on $1.00 of EPS, and a ~$13.4B market capitalization. Even setting earnings aside as distorted and using EBITDA, ~$640M of annualized EBITDA against a $13.4B equity value is roughly 21x on equity alone, and the enterprise multiple is materially higher once the large project-level debt typical of an IPP is added (we cannot precisely size it given the disclosure gap, but it is substantial).

The clearest signal is relative to the sell-side. The full target range is $37 to $123; the stock sits in the top quartile of even that distribution. The guardrail we apply here: analyst targets behave as floors only on stocks that look cheap relative to growth (forward P/E below ~30x or PEG below ~1.5). At 232x forward earnings, ENLT is the opposite case; targets are functioning as ceilings, and the price has already cleared most of them. Prior appreciation is not, by itself, a reason to call a stock expensive; the reason ENLT is expensive is that the current price embeds successful, large-scale, multi-year pipeline execution with no margin for slippage on financing costs, interconnection, permitting, or policy.

Sector-theme momentum (data-center demand) is real, but it does not rescue valuation on a name already above 50x. The 2025–2026 software de-rating demonstrated that a correct demand theme does not protect premium-multiple stocks from sharp multiple compression. We score valuation 3.0: a stretched multiple with asymmetry tilted to the downside and no identifiable near-term catalyst that would justify paying above the analyst high-end.

## Competitive Landscape

Enlight's competitive trajectory is improving. The company competes against far larger IPPs and developers (NextEra Energy Resources, Brookfield Renewable, and regional European players), but its differentiation is credible: multi-geography diversification that spreads regulatory and merchant risk, a scaling U.S. platform in Clēnera, and now a foothold in hyperscaler offtake. The integrated developer-operator model captures margin across origination, development, financing, construction, and operation rather than ceding it to third parties.

The structural tailwind is genuine and broad-based. Hyperscaler and AI-driven data-center electricity demand is one of the strongest secular drivers in power markets, and long-dated corporate PPAs from large buyers improve both revenue visibility and the credit quality of the contracted book. Against this, the company faces sector-wide headwinds shared by all developers: interconnection queue bottlenecks, module supply and tariff exposure, and U.S. IRA policy uncertainty around ITC/PTC and transferability. On balance the trajectory is positive; we score competitive position 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

Several risks are material and not obviously priced into a 232x multiple:

- **Israel domicile and regional conflict.** A meaningful portion of operating assets and the corporate domicile sit in Israel, exposing the company to active regional conflict, NIS currency risk, and Israeli regulatory/tax risk. This is a differentiated risk versus U.S.-only renewable peers, and it is elevated in the current environment.
- **Leverage and rate sensitivity.** As a capital-intensive, levered IPP, project economics and refinancing terms depend on rates and credit availability. With long-end yields elevated, rising rates compress development returns and raise refinancing cost. The unverified balance sheet makes this the single largest unquantified item.
- **Capital-recycling dependence.** Funding growth via asset sell-downs (Sunlight cluster) introduces market-timing and valuation risk. Weak sale multiples would impair the funding model and the earnings that flow from gains.
- **U.S. policy.** U.S. solar/storage economics depend on IRA tax credits and on module tariff/trade policy. Rollbacks or tariff escalation would directly hit the U.S. growth thesis.
- **Execution.** The $2.1B-by-2028 target depends on bringing a large pipeline online on time and on budget; interconnection, permitting, and supply-chain delays are recurring industry frictions.
- **Emerging customer concentration.** Large hyperscaler offtakes improve revenue quality but introduce single-counterparty concentration over time.

We score risk factors 3.5, reflecting the cluster of binary/structural exposures that are not adequately discounted at the current price.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is signaling near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 73.0% sits well above 1-year realized volatility of 51.8% (a rich 21-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 73.0% versus 60.7% six-month), which typically indicates the market is pricing an imminent event. The near-term put/call volume ratio is elevated at 2.40, and skew turns negative further out (-10.4% at three months, -5.8% at six months), consistent with demand for downside protection. The nearest expiry implies a ±11.9% move. This reinforces that the consensus is positioned for two-sided risk with a downside tilt around the next catalyst, not a continuation of the melt-up. Short interest, by contrast, is negligible at 0.4% of float: the bearish case is uncrowded, so there is little squeeze fuel and little informed short positioning to lean on.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$123, +17%):** Enlight executes its pipeline on schedule, converts the $2.1B-by-2028 target into visible contracted EBITDA, signs additional hyperscaler PPAs that re-rate the U.S. business, and benefits from any easing in financing costs. Capital recycling continues to fund growth at attractive multiples. The stock holds its premium as a scarce, multi-geography pure-play on data-center-driven clean power demand. This is roughly the analyst high target.

**Bear case (~$65, -38%):** A leveraged balance sheet meets a higher-for-longer rate environment, compressing development returns and lifting refinancing cost. Pipeline slippage, an IRA/tariff policy setback, or an Israel-related shock interrupts the growth narrative, and the multiple compresses toward the sell-side's view of fair value. This is Deutsche Bank's Hold target, a level the stock traded near as recently as early spring and that requires only multiple normalization, not operational failure.

**Base case (~$81–82, -22%):** The business continues to grow operationally, but the multiple reverts toward the analyst median as the market reconciles a strong franchise with a price that overshot. The stock spends the next several quarters digesting its run while EBITDA grows into the valuation.

The asymmetry from the current price favors the downside: roughly 17% to the most optimistic published target against 22–38% to base/bear. That distribution is why this is a hold-to-trim rather than an add.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (rationale: uncertain). The operating business is improving and the data-center demand catalyst is real, but the price has run ahead of fundamentals and near-term risk is skewed lower. We do not have sufficient conviction in 12-month appreciation from ~$105 to call this a growth hold, nor is the business impaired enough to avoid.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A meaningful multiple reset toward the low-$70s or below (closer to the analyst median) on unchanged or improving fundamentals: a better entry point on a still-intact pipeline.
- 20-F disclosure confirming a manageable, well-laddered debt structure with limited near-term refinancing risk and sustained positive free cash flow visibility.
- Two-plus additional hyperscaler/corporate PPAs of comparable scale, demonstrating the Google deal is a repeatable platform rather than a one-off.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fourth consecutive material EPS miss accompanied by a guidance cut or a downward revision to the 2026 revenue range.
- Evidence of strained financing: a failed or distressed asset sell-down, a covenant issue, or refinancing at materially higher rates.
- A U.S. policy reversal (ITC/PTC rollback, tariff escalation) or an Israel-specific event that impairs a meaningful share of operating assets, combined with the stock breaking below its 200-day moving average.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly results (6-K) and any update on pipeline commissioning and the Solstice/Google project timeline.

## Conclusion

Enlight is a high-quality, fast-growing renewable IPP with a credible multi-geography platform and a genuine new tailwind from hyperscaler data-center demand, crystallized in the 15-year Google PPA. The operating numbers are real and well above sector norms. None of that is in dispute.

The dispute is price. At ~$105 the stock trades at roughly 232x earnings and ~27–29% above the analyst mean/median targets, with the bottom line distorted by capital recycling, three straight earnings misses, an unverified and likely substantial debt load, Israel and rate and policy risks that are poorly discounted, and an options market in backwardation pricing imminent downside. Broad insider selling into the highs (vesting-driven and therefore low-signal on its own, but conspicuous in its breadth and the complete absence of open-market buying) adds to the sense that the move has gotten ahead of itself. The franchise is worth owning at a sensible price; ~$105 is not it. We rate ENLT a **HOLD** and would revisit constructively on a multiple reset toward the analyst median or on hard evidence that the balance sheet and pipeline can carry the valuation the market has already assigned.
