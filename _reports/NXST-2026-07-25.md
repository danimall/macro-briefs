**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nexstar is the largest local television broadcaster in the United States, and following the completed March 19, 2026 acquisition of TEGNA it now controls 265 full-power stations across 132 markets, reaching roughly 80% of U.S. television households. The strategic logic is straightforward: scale confers leverage in retransmission-consent negotiations with cable/satellite distributors and in affiliation-fee negotiations with the networks, plus a larger political-advertising footprint in an election-cycle business. The problem is that Nexstar has already paid $3.7 billion and financed $9.4 billion of new debt to buy TEGNA, but a federal court in the Eastern District of California enjoined it from integrating the asset on April 17, 2026. The company owns TEGNA outright while legally barred from capturing the operating synergies that justify the leverage.

The deal has closed and the debt is on the books; total debt jumped from $6.3 billion to $12.2 billion and now stands at 84.9% of total capitalization against roughly $2.15 billion of equity. The Altman Z-Score sits in the distress zone near 0.95. What remains uncertain is whether an antitrust coalition — DirecTV plus thirteen state attorneys general — succeeds in forcing divestiture, indefinite hold-separate, or unwind. That is a materially worse asymmetry than a merger-arb spread, because Nexstar is bearing the full cost of the transaction while the benefit is legally frozen.

The regulatory picture is two-sided and the forces run on different timelines. On August 6, 2026, the FCC under Chairman Carr votes on repealing the 39% national ownership cap, a near-term positive that already drove an 8.8% rally on July 16 and would neutralize the WPIX/cap-related jeopardy. But the FCC does not control the antitrust channel; DirecTV and the state AGs proceed independently in federal court regardless of what the FCC does. Founder-CEO Perry Sook's ~$2 million open-market purchase at $162.26 on June 26 — after the injunction, near 52-week lows — is the strongest idiosyncratic conviction signal available and argues the ultimate outcome resolves favorably. We rate NXST a HOLD. The stock's path over the next two quarters will be driven far more by the August 6 vote and the 9th Circuit appeal than by normalized earnings, and the binary distribution of outcomes does not support a directional call.

## Company Overview

Nexstar owns, operates, or provides services to 265 full-power TV stations affiliated with all major networks (CBS, NBC, ABC, FOX, The CW). The portfolio extends beyond local stations: 80.8% ownership of The CW, the NewsNation cable news network, the Premion connected-TV advertising platform (acquired via TEGNA), national digital assets (The Hill, BestReviews), a 31.3% stake in TV Food Network (partnered with Warner Bros. Discovery), and a large multicast/FAST-channel roster. The household reach is up from ~62% before the TEGNA close.

Two revenue engines drive the business. Distribution revenue (roughly 60% of the total, $837 million in Q1'26) consists of contractual retransmission-consent fees from cable, satellite, and virtual MVPDs — recurring, with annual escalators, and the cash-flow ballast. Advertising revenue (~39%, $548 million in Q1'26) splits between core advertising (in secular decline) and political advertising, which spikes in even-numbered years. The enlarged footprint is deliberately weighted toward markets with contested elections. Nexstar also uses a controversial "VIE" structure (Mission Broadcasting, White Knight, Vaughan) to effectively control roughly 35 additional stations it does not legally own, a long-standing regulatory flashpoint.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant financial fact is the political-advertising cycle layered on top of a transformational, debt-funded acquisition. FY2025, an odd/non-election year, produced roughly $4.9 billion of revenue with net income collapsing to ~$83 million from $683 million in the FY2024 election year — a whipsaw that is structural to the model rather than a red flag by itself. Q1 2026, which included only about two weeks of TEGNA, showed net revenue up 13.1% to $1.40 billion, advertising up 19.1% (political ads at legacy stations rose $35 million to $41 million as 2026 is a midterm year), and distribution up 9.8%. Net income to Nexstar was $164 million versus $108 million; diluted EPS was $5.09 versus $3.37. On a pro forma basis, with TEGNA owned for the full quarter, revenue would have run around $2.0 billion per quarter, or roughly $8 billion annualized.

The balance sheet is the central concern. Total assets ballooned from $10.8 billion at year-end 2025 to $18.1 billion at March 2026 on the TEGNA purchase (~$3.66 billion price generating $2.14 billion of new goodwill, $2.16 billion of FCC licenses, and $1.4 billion of affiliation intangibles). Nexstar raised the $9.4 billion of new debt in Q1 (Term Loan B due 2033, $3.39 billion of 6.50% secured notes due 2033, and bridge financing), repaid TEGNA's notes, and in April issued another $1.725 billion of 7.25% notes due 2034 to refinance 2027 maturities. For a broadcaster with stable retransmission cash flows this leverage is not fatal, but it leaves little margin for error if the TEGNA synergies remain blocked.

Cash-flow quality warrants scrutiny. Operating cash flow was $289 million in Q1'26, down from $337 million on timing, and roughly $84 million of that figure was a return-of-capital distribution from TV Food Network — a recurring but finite source, since the partnership carries a December 31, 2026 liquidation trigger that management intends but is not guaranteed to renew with WBD. Interest paid ran $126 million in the quarter, or roughly $500 million-plus annualized and rising, with interest expense up 23.7% year over year. There are no interest-rate hedges in place, so a 100bp move in SOFR swings annual interest by about $57 million. On capital allocation, the $1.86 quarterly dividend (~$224 million per year) was maintained, but no buybacks occurred in Q1 despite $1.4 billion remaining on the authorization — capital is being conserved for debt service and integration, which confirms leverage is now the binding constraint.

Two customers each represent roughly 14% of consolidated revenue — 28% combined — a concentration on the distribution side that matters given the retransmission-freeze condition discussed below.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term revenue trajectory has two crosscurrents. The tailwind is the 2026 midterm cycle: political ad revenue is already ramping, and the enlarged footprint should amplify the second-half surge. The headwind is an FCC-imposed retransmission rate freeze for certain MVPDs through November 30, 2026, which caps a slice of the most reliable revenue stream through year-end.

Estimate-revision data shows sharply rising forward numbers, but the mechanics deserve care. Current-year EPS was revised up 34% over 90 days (to $34.19) and next-year up 35% over 90 days (to $24.20). These revisions reflect the addition of TEGNA's earnings base and the political-cycle year, not organic acceleration in a depressed business — the pattern where large percentage moves reflect a change in the reporting entity rather than accelerating underlying demand. The current-quarter estimate ($7.47, +29% over 30 days) and next-quarter estimate ($8.83, +50% over 30 days) are supported by a single analyst and should be treated as low-confidence. The earnings-surprise record is volatile: a 74.6% beat in Q4'25 and a 27.7% beat in Q2'25, but a 17.6% miss in Q3'25. That variability is consistent with the political-cycle whipsaw and reduces the reliability of point estimates.

The durable growth thesis rests on the ability to integrate TEGNA and convert scale into higher retransmission fees and steadier affiliation economics. That thesis is currently frozen by the injunction. Growth prospects are real if the legal outcome resolves favorably, but they are contingent on an event the company does not control, which is why we score this dimension as modestly positive rather than strong.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline trailing P/E of ~38x against a $183.31 price and ~$5.6 billion equity value is not the right lens, but neither is a naive forward multiple. Consensus current-year EPS puts the stock at roughly 5.4x, and next-year implies ~7.6x — both of which look cheap in isolation. The catch is that these are the higher-cash-flow midterm-year and combined-entity figures, and the enterprise is far larger than the equity implies. Enterprise value is approximately $18 billion given the $12.2 billion of debt, so the equity is a thin, highly levered slice of a big EV. Small changes in enterprise value — or an adverse legal ruling — are amplified enormously at the equity level.

Against Broadcasting peers, the picture is muddled. The peer median P/E is 16.3x (TGNA 16.3x, FOXA 17.9x, FOX 16.0x), and NXST's ~5-8x forward multiple appears to be a deep discount. But a discount at 84.9% debt-to-capitalization with an active antitrust injunction that could force divestiture is not obviously mispricing — it is the market pricing a wide distribution of outcomes. The 4.1% dividend yield provides some absolute support and is well above the 0.9% peer median, though dividend coverage tightens against the interest burden.

The equity lessons here counsel against treating a low multiple as a buy signal on its own; cheapness has been a persistent value-trap flag rather than a source of return. In this case, the cheapness is a direct function of the binary legal risk. Sell-side price targets ($205 low, $290 high, $254 median) sit roughly 12-58% above the current price, but on a name where the central variable is a court ruling rather than an earnings beat, we treat those targets as reflecting a favorable-resolution scenario rather than an independent floor. The valuation is fair-to-cheap conditional on the injunction resolving in Nexstar's favor, and expensive if it does not — a distribution that averages to roughly neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

Nexstar's competitive position at the operating level is strong and structurally improving. Its ~80% household reach is the largest in local broadcasting, and that scale is precisely the lever that gives it negotiating power over both MVPDs (higher retransmission fees) and networks (steadier affiliation economics). The five-year total shareholder return of 217 versus the peer group's 113 evidences durable outperformance under founder leadership.

The regulatory environment has bifurcated in a way that cuts both directions. On the tailwind side, the FCC under Chairman Carr is pro-consolidation, approved the TEGNA deal, and has scheduled the August 6 vote on repealing the 39% national ownership cap — which would remove the single biggest structural constraint on broadcaster M&A, legitimize Nexstar's ~80% reach, and likely neutralize the WPIX/cap-related NAL jeopardy. On the headwind side, DirecTV and thirteen state AGs are attacking the deal through antitrust law, a channel the FCC cannot control. The distributors fear exactly the enlarged retransmission-negotiating leverage that constitutes Nexstar's synergy. The competitive advantage is real, but its realization is currently gated by the courts.

We mark competitive position at 6.0 — above neutral on the strength of the moat and scale, but tempered because the trajectory of that advantage is hostage to an unresolved legal binary and to the retransmission freeze that caps pricing power through November.

## Risk Assessment

The defining risk is the TEGNA hold-separate injunction. This is more severe than typical merger risk because Nexstar cannot integrate the asset it owns. Adverse outcomes range from forced divestiture to indefinite hold-separate to unwind — any of which would be acute against the thin equity cushion. Nexstar appealed to the 9th Circuit but did not seek a stay, and a trial on the merits is pending. The Local TV Advertising antitrust MDL (trial set for November 1, 2027) and the WPIX/Mission NAL add further regulatory exposure.

The financial risks compound the legal ones. At 84.9% debt-to-capitalization with unhedged floating-rate exposure, rising interest expense, and a $2.4 billion 2027 maturity wall, a ratings downgrade would raise refinancing costs at exactly the wrong time. The retransmission freeze through November 30, 2026 is an underappreciated near-term drag on the most reliable revenue line. Secular MVPD subscriber attrition erodes the retransmission base, and core advertising is in structural decline. The TV Food Network partnership's December 31, 2026 liquidation trigger is a wildcard for both cash flow and potential monetization.

On governance, CEO comp of $39.5 million ($46.5 million "comp actually paid") rose in a year net income fell, benchmarked to Adjusted EBITDA rather than GAAP net income — a yellow flag on pay magnitude and the combined Chairman/CEO role, partially offset by genuine long-run outperformance and meaningful personal ownership. The Tribune/Chicago Cubs IRS dispute carries potential ~$225 million-plus exposure that management believes is not material.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated, evenly distributed uncertainty. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 49.2% is rich against 35.2% one-year historical volatility, a 14-point spread, and the term structure is flat (near 49.2% ≈ far 48.4%) — the market sees risk distributed across multiple resolution dates rather than concentrated in a single event, consistent with the August 6 vote, the 9th Circuit timeline, and the antitrust trial all mattering. The 1-month implied move is ±10.7%, and put/call skew of 3.8% shows modestly elevated put demand. Short interest at 10.6% of float (4.4 days to cover) reflects institutional bearish positioning, though it is not at squeeze-inducing levels. The rich IV and flat term structure argue against buying premium here and confirm that the market treats this as a high-variance, catalyst-driven situation rather than a stable cash-flow compounder.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The August 6 FCC cap repeal passes, legitimizing Nexstar's reach and removing the WPIX/cap jeopardy. The 9th Circuit lifts or narrows the injunction, allowing TEGNA integration and the capture of retransmission and affiliation synergies that justify the leverage. The 2026 midterm political surge, amplified by the enlarged footprint, drives second-half revenue and de-levers the balance sheet through free cash flow. The CEO's open-market purchase proves prescient. In this scenario, the stock re-rates toward the sell-side high of $290 as the equity's leveraged upside to a resolved enterprise value plays out.

**Bear Case.** The antitrust coalition prevails at the 9th Circuit or trial, forcing divestiture or indefinite hold-separate. Nexstar carries the full debt load and rising, unhedged interest against a frozen synergy base. A downgrade raises refinancing costs into the 2027 maturity wall. The retransmission freeze caps the reliable revenue line through year-end, TV Food Network's return-of-capital distribution lapses, and the thin equity cushion absorbs the damage disproportionately. The stock falls toward or below the 52-week low of $154.47, with further downside if the legal outcome is severe.

The two cases are unusually acute and the resolution turns on legal binaries the company does not control, which is why a directional call is not warranted.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (uncertain rationale). The evidence is two-sided, and the outcome hinges on legal events we cannot handicap with confidence rather than fundamentals.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The 9th Circuit lifts or materially narrows the TEGNA hold-separate injunction, permitting integration and synergy capture.
- The August 6 FCC cap repeal passes AND the antitrust coalition's claims are dismissed, settled, or narrowed to non-material divestitures.
- Demonstrated de-leveraging progress — first-lien net leverage trending back toward the 4.25x covenant with visible free-cash-flow paydown after the political-cycle surge.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A permanent injunction, forced divestiture of material stations, or an unwind order from the 9th Circuit or trial court.
- A credit downgrade or covenant pressure that raises refinancing costs into the 2027 maturity wall.
- Non-renewal of the TV Food Network partnership at the December 31, 2026 trigger combined with the retransmission-freeze drag materially compressing cash flow.

**Reassessment timeline:** The August 6, 2026 FCC vote is the first hard checkpoint, followed by the 9th Circuit appeal decision and the November 30, 2026 retransmission-freeze expiration. The Q2'26 earnings release (which will show the first full quarter of combined operations and political-ad ramp) is the next natural fundamental read. Reassess within one to two quarters as these catalysts resolve.

## Conclusion

Nexstar is a dominant, scale-advantaged local broadcaster that made an aggressive, correctly reasoned strategic bet — buy TEGNA, reach 80% of U.S. households, and convert scale into pricing leverage — and then found itself legally barred from integrating the asset it already owns and financed. The equity is a thin, highly levered slice of an $18 billion enterprise, which amplifies both the upside if the antitrust injunction lifts and the downside if it does not. The founder-CEO's open-market buy during the period of maximum uncertainty is the strongest conviction signal in the file and tilts our qualitative read toward eventual favorable resolution. But the CEO's confidence is not our confidence: the outcome depends on a federal antitrust ruling and an FCC vote whose interaction is two-sided, with the courts operating on a channel the FCC cannot fix.

The score trajectory (4.6 → 5.6 → 4.5) and the oscillating AVOID/HOLD history reflect this irresolution — the stock keeps re-rating on regulatory headlines without a fundamental resolution. We land on HOLD rather than either extreme because the risk/reward is close to symmetric around a set of binary legal events, the balance sheet leaves little margin for error, and the cheapness of the forward multiple is a direct function of that unresolved risk rather than a mispricing we can underwrite. Wait for the August 6 vote and the 9th Circuit appeal to clarify the distribution before taking a directional position.
