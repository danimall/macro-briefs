## Executive Summary

CF Industries is generating the best earnings in its history outside the 2022 post-invasion peak, and it is doing so for a reason that has nothing to do with the company. The March 2026 closure of the Strait of Hormuz halted fertilizer vessel traffic out of a region that supplies approximately 25-30% of globally traded ammonia and 35-40% of globally traded urea, while Qatar's LNG outage (~18% of global LNG trade) simultaneously shut or throttled gas-dependent nitrogen producers in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh. The marginal cost of the last ton sold rose sharply, and CF — running on domestic North American gas — captured the spread. Q1 2026 realized prices: ammonia +25% to $568/ton, granular urea +17% to $457, UAN +39% to $349, AN +45% to $446.

The second-quarter print is the first evidence that the windfall is partially self-cancelling. Adjusted EPS of $4.73 missed the $5.67 consensus by 16.6%, and GAAP net income of $727M confirms the magnitude; the basis of the $727M figure (consolidated versus attributable to common) is not confirmed from filings, so the implied per-share figure cannot be reliably derived. Two mechanisms explain the shortfall. First, the same disruption that lifted nitrogen prices pulled US natural gas higher through LNG substitution demand: gas rose from 28% of production costs in FY2024 to 34% in FY2025 to 39% in Q1 2026, with Henry Hub averaging $6.32/MMBtu between January 1 and February 20, 2026 against a FY2025 average of $3.53. CF carries only 9.0M MMBtu of basis swaps — a low-single-digit percentage of consumption, and basis swaps hedge locational spread, not outright price. Second, the November 2025 Yazoo City incident idles all production at that site until "late in the fourth quarter of 2026 at the earliest," cutting FY2026 ammonia guidance to ~9.5M tons from 10.12M produced in FY2025 and driving the AN segment to a negative gross margin in Q1.

We maintain HOLD, and the score edges down to 5.1 from 5.2 in July. The balance sheet is not the issue: recalculating from the audited statements gives total equity of $7,775M (CF stockholders' $4,838M plus NCI $2,937M) against total liabilities of $6,313M, net debt of roughly $1.2-1.4B, no bond maturities before 2034, an undrawn $750M revolver, and CF-attributable free cash flow of ~$2.09B (11.5% yield at $118.11). The issue is that consensus already models FY2027 EPS of $10.34 against a FY2026 bottom-up sum of roughly $15.0 — a decline of roughly a third — 30-day revisions are negative across all four forecast periods, and the variable driving earnings upside (nitrogen prices) is the same variable driving the antitrust and political-intervention tail. Base case $120, bull $155, bear $90.

## Company Overview

CF converts natural gas and atmospheric nitrogen into anhydrous ammonia, then upgrades it into granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate and specialty products (DEF, urea liquor, nitric acid). Nine complexes: six US (Donaldsonville LA, Port Neal IA, Yazoo City MS, Verdigris OK, Woodward OK, Waggaman LA), two Canadian (Medicine Hat, Courtright), one UK (Billingham). Donaldsonville alone was approximately 40% of ammonia capacity at 12/31/25.

FY2025 segment net sales and gross margin: Ammonia $2,176M/$682M; Granular Urea $1,781M/$837M; UAN $2,161M/$921M; AN $421M/$79M; Other $545M/$205M. Total $7,084M/$2,724M — a 38.5% gross margin on 19,057 thousand product tons at an average selling price of $372/ton. Geographically, North America was $5,994M and Europe/other $1,090M.

Two structural features shape how the financials should be read. Five of the six US plants sit inside CF Industries Nitrogen, LLC, of which CF owns ~90% and CHS Inc. ~10%. The CFN noncontrolling interest was $2,506M at 3/31/26 and distributed $201M to CHS in January 2026. CHS is simultaneously the largest customer (13% of consolidated net sales in 2025, 12% in 2024, 13% in 2023), a related party, and a permanent claimant on roughly a tenth of the US asset base's cash flow, with contractual annual purchase rights of ~1.1M tons of granular urea and 580,000 tons of UAN. Second, the Blue Point joint venture (CF 40%, JERA and Mitsui the balance) is consolidated, so consolidated capex overstates CF's own cash burden — 2026 guidance of ~$1.3B includes ~$360M funded by partners through financing inflows.

If the $727M is attributable to common, H1 2026 earnings to common of roughly $1,342M would nearly match all of FY2025's $1,455M — but that comparison depends on the unconfirmed basis and should be treated as illustrative only. Expectations had run ahead of the spread mechanics.

The Orica settlement and insurance recovery were receivables at quarter-end, with cash arriving 4/30/26 and in April. Roughly $245M of the gap resolves in Q2, so this is mostly timing — but it demonstrates that in a rising-price environment receivables inflate faster than customer advances ($132M at 3/31/26), so reported earnings and cash diverge. Q1 FCF of $273M should not be annualized: Q2 is the seasonal peak, capex is back-end weighted as Blue Point construction ramps, and the Yazoo rebuild capex beginning in Q2 2026 is excluded from the $550M existing-operations guidance and remains unquantified.

Offsetting items: unrecognized tax benefits of $288M with a $357M total reserve including interest, primarily Canadian transfer pricing post-2011, and cumulative deposits of CAD 493M (~$362M).

The relevant share dynamic runs the other way: issued shares fell from ~195.6M at the start of 2023 to 153.9M at 3/31/26, a 21% reduction, with $1,706.8M of authorization remaining through 2029.

## Growth Analysis

There is no volume growth thesis. FY2026 ammonia production is guided to ~9.5M tons versus 10.12M produced in FY2025, a ~6% reduction driven by Yazoo City. Q1 volumes fell 6%. Every dollar of incremental revenue since 2024 is price.

Forward earnings direction is negative. Consensus FY2027 EPS is $10.34 against a bottom-up FY2026 sum of roughly $14.98 (Q1 $2.89 + Q2 $4.73 + Q3E $3.80 + Q4E $3.56); note the stated annual FY2026 figure of $16.81 is inconsistent with that sum and appears stale relative to the post-Q2 quarterly cuts. Thirty-day revisions are negative in all four periods: current quarter -14.4%, next quarter -10.2%, current year -4.6%, next year -6.4%. The 90-day current-year figure remains +14.3%, so the deterioration is recent and pointed. This is the mirror image of the estimate-momentum pattern that would support an aggressive call.

Growth capital is real but distant. Blue Point is a ~$3.7B JV facility plus ~$550M of wholly-owned Common Facilities, implying a ~$2.03B total CF commitment of which roughly $273M has been contributed, leaving ~$1.76B through 2029. Funding is comfortable against $1.8-2.1B of annual free cash flow. First production is late-decade, and the technology risk is disclosed candidly: autothermal reformers "have not been widely used for ammonia production and not at the scale contemplated." Roughly one-third of estimated cost is imported material arriving in Louisiana mostly in 2028, exposed to steel and aluminum derivative tariffs that the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling did not disturb.

The decarbonization overlay is the most credible incremental earnings stream and remains small. CF booked $42M of 45Q credits in FY2025 (Donaldsonville CCS in service from July 2025) and $24M in Q1 2026 alone, recorded in other operating income rather than net sales — so it flatters operating earnings without appearing in revenue, gross margin or per-ton pricing. A single quarter should not be annualized, since credits scale with gross ammonia production and CO2 consumed in upgraded products, but the direction is upward, with Yazoo City CCS adding up to 500,000 metric tons/year from 2028. First premium low-carbon ammonia sales occurred in 2025 into Europe and Africa; CF discloses neither the premium nor the tonnage, which leaves the commercial value of the strategy unquantified. Policy support is wobbling on both pillars: EU CBAM took effect for nitrogen importers on January 1, 2026, but French and Italian officials are advocating suspension or exclusion for fertilizers, and a proposed US repeal of GHG emissions reporting could, absent conforming IRS guidance, "delay or eliminate" credit realization.

Score: 4.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Nitrogen is a delivered-price commodity. Management concedes the products have "little or no product differentiation," with customers deciding "principally on the basis of delivered price." The moat is threefold: a North American gas cost advantage relative to the marginal global producer who sets the clearing price; an owned Midwest terminal, storage and deep-water docking network that is expensive to replicate; and scale in ammonia.

The first and most valuable leg is measurably eroding. Gas moved from 28% to 34% to 39% of production costs across FY2024, FY2025 and Q1 2026. The mechanism is disclosed in CF's own risk factors: increased US LNG exports following resumed permitting in 2025, plus Gulf Coast industrial gas demand. The Qatari outage aggravates this by pulling harder on US LNG cargoes. This is why the Q2 spread compressed even as nitrogen prices held — CF is long a commodity spread whose two legs are now correlated in the wrong direction, and it is essentially unhedged on the cost leg by deliberate policy.

Trade policy is redrawing flows in both directions. The EU imposed escalating tariffs on Russian nitrogen from July 2025, tightening European supply in CF's favor, but the European Commission extended definitive anti-dumping duties through January 6, 2031 on EU imports of UAN made in Russia, Trinidad and the United States — a direct constraint on arbitraging CF's largest-margin segment into the tightest market. The February 2026 tariff reconstruction exempts most fertilizer products (urea, UAN, AN) but not ammonia, and preserves USMCA treatment protecting Medicine Hat and Courtright.

Latent supply is the structural overhang: state-owned and subsidized producers with cheaper gas and higher tolerance for low returns; Chinese export restraint that could reverse on policy, currency or coal cost; and CF's own Blue Point adding to industry capacity late in the decade. Demand is narrower than it appears — US ethanol is ~35% of US corn demand and is policy-set, so Renewable Fuel Standard changes transmit straight into nitrogen volumes, and renewable-diesel incentives that shift acreage from corn to soybeans reduce nitrogen intensity per acre.

Customer concentration is not improving. CHS was 13%, 12% and 13% of net sales across 2023-2025 and is contractually anchored through its CFN stake and purchase rights, so it will not decline through commercial diversification. Geographic and product mix shifts toward Europe and low-carbon products say something about market access; they say nothing about single-customer dependence.

Score: 5.0 — a real cost advantage on a deteriorating trajectory, partially offset by 45Q monetization and European trade positioning.

## Risk Assessment

**Hormuz reopening is a direct revenue-deflation event.** The entire 2026 price structure rests on disrupted transit. If sustained commercial transit resumes, 25-40% of traded ammonia and urea supply returns while stranded Asian LNG-dependent producers restart. A reopening would also relieve US gas via reduced LNG pull, but the Q1 margin decomposition shows the asymmetry: +$401M from price against -$76M from gas. The price leg is roughly five times the cost leg. Net effect of normalization is materially negative.

**Antitrust litigation, newly disclosed.** On March 7, 2026 a putative class of direct purchasers of NPK fertilizer sued CF entities alongside Mosaic, Nutrien, Koch, Yara and Canpotex in the Northern District of Illinois alleging a Sherman Act conspiracy, with parallel direct- and indirect-purchaser complaints in other districts adding Clayton Act, state antitrust, consumer-protection and unjust-enrichment claims. Class periods generally allege conduct beginning in 2020 or 2021; plaintiffs seek treble and punitive damages. A JPML consolidation hearing was set for May 28, 2026. CF disputes the allegations and "is unable to estimate the amount or range of potential losses, if any." Industry-wide fertilizer antitrust suits frequently resolve without material liability, and the alleged class periods predate the 2026 spike — the existence of a complaint is not evidence of conduct. But it is unaccrued, unquantified, and it did not exist in the 10-K's risk factors.

**Political price intervention, structurally correlated with the earnings driver.** A December 6, 2025 Executive Order directs the Attorney General and FTC each to establish Food Supply Chain Security Task Forces investigating price-fixing and anti-competitive practices "including fertilizer." The Q1 10-Q states that following the Hormuz closure and resulting price increases, "the Trump administration and Congress have increased their scrutiny of the fertilizer industry, and the Trump administration has announced intentions to improve fertilizer costs for farmers." Grower organizations were reported in late July pressing agencies to accelerate the probe. The higher nitrogen prices go, the stronger CF's cash flow and the stronger the impulse to cap it — and the larger any claimed damages base.

**Yazoo City.** All site production idle until late Q4 2026 at the earliest; $25M fixed-asset impairment in FY2025; AN segment gross margin of -$11M (-19.0%) in Q1 on a 60% volume collapse, with the Other segment falling from 41.4% to 19.5%. Insurance covers property damage and business interruption, with $75M of initial proceeds received in April 2026, but BI recoveries are gain contingencies recognized only on realization, making quarterly earnings lumpy. Rebuild capex begins Q2 2026, is excluded from guidance, and is unquantified.

**Blue Point permitting.** The carbon chain depends on an Occidental/Enbridge JV sequestering ~2.3M metric tons/year in a Class VI well at the Pelican Sequestration Hub, "currently under development," while Louisiana imposed an indefinite moratorium in October 2025 on all new Class VI applications. Whether Pelican's application is pending (potentially grandfathered) or new is not disclosed. Without the well, 45Q eligibility and the low-carbon certification underpinning the JERA/Mitsui offtake are impaired. CF neither owns the well nor controls the permit.

**Other quantified items.** Donaldsonville is ~40% of ammonia capacity in a hurricane-exposed corridor alongside Waggaman and Blue Point; certain plants depend on a single gas pipeline. The $357M tax reserve with CAD 493M of cumulative deposits is a real cash drag with uncertain recovery timing. The 50%-owned PLNL Trinidad gas contract expires 1/1/2027 with supply beyond 2026 requiring renegotiation ($39M carrying value; CF buys 50% of its ammonia).

**Governance.** Six Item 5.02 filings between January 2025 and May 2026. The CFO seat was vacant for roughly four and a half months after Gregory Cameron's January 12, 2026 separation, with the Q1 10-Q signed by an interim principal financial officer; Andrew Scribner became CFO effective May 26, 2026. A CEO transition to Christopher Bohn occurred with an unconfirmed effective date, W. Anthony Will now signing Form 4s as a director. New SVP of Manufacturing appointed April 2026. The turnover brackets the Yazoo incident, though no causal link is disclosed and none should be inferred.

Score: 3.5. Several of these are binary, unaccrued, and negatively correlated with the earnings driver.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($155, +31%).** The disruption persists. Qatar has said restoring full LNG capacity will take "multiple years," which strands Asian nitrogen capacity independent of any Hormuz settlement, and CF's own 10-Q says the constraints "will persist in the near-term." Yazoo City restarts in Q4 2026 with insurance covering the majority of the rebuild, restoring ~6% of ammonia volume and the AN/Other margins into a still-tight market. Gas normalizes toward $4 as the winter spike rolls off, restoring the spread. FY2027 EPS lands near $14 rather than $10.34, and at 11x the stock is $154. Optionality: $1.71B of buyback authorization available through 2029 into any weakness, 45Q credits scaling from a $96M annualized Q1 rate toward more as Yazoo CCS comes online in 2028, and a European trade position tightened by Russian import duties.

**Bear case ($90, -24%).** Sustained Hormuz transit is verified, Middle East ammonia and urea return to the seaborne market, and the marginal cost of the last ton falls. Realized prices retrace toward 2024's $313/ton against a gas cost structure that stays at 35%+ of production cost because US LNG export demand is structural. The 2015-2017 precedent (ASP -34% over two years on new capacity) reasserts, aided by Blue Point and any relaxation of Chinese export restraint. FY2027 EPS undershoots to $8; at 11x that is $88. Yazoo rebuild capex plus a partial insurance shortfall consumes $200-300M. The antitrust docket advances toward class certification while a federal action to lower farmer input costs caps realized pricing. The 52-week low of $75.42 defines the tail.

**Base case ($120, +1.6%).** Partial normalization through 2027. Blended: 11.5x FY2027 consensus $10.34 gives $119; 7.9x the FY2026 bottom-up ~$15.0 gives $118. Both land at approximately $120, with an 11.5% attributable FCF yield and a 2.0% dividend providing support and the buyback authorization capping downside momentum. The current price is already at base case, which is why this is a HOLD rather than either an accumulation or an exit.

**On the score trajectory.** Scores have fallen from 5.9 in June to 5.2 in July, with valuation, management quality and growth each stepping down. The Q2 miss and the negative 30-day revisions confirm rather than reverse that direction on growth. We are not extending the decline mechanically: financial health is stronger than the earlier screens implied once the NCI is correctly separated from liabilities, and the documented buyback discipline argues management quality was cut too far at 4.5. The composite lands at 5.1 — a fifth consecutive HOLD, held because the two sides of the case are genuinely balanced rather than because the evidence is thin.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** Q2 2026 10-Q filing (expected mid-August 2026) for segment volumes, realized gas cost, cash conversion and the first Yazoo rebuild capex disclosure; the Q3 print in early November 2026 for the Yazoo restart schedule and 2027 pricing commentary; the consolidated antitrust docket following JPML action; and any verified sustained resumption of commercial Hormuz transit, which can arrive at any time.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price at or below $95 without fundamental deterioration — 9.2x FY2027 consensus $10.34 and a ~14% attributable FCF yield, in the zone where management itself was repurchasing stock in early 2026.
2. Two consecutive quarters with realized cost of gas below $4.00/MMBtu while average selling price holds above $400/ton — evidence the spread is widening rather than compressing, which is the specific mechanism the Q2 miss called into question.
3. Yazoo City restart confirmed for Q4 2026 with rebuild capex quantified and insurance recovery covering the majority, accompanied by resumption of program buybacks below $110.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Verified sustained Hormuz reopening (72+ hours of uninterrupted commercial transit) accompanied by ammonia or urea realized prices falling more than 20% from Q1 2026 levels.
2. FY2027 consensus cut below $9.00, or a second consecutive quarter of double-digit adjusted EPS misses.
3. Class certification, a recorded accrual, or a settlement framework in the NPK antitrust litigation; or a federal action that directly caps fertilizer pricing.
4. Yazoo restart slipping beyond Q1 2027, or rebuild capex net of insurance exceeding $300M.

**Entry sensitivity.** If the extended-hours $109.30 print holds into regular trading, base-case upside widens to roughly 10% and downside to bear narrows to 18%. That is an improvement, not a change of thesis; we would want the $90s before adding.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** CF is a well-financed, low-cost operator earning peak profits from a supply shock it does not control, with the shock's beneficiary and its principal risks driven by the same variable. The balance sheet supports patience — 0.4-0.5x net leverage, no maturities before 2034, an undrawn revolver, 11.5% attributable free cash flow yield, and a 21% smaller share count than three years ago. Capital allocation has been better than the sector norm: buying below $100, abstaining above $130, preserving $1.71B of authorization, and raising the dividend 20% instead.

What prevents a constructive call is the combination of a 16.6% Q2 miss, negative 30-day estimate revisions across every forecast period, gas rising to 39% of production costs against an essentially unhedged position, a 6% volume reduction from Yazoo City with unquantified rebuild capex, and a consensus FY2027 number that already embeds a roughly one-third earnings decline from the FY2026 bottom-up base. The single-digit current-year multiple reflects those facts rather than contradicting them. At $118.11 the stock sits at our base case with roughly symmetric scenarios. Existing holders collect a 2.0% dividend and wait for the Q2 10-Q; new capital should wait for the $90s or for evidence the gas-to-nitrogen spread is widening again.
