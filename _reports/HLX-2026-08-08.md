## Executive Summary

Helix is no longer primarily a question about offshore well intervention. On April 22, 2026 the company signed an all-stock merger agreement with Hornbeck Offshore Services under which Hornbeck securityholders receive 10.27167 Helix shares each and end up owning approximately 55% of the combined company on a fully diluted, as-converted basis. Every regulatory gate has cleared (FTC early termination June 11, Brazil's CADE June 11/26, Poland's UOKiK May 28, U.K. ISU July 9), Hornbeck's own holders consented by written consent on announcement day, and the Form S-4 was declared effective July 31 with the 424(b)(3) filed the same day. The Helix special meeting is scheduled for **August 31, 2026**, with closing expected **September 1, 2026**. The entity will be renamed Hornbeck Offshore Services and trade as HOS. Todd Hornbeck becomes CEO; Hornbeck takes four of seven board seats; Helix retains the chairmanship. A purchase of HLX today is a decision to own 45% of a combined company whose majority component's revenue, EBITDA and debt could not be verified from primary sources within this research scope.

The standalone case is better than it was three months ago. Q2 2026 continuing-operations revenue rose 21% to $304.0M, gross margin went to 18.5% from 5.4%, and continuing Adjusted EBITDA doubled to $74.7M on well-intervention vessel utilization of 91% versus 72%. The balance sheet holds $652.2M of cash against $304.3M of debt, $347.9M net cash, and $716.5M of total liquidity; there is no goodwill. On disclosed numbers the equity trades at roughly 3.9x LTM EV/Adjusted EBITDA (5.0x adding on-balance-sheet operating leases) and 0.91x tangible book. Three offsets matter. The utilization recovery is measured against a 2025 comparison depressed by a 57-day Q5000 docking, a 45-day Q4000 demobilization and a warm-stacked *Seawell*. 1H 2026 operating cash flow of $115.7M reflects a ~$110M year-over-year swing in receivables (a $38.2M decrease this year against a $71.3M increase last year) plus deferred certification additions of only $8.4M versus $30.6M, against guided remaining-2026 spend of $30-35M certification plus $28-33M capex. And Robotics has a company-disclosed hole: the *Grand Canyon II* repositioned to the North Sea for a trenching contract that does not start until 2027, while its charter runs to December 2030 within $705.7M of total charter commitments, $162.1M of which falls due inside twelve months.

We maintain HOLD, and classify it as an uncertain hold rather than a growth or income hold. Base case $10.50 (+7.0%), bull $13.25 (+35.1%), bear $7.50 (−23.5%). The probability-weighted return is modestly positive but the dispersion is unusually wide for a $1.4B market cap, and the single largest driver — the pro forma leverage and earnings of the surviving company — is not underwritable from the evidence available. New capital has no reason to initiate ahead of the vote. Holders who cannot form a view on Hornbeck's balance sheet have a defensible reason to trim into the event even though we are not rating the stock REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Helix is a Houston-based offshore energy services company. Following the May 1, 2026 sale of Helix Alliance (the entire Shallow Water Abandonment segment) to C-Dive, L.L.C. for $107.5M cash, the continuing business runs three segments.

**Well Intervention** generated $417.5M of 1H 2026 revenue, 73% of the continuing total. Seven purpose-built vessels — the semisubmersible-derived *Q4000*, *Q5000* and *Q7000*, the North Sea monohulls *Seawell* and *Well Enhancer*, and the chartered Brazil units *Sea Helix 1* and *Siem Helix 2* — work with 12 intervention systems. The commercial premise is that a purpose-built vessel performs production enhancement and well abandonment more cheaply than a drilling rig.

**Robotics** (1H 2026: part of the $570.7M continuing total; Q2 revenue $76.4M) operates 41 work-class ROVs, six trenchers and three boulder grabs off a fleet of chartered support vessels. This is where offshore-wind exposure sits.

**Production Facilities** ($48.5M in 1H 2026) comprises the *Helix Producer I*, the Fast Response System well-containment offering, and direct working interests in two mature Gulf of America fields, Droshky and Thunder Hawk.

Management frames demand through three market strategies. For 1H 2026: decommissioning $282.2M (49% of revenue, +23% year over year), production maximization $192.6M (34%, −8%), renewables $84.2M (15%, +43%). Decommissioning becoming the largest pool matters because abandonment spending is regulatorily compelled rather than discretionary, which decouples a growing share of revenue from the oil price.

FY2025 was a step backward: revenue −4.9%, gross profit −27.5%, operating income roughly halved. The 10-K attributes most of it to Well Intervention, whose gross margin fell to about 6% from about 13%, driven by the stacked *Seawell*, the *Q5000* docking, the *Q4000* demobilization and a shut-in Thunder Hawk field that also took an $18.1M non-cash impairment in Q4. Reported net income of $30.8M was helped by SG&A falling $15.8M and a lower tax provision ($11.7M versus $26.4M). The tax line is chronically punitive for this company: in FY2023 pretax income of $7.5M carried an $18.4M provision, producing a net loss; the FY2025 effective rate was 27.4% and Q2 2026 was 31.9%.

**Q2 2026 (continuing operations).** Revenue $304.0M versus $251.7M; gross profit $56.2M versus $13.5M (18.5% versus 5.4%); income from operations $26.7M versus a $3.0M loss after $8.3M of transaction costs; continuing Adjusted EBITDA $74.7M versus $36.6M. Segment detail: Well Intervention revenue $208.1M (+33%) with an 11% margin against a prior-year 8% gross loss; Production Facilities $29.7M (+74%) at 54% margin versus 28%; Robotics $76.4M (−11%) with margin compressing to 22% from 25% as chartered support-vessel utilization fell to 69% on six vessels from 95% on seven.

Two items keep Q2's 18.5% margin from being a run rate in either direction: the *Q7000* transited to West Africa in May-June with revenue and mobilization costs deferred, and *Sea Helix 1* began a five-year regulatory docking in mid-June.

**Cash flow quality.** 1H 2026 operating cash flow was $115.7M against negative $0.7M a year earlier, with $10.0M of capex, producing $105.7M of total free cash flow. This is not annualizable. Receivables decreased $38.2M this year versus increasing $71.3M last year — a ~$110M swing, roughly $63.3M of which is the mechanical removal of Helix Alliance receivables from the consolidated balance sheet (permanently smaller working capital, not a repeating source). Deferred regulatory certification additions were $8.4M versus $30.6M, against guided remaining-2026 spend of $30-35M. Quarterly Adjusted EBITDA is markedly seasonal (Q3 is the peak: $88M in 2024, $104M in 2025; Q1 is the trough: $32M in 2026). FY2025 free cash flow of $120.4M is the better anchor than a 1H annualization.

A definitional trap worth naming: FY2025 reported capex of $16.3M against $3.02B of gross PP&E does not represent the maintenance burden. Helix defers regulatory certification and drydock costs — every roughly 30 months per vessel, $5-15M each — as an asset amortized through operating cash flow rather than investing capex. The deferred net asset was $56.1M at 6/30/26 versus $70.0M at 12/31/25, with 1H amortization of $19.7M against $8.4M of additions. Management's own capital-additions guidance is $70-80M, roughly 4.3x the reported capex line. Free cash flow computed as OCF minus reported capex is not overstated (the recertification cash already sits inside OCF), but any capital-intensity or conversion analysis using $16.3M will misstate the business badly.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/26: cash $652.2M, total debt $304.3M carrying ($309.9M principal), net cash $347.9M, total liquidity $716.5M, current ratio 2.75, equity $1,583.7M, no goodwill. Net cash has built from $52.9M (12/31/24) to $137.2M (12/31/25) to $347.9M — with the Q2 step-up substantially one-time ($104.2M of divestiture proceeds plus the receivable release).

Debt is $300M of 9.75% senior unsecured notes due March 2029 (callable from 3/1/26 at 104.875%, fair value $314.6M at 6/30/26) plus $9.9M of 4.93% MARAD debt amortizing to February 2027 and collateralized by the *Q4000*. The $120M ABL matures August 2029 with no borrowings; availability fell from $110.9M to $66.9M as the borrowing base shrank post-divestiture. The springing 1.0x fixed-charge covenant is not triggered.

The leverage that matters is in charters, not debt. On-balance-sheet operating lease liabilities were $306.1M, but disclosed total operating lease commitments are $705.7M ($162.1M due within twelve months) because charter contracts bundle a services component — approximately $339.9M of which is not on the balance sheet. Recognized obligations of $610.4M sit against $652.2M of cash; including the undiscounted services piece, contractual debt-plus-charter obligations run roughly $950M to $1.01B. Charter hire is payable regardless of vessel employment, which is precisely what makes the Robotics gap expensive.

Returns remain weak in absolute terms: ROA 1.2%, ROE 2.0%, driven by heavy depreciation and a 9.75% coupon. The Altman Z of 2.58 sits in the grey zone with EBIT/assets of 0.025 as the binding component; for a vessel owner with $652M of cash and no drawn revolver, that reads as low earning power rather than solvency stress.

One deliberate inefficiency: $300M at 9.75% costs $29.25M of annual cash coupon while 1H interest income of $6.7M on average cash of roughly $549M implies about a 2.4% yield — roughly $22M per year of negative carry. Two rational explanations coexist: preserving liquidity into a transformational merger, and waiting for the indenture's change-of-control repurchase offer at 101% rather than paying the 104.875% call price. Note that with the notes marked at approximately 105% of par (fair value $314.6M), a 101% offer would be uneconomic for holders to accept, so the change-of-control provision is unlikely to produce a material cash call.

## Growth Analysis

The genuine growth is in decommissioning and international renewables. Decommissioning revenue of $282.2M (+23%) is regulatorily compelled work, and the U.K.'s stronger abandonment enforcement is a durable driver even as the Energy Profits Levy at 38% through March 2030 suppresses discretionary North Sea spending — visible in Q1 2026 North Sea well-intervention utilization of 47%. Renewables revenue of $84.2M (+43%) is entirely international: the January 2025 U.S. OCS leasing withdrawal and the DOI pause on large-scale projects have frozen the domestic opportunity, while Europe and Asia Pacific remain active. NKT in the backlog and 137 of 374 Q2 chartered vessel days on boulder-grab site clearance corroborate that mix.

Against this sit four measurable negatives. Backlog fell from $1.3B at 12/31/25 to roughly $1.1B at 6/30/26, with only $420.9M attributable to the remainder of 2026, $449.8M to 2027 and $269.8M to 2028+; the company states backlog is not a reliable revenue indicator and that certain contracts are cancelable without penalty, so this is disclosed unsatisfied performance obligations rather than contracted revenue. Production maximization revenue fell 8%. Robotics has the dated 2H26-1H27 utilization gap already described. And consensus estimates are drifting down: current-year EPS $0.29 (−5.5% over 30 days, +10.3% over 90), next-year $0.54 (−2.7% / −0.5%), current-quarter $0.20 (−7.1% / +2.6%). Only next-quarter estimates rose meaningfully (+24.1% / +44.0%) on a base of three analysts. The >30% ninety-day revision threshold that would flag a genuine earnings inflection is not present; the aggregate signal is mildly negative.

FY2026 guidance is stale and unusable. The April 2026 ranges (revenue $1,200-1,400M, Adjusted EBITDA $230-290M, capital additions $70-80M, FCF $100-160M) predate the divestiture and include $160-190M of now-sold Shallow Water Abandonment revenue that carried negative EBITDA (−$9.9M in 1H 2026). No reissued post-divestiture guidance was located. As a labeled sensitivity rather than a forecast: if 2H continuing Adjusted EBITDA merely repeats 1H's $112.0M, FY2026 continuing Adjusted EBITDA is about $224M; with normal Q3 seasonality it could reach $250-280M.

The merger's $75M synergy target — "$75 million or more in annual revenue and cost synergies within three years" — includes "reducing reliance on third-party vessel charters," which is the most mechanically credible piece of the rationale given Helix's $705.7M of charter commitments against Hornbeck's owned OSV/MPSV fleet. It is a three-year target with management indicating initial contributions in year one.

The earnings-surprise record argues for modest confidence in point estimates: one beat and three misses across the last four quarters (Q1 2026 −16.6%, Q4 2025 +761%, Q3 2025 −17.9%, Q2 2025 −166%). Quarterly outcomes here are driven by drydock timing and mobilization, which sell-side models track poorly.

**Growth Prospects: 5.0.**

## Valuation Assessment

At $9.81 on 147.38M shares, market capitalization is $1,446M and enterprise value is $1,098M ($1,446M + $304.3M debt − $652.2M cash).

- **LTM Adjusted EBITDA (total basis)** = FY2025 $272.0M − 1H25 $94.4M + 1H26 $102.1M = **$279.7M** → **EV/EBITDA 3.9x**; adding $306.1M of operating leases to EV, **5.0x**.
- On a continuing-operations basis, LTM Adjusted EBITDA is approximately **$295-298M** (Helix Alliance contributed negative EBITDA in both periods) → roughly **3.7x**, **4.7x lease-adjusted**. This is an estimate; FY2025 discontinued-operations Adjusted EBITDA was not separately disclosed.
- **Price/tangible book** = $1,446M / $1,583.7M = **0.91x**.
- **FCF yield** on FY2025's $120.4M = **8.3%**.
- **Forward P/E** = 33.8x on current-year consensus $0.29 and 18.2x on next-year $0.54.

A data conflict requires flagging: the supplied trailing P/E of 100.9x is irreconcilable with the supplied trailing EPS of $0.20765, which at $9.81 implies 47.2x. Neither is a useful anchor for a company whose GAAP earnings are compressed by depreciation on $3.02B of gross PP&E, a high and volatile tax rate, and a 9.75% coupon. EV/EBITDA, lease-adjusted EV/EBITDA, and price/tangible book are the defensible metrics.

Peer medians in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services are unhelpfully dispersed (AESI −20.7x, VTOL 11.2x, RES 76.6x, INVX 38.2x, CLB 21.5x; median 38.2x), reflecting a heterogeneous group at different cycle positions. Price/book with net cash and no goodwill is the cleaner cross-check, and 0.91x for a fleet owner earning 2.0% ROE is neither obviously cheap nor obviously expensive — it prices approximately the return on capital the business currently generates.

The instinct to call 3.9x EV/EBITDA cheap deserves resistance. Three qualifications apply. First, the lease-adjusted figure of 5.0x is the economically correct one, because $705.7M of charter obligations are fixed against variable utilization. Second, LTM EBITDA is measured across a period whose prior-year half was depressed by planned downtime; the recovery is real but the base is flattering. Third, and decisively, the multiple applies to an entity that ceases to exist in roughly three weeks. After September 1, holders own 45% of a company whose other 55% we cannot value: Hornbeck's revenue, EBITDA and debt are unverified, and the joint press release's own risk language contemplates "an inability to de-leverage on the expected timeline, or at all" — a phrase that cannot be reconciled with Helix's standalone $347.9M net cash and most plausibly refers to debt arriving with Hornbeck. That is an inference from risk-factor drafting, not an established fact, and it is the single largest gap in the analysis.

Deal arithmetic, derived: if 147.4M Helix shares equal 45% of the combined fully diluted count, total combined shares are approximately 327.5M, new shares issued approximately 180.1M (a ~122% increase in share count), implied Hornbeck fully diluted shares approximately 17.5M, implied combined equity value at $9.81 approximately $3.21B, with roughly $1.77B ascribed to Hornbeck's 55%. Two dilution measures should not be conflated: share count rises ~122%, while existing holders' ownership falls from 100% to ~45%.

Analyst targets (five contributors, three strong buy, one buy, one hold, stable) span $9.00 to $14.00 with a $12.50 mean and $13.50 median. At a 33.8x current-year multiple with falling revisions, these are not the profile in which consensus targets function as floors; they are more likely standalone-basis marks that have not been rebuilt on pro forma Hornbeck economics.

**Valuation: 5.0** — modestly better than our prior 4.5 because Q2 lifted LTM EBITDA faster than the price rose, but the cheapness is not underwritable while 55% of the surviving entity is opaque.

## Competitive Landscape

The purpose-built intervention fleet is difficult to replicate — the *Q7000* is bespoke, and Jones Act qualification is a genuine regulatory barrier in the Gulf of America. The moat has a hard ceiling, however, stated plainly in the filings: the substitute asset class is the drilling rig. When operators hold rigs under existing long-term contracts, marginal intervention work migrates to those rigs at near-zero incremental commitment, and rig day rates set the pricing benchmark. Helix competes against a much larger capacity pool it does not control, which is the better explanation for FY2025's Well Intervention margin collapse to ~6% than any Helix-specific pricing failure.

Customer concentration has not improved even as end-market mix has. FY2025 revenue was ~18% Shell and ~10% Petrobras. Backlog concentration was six customers at ~82% at 12/31/25 and approximately 80% at 6/30/26 across Shell, Subsea 7, Petrobras, Talos, NKT and CNR — within a backlog that shrank from $1.3B to ~$1.1B. Two different things are being measured; only the mix diversified. Note also that Subsea 7 is simultaneously a competitor in subsea construction and robotics and a top backlog counterparty, so part of the backlog depends on a rival's own project awards.

Contract visibility is uneven across the fleet. The Brazil charters are well covered (*Sea Helix 1* with Petrobras into Q4 2028, *Siem Helix 2* into Q1 2028). The two highest-value assets are not: management identifies the *Q4000* and *Q7000* as carrying ongoing utilization uncertainty, with the *Q4000* having schedule gaps between contracts and the *Q7000* relocating to West Africa on identified but not fully contracted opportunities.

Post-merger, the competitive position changes character. The combined company would own rather than charter a large share of its tonnage, span cabotage-protected U.S. markets plus Brazil, Mexico, the North Sea, West Africa and Asia Pacific, and add U.S. government and offshore-wind exposure through Hornbeck. It would compete with Tidewater, Edison Chouest, SEACOR Marine and Bourbon in OSVs and with Subsea 7, TechnipFMC, Oceaneering, DOF and Saipem in subsea services. Whether the differentiated intervention franchise is strengthened by asset ownership or diluted inside a larger OSV operator is the open question, and Helix holders will not control the answer.

**Competitive Position: 5.0.**

## Risk Assessment

**Merger and control transfer.** The Q2 10-Q added two risk factors relative to the FY2025 10-K: merger-related litigation, and non-consummation or delay. The August 31 vote is the last real gate; completion probability appears high. The exchange ratio is fixed with no collar, so Helix holders bear full relative-value risk. If the deal breaks, the reference points for a reset are instructive: RSUs were granted at a $6.27 grant-date fair value on January 1, 2026, PSUs at $6.93, director grants at approximately $7.25 in December 2025, and the COO sold at $6.879 in November 2025. Any "return to pre-announcement" bear case must acknowledge that oil is also materially higher now, so a break does not mechanically return the stock to $6.50.

**Change-of-control exposure.** The notes' 101% repurchase provision is unlikely to bind (fair value approximately 105% of par). The more material item is the press release's own risk language naming "the ability of certain customers to terminate or amend contracts upon a change of control" — acutely relevant with ~80% backlog concentration in six counterparties. Whether the merger constitutes a change of control under the indenture, and how notes and ABL are treated at closing, is unresolved; the 10-Q references "potential breakage fees" without specification.

**Fixed charter hire against variable utilization.** $705.7M of commitments, $162.1M within twelve months, payable regardless of employment. The *Grand Canyon II* sitting idle on a December 2030 charter until a 2027 contract start is this risk crystallizing, and it is company-specific rather than macro. Partial offsets exist: the *Shelia Bordelon* charter was set to expire at end-Q2 2026 if not extended, and the *North Sea Enabler* took a two-year extension from July 2026.

**Oil and gas working-interest liabilities.** Recorded ARO was $71.9M at 6/30/26; undiscounted obligations are $80.9M for Thunder Hawk and $37.1M for Droshky, partially offset by a $30.0M undiscounted Marathon Oil receivable carried at $28.6M. Thunder Hawk took an $18.1M impairment in Q4 2025, was shut in through 2025, and its workover drove Production Facilities to a −40% gross margin in Q1 2026 before recovering to +54% in Q2. This is real earnings volatility unrelated to the services franchise.

**Policy and jurisdiction.** U.K. Energy Profits Levy at 38% through March 2030 suppresses North Sea discretionary spend while accelerating abandonment. The January 2025 U.S. OCS wind leasing withdrawal and DOI pause remain in litigation. Jones Act dependence cuts both ways.

**Macro whipsaw.** The 10-Q describes oil entering 2026 in the mid-$50s and rising sharply after the March 2026 closure of the Strait of Hormuz, remaining elevated and volatile since, with management expecting normalization once transit resumes. A Marine Link headline dated August 4, 2026 ("Oil Rises as Uncertainty Clouds US-Iran Peace Talks") corroborates that the situation remained unresolved as of that date. Management does not treat the tailwind as durable, and we do not either. Helix's decommissioning weighting partially insulates revenue from the oil price in both directions.

**Post-close overhang.** Registration Rights and Securityholders Agreements dated April 22, 2026 give Hornbeck's concentrated holders — Ares Management funds among them — contractual rights in the combined company. Registration rights over a 55% position create a plausible future equity overhang, though the agreements were not read and standard post-merger mechanics is an equally available reading.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 70.3% against 44.2% one-year realized volatility, with backwardation into a 49.2% six-month tenor, indicates the market is pricing an imminent, dated event. The signal quality is poor: the chain carries only 25 contracts across three expirations, put/call open-interest ratio reads 0.00 (essentially no open put interest, which contradicts the reported 6.2% put-demand skew), and put/call volume is 0.08. The most usable reading is the September 18 tenor — the one that brackets the August 31 vote and September 1 close — which shows an 8.8% put skew and a 12.2% implied move at a more sober 44.6% IV. Meanwhile ATM IV rose from 56.1% to 68.1% over roughly three weeks on a flat price, consistent with rising uncertainty into the event rather than directional conviction. Short interest of 6.3% of float and 4.2 days to cover is unremarkable and neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view. Treat the options complex as corroborating event risk around the vote, not as evidence about direction.

**Risk Factors: 3.8.** The stack is heavy, much of it dated inside four weeks, and the largest component — pro forma leverage of the surviving company — is unquantified.

## Management Assessment

Owen Kratz's beneficial holding of approximately 7.77M shares (about 5.3% of shares outstanding) is a substantial, long-tenured alignment. The pay-versus-performance mechanism functions as designed: with the TSR index falling from 221.9 to 149.3 in 2025, PEO compensation actually paid went to negative $2.02M and average NEO compensation actually paid to negative $0.44M. Performance measures are Adjusted EBITDA, TSR and free cash flow, a defensible set for a capital-intensive cyclical. Over the five-year window, however, the company TSR index of 149.3 trailed its own selected peer index of 181.7.

Capital allocation shows a real positive and a real question. The positive: selling Helix Alliance for $107.5M when that segment produced negative $9.9M of Adjusted EBITDA on $29.3M of 1H 2026 revenue (a $14.6M gross loss, worsening from a $10.1M loss on $67.4M a year earlier) was good discipline, and $30.2M of FY2025 buybacks at prices well below today's reduced average basic shares from 152.0M to 148.3M. The question: long-term incentives are heavily cash-settled — RSUs are liability-classified and excluded from diluted EPS, the 2023 PSU tranche vested at 151% and paid $4.6M in cash on 489,498 units in Q1 2026, and fixed-value cash awards of $5.9M were granted in 1H 2026 after $5.7M in FY2025. That suppresses dilution but creates a recurring cash cost that Adjusted EBITDA presentations obscure and weakens equity alignment. Approximately 7.9M shares remain available under the plan; those are contingent and do not belong in a current fully diluted count.

Repurchases were suspended effective April 22, 2026 with $128.4M remaining under authorization, removing the only active shareholder-return mechanism through closing. There is no dividend.

The merger itself is the dominant management judgment. Economically it is a sale of control without a cash premium, at a fixed ratio with no collar, with Helix's CEO not surviving in the top role and the surviving name, ticker and CEO all Hornbeck's. Helix retains the chair and three of seven seats — meaningful minority protection. Retaining Veriten as independent strategic advisor alongside Goldman Sachs is consistent with a board seeking additional validation where its own management does not continue; it is equally consistent with wanting sector-specific strategic input. Insider activity is thin and low-signal: one open-market sale (COO Sparks, 40,000 shares at $6.879 in November 2025, leaving 301,042) and no open-market purchases, with buying typically restricted during a pending registered merger.

**Management Quality: 5.0.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $13.25 (+35.1%)

The combined company is a scaled, Jones Act-qualified offshore operator with owned tonnage replacing a meaningful share of Helix's $705.7M of third-party charters. The synergy channel is mechanical rather than aspirational: internalizing chartered capacity converts fixed hire into owned utilization, and management expects initial contributions within year one of the $75M three-year target. Helix's continuing business is inflecting — 91% well-intervention utilization, 18.5% Q2 gross margin, decommissioning revenue up 23% into a regulatorily compelled demand pool, international renewables up 43%. Helix arrives with $347.9M of net cash and no goodwill, at 0.91x tangible book. If continuing Adjusted EBITDA reaches $320M in a first full combined year with synergies beginning, roughly 5.9x lease-adjusted EV/EBITDA supports approximately $13.10 per pre-merger Helix share, close to the $13.50 analyst median. Sustained oil above $80 extends the production-maximization recovery that lagged in 1H.

### Bear Case — $7.50 (−23.5%)

Helix holders trade control for 45% of an entity whose leverage is disclosed only through a forward-looking risk factor warning of "an inability to de-leverage on the expected timeline, or at all." If Hornbeck carries material debt, Helix's net cash becomes repayment capacity for the majority party rather than optionality for Helix holders, and the market marks the combined equity down once pro forma leverage is visible. Ares registration rights over a 55% position create an equity overhang. Approximately 0.70x tangible book gives $7.52.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** The classification is deliberate. This is not a growth hold — estimate revisions are negative and the standalone growth story has a dated Robotics gap. It is not an income hold — there is no dividend and the buyback is suspended. It is a hold because the decisive variable, the pro forma economics of the surviving company, is not knowable from the evidence in hand, and neither an upgrade nor a REDUCE can be defended on that basis.

**Reassessment timeline:** the August 31, 2026 special meeting and expected September 1 close, then the first combined-company quarterly report (likely early November 2026) with pro forma segment detail and post-close leverage.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. The 424(b)(3) pro formas or the first post-close disclosure show combined net leverage below approximately 2.0x with a credible path to the $75M synergy target, at a lease-adjusted combined EV/EBITDA below 4.5x.

Existing holders who cannot form an independent view on Hornbeck's balance sheet have a defensible reason to trim into the event, particularly given that the September tenor prices a 12.2% move with elevated put skew; we are not rating the stock REDUCE because the probability-weighted return is not negative, only wide.

## Conclusion

Helix presents an unusual scoring problem: a business that is operationally improving, a balance sheet that is genuinely strong on a net-cash basis, a valuation that screens cheap on every conventional metric, and a corporate event three weeks away that renders all of it secondary. Q2 2026 was a real quarter — 91% utilization, an 18.5% gross margin, $74.7M of continuing Adjusted EBITDA — but it runs against a comparison depressed by planned downtime, and 1H free cash flow of $105.7M was inflated by a ~$110M working-capital swing and abnormally low drydock spend that reverses in 2H. Set against $705.7M of charter commitments payable regardless of utilization, a backlog that has fallen to ~$1.1B with only $420.9M for the rest of 2026, and a disclosed Robotics revenue gap into 2027, the standalone case at 5.0x lease-adjusted EV/EBITDA and 0.91x tangible book is fair rather than compelling.

The merger dominates. Approval and closing appear highly likely — all clearances obtained, Hornbeck's holders consented, the S-4 effective July 31 — and holders will own 45% of a company controlled by the counterparty, at a fixed exchange ratio with no collar and no cash premium. Whether that is a good outcome depends almost entirely on Hornbeck's leverage and earnings, which we could not verify, and the one textual hint available (the combined disclosure's warning about an inability to de-leverage) points in the less favorable direction without establishing it. Base case $10.50 against $9.81, bull $13.25, bear $7.50: approximately fair expected return with an unusually wide distribution and a dated binary in between.

**HOLD.** Overall score 5.0, unchanged from the prior three reviews. The stability is not inertia — Q2 improved the operating picture and the LTM multiple, while the risk score fell as the event approached and the information gap on 55% of the pro forma entity remained open. Revisit after the September 1 close, when the first pro forma leverage disclosure will make this a company that can actually be underwritten.
