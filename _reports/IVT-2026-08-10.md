## Executive Summary

InvenTrust Properties owns 78 open-air, grocery-anchored and power-center retail properties totaling 12.3 million square feet, essentially all in Sun Belt metros, with Texas at 35.2% of annualized base rent and Austin–Round Rock alone at 14.2%. The reported operating results look strong: Q2 2026 total income rose 12.6% to $82.8 million, Core FFO reached $0.48 per diluted share (+9.1%), same-property NOI grew 4.1%, and trailing-twelve-month blended releasing spreads were +11.1% on 1.3 million square feet. H1 2026 Core FFO of $0.98 was up 8.9% year over year.

The reported figures overstate the forward run rate, and management's own guidance says so. FY2026 Core FFO guidance was reaffirmed at $1.92–$1.96 while Nareit FFO and net income guidance were both raised. Against H1 actual of $0.98 and H2 2025 actual of $0.93, the reaffirmed range implies H2 2026 Core FFO of $0.94–$0.98, or +1.1% to +5.4% year over year against +8.9% in H1. Three mechanisms explain the step-down. First, growth is acquisition-driven, not organic: the Q2 lease-income bridge shows +$12.8 million from acquisitions, −$4.1 million from dispositions, and roughly +$0.5 million net from same properties, with same-property total income up only 1.5%. Second, roughly 48% of the Q2 same-property NOI increase came from a 9.2% decline in real estate taxes; holding that line flat, same-property NOI growth would have been about 2.2% rather than 4.1%. Third, interest expense is contractually escalating: $200 million of term loan rolls from a 2.66% effective rate to 4.50% on September 22, 2026, and a second $200 million tranche rolls from a 3.75% weighted average to 4.575% on March 22, 2027, a $5.33 million annualized step-up equal to $0.068 per diluted share, on top of the $250 million private placement funded June 29 at a 5.386% weighted-average coupon.

At $32.70 the stock is 16.9x the $1.94 guidance midpoint, a 6.4% implied cap rate on annualized most-recent-quarter NOI of $230.2 million, and a 3.05% dividend yield covered at 51.5% of Core FFO. That is roughly fair value for a portfolio delivering 1.5–2% organic revenue growth with a known 2027 interest drag and same-property leased occupancy down 130 basis points year over year. The offsetting positives are concrete: $489 million of liquidity, no debt maturing in the remainder of 2026 and only $26.8 million in 2027, covenant headroom of roughly 2x on every test, roughly $5.6 million of signed-but-not-open annualized rent (2.3% of ABR) with 77% expected to commence by year-end, and three redevelopments at 7–10% incremental yields. The 12% decline from the July 17 high of $37.22 removed the overvaluation that existed a month ago without changing guidance. HOLD.

## Company Overview

IVT operates as a single reportable segment — multi-tenant retail — and describes its strategy as owning grocery-anchored neighborhood and community centers plus power centers with a grocery component in high-growth Sun Belt markets. Portfolio count moved from 68 properties and 11.0 million square feet at year-end 2024 to 78 properties and 12.3 million square feet at June 30, 2026. ABR per square foot rose from $20.07 to $20.94 over the same period.

Two composition facts matter more than the branding. First, the portfolio splits into 7,583 thousand square feet of anchor space at $13.22 ABR/sf (98.1% leased) and 4,695 thousand square feet of small-shop space at $34.26 ABR/sf (93.2% leased); small-shop tenants are 60.6% of total ABR. The rent is concentrated in the more credit-fragile cohort. Second, grocery and drug tenants generate 17.4% of ABR, while quick-service and full-service restaurants combined are 21.1%, personal health and beauty services 10.8%, medical 9.7%, off-price 6.1% and apparel 6.0%. The grocer drives traffic; the cash flow is substantially food-and-beverage and services. Personal services and medical at 20.5% combined are genuinely e-commerce-resistant, but the aggregate discretionary sensitivity is higher than "necessity retail" implies.

Tenant diversification is adequate at the top: top-25 tenants are 31.3% of ABR and no single tenant exceeds 10% of lease income. Largest exposures are Kroger/Harris Teeter 3.3%, Publix 3.0%, TJX 2.6%, Amazon (Whole Foods) 2.1%, Albertsons 1.8%, H-E-B 1.8%. Speculative-grade names inside the top 25 total roughly 7–8% of ABR, including Michaels (B−) at 1.2% and Petco (B) at 0.8%.

The company also runs a captive insurance subsidiary formed in April 2023 covering wind, flood, named windstorm, earthquake and fire below deductibles, and since January 2025 the first layer of general liability. Unpaid loss liability was $392 thousand at June 30, 2026 — immaterial today, but it means IVT retains first-dollar catastrophe risk across a footprint with Florida at 20.7% of ABR plus coastal Carolinas and the Texas Gulf.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings quality and the right metric.** FY2025 net income of $111.4 million versus $13.7 million in 2024 is almost entirely the $90.9 million gain on the June 2025 sale of a five-property California portfolio for $306.0 million. GAAP net income is not the operating signal, which is why the reported 173x trailing/forward P/E in the market data (implying roughly $0.19 of EPS) is an accounting artifact rather than a valuation input; company guidance for 2026 net income is $0.12–$0.18 per share, and the $1.42 EPS field in the same dataset conflicts with that basis. For the same reason the four-quarter GAAP EPS surprise record (two beats, two misses, including a 79.2% miss in Q4 2025) carries little information — it measures depreciation and gain timing, not operations. Core FFO per diluted share is the company-selected performance measure and the correct lens: $1.40 (2021), $1.57, $1.65, $1.73, $1.83 (2025), a 6.9% four-year CAGR, guided to $1.92–$1.96 for 2026.

**Same-property decomposition.** Q2 2026 same-property NOI rose $1,915 thousand (+4.1%) to $48.5 million. Of that, $994 thousand came from income (+1.5%, with minimum base rent +3.1% but real estate tax recoveries −8.5% and the credit-loss provision worsening 63.5%) and $921 thousand from lower operating expenses, of which real estate taxes contributed $879 thousand (−9.2%). Neutralizing the tax line puts underlying growth near 2.2%. The H1 figure is cleaner — same-property NOI +3.3% with $2,765 thousand of the $3,143 thousand increase from income — so Q2 may be the anomaly rather than the trend. Either way, organic revenue growth of 1.5–2.1% is the operating reality behind 12.6% headline revenue growth.

**Occupancy.** Total economic occupancy fell 90 basis points year over year to 94.6% and leased occupancy 110 basis points to 96.2%. The same-property pool tells the same story on a near-identical base (10,243 thousand versus 10,225 thousand square feet): economic occupancy −80 basis points to 94.7%, leased occupancy −130 basis points to 96.0%. Acquisitions arriving at 98–100% leased (Nashville West 98.0%, Sweetgrass 100%, Western Plaza 98.6%, 3609 South 100%) mask the same-store softening. Specific weak assets: Gateway Market Center (Tampa, 231 thousand sf) at 64.0% leased, Garden Village (Los Angeles) 76.0%, Westpark (Richmond) 81.3% following loss of the Painted Tree anchor. Six vacant big boxes exist, four tied to redevelopment or disposition plans — intentional vacancy rather than distress in those four cases.

**Balance sheet.** This is the largest change in the case. Company-reported net debt/Adjusted EBITDA moved 4.1x (12/31/24) → 4.5x (12/31/25) → 5.5x TTM at 6/30/26 (5.3x quarter-annualized), with total debt rising $269.6 million in six months to $1,092.2 million. The TTM figure overstates run-rate leverage because the two June acquisitions ($116.0 million combined) contributed under two weeks of EBITDA; a full-period adjustment plus the July 1 New Garden Crossing acquisition at an assumed 6.2% NOI yield puts pro forma leverage near 5.2x. That is an estimate, not a disclosure. Management's stated path to at or below 5x as assets season is credible but unproven. The FY2025 10-K's "sector-low leverage" positioning was accurate at its 12/31/25 measurement date and no longer describes the current figure.

Solvency is not the question. Covenant leverage is 29.2% against a 60% limit, fixed charge coverage 5.0x against 1.50x, unsecured interest coverage 6.0x against 1.75x, secured recourse debt 0% against 10%. Liquidity is $489.3 million ($64.3 million cash plus $425.0 million revolver availability). No debt matures in the balance of 2026; $26.8 million matures in 2027; the walls are 2029 ($337.2 million) and 2030 ($206.0 million).

**Interest expense.** The swap roll-ups are already executed, which locks the cost and removes uncertainty but makes the drag non-discretionary: $3.68 million annually from the September 2026 roll and $1.65 million from the March 2027 roll, $5.33 million combined or $0.068 per diluted share on 78.75 million Q2 diluted shares — about 3.5% of the guidance midpoint. Separately, the $250 million June 2026 notes (5.09% due 2029, 5.32% due 2031, 5.60% due 2033) carry a 5.386% weighted average, $13.47 million annually, of which Q2 captured essentially nothing. A bottom-up fully stepped-up build across mortgages, term loan, both note series, revolver and amortization reaches roughly $58 million versus FY2025 actual net interest of $34.5 million and FY2026 guidance of approximately $45 million (itself raised $1 million at Q2). The 2027 walk from H2 2026 annualized is therefore roughly $10–13 million, or $0.13–$0.17 per share, before offsetting NOI or deleveraging. Note that this is a step-up from unusually favorable 2021-vintage hedges, not evidence of credit deterioration; a secondary summary of the Q2 call that described the placement as "4.36%" conflated it with the weighted-average rate on the entire debt stack, understating marginal debt cost by roughly 100 basis points.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** H1 operating cash flow was $74.1 million (+7.7%); investing −$264.2 million (acquisitions $250.3 million, capital investments $14.0 million); financing +$224.5 million on the $250 million placement plus a $20 million net revolver draw. All 2026 growth has been debt-funded. Share count rose only 0.35% in H1, entirely from stock compensation, with zero ATM issuance in H1 2026 or H1 2025 despite $236.7 million of capacity. The dividend was raised 5.2% to $0.2500 quarterly, a $1.00 annualized rate at 51.5% of the guidance midpoint. One item to watch: H1 capital investments of $14.0 million were down 23% on a 16% larger portfolio, against TTM new-lease TI of $27.98/sf plus $17.25/sf commissions and non-comparable lease TI of $51.71/sf. Management's disclosure that Q3 carries heavier operating expenses tied to scheduled projects supports timing rather than deferral, but six vacant big boxes will require backfill capital at high per-foot cost.

An Altman Z-Score of 2.06 for this company should be disregarded. The retained-earnings term is structurally and permanently negative for a REIT — IVT carries $(3.98) billion of distributions in excess of accumulated net income against $5.74 billion of APIC, reflecting the 90% distribution requirement and the company's legacy as the non-traded Inland American Real Estate Trust. Covenant headroom, liquidity and the maturity ladder are the relevant solvency evidence, and all three are sound.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth arithmetic is unusually transparent because management supplied both halves of it.

**Near term (through 1Q27).** The bridge is the 160-basis-point spread between leased (96.2%) and economic (94.6%) occupancy, representing roughly $5.6 million of annualized base rent already signed but not paying, about 2.3% of the $243.1 million ABR. Management expects roughly 77% to commence by year-end 2026 and guided to near-record occupancy by the end of Q1 2027. That is a genuine, contracted catch-up worth roughly $0.05–$0.06 per share when fully in place. It is a one-time level shift, not a growth rate.

**Organic engine.** Same-property revenue growth of 1.5% (Q2) and 2.1% (H1), leased occupancy down 130 basis points same-store, and three consecutive quarters of narrowing blended releasing spreads (+14.1% Q4 2025 → +10.5% Q1 2026 → +8.5% Q2 2026, with renewals decelerating from +14.0% to +7.9%) all point the same direction. Retention remains high at 88%, and TTM spreads of +11.1% are still strongly positive, so this is deceleration from an excellent level rather than reversal.

**Acquisition accretion is thin.** Management describes a roughly $2 billion pipeline with target initial yields in the low-6% range and unlevered returns in the low-to-mid 7s, while flagging rising competition and buy-side yields ranging 5.5–7.0%. Against a 5.386% marginal unsecured cost of debt, a low-6% entry yield leaves 60–70 basis points of spread on debt-funded acquisitions. That explains why leverage rose 1.4 turns without a commensurate jump in per-share growth: the accretion case now depends on post-acquisition NOI growth and mark-to-market, not on the entry spread.

**Highest-return capital is small.** Three active redevelopments carry $21.6 million of projected incremental cost ($5.4 million spent) at estimated 7–10% incremental yields — 100 to 380 basis points above acquisition cap rates, with eight additional projects identified. This is the best capital IVT deploys and it is roughly 5% of the annual acquisition program.

**Estimate revisions require interpretation, not extrapolation.** The consensus table shows a single analyst cutting GAAP EPS to −$0.87 for the current year from $0.00 ninety days ago. That directly contradicts the company's raised net income guidance of $0.12–$0.18 and is almost certainly a modeling-basis issue; even taken at face value, GAAP EPS for a REIT absorbing $420–455 million of gross acquisitions is dominated by incremental depreciation and purchase-price intangible amortization (the 2026 allocations include $44.6 million of in-place lease intangibles amortizing over 7.7 years). This is not the +30% inflection signal or the deterioration signal it superficially resembles; I am giving it minimal weight and anchoring instead on the Core FFO guidance and the H2 arithmetic.

**Net view.** 2027 Core FFO in the $1.96–$2.02 range is the reasonable central case: roughly $0.10–$0.14 of NOI and occupancy-recovery contribution against $0.08–$0.10 of incremental net interest, before disposition-funded deleveraging. That is 1–4% per-share growth on a base that grew 6.9% annually from 2021 to 2025.

## Competitive Landscape

The strategic bet is defensible. Essentially no new open-air shopping center supply has been built at scale in Sun Belt metros since the financial crisis, so population and income growth translates into occupancy and rent rather than into competing product. IVT's data supports the demand side: TTM blended spreads of +11.1%, 88% retention, ABR/sf up 3.8% year over year, anchor leased occupancy of 98.1%, and an operating platform with field offices within two hours of more than 95% of centers (103 employees).

Three counterpoints emerge from the filings rather than from outside commentary.

The acquisition market has become the binding constraint on value creation. IVT competes with Kimco, Regency, Brixmor, Phillips Edison, Acadia, private capital and 1031 buyers for the same product. The scarcity that protects existing assets makes adding assets expensive, and at a 60–70 basis point spread over marginal debt cost the external growth engine is running near its economic limit unless leverage is refinanced lower or funded with equity — and equity issuance at 16.9x FFO on 3% growth is not obviously accretive either.

Organic performance is decelerating while the market prices growth. Same-property leased occupancy down 130 basis points, three quarters of narrowing spreads, and 1.5–2.1% same-property revenue growth are all trajectory negatives, and trajectory matters more than level in judging competitive position.

Geographic diversification is improving; tenant diversification is unproven. Texas fell from 37.7% to 35.2% of ABR between December and June as capital rotated into North Carolina, Tennessee and South Carolina. That is real diversification of one kind. Top-25 tenant concentration of 31.3% has no comparable prior-period figure available, so no trend claim is supportable there.

## Risk Assessment

**2027 anchor rollover.** 2026 expirations are nearly clean at 2.0% of ABR. 2027 includes 28 anchor leases totaling 853 thousand square feet (11.5% of GLA) at $13.22/sf, or $11.3 million of ABR, within total 2027 expirations of 11.0% of ABR ($29.1 million). Anchor renewals have been the weakest spread cohort (+8.9% in FY2025), so this is where re-leasing risk concentrates, and it arrives at the same time as the second swap roll.

**Restaurant and small-shop credit.** Restaurants are ~21% of ABR and management acknowledged monitoring a handful of weaker locations in what it called a higher-turnover category with operators facing capital and operating challenges. The credit loss provision was $278 thousand in Q2 versus $170 thousand (+63.5%) and $434 thousand in H1 versus $138 thousand (+214.5%). Absolute magnitude is small — roughly 0.3% of revenue against guidance assuming 30–70 basis points — but the direction is unambiguous and it sits atop small-shop dependence of 60.6% of ABR.

**Geographic and catastrophe concentration.** Texas 35.2% of ABR, Austin–Round Rock 14.2% across eight properties, Florida 20.7%. Austin ABR of $17.35/sf is below the portfolio average and the market is 98.0% leased, so the Austin risk is macro (in-migration and tech employment) rather than current occupancy. The captive insurer retaining first-dollar wind, flood and named-windstorm exposure below deductibles makes a major named storm an earnings event rather than a solvency event, but it is a genuine retained exposure with only $392 thousand of reserves against it.

**Interest reset, already crystallized.** Quantified above. The mitigant is that the forward swaps are executed, so the rate is locked rather than floating; residual floating exposure is the $75 million revolver at roughly ±$0.8 million per 100 basis points.

**Implied disposition dependence.** Net investment activity guidance of approximately $300 million was left unchanged while gross acquisitions ran $420–455 million through early July (the $290 million and $165 million figures management disclosed may double-count the $34.0 million New Garden Crossing purchase). With zero dispositions in H1, reconciling to ~$300 million net requires roughly $120–155 million of H2 dispositions — consistent with four of six vacant big boxes being tied to redevelopment or disposition plans, and the most likely reconciliation of the ~$45 million interest guide against a ~$49 million bottom-up H2 estimate. Execution risk is real: if dispositions slip, either leverage stays near 5.5x or the interest guide is missed.

**Governance.** The 8-K record back to September 2024 is clean — no restatements, no auditor changes, no material litigation filings, one Item 5.02. Compensation metrics (Core FFO/share, same-property NOI, relative TSR against the NAREIT shopping center index) are appropriate for a rate-sensitive REIT, and IVT's TSR index exceeded that peer index in each year 2021–2025. The concern is level and slope: CEO total compensation rose 104% from $3.72 million (2021) to $7.60 million (2025), including +24.6% in a year when the TSR index fell roughly 15%, against Core FFO/share growth of 6.9% annually. Comp actually paid declined from $8.24 million to $7.88 million, which moves the right way. With no disclosed leverage gate in the incentive structure, debt-funded acquisitions that are modestly accretive to Core FFO/share can score well even when they thin risk-adjusted returns. That is an incentive-design observation, not an allegation; management volunteered the leverage increase and its intent to bring it back below 5x. One item remains unverified: whether a director's employment relationship creates any related-party dimension with respect to IVT's largest tenant. No related-party transactions were disclosed in the material reviewed.

**Insider signal is neutral.** Across roughly 25 Form 4 filings from January to July 2026 there were no open-market purchases and no open-market sales — only grants and dispositions to the issuer for tax withholding. Executives are accumulating through awards (Busch 239,713 shares; Lynn David 134,434; Phillips 93,770) and are not selling into strength, but there is no conviction signal. Unvested RSUs of 1,405,996 represent 1.80% of shares outstanding (1.77% post-issuance ownership dilution if all vest), with the 1,061,240 performance/market-based units contingent on Core FFO/share, same-property NOI and relative TSR conditions and therefore outside current diluted capitalization.

**Capital-return inaction.** A $150.0 million repurchase authorization stood entirely unused at June 30 while the stock fell from roughly $36.67 (a July 24 Form 4 reference price) to $32.70. At 5.5x TTM leverage, debt-funded buybacks would worsen the metric management has publicly committed to reducing, so restraint is defensible — but it means no valuation-based capital discipline is being applied in either direction.

**Short interest.** 8.9% of float short with 3.6 days to cover is elevated for a small-cap retail REIT with no obvious binary catalyst. It is more likely a rate-duration or leverage-trajectory expression than a company-specific bear thesis, and it modestly raises the odds of a sharp move on either a disposition announcement or a dovish rate surprise.

No options data was available for this name, so no options-based positioning read is offered.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($39.50, +20.8%).** Signed-but-not-open rent commences on schedule, occupancy reaches near-record levels by 1Q27 as guided, and same-property NOI holds in the 3.25–4.25% guided band. H2 dispositions of $120–155 million pull leverage to 5.0x or below, allowing the $45 million interest guide to hold and reducing the 2027 step-up. The 2027 anchor block re-leases at high-single-digit spreads. Redevelopments deliver 7–10% incremental yields, and the pipeline is executed selectively at 6.5%+ rather than low-6% entry yields. 2027 Core FFO reaches $2.02–$2.05, and the multiple expands toward 19x on evidence that leverage is normalizing. This case requires the long end to stabilize; sustained pressure on rates works directly against the multiple.

**Base case ($34.50, +5.5% price, +8.6% total return including dividend).** H2 Core FFO lands near the guidance midpoint at $0.96, FY2026 at $1.94. Signed-but-not-open rent commences with a quarter or two of slippage. Dispositions execute at the low end, leverage settles near 5.2x, and 2027 Core FFO reaches $1.99 (+2.6%) as roughly $0.12 of NOI and occupancy contribution offsets roughly $0.09 of incremental net interest. The multiple holds at 17.3x on 2027 estimates. The dividend rises to roughly $1.05, sustaining a ~3.1% yield at 52% payout.

**Bear case ($26.75, −18.2%).** The H2 consumer slowdown transmits through the tenant base: restaurant closures at 21% of ABR and small-shop failures at 60.6% of ABR push the credit-loss provision above the 70 basis point guidance ceiling, same-property NOI growth falls below 2%, and the leased-to-economic spread widens rather than closes as new vacancy offsets commencements. The 2027 anchor rollover clears at flat-to-low-single-digit spreads with backfill TI near $50/sf. Dispositions slip, leverage stays at 5.5x, and full-year 2027 interest reaches the $58 million bottom-up run rate, holding Core FFO flat near $1.90. The multiple compresses to 14x as the long end stays elevated. This lands the stock at roughly the $26.81 52-week low.

Probability-weighting these at roughly 20% bull / 55% base / 25% bear produces an expected total return near +5%, which is below a reasonable required return for a levered equity REIT but not negative. That combination — modest positive expected value, roughly symmetric scenario spread, no margin of safety — is a HOLD rather than either ACCUMULATE or REDUCE. The distinction from REDUCE rests on the balance sheet (covenant headroom of roughly 2x, $489 million liquidity, essentially no maturities before 2029), the 51.5% payout providing a covered 3.05% yield, and the fact that the 12% pullback already removed the overvaluation that existed at $36.67. The distinction from ACCUMULATE rests on 16.9x forward FFO against 1–4% forward per-share growth, a contracted interest headwind, and same-store occupancy and spread trajectories that are both negative.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints at the Q3 2026 print (late October/early November 2026) and the Q1 2027 print, when management's "near-record occupancy" claim is verifiable.

**Targets from the $32.70 basis:**
- Base: **$34.50** (17.3x 2027E Core FFO of $1.99), +5.5% price, +8.6% including the $1.00 dividend
- Bull: **$39.50** (19.3x 2027E $2.05), +20.8%
- Bear: **$26.75** (14.1x $1.90), −18.2%

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. Q3 2026 Core FFO guidance raised above $1.96 with the interest expense guide held at ~$45 million, confirming dispositions executed rather than deferred.
2. Same-property leased occupancy recovering to 96.5% or higher with the leased-to-economic spread narrowing below 120 basis points, evidencing signed-but-not-open commencement on schedule.
3. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA printing at or below 5.0x on a quarter-annualized basis alongside at least $120 million of announced dispositions.
4. Price below $29.50, which would put the stock at roughly 15x 2027E Core FFO and a 3.4% yield — a level at which the growth deceleration is priced.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. FY2026 Core FFO guidance cut below $1.92, or FY2027 initial guidance below $1.95, which would confirm the interest step-up is not being earned back.
2. Credit-loss provision exceeding 70 basis points of revenue in any quarter, or two or more additional anchor vacancies without redevelopment or disposition plans attached.
3. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA at or above 5.5x at year-end 2026 with net investment guidance raised and funded by additional debt rather than dispositions.

## Conclusion

IVT owns a good portfolio in the right geographies with a sound balance sheet, and it is priced accordingly. The gap between the reported +8.9% H1 Core FFO growth and the +3.2% implied by management's own reaffirmed H2 guidance is the central fact in the case, and it is not a forecast — it is arithmetic from disclosed figures. Underneath it, same-property revenue is growing 1.5–2.1%, roughly half of Q2's same-property NOI gain came from a real estate tax decline of uncertain repeatability, same-property leased occupancy is down 130 basis points year over year, blended releasing spreads have narrowed for three consecutive quarters, and $5.33 million of annualized swap step-up plus a 5.386% coupon on $250 million of new notes create a $0.13–$0.17 per-share drag that must be earned back in 2027.

Offsetting that: $489 million of liquidity, no maturities of consequence before 2029, covenant headroom of roughly 2x on every test, roughly $5.6 million of contracted signed-but-not-open rent, a 3.05% dividend covered at 51.5% of Core FFO, zero ATM dilution, and management's stated and plausible path back below 5x leverage via H2 dispositions.

At 16.9x the guidance midpoint on a 6.4% implied cap rate, the stock offers roughly 5–9% total return in the base case against 18% downside if the consumer weakens into a rent roll where 21% of ABR is restaurants and 60.6% is small shops. Existing holders are adequately compensated to wait for the occupancy bridge and the disposition program. New capital should require either $29.50 or confirmed evidence — raised Core FFO guidance with the interest guide intact and leverage at 5.0x — before committing. **HOLD.**
