**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Blackbaud is the dominant vertical software provider to the nonprofit, education, foundation, and corporate social-responsibility markets, generating $1.128B in FY2025 revenue across a recurring/transactional model with high switching costs and ~92% gross dollar retention. The stock has fallen 55% over twelve months, from a $74.88 high to $27.04, and now trades at ~9x trailing earnings and roughly 10x normalized free cash flow. The central analytical question is whether this de-rating reflects genuine fundamental deterioration or multiple compression on a business whose underlying numbers are stable to modestly improving.

The evidence points to the latter. Organic revenue grew 5.5% in FY2025 (the 2.3% GAAP decline is entirely the EVERFI divestiture), Q1 2026 organic recurring revenue reaccelerated to +5.0%, operating cash flow rebounded to $51.5M in the quarter, and full-year and next-year consensus EPS are flat at $5.19 and $5.87. The company beat or met in all four of the last four quarters. Non-GAAP operating margin is 30.5% and the Rule of 40 sits at ~41%. This is a healthy-but-not-elite SaaS profile. The Altman Z-Score of 0.68 that screens as "distress" is a false positive: equity has been mechanically eroded from $808M (2023) to $34.6M (March 2026) by deliberate debt-funded buybacks, while the company sits comfortably inside its 3.75x leverage covenant at 2.67x with 6.47x interest coverage.

The bear case rests almost entirely on a single, two-sided risk: roughly 40% of contracts renew in 2026, a cohort whose ARR up for renewal is ~40% larger than 2025's, which mathematically raises absolute churn dollars even if retention rates hold. This is the swing factor for the year, and it is unresolved. Management's own leading indicators — 20%+ of renewals now at 4-year-plus terms, with customers requesting 5-year contracts — point toward retention holding. With the renewal risk already heavily reflected in options pricing and short interest, against a depressed stock where management retains $878.5M of buyback authorization, the risk/reward is roughly balanced. I rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

Blackbaud operates as a single reportable segment serving the "social impact" economy: nonprofits, foundations, K-12 private schools, higher education, healthcare nonprofits, and corporate grantmaking functions. It brands itself as the leading provider of AI-powered software for social impact and operates across the US, UK, Canada, Australia, Costa Rica, and India, serving users in 100+ countries.

The $1.128B revenue base breaks into three streams. Contractual recurring (~64%) is subscription/cloud software — flagship products include Raiser's Edge NXT, financial management, education management, and CSR/grantmaking platforms — sold on standard 3-year contracts (20%+ now 4-year or longer), billed annually in advance and recognized ratably with embedded mid-to-high-single-digit price escalators. Transactional recurring (~34%) is payments and giving volume: Blackbaud Integrated Payments (~55% of transactional), JustGiving consumer/peer-to-peer giving (~20%), Blackbaud Tuition Management (~20%), and event-based usage. One-time services (~2%) are being deemphasized.

The moat rests on deep vertical specialization, high switching costs in mission-critical fundraising and financial systems, and an integrated payments layer that monetizes donation flow. The 10-K candidly acknowledges low barriers to entry in many segments and the risk that a large diversified software enterprise could enter directly.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** GAAP revenue declined 2.3% in FY2025 ($1.128B vs $1.155B), entirely attributable to the EVERFI divestiture ($82.6M of prior-year revenue). Organic revenue grew 5.5% (5.2% constant-currency), in line with the company's mid-single-digit target. Q1 2026 reaccelerated: GAAP revenue +4.2% to $281.1M, organic recurring +5.0%. This stabilization after several quarters of deceleration is the core of the constructive case.

**Profitability.** FY2025 GAAP operating income swung to +$190.8M (16.9% margin) from a -$270.5M loss in 2024, but the 2024 loss was almost entirely the $405.4M EVERFI loss-on-disposition (including $390.2M non-cash impairment). On a normalized basis, non-GAAP operating margin was 30.5% (vs 27.6% in FY2024), the Rule of 40 was 41.4% (5.5% organic growth + 35.9% adjusted EBITDA margin), and gross margin expanded ~410 bps to 58.8% GAAP / 62.3% non-GAAP. Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $1.14 beat the $1.08 consensus; non-GAAP operating margin was 29.6%.

**Cash flow.** This is the key support for the equity. FY2025 operating cash flow was $265.6M and non-GAAP free cash flow was $203.5M (18% margin). Headline FCF figures that net only $7.8M capex overstate true FCF because they ignore ~$54M of capitalized software development — the realistic figure is ~$200M. OCF rebounded to $51.5M in Q1 2026 from $1.4M a year prior, reversing the working-capital timing drag that pulled full-year 2025 OCF down from $296M in 2024.

**Balance sheet.** The headline tension is the engineered capital structure. Stockholders' equity collapsed from $808M (2023) to $142M (2024), $85M (Dec-2025), and $34.6M (Mar-2026), producing a D/E of 27x and a sub-1 current ratio. The Altman Z-Score of 0.68 reads as "distress," but it is mechanically driven by this near-zero equity base, not operational stress. Net leverage was 2.52x (Dec-2025) and 2.67x (Mar-2026) against a 3.75x covenant — comfortable headroom — with 6.47x interest coverage. Debt is ~$1.19B, mostly variable-rate term loans and revolver at ~5.5%, maturing April 2029. $800M notional of interest-rate swaps now hedge the variable-rate exposure, materially blunting the rate-sensitivity concern. A further nuance: ~$425M of the $866M reported current liabilities is "due to customers" — restricted payment-processing float matched by restricted cash, not a true liquidity obligation. The negative working capital is largely an artifact of this float.

**Capital allocation.** Debt-funded buybacks define the strategy. The company repurchased $214M (7.9% of shares) in 2025 and $418M in 2024, expanded the authorization to $1.0B in December 2025, and intends to repurchase 5–10% of shares in 2026 and commit ≥50% of 2026–2030 FCF to buybacks. Diluted shares fell from 53.7M (2023) to 46.4M (Q1 2026). The problem: prior buybacks were executed at $49+ averages well above today's $27, levering up the equity at the wrong price. If the stock is genuinely cheap at ~10x FCF, continued buybacks now would be accretive, but the track record shows poor price discipline.

## Growth Analysis

Blackbaud's growth is structurally mid-single-digit, driven by price escalators and payments volume rather than logo expansion in a mature vertical. The 5.5% organic FY2025 figure and ~5% Q1 2026 organic recurring growth are consistent with this profile and represent stabilization after a period of deceleration. The Rule of 40 at ~41% places the company in the healthy tier — strong margins compensating for modest top-line growth.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than directional. Current-year consensus EPS is flat at $5.19 (unchanged over 30 and 90 days) and next-year is flat at $5.87. The current-quarter estimate was trimmed 5.9% over 30 days to $1.28, but next-quarter rose 8.6% over 90 days to $1.32 — a wash. This is not the +30% revision velocity that signals an acceleration, nor the falling-estimate profile that signals deterioration. It is a steady-state business.

Two forward levers deserve weight. The OBBBA tax legislation (enacted July 2025) restored immediate R&D expensing, reducing US cash taxes through 2027 and shrinking the valuation allowance — a multi-year FCF tailwind that directly funds the buyback. The Hyderabad Global Capability Center build-out and closure of legacy private data centers carry $6–8M of transition costs in 2026 but deliver cost benefits from 2027. On the demand side, agentic AI products ("Agents for Good," Blackbaud AI Chat, a fundraising development agent priced in "tens of thousands" per customer) are early-stage monetization with uncertain contribution.

## Valuation Assessment

At $27.04, BLKB trades at 9.0x trailing earnings and ~10x normalized FCF. Against the Application Software peer set, the peer-median P/E is 22.8 (BL 65.6, CXM 46.3, SPSC 22.8, RAMP 16.9, ALRM 19.3). Blackbaud trades at roughly 40% of the peer median multiple. Some discount is warranted — Blackbaud grows slower than the high-multiple names and carries more leverage — but a ~60% discount to peers for a business generating 30%+ operating margins and ~18% FCF margin with stable estimates is steep.

This is the crux of the audit reconciliation. A low multiple alone is not a buy signal and frequently marks a value trap; cheapness must be paired with a catalyst or evidence the market is wrong. Here, the de-rating is driven by the 2026 renewal overhang plus general SaaS multiple compression, not by confirmed earnings erosion. The estimates are flat, the cash flow is intact, and the leverage is hedged. That argues the stock is fairly-to-cheaply valued, not structurally impaired. The offsetting reality is that the renewal cohort is genuinely unresolved, and the market is pricing meaningful downside risk through it (76% IV, backwardation, 15% put skew). I score valuation modestly above neutral: the asymmetry favors the long side at this price, but not with the conviction that would justify a BUY given the unresolved binary.

Analyst targets ($45 low, $65 high, $50 median) sit far above the current price. Evercore cut its target to $45 while reiterating In Line; Seeking Alpha upgraded on the single-digit P/E and ~10x FCF. These targets are not floors I would treat as reliable on a name with a genuine fundamental overhang — they predate full visibility into the renewal cohort — but they frame the magnitude of the gap between price and analyst fair-value estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

Blackbaud is the dominant vertical incumbent in nonprofit and education fundraising and financial management. Its payments monetization (donation processing, tuition management) is a differentiated, volume-leveraged asset, though it ties ~34% of revenue to charitable-giving cycles and macro conditions.

Competitive threats are stable rather than accelerating: horizontal CRM/payments players such as Salesforce's nonprofit cloud, lower-cost point solutions, the risk a large software enterprise enters via acquisition, and AI-native fundraising tools. The 10-K's acknowledgment of low entry barriers in some segments is a candid flag, but there is no evidence of share loss in the current data — organic growth is holding at the mid-single-digit level the model targets. The lengthening of contract durations (20%+ now 4-year-plus, with 5-year requests) is a tentative sign that switching costs remain high and customers are deepening rather than reducing their commitment. Competitive position is stable, neither clearly widening nor eroding.

## Risk Assessment

**The 2026 renewal cohort (the central risk).** With ~92% gross dollar retention, the larger renewal cohort mechanically means more absolute churn dollars even if retention rates hold, and management is explicit about this. The contradicting leading indicator — 20%+ of renewals now at 4-year-plus terms with 5-year requests — points toward retention holding. I probability-weight this as a downside-skewed but genuinely two-sided outcome: more likely retention holds near historical levels (in which case the overhang clears and the stock re-rates), with a tail where the larger cohort plus any macro softness in nonprofit funding dents recurring revenue growth into the low-single-digits.

**Engineered leverage — optical, not binding.** Equity is near zero, but this is a deliberate buyback artifact. Net leverage of 2.67x against a 3.75x covenant with 6.47x coverage and $800M of swaps hedging the variable-rate debt means leverage is not a binding risk to the equity over the next 12 months absent a severe operational stumble. The 2029 maturity is distant. I treat this as an optical concern that distorts screens, not a live solvency threat.

**Customer funding sensitivity.** A risk factor flags that changes in US federal/state grant and funding programs could impair nonprofit customers' purchasing — a pointed concern given the current US fiscal environment and pressure on nonprofit funding. This is the channel through which a macro shock would reach the transactional revenue base.

**The 2020 Security Incident — resolved with a tail.** SEC, FTC, 49-state AG, California AG, and international investigations are all settled with no remaining loss-contingency liability as of December 2025. Incident-related expenses fell to $3.1M in 2025 from $13.7M. Blackbaud must maintain cybersecurity program obligations through May 2044, a long but manageable tail.

**Other.** $1.06B goodwill on a $2.1B asset base (EVERFI demonstrated impairment risk is real, and a falling stock is a stated trigger); a Q3 2025 immaterial accounting revision to correct a non-cash income-tax valuation-allowance error (controls reported effective); and chronic TSR underperformance (5-year cumulative 110 vs peer 148.5) that, combined with heavy 2025 board/officer churn and a December 2025 bylaw amendment, fits the profile that attracts activist interest. No 13D surfaced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 76.3% sits well above 41.9% one-year historical volatility (a 34.4-point rich spread), with a 15.0% put/call skew indicating elevated put demand and a term structure in backwardation (near-term 76.3% > long-term 70.1%) that signals the market is pricing imminent stress around the renewal cohort and upcoming earnings. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±18.3%. The options-delta feed showing IV spiking to an implausible 1514.8% is a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. The renewal risk that anchors the bear case is already heavily priced. Short interest of 13.3% of float (5.6 days to cover) reinforces that bearish positioning is crowded, which raises squeeze risk on any positive renewal surprise and is a meaningful counterweight to a bearish call.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($45–55).** The 2026 renewal cohort holds dollar retention near historical levels, the overhang clears, and the stock re-rates from ~9x toward the low-to-mid teens earnings multiple that a 30%-margin, ~5%-organic-growth, ~$200M-FCF business merits. The OBBBA cash-tax tailwind and Hyderabad cost program lift FCF into 2027; the $878.5M buyback authorization, deployed at $27 rather than $49, becomes genuinely accretive and shrinks the share count meaningfully. Lengthening contract durations confirm switching costs are intact. At 14–16x $5.19–5.87 EPS, the shares reach $45–55, broadly consistent with the analyst target range.

**Bear case ($20–24).** The larger renewal cohort, compounded by macro pressure on nonprofit funding, elevates absolute churn and pulls organic recurring growth toward low-single-digits. Estimate revisions turn negative, the multiple stays compressed at 8–9x on declining earnings, and continued debt-funded buybacks into a falling stock raise governance concerns. Goodwill impairment scrutiny intensifies as the stock stays depressed. The shares drift to $20–24, roughly 11–26% below the current price.

The base case sits between these: the renewal outcome is more likely benign than severe, but the binary is unresolved and the catalyst is multi-quarter and not cleanly observable. With downside of ~15-20% against re-rating upside of 30%+ plus squeeze risk on a crowded short, the asymmetry modestly favors the long side but not with conviction. That is a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next two-to-three quarterly reports (Q2 2026 onward) are the natural reassessment points, as the 2026 renewal cohort works through the books. Management commentary on renewal dollar retention and recurring-revenue organic growth is the data to watch.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 results showing organic recurring revenue growth holding at ≥5% with explicit management confirmation that renewal dollar retention is tracking near historical ~92% levels — i.e., the larger cohort did not elevate net churn.
- Resumption of buybacks at/near current prices ($27–30) on the $878.5M authorization, demonstrating improved capital-allocation discipline and per-share accretion.
- Positive estimate revisions (current-year EPS rising above $5.19) confirming the renewal overhang has cleared.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of organic recurring growth decelerating below 3%, or explicit management disclosure of elevated renewal churn dollars materially above plan.
- A guidance cut, negative EPS revision exceeding 10%, or net leverage approaching 3.25x against the 3.75x covenant.
- A goodwill impairment charge or a 13D filing accompanied by a forced strategic action.

## Conclusion

Blackbaud is a cash-generative vertical software incumbent trading at a steep discount to peers after a 55% drawdown. The decline reflects multiple compression and a genuine but unresolved 2026 renewal-cohort overhang, not confirmed fundamental deterioration. The leverage that screens as distress is an engineered buyback artifact, hedged by swaps and sitting comfortably inside covenants. The bear catalyst — the larger renewal cohort — is real but two-sided, with management's own lengthening-contract-duration data pointing toward retention holding, and it is already heavily reflected in implied volatility, put skew, and short interest.

The setup is roughly balanced: ~15-20% downside against 30%+ re-rating upside plus squeeze risk, on a name with intact cash flow and a large buyback at depressed prices. That asymmetry does not support a conviction bearish call, nor does the unresolved renewal binary support a high-conviction long. I rate the shares HOLD, with the renewal cohort as the variable that resolves the thesis in either direction over the next two-to-three quarters.
