## Executive Summary

Masimo is no longer an investable public equity. The weight of evidence — a definitive merger proxy (DEFM14A) filed April 1, 2026, an 8-K dated June 10, 2026 matching the reported closing date, a filing-agent transition to a merger-services filer beginning February 17, 2026, and five independent outlets reporting Danaher's completed acquisition at $180.00 per share in cash for approximately $9.9 billion inclusive of assumed debt — establishes with high confidence that the transaction closed in June 2026. The market data corroborates it mechanically: the last three daily prints (June 10–12, 2026) are identical at $179.95 on **zero volume**, the authoritative quote feed returns no price at all (six distinct errors including `schwab_quote_unavailable` and `missing_authoritative_price`), and all five remaining sell-side targets are stacked at exactly $180.00 — low, high, mean and median identical. Analyst targets do not cluster to the penny on a going concern; they cluster to the penny on a fixed cash consideration.

The consequence for a holder is arithmetic rather than analytical. At the frozen basis of $179.95, the remaining consideration to a $180.00 cash price is **+$0.05, or 0.028%**. There is no forward participation in the franchise, and no participation in the litigation outcome: reporting indicates the upheld Apple patent damages (widely cited at $634M, with one outlet reporting $643M) are payable to Danaher as Masimo's new owner. The standalone asset Danaher bought is genuinely good — FY2025 continuing-operations revenue of $1,526.9M at a 61.9% gross margin, $310.0M of operating income (20.3% margin), $198.4M of free cash flow, and roughly 1.05x net debt/EBITDA — which is why Danaher guided the deal as accretive to earnings within the year. None of that quality is accessible through the MASI ticker.

This is the fifth consecutive report on Masimo, and the first that is not a HOLD. The prior sequence (5.6 → 5.8 → 5.6 → 5.5, HOLD throughout) reflected a reasonable debate about a re-rated patient-monitoring franchise with a stale sell-side complex. That debate has been settled by a cash transaction. The downgrade to AVOID at an overall 3.8 records the disappearance of the security, not a deterioration in the business; scoring an instrument that pays a fixed $180.00 requires marking valuation and growth to the consideration, which is what drives the composite lower.

## Company Overview

Masimo, headquartered in Irvine, California, built its franchise on Signal Extraction Technology (SET) pulse oximetry — noninvasive blood-oxygen measurement that functions through patient motion and low perfusion, historically the two failure modes of conventional oximetry. The commercial model is razor-and-blade: monitoring hardware placed into hospitals under multi-year contracts, monetized through single-use sensors and consumables. The FY2025 financial signature is consistent with that description — a 61.9% gross margin against capital expenditure of only $19.4M (1.3% of revenue) and inventory of $380.3M. A hardware-weighted monitoring business does not sustain 62% gross margins.

The dominant structural event of the last three fiscal years was the acquisition and subsequent removal of the Sound United consumer-audio business (Bowers & Wilkins, Denon, Marantz, Polk), bought for roughly $1B in 2022 and divested during FY2025. That transaction is the reason reported revenue fell from $2,094.4M in FY2024 to $1,526.9M in FY2025 — a $567.5M decline that is a deconsolidation artifact, not organic erosion. It is also the reason the founder-CEO was removed following an activist campaign, and the reason the company was a plausible acquisition target once the low-margin segment was gone.

Post-close, Masimo operates as a Danaher operating company. Tim Benner was appointed president in August 2026, per Orange County Business Journal reporting. Masimo will not report standalone financial results going forward.

Two caveats govern this section. The FY2025 10-K's Item 1 (Business), Item 1A (Risk Factors), Item 3 (Legal Proceedings), Item 7 (MD&A) and Item 8 (Financial Statements) were all inaccessible, as were the contents of the merger proxy and every 8-K. The business description therefore rests on XBRL structured data and secondary reporting. The identity of the Sound United buyer and the proceeds received are entirely unverified.

## Financial Analysis

**Fiscal labeling.** Masimo used a 52/53-week fiscal year ending near calendar year-end. The XBRL column labeled "2026" is fiscal 2025, covered by the 10-K filed February 26, 2026 (amended by a 10-K/A on May 1, 2026). FY2025 reflects continuing operations only; FY2022–FY2024 include consumer audio. Growth rates computed across that boundary are meaningless.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $2,035.8M | 52.0% | $210.0M | 10.3% | — |
| FY2023 | $2,048.1M | 49.0% | $136.5M | 6.7% | — |
| FY2024 | $2,094.4M | 48.0% | −$266.7M | −12.7% | −$304.9M |
| FY2025 | $1,526.9M | **61.9%** | **+$310.0M** | **20.3%** | −$151.5M |

Three points matter.

**The headline FY2025 net loss is a disposal artifact.** Pretax income of $272.6M less tax of $64.9M (a 23.8% effective rate, which looks normal and suggests a clean continuing-operations figure) implies continuing-operations net income of roughly $207.7M. Against reported net income of −$151.5M, the implied loss from discontinued operations is approximately **$359.2M**. Balance-sheet evidence corroborates a goodwill-heavy disposal: goodwill fell from $407.7M (FY2023) to $96.7M (FY2024) to $101.0M (FY2025), and intangibles from $419.9M to $350.9M to $52.1M — a combined ~$675M reduction over two years. The FY2023→FY2024 leg is better explained by impairment of the consumer segment; FY2025 captures the disposal. The $359.2M figure is a derivation from three line items, not a disclosed number, and its split between disposal loss and pre-closing segment operating results is unverified because the discontinued-operations footnote was inaccessible.

**The 1,390bp gross-margin expansion is mix, not operations.** Gross margin moved 48.0% → 61.9% precisely as consumer audio left continuing operations. Consumer audio was diluting blended gross margin by roughly 10–14 points. Any screen reading "gross margin up 1,390bp" as operational improvement is wrong. Some portion could reflect genuine pricing or consumables mix, but without restated prior-period continuing-operations gross margin the apportionment is unavailable, and magnitude and timing make mix the dominant term.

**Cash generation is solid but converts below accounting earnings.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $217.8M against $310.0M of operating income is a 70% conversion rate; free cash flow of $198.4M is a 13.0% FCF margin. Working-capital deltas — inventory $459.2M → $380.3M, receivables $426.5M → $275.8M, payables $252.8M → $103.0M — mix deconsolidation effects with true operating movements and cannot be read as clean cash drivers.

**Capital structure improved materially into the sale.** Long-term debt fell $871.7M → $727.9M → $518.0M, a $353.7M reduction (40.6%) over two years. Against $152.3M of cash, net debt is $365.7M; against an EBITDA proxy of $348.8M ($310.0M operating income plus $38.8M reported depreciation), that is roughly **1.05x net leverage**. The current ratio improved from 1.98x to 2.49x. Book equity fell from $1,364.8M to $721.2M and retained earnings by $456.4M, which reconciles exactly to the sum of the FY2024 (−$304.9M) and FY2025 (−$151.5M) losses — implying no material dividend and no meaningful buyback during the period, so divestiture proceeds and operating cash flow were most plausibly directed to debt paydown.

**One reconciliation failure to flag.** Tagged interest expense of $3.0M (FY2025) against $518.0M of long-term debt is not credible; a $518M balance at even 5% costs ~$26M. The operating-to-pretax bridge of $37.4M ($310.0M → $272.6M) is a better approximation of net non-operating expense, though it could also contain FX or one-time items. The debt footnote was inaccessible, so instrument terms, rates, maturities and covenants are unverified. Do not model standalone Masimo from the tagged interest figure.

**Final standalone quarter.** The March 2026 quarter printed EPS of $1.08 against a $1.46 estimate, a 26.0% miss, breaking a streak of three consecutive beats (+10.2%, +6.6%, +7.7%). That is the last reported quarter before close and plausibly carries transaction and retention costs. It had no effect on the fixed consideration: once a cash price is agreed, operating results stop being a valuation input.

Standard screening scores were unavailable — neither Altman Z nor Piotroski F could be computed. With a negative bottom line driven by a non-cash disposal loss, that is expected; the leverage, liquidity and margin components of such screens would have looked solid.

## Growth Analysis

For a holder of MASI, forward growth is zero by construction. The consideration is fixed in cash; every dollar of subsequent revenue, margin expansion, litigation recovery or Danaher Business System synergy accrues to the acquirer.

The more interesting question — what growth rate the underlying healthcare franchise was actually running — cannot be answered from the available evidence, and it is the single most important missing number. Consolidated revenue grew +0.6% in FY2023 and +2.3% in FY2024, but those figures blend a decelerating consumer-audio business with the medtech franchise. Segment disclosure and restated prior-period continuing-operations revenue were both inaccessible. Two readings remain live: either healthcare was compounding meaningfully faster than 2% with audio masking it (consistent with a ~6.5x revenue transaction multiple), or Danaher paid primarily for cost synergies, DBS-driven margin expansion, and litigation proceeds, in which case the flat-growth read is accurate and the premium is a self-help story. The evidence does not discriminate between them.

Two forward-looking data points on the franchise are worth recording even though they no longer accrue to former holders. Masimo received FDA clearance for an AI-enabled device around June 25, 2026 (Danaher shares rose 2.8% intraday on the report), which argues against the pipeline being empty. Against that, R&D fell to $126.4M, or 8.3% of revenue — the lowest ratio in the four-year series, versus 8.6%–10.6% previously. Part of that decline is audio deconsolidation, but in a business whose moat rests on patent-protected signal processing, sustained under-investment would be a durability concern. Without segment-level R&D history the two explanations cannot be separated.

The remaining sell-side estimate feed illustrates how stale coverage becomes on a closed deal: current-year EPS of $6.04 (one analyst), next-year $6.70 (three analysts), with revisions of +2.2% and +2.0% over 90 days. Those estimates describe a company that will not report. The "recent change" showing hold ratings falling from 8 to 5 is labeled an upgrade by the data feed; it is coverage being dropped.

The authoritative valuation basis for this report returns no price. The arithmetic that matters:

- Reported cash consideration: **$180.00** (externally sourced, not filing-verified)
- Remaining consideration: **+$0.05, or +0.028%**
- Consensus target low / high / mean / median: **$180.00 / $180.00 / $180.00 / $180.00**

A five-analyst consensus with zero dispersion is a deal-price anchor. It carries no information about intrinsic value and should not be treated as independent confirmation of anything.

There is an internal inconsistency in the supplied data worth naming. Reported market capitalization of $9,423.8M against $179.95 implies 52.4M shares, while Masimo's FY2025 XBRL share counts are 53.6M basic and 54.2M diluted. At 53.6M shares, $9,423.8M implies $175.8 per share — consistent with a pre-close arbitrage discount to $180 rather than with the June 12 print. The market-cap field is most likely a stale snapshot taken shortly before close. Using the more internally consistent share counts, the transaction reconciles: 53.6M × $180 = $9.648B equity, 54.2M × $180 = $9.756B, plus $365.7M net debt gives $10.01B–$10.12B against the ~$9.9B reported total transaction value. The ~$0.1–0.2B gap is within what option and RSU treatment, revolver balances and closing-date cash would explain. That is a consistency check on the $180 figure, not proof of it.

**What Danaher paid, for context.** At ~$9.9B enterprise value against FY2025 continuing operations: ~6.5x revenue, ~28.4x the $348.8M EBITDA proxy, ~49.9x free cash flow, and ~46.4x the $207.7M of implied continuing-operations earnings. These are directional only — the ~$9.9B figure is externally reported, and the EBITDA proxy may understate true EBITDA if intangible amortization is tagged separately from the $38.8M depreciation line, which would lower the multiple. A full strategic price at roughly 28x EBITDA implies Danaher underwrote margin expansion, revenue re-acceleration, litigation recovery, or some combination. The $634M Apple award alone is approximately 6.4% of the purchase price.

The valuation score of 1.5 follows directly. Upside is capped at 0.028%; the earnings power, the FDA-cleared pipeline, and the litigation proceeds have all been transferred. A high valuation score requires identifying why the market is mispricing a security. Here the market is pricing the security exactly correctly, at the contractual consideration.

## Competitive Landscape

Masimo competed in hospital patient monitoring against Medtronic (Nellcor), Philips, GE HealthCare and Baxter/Hillrom, differentiated by SET signal-processing intellectual property. The strength of that IP was court-tested: a federal judge upheld a damages verdict against Apple in July 2026 and denied both Apple's motion to overturn and its motion for a new trial. Extracting a nine-figure award from the most heavily resourced consumer-electronics litigant is meaningful evidence of patent quality.

The boundaries of that moat are equally clear. Masimo did not obtain a permanent ban on Apple Watch sales or on use of the blood-oxygen sensor, and Apple's redesigned workaround passed legal scrutiny in March 2026. The estate blocks copying; it does not block competition. That distinction matters for anyone assessing the durability of the franchise Danaher now owns.

The strategic fit is straightforward. Danaher favors high-gross-margin assets with large recurring consumable streams run through the Danaher Business System, and Masimo's FY2025 profile — 61.9% gross margin, 20.3% operating margin, 1.3% capex intensity, ~1.05x net leverage — matches that template. Buying it after the prior management had removed the low-margin consumer segment meant acquiring a cleaner asset than existed eighteen months earlier. With Danaher down 22% year-to-date at announcement amid a life-sciences tools slowdown, hospital-admissions-driven monitoring demand is a diversification away from biopharma capital spending.

Peer benchmarking is unusable here. The single supplied comparable is BridgeBio Pharma at a −18.0x P/E — a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company with no economic relationship to a profitable patient-monitoring franchise. No conclusion should be drawn from it.

The competitive-position score of 5.0 reflects a genuine, court-validated but narrowing technical moat, scored against the fact that no shareholder can access it through this ticker.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile has inverted. Ordinary business risks — hospital capital budgets, GPO contract renewals, reimbursement, Medtronic and Philips competition — no longer touch a former holder. What remains is transaction mechanics and evidence quality.

**Consideration and settlement risk (low but non-zero).** The fact of a completed Danaher acquisition is high-confidence, resting on the DEFM14A, the June 10 closing 8-K, the merger-agent filing pattern, five independent outlets, a post-close president appointment, and the reconciling share-count arithmetic. The **exact $180.00 per-share figure is externally sourced only** — the merger proxy and the closing 8-K were both unread. A price adjustment or litigation-driven amendment cannot be formally excluded, though the arithmetic consistency argues against a large deviation. Any resolution in either direction moves the outcome by cents.

**Zero upside asymmetry.** This is the dominant risk-scoring input. The Apple damages, the AI-enabled device clearance, and any DBS margin expansion accrue to Danaher. Reporting on the Apple award conflicts on amount ($634M in three outlets, $643M in one, with $634M better corroborated) and overstates finality — district-court denial of post-trial motions does not preclude a Federal Circuit appeal, and whether any amount has been paid is unknown. None of it is a former shareholder's economics.

**Assumed contingent liabilities.** Founder-related litigation reportedly continues post-close ("Kiani lawsuits continue," per July 29 reporting). Nature and quantum are unknown; Item 3 was inaccessible. This is Danaher's exposure.

**Evidence limitations that would normally be disqualifying.** Item 1A risk factors, Item 7 MD&A, Item 8 financial statements, the merger proxy, all 8-K contents, insider transactions, and any earnings transcript were unavailable. I cannot identify a single added, removed or modified risk factor. On a going concern that gap would preclude a rating; here the decisive fact — the security no longer trades — does not depend on any of it.

**Stale positioning data.** Reported short interest of 7.3% of float (3,501,739 shares, 3.8 days to cover) is most plausibly merger-arbitrage-related positioning captured before close, and should not be read as a directional bet on a security with no forward price.

### Options Market Signal

The options data is the cleanest independent corroboration of the acquisition in the entire dataset. Between April 22 and May 20, 2026, ATM implied volatility ran **3.8% rising to 7.9%** while the price moved from $178.39 to $178.83 (+0.2%). Single-digit implied volatility on a medical-device stock is not a market view on fundamentals; medtech names of this size typically carry higher ATM IV. Single-digit IV with a price pinned within 0.7% of a fixed $180 consideration is the standard structure of options written on a stock whose terminal value is contractually determined, with the residual volatility representing deal-break probability and timing to close. The rise from 3.8% to 7.9% is consistent with an approaching close date compressing time value into a narrow window rather than with rising uncertainty about the business.

The put/call open-interest ratio moving from 0.04 to 0.25 fits the same reading — accumulation of cheap downside protection against deal-break risk by arbitrageurs, off an extremely low base. Both readings confirm the fundamental conclusion.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (Value Realization) — $180.00, +0.03%

The transaction closed as reported, the $180.00 cash consideration was received in June 2026, and holders realized a full strategic price at roughly 28x EBITDA on a business that had reported a GAAP net loss the prior fiscal year. Measured against the alternative of remaining a standalone company whose consolidated revenue grew 0.6% then 2.3%, whose shareholder returns trailed the peer group for three consecutive years (TSR index gaps of −31.2, −55.6 and −34.9 points in FY2022–FY2024), and whose stock at FY2024 stood 41.6% below its FY2021 peak, the exit was a good outcome. The board sold after the dilutive segment had been removed and the balance sheet deleveraged 40.6%, which is when the asset commanded the best price. That is the entire bull case, and it is already banked.

### Bear Case (For Anyone Still Holding a Position or Considering One) — $0 of incremental return

There is nothing left to buy. Upside is 0.028%, below any transaction cost. The residual value that a fundamental investor would want — a 61.9% gross-margin consumables franchise, a court-validated patent estate, $198.4M of annual free cash flow, an FDA-cleared AI-enabled device, and the Apple damages award — was transferred with the company. Capital committed here earns nothing while genuine dispersion exists elsewhere. The stale sell-side apparatus (five holds at exactly $180.00, current-year EPS estimates of $6.04 for a company that will not report) creates a real risk that screens still treat MASI as a live, "reasonably valued" 26.9x-forward healthcare name. It is not.

### Management Assessment

The record is mixed and worth preserving because it explains the outcome. The negative: roughly $1B deployed into consumer audio diluted gross margin by 10–14 points, contributed to a $311M goodwill reduction and a −$266.7M FY2024 operating loss, and was reversed at an implied further ~$359M loss through discontinued operations — cumulative goodwill-plus-intangible destruction of ~$675M across FY2023–FY2025. Compensation actually paid to the PEO was negative for three consecutive fiscal years (−$34.05M, −$22.20M, −$4.30M) while grant-date total compensation sat in a rigid $15.48M–$16.51M band every year from FY2020 through FY2024, a 6.7% range across five years. The incentive plan's company-selected measure was Total Revenue, with no disclosed return-on-capital or relative-TSR metric; consolidated revenue rose 78% from FY2020 to FY2022 spanning the audio acquisition while the TSR gap versus peers swung from +30.5 to −31.2 points. I do not infer that management pursued the acquisition to earn incentive pay — flat grant-date pay is inconsistent with a pay-maximizing narrative, and the plan did include an adjusted operating-income measure — but the incentive geometry and the observed outcome are consistent.

The positive: the successor leadership divested the segment, paid down $353.7M of debt, restored a 20.3% operating margin, and sold the company for cash at a full multiple. Insider-transaction data was unobtainable, so there is no direct alignment evidence either way. A 4.5 reflects documented capital-allocation destruction offset by a competent terminal outcome.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: None. There is no forward event that could move a security trading at a fixed cash consideration. The merger closed on or about June 10, 2026; the price is pinned within $0.05 of the stated $180.00 consideration.

**Timing window**: Not applicable. The value-determining event has already occurred.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: The reported 7.3% of float is almost certainly stale merger-arbitrage positioning captured before close, representing the long-cash/short-stock or hedge legs of arb books rather than directional bearish views. In a completed cash deal, short positions are covered at the consideration price; there is no squeeze dynamic because there is no price discovery.

**Borrow and options signal**: ATM implied volatility of 3.8%–7.9% is the lowest-signal options environment possible — the market prices essentially no volatility, which is precisely what a fixed-consideration structure produces. No cost-to-borrow inference is available. Post-delisting, borrow would in any case be unobtainable.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Maximum theoretical gain to a short is $180.00 minus the deal price, which is zero. Reward:risk is undefined and effectively 0:1 net of transaction costs and any settlement mechanics.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE**. The AVOID rating records the absence of an investable instrument, not an actively deteriorating business. Shorting requires a security with a forward price; this one has a contractual terminal value.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** There is no MASI-linked security to re-enter. Specifically:

1. **No investable common equity.** The June 10, 2026 closing 8-K, the DEFM14A shareholder vote, and the zero-volume terminal price prints establish that MASI common stock ceased to be a trading instrument. Remove it from the coverage universe pending confirmation of deregistration.
2. **No residual contingent security identified.** No CVR, stub equity, escrow interest, or tracking instrument was disclosed in any source reviewed. Reporting indicates the Apple damages proceeds accrue to Danaher, so the litigation upside is not retained by former holders. If a CVR or escrow release mechanism surfaces in the unread merger agreement, that instrument — not the common stock — would require separate evaluation.
3. **Exposure to the underlying franchise runs through Danaher (DHR).** An investor who wants the patient-monitoring franchise, the FCF stream, the AI-enabled device pipeline, and the Apple damages recovery must own DHR. The Masimo share of Danaher's portfolio is not established in the available sources. That is a distinct thesis requiring its own analysis; nothing in this report supports or opposes it.

**Timeline.** Permanent. Cash mergers do not reverse.

**Verification items that would refine, not reverse, this conclusion**: reading the DEFM14A to confirm the exact $180.00 consideration and any adjustment mechanism; reading the June 10 8-K to confirm closing and consideration paid; confirming Form 25/Form 15 delisting and deregistration; confirming whether the Sound United divestiture proceeds were as inferred. None of these change the recommendation.

**On the score trajectory.** Four prior reports carried HOLD at 5.6, 5.8, 5.6 and 5.5, with competitive position declining 7.2 → 6.5 and growth prospects 5.5 → 5.0 while valuation improved 4.0 → 4.5. Those trends described a fairly-valued franchise with narrowing differentiation, a defensible reading at the time. The change to AVOID at 3.8 is not a continuation of that decline and should not be read as new fundamental deterioration; the standalone business improved materially in FY2025 on every operating metric. The composite falls because valuation must be marked to a fixed $180.00 consideration (0.028% remaining) and growth to zero forward participation.

## Conclusion

**AVOID — no investable instrument.** Masimo was acquired by Danaher for cash, with the transaction closing on or about June 10, 2026. The frozen valuation basis is $179.95 (June 12, 2026, zero volume), the authoritative quote provider returns no price, and all five remaining analyst targets sit at exactly $180.00 — leaving 0.028% of consideration and no forward participation in the business.

The underlying asset was good and getting better. FY2025 continuing operations generated $1,526.9M of revenue at a 61.9% gross margin, $310.0M of operating income (20.3%), and $198.4M of free cash flow, against roughly 1.05x net leverage after a $353.7M two-year debt reduction. The reported $151.5M net loss is a disposal artifact masking approximately $207.7M of continuing-operations earnings. Danaher paid a full strategic price — roughly 6.5x revenue and 28x an EBITDA proxy — and guided the deal as accretive within the year, which is credible on those numbers. Former shareholders realized that value in cash. The residual upside, including the upheld Apple damages award of approximately $634M, now belongs to the acquirer.

Analytical humility is warranted on two points. The exact $180.00 per-share figure is externally reported rather than filing-verified, because the merger proxy and closing 8-K were inaccessible; the arithmetic reconciliation (53.6M–54.2M shares at $180 plus $365.7M net debt ≈ $10.0–10.1B against ~$9.9B reported) supports it without proving it. And the full 10-K narrative sections were unavailable, so no risk-factor analysis was possible. Neither gap affects the operative conclusion, which is that the security has stopped existing.
