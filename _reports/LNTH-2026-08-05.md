## Executive Summary

On August 3, 2026, Lantheus and Curium US Holdings announced a definitive merger agreement: **$102.50 per share in cash at closing plus non-transferable contingent value rights of up to $12.00 per share**, aggregate transaction value up to approximately $8.0 billion, expected to close in **H1 2027**, with no financing condition and unanimous board approval. This single fact governs the security. Fundamental analysis of PYLARIFY reimbursement, DEFINITY share, or the MK-6240 pipeline now matters only to the extent it informs two questions: whether the deal closes, and what the CVRs are worth.

The arithmetic is unforgiving for new capital. At $101.11, the spread to cash is $1.39, or **1.37%**. Assuming a close around mid-2027 — roughly eleven months out, consistent with the company's own H1 2027 guidance — that annualizes to about **1.5%**. Holding to close on the cash component alone therefore delivers a low single-digit annualized return. The entire incremental return sits in CVRs that are non-transferable, measured against FY2028-FY2030 sales thresholds, and payable by a privately held, debt-financed acquirer. Against that, a deal break plausibly returns the stock to $75-85 given the standalone business has deteriorated since the May 21, 2026 unaffected close of $94.47 — the LNTH-2501 Complete Response Letter arrived in late June, FY2026 guidance was suspended, and the company is being run by an interim CEO drawn from the board chair.

Deal risk is not trivial. Curium is a direct competitor in radiopharmaceuticals and is simultaneously **Lantheus's licensee for piflufolastat F-18 in Europe**, where it markets the product as PYLCLARI. Curium's own CEO described the acquisition as adding "a complementary F18-isotope based prostate diagnostics franchise." A ten-to-eleven month expected close for an all-cash deal with no financing condition is itself evidence that both parties anticipate substantive antitrust review. The options market disagrees with this caution: ATM implied volatility collapsed from 64.1% on July 19 to 9.1%, and the nearest expiry prices a ±2.8% move. That is a market pricing near-certainty of completion. My assessment is that completion probability is high but not the ~95% the 1.37% spread implies once CVR value is stripped out.

Recommendation: **HOLD**, with the honest classification that the residual return is uncertain rather than confidently positive. Fifth consecutive HOLD, but on entirely different grounds than the prior four.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet is genuinely strong, and this is the dimension that has improved.** At March 31, 2026: cash and equivalents $498.6M, up from $359.1M at year-end 2025; the $750M revolver undrawn with maturity extended to December 2029; only funded debt is $574.996M of 2.625% convertible senior notes due December 15, 2027 (carrying value $570.4M). Net debt is therefore roughly **$76M** — effectively unlevered. Covenants (3.00x minimum interest coverage, 3.50x maximum total net leverage) were in compliance.

A definitional correction worth carrying: screens reporting "debt/equity 1.04" are dividing total liabilities ($1,137.6M) by equity ($1,089.8M). Interest-bearing debt to equity is approximately **0.52**.

**Cash generation is solid but should not be run-rated.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $125.1M against $3.2M capex, giving roughly $121.9M free cash flow. FY2025 free cash flow was $354.1M on a 23.0% margin. The Q1 figure benefited from named working-capital timing — a $13.9M income tax receivable decrease, an $18.6M taxes-payable increase, $23.0M from accrued expenses — so the simple $500M annualization is a sensitivity, not a forecast. A normalized FY2026 figure anchored on FY2025 actuals sits nearer $380-420M.

**Reported earnings quality in Q1 2026 was poor.** Net income of $118.4M and diluted EPS of $1.80 versus $72.9M and $1.02 look like 62% growth. Pretax income of $156.4M included a **$59.3M gain on the SPECT sale** net of transaction costs and a **$14.9M unrealized mark on equity securities** (Perspective Therapeutics and Radiopharm Theranostics holdings). Stripping both leaves core pretax income near $82.2M; at the reported 24.3% effective rate, core net income is approximately $62.2M, or roughly **$0.95 per diluted share** — about half the reported figure. Operating income actually **fell 20.3%** to $81.3M.

**Margins are compressing on price, not volume.** Gross margin declined from 63.8% to 61.2%; operating margin from 27.4% to 21.6%. Management names three causes: PYLARIFY net selling price, higher intangible amortization from the LMI deal (amortization $16.7M versus $8.0M), and the SPECT sale. Adding back $7.8M of strategic-program charges and $3.9M of acquisition costs gives roughly $93.0M of adjusted operating income, a 24.6% margin — still 280bp below the prior year. FY2025 operating income of approximately $311M was down roughly 32% from FY2024 on flat revenue.

**Gross-to-net erosion is the most underappreciated line in the filings.** Rebate provisions rose from $63.5M in FY2024 to $167.6M in FY2025 against flat revenue. The Q1 2026 provision of $49.848M equals **13.2% of revenue**. Volume rose in both periods while revenue fell, which points to price concession. The filings do not disaggregate rebates by product, so part of the increase may reflect Neuraceq's contracting and Medicaid obligations rather than PYLARIFY discounting alone; that split cannot be verified from public disclosure.

**Convertible dilution, stated precisely.** Conversion rate 12.5291 shares per $1,000 principal implies a $79.81 conversion price and 7.204M shares gross. Principal must be settled in cash; only the spread may be share-settled at the company's election. At current prices the spread is roughly $156M, or **1.539M shares — a 2.36% increase in share count, equivalent to 2.31% ownership dilution**. Reported Q1 2026 diluted shares of 65,772K included zero convertible effect because the average price was below $79.81. The 17.8539-share cap (10.266M shares) is reachable only through make-whole adjustments in specified corporate events and should not be added to current share count. The notes mature December 2027, after expected deal close; a fundamental-change repurchase right at 100% of principal plausibly triggers on the merger, implying Curium funds roughly $575M-plus beyond the equity consideration. That treatment has not been verified from the merger agreement.

**Contingent consideration: large notional, small carrying value.** Aggregate potential contingent payments under license agreements approach $4.9B notional; recorded fair value was **$94.0M** at March 31, 2026. The gap is probability-weighting and discounting of milestones tied to approvals and sales thresholds not yet achieved, not aggressive accounting. Note the direction of travel: the company flagged that a change in probability on the first LNTH-2501 regulatory milestone could cause material fluctuation. Following the June CRL, the liability should be expected to decrease, producing a **credit to operating expense** in Q2 or Q3 2026 — a low-quality earnings tailwind to discount when the Q2 numbers land.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is negative and the acquisitions are masking it. FY2025 revenue of $1,541.6M was up 0.5%, including roughly $51.4M of newly acquired Neuraceq and CDMO revenue. Q1 2026's +1.2% headline combines $35.4M of new Neuraceq revenue and $5.6M of partnership growth against a $16.7M PYLARIFY decline and a $25.2M SPECT gap.

FY2026 guidance of $1.40-1.45B, issued in February and reiterated in May, implied roughly flat to −3% against a pro-forma continuing base near $1,442M — and that is before crediting six-plus additional months of Neuraceq ownership. Management's own guidance embedded a negative organic trend. **That guidance was suspended on August 3** on account of the merger, and Q2 results will be released August 6 pre-market **without a conference call**, removing the normal channel for reconciling the quarter.

Pipeline outcomes over the last eighteen months have been adverse. PNT2002 was abandoned after SPLASH overall-survival results were confounded by crossover, writing off most of a $250M upfront to POINT Biopharma. LNTH-2501 (Octevy) received a **Complete Response Letter in late June 2026** on unresolved third-party manufacturing conditions, with no safety or efficacy concerns raised — the asset was a principal rationale for the $276.4M Evergreen acquisition. A piflufolastat renal-carcinoma study was discontinued in late 2025.

Two forward items remain live. **MK-6240**, an F-18 tau PET agent, met co-primary endpoints in two pivotal studies with an NDA accepted October 2025 and a **PDUFA target date of August 13, 2026**. **PYLARIFY TruVu** was approved March 6, 2026 with rolling commercial availability from Q4 2026; Lantheus will seek three fresh years of transitional pass-through status, which if granted restores ASP-plus-6% payment. Both matter mainly through the CVRs now.

Estimate revisions do not signal inflection. Current-year consensus rose 3.7% over 90 days to $5.36, but next-year consensus was cut 1.4% in 30 days to $6.30 and is roughly flat over 90 days (+0.1%). This is nowhere near the revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection. The earnings track record is mixed — two beats, two misses across four quarters, including a 40.2% beat in Q4 2025 and an 8.0% miss in Q2 2025 — which argues for modest confidence in forward estimates that are now unanchored by company guidance.

## Valuation Assessment

The relevant valuation is the deal, but the standalone anchor is worth establishing first, on matching bases. Trailing GAAP EPS of $3.41 gives 29.6x at $101.11 — but that GAAP figure is contaminated by the SPECT gain and equity marks. Non-GAAP adjusted EPS across the last four reported quarters ($1.57, $1.27, $1.67, $1.46) sums to $5.97, or 16.9x. On consensus adjusted estimates, $101.11 is **18.9x FY2026 ($5.36)** and **16.0x FY2027 ($6.30)**. The deal cash of $102.50 is 19.1x and 16.3x respectively.

The peer set is close to uninformative: median P/E 60.1 across MMSI at 28.8x, NVST at 60.1x, ICUI at 77.1x, HAE at 38.9x and XRAY at negative earnings. These are device and dental companies with different capital intensity and no CMS radiopharmaceutical payment exposure. LNTH's discount to that median is explained by negative organic growth and a structural reimbursement disadvantage, not by mispricing. Cheapness relative to a distorted peer set is not a reason to own this.

**Deal arithmetic from the authoritative $101.11 basis:**

| Outcome | Value | Return from $101.11 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash at closing only | $102.50 | +1.37% |
| Cash + PV of probable CVRs | ~$104.00-105.50 | +2.9% to +4.3% |
| Cash + full $12.00 CVR (nominal) | $114.50 | +13.2% |
| Deal blocked or terminated | $78-85 | −16% to −23% |

The cash spread of 1.37% over roughly eleven months annualizes to about **1.5%**. That means the market is pricing either near-certainty of close plus meaningful CVR value, or is simply crowded with arbitrageurs accepting thin carry. The August 3 session traded 8.24M shares against 1.25M average — a 6.6x turnover consistent with a wholesale handoff from fundamental holders to arbitrage desks.

**CVR valuation.** The milestone structure is favorable in its definitions — "Aggregate Adjusted Sales" is net only of discounts, rebates and allowances (explicitly not reduced for sales commissions) and adds royalties, sublicense income, clinical collaboration revenue and DEFINITY kit revenue, across globally scoped franchise definitions that sweep in PYLCLARI, GE's Japanese royalties, RM2, MK-6240 and NAV-4694. Tranche-by-tranche:

- **Neurology >$300M in any of FY2028-FY2030 ($2.00)** is the most achievable single tranche. Neuraceq annualizes near $142M; MK-6240 approval on August 13 plus NAV-4694 would need to roughly double the franchise across a three-year measurement window in which any one year qualifies. Amyloid and tau PET demand is tied to anti-amyloid therapy uptake, which is expanding.
- **Prostate >$950M in FY2030 ($1.00)** requires PYLARIFY/TruVu plus ex-U.S. royalties to hold roughly flat against FY2025's $989M over five years, through an exclusivity expiry and a full site-by-site product transition. Plausible; the $1,200M-plus tiers requiring 21% to 77% growth are considerably less so.
- **DEFINITY >$400M in FY2030 ($1.00)** needs about a 4.3% CAGR from the $338M annualized run rate, against a disclosed CREATES Act sample request signalling active generic development.

A reasonable expected nominal payout is $3-6 of the $12.00. Discounting to present value is where the structure loses most of its worth: the CVRs are **non-transferable**, so holders cannot monetize, and payments fall in roughly 2029-2031. At a 12% rate appropriate to an illiquid claim on a CapVest-controlled private acquirer that will be substantially more levered post-close, four-to-five year discount factors of 0.57-0.64 reduce that to roughly **$1.50-3.50 of present value**.

A probability-weighted check: 85% × ~$104.50 plus 15% × $82 equals $101.1 against a $101.11 close. The stock is fair on my assumptions. That is the basis for a valuation score of 4.2 — not a value trap in the classic sense, but an asymmetry that has already been consumed.

Analyst targets now cluster mechanically around deal terms: low $102.50 (the cash), high $115.00 (approximately cash plus full CVR), mean $107.22. The "degrading" sentiment reading — strong buys 2→1, buys 8→3, holds 3→7 — is the standard analyst response to a cash deal near the offer price, not new fundamental information.

## Competitive Landscape

PYLARIFY's competitive problem is a payment asymmetry, and it is already realized rather than prospective. Transitional pass-through status expired December 31, 2024, since which traditional Medicare has paid on **mean unit cost** rather than **ASP plus 6%**. Competing PSMA PET agents retained TPT status, and a competing agent obtained fresh TPT effective October 1, 2025. Competitors are better reimbursed for the same clinical purpose. CMS acknowledged in the 2026 OPPS preamble that ASP could have value for diagnostic radiopharmaceutical payment but wants universal reporting first, and ASP reporting remains voluntary. This is multi-year advocacy with no committed timeline.

PYLARIFY's new chemical entity exclusivity expired in May 2026, opening the ANDA filing window; six Orange Book patents run to 2037 and no Paragraph IV notice had been received as of the FY2025 10-K. The TruVu transition adds self-inflicted execution risk that the company describes with unusual candor: once a PET manufacturing facility begins supplying TruVu it will stop supplying PYLARIFY, and "if customers do not adopt PYLARIFY TruVu we may not be able to reintroduce PYLARIFY and we would then lose customers to competitors." Lantheus is retiring a $989M product site by site in favor of a successor whose reimbursement status is not yet granted, with no disclosed fallback.

DEFINITY retains 80%-plus share against three approved competitors, but composition-of-matter protection lapsed in June 2019 and protection now rests on method-of-use patents to 2037 plus manufacturing patents. The CREATES Act sample request establishes that a developer is pursuing a generic; it establishes nothing about formulation success, filing timing, or patent outcome.

The acquirer relationship deserves literal statement because it drives deal risk. Curium **licenses piflufolastat F-18 from Lantheus for Europe** and markets it as PYLCLARI — Curium is Lantheus's licensee and pays Lantheus, not a customer. Curium is separately a direct competitor: founded 2017 by CapVest, recapitalized last year near $7B enterprise value, 3,800-plus employees, 80-plus manufacturing sites, 45-plus products, with a late-stage prostate cancer and neuroendocrine therapeutic pipeline. Its CEO framed the rationale as acquiring U.S. commercial infrastructure plus "a complementary F18-isotope based prostate diagnostics franchise." A licensee acquiring its licensor while both compete in U.S. PSMA PET diagnostics is precisely the structure that attracts a second request. The eleven-month expected close corroborates that expectation.

## Risk Assessment

**Antitrust is the dominant unpriced risk.** The horizontal overlap in prostate diagnostics is acknowledged in the acquirer's own framing. Remedies (divestiture of overlapping assets) are a more likely outcome than a block, and remedies typically delay rather than kill; but the market's 1.37% spread leaves no compensation for either delay or failure. A twelve-month extension of the timeline alone destroys the arb's carry.

**Break downside.** The May 21 unaffected close was $94.47. Since then the standalone business absorbed the LNTH-2501 CRL, guidance suspension, and a further quarter of PYLARIFY price erosion, with no permanent CEO in place. A 2027 termination plausibly resolves to $75-85, or −16% to −23%.

**Governance and litigation.** Brian Markison retired as CEO effective December 31, 2025; board chair Mary Anne Heino became interim CEO January 1, 2026 pending a search; President Paul Blanchfield departed November 2025. A consolidated securities class action (*In re Lantheus Holdings Securities Litigation*, S.D.N.Y., amended complaint March 13, 2026, motion to dismiss due May 11, 2026) and a stayed derivative action are pending. Novartis/AAA patent litigation over the PNT2003 ANDA was tried in December 2025 with no decision issued as of the Q1 10-Q.

**Deal-specific consideration risk.** The SPECT sale consideration is only partly cash ($31.4M of $130.5M fair value); the seller note is explicitly unsecured with no collateral, and contingent consideration may be settled in equity. That is a separate, smaller collection risk sitting inside the balance sheet Curium is buying.

**CVR structure risk.** Non-transferability is the binding constraint. Former holders receive an illiquid claim, cannot hedge or sell it, and depend on a private acquirer's audited sales definitions three to five years out. Definitional disputes over "Aggregate Adjusted Sales" are a realistic source of shortfall.

### Options Market Signal

The options market has priced the deal as close to done. ATM implied volatility collapsed from 64.1% on July 19 to 9.1% by August 5; near-term ATM IV of 14.5% sits 35.7 percentage points below 50.2% trailing realized volatility, with the nearest expiry implying only ±2.8%. Put/call open interest of 0.13 and volume of 0.07, with a call-heavy skew reading of −13.9%, indicate essentially no demand for downside protection and residual call interest consistent with CVR-upside speculation. The term structure is technically backwardated (14.5% at one month, 22.6% at three months, 10.6% at six months) — the three-month bulge captures the August 6 Q2 release, the August 13 MK-6240 PDUFA and the likely proxy filing window, after which vol is priced to die into a pinned deal price.

The divergence worth flagging: **13.1% of float is short with 6.5 days to cover**, which sits awkwardly against a 9% implied vol. Much of that is likely mechanical — convertible arbitrage hedging notes that are now in the money at $101.11 against a $79.81 conversion price, plus merger-arb hedging. It should not be read as a fundamental bearish position, but it does mean the shares are technically tight if the deal breaks and hedges unwind in either direction.

My reading is that options pricing is more confident than the evidence supports. Cheap vol relative to realized is a reason to prefer defined-risk expressions over the common stock, not a confirmation of the equity.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $114.50 (+13.2%)

Regulatory clearance arrives in H1 2027 with limited or no remedy. Holders collect $102.50 cash and retain CVRs that ultimately pay near the maximum: MK-6240 is approved August 13, 2026 and, with Neuraceq and NAV-4694, drives the Neurology franchise past $350M in one of FY2028-FY2030 ($3.00); TruVu secures fresh TPT status, restoring ASP-plus-6% payment and stabilizing then growing the prostate franchise past $1,200M in FY2030 with RM2 and ex-U.S. royalties included ($4.00-8.00); DEFINITY compounds past $400M absent generic entry ($1.00). Nominal realization approaches $114.50 across 2029-2031. Note that even this path delivers roughly 13% nominal over four-plus years — a low single-digit annualized return.

### Bear Case — $80 (−20.9%)

Antitrust review escalates into a second request in late 2026 and a challenge in 2027 given the acquirer's status as both competitor and European licensee of the target's lead molecule. Either the parties abandon the transaction or a required divestiture proves unacceptable. Lantheus re-emerges standalone in mid-2027 with PYLARIFY exclusivity expired for a year, the TruVu site transition mid-flight and TPT status unresolved, LNTH-2501 still unapproved, an interim CEO, a live securities class action, and $575M of converts maturing December 2027. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $6.30 — which already looks stale against suspended guidance and 30-day downward revisions — is cut materially. At 14x a reduced $5.50-6.00, the stock is $77-84.

### Base Case — approximately $104.00-105.50 realized

Deal closes in H1 2027, potentially with a modest divestiture, at $102.50 cash. CVRs pay $3-6 nominal across 2029-2031, worth roughly $1.50-3.50 in present value. Total realized value of $104.00-106.00 against $101.11 today, spread over up to five years. Probability-weighting the outcomes (85% close, 15% break) produces roughly $101.10 of expected value — approximately today's price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders should stay: the $102.50 cash floor sits above the current price, and the CVRs carry free optionality on MK-6240 and the amyloid/tau PET market that cannot be replicated after tendering. New capital should not enter at $101.11 — 1.4% of spread plus illiquid long-dated optionality is not adequate compensation for 16-23% break risk in a transaction with genuine horizontal overlap.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Price below $96** while the merger agreement remains in force and no second request has issued — a 6.8%-plus cash spread would compensate for antitrust risk and make the CVRs a free option.
2. **HSR clearance or expiration of the waiting period without a second request**, which would compress break probability toward 3-5% and validate CVR-driven upside toward $107-110.
3. **MK-6240 approval on or near August 13, 2026 with a launch trajectory that puts the Neurology franchise credibly above $300M by FY2028**, materially raising the probability-weighted CVR value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **A second request from the FTC or DOJ, or a formal challenge**, particularly one focused on U.S. PSMA PET overlap — at that point the spread should widen well beyond current levels and the correct action is to exit rather than average.
2. **Any amendment reducing cash consideration, extending the outside date beyond 2027, or adding a financing condition.**
3. **Termination of the merger agreement**, which converts this back into a standalone equity with negative organic growth, an interim CEO, and a December 2027 convert maturity.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 results August 6 (pre-market, no call — read the contingent-consideration credit and the PYLARIFY rebate provision carefully); MK-6240 PDUFA August 13, 2026; the definitive merger proxy and shareholder vote, likely Q4 2026, which will disclose the merger agreement's treatment of the convertible notes, break-fee structure and regulatory-efforts covenants; HSR milestone disclosures thereafter. The next hard information event is 24 hours away.

## Conclusion

Lantheus scores 4.9, down from 5.3, and the composition of that decline matters more than the level. Financial health remains the strongest dimension at 6.5 — $498.6M of cash against roughly $76M of net debt and 23% free cash flow margins — and the improvement trend from prior reports is confirmed by the Q1 cash build. Competitive position continues the decline flagged in prior work, now with harder evidence: the CMS mean-unit-cost disadvantage against TPT-advantaged competitors, NCE expiry in May 2026, a CREATES Act sample request on DEFINITY, and a TruVu transition management itself describes as irreversible if adoption fails. Growth and valuation both sit near 4.2 because the withdrawn guidance implied flat-to-negative organic revenue and because the equity's return is now capped by a $102.50 cash price 1.37% above the market.

The prior four HOLDs rested on a fundamental thesis. This one does not. The correct frame is a merger-arbitrage position with a thin spread, a long and antitrust-exposed timeline, and a non-transferable CVR whose present value is perhaps $1.50-3.50 against a $12.00 headline. The market, judged by 9% implied volatility, treats completion as near-certain. Given that the acquirer is both a competitor and the target's European licensee for its lead molecule, and given the parties' own eleven-month close estimate, that confidence is not fully earned. Hold if you own it for the cash floor and the option. Deploy new capital elsewhere, or wait for a spread near 7%.
