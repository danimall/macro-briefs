## Executive Summary

HF Sinclair is a mid-size independent refiner operating seven complex refineries (678,000 BPSD) with integrated marketing, midstream, lubricants, and renewable diesel operations. The stock has appreciated 84% over the past year from its $34.42 low, driven by recovering refining margins and strong earnings beats.

The fundamental setup is constructive: current-quarter EPS estimates have been revised up 86% in 90 days, the company has beaten consensus in four consecutive quarters (including a 1,157% beat in Q1 2026), and forward P/E sits at approximately 9x on FY2026 consensus of $7.67. However, two factors prevent a full upgrade: (1) the unprecedented simultaneous departure of both CEO and CFO over disclosure-process concerns creates a governance vacuum with no permanent replacements named, and (2) approximately $485M (66%) of FY2025 pre-tax income came from EPA small refinery RINs waivers that are under active legal challenge and may not recur.

The 12-month thesis rests on whether the current energy supply environment sustains elevated crack spreads long enough for the company to demonstrate earnings power independent of regulatory windfalls, and whether leadership stabilization restores institutional confidence. At 9x forward earnings with a 2.9% dividend yield, the stock is cheap enough to hold through this uncertainty.

## Company Overview

HF Sinclair operates across five segments: Refining (seven complex refineries in Kansas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, Wyoming, Washington, Utah), Renewables (three renewable diesel units, 378M gallons/year capacity), Marketing (1,744+ branded Sinclair stations with 10% annual growth target), Lubricants & Specialties (Petro-Canada Lubricants, Red Giant Oil, Sonneborn), and Midstream (pipelines, terminals, tankage). The company was formed through HollyFrontier's 2022 acquisition of Sinclair Oil and completed its HEP midstream internalization in December 2023.

Geographic positioning in the Mid-Continent, Rocky Mountain, and Pacific Northwest regions provides structural margin protection: transportation costs limit Gulf Coast refiner competition in these inland markets. The complex refinery configuration allows processing of discounted heavy/sour Canadian crudes into high-value light products.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Recovery:**
Revenue declined from $38.2B (2022 peak) to $26.9B (2025), while operating income fell from $4.05B to $927M over the same period. FY2025 represented a partial recovery from the 2024 trough ($177M net income), with net income reaching $579M. However, $485M of pre-tax income came from one-time RINs waiver benefits — stripping this out, underlying pre-tax earnings power was approximately $247M, barely better than 2024's $218M.

Q1 2026 was genuinely strong: $648M net income ($3.56/share GAAP, $0.69 adjusted) versus consensus expectations for a loss. Higher refining margins, improved throughput (guidance for Q2 at 600-630K bpd), and product price realization drove the beat.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Cash: $978M; Total liquidity: ~$3.0B (including $2B undrawn revolver)
- Long-term debt: $2.8B (all fixed-rate, maturities pushed to 2031-2035 via 2025 refinancing)
- Current ratio: 1.94
- Debt-to-equity: 0.79
- Altman Z-score: 3.33 (safe zone)

The 2025 refinancing ($1.9B in new notes at 5.5-6.25%) eliminated near-term maturity risk. No revolver drawings. The balance sheet is solid for a cyclical refiner.

**Capital Allocation:**
- $340M share repurchases in 2025 (share count down ~8% from 2022)
- $376M dividends ($2.00/share annualized, 2.9% yield)
- Total capital returned to shareholders in 2025: $716M (buybacks + dividends)
- FY2026 capex guidance: $775M ($650M sustaining + $125M growth)
- $459M remaining buyback authorization

**Free Cash Flow:**
FY2025 operating cash flow of $1.315B less estimated capex of ~$450M suggests FCF around $866M. At the current $12.6B market cap, that implies a 6.9% trailing FCF yield — attractive for the sector.

**Structural Growth Vectors:**

## Competitive Landscape

DINO is a second-tier refiner competing against significantly larger players (Marathon Petroleum, Valero, Phillips 66, ExxonMobil). Its competitive advantages are geographic (inland positioning limits coastal competition), operational (complex refinery configuration allows discounted crude processing), and integrative (midstream + marketing provides captive demand).

Disadvantages include scale (678K bpd vs. Marathon's 2.9M bpd), lower capital efficiency (5-year TSR of 209 vs. peer group at 330), and the unprofitable renewables segment that larger peers have managed more effectively. The loss of senior leadership also raises execution risk versus peers with stable management teams.

The marketing segment (Sinclair brand) is a genuine differentiator — 1,744 branded stations with improving unit margins ($0.11/gallon, up from $0.07) provide both demand capture and brand value. The 10% annual growth target is being achieved.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Governance Vacuum (Critical):**
Both CEO and CFO departed in early 2026 over disclosure-process concerns. While the Audit Committee cleared the financials, the interim leadership structure (board chair as CEO, controller as CFO) creates strategic uncertainty. No permanent CEO has been named. This is unprecedented for a company of this size and will weigh on institutional ownership until resolved.

**2. RINs Waiver Dependency (High):**
$485M of FY2025 pre-tax income (66% of total) came from EPA small refinery exemptions. These are under active litigation from the renewable fuel industry and are not guaranteed for future years. If RINs compliance costs return to $475M annually without offsets, earnings would be significantly impaired.

**3. Crack Spread Cyclicality (Moderate):**
The entire thesis depends on sustained elevated refining margins. While the current geopolitical environment supports this, any diplomatic resolution or demand destruction from recession would compress margins rapidly. FY2027 consensus already models 18% EPS decline, suggesting the market expects mean-reversion.

**4. Renewables Drag (Moderate):**
The renewables segment loses money at -$0.16/gallon and is consuming capital that could be deployed more productively. Write-down risk exists on the ~$2.98B goodwill pile if margins don't recover.

**5. Tariff Risk (Moderate):**
The 10% tariff on Canadian crude directly impacts four of DINO's seven refineries that source heavy Canadian barrels. USMCA exemption status uncertain through 2026 review.

## Conclusion

DINO presents a genuinely conflicted setup: strong cyclical tailwinds and cheap valuation colliding with meaningful governance and regulatory risks. The stock is cheap at 9x forward earnings with estimates accelerating. The refining macro is favorable, with geopolitical disruptions and structural supply deficits supporting sustained elevated margins through at least H2 2026.

However, the loss of both CEO and CFO creates a governance overhang that will constrain multiple expansion until resolved. The RINs waiver dependency introduces binary regulatory risk. These factors create a distribution of outcomes too wide for a high-conviction directional call in either direction.

HOLD at current levels.
