## Executive Summary

Churchill Downs is executing the best operating stretch in its history into a falling multiple. Q2 2026 delivered record revenue of $980M (+4.9%), record Adjusted EBITDA of $477M (+5.8%) at a 48.7% margin, GAAP operating margin of 36.4% versus 35.0%, and diluted EPS of $3.42 against $2.99. The 152nd Kentucky Derby set records for Derby Week EBITDA contribution, all-sources handle, and NBC viewership (24.4 million peak, +12%). Yet the shares have fallen roughly 32% from an implied end-2024 level near $133, and enterprise value now sits at 8.8x trailing Adjusted EBITDA of $1,243M versus approximately 12x at the end of 2024. This is multiple compression against rising earnings, and the causal chain is identifiable: 4.4x consolidated leverage repricing in a higher-for-longer rate environment, regional gaming consumer softness, and the arrival of prediction markets as a named competitor in the company's own risk factors.

The decision-relevant new fact is the July 29, 2026 disclosure of a strategic alternative review. New safe-harbor language in the Q2 10-Q and earnings release references "the terms, structure, benefits and costs of any strategic transaction" and whether one "will be consummated on the terms proposed or at all," language absent from the FY2025 10-K. Secondary reporting, corroborated across multiple outlets citing that filing, indicates nine regional gaming properties are being marketed, with Presque Isle (PA), del Lago (NY), Ocean Downs (MD), Hard Rock Sioux City (IA) and Terre Haute (IN) named. The company has disclosed no perimeter, no expected proceeds, no use of proceeds and no timeline. The marketed casinos plausibly carry $250–290M of Adjusted EBITDA, worth $1.6–2.3B gross at regional multiples of 6.5–8x and perhaps $1.5–1.8B net of cash taxes, which would leave roughly 2.8–3.1x leverage against a retained ~$950M–1.0B EBITDA base versus 3.7x reported today. Deleveraging alone does not create the value. The value case requires the retained racing/HRM business to re-rate from today's blended 8.8x toward the 10–12x that a 49%-margin, statutorily protected franchise with the Kentucky Derby attached might command.

That is a credible path, and it is why all eleven covering analysts carry a positive rating with targets from $100 to $157 (mean ~$132). It is also unverified in every economically material respect, and the transaction would concentrate the company into HRMs, a business whose legality rests on state statutes, one of which the Louisiana Supreme Court voided in March 2025, costing $18.9M of revenue and $8.1M of EBITDA and forcing the relocation of ~500 machines. Probability-weighting a $72 bear, $103 base and $128 bull produces roughly +10% expected return over twelve months against a 21% downside leg on a levered equity with a binary process outcome. That does not clear the bar for adding capital. **HOLD**, fifth consecutive, with the classification that the evidence does not yet support a stronger call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Churchill Downs runs three segments, and the racing/HRM complex, not the casinos, is the profit center.

**Live and Historical Racing** contributed $637.0M of FY2025 segment Adjusted EBITDA (+10.9%) and 63% of Q2 2026 segment EBITDA on Derby seasonality. It combines the Churchill Downs Racetrack and Kentucky Derby ($247M of Q2 external revenue versus $228M) with 17 historical racing machine venues operating 10,190 machines across Kentucky, Virginia and New Hampshire. Historical racing pari-mutuel revenue was $265M in Q2 and $522M in H1 2026, about 32% of consolidated revenue and the single largest line item in the company. H1 segment Adjusted EBITDA margin was 49.2%.

**Wagering Services and Solutions** ($177.3M FY2025 EBITDA, +7.1%) holds TwinSpires (H1 handle $1,009M versus $993M), Exacta Systems (the central-determinant technology serving both CHDN venues and third-party HRM operators in six states) and 51% of United Tote. TwinSpires supplies FanDuel and DraftKings ADW platform technology and operations for horse-race wagering. They are commercial counterparties buying CHDN technology, not owners of it.

**Gaming** ($483.0M FY2025 EBITDA, −4.7%) is ten wholly-owned casinos plus two equity-method investments (61.3% of Rivers Des Plaines, accounted for under the equity method because of Rush Street participating rights, and 50% of Miami Valley Gaming). Roughly $195–200M of that segment EBITDA is the equity-method contribution, so the wholly-owned casino portfolio generates approximately $285–290M, and it is this portfolio, largely, that is now for sale.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue of $2,925.9M rose 7.0%. GAAP operating income fell $25.2M to $683.8M and net income fell 10.3% to $383.0M, but the MD&A bridge is dominated by non-cash and non-recurring items: $43.6M of incremental impairment expense, $17.2M of transaction expense, $8.5M of SG&A, and a $10.3M Gaming segment decline. Adjusted EBITDA rose 4.0% to a record $1,205.3M. The cash engine improved while GAAP earnings did not, and the distinction is mechanical rather than promotional.

H1 2026 accelerated: revenue $1,643M (+4.2%), operating income $500M (+8.2%), net income to CDI $324M (+10.2%), diluted EPS $4.58 versus $3.98, Adjusted EBITDA $734M (+5.5%). Gaming stabilized in the quarter (+$6M EBITDA, +4.7%) with equity investments contributing +$4M on strength at Rivers Des Plaines and Miami Valley Gaming, reversing the FY2025 drag.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $512M against net income of $325M, and total capex was $117M ($38M maintenance, $79M projects), leaving roughly $395M of half-year free cash flow. Do not annualize: Q2 alone was 38.4% of trailing-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA because Derby Week lands there, and H1 absorbs a $31M deferred-revenue drawdown as Q1-collected Derby cash converts to Q2 revenue. The normalized frame is FY2025: $769.8M of operating cash flow less approximately $291M of total capex (net investing outflow of $471.5M less the ~$180M Casino Salem asset acquisition) leaves roughly $479M of free cash flow, a 7.6% yield on today's $6.33B market capitalization. That is the most robust single valuation fact in the file.

**Leverage — three definitions, one conclusion.** Total debt at June 30, 2026 was $4,795M, down $360M in six months. Company-reported net bank leverage of 3.7x reproduces exactly: ($4,795M − $196M cash) / $1,243.3M TTM Adjusted EBITDA = 3.70x, improved from 4.11x at year-end. The covenant test — consolidated total *secured* net leverage of 1.4x against a 4.0x maximum, interest coverage 3.9x against a 2.5x minimum — is far looser than it appears because it excludes roughly $3.1B of unsecured notes. Near-term covenant breach risk is remote. But Adjusted EBITDA includes CHDN's share of JV EBITDA (~$200M annualized) while the JVs' own $765M of debt sits outside CHDN's balance sheet; on consolidated EBITDA alone, leverage is $4,599M / ~$1,043M ≈ **4.4x**. Cross-company comparisons should use that figure. The JV earnings do convert to cash at a high rate (H1 distributions of $72M against $77M of equity income), so this is a comparability issue rather than an earnings-quality problem.

**The maturity mechanic is the binding constraint on capital allocation.** Current maturities jumped from $63M to $663M as the $600M 5.50% 2027 Notes (due April 1, 2027) moved current, dropping the current ratio to 0.36 from 0.60. The Revolver and Term Loan A, stated 2029, carry a springing maturity if material debt maturing before July 3, 2029 is not refinanced or extended past that date at least 91 days before its stated maturity, which places a practical deadline around **December 31, 2026**, roughly four months out. The same test hits the $700M 2028 Notes around October 2027. The incremental cost is small in isolation (refinancing $600M from 5.50% to ~6.5% adds ~$6M of annual interest), and $861M of revolver availability makes execution near-certain. The constraint is not solvency; it is that this deadline plausibly explains why $425.3M of FY2025 repurchases became **zero** in H1 2026, with $1,006M of debt repayments against $646M of borrowings.

An Altman Z-Score of 0.93 will screen this company as distressed. The components are traceable: negative working capital by design (casinos and racetracks collect before they spend, and the 2027 notes are now current), thin book equity of $1,341M against $3.4B of goodwill and intangibles precisely because $425M of buybacks reduced equity, and low asset turnover on a $2.9B PP&E base. The model was calibrated on manufacturers. Interest coverage of 3.9x, $861M of undrawn revolver, $512M of half-year operating cash flow and net debt down $355M in six months are the decision-useful solvency facts. The screen is nonetheless a correct pointer to structurally negative working capital and a $663M current maturity.

**Financial health: 5.3.** Strong and improving cash generation, ordinary gaming-sector leverage, remote covenant risk, offset by 4.4x consolidated leverage in a higher-for-longer environment, a near-term maturity that has already consumed the buyback, $465M of committed projects with spending back-loaded into 2027, and $1.7B of variable-rate debt where +100bp costs ~$13M of net income.

## Growth Analysis

The organic engine is intact and running above the industry. Live & Historical Racing EBITDA grew 10.9% in FY2025 and the Derby franchise set records again in 2026, with Oaks day in primetime for the first time, 2.4 million viewers, and record Oaks-day handle. HRM capacity is still expanding: Marshall Yards (225 machines) opened February 25, 2026; Rockingham Grand Casino ($180–200M, targeted mid-2027) replaces the temporary Salem venue; Victory Run at the racetrack ($280–300M total, April 2028) upgrades the Derby's highest-yield hospitality inventory. Exacta collects a toll on third-party HRM operators, including competitors, and is expanding internationally.

Consensus reflects steady rather than inflecting growth: current-year EPS $6.90 (+0.3% over 30 days, +0.5% over 90) and next-year $7.38 (+1.9% over 30 days, +1.1% over 90). Estimates are broadly rising, which is directionally confirming, but nowhere near the magnitude that signals a fundamental change. The Q2 print itself was a marginal miss: adjusted EPS $3.45 against $3.49, and the four-quarter record is two beats, two misses. Forward visibility is adequate, not high-confidence. No FY2026 revenue or EBITDA guidance was located; the only verified forward figure is project capex of $180–220M.

Three counterweights temper the growth score. First, the divestiture shrinks the revenue base by roughly 20–25% of EBITDA; per-share growth then depends on proceeds redeployment that has not been described. Second, Central Virginia HRM revenue fell $4M and EBITDA $5M in Q2 (H1: −$5M / −$9M) on "increased competition," and Virginia has been the growth engine and the destination for the relocated Louisiana machines. Northern Virginia (+$11M H1 EBITDA from The Rose Dumfries) more than offset it, but the deterioration is in the company's own disclosure and its cause is not identified. Third, the underlying sport is contracting: US thoroughbred races fell 4.7% in 2025 to roughly 29,000. CHDN is taking share of a shrinking pool, and its ability to race at all depends on annually negotiated Horsemen's Group consents.

**Growth prospects: 5.9.** Durable mid-single-digit organic growth with margin support and a visible project pipeline, against a shrinking sport, a competitive intrusion in a key market, and a restructuring that reduces the base before it improves the multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

Three anchors, all consistent with a stock priced below its own recent history and below any coherent peer read.

**Enterprise multiple.** EV = $6,330M equity + $4,599M net debt + $50M redeemable NCI = $10.98B, or **8.8x** TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $1,243.3M. At end-2024, on similar EBITDA and roughly $133 of implied share price, the same calculation would have produced approximately 12x. Roughly 3.2 turns of enterprise multiple have come out with earnings rising.

**Earnings multiple.** At $90.82 the stock trades at **13.2x** current-year consensus of $6.90 and **12.3x** next-year $7.38. The profile's 15.24x trailing figure does not reconcile with $90.82 on FY2025 diluted EPS of $5.33 (which computes to 17.0x) and appears to reflect a stale price; I have used consensus-based multiples throughout.

**Free cash flow.** Normalized FY2025 FCF of roughly $479M is a 7.6% yield. The annual dividend of $0.438 is a 0.49% yield, essentially no income support, and the 15-year increase streak is a governance signal rather than a return.

The peer table is close to unusable: CZR at −13.2x and PENN at −3.0x are loss-making, BYD's 3.7x is distorted by non-operating items, and RSI's 82x is a different business model. RRR at 21.5x is the only comparable regional operator, and the 21.5x median it anchors is not a defensible benchmark. More useful is the internal comparison: regional casinos transact at 6.5–8x EV/EBITDA and CHDN's blended multiple is 8.8x, which implies the market is assigning only a modest premium to a racing/HRM franchise running 49% segment margins with statutory protection and a 152-year-old, single-location media property.

**The sum-of-the-parts arithmetic, with every input stated.** Wholly-owned casino EBITDA of $285–290M less the properties likely retained implies roughly $250–290M in the marketed set. At 6.5–8x that is $1.6–2.3B gross; after cash taxes on gain (basis largely established in 2019–2022 acquisitions), perhaps $1.5–1.8B net. Applied to $4,599M of net debt, leverage on a retained ~$950M–1.0B EBITDA base falls to roughly 2.8–3.1x from 3.7x today. Deleveraging of that magnitude is worth something but not a re-rating by itself. Re-rate the retained business to 10x and equity is roughly $99/share; 11x gives ~$114; 12x gives ~$128. Every input in that chain — perimeter, EBITDA split, multiple, tax leakage — is an estimate with wide error bars, and an OpCo/PropCo structure with a gaming REIT would change the math entirely by raising headline proceeds while leaving rent behind.

Analyst targets span $100 (BofA, cut from $110) to $157 (Mizuho, raised from $155), with Susquehanna at $124 and J.P. Morgan reiterating Buy, mean ~$131.6, median $132, every visible target above the current price. On a de-rated, levered name whose central catalyst has no disclosed economics, I treat that dispersion as evidence of genuine disagreement about restructuring value rather than as independent confirmation of upside. Targets clustered 10–70% above spot with eleven positive ratings and zero holds also suggests some anchoring to pre-de-rating multiples.

**Valuation: 6.3.** Cheap on every internal anchor, with an identifiable catalyst rather than bare cheapness, but the catalyst's economics are unverified, and at 4.4x consolidated leverage each turn of EV/EBITDA compression costs roughly $18 of equity value per share, which is why the multiple fell as far as it did.

## Competitive Landscape

The retained assets have unusual characteristics. The Kentucky Derby is a single-location, non-replicable media and hospitality property with contracted NBC rights, sponsorships and personal seat licenses; multi-year contracted remaining performance obligations total $197M ($64M in 2027, $39M in 2028), though this is scope-limited to contracts longer than one year and excludes the majority of Derby revenue that turns inside twelve months. HRMs operate in a protected statutory niche with limited in-state competition and 49% segment margins, and Exacta monetizes third-party operators' machines.

The trajectory is more mixed than the level. iGaming is live in seven states with Maine authorizing in January 2026, and CHDN owns Oxford Casino there. Sports betting operates in 39 states plus DC. Prediction markets now appear by name in the FY2025 10-K's competition risk factor, described as CFTC-regulated with multiple states asserting that event contracts fall under state gambling law and litigation ongoing. For a pure casino operator that is wallet-share erosion. For CHDN there is a second channel that the company does not disclose and that I infer from the structure of the business: if horse-race event contracts are permitted at scale, they divert handle out of the pari-mutuel pools that generate TwinSpires takeout, fund purses, and underpin the legal theory that HRMs constitute wagering on historical races. Prediction-market horse-racing volumes are currently immaterial, and an incumbent holding the Derby's content rights may be able to license into those venues, but the mechanism is real and it is the most plausible explanation for why the market has repriced this specific franchise more harshly than its operating results warrant.

The strategic logic of the divestiture is coherent on its face: exit a competitively contested, capital-hungry regional casino business to concentrate capital where pricing power exists. The counter-argument deserves equal weight. The casinos hold conventional, unambiguous gaming licenses. HRMs depend on a statutory construct. Selling the former to concentrate in the latter increases regulatory-construct risk and reduces product and jurisdictional diversification, shifting exposure toward Kentucky, Virginia and New Hampshire. Louisiana in 2025 demonstrates the construct can fail.

**Competitive position: 6.6.** A genuine, narrow moat around the Derby and the HRM statutes, offset by share loss in Central Virginia, a sport contracting 4.7% annually, three encroaching competitive formats, and a restructuring that concentrates regulatory exposure.

## Risk Assessment

**HRM legal construct (low probability, high magnitude).** The Louisiana Supreme Court affirmed the 2021 HHR Act unconstitutional on March 21, 2025 and denied rehearing May 8. CHDN ceased Louisiana HRM operations, relocated ~500 machines primarily to Virginia, and absorbed −$18.9M revenue / −$8.1M EBITDA in FY2025, still lapping at −$4M / −$2M in Q2 2026. The Louisiana defect was procedural — the state constitution requires parish-level referenda for gambling expansion, which the HHR Act bypassed — and Kentucky and Virginia authorized HRMs by explicit statute, so read-across is weak. But it is the first instance of a state's highest court voiding an HRM authorization against a business line generating $522M in H1 2026 alone, and the machine relocation demonstrates the asset base is redeployable rather than stranded.

**Refinancing and the springing maturity.** $600M of 5.50% notes due April 1, 2027, with a practical refinancing deadline around December 31, 2026 to avoid accelerating the Revolver and Term Loan A. Execution risk is low; the risk is that the deadline continues to preempt shareholder returns while $465M of committed projects ramp in 2027.

**Held-for-sale impairment.** The April 1, 2026 annual test found no impairments, but the Q2 10-Q explicitly warns that monitoring of Presque Isle's regulatory and competitive environment "could increase the risk of a future impairment." A held-for-sale reclassification shifts measurement to fair value less costs to sell. If marketed bids arrive below carrying value, impairment charges against $900M of goodwill and $2,513M of other intangibles are the mechanism by which that becomes visible: non-cash, but a direct hit to already-thin book equity and to any screen-driven ownership.

**Process failure.** No transaction has been announced, signed or closed. A terminated process leaves a stranded-strategy discount plus process costs, and the Q2 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors, meaning the strategic-review risks appear so far only in forward-looking-statement lists rather than as formal Item 1A disclosure.

**Perimeter ambiguity.** "Nine properties across seven states" does not reconcile with the 10-K's ten wholly-owned casinos across nine states under any single exclusion. Whether Fair Grounds (LA, holding a racing license and the Louisiana Derby) and Calder (FL) sit inside the perimeter is unresolved and materially affects the retained-EBITDA base.

**Execution.** Victory Run ($280–300M, April 2028) and Rockingham ($180–200M, mid-2027) are both under construction with spending concentrated in 2027, the same year the 2028 Notes' springing test arrives. The temporary Salem venue is already closed (−$2M Q2 EBITDA).

**Risk factors: 4.7.** Multiple live binary items — a strategic process with no disclosed economics, an impairment trigger tied to that process, a four-month refinancing deadline, and a statutory tail on the largest revenue line — against a valuation that has already de-rated but not to distressed levels.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 51.2% against 33.7% one-year realized is rich (17.6 points), and the term structure is backwardated (51.2% at three days, 31.6% at one month, 33.8% at six months). ATM IV also rose 4.1 points since the July 23 snapshot while the stock gained 7.5%. Volatility rising with price is consistent with the market pricing an event rather than a drift, plausibly a transaction announcement or refinancing news, though the three-day tenor makes the 51.2% reading partly a mechanical artifact of near-expiry pinning.

Positioning does not corroborate a bearish stance. Put/call volume is 0.03 and put/call open interest 0.05, almost no put open interest at all, with near-term skew described as balanced at 1.8%. The one-month tenor carries a 9.1% skew reading, but with the sign convention undefined I will not assign it direction. The collapse in the put/call OI ratio from 6.61 to 0.05 across the two snapshots is most likely a contract-roll artifact and should not be read as a sentiment reversal. Short interest is unremarkable at 5.6% of float and 3.7 days to cover. Event risk is priced, direction is not, and the option market is not confirming the downside case any more than the upside one. Elevated near-term volatility argues against paying up for a position ahead of the December refinancing window.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $128 (+41%)

The strategic review resolves into announced sales of the nine regional properties at 7–8x EBITDA, delivering roughly $1.8B net of taxes. Net debt falls toward $2.8B and consolidated leverage to approximately 2.9x on a retained ~$950M–1.0B EBITDA base. The market then values what remains — the Kentucky Derby with contracted media and sponsorship revenue, 10,190 HRMs at 49% segment margins with statutory protection, TwinSpires and Exacta — at 11–12x, versus 8.8x blended today. That yields $114–128 per share before any proceeds redeployment. The $430M unused repurchase authorization reactivates once the 2027 notes are addressed, restoring the share-count reduction that took diluted shares from 74.6M to 70.0M in eighteen months. Victory Run and Rockingham complete into the 2028 Derby and a full-year Rockingham contribution. Every visible analyst target sits above the current price, and Mizuho's $157 approximates a 13x retained multiple with full proceeds redeployment.

### Bear Case — $72 (−21%)

The process yields no transaction, or bids arrive below carrying value and trigger impairments against $3.4B of goodwill and intangibles. The company carries a stranded-strategy discount plus process costs into 2027, with the buyback still suspended, the $600M refinancing done at 6.5%+, and $465M of project capex peaking. Central Virginia's competitive erosion (−$9M H1 EBITDA) broadens rather than being offset by Northern Virginia. Prediction markets scale into horse-race event contracts, diverting handle from the pari-mutuel pools that fund purses and underpin the HRM legal theory. iGaming legalization in Kentucky, Virginia or New Hampshire directly attacks HRM venue economics. Consumer discretionary spending softens. At 7.6x EV/EBITDA on flat $1,243M EBITDA against $4.65B of net debt, equity is $72. A second state supreme court taking Louisiana's view of an HRM statute would put the bear case well below that, though I assign that low probability.

### Weighing

Assigning 45% to the base case, 25% to the bull and 30% to the bear produces a probability-weighted expected return near +10%, plus a 0.5% dividend. That is positive but thin relative to a 21% downside leg on an equity with 4.4x consolidated leverage and a binary catalyst whose economics management has not quantified. Two of the dimensions I weight most heavily — financial health at 5.3 and management quality at 5.2 — sit below 6, and the sector has been a reliable source of negative relative returns. Cheapness with a catalyst is the right frame, but the catalyst is not yet a fact.

**On management.** CEO Carstanjen's FY2025 total compensation rose 27% to $19,172,839, 4.5x the $4,256,383 NEO average, in a year when net income fell 10.3% and the proxy's own TSR index fell from 138.75 to 118.66 (−14.5%) against a peer index that fell 3.5%. The payout is mechanically consistent with the plan, whose company-selected measure is Adjusted EBITDA, which set a record. The design consequence is that record pay is achievable in a year shareholders lose 14.5%, because Adjusted EBITDA is by construction insensitive to impairments, to $297.7M of interest expense, and to multiple compression. I cannot exclude that the increase reflects grant-date valuation timing or a new multi-year cycle. Alignment is genuine — Carstanjen holds 1,685,609 shares, roughly 2.4% of the company, and COO Mudd 732,067. Insider activity is strictly neutral: only grants, vestings and tax-withholding dispositions, with no open-market purchases or sales. The absence of buying on a 32% drawdown is a mild negative by absence, and a live strategic process would independently preclude purchases. The FY2025 buyback of $425.3M, executed partly with revolver borrowings at prices well above today's, and the $85.1M Chasers gaming-rights write-off eighteen months after acquiring that platform, are the two capital-allocation marks that hold the score at 5.2. The current deleveraging is the right decision; it arrived after the expensive one.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case: $103 (+13.4% from $90.82).** 14x next-year consensus EPS of $7.38, a multiple that assumes the strategic review resolves constructively but without a full re-rating of the retained business, and that leverage continues drifting toward 3.4x. Cross-checked against 9.2x EV/EBITDA on approximately $1,255M of forward Adjusted EBITDA less $4.5B of net debt, which gives $101.

**Bull case: $128 (+41%).** 11.5x on ~$950M–1.0B retained EBITDA post-divestiture, less ~$2.8B pro forma net debt.

**Bear case: $72 (−20.7%).** 7.6x EV/EBITDA on flat $1,243M EBITDA against $4.65B of net debt: no transaction, refinancing done at higher cost, buyback still suspended.

**Timeframe: 6–12 months**, gated on three dated events: the ~December 31, 2026 practical deadline for refinancing the 2027 notes to avoid the springing maturity; the Q3 2026 print (late October) which should show whether properties have moved to held-for-sale and whether impairments accompany that; and the FY2026 10-K (late February 2027) which should formalize the strategic-review risks in Item 1A and disclose any transaction perimeter.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY on any of:**
1. A signed transaction with disclosed proceeds implying net cash of $1.5B+ against the marketed portfolio, or an OpCo/PropCo structure with quantified rent, combined with stated use of proceeds.
2. The 2027 notes refinanced or extended past July 3, 2029 **and** the $430M repurchase authorization reactivated, which together would restore the shareholder-return leg and remove the springing-maturity overhang.
3. Central Virginia HRM EBITDA returning to growth for two consecutive quarters alongside continued Northern Virginia expansion, confirming the retained growth engine is intact.
4. A price near $79–80 — the July 30 intraday low of $79.40 — on unchanged fundamentals, which would take the base case to roughly +29% and widen the margin of safety enough to justify the leverage and process risk.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID on any of:**
1. Termination of the strategic review without a transaction, or held-for-sale marks producing impairments above $300M against the $3.4B intangible base.
2. Adverse HRM judicial or legislative action in Kentucky, Virginia or New Hampshire, or iGaming authorization in any of the three.
3. Retained-segment Adjusted EBITDA declining year over year in any quarter, or net bank leverage rising back above 4.0x.
4. Consensus next-year EPS falling below $7.00 (from $7.38), which would signal the estimate momentum has reversed.

**Next reassessment: the Q3 2026 print in late October**, or immediately on any 8-K disclosing a definitive agreement or a held-for-sale reclassification.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** The overall score falls to 5.7 from 6.0, a fifth consecutive HOLD with a deliberately raised bar. The reduction sits in risk factors (4.7 from a prior implied higher level) and management quality (5.2), not in the operating business: revenue, EBITDA and EPS all reached records in Q2 2026, margins expanded, Gaming stabilized, net debt fell $360M in six months, and estimates are drifting higher. The stock is up 8.8% since the prior review, so this is not a case of confusing price weakness with fundamental deterioration; it is a case of a modestly higher price against a catalyst whose economics remain undisclosed and a maturity deadline four months away.

At 8.8x EV/TTM Adjusted EBITDA, 12.3x next-year earnings and a 7.6% normalized free-cash-flow yield, CHDN is inexpensive against its own history and against any defensible read of the retained franchise. The mechanism by which that becomes shareholder value is a re-rating of racing and HRM assets from a blended casino multiple toward a specialty-franchise multiple, unlocked by selling the casinos. I can construct that path to $114–128. I cannot yet verify the perimeter, the proceeds, the tax leakage, the timeline, or whether the process concludes at all, and the same transaction would concentrate the company into a business whose legality is statutory rather than physical, in a year when one state's supreme court voided exactly such a statute.
