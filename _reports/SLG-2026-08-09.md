**Research Date: August 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SL Green has two things happening at once, and the reported numbers blend them in a way that flatters the weaker one. The operating recovery is real and cash-verifiable: Manhattan same-store cash NOI including SLG's share of joint ventures rose 4.3% in Q2 2026 and 3.4% in H1, same-store office occupancy including signed-not-commenced leases reached 94.7% at June 30 against 90.4% a year earlier, and leases *signed* in H1 carried starting rents of $109.59/rsf, 16.6% above prior fully escalated rents. That last figure is the leading indicator: the FY2025 10-K showed 2.0 million rsf of *commenced* leases at $80.73/rsf against $94.04 of expiring escalated rent, a −14.2% roll-down. The signed cohort indicates what the commenced cohort looks like in 2027–2028.

The capital structure has not recovered on the same timetable. Total debt at SLG's share is $10,536.7M — $4,549.1M consolidated plus $5,987.6M of unconsolidated JV debt that never appears in a standard screen. Netting cash gives roughly $10,219M against a $4,306M common equity market value including OP units, or about 2.37x, and net debt is roughly 68% of a ~$15.0B enterprise value. On an annualized H1 NOI-plus-fees EBITDA proxy of approximately $887M (stripping the non-cash item), net debt/EBITDA is around 11.5x. The company's own 6.50% Series I preferred traded at $21.39 on August 7, a 14.4% discount to the $25 liquidation preference and a 7.60% current yield. Credit is pricing this balance sheet more cautiously than the equity narrative implies.

The reason we are not upgrading despite the leasing data: management raised 2026 FFO guidance from $4.40–$4.70 to $5.60–$5.90, and attributed $0.80 of the $1.20 raise to "additional income that will be recognized from One Vanderbilt Avenue." The 10-Q filed August 6 identifies the mechanism: $26.6M of Q2 income from reduction of the negative carrying value of an unconsolidated JV, recognized over the term of related tenant obligations. That is roughly $0.35 per diluted share and unit. Q2 reported FFO of $1.43 beat the $1.19 consensus by $0.24; excluding the item, the cash-based result was approximately $1.08, modestly *below* consensus. Adjusting the full year gives a cash FFO midpoint near $4.95, putting the stock at 11.4x rather than the 9.8x the headline implies. At $56.36 (above the $55.39 analyst mean and $55.00 median, 62% above the 52-week low of $34.77 reached in late March 2026), the recovery is substantially priced while the 2027 refinancing question is unresolved.

## Company Overview

SL Green is a vertically integrated REIT concentrated almost entirely in Manhattan commercial real estate, describing itself as Manhattan's largest office landlord. At June 30, 2026 it held interests in 54 buildings totaling 30.6 million square feet, comprising 29.2 million square feet of ownership interests plus 1.4 million square feet securing debt and preferred equity investments, and managed four additional buildings for third parties.

The structure matters more than the square footage. Of the 27-building, 23.97 million square foot Manhattan office portfolio, only 10.1 million square feet sits in 17 consolidated buildings; 13.87 million square feet sits in ten unconsolidated joint ventures accounted for under the equity method. The trophy assets are in the JV bucket: One Vanderbilt (55.01%), 11 Madison Avenue (60%), 245 Park Avenue (50.10%), 1515 Broadway (56.87%), 280 Park Avenue (50%), 100 Park Avenue (50.80%), One Madison Avenue (25.50%). Consolidated FY2025 revenue of $1,003.0M therefore captures well under half the economic portfolio, and consolidated debt understates true obligations by roughly $6 billion.

Four business lines are identifiable: Manhattan office ownership and operation (the core, with six assets producing 45.1% of SLG's share of office annualized cash rent at Q2 2026); SUMMIT One Vanderbilt, the observation attraction (FY2025 revenue $122.3M, 2.2 million visitors, Paris expansion planned for 2027); debt and preferred equity plus third-party fund management (the balance-sheet DPE book has shrunk from $303.7M at 12/31/24 to $113.1M at 6/30/26, while the SLG Opportunistic Debt Fund closed $1.3B of commitments of which SLG's own is only $118.6M); and fee businesses earning asset management, development, leasing and special servicing fees largely from its own JVs and transaction program (fee income $39.4M in H1 2026 versus $24.5M, +60.8%).

## Financial Analysis

### Unwinding reported FFO

Headline FFO deteriorated sharply: FY2025 Nareit FFO of $437.7M was 23.2% below FY2024's $569.8M, and H1 2026 diluted FFO per share of $2.26 was 25.4% below $3.03. Both comparisons are distorted, in opposite directions.

H1 2025 included $71.6M ($0.94/share) from repayment of the 522 Fifth Avenue mortgage investment, net of $14.5M ($0.19) of investment reserves and $4.3M ($0.06) of negative derivative marks. H1 2026 is net of a $4.8M ($0.06) write-off of deferred financing costs and includes $2.4M ($0.03) of positive derivative marks. On those adjustments H1 2025 normalizes to roughly $2.34 and H1 2026 to roughly $2.29, essentially flat rather than down 25%.

The second adjustment runs the other way and is larger prospectively. Q2 equity in net income from unconsolidated JVs of +$14.9M (versus −$22.8M) includes the $26.6M non-cash reversal described above. Excluding it, Q2 FFO of $1.43 becomes approximately $1.08 against a comparable prior-year figure near $1.02, roughly +6% year over year. Applying the full-year $0.80 attribution, the cash-based 2026 FFO midpoint is approximately $4.95 against the $5.75 guided midpoint.

Two caveats belong with that adjustment. The reversal reflects a genuine reduction in obligations at SLG's most valuable asset and will recur over several periods, so treating it as a single one-time item understates near-term reported earning power. But it produces no cash, and the total reversible balance, the obligation term, and whether recognition is straight-line are not established in the material reviewed. The two JVs with negative carrying values total $206.9M, and for one the maximum negative carrying value has been reached.

### The load-bearing metric

Same-store cash NOI including SLG's share of JVs, excluding lease termination income, rose from $152.4M to $159.0M in Q2 (+4.3%) and from $299.0M to $309.1M in H1 (+3.4%). A $26.6M item cannot fit inside a $6.6M quarterly increase; it appears instead in "NOI from other properties/affiliates," which rose $44.9M from $22.0M to $66.9M, a line that also captures the Park Avenue Tower acquisition and the 800 Third Avenue and 315 W 33rd Street consolidations. The same-store series is therefore clean of the accounting item and is the number to model from. Underlying cash property performance is improving 3–4% like-for-like alongside a 170bp year-over-year occupancy gain.

### Cash flow and dividend funding

Consolidated operating cash flow was $82.9M in FY2025 (down from $129.6M) and $70.4M in H1 2026 (versus $61.2M), against FY2025 declared common and preferred distributions on the order of $260M. The reconciliation is structural rather than suspicious: JV cash returns arrive as distributions, and the portion classified as distributions in excess of cumulative earnings lands in investing cash flow — $183.1M in H1 2026 versus $45.2M in H1 2025. Operating cash flow plus those distributions ($253.5M) covers the dividend comfortably.

What funded the $183.1M step-up is not fully resolvable from the filings. One Madison Avenue's mortgage was refinanced in H1 2026, with gross debt rising from $1,163.8M floating to $1,650.0M fixed at 5.81% to April 2031, lifting SLG's share from $296.8M to $420.75M, with a $9.5M JV loss on early extinguishment. Some portion of the distribution is therefore leverage-funded return of capital; One Vanderbilt's operating cash generation is the other plausible source, and the disclosures do not allocate between them. The practical implication is that distribution capacity is linked to continued access to JV-level debt and to the disposition program, not solely to property operations.

The dividend was reset to $0.6175 quarterly, $2.47 annualized, a 20.1% reduction from $3.09, with payment shifted from monthly to quarterly (dividends payable jumped from $2.5M at 12/31/25 to $49.0M at 6/30/26; H1 cash dividends paid fell from $120.4M to $52.7M, mostly a timing effect). At $56.36 the forward yield is 4.38%. Against guided FFO of $5.75 on ~77.1M units, total distributions of roughly $213M represent a 48% payout; against the $4.95 cash midpoint, roughly 56%. Neither is stressed, which supports reading the cut as deliberate capital retention ahead of the 2027 maturities and TI/LC funding rather than forced action.

The March 18, 2026 credit facility was the most consequential balance-sheet event of the period: a $1.25B revolver to June 2030 (extendable to 2031) at S+125, a new $750M Term C to June 2031 at S+145, Term A cut to $300M due May 2027, Term B $100M due November 2026, accordion to $4.5B, covenant compliant at both 6/30/26 and 12/31/25. That converted a near-term corporate cliff (a $640M-drawn revolver maturing May 2026) into a 2030–2031 profile. The costs are visible: the revolver is $850M drawn of $1.25B (68% utilization, $393M undrawn), liquidity fell to roughly $600M, and variable-rate debt more than doubled to $929.4M. The 10-Q's own sensitivity puts 100bp at $7.9M of consolidated interest plus $4.0M at SLG's JV share.

Three items should be neutralized before applying any screening ratio: $1,031.2M of securitization-vehicle loans matched exactly by $1,031.2M of non-recourse senior obligations (pure gross-up); $499.0M of noncontrolling interests in other partnerships, largely third-party debt-fund capital in a vehicle SLG consolidates but owns roughly 9% of; and $79.4M drawn on the Fund's subscription line, non-recourse to the Company but inside consolidated debt. Distress scores computed on this balance sheet are unreliable — they penalize a structurally negative retained deficit that is a mechanical consequence of REIT distribution requirements, and roughly $1.5B of non-recourse gross-up. The leverage concern is real at ~11.5x net debt/EBITDA; the distress score is the wrong instrument for measuring it.

## Growth Analysis

The forward drivers are identifiable and mostly cash-based.

**Free-rent burn-off and signed-not-commenced.** H1 signings totaled 1,374,425 square feet across 104 Manhattan office leases, with concessions improving to 8.8 months free rent and $91.89/rsf TI on 8.5-year terms, from $118.06/rsf and 10.4 months in FY2025. Management cited economic occupancy up 300bp in Q2. As 2026-signed leases at $109.59/rsf starting rents commence in 2027–2028, the commenced mark-to-market should turn from −14% toward positive, and leased occupancy converts to cash NOI.

The selection effect deserves stating: only 975,470 of 1,374,425 square feet signed in H1 (71%) qualified as replacement leases eligible for the +16.6% spread. Roughly 29%, plausibly including longer-vacant space at weaker economics, is excluded from the headline figure. The comparison base also flatters mechanically where recently re-let space carries low escalated rents.

**Market backdrop.** Manhattan average asking rent reached $78.03/sf in Q2 2026, the highest since July 2020; H1 leasing of 23M square feet was the most since 2002; availability fell from 18% in June 2023 to 13% in June 2026. Little new Manhattan office supply is under construction. SLG's H1 average starting rent of $101.25/sf against the $78.03 market average quantifies where it sits in the quality distribution. Tenant demand skews toward AI and technology alongside finance — Harvey AI (96,781 sf), Legora (98,420 sf, filling 11 Madison's final vacancy), Pinterest (82,812 sf), IBM (92,663 sf expansion), Sigma Computing — a concentrated new vertical that has been strong but is unproven through a downturn.

**Fee and fund platform.** Fee income of $39.4M in H1 (+60.8%) includes $4.2M from the 346 Madison partial sale and $2.1M from 7 Dey, so a meaningful share is transaction-contingent. The debt fund manages roughly $1.18B of third-party capital against SLG's $118.6M commitment, which is genuinely capital-light. This shifts revenue mix but does not diversify risk, since the fee streams derive from Manhattan office assets SLG co-owns and correlate with the core exposure. Direct tenant and property concentration is flat to slightly worse, not improving.

**Offsets.** Investment income fell 77.8% in H1 as the DPE book shrank. Consolidated interest expense rose 19.2% in Q2 and SLG's share of JV interest 11.5% in H1. The $2.5B disposition program removes rent as it removes debt: a 245 Park sale takes out 7.2% of office cash rent. And the 2027 refinancing cohort carries in-place coupons of 4.30%, 3.93%, 4.24% and 4.25% against materially higher current rates.

**On the estimate revisions.** Consensus current-year EPS improved from −$6.30 to −$1.75 (+72.2%) and next-year from −$1.82 to −$1.28 (+29.8%). These should not be treated as a classic improvement signal: coverage is one to two analysts, the basis is GAAP EPS where REIT depreciation suppresses reported earnings, and the current-year figure of −$1.75 is inconsistent with management's own GAAP net income guidance of $0.20–$0.50. The direction is consistent with improvement; the magnitude carries little information. The GAAP surprise record (−161.4%, −67.3%, +36.5%) is similarly noise for a REIT.

## Valuation Assessment

Office REIT P/E comparisons are unusable — the peer set prints 36.2x (HIW), 25.0x (CDP), 21.1x (KRC), −907x (CUZ) and −75x (DEI). The relevant comparisons are FFO multiple, yield, and leverage.

At $56.36:
- **Headline P/FFO**: $56.36 / $5.75 = **9.80x**
- **Cash-adjusted P/FFO** (excluding the $0.80 One Vanderbilt reversal): $56.36 / $4.95 = **11.39x**
- **Forward yield**: $2.47 / $56.36 = **4.38%**, versus a 5.6% peer median
- **Net debt / EBITDA proxy**: ~**11.5x** at SLG's share
- **Net debt / EV**: ~**68%**

SLG is the lowest-yielding and most levered name in its cohort after a 20% dividend cut. The 11.4x cash multiple is defensible for trophy Manhattan assets — low-cap-rate real estate carries higher debt-to-EBITDA at any given LTV, and One Vanderbilt was characterized on the Q2 call as a $4.7B asset — but it is not a distressed multiple, and it is not the 9.8x the guidance headline suggests.

The equity is effectively a levered claim on Manhattan trophy NAV: net debt at 2.37x the common equity market value means a 10% change in gross asset values moves equity roughly 24%. That is the source of both the bull and bear cases, and it is why the credit tranche's view matters. The Series I preferred at $21.39 yields 7.60% current. Some of that discount is duration and the general level of long rates rather than issuer credit, but it is an independent read that equity-side enthusiasm can obscure.

The price sits above the $55.39 analyst mean and $55.00 median, with a $38–$79 range and a sentiment trend classified as degrading (2 strong buy, 5 buy, 10 hold, 1 sell; buys have moved from 6 to 5 over three months). This is not a genuinely cheap setup where analyst targets could be exceeded: the stock trades at a double-digit cash FFO multiple, the estimate revisions come from one to two analysts on a GAAP basis, and the price has already recovered 62% from the 52-week low. The valuation is fair, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

The defensible advantages are legible in the filings. Scale and a single-market franchise generate deal flow and leasing velocity — the 23M square feet of H1 Manhattan leasing was the most since 2002, and SLG participated at the top of the rent distribution. A small number of assets are close to irreplaceable: One Vanderbilt sits atop Grand Central, is 100% leased, and signed New York's first $320/sf lease in Q1 2026; 11 Madison reached full occupancy in July 2026. The co-investment model lets SLG control large assets on thin equity while earning management, development and leasing fees. Several JV mortgages carry deeply below-market fixed coupons: One Vanderbilt at 2.95% to July 2031, 1515 Broadway 3.93%, Worldwide Plaza 3.98%, 245 Park 4.30%.

Trajectory is improving: occupancy from 90.4% to 94.7% year over year, signed mark-to-market from −14% commenced to +16.6% signed, and a proxy TSR index of 104 against a peer group at 65 over five years (though the index fell from 146 to 104 during 2025).

The counterweights are concentration and the distressed tail. Six properties produce 45.1% of SLG's share of office annualized cash rent; One Vanderbilt alone is 11.9%. Paramount Global was the largest tenant at 5.3% of portfolio rent in the FY2025 10-K, principally at 1515 Broadway. The Alternative Strategy Portfolio was four properties totaling 2,492,157 square feet at 58.7% occupancy at 6/30/26, down from five at 59.3%, after a lender foreclosed on a JV property in April 2026. Transaction liquidity has improved — $3.5B of Manhattan office traded in H1 2026, +31% year over year — which matters directly because the 2026 plan depends on selling $2.5B of assets.

## Risk Assessment

**2027 maturity concentration.** Approximately $3.17B of combined consolidated and SLG-share JV debt matures in 2027 ($1,383.7M consolidated / $1,781.8M JV share), headlined by 245 Park Avenue's $1,768M at 4.30% with a June 2027 current *and* final maturity and no extension options; SLG's share is $885.8M. Refinanced at +200bp, SLG's share of incremental annual interest is $885.8M × 2.0% = $17.7M, roughly $0.23 per diluted share and unit; at +150bp, $13.3M or $0.17. Management characterized 245 Park as "next up to be sold" and in advanced stages on the July 23 call, per trade-press reporting. No 8-K or contract disclosure confirms a transaction, and neither scope nor price is known.

**Near-term asset-level items.** 650 Fifth Avenue (retail leasehold, 50%) carries $65M fixed plus $210M floating, both maturing July 2026, past due at the August 6 filing date, with the 10-Q stating discussions with the lender on resolution; SLG's share is $137.5M. 115 Spring Street ($66.6M, September 2026, SLG share $33.9M) and 280 Park Avenue ($1,075M at 5.84%, September 2026 current / September 2028 final, SLG share $537.5M) follow.

**Off-balance-sheet leverage and non-cash earnings mechanics.** The $5,988M JV share exceeds consolidated debt and is invisible to standard screens. The equity-method line can swing on carrying-value mechanics rather than operations, as it did by $26.6M in Q2.

**Liquidity headroom.** $393M undrawn revolver at 68% utilization and ~$600M total liquidity, down from $781.9M. Restoration depends on completing the disposition program; the 10 East 53rd Street sale ($312.2M gross, ~$100M net earmarked for corporate debt repayment) closed in Q3.

**Residual DPE credit.** Of $113.1M remaining, $88.1M (78%) is internally rated "2 — watch list" against a $0.3M allowance, with one $9.27M loan on non-accrual since January 2025 and in default since August 2025. Worst-case loss is bounded near 2.5% of common equity market value, a source of negative surprises rather than a solvency issue.

**Tenant risk, unverified.** Paramount Skydance is pursuing an $81B acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. Media consolidation commonly produces real estate consolidation. No SLG filing addresses Paramount's lease expiration, renewal posture or downsizing intent, and none should be assumed.

**Ground-lease exposure.** $225.2M, 15.9% of SLG's share of portfolio annualized cash rent, comes from leasehold or operating sublease interests, with $844.8M of operating lease liabilities plus $108.8M of financing lease liabilities. Leaseholds are harder to finance; 650 Fifth is one.

**Rates.** The macro setting is unhelpful for the 2027 roll: every 100bp costs $11.9M across consolidated and JV-share interest, and the 2027 roll economics are a direct function of the long end. ### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 36.0% sits slightly below 38.2% one-year realized vol (spread −2.2%, "fair"), and IV has compressed from 42.2% to 36.0% since July 23 as the price rose 5.1%, consistent with normal post-earnings decay rather than complacency. Term structure is in contango (36.0% near, 43.7% at six months), a normal shape that does not signal near-term dislocation. Put/call open interest is 1.27 and rose from 1.12, while the put/call volume ratio is 0.23 — protection held in size against light current put buying.

The informative tenor is September (39 days): ATM IV 40.3%, a 16.0% put/call skew reading, a 1.11 put/call volume ratio, and a ±10.6% implied move — a concentration of hedging activity in a window that spans the 280 Park September maturity. Nothing here contradicts a HOLD; it is consistent with a market that has resolved the earnings event and is positioning for the autumn refinancing and rate calendar.

**Short interest is the more consequential market-structure fact**: 21.8% of float, 10.4M shares, 9.3 days to cover. That cuts both ways. It signals persistent structural doubt about the leverage, and it creates genuine squeeze mechanics on any 245 Park sale announcement at a strong valuation. For a HOLD it is a wash; it makes the position unattractive to short and adds convexity to the upside tail.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($73, +29.5%)

245 Park sells at or above book in a transaction market up 31% year over year, removing the largest 2027 exposure, generating proceeds toward the $2.5B target, and printing a visible NAV data point. The remaining 2026 dispositions complete, restoring revolver headroom. Occupancy reaches the 95.0% year-end target and continues higher; free-rent burn-off and the commencement of leases signed at $109.59/rsf lift same-store cash NOI growth from 3–4% toward 5–6% in 2027–2028. Cash FFO reaches $5.50 by 2027 and the multiple expands to 13x on de-risked maturities, giving roughly $71–75. With 21.8% short interest, the re-rating would be amplified. The embedded value in One Vanderbilt's 2.95% mortgage and 1515 Broadway's 3.93% coupon is preserved through the 2030s.

### Bear Case ($41, −27.3%)

The 245 Park sale does not execute, and the $1,768M refinances at +200bp, costing SLG $0.23 per unit annually, with 1515 Broadway ($382.3M share) adding $0.10 at the same spread. Long rates stay at or above current levels, compressing exit values and lifting the cost of the entire 2027 cohort. 650 Fifth's past-due loan resolves through foreclosure; the Alternative Strategy Portfolio deteriorates further from 58.7% occupancy. Paramount downsizes at 1515 Broadway alongside its merger integration. Revolver utilization stays high, liquidity thins, and the JV distributions that fund the dividend prove partly dependent on refinancing proceeds no longer available on the same terms. Cash FFO falls toward $4.40 and the multiple compresses to 9x, giving roughly $40.

### Base Case ($58, +2.9%)

Dispositions largely execute but with timing slippage; 245 Park resolves through either a partial sale or a refinancing at a moderate spread increase. Same-store cash NOI compounds at 3–4%, occupancy holds near 95%, and free-rent burn-off offsets most of the incremental interest cost and the rent lost to asset sales. 2027 cash FFO lands near $5.10–$5.30 against roughly $4.95 in 2026, and the multiple holds near 11x. Total return is the 4.4% dividend plus low single-digit price appreciation.

Probability-weighting base at 50%, bull at 25% and bear at 25% gives an expected value near $57.50, or roughly 2% price return plus the 4.4% yield, approximately fair for an entity at 11.5x net debt/EBITDA facing a $3.17B refinancing year.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (hold rationale: uncertain). What justifies it: the fundamental picture has improved in exactly the way the prior thesis required — the March 2026 credit facility resolved more than the specific 1515 Broadway refinancing flagged as an upgrade condition, four of eleven earmarked dispositions have closed, and same-store cash NOI growth is verifiably positive and clean of the accounting item. What prevents an upgrade: the price has moved to $56.36 from $53.61 at the last review and $34.77 at the 52-week low, now sits above the $55.39 consensus mean, and the August 6 10-Q established that two-thirds of the headline guidance raise is non-cash. On a cash basis, Q2's apparent $0.24 beat was a modest shortfall. The improvement is real and now in the price, and the decisive variable is a transaction that has not been contracted.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results in late October, or earlier on an 8-K disclosing a 245 Park transaction. The 280 Park September maturity is the near-term asset-level checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. An executed 245 Park transaction — 8-K or definitive disclosure — at a valuation implying a cap rate at or below 5.5% on SLG's $885.8M debt share, with proceeds applied to corporate debt. This simultaneously removes the largest 2027 exposure and supplies a NAV mark.
2. Q3 or Q4 disclosure showing revolver utilization back below 50% ($625M drawn or less) and total liquidity above $900M, evidencing that the $2.5B program is self-funding.
3. Same-store cash NOI growth accelerating above 5% year over year with commenced-lease mark-to-market turning positive, confirming the signed-to-commenced conversion.
4. A price below $46 on unchanged operating data, which would restore roughly 25% upside to base case and would move this to ACCUMULATE on arithmetic alone.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Failure to place 245 Park by Q1 2027 — either sale or refinancing commitment — leaving a $1,768M no-extension maturity inside twelve months.
2. Resolution of 650 Fifth Avenue through foreclosure or deed-in-lieu, or any second past-due maturity appearing in the JV debt table, which would signal that asset-level lender patience is exhausted rather than isolated.
3. Any covenant waiver or amendment request on the March 2026 facility, or revolver utilization above 85% ($1,062M drawn).
4. Guidance reduction for 2027 attributable to refinancing costs exceeding roughly $0.35 per unit, or a second dividend reduction.
5. Confirmed downsizing or non-renewal by Paramount at 1515 Broadway.

**Position guidance for existing holders.** Maintain exposure; the yield is covered at a 48–56% payout and the operating trend is favorable. Do not add at $56.36. Prospective buyers should wait for either the 245 Park confirmation or a price in the mid-$40s. Do not short: 21.8% short interest at 9.3 days to cover against a live positive catalyst is unfavorable mechanics regardless of the leverage view.

## Conclusion

SL Green has executed well on the two things within its control. The March 2026 credit facility converted a corporate maturity cliff into a 2030–2031 profile, and the leasing franchise is capturing the strongest Manhattan market since 2002 at the top of the rent distribution — $109.59/rsf starting rents on signed replacement leases against a $78.03 market average, occupancy at 94.7%, same-store cash NOI compounding 3–4% on cash.

Two things remain outside its control and unresolved. The first is $3.17B of 2027 maturities carrying in-place coupons of 3.93–4.30% into a market where long rates are elevated; 245 Park's $1,768M with no extension option is the pivot, and the reported sale process is management commentary rather than a contract. The second is the price. At $56.36 the stock trades at 11.4x cash-adjusted 2026 FFO rather than the 9.8x the headline guidance implies, above the analyst mean, 62% above the 52-week low, with net debt at 68% of enterprise value and the company's own preferred demanding a 7.60% current yield. Probability-weighted return of roughly 6% including the dividend, against a −27% bear tail, is fair rather than compelling.

The recommendation is to hold existing positions, collect the covered 4.4% yield, and let the 245 Park outcome decide the next move.
