**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SkyWest is the largest regional airline in the United States, flying branded feed for United, Delta, American, and Alaska under capacity purchase agreements (CPAs) that shield roughly 84% of its revenue from fuel and ticket-demand risk. Coming out of the 2022-23 pilot-shortage trough, the business staged one of the sharpest recoveries in the sector: revenue rose 15% to $4.06B in 2025, net income climbed to $428M ($10.35 diluted EPS), and Adjusted EBITDA hit a record $925M as captain availability normalized and the company began flying the full schedules its partners requested. On the trailing numbers the stock looks inexpensive at roughly 9x earnings and 5.7x EV/EBITDA, with over $900M of reported free cash flow and simultaneous deleveraging and buybacks.

The problem is that the recovery has turned. Q1 2026 masked a 53% collapse in the core flying segment's profit ($54.3M to $25.5M) behind a rising Leasing segment and a one-time 5.6% tax rate; consolidated EPS was flat only because of those two offsets. Q2 2026, reported July 23, confirmed the deterioration: EPS of $2.54 fell from $2.91 a year earlier and missed consensus of $2.72, the first clear YoY earnings decline of the recovery. Management attributes the cost pressure to higher pilot attrition and training expense that has been rising since March 2025, plus a growing fuel drag as the prorate mix expands. Consensus current-quarter estimates have been cut 19.7% over 90 days and the full-year figure trimmed 3.9%. The tax-rate tailwind that flattered 2025 and Q1 2026 is also reversing.

The bull case (contracted revenue, strong FCF, disciplined buybacks, a cheap multiple, and recently extended United/Delta contracts) and the bear case (a re-tightening labor cycle turning EPS negative, a Q2 miss, and 73% fleet concentration in two partners) are both well-supported by the filings. We rate SKYW a HOLD. The multiple is low, but it is low against trailing earnings that are now rolling over, and until the labor-cost cycle and segment margins stabilize the cheapness is more a warning than an opportunity.

## Company Overview

SkyWest operates through SkyWest Airlines, flying approximately 2,370 daily departures with 500 aircraft in scheduled service (639 in the total fleet). It does not sell tickets under its own brand; it provides regional feed for four major carriers as United Express, Delta Connection, American Eagle, and Alaska.

The revenue model is the crux of the investment case. Capacity purchase agreements account for roughly 83-84% of flying revenue. Under a CPA, the major partner controls scheduling, pricing, and ticketing, and bears fuel and passenger-demand risk; SkyWest is paid fixed fees per block hour, per departure, and per aircraft per month, plus incentives, with a portion recognized as aircraft lease revenue. This structure insulates SkyWest from the volatility that defines the mainline airline business. The remaining 16-17% is prorate plus charter (SWC), where SkyWest controls the route, keeps a share of the fare, and bears fuel and operating cost risk. Prorate is growing 28-33% YoY and is the higher-risk, higher-reward part of the mix.

The company reports two segments: SkyWest Airlines plus SWC (the flying operation) and SkyWest Leasing (the debt-financed E175 fleet plus third-party aircraft and engine leasing). Leasing is the more stable, higher-margin profit engine and did much of the work holding up Q1 2026 results. Fleet strategy is migrating toward larger dual-class jets, the Embraer E175 (271 aircraft, the workhorse) and CRJ900/700/550, while retiring 50-seat CRJ200s. Newer initiatives include the CRJ450 (a premium 41-seat reconfiguration launching by end-2026) and SWC commuter authority granted by the DOT in September 2025.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory is a post-shortage recovery followed by a fresh downturn in the core operation:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 (trough) | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.00B | $2.94B | $3.53B | $4.06B (+15.0%) |
| Operating income | $181M | $104M | $495M | $618M (15.2% margin) |
| Net income | $73M | $34M | $323M | $428M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $7.77 | $10.35 |
| Adj. EBITDA | $656M | $732M | $834M | $925M |
| Block hours | — | 1.14M | 1.29M | 1.48M (+14.7%) |

The 2023-25 improvement was driven by captain availability. A 2022-23 shortage of qualified captains, as pilots left for higher-paying majors, forced reduced schedules, operating inefficiency, and near-breakeven results. Attrition eased through 2024, and by end-2025 SkyWest was flying the full schedules its partners requested, lifting utilization and margins.

Cash flow and balance sheet metrics are strong within the model's context. 2025 operating cash flow was $940M and reported FCF $908M, though that figure flatters underlying capital intensity because gross aircraft and engine purchases are debt-financed and net capex was only about $32M. Total debt fell to roughly $2.4B (from $2.7B in 2024) at an average rate near 4.3-4.4%, against $2.75B of equity, a capital mix around 53% equity/47% debt, with debt being paid down. Cash and marketable securities were $706.9M at end-2025, drawn to $627.3M by Q1 2026 as buybacks continued.

Headline health ratios look weak but are misleading for this model: an Altman Z-Score of 1.48 ("distress zone") and a current ratio of 0.65 do not capture that 84% of revenue is contracted, that fuel and demand risk sit with the majors, and that aircraft debt is matched by CPA ownership-cost reimbursement. Piotroski at 4/5 and a manageable 2.69 debt/equity are more representative. Near-term solvency is not the risk here.

The critical, non-obvious finding is that the recovery is decelerating in the part of the business SkyWest actually controls. Q1 2026 consolidated revenue rose 6.8% and net income was roughly flat at $101.7M ($2.50 EPS), but the SkyWest Airlines plus SWC segment profit collapsed 53%. Salaries and wages jumped 11.9%, explicitly driven by higher pilot training costs from rising attrition since Q1 2025. Consolidated net income held only because the Leasing segment profit rose 24% and a one-time 5.6% tax rate (an equity-comp discrete benefit) replaced the prior year's 16.7%. Q2 2026 removed any ambiguity, with fuel and cost inflation cited alongside the earnings decline.

Two additional quality flags: the tax-rate tailwind (5.6% in Q1 2026, 24.3% in FY2025) depends on a rising stock price to generate equity-comp deductions and reverses if the stock stalls; and roughly $58M of 2025 revenue was recognized ahead of cash under CPA deferral accounting (net deferred balance around $241M), with contract amendments shifting revenue between flight-operations and aircraft-lease components in ways worth monitoring for timing effects.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story now cuts both ways. The forward fleet and contract pipeline is genuinely positive: 8 more E175s to United in 2026, 16 E175s to Delta in 2027-28 (replacing CRJ900/700s), and 18 used CRJ550s to United by 2027. In January 2026 the company extended terms on 40 E175s with United and 13 with Delta, pushing some termination dates out to 2035/2038 and de-risking the two largest relationships. The CRJ450 premium product and SWC commuter authority add optionality on new route types.

Against that, near-term earnings momentum is negative. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut from $3.17 to $2.55 over 90 days (-19.7%), next quarter trimmed to $3.14 (-7.6% over 90 days), current year to $10.85 (-3.9%), and next year to $11.99 (-1.4%). The 4-quarter surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss) turned to a 6.5% miss in the most recent quarter, and the estimate cuts are broad-based. The estimate-revision signal here is unambiguously to the downside, which weighs directly against any growth-momentum thesis. Structurally, the block-hour and utilization tailwind that powered 2024-25 has largely been spent, since SkyWest is already flying full schedules, so incremental growth depends on net fleet additions (capped per aircraft by 76-seat scope clauses) and prorate expansion, which carries fuel risk. Scope clauses at the majors permanently limit the seats-per-aircraft lever.

## Valuation Assessment

At $95.70, SKYW trades at roughly 9x trailing 2025 EPS of $10.35 and about 9.2x forward EPS, with EV/EBITDA near 5.7x. The passenger-airline peer set is heterogeneous and mostly unprofitable or expensive: ALGT and JBLU show negative P/Es, ALK trades at 75.6x, SNCY at 22.1x, and aircraft lessor AL at 7.0x. The peer median P/E is 22.1x. On the surface, SKYW's single-digit multiple looks like a deep discount to the group.

The cheapness is precisely what requires skepticism. A low multiple is not a buy signal on its own, and in this case the trailing E is being generated at a cyclical peak that is now turning: the core flying segment profit fell 53% in Q1, Q2 EPS declined YoY and missed, and estimates are being cut. The proper lens is normalized forward earnings, not trailing peak earnings. Consensus current-year EPS of $10.85 is falling, not rising, so trailing multiples do not understate a recovery here. The estimate-revision guardrail matters: the strong-buy signal from rising estimates plus accelerating revenue does not apply because estimates are declining. The analyst target range ($105 low, $150 high, $123 mean, $122.5 median) implies material upside, but the sentiment trend is degrading, one broker moved from buy to hold, and those targets look stale against a Q2 miss and a decelerating core segment. On a stock where near-term fundamentals are deteriorating, consensus targets are ceilings under revision, not floors.

There is a valuation floor: over $900M of reported FCF, contracted revenue coverage, and ongoing buybacks retiring shares (count down from ~50.5M in 2022 to ~40.3M in 2025) provide genuine per-share support. But that supports a HOLD, not an aggressive add. We assign a mid-range valuation score reflecting the tension between real absolute-yield/FCF support and the risk that the low multiple reflects rolling-over earnings rather than mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

SkyWest's competitive advantages are its scale, four-partner diversification, and ability to finance aircraft, which make it the partner of choice for growth E175 flying and the largest operator in the sector. It competes against Republic, GoJet, CommuteAir, and the major-owned regionals (Endeavor at Delta; Envoy, PSA, and Piedmont at American; Horizon at Alaska). The permanent ceiling is that several majors own their own regional feed and can direct flying in-house, capping SkyWest's structural share.

The competitive position is best described as stable-to-modestly-improving on the contract side (the January 2026 extensions reduce renewal risk and push termination dates out) but pressured on the operating side by the labor cycle. Regional airlines are structurally hostage to major-airline scope clauses and the pilot labor cycle: when majors hire aggressively, regional captains drain upward and regionals cannot fly full schedules. The early-2026 attrition data suggest that dynamic is re-tightening, the same force that crushed 2023 earnings. The trajectory therefore is not deteriorating in market share but is facing a headwind the company does not control.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration.** 353 of 487 aircraft (~73%) fly for United or Delta, with United alone at roughly 45% of the fleet. Loss or non-renewal of either agreement would be materially adverse with no immediate replacement revenue. The recent contract extensions mitigate but do not eliminate this.

**Pilot/captain attrition, re-emerging.** This is the single most important operating variable and it is moving the wrong way. Higher training costs directly compressed the core segment margin.

**New disclosures.** The 10-K adds explicit language that U.S. tariffs are significantly raising the cost of non-U.S. E175 components imported from Brazil, hitting the economics of 68 firm E175 orders. Separately, the company now carries no cybersecurity insurance despite acknowledged past incidents.

**Fuel exposure.** Fuel per gallon rose from $3.03 to $3.40 in Q1 2026 and is a growing drag as the prorate/SWC segment (where SkyWest bears fuel) expands.

**Leverage and commitments.** The $2.4B of debt sits alongside roughly $2.24B of firm aircraft/engine purchase commitments through 2032, to be 75-85% debt-financed; a higher-for-longer rate environment raises financing costs on this pipeline.

**Other.** Prorate agreements are terminable on 180 days' notice (Delta prorate on 30 days); EAS-subsidized routes depend on DOT funding and are exposed to government-shutdown risk; residual-value/impairment risk sits on $5.8B of PP&E; and GE is the sole engine manufacturer.

Many of these risks (concentration, scope clauses, the labor cycle) are structurally known and partly priced. The incremental, less-priced items are the re-emerging attrition, the tariff cost inflation, and the reversing tax-rate tailwind. On balance, the near-term downside catalysts (further margin compression, additional estimate cuts) are more identifiable than the upside, keeping the risk score modest.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 45.6% versus 36.7% one-year historical vol, rich, at the 74th percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (45.6% near-term vs 37.3% at six months), the signature of an imminent event or period of uncertainty. The 1-month put/call skew is elevated at 5.7% with a put/call volume ratio of 6.19, and ATM IV rose 8.5 points (37.1% to 45.6%) over the past month as the stock ground higher. This confirms the cautious fundamental read: the market is paying up for downside protection into a period of deteriorating earnings visibility. For a would-be buyer, rich near-term IV argues against chasing and in favor of waiting for post-event vol compression.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** SkyWest is a best-in-class regional with a fortress contracted-revenue model, 84% of revenue insulated from fuel and demand, over $900M of FCF, and a demonstrated ability to deleverage and buy back stock simultaneously. At ~9x earnings and 5.7x EV/EBITDA it is cheap on absolute and relative terms, the January 2026 United/Delta extensions de-risk the two key relationships into the mid-2030s, and the E175/CRJ550/CRJ450 pipeline supports multi-year fleet growth. If attrition eases as it did in 2024, margins re-expand and the multiple re-rates toward the low-teens the sector has historically supported. Bull target roughly $120-124, in line with the prior 52-week high and the upper analyst range.

**Bear case.** The recovery has turned. The core flying segment profit fell 53% in Q1, Q2 EPS declined YoY and missed, pilot attrition and training costs are rising, fuel is a growing drag on the expanding prorate mix, and the tax-rate tailwind that flattered 2025-Q1 2026 is reversing. Estimates are being cut across every window. The cheap multiple is on peak trailing earnings that are rolling over, making it a value trap rather than an entry point. Concentration in United/Delta and the structural scope-clause ceiling cap the upside. Bear target roughly $78-80, near the 52-week low, if margin compression accelerates and estimates keep falling.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD**. The evidence supports neither a confident buy nor an outright avoid; the cheap valuation and FCF are real, but the earnings deterioration is genuinely negative and unresolved.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or expanding SkyWest Airlines + SWC segment margin, confirming pilot attrition and training costs have peaked.
- A turn in estimate revisions to flat-or-positive on current-year EPS over a 90-day window (currently -3.9%).
- A pullback toward the low-$80s that would reset risk/reward against a stabilizing (not falling) earnings base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A third consecutive quarter of YoY EPS decline with continued core-segment margin erosion.
- A material adverse change to a United or Delta agreement, or any indication of non-renewal.
- Current-year EPS estimates cut below roughly $10.00, or a normalized tax rate plus fuel/labor inflation compressing forward EPS below $9.50.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q3 2026), which will show whether the Q1/Q2 core-segment deterioration is a two-quarter labor-cost air pocket or the start of a durable downcycle.

## Conclusion

SkyWest is a high-quality regional airline with a defensible, contracted-revenue model and shareholder-friendly capital allocation, trading at a genuinely low multiple. But the latest data confirm a real fundamental turn. The core flying segment's profit fell 53% in Q1, Q2 EPS declined YoY and missed consensus, estimates are being cut across the board, and the tax-rate tailwind is fading. The 9x multiple is cheap only if trailing peak earnings hold; the current evidence points the other way. This is a HOLD: worth owning for the FCF and contract coverage, but not worth adding until pilot attrition, training costs, and segment margins show they have stabilized. We would revisit constructively on a return to positive estimate-revision momentum or a pullback toward the low-$80s.
