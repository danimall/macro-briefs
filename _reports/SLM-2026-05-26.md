## Executive Summary

SLM Corporation (Sallie Mae) is the dominant private student loan originator in the United States, serving more families than any other private student loan lender, with $7.4 billion in annual originations. The stock trades at $21.87, down approximately 37% from its 52-week high of $34.97, despite posting FY2025 EPS of $3.46 (up 29% YoY) and raising 2026 guidance to $3.10-$3.20 after a strong Q1 beat. At 6.9x forward earnings with a 56% annualized ROE, the valuation implies the market is pricing in material credit deterioration or competitive displacement that has not yet fully materialized in the data, though delinquencies have risen.

The critical near-term catalyst is the July 1, 2026 implementation of H.R.1, which eliminates federal Graduate PLUS loans entirely and caps Parent PLUS loans at $20,000/year. This creates a structural funding gap for graduate students in law, medicine, business, and other high-cost programs — precisely the segments SLM already serves with dedicated products. Management guides 12-14% origination growth for 2026 and anticipates a gradual positive impact on originations, but the full-year impact won't be felt until the 2026-2027 academic year beginning in fall 2026, suggesting a multi-year growth runway.

The primary risks are elevated short interest (estimated at 15.2% of float), rising delinquencies (4.0% vs. 3.7% YoY), increased loan consolidation activity to third parties (10.6% vs. 8.7%), and the untested nature of the strategic partnership "originate-to-distribute" model. The bear case requires either a recession-driven credit deterioration sufficient to overwhelm the H.R.1 tailwind, or competitive entry from well-capitalized banks that erodes SLM's pricing power.

## Company Overview

SLM Corporation operates through Sallie Mae Bank, a Utah-chartered industrial bank regulated by the FDIC and CFPB. The company originates, services, and manages private education loans funded primarily through $21 billion in deposits (42% brokered, 58% retail/other) and $4.9 billion in ABS securitizations. The lending portfolio totals approximately $20.3 billion, with an average borrower FICO of 755 at approval and 92.8% cosigned loans.

The company has been pivoting toward an "originate-to-distribute" model, selling $4.95 billion of loans in 2025 (up from $3.69 billion in 2024), generating $369 million in gain-on-sale income while retaining servicing rights and fee income. This model reduces balance sheet growth, improves capital efficiency, and supports higher ROE, but introduces counterparty concentration risk with a single unnamed "leading global investment firm."

SLM maintains relationships with 2,100+ higher education institutions, the largest financial aid office network in the industry. This distribution advantage appears difficult to replicate and, in our assessment, represents a meaningful competitive moat.

The sequential decline in EPS guidance ($3.46 in FY25 to $3.10-$3.20 in FY26) reflects the ongoing business model transition and multiple factors. While the shift toward loan sales generates upfront gain-on-sale income, Q1 2026 results showed net interest income roughly flat year-over-year at $375 million while gains on sale actually declined to $146 million from $188 million in Q1 2025 despite higher loan sale volumes. The company also benefited from negative credit provisions tied to loan sales and loans transferred to held-for-sale. Q1 2026 EPS of $1.54 annualizes to $6.16, though Q1 contains seasonal strength and disproportionate loan sale activity.

**Credit Quality:**

Net charge-offs improved to 2.15% (from 2.19% in FY24 and 2.44% in FY23). The allowance stands at $1.51 billion (6.0% of total loan exposure), providing 4.14x coverage of annual net charge-offs. Delinquencies at 4.0% are elevated versus 3.7% prior year (and Q1 2026 delinquencies of 3.98% versus 3.58% in Q1 2025), partly attributable to changes in loss mitigation program availability (restricting modifications to later-stage delinquent borrowers) and the denominator effect of transferring newly originated loans off-balance-sheet.

The modified loan cohort from late 2023 will exit modification during 2026. Management acknowledges "longer-term performance is unknown." This is the key credit watchpoint for the next 2-3 quarters.

**Capital Position & Returns:**

As of March 31, 2026, the Bank's CET1 ratio stood at 12.4% and total capital ratio at 13.7%, well above regulatory minimums (7.0% and 10.5% respectively). The company maintains approximately $5.2 billion in cash and cash equivalents plus $1.9 billion in investments, along with a $2.5 billion secured borrowing facility (though the revolving period is set to expire June 12, 2026, requiring renewal). The company returned approximately $480 million in capital to common shareholders in FY25 through dividends ($0.52/share) and share repurchases (12.8 million shares for $373 million), and is on pace for similar or higher levels in FY26 ($259M in Q1 repurchases including a $200M ASR launched in March).

Share count has declined from 258M (2022) to approximately 189M today — a 27% reduction in three years. As of March 31, 2026, $242 million remained under the current buyback authorization (which includes the impact of the $200M ASR initiated in Q1).

## Growth Analysis

**H.R.1 Structural Catalyst (Effective July 1, 2026):**

The elimination of federal Graduate PLUS loans and capping of Parent PLUS loans represents the most significant structural change to higher education financing in decades. The addressable market expansion for private lenders is substantial:

- Graduate students in law, medicine, business, and other high-cost programs previously able to borrow up to full cost of attendance from the federal government will now face significant funding gaps
- Parent PLUS caps at $20,000/year ($65,000 lifetime per dependent undergraduate student) create additional gaps for families already stretched
- Graduate unsubsidized loans capped at $20,500/year ($100,000 lifetime) and professional programs at $50,000/year ($200,000 lifetime)
- SLM already has dedicated products (Law School Loan, MBA Loan, Medical School Loan, Dental School Loan) positioned for these segments

Management guides 12-14% origination growth for 2026. The full impact begins with fall 2026 enrollment (academic year 2026-2027). The 2027-2028 academic year will be the first full year under the new framework, suggesting a multi-year growth runway.

**Gain-on-Sale Revenue Growth:**

Gain-on-sale income grew from $160M (FY23) to $255M (FY24) to $369M (FY25), representing a 131% increase over two years. Q1 2026 generated $146M ($3.3B in sales). Management's decision to sell an additional $1 billion in loans above prior plans (the basis for the guidance raise) suggests robust demand from their strategic partner and a sustainable distribution channel.

**Headwinds:**

Loan consolidation to third parties increased from 8.7% to 10.6% of loans in P&I repayment, driven by lower interest rates making refinancing more attractive. This is a meaningful headwind to on-balance-sheet portfolio growth and NII. However, it partially validates the originate-to-distribute strategy: loans sold to partners are removed from SLM's balance sheet, reducing direct NII/prepayment exposure, though borrowers can still refinance loans serviced for third parties, potentially affecting servicing economics.

SLM's existing 2,100+ institutional relationships, established product suite, and underwriting capabilities create a first-mover advantage that would take competitors years to replicate. The guidance raise (to $3.10-$3.20 from $2.70-$2.80) demonstrates management's confidence. SLM itself anticipates the H.R.1 demand tailwind will exceed the competitive headwind, though the company also acknowledges increased competition as a risk factor.

## Competitive Landscape

SLM is the largest private student loan originator in the United States. Based on SLM's $7.4 billion in originations against estimated total industry private student loan originations of approximately $14 billion, SLM's market share is estimated at approximately 50%, though the total market denominator is not precisely defined by a single authoritative source. Competitors include Citizens Financial, PNC Bank, SoFi, and College Ave, but none match SLM's institutional distribution network or product breadth.

H.R.1 will attract new entrants. The 10-K explicitly warns of "increased competition" from banks, FinTechs, and potentially other large financial institutions seeking to capture graduate lending. However, several factors protect SLM's position:

1. Financial aid office relationships take years to build — these institutions want established, reliable partners
2. Underwriting expertise in student lending (FICO distributions, cosigner models, school-level default data) is proprietary and built over decades
3. Servicing infrastructure and regulatory compliance (FDIC, UDFI, CFPB oversight) create barriers
4. First-mover advantage: SLM already has products (Law School Loan, MBA Loan, etc.) and is originating to this population while competitors are still developing programs

The competitive position appears stable-to-improving on the origination front. Market share may compress modestly as new entrants capture some demand, but the absolute market is growing faster than any anticipated share loss, resulting in net volume growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Macro-driven credit deterioration:** A recession would increase default rates beyond the current 2.15% net charge-off rate. The allowance sensitivity analysis shows a 100% weighting to the 90th percentile adverse scenario would increase provisions by $196M (~$1.00/share pre-tax impact). Macroeconomic uncertainty, including potential consumer spending weakness in H2 2026, represents a material near-term risk.

2. **Modified loan performance cliff:** The cohort of loans modified in late 2023 exits modification in 2026. If re-default rates are elevated (e.g., 40%+ of modifications cure temporarily then re-default), this could add 50-100bps to charge-offs.

3. **Strategic partner concentration:** The originate-to-distribute model depends on an unnamed single partner. If this partner reduces appetite (due to their own funding constraints, credit concerns, or strategic shift), SLM would need to hold more loans on balance sheet, consuming capital and reducing EPS.

4. **Rising interest rates:** If the Fed hikes, SLM faces mixed effects: NIM may expand, but consolidation risk declines (less refinancing incentive). However, higher rates increase cost of funds and could pressure gain-on-sale margins.

5. **Short interest (estimated at 15.2% of float):** Elevated short positioning creates both risk and opportunity. Short sellers are presumably betting on credit deterioration or competitive displacement. If Q2 results demonstrate H.R.1 demand materialization, a short squeeze would amplify upside.

**Options Market Signal:**

Put/call OI ratio of approximately 0.12 indicates relatively low institutional hedging demand despite elevated short interest. The implied move of approximately ±8.1% (near-term) and ±19.3% (6-month) reflects recognition of binary outcomes around H.R.1 implementation and credit performance.

The combination of elevated short interest with limited put protection suggests short sellers are either very confident (no hedging needed) or the position is aging/stale. If the next earnings report demonstrates H.R.1-driven origination growth, short covering could provide meaningful incremental upside.

## Investment Thesis

## Conclusion

SLM Corporation offers asymmetric risk/reward at 6.9x forward earnings with a structural demand catalyst weeks from implementation. The stock has de-rated approximately 37% from its 52-week high despite accelerating earnings, raised guidance, and aggressive capital returns. The market is pricing in credit deterioration and competitive displacement. Delinquencies are elevated and rising, but NCOs are improving, and origination growth continues at 5%+ even before H.R.1 takes effect.

The elevated short interest creates both risk and opportunity. If the next 2-3 quarters demonstrate H.R.1 demand materialization without material credit deterioration, the combination of earnings beats, multiple re-rating, and short covering could drive 30-55% appreciation. The bear case requires a recession severe enough to overwhelm the demand tailwind — possible but not the base case.

At 6.9x earnings, 56% ROE, and a 2.3% dividend yield, the stock compensates holders while the structural catalyst takes effect.
