## Executive Summary

Artisan Partners is a high-quality active asset manager experiencing structural deterioration in its core business. Net client outflows accelerated from $3.7B in 2024 to $12.7B in 2025 and continued at $3.1B in Q1 2026, driven by performance weakness in key equity strategies and the industry's ongoing shift toward passive management. Despite management's efforts to diversify into credit and alternatives—including the firm's first-ever acquisition (Grandview Property Partners) and a filed SEC application for ETF share classes—88% of AUM remains in equity strategies where organic growth is decisively negative.

The stock trades at 9.5x trailing earnings with an 8%+ variable dividend yield, which superficially appears cheap against a peer median P/E of ~24x. However, the discount reflects real structural impairment: falling estimates (-7% to -9% over 90 days), a fresh earnings miss (-8.8% in Q1 2026), key person departures (Daniel Kane stepping back June 2026), and a 5-year TSR that trails its peer group by 75 percentage points. The dividend is variable and will decline proportionally with AUM in any market downturn. Consensus estimates embed continued decline, and the most recent quarter broke what had been a three-quarter beat streak.

With 11.3% short interest, falling analyst estimates, and no identifiable near-term catalyst to reverse outflows, APAM lacks the conditions for a constructive 12-month outlook. The credit/alternatives diversification is a multi-year project that may eventually transform the platform, but the interim path involves continued equity AUM attrition that the market has not yet fully discounted.

## Company Overview

Artisan Partners Asset Management ($2.7B market cap) is a publicly-traded, pure-play active investment management firm operating 26 strategies across 11 autonomous investment teams. The firm managed $179.9B in AUM as of December 31, 2025, and reported $183B as of April 30, 2026. The asset mix is heavily equity-concentrated: 88% equity, 10% credit, 2% alternatives.

The autonomous team model—where each investment team operates independently with transparent, formula-based compensation (generally 25% of management fee revenue)—is the firm's primary structural differentiator. It attracts high-caliber portfolio managers and has produced strong long-term track records: 12 of 12 long-tenured strategies outperform benchmarks since inception net of fees.

The firm distributes approximately 80% of quarterly cash generation as dividends, making it effectively an income vehicle for shareholders. Capital retention is minimal by design.

The tripling of net outflows from $3.7B to $12.7B in 2025 is the central negative thesis. Equity strategy outflows reached $15.6B, partially offset by $2.9B of credit inflows. The outflows are concentrated in strategies with weaker recent performance (Global Opportunities underperformed MSCI ACWI by ~12 percentage points in 2025).

**Positive Flow Channels:**
- Credit: 15 consecutive quarters of positive flows; AUM grown from $7.1B (2023) to $17.9B (2025)—152% in two years
- Alternatives: Small ($4B) but growing via Grandview acquisition
- International Value ($53B): Outflows described as "rebalancing" rather than terminations—benign

**Negative Flow Channels:**
- Global Opportunities, U.S. Mid-Cap Growth, Non-U.S. Small-Mid Growth all experiencing termination-driven outflows
- Industry-wide active-to-passive shift continues
- No ETF wrapper today (filing pending with SEC)

**Strategic Initiatives:**
- Grandview acquisition adds real estate PE (~$895M AUM, Fund IV launching 2026)
- "Strong possibility" of credit-focused acquisition by year-end 2026
- ETF share class filing—if approved, could materially improve fund flows
- These are 2-3 year payoff initiatives, not near-term catalysts

**Estimate Revision Signal:** Current quarter EPS estimates have fallen 9.1% over 90 days and 4.5% over 30 days. Full-year 2026 down 7.1% over 90 days. Combined with the Q1 miss, it suggests the market may not have fully discounted the deterioration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.84, APAM trades at:
- 9.5x trailing P/E (vs. peer median 23.9x)
- 9.9x forward P/E on consensus 2026 EPS of $3.83 (declining)
- ~8.1-10.2% dividend yield depending on run-rate assumptions
- 22% below its 52-week high of $48.50

The deep discount to peers (60% below peer median P/E) reflects genuine structural differences:
1. Artisan's revenue is 100% management fees with negative organic growth; peers like WisdomTree and Hamilton Lane have positive organic growth or alternative revenue streams
2. The variable dividend structure means the "yield" declines in proportion to market drawdowns
3. 5-year TSR of 121.7% vs. peer group 196.1% confirms persistent underperformance

**Is the discount overdone?** Federated Hermes (FHI) at 10.5x P/E is the closest comparable—also a traditional active manager with similar challenges. APAM's slightly lower multiple reflects its more acute outflow problem. The valuation is not obviously cheap because the earnings power is declining; $3.83 in consensus 2026 EPS may still prove optimistic if outflows continue at the current pace and markets pull back.

The forward P/E of 9.9x on falling estimates with negative organic growth and 88% equity AUM concentration in a potential stagflation environment does not present asymmetric upside. The dividend yield provides a floor but is itself at risk of compression.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural Position:** Artisan occupies a declining niche—pure-play, high-fee active equity management—in an industry experiencing permanent structural shift toward passive and alternatives. The autonomous team model is genuinely differentiated and attracts talent, but talent retention (Daniel Kane departure, non-competes only 1 year) creates ongoing risk.

**Competitive Trajectory: Deteriorating.** Key indicators:
- Net outflows accelerating (competitive losses)
- Fee compression continuing (pricing power declining)
- 5-year TSR 75 percentage points behind peers (market's judgment)
- No ETF wrapper while industry rapidly adopting

**Counterargument:** Credit platform growing rapidly (152% in 2 years), investment performance remains excellent long-term, and the ETF filing could be transformative. But these are emerging trends within a dominant equity book that is structurally leaking assets.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Outflow Acceleration** (High probability, High impact): If Global Opportunities performance doesn't recover or if a market correction triggers rebalancing away from value strategies, outflows could exceed $15B in 2026.

2. **Key Person Concentration** (Medium probability, Extreme impact): David Samra manages $53B (30% of AUM) in International Value; Dan O'Keefe manages $36B (20%) in Global Value. Loss of either would be existential—$50-90B of AUM at risk with only 1-year non-competes.

3. **Market Correction Impact** (Medium-High probability): With 88% equity AUM, a 20% market decline would reduce AUM by ~$32B, revenue by ~$22M quarterly, and the dividend by approximately $0.20/share per quarter. In the current stagflation environment with potential rate hikes, equity market risk is elevated.

4. **Daniel Kane Departure** (Confirmed): Stepping back from PM role June 30, 2026. U.S. Value team will need to demonstrate stability.

5. **Tax Rate Step-Up in 2027** (Confirmed): 1-3% effective rate increase from OBBBA limitations will reduce distributable cash.

6. **Diversification Execution Risk**: The Grandview acquisition and credit expansion require successful integration and fundraising. Artisan has never done M&A before.

**Options Market Signal:**

Put/call OI ratio at 0.80 is slightly call-biased, suggesting limited directional conviction. The elevated IV without price movement suggests upcoming catalyst pricing (likely next quarter's AUM/flow data).

**Short Interest:**

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Continued outflow reporting (monthly AUM releases), potential next earnings miss (Q2 2026 report expected late July), and broader equity market weakness in a stagflation environment that would compress AUM and fees simultaneously. The most immediate catalyst is the Q2 2026 earnings report where consensus of $0.89 has already been cut but may need further reduction given macro headwinds.

**Timing window:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July) and monthly AUM disclosures through summer. The 3-6 month window captures seasonal weakness plus potential market correction.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 11.3% SI with 7.7 days to cover is elevated but manageable. Institutional ownership provides lending supply. The 8%+ dividend yield creates carrying cost for shorts (must pay dividend).

**Borrow and options signal:** IV at 32.6% is moderately elevated. P/C ratio of 0.80 is slightly call-biased, which does not confirm the bearish thesis. Options market positioning is neutral. The dividend carrying cost (~2% per quarter) is the primary friction.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $28-32 represents 15-26% downside. Upside risk to $43 (short covering + market rally) represents 14% upside risk. Reward:risk of approximately 1.5-1.8:1 after accounting for dividend carrying cost.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The risk/reward is below 2:1 when accounting for the 8%+ dividend carrying cost, and options positioning does not confirm the bearish thesis. The high days-to-cover (7.7) creates exit risk. The thesis is "gradually deteriorating business" without a hard binary catalyst—this makes it an AVOID rather than an active short. A market correction could provide better short entry if the stock rallies first.

## Conclusion

Artisan Partners is a well-run firm with excellent investment talent and a differentiated operating model, operating in a structurally challenging industry segment. The accelerating outflows, declining estimates, recent earnings miss, and key person transitions create a fundamentally negative trajectory that the 9.5x P/E does not adequately compensate for—the multiple is low because the earnings power is declining. The 8%+ dividend yield provides income support but is itself at risk of compression in any market downturn.

Management's diversification strategy (credit growth, Grandview acquisition, ETF filing) is strategically sound and may eventually transform the platform, but these are 2-3 year payoff initiatives while the core business deteriorates quarterly. With no identifiable catalyst to reverse the flow trajectory within 12 months, 11.3% short interest reflecting institutional skepticism, and a macro environment (potential rate hikes, equity market vulnerability) that would amplify APAM's weaknesses, the stock does not offer a compelling risk-adjusted return from current levels.

Recommendation: **AVOID.** Revisit if organic flows stabilize, ETF approval is granted, or the price declines sufficiently (below $30) to provide genuine margin of safety against continued deterioration.
