**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SkyWest is the largest independent regional airline in the United States, operating roughly 2,370 daily departures as United Express, Delta Connection, American Eagle, and Alaska feed under capacity purchase agreements (CPAs). The stock trades at $81.85, near its 52-week low of $77.89 and down 34% from the August 2025 high of $123.94. On trailing EPS of $10.35 the multiple is 7.9x, well below the passenger-airlines peer median of ~22x and roughly in line with the asset-light lessor Air Lease (7.0x). This is a cheap, cash-generative business that grew revenue 15% to $4.06B and increased net income by roughly a third to $428M in 2025.

The complication is that the engine of that 2025 rebound has reversed. The 2024-25 earnings surge came almost entirely from easing captain attrition, which let SkyWest fly its existing fleet harder — block hours rose 14.7% in 2025 on a flat aircraft count. The Q1 2026 10-Q shows that dynamic turning: pilot attrition rose again, salaries and wages climbed 11.9%, block-hour growth decelerated to 3.1%, and the SkyWest Airlines operating segment's profit fell 53% year-over-year to $25.5M. Operating income declined to $123.7M from $139.4M. Reported EPS of $2.50 beat consensus, but only because the tax rate collapsed to 5.6% on a discrete equity-award benefit; pre-tax operating profitability deteriorated. Consensus EPS estimates for the current quarter have been cut 14.4% over 30 days, and current-year estimates are down 5.1% over 90 days.

The result is a stock that is cheap for a reason that is becoming visible in the numbers. The leasing segment — now the larger and more stable profit contributor at $82.3M of $108M pre-tax in Q1, up 24% year-over-year — provides a genuine quality anchor, and management is buying back stock aggressively into the weakness. But with the operating airline margin compressing, estimates falling, and the pilot labor cycle turning unfavorable, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. HOLD, with a recommendation to revisit if block-hour growth and segment margins stabilize over the next two prints.

## Company Overview

SkyWest operates through SkyWest Airlines and SkyWest Charter (SWC), flying a fleet of 639 aircraft (500 in scheduled service as of Q1 2026): 271 Embraer E175 dual-class jets plus Bombardier CRJ900/700/550/200s. The business splits into two segments:

- **SkyWest Airlines + SWC** operate the aircraft. About 84% of flying revenue comes from CPAs, under which the major airline partner pays fixed fees per block hour, departure, and aircraft, and bears fuel and ticket-price risk. The remaining ~16% is prorate/charter flying where SkyWest controls pricing but absorbs fuel cost.
- **SkyWest Leasing** owns and finances the E175 fleet and leases aircraft and engines to third parties. This segment carries higher and more stable margins and has become the dominant profit center.

The CPA model insulates SkyWest from fare and most fuel volatility, which dampens both downside and upside relative to mainline carriers. The dominant industry variable is the pilot labor cycle: when major airlines hire aggressively, regional captains get poached and SkyWest's capacity is constrained; when major hiring slows, attrition eases and utilization (and profit) rise. The 2024-25 boom reflected the favorable phase; the Q1 2026 reversal suggests the cycle is turning the other way.

Scope clauses in the majors' pilot contracts cap regional aircraft at ≤76 seats and structurally limit growth. SkyWest's competitive edge is its scale, multi-partner diversification (United, Delta, American, Alaska), and ability to finance and operate aircraft reliably, versus major-owned regionals (Endeavor, Envoy, PSA, Horizon) and independents (Republic, GoJet, CommuteAir).

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year recovery is real and substantial. Revenue rose $2.94B (2023) → $3.53B (2024) → $4.06B (2025, +15%). Operating income climbed $104M → $495M → $618M. Net income reached $428M / $10.35 EPS in 2025, up from $323M / $7.77 in 2024. Operating cash flow was $940M; reported FCF was $908M (22.4% margin), though that headline overstates true free cash because aircraft purchases are debt-financed and flow through investing/financing rather than capex (net capex was only $32M).

The balance sheet improved through the cycle. Total assets were $7.39B against $2.75B equity; long-term debt was cut from $2.7B (2024) to $2.4B (2025), shifting the capital mix to 53% equity / 47% debt from 47/53. Liquidity was $782.5M at year-end. Interest coverage is comfortable at roughly 6x ($618M operating income against ~$104M interest). The Altman Z-Score of 1.48 flags "distress," but that is a screening artifact for an asset-heavy, debt-financed airline with large continuously-refinanced current debt maturities and a 0.65 current ratio that is structurally normal here; the Piotroski 4/5 and 15.6% ROE tell a healthier story.

The Q1 2026 print is where the trend turns. Revenue grew 6.8% to $1.01B and net income was roughly flat at $101.7M / $2.50 EPS, but the underlying operations weakened materially:

- The cause was renewed pilot attrition, which pushed salaries/wages up 11.9% and raised training and hotel costs while limiting block-hour growth.
- Cash and securities fell to $627M from $707M.
- Fuel cost per gallon rose to $3.40 from $3.03, pressuring the prorate book.

The EPS beat (versus a $2.21 estimate) was carried by the discrete equity-vesting tax benefit, not by operating performance. The Leasing segment is now carrying consolidated profitability while the operating airline compresses. This quality-of-earnings divergence is the central thread of the analysis.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth record is strong — four consecutive earnings beats (Q2 2025 +19.7%, Q3 +10.4%, Q4 +1.4%, Q1 2026 +13.1%) and substantial EPS growth over two years. But forward indicators point to deceleration:

- Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut to $2.80 from $3.27 thirty days ago, against a $2.99 figure 90 days ago (-6.5%).
- Current-year EPS is now $10.91, down from $11.49 ninety days ago.
- Next-year EPS is $11.99, down 2.1% over 90 days.

The deceleration is concentrated in the near-term quarters where attrition and training costs bite, which is consistent with the pilot-cycle mechanism. Block-hour growth slowing from 14.7% (FY25) to 3.1% (Q1 26) directly compresses the operating leverage that drove the rebound.

There are genuine growth levers that partly offset this. The leasing book is growing 24% and has annuity-like economics. The CRJ450 program (41-seat, dual-class configuration with 7 first-class seats, converting ~50 CRJ200s by 2028) is a capital-light margin upgrade that turns unprofitable 50-seaters into dual-class revenue jets. Fleet modernization adds 8 E175s for United (2026) and 16 for Delta (2027-28). January 2026 contract extensions on 40 United E175s and 13 Delta E175s pushed termination dates out to 2035-2038, materially improving revenue visibility. The firm order book of 68 E175s (~$2.24B through 2032) embeds multi-year capacity growth, capped by scope clauses.

The net assessment is that the structural growth story is intact but the cyclical operating tailwind has reversed, and consensus is catching down to that reality.

## Valuation Assessment

At $81.85 on $10.35 trailing EPS, SKYW trades at 7.9x trailing and ~7.5x current-year consensus ($10.91). The passenger-airlines peer set is heterogeneous — ALGT (-48.6x), JBLU (-2.8x) and ALK (74.6x) are all distorted by depressed or negative earnings, SNCY sits at 22.1x, and the cleanest comparable, lessor Air Lease (AL), trades at 7.0x. SkyWest's hybrid operating-plus-leasing model warrants a multiple between the regional-carrier and lessor cohorts.

A low multiple alone is not a buy signal, and here the discount partly reflects the deteriorating operating segment and falling estimates rather than pure market mispricing. The case for genuine cheapness rests on two arguments. First, the leasing segment is being valued at the same depressed multiple as a low-margin regional carrier, when its economics are closer to a lessor that would command a higher multiple on its own. Second, even on the cut current-year estimate of $10.91, the stock is at 7.5x, with FY27 consensus of $11.99 implying ~6.8x. If the pilot cycle stabilizes — as it did in 2024 — earnings power and the multiple can both recover.

The offsetting consideration is that the analyst target distribution (mean $121.5, median $128, low $95, high $150) was set largely before the Q1 print and the subsequent estimate cuts. Treating those targets as a floor would be a mistake given the negative revision trend; they are stale and likely to come down, as the Citi target cut on May 23 already signals. The valuation is supportive but not asymmetric — cheap enough to limit downside, not cheap enough to override the earnings inflection. A fair-value range of $90-105 (8.5-9.5x on a stabilized ~$11 earnings base) is reasonable if margins hold, with downside toward the high-$60s/low-$70s if the operating segment continues to compress.

## Competitive Landscape

SkyWest remains the largest and most diversified independent regional operator, with the structural advantage of playing four major partners against one another and a demonstrated ability to finance and operate aircraft reliably. The January 2026 contract extensions and continued fleet placements confirm the partners continue to favor SkyWest for incremental flying.

The competitive risk runs in two directions. Major-owned regionals (Endeavor for Delta; Envoy, PSA, Piedmont for American; Horizon for Alaska) can in-source flying or favor captive carriers, and the same partner concentration that provides scale (353 of 487 aircraft fly for United or Delta; United alone ~45%) is a vulnerability. The pilot labor cycle is the dominant competitive dynamic, and its recent turn unfavorable is squeezing SkyWest's cost base. The competitive position is roughly stable — share is not eroding, and the leasing book is widening the moat at the margin — but the operating airline is in the weaker phase of its cycle.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Pilot/labor attrition — the swing factor.** This single variable drove both the 2024-25 rebound and the Q1 2026 reversal. Renewed attrition is the most important near-term risk. The 89.6% of employees in in-house associations (not national unions) preserves cost flexibility, but outside-union organizing is an ongoing threat.

2. **Customer concentration.** Loss or material modification of either the United or Delta CPA would be severely adverse.

3. **Capital and debt commitments.** Firm orders of 68 E175s are 75-85% debt-financed, plus $550M of debt maturing in 2026 against a falling cash balance and continued buybacks. Refinancing is routine in this model but rising-rate refinancing raises interest cost.

4. **Tariff exposure.** Newly enacted U.S. tariffs raise the cost of Brazilian-sourced E175 parts — an under-appreciated, fleet-specific headwind given the Embraer-heavy order book.

5. **Fuel and prorate exposure.** ~17% of flying revenue bears fuel risk.

6. **No cybersecurity insurance.** Coverage was dropped, relevant given prior disclosed incidents.

7. **Government shutdown / EAS and ATC staffing risk** for prorate routes.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 44.0% against 36.4% one-year historical volatility — rich by 7.6 points and at the 63rd percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (44.0% one-month vs 39.2% six-month), with the market pricing an implied ±7.2% move into the June 18 expiry. Put/call skew is balanced (1.3% near-term, modestly negative at 3-6 months), and the P/C OI ratio rose intraday from 0.56 to 0.98. The market is pricing imminent two-sided uncertainty rather than a directional collapse, consistent with a stock caught between a cheap valuation and a deteriorating operating trend. Short interest at 4.5% of float (3.2 days to cover) is low and uncrowded, so neither a squeeze setup nor heavy informed bearish positioning is present.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$120-128).** The pilot cycle stabilizes as major-airline hiring slows again, attrition eases, and block-hour growth re-accelerates back toward double digits. The leasing book continues compounding at ~20%+ and the market begins to value it at lessor-like multiples. The CRJ450 conversion and E175 placements lift segment margins, contract extensions de-risk the revenue base, and aggressive buybacks (share count down ~21% from 50.5M to 39.7M) drive EPS higher on a shrinking float. On a recovered ~$12-13 earnings base at 9.5-10x, the stock returns toward the consensus mean/median targets.

**Bear Case (~$65-72).** Pilot attrition continues to worsen, training and wage costs keep climbing, and the airline segment margin stays compressed or deteriorates further. Estimates continue falling, the buyback consumes a declining cash balance against $2.24B of aircraft commitments and the 2026 maturities, and tariff-inflated parts costs add to the squeeze. The stock de-rates toward 6-6.5x on a $10.50-11 earnings base.

The base case sits between these: margins stabilize but do not quickly recover, the leasing book offsets airline weakness, and the stock trades in a $90-105 range over 12 months as the valuation discount partly closes against modestly lower-than-expected near-term earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The valuation is genuinely cheap and the leasing franchise is a real quality asset, but the operating inflection and falling estimates remove the asymmetry needed for a higher-conviction call. The next one to two quarterly prints will resolve whether the Q1 weakness was a one-quarter step-down or the start of a downcycle.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (~late July 2026), with the June 18 options-implied event window as an earlier sentiment checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Block-hour growth re-accelerates above ~6-8% YoY and SkyWest Airlines segment profit stabilizes or recovers toward prior-year levels for two consecutive quarters.
- Current-quarter/current-year EPS estimate revisions turn flat-to-positive.
- Evidence that pilot attrition has peaked (declining training/wage cost intensity in the MD&A).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Airline segment profit declines another quarter while consensus continues cutting current-year EPS below ~$10.
- Loss, material reduction, or in-sourcing of United or Delta contracted flying.
- Cash balance falls toward $400M with the buyback continuing into rising leverage and the 2026 maturities.

## Conclusion

SkyWest is a well-run, cash-generative regional carrier with a growing high-margin leasing book, improving capital structure, and a stock trading at 7.5x earnings after a 34% drawdown. Those facts argue for owning it. But the Q1 2026 print exposed a turn in the pilot labor cycle that drove the airline operating segment's profit down 53%, with reported EPS propped up by a one-time tax benefit, and consensus estimates are now falling across all near-term periods. The cheapness is real, but it co-exists with a deteriorating operating trend and stale, likely-to-be-cut analyst targets. This balances out to a HOLD: the downside is cushioned by valuation and the leasing franchise, but the upside requires confirmation that the labor cycle has stabilized, which the data does not yet provide. The improving scores through May reflected the 2025 recovery; the Q1 operating inflection partly reverses that, and the appropriate posture is to wait for the next two prints before adding capital.
