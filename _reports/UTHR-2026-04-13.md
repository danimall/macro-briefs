## Executive Summary

United Therapeutics is a specialty biopharmaceutical company generating $3.18B in annual revenue (FY2025) from treprostinil-based therapies for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), with 42% net margins, $4.7B in cash and investments, zero debt, and $1.04B in free cash flow. The stock has doubled over the past year, driven by two events: (1) the September 2025 step-change when xenotransplantation/pipeline milestones drove the stock from ~$305 to ~$400+, and (2) the March 30, 2026 TETON-1 Phase 3 readout showing Nebulized Tyvaso met its primary endpoint in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) with a 130.1 mL FVC improvement, sending shares from ~$523 to ~$588.

At $571.73, UTHR trades at ~20x forward earnings — in line with Biogen (20.0x) and below Neurocrine (30.4x). The IPF opportunity ($5-10B addressable market per Jefferies) is the primary catalyst: two positive Phase 3 studies virtually guarantee FDA approval, with a supplemental NDA filing expected by end of summer 2026. Treprostinil concentration risk (91% of revenue from one molecule) and competitive threats from Merck's Winrevair and Liquidia's Yutrepia are material headwinds, but the balance sheet provides an extraordinary cushion and the earnings trajectory is supported by rising next-year estimates (+6.2% over 90 days). The stock is a BUY with a base case target of $660.

## Company Overview

United Therapeutics, a Delaware public benefit corporation headquartered in Silver Spring, MD, was founded by CEO Martine Rothblatt to develop treatments for PAH — a condition affecting her daughter. The company has built a dominant franchise around treprostinil, a prostacyclin analogue delivered across four formulations: Tyvaso DPI (inhaled dry powder, $1.29B, 41% of revenue), Nebulized Tyvaso ($586M, 18%), Remodulin (subcutaneous/IV, $527M, 17%), and Orenitram (oral, $497M, 16%). Unituxin ($227M, neuroblastoma) and Adcirca ($30M, license expiring December 2026) constitute the remainder.

Revenue is 95% U.S.-sourced ($3.04B of $3.18B). The company employs approximately 1,400 people, generating ~$2.25M per employee, among the highest in the biopharmaceutical industry. Beyond the commercial franchise, UTHR is the most advanced xenotransplantation company globally, with FDA IND clearances for UKidney and UThymoKidney and active human clinical trials.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance Sheet:** Exceptional. $4.70B in cash and investments against zero outstanding debt, producing a 0.11 debt-to-equity ratio and a 6.6x current ratio. Altman Z-Score of 8.2 places the company firmly in the "safe" zone. Stockholders' equity reached $7.10B, supported by $8.56B in accumulated retained earnings. An undrawn $2.5B revolving credit facility (April 2025) provides additional optionality.

**Profitability:** Gross margins of approximately 88% reflect the specialty pharma model. Operating margins have compressed modestly from 50.9% (FY2023) to 46.9% (FY2025), driven by increased R&D investment ($550M, up 14% YoY) and a $74.1M Sandoz litigation accrual in SG&A. Net margins remain robust at 41.9%, producing $1.33B in net income on $3.18B revenue.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $1.56B (FY2025, +18% YoY) comfortably supports $520.5M in CapEx (primarily DPF facility construction for xenotransplantation), generating $1.04B in free cash flow (32.7% FCF margin). FCF conversion relative to net income is approximately 78%, modestly below the 100%+ threshold that signals premium quality earnings, but acceptable given elevated CapEx for long-term facility investments.

**Capital Allocation:** $2.0B returned to shareholders via accelerated share repurchases ($1B in 2024, $1B in 2025), reducing diluted shares from 49.7M to 47.9M. The cash balance remained essentially flat at $4.7B despite these buybacks, demonstrating the strength of cash generation.

**Revenue Growth:** Three-year CAGR of ~17%. Growth decelerated from 24% (FY2024) to 11% (FY2025) as Tyvaso DPI's growth moderated from 41% to 25% and Nebulized Tyvaso flattened amid patient transitions to DPI. This deceleration is natural product lifecycle behavior, partially offset by 14% growth in Orenitram.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term (FY2026-2027):** Consensus estimates project $28.39 EPS for the current year and $32.68 for next year. Next-year estimates have risen +6.2% over 90 days, reflecting the TETON-1 readout's impact on the earnings trajectory. The implied 15% EPS growth from current to next year is underpinned by: (1) continued Tyvaso DPI adoption in PAH/PH-ILD, (2) potential IPF approval adding incremental prescriptions, and (3) share count reduction via buybacks.

**IPF Opportunity — The Primary Growth Driver:** TETON-1 showed a 130.1 mL FVC improvement vs. placebo at 52 weeks, exceeding the TETON-2 result of 95.6 mL. Two positive Phase 3 studies with the second outperforming the first is rare and builds strong confidence in reproducibility. The IPF patient population (~100,000 in the U.S.) is comparable in size to the current PAH franchise. Jefferies estimates the opportunity at $5-10B. UTHR plans to file a supplemental NDA by end of summer 2026 and will seek Priority Review (orphan drug designation already granted). Progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF), with an additional ~100,000-180,000+ patients, represents a further expansion opportunity (TETON PPF study still enrolling).

**Pipeline Catalysts:**
- **ADVANCE OUTCOMES (ralinepag Phase 3):** Data expected late Q1 or early Q2 2026 — imminent.
- **Xenotransplantation:** UKidney IND cleared (Jan 2025), first EXPAND study transplant in Q4 2025, UThymoKidney IND cleared (Aug 2025). Long-term optionality with limited current valuation attribution.
- **Miromatrix miroliverELAP:** Phase 1 completed January 2026 — first human clinical trial of a manufactured organ alternative.

**Growth Deceleration Risks:** The core PAH franchise is decelerating. Tyvaso DPI growth slowed from 41% to 25%, Nebulized Tyvaso is flat, and Remodulin is declining. Without the IPF indication, the organic growth trajectory would compress to mid-to-high single digits.

## Valuation Assessment

At $571.73, UTHR trades at approximately 20.1x current-year consensus EPS ($28.39) and 17.5x next-year EPS ($32.68). Biogen's peer median P/E of 20.0x makes UTHR's valuation essentially at parity on trailing metrics. However, UTHR's superior growth profile (15% EPS growth vs. Biogen's flatter trajectory), fortress balance sheet, and IPF optionality argue for a premium.

The analyst consensus median target of $660 implies 15.4% upside from the current price. The low target of $519 sits 9.2% below current levels, while the high target of $733 implies 28.2% upside. With 11 of 15 analysts at Buy or Strong Buy, the consensus is constructive.

Applying a 20x multiple to next-year consensus EPS of $32.68 yields a $654 fair value. A modest premium to 21x for IPF optionality yields $686. The stock's current price embeds some IPF value but likely not the full addressable market.

The stock has appreciated 105% over the trailing year. The March 30 TETON-1 jump brought shares from ~$523 to ~$588, but they have since pulled back to $572. The pullback suggests the market has partially digested the news, and the remaining upside depends on FDA filing progress, approval timing, and commercial launch execution.

## Competitive Landscape

**PAH — Treprostinil Dominance Under Pressure:**
- **Merck's Winrevair (sotatercept):** Approved March 2024, first new mechanism of action in PAH (activin signaling inhibitor). Management reports no material commercial impact to date, as most Winrevair patients use it alongside background prostacyclin therapy. However, Merck's studies of earlier-line use could compress prostacyclin initiation over time.
- **Liquidia's Yutrepia:** Approved May 2025, a direct treprostinil competitor to Tyvaso DPI. UTHR's patent litigation could strip the PH-ILD indication (patent through 2042). Trade secret litigation adds another legal vector.
- **Insmed's TPIP:** Once-daily inhaled treprostinil — Phase 3 in PAH expected H1 2026. A convenience advantage over Tyvaso DPI's four-times-daily dosing could be disruptive.

**IPF — Favorable Competitive Positioning:**
Only three branded therapies currently treat IPF: Ofev (Boehringer Ingelheim), Esbriet/generic pirfenidone (Roche), and Jascayd (Boehringer Ingelheim, approved October 2025). Tyvaso's prostacyclin mechanism is distinct, positioning it as combination therapy or standalone. Two successful Phase 3 studies give UTHR a significant evidence base advantage.

**Generic Erosion Timeline:** Watson can launch generic Nebulized Tyvaso as early as January 2026 (pending FDA approval). Orenitram generics face launch windows in mid-2027. Tyvaso DPI carries the strongest IP protection through 2042. The generic timeline on older formulations creates a 2-3 year headwind, partially offset by DPI conversion and IPF expansion.

**Structural Advantage:** UTHR's cross-formulation treprostinil expertise, combined with Tyvaso DPI's patent protection through 2042 and the emerging IPF franchise, creates a durable competitive position. The balance sheet provides capital for M&A, pipeline investment, and share buybacks simultaneously.

## Risk Assessment

**Treprostinil Concentration (CRITICAL):** 91% revenue from one molecule across four formulations. Any fundamental safety signal, unexpected competitive displacement, or pricing action targeting treprostinil broadly would have existential impact.

**MannKind Single-Source Dependency (HIGH):** Tyvaso DPI ($1.29B, largest and fastest-growing product) relies entirely on MannKind for manufacturing with no backup source. A manufacturing disruption would directly impair the company's most important revenue stream.

**Drug Pricing / Regulatory Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH):** The IRA's Medicare Part D redesign creates new manufacturer discounts. GLOBE/GUARD proposed regulations for most-favored-nation pricing via mandatory Medicare rebates could materially reduce U.S. revenue if enacted. Medicaid funding cuts (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, July 2025) add further pressure.

**Generic Erosion (MEDIUM):** Nebulized Tyvaso and Orenitram face generic entry over 2026-2027. Combined, these products represent ~$1.08B (34% of revenue). Transition to Tyvaso DPI mitigates this, but the overlap period creates uncertainty.

**Key Person Risk (MEDIUM):** CEO Rothblatt (age 71) is the founder and visionary. No key person life insurance is maintained. Succession planning is opaque.

**Earnings Consistency (MODERATE CONCERN):** The trailing four-quarter earnings record shows 3 misses and 1 beat. The Q2 2025 miss was substantial (-9.5%). While the most recent quarter beat (+5.1%), the inconsistent track record warrants modest caution on forward estimates.

**Xenotransplantation Execution Risk (MEDIUM):** Capital-intensive DPF facility construction (~$400M through 2028) with uncertain timelines. Management explicitly acknowledges these facilities would be difficult to repurpose if xenotransplantation demand disappoints.

## Conclusion

United Therapeutics combines the financial profile of a mature specialty pharma company with a growth catalyst in IPF that could meaningfully expand the revenue base. The TETON-1 readout removed the primary binary risk, and two successful Phase 3 studies make FDA approval highly probable. At ~20x forward earnings, the stock is priced for the existing PAH franchise but captures only partial value from IPF.

The risks are real: treprostinil concentration, generic erosion on older formulations over 2026-2027, MannKind single-source dependency, and an inconsistent recent earnings beat record (1 of 4 quarters). These prevent maximum conviction. But the balance sheet absorbs competitive and regulatory shocks better than nearly any biotech peer, and the IPF market expansion provides a defined, large catalyst with a clear regulatory timeline. The stock is a BUY with a $660 base case target and a 12-18 month horizon anchored to IPF filing and approval milestones.
