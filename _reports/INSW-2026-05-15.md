## Executive Summary

International Seaways is one of the world's largest publicly traded tanker operators with 67 vessels including newbuilds (7.9M dwt) spanning VLCCs, Suezmaxes, Aframaxes, LR1s, and MRs. The stock has appreciated 122% over the past year, driven almost entirely by the Strait of Hormuz disruption which has pushed tanker spot rates to record levels. Q1 2026 delivered adjusted EPS of $3.90 (beating consensus by approximately 57%), and analyst estimates have been revised significantly upward in recent weeks.

At $84.58 (as of early May 2026), INSW trades at 5.7x forward earnings on a current-year estimate of $14.79. The balance sheet carries net loan-to-value of less than 7% with $918M in total liquidity. The company declared its largest-ever dividend of $4.55/share in Q1 2026, though this includes a substantial supplemental/discretionary component tied to elevated market conditions.

The investment case hinges on the persistence of elevated tanker rates driven by Hormuz disruptions. If the conflict persists through H2 2026, INSW could generate $14-15/share in adjusted earnings power. Even in a resolution scenario, the stock's downside is estimated in the $55-60 range based on normalized earnings of $4-5/share at historical multiples — a 30-35% decline from current levels. The risk/reward profile remains favorable given the asymmetry: limited downside vs. potential for continued re-rating if rates remain elevated.

## Company Overview

International Seaways operates a diversified fleet of crude oil and petroleum product tankers primarily in the international spot market. The company participates in six commercial pools spanning all major tanker classes, providing exposure to the full spectrum of global crude and refined product seaborne trade.

**Fleet Composition (March 31, 2026):**
- Crude Tankers: 10 VLCCs, 13 Suezmaxes, 4 Aframaxes
- Product Carriers: 1 LR2, 8 LR1s, 28 MRs
- Newbuilds: 3 LR1 dual-fuel-ready vessels (one delivered in April 2026; remaining two expected by Q3 2026)

The company generates approximately 82% of TCE revenues from spot market exposure, making it among the most operationally leveraged tanker companies to rate movements. In January 2026, INSW acquired 100% of Tankers International, the world's largest VLCC pool manager, adding fee income and commercial management advantages.

### Balance Sheet

The company's financial position is among the strongest in global shipping:
- **Net LTV:** <7% (down from 16.5% at year-end 2025)
- **Total liquidity:** $918M ($377M cash + $541M undrawn revolvers)
- **Current ratio:** 3.71x
- **Debt-to-equity:** 0.32x
- **31 vessels unencumbered** (~46% of fleet as of Q1 2026)
- **Debt maturities:** Spread 2030-2037, no near-term refinancing pressure
- **Altman Z-Score:** 3.79 (safe zone)

The balance sheet has been systematically strengthened from 0.58x D/E in 2022 to 0.32x today, providing substantial capacity to weather any rate downturn without balance sheet stress.

### Cash Flow & Capital Returns

Operating cash flow was $380M in FY2025. The company has returned over $1B to shareholders since 2020 through dividends and modest buybacks. The Q1 2026 dividend of $4.55/share ($0.12 regular + $4.43 supplemental) reflects a raised payout ratio of 85% of adjusted net income plus a discretionary component. The supplemental dividend is variable and tied to market conditions — INSW explicitly stated the increased payout reflects "strong performance and market conditions" rather than a fixed commitment. At the current share price, the Q1 dividend annualizes to approximately 21.5%, though investors should not assume this level is sustainable through a rate cycle.

Fleet renewal is self-funding: INSW sold 7 vessels in Q1 2026 for $216M in proceeds, generating $88M in gains. The company is effectively cycling out older tonnage (average 17 years) at elevated asset values while ordering modern, dual-fuel-ready newbuilds.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Earnings Power

Analyst estimate revisions have been substantial, though precise revision magnitudes vary by data provider and timeframe. The market systematically underestimated how elevated rates translate through INSW's highly leveraged spot exposure, as evidenced by the ~57% Q1 earnings beat.

Q1 2026 spot rates illustrate the magnitude:
- VLCCs: $86,693/day (vs. $33,531 year-ago) — +158%
- Suezmaxes: $68,027/day (vs. $30,911) — +120%
- LR1s: $70,664/day (vs. $27,367) — +158%
- MRs: $37,224/day (vs. $21,408) — +74%

Based on Q1 2026 disclosed TCE revenue of $317.2M over approximately 90 days, the fleet generates roughly $3.5M per day in aggregate TCE revenue at current rates — translating to $4-5/share per quarter in adjusted earnings power.

### Medium-Term Growth Drivers

1. **Tankers International acquisition:** Full ownership of VLCC pool management + Suezmax pool launch creates fee income and commercial advantages.
2. **Fleet renewal:** LR1 newbuilds delivering in 2026 add modern, efficient capacity at low marginal cost.
3. **Ton-mile demand:** Rerouted trade flows (Russia/Ukraine + Iran sanctions) structurally increase voyage distances regardless of spot rate levels.
4. **Sanctioned fleet separation:** "Dark fleet" vessels removed from compliant trade reduce effective available supply.

### Growth Risks

The tanker orderbook has grown from 7% to 17% of existing fleet — the fastest growth since 2015. New deliveries in 2027-2028 will pressure rates if demand normalizes. Current-year earnings are almost entirely geopolitically driven, and consensus estimates for next year ($6.91 EPS) imply ~53% earnings decline, confirming the market expects substantial rate normalization.

## Valuation Assessment

At $84.58 (early May 2026), INSW trades at:
- **13.6x trailing P/E** (on $6.23 FY2025 EPS — a weak year)
- **5.7x current-year P/E** (on $14.79 FY26E)
- **12.2x next-year P/E** (on $6.91 FY27E)
- **~4.3x annualized Q1 adjusted earnings**

Relative to peers: median sector P/E is 24.9x and median yield is 5.7%. INSW at 13.6x trailing with elevated current yield is discounted on spot-earnings metrics. The closest peer, Dorian LPG (LPG), trades at 14.2x with 7.9% yield.

The critical valuation question is what "normalized" earnings should be used. Using FY2025 ($6.23 EPS) as a through-cycle baseline implies a 14x P/E — already below peers. Using the consensus FY2027 estimate ($6.91) implies 12.2x. Using Q1 2026 annualized adjusted (~$15.60) implies 5.4x.

The stock is cheap because investors assign low probability to rates persisting at current levels. The Strait of Hormuz disruption has created conditions where rate normalization mechanisms have been impaired: INSW's 10-Q notes that a "prolonged disruption would place considerable strain on global markets" and that tanker demand has been materially affected by the situation. Elevated rates may therefore persist longer than consensus expects.

**Downside estimate:** At $55-60 (based on normalized $6-7 EPS at ~9x multiple), the stock would face ~30-35% downside from current levels. This is meaningful but moderated by the balance sheet. However, a severe rate collapse combined with multiple compression could produce worse outcomes in a tail scenario.

**Upside case:** At $100-120, the stock would trade at 7-8x on current-year estimates of $14.79. This represents 18-42% upside and is achievable if rates persist through H2 2026.

## Competitive Landscape

INSW holds a top-tier position in global tanker shipping with several differentiating factors:

1. **Fleet diversification:** Exposure across all major vessel classes (VLCCs through MRs) provides portfolio diversification that pure-play operators lack. This enables INSW to capture rate strength wherever it appears in the market.

2. **Pool management ownership:** The Tankers International acquisition provides INSW with commercial intelligence advantages, pool management fee income, and expanded commercial management capabilities across VLCCs and Suezmaxes. While tanker markets remain highly fragmented and no single participant can influence rates, the pool platform provides scheduling optimization and commercial coordination benefits.

3. **Balance sheet superiority:** Net LTV <7% and ~46% unencumbered fleet positions INSW as a buyer of distressed assets in any downturn — a countercyclical growth option that over-leveraged competitors cannot replicate.

4. **Fleet modernity:** Weighted average age of 10.9 years, with dual-fuel-ready newbuilds positioning for IMO/EU emissions regulations that will disadvantage older competitors.

5. **Scale:** At 67 vessels and 7.9M dwt, INSW has operational scale for favorable charter terms, pool participation, and institutional relevance.

The competitive trajectory is improving: INSW is gaining commercial advantages through Tankers International ownership, adding modern tonnage, and cycling out older vessels at peak asset values.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risk: Geopolitical Resolution → Rate Collapse

This is the dominant risk factor. A ceasefire or diplomatic resolution would likely cause spot rates to decline 40-60% within weeks. A sustained disruption scenario supports elevated rates, while further escalation could push rates even higher. Diplomatic resolution remains a low-probability near-term outcome given publicly observable failures in negotiation channels, but its probability increases over longer time horizons.

*Note: The scenario probabilities used in this analysis are subjective analyst estimates based on publicly available geopolitical reporting; they are not derived from company filings, official government forecasts, or quantitative models.*

### Secondary Risks:

- **Orderbook growth (17% of fleet):** Medium-term supply headwind. Not immediate given shipyard constraints, but creates structural pressure for 2027-2028.
- **U.S.-China port fee regime:** 9 Chinese-built INSW vessels potentially affected. Currently suspended for one year (from November 2025) but creates regulatory uncertainty.
- **Section 883 tax exemption fragility:** Near-zero effective tax rate depends on maintaining exemption status.
- **Spot market concentration (82% of TCE revenues):** Provides maximum upside exposure but no floor protection in downturns.
- **Management selling:** Per SEC filings, CEO Zabrocky sold 2,000 shares on April 15, 2026 at approximately $74.57 under a Rule 10b5-1 plan. This is modest and routine in context of her total holdings of approximately 206,745 shares and the $4.55/share quarterly dividend.

A ceasefire/resolution scenario would deliver 30-35% drawdown, though this scenario's near-term probability appears low based on current conditions. The expected value calculation favors ownership at current prices for investors with appropriate risk tolerance for event-driven, spot-exposed shipping equities.
