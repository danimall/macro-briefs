**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Expedia Group is executing a credible transition from a mature consumer online travel agency into a higher-margin business anchored by B2B distribution and advertising. FY2025 revenue rose 8% to $14.73B, but the more important development is margin: operating margin expanded from 8.0% in 2023 to 12.7% in 2025, adjusted EBITDA grew 19% to $3.50B, and free cash flow reached $3.11B on a 21.1% FCF margin. The company generates this cash under a merchant model with structurally negative working capital, which distorts headline balance-sheet ratios (Altman Z of 0.74, D/E ~19x) that would flag distress in a normal industrial company but here simply reflect deferred-booking float and a buyback-depleted equity base. Investment-grade credit ratings (BBB/Baa2/BBB, all stable), $5.7B in cash and short-term investments, and an untapped $2.5B revolver confirm the underlying financial health.

The stock has moved from ~$190 in mid-2025 to $259.94, up 36% over the trailing year, after a Q4 2025 beat and despite a Q1 2026 print where earnings beat but the stock fell on soft full-year 2026 demand guidance. At ~27x trailing earnings ($9.81 EPS) and ~13x forward EPS on the $19.92 current-year consensus, the valuation sits below the peer median P/E of 30.8 but reflects a legitimate discount: consumer bookings are decelerating (B2C revenue +2%), US travel demand softened in H1 2025, and the OTA model faces a specific, escalating disintermediation threat from AI travel agents and Google Travel that the FY2025 10-K addresses at unusual length.

We rate EXPE a HOLD (growth_hold). The bull case rests on durable margin expansion funded partly by AI-driven cost efficiency, B2B leadership (bookings +20%), and a shareholder-return program that has retired ~18% of shares since 2022 while reinstating and growing a dividend. The bear case is the combination of soft 2026 demand guidance and the unresolved question of whether AI agents structurally compress OTA economics over the next several years. At the current price the risk/reward is roughly balanced; the mispricing that would justify a BUY is not clearly present.

## Company Overview

Expedia Group operates one of the world's largest online travel marketplaces across three reportable segments. B2C (~64% of revenue, $9.47B) comprises the flagship Expedia, Hotels.com, and Vrbo brands plus legacy properties (Orbitz, Travelocity, ebookers, Wotif). B2B (~33%, $4.84B, +18% YoY) powers airlines, offline agents, corporate travel managers, and financial institutions through the Rapid API, white-label solutions, and the TAAP agent tool. trivago (~3%, $417M third-party revenue, +33%) is a majority-owned, separately Nasdaq-listed hotel metasearch business.

The revenue model splits into merchant (~70%, where Expedia is merchant of record and collects cash at booking), agency (~22%, commission-based, mostly air), and advertising (~8%). Lodging is the dominant product at 80% of revenue, spanning ~3.6M properties including 2.4M alternative accommodations via Vrbo. Gross bookings reached $119.6B in 2025 (+8%), still a single-digit share of the >$2T global travel market, with a steady ~12.3% revenue take rate.

Structurally, the business benefits from global scale, a unified technology platform (Hotels.com and Vrbo migrated onto the Brand Expedia stack post-2020), rich first-party booking data, and a growing high-margin advertising business. The One Key unified loyalty program, launched in the US in 2023 and expanding internationally, is the key lever for driving repeat, direct bookings against rising customer-acquisition costs.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has been steady rather than spectacular: $12.84B (2023, +7%), $13.69B (2024, +7%), $14.73B (2025, +8%). Room nights grew 8% and lodging revenue 7%. The meaningful story is operating leverage. Operating income rose from $1.03B (2023) to $1.32B (2024) to $1.87B (2025); operating margin moved 8.0% → 9.6% → 12.7%. Adjusted EBITDA went from $2.68B to $2.93B to $3.50B, a 19% gain in 2025 with margin near 23.8%. The margin expansion came from cost discipline: technology and content spend fell 3%, G&A fell 5%, and cost of revenue was held flat while revenue grew 8%. A February 2024 restructuring, expanded through 2025, drove the headcount reductions, with roughly $60M in additional charges expected in Q1 2026. Management has explicitly linked part of the efficiency to AI: more than 30% of self-serve customer support is now automated, and developer-productivity gains are contributing to the cost base reduction.

Cash generation is high quality. Operating cash flow was $3.88B in 2025 (up from $3.09B), and free cash flow was $3.11B for a 21.1% FCF margin. The negative-working-capital merchant model is a genuine structural float source: cash is collected at booking before suppliers are paid, funding a portion of the balance sheet at zero cost. This dynamic is the reason the current ratio (0.73) and Altman Z-Score (0.74) look alarming but are not. Thin equity ($1.28B) and the resulting ROE of ~101% and D/E of ~19x are artifacts of years of buybacks depleting retained earnings, not signs of leverage stress.

Real leverage is moderate: roughly $6.3B face-value debt against cash and short-term investments and $3.5B EBITDA, which is about 1.8x gross and ~0.2x net. The revolver is essentially untapped. In 2025 the company issued $1B of 5.40% senior notes due 2035 and redeemed $1B of 6.25% notes, and $1B of convertible notes matured in February 2026. Credit ratings are investment grade and stable across all three agencies.

Capital allocation has been aggressive and, by the numbers, sensible. Expedia repurchased 9.0M shares in 2025 at an average $184.76 ($1.66B), with $1.6B still authorized, and cut diluted share count from 161.8M (2022) to 131.9M (2025), an ~18% reduction. It reinstated a dividend in Q1 2025 ($1.60/share for 2025) and raised the declared quarterly dividend to $0.48 in February 2026. One caution: shares were bought at ~$185 in 2025 versus the current $260, so those repurchases have been value-accretive, but the pace of future buybacks at higher prices deserves monitoring.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in near-term estimates: four consecutive beats, including a 38.4% beat in Q1 2026 ($1.95 vs. $1.41 estimate) and a 10.4% beat in Q4 2025. The tax and litigation exposure is the genuine cash-flow wildcard: IRS transfer-pricing adjustments of $244M (2011–13) and $431M (2014–16) plus interest, with 2017–2020 under exam, and recurring European settlements ($178M Italian withholding tax in 2025, $107M Italian VAT and $30M Canadian DST in 2024). These are lumpy and real, and "pay-to-play" prepayment rules could accelerate the cash timing.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine has shifted decisively away from consumer OTA. B2C revenue grew only 2% and consumer bookings only 3%, while B2B revenue grew 18% and bookings grew 20%, and advertising (EG Advertising +19%, trivago third-party +33%) delivered high-margin incremental revenue. This mix shift toward B2B and media is the durable part of the growth story and is arguably less exposed to AI-agent disintermediation than pure consumer OTA traffic, since B2B distribution embeds Expedia's supply into partners' own products.

Estimate revisions are broadly positive but modest. Current-quarter EPS consensus rose 5.6% over 90 days to $5.28, current-year rose 3.1% to $19.92, but next-year estimates edged down 0.5% over 30 days to $22.97. The 90-day revision velocity is well short of the +30% threshold that would flag a decisive inflection; this is a steadily improving estimate picture, not a discontinuous re-rating catalyst. Revenue growth in the high single digits also sits below the 40%+ level that would justify treating trailing multiples as irrelevant. The growth thesis is real but incremental: mid-single-digit revenue growth, high-single-digit EBITDA growth, and low-double-digit EPS growth amplified by share-count reduction.

The near-term overhang is demand. Management flagged weaker-than-expected US travel demand in H1 2025 and softer inbound international travel to the US early in the year, improving in H2. The Q1 2026 stock reaction, beating on earnings but selling off on a disappointing full-year 2026 forecast, captures the tension. If US consumer discretionary spending weakens into H2 2026, as broader indicators suggest is a live risk, bookings growth could decelerate further. Against that, B2B and advertising provide a partial offset because they are less tied to US consumer sentiment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $259.94, EXPE trades at ~27x trailing EPS of $9.81 and roughly 13x forward EPS on the $19.92 current-year consensus. Against the Hotels, Resorts & Cruise Lines peer median P/E of 30.8, the stock looks cheap, but the peer set is a poor comparable: it mixes cruise operators (CCL 12.6x, NCLH 15.8x), hotel franchisors (WH 30.8x), and a ski resort (MTN 31.2x), with a distorted median driven by Hyatt's negative earnings. The more relevant benchmark is Booking Holdings, which trades at a premium to Expedia and carries higher margins and faster consumer bookings growth. Expedia's discount to Booking is justified by its slower B2C growth, thinner consumer moat, and greater relative exposure to the AI/Google disintermediation vector.

On its own numbers, ~13x forward earnings for a business compounding EPS in the low double digits (via mid-single-digit revenue plus buybacks) is reasonable rather than obviously mispriced. The consensus mean target of $289.31 and median of $290 imply ~11–12% upside, with a wide range ($240 low to $409 high) reflecting genuine disagreement about the AI-disruption tail. Analyst sentiment is stable (17 buys, 21 holds), with the split between buy and hold ratings itself signaling a lack of consensus that this is a clear buy.

The valuation discipline here matters. A cheap-looking multiple is not on its own a reason to expect outperformance, and in the current environment low multiples have more often marked value traps than opportunities. The specific reason EXPE trades below where a clean compounder would is a defensible market judgment about AI risk and consumer deceleration, not an obvious error. We do not see a specific catalyst that forces a near-term re-rating: estimate revisions are positive but modest, the demand guidance is a headwind not a tailwind, and the buyback support is already reflected in current EPS. The stock's ~36% appreciation off its low reflects real margin improvement rather than multiple inflation, so we do not treat prior gains as a valuation negative. The valuation is fair, roughly a 5.3, offering neither compelling asymmetric upside nor a stretched multiple begging to compress.

## Competitive Landscape

Expedia competes in a consolidating OTA market against a larger and higher-margin Booking Holdings (Booking.com, Kayak, Agoda), Airbnb in alternative accommodations, and Google, which is simultaneously a critical traffic source and a disintermediation threat via Google Travel, Flights, and Hotel Ads. The newest competitive vector is AI travel agents from OpenAI, Perplexity, and Google Gemini, which could intermediate the customer relationship and force Expedia to pay to appear in AI-generated responses.

Expedia's competitive trajectory is mixed. It is losing the pure consumer growth race (B2C bookings up only 3%) but winning in B2B, where +20% bookings growth reflects a genuine leadership position, and in advertising, where 19% growth signals monetization of first-party data and supply breadth. The strategic bet is that scale, a unified platform, and rich booking data can be monetized through B2B distribution and media even if consumer OTA traffic is pressured. That bet is plausible but unproven against the AI threat, and it is why we score competitive position at 5.5, down from earlier reads: the trajectory in the highest-visibility segment (consumer) is weakening even as B2B and advertising improve.

The company is not a passive incumbent on AI. It automates more than 30% of self-serve support, uses AI to drive developer productivity and the cost cuts behind margin expansion, has added board-level AI expertise (Alexandr Wang of Scale AI, alongside Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi and Brex founder Henrique Dubugras), and has built booking integrations across AI platforms. Being distributed through third-party AI platforms turns those platforms into channels rather than owners of Expedia's products. Whether that keeps Expedia relevant or commoditizes its supply into someone else's interface is the central unresolved competitive question.

## Risk Assessment

The defining risk is AI disintermediation, which the FY2025 10-K addresses far more extensively than prior filings, stating that failure to navigate the "agentic revolution" could have a material adverse effect. If AI travel assistants capture the discovery-and-booking flow, Expedia faces lower direct-booking rates, higher acquisition costs to surface in AI responses, and amplified fraud risk from agentic bookings. This is a multi-year structural risk, not a dated binary, but it is the reason the stock will not re-rate to a clean compounder multiple until the market gains confidence Expedia can defend or monetize its position.

Google dependence compounds this: algorithm and product changes can raise acquisition costs or reduce organic traffic at any time. Supplier and direct-booking pressure is a persistent margin headwind, and Expedia has reduced its economics in certain geographies to drive room-night growth, a drag partly masked by the advertising mix shift. Tax and legal exposure is a real cash risk, with combined IRS transfer-pricing exposure of roughly $675M plus interest and recurring European settlements. Alternative-accommodations regulation, including the EU Short-Term Rental Law effective May 2026, could reduce Vrbo listings. Demand cyclicality remains high given consumer discretionary sensitivity and the soft US demand flagged in 2025.

Governance is a persistent overhang. Barry Diller controls 100% of Class B shares (~32% of voting power) on a ~4.6% economic stake, the dual-class structure and takeover-deterring provisions limit any buyout-premium optionality, and IAC board overlaps (Diller, Chelsea Clinton, Alexander von Furstenberg) create disclosed conflicts. An April 23, 2026 8-K disclosed a director/officer departure whose details warrant follow-up given the company's history of leadership churn. These factors weigh on both management-quality and risk scoring. Insider activity is otherwise routine: director grants and vesting, plus a Chief Legal Officer sale of 4,702 shares at $233 in June 2026, with no open-market buying cluster and no alarming discretionary selling.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 43.4% sits slightly below one-year historical volatility of 47.6% (a -4.2% spread, cheap), and IV percentile within the current chain is 1%. ATM IV collapsed from 89.0% to 43.4% since the mid-June snapshot as the earnings event passed, and the P/C OI ratio fell to 0.35. The term structure is in contango (near-term 43.4% < long-term 47.7%), a normal, complacent shape, though the 6-month tenor shows a -4.8% put skew and an outsized P/C volume reading, consistent with some longer-dated hedging of the AI/demand tail. Short interest at 9.1% of float with 5.1 days to cover is unremarkable. The net signal is a market that sees no imminent binary catalyst but retains a modest longer-dated hedge against downside, broadly consistent with our balanced HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Margin expansion is durable and partly structural: AI-driven support automation and developer productivity are lowering the cost base while B2B (+20% bookings) and high-margin advertising (+19%) grow the revenue mix toward more defensible streams. Adjusted EBITDA compounds at high-single to low-double digits, share count keeps falling (~18% since 2022), the dividend grows, and $3.1B of annual FCF funds continued returns. If the AI threat proves manageable and Expedia becomes embedded distribution across AI platforms, the stock re-rates toward Booking-like multiples. Bull case ~$340–360, roughly 30–38% upside, on ~18x $22.97 next-year EPS with multiple expansion as AI fears fade.

**Bear Case.** US consumer travel demand weakens into an H2 2026 discretionary slowdown, consumer bookings decelerate below the current 3%, and AI agents begin measurably compressing OTA economics: lower direct-booking rates, higher costs to appear in AI responses, supplier disintermediation. Combined with lumpy tax cash outflows and the governance discount, the market applies a persistent structural discount. Bear case ~$205–215, roughly 17–21% downside, on ~10x forward EPS reflecting terminal-decline fears.

The tension is record margins and aggressive capital return against decelerating consumer growth and an unresolved technology question. At $259.94 the market has re-rated the stock up on the margin story while retaining a discount for the AI/demand risk. That is a reasonable equilibrium, which is why the risk/reward is roughly balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (early August 2026), which will update full-year 2026 guidance and provide fresh reads on US demand and B2B/advertising momentum. The FY2026 guidance trajectory is the single most important near-term variable.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY2026 revenue/bookings guidance raised or reaffirmed with acceleration in consumer bookings above ~5%, easing the demand overhang.
- Evidence that AI-platform integrations are generating incremental, monetizable booking volume rather than commoditizing supply, for example disclosed booking share or take rate from AI channels.
- A pullback toward ~$215–225 that widens the discount to intrinsic value without any fundamental deterioration, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Consumer bookings turning negative, or adjusted EBITDA margin contracting for two consecutive quarters, signaling the margin story has stalled.
- Concrete evidence of AI-agent disintermediation compressing take rates or forcing a step-change in marketing spend to defend traffic.
- A materially adverse resolution of the IRS transfer-pricing exams or a governance event that impairs capital allocation or strategic flexibility.

**Base case:** ~$285–290, roughly 10–12% upside, aligned with the consensus mean/median targets, on ~13x the $22.97 next-year EPS with modest multiple support from continued buybacks and margin resilience, over a 12-month horizon.

## Conclusion

Expedia is a financially healthy, highly cash-generative online travel agency executing well on cost discipline, B2B growth, advertising monetization, and shareholder returns. The FY2025 numbers describe a company compounding earnings power at a respectable rate under a durable, float-advantaged model. The distress-signaling balance-sheet ratios are artifacts of the merchant model and buybacks, not genuine leverage risk, and should not be read as such.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is valuation and risk symmetry, not fundamental weakness. At ~13x forward earnings the stock is fairly priced for its growth and quality, with a defensible discount reflecting decelerating consumer bookings, soft 2026 US-demand guidance, and the unresolved AI-disintermediation question. Estimate revisions are positive but too modest to signal a decisive inflection, and no specific catalyst forces a near-term re-rating. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$220s, on confirmation that consumer demand is stabilizing, or on evidence that AI integrations add rather than erode economics. We rate EXPE a HOLD (growth_hold), with a base-case target of ~$285–290 over 12 months.
