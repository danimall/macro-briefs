**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Occidental completed its most consequential portfolio move since the 2019 Anadarko acquisition: the sale of OxyChem to Berkshire Hathaway for $9.5B net cash, closed January 2, 2026. The proceeds funded an aggressive debt paydown: face-value borrowings fell from $20.4B at year-end 2025 to $13.8B at the end of Q1 2026 (with an additional $0.4B repaid post-quarter), and management lowered its principal-debt target from ~$14.3B to ~$10.0B. The maturity wall is effectively cleared through 2029 ($48M due 2027, $14M 2028, $13.3B concentrated 2030+). This is a materially safer balance sheet than the one that carried Oxy through the post-CrownRock period, and it is the single strongest element of the current thesis.

The offset is that Occidental is now a more concentrated bet on the price of crude. With the cash-generating chemical business gone, the 10-K states plainly that Oxy is "more exposed to fluctuations in the markets for oil." Management, which historically ran unhedged, put on crude two-way collars in February 2026 (100 Mbbl/d, $55 floor / $75.89 ceiling), indicating that the new leadership prioritizes protecting deleveraging cash flows over maximizing commodity upside; the collars produced a $339M derivative loss in Q1 as prices rose. The equity trades at $54.86, below Berkshire's $59.59 warrant strike, which caps the practical upside for common holders and signals continued market skepticism despite a rare convergence of positive catalysts.

We maintain a HOLD. The score improves modestly to 5.5 (from 5.8 prior, reflecting a re-weighting of falling near-term estimate revisions and the two-sided Hormuz binary rather than any fundamental reversal). Occidental has de-risked its balance sheet and installed a new CEO who bought stock on the open market. What holds us back from a BUY is a combination the alternatives sectors and value-trap history counsel against rewarding on cheapness alone: negative near-term estimate momentum, a Berkshire capital stack ($8.3B/8% preferred plus warrants) that sits ahead of common equity upside, a ~$3.8B retained environmental tail, a ~$2.3B Tronox tax contingency, and a valuation that is not cheap on a normalized basis once the OxyChem gain is stripped out.

## Company Overview

As of January 2, 2026, Occidental is a pure-play upstream and midstream/carbon-management business organized into two reporting segments:

- **Oil & Gas (~97% of net sales).** One of the largest U.S. liquids producers. FY2025 total production was 1,434 Mboe/d, up 8% YoY on a full year of CrownRock, of which ~84% is U.S. onshore/offshore. The Permian Basin (786 Mboe/d, ~$3.4B of 2025 capex) is the core asset, split between Permian Resources (unconventional/horizontal) and Permian EOR (industry-leading CO₂ flooding across 34 active floods with 50+ years of experience). Additional legs: Rockies/DJ Basin/Powder River (284 Mboe/d), Gulf of America (132 Mboe/d, 4th-largest deepwater producer), and international PSCs in Oman, UAE, Algeria, and Qatar (~19% of net sales). Proved reserves stand at 4,603 MMboe (47% oil, 72% proved developed).
- **Midstream & Marketing + Low-Carbon Ventures (OLCV).** A 40.8% economic interest in Western Midstream (WES), the Dolphin pipeline, Al Hosn sulfur, and carbon management. STRATOS, the first commercial-scale Direct Air Capture facility (Ector County, TX), is funded 50/50 with BlackRock ($550M NCI); CO₂ sequestration is expected to begin in 2026. Oxy also holds 40.3% of NET Power.

The differentiators are unmatched Permian EOR/CO₂ expertise, decades of low-decline conventional inventory, and carbon-management optionality that is genuine but pre-commercial. The international PSCs give Oxy a partial natural hedge, since its reserve share rises when prices fall.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $21.6B, down from $26.7B in 2024 and $28.3B in 2023. The decline is price-driven: WTI averaged $64.81 in 2025 vs. $75.72 in 2024 (–14%), while volumes rose 8%. Net income to common was $1.65B ($1.61 diluted EPS) vs. $2.38B in 2024, reflecting lower realizations and higher DD&A ($7.5B on CrownRock). Lease operating expense improved to $8.94/Boe from $9.75, a genuine efficiency gain from Permian optimization.

**Cash generation.** Occidental generated $10.5B operating cash flow and $4.1B FCF in 2025 and is investment-grade (Baa3/BBB/BB+). The screening Altman Z-Score of 1.22 flags "distress," but this is a false signal for capital-intensive E&P: the metric penalizes low asset turnover (0.26) and thin working capital (current ratio 0.94), both structural to oil and gas, not indicative of solvency risk. Piotroski reads a healthier 4/5.

**The defining event — OxyChem sale and deleveraging.** The $9.5B net cash booked a $3.1B after-tax gain that drove Q1 2026 headline net income to $3.36B ($3.13 EPS). Continuing-operations EPS was $0.05, dragged by the $339M collar derivative loss and a $186M Permian asset-sale loss. Long-term debt net dropped from $20.6B to $15.2B. Management retired $6.7B of debt in Q1 with a $237M early-extinguishment charge. After the $10.0B principal-debt goal, cash goes to further reduction and/or opportunistic buybacks ($1.2B remaining on the $3.0B authorization).

**Capital allocation.** 2026 capex is guided to $5.5–5.9B (roughly flat, disciplined). The dividend was raised to $0.26/quarter in Q1 2026 (from $0.96 annualized). Management targets >$1.2B of incremental FCF in 2026 at flat prices.

The earnings surprise record is strong (4 consecutive beats, most recently a 77.5% beat in Q1 2026 on $1.06 actual vs. $0.60 estimate), but that pattern is amplified by the OxyChem gain and commodity torque rather than durable operational outperformance. It supports confidence in near-term cash generation, not a structural earnings-power step-up.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is a commodity-price story overlaid on modest volume growth and portfolio high-grading. Volumes rose 8% in 2025 on CrownRock, but 2026 capex is flat and the company is selling non-core Permian royalty/mineral interests and gas plants (a ~$186M book loss in March 2026), so the volume trajectory is closer to stable-to-modestly-growing than expansionary. The revenue trajectory into 2026 is dominated by WTI, which averaged $71.93 in Q1 2026 (up sharply from $59.14 in Q4 2025) as Hormuz risk re-firmed crude.

The estimate revision picture is mixed and net negative on near-term momentum, which is the key growth-scoring input. Current-year EPS estimates are up +30.5% over 90 days ($4.16 → $5.43), a strong figure that reflects the oil recovery, but they have fallen –4.1% over the last 30 days ($5.66 → $5.43). Next-quarter estimates are down –18.9% over 30 days. The consensus reads "estimates broadly falling — negative earnings momentum" on the near-term windows even as the 90-day trend remains positive. This is a commodity-driven mark-up that has begun to fade at the margin as the near-term oil path becomes two-sided on the Hormuz outcome, not the clean, accelerating +30% signal that would push us toward a BUY. Growth prospects are real but cyclical and price-dependent, warranting a middling score.

The carbon-management push (STRATOS/DAC with BlackRock, CCUS, NET Power) is a call option, not an earnings contributor: a $401M NET Power impairment was taken in Q4 2025, and 45Q tax-credit dependence carries policy risk. We do not underwrite it as growth.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline trailing P/E of 74x is not meaningful; it reflects the collar loss and asset-sale drag on continuing-operations EPS, not run-rate earnings power. The more useful anchor is forward consensus of $5.43 current-year EPS, which puts OXY at roughly 10.1x forward earnings, and $3.86 next-year EPS at ~14.2x. That next-year figure is instructive: consensus already models a normalization back toward the mid-$3 range as commodity tailwinds fade, so the "cheap" 10x current-year figure is a cyclical peak-ish print, not a durable trough multiple.

Against peers, the picture is genuinely mixed. Peer-median P/E is 13.5x (EOG 13.5x, DVN 12.4x, EQT 9.7x); FANG (197x) and TPL (50.7x) are distorted. On next-year earnings OXY at ~14.2x sits roughly in line with the peer median, neither a discount that signals mispricing nor a premium. The dividend yield of ~1.9% is below the peer median (2.2%) and well below DVN (2.7%) and EOG (2.9%), so absolute yield support is thinner than at cash-return-focused peers.

A compressed multiple on cyclical peak earnings is a warning rather than a buy signal: the valuation is fair, not asymmetrically attractive. The specific structural feature that caps upside is Berkshire's $59.59 warrant strike; the stock trading below it tells you that a large, informed holder has a claim on the equity's re-rating above that level. The $8.3B/8% preferred ($679M/yr, senior to common) is a fixed drain on distributable cash flow that ranks ahead of common. Energy is the worst-alpha large-sample sector in our framework, so a positive raw setup here is largely beta and demands a company-specific catalyst that clears cheapness. The buyback pivot at the $10B debt target is that potential catalyst, but it is prospective, not in the numbers today.

Base-case fair value on ~$4.50 blended normalized EPS at ~12.5x is roughly $56, close to current price, consistent with a HOLD. The consensus mean target of $64.52 (median $65) implies ~18% upside, but the sentiment trend is degrading (one strongBuy downgraded, sells rising from 1 to 2) and the low target ($55) sits at current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Occidental's competitive position is stable, with a genuine edge in Permian EOR/CO₂ flooding and a differentiated (if unproven-at-scale) carbon-management platform. The 10-K acknowledges competitors that are "larger and better funded"; XOM and CVX have superior balance-sheet firepower, so Oxy's advantage is asset-quality and technical expertise rather than scale of capital. Lease operating expense improvement to $8.94/Boe and 8% volume growth from CrownRock demonstrate the Permian franchise is executing, and reserve replacement was healthy (340 MMboe of extensions/discoveries, mostly Permian).

The trajectory is neither clearly improving nor deteriorating. The balance-sheet transformation improves competitive flexibility (less debt-servicing constraint on capex and M&A optionality), but the sale of OxyChem removed a cash-flow-diversifying, counter-cyclical business, leaving Oxy more exposed to the same commodity cycle as every pure-play E&P. Carbon management is a real differentiator versus peers, but until STRATOS proves commercial economics it is optionality, not a moat that generates returns. We score this dimension in the middle: a quality asset base with a differentiated technical edge, offset by increased commodity concentration and no clear share-gain trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks not fully in the valuation cluster into five buckets:

1. **Higher commodity concentration, now partially collared.** Post-OxyChem, Oxy is a more concentrated oil bet. The February 2026 collars protect the deleveraging plan but cap upside: the $75.89 ceiling means the equity does not fully capture an oil spike above that level, while the $55 floor limits downside participation. The two-sided Hormuz binary is the dominant near-term swing factor: a verified reopening pulls crude lower and compresses upstream realizations; a decisive failure spikes crude but caps Oxy's participation at the ceiling.

2. **Tronox/Anadarko tax contingency (~$2.3B).** If the U.S. Tax Court rules Anadarko's $5.2B Tronox settlement non-deductible, Oxy repays ~$1.4B in taxes plus ~$1.0B accrued interest. A decision could come at any time; it is not insured. This is a real cash exposure the market under-discounts.

3. **Retained OxyChem environmental tail.** Oxy kept the legacy environmental liabilities and indemnified Berkshire. The Diamond Alkali/Passaic River Superfund exposure is significant: EPA assigned Oxy ~85% of OU2+OU4 (~$1.4B + ~$440M), a $925M reserve increase was booked Q4 2024, and cleanup runs 10-20+ years. Total remediation reserve is $1.86B with up to $1.9B reasonably possible additional (roughly $3.8B combined).

4. **Berkshire capital stack.** The $8.3B/8% preferred ($679M/yr senior dividend), the $59.59 warrants (83.9 million), and ~28% common ownership make Berkshire simultaneously largest holder, preferred creditor, and OxyChem buyer/indemnified party. This anchors stability but structurally overhangs common-equity upside.

5. **Carbon-management execution and policy risk.** STRATOS/DAC and CCUS depend on 45Q credits facing OBBBA and executive-order uncertainty. The $401M NET Power impairment in Q4 2025 shows the downside is not theoretical.

The CEO transition (Hollub retired June 1, 2026; Jackson succeeded) adds execution risk during the transformation, though Jackson's open-market buy mitigates the signal.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is call-heavy and does not confirm a bearish setup. Near-term ATM IV of 37.2% is roughly fair against 34.3% historical vol (2.8pp spread), and IV percentile within the chain is low at 13%, so options are not pricing extreme stress and entry timing is not penalized by rich premium. The put/call skew is –3.6% (call-tilted), the put/call volume ratio is a very low 0.10, and unusual activity is concentrated in short-dated upside calls ($56 and $55 strikes expiring 7/24, 7.9x and 4.4x volume/OI). The term structure is in mild backwardation (near 37.2% > long 33.9%), consistent with the market pricing an imminent event (likely the Hormuz outcome and next earnings) rather than a directional bearish view. Short interest is negligible at 0.0% of float. The options market is leaning modestly bullish/neutral, which mildly supports the HOLD-with-upside framing rather than contradicting it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($68–75).** The $10B debt target is hit, freeing incremental FCF for the remaining buyback and dividend growth. Elevated oil prices lift realizations toward the collar ceiling and sustain current-year EPS near $5.43+. The market re-rates the cleaner balance sheet toward peer multiples, the stock clears the $59.59 warrant strike, and carbon-management optionality begins to be assigned value. Consensus high target is $75.

**Bear case ($42–46).** A verified, held Hormuz reopening pulls crude lower, compressing upstream realizations; next-year EPS normalizes toward the $3.86 consensus or below. The Tronox ruling lands adversely, Passaic reserves rise further, and the buyback is deferred to keep deleveraging on track. The stock de-rates toward 11-12x normalized EPS, roughly $42-46 (~16-24% downside).

**Base case ($56–60).** Oil grinds in a firm range on the most-probable Hormuz scenario, Oxy hits its debt target and begins modest buybacks, and blended normalized EPS lands near $4.50. At ~12.5x that is ~$56, with the warrant strike acting as a soft ceiling until the buyback flow materializes. Base fair value ~$56-60, essentially in line to modestly above current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026) and any Tronox Tax Court ruling are the near-term catalysts; the Hormuz physical-verification outcome (72+ hours sustained commercial transit) is the dominant macro swing over the next 1-3 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Occidental reaches the ~$10B principal-debt target and confirms redirection of FCF to the remaining $1.2B buyback authorization, with the stock still below the $59.59 warrant strike.
- Current-year and next-year estimate revisions turn positive on the 30-day window (reversing the current –4.1% / –4.4% near-term declines) alongside sustained higher WTI.
- Favorable or de minimis resolution of the Tronox tax contingency, removing the cash overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified, held Hormuz reopening drives crude lower with next-year EPS estimates cut below ~$3.50 and the debt target slipping past the buyback pivot.
- Adverse Tronox ruling combined with a material Passaic reserve increase that defers capital returns.
- Dividend or buyback cut to preserve the deleveraging plan.

## Conclusion

Occidental executed a genuine balance-sheet transformation: OxyChem proceeds cut debt toward the lowered $10B target, cleared the maturity wall through 2029, and a new CEO backed the story with an open-market purchase at $52.38. That is real de-risking, and it is why Financial Health improves to 6.5 and Management Quality holds at 6.0 despite the leadership handoff.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the equity is now a more concentrated, collared oil-price call encumbered by a Berkshire capital stack that ranks ahead of common upside, priced at roughly 14x next-year earnings (in line with peers, not cheap), with near-term estimate momentum turning negative, a two-sided Hormuz binary, and a combined ~$6B of retained environmental and Tronox tax exposure. Energy is a negative-alpha sector where cheapness alone is a value-trap warning, and the specific catalyst that would justify a BUY — the pivot from deleveraging to buybacks with the stock still below the warrant strike — is prospective. We rate OXY a HOLD (growth_hold: the balance-sheet clean-up and buyback pivot are credible 12-month catalysts, but they need to materialize before the risk/reward turns asymmetric). Base-case fair value ~$56-60, roughly in line with the $54.86 current price.
