**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Southwest Gas Holdings has completed a multi-year transformation into a pure-play regulated natural gas distribution utility. With the final Centuri shares sold in September 2025 and MountainWest divested in early 2023, what remains is Southwest Gas Corporation, the largest regulated gas distributor in Arizona and Nevada, with a smaller California presence, serving roughly 2.29 million customers. The business is now clean, defensive, and exposed to some of the fastest-growing utility service territories in the country. Full revenue decoupling in all three states insulates margin from weather and conservation, and the customer base is >99% residential and small commercial.

The investment story is a regulated-utility growth compounder: management reaffirmed FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $4.17–$4.32, a 12–14% long-term EPS growth target, and a $6.3B 2026–2030 capital plan, while stating it does not expect to issue equity in 2026. The key optionality sits in the Great Basin 2028 Expansion (~$1.7B headline), where an April 2026 open season drew ~2.5 Bcf/d of interest against a ~0.3 Bcf/d offering, roughly 8x oversubscribed, with demand stretching to 2035. This points to a potential multi-phase transmission platform that could extend the rate-base growth runway beyond the current plan, and underpinned JP Morgan's May 2026 upgrade to Overweight.

The constraint is price. SWX trades at ~26.7x trailing and ~20.7x forward earnings against a gas-utility peer median near 15.6x, while offering a below-peer dividend yield (2.9% vs. 3.5% median). The premium is partly earned by superior territory growth and decoupling, but the gap is wide and arrives alongside genuine idiosyncratic concerns: an unremediated material weakness in financial reporting controls (with an associated non-reliance restatement), an unusual cadence of executive and director turnover, and a partial-disappointment Arizona rate case that highlights regulatory-lag risk in the company's largest jurisdiction. We rate SWX a HOLD on a growth basis: own the compounding, wait for a better entry.

## Company Overview

Southwest Gas Holdings is the parent of Southwest Gas Corporation, a regulated natural gas LDC. The portfolio has been radically simplified:

- **MountainWest** (interstate pipeline/storage) sold February 2023.
- **Centuri** (utility infrastructure services) IPO'd April 2024, then fully divested through 2025 (final shares sold September 5, 2025; deconsolidated August 2025 after dropping below a controlling interest at 30.9%). Centuri is now reported as discontinued operations.

The remaining utility serves ~2,287,000 customers as of Q1 2026: ~1.23M in Arizona (53% of operating margin), ~852K in Nevada (35%), and ~208K in California (12%). The mix is >99% residential and small commercial, and ~85% of operating margin comes from that defensive customer class. All three jurisdictions feature full revenue decoupling, and purchased gas adjustment mechanisms pass gas costs through dollar-for-dollar with no markup. The company also operates two FERC-regulated interstate pipelines, including Great Basin Gas Transmission in northern Nevada, the platform for the major expansion described below.

As a regulated monopoly distributor, the company's moat is its franchise and regulatory construct. Organic growth (~37,000 first-time meter sets in 2025, ~1.0–1.6% customer growth) is driven by Sun Belt population and economic expansion in Phoenix/Tucson and Las Vegas/Reno.

## Financial Analysis

**The clean earnings number requires care.** 2025 reported net income of $439.8M ($6.09 basic EPS) is inflated by a $343.1M gain on Centuri deconsolidation (including a $222.9M remeasurement gain) booked in discontinued operations. The relevant go-forward figure is continuing-operations EPS of $3.25 for 2025. A naive screen reading the $6.09 GAAP number, and the resulting low headline P/E, would badly misjudge this stock.

**Distribution earnings trajectory (continuing ops):** Natural Gas Distribution net income grew from $242.2M (2023) to $261.2M (2024) to $300.3M (2025), steady ~8–15% annual growth on rate relief and customer additions. Q1 2026 continuing-ops EPS was $1.91 vs. $1.86 prior year, with operating margin (revenue less cost of gas) rising to $477.0M (+$15.1M) on rate increases and ~1.0% customer growth, partly offset by higher D&A, taxes, and non-deductible executive comp.

**Margins and returns (continuing ops, 2025):** net margin ~22.4%, ROE ~11%, ROA ~4.2%. Operating margin as the company defines it was $1.44B in 2025 vs. $1.33B in 2024.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:** 2025 operating cash flow was $594M against $808M capex, producing roughly negative $214M FCF. Negative FCF is structural and normal for a capital-intensive growth utility funding rate-base expansion; it is not a distress signal. The 2025 deleveraging materially de-risked the holding company: ~$1.3B of Centuri proceeds funded $709M of debt repayment, eliminating the full $550M floating-rate holding-company term loan and a $159M revolver paydown. Long-term debt fell from $4.35B (2024) to $3.43B (2025); cash ended 2025 at $577M and $485M at Q1 2026. Total equity was $3.96B (2025), rising to $4.10B at Q1 2026. The screen-reported D/E of 0.03 is a computation artifact; the real structure is roughly balanced (~$3.4B debt vs. ~$4.1B equity), consistent with the ~50% equity layers used in rate cases. Investment-grade ratings are affirmed Stable: Holdings at Baa2/BBB+/BBB; Southwest Gas Corp one notch higher (Baa1/BBB+/A-).

**On the material weakness and restatement — and why this is not a going-concern situation.** In January 2026, SWX identified a material weakness related to a management review control over estimated future state apportionment rates for deferred taxes, surfaced by the change in business structure following the Centuri divestiture. This caused misstatement of income tax expense and deferred tax liabilities and triggered an Item 4.02 non-reliance notice and restatement of prior unaudited periods. The errors trace to 2017. The weakness remained unremediated at March 31, 2026 (Holdings' disclosure controls deemed not effective; Southwest Gas Corp's effective). The magnitude is modest: a ~$36.7M cumulative retained-earnings reduction at Southwest Gas and a ~$0.3M quarterly net income revision. This is a controls and quality-of-earnings concern, not a solvency or going-concern matter. There is no going-concern or substantial-doubt language in the filings; ratings are investment-grade and Stable; liquidity is ample ($485M cash plus $340M unused ATM); the over-collected PGA refund is a timing item; and management states it does not need equity in 2026. The liquidity runway extends well beyond a 12-month holding period. A new consolidation software implementation is planned for mid-2026 as part of remediation. We weight this as an elevated audit/governance watch-item that caps the Risk and Management scores, not as a thesis-killer.

**Earnings surprise and revision context:** Q1 2026 missed consensus by 10% ($1.91 vs. $2.12 estimate), while Q4 2025 was a narrow beat. Estimate revisions are mixed-to-soft: current-quarter EPS revised +12% over 30 days but −2.7% over 90 days; current-year is roughly flat (−0.8% / +0.8%); next-year is down modestly (−0.1% / −2.5%). The estimate signal is neutral-to-slightly-negative, which argues against treating this as an inflection BUY.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs:

1. **Rate-base-driven utility growth.** The capital plan (~$1.3B/yr base) supports the 12–14% long-term EPS growth target. Management expects operating cash flow to fund ~60% of capex plus dividends, with the balance from debt and parent equity contributions, and no equity issuance expected in 2026, favorable for per-share growth.

2. **Organic customer growth.** Arizona and Nevada are among the fastest-growing utility territories in the country, providing a structural tailwind (~1.0–1.6% customer growth) that most mature gas LDCs lack. Decoupling converts this volume growth into stable margin.

3. **Great Basin expansion optionality.** The initial project (800 MMcf/d) targets surging northern Nevada demand (data centers, regional growth, electrification of other end-uses). Of the ~2.5 Bcf/d of open-season interest, ~1.0 Bcf/d is for 2028–2029 and ~1.5 Bcf/d for phased service 2030–2035. This suggests a multi-phase, multi-billion-dollar transmission growth platform beyond the current plan. The ACC approved a $1.7B financing waiver (April 2026); a CA financing application for up to $1.15B incremental debt is pending (decision expected end-2026).

The growth thesis has durability, anchored in regulated rate base, demographic tailwinds, and now a demonstrably large transmission demand signal. The offset is delivery: returns depend on constructive rate-case outcomes, and execution risk on Great Basin (FERC approval, scarce specialized labor, long-lead materials, construction beginning Q4 2027 for late-2028 in-service) is real. We score Growth above neutral but short of high-conviction, reflecting durable drivers tempered by a soft near-term estimate trend and execution dependency.

SWX trades at a ~33% premium to the peer median P/E (20.7x forward vs. 15.6x) while offering a lower dividend yield (2.9% vs. 3.5%). A portion of the premium is defensible: superior territory growth, best-in-class decoupling, a de-risked balance sheet post-deleveraging, and the Great Basin option. But the 12–14% EPS growth target is broadly in line with the upper end of what several of these peers can deliver via their own capital plans, and SWX's recent Q1 miss plus a flat-to-negative revision trend do not support paying a premium of this size.

On growth-adjusted terms, a 20.7x forward multiple against a 12–14% growth target implies a PEG near 1.6–1.7, not cheap for a regulated utility, particularly one carrying an unremediated controls weakness. The dividend, while reliably paid since 1956 and raised ~4% to $0.645 quarterly, does not provide the yield support that anchors valuation in this peer group.

The stock has appreciated ~22% over the trailing year and sits near its 52-week high ($94.43), only ~7% below the lowest analyst target ($94) and well below the $99.25 mean. This is consistent with a name where the favorable news (deleveraging, guidance reaffirmation, JP Morgan upgrade) is largely in the price. We do not lower the valuation score because the stock has run; we lower it because current price versus current/forward fundamentals leaves limited asymmetric upside without further multiple expansion that the soft estimate trend does not justify. Valuation scores 4.3.

## Competitive Landscape

As a regulated monopoly, SWX faces no direct retail competitor; its "competition" is electric utilities for residential/commercial heating load and interstate pipelines for large end-users who could theoretically bypass. The franchise is durable, and the territory growth profile is genuinely superior to the average mature gas LDC. Decoupling in all three states is best-in-class for earnings stability.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving on the operating side. The principal structural tension is decarbonization/electrification policy. California is hostile to gas expansion, but SWX's California exposure is small (12% of margin). Arizona and Nevada regulatory environments remain constructive toward gas infrastructure (system integrity programs, RNG, even CCS-enabled "responsibly sourced gas" proposals in Nevada). The Great Basin oversubscription suggests durable, real demand for incremental transmission capacity. The pivot to formula rates (an Annual Rate Adjustment Mechanism) in the February 2026 Arizona case, if approved (rates ~April 2027), would structurally reduce regulatory lag in the largest jurisdiction, an under-appreciated potential improvement to earnings quality.

The competitive position is solid and slightly improving, but the prior trajectory marked it down from 7.8 to 7.0, and we hold near that level (6.8) given the regulatory-lag headwind and the lower long-term gas-demand question that, while distant, is a genuine structural overhang.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Material weakness / restatement (idiosyncratic, elevated).** Unremediated at Q1 2026, rooted in a 2017 error undetected for ~8 years, surfaced by the Centuri divestiture. Small dollar magnitude but a meaningful quality-of-earnings and audit-risk flag, occurring precisely as the company embarks on its largest-ever capital project and swaps consolidation software.

2. **Executive/director turnover.** The 10-K explicitly flags 2025 turnover "including at the executive level," and the 8-K record shows an unusual cadence of Item 5.02 changes (Jun, Jul, Nov 2025; Jan and two in Feb 2026). CEO Karen Haller remains in place per the March 2026 proxy. This is a governance watch-item, amplified by the concurrent control-remediation period.

3. **Regulatory concentration and lag.** ~88% of operating margin from two states (AZ 53%, NV 35%). The 2024 Arizona case disappointed: $80.2M granted vs. $125.6M requested, 9.84% authorized ROE (vs. 10.15% requested), and the System Integrity Mechanism capped at $50M/yr. Pending cases (AZ +$101.0M/10.25% ROE; NV +$71.3M/10.0% ROE; CA partial settlement +$39.5M) will shape achieved returns.

4. **Great Basin execution risk.** FERC approval, scarce specialized labor, long-lead materials, and rights-of-way; cost overruns or delays would compress returns.

5. **Holding-company structure and rate sensitivity.** Dependence on upstream dividends; utility valuations and authorized returns are rate-sensitive.

The risk score (5.0) reflects that several of these are specific, non-priced, and concurrent; the controls weakness and turnover in particular are not generic sector risks.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing acute event risk: 1-week ATM IV of 137.9% against 1-year historical volatility of just 18.1%, with steep backwardation (1-week 137.9% vs. 6-month 18.8%) and an implied move of ~±5.6%. The spike is concentrated in the nearest expiry (the 1-month tenor sits at a far more normal 28.9%), which suggests a near-dated catalyst rather than a structural collapse signal; the 6-month skew is essentially flat (−0.1%) and put/call OI ratio is benign at 0.35. Put skew of 3.1% indicates modestly elevated near-term put demand. The richness of front-month IV versus historical vol argues against buying upside calls here and is consistent with a wait-for-better-entry stance. Short interest is low at 2.6% of float (3 days to cover), so there is no crowded-positioning signal in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** SWX is a clean, investment-grade, fast-growing-territory pure-play gas LDC with full decoupling, a de-risked balance sheet, a credible 12–14% EPS growth target, and no equity issuance needed in 2026. The Great Basin expansion is a multi-phase transmission growth platform that could extend rate-base growth well beyond the current $6.3B plan. Formula rates in Arizona, if approved, structurally lift earnings quality. On these drivers, EPS compounds at the high end of the guidance range and the multiple holds or expands. Bull case ~$106 (analyst high), ~20% above current.

**Bear Case.** The premium multiple compresses toward the peer group as the Q1 miss and soft revision trend persist, Arizona and Nevada rate cases land below request again, the material weakness produces a further restatement or delays remediation, and Great Basin slips on labor/materials. A re-rate toward ~17x forward EPS implies roughly $72, about 18% downside, near the 52-week low.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced-to-slightly-negative at the current price: the upside requires continued multiple support that the estimate trend does not corroborate, while the downside is a credible peer-multiple convergence.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive (regulated rate-base compounding plus Great Basin optionality), but the entry point is unattractive given the peer-relative premium and concurrent idiosyncratic risks. Existing holders should retain for the compounding and the ~2.9% yield; new capital should await a better entry.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward the low-$80s (forward P/E ~18–19x), compressing the peer premium to a defensible level.
- Remediation of the material weakness confirmed in a quarterly filing, with no further restatement.
- Constructive resolution of the Nevada (rates ~Oct 2026) and Arizona rate cases at/near requested ROEs, or formal advancement of a second Great Basin phase from the oversubscribed open season.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new or expanded restatement, or material weakness remaining unremediated through year-end 2026 alongside further executive departures.
- A rate-case outcome materially below request that forces a cut to the 12–14% growth target.
- Guidance reduction below the $4.17–$4.32 FY2026 range, or a forced equity raise contradicting the no-issuance commitment.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), with the CA rate-case settlement (Q2 2026), NV case (rates ~Oct 2026), and the CA financing decision (end-2026) as interim catalysts.

## Conclusion

Southwest Gas has emerged from its portfolio simplification as a higher-quality, more focused business: a pure-play regulated gas distributor in superior-growth Sun Belt territories, with full decoupling, a de-risked balance sheet, a credible double-digit EPS growth target, and a multi-phase transmission growth option in Great Basin. The fundamentals do not show deterioration; the trajectory's prior score declines reflect a richening valuation and the emergence of the controls and turnover concerns, not a broken franchise.

The block to a BUY is price and risk overlay, not the business. At ~20.7x forward against a 15.6x peer median, with a below-peer yield, a Q1 miss, a flat-to-soft revision trend, an unremediated material weakness, and notable executive turnover, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The going-concern concern raised in the prior review does not apply: this is a controls/quality-of-earnings matter of immaterial dollar magnitude, with investment-grade ratings affirmed Stable, ample liquidity, and no equity needs in 2026, so the company's runway extends well beyond a 12-month horizon. We rate SWX a HOLD with a constructive 12-month bias, looking to upgrade on a pullback to the low-$80s or confirmed remediation plus a constructive rate-case outcome.
