**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HF Sinclair is a mid-cap independent downstream energy company (678,000 BPSD of crude capacity across seven inland/Rockies-focused refineries, plus differentiated Lubricants & Specialties and Sinclair-branded Marketing segments) trading at $64.75, roughly 13% below its $74.73 52-week high reached in early May. The investment question has been reframed by the data over the past 90 days. Consensus EPS estimates have moved sharply higher — current-year up 71.8% ($5.39 → $9.26), next-year up 58.0% ($4.78 → $7.56), across 13 analysts — while the stock trades at 9.92x forward earnings, at or below the peer median of 10.8x. Four consecutive large beats (+1157%, +161%, +36%, +63%) confirm operations are running ahead of consensus.

Prior reports on this name carried a HOLD on the view that the estimate-revision momentum was reversible commodity noise and that earnings quality was too poor to support a constructive call. That framing deserves revisiting. The two-sided thesis previously leaned on a specific de-escalation scenario (Hormuz reopening compressing cracks toward a defined band). Stripping the unsupported macro specifics, the surviving cautious view — Mizuho's May 27 downgrade — argues refining cracks do not normalize until 2027. That is a sustained-margin thesis, not a symmetric-risk one. When the bear's own timeline implies the tailwind persists for 18+ months, a sub-10x multiple sits on rising estimates, and the low analyst target sits only ~4% below spot, the reward-to-risk skews positive rather than balanced.

We upgrade to BUY with disciplined sizing. Earnings quality is the central caveat: 2025 net income was flattered by ~$485M of small-refinery RINs waivers, and Q1 2026's headline $648M was inflated by a $672M non-cash LCM inventory reversal. Underlying refining and Renewables margins are genuinely improving, but investors must normalize the headline. The governance episode (CFO accusing CEO of disclosure tone-at-the-top problems; both subsequently exiting; no permanent CEO/CFO named) is a real overhang, but the Audit Committee found no material weakness and no restatement, and interim CEO Myers bought ~$1.04M of stock above the current price. The balance sheet removes downside-tail risk.

## Company Overview

HF Sinclair was created from the 2022 HollyFrontier/Sinclair Oil merger, with Holly Energy Partners fully absorbed in December 2023. It runs seven complex refineries in two regions: Mid-Continent (El Dorado, KS — 135k BPSD coker; Tulsa, OK — ~125k) and West (Puget Sound, WA — 149k; Navajo, NM — 100k; Parco, WY — 94k; Woods Cross, UT — 45k; Casper, WY — 30k). The geographic positioning matters: an inland/Rockies footprint insulates the company from Gulf Coast competition because of product transportation economics, and gives access to advantaged Canadian, Permian, and regional crudes.

Five operating segments: Refining (the core earnings driver), Renewables (~378M gal/yr renewable diesel across three units), Marketing (1,769 Sinclair-branded sites plus ~350 licensed, growing ~10%/yr), Lubricants & Specialties (Petro-Canada Lubricants, Sonneborn, Red Giant — a leading North American Group III base oil and pharmaceutical white-oil producer), and Midstream (legacy HEP pipelines/terminals, largely captive). The Lubricants and branded-Marketing franchises provide earnings ballast that is less commodity-correlated than a pure-play refiner, a genuine structural differentiator. Headcount is ~5,165, of whom ~1,588 are unionized.

The dominant theme is severe cyclical swing. The 2022 super-cycle has normalized, with 2024 as the trough:

| Revenue | $38.2B | $32.0B | $28.6B | $26.9B |
| Net income (attrib.) | $2.92B | $1.59B | $177M | $579M |
| EBITDA | — | $2.90B | $1.13B | $1.81B |
| Adj. refinery GM/bbl | — | $21.06 | $10.43 | $15.37 |
| Diluted EPS | — | $8.29 | $0.91 | $3.08 |

Two earnings-quality flags must be carried throughout the analysis. First, $485M of 2025 pre-tax earnings came from one-time small-refinery RINs waivers; without them, 2025 underlying refining profitability is thin. Second, Q1 2026's headline net income of $648M ($3.56 EPS) was inflated by a $672M non-cash LCM inventory benefit (reversing a $417M 2025 LCM charge). The genuinely improving drivers underneath those non-cash items are West-region refining margins and a sharply better Renewables segment ($156M adjusted gross margin in Q1 2026, including a $49M prior-year PTC catch-up). Operating cash flow swung to a positive $457M in Q1 2026.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story and is what removes downside-tail risk: Altman Z of 3.33 (safe zone), current ratio 1.94, debt/equity 0.79, ~$3.1B liquidity, $2.8B of face debt fully termed out with no maturities until 2028 and no revolver draw. The 2025 refinancing extended maturities and put a $2.0B facility in place to 2030. The company has the financial capacity to absorb both cyclicality and a leadership transition without distress.

Capital allocation has been disciplined. The 2026 capex guide is $775M ($650M sustaining including $325M turnarounds, $125M growth). The dividend is $0.50/quarter (~3.1% yield). Buybacks totaled $340M in 2025 plus $76M in Q1 2026, with $383M remaining authorization; share count has fallen from 202.6M (2022) to ~180.7M. ROE is modest at 6.4% and net margin thin at 2.2%, both reflecting the trough-ish operating environment, which is precisely the point of buying near a cyclical recovery.

Revisions are accelerating (30-day moves of +15-21% sit inside the 90-day moves) and broad-based across 10-13 analysts. The four-quarter beat record confirms that consensus has been systematically chasing reality higher, not the reverse.

The prior HOLD discounted this velocity as "substantially commodity-derived." That is partly true — refining is a margin business and crack spreads drive the swing — but it is not a reason to dismiss the signal. The relevant question is whether the margin tailwind is durable. The most bearish published view (Mizuho, May 27) argues that refining cracks will not normalize until 2027. If the bear case is elevated margins for another 18 months, that supports sustained earnings, not symmetric downside. Morgan Stanley (June 12, PT $78, Overweight) and Zacks (June 22, Rank #2 Buy) read the same setup constructively. The growth is genuine even after normalizing out the RINs windfall and the LCM reversal: West-region margins, the Renewables turnaround, and ~10%/yr Marketing-site growth are real underlying drivers.

The durability caveat: this is a cyclical recovery, not a secular growth story. We weight growth prospects above neutral because the revision velocity is high and the cycle has 18 months of analyst-expected runway, but we do not assign it a premium-secular score.

## Valuation Assessment

At 9.92x forward earnings ($9.26 current-year consensus on a $64.75 price), DINO sits at or below the peer median P/E of 10.8x. Peers are a heterogeneous and largely distressed set — PBF at 10.8x, PARR at 6.2x, while IEP (-13.3x), CVI (-72.7x), and WKC (-3.0x) carry negative or meaningless multiples — which underscores that DINO is one of the higher-quality assets in the group, with the differentiated Lubricants and Marketing franchises and the strongest balance sheet, yet trades at no premium.

The cycle-adjusted lens matters here. Trailing P/E (~9.9x) understates the recovery because it embeds the depressed 2024-2025 base. On the rising forward estimates, the stock is cheap relative to the earnings power being delivered. The framework caution against treating low P/E as a buy signal is well taken — cheapness without a catalyst is a value trap. The catalyst here is specific and present: estimate revisions exceeding +70% over 90 days with accelerating velocity, a configuration that has historically preceded continued re-rating as consensus catches up.

On analyst targets, the median is $79 and the mean $76.36 against a $64.75 price — roughly 18% upside to the median. The low target of $62 sits only ~4% below spot. The prior report cited the $62 low as a bearish tell, but it implies the opposite: even the most cautious published target sees limited downside. A distribution with ~4% downside to the bears and ~18-22% upside to the median/mean is favorable asymmetry. On rising-estimate stocks trading below 30x forward, consensus targets typically lag and revise upward; the $79 median is more likely a floor than a ceiling.

We score valuation constructively. The risk to this view is the earnings-quality problem: if normalized (ex-RINs, ex-LCM) earnings power is materially below the $9.26 headline, the 9.9x is flattered. We address that by sizing the position with discipline rather than treating the headline multiple as gospel.

## Competitive Landscape

DINO is a mid-cap inland/Rockies refiner whose competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than rapidly gaining or losing share. The geographic niche provides a transportation-cost moat against Gulf Coast product, and access to advantaged regional crudes supports differentials. The Lubricants & Specialties segment (global, branded, less commodity-correlated) and the growing Sinclair-branded retail channel (the new Green Trail Fuels JV in CO/NM) provide structural earnings ballast that pure-play refiners lack. The January 2026 $38M Industrial Oils Unlimited bolt-on extends the specialty-fluids franchise.

The competitive trajectory is neither a clear share-gain story nor an erosion story, which is why we hold this dimension near neutral. The recent signal that matters for the cycle — refining margins re-widening on Middle East geopolitical disruption — is an industry tailwind DINO participates in but does not uniquely own. The April 7, 2026 DC Circuit ruling vacating EPA's denial of the Parco refinery's 2024 SRE is a regulatory win specific to DINO's small-refinery structure, though broader SRE litigation continues (DINO brief due July 10, 2026).

## Risk Assessment

**Refining margin cyclicality** is the overwhelming driver — adjusted refinery GM/bbl nearly halved from $21.06 (2023) to $10.43 (2024) before recovering to $15.37 (2025). This is permanently priced into a refiner and is not a novel risk, but it bounds how much weight the current estimate signal deserves.

**Regulatory earnings volatility from RINs / the Renewable Fuel Standard** is unusually consequential for DINO given its multiple small refineries. SREs added $485M to 2025 earnings; the open RINs obligation swung from $43M (YE2025) to $306M (Q1 2026). These outcomes are litigation-dependent and binary — a tailwind in 2025, but inherently unpredictable. This is the single largest source of low earnings quality.

**Governance.** The early-2026 C-suite episode — CFO Atanasov accusing CEO Go of an unfavorable "tone at the top" on FY2025 disclosure, both subsequently taking leave and then exiting (Go departed May 11, Atanasov terminated May 13), with no permanent CEO or CFO yet named — is the most company-specific risk. The mitigating evidence is substantial and should be weighted, not just acknowledged: the Audit Committee concluded there was no unfavorable tone at the top, found controls effective with no material weakness, and there was no restatement. Interim CEO/Chairman Myers bought 15,000 shares at $69.11 (~$1.04M) on May 18, and director Hardy bought ~$100k at $66.32 on June 16, both above the current price. The overhang is real but shrinking: the controls question is resolved, the principals are gone, and insiders are buying above market. What remains is leadership-continuity uncertainty (interim/acting status) during a volatile margin environment, which justifies a discount but not a blocking veto.

**Founder-family supply.** REH Advisors (the Sinclair family entity) is steadily selling shares back to the company under the buyback ($24M Nov 2025, $100M May 2026, ~11.26M shares remaining). This is an orderly board-authorized wind-down, not open-market dumping, but it is a persistent supply factor.

**Operational/environmental.** A heavy 2026 turnaround schedule (Puget Sound, Woods Cross, Parco, Navajo, plus unplanned El Dorado maintenance in Q2), a $189M environmental remediation accrual, and the 2025 Navajo Consent Decree ($35M penalty + ~$30M 2026 capex) are bounded, known items.

On balance, the idiosyncratic risks (RINs volatility, governance, turnaround cadence) are largely visible and increasingly priced, while the balance sheet removes the downside-tail. We score risk modestly below neutral, reflecting that the governance and earnings-quality issues are genuine but no longer blocking.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($79-87)
Refining cracks stay elevated through 2027 (the Mizuho timeline), validating the estimate revisions. West-region margin strength and the Renewables turnaround prove structural rather than one-off. A permanent, credible CEO/CFO is named, removing the governance discount. The buyback continues to shrink share count, and the median target re-rates higher as consensus catches up. Upside to $79-87, ~22-34% from spot.

### Bear Case ($55-62)
Crack spreads compress faster than the bear-case timeline as geopolitical risk de-escalates and demand softens. The RINs/SRE windfalls do not recur, exposing thin normalized refining profitability, and the LCM reversal unwinds. Interim leadership stumbles or a governance shoe drops. Even in this case, the low analyst target and the balance-sheet floor cap downside near $55-62, roughly 4-15% below spot — a shallow drawdown given the dividend and buyback support.

The asymmetry between a ~22-34% bull case and a ~4-15% bear case, with the balance sheet underwriting the floor, moves this from HOLD to BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $76.** Applying ~9.5x to a conservatively normalized FY26 EPS of ~$8.00 (haircutting the consensus for recurring RINs/LCM noise) yields ~$76, consistent with the mean analyst target. This sits between the bear ($55-62) and bull ($79-87) scenarios. Upside to base ~17%.

**Bull case target: $79-87** (~22-34% upside) — cracks elevated through 2027, governance resolved, estimate revisions sustained.

**Bear case target: $55-62** (~4-15% downside) — de-escalation compresses cracks, windfalls do not recur, but balance-sheet and dividend support cap the decline near the $62 low analyst target.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the next two earnings prints, the July 10, 2026 SRE litigation brief, and the cadence of estimate revisions.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Current-year consensus EPS revisions turn negative (>5% downward over 90 days), signaling the crack-spread tailwind is rolling over faster than the 2027 timeline.
2. A material weakness, restatement, or new governance disclosure surfaces — reversing the Audit Committee's clean conclusion.
3. Adjusted refinery margin/bbl falls back toward the 2024 trough (~$10/bbl) in a reported quarter, indicating the recovery was transient.

## Conclusion

We upgrade DINO to **BUY** with disciplined sizing. The prior HOLD rested on a two-sided thesis that does not survive scrutiny once the unsupported macro specifics are removed: the surviving cautious view (Mizuho) argues cracks stay elevated through 2027, which supports sustained margins. The data assembles a recovery profile that meets the relevant bar — estimate revisions over 90 days, a forward multiple below the peer median, four consecutive large beats, ~18% upside to the median target with the bear's low only ~4% below spot, and interim-CEO open-market buying above market — against a strong balance sheet (Altman Z 3.33, no maturities to 2028, ~$3.1B liquidity) that removes the downside tail.

The case is not unqualified. Earnings quality is genuinely low — the 2025 recovery and the Q1 2026 headline were heavily flattered by RINs windfalls and a non-cash LCM reversal — and the governance episode, while substantially resolved on controls, leaves leadership on interim/acting footing. These are reasons to size the position with discipline and to monitor the invalidation triggers closely. When estimate velocity, sub-10x forward valuation, favorable target asymmetry, insider buying above market, and balance-sheet strength all point the same direction, the reward-to-risk is positive.
