## Executive Summary

Pebblebrook Hotel Trust is the largest owner of urban and resort lifestyle hotels in the United States, with 44 properties totaling ~11,000 rooms across 13 gateway markets. The stock has appreciated 51.6% over the past year from its May 2025 low of $8.69, driven by a Q1 2026 operational inflection that saw same-property RevPAR grow 11.8% and Hotel EBITDA surge 27.6% year-over-year.

The investment thesis hinges on a specific catalyst: San Francisco's structural recovery (RevPAR +44.5% in Q1 2026) combined with declining industry-wide hotel supply is driving an FFO/share recovery from the 2025 trough. Management has raised full-year 2026 Adjusted FFO guidance to $1.60-$1.70/share, implying a current forward P/FFO of only 8.7-9.2x versus a 12-15x historical range for hotel REITs. The company continues repurchasing shares at an estimated 37% discount to NAV, having retired 14% of outstanding shares since October 2022.

Material risks constrain conviction: net debt/EBITDA remains elevated at 5.5x, a $677M preferred stock liquidation preference represents ~40% of common equity market cap, Washington D.C. RevPAR collapsed 24.1% in Q1 on government spending cuts, and 21.9% short interest signals significant institutional skepticism. The stock is a HOLD with a positive growth thesis that requires continued RevPAR recovery and leverage reduction to convert into a higher-conviction call.

## Company Overview

Pebblebrook Hotel Trust (NYSE: PEB) is an internally managed REIT founded in 2009 by Chairman & CEO Jon Bortz. The company owns upper-upscale, full-service hotels in major gateway coastal markets including San Francisco, Los Angeles, San Diego, Boston, Chicago, Portland, and Washington D.C. With only 52 corporate employees, PEB operates through a lean headquarters structure, engaging third-party independent management companies to run day-to-day hotel operations.

Key differentiators include the Curator Hotel & Resort Collection (a proprietary independent lifestyle hotel platform providing distribution and procurement benefits), flexible management contracts allowing operator replacement, and a portfolio that has evolved to roughly 47% leisure/resort exposure for diversification against business travel cyclicality. The company does not rely on major branded hotel agreements for most properties, giving it flexibility but also creating distribution challenges relative to peers locked into Marriott or Hilton systems.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Operating Performance:**

Revenue has grown modestly from $1.39B in 2022 to $1.48B in 2025 (+6.5% over three years), reflecting a combination of organic RevPAR recovery and portfolio shrinkage (from 46 to 44 hotels through dispositions). Full-year 2025 was a trough due to the Los Angeles wildfires, Washington D.C. government disruptions, and $48.9M in impairment charges on three hotels.

Q1 2026 represents a clear inflection:
- Same-Property RevPAR: $216 (+11.8% YoY)
- Same-Property Hotel EBITDA: $82.2M (+27.6% YoY)
- Adjusted FFO/share: $0.32 (doubled from $0.16 YoY)
- Hotel EBITDA margin expanded 327 bps to 23.9%
- Revenue grew 10.2% while expenses grew only 5.6%, delivering >50% flow-through

**Cash Flow & Capital Structure:**

Operating cash flow was $249.7M in 2025. Capital investments were $97.4M (normalizing to $65-75M in 2026). Free cash flow outlook for 2026 is $114.5-$116.5M after capex and dividends. The common dividend is a token $0.01/quarter ($0.04/year), preserving essentially all free cash flow for debt reduction and buybacks.

Total debt stands at $2.15B: $902M unsecured term loans, $750M convertible notes, $400M unsecured senior notes, and $93M mortgage. The weighted-average interest rate of 4.1% is among the lowest in the sector, with 98% effectively fixed-rate and 98% unsecured. Available liquidity is ~$838M. However, $392.3M in principal plus $92.6M interest comes due by December 31, 2026, creating near-term refinancing requirements in an elevated rate environment.

Net debt/EBITDA improved from 5.9x at year-end 2025 to 5.5x in Q1 2026. While declining, this remains elevated relative to REIT peers and constrains financial flexibility.

**Preferred Stock Overhang:**

Four series of preferred stock carry an aggregate liquidation preference of $676.7M with annual preferred dividends of ~$46.4M. At a current common equity market cap of ~$1.67B, the preferred represents ~40% of total equity claims. This priority claim significantly dilutes the common shareholder's participation in any NAV realization and creates structural leverage that magnifies both upside and downside in the common equity.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Growth Drivers (12-month horizon):**

1. **San Francisco structural recovery (+44.5% RevPAR in Q1 2026):** This is the single most important catalyst. PEB has significant SF exposure, and the market's recovery from extreme post-COVID depression (tech return, convention business rebuilding) has room to continue. SF remains well below 2019 RevPAR levels.

2. **Los Angeles recovery from wildfire disruptions:** Q1 2026 LA RevPAR grew 31.5% as demand returned and displaced travelers filled capacity. The recent Mondrian-to-Valorian rebrand (Hilton Curio Collection) adds distribution power at no capital cost.

3. **Declining new hotel supply:** Industry-wide supply growth is running significantly below historical averages, particularly in PEB's barrier-to-entry coastal markets. Reduced competition for new rooms provides structural pricing power.

4. **Per-share value accretion from buybacks:** 18.8M shares retired since October 2022 (~14% of outstanding) at an average $13.34 — roughly 43% below management's published NAV. If buybacks continue at current discount, each remaining share's claim on portfolio value increases.

5. **Guidance raise and estimate revisions:** Full-year FFO/share guidance raised to $1.60-$1.70 (from $1.50-$1.62). Analyst estimates for the current year have risen 52.1% over 90 days — a strong positive revision signal.

**Growth Constraints:**

- Washington D.C. RevPAR collapsed 24.1% in Q1 2026 due to DOGE/government spending cuts — a direct offset to SF/LA strength
- Industry-wide RevPAR growth expected at only 0-2% for 2026
- Management flagged "shortened visibility since late March" and increased geopolitical/economic risk
- The macro environment directly threatens leisure travel demand in H2 2026
- ADR compression in full-year 2025 (down 2.7% despite occupancy gains) suggests limited pricing power outside recovery markets

**Estimate Revision Assessment:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen 52.1% over 90 days, meeting the threshold for a strong positive revision signal. However, this inflection is off a deeply negative base (from -$0.53 to -$0.26 — still negative). The forward P/E is -18.3x (meaningless given GAAP losses from depreciation). The relevant metric is forward P/FFO at 8.7-9.2x, which is cheap but not dramatically so given the risk profile.

If the true NAV is $18-20/share (assuming 15-20% haircut to management's estimate for impairment risk and market-specific challenges), the current price still represents a 19-27% discount — meaningful but not as extreme as management suggests.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Leverage & Refinancing (HIGH):**
Net debt/EBITDA at 5.5x with $392.3M due by year-end 2026. In the current environment with elevated rates and credit conditions tightening, refinancing may come at materially higher costs. The September 2025 convertible issuance (1.625% coupon) was done at relatively favorable terms; future issuance may not replicate this.

**2. Preferred Stock Structural Subordination (HIGH):**
$677M in preferred equity with $46.4M annual preferred dividends creates a significant priority claim. In a downturn, preferred dividends must be paid before any common recovery occurs. The preferred overhang effectively leverages the common equity's exposure to both upside and downside.

**3. Consumer Demand / Recession Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):**
Upper-upscale hotels ($295 ADR) are discretionary spending targets. Direct macro sensitivity threatens leisure travel demand in H2 2026.

**4. Washington D.C. Deterioration (MODERATE):**
The 24.1% Q1 RevPAR decline in D.C. reflects a structural shift in government travel demand that may persist for years under current fiscal policies.

**5. Impairment History (MODERATE):**
$179M in impairments over 2023-2025 across multiple properties suggests some portfolio assets are worth less than carrying value. Future impairments remain possible, particularly for properties in markets with slow recovery trajectories.

**6. Short Interest (NOTABLE):**
21.9% short interest with 6.8 days to cover represents significant institutional skepticism. While this creates squeeze potential, it also means well-resourced investors are actively positioned for the stock to decline. The fundamental short thesis likely centers on leverage, the preferred overhang, and macro sensitivity of upper-upscale demand.

**7. Natural Disaster Exposure (MODERATE):**
LaPlaya Beach Resort has been hit by multiple hurricanes since 2022. California wildfire risk is recurring. While insured, disruptions impact cash flow and portfolio value.

**Options Market Signal:**

No options data was provided, but the 21.9% short interest and polarized analyst recommendations suggest the options market likely shows elevated implied volatility and put skew, consistent with a binary outcome profile: either the operational inflection sustains and the NAV discount closes, or macro deterioration and leverage compound into significant downside.

## Conclusion

Pebblebrook Hotel Trust presents a specific growth thesis with identifiable catalysts: San Francisco's structural recovery, declining industry supply supporting pricing power, and aggressive buybacks at a deep NAV discount creating per-share value accretion. The Q1 2026 results were genuinely strong, with Hotel EBITDA growing 27.6% and margin expanding 327 bps on operating leverage. Estimate revisions are strongly positive (+52% over 90 days on current-year FFO).

However, the risk profile prevents a higher-conviction recommendation. Leverage at 5.5x with near-term maturities, a $677M preferred overhang that structurally subordinates common equity, a 5-year track record of massive TSR underperformance versus peers (-39% vs. +27%), persistent impairments suggesting NAV optimism, 21.9% short interest from informed institutional investors, and direct macro sensitivity to a consumer showing stress signals collectively constrain upside potential and widen the downside range.

The stock is a HOLD with a positive growth orientation. The 12-month thesis depends on the operational inflection sustaining beyond Q1's seasonal strength and PEB demonstrating consistent deleveraging while the macro environment cooperates. At current levels (near 52-week highs, above consensus mean target), the entry point does not offer sufficient margin of safety given the identified risks to warrant a BUY recommendation.
