**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Boeing is a credible operational turnaround that is roughly 60% complete. The case for owning it rests on a record $695B backlog, the structural duopoly with Airbus, and a sequence of concrete de-risking events over the past nine months: FAA clearance to lift 737 production to 47/month (with a fourth line planned), a ~200-aircraft China commitment that reopens a channel closed for nearly a decade, $6.95B of Q1 2026 debt repayment, and resolution of the DOJ 737 MAX criminal matter via non-prosecution agreement. These developments give management's ~$5B FY2026 free-cash-flow target — a swing of roughly $7B from 2025's -$1.9B — a real basis.

The case against owning it is the balance sheet and the quality of 2025's reported earnings. The GAAP net income of $2.2B was manufactured almost entirely by a $9.57B pre-tax gain on the Digital Aviation Solutions divestiture; strip it out and both manufacturing segments lost money (BCA -$7.1B including a fresh $4.9B 777X reach-forward loss; BDS -$0.1B). Core EPS was $1.19. The Altman Z-Score of 0.83 sits deep in the distress zone, equity is a razor-thin $5.5B against $54B of debt, and the Q1 debt paydown came from divestiture cash and unwound investments, not operating cash flow. Share count has grown from ~606M (2023) to 788M, with another ~33-40M shares due from mandatory preferred conversion by October 2027.

The recommendation is HOLD with a growth rationale. The trajectory of narrowing manufacturing losses, rising deliveries (143 in Q1 vs 130; 60 in May, +33% YoY), and an active deleveraging program supports appreciation toward the low end of the $230-300 analyst target band over 12 months if the 737 ramp converts to cash. The thesis is binary on execution — hitting the ~$5B FCF target and navigating the October 2026 SPEEA engineering-union negotiation — and the financial structure has no margin for a setback. That asymmetry, with limited downside protection from the fragile balance sheet, is why this is a hold rather than a buy.

## Company Overview

Boeing operates three segments. Commercial Airplanes (BCA, ~46% of 2025 revenue, $41.5B) produces the 737, 767, 777/777X, and 787; roughly 85% of BCA's $567B backlog is with non-U.S. airlines. Defense, Space & Security (BDS, ~30%, $27.2B) covers fighters, rotorcraft, the KC-46 tanker, P-8, satellites, and space programs including 50% of United Launch Alliance. Global Services (BGS, ~23%, $20.9B) is the reliable profit engine at ~18% margins, selling aftermarket parts, maintenance, and training off the installed base.

The competitive moat is real: a duopoly in 100+ seat aircraft, the record backlog, and deep aftermarket economics. The moat has been damaged by self-inflicted quality and safety failures since 2019 (the two MAX crashes, the 2024 door-plug blowout), which is why Airbus has gained share. Boeing's binding constraint is supply — production system stability, certification, and quality — not demand. The December 2025 re-acquisition of Spirit AeroSystems ($8.39B) brings fuselage and structures production back in-house to address the root cause of quality escapes, at the cost of ~$10.4B of provisional goodwill and integration risk.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 income statement requires careful reading. Revenue rose 34.5% to $89.5B and Boeing reported $4.3B of operating income and $2.2B of net income, a sharp reversal from 2024's -$10.7B operating and -$11.8B net. But BGS operating earnings jumped to $13.5B from $3.6B almost entirely on the divestiture gain. Excluding it, the consolidated business still operated at a loss. The two manufacturing segments confirm this: BCA lost $7.1B (including the fresh 777X reach-forward charge) and BDS was roughly breakeven at -$0.1B. Core (non-GAAP) EPS was $1.19, and full-year free cash flow was -$1.9B.

The balance sheet is the central risk. Stockholders' equity recovered to a positive $5.5B (from -$3.9B in 2024 and -$17.2B in 2023), but against $54.1B of total debt that produces a reported D/E near 10x and a Z-Score in distress territory. Inventory stands at $84.7B, including ~35 undeliverable 737-7/-10 jets pending certification and rising 777X/787 deferred production costs. A $9.75B deferred-tax valuation allowance and the company's stated "recent history of losses" prevent Boeing from booking future earnings into deferred-tax recovery, a marker of fragility rather than a signal of imminent failure.

Q1 2026 trends are encouraging on trajectory. Revenue rose 14% YoY to $22.2B; deliveries reached 143 versus 130. Debt was cut from $54.1B to $47.2B (a $6.95B repayment) using divestiture proceeds and unwound short-term investments. Cash and short-term investments stood at ~$20.9B, comfortably covering the ~$8.5B of debt maturing within 12 months. The net loss narrowed to -$4M to shareholders (-$90M to common after preferred dividends), and operating cash flow was modestly negative at -$0.2B. The earnings-surprise record reflects this volatility: a 76.7% beat in Q1 2026 ($-0.20 vs $-0.86 estimate) following a -179% miss in Q4 2025 and a -57.9% miss in Q3 2025. Visibility remains low.

The deleveraging so far has been funded by asset sales, not operating cash. The bull case requires the 737 ramp to 47/month and 787 to 8-10/month to actually convert to the ~$5B FY2026 FCF target. The Z-Score will only durably improve when operating cash, not divestiture proceeds, funds debt reduction.

## Growth Analysis

Demand is not the issue. Boeing's 20-year Commercial Market Outlook projects ~43,600 new aircraft (3.1% fleet CAGR), and IATA estimates industry net profits of $39.5B in 2025 rising to ~$41B in 2026, indicating healthy customers able to take delivery. Both single-aisle and wide-body demand exceed supply. The backlog represents years of revenue that will convert as production normalizes.

The growth lever is entirely production rate and certification. The FAA's May 2026 capstone review clearing 737 to 47/month is the most important operational development — each rate break flows disproportionately to cash. The 787 moving from 8 toward 10/month adds widebody cash. The China commitment, described by management as "first of many," reopens a channel that had been closed by tariff disputes since Q2 2025. May 2026 deliveries of 60 (+33% YoY) were the strongest of the year, providing tangible evidence the ramp is real.

The estimate-revision picture is the counterweight, and it is genuinely mixed. Next-year EPS consensus has fallen modestly to $4.18 (from $4.70 ninety days ago, -11.1%), and current-year estimates collapsed to -$0.39 from +$0.38 over 90 days as analysts marked in the 777X and fixed-price losses. These are not the upward revisions that signal a clean earnings inflection. The growth thesis is forward-loaded into 2026-2027 cash conversion rather than near-term EPS, which is why I score Growth Prospects at 6.5 rather than higher — the demand backdrop and rate ramp are strong, but the path runs through programs (777X, fixed-price defense) that keep generating downward revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

Boeing's trailing P/E of 87.8x is not a meaningful metric — it is computed on $2.48 of trough/artifact EPS. The more relevant frame is next-year consensus EPS of $4.18, putting the stock at ~52x forward earnings, and management's FCF target against a $172.6B market cap, which is a ~2.9% FCF yield if achieved. Neither is cheap, and both depend on execution that has repeatedly disappointed.

Against peers, the comparison is awkward because Boeing is the only large aerospace name not generating clean earnings. Peer P/Es: LMT 26.6x, GD 22.6x, NOC 17.3x, RTX 34.6x, HWM 61.4x (median 26.6x). Boeing trades well above the median on any earnings metric while carrying the weakest balance sheet in the group. The appropriate valuation lens is normalized mid-cycle cash earning power: if Boeing reaches a ~$10B annual FCF level (consistent with a stabilized 737, 787 at 10/month, and 777X in service) by 2027-2028, the current price represents ~17x that normalized FCF — reasonable for a duopoly franchise, but two to three years and considerable execution risk away. Analyst targets ($230 low, $275 median, $300 high) imply 5-37% upside, with the low target itself above the current $219. I do not treat these as floors here — Boeing trades at a premium multiple on depressed/forward earnings, not as a cheap inflection name, so the guardrail applies: the targets reflect the same recovery extrapolation as the price and are vulnerable to any program disappointment. I score Valuation at 4.5: the asymmetry is two-sided rather than clearly favorable, with the fragile balance sheet limiting downside protection and the premium multiple already pricing a successful ramp.

## Competitive Landscape

The duopoly structure is intact and Boeing's installed base guarantees decades of aftermarket revenue at attractive margins. The trajectory question is whether Boeing is regaining the share it lost to Airbus. Recent evidence is mixed-to-improving: the 737 rate clearance, the Spirit re-integration removing the supplier quality failure point, and the China reopening all point to stabilization. But Airbus retains a meaningful order-book lead built during Boeing's troubles, and the 777X delay to 2027 (777-8 not before 2030) cedes the high end of the widebody market to the A350 for longer than planned.

On defense, BDS faces intensifying competition from defense consolidation and private-capital-backed non-traditional entrants, alongside budget uncertainty (FY26 funded at $839B; FY27 request $1,450B). The fixed-price legacy contracts (KC-46, T-7A, MQ-25, VC-25B, Starliner) are structural margin drags rather than competitive positioning issues. I score Competitive Position at 6.5 — the moat level is high, the trajectory is stabilizing after years of erosion, but Boeing is recovering ground rather than extending a lead.

## Risk Assessment

**777X — the largest accounting exposure.** A 12-year program with $4.9B of reach-forward losses in 2025 alone (on $3.5B in 2024), first delivery slipped to 2027, and a newly identified engine durability issue still unresolved. Management explicitly warns of further reach-forward losses. Each quarterly re-estimate carries charge risk.

**737 certification and rate ramp.** The 737-7 and -10 remain uncertified with ~35 finished jets in inventory awaiting FAA sign-off. The cleared move to 47/month is the key cash lever, but every increment requires FAA concurrence.

**Fixed-price defense losses.** $5.0B in 2024 and $0.8B in 2025 across legacy contracts; KC-46 took another $714M in 2025. These are recurring and structural.

**Labor — the most concrete near-term risk.** Two strikes in 13 months (IAM 751: 53 days; IAM 837: 101 days), both occurring despite leadership-recommended tentative agreements. The SPEEA engineering union (~16,000 employees) contract expires October 2026. Given the recent pattern, this is the most likely event to derail the 2026 FCF target.

**Credit.** All three agencies rate Boeing at the lowest investment-grade rung (BBB-/Baa3), with outlooks revised to stable in 2025. A downgrade to junk would raise borrowing costs and impair capital-markets access.

**Dilution.** Share count growth, plus the mandatory preferred conversion and Spirit Exchangeable Notes, means per-share recovery must overcome ongoing share-count growth.

**ULA Vulcan anomaly.** Boeing's 50% JV suffered a launch anomaly in Q1 2026; Boeing may have to provide financial support.

The DOJ non-prosecution agreement (approved November 2025, victims' appeal denied March 2026) materially de-risked the equity by removing the threat of a guilty plea and potential federal-contracting debarment. Civil and securities litigation continues (a $49.5M Ethiopian-crash jury verdict in May 2026; a partially certified securities class action), but the existential criminal tail is largely closed. I score Risk Factors at 4.5 — the 777X reach-forward risk, October SPEEA negotiation, and credit-rating fragility are specific, near-term, and not fully priced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm relative to Boeing's fundamental fragility. Near-term ATM IV is 33.9% against 31.8% one-year historical volatility — a 2.1pp spread that prices the stock as fairly, not richly, valued for uncertainty. IV sits in the 20th percentile of its own chain and has fallen from 40.6% in mid-May, indicating the market views recent catalysts (737 clearance, deliveries) as reducing rather than raising uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.8%, the put/call volume ratio is a benign 0.55, and the term structure is flat (33.9% near vs 34.0% far). Short interest is negligible at 0.1% of float. The market is not pricing a binary event in the near term, which both reduces near-term crash risk and means there is little cushion of bearish positioning to squeeze. The longer-dated 6-month skew (-4.8% put skew) is the only structural caution flag, consistent with the October SPEEA risk landing outside the front months.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($300).** The 737 ramp to 47/month and 787 to 10/month convert to cash, delivering the FCF target. The SPEEA contract settles without a strike. The China channel reopens at scale (~200 jets plus follow-ons). 777X stabilizes toward 2027 first delivery without further major charges. Operating cash, not asset sales, begins funding deleveraging, the Z-Score recovers, and a credit-outlook upgrade follows. The stock re-rates toward the $300 high target as the market prices a path to mid-cycle FCF generation.

**Bear case (~$177, -19%).** A SPEEA strike in Q4 2026 (consistent with the prior two negotiations) disrupts production and pushes the FCF target out a year. The 777X engine durability issue triggers another multi-billion reach-forward loss. The 737 ramp stalls at FAA concurrence. Negative operating cash flow forces additional debt or equity issuance, a junk downgrade raises borrowing costs, and the divestiture-funded deleveraging story is exposed as non-recurring. The stock retraces toward the 52-week low of $176.77 and below as the turnaround timeline extends.

The probability-weighted center sits closer to the bull-case mechanics succeeding partially: deliveries and rate clearances are real and tracking, but the 777X and labor risks make a clean print unlikely. This is a holding for investors who can tolerate a binary outcome and have a 12-18 month horizon.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) for FCF-conversion evidence; the October 2026 SPEEA contract resolution; and FY2026 results (early 2027) for the FCF target verification.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY2026 free cash flow tracking to ≥$4B on Q2/Q3 cash generation from operations (not asset sales), confirming the rate ramp converts to cash.
- SPEEA engineering contract settled without a strike, removing the largest near-term operational risk.
- A credit-rating outlook upgrade or BCA returning to segment operating profitability for two consecutive quarters.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A SPEEA strike, or a 777X reach-forward loss exceeding ~$2B in any quarter.
- FY2026 FCF guidance cut below ~$2B, signaling the rate ramp is not converting.
- A credit downgrade to junk, or any equity issuance to fund operations.

The thesis plays out over 12-18 months and is gated entirely on execution. The current price of ~$219 offers ~5% upside to the $230 low analyst target and ~26% to the $275 median, against ~19% downside to the bear case — an asymmetry that is roughly balanced rather than clearly favorable, which is why this is a hold rather than a buy.

## Conclusion

Boeing has done the hard work of removing existential tail risks: the DOJ criminal matter is resolved, the credit outlook has stabilized, the Spirit re-acquisition addresses the root cause of quality escapes, debt is being paid down, and the FAA has cleared the 737 to 47/month. The demand backdrop and backlog are as strong as the franchise has ever seen. Two directors bought stock on the open market in 2026 near the current price, a modest but constructive signal.

What holds this back from a buy is the gap between the operational improvement and the financial reality. The 2025 profit was a divestiture artifact, free cash flow was negative, the balance sheet is in distress territory on a Z-Score basis, the deleveraging so far was funded by asset sales rather than operations, and per-share recovery must outrun continued dilution. The October SPEEA negotiation and recurring 777X charges are concrete risks the calm options market is not pricing. This is a credible turnaround in progress, worth holding for investors who can underwrite a binary 12-18 month execution outcome, but it does not clear the bar for a high-conviction long until operating cash flow begins funding the recovery. **HOLD.**
