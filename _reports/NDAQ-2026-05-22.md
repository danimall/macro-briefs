## Executive Summary

Nasdaq has executed a strategic transformation from exchange operator to diversified financial technology platform. The $10.5B Adenza acquisition is now delivering $160M+ in synergies, the Index business has compounded AUM from $315B to $882B in three years, and Financial Technology ARR is growing 14% annually. FY2025 delivered 60% GAAP EPS growth, 30% operating income growth, and $2B in free cash flow. Q1 2026 beat consensus with 14% revenue growth. The company serves all G-SIBs and 2,750+ financial institutions with mission-critical compliance and trading technology.

Three specific risks prevent a BUY recommendation at current levels. First, U.S. exchange market share has declined every year for three consecutive years (options: 31.0% → 29.1%; cash equities: 16.5% → 14.2%), and competition from CBOE, MEMX, MIAX, and the new TXSE is intensifying. Second, the SEC access fee rule — Nasdaq's legal challenge was denied in October 2025 — takes effect November 2026 and will structurally reduce the company's ability to monetize order flow through rebates. Third, $9B in debt (1.54x D/E) against a backdrop of 30Y yields above 5% means refinancing risk is real, even though current debt is fixed-rate. These are dated, quantifiable headwinds with specific timelines that the current ~26x forward multiple does not adequately discount.

## Company Overview

Nasdaq operates through three segments: Capital Access Platforms ($2.14B revenue), Financial Technology ($1.85B), and Market Services ($4.21B gross / $1.20B net). The company has deliberately shifted its revenue mix toward recurring technology subscriptions, with ~65%+ of net revenue now recurring/subscription-based. It operates 19 exchanges across North America and the Nordics/Baltics, provides market infrastructure technology to 135+ marketplaces in 55+ countries, and its Verafin anti-financial-crime platform serves 2,750+ North American financial institutions.

The Adenza acquisition (closed November 2023) added AxiomSL (regulatory reporting across 170+ regulators) and Calypso (cross-asset trading/risk platform), giving Nasdaq a comprehensive financial technology stack serving the entire capital markets value chain. Integration was completed by December 31, 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is strong and accelerating.** Net revenue (ex-transaction expenses) grew 12.9% in FY2025 to $5.25B, with all three segments contributing. Q1 2026 showed acceleration to 14% YoY growth. The Financial Technology segment led at 14% ARR growth ($1.71B ARR), while the Index business grew 17.1% to $827M on $882B in AUM.

**Operating leverage is materializing.** Operating expenses grew only 2.3% vs. 11.6% revenue growth in FY2025, driving operating income up 29.7% to $2.33B. This reflects both Adenza synergies and inherent scalability in the Index and technology licensing businesses.

**Free cash flow is robust.** FCF of $1.99B (24.1% of revenue) supports both deleveraging and capital return. The company repaid $400M in maturing notes and repurchased $444M in additional debt in FY2025, while increasing dividends 11.7% and share repurchases by $471M.

**Balance sheet concerns are real but manageable.** Total debt of $9.0B at a weighted average rate of ~3.7% (all fixed) creates no near-term refinancing stress — but $431M in short-term debt and upcoming maturities in a 5%+ rate environment will increase interest expense upon refinancing. The $14.4B goodwill (46% of assets) is concentrated in Financial Technology ($7.9B from Adenza) and requires sustained double-digit FinTech growth to justify carrying value.

**Key financial metrics:**
- Operating margin on net revenue: ~44.4%
- FCF yield (at ~$89 share price, ~580M shares): ~3.9%
- Non-GAAP EPS: $3.48 (FY2025), implying ~25.6x trailing at $89
- Dividend yield: ~1.2% ($1.05/share)
- Net debt/EBITDA: ~3.0x (declining)

## Growth Analysis

Nasdaq has multiple compounding growth vectors:

**Index business (17% growth, $827M revenue):** The $882B AUM franchise scales automatically with market appreciation and passive investing flows. Record $99B in net inflows during 2025. This is an asset-light, high-margin annuity that requires minimal incremental investment.

**Financial Technology ARR (14% growth, $1.71B):** Cross-selling across 3,800 clients (including all G-SIBs) provides a long runway. Verafin's expansion into larger banks, AxiomSL benefiting from regulatory complexity growth, and Calypso's front-to-back platform each have independent growth drivers.

**Extended trading hours (H2 2026):** 23-hour trading on the Nasdaq Stock Market, pending regulatory approval, could capture international order flow and generate additional data/connectivity revenue. This is incremental rather than transformational.

**AI integration:** Financial crime detection, surveillance, and regulatory technology are natural AI applications. Nasdaq's 20+ years of ML experience in these domains provides competitive advantage, though execution risk exists.

**Countervailing factor:** Market Services organic growth depends on volume (growing) but share (shrinking). The 2-3pp annual share decline in both options and cash equities is structural, driven by fragmentation and new entrants. Revenue growth from this segment requires volume increases to persistently exceed share losses.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$89/share with non-GAAP EPS of $3.48 (FY2025), Nasdaq trades at ~25.6x trailing non-GAAP earnings. Assuming 12-15% EPS growth in FY2026 (driven by ARR growth, operating leverage, and share repurchases), the forward multiple is ~22-23x FY2026E earnings.

This is a reasonable multiple for a company growing revenue 12-14% with improving margins and a high-quality recurring revenue mix. Peers in the exchange/financial infrastructure space (CME, ICE, CBOE) trade at 22-30x forward earnings, with CME at ~24x and ICE at ~23x. Nasdaq's multiple is neither cheap nor expensive relative to peers, though its growth rate is modestly higher due to the FinTech transformation.

The specific valuation concern: the SEC access fee rule (November 2026) will create a quantifiable revenue headwind in Market Services that consensus estimates may not fully reflect. If access fee reductions cost $50-100M in annual net revenue (reasonable estimate based on current fee structures), that represents 1-2% of net revenue — enough to pressure near-term earnings growth in a segment that already faces share erosion.

At ~3.9% FCF yield, the stock offers modest but not compelling free cash flow return when risk-free rates are above 4.5%.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths improving:** The FinTech platform (Verafin + AxiomSL + Calypso) creates a comprehensive technology ecosystem with high switching costs once embedded in client workflows. Cross-sell opportunity across 3,800 clients is differentiated vs. point-solution competitors. The Index franchise is a near-monopoly on Nasdaq-100 licensing with powerful network effects.

**Position deteriorating:** Exchange market share decline in cash equities and options over FY2023-FY2025 reflects structural fragmentation. CBOE is gaining in options, MEMX in equities, and TXSE represents a new competitive front. Each percentage point of share loss matters less as total volumes grow, but the trend creates a persistent revenue headwind that technology revenue must offset.

**Net assessment:** Competitive position is stable to slightly improving on a consolidated basis, as FinTech growth more than offsets Market Services share loss. But the score trajectory decline from 8.0 to 7.0 reflects the reality that the exchange franchise — still generating $1.2B in net revenue — faces intensifying structural pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**SEC Access Fee Rule (November 2026):** Implementation will reduce Nasdaq's ability to pay rebates to attract liquidity, potentially accelerating market share loss. Management has acknowledged it "may adversely impact our business and revenue." The magnitude is uncertain but likely $50-100M in annual impact.

**Market share trajectory:** Three consecutive years of decline in both options and cash equities. If volume growth decelerates (e.g., in a recession scenario), share losses would no longer be offset by volume increases.

**Leverage in a rising-rate environment:** As maturities come due (next material tranche after the $431M in current portion), refinancing will occur at 5%+ rates, increasing annual interest expense by $50-100M over 3-5 years. This is a headwind to earnings growth rather than a solvency concern.

**Goodwill impairment:** $14.4B in goodwill requires sustained growth to justify. If FinTech ARR growth decelerates below 10%, qualitative goodwill tests may require quantitative analysis, and impairment charges could materially impact GAAP results.

**AWS single-vendor dependency:** $1.5B in purchase commitments with AWS for cloud migration of exchange infrastructure. October 2025 AWS outage already affected certain Nasdaq offerings. Mission-critical exchange infrastructure on a single cloud provider is a concentration risk without near-term precedent.

### Options Market Signal

P/C OI ratio collapsed from 2.07 to 0.17 between April 13 and May 6, while the stock appreciated 3.5% and ATM IV remained essentially flat at ~30.6%. The dramatic reduction in put open interest relative to calls suggests protective positioning has been removed. This is modestly bullish for near-term sentiment but also means the stock lacks accumulated short interest to fuel further upside. IV at 30.6% is moderate, suggesting neither complacency nor acute concern.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$100-105, +12-18%):** Adenza synergies exceed initial targets, FinTech ARR accelerates to 15%+, Index AUM continues compounding on passive flow trends, extended trading hours drives incremental revenue, and SEC access fee impacts are more manageable than feared. Non-GAAP EPS reaches $4.00+ in FY2026, and the stock re-rates to 26x forward = $104.

**Bear Case (~$72-75, -16-19%):** SEC access fee rule accelerates market share losses, volume growth stalls in economic downturn, FinTech cross-sell underperforms, and refinancing $2-3B of debt at 5.5%+ adds $75-100M in annual interest expense. EPS growth stalls at $3.60-3.70 and the multiple compresses to 20x = $72-74.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $80-82 (forward P/E compresses to ~20x FY2026E), providing 15%+ upside to base case
2. FinTech ARR growth accelerates to 16%+ for two consecutive quarters, validating cross-sell thesis beyond current expectations
3. SEC access fee rule implementation reveals more limited revenue impact than feared (< $50M annually), removing the primary near-term headwind

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. U.S. options matched market share falls below 27% (from current 29.1%), indicating competitive deterioration is accelerating
2. FinTech ARR growth decelerates below 10%, threatening goodwill carrying value
3. Debt refinancing occurs at spread materially above current levels (>200bp) without corresponding EBITDA growth

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely July 2026) will provide first post-SEC-rule-announcement quarter of Market Services data and updated FinTech ARR trends. November 2026 access fee implementation is the key catalyst to monitor.

## Conclusion

Nasdaq is a well-managed company executing a genuine strategic transformation with strong financial results. The growth vectors — FinTech ARR, Index AUM compounding, and AI-driven product expansion — are real and durable. However, the current valuation (~25.6x trailing non-GAAP, ~22-23x forward) prices in continued execution without adequately discounting three specific, dated headwinds: structural exchange market share erosion, the SEC access fee rule (November 2026), and rising refinancing costs as debt matures into a 5%+ rate environment. At $80-82 (a 10% pullback), the risk/reward becomes compelling for a BUY. At current levels, the stock is fairly valued for its quality and growth profile, making HOLD the appropriate stance for new capital allocation.
