## Executive Summary

Coterra Energy is in the final hours of its independent existence. The all-stock merger with Devon Energy (0.70 DVN shares per CTRA share) received shareholder approval on May 4, 2026, and closes May 7, 2026. At CTRA's last traded price of $35.63 (May 5), the implied DVN value is $50.90 per share (= $35.63 / 0.70). With DVN trading in the low-$50s range, the merger arbitrage spread has compressed to approximately 1-3%, well below the threshold that would justify a new position entry given the one-day holding period remaining.

Coterra's standalone fundamentals are strong: $2.35B guided FCF at strip prices, a sub-1.0x net debt/EBITDAX balance sheet, and successful integration of the $4B FME/Avant Delaware Basin acquisitions. These merits are now fully embedded in a combined entity story. The 41% trailing-year price appreciation has closed the valuation gap that previously justified a BUY thesis.

The appropriate recommendation is HOLD for existing shareholders, who will receive DVN shares at close and should evaluate the combined entity on its own merits (higher leverage, integration risk, Kimmeridge activist pressure, but also $1B+ synergy potential and structural oil/gas tailwinds). For new capital, there is insufficient upside in the 1-2 days remaining before the security ceases trading to justify initiating a position.

## Company Overview

Coterra Energy operates across three U.S. basins: the Delaware Basin (Permian, 46% of production), Marcellus Shale (44%), and Anadarko Basin (10%). The company was formed in 2021 through the Cabot Oil & Gas / Cimarex Energy merger and significantly expanded its Permian footprint through the 2025 acquisitions of Franklin Mountain Energy ($2.5B) and Avant Natural Resources ($1.5B).

Total 2025 production reached 782.4 MBoepd, with oil production of 159.9 MBbl/day (up 47% YoY from the Delaware acquisitions). The company holds 2,565 MMBoe in proved reserves with only 17% classified as PUD, indicating conservative booking practices.

On February 1, 2026, Coterra entered a definitive merger agreement with Devon Energy. The combined entity will be the largest U.S. independent shale producer, with Devon shareholders owning ~54% and Coterra shareholders ~46%.

## Competitive Landscape

The combined Devon-Coterra creates the largest U.S. independent shale producer with dominant positions in the Delaware Basin, Marcellus, Williston, and Eagle Ford. Competitive advantages include:

- **Scale economics** in the Delaware Basin (largest contiguous acreage position)
- **Gas optionality** from Marcellus at premium pipeline corridors (Transco Zone 6, Leidy)
- **Multi-basin diversification** reducing single-basin concentration risk
- **Operational DNA** demonstrated by Coterra's FME/Avant integration success (40% flare intensity reduction, 74% large emissions reduction)

The competitive position is strong but largely priced into the combined entity valuation. The $1B synergy target is achievable given the acreage overlap and headquarters rationalization, but represents an incremental catalyst rather than a transformative re-rating.

The Devon merger, closing May 7, 2026, creates the largest U.S. independent shale producer with legitimate synergy potential and structural tailwinds from the Iran conflict's impact on oil prices and rising natural gas demand.

The investment case at $35.63 is entirely about the combined Devon entity. **Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing shareholders should hold through close and reassess the combined DVN entity after Q2 2026 earnings.
