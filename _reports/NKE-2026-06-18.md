**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nike is roughly eighteen months into a turnaround under returning CEO Elliott Hill, and the share price reflects deep skepticism that it will work. The stock sits near $44, down about 26% over the trailing twelve months and approximately 45% below its 52-week high of $80, trading near a 12-year low. The fundamentals justify caution: revenue fell 10% in FY25 to $46.3B, net income dropped 44% to $3.22B, gross margin compressed to 42.7% (and to 40.2% in the most recent quarter), and operating cash flow collapsed from $7.4B in FY24 to $3.7B in FY25. Greater China is contracting, Converse has turned EBIT-negative, and tariffs added roughly 270bps of North America product cost in Q3.

The prior three reports issued AVOID at progressively lower scores as the price fell. That call captured the decline. The question now is forward-looking, and several signals cut against extending a bearish thesis at $44. Estimate revisions have stabilized over the trailing 30 days (current-year EPS flat at $1.50 vs. the -5.9% 90-day move, current-quarter +1.0% on 30 days) after sharp 90-day cuts; the deterioration has decelerated. The sell-side consensus distribution is migrating out of HOLD into BUY (strongBuy moved 2→5, buy 15→19, hold 19→13 over recent periods; sentiment labeled "improving"). The company has beaten EPS estimates four straight quarters, including by 78% and 39%. A cluster of the most informed insiders — CEO Hill (two $1M open-market buys), director Tim Cook (~$4M across two purchases), and ex-Intel CEO Robert Swan — bought with cash as the stock fell to its lows.

The balance sheet is not distressed: $8.1B in cash and short-term investments, investment-grade ratings (now A+/A2 after downgrades), a current ratio of 2.2, and a prudently paused buyback that preserves liquidity. Nike is a leader losing share and working through a cyclical/transitional margin trough, not a structurally broken franchise facing solvency stress. That combination of real near-term drag, real stabilization signals, insider conviction, and a non-distressed balance sheet describes a balanced situation. We move to HOLD (uncertain) from AVOID. The evidence no longer supports an active bearish call at a price that has already bottomed and begun recovering, but the trough is not yet clearly behind, which precludes a BUY.

## Company Overview

Nike is the world's largest seller of athletic footwear and apparel, distributing through NIKE Direct (owned stores and digital, ~42% of brand revenue) and wholesale (~58%). It reports four geographic segments (North America, EMEA, Greater China, Asia Pacific & Latin America), plus Converse and Global Brand Divisions. Product mix is approximately 64% footwear, 28% apparel, 5% equipment. Jordan Brand, a major sub-brand, generated about $7.3B in FY25, down 16%.

The moat historically rested on brand, scale, athlete endorsements ($16.2B in contractual endorsement obligations), and product innovation. Elliott Hill, a 32-year Nike veteran, returned as CEO in October 2024 to replace John Donahoe, whose DTC-first strategy and over-reliance on retro franchises (Air Force 1, Dunk, Jordan) left an innovation gap. Hill's strategy reverses course: re-engaging wholesale partners Nike had abandoned, repositioning digital as a full-price platform, and accelerating new product. CFO is Matthew Friend; Mark Parker is Executive Chairman.

## Financial Analysis

Nike is in an earnings recession. The annual trajectory (fiscal year ends May 31):

| Metric | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $46.7B | $51.2B | $51.4B | $46.3B (-10%) |
| Gross margin | 46.0% | 43.5% | 44.6% | 42.7% |
| Net income | $6.05B | $5.07B | $5.70B | $3.22B (-44%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | $3.23 | $3.73 | $2.16 |
| EBIT margin | — | 12.1% | 12.7% | 8.2% |
| ROIC | — | — | 34.9% | 20.2% |

Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended Feb 28, 2026): revenue $11.28B (flat reported, -3% currency-neutral), net income $520M (-35% YoY), EPS $0.35, gross margin 40.2% (-130bps). Nine-month FY26: revenue $35.4B (+1% reported), net income $2.04B (-32%), gross margin 41.0% (-250bps).

The margin story has several moving parts. Tariffs are now the dominant driver: Nike has paid approximately $1.0B in IEEPA tariffs cumulatively. The deliberate channel re-shift is working on revenue but is margin-dilutive near term: North America wholesale grew +11% in Q3 and +15% over nine months as Nike re-entered abandoned accounts, while digital fell 9% in Q3 and 12% over nine months as Nike repositioned it for full price. The mix shift toward lower-margin wholesale compresses gross margin even as the top line stabilizes.

Cash flow deteriorated sharply. Nine-month FY26 operating cash was just $1.23B versus $3.24B prior year, hurt by rising receivables (wholesale growth) and falling payables. The balance sheet remains investment-grade but is weakening: cash plus short-term investments down from $9.2B, long-term debt around $8B. The headline-scary metrics — D/E of 2.77, negative retained earnings of -$610M, an Altman Z of 2.42 in the "grey zone" — are largely artifacts of years of aggressive buybacks exceeding cumulative earnings and depressed cyclical EBIT, not operating losses or solvency risk.

Capital allocation has pivoted defensively. Nike bought only $122M of stock over nine months versus $2.8B a year earlier, while maintaining and modestly raising the dividend (~$0.41/quarter, ~3.6% yield). Pausing buybacks to preserve cash through the trough is the correct decision and a credible signal of management discipline.

## Growth Analysis

Forward growth is the weakest part of the thesis, but it is stabilizing rather than collapsing. Next-year EPS is $1.84, down 22.4% over 90 days but essentially flat (-0.4%) over 30 days. The current-quarter estimate was cut 46.5% over 90 days but is up 1.0% over the last 30. The pattern across every line is the same: heavy 90-day cuts that have decelerated to flat-to-slightly-positive over 30 days. This is consistent with estimates bottoming, not continuing to deteriorate. The four consecutive EPS beats — +22.9%, +39.2%, +78.1%, +7.5% — show management is clearing (admittedly lowered) bars consistently, which raises confidence in the current consensus floor.

The turnaround is real but back-end loaded. The wholesale rebound is the clearest evidence Hill's strategy is gaining traction, and the digital decline is intentional rather than pure demand loss. Management targets completing the cost-and-channel realignment by end of December 2026, with China lagging into FY2027. Converse, once a reliable contributor, is now a drag — revenue fell 37% currency-neutral in Q3 and the brand turned EBIT-negative (-$40M vs. +$39M prior year) amid a strategic reset. Greater China is the structural overhang: revenue down 10% currency-neutral in Q3, EBIT down 20% over nine months, with management explicitly warning of weakness through fiscal 2027 driven by declining traffic, heavy promotion, channel inventory glut, and local-brand competition from Anta and Li-Ning.

Underwriting this name requires patience. The growth inflection is plausibly two to four quarters out, and China is a multi-year repair. The mechanism for recovery is identifiable: tariff mitigation (sourcing shifts, selective pricing) plus the wholesale revenue base re-establishing plus Converse and China stabilizing. None of these is guaranteed on a near-term timeline.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing EPS of $2.16, Nike trades at ~20x; on depressed forward EPS of $1.50, ~29x. Neither is the right lens. The relevant question is normalized earnings power. Nike earned $3.73 in FY24 and $3.23 in FY23 at EBIT margins of 12-13%. If Hill's turnaround restores EBIT margins toward 11-12% (below the historical peak, accounting for permanent tariff drag and a lower-margin channel mix), normalized EPS of roughly $3.00-3.50 is defensible. At $44, that implies 13-15x normalized earnings, undemanding for a brand that, even diminished, remains the category leader with $8B+ liquidity and a ~3.6% dividend.

Peer context is mixed. Tapestry trades at 46.8x, Ralph Lauren at 26.0x (peer median), and Lululemon at 9.8x (itself deeply de-rated, -54% from its high). Nike's ~29x forward on trough earnings sits above the median, but that comparison is distorted by the cyclical trough; on normalized earnings Nike screens cheaper than the median. The dividend yield of 3.6% is well above the peer median of 1.1%, providing absolute support.

The analyst target range is wide (low $23, high $120, mean $59.6, median $55), reflecting genuine disagreement about whether the turnaround works. RBC downgraded to Sector Perform with a $50 PT in June 2026, citing a slower-than-expected turnaround. The median target of $55 implies ~25% upside from $44.

I am scoring valuation at 5.5, modestly above neutral. There is genuine cycle-adjusted upside, and the price has already absorbed a 26% decline. But a high valuation score requires a clean catalyst the market is missing, and here the catalyst (margin recovery) is real but its timing is uncertain and partly outside management's control (China, tariff litigation). Cheapness on normalized earnings is supportive but not, by itself, a reason to chase, given the broader caution warranted around discretionary names whose recovery depends on consumer and macro variables.

## Competitive Landscape

Nike is a leader losing share. Footwear unit volumes fell 8% in FY25. The brand is ceding ground to On (ONON) and Deckers/HOKA (DECK) in performance running and to a resurgent Adidas in lifestyle. The 10-K concedes "a reduction in barriers to starting new footwear companies" and nimbler competitors. This deteriorating trajectory matters more than the still-dominant absolute position.

The offsetting consideration is that the share loss has identifiable causes that management is addressing: the prior DTC-first strategy alienated wholesale partners (now being re-engaged with measurable success), and the innovation gap from over-reliance on retro franchises is being closed with new product. Whether Nike re-accelerates innovation faster than On and HOKA compound their gains is the central competitive question, and it is genuinely unresolved. The brand, scale, and $16.2B endorsement book remain formidable assets that a challenger cannot replicate quickly. I score competitive position 4.5 — a deteriorating trajectory from a strong base, with a credible but unproven repair underway.

## Risk Assessment

The principal risks are margin and demand, not solvency. Specific overhangs:

- **Tariffs** are the largest active margin headwind and a genuine two-sided wildcard. The U.S. Supreme Court ruled (Feb 20, 2026) that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized but did not address refunds. Nike books no recovery (deemed not probable), creating ~$1B of asymmetric upside if refunds materialize. Simultaneously, consumers sued Nike (May 8, 2026) for not refunding tariff costs passed through as higher prices. Net effect is uncertain, but the conservative accounting means a refund would be pure upside.
- **Greater China** structural decline persists into FY2027 — the longest-dated and least controllable headwind.
- **Converse** is in freefall and may warrant divestiture or deeper write-down.
- **Credit downgrades** (S&P AA-→A+, Moody's A1→A2) raise borrowing costs at the margin.
- **Legal/tax overhangs**: Belgian customs duty claims, a Netherlands EU State Aid investigation, securities class-action and derivative suits tied to the stock collapse, and an IRS audit with ~$969M of gross unrecognized tax benefits.
- **Restructuring execution**: $304M in nine-month severance charges plus an ERP rollout in EMEA create operational and control risk during transition.

Much of this is now priced. Short interest is at 5.4% of float (2.4 days to cover), and the bear narrative (share loss, China, tariffs) is well-circulated. The asymmetry has shifted: the downside scenarios are widely understood, while the tariff-refund optionality and a faster-than-expected margin recovery are upside that the consensus is not crediting.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is balanced-to-mildly-constructive, contradicting an active bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 35.4% versus 38.5% one-year historical vol — implied vol is cheap (spread -3.1%, 7th percentile within the current chain), not pricing elevated downside. Put/call skew is balanced at -2.3%, and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.36. The put/call OI ratio collapsed from 1.54 to 0.84 over the prior month while the price rose 7.1%; bearish positioning has been actively unwinding. The term structure is in normal contango (near 35.4% < long 42.5%), indicating no acute near-term fear. Unusual activity around the $44 strike is roughly balanced between calls and puts. None of this confirms a bearish setup; if anything, it argues the worst-case positioning has cleared.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Hill's turnaround takes hold on the targeted timeline. The wholesale rebound re-establishes a durable revenue base; tariffs are mitigated through sourcing and selective pricing; China stabilizes through FY2027; and Converse's reset stops the bleeding. EBIT margins recover toward 11-12%, normalized EPS reaches $3.00-3.50, and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median. The tariff-refund optionality (~$1B booked at zero) provides upside. Insider buying by the CEO, Tim Cook, and Robert Swan proves prescient. Stock toward $65-80 over 18-24 months (analyst high $120 is a tail scenario assuming full normalization plus refund).

### Bear Case

China weakness deepens beyond FY2027, On and HOKA continue compounding share gains in running, and the wholesale rebound proves to be margin-destructive volume rather than profitable growth. Tariff costs persist without refunds while consumer litigation creates reputational drag. Margins stall at 40-41% rather than recovering, normalized EPS settles closer to $2.00-2.25, and the multiple compresses on a "structurally diminished brand" narrative. Stock toward $33-36 (RBC's $50 PT sits between the cases). The decline to date suggests the market is already partway down this path.

The honest read is that these scenarios are roughly balanced, with the bull case requiring patience and the bear case requiring the turnaround to fail outright. At $44, the price reflects substantial pessimism, which limits incremental downside relative to a clean BUY/AVOID asymmetry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as uncertain — the evidence does not support either a confident BUY or an active bearish call. Next natural reassessment is Q4 FY2026 results due June 30, 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or expands sequentially for two consecutive quarters (Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27), signaling the tariff/channel-mix drag has been absorbed.
- Greater China revenue returns to currency-neutral flat-to-positive growth ahead of the FY2027 guidance.
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimates resume net upward revisions over 30- and 90-day windows (currently stabilized, not yet rising).
- A favorable resolution of the tariff-refund question that converts the ~$1B off-balance-sheet optionality into a booked receivable.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q4 FY26 gross margin falls below 39% with management guiding further compression, indicating the margin trough is not yet in.
- Wholesale revenue growth reverses or proves persistently dilutive to consolidated operating margin.
- The dividend coverage deteriorates such that a cut becomes plausible (operating cash flow falling materially below dividend plus minimum capex).
- 30-day estimate revisions turn sharply negative again, reversing the recent stabilization.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q4 FY26 (June 30, 2026), then Q1 FY27 (late September 2026). The turnaround is explicitly a multi-quarter story with China lagging into FY2027; do not expect resolution before two to three earnings cycles.

## Conclusion

Nike has been a correct AVOID through a 26% decline, but the conditions that justified that call have shifted. The estimate cuts that drove the bearish trajectory have decelerated to flat over 30 days; the analyst consensus is migrating out of HOLD into BUY; the company has beaten four consecutive quarters; the options market shows bearish positioning unwinding; and the most informed insiders — the CEO and two prominent directors — have bought with cash at these levels. The balance sheet is investment-grade with $8B+ liquidity and a prudently paused buyback, so this is a margin and demand problem, not a solvency problem. On normalized earnings of $3.00-3.50, the stock trades at 13-15x, undemanding for the category leader, even a diminished one.

What prevents a BUY is equally clear. The turnaround is back-end loaded, China is impaired into FY2027, Converse is a drag, gross margin is still compressing, and the share-loss trajectory to On and HOKA remains unbroken. The bull and bear scenarios are genuinely balanced. At $44, near a 12-year low and recovering off the $41.35 floor, the bearish thesis is substantially in the price, but the catalysts for a confident upgrade have not yet printed. HOLD, with explicit triggers to reassess at the next two earnings reports, is the defensible call. We would turn constructive on two quarters of margin stabilization or a tariff-refund resolution, and turn negative if Q4 margins break below 39% with guidance for further compression.
