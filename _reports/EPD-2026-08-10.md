## Executive Summary

Enterprise Products reported the strongest quarter in its history in Q2 2026 — revenue $18,269M (+60.8% YoY), segment gross operating margin $3,004M (+21.6%), GAAP diluted EPS $0.84 versus a $0.74 consensus. The composition matters more than the headline. WTI averaged $92.71 in Q2 2026 against $63.87 a year earlier, and reading the segment detail in the 10-Q, a meaningful share of the improvement is commodity marketing margin rather than fee income: natural gas marketing supplied +$201M of the Natural Gas segment's +$278M 1H26 GOM increase, and in Crude Oil the Q2 improvement of +$69M in Texas pipelines & marketing came with transportation revenue **down $19M** on lower average tariffs. Management, per the earnings-call summary, quantified roughly $200M of one-time uplift from an April–May demand and differentials spike and said those cash differentials "have largely normalized." Strip that and normalized Q2 EBITDA is ~$2.6B, or ~$10.4B annualized versus ~$11.2B on the reported figure — an 8% gap that is the single most consequential input to any 2026-based valuation.

Underneath the commodity noise, the operating base is genuinely growing: NGL pipeline throughput 4,913 MBPD in Q2 versus 4,562, fractionation 1,858 versus 1,667 MBPD, marine terminal volumes +33%, Permian gas processing inlet +14% YoY to 4.3 Bcf/d. But the earnings translation of that volume growth is being taxed by rate. EHT LPG gross operating margin fell $135M in FY2025 with $123M of that from lower average loading fees; 1H26 lost a further $42M on LPG and $16M on ethane loading fees, and combined Morgan's Point + Neches River + EHT GOM was **−$8M for the half despite record export volumes**. EPD is adding ~300 MBPD of LPG capacity (Ref 4, end-2026) plus Neches River phase 2 into exactly that rate environment. Simultaneously, the 2026 organic capex guide was raised from $3.1–3.5B (February 10-K) to $4.1–4.6B (August 10-Q), a 32% increase at midpoints, with the identified project backlog extended from $4.8B through 2027 to ~$6.5B through Q1 2029. The free-cash-flow inflection that looked imminent six months ago has been deferred into a new build cycle.

That leaves a fair-value security with a good income profile. At $37.75, EPD trades at 12.8x the $2.96 current-year consensus, 12.0x the $3.15 next-year consensus, ~11.0x EV/TTM EBITDA proxy and ~10.7x EV on normalized 2026 EBITDA of ~$10.8B. The $2.24 annualized distribution yields 5.93% and is covered 1.83x on operational DCF, funded by A-/A3/A- credit at a 4.7% weighted-average cost with only $450M due in 2H26 and $1,575M in 2027 against $27.2B after 2030. Consensus mean target is $41.20 (range $37–$46), roughly 9% above spot. Probability-weighting our scenarios gives ~4% expected price return plus the 5.9% distribution — a market-like total return with limited asymmetry, no identified catalyst, and a commodity tailwind that reverses if the crude strength proves geopolitical rather than structural. **HOLD for income.**

## Company Overview

EPD is a Delaware limited partnership operating substantially all of its business through Enterprise Products Operating LLC, the debt issuer. Two structural features distinguish it: the general partner interest is **non-economic** with no incentive distribution rights, and the partnership has **no employees** — roughly 8,000 EPCO personnel perform all functions under an administrative services agreement costing $840M in 1H26.

Four segments, with FY2025 gross operating margin:

| Segment | FY2025 GOM | FY2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| NGL Pipelines & Services | $5,559M | ~$5,559M | ~flat |
| Crude Oil Pipelines & Services | $1,501M | ~$1,646M | −8.8% |
| Natural Gas Pipelines & Services | $1,558M | $1,277M | +22% |
| Petrochemical & Refined Products | $1,436M | $1,548M | −7.2% |
| **Total** | **$10,030M** | **$9,984M** | **+0.5%** |

The asset base is the moat. Mont Belvieu couples 1,279 MBPD of net fractionation to 169.5 MMBbls of salt-dome storage and to three separate water outlets — Enterprise Hydrocarbons Terminal (eight deep-water docks, 45-ft draft, ~835 MBPD LPG loading), Morgan's Point (ethane), and Neches River. The 10-K states EHT's waterfront "enables us to handle larger vessels than our competitors because our waterfront has fewer draft and beam restrictions," which is not replicable. Within-corridor optionality is also real: in January 2026 EPD converted a 440-mile Midland-to-Sealy segment of the Seminole NGL system to crude service (+225 MBPD), moved displaced NGL volumes onto the new Bahia pipeline, and retained the right to convert back.

Revenue is a poor lens on this business — FY2025 revenue fell 6.4% to $52,596M while segment GOM rose 0.5%, because roughly −$7.9B of price effect was offset by +$3.1B of volume. Gross operating margin and EBITDA are the operative measures.

## Financial Analysis

**2026 first-half results.** Segment GOM $5,646M versus $4,935M (+14.4%), operating income $4,144M versus $3,556M, net income to common $3,322M versus $2,828M, diluted EPS $1.52 versus $1.29. Against FY2025's +0.5% GOM year, this is a step change in level. The decomposition, however, splits roughly between real volume-driven fee growth in NGLs (gas processing & NGL marketing +$171M in Q2, pipelines/storage/terminals +$25M, fractionation +$52M) and price-driven marketing margin in gas and crude.

**Cash flow.** The gap between EPD's framing and a strict free-cash-flow view is wide and both readings are defensible.

FY2025, reproducible from the cash-flow statement: OCF $8,585M less total capital investments $5,620M (growth $4,393M, sustaining $595M, acquisitions $632M) = $2,965M, against distributions $4,752M and buybacks $300M — a **$2,087M shortfall** funded with debt, which rose from $30,746M to $32,770M. EPD's own metric deducts only sustaining capex: FY2025 DCF of $8,000M against $4,752M of distributions is 1.7x coverage with $3,248M "retained."

1H26 closed the gap: OCF $4,650M plus $599M of Bahia sale proceeds, less capex $2,141M, less distributions/DERs/NCI $2,446M, less buybacks $275M = **+$387M surplus**, with total debt principal down $1,175M to $33,532M. Two caveats prevent extrapolation. The $599M asset sale is non-recurring. And Q2 OCF included a **+$666M favorable working-capital swing** — receivables up $2,904M and inventories up $754M over 1H26, offset by accrued product payables up $3,468M. That is commodity-price-driven balance-sheet inflation, and it reverses when prices fall. Q2 OCF of $3,181M should not be annualized. Stripping the asset sale and the working-capital benefit leaves 1H26 near breakeven on discretionary cash flow, consistent with management's own full-year figure of "approaching about $1 billion" rather than a structural inflection.

**Balance sheet.** This is the strongest part of the case. At 6/30/26: debt principal $33,532M, cash $246M ($345M with restricted), total equity $31,064M, working capital **−$1,247M** (from +$528M at year-end). Debt is **98.3% fixed-rate at a 4.7% weighted-average cost with ~16.8-year weighted-average maturity**, rated A-/A3/A-. Maturities: $450M in 2H26 (commercial paper), $1,575M 2027, $1,800M 2028, $1,250M 2029, $1,250M 2030, **$27,207M thereafter**. There are no interest-rate derivatives. Fair value of fixed-rate debt is $30.2B against $32.5B carrying — the low-coupon long stack trades ~7% below par, a real economic benefit in a higher-rate environment. Liquidity was $4.0B at quarter-end, lifted to ~$5.2B of revolver capacity by a $1.0B incremental facility added in July 2026 explicitly to "support our working capital requirements amid increased commodity price volatility."

**Leverage.** Building a TTM EBITDA proxy from GAAP inputs (FY2025 operating income $7,266M + D&A/accretion $2,551M = $9,817M; 2H25 = $5,017M; 1H26 $4,144M + $1,387M = $5,531M) gives **$10,548M**. Net debt/proxy = 3.16x, or 3.05x giving 50% equity credit to the $2,282M of junior subordinated notes — inside management's 2.75–3.25x target. This proxy excludes equity-method and other adjustments EPD includes in reported Adjusted EBITDA and should be treated as approximate.

Two items leverage screens will miss: June 2026 compressor-lease modifications capitalized ~$345M of operating and ~$177M of finance lease liabilities (~$522M of new fixed obligations), and the screening Altman Z-Score of ~1.75 is an artifact of deliberately minimal working capital and $52.2B of net PP&E, contradicted directly by the ratings, maturity ladder, and 3.05–3.16x leverage.

**Interest expense optics.** Reported interest rose from $672M (1H25) to $769M (1H26), +14.4%. Capitalized interest fell from $98M to $47M, so gross interest incurred rose only $46M (+6%) while period-end debt declined — average balances were higher after the FY2025 debt build. As the $6.5B program progresses, capitalized interest should rise again and temporarily flatter reported interest, reversing on completion.

**Earnings visibility.** Two beats and two misses over four quarters (+13.2%, −5.2%, +7.3%, −10.1%) argues for modest confidence in point estimates. Estimate revisions are stable, not inflecting: current-year consensus +2.8% over 90 days to $2.96, next year +0.8% to $3.15. This is nowhere near the revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection.

## Growth Analysis

**Volume growth is real and verifiable.** NGL pipeline net throughput 4,646 MBPD in 2025 versus 4,426 and 4,116; Q2 2026 at 4,913 versus 4,562. Fractionation 1,858 versus 1,667 MBPD. Morgan's Point ethane loading 232 MBPD (2025) versus 213 and 198. Natural gas pipeline utilization improved to 70.4% from 65.7% and 65.0%. Permian gas processing inlet +14% YoY to 4.3 Bcf/d.

**Earnings translation is weaker than volume growth implies.** Three mechanisms:

1. *Export fee compression.* EHT LPG loading was flat at 697 MBPD (2025) versus 702 (2024) against ~835 MBPD capacity — ~83% utilization. Fees fell $123M in FY2025 and a further $58M across LPG and ethane in 1H26. Combined terminal GOM was −$8M for the half despite record volumes. Ref 4 adds ~300 MBPD by end-2026, roughly a 36% increase in EPD's own LPG capacity, into a market where management concedes new supply "has pushed down listed LPG shipping rates" and expects fees "lower and less volatile." Management's mitigant — ~90% of system-wide LPG export capacity contracted — is meaningful, but contracted is not the same as loaded at prior rates.

2. *Declining processing utilization against an aggressive build.* Weighted-average gas processing utilization has fallen three consecutive years (69.6% → 68.4% → 67.8%) while EPD sanctions Athena 2, Midland Plant 11, Delaware Plants 12 and 13, plus Mentone West train 2. Fractionation utilization dropped below nameplate for the first time in three years (99.6% versus ~106%) after Frac 14 started, with Frac 15 (150 MBPD) sanctioned for Q1 2028. The filings justify each on long-term acreage dedications and, for Delaware Plant 12, minimum volume commitments. Whether these are demand-pulled or ahead of demand will be visible in 2028–2029 utilization, not now.

3. *Tariff reset risk.* Management is in active discussions on certain pipelines "such as ATEX, where current tariffs may exceed product value." ATEX is the 1,210-mile Appalachia-to-Mont Belvieu ethane line; a downward reset is a permanent fee reduction on a wholly owned asset. Separately, FERC's five-year liquids index period expired June 30, 2026, making the next Index Level determination an open variable.

**Contracted revenue.** Remaining Performance Obligations were $25,935M at 6/30/26 ($2,344M 2H26, $4,538M 2027, $4,009M 2028, declining thereafter, $9,545M after 2030). The 10-Q scopes RPO to fixed consideration under contracts with minimum volume commitments or deficiency provisions exceeding one year — it is not total backlog, and deferred revenue of $440M is not a proxy either.

**Demand-side structural drivers** the filings support without embellishment: the Acadian system's Gillis Lateral delivers Haynesville gas to South Louisiana LNG, and gas pipeline utilization improved 470bp. The AI-datacenter power-demand framing is directionally consistent with rising gas throughput but is not separately quantified in the filings; Permian volume growth is the verifiable driver.

**Growth arithmetic.** Consensus takes EPS from $2.96 to $3.15 (+6.4%), and 1H26 distributions declared were $1.11 versus $1.08 (+2.8%). A 5.9% yield growing ~3% is a ~9% total return before multiple change. That is a sound income proposition and a modest growth proposition.

## Valuation Assessment

Working from the frozen $37.75 basis:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Equity value (2,159.5M units) | $81.5B |
| Enterprise value | ~$115.6B |
| Trailing P/E ($2.657 EPS) | 14.2x |

| Distribution yield ($2.24) | 5.93% |
| Consensus target mean / range | $41.20 / $37–$46 |

Three observations shape the valuation score.

**First, the correct earnings base is normalized, not reported.** Annualizing Q2's ~$2.8B reported EBITDA gives ~$11.2B and 10.3x EV/EBITDA, which looks attractive. Removing the ~$200M differential uplift, and recognizing that Q2's WTI average of $92.71 sits well above any reasonable mid-cycle assumption, gives ~$10.4–10.8B and 10.7–11.1x. That is not a distressed multiple for a midstream partnership; it is at the upper end of where EPD has historically traded.

**Second, the market is not obviously wrong about anything specific.** A high valuation score requires an identifiable mispricing. Here the sell side sits at 3 strong buy, 8 buy, 9 hold, 1 sell — genuinely split — with a mean target 9% above spot and a low target of $37, essentially at the market. Estimate revisions are flat. Neither the bull nor the bear side has an informational edge visible in the data. The 6% discount to the 52-week high of $40.165 is not a margin of safety.

**Third, the commodity tailwind is a valuation risk, not support.** Q2's marketing margins were earned in an elevated crude environment. If crude reverts toward the $80s or below, gas and crude marketing margins compress toward the FY2025 baseline where total segment GOM grew 0.5%. The crude marketing derivative portfolio's fair value already moved from +$102M (12/31/25) to −$65M (6/30/26) to −$149M (7/15/26), and $122M of hedging losses were reclassified into 1H26 revenue versus $53M of gains in 1H25 — the arithmetic cost of hedging into a rally, which suppresses reported marketing margin if prices stay elevated and reverses if they fall.

Set against that, the absolute yield provides genuine support. A 5.93% distribution covered 1.83x on operational DCF, funded by A- credit with essentially no near-term refinancing need, establishes a floor that limits downside in the absence of a distribution problem. There is no distribution problem in view.

Scenario framework:

- **Bear $31.50 (−16.6%):** crude reverts to the low $70s, marketing margins normalize to FY2025 levels, LPG fees compress further as Ref 4 and third-party capacity land, ATEX resets down, 2026 EBITDA settles near $10.0B. Roughly 9.5x EV/EBITDA and a 7.1% yield — near the 52-week low of $30.01.
- **Base $40.50 (+7.3%):** normalized 2026 EBITDA $10.8B, 2027 near $11.3B on new assets, distribution grows ~3% to ~$2.30, multiple holds at ~11x EV/EBITDA. Cross-check: 12.9x the $3.15 FY27 consensus = $40.60. Total return with distribution ~13%.
- **Bull $46.00 (+21.9%):** crude stays elevated through 2027, Permian volumes sustain 14% growth, LPG fees stabilize as contracted capacity fills, 2027 capex steps down to ~$3B and discretionary FCF exceeds $2B enabling accelerated buybacks, EBITDA reaches ~$11.8B at 11.5x. Matches the high analyst target.

Probability-weighted at 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear: ~+4.2% price plus 5.9% distribution ≈ **10% total return**. Positive, but the dispersion is wide relative to the reward and the bear case is a −17% price move on a plausible macro reversal.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's competition disclosure is unusually candid that competition is fee-based in nearly every line — NGL pipelines "in terms of transportation fees," fractionation "primarily based on the fractionation fee charged," storage "primarily in terms of the fees charged," export terminals "primarily in terms of loading and offloading throughput capacity and access to related pipeline and storage infrastructure." Competitors include integrated majors, chemical companies, other midstreamers, and "financial institutions with commodity trading platforms" in the marketing lines.

EPD's advantages are asset-structural rather than contractual: the Mont Belvieu fractionation-plus-storage complex feeding three water outlets, the unrestricted Houston Ship Channel waterfront, and within-corridor pipeline convertibility. A competitor must replicate several links to compete on any one, which should make EPD the low-cost survivor and a volume-share gainer through the current LPG dock capacity cycle.

The problem is that volume share gains at compressing rates produce less GOM than the volume statistics suggest, which is exactly what the 1H26 −$8M combined terminal GOM demonstrates. The marginal dock is being built simultaneously by EPD and by third parties, and rate is the clearing variable. Two plausible readings of EPD's own capacity additions coexist and cannot be distinguished from the disclosure: either the underwriting assumptions of two years ago are no longer achievable on Ref 4's incremental margin, or EPD deliberately traded rate for term and volume to lock utilization ahead of the competitive wave. We do not infer management intent from the fee trajectory.

On the supply side, the filings name two demand-destruction mechanisms specific to EPD: ethane rejection, which shrinks transport, fractionation, storage and marketing volumes simultaneously when processing economics turn; and Waha basis volatility with uncertain timing on shut-in volumes returning. The 10-K also discloses that "nearly all of the transportation revenues generated by our San Juan Gathering System are based on a percentage of a regional natural gas price index" — commodity linkage inside what presents as a fee business. Operationally, a TRIR of 0.36 in 2025 compares favorably to the seven-year midstream average.

## Risk Assessment

**Export fee compression (highest-conviction, currently realizing).** Quantified in three separate places across two filings: FY2025 −$123M of loading fees at EHT, 1H26 −$42M LPG and −$16M ethane. EPD is adding ~300 MBPD by end-2026 into that environment. This is the most specific unpriced risk in the name.

**Capex re-acceleration.** The February guide of $3.1–3.5B became $4.1–4.6B in August, +32% at midpoints, with backlog extended from $4.8B through 2027 to ~$6.5B through Q1 2029. Sanctions announced April–July 2026 include Athena 2, Midland Plant 11, Delaware Plants 12 and 13, Frac 15, the Bahia expansion, and the EHT export expansion. Two implications: the 2026 self-funding milestone is deferred, and 2H26 capex must accelerate sharply from 1H26's $2,141M to reach even the low end of guidance, which will consume the 1H26 surplus.

**Commodity marketing dependence.** With ~$200M of Q2 uplift already characterized as normalized, and marketing supplying the majority of the Natural Gas segment's 1H26 GOM growth, a reversion in crude and gas prices removes a large part of 2026's earnings improvement. If the crude strength proves geopolitical rather than structural, EPD's marketing margins compress toward the FY2025 baseline. This cuts both ways and is not priced in either direction.

**Governance.** Unitholders do not elect directors. Removing the general partner requires a 60% vote. Any holder of 20%+ loses voting rights on those units. EPD uses NYSE limited-partnership exemptions from majority-independent-board, compensation-committee and nominating-committee requirements, and the most recent DEF 14A is dated October 2022 — so no say-on-pay, pay-versus-performance or CEO-compensation disclosure is available. EPCO and affiliates hold 32.5% of common units, of which 59,976,464 units (2.8% of total units) are pledged under EPCO's separate credit facilities, with the 10-Q flagging that a default and foreclosure could change ownership and affect the unit price.

**CEO succession and control concentration.** Co-CEO Jim Teague retires effective January 4, 2027, with Co-CEO Randall Fowler assuming his responsibilities (8-K Item 5.02 dated 2026-07-01; the filing text was not retrievable and the date rests on press reports). Fowler is simultaneously one of three voting trustees of the Dan Duncan LLC voting trust that controls the general partner, so sole-CEO Fowler concentrates operating authority and control trusteeship more than the prior arrangement. Teague has been the commercial architect of the export and Permian buildout.

**Potential change of tax classification.** The FY2025 10-K risk factors state the general partner's board "may determine it is in our unitholders' best interest to change our classification as a partnership for federal income tax purposes," subject to unitholder approval, citing the shrinking MLP sector and absence of a historic valuation premium. The current effective tax rate is ~1.0%. A conversion would introduce entity-level federal tax while potentially broadening the investor base — genuinely two-sided, and disclosed as a live consideration rather than an announced plan. We could not establish whether the language is new or longstanding.

**Working capital.** Swung $1.8B adversely in six months to −$1,247M as accrued product payables rose to $11,683M on higher prices. This is price-linked balance-sheet expansion rather than overdue obligations, and management's July addition of $1.0B of incremental revolver capacity is the appropriate mitigant — and an acknowledgment that the exposure is live.

**Unaccrued environmental matters.** Three open items — a June 2019 EPA NOV on Baton Rouge facilities, an August 2022 EPA NOV on gasoline standards at two Texas terminals, and 2024–2025 New Mexico enforcement over emission exceedances at the acquired Piñon Midstream assets covering largely pre-acquisition periods. No litigation accruals at either 6/30/26 or 12/31/25.

**Guarantor structure.** Guaranteed Debt was $34.1B at 6/30/26 while the Obligor Group's investments in Non-Obligor Subsidiaries totaled $55.2B, and excluding equity in Non-Obligor earnings the Obligor Group recorded a net loss of $369M in 1H26. Standard supplemental disclosure for an investment-grade issuer; a structural consideration for creditors rather than an equity alarm.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing complacency, not risk. Near-term ATM IV is 15.0% against 17.3% one-year realized (spread −2.4%, fair-to-slightly-cheap), with IV in the 1st percentile of the current chain and a ±1.3% implied move on the nearest expiry. Put/call open interest is **0.13** — heavily call-tilted and among the most one-sided readings we see — with a 0.50 put/call volume ratio and balanced skew (−1.6%). Term structure is in contango (15.0% near versus 21.4% at six months), and the six-month tenor carries the only negative skew (−4.3%), so what hedging exists sits in the December expiry rather than around the near-term. Short interest is 2.3% of float at 6.0 days to cover.

Read together: no one is paying up for near-term protection, and positioning is mildly bullish. That corroborates a HOLD rather than challenging it — there is no options-implied dislocation to exploit, and cheap near-term vol means the market assigns low probability to a shock in the next month. The six-month skew is the one place where someone is paying for downside, which lines up with the timing of Ref 4 coming online and the 2027 capex/ATEX-reset decisions.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $46.00 (+21.9%)

The volume growth is real and independently verifiable: NGL pipeline throughput +7.7%, fractionation +11.5%, marine terminals +33%, Permian inlet +14%. If those volumes persist, the ~$6.5B backlog through Q1 2029 converts into EBITDA of ~$11.8B by 2027–2028, and the fee base grows past the point where LPG rate compression matters. Management's ~90% contracted position on LPG export capacity means the rate reset is bounded and largely absorbed. Capex steps down to ~$3B in 2027, discretionary FCF exceeds $2B, and the remaining ~$3.3B of buyback authorization (~4% of market cap) shifts from anti-dilutive to accretive. The balance sheet is a genuine differentiator in this rate environment: 98.3% fixed at 4.7% with 16.8-year average maturity, debt trading 7% below par, and only ~$2.0B due through 2027 in a higher-rate environment. Zero ATM issuance in 1H26 and zero equity funding of a $4B+ capex program is the discipline the MLP sector spent a decade failing to demonstrate. A tax reclassification, if pursued, could add index eligibility and a materially broader institutional buyer base. Total return with the distribution approaches 28%.

### Bear Case — $31.50 (−16.6%)

Q2 2026 was a trading quarter dressed as an operating quarter. Marketing margin supplied +$201M of the Natural Gas segment's +$278M 1H26 gain and +$52M of Crude Oil's +$69M Texas improvement, while crude transportation revenue fell $19M on lower tariffs. If crude reverts to the low $70s, that margin evaporates and the operating picture reverts to FY2025's +0.5% GOM. Simultaneously, the fee base is under structural rate pressure: LPG loading fees down two years running, ~83% dock utilization with 36% more capacity coming, gas processing utilization down three consecutive years into four new Permian trains, fractionation below nameplate for the first time in three years with Frac 15 sanctioned, and ATEX tariffs above product value with a reset under discussion. Growth capex was raised 32% in six months, so the FCF inflection is deferred and 2H26 cash flow will absorb a sharp capex acceleration just as the $666M working-capital tailwind reverses. Distribution growth of 2.8% is modest, and the buyback shrank the unit count by only 0.106% despite $275M spent because ~5.0M units of phantom vesting offset two-thirds of it. Governance offers no remedy: no director elections, no independent-board requirement, no proxy since 2022, and the Chairman and Vice Chairman received larger February 2026 equity grants (500,000 and 350,000 phantom units, ~$18M and ~$13M) than either Co-CEO.

### Where we come out

The bear case is more specific than the bull case, but it requires a macro reversal in crude prices, and it does not threaten the distribution — 1.83x operational DCF coverage absorbs a 45% EBITDA decline before coverage reaches 1.0x. The bull case requires rate stabilization in exactly the market where the disclosed evidence points the other way. Fair value is close to spot.

## Conclusion

EPD remains one of the highest-quality balance sheets in North American midstream — 98.3% fixed-rate debt at a 4.7% weighted-average cost with a 16.8-year average maturity, $27.2B of the stack not due until after 2030, A-/A3/A- ratings, and a distribution covered 1.83x by operational DCF and funded without a dollar of ATM equity issuance in 1H26. In a higher-rate regime where financing-heavy infrastructure issuers are being penalized, that liability structure is worth more than the market typically credits.

What the current price does not offer is asymmetry. Roughly 12.0x next-year consensus and ~10.7x EV on normalized EBITDA is a fair multiple for a business whose FY2025 gross operating margin grew 0.5%, whose 2026 acceleration is substantially commodity marketing margin earned at a $92.71 WTI average that management says has already normalized, and whose most specific fee line — LPG export loading — has now compressed for two consecutive reporting periods while 36% more of its own dock capacity comes online. Add a growth capex guide raised 32% in six months, a buyback that shrank the unit count by 0.106% while spending $275M, distribution growth of 2.8%, and a governance structure that gives unitholders no vote on directors and has not filed a proxy since 2022, and the case for paying up 6% below the 52-week high is thin.

Own it for the 5.93% yield and the credit quality. Add on weakness toward $34, or on two quarters of evidence that export loading fees have stopped falling.
