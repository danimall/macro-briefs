## Executive Summary

Hologic, Inc. completed its take-private acquisition by a Blackstone and TPG-led consortium on April 7-8, 2026. Common shares received $76.00 in cash and were subsequently delisted from Nasdaq. The transaction valued the company at approximately $18.3 billion in enterprise value, representing a 46% premium to the undisturbed share price on May 23, 2025. Shareholders also received a non-tradeable contingent value right (CVR) worth up to $3.00 per share, contingent on Breast Health segment revenue targets in FY2026 and FY2027.

This report serves as a final assessment of Hologic as a public equity and provides context for CVR holders evaluating the residual financial interest. The stock is no longer investable. Any remaining analytical value lies in understanding the CVR risk profile and what the transaction structure reveals about the PE sponsors' view of the business.

## Company Overview

Hologic operated across four segments: Diagnostics (~44% of revenue), Breast Health (~36%), GYN Surgical (~17%), and Skeletal Health (~3%). The company held market-leading positions in digital mammography (3D Dimensions system), molecular diagnostics (Panther platform for STI/HPV testing), and minimally invasive GYN surgical devices (MyoSure, NovaSure). Headquartered in Marlborough, Massachusetts with approximately 7,070 employees, Hologic generated $4.1 billion in FY2025 revenue.

The company's profile was a textbook private equity target: predictable free cash flow (~$1B annually), low capital intensity ($58.8M CapEx), market-leading positions in defensible niches, recurring revenue streams from service and consumables, and a stock trading at a relative discount (pre-deal P/S ~3.5x) due to post-COVID revenue normalization.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** Revenue declined 17.1% from FY2022 ($4.86B) to FY2023 ($4.03B) as COVID testing revenue evaporated. The core business stabilized in FY2024 ($4.03B) and grew modestly in FY2025 ($4.10B, +1.7%). Q1 FY2026 revenue was $1.048B (+2.5% YoY), with organic Diagnostics growth of only 1.2% excluding COVID.

**Margins:** Gross margins compressed from 61.6% (FY2022, inflated by COVID testing) to 51.6% (FY2025). Q1 FY2026 adjusted gross margin was 60.1%, down 150 bps YoY due to tariff impacts. Operating margins followed a similar pattern: 33.7% (FY2022) → 17.4% (FY2025).

**Free Cash Flow:** FY2025 FCF of approximately $1.0B on $4.1B revenue represents a 24.4% FCF margin. CapEx requirements are minimal at $58.8M, making this an asset-light business with strong cash conversion.

**Balance Sheet:** Pre-close, the company held $2.17B in cash against $2.5B in long-term debt, producing a net leverage ratio of approximately 0.3x. Current ratio was 3.75x. Goodwill and intangibles totaled $4.2B (47% of total assets), reflecting the company's acquisition-driven history.

**Capital Return:** Share count declined from 254M (FY2022) to 226M (FY2025), an 11% reduction through buybacks at prices well below the eventual $76 deal price.

**Earnings Track Record:** The most recent four quarters showed two beats and two misses, including a 6.4% miss in Q1 FY2026 ($1.04 actual vs. $1.11 consensus). This inconsistency likely contributed to the stock trading at a discount prior to the deal announcement.

## Growth Analysis

Hologic's organic growth profile, stripped of COVID windfall, was low single digits. Core Diagnostics growth of 1.2% and Breast Health capital sales declining 2.2% (due to longer hospital procurement cycles) suggest a mature business. The GYN Surgical segment showed the most promise with the Gynesonics acquisition expanding the addressable market.

Estimate revisions prior to the deal were modestly negative: current year EPS estimates fell 2.2% over 90 days, and next year estimates declined 5.7%. The 30-day revisions turned slightly positive (+0.8% to +1.4%), but this largely reflects post-deal mechanical adjustments rather than fundamental improvement.

The PE sponsors likely see growth acceleration through: (1) unencumbered R&D investment without quarterly EPS pressure, (2) bolt-on M&A in fragmented women's health segments, (3) international expansion (constant-currency international growth was -1% in Q1 FY2026, suggesting room for operational improvement), and (4) the FDA-approved Aptima HPV primary screening assay expanding the cervical cancer testing franchise.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $76 deal price, the transaction valued Hologic at:
- ~4.5x trailing revenue
- ~18x trailing free cash flow
- ~25x trailing operating income (FY2025)
- ~15.7x forward P/E (based on $4.46 current-year consensus EPS, adjusted for full-year run-rate)

For a medtech company with low single-digit organic growth, this represents fair-to-full valuation. The 46% premium to undisturbed price reflected the gap between intrinsic value (defensible franchise, strong FCF) and the depressed public market multiple (post-COVID disappointment, inconsistent earnings execution).

The current stock price of $76.01 (frozen at the cash tender offer price) leaves no public equity opportunity. The only residual value exposure is through the CVR.

## Competitive Landscape

Hologic maintained leadership positions across its core markets:

- **Mammography:** Dominant installed base globally in 3D digital mammography. Competitors include GE HealthCare and Siemens Healthineers, but switching costs are high given radiologist workflow familiarity and proprietary image processing.
- **Molecular Diagnostics:** The Panther platform competes with Roche Cobas, Abbott Alinity, and BD MAX. Hologic's advantage is the broad menu of women's health-focused assays and high-throughput capability.
- **GYN Surgical:** MyoSure and NovaSure face competitive pressure from newer entrants, but established clinical evidence and physician familiarity provide a meaningful moat.

The narrow women's health focus was both a strength (deep domain expertise, limited overlap with diversified device companies) and a limitation (smaller TAM, dependence on niche markets that can saturate).

## Risk Assessment

For CVR holders, relevant risks include:

1. **Brevera Recall Impact on CVR:** The voluntary recall of Brevera 9-Gauge Needles (4.7% of Breast Health revenue) creates direct risk to meeting the Breast Health revenue targets that determine CVR payout. Timeline for resolution is uncertain.

2. **Tariff Headwinds:** The 150 bps gross margin impact from tariffs in Q1 FY2026, combined with the current elevated tariff environment, could pressure the new management team's ability to invest in Breast Health growth initiatives.

3. **Capital Equipment Sales Cycles:** Longer hospital procurement cycles for mammography systems could suppress Breast Health revenue growth below CVR targets.

4. **New Management Execution:** José Almeida (former Baxter International CEO) brings operational credibility but faces a transition period during which strategic decisions may be deferred.

5. **No Liquidity:** CVRs are non-tradeable, meaning holders cannot exit the position before the revenue measurement periods conclude.

The 99.8% shareholder approval vote and the narrow analyst target range ($76-$78) confirm the market viewed $76 as fair value for the equity, with the CVR representing speculative upside.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (CVR Value: $2-3/share)
Breast Health revenue targets are achievable if: (a) Brevera recall is resolved quickly with limited market share loss, (b) capital equipment replacement cycles accelerate under PE ownership's willingness to discount/bundle, (c) new CEO drives operational efficiency that funds commercial investment, and (d) international growth recovers from the -1% constant-currency base. The sovereign wealth co-investors (ADIA, GIC) suggest multi-year patience with growth investment.

### Bear Case (CVR Value: $0-1/share)
Breast Health revenue targets are missed if: (a) Brevera recall creates lasting physician distrust or competitive displacement, (b) hospital capital budgets remain constrained, (c) tariff headwinds worsen under evolving trade policy, or (d) management transition creates a strategic execution gap during the measurement period.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**This stock is no longer publicly tradeable.** The analysis applies only to CVR holders.

**CVR measurement periods:** FY2026 and FY2027 Breast Health revenue (fiscal years ending September 2026 and September 2027).

**Upgrade triggers (for CVR optimism):** Brevera recall resolution with full return to market within 60 days; evidence of Breast Health revenue acceleration in Q2-Q3 FY2026; capital equipment cycle improvement signaled by competitor results.

**Downgrade triggers (for CVR pessimism):** Extended Brevera recall; management commentary indicating Breast Health restructuring; hospital capital budget freezes reported across the sector.

**Reassessment timeline:** No public reporting will be available unless the CVR trustee provides periodic updates. Former shareholders should monitor for any public disclosures related to CVR milestones.

## Conclusion

Hologic is no longer a publicly investable security following the Blackstone/TPG take-private at $76/share. The transaction closed April 7-8, 2026, and shares have been delisted. The business remains a high-quality women's health franchise with strong free cash flow generation, market-leading positions, and defensible recurring revenue, but these attributes now accrue to private equity owners.

For former shareholders, the only remaining financial exposure is through the non-tradeable CVR (up to $3/share tied to Breast Health revenue). Given the Brevera recall, tariff headwinds, and management transition, achieving maximum CVR payout faces meaningful execution risk but is not improbable.

Current recommendation: HOLD (the CVR, as there is no ability to trade it). This reflects an "uncertain" classification rather than active conviction in either direction.
