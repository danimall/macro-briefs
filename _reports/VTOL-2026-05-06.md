## Executive Summary

Bristow Group is the world's leading publicly-traded offshore helicopter operator, with a compelling structural thesis centered on tight supply/demand dynamics, 50% of offshore energy services (OES) contracts repricing at higher rates in 2026, and a government services margin inflection as costly contract transitions (IRCG, UKSAR2G) conclude. The company is guiding to $295-$325M in Adjusted EBITDA for FY2026, representing approximately 26-39% growth over FY2025's $233.7M.

However, the investment case has deteriorated since our prior BUY recommendation. Two consecutive quarterly EPS misses (Q4 FY2025: -31.7%, Q1 FY2026: -56.5%), analyst estimates falling 15% in the past 30 days, significant CEO selling ($4.5M in Feb-March 2026), and the stock's 65% rally from May 2025 lows to $48.90 have collectively compressed the risk/reward ratio. At $48.90, upside to our base case target of $58 is approximately 19%, while downside to our bear case of $35-38 represents 22-28% — roughly a 0.7:1 ratio that does not meet the threshold for a BUY recommendation.

The structural thesis remains intact, and the catalysts (OES repricing, government margin normalization, UKSAR2G completion) should materialize in H2 2026. We downgrade to HOLD and would re-enter as a BUY at $42-44, where the risk/reward rebalances favorably.

## Company Overview

Bristow Group operates 214 aircraft (196 helicopters, 14 fixed wing, 4 UAS) across 15 countries under 12 Air Operator Certificates. Revenue segments:

- **Offshore Energy Services (66%, $990M FY2025):** Personnel transport to/from offshore oil & gas platforms, primarily production-focused (less cyclical than exploration). Geographic exposure spans Europe (UK, Norway), Americas (U.S. Gulf, Brazil, Trinidad, Suriname), and Africa (Nigeria).
- **Government Services (26%, $379M):** Long-duration SAR and emergency response contracts including UKSAR2G (10-year, UK), Irish Coast Guard (~€670M, 10-year), Netherlands, and U.S. BSEE.
- **Other Services (8%, $121M):** Regional airline (Airnorth, Australia), helicopter dry-leasing, Humberside Airport.

The top 10 customers represent approximately 63% of revenue, with the UK SAR contract alone comprising ~19%. This concentration is partially mitigated by the long-term nature and government-backed stability of the SAR contracts.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability Trajectory:**

Revenue has grown from $1,185M (FY2022) to $1,491M (FY2025), a CAGR of approximately 8%. Q1 FY2026 revenue of $388.7M (+10.9% YoY) beat consensus despite the EPS miss. Management is guiding FY2026 revenue to $1.58-$1.69B.

Net income has inflected from -$15.8M (FY2022) to +$129.1M (FY2025), with operating income growing 19.8% YoY to $158.8M. OES operating margins expanded from 14% to 17%.

**The Earnings Miss Problem:**

The two consecutive EPS misses require careful disaggregation:

- **Q4 FY2025 (Dec 2025 quarter): EPS $0.61 vs. $0.89 estimate (-31.7%).** This quarter included elevated government services transition costs (IRCG final bases going live in February) and FX headwinds as GBP weakened against USD.
- **Q1 FY2026 (Mar 2026 quarter): EPS $0.44 vs. $1.01 estimate (-56.5%).** Drivers: elevated depreciation (+$6M sequentially from new OES aircraft entering service), $2.8M loss on debt extinguishment from January refinancing, $4.6M FX loss (vs. prior gains), and seasonal weakness in Other Services.

Adjusted EBITDA in Q1 FY2026 was $59.3M — roughly flat with Q4's $60.1M and not showing fundamental deterioration. Revenue beat. The misses are below-the-line items (non-cash depreciation, one-time refinancing charges, FX). However, these are still real economic outcomes that demonstrate earnings quality concerns and forecasting difficulty — the pattern cannot be dismissed as entirely noise.

**Balance Sheet:**

The January 2026 refinancing extended maturities significantly: $500M Senior Secured Notes at 6.750% due 2033 replaced 6.875% Notes due 2028. No material maturities before 2031. Cash of $342M (March 2026) provides adequate liquidity. Net debt of $432M on $310M midpoint EBITDA guidance implies 1.4x net leverage — comfortable for a capital-intensive aviation business.

**Cash Flow:**

Operating cash flow was $198.4M in FY2025, with capex of $142M (down from $255M in the prior year's heavy investment period). FCF was $56.4M. LTM FCF through Q1 2026 is approximately $173M, though Q1 itself was -$8.3M due to seasonal working capital.

## Growth Analysis

**Three-Pronged Growth Thesis:**

1. **OES Contract Repricing (2026-2027):** Approximately 50% of OES contracts are renewing in 2026 into a supply-constrained market. Effective utilization for medium/heavy offshore helicopters is near 100%, with 24+ month delivery lead times for new builds. Leading-edge rates are significantly above legacy contract levels. This is the highest-visibility growth driver and is partially industry-structural rather than Bristow-specific.

2. **Government Services Margin Normalization (H2 2026):** Government Services operating margin collapsed to just 1% in FY2025 due to IRCG and UKSAR2G transition costs (subcontractor costs +$28.2M YoY). The company reported a 76% YoY decline in Government Services operating income despite 15% revenue growth. The IRCG contract went fully operational in February 2026. UKSAR2G is completing transition by late 2026. As subcontractor costs subside, this segment should contribute materially higher operating income.

3. **Activity Recovery (H2 2026):** Rystad Energy data (April 2026) indicates global floating rig demand picking up in H2 2026 after a mid-cycle lull.

**Against the growth thesis:**

- Analyst estimates have been falling: current-year EPS revised down 14.7% in the past 30 days from $6.37 to $5.43 (only 2 covering analysts).
- The growth inflection is always "next half" — the same narrative was present before both recent misses.
- The COO Government Services retirement (Alan Corbett, April 2026) during the UKSAR2G transition creates execution risk on the highest-visibility growth driver.

- **Trailing P/E ≈ 11.3x** (on $4.32 TTM EPS)

Relative to peers (median P/E 54.3x), VTOL trades at a steep discount. However, the peer set is highly heterogeneous — comparing an offshore helicopter operator to Helix Energy (103.9x) or RPC (54.3x) is analytically weak. The relevant comparison is historical VTOL multiples and the absolute level of cash flow generation per dollar invested.

On an EV/EBITDA basis, 5.6x for a company guiding to 26-39% EBITDA growth is objectively inexpensive. If the $295-$325M EBITDA guidance is achieved and the market applies 6-7x (a modest premium to current), fair value is:
- Low end: $295M × 6x = $1.77B EV → subtract $432M net debt → $1.34B equity / 29.6M shares = **$45/share**
- High end: $325M × 7x = $2.28B EV → subtract $432M → $1.84B / 29.6M = **$62/share**
- Midpoint: **$53-54/share**

The analyst consensus median target of $60 implies roughly 7x on the high end of guidance — achievable but requiring execution confirmation.

**Price sensitivity:** At $48.90, you are paying roughly 6.2x the midpoint of unconfirmed guidance that has yet to be validated by quarterly results. The stock has already re-rated 65% from May 2025 lows ($26.53), pricing in much of the inflection thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

Bristow's competitive position is strong and improving:

1. **Supply constraints are structural:** Helicopter OEMs are production-constrained with military orders absorbing capacity. No meaningful new supply entering the offshore market for 24+ months.
2. **Fleet modernization:** S76D transition (13 aircraft being replaced by AW189s by early 2027) improves fleet economics and customer alignment.
3. **Barriers to entry:** High regulatory barriers (12 AOCs across 15 countries), safety requirements, and specialized infrastructure make competitive entry difficult.
4. **Contract structure improving:** New contracts include more stringent cancellation provisions and better risk allocation for operators.
5. **Scale advantage:** As the largest publicly-traded offshore helicopter operator, Bristow can serve global IOC customers across multiple basins with a single relationship.

The primary competitive risk is the S92 fleet (61 aircraft, 29% of fleet) experiencing parts delivery delays that have caused aircraft groundings and lost business opportunities. This is an OEM supply chain issue, not a Bristow-specific failure, but it directly constrains revenue recognition.

## Risk Assessment

**Key Risks (weighted by probability × impact):**

1. **Execution risk on H2 2026 margin inflection (HIGH).** The COO Government Services retired in April 2026, precisely during the UKSAR2G transition that underpins the margin normalization thesis. If the transition encounters additional delays or cost overruns, the catalyst timeline extends further. Two consecutive misses demonstrate forecasting difficulty even with management guidance affirmed.

2. **Oil price sensitivity (MODERATE-HIGH).** 66% of revenue depends on offshore energy activity. Below $50-60/bbl sustained, customer budgets would face pressure. The company notes a potential "mid-cycle activity plateau" in 2026.

3. **FX volatility (MODERATE).** 83% of revenue is generated outside the U.S. The $22.5M FX gain in FY2025 followed by $4.6M loss in Q1 FY2026 demonstrates meaningful quarterly earnings noise. This makes EPS forecasting unreliable on a quarter-to-quarter basis.

4. **Customer concentration (MODERATE).** UK SAR (19% of revenue) is terminable at will with a cancellation fee. Top 3 customers = 36% of revenue. Any contract loss or downsizing creates meaningful impact.

5. **Nigeria operational risk (MODERATE).** 13% of revenue from a politically volatile operating environment with local content requirements and FX controls.

6. **S92 supply chain constraints (MODERATE).** Parts delays causing aircraft groundings represent lost revenue opportunities with uncertain resolution timeline.

## Conclusion

Bristow Group operates from a position of competitive strength in a supply-constrained market with visible growth catalysts. The structural thesis — tight helicopter supply, contract repricing, government margin normalization — is sound. However, the stock has appreciated 65% from its 2025 lows and now trades at $48.90, pricing in substantial future improvement that has yet to materialize in reported results.

Two consecutive EPS misses, falling analyst estimates, the COO Government Services retirement during a critical execution period, and CEO selling collectively argue for patience. The appropriate posture is to wait for either (a) a lower entry price restoring favorable risk/reward, or (b) Q2 FY2026 earnings confirmation that the margin inflection is real.

We downgrade from BUY to HOLD with a positive 12-month growth thesis. Entry at $42-44 would restore the BUY recommendation.
