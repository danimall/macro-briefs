**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly is the dominant franchise in the incretin/GLP-1 class, the fastest-growing therapeutic category in pharmaceutical history. FY2025 revenue rose 45% to $65.2B and Q1 2026 accelerated further to +56% YoY ($19.8B), with net income up 168% to $7.4B. Growth is overwhelmingly volume-driven: FY2025 volume contributed +50% against a –6% price drag, and the mix runs at ~83% gross margin. Mounjaro and Zepbound together generated 65% of Q1'26 revenue, and management has raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $82–85B, implying another 26–30% of growth off the 2025 base.

The stock at ~$1,179 trades at roughly 34x current-year consensus EPS ($34.85) and ~26x next-year ($44.91). On trailing GAAP EPS of $22.95 the multiple is ~41x, but with EPS running well above trailing and next-year estimates rising +6.6% over 90 days, the forward metrics are the relevant anchors. The central debate is whether the well-telegraphed headwinds (single-class concentration, IRA price-setting, most-favored-nation pricing pressure, and Novo Nordisk's head-start in the oral obesity segment) are sufficient to derail a business compounding at this rate. Our read is that the November 2025 pricing agreement is net-positive on a volume-weighted basis (expanded Medicare obesity access is a genuine TAM unlock for a high-margin, volume-driven model), and that the oral GLP-1 franchise (Foundayo approved April 2026, retatrutide Phase 3 positive) extends the runway.

We maintain a BUY with a base-case 12-month target of ~$1,400. The recommendation is scored 6.9, reflecting a modest step-down in management-quality and competitive-position sub-scores over recent reads (accelerating BD spend, Novo's oral lead) offset by improving forward valuation as EPS estimates ratchet higher. We recommend disciplined sizing rather than maximum conviction: the concentration and pricing-policy tails are real, and the current-quarter estimate has been cut 21% in 30 days on pricing-agreement timing, which we address below.

## Company Overview

Lilly is a ~150-year-old Indianapolis-based pharmaceutical company operating as a single human-pharmaceuticals segment, selling in ~90 countries with ~50,000 employees (~12,000 in R&D). Revenue concentrates in four therapeutic areas:

- **Cardiometabolic Health** (the engine): Mounjaro (tirzepatide, type-2 diabetes), Zepbound (tirzepatide, obesity/OSA), Trulicity, Jardiance (Boehringer collaboration), insulins, and Foundayo (orforglipron), the first oral non-peptide GLP-1 approved for obesity (FDA approval April 2026, no food/water timing restrictions).
- **Oncology**: Verzenio, Jaypirca, Retevmo, Inluriyo.
- **Immunology**: Taltz, Omvoh, Ebglyss.
- **Neuroscience**: Kisunla (Alzheimer's), Emgality.

The competitive moat rests on three pillars: tirzepatide compound patents to 2036 (US) / 2037 (EU) / 2040 (Japan), unusually long for a category leader; a multi-billion-dollar manufacturing build-out across North Carolina, Wisconsin, Indiana, Virginia, Texas, Alabama, Pennsylvania, Ireland, Germany, and the Netherlands; and a deep next-generation incretin pipeline (orforglipron oral, retatrutide triple-agonist, eloralintide, insulin efsitora). US distribution runs primarily through three wholesalers (McKesson, Cencora, Cardinal Health), each >10% of revenue, supplemented by a growing direct-to-patient channel (LillyDirect) that manages payer/PBM friction and supports international out-of-pocket launches.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue trajectory is exceptional and accelerating:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 28.5 | 34.1 | 45.0 | 65.2 | 19.8 (+56% YoY) |
| Net income ($B) | 6.2 | 5.2 | 10.6 | 20.6 | 7.4 (+168% YoY) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | 11.71 | 22.95 | 8.26 |

US revenue grew +43% and ex-US +48% in FY2025. Mounjaro nearly doubled to $23.0B; Zepbound rose 175% to $13.5B. Q1'26 ex-US revenue was up 81% as Mounjaro rolled into major international markets. Net income growth outpaced revenue on lower acquired-IPR&D charges and gross-margin leverage.

Margins are the defining feature: gross margin dipped to 81.9% in Q1'26 from ~83% on lower realized prices, a controlled, expected effect of the pricing agreement rather than a structural break. Operating cash flow reached $16.8B in FY2025 (roughly 2x 2024's $8.8B), with FCF of $9.0B after $7.8B of capex; Q1'26 OCF was $5.3B. Capex is elevated and rising to fund the capacity build-out, the correct use of capital given demand outstripping supply through much of the tirzepatide ramp.

On the balance sheet, total debt was $42.5B at FY2025 (rising to $43.4B in Q1'26) against equity of $26.5B → $31.2B. The reported D/E of ~1.6x (FY2025) and an Altman Z-Score of 1.25 would, on a naive screen, flag distress. That reading is wrong. The Z-Score penalizes Lilly's thin book equity (a function of aggressive buybacks and IPR&D expensing) and a technically negative operating margin caused by large non-cash acquired-IPR&D charges, while the company posts a 31.7% net margin, ~78% ROE, and 18.4% ROA. Current ratio is 1.58 and interest coverage is comfortable. This is leverage deployed offensively, not stress. The genuine caution is not solvency but FCF-to-net-income conversion being pressured by capex and rising BD spend; the earnings are high-quality, but the free cash flow is being reinvested heavily, which we treat as appropriate at this stage rather than a red flag.

Capital return: the dividend was raised to $1.73/quarter ($6.92 annualized for 2026), a ~0.6% yield that is immaterial to the thesis; a $15B buyback authorized December 2024 had $8.6B remaining as of Q1'26.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, ranging +11.9% to +27.0%, most recently a 27% beat in Q1'26 ($8.55 actual vs $6.73 estimate). This is a management team that has consistently under-promised on a rapidly ramping franchise.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is unusually broad and durable, which matters more for winner-selection than almost any other dimension. Three independent data points confirm the structural demand: (1) the raised FY2026 guide implies ~26–30% further growth; (2) international is inflecting (ex-US +81% in Q1'26) as the Mounjaro rollout reaches major markets and China NRDL inclusion trades price for volume; and (3) the pipeline extends the runway well beyond the current injectables.

The estimate-revision picture is two-tiered and requires care. The current-quarter estimate was cut 21.3% over 30 days (to $6.94) and the current-year estimate slipped 3.8% over 30 days (to $34.85), driven mostly by near-term pricing-agreement timing and lower realized prices. But next-quarter (+3.0% over 90 days), current-year (+0.6% over 90 days), and especially next-year (+6.6% over 90 days, to $44.91) estimates are rising. The near-term cut reflects pricing/copay-program mechanics rather than demand deterioration; the medium-term revisions confirm the underlying earnings trajectory is intact and rising. We do not treat this as the strong +30%/90d BUY signal, since the pattern is a near-term air pocket against a rising medium-term curve, but the net signal remains positive for forward earnings power.

Pipeline optionality is substantial: Foundayo (orforglipron) gives Lilly a scalable, pharmacy-friendly, potentially TAM-expanding asset, and its Phase 3 T2D trial also met its primary endpoint. Retatrutide (triple agonist) met its Phase 3 T2D primary endpoint and is a candidate for best-in-class weight loss. Business-development spend is ramping sharply (pending 2026 deals up to ~$12B vs <$3B a quarter earlier), diversifying into cardiovascular gene editing (Verve) and oral inflammation (Ventyx), prudent given concentration risk though it warrants monitoring for capital-allocation discipline.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer medians in the pharmaceuticals cohort sit at ~20x P/E and ~2.9% yield:

| JNJ | $579B | 27.6 | 2.2% |
| MRK | $295B | 33.8 | 2.9% |
| PFE | $149B | 20.0 | 6.7% |
| BMY | $117B | 15.9 | 4.5% |
| ZTS | $34B | 13.3 | 2.6% |

Lilly trades at a clear premium to this group. That premium is warranted: the peers are largely low-single-digit to mid-teens growers facing patent cliffs (BMY at 15.9x is cheap for a reason), while Lilly is compounding revenue at 45–56% with the longest patent runway in the category. On next-year EPS the PEG is well below 1.0 if one credits even half the current growth rate, a reasonable haircut given pricing pressure. The relevant comparison is MRK at 33.8x growing far more slowly; Lilly's forward multiple is not stretched relative to its growth-and-quality profile.

We deliberately anchor on forward and normalized earnings rather than the 41x trailing GAAP figure, which understates earnings power in a franchise where EPS is ramping this quickly and where the current-quarter dip is pricing-mechanics-driven. Analyst targets (mean $1,270, median $1,300, high $1,600, low $850) place the stock modestly below consensus mid-point with room to the high case; on a rising-estimate, sub-30x-next-year name, we treat consensus targets as a floor that will drift up as FY2027 estimates firm.

The valuation score of 5.5 reflects genuine two-sidedness rather than deep asymmetry. The premium is justified, but at ~26x next-year on a name with real pricing-policy and concentration tails, the market is not obviously mispricing this stock in the way a 5.5 → 7 upgrade would require. The argument is quality growth at a defensible premium, not cheapness. We keep the score anchored below 6 partly because a low apparent multiple on any single metric has historically been a weak buy signal on its own; the durable case rests on growth and competitive position, not on the stock being cheap.

## Competitive Landscape

The obesity/incretin market is a Lilly-versus-Novo Nordisk duopoly, and Lilly is winning the injectable battle decisively: Novo cut ~9,000 jobs in 2026 amid a constant-currency sales decline while Lilly grew 56%. The dynamic has inverted in the oral segment: Novo secured a ~3-month US head-start and EU approval for oral Wegovy, and channel research attributes Novo's oral momentum partly to brand familiarity. Lilly's counter is Foundayo's superior convenience (no food/water timing) and a potential China approval by year-end 2026. This is the single most important near-term competitive uncertainty, and it is the main reason the competitive-position sub-score has stepped down from 9.0 toward 7.8 over recent reads; Lilly's oral dominance is not a foregone conclusion, and script-share data over the next two quarters is the tell.

Longer term, the class faces IRA price-setting (Jardiance government-priced in 2026 at a 66% discount; Trulicity and Verzenio selected for 2028, with management flagging that "additional significant products" in future years would accelerate revenue erosion prior to exclusivity expiry), MFN pricing pressure, and eventually biosimilar/generic competition. The tirzepatide franchise is protected to 2036–2040, but orforglipron as a small molecule faces the shorter 9-year IRA clock, a specific concern for its long-run economics. Lilly's defense is a combination of DTC/telehealth out-of-pocket channels (LillyDirect, being exported to Europe in H2'26/early'27 to sidestep reference-pricing) and continuous pipeline innovation. The competitive trajectory remains improving-to-stable in the aggregate, with the oral segment the clear soft spot.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and not fully de-risked, which drives the 5.0 risk-factor score:

1. **Single-class concentration.** Incretins are ~65% of revenue. A tirzepatide safety signal, manufacturing disruption, or competitive displacement would be disproportionately damaging. Management explicitly flags that "incretin volume fluctuations due to channel dynamics can have a disproportionate impact."
2. **US pricing / MFN policy.** The November 2025 voluntary agreement commits Lilly to lower Medicaid and other prices and "balanced" global launch pricing; the Medicare GLP-1 "Bridge Program" runs July 2026–December 2027. Realized prices are already falling (US –7% in Q1'26; –10% in FY2025). The net effect is ambiguous-to-positive (see below), but the near-term optics pressure realized-price-sensitive quarters.
3. **IRA price-setting escalation.** The 2028 selection of Trulicity and Verzenio, and management's expectation of more products in future years, is a structural, dated erosion risk.
4. **Oral competition from Novo** and emerging Chinese biotech licensing.
5. **Compounded/counterfeit incretins** eroding price and diverting sales.
6. **Tariffs / supply chain / China** (BIOSECURE, Section 232 — a 3-year tariff grace period contingent on meeting US manufacturing commitments).
7. **Litigation:** GLP-1 product-liability MDLs (GI injuries, NAION), 340B disputes, insulin-pricing MDL, and a reversed Emgality patent ruling (April 2026, Teva, charge recognized).

The Q1 2026 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors since the 10-K, which limits the surprise element. The most important reframing is on pricing: per STAT reporting, the headline $245/month Medicare/Medicaid price carries a loophole — it is contingent on private Medicare insurers covering the drugs with a $50 copay. In practice, drugmakers secured expanded volume without commensurate price cuts in some cases. For a volume-driven business at ~83% gross margin, expanded Medicare obesity access (a previously uncovered population) is a TAM unlock that likely outweighs the price concession. A separate, non-financial governance/headline thread surfaced in June 2026 (an experimental-retatrutide compassionate-use controversy and reports the President held LLY stock during the pricing deal), not material to fundamentals but worth flagging.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-favorable. Near-term ATM IV of 33.7% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 38.5% (cheap by 4.8pp), and the IV percentile within the current chain is at the 1st percentile — the market is pricing relatively low near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced (1.4%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.92, and OI ratio 0.76. The term structure is in normal contango (near 33.7% < long-term 38.6%), with a longer-dated modest put skew (–3.7% at 6m) consistent with hedging around the pricing/oral-competition swing factors rather than acute fear. IV has risen ~5.3pp since late June alongside a 7.3% price gain, indicating the market is pricing an upcoming catalyst (next earnings) but not distress. Short interest is negligible at 1.1% of float (2.7 days to cover). The options market is not challenging the bullish fundamental thesis, and current IV levels are not an unfavorable entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$1,600):** FY2026 revenue lands at the top of the guide; Foundayo captures meaningful oral share as its convenience advantage plays out in scripts; retatrutide advances toward best-in-class weight-loss positioning; international continues compounding at 60%+; the pricing agreement proves volume-accretive; and FY2027 estimates ratchet toward and beyond current $44.91, driving a multiple that holds ~30x forward on the higher base. This matches the Street high target.

**Bear case (~$850):** A tirzepatide safety signal or a broader class safety concern; Novo's oral head-start compounds into durable share loss; IRA/MFN price erosion accelerates faster than volume can offset; and aggressive BD spend (~$12B pending) dilutes returns without near-term revenue. On a de-rating to ~20x a lowered forward number, the stock approaches the Street low. This is a ~28% decline from current levels.

**Base case (~$1,400):** Growth decelerates gradually but remains well above pharma-peer norms (revenue +26–30% in 2026, moderating thereafter); the oral segment settles into a competitive but Lilly-participating market; pricing pressure is real but volume-offset; and next-year EPS of ~$45 supports ~31x forward. This is roughly consistent with the mean/median analyst target continuing to drift up as FY2027 firms.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: ~$1,400.** ~31x next-year consensus EPS of $44.91 = ~$1,392. This sits between the bear ($850) and bull ($1,600) scenarios. Upside to base from ~$1,179 is ~19%.
- **Bull case target: ~$1,600** (~+36%).
- **Bear case target: ~$850** (~–28%).
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, tied to the FY2026 earnings cadence (Q2'26 late July/early August, Q3'26 late October) and oral-GLP-1 script-share data over H2'26.
- **Thesis invalidation triggers:** (1) FY2026 revenue guidance cut below the $82B floor, or a quarter showing incretin volume growth decelerating below ~20% YoY; (2) a confirmed tirzepatide/class safety signal that triggers label restriction or MDL escalation with quantified liability; (3) Novo's oral Wegovy sustaining a script-share lead that stalls Foundayo's ramp below internal expectations across two consecutive quarters; (4) next-year EPS estimates reversing to a falling trend (currently +6.6% over 90 days).

## Conclusion

Lilly remains a BUY, scored 6.9, with disciplined sizing. The company is the leader in the most powerful growth category in pharma, compounding revenue at 45–56% with the longest patent runway in the incretin class, ~83% gross margins, and a pipeline (oral orforglipron, triple-agonist retatrutide) that extends the story well beyond the current injectables. The forward multiple (~26x next-year) is a defensible premium for this growth-and-quality profile, and next-year estimates are rising even as the near-term quarter absorbs pricing-agreement mechanics. We recommend a measured position rather than a full one: the ~65% single-class concentration, the IRA/MFN price-erosion path, and Novo's genuine head-start in oral obesity are the reasons the risk-factor and competitive-position sub-scores sit where they do. On balance, the volume-positive resolution of the pricing overhang and the breadth of the growth signal outweigh those tails over a 12-month horizon, with a base-case target of ~$1,400 and upside to $1,600 if the oral franchise and international ramp deliver.
