## Executive Summary

Trimble reported Q2 FY2026 on August 12: revenue $972.0M (+11% reported, +10% organic), record ARR of $2.509B (+14% reported, +12% organic), non-GAAP EPS $0.86 versus $0.71, and segment operating margin expansion in all three reportable segments. FY2026 guidance was raised to $3,900-3,950M of revenue and $3.60-3.70 of non-GAAP EPS, against FY2025 non-GAAP EPS of $3.13 — roughly 17% earnings growth on roughly 9-10% revenue growth. The same release contained a $562.0M goodwill impairment in Transportation & Logistics, producing a GAAP net loss of $(471.7)M and a guided FY2026 GAAP tax rate of 145%, which renders every GAAP-based screen on this name uninterpretable for the year.

The important analytical point about the impairment is its disclosed trigger: a sustained decline in Trimble's own market capitalization and lower market multiples for software businesses. T&L revenue grew 6.3% to $141.0M in the quarter and segment operating margin expanded 240bp to 24.0%. The charge is non-cash, largely non-deductible (hence the 145% rate), carries no covenant or liquidity consequence, and is presented below the operating line — GAAP operating income was a positive $132.0M. It is a mark-to-market of a 2023 acquisition price against a 2026 multiple environment, and the peer table confirms the environment rather than the company: GWRE is 37% below its 52-week high, TYL 48%, PTC 31%, DOCU 31%. Trimble at 32% below its high is participating in a sector derating, not an isolated collapse.

What separates this from a straightforward "cheap grower" case is the governance defect. KPMG issued an **adverse opinion on internal control over financial reporting** as of January 2, 2026, naming two material weaknesses — ineffective IT general controls, and ineffective process-level controls over revenue, income taxes and other financial reporting processes. Those weaknesses were still unremediated as of the Q1 FY2026 10-Q. The audit relationship changed after 39 years (EY 1986 through 2025, KPMG since), a re-audit occurred at roughly $34M of FY2025 cost with expense still running, and the December 2025 credit facility contains a covenant requiring timely delivery of quarterly reports and compliance certificates — the specific covenant a control failure can breach. At 15.7x guided FY2026 non-GAAP EPS and roughly a 5.6% normalized free-cash-flow yield, a lot of that risk is compensated. It is not resolved, which is why this is ACCUMULATE rather than BUY.

## Company Overview

Trimble sells positioning hardware, modeling software and workflow platforms into construction, geospatial and transportation end markets, in more than 160 countries, with over 11,500 employees and over 1,000 issued patents. Three segments (FY2025 revenue / segment margin):

- **Field Systems — $1,539.5M / 31.1%.** GNSS and optical positioning, 3D laser scanning, machine control (Earthworks), site software, and **Positioning Services**, a subscription GNSS-correction franchise (VRSNow, CenterPoint RTX). Distribution runs through independent SITECH/Trimble Technology dealers. Joint ventures with Caterpillar (machine control) and Nikon (optical) sit here.
- **AECO — $1,498.6M / 34.2%.** Pure software: SketchUp, BIM/VDC, contractor estimating and project management, owner asset lifecycle.
- **T&L — $549.2M / 21.9%.** Carrier TMS and fleet maintenance, **Transporeon** (acquired 2023 for €1.9B/$2.1B; $1,390.1M allocated to goodwill), and MAPS routing (PC*Miler, CoPilot).

The portfolio has been reshaped twice. Agriculture went to AGCO in April 2024 for $1.9B cash plus a 15% stake in the PTx Trimble JV, producing a $1.7B pre-tax gain. Mobility telematics went to Platform Science in February 2025 in exchange for 28.5% preferred equity plus warrants for a further 4%, carried at $253.9M under the measurement alternative. Both exits removed lower-margin hardware revenue. Subscription and services rose from 53.4% of revenue in FY2023 to 68.4% in FY2025, and consolidated operating margin expanded 470bp over the same two years while reported revenue fell.

Customer concentration is genuinely low — no customer at 10% of revenue or receivables in any of the last three years. Only the United States exceeds 10% of revenue by geography ($1,905.8M, 53%).

## Financial Analysis

**Reported revenue decline is a divestiture artifact.** Total revenue went $3,798.7M (FY2023) → $3,683.3M (FY2024, a 53-week year) → $3,587.3M (FY2025). Segment operating income over the same period went $1,050.7M → $1,060.7M → $1,110.7M. FY2024 net income of $1,504.4M contains the $1,687.9M divestiture gain and is not a usable base for anything.

**H1 FY2026 is the inflection.** Revenue $1,911.9M versus $1,716.3M (+11.4%), non-GAAP operating margin 26.4% from 24.5%, adjusted EBITDA margin 28.0% from 26.3%, non-GAAP diluted EPS $1.65 from $1.32. Q2 segment detail: AECO $388.5M (+10.9%, margin 30.6%), Field Systems $442.5M (+12.7%, margin 32.9% from 30.8%), T&L $141.0M (+6.3%, margin 24.0% from 21.6%). Field Systems is the standout — 210bp of margin expansion on 12.7% growth, split roughly evenly between gross margin/mix (cost of sales 40.1% of segment revenue from 41.2%) and operating leverage (opex 27.0% from 28.0%).

**Cash flow requires two corrections in opposite directions.** Reported FY2025 operating cash flow of $386.2M and FCF of $360.9M look like a 27% deterioration. They include **$277.4M of cash tax on the Ag divestiture** (inside $289.3M of Netherlands payments, Note 14), with income taxes payable running off from $325.0M to $17.7M. Normalized FY2025 FCF is roughly $638M — a 17.8% margin, up from ~$620M normalized in FY2024. That is the single most important fix to a mechanical data pull on this name.

The offsetting correction: **H1 FY2026 FCF of $501.8M must not be annualized.** It includes a $250.4M receivables release (AR $856.0M → $598.1M) driven by AECO term licenses renewing January 1, which peaks year-end receivables for H1 collection; deferred revenue declined $51.6M over the same period. A defensible sensitivity — not a forecast — is FY2026 normalized FCF near $745-765M if cash generation tracks non-GAAP earnings growth, implying a **5.6% FCF yield on the $13.4B equity value** and ~5.1% on EV. Cash tax timing, incentive-comp accruals and continuing remediation costs could move this in either direction.

**Balance sheet is conservative.** Two unsecured notes — $600M at 4.90% due 2028 and $800M at 6.10% due 2033 — with **no principal due before maturity**. A new $1.25B five-year revolver (December 2030, $500M accordion) replaced the 2022 facility. At Q2: cash $214.4M, short-term debt $16.4M and long-term debt $1,442.9M (total $1,459.3M), **net debt ≈ $1,245M**. Against implied FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $1.10-1.12B (28.0% H1 margin on the $3,925M guide), **net leverage is ~1.1x** and interest coverage on FY2025 non-GAAP operating income is roughly 13x.

Two screen traps worth naming. The Q2 current ratio of 0.95x reflects $833.9M of deferred revenue inside current liabilities — a software delivery obligation, not a cash claim; excluding it the ratio is 2.33x. And the FY2026 GAAP loss will mechanically depress the EBIT- and retained-earnings-to-assets terms of any Altman-type score computed on trailing GAAP figures while cash generation improved. The genuine balance-sheet caution is different: goodwill of $4,826.6M against book equity of $5,110.8M means book value is almost entirely intangible, so a second impairment would consume a large fraction of stated equity.

**Capital allocation is where the arithmetic turns unflattering.** FY2025 buybacks were 12.2M shares at an average of **$71.86** ($875.4M), and Q1 FY2026 repurchases averaged roughly $67.59. Against $57.32 that is a mark-to-market shortfall of about 20% and 15% respectively. A new $1.0B authorization was announced August 12, cancelling $608.2M of remaining prior capacity — the third $1.0B headline in nineteen months, which adds roughly $392M of incremental capacity rather than $1.0B. Liquidity comfortably supports the full amount (undrawn $1.25B revolver plus ~$750M of normalized FCF); I draw no inference about intent from the structure, but readers should not treat the headline as new spending capacity.

**Earnings quality.** Four consecutive beats (+5.3%, +7.9%, +1.9%, +10.6%) supports confidence in the guide. Against that, the GAAP-to-non-GAAP wedge widened from $94.8M to $128.6M in a single quarter, now 13.2 points of revenue: SBC and deferred comp $44.3M (4.6%), purchased-intangible amortization $44.1M (4.5%), acquisition/divestiture items $23.9M (from $2.7M), restructuring $12.6M. FY2026 guidance embeds $0.67/share of SBC and $0.74/share of amortization. Deal and re-audit costs are genuinely transitory; SBC is not. Adjusting the $3.65 midpoint for SBC alone gives $2.98, or **19.2x** — still not a demanding multiple for a 10% organic grower at 28% EBITDA margins, but the honest number to hold in mind.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth accelerated from roughly flat in FY2025 (adjusting for divestitures and the FY2024 53rd week) to +12% in Q1 FY2026 and +10% in Q2. ARR of $2.509B grew 12% organic. Guidance implies this continues rather than fades: the $3,925M midpoint requires H2 revenue of $2,013M, +10.5% over H2 FY2025's $1,821M, and the Q3 midpoint of $965.5M is +10.2%.

A definitional caution the company itself supplies: ARR is an annualized single-quarter run-rate of subscription, maintenance and the in-quarter portion of term-license value. It is not contracted future revenue and not additive to deferred revenue. Remaining performance obligations were approximately $2.0B at FY2025 year-end with $1.4B expected within twelve months. Neither figure is backlog and neither should be described that way. The better-behaved leading indicator is deferred revenue, which rose 11.5% to $998.7M in FY2025 while reported revenue fell 2.6%, with billings net of revenue recognized of $877.9M versus $786.9M.

Where the growth comes from matters for durability. Field Systems (+12.7%) is the surprise — the segment most readers dismiss as hardware is growing fastest, helped by the Positioning Services subscription layer inside it. AECO at +10.9% is growing into an already software-like 34.2% margin, so incremental value there has to come from volume, not margin. T&L at +6.3% is the laggard, and it is the segment carrying the impaired goodwill.

The end-market question cuts both ways. Roughly 85% of revenue is construction-linked, and the company's own forward-looking language flags fluctuations in interest rates, heightened trade tensions and export-control restrictions, and associated supply-chain disruptions. Non-residential and infrastructure activity is the relevant exposure, not housing. Field Systems' 12.7% suggests dealer-level demand has not cracked. The company's own language flags "the pace at which our dealers work through their inventory" as a swing factor, so a slowdown would show up with a lag. [The specific claims about the policy rate at 3.50-3.75% with three hawkish dissents, the 30-year at a 2007 high, mortgage rates at a one-year high, Caterpillar's largest beat in five years, and AI data-center contractors growing above 30% are not traceable to the provided sources — unverified — and have been removed.]

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current year $3.59 (+1.3% over 90 days), next year $4.08 (+0.9%), with the current quarter down 1.0% and next quarter down 2.2%. This is not the >30% revision inflection that constitutes a standalone buy signal; it is quiet confirmation that consensus is neither chasing nor abandoning the story.

R&D was $630.7M in FY2025 (17.6% of revenue), down from $662.3M and $664.3M, consistent with the divested businesses; over 70% goes to software and services. Management's claim that its "data estate based in the physical world" creates a unique AI advantage is an assertion — plausible given that Trimble originates survey, machine-position, model and freight-transaction data, but nothing in the filings quantifies AI-attributable revenue or win rates.

## Competitive Landscape

**Field Systems is the strongest and improving position.** Competitors are effectively Hexagon (Leica) and Topcon — a concentrated field. The moats are hard to replicate: precise GNSS and optical IP inside a 1,000+ patent portfolio, the SITECH/Trimble Technology dealer network, OEM-adjacent JVs with Caterpillar and Nikon, and the RTX correction-network subscription layer with high switching costs. Twelve-plus percent growth at a 32.9% margin is the validation.

**AECO faces the toughest gradient.** The 10-K concedes that broadening across the construction lifecycle means "we increasingly compete with large, established companies that offer similar or comparable portfolios" — Autodesk, Procore, Bentley, Nemetschek, Hexagon. Trimble's differentiator is field-to-office integration: nobody else owns both the total station and the BIM model. That is defensible, and it is also the reason Trimble is less exposed than horizontal seat-based SaaS to the AI-displacement pressure now compressing application-software multiples generally — the data originates from physical instruments Trimble sells.

**T&L is contested, and the market has already priced that view.** Transporeon's 2025 repositioning as a global shipper TMS is strategically coherent (owning both sides of the shipper/carrier network) and simultaneously an admission that the original marketplace framing needed reframing three years after a $2.1B purchase. Competitors are fragmented — Descartes, Manhattan, project44, e2open, McLeod, plus Oracle/SAP modules.

Two structural dependencies deserve emphasis. Field Systems sells primarily through independent dealers, so Trimble's revenue is one step removed from end demand in its largest segment. And related-party revenue from investees was $124.1M in FY2025 (3.5% of total), up 13.8%, where **Trimble is the seller and its own JVs are the buyers** — GNSS technology, guidance products, positioning services, grade control. That is demand governed partly by partners in which Trimble holds 15-50% influence, not diversification. Because the Ag transaction excluded certain GNSS and guidance technologies, Trimble remains an agriculture component supplier to PTx Trimble alongside its 15% equity stake. The Platform Science position (28.5% preferred plus 4% warrants, carried at $253.9M under the measurement alternative) generates no cash, is not marked absent an observable transaction, and is specifically named in the company's own risk language.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Adverse ICFR opinion with two unremediated material weaknesses.** The dominant idiosyncratic risk. KPMG named ineffective IT general controls and ineffective process-level controls over revenue, income taxes and other financial reporting processes, including retention of evidence supporting control operation. KPMG's critical audit matter is the evaluation of standalone selling prices across multi-element contracts — landing on the same account as the revenue material weakness, so audit risk and control risk are concentrated together at a company whose bundles increasingly mix hardware, term license, maintenance and SaaS. The Q1 FY2026 10-Q disclosed disclosure controls were not effective and the weaknesses were not remediated. Consequences that matter commercially: the 2025 credit facility requires timely delivery of quarterly reports and compliance certificates; late filings impair S-3 shelf eligibility; roughly $34M of FY2025 remediation cost is still running ($16.3M of restructuring-and-other add-back in Q2 versus $8.4M a year earlier). Mitigants: KPMG issued an unqualified opinion on the FY2025 **financial statements**; Note 11 states there are no material legal proceedings; and the September 2025 hire of Kenneth Bement as Chief Accounting Officer — prior CAO roles at Vista Outdoor, Ancestry, Gopuff and Conservice, senior roles at Alphabet and RTX, and FASB experience managing the revenue project that produced ASC 606 — is about as precisely targeted a remediation hire as could be specified.

**2. Residual T&L goodwill.** Roughly $1.7B remains after the $562.0M charge (total goodwill $5,239.7M → $4,826.6M, a $413.1M decline implying ~$149M of FX and acquired additions). Against ~$561M annualized T&L revenue that is roughly 3x segment revenue. Segment carrying amounts and reporting-unit fair values are not disclosed ("asset information by segments is not regularly reviewed by the CODM"), so headroom estimates carry wide error bars. If software multiples compress further or T&L's ~6% growth stalls, a second charge of several hundred million dollars is plausible. It would be non-cash and non-covenant, but it would consume a meaningful share of a book equity that is already almost entirely intangible. Part of the FY2026 charge may simply unwind the $185.0M of FX that inflated T&L goodwill in FY2025; a step-one failure typically also reflects trimmed cash-flow assumptions, which the disclosure neither confirms nor excludes.

**3. Presentation opacity.** The impairment sits in "non-operating (expense) income, net," so GAAP operating income was a positive $132.0M and GAAP operating margin of 13.6% is unaffected. Divestiture gains receive the same treatment. This changes nothing about net income, EPS, cash flow or covenants, but it means GAAP operating margin is not a comprehensive profitability measure for Trimble in FY2026, and readers screening on it will not see the charge.

**4. Cyclicality and channel.** ~85% construction-linked revenue into a rate-sensitive environment, with dealer inventory as an amplifier. Hardware manufacturing is outsourced principally to Jabil and Benchmark, with reliance on a limited number of suppliers for critical components. Europe is 28% of revenue with a euro-denominated Transporeon asset base that swung translation OCI by +$393.1M in FY2025.

**5. Governance and alignment.** Cumulative TSR indexed to a FY2021 base of 100 stood near 117 for Trimble versus ~258 for the peer group, while FY2025 CEO total compensation was approximately $19.1M. Buybacks were executed at $71.86 (FY2025) and ~$67.59 (Q1 FY2026) against $57.32 today. No open-market insider purchases appear in recent Form 4 activity; CEO Painter sold 7,500 shares near $66.94 in April 2026 and an SVP sold 16,725 shares. Absence of buying near multi-year lows removes a confirming signal but is weak evidence on its own, particularly where control remediation and filing timing may constrain trading windows. Two 8-K Item 5.02 filings (2026-03-24, 2026-05-26) remain unidentified — a verification gap. PSU leverage runs 0-220% of target and up to 300% for certain awards; on 1.0M unvested PSUs even a 300% outcome adds ~3.0M shares, about 1.3% of the count, leaving existing holders at roughly 98.7% of prior proportional ownership. Material but not structurally threatening.

**6. Unconfirmed acquisition.** H1 FY2026 shows $230.5M of acquisitions net of cash (versus $4.4M a year earlier), corroborated by acquisition/divestiture items rising to $23.9M and G&A up $32.1M. Implied inorganic contribution of roughly 1 point of revenue and 2 points of ARR suggests a price near 5x acquired ARR. The target's identity could not be re-verified and should be treated as unknown; the cash outflow is a filing fact.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $84 (+46.5%)

Organic growth holds at 10-12% through FY2027 as infrastructure and non-residential construction offsets rate-sensitive private demand. Field Systems compounds above 10% with Positioning Services carrying margin; AECO holds low-double-digit growth at 34% margins; T&L stabilizes above 6% and avoids a second charge. Both material weaknesses are remediated in the FY2026 10-K, removing the discrete governance discount and restoring normal S-3 access. Normalized FCF reaches $850M+ in FY2027 against a shrinking share count funded by the $1.0B authorization. The multiple re-rates to 20x FY2027 EPS of roughly $4.20 — still a discount to the application-software median — giving $84, near the 52-week high of $84.64 and below the $94 high published target.

### Bear Case — $46 (-19.8%)

Non-residential construction rolls over as rate pressure bites; dealer inventories build and Field Systems decelerates to mid-single digits with a 5-7 quarter lag effect. T&L growth stalls, and a second impairment of $300-500M lands against ~$1.7B of residual goodwill, consuming a further tranche of an already intangible-heavy book. Material weaknesses persist into FY2027, a filing delay triggers a covenant notice or a Nasdaq letter, and the governance discount widens rather than closes. The multiple compresses to 11-12x on FY2027 EPS trimmed to $3.80, giving $44-46 against a 52-week low of $47.92.

### Base Case — $70 (+22.1%)

Revenue lands near the $3,925M guide midpoint with FY2027 growth of 8-9%; non-GAAP EPS reaches $4.08-4.15; adjusted EBITDA approaches $1.22B. Remediation progresses but is not fully certified until the FY2026 10-K, so a partial governance discount persists. The multiple settles at 17x FY2027 EPS of $4.08 ($69.4) — cross-checked at 14x EV/EBITDA on $1.22B (EV $17.1B less $1.24B net debt, $67.8/share) and at a 4.7% normalized FCF yield on $770M ($16.4B equity, $70/share). Call it $70.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: ACCUMULATE.** Probability-weighted expected return at $57.32: 55% base (+22.1%), 20% bull (+46.5%), 25% bear (-19.8%) = **+16.5%** over twelve months. That is a positive risk-adjusted return with adequate margin of safety, but confidence is capped by an unresolved control opinion that makes every reported figure marginally less reliable and by roughly $1.7B of goodwill whose carrying value depends on a multiple environment nobody controls. Those are precisely the conditions ACCUMULATE describes rather than BUY.

**Price-basis sensitivity.** If the $61.63 print in the quote feed reflects the post-earnings level rather than the $57.32 last trade, upside to base compresses to +13.6% and expected return to roughly +8.4% — which is a HOLD. Entry price therefore does material work here. Do not chase above $60.

**Staged entry plan.**
- Initiate roughly one-third to one-half of target weight at or below $58.
- Add a second tranche toward **$50-53** (the early-July trough region, and the level at which base-case upside exceeds 30%).
- Reserve the final tranche for confirmation of remediation in the FY2026 10-K (expected February-March 2027) or for a Q3 print holding organic ARR growth at or above 10%.

**Targets:** base $70 (+22.1%), bull $84 (+46.5%), bear $46 (-19.8%). Timeframe twelve to eighteen months, keyed to the Q3 FY2026 report (late October 2026), the FY2026 10-K and its ICFR opinion (Feb-Mar 2027), and the FY2027 guide.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. FY2026 10-K disclosing remediation of **both** material weaknesses with an unqualified ICFR opinion.
2. Price at or below $50 with organic ARR growth still at or above 10%.
3. Two consecutive quarters of organic revenue growth at or above 11% with FY2027 consensus EPS revised above $4.25.

**Downgrade to HOLD or REDUCE on any of:**
1. Organic revenue growth below 6% in any quarter, or organic ARR growth below 8%.
2. A second T&L impairment **accompanied by** a reduction in T&L forecast growth or guidance — the market-multiple-triggered charge is tolerable; an operationally triggered one is not.
3. Any late filing, covenant notice, or Nasdaq listing-rule notification.
4. FY2027 consensus EPS cut below $3.80, or FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance reduced.
5. Buybacks executed above $70 while organic growth decelerates.

**Next reassessment:** Q3 FY2026 results, late October 2026.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this report was declining (7.0 in February to 5.8 in July), with management quality down 2.5 points and risk factors down 1.5 — driven by the adverse control opinion, the auditor change and the buyback-versus-price arithmetic. Those deductions remain warranted and I have not reversed them: management quality stays at 4.8 and risk factors at 4.5. What has changed is the operating evidence. Two consecutive quarters of double-digit organic growth, margin expansion in all three segments, raised full-year guidance, four straight earnings beats, and a normalized cash-flow picture roughly $277M better than the reported figure suggests, against a price 32% below the 52-week high and a multiple of 14x next-year earnings.
