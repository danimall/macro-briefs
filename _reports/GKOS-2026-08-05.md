## Executive Summary

Glaukos is executing one of the cleaner category-creation stories in small-cap medtech. Q2 2026 revenue of $185.610M grew 49.5% year over year, GAAP gross margin expanded to 81.7% from 78.3%, and management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $680-700M from $620-635M — roughly a 10% increase to the midpoint. Consensus has followed: current-year loss estimates narrowed 43.5% over 90 days (to $(0.29) from $(0.51)), next-quarter consensus flipped positive to $0.11, and FY2027 estimates rose 13.2% in 30 days. The balance sheet carries no funded debt, $289.3M of cash, short-term investments and restricted cash, and a 5.04x current ratio. On the arithmetic, the guidance raise outpaced the share price: enterprise value to FY2026E revenue is now approximately 14.1x versus roughly 14.4x at our prior review in mid-July, so the stock is marginally cheaper on forward sales despite a 7% higher price.

The reason we are stepping back from three consecutive BUY calls is a specific, newly surfaced binary. iDose TR generated approximately $74M in Q2 — about 62% of U.S. Glaucoma revenue and roughly 40% of total company revenue — and it is reimbursed under a temporary CPT code with physician fees published by only five of seven Medicare Administrative Contractors. During Q2 2026, five of the seven MACs issued proposed Local Coverage Determinations setting coverage criteria for iDose TR. The comment period closed July 4, 2026; nothing has been finalized, no 8-K has disclosed a resolution, and the proposed criteria themselves are not disclosed in any filing we reviewed. Glaukos's own five-year revenue history contains a 3.8% decline in 2022 that management attributes directly to a CMS physician-fee cut on trabecular bypass stents, so this risk is demonstrated rather than theoretical. Product concentration has increased, not diversified, as iDose TR has scaled.

The second reason is arithmetic on the guidance shape. H1 2026 revenue was $336.181M. The $690M guidance midpoint implies H2 of $353.8M, or a quarterly average of roughly $177M — below Q2's actual $185.6M, in a business management describes as seasonally strongest in Q4. Part of that is structurally explicable (Photrexa's commercial availability concludes by the end of Q3, with Epioxa at only ~$11M in Q2 and its J-code effective July 1), and part is likely conservatism, echoed by the COO's "we want to make sure that we're staying somewhat conservative here." Either way, the sequential trajectory is set to break for at least one quarter. Combining a base case of $190 (+13.0%) against a bear case near $110 (-34.6%), with the swing factor genuinely unmodelable, produces reward-to-risk below 0.4:1 to base and roughly 1.15:1 to bull. That is a hold, not an add.

## Company Overview

Glaukos describes itself as an ophthalmic pharmaceutical and medical technology company focused on "dropless" platform therapies for glaucoma, corneal disorders and retinal disease — replacing daily topical drops, where chronic non-adherence is the clinical problem, with physician-administered implants and procedures. It reports as a single operating segment with three revenue franchises.

| Franchise | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. Glaucoma | $118.549M | $72.282M | +64% |
| International Glaucoma | $36.631M | $31.251M | +17% |
| Corneal Health | $30.430M | $20.587M | +48% |
| **Total** | **$185.610M** | **$124.120M** | **+49.5%** |

Geographically, the U.S. contributed $146.739M (79.1%) and international $38.871M (20.9%). Five platforms are in commercial use or development: iStent micro-invasive surgical devices (first launched 2012, the company created the MIGS category), iDose TR sustained-release intracameral pharmaceuticals (FDA-approved December 2023), iLink bio-activated corneal cross-linking (Epioxa approved October 2025), iLution transdermal, and Retina XR bio-erodible sustained release. Cumulative R&D investment since 2018 exceeds $900M.

Manufacturing for iStent and iDose is concentrated at a single ~120,000 sq ft campus in San Clemente, California, with bioactivation systems in Burlington, Massachusetts and drug formulation at third parties in the U.S. and Germany. A 200,000 sq ft R&D/manufacturing facility in Huntsville, Alabama, budgeted at more than $80M of capital expenditure over a multi-year project, begins construction in 2026 and would eventually diversify that single point of failure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2021 $294.011M; FY2022 $282.862M (−3.8%); FY2023 $314.711M (+11.3%); FY2024 $383.481M (+21.9%); FY2025 $507.442M (+32.3%); FY2026 guidance $680-700M (+34-38%). H1 2026 was $336.181M, up 45.7%, with Q2 growing 23.3% sequentially from a derived Q1 of $150.571M. The 2022 decline is the most instructive data point in the series for risk assessment, because its cause — a Medicare physician fee-schedule cut on stent implantation in the ambulatory surgery center setting — is the same category of event now pending on iDose TR.

**Margins and operating leverage.** Q2 2026 GAAP gross margin was 81.7% (non-GAAP 84.5%; the gap is Avedro developed-technology amortization of $4.8M plus Mobius $0.5M). Because production volumes remain low relative to capacity, a significant share of per-unit cost is fixed manufacturing overhead, which is the mechanical source of continued gross-margin leverage as volumes scale.

Operating leverage below the gross line, however, is essentially absent by choice. Q2 SG&A was $116.060M (+39%), R&D $51.301M (+40%), acquired IPR&D $1.5M — total opex of $168.861M, or 91.0% of revenue. GAAP operating loss was $(17.267)M and non-GAAP $(7.589)M. Working the incremental: revenue rose $61.490M year over year, gross profit rose $54.370M, and operating expenses rose $48.948M, so the GAAP operating loss improved only $5.422M. Roughly 10% of incremental gross profit reached the operating line (about 15% on a non-GAAP basis). The path to profitability is a management decision variable, not an imminent mechanical outcome, and the compensation plan contains no profitability or return metric to constrain it (Net Sales, Operating Expenses and Pipeline Product Development are the three measures; Net Sales is company-selected).

Stock-based compensation was $28.865M in Q2 and $48.009M in H1, equal to 14.3% of H1 revenue versus 13.4% a year earlier. Of the Q2 SG&A increase, $9.4M was incremental stock compensation, the majority tied to performance awards that vested during the quarter; the company characterizes roughly $15M of the quarter's expense as a one-time performance-award trigger. Unamortized SBC at June 30 was $122.1M.

**FY2025 gross-margin discontinuity — flagged, not carried as a fact.** Reported FY2025 gross profit was $282.761M on $507.442M (55.7%), against $289.5M on $383.481M (75.5%) in FY2024 — gross profit fell in absolute dollars while revenue rose $124M. Reconstructing cost of sales ($224.681M full year against only $51.212M in H1) leaves an unexplained residual of roughly $113M, corroborated by net intangibles falling ~$121.5M during FY2025 against ~$25-30M of normal amortization and ~$23M of additions, and by the June 30, 2026 intangibles table showing Photrexa developed technology written down to $7.801M gross while a new finite-lived "Epioxa developed technology" of $111.700M gross appeared on a six-year life. The most plausible reading is a non-cash write-down of the Photrexa asset upon Epioxa's approval — recognition of deliberate internal cannibalization rather than demand deterioration. An alternative reading, which we cannot exclude, is a broader downward revision to total corneal-health cash flows; Photrexa lost orphan-drug exclusivity in 2023. We did not read the audited FY2025 line item, so the caption and amount remain unverified. The practical implication is narrow but important: any FY2025-based margin or profitability screen on GKOS is measuring a one-time non-cash charge. Ex-charge, FY2025 gross margin was approximately 78.0%, and Q2 2026's 81.7% confirms the underlying direction is up. Leverage, liquidity and asset-turnover inputs are unaffected.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was +$2.297M versus $(11.542)M a year earlier — genuine progress. Composition matters: net loss $(38.160)M, non-cash add-backs of $75.5M of which SBC was $48.009M (64%), and working capital consumed $35.0M dominated by a $31.912M receivables build. Less $6.697M of capex, free cash flow was $(4.400)M. We will not annualize a half-year that includes the Q1 bonus payout (accrued bonuses fell from $25.270M to $15.827M), a seasonally soft Q1, and a non-recurring performance-award SBC trigger. As a labeled sensitivity only: if H2 revenue lands at the guidance-implied ~$354M with stable margins and no further receivables build, operating cash flow could turn modestly positive; capex is explicitly guided higher for 2026 than 2025, and Huntsville absorbs cash from 2026 through 2029. The honest characterization is that operating cash flow is positive only because equity compensation equal to 14.3% of revenue is added back, and shares outstanding rose from 57.539M to 58.886M in six months (+2.3%).

**Receivables — investigated and benign so far.** Net accounts receivable rose to $138.900M from $108.608M at year-end and roughly $60.7M at end-2024. Computing days sales outstanding on quarterly rather than annual revenue avoids the growth artifact: Q2 2026 is 68.1 days ($138.900M ÷ $185.610M × 91) versus approximately 66.6 days at December 2025 on an estimated ~$150M Q4. DSO is roughly flat. Supporting evidence: the allowance fell to 6.1% of gross receivables from 7.1%; the H1 provision for doubtful accounts was $0.986M versus $4.067M a year earlier; write-offs were "not significant"; no single customer exceeds 10% of net receivables. Management attributes the build to mix — iDose TR and Epioxa carry both extended payment terms and higher price per unit. The counter-consideration is that extended terms are themselves a commercial concession, and the filings do not specify their length. DSO above roughly 75 days on sustained growth would reverse this benign read; it is the single most useful leading indicator to monitor.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30: cash $114.485M, short-term investments $171.741M, restricted cash $3.115M ($289.341M total); current assets $515.778M against current liabilities $102.329M (5.04x); total liabilities $238.035M against equity $682.486M (0.35x). No funded debt — the 2.75% convertible notes due 2027 were fully exchanged, converted or redeemed in 2024. Debt-like obligations are a $68.822M finance lease (Aliso Viejo headquarters) and $37.058M of operating leases, plus an $8.714M cash contingent-consideration liability from Mobius. Accumulated deficit is $971.290M. Federal NOLs of $546.9M and federal R&D credits of $56.3M are a real but contingent asset under a full valuation allowance, subject to §382 limitation risk.

**Earnings surprise record.** Three beats of 34.9%, 35.3% and 39.1% against non-GAAP loss estimates, and one 39.9% miss in the December 2025 quarter (actual $(0.28) versus $(0.20) estimate). The miss landed in the seasonally strongest quarter, which is a modest caution on visibility rather than a pattern.

**Financial Health: 6.3.** No funded debt, five-times current-ratio coverage, ~$289M liquid against approximately breakeven operating cash flow, and $25.8M of H1 option-exercise and ESPP proceeds. Offsetting: negative free cash flow, cash conversion dependent on adding back equity compensation, dilution running ~2.3% per half-year, and rising capex commitments. Marginally below the prior 6.5.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the strongest element of the case and is corroborated across three independent inputs: reported revenue (+49.5% in Q2, +45.7% in H1), management guidance (raised $60-65M mid-year), and consensus revisions (current-year loss narrowed 43.5% in 90 days; next quarter to +$0.11 from +$0.01; FY2027 to $0.50 from $0.44 thirty days ago).

The pipeline delivered two events of genuine economic consequence. First, in January 2026 the FDA approved a labeling supplement permitting **unlimited re-administration** of iDose TR in patients maintaining a healthy cornea. iDose TR is designed to deliver therapy for up to three years; unlimited re-administration converts a bounded per-patient event into a potentially recurring stream in a chronic progressive disease, materially raising lifetime value per converted patient. Two caveats: the label carries a clinical gating condition and no disclosure quantifies what share of patients will qualify at year three, and re-administration frequency is precisely the parameter the pending LCDs could constrain. Second, Epioxa became commercially available in March 2026 with a permanent J-code (J2789) assigned April 15 and effective July 1 — the first and only FDA-approved epithelium-on corneal cross-linking therapy for keratoconus, in a population largely below Medicare age and therefore commercially reimbursed.

Beyond those: the iStent infinite PMA pivotal trial in mild-to-moderate glaucoma completed enrollment in Q4 2025 (a label expansion into a much larger patient pool); PRESERFLO MicroShunt 510(k) pivotal enrollment completed July 2026 after the prior pathway failed to secure approval; iDose TRIO Phase 3b and iLution blepharitis Phase 2 enrollments completed; Retina XR first-in-human enrollment completed; a keratoconus screening device planned for 2H 2026 as demand generation.

Two qualifications temper the score. First, iDose TR growth is masking a stagnant legacy base: the FY2025 10-K describes non-iDose U.S. glaucoma revenue as declining modestly under the November 2024 LCDs that confirmed non-coverage for MIGS-on-MIGS combinations, and the Q2 2026 MD&A describes only "modest growth" in non-iDose products. Second, guidance embeds deceleration. Applying last year's H2/H1 uplift of +19.9% to H1 2026 would produce ~$403M and a full year near $739M, well above the top of the range — but the 2025 ratio was itself inflated by the iDose launch ramp and is not a clean comparator, so we present it as a sensitivity, not a forecast. What is unambiguous is that the midpoint implies quarterly revenue below Q2's level for the average of H2, with three named headwinds: the Photrexa wind-down through Q3, operationalizing Epioxa's J-code, and LCD uncertainty. A small tailwind to underlying growth: the Ivantis-related royalty stream expired April 26, 2025, so product growth is modestly better than the headline suggests.

**Growth Prospects: 7.5.** Multi-year 35-45% revenue growth with a broad, funded pipeline and two high-value regulatory wins already banked, discounted for a guided sequential air pocket and a stagnant non-iDose base.

- Enterprise value = $9.91B − $289.3M liquid + $68.8M finance lease ≈ **$9.69B**
- **EV / FY2026E revenue** at the $690M midpoint = **14.0x**
- **EV / TTM revenue** ($507.442M + $336.181M − $230.784M = $612.839M) = **15.8x**
- **Forward P/E**: FY2027 consensus EPS of $0.50 implies **336x**. Earnings provide no valuation anchor.
- Trailing EPS is $(3.28) and the quoted P/E of −52.5 is not interpretable.

Peer comparables in Health Care Equipment are heterogeneous and mostly profitable (median P/E 34.8: MASI 126.7, PODD 34.8, GMED 18.8, with BRKR and BAX loss-making), so cross-sectional P/E is unusable here. The relevant benchmark is revenue multiples for high-growth, high-gross-margin medtech, where 10-15x is the standard band for 40%+ growers and 5-8x for mature assets. At 14.0x forward revenue on 34-38% guided growth decelerating toward roughly 27% in FY2027, Glaukos sits in the upper half of that band with no earnings floor.

Two observations cut in the bull's favor. The multiple compressed slightly since our July review — at $156.83 and the prior $620-635M guidance, EV/FY2026E was approximately 14.4x, versus 14.0x now — so the guidance raise more than absorbed the price move. And all thirteen covering analysts sit above spot: low target $172, mean $193.6, median $193.0, high $215.0. But the guardrail applies: analyst targets function as floors only when a stock is cheap relative to improving estimates. At 14x revenue with no earnings, the targets embed the same growth extrapolation as the price and are not independent confirmation. The recorded shift of one analyst from buy to hold after the print is consistent with valuation-driven caution rather than thesis change.

The estimate-revision signal deserves the same discipline. A 43.5% narrowing of a loss estimate and a 13.2% lift to a $0.50 out-year figure are directionally strong, but the stock is not cheap against those estimates, which is where the revision signal does its work. This is not the classic cheap-inflection setup.

## Competitive Landscape

Glaukos created the MIGS category in 2012 and is the first mover in intracameral sustained-release glaucoma pharmaceuticals, with 24 peer-reviewed iDose TR publications and vertically integrated manufacturing. Gross-margin expansion from 78.3% to 81.7% year over year is consistent with pricing power plus fixed-cost absorption rather than share purchased with discounts.

Trajectory, which matters more than level, is mixed. The company's own FY2026 guidance considerations name "combo-cataract MIGS competition globally" and "the continued estimated impact on U.S. Glaucoma volumes related to professional fee reimbursement for combination-cataract trabecular bypass surgery versus other more invasive alternatives." That is an explicit statement that relative reimbursement economics are steering surgeons toward competing, more invasive procedures in the segment where Glaukos already lost share in 2022-2023. Non-iDose U.S. glaucoma products show only modest growth. Competitors are described as large diversified companies with greater resources and established payer relationships.

The most interesting competitive thread is SpyGlass Pharma, an Aliso Viejo-based developer of an intraocular-lens-mounted glaucoma drug-delivery platform — the same clinical problem as iDose TR, in the combination-cataract setting where Glaukos is already pressured. Glaukos is the **plaintiff** in *Glaukos v. SpyGlass Pharma and Long Doan* (C.D. Cal.), alleging that a former Glaukos senior clinical leader uploaded more than 13,000 company documents, including more than 11,000 confidential and trade-secret files, before joining SpyGlass. Glaukos has been winning procedurally: an injunction was entered December 11, 2025 after the court found Glaukos likely to prevail and at risk of irreparable harm, and on July 14, 2026 the court denied both motions to dismiss in their entirety. Trial is set for January 26, 2027; litigation expense was $3.3M in Q2 and $4.0M in H1. Defendants have counterclaimed, including under California statutes restricting employee non-compete restraints, and Glaukos's own risk factors state that its U.S. employees, including senior management, are not subject to non-competition agreements. Externally sourced and unconfirmed: SpyGlass secured an add-on Category III CPT code in May 2026 and appears to trade on Nasdaq. A Category III code establishes a billing pathway, not coverage or payment amounts. A preliminary-injunction finding is not a merits verdict, and we do not treat this litigation as a durable moat.

The shift in revenue mix toward iDose TR is not diversification. Single-product dependence has increased, and the product in question sits on a temporary reimbursement code under active review. Customer concentration, by contrast, is genuinely low — no customer exceeds 10% of net receivables.

**Competitive Position: 7.0**, reduced from 8.0. The new-category franchises are strengthening; the legacy base is flat under adverse coverage policy, and a direct competitor has secured its own reimbursement pathway.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The proposed iDose TR Local Coverage Determinations (dominant).** The proposed criteria are not disclosed in the filings, and iDose TR is roughly 40% of the company. Precedent runs both ways: in November 2024 five of seven MACs finalized non-coverage for MIGS-on-MIGS combinations, which the company says disrupted ordering patterns and may have adversely affected U.S. glaucoma sales in 2024 and 2025; but in 2023 a proposed non-coverage LCD covering iAccess procedures was adopted and then reversed. That the current proposals set criteria rather than blanket non-coverage is less severe than the 2023 precedent. The quieter bear path is criteria — prior-therapy-failure requirements, IOP thresholds, restrictions on concurrent procedures or on re-administration frequency — that narrow the addressable pool without denying coverage, and are harder to reverse. Content, timing and severity are unknown; this should be modeled as neither a certainty nor a non-event.

**2. Structural reimbursement exposure.** Both iDose TR and iStent infinite sit on temporary CPT codes, and the filings warn that transition to permanent Category I codes could reduce physician and facility payment, citing the 2022 iStent precedent that produced an actual revenue decline. Approximately 80% of U.S. iDose TR and iStent procedures occur in ASCs. CMS's 2027 proposed rules maintain APC assignments and generally maintain ASC rates for Glaukos procedures but include modest physician-fee reductions for several Category I ophthalmology codes; the filing clarifies these do not affect iDose TR or iStent infinite. Germany is cited as a specific international reimbursement challenge.

**3. Product-transition execution.** Photrexa availability concludes by the end of Q3 2026, and management anticipates potential disruption in U.S. Corneal Health during 2026. Corneal Health was $30.430M in Q2 with Epioxa at only ~$11M, so the Q3 air pocket is arithmetically real, not merely conservative guidance language.

**4. Single-site manufacturing.** Nearly all implanted device products — the large majority of revenue — are manufactured, inspected, packaged, released and shipped from one San Clemente campus, with a risk factor explicitly naming earthquake, fire, flood and the possibility of losing or being unable to renew California property insurance. Several third-party suppliers are sole-source. Huntsville only begins construction in 2026.

**5. Governance and personnel.** No non-competition agreements for U.S. employees including senior management — the precise structural vulnerability at issue in the SpyGlass matter. Anti-takeover architecture is comprehensive (classified board, supermajority removal for cause, no written consent, 5,000,000 blank-check preferred, Delaware §203, exclusive forum), which reduces the probability of a takeover premium.

**6. Smaller items.** A payroll-withholding administration error surfaced in Q2 non-GAAP reconciliations ($0.7M provision plus $0.369M interest) — immaterial, remediated, with disclosure controls concluded effective, but worth monitoring as a back-office strain indicator during rapid scaling and a major system integration. The 10-Q also cites the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruption as a potential logistics and raw-material cost factor, and notes the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariffs alongside new tariffs on certain patented pharmaceuticals and APIs; company exposure is described as limited given primarily U.S. sourcing, though Photrexa/Epioxa formulations are made in the U.S. and Germany.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 51.3% against 51.4% one-year realized — fairly priced, and down sharply from 61.5% at the July 19 snapshot as the earnings event resolved. Term structure is flat (51.3% near, 51.4% at six months), which is what an undated binary looks like: the market is distributing uncertainty evenly rather than concentrating it in a dated expiry. Put/call open-interest ratio rose from 1.24 to 2.09 while volume skew stayed call-heavy at −3.3%, a combination consistent with holders accumulating downside protection on a position they are not exiting. The nearest expiry (August 21) prices a ±8.5% move; September 18 prices ±13.8%. Short interest is 9.7% of float at 5.4 days to cover — uncrowded enough that a bad LCD outcome would not be cushioned by a squeeze, and not high enough to create one on good news. The accumulation of protection alongside a fairly priced, flat vol surface is consistent with an unresolved, undated regulatory binary.

**Risk Factors: 4.0.** A binary regulatory event on ~40% of revenue with undisclosed content and unknown timing, single-site manufacturing concentration, rising product concentration, temporary codes on both lead products, a Q3 transition air pocket, and no earnings floor beneath a 14x revenue multiple.

## Management Quality

Leadership has been stable — no Item 5.02 8-K back to December 2024 — and delivery against the top line has been strong (five-year TSR index 150.03 versus a peer group at 77.88, roughly +50% against −22%). Capital structure decisions have been sound: the 2.75% converts were fully retired in 2024, leaving no funded debt, and the balance sheet funds a 45%-growth business without a raise.

Three items pull the score toward average. First, the incentive design uses Net Sales, Operating Expenses and Pipeline Product Development, with Net Sales company-selected, and contains no profitability, cash-flow or return-on-capital measure — for a company reinvesting roughly 90% of incremental gross profit at 14x revenue, that is an alignment gap. CEO total compensation rose 33% to $12.03M in FY2025 while the TSR index fell 24.7%, though compensation actually paid did fall from $25.16M to $9.99M, so the mark-to-market mechanism functioned.

Second, insider activity is one-sided. Across Form 4 filings from April through July 31, 2026 there were **no open-market purchases by any officer or director**. The CFO monetized 40,000 option shares in four tranches at successively higher prices ($140, $150, $155, $160) against a $38.68 strike and adopted a 10b5-1 plan on June 9 for up to 20,000 shares commencing September 8; the President/COO sold 19,573 shares outright at $143.54 on May 1; the Chief Development Officer sold repeatedly and adopted a plan June 12 for 15,657 shares commencing September 16. We weight this modestly. Option monetization against a low strike with finite expiry, and concentration-risk management for executives whose net worth sits in one small-cap name, is a sufficient explanation, and it co-occurs with sharply rising estimates and a $60-65M guidance raise — conditions under which selling is low-signal. The CEO, who holds the largest disclosed executive stake, made no discretionary sales. The residual signal is the absence of buying during a period when management was raising guidance materially; we draw no inference about management's private view of the LCD outcome.

Third, equity is doing a large share of the compensation work — SBC at 14.3% of revenue and 64% of non-cash add-backs — which shifts real cost from cash flow to existing owners at roughly 2.3% per half-year.

**Management Quality: 5.5.**

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($235, +39.8%).** Final LCDs adopt coverage criteria that preserve physician discretion, removing the overhang and re-rating a business already growing 45%+. Unlimited iDose TR re-administration converts a three-year therapy into a recurring per-patient annuity in a chronic disease, extending the growth runway well beyond the current installed base. Epioxa scales on its permanent J-code against >125M covered commercial lives (company claim, unverified) in a largely undiagnosed keratoconus population, with a screening device launching in 2H 2026 to generate demand. iStent infinite's PMA in mild-to-moderate glaucoma expands the label into a much larger pool. Gross margin continues climbing from 81.7% toward 85% on fixed-cost absorption, and non-GAAP operating profit crosses into positive territory in FY2027 as opex growth decelerates below revenue growth. Guidance proves conservative — H2 at last year's seasonal uplift would imply ~$739M against a $700M cap. FY2027 revenue reaches $950M and the market pays 14.5x forward sales.

**Bear case ($110, −34.6%).** Final LCDs impose prior-therapy-failure requirements, IOP thresholds, or limits on re-administration and concurrent procedures. iDose TR growth halves without any formal coverage denial, and because the legacy MIGS base is already flat under the November 2024 LCDs, total growth falls to mid-teens. The Q3 Photrexa-to-Epioxa transition proves more disruptive than "transient," and the guided H2 sequential decline extends into 2027. With no earnings, a decelerating revenue line derates from 14x toward 8x — mature-medtech territory — which mechanically halves the equity even on modestly higher revenue. Receivables DSO drifts above 75 days as extended terms are used to sustain volume, converting a benign working-capital story into a revenue-quality question. ## Conclusion

The score trajectory here has been strongly positive — 5.2 in March to 6.6 in July, with three consecutive BUY calls — and the Q2 print did nothing to reverse it operationally. Revenue accelerated to +49.5%, gross margin expanded, guidance rose 10%, consensus followed, and enterprise value to forward revenue actually compressed from roughly 14.4x to 14.0x despite a 7% higher share price. On the standard test of whether price action has outrun fundamentals, it has not.

What has changed is the risk composition. Five of seven Medicare Administrative Contractors have proposed coverage criteria for a product that now generates roughly 40% of revenue and sits on a temporary CPT code; the criteria are undisclosed, the timing is unknown, and the company's own 2022 revenue decline demonstrates that reimbursement policy can turn growth negative here. Simultaneously, the raised guidance implies H2 quarterly revenue below Q2's actual level, part of which is the structural Photrexa exit rather than sandbagging. With no earnings beneath a 14x revenue multiple, the base case sits ~13% above spot against ~35% downside if a regulator writes restrictive criteria — reward-to-risk below 0.4:1.

That combination — intact growth, unfavorable asymmetry, unmodelable binary — is a hold. We would own Glaukos through the LCD resolution rather than sell into an unresolved event, and we would add either on confirmation that final coverage criteria preserve physician discretion, or on a pullback toward $130-140 where the multiple leaves room for the outcome to disappoint.

**Overall score: 5.86. HOLD.**
