## Executive Summary

Occidental Petroleum completed the $9.7B sale of OxyChem to Berkshire Hathaway in early 2026 and used proceeds to retire $5.4B of debt. The stock has rallied 51% from its December 2025 low of ~$38.92 to $56.87, driven by the deleveraging event and a geopolitically-fueled surge in oil prices. Consensus EPS estimates have risen from $1.64 ninety days ago to $4.20 today, reflecting both the OxyChem gain and elevated oil price assumptions.

OXY is now a pure-play oil and gas company with zero commodity hedges, and the current oil environment is defined by a 10-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire that has already crashed WTI below $83. The stock trades at 42x trailing EPS of $1.35 and approximately 13.5x a 2026 estimate that embeds oil prices well above current spot. Remaining structural risks — $15B of debt, $800M/year in Berkshire preferred dividends, a $2.3B Tronox contingent tax liability, and regulatory/environmental overhang — are material. The consensus analyst distribution: 15 holds, 2 sells, 1 strong sell vs. 8 buys. With the easy gains from the OxyChem catalyst already captured, the risk/reward at $56.87 skews unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Occidental Petroleum is one of the largest U.S. oil producers, generating approximately 1,434 Mboe/d in 2025 (up 8% YoY). Operations span the Permian Basin (786 Mboe/d, ~55% of total), DJ Basin/Rockies (284 Mboe/d), Gulf of America deepwater (132 Mboe/d), and international assets in Oman, Algeria, Qatar, and the UAE (232 Mboe/d combined). The company holds 2.9 million net Permian acres and accounts for approximately 10% of total Permian Basin oil production.

Following the January 2, 2026 sale of OxyChem, Occidental is structured as a pure-play upstream oil and gas company with carbon management assets (STRATOS direct air capture facility, NET Power stake). The company retains a 40.9% economic interest in Western Midstream Partners (WES). Berkshire Hathaway owns approximately 29% of common shares plus $10B in 8% preferred stock.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings

Revenue declined from $36.6B in 2022 to $21.6B in 2025, a 41% contraction driven entirely by commodity prices. WTI averaged $64.81 in 2025 vs. $75.72 in 2024 (-14%). Production growth from the CrownRock acquisition (closed August 2024, adding ~100 Mboe/d) partially offset the price headwind, but net income fell to $1.6B from $2.4B in 2024.

Trailing EPS of $1.35 (or ~$1.61 diluted for FY2025) against a $56.87 share price produces a 42x P/E, among the highest in the E&P peer group. Adjusted EPS for the first nine months of 2025 was $1.90, implying a Q4 run-rate of roughly $0.31 (which matched reported adjusted Q4 EPS, beating the $0.19 consensus by 63%).

### Operating Efficiency

Lease operating expense per Boe fell to $8.94 in 2025 from $9.75 in 2024 and $10.48 in 2023 — a 15% two-year improvement from operational efficiencies and lower Oman energy costs. NGL realizations improved to 32% of WTI from 28%. These operational improvements enhance OXY's competitiveness within the E&P sector.

### Balance Sheet — Post-OxyChem

Pre-transaction (12/31/2025), long-term debt stood at $20.6B with a D/E ratio of 2.34x and an Altman Z-Score of 1.22 (distress zone). Post-transaction, $5.4B was repaid in January 2026, bringing principal debt to approximately $15B. Pro-forma D/E drops to roughly 0.42x, and available liquidity (cash + revolver) exceeds $6.1B. The debt maturity profile is exceptionally long-dated: only $24M due in 2026, $48M in 2027, $14M in 2028, and $367M in 2029. There is no near-term refinancing risk.

However, $15B of absolute debt remains substantial. Servicing this debt plus the $679-800M annual preferred dividend to Berkshire Hathaway represents a fixed cash drain that compresses free cash flow available to common shareholders. At 2025 oil prices ($64.81 WTI), FCF was $4.1B on $10.5B OCF less $6.4B capex. Subtract ~$1.5B in total dividend obligations (common + preferred), and the equity free cash flow yield is modest relative to peers.

### Forward Estimates

Current-year EPS has been revised from $1.64 (90 days ago) to $4.20, a 156% increase. Next-quarter estimates surged 361% from $0.31 to $1.45. These revisions reflect two factors: (1) the OxyChem sale gain flowing through 2026 numbers, and (2) sharply higher oil price assumptions embedded in analyst models following the Hormuz crisis.

At $83 WTI (today's post-ceasefire price), OXY's 2026 EPS would likely come in below the $4.20 consensus, which probably embeds $90-100+ oil. At $70-75 WTI, EPS would compress toward $2.50-3.00. With zero commodity hedges, there is no floor.

## Growth Analysis

Organic production growth is minimal. The 2026 guidance targets ~1.45M boe/d, essentially flat with Q3 2025 run-rates, on capex of $5.5-5.9B. This is maintenance-level spending. The CrownRock acquisition provided a one-time production step-up in 2024-2025, but there is no comparable organic growth driver for 2026-2027.

STRATOS (direct air capture) represents a long-duration call option on carbon credit markets and 45Q tax credits. The facility completed construction in 2025 but has not yet demonstrated commercial-scale sequestration. The 10-K explicitly warns these technologies "may not perform as intended, may fail to achieve commercial viability, or may never develop to the stage necessary." Capital invested in midstream/STRATOS was approximately $720M in 2025. If the carbon credit market fails to materialize or tax credits are rescinded, impairment risk is material.

The company's reserves replacement ratio is adequate: 571 MMboe added vs. 523 MMboe produced in 2025, maintaining the reserve base of ~4,603 MMboe. This is maintenance, not growth.

## Competitive Landscape

OXY's competitive strengths include: (1) scale in the Permian (2.9M net acres, 10% of basin output), (2) decades of CO₂-EOR expertise (34 active floods), (3) a diversified international portfolio with long-dated concessions (Oman Block 53 extended to 2050), and (4) a first-mover position in DAC technology.

Weaknesses relative to peers: (1) significantly higher leverage than EOG, FANG, or DVN, even post-OxyChem, (2) the Berkshire preferred structure extracts $800M/year before common shareholders, (3) zero commodity hedges vs. peers that typically hedge 30-50% of production, and (4) the STRATOS/DAC program is a capital-consuming call option with uncertain payoff, while peers deploy FCF toward buybacks.

The 29% Berkshire ownership provides a price floor of sorts — the market perceives Buffett's involvement as a strategic endorsement. But it also creates structural issues: preferred dividends that don't decline with oil prices, warrant exercises that dilute common shareholders (~55M shares added over 2024-2025), and a relationship where Berkshire extracts value through multiple channels (preferred dividends, OxyChem purchase, warrant exercise).

## Risk Assessment

### Commodity Price Risk (Critical)
OXY has zero hedges and no chemical earnings buffer. Every $5/bbl decline in WTI translates to approximately $2.6B in annual revenue impact (1.434M boe/d × 365 × $5). At current ~$83 WTI (post-ceasefire), earnings would be well above 2025 levels but potentially below the consensus $4.20 estimate. If ceasefire negotiations produce a durable framework and oil retreats to $70-75, earnings power converges toward $2.50-3.00 — a scenario the stock does not appear to price.

### Tronox Tax Liability ($2.3B Contingent)
The IRS disallowed Anadarko's $5.2B Tronox settlement deduction. OXY faces a potential $2.3B cash payment ($1.4B taxes + $0.9B interest). The matter is pending in U.S. Tax Court. No tax benefit has been recorded for the tentative refund. A $2.3B adverse ruling would consume roughly 15% of current liquidity and represent a 4.4% hit to market cap.

### IRS Review of $2.7B Tax Benefit
The IRS is reviewing OXY's 2022 legal entity reorganization that generated a $2.7B tax benefit. If disallowed, a material portion could be reversed. Combined with Tronox, total contingent tax exposure approaches $5B.

### Environmental Legacy Liabilities
Environmental remediation liabilities estimated at ~$1.9B. In 2024, a $925M pre-tax charge was recorded for the Passaic River (DASS) remediation. OXY retains legacy tort claims from OxyChem and has indemnification obligations to Berkshire for pre-closing liabilities.

### Share Dilution
Diluted shares grew from ~948M (Q1 2024) to ~1,003M (Q3 2025) — a 6% increase primarily from Berkshire warrant exercises (at $59.624 strike). At current prices below the strike, further dilution is paused, but if OXY rallies above $60, warrant exercises resume.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 82.2% versus 33.1% historical volatility reflects extreme implied uncertainty — the 49.1% spread and 91st percentile IV rank indicate the market is pricing a significant near-term binary event. The steep backwardation (82.2% near-term → 32.2% at 6-month) suggests this stress is event-specific rather than structural. Call-heavy positioning (P/C volume 0.56, OI 0.59) with unusual call activity in $53-55 strikes for April 17-24 expiry indicates speculative upside bets. The 6-month P/C ratio flips to 1.40, indicating longer-duration investors are buying puts, consistent with skepticism about sustained oil prices.

## Investment Thesis

STRATOS achieves commercial-scale sequestration and carbon credits gain regulatory support. Debt reduction continues toward the $14.3B target. ### Bear Case ($38-46)

STRATOS faces delays or impairment. The market reprices OXY as a leveraged E&P with above-peer leverage, preferred drains, and no hedges.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The primary catalyst is oil price normalization following a durable Hormuz ceasefire/framework. If weekend Iran-US talks produce concrete terms (25-30% probability), oil could retreat to $75-85. A secondary catalyst is the Tronox Tax Court ruling, though timing is uncertain.

**Timing window**: Oil-driven repricing would occur within 2-6 weeks of a framework agreement. Q1 2026 earnings (likely May 2026) would be the first opportunity for the market to reassess estimates. The ceasefire window is 10 days from April 17.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is negligible at 0.0% of float with only 114K shares short. The trade is completely uncrowded with essentially no squeeze risk. However, the absence of informed institutional short positioning despite apparent overvaluation warrants caution.

**Borrow and options signal**: Near-term IV at 82.2% makes put purchases expensive for immediate-expiry instruments. The 1-month IV at 35.2% is more reasonable. Borrow should be easy given negligible short interest.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Base downside target of $42-46 (at 14-15x $3.00 EPS under normalized oil). Current price $56.87. Downside of $11-15 (19-26%). Upside risk to $65-70 if oil spikes on ceasefire failure (15-23%). Risk/reward ratio approximately 1.2:1 to 1.5:1 — below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE**. The risk/reward ratio is insufficient at current prices and the timing of oil normalization is genuinely uncertain given 8 prior false signals. The 29% Berkshire ownership creates a soft floor. The better expression of a bearish oil view is through other instruments (oil futures, calls on less-leveraged E&Ps). OXY is overvalued but not shortable.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: OXY becomes interesting as a BUY if (1) the stock pulls back to $42-45 (14-15x a normalized $3.00 EPS), (2) WTI stabilizes at $80+ with a visible structural supply deficit, or (3) STRATOS demonstrates commercial-scale sequestration with verifiable revenue. The current setup — elevated price, elevated estimates, elevated oil — requires too many things to go right simultaneously.

**Timeline**: The AVOID thesis should hold for 3-6 months. The ceasefire window (10 days) and subsequent diplomatic timeline (weeks to months) will determine oil price trajectory. Q1 2026 earnings (May) will provide the first read on whether the $4.20 consensus is achievable. The Tronox Tax Court ruling has no specific date but could resolve in 2026.

## Conclusion

Occidental Petroleum has executed well on the OxyChem sale and debt reduction, and the operational improvements (15% LOE reduction, solid reserve replacement) are real. But at $56.87 the stock prices in a commodity environment that may not persist. The forward multiple of 13.5x depends on consensus estimates revised upward 156% in 90 days on geopolitically-elevated oil prices. With zero hedges and a ceasefire already pressuring oil prices, the risk/reward at current levels is unfavorable. AVOID until the commodity price outlook clarifies and the stock reprices accordingly.
