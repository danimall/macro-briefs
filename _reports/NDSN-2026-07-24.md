**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nordson is a diversified precision-technology company built around niche leadership in fluid dispensing, coating, test/inspection, and single-use medical components. FY2025 (ending October 31) was a trough: revenue rose 3.8% but declined 2.5% organically, with acquisitions carrying the top line. The recovery is now underway in FY2026. First-half revenue is up 8.6% total and 6.6% organic, and the Advanced Technology Solutions (ATS) segment (precision dispensing and inspection equipment sold into the semiconductor and advanced-packaging supply chain) has swung from –11.2% organic in FY2024 to +13.8% in H1 FY2026. Backlog is up roughly 18% organically year over year, the cleanest forward signal in the data set. Management guides FY2026 adjusted EPS to $11.30–$11.80 against FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS of $8.51.

The business quality is not in dispute: gross margin near 55%, operating margin around 25.5%, FCF margin of 23.7%, and a razor/razor-blade recurring-consumables layer under the capital equipment. The debate is about price. At $292, the stock trades at roughly 25x the midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EPS and about 24x the FY2027 consensus of $12.42. Against a peer median P/E of ~32x, that looks reasonable, but the peer set is heterogeneous, and the more relevant comparison is that Nordson's own estimate revisions have gone flat (+0.1% on the current year over 90 days) even as the stock has run ~33% off its August low and sits within ~5% of its 52-week high. The re-rating that was available at $210 has largely happened.

We rate NDSN a HOLD (growth_hold). The cyclical recovery is real and confirmed by backlog and segment data, which supports owning the stock for continued earnings progression. But with the multiple in line with peers, estimate revisions stalled, a thin margin of safety on medical-segment goodwill, and a CFO transition mid-cycle, the setup is balanced. We would upgrade on either a pullback toward the low-$250s or a resumption of positive estimate-revision velocity, and downgrade if organic growth decelerates or an MFS impairment surfaces.

## Company Overview

Nordson (incorporated in Ohio in 1954; headquartered in Westlake, OH; approximately 8,200 employees) engineers and manufactures differentiated equipment for the precision dispensing, applying, and controlling of adhesives, coatings, polymers, sealants, and biomaterials, plus test/inspection and surface-treatment systems. It sells primarily through a direct global application-engineering sales force across 35+ countries, with roughly 67% of revenue generated outside the U.S. No single customer represents 10% or more of sales. The model layers recurring consumables, parts, and service (pulled through a large installed base) onto lumpier capital-equipment sales.

Three reportable segments, by FY2025 revenue mix:

- **Industrial Precision Solutions (IPS), ~48%**: adhesive dispensing (packaging, nonwovens/hygiene), industrial coatings, polymer processing, and precision agriculture (the ARAG acquisition). Highest-margin segment at roughly 37% segment EBITDA margin.
- **Medical and Fluid Solutions (MFS), ~30%**: interventional medical components (catheters, balloons, cannula) and engineered/single-use fluid solutions, materially expanded by the Atrion acquisition.
- **Advanced Technology Solutions (ATS), ~22%**: electronics dispense systems and test/inspection for semiconductors and PCBs. The most cyclical segment and currently the primary growth engine.

The competitive position rests on leadership niches in precision fluid dispensing, a large installed base generating recurring revenue, over 2,100 patents, and the direct application-engineering sales model. Strategy is organized under the "Ascend" framework (NBS Next portfolio segmentation, an "Owner Mindset" divisional structure). Fiscal year ends October 31, which matters for reading the quarterly cadence.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 delivered net income of $484M and diluted EPS of $8.51 (+5%). H1 FY2026 EPS was $4.47, up 23.5% year over year, aided partly by the absence of prior-year severance and lower acquisition step-up amortization. The segment breakdown is the point of interest: ATS organic growth ran –11.2% (FY2024) → +4.1% (FY2025) → +13.8% (H1 FY2026), and IPS organic swung from –5.1% (FY2025) to +4.1% (H1 FY2026).

Profitability and cash generation are elite. Segment EBITDA margin runs around 34%. FY2025 operating cash flow was $719M and FCF was $661M — a 23.7% FCF margin, well above net income, indicating high earnings quality — on low capex intensity (~2% of sales). ROE runs ~15.9%.

The balance sheet is the watch item. Goodwill grew from $1.8B (FY2022) to $3.3B (FY2025) through the ARAG and Atrion deals; goodwill plus intangibles now approximate $4.0B of $5.9B in total assets. Debt-to-equity sits around 1.94 and the Altman Z-score is roughly 2.0, in the "grey zone." That reflects acquisition financing, not operating distress. Management is deleveraging: gross debt fell from $2.0B to $1.895B by Q2 FY2026 (about $105M in H1), and the revolver was refinanced into a $1.2B facility maturing January 2031, removing the June 2026 term-loan maturity wall. The company remains in full compliance with leverage and interest-coverage covenants.

Capital allocation is balanced. FY2025 returned roughly $485M to shareholders ($179M dividends, $306M buybacks) while cutting debt $224M. Nordson is a Dividend Aristocrat; the quarterly dividend rose to $0.82 in FY2026, and a $500M buyback authorization from August 2025 had ~$599M remaining as of 4/30/2026. The 1.1% dividend yield is modest and roughly matches the peer median. The earnings-surprise record has softened at the margin: two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with the two most recent quarters (June and March 2026) missing by 0.5% and 2.2% respectively. These are small misses, but the pattern matters against a stock priced for delivery.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is a segment-level cyclical recovery rather than a broad secular acceleration. ATS is the engine, and its demand is tied to the semiconductor and advanced-packaging capex cycle. Nordson supplies precision dispensing and test/inspection equipment used in semiconductor packaging — a picks-and-shovels beneficiary of AI-infrastructure buildout, not a chip or AI company itself. That attribution matters: the demand is real and confirmed in hard data (ATS +13.8% organic in H1, Asia Pacific +16.4% and now ~33% of sales), but it carries the cyclicality of customer capex programs, which is exactly what produced the –11% ATS collapse a year earlier.

The strongest forward indicator is backlog, up roughly 18% organically year over year as of Q2 FY2026, with all three segments contributing. That underpins the FY2026 adjusted EPS guide, a meaningful step-up from the GAAP diluted print in FY2025 (though the adjusted figure excludes acquisition amortization and one-time items, so the two are not directly comparable). Europe is recovering (+10.8% in H1, FX-aided); the Americas are softer (+1.8%).

The diversification beyond industrial cyclicality is a structural positive. MFS provides secular, less-cyclical growth in interventional devices and single-use components — the strategic rationale for Atrion — and precision agriculture (ARAG) adds a data/software-enabled vector within IPS. The read-through from the broader industrial-machinery complex and semiconductor-packaging demand supports the durability of the near-term recovery.

The caution is that consensus estimate revisions have gone flat. Current-year EPS estimates moved +0.1% over 90 days and were unchanged over 30 days; next-year estimates rose +0.7% over 90 days. This is not the +30% revision velocity that flags a systematic underestimation of an inflection; it is a market that has already priced the recovery. Estimate revisions are one of the more reliable forward signals, and here they are directionally neutral. That tempers the growth score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $292, Nordson trades at roughly 25x the midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EPS ($11.55) and about 24x the FY2027 consensus. On trailing GAAP EPS the multiple is ~34x, but the trailing figure reflects a trough year, so the forward metric is the correct anchor. The peer comparison is favorable at first glance: peer median P/E is ~32x (IEX 32.1x, ITT 32.9x, FTV 35.2x, LECO 26.6x), which places Nordson at a discount on a headline basis. But the peer set is heterogeneous — Mueller Industries at 17.7x drags the range wide — and Nordson's low-teens organic growth in a cyclical rebound does not obviously justify a premium re-rating from here.

The core issue is that this is not a mispricing with an identifiable catalyst the market is missing. The inflection has been recognized: the stock is up ~33% from its August 2025 low and sits ~5% below its 52-week high of $307.74. With revisions flat, the consensus $319 mean / $331 median target reflects the same recovery view already embedded in the price. On an inflection stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x forward multiple, analyst targets would function as floors; here, with revisions stalled, the targets are more likely to be roughly fair rather than stale.

Applying skepticism to a "cheap-vs-peers" read is warranted: a modest discount to a heterogeneous peer median is a weak basis for a high valuation score, and cheapness relative to peers has historically been a poor standalone predictor without an independent catalyst. There is no absolute-yield support (1.1% dividend), no compressed multiple signaling a value opportunity, and no evidence of cycle-adjusted earnings power meaningfully above where consensus already sits. A 24–25x forward multiple on a business with mid-single-digit through-cycle organic growth, elite margins, and quality cash flows is a fair price, not a bargain. We score valuation below neutral: the multiple is defensible but offers little asymmetric upside, and the balance of risk is that any ATS deceleration compresses the multiple faster than earnings can offset.

## Competitive Landscape

Nordson holds durable niche leadership in fragmented precision-technology markets against numerous large, well-established competitors across bonding, sealing, coating, and inspection techniques. The moat is real: a large installed base pulling recurring consumables and service, deep application engineering, and an IP portfolio exceeding 2,100 patents. Gross margins near 55% and segment EBITDA margins around 34% corroborate genuine pricing power in the core dispensing franchises.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving on a cyclical basis. ATS is regaining share of customer capex as semiconductor and advanced-packaging demand recovers, and the geographic mix skew toward Asia Pacific (now ~33% of sales) confirms exposure to the fastest-growing end market. MFS provides a secular, less-cyclical counterweight, and precision agriculture adds a differentiated data-enabled growth lane. The company is not obviously gaining or losing structural share; it is a high-quality incumbent riding the cycle up, which argues for a competitive-position score modestly above neutral rather than a high mark.

One governance-adjacent data point bears on the competitive read: cumulative TSR of 127.4 versus a peer group at 207.3 over the proxy measurement window, despite record operating profit of $712M. Nordson has been a relative laggard, and the 2026 rally is in part correcting that gap. That underperformance is not evidence of eroding competitive position — it reflects the multiple compression during the trough — but it does caution against assuming the market will award a durable premium.

## Risk Assessment

**Acquisition-driven goodwill and leverage.** The FY2025 impairment test showed comfortable cushions (IPS 311% fair value over carrying value, ATS 205%), but MFS at 132% is the thinnest. MFS carries the largest goodwill (~$1.66B, mostly Atrion) and management noted near-term product start-up headwinds in the medical unit. An MFS impairment is the single most plausible negative surprise and is not fully priced.

**Tariffs and trade policy.** With 67% of sales international, manufacturing in China, and imported components, management repeatedly flags tariff exposure to cash flow and liquidity. This is a live macro risk given the current trade environment.

**Cyclicality.** ATS and IPS polymer processing are tied to customer capex, which whipsaws (–11% then +14% within two years in ATS). This is inherent to the business and partly priced, but the current multiple assumes the upswing continues.

**CFO transition amid executive turnover.** A cluster of 2025 Item 5.02 filings and the replacement of long-tenured CFO Joseph Kelley by Daniel Hopgood (confirmed by June 2026) warrants monitoring. No restatement or internal-control issues have surfaced — disclosure controls were affirmed effective as of 4/30/2026 — but a mid-cycle finance-leadership change during acquisition integration is a governance flag.

**FX and non-operating noise.** Material EUR/JPY/GBP/CNY exposure, managed via ~$921M notional cross-currency swaps. A newly listed minority investment (marked at $21.7M, locked up to Dec 2028) and FX remeasurement inject quarter-to-quarter volatility into non-operating "Other income" (+$12.4M H1 gain but –$9.8M in Q2 alone), which analysts should normalize.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 27.9% against 21.2% one-year historical volatility — modestly rich, meaning the market is pricing slightly elevated uncertainty. The put/call skew of 4.3% and a put/call volume ratio of 2.33 indicate some near-term put demand, though the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.50 is not defensive. Term structure is in normal contango (near 27.9% rising to 30.5% at six months), consistent with a non-crisis backdrop. Short interest is low at 3.7% of float (4.2 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded bear position nor a squeeze setup. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.0%. Net read: options positioning is broadly neutral with mild near-term hedging, neither confirming nor contradicting the fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($345–$360).** The ATS recovery has legs — semiconductor and advanced-packaging capex sustains the +14% organic growth, backlog converts cleanly, and FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at or above the $11.80 high end, with FY2027 pushing toward $13. Margin discipline plus ongoing buybacks drive EPS above consensus, estimate revisions turn positive, and the multiple holds at ~28x on rising forward earnings. The medical ramp normalizes, removing the MFS impairment overhang. This tracks the analyst high target of $345.

**Bear Case ($245–$260).** ATS is a cyclical segment near a local peak; a semiconductor-capex pause reverses the organic growth as sharply as it rose, and the two consecutive small EPS misses (Q1 and Q2 FY2026) are the leading edge of decelerating delivery. The MFS goodwill cushion (132%) proves too thin against medical start-up headwinds, triggering an impairment. Tariff costs pressure cash flow. The multiple compresses toward 20x on a spooked cycle read, and the stock retraces toward the analyst low of $260 or below, roughly 11–16% downside from current levels.

**Base Case ($305–$315).** The recovery continues at a decelerating pace, FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near the $11.55 midpoint, FY2027 reaches ~$12.40, and the multiple holds around 25x. The stock grinds modestly higher with the earnings progression but without a re-rating, since revisions are flat and the price already reflects the inflection. This implies roughly 5–8% upside plus a ~1.1% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Timeframe: 12 months.**

- **Base case target: $310.** 25x forward P/E on FY2027 consensus EPS of $12.42 = $310. Roughly 6% above the current $292, plus a ~1.1% dividend.
- **Bull case target: $348.** 28x on $12.42 forward EPS, assuming ATS momentum sustains and revisions turn positive. ~19% upside.
- **Bear case target: $255.** ~20.5x on flat-to-lower forward EPS if the cycle rolls over or an MFS impairment surfaces. ~13% downside.
- **Upside to base: +6%; upside to bull: +19%; downside to bear: –13%.** The near-symmetry of the risk/reward is why this is a hold rather than a buy.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A pullback toward the low-$250s (roughly 20x forward), restoring a margin of safety.
2. Current-year or next-year EPS estimate revisions turning positive by more than 3% over a 90-day window, confirming that consensus is chasing the inflection rather than sitting flat.
3. ATS organic growth sustaining double digits into H2 FY2026 with backlog holding above +15%, demonstrating the semiconductor-capex tailwind is durable rather than a single-quarter spike.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. ATS organic growth decelerating below mid-single digits, signaling the cycle has peaked.
2. An MFS goodwill impairment charge, or the FV/CV cushion narrowing below ~115% at the next annual test.
3. A third consecutive EPS miss, or FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below the $11.30 floor.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings release (late August 2026), which will confirm whether the backlog is converting and whether ATS momentum is holding into the back half.

## Conclusion

Nordson is a high-quality precision-technology franchise experiencing a genuine, data-confirmed cyclical recovery. The margin profile and the deleveraging balance sheet support the quality case. The problem is price. At ~25x forward guided EPS and ~24x FY2027 consensus — roughly in line with a heterogeneous peer median — the stock has already re-rated ~33% off its trough, estimate revisions are flat, and the risk/reward is balanced (~6% to base, ~19% to bull, ~13% to bear). The thin MFS goodwill cushion, two consecutive small earnings misses, and a CFO transition mid-integration are watch items rather than deal-breakers, but they cap the case for chasing here.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the score has drifted lower (6.5 → 5.7) as the multiple caught up to the fundamentals. Nothing in the current data reverses that: the operational story confirms owning the stock through the recovery, but the entry point is not compelling. We maintain **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale, and would become constructive on a pullback toward the low-$250s or a resumption of positive estimate-revision velocity.
