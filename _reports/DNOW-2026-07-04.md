**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

DNOW is a Houston-based distributor of pipe, valves, fittings (PVF), pumps, and process equipment serving the energy value chain and industrial end-markets. On November 6, 2025 it closed an all-stock acquisition of MRC Global, issuing ~82M new shares for roughly $1,763M of consideration net of cash and assumed debt. The deal roughly doubled the company's revenue base and removed its largest direct competitor from a fragmented market. Every element of the current investment case runs through this transaction and its integration.

The headline financials show revenue nearly doubling — Q1 2026 came in at $1,183M, up 97.5% year-over-year — while GAAP earnings collapsed into a loss. That loss is largely a non-cash artifact of purchase accounting: a $135M inventory fair-value step-up in Q4 2025 followed by $41M in Q1 2026, plus a LIFO increase and FX impairment. Stripping those out, the more concerning signal is that adjusted EBITDA margin compressed from 7.7% a year ago to 3.3% in Q1 2026, and adjusted diluted EPS fell to $0.01 from $0.22. The combined company is currently less profitable on a percentage basis than legacy DNOW, reflecting MRC's lower-margin mix and ERP-driven operational inefficiencies.

The central issue is execution risk around the MRC U.S. ERP system. Management publicly described that system as "world-class" on the pre-close November 5 call, then admitted roughly 3.5 months later (February 20, 2026) that it was "an obstacle" causing continuing negative operating and financial impacts, and withdrew 2026 guidance. The stock fell ~19% that day, and a plaintiff firm opened a securities investigation into the pre-February disclosures. Against this, the strategic thesis is coherent: MRC materially increases exposure to stable gas-utility replacement demand and to midstream growth tied to LNG and data-center power. Whether ERP remediation and synergy capture lift margins over the next several quarters is a binary outcome not yet visible in the numbers, which supports a HOLD.

## Company Overview

DNOW operates roughly 300 locations across the U.S., Canada, and select international markets under the DNOW and newly acquired MRC Global brands, with about 5,150 employees. It reports three geographic segments: United States (~83% of Q1 2026 revenue), International (~13%), and Canada (~4%).

The Q1 2026 end-market mix was Upstream 39%, Gas Utilities 23%, Downstream & Industrial 20%, and Midstream 18%. The strategic rationale for the MRC deal is to shift the mix away from cyclical upstream drilling activity toward more stable and structurally growing verticals. Gas Utilities demand is driven by aging infrastructure replacement — roughly a third of U.S. gas distribution lines are more than 40 years old — and is largely independent of commodity prices. Midstream benefits from LNG export buildout and natural-gas demand for AI and data-center power generation, with DNOW negotiating master service agreements for data-center cooling PVF.

Competitive advantages center on scale in a fragmented distribution market, supplier relationships that enable tariff pass-through, and digital platforms (DigitalNOW, MRCGO). The company generates no dividend and has grown partly through acquisition — Edge Controls, a $46M automation/SCADA deal in February 2026, was its 26th.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue transformation.** FY2024 revenue was ~$2.37B; FY2025 was ~$2.82B, capturing only about two months of MRC. Q1 2026, the first full combined quarter, delivered U.S. revenue up 108% and International up 133% year-over-year. Canada declined 18% on weaker project activity.

**Profitability.** FY2025 swung to a net loss of ~$89M from +$81M in 2024. Q4 2025 was the trough: revenue $959M, gross profit only $68M (7.1% margin), operating loss of −$170M, and a net loss of −$147M (−$0.95/share), driven mainly by the inventory step-up and a $12M FX impairment. Q1 2026 net loss was −$44M (−$0.24/share), including a $16M LIFO increase. These step-up charges are non-cash and will roll off, so GAAP losses should narrow through 2026. That makes GAAP figures largely uninformative in the near term; the metrics that matter are adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS, and both are weak — adjusted EBITDA of $39M in Q1 2026 versus $46M (7.7%) a year ago and $61M (6.4%) in Q4 2025.

**Margin compression is the crux.** Even excluding deal noise, the combined company is currently less profitable as a percentage of revenue than legacy DNOW. This reflects MRC's lower-margin business mix plus ERP inefficiencies that inflate safety stock, slow order processing, and require more labor. The entire bull case rests on synergy capture and ERP remediation lifting combined margins back toward legacy DNOW levels, and that has not yet appeared in reported results.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets more than doubled to ~$3.9B. Goodwill is $652M, intangibles $563M, and inventories $1.19B, all inflated by MRC consolidation. Equity is ~$2.14B. Net debt is $455M, or 2.3x TTM adjusted EBITDA — manageable but no longer the debt-free profile legacy DNOW carried. The new $850M ABL revolver (accordion to $1.35B) matures November 2030; $571M was drawn at March 31, 2026 with ~$263M availability. All borrowings float, and availability ties to a borrowing base. The company is in compliance with a springing 1.00:1.00 fixed-charge covenant.

**Cash flow is the near-term red flag.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was −$95M versus −$16M a year ago, driven by a $115M working-capital drain from deal-related severance and professional fees plus AR and inventory build. Despite this, the company repurchased $50M of stock and spent $46M on Edge Controls, funding the gap with $160M of net revolver borrowings. Cash fell to $116M. Deploying capital into buybacks and M&A while burning cash and drawing the revolver during an integration-stress period is defensible as opportunistic but increases leverage at a delicate moment.

## Growth Analysis

Reported revenue growth is dominated by the acquisition and does not represent organic momentum. Underneath the M&A, the demand signals are mixed. Upstream revenue rose 12% year-over-year in Q1 2026, but industry forecasts point to flat-to-lower 2026 upstream spend as producers stay capital-disciplined; the U.S. rig count is declining year-over-year at roughly 548. WTI recovered to ~$72/bbl from Q4 2025's ~$60, a modest tailwind.

The genuine growth stories are Midstream (Q1 2026 revenue up 70% year-over-year, tied to LNG export capacity and data-center power demand) and Gas Utilities (a multi-year replacement cycle largely decoupled from commodity prices). These are the verticals MRC most strengthens, and they align with credible secular themes — natural-gas infrastructure demand from AI/data-center power buildout and the aging gas-distribution base. Data-center demand is now visible across multiple parts of the energy and industrial supply chain, which lends the thesis durability at the demand level.

Estimate revisions temper the near-term outlook. Current-year consensus EPS fell 54% over 90 days to $0.32, and next-quarter estimates fell 47%, reflecting the withdrawn guidance and ERP drag. The 30-day trend has stabilized (current quarter +1.0%, next quarter +7.8%), suggesting analysts have reset to a lower base rather than continuing to cut. Next-year consensus of $0.85 implies a meaningful recovery, but that number embeds an assumption that ERP remediation and synergy capture succeed. The earnings surprise record has deteriorated — after beats of 24% and 9% in mid-2025, Q1 2026 missed by 88% ($0.01 versus $0.08 estimate), signaling reduced visibility during integration.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are meaningless here: the trailing P/E is negative because purchase-accounting charges pushed earnings into a loss. The relevant lens is forward and normalized. On next-year consensus EPS of $0.85, the stock trades at ~15x forward — below the peer median P/E of 31.1 (DXPE 31.3, MSM 31.1, AIT 30.1, CNM 21.2). That discount is not a clean bargain signal. It reflects genuine uncertainty: the $0.85 estimate assumes margin recovery that is unproven, and the company itself declined to provide 2026 guidance, so consensus rests on shakier ground than usual.

Applying the estimate-revision framework, the current-year revision of −54% over 90 days is a warning, well short of the >+30% threshold that would argue strongly for a re-rating. The stabilization in 30-day trends is constructive but not sufficient to treat the discount to peers as asymmetric upside. On EV/EBITDA, using a normalized annualized adjusted EBITDA run-rate of roughly $160-200M against an enterprise value near $2.8B, the multiple sits in the mid-teens — reasonable if margins recover toward the mid-single digits, expensive if the 3.3% Q1 rate persists.

The cheapness-relative-to-peers case is real but conditional. Historically, a compressed multiple that reflects an unresolved operational impairment behaves as a value trap absent a catalyst that lifts earnings power. Here the catalyst — ERP remediation and synergy realization — is identifiable but binary and unscheduled. That keeps the valuation score neutral rather than favorable. Analyst price targets ($16.00 low, $17.00 high, $16.25 mean) sit well above the $12.60 price, implying ~29% upside to the mean, but those targets predate the full extent of the ERP and cash-flow deterioration and should be treated as stale rather than as independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

The MRC acquisition consolidates the fragmented PVF/energy-distribution market by removing DNOW's largest direct competitor. Combined scale strengthens supplier relationships, purchasing power, and the ability to pass through tariff-driven cost increases. This improves structural positioning versus smaller regional distributors and reduces price competition in overlapping product lines.

The offsetting concern is that the integration is degrading, rather than enhancing, near-term competitive execution. Management's own risk disclosures state the inherited ERP is "slow, impedes customer service, requires more resources, increases safety stock, and [causes] difficulty processing orders." MRC's revenue declined in Q4 2025 partly because of ERP problems. Poor order fulfillment and service levels during integration create an opening for competitors to take share precisely when DNOW should be leveraging its enhanced scale. A structural risk across the industry is manufacturer disintermediation via direct-to-end-user sales.

The prior report lifted competitive position to 7.0 on the consolidation thesis. New data warrants trimming that: the strategic positioning is genuinely improved, but the operational execution that converts positioning into share and margin is impaired in the near term. The trajectory is favorable structurally but presently hampered, which places this dimension at a moderate level rather than a strong one.

## Risk Assessment

**MRC ERP stabilization is the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** The 10-K carries a dedicated risk factor acknowledging the company has "already experienced operational disruptions." This directly caused the withdrawal of 2026 guidance and is the mechanism behind the margin compression and the working-capital-driven negative operating cash flow.

**Securities investigation overhang.** A plaintiff firm announced a securities investigation on May 21, 2026 into whether DNOW's pre-February disclosures characterizing the ERP as "world-class" were misleading, given the reversal 3.5 months later. This is at the investigation stage with no complaint filed as of the available source, but it is a live overhang with litigation and reputational tail risk.

**Internal control risk.** A new risk factor flags that MRC "may initially operate outside our existing control environment" and that ERP instability could produce control deficiencies or a material weakness, audit adjustments, and delayed reporting. Q1 2026 disclosure controls were still concluded effective, and there was a May 2025 auditor change worth monitoring against this backdrop.

**Inherited legal and contingent liabilities.** MRC brings asbestos litigation (~446 lawsuits / ~500 claims at March 31, 2026), substantially insured but with insurer-solvency and cap risk, plus a multi-state unclaimed-property audit.

**Commodity, tariff, and leverage exposure.** Section 232 steel tariffs remain in effect; LIFO accounting amplifies P&L volatility; some downstream projects have been delayed or cancelled on tariff-driven cost increases. All borrowings float, tying interest cost to the rate path in an environment where higher rates raise financing costs.

**Cyclicality.** Upstream, still 39% of revenue, faces flat-to-lower 2026 spend. This is a permanent feature of the business and largely priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing calm rather than fear. ATM IV of 33.2% sits below the 1-year historical volatility of 41.5% (cheap, −8.3% spread) and in the 5th percentile of the current chain. The put/call skew is heavily call-tilted (−50.6%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.21, and OI ratio 0.14. The term structure is in contango, a normal/complacent shape. ATM IV also fell sharply from 45.5% to 33.2% since late May, and the P/C OI ratio dropped from 0.63 to 0.14, indicating the market is not aggressively hedging downside despite the fundamental overhangs. This complacency cuts both ways: options are cheap for anyone wanting protection, but the absence of put demand suggests the market does not see an imminent binary catalyst. Short interest of 10.6% of float (4.9 days to cover) reflects meaningful but not extreme institutional bearishness.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
ERP remediation succeeds over the next 2-3 quarters, safety stock normalizes, working capital reverses to a source of cash, and synergy capture lifts combined adjusted EBITDA margin back toward the 5-7% legacy DNOW range. On a recovered margin and next-year consensus EPS of ~$0.85, at a discount-to-peer multiple of ~20x the stock reaches ~$17. Secular midstream growth and the aging gas-utility replacement cycle provide durable demand, and the removal of a major competitor strengthens pricing. Bull target ~$17.

### Bear Case
ERP dysfunction persists, service levels erode share, the 3.3% adjusted EBITDA margin proves sticky rather than transitional, and the working-capital drain continues, forcing further revolver draws and pushing net leverage above 3x. The securities investigation escalates to a filed complaint, and management credibility remains impaired. Continued cash burn during a flat-to-lower upstream cycle compresses the multiple. Bear target ~$9-10, roughly 21-29% below the current price.

### Base Case
Integration proceeds unevenly, with partial ERP progress and gradual margin recovery toward the low-to-mid single digits over 2026, but no decisive inflection within the next two quarters. The stock ranges around current levels as the market waits for evidence in reported numbers. Base target ~$13-14.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the strategic logic is credible but the near-term outcome is binary and execution-dependent, and there is insufficient evidence for a confident call in either direction.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next two quarterly earnings reports (Q2 2026 and Q3 2026), when ERP remediation progress, working-capital reversal, and adjusted-EBITDA-margin trajectory should begin to show. Reinstatement of forward guidance would be a key marker.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted EBITDA margin recovers above ~5% for two consecutive quarters, confirming synergy capture and ERP stabilization.
- Operating cash flow turns positive as working capital reverses, and net leverage holds below 2.5x.
- Management reinstates 2026 guidance, and/or open-market insider buying emerges at depressed prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adjusted EBITDA margin remains at or below ~3.5% into the second half of 2026, indicating structural rather than transitional impairment.
- Continued negative operating cash flow forces net leverage above 3.0x.
- The securities investigation converts to a filed complaint alleging material misstatements, or a material weakness in internal controls is disclosed.

## Conclusion

DNOW sits at the midpoint of a transformational merger whose strategic rationale is sound — greater scale, diversification into stable gas-utility and secularly growing midstream demand, and the elimination of its largest competitor. The prior trajectory (AVOID → HOLD, scores rising from 4.5 to 5.6) reflected growing appreciation of that logic. New data tempers the improvement: adjusted EBITDA margins compressed sharply, operating cash flow turned deeply negative, guidance was withdrawn, and a securities investigation opened. The stock is down ~12% since the last review, but the more important change is fundamental deterioration in cash generation and margin during integration, not merely a lower price.

The valuation discount to peers is conditional on a margin recovery that has not yet appeared, and the estimate-revision picture (−54% current-year over 90 days, now stabilizing) is a reset, not a re-rating signal. The absence of open-market insider buying, despite management's willingness to spend company money on buybacks, removes a conviction signal. The investable question is whether ERP remediation and synergy capture lift margins upward over the next several quarters — a binary outcome that the reported numbers do not yet resolve. HOLD, with the next two earnings reports as the decision point.
