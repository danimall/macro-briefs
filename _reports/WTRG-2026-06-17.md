**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Essential Utilities trades as a function of one variable: the American Water Works (AWK) merger. On October 26, 2025, AWK agreed to acquire WTRG in an all-stock transaction at a fixed exchange ratio of 0.305 AWK shares per WTRG share, with no collar. Both shareholder bases approved the deal on February 10, 2026, and the targeted close is the end of Q1 2027. A conventional valuation screen (a $10.5B regulated water utility at $36.95, 19.2x forward earnings, 3.65% yield) misses the controlling fact. From the closing date forward, WTRG is American Water; today it is a claim on AWK stock plus a residual option on deal completion.

At $37.91, WTRG implies an AWK price of roughly $124 ($37.91 ÷ 0.305), which sits at or near AWK's actual trading level. There is effectively no merger-arb spread left to capture. The upside is bounded by AWK's own trajectory (AWK targets ~8% EPS growth and frames the deal as accretive in 2028), while the downside is a discrete re-rating to WTRG's standalone value if the Pennsylvania PUC blocks the transaction or imposes conditions severe enough to trigger the "Burdensome Effect" walk-away clause. WTRG has historically traded at a discount to water peers (cumulative TSR 93.7 vs. peer-group 163.6), so a deal break is a step down rather than a return to fair value.

The deal is meaningfully de-risked as of mid-2026. Two of the larger required state approvals are secured: Kentucky (April 2026) and Ohio (May 14, 2026). Pennsylvania PUC and HSR antitrust clearance remain the principal open gates. Asymmetric termination fees ($835M owed by AWK vs. $370M by WTRG) signal AWK is the more committed counterparty. Underlying utility fundamentals are stable-to-growing (Q1 2026 adjusted EPS $0.83, +7%; reaffirmed 5–7% standalone EPS growth), and the dividend is intact at 80 consecutive years. We rate WTRG a HOLD on an income-and-completion thesis. Existing holders are positioned to convert into AWK at favorable terms and collect the dividend in the interim; new capital faces capped upside and binary PA-approval risk with no spread to compensate.

## Company Overview

Essential Utilities is a Pennsylvania-based holding company operating regulated water, wastewater, and natural gas utilities serving roughly 5.5 million people across nine states (PA, OH, TX, IL, NC, NJ, IN, VA, KY). The water and wastewater operations run under the Aqua brand; the natural gas distribution business (Peoples, a 2020 acquisition) operates in western Pennsylvania.

The business has two reportable segments (Regulated Water, organized into eight state operating segments, and Regulated Natural Gas) plus a small non-regulated "Other" category for line-protection and gas-marketing activities. The model is capital-intensive rate-base growth: invest in infrastructure, earn a regulated return, and supplement organic growth with tuck-in acquisitions of small municipal systems. The 2026–2030 capital plan is approximately $8.7 billion.

Two structural features shape the company. First, heavy Pennsylvania concentration: Aqua Pennsylvania serves about half of all water/wastewater customers, and roughly 95% of gas customers are in western PA, which makes the Pennsylvania PUC the single most important regulator both operationally and for the merger. Second, the ownership of a large regulated natural gas business makes WTRG unusual among pure-play water peers and is the most complex piece of the AWK integration, since American Water has no gas operations.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results.** Operating revenue was $861.8M, up 10.0% YoY (Water +7.4%, Gas +12.5%, driven largely by passed-through gas costs and rate increases). The headline net income figure of $224.4M, down 20.9% YoY, with diluted EPS of $0.79 versus $1.03, is a comparability artifact. Q1 2025 benefited from a one-time $22.6M income-tax reserve release tied to an Aqua PA rate order that did not recur, and Q1 2026 absorbed roughly $16.3M of pre-merger transaction expenses in O&M. Adjusted EPS, excluding $0.04 of transaction costs, was $0.83, up about 7% on a like-for-like basis. O&M expense rose 27.6%, inflated by the merger costs plus weather-related main-break activity and insurance/bad-debt comparability items.

**Multi-year trajectory.** Net income has grown steadily: $431.6M (2021) → $465.2M (2022) → $498.2M (2023) → $595.3M (2024) → $616.4M (2025). Revenue rose from approximately $2.09B (2024) to $2.47B (2025). This is the steady, regulated compounding profile the rate-base model is built to deliver.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets stand at $19.78B against ~$8.4B of long-term debt and $6.89B of stockholders' equity. The debt-to-capital ratio is high but normal for a regulated utility whose assets are rate-base-backed; the Altman Z-Score reading near 1.0 ("distress") is a false signal for this business model and should not be read literally. Credit ratings are A–/Positive at the issuer level (S&P) and Baa2/Negative (Moody's), with senior unsecured at BBB+/Baa2. The Moody's negative outlook reflects leverage and the merger overhang rather than operational deterioration.

**Cash flow and the dividend.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $265.4M (down from $299.5M on vendor-payment timing) against roughly $269M of quarterly capex. WTRG is FCF-negative at the equity level and funds the gap with debt, commercial paper, and at-the-market equity issuance, standard for a capital-intensive utility in a heavy-build phase. The quarterly dividend was recently raised to $0.3426 (about $97M per quarter), extending an 80-year payment record. The dividend is funded out of regulated cash flow and capital-markets access, not free cash flow, which is normal for the sector but does mean dividend durability is tied to continued financing access and supportive rate outcomes.

**Goodwill watch item.** Goodwill of $2.35B represents 12% of assets, of which $2.28B sits in the Peoples gas unit. This is an impairment-watch item given energy-transition exposure, though not currently triggered.

## Growth Analysis

Management reaffirmed standalone 5–7% EPS growth through 2027 alongside the Q1 results. The growth engine is rate-base expansion funded by the ~$8.7B 2026–2030 capital plan, supplemented by tuck-in acquisitions. PFAS compliance capex (≥$450M by 2029 under the EPA's 2024 drinking-water standard) and ongoing infrastructure replacement provide a long runway of recoverable investment, and the company is running an active rate machine during deal pendency: a $163M PA natural gas rate case filed March 27, 2026, plus pending or interim cases in NJ, VA, OH, TX, and NC.

Estimate revisions are modestly mixed: current-year consensus is roughly flat (+0.0% over 90 days, +0.3% over 30 days) at $2.25, while next-year estimates have drifted down 1.9% over 90 days to $2.39. This is a regulated utility delivering mid-single-digit growth with affordability politics capping the pace of rate increases, not a profile that would justify aggressive growth scoring. The earnings record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the lone Q1 2026 "miss" attributable to the comparability items discussed above.

For the controlling question (the merger), the growth lens shifts to AWK. American Water targets roughly 8% EPS growth and frames the combination as accretive in 2028. If the deal closes, WTRG holders inherit a marginally faster-growing platform. The standalone growth thesis matters mainly as the floor that supports value if the deal breaks.

## Valuation Assessment

WTRG's forward P/E of 19.2x sits below the water-utility peer median of 22.1x (AWK 22.1, AWR 22.4, CWT 22.7, MSEX 21.9, HTO 19.6). This is not asymmetric-upside cheap; it is the structural discount that has followed WTRG for years. The pay-vs-performance disclosure quantifies it (cumulative TSR of 93.7 against a peer-group 163.6), and the gas-business goodwill, energy-transition exposure, and Pennsylvania concentration are the reasons the market has assigned the discount. Treating the sub-peer multiple as a buy signal would be a value-trap error: the discount reflects identifiable structural factors, not mispricing.

The more relevant valuation reality is the merger math. At $37.91, WTRG trades at roughly the implied deal value (0.305 × ~$124 AWK = ~$37.8). The arb spread is effectively closed. There is no captured premium waiting to be realized at close, only the prospect of riding AWK's share price between now and Q1 2027, plus the dividend. On the downside, a deal break removes the AWK floor and reverts the stock toward its standalone discount, which on 19x trailing earnings against a peer group at 22x, and accounting for the merger-pendency overhang the price would shed, implies a move into the low-to-mid $30s.

Analyst targets bracket this: low $37.00, median $38.00, mean $39.80, high $43.00, a tight range consistent with a stock pinned to deal parity rather than one with wide fundamental dispersion. The high $43 figure approximately reflects AWK appreciating into 2027 at its growth target. The dividend (3.65% vs. peer median 2.8%) is the one genuine valuation support, providing carry while the deal resolves. The valuation offers fair value to the deal with capped upside and binary downside, not the asymmetric setup that would warrant a high score.

## Competitive Landscape

The water-utility sector is consolidating, and this merger is the landmark transaction: American Water, the largest U.S. publicly traded water utility, absorbing a top-three player to create a dominant national platform. WTRG competes with municipalities, other investor-owned utilities, and financial buyers for tuck-in systems, competition that has bid up acquisition prices and was itself cited as a growth headwind.

The regulated franchise is durable by nature: water and wastewater are local monopolies with high barriers to entry. The company's decision to sell, against a backdrop of chronic TSR underperformance versus peers, signals that its standalone competitive trajectory was lagging. The gas business is the structural complication: it concentrates goodwill ($2.28B), carries an active fatal-explosion liability, and is the single non-water asset AWK is taking on, making its regulatory treatment and integration the most company-specific question in the deal.

Sector-wide, intensifying affordability politics and regulatory lag are pressuring the entire investor-owned utility model as rates rise faster than inflation. The FY2025 10-K explicitly flags "mounting focus on affordability" and "customer dissatisfaction reaching historic highs," with commissions pushed toward smaller rate increases, a genuine margin headwind for a company seeking to recover ~$8.7B of capex, though it applies sector-wide rather than to WTRG alone.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger-specific risks dominate.** The principal binary risk is regulatory approval, most critically the Pennsylvania PUC, plus HSR antitrust clearance. Either party can walk, generally without a termination fee, if approval conditions create a defined "Burdensome Effect" such as forced divestitures. The fixed 0.305 ratio with no collar means WTRG holders bear full exposure to AWK's share price between now and close. Termination fees are asymmetric (WTRG owes $370M, AWK owes $835M), which leans the completion odds modestly in WTRG's favor by raising AWK's cost of walking. Deal pendency itself carries drag: management distraction, restrictions on financing/acquisitions, potential attrition, and deal litigation, plus a $60M financial-advisor fee ($25M incurred through Q1 2026). Accretion is only expected in 2028 and is not guaranteed.

The deal has de-risked materially since the prior review. Kentucky approved in April 2026 and Ohio on May 14, 2026, clearing two of the larger state gates. That progress is the reason we do not extend the recent score decline despite a modestly lower price; the risk profile has improved on completion certainty even as the stock drifted toward the low end of its range.

**Standalone risks.** Peoples faces 12 lawsuits arising from an August 2023 home explosion that killed six people, being vigorously defended, an inherent gas-business hazard. The DELCORA acquisition ($276.5M, ~198,000 EDUs) is stuck in litigation as the City of Chester sues to block the asset transfer; it is the largest pending tuck-in and may simply transfer to AWK. PFAS compliance requires ≥$450M of capex by 2029, partly offset by litigation recoveries (~$46.2M from 3M/DuPont in 2025, $7.26M from Tyco in April 2026, with more expected over a decade). Affordability politics and regulatory lag remain a sharpened standalone risk for the heavy capital plan. Goodwill impairment in the Peoples unit is a watch item, not a trigger.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign. The put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.43 to 0.22 over the past five-plus weeks while the price held roughly flat (~$37), indicating reduced hedging demand and no accumulation of downside bets, consistent with a stock pinned to deal mechanics rather than one bracing for a catalyst. Short interest is negligible at 1.7% of float (2.3 days to cover), confirming there is no meaningful bearish positioning ahead of the PA decision. The absence of elevated put skew or rising IV is what you would expect for a name trading as an AWK proxy with low standalone volatility.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The Pennsylvania PUC approves the merger with manageable conditions, HSR clears, and the deal closes around Q1 2027 at the fixed 0.305 ratio. WTRG holders convert into AWK at favorable terms, capturing AWK's ~8% EPS-growth trajectory and 2028 accretion while collecting the 3.65% dividend in the interim. The asymmetric $835M AWK termination fee underscores AWK's commitment, and the KY/OH approvals demonstrate regulators are amenable. The stock tracks AWK higher into close; the high analyst target of $43 corresponds roughly to AWK appreciating at its growth rate. Bull target: ~$43 (+13% from $37.91).

### Bear Case
The Pennsylvania PUC denies the merger or attaches conditions severe enough to trigger the Burdensome Effect clause, and one party walks. WTRG re-rates to its standalone value, shedding the AWK floor and the deal-pendency support. Given the company's documented discount to peers (TSR 93.7 vs. 163.6), the structural gas-business and goodwill overhangs, and softening next-year estimates, the standalone clearing price sits in the low-to-mid $30s. The dividend cushions but does not prevent the move. Bear target: ~$33 (–13% from $37.91).

### Base Case
The deal proceeds toward a Q1 2027 close. With two major approvals secured and AWK strongly incentivized to complete, completion probability is high but not certain pending PA and HSR. The stock continues to track deal parity, drifting with AWK and the dividend providing carry. Base target: ~$39 (consensus mean $39.80, median $38.00).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The holding rationale is the dividend/stability profile plus a high-probability, positive-but-bounded merger completion that converts the position into AWK. There is no asymmetric appreciation to underwrite a BUY (the spread is closed and upside is capped at AWK's trajectory), and the deal de-risking plus intact fundamentals and 3.65% yield do not support an AVOID.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Pennsylvania PUC decision and HSR clearance are the decisive events, with the deal targeted to close by the end of Q1 2027. Monitor the regulatory docket continuously; the next earnings update and any 8-K Reg-FD merger filings are the natural checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pennsylvania PUC approves the merger on terms that avoid the Burdensome Effect clause, plus HSR clearance, with the deal effectively de-risked and AWK trading at a level that opens a positive spread to the 0.305 ratio.
- AWK's share price rises such that 0.305 × AWK exceeds WTRG's price by a margin (>5–7%) that compensates for residual deal risk and time-to-close.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The Pennsylvania PUC signals or imposes conditions likely to trigger the Burdensome Effect clause, or either party publicly raises the prospect of termination.
- A material goodwill impairment in the Peoples unit, or a deterioration in the standalone EPS guidance below the reaffirmed 5–7% range.
- Adverse resolution of the August 2023 gas-explosion litigation at a magnitude that meaningfully affects standalone value.

## Conclusion

Essential Utilities is a merger-completion and income position, not a standalone utility bet. The stock tracks American Water at a fixed 0.305 exchange ratio, trades near deal parity, and offers no captured spread. The realistic outcome distribution is narrow: the deal closes (high probability, de-risked by the Kentucky and Ohio approvals and AWK's asymmetric commitment) and holders convert into AWK while collecting a 3.65% dividend, or Pennsylvania blocks it and the stock re-rates to its historical discount. Upside is capped at AWK's ~8% EPS-growth trajectory; downside is a discrete step lower on a PA denial.

For existing holders, the carry, the dividend, and the favorable conversion terms justify holding through close. For new capital, the absence of any arb spread and the binary PA-approval gate mean the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate WTRG a HOLD, with the Pennsylvania PUC decision the event that will resolve the thesis in either direction.
