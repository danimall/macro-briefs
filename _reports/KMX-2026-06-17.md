**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CarMax presents a genuine analytical pivot. Two prior reports rated the stock AVOID (scores 4.3 then 3.9) as comp-store used units collapsed to -9.0% in Q3 FY26, FY26 net income halved on a $141.3M goodwill write-off, CAF credit metrics deteriorated, and the company churned through a wholesale leadership change. What has changed is the Q1 FY27 print (reported June 17, 2026, the first full quarter under new CEO Keith Barr): adjusted EPS of $1.31 versus $0.96 consensus, revenue up 6.2% to $8.0B, combined units +3.3%, SG&A down 3.7% with SG&A per unit improving 6.8%, and comp-store used units recovering to -0.8% from the Q3 trough of -9.0%. The deliberate margin-for-volume strategy, cutting used GPU by $230 to drive throughput while compounding finance and warranty economics, produced early traction.

The bull and bear case both rest on whether one quarter constitutes a durable trend. The encouraging read is that FY26 carried "kitchen-sink" characteristics: the goodwill impairment, $49.8M restructuring, and margin reset all landed in trough quarters around a CEO transition that lowers the bar for the incoming executive, and Q1 FY27 cleared that bar. The cautionary read is that the stock has re-rated roughly 69% off its November low (from ~$30.88 close to ~$52.11) to about 31x trailing earnings, the consumer backdrop is weakening into a probable H2 cliff, and the strategy intentionally pushes CAF penetration toward 50% and deeper into Tier-2 credit precisely as net loss rates rise (2.34% and climbing) and recovery rates fall (45.5% and falling). The turnaround has not yet been de-risked, and the entry point has moved against new capital.

I am moving the recommendation from AVOID to HOLD with a growth rationale. The fundamental trajectory has reversed, which is the dominant signal, but the valuation no longer offers the asymmetric discount that existed at $37 in May, and the credit risk remains a live binary. This is a turnaround worth monitoring on confirmation of a second clean quarter rather than a stock to chase at 31x trailing into a deteriorating consumer.

## Company Overview

CarMax is the largest used-vehicle retailer in the United States, operating 256 superstores across 110 TV markets reaching roughly 85% of the population. The business runs in two reportable segments. CarMax Sales Operations covers retail used-vehicle sales (780,684 units in FY26), wholesale auctions of trade-ins that fail retail standards (538,203 units at ~99% sell-through), extended protection plans, vehicle service, and the Edmunds digital lead-generation property acquired in 2021. CarMax Auto Finance (CAF) is the captive lender, financing 42.4% of retail units in FY26 and servicing roughly 1.0 million accounts in a $16.4B loan portfolio.

The competitive architecture rests on national scale, no-haggle pricing, a vertically integrated appraisal and sourcing engine (CarMax buys ~1.1 million vehicles a year directly from consumers and dealers, its cheapest and highest-margin inventory source), proprietary pricing and inventory algorithms, and an omni-channel platform where 81% of transactions are digitally enabled. Market share of 0–10-year-old vehicles slipped to ~3.6% from 3.7%, with losses concentrated in the second half of calendar 2025. The 10-K contains an unusually candid admission: "after decades of industry leadership, it is clear CarMax needs change."

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years as used-vehicle prices normalized post-pandemic: $29.7B (FY23) → $26.5B (FY24) → $26.4B (FY25) → $25.9B (FY26, -1.8%). FY26 net income fell 50.6% to $247.3M, with diluted EPS of $1.68 versus $3.21 prior. The reported figure was magnified by two non-operational items: a $141.3M non-cash goodwill impairment in Q4 (writing off essentially all of the Edmunds goodwill, triggered by the stock decline and lowered forecasts), and $49.8M of restructuring (CEO severance plus a ~30% cut to the Customer Experience Center). Because the goodwill impairment is non-deductible, the effective tax rate spiked to 35.5% from 25.2%. Adjusted EPS was $2.91 versus $3.27, an ~11% decline that better reflects underlying operations than the headline.

Used gross profit per unit dipped $58 to $2,253 in FY26. SG&A deleveraged to 87.4% of gross profit from 84.0% as the cost base shrank slower than gross profit. The Q1 FY27 print reverses this trend, with SG&A per unit improving to $1,619, the first concrete evidence the $200M exit-rate cost program is biting.

CAF is the key area to monitor. CAF income fell 3.3% to $562.7M in FY26 while the provision for loan losses rose to $391.2M from $334.7M. Net credit losses climbed to 2.34% of average loans (from 2.03%, and 1.93% in FY24), the recovery rate on repossessions fell to 45.5% from 47.2%, and past-due accounts rose to 5.11% at year-end. Management attributes the deterioration to 2022–2023 vintage loans originated at peak vehicle prices and stressed by inflation; loans originated after the April 2024 underwriting tightening reportedly perform in line.

On leverage, the headline screens are misleading. The Altman Z-Score of 1.6 ("distress zone") and D/E of 3.48 would flag a solvency concern, but of $18.05B total debt, $15.83B is non-recourse notes payable that fund CAF's loan book and are legally isolated. True recourse leverage is modest: roughly $840M revolver draw, $499M term loan, $400M senior notes, and $484M financing obligations against $6.1B of equity. The company is covenant-compliant with $2B of revolver capacity and over $4B of unused warehouse capacity. The genuine leverage signal is subtler: management paused buybacks in Q4 FY26 because leverage drifted "slightly above target," after repurchasing $642.8M during FY26, much of it at higher prices. FY26 operating cash flow of $1.78B is flattered by a $909M non-prime loan sale and inventory drawdown and should not be extrapolated. Capex is being cut from $541M (FY26) to ~$400M (FY27) with only four new stores planned.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is a turnaround inflection, not secular expansion. The Q1 FY27 results are the foundation: combined units +3.3% to 392,357, wholesale units +8.4%, and CAF penetration +150 bps to 43.3%. The deliberate strategy is to trade margin for volume, cutting used GPU $230 to $2,177 to recapture share, while compounding finance and warranty economics and stripping out cost. Early elasticity evidence supports the bet: the GPU cut coincided with comp-store used units recovering from the Q3 FY26 trough to -0.8%.

Estimate revisions are mixed but stabilizing. Current-quarter consensus is +2.2% over 30 days but still -12.1% over 90 days; current-year is +1.2%/30d and -8.0%/90d; next-year is +2.4%/30d and -15.1%/90d. The pattern shows estimates finding a floor and ticking up over the most recent month after a steep prior cut, consistent with consensus catching down to the FY26 reset and then beginning to respond to the Q1 beat. This does not meet the bar of the strongest forward signal (90-day revisions are still negative), so I am not treating it as a high-conviction inflection trigger.

The earnings beat track record is strong: three beats and one miss across four quarters, with beats of 40.0%, 46.8%, and 34.5% surrounding the single -40.1% miss in the trough quarter. The magnitude of recent beats argues management is now guiding conservatively, which raises confidence in near-term estimates. The durability question is whether demand elasticity holds for several quarters and whether the consumer cliff (rising mortgage costs, negative real wages, 401k hardship withdrawals, weakening discretionary spend) overwhelms the margin-for-volume bet. Used-vehicle retail sits squarely in the discretionary category most exposed to that scenario.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$52.11, KMX trades at ~31x trailing EPS of $1.68. The trailing figure is distorted by the goodwill impairment and restructuring, so forward metrics matter more. On current-year consensus EPS of $2.35, the stock is ~22x; on next-year $2.78, ~19x. Those are not cheap for a low-margin auto retailer with a stagnant top line, and they sit well above the auto-retail peer median P/E of 12.5 (LAD 10.8, AN 10.6, GPI 12.5). CarMax has historically commanded a premium to franchised dealers because of its captive finance arm and brand, but the premium is currently wide, roughly 1.5–1.8x the peer median on forward earnings, at a moment when the company is in early-stage turnaround with unproven margin recovery.

The valuation question is whether the market is mispricing this stock today. At $37 in May, the answer was plausibly yes: that was ~16x forward on the eventual FY27 number, a discount to historical CarMax multiples with a credible turnaround catalyst pending. At $52, after a ~40% run since mid-May and ~69% off the November low, that discount has closed. The analyst target range is extraordinarily wide (low $19, high $99, mean $43.46, median $41), which itself signals binary outcome dispersion rather than a clear undervaluation. The current price already exceeds both mean and median targets. I do not see a specific reason the market is underpricing the stock at this level; if anything, the price has run ahead of the still-recovering fundamentals. This warrants a below-average valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

CarMax operates in a vast, fragmented market (~39M used vehicles sold in CY2025, ~20M aged 0–10 years, over 18,000 franchised dealers). Its ~3.6% share leaves a long runway but also signals vulnerability. The core dynamic is the clash between CarMax's physical-plus-omni-channel hybrid and pure-play online disruptors, principally Carvana, which the 10-K and recent commentary flag as resurgent and "making its most disruptive move," plus franchised dealers' manufacturer-backed CPO programs.

CarMax's structural edge is its appraisal-sourcing flywheel (the largest consumer buyer of used cars in the country, producing its cheapest inventory) and its captive finance arm, neither of which online-only rivals fully replicate. The CAF full-spectrum expansion is framed as widening the moat by capturing finance economics across all credit tiers. The trajectory, however, is the concern: share losses concentrated in late 2025, and the candid 10-K admission of needed change confirms a leader that had been ceding ground. The Q1 FY27 volume recovery suggests the bleeding may have stopped, but a single quarter of stabilization does not yet establish that share is being regained against a more aggressive Carvana. Competitive position is stabilizing from a deteriorating base, which I score modestly below neutral.

## Risk Assessment

CAF credit normalization is the single most important financial risk and is not fully priced. Rising net loss rates, falling recovery rates, and a deliberate push to expand penetration toward 50% with Tier-2 originations reaching ~30% of that spectrum intentionally add higher-loss loans. A 10% downside macro scenario would add ~$51.8M to the allowance. In a benign economy this is accretive (~$10–12M lifetime pre-tax income per point of penetration); in the H2 consumer cliff scenario, it amplifies losses precisely when retail volume also softens. The funding model depends on continuous access to warehouse facilities and ABS markets, which would curtail lending in a disruption.

Affordability remains a structural headwind: elevated used prices plus high interest rates suppress demand, and the rate chain (higher oil → higher long-end yields → higher financing costs) works against the consumer. Competition from better-capitalized online disruptors could erode share. Leadership-transition execution risk is elevated given the wholesale C-suite and board turnover (CEO departure, interim arrangements, outside hire, and a cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks). A securities class action (Jason Cap v. CarMax, D. Md., covering June 20–Nov 5, 2025) is an overhang. Geographic concentration (Southeast, CA, TX, DC/Baltimore), tariffs, and EV-transition reconditioning demands add secondary risks.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 66.8% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 55.8% (rich by 11.1 points), with a put/call skew of 7.0% indicating elevated put demand and an implied move of ±12.2% on the nearest expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (66.8% near-term versus 51.8% longer-dated), the signature of a discrete near-term event the market expects to resolve. The 6-month skew of -4.6% shows persistent downside hedging further out. The IV jump in the options delta data (the 210% figure appears to be a stale or single-strike artifact) coincides with the 40.6% price run. Short interest is elevated at 12.1% of float with 5.4 days to cover, meaning meaningful institutional bearish positioning persists despite the rally. The combination of rich near-term IV, put skew, and elevated short interest into a sharp rally confirms the market views this as a binary turnaround that is not yet de-risked, consistent with the HOLD framing. Rich IV also makes this a poor moment to pay up for upside via options.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($65–75):** The Q1 FY27 beat marks a genuine inflection. The margin-for-volume strategy works: GPU cuts drive durable volume recovery, the $200M exit-rate SG&A program delivers operating leverage, CAF penetration climbs toward 50% adding finance income, and EPS recovers to $3.00+ within 12–18 months. FY26 was a deliberate kitchen-sink year that reset the base for an outside CEO, the 2022–2023 credit vintages roll off while post-April-2024 originations perform clean, and the consumer proves more resilient than feared. Director open-market buying near the lows ($45–46) marks the bottom. At $3.00 normalized EPS and a 22–25x multiple, the stock reaches $66–75.

**Bear Case ($35–40, -23% to -33%):** One quarter does not make a trend. The H2 consumer cliff materializes — rising unemployment, affordability pressure — and CAF losses accelerate just as the company deliberately added Tier-2 risk and pushed penetration. Net loss rates breach 2.5%+, the allowance builds, and the margin-for-volume trade fails as elasticity disappears in a weakening economy. Carvana takes incremental share. The stock de-rates back toward peer multiples (12–15x forward on a cut $2.30 estimate), reaching $35–40. The $19 analyst low target reflects the tail scenario where credit deterioration compounds with volume decline.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY27, approximately September 2026) is the decisive checkpoint, testing whether the Q1 inflection is a trend or a single-quarter beat aided by easy comps and a kitchen-sink prior year.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A second consecutive clean quarter: comp-store used units turning positive, SG&A program continuing to deliver, and adjusted EPS beating consensus again — combined with a pullback toward $42–45 (under ~18x forward) that restores valuation asymmetry.
- CAF net loss rate stabilizing or declining (below ~2.3%) with recovery rate improving, confirming the credit normalization has peaked rather than accelerating.
- 90-day estimate revisions turning positive across current-year and next-year, confirming consensus is catching up to the inflection.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CAF net credit losses breaching ~2.5% of average loans or past-due accounts climbing above ~5.5%, signaling the Tier-2 expansion is misfiring into a weakening consumer.
- Comp-store used units reverting to negative mid-single digits or worse, indicating the volume recovery was transient.
- A guidance cut or a quarterly miss that breaks the three-of-four beat pattern, or material adverse development in the securities litigation.

## Conclusion

The trajectory of three declining scores and two AVOIDs reflected a business in genuine deterioration through Q3 FY26. New data reverses that trend: the Q1 FY27 beat under a new CEO is a real fundamental improvement that no longer supports an AVOID. The earnings beat track record (three of four, with large surprises) and director open-market buying near the lows add to the constructive case.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is valuation and unresolved credit risk, not a deteriorating thesis. The stock has re-rated through both the mean ($43) and median ($41) analyst targets, and the asymmetric discount that existed at $37 has closed. Meanwhile the deliberate CAF penetration push into Tier-2 credit creates a live binary into a probable H2 consumer cliff, and the options market (rich near-term IV, put skew, 12.1% short interest, backwardation) confirms the market sees an unresolved binary rather than a de-risked recovery. The turnaround is credible and worth holding through the next print, but the entry point favors waiting for either a second confirming quarter or a pullback that restores valuation support before adding new capital. I rate KMX a HOLD with a growth rationale, tied explicitly to the September Q2 FY27 reassessment.
