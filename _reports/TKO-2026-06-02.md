**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TKO Group Holdings owns the two strongest properties in combat sports and sports entertainment (UFC and WWE) alongside a lower-margin sports-marketing and hospitality business (IMG, On Location, PBR) added in the February 2025 Endeavor Asset Acquisition. The investment case rests on a structural advantage that few media businesses possess: owned, controlled, year-round intellectual property with no league owners, no players' union, and talent classified as independent contractors. That control lets TKO extract escalating site fees and media-rights economics from a bidding war among Paramount, Netflix, ESPN, and Amazon for scarce live content.

The 2026 story is a contracted media-rights step-up. UFC's new seven-year Paramount deal (~$1.1B/year average, replacing prior ESPN economics) began in 2026; WWE's Raw is on a ten-year Netflix deal, and WWE premium live events moved to a five-year ESPN deal in September 2025. This drove Q1 2026 revenue up 26% YoY to $1.597B and underpins FY26 guidance of $5.675B–$5.775B, roughly 20%+ growth. The owned-IP engines (UFC 57% Adj. EBITDA margin, WWE 52%) generate high-quality free cash flow ($1.16B in 2025, ~24.5% FCF margin), and the balance sheet carries manageable ~1.8x net leverage against a covenant at 8.25x.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are concentrated and specific. The stock trades at ~46x consensus FY26 EPS of $4.49, a meaningful premium to the entertainment peer median (~28x) even adjusting for quality. Near-term consensus EPS estimates have fallen sharply (current quarter -15.5% over 30 days and -37% over 90 days), and the company has missed earnings in three of the last four quarters, undercutting confidence in forward numbers despite the revenue inflection. Governance is a permanent discount: Silver Lake, through Endeavor, controls ~63% of voting power, TKO is a controlled-company exemption from key NYSE rules, and competitive-opportunity carve-outs let insiders pursue deals away from the company. The mid-May 2026 cluster of open-market C-suite purchases is a genuine positive signal, but it does not offset a full multiple on falling near-term estimates. We rate TKO a HOLD with a positive bias.

## Company Overview

TKO was formed in September 2023 by combining UFC (the leading global MMA organization) and WWE (the global leader in sports entertainment). On February 28, 2025, the company expanded through the Endeavor Asset Acquisition (~$3.25B plus a $50M adjustment), adding IMG (sports-marketing and media-rights advisory), On Location (premium experiential hospitality at events like the Olympics, FIFA World Cup, and Super Bowl), and PBR (Professional Bull Riders). TKO also operates a Zuffa Boxing joint venture with Saudi Arabia's Sela Company.

The company reports in three operating segments plus Corporate/Other, with 2025 figures as follows:
- **UFC** — revenue $1,502M, 57% Adj. EBITDA margin (best-in-class)
- **WWE** — revenue $1,709M, 52% Adj. EBITDA margin
- **IMG** — revenue $1,367M, 12% Adj. EBITDA margin (lower-margin agency/hospitality)
- **Corporate/Other** (PBR, boxing) — revenue $199M, negative EBITDA

Monetization runs through four channels: media rights and content, live events and hospitality, partnerships and marketing, and consumer-products licensing. The structural advantage is that UFC and WWE own and control their IP and operate continuously rather than within a defined season, which allows global event placement to maximize host-market site fees.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is distorted by acquisition accounting.** Reported consolidated revenue grew from $1.67B (2023) to $2.80B (2024) to $4.74B (2025), but the Endeavor assets were a common-control acquisition, so the like-for-like comparison in the MD&A shows 2025 revenue of $4,735M against a recast $4,884M in 2024, a comparable decline of roughly 3%. That decline was driven entirely by IMG/On Location, where the 2024 Paris Olympics created a tough comp and IMG revenue fell 31%. The owned-IP engines grew: UFC +7%, WWE +22%. Treat the headline tripling as an accounting artifact; the operative growth signal is the 2026 inflection from the new media deals.

**Profitability and cash flow are high quality.** Reported 2025 net income was $546M versus a $246M loss in 2024, where the prior-year loss was driven largely by a $375M UFC antitrust settlement charge. Net income attributable to Class A holders was $195M. Operating income reached $835M (17.6% operating margin), and Adjusted EBITDA was $1,585M (33% margin), up from $1,082M (22%). Operating cash flow rose to $1,286M from $586M, and free cash flow was $1,159M (~24.5% FCF margin) against light capex of ~$127M. This is a high-FCF-conversion IP licensor.

**Leverage is manageable; the distress screen is a false positive.** TKO carries ~$3.7B in term loans (refinanced September 2025, adding $1.0B incremental at SOFR+2.00%, ~5.87% all-in, maturing November 2031). Net debt against ~$1.6B EBITDA is ~1.8x, well below the 8.25x covenant. The Altman Z-Score of 1.22, nominally "distress zone," is mechanically depressed by ~$8.4B goodwill, ~$3.3B intangibles, and negative GAAP retained earnings (-$797M, an artifact of the structure and prior settlement charge). A high-margin, high-FCF business at 1.8x leverage is not financially distressed, and screening on that number would mislead.

**Capital return is active.** Against a $2.0B buyback authorization from October 2024, the company executed $866.8M in 2025 (including an $800M ASR), doubled the quarterly dividend in September 2025 from $0.38 to $0.76 (~$150M/quarter, current yield ~1.5%), and added another $1B to the repurchase authorization with the Q1 2026 print.

**Earnings delivery is a watch item.** The track record over the last four quarters is one beat and three misses: -6.7% in Q1 2026, -126.3% in Q4 2025 (an actual loss versus a small expected profit), -36.0% in Q3 2025, and +10.5% in Q2 2025. The misses partly reflect timing of events and rights recognition, but they reduce confidence in the precision of forward consensus and are difficult to square with a premium multiple.

## Growth Analysis

The genuine growth driver is contractual media-rights re-rating, not organic event volume. UFC moved from prior ESPN economics to a seven-year Paramount deal averaging ~$1.1B/year (~$7.7B total, exclusive US starting 2026). The Q1 2026 revenue growth and FY26 guidance are direct consequences of these step-ups flowing into the income statement.

The durability of this growth is higher than a single-company story because it sits on long-dated contracts with credit-quality counterparties, and because the bidding tension among streamers and legacy media for scarce live content is structural. The mechanism is straightforward: live combat sports and sports entertainment deliver appointment viewing and large, demographically attractive audiences that streamers need to justify subscriber economics; with UFC and WWE owning year-round IP and no competing scaled rivals, renewal economics step up at each cycle.

The growth qualifier is the near-term estimate trend. Despite the revenue inflection, consensus EPS estimates are falling across every horizon: current quarter -15.5% (30d) / -37.0% (90d), current year -13.5% / -33.3%, and next year -10.8% / -28.9%. Current-year EPS consensus has dropped from $6.74 to $4.49 over 90 days. Falling EPS against rising revenue points to margin or below-the-line pressure, likely a combination of higher rights-amortization and content cost from the new deals, interest expense on the upsized term loan, and the dilution of the lower-margin IMG mix. The WWE wrestler pay-cut effort reported in May 2026 is a margin lever consistent with the segment's 52% Adj. EBITDA margin, but it carries talent-relations and reputational risk. The combination of strong top-line growth with sharply declining EPS estimates is the central tension in the forward model.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$208 and consensus FY26 EPS of $4.49, TKO trades at ~46x forward earnings. Against the Movies & Entertainment peer median P/E of ~27.8x (NFLX 27.8x, DIS 16.3x; LYV and PSKY are loss-makers with non-meaningful ratios), TKO commands a substantial premium. Some premium is defensible given UFC's 57% and WWE's 52% segment margins, ~24.5% FCF conversion, and the contracted rights tailwind, economics that are genuinely superior to most peers. But the magnitude of the premium is hard to justify when near-term EPS estimates are falling 30%+ over 90 days, the company has missed three of the last four quarters, and the multiple is set on a consensus number that has itself been cut repeatedly.

On free cash flow, ~$1.16B against a ~$15.6B Class A market cap is a ~7.4% FCF yield, which is more supportive than the earnings multiple implies and reflects the gap between GAAP EPS (depressed by amortization and minority interest) and cash generation. This FCF yield is the strongest pillar of the valuation case and a reason the stock is not overvalued on a cash basis. It does not, however, create asymmetric upside at the current price.

Analyst price targets bracket the stock at a low of $185 and a high of $275, with a mean of $234 and median of $234.50. The mean sits ~12% above the current price. Given the falling-estimate backdrop, we treat these targets as reflecting the same growth extrapolation embedded in the stock rather than as an independent floor; at ~46x forward the target and the price share consensus optimism vulnerable to any disappointment. We score valuation 4.5: the FCF yield and quality prevent a low-3s value-trap framing, but a full forward multiple on falling estimates does not offer the asymmetric upside required for a higher score.

## Competitive Landscape

TKO's competitive position is its strongest dimension. UFC has no credible scaled MMA competitor; PFL and ONE Championship are far smaller. WWE's nearest rival, AEW, is materially smaller in reach and rights economics. The owned, year-round, non-seasonal IP model is the source of pricing power, and the recent rights step-ups are direct evidence the moat is widening rather than holding flat.

The IMG/On Location addition is competitively weaker: sports-marketing agencies and premium hospitality are more fragmented, lower-margin markets (12% segment Adj. EBITDA). These businesses dilute consolidated margins but add scale, rights-advisory relationships (EPL, Wimbledon, NFL, FIFA), and premium-hospitality reach that can be cross-sold against the owned-IP franchises. On balance, the trajectory is favorable: the core franchises are gaining economic value at each renewal, and the company is positioned for the secular escalation in premium live-sports rights. We score competitive position 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Media-rights concentration and renewal cyclicality.** The equity story rests on a handful of mega-contracts. The WWE/Netflix Raw deal contains a Netflix opt-out after year five, explicitly flagged as a risk. Non-renewal or adverse renegotiation of UFC/Paramount, WWE/Netflix, or WWE/ESPN would materially impair results. This concentration is the defining idiosyncratic risk.

**Controlled-company governance.** Endeavor holds its economic stake through TKO OpCo (with tax/structural interests that differ from Class A holders), provides services via a Transition Services Agreement, and competitive-opportunity provisions let Endeavor-affiliated insiders pursue opportunities away from TKO. TKO opted out of DGCL Section 203. Minority holders bear meaningful agency risk, and this is the clearest justification for a persistent governance discount.

**Holding-company and tax structure.** TKO Group Holdings' only asset is its TKO OpCo units; it depends on OpCo distributions for taxes and dividends. Tax-receivable-agreement dynamics, the 1% buyback excise tax, and negative-tax-capital risks from debt-funded buybacks add complexity and potential cash drag.

**McMahon legacy.** Vince McMahon resigned all TKO positions in January 2024; the SEC settled charges against him (January 2025) for circumventing WWE internal controls. Ongoing litigation includes a Trafficking Victims Protection Act suit. McMahon has personally paid related settlements and is expected to reimburse the company, but reputational and regulatory tail risk persists.

**Talent classification and Saudi dependence.** Talent are independent contractors; California/New York reclassification efforts could raise costs. Saudi Arabia is both a growth engine (site fees, Zuffa Boxing JV with Sela, WWE events) and a concentrated geopolitical risk; management confirmed Saudi will continue honoring commitments to TKO even as it pulls funding from LIV Golf, but the company is monitoring regional conflict ahead of a major WWE Saudi event. Given the current Middle East environment, an event disruption or reputational episode is a live, named risk.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing is consistent with near-term event risk. Near-term ATM IV is 39.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 31.4%, leaving IV rich by ~7.8 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (39.2% near-term vs. 37.1% longer-dated), which markets typically read as pricing imminent stress around an upcoming catalyst. The 1-month implied move is ±6.8%, and put/call skew is elevated at 5.9% (heavier put demand) at the near tenor, fading to call-tilted skew at the 3- and 6-month tenors. Put/call OI dropped sharply from 0.76 to 0.11 over the past month as the stock rose ~10%, indicating prior downside hedges were lifted. The market is hedging a near-term event but is not structurally bearish on a multi-month horizon. This sits alongside elevated short interest of 14.3% of float (6.9 days to cover), which signals institutional skepticism and argues for caution on a long-side conviction call.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
TKO owns two irreplaceable, year-round IP franchises entering a contracted media-rights step-up that has only begun to flow through the P&L. UFC/Paramount and WWE/Netflix + ESPN re-rate high-margin rights revenue upward, drive 20%+ FY26 revenue growth, and expand Adjusted EBITDA beyond $1.6B with light capex. Margin levers (WWE talent-cost discipline, IMG normalization off the Olympics comp) lift EPS as the rights ramp matures. The mid-May 2026 cluster of open-market C-suite purchases (CEO ~$2.0M, COO ~$2.0M, CFO ~$0.5M, plus director Kraft ~$1.0M within 48 hours) signals insider conviction at ~$184–190. On a ~7.4% FCF yield and continued buybacks, the stock re-rates toward the $260–275 range over 12–18 months as estimates stabilize and the rights economics compound.

### Bear Case
The ~46x forward multiple is set on a consensus EPS number that has been cut 33% in 90 days, and the company has missed three of the last four quarters. If EPS estimates keep falling, because new-deal content/amortization, interest on the upsized term loan, and the IMG mix compress margins more than expected, the multiple compresses against a lower base. The Netflix opt-out (after year five), a Saudi event disruption, or renewed McMahon-related litigation provides catalysts for de-rating. Governance (63% Silver Lake/Endeavor control, controlled-company exemptions, insider competitive-opportunity carve-outs) caps the multiple a quality operator would otherwise earn. The stock retraces toward the 52-week low area near $152, roughly 27% below current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive (contracted rights step-up, high FCF, insider buying), but the combination of a full forward multiple, sharply falling near-term EPS estimates, and a poor recent delivery record prevents a BUY. We want confirmation that estimates stabilize and that the revenue inflection converts to EPS before paying ~46x forward.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters where reported EPS meets or beats consensus AND current-year EPS estimate revisions turn flat-to-positive over a 90-day window (reversing the current -33% trend).
- Confirmation that FY26 EPS lands at or above $4.49 with FY27 guidance implying margin expansion as the rights deals fully ramp, which would bring the forward multiple toward the low-30s on rising numbers.
- A pullback toward $170–180 with fundamentals intact, restoring asymmetric upside to the $234–275 target range.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Netflix opt-out signal on the Raw deal, or any material adverse renegotiation of a flagship rights contract.
- Current-year EPS estimates falling below ~$4.00 alongside another reported miss, indicating margin pressure is structural rather than transitional.
- A Saudi event disruption or escalation of McMahon-related litigation that materially impairs revenue visibility or reputation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the natural checkpoint, with particular attention to the EPS estimate trend into and out of the print and any update on the WWE/Netflix relationship.

## Conclusion

TKO is a high-quality business with a widening moat in scarce, owned live-sports IP, entering a multi-year, contracted media-rights re-rating that is real and durable. Free cash flow conversion is strong, leverage is modest despite a misleading distress screen, and the recent C-suite open-market buying is a credible conviction signal. The constraints are equally concrete: a ~46x forward multiple on EPS estimates that have fallen 33% in 90 days, three misses in the last four quarters, and a permanent governance discount from Silver Lake/Endeavor's ~63% voting control. The recommendation is HOLD with a positive bias: own it for the rights inflection and FCF, but wait for estimate stabilization or a better entry before adding, as the current price embeds a full multiple on a consensus number the market is still cutting.
