## Executive Summary

Middleby completed a two-step portfolio separation in the span of five months, and the company that trades today bears little resemblance to the one in most databases. On February 2, 2026 it sold 51% of Residential Kitchen Equipment to a 26North Partners affiliate at an $885M transaction value, retaining a 49% non-controlling stake. On July 6, 2026 it spun off Food Processing Equipment as Midera Food Processing (Nasdaq: MFP). What remains is a roughly $2.5B-revenue pure-play commercial foodservice equipment manufacturer — 75 brands, 43 production facilities, ~7% of a fragmented $35B global market — plus two illiquid financial assets and a retained UK defined-benefit pension scheme attached to a business it no longer controls.

The operating news is genuinely better than the two prior years. After Commercial Foodservice organic revenue declined 4.2% in FY2024 and 1.7% in FY2025 on weak chain-customer traffic and deferred replacement demand, Q1 FY2026 organic growth turned +8.1% (domestic +8.3%), and Q2 revenue of $875.5M beat the $836.8M consensus with adjusted EPS of $2.35 against $2.28. That is four consecutive quarterly beats. The problem is what the growth is worth: Commercial Foodservice gross margin fell 130bp to 40.0% and segment adjusted EBITDA margin fell 120bp to 25.7% *despite* 9.4% revenue growth — negative operating leverage driven by tariffs, input inflation and mix that management explicitly says it cannot assure it will fully offset. Full-year guidance of $2.48-2.53B revenue and $6.73-6.89 adjusted EPS implies Q3 revenue growth decelerating to roughly +7% and only ~2.7% half-over-half EPS growth. The stock fell 7% on the print and a further 8% the following session.

At $116.86 the stock trades at 17.2x the guided adjusted EPS midpoint of $6.81, below the peer median P/E of 28.8 and closer to the nearest analogues (ATMU 20.1x, MWA 18.8x). But Q2 GAAP EPS of $1.21 against $2.35 adjusted is a 48% haircut, and for a serial acquirer that has just written off $867M against a platform it assembled, capitalizing 17x on earnings that exclude roughly half of reported deductions is not the cushion the headline suggests. On normalized GAAP the multiple is closer to 21-23x. Base case is $130 (+11%); bear case $88 (-25%). We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

Middleby designs, manufactures, distributes and services commercial foodservice equipment, IoT platforms and universal controllers. The FY2025 10-K reported Commercial Foodservice at $2,351.0M (73.4% of continuing revenue) at a 40.2% gross margin and Food Processing at $850.2M (26.6%) at 36.3%; the latter is now gone. The remaining business runs 25 domestic and 18 international plants with 5,878 segment employees.

The moat, such as it is, rests on breadth and service density rather than product-level scale: roughly 75 trade names (Taylor, TurboChef, Blodgett, Pitco, Southbend, Follett, Star, Lincat, Josper, Synesso, QualServ), over 100 authorized parts distributors and 3,000 independent certified technicians, direct relationships with multinational chain accounts alongside a non-exclusive dealer network, and demonstration kitchens in Dallas, Germany and Spain. Named competitors are largely subsidiaries of much larger parents — ITW (Hobart, Vulcan-Hart), Berkshire's Marmon Foodservice Technologies, AB Electrolux, Midea, Haier, Panasonic — with Ali Group and Rational AG as focused pure-plays. The 10-K concedes several competitors have greater financial and personnel resources.

Two residual items matter. Middleby holds a 49% non-controlling interest in Composition Brands (carrying value $155.3M) accounted for under Hypothetical Liquidation at Book Value because of 26North's liquidation preferences, reported on a one-quarter lag with no earnings recognized in Q1. It also holds a $135.0M promissory note *receivable from* Composition Brands — $125.0M of it non-interest-bearing, maturing August 2033 — carried at $84.2M fair value, a 39% discount. Middleby is simultaneously Composition Brands' minority owner, its creditor, and its supplier of engineering, manufacturing and distribution services for up to three years. Combined, these assets are ~4.5% of market cap and are Level 3, illiquid and contingently realizable.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Continuing-operations revenue was $3,242.1M (FY2023), $3,150.2M (FY2024, -2.8%) and $3,201.2M (FY2025, +1.6%), with organic growth negative in both later years. Commercial Foodservice, the business that *is* Middleby now, was the weak link at -4.2% and -1.7% organic. Q1 FY2026 broke the pattern: total continuing revenue $839.9M (+15.0%, +11.9% organic), Commercial Foodservice $615.5M (+9.4%, +8.1% organic). Q2 revenue of $875.5M beat consensus but still consolidated Food Processing through July 4, so it is not a clean post-spin datapoint. We note one external report of "-10.5% year on year" Q2 revenue; that is inconsistent with the restated continuing-operations basis and appears to compare against an originally-reported prior-year total including Residential. We do not treat it as established.

**Margins are the problem.** Consolidated gross margin: 39.6% (FY2023), 39.7% (FY2024), 39.1% (FY2025), 38.5% (Q1 FY2026). Commercial Foodservice specifically fell from 41.3% in Q1 FY2025 to 40.0% in Q1 FY2026, attributed in the MD&A to tariffs, input cost inflation and mix. Income from continuing operations as a percentage of sales went 20.5% → 18.0% → 15.9%. SG&A rose from 18.7% of sales in FY2024 to 20.7% in FY2025 to 22.4% in Q1 FY2026. Some of that is identifiable and transitory — $19.8M of strategic transaction costs in FY2025 and $6.5M in Q1 FY2026 — but share-based compensation jumped from $2.3M to $10.1M year-over-year in Q1, consistent with separation-driven award modifications. The mechanism is straightforward: stainless and aluminized steel are the primary inputs, tariffs raise landed cost, and Middleby's pass-through through a non-exclusive dealer network lags by quarters. Segment adjusted EBITDA of $158.4M on $615.5M revenue in Q1 FY2026 versus $151.4M on $562.7M a year earlier means 9.4% revenue growth produced 4.6% EBITDA growth.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 continuing-operations operating cash flow was $564.6M against capex of $70.7M — $493.9M of free cash flow, a 15.4% margin, and comfortably above continuing net earnings of $367.3M. That is a genuine earnings-quality positive. Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow was only $87.8M versus $137.3M, entirely working capital: inventories -$40.6M, receivables -$38.3M, prepaids -$27.1M. We do not annualize either figure. Q1 is seasonally weak for a business management describes as stronger in Q2 and Q3, and receivables build mechanically when revenue inflects. Post-spin, on a ~$2.5B base with a higher-margin mix but also higher interest expense, we model $250-320M of free cash flow, a 4.7-6.1% yield on the $5.28B market capitalization. There is no dividend; the peer median yield is 0.5%.

**Balance sheet.** At April 4, 2026: total debt $1,874.0M, cash $177.1M, net debt $1,696.9M, equity $2,374.9M. Total-debt-to-equity is 0.79x and total-liabilities-to-equity 1.28x — materially less levered than screening outputs that report "2.27," which is the equity multiplier (assets ÷ equity), not debt-to-equity. Recomputing Altman Z with correct inputs (WC/TA 0.265, RE/TA 0.641, EBIT/TA 0.091, book equity/TL 0.785, sales/TA 0.507) gives 2.49 on book equity and 3.35 on market values — not the distress reading screens produce. Similarly, negative trailing net income, ROA and ROE all trace to the $645.0M discontinued-operations loss containing $709.1M of non-cash impairments on a business Middleby no longer controls; continuing operations earned $367.3M at an 18.0% operating margin.

That correction does not make the leverage benign. $1,765.9M of the $1,874.0M matures in 2028 (the revolver was extended from October 2026 to April 2028 by an August 2025 amendment, with $2.0B of remaining capacity and full covenant compliance). Borrowings are floating at SOFR + 1.485% all-in, averaging 4.73% inclusive of hedges, and the cheap hedges are rolling off — $155M notional at 1.05% within 12 months and $160M at 1.50% within 12-23 months. Interest expense already rose from $18.8M to $25.5M year-over-year in Q1 (+36%) on *lower* average debt, reflecting the retirement of the $747.5M 1.00% convertible notes (converted in Q3 FY2025, which also removed ~1.03M shares of dilution) plus hedge roll-off. Against an estimated ~$555M of post-spin adjusted EBITDA and estimated post-spin net debt of $1.4-1.6B, net leverage is roughly 2.7x — moderate for an industrial, but enough to constrain the buyback that has been the primary per-share value lever.

**The unresolved input.** We cannot establish post-spin net debt from available filings. Three items are unquantified: how the $233M distribution from Midera to Middleby Marshall was applied, Q2 buyback and free cash flow, and — most importantly — whether any of the $1,874.0M credit-facility debt was assumed by or allocated to Midera. Midera entered its own $1.0B five-year facility. Until the next 10-Q, all EV/EBITDA and leverage figures here carry that uncertainty.

## Growth Analysis

The bull case is a replacement-cycle recovery. Chain restaurant equipment purchases are discretionary capex deferrable for years; two years of deferral (domestic Commercial Foodservice organic -6.6% in FY2024, -1.8% in FY2025) created pent-up replacement demand, and management describes Q1 FY2026 as "an inflection in demand and higher volumes." The structural overlay is labor substitution — management's stated bet that automated equipment, IoT platforms and universal controllers deliver measurable ROI to operators facing wage inflation. That is a credible multi-year driver in a market management sizes above $35B.

Three things temper it. First, guidance implies deceleration. Q3 revenue guidance of $620-640M against the FY2025 Commercial Foodservice quarterly average of $587.8M is roughly +7% year-over-year, down from +9.4% reported in Q1. Reconciling the FY midpoint of $2,505M and $6.81 against Q3 guidance of ~$630M/$1.75 implies H1 of roughly $1,258M/$3.36 and H2 of roughly $1,247M/$3.45 — about 2.7% half-over-half EPS growth. This reconciliation also indicates the FY2026 guide is presented on a recast Commercial-Foodservice-only basis for all four quarters, which we treat as a derived inference rather than a disclosed fact; it is the only reading that reconciles with reported H1 revenue of $1,715.4M.

Second, backlog is not confirming. Commercial Foodservice backlog was $260.4M at January 3, 2026 versus $265.3M a year earlier, down 1.8%. Commercial Foodservice is largely book-and-ship — revenue recognized at the point control transfers on contractual shipping terms — so low backlog is normal and not directly comparable to the +59% growth in Food Processing's long-cycle project book. Still, flat backlog entering a year of 8-9% organic growth means the growth is being drawn from short-cycle order flow with roughly one quarter of visibility.

Third, the end market faces a consumer headwind. Chain store traffic drives replacement demand, and management noted that "recently announced interest rate cuts are expected to reduce demand headwinds in the long term." Middleby's cyclical torque works in both directions.

**On the estimate revisions.** The revision table shows current-year consensus falling 11.6% over 90 days to $8.22 and next-year falling 15.7% to $8.81. These are largely a basis change, not fundamental deterioration: H1 as-reported adjusted EPS was $4.51 ($2.16 + $2.35), and $4.51 plus H2 guidance of ~$3.45 equals $7.96 — close to the $8.22 consensus, confirming that sell-side models are on a blended as-reported-H1-plus-post-spin-H2 basis while management guides on a recast basis. We therefore do not read the -14% to -19% revisions as a fundamental signal, but nor do we credit next-year consensus of $8.81 as a usable post-spin base. Our own FY2027 estimate is $7.30-7.60, built on 4-6% revenue growth, partial tariff recovery and modest continued repurchase.

Peer P/Es (ESAB 28.8, FELE 31.6, JBTM 33.3, ATMU 20.1, MWA 18.8) are trailing GAAP and span a wide range; the closest structural analogues are the lower two. Comparing MIDD's 17.2x adjusted figure against a 28.8x GAAP median overstates the discount. The relevant question is what the adjusted-to-GAAP gap represents. Q2 GAAP EPS was $1.21 against $2.35 adjusted. Some of that gap — separation transaction costs, retention award modifications — is genuinely transitory. But acquired-intangible amortization is a recurring economic cost for a company that has impaired goodwill or trademarks in each of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 on continuing operations, and wrote off $709.1M plus a $62.8M valuation allowance plus a $94.9M deconsolidation loss ($866.8M total) on the Residential platform against $797.8M of value received. Excluding those charges from the multiple while ignoring that the underlying assets required write-down is not conservative.

Adjusted EBITDA cross-checks to roughly $555M: Q1 Commercial Foodservice segment adjusted EBITDA of $158.4M plus Corporate & Other of $(19.2)M annualizes near $557M, and working back from $308M of implied adjusted net income at a 24.5% tax rate plus $75-85M interest plus $50-85M D&A gives $540-575M. At $5.28B market cap, estimated $1.5B net debt and $239.5M of non-core assets, EV/EBITDA is ~11.9x.

**On price history and drawdown.** The 52-week high of $180.13 is a pre-spin, cum-Midera figure and must not be used as a drawdown reference. The first post-spin session closed at $139.26 on July 7. From there to $116.86 is -16.1%, a moderate decline. That decline is not unexplained: the Q2 guide introduced new information about the durability of the inflection, and the market repriced the multiple from roughly 19.5x to 17.2x on guided adjusted EPS. This is partly earned derating rather than pure sentiment, which is why we do not treat the lower price as automatically improving the risk/reward to an ACCUMULATE threshold — though it does move it in the right direction.

**On analyst targets.** Reported targets of $151 low / $168 mean / $206 high imply 29-76% upside. We treat these as unreliable. The most recent identified rating was a Buy with a $205 target dated before the July 6 spin, when the share carried Midera value. The current-year consensus figure itself is demonstrably on a blended basis, indicating incomplete recasting. Targets should not be read as floors here: the guardrail for treating consensus as stale-and-rising requires positive estimate momentum on a consistent basis, which is absent.

## Competitive Landscape

Middleby holds roughly 7% of a fragmented market where competition turns on brand recognition, features, reliability, price, lead times, serviceability and after-sale service. The realistic read on trajectory is stable. There is no evidence of share loss — Q1 domestic organic growth of +8.3% is unlikely against a losing position — and the retained segment is the higher-margin, higher-ROIC, less working-capital-intensive one at 25.7% segment EBITDA versus Food Processing's 18.5%. The service network of 3,000 certified technicians and 100-plus parts distributors is a genuine switching-cost asset in a market where downtime in a commercial kitchen is expensive.

Against that: the spin transferred the faster-growing, backlog-visible, margin-*expanding*, acquisition-fed segment to Midera. Five of Middleby's last six disclosed acquisitions went to Food Processing. Post-spin Middleby has flat backlog, compressing margin, and roughly one quarter of forward visibility, competing against divisions of ITW, Berkshire, Electrolux and Midea that can absorb tariff costs across broader portfolios. Acquisition activity has essentially stopped — $32.0M of cash used in FY2025, zero in Q1 FY2026 — which is prudent at 2.7x leverage but removes the growth mechanism the company relied on for two decades.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs (highest-conviction, currently biting).** Both the 10-K and 10-Q state tariffs have increased raw material and component costs with "no assurance of the company's ability to offset the impact of these tariffs fully." This is the named driver of the 130bp Commercial Foodservice gross margin decline. With heavy stainless and aluminized steel intensity and 18 international plants exposed to retaliatory measures, this is structural rather than transient.

**Goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible impairment.** Goodwill was 28% and indefinite-lived intangibles 13% of total assets at January 3, 2026; post-spin Commercial Foodservice goodwill is $1,295.3M and trademarks $837.7M. Management's conclusion that no interim indicators existed through April 4, 2026 was "supported by the review of order rates, backlog levels and financial performance" — but backlog was flat and segment margin was compressing. Given the demonstrated impairment record, this is a live risk to book equity and to GAAP earnings, though non-cash.

**Retained UK pension.** The AGA Rangemaster Group Pension Scheme covers employees of a division within the divested Residential group and was explicitly excluded from that transaction. Quarterly interest cost of $10.8M and expected asset return of $14.0M imply, at plausible discount and return assumptions, a gross plan on the order of $800-950M — roughly 15-18% of market capitalization — currently overfunded ($107.8M benefit asset) and generating ~$10M of annual non-cash pretax income. That reverses if discount rates fall or asset returns disappoint. The precise PBO is a verification gap.

**Refinancing.** $1,765.9M due 2028 on floating rates with cheap hedges rolling off. It caps the buyback.

**Undisclosed items.** Post-spin net debt, stranded corporate costs (Q1 Corporate & Other adjusted EBITDA drag was $(19.2)M per quarter with the Middleby/Midera split undisclosed), Q2 segment detail and margins, and customer concentration. No customer was disclosed above 10% of revenue in the sections reviewed; concentration is neither confirmed nor refuted, and the revenue-mix change from the separations establishes nothing about it.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 39.7% sits below one-year realized volatility of 44.0% (a -4.4% spread) yet at the 68th percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (39.7% at 8 days versus 34.1% at six months). Put/call open interest is 1.68 with a 5.0% skew indicating elevated put demand across all three tenors. IV compressed 3.5pp intraday after the print. The read: hedging demand persists after the guidance-driven derating rather than resolving with it, which corroborates rather than challenges the cautious fundamental view. Six-month IV at 34.1% against 44% realized makes longer-dated protection comparatively inexpensive for holders concerned about the 2028 refinancing or a goodwill event. Short interest of 4.7% of float and 2.8 days to cover means the bearish view is uncrowded — there is no squeeze cushion, and equally no positioning tailwind.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($160, +37%).** Tariff pass-through completes over two to three quarters and Commercial Foodservice gross margin recovers toward 41%, restoring the operating leverage that 8% organic growth should produce. Replacement-cycle demand proves durable as chains work through deferred capex, and the labor-substitution product set (IoT, universal controllers, automated cooking) sustains mid-to-high single-digit organic growth. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $8.20; a clean, higher-margin pure-play with 26% segment EBITDA margins earns 20x. Continued repurchase against the remaining 4.47M-share authorization (9.9% of shares) adds 3-4% annually. The activist director who bought ~$15M at prices of $143.57-$146.92 in December 2025 — a pre-spin, cum-Midera level — is proven right.

**Bear case ($88, -25%).** Chain store traffic weakens with the consumer and discretionary equipment capex is deferred again. Tariffs persist and margin compression continues, so revenue growth converts to nothing at the EBITDA line. Flat backlog and compressing margin become impairment triggers against $2.1B of goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles. Stranded corporate costs prove larger than modeled, 2.7x leverage plus the 2028 wall shuts down the buyback, and the composition-brands note and equity stake sit dead. FY2027 adjusted EPS of $5.80-6.00 at 14x.

**Base case ($130, +11%).** 17.5x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $7.40. Revenue grows 4-6%, half the tariff drag is recovered, buyback runs at a reduced pace given leverage, and the market keeps the multiple in the 17-18x range appropriate for a mid-single-digit grower with 2.7x leverage and one quarter of order visibility.

Probability-weighting 50% base, 20% bull and 30% bear yields roughly +6% expected return with a 2.3:1 downside-to-upside ratio against the bear case. That is a HOLD.

**On the score trajectory.** Scores have moved 5.7 → 6.5 → 6.3 → 5.8 → 5.4 across five reports, all HOLD. The step down this cycle is a basis change, not a price reaction: the $493.9M of FY2025 free cash flow and the $3.2B revenue base no longer belong to this company, the retained segment is the one with flat backlog and compressing margin, and guidance now quantifies the deceleration that was previously only a risk. Financial health moves down modestly from 6.3 to 5.9 for the same reason — the corrected leverage picture (0.79x debt/equity, Z of 2.49-3.35) is genuinely better than screens show, but leverage against the *reduced* EBITDA base is ~2.7x with an unresolved post-spin net debt figure. Valuation improves slightly from 5.5 as the multiple compressed to 17.2x. A fifth consecutive HOLD requires a higher bar; it clears here because the rating now rests on different evidence — an uncertain post-spin earnings base at a no-longer-demanding entry price, rather than fair value on a larger, more diversified business.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 FY2026 10-Q (expected mid-November 2026) is the decisive filing. It should resolve post-spin net debt, stranded corporate costs, the first clean quarter of standalone Commercial Foodservice margin, and an updated backlog.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Commercial Foodservice gross margin recovers to 40.8% or better for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating tariff pass-through, with segment adjusted EBITDA margin back above 26.5%.
2. Post-spin net debt confirmed at or below $1.4B with net leverage under 2.5x, and the buyback resuming at scale against the remaining 4.47M-share authorization.
3. Commercial Foodservice backlog growing year-over-year alongside organic growth sustained above 6%, which would convert the inflection from order-flow-dependent to visible.
4. Price toward $100-105, which at $7.40 FY2027 adjusted EPS is 13.5-14.2x and restores a genuine margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Organic Commercial Foodservice growth decelerating below 3% year-over-year, or FY2026 revenue guidance cut below $2.48B.
2. Segment adjusted EBITDA margin below 24.5%, indicating tariff and inflation pressure is permanent rather than a pass-through lag.
3. Any goodwill or indefinite-lived intangible impairment against the $1,295.3M / $837.7M carrying values, or an interim triggering-event disclosure.
4. Post-spin net debt disclosed above $1.8B, or evidence that stranded corporate costs exceed $40M annually.
5. Covenant amendment or buyback suspension ahead of the 2028 maturity.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** maintain. The 15.8% share-count reduction over 18 months at a distribution-adjusted average roughly in line with current combined value, plus an aligned ~7.5% holder on the board who bought in the open market at $143.57-$146.92 (a pre-spin level), argue against selling into a guide-driven derating. New capital has no urgency at $116.86; the asymmetry improves materially below $105.

## Conclusion

Middleby has executed a real simplification: two separations in five months, a 15.8% reduction in shares outstanding, a retired convertible, and a portfolio narrowed to the higher-margin business. The balance sheet is better than standardized screens report — 0.79x debt-to-equity, not 2.27x; a corrected Altman Z of 2.49-3.35, not 2.0; and continuing-operations net income of $367.3M rather than the headline loss driven by non-cash impairments on a divested platform.

What the simplification did not do is resolve the earnings question. The retained business grew 8.1% organically in Q1 and produced 4.6% EBITDA growth, because tariffs and input inflation took 120-130bp of margin. Guidance says the growth decelerates from here. Backlog is flat and the business converts orders to revenue within a quarter, so there is roughly one quarter of visibility. Half of guided adjusted EPS is excluded from GAAP earnings, at a company whose acquired intangibles required $866.8M of write-downs against $797.8M of value received. And three inputs that determine whether 17.2x is cheap or fair — post-spin net debt, stranded costs, and the guidance basis — are not yet in the public record.

Base case $130 (+11%) against bear case $88 (-25%) is a positive but unattractively skewed expected return. **HOLD.**
