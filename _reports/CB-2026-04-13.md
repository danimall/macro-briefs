## Executive Summary

Chubb Limited is the world's largest publicly traded P&C insurer, and its FY2025 results confirm the quality of the franchise: record net income of $10.31B (+11.2% YoY), record net investment income of $6.5B, and a current accident year combined ratio (ex-cats) of 81.9% — a 200 basis point improvement over two years. The company returned $4.9B to shareholders through buybacks ($3.4B) and dividends ($1.5B), and shareholders' equity grew 15.2% to $73.8B.

The stock has appreciated ~15% over the past year and now trades at approximately 12.3x forward earnings on consensus FY2026 EPS of $26.86. This remains cheap relative to the quality of the business. Two structural headwinds temper the outlook: Bermuda's new 15% corporate income tax has pushed the effective rate from 5.4% (2023) to 18.6% (2025), and catastrophe losses are trending higher ($1.8B → $2.4B → $2.9B over three years), with the California wildfires alone costing $1.47B in 2025. Geopolitical risk exposure through the Huatai China consolidation ($8.5B in Chinese corporate bond holdings) adds a tail risk in the current environment.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) sustained higher interest rates generate incremental investment income — the portfolio's 5.0% market yield on fixed maturities exceeds the 4.5% book yield, creating a reinvestment tailwind that adds roughly $300-500M in annual investment income as the portfolio turns over; (2) underlying underwriting profitability continues to improve, with casualty rate increases outpacing loss trends in most lines; (3) aggressive capital return reduces share count by approximately 2-3% annually. At 12.3x earnings with these dynamics, Chubb remains a BUY.

## Company Overview

Chubb operates six segments across 54 countries, writing $54.8B in net premiums in FY2025. North America Commercial P&C ($21.3B, 39% of NPW) is the largest segment, followed by Overseas General ($15.0B, 27%), Life Insurance ($7.3B, 13%), North America Personal P&C ($7.0B, 13%), Agriculture ($2.9B, 5%), and Global Reinsurance ($1.3B, 2%). The company's competitive moat derives from its global distribution network, best-in-class underwriting discipline, dominant position in high-net-worth personal lines and specialty commercial, and a $169B investment portfolio that generates durable float income.

The 2016 ACE-Chubb merger created this entity, and the more recent Huatai Insurance Group consolidation (~83.2% stake) makes Chubb the first foreign company to hold majority ownership of a Chinese financial services company, providing access to 19M customers across 400+ branches in 20 Chinese provinces.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Power

FY2025 net income of $10.31B represents a 24.8% three-year CAGR from the $5.3B earned in FY2022. The composition of earnings has improved meaningfully:

- **Underwriting income**: The P&C combined ratio improved to 85.7% (from 86.6% in FY2024), with the current accident year combined ratio (ex-cats) at 81.9% vs. 83.9% two years ago. This 200bp improvement on $47.5B in P&C net premiums translates to roughly $950M in incremental underwriting profit.
- **Investment income**: Record $6.5B, up from $5.9B in FY2024 and $4.9B in FY2023. The portfolio's average credit quality is A/A with a 5.0-year duration. Because the 5.0% market yield on fixed maturities exceeds the 4.5% book yield, approximately $30-35B of the portfolio matures or turns over annually, providing incremental yield pickup.
- **Life insurance**: A meaningful and growing contributor, with 15.1% premium growth (17.3% constant currency) driven by North Asia and the Chubb Benefits worksite business (+32.1%).

### Balance Sheet

Total assets of $272.3B support $73.8B in shareholders' equity. Financial debt of $17.2B represents a conservative 18.4% debt-to-capital ratio. Goodwill of $20.2B (27% of equity) is substantial but stable, reflecting the ACE-Chubb merger and subsequent acquisitions. Net unpaid loss reserves of approximately $70B (gross $88B) have consistently proved conservative, with $1.13B in favorable prior period development in FY2025 vs. $856M in FY2024.

The Altman Z-Score of 0.84 is a false signal; the model was designed for industrial companies and breaks for insurers due to their liability-heavy balance sheets, low asset turnover, and negative working capital by design. The actual indicators — 14.4% ROE, AA financial strength ratings, $12.8B operating cash flow — confirm robust financial health.

### Capital Return

Chubb returned $4.9B to shareholders in FY2025: $3.4B in buybacks (at an average price of $282.57, below the current price) and $1.5B in dividends ($3.88/share annually, yielding ~1.2%). A new $5.0B repurchase authorization took effect in July 2025 with $2.1B remaining as of late February 2026. At the current pace, the company reduces share count by approximately 2-3% annually, creating a compounding effect on per-share earnings growth. Dividends are paid from capital contribution reserves exempt from Swiss 35% withholding tax, sustainable through 2032-2036.

### Earnings Surprise Track Record

Over the last four quarters, Chubb beat consensus in three of four, with surprise magnitudes of +12.3%, -0.2%, +18.2%, and +7.7%. The Q3 2025 result (+18.2% beat) was driven by favorable loss experience and strong investment income. The single miss in Q2 2025 was negligible (-0.2%). This pattern supports confidence in current consensus estimates.

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year (FY2026) consensus EPS has risen from $26.28 to $26.86 over the past 90 days (+2.2%). Next-year (FY2027) estimates have risen from $28.60 to $29.12 (+1.8%). The 30-day revisions are more modest (+0.3% and +0.2%), suggesting the upward trajectory is decelerating but still positive. No analyst has cut estimates meaningfully, and the revision breadth across 24-25 covering analysts indicates broad-based confidence.

## Growth Analysis

Chubb's growth profile combines modest premium growth with margin expansion and investment income accretion:

- **Premium growth**: Net premiums written grew 6.4% in FY2025 ($54.8B vs. $51.5B). Constant currency growth was approximately 8%. The primary drivers were casualty rate increases, North America personal auto repricing (+19.6%), and international life/health growth (+17.3% constant currency).
- **Investment income**: Growing at roughly 10% annually as the portfolio turns over into higher yields. This tailwind persists as long as rates remain elevated, and with the Fed funds rate at 3.64% and inflation tracking above target, rate cuts appear unlikely in 2026.
- **Life segment**: The fastest-growing segment at 15% premium growth, driven by Huatai's Chinese operations and the Chubb Benefits worksite franchise. This segment contributed $685M in income in FY2025, up 33% from $515M in FY2024.
- **Share count reduction**: 2-3% annual buyback yield adds to per-share growth.

The prior score trajectory shows Growth Prospects declining from 7.5 to 6.5 over the past two months, reflecting pricing deceleration in certain lines (brokerage property, financial lines) and the math of growing off a higher base. Premium growth has moderated from the mid-single-digit constant-currency pace seen in prior periods. However, the Life segment acceleration and continued casualty rate adequacy provide reasonable offsets. The 6.5 score remains appropriate.

The forward P/E of 11.3x on FY2027 is cheap for a company compounding book value at 10-12% annually with a 14.4% ROE.

## Competitive Landscape

Chubb's competitive position is its strongest attribute. Key advantages:

1. **Underwriting discipline**: An 81.9% CAY combined ratio (ex-cats) is among the best in the global P&C industry, reflecting decades of institutional underwriting culture that survived the ACE-Chubb merger integration intact.

2. **Geographic diversification**: Operations in 54 countries provide natural diversification of catastrophe risk, regulatory regimes, and growth opportunities. No single geography dominates the premium base.

3. **Distribution breadth**: The company serves every segment from large multinational corporates to individual high-net-worth households, through brokers, agents, direct-to-consumer, and digital channels. This breadth provides pricing intelligence across the entire risk spectrum.

4. **Investment portfolio scale**: A $169B portfolio generating $6.5B in income is a structural advantage that smaller competitors cannot replicate. The ability to invest patient capital at scale across asset classes, including $12.9B in below-investment-grade holdings managed by a dedicated team, generates excess returns.

5. **China access**: The Huatai consolidation provides a first-mover advantage in China's insurance market that no Western competitor can match. While this introduces geopolitical risk, the long-term option value of accessing a market projected to be the world's second-largest by premiums is substantial.

The primary competitive threats are (a) pricing pressure in property-related lines as reinsurance capital returns post-catastrophe, (b) InsurTech competitors in small commercial and personal lines, and (c) financial lines loss trends that may require repricing.

## Risk Assessment

### Catastrophe Trend

Net catastrophe losses have escalated: $1.8B (2023) → $2.4B (2024) → $2.9B (2025). The California wildfires ($1.47B) were the dominant driver in 2025, and Chubb's personal lines exposure to high-value properties in cat-prone zones creates recurring vulnerability. The company's modeled 1-in-100 worldwide annual aggregate PML of $5.9B (7.9% of equity) would absorb a year's worth of underwriting income but would not threaten solvency. Climate-related cat frequency is a secular headwind.

### Bermuda Corporate Income Tax

The new 15% Bermuda CIT is a permanent structural change. The effective tax rate has tripled from 5.4% (2023) to 18.6% (2025). OECD Administrative Guidance from January 2025 could cause additional tax if the deferred tax asset established in 2023 reverses after 2026. This represents a 2-4% headwind to after-tax earnings growth that did not exist three years ago.

### Long-Tail Reserve Risk

$88B in gross reserves, 76% casualty-related. Workers' comp sensitivity: 1% change in tail factor = $1.1B reserve change. Excess/Umbrella: 5% tail factor change = $900M. Legacy asbestos survival ratio of only 2.9 years (net). Corporate run-off had $306M in adverse development in 2025. Social inflation — nuclear verdicts, litigation funding, reviver statutes for molestation claims — is an industry-wide headwind that Chubb's casualty concentration makes it particularly vulnerable to.

### China / Geopolitical Exposure

The Huatai consolidation introduces tail risk in a period of escalating U.S.-China tensions. The 50% tariff threat on Chinese goods and intelligence-related disputes over Iranian weapons shipments create a backdrop where any deterioration in bilateral relations could impair Chubb's ability to operate or repatriate capital from China.

### Interest Rate Sensitivity

While higher rates benefit investment income, the liability side is affected if rates decline unexpectedly. The portfolio's 5.0-year duration vs. 7.0-year liability duration means Chubb is slightly short duration — protective in a rising rate environment but modestly negative if rates fall. Given the current inflation trajectory (March CPI 3.3%, 17+ inflationary channels active), rate cuts appear unlikely, making this risk low-probability for the next 12 months.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($370-385)

Rate adequacy in casualty lines persists through 2026-2027, driving continued CAY combined ratio improvement toward 80%. Investment income grows to $7.0B+ as portfolio turnover captures higher reinvestment yields. Life segment maintains 15%+ growth, with Huatai contributing incremental margin. Share count declines 3% annually. Catastrophe experience normalizes toward $2.0-2.5B. On $29+ in FY2027 EPS at 13x, the stock reaches $377-385.

### Bear Case ($264-280)

Above-average catastrophe year (major U.S. hurricane + continued wildfire losses) pushes cat losses to $4.0B+. Casualty reserve development turns adverse as social inflation accelerates. China operations face regulatory friction from geopolitical tensions. Tax rate continues climbing toward 20%+. On reduced EPS of $23-24 at 11.5x, the stock trades to $264-276.

The 13.6x multiple reflects a modest premium to historical averages, justified by the durability of the investment income stream at elevated rates.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. If the FY2026 CAY combined ratio (ex-cats) deteriorates above 83.0% (reversing the two-year improvement trend), exit the position — this would signal pricing adequacy has reversed.
2. If net catastrophe losses exceed $4.0B in any 12-month period while management does not materially reduce personal lines cat exposure, reassess position sizing.
3. If China-related regulatory actions impair Chubb's ability to operate or repatriate capital from Huatai, exit the position.

## Conclusion

Chubb remains a high-conviction BUY at $329. The franchise quality — reflected in an 81.9% CAY combined ratio, $6.5B in investment income, and $73.8B in equity — is exceptional among global P&C insurers. The valuation at 12.3x forward earnings does not fully reflect the durability of the investment income tailwind in a higher-rate environment, the continued underwriting margin improvement, or the compounding effect of annual share count reduction.

The score trajectory has declined from 7.8 to 7.4 over the past two months, driven by Growth Prospects deceleration and stock price appreciation reducing the valuation cushion. Premium growth is moderating and the stock is no longer at a deep discount. However, the fundamental earnings trajectory remains positive (estimates rising, investment income compounding, underwriting improving), and the risk-adjusted return profile at 12.3x earnings compares favorably to most alternatives in the current macro environment. The primary risks — catastrophe losses, Bermuda CIT, and China exposure — are genuine but quantifiable and manageable within the current capital structure.

At an overall score of 7.24, reflecting the modestly declining trajectory, Chubb warrants continued ownership with a 12-month base case target of $365 (11% upside).
