## Executive Summary

Cheniere's operating year has improved materially since our last review, and the stock has moved less than the fundamentals. Management raised 2026 Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA guidance for a second consecutive quarter to $7.9–8.4B, against FY2025 actual of $6,943M. Corpus Christi Stage 3 finished the quarter 98.4% complete with six of seven midscale trains at substantial completion and Train 7 in commissioning ahead of its guaranteed 2027 date. The June 2026 election of the normal-purchases-normal-sales (NPNS) scope exception on ~73% of fixed minimum IPM volumes cut disclosed 10% commodity sensitivity from $2,722M to $649M and shrank the Level 3 derivative position from a $2,902M asset to $306M — a 76% reduction in reported-earnings volatility with no change to cash flow, contract terms, or incentive metrics. Three rating agencies upgraded within twelve months, and leverage on 2026E guidance sits at 3.15x against a stated ~4x target.

The reason this does not translate into a stronger rating is composition and price. Roughly $370M of the 2026 EBITDA guidance is a non-recurring recognition of 2018–2024 federal alternative-fuel excise credits following an IRS closing letter; ex-credit, 2026E is ~$7,780M and growth over FY2025 is +12.1% rather than +17.4%. More important, the marketing margin that drove the raise is already sold: Q2 marketing realizations were $16.24/MMBtu on 123 TBtu versus $6.64/MMBtu on 534 TBtu of long-term SPA volume (81% of the quarter, *down* 7.8% year over year), and management indicated less than 1 Mt of open 2026 volume remains with a $1 margin change now worth under $50M of full-year EBITDA. Consensus agrees: next-year EPS has been cut 4.6% in 30 days to $18.61 even as current-year estimates rose 22.9% over 90 days.

At $271.64 the shares trade at 10.5x 2026E Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA (11.0x ex-credit) on a $85.9B enterprise value that properly includes the $5.3B non-controlling interest, and at 10.2x 2026E distributable cash flow per share of $26.70. Our base case of $290 offers +6.8% plus a 0.8% dividend; a bear case of $200 is -26%. Probability-weighted, expected 12-month total return is roughly +3%. That is a HOLD — held for the visible multi-decade capacity build and the $9.1B remaining buyback authorization (16.2% of market cap), not for near-term asymmetry.

## Company Overview

Cheniere is the largest U.S. LNG producer and the second-largest LNG operator globally by liquefaction capacity, with over 60 mtpa of expected production capacity inclusive of debottlenecking, of which over 6 mtpa was under construction at 6/30/2026. The asset base sits in two structurally separate financing silos: Sabine Pass (>30 mtpa) held through the publicly traded MLP Cheniere Energy Partners, in which Cheniere owns 100% of the GP interest, a 48.6% LP interest and 100% of the incentive distribution rights, with Blackstone, Brookfield affiliates and the public holding the 49.4% non-controlling LP interest; and Corpus Christi (>30 mtpa), wholly owned, including the seven-train Stage 3 midscale project and the ~5 mtpa Trains 8 & 9 project.

The commercial model is a toll, and the distinction between the two contract types drives the accounting. Under SPAs, Cheniere is the seller and collects a fixed fee per MMBtu on contracted volumes whether or not the customer lifts the cargo, plus a variable fee generally set at 115% of Henry Hub sized to cover feedgas, transportation and liquefaction fuel — a structure that largely neutralizes U.S. gas price exposure. Roughly 30 third-party customers hold SPAs with initial terms of ten years or longer. Under IPM agreements Cheniere is the *buyer* of gas priced off a global LNG or international gas index less a fixed liquefaction fee and shipping. Across both, 90% or more of anticipated production is contracted through the mid-2030s with a weighted average remaining life of approximately 15 years.

That buyer role explains why 1H 2026 GAAP results look catastrophic while cash flow accelerated. When global LNG prices spike relative to Henry Hub, the mark on the IPM purchase obligation moves against Cheniere immediately, while the offsetting LNG sales at those elevated prices are recognized on delivery. Cheniere reported a 1H 2026 net loss attributable to the company of $(434)M, or $(2.08) per diluted share, while operating cash flow rose 29% to $2,658M and guidance was raised twice.

## Financial Analysis

**The accounting problem is now substantially fixed.** For four years, GAAP earnings have been dominated by non-cash derivative marks: the FY2025 10-K discloses $3,600M of pre-tax derivative fair-value gains inside FY2025 net income, or 43.5% of the $8,282M pre-tax figure ($3,600 ÷ $8,282). Q2 2026 carried a $1.4B favorable swing; 1H 2026 carried a $3.4B unfavorable swing. The June 2026 NPNS designation removed 4,266 TBtu of notional from derivative accounting, reclassifying the position into a net $349M deferred liability that amortizes into cost of sales on a disclosed schedule: +$56M (2026), +$100M (2027), +$101M (2028), +$85M (2029), +$56M (2030), then $(747)M "thereafter." The election is not retrospective, so the $(2.2)B of 1H marks stay in the P&L, and the contracts snap back to fair value with immediate recognition if they ever fail the NPNS test.

**Reconciling the reported multiples.** The data feed shows trailing EPS of $24.13 (that is FY2025) alongside a trailing P/E of 19.9. Summing the four reported quarters — $4.75 + $10.68 + $(16.75) + $14.65 — gives TTM GAAP diluted EPS of $13.33, which at $271.64 is 20.4x and reconciles the feed's P/E. Coincidentally, that lands inside our estimate of FY2025 EPS excluding derivative marks (~$12–14 per share, derived by allocating 78–90% of the $3,600M gain to Cheniere at the 18.0% consolidated effective rate over 220.3M diluted shares). The practical conclusion is that neither GAAP EPS number is usable as a valuation anchor; EBITDA and DCF are.

**Cash generation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $5,539M against $3,078M of capex, giving $2,461M of FCF. Rolling to TTM: OCF ≈ $6,138M, capex ≈ $3,347M, FCF ≈ $2,791M, or 5.0% of market cap. That number should not be treated as steady state — capex is overwhelmingly growth spend on Stage 3, Trains 8 & 9 and the SPL Expansion LNTP, and derivative settlement timing swung by roughly $1.0B between 1H periods.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt was $24,257M at 6/30/2026, up $1,262M from year-end, plus $1,413M of finance lease liabilities. Against FY2025 Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $6,943M that is 3.70x; against the $8,150M 2026E midpoint, 3.15x; net of $1,519M cash and current restricted cash, 2.96x — inside the company's ~4x target and improving because EBITDA is growing faster than debt. Liquidity was $7,475M with all covenants in compliance. Financing was actively termed out and cheapened: parent issues of $1.0B 5.200% '36 and $750M 6.000% '56 in March; CQP issues of $1.0B 5.350% '36 and $750M 6.050% '56 in June, fully redeeming $1.5B of SPL 5.00% notes due 2027; the parent revolver upsized $500M to $1.75B; CCH's working capital facility restated into a $1.0B revolver at lower pricing. Moody's took Cheniere senior unsecured to Baa2 and CCH senior secured to Baa1 in February 2026; S&P moved Cheniere and CQP to BBB+ in November 2025; Fitch to BBB in February 2025.

Two structural qualifiers matter. First, $17.2B of consolidated VIE assets and $16.9B of VIE liabilities are CQP obligations with explicitly no recourse to the parent — roughly 46% of consolidated liabilities — while Cheniere's economic claim on Sabine Pass is 48.6% LP plus GP and IDRs. Second, SPL and CCH must satisfy both historical and projected 1.25:1.00 debt service coverage before distributing, so consolidated cash is not fully fungible to the parent. The screen-level Altman Z of 1.41 is a construction artifact here: substituting market equity for book in the X4 term ($56.5B ÷ $34.8B of liabilities × 0.6) adds roughly +0.83 to about 2.24, book equity is mechanically suppressed by $9,949M of treasury stock, and sales/assets of 0.42 penalizes long-lived infrastructure by design. The underlying flags are still real — current ratio 0.94, $1,411M of current debt against $1,099M of cash — but three upgrades and $7.5B of liquidity settle the solvency question.

**Capital returns.** FY2025 repurchases were 12.1M shares for $2.7B at a $221.55 weighted average. 1H 2026 was 4.88M shares for $1,087M at $222.59, with monthly Q2 volume rising as the price fell (593,373 at $261.29 in April; 1,057,093 at $243.80 in May; 582,124 at $235.76 in June). The February 2026 authorization increase to ~$10B for 2026–2030 leaves $9,113M outstanding, 16.2% of current market cap. The 1H pace annualizes to ~$2,174M, or 3.87% of market cap; the $2.22 annual dividend adds 0.83%, for ~4.7% of total shareholder yield. Shares outstanding fell from 224.0M (12/31/2024) to 207.9M (6/30/2026), -7.2% in eighteen months. Dividends consume roughly 8.3% of 2026E DCF, leaving ample coverage for the stated ≥10% annual growth commitment.

One arithmetic note without an inference attached: 1H 2026 OCF of $2,658M funded $3,920M of capex, buybacks, dividends, NCI distributions and redemptions, with the $1,262M gap covered by $1,248M of net debt issuance. Cash is fungible and the filings do not assign sources to uses; what is established is that total debt rose $1.26B in six months while leverage-to-EBITDA fell.

## Growth Analysis

The volume pipeline is the largest in the industry and it is brownfield, which is the relevant distinction because it removes siting, permitting and interconnect risk from the incremental capacity.

- **Stage 3** was 98.4% complete at quarter-end (construction 96.0%), up from 94.1% at year-end. Trains 5 and 6 reached substantial completion in March and June 2026; Train 7 is in commissioning with first LNG imminent and substantial completion expected within months, ahead of the guaranteed 2027 date.
- **Trains 8 & 9** (~5 mtpa, FID June 2025, ~$2.9B Bechtel lump-sum turnkey) advanced from 31.8% to 48.3% complete, expected 2H 2028.
- **FERC authorized a ~5 mtpa aggregate capacity increase** in June 2026 to already-approved Stage 3 and Trains 8 & 9 — capacity gained without new construction approval risk.
- **SPL Stage V Phase 1** signed a Bechtel LSTK EPC contract on May 22, 2026 (~$4.7B per the earnings call) covering Sabine Pass Train 7 (~5 mtpa) plus a boil-off-gas reliquefaction unit adding ~1 mtpa, with $99M spent in 1H under a limited notice to proceed and formal FID expected early 2027.
- **CCL Expansion** — four trains, up to 24 mtpa — was filed with FERC in February 2026, targeting FID 2027/2028.

Guidance composition is better than the headline. Management attributed more than two-thirds of the increase from the original production outlook to reliability improvement, lower downtime and reduced maintenance, with roughly one-third from the Stage 3 ramp. Reliability gains compound across the fleet; a startup ramp is a step function. Production guidance tightened to 53–54 Mt.

Against that, per-share earnings growth faces a 2027 air pocket. The $370M excise credit does not repeat (4.5% of guidance midpoint). Marketing margin normalizes if Hormuz reopens — Bloomberg reported on August 11 that Qatar is loading the most LNG in months in preparation. Ad valorem property tax abatements begin expiring at Corpus Christi in 2026 and Sabine Pass in 2027 against a FY2025 abated base of only ~$89M, and the 115%-of-Henry-Hub variable fee is scoped to gas, transportation and fuel, so this lands on margin rather than on customers. Cash taxes normalize: 1H 2026 cash taxes paid net of refunds were $30M versus $411M in 1H 2025, the Q2 effective rate was 9.1% versus 18.3%, and $3,629M of deferred tax liabilities means bonus depreciation deferred rather than forgave the obligation, even with the new FDDEI deduction providing roughly a 14% effective rate on eligible export income.

Consensus captures this shape: current-year EPS revised +21.5% in 30 days and +22.9% in 90 days to $21.75, while next-year fell -4.6% and -3.4% to $18.61. The current-year revisions are partly a mixed-basis artifact — Q2 actual GAAP EPS of $14.65 against a $3.07 estimate reflects derivative marks that the estimate excluded — so the out-year cut is the more informative forward signal. We do not treat the +22.9% current-year revision as an inflection buy signal here, because it does not reflect a change in cash earnings power.

## Valuation Assessment

Because Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA consolidates 100% of CQP, the matching enterprise value must include the non-controlling interest:

| Component | $M |
|---|---|
| Equity market cap (207.9M shares × $271.64) | 56,474 |
| Total debt (6/30/26) | 24,257 |
| Finance lease liabilities (PV) | 1,413 |
| Less cash + current restricted cash | (1,519) |
| Plus non-controlling interest (book) | 5,299 |
| **Enterprise value** | **85,924** |

- EV / 2026E Consolidated Adj. EBITDA ($8,150M midpoint) = **10.5x**
- EV / 2026E ex-excise-credit ($7,780M) = **11.0x**
- EV / FY2025 actual ($6,943M) = **12.4x**
- 2026E DCF per share = $5,550M ÷ 207.9M = **$26.70** → **10.2x**, a 9.8% DCF yield
- 2027 consensus EPS $18.61 → **14.6x**
- TTM GAAP diluted EPS $13.33 → **20.4x**

Two caveats on the EV: NCI is carried at book rather than the market value of CQP units, so if CQP trades above book the multiples are understated; and Cheniere's DCF is a parent measure struck after debt service, maintenance capex and cash taxes but *before* growth capex, so a 9.8% DCF yield is not a free-cash-flow-to-equity yield. GAAP TTM FCF yield is 5.0%.

Peer context is imperfect. The supplied Energy comparables median 16.0x P/E, but they span E&P (DVN 9.6x, OXY 8.8x, FANG 39.0x) and midstream (TRGP 25.3x, OKE 16.0x) on GAAP earnings that are not comparable to Cheniere's mark-distorted figures. On the EV/EBITDA basis that matters for contracted infrastructure, 10.5x — 11.0x ex-credit — is broadly in line with large-cap midstream, with Cheniere carrying a materially longer contracted duration (~15-year weighted average) and a far larger identified growth runway, offset by more leverage and a 51.4% non-controlling claim on its largest asset.

We do not find a specific mispricing. The market appears to be discounting the 2026 marketing windfall correctly, which is what the falling 2027 estimates and the 9.7% discount to the March high represent. The consensus mean target of $306.73 (median $305.50) sits 13% above spot, but at ~11x EBITDA with out-year estimates being cut, analyst targets are not a reliable floor — that condition applies to cheap stocks with rising estimates, and only half of it holds here.

Our base case values the equity at 11.0x 2026E EBITDA of $8.15B (assuming full-year Stage 3 volumes in 2027 offset marketing-margin compression): EV $89.65B, less $24.26B debt and $1.41B finance leases, plus $1.52B cash, less $5.30B NCI = $60.2B, or **$290 per share**. That is +6.8% from $271.64, +7.6% with the dividend.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's dominant 2026 advantage is geographic. As the largest U.S. exporter shipping from the Gulf Coast, it has zero Strait of Hormuz transit exposure, while Qatar (~77–80 mtpa of capacity, no pipeline bypass) has been substantially cut off. Management put the Qatari and UAE supply loss at roughly 18 Mt in Q2 alone against a ~3 Mt year-over-year decline in global LNG exports, and reported that LNG transit recovery through Hormuz remained below 10% of pre-conflict levels even after the mid-June ceasefire versus ~25% for crude tankers. U.S. exports to Asia hit a quarterly record ~11 Mt, and Europe exited Q2 with an ~11 bcm storage deficit.

The durable advantages are structural: brownfield expansion land already owned at two permitted sites with existing berths, storage and pipeline interconnects; DOE export authorizations to December 31, 2050; a repeat Bechtel lump-sum turnkey EPC relationship that transfers cost-overrun risk to the contractor in an inflationary construction market; and an operating record of over 4,940 cumulative cargoes and 340 million tonnes to more than 40 countries as of July 31, 2026. The LSTK protection is not absolute — Q2 Exhibit 10.1 is Change Order CO-00120, an "Acceleration Program Extension" for Trains 6 and 7 dated April 6, 2026, with portions omitted, evidence that change orders do flow back to the owner. Cheniere has also positioned for EU methane regulation with intensity below its 0.03% target under third-party limited assurance, OGMP 2.0 Gold Standard in 2025, and cargo emissions tagging since 2022 — a compliance moat against less-instrumented suppliers as EU Regulation 2024/1787 phases in.

The trajectory is where we hold the score at 7.0 rather than higher. The FY2025 10-K reported ~20 mtpa of global supply added in 2025 with Asian demand down 4% (China -16%) and management's own expectation that "the market will loosen and prices trend lower" — a view the Q2 10-Q reverses to "continued tightening." Both were accurate to their moment, which is the point: the marketing spread is not forecastable, and a 2027–2030 supply build is scheduled regardless of Hormuz. Customer concentration also reappeared: no customer exceeded 10% of FY2025 revenue, but Q2 2026 Note 15 shows Customer C at 12% of revenue and 11% of receivables, with Customer A flat at 15% of trade receivables and contract assets at both 6/30/2026 and 12/31/2025. The shift toward more short-term marketing counterparties changed the sales channel; it did not reduce credit concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin headwinds that land on Cheniere, not customers.** Ad valorem abatement roll-off begins now, and the 10-K does not disclose the fully unabated level — the ~$89M FY2025 figure is the abated amount, so the step-up magnitude is unquantified. The Section 301 action requires 1% of U.S. LNG exports on U.S.-built vessels from April 2029, rising to 15% by April 2047; the November 2025 China trade deferral did not modify that timeline, and the 10-K flags feasibility and cost concerns.

**Shipping obligations.** Beyond $3,048M of operating and $1,889M of finance lease payments on the books, the 10-Q discloses ~$6.0B of legally binding minimum payments for leases executed at 6/30/2026 that commence in future periods, primarily vessel charters with fixed terms up to fifteen years. Total forward lease commitment is roughly $10.9B. Sublease income fell from $28M (Q2 2025) to $11M (Q2 2026) with future minimum sublease receipts of just $2M — the fleet is being absorbed internally, so DAP cargo economics must carry the charter cost.

**Accounting.** The NPNS amortization schedule is front-loaded with gains (+$398M cumulative 2026–2030, roughly $100M/year, ~1.2% of 2026E EBITDA) and back-loaded with $(747)M "thereafter." The pattern is mechanical given delivery schedules, but it flatters the next five reporting years and defers the offset. The 27% of IPM volumes delivered upstream of the plants remain marked to market, and a failed NPNS test triggers immediate fair-value recognition.

**Contract and structural.** SPAs may be terminated for failure to make scheduled cargo quantities available, for delays in commercial operations, and in a majority of cases for force majeure; loss of a DOE authorization could itself be a force majeure event. Only ~70% of 2026 forecast feedgas was secured under long-term supply agreements at year-end (78% including IPM), with coverage declining as a percentage beyond 2026; residual exposure is basis and transportation, with Henry Hub basis spread inputs at 6/30/2026 ranging from $(2.663) to $0.195 per MMBtu.

**Funding the SPL Expansion.** Management indicated roughly half of Phase 1 will be funded with equity cash flow "including through flexibility in the variable component of the Cheniere Partners distribution." As GP, Cheniere can establish reserves at CQP that retain cash for the Sabine Pass build. That is a genuine low-cost lever, but it is not free capital — reduced distributions hit all CQP unitholders, including Cheniere's own 48.6% LP interest, and consolidated Cheniere DCF may diverge from CQP distributions going forward.

**Verification gaps.** All 2026 guidance figures, the ~$4.7B SPL Stage V EPC value, the <1 Mt open-volume statement, the guidance-raise composition, the reliability-versus-ramp split, the ≥10% dividend growth commitment, and the market statistics on Hormuz and European storage derive from a secondary report of the August 6 earnings call; the company press release and transcript were not retrievable. They are internally consistent with the 10-Q but are not filing-verified. Whether the $370M excise credit sits inside Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA is an inference from its recognition as a cost-of-sales reduction. The contents of the Third Employment Agreement Amendment with CEO Jack Fusco (dated April 2, 2026) are unknown, which is a real governance gap given his combined Chairman/President/CEO role and the disclosure that Cheniere has no employment contracts with key personnel other than the CEO.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 32.2% against 27.2% one-year realized is modestly rich, but the reading is distorted by a three-day expiry (August 21) where same-day mechanics dominate; the one-month tenor at 28.4% and six-month at 27.9% are essentially at realized vol. ATM IV collapsed from 96.8% on July 31 to 32.2% — an event-vol crush around the August 6 print, meaning the market has resolved rather than accumulated uncertainty. Put/call open-interest ratio fell from 0.34 to 0.20, the lowest in our energy coverage and call-tilted; put/call volume is 0.42. Short interest is 1.8% of float at 2.0 days to cover. There is no meaningful institutional hedging demand in this name. The six-month implied move of ±14.3% is narrower than the spread between our bull ($340, +25%) and bear ($200, -26%) cases, so options are not paying to express either tail. Longer-dated skew values are reported as negative (-1.7% at one month to -3.8% at six months) but we do not infer direction from the sign convention without a defined basis. Hedge fund holders declined to 74 from 81 in the prior quarter, a mild negative in positioning that does not change the fundamental picture.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($340, +25%).** Hormuz transit recovery stays below 10% into 2027 while Europe fails to reach 70% storage before winter, sustaining marketing margins above the $16.24/MMBtu Q2 realization on a larger open book as Stage 3 volumes and the June FERC +5 mtpa uplift lift production above 58 Mt. SPL Stage V takes FID early 2027 with mid-single-digit millions of tonnes of new offtake signed, CCL Expansion reaches FID, and reliability gains prove recurring as management claims. EBITDA reaches ~$8.6B and the market pays 11.75x for a de-risked, NPNS-cleaned earnings stream with a decade of contracted brownfield growth. Buybacks retire another 4% of the float, and leverage falls below 3x. That is $340 — the analyst high, and the level implied by 11.75x on $8.6B after deducting $24.3B debt, $1.4B leases and $5.3B NCI.

**Bear case ($200, -26%).** Hormuz normalizes in the second half of 2026, Qatari volumes return in full, and the 2027–2030 supply wave arrives on schedule against Asian demand that fell 4% in 2025. Marketing margin reverts toward the fixed-fee spread; the $370M excise credit does not repeat; ad valorem abatements roll off at both terminals; cash taxes step up toward the mid-teens FDDEI rate against a $3,629M deferred liability; the $6.0B of not-yet-commenced vessel charters begins accruing with no sublease relief. EBITDA settles near $7.1–7.2B and the multiple compresses to 10x as growth visibility shifts out to 2029. Equity value: roughly $200, near the December 2025 low of $186.20.

**Base case ($290, +6.8%).** Full-year Stage 3 volumes plus the FERC uplift roughly offset marketing-margin compression and the excise-credit roll-off, holding 2027 EBITDA near the 2026 midpoint of $8.15B. At 11.0x that is $290. DCF per share holds near $26–27 as share count shrinks ~4% annually, and an 11x DCF multiple triangulates to the same figure. Probability-weighted at 55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear: +2.2% price return, ~+3.0% including the dividend.

The distinction from ACCUMULATE is the absence of margin of safety and the composition of the 2026 base. A stock whose current-year earnings include a 4.5% non-recurring credit and a war premium the company has already sold forward, trading 9.7% below its all-time high at 11x ex-credit EBITDA, with out-year consensus being cut, does not offer the asymmetry that would justify adding capital today. The distinction from REDUCE is equally clear: 90% contracted revenue at ~15-year weighted average duration, 3.15x leverage inside a 4x target, three ratings upgrades, $7.5B of liquidity, a $9.1B buyback authorization equal to 16.2% of market cap executed at $222 weighted-average prices, and the largest brownfield expansion runway in the industry make this a business we want to own through the cycle.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth_hold) | Horizon: 12–18 months**

- **Base case: $290** (11.0x 2026E Consolidated Adj. EBITDA of $8.15B, cross-checked at 11x DCF/share of ~$26.50) — **+6.8%**, +7.6% with the dividend.
- **Bull case: $340** (11.75x on ~$8.6B) — **+25.2%**.
- **Bear case: $200** (10.0x on ~$7.1B) — **-26.4%**.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price in the **low $230s** (roughly 10x ex-credit 2026E EBITDA and ~8.7x DCF), where the base case restores a 25%+ margin of safety. Management has demonstrated it buys more aggressively into weakness, repurchasing 1.06M shares at $243.80 in May and 582K at $235.76 in June.
2. **SPL Stage V FID in early 2027 accompanied by executed long-term SPAs covering the incremental >6 mtpa**, plus mid-single-digit millions of tonnes of CCL Expansion offtake signed within 12–18 months. Contracted expansion converts the growth runway from optionality into fixed-fee cash flow.
3. **2027 guidance at or above $8.0B of Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA excluding the excise credit**, with the ad valorem step-up quantified and absorbed. This would demonstrate that reliability gains and Stage 3 volumes more than replace the marketing windfall.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. **72+ hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial Hormuz transit followed by 2027 EBITDA guidance below $7.5B**, confirming that the war premium was the earnings driver rather than reliability.
2. **Leverage above 3.75x** on forward EBITDA, or a slowdown in buybacks below a $1.5B annualized pace while debt continues rising.
3. **Any NPNS de-designation**, or an SPA termination or force majeure declaration by a top-five customer. Customer C at 12% of Q2 revenue and Customer A at 15% of receivables are the exposures to monitor.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 print (early November 2026), which should confirm Train 7 substantial completion, provide the first clean post-NPNS quarter of reported earnings, and give an early read on 2027 volumes and marketing exposure. The SPL Stage V FID decision in early 2027 is the second checkpoint.

## Conclusion

The trajectory of our scoring on Cheniere has been mildly declining (6.6 in July to 6.23 now) even as the operating news improved, and the reason is worth stating plainly: guidance rose to $7.9–8.4B while the share price rose from $263.57 to $271.64, so most of the improvement was already in the price, and the improvement itself is partly non-repeatable. The two genuinely durable developments this cycle are the near-completion of Stage 3 ahead of schedule and the NPNS election, which restores usable GAAP earnings after four years of derivative distortion. The two genuinely negative developments are the 4.6% cut to next-year consensus EPS and the confirmation that less than 1 Mt of 2026 volume remains open, which caps the war premium at what has already been earned.
