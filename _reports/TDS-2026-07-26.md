**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Telephone and Data Systems is a Communication Services holding company that no longer resembles the business its historical financials describe. Over roughly a twelve-month span, TDS converted the former UScellular wireless operation into cash and non-operating assets: the August 2025 sale of wireless operations and select spectrum to T-Mobile ($4.29B), followed by spectrum sales to AT&T ($1.02B, closed January 2026), Verizon ($1.0B, closed June 2026), and additional T-Mobile spectrum (~$254M). Gross monetization exceeds $6.5B. What remains is a wholly-owned fiber/broadband operator (TDS Telecom), an 81.9%-owned tower company (Array Digital Infrastructure, NYSE: AD) that holds ~4,450 towers plus ~$1.58B of unsold C-Band spectrum and carrier-partnership stakes, and a de-levered, cash-rich parent balance sheet. The market recognized this: cumulative TSR reached 260.75 versus a peer group at 123.65.

The problem for a new buyer is that the value-unlock is largely complete and largely paid out. Array declared three special dividends over the period ($23.00, $10.25, and $11.00/share), TDS captured ~82% of each, and Array has stated it does not anticipate further 2026 dividends. Raymond James downgraded TDS to Market Perform on May 11, 2026, on the view that the re-rating has run its course. The stock has fallen 13.9% over the trailing period to $34.37, now near the 52-week low of $33.01 and down from a $49.12 high set on the Q1 earnings date. That decline reflects the ex-dividend mechanics and the shift in market attention from monetization to the residual operating businesses, both of which are structurally weak.

The residual businesses are the core of the forward case, and they are not compelling on their own. TDS Telecom is a sub-scale rural fiber overbuilder running a small operating loss, negative free cash flow, and guiding capex up to $550–600M, a cash sink whose payoff is years out and contested by better-capitalized cable, telco, and fixed-wireless rivals. Array's headline tower revenue growth is inflated by a rolling-off one-time imputed spectrum lease and ~1,800 cancellable interim T-Mobile leases that management expects to be terminated early, alongside a DISH default now recognized on a cash basis. The one live catalyst is the pending TDS proposal to buy in the Array minority, which would let TDS capture 100% of tower/spectrum cash flow, but its price and outcome sit with an independent special committee and are controlled by the Carlson family. This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the monetization skill is real, but the forward risk/reward at $34.37 lacks a confident directional edge.

## Company Overview

TDS is a diversified telecom holding company controlled by the Carlson family through the TDS Voting Trust. It comprises two dissimilar pieces.

**TDS Telecom (100%-owned)** is a fiber-focused broadband, video, and voice provider serving roughly 1.06 million connections across 30 states, concentrated in small-to-mid-sized and rural markets. As of Q1 2026 it reports 1.9 million service addresses (up 5% year-over-year), fiber to 50% of its incumbent footprint, and 1Gig+ availability to 79% of the footprint. Wholesale revenue includes regulatory support via E-ACAM, which runs through 2038 and carries build obligations. This is the intended long-term operating core.

**Array Digital Infrastructure (81.9%-owned)** is the renamed remnant of UScellular. After selling wireless operations and select spectrum to T-Mobile, Array is now a tower company (4,452 towers in 19 states) that also holds equity stakes in Verizon- and AT&T-managed partnerships generating large cash distributions, plus a shrinking pool of retained spectrum (primarily C-Band, ~$1.58B book value) being monetized opportunistically.

The former UScellular wireless business is reported as discontinued operations, which is why FY2025 continuing revenue of $1.23B appears to collapse against FY2024's $4.96B. The comparison is a reporting artifact of the divestiture, not organic decline.

## Financial Analysis

The GAAP optics are distorted by the transition; the cash and balance-sheet story is the substantive one.

**Revenue and profitability.** Continuing-operations revenue was $1.23B in FY2025 (TDS Telecom $1.04B; Array $163M; Other $27M). TDS Telecom revenue fell 2% as legacy voice, video, and CLEC declines and divestitures outpaced fiber growth. Array site-rental revenue rose 58% on the new T-Mobile Master Lease Agreement. Adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was $528.9M, up 18%, but this benefits from the T-Mobile MLA ramp and a one-time ~$69M short-term imputed spectrum lease item. GAAP operating loss was -$97.4M in FY2025, still burdened by spectrum impairments and transition costs.

**Q1 2026.** Continuing-ops diluted EPS was $1.11 (versus -$0.20 prior-year), inflated by a $150.9M pre-tax book gain on the AT&T spectrum sale. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was $144.5M, up 49% year-over-year. The reported earnings surprise history (+347.5% in Q1 2026, +298.6% in Q4 2025, +176.4% in Q3 2025) reflects the difficulty of estimating a business in transition, not repeatable operating beats. These are gain-on-sale and mix-driven distortions, and should not be read as underlying earnings momentum. TDS Telecom itself was soft in Q1: revenue -3%, Adjusted EBITDA -3%, a small operating loss as the capex-heavy fiber build lifts D&A.

**Balance sheet.** The transformation de-levered the company dramatically. Cash rose to $1.37B at 3/31/26 (from $766M at YE25 and $364M at YE24), before the June Verizon proceeds. Long-term debt was cut from ~$4.05B at YE24 to $673M in Q1 2026: TDS repaid $781M of term loans, $300M secured, and a $150M EDC facility, while Array exchanged $1.68B of notes to T-Mobile and took a new $325M CoBank term loan. Stockholders' equity is ~$4.9B; total assets $8.2B, including $1.6B of licenses and $737M of assets held for sale. The consolidated leverage covenant of ≤3.5x and interest coverage of ≥3.0x are comfortably met.

**On solvency screens.** A naïve Altman Z-Score of 1.32 places TDS in the distress zone, but this is a false signal driven mechanically by the negative continuing-ops EBIT (a transition-year artifact) and a low revenue-to-assets ratio on a balance sheet loaded with cash and held-for-sale items. With $1.37B+ cash pre-Verizon, only $673M of debt, and billions of incoming proceeds already realized, genuine bankruptcy risk is negligible. The Piotroski 5/5 (positive net income, positive and growing operating cash flow, OCF > NI, current ratio 2.1) is the more relevant read. This informs a Financial Health score of 6.5: the capital structure is strong and de-levered, but the operating core still burns cash, which caps the score below the top tier.

**Capital allocation.** Fiber capex is the stated priority: 2026 guidance of $550–600M for TDS Telecom, up from ~$406M in 2025. Buybacks totaled $108M in 2025 with a new incremental $500M authorization added in November 2025 (~$524M available), though none were executed in Q1 2026. The TDS common dividend was cut from $0.185/quarter to $0.04/quarter, signaling that cash is being redeployed to fiber and buybacks and that future dividend policy is explicitly uncertain. At the current price the dividend yield is only ~0.47%, so there is no meaningful income support to the thesis.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the weakest part of the story, and the trajectory has been declining across recent assessments for good reason.

At Array, the reported tower revenue growth is partly a mirage. It is inflated by a rolling-off one-year imputed spectrum lease income from T-Mobile and by roughly 1,800 interim tower leases (up to 30 months, cancellable tower-by-tower) that management expects T-Mobile to terminate early. The durable base is the minimum 2,015-tower, 15-year T-Mobile MLA. Underlying run-rate tower economics are lower than the headline suggests. The tower tenancy ratio is a low 1.03, falling to 0.96 in Q1 2026 after excluding DISH, the opposite of the multi-carrier lease-up dynamic that drives value at the larger tower REITs. DISH's default (letter in September 2025 claiming its obligations are excused, payments stopped, $2.9M contract-asset writeoff in Q1 2026, now recognized on a cash basis) removes a contracted revenue stream that ran through 2035.

At TDS Telecom, the fiber build is genuine but the operating trends are soft. Voice was down 16% year-over-year in Q1, video -10%, commercial -11%, and copper broadband -25%. Fiber growth (incumbent fiber +9%, expansion fiber +26%) is offsetting these but total connections still fell 6% year-over-year. Broadband net adds slowed sharply to 100 in Q1 2026 from 2,800 in the prior-year quarter, and residential broadband churn rose to 1.8% from 1.3%. Fixed-wireless from T-Mobile and Verizon is a direct competitive pressure on the legacy DSL/copper base that TDS is trying to convert.

The bull case on growth requires the heavy fiber capex to eventually convert into margin expansion and positive free cash flow. That is a multi-year proposition against structural competitive headwinds and no near-term inflection visible in the data. Growth Prospects scores 4.5.

## Valuation Assessment

TDS is difficult to value on standard multiples because reported earnings are dominated by non-recurring gains and the balance sheet holds large non-operating assets. Trailing and forward P/E both print at 20.6x on $0.388 EPS, but that EPS figure blends transition-year distortions and gain-on-sale items, making the multiple close to meaningless as a forward guide.

Against Communication Services peers (median P/E 24.5x, median yield 1.1%), TDS looks modestly cheaper, but the peer set is heterogeneous (a cinema chain, a music label, a broadcaster, a holding company, and a fiber REIT) and offers little clean read-across. The relevant framework is sum-of-the-parts: the incoming and realized cash, ~$1.58B of retained C-Band at book, ~4,450 towers, the carrier-partnership stakes (~$176M of 2025 equity earnings plus large distributions), and a wholly-owned fiber business. The market has already re-rated toward this SOTP value, and the special dividends have distributed a large portion of the realized cash to shareholders.

A compressed or ambiguous multiple on a business in structural transition is not automatically cheap. The lessons of value-trap risk apply directly: cheapness without an independent, near-term catalyst is a warning, not a buy signal. The live catalyst, the Array minority buy-in, could crystallize additional value for TDS holders by capturing 100% of Array's cash flow, but its price and timing are controlled by the special committee and the Carlson family, and it is non-binding and unresolved. The retained C-Band spectrum monetization is uncertain (high-band spectrum has been impaired twice, in 2024 and 2025, on industry-wide operationalization challenges), so book value is not a reliable proxy for realizable value.

Given the realized-and-distributed nature of the value unlock, the soft operating businesses underneath, and the absence of a confidently datable near-term re-rating catalyst, Valuation scores 5.0: neither clearly cheap nor clearly expensive, with the risk skewed toward the "value that's already been paid out" reading rather than fresh upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Both operating pieces are sub-scale in industries dominated by larger players.

Array is a fourth-tier tower operator against American Tower, Crown Castle, and SBA. Its economics are dominated by a single anchor tenant (T-Mobile), and its low and falling tenancy ratio means it lacks the multi-carrier lease-up that generates incremental-margin growth at scaled peers. Fixed annual escalators are a modeled positive but a flagged risk if tenants push back.

TDS Telecom competes in the capital-intensive rural and edge-market fiber overbuild against cable incumbents, larger telcos, and fixed-wireless. The 10-K explicitly names "lack of scale relative to larger competitors" as a key risk. The cluster-expansion strategy is coherent, and E-ACAM support through 2038 is a stabilizer, but the legacy base is eroding faster than fiber is being added on a total-connections basis, and net-add momentum has decelerated sharply. The competitive trajectory is deteriorating rather than improving. Competitive Position scores 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-anchor concentration at Array (T-Mobile).** Tower revenue is almost entirely a function of the T-Mobile MLA plus the cancellable interim leases. Management expects T-Mobile to cancel the interim leases early, producing a near-term tower revenue decline hidden inside the reported growth.

**DISH Wireless default.** The default removes a stream that was ~$6.5M of 2025 site revenue with commitments through 2035. This is now impaired and litigation-prone.

**Spectrum monetization/execution.** ~$1.58B of retained spectrum (mostly C-Band) is unsold. High-band spectrum has been impaired twice ($136M in 2024, $48M in 2025). Realizing the remainder at book is uncertain.

**TDS Telecom cash burn.** The keeper business runs a small operating loss and negative free cash flow (FY2025 continuing-ops FCF ≈ -$54M; Q1 2026 ≈ -$81M) while guiding capex up. The bear case is a sub-scale operator perpetually outspending against better-capitalized rivals.

**Governance/control.** The TDS Voting Trust (Carlson family) controls the company, with multiple Carlsons in board and executive roles. Charter provisions entrench control and can block change-of-control. Minority holders have limited say, which is especially relevant to the Array buy-in pricing.

**Legal/regulatory contingencies.** The Advantage Spectrum False Claims Act litigation over FCC auction bid credits was reinstated on appeal (D.C. Circuit, September 2025) and remanded; a shareholder derivative suit over 2022 disclosures is pending. Both are flagged as unquantifiable.

**Buy-in proposal itself as a risk.** The Q1 2026 10-Q adds the Array minority buy-in as a new risk factor: uncertainty over acceptance/rejection, management distraction, and expense.

Many of these risks are already partly reflected in the depressed price and the Raymond James downgrade, which is why Risk Factors scores 5.0 rather than lower: the balance sheet is fortress-like, but the concentration, execution, and governance risks are real, specific, and not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 47.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 25.8%, a rich spread of 21.7 points at the 65th IV percentile. The term structure is in backwardation (47.5% near-term versus 42.1% at six months), which indicates the market is pricing imminent stress rather than a smooth glide. The put/call skew is elevated at 4.9%, reflecting demand for downside protection. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.5%. This positioning is consistent with an unresolved binary, most plausibly the Array buy-in special-committee outcome and the direction of the residual operating businesses, and argues against paying up on the fundamental thesis until the near-term event resolves. Short interest is negligible (0.1% of float, 41,001 shares), so this is not a crowded bearish position; the caution is expressed through options, not the borrow.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** TDS management has demonstrated genuine skill converting a declining wireless asset into ~$6.5B+ of realized value at attractive prices, de-levering from ~$4.05B to $673M of debt and building a $1.37B+ cash position. The Array minority buy-in, if executed at a reasonable price, lets TDS capture 100% of future tower and spectrum cash flow and simplifies a discounted holding structure. The ~$1.58B of retained C-Band offers optionality if it monetizes near book. The fiber build eventually converts to margin expansion and positive FCF, and the ~$524M buyback authorization plus a still-low share count support per-share value. Bull case ~$42–45, roughly the pre-decline trading range, contingent on a value-accretive Array buy-in and evidence of fiber FCF inflection.

**Bear Case.** The monetization is done and distributed, and what remains is a sub-scale fiber operator burning cash against structural competition and a single-anchor tower business facing an interim-lease cliff, a DISH default, and uncertain spectrum monetization. The Carlson control structure means minority holders are along for the ride on the buy-in price. Total connections keep declining, broadband net adds stay near zero, and the capex outspend does not convert to returns. Bear case ~$28–30, roughly 13–19% below the current price, reflecting continued erosion of the operating base and a spectrum-value markdown.

**Base Case.** The stock trades sideways-to-modestly-higher as the Array buy-in resolves and the market waits for evidence on fiber economics. Base case ~$36, roughly 5% above the current $34.37, reflecting residual SOTP value net of the operating drag and no fresh re-rating catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the resolution of the Array minority buy-in proposal by the independent special committee (unscheduled but active, advised by PJT/Cravath) and the Q2 2026 earnings report, which will show underlying tower run-rate economics as the imputed lease rolls off and interim leases begin to cancel.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Announcement of the Array buy-in at a price accretive to TDS holders, with a clear path to capturing 100% of Array cash flow.
- Evidence of a fiber FCF inflection: TDS Telecom broadband net adds re-accelerating meaningfully (back toward prior-year 2,000+ per quarter) with stabilizing churn and segment EBITDA turning positive on a run-rate basis.
- Monetization of a material portion of the retained C-Band spectrum at or above book value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Confirmation that T-Mobile is cancelling interim leases faster than modeled, driving a sharper-than-expected tower revenue decline.
- A spectrum impairment materially reducing the ~$1.58B retained book value.
- An Array buy-in structured to disadvantage minority holders, or a widening governance discount.
- TDS Telecom capex escalating further with no improvement in connections or FCF trajectory.

## Conclusion

TDS is a well-executed value-unlock that has already been recognized and largely distributed. The company sits on a fortress balance sheet ($1.37B+ cash, $673M debt, Piotroski 5/5), and the naïve distress screen is a transition-year artifact, not a solvency signal. But the forward case reduces to a sub-scale fiber operator that currently burns cash and a single-anchor tower business with a rolling-off revenue base, a DISH default, and uncertain spectrum value. The special dividends are paid, the spectrum is mostly sold, and Raymond James's downgrade to Market Perform reflects the reasonable view that the easy money has been made.

The one live catalyst, the Array minority buy-in, is genuine but controlled by the Carlson family and unresolved on both price and timing, and the options market is pricing imminent stress through backwardation and elevated put skew. The balance-sheet quality is unambiguous but the directional edge at $34.37 is not. The declining score trajectory (6.6 in May to 5.5 in June) is confirmed rather than reversed by the current data: valuation, growth, and competitive position have all weakened as attention shifts from monetization to the residual operating businesses. The recommendation is HOLD with an uncertain rationale: there is neither a confident bull case at the current price nor a distress case that warrants avoidance. The prudent stance is to wait for the Array buy-in outcome and Q2 evidence on fiber and tower run-rate economics before taking a directional view.
