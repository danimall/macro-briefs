**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CVR Energy is a diversified energy holding company controlled roughly 71% by Carl Icahn / Icahn Enterprises, combining two Mid-Continent refineries (206,500 bpd combined at Coffeyville, KS and Wynnewood, OK) with a controlling stake in the CVR Partners nitrogen fertilizer MLP. The company just passed through what management and the data both indicate was a cyclical earnings trough in Q1 2026 (net loss of $160M, or -$1.91/share), and a sharp sequential recovery is underway in Q2 driven by the Iran conflict: management reports Group 2-1-1 crack spreads averaging $38.36/bbl quarter-to-date versus $21.58 Group 3 2-1-1 in Q1, with fertilizer prices spiking as roughly 30% of global nitrogen supply transits the Strait of Hormuz. Operating cash flow inflected to +$64M in Q1 from -$195M a year earlier, and the dividend was reinstated at $0.10/quarter, described by management as non-variable.

The composite score has climbed from 4.0 in April to 4.8 in mid-June, with growth prospects, valuation, and financial health all improving as the trough got confirmed. What holds this back from a BUY is the quality and durability of the earnings signal. The Q2 tailwind is geopolitically driven and potentially transient; management placed large crack-swap hedges (~15% of 2026 production) early into a backwardated market that will cap upside during exactly the recovery the bulls are underwriting; the RFS/RIN liability ballooned from $72M to $204M in a single quarter and the pivotal Wynnewood Small Refinery Exemption ruling is overdue and politically contested; and consensus next-quarter and current-year EPS estimates have been slashed (-29.2% next-quarter and -108.7% current-year over 30 days) even as next-year estimates rose. The estimate picture is internally contradictory rather than uniformly inflecting.

The stock closed at $35.13 on 7/17/2026, having rebounded roughly 29% off its 6/23/2026 trough of $27.20 but still sitting about 16% below its 52-week high of $41.67 (late October 2025). The near-term optimism is not fully priced; the stock is neither at its high nor at its low. We rate CVI **HOLD (uncertain)**. The recovery is real, but the combination of hedged upside, a binary regulatory overhang, negative-alpha sector dynamics, and governance instability leaves the risk/reward two-sided rather than asymmetrically favorable.

## Company Overview

CVR operates two reportable segments:

**Petroleum (core).** An independent, non-integrated merchant refiner running two Group 3 (southern PADD II) refineries with 206,500 bpd combined crude capacity. CVR has no retail or marketing arm, so it cannot offset weak refining margins with downstream fuel-marketing profits, a structural disadvantage versus integrated peers that the 10-K explicitly flags. Crude is sourced via CVR's own Kansas/Oklahoma gathering operation plus a Gunvor intermediation agreement running through January 2029. Q1 2026 crude utilization was a healthy 96.8%.

**Nitrogen Fertilizer.** Held through the publicly traded MLP CVR Partners (UAN), of which CVR owns ~37% of units and 100% of the general partner, consolidated as a VIE. Two plants: Coffeyville, KS (unusual pet-coke gasification feedstock, a genuine cost differentiator when natural gas prices are elevated) and East Dubuque, IL (conventional natural gas). Because CVR only receives ~37% of CVR Partners' distributions, consolidated financials understate the sum-of-parts complexity.

A meaningful strategic change occurred in December 2025: CVR reverted the Wynnewood renewable diesel unit back to hydrocarbon processing after the $1/gal Blenders Tax Credit expired and RD margins deteriorated. As of Q1 2026, the Renewables segment no longer exists as a reportable line, folded into "Other." This concedes that the renewable diesel business was a money-loser post-BTC, though the optionality to switch back "if incentivized" was retained.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year picture is a severe cyclical downcycle now bottoming:

- **Revenue** fell from ~$9.2B (2023) to ~$7.2B (2025). **Net income** cratered from $769M (2023) to ~$27M (2025). Company-selected Adjusted EBITDA fell from $1.16B (2023) to $317M (2024) to $393M (2025).
- **Q1 2026 (trough):** Consolidated EBITDA loss of $52M. Adjusted EBITDA was +$37M after backing out a $158M unrealized derivative loss, a $51M unfavorable RFS change, and $120M favorable inventory valuation.
- **Segment divergence is stark.** Petroleum posted a $193M operating loss (Adjusted EBITDA -$50M, worse than -$30M in Q1'25, hammered by RINs) while Fertilizer posted $58M operating income (Adjusted EBITDA +$78M, up from $53M). The fertilizer MLP is currently carrying the company.
- **Cash flow inflected.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was depressed a year earlier by the Coffeyville turnaround. FCF was ~$21M, roughly $63M of which came from Fertilizer.
- **Balance sheet.** Total debt ~$1.78B; consolidated cash $512M; total liquidity ~$1.1B (including $539M ABL availability). Equity has eroded; the accumulated deficit grew to $969M at Q1 2026 from $777M at YE2025. Management targets $1B gross leverage (ex-CVR Partners) as its deleveraging goal.
- **Capital return.** Dividend suspended October 2024, reinstated in Q1 2026 (~$10M/quarter, ~$7M to IEP).
- **Refinancing (Feb 2026).** Issued $1B new notes ($600M 7.500% due 2031; $400M 7.875% due 2034), redeemed the 8.500% 2029 notes at 104.25% (a $28M extinguishment loss), repaid the Petroleum term loan, and partially redeemed the 5.750% 2028 notes. Upsized the ABL from $345M to $550M and extended to 2031. The maturity runway is extended and liquidity is boosted, but at a higher coupon; Q1 interest expense rose to $58M from $25M.
- **2026 capex** guide: $200M–$240M consolidated, with no refinery turnaround in 2026 (next is Wynnewood in 2027; East Dubuque fertilizer turnaround August 2026).

The financial health picture is a levered merchant refiner with adequate near-term liquidity, a manageable maturity ladder post-refinancing, but eroding equity, a higher interest burden, and earnings power hostage to crack spreads and RIN costs. That is a below-average-but-not-distressed profile, appropriate for a mid-4s score.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth story is a cyclical recovery, not a secular expansion. The Q1 loss is very likely the trough. Q2 quarter-to-date metrics point to a sharp sequential improvement: Mid-Con gasoline inventories down 17% and diesel down 20% YTD, ammonia at ~$950/ton and UAN at ~$525/ton QTD.

The estimate revision picture is internally contradictory and does not cleanly meet the inflection-BUY signal:

- Current-quarter EPS estimate: $0.28, revised down -70.9% over 30 days.
- Next-quarter: $0.65.
- Current-year: -$0.10.
- Next-year: $1.74, up +6.6% over 30 days and +33.9% over 90 days.

Only the next-year line is rising. The near-term estimates have been slashed; the Q1 trough was deeper than analysts expected (the -210.8% Q1 miss underscores this), and the current-year turned negative. The classic inflection-BUY signal (rising near-term estimates + accelerating revenue) is not present; instead we have a collapsing front end and a rising back end. That pattern is consistent with a market that expects the current geopolitical crack tailwind to fade before durable earnings power reasserts in 2027. The +33.9% 90-day revision on next-year EPS is a genuine positive, but it is a single line against three negative ones, and it sits far enough out that it does not carry the same conviction as accelerating near-term momentum would.

Structural growth levers exist but are modest and multi-year: the $136M Wynnewood alkylation unit conversion (operational ~2027), the $53M Coffeyville flare gas recovery, repurposing ex-RDU rail assets to reach higher-margin markets, the Kansas–Denver pipeline (online late 2026) and future Western Gateway Pipeline to the Gulf, and potential dual-feedstock capability at Coffeyville fertilizer. The largest genuine growth vector is M&A: the 10-Q discloses that IEP and CVR are exploring "material amounts of refining assets" via mergers or asset purchases. That is a real, live catalyst, but its timing and terms are unknowable and its conflict-of-interest profile (Icahn-directed) is a double edge.

Weighting the confirmed trough and next-year revision positively against the collapsing near-term estimates and the transient nature of the crack tailwind, growth prospects earn a 5.5, an improving but not yet durable growth thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are meaningless here; the trailing P/E is -80x on a near-zero/negative EPS through a downcycle. The relevant lens is forward and normalized earnings power.

On the rising next-year consensus of $1.74, CVI trades at roughly 20x forward earnings at $35.13. That sits comfortably inside the sub-30x inflection threshold, but it is above the peer median P/E of 10.8x for the Oil & Gas Refining & Marketing group (PBF 10.8x, PARR 6.2x, REX 15.3x). The peer set is heterogeneous and several peers carry negative or distressed multiples (IEP at a 27.8% yield and negative P/E, WKC negative), so the median is a rough benchmark. But on a normalized basis, CVR does not screen cheap versus better-capitalized merchant refiners; a 20x forward multiple on a levered, no-retail-hedge merchant refiner in an oversupplied basin is not obviously mispriced to the downside.

The upside case rests on cycle-adjusted earnings power well above the trailing base. If crack spreads sustain near current elevated levels and a favorable Wynnewood SRE ruling materializes, mid-cycle earnings could support a materially higher value; the accretion of an Icahn-directed acquisition could add scale and re-rate the multiple. The downside case is a value trap: cracks normalize as the geopolitical premium fades, the mistimed hedges cap the recovery, the SRE ruling goes against Wynnewood, and 2026 delivers a small loss with the multiple derating on falling near-term estimates.

Cheapness has been reliably anti-predictive across the scoring history, and CVI is not even screening cheap on forward numbers. Energy is among the worst alpha sectors (structurally negative), which raises the bar for a directional call. The valuation is not stretched enough to warrant a low score, nor supported by a clear, independent catalyst-driven mispricing sufficient for a high one. We assign 4.8, reflecting a fair-to-slightly-rich forward multiple on estimates whose near-term trajectory is down.

Analyst price targets bracket the current price awkwardly: high $35.00, mean $28.80, median $28.00, low $25.00. The current price sits above the mean and median and at the high. Because CVI is not a clean inflection stock (near-term estimates are falling, the multiple is not compressed below the growth rate), the "analyst targets are floors" principle does not apply here; the targets more likely reflect a sober view of a cyclical name whose Q2 tailwind is transient. The 3 sell / 1 strong-sell / 2 hold recommendation split reinforces cautious analyst positioning.

## Competitive Landscape

CVR's competitive position is a mixed, largely stable-to-slightly-improving picture with structural handicaps:

- **Structural disadvantage:** No retail/marketing arm means no downstream buffer against weak refining margins. As a pure merchant refiner in the landlocked, oversupplied Group 3 basin, CVR has historically suffered a basis discount versus Gulf Coast refiners.
- **Improving margin capture:** Management's central strategic thrust is reaching higher-demand markets via ex-RDU rail assets, the Kansas–Denver pipeline (late 2026), and the future Western Gateway Pipeline. If executed, these narrow the geographic discount.
- **Crude sourcing tailwind:** CVR increased WCS (Western Canadian) runs to ~18,000 bpd (~8% of slate) after January 2026 Venezuela sanctions widened the WCS differential to ~$15.46/bbl under WTI, a genuine margin tailwind.
- **Industry backdrop:** US operable refining capacity has declined on a net basis since 2020, few new global refineries are scheduled, and recent damage to Middle East and Russian refining capacity tightens global product balances. This is a genuine medium-term support for merchant crack spreads.
- **Fertilizer positioning:** Low-cost US nitrogen producer benefiting from a structural European natural-gas shortage that raises the global cost curve into 2027. The pet-coke gasification feedstock at Coffeyville is a real differentiator. Offset by a new USDA/DOJ antitrust MOU (Sept 2025) investigating agricultural input pricing.

The competitive trajectory is neither clearly widening nor clearly eroding: the geographic-reach initiatives are constructive but unproven, the no-retail structure is a permanent handicap, and the RIN regulatory regime cuts against merchant refiners structurally (the new 2026/2027 RFS Final Rule sets the highest RVO in history). A 4.5 reflects a stable-to-slightly-improving position with material structural constraints.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk load here is heavy and several items are binary or asymmetric:

1. **RFS/RIN exposure — the single biggest earnings swing.** RIN prices more than doubled YoY. Net RINs expense was $143M ($7.37/bbl) in Q1, cutting the capture rate by ~34%. The new 2026/2027 RFS Final Rule adds back 70% of exempted volumes (~2.89B RINs), structurally negative for merchant refiners.

2. **Small Refinery Exemption binary.** CVR believes Wynnewood qualifies for a 100% SRE and is litigating; the EPA has missed its statutory 90-day deadline on Wynnewood's 2025 petition. A favorable ruling would have improved Q1 capture ~12%, a significant upside optionality, but binary, and farm-state lawmakers (E15 Council) are actively lobbying to limit small-refinery eligibility. Federal policy is contradictory: the administration is rhetorically pro-refiner while the RVO and SRE politics cut against small refiners.

3. **Mistimed hedges.** CVR placed large crack-swap swaps (9.9M bbl diesel, 2.4M bbl gasoline) at the outset of the Iran conflict, driving a $182M total / $158M unrealized derivative loss in Q1. The CFO conceded "going a little slower might have been better." These hedges will cap upside during the very recovery the bull case underwrites.

4. **Icahn concentration / controlled-company risk.** CVR is a controlled company exempt from most NYSE independent-board requirements. This is a genuine two-edged risk. Icahn's aggressive open-market buying (~$16–$21/share in 2025–early 2026, versus $35 today) is a bullish alignment signal from the controlling holder and reads as viewing CVR as a consolidation vehicle at cyclical lows. But IEP itself trades at a distressed 27.8% dividend yield and negative P/E, and the note indentures contain change-of-control repurchase obligations that could be triggered by a change in Icahn's ownership. If IEP were forced to sell down its CVR stake to address its own funding needs, that could both pressure CVI shares and trip change-of-control repurchase obligations across the note complex. This leverage/concentration interaction is a material, under-appreciated tail.

5. **Litigation cluster.** Multiple suits from an October 2025 ammonia release at the Coffeyville fertilizer facility; a Kansas environmental/medical-monitoring class action; an ExxonMobil guaranty dispute; and legacy CVR Refining call-option insurance litigation.

6. **Governance instability.** Two CEO changes in roughly twelve months: David Lamp (named in the April 2026 proxy) → Mark Pytosh → Dane Neumann, with Pytosh's abrupt "personal reasons" exit effective June 18, 2026 after only ~6 months in the seat. The stock fell roughly 18% from $33.32 (June 8) to $27.40 (June 18), coinciding with the CEO-transition window, before rebounding. That drawdown is consistent with the market reading the handoff negatively, though it also coincided with broader energy-sector weakness and cannot be attributed solely to the transition. Two CEO changes inside a year at a controlled company warrant scrutiny.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 72.2% versus 52.3% one-year historical vol, rich by ~20 points, at the 97th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (72.2% near-term vs 62.7% long-term), which markets typically price ahead of a discrete catalyst, here likely the Q2 print showing the crack-spread recovery and the still-unresolved SRE ruling. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±17.4%. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-6.1%) and the put/call OI ratio is a low 0.17, which cuts against a purely bearish read: positioning is not one-sidedly negative despite 16.2% short interest and elevated IV. The market expects a large move, direction uncertain, with rich premium making this a poor spot to pay up for optionality.

Netting these, the risk score is 3.8: the RIN liability, the binary SRE ruling, the mistimed hedges, the Icahn/indenture interaction, and the governance turnover collectively represent material idiosyncratic downside that is not fully discounted at $35.13.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to ~$45–48)
The Q1 loss was the trough. Q2 delivers a large sequential earnings recovery as Group 2-1-1 cracks flow through, fertilizer prices stay elevated on Hormuz-linked nitrogen tightness, and operating cash flow builds on the Q1 base. The WCS differential adds sustained crude-cost tailwind. The overdue Wynnewood SRE ruling comes back favorable, releasing a chunk of the RIN liability and stepping up capture. Icahn's accumulation proves prescient as an M&A transaction adds refining scale and geographic diversity, re-rating the multiple. Next-year consensus proves conservative, and on ~$2.00+ of normalized EPS at a 12–13x cycle multiple the stock reaches the mid-$40s.

### Bear Case (to ~$25)
The crack-spread tailwind is a transient geopolitical premium; as the Iran situation de-escalates, Group 3 cracks normalize back toward the low-$20s, and the mistimed hedges cap what upside remains. The Wynnewood SRE ruling goes against CVR, and the highest-ever RVO structurally penalizes the merchant model. Current-year EPS lands near consensus, the accumulated deficit keeps growing, and the 7.5–7.9% coupons on the refinanced notes weigh on FCF. Governance turnover proves symptomatic of deeper issues, and an IEP-funding-driven stake sale pressures the shares toward the $25 analyst low target and the June-adjacent trough.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (expected late July / early August 2026), which will confirm the magnitude of the crack-spread recovery, the net hedge drag, and any movement on the Wynnewood SRE petition. The August 2026 East Dubuque fertilizer turnaround and any M&A announcement are secondary checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable Wynnewood SRE ruling that materially reduces the RFS liability and lifts the petroleum capture rate.
- Near-term estimate revisions turn positive (current-quarter and current-year EPS revised upward) alongside a Q2 print confirming sustained, hedge-adjusted crack capture above ~$30/bbl Group 2-1-1.
- A clearly accretive, minority-friendly M&A transaction that adds scale and geographic diversity at a sensible multiple.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cracks normalize back toward the low-$20s while the hedges crystallize losses, and Q2 fails to show a decisive sequential recovery.
- An adverse SRE ruling combined with the full RVO add-back materially raising the structural RIN burden.
- Evidence that IEP's own funding stress forces a CVR stake reduction, or a change-of-control event triggering note-indenture repurchase obligations.
- Further governance disruption or a dividend reversal signaling that management's "non-variable" characterization was overstated.

## Conclusion

CVR Energy sits at a genuine cyclical inflection with confirmed trough earnings, an inflecting cash-flow line, a reinstated dividend, and a controlling shareholder buying aggressively below the current price. Those are real positives, and they justify the improving score trajectory (4.0 → 4.8) and a HOLD rather than AVOID. But the recovery is powered by a geopolitically driven crack-spread premium that is likely transient, it is partially hedged away by mistimed swaps, and the near-term estimate revisions are collapsing rather than rising, so this does not clear the inflection-BUY bar, which requires rising near-term estimates on a compressed forward multiple. At ~20x forward EPS the stock is not screening cheap versus peers, cheapness has been an unreliable signal regardless, and Energy is a structurally negative-alpha sector that raises the bar for any directional call.

The binary regulatory overhang (the overdue SRE ruling and the highest-ever RVO), the RIN liability, the Icahn/indenture concentration interaction, and two CEO changes in twelve months collectively leave the risk/reward two-sided. At $35.13, rebounded ~29% off the June trough but ~16% below the October high, and above the $28.80 analyst mean, there is neither a compelling entry nor a clear reason to avoid outright. We rate CVI **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale: the evidence balances, and the Q2 print plus the SRE ruling are the events that should tip the call in one direction or the other.
