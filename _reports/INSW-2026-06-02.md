**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Seaways is one of the largest publicly traded crude and product tanker companies, operating a 67-vessel fleet (down from 70 at year-end 2025 after counter-cyclical vessel sales) split roughly evenly between crude tankers and product carriers. The company combines an asset-owning model with ~82% spot-market exposure deployed through six commercial pools, which makes its earnings a leveraged bet on tanker rates and, by extension, on geopolitical disruption to global oil flows. Q1 2026 net income of $286M ($5.75 diluted EPS) nearly matched the entirety of FY2025 ($309M), driven by VLCC spot rates surging to $86,693/day versus $33,531 a year earlier, plus $88M of vessel-sale gains. That spike is a direct product of the Strait of Hormuz/Iran conflict disrupting trade flows and lengthening ton-miles.

The mechanism that produced these results is the same one that makes them fragile. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised up sharply (+302% on the current quarter, +163% on the current year over 90 days), but these revisions are tracking a disruption premium on spot rates, not a structural improvement in the franchise. The world model's central energy thesis is that the Hormuz situation is a binary outcome with a widened distribution: a reopening framework has regained traction, which would compress the very rates driving INSW's earnings. INSW is among the most reopening-exposed names in the energy complex because it monetizes the disruption premium directly. The stock has declined ~15% from its May 8 high of $92.66 to ~$79, and the front-month options market is pricing a ±13.1% implied move with near-term IV at 70.1% in backwardation, consistent with an imminent binary event.

The balance sheet is a genuine differentiator: net loan-to-value below 7%, 44% of the fleet unencumbered, and ~$918M liquidity position INSW to buy assets in a downturn. Balance-sheet quality does not resolve the directional question on rates. With the stock at ~7.2x trailing earnings, the dividend variable rather than an anchor, insiders net sellers post-rally, and a rising orderbook threatening 2026-2028 supply, the setup is balanced. This is a HOLD, with the recommendation contingent on physical verification of the Hormuz outcome rather than on diplomatic headlines.

## Company Overview

International Seaways, incorporated in the Marshall Islands with vessel-owning subsidiaries redomiciled to Bermuda in December 2025, operates through two segments. **Crude Tankers** (52% of 2025 TCE revenue) comprises VLCCs, Suezmaxes, Aframaxes, plus an asset-light U.S. Gulf ship-to-ship lightering business. **Product Carriers** (48%) comprises MRs, LR1s, and one LR2 carrying refined products.

The operating model is hybrid: INSW owns vessels but outsources technical management to third parties and deploys ~82% of capacity in the spot market via six commercial pools (Tankers International, Maersk Suezmax, Panamax International, CPTA, Norden, Aframax International). The pools provide scale, utilization, and cargo access; the ~18% on time charters provides baseline predictability (~$223M future contracted revenue as of April 2026). The fleet's weighted-average age is ~10.9 years.

In January 2026, INSW acquired CMB.Tech's 50% stake in Tankers (UK) Agencies, taking 100% ownership of the commercial manager of the Tankers International VLCC pool, a vertical-integration step that captures management economics and deepens cargo relationships while adding the first goodwill ($7.4M) to a historically asset-only balance sheet. Tankers International launched a new Suezmax pool in March 2026.

## Financial Analysis

The headline financial story is a cyclical whipsaw that a standardized "declining revenue" data pull would badly mischaracterize.

**Annual trajectory (GAAP):**

| Year | Revenue | Net Income | Adj. EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $1,071.8M (peak) | $556.4M | — |
| 2024 | $951.6M | $416.7M | $583.3M |
| 2025 | $843.3M | $309.3M | $474.7M |

FY2025 revenue fell 12% YoY as the 2023-24 geopolitical rate spike partially normalized, driven almost entirely by Product Carrier rate weakness (segment adjusted income $143M vs $266M); Crude Tankers held roughly flat at $213M.

**Q1 2026 reversed the trend:**
- Net income $286M ($5.75 diluted EPS) vs. $50M ($1.00) in Q1 2025.
- Adjusted net income $194M ($3.90); Adjusted EBITDA $244M vs $91M.
- VLCC spot rates $86,693/day (vs $33,531), Suezmax $68,027, Aframax $51,379, LR1 $70,664.
- $88M of vessel-sale gains (7 vessels, $216M proceeds).

Rates rose because the Hormuz/Iran conflict disrupted trade flows. The $88M of vessel-sale gains is non-recurring and reflects management selling old tonnage into a strong asset market.

**Cash flow and balance sheet are high quality.** 2025 operating cash flow of $380M exceeded net income (positive earnings-quality signal); Q1 2026 OCF was $141M. Liquidity stood at ~$918M at March 31, 2026 (cash + ST investments $377M, undrawn revolver $541M). Net debt was ~$567M, net debt-to-capital 16.5% at year-end 2025, falling to net LTV below 7% by Q1 2026; total equity $2.19B. 31 of 70 vessels (44%) are unencumbered, with debt maturities spread 2030-2037. Altman Z of 3.79 sits comfortably in the safe zone.

**Earnings surprise track record is strong but accelerating off a moving base:** four consecutive beats, with surprise magnitude widening from +5.6% (Q2 2025) to +47.6% (Q1 2026). The widening beats reflect estimates chasing a fast-moving spot market, not management sandbagging a stable business.

**Margins/returns (FY25):** operating margin ~41%, net margin ~37%, ROE 15.3%, ROA 11.6%.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data is large in magnitude: current-quarter EPS revised +302.4% over 30 days, current-year +131.1% over 30 days and +162.8% over 90 days, next-year +84.7% over 90 days. Under normal circumstances, revisions of this velocity combined with an earnings inflection would be a powerful BUY signal.

The revisions are tracking a disruption premium on spot tanker rates rather than a durable acceleration in the underlying franchise. The current-quarter estimate of $5.68 and current-year estimate of $13.90 capitalize the Hormuz-driven rate spike directly. The next-year estimate of $6.42 — less than half the current-year $13.90 — embeds analysts' own expectation that 2026's earnings power does not persist. When the consensus itself models a ~54% earnings decline the following year, the revision velocity signals a cyclical peak, not an inflection that consensus is underestimating.

Structural growth drivers are genuine but slow: an aging global fleet facing decarbonization-driven recycling, limited near-term yard slots (capacity diverted to LNG/containers/bulkers), and sanctions enforcement bifurcating compliant versus "dark" tonnage. The Tankers International vertical integration adds incremental management economics. Against these, the orderbook has climbed to ~17% of the world fleet (vs 14% in 2024, 7% in 2023), with the VLCC orderbook up ~19M dwt YoY — the classic delayed-supply threat that ends tanker upcycles, with deliveries weighted to 2026-2028.

The honest growth assessment: 2026 earnings will be exceptional if Hormuz remains disrupted, then decline as the conflict premium fades and supply arrives. This is a cyclical peak being mislabeled by raw revision data as secular growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$79 on trailing EPS of ~$6.23, INSW trades at ~7.2x. Against peer medians (P/E 24.4, yield 6.0%) the headline multiple looks deeply discounted, but this comparison is misleading because the peers (KNTK, AM, DTM) are stable-cash-flow midstream operators while LPG (Dorian LPG, 8.9x) is the relevant spot-exposed comparable, and it trades at a similar single-digit multiple because the market refuses to capitalize peak-cycle shipping earnings at midstream multiples.

The valuation question reduces to which earnings number to capitalize. On the current-year $13.90 consensus, the stock trades at ~5.7x — cheap, but on earnings that consensus itself models declining to $6.42 next year (~12.3x). The market is correctly applying a low multiple to earnings it expects to be transient. Applying a normalized mid-cycle EPS in the $4-6 range (consistent with FY2024-25 results once the conflict premium fades) and a typical 6-8x mid-cycle tanker multiple yields a fair value range of roughly $30-48 — well below the current price — while the bull case of sustained disruption supports materially higher.

This is the core asymmetry problem. The cycle-adjusted valuation guidance is designed for stocks at cyclical troughs where trailing P/E overstates expense; INSW is the inverse, a stock near a cyclical peak where trailing/forward P/E understates expense because the "E" is inflated by a war premium. The $4.55 Q1 dividend (vs $2.93 for all of 2025) reflects a formalized 85%-of-adjusted-net-income variable policy, so the ~0.6% trailing yield in the data understates the cyclical-cash return but should not be capitalized as a stable yield anchor.

Analyst price targets (mean $91.17, median $91.50, range $56-120) sit above the current price, with sentiment improving and 5 buys/1 hold. Several of these targets were set into the rate spike, and the research noted that as of mid-April the average target implied modest downside before the subsequent pullback. On a peak-cycle commodity name, analyst targets are not reliable floors; they reflect the same rate extrapolation as the price.

## Competitive Landscape

INSW's competitive position is solid but the industry is structurally fragmented and price-taking; no single owner influences rates. The company's edge is a modern, balanced, diversified fleet, a strong balance sheet enabling counter-cyclical asset purchases, and disciplined capital allocation with a demonstrated track record (>$1B returned since 2020). Cumulative TSR of 470.75 versus a peer group at 196 confirms relative outperformance through the cycle.

The Tankers International buyout deepens the moat modestly by capturing pool-management economics, alongside the launch of a new Suezmax pool. Counter-cyclical positioning positions INSW to be a buyer in the next downturn while leveraged competitors are forced sellers. The trajectory is stable-to-improving on balance-sheet and integration metrics, but the fundamental constraint is that INSW competes in a market where its product (vessel capacity) is undifferentiated and pricing is set by global supply-demand it cannot influence.

## Risk Assessment

**Extreme spot-market cyclicality (82% spot exposure)** is the dominant risk and the binary at the center of the thesis. The 10-K explicitly warns that geopolitical tailwinds (Russia/Ukraine rerouting, Red Sea/Houthi attacks, Venezuela sanctions, Hormuz) that inflated 2023-2025 rates could normalize, causing rates to "decline significantly."

The macro backdrop is a binary Hormuz outcome with a widened distribution. A reopening framework has regained market traction, which would compress VLCC rates and INSW's earnings quickly. The countervailing consideration is that this is a diplomatic signal in a long series that has reversed repeatedly within 48-72 hours, with physical verification (sustained uninterrupted commercial transit) absent. A confirmed reopening is the primary downside catalyst, while a deal failure and rate spike toward prior peaks is the primary upside catalyst.

**Rising supply / orderbook** (VLCCs leading) is the structural risk that ends the upcycle independent of geopolitics.

**US-China Section 301 port-fee war:** INSW owns 13 China-built vessels; reciprocal fees were imposed then suspended for one year on November 10, 2025. Re-escalation is a real cost/operational risk.

**Tax-structure fragility:** reliance on the U.S. Section 883 exemption and, post-redomiciliation, the Bermuda Qualifying International Shipping Income exclusion under the new 15% Pillar Two-aligned regime. Both depend on substance tests that could be challenged.

**Governance:** the Amended & Restated Rights Plan (poison pill) was set to expire April 10, 2026; the April 9, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01 + 3.03) is consistent with the Board renewing the anti-takeover protection on the eve of expiry, though the underlying 8-K has not been directly verified. Combined with the Marshall Islands domicile (weaker shareholder rights) and the Bermuda subsidiary structure, this reduces the odds and ease of a takeover premium even amid sector consolidation interest.

**Other:** dependence on third-party technical managers (90-day termination notice) and pools; nine MRs carried ~$43.3M above appraised market value with no impairment taken.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing an imminent binary event. Near-term ATM IV is 70.1% versus 36.9% historical volatility (rich by 33.2pp), at the 83rd percentile within the chain, with the term structure in backwardation (70.1% near-term vs 55.6% long-term). Put/call skew is balanced (-1.9%), and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.06 to 0.42 over the last snapshot interval while IV ticked up 5.2pp on a 1.2% price gain. Rising IV alongside a roughly stable price signals the market is pricing an upcoming catalyst (the June 18 FOMC sits in the window, but the dominant driver is the Hormuz resolution that directly determines spot rates). The balanced skew indicates the market views the binary as two-sided rather than a one-directional crash, consistent with the wide $56-120 analyst range. This confirms rather than challenges the HOLD: the options market is independently pricing the same unresolved binary that caps fundamental conviction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$110-120):** The Hormuz disruption persists or escalates (the world model assigns 30-40% to a deal-failure scenario). VLCC rates sustain near or above $86,693/day, 2026 EPS lands near or above the $13.90 consensus, the variable dividend continues to pay out cyclical cash, and the strong balance sheet lets INSW buy distressed tonnage. At ~8x sustained $14 earnings the stock approaches $110, with the $120 high target achievable on a deal-failure rate spike toward prior peaks.

**Bear Case (~$45-50, -37% to -43%):** A Hormuz reopening framework holds (world model 25-30%). VLCC rates revert toward mid-cycle levels, earnings normalize toward the $6.42 next-year consensus or below, the variable dividend compresses sharply, and the rising orderbook adds supply pressure into 2027-2028. At 7-8x normalized $6 earnings the stock falls to the mid-$40s, with the analyst low of $56 representing a milder normalization.

**Base Case (~$72-80):** The conflict grinds on without clean resolution (world model 30-40% sustained-closure scenario), rates stay elevated but volatile, and the stock trades in a wide band around current levels as the market refuses to capitalize peak earnings at a high multiple while the binary remains unresolved. The balance sheet provides downside support; the orderbook and variable dividend cap upside re-rating.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric around the current price with a fat downside tail on confirmed reopening. The fundamentals have not deteriorated — Q1 was a record, the balance sheet improved, integration is progressing — but the ~15% pullback from the May high reflects the market beginning to price the reopening framework, not a thesis break. At a cyclical peak with a binary catalyst pending, balance-sheet quality does not convert a two-sided bet into an asymmetric long.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The directional question on Hormuz dominates the fundamental quality of the business, and that question is genuinely two-sided with physical verification absent. This is an honest acknowledgment that the central variable is unresolved rather than a confident growth or income hold.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Physical verification that the Hormuz disruption is durable — 72+ hours of sustained chokepoint stress or escalation rather than diplomatic de-escalation — combined with VLCC spot rates holding above ~$70,000/day, which would support sustained earnings well above the $6.42 next-year consensus.
- A pullback toward normalized fair value (mid-$50s or below) that would re-establish asymmetric upside even under a rate-normalization scenario.
- Clustered open-market insider buying (3+ C-suite), which would corroborate management conviction currently absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Confirmed Hormuz reopening with 72+ hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial transit, which would compress the disruption premium directly driving earnings.
- VLCC spot rates falling below ~$40,000/day on a sustained basis, signaling cycle rollover.
- Re-escalation of US-China Section 301 port fees materially raising operating costs on the 13 China-built vessels.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August) will reveal whether the rate spike persisted into the quarter. The Hormuz physical-verification status is the more immediate trigger and should be monitored continuously. The June 18 FOMC is a secondary variance event for the broader energy complex.

## Conclusion

International Seaways is a well-managed, exceptionally well-capitalized tanker operator that delivered record Q1 2026 results. The quality of the balance sheet (net LTV <7%, ~$918M liquidity, 44% unencumbered fleet) and the demonstrated capital-allocation discipline (>$1B returned, TSR 470.75 vs peer 196) are real and durable. What is not durable is the earnings level driving the +302% estimate revisions and the ~7.2x optical multiple. Those figures capitalize a war-driven VLCC spike to $86,693/day, a temporary disruption premium that consensus itself expects to halve next year. The macro backdrop is a binary Hormuz outcome with a widened distribution where a de-escalation framework has regained traction even as physical verification remains absent.

The stock's ~15% pullback from its May high reflects the market beginning to discount that reopening, not a fundamental break; but at a cyclical peak with a two-sided binary pending, the lower price does not create asymmetric upside. The options market independently prices an imminent ±13% event with backwardated IV. With insiders net sellers post-rally, the dividend variable rather than an anchor, a rising ~17%-of-fleet orderbook threatening 2026-2028, and a renewed poison pill capping takeover optionality, the setup is balanced rather than compelling in either direction. **HOLD.** Become a buyer on physical verification of durable disruption or on a pullback toward normalized fair value in the mid-$50s; move to avoid on confirmed sustained Hormuz transit.
