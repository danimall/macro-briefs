**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Gaming & Leisure Properties is the original gaming-focused triple-net-lease REIT, spun from PENN Entertainment in 2013. It owns the real estate under 69 casinos across 20 states (expanding to 22 via new California and Virginia deals), leases them back under long-term master leases where tenants bear all property costs, and collected 100% of contractual cash rent in 2025, as it has through every prior stress period including COVID closures. The financial reality is that of a stable, cash-generative landlord: 2025 revenue of $1,594.8M (+4.1%), net income to common of $825.1M (+5.2%), operating cash flow of $1,129.4M, AFFO of $1,120M, and a dividend recently raised 5% to $0.82/quarter (annualized ~$3.28, yielding ~7.3%) at a comfortable ~78% AFFO payout.

The stock has drifted from its $49.95 52-week high (March 2026) to $45.17, roughly 17% below the $54 consensus target and just above the $45 low target. The move reflects the interest-rate sensitivity inherent to a 7%-yielding, spread-dependent REIT rather than any fundamental deterioration; estimate revisions are modestly positive (current-year EPS revised +1.4% over 90 days) and the deal pipeline continues to add accretive rent at 7-12% cap rates. This is a genuine income vehicle with a well-covered, rising distribution.

The case for a stronger call is capped by three durable issues that keep risk/reward balanced. First, tenant concentration is rising: PENN operates 34 of 69 facilities and Bally's, a more leveraged, more speculative operator, has expanded to 15 facilities plus the $940M Chicago development. Second, GLPI is increasingly a specialty construction and tribal lender, layering credit and completion risk (and sovereign-immunity enforcement gaps) onto what was a stabilized-real-estate story. Third, a higher-for-longer rate path raises refinancing costs (visible in the 5.25%/5.75% 2025 notes replacing retired 5.375% paper) and pressures the multiple on a rate-sensitive yield. We rate GLPI a HOLD on an income basis.

## Company Overview

GLPI is a self-managed, gaming-focused net-lease REIT structured as an UPREIT, with substantially all operations conducted through GLP Capital, L.P. (GLPI owns 97.1%). The UPREIT structure lets property sellers contribute assets for OP Units and defer taxes, a meaningful acquisition tool in a competitive market. The portfolio comprises interests in 69 gaming and related facilities on roughly 6,600 acres, 100% occupied, leased under triple-net master leases.

The business model is straightforward. Tenants pay rent and bear all property operating costs — maintenance, insurance, taxes, utilities, and capex — which produces structurally high margins (net margin above 50%) because GLPI's own operating expenses run only ~$393M, of which ~$266M is non-cash depreciation. Revenue grows through three channels: contractual escalators, sale-leaseback acquisitions, and development/construction funding that converts to lease rent. The strategy centers on regional, drive-to gaming markets, which are less dependent on air travel and have proven resilient across cycles.

Tenant concentration is high by design. Roughly 97% of cash rent comes from five tenants: PENN, Bally's, Caesars (6), Boyd (4), and Cordish (3), with smaller contributions from Hard Rock-managed Rockford, Strategic Gaming, and American Racing. The only direct public peer is VICI Properties, which is larger, carries a lower cost of capital, and is more Las Vegas-weighted. GLPI occupies the regional/drive-to niche and differentiates through its willingness to fund development and tribal casino projects that pure sale-leaseback buyers avoid.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily on escalators and acquisitions: ~$1.45B (2022), ~$1.46B (2023), $1,531.5M (2024), and $1,594.8M (2025). Operating income rose to $1,201M. The 2025 revenue bridge is instructive: +$73.6M from recent acquisitions and development funding, +$17.7M from lease escalations, and +$2.3M from higher percentage rent. Percentage rent is only 4.8% of cash income, so variable exposure to tenant operating performance is modest.

Cash generation is high quality. Operating cash flow exceeded net income, and AFFO covers the $871.9M in dividends paid, comfortable coverage that supports the recent distribution increase. Diluted EPS of $2.95 grew 2.8%. Adjusted EBITDA of $1,467M rose 6.7%. One earnings-visible swing factor to monitor is the CECL provision for credit losses, which fell from $37.3M (2024) to $8.7M (2025); as the financing-receivable and development-loan book grows, this line will become more volatile.

The balance sheet is investment-grade oriented. Total debt is ~$7.2B ($6,350M senior unsecured notes, $600M term loan, $331.6M revolver), only ~13% variable-rate, with D/E of ~1.58x. GLPI is in compliance with all covenants (max 60% debt/asset value, min 1.5x fixed-charge coverage). In 2025 it issued $600M of 5.25% notes (2033) and $700M of 5.75% notes (2037) to redeem $975M of 5.375% notes due 2026, terming out maturities but at higher coupons, which pushed interest expense to $373.9M. The company funds growth with a debt/equity mix, settling forward equity sales (8.17M shares for $404M in June, 7.59M more in Q3 for $363M gross) and maintaining a $1.25B ATM program ($886.7M remaining); this issuance dilutes per-share metrics but keeps leverage contained.

A note on the Altman Z-Score of 1.25 that would flag "distress" on a standardized screen: this is a model artifact for a REIT. The Altman formula penalizes low asset turnover (0.02, inherent to a rent-collecting landlord) and treats REIT leverage as risk. The Piotroski partial score of 4/4 (positive net income, ROA, OCF, and OCF above net income), 100% rent collection, and covenant compliance describe the actual credit profile of a sound, cash-generative business. The earnings surprise record is choppy quarter-to-quarter (a 19.1% beat in Q4'25, a 17.5% beat in Q3'25, a 29.5% miss in Q2'25, a 1.8% miss in Q1'26), driven largely by timing of gains, CECL provisions, and one-time items rather than operating trend.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is durable but modest. The base is contractual: escalators added $17.7M in 2025, and the master leases carry annual rent bumps. On top of that, GLPI has deployed roughly $3.7B in transactions since January 2024 at accretive cap rates. Recent deals include Sunland Park ($183.75M at 8.16%), PENN's M Resort tower (7.79%) and Joliet relocation (7.75%), Casino Queen Baton Rouge landside (9.0%), Bally's Chicago funding ($201.6M of a $940M commitment at 8.5%), and new tribal partnerships (Ione at 11%; Dry Creek/Sonoma at a 12.5% term loan converting to a 9.75% sale-leaseback cap). Announced expansions into Virginia (Live! Virginia, $467M commitment at 8.0%) and California (Dry Creek) add pipeline. With a blended cost of capital below these cap rates, each funded deal is accretive to AFFO.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum. Current-quarter EPS is $0.80 (+0.9% over 90 days), next-quarter $0.81 (+2.6%), and current-year $3.22 (+1.4%); next-year sits at $3.33 (-0.8% over 90 days, essentially flat). This is a low-single-digit compounding profile — escalators plus accretive spread investing minus dilution — not the double-digit revision velocity that would signal an earnings inflection. The realistic AFFO/share growth trajectory is mid-single-digit, which combined with the ~7.3% yield frames a high-single-digit-to-low-double-digit total-return expectation if the multiple holds.

The growth ceiling is set by cost of capital. As a REIT distributing at least 90% of taxable income, GLPI must continuously raise external debt and equity to grow. A higher-for-longer rate environment simultaneously raises debt costs and depresses the equity valuation used to fund deals, narrowing the accretion spread. The pivot toward higher-yielding development and tribal loans (11-12.5% coupons) is partly a response to that pressure, and it trades incremental yield for incremental credit and completion risk.

## Valuation Assessment

GLPI trades at a trailing/forward P/E of ~14x and ~14x forward AFFO on ~$3.22 estimated 2026 EPS. Against the "Other Specialized REITs" peer set (median P/E 22.4, median yield 4.5%), GLPI screens cheap on the multiple and rich on the yield. That comparison is imperfect: the peer group blends billboard REITs (LAMR 28.1x, OUT 29.0x) with net-lease names (EPR 18.3x at a 6.3% yield, FCPT 22.4x at 5.8%, SAFE 10.2x). The cleaner comparables are the net-lease/experiential names, against which GLPI's ~14x is a discount and its 7.3% yield a premium, justified partly by tenant concentration and the 2031 lease-renewal overhang.

Our calibration treats a low multiple as a value-trap warning absent a specific catalyst, and the record shows cheap Real Estate names have not reliably outperformed. GLPI is not a value trap — the cash flows are real and the dividend is covered at 78% of AFFO — but the discount to net-lease peers reflects two structural discounts rather than a clear mispricing: (1) the concentration and renewal risk that VICI (more diversified, lower cost of capital) does not carry to the same degree, and (2) rate sensitivity on a 7%+ yield. The absolute yield provides genuine support and is the durable component of the return. The re-rating case rests on the consensus $54 target (mean $54.13, median $54.00, range $45-62), which implies ~20% upside plus yield. We view that as achievable only if rates ease and the deal pipeline continues to convert without credit events; the more probable base case is a total return anchored on the yield plus mid-single-digit AFFO growth.

We do not treat the ~10% decline from the March high as thesis deterioration: fundamentals are intact and estimates are stable-to-rising, which improves the entry yield. But the decline is moderate (under 15%) and rate-driven, not a mispricing the market is missing. The valuation score reflects a genuinely cheap multiple with real yield support, discounted for the absence of an independent re-rating catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

GLPI's moat is its scale and structure within a two-player public gaming-REIT market. VICI is the larger, lower-cost-of-capital competitor, weighted toward Las Vegas Strip assets (Caesars/MGM), while GLPI specializes in regional, drive-to markets. GLPI's UPREIT structure and its willingness to underwrite development and tribal financing let it access deals that pure sale-leaseback buyers avoid; the tribal loans (Ione 11%, Dry Creek 12.5%) and construction commitments (Bally's Chicago) are examples of yield GLPI can capture that VICI generally does not pursue. The trade-off is that this differentiation moves GLPI up the risk curve from stabilized real estate toward specialty lending.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving. GLPI competes for acquisitions against VICI, private equity, sovereign funds, and lenders, several with lower costs of capital, which pressures cap rates on the best assets. Its own diversification narrative is complicated by rising Bally's exposure: the Standard General merger (Feb 2025), Casino Queen absorption, and Chicago development have concentrated more rent in a more leveraged operator, with several Bally's lease coverage tests explicitly tied to parent net leverage exceeding 5.5x. On the demand side, the newly emphasized secular threat is federally regulated prediction markets, which can operate even in states banning online gaming and could drain revenue from tenants' regional casinos, a novel headwind to the underlying operator health that supports GLPI's rent coverage.

## Risk Assessment

**PENN tenant concentration** is the dominant risk. PENN subsidiaries generate the largest single revenue share (over $940M across the Amended PENN, PENN 2023, Amended Pinnacle, and Morgantown leases). PENN's interactive/ESPN Bet segment has been a drag on the parent, and any material adverse event — including a change of control or board composition — is explicitly flagged as potentially materially adverse to GLPI. Rent coverage on PENN leases remains healthy (1.86-2.12x), which cushions the risk but does not eliminate it.

**The 2031 lease cliff** is under-discussed. The Amended Pinnacle (PENN) master lease (~$365M cash income in 2025) and the Boyd master lease (~$107M) both expire 4/30/2031, and renewal is at the tenant's option, not GLPI's. PENN's two large master leases expire 10/31/2033. These are multi-year forward risks that do not appear in current metrics.

**Development and financing execution risk is rising.** GLPI's shift into construction lending and tribal financing (Bally's Chicago up to $940M with only $201.6M funded; Ione $110M; Dry Creek $225.3M; Live! Virginia $467M; Bally's Lincoln $700M call right) carries cost-overrun, delay, and materials-inflation risk (tariffs explicitly cited for Chicago). Tribal loans carry sovereign-immunity enforcement risk: GLPI cannot exercise customary foreclosure remedies. The CECL provision's swing between years shows this book's earnings volatility.

**Rate and capital-structure sensitivity** is structural. GLPI must continuously raise external capital, so higher rates raise refinancing costs and depress the equity valuation used to fund deals. The ~7.3% yield is itself rate-sensitive and competes with a rising risk-free rate.

**Bally's leverage** is the sharpest tenant-quality concern given the operator's more speculative profile and coverage tests tied to parent net leverage above 5.5x. The 7/1/2025 reshuffle moving DraftKings at Casino Queen and Baton Rouge into the corporate-guaranteed Bally's Master Lease II ($28.9M reallocated) strengthened GLPI's credit protection, a positive mitigant.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 22.8% is modestly rich versus 18.7% one-year historical volatility (a 4.1pp spread), and IV has risen 3.1pp since mid-June while the price rose only 1.3%, consistent with the market pricing rate/macro uncertainty rather than a company-specific catalyst. The 6.2% one-month put/call skew shows elevated near-term put demand, and the put/call OI ratio compressed from 0.96 to 0.47. The term structure is flat (near 22.8% ≈ far 24.4%), so uncertainty is evenly distributed. Net read: mildly cautious positioning, no acute stress, and no signal that overrides the fundamental view. The ±5.6% implied one-month move is unremarkable for a REIT into a macro-driven tape.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$54-62):** Rates ease, compressing the required yield and re-rating GLPI toward the net-lease peer multiple. The 7-12% cap-rate pipeline (Virginia, California/Dry Creek, Bally's Chicago) converts to rent without credit events, driving mid-single-digit AFFO growth on top of the ~7.3% yield. Dividend growth continues at ~5%, and the consensus $54 target (up to a $62 high) is achieved, delivering ~20-37% upside plus yield. The 100% rent-collection track record and covered payout underpin the income floor.

**Bear Case (~$38-40):** A higher-for-longer or rising-rate path pushes the required yield toward 8%+, derating the stock 12-15%. PENN or Bally's tenant stress (interactive drag at PENN, leverage at Bally's) pressures coverage and raises re-leasing risk into the 2031 cliff. A development or tribal-loan credit event (cost overruns at Chicago, an unenforceable tribal default) drives CECL provisions back toward the $37M+ level and dents AFFO. Continued equity issuance dilutes per-share growth.

**Base Case (~$46-48):** Total return anchored on the ~7.3% yield plus mid-single-digit AFFO growth, with the multiple roughly stable. The stock delivers high-single-digit total return over 12 months absent a rate tailwind, closing part of the gap to the $54 consensus but not fully re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income hold. The return is dominated by the covered ~7.3% distribution plus modest AFFO growth; capital appreciation to the $54 consensus is a rate-dependent kicker, not the base case.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed shift toward Fed easing that compresses long-end yields, materially improving the accretion spread and the re-rating case.
- The multiple compresses further (price toward the low-$40s / yield toward ~8%) with fundamentals unchanged, restoring asymmetric upside to the $54 target.
- Evidence of pipeline conversion without credit deterioration — CECL provision stable near 2025's $8.7M and new deals (Virginia, Dry Creek) closing on stated terms — alongside continued positive estimate revisions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material PENN or Bally's tenant stress that pushes rent coverage below required thresholds, or any change-of-control/bankruptcy event at a top-two tenant.
- A development or tribal-loan credit event driving the CECL provision back above ~$30M, or a Bally's Chicago cost-overrun/impairment disclosure.
- A dividend that is no longer covered by AFFO (payout above ~90%) or a distribution cut.
- Rates rising materially further with the stock breaking below the $41.17 52-week low on a fundamental (not purely macro) basis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the subsequent dividend declaration, with the 2031 lease-renewal question moving into sharper focus over the next 12-24 months as tenant negotiations approach.

## Conclusion

GLPI is a sound, investment-grade-oriented gaming REIT with 100% rent collection, a well-covered ~7.3% dividend at a ~78% AFFO payout, and a genuinely accretive 7-12% cap-rate deal pipeline. At ~14x forward AFFO and ~17% below the $54 consensus target, it is cheap on the multiple and offers real yield support; the income case is intact and the recent price decline has improved the entry yield rather than signaling deterioration. The score has drifted lower over the past four reports (6.3 → 6.0), driven by declining risk and competitive-position marks; the current data confirms that trajectory rather than reverses it, as rising PENN/Bally's concentration, the pivot into development/tribal lending, the 2031 lease cliff, and rate sensitivity all cap the re-rating case even as the yield remains dependable.

The recommendation is HOLD, classified as an income hold. Own GLPI for the covered, growing distribution and the modest escalator-plus-acquisition growth, not for a near-term re-rating. Risk/reward is balanced: the yield and coverage anchor the downside, while the structural discounts to net-lease peers are fairly earned rather than a clear mispricing. A rate tailwind or a further multiple compression toward an ~8% yield would be the cleanest path to a BUY.
