**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Asbury Automotive Group is one of the largest franchised auto retailers in the U.S., operating 202 new-vehicle franchises across 158 locations plus its vertically-integrated finance-and-insurance underwriter, Total Care Auto (TCA). The investment question is whether the operating deterioration is already in the price. The stock has fallen roughly 30% from its July 2025 high of $274.50 to ~$193, and now trades at ~7-8x trailing GAAP EPS against an automotive-retail peer median near 26.7x.

The central fact of the franchise is post-COVID new-vehicle margin normalization. New-vehicle gross profit per unit fell from $3,697 (2024) to $3,432 (2025) to $3,061 on a same-store basis in Q1 2026 (-10% YoY), and that re-rating is not finished. GAAP net income rose 42% to $187.8M in Q1 2026, but that figure is entirely a $125.8M pre-tax divestiture gain. Income from operations fell 17% to $193.9M, adjusted EPS of $5.37 missed consensus ($5.68) and dropped ~21% from $6.82 a year earlier, and SG&A as a percentage of gross profit spiked to 70.2% from 63.0% as the cost base outran the now-normalizing revenue.

The bear case is real but well-telegraphed and broadly shared across the public dealer group. Offsetting it: a 3.3% FCF margin, an aggressively shrinking share count (diluted shares down from 22.4M in 2022 to ~18.6M, with $147M repurchased in Q1 2026 alone and authorization raised to $500M), open-market insider buying by both the CEO (~$205) and a director (down to $182) above today's price, and durable profit pools in parts-and-service (~48% of gross profit) and F&I (~23%) underpinned by a record 12.8-year average U.S. vehicle age. With the lowest sell-side target ($202) sitting above the current price, 11% short interest creating squeeze risk on any upside surprise, and no identifiable catalyst in either direction, the balance of evidence points to HOLD. This is a deteriorating but profitable, cash-generative, buyback-supported cyclical whose discount already reflects the margin story the market has been watching for two years.

## Company Overview

Asbury operates 202 new-vehicle franchises across 158 locations spanning 34 brands and 14 states, plus 37 collision centers and TCA, its in-house F&I product underwriter. Headquartered in Atlanta with roughly 15,000 employees, the company reports two segments: Dealerships and TCA.

The revenue mix understates where profit comes from. In FY2025 new vehicles were 52.8% of revenue, used 29.1%, parts-and-service (P&S) 13.9%, and F&I 4.3%. On gross profit, the picture inverts: P&S contributes ~48%, F&I ~23%, new vehicles only ~20%, and used ~8%. New-vehicle sales function as a low-margin customer-acquisition funnel feeding the higher-margin, more recession-resilient service and finance businesses.

Brand exposure on new-vehicle revenue is 40% import, 32% luxury, 28% domestic, with Toyota/Lexus the largest OEM at 30%, followed by Ford/Lincoln 14% and Honda/Acura 10%. The moats are conventional for the group: state franchise-law protection, OEM-certified warranty and service work that independents struggle to match on increasingly complex vehicles, local brand equity, and the differentiated TCA underwriting engine that captures F&I economics most peers outsource.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory.** Revenue moved from $14.6B (2022) to $14.1B (2023) to $17.2B (2024) to $18.0B (2025, +5%). Growth has been acquisition-driven (Koons in December 2023, Herb Chambers in July 2025), not organic — same-store volumes are declining. Net income fell from $997M (2022) to $603M (2023) to $430M (2024) and rebounded to $492M (2025), but the 2025 figure is flattered by $80.2M of divestiture gains. Net margin compressed from 6.8% (2022) to 2.7% (2025).

**Q1 2026 in detail.** Revenue of $4.11B was down 1% YoY. SG&A as a percentage of gross profit was driven higher by the Herb Chambers cost base, ~$6.1M of Tekion DMS implementation costs, and a tough comparison (the prior-year quarter included a $10M cyber-insurance recovery). Same-store new units fell 11% and used units 12%; SAAR slipped to 15.6M from a tariff-pull-forward-inflated 16.5M. The bright spots were used-vehicle GPU resilience and P&S, supported by the record vehicle age.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $775M and FCF $589M, a 3.3% FCF margin. The balance sheet warrants caution: Altman Z of 2.45 (grey zone), D/E ~3.0, current ratio 0.95 (sub-1), and transaction-adjusted net leverage of 3.2x, up from 2.9x and at the high end of the 2.5–3.5x target range. Roughly $1.97B of variable-rate debt means a 100bp move shifts annual interest by ~$20M. Senior notes (4.5%–5.0% coupons, 2028–2032 maturities) are well-laddered and below current market rates, so there is no near-term refinancing cliff. Restricted-payment capacity ($1.34B) depends on staying at or below 3.0x total leverage, which constrains both buybacks and further debt-funded M&A.

**Capital allocation.** The defining recent move is share shrink: diluted shares fell from 22.4M (2022) to ~18.6M, with $99.9M repurchased in 2025; the authorization was raised to $500M on 2/25/2026 ($453M remaining). No dividend. Management is recycling capital — divesting 24 franchises in 2025 and 14 in Q1 2026 ($361.5M proceeds, $125.8M gain), pruning underperformers and high-tax-state stores while buying back stock near multi-year lows.

A note on earnings quality: in both 2025 and Q1 2026, divestiture gains were a major contributor to pre-tax income. This is a deliberate portfolio-optimization strategy, but it inflates reported profitability versus underlying operations, and investors should anchor to adjusted operating earnings rather than GAAP net income.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is negative. Same-store new and used unit volumes fell double digits in Q1 2026 as SAAR normalized off tariff-pull-forward highs. Top-line growth is acquisition-funded and is now constrained by the leverage ceiling. The growth levers that remain are second-order:

- **TCA rollout** across acquired stores (Koons done, Herb Chambers planned for 2026) is a genuine earnings lever but carries a J-curve. Rolling TCA across acquired dealerships defers F&I revenue over the contract term versus immediate recognition for third-party products, depressing near-term reported F&I (gross profit down 4% in Q1 2026) while building a deferred-revenue annuity. The optical drag understates long-term economics.
- **P&S annuity** is the most durable growth source. A record 12.8-year average vehicle age and an expanding installed base of complex, warranty-bearing vehicles support steady service demand. This is the one part of the business with a structural tailwind.

Against these, estimate revisions are mildly negative: current-year consensus EPS is $25.93, down 2.9% over 90 days, with the current quarter trimmed 5.2% to $6.46. This is a modest downward drift, not a collapse — next-year consensus ($29.58) is essentially flat (+0.1%) over 90 days, implying the Street sees earnings stabilizing in FY27 as the cost base right-sizes and TCA deferral effects mature. The growth score reflects negative organic volumes and falling near-term estimates, tempered by the genuine (if back-end-loaded) TCA and service annuities.

## Valuation Assessment

ABG trades at ~7-8x trailing GAAP EPS (forward P/E 7.08 on the data provided), a steep discount to the automotive-retail peer median P/E of 26.7x. The closest comparable, Group 1 Automotive (GPI), trades at 12.5x — itself a low-multiple group reflecting shared margin-normalization concerns — so even versus the most relevant peer, ABG carries a ~40% discount.

The discount is partly earned and partly excessive. It is earned because reported earnings are inflated by divestiture gains, operating income is falling, and the cost base is not yet right-sized for normalized GPU. It is excessive because the business still generates a 3.3% FCF margin, is shrinking its share count at a double-digit annual pace near trough valuations, and carries durable profit pools that the market treats as if they were as cyclical as new-vehicle sales. On FY27 consensus EPS of ~$29.58, the stock trades at ~6.5x — a multiple that prices in continued deterioration rather than stabilization.

The most direct evidence the discount is overstated comes from insiders and the sell side. The CEO bought 5,000 shares in the open market at ~$205 in March 2026; a director bought as recently as May 2026 at $182.31. The lowest analyst target is $202, the mean $232, and the median $225 — every published target sits above the current price. Issuing a maximally bearish view below the price insiders paid and below the entire sell-side range is difficult to defend when the bear thesis (GPU normalization) is sector-wide, well-telegraphed, and has already driven a 30% drawdown. The cheapness alone is not a buy signal (cyclically depressed dealers can stay cheap), but it is also not grounds for AVOID when the deterioration is gradual, the balance sheet is solvent, and buybacks support the floor.

The valuation score reflects a stock that is cheap on both trailing and forward earnings with a specific reason the discount may be overdone (insider buying, FCF support, buyback floor), offset by the absence of a catalyst to close the gap and genuine earnings-quality concerns that justify some of the discount.

## Competitive Landscape

The franchised-dealer sector is in a synchronized post-COVID margin normalization, with new-vehicle GPUs reverting toward pre-pandemic levels across all public dealers as OEM inventory rebuilds (ABG ended 2025 at ~52 days' supply, still below historical norms). ABG's competitive position is stable rather than improving or eroding. Its structural defenses — gross profit anchored by P&S and F&I, the record vehicle-age service tailwind, and the vertically-integrated TCA underwriter — are intact and differentiate it modestly from peers who outsource F&I.

The competitive trajectory is roughly flat. Acquisitions are adding scale and geographic reach (Northeast expansion into MA/RI), but same-store volume declines and recurring franchise-rights impairments ($117M in 2023, $149.5M in 2024, $141M in 2025) signal that a portion of the acquired base is underperforming. The longer-term structural threat is EV direct-to-consumer models (Tesla, Rivian) that are exempt from state franchise laws and erode the regulatory moat — a slow-moving but real headwind. On balance, ABG is holding its position in a stable competitive structure, neither gaining nor losing meaningful share.

## Risk Assessment

1. **New-vehicle margin normalization (primary, ongoing).** GPU is still falling and the Q1 SG&A deleverage shows the cost base is not yet sized for normalized margins. This is the dominant earnings risk but it is well understood and already reflected in the 30% drawdown.
2. **Tariffs / trade policy.** 25% tariffs on imported autos/parts against 40% import + 32% luxury exposure. The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA-based tariffs in February 2026 (refunds processing), but Section 232/301/122 tariffs remain. Net effect on cost structure and demand is unpredictable.
3. **Leverage and rate sensitivity.** 3.2x net leverage, sub-1 current ratio, ~$2B variable-rate debt, grey-zone Altman Z. Restricted-payment capacity depends on staying ≤3.0x. The cushion has thinned post-Herb Chambers, constraining further M&A.
4. **FTC enforcement action.** An administrative action (filed 8/16/2024) alleges deceptive add-on F&I sales and ECOA violations at three David McDavid stores; ABG counter-sued on constitutional grounds. Outcome and loss range not estimable — an idiosyncratic, hard-to-handicap overhang on the F&I profit pool.
5. **Tekion DMS migration.** Only ~38 of ~158 stores converted by end-2025; dual-system costs and integration risk weigh on near-term SG&A.
6. **Integration risk.** Herb Chambers ($1.76B, 100% debt-funded) — synergy realization, TCA rollout, and control-environment integration ongoing. ICFR was ineffective at 12/31/2024 (since remediated), worth monitoring as new acquisitions fold into controls.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress but not directional fear. Near-term ATM IV of 62.3% is rich against 32.2% one-year historical volatility, with a steep backwardation (62.3% at one week versus 33.6% at six months), indicating the market expects an imminent event-driven move rather than sustained elevated volatility — though with no scheduled catalyst, this likely reflects thin near-dated liquidity and post-earnings residual. The put/call skew is balanced (1.6% near-term, modestly negative -3.2% at six months), and the put/call OI ratio is very low at 0.08, which does not corroborate aggressive bearish positioning. IV jumped 25.8pp since mid-May while price rose 9.5%, consistent with a market hedging uncertainty rather than betting on a directional break. Combined with 11% short interest (4.1 days to cover), the setup carries squeeze risk on any positive surprise, which argues against a maximally bearish stance.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
New-vehicle GPU stabilizes near current levels rather than continuing to fall, the Herb Chambers cost base right-sizes through 2026, and SG&A/gross profit retraces toward the low-60s. The TCA rollout's deferred-revenue annuity begins to flow through, and the P&S business continues to compound on the aging vehicle fleet. At ~6.5x FY27 consensus EPS with double-digit annual share-count reduction, even modest multiple re-rating toward GPI's 12.5x implies substantial upside. Insider buying at ~$205 and the raised buyback authorization signal management conviction. Bull case ~$280-310, roughly the analyst high and a return toward the prior peak.

### Bear Case
GPU normalization continues past current levels, SG&A stays elevated, and same-store volumes keep declining as SAAR remains soft. Tariffs raise the cost of the import mix and dampen demand. Leverage at 3.2x and a grey-zone Z-score limit flexibility if a downturn deepens, and the FTC action or further franchise impairments add pressure. Divestiture gains can no longer mask falling operating earnings. Bear case ~$172-180, roughly the 52-week low region, ~7-11% below current.

The base case sits between these: gradual GPU stabilization, a slow SG&A retrace, and earnings broadly flat-to-modestly-down before recovering in FY27, with buybacks providing per-share support. That maps to ~$220-235, in line with the analyst mean/median, ~14-22% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on uncertain grounds — the evidence does not support a confident directional call. The deterioration is real but priced; the value support and insider conviction are real but lack a near-term catalyst to be realized.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- New-vehicle same-store GPU stabilizes for two consecutive quarters (e.g., same-store new GPU holding above ~$3,000), confirming the margin floor.
- SG&A as a percentage of gross profit retraces below ~64%, demonstrating the Herb Chambers cost base is right-sized.
- Adjusted EPS (excluding divestiture gains) returns to YoY growth, removing the earnings-quality overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- New-vehicle GPU breaks materially below ~$2,800 same-store with no offsetting SG&A control, signaling margin normalization is overshooting.
- Net leverage rises above ~3.5x (covenant/restricted-payment threshold pressure) or buybacks are suspended.
- An adverse FTC ruling or a material new franchise-rights impairment that signals broader acquired-store underperformance.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July) is the natural checkpoint — specifically GPU trajectory, SG&A ratio, and whether adjusted operating earnings stabilize. The leadership transition (Daniel Clara to CEO, May 2026) adds an execution variable worth monitoring through the year.

## Conclusion

Asbury is a competently run, cash-generative franchised dealer working through a sector-wide new-vehicle margin normalization that is genuine and not yet complete. Q1 2026 operating income fell 17% and adjusted EPS fell ~21%, with a GAAP print flattered by divestiture gains and earnings quality increasingly dependent on portfolio churn. Those are real concerns and they justify the discount.

The discount is also the basis of the call. At ~7-8x trailing and ~6.5x forward earnings, below the lowest analyst target and below the prices both the CEO and a director paid in open-market buys, with a 3.3% FCF margin, a buyback shrinking the share count at a double-digit pace, durable P&S and F&I profit pools, and 11% short interest creating upside squeeze risk, the bear thesis is largely in the price. There is no catalyst to force the gap closed in either direction. The margin headwind and earnings-quality flags are offset by deep value and durable annuity economics, leaving a balanced HOLD. The prior trajectory of declining scores reflects the real operating deterioration; the current price has fallen far enough that risk/reward is now roughly symmetric rather than skewed to the downside.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Reassess at Q2 2026 earnings on GPU and SG&A trajectory.
