## Executive Summary

Merchants Bancorp is a $20.3 billion balance sheet operating through seven Indiana branches. The GICS "Regional Banks" label is close to meaningless here: MBIN is a nationally scaled affordable-multifamily agency lender, one of the larger mortgage warehouse lenders in the country, a LIHTC equity syndicator, and a large agency servicer, all funded substantially by custodial escrow deposits generated inside its own servicing book. That structure is a genuine funding advantage and the reason a seven-branch bank funded $66.3 billion of warehouse volume in 2025.

The investment situation is the back half of a credit-event recovery. FY2025 broke a decade-long record: net income fell to $218.8 million from $320.4 million, ROE collapsed from 16.86% to 9.88%, net charge-offs reached $124.1 million (roughly 1.1% of loans, $114.3 million of it multifamily and roughly 70% concentrated in five borrower relationships), and Merchants Bank entered a Memorandum of Understanding with the FDIC and the Indiana DFI on June 30, 2025. Since then the evidence has turned, and it has turned from independent sources rather than management framing alone. The MOU was terminated during Q1 2026. Criticized loans have declined for five consecutive quarters, from roughly $730 million to $505.5 million at 3/31/2026 and lower through Q2. Q2 2026 produced net income of $78.3 million, adjusted diluted EPS of $1.48 against a $1.22–1.24 consensus, a provision down 83% year over year, and record tangible book value of $39.93 per share. Consensus EPS has been revised up 6.5% for the current year and 4.4–5.2% for next year over 90 days, and the company has beaten in four consecutive quarters by an average of roughly 20%.

The problem is price. The stock has advanced 75.8% over twelve months and 11.1% since early July, and at $54.24 it sits above every verifiable 2026 transaction price except one insider sale, including the $41.03 average at which the company itself repurchased shares in Q1, after which the buyback essentially stopped with $97.0 million of the $100 million authorization unused. At 10.0x current-year consensus of $5.44, 9.1x next-year of $5.99, and 1.36x tangible book on a ~15% ROE, the shares embed roughly a 12.5% cost of equity, a reasonable discount for the risk set rather than an exploitable one. Three specific items remain unresolved and are worth more than the remaining upside: allowance of 0.67% of loans covering only 31% of $247.5 million of nonperforming loans; a single held-to-maturity non-agency multifamily security carried at $394.8 million (~22% of common equity) whose underlying pool was ~36% delinquent; and holdco CET1 falling 50 basis points in one quarter to 9.4% on warehouse-driven risk-weighted asset growth, at a company with a documented history of returning to the equity and preferred markets. Existing holders should stay for book-value compounding. New capital has no urgent entry signal here.

## Company Overview

Merchants Bancorp (Carmel, Indiana; founded 1990 as a mortgage bank, IPO 2017) reported $19.4 billion of assets, $13.0 billion of deposits and $2.3 billion of equity at 12/31/2025, growing to $20.3 billion of assets by 3/31/2026. Headcount is approximately 735. Goodwill is $8.0 million, reflecting an organically built franchise.

**Multi-family Mortgage Banking** originates and services FHA/HUD, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac affordable and USDA 538 loans, mostly selling fixed-rate production into agency MBS within about 30 days while retaining servicing. Two adjacent businesses sit inside the segment: Merchants Capital is a fully integrated LIHTC equity syndicator, and Merchants Investment Partners is a registered adviser deploying third-party institutional capital into Merchants-originated assets while also facilitating credit-risk-transfer vehicles and securitizations.

**Mortgage Warehousing** provides repurchase facilities, participations and MSR-collateralized lines to non-depository originators, including large agency seller/servicers. Funded volume was $33.0 billion (2023), $45.6 billion (2024), $66.3 billion (2025), and $19.6 billion in Q1 2026 versus $11.9 billion in Q1 2025.

**Banking** covers multifamily and healthcare bridge/construction loans referred from Merchants Capital, owner-occupied CRE, C&I, agricultural lending, single-family retail/correspondent mortgage banking, and SBA lending.

The businesses interlock: servicing custodial escrow funds warehouse lending; securities funded by those deposits are pledged to the FHLB to create surge capacity; the in-house mortgage platform provides a ready outlet for warehouse collateral. It is also a correlated exposure, because the same non-depository originators are simultaneously the largest borrowers and the largest depositors. Management's stated mitigant is the ability to stop funding an institution's loans if it withdraws deposits (typically 180 days' notice). That is a commercial lever effective institution by institution; it does not protect against a sector-wide non-bank mortgage funding event.

ROE was compressing before 2025 as capital was layered on; 2025 was a break in kind. The TSR index peaked at 340 in 2023 and fell to 195 by end-2025 while the peer index rose to 187, erasing roughly five years of relative outperformance in two. That erasure is what the 2026 trade has been recapturing.

**Q1 2026 (10-Q filing facts):** net income $67.7 million, diluted EPS $1.25 (versus $0.93), net interest income $128.6 million, NIM 2.92% (versus 2.89%), provision $15.3 million, noninterest income $46.6 million (versus $23.7 million), noninterest expense $75.6 million (+23%), effective tax rate 19.7% (versus 23.9%), TBVPS $38.55.

Quality of earnings requires two adjustments. Servicing-rights fair-value marks swung to +$8.9 million from –$0.8 million and derivative marks to +$2.7 million from –$2.3 million: roughly $11.6 million of the $22.9 million year-over-year noninterest income increase is non-cash mark-to-market. At the 19.7% rate that is about $9.3 million after tax, or roughly $0.20 of the $1.25 reported EPS on ~46.0 million diluted shares. MSR marks are a deliberate natural hedge against production volume, so they recur, but they are rate-driven and reverse. The tax rate also helped: transferable tax-credit purchases of $19.7 million in Q1 (following $151.3 million in FY2025) drove roughly four points of rate benefit and built an income tax receivable of $204.5 million, about 12% of common equity. Noninterest expense included $3.1 million of "collateral preservation expense," a direct cash cost of working out the problem multifamily credits alongside $60.2 million of OREO.

**Q2 2026 (externally reported 7/28/2026; the 10-Q was not yet on EDGAR at the time of research):** net income $78.3 million, adjusted diluted EPS $1.48 versus $1.22 non-GAAP consensus, revenue $182 million versus ~$179 million expected, provision down 83% year over year, criticized loans down a fifth consecutive quarter, TBVPS a record $39.93. The arithmetic reconciles: $78.3M less $10.265M of quarterly preferred dividends equals $68.0M to common, divided by $1.48 gives 46.0 million diluted shares, consistent with a $2.49 billion market cap at $54.24 on ~45.9 million shares. That internal consistency raises confidence, but the figures are not yet primary-filing confirmed, and the beat compares non-GAAP to non-GAAP.

**A data reconciliation note.** The supplied trailing/forward P/E of 10.86x is consistent with the frozen $54.24 price only against trailing four-quarter EPS of approximately $4.98 ($0.97 + $1.28 + $1.25 + $1.48). The separately supplied EPS field of $3.78 appears to be an FY2025 figure; dividing price by it yields 14.3x. I use consensus forward estimates ($5.44 current year, $5.99 next year) as the primary multiples throughout.

**Asset quality — the genuine tension.**

Q1 2026 net charge-offs of $23.0 million came from seven relationships with roughly 75% in two, the same lumpy multifamily signature as 2025 rather than diffuse deterioration. Nonperforming loans rose 25% sequentially while the allowance ratio fell and coverage dropped from 42% to 31%.

The company-consistent reading is that these are collateral-dependent credits already written down to appraised value through charge-off, so incremental allowance would double-count; criticized loans are the leading indicator falling while nonaccruals are the lagging indicator rising as problems migrate toward resolution. The bear reading is that 0.67%/31% leaves no cushion for appraisal error in a multifamily market where cap rates have repriced, and that Q2's 83% provision decline is under-reserving into a recovering stock. The disclosure does not resolve this. It is worth roughly $0.35–0.70 of annual EPS, and the Q2/Q3 nonaccrual trajectory alongside loss severity on resolved credits is the decisive test.

**The largest under-screened exposure.** The Q1 10-Q discloses a held-to-maturity non-agency multifamily MBS with carrying value ~$394.8 million in which approximately 36% of the underlying loan pool was delinquent, internally classified Special Mention, with Merchants owning the senior tranche behind approximately 17.9% of first-loss subordination held by junior tranche holders. Merchants is the holder of the senior claim, not a guarantor; delinquency is not loss, and 17.9% subordination is substantial protection unless realized pool losses are severe. But $394.8 million is roughly 22% of common equity (~$1.77 billion on TBVPS $38.55 × ~46 million shares) in a single position, and HTM accounting means neither fair-value moves nor OCI reflect deterioration; only a management credit-impairment determination would. Whether this is a retained senior interest in a securitization of Merchants-originated collateral (which would make it a second window onto the same borrower problems) or a third-party deal bought for yield and pledgeability is not established by the filings.

**Capital and funding.** Preferred stock of $551.3 million carries ~$41.1 million of annual dividends, a 7.45% blended cost absorbing roughly 19% of FY2025 consolidated earnings before common holders. Holdco CET1 was 9.4% at 3/31/2026, down from 9.9%; Bank CET1 11.9% from 12.8%. The driver was risk-weighted asset growth, not losses, with implied RWA of roughly $18.8 billion against $20.3 billion of assets, a ~93% RWA density reflecting warehouse repo and multifamily bridge risk weights. Borrowings of $4.8 billion include $4.36 billion of FHLB advances, with $2.0 billion maturing 5/14/2026 and $2.4 billion maturing 6/29/2026, both with FHLB put options, leaving roughly 21% of assets dependent on one wholesale counterparty. Core deposits are 93% of total, brokered deposits were cut roughly in half to $886 million, and uninsured deposits are $3.5 billion (27%). CRE concentration was 309% of Tier 1 plus allowance, down from 324% but above the 300% supervisory guidance threshold. Credit-risk-transfer positions (Merchants is the protection buyer and premium payer) reference roughly $2.5 billion of UPB plus a warehouse-referenced CDS, which is one reason the RWA density is tolerable but which adds counterparty performance risk and recurring premium expense.

Standard screening scores do not work here. Piotroski's operating-cash-flow-versus-net-income test in particular is uninterpretable: originations of loans held for sale run through operating cash flow, so an originate-to-sell warehouse bank reports deeply negative operating cash flow in precisely its strongest years.

## Growth Analysis

Two engines, on different footings.

**Warehouse share capture is real and quantified.** Q1 2026 funded volume of $19.6 billion versus $11.9 billion was +65%, against Mortgage Bankers Association industry origination growth of 43% over the same period. FY2025 volume of $66.3 billion was +45%. Supply of warehouse financing contracted after March 2023 as regional banks retreated from wholesale-funded, low-spread, high-RWA lending, and MBIN's custodial-escrow funding gives it a marginal cost of funds no brokered-CD-funded competitor can match.

The forward constraint is the mortgage market itself. Warehouse balances are a direct derivative of origination volume. MBIN grew 65% against a +43% industry backdrop; if the industry slows, growth requires share gains alone. Whether that share is being won on relationship and service or on price and credit terms cannot be determined from disclosure. [unverified — the brief contains no data on current mortgage rate levels, application demand trends, or buyer behavior]

**The fee engine is the more durable growth vector.** Zacks attributed part of the Q2 beat to stronger loan servicing and asset management fees, meaning the Merchants Capital servicing book and the MIP/LIHTC syndication complex rather than spread income. Servicing on a large agency book with typical ten-year call protection on multifamily is annuity-like. LIHTC syndication and third-party institutional capital management generate fees without consuming much balance sheet, which matters at 9.4% CET1. There is also $33.4 million of future LIHTC general-partner fees fully constrained with no revenue recognized (contingencies extending to 2043), unrecognized optionality that should not be capitalized.

**Estimate momentum is positive but modest.** Current-quarter consensus rose 7.4% in 30 days and 6.6% in 90; current year +6.5%; next year +4.4% over 30 days and +5.2% over 90. Four consecutive beats (18.9%, 6.5%, 33.7%, 20.5%) support confidence in the $5.44/$5.99 path. These are meaningfully positive revisions on a stock at 10x forward, but they are an order of magnitude below the +30% velocity that signals systematic underestimation of a fundamental change in earnings power.

**Capital is the binding constraint on the growth story, not credit.** At 9.4% holdco CET1 falling 50 basis points per quarter on RWA growth, two more comparable quarters would put the holdco near 8.4%. The available paths are slower growth, more credit-risk transfer (already in place at a recurring premium cost), earnings retention (payout is only ~8–9%, so this is already maximized), or issuance. The 10-K documents a repeated history of the last: the 2017 IPO, a May 2024 secondary, and multiple preferred offerings from 2019 through 2024. Q1 is seasonally the start of the mortgage year and warehouse balances are volatile, so the CET1 decline may partially reverse, but the structural arithmetic of ~93% RWA density means warehouse growth converts nearly one-for-one into capital consumption.

## Valuation Assessment

**Earnings multiples.** At $54.24 against consensus of $5.44 (current year) and $5.99 (next year): **10.0x and 9.1x**. Trailing four quarters of $4.98 gives **10.9x**. Peer median P/E is 12.0x (BANR 11.3x, WAFD 12.0x, CUBI 9.8x, FBNC 21.0x, BBT 22.1x).

**Normalized earnings.** Two adjustments matter. Reported H1 2026 EPS of $2.73 includes roughly $0.20 per quarter of MSR and derivative fair-value marks, and Q2's provision (down 83% year over year) is not a sustainable run-rate for a company that took $124.1 million of charge-offs in FY2025 and $23.0 million in Q1 2026 alone. Stripping the marks and assuming a provision roughly $10 million per quarter above the Q2 level (~$0.17 after tax) yields normalized H1 EPS of approximately $2.15–2.35, annualizing to **$4.30–4.70, or 11.5x–12.6x**, at or slightly above the peer median rather than below it. Both adjustments are sensitivities with stated assumptions, not forecasts; the provision-normalization input rests on an unverified Q2 2025 provision level.

**Book value.** $54.24 ÷ $39.93 TBVPS = **1.36x**. Run-rate ROE on common is roughly 14.8% ($68.0M quarterly to common annualized ÷ ~$1.84 billion common equity). A justified P/TBV of (ROE – g)/(COE – g) at 14.5% ROE and 7% sustainable growth solves to 1.36x at a **12.5% cost of equity**. That is the cleanest statement of where the stock sits: the market is applying roughly a 100–150 basis point cost-of-equity premium to a comparable-ROE bank, which is a defensible price for 0.67% reserves, 31% NPL coverage, 309% CRE concentration, 22% of equity in one delinquent-pool HTM security, and a supervisory MOU that existed as recently as Q1. If credit fully normalizes and that premium compresses to 11.5%, justified P/TBV rises toward 1.65x, the mechanism of the bull case, worth roughly $66–68 on TBV near $40.

**Transaction anchors.** The company repurchased 73,164 shares at an average $41.03 in Q1 2026 and has used only ~$3.0 million of a $100 million authorization since. Insider sales occurred at $44.05 (February), $46.75–46.86 (May) and $55.20 (July 30). At $54.24 the stock is 32% above the company's own buying level five months ago and above every observed transaction except the post-earnings sale. Analyst mean and median targets are $56.50 (range $49–64), implying 4.2% upside, and the recommendation mix moved from two strong-buys and one hold to one strong-buy and two holds.

**Yield.** The dividend was raised to $0.11 quarterly ($0.44 annualized), a **0.81%** yield against a 3.0% peer median and an 8–9% payout ratio. Total return here comes from book compounding, not distributions. That is the correct capital priority at 9.4% CET1, but it removes the yield support that anchors most regional-bank valuations.

Two external references should be discounted. A widely circulated DCF-derived fair value of $137.51 is methodologically inapplicable to a bank, where free cash flow is not an economically interpretable quantity and regulatory capital binds distributions. A cited 11.9x trailing P/E versus a 14x peer average does not reconcile precisely with the reported quarterly EPS series.

**Verdict: fair, with mild positive skew from estimate momentum offset by unresolved reserve and capital questions.** A low multiple on a bank with thin reserves and concentrated collateral is precisely the pattern that produces value traps, and it does not by itself constitute mispricing. Valuation score 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is structural and specific. Warehouse lending requires cheap, stable, short-duration funding; MBIN generates it internally from servicing custodial escrow rather than buying it in the brokered market. Affordable-multifamily agency lending requires scarce FHA/HUD, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac affordable and USDA 538 approvals. MBIN's differentiator is bundling: a sponsor can obtain construction and bridge financing, permanent agency debt, and LIHTC equity from one counterparty, with third-party institutional debt capital available through MIP. Monoline competitors cannot replicate that stack, which is why the fee streams (servicing, syndication, asset management) are durable even when spread income is thin. NIM was 2.92% in Q1, low by community-bank standards because warehouse assets are low-spread, with correspondingly low duration risk.

The trajectory is improving in warehouse (share gains against a growing industry) and stable-to-improving in multifamily fee generation. The flip side is inseparable from the moat: a single large sponsor relationship can span bridge loan, construction loan, permanent agency loan, servicing and syndicated equity. That is exactly the shape of exposure that produced $114.3 million of multifamily charge-offs from five relationships. The synergy that makes the fee stream attractive makes the credit lumpy.

The market-position claims ("one of the largest warehouse lenders in the country," "one of the largest government agency servicers," "consistently one of the top ranked agency affordable lenders") are 10-K self-descriptions rather than independently verified league-table positions.

Policy dependence is the third-party risk to the franchise. HUD compliance violations could produce monetary penalties and FHA program ineligibility; a federal shutdown or HUD staffing reduction is disclosed as potentially materially adverse to FHA origination; and the 10-K explicitly flags the current administration's consideration of ending Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac conservatorship. The entire origination, sale and servicing model runs on agency rails.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Reserve adequacy (highest materiality).** ACL of 0.67% of loans, 31% coverage of $247.5 million of nonaccruals, on collateral-dependent multifamily credits in a market where cap rates have repriced. Regulators periodically review the allowance and the values attributed to nonaccrual loans and OREO and may require adjustment. Moving coverage from 31% to 60% would require roughly $72 million of additional provision, ~$58 million after tax, ~$1.26 per share of book value; survivable, but it would break the current earnings run-rate.

**2. The $394.8 million HTM non-agency multifamily security.** ~22% of common equity, ~36% pool delinquency, Special Mention, senior behind ~17.9% subordination. Carried at amortized cost; only a management impairment determination moves it through earnings. Invisible to any screen.

**3. Concentration in both directions on the same counterparties.** Deposits concentrated in large non-depository mortgage institutions that are simultaneously the largest warehouse borrowers, with a 180-day notice convention and the ability to stop funding as the stated mitigant. Effective per institution; not a hedge against sector-wide non-bank mortgage stress.

**4. Capital.** Holdco CET1 9.4%, –50bp in one quarter, ~93% RWA density, CRE concentration 309% of Tier 1 plus allowance, preferred coupon absorbing ~19% of earnings, and a documented pattern of returning to the equity and preferred markets. Dilutive issuance is a live possibility if warehouse growth continues at recent rates.

**5. Wholesale funding.** $4.36 billion of FHLB advances (~21% of assets) with large tranches maturing within one quarter and embedded FHLB put options. Intentional asset-liability management for a warehouse bank, but single-counterparty dependent.

**6. Governance and disclosure.** No standalone risk factor names the multifamily loss event despite $114.3 million of charge-offs reportedly involving borrower misconduct; the closest is a generic reliance-on-customer-information factor. Neither the entry nor the termination of the FDIC/IDFI MOU generated an 8-K; both were disclosed only in periodic reports. Item 3 in both the FY2025 10-K and Q1 10-Q disclosed no material pending litigation, so the legal characterization, counterparty identities, and recovery prospects on the fraud matter are not established. The company has been subject to continuous supervisory monitoring since 2021, so heightened attention predates and outlasts the MOU.

**7. Compensation design.** Disclosed performance measures are total revenue, EPS and ROE, with ROE company-selected. There is no asset-quality or credit metric among them, at a lender whose worst year in a decade was caused by credit. Average NEO total compensation ($2,893,159) exceeded the CEO's ($2,534,695), a 0.9 ratio consistent with production-linked pay for origination leadership. CEO compensation did fall 14% (–29% on the actually-paid measure) in a year net income fell 32%.

**8. Policy.** GSE conservatorship exit, HUD budget and staffing, and the federal transferable tax-credit regime (worth roughly four points of effective tax rate and a $204.5 million receivable) are all live variables outside management control.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 69.2% sits far above 34.8% one-year realized volatility, with a backwardated term structure (69.2% at 13 days versus 39.2% at 41 days and 36.6% at 104 days), a positive put/call skew reading described by the data source as elevated put demand, and a ±10.3% implied move on the nearest liquid tenor. Implied volatility rose from 70.6% to 91.1% between snapshots as the price advanced 11.0%. Short interest is 6.2% of float at 7.8 days to cover, which is uncrowded.

Two caveats: only 28 contracts across three expirations were observed, so these readings are thin and unreliable in the way small-cap bank option chains usually are. The direction is consistent with an identifiable near-term catalyst rather than noise. The Q2 2026 10-Q has not yet been filed, and it is the document that will disclose the nonaccrual trajectory, the allowance ratio, holdco CET1, and the status of the $394.8 million HTM security. Paying rich near-term volatility ahead of that filing is not attractive; neither is initiating equity exposure into it at the high end of the twelve-month range. This reinforces the fundamental read that waiting for the filing is warranted.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $68 (+25%)

Credit normalization completes. The Q2 10-Q confirms nonaccruals declining alongside the five-quarter criticized-loan trend, and loss severity on resolved credits validates the collateral marks, removing the reserve-adequacy objection. The MOU termination is retrospectively read as examiners with full access closing the file before the market did. Warehouse share gains continue as regional-bank competitors stay retrenched, and the servicing/LIHTC/asset-management fee engine grows without consuming balance sheet. Consensus of $5.99 for next year proves conservative given four straight beats averaging ~20%, and the tax-credit lever holds the effective rate near 20%. Holdco CET1 stabilizes as warehouse balances seasonally normalize, the $97 million buyback resumes, and the cost of equity the market applies compresses from ~12.5% toward 11.5%, taking justified P/TBV to roughly 1.65x on tangible book compounding toward $42–43. That is $68–70, corroborated by the $64 high analyst target and 11.4x on $5.99.

### Bear Case — $39 (–28%)

The 31% NPL coverage proves to be under-reserving. Multifamily appraisals prove optimistic, the provision line moves back toward $30–40 million per quarter, and the $394.8 million HTM security requires a credit-impairment determination as the 36% pool delinquency converts to realized losses that erode the 17.9% subordination. If mortgage origination slows, warehouse balances fall and MBIN's 65% volume growth reverses, removing the fee and spread contribution while capital consumption already forces a choice. Holdco CET1 approaches 8.5% and the company issues equity or preferred at a discount, following the 2017/2024/2019–2024 pattern. Earnings normalize to $4.00, the multiple compresses to 9.75x, and the stock trades to roughly 0.95x tangible book. That is $38–40, near the $49 low analyst target only if credit stabilizes partway.

Probability-weighting base at 50%, bull at 20% and bear at 30% gives an expected return of roughly +2%; shifting to 55/20/25 gives roughly +5%. Either sits inside the band of approximately fair risk-adjusted return, with the added constraint that the single most important verification document, the Q2 10-Q, is unfiled. Price outran verified fundamentals; the Q2 print, the provision decline, the MOU termination and the estimate revisions justify moving the overall score up to 5.2. They do not change the recommendation, because price advanced 11.1% over the same interval and consumed the improvement. This is a HOLD rather than a move in either direction because the confirmed improvement and the confirmed re-rating are approximately equal in magnitude.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month appreciation mechanism is tangible book compounding at roughly 13–14% net of an 8–9% payout, taking TBVPS from $39.93 toward $44–45, with the multiple holding near 1.36x. That path produces roughly $60 without any multiple expansion, and the catalysts are dated.

**Base case: $59 (+8.8% from $54.24).** Approximately 9.85x next-year consensus of $5.99, cross-checked at 1.42x forward tangible book of ~$41.50. This sits between the bear and bull cases above and slightly above the $56.50 analyst mean.

**Bull case: $68 (+25%).** 11.35x $5.99, or 1.6x tangible book of ~$42.50.

**Bear case: $39 (–28%).** 9.75x normalized $4.00, or 0.95x tangible book of ~$41.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints at the Q2 2026 10-Q filing (imminent), Q3 2026 results (late October), and the Q3 10-Q.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Q2/Q3 10-Q shows nonperforming loans declining sequentially *and* ACL/NPL coverage rising above 40%, with charge-off severity on resolved credits at or below carrying value, resolving the reserve-adequacy question in the company's favor.
2. Holdco CET1 stabilizes at or above 9.4% for two consecutive quarters with the $97 million buyback resuming at scale, evidence that warehouse growth is being funded without dilution.
3. A price near $46–48 on unchanged or improving fundamentals, which would restore roughly 25% upside to the base case and put entry near the May insider-transaction level.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any credit-impairment charge on the $394.8 million HTM non-agency multifamily security, or disclosure that its underlying pool delinquency has risen materially above 36% or that subordination has been eroded.
2. Provision returning above $30 million in a quarter, or nonaccruals exceeding 2.5% of loans while the allowance ratio stays below 0.75%.
3. Announcement of a common equity raise, or holdco CET1 falling below 9.0%.
4. Warehouse funded volume declining year over year for two consecutive quarters, which would remove the growth leg while the credit leg is still unresolved.

**Reassessment timeline:** on the Q2 2026 10-Q filing, and again at Q3 earnings in late October 2026.

## Conclusion

Merchants Bancorp has done the hard part. A $124.1 million charge-off year concentrated in five multifamily relationships, an ROE collapse from 16.9% to 9.9%, and a supervisory MOU have given way to five consecutive quarters of declining criticized loans, an MOU terminated by the regulators who imposed it, a provision down 83% year over year, four consecutive earnings beats averaging roughly 20%, record tangible book value of $39.93, and consensus estimates rising 6.5% for the current year. The franchise underneath, custodial-escrow-funded warehouse lending taking share at +65% against a +43% industry plus an integrated agency-debt/bridge/LIHTC-equity platform generating durable fees, is genuinely differentiated and hard to replicate.

What is left to buy is the residual, and it is thin. At $54.24 the shares trade at 10.0x current-year and 9.1x next-year consensus, 11.5–12.6x on a conservatively normalized basis that strips the fair-value marks and normalizes the provision, and 1.36x tangible book, a level that solves to roughly a 12.5% cost of equity on a ~15% ROE. That premium to peer cost of equity is the right price for what remains unresolved: 0.67% reserves covering 31% of $247.5 million of nonaccruals; $394.8 million (22% of common equity) in one held-to-maturity security whose collateral pool is a third delinquent; 309% CRE concentration; 9.4% holdco CET1 falling 50 basis points per quarter with a documented history of issuance; and a compensation plan that measures revenue, EPS and ROE but no credit metric. The company stopped buying its own stock at $41.03 with $97 million of authorization unused, no insider has bought a share since February, and the CEO sold 9,000 shares at $55.20 the day after the Q2 beat. Near-term options are backwardated at 69.2% implied against 34.8% realized ahead of a Q2 10-Q that has not been filed.

**HOLD.** Existing holders are compensated by book value compounding at a mid-teens ROE and should stay through the Q2 and Q3 filings. New capital should wait for the 10-Q to resolve the nonaccrual and coverage trajectory, or for a price near $46–48, before establishing a position.
