## Executive Summary

United States Lime & Minerals is a high-quality asset trading at a full price on earnings that were built by a pricing cycle that has now closed. The economics are real: lime has a low value-to-weight ratio, products ship within roughly a 400-mile radius of the plant, and that freight math converts each facility into a local quasi-monopoly. The company earned a 48.9% gross margin and 42.4% operating margin in FY2025 on $372.7M of revenue, generated $165.0M of operating cash flow, and sits on $402.6M of cash against zero funded debt. Return on invested capital excluding cash is roughly 48-52%.

The problem is the growth arithmetic. The margin expansion from 36.6% (FY2023) to 48.9% (FY2025) was overwhelmingly a pricing event: FY2024 delivered +12.9% revenue growth on negative 1.2% volume, meaning price alone contributed +14.2%. That figure fell to +5.6% in FY2025 and +0.7% in H1 2026. Stripping the disclosed freight pass-through (which the company states approximates the freight embedded in cost of revenues, and is therefore roughly margin-neutral), core H1 2026 revenue grew 0.6% year over year, $160.1M versus $159.2M. Q2 2026 set records in absolute dollars ($99.1M revenue, $46.7M gross profit, $1.20 diluted EPS) and gross margin expanded 130bp to 47.1%, but the underlying franchise is growing at a low-single-digit rate, not the high-teens rate the 2021-2025 record implies. Consensus has caught this: current-year EPS has been cut 7.3% and next-year 7.8% over 90 days, and the company has missed in three of the last four quarters.

At $121.50 investors pay 26.0x trailing EPS of $4.67, 24.1x the current-year estimate of $5.04, 21.3x next-year $5.70, and 16.7x TTM EV/EBITDA of $184.6M, for a 2.6% free-cash-flow yield. The peer median P/E is 27.2x, so USLM is not obviously expensive relative to construction materials, but that comparison flatters a company whose revenue is flat while Vulcan and Martin Marietta carry structural aggregate-pricing stories. Our base case is $126 (+3.7%), bull $156 (+28%), bear $100 (-18%). Probability-weighted, the expected return is roughly 3%. That is a HOLD: own it if you own it, and require either a lower price or evidence resolving the pricing question before adding.

## Company Overview

USLM operates as a single reportable segment producing crushed limestone, pulverized limestone (PLS), aggregate, quicklime, hydrated lime and lime slurry, with 100% of operations and sales in the United States. Facilities are located in Arkansas, Colorado, Louisiana, Missouri, Oklahoma and Texas; products are sold into nine states. Quicklime capacity totals roughly 1.4 million tons across Texas Lime (500,000 tons plus 800,000 tons PLS), Arkansas Lime (650,000 plus 300,000 PLS), St. Clair (250,000 plus 150,000 PLS), with Carthage adding 900,000 tons of crushed/PLS and Mill Creek 300,000 tons of dolomitic PLS. The system ran at approximately 80% of capacity in 2025. A small subsidiary holds royalty and non-operated working interests in Barnett Shale gas wells, which management determined in 2024 does not constitute an operating segment.

End markets span construction contractors, industrial (paper, glass), environmental (municipal water, flue-gas treatment), steel, roof shingle manufacturers, poultry agriculture and oil-and-gas services. The company discloses roughly 675 customers with none above 10% of revenue, served by nine sales employees, and 346 total employees, implying revenue per employee of about $1.08M, exceptional for construction materials and a function of a highly automated, capital-intensive kiln fleet.

Mineral reserves total 223,150 thousand tons (151,647 proven, 71,503 probable), with 156,050 thousand tons of additional measured-and-indicated resources. Reserve lives at the two most important quarries are ~70 years (Texas Lime) and 80+ years (Love Hollow, Arkansas). Batesville Quarry has only ~17 years disclosed and produced 709 thousand tons in 2025, but Love Hollow sits 21 miles away, is rail-connected to the same Arkansas Lime plant, and ramped from 413 to 941 thousand tons in 2025; read together, the Arkansas franchise is not reserve-constrained.

### The freight adjustment

The Q2 10-Q discloses external freight billed to customers and included in revenue of $15.2M in Q2 2026 (vs $12.2M) and $26.9M in H1 2026 (vs $23.6M), and states this "approximates" the freight included in cost of revenues. Removing it:

- Q2 2026 ex-freight revenue: **$83.9M vs $79.3M, +5.8%** (reported +8.3%)
- H1 2026 ex-freight revenue: **$160.1M vs $159.2M, +0.6%** (reported +2.3%)

Roughly 40% of Q2's reported growth and three-quarters of H1's came from zero-margin freight recovery. The same adjustment corrects the cost narrative: the press release cited "higher fuel and transportation costs," and the segment table shows fuel/energy/transportation of $48.9M in H1 2026 vs $44.4M, but ~$3.3M of that $4.5M increase is the freight pass-through. Underlying fuel and energy rose only ~$1.2M on +1.7% volume; in Q2, freight rose ~$3.0M while the total category rose $2.8M, implying underlying kiln energy cost was flat to slightly lower on +6.6% volume. Cost control is better than the headline language suggests, consistent with an all-preheater rotary fleet.

### Quarterly texture

Q1 2026 (derived by subtracting reported Q2 from reported H1) showed revenue of $87.833M versus $91.253M, a 3.7% decline, gross margin down 310bp to 47.5%, and net income down 10.4% to $30.582M. The 10-K pre-flagged both causes: an expansive January 2026 winter storm that interrupted shipments (plants undamaged) and roof shingle customer weakness first seen in Q4 2025. Q2 then rebounded to record absolute levels with margin expanding year over year. The margin structure is holding around 47-48%; the constraint is the top line.

Second, removing the cash does not make the stock cheap: ex-cash price $107.47 against core EPS of ~$4.30 is 25.0x.

At 6/30/2026: cash $402.6M, current assets $500.4M against current liabilities of $18.2M (current ratio 27.5x), net PP&E $241.9M, total assets $747.4M, total liabilities $51.6M, equity $695.7M, **zero funded debt**. Fortress resilience combined with meaningful capital drag supports a score of 7.5 rather than 9.

## Growth Analysis

Three separate growth vectors are worth separating.

**Price.** Effectively exhausted at +0.7% (H1) and +1.7% (Q2). The 10-K's own risk language explains the mechanism: "recent increases in the price of lime have made us more susceptible to new entrants into our markets and/or multi-national competition," and the Q2 10-Q adds a caution about "other changes to the competitive landscape" that I do not find in the 10-K list. Whether the deceleration is industry-wide normalization after a post-inflation catch-up or a competitive response to USLM's own price actions is the central unresolved question, and the filings do not adjudicate it.

**Volume.** Recovering, attributed to construction (explicitly including "some large data centers in the regions we serve"), environmental and steel, partially offset by roof shingle weakness. The company discloses no revenue by end market, so data-center exposure cannot be sized. USLM's role is as a supplier of crushed limestone, aggregate and lime products into site work and soil stabilization, which participates in construction volume without participating in data-center economics. The 10-K itself names "moderation in current areas of active growth, including data center construction" as a risk.

**Capacity.** The Texas Lime vertical kiln, ~$65M total cost, had $57.7M incurred and $56.8M paid through 6/30/2026 with only ~$2.5M of open purchase orders, and management guided that it comes online "this summer." The company has not disclosed the kiln's incremental tonnage, expected incremental revenue, or fuel-efficiency benefit anywhere in the filings. With system utilization at ~80% and pricing growth near zero, added capacity is as likely to pressure regional price as to generate incremental margin, and the 10-K explicitly cautions there is "no assurance that demand and prices... will enable us to fully utilize any additional production capacity." Incremental depreciation of roughly $2.2-3.3M annually is a modest EPS headwind.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration rather than contradicting it: current-quarter $1.48 (−2.6% over 90 days), next-quarter $1.30 (−11.6%), current-year $5.04 (−7.3%), next-year $5.70 (−7.8%). Coverage is one analyst, so the revisions carry limited statistical weight, but the direction aligns with the ex-freight revenue math and with three misses in four quarters (−9.5%, −13.4%, −8.0%, after one +10.3% beat). Growth Prospects scores 3.8.

Peers are dispersed: Eagle Materials 16.9x and CRH 17.7x versus Martin Marietta 35.6x and Vulcan 33.5x. USLM at 26.0x trailing sits mid-pack, which would be defensible if revenue were compounding. It is not: TTM revenue is +1.1% versus FY2025 and core ex-freight revenue is roughly flat. Paying a peer-level multiple for below-peer top-line momentum is the core valuation objection.

The bull argument on valuation is the free-cash-flow inflection. TTM capex of $70.8M includes the kiln; the 10-K guides to approximately $25.0M annually for normal recurring capital and re-equipping thereafter. Substituting maintenance capex into TTM operating cash flow of $162.6M gives ~$137.6M, a 3.95% FCF yield on market cap and 4.5% on EV. That is a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast, and three items must be reconciled against it: TTM operating cash flow includes a $7.5M non-cash deferred-tax add-back (deferred tax liability rose from $23.0M to $30.5M, consistent with permanent 100% bonus depreciation), H1 absorbed ~$18.2M of working-capital build including $13.1M of seasonal receivables, and Q2/Q3 are seasonally the strong quarters. A ~4% normalized FCF yield on a no-growth, unlevered franchise is fair, not cheap.

The bear arithmetic on valuation is the margin sensitivity. Reverting gross margin to FY2023's 36.6% on TTM revenue of $376.9M yields gross profit of $137.9M; less ~$24M SG&A and plus ~$13.6M interest income gives pretax of ~$127.5M, net income near $100M and EPS of about $3.48, **34.9x at today's price**. Investors are paying a full multiple on peak-cycle margins with no valuation support underneath if those margins normalize. I do not treat full reversion as the base case (Q2 margins expanded, underlying energy cost was flat), but it defines the downside geometry.

Analyst input is thin and near current levels: a single contributing analyst with a $132 target (high, low, mean and median all $132), implying 8.6% upside. Older targets in the file, Freedom Capital's $138 (March 2, 2026) and a $140.76 aggregate (March 27, 2026), predate Q1 results and the subsequent decline and should be treated as stale. With estimates falling, a lone analyst target is corroboration at best, not an independent floor.

Valuation scores 4.5: no identified mispricing, a full multiple on peak margins, and an FCF inflection that is mechanical rather than earnings-accretive.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's own competitive disclosure is the best available evidence. The industry is "highly regionalized and competitive," competitors are "predominantly large private companies," and consolidation has left the three largest North American producers with more than two-thirds of capacity. USLM, at roughly 1.4 million tons of quicklime capacity, is a regional participant rather than a price setter. Because Lhoist, Graymont, Carmeuse and Mississippi Lime are private, there is no public comparable against which to benchmark USLM's 48.5% gross margin or its pricing trajectory, a genuine analytical constraint.

Barriers to entry are structural: scarcity of permittable high-quality deposits, required proximity to market with rail/road access, difficulty obtaining clean-air permits for new kilns, and high capital intensity. The 2024 EPA redesignation of the Dallas-Fort Worth nonattainment area (which contains Texas Lime) to "severe" under the 2008 ozone standard and "serious" under the 2015 standard, with attainment dates in 2027, cuts both ways: it raises USLM's own permitting risk at its largest and best-reserved asset while making new stationary-source permits harder for anyone else in the same airshed.

One underappreciated asymmetry: the 10-K states the EPA's July 2024 amended NESHAP/MACT rules impose stringent hazardous-air-pollutant limits requiring additional control equipment on covered lime kilns, but that USLM's current operations are not subject to them because existing emissions fall below the threshold. If durable, that avoids a compliance capex cycle competitors must fund. I have no independent evidence of competitors' NESHAP exposure, so the advantage is inferred from USLM's non-applicability alone.

Against that, trajectory is the scoring criterion, and trajectory is flat to mildly negative: pricing power has evaporated, management identified its own price increases as the entry incentive, flue-gas treatment demand faces secular decline as utilities shift to gas and renewables, roof shingle demand is weak, and a regional Texas competitor (Austin White Lime) changed hands in March 2024. Position level is high, direction is not improving. Competitive Position scores 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin reversion is the dominant risk and it is not priced.** At 26.0x trailing on 48.5% gross margins built by a two-year pricing window that has closed, the multiple embeds durability the disclosure cannot confirm.

**No guidance, no earnings call.** USLM publishes no revenue, EPS, volume or capacity guidance and appears not to hold conference calls; the 8-K record is almost entirely Item 5.07 annual-meeting results. Forward visibility for outside investors is minimal, which mechanically increases the frequency and magnitude of consensus misses, plus an externally reported Q2 revenue shortfall of roughly 8% versus an unconfirmed $108M estimate.

**End-market opacity.** One reportable segment, no revenue split by end market. Low single-customer concentration is established (675 customers, none >10%); end-market diversification is not. Data-center dependency is acknowledged qualitatively by management and unquantified.

**Governance and float.** Inberdon Enterprises holds 17,653,780 shares, 61.58% of the 28,670,056 outstanding as of 3/13/2026, held indirectly by George M. Doumet; cumulative voting is prohibited; directors and officers hold 1.50%. Public float is therefore roughly 10.6-10.7 million shares (~$1.29-1.30B, about 37% of market cap), which matters for liquidity, index weighting and any takeover-premium analysis. Minority holders have no practical mechanism to influence board composition, compensation or capital allocation. Whether USLM was affected by the ~June 2026 S&P SmallCap 600 rebalance is unresolved in the file; in a 10.7M-share float, index flows could be material either way.

**Near-dated operational item.** The Texas Lime collective bargaining agreement, covering the facility with the largest quicklime capacity, expires **November 2026** (Carthage May 2028, Arkansas January 2029). 110 of 346 employees are union-represented.

**Energy and freight are unhedged.** Energy is the primary variable cost (coal, petroleum coke, diesel, natural gas, electricity, freight) and the company states it has not engaged in significant hedging. Mitigation is fuel-mix flexibility and pass-through/surcharges, which worked in H1 2026 but is a mechanism rather than a guarantee.

**Carbon.** CO2 is chemically inherent in calcination. The EPA finalized a February 2026 rule eliminating a substantial portion of the greenhouse-gas reporting program applicable to USLM; it was immediately challenged by state attorneys general and environmental groups with uncertain judicial outcome. Any future carbon tax or cap-and-trade regime is a structural cost that cannot be engineered away.

Offsetting all of this: no funded debt, no covenant or refinancing risk, no customer concentration, no restatement or ICFR issues (unqualified Grant Thornton opinions on FY2025 financials and internal control, no critical audit matters), and no unexpected executive departures. Risk Factors scores 4.5: the balance sheet eliminates the catastrophic tail, but the specific unpriced downside (margin normalization on a 26x multiple, plus a November union expiry and a thin float) is material.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 39.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 40.5% is fair (−1.0% spread) and sits at only the 26th percentile within the current chain, so options are not pricing a crisis. Two features warrant attention: the term structure is in **backwardation** (39.5% at 10 days versus 37.5% at 101 days), and the near-dated put/call skew is +8.4% with a put/call volume ratio of 2.33, elevated near-term put demand that fades by the 38-day tenor (skew +1.9%) and inverts at six months (−2.2%). Put/call open interest of 0.84 is not defensively positioned. Read together, this looks like short-dated hedging around a near-term event rather than a structural bearish position. The nearest expiry prices a ±5.6% move; the 38-day tenor prices ±9.8%. Short interest is 8.0% of float with 2.9 days to cover, not crowded and not squeeze-prone. None of this changes the fundamental read; it is consistent with a market that is uncertain about the next print in a name with no guidance.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~20% probability) — $156

Lime pricing at current levels proves a durable structural reset rather than a cycle peak, because permitting barriers genuinely prevent new kiln capacity from entering. Margins hold at 47-49%, supported by Q2's 130bp year-over-year expansion. The Texas kiln converts to incremental volume rather than regional price pressure, capex steps down from ~$71M toward ~$25-30M, and free cash flow rises from $91.8M toward $135-140M. Data-center and Texas highway construction volume carries 6-8% volume growth for two years, delivering EPS near $6.25 by FY2028. At 25x that is $156, +28% from $121.50. The regulatory asymmetry (NESHAP non-applicability, DFW permitting difficulty for entrants) widens the moat. Optionality: the cash pile funds a rare permitted-asset acquisition at accretive economics, or the board authorizes a repurchase, which the credit agreement already permits.

### Bear Case (~25% probability) — $100

Pricing deceleration is competitive response, not normalization; management said as much in its own risk factors. Volume growth moderates as data-center construction cools and roof shingle weakness persists; the new kiln adds supply into an 80%-utilized system, and price goes flat to negative. Core ex-freight revenue stays near zero for another two years while energy costs eventually rise, compressing gross margin toward the low 40s. EPS stalls near $5.00, and a no-growth, family-controlled small-cap with 37% float re-rates to 20x, $100, −18%. Interest income, currently 7.9% of pretax profit, falls with any Fed easing, adding a second-order EPS headwind. Full reversion to FY2023's 36.6% gross margin would imply ~$3.48 EPS and a considerably worse outcome; I assign that low probability given Q2's margin performance, but it is the tail.

### Base Case (~55%) — $126

Margins hold in the 46-48% range. Volume grows mid-single-digit on construction; price contributes 1-2%. FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $5.70, with kiln depreciation offsetting modest volume gains. Capex normalizes and FCF yield improves toward 4%, which supports but does not expand the multiple. 22x $5.70 = $125.40, call it $126, +3.7%. A cross-check: 16x on ~$190M of forward EBITDA gives an EV of $3,040M, plus ~$430M of year-end cash, or roughly $121 per share; the two methods bracket $121-126.

Probability-weighted: 0.55×$126 + 0.20×$156 + 0.25×$100 = **$124.3, or +2.3%** from $121.50. That is approximately fair, with negative skew if the pricing question resolves adversely.

The disconfirming evidence deserves a fair hearing. Q2 2026 was the best quarter in company history in absolute gross profit and net income, gross margin expanded year over year, underlying energy cost was flat on +6.6% volume, and SG&A declined. If the pricing plateau is a high, durable level rather than a peak, the current multiple is not demanding for a 48%-margin, zero-debt franchise with 70-80 year reserve lives. That argument does not get me to ACCUMULATE because it requires resolving, in the bulls' favor, a question the disclosure explicitly leaves open, while paying 26x for the answer and holding an equity where the controlling family, not the minority, decides how the cash gets used.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Existing holders can maintain exposure; the balance sheet and margin structure do not require action. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $121.50.

**Targets from the $121.50 basis:**
- Base case **$126** (+3.7%) — 22x next-year consensus $5.70; cross-checked at 16x ~$190M forward EBITDA plus cash
- Bull case **$156** (+28.4%) — 25x FY2028 EPS of ~$6.25 on durable pricing and kiln volume conversion
- Bear case **$100** (−17.7%) — 20x stalled EPS of ~$5.00 on flat core revenue and multiple compression
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months. The decisive checkpoints are the Q3 2026 report (late October), the first quarter in which the Texas kiln may contribute, and the FY2026 10-K (late February 2027), which will disclose the full-year price/volume decomposition.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE / BUY if:**
1. Average selling price growth re-accelerates above +4% for two consecutive quarters with gross margin holding at or above 47%, resolving the cyclical-normalization versus competitive-response question in the bulls' favor.
2. The company discloses the Texas kiln's incremental tonnage and it converts to volume growth above 6% without price concession, or capex normalizes to ~$25-30M annualized with free cash flow running above $130M (≈4% yield).
3. Price falls to **$100-105** (roughly 18-21x next-year consensus, ~4.7% normalized FCF yield) with fundamentals intact, restoring a margin of safety adequate for a BUY.

**Downgrade to REDUCE / AVOID if:**
1. Gross margin falls below 45% in any quarter, or average selling prices turn negative year over year, the first hard evidence of competitive entry.
2. Ex-freight core revenue declines year over year for two consecutive quarters (calculate from the disclosed freight-in-revenue figures in each 10-Q).
3. The November 2026 Texas Lime labor agreement lapses into a work stoppage at the largest quicklime facility.
4. Next-year consensus is cut below $5.00 (currently $5.70, already down 7.8% in 90 days) while the multiple stays above 24x.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026, which will provide the first read on kiln contribution, the fourth consecutive quarter of the pricing series, and post-storm-normalized volume.

## Conclusion

USLM is a well-run, structurally advantaged, unlevered franchise whose reported financials are at their best-ever absolute level and whose growth engine has stalled. The multiple prices the record, not the stall. Core revenue excluding pass-through freight grew 0.6% in the first half of 2026; realized price growth fell from +14.2% two years ago to +0.7%; consensus has been cut 7-8% over 90 days; and management's own risk disclosure identifies its prior price increases as an invitation to entrants. Set against that, gross margin expanded in Q2, underlying energy cost was flat, the balance sheet carries $402.6M of cash and no debt, and free cash flow should improve mechanically toward ~$135M as the $65M Texas kiln finishes.

Governance limits what can be done about any of it. A 61.58% control block, a contractually guaranteed 47,500-share annual CEO grant with no performance condition (74% of $7.67M FY2025 compensation), an EBITDA bonus scale set roughly 25% below the prior year's realized result, ~$4.16M of incremental cash taxes over three years from Section 162(m) non-deductibility, no compensation consultant, no open-market insider purchases in the observed window, and no repurchase program despite explicit covenant permission does not point toward the minority-shareholder-friendly capital allocation that would close the value gap. The CEO's September 2025 sales at $119-124 sit essentially at today's price eleven months later.

The probability-weighted expected return of roughly 2% from $121.50, with −18% in the bear case against +28% in the bull, does not clear the bar for adding capital, and the business quality and balance sheet do not warrant selling. **HOLD.** The items to watch are the price/volume line in the next 10-Q and any disclosure of the new kiln's economics; both would move this rating materially in one direction or the other.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Q3 2026 report will NOT be the first quarter with Texas kiln contribution (the kiln comes online "this summer" 2026, so Q3 is plausibly a first partial contributor but the report's "first quarter with the Texas kiln contributing" framing overstates certainty — the brief only supports "expected online this summer"); and the probability-weighted expected return corrects from ~3% to ~2%, marginally weakening the already-thin case for HOLD versus a lower entry point.
