## Executive Summary

WaFd is a $2.4B market cap regional bank headquartered in Seattle, operating 208 branches across nine western states. The bank is in the middle of a strategic transformation: exiting single-family mortgage origination entirely (January 2025) and pivoting toward commercial lending, while integrating the March 2024 Luther Burbank Corporation acquisition. Net interest margin has begun recovering (2.58% FY2025 → 2.70% Q1 FY2026), and management is aggressively returning capital via buybacks (6.5% of float repurchased since October 2024).

The offsetting risk is a sharp deterioration in credit quality. Non-performing assets nearly tripled from $77M to $203M over 15 months, with total delinquencies doubling in a single quarter to 1.07% of loans. Management attributes the increase to two specific commercial relationships, but the broader rise in criticized/classified loans ($329M in "special mention" plus $581M in substandard) suggests a wider stress pipeline. The allowance-to-non-accrual coverage ratio dropped to 104% from 293% a year earlier, which means further reserve builds could be necessary if these trends continue.

At ~10x forward earnings and a 3.4% dividend yield, the stock is reasonably priced for a well-capitalized regional bank with improving margins. But the credit trajectory introduces meaningful earnings uncertainty over the next 2-4 quarters, and falling analyst estimates (-2.6% over 90 days for next year) reflect this concern. Income-oriented hold.

## Company Overview

WaFd Bank was founded in 1917 and operates as a traditional community and commercial bank. Total assets stand at $27.3B as of December 2025. The loan portfolio of $20.0B is dominated by single-family residential ($7.8B, 37% — now in run-off), multi-family ($4.7B, 22%), commercial real estate ($3.6B, 17%), commercial & industrial ($2.5B, 12%), and construction ($1.7B, 8%).

The March 2024 Luther Burbank acquisition added ~$7.7B in assets and 10 California branches through an all-stock transaction (17.1M shares at $27.24). Post-merger, WaFd sold $3B of acquired loans and has been deploying the proceeds into investment securities, which grew from $3.0B to $4.9B over 15 months.

The January 2025 exit from consumer mortgage origination is the largest strategic shift in the bank's recent history. Management estimated $17M in annual expense savings, with the $8B residential portfolio expected to run off over approximately 8 years.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Recovery Underway:** Q1 FY2026 (December 2025) EPS of $0.79 represents a 46% increase over the prior-year quarter ($0.54). Full-year FY2025 net income was $226M ($2.63 EPS), up 13% from FY2024's $200M ($2.50 EPS). The current-year consensus estimate is $3.04 EPS, implying continued improvement.

**NIM Trajectory Is the Core Positive Driver:** NIM bottomed at 2.58% in FY2025 and recovered to 2.70% in Q1 FY2026. The interest rate spread improved sequentially from 1.88% (Dec 2024) to 2.32% (Sep 2025). Weighted average deposit rates fell from 3.09% to 2.42%, while asset yields declined less. This spread expansion is the primary earnings driver and should continue as higher-cost CDs reprice.

**Efficiency Improving:** Non-interest expense declined 4.6% in FY2025 to $427M, reflecting merger cost elimination and the mortgage exit restructuring. Headcount fell from 2,208 to an estimated 1,979. The efficiency ratio improved from 62.1% to 58.9%.

**Capital Position Is Strong:** CET1 of 11.69% (holding company) and 13.44% (bank) are well above regulatory minimums. Equity-to-assets of 11.1% provides a thick buffer. Tangible book value per share is approximately $33.50, meaning the stock trades at ~0.95x TBV — a slight discount.

**Capital Return Is Aggressive:** The company returned 82.5% of FY2025 net income to shareholders. In Q1 FY2026, WaFd repurchased 1.95M shares at an average of $29.75 (~$58M). Since October 2024, approximately 5.3M shares have been retired, reducing the share count by ~6.5%. With 8.16M shares still authorized, the buyback runway extends well into FY2027.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $237M in FY2025 and $60.5M in Q1 FY2026. Loan repayments of $5.1B in FY2025 provide additional liquidity. The cash flow profile supports both the dividend ($1.07/share annualized, ~41% payout ratio) and the aggressive buyback.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are mixed. The positive trajectory in NIM and expense reductions provides near-term EPS momentum, but several factors limit the growth ceiling:

**Loan Portfolio Shrinking:** Total loans declined from $22.3B (post-merger) toward $20.0B as single-family and construction portfolios run off. C&I loans are growing ($2.5B, +5.8% QoQ), but this growth must accelerate materially to offset residential and construction attrition. The bank needs to grow C&I and CRE originations by roughly $1-1.5B annually to maintain flat total loan balances.

**Investment Portfolio Substitution:** The $4.9B securities portfolio (up from $3.0B) generates income but at lower yields than loans. This is a rational deployment of excess cash from LBC loan sales, but it produces lower ROA than loan growth would.

**New Revenue Streams Are Small:** WaFd Wealth and Pike Street Labs (technology subsidiary) were both launched in FY2025. Insurance brokerage revenue was $3.3M in FY2025. These are marginal contributors today and won't materially impact earnings for several years.

**Estimate Revisions Are Negative:** Current-year EPS estimates declined 0.5% over 30 days and 0.7% over 90 days. Next-year estimates fell 2.6% over 90 days. This is a negative momentum signal.

**Earnings Surprise Record:** 3 beats in 4 quarters, with the most recent quarter (Dec 2025) beating by 2.9%. The one miss (Sep 2025, -8.4%) broke an improving trend and coincided with the credit quality deterioration. The mixed record provides moderate confidence in forward estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

WAFD trades at 11.0x trailing earnings and ~9.8x forward earnings (based on $3.04 consensus). This is below the broader regional bank average of ~11-12x forward. Key valuation metrics:

- **P/TBV:** ~0.95x — a slight discount to tangible book, which is unusual for a bank with ROE in the 8-9% range
- **Dividend Yield:** 3.4% — attractive and well-covered at 41% payout
- **Analyst Targets:** Low $30.50, high $34.00, median $33.00 — all four analysts rate HOLD, suggesting limited upside from current $31.81

The forward multiple of ~10x appropriately reflects both the NIM recovery story and the credit quality risk. If NPAs stabilize, the stock could rerate to 11-12x forward (suggesting $33-36 fair value). If NPAs continue rising and reserves need bolstering, earnings would be reduced and the multiple could compress to 8-9x ($24-27).

At 0.95x TBV, the stock has limited downside to book value unless actual loan losses materialize. The preferred peer comparison (Klarna, at -36x P/E) is not relevant given the completely different business model; WAFD should be benchmarked against peers like PACW, COLB, BANR, and other Pacific Northwest/western regionals trading at 10-13x forward.

## Competitive Landscape

WaFd operates in a highly competitive multi-state banking market. Key competitive considerations:

**Construction Lending Niche:** 40+ years of Pacific Northwest construction lending expertise gives the bank differentiation in this category. However, construction loans are declining ($1.7B from $3.3B in FY2023) as the cycle matures and the bank manages down exposure.

**Luther Burbank Integration Challenges:** California deposit attrition of 16.2% YoY ($4.4B → $3.7B) suggests the former LBC franchise is leaking. WaFd has no prior operating history in California's competitive market.

**Commercial Lending Pivot:** The shift toward C&I and SBA lending puts WaFd in direct competition with well-established commercial banks. Building a commercial lending franchise takes years, and the bank's commercial team is being assembled mid-cycle.

**Technology Investment:** IT spending of $60M (+12.8% YoY) and Pike Street Labs reflect increased technology spending to compete with digital-native alternatives.

**Deposit Franchise:** Transaction accounts grew to 60% of deposits (from 55%), improving stickiness and cost. Uninsured deposits of $5.3B (25%) are moderate but warrant monitoring in a regional bank stress scenario.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit Quality (High Risk):** The primary concern. NPAs rose from 0.28% to 0.75% of assets in 15 months. Non-accrual loans nearly tripled to $191M. C&I non-accruals surged from $11M to $58M in a single quarter. The allowance coverage ratio (ACL/non-accrual) dropped to 104% from 293%. Management attributes the increase to two specific relationships with adequate collateral, but the classified/criticized pipeline ($329M special mention + $581M substandard) suggests further NPA formation is probable. If even 10% of the $392M in substandard-but-performing loans migrate to non-accrual, reserves would need significant bolstering, reducing EPS by an estimated $0.15-0.25.

**CRE Concentration (Moderate Risk):** Office CRE exposure of $803M is the most exposed category. Non-owner occupied CRE represents $2.99B (83% of the CRE portfolio). In the current rate and occupancy environment, CRE remains a source of potential loss content.

**Business Model Transition Risk (Moderate Risk):** The mortgage exit eliminates a ~$8B asset class that will take 8 years to run off. If commercial loan growth disappoints, total earning assets could shrink, pressuring revenue. The transition requires building capabilities in commercial and SBA lending where WaFd is a relative newcomer.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity (Low-Moderate Risk):** A -200bp shock would increase NII by $68M (+9%), making WaFd a beneficiary of rate cuts. However, the current rate regime (hold-with-hawkish-optionality) suggests cuts are unlikely in 2026. The large fixed-rate residential portfolio creates duration risk in a rising-rate scenario, though management has $3.6B in hedges mitigating this.

**Geographic Concentration (Moderate Risk):** All 208 branches are in nine western states. Washington (27.5% of exposure), California (14%), and Texas (12%) represent the largest concentrations. Economic deterioration in any of these markets would disproportionately impact the bank.

**Macro/Tariff Risk:** The 10-K explicitly flags trade policy uncertainty and tariffs as risks to commercial borrowers. In the current stagflation environment with oil at ~$107, commercial borrowers in energy-importing western states face margin pressure.

**Short Interest:** 5.1% of float with 4.8 days to cover. Moderately elevated but not extreme. Consistent with market skepticism about credit quality rather than a structural short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- NIM continues expanding as deposit costs reprice lower, driving EPS toward $3.20-3.40 in FY2027
- The two large NPA relationships resolve with minimal loss content, validating management's collateral sufficiency claims
- Aggressive buyback continues, reducing share count by another 5-7% annually
- Commercial loan growth accelerates to $500M+ annually, offsetting residential run-off
- CRA appeal success removes M&A constraint, enabling opportunistic acquisitions
- At 0.95x TBV, limited downside with 15-20% upside to $34-36 on rerating

### Bear Case
- Credit quality continues deteriorating: the $329M in special mention loans and $581M substandard migrate toward non-accrual, requiring $50-75M in additional provisions over the next 2-3 quarters
- NIM expansion stalls if rates hold higher for longer (asset repricing catches up to deposit repricing)
- California franchise continues leaking deposits, generating losses on the LBC transaction
- Commercial loan growth disappoints as economic conditions weaken in western states
- Stock de-rates to 8x forward earnings ($24-25) on sustained credit concerns

## Conclusion

WaFd is a well-capitalized regional bank with an improving NIM trajectory and aggressive capital return program. The stock trades at a reasonable 10x forward earnings and a slight discount to tangible book value, offering a 3.4% dividend yield that is well-covered by cash flow.

The critical uncertainty is credit quality. Non-performing assets have tripled in 15 months, and the classified loan pipeline suggests further deterioration is possible. Until the NPA trajectory stabilizes — showing a flattening or decline over the next 1-2 quarters — the credit risk overhangs earnings and prevents a more constructive recommendation.

The CEO's open-market purchase of preferred shares is a mildly positive signal, and the successful CRA appeal removes a strategic constraint. But negative estimate revisions, rising NPAs, and a multi-year business model transformation warrant a HOLD. The income case (3.4% yield + buyback accretion) is the primary reason to own the stock at current levels while credit quality clarity emerges.

**Monitoring triggers for upgrade:** (1) NPAs flatten or decline for two consecutive quarters; (2) Q2 FY2026 charge-offs below 10bps; (3) C&I loan growth exceeds $300M in next 2 quarters; (4) California deposit attrition slows to <5% annualized.

**Monitoring triggers for downgrade:** (1) NPAs exceed 1.0% of assets; (2) Net charge-offs exceed 25bps in any quarter; (3) ACL/non-accrual falls below 90%; (4) Management suspends or reduces buyback.
