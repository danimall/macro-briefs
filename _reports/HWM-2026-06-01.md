**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Howmet Aerospace has delivered one of the most convincing profitability transformations in the aerospace supply chain. Since separating from Arconic in April 2020, the company has converted a commodity-tinged metals business into a structurally high-margin engineered-components franchise. Engine Products segment EBITDA margins moved from 27.2% in 2023 to 33.3% in FY25 to 36.6% in Q1'26, Fastening Systems went from 20.6% to 30.4%, and the consolidated operating margin reached 24.8% in 2025 and 32.6% in Q1'26 (the latter flattered by a $93M divestiture gain). Operating leverage is compounding on top of pricing power that runs ahead of inflation pass-through, plus a favorable mix shift toward high-margin engine aftermarket spares. The trajectory is corroborated by three credit-rating upgrades in 18 months (Fitch to A- in February 2026, S&P to BBB+, Moody's to Baa1) and accelerating free cash flow.

The forward fundamentals remain strong. Q1'26 revenue grew 19.1% to $2,313M, management raised the FY26 revenue outlook to roughly $9.58B, and consensus EPS estimates have risen +9.7% over 90 days for both the current and next year. The company beats consistently (four straight quarters, most recently by 9.1%). End markets are layered: commercial aerospace recovery as Boeing 737 MAX rates ramp (FAA lifted the production-rate cap in October 2025), high-margin recurring engine spares, defense aerospace, and a newer industrial-gas-turbine leg tied to data-center power demand.

The constraint is price. At $256.94, HWM trades at roughly 51x current-year consensus EPS ($5.04) and 43x next-year ($6.02), against an aerospace/defense peer set that prices GD at 21.8x, LMT at 25.7x, NOC at 17.6x, and even TransDigm (the highest-quality comparable on margin profile) at 39.3x. The multiple embeds continued execution at the recent pace with little margin for disappointment. The fundamental thesis is intact and arguably strengthening, but the entry point does not offer the asymmetric upside that distinguishes a high-conviction buy from a high-quality company at a full price. The recommendation is HOLD with a growth lean, and the bar for upgrading is a pullback or an estimate-revision acceleration that the multiple has not already discounted.

## Company Overview

Howmet manufactures advanced engineered metal components across four product-organized segments:

- **Engine Products (52% of FY25 sales, $4,320M):** Investment-cast airfoils, seamless rolled rings, and forged disks for jet engines and industrial gas turbines. FY25 segment adjusted EBITDA margin 33.3%, up from 27.2% in 2023. Management claims capability to produce >90% of structural and rotating aero-engine components.
- **Fastening Systems (21%, $1,745M):** Aerospace and industrial fasteners under the Huck brand. FY25 margin 30.4%, up sharply from 20.6% in 2023.
- **Engineered Structures (14%, $1,148M):** Titanium ingot/mill products, forgings, and extrusions for airframes and defense. FY25 margin 21.2%.
- **Forged Wheels (13%, $1,039M):** Forged aluminum truck wheels under the Alcoa Wheels brand. Margin 28.5%; the only cyclically soft segment given the commercial-transportation downturn.

End-market mix in FY25 was Aerospace-Commercial 53%, Aerospace-Defense 17%, Commercial Transportation 15%, Gas Turbines 11%, Other 4% — roughly 70% aerospace-levered. North America accounts for 72% of sales, Europe 22%. The competitive moat rests on materials science, multi-year OEM qualification barriers, ~1,020 patents, and entrenched sole- or multi-source positions. The principal competitor is Precision Castparts, owned by Berkshire Hathaway.

The mechanism behind the improvement is clear. Revenue grew 11% in 2025 while segment adjusted EBITDA grew 25% and net income 31%. COGS as a percentage of sales fell from 71.9% (2023) to 65.8% (2025) to 63.1% in Q1'26. Pricing increases consistently run ahead of material and inflation pass-through, and the rising mix of high-margin engine spares (recurring aftermarket revenue as the installed fleet flies more hours) reinforces the trend.

Cash generation is strong and improving: operating cash flow moved from $901M (2023) to $1,298M (2024) to $1,884M (2025), with Q1'26 OCF of $453M versus $253M a year earlier. Capex is disciplined at roughly 5% of sales ($453M in 2025), weighted toward Engine Products capacity. ROE was 28.2% and ROA 13.5% in FY25.

The balance sheet supports the growth profile. Total debt fell to $3,050M at YE2025 (a $265M net reduction, including early redemption of 5.9% 2027 notes), though it rose to roughly $4,686M at Q1'26 after financing the $1.8B CAM acquisition with March 2026 notes plus commercial paper, with cash building to $2,435M ahead of the April close. The Altman Z of 2.78 sits in the grey zone but understates resilience: it is depressed by ~$4.0B of legacy-conglomerate goodwill inflating the asset base. The three rating upgrades, conservative coverage, and an investment-grade A-/BBB+ profile are the more relevant signals.

Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly: $700M of buybacks in 2025 (4.4M shares) plus $300M in Q1'26, a dividend raise to $0.12/quarter, full redemption of the Class A preferred in December 2025, and ~$1,047M of buyback authorization remaining. Share count has declined from 416M (2022) to 401M (Q1'26). The dividend yield is negligible (~0.19%); this is a capital-appreciation and buyback story, not an income vehicle.

## Growth Analysis

Several growth vectors operate simultaneously. Commercial aerospace is recovering as Boeing 737 MAX production-rate increases were approved by the FAA in October 2025; the 10-K names Boeing directly as a swing factor with material impact, making the ramp a discrete, partly-visible catalyst already showing in the Q1'26 acceleration. Engine aftermarket spares grow with fleet utilization and carry the highest margins. Defense aerospace spending provides a stable floor. A newer leg is industrial gas turbines tied to data-center electricity demand, which management has emphasized via the Q4'25 segment reclassification.

The estimate-revision picture is firmly positive: current-year consensus EPS rose +8.1% over 30 days and +9.7% over 90 days to $5.04; next-year rose +7.1%/+9.7% to $6.02. The earnings-surprise record shows four consecutive beats (9.1%, 7.7%, 3.8%, 3.3%), with the beat magnitude accelerating.

The two acquisitions — CAM ($1.8B, closed April 6, 2026) and Brunner (~$120M, February 2026) — both land in Fastening Systems, the most-improved-margin segment, and increase aircraft and defense program exposure. The simultaneous sale of the Savannah disk-forging plant ($230M, ~$93M gain, March 2026) signals deliberate portfolio reshaping toward the highest-margin aero/fastener exposure, accepting lower Engineered Structures revenue in 2026 to maximize profitability. This is unusual discipline during a demand upcycle and supports the durability of the margin trajectory.

The +9-10% revision velocity combined with ~19% revenue growth is a genuinely strong forward signal. The reason it does not translate to a BUY is the valuation guardrail discussed below: the multiple already prices in continued beats. The signal is most powerful when a stock is cheap relative to rising estimates; here the estimates are rising into an already-premium multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the case stalls. Against the aerospace/defense peer set:

| Ticker | P/E | Notes |
|--------|-----|-------|
| NOC | 17.6 | Defense prime |
| GD | 21.8 | Defense prime |
| LMT | 25.7 | Defense prime |
| TransDigm | 39.3 | Closest margin/quality comparable |
| **HWM** | **~51 (CY) / ~43 (NY)** | Premium to all |

The peer median P/E is 25.7. HWM trades at roughly 2x that on a trailing basis and ~1.7x on a forward basis. The most relevant comparable is TransDigm, which shares the high-margin, qualification-gated aftermarket profile; HWM trades above even TDG's multiple. The premium reflects genuine fundamental superiority — accelerating margins, A- credit, double-digit revenue growth — but it does not leave asymmetric upside.

Applying the cycle-adjustment principle correctly: HWM is not a trough-earnings story trading at an artificially high trailing multiple. Earnings are at or near a cyclical and structural high, with margins already at record levels (Engine Products 36.6%). On forward EPS, the stock is at 43x — a demanding multiple even adjusted for ~15-17% earnings growth, implying a PEG near 2.5-2.8. The estimate-revision and prior-appreciation guardrails do not rescue the case here: prior appreciation is not the reason for caution, and analyst targets (mean $303, median $301.50, high $340) are not being treated as a ceiling — they imply ~18% upside to mean. But at >40x forward P/E, analyst targets reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation. The 2025-2026 derating of premium-multiple growth names demonstrated that correct sector themes do not protect 40x+ stocks from sharp drawdowns on any disappointment.

A reasonable base case applies ~46x to FY27 EPS of ~$6.02 plus modest accretion, yielding roughly $277 — about 8% above current. The asymmetry is the problem: ~8-18% to base/consensus versus a bear case (de-rate to high-20s/low-30s multiple on a tariff or Boeing stumble) that implies 25-30% downside. That risk/reward does not clear the bar for a BUY, which requires limited downside and substantial upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Howmet occupies an oligopolistic, qualification-gated niche with high switching costs. Components require multi-year OEM certification, creating durable incumbency. The competitive trajectory is improving: the company is gaining margin and consciously reshaping its portfolio toward the highest-value segments, and segment margin expansion across Engine Products and Fastening Systems outpaces what a share-losing or moat-compressing business would show.

The structural tailwinds are multi-engine and broad-based: commercial aero recovery (Boeing/Airbus rate ramps), recurring engine spares, defense spending, and industrial gas turbines for power demand. The main threats are Precision Castparts (Berkshire's deep-pocketed competitor), the latent in-sourcing capability of large customers (GE and RTX operate captive superalloy furnaces and could in-source), and rising low-cost Asian competition in Forged Wheels. On balance the competitive position is strong and improving, supporting a score in the high-7s, but customer concentration caps it short of a top-tier moat rating.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the second factor restraining the score. Specific, company-level risks not fully priced at a 43x forward multiple include:

1. **Customer concentration.** GE Aerospace and RTX were each ~11% of FY25 sales, with GE rising to 14% in Q1'26 and RTX at 10%. Loss or reduction from either would be material, and both are vertically capable of in-sourcing.
2. **Boeing dependency.** The 10-K explicitly names Boeing's MAX production rates as having a material impact. This is the largest upside swing factor but also a downside one if rates stall again.
3. **Tariffs.** Heavily emphasized in both filings. Pass-through works but with margin-timing lags; this is the most likely source of a near-term negative surprise in an otherwise pristine setup.
4. **Raw-material concentration.** Titanium sponge and specialized alloys from limited/sole-source suppliers; Russia-Ukraine titanium volatility; reliance on customer return of titanium revert scrap.
5. **CAM integration.** A newly added risk factor; $1.8B deal with integration, financing-cost, and synergy-realization risk. Leverage rose modestly (covenant allows 4.25x post-material-acquisition), though ~$1.9B OCF makes the financing comfortable.
6. **Legacy/separation liabilities.** Grenfell Tower litigation (indemnified by Arconic, trial expected 2028-29) and a ~$1.1B contingent guarantee on an Alcoa energy agreement (viewed as remote).

The Q1'26 GAAP EPS of $1.44 is flattered by the $93M Savannah gain; adjusted EPS of ~$0.86 is the cleaner figure and still beat consensus handily. Investors extrapolating the headline GAAP number overstate the run-rate.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 44.1% against 30.3% one-year historical volatility — rich by 13.8 points, placing IV at the 63rd percentile within the current chain. The term structure is flat (near 44.1% ≈ far 42.7%), with uncertainty evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a single near-term event. The put/call skew is call-heavy (-9.3% at the front, near-neutral further out), and the put/call OI ratio of 0.94 is balanced. The recent shift shows IV easing (47.5% to 44.1%) on a roughly flat price with the P/C OI ratio rising from 0.59 to 0.94. The read: the options market is not pricing acute downside fear, but the elevated IV signals the market expects continued large moves, consistent with a premium-multiple stock where beats are expected and any disappointment is punished. The call-heavy front-end skew leans modestly bullish and does not contradict the fundamental thesis, but the rich IV reinforces that this is an expensive setup where the asymmetry has been removed.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$300-340, +17% to +32%):** Margin expansion continues as Boeing MAX rates ramp, engine spares mix improves, and CAM accretes into the highest-margin segment. FY27 EPS pushes toward $6.50-7.00 on continued operating leverage, and the multiple holds at ~45-48x as estimate revisions keep rising. Industrial-gas-turbine demand from data centers adds an under-appreciated growth leg. This matches the high analyst target of $340 and Citi's $303.

**Base Case (~$277, +8%):** Growth and margins continue at a decelerating but healthy pace; FY27 EPS ~$6.02-6.50; the multiple compresses modestly toward 44-46x as the law of large numbers slows growth. Returns track earnings growth plus buybacks, roughly mid-single to high-single digits.

**Bear Case (~$185-195, -24% to -28%):** A tariff-driven margin-timing shock, a Boeing rate stall, or a broad market de-rating of premium-multiple growth names compresses HWM toward a high-20s/low-30s multiple on ~$6 EPS. Fundamentals need not deteriorate severely; multiple compression alone drives the downside, as the 2025-2026 derating of premium-multiple names showed for richly valued names.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean), 12-month horizon.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$280s to $270 floor, or below, that restores asymmetry (forward P/E toward ~38-40x against rising estimates).
- Resumption of double-digit upward EPS revisions for FY27 that meaningfully outpaces the current ~$6.02 consensus, lowering the effective forward multiple while the price lags.
- Confirmed Boeing MAX rate acceleration translating into a guidance raise beyond the current ~$9.58B FY26 outlook.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A tariff or raw-material shock that compresses segment margins for two consecutive quarters (Engine Products EBITDA margin falling below ~32%).
- Loss or material reduction of GE or RTX volume, or evidence of customer in-sourcing.
- Estimate revisions turning negative while the multiple stays above 45x — the asymmetric-downside setup for premium names.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2'26, early August 2026), which will show the first full quarter of CAM integration and the Boeing-rate trajectory. The CEO-succession question (Plant is 72, with a recently installed CFO) is a standing governance item to monitor.

## Conclusion

Howmet is a genuinely high-quality business executing a margin transformation that is still accelerating, with strong free cash flow, three credit upgrades, disciplined capital allocation, and broad-based structural tailwinds. The fundamental scorecard is excellent — financial health, growth, and competitive position all rate in the high-7s to low-8s. The recommendation is constrained entirely by valuation and the resulting asymmetry.

Addressing the prior validation concern directly: the specific, falsifiable reason this is not a BUY is the forward multiple. At ~43x next-year EPS (PEG ~2.5-2.8 on ~15-17% growth), the stock trades at a premium to every aerospace/defense peer including TransDigm, the closest quality comparable. The base-case upside to ~$277 is ~8% and the consensus-mean target ($303) implies ~18%, while a credible bear case — driven by tariff margin-timing risk and/or a premium-multiple de-rating, both concrete and monitorable — implies 24-30% downside. That reward/risk is below the 2:1, limited-downside profile a BUY requires, and the estimate-revision BUY signal is materially weakened (per the explicit premium-multiple guardrail) because at >40x forward, rising estimates are already discounted and beats no longer drive re-rating while misses are punished. The prior BUY-to-HOLD shift reflects price catching up to fundamentals, not fundamentals weakening. I would become a buyer on a pullback toward the low-$280s/$270 or on a fresh leg of double-digit FY27 estimate revisions that the price has not yet absorbed.
