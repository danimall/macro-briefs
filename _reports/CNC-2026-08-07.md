## Executive Summary

Centene is roughly twelve months into repairing a genuine catastrophe. FY2025 produced a $6,674M net loss, a $6,723M goodwill write-off, adjusted EPS collapsing from $7.17 to $2.08, and a health benefits ratio (HBR) that blew out 360 basis points to 91.9%. The repair is measurable: 1H 2026 adjusted diluted EPS of $5.88 against $2.75 in 1H 2025, HBR down to 88.4%, debt-to-capital cut from 46.5% to 41.6% against a 0.60 covenant, and four consecutive earnings beats including a 129% surprise in Q2. Consensus FY2027 EPS has risen 17.6% over 90 days, from $4.46 to $5.25, while the stock is flat. On a mechanical basis, that compresses the forward multiple and improves risk/reward.

The complication is that the market already re-rated the equity from roughly 0.77x book in March to 1.44x book today, and three specifics govern what remains. First, the Q2 headline overstates the run rate: on June 30, 2026 CMS published final 2025-benefit-year risk-adjustment transfers, reducing Centene's net payable by $541M and producing a $481M net pre-tax benefit. That is approximately $0.71 per share after tax, and it is *not* excluded from adjusted EPS; Q2's operating run rate is closer to $1.80 than $2.51. Second, the company's own FY2026 guidance of ">$4.80" against 1H actual of $5.88 implies second-half adjusted EPS of ≤$(1.08), and the $315–365M voluntary separation charge cannot explain it because severance is excluded from adjusted EPS. Consensus now models this shape precisely: Q3 at $0.10 and Q4 at $(1.09) sum to $(0.99), tying to the $4.89 full-year figure. Third, Medicaid is 57% of revenue at a 5.2% FY2025 gross margin, and RBC's post-print view is that Medicaid margins may not meaningfully recover until H2 2027.

At $65.875 the stock trades at 13.5x FY2026 consensus of $4.89, 12.5x FY2027 consensus of $5.25, 1.44x book value of $45.67, and 4.33x tangible book of $15.23. That is roughly in line with the cheapest large-cap peer (Elevance at 17.0x trailing) rather than at a discount, and it sits 5.0% below the 52-week high and 8.1% below the $71.67 analyst mean. Our base case of $73 offers +10.8%; the bear case of $45 implies -31.7%. That asymmetry, combined with a dense stack of unquantified regulatory binaries in a business with essentially no pricing power, argues for holding existing exposure rather than adding. We maintain HOLD with the overall score ticking to 4.85 from 4.8.

## Company Overview

Centene is the largest US managed care organization by member count (25,885,400 at-risk members at 6/30/2026, down 8% from 28,004,900 a year earlier) and is effectively a pure-play government-sponsored healthcare contractor. FY2025 revenue of $194,777M (including $20,196M of premium tax) breaks into four segments:

| Segment | FY2025 revenue | % of total | Gross margin | GM % | YoY GM change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medicaid | $110,434M | 57% | $5,690M | 5.2% | −9% |
| Medicare (MA + PDP) | $37,210M | 19% | $2,983M | 8.0% | +15% |
| Commercial | $42,003M | 22% | $5,101M | 12.1% | −33% |
| Other | $5,130M | 3% | $435M | 8.5% | −23% |

Medicaid is a bid-and-defend business: state agencies award contracts by competitive procurement, typically for 3–6 years, and state actuaries reprice annually or semi-annually. Revenue is contractual and recurring but the entire book is periodically re-tendered. Within Medicare, standalone Part D (8,803,000 members, up from 7,845,800) has grown far faster than Medicare Advantage (980,000), which means the segment's 62% FY2025 revenue growth was overwhelmingly a low-margin, benchmark-driven commodity book rather than a quality-rated one. Commercial is predominantly ACA Marketplace, where membership fell from 5,541,400 at 12/31/2025 to 3,494,700 at 6/30/2026, a 37% decline in six months and 40% year-over-year, following expiry of enhanced advance premium tax credits.

Effectively all revenue derives from a government or government-subsidized payer. Medicaid rates are set by state actuaries, Medicare Advantage rates by CMS, Part D benchmarks by CMS, and Marketplace economics by the ACA risk-adjustment mechanism plus federal subsidies. Centene has bidding power and cost-management capability, not pricing power. When the rules change mid-plan-year, as they did comprehensively in 2025, there is limited defensive room. The revenue mix shift toward Medicare is concentration into standalone Part D, whose economics CMS reset when the Inflation Reduction Act cut federal catastrophic-phase reinsurance from 80% to 20%. It is a mix change, not payer diversification.

## Financial Analysis

### The 2025 damage and what it did to the balance sheet

Premium and service revenue grew 20.0% in FY2025 to $174,581M. Growth was not the problem; medical costs grew 25% against that. The 360bp HBR deterioration destroyed roughly $6.2B of gross margin on approximately $171.6B of premium revenue. Loss from operations was $(7,623)M against +$3,175M in FY2024, partially cushioned by SG&A discipline (ratio down to 7.4% from 8.5%).

Three shocks converged: a Marketplace risk-adjustment reversal after Wakely industry data showed higher aggregate market morbidity than assumed (ACA risk adjustment is a zero-sum transfer calibrated to *relative* morbidity, so a healthier-than-market book becomes a large net payer); a Medicaid acuity/rate mismatch driven by behavioral health, home health, high-cost drugs and applied behavior analysis utilization; and Medicare Advantage pricing inadequacy that required a premium deficiency reserve peaking at $389M in Q2'25 before being released in Q4'25 with none established for 2026.

The $6,723M goodwill write-off (goodwill fell from $17,558M to $10,835M, or $13.63 per share) plus $513M on Magellan held-for-sale and $75M of other items produced $7,311M of total impairment. Because goodwill is non-deductible, the effective tax rate was 0.8%. The write-off is non-cash and touches neither statutory capital nor covenants: FY2025 operating cash flow was +$5,088M and free cash flow $4,321M after $767M of capex, generated in the year of a $6.7B reported loss.

Statutory measures are the meaningful solvency test for an insurer, and they are comfortable: statutory capital and surplus of $19.7B against a required minimum of $11.3B, with risk-based capital above 350% of the Authorized Control Level. FY2025-based screens showing an Altman Z in the grey zone are mechanically distorted by the impairment year and by a coefficient set calibrated on manufacturers; they should not drive the assessment either way.

### The Q2 2026 recovery, decomposed

| Metric | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | 1H 2026 | 1H 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $53,579M | ~$48,700M | $103,523M | — |
| Earnings from operations | $1,198M | $(458)M | — | — |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $2.19 | $(0.51) | $5.30 | — |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $2.51 | $(0.16) | $5.88 | $2.75 |
| HBR | 89.6% | 93.0% | 88.4% | 90.2% |
| Adjusted SG&A ratio | 6.9% | ~7.1% | 7.3% | 7.5% |

Total segment gross margin rose 53% to $4,617M, with Commercial up 106% to $1,947M. But the $481M net pre-tax benefit from CMS's June 30 publication of final 2025-benefit-year transfers is predominantly a Commercial item. Adjusting: $481M × (1 − 0.265 adjusted ETR) = $353.5M, divided by 497,637k diluted shares = approximately $0.71 per share, or 28% of reported adjusted EPS. Ex-item, Q2 adjusted EPS is roughly $1.80 and Commercial gross margin growth is +55% rather than +106%. Adjusted EPS excludes intangible amortization, acquisition costs, enterprise optimization, severance, debt-extinguishment gains and real estate gains; it does not exclude prior-year risk-adjustment true-ups.

A counter-check supports the recovery's quality. Prior-year favorable reserve development was $(1,778)M in 1H 2026 versus $(1,747)M in 1H 2025, nearly identical in dollars and *lower* as a share of opening net reserves (8.7% versus 9.5%). Since 1H 2025 delivered only $2.75 of adjusted EPS on the same dollar development, the $3.13 of year-over-year improvement comes from current-period drivers: Marketplace corrective pricing filed in states covering ~95% of Marketplace membership, the current-year risk-transfer estimate, Medicaid rate updates and cost management. That is a genuine quality-of-earnings positive.

The offsetting observation is the *level* of favorable development: $(1,778)M against 1H pre-tax earnings of roughly $3,581M is about half of pre-tax income, and the multi-year pattern (12.6% of opening reserves in FY2025, with similar magnitudes in FY2024 and FY2023) means reported earnings are persistently supported by prior-period estimate revisions. A consistently conservative reserving culture is the most natural reading, and it is supported by the pattern holding in 2025 when smoothing would have been most useful and did not prevent the miss. Earnings smoothing cannot be excluded from public disclosure alone. Days in claims payable fell from 54 (2023) to 46 (2025) but stabilized at approximately 47.2 days at 6/30/2026, consistent with Part D mix shift (pharmacy claims adjudicate far faster than medical) rather than continuing reserve erosion.

### Cash flow: do not annualize the first half

Reported 1H 2026 operating cash flow was $7,956M against $3,295M. Roughly $4,500M of state pass-through payments sat in accounts payable at 6/30/2026 versus $1,182M at 12/31/2025, a +$3,318M build the company says reverses primarily in Q3. Normalizing: $7,956M − $3,318M = approximately $4,638M, less $374M capex, gives roughly $4.3B of normalized 1H free cash flow. That figure also benefits from a $729M year-over-year tax swing (net refunds of $225M versus payments of $504M in 1H 2025). The correct comparison is FY2025's full-year $4,321M of FCF, not $15B annualized. As a labeled floor sensitivity only: if 2H generated zero incremental normalized operating cash flow, FY2026 FCF would still be ~$4.3B, a 13.2% yield on the $32.5B cap.

That yield does not translate into shareholder returns at anything close to that rate, because the cash sits inside regulated subsidiaries. Consolidated cash of $24,151M looks abundant; unregulated cash and investments are $1.8B, of which $715M is available for general corporate use (up from $400M at 12/31/2025), with only ~$600M of net subsidiary dividends expected in the remainder of 2026 against ~$400M of additional capex. Parent-level liquidity, not consolidated cash, is the binding constraint on buybacks and M&A.

Deleveraging is genuine and well-sequenced. Total debt fell from $17,351M to $16,105M; senior notes par from $15,490M to $14,204M, with 2027 maturities cut from $2,211M to $1,067M via $1,289M par of repurchases for $1,282M. Debt-to-capital improved from 46.5% to 41.6% against a 0.60 covenant maximum. The $4.0B revolver (March 2030) is undrawn. A February 2026 master receivable purchase agreement permits up to $4,250M of Part D CMS receivable sales; $1,000M was sold in March for $970M net (a 3.0% discount recorded as a $30M SG&A loss), with $3,250M of capacity unused. That structure is standard and fully disclosed; it is also evidence that management valued holdco liquidity enough to pay a 3% discount on a US-government receivable.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question here is really a *recovery* question, and the distinction matters for scoring. Revenue guidance of approximately $195.5B for FY2026 is roughly flat against FY2025. Membership is down 8% year-over-year and Marketplace down 40%. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $5.25 against FY2026's $4.89 implies only +7% earnings growth. Getting back toward the FY2024 adjusted EPS of $7.17 requires margin recovery on a permanently smaller Marketplace revenue base, since enhanced APTC expiry shrank the addressable pool rather than merely shifting share.

The estimate revision picture requires care. Current-year consensus rose 39.3% over 30 days ($3.51 → $4.89) and 38.9% over 90 days. That is largely mechanical: 1H actuals beat by large margins, so the full-year figure rises arithmetically. The forward-looking component is FY2027 rising 17.2% over 30 days and 17.6% over 90 days ($4.46 → $5.25), which is a genuine revision to future earnings power. Q4 2026 consensus *deteriorated* from $(0.96) to $(1.09) over 90 days. Analysts raised the base and lowered the second-half shape, which is exactly what the guidance communicated.

Identified 2027 headwinds are dated and visible in the filings, though unquantified: Florida Comprehensive Medical Services non-renewal ending September 2026; New York Essential Plan-5 terminated July 2026; Hawaii QUEST Integration transitioning to successor plans January 1, 2027 (CCS extended only through October 31, 2026); California Medi-Cal moving the Unsatisfactory Immigration Status population (~250,000 Health Net members) to fee-for-service January 1, 2027. Offsetting: Illinois HealthChoice retained in July 2026 for a four-year term from January 2027, plus prior wins in Iowa, Mississippi, Kansas and the Florida SMMC expansion. Texas and Georgia losses are under protest; Pennsylvania Community HealthChoices was won in 2024 but remains in bid-protest litigation.

The genuine positive growth vector is Star Ratings. The 2026 ratings (driving 2027 revenue) place roughly 60% of MA membership in 3.5+ star plans and approximately 20% in 4-star plans, versus 55% and roughly 1% respectively in the 2025 vintage. The move from 1% to 20% at the 4-star quality-bonus threshold is materially revenue-positive for 2027, and it is the clearest identified earnings tailwind in the file. Against that, CMS's draft 2027 MA rate announcement (January 26, 2026) drew an unusually direct statement in the 10-K: management believes the rates are "insufficient to reflect the increases in continuing medical cost trend."

Marketplace is being deliberately shrunk to profitability, and the arithmetic says it is working: membership down 37% in six months while Commercial segment revenue fell only 7%, with a stated 4.5%–5% pre-tax margin target. That is a sound trade. It does not diversify the payer base; the remaining revenue is *more* weighted to Medicaid at 57% and to federally set Part D benchmarks.

Third, the price already embeds a substantial share of the recovery: the multiple expanded from roughly 0.77x book to 1.44x book, and 4.33x tangible book is not a value multiple for a business earning a 5.2% Medicaid gross margin with zero pricing power.

We note the guidance/consensus alignment as a mild positive for the base case: because consensus already models a negative second half, that shape is not a hidden landmine. The risk is that the actual second half is *worse* than the guided floor because of a development management has not quantified, which the disclosure cannot resolve.

Set against a four-for-four beat record with 129% and 56% surprises in the last two quarters, the probability that management is sandbagging is material. That argues the base case should sit above the consensus multiple applied mechanically.

**Base case $73** — 13.0x a blended FY2027/FY2028 figure of roughly $5.60, reflecting Star Ratings uplift and partial Medicaid catch-up net of the dated 2027 contract losses. +10.8% from $65.875.

## Competitive Landscape

Centene's structural advantage is scale at the state level: contracts across a large number of states give it a reusable operating template, actuarial data depth, and the capacity to absorb a single-state loss. Its disadvantage is that the payer is a government in every line.

The 2025 Marketplace shock was industry-wide (Becker's framing of Q2 2026 has Centene, Cigna and HCA all adjusting forecasts on ACA changes), but Centene's exposure was the largest in absolute terms because it entered 2025 with the biggest Marketplace book in the country at 5.86M members. The market is now differentiating between ACA-only carriers and Medicaid-heavy hybrids to Centene's disadvantage; IBD's July 28 framing was "Oscar Health flashes buy signal as Medicaid weighs on Centene."

Trajectory assessment is genuinely mixed. Improving: Star Ratings at the 4-star threshold (1% → 20% of MA membership), Marketplace repricing delivering +55% underlying gross margin growth toward a 4.5–5% pre-tax target, SG&A ratio down 110bp, and Illinois retained. Deteriorating: total membership down 8%, Medicaid contract losses in Florida, Hawaii, New York and California partially offsetting wins, and Part D share capture achieved by bidding below benchmark in all 34 CMS regions (a volume strategy in a business where the IRA cut federal reinsurance from 80% to 20% and CMS eliminated the narrowed risk corridors for 2026). Centene now carries materially more risk on 8.8M Part D lives than two years ago, and that book generates the second-half seasonal earnings drag as members progress toward the $2,100 out-of-pocket cap.

Net: stable-to-slightly-improving competitive position in a business model that structurally caps returns.

## Risk Assessment

The density of unquantified regulatory binaries here is unusually high, and this is the dimension where we score Centene most conservatively.

**OBBBA state provider-tax equalization.** CMS guidance of November 14, 2025 implements an OBBBA requirement that states may no longer tax Medicaid MCOs at higher rates than commercial insurers, effective by the end of the state fiscal year ending in 2026. Centene's disclosed concern is specific: if states raise the rate applicable to its Marketplace line during 2026, it cannot offset the cost because 2026 rates are already approved. The filing states a possible material impact on Marketplace profitability with no quantification. Which direction states equalize is not established; they could cut the Medicaid rate, phase in changes, or seek waivers. This is the most plausible candidate for management's second-half conservatism and it is invisible to any screening dataset.

**2026 Marketplace risk adjustment.** The estimate remains in motion through 2026 and 2027 as Wakely data arrives. This is the same mechanism that produced the 2025 blowup, and Q2 relied on the first data round.

**Medicaid margin timing.** 57% of revenue at a 5.2% FY2025 gross margin, with margin recovery externally placed at H2 2027. Maximum operating leverage if rates catch up; maximum downside if they keep lagging.

**RADV.** CMS is accelerating and expanding Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment data validation audits and has removed the fee-for-service adjuster beginning with audit year 2018, materially raising potential extrapolated recoveries across a multi-year lookback. Centene provides no quantification.

**Work requirements.** CMS's June 3, 2026 interim final rule was challenged by 26 states on June 29, 2026. Risk runs both ways: enrollment loss and adverse selection, since members failing administrative requirements skew healthier.

**Litigation and governance.** A putative securities class action (*Brock Lunstrum v. Centene*, SDNY, July 9, 2025) plus five derivative suits naming CEO London are pending. Centene states accruals are "not material." This is a credibility overhang rather than a solvency one; scienter has not been adjudicated.

**Holdco liquidity.** $715M of general-corporate cash against $16.1B of debt is thin, and consolidated cash is largely trapped. The constraint is permanent and normal for a regulated insurer, and it improved from $400M, but it does bind capital returns.

**Reserve sensitivity.** A 1% change in medical claims liability moves net earnings by $204M, against $12,898M of IBNR plus expected development at 6/30/2026.

Two items are unresolved in the available disclosure and should be treated as verification gaps: the contents of Item 5.02 8-Ks dated June 22 and July 28, 2026 (London, Asher and Pienkos all signed the 10-Q on July 28, so the changes do not involve CEO, CFO or chief accounting officer), and the precise construction of the ">$4.80" guidance figure, which comes from press coverage rather than a directly read press release. Our implied second-half arithmetic depends entirely on that figure.

### Options Market Signal

The headline "near-term ATM IV 97.8%" is a one-day-expiry artifact; that tenor prices only a ±1.6% move, which is same-day pinning mechanics, not stress. Discount it. The informative reads are further out: 1-month and 3-month ATM IV at 39.4% and 6-month at 41.9%, all *below* 1-year realized volatility of 49.2%. Forward vol is cheap to realized, which is not the configuration you see when the options market anticipates a repeat of 2025. Put/call skew is balanced across every tenor beyond one day (−0.3% to −1.0%), and put/call open interest at 0.37 is call-tilted. Short interest of 3.1% of float with 2.6 days to cover indicates no crowded bearish positioning.

The August 21 tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 4.83 against a 6.3% implied move, with skew still balanced. Heavy put volume without a skew premium reads more like hedging into the next data cycle than directional conviction. The ATM IV increase from 45.6% on July 19 to 97.8% is the same expiry-mechanics artifact and should not be read as a rise in perceived risk.

Net: the options market corroborates our fundamental read of a stabilizing but unresolved situation, with no imminent dislocation priced and no strong directional bet either way.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($88, +33.6%)

The operating recovery is real and not manufactured from reserve releases, which is arithmetically demonstrable, since prior-year development was flat in dollars year-over-year while adjusted EPS improved $3.13. Marketplace corrective pricing filed across states covering ~95% of membership is delivering 55% underlying gross margin growth toward a 4.5–5% pre-tax target. Star Ratings improvement from 1% to 20% of MA membership at the 4-star bonus threshold is a mechanical 2027 revenue tailwind. Deleveraging from 46.5% to 41.6% debt-to-capital restored covenant headroom and cut the 2027 maturity from $2,211M to $1,067M. Four consecutive beats, two of them enormous, suggest the ">$4.80" guidance is heavily sandbagged by a management team that lost all credibility in 2025 and is now under-promising. Normalized FY2028 earnings power of $6.75 at 13x supports $88, and statutory capital of $19.7B against an $11.3B minimum removes solvency from the discussion.

### Bear Case ($45, −31.7%)

Roughly 28% of Q2's adjusted EPS was a prior-benefit-year settlement, so the operating run rate is $1.80 rather than $2.51. The company's own guidance implies a negative second half that severance does not explain, and the most plausible candidate, OBBBA state provider-tax equalization hitting Marketplace inside a plan year with no ability to reprice, is entirely unquantified. The 2026 Wakely relative-morbidity data is still arriving, which is precisely how 2025 went wrong. Medicaid at 57% of revenue may not see margin recovery until H2 2027 while a dated schedule of 2027 contract losses (Florida, Hawaii, California UIS ~250,000 members, New York) reduces the base by an amount the filings do not quantify. Management declined to repurchase a single share under a $1.83B authorization at $35 (roughly 0.77x book), which is at minimum a revealed constraint. At 4.33x tangible book with $10.8B of goodwill still carried after a $6.7B write-off, there is no asset-value floor.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (`uncertain`). Existing holders should maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $65.875. The central question, whether the guided second-half decline reflects sandbagging or an unquantified cost development, cannot be resolved from available disclosure, and it is worth roughly 30 percentage points of outcome dispersion.

**Base case: $73** (+10.8%) — 13.0x blended FY2027/FY2028 adjusted EPS of approximately $5.60.
**Bull case: $88** (+33.6%) — 13.0x FY2028 adjusted EPS of $6.75.
**Bear case: $45** (−31.7%) — 11.25x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.00.
**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, gated on the Q3 2026 print (late October 2026) and the FY2027 initial guidance (roughly February 2027).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 adjusted EPS materially above the $0.10 consensus *and* explicit confirmation that no OBBBA state provider-tax impact materialized in Marketplace. This would validate the sandbagging thesis and reset the base case toward $80.
2. FY2027 initial guidance at or above $6.00 adjusted EPS with quantified Medicaid rate updates covering the acuity gap, alongside a quantified net revenue impact from the Florida/Hawaii/California/New York contract changes that proves smaller than feared.
3. Resumption of program share repurchases at scale (>$500M in a quarter) combined with holdco general-corporate cash above $1.5B, which would demonstrate that the FCF is reachable by equity holders.
4. A price near $50 (roughly 1.10x book and 9.5x FY2027 consensus) with fundamentals unchanged.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any adverse revision to the 2026 Marketplace risk-adjustment estimate as later Wakely rounds arrive. This is the 2025 mechanism repeating, and a reversal of even half the 2025 magnitude would reset the bear case.
2. Q3 2026 adjusted EPS below $(0.25) against $0.10 consensus, or FY2026 guidance reduced from the ">$4.80" floor.
3. Quantified OBBBA state provider-tax cost in Marketplace, or FY2027 initial guidance below $4.75.
4. HBR moving back above 90% for a full quarter, or prior-year favorable development falling below $1.2B on a half-year basis (which would remove the support that has been carrying roughly half of pre-tax earnings).

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings, expected late October 2026. That print absorbs the $3,318M pass-through cash reversal, the Part D seasonal drag, the Florida contract expiry in September, and the first opportunity for management to quantify the state provider-tax exposure.

## Conclusion

Centene has done the hard operational work.
