## Executive Summary

Victoria's Secret is in the early-to-middle innings of a legitimate brand turnaround. FY2025 delivered 5% comparable store sales growth, 7.8% Q4 revenue growth, adjusted EPS of $3.00 (up 12% YoY), and operating cash flow of $499M. The return of the Fashion Show in October 2025 catalyzed brand heat, international revenues grew 27%, and guidance for Q1 FY2026 implies continued mid-single-digit growth. New CEO Hillary Super (from Savage X Fenty) has overseen a near-total C-suite overhaul and the stock has appreciated ~140% from its mid-2025 lows.

However, the turnaround thesis is now partially priced. At $48, the stock trades at ~25x trailing EPS and ~13.8x forward consensus ($3.48 FY2026E), which is at a premium to apparel retail peers (median 13.9x). The macro backdrop for consumer discretionary is the worst of this cycle — savings rates at 3.6%, gas approaching $5/gallon, mortgage rates near 7.5%, and Whirlpool declaring "recession-level" conditions. Additionally, 20.8% short interest, a contentious proxy fight (poison pill expiring May 18, 2026), high leverage (D/E 4.79x), and the failed $591M Adore Me acquisition all weigh on the risk profile. The risk/reward at current levels is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Victoria's Secret & Co. is the world's largest intimate apparel company, operating two flagship brands (Victoria's Secret and PINK) along with a beauty/fragrance business and the acquired Adore Me digital brand. The company was spun off from L Brands (now Bath & Body Works) in August 2021.

**Operational footprint:** ~858 company-operated stores (766 US, 24 Canada, 65 China JV, 3 Adore Me), 562 partner-operated stores across ~70 countries. Revenue split: North America Stores 54%, Digital 31%, International 15%. Approximately 33,000 employees.

The strategic plan ("Path to Potential") centers on four pillars: (1) reasserting bra category authority, (2) recommitting to the PINK brand for younger consumers, (3) growing beauty/fragrance, and (4) evolving brand positioning and go-to-market strategy.

The term loan was repriced in December 2025, reducing the rate by ~61-93 bps and saving ~$3-4M annually. The ABL was extended to May 2030 with improved terms. Both refinancings signal improving credit market perception.

**Capital Allocation:** $250M share repurchase authorization (March 2024) remains entirely unused. No dividends. Management appears prioritizing debt reduction and cash accumulation over shareholder returns — defensible given the leverage profile but suboptimal given the ~$312M in FCF.

**Seasonal Pattern:** Cash flows are extremely seasonal. Q3 shows $375M ABL drawn and negative operating cash flow, while Q4 generates the majority of annual earnings and cash. Mid-year leverage snapshots systematically overstate year-round risk.

## Growth Analysis

**Domestic Comps:** FY2025 delivered +5% comp store sales (stores +4%), the strongest in VSCO's independent history. Store productivity reached $624/sq ft (+6% YoY).

**International:** +27% growth for the full year, driven by China JV, wholesale expansion, and franchising. Partner stores grew from 505 to 562. This is the highest-growth segment with the longest runway.

**Beauty/Fragrance:** Third strategic pillar with above-average growth rates. Vertically integrated manufacturing in Ohio provides margin advantage.

**Analyst Estimates:**
- FY2026E EPS: $3.48 (up 16% from $3.00 adjusted)
- FY2027E EPS: $4.15 (up 19%)
- Current quarter estimate: $0.31 (up 290% from 90 days ago, reflecting the seasonal trough)
- Full-year estimate revisions: +15.9% over 90 days

The estimate revision trend is decisively positive. The 90-day revision of +290% for the current quarter reflects an upgrade from near-zero to positive territory as analysts rerate the company post-Q4 beat. Full-year revisions of +16% over 90 days confirm building momentum.

**Growth Constraints:** The company is closing stores (882→858), suggesting growth must come from comp improvement, digital, and international rather than store expansion. Q1 FY2026 guidance of $1.51B midpoint (+6.4%) implies the momentum is carrying forward.

## Competitive Landscape

Victoria's Secret remains the dominant U.S. intimate apparel brand by revenue, though it lost significant share from 2018-2023 as DTC challengers (ThirdLove, Savage X Fenty, Skims, Aerie) capitalized on the brand's tone-deaf positioning during the #MeToo era. The hiring of Hillary Super from Savage X Fenty signals the company is importing competitive intelligence directly.

**Competitive strengths:**
- Fashion Show return generated cultural relevance that no competitor can replicate at scale
- International infrastructure (562 partner stores) is a structural advantage no DTC brand can match
- Beauty/fragrance vertical integration provides margin protection
- PINK repositioning addresses the key demographic vulnerability

**Remaining vulnerabilities:**
- Mall traffic dependency (54% of revenue from stores)
- DTC brands continue gaining share in younger demographics
- Amazon and mass-market private label eroding the bottom end

## Risk Assessment

**1. Consumer Macro (HIGH):** This is the single most relevant near-term risk. With gas approaching $5/gallon, savings rates at 3.6%, consumer confidence collapsing, and multiple large retailers (Whirlpool, BofA spending data) confirming contraction, intimate apparel — while somewhat needs-based — faces material discretionary demand risk. The May 19-21 consumer earnings cluster (HD, TGT, WMT) will provide a read-across.

**2. Proxy Fight / Governance (MEDIUM-HIGH):** BBRC (Brett Blundy) proxy contest creates uncertainty. Poison pill expires May 18, 2026. Board member departure signals internal pressure. Could be positive (discipline, capital returns) or negative (distraction, management flight risk).

**3. Leverage (MEDIUM-HIGH):** D/E 4.79x, Z-Score 1.94 (grey zone). In a rising-rate environment with recession probability at 60-65%, this leverage profile creates meaningful financial stress potential. Nearest maturity is August 2028, providing runway, but covenant tests under a revenue downturn could constrain flexibility.

**4. Short Interest (MEDIUM):** At 20.8% of float with 4.3 days to cover, the stock has meaningful short interest. This creates both squeeze potential (if Q1 earnings surprise) and informed bearish positioning (sophisticated investors see risk not in the price).

**5. Tariff Exposure (MEDIUM):** $85M net impact in FY2025. The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs, replaced by 10% global under Section 122. Potential for partial refunds as a positive catalyst, but the tariff environment remains fluid with the current trade conflicts.

**6. Adore Me Write-off (LOW, but informative):** $120M impairment + $36M restructuring on a ~$591M acquisition demonstrates questionable prior capital allocation. The new management team inherited this problem, and their decision to write it down rather than protect it suggests intellectual honesty.

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is in backwardation (81% near-term vs 72% long-dated), confirming imminent catalyst pricing (proxy fight resolution, consumer earnings read-across). ## Investment Thesis

- Turnaround continues: FY2026 delivers $3.50+ EPS, FY2027 $4.15+
- Proxy fight resolved constructively — capital returns initiated (buyback deployed)
- Tariff refund provides $30-50M one-time earnings tailwind
- International sustains 20%+ growth; Beauty becomes third growth engine
- Multiple re-rates toward 16-18x forward as turnaround gains credibility

**Bear Case ($30-35, ~30-35% downside):**
- Consumer recession materializes — comp sales go flat to negative in H2 2026
- Leverage becomes a constraint if EBITDA declines; rating downgrades pressure debt costs
- Proxy fight destabilizes management — key executives depart amid uncertainty
- International growth decelerates as global economy weakens
- The stock has already made its turnaround move ($17.53 → $67 → current $48); early money was made

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $38-40 range (offering ~10x forward FY2027E EPS) on broad consumer weakness without VSCO-specific deterioration — this would offer genuine asymmetric entry
2. Proxy fight resolves and management announces deployment of $250M buyback authorization
3. Q1 FY2026 earnings (expected ~June 2026) beats with raised full-year guidance, confirming turnaround durability through macro headwinds

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q1 FY2026 comps turn negative (below -2%), indicating the consumer recession is hitting intimate apparel directly
2. Covenant breach risk materializes — either ABL utilization approaches limits during seasonal trough, or EBITDA declines force covenant renegotiation
3. CEO or CFO departure amid proxy contest — would signal turnaround thesis is broken

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q1 FY2026 earnings (likely early June 2026) is the next natural decision point. The consumer earnings cluster (May 19-21) provides a read-across on whether the macro environment is overwhelming retailer-specific stories.

## Conclusion

Victoria's Secret is executing well on a genuine turnaround, with improving comps, rising earnings estimates, and competent new leadership. The fundamental trajectory justifies holding the stock. However, the risk/reward at $48 is balanced rather than compelling: (1) consumer discretionary faces the worst macro configuration of this cycle, (2) leverage is high for a potential recession environment, (3) the proxy fight creates governance uncertainty through May 18, (4) 20.8% short interest reflects informed bearish positioning, and (5) the stock has already appreciated ~140% from its mid-2025 lows, placing it at fair value relative to peers.

The correct positioning is to hold for the growth trajectory while awaiting either a pullback that creates asymmetric entry or catalyst resolution (proxy, Q1 earnings, tariff refund) that confirms the thesis can withstand macro headwinds. For new capital, better entry points likely exist in the $38-42 range.
