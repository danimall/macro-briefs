**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NNN REIT is a pure-play triple-net lease retail REIT with one of the most durable income models in the sector: 3,711 freestanding single-tenant properties, 98.6% leased, a 10.1-year weighted-average lease term, and a 36-consecutive-year dividend-increase streak that ranks third-longest among all public REITs. The business runs on 85 employees, a 95.9% NOI margin, and a self-funded model that generates roughly $212M of free cash flow after dividends. Core FFO per share has compounded steadily from $2.86 in 2021 to $3.41 in 2025, and management raised 2026 guidance to $3.48–$3.54 Core FFO / $3.53–$3.59 AFFO, implying ~3.5% midpoint growth. This is a well-run, investment-grade (Baa1) income compounder with a fortress balance sheet.

The problem is price. The stock has risen ~16% over the trailing year to $49.38, sitting at the very top of its 52-week range ($38.90–$50.00) and above every analyst price target in the coverage set (mean $46.63, median $46.00, high $50.00). The move has compressed the dividend yield to roughly 4.9%, now above the Retail REIT peer median of 3.9% but well through NNN's own recent ~5.7% level. At ~13.9x 2026 AFFO guidance, NNN is priced fairly-to-fully for a business growing mid-single digits. The estimate-revision picture is neutral-to-slightly-negative (current-year consensus revised down 2.7% over 90 days), and analyst sentiment is degrading, with the lone strong-buy dropping to zero this month.

Beneath a placid operational surface, two trends bear watching: property impairments rose roughly 4x year-over-year (to $28.6M in 2025 and $10.7M in Q1 2026) and cash-basis tenants climbed to 14 (4.1% of ABR) from 10. Management frames both as proactive de-risking of already-identified problem assets, and cash bad debt is running at 15 bps versus a 60 bps assumption, so the near-term earnings threat is contained. The direction of travel in cyclical categories (restaurants, entertainment, dealerships) is the leading indicator to monitor. We rate NNN a HOLD on an income basis: own it for the well-covered, reliably-growing yield, but the appreciation case is thin at a stock trading above consensus targets with no identifiable near-term re-rating catalyst.

## Company Overview

NNN REIT, formed in 1984 and headquartered in Orlando, is a triple-net lease retail REIT. Its tenants pay rent plus all property operating expenses (taxes, insurance, maintenance, and capital expenditures) under long-term net leases (typically 10–20 year initial terms). This structure produces exceptionally high margins (95.9% NOI margin) with minimal overhead, which is why the company operates ~39.6M square feet across 3,711 properties in all 50 states, DC, and Puerto Rico with only 85 employees.

The portfolio is deliberately tilted toward small-format, service-oriented and necessity retail: automotive service (18.6% of ABR), convenience stores (16.3%), restaurants (14.3%), entertainment (7.2%), and auto dealerships (6.6%) collectively represent roughly 63% of annualized base rent. The largest tenants are 7-Eleven (4.3%), Mister Car Wash (3.8%), Dave & Buster's (3.6%), Camping World (3.5%), and Kent Distributors (2.6%), with the top five totaling 17.8% of ABR. Geographic concentration skews to Texas (18.4%), Florida (8.7%), Illinois (5.1%), Georgia (4.5%), and Ohio (4.2%).

The structural advantages are real. The balance sheet is entirely unsecured with no encumbered assets, a 10.5-year weighted-average debt maturity (the longest in the net-lease space and well-matched to the 10.1-year lease duration), and only 1.6% floating-rate debt. The self-funded model reduces reliance on external capital, and decades of disciplined, low-cost-basis acquisitions give the company disposition flexibility; it recently generated 6%+ economic gains selling occupied assets with only ~3 years of remaining lease term.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue has grown steadily on an acquisition-driven basis: $773M (2022) → $828M (2023) → $869M (2024) → $926M (2025), roughly 6–7% annually. Q1 2026 revenue was $240.4M, up 4.1% year-over-year. Net income was $389.8M in 2025, slightly below 2024's $396.8M, reflecting higher impairments and interest expense. Operating margin was 63.6% and net margin 42.1% in 2025; these figures understate the economics because of non-cash depreciation, which is why FFO is the appropriate lens.

**Core FFO/AFFO.** Core FFO per share grew from $2.86 (2021) to $3.41 (2025), a consistent mid-single-digit trajectory. Q1 2026 Core FFO was $0.86 and AFFO $0.87, both roughly flat year-over-year, but depressed by approximately $0.04 from lower lease-termination fees ($739K versus $8.2M in the prior-year quarter). Normalizing for that headwind, AFFO grew ~4.8%. The 2026 guidance raise implies an acceleration from 2.7% growth in 2025.

**Capital allocation and spreads.** 2025 acquisitions totaled 239 properties for $931M at a 7.4% cap rate; Q1 2026 added $145M at a 7.5% cash yield with a 19-year WALT (sale-leaseback-focused). Dispositions are used for portfolio optimization: 116 properties for $190M in 2025 at a 6.4% cap rate. With WACC in the high-6% area against ~7.5% acquisition yields, investment spreads remain positive but thin, the central constraint on external growth in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt was $4.82B at year-end 2025, all unsecured, with net debt/EBITDA of 5.6x and debt/gross assets of ~42%, appropriate leverage for an investment-grade net-lease REIT and well within sector norms. The company drew a $300M unsecured term loan in Q1 2026 (swapped to 4.1% fixed) and sold 1.7M shares forward at ~$45 (~$74M of future proceeds). The next maturity is a $350M unsecured note due December 2026, with multiple leverage-neutral refinancing options available (term-loan accordion, IG-spread issuance, forward equity). Liquidity stands at roughly $1.2B.

**A note on the Altman Z-Score.** The 1.5 reading that flags "distress" is a false signal. The Z-Score mechanically penalizes REITs for high leverage and low asset turnover (0.1x); NNN is investment-grade, covenant-compliant, and has ample liquidity with well-laddered maturities. The Piotroski check (all four tested signals positive) and the steady $667M of 2025 operating cash flow are the more accurate read of financial resilience.

**Dividend.** NNN raised the quarterly dividend to $0.60 in 2026 (3.4% growth), for a 69% AFFO payout, conservative for the sector and comfortably covered.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is deliberate and unspectacular: mid-single-digit Core FFO/share compounding driven by accretive acquisitions funded through a mix of retained free cash flow, forward equity, and staggered unsecured debt. The 2026 guidance raise is a modest positive, and the sale-leaseback pipeline is described as elevated versus 2025 (itself a record year), as sellers use sale-leasebacks for balance-sheet management, a favorable sourcing tailwind.

The earnings-surprise record is solid but not accelerating: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter (Q1 2026) a 2.2% miss driven by impairments rather than operational shortfall. Estimate revisions are the more telling signal, and they are neutral-to-slightly-negative: current-quarter estimates were revised down 1.9% over 90 days (though up 1.1% over 30 days), and current-year consensus was cut 2.7% over 90 days to $2.04. This is not the positive-revision velocity that would support a growth-driven re-rating; it is stable-to-fading.

The durability of the model is its growth strength: the tilt toward service and experiential retail (auto service, car washes, convenience stores, QSRs) is relatively internet-resistant, and occupancy improved 30 bps to 98.6% in Q1 with vacant properties falling from 64 to 53. The constraint is structural: positive but thin investment spreads cap the pace of accretive external growth, and there is no evidence of a step-change in that dynamic. This is a compounder, not a reacceleration story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $49.38, NNN trades at ~13.9x 2026 AFFO guidance (midpoint $3.56) and offers a ~4.9% dividend yield. On a P/E basis (23.7x trailing/forward on GAAP EPS of $2.07), the reported multiple is roughly in line with the Retail REIT peer median of 22.5x, but P/E is a poor lens for REITs because of depreciation, and the AFFO multiple is the operative metric.

The relative-value picture is neutral-to-unfavorable. Peer yields cluster at 3.9% (BRX, median), 3.7% (FRT), 4.0% (KRG), and 4.2% (ADC), so NNN's yield still screens as a premium to the shopping-center-heavy comp set, a partial support. The more relevant comparison is NNN's own history: the stock traded at a ~5.7% yield in the low-$40s as recently as a few months ago, and the appreciation has compressed that cushion. The stock now sits at the top of its 52-week range and trades above every analyst price target.

The cycle-adjusted and reacceleration tests that would justify a high valuation score do not apply here. There is no earnings trough being lapped (Core FFO/share has grown steadily for five years), no estimate-revision velocity above the threshold that precedes re-rating (revisions are flat-to-negative), and no specific catalyst for multiple expansion. When a stock trades above consensus targets on flat-to-negative estimate revisions and a fading sentiment trend, proximity to targets warns that the near-term upside is priced in rather than providing a floor. A compressed multiple on a quality business can be cheap, but NNN's multiple has expanded toward the high end of its range, and the yield support that made it attractive at $42 is materially weaker at $49.

We do not penalize NNN for having risen; price appreciation on improving fundamentals is not a valuation input. The fundamentals here have improved only modestly (3.5% guided growth), while the price rose ~16%, so the multiple has done more work than the earnings. That combination argues for a below-average valuation score. Fair value on a mid-single-digit AFFO compounder with a ~4.9% yield sits closer to the low-to-mid $40s than to current levels; the base case offers limited appreciation from here.

## Competitive Landscape

The net-lease sector is chronically competitive and has been for two decades; management's own framing is that "the names change" (non-traded REITs, now financial-institution-sponsored REITs, plus persistent private capital). NNN reported modest cap-rate compression of ~15–25 bps in its core auto-service and convenience-store categories in early 2026, driven by heightened deal competition rather than structural change. Large private capital largely competes in industrial net-lease, leaving NNN's small-format retail niche relatively insulated.

NNN's competitive standing is stable-to-slightly-positive on operations but flat on relative performance. On the plus side: the sale-leaseback pipeline is elevated, occupancy is above its long-term average, and the 7-Eleven concentration is less alarming than it screens; the exposure came via acquired regional operators (not a direct 7-Eleven corporate relationship), at a low $2.2M average cost basis per store, with none on 7-Eleven's closure lists and a major renewal completed in 2025.

The offsetting evidence is a persistent relative-performance gap. NNN's 5-year cumulative TSR of 102.13 trails its peer group's 119.49; the market has consistently valued NNN's steadiness at a discount to faster-growing net-lease peers such as Agree Realty (trading at 40x P/E on faster growth) and Essential Properties. This is not deterioration, but it is not share-gain or moat-widening either; it is stable competitive positioning in a mature, competitive niche. The trajectory does not justify a score above the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

**Impairment and cash-basis tenant creep (the key thread).** Impairments jumped to $28.6M in 2025 (from $6.6M in 2024) and $10.7M in Q1 2026 (from $1.5M a year earlier). Cash-basis GLA nearly doubled to 6.9%. This signals pockets of tenant stress in cyclical categories even as headline occupancy improved. The counterpoint is meaningful: actual cash bad debt was only 15 bps in Q1 versus a 60–75 bps assumption, and management frames the impairments as non-cash write-downs on already-vacant assets being repositioned (AMC theaters, Frisch's). Badcock assets resolved at ~100% recovery. The trend is worth monitoring as a leading indicator, but it is not yet an earnings threat.

**Concentration.** Top six lines of trade represent ~63% of ABR; top five tenants 17.8%; top five states 40.9% (Texas alone 18.4%). Single-tenant properties mean a default causes complete revenue loss on that asset, and build-to-suit properties can be costly to re-tenant.

**Consumer-discretionary exposure.** Restaurants, entertainment (Dave & Buster's), theaters (AMC on the watch list), and dealerships are cyclical and consumer-spending-sensitive.

**Rate and refinancing risk.** A higher-for-longer rate path directly compresses NNN's positive-but-thin investment spreads and raises the cost of refinancing the ~$1.5B of notes maturing within five years, starting with the December 2026 note. This is well-mitigated by staggered maturities, the IG rating, and only 1.6% floating-rate debt, but it caps the accretive-growth engine.

**Environmental.** 67 properties are under remediation or monitoring, reflecting underground petroleum storage tank exposure in the convenience-store and auto-service portfolios.

Most of these risks are sector-standard and already priced. The impairment/cash-basis trend is the one idiosyncratic item the market may be under-weighting, but its near-term earnings impact is contained. On balance, risk is around the sector average: no binary catalysts, but no asymmetric recovery upside either given the elevated price.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 18.9% sits fairly against 17.2% historical volatility (a 1.7-point spread) and in the 26th percentile of the current chain; the options market is not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The put/call skew is somewhat elevated at 7.6% in the front month, indicating modest hedging demand, but the put/call volume ratio (0.09) and OI ratio (0.02) are very low, consistent with a low-drama income name rather than positioning ahead of a catalyst. The term structure is in contango (near-term 18.9% below 6-month 21.4%), a normal/complacent structure. IV compressed from 20.5% to 18.9% since the prior snapshot as the stock rose 6.8%. Nothing in the options data challenges the fundamental view; it corroborates a stable, low-volatility income profile.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($52–$55).** Net-lease demand stays firm, the sale-leaseback pipeline delivers acquisitions at accretive spreads, occupancy holds above 98.5%, and 2026 AFFO lands at the high end of guidance ($3.59). If long-end rates ease, high-quality net-lease yield names re-rate as the yield gap to Treasuries widens in NNN's favor. In that scenario the yield could compress toward 4.5% and the stock reaches the low-to-mid $50s on multiple support plus the ~5% dividend, for a total return in the high-single/low-double digits.

**Bear case ($40–$42, roughly -15% to -19%).** The impairment and cash-basis tenant trend accelerates in cyclical categories, bad debt migrates toward the 60 bps assumption or higher, and higher-for-longer rates keep investment spreads thin while raising refinancing costs on the December 2026 note. With the stock already above every analyst target, any disappointment de-rates it back toward the ~5.7% yield that prevailed in the low-$40s just months ago. The move would be a multiple contraction, not a dividend cut; the payout remains covered at 69% of AFFO.

The base case sits between these: mid-single-digit AFFO growth, a maintained and growing dividend, and a stock that is fairly valued at ~13.9x AFFO. The reliable ~4.9% yield plus ~3.5% growth frames a ~8% total-return profile if the multiple holds, but the multiple has already expanded toward the top of its range, and the appreciation component is where the risk lies.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on an income basis, not a growth thesis. The recommendation has now been HOLD across four consecutive reports, and nothing in the current data warrants a change: the stock has appreciated into fair-to-full value while fundamentals improved only modestly, which supports holding for yield but not adding for appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$40s that restores the yield to ~5.5%+ and re-opens a margin of safety below fair value.
- A durable turn in estimate-revision velocity (current-year AFFO revised up rather than the recent -2.7% over 90 days), signaling reacceleration of accretive growth.
- Meaningful long-end rate relief (10Y sustainably lower) that structurally widens NNN's yield advantage and re-rates the sector.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Cash bad debt migrating above the 60 bps assumption, or cash-basis tenants rising materially above 4.1% of ABR, confirming the impairment trend as genuine credit deterioration rather than proactive de-risking.
- Occupancy falling below ~97.5% or a top-five tenant entering bankruptcy.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration pushing the AFFO payout above ~80%, or any language signaling the 36-year streak is at risk.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings on August 5, 2026, is the next natural checkpoint; watch the impairment/cash-basis tenant trend, occupancy, and any change to full-year AFFO guidance. The December 2026 note refinancing is a secondary monitoring point for spread/cost color.

## Conclusion

NNN REIT is a high-quality, investment-grade income compounder with a fortress balance sheet, a 36-year dividend-increase streak, and a durable, service-tilted net-lease portfolio. The operational story is sound: occupancy improved, guidance was raised to ~3.5% AFFO growth, and the payout is conservatively covered at 69%. Financial health is genuinely strong once the misfiring Altman Z-Score is set aside.

The recommendation is constrained by price, not quality. After a ~16% run to $49.38, the stock sits at the top of its 52-week range, above every analyst price target, with a compressed ~4.9% yield, flat-to-negative estimate revisions, and degrading sentiment. There is no earnings reacceleration, no re-rating catalyst, and no margin of safety at current levels; the multiple has expanded faster than the earnings. Beneath the surface, rising impairments and cash-basis tenant creep are the trend to watch, though their near-term earnings impact is contained by cash bad debt running well below assumptions.

We rate NNN a HOLD (income). It is worth owning for the reliable, growing ~4.9% yield and the durability of the model, and it should be accumulated on weakness toward the low-$40s where the yield cushion and margin of safety return. At $49.38, the appreciation case is thin, and the risk/reward is balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable.
