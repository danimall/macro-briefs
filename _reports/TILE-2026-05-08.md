## Executive Summary

Interface is a global flooring company experiencing pronounced earnings growth driven by operating leverage, mix improvement, and a structural reduction in interest expense following a $236M debt paydown over two years. Net income grew 161% from FY2023 to FY2025 ($44.5M to $116.1M) on only 10% revenue growth, demonstrating substantial operational leverage. The stock trades at approximately 14.1x consensus FY2026 EPS of $2.10, with estimates rising across recent periods.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) continued margin expansion from manufacturing automation investments and a now-structurally-lower interest burden (~$10M vs. $32M two years ago); (2) revenue growth from diversification beyond corporate office into healthcare, education, and transportation end markets; and (3) a clean balance sheet (D/E 0.27, net debt ~$110M) that enables capital return and strategic flexibility. The primary risks are tariff escalation affecting South Korean LVT and German rubber imports, cyclical exposure to commercial construction, and early-stage PFAS litigation.

At current levels ($29.55), the stock offers approximately 18-29% upside to analyst targets ($35-$38) with a clear earnings catalyst path through FY2026 guidance raises and continued quarterly beats.

## Company Overview

Interface Inc. is a global leader in modular carpet tile and commercial flooring solutions, generating $1.387B in FY2025 revenue across two segments: Americas (AMS, ~61% of sales) and Europe, Africa, Asia & Australia (EAAA, ~39%). The company's product portfolio spans modular carpet tile, luxury vinyl tile (LVT), nora-branded rubber flooring, and FLOR residential area rugs.

The company operates manufacturing in the U.S. (2 plants), Netherlands, UK, China, Australia, and Germany (rubber). LVT is sourced from a third-party in South Korea. Interface employs approximately 3,570 people globally.

Interface's "One Interface" strategy integrates its multi-product platform to offer institutional buyers a single-source flooring solution, differentiated by sustainability credentials (28 consecutive years as a Top 10 global sustainability leader) and design innovation through its long-standing David Oakey partnership.

## Competitive Landscape

Interface is a market leader in global modular carpet tile and holds the leading position in commercial rubber flooring (via nora). It competes against significantly larger diversified players:

- **Shaw Industries** (Berkshire Hathaway subsidiary): Dominant in broadloom and residential; less focused on modular carpet tile's premium segment
- **Mohawk Industries**: $10B+ flooring conglomerate with massive scale advantages in residential
- **Tarkett/Forbo**: European competitors in commercial flooring

Interface's competitive moat derives from:
1. **Sustainability differentiation** — 200+ carbon-negative carpet tile products; increasingly a purchasing criterion for institutional ESG mandates
2. **Design innovation** — Biomimicry-inspired products, David Oakey partnership
3. **Integrated solution** — carpet + LVT + rubber from a single supplier simplifies procurement for facility managers
4. **Customization capability** — Made-to-order production at scale

The competitive position is stable-to-improving: healthcare and education diversification reduces dependency on the weakening corporate office segment, and sustainability credentials are becoming more valuable over time as institutional purchasing policies tighten.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Exposure (HIGH):**
LVT sourced entirely from South Korea and rubber manufactured solely in Germany expose the company to U.S. trade policy. FY2025 tariff costs were $7.3M (~4.5% of operating income). Escalation beyond current levels could compress margins 50-100bps if pricing offsets lag. Management has demonstrated pass-through capability but acknowledged "dynamic trade policy environment" as a risk.

**2. Cyclicality (MODERATE):**
Commercial construction/renovation spending drives demand. GDP growth at 2% and elevated interest rates create headwinds for office renovation cycles. However, diversification into healthcare, education, and transportation partially buffers this risk.

**3. Single-Source Suppliers (MODERATE):**
The sole LVT supplier in South Korea and sole rubber plant in Germany create concentration risks. A disruption to either could materially impact revenue for 6-12 months given limited alternatives.

**4. PFAS Litigation (LOW/EMERGING):**
Named in MDL No. 2873 regarding PFAS contamination. Early stage with "meritorious defenses" asserted. Unlikely to be material in near term given industry-wide nature of MDL, but warrants monitoring.

**5. Corporate Office Secular Decline (MODERATE):**
Hybrid work permanently reduces corporate office renovation demand. Interface has diversified its end-market exposure, but corporate office remains a significant segment and this represents a structural headwind.

**6. Limited Analyst Coverage:**
Only 2-3 analysts cover the stock. This creates information asymmetry (opportunity) but also reduces liquidity support during selloffs and creates wider bid/ask spreads.

## Conclusion

Interface represents a compelling mid-cap value opportunity trading at ~14x forward earnings with 7%+ FCF yield, a consistent recent history of earnings beats, a clean balance sheet, and structural tailwinds from interest expense reduction and automation investment. The stock appears undervalued relative to its own earnings growth trajectory.

The key risk is tariff escalation on imported LVT and rubber products, which could compress margins if pass-through pricing fails. However, the company has demonstrated ability to offset cost headwinds through pricing and mix in recent quarters, and the $243M in undrawn credit provides financial flexibility. The latest backlog figure from the FY2025 10-K ($222.8M at February 2, 2026, roughly in line with $223.4M a year earlier) suggests stable demand, though not the acceleration that would further de-risk the thesis.

At $29.55, the asymmetry is favorable: 18-29% upside to analyst targets versus ~29% downside in the bear case. The stock warrants a BUY at 2-3% portfolio sizing, with the understanding that this is a cyclical industrial with moderate growth and tariff policy is an evolving risk requiring monitoring.
