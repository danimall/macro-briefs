## Executive Summary

Envista is two years into a margin repair after a severe 2022-2024 derating. The company's own pay-versus-performance disclosure gives the cleanest picture: adjusted EBITDA margin ran 20.1% in 2022, collapsed to 11.8% in 2024, and recovered to 13.7% in 2025. Roughly 640 basis points were destroyed; about 190 have come back. Management's long-term incentive metrics — core sales growth, free cash flow, and adjusted EBITDA margin — are pointed directly at the remaining gap, and the 2026 evidence (Q2 segment margins of 12.8% in Specialty Products & Technologies and 17.7% in Equipment & Consumables, versus 10.2% and 15.2% a year earlier) shows progress. Four consecutive adjusted EPS beats of 13-20% support the case that consensus has been too conservative on the recovery slope.

The problem is what sits underneath the most recent beat. The Q2 2026 10-Q discloses $12.6M of IEEPA tariff refunds received and recorded in cost of sales, and attributes essentially all of SP&T's 260bp segment margin gain to that refund. Recomputing from the filed figures: gross margin excluding the refund was 54.0% versus 54.2% in Q2 2025, a ~20bp contraction rather than the reported 150bp expansion. The after-tax per-share effect at the 27.8% quarterly rate on 164.1M diluted shares is about $0.055, or roughly 69-75% of the $0.068 beat against consensus. Excluding the refund and a $3.2M property-sale gain in SG&A, operating margin was ~8.8% versus 6.8%, and ~9.3% adding back the $3.7M year-over-year increase in restructuring — 200-250bp of genuine expansion rather than the reported 420bp. One caveat matters: it could not be confirmed whether Envista excluded the refund from its own adjusted-EPS definition. If it did, this attribution falls away.

Valuation reflects most of the recovery already. At $27.96 the stock trades at 20.0x the FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $1.40, 19.3x current-year consensus of $1.45, 17.8x next-year consensus of $1.57, and about 12.9x EV/EBITDA on FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $372.6M. The base case gets to roughly $31 and the bear case to roughly $21 (the low end of the published analyst range), an unfavorable 11%-up/25%-down skew against a 52-week high of $30.42. Prior reports scored this name 5.4 → 5.6 → 5.9 → 5.6 → 5.9 with four HOLDs; this report holds at 5.8 with a modest reduction driven by identifiable items — the composition of the Q2 beat, SP&T core deceleration to +3.1%, and a third consecutive increase in largest-customer concentration — not by price action, since the stock is higher than at the last review.

## Company Overview

Envista is a pure-play global dental products company spun out of Danaher in 2019 and headquartered in Brea, California, with operations in more than 30 countries. FY2025 sales were $2,719.5M across two reportable segments:

| Segment | FY2025 Sales | % of total | Segment Op. Profit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Specialty Products & Technologies | $1,752.8M | 64.5% | $191.2M | 10.9% |
| Equipment & Consumables | $966.7M | 35.5% | $158.0M | 16.3% |
| Corporate and other | — | — | $(133.1)M | — |
| **Consolidated** | **$2,719.5M** | | **$216.1M** | **7.9%** |

SP&T contains Nobel Biocare implants, regenerative solutions, prosthetics, Ormco orthodontic brackets, the Spark clear-aligner line, lab products and loupes. E&C contains DEXIS digital imaging, endodontics, restorative materials, rotary burs, impression materials and infection prevention.

Geographically, North America was 51.3% of FY2025 sales (U.S. alone 47.1%), Western Europe 22.6%, other developed 4.5%, and emerging markets 21.6% (China 7.1%). In Q2 2026, 54.5% of sales came from outside the U.S., up from 53.6%.

The 10-K describes Envista as "a leading worldwide provider" without claiming category leadership, and characterizes its markets as "highly competitive... in most markets" with "rapid technological development" and "consolidation of our competitors." Functionally this is a portfolio of mid-tier positions across dental sub-categories rather than a single dominant franchise, which distinguishes it from Align in aligners or Straumann in premium implants. Brands are run as semi-autonomous units (Form 4 titles confirm separate presidents for Nobel Biocare, Orthodontics and Diagnostics), and Envista does not disclose brand-level revenue, so Spark and Nobel performance cannot be isolated externally.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and the composition of growth

| ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | 2,566.5 | 2,510.6 | 2,719.5 | 1,436.0 |
| YoY reported | — | (2.2)% | +8.3% | +10.5% |
| Core sales growth | — | — | +6.5% | +7.1% |

FY2025's core growth decomposes, per the MD&A, into a 4.5% figure that bundles volume with "the timing of deferred revenue recognition related to our clear aligner treatment plans," plus 2.0% of price. The bundling matters because Note 17 shows revenue recognized from the opening contract-liability balance rose to $153.5M in FY2025 from $97.1M in FY2024 — a $56.4M acceleration worth about 2.1% of sales — and the pattern continued into 2026 ($126.8M released in H1 2026 versus $88.6M in H1 2025). Note 2 discloses that the aligner "usage rate" (expected refinement orders) is continually reviewed and that revisions change the pattern of recognition. A lower assumed usage rate would front-load revenue.

This is a hypothesis with financial-statement support, not an established fact. Envista never states it changed the usage rate, no control deficiency or restatement was disclosed, and the higher release could equally reflect a larger opening balance, faster actual refinement ordering, or a mix shift to shorter plans. Two scope points: contract liabilities are a balance and still grew ($166.8M → $207.2M → $215.0M), so cash collection is unaffected; and remaining performance obligations of $177.4M at July 3, 2026 are not backlog — Note 17 excludes contracts with original terms of one year or less, so RPO covers well under one month of revenue. The practical takeaway is that FY2025 run-rate demand was somewhat weaker than 6.5% core growth implies, and the 2026 MD&A drops the deferred-revenue language entirely, consistent with a 2025-specific effect.

A fiscal-calendar artifact also distorts the 2026 cadence. Q1 2026 ran 93 days (Jan 1 – April 3) against Q1 2025's 87 days (Jan 1 – March 28); Q2 and Q3 are 91 days in both years; Q4 2026 will run 90 days against Q4 2025's 96. Six extra selling days flattered Q1's +9.5% core growth and six fewer will depress Q4. Part of the Q1→Q2 core deceleration (9.5% → 5.0%) is therefore mechanical. This is a directional adjustment only — revenue is not linear in calendar days, early January is seasonally weak for dental offices, Envista quantifies no day-count effect, and emerging-market softness is an independent, non-calendar drag (SP&T emerging-market core sales fell 0.5% in H1 2026; total emerging-market core growth was +0.8% in H1 against +8.5% in developed markets).

The tariff-refund adjustment described in the Executive Summary is the central earnings-quality item. Its segment fingerprint is explicit: the 10-Q attributes "approximately 260 basis point improvement related to lower tariff costs due to receipt of tariff refunds" to SP&T's gain, with +100bp from higher sales offset by −100bp of commercial growth investment. E&C's 250bp improvement was attributed to operating leverage (+530bp) net of growth investment (−180bp) and restructuring (−120bp), a more durable-looking composition. Underlying operating improvement is real, roughly 200-250bp company-wide, and concentrated in the segment that Envista does not market as premium.

### The FY2025 tax distortion

FY2025 GAAP net income of $47.0M and diluted EPS of $0.28 are not a usable baseline. Pretax income was $177.2M against $130.2M of tax — a 73.5% effective rate. Note 19's new rate reconciliation isolates a $63.6M charge (35.9 percentage points) for "change in indefinite reinvestment assertion," which both the 10-K and the Q2 10-Q tie to evaluating the One Big Beautiful Bill Act enacted July 4, 2025. Other items include $17.5M from elimination of intercompany transactions, $11.9M of Subpart F/GILTI, and $9.9M from a Swiss valuation allowance, partly offset by a $22.7M Luxembourg statutory interest deduction. Normalizing only the assertion charge gives a ~37.6% rate, ~$110.6M of net income and roughly $0.65 of EPS on 169.2M diluted shares. Q1 and Q2 2026 effective rates of 27.0% and 27.8% confirm normalization. The trailing P/E of 68.5x reported in the market-data feed (and the identical "forward" figure, which is a data artifact) is therefore uninformative; the forward adjusted basis is the correct lens. Part of the 2025 burden was cash — taxes paid rose to $79.8M from $32.9M, with Canada the largest single jurisdiction at $30.8M against only $10.2M of U.S. federal — and the Canadian figure is unexplained in the filing.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus expects $1.45 of current-year adjusted EPS and $1.57 next year, +8.3%. Revisions are positive but modest — current year +1.6% over 90 days and +1.3% over 30 days, next year +0.8% over 90 days and flat over 30. This is nowhere near the >30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection, so the growth case rests on ordinary compounding rather than a re-rating catalyst.

The revenue engine is mid-single-digit core growth with a favorable currency tailwind (FX added 1.6% to Q2 and 2.8% to H1 sales). The composition is informative: H1 2026 core growth was +9.9% in E&C (volume +7.2%, price +2.7%) against +5.6% in SP&T (volume +4.4%, price +1.2%); in Q2 the split was +8.5% versus +3.1%. Envista is taking nearly three points of price in imaging and consumables and barely one point in premium implants and aligners. Management's Q1 characterization — ortho consumables and diagnostics up double digits, implants up mid-single digits — matches.

Earnings growth beyond revenue depends on margin recovery. Getting adjusted EBITDA margin from 13.7% back to 17% on roughly $3.0B of sales would produce approximately $510M of EBITDA against $372.6M in FY2025, and plausibly $1.85-2.00 of adjusted EPS with continued buyback. Two mechanical helpers exist: SP&T's purchased-intangible amortization steps down from $63.6M in 2026 to $20.0M by 2029, and the diluted share count is falling (171.7M weighted average in H1 2025 to 165.3M in H1 2026).

The near-term constraint is the implied H2 profile. H1 2026 adjusted EPS was $0.77 ($0.36 + $0.41). Against the $1.35-1.45 FY2026 guidance reaffirmed in May, that implies H2 of $0.58-0.68, a midpoint roughly 18% below H1. Consensus quarterly estimates of $0.34 (next reporting quarter) and $0.35 sum with H1 to about $1.46 — the top of the guidance range. The step-down itself is therefore already in consensus, explained by the six-day Q4 calendar headwind, non-recurrence of the tariff refund and continued growth investment. The residual risk is that consensus sits at the optimistic end of guidance and depends partly on further Phase 2 tariff refunds whose timing Envista cannot forecast (recognition occurs only on receipt). Whether guidance was raised alongside the August 5 print could not be verified; if it was, the implied H2 decline narrows and this concern eases materially.

## Competitive Landscape

The most revealing structural fact is that the premium-sounding segment earns lower margins than the commodity-sounding one, in every recent period: SP&T versus E&C operating margins of 14.1%/16.9% (FY2023), 5.6%/17.0% (FY2024), 10.9%/16.3% (FY2025) and 12.8%/17.7% (Q2 2026). Part of the gap is acquisition accounting — $86.0M of FY2025 D&A sat in SP&T against $28.2M in E&C — and that burden mechanically declines through 2029. The rest reflects the Spark aligner build-out (manufacturing scale-up, direct orthodontist selling) and premium-implant price pressure from value-tier competitors and China's volume-based procurement regime, which the filings describe as having "resulted in significant price reductions for medical and dental consumables." China revenue has been flat-to-down: $205.7M (FY2023), $193.5M (FY2024), $194.4M (FY2025).

The channel is the more pressing competitive issue. Largest-customer share of sales has risen from 10% (FY2023) to 10% (FY2024) to 12% (FY2025) to 13% (Q2 and H1 2026, versus 11% a year earlier). FY2025 risk factors identify the customer as Henry Schein and disclose that no master distribution agreement covers the U.S. and Canada. Henry Schein also competes in adjacent categories. Rising share with a large distributor can reflect Envista winning shelf space rather than growing dependence, and without Henry Schein's purchase mix both readings are available. The measured concentration has nonetheless worsened for three consecutive reporting periods, and geographic diversification does not offset it — those are different measures. The 10-Q's own forward-looking risk list names "loss of a key distributor" and "fluctuations in inventory of our distributors," which indicates where management's concern sits.

A related datapoint: the January 2022 RSU agreement with Pacific Dental Services, awarding a dental support organization $12.5M of Envista equity (273,522 units) vesting on performance milestones, of which 182,348 had vested by December 31, 2025. The amount is immaterial; the structure is evidence that Envista pays equity consideration to hold large DSO relationships. Buyer concentration is tightening at both ends of the channel.

Competitive position score: 5.5. E&C is strengthening (price +2.7%, volume +7.2%, durable operating leverage); SP&T — nearly two-thirds of revenue and the segment that should carry the moat — is growing +3.1% core with 1.2% price and under structural pricing pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**Earnings-quality dependence on non-recurring items.** The Q2 beat leaned on a $12.6M tariff refund and a $3.2M property gain. Phase 2 refunds may repeat, but recognition is on receipt and unforecastable. The forward tariff position is unresolved: the 10-Q states that although IEEPA tariffs were invalidated in February 2026, "the U.S. Administration initiated new tariffs on all imports, subject to certain exceptions, and may impose additional tariffs," and concedes cost of sales has already risen on higher tariffs. With manufacturing in Sweden, the Czech Republic, China and Mexico, the exposure is structural.

**Goodwill against $110M of tangible book.** Goodwill of $2,358.2M is 41.5% of assets and indefinite-lived intangibles are another 6.4%. Ernst & Young flagged both goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible impairment as Critical Audit Matters in the FY2025 audit, citing discount-rate inputs. Cumulative impairments already taken total $1,172.8M of goodwill plus $147.1M of trade names and $92.2M of finite-lived assets. A further write-down would compress reported equity materially while leaving cash, EBITDA-based covenants and cash taxes unaffected.

**2028 refinancing concentration.** Every dollar of the $1.46B stack matures in 2028, with the springing acceleration on the credit facilities. Blended cash interest was $46.1M on ~$1.45B in FY2025 (~3.2%). The 1.75% converts are not replicable at current rates unless the stock rises toward the $46.31 conversion price (about 66% above the current basis). Refinancing the converts near 6% would add roughly $21M pretax, and the 2.98% euro term loan adds further pressure — plausibly $25-30M of incremental pretax interest, or $0.12-0.14 per share after tax. This is a 2027-2028 decision with no liquidity pressure before then, but it caps the terminal earnings power the bull case can assume.

**Demand discretionary and out-of-pocket.** The 10-Q states dental costs are largely out-of-pocket, so utilization varies with economic growth. Implants and aligners sit at the discretionary end. Deferral of elective dental spending in an economic slowdown is a live risk that a 20x multiple does not obviously discount. [unverified — the specific macro figures (core PCE at 3.3%, mortgage rates at one-year highs, three hawkish Fed dissents) are not traceable to the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

**Aligner revenue-recognition estimate risk.** Usage-rate revisions shift the pattern, not the cash. A favorable revision creates a one-time revenue tailwind and an unfavorable one the reverse — a source of core-growth noise that outside models cannot anticipate.

**Dilution — reported versus contingent.** Reported diluted shares were 164.1M in Q2 2026. The 2028 converts represent a maximum of 10.80M shares (6.7% of the 160.6M outstanding, 6.3% post-issuance ownership dilution), but the company settles principal in cash and issues shares only for conversion value above principal; the if-converted value was below principal at both December 31, 2025 and July 3, 2026, so no shares are currently issuable. Equity-plan overhang (7.7M options at a $24.25 weighted-average strike, 3.2M unvested RSUs/PSUs) belongs in scenario analysis given undisclosed performance conditions.

**Unresolved disclosure items.** The Q2 10-Q directs readers to risk factors added or modified in the Q1 2026 10-Q, which could not be retrieved; a tariff-related revision is the likely subject but is unverified. Three Item 5.02 filings (July 16, 2025, September 17, 2025, June 3, 2026) are also unretrieved. Faez Kaabi signed the Q2 10-Q as Chief Accounting Officer and Eric Hammes appears as CFO in February 2026 Form 4s, so there is no evidence of CEO or CFO turnover, but the June 2026 change is unidentified.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility rose from 50.2% to 59.1% (+8.9 points) between July 19 and the August 6 snapshot while the price rose 7.1%. IV expanding through an earnings print, rather than collapsing after it, is the notable feature: the options market is pricing more forward uncertainty after the Q2 report than before it, which is consistent with unresolved questions about H2 guidance, tariff-refund recurrence and the Q4 calendar headwind. Short interest of 6.5% of float and 3.4 days to cover is unremarkable and provides no directional information. Neither signal contradicts the fundamental read; the IV expansion corroborates the view that the quality of the beat, not its headline size, is the open question.

Risk factors score: 5.0. The individual risks are identifiable and mostly disclosed, but the combination of goodwill concentration against minimal tangible equity, a single-year refinancing wall, rising distributor concentration without a master agreement, and earnings that currently depend on non-recurring tariff recoveries is more idiosyncratic risk than a 20x multiple compensates for.

## Management Quality

Capital allocation is the strongest element of the file. A $250M repurchase authorization from February 2025 was supplemented by a new $300M program on May 5, 2026 running to December 31, 2029. Cumulative repurchases through July 3, 2026 total $267.1M for 13.2M shares. FY2025 buying averaged roughly $18.03 (9.2M shares for $165.9M); H1 2026 averaged roughly $25.30 (4.0M shares for $101.2M), with Q2 detail showing 2.44M shares at about $24.01 — including 1.41M shares at $23.83 in May. Buying meaningfully below the current price is evidence of price discipline rather than mechanical execution. Shares outstanding fell from 172.2M to 163.8M to 160.6M, a 6.7% reduction in eighteen months. There is no dividend. M&A has been small and adjacent: Versah LLC (osseodensification burs) closed February 2, 2026 for ~$54.7M cash, immaterial for pro forma purposes and contributing +0.6% to H1 reported growth.

Incentive design is appropriate for the situation: long-term metrics are core sales growth, free cash flow and adjusted EBITDA margin, with adjusted EBITDA as the company-selected measure — paying for organic growth, cash conversion and margin repair rather than for a reported EPS line distorted by tax and impairment noise. CEO "compensation actually paid" was negative in 2022 (−$4.5M) and 2023 (−$1.8M) before recovering to $4.2M in 2024 and $14.3M in 2025, so the equity-heavy structure does transmit shareholder outcomes.

Against that: the five-year TSR record is poor. Indexed to 100, Envista's cumulative TSR ran 134 (2021), 100 (2022), 71 (2023), 57 (2024), 64 (2025) against a peer group at 136 — a 72-point relative shortfall. CEO Paul Keel's FY2025 summary compensation of $10.56M and compensation actually paid of $14.25M were earned in a year of $47.0M reported net income with the TSR index 36% below its baseline, which will draw criticism regardless of the calculation's mechanical accuracy.

Insider activity offers no positive confirmation. Form 4 filings through May 2026 show no open-market purchases by any officer or director: February 25, 2026 annual grants at a $29.59 reference price, routine Code-F tax withholdings (Keel disposed 54,097 shares at $20.35 in November 2025 and 12,811 at $23.43 in May 2026, holding 371,574 after), director grants of 9,330 shares each in May 2026, and one discretionary transaction — SVP Strategy Mischa Reis exercised 9,675 options and sold all of them at $30.00 on February 10, 2026, retaining 32,382 shares. That single modest sale by a non-CEO/CFO officer carries little signal. The absence of buying while the company itself repurchased 2.44M shares at $23.54-$26.37 is a mild negative on conviction, tempered by blackout windows around the May 6 earnings release and May 21 annual meeting, and by the 10-Q's confirmation that no 10b5-1 plans were adopted, modified or terminated in Q2.

Controls are clean: ICFR effective at December 31, 2025 with an unqualified E&Y opinion, disclosure controls effective at July 3, 2026, no restatements, and gross unrecognized tax benefits of only $2.2M — unusually low for this degree of cross-border structuring.

Management quality score: 5.8, up modestly on demonstrated buyback price discipline and well-chosen incentive metrics, held below 6.5 by the absence of any insider purchase, the five-year relative TSR record, and pay levels that outran results in 2025.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $36 (+29%)

Margin recovery continues on its own momentum: E&C's operating leverage (+530bp gross contribution in Q2 before growth investment) persists, SP&T's amortization burden steps down from $63.6M to $20.0M by 2029, and adjusted EBITDA margin reaches 16-17% on roughly $3.0-3.1B of sales, producing $480-510M of EBITDA. Buyback retires 4-5% of shares annually under the $282.9M remaining authorization. FY2027-2028 adjusted EPS reaches $1.85-2.00, and a 19-20x multiple gets to $36. Supporting evidence: four consecutive beats of 13-20%, Q4 2025 core growth of 10.8%, and a management incentive structure aligned to exactly these outcomes. Phase 2 tariff refunds provide incremental near-term cushion.

### Bear Case — $21 (−25%)

The FY2026 guidance step-down proves binding rather than conservative: Q4's six-day calendar headwind, the non-recurrence of the $12.6M refund and continued growth investment push full-year adjusted EPS to the $1.35-1.40 low end against consensus at $1.45. SP&T core growth stays near 3% as China VBP and value-tier implant competition compress price, and emerging markets remain flat. A discretionary-spending slowdown in the U.S. — 47% of sales, with dental costs largely out-of-pocket — pulls core growth to 3%. A goodwill review under higher discount rates produces a write-down against $110M of tangible book. The multiple compresses to 14x on $1.45-1.50, giving roughly $21, which matches the published analyst low.

### Base Case — $31 (+11%)

Core growth of 5-6% with a modest FX tailwind, adjusted EBITDA margin advancing 75-125bp per year toward 15%, FY2027 adjusted EPS of $1.60 (a touch above the $1.57 consensus, reflecting share retirement), capitalized at 19.5x: $31.2. Free cash flow normalizes toward $260-290M, a 5.8-6.5% yield, funding continued repurchase. This assumes no further large one-off gains in reported results and no impairment.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive but modest — roughly 11% to base case against 25% to bear case. Existing holders are compensated by margin recovery and a shrinking share count; new capital has better entry points available.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Confirmed FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised above $1.45**, with the raise explained by underlying operating performance rather than tariff-refund receipts. This resolves the largest open item and would validate consensus sitting at the top of the prior range.
2. **Adjusted EBITDA margin of 15.5% or better for two consecutive quarters, with gross margin expanding excluding tariff refunds.** The specific test is gross margin above 54.2% (the Q2 2025 base) on a refund-adjusted basis, which Q2 2026 failed at 54.0%.
3. **Price at or below $24.00** with fundamentals intact — roughly the level at which the company itself repurchased 1.41M shares in May 2026, and a level that would put the stock at 16.6x next-year consensus with base-case upside near 30%.
4. **SP&T core growth re-accelerating above 6% with price contribution above 2%**, evidencing that premium-implant and aligner pricing pressure is easing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Full-year adjusted EPS guidance cut below $1.35**, or two consecutive quarters of core growth below 3%.
2. **A goodwill or indefinite-lived intangible impairment**, which against ~$110M of tangible book would signal that the discount-rate and cash-flow assumptions E&Y flagged as Critical Audit Matters have broken.
3. **Loss or material renegotiation of the Henry Schein relationship**, or largest-customer concentration rising above 15% without a master U.S./Canada distribution agreement.
4. **A 2027 refinancing executed at a blended cost above 6.5%**, or any covenant-driven amendment, which would transfer more than $0.14 of annual EPS to lenders.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (late October 2026) is the first clean test — it carries no calendar distortion (91 days in both years), so reported core growth will be a fair read on underlying demand, and the gross-margin line will show whether the refund-adjusted 54.0% was a floor. FY2027 guidance in February 2027 is the second checkpoint, and the refinancing decision should surface during 2027.

## Conclusion

Envista has done the harder part of a turnaround: it has stabilized revenue after the 2024 decline, rebuilt 190bp of adjusted EBITDA margin, held net leverage near 0.6-0.8x, retired 6.7% of its shares at average prices below today's level, and aligned executive pay to core growth, free cash flow and margin. Four consecutive double-digit percentage beats are not an accident.

What holds the rating at HOLD for a fifth time is the composition of those results at the current price. Roughly 69-75% of the Q2 adjusted EPS beat traces to a $12.6M tariff refund that Envista recognizes only on receipt; excluding it, gross margin contracted about 20bp year over year and operating margin expansion was 200-250bp rather than 420bp. FY2025's 6.5% core growth included an unquantified aligner deferred-revenue timing component. Reported FY2025 EPS of $0.28 reflects a disclosed 73.5% tax rate rather than earning power. The premium two-thirds of the portfolio is growing at 3% core with 1.2% price while the commodity third grows at 8.5% with 2.7% price. Distributor concentration has increased in each of the last three reporting periods to 13% with no master agreement. At 20.0x guidance midpoint, 17.8x next-year consensus and 12.9x EV/EBITDA, with the stock at 79% of its 52-week range, the price already reflects the visible recovery and leaves 11% of base-case upside against a 25% bear case anchored at the published analyst low. Own it if you hold it; add toward $24, or on a guidance raise that is demonstrably operational rather than a tariff recovery.

**Overall score: 5.8 / 10. HOLD.**
