**Research Date: June 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nucor is in the middle of a sharp, verifiable cyclical earnings recovery. First-quarter 2026 net income rose to $743M ($3.23 diluted EPS) from $156M ($0.67) a year earlier, gross margin jumped to 16% from 8%, and mills ran at 86% utilization on record quarterly shipments. Management guided Q2 2026 EPS to $4.70–$4.80 (roughly 80% YoY growth, excluding a non-cash Helion investment mark), implying an annualized run-rate near $19 against $7.52 of full-year 2025 EPS. Consensus estimates have chased the improvement upward: current-year EPS is now $16.88, revised +35.9% over 90 days, with the next quarter revised +51.1% over 90 days. That combination of accelerating estimates and a real volume/price inflection is a legitimate positive signal.

The mechanism behind the recovery is specific and largely policy-driven. Full reinstatement of Section 232 tariffs in 2025 (no exclusions, broadened to ~600 fabricated products) plus 142 active AD/CVD orders cut the imported share of the US finished-steel market from over 22% in early 2025 to roughly 15% in Q1 2026. That import displacement, combined with higher mill utilization and lower per-ton conversion costs, drove the metal-margin expansion. The recovery is real, but it is disproportionately a trade-policy beat rather than a pure end-demand beat, which is the crux of the risk assessment. Rate-sensitive markets (auto, residential) remain soft; the demand pillars are data centers, infrastructure, energy, and reshoring.

We maintain a HOLD. The balance sheet is the strongest in North American steel (A-/A-/A3, funded-debt-to-capital ~24% against a 60% covenant), the West Virginia mega-mill capex cliff sets up a meaningful 2026–2027 FCF step-up, and a new $4.0B buyback (~8% of market cap) signals management conviction. But the stock has already re-rated substantially off its 52-week low of $128.46 and sits below its 52-week high of $270.90, the earnings power is levered to reversible tariffs, and Materials is a sector where our selection framework demands a higher company-specific bar than Nucor currently clears at $223.

## Company Overview

Nucor is North America's largest and most diversified steel producer and its largest recycler, processing roughly 20 million gross tons of scrap in 2025. It runs a 100% electric-arc-furnace (EAF) mini-mill model that melts scrap, giving it lower fixed costs, greater production flexibility, and roughly one-third the greenhouse-gas intensity of integrated blast-furnace steelmaking. The carbon advantage is increasingly monetizable through branded low-carbon lines (ECONIQ, AEOS, ELCYON).

The business runs in three segments:
- **Steel Mills (~62% of external sales):** sheet (85% under 6–12 month contracts), bar, structural (Nucor-Yamato is the only North American producer of high-strength low-alloy beams), and plate. Largest and most cyclical.
- **Steel Products (~32%):** the higher-value "Expand Beyond" portfolio — Vulcraft joist/deck (#1 in the US), rebar fabrication, tubular, metal buildings, insulated panels, overhead doors (C.H.I./Rytec), data-center racking, and utility towers. Designed for higher ROIC and lower earnings volatility.
- **Raw Materials (~6%):** DJJ (largest NA ferrous scrap broker), two DRI plants (~3.3M metric tons/yr), natural gas production, and industrial gases.

Vertical integration into raw materials is a core structural advantage: it gives Nucor control over its single largest cost (scrap and scrap substitutes at ~$392–403/gross ton) and lets it shift metallic mix as prime scrap tightens. The company employs ~33,000 non-union teammates under a decentralized, pay-for-performance culture that also functions as a natural cost-flex mechanism through cycles.

## Financial Analysis

The financial arc is a textbook cyclical trough-and-recovery.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $41.5B | $34.7B | $30.7B | $32.5B (+6%) |
| Net income | $7.6B | $4.5B | $2.03B | $1.74B |
| Gross margin | ~30% | ~22.5% | 13% | 12% |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $8.46 | $7.52 |
| ROE | ~47% | 23% | 9.8% | 8.5% |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | $3.98B | $3.23B |

FY2025 revenue rose 6% on 7% higher shipments (mill utilization improved to 83% from 76%) even as average price per ton dipped 2%. The earnings decline that year came from Steel Products margin compression (joist/deck, buildings, rebar), where pricing lagged higher steel input costs. Steel Mills earnings rose on higher metal margins and volume, and mill backlog hit a historically high $3.35B versus $2.13B a year earlier, a leading indicator that flowed through in Q1 2026.

In Q1 2026, Steel Mills pre-tax earnings nearly quintupled to $1,128M from $231M on record quarterly shipments, a 14% rise in average mill price to $1,074/ton, and lower conversion costs. The earnings surprise history is mixed on a quarterly basis (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters), but the Q1 beat was large (+13.5%), and the Q3'25 beat (+19.1%) shows the model has upside torque as margins recover.

The balance sheet is the clearest strength. Current ratio 2.9; Altman Z of 2.99 in the safe zone; cash and short-term investments of $2.48B at Q1'26. Long-term debt of $6.9B is well-laddered with only $66M due in 2026. During 2025 Nucor refinanced $1B of maturing notes at 4.65% (2030) and 5.10% (2035) and issued $220M of 40-year West Virginia IDRBs. The credit profile lets Nucor invest counter-cyclically and buy back stock without covenant stress.

Capital allocation has been disciplined through the trough. Nucor invested ~$8.9–9.7B over three years (91% capex, dominated by the ~$4B West Virginia sheet mill, net outlay ~$3.65B, completing end-2026). Capex steps down to ~$2.5B in 2026 from $3.42B in 2025, a meaningful FCF tailwind as the mega-project winds down. The company returned ~$1.2B to shareholders in 2025 ($512M dividends + $700M buybacks, the latter cut sharply from $2.22B in 2024 to preserve the balance sheet during the trough) and delivered its 53rd consecutive annual dividend increase. In February 2026 the board authorized the new $4.0B buyback replacing prior programs, a credible signal of confidence in normalized FCF.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth signal is strong and specific. Estimate revisions are the standout data point: current-quarter EPS revised +28.9% over 90 days, next-year +24.8% to $17.06. This is the magnitude of revision velocity that typically precedes continued re-rating as consensus catches up to a genuine inflection. Combined with Q1 net income up ~4.8x YoY, the near-term earnings trajectory is unambiguously positive.

The durability question sits underneath. The revenue base is levered to nonresidential construction, autos, appliances, and energy. Interest-rate-sensitive end markets remain soft, which caps the demand-led component of the recovery. The offsetting growth pillars are data-center construction (Nucor supplies racking, structural steel, and utility towers directly into that buildout), infrastructure spending, reshoring/advanced manufacturing, and low-carbon steel demand. The data-center demand vector is corroborated across the broader industrial complex, which supports its durability more than a single-company claim would.

Two structural growth catalysts stand out. The West Virginia sheet mill (3M tons, completing end-2026) adds new earnings power in 2027+ just as capex declines, a rare combination of volume growth and FCF unlock. The "Expand Beyond" strategy into higher-value, less-cyclical products (doors, panels, racking, towers) is a deliberate margin-stabilization play, though it carries integration and margin-lag risk as demonstrated by 2025's Steel Products compression. The Nucor Steel Lexington rebar micro-mill opened May 2026, adding infrastructure-linked capacity.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing EPS of $7.52, Nucor trades at ~22.7x, but that is a trough-earnings multiple and largely meaningless given the inflection. The relevant metrics are forward. On current-year consensus of $16.88, the stock trades at ~13.2x; on next-year $17.06, ~13.1x; and on the ~$19 annualized run-rate implied by Q2 guidance, closer to ~11.8x. Those are reasonable, not stretched, multiples for a franchise growing off a trough.

Peer context is mixed and requires care because the steel group spans wildly different quality. Peer median P/E is 29.9x (Steel Dynamics), with Reliance at 26.8x, Commercial Metals at 17.4x, and Cleveland-Cliffs negative. On trailing numbers Nucor looks cheap versus STLD/RS; on forward numbers the gap narrows because those peers are also being valued on recovering earnings. Analyst targets — mean $260.76, median $272, high $290, low $138.17 — sit above the current $223, and the recent KeyBanc upgrade to Overweight with a $274 target reflects the same recovery thesis. On an inflection stock with rising estimates and a sub-15x forward multiple, those targets are more floor than ceiling.

The reason we do not score valuation higher despite the cheap-looking forward multiple is threefold. First, the "cheap" signal in the current regime has been a reliable value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, particularly in commodity sectors, and Nucor's cheapness is a direct function of investors correctly discounting cyclical and policy-reversal risk into the earnings. Second, the forward EPS that makes the stock look inexpensive is disproportionately a Section 232 tariff artifact; normalizing for a partial import-share reversion produces lower mid-cycle earnings and a higher multiple. Third, the stock has already captured the bulk of the re-rating. We assign a 5.5: the forward math is supportive, but the earnings base carries a policy-dependent quality discount that keeps this from being an asymmetric-upside setup.

## Competitive Landscape

Nucor is the low-cost, most-flexible domestic producer with the strongest balance sheet in the sector. It competes against integrated producers (Cleveland-Cliffs, U.S. Steel) burdened with higher fixed costs, other EAF mini-mills (Steel Dynamics, its closest structural comp), imports, and substitute materials. Its cost leadership stems from the EAF model, vertical integration into scrap (DJJ) and scrap substitutes (DRI), scale, product breadth, and logistics from a distributed micro-mill footprint.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin. The import-share decline directly benefits domestic producers, and Nucor's low-carbon profile is increasingly advantaged as Buy Clean standards and EPD requirements spread. The "Expand Beyond" portfolio is capturing structural share in data-center and utility-infrastructure niches. Against that, the dominant secular headwind is global overcapacity: the OECD estimates ~704M net tons of excess capacity in 2025 (8x total US production), projected to grow ~20% to ~795M tons by 2027, with China producing over 1B tons and exporting a record 131M tons in 2025. Circumvention through Chinese-built capacity in Southeast Asia and Africa is a growing evasion channel that trade barriers address imperfectly. Nucor's competitive position is strong and improving within its protected domestic market, but the moat is being reinforced by a policy wall rather than a widening cost advantage over global supply.

## Risk Assessment

The central idiosyncratic risk is that the earnings recovery is policy-dependent and therefore reversible. The margin expansion traces directly to the import-share decline, which traces to Section 232 durability. Any relaxation, expiration, legal challenge, or a stronger dollar could reverse the import trend quickly, and consensus estimates would then have to unwind the increases they just added. A genuine but policy-contingent beat priced as if it were a demand-led secular recovery is the reason the risk score is 5.0 rather than higher.

Secondary risks: (1) global overcapacity structurally rising, which caps pricing power over the medium term regardless of tariffs; (2) scrap price volatility and pass-through lag, which compressed Steel Products margins in 2025; (3) energy cost and GHG regulatory exposure, partially hedged by the low-carbon profile; (4) impairment risk — $4.3B goodwill plus $2.9B intangibles, with the NJSM Mexico JV having tripped an impairment triggering event in Q4 2025 (it passed only the undiscounted-cash-flow test, not fair value, so a forecast downgrade could force a charge); and (5) an orderly-but-notable CFO transition to Jack Sullivan, worth monitoring for capital-allocation continuity. The Helion fusion mark that flatters Q2 headline EPS is a non-cash gain on a minority venture stake; investors should focus on the ex-Helion operating number.

Insider activity is neutral-to-mildly-cautious: routine grants and tax-withholding across the executive team near $250, EVP option exercise-and-sell (~$2.7M) and an EVP sale (~$3.4M), with no open-market conviction buying to corroborate the bullish setup. This does not contradict the estimate revisions, but it also does not add a positive management signal.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not flashing stress. Near-term ATM IV of 35.9% is modestly rich versus 1-year historical vol of 30.2%, but sits at only the 4th percentile within the current chain, and put/call skew is balanced at 2.6%. The put/call volume ratio (0.41) and OI ratio (0.27) are call-tilted, consistent with bullish-to-neutral positioning rather than hedging. IV rose 1pp while price fell 8.1% since late May, a mild uptick in priced uncertainty but nothing that overrides the fundamental read. The flat term structure suggests the market is not pricing a discrete near-term catalyst. Net: options confirm a constructive-to-neutral stance, consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$290–310):** Section 232 holds and demand pillars (data centers, infrastructure, reshoring) firm up. Nucor sustains mid-cycle EPS near $18–19, capex falls to ~$2.5B, the West Virginia mill ramps, and the ~$4B buyback shrinks the share count materially. At ~16x $18–19 EPS the stock reaches $290–310, roughly matching the analyst high and above the median target. FCF step-up funds continued dividend growth (54th year) and aggressive repurchase.

**Bear Case (~$150–165, ~26–33% downside):** Tariffs relax or a stronger dollar erodes the import advantage; import share drifts back toward 20%+, metal margins compress, and China overcapacity continues to grow. Mid-cycle EPS normalizes toward $10–12, the NJSM JV takes a goodwill charge, and the stock de-rates to a low-double-digit multiple on lower earnings. This is the scenario the current "cheap" forward multiple is partially discounting.

**Base Case (~$255):** Tariffs largely persist, demand stays mixed (soft rate-sensitive markets, firm data-center/infrastructure), and Nucor earns ~$16–17 in 2026 stepping toward $17+ in 2027 as WV ramps. At ~15x forward EPS the stock reaches ~$255, roughly 14% above current, in line with the mean analyst target. The buyback and rising FCF support the multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is real — inflecting earnings, rising estimates, a capex cliff unlocking FCF, and a large buyback — but the recovery's dependence on reversible trade policy and the substantial run off the lows keep the risk/reward from clearing the bar for a fresh BUY in a structurally negative-alpha sector. This is a hold on the appreciation case, not on yield (the 0.9% dividend is modest).

- **Base case target:** ~$255 (15x ~$17 forward EPS), ~14% above the current $223.
- **Bull case target:** ~$290–310 (16x $18–19 EPS), ~30–39% upside.
- **Bear case target:** ~$150–165, ~26–33% downside.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, keyed to the Q2 2026 print (already guided), the second-half 2026 tariff/import-share data, and the West Virginia mill completion at year-end 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2/Q3 2026 prints confirm import share stabilizing at or below ~15% with metal margins holding, demonstrating the recovery has demand support beyond the initial tariff step-change.
2. A pullback to the low-to-mid $190s or below on unchanged/rising estimates, restoring asymmetric upside (forward P/E toward ~11x).
3. Evidence of open-market insider buying, which would corroborate the fundamental inflection.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Any material relaxation, legal reversal, or expiration of Section 232, or import share drifting back above ~20%.
2. Estimate revisions turning negative (current-year consensus falling below ~$15) or a Q2/Q3 miss that signals margin peaking.
3. A confirmed NJSM (or other) goodwill impairment charge, indicating deteriorating segment forecasts.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, followed by second-half import-share and tariff-policy developments.

## Conclusion

Nucor is executing a genuine cyclical recovery from a well-defined trough, with the strongest balance sheet in North American steel, a disciplined capital-allocation record, and a near-term earnings trajectory backed by rapidly rising consensus estimates. The forward multiple is not demanding for the growth on offer, and analyst targets sit above the current price. Those are the reasons this is a HOLD with a positive growth lean rather than an AVOID.

The reasons it is not a BUY are equally concrete. The margin recovery is disproportionately a Section 232 tariff outcome rather than a demand-led one, which makes the impressive forward EPS carry a policy-reversal discount that the cheap-looking multiple is correctly reflecting. The stock has already re-rated substantially off its low and sits in a sector where company-specific outperformance has been hard to find. Global overcapacity continues to build, and insiders are taking profits near highs rather than buying. We would turn constructive on a pullback into the low-$190s that restores asymmetry, or on second-half data confirming the recovery has demand legs beyond the tariff step-change. Until then, hold for the growth trajectory while monitoring the trade-policy linchpin.
