## Executive Summary

Diageo has stopped being an income compounder and become a leveraged operational turnaround. The FY2026 preliminary results released on 6 August 2026 showed net sales of $19,643m, down 3% reported and 2% organic on volumes down 0.4%, the fourth consecutive year of flat-to-declining reported revenue. Reported operating profit was approximately $3,156m, down 27% year on year and down 47% from FY2024's $6,001m, against a headline of "organic operating profit +2%." The gap is exceptional charges, disposals and FX, including $0.8bn of cost incurred in FY2026 on a new $1.2bn two-year restructuring programme that management says represents about 70% of the programme's total cost. The dividend was halved in February 2026 (from $1.035 to $0.500 per ordinary share, or $2.00 per ADR), and buybacks have now been zero for two consecutive years after roughly $5.6bn of repurchases across FY2022-FY2024.

The composition of the decline is worse than the aggregate. North America, Diageo's largest profit pool, fell 8.4% organically on tequila weakness, and APAC fell 6.3% partly on Chinese government policy affecting white spirits in the consolidated Sichuan Swellfun subsidiary. Growth came from Africa (+13.3%), LAC (+7.7%) and Europe (+3.4%), which are smaller, higher-FX-volatility, lower-absolute-profit bases whose growth mechanically increases translation drag. Within the portfolio, the growth engine is Guinness (+10.9% organic in H1 FY26 with share gains in its three largest markets) and RTD (+17% in H1), while spirits gross sales have fallen 4.6% from the FY2023 peak. Group gross margin has eroded 190bps since FY2022 to 59.5%, and Sir Dave Lewis is now deliberately repositioning price points downward on aspirational brands such as Casamigos.

At $96.35 the ADR capitalises roughly $53.7bn of equity and $75.8bn of enterprise value, or about 15x an estimated FY2026 underlying EPS of $6.00-6.60 per ADR and roughly a 5.5% free cash flow yield on derived FCF of $2.9-3.2bn. That is a genuine discount to Diageo's own history and defensible against global spirits peers. The market has assigned that discount for identifiable reasons: FY2027 organic net sales are guided "broadly flat," the medium-term ambition to 2029 is only low-single-digit growth, estimated leverage sits near the top of a 2.5-3.0x style range, tangible book value is negative $2,504m, and none of the FY2026 income statement, balance sheet or cash flow figures used here were read from a primary filing, because the FY2026 20-F has not been filed and EDGAR retrieval failed. The stock has already risen roughly 9% in three sessions on the cost plan and about 33% from the 52-week low of $72.45 (reached 24 March 2026). We see roughly 9% to base case against 22% to bear case. HOLD, classified as uncertain: the central question of whether US spirits weakness is an affordability cycle or a category problem is not resolvable from the available evidence, and it determines whether the cost programme fixes the business or merely funds a slower decline.

## Company Overview

Diageo is the largest international premium spirits company by net sales, with Johnnie Walker, Don Julio, Casamigos, Smirnoff, Cîroc, Captain Morgan, Tanqueray, Gordon's and Crown Royal in spirits; Guinness in beer; and a growing RTD portfolio led by Smirnoff. It reports five regions: North America, Europe, Asia Pacific, Latin America & Caribbean, and Africa. It also holds a 34% equity-accounted stake in Moët Hennessy as a minority shareholder rather than operator, which is the principal component of the $5,305m long-term investments line and appears to contribute roughly $250-300m of annual associate income.

Two structural features are frequently lost in summary data. First, the wedge between gross and net sales is very large: FY2026 product-line gross sales sum to $27,762m against net sales of $19,643m, a difference of $8,119m or 29.2%. Excise duty is the dominant component, which makes alcohol duty policy a P&L-level variable rather than a footnote; a 1pp change in the effective take, unmitigated by price, is worth roughly $278m of net sales, comparable to a third of the new annual savings target.

Second, a meaningful slice of the group does not belong to Diageo shareholders. Non-controlling interests hold $2,083m of the $12,954m of total equity, reflecting consolidated majority-owned listed subsidiaries: United Spirits (India), Sichuan Swellfun (China), Guinness Nigeria and East African Breweries. Reported group net income of $1,958m against reported EPS of $3.12 per ADR on approximately 558m ADRs implies roughly $1,740m attributable to Diageo shareholders, with about 11% of group earnings accruing to minorities. That matters for the Royal Challengers Bengaluru disposal discussed below, since it was held through United Spirits.

The durable competitive assets are real: $10,529m of maturing inventory (22.5% of total assets) that cannot be replicated on demand, Scotch category leadership, and Guinness, which delivered eight consecutive half-years of double-digit growth through H1 FY2025 and grew 10.9% organically in H1 FY2026.

FY2024 and FY2025 show positive organic growth against negative reported growth, meaning FX and portfolio were persistent drags. In FY2026 both turned negative. The regional split (NAM −8.4%, APAC −6.3%, Europe +3.4%, LAC +7.7%, Africa +13.3%) means the aggregate 2% decline understates the damage in the profit pool that matters most.

### Margins

| FY | Gross margin | Reported operating income ($m) | Operating margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 61.4% | 5,897 | 28.7% |
| 2023 | 59.7% | 5,547 | 27.0% |
| 2024 | 60.2% | 6,001 | 29.6% |
| 2025 | 60.1% | 4,335 | 21.4% |
| 2026 | **59.5%** | **3,156** | **16.1%** |

Gross margin erosion of 190bps over four years is modest. The collapse is below the gross line: reported operating profit has fallen 47% in two years while management reported organic operating profit of −0.7% in FY2025 and +2% in FY2026. Only one large component is identified, the $0.8bn FY2026 programme cost, which does not bridge the $1.2bn FY2026 decline and explains nothing of the $1.7bn FY2024-to-FY2025 decline.

We flag this as an unresolved gap rather than asserting impairment. Other intangible assets fell from $14,776m to $13,375m in FY2026, a $1,401m reduction consistent with impairment but equally consistent with disposals or currency translation of non-USD brand assets. Separately, the reported D&A line rises from $493m (FY2024) to $1,718m (FY2025) to $2,480m (FY2026) against roughly flat PP&E, which is not plausible as true depreciation and almost certainly absorbs non-cash charges. Any EBITDA built from that line is unusable, which is why we have not used it for leverage.

The practical consequence: reported earnings, reported book value and any EBIT-based screen, including the Z-Score below, are depressed relative to underlying economics. Anchoring on the 31.1x reported P/E is wrong. So is anchoring on "+2% organic" while ignoring $1.2bn of real cost.

### Cash flow

Operating cash flow rose 2.2% to $4,392m in FY2026, holding up while reported operating profit fell 27%, consistent with the profit decline being substantially non-cash. FY2026 capex was not populated in the available dataset, which is why the widely circulated FY2026 free cash flow figure of $4,392m (a 22.4% FCF margin, 10.2x P/FCF) is an artefact and should be discarded. Using either the half-year capex run rate doubled ($1,210m) or the FY2023-FY2025 average ($1,513m), FY2026 free cash flow lands in a $2.9-3.2bn range, an FCF margin of roughly 15-16% against FY2025's 13.3%. On $53.7bn of market capitalisation that is a 5.4-6.0% FCF yield. This is genuine improvement, roughly $1.2-1.5bn below the headline a screen would print.

Deleveraging is real and modest: net debt fell $1,557m, funded by net debt repayment of $1,775m, dividends cut to $1,846m from $2,298m, and zero buybacks. Note the definitional caution: $19,991m is total borrowings less cash and will not match Diageo's own reported net borrowings, which includes derivative and lease adjustments.

On leverage, using reported FY2026 EBIT is misleading. Building an underlying operating profit of roughly $5.6bn (FY2024 reported $6,001m, adjusted for organic movements plus disposal and FX drag) plus roughly $1.3bn of true D&A gives underlying EBITDA near $6.9bn and net debt/EBITDA of about 2.9x. That is an inference on an estimated base; a plausible range on Diageo's own definition is 2.6x-3.3x. Interest coverage derives to roughly 3.5x on reported EBIT and 6.3x on estimated underlying EBIT, with net finance charges near $850-890m. Current ratio is 1.60x; short-term debt of $2,449m against $1,520m of cash means continuous reliance on committed facilities and capital-markets access, which is normal for an investment-grade staple but not costless if ratings or refinancing conditions drift. *(We did not verify Diageo's credit ratings and make no claim about them.)*

An Altman Z-Score computed from FY2026 inputs lands at approximately 2.01 (grey zone), rising to about 2.19 if estimated underlying EBIT is substituted. The grey-zone reading is driven principally by deliberate leverage and low asset turnover, both characteristic of a business holding years of maturing stock. It is not evidence of distress. It does say the balance sheet carries less cushion than the "defensive staple" label implies, which is why the board cut the dividend.

**Financial Health: 5.0.** Strong and improving cash conversion, real deleveraging, and coverage that is comfortable on underlying earnings. Against that: leverage at the top of a target range, negative tangible book, refinancing dependence, and the elimination of both discretionary capital-return levers. The balance sheet is serviceable but has no slack.

## Growth Analysis

FY2027 organic net sales are guided "broadly flat," with low-single-digit growth as the medium-term ambition to 2029. That follows four years of flat-to-down reported sales and the abandonment of a long-standing sales target on tariff uncertainty. Earnings growth over the next two years therefore has to come from cost, not volume: the new programme targets $850m of savings over two years (BeverageDaily, quoting Lewis), while Reuters described it as $1bn over three years, and the interaction with a previously announced c.$625m/three-year programme from August 2025 is unreconciled. That difference, roughly $1.0bn versus roughly $1.6bn of gross savings, is material to any FY2028 EPS build and we cannot resolve it. Lewis has also said the savings "will allow us to invest in the turnaround without needing to reduce operating profit," which reads as substantial reinvestment rather than clean flow-through to EPS.

The mix story is genuinely two-sided. Beer gross sales grew from $3,808m (FY2022) to $4,554m (FY2026), up 19.6%, and RTD grew 12.5% in FY2026 alone to $1,113m. Spirits fell 4.6% from the FY2023 peak of $22,855m to $21,805m. Guinness Microdraught is opening new distribution and Smirnoff RTDs gained share in four of five regions including North America. The company does have growing franchises, but they are lower-gross-margin franchises than the premium spirits core that generated its historical pricing power and multiple. We cannot decompose the 190bps gross margin decline between mix, input cost and price repositioning, and note these product lines are stated gross of excise, so mix conclusions on a net-sales basis could differ.

The bull-side growth argument rests on North America being self-inflicted and therefore fixable. Lewis criticised Diageo's own "frankly very poor" service to distributors at the February interims and has framed the priority as "recovering our competitiveness in NAM." If service and price-point coverage are the constraint, an 8.4% decline can reverse quickly. Diageo's own H1 language points to the harder version, "pressure on disposable income, and competitive pressure from more affordable alternatives," which is a share-loss admission that service improvements alone will not reverse.

**Growth Prospects: 3.5.** Guided flat FY2027, four years of no revenue growth, the declining segment is the high-margin one, and near-term earnings improvement depends on a savings programme whose size is not reconciled and whose flow-through management has already partly committed to reinvestment.

## Valuation Assessment

Start with the data quality. The profile shows trailing and forward P/E of 123.7x on EPS of $0.779, which is price divided by a single quarter's earnings and should be ignored entirely. The dividend yield fields are similarly unreliable: the profile shows 1.64%, a trailing DPS of $3.239 per ADR implies 3.34%, and the rebased FY2026 declared rate of $0.500 per ordinary share ($2.00 per ADR) gives 2.08% at $96.35. The forward run-rate is roughly 2%, and DEO is no longer an income holding.

Working from derived figures at the $96.35 basis:

|---|---|

| Market capitalisation | ~$53.7bn |
| Enterprise value (equity + net debt + NCI) | ~$75.8bn |

| Forward dividend yield ($2.00/ADR) | 2.08% |
| Net debt / attributable equity | 1.84x |

The underlying P/E estimate carries the whole cheapness argument, and it is an extrapolation: H1 FY2026 EPS before exceptionals was 95.3 US cents per ordinary share, FY2025 full-year was 164.2c, and we assume FY2026 lands at 150-165c ($6.00-6.60 per ADR). We could not verify the FY2026 full-year before-exceptionals figure. If H2 phasing was weaker than assumed, the multiple is higher than 15x.

Against Diageo's own trading history, 15x is a substantial derating. Against the current global spirits cohort, which has derated broadly through a sector-wide demand downturn, it is closer to a market-clearing multiple than a bargain. The FCF yield of roughly 5.5% is the more robust support, but the cash is committed: deleveraging plus brand reinvestment absorb the roughly $1.19bn per year of released dividend capacity and the roughly $1bn per year of former buyback capacity. There is no near-term mechanism by which that cash returns to shareholders.

Analyst dispersion is wide: mean $104, median $103, low $76, high $136, with sentiment classified as degrading (one strong buy moved to zero, holds from two to three). At $96.35 the stock sits 7-8% below consensus mean. Analyst targets should not be treated as a floor here; that principle applies to names with rising estimates, and Diageo's estimate direction is downward with a flat FY2027 revenue guide. The consensus low of $76 is 4.9% above the 52-week low of $72.45, meaning the bear case is a retest of a level reached roughly four to five months ago, not a hypothetical.

Our scenario set:

- **Base $105 (+9.0%):** roughly 16x FY2027 underlying EPS of $6.40-6.60, assuming NAM stabilises, cost savings offset reinvestment and FX, and net debt falls toward $18bn on RCB proceeds and disposals.
- **Bull $130 (+34.9%):** roughly 19x on $6.80, requiring NAM to return to growth in FY2028, tariffs resolved without a material margin hit, and a Swellfun or Moët Hennessy disposal accelerating deleveraging below 2.5x.
- **Bear $75 (−22.2%):** roughly 13x on $5.60, with continued NAM share loss, an unmitigated tariff cost, further brand impairment against negative tangible book, and price repositioning compressing gross margin below 58%.

Weighting these 40/25/35 gives an expected price return near +5%, plus roughly 2% of dividend. That is approximately fair, not compelling.

**Valuation: 5.5.** Cheap versus its own history and supported by a 5.5% FCF yield, but the discount is earned by declining earnings and an unresolved demand question, and the stock has already captured roughly 33% off the low and 9% in the three sessions since the print. Cheapness without a revenue catalyst is a value-trap configuration, and we are not willing to score it as asymmetric mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The downturn is sector-wide, which matters for attribution: trade coverage describes "a historic downturn in spirits demand," and both of Diageo's categories, spirits and beer, face pressure. Within that, Diageo's relative position is mixed rather than uniformly deteriorating.

Diageo is better than peers in beer. Guinness grew 10.9% organically in H1 FY2026 with share gains in its three largest markets, in a category the same trade press describes as structurally challenged. Diageo Beer Company grew around 4% in FY2026. That is genuine outperformance and it is the reason group organic decline was 2% rather than deeper.

Diageo is worse than the group where it matters most. NAM fell 8.4% while Diageo's own commentary cites more affordable alternatives, which is share loss rather than market decline. This is the largest profit pool, and the brands under pressure, tequila in particular, are the ones that drove the 2021-2022 premiumisation narrative.

The strategic pivot is a bet on price-point coverage rather than category rejection: selective, "surgical" price repositioning including cuts on Casamigos, mass-market accessibility, and heavy RTD investment. The bear objection is direct: gross margin has already fallen 190bps and lowering price points on aspirational brands risks permanent brand-equity impairment for temporary volume. RTD is the one category where Diageo has both historic credibility (Smirnoff Ice, nearly 30 years old) and current momentum.

One speculative competitive item deserves mention without weight: a single trade publication raised the possibility of a Pernod Ricard/Brown-Forman combination in April 2026. That is a question posed, not a transaction. If it happened it would create a scaled rival concentrated precisely in the US market where Diageo is weakest.

**Competitive Position: 4.5.** Category leadership in Scotch and tequila is intact and Guinness is gaining share, but the trajectory in the highest-margin part of the portfolio and the largest market is negative, and the mix is shifting toward structurally lower-margin categories.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs (unquantified).** Diageo's highest-margin US propositions are structurally import-dependent and cannot be onshored: Scotch from the UK, tequila legally from Mexico, Canadian whisky from Canada. Renewed US tariff uncertainty reportedly forced abandonment of a long-standing sales target. No annualised cost or mitigation plan is verifiable. This is the largest single unpriced risk we can identify.

**Chinese white spirits policy.** APAC fell 6.3% with white spirits weakness in China cited; Lewis referenced "working through the consequences of Government policy in Chinese white spirits." This sits inside a consolidated roughly 63%-owned subsidiary, so it is a demand shock in reported results, not a translation issue.

**Further impairment against negative tangible book.** Tangible book is −$2,504m and the unexplained $1,401m fall in other intangibles leaves open the possibility of further brand write-downs. Equity provides thin protection.

**Inventory, with precedent.** $10,529m of maturing stock is a moat in a growing market and a cash-absorption and write-down risk in a prolonged downturn. Diageo has already misjudged channel inventory once, in the November 2023 LAC profit warning.

**Excise and duty policy.** At roughly 29% of gross sales, duty is a first-order variable. Diageo accounts for approximately £1 in every £10 of UK food and drink exports, which gives it policy salience in trade negotiations and visibility as a target for duty and public-health measures.

**EM concentration in the growth line.** Africa, LAC and Turkey are carrying group growth and carry the highest FX, inflation and policy volatility. Growth there mechanically increases the translation drag that turned +1.7% organic into −0.1% reported in FY2025.

**The unresolved diagnosis.** Lewis attributes weakness to disposable income rather than GLP-1 drugs or reduced youth drinking. Fundsmith exited in 2024 on the opposite structural view. The entire turnaround is coherent only if affordability is the constraint. The FY2026 regional pattern (Europe +3.4%, LAC +7.7%, Africa +13.3% versus NAM −8.4%) is more consistent with a US-specific income-and-competition problem than a global structural one, which modestly supports Lewis. That is an interpretation of regional data, not proof, and both explanations can hold in different cohorts.

**Governance and execution intensity.** Three CEOs in under three years. Reuters reports some teams cutting 20-30% of staff. Cuts of that depth in commercial organisations risk degrading the distributor-service capability Lewis himself identified as broken in North America.

**Verification risk.** No primary filing was read. The FY2026 20-F has not been filed, EDGAR retrieval returned HTTP 403, and all FY2026 statement figures come from third-party standardisation with at least one identified error (blank FY2026 capex producing a fictitious 22.4% FCF margin). The underlying EPS on which the valuation case rests is unverified.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 23.0% against one-year realised of 33.4%, a −10.4 point spread that puts implied vol in the 2nd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in contango (23.0% near, 26.5% at six months). Together these read as post-event complacency: the FY2026 print has cleared, near-term realised vol should compress, and the market is pricing only a ±3.9% move over the next ten days. The put/call skew reading is 5.4% at the one-week tenor, flagged as elevated put demand, but flips to −0.2% at three months and −7.9% at six months, and put/call volume and open-interest ratios are 0.47 and 0.40, which is light. Short interest is 0.7% of float with 3.9 days to cover, which is negligible; there is no crowded bearish positioning to squeeze and no informed short base to defer to.

Options are not corroborating a directional view in either direction. Cheap near-term vol against 33% realised means a holder can finance the position with covered calls at low opportunity cost, and anyone who wants exposure to the FY2027 stabilisation story can buy longer-dated calls at 26.5% vol rather than paying up in the equity. The absence of stress in credit-sensitive positioning is mildly reassuring against the grey-zone Z-Score.

**Risk Factors: 4.5.** The roughly 55% derating since January 2022 has priced a great deal. The tariff exposure, further impairment tail, and the Chinese policy shock inside a consolidated subsidiary are specific, unquantified and not obviously in the price.

## Management Quality

The forward-looking record is mixed and the incentive structure is unknowable. Diageo is a foreign private issuer: no DEF 14A, no Section 16, therefore no Form 4 data. The absence of disclosed insider buying is a reporting-regime artefact and carries no signal. We do not know whether Lewis's long-term awards are tied to organic sales, margin, leverage or TSR, which would materially change confidence in the turnaround's alignment and is the single most valuable missing input.

What we can assess is capital allocation. The prior regime repurchased $2,985m of stock in FY2022, $1,673m in FY2023 and $987m in FY2024, roughly $5.6bn, largely debt-funded, at valuations near the January 2022 peak when the company was worth approximately $121bn. Leverage rose, the demand environment turned, and the outcome was a suspended buyback and a 51.7% dividend cut in February 2026 ending a streak of increases the company had itself highlighted as running since formation over 25 years ago. That sequence is poor timing on a large scale and it constrains the current management's options.

Lewis's decisions to date look rational: cut $1.2bn of cost to release $850m-plus of savings, redirect roughly $1.19bn per year of former dividend capacity to deleveraging, dispose of non-core assets (Royal Challengers Bengaluru for a reported £1.4bn, with Swellfun and possibly Moët Hennessy discussed), and address distributor service in North America. He is a turnaround generalist, with three decades at Unilever, then Tesco from 2014, and no prior spirits operating experience, executing a strategic reversal away from premiumisation.

Board oversight is a live question. On FT reporting, chair John Manzoni is seeking non-executive directors with drinks commercial experience because the 11-person board has only one NED who has held a senior spirits role (Valérie Chapoulaud-Floquet, ex-Rémy Cointreau), with concern the board lacks the sector knowledge to "stop him from going too far." Diageo's on-record response is that the board has "relevant consumer industry experience." The sourcing is unnamed; the verifiable core, one spirits-experienced NED of eleven, is what we weight. A chair deliberately building a challenge function is, on balance, a governance positive, and routine refreshment after a chair change is a benign alternative explanation. Either reading raises the variance of outcomes.

**Management Quality: 4.5.** Prior capital allocation was value-destructive at scale; current allocation is defensible but unproven; leadership churn has been severe; and the incentive structure is not disclosable to us.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($130, +34.9%)

North America is self-inflicted. Distributor service failures and missing price-point coverage, both fixable by a cost-and-execution operator, explain most of the 8.4% decline, and the FY2026 regional pattern (Europe, LAC and Africa all positive) supports a US-specific rather than global structural read. The $1.2bn programme delivers $850m-$1bn of savings, and reinvestment restores NAM growth by FY2028. Guinness compounds at high single digits with a real distribution runway via Microdraught, and RTD at mid-teens growth captures the affordability shift rather than losing to it. RCB proceeds land in FY2027 investing cash flow, and a Swellfun or Moët Hennessy disposal takes leverage below 2.5x, restoring buyback capacity by FY2029. On $6.80 of underlying EPS at 19x, a partial re-rating toward Diageo's own history, the ADR is worth $130, close to the analyst high of $136.

### Bear Case ($75, −22.2%)

The affordability diagnosis is wrong, or only half right. Younger cohorts and GLP-1 users are drinking less, spirits volume does not recover, and cutting price on Casamigos and other aspirational brands permanently impairs brand equity while gross margin falls below 58%. Tariffs land unmitigated on a portfolio that cannot be onshored, costing several hundred basis points of NAM margin. The $1,401m intangibles reduction proves to have been impairment and more follows against negative tangible book of −$2,504m. Deep headcount cuts degrade the very distributor capability management said was broken. Leverage stays near 3x, and refinancing $2,449m of short-term debt occurs at higher coupons. FY2027 comes in below "broadly flat," the medium-term low-single-digit ambition is withdrawn as the prior target was, and 13x on $5.60 gives $75, a retest of the 52-week low.

### What Would Settle It

The FY2027 interim result in February 2027 is the first checkpoint with real information: NAM organic sales, gross margin under price repositioning, and the FY2027 half-one realisation of programme savings. Before that, the FY2026 20-F when filed should resolve the exceptional-items composition, the true capex and free cash flow, reported net borrowings and leverage on Diageo's own definition, and the FY2026 full-year underlying EPS on which the entire cheapness argument rests.

**Reassessment timeline:** FY2026 20-F when filed, then the FY2027 interim results in February 2027.

## Conclusion

Diageo at $96.35 is priced as a business that has stopped growing and needs to fix its largest market, which is a fair description. Roughly 15x estimated underlying earnings and a 5.5% free cash flow yield for the world's largest premium spirits company, with genuine outperformance in Guinness and RTD and real deleveraging underway, is not expensive. But the cheapness rests on an estimated earnings figure we could not verify, the free cash flow figure circulating in screens is an artefact roughly $1.3bn too high, the forward dividend yield is about 2% rather than the 3.3% a trailing screen shows, and FY2027 revenue is guided flat with only low-single-digit growth as the medium-term ambition. North America fell 8.4% while the company itself cited losing ground to more affordable alternatives, and the tariff exposure on a portfolio that legally cannot be onshored is unquantified.

The near-term arithmetic is roughly 9% to base case against 22% to a bear case that would only retest a price seen in March. The stock has already risen roughly 33% from that low and 9% in the three sessions since the FY2026 print, so the market has paid for the cost plan. It has not yet paid for a stabilising North America, which is also the thing that would justify adding. Hold the position, collect the reduced dividend, and let the FY2027 interims settle whether this is an affordability cycle or something harder.
