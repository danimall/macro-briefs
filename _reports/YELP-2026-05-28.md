**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Yelp is a profitable local business discovery platform generating $1.46B in revenue and $324M in free cash flow, trading at approximately 4x FCF ($1.3B market cap). The balance sheet is clean (zero long-term debt, $319M in liquid assets, Z-Score 5.35). On surface metrics, the stock appears deeply undervalued.

The problem is structural. Google AI Overviews now appear in 58% of searches and reduce click-through rates by 61%. Zero-click searches represent 60%+ of queries. Yelp's traffic is declining across every channel (desktop -6%, mobile web -7%, app -2% in 2025), ad clicks fell 7% in 2025, paying advertising locations contracted 5%, and management guided 2026 as the first year of overall revenue decline since 2020. The company is attempting a pivot toward subscription/API revenue (targeting $250M annualized by 2028 from $116M today), but this requires execution of an unproven strategy through the largest acquisition in company history ($270M Hatch deal) while the core advertising business deteriorates.

The market is pricing secular decline, not a temporary setback. At 10.6x trailing earnings with revenue turning negative, negative estimate revisions (-53% on current quarter over 90 days), 23.6% short interest, zero insider buying, and CFO actively selling, the weight of evidence supports the market's verdict over the optically cheap valuation.

## Company Overview

Yelp operates a local business review platform with 330M+ cumulative reviews across the US and Canada. Revenue (~$1.46B in 2025) derives ~95% from performance-based CPC advertising, primarily sold to local small and medium businesses through automated auctions. The business segments into Services (home, auto, professional — $948M, +8% YoY) and Restaurants, Retail & Other ($444M, -6% YoY), plus a small but fast-growing Other segment ($74M, +17% YoY) comprising subscriptions and API products.

The company operates with a fully remote workforce and has been an aggressive capital returner, repurchasing nearly $2B in stock since 2017. In February 2026, Yelp acquired Hatch for ~$270M to accelerate its AI-powered subscription product strategy for Services businesses.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory — Decelerating into Contraction:**

| Year | Revenue | Growth |
|------|---------|--------|
| 2023 | $1.34B | +12% |
| 2024 | $1.41B | +6% |
| 2025 | $1.46B | +4% |
| 2026E | $1.455-1.475B | ~flat to -1% |

The core advertising engine is sputtering. Full-year 2025 ad clicks declined 7%, offset by 10% CPC increases. This pattern of fewer clicks at higher prices generates revenue in the short term but risks advertiser fatigue and is unsustainable. Paying advertising locations fell to 496K in Q4 2025 from 521K a year prior (-5%). Q4 2025 advertising revenue turned negative (-2% YoY) for the first time in years.

**Profitability & Cash Flow (2025):**
- Operating income: $185M (12.6% margin), +22% YoY
- Free cash flow: $324M (22.1% FCF margin)
- Adjusted EBITDA: $369M (25.2% margin)

The margin profile remains attractive, driven by cost discipline and the remote workforce model. FCF conversion is strong. However, Q1 2026 showed margin compression: adjusted EBITDA fell 7% YoY to $79M (22% margin vs. 25%+ in 2025), reflecting Hatch integration costs and investment spending.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Cash/securities: $319M
- Long-term debt: Zero (as of Dec 2025); ~$80M drawn on revolver for Hatch
- Current ratio: 3.0x
- Available credit: $241M remaining on $325M facility

The balance sheet provides substantial cushion. The concern is deployment: $270M on Hatch plus $292M in buybacks in 2025 consumed most of the FCF generation while the business deteriorated.

**Share Repurchase Paradox:** Yelp repurchased ~$2B since 2017, reducing share count from ~71M to ~60M. Yet shares trade near multi-year lows ($23.51 vs. $39.58 52-week high). The buybacks have not prevented value destruction. Average repurchase price in 2025 was ~$33/share — significantly above today's price.

| Next Quarter | $0.58 | -28.7% |

Current quarter estimates have been cut by more than half in 90 days. Full-year 2026 consensus at $1.88 implies ~16% EPS decline from $2.24 trailing, with only modest recovery to $2.60 in 2027.

**The "Other Revenue" Pivot:**
Management targets $250M annualized run rate in non-advertising revenue by end of 2028 (from ~$116M today, based on Q1's $29M × 4). This segment grew 75% YoY in Q1 2026 and includes Yelp Guest Manager subscriptions, API programs, and Hatch-related SaaS tools. If achieved, it would reduce advertising dependency from 95% to ~83% of a ~$1.5B revenue base.

The math requires roughly 115% growth in "Other" over 2.75 years (~30% CAGR). Given Q1's 75% growth rate, the trajectory is plausible in isolation. But: (1) Hatch integration creates meaningful execution risk, (2) the underlying Services category that feeds these products is weakening, and (3) even $250M in other revenue cannot offset a core advertising business that may be in structural decline.

**AI Strategy — Correct Direction, Unproven Execution:**
Yelp Assistant now drives 15% of Request-a-Quote projects and the company launched 35+ AI features in Q1 2026. The strategic vision of becoming an AI-native answers-and-actions platform addresses the competitive threat. But the company has "limited experience" with complex acquisitions, and the transformation requires sustained investment during a period of revenue pressure.

## Valuation Assessment

At $23.51, Yelp trades at:
- 10.6x trailing P/E ($2.24 EPS)
- 12.5x forward P/E on 2026E ($1.88)
- 9.0x 2027E P/E ($2.60)
- ~4x trailing FCF ($324M / $1.3B market cap)
- ~3.5x EV/EBITDA on guided $310-330M

Against peers: median P/E is 23.2x (CARS.com). ZoomInfo (GTM) at 8.1x is the closest comp, also facing search/lead-gen disruption. TripAdvisor at 64.7x reflects different dynamics.

The optically cheap multiple is real — this is a profitable company with 22% FCF margins and no debt trading at 4x cash flow. The central question is whether FCF is sustainable. If ad clicks continue declining 7-8% annually and CPC increases cannot compensate, revenue and FCF will compress. A 5-year DCF at 10% WACC with -2% revenue growth and 20% FCF margins yields ~$22-25/share — essentially where the stock trades today. The market is already pricing in mild secular decline. A BUY thesis requires believing either (a) the decline stabilizes/reverses, or (b) the subscription pivot succeeds at scale. Neither has evidence today.

The cheap multiple represents accurate pricing of a declining business that generates substantial current cash flow but faces accelerating structural headwinds.

## Competitive Landscape

Yelp's competitive position is deteriorating along its most important axis: Google.

**Google AI Overviews:** Forbes data shows AI Overviews now appear in 58% of Google searches and reduce click-through rates by 61%. Zero-click searches represent 60%+ of queries. Yelp's 10-K explicitly acknowledges this: "Google has incorporated AI-generated responses to search queries above their organic search results, which has reduced the prominence of links to our platform and may be having a negative impact on our traffic." This is not a future risk — traffic has been below pre-pandemic levels since 2020 and is declining across all channels.

**Apple Maps:** Historically provided meaningful traffic through content integration. Changes to how Apple displays Yelp content have already reduced this channel. If Apple launches proprietary reviews, the partnership faces further erosion.

**AI Chatbots (ChatGPT, Perplexity):** Emerging as alternative discovery channels for local recommendations. Companies are scraping Yelp's 330M reviews to train models, potentially diminishing Yelp's moat as a unique content aggregator while providing no revenue to Yelp.

**Category Competition:** DoorDash/Uber Eats in restaurants (explaining RR&O's -6% decline), Angi/Thumbtack in home services (Yelp's growth area), and Meta/Google with superior scale in local SMB advertising.

The defensive moat — trusted human-generated reviews, brand recognition, deep SMB relationships — is eroding as traffic declines make the platform less valuable to advertisers, which reduces investment in content quality, creating a negative flywheel.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk — Google AI Overviews (Existential):** The single most important variable. If AI Overviews expand further, Yelp's traffic decline accelerates, ad click volumes compress faster, and the advertising revenue model faces structural impairment beyond what CPC increases can offset. This risk is accelerating, not stable.

**Macro Sensitivity:** SMBs with limited budgets are disproportionately exposed to consumer spending weakness. In a stagflationary environment with real wages negative, local businesses cut marketing spend quickly. Paying advertiser locations already contracted 5% — this could accelerate in H2 2026.

**Acquisition Integration Risk:** The $270M Hatch acquisition is Yelp's largest ever. Management acknowledges "limited experience" with complex acquisitions. Retention packages of ~$30M over 2-3 years suggest meaningful integration complexity.

**Advertising Concentration:** ~95% advertising revenue from SMBs who can cancel at any time with zero switching costs creates revenue fragility in downturns.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($30-32):** The subscription/API pivot succeeds, driving "Other revenue" toward the $250M target ahead of schedule. Hatch integration produces tangible advertiser retention improvements. Google's AI Overviews stabilize at current penetration levels. The short interest squeezes if management delivers on turnaround execution. At $30, stock trades at ~6x trailing FCF, still cheap for a stabilizing business.

**Bear Case ($14-17):** Google AI Overviews expand to 75%+ of searches by 2027, accelerating traffic losses. Ad clicks decline 10-12% annually as CPC increases hit a ceiling. Revenue contracts 5-8% annually. Advertiser locations drop below 450K. The Hatch acquisition fails to deliver ROI. FCF margin compresses to 15% on a $1.3B revenue base (~$195M FCF), stock trades at 5x depressed FCF = $18. Further deterioration to 3x = $11.

**Base Case ($20-24):** Revenue declines modestly (1-3% annually), subscription revenue grows but slower than management's targets, FCF margin compresses slightly to 19-20%, and the stock trades at 4-5x FCF on $280-300M in cash generation. This roughly equals the current price — limited upside from here without a catalyst.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** No single dated binary event, but the continuous deterioration has identifiable checkpoints: Q2 2026 earnings (~August 2026) should reveal whether the Q1 beat was sustainable or a one-time positive. If current quarter estimates ($0.36, already cut 53%) are missed, the stock re-tests February lows ($19.60). Google I/O announcements expanding AI Overviews into more verticals would compress traffic further.

**Timing Window:** 3-6 months. Next earnings in August 2026. Current quarter estimates already slashed, so a miss would signal estimates are still too high despite massive cuts.

**Short Interest and Squeeze Risk:** At 23.6% SI and 6.9 days to cover, the short is crowded. Any positive catalyst (acquisition rumor, better-than-expected subscription growth, Google partnership) could trigger a violent squeeze of 20-30%. This is the primary constraint on shorting.

**Borrow and Options Signal:** Put/call volume ratio of 6.55 confirms bearish activity. IV is rich (46.9% vs. 38.7% HV), increasing the cost of put protection.

**Downside Target and Risk/Reward:** Base downside is $17-19 (25-28% below current). Upside squeeze risk is $28-30 (19-28% above current). Risk/reward is approximately 1:1 from current levels given the crowded positioning.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bear thesis is correct — the business faces structural decline from Google AI Overviews and decelerating advertiser demand. However, 23.6% short interest, 6.9 days to cover, and a $1.3B market cap (small enough for activist/takeout scenarios) make the remaining downside limited relative to squeeze risk at current levels. The time to short was at $35-38, not $23.51.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry Criteria (conditions that would change this to HOLD or BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of ad click growth (reversing the -7% annual decline) — would signal traffic stabilization
2. "Other revenue" reaching $50M+ quarterly run rate ($200M+ annualized) with visible path to $250M target
3. Insider buying (open-market purchases by CEO or CFO of >$500K)
4. Stock reaching $17-18 (true margin of safety even in bear case) with stable estimates

**Timeline:** The structural headwinds (Google AI Overviews, traffic decline) are intensifying over 12-24 months. The subscription pivot requires 2-3 years to prove out. No resolution is expected before mid-2027 at earliest.

## Conclusion

Yelp presents the classic value trap profile: a profitable business with a clean balance sheet and optically cheap multiples, combined with structural competitive deterioration, declining traffic, contracting advertiser counts, negative estimate revisions, and zero insider conviction. The 4x FCF multiple accurately reflects a business generating substantial current cash flow against accelerating secular headwinds from Google's AI transformation. The subscription pivot is the correct strategic response but requires 2-3 years and flawless execution on Yelp's largest-ever acquisition to materially change the trajectory. No insider is buying. The CFO is selling. Estimates are being cut dramatically. The base case from current levels is flat, with meaningful downside risk if the advertising decline accelerates. Avoid.
