## Executive Summary

TC Energy is a Canadian MJDS filer that has completed a structural transformation: the liquids business contributed C$1,145M of comparable EBITDA in 2024 and zero in 2025, leaving a pure-play natural gas transmission and power franchise moving roughly 54 Bcf/d across Canada, the U.S. and Mexico. Continuing-operations comparable EBITDA compounded at 7.53% annually from C$9,472M (2023) to C$10,952M (2025), and H1 2026 accelerated to +13.2% (C$6,036M vs C$5,334M). On the July 30, 2026 print management moved 2026 guidance to the upper end of the C$11.6-11.8B range, which at the top implies +7.7% growth over 2025. Comparable EPS of C$0.94 beat the C$0.84 consensus by 11.5%, consensus current-year EPS has been revised +2.4% over 30 days, and the Bruce Power Unit 3 refurbishment finished seven months early at a 15% cost reduction versus Unit 6. This is a well-run quarter in a business with genuine structural demand behind it: LNG feedgas deliveries on the U.S. systems grew 13% year over year to 3.9 Bcf/d, and Q2 sanctions included a Columbia Gas expansion explicitly built to serve new gas-fired generation for data-centre development, at 4.4x and 6.4x build multiples under 20-year take-or-pay contracts.

The share price has fallen 10.3% from US$70.59 on July 24 to US$63.33, entirely after a beat-and-raise. The mechanism appears to be rates rather than fundamentals: a 4% yielding, 4.8x-levered infrastructure name derates through the multiple when long rates rise. The pullback improves risk/reward rather than signalling impairment, but the starting multiple was full, so the move takes valuation from rich to fair rather than to cheap. Converting the declared C$3.51 annualized dividend against the quoted 3.918% yield implies a spot USD/CAD near 1.415 (the brief used the company's YE2025 translation rate of 1.37). On either rate, the shares trade at 22-24x FY2027 consensus comparable EPS of C$3.92 and roughly 13x consolidated EV/EBITDA on guided 2026 EBITDA — full for an asset base whose largest segment (Canadian Natural Gas Pipelines, 31% of EBITDA) grew 3.7% in H1 and whose GAAP segmented earnings there declined.

Three specifics temper the growth optics. First, Mexico produced C$289M of the C$702M H1 EBITDA increase — 41% of growth from 13.9% of the base — while Mexican physical flows fell year over year, so that growth is contract-payment driven and rests on counterparty credit that was not verifiable in the documents reviewed. Second, allowance for funds used during construction fell C$263M (C$362M → C$99M), the largest single offset converting +C$702M of EBITDA growth into +C$184M of comparable-earnings growth; the non-cash tailwind that flattered 2023-2025 EPS is now a headwind. Third, comparable funds from operations do not cover capex plus dividends: doubling H1 FFO of C$4,332M gives roughly C$8.7B against C$6.0-6.5B of gross capex and ~C$3.78B of common and preferred dividends, leaving a C$1.1-1.6B gap before roughly C$0.5B of minority-partner capital implied by the gross-versus-net capex guidance. Debt rose C$720M in 2025 and leverage was flat at 4.8x. **HOLD** — a covered ~4% yield plus 5-6% earnings growth is a reasonable ~9-10% return if the multiple holds, but the probability-weighted 12-month total return of roughly 6% does not clear the accumulate bar with the long end still unsettled.

## Company Overview

TC Energy operates four segments. Using H1 2026 comparable EBITDA of C$6,036M: U.S. Natural Gas Pipelines C$2,715M (45.0%), Canadian Natural Gas Pipelines C$1,880M (31.1%), Mexico Natural Gas Pipelines C$841M (13.9%), Power and Energy Solutions C$604M (10.0%), with Corporate at C$(4)M.

The U.S. franchise (Columbia Gas, Columbia Gulf, ANR, Great Lakes, Northern Border, North Baja, Gillis Access, Portland, Bison XPress) moved 27.0 Bcf/d in Q2, up 5%, of which 3.9 Bcf/d — 14.4% — went to LNG facilities, up 13%. The Canadian systems (NGTL, Canadian Mainline) delivered 24.2 Bcf/d, up 1%, and operate under cost-of-service or negotiated tolls, so earnings track approved rate-base additions rather than throughput. Mexico moved 3.4 Bcf/d, below Q2 2025, with 1.4 Bcf/d to power generation. Power and Energy Solutions is anchored by an interest in Bruce Power, which ran at 98.5% availability with zero forced outage days in Q2, alongside a cogeneration fleet at 89.6% availability during spring planned outages.

The economic engine is corridor incumbency expressed through brownfield build multiples. Q2 sanctions ran 4.4x (Clark, Columbia Gulf, ~US$0.1B, up to 0.3 Bcf/d, 2028) and 6.4x (Central Virginia Capacity, Columbia Gas, ~US$0.3B, up to 0.4 Bcf/d, 2028 and 2030), a weighted ~5.8x on roughly US$0.4B of capital. A 5.8x multiple implies an unlevered EBITDA yield near 17% on invested capital and about US$69M of incremental annual EBITDA from that capital. Build multiple is a company-defined, expected (not realized) metric that excludes maintenance capital and the cost of the existing right-of-way, but the directional point holds: in-corridor expansion is available to the incumbent at economics a greenfield entrant cannot approach.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory.** Continuing-operations comparable EBITDA: C$9,472M (2023) → C$10,049M (2024) → C$10,952M (2025), a 7.53% two-year CAGR. H1 2026 comparable EBITDA C$6,036M (+13.2%); Q2 C$2,948M (+12.3%). GAAP total segmented earnings +11.2% in the quarter. Comparable EPS C$0.94 (+14.6%) in Q2 and C$1.93 (+9.7%) for H1. GAAP basic EPS from continuing operations was C$0.95 in Q2, marginally above comparable EPS, meaning non-comparable items were a small net benefit to GAAP, a cleaner quarter than most.

**The earnings bridge is the most instructive disclosure.** H1 comparable EBITDA grew C$702M but comparable earnings grew only C$184M (C$1,831M → C$2,015M). The offsets: D&A −C$111M (1,460 vs 1,349), AFUDC −C$263M (99 vs 362), interest income and other −C$33M, tax −C$46M, non-controlling interests −C$37M, FX −C$16M, interest expense −C$11M, preferreds −C$1M. AFUDC (capitalized interest on construction in progress) fell 73%. This is the arithmetic signature of a company exiting a mega-project build cycle: while large projects are under construction AFUDC credits earnings without generating cash; when they enter service AFUDC stops and D&A steps up. The favourable reading is that H1 comparable EPS still grew 9.7% while absorbing a C$263M non-cash headwind, so underlying cash earnings improved faster than EPS suggests. The alternative reading, that project scale has permanently shrunk toward US$0.1-0.3B in-corridor expansions, points the same direction for earnings quality: future EPS growth must come from cash EBITDA.

**Cash flow, with a caution.** H1 net cash from operations rose 36.5% (C$3,532M → C$4,820M) while comparable FFO rose 10.7% (C$3,913M → C$4,332M). The gap between the two swung roughly C$869M year over year (−C$381M in H1 2025 to +C$488M in H1 2026), almost certainly working-capital, collateral and cash-tax timing. H1 operating cash flow should not be annualized. Comparable FFO growth of 10.7%, tracking comparable earnings growth of 10.0%, is the reliable underlying figure and indicates earnings are cash-backed.

**Funding.** As a labelled sensitivity: doubling H1 comparable FFO gives roughly C$8.7B for 2026 against guided gross capex of C$6.0-6.5B, common dividends of about C$3.66B (C$3.51 × 1,042M) and ~C$115M of preferred dividends. That leaves a C$1.1-1.6B shortfall before roughly C$0.5B of non-controlling-interest capital implied by the gross-versus-net capex guidance spread. If H2 FFO runs below H1 on seasonality, the gap widens. TC Energy is not organically free-cash-flow positive after dividends at this capex level, which is normal for regulated pipelines but makes continued investment-grade market access a precondition of the plan rather than a convenience. Year-end cash was C$168M against C$60.1B of reported debt, consistent with a commercial-paper-funded treasury backstopped by committed revolvers, though facility size, maturities and covenants were not verifiable.

**Leverage and its definition.** Adjusted debt/adjusted comparable EBITDA: 5.1x (2023) → 4.8x (2024) → 4.8x (2025); recalculation confirms 5.11x, 4.816x, 4.813x. Deleveraging happened in 2024 alongside the liquids separation and then stalled; reported debt rose C$720M in 2025 (C$59,366M → C$60,086M). Management's 4.75x target will most plausibly be reached by growing the denominator: at C$55.4B of adjusted debt, 4.75x requires ~C$11.67B of adjusted comparable EBITDA, within reach of 2026 guidance. The ratio is definition-sensitive: removing the 50% equity credit on C$12.1B of junior subordinated notes takes leverage to 5.34x; reported debt over adjusted EBITDA is 5.22x. The company concedes agency calculations differ, and no current agency ratings or outlooks were obtainable, a genuine limitation on any solvency conclusion.

One definitional item deserves naming. Beginning in 2025 TC Energy entered a subordinated demand revolving facility under which **TC Energy borrows from the Sur de Texas joint venture**, receiving C$111M in the year, and adds that amount in arriving at adjusted comparable EBITDA. Borrowing proceeds are a financing cash flow; including them in the EBITDA denominator improves leverage by about 0.05x (4.86x without it). The plausible commercial purpose is efficient upstreaming of cash from a Mexican JV where formal distributions may be constrained; it may equally be routine intra-group liquidity management. The company discloses it plainly and the magnitude is immaterial to the conclusion, but it is a definitional stretch in a metric measured against a public target.

**Interest-rate mechanics.** H1 comparable interest expense of C$1,698M annualizes near C$3.4B on C$60.1B of debt, an average cost around 5.6%. If roughly 10% of the stack refinances annually at 100bp higher, the drag is about C$60M pretax, or roughly C$0.04 per share, near 1% of EPS per year of elevated rates, cumulative and only partially recoverable through rate cases with a lag. That is the channel through which a higher-for-longer rate regime reaches this income statement, and it is why the equity behaves like a long-duration bond proxy.

## Growth Analysis

Segment decomposition of H1 comparable EBITDA growth: Mexico +52.4% (C$841M vs C$552M), Power +15.0% (C$604M vs C$525M), U.S. +10.5% (C$2,715M vs C$2,456M), Canada +3.7% (C$1,880M vs C$1,813M). Mexico contributed 41% of the C$702M increase from 13.9% of the base; U.S. contributed 37%; Canada 10%, with Canadian GAAP segmented earnings declining (C$1,054M vs C$1,067M) on 1% delivery growth.

A blended forward growth expectation should therefore be weighted toward the low-single-digit regulated Canadian outcome for 31% of EBITDA. That is roughly what consensus does: current-year EPS of C$3.72 (+6% over the ~C$3.51 comparable EPS implied for 2025) and next-year C$3.92 (+5.4%). Revisions are positive but modest: current year +2.4% over 30 days and +2.2% over 90 days, next year +1.8% and +1.7%. Next-quarter estimates were cut 2.0% over 30 days, likely seasonality and the AFUDC roll-off. These are the revision velocities of a mid-single-digit compounder, not those that mark an acceleration.

The structural demand case is credible and partly corroborated. Management's forecast of ~51 Bcf/d of North American demand growth from 2025 to 2035 is a company assumption, not independent data. The corroborating evidence is verifiable: LNG feedgas on the U.S. systems up 13% to 3.9 Bcf/d, all-time delivery records at North Baja (886 MMcf, June 17) and Gillis Access (1.5 Bcf, July 3), and the Central Virginia Capacity project sanctioned specifically to serve new gas-fired generation supporting data-centre development. On the AI power theme, TC Energy sits two steps removed: it contracts with the pipeline shipper, which supplies a generator, which serves a data centre. That intermediation is the conservative position, since capacity payments run for 20 years regardless of whether any particular data centre is built, but the counterparty is the shipper/generator, whose credit and completion risk matter.

Canadian growth has a later-decade path. The Canadian Mainline received CER approval for a four-year negotiated settlement covering January 2027 through December 2030; the Greater Edmonton Area delivery offering (up to ~0.26 Bcf/d) was fully subscribed; roughly 1.0 Bcf/d of marketed delivery capacity is in play for 2030-2032 across intra-Alberta markets and the Empress/McNeill borders; and an Empress open season has commenced with up to C$200M of capital committed subject to FID. Approximately C$3B of growth has been sanctioned year to date, C$1.8B placed in service in H1, and C$1.6B guided for H2. On the power side, Bruce Unit 4 remains on time and on budget, and the Ontario IESO approved C$300M for Bruce C impact assessment and pre-development, with the system operator rather than TC Energy funding early-stage nuclear development, which is unusually favourable risk sharing. It is funding for assessment only, not approval to construct.

The counterweight on power economics is explicit: Unit 3's seven-month-early, 15%-cheaper completion results in Bruce Power returning approximately C$150M to Ontario ratepayers through the IESO. Refurbishment outperformance is shared, so it should not be capitalized at full value.

## Valuation Assessment

**FX handling.** The quoted 3.918% yield against the declared C$3.51 annualized dividend implies a spot USD/CAD near 1.415, versus the 1.37 YE2025 translation rate in the company's own disclosure. I use 1.415 and show sensitivity, because the conclusion should not depend on the rate chosen.

**Earnings multiples.** FY2026 consensus comparable EPS C$3.72 = US$2.63 at 1.415 (US$2.72 at 1.37) → **24.1x** (23.3x). FY2027 consensus C$3.92 = US$2.77 (US$2.86) → **22.9x** (22.1x). The quoted trailing/forward P/E of 25.5x on US$2.53 of trailing EPS is consistent. So the shares trade in the low-to-mid 20s against 5-6% consensus EPS growth, a PEG well above 3 on earnings growth, which is the relevant denominator here.

**Enterprise value.** Market cap US$66.0B = C$93.4B at 1.415. Adding C$60.1B of reported debt and C$2.3B of preferred, less C$0.2B of cash, gives roughly C$155.6B before non-controlling-interest equity. Against guided 2026 comparable EBITDA of C$11.8B that is **~13.2x**. Because consolidated comparable EBITDA includes minority-attributable earnings (NCI absorbed C$369M of H1 comparable earnings, and gross capex exceeds net by ~C$0.5B), the multiple on TRP's economic share is higher, plausibly 13.5-14x on roughly C$10.6B. This is a full multiple for large-cap gas infrastructure even after the sector's re-rating on LNG and power-demand growth, and it is the reason I do not score valuation as attractive despite the pullback.

**Yield support.** The ~3.92% indicated yield is covered at 42% of H1 comparable FFO (C$1,829M of common dividends on C$4,332M) but 91% of comparable EPS, because H1 D&A was C$1,460M. On FFO the dividend is safe; on earnings after depreciation and after capex it is not self-funded. The 3.24% year-over-year dividend increase (C$0.85 → C$0.8775 quarterly), well below the 7.5% EBITDA CAGR, reads as a deliberate choice to retain cash for growth and the leverage target. Structurally, dividend growth is likely capped near 3-5% until FFO per share accelerates.

Against a long-end Treasury yield in the mid-4% range, a 3.9% yield growing 3-4% offers a total return in the high single digits with no valuation cushion. That is fair, not asymmetric.

**Analyst context.** The USD target set is low 58.34, mean 67.02, median 69.49, high 73.22, implying −7.9% to +15.6% from spot with the mean at +5.8%. Recommendation distribution is 5 strong buy, 7 buy, 11 hold, 0 sell, 1 strong sell, broadly stable for three months. The CAD targets reported secondhand (RBC C$106, TD C$102, BMO C$99, Goldman Hold) translate roughly to US$70-75 at 1.415. Targets are not independent confirmation for a name at 23x forward earnings with mid-single-digit growth; at premium multiples targets and price tend to embed the same assumptions.

**Base/bull/bear.**
- **Base US$66.50** — 24x FY2027 consensus US$2.77, holding the current multiple as EPS grows 5-6%. +5.0% price, ~9% total return with the dividend.
- **Bull US$73.50** — 26.5x FY2027 EPS, requiring a lower long-end rate environment, confirmation of leverage at or below 4.75x, and incremental sanctions (Empress FID, additional data-centre-linked capacity) at sub-6x build multiples. +16.1%.
- **Bear US$54.00** — 19.5x FY2027 EPS, driven by a further long-end widening, or by evidence that part of Mexico's +52% H1 growth was non-recurring, or a disappointing Great Lakes settlement. −14.7%.

Probability-weighting 30% bull / 40% base / 30% bear gives US$64.85, or +2.4% price plus ~3.9% dividend ≈ **+6.3% expected 12-month total return**. That is approximately fair for a low-beta infrastructure name and sits below the threshold for staged accumulation.

## Competitive Landscape

TC Energy is insulated by mechanism rather than pricing power. First, administrative return-setting: the Canadian Mainline settlement runs 2027-2030, ANR's FERC settlement was filed in May and approved in July 2026, and Great Lakes filed in June with approval anticipated in Q4 2026. Three major franchises therefore have tolls locked or near-locked for multi-year periods, which de-risks 2027-2030 revenue and simultaneously caps upside; the Empress open-season disclosure that targeted returns "exceed the approved return on equity" is an explicit acknowledgment that approved ROE binds the base business. Second, long-dated contracting: 20-year take-or-pay on both new U.S. expansions. Third, corridor incumbency, which makes 4-6x brownfield expansion available to TC Energy and effectively unavailable to a new entrant facing greenfield permitting.

The competitive risks are regulatory reset and permitting rather than price competition, plus rival incumbents in adjacent basins competing for the same shipper commitments. Trajectory is mixed but net positive: the U.S. segment is gaining share of a growing LNG feedgas market (+13% year over year, now 14.4% of segment volumes) and adding power-linked capacity; Mexico is growing earnings on newly in-service capacity; Canada is flat with a later-decade path through NGTL rate-base additions.

Partner capital is used systematically: Bison XPress cost US$0.4B total of which TC Energy's share was US$0.2B, and NCI took C$369M out of H1 comparable earnings. This accelerates footprint growth and moderates the funding gap, but it means a growing slice of consolidated EBITDA does not accrue to TRP shareholders, which flatters any consolidated EV/EBITDA calculation.

Indigenous engagement is being managed as a gating item: 36 letters of support secured ahead of NGTL Multi-Year Growth Plan filings. That is a positive datapoint, not evidence of the absence of opposition elsewhere.

## Risk Assessment

**Mexico concentration (highest-priority idiosyncratic risk).** Mexico delivered 41% of H1 growth and rose to 13.9% of comparable EBITDA from 10.3%, while physical flows declined "primarily attributed to adjustments to pipeline flows." Earnings are capacity-payment driven, so durability rests on counterparty credit and contract enforceability rather than utilization. Counterparty identity, contract tenor and credit support were not verifiable in the documents reviewed; the frequently cited state-utility offtaker should not be treated as established here. Because the mix shifted toward a single country and likely a small counterparty set, this concentrates rather than diversifies exposure. Part of the +52% could also be non-recurring (FX translation, ramp-up true-ups, contractual settlements), which cannot be excluded without the segment note.

**Rate and funding risk.** Roughly C$1.1-1.6B of annual external funding need before minority contributions, C$168M of year-end cash, debt up C$720M in 2025, and a ~5.6% average cost of debt. Each 100bp on refinanced debt costs roughly C$0.04 of annual EPS. A negative agency action (ratings were not obtainable) would be the clearest escalation.

**AFUDC roll-off.** The C$263M H1 headwind repeats until a new large-scale project rebuilds construction-in-progress. Consensus appears to reflect this (5-6% EPS growth on 7-8% EBITDA growth), but it removes a cushion against operating disappointment.

**Non-GAAP divergence in Power.** Q2 Power comparable EBITDA rose to C$361M from C$301M while GAAP segmented earnings fell to C$240M from C$312M, a roughly C$130M directional divergence, most plausibly unrealized mark-to-market on power/commodity risk-management contracts, though asset-specific or outage items cannot be excluded. This is the segment where the GAAP/non-GAAP gap was widest.

**Trade and tariff policy.** Management lists ongoing trade negotiations among its forward-looking assumptions, unquantified. The asset base physically crosses both the Canada-U.S. and U.S.-Mexico borders and the capital program is steel-intensive.

**Benefit-sharing at Bruce.** Cost underruns flow substantially back to Ontario ratepayers (C$150M expected return via the IESO), capping the value of refurbishment outperformance.

**Execution.** The Berland River compressor slipped to July 14, 2026 pending a third-party power transmission connection, a concrete reminder that in-service dates depend on counterparties TC Energy does not control. Management's H2 commitment is ~C$1.6B placed in service "largely on time and on budget or better."

**Disclosure limitations.** As an MJDS filer TC Energy files 40-F and 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K, and its insiders are exempt from Section 16, so no Form 4 record exists. The absence of insider filings carries no signal in either direction. The FY2025 audited statements, the risk-factor section, agency ratings, credit-facility terms, and the management information circular (compensation, board independence, related-party transactions) were not accessible. The only verified compensation linkage is that the 40-55% methane-intensity reduction target by 2035 is tied to executive incentives. These gaps limit confidence rather than establish problems, and they are reflected in the financial-health and management scores.

IV percentile within the chain is only 24%, so near-dated vol is elevated in absolute-but-modest terms rather than crisis pricing. Skew is balanced (−0.1% near, −2.3% at three months), so there is no directional put bid. Put/call open interest of 3.66 indicates substantial standing protection, but current flow is call-tilted (volume ratio 0.56), consistent with legacy hedges rather than fresh bearish positioning. Short interest of 32.4M shares is 3.1% of shares outstanding with 8.1 days to cover, consistent with index and pair hedging; squeeze risk is negligible and the crowd is not short the thesis. The options market is pricing a non-directional volatility episode over the next few weeks, consistent with the view that the 10% drawdown is a discount-rate event rather than a business event, and with a HOLD that does not require paying up for immediate exposure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (US$73.50, +16.1%).** The long end stabilizes and retreats, re-rating a 4% yielding infrastructure compounder back toward its May high. 2026 comparable EBITDA lands at the C$11.8B upper end (+7.7%), adjusted leverage prints at or below 4.75x, and the 2027 capital plan issued with the annual report includes a higher dividend-growth range. Incremental sanctions convert the LNG and power-demand pipeline into contracted rate base: Empress FID (up to C$200M), further Columbia Gas/Gulf expansions at sub-6x build multiples, and progress on Bruce C with IESO-funded pre-development. Canadian growth reaccelerates late-decade as the 2027-2030 Mainline settlement and ~1.0 Bcf/d of marketed NGTL delivery capacity for 2030-2032 convert to rate base. Comparable EPS reaches C$3.92 in 2027 with the multiple at 26.5x.

**Bear case (US$54.00, −14.7%).** Long rates stay elevated and a 3.9% yield reprices toward 4.6%, which alone implies roughly a 15% price decline. The funding gap collides with higher spreads: C$1.1-1.6B of annual external need meets a wider cost of debt, and management either slows capex (cutting the growth rate consensus is capitalizing) or issues equity (breaking the +0.2% share-count discipline). Mexico's +52% H1 growth proves partly non-recurring or the counterparty deteriorates, removing 41% of the growth engine. AFUDC stays near C$99M, D&A keeps stepping up, and EPS growth slips to 2-3% against a 91% payout on earnings, at which point the market applies a utility-like 19-20x multiple to a business with a 4.8x leverage ratio that flatters to 5.34x without the junior-subordinated-note equity credit.

**What tips the balance.** Both cases hinge on the discount rate more than on operations, which is why this is a HOLD. The operating evidence is good: 12-13% EBITDA growth, guidance raised, projects delivered early and under budget, 20-year contracted expansions at 17% unlevered EBITDA yields on capital. The problem is that the market already pays 23x forward earnings and ~13x EV/EBITDA for that evidence, leaving the ~4% yield as the reliable component of return and the multiple as the swing factor. Existing holders are compensated for waiting; new capital has no margin of safety.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis for continued ownership is a ~3.92% dividend covered at 42% of comparable FFO, growing 3-4% annually, on a contracted asset base with tolls locked through 2030 on major franchises. Expected 12-month total return of roughly 6% (30/40/30 weighting of US$73.50 / US$66.50 / US$54.00, plus dividend) is approximately fair.

**Targets:** Base US$66.50 (24x FY2027 consensus US$2.77 at 1.415 USD/CAD; +5.0% price, ~9% total return). Bull US$73.50 (26.5x; +16.1%). Bear US$54.00 (19.5x; −14.7%). At a 1.37 exchange rate the same multiples map to US$68.6 / US$75.8 / US$55.8, so the conclusion is FX-robust.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Price at or below **US$56-58** with guidance intact — roughly 20-21x FY2027 consensus and a 4.3-4.5% yield, which restores a margin of safety on unchanged fundamentals.
2. Adjusted leverage confirmed **at or below 4.75x** on the Q3 or Q4 print *and* a 2027 dividend-growth range above 5% announced with the annual report.
3. Sanction of more than **C$1B** of new take-or-pay capacity at build multiples below 6x — including Empress FID — which would raise the sustainable EBITDA growth rate above the current mid-single-digit EPS trajectory.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Mexico comparable EBITDA growth decelerating below **+10% year over year**, or disclosure that a material portion of the H1 +52.4% was non-recurring.
2. Adjusted leverage above **5.0x** on the company's own definition, or a negative outlook from any rating agency.
3. An equity issuance or ATM program to fund capex, or 2026 gross capex guided above **C$6.5B** without accompanying build-multiple disclosure — either would convert the funding gap into dilution or unremunerated spend.
4. Rejection or material adverse modification of the **Great Lakes** FERC settlement (approval anticipated Q4 2026), or reopening of the Canadian Mainline 2027-2030 settlement.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints: Q3 2026 results (late October / early November 2026), which will show whether H2 comparable FFO holds near the H1 run rate and whether the AFUDC line stabilizes; the Great Lakes settlement decision in Q4 2026; and the 2027 capital plan and dividend-growth guidance issued with the FY2026 annual report in February 2027. Monitor the 10-year and 30-year Treasury continuously; the multiple, not the operations, is what moves this equity over the next two quarters.

## Conclusion

TC Energy has executed well. Comparable EBITDA is compounding at 7.5% with H1 2026 at +13.2%, guidance moved to the upper end of C$11.6-11.8B, Q2 comparable EPS beat consensus by 11.5%, Bruce Unit 3 finished seven months early and 15% cheaper than Unit 6, and new expansions are being sanctioned at 4.4-6.4x build multiples under 20-year take-or-pay contracts tied to LNG exports and gas-fired generation serving data-centre load. Share count is essentially flat at 1,042M, the dividend is covered at 42% of comparable FFO, and rate settlements now lock tolls on the Canadian Mainline through 2030 and on ANR near term.

The valuation does not leave room for that to be rewarded. At US$63.33 the shares trade at roughly 24x current-year and 23x next-year consensus comparable EPS translated to USD, and about 13x consolidated EV/EBITDA (higher on TRP's economic share, given minority interests), against consensus EPS growth of 5-6% and dividend growth of 3.2%. The C$263M AFUDC roll-off means EPS growth must now be earned from cash EBITDA; 41% of H1 growth came from Mexico on declining volumes and unverified counterparty terms; and comparable FFO does not cover capex plus dividends, leaving C$1.1-1.6B of annual external funding need.

The 10.3% decline since July 24 appears to be a discount-rate event on improving fundamentals, so risk/reward has improved, but from poor to fair, not to compelling. Own it for the covered yield and the contracted growth backlog; wait for US$56-58, confirmed sub-4.75x leverage with a higher dividend-growth range, or more than C$1B of new sub-6x sanctions before committing new capital.
