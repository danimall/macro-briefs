## Executive Summary

Verra Mobility's equity has fallen roughly 82% from its 52-week high of $25.57, with the bulk of the decline concentrated in a single session on May 27, 2026, when the stock dropped from $13.08 to $3.85 on 75.5 million shares. The cause was not a liquidity or covenant event. On May 26 the company disclosed that Avis Budget Group, one of three rental-car customers that together generate roughly 79% of Commercial Services revenue, had issued a termination notice effective September 2026. Over the following ten weeks Verra lost its CEO of twelve years, wrote off $104.4 million of Parking Solutions goodwill and intangibles, re-signed Avis (seven years) and Hertz (five years) on terms it describes as "materially less favorable to us than the prior agreement," and cut full-year guidance twice.

The existential question has been answered favorably: Verra retains both anchor relationships under multi-year contracts, and the credit structure is not stressed. Company-reported net leverage is 2.4x TTM adjusted EBITDA, derived first-lien net leverage is 1.54x, interest coverage is 6.6x, the $150M revolver was undrawn at both recent quarter-ends with ~$146M available, and the nearest maturity is the $350M 5.50% notes due April 2029. Guidance calls for $105–115M of free cash flow. At $4.57 the equity trades at 4.0x the $1.14 midpoint of guided adjusted EPS, 4.6x guided EV/EBITDA, and a 15.8% FCF yield on equity.

What has not been answered is where earnings power settles. Guidance implies second-half 2026 revenue down 10.0% and adjusted EBITDA down 21.8% year over year, at a 36.0% margin versus 41.4%. Consensus for next year has been cut 39.6% in ninety days to $0.95 and fell another 7.8% in the last thirty days. A third rental-car renewal is expected within twelve months, and the two completed resets establish the base case for it. Both new contracts also grant the customer an option to modulate enrolled fleet volume, transferring volume risk to Verra for the first time. Neither the price concession nor the scope reduction has been quantified by the company. That leaves a normalized EBITDA range of roughly $280–380M, which, on a capital structure where equity is only 41% of enterprise value, maps to equity values from about $3.00 to $9.75. We move to HOLD from AVOID because the survival thesis has been retired, while declining to go further because the central variable remains undisclosed.

## Company Overview

Verra Mobility provides smart-mobility technology services from Mesa, Arizona, with 1,901 employees at year-end 2025 and operations in the U.S., Australia, Europe, and Canada. It reported three segments in FY2025.

**Commercial Services** ($435.8M FY2025 revenue, ~45% of total) is the profit engine and carried a 67% segment margin in Q2 2026 ($77.2M on $115.1M). Verra intermediates between rental car companies, commercial fleets, and fleet managers on one side and 50-plus U.S. tolling authorities and violation-issuing bodies on the other: transponder installation and vehicle association, toll and fine payment, liability transfer to drivers, and billing and collection from individual drivers. Toll management is ~39% of company revenue, violations management ~4%, title-and-registration (17 state DMVs) ~2%. The European operation collects unpaid penalties from foreign-registered vehicles across 20 countries.

**Government Solutions** ($460.7M, ~47%) provides photo enforcement — red-light, speed, school bus, bus lane — to states, municipalities, and school districts on three-to-five-year contracts. Q2 2026 segment margin was 24%, down from 28%.

**Parking Solutions** ($82.6M, ~8%) serves roughly 1,775 university, municipal, healthcare, and commercial customers. Q2 2026 segment profit was $2.3M on $20.0M of revenue at an 11% margin, against $201.5M of cumulative write-offs.

Verra disclosed on June 17, 2026 that it is evaluating whether the organizational realignment changes its reportable segments. A collapse to a single segment would eliminate external visibility into Commercial Services profitability.

## Financial Analysis

### The reported quarter and the guided year point in opposite directions

Q2 2026 revenue was $263.6M, up 11.7%; H1 was $487.2M, up 6.1%. Adjusted EPS of $0.38 rose from $0.34 and beat the $0.33 non-GAAP consensus. Yet full-year guidance of $945–965M implies a decline of 1.4–3.5%.

The arithmetic reconciles cleanly. Guidance midpoint $955M less H1 actual $487.2M leaves $467.8M of implied H2 revenue against $519.8M a year earlier — down 10.0%. On EBITDA: $365M midpoint less H1 actual $196.7M implies $168.3M in H2 versus $215.1M, down 21.8%, at a 36.0% margin versus 41.4%. Full-year implied margin of 38.2% compares with 42.5% in FY2025, a 430 basis point compression in one year.

Three separate mechanisms produce that compression, and conflating them overstates the structural damage:

1. **Rental-car repricing.** Both extensions are on materially less favorable terms. Magnitude undisclosed. The Avis transition takes effect in September, so only Q4 captures it.
2. **New York City repricing and mix.** The NYCDOT contract effective January 1, 2026 runs five years at $998M total value (~$199.6M/year average) with added service credits, liquidated damages, and subcontracting requirements. H1 Government Solutions revenue rose $24.9M while segment profit *fell* $7.5M — a negative incremental margin. Government's 24% margin against Commercial's 67% means growth in the mix is dilutive by construction.
3. **Low-margin installation workload.** Cost of service revenue rose 207% in Q2 to $14.2M and 130% in H1 to $21.6M, tracking camera installation rather than a cost problem in the recurring base. Reported revenue growth is currently lower quality than the headline implies.

The Q2 GAAP net loss of $(48.2)M against $110.6M of adjusted EBITDA is almost entirely the non-cash Parking impairment. Commercial Services margin improved to 67% from 66% partly on lower credit loss expense ($4.6M versus $5.7M), with the allowance down from $23.0M to $20.4M. Better collections are real but not a durable margin driver.

| Capex | 56.1 | 55.0 | −1.9% |

Accounts receivable plus unbilled receivables rose from $290.4M to $356.7M, up 22.8%, on 6.1% revenue growth; unbilled alone rose 73% to $97.3M. On a consistent trailing-twelve-month basis this is roughly 129 days versus 108 days at year-end — a 21-day deterioration in six months. Verra drew and repaid $110.5M on the revolver during H1, ending undrawn. Guidance embeds a $30M working capital use for the year and $135M of capex (up from $119.1M) for camera installations and the MOSAIC implementation.

Management attributes the build to NYC installation billing milestones ($33.1M of NYCDOT unbilled revenue disclosed) and the rental-car transitions, which would be self-reversing. The less benign reading is structurally slower NYCDOT payment terms; the company's own risk list flags NYCDOT "timing of payments." One half-year does not distinguish these, and the intra-quarter revolver activity indicates real timing pressure.

### The balance sheet screens worse than it is

A standardized Altman Z-Score of 1.62 places Verra in the distress zone. That reading is driven by structurally thin book equity in a goodwill-heavy, buyback-shrunk capital structure, and it is computed from FY2025 data that predates the Avis event. Additional paid-in capital of $541.5M is offset by a $308.1M accumulated deficit built from post-2018 purchase-accounting amortization, $201.5M of Parking write-offs, and roughly $384M of share retirements since 2024. Goodwill of $676.8M is 42% of assets.

The credit metrics tell a different story: net debt $993.2M and net leverage 2.4x on $411.8M TTM adjusted EBITDA; derived first-lien net leverage 1.54x; interest coverage 6.6x TTM and 5.9x on guided EBITDA; current ratio 2.06; term loan extended to October 2032 at SOFR+200 in the October 2025 refinancing; notes fixed at 5.50% to April 2029; revolver to October 2030. Roughly 34% of debt is fixed, so each 100bp move in rates is ~$6.8M of annual interest on $683.6M floating — a live consideration given the current rate regime.

A ratio screen misses two adverse items. First, the Q2 10-Q fair-value footnote shows the term loan at $618.7M against $677.6M carrying (~90.5¢) and notes at $308.0M against $348.1M (~88.0¢); at December 31 the term loan's fair value *exceeded* carrying. Roughly $99M, or 9.6%, of market value left the debt stack in six months. That is credit-market confirmation that the earnings reset is substantive, though 10% is far short of distress pricing. Second, liabilities-to-equity worsened from 4.62x to 6.23x as equity fell $69.3M to $223.6M, and net leverage rises mechanically to 2.72x on guided EBITDA. A Tax Receivable Agreement liability of $38.7M payable to Lakeside Smart Holdco L.P. (~$5.3M annually) sits ahead of equity and equals 5.6% of market capitalization.

## Growth Analysis

FY2025 was the best growth year of the prior four (+11.4%), aided by roughly $38.4M of non-recurring New York red-light camera installation revenue. FY2026 inflects to decline, and consensus sees no recovery in 2027: next-year EPS estimates have been cut from $1.57 to $0.95 over ninety days, with the current quarter down 33.0% and the next quarter down 37.3%. Estimates are still falling — 7.8% off next year in the last thirty days alone. This estimate-revision pattern is the single strongest argument against buying the multiple.

Three levers determine whether the decline is one-time or ongoing:

**Volume.** TSA passenger throughput grew less than 1% in FY2025 and ran approximately 1% *below* prior year in Q2 2026. Commercial Services revenue still grew 5.5%, attributed to product adoption and tolling activity — penetration and take-rate rather than traffic. With volume flat-to-down and price resetting lower, both levers now work against the segment.

**Government Solutions.** The genuine growth vector. The Los Angeles City Council selected Verra for what press reporting describes as California's largest speed safety program at 125 sites; the company confirmed the selection but disclosed no contract value, timing, or margin. The NYCDOT contract adds a $998M five-year base. Nine U.S. toll facilities covering more than 100 miles were added in FY2025. Against that: 24% segment margins, $135M of capex, negative H1 incremental margins, and government counterparties that extract liquidated damages and service credits.

**International.** Shrinking. Q2 international revenue fell 22% to $25.8M — Australia $13.8M (from $16.1M), UK $4.7M (from $8.1M), Canada $5.3M (from $7.1M) following the Ontario exit that triggered a $9.4M impairment in FY2025.

Growth prospects score 3.5: revenue and EBITDA both contracting in FY2026, the profitable segment repricing lower with volume risk newly transferred to Verra, and the growing segment consuming capital at a third of the margin.

Cheapness alone does not establish mispricing, and the structural case for a discount is real: the profit engine's pricing power was demonstrably weaker than the market assumed, a third reset is ahead, and estimates continue falling. The valuation question reduces to normalized EBITDA and the multiple applied to it.

Taking the implied H2 2026 EBITDA of $168.3M and weighting H2 at its FY2025 seasonal share of 51.7% gives a crude annualized run rate near $326M. That brackets normalized EBITDA at roughly $300–340M, with a bull path back toward $370M if the resets prove shallower than guidance implies and Government Solutions margins recover once installation work rolls off.

## Competitive Landscape

Verra's 10-K claims that no single company competes across all its segments. The 2026 events show why breadth is a weaker moat than it reads. The binding constraint is customer bargaining power, and the 10-K named it twice in Item 1 before it materialized: competition "from our own customers, who may choose to invest in developing internal solutions," and vendors offering new technologies or "financial models."

The mechanism is straightforward. The U.S. rental-car industry is a three-firm oligopoly; Verra sells the same service to all three; each can benchmark the others. Integration complexity — 50-plus tolling authority relationships, transponder-to-vehicle association, DMV interfaces in 17 states, driver-level billing and collections — was deep enough that Avis withdrew its termination notice rather than switching providers. It was not deep enough to preserve the rents. The outcome is a seven-year contract at materially worse pricing under which Avis "will have the option to selectively perform certain activities internally" and can modulate enrolled fleet volume. Hertz's five-year extension carries the same volume-modulation right.

This affects how the recurring-revenue statistic should be read. Verra defines recurring revenue as everything except product sales, 93.8% of FY2025 revenue. The definition is unchanged; the durability underneath a portion of it now depends on customer election.

Concentration has not improved in the place that matters. Three Commercial Services customers were 11.0%, 12.8%, and 10.5% of Q2 total revenue — 34.3% combined, down from 36.9%. But 34.3% of $263.6M is $90.4M against segment revenue of $115.1M, or 78.6% of the segment, versus 79.8% a year earlier. The company-wide improvement is a denominator effect from New York installation revenue. Add NYCDOT at 21.8% of Q2 revenue (and 28.5% of net receivables) and four counterparties are roughly 56% of revenue.

Competitive position score 3.0 on trajectory: rents compressing in the high-margin segment, volume risk newly absorbed, international revenue down 22%, Parking written down $201.5M, and a third negotiation ahead against a counterparty that can now observe two completed resets.

## Risk Assessment

**The third rental-car reset.** Disclosed as expected within twelve months. Both completed resets were materially less favorable. This is the largest single unquantified variable and, on the evidence, the base case should assume a further concession.

**Volume modulation.** Verra now bears fleet-volume risk it previously did not. Its own language: fluctuations "could have a material adverse effect."

**Securities litigation.** *Otucu v. Verra Mobility*, filed June 4, 2026 in the District of Arizona, alleges §10(b)/§20(a) violations for a class period of February 24 to May 26, 2026. No loss contingency recorded as of June 30. The FY2025 10-K's twice-stated disclosure of customer-insourcing risk is a relevant defense fact; the merits are unresolved.

**Leadership vacuum.** Jon Keyser is Interim President and CEO following David Roberts' June 1 departure after roughly twelve years; a search firm is engaged. Retention equity and cash bonus agreements are in place for the Interim CEO, CFO, and Chief Customer Officer.

**Regulatory model risk.** Bills in multiple states would limit what rental companies may charge for transponder use, cap toll-processing administrative fees, and impose disclosure requirements, with attorneys general focused on rental tolling programs. Because Verra's economics depend partly on driver-billed convenience fees, this reaches the revenue model directly. Government Solutions faces the mirror risk of declining political acceptance of automated enforcement.

**Disclosure and control risk.** A collapse to one reportable segment would remove Commercial Services profit visibility — the disclosure that makes the concentration analysis above possible. A new global ERP was implemented in Q2 concurrent with the leadership transition and reorganization; management concluded controls were effective.

**Further impairment.** Parking goodwill of $38.0M remains, capping downside there. The stated impairment triggers included a sustained share-price decline, loss of a significant customer, and leadership change — two of which are consolidated-level events, meaning the write-down partly reflects a higher discount rate on unchanged cash flows.

Risk factors score 3.5: an 80% decline has priced a great deal, but two binary items (third reset, litigation) remain live and unquantified.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 174.5% on May 28 to 70.4% on August 17, while the price rose 13.4%. The options market has retired the survival scenario it was pricing in late May. Put/call open interest of 0.36 is call-tilted, so there is no aggressive downside positioning despite the drawdown, and short interest of 9.6% of float at 2.3 days to cover is not crowded. Both readings support a "repriced and now uncertain" interpretation rather than an accelerating collapse. At 70.4% IV is extraordinarily elevated in absolute terms for a business-services company — the options market agrees with our view that the distribution of outcomes remains wide, which argues against sizing a directional position here.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $9.75 (+113%)

Guidance under an interim CEO with an incentive to reset the bar proves conservative. Second-half EBITDA lands at or above the implied $168.3M, the $30M working capital use reverses as New York installation milestones bill and collect, and receivable days retrace toward 108. The third rental-car reset is milder than the first two because that customer's alternatives are worse. Camera installation revenue rolls off, restoring Government Solutions margins toward 28%, while the Los Angeles program and NYCDOT ramp add recurring revenue at normal margins. Realignment savings surface in 2027. Normalized EBITDA stabilizes near $370M; the board resumes buybacks with $66.3M of authorization, retiring up to 9.5% of shares below $6; a permanent CEO articulates a capital plan. At 7x on $370M, equity is $9.68.

### Bear Case — $3.00 (−34%)

The third reset removes another $25M of EBITDA, and Avis exercises its insourcing and volume-modulation options, taking enrolled vehicles down. NYCDOT payment terms prove structurally slower, so the working capital drag persists rather than reversing and FY2026 FCF lands below $100M. Government Solutions margin dilution turns out to be the new steady state rather than installation mix. Normalized EBITDA settles near $290M, net leverage rises above 3.4x, the debt stack trades further below par, and the segment structure collapses to one reportable unit, removing the disclosure that would let outside investors verify any recovery. At 5.25x on $290M, equity is $2.98.

### Base Case — $5.95 (+30%)

Normalized EBITDA of $330M at 5.75x. Avis and Hertz economics are locked for five to seven years at lower unit prices; the third reset costs $15–25M; Government Solutions growth partly offsets, at dilutive margins and with elevated capex. FCF runs $100–120M, servicing $62M of interest with room to reduce debt or repurchase stock. Leverage drifts to 2.8–3.0x without approaching covenant or maturity pressure.

Probability-weighting 40% base, 20% bull, 40% bear yields an expected return near +22%. That return is positive, which is why this is no longer an AVOID. It is also produced by a distribution spanning −34% to +113% on a variable the company has not disclosed, which is why it is not a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 print in early November — the first quarter to contain the Avis transition (effective September) and the first opportunity to test whether the receivable build reverses.

**Base case $5.95 (+30%)** — 5.75x EV/EBITDA on $330M normalized, giving EV $1,898M less $993M net debt = $905M equity over 152M shares. Consistent with the $6.00 analyst median.
**Bull case $9.75 (+113%)** — 7x on $370M. Consistent with the $10.00 high target.
**Bear case $3.00 (−34%)** — 5.25x on $290M.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Q3/Q4 results delivering H2 adjusted EBITDA at or above the implied $168.3M, with receivables plus unbilled days retracing below 115 and FY2026 FCF at or above the $110M midpoint.
2. The third Commercial Services renewal signed without a third guidance cut, plus FY2027 EBITDA guidance at or above $340M.
3. Buyback resumed at or below $6.00 and a permanent CEO named with a stated capital allocation framework.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A third guidance cut, or disclosure that a rental-car customer has exercised fleet-volume modulation or insourced a material activity.
2. Receivables plus unbilled days above 135, FY2026 FCF below $100M, or revolver borrowings outstanding at a quarter-end.
3. Net leverage above 3.25x, or a further decline in disclosed debt fair values below roughly 85¢.
4. Collapse to a single reportable segment, removing Commercial Services profit disclosure.
5. A recorded loss accrual in *Otucu*.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release, expected early November 2026.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Verra generates 38%-plus EBITDA margins on a 94%-recurring revenue base, covers interest 6.6x, faces no maturity before April 2029, and guides to $105–115M of free cash flow — a 15.8% yield on a $695M equity. At 4.0x guided earnings and 4.6x EV/EBITDA, the price already embeds substantial permanent decline. The prior AVOID rested on an existential question about the largest customer, and that question has been answered: Avis signed for seven years and Hertz for five.

The reason to go no further is specific. The market is not wrong that pricing power in the segment producing roughly two-thirds of profit was overestimated; management confirmed as much in its own contract language, the credit market took 9.6% out of the debt stack, and next-year consensus has been cut 39.6% and is still falling. Verra has converted an existential risk into an annuity haircut of undisclosed size, locked in for five to seven years, while accepting volume risk it previously did not bear and facing one more negotiation against a counterparty that can now observe two completed resets. With equity at 41% of enterprise value, the gap between $290M and $370M of normalized EBITDA is the difference between $3.00 and $9.75 per share. No insider has bought a share in the open market since the collapse, while withholding-price data shows executives crossing $16.79, $13.79, and $4.90 without a single purchase.

Existing holders have adequate reason to wait for the November print. New capital should require either the third renewal resolved without a further cut, or a price near $3.50 that provides margin of safety against the bear case.
