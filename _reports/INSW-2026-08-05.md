## Executive Summary

International Seaways is a nearly debt-free, spot-exposed international tanker owner in the middle of the best rate environment since 2022. Q1 2026 VLCC spot TCE printed $86,693/day against $33,531/day a year earlier (+159%); Suezmax rose 120%, LR1 158%, MR 74%. GAAP net income was $286.1M in one quarter versus $309.3M for all of FY2025. The company has responded by selling ships rather than buying them — seven vessels averaging 17 years old went for ~$216M net at ~$88M of gains in Q1 alone, 19 vessels across FY2025 and Q1 2026 — and by raising the minimum dividend payout to 85% of adjusted net income plus a discretionary top-up. The Q1 dividend of $4.55/share (~$225M) equals roughly 116% of adjusted net income, with management explicitly citing vessel-sale proceeds as support for the increase.

Two facts define the investment case and pull in opposite directions. First, the rate spike is a second-order consequence of the Arabian Gulf conflict that began February 28, 2026: INSW has no vessels in the AG, so it is capturing re-routing ton-miles and the withdrawal of AG-trapped tonnage from effective supply rather than war-risk premia. That is a cleaner exposure than owning ships in the war zone, and it is also entirely contingent on the disruption persisting. Diplomatic activity aimed at restoring freedom of navigation is now explicit and dated in press reporting. Second, the earning asset base is shrinking: revenue days fell to 5,799 in Q1 2026 from 6,635 (–12.6%), operating dwt from ~8.4M to 7.635M in one quarter, the operating fleet from 70 vessels to 64. Only two LR1 newbuilds remain to deliver (by September 2026) and no new orders have been announced, against an industry orderbook that went from 7% of the fleet at end-2023 to ~17% at end-2025, with the VLCC orderbook alone growing 19.0M dwt in FY2025 for 2027-2029 delivery.

The valuation reflects the good news. At $92.57 the stock is 2.09x March book value of $44.37, 6.8x FY2026 consensus EPS of $13.59, and 12.80x FY2027 consensus of $7.23 — and consensus itself models sharp normalization, with the current quarter at $5.26 falling to $2.04 the following quarter. A rough NAV estimate of $60-68/share implies a 36-54% premium, which is historically unusual for tanker equity. The variable dividend remains substantial — even on consensus normalization, an 85% payout on the next four quarters of estimates implies roughly $9-10/share, or ~10% of the current price — but that cash is being distributed out of a shrinking asset base at a premium to book, which mechanically raises the multiple on the remaining stub. This is a HOLD: own it if you do, collect the distributions, and do not add here.

## Company Overview

INSW owns and operates international-flag crude and product tankers, incorporated in the Marshall Islands and headquartered in New York. At 3/31/2026 the operating fleet was 64 vessels / 7.635M dwt (56 owned, 8 chartered-in) plus three LR1 newbuilds under construction: Crude Tankers of 27 vessels / 5.52M dwt (10 VLCCs, 13 Suezmaxes, 4 Aframaxes) and Product Carriers of 37 vessels / 2.12M dwt (1 LR2, 8 LR1s, 28 MRs). Average fleet age at year-end 2025 was 10.9 years. Corporate overhead is thin: 2,763 employees at 12/31/25, of whom 2,697 were seafarers and 66 shoreside; FY2025 G&A was $50.2M on $843.3M of revenue.

The commercial model runs through pools — $248.5M of Q1 2026's $325.5M of shipping revenue (76%) came through pool arrangements. The strategically notable change is that INSW moved from pool participant to pool owner: on/around January 27, 2026 it bought CMB.Tech's 50% of Tankers (UK) Agencies Limited, the commercial manager of the Tankers International VLCC pool, taking it to 100% ownership for $4.5M net cash and recognizing $7.4M of goodwill (the first on its balance sheet) plus a $3.9M non-cash holding gain. Tankers International then launched a proprietary Suezmax pool in March 2026, and INSW is migrating Suezmax tonnage onto its own platform.

Exposure is predominantly spot: 82% of FY2025 TCE revenue, and at 4/1/2026 only 14 vessels were on time charter with a 1.4-year average duration and ~$223M of future contracted revenue excluding profit share. The equity is a leveraged claim on spot rates with a thin fixed floor. Three dual-fuel LNG VLCCs on profit-sharing time charters are the reason "fixed" VLCC TCE printed at $128,264/day in Q1 2026, above the spot rate — those contracts are themselves rate-linked.

FY2025 was the third consecutive year of declining revenue. The composition matters: Crude Tanker daily TCE actually rose to $42,510 from $41,345, while Product Carrier TCE fell 22% to $24,787 from $31,846. Products were 48% of FY2025 TCE, so that decline set the year. The inflection began in Q4 2025 (VLCC spot $75,566/day versus $34,809 in Q3) and accelerated through Q1 2026.

**Margin quality.** Q1 2026's ~89% GAAP operating margin is not an operating result: it includes the $88.2M vessel-disposal gain and the $3.9M holding gain, both sitting inside operating income. Excluding them, income from vessel operations was roughly $200M on $325.5M of revenue (~62%) — still exceptional, and a fair illustration of operating leverage. Vessel expenses *fell* to $61.0M from $67.0M on a smaller, younger fleet, and G&A fell to $9.3M from $13.2M. The cost side is not inflating with revenue, though management does disclose rising bunker and insurance costs from the conflict.

**Cash conversion.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $141.1M against $286.1M of net income. The bridge: +$40.6M D&A, +$1.5M stock comp, +$1.3M financing-cost amortization, –$88.2M disposal gain, –$3.9M holding gain, –$13.9M drydock, and –$82.0M from working capital. Excluding the working-capital swing, underlying OCF was ~$223.1M. The driver is identifiable: voyage receivables rose from $177.9M at 12/31/25 to $242.5M at 3/31/26 (+$64.6M) as pool distributions and voyage settlements lagged a sharp rate spike. Two implications follow. Neither quarter's OCF should be annualized. And if rates plateau or fall, this reverses into a cash inflow that will temporarily flatter OCF while earnings decline — a point worth remembering when reading the Q3 or Q4 cash flow statement.

FY2025 free cash flow, derived: OCF $380.1M less ~$147.3M of vessel/construction/other capex ≈ $232.8M. FY2025 dividends of $144.6M were 38% of OCF and 62% of that FCF estimate. The Q1 2026 dividend of ~$225M is a materially higher payout ratio, funded in part by the $216M of vessel-sale proceeds.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/2026: total assets $2,871.3M, total liabilities $677.1M, equity $2,194.3M (76.4% of assets). Debt $602.1M against cash $141.8M plus short-term investments $235.0M, giving derived net debt of $225.2M — 9.3% of net debt plus equity, with company-reported net loan-to-value below 7% and total liquidity ~$918M including a $541M undrawn revolver. FY2025 was a deliberate deleveraging year: a $250M 7.125% senior unsecured 2030 bond issued at par in September, a Korean ECA facility at SOFR+125bps secured on the LR1 newbuilds, and the November exercise of purchase options on six VLCCs under the Ocean Yield lease for $257.8M, which eliminated ~$22M/year of mandatory amortization and lifted unencumbered vessels to 31 of 70 (44%).

Covenant headroom is not a live issue: maximum leverage ≤0.60:1.00 against ~0.09 net; current assets excluding current debt of $666.5M against $62.6M; collateral FMV ≥135% of principal. Maturities are spread 2030-2037. There is no refinancing wall. One unexplained item: at 12/31/25 fixed-rate debt with $496.7M carrying value was marked at $463.2M fair value, roughly 93 cents, three months after the 7.125% bond priced at par. The driver was not isolable from the disclosure.

**Tax.** FY2025 pretax income of $308.9M carried an income tax *benefit* of $0.4M. This rests on US Section 883 relief for international shipping income — which fails if 5% shareholders collectively own ≥50% of the stock for more than half the taxable year, with a penalty of 4% on 50% of gross US-source shipping income with no deductions — and on Bermuda's international-shipping exclusion from its new 15% corporate income tax, following redomiciliation of vessel-owning subsidiaries from the Marshall Islands and Liberia to Bermuda between September and December 2025. Nothing suggests either test is currently at risk, but the effective tax rate is a structural feature that could change without any operational deterioration.

**A data conflict to note:** the market-data feed reports trailing and forward P/E both at 8.43 alongside EPS of $6.23, which do not reconcile at any recent price. Derived TTM GAAP EPS is approximately $11.00 (FY2025 $309.3M less Q1'25 $49.6M, plus Q1'26 $286.1M, over ~49.7M diluted shares), which does reconcile to the 8.4x trailing multiple. The $6.23 figure appears stale and is not used here. Note that ~$120M+ of that TTM figure is vessel-sale gains; ex-gains TTM EPS is roughly $8.50, or ~10.9x.

The revision-velocity signal that would normally support a BUY does not apply cleanly here for two reasons. It is a peak-cycle rather than trough inflection: the revisions are pricing a war premium into a commodity rate, not a durable business improvement, and consensus itself says so by cutting FY2027 to roughly half of FY2026. And the earning asset base is contracting. Revenue days –12.6% year-over-year, dwt –9% in one quarter, VLCCs from 12 to 10, MRs from 33 to 28. Any model that applies Q1 rate levels to a static fleet overstates INSW's earnings; the correct exercise applies rising rates to a falling day count.

The supply picture is the structural constraint on multi-year growth. The reason VLCC rates exploded is that essentially no VLCCs delivered in 2025 (fleet growth +0.6M dwt, roughly two ships net) into a market whose ton-miles lengthened sharply. The response is already ordered: the VLCC orderbook grew 19.0M dwt in FY2025 alone (roughly 60+ VLCC-equivalents), Suezmax +6.4M dwt, taking the total tanker orderbook to ~17% of the existing fleet from 7% two years earlier, with delivery windows in 2027-2029. Underlying demand growth is unremarkable — the 10-K cites IEA global consumption of ~104.0 mb/d in 2025 rising ~1.0% to ~105.0 mb/d in 2026. What is driving rates is ton-mile inefficiency and barrels on water, which is precisely the kind of demand that reverses quickly when the dislocation clears, arriving into a materially larger fleet.

## Valuation Assessment

At the $92.57 basis:

- **Book value:** $2,194.275M / 49.46M basic shares = $44.37 → **2.09x P/B**. The ~$225M June dividend reduces equity by ~$4.55/share, roughly offset by Q2 earnings, so mid-year P/B is likely ~2.05-2.10x.
- **Trailing:** ~8.4x TTM GAAP EPS of ~$11.00; ~10.9x excluding vessel-sale gains.
- **Peak sensitivity (not a run rate):** 4 × Q1 adjusted EPS of $3.90 = $15.60 → 5.9x. 4 × Q1 adjusted EBITDA of $243.6M = $974M against derived EV of ~$4.81B → **4.9x EV/EBITDA**.
- **Consensus:** 6.81x FY2026 ($13.59) and **12.80x FY2027 ($7.23)**.
- **Normalized:** FY2025 net income excluding ~$42.5M of sale gains was ~$266.8M, or $5.38/share → **17.2x**. On FY2024 earnings, ~10.7x.
- **Dividend:** the $0.12 regular quarterly is a 0.52% yield. The Q1 variable dividend of $4.55 annualized would be 19.7%, which is a sensitivity embedding peak rates, an above-policy discretionary component, and asset-sale proceeds. A more defensible forward estimate applies the 85% minimum to the next four quarters of consensus ($5.26 + $2.04 + Q4 + FY2027 partial) at ~$9-10/share, ~10-11% of price.

**NAV.** Disclosed vessel carrying value at 12/31/25 was $2,073.1M. The best market evidence is the Q1 sales: seven vessels for $222.8M of proceeds against an $88.2M gain implies ~$134.7M of net book value, a ~1.65x realization. Applying a 1.4-1.6x mark to the fleet and adding cash and short-term investments ($376.8M), receivables ($268.2M), construction in progress ($64.2M) and pool deposits ($27.6M), less total liabilities ($677.1M), yields roughly $2.96-3.38B, or **$60-68/share**. At $92.57 that is a **36-54% premium**. This is an analyst estimate with wide error bars, and the contrary evidence must be stated: the 12/31/25 impairment disclosure showed ten vessels carried *above* market — nine MRs at $327.2M carrying versus ~$283.9M market, and one VLCC $1.7M above — so a blanket 1.4-1.6x uplift is not supported by the company's own year-end marks. Those marks predate the 2026 rate surge and secondhand values have almost certainly risen. No independent broker valuation was obtained. Treat the premium as a hypothesis with a plausible range, not a measurement. It is, however, directionally hard to construct a NAV at or above $92.57 without assuming that peak asset values persist.

**Peers.** The supplied comparable set is mostly midstream infrastructure — KNTK 19.3x, AM 24.9x, DTM 31.1x, VNOM negative — with fee-based or contracted cash flows that bear no resemblance to spot tanker economics. A median P/E of 24.9 is not a relevant anchor and should not be read as evidence INSW is cheap. The one genuine shipping comparable, Dorian LPG, trades at 9.6x with a 6.8% yield, 8% below its 52-week high. Against that reference INSW's trailing multiple is not a discount.

**Mechanics worth stating plainly.** Paying out 85%+ of earnings while selling ships and ordering nothing means INSW is distributing NAV. When a company trading above book distributes cash, price and book fall by the same absolute amount, so P/B mechanically rises. Each large dividend therefore leaves a progressively more expensive stub on book value unless the earnings power justifying the premium persists. That is the arithmetic reason a 2.09x multiple and a 19% headline yield are not independent positives.

**Valuation score: 4.0.** The stock is cheap on trailing and current-year earnings and expensive on every normalized measure — NAV premium, 12.8x on a FY2027 consensus that is itself 34% above normalized FY2025, and 17.2x on FY2025 ex-gains. Analyst targets (low $88, mean $98.67, median $97, high $120) bracket the price tightly, with the current price only 6.2% below the mean; on a cyclical at peak rates, targets are not independent confirmation of upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Tanker shipping is a fragmented, price-taking industry where returns are set by vessel supply, voyage distance and utilization, not firm-level differentiation. Within that, INSW's positioning is above average on three counts and deteriorating on one.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt of $225M against $2.19B of equity, ~$918M of liquidity, maturities beginning 2030 and 44% of vessels unencumbered. In an industry whose historical mechanism of permanent capital loss is a covenant breach or a forced sale at the trough, this materially reduces downside risk and is what permits an 85%+ payout that levered peers cannot match.

**Commercial platform.** Owning 100% of the commercial manager of the TI VLCC pool and launching a proprietary Suezmax pool converts INSW from tonnage provider to platform owner. Scale in a pool improves cargo access and utilization, and the platform can aggregate third-party ships without INSW taking asset risk. A new "Other Operating Income" line appeared at $1.9M in Q1 2026 (nil a year earlier); the most plausible source is third-party pool management fees consolidated through TUKA, though the company does not label it, so that attribution is inference.

**Counter-cyclical asset behaviour.** While the industry orderbook went from 7% to 17% of the fleet in two years, INSW ordered nothing beyond the six pre-existing LR1s and sold 19 vessels. Whether one reads this as a considered view that assets are dear or simply as opportunistic harvesting, the effect is the same: INSW is not adding capacity into the delivery wave that peers are creating.

**The offset.** Fewer ships earn less in any rate environment. Diversification across crude (52% of FY2025 TCE) and products (48%) smooths but does not remove cyclicality — in FY2025 it hurt, as products fell 22% while crude rose 3%. And note a labelling quirk: all seven LR1s trade in the Panamax International pool carrying crude exclusively, so the segment split understates crude exposure. The small US Gulf lightering business is shrinking (operating income before D&A and G&A of $7.8M in FY2025 versus $23.3M in FY2024, on 329 service-support lighterings versus 459).

Trajectory is genuinely mixed — commercially improving, physically shrinking — which supports a mid-range score rather than a high one.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The conflict is both the earnings driver and the principal risk.** The Q1 2026 10-Q added a new risk factor in unusually stark language: hostilities began February 28, 2026; by late April more than 30 merchant vessels had been attacked in the Arabian Gulf and Strait; Iran declared a blockade and may have mined portions of the AG; traffic through the Strait fell approximately 90-95%, "largely resulting in a continued closure of the Strait as a practical matter." Roughly 20% of world oil consumption normally transits Hormuz. INSW has no vessels in the AG and none have been involved in attacks — so it earns from re-routing ton-miles and withdrawn tonnage rather than war-risk voyages, which is a lower-tail-risk way to capture the dislocation but also confers no privileged position and is fully shared with competitors. Management's own bull bridge — CEO Zabrocky's expectation that "as conditions normalize, we would still expect tanker markets to benefit from the rebalancing of trade flows and the replenishment of inventories" — is an expectation, not contracted cash flow. Independent reporting through early August confirms the disruption persists (vessels turning back after attacks in July; three commodity transits on a single mid-July day) alongside active diplomatic efforts to restore navigation. A verified reopening is the single most probable near-term catalyst and it is negative for INSW's rates.

**2. The orderbook.** 17% of the fleet, with the VLCC book up 19.0M dwt in one year for 2027-2029 delivery, against ~1% annual demand growth. This is the cure being manufactured for the current rate level.

**3. A dated policy catalyst around November 2026.** US fees on China-built or Chinese-operated vessels entering US ports took effect October 2025; China imposed reciprocal measures; both regimes were suspended for one year on November 10, 2025. INSW discloses 13 China-built owned vessels (nine above the 55,000 dwt threshold and thus potentially subject), one China-built chartered-in vessel, and three non-Chinese-built vessels bareboat-chartered from a Chinese financial institution. Fee amounts, exemptions and any extension are unresolved, so the P&L magnitude is not estimable, but the date is fixed.

**4. Impairment exposure on the older product fleet.** Nine MRs were carried $43.3M above market at 12/31/25 with no impairment taken. That exposure has likely receded with 2026 asset values, and five old MRs were sold at a gain in Q1, but it marks where pressure reappears in a downturn.

**5. Tax-status fragility.** An effectively untaxed income statement depends on Section 883 (which turns on the shareholder register, outside management's control) and the Bermuda shipping exclusion.

**6. Regulatory framework unresolved.** The October 2025 extraordinary MEPC session failed to adopt the IMO net-zero framework and deferred to an expected October 2026 session. EU ETS reaches 100% phase-in in 2026 from 70% in 2025; FuelEU Maritime costs are largely passed to charterers.

**7. Verification gaps.** Three items could not be read: a May 11, 2026 8-K reporting an Item 1.01 material definitive agreement (subject unknown — potentially financing, a vessel transaction or a newbuild contract); a June 12, 2026 8-K carrying Items 5.02 and 5.07 (at least one board change plus annual-meeting voting results); and the terms of the Second Amended and Restated Rights Agreement executed April 9, 2026, one day before the prior plan's expiry — trigger threshold, term and whether ratification was sought are all unverified. Renewing a rights plan after a 4.7x five-year TSR is consistent with defensive positioning against opportunistic accumulation in a consolidating sector, and equally consistent with routine continuation. No motive should be treated as established, but the unread filings are a real information deficit for a $4.6B issuer.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 62.1% against 38.0% one-year historical volatility is rich (24.1pp spread, 76th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (62.1% at 1m versus 54.1% at 6m) — the market is pricing an imminent event, almost certainly the Q2 print due within days. The Aug 21 expiry implies a ±10.6% move. ATM IV rose 6.9pp since mid-July while the price rose 4.6%, so uncertainty and price advanced together rather than vol falling into strength.

The most notable change is positioning: put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 0.35 to 0.02, and put/call volume ratio is 0.00 at the front tenor. Open interest is almost entirely calls. Short interest is a modest 6.2% of float at 3.4 days to cover. Taken together, there is very little downside protection outstanding and no crowded short — consistent with consensus-long positioning into the print. The data source characterises the 1m skew of 1.1% as balanced; the 3m and 6m skew readings are –4.4% and –5.1%, reported without a directional inference. For a holder, expensive near-dated volatility argues for monetising rather than buying protection; for a prospective buyer, paying up 5.8% below the 52-week high into a ±10.6% implied move with no put support is poor entry timing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $118 (+27%)

The disruption persists into 2027. Transits remain suppressed, ton-miles stay long, and the VLCC orderbook does not deliver until 2027-2029. Q2 beats the $5.26 consensus (four consecutive beats averaging 25% surprise), the accompanying dividend approaches or exceeds $4.50, and consensus FY2027 continues the +12.2% 30-day trend upward toward $9-10. Even on partial normalization, management's rebalancing-and-restocking thesis provides a bridge: inventories rebuild, trade flows re-optimise, and rates settle above pre-conflict levels rather than at them. Meanwhile INSW keeps selling 15-18-year-old tonnage at ~1.65x book into a strong asset market and distributing the proceeds, so holders receive a meaningful portion of the return in cash regardless of the multiple. Near-zero leverage means no forced action at any point in the cycle, and $918M of liquidity allows opportunistic secondhand acquisition if values correct. $118 is roughly 16x FY2027 consensus of $7.23 or 7.5x a sustained $15+ earnings run rate, and sits below the $120 high analyst target.

### Bear Case — $62 (–33%)

A navigation agreement is reached. Transit normalizes, the re-routing premium evaporates within a quarter or two, and spot rates revert toward FY2025 levels where crude TCE was $42,510/day and products $24,787/day. Applied to a fleet with 13% fewer revenue days, normalized EPS lands near or below the FY2025 ex-gains figure of $5.38. The 85% payout mechanically collapses with earnings — the dividend is a percentage of profit, not a commitment. The multiple compresses toward NAV, as tanker equities historically do outside extreme spot markets, taking the price to $60-68; the low end of that range is reinforced by the 17%-of-fleet orderbook delivering into 2027-2029 and by the November 2026 port-fee reciprocity risk on 9-13 vessels. Impairment pressure returns to the older MR fleet. $62 is roughly 1.4x mid-2026 book value and near the midpoint of the NAV estimate.

The distribution of outcomes is unusual in that the bear case is triggered by a geopolitical de-escalation that is being actively negotiated and publicly telegraphed, while the bull case requires a war to continue. That is not a comfortable position from which to add exposure.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (do not add, do not reduce). Horizon: reassess at the Q2 print, due within days, and again at the November 2026 port-fee suspension expiry.

**Base case: $95 (+2.6% from $92.57)**, derived as ~13.1x FY2027 consensus EPS of $7.23. Total return over twelve months is dominated by distributions rather than price: applying the 85% minimum payout to the next four quarters of consensus implies roughly $9-10/share of dividends, ~10-11% of the current price, with the caveat that each dollar distributed reduces book value and therefore raises the multiple on the residual.

**Bull case: $118 (+27%)** | **Bear case: $62 (–33%)**

The asymmetry — roughly flat base case, 27% upside requiring conflict persistence, 33% downside on a resolution that is being actively negotiated — is what caps this at HOLD rather than BUY, and the balance sheet plus the distribution stream is what keeps it from being an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Price in the mid-$60s or below, i.e., at or near the NAV estimate, with rates still elevated. That combination restores asymmetry regardless of the geopolitical outcome.
2. Evidence that post-normalization rate strength is durable rather than war-contingent: two consecutive quarters in which crude TCE holds above ~$55,000/day *after* verified Hormuz transit normalization, or a material extension of the time-charter book (currently 14 vessels, 1.4 years, ~$223M) at rates above $50,000/day, which would convert spot optionality into contracted cash flow.
3. Redeployment of the ~$918M liquidity into secondhand tonnage at a discount to current asset values, which would convert the harvest strategy into a value-accretive cycle trade rather than a liquidation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Verified sustained Hormuz normalization — 72+ hours of uninterrupted commercial transit or a signed freedom-of-navigation agreement — without a corresponding decline in the share price. At $92.57 the equity is not priced for reversion.
2. FY2027 consensus reversing below ~$5.50 (currently $7.23), which would remove the +12.2% 30-day revision support and place the stock above 17x on normalized earnings.
3. A dividend declaration materially below the 85% policy minimum, or an impairment charge on the MR fleet, either of which would signal that management's own asset-value view has turned.
4. Lapse of the US–China port-fee suspension in November 2026 without exemption for the nine-plus affected vessels, alongside softening rates.

## Conclusion

INSW is a well-run, nearly unlevered asset owner executing a coherent harvest strategy at what may prove to be a cyclical peak: selling 15-18-year-old ships at ~1.65x book, ordering nothing into a 17%-of-fleet orderbook, and distributing 85%+ of earnings plus part of the sale proceeds. Financial health is genuinely strong — $225M net debt against $2.19B equity, $918M liquidity, no maturity before 2030, enormous covenant headroom. Management's capital allocation has been disciplined and the five-year TSR index of 470.75 against a peer index of 196.0 speaks to that.

The constraint is price and contingency. Revisit in the mid-$60s, or on evidence that rate strength survives normalization.
