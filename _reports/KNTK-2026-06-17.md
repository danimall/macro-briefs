**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kinetik Holdings is a Delaware Basin-focused midstream pure-play trading at $45.40 with a ~$3.4B Class A market capitalization. On the screen it presents as a cheap, high-yield infrastructure name: an 18.5x trailing P/E against a peer-median 24.9x, and a 7.1% dividend yield well above the peer-median 4.2%. Both of those headline metrics are misleading. The 18.5x trailing multiple is calculated on $2.63 of EPS that was inflated by a one-time $415.4M gain on the October 2025 EPIC pipeline sale; on the falling consensus forward estimate of $1.32 for the current year, the stock trades closer to 34x. The 7.1% yield is not covered by organic free cash flow after growth capex, and has been funded by serial asset sales and the revolver.

The fundamentals are deteriorating along the dimension that matters most. Consensus forward EPS has been cut 32% (current quarter) to 47% (next quarter) over the past 90 days, the next-year estimate is down 30% to $1.68, and Q1'26 produced a GAAP net loss of $(5.1)M on an 8% revenue decline and a 27% drop in service (fee) revenue. Adjusted EBITDA was essentially flat in 2025 (+2% to $987.7M) despite 19% revenue growth, because most of that growth was low-margin commodity flow-through, not high-margin fee expansion. Two specific overhangs compound the earnings problem: a bench trial in the Energy Transfer NGL-contract litigation concluded in spring 2026 with a verdict pending and reserves described as "immaterial," and I Squared Capital is liquidating its stake into the market (roughly $229M+ sold across February-April 2026).

We move KNTK from HOLD to AVOID. This reverses a four-report HOLD streak in which the composite score drifted from 5.5 to 5.1, and the current reading of 4.4 reflects fundamental deterioration rather than price action. The negative stockholders' equity and 0.11 Altman Z-Score are structural artifacts of the Up-C corporate form and should not be read as distress. The genuine concern is an uncovered dividend dependent on a finite supply of monetizable JV stakes, set against falling estimates and a binary litigation verdict, which is more dangerous than the screen suggests.

## Company Overview

Kinetik is an integrated midstream company concentrated entirely in the Delaware Basin (western Permian, Texas and New Mexico), where it is the fourth-largest natural gas processor by capacity. The business runs through two segments:

- **Midstream Logistics** (~99% of consolidated revenue, ~64% of Adjusted EBITDA): gas gathering and processing (4,200+ miles of pipe, 825,000+ HP compression, 2.4+ Bcf/d cryogenic processing across eight complexes), crude gathering and storage, and produced-water gathering and disposal. Essentially all commodity-price exposure sits here.
- **Pipeline Transportation** (~36% of Adjusted EBITDA): two equity-method interests (55.5% of Permian Highway Pipeline (PHP, operated by Kinder Morgan) and 33% of Breviloba/Shin Oak NGL (operated by Enterprise)) plus the wholly-owned Kinetik NGL and Delaware Link pipelines. More than 89% of this segment's EBITDA comes from JVs Kinetik does not operate.

The corporate structure is central to interpreting the financials. KNTK Class A is an Up-C holding company whose only significant asset is a ~42-45% LP interest in Kinetik Holdings LP. The remaining ~55-58% is held by Class C/Common Unit holders — sponsors Blackstone and I Squared Capital plus legacy APA/Apache holders. The minority interest (~$4.5B) is classified as redeemable noncontrolling interest in mezzanine equity, which pushes reported stockholders' equity negative. This is an accounting artifact, not a solvency event. The company was in compliance with all debt covenants at quarter-end.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth without profit growth.** Revenue climbed from $1.21B (2022) to $1.26B (2023) to $1.48B (2024) to $1.76B (2025, +19%). Adjusted EBITDA over the same span was nearly flat at $987.7M (+2%). Product revenue rose 23% to $1.31B on higher volumes and gas prices, but that flow-through carries thin margins. Operating income fell 8% to $164.9M as operating expenses rose 38%, partly on the Durango integration. GAAP net income of $525.9M was flattered by the $415.4M EPIC gain; underlying earnings are modest.

**Q1'26 deterioration.** Net loss of $(5.1)M versus +$19.3M in Q1'25, operating loss of $(3.8)M, revenue down 8% YoY. Reported Adjusted EBITDA held near $251M but leaned on add-backs of $47M of unrealized hedging losses and $11.6M of litigation costs. Service (fee) revenue fell 27% YoY; management attributes part of this to an agent-versus-principal accounting shift, but the trend warrants monitoring because fee revenue is the high-quality, less commodity-sensitive part of the model.

**Leverage and floating-rate exposure.** Total debt is ~$3.85B: $1.05B 6.625% notes due 2028, $1.0B 5.875% notes due 2030, a $1.15B term loan, ~$468M revolver, and ~$187M A/R facility. About $1.81B is floating-rate; a 1% rate move is ~$18M of annual interest before the ~$1.0B notional of swaps at ~3.1-3.4%. With the Fed holding at 3.50-3.75% as of early 2026, the floating book leaves no margin if guidance turns hawkish. The 2028 notes mature December 2028; refinancing is not imminent. Cash was just $0.7M at Q1'26, with ~$1.16B of revolver liquidity available.

**The dividend is the central financial concern.** The distribution is $0.81/quarter ($3.24 annualized, ~7.1% yield). In FY2025, operating cash flow of $604M faced capex of $492M plus dividends of $500M — roughly $992M of priority outflows. The ~$388M gap was plugged by the $504M EPIC sale and incremental debt, not organic generation. In Q1'26, OCF of $180M covered capex of $89M but not the full $131M dividend plus capex outflow, which was again funded with revolver draws. EMI distributions (~$68M/quarter from PHP and Breviloba) effectively fund a chunk of the payout, yet these come from non-operated JVs whose distributions Kinetik cannot control, and the EPIC sale just removed one of those streams. 2026 capex is guided to $450-510M, roughly 70% directed at New Mexico expansion. Management has stated it will not meaningfully expand buybacks "until gross debt is reduced below certain thresholds," which is the correct priority but also confirms the balance sheet has limited slack.

The screen's negative equity and 0.11 Z-Score are Up-C artifacts and do not signal bankruptcy risk. The real issue is a high payout sustained by a finite supply of monetizable JV stakes.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the case, and growth is the dimension that most reliably separates winners from losers. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across the board:

|--------|-------------|---------|---------|----------|----------|
| Current Quarter | $0.19 | $0.28 | $0.33 | -32.2% | -42.5% |
| Next Quarter | $0.25 | $0.40 | $0.48 | -36.4% | -47.5% |
| Current Year | $1.32 | $1.28 | $1.62 | +3.2% | -18.6% |
| Next Year | $1.68 | $2.01 | $2.41 | -16.4% | -30.4% |

This is negative earnings momentum, and the deterioration is accelerating at the quarterly level (30-day cuts deeper than the implied trend). The lone positive — a +3.2% current-year revision — is overwhelmed by the 16-30% cuts to next-year and forward-quarter numbers. The strong-buy-signal pattern (revisions up >30% with revenue accelerating) is precisely inverted here: revisions are down 30-47% while revenue contracted 8% in Q1.

There is a credible organic-growth pipeline. The Kings Landing complex (200+ MMcf/d) reached full service in September 2025, the ECCC Pipeline is expected in-service in Q2 2026, and the Kings Landing AGI/45Q carbon-sequestration project is targeted for year-end 2026. Eddy County, New Mexico provides early-mover positioning. These projects support volume growth. But the 2025 experience — 19% revenue growth converting to 2% EBITDA growth and an 8% operating-income decline — argues that incremental volumes are not flowing through to high-margin profit at the rate the top line implies. Until fee revenue stabilizes and EBITDA reaccelerates, the growth thesis is unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation looks cheap only on a distorted trailing number. The quoted 18.5x P/E rests on trailing EPS that included the EPIC gain. Stripping that out, and using the falling forward consensus, the picture inverts:

- On the $1.32 current-year estimate: ~35x forward P/E
- On the $1.68 next-year estimate: ~27x forward P/E

Against a peer-median P/E of 24.9x, KNTK is at best in line and arguably rich relative to peers that are not seeing 30-47% downward revisions. AM trades at 24.9x and DTM at 31.1x, but both are larger, more diversified midstream names without KNTK's single-basin concentration, dividend-coverage gap, or pending litigation verdict.

The 7.1% yield is the strongest pro-bull data point. But a yield is only a value support if it is covered, and this one is not covered by organic FCF after growth capex. The market is paying for the yield with a discount to peers on quality, not handing out a free 280bps of carry. Where dividend coverage depends on asset sales and a revolver, the high yield reads as a risk premium rather than a margin of safety.

Analyst targets sit above the current price — mean $52.57, median $52, low $48, high $61 — implying ~15% upside to the mean. We treat these as stale. The principle that analyst targets are floors rather than ceilings applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples; KNTK is the opposite case, with estimates falling sharply and a forward multiple at or above peers. When estimates are being cut 30-47% over 90 days, consensus price targets lag downward, not upward. The 11 buy/strong-buy versus 4 hold split is more likely to compress than to be validated by a re-rating.

A "cheap" trailing multiple paired with falling forward estimates and an uncovered payout is the classic value-trap configuration. We do not credit the headline P/E as an entry point.

## Competitive Landscape

Kinetik's #4 processor ranking in the Delaware Basin gives it genuine scale and relevance, and its focus on Eddy County, New Mexico positions it ahead of some peers in a growth sub-basin. But the competitive trajectory is stable-to-softening, not improving. The company competes against larger integrated players — Targa, Energy Transfer, Enterprise, ONEOK, Kinder Morgan — several of which are simultaneously its downstream takeaway counterparties and, in Energy Transfer's case, its courtroom adversary. KNTK lacks the diversification and downstream integration of the majors, and its single-basin concentration leaves it more exposed to Permian-specific issues (Waha takeaway constraints, negative gas pricing, producer consolidation, and seismicity-driven curtailment of disposal wells).

Two data points point to relative weakness. The decline in Q1 service revenue, even with the agent/principal explanation, suggests fee-based positioning is not strengthening. And total-shareholder-return indexed to 206 versus a peer-group 347 at YE2025 marks meaningful relative underperformance. The strategic model — recycle mature long-haul pipeline equity into organic Delaware/New Mexico growth — is coherent, but it depends on the company continuing to find buyers willing to pay premiums for its non-operated stakes, and on those organic projects generating returns above the cost of the capital recycled into them.

## Risk Assessment

**Energy Transfer litigation (material, near-term, under-reserved).** Energy Transfer GC NGL Product Services (formerly Lone Star) brought two consolidated suits alleging breach of two long-term NGL purchase agreements, seeking damages, declaratory relief, and specific performance. An adverse ruling — particularly one ordering specific performance on the NGL contracts — could be a meaningful negative surprise that the balance sheet has not provisioned for. This is a binary event not visible in any quantitative screen.

**Dividend sustainability.** The well of monetizable JV stakes is finite, and the EPIC sale removed one of the EMI cash streams that fund the payout. A dividend cut, while not announced, is a live downside scenario if asset-sale opportunities thin out or the revolver tightens.

**Sponsor overhang.** I Squared Capital's liquidation — 4.0M shares at $44.85 on February 26, 2026 (~$179M), multiple April blocks at $48-51 (~$50M+), and a 1.5M-unit conversion — combined with Blackstone's registration rights, is a multi-year supply headwind that caps near-term upside.

**Single-basin and regulatory concentration.** Essentially 100% Delaware Basin exposure. Texas RRC Seismic Response Areas can curtail or suspend produced-water injection, a direct threat to the water segment and indirectly to gathering volumes.

**Commodity and rate exposure.** Product revenue and a portion of opex track NGL/gas/condensate prices, generating large hedge mark-to-market swings. The floating-rate book is sensitive to a hawkish rate path.

Within the broader energy complex, midstream is more insulated than E&P from oil-price swings, since fee-based transport volumes matter more than the spot price. That cushion is real but does not offset the company-specific issues above.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 104.2% against a 1-year historical vol of 35.9% — a 68pp premium — with the term structure in steep backwardation (104.2% near-term versus 39.3% at three months) and the near-term IV in the 80th percentile of its own chain. ATM IV jumped from 57.0% to 88.0% over the past two weeks on a flat price, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.04 to 0.47. Put/call skew is balanced (-3.0%), so the market is pricing a binary near-term event with elevated two-sided uncertainty rather than a directional crash, consistent with the pending Energy Transfer verdict. The rich premium makes near-term options an expensive way to express any view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
KNTK is an underfollowed Delaware Basin pure-play with a 7% yield and a credible organic-growth pipeline (Kings Landing, ECCC in-service Q2 2026, AGI/45Q at year-end 2026). If fee revenue stabilizes, the New Mexico build-out converts to high-margin EBITDA, and the Energy Transfer verdict comes in benign or settles modestly, the dividend coverage gap narrows as growth capex moderates post-2026. Analyst targets to $52-61 imply 15-34% upside, and the sponsor overhang eventually clears, removing a technical cap. A calming of broad energy volatility could let the yield compress toward peers, supporting price. Bull case ~$58.

### Bear Case
The dividend is cut or the market loses confidence in its sustainability. Forward estimates continue falling, the Energy Transfer verdict orders specific performance or material damages against an "immaterial" reserve, and the I Squared unwind keeps supply on the tape. Fee revenue continues to erode, EBITDA stays flat while floating-rate interest expense pressures coverage, and the stock de-rates toward its 52-week low of $31.33 as the yield re-rates higher to reflect coverage risk. Bear case ~$36 (-21%).

The probability-weighted distribution favors the bear configuration given the direction of estimate revisions, the uncovered payout, and the under-reserved litigation. The cheap-looking trailing multiple does not provide downside protection because it is built on non-recurring earnings.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The two candidate catalysts are the pending Energy Transfer verdict (timing uncertain) and a potential dividend reassessment driven by the coverage gap. Neither is precisely dated. Continued estimate cuts are a slow-bleed catalyst rather than a discrete event.

**Timing window.** The verdict could land within weeks to a few months but is not on a fixed calendar; a dividend decision would come at a quarterly board action with no announced reassessment. The absence of a hard date weakens any short thesis.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 9.1% of float with 4.7 days to cover — moderate, neither crowded nor uncrowded. Sponsor selling adds supply that limits squeeze risk, but a benign verdict or maintained dividend could trigger a sharp relief rally given the depressed sentiment.

**Borrow and options signal.** The 104% near-term IV with steep backwardation makes options-based shorts expensive, and the balanced put/call skew shows the options market is not confirming a strongly bearish lean. Shorting the stock outright means paying the ~7% dividend, a steep carry cost on an uncertain timeline.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** A dividend cut or adverse verdict could take the stock toward $36 (-21%); upside risk on a benign verdict or broad energy relief is comparable in magnitude. With no dated catalyst, ~7% carry against the position, and roughly symmetric outcomes, the risk/reward falls below the 2:1 threshold required to short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish thesis is sound but lacks a precisely dated trigger, and the 7% dividend carry plus relief-rally risk make this a name to avoid owning rather than to short actively.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID with a 6-12 month horizon over which the negative earnings revisions and litigation verdict should resolve.

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit the name if: (1) organic free cash flow covers the dividend without asset-sale or revolver support for two consecutive quarters, demonstrated by OCF exceeding capex plus distributions; (2) the Energy Transfer litigation resolves benignly or via a quantified, manageable settlement, removing the under-reserved binary; (3) forward EPS estimate revisions turn positive and fee/service revenue stabilizes, confirming the New Mexico build-out is converting to high-margin EBITDA; and (4) the I Squared sponsor unwind substantially completes, clearing the supply overhang. A compression of the forward P/E below the next-year growth rate, or a yield that becomes covered, would also reset the value case.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — falling estimates, the verdict, the sponsor selling, the dividend-coverage gap — are likely to persist through at least the next two to three quarters, with the litigation verdict the most important near-term unknown.

## Conclusion

Kinetik screens as a cheap, high-yield midstream name, but the cheapness is an artifact of a one-time gain inflating trailing EPS, and the yield is not covered by organic free cash flow. On real forward earnings the stock trades at 27-35x against falling estimates, the dividend has been funded by a finite stream of JV-stake sales, Q1'26 swung to a GAAP loss, fee revenue fell 27%, an under-reserved Energy Transfer verdict is pending, and the largest sponsor is liquidating into the market. The structural negative-equity and Z-Score readings are not the risk; the uncovered payout, deteriorating estimates, and binary litigation are. We move from HOLD to AVOID, consistent with the four-report decline in the composite score and the fundamental deterioration behind it. The overall score is 4.4. Income investors attracted to the 7% yield should weight the coverage gap and the verdict risk before treating that yield as durable.
