**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sarepta Therapeutics is a commercial-stage rare-disease biopharmaceutical company whose Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) franchise generated $1.86B in net product revenue in 2025 across four approved products, yet whose equity has lost roughly 87% on an indexed total-return basis over the crisis period. The company entered 2026 fighting on multiple fronts: two patient deaths from acute liver failure tied to its gene therapy ELEVIDYS, a boxed warning, removal of the non-ambulatory indication, revocation of the AAVrh74 platform designation, the November 2025 failure of the ESSENCE confirmatory trial (which threatens nearly $1B of PMO revenue under accelerated-approval rules), clinical holds across the gene-therapy pipeline, and an EU marketing refusal. The FY2025 income statement swung from +$235M net income to a –$713M loss, and the balance sheet sits in the Altman distress zone at a Z-score of 1.17.

The bear case is coherent and largely correct on the facts. What complicates the rating is that most of it is already reflected in a stock that has collapsed to ~$16 against a $20 median and $22 mean analyst target, with a 32.6% short float and 7.4 days to cover. The headline loss is misleading: roughly $884M of the 2025 R&D expense was Arrowhead collaboration up-front and milestone charges (an offensive capital-allocation choice), plus a $165M inventory write-down. The underlying franchise remains cash-consumptive but not in operational free-fall. Q1 2026 beat consensus by 185%, management maintained 2026 net product revenue guidance of $1.2–1.4B, and on June 30, 2026 the FDA accepted for filing the sNDAs for AMONDYS 45 and VYONDYS 53, materially de-risking the single largest withdrawal tail. The stock has recovered from a ~$15.00 low in mid-June to a $16–20 range in July, coincident with that catalyst.

I am moving to HOLD (uncertain) from a prior AVOID. This is not a conviction endorsement of the equity: the balance sheet is genuinely distressed and the franchise faces structural erosion, but the combination of a fresh positive catalyst, a crowded short, improving analyst posture, and price recovery off the lows makes the risk/reward option-like rather than one-directionally negative. An AVOID here would be asserting downside that the market has substantially already priced, and the setup (heavy short base, two-sided binary catalysts, sentiment turning) is the configuration in which a depressed name gets squeezed higher. The honest characterization is a balanced binary, which HOLD describes better than AVOID.

## Company Overview

Sarepta commercializes precision genetic medicines for rare diseases, anchored in DMD. The portfolio splits into two franchises:

- **PMO exon-skipping products** — EXONDYS 51, VYONDYS 53, AMONDYS 45 — RNA-targeted antisense oligonucleotides approved under the accelerated-approval pathway on a dystrophin surrogate biomarker. 2025 PMO revenue was $965.6M, roughly flat year over year. These address distinct genetic subsets (exon 51 ~13%, exon 53 ~8%, exon 45 ~8% of DMD patients).
- **ELEVIDYS (delandistrogene moxeparvovec)** — the first approved DMD gene therapy and the intended growth engine. 2025 revenue was $898.7M (+9% YoY). Roche holds ex-US commercialization rights; Sarepta owns and manufactures the product.

The pipeline extends into siRNA and other modalities largely through a major Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals collaboration (FSHD, DM1, SCA2, Huntington's, IPF), in which Sarepta is a licensee reliant on Arrowhead for siRNA drug substance. The competitive set is intensifying: viltolarsen (Nippon), Capricor's Deramiocel, and next-generation antibody-oligonucleotide conjugates from Dyne and Avidity that promise higher dystrophin expression, alongside competing DMD gene therapies from Regenxbio and Solid/Ultragenyx.

The FY2025 reversal is stark on the surface:

| Cash + investments | $1,504M | $954M | –37% |
| Operating cash flow | –$206M | –$205M | ~flat |
| FCF | — | –$307M | — |

The loss is dominated by discretionary and non-recurring items: $883.8M of Arrowhead up-front and milestone R&D expense ($583.6M acquired IPR&D + $300M DM1 milestone), a $165.3M inventory write-down flowing through cost of sales, and a $42M restructuring charge tied to the July 2025 reduction of ~36% of the workforce. Stripping these out, the underlying franchise still produced $1.86B in product revenue and burned cash at a more moderate rate. Operating cash flow was flat year over year at –$205M, which is the more telling operational metric than the accrual-driven net loss.

The balance sheet is the central concern. The Altman Z-score of 1.17 sits in the distress zone, driven by negative EBIT/assets and thin equity. D/E is 1.94, with ~$829M of convertible debt outstanding. The most important defensive action was the August and December 2025 debt exchanges, which pushed $893.4M of maturities to 2030 (from 2027), reduced principal by ~$98M, and cost ~5.9M shares of dilution plus a higher coupon. The current ratio of 2.32 and an undrawn $600M secured revolver provide near-term cushion, but the revolver's covenants (max secured net leverage 3.5x, min interest coverage 2.5x) could bind if EBITDA remains negative. Heavy Arrowhead obligations ($250M guaranteed at $50M/yr through ~2030, plus a $200M DM1 milestone paid January 2026) and $507M of manufacturing commitments due within one year keep the liquidity picture tight. Management asserts sufficiency for "at least twelve months" but conditions it on resuming non-ambulatory ELEVIDYS and avoiding adverse ESSENCE-related action.

The earnings-surprise record is volatile and hard to lean on: Q1 2026 beat by 184.7% ($2.88 vs $1.01 est), Q4 2025 missed by 213%, Q3 2025 beat by 98.8%, Q2 2025 beat by 146.8%. This dispersion reflects the lumpiness of milestone recognition and write-downs rather than a stable earnings base.

## Growth Analysis

The forward picture is a reset, not growth. Management's 2026 net product revenue guidance represents a 25–35% decline from 2025, reflecting the loss of the non-ambulatory ELEVIDYS market. The equity has repriced to that reset — an 87% indexed-TSR decline is not consistent with a market expecting a return to growth.

Estimate revisions are broadly negative. Current-quarter consensus fell 55.9% over 90 days (to $0.49), next-quarter fell 36.0%, and next-year fell 18.5% (to $2.45). The one exception is current-year EPS, revised up 19.8% over 90 days to $4.79, but this is an artifact of milestone and one-time accounting dynamics rather than clean operating momentum, and it does not reconcile to a durable earnings run-rate. The falling forward-quarter and next-year estimates are the more reliable signal, and they point to negative earnings momentum. The strong-BUY estimate-revision signal that would favor a bullish call (>30% upward revisions combined with accelerating revenue growth) is absent here — revisions are falling and revenue is guided down.

Growth optionality rests entirely on two 2026 binary catalysts. First, Cohort 8 of ENDEAVOR (enhanced immunosuppression with sirolimus), with data targeted by year-end 2026, determines whether the lucrative non-ambulatory ELEVIDYS market reopens; resumption is not assured even on positive data. Second, the ESSENCE/PMO regulatory resolution determines whether ~$1B of PMO revenue survives; the June 30, 2026 sNDA acceptance is an encouraging procedural step but not a final approval. Beyond these, the Arrowhead siRNA programs are early-stage and years from meaningful revenue, and the LGMD gene-therapy programs are on clinical hold.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$16, the stock trades on a wide range of possible outcomes rather than a stable multiple. Trailing EPS is –$7.13, making trailing P/E meaningless. On consensus forward EPS of $4.79 (current year) and $2.45 (next year), the implied forward multiples are ~3.3x and ~6.5x — optically very cheap. The audit is correct that a value-trap thesis requires showing why those forward numbers collapse toward loss-making rather than simply asserting it, and the honest answer is that the $4.79 current-year figure is inflated by one-time milestone and accounting items and is not a clean run-rate. The $2.45 next-year figure at ~6.5x is more representative, and even that assumes the franchise stabilizes near the guidance range without a PMO withdrawal — a live risk, not a certainty.

Peer context is not decisive here. The biotech comp set is heterogeneous (peer median P/E 14.2, but with several negative-earnings names), and Sarepta's situation is idiosyncratic — a distressed franchise with binary regulatory catalysts, not a stable earnings compounder to be multiple-benchmarked.

The clearer valuation anchor is the analyst distribution: a $20 median and $22.17 mean target against a $16 price, with a $5 low and $38 high and sentiment characterized as improving. That is a positively skewed distribution with roughly 25–40% upside to consensus and meaningful tail upside if catalysts break right. The $5 low reflects a disorderly-scenario or PMO-withdrawal outcome. I treat cheapness here with appropriate skepticism — a compressed multiple on a franchise facing structural erosion and distressed liquidity is a classic value-trap flag, and cheapness has historically sorted the wrong way. But the specific mispricing case is not the multiple; it is the option value: the stock is priced near the middle of a bimodal outcome tree where the downside is largely known and the upside catalysts are dated and improving. That supports a valuation score modestly above midpoint, not a low value-trap score, because the market has already inflicted most of the punishment and a fresh positive catalyst has landed.

## Competitive Landscape

Sarepta remains the DMD incumbent but its moat is compressing on two fronts. The ELEVIDYS safety crisis has undermined the "one-time transformative therapy" value proposition and opened the door to Regenxbio and Solid/Ultragenyx in gene therapy. On the exon-skipping side, next-generation antibody-oligonucleotide conjugates from Dyne and Avidity promise materially higher dystrophin expression and are widely viewed as structural threats to the PMO franchise even if ESSENCE-related withdrawal is avoided. Reimbursement is also eroding at the margin: some payers have restricted ELEVIDYS coverage for ambulatory patients despite FDA's resume recommendation, and the product lost pediatric designation, increasing chargeback obligations. The competitive trajectory is deteriorating, which is why I score this dimension low despite the company's incumbent revenue base.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk stack is unusually dense and much of it is now surfaced rather than latent:

- **ELEVIDYS ALF deaths and the non-ambulatory suspension** — the defining safety risk. Two non-ambulatory DMD patient deaths from ALF (announced March and June 2025), plus a third ALF death in the SRP-9004 LGMD trial (July 2025). The path back hinges on Cohort 8 sirolimus data by year-end 2026, with resumption not assured.
- **ESSENCE failure and PMO withdrawal risk** — nearly $1B of revenue exposed to accelerated-approval revocation under FDORA's expanded authority. The June 30, 2026 sNDA acceptance is a meaningful mitigant but not a resolution.
- **Clinical holds** across LGMD programs (SRP-9003/9004/6005/9005) and the ELEVIDYS ENVISION study.
- **Liquidity and leverage** — distress-zone Z-score, secured revolver covenants that could bind on continued negative EBITDA, heavy Arrowhead and manufacturing commitments, and up to $12.1B of off-balance-sheet contingent milestone obligations.
- **Litigation** — a securities class action filed June 26, 2025 plus derivative suits tied to the safety disclosures.
- **Internal-control risk** explicitly flagged after the restructuring disrupted the financial-reporting team.
- **Governance instability** — persistent C-suite turnover across 2024–2026.

The reason I do not score risk lower is that these are almost entirely known, disclosed, and priced — the 87% drawdown, the $5 analyst low, and the 32.6% short float all reflect them. The remaining risk is genuinely two-sided: the two 2026 catalysts can resolve favorably (Cohort 8 positive, PMO approvals preserved) or unfavorably (withdrawal, disorderly liquidity), and the equity behaves like an option on that resolution.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 84.3% is fair against 1-year historical volatility of 84.9% (spread –0.6%), so the market is not pricing an unusual premium for imminent stress beyond baseline. The term structure is in backwardation (84.3% near-term vs 71.4% long-term), consistent with a dated binary event on the calendar. The put/call skew of 11.5% shows elevated put demand, and the 1-month tenor shows a pronounced 31.5% skew — hedging into the catalyst window. The put/call OI ratio collapsed from 0.54 to 0.10 since early May, and IV fell from 96.5% to 84.3% even as the stock dropped 23.9% over that window; falling put positioning into a price decline is not the signature of accelerating bearish conviction. The overall options picture is consistent with a two-sided catalyst rather than a one-way collapse being priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (toward $20–38)

The underlying franchise is not in operational free-fall — the –$713M loss is dominated by ~$884M of Arrowhead charges and a $165M write-down, while operating cash flow was flat and Q1 2026 beat by 185%. The June 30 sNDA acceptance signals a live path to preserve ~$1B of PMO revenue. If Cohort 8 data reads out positively by year-end 2026 and the non-ambulatory market reopens, revenue re-accelerates off the $1.2–1.4B base. On a stabilized ~$2.45 next-year EPS at even a low-teens multiple, the stock re-rates toward the $20 median / $22 mean / $38 high analyst range. A 32.6% short float with 7.4 days to cover provides a mechanical squeeze accelerant on any positive catalyst; the near-term upside from a short cover alone is material. Analyst sentiment is characterized as improving, and the stock has already begun recovering off its ~$15 low.

### Bear Case (toward $5–10)

The competitive moat is eroding structurally — next-gen conjugates threaten the PMO franchise regardless of ESSENCE resolution, and the ELEVIDYS safety crisis undermines the gene-therapy thesis. If Cohort 8 disappoints or PMO accelerated approval is ultimately revoked, revenue falls well below guidance while the distressed balance sheet (Z-score 1.17, negative FCF, secured-revolver covenants) turns binding. Forward estimates are already falling (next-year –18.5% over 90 days), and the $4.79 current-year EPS is an accounting artifact, not run-rate power. In a disorderly scenario, the equity approaches the $5 analyst low. Cheapness on a compressing franchise is a value trap, and there is no open-market insider buying to signal management conviction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (uncertain); the evidence is genuinely balanced and the equity is best understood as an option on 2026 catalysts. Reassessment points are dated.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Positive Cohort 8 / ENDEAVOR sirolimus data (targeted year-end 2026) supporting reopening of the non-ambulatory ELEVIDYS market.
- Full FDA conversion/approval of the AMONDYS 45 and VYONDYS 53 sNDAs (beyond the June 30 filing acceptance), removing the ~$1B PMO withdrawal tail.
- Evidence of balance-sheet repair — a return to positive operating cash flow or a capital raise that removes covenant/refinancing overhang without excessive dilution.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Negative Cohort 8 data or an FDA action revoking PMO accelerated approval / mandating withdrawal.
- Any signal the secured-revolver covenants become binding (leverage above 3.5x secured net, coverage below 2.5x) or a distressed capital raise.
- Guidance cut below the $1.2B floor, indicating ambulatory demand is also deteriorating.

**Structural-impairment / re-rating levels:** The business retains a real revenue base and dated catalysts, which is why this is HOLD rather than AVOID. If the stock re-rates toward the $20 median target on a favorable catalyst, the risk/reward flattens and I would trim rather than chase. On the downside, a break below ~$10 without a resolved catalyst would reflect the market pricing the disorderly/withdrawal scenario, at which point the thesis converts to AVOID pending the binary outcomes.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoint is the year-end 2026 Cohort 8 readout and the PMO sNDA review progression through 2H 2026, with quarterly earnings (next reporting cycle) as interim data points on ambulatory demand and cash burn.

## Conclusion

I am moving Sarepta to HOLD (uncertain) from a prior AVOID. The bear case is factually sound — a distressed balance sheet, a franchise under acute safety and regulatory pressure, revenue guided down 25–35%, falling forward estimates, and unstable governance with no insider conviction buying. But an AVOID rating requires that the downside not already be in the price, and here it substantially is: the stock has fallen ~87% on indexed TSR to ~$16 against a $20 median / $22 mean analyst target with improving sentiment, carries a 32.6% short float with 7.4 days to cover, just received a de-risking sNDA acceptance on June 30, and has recovered off its June lows into a $16–20 July range. The prior "grinding lower" framing does not survive the most recent tape.

The honest characterization is a balanced, option-like binary. The equity is priced near the middle of a bimodal outcome tree where the downside (PMO withdrawal, disorderly liquidity, ~$5) is largely known and the upside (Cohort 8 positive, PMO preserved, short squeeze toward $20–38) is dated and improving. That configuration — a heavily crowded short on a depressed name with a fresh positive catalyst — is where an AVOID gets run over, and it is where neither aggressive ownership nor shorting carries favorable asymmetry. HOLD describes it more accurately than AVOID: don't chase it, don't short it, and reassess at the year-end 2026 catalysts. The overall score of 4.15 reflects the genuine fundamental impairment weighed against a valuation and technical setup that has already absorbed most of the bad news.
