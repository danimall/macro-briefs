## Executive Summary

Pinnacle West Capital is a regulated electric utility serving 1.4 million customers through its Arizona Public Service (APS) subsidiary in the Phoenix metro area, one of the fastest-growing population centers in the US. The company benefits from two secular demand drivers: Arizona population growth (2.2% customer growth annually) and data center/AI infrastructure buildout projected to add 4-6% annual kWh growth through 2030. Its "growth pays for growth" subscription model for data center customers, if approved by regulators, would be structurally differentiated from peers.

The investment case faces meaningful near-term headwinds. A $579.5M rate case (13.99% increase) is before the Arizona Corporation Commission with hearings scheduled for May 2026 and the state Attorney General publicly opposing the filing. Capital expenditures of $2.6-2.7B annually produce deeply negative free cash flow (-$820M in 2025), necessitating continuous equity issuance (~3%/year dilution) and driving interest expense up 66% over three years. At ~19x forward earnings with flat estimate revisions, the stock is fairly valued but offers limited near-term asymmetry until the rate case resolves.

The stock trades at $99.49, approximately 5% below its 52-week high of $104.92, with consensus analyst targets of $87-$123 (median $106). The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (35.2% IV vs 16.1% HV) consistent with the pending rate case catalyst.

## Company Overview

Pinnacle West Capital is a holding company whose sole material operating subsidiary is Arizona Public Service Company, Arizona's largest and longest-serving electric utility. APS operates the Palo Verde Nuclear Generating Station (29.1% interest in the nation's largest nuclear plant), owns a diversified generation portfolio spanning natural gas, solar, wind, battery storage, and geothermal, and maintains a transmission/distribution network across 11 of Arizona's 15 counties.

Key strategic assets include:
- **Palo Verde Nuclear** — ~18% of owned/leased capacity, critical for carbon-free baseload and increasingly valuable as AI data centers seek 24/7 clean power
- **TransCanyon JV** (50/50 with Berkshire Hathaway Energy) — pursuing the Cross-Tie 500-kV transmission project approved December 2025
- **Markets+ participation** — joining SPP's day-ahead market (live October 2027) for cost optimization
- **Sole provider position** in a high-growth service territory with no realistic competitive alternative for large-load customers

**Cash Flow and Capital Structure:**

Operating cash flow of $1.81B (2025) grew 12.1% YoY, but capex of $2.62B produced free cash flow of -$820M. This negative FCF position will persist through at least 2028 given the $7.95B three-year capital plan. The company funds the gap through:

Shares outstanding grew from 113.4M (2023) to 119.7M (2025) — 5.5% dilution over two years. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $4.70 and FY2027 of $5.64 imply forward growth of ~20% year-over-year, but much of that reflects rate case recovery rather than organic earnings power expansion on a per-share basis.

**Dividend:** $3.60/share (3.6% yield) with a ~69% payout ratio. Covered by earnings but not by free cash flow, which is negative. The dividend is ultimately backed by the regulatory compact's assurance of earned returns on invested capital.

## Growth Analysis

PNW's growth story rests on three pillars:

**1. Service territory expansion (2.2% customer growth/year).** Phoenix remains one of the fastest-growing metros in the US. This is structural and has persisted for decades.

**2. Data center and large industrial load (4-6% incremental kWh growth/year through 2030).** APS reports that data center demand "far exceeds available generation and transmission resource capacity in the Southwest region." The company has developed a queue system and subscription model to manage this demand. Management confirmed in Q1 2026 that subscription contracts will be filed with the ACC in 2026.

**3. Rate base growth ($2.6-2.7B annual capex).** The capital plan includes gas generation ($635M in 2026), transmission ($550M), and distribution ($765M). Rate base of $12.5B grows ~8% annually with this spend profile.

The management-guided 5-7% average annual kWh sales growth is ambitious but supported by signed interconnection agreements and Arizona's semiconductor manufacturing expansion (TSMC, Intel). The risk is explicit in the 10-K: "significant uncertainties exist regarding the future energy demand associated with data centers and AI."

Estimate revisions are flat: current year EPS consensus at $4.70 (+0.1% over 30 days, +0.5% over 90 days); next year at $5.64 (-0.1% over 30 days, -0.3% over 90 days). Estimates are stable but lack upward momentum that would signal underappreciated growth.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural advantages:**
- Monopoly provider in a fast-growing territory with no bypass risk
- Palo Verde nuclear ownership provides firm, carbon-free baseload increasingly valued by data center operators seeking 24/7 clean energy
- Sole utility operator in Arizona means data centers have limited alternatives
- The subscription model, if approved, creates a framework where growth customers internalize their own infrastructure costs

**Trajectory:** The competitive position is strengthening as AI-driven power demand makes Arizona real estate (available land, tax incentives) more attractive to hyperscalers while simultaneously creating supply-demand imbalance that favors the incumbent utility. The queue-based system gives APS pricing power in negotiations with data center operators.

**Weakness:** Complete dependence on a single regulatory jurisdiction (ACC) means one adverse decision can significantly impair returns. No geographic diversification.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate Case Outcome (HIGH — near-term catalyst):** The AG's opposition creates political headwind, though the previous 2022 case was approved at $491.7M (84.8% of the request). A haircut to 70-80% of the current request ($406-464M) would still be constructive; a sub-60% outcome would signal regulatory deterioration.

**Wildfire Liability:** Active PSPS events in April 2026 (6,000 Flagstaff customers affected) confirm this is an operational reality. Arizona's 2025 legislation defining standard of care provides some legal protection that California's PG&E-era regulations did not. The probability of a California-scale liability event is lower given Arizona's climate and regulatory framework, but non-zero.

**Execution Risk on Capital Plan:** $7.95B over three years with equipment cost inflation (transformers up 60-90%), tariff uncertainty, and pipeline construction timeline (late 2029 target). Any significant delay or overrun extends the period of negative FCF and dilution.

**Data Center Demand Realization:** If AI capex cycles decelerate or data centers choose alternative locations, the forward growth projections collapse and the capital already deployed could become partially stranded.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** At $9.2B in long-term debt with ongoing issuance needs, even modest increases in borrowing costs directly compress the earned ROE spread vs. the regulated return.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($112-123):**
- Rate case approved at $450M+ (>75% of request) with a formula rate adjustment mechanism, reducing regulatory lag going forward
- Data center subscription model approved by ACC, creating a structural framework for load growth monetization
- FY2027 EPS of $5.60-5.80 commands 20-21x multiple given growth visibility = $112-122
- Wildfire risk perceived as manageable under new Arizona legislation; discount to peers narrows

**Bear Case ($85-92):**
- Rate case significantly cut (below 60% of request) or AG-driven political dynamic results in punitive ROE (below 9.0%)
- Formula rate plan permanently enjoined by courts, perpetuating regulatory lag
- Data center demand disappoints — cancellations or delays reduce projected load growth to 1-2%
- Interest rates remain elevated, compressing utility valuations sector-wide
- FY2027 EPS of $5.00-5.20 at 17x multiple = $85-88

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Rate case decision approves >75% of the $579.5M request ($435M+) AND includes the formula rate adjustment mechanism — this would derisk the regulatory thesis and support re-rating to peer-median multiple (~21x)
2. ACC formally approves the data center subscription model, providing regulatory certainty for load growth monetization
3. Stock pulls back to $90-93 (representing 16-16.5x FY2027E of $5.64), creating a more favorable risk/reward while fundamentals remain intact

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Rate case approved at <$290M (<50% of request) or approved ROE below 9.0%, signaling structural regulatory deterioration
2. Data center customer cancellations or delays reducing projected load growth by >50%
3. Wildfire liability event exceeding insurance coverage with direct balance sheet impact

**Reassessment timeline:** Rate case hearing May 2026 → decision expected 2H 2026. This is the primary reassessment event. Secondary: Q2 2026 earnings (August) for data center pipeline update and capital plan confirmation.

## Conclusion

Pinnacle West is a well-positioned utility operating in one of the strongest growth territories in the US, with differentiated exposure to AI-driven power demand through its data center subscription framework. The Palo Verde nuclear asset provides structural competitive advantage as clean baseload becomes increasingly scarce and valuable.

At $99.49 (~19.7x trailing, ~17.6x FY2027E), the stock is fairly priced for a utility facing a binary rate case outcome within months, continuous shareholder dilution (~3%/year), and rising interest expense. With consensus targets implying only ~7% median upside and no positive estimate revision momentum, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

The optimal entry point is either: (a) post-rate case if the outcome is favorable, at which point certainty warrants paying 20-21x, or (b) on a pullback to $90-93 where the growth premium comes free. At current levels, the position should be held by those with existing exposure but does not offer sufficient asymmetry for new capital deployment.
