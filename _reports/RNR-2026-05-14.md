## Executive Summary

RenaissanceRe is one of the world's leading property catastrophe reinsurers, trading at approximately 5.2x trailing earnings and ~7.6x current-year consensus EPS (~$38) — a substantial discount to intrinsic value given a business capable of generating mid-20s% ROE in favorable years. The company's three-driver model (underwriting income, fee income from $12B+ in managed third-party capital, and $1.7B in annual net investment income) provides structural resilience that the market underprices relative to the apparent earnings volatility inherent in catastrophe-exposed businesses.

The stock has pulled back from recent highs, now sitting at $290.01. This decline coincides with the onset of Atlantic hurricane season and general market uncertainty, creating a favorable risk/reward entry point. Q1 2026 operating EPS of $13.75 beat consensus by approximately 23%, continuing a strong multi-quarter beat streak.

The primary risks are reinsurance market softening as record capital enters the sector, adverse casualty reserve development from social inflation, and catastrophe seasonality. These are real but manageable given RNR's demonstrated ability to generate strong ROE even in elevated loss years (2025 included $786M in cat losses and still produced 25.9% ROE). At current valuation, the stock prices in significant pessimism about cycle dynamics while the operating reality remains exceptional.

## Company Overview

RenaissanceRe, domiciled in Bermuda with global operations across 8 countries, operates as a specialty reinsurer with $11.7B in gross premiums written. The company segments into Property (42% of GPW, combined ratio 61.4% in 2025) and Casualty & Specialty (58% of GPW, combined ratio 104.4% in 2025).

The business model rests on three pillars: (1) underwriting income from a highly selective portfolio managed via proprietary risk models (REMS©), (2) fee income ($329M in 2025) from the Capital Partners platform managing vehicles like DaVinci, Fontana, Medici, and Vermeer, and (3) net investment income ($1.7B in 2025) from a $36B portfolio. This diversification means RNR can generate positive returns in moderately adverse catastrophe years — a structural advantage over pure-play cat reinsurers.

Post-Validus acquisition (closed November 2023), RNR has cemented its position as a top-10 global reinsurer by premium volume (ranked #10 by S&P Global in its 2025 Top 40 Global Reinsurers report), with A+ ratings from A.M. Best, S&P, and Fitch (outlooks revised to positive as of October 2025), and A1 from Moody's.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** Full-year 2025 delivered $2.65B net income ($56.03 diluted EPS), representing 25.9% ROE — achieved despite absorbing $786M in net catastrophe losses (California Wildfires: $594M, Hurricane Melissa: $59M, other events: $132M). The combined ratio of 87.2% demonstrates underwriting quality even in an elevated loss year.

**Q1 2026:** Operating income of $591M ($13.75/share) beat the consensus estimate by approximately 23%. Annualized operating ROE of 22%. Management noted ongoing portfolio reshaping to reduce social inflation exposure.

**Cash Generation:** Operating cash flow of $3.7B in 2025 — roughly 1.4x net income, indicating high-quality earnings with strong premium collection dynamics. This funds both substantial buybacks and portfolio growth without requiring external capital.

**Capital Return:** $1.6B in buybacks during 2025 (12.5% share count reduction). In 2026 through April 24, approximately $457M in additional repurchases, completing the $668M authorization from November 2025. Shares outstanding declined from 52.7K (end 2023) to approximately 42.6K currently — a 19% reduction in 2.5 years. At current prices, management is repurchasing at roughly 1.17x book value.

**Balance Sheet:** $11.6B shareholders' equity. Debt-to-equity of 0.20x. The $36B investment portfolio is approximately 69% investment-grade fixed income (AA weighted average credit quality). At March 31, 2026, the portfolio had a weighted average yield of 5.1% and duration of 2.9 years. $22.3B in claims reserves represent the largest liability but are industry-standard for the premium volume.

**Book Value Compounding:**
- 2023: +59% (book value per share growth + dividends)
- 2024: +19%
- 2025: +27%
- Three-year arithmetic average: ~35% annually (note: compound CAGR over this period would be lower than the arithmetic average)

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth in the traditional sense is not the right lens for a reinsurer. The relevant metrics are premium growth, ROE sustainability, and book value per share compounding.

**Premiums:** Gross premiums written of $11.7B have plateaued following the Validus integration. Management is actively reallocating within the portfolio — reducing casualty exposure vulnerable to social inflation while growing credit/mortgage (up 36% in 2025) and selectively deploying in property catastrophe at still-favorable rates.

**January 2026 Renewals:** RNR reported maintaining portfolio and selectively deploying additional capacity into high-margin property catastrophe. However, Q1 2026 property gross premiums written declined 19.9% YoY, partly reflecting the absence of prior-year wildfire reinstatement premiums. The company acknowledged increased competition and supply pressure but emphasized rate adequacy relative to the past 15 years.

**Fee Income Growth:** The Capital Partners platform continues expanding — the Medici UCITS launch (March 2025) brought $342M initial capital and opens the European institutional market. Fee income of $329M provides a growing, capital-light revenue stream.

**Headwinds:** Current-year EPS estimates have risen 8-10% over 90 days but next-year estimates have declined 6.2%, reflecting analyst concern about rate softening in 2027. This is the market's primary growth concern — that the hard-market tailwind is fading.

**Structural Growth Drivers:** Rising global insured losses from climate change (long-term demand driver), increasing insurance penetration in emerging markets, and secular growth in reinsurance complexity (cyber, climate adaptation) provide multi-year tailwinds that partially offset cyclical rate softening.

## Valuation Assessment

RNR trades at:
- **~5.2x trailing EPS** ($56.03)
- **~7.6x current-year consensus** (~$38 per publicly available estimates)
- **1.17x book value** ($247/share)
- **0.55% dividend yield** (minimal; capital returned via buybacks)

**Peer comparison:** RNR generates among the highest ROEs in the reinsurance peer group (25.9% in 2025, though this varies significantly year to year — 19.3% in 2024, 40.5% in 2023, -22.0% in 2022). Even adjusting for volatility, the operating ROE of 22% in Q1 2026 supports a valuation at or above peer multiples rather than a discount.

**Why is RNR cheap?** Three factors: (1) earnings volatility inherent in catastrophe reinsurance creates a persistent valuation discount, (2) Bermuda's new 15% CIT reduces after-tax earnings by ~$300-400M annually on a normalized basis, and (3) market concern about reinsurance cycle softening. However, the three-driver model structurally reduces earnings volatility compared to pure cat reinsurers, the tax is already embedded in trailing results, and even in a softening environment, the company's demonstrated ability to generate high-teens ROE at the trough of prior cycles (2024's 19.3%) suggests the current multiple is too low.

**Buyback Support:** At current prices, RNR is repurchasing shares at ~1.17x book value on a business generating 22%+ operating ROE — economically equivalent to a high single-digit earnings yield reinvested at strong returns. This creates a floor under valuation and compounds per-share value regardless of market sentiment.

**Intrinsic Value Estimate:** At a modest 1.5x book value (appropriate for a business capable of generating 20%+ ROE in specialty reinsurance), fair value is approximately $370/share. At current consensus of ~$38 in current-year EPS and a 9x multiple, target is approximately $340. These estimates bracket the analyst consensus mean target.

## Competitive Landscape

**Risk Modeling Edge:** The proprietary REMS© platform and dedicated Risk Sciences team tracking climate change impacts provide an informational advantage in pricing catastrophe risk. This advantage compounds over time as climate models become more critical for accurate pricing.

**Capital Partners Platform:** Managing $12B+ in third-party capital through joint ventures generates fee income while allowing RNR to write larger lines without proportional balance sheet commitment. Few competitors can match this dual-mandate structure (Arch Capital's Watford was dissolved; Munich Re's State National is much smaller).

**Post-Validus Scale:** The acquisition gave RNR direct access to Lloyd's of London, expanded the Casualty & Specialty book, and increased the Capital Partners platform. Integration is complete, and the company now has the scale to be a leading provider across multiple reinsurance lines.

**Threats:** Record reinsurance capital entering the market creates rate pressure. Munich Re and Swiss Re retain scale advantages. ILS funds (catastrophe bonds) compete directly for property cat capacity. However, RNR's integrated model (managing ILS capital rather than competing against it) turns this structural trend into a partial advantage.

**Broker Concentration:** 81.3% of GPW flows through three brokers (Aon 34.5%, Marsh 33.8%, Gallagher 13.0%). This is a structural vulnerability but also reflects the depth of distribution relationships built over three decades.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Hurricane Season / Catastrophe Risk (Near-term, HIGH):** June-November Atlantic hurricane season is the primary source of earnings volatility. A major hurricane making U.S. landfall could produce losses of $500M-$2B depending on location and severity. The company demonstrated in 2025 that it can absorb $786M in cat losses and still generate 25.9% ROE, indicating substantial loss absorption capacity.

**2. Casualty Reserve Deterioration (Medium-term, MODERATE):** Accident years 2018-2019 have developed adversely (18% and 16% increases respectively). With $16.6B in casualty reserves and $12.9B in IBNR, sensitivity analysis shows a 10% loss ratio increase could add $2.7B in reserves (23% of equity). Management is proactively reducing general liability and professional liability exposure, but the tail risk is real.

**3. Market Softening (Medium-term, MODERATE):** Record capital inflows are pressuring rates. Gallagher Re's global property-cat rate-on-line index fell 15%, Howden Re's fell 14.7%, and Guy Carpenter's fell 12% at January 1, 2026 renewals. If property catastrophe rates continue declining at the 2027 renewals, RNR's property segment margins compress — though from a 61.4% combined ratio, substantial compression is tolerable before reaching breakeven.

**4. Bermuda Tax (Structural, PRICED IN):** The 15% CIT is a permanent $300-400M annual headwind already reflected in 2025 and 2026 results. No incremental surprise risk.

**5. Investment Portfolio Duration Risk:** A 100bp increase in interest rates would produce mark-to-market losses on the $24.9B fixed income portfolio (duration ~2.9 years ≈ ~$720M unrealized loss). However, held-to-maturity income of $1.7B annually provides substantial offset.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term event risk, consistent with hurricane season onset and post-earnings positioning. Near-term implied volatility is elevated relative to realized volatility, suggesting seasonal hedging demand rather than fundamental deterioration. Long-term call-heavy open interest positioning remains constructive.

**Interpretation:** The options market is pricing seasonal risk consistent with owning a catastrophe reinsurer heading into hurricane season. This creates a cost for short-dated options strategies but does not signal informed selling or structural concern.

## Conclusion

RenaissanceRe at $290 represents a high-quality compounder available at a discount to intrinsic value. The ~5.2x trailing P/E and 1.17x book value multiple inadequately price a business that generated 25.9% ROE in 2025 and 22% operating ROE in Q1 2026, supported by structural resilience from its three-driver model. ROE has varied significantly year to year (ranging from -22% in 2022 to 40.5% in 2023), but management's track record of compounding book value per share plus dividends at attractive rates through cycles supports a higher valuation multiple.

The current entry point benefits from seasonal hurricane anxiety and broader market softness. The risks (cat season, casualty reserves, rate cycle) are known, manageable, and already partially reflected in the discount multiple. With management buying back 8-10% of shares annually at prices they consider below intrinsic value, time compounds in favor of the long investor. Maintain BUY with standard 2-3% position sizing.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Forward P/E multiple is closer to 7.6x (using publicly available consensus EPS of ~$38) rather than 7.1x, and ROE is not consistently 22-26% on a GAAP basis; these corrections modestly reduce the degree of apparent valuation cheapness supporting the recommendation.
