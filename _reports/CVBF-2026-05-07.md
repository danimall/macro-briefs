## Executive Summary

CVB Financial Corp is a California-based bank holding company that operates Citizens Business Bank with ~$22 billion in combined assets following its April 2026 completion of the Heritage Commerce Corp acquisition. The company's defining competitive asset is its deposit franchise — 56-59% noninterest-bearing deposits with a total cost of funds below 1% — which generates structural earnings power across rate cycles and is estimated to rank among the best funding profiles in U.S. community banking.

The Heritage merger, completed April 17, 2026, expanded CVBF into the Bay Area/Silicon Valley market at an attractive valuation (acquired after the 2023 banking crisis depressed regional bank multiples). Q1 2026 standalone results showed NIM expansion to 3.44%, loan originations 90% above prior year, and net recoveries on credit losses despite a $3.0M provision tied to specific C&I credits. With the combined entity now generating over $200M annually in organic capital, management has significant excess capital that could support buyback resumption once Heritage integration stabilizes.

The stock trades at 13.5x trailing earnings and approximately 11.0x estimated FY2027 earnings, with a 3.9% dividend yield. At a conservative 12.5x FY2027 EPS, fair value is approximately $23.50, implying 15% price upside plus dividend yield.

## Company Overview

CVB Financial Corp is the parent company of Citizens Business Bank (CBB), a national banking association serving small-to-medium-sized businesses across California. Prior to the Heritage merger, the bank operated 62 banking centers with $15.6 billion in total assets, focused primarily on Southern and Central California markets (LA County, Inland Empire, Orange County, Central Valley).

The April 2026 Heritage Commerce acquisition added approximately $7B in assets. According to deal materials, the transaction is expected to be approximately 13.2% accretive to EPS by 2027, with cost savings approximately 75% phased in during the second half of 2026 and 100% thereafter, offset near-term by approximately $75 million in one-time pre-tax merger expenses. The combined institution has over $22 billion in assets, more than 75 banking centers plus trust offices, and California's most economically diverse geographic footprint among community banks. CBB also operates CitizensTrust, a wealth management division with approximately $5.1 billion in AUM/AUA.

The bank converted from a California state charter to a national banking association (OCC-regulated) in December 2025, simplifying the Heritage integration regulatory framework.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** CVBF has posted 196 consecutive quarters of profitability (49 years without a loss). FY2025 standalone net income was $209.3M ($1.52 diluted EPS), with ROAA of 1.36% and ROATCE of 14.28%. The efficiency ratio of 46.0% is best-in-class among comparable regional banks.

**Net Interest Margin:** NIM expanded from a 2024 trough of 3.09% to 3.36% for FY2025 and further to 3.44% in Q1 2026. This recovery was driven by successful deleveraging of $1B+ in high-cost FHLB borrowings and a decline in total cost of funds to 0.97% (Q1 2026). The funding cost advantage is structural: at 56-59% noninterest-bearing deposits and 0.78% cost of deposits in Q1 2026, CVBF carries an estimated 50-100bps NIM advantage over most regional bank peers regardless of the rate environment.

**Credit Quality:** Nonperforming assets fell from $47.1M (0.31% of assets) at YE2024 to just $4.8M (0.03%) at YE2025. The bank recorded provision recaptures of $3.5M in 2025 and $3.0M in 2024. However, Q1 2026 saw a reversal of this trend, with a $3.0M credit-loss provision recorded — largely tied to a specific C&I credit relationship — even as net recoveries continued. The ACL rose to 0.93% of loans at March 31, 2026, providing substantial cushion against management's own pessimistic macro assumptions (unemployment >5%, CRE value declines through 2026).

**Capital Position:** CET1 stood at 16.3% as of March 31, 2026, on a standalone basis prior to the inclusion of Heritage's financial results (which will be reflected beginning in Q2 2026). Pro forma post-merger capital ratios have not yet been filed; they will be included in a subsequent amendment. The standalone capital position far exceeds well-capitalized minimums and strong organic capital generation ($200M+ annually) supports potential buyback resumption once Heritage integration stabilizes.

**Loan Growth Inflection:** Q1 2026 originations were approximately 90% above Q1 2025 levels — a material acceleration from the modest loan declines experienced since the 2022 peak of $9.08B. Management upgraded growth guidance language from "low single-digit" to potential "mid-single-digit" growth, citing investor CRE activity returning across asset classes.

**Balance Sheet Risk:** Unrealized securities losses total approximately $652M across AFS ($299-310M) and HTM (~$345M) portfolios. However, with $5.58B in available borrowing capacity and no intention to sell, this is a mark-to-market concern rather than a capital impairment issue. The 62% fixed-rate loan book limits rate sensitivity.

## Growth Analysis

Growth for CVBF comes from three identifiable sources over the next 12-18 months:

**1. Heritage Accretion (Most Material):** The Heritage Commerce merger adds approximately $7B in assets, with expected EPS accretion of 13.2% by 2027 through cost synergies (branch consolidation, back-office rationalization, Heritage mortgage pool sales) and balance sheet optimization. Near-term GAAP earnings will contain approximately $75 million in one-time pre-tax merger charges and integration noise, with cost savings phased at 75% in H2 2026 and 100% thereafter. Heritage's deposit pricing was higher than CBB's — management has explicitly stated there is "work to do" on pricing Heritage's funding down toward CBB levels. If Heritage deposit costs are reduced by even 30-50bps, the impact on the combined NIM is 5-8bps, worth approximately $0.05-0.08/share annually.

**2. Organic Loan Growth Acceleration:** The 90% YoY increase in Q1 originations marks a genuine inflection from the flat/declining loan trajectory of 2023-2024. New origination yields of approximately 6% on an expanding loan book drive incremental net interest income without requiring additional capital allocation. Mid-single-digit loan growth on a ~$12B combined loan book translates to $600-700M in new balances annually.

**3. Capital Return:** With CET1 well above 15% on a standalone basis and organic earnings covering the dividend easily, management has significant buyback capacity. CVBF repurchased 4.32 million shares at $18.60/share ($80.4M) in 2025, though no shares were repurchased in Q1 2026. The timing of buyback resumption will depend on integration progress and regulatory considerations. At current prices (~1.8x standalone TBV), buybacks would be accretive to EPS given the company's 14%+ ROATCE, though they would be modestly dilutive to tangible book value per share given the premium to TBV.

Estimated FY2027 EPS of approximately $1.88 (based on consensus estimates) appears achievable given the NIM expansion path and cost synergy realization timeline, representing approximately 13% growth over FY2026 estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

CVBF trades at 13.5x trailing EPS ($1.52) and approximately 10.9x estimated FY2027 earnings (~$1.88). The premium on trailing earnings is modest and justified by:

- Deposit franchise quality: 56-59% NIB deposits vs. typical peer at 25-35%
- Efficiency ratio: 46% vs. peer average 55-60%
- Credit quality: Near-zero losses and provision recaptures through FY2025
- Growth trajectory: Heritage accretion + loan origination acceleration

The stock yields 3.9% ($0.80/year). At approximately 1.8x standalone pre-merger tangible book ($11.42 at March 31, 2026), it trades at a discount to its historical average given the quality of the deposit franchise — banks with this funding profile historically trade at 2.0-2.5x TBV in normal rate environments. The pro forma post-merger TBV/share will likely be lower than $11.42 given the expected 7.7% TBV dilution from the Heritage acquisition (with an estimated earnback period of approximately 2.5 years).

At a conservative 12.5x FY2027 EPS of $1.88, fair value is approximately $23.50, implying 15% price upside plus dividend yield.

The expectation gap test: at the current 13.5x multiple, the market is pricing roughly $1.67-1.70 in sustainable earnings power. The Heritage merger creates a specific, identified catalyst (cost synergies + deposit repricing + loan growth capacity expansion) to drive earnings toward $1.88+ by FY2027. The market appears to be discounting integration risk more heavily than the probability-weighted upside warrants.

## Competitive Landscape

CVBF's competitive moat is its deposit franchise. With 56-59% noninterest-bearing deposits, 78% business customer base, and 47% of depositors having banked with CBB for over 10 years, the stickiness of the funding base is exceptional. This translates to a structural cost-of-funds advantage estimated at 50-100bps vs. typical regional bank peers, an advantage that compounds every quarter through higher NIM on comparable asset yields.

The Heritage acquisition strategically addresses CVBF's primary competitive limitation (Southern California concentration) by adding Bay Area/Silicon Valley market presence. Heritage clients, previously capped by Heritage's smaller lending limits, now have access to CBB's significantly larger balance sheet, creating cross-sell and relationship deepening opportunities.

Competitive threats include intensifying pressure from Pacific Premier, Columbia, Fifth Third, BMO, and particularly Wells Fargo (now more active following the June 2025 asset-cap removal by the Federal Reserve). However, CVBF competes on service quality and relationship depth rather than price, and new origination pipelines confirm this approach remains effective.

The 46% efficiency ratio provides significant operating leverage — the cost infrastructure is already built for a much larger asset base, meaning Heritage integration captures meaningful scale economies with limited incremental fixed costs.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration Execution (Primary Risk):** Heritage is CVBF's largest-ever acquisition. Q2 and Q3 2026 financials will contain "noise" from restructuring charges, systems conversion costs, and balance sheet repositioning. Deposit attrition in Heritage's Bay Area markets is a realistic risk, though mitigated by CBB's expanded lending capacity and product set available to Heritage's commercial clients.

**CRE Concentration:** 76% CRE concentration (237% of risk-based capital) is the highest risk concentration on the balance sheet. Office properties at $989.8M (15% of CRE) carry average 55% LTV at origination — conservative underwriting, but office fundamentals remain pressured nationally. Management's own ACL model assumes CRE value declines through 2026.

**Unrealized Securities Losses:** $652M combined paper losses on the securities portfolio. While manageable given liquidity and no forced-sale scenario, a deposit run or severe stress could crystallize losses. Uninsured and uncollateralized deposits were approximately $5.3 billion (42% of total deposits and customer repurchase agreements) at December 31, 2025, which is above average for community banks, though relationship tenure mitigates run risk.

**Macroeconomic Sensitivity:** In a severe recession scenario (unemployment >6%, GDP contraction), the CRE-concentrated book would face meaningful credit migration. The current near-zero loss rate is cyclically exceptional and will normalize. The ACL at 0.93% of loans provides a buffer, but a prolonged downturn would require significant additional provisioning.

**Classified Loan Uptick:** Q1 2026 saw classified loans rise from $52.7M to $83.1M — driven by downgrades of four C&I loans totaling $18.1M, three CRE loans totaling $12.9M, and two dairy/livestock loans totaling $4.4M, partially offset by paydowns/payoffs. Management expects resolution on the larger relationships, but this warrants monitoring as a potential early signal of broader credit migration.

**Credit Provisioning Normalization:** After two years of provision recaptures, Q1 2026 included a $3.0M credit-loss provision. While modest relative to the loan book, this marks a shift from the net-release trend and may continue as the Heritage portfolio is integrated and assessed under CVBF's ACL methodology.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($25-27, +21-31%):** Heritage integration proceeds smoothly with deposit costs repriced toward CBB levels by Q4 2026. Combined entity achieves $1.90+ EPS in FY2027. Buyback resumption removes 3-5% of float. CRE markets stabilize as interest rate moderation supports commercial property values. Market re-rates the stock to 2.0x+ TBV on combined franchise quality. Short covering provides additional upward pressure.

**Bear Case ($17-18, -13-17%):** Heritage integration proves more complex than expected — meaningful deposit attrition in Bay Area markets as clients test relationships with new management. CRE credit migration accelerates beyond the classified relationships already identified. NIM compresses if rate cuts accelerate faster than asset yields reprice. Merger charges depress near-term GAAP earnings. Market imposes integration discount for 4-6 quarters, and short sellers prove correct that the deal was dilutive to near-term earnings quality.

**Base Case ($23-24, +12-17%):** Integration progresses with typical community bank deal friction (2-3 quarters of elevated expenses, modest deposit attrition of 5-8%), but cost synergies are realized by Q1 2027. EPS trajectory reaches $1.80-1.88 in FY2027. Stock re-rates modestly toward the $23-24 range as integration uncertainty clears.

## Conclusion

CVB Financial offers an identifiable path to 12-17% price appreciation plus 3.9% dividend income over 12 months, driven by Heritage Commerce merger accretion, loan growth acceleration, and eventual buyback resumption. The company's exceptional deposit franchise (sub-1% cost of funds, 56-59% noninterest-bearing) provides durable earnings power that the market underappreciates at 13.5x trailing P/E given the quality differential vs. peers.

The specific catalyst for re-rating is Heritage integration proving successful through Q2-Q3 2026 earnings reports. The risk is that integration proves more complex or costly than anticipated, but even in that scenario, the underlying franchise quality and capital levels provide a meaningful floor on downside.

Recommended position sizing: 2% portfolio weight, appropriate for a community bank with identifiable catalysts but meaningful single-event risk (integration execution).
