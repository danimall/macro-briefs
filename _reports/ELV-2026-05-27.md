## Executive Summary

Elevance Health is the second-largest U.S. health insurer by membership (45.2 million medical members), operating Blue Cross Blue Shield plans in 14 states plus a growing healthcare services platform through its Carelon subsidiaries. The stock trades at $392, approximately 4% below its 52-week high of $408, having recovered substantially from a trough near $274 in August 2025. This recovery was driven by Q1 2026 results showing medical cost stabilization and a raised full-year guidance to ≥$26.75 adjusted EPS.

The investment case centers on whether the 2024-2025 medical cost cycle — which compressed the benefit expense ratio from 87.0% to 90.0% — has definitively troughed. Q1 2026 provides one data point of confirmation, but one quarter does not constitute a trend. At 14.6x forward adjusted EPS, ELV trades at a significant discount to peers (median 35.8x), reflecting lingering skepticism about margin recovery. This discount is justified by the binary nature of the risk: if medical costs re-accelerate, or if OBBBA/PBM reform structurally impairs earnings power, the stock returns to the $280-320 range it occupied for most of early 2026.

The thesis is a growth HOLD pending confirmation of margin recovery sustainability over Q2-Q3 2026. The stock deserves ownership for its valuation discount, Carelon platform optionality, and the informed director purchase at $290, but the evidence for a decisive turn remains incomplete.

## Company Overview

Elevance Health generates $198B in annual revenue across four segments: Health Benefits ($167B, traditional insurance), CarelonRx ($43B, pharmacy services), Carelon Services ($28B, behavioral health/post-acute/analytics), and Corporate ($0.5B). The company holds exclusive BCBS licenses in 14 states, providing durable competitive positioning in those geographies.

Membership is diversified across employer groups (45.5% fee-based), Medicaid (18.8%), Medicare (6.9%), Individual/ACA (2.9%), and BlueCard (14.4%). The Carelon platform represents management's strategic bet on vertical integration — owning pharmacy fulfillment, behavioral health delivery, and value-based care analytics rather than simply processing claims.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2025 revenue reached $197.6B (+12.8% YoY), with growth driven by CarelonRx (+20.7%) and Carelon Services (+57.7%, boosted by the CareBridge acquisition). Health Benefits grew 11.2% on premium rate increases and Medicare Advantage expansion.

**Profitability deterioration and early recovery:** The critical metric is the benefit expense ratio, which rose from 87.0% (FY2023) to 88.5% (FY2024) to 90.0% (FY2025). This 300bp deterioration eliminated all revenue-driven earnings growth. FY2025 net income was $5.66B (-5.3% YoY) despite 12.8% revenue growth. Health Benefits segment operating margin compressed to 2.5% from 4.2%. Q1 2026's EPS of $12.58 (vs. $10.93 estimate, +15.1% beat) suggests this trend is reversing, but the magnitude of the beat partly reflects conservative initial guidance.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $4.29B represents 0.76x net income — below the typical 1.0x+ conversion for a health insurer. The shortfall was driven by the $666M BCBSA provider settlement payment and unfavorable working capital timing. FCF of $3.17B funded $2.6B in buybacks and $1.5B in dividends, totaling ~$4.1B in capital return — consuming more than available free cash.

**Balance sheet:** Debt-to-capital of 42.1% with $30.8B long-term debt is appropriate for a large managed care company. Goodwill/intangibles of $39.5B (90% of equity) is the primary balance sheet risk — a function of aggressive M&A (Centers Plan, CareBridge, historical acquisitions). Cash of $9.5B and an undrawn $5.0B facility provide adequate liquidity. Credit ratings (S&P A-, Moody's Baa2) reflect investment-grade standing.

**Capital allocation:** Cumulative share repurchases exceed $53.8B historically, with $6.7B remaining authorization. The dividend yields 1.7%. Total capital return of ~$4.1B in FY2025 exceeded free cash flow — sustainable only if cash flow normalizes in 2026.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth drivers:**
- Medicare Advantage membership grew 7.9% in FY2025; Star Ratings improvement (59% in 4.0+ plans for 2027 payment vs. 40% prior year) should enhance 2027 bonus revenue
- CarelonRx prescription volume growing mid-single-digits with specialty/infusion pharmacy as higher-margin contributor
- Carelon Services integration of CareBridge provides exposure to home-based care for complex populations
- Premium rate increases across all lines catching up to elevated medical cost trends

**Growth constraints:**
- Current year EPS estimates at $26.91 (+6.7% vs. FY2025) represent modest recovery, not acceleration
- Next year estimates at $29.25 (+8.7%) are declining at the margin (90-day revision -0.9%)
- Current quarter estimates revised down 7.2% over 90 days, suggesting near-term cost pressure persists
- Medicaid membership still declining (8.5M vs. 10.5M peak), with OBBBA introducing new redetermination requirements in 2027-2028
- PBM reform legislation (rebate pass-through, Part D delinking) could structurally impair CarelonRx margins by 2028

The growth profile is recovery from a cyclical trough rather than secular acceleration. Revenue growth of 12.8% in FY2025 translated to -5.3% earnings decline — the lever that matters (medical cost ratio) is not yet confirmed as sustainably improved.

## Valuation Assessment

At $392, ELV trades at:
- 14.6x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($26.91 consensus)
- 13.4x FY2027 adjusted EPS ($29.25 consensus)
- 16.5x trailing GAAP EPS ($25.21, including tax benefits)
- ~11x forward operating earnings (Health Benefits + Carelon combined)

Relative to peers: UNH at 29.3x, HUM at 32.9x, MOH at 51.2x, HQY at 35.8x. ELV's discount is approximately 60% below the peer median P/E of 35.8x. This is the widest discount ELV has traded at relative to peers in recent years.

The discount reflects: (1) margin uncertainty — investors are not convinced the 90.0% benefit expense ratio is the peak; (2) flat earnings trajectory — net income has been $5.7-6.0B for four consecutive years while revenue grew 26%; (3) TSR underperformance — ELV's 5-year indexed TSR of 117 vs. peer group's 148 reflects sustained multiple compression.

The valuation is attractive if the margin recovery is real. With a realistic path to $29-30 in FY2027, the stock offers 20-25% upside if re-rated even modestly toward 17-18x. The analyst consensus target of $404 (median $399) implies only 3% upside — reflecting caution about medical cost sustainability. The high target of $498 implies 27% upside and assumes full margin normalization.

## Competitive Landscape

ELV's competitive position is stable with structural advantages in its 14 BCBS-exclusive states. The BCBS brand carries recognition and provider network breadth that competing plans cannot replicate in those geographies. The vertical integration strategy through Carelon differentiates ELV from pure-play insurers and creates internal margin capture.

However, relative competitive position is deteriorating compared to UnitedHealth Group, which has executed its Optum services strategy more effectively and maintained higher margins through the 2024-2025 cost cycle. UNH's market cap is 4x ELV's ($342B vs. $85B), reflecting a compounding divergence in execution quality.

The Carelon platform is strategically sound but faces execution risk: CarelonRx depends on CVS through 2027 for core pharmacy services (a competitor-as-vendor arrangement), and Carelon Services' growth is acquisition-driven rather than organic. The $39.5B in goodwill represents the cost of building this platform — management must demonstrate returns exceed cost of capital.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Medical cost re-acceleration (probability: 30-35%).** One quarter of improvement does not confirm a trend. The benefit expense ratio could worsen again in Q2-Q3 if utilization trends — particularly in ACA/Individual populations — remain elevated. Management's claim of "greater clarity" may prove premature.

2. **OBBBA Medicaid disruption (2027-2028).** New legislation requires more frequent eligibility redeterminations, work requirements, and cost-sharing for expansion populations. This could trigger another membership loss cycle similar to 2023-2025, with associated margin pressure during transition.

3. **PBM reform structural margin impairment.** The Consolidated Appropriations Act (February 2026) requires full rebate pass-through and delinks Part D compensation from drug prices (2028). CarelonRx's $43B revenue segment generates margins that depend on spread pricing and retained rebates — the long-term revenue model is under legislative pressure.

4. **Claims reserve conservatism declining.** Prior year redundancies dropped from 12.3% to 9.0% of prior year reserves, reducing the cushion for adverse development. If actual claims exceed current-year reserves, the earnings hit arrives without the prior-year release offset.

5. **Executive turnover.** Multiple C-suite changes in 2025-2026 (new CLO, CHRO, Chief Digital Officer) create execution risk during a critical operating transition period.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 32.6% is trading 6.6 percentage points below 1-year historical volatility (39.2%), placing IV at the 6th percentile within the current chain. This is unusually cheap implied volatility following a period of high realized vol. The put/call skew is balanced (1.4%), the OI ratio is 0.49 (call-weighted), and the term structure is flat. The options market is pricing ELV as a low-uncertainty situation, which contrasts with the fundamental uncertainty around medical cost sustainability. This could represent favorable entry timing if the thesis proves correct, or complacency if Q2 costs disappoint.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($440-498, 12-27% upside):** Medical cost moderation confirmed over Q2-Q3 2026, benefit expense ratio returns to 88-89% range by FY2027, CarelonRx/Services growth sustains 15-20% CAGR, Star Ratings improvement drives Medicare Advantage margin expansion. EPS reaches $30-32 by FY2027 and stock re-rates to 15-16x → $450-500. Director purchase at $290 proves prescient.

**Bear Case ($280-320, 18-28% downside):** Q1 2026 proves anomalous; medical costs re-accelerate in Q2-Q3 on utilization rebound. OBBBA implementation creates new Medicaid uncertainty ahead of 2027. PBM reform forces CarelonRx margin disclosure that disappoints. Stock returns to the $280-320 range it occupied for most of early 2026.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 benefit expense ratio ≤89.5% — confirming sustainable improvement, not a one-quarter anomaly
2. Full-year 2026 EPS guidance raised above $28.00 (indicating margin recovery exceeding current trajectory)
3. CarelonRx margin disclosure that demonstrates resilience to PBM reform provisions

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 benefit expense ratio ≥90.5% — indicating Q1 was a false positive
2. Full-year EPS guidance reduced below $25.00
3. Second consecutive quarter of operating cash flow below 0.8x net income (indicating structural cash flow impairment)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely mid-July 2026). Q2 2026 results will be the decisive data point on whether the medical cost recovery is real.

## Conclusion

ELV is a high-quality franchise trading at a substantial discount to peers because the market demands proof that the 2024-2025 margin cycle has troughed. Q1 2026 provides the first evidence of recovery, supported by raised guidance and a BofA upgrade. The informed director purchase at $290 adds confidence.

The specific risk preventing a BUY recommendation: **Q1 2026 is a single data point on margin recovery, and the quarterly estimate revision trend for Q2 2026 is negative (-7.2% over 90 days), directly contradicting the notion of a confirmed recovery.** When the current quarter consensus is declining at the same time management claims "greater clarity on medical costs," one side is wrong. If management is right, the stock reaches $440+. If the consensus revision trend is right, Q2 disappoints and the stock retests $340-350. This asymmetry — declining near-term estimates vs. optimistic annual guidance — is a falsifiable divergence that resolves with Q2 results in approximately 7 weeks. Owning the stock through that resolution requires conviction in the recovery thesis that one quarter of data does not yet support.

HOLD with growth rationale, pending Q2 confirmation.
