## Executive Summary

Black Hills Corporation is a 142-year-old regulated electric and natural gas utility serving approximately 227,000 electric and 1,138,000 gas customers across eight states in the Rocky Mountain and Midwest regions. The company faces two intersecting catalysts: (1) a pending all-stock merger of equals with NorthWestern Energy Group (NWE), approved by both shareholder bases on April 2, 2026 and awaiting regulatory approvals for an expected H2 2026 close; and (2) rapidly accelerating data center demand in Wyoming, where Microsoft is tripling its Cheyenne footprint and industrial electric volumes grew 20.6% in fiscal 2025.

The stock has rallied approximately 34% from its June 2025 lows near $55.50 to a current price of $74.17, with a sharp spike in mid-April 2026 coinciding with Microsoft's 3,200-acre land purchase announcement. At 18.6x trailing earnings ($3.98 EPS) and a forward consensus of $4.35 for FY2026, BKH trades at a discount to the multi-utility peer median P/E of 21x. The discount partially reflects merger execution uncertainty (FERC, Montana PSC, Nebraska PSC, and South Dakota PUC approvals are still outstanding) and a concerning recent earnings track record of 3 misses in 4 quarters. The 3.8% dividend yield and 55-year consecutive increase streak provide income support, but the payout ratio at 68% exceeds management's 55-65% target, and dividends are capped at 4% annual increases during the merger pendency period. We rate BKH HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis, awaiting greater clarity on merger regulatory outcomes before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Black Hills operates two segments: Electric Utilities (serving Colorado, Montana, South Dakota, and Wyoming with 1,386 MW of generation capacity) and Gas Utilities (serving Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, and Wyoming through 44,840 miles of distribution mains). Revenue in 2025 was $2.31B, though the more relevant metric for a regulated utility is utility margin, which grew to $683.2M (electric) and $810.5M (gas) — a combined 8.8% increase over 2023.

The company owns the Wyodak coal mine in Wyoming's Powder River Basin with 172 million tons of recoverable reserves, providing coal at $1.26/MMBtu — a structural cost advantage for its Wyoming generation fleet. BKH is gradually transitioning away from coal (25.5% of generation in 2025, down from 35% in 2023) toward natural gas and renewables, including a CPUC-approved 250 MW clean energy plan in Colorado.

The pending merger with NorthWestern Energy would create a combined entity with ~700,000 electric and ~1.5 million gas customers, ~$15.4B in enterprise value, headquartered in Rapid City, SD. BKH shareholders would own approximately 56% of the combined company.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** Revenue declined from $2.55B (2022) to $2.13B (2024) as falling natural gas commodity prices passed through to customers, then recovered to $2.31B in 2025. The underlying business performed well: Gas Utility margin expanded from $701M to $810.5M (2023-2025), a 7.5% two-year CAGR, driven by $60.9M in new rate and rider recovery from successful rate cases in Arkansas, Iowa, Kansas, and Nebraska.

**Earnings:** EPS from ongoing operations (management's key metric) grew from $3.93 (2023) to $3.91 (2024) to $4.10 (2025). GAAP diluted EPS was $3.98 in 2025. Management has guided for approximately 6% EPS growth in 2026, implying approximately $4.35 — consistent with current consensus.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $4.70B at 55.1% of total capitalization. Debt-to-equity of 2.84x is elevated but standard for a regulated utility with investment-grade ratings (BBB+/Baa2, both stable). Liquidity is adequate at $929.6M. The Altman Z-Score of 1.11 is structurally low due to the asset-heavy, low-turnover nature of the utility model rather than indicating genuine distress.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $673.4M in 2025, but capex of $819.8M produced negative free cash flow of -$146.4M. This is structural: the five-year capital plan totals $4.7B ($934M/year average), funded through a mix of operating cash flow, ATM equity issuance ($219M in 2025), and long-term debt. A $400M note maturing January 2027 needs refinancing.

**Interest Expense:** Net interest expense grew from $167.9M (2023) to $181.7M (2024) to $200.1M (2025), compressing the net earnings benefit of rate increases. Long-term debt increased $900M over the same period.

**Dividend:** 55 consecutive annual increases. Current annualized rate of $2.812/share (3.8% yield). Payout ratio of 68% exceeds management's stated 55-65% target range. Dividend growth is capped at 4% during the merger pendency period.

## Growth Analysis

BKH has three distinct growth vectors:

**1. Data Center Demand in Wyoming (High Impact, Multi-Year):** Wyoming Electric's peak demand surged 21% YoY to 379 MW in 2025, driven by Microsoft and Meta data center load under the Large Power Contract Service (LPCS) tariff. Microsoft's April 2026 announcement to purchase 3,200 additional acres in Cheyenne — tripling its physical footprint — signals a multi-year, sequenced buildout that could drive substantial rate base growth. The LPCS tariff structure requires data centers to fund their own infrastructure and power procurement costs, theoretically insulating residential ratepayers but also limiting margin expansion per unit of load growth. The combined BKH/NWE entity would be better positioned to serve large-load customers across a broader service territory.

**2. NorthWestern Energy Merger (High-Impact but Binary):** The merger creates geographic and commodity diversification, scale advantages in capital markets access, and potential operating synergies. NWE brings $452M in NOLs. However, integration risk is real: approximately 60.2 million new shares will be issued, roughly doubling the share count. The combined entity needs to deliver material synergies to avoid EPS dilution.

**3. Organic Rate Base Growth:** The $4.7B five-year capex plan supports mid-single-digit rate base growth. Gas Utilities capex accelerates from $397M (2025) to $591M (2029). Successful rate case outcomes have been consistent, with $85.9M in combined new rates and rider recovery in 2025 alone.

Management's 6% EPS growth guidance for 2026 is credible given rate base growth and data center demand, though the recent earnings miss pattern suggests execution risk in meeting specific quarterly targets.

## Valuation Assessment

At $74.17, BKH trades at 18.6x trailing EPS ($3.98) and approximately 17.0x FY2026 consensus EPS ($4.35). The multi-utility peer median P/E is 21.0x (MDU at 23.6x, NWE at 24.9x, OGE at 21.0x, PNW at 20.6x). BKH's discount of approximately 19% to peer median reflects merger execution uncertainty and the recent earnings miss pattern.

The 3.8% dividend yield compares to a peer median of 3.5%, providing a modest income premium. On a P/E basis, only Avista (17.6x) trades cheaper in the peer set, and AVA offers a materially higher 4.8% yield.

If the merger closes successfully and the combined entity achieves consensus FY2027E EPS of $4.62, a re-rating to 19-20x P/E (still below peer median) implies $88-92 per share. If the merger fails and the stock reverts to standalone valuation at 17-18x the standalone earnings trajectory of roughly $4.20, the stock could trade back to $71-76.

The current price already reflects a significant portion of the data center optionality following the 34% rally. The analyst consensus mean target of $83 and median of $82 imply 10-12% upside, with a range of $76-$90.

## Competitive Landscape

BKH operates in regulated, low-competition rural utility markets with limited competitive threats to its retail franchise. Its competitive advantages include:

- **Integrated coal mine**: The Wyodak mine delivers coal at $1.26/MMBtu, providing a structural cost advantage for baseload generation.
- **Data center positioning**: The LPCS tariff structure in Wyoming is attracting attention as a template for other utilities. Wyoming's regulatory environment, low population density, and BKH's available transmission capacity create a differentiated proposition for hyperscale data center operators.
- **Rate recovery mechanisms**: Extensive fuel adjustment clauses, integrity riders, and weather normalization clauses across jurisdictions reduce regulatory lag.
- **Multi-state diversification**: Operating across eight states reduces exposure to any single regulatory jurisdiction.

Gas utility margin grew 15.6% over two years, data center demand is accelerating, and the NWE merger would further strengthen geographic diversification. The primary competitive risk is that Wyoming's data center opportunity attracts utility competitors or that political/regulatory pushback in Wyoming constrains future development (growing local skepticism about water usage, housing impacts, and rate effects is documented in the 10-K risk factors).

## Risk Assessment

**Merger Regulatory Risk (HIGH):** FERC, Montana PSC, Nebraska PSC, South Dakota PUC, HSR clearance, and FCC consent are all outstanding. Conservation groups in Montana have already raised concerns. Any state commission could impose conditions that limit synergies (rate freezes, service quality commitments, employee retention mandates) or block the deal. The $100M termination fee is relatively small (~0.65% of combined enterprise value), suggesting both parties view the deal as completable but are not willing to bear large penalties. This is a binary outcome that could move the stock $5-10 in either direction.

**Earnings Execution Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** The Q3 2025 miss was particularly large (-14.8%). Current quarter estimates have been revised down 8.1% over the past 30 days ($2.01 → $1.84), suggesting analysts are still calibrating.

**Generation Reliability (MODERATE):** Coal fleet availability at Wygen III dropped to 77.7% from 89.8%, forcing costly market power purchases and contributing to a $10.5M decline in Electric Utilities operating income despite $25M in new rate recovery. Unplanned outages in an aging coal fleet represent ongoing operational risk.

**Capital Markets Dependence (MODERATE):** The company structurally requires continuous access to equity and debt markets. The ATM program raised $400M over 2024-2025 with ongoing dilution. A credit rating downgrade from BBB+/Baa2 would increase borrowing costs on the $4.7B five-year capex plan.

**Interest Expense Escalation (MODERATE):** The combined post-merger entity will carry even more debt, amplifying interest expense compression of rate-case earnings gains in a persistent high-rate environment.

**Wildfire Liability (MODERATE):** Wyoming enacted wildfire liability mitigation legislation in 2025, but Colorado and South Dakota lack comparable protections. Given the company's rural service territory with significant vegetation-to-wire contact risk, this remains an asymmetric tail risk.

**Data Center Customer Concentration (EMERGING):** Dependence on Microsoft and Meta for Wyoming Electric industrial load creates concentration risk. Loss of a major data center customer or slowdown in buildout plans would materially impact the growth narrative.

The options market is pricing substantially more risk than the stock's recent rally implies, which warrants caution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$88-92, +19-24%)
- NWE merger closes in H2 2026 with manageable regulatory conditions
- Combined entity re-rates toward 19-20x forward P/E as integration proceeds
- Microsoft's Cheyenne expansion drives multi-year industrial load growth, boosting Wyoming Electric rate base over 5 years
- Gas utility margin continues expanding through rate recovery
- FY2027E EPS of $4.62+ on the combined entity achieves consensus
- Yield support at 3.5-4.0% attracts income investors to the scaled-up platform

### Bear Case (~$58-62, -16-22%)
- Merger blocked or delayed with onerous conditions; stock re-rates to standalone valuation of 15-16x trailing EPS
- Data center buildout slows due to power supply constraints, regulatory pushback, or hyperscaler capex reductions
- Continued earnings misses erode analyst confidence and compress the multiple
- Rising interest expenses and persistent negative free cash flow force additional dilutive equity issuance
- Coal fleet reliability continues deteriorating, increasing purchased power costs
- Credit downgrade from BBB+ constrains capital plan execution

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. FERC approval received with no material adverse conditions (first and most important regulatory milestone)
2. Montana PSC approval received (the most contentious jurisdiction given conservation group opposition)
3. FY2026 Q1 or Q2 earnings beat estimates by 3%+ with clean execution on utility margins

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Any state regulatory commission issues a preliminary recommendation to deny or impose conditions that would eliminate >50% of projected synergies
2. Two consecutive quarters of negative estimate revisions exceeding 5%
3. Microsoft or Meta announces significant delays or cancellations of Wyoming data center expansion plans

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected May 2026) and FERC preliminary review timeline (expected mid-2026). The merger regulatory outcome is the primary driver and should have substantial clarity by Q3 2026.

## Conclusion

Black Hills Corp sits at the intersection of AI-driven data center demand in Wyoming and a merger of equals, but the stock's 34% rally has already priced meaningful optionality, and the path forward depends on binary regulatory outcomes 3-6 months from resolution. The 18.6x trailing P/E discount to multi-utility peers reflects genuine merger uncertainty that the options market and short sellers are actively pricing. The execution risk from recent earnings misses adds further caution. The 3.8% yield provides income support, but the elevated payout ratio and dividend growth cap during the merger period limit near-term income appeal. We rate BKH HOLD: the data center story and merger combination could drive the stock toward $85-90 over 12-18 months if regulatory approvals proceed cleanly, but the risk-reward at current levels does not warrant new capital commitment ahead of these binary catalysts.
