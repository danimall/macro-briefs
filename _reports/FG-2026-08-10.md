## Executive Summary

F&G Annuities & Life is a top-ten U.S. fixed indexed annuity and pension-risk-transfer writer, 72% owned by Fidelity National Financial, with $74.7B of AUM before reinsurance and $55.9B retained at June 30, 2026. At $27.04 the stock trades at 0.589x book value per share excluding AOCI ($45.93), 0.81x GAAP common book ($33.29), and roughly 7.8x the $3.45 current-year adjusted EPS consensus. On screening metrics it looks inexpensive against a Life & Health peer median P/E of 19.1.

The discount is doing work that the screens do not show. Fidelity & Guaranty Life Insurance Company paid no dividends to its intermediate holding company in 2025 and management discloses that its maximum ordinary dividend capacity for 2026 is $0, because Iowa law permits ordinary dividends only from earned profits. Meanwhile the guarantor group carries $2,239M of notes payable against $340M of non-goodwill assets, and its cash plus investments fell from approximately $520M at December 31, 2025 to $248M at June 30, 2026. In the same half, F&G returned approximately $195M to shareholders — precisely 100% of first-half adjusted net earnings to common — and ran the repurchase authorization down to roughly $12M remaining. Separately, 27% of financial assets carried at fair value are Level 3, of which about $13.5B is priced from third-party or broker quotations that the company states it does not adjust. For an insurer whose entire investment case rests on the credibility of reported book value, that combination gives a rational basis for a sub-book multiple rather than evidence of mispricing.

The operating trend has also weakened at the margin. Consensus 2026 EPS has been cut 9.1% over 90 days to $3.45 after misses of 26.6%, 20.9% and 18.8% in the last three reported quarters. Second-quarter gross sales fell 34% to $2.7B, life premiums and other fees fell 35%, contractholder deposits net of withdrawals turned to a $567M outflow from a $1,070M inflow a year earlier, and adjusted ROA fell to 68bps from 87bps for FY2025. Against that, book value per share ex-AOCI grew 5.9% year over year, the alternative-investment shortfall versus management's expected return moderated to roughly $93M in the first half from $278M for all of 2025, core retail FIA and IUL had one of their strongest quarters on record, and the CEO and a director bought stock on weakness in March at ~$21. Our probability-weighted expected return from $27.04 is slightly negative. Existing holders should trim; new capital has no reason to enter here.

## Company Overview

F&G writes fixed indexed annuities (~46% of FY2025 gross sales), fixed rate annuities principally MYGA (26%), pension risk transfer (15%), funding agreements (12%) and indexed universal life (~1%). Gross sales grew from $3.9B in 2019 to $14.6B in FY2025; AUM rose from $26.5B at FNF's June 2020 acquisition to $57.6B at December 31, 2025 and $74.7B before reinsurance at June 30, 2026. Inforce liabilities were $56.8B at year-end 2025 with roughly five-year liability duration.

Distribution runs through approximately 300 independent marketing organizations representing ~173,000 agents, plus ~26 banks and broker-dealers representing ~14,000 advisors. Two concentrations matter: the top five bank/BD firms were 81% of that channel's FY2025 sales, and the top five states were 40% of retail sales. Bank/BD annuity sales fell to $3.8B in FY2025 from $5.0B in 2024.

Investment management is outsourced. Blackstone ISG-I Advisors managed 78% of the $69B portfolio at December 31, 2025, for fees of $182M in 2025. An omnibus termination side letter obligates F&G and FNF to keep the insurance subsidiaries engaged with Blackstone; before June 1, 2029 termination is available only for narrowly defined cause, and Blackstone's remedies for a non-permitted termination include specific performance and full contract damages. The relationship is a genuine sourcing advantage in private credit, and it is also a multi-year contractual constraint on F&G's ability to reprice or replace its largest expense-and-risk relationship.

Carrier financial strength ratings are A (A.M. Best), A− (S&P), A− (Fitch) and A3 (Moody's), all stable. The holding company is rated BBB− (S&P), BBB (Fitch) and Baa3 (Moody's) — one notch above high yield at two of three agencies — with the $375M junior subordinated notes due 2065 at BB/Ba1.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $5,731M; net earnings to common were $248M against adjusted net earnings of $482M, down 12% from $546M in 2024. Management disclosed $30M of favorable one-time items inside the FY2025 adjusted figure (a $16M reinsurance true-up, $10M tax valuation-allowance benefit, $4M reserve release), so the clean base is approximately $452M.

Second-quarter 2026 was a GAAP loss: $(81)M, or $(0.62) per diluted share, versus +$35M / $0.26 a year earlier. Adjusted net earnings to common were $85M ($0.65), down 17%. The largest add-back in the bridge was $137M of net realized and unrealized losses on fixed maturities, equities and other invested assets, plus $30M for reinsurance embedded derivatives and $31M for other derivatives. Excluding realized AFS losses from adjusted earnings is defensible on a book-value basis — selling an AFS bond at a loss shifts the loss from AOCI into income without changing equity — but it means adjusted earnings do not tell you whether reinvestment is accretive.

For the half, adjusted net earnings to common were $195M versus $194M, essentially flat. Because FY2025's $482M implies $288M in the second half against $194M in the first, a simple annualization of first-half 2026 ($390M, ~$2.96 per share) would understate the year if the seasonal skew repeats. A range of roughly $450–480M of adjusted net earnings — second half tracking 2H2025 less the $30M of one-timers — implies $3.45–3.65 of adjusted EPS, or 7.4–7.8x at $27.04. We would not put a point estimate on it: the alternative-investment line alone swung $278M against expectation in 2025.

Note that the reported P/E fields in the market data (9.13x on stated EPS of $1.88) do not reconcile to each other or to the price; $27.04 ÷ $1.88 is 14.4x on trailing GAAP. We therefore anchor on consensus adjusted EPS and on book value.

**The alternative-investment drag is the clearest identifiable upside lever.** Alternative investment income was $278M below management's long-term expected return in FY2025, after shortfalls of $145M (2024) and $153M (2023). In 2026 the gap narrowed to $44M in Q1 and $49M in Q2 — roughly $93M for the half. Critically, this shortfall is disclosed as commentary and is *not* added back in the non-GAAP bridge, so adjusted earnings already carry it. Full normalization would lift the reported 8.0% adjusted ROE toward management's pricing expectation. We have not verified whether the expected-return assumption was raised from ~10% to ~12% between the 10-K and the Q2 10-Q; if it was, the disclosed shortfall looks larger but adjusted earnings are unaffected.

**Balance sheet and the valuation crux.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $103,592M, F&G shareholders' equity $4,609M, AOCI $(1,658)M (deteriorated from $(1,488)M). Book value per share excluding AOCI and the $250M preferred liquidation preference computes to ($4,609 + $1,658 − $250) ÷ 130.94 = $45.95, reproducing the company's reported $45.93 and up from $43.39 a year earlier. Notes payable of $2,239M against equity including non-controlling interests puts financial leverage at 32.3% of total capital including AOCI, or 26.0% excluding it — normal for a life insurer. The screening figure of ~19x debt/equity reflects total liabilities, 95% of which are policyholder obligations, and is not a leverage measure.

A sustainable 8.0% adjusted ROE capitalized at 0.589x book implies a market-required return near 13.6%. Part of that spread is defensible on fundamentals alone — an 8% ROE against a 9–10% cost of equity justifies some sub-book multiple without any mark skepticism. The remainder is best explained by mark quality (Level 3 at 27% of fair-valued assets, priced off unadjusted third-party quotes) and by the control structure (FNF at 71.9% and rising, public float below 30%).

**Asset quality deteriorated across both mortgage books in a single half.** Residential non-performing loans rose from $67M (1.4%) to $129M (2.3%) of a growing book; commercial mortgages with debt-service coverage below 1.00x rose from $52M to $169M, a 3.2x increase; weighted-average CML DSC fell from 2.3x to 2.1x; total non-accrual mortgages went from $79M to $138M; and mortgage credit provisions were $22M in the first half versus $5M a year earlier. Absolute magnitudes are immaterial against $4.6B of equity, and offsets exist — the AFS credit loss allowance actually fell from $104M to $71M, CML loan-to-value improved slightly to 56%, and five-year average credit impairment of 6bps is genuinely low. But a roughly 4x increase in provisions and a 3.2x increase in sub-1.0x-DSC commercial loans occurring together is the pattern that precedes larger losses if it persists. One specific disclosure warrants attention: 82 investment-grade securities with $1,111M of amortized cost carried at $719M of fair value — a 35% markdown — with no credit loss allowance recorded, attributed to interest rates.

**Duration and rate sensitivity.** Asset duration of 4.5 years sits below liability duration of 5.2 years. A +100bp parallel shift would reduce fixed-maturity fair value by approximately $3.4B against $4.6B of equity, with partial liability offset under current discounting. Economically the position favors rising rates and is hurt in a falling-rate reinvestment environment; either way, the AOCI deficit sustains the gap between GAAP book and the ex-AOCI metric management reports.

**Capital returns and the funding constraint.** Approximately $195M was returned in the first half — $120M of repurchases (roughly 4.6M shares at ~$26 average) plus dividends — equal to 100% of first-half adjusted earnings to common. Remaining authorization was approximately $12M at June 30. The $1.00 annualized common dividend yields 3.70% at $27.04 and costs roughly $131M, with a further ~$17M for the 6.875% preferred; earnings cover that comfortably, but the binding constraint is holdco cash, not the P&L. FGL Insurance's RBC was ~430% against a 400% target, and three of its IRIS ratios were outside the usual range at year-end 2025, including "surplus relief."

## Growth Analysis

The headline AUM number is growing and the flow composition is not.

Records first: AUM before reinsurance reached $74.7B (+8%), interest and investment income rose to $718M in Q2 from $682M, indexed annuity account balances grew 5% in the half to $34,903M, and management states core retail FIA plus IUL had one of its strongest quarters on record. Middle-market IUL distributed through network marketing groups shows a claimed three-year sales CAGR of 24% against an industry 7%.

Against that: Q2 gross sales fell 34% to $2.7B from $4.1B; life premiums and other fees fell 35% to $394M (PRT gross premiums $550M in the half versus $743M); fixed rate annuity balances fell 3%; and contractholder deposits of $4,437M against withdrawals of $5,004M produced a $567M net outflow on the financing line, reversing a $1,070M inflow. That line excludes PRT and life-contingent premiums, so it is not total net flow, but the direction is a change.

Management frames the sales decline as deliberate withdrawal from rate-competitive "opportunistic" MYGA, which is credible given the explicit core/opportunistic segmentation in the 10-K and is the correct behavior when spread economics compress. The effect on future AUM growth is identical regardless of intent.

The estimate revision picture is unambiguous and negative: current quarter $0.92 (−3.0% in 30 days, −7.0% in 90), current year $3.45 (−2.6% / −9.1%), next year $4.97 (−2.4% / −3.5%). Three consecutive misses of 18.8%, 20.9% and 26.6% mean the two-analyst consensus has poor predictive calibration on this name; the next-year figure implying a large step-up in earnings deserves particular skepticism given adjusted earnings were flat year over year in the first half.

The stated strategic targets — a ~45bps operating expense ratio by year-end 2027 and ~25% fee-based earnings mix by year-end 2028 — come from call summaries rather than filings, and the fee transition is being achieved by ceding risk. Indexed annuity cessions roughly doubled as a share of the block in six months, from $3,198M of $33,547M (9.5%) to $6,681M of $35,122M (19%), and fixed rate annuities are now approximately 68% ceded. Total reinsurance recoverable rose from $17,545M to $20,876M. That reduces capital intensity and moves cash closer to the parent, which is rational for a constrained holding company, but third-party capital providers capture the incremental spread. Adjusted ROA falling from 87bps to 68bps is consistent with that trade, though the comparison mixes a quarter against a year and is affected by the $49M alternative shortfall.

## Valuation Assessment

**Absolute.** At $27.04: 0.589x book ex-AOCI, 0.81x GAAP common book, 7.8x the $3.45 current-year consensus, 5.4x the $4.97 next-year consensus, and 3.70% dividend yield.

**Relative.** The Life & Health peer set is heterogeneous and the median P/E of 19.1 is not a usable anchor: Brighthouse trades at 4.8x, Jackson Financial at 154.5x on distorted GAAP, Primerica at 12.9x, CNO at 19.1x, Trupanion at 50.5x. The economically comparable names are the sub-book, GAAP-noisy annuity writers — Brighthouse and Jackson — and F&G sits between them on price-to-book. Its ex-AOCI multiple is not an outlier within the spread-annuity cohort; the sector as a whole is priced for mark skepticism and rate sensitivity.

**Is the market wrong?** The bull argument is that book value ex-AOCI is growing ~6% annually, the AOCI deficit is rate-driven and pulls to par as bonds season, alternative returns normalizing would add to EPS, and a Peak Altitude monetization would crystallize value against roughly $700M of invested cost while easing the holdco constraint. All four are legitimate.

The bear argument is more specific. Twenty-seven percent of fair-valued assets are Level 3, and $13.5B of that is priced off quotes the company does not independently model — so the denominator in the price-to-book calculation is partly an estimate F&G does not control. Reinsurance recoverables and deposit assets across six counterparties total roughly $20.4B against $4,609M of equity (~4.4x), with Ancient Re ($1,711M) and Fort Greene ($1,221M) carrying no shown financial strength ratings; funds-withheld structures in which F&G retains the assets materially mitigate this, but F&G stays directly liable to policyholders regardless. The controlling shareholder holds 71.9% and rising, and the float is contracting because FNF did not sell into the buyback. Finally, the holdco constraint is a first-order valuation input: an equity whose distributions depend on upstreaming from a carrier with $0 of ordinary dividend capacity for the year deserves a discount to a comparable business without that constraint.

A cheap multiple accompanied by falling estimates, three consecutive large misses, and identifiable reasons for the discount is the standard configuration of a value trap. We score valuation modestly above neutral — the book-value floor and 3.70% yield are real — but we do not treat 0.589x as evidence of mispricing.

**Targets.** Base case $27: book ex-AOCI compounding to approximately $47.50 by mid-2027 at 0.57x, cross-checked as roughly 7.8x $3.45 of adjusted EPS. That is essentially the current price plus the dividend, and it matches the sole external target of $27.00. Bull case $34: alternative returns normalize toward the long-term assumption, Peak Altitude is monetized at or above carrying value, a new repurchase authorization restores the buyback bid, and the multiple re-rates to roughly 0.72x book ex-AOCI (+26% from spot). Bear case $19.50: mortgage and structured-credit provisions accelerate, an S&P or Moody's downgrade takes holdco debt to high yield, the common dividend or buyback is cut to preserve liquidity, and the multiple compresses to 0.42x book (−28%).

Probability-weighting 50% base (+4% total return with dividend), 20% bull (+26%) and 30% bear (−28%) gives an expected return of approximately −1%. That is a REDUCE, not an AVOID: the distribution is wide and centered near zero, not skewed toward impairment.

## Competitive Landscape

F&G ranks 6th in industry FIA sales, 11th in MYGA, 7th in IUL and 7th in PRT — a credible top-ten franchise that is not a scale leader against Athene, Corebridge, Global Atlantic or Allianz Life. Its two stated advantages are distribution breadth and access to Blackstone-originated private credit. The second is contractual rather than proprietary, cannot be exited before mid-2029 except for narrowly defined cause, and is the same private-credit-backed annuity architecture deployed at larger scale by Apollo/Athene, KKR/Global Atlantic, Brookfield and Ares/Aspida. F&G participates in the model.

Two trajectory observations. First, the shift from spread to fee is being executed by ceding FIA and MYGA business to reinsurers backed by competitors' capital — Aspida (Ares-backed), Somerset, Everlake, Fort Greene (approximately $1B of Blackstone-fund commitments, with F&G holding no ownership stake) and Ancient Re. This lowers required capital and generates fee income closer to the parent while transferring incremental spread to third parties. Second, product-mix diversification has genuinely improved while direct concentration has not: top-five bank/BD firms at 81% of channel sales, top-five states at 40% of retail sales, and counterparty exposure more concentrated in dollar terms than a year ago.

The demand backdrop is favorable and durable — more than 11,000 Americans turn 65 daily, the PRT market runs ~$45B annually against $3T of private DB assets, and the FABN market is ~$276B. Industry growth is not the issue; F&G's share of the resulting economics is.

## Risk Assessment

**Holding-company liquidity (highest priority).** FGL Insurance paid no dividends to its intermediate holding company in 2025 and has estimated maximum ordinary dividend capacity of $0 for 2026. The guarantor group held $2,009M of total assets at June 30, of which $1,669M was goodwill, against $2,361M of liabilities including $2,239M of notes payable; net assets moved from $(53)M to $(352)M in six months and liquid assets from ~$520M to ~$248M. Interest expense was $164M in FY2025. This is not consolidated insolvency — the guarantor group excludes the insurance subsidiaries where the value sits — and alternative sources exist (CF Bermuda and F&G Cayman Re dividends under different regimes, extraordinary Iowa dividends with regulatory approval, a $750M revolver, a $200M FNF facility, ~$102M from the March 2026 F&G Life Re sale). The filings do not provide a sources-and-uses bridge, so a benign reading — capital downstreamed into the carriers to support FIA and PRT growth — remains open. Either way, the buyback authorization running to ~$12M is consistent with a parent managing its cash tightly.

**Reinsurance counterparty concentration.** Roughly $20.4B across six counterparties versus $4,609M of equity, with two unrated. Funds-withheld and coinsurance-funds-withheld structures, trusts and letters of credit reduce economic exposure substantially. The precise split between GAAP reinsurance recoverable ($20,876M) and deposit assets ($15,168M) under non-risk-transfer treaties is not verified.

**Contingent capital-support obligations.** A Kubera note-purchase commitment of up to $435M; a Corbeau Re / Canada Life Barbados excess-of-loss arrangement of up to $2,400M with an F&G capital-contribution obligation if Corbeau Re surplus falls below defined levels; and a $150M Raven Re / Nomura letter of credit with an F&G repayment backstop that terminates October 1, 2027, with earlier termination possible. None has been drawn materially, but the October 2027 expiry is a datable renewal risk on statutory reserve financing.

**Surrender-charge roll-off.** Of $36,109M of fixed and indexed annuity account value at year-end 2025, 7% was already out of surrender charge, 6% expired in 2025 and 24% expires 2026–2028 — roughly 37% losing meaningful protection within three years, overlapping precisely with the fee-transition window. Market-value-adjustment features on ~83% of the block currently favor the insurer.

**Rating sensitivity.** BBB−/Baa3 senior unsecured is one notch above high yield. A downgrade would raise funding cost exactly when holdco liquidity is thin.

**Governance and management transition.** The CEO, CFO and Chief Liability Officer all changed within roughly two months of mid-2026, with the new CEO promoted from CFO — internal continuity on capital, less so on liability pricing, where the Chief Liability Officer transitions out August 10, 2026. The departing CEO now runs Peak Altitude, the owned-distribution platform under strategic review; we draw no inference about motive but note the fact pattern. A related-party arrangement obligates F&G to pay BilCar, LLC (50% owner of MVB Management, an affiliate of Executive Chairman William Foley) fees it would otherwise have received from Blackstone through March 31, 2033; the annual amount is not disclosed in the filings reviewed. Compensation performance measures are Adjusted Net Earnings, Sales and AUM — two of three are volume metrics, with no book-value-per-share or risk-adjusted measure.

**Regulatory and tax.** The DOL fiduciary rule remains stayed. Beginning in 2026 F&G can no longer file a consolidated return with FNF but remains subject to the 15% corporate alternative minimum tax because of FNF's ownership. A $40M valuation allowance sits against a $125M net deferred tax asset.

**Preferred conversion.** The 6.875% Series A Mandatory Convertible (5.0M shares, $250M preference) automatically converts January 15, 2027. At $27.04, far below the $45.00 lower threshold, the maximum 1.1111 ratio applies: 5,555,500 shares, or 4.24% of shares outstanding and 4.07% post-issuance ownership dilution. Known, modest, and economically borne by the preferred holder, whose $250M preference converts into roughly $150M of stock.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 55.4% against 38.0% one-year realized — rich by 17.4 points, at the 62nd percentile within the chain — with backwardation into the longer tenors (48.1% at three months, 44.9% at six). Put/call skew is balanced at −0.4% and put/call open interest is 0.69, tilted toward calls. The reading is that the market prices elevated near-term uncertainty without a directional view: earnings are already behind us (August 5), so the residual near-term event risk plausibly relates to the Peak Altitude process, a new repurchase authorization, or holdco funding actions. For a REDUCE, the practical implication is that hedging or exiting via options is expensive; outright trimming is the cheaper expression. Short interest at 13.6% of float with 6.5 days to cover indicates the bearish case is already substantially held in a name with under 30% public float, which raises squeeze risk on any positive announcement and is a reason to trim rather than short.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $34 (+26%)

Book value ex-AOCI compounds mid-single digits and the AOCI deficit pulls toward par as the portfolio seasons, so the ex-AOCI metric proves the right denominator. Alternative-investment returns normalize toward management's expectation, lifting adjusted ROE from 8% toward management's pricing expectation. The Peak Altitude strategic review produces a sale or partial monetization at or above the roughly $700M invested, delivering cash directly to a liquidity-constrained parent and validating a sum-of-parts argument the market currently ignores. A new repurchase authorization restores the bid, and buybacks at 0.59x book add materially to book value per share. Core FIA and IUL momentum continues while opportunistic MYGA pricing discipline protects spread. The multiple re-rates to roughly 0.72x book ex-AOCI. Insider purchases by the then-CEO (20,000 shares including 10,000 at $20.99) and a director support this reading.

### Bear Case → $19.50 (−28%)

The credit trend that emerged in the first half persists: residential non-performing loans continue climbing, sub-1.0x-DSC commercial mortgages keep expanding, and provisions scale from $22M per half into materiality. Level 3 marks on 27% of fair-valued assets are tested, and the 82 investment-grade securities carried 35% below amortized cost with no allowance require one. Alternative returns stay below assumption for a fourth consecutive year. The holdco liquidity squeeze forces a choice: with FGL Insurance unable to pay ordinary dividends and guarantor-group liquid assets at $248M against $164M of annual interest and roughly $148M of dividends, either the buyback stays suspended indefinitely or the common dividend is cut. An S&P or Moody's downgrade takes holdco debt to high yield, raising funding cost. Estimate revisions continue their present course and the next-year $4.97 consensus proves aspirational, as three consecutive misses of 19–27% suggest. Surrender-charge roll-off on 37% of the deferred block accelerates lapses into 2027–2028. The multiple compresses to 0.42x book — near where the stock traded in March 2026 at ~$21.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Base case $27 (approximately flat, +4% with the dividend); bull $34 (+26%); bear $19.50 (−28%). Probability-weighted expected return approximately −1% over 12 months.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into strength rather than at market. The stock has traded a $26–33 range since April; scale out on rallies above $29–30, which is where the multiple approaches 0.65x book ex-AOCI with no resolution of the holdco constraint. Retain no more than a token position pending the Peak Altitude outcome — 13.6% short interest and under 30% float mean a monetization announcement could move the stock sharply, which argues against a zero position and firmly against shorting. Do not hedge with listed options at 55.4% near-term implied volatility against 38% realized.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. Disclosure of a funded path for holdco distributions — an extraordinary dividend approval from the Iowa Insurance Division, a Peak Altitude transaction with quantified proceeds, or a new repurchase authorization of $200M+ with stated funding sources.
2. Alternative-investment shortfall below $25M in a single quarter, confirming normalization toward the long-term assumption, with adjusted ROE ex-AOCI printing 9.0%+.
3. Two consecutive quarters of stable-to-rising consensus EPS revisions and an in-line or better adjusted EPS print, breaking the three-quarter miss streak.
4. Price near $22–23 (roughly 0.48x book ex-AOCI), where bear-case downside compresses to roughly 12% and the dividend yield exceeds 4.3%.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID (any one):**
1. A common dividend reduction, or a second consecutive quarter with no repurchase activity and no new authorization.
2. A holding-company downgrade to sub-investment grade by S&P or Moody's.
3. Mortgage credit provisions exceeding $40M in a single quarter, or a credit loss allowance established against the $1,111M amortized-cost investment-grade watch-list block.
4. Any credit event, ratings action, or capital shortfall at Ancient Re, Fort Greene, Somerset or Aspida, or a required capital contribution to Corbeau Re or Raven Re.
5. Failure to renew or replace the $150M Raven Re / Nomura facility ahead of its October 1, 2027 termination.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next scheduled checkpoint is the Q3 2026 report in early November 2026, with three items to verify: whether a new repurchase authorization was granted, the sequential direction of mortgage non-performing balances and provisions, and the alternative-investment shortfall. Interim triggers are any 8-K on Peak Altitude, and the January 15, 2027 mandatory preferred conversion, which is mechanical and already sized at 4.07% post-issuance dilution.

## Conclusion

F&G is a real franchise — top ten in fixed indexed annuities, PRT and IUL, with record AUM, growing book value per share excluding AOCI, and a differentiated middle-market IUL niche. It is also a 72%-controlled subsidiary whose principal carrier cannot pay ordinary dividends this year, whose holding company holds $248M of liquid assets against $2,239M of debt, whose repurchase authorization is functionally exhausted, whose consensus EPS has been cut 9.1% in 90 days after three misses averaging 22%, and whose reported book value depends on Level 3 marks it does not independently model across 27% of fair-valued assets.

At 0.589x book excluding AOCI and 7.8x this year's adjusted EPS, the stock is priced for those facts rather than in spite of them. The identifiable upside levers are genuine — alternative-return normalization, and a Peak Altitude monetization that would address the holdco constraint directly — but neither is dated, and the sole external analyst target of $27.00 sits at the current price, providing no independent confirmation in either direction. Our probability-weighted return from $27.04 is approximately −1% over twelve months, with 26% upside against 28% downside.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength above $29. Revisit toward $22–23, or on confirmed holdco funding relief combined with a second quarter of contained alternative-investment shortfall.
