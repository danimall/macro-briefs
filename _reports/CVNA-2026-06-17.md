**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carvana has completed one of the more dramatic corporate turnarounds in recent memory. After a 2022 net loss of $2.89B that put it near bankruptcy, the company reported FY2025 net income of ~$1.9B, Q1 2026 GAAP operating income of $581M (9.0% margin), and adjusted EBITDA of $672M (10.4% margin). Retail unit growth has now run above 40% year-over-year for six consecutive quarters (187,393 units in Q1 2026, +40%), and revenue grew 52% to $6.43B. While CarMax is warning of soft used-car volumes, higher acquisition costs, and subprime credit risk, Carvana is taking share through a lower-cost, vertically integrated model. The operating story is genuine and not yet mature.

The complication is that a substantial portion of profit is not operating profit in the conventional sense. Roughly $354M of Q1 2026 gross profit (of $1.27B total) is gain-on-sale from originated loans — 100% margin and the single largest swing factor in reported profitability. This is a leveraged exposure to subprime auto credit and securitization spreads, both of which directly drove CarMax's warning and CVNA's ~8% sympathy decline in early June. Q1 2026 already disclosed "lower loan sale spreads." A standard revenue/EPS screen does not reveal that the marginal dollar of Carvana profit depends on functioning forward-flow and securitization markets and benign subprime credit.

At ~$64 (post a ~5-for-1 May 2026 split), CVNA trades at roughly 41x earnings versus an automotive-retail peer median near 21.5x, and roughly 89-91x EV/FCF on bear-case math that incorporates the ~$5.0B debt load. The score trajectory has improved meaningfully (4.4 → 5.0 → 5.8 across the prior three reports, AVOID → AVOID → HOLD), and the operating data confirms that trend. But the combination of a premium multiple, the credit-cycle dependence of reported profit, an unresolved SEC subpoena, a $2.2B related-party tax liability, and a Garcia-family control structure keeps this at HOLD rather than BUY. The growth thesis supports holding; the valuation and structural risk profile do not support adding.

## Company Overview

Carvana is the largest pure-play e-commerce platform for buying and selling used cars in the U.S. Customers complete the entire transaction online — browsing 70,000+ listings, financing, trading in, and adding vehicle service contracts, GAP coverage, and Root-powered insurance — with home delivery or pickup at its glass "vending machine" towers.

The model is vertically integrated end-to-end: vehicle acquisition (largely direct from consumers, avoiding auction fees), in-house inspection and reconditioning centers, a proprietary hub-and-spoke hauler network, in-house loan origination, and the ADESA wholesale auction network (acquired 2022) that is being progressively converted into reconditioning capacity. This integration is the source of Carvana's structural cost advantage and the multi-year capacity runway toward management's ~3 million-unit ambition.

The addressable market is large and fragmented: ~37 million U.S. used-car transactions per year, with the top 10 retailers holding less than 10% combined share. Carvana operates across 316 MSAs reaching more than 80% of the U.S. population, as a single reportable segment. The core bull thesis is online penetration of an offline-dominated, fragmented category.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and units.** FY2025 revenue was ~$20.3B, up 49% from ~$13.7B in 2024. Cumulative volume since 2012 is ~2.9M vehicles / $90.5B revenue.

**Profitability inflection.** The trajectory from the 2022 loss is steep: -$2.89B (2022) → ~$150M (2023) → ~$404M (2024) → ~$1.9B (FY2025). The FY2025 figure is inflated by a large deferred-tax valuation-allowance release in Q4 2025, so it overstates run-rate earnings. The cleaner read is Q1 2026 operating income (+47% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA, neither of which depends on the tax item. Total GPU was ~$6,783 (non-GAAP ~$6,911), roughly flat-to-slightly-down YoY on mix, offset by SG&A-per-unit leverage (non-GAAP SG&A/unit fell to $3,325 from $3,495). The cost line is doing the work.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is ~$5.0B (mostly 9% senior secured notes due 2030/2031); cash ~$2.4B; total liquidity resources ~$6.9B. Stockholders' equity recovered to $4.6B in Q1 2026 from negative equity in 2022. Interest expense is falling — $99M in Q1 2026 versus $139M a year earlier — after the 2023 distressed exchange and subsequent refinancing. Altman Z-Score is back in "safe" territory (~3.0), though D/E remains elevated at ~2.6x. The balance sheet is much improved but still levered, with 9% coupons that restrict dividends, additional debt, and asset sales.

**Cash flow quality — the central caveat.** Operating cash flow was only $107M in Q1 2026, down from $232M, pressured by an inventory build to support growth. The largest component of "other gross profit" is gain on loan sales: $354M in Q1 2026 alone (versus $273M a year earlier). This is high-quality reported profit, but it is contingent on (a) Ally's $6.0B forward-flow commitment ($3.3B unused), (b) $12B in fixed-pool loan-purchase agreements through 2027 ($9.9B unused), and (c) functioning securitization markets at favorable spreads. The mechanism that matters: subprime credit deterioration → wider spreads → lower gain on each loan sold → disproportionate hit to the 100%-margin profit layer → reported EPS compression even if unit volumes hold. This is precisely the dynamic CarMax warned about in June.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is the strongest dimension here and is broad-based rather than a single-line story. Six consecutive quarters of 40%+ unit growth, revenue +52% in Q1, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 10.4% together demonstrate operating leverage on a vertically integrated cost base. Management has guided to sequential Q2 records in both retail units and adjusted EBITDA, plus "significant" FY2026 growth.

Estimate revisions confirm positive earnings momentum: current-year EPS consensus has risen 10.3% over 90 days to $1.57, next-year up 4.1% to $2.10, with the current and next quarters revised up 11-13% on a 90-day basis. This is meaningful upward momentum, but it is short of the +30%/90-day velocity that typically marks a genuine re-rating, and the 30-day revisions have flattened (current quarter -0.1%, current year +2.5%). The earnings surprise record is mixed — two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, including a -19.9% miss in Q3 2025 — which argues for moderate rather than high confidence in forward consensus.

The structural runway is real: ADESA reconditioning conversions provide multi-year capacity expansion toward ~3M units, against a fragmented market where the top 10 retailers hold under 10% share. CVNA's 40% unit growth while CarMax struggles with volumes confirms share gains. The durability question is whether unit growth can continue at this pace if subprime affordability tightens — the same credit cycle that threatens the loan-sale gains also constrains the demand side.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$64, CVNA carries a trailing/forward P/E of ~41x versus an automotive-retail peer median of 21.5x. The closest direct comp, CarMax, trades at 30.7x; AutoZone 21.2x, O'Reilly 29.3x, Penske 13.1x. Carvana commands a premium to all of them.

The growth-adjusted case is more nuanced. On $1.57 current-year and $2.10 next-year consensus EPS, the multiple is ~41x current-year and ~31x next-year. Even crediting the superior growth rate, a PEG framework only partially closes the gap: against ~30% forward earnings growth, the forward PEG sits around 1.0-1.4x — not egregiously stretched, but not cheap. The cycle-adjusted framework that would justify ignoring a high trailing multiple does not fully apply here, because the trailing period is not a trough — it includes record adjusted EBITDA and a tax-driven net income boost. This is closer to peak-cycle earnings power than trough.

The valuation guardrail is decisive: at ~41x, this is not a sub-30x stock where rising estimates alone justify aggressive re-rating. The risk is asymmetric in the way premium multiples typically are — beats are largely priced in, and a credit-spread-driven miss on the loan-sale line would compress both the earnings and the multiple simultaneously. The bear-case ~89-91x EV/FCF figure, which incorporates leverage and the modest $107M Q1 operating cash flow, captures why the FCF-based valuation looks far less benign than the P/E.

Analyst targets span a wide $67 (low) to $120 (high), mean ~$92, median ~$93. The mean implies ~44% upside, and Morgan Stanley's ~$102 reflects a "scaling toward 3M units" bull case. I treat these as a genuine range rather than a floor: at a premium multiple, the high targets embed the same growth extrapolation as the bulls, so they are not independent confirmation. A valuation score of 4.5 reflects that the price already discounts much of the achievable operating success while leaving the credit-cycle and structural risks unpriced.

## Competitive Landscape

Carvana's competitive position is improving, which supports a 7.0. The clearest evidence is relative performance: CVNA is growing units ~40% and holding ~10% adjusted EBITDA margins in the same quarter CarMax is warning on volumes, acquisition costs, and subprime risk. That divergence is direct evidence of share capture through a structurally lower-cost model. The vertical integration — direct consumer acquisition (no auction fees), in-house reconditioning, proprietary logistics, in-house origination, and the ADESA network — is difficult to replicate and gives Carvana a per-unit cost advantage that widens as it scales.

The moat has two qualifications. First, the used-car category is intensely competitive and fragmented; Carvana's lead is in execution and cost, not in a network effect that locks out competitors. Second, the same credit-cycle exposure that threatens loan-sale economics is a sector-wide condition, so Carvana's relative position can improve even as the absolute environment deteriorates. On balance the trajectory is positive and underappreciated relative to the cyclical caution the market is applying.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the weakest dimension and the binding constraint on the rating, justifying a 4.0.

**Loan-sale-gain dependence (the crux).** The 100%-margin gain on loan sales is contingent on subprime credit health, securitization spreads, and forward-flow capacity. Spreads already compressed in Q1. A subprime deterioration or spread-widening event would hit reported profit disproportionately while standard screens show nothing. This is a specific, currently-active risk, not a sector truism.

**SEC subpoena (June 2025).** Following a January 2025 short-seller report alleging accounting and related-party issues, Carvana received an SEC subpoena "primarily relating to" those allegations. It is unresolved and the company is cooperating. A regulatory overhang of this type is a genuine binary that the market may be underweighting.

**Securities class action.** *In re Carvana Co. Securities Litigation* (D. Ariz.) survived a motion to dismiss in part on 10(b)/10b-5 and Section 11 claims and is in discovery, with only a $14M accrual.

**$2.2B Tax Receivable Agreement.** Carvana owes LLC unitholders 85% of realized tax savings, $1.7B of which is owed to the Garcia parties. A change of control or early termination accelerates this liability — a poison-pill-like overhang on M&A and a structural cash drain ($37M paid in Q1 alone).

**Control and related-party structure.** The Garcias hold super-voting Class B stock (10 votes/share) and Carvana Co. owns only 64.9% of the operating entity (35.1% non-controlling interest). A dense DriveTime related-party web (VSC administration $111M commissions in Q1, warranty admin, leases, loan servicing, auctions, aircraft) means meaningful related-party leakage and limited minority influence.

**Leverage and macro/credit sensitivity.** $5.0B debt at 9% coupons, with both demand (affordability) and gain-on-sale economics exposed to the subprime cycle, rates, and tariffs.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 91.6% versus 1-year historical volatility of 59.8% — rich by ~32 points and in the 89th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (91.6% near-term versus 73.3% long-term), which indicates the market expects an imminent catalyst. The put/call volume ratio is 2.04 and OI ratio 1.82, with the OI ratio having risen sharply (0.88 → 1.82) since the prior snapshot, signaling growing defensive positioning. The 6-month tenor shows a -4.8% put skew, consistent with hedging against a credit-cycle-driven decline. Short interest is elevated at 13.6% of float (6 days to cover). This positioning corroborates the fundamental concern around the loan-sale line and the SEC overhang: the bearish options structure and the structural risks point the same direction, which reinforces the decision not to add.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Carvana is the structural winner in online used-car retail, scaling toward 3M units in a fragmented $1T+ market while gaining share from a struggling CarMax. Operating leverage on a vertically integrated cost base is real (SG&A/unit falling, 10.4% adjusted EBITDA margin), unit growth has run 40%+ for six quarters, and ADESA conversions provide multi-year capacity. Estimates are rising, the balance sheet has de-levered to "safe" Altman Z territory, interest expense is falling, and S&P 500 inclusion broadens the holder base. If credit markets stay benign and growth continues, the loan-sale layer compounds as a capital-light monetization engine and the stock re-rates toward the ~$102-120 bull targets.

**Bear case.** Roughly 28% of gross profit is a leveraged bet on subprime auto credit that already shows spread compression; a CarMax-style deterioration would gut the 100%-margin profit layer and compress both EPS and the ~41x multiple at once. The SEC subpoena and securities litigation are unresolved binaries, the $2.2B TRA is a related-party cash drain with an acceleration trigger, and the Garcia control structure aligns the company to insiders over minority holders. Operating cash flow was only $107M in Q1 against $5.0B of 9% debt. On bear math the stock is ~89-91x EV/FCF. A credit-cycle turn could take the stock toward the low-$40s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The operating thesis is durable enough to justify retaining existing positions through the next several quarters of expected unit and EBITDA records, but the premium valuation and credit-cycle/structural risks preclude adding new capital here.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (expected late July 2026, for Q2) is the key checkpoint. The loan-sale spread commentary and gain-on-sale dollar figure are the lines to watch.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Loan-sale spreads stabilize or widen for two consecutive quarters while gain-on-sale dollars continue growing — demonstrating the profit engine is durable through the credit cycle, not just in a benign one.
- Resolution of the SEC subpoena without material findings, removing the regulatory binary.
- A pullback that compresses forward P/E below ~30x (roughly the high-$40s post-split) with estimates intact — restoring an asymmetric entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Material widening of loan-sale spreads or a disclosed step-up in subprime delinquency/loss rates that pressures gain-on-sale dollars.
- Adverse SEC subpoena development or unfavorable securities-litigation ruling.
- Retail unit growth decelerating below ~20% YoY (signaling the demand side is cracking under affordability pressure), or operating cash flow turning negative without a clear inventory-cycle explanation.

## Conclusion

Carvana has earned the score-trajectory improvement reflected across the prior three reports (4.4 → 5.0 → 5.8); the Q1 2026 results confirm rather than reverse that trend. The operating turnaround is genuine: share gains, expanding margins, and a credible path toward 3M units. The reason this stops at HOLD rather than BUY is that the headline numbers obscure a profit engine (gain-on-loan-sale) that is a leveraged exposure to a subprime credit cycle now flashing early warning signs, layered on a premium ~41x multiple, an unresolved SEC inquiry, a $2.2B related-party tax liability, and insider-controlled governance. The options market and elevated short interest corroborate that the near-term risk is real. Hold the operating story; demand either a cheaper entry or evidence that the loan-sale economics survive a tougher credit environment before adding.
