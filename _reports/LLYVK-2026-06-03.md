**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Liberty Live Holdings I (LLYVK) is a single-asset holding vehicle, not an operating company. Its value is driven almost entirely by approximately 69.6 million shares of Live Nation Entertainment (LYV), representing roughly 30% of Live Nation. At LYV's recent ~$152 level, that stake is worth approximately $10.6 billion. The company also consolidates QuintEvents, a premium sports/entertainment hospitality business generating ~$382M of FY2025 revenue but operating at a loss; economically it is a rounding error against the LYV position. Standard distress screens (Altman Z near 1.3, Piotroski 1/5, current ratio 0.33, negative book equity) are false negatives here. They reflect equity-method accounting that carries a $10.6B asset at ~$491M of book value, plus mark-to-market losses on exchangeable debt that grow precisely when LYV stock rises.

The entire investment case reduces to a discount-to-NAV story. Netting the LYV stake (~$10.6B) against principal debt (~$1.15B, carried at fair value ~$1.82B), adding ~$0.5B cash and a modest Quint contribution, yields rough net asset value of $9.0-9.5B against a market capitalization near $6.0B, a discount of roughly 35-40%. The split-off from Liberty Media, completed December 15, 2025, was explicitly designed to narrow Liberty's historically wide tracking-stock discount. Whether that gap closes is the central question, and it is currently held wide by a live, two-sided antitrust catalyst.

On April 15, 2026, a jury found for the litigating states against Live Nation on monopoly claims; Live Nation accrued $450M as a best estimate and, in May 2026, filed post-trial motions to overturn the verdict, with an appeal to the 2nd Circuit expected. The states are pushing for a forced Ticketmaster divestiture. This is the dominant driver of both LYV's valuation and the NAV discount, and it cuts both ways. We rate LLYVK a HOLD. The discount plus a dated re-rating catalyst is enough to keep this on a watch footing rather than recommend selling, but non-voting Series C status, Malone's super-voting control, embedded equity options on the debt, encumbered shares, and an uncapped tax indemnity prevent a higher-conviction call.

## Company Overview

LLYVK is the Series C, non-voting common share class of Liberty Live Holdings, the entity created by Liberty Media's split-off of its Liberty Live tracking-stock group and re-domiciled in Nevada. The structure has two components.

The core asset is the ~30% Live Nation stake. Live Nation is the dominant global integrated live-events company spanning concert promotion, venue operation, and primary ticketing through Ticketmaster. This is what an investor in LLYVK is buying, in leveraged and discounted form.

The operating piece is QuintEvents ("Quint"), a premium hospitality and official-experience provider for events including Formula 1, MotoGP, FIFA, and golf. It carries $127.5M of goodwill, already impaired $67M in 2024. It adds complexity, related-party exposure, and event-calendar sensitivity without materially moving NAV.

The structural advantage is discounted access to a high-quality asset combined with Liberty's structuring and tax-efficient financing capabilities. Management uses LYV shares as collateral to raise cheap, low-coupon (2.375%) structured debt rather than selling shares, preserving the tax basis on a highly appreciated position.

## Financial Analysis

The GAAP financials require careful interpretation because the headline figures invert the underlying economics.

| Metric | FY2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue (Quint) | ~$382M | ~$63.6M |
| Operating loss | ~($52M) | — |
| Net loss | ~($87M) | ~($294M), EPS ~($3.20) |
| Stockholders' equity | — | Negative ~($318M) |
| Cash | — | ~$524M ($426M corp / $99M Quint) |
| Debt (principal) | $1.15B | $1.15B (fair value ~$1.82B) |

Three points explain why the GAAP loss misleads:

1. The LYV stake sits under the equity method at ~$491M book value against ~$10.6B market value. Negative book equity is an accounting artifact, not insolvency.

2. The Q1 2026 net loss was driven by non-cash mark-to-market losses on the exchangeable debentures and forward contracts. These losses occur because LYV stock rose: the embedded equity option in the debt becomes more valuable, increasing the fair-value liability. The headline loss is a symptom of the underlying asset appreciating.

3. The equity-method "share of Live Nation losses" (-$124M in Q1) is also non-cash to LLYVK.

Liability management has been constructive. In March 2026 the company exchanged ~$1,116M of old debentures into new 2.375% notes due 2053, pushing the first put date from 2028 out to September 30, 2032. This reduced near-term refinancing and put risk that would otherwise loom given LYV trades well above the ~$104.91 exchange price, which had granted holders an early exchange/put right in the April-June 2026 window and forced the fair-value debt into the current liabilities bucket (driving the 0.33 current ratio).

Earnings estimate data for this structure is largely noise. The reported swing in consensus current-year EPS from $0.75 to -$3.30 over 30 days reflects analysts marking the non-cash debt revaluation through the income statement, not deteriorating cash economics. With a single covering analyst, the estimate revision series carries little signal here and should not be read as a fundamental deterioration warning of the kind it would represent at an operating company.

## Growth Analysis

LLYVK has no organic earnings growth profile of its own in the conventional sense. NAV growth depends on two variables: appreciation in Live Nation shares and narrowing of the holding-company discount.

On the first, Live Nation enjoys structural post-pandemic demand for live events, pricing power, and a deep moat in artist relationships, venue networks, and ticketing scale. The antitrust verdict is the swing factor: structural separation of Ticketmaster would impair LYV's earnings power and could compress its multiple, while a successful appeal or favorable remedy could re-rate the stock higher.

On the second, the discount is the growth lever specific to LLYVK. If it narrows toward the 15-25% range typical of cleaner single-asset holding companies, that alone delivers meaningful per-share appreciation independent of LYV's price. The headwinds against closure are the non-voting share class, Malone control, embedded leverage, and the uncapped tax indemnity, each of which justifies some structural discount and limits how far it can compress.

Quint adds modest, event-dependent revenue. The 2026 motorsport calendar took a direct hit: cancellation of the Bahrain and Saudi F1 Grands Prix and postponement of the Qatar MotoGP due to Middle East geopolitical disruption are adverse to Quint's 2026 results. This is immaterial to overall NAV but reinforces that the operating business is not a growth engine.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation case is the discount to NAV, which is specific and quantifiable rather than a vague "cheap" assertion. The market is pricing the LYV stake at roughly 60-65 cents on the dollar after accounting for net debt and the Quint contribution.

Three factors warrant a discount: non-voting Series C status, the embedded leverage with mark-to-market option liabilities, and the uncapped split-off tax indemnity. The relevant question is whether 35-40% over-compensates for these. Comparable single-asset Liberty and Malone-affiliated vehicles have historically traded at discounts in the 15-30% range; the current gap sits at the wide end of that history, consistent with elevated antitrust uncertainty layered onto the standard structural discount.

The single analyst covering the name carries a $115 price target against a recent price near $98, implying roughly 17% upside, broadly consistent with partial discount closure plus modest LYV appreciation. We treat this as one data point, not a robust consensus, given the coverage of one.

A note on the trailing/forward P/E figures (around -25.6x): they are meaningless for this structure because earnings are dominated by non-cash debt revaluation and equity-method accounting. Valuation here must be done on sum-of-the-parts NAV, not earnings multiples. On that basis the stock is modestly cheap, with the depth of the discount appropriately tied to the binary antitrust path. We score valuation a 6.5: attractive on the NAV gap, but not deeply asymmetric given the legitimate structural reasons for a discount and the two-sided litigation risk.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive analysis runs through Live Nation. LYV's moat (artist relationships, the largest global venue network, and Ticketmaster's primary-ticketing scale) is precisely what triggered the antitrust action. The same vertical integration that produces the moat is the target of the structural remedy the states seek.

Live Nation's industry position is improving on the demand side: live-event spend, pricing, and sponsorship remain strong, and the company retains share leadership across promotion and ticketing. The competitive trajectory is therefore favorable operationally but clouded by the regulatory threat to its structure. A forced Ticketmaster divestiture would weaken the integrated model and the cross-segment economics that underpin the moat.

For LLYVK specifically, its "competitive position" is its access to this asset at a discount and Liberty's financing apparatus. That is a structural feature rather than an operating advantage, and it is replicable in form by any holding-company structure. We score competitive position 5.5, reflecting a strong but regulatorily challenged underlying asset accessed through a structure with no operational moat of its own.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by concentration and a live legal catalyst, which is why this dimension scores low (4.0).

**Single-asset concentration.** Over 90% of value is one stock. A 10% LYV move shifts NAV by roughly $1.06B. There is no diversification.

**Antitrust litigation — the dominant overhang.** The DOJ and 39 states/DC sued Live Nation/Ticketmaster in May 2024 seeking divestiture. Live Nation settled with the US DOJ in March 2026 (term sheet ~March 9: injunctive/structural relief plus a $280M settlement fund, Tunney Act approval pending) and six states settled (~$18.6M). The remaining monopoly verdict is genuine two-way risk: a structural remedy or large penalties impair NAV, while a reversal on appeal could re-rate LYV and narrow the discount.

**Leverage with embedded equity options.** The exchangeable debentures carry an embedded option that drives the volatile mark-to-market losses. The March 2026 exchange pushed the first put to 2032, materially reducing near-term refinancing risk, which is a constructive offset.

**Encumbered shares / forced-sale risk.** Roughly 19.5M+ LYV shares are pledged across the 2025 forward contracts (~10.5M shares, ~$1.6B collateral, deliverable/cash-settle by ~Q1 2027) and the undrawn $400M margin loan (9.0M shares pledged). A sharp LYV decline could trigger collateral pressure.

**Uncapped tax indemnity.** LLYVK indemnifies Liberty Media if the split-off is later deemed taxable (e.g., a Section 355(e) >50% ownership-change event). This uncapped contingent liability constrains corporate actions and M&A.

**Quint-specific.** 2026 F1/MotoGP cancellations and postponements hurt results; related-party concentration (F1/Delta Topco and MotoGP are Liberty/Malone-affiliated); ~$25.6M VAT/sales-tax exposure.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 38.8% against 30.5% one-year historical volatility (rich by 8.3 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (38.8% at 1-month vs 32.1% at 6-month), signaling the market expects a discrete event within the next several weeks, consistent with the active post-trial motion timeline and any antitrust developments that move LYV. The put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-4.7%), so positioning is not aggressively bearish. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.2%. This reads as event hedging around the litigation calendar rather than systemic distress.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The split-off does its job and the NAV discount narrows toward 15-25%, the historical norm for cleaner single-asset Liberty vehicles. If Live Nation prevails on appeal or settles into a remedy materially lighter than a forced Ticketmaster divestiture, LYV re-rates and the discount compresses simultaneously, a double tailwind. The March 2026 debenture exchange removed near-term refinancing risk; cash provides flexibility. On NAV of ~$9.0-9.5B, full discount closure toward 20% would imply equity value around $7.2-7.6B, roughly $113-119 per share, broadly aligned with the lone analyst's $115 target. Continued LYV appreciation layers on top.

### Bear Case

The litigating states win a structural remedy: Ticketmaster is divested, impairing LYV's integrated economics and compressing its multiple. A 20-25% decline in LYV would cut NAV by ~$2-2.6B and could trigger collateral pressure on the encumbered shares. The discount stays wide or widens as antitrust uncertainty persists for years through the appellate process. The uncapped tax indemnity crystallizes, or non-voting holders see value extracted toward Malone-aligned control. In this scenario a combination of lower NAV and a persistent or widening discount could take the equity toward $72-78, near the 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale tied to a specific catalyst, not an income or default-neutral hold.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Live Nation's post-trial motions succeed or the 2nd Circuit reverses the monopoly verdict, removing the divestiture threat, the single largest swing factor for both LYV and the discount.
- The holding-company discount narrows demonstrably (e.g., to <25% of NAV) on observable progress in Liberty's stated discount-closure strategy, such as buybacks at sensible NAV discounts or a value-realizing transaction.
- Evidence the remedy is contained to monetary penalties (the accrued $450M order of magnitude) rather than structural separation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A court order mandating Ticketmaster divestiture is upheld, structurally impairing LYV.
- A sharp LYV decline triggers margin/collateral calls on encumbered shares, forcing share sales at depressed prices.
- The split-off tax indemnity is triggered, creating a material uncapped liability.
- The NAV discount widens persistently beyond 45% with no management action, signaling the structure is failing to deliver its intended value realization.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next at the resolution of Live Nation's post-trial motions and any 2nd Circuit appeal schedule, and at the next quarterly disclosure of NAV components and litigation status. The options market's backwardation suggests the near-term event window is within weeks.

## Conclusion

LLYVK is a leveraged, discounted claim on Live Nation, wrapped in a holding-company structure with real but identifiable frictions. The discount to NAV is genuine and quantifiable, and the path to closure is anchored by a dated, two-sided antitrust catalyst rather than vague optionality. The constructive March 2026 liability management and substantial cash position reduce near-term financial risk, and the headline GAAP losses are non-cash artifacts that misread as distress.

We stop short of a BUY because the structure embeds legitimate reasons for a discount (non-voting Series C status, Malone's super-voting control, embedded equity options on the debt, encumbered shares, and an uncapped tax indemnity) and because the dominant catalyst is genuinely binary: a forced Ticketmaster divestiture would impair the underlying asset that constitutes 90%+ of value. Berkshire trimming its stake in January 2026 is a sentiment data point that cuts against unreserved bullishness. The discount plus a live re-rating catalyst is sufficient to hold and monitor closely, with clearly defined triggers for moving in either direction. Recommendation: HOLD.
