**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sanmina is a top-tier global electronics manufacturing services (EMS) company that has been remade over the past nine months by a single transaction. The October 2025 acquisition of ZT Systems' data-center manufacturing business — bought from AMD for $1.62B plus up to $450M in earnouts — moved Sanmina from a diversified, margin-stable contract manufacturer into a primary builder of AI and hyperscale compute infrastructure. Q2 FY2026 (ended 3/28/26) revenue was $4.01B, up 102% YoY, with ZT contributing $1.9B (47% of the total). The Communications Networks & Cloud/AI Infrastructure segment grew 278% YoY to $2.77B, now roughly 69% of revenue. The stock has appreciated 117% over the trailing twelve months to $218.56.

The transformation is real and executing ahead of plan. The clearest tell is that the ZT earnout has been marked upward from $111M at close to $170M by March 2026, implying ZT is tracking above its revenue and gross-profit thresholds. IMS segment gross margin rose to 8.5% from 7.7% with ZT included, meaning the acquisition is accretive to the core segment's margin — unusual for a large EMS deal and the likely driver of the bullish re-rating. Four consecutive earnings beats, capped by a 29.2% surprise in the March quarter, and +10% current-year estimate revisions over 90 days corroborate the operating momentum.

The offsetting concerns hold this at a HOLD rather than a BUY. Customer concentration jumped materially: two customers now exceed 10% of revenue (was one) and top-10 concentration rose to ~70% (from ~50%). ZT's economics are levered to AMD's success in AI, a market where AMD is a distant second to Nvidia. Sanmina funded the deal with $2.2B of fresh variable-rate debt against a 4.0x net-leverage covenant, and is simultaneously running a $600M buyback that competes with deleveraging. Insider activity is uniformly sales into strength, including a ~$27M CEO sale. At ~19–20x forward EPS after a doubling, the multiple is no longer cheap enough to offer asymmetric upside, and the near-term options market is pricing a ±21% move with IV in backwardation. The growth thesis is credible; the risk/reward has flattened.

## Company Overview

Sanmina provides end-to-end EMS to OEMs, deliberately targeting mission-critical, highly-regulated, higher-complexity products rather than commodity high-volume assembly. It operates ~39,000 employees across 20 countries and historically generated ~80% of sales offshore. The company reports two businesses:

- **IMS (Integrated Manufacturing Solutions):** PCB assembly/test, high-level assembly, direct-order fulfillment. Historically ~80% of revenue; now ~90% post-ZT.
- **CPS (Components, Products & Services):** higher-margin vertically-integrated components (advanced PCBs, backplanes, cable/metal/machined parts) plus proprietary product lines: Viking Technology (memory/SSD/CXL), Viking Enterprise Solutions (hyperscale storage), SCI (defense/aerospace), Advanced Microsystems (optical, RF), and 42Q (cloud MES software).

The company competes with Celestica, Flex, Jabil, Foxconn, Benchmark, and Plexus. Its historical differentiation was vertical integration and a focus on complex, regulated end markets. The ZT deal repositions it as a major AI/hyperscale infrastructure manufacturer, directly in the path of data-center capex, and re-shored a large share of production: Americas revenue is now 71% U.S.-based, up from 30%, reducing tariff exposure and aligning with onshoring demand.

CEO Jure Sola (74), co-founder, remains Chairman and CEO; CFO Jonathan Faust (ex-HP/HPE) joined in December 2023.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended 9/27/25), pre-ZT:** Revenue $8.13B (+7.4% YoY, recovering from a $7.57B FY2024 trough and $8.94B in FY2023). Gross margin 8.8%, operating margin 4.4%, net income $245.9M. These are thin but stable EMS margins. Operating cash flow was strong at $620.7M, aided by customer inventory advances. The pre-deal balance sheet was pristine: cash $926M, LT debt $283M, Altman Z of 4.01 (safe zone), current ratio 1.72. Retained earnings remain negative (–$2.46B), a legacy of large historical write-offs rather than current operations.

**Q2 FY2026 (ended 3/28/26) — first full quarter with ZT:**
- Six-month revenue $7.20B, +80.5%.
- Legacy Industrial/Medical/Defense/Auto roughly flat at ~$1.24B.
- Gross margin held at 8.8%; diluted EPS $1.70 (vs $1.16).
- One-time drag was heavy: $116M of acquisition/integration charges over six months (including a $59M increase in contingent-consideration fair value and $45M professional fees), plus $49M inventory step-up amortization. Interest expense rose to $32M/quarter from $5M.

**Balance sheet post-deal:** Total assets nearly doubled to $9.67B (from $5.86B). Inventories rose to $3.03B (from $2.0B), cash to $1.58B, and LT debt to ~$2.0B (from $283M). The cash conversion cycle improved to 51 days. Operating cash flow was $577M for six months. Pro forma FY2025 combined revenue would have been ~$10.3B.

The financing structure warrants attention: a new $3.5B credit facility ($2.0B Term Loan A, $800M Term Loan B, $1.5B revolver), with ~$2.2B drawn. Variable-rate exposure is partly hedged via $1.45B notional interest-rate swaps at an ~5.1% effective rate. Covenants are max net leverage 4.0x and min interest coverage 3.0x, both in compliance. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, the variable-rate portion is a live cost, and the swap only partially caps it.

Financial Health has declined from 6.8 to 5.8 across prior reports. This is not a fundamental deterioration in operating performance — cash generation remains robust and the deal is accretive — but a rational repricing of a balance sheet that went from net-cash-like to ~$2.0B of leverage in a single quarter. The 5.8 reflects strong cash conversion offset by the covenant constraint and the buyback-while-levering tension.

## Growth Analysis

The growth acceleration is confirmed across multiple data points, not a single-quarter artifact:
- Revenue doubled YoY, with a ~$16B run-rate (Q2 annualized) versus an $8.1B pre-deal base.
- Four consecutive earnings beats: +5.9%, +4.3%, +9.5%, and +29.2% (March quarter). The acceleration in the beat magnitude is the key signal.
- Estimate revisions are positive but decelerating in velocity: current-year EPS revised +10.2% over 90 days (to $11.20) but flat over the last 30 days. Next-year EPS is $13.02, up 7.1% over 90 days but also flat over 30 days.
- The earnout being marked up ($111M → $170M) is an independent, cash-backed confirmation that ZT is exceeding its revenue/gross-profit thresholds.

The 90-day revision trend of +10% sits below the +30% threshold that would flag a decisive-acceleration BUY signal, and the 30-day figures show revisions have gone flat. The momentum is real, but consensus has largely caught up to the near-term trajectory rather than chasing it upward. Growth durability now rests on whether AI/hyperscale capex remains elevated and, more specifically, whether AMD's AI silicon gains share. The broader sector picture supports the demand side — data-center load and AI-infrastructure spend are confirmed across hyperscaler capex, power, and equipment supply chains — but Sanmina's specific exposure is concentrated in AMD's AI positioning, the narrower and riskier thread.

Growth Prospects earns a 7.2: strong confirmed acceleration with catalyst support, tempered by decelerating revision velocity and single-customer dependence on the durability of the trend.

## Valuation Assessment

At $218.56, Sanmina trades at ~46x trailing GAAP EPS ($4.46), but trailing GAAP is distorted by the six-month acquisition charges and inventory step-up amortization, so it is not the relevant metric. On forward consensus, the picture is more reasonable: current-year EPS of $11.20 implies ~19.5x, and next-year EPS of $13.02 implies ~16.8x. Market cap is ~$11.4B (on ~54M shares), and enterprise value is higher given the net incremental debt.

Against EMS peers (Celestica, Flex, Jabil), a high-teens-to-low-20s forward multiple is a modest premium justified by faster growth and the AI-infrastructure re-rating. The PEG is favorable if next-year growth holds: ~17x forward on ~16% growth implies a PEG near 1.0. Consensus targets provide context: low $185, high $280, mean $240, median $247.5. The current price sits below the mean and median, which on a genuine-acceleration stock would argue that targets are lagging.

Two considerations pull against treating the targets as floors. First, valuation as a dimension has been a reliable value-trap warning in recent cycles: a cheap-looking forward multiple is not, by itself, a buy signal, and requires an independent catalyst. Sanmina has that catalyst in ZT, but it is already partly reflected in the 117% run. Second, the near-term options market is pricing a ±21% implied move with ATM IV at 95.4% (vs 70.2% historical vol) and term structure in backwardation; the market is pricing meaningful binary risk into the next earnings print, which caps the case for aggressively adding here.

Net, the forward multiple is fair-to-modestly-attractive rather than a clear mispricing. The stock has re-rated from ~$100 to ~$218 over twelve months on real fundamental improvement; that appreciation is not a reason to call it expensive, but the current price no longer embeds the asymmetric upside that existed at ~$124 in March 2026. Valuation scores 5.7: reasonable on forward earnings, not a standout bargain, with genuine near-term event risk priced in.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive trajectory is improving. The ZT deal gave Sanmina an AMD-anchored NPI (new-product-introduction) manufacturing partnership for AI rack/cluster-scale systems, a differentiated position that peers chasing AI/cloud manufacturing largely lack. The accretion to IMS margin demonstrates that this is higher-value work than Sanmina's legacy mix, and the re-shoring to 71% U.S.-based Americas revenue aligns with hyperscaler onshoring preferences and reduces tariff friction.

The trade-off is structural. In exchange for growth and margin accretion, Sanmina took on customer concentration and cyclicality it historically avoided. EMS is a thin-margin, low-switching-cost business with no firm long-term volume commitments; the moat is execution, scale, and integration rather than pricing power. Sanmina's ability to defend the AMD relationship and win adjacent hyperscaler work will determine whether the improved position is durable or a single-customer bet. The competitive position is stronger than a year ago, but its durability is unproven through a full AI-capex cycle. Competitive Position scores 6.4.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile has deteriorated meaningfully with the transformation, and this is the dimension most responsible for the HOLD. Prior reports have already marked Risk Factors down from 5.8 to 4.5, and the current data supports keeping it there:

- **AMD/ZT single-thread dependence (central, new):** The 10-Q explicitly states success depends in part on AMD's products achieving acceptance in the AI marketplace. AMD is the distant #2 to Nvidia in AI accelerators, so this is a leveraged bet on a challenger's share gains, layered on top of already-cyclical hyperscaler capex.
- **Leverage and rate risk:** The new variable-rate debt (partly swapped to ~5.1%) sits against the 4.0x net-leverage covenant and 3.0x interest-coverage requirement. Compliant now, but an AI-demand or margin shock combined with the buyback could pressure the covenants.
- **Growing earnout:** Up to $450M contingent consideration, already re-marked to $170M — a positive operating tell but a growing cash and P&L liability.
- **Integration and goodwill:** $328M new goodwill; ZT was excluded from the FY internal-control assessment (28% of assets, ~47% of Q2 revenue). Integration execution is unproven, and goodwill impairment is a tail risk if AI demand softens.
- **IRS dispute:** An IRS RAR proposes disallowing a $503M worthless-stock deduction (FY2009), which could reduce NOLs and create material back-taxes/interest for FY2021+. Unresolved and potentially material to the deferred-tax position.
- **SCI qui tam (Eckert):** A False Claims Act suit alleging ~$100M in fraud on government contracts survived a partial motion to dismiss (Sept 2025); DOJ declined to intervene. Loss deemed "not currently probable or estimable" — an un-accrued binary.
- **Key-person risk:** CEO Sola is 74 with no publicly named successor.

These are specific, largely un-priced idiosyncratic risks — the AMD concentration and the two legal/tax overhangs in particular. That combination justifies a Risk Factors score of 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 95.4% versus 70.2% historical vol (rich by 25 points), IV sits at the 84th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (95.4% near-term vs 81.3% at six months); the market is pricing imminent stress, most likely the next earnings print, with an implied ±21.2% move. Put/call skew is slightly call-heavy (–3.6%) and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.22 to 0.50 over the past three weeks as the price fell 3%, indicating some hedging demand building. This does not contradict the fundamental thesis, but it confirms that the market views the next catalyst as genuinely two-sided, reinforcing the case for patience over aggressive entry. Short interest is low at 3.3% of float (0.6 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$280):** ZT continues to outperform, the earnout keeps marking up, AMD gains AI-accelerator share, and hyperscaler capex stays elevated through FY2027. IMS margin continues to benefit from the higher-value mix, EPS approaches or exceeds the $13.02 next-year consensus with upside beats, and the multiple holds at ~20x on rising estimates. The buyback shrinks the share count into a rising earnings base. This maps to the analyst high target of $280 (~28% upside).

**Base Case (~$245):** ZT integration proceeds without major surprises, growth decelerates from the doubling pace to a healthy but more normal cadence, and EPS lands near consensus. The multiple stays in the high-teens. This aligns with the analyst mean/median of $240–$247.5 (~10–13% upside).

**Bear Case (~$150–160):** AMD fails to gain durable AI share, hyperscaler capex softens, or a demand air-pocket hits the concentrated customer base. Thin EMS margins amplify any volume decline, the variable-rate debt and buyback pressure the balance sheet, and one of the tail risks (IRS disallowance, SCI qui tam) crystallizes. The multiple compresses toward peer levels on decelerating growth. This implies ~27–31% downside from current levels.

The distribution is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-favorable on a probability-weighted basis, but not asymmetric enough to justify a BUY after the run. The single most important swing factor is AMD's AI-market trajectory, which sits outside Sanmina's control.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth HOLD** with a 12-month reassessment horizon anchored to the FY2026 print cadence.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions re-accelerate above +30% over 90 days, or the earnout marks up again materially, confirming ZT is still exceeding thresholds — combined with a pullback toward the low-to-mid $180s (near the analyst low target) that restores asymmetric upside.
- Evidence of customer diversification within the AI-infrastructure segment (a second hyperscaler or a non-AMD AI win that reduces the single-thread dependence).
- Net leverage declining below ~2.5x while maintaining the buyback, demonstrating the balance sheet can absorb both.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- AMD AI-share disappointment or a hyperscaler capex-guidance cut that threatens ZT volumes, visible in a Comms/Cloud segment sequential decline.
- Covenant pressure (net leverage approaching 4.0x) or a forced buyback pause.
- Crystallization of a tail risk: an adverse IRS resolution impairing NOLs/DTAs, or the SCI qui tam moving to a probable/estimable loss.
- Two consecutive quarters of falling estimate revisions alongside margin compression.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q3 FY2026, expected late July/early August 2026) is the immediate checkpoint; the options market is pricing a ±21% move around it. FY2026 is a 53-week year, which will flatter Q4 optics; adjust for the extra week when evaluating growth.

## Conclusion

Sanmina executed a transformative acquisition that is paying off faster than the market expected, doubling revenue, improving core-segment margins, and repositioning the company as a major AI/hyperscale infrastructure manufacturer with an AMD-anchored NPI partnership. Four consecutive beats, rising forward estimates, and an upward-marked earnout all corroborate real operating momentum. The forward multiple of ~17–20x is reasonable for the growth delivered.

The reasons this is a HOLD rather than a BUY are the risk side of the ledger and the flattened risk/reward after the run. Customer concentration has jumped to a leveraged bet on AMD's AI-share gains; the variable-rate debt sits against the covenant while the company runs a buyback; insiders are uniformly selling into strength; and two un-accrued tail risks (the IRS dispute and the FCA suit) remain open. The near-term options market signals that the next catalyst is genuinely two-sided. The scores have drifted down (6.3 → 6.3 → 6.0) primarily on risk and balance-sheet repricing, not on operating deterioration, and this report confirms that pattern rather than reversing it.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth thesis is credible and worth staying with, but I would add on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $180s or on evidence of customer diversification, rather than chasing at current levels ahead of a binary earnings print.
