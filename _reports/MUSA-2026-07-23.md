**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Murphy USA is a low-price, high-volume fuel-and-convenience retailer operating 1,803 stores across 27 states, the majority sited on prime locations adjacent to Walmart Supercenters. The business is differentiated by a fee-simple-heavy real-estate base (~73% company-owned land), integrated fuel sourcing (pipeline shipper status, terminal access, RIN-generation capability), and a small-format, low-labor cost structure that lets it undercut competitors on price while sustaining a stable ~$1.0B adjusted EBITDA. Capital allocation is the defining feature: management has retired roughly 21% of shares outstanding in four years, financed by steady cash flow and modest incremental debt, and just authorized a fresh $2.0B buyback through 2030.

The problem for a new-money buyer is price. At ~$623, the stock trades at roughly 21x trailing and forward EPS, near a 52-week high, after a 42% one-year advance. That multiple carries a substantial premium to the auto-retail peer group (median P/E ~13x), and while MUSA's asset quality and buyback intensity justify some premium, the earnings base is flattered by a Q1 2026 beat that was disproportionately driven by a fuel-supply and RIN timing windfall (9.6 cpg vs 1.7 cpg a year earlier) that management explicitly says reverses through COGS over time. Strip out the non-recurring component and the durable retail fuel margin rose only modestly. Retail fuel volume per store has declined every year since 2021 (244.6K → 235.8K gal/store-month), and same-store fuel gallons fell 2.6% in 2025 — the EV/efficiency headwind showing up at the unit level, masked by network expansion.

The prior three reports all landed on HOLD, with scores oscillating (5.1 → 6.7 → 5.6) as the market repriced the stock and as the balance-sheet optics were better understood. We maintain HOLD. The business is high-quality and the balance sheet is far healthier than the reported D/E of 6.58 and ROE of 75.5% suggest (both artifacts of a deliberately shrunken equity base; true leverage is ~1.9x EBITDA). But the valuation is full, the recent earnings strength is partly borrowed from timing gains, and there is no identified catalyst to re-rate the multiple higher from here. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$400s/low-$500s.

## Company Overview

Murphy USA was spun off from Murphy Oil in 2013 and acquired QuickChek in 2021. The store base comprises 1,655 Murphy USA/Murphy Express kiosks (1,400–2,800 sq ft) and 148 larger-format QuickChek stores (5,000–7,000 sq ft) in NJ/NY. The company sold approximately 4.8 billion gallons of fuel in 2025 and reports a single "Marketing" segment combining retail and product supply/wholesale.

The competitive moat rests on three pillars. First, Walmart adjacency: the majority of Murphy-branded stores sit near Walmart Supercenters, drawing shared value-conscious traffic, and the chain participates in Walmart+ fuel discounts. Second, fuel supply-chain scale: shipper status on major pipelines, access to numerous terminals, and RIN-generation capability from ethanol blending create a low-cost sourcing advantage management cites as difficult to replicate. Third, cost leadership: small footprints, one-to-two associates per store, and a store-level coverage ratio (merchandise contribution ÷ operating cost) embedded in incentive compensation.

The most material recent corporate event is CEO succession. R. Andrew Clyde, CEO since the 2013 spin, announced his intent to retire at the end of 2025, which explains the cluster of Item 5.02 leadership-change 8-Ks between October 2025 and April 2026 and the $12.6M FY2025 restructuring/severance charge. The transition is described as orderly and planned, with a functioning executive team (CFO Donald R. Smith Jr. among them) signing the Q1 2026 10-Q. The named successor has not been independently confirmed from the available sources.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue optics vs. economics.** Revenue declined from $23.4B (2022) to $19.4B (2025), but this tracks fuel prices, not demand — the FY2025 4.2% decline was driven almost entirely by a 7.5% drop in average retail fuel price (crude ~$65/bbl vs ~$77 in 2024) while fuel volumes rose 0.6%. Roughly $2.4B of "revenue" is pass-through excise tax. Profitability is margin-driven: net income fell from $672.9M (2022) to $470.6M (2025), but adjusted EBITDA has been stable at $1,058M / $1,007M / $1,019M across 2023–25.

**Fuel economics.** Total fuel contribution was 30.7 cpg in 2025 vs 30.5 in 2024, with retail fuel margin steady at 28.1 cpg. The structural concern is per-store retail fuel volume: 244.6K gal/store-month (2021) → 240.6 (2024) → 235.8 (2025), with SSS fuel gallons down 2.6% in 2025. Chain volume grows only because store count grows.

**Merchandise.** Merchandise contribution rose to $869M (2025) from $833.7M, with unit margin improving to 20.2%, but same-store merchandise sales were roughly flat (-0.3%). Nicotine is ~64% of merchandise SSS revenue — high-margin but structurally declining and regulatory-exposed.

**Q1 2026 — a strong but partly non-recurring beat.** Net income was $136.3M ($7.28 diluted EPS) vs $53.2M ($2.63) a year earlier, up 156%. The driver was total fuel contribution of 35.0 cpg vs 25.4 cpg, of which fuel supply + RIN contribution surged to 9.6 cpg from 1.7 cpg — largely timing/market-driven PS&W and RIN gains (RINs averaged $1.31 vs $0.74). Retail fuel margin, the durable component, rose more modestly to 25.4 cpg from 23.7. Management explicitly cautions that RIN revenue is offset by COGS over time and that the business does not depend on RINs. Operating cash flow jumped to $320M from $128.5M, aided by a $95M working-capital swing. The 41.5% EPS beat that quarter should be read against this backdrop rather than extrapolated.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** The headline ratios — D/E of 6.58 and ROE of 75.5% — are self-inflicted by buybacks, not signs of distress. Share count fell from 23.5M (2022) to ~18.5M weighted in Q1 2026, roughly 21% retired in four years; $652M was repurchased in 2025 (avg ~$424/share), and $4.1B since the 2013 spin. The equity base is now an accounting residual ($623.5M) after $4.1B of treasury stock. Actual leverage is conservative: total leverage ratio was 2.11x at YE2025 and 1.87x at Q1 2026, well inside the 5.0x covenant and below the 3.0x threshold that would restrict dividends/buybacks. Altman Z of 4.83 confirms low bankruptcy risk. Debt is well-laddered: $1.3B senior notes (2027/2029/2031 at 5.625%/4.75%/3.75%), a $600M term loan (2032, ~5.4%), and revolver draws, with April 2025 refinancing extending maturities. Notes are below-investment-grade; credit facilities are IG. The current ratio of 0.8 is normal for a fast-inventory-turn model, not a liquidity concern. The dividend has grown double-digits ($0.48/qtr Q4 2024 → $0.64 declared May 2026) but is small relative to buybacks (~$41.5M vs $652M in 2025). 2026 capex is guided to $475–525M (up from $432M), with $375–400M for growth (45–55 new stores + up to 30 raze-and-rebuilds) and FCF of ~$374M in FY2025.

The financial-health picture is strong once the buyback-distorted optics are set aside: stable EBITDA, conservative real leverage, and disciplined capital return. This is the dimension where MUSA scores best.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is a mix of durable unit expansion and a structurally decaying core. On the positive side, MUSA is adding 45–55 new stores plus up to 30 raze-and-rebuilds annually, pushing into larger formats and QuickChek food-and-beverage, and is transitioning the Murphy Express brand to Murphy USA. Merchandise unit margins are expanding, and per-share EPS growth is amplified by the continuous share-shrink.

Estimate revisions have been meaningfully positive: current-year consensus EPS moved from $26.32 (90 days ago) to $33.07 (+25.6%), and next-year from $27.01 to $29.76 (+10.2%). Under normal circumstances a +25% 90-day current-year revision would be a strong buy signal. Here it warrants heavy discounting because much of the current-year revision reflects the Q1 2026 windfall being folded into full-year estimates, and next-year consensus of $29.76 actually sits below the current-year figure — the Street is implicitly modeling normalization back down.

The concerning trend is per-store fuel volume declining every year since 2021 alongside flat same-store merchandise sales. Chain-level growth depends on continued store additions to outrun the per-unit erosion. That is a functioning growth model, but growth prospects are modest and heavily dependent on execution of the build-out and on fuel-margin stability that is inherently volatile.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$623, MUSA trades at roughly 21x trailing and forward EPS. Against the automotive-retail peer group, that is a steep premium: Penske 13.1x, Lithia 10.8x, AutoNation 10.6x, with a peer median around 13x. CarMax (30.7x) and Valvoline (50.5x) sit higher but are structurally different businesses. MUSA's premium is partly defensible — higher-quality fee-simple real estate, more stable margins, and aggressive per-share compounding via buybacks — but 21x is close to the upper end of where this stock has historically traded, and it is being applied to an EPS base flattered by non-recurring fuel-supply/RIN gains.

This is not a cyclical trough where trailing P/E understates normalized earnings; if anything, the trailing and current-year figures are near a cyclical high on the fuel-supply/RIN component. On normalized earnings power closer to the $26–30 range, the stock trades at roughly 21–24x — full for a business with flat-to-declining structural fuel demand.

Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly: low $517, mean $620.7, median $625, high $710. With the stock at ~$623, it sits essentially at the mean/median target. On an inflection stock trading cheaply relative to rising estimates, consensus targets would be treated as stale floors. But MUSA is not cheap relative to its growth (PEG well above 1.5 on normalized numbers), and the revision velocity is contaminated by timing gains, so the guardrail applies: proximity to consensus here signals a fully-valued stock. The valuation dimension is where our caution is greatest — a rich absolute multiple on peak-ish earnings with no identified re-rating catalyst. Our own scoring history reinforces this: "cheap" screens have been anti-predictive, and the inverse also holds — a full multiple on non-recurring-flattered earnings is a poor entry point regardless of business quality.

## Competitive Landscape

MUSA operates in a fragmented, intensely price-competitive market, and its scale, Walmart adjacency, and supply-chain integration give it a durable low-cost edge over independents. The competitive threats are real and structural. Non-traditional fuel retailers (Costco, Sam's Club, Kroger) use fuel as a loss-leader for in-store traffic, directly overlapping MUSA's value proposition. Larger-format c-store chains (Casey's, Wawa, QuikTrip, RaceTrac, Buc-ee's) compete on food and beverage, which is why MUSA is investing in QuickChek F&B and larger 2,800 sq ft formats. MUSA does not refine, so it lacks the integrated hedge majors have in depressed-margin environments, though multi-supplier sourcing and RIN capture partially offset this.

The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving on the merchandise-mix and larger-format initiatives, but the per-store fuel-volume decline suggests the core traffic advantage is eroding at the margin, and the industry backdrop (flat-to-declining gasoline demand from efficiency and EV adoption) is a persistent headwind. A single-supplier merchandise concentration (over 78% from Core-Mark, contract renewed through 2031) is a strength for cost but a concentration risk. Trajectory is roughly stable rather than clearly improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Walmart contractual overhang.** Beyond traffic reliance, the Easements/Covenants/Restrictions and master lease give Walmart a right of first refusal on property sales, a prohibition on selling to Walmart "competitors" (grocers, clubs, discount/drug stores), and an option to buy stores at fair market value if MUSA undergoes a change of control. Combined with a classified board and other anti-takeover armor, this materially reduces the probability and value of any acquisition, capping terminal/strategic value that pure-financial screens miss.

**Secular fuel-demand decline.** Falling per-store gallons are the EV/efficiency thesis materializing at the unit level. 2025 federal rollbacks of GHG/CAFE standards provide a near-term demand tailwind, but the long-term direction is negative.

**Nicotine regulatory exposure.** ~64% of merchandise SSS revenue is vulnerable to taxes, flavor bans, and rebate changes.

**Fuel-margin and RIN volatility.** Earnings can swing meaningfully quarter to quarter, as Q1 2026 demonstrated, making the business hard to model and creating extrapolation risk when a windfall quarter feeds full-year estimates.

**CEO transition.** A founder-era CEO departing after 12 years amid a restructuring is a genuine continuity risk, though signaled as orderly.

**Leverage/rating.** Below-IG notes; a downgrade would raise borrowing costs, and continued debt-funded buybacks add sensitivity, though real leverage of ~1.9x is comfortable.

Most of these risks are known and partly priced, but the combination of peak-ish margins, extrapolation risk in estimates, and a leadership transition against a full valuation keeps the risk score in the middle rather than high.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning suggests near-term stress is being priced. Near-term ATM IV of 43.5% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 39.9% (rich by ~3.6pp), at the 73rd percentile within the current chain, and IV has risen 6.9pp since mid-June. The term structure is in backwardation (43.5% near-term vs 36.7% at six months), with a ±10% implied move on the nearest expiry — the market is pricing an imminent catalyst, most likely the Q2 earnings print. Put/call skew is balanced (-2.4%) and the put/call OI ratio has fallen to 0.22, so there is no pronounced bearish tilt in positioning. The options market expects a large move around the next report but is not directionally bearish. For a HOLD, elevated near-term IV argues against chasing at current levels and favors waiting for the event-driven vol crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** MUSA is a proven cash-compounding business: stable ~$1.0B EBITDA, disciplined capital return, and a per-share shrink that turns flat-to-modest chain growth into double-digit EPS growth. The Walmart-adjacent, fee-simple real estate is a durable low-cost moat, merchandise mix and larger formats are diversifying away from fuel, and the fresh $2.0B buyback provides multi-year support under the shares. If fuel margins stay elevated and the store build-out executes, normalized EPS grinds toward $30+ and the stock can reach the high-$600s/low-$700s. Bull target ~$710 (top of analyst range, ~24x on ~$30 normalized EPS).

**Bear case.** The Q1 2026 windfall reverses, per-store fuel volumes keep declining, and the market re-rates a peak-margin, EV-exposed retailer back toward the low end of its history. On ~$26–28 normalized EPS at 17–18x, the stock trades to roughly $460–500. The CEO transition introduces execution risk, and the Walmart change-of-control option caps any strategic-premium optionality. Bear target ~$460 (roughly -26% from current).

The setup is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable: a high-quality business at a full price on flattered earnings, with modest upside to a stretched bull case and meaningful downside if margins normalize.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (rationale: uncertain — a high-quality business whose valuation is full and whose recent earnings strength is partly non-recurring, leaving no confident directional edge at the current price).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$400s/low-$500s that restores asymmetry (e.g., <18x normalized EPS).
- Evidence that retail fuel margin (the durable component, not fuel-supply/RIN) is structurally re-rating higher, and per-store fuel volumes stabilize (SSS fuel gallons flat-to-positive for two consecutive quarters).
- Confirmation of an orderly, credible CEO succession with continuity in strategy and capital allocation.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut or two consecutive quarters where retail fuel margin compresses meaningfully while per-store volumes accelerate their decline.
- Leverage rising above ~2.5x EBITDA to fund buybacks at these prices, signaling capital indiscipline.
- A material adverse development in the Walmart relationship or nicotine regulatory regime.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August), which the options market is pricing for a ±10% move. That print is the near-term test of whether the Q1 fuel-supply/RIN windfall normalizes and how the durable retail fuel margin trends.

## Conclusion

Murphy USA is a well-run, structurally advantaged fuel-and-convenience retailer executing one of the more disciplined per-share compounding programs in retail, backed by a balance sheet that is far healthier than its buyback-distorted optics suggest. The trajectory of prior scores (5.1 → 6.7 → 5.6) reflects the market's evolving read on those optics and the stock's sharp run; the current data confirms strong financial health, modest and execution-dependent growth, and a valuation that has caught up to the fundamentals. The 42% one-year advance is not itself a reason to be cautious, but the combination of a ~21x multiple on earnings flattered by a non-recurring windfall, a per-store fuel-volume decline, a leadership transition, and no identified re-rating catalyst leaves the risk/reward balanced at best from ~$623.

We rate MUSA a **HOLD**. Own it for the quality and the buyback if already held; wait for a pullback toward the high-$400s/low-$500s, or for confirmation that the durable retail fuel margin is re-rating higher and per-store volumes are stabilizing, before adding new capital.
