**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Madison Square Garden Sports is the purest large-cap sports-franchise equity on U.S. public markets: a holding company whose crown-jewel assets are the New York Knicks (NBA) and New York Rangers (NHL). The investment case has almost nothing to do with reported earnings and almost everything to do with the gap between the company's ~$7.7B market capitalization and the private-market value of the two franchises. Forbes values the Knicks alone at roughly $10B, and the Rangers add several billion more. The stock trades on scarcity, franchise-value inflation, and, most concretely, on a board-authorized exploration (announced February 18, 2026) of a tax-free spin-off separating the Rangers from the Knicks into two independent public companies. That spin-off, if executed, is the single mechanism through which the trapped value gets crystallized for minority holders.

The reported financials are, by conventional screening standards, alarming and by design misleading. FY25 operating income collapsed 90% to $14.8M and the company posted a $22.4M net loss, driven almost entirely by the Knicks becoming a large NBA luxury-tax payer (a ~$50M swing on the tax line alone) plus $48.4M of roster-improvement charges. Stockholders' equity is negative (‑$295M at Q3 FY26) and the Altman Z-score reads 0.12. None of these signal distress here: the teams are carried at ~$103M of intangibles plus $226M of goodwill against real-world values north of $10B, so book-value and solvency metrics are structural artifacts, not warnings. The Knicks then won the 2026 NBA Championship; the cost of that title showed up in the P&L, the value in the franchise.

We rate MSGS a HOLD (growth hold). The spin-off thesis is real and the assets are genuinely scarce, but the stock has run ~95% over the trailing twelve months to sit near its 52-week high, with the championship and the spin-off narrative largely reflected in the price. The setup offers a credible path to further appreciation on spin-off execution but no margin of safety at current levels, and the governance profile (Dolan family control sitting on both sides of the MSG Networks rights cut, a CEO share pledge to a margin line, and no open-market insider buying against an enthusiastic sell-side tone) argues for patience over conviction.

## Company Overview

MSG Sports owns the New York Knicks and New York Rangers, plus two development affiliates (Westchester Knicks in the G-League, Hartford Wolf Pack in the AHL) and the Greenburgh training center. Both marquee teams play at Madison Square Garden, which the company does **not** own: the arena belongs to MSG Entertainment, a separate Dolan-controlled entity, and the teams play there under Arena License Agreements running to 2055 with fees escalating 3% annually. Fiscal year ends June 30. The company is a Nevada corporation (reincorporated June 2025) with a single reportable segment.

Revenue (FY25 ~$1.04B) comes from four buckets: event-related revenue (tickets, food/beverage, merchandise at The Garden); media rights (both local rights from MSG Networks and the company's pro-rata share of national NBA/NHL media deals); sponsorship, signage, and suite licenses; and league distributions. A meaningful portion of the top line is contracted multi-year, which dampens but does not eliminate the swings driven by on-court and on-ice performance.

The defining structural feature is Dolan family control: approximately 70.8% of voting power via Class B super-voting shares. The company operates as a "controlled company" (exempt from independent-board-majority requirements) and is embedded in a dense web of related-party arrangements with the other Dolan entities (MSG Entertainment for the arena, Sphere Entertainment / MSG Networks for local media rights, and AMC Networks) spanning arena licensing, sponsorship sales, ticketing, corporate services, and shared executives.

## Financial Analysis

The financial story is a robust top line masking a collapsed bottom line, where the collapse is largely self-inflicted and strategic rather than a sign of business deterioration.

| Metric ($M) | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 821 | 887 | 1,027 | 1,039 (+1%) |
| Operating income | 86 | 85 | 146 | 14.8 (-90%) |
| Adjusted OI (non-GAAP) | 142 | 115 | 172 | 38.2 (-78%) |
| Net income | 51 | 48 | 59 | (22.4) |
| Stockholders' equity | (147) | (337) | (266) | (281) |

FY25 revenue rose only 1% because the Knicks' deeper playoff run was offset by the Rangers missing the 2024-25 playoffs and by the MSG Networks rights-fee cut. Direct operating expenses jumped 22% (+$138.6M):

- **+$62.6M** in NBA luxury tax and league revenue sharing. The Knicks moved from *receiving* ~$12M in FY24 to paying a $38M luxury-tax expense in FY25, roughly a $50M swing driven mechanically by carrying a championship-caliber, high-payroll roster.
- **+$48.4M** in team personnel transaction charges (waivers/contract terminations $39.2M; trades $9.8M): lumpy roster-improvement costs.
- **+$32.8M** in higher team compensation.

FY26 year-to-date (nine months ended 3/31/26) confirms the pressure persists: revenue rose 5% to $875M (helped by the NBA's new national media deal), but the company swung to a $3.3M operating loss (versus +$37.4M prior year) and a $20.5M net loss. Luxury tax and revenue sharing rose another ~$34M over the nine-month period; adjusted OI fell 65% to $19.2M.

The balance sheet looks like a solvency crisis and is not one. Negative stockholders' equity of ~(‑$295M) at Q3 FY26 and an Altman Z of 0.12 are artifacts of franchise accounting: the teams carry at ~$103M of indefinite-lived intangibles plus $226M of goodwill against market values likely exceeding $10B for the Knicks alone. The current ratio of 0.45 is further distorted by deferred revenue (pre-sold tickets and suites), which is an operational positive. Reading these metrics at face value produces exactly the wrong conclusion.

Liquidity is adequate and improving. FY25 free cash flow was ~$88M (an 8.5% FCF margin); cash was $107M at 3/31/26. Debt is modest against asset value: the Knicks revolver was drawn ~$242M (down from $267M), the Rangers revolver was undrawn, plus a $16.5M NHL advance. In November 2025 the company refinanced and extended both credit facilities to November 2030 (Knicks capacity raised to $425M, Rangers to $250M), leaving substantial undrawn capacity and staying in compliance with the 1.5x debt-service covenant. That refinancing is a concrete lender endorsement of the underlying asset value.

On capital allocation, roughly $185M remains under a $525M buyback authorization, but no shares were repurchased in FY25 or the first nine months of FY26: cash is being conserved amid losses and ahead of the potential spin-off.

## Growth Analysis

Growth at MSGS operates on two separate clocks. The GAAP earnings clock is negative and volatile: luxury-tax escalation under the 2023 NBA CBA steps up materially with the 2025-26 season (harsher repeater penalties and "second apron" restrictions), meaning a championship-quality Knicks roster generates recurring, large, and unpredictable tax bills. This is the mechanism suppressing reported profitability, and it will not abate as long as the team stays competitive.

The asset-value clock, by contrast, is compounding at double-digit rates. NBA and NHL franchise valuations have inflated rapidly on scarcity, sovereign and private-equity capital entering ownership, and strengthening national media economics. The NBA's new 11-year national media deal (Disney/NBCUniversal/Amazon), effective with the 2025-26 season, is a genuine contracted top-line tailwind, contributing +$34M over the first nine months of FY26 to MSGS's national league distributions.

The estimate revision picture is favorable but requires care. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose to $0.63 from ‑$0.89 ninety days ago (+170.8%) and current-year estimates rose 22.3% over 30 days. Ordinarily estimate revisions of this magnitude are a strong forward signal. Here, they reflect the timing of high-luxury-tax and low-luxury-tax quarters within a seasonal business rather than a durable earnings shift: next-year EPS remains negative at ‑$0.59 and revised sharply lower over 90 days. The revision velocity should be treated as noise around a structurally lumpy, tax-driven earnings profile.

The real growth catalyst is corporate-structural: the board-authorized Rangers spin-off. If executed as a tax-free separation, it would give investors two cleaner pure-play team equities, each valued on its own franchise merits, and would be the mechanism through which the market-cap-to-asset-value discount narrows.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples are useless here. Trailing and forward P/E print at ‑428x on negative EPS. The business must be valued on a sum-of-the-parts / franchise-value basis, not on earnings.

The core arithmetic: market cap ~$7.7B (enterprise value modestly higher given ~$242M of net-of-cash Knicks borrowings) against a Forbes-implied Knicks value of ~$10B plus a Rangers franchise worth several billion. Even applying a meaningful control/liquidity discount for the Dolan structure, the implied private-market value of the two franchises comfortably exceeds the current enterprise value. This is why the stock trades at a large premium to book (which is negative) and why sophisticated holders such as Ariel Investments carry it as a top pick.

Peer multiples offer limited help given the heterogeneity of the "Movies & Entertainment" cohort (peer median P/E 33.7x, but the set spans profitable operators like WMG and CNK to loss-making TKO comps and negative-P/E names like PSKY and LYV). None is a true sports-franchise-holding comparable. The relevant valuation anchor is the discount to appraised franchise value, and on that basis the stock is cheaper than its screen suggests.

We do not score valuation higher despite the asset-value gap because the stock has appreciated ~95% over the trailing year to sit near its 52-week high of $411.73, and both the 2026 championship and the spin-off exploration are now in the price. Susquehanna's $430 target and consensus mean of $432.50 sit ~9% above the current $396.40, implying modest embedded upside. The value-realization mechanism (the spin-off) remains subject to board, league, and tax approvals; it is not committed. The scarcity discount is real, but the catalyst to close it is contingent and the entry point is near a high. We note the calibration caution that "cheap" screens have been unreliable predictors recently; here the cheapness is genuine on an asset basis but gated by execution risk, which is why we hold valuation at a modestly constructive 6.0 rather than a full BUY signal.

## Competitive Landscape

MSGS operates in the most crowded U.S. sports market (Nets, Islanders, Devils, plus MLB and NFL competition for the entertainment dollar), yet the Knicks and Rangers enjoy near-monopoly brand equity, sold-out-caliber demand, and pricing power in the largest U.S. media market. These are irreplaceable, appreciating scarcity assets: you cannot manufacture a second Original Six hockey franchise or a second original NBA team in New York.

The competitive picture is bifurcated. Franchise values are inflating, and the national media economics underpinning them are strengthening. But the regional sports network (RSN) model is in secular collapse, and MSGS has already taken a direct hit: the MSG Networks debt restructuring in June 2025 forced local rights-fee cuts of 28% (Knicks) and 18% (Rangers), retroactive to January 2025, with escalators eliminated and the term shortened to 2028-29. Stated annual local rights fees fall from $162.9M to $139.2M, and this revenue line will keep shrinking as cord-cutting proceeds. The offsetting positive from strengthening national media economics is real but leaves the local-rights line as a structural drag.

We hold competitive position at 7.5, reflecting the prior report's slight downgrade from 8.0. The franchise moat is as wide as any in the market, but the trajectory carries a genuine negative in the RSN erosion, and the trophy-asset status is a level, not an improving trend, once the championship is banked.

## Risk Assessment

**MSG Networks solvency — the standout idiosyncratic risk.** The local media rights counterparty, a Dolan-controlled Sphere subsidiary, restructured its debt in June 2025 with lenders writing off ~$510M. Even post-restructuring, MSG Networks could still enter bankruptcy, which would eliminate a ~$139M/year recurring revenue stream and could trigger a borrowing block or event of default under the Knicks and Rangers credit facilities. This is a binary tail with contractual cross-default mechanics, and it is not fully priced.

**Luxury-tax escalation.** Under the 2023 NBA CBA, tax rates and apron penalties step up beginning 2025-26. A championship-caliber roster means recurring, large, volatile tax bills, the primary driver of the earnings collapse and a structural cap on reported profitability.

**Roster-cost volatility.** Waiver, trade, and contract-termination charges ($49M in FY25) are lumpy and can swamp operating income in any given year.

**Dolan control and conflicts.** ~70.8% voting power, controlled-company exemptions, and extensive shared executives ("Overlap Persons") create structural conflicts. The MSG Networks rights cut is the sharpest example: MSGS reduced its own rights fees (aiding MSG Networks' lenders in the family's other entity) in exchange for penny warrants for a 19.9% stake carried at zero income. Minority Class A holders have limited recourse to police whether such trades favor the family.

**The Garden is leased, not owned.** The arena's special zoning permit was renewed in 2023 for only five years, and Penn Station redevelopment pressure could force changes at or relocation of The Garden over time.

**NYC real estate tax exemption ($43M/year)** faces recurring political scrutiny; repeal would raise the teams' costs.

Additional risks: dependence on on-court/ice success; labor/CBA dynamics (NHL ratified a new 4-year CBA through 2029-30 on 7/8/25; NBA runs through 2029-30 with an opt-out after 2028-29); variable-rate debt; and pronounced fiscal Q2/Q3 seasonality.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 34.6% versus 28.9% one-year historical volatility (modestly rich), and the term structure is in backwardation (34.6% near-term vs. 31.4% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with anticipation around spin-off developments and earnings timing. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (‑1.5% near-term), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.4%. ATM IV has fallen sharply from 48.9% a month ago as the price rose 10.2%, and the put/call OI ratio eased to 0.55: the options market has grown less defensive as the stock climbed. Short interest is moderate at 6.5% of float (4.3 days to cover), neither confirming a bearish thesis nor creating meaningful squeeze dynamics. The overall options read is consistent with a market awaiting a specific catalyst rather than positioning for a directional collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The Rangers spin-off proceeds as a tax-free separation, and the market re-rates two cleaner pure-play franchise equities toward appraised values. Even a partial narrowing of the discount between current enterprise value near $8B and appraised franchise value drives material upside. The 2026 championship strengthens the Knicks' brand and pricing power; the NBA's new national media deal provides a contracted, growing distribution stream; and franchise-value inflation continues. Sell-side and value-investor sponsorship (Susquehanna $430, Ariel top pick) provides support. Bull target ~$470 (roughly the high analyst target and a modest step toward full asset value realization on spin-off execution), ~19% above current.

**Bear Case.** MSG Networks enters bankruptcy, eliminating recurring local rights revenue and potentially triggering a credit-facility default; the spin-off stalls on league or tax hurdles; luxury-tax bills escalate further as the Knicks defend their title; and the Dolan structure keeps the discount permanently in place because minority holders cannot force value crystallization. The stock has already priced the good news and gives back the trailing-year run. Bear target ~$300, ~24% below current.

**Base Case.** The spin-off exploration advances but takes time and remains subject to approvals; franchise value continues compounding; earnings stay negative-to-lumpy on luxury tax; and the stock grinds modestly higher toward the consensus target as analysts and the market credit progress toward the separation. Base target ~$432 (consensus mean), ~9% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth hold with a 12-to-18-month horizon tied primarily to the spin-off process.

- **Base case target: ~$432** (consensus mean, ~9% upside). Reasoning: modest continued discount narrowing as spin-off exploration advances, supported by franchise-value compounding and national media tailwinds, absent full separation.
- **Bull case target: ~$470** (~19% upside), on spin-off execution and re-rating toward appraised asset value.
- **Bear case target: ~$300** (~24% downside), on MSG Networks bankruptcy / credit-facility trigger or a stalled spin-off with the trailing-year run reversing.
- **Timeframe: 12-18 months**, keyed to spin-off milestones (board/league/tax approvals), the June 30 fiscal year-end, and the FY26 full-year report.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Board formally approves and files a definitive tax-free spin-off structure with league sign-off, giving a concrete value-realization path.
- A pullback toward the low-$300s that restores a meaningful discount to appraised franchise value with the spin-off thesis intact.
- MSG Networks solvency resolved (e.g., a durable recapitalization or acquisition removing the credit-facility cross-default tail).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- MSG Networks files for bankruptcy or a credit-facility borrowing block / default is triggered.
- The Rangers spin-off is formally abandoned, removing the primary value-realization mechanism.
- A forced-sale event from the CEO's pledged shares, or new related-party transactions that transfer value away from minority holders.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the FY26 full-year earnings report (following the June 30 fiscal year-end) and any 8-K disclosure on spin-off progress.

## Conclusion

MSGS is a scarcity-asset story wrapped in deliberately misleading financial statements. The negative equity, sub-0.5 current ratio, and 90% earnings collapse are structural artifacts of franchise accounting and the strategic decision to pay for a championship; the balance sheet understates the teams' value by roughly an order of magnitude. The equity thesis rests on the pending tax-free Rangers spin-off crystallizing the gap between a ~$7.7B market cap and $10B-plus of franchise value.

We rate the stock a HOLD (growth hold). The assets are genuinely irreplaceable and the discount to private-market value is real, but the stock has appreciated ~95% over the trailing year to near its 52-week high with the championship and spin-off narrative largely reflected in the price, leaving only high-single-digit embedded upside to consensus. The catalyst that would close the discount, the spin-off, is contingent on board, league, and tax approvals; the MSG Networks solvency tail carries hard cross-default mechanics; and the governance profile, with Dolan control on both sides of the rights cut, a CEO margin-pledge, and no open-market insider buying against a bullish sell-side tone, argues for patience. We would turn constructive on formal spin-off approval or a pullback toward the low-$300s that restores a margin of safety.
