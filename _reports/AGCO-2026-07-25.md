**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AGCO is the world's third-largest full-line agricultural equipment manufacturer, behind Deere and CNH Industrial, with a portfolio anchored by the premium Fendt tractor brand, the mass-market Massey Ferguson line, Valtra, and the PTx precision-ag platform. The company is emerging from a sharp two-year downcycle: revenue fell roughly 30% from a $14.4B 2023 peak to $10.1B in 2025 as global farm income weakened and dealers destocked high-horsepower tractors. Q1 2026 marked a clear positive inflection: net sales rose 14.3% year-over-year to $2.34B, tractor and combine sales rose 20.3%, and production hours climbed 14.9%. Current-year consensus EPS estimates have been revised up 4.9% over the past 90 days, and the company has beaten estimates in each of the last four quarters, including a 107.5% surprise in Q1 2026.

The recovery is genuine at the operating level: gross margin improved to 25.5% in 2025 on aggressive cost reduction even as revenue fell. But the reported 2025 net income of $726.5M ($9.75 EPS) is materially inflated. Roughly $250M came from a one-time gain on the sale of AGCO's stake in Indian supplier TAFE, and $180M-plus from a legal-entity reorganization tax benefit. Normalized earnings power is closer to the $6.11 current-year and $7.98 next-year consensus, which is the correct basis for valuation. On those figures, AGCO trades at roughly 20x current-year and 15x next-year earnings, a substantial discount to Deere (32x) and CNH (33x) that partly reflects AGCO's scale disadvantage, higher leverage, and Europe-heavy footprint.

We maintain a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The cyclical-recovery thesis is intact and the valuation is undemanding relative to normalized earnings and to peers, but three factors cap conviction: North America and Latin America segments are currently loss-making on tariff input costs, the balance sheet sits in Altman Z grey-zone territory (2.01) while management runs an aggressive buyback into that cushion, and the single largest swing factor — tariff refunds and potential relief — remains binary and unmodeled in guidance. The score has drifted higher across the last three reports (5.0 → 5.2 → 5.4), driven by improving growth and risk readings; the Q1 inflection and rising estimates confirm that trajectory, and we nudge the overall to 5.5, but the asymmetry is not yet strong enough to warrant a buy at $121.

## Company Overview

AGCO is Delaware-incorporated but predominantly an ex-US business: the majority of both sales and manufacturing occur outside the United States, with primary plants in Germany, France, Finland, Italy, Brazil, and China. Europe/Middle East generates roughly 64% of sales and is the profit engine, home to the high-margin Fendt franchise. The product mix skews heavily toward tractors (66% of 2025 sales) and replacement parts (19%). The parts business is a valuable counter-cyclical annuity that grew from 13% of sales in 2023 to 19% in 2025 as equipment volumes fell, providing a high-margin buffer through the downturn.

The four brands serve distinct segments: Fendt (premium, high-horsepower, Europe-concentrated), Massey Ferguson (global mass-market), Valtra (Nordic/Latin American), and PTx (precision ag, comprising Precision Planting and the PTx Trimble joint venture in which AGCO holds 85%). AGCO builds its own 75–500 hp off-road diesel engines through AGCO Power, distributes through roughly 2,800 independent dealers across 140 countries, and provides retail/wholesale financing through AGCO Finance joint ventures owned 49% by AGCO and 51% by Rabobank, backing a finance portfolio of about $15.1B.

The company has reshaped its portfolio deliberately. In 2024 it formed the PTx Trimble JV (closed April 1, 2024) to build a mixed-fleet, brand-agnostic precision-ag platform, and divested the majority of its lower-margin Grain & Protein business (November 1, 2024). This pivot toward higher-tech precision ag and away from grain infrastructure differentiates AGCO's open-architecture approach from Deere's closed ecosystem.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is that of a cyclical business bottoming and inflecting. The multi-year trajectory:

| Metric | 2023 (peak) | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $14.41B | $11.66B | $10.08B |
| Operating income | $1.70B | $(0.12)B | $0.60B |
| Net income (to AGCO) | $1.17B | $(0.42)B | $0.73B |
| Diluted EPS | ~$15.6 | $(5.69) | $9.75 |
| Operating cash flow | — | $0.69B | $0.99B |

The 2024 loss was distorted by a $507M loss on the Grain & Protein sale and $369.5M of impairments, largely goodwill on PTx Trimble North America. The 2025 rebound is real at the operating line, with gross margin improving on lower manufacturing costs and structural cost cuts, but reported net income is flattered by the TAFE stake sale and the entity reorganization tax benefit. Backing those out puts normalized net income closer to $290–300M, consistent with the $6.11 current-year consensus. The correct valuation basis is normalized/forward EPS, not the $9.75 headline that produces the misleadingly low 11x trailing multiple.

Q1 2026 confirmed the operating inflection, with EPS of $0.94 versus $0.14. The regional split is the critical detail. Europe/Middle East carried the company with $259M of Q1 operating income, while North America (–$51M) and Latin America (–$40.9M) posted losses. The North America loss is directly attributable to tariff input costs; Latin America reflects weak Brazilian demand and dependence on government financing subsidies. The recovery is uneven, driven by European premium tractors and parts, not yet by the Americas.

Balance-sheet health is adequate but not robust. The Altman Z-Score of 2.01 sits in the grey zone — not distress, but a leverage/liquidity profile that warrants attention, with D/E around 1.72. A partial Piotroski read scores 5/5 (profitable, operating cash flow exceeding net income, current ratio 1.39). Total debt is manageable at roughly $2.4B against $861.8M year-end cash, with no revolver drawn at year-end (though $115M was drawn by Q1 2026 for seasonal working capital). Debt-to-cap improved to 35.8%. Free cash flow was approximately $740M in 2025 (a 7.3% margin), aided by capex discipline — capital spending was cut from $393M in 2024 to $248M in 2025.

The most fragile item is the PTx North America reporting unit goodwill, which passed impairment testing with only about 16% headroom; a one-point higher discount rate would compress that to roughly 3%. Against roughly $1.9B of total goodwill, this is a real impairment risk if the North America segment stays impaired by tariffs. Separately, approximately $1.8–2.1B of receivables sold to the finance JVs are accounted for off-balance-sheet, which understates effective leverage.

On capital allocation, management is returning cash aggressively into the trough: a $1.0B buyback authorized July 2025 (roughly $785M remaining), a $250M accelerated share repurchase executed November 2025, planned $350M of Q2 2026 repurchases, and a dividend raised to $0.30/quarter in April 2026. The recurring dividend yields only about 1.0%; the large 2024 headline dividend included a $2.50 special variable payout that is not part of the recurring base. Buying back stock at a cyclical trough signals confidence, but it also consumes the balance-sheet cushion in a grey-zone Z-score environment.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth signal is positive. Q1 2026 revenue accelerated off a depressed base, with the volume drivers confirming that dealers are restocking and end demand is stabilizing. The four consecutive earnings beats (22.7%, 8.5%, 14.5%, and 107.5% surprises over the last four quarters) suggest consensus has been persistently too conservative on the recovery path, which raises confidence in the forward estimates.

Estimate revisions are constructive but not uniformly so. Current-year EPS has been revised up 2.0% over 30 days, genuine positive momentum. However, next-year estimates have edged down 1.3% over 30 days and 2.0% over 90 days (to $7.98), and current-quarter/next-quarter revisions are roughly flat. This is a modest recovery inflection, not the explosive +30%-in-90-days revision velocity that flags a high-conviction earnings turn. The revision picture is directionally right but qualifies AGCO as a moderate cyclical recovery rather than a systematic-underestimation setup.

Management's own outlook calls for flat global industry demand in 2026. The growth thesis is not a volume-led industry boom but rather (a) AGCO capturing operating leverage as it recovers off the trough, (b) the counter-cyclical parts annuity providing margin stability, (c) the mix shift toward higher-tech precision ag improving through-cycle margins, and (d) an option on tariff relief converting the North America segment from a loss to a contributor. The structural cost work done in the downturn — an $82M–$173M/year severance program, capex cuts, and portfolio pruning — should mean the recovery converts to higher incremental margins than the prior cycle.

The longer-term growth wedge is the mixed-fleet precision-ag strategy (PTx, Precision Planting, PTx Trimble, FarmENGAGE, Outrun autonomy), which is designed to sell technology onto competitors' machines rather than only AGCO's own equipment. If executed, this expands the addressable market beyond AGCO's ~third-place hardware share. The risk is that tech startups and Deere's scale intensify competition in the software/autonomy layer.

## Valuation Assessment

On normalized earnings, AGCO is inexpensive. At $121.21, the stock trades at roughly 20x the $6.11 current-year consensus and about 15x the $7.98 next-year consensus. The 11x trailing multiple in the headline data is an artifact of the one-time-inflated $9.75 EPS and should be disregarded. Against peer medians — Deere at 32.2x, CNH at 33.4x, Toro at 26.7x, Lindsay at 21.0x — AGCO trades at a meaningful discount even on a growth-adjusted basis.

Part of that discount is justified. AGCO is the clear #3 at a scale disadvantage, carries higher leverage than Deere, generates loss-making results in two of three geographic segments today, and derives the bulk of profit from a Europe that itself faces recession risk. But the gap to Deere and CNH — roughly 40% on forward earnings — appears wider than those factors alone warrant given AGCO's genuine operating inflection and improved cost structure.

The valuation lessons here counsel caution against over-crediting cheapness. Historically, a low multiple has been an anti-predictive signal absent an independent catalyst, and cyclical ag machinery sits within a sector where BUY calls have shown negative alpha to the market. AGCO does have a partial catalyst set — the Q1 volume inflection, rising current-year estimates, D.A. Davidson's July 2026 Buy initiation at a $160 target, and the tariff refund/relief optionality — which distinguishes it from a pure value trap. But those catalysts are not yet decisive enough to justify a high valuation score. The consensus price-target range of $105–$160 (mean $129.87, median $130) implies roughly 7% upside to the mean, with the bull case ($160) requiring tariff relief and a firmer 2027 cycle that guidance does not currently assume.

Net: the stock is fairly-to-modestly cheap on normalized earnings, with asymmetric upside contingent on tariff resolution. That supports a HOLD with an upgrade path rather than an outright buy at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

AGCO occupies a stable #3 position in a three-player full-line oligopoly (Deere, CNH, AGCO). It cannot match Deere's or CNH's scale, but it holds two genuine differentiators. First, Fendt premium high-horsepower tractors remain strongly profitable through the downturn and anchor the Europe/Middle East segment that carries the company. Second, the brand-agnostic precision-ag strategy — building retrofit technology that works on competitors' machines — is a structurally different bet from Deere's walled-garden ecosystem, and if it gains traction it could expand AGCO's share of the ag-tech value pool independent of its hardware share.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable rather than clearly improving or deteriorating. On the positive side: the parts mix has grown, the January 2025 patent litigation win against Deere removes an overhang on the Precision Planting franchise, and the cost restructuring should improve relative through-cycle economics. On the negative side: AGCO remains scale-disadvantaged, faces intensifying software/autonomy competition from both Deere and tech entrants, and the North America segment is losing money in an environment where Deere is far better insulated from tariff input costs given its US manufacturing footprint. The precision-ag pivot is the key variable that could shift AGCO from stable challenger to share-gainer, but that thesis is unproven in the numbers so far.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclical dependence.** Revenue, margins, and used-equipment residuals all track farm income and commodity prices. The company explicitly warns that past downturns have produced significant and prolonged declines. This is a permanent feature of the business, largely priced, but it caps the risk score.

**Tariffs — the dominant swing factor.** Tariff input costs directly caused the North America Q1 2026 loss. The situation is fluid: the US Supreme Court ruled IEEPA tariffs unauthorized (February 2026), the Court of International Trade ordered CBP to process refunds, and AGCO has filed refund claims — but it cannot yet confirm timing or collectability, and the administration plans to re-impose tariffs under alternative authority. This is genuinely binary and asymmetric: refunds plus a potential 25%→15% equipment-tariff cut represent unmodeled upside to a segment currently running at a loss, while re-imposition under new authority is the downside. Guidance assumes the old, higher regime.

**Balance-sheet fragility at the margin.** The grey-zone Altman Z is drawn down by aggressive buybacks. The PTx North America goodwill (~16% headroom, roughly $1.9B total goodwill) is the most fragile item — an impairment is plausible if the North America segment stays tariff-impaired.

**Financing dependence on Rabobank.** Roughly half of tractor/combine retail sales are financed through the Rabobank-controlled JVs. The April 2026 agreement to sell AGCO's 49% US/Canada finance-JV stakes to Rabobank for ~$190M shifts toward a capital-light model that frees cash for buybacks, but it increases reliance on Rabobank as a pure financing partner and reduces the ~$40–72M/year equity-in-affiliate earnings contribution.

**Emerging-market and FX exposure.** Turkey and Argentina are designated highly inflationary; Argentine capital controls limit repatriation; Brazil (dependent on government financing subsidies) is currently weak and contributing to the Latin America loss.

**Governance residuals.** The combined Chairman/President/CEO/CODM role in Eric Hansotia concentrates authority, and two consecutive Item 5.02 executive-change 8-Ks in early March 2026 are a watch-item, though the CFO and CEO both signed the May 2026 10-Q, indicating continuity at the top posts. Offsetting these, the TAFE activist dispute has been settled via a June 2025 Cooperation Agreement, and the Deere patent case is won — two prior overhangs have cleared.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility of 43.8% is rich relative to 33.7% one-year historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (43.8% near-term versus 39.3% at six months), indicating the market is pricing an imminent catalyst — consistent with the tariff-refund/relief timeline and upcoming earnings. The put/call skew is call-heavy (–6.0%) and the put/call volume ratio is very low at 0.18, so options positioning does not confirm a bearish thesis; if anything, near-dated flow leans mildly bullish. The put/call open-interest ratio of 1.65 reflects longer-dated protective positioning rather than active bearish trading. The rich IV argues against paying up for exposure through options here and favors waiting for the near-term event (and IV crush) before adding. The ATM IV reading collapsed from a clearly corrupted 724.8% in the prior snapshot to a normalized 43.8%, so the current level is the credible data point.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($150–160).** AGCO is inflecting off a genuine cyclical trough with a leaner cost base. Volume recovery converts to strong incremental margins given the restructuring. Tariff refunds arrive and/or equipment tariffs are cut from 25% to 15%, turning the North America segment from a loss to a contributor and delivering unmodeled upside to the $7.98 next-year consensus. The precision-ag mixed-fleet strategy gains traction, expanding AGCO's addressable market. On roughly $8.50–9.00 of recovered EPS at an 18x multiple as the cycle firms, the stock reaches D.A. Davidson's $160 target.

**Bear case ($95–105).** Global ag demand stays flat-to-down, tariffs are re-imposed under new authority, North America and Latin America losses persist, and the PTx North America goodwill is impaired. Aggressive buybacks into a grey-zone balance sheet leave less cushion if the cycle extends. Normalized EPS stalls near $6, and the stock de-rates toward 16x or lower on the low end of the analyst range ($105), roughly 13% below current levels.

**Base case ($130).** The recovery continues at a moderate pace, Europe carries the company while the Americas gradually stabilize, tariff resolution is partial and slow, and normalized EPS approaches the $7–7.50 range. At roughly 17–18x, that supports the consensus median around $130, about 7% above the current price plus a ~1% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold)** over a 12-month horizon.

**Base case target: $130** — approximately 18x a normalized ~$7.20 EPS as the cycle firms, matching the consensus median and implying roughly 7% upside plus a ~1% dividend.

**Bull case target: $160** — requires tariff relief plus a firmer 2027 cycle lifting normalized EPS toward $8.50–9.00; roughly 32% upside.

**Bear case target: $105** — flat demand, tariff re-imposition, persistent Americas losses, and a possible goodwill impairment; roughly 13% downside.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the tariff-refund/relief resolution, the next two earnings prints (which will show whether the Americas segments narrow losses), and the fiscal 2027 industry-demand setup.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Tariff refunds confirmed as collectable and/or equipment tariffs cut to 15%, turning the North America segment profitable.
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions turn decisively positive (currently drifting down 1.3–2.0%), confirming a durable recovery rather than a moderate one.
- A pullback toward the low-$110s or below, widening the discount to normalized earnings and improving risk/reward asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Tariffs re-imposed under new authority with no refund collectability, keeping the North America segment loss-making.
- PTx North America goodwill impairment (the ~16% headroom is the fragile item).
- Current-year estimate revisions roll over negative alongside a Q2/Q3 miss, breaking the four-quarter beat streak.
- Balance-sheet deterioration — sustained revolver reliance or a Z-score drop into distress territory — while buybacks continue.

**Next reassessment point:** the next quarterly earnings release and any material tariff-litigation or policy development, whichever comes first.

## Conclusion

AGCO is a legitimate cyclical-recovery story trading at a reasonable discount to normalized earnings and to its larger peers. The Q1 2026 inflection, four-quarter beat streak, rising current-year estimates, and improved cost structure all support the constructive trajectory reflected in the rising score history (5.0 → 5.2 → 5.4 → 5.5). The current price of $121, up modestly from prior reports, does not change the thesis; fundamentals are stable-to-improving, so the risk/reward is marginally better than deteriorating.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of a binary and unresolved tariff situation that drives the entire North America segment, reported earnings quality that is flattered by one-time items, a grey-zone balance sheet being drawn down by aggressive buybacks, and a peer/sector context where cheap cyclical machinery names have historically required a specific catalyst to reward. The upside to the base case is a modest 7%, and the more attractive $160 bull case is contingent on tariff relief that guidance does not assume. We would move to a BUY on confirmed tariff resolution, a decisive turn in next-year estimates, or a pullback into the low-$110s. For now, hold for the cyclical-recovery thesis while monitoring the tariff and Americas-segment data points that will determine whether the recovery broadens beyond Europe.
