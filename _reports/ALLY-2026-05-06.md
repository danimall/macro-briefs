## Executive Summary

Ally Financial is the largest online-only bank in the United States (as measured by retail deposit balances) and one of the top two indirect auto lenders, with $196B in assets and 3.5 million primary deposit customers. After a painful credit normalization cycle in 2023-2024 that compressed EPS from ~$5.03 to ~$1.80, the company is now demonstrating clear earnings recovery: Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.11 beat estimates by 16.5%, marking the fourth consecutive beat. Net charge-offs improved on an annual basis in 2025, NIM is expanding, and the company has simplified its portfolio by exiting credit cards, mortgage originations, and personal lending.

At $44.30 per share, Ally trades at approximately 8.3x forward earnings (on $5.33 2026E EPS) and roughly 1.04x tangible common equity. This multiple implies the market still prices Ally for returns near or modestly above cost of equity, yet management is guiding toward mid-teens Core ROTCE as NIM approaches 3.8% and credit provisions normalize. Both the CEO and CFO made open-market purchases in January 2026 at $41-43. The primary risks are tariff-related auto industry disruption, used vehicle residual value pressure (particularly on EVs), and the still-elevated absolute level of net charge-offs relative to pre-COVID norms.

The consensus analyst target of $54 implies 22% upside, with the high target at $70 (58% upside). Given the confirmed earnings inflection, buyback authorization, and forward valuation discount, the risk/reward favors ownership with a 12-month horizon.

## Company Overview

Ally Financial (formerly GMAC) has operated in auto finance for over 100 years and transformed into a digital-first bank holding company over the past decade. The business generates revenue through four segments:

- **Automotive Finance** ($5.6B net revenue, $1.6B pretax): Consumer and dealer auto lending across ~21,400 active dealer relationships. 2025 originations of $43.7B (+11.5% YoY).
- **Insurance** ($1.7B revenue, $200M pretax): Vehicle service contracts, GAP coverage, and dealer inventory insurance.
- **Corporate Finance** ($538M revenue, $365M pretax): Middle-market lending to PE-sponsored companies ($13B portfolio).
- **Corporate/Other** ($79M revenue, -$1.15B pretax): Treasury, deposits, mortgage run-off, and OID amortization.

The branchless model funds through Ally Bank's $151.6B deposit base (92% FDIC-insured), providing structural cost advantages over branch-dependent competitors. The company is positioned as the primary alternative to captive finance arms (GM Financial, Ford Motor Credit) for dealer floor-plan and retail auto lending.

The 2026 EPS consensus of $5.33 represents a significant recovery from 2025's ~$2.37, driven by three reinforcing tailwinds:

1. **NIM expansion** (3.43% → ~3.8%): Deposit costs fell from 4.18% to 3.56% in 2025 and will continue repricing lower. Auto loan yields are rising as higher-rate vintages season in (9.18% ex-hedges in 2025 vs. 8.88% in 2024).

2. **Provision normalization**: 2025 provision of $1.48B was already down 32% from 2024's $2.17B. With the annual NCO ratio improving and coverage at 255% of NPLs, further provision tailwind is likely, though Q1 2026 provision of $467M (vs. $191M in Q1 2025) was elevated due to the prior-year provision benefit associated with the Ally Credit Card transfer to held-for-sale.

3. **Non-recurring charges dropping off**: The $495M securities repositioning loss and $305M credit card goodwill impairment in 2025 will not repeat.

**Balance Sheet Quality:**
- CET1: 10.23% (well above 7.1% requirement)
- Total liquidity: $66.1B
- Allowance/loans: 2.5% (conservative coverage)
- Deposits/total funding: 87%
- Securities AOCI drag: -$2.8B (excluded from regulatory capital per election)

**Capital Return:**
The December 2025 $2.0B buyback authorization is the first sizable new program after a pause in 2023-2025 (Ally had previously repurchased $1.65B under a 2022 authorization). As of March 31, 2026, approximately $1.84B remained available after Q1 repurchases of 3.6M shares. Combined with the $1.20/share dividend (2.7% yield), total return of capital capacity is substantial.

## Growth Analysis

Ally's growth story is primarily an **earnings recovery** rather than revenue expansion. Total net revenue has been relatively flat ($8.2-8.4B range) with 2025 at $7.9B including the securities repositioning drag. The growth vectors are:

1. **NIM expansion** provides the largest earnings lever. Each 10bp of NIM improvement translates to approximately $130-150M of pre-tax income given the ~$150B interest-earning asset base. The path from 3.43% to 3.8% implies $500-550M of incremental pre-tax income.

2. **Auto origination growth**: 2025 originations of $43.7B (+11.5%) demonstrate market share gains as Ally diversifies beyond GM/Stellantis toward other OEMs (39% of originations) and digital retailers (Carvana at 11%). Weighted average yields on new originations exceed portfolio average, creating natural yield accretion.

3. **Credit normalization**: The improving annual loss trajectory (NCOs 2.2% → 2.0% in 2025) allows lower provision expense without de-risking. Newer vintages underwritten at tighter standards should continue this trend, though quarterly comparisons remain noisy due to the credit card portfolio sale effects.

4. **Operating leverage from simplification**: Exiting credit cards, personal lending, and mortgage originations eliminates drag on efficiency ratio and management attention.

The negative revision on current/next quarter estimates (-1.0% to -2.6% over 90 days) likely reflects macro uncertainty around tariffs and auto industry health, partially offset by the positive full-year revision (+1.6% over 30 days, +0.7% over 90 days) and next-year revision (+2.1% over 30 days, +2.2% over 90 days). This pattern suggests near-term caution but confidence in the medium-term trajectory, consistent with a credit-recovery story where quarterly cadence is noisy but the direction is clear.

Ally's premium to pure-play consumer finance peers (SYF, BFH) is warranted by its deposit funding advantage, diversification across auto, insurance, and corporate finance, and its improving ROTCE trajectory. However, the stock remains below the multiple appropriate for a mid-teens ROTCE bank.

**Implied valuation at target ROTCE:**
At 14% Core ROTCE on ~$42.61/share tangible common equity, normalized earnings would be approximately $6.00/share. A 10x multiple on that earnings power implies $60/share — consistent with analysts' above-consensus targets. At 1.3x tangible common equity (appropriate for 14-15% ROTCE), the stock would be ~$55.

The market appears anchored to 2024's trough profitability rather than forward earnings power. The ~1.0x tangible common equity multiple is only appropriate if ROTCE remains near or below cost of equity (~10%). With Core ROTCE already at 10.4% in 2025 and improving, this multiple should expand as the earnings trajectory confirms.

## Competitive Landscape

Ally operates in a favorable competitive position within auto finance:

**Structural advantages:**
- Branchless deposit model provides ~50-100bp funding cost advantage vs. traditional bank competitors
- 100+ years of auto lending experience and proprietary credit models
- 21,400 active dealer relationships with deep integration into dealership operations
- SmartAuction platform (573K vehicles sold in 2025) creates network effects and data advantages
- Full-spectrum service (retail lending, leasing, floor-plan, insurance, remarketing) makes Ally a one-stop shop for dealers

**Competitive dynamics:**
- Captive finance (GM Financial, Ford Motor Credit) competes on OEM-subsidized rates but cannot serve non-franchise dealers
- Capital One and other banks compete in prime indirect auto but lack Ally's dealer service depth
- Fintech platforms (Upstart, Carvana) are partners more than pure competitors — Ally is Carvana's primary lending partner
- SYF and BFH compete in broader consumer finance but not directly in auto

**Evolving positioning:**
- Stellantis concentration declining (20% → 13% of originations) — the dealer base is actively diversifying
- Carvana partnership growing (10.4% of receivables) — captures digital retail trend via a $6B committed facility (364-day term, renewed/expanded in Q4 2025)
- Other OEM franchise dealers growing (33% → 39-40% of originations) — reduces any single-OEM dependency

The competitive trajectory is positive: market share stable-to-growing, channel diversification progressing, and the digital banking model becoming more relevant as consumer preferences shift.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff & Auto Industry Disruption (Moderate-High):**
Tariffs on imported vehicles and parts would increase new vehicle prices, potentially suppressing sales volumes from 15.7M SAAR. This creates three second-order risks for Ally: (1) fewer origination opportunities, (2) consumer credit stress if monthly payments become unaffordable, and (3) used vehicle value disruption. The 10-K explicitly flags this as a primary risk factor.

**Used Vehicle Residual Value:**
The shift from $1,033/vehicle remarketing gains in 2024 to $323/vehicle losses in 2025 signals a turning used car market. EV/PHEV residual exposure ($4.2B operating leases) is partially mitigated by OEM guarantees (now 39% of leases, up from 24%), but the trend is negative.

**Interest Rate Path Uncertainty:**
Ally is modestly asset-sensitive short-term but liability-sensitive beyond 12 months. An unexpected rate increase of 200bp would reduce NIM by $221M. The current thesis depends on rate stability or moderation — a "higher for longer" scenario impairs the NIM expansion story.

**Carvana Concentration:**
At 10.4% of consumer auto receivables with a $6B committed facility, Carvana represents meaningful counterparty risk. While credit performance has been consistent with expectations, Carvana's volatile history and aggressive growth strategy introduce headline and liquidity risk to this channel.

**Credit Cycle Reversion:**
While annual NCOs improved in 2025, they remain above pre-COVID norms (~1.2-1.5%). Q1 2026 consolidated NCOs were $417M (1.21% rate) vs. $366M (1.10%) in Q1 2025, reflecting the absence of the prior-year credit card sale benefit. A recession pushing unemployment above 5.5% could reverse the improving trend. Management's downside scenario suggests allowance could increase 18-24% under stress.

### Options Market Signal

ATM IV of 31.9% sits modestly above 1-year historical vol of 29.8% — essentially at fair value. IV compressed from 64.2% (pre-earnings, April 14) to 31.9% today, reflecting resolved event risk from the Q1 beat. Put/call skew at 3.0% indicates mildly elevated put demand but nothing extreme. The contango term structure (near 31.9% < long-term 34.5%) reflects normal conditions with no near-term stress pricing. OI P/C ratio rising from 0.72 to 0.99 suggests some protective positioning building post-earnings rally, but absolute levels remain neutral. Overall, the options market is not signaling either elevated optimism or stress — consistent with a stock trading at fair value in the near-term with moderate upside over a longer horizon.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($58-65, 31-47% upside):**
NIM reaches 3.8%+ in H2 2026 as deposit repricing accelerates. NCOs continue declining toward 1.7-1.8%. Auto originations grow 5-10% annually with stable yields. Buyback program executes $1B+ over 12 months. Core ROTCE reaches 14-15% by 2027. Stock re-rates to 1.3-1.4x tangible common equity or 10x 2027 EPS.

**Bear Case ($34-38, 14-23% downside):**
Tariff escalation disrupts auto industry — new vehicle sales fall below 14M SAAR, used values decline 10-15%, NCOs reverse to 2.5%+. Rate environment stays higher for longer, stalling NIM expansion. Carvana faces financial difficulties, creating headline risk and potential losses. ROTCE stays in 8-10% range, justifying current multiple.

**Base Case ($52-56, 17-26% upside):**
NIM expands to 3.6-3.7% (below guidance due to rate uncertainty). NCOs stabilize at 1.8-2.0%. EPS achieves $5.00-5.50 in 2026. Stock re-rates to 10-10.5x forward earnings or 1.2-1.3x tangible common equity as profitability recovery confirms. Buyback provides ~3% accretion.

## Conclusion

Ally Financial presents a clean earnings recovery story at a valuation that does not yet fully reflect the improving trajectory. The credit cycle improvement is evident on an annual basis (four consecutive earnings beats, declining 2025 NCOs, falling provision expense), though quarterly comparisons in early 2026 remain somewhat mixed due to the credit card portfolio sale effects. NIM expansion is underway with a clear mechanical path to 3.8%, and management is signaling confidence through open-market purchases and a renewed buyback program. At approximately 1.04x tangible common equity and ~8.3x forward earnings on what is likely still a below-normalized earnings year, the market is pricing insufficient credit for the recovery trajectory.

The primary risk is macro-driven: tariffs disrupting the auto industry, a consumer recession reversing the credit improvement, or rates staying elevated enough to stall NIM expansion. These are real risks — Ally is fundamentally a levered bet on the American consumer and the auto market. But the forward valuation on 2026-2027 earnings provides margin of safety even if recovery proceeds more slowly than guided.

The score trajectory from prior reports (5.3 → 5.3 → 6.5) reflects the improving fundamental picture. This analysis maintains the BUY recommendation with updated scores reflecting confirmed Q1 2026 execution while acknowledging that the stock has appreciated 34% from its $32.28 low. At $44.30, meaningful upside remains to intrinsic value, but the largest portion of the move (buying at sub-$35 alongside management) has already occurred.
