**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Prosperity Bancshares is a Houston-based community-banking franchise running 283 banking centers across Texas and Oklahoma (pre-acquisition footprint), distinguished by a low-cost deposit base, top-decile efficiency, and fortress capital. The 2025 numbers are strong on their own terms: net income of $542.8M (+13.2% YoY), diluted EPS of $5.72, net interest margin expanding to 3.22% tax-equivalent from 2.78% two years earlier, and an efficiency ratio improving to 44.55%. CET1 stands at 17.55% and the leverage ratio at 11.93%, both well above regulatory minimums. This is one of the better-capitalized, more efficient regional banks in the country.

The defining event is an M&A wave. In a roughly five-week window, PB closed the American Bank Holding acquisition (Jan 1, 2026, 18 South/Central Texas offices) and Southwest Bancshares (Feb 1, 2026, 11 Central Texas offices), and signed a definitive agreement to acquire Stellar Bancorp (announced Jan 28, 2026, ~$2.0B, 52 greater-Houston offices) pending regulatory approval. All three are predominantly stock-funded, which dilutes near-term per-share metrics in exchange for franchise density in PB's highest-growth metros. Management's stated track record of 12-month accretion on every prior deal is the central pillar of the bull case, but it is unproven on three simultaneous integrations of this combined scale.

The case for caution sits on the earnings and credit lines. PB has missed consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters, including a sharp -19.4% Q1'26 surprise ($1.16 actual vs $1.44 estimate), and forward estimates are drifting lower across all horizons. Nonperforming assets nearly doubled to $150.8M (0.69% of loans) while the bank booked zero loan-loss provision in 2025 and released $18.9M of resolved-PCD reserves; earnings quality is clean but the absence of provisioning against rising NPAs likely means provision expense returns as a headwind. At ~13.2x trailing/forward EPS and roughly 1x stated book (a meaningful premium to thin tangible book given $3.50B of goodwill), the stock is fairly valued. We rate PB a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Prosperity Bancshares operates through Prosperity Bank, a Texas-chartered, FDIC-insured commercial bank founded in 1949, with the holding company formed in 1983. As of December 31, 2025, the bank served roughly 804,300 deposit accounts and 65,700 loan accounts across Houston, Dallas/Fort Worth, South Texas/Corpus Christi, Central Texas (Austin–San Antonio), West Texas (Lubbock, Midland-Odessa), East Texas, Bryan/College Station, and Tulsa/Oklahoma City. It manages ~$2.89B in trust assets and employs 3,941 people.

The operating model is community banking at scale. Each banking center functions as a separate profit center with local presidents holding lending authority, backed by centralized data processing, loan/deposit operations, audit, and compliance. The bank reports as a single segment and has been profitable every year of its existence. Its strategic edge is a serial-acquirer playbook: management states that every acquisition since inception has been accretive within 12 months, a claim now being tested at unprecedented scale.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and returns.** Net income rose to $542.8M in 2025 from $479.4M (2024) and $419.3M (2023). Diluted EPS climbed to $5.72 from $5.05 and $4.51. ROA was 1.42%, while ROE of 7.14% looks unimpressive — a direct artifact of the very large equity base loaded with goodwill. On tangible common equity (~$4.06B after stripping $3.50B goodwill and $51.6M intangibles), returns are considerably higher and more representative of underlying profitability.

**Net interest margin — the core positive.** NII grew 5.4% to $1.08B, a ~44 bp two-year margin improvement. The mechanism is straightforward balance-sheet repricing: cost of funds fell to 1.61% from 1.87% as PB ran off ~$1.25B of expensive borrowings (average FHLB/BTFP balances fell from $3.8B to $2.4B) and benefited from lower deposit costs (average cost of deposits 1.37%, with noninterest-bearing deposits ~33% of total). The bank has deliberately repositioned to asset-sensitive: a +200 bps rate move would add 3.5% to NII (up from 1.0% a year ago), achieved by shrinking the low-yielding fixed-rate securities book.

**Balance sheet.** On a standalone, pre-merger basis, total assets were $38.46B (down 2.8% YoY), loans $21.81B (down 1.6%), and deposits $28.48B (flat). The loans/deposits ratio of 76.6% is conservative. The asset contraction reflects securities runoff and borrowing paydown, not stress.

**Securities portfolio.** The $10.6B portfolio (27.6% of assets) yields just 2.26% with a 4.31-year weighted-average life. The held-to-maturity book carries roughly $840M of unrealized losses (amortized cost $10.28B vs fair value $9.43B). These are economic/AOCI losses, not credit losses, and under the bank's election they do not flow through regulatory capital, but they are real opportunity cost and a reminder of embedded duration risk.

**Credit quality.** This is the clearest negative inflection. Nonperforming assets nearly doubled to $150.8M (0.69% of loans + OREO) from $81.5M (0.37%), with nonaccruals rising to $137.2M from $73.6M. Net charge-offs remain tiny at 0.08% of average loans ($18.1M). The allowance for credit losses sits at 1.53% of loans ($333.7M), but ACL coverage of NPLs fell sharply to 242.7% from 463.9%. The provision for credit losses was $0 in 2025 (vs $9.1M in 2024), and $18.9M of resolved-PCD reserves was released to general reserve. Earnings were not manufactured by reserve building, which speaks to clean quality, but the implication is that provision expense is likely to return as a 2026 headwind as NPAs rise and three acquired loan books are onboarded.

**Capital allocation.** PB paid $221.4M in dividends and repurchased $157.2M of stock (2.3M shares at ~$67.04 average) in 2025, and announced a new 5% (~4.87M share) repurchase authorization in January 2026. Free cash flow was approximately $517M. The 3.4% dividend yield is well-covered.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is overwhelmingly inorganic. Organically, the standalone balance sheet shrank in 2025 — loans down 1.6%, assets down 2.8% — as management prioritized margin and funding-cost discipline over balance-sheet expansion. NIM expansion, not loan growth, drove the 13% earnings gain.

The acquisition wave reshapes the trajectory. American (18 offices, ~4.44M PB shares) and Southwest (11 offices, ~4.09M PB shares) closed in early 2026 and are already contributing. The pending Stellar deal (52 greater-Houston/Beaumont offices, 0.3803 PB shares + $11.36 cash per Stellar share) materially deepens density in PB's home market. Pro forma, PB becomes a markedly larger Texas franchise, and trailing financials understate true scale.

The near-term arithmetic is dilution before accretion. The all-stock structure issues meaningful share count up front; cost synergies and Zalman's historical 12-month accretion track record are the offset. The risk is that integrating three banks essentially simultaneously, each carrying its own credit marks, strains the playbook that historically handled deals one at a time. Forward consensus does anticipate growth — next-year EPS of $7.22 vs current-year $6.24 implies ~16% growth as synergies materialize — but those estimates have been drifting lower (current quarter -2.3% over 90 days, current year -0.6%, next year -0.3%). The revision trend is mildly negative across every horizon, which weighs against treating the growth thesis as high-conviction. The earnings-momentum signal is the opposite of the positive revision velocity that would support an upgrade.

## Valuation Assessment

PB trades at $72.66, a market cap of ~$7.3B, on trailing and forward P/E of 13.2x with a 3.4% dividend yield. Against the regional-bank peer set, this is in line to slightly above the median: CBSH 13.6x, VLY 13.0x, FNB 11.3x, UBSI 12.6x, GBCI 22.8x, with a peer median P/E of 13.0x and median yield of 2.9%. PB's modest premium on P/E and yield is consistent with its superior efficiency and capital position, but it does not represent a discount.

On book value, PB trades near 1x stated book ($7.62B equity vs $7.3B market cap) but at a clear premium to tangible book (~$4.06B TCE), which is the appropriate frame for a serial acquirer carrying $3.50B of goodwill. The all-stock Stellar/American/Southwest deals will add substantially more goodwill, further widening the gap between reported and tangible equity.

The analyst consensus target mean is $77.92 (median $78.00, range $68–$85), implying ~7% upside to the mean. That is not the kind of asymmetric setup that warrants a BUY, particularly given the negative estimate revision backdrop. Cheapness on a low P/E alone is not a buy signal in this regime, and here the multiple is not even cheap relative to peers. There is no identifiable catalyst for a re-rating beyond a clean Stellar close and demonstrated accretion, both of which are still ahead and unproven. We score valuation as balanced rather than attractive. The Altman Z-Score of 0.43 ("distress") is structurally inapplicable to banks, whose business model is inherently high-leverage, and should be disregarded.

## Competitive Landscape

PB's competitive position is genuinely strong and, on balance, improving. Its differentiation rests on three durable pillars: a low-cost, sticky deposit franchise (1.37% average cost of deposits, ~33% noninterest-bearing); best-in-class efficiency (down from 50.3% in 2023); and fortress capital that gives it premium acquisition currency. The Texas community-banking market remains highly fragmented outside the major metros, which is precisely the substrate PB's acquisition engine consumes.

The acquisition wave strengthens market position by deepening density in Houston, San Antonio, Austin, and Corpus Christi — the state's highest-growth corridors. Stellar in particular reinforces the Houston home market. The 2025 industry backdrop favored PB: funding-cost relief after the Fed cuts produced NIM expansion across the sector, and PB captured more of it than most peers (+29 bps NIM in a single year).

The trajectory caveat is execution. A market leader compounding via acquisition is only as strong as its integration discipline, and three simultaneous deals tests that discipline. The competitive position is high-quality and stable-to-improving, but the recent edge requires confirmation through clean integration before treating it as conclusive.

## Risk Assessment

**Acquisition integration and execution — the dominant near-term risk.** A new, expanded risk factor in the 10-K explicitly covers the pending Stellar deal and the recently closed American/Southwest transactions: deal-close risk, integration difficulty, deposit/customer attrition, transaction costs, management distraction, and synergy shortfall. Three simultaneous integrations magnify each of these.

**Credit deterioration.** NPAs +85% YoY with NPL coverage halving (464% → 243%), against zero provisioning, is the clearest negative inflection in an otherwise pristine credit record. Absolute levels remain benign (0.08% NCOs), but the direction matters as three acquired loan books with their own marks are consolidated.

**Real estate and geographic concentration.** Roughly 80% of loans are real estate (37.9% 1-4 family, 29.5% CRE including farmland/multifamily, 12.6% construction/land). CRE ex-farmland is 26.5%. The franchise is geographically concentrated in Texas/Oklahoma; direct energy exposure is modest (~2.0% of loans ex-Warehouse), but a Texas downturn tied to oil would hit the broader book.

**Goodwill impairment.** Goodwill of $3.50B is nearly half the $7.62B equity base and will grow with the pending deals, deepening tangible-book exposure.

**Funding and rate risk.** Uninsured deposits of $12.45B (~44% of deposits) carry 2023-style confidence-shock risk. The asset-sensitive positioning (-100 bps = -3.5% NII) makes PB a relative loser in an aggressive Fed cutting cycle, while higher-for-longer rates are a relative benefit. The Warehouse Purchase Program ($1.30B, 6% of loans) introduces month-end balance volatility.

**Earnings visibility.** Four consecutive quarterly misses point to deteriorating near-term forecastability, likely tied to merger costs and credit marks. This is the single most concrete reason to demand resolution before adding.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sending a benign, complacent signal. Near-term ATM IV is 27.6% versus 23.3% one-year historical volatility — modestly rich (4.3% spread), with IV in only the 26th percentile of its own chain. Put/call skew is balanced (0.0%), and the put/call OI ratio is very low at 0.12. The term structure is in contango (27.6% near-term vs 31.3% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. The six-month tenor shows a -4.4% put skew, consistent with some hedging demand around the Stellar close timeline, but nothing alarming. The implied one-month move of ~9.8% is roughly in line with a bank navigating M&A integration. Short interest is moderate at 6.5% of float (6.5 days to cover) — not a crowded short. Overall, options confirm rather than challenge a HOLD: the market is not pricing acute stress, and there is no cheap-IV entry incentive given the modest richness.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
PB is a premium-quality, fortress-capitalized Texas franchise using its strong currency to consolidate a fragmented market during the post-2023 regional-bank consolidation cycle. The bank is now asset-sensitive, positioning it to benefit if rates stay higher-for-longer. If Zalman's track record of 12-month accretion holds on the three deals, pro forma earnings power steps up materially through 2027, cost synergies drive the efficiency ratio lower still, and consensus next-year EPS of $7.22 proves conservative. At a peer-multiple re-rating to the mid-teens on that base, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range. Bull case: ~$85 (the top analyst target), roughly 17% above current price.

### Bear Case
Integrating three banks simultaneously proves messier than the historical one-at-a-time playbook. Deposit attrition and merger costs compress near-term earnings (the four-quarter miss streak extends), credit deterioration accelerates as acquired loan books reveal marks and 2026 provisioning normalizes off a zero base, and the goodwill load grows just as a Texas/CRE credit cycle bites. Estimates continue to drift lower, the multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer band, and the stock retests its 52-week low. Bear case: ~$61 (near the 52-week low), roughly 16% below current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (rationale: uncertain).** The thesis is genuinely two-sided. A high-quality franchise with real NIM expansion and a credible acquisition engine is offset by negative estimate momentum, a clean four-quarter miss streak, credit deterioration against zero provisioning, and unproven simultaneous integration. The valuation offers no asymmetric edge at ~13.2x and ~1x book.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Stellar regulatory approval/close, the Q2 2026 print (first full quarter with American/Southwest consolidated and provision-expense normalization visible), and any 8-K disclosures on integration progress.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stellar closes cleanly and management quantifies cost synergies that drive consensus next-year EPS estimate revisions back to positive (vs the current -0.3% to -2.3% drift), with a return to estimate beats.
- A pullback toward the high-$50s/low-$60s that compresses forward P/E meaningfully below the peer median while fundamentals (NIM, capital, deposit costs) remain intact.
- Credit stabilization — NPAs flattening or NPL coverage stabilizing alongside a resumption of normalized, manageable provisioning.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NPAs continue climbing toward/above 1.0% of loans with NCOs accelerating, signaling a genuine Texas/CRE credit cycle rather than benign noise.
- Stellar integration produces material deposit attrition or a synergy shortfall that breaks the 12-month accretion record, with EPS estimates cut sharply.
- A goodwill impairment charge or evidence that the all-stock roll-up has overpaid relative to franchise value.

## Conclusion

Prosperity Bancshares is a well-run, exceptionally capitalized Texas community bank in the middle of a large roll-up. The fundamentals that matter most over the long run — funding cost, efficiency, capital — are best-in-class, and the franchise is deepening in the right markets. The case for owning the stock today is held in check by concrete, current data: forward estimates falling across every horizon, four consecutive quarterly misses including a sharp Q1'26 negative surprise, nonperforming assets doubling against zero provisioning, and the execution risk of three simultaneous integrations. At a peer-comparable ~13.2x earnings and roughly 1x book (a premium to thin tangible book), the valuation prices the quality without offering asymmetric upside.

The score trajectory (5.9 → 6.5 across prior reports, with Growth, Competitive Position, and Financial Health all improving) reflected the constructive M&A narrative, and the franchise quality remains real. The new data — the Q1 miss, the falling estimates, and the credit inflection — argues for moderating rather than extending that improvement. We hold the recommendation at HOLD and want to see the Stellar close and a clean post-merger quarter before underwriting the accretion thesis with conviction. We would add on either a cheaper entry or proof that the integration playbook scales.
