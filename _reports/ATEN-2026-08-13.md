## Executive Summary

A10 Networks delivered its best revenue quarter in years — Q2 2026 revenue of $80.137M, up 15.5% — and raised FY2026 guidance for the second time this year, to 12–14% revenue growth and 14–16% non-GAAP EPS growth. Gross margin held at 79.1% GAAP despite an acknowledged memory-cost squeeze, the balance sheet carries $357.3M of cash and marketable securities against $225M of convertible principal for net cash of roughly $132M, and trailing free cash flow is $60.9M. On the headline operating metrics this is a business that is working better than it was a year ago.

The Q2 10-Q also disclosed the mechanics behind the growth, and they are less comfortable. Customer A — confirmed by CEO Dhrupad Trivedi on the August 5 call as Microsoft — rose from 30% of quarterly revenue to 38%; top-ten end customers went from 46% to 58%; a single distribution channel now represents 40% of revenue and 57% of gross accounts receivable against an allowance for credit losses of $19 thousand. The widely-reported "shift to enterprise" (40% → 60% of revenue in one year) is substantially the same counterparty buying a different product set, which Trivedi confirmed directly under questioning. Two of three geographies are contracting outright (APJ −15%, EMEA −22% in H1). On August 3 the company issued Microsoft a warrant for up to 800,000 shares at $0.01, vesting on Microsoft's own purchase volumes, and states it "is currently unable to reasonably estimate the financial effect." This unresolved item could land as contra-revenue, as sales expense, or at a fair value well below its ~$22M intrinsic value.

At $27.49 the stock is 26.4x FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.04, 23.1x FY2027's $1.19, 6.0x EV/TTM revenue and 30.5x EV/TTM free cash flow, with consensus estimates essentially unrevised over 90 days (+0.7% current year, −0.1% next year) and a one-cent non-GAAP miss in the most recent quarter. That is a full-to-fair multiple with no discount embedded for a 38% single-customer dependency, an unquantified accounting item tied to that customer, or a convertible conversion window that noteholders control through September 30. My base case is $29, bull $38, bear $18. Probability-weighted, expected return is approximately flat-to-negative with roughly 6:1 adverse skew between base upside and bear downside. Recommendation: **REDUCE**, breaking three consecutive HOLDs.

## Company Overview

A10 sells secure application-delivery and network-infrastructure products: hardware appliances running the proprietary ACOS software platform, perpetual and term licenses, a growing SaaS portfolio, and attached post-contract support. The FY2025 10-K organizes the portfolio into legacy networking (carrier-grade NAT, IPv4-to-IPv6 transition), next-generation networking (application delivery controllers and load balancing), and network security (A10 Defend: DDoS protection, SSL inspection, convergent firewall, and cloud WAAP acquired with ThreatX in February 2025). The June 2026 acquisition of TrojAI adds AI model red-teaming and runtime protection for agentic workflows.

The operating model is asset-light and unusually concentrated in its dependencies. All hardware is manufactured by third-party ODMs in Taiwan — Lanner, AEWIN and iBase are named — with **no long-term guaranteed capacity or pricing commitments**; open purchase commitments were $35.0M at 6/30/26. Ninety-six percent of FY2025 revenue moved through distributors and resellers. Headcount is 494, producing roughly $588K of revenue per employee at 79% gross margin. The company reports as a single segment with the CEO as chief operating decision maker, and discloses revenue by source (products/services), by vertical (enterprise/service provider), and by three geographies.

One structural item deserves flagging: as part of a litigation settlement, A10 granted Broadcom a license to all of its issued patents, pending applications and patents filed through May 2025, for the life of each patent. Broadcom owns VMware, which A10 names as a competitor. A10's 211 U.S. patents cannot be asserted against that competitor.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is accelerating and is entirely product-led.**

| Period | Revenue | YoY | Products | Services |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | $261.696M | — | $139.8M | $121.9M |
| FY2025 | $290.557M | +11.0% | $167.086M (+19.5%) | $123.471M (+1.3%) |
| Q2 2026 | $80.137M | +15.5% | $49.024M (+25.1%) | $31.113M (+3.0%) |
| H1 2026 | $155.137M | +14.5% | $93.010M (+23.8%) | $62.127M (+2.9%) |

Services — the support and maintenance base — grew 1.3% in FY2025 and 2.9% in H1 2026. Term licenses and subscriptions are recognized point-in-time at delivery and booked in products, so the split does not cleanly measure recurring revenue; but A10 discloses no ARR or any other recurring-revenue metric, which is a genuine gap for a company positioning itself as a software and security platform.

**GAAP operating leverage inverted in Q2.** Revenue rose 15.5% while GAAP operating income fell 12.5%, from $10.311M (14.9% margin) to $9.024M (11.3%). The single dominant driver is stock-based compensation, which doubled from $4.409M to $8.966M — from 6.4% to 11.2% of revenue. Adding back the $4.557M increase produces pro-forma operating income of roughly $13.58M, or 16.9% of revenue, up about 32% year over year. On a cash-cost basis the business did generate leverage. Sixty-two percent of the SBC increase landed in G&A, which rose 57.4%; R&D rose 30.1%. The 10-Q does not disaggregate the cause. Performance-share unit conversions for the CEO, CFO and General Counsel in early May, an accelerated-vesting provision in the May 2 separation agreement with the departed EVP of Sales and Marketing, and TrojAI retention grants are all plausible mechanical explanations under ASC 718 recognition rules, but none is confirmed, and whether 11.2% of revenue is a run rate or an outlier is unknown. Unrecognized SBC is $38.0M over a weighted-average 1.8 years.

The consequence for valuation is that the GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is wide and widening: Q2 diluted GAAP EPS $0.12 versus non-GAAP $0.25, a 2.1x ratio, with the bridge roughly $8.966M of SBC plus ~$0.5M intangible amortization plus $1.3M of TrojAI transaction costs. Management's "14–16% EPS growth" guidance is a non-GAAP figure whose largest add-back is growing faster than revenue.

**Cash flow is solid in level and decelerating in trend.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $84.894M, down 6.2% from FY2024's $90.492M on 11% revenue growth; capex $20.128M for free cash flow of $64.766M (22.3% of revenue). H1 2026 operating cash flow was $31.346M versus $39.384M, down 20.4%, on higher net income — driven by a $13.8M inventory build (raw materials up 114% to $22.375M, consistent with pre-buying ahead of memory cost increases), $10.1M of receivables and $8.0M of accrued liabilities, partly offset by $12.3M of payables and $11.8M of deferred revenue. H1 capex fell by half to $4.530M, but construction-in-process dropped $16.7M while the PP&E software line rose $17.8M, indicating capitalized SaaS projects were placed in service rather than a real spending cut. Trailing twelve-month free cash flow, derived from the disclosed periods, is $60.9M. Management's reported H1 free cash flow of $20.2M is $6.6M below the GAAP operating-cash-flow-less-capex arithmetic of $26.8M and the definition is not reconciled in the filings; neither figure should be annualized given the inventory and payables timing.

**The balance sheet is stronger than screens suggest, and the liquidity flexibility is weaker than the headline cash balance suggests.** At 6/30/26 cash plus marketable securities was $357.348M against $225M of convertible principal — net cash of roughly $132.3M. Screened current ratio collapses from 3.56 to 1.29 purely because $219.5M of convertible notes reclassified from long-term to current; excluding the notes it is 3.12x, essentially unchanged. Screened debt/equity of ~1.98 is total liabilities over equity, and $154.8M of those liabilities is deferred revenue — customer prepayments, not borrowings. Interest-bearing debt to equity is 0.95x gross and negative on a net basis. The notes carry no financial maintenance covenants.

The convertible is the live mechanical issue. The $225M 2.75% notes due April 2030 have a conversion price of $23.46, and as of June 29 the stock had satisfied the >130% test ($30.50) for the requisite 20 of 30 trading days, opening a **holder-elective conversion window from July 1 through September 30, 2026**. Full conversion is 9,590,783 shares — 13.2% of existing shares, or 11.7% post-issuance ownership dilution. A10 may settle in cash, shares, or a combination at its election; full cash settlement at $27.49 would require roughly $263.6M against $357.3M of liquid assets, leaving under $95M. The 10-Q itself warns this "could reduce the cash resources available to fund our other liquidity needs, including our stock repurchase program and quarterly cash dividend." With 3.7 years of remaining option value, most holders are unlikely to convert voluntarily at only 117% of the conversion price; the practical near-term probability of a large cash drain is low. But the option is theirs, not A10's, and the overhang is one reason program buybacks stopped.

**Capital allocation shifted abruptly.** FY2025 saw $68.9M of repurchases (3.7M shares at roughly $18.62) and $17.4M of dividends. In H1 2026 program repurchases were $0.317M — 16,708 shares at ~$18.96, all in Q1 — with zero in Q2 and the $53.077M remaining authorization unchanged across all three months. Dividends were $8.630M and $34.681M of cash went to TrojAI. Suspending buybacks at $27–36 after buying at $19 is defensible price discipline, and conserving cash into a live conversion window is prudent. It does remove a support that reduced share count in 2025.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue acceleration is real: 11.0% in FY2025, 14.5% in H1 2026, 15.5% in Q2. Guidance was raised twice, to 12–14% for FY2026, which on the $290.557M base implies $325.4M–$331.2M. Netting H1's $155.137M leaves H2 revenue of $170.3M–$176.1M against H2 2025's $155.037M — growth of **+9.8% to +13.6%**, a deceleration from H1's 14.5% at the low end and roughly in line at the high end. The earnings track record supports moderate confidence in the guide: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with beat magnitude compressing from 7.4% to 1.5%, and the Q2 non-GAAP EPS of $0.25 landing a penny below the $0.26 non-GAAP consensus.

The composition of growth is the problem. Enterprise revenue rose 72.9% in Q2 to $48.018M while service-provider revenue fell 22.8% to $32.119M, which reads as diversification — but Trivedi confirmed on the call that Microsoft has historically been classified as service provider, that no historical revenue was recategorized, and that Microsoft business is now appearing in enterprise "based on a completely different product set." Direct customer concentration worsened in every dimension. The mix shift and the concentration data measure different things: wallet-share expansion at one hyperscaler is real, and selling a second unrelated product line into an existing hyperscaler relationship carries genuine switching costs and technical stickiness. Customer diversification did not happen. Management asserts the business excluding these factors is "growing close to double digits"; that is not disclosed or verifiable from the filings.

Geographic momentum is negative outside the United States. Americas revenue rose 41% in H1 (U.S. +44%) while APJ fell 15% and EMEA fell 22%. Non-U.S. revenue went from 55% of total in FY2023 and FY2024 to 45% in FY2025 and roughly 36% in H1 2026. Management guided the service-provider business ex-Microsoft to only "slightly better than 2025," with Japan "equal or slightly worse" and Europe "neutral."

There is no forward visibility metric to corroborate the guide. Total remaining performance obligations of $154.826M at 6/30/26 are **identical** to total deferred revenue, bucket by bucket — meaning the disclosed RPO contains no material unbilled component and is overwhelmingly support renewals ($137.4M of services deferred revenue against an average maintenance term of ~21 months). RPO here is a billing figure, not an order book. One anomaly may be a leading indicator: product deferred revenue rose from $2.783M to $17.452M, +$14.669M, while services deferred revenue declined; TrojAI contributed only $0.2M, and the 10-Q's explanation ("timing of service contract bookings") does not fit the product line. A large term-license booking billed but not yet delivered is plausible, and $14.7M is about 4.5% of guided FY2026 revenue. It is unexplained.

Estimate revisions provide no support for an inflection thesis: current-year consensus is $1.04, up 0.7% over 90 days, next-year $1.19, down 0.1%, with the current quarter cut 1.2% over 30 days. Six analysts cover the name. This is a stable-estimate story, not a revision-momentum story, which matters because the fastest-and-strongest forward signal available on this name is neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

A10 competes in mature, contested categories: application delivery controllers and load balancing, DDoS mitigation, SSL inspection, and now cloud WAAP and AI security. The 10-K names Arbor Networks (NetScout), Radware, F5, NetScaler, VMware/Broadcom, Cisco, HPE, Fortinet, Akamai, Cloudflare, Imperva and AWS. Radware announced cloud-augmented DefensePro X protection on July 15 and was named a Leader in QKS Group's 2026 DDoS mitigation SPARK Matrix in August, corroborating that this is an active market. A10 is a mid-tier participant with 494 employees against competitors with tens of thousands.

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. On the positive side: 79%+ gross margin has held through a memory-cost squeeze, which indicates pricing power at the product level; the company has expanded from one product family to a second inside a hyperscaler, which is a real technical win; and TrojAI adds credible AI-model red-teaming and runtime capability, though management explicitly frames standalone "native AI solutions" as a one-to-two-year project. Trivedi's structural argument — that AI is erasing the distinction between how enterprises and service providers build networks, making A10's carrier-grade heritage an enterprise asset — is coherent and would be a genuine moat extension if it holds.

On the negative side: the Broadcom patent license permanently removes A10's ability to assert its IP against VMware; A10 has no U.S. government facility security clearance, which the 10-K says blocks certain government sales; APJ and EMEA share loss is visible in declining absolute revenue; and in AI security specifically Trivedi's stated differentiator is trust and scale relative to six-person startups, which is not a differentiator against Cloudflare, Fortinet or Cisco. Absent an ARR disclosure or a customer-count metric, there is no way to verify share gains outside the hyperscaler relationship.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer and channel concentration (primary).** Customer A moved from 23% of H1 2025 revenue to 38% of H1 2026, and from 30% to 38% of quarterly revenue. Top-ten end customers went from 42% to 55% (H1) and 46% to 58% (Q2). A single distribution channel is 40% of revenue and **57% of gross accounts receivable** — roughly $41M — against an allowance for credit losses of **$19 thousand**, down from $66 thousand. Large distributors are typically creditworthy, so this is not necessarily an error, but it is a single-counterparty credit exposure with effectively no reserve. The 10-Q states there have been "no material changes to the risk factors disclosed in the 2025 Annual Report" despite the largest customer's share rising 15 points and the notes becoming convertible; both are disclosed in the notes and MD&A, but Item 1A was not updated.

**The Microsoft warrant is an unresolved accounting exposure.** On August 3 A10 issued Microsoft a warrant for up to 800,000 shares at $0.01, vesting in two tranches based on purchases by Microsoft and its affiliates in measurement periods ending 6/30/2027 and 6/30/2028, exercisable from January 2028 and January 2029, expiring in 2036 or on acquisition of A10. Read literally: the vendor is granting equity to its buyer, conditional on the buyer purchasing more. Maximum dilution is small — 1.09% post-issuance. The P&L effect is not small and not knowable: A10 says it cannot yet estimate it. If treated as consideration payable to a customer under ASC 606 it reduces the transaction price as related sales occur; if measured at probability-weighted grant-date fair value under ASC 718/815 it could be materially below the ~$22M intrinsic value; it could also be classified as sales expense. The $22M figure is arithmetic on 800,000 shares at $27.49, not a booked amount. This is a genuine unquantified overhang on reported revenue and margin for FY2027–FY2028, and it is the kind of item that produces a guidance reset when finally quantified. The change-of-control expiry clause, combined with a transaction-heavy five-person board (activist Eric Singer, former Deutsche Bank tech M&A head Tor Braham, Summit Partners' Peter Chung) and a persistent 2019 strategic-alternatives risk factor, is suggestive of M&A optionality but is speculation, not evidence.

**Supply chain and input costs.** All finished goods come from Taiwan ODMs with no guaranteed capacity or pricing. Trivedi acknowledged the memory constraint "is expected to last for a while… many more quarters" and said A10 prioritizes delivery over cost if forced to choose. Gross margin has held at 79.1%, and the $13.8M inventory build with raw materials up 114% is consistent with pre-buying. The risk is a deferred margin problem in FY2027 as pre-bought inventory rolls off.

**Trade policy.** The 10-K discusses the January 20, 2026 *Learning Resources v. Trump* decision striking IEEPA tariffs and the pivot to Section 122 and Section 301 tools. The Q2 MD&A adds that "spending patterns remain uneven due to the unpredictable impact of trade policies, and we may need to implement tariff-related input cost increases." Management forecasts neither direction.

**Execution and controls.** The EVP of Sales and Marketing separated May 2, 2026 with no successor identified in the filings, at the same time the 10-Q names strengthening North American sales as a priority and Trivedi describes 6–9 month enterprise sales cycles with results "later this year, early next year." The 10-K discloses prior material weaknesses, a restatement, a securities class action and SEC investigations; controls were assessed effective at 6/30/26, but two purchase-price allocations (ThreatX and TrojAI) remain preliminary, and TrojAI put 83% of a $39.7M purchase price into goodwill.

**Governance and alignment.** CEO total compensation rose from $3.135M (2021) to $6.974M (2025), +122% over four years. The company-selected performance measure is 100-day VWAP stock price, which pays on multiple expansion as well as execution and was highly favorable in 2026 as the stock ran from ~$17 to $38. Adjusted EBITDA, another metric, excludes the SBC generated by the awards themselves. On the Q2 call management described "$6.7 million returned to shareholders" including $2.4M of shares withheld for employee tax obligations — technically defensible as treasury shares, but that cash went to tax authorities, and discretionary program buybacks in the quarter were zero. That overstates discretionary returns by 36%.

**Insider activity.** Selling only, no open-market purchases in the reviewed window: CEO Trivedi 100,000 shares across February 11–13 at ~$20.60 (~$2.06M), retaining ~888K shares; Director Singer 24,698 shares at $27.21 in May; General Counsel Weber 14,086 shares at $27.86 on August 11 under a 10b5-1 plan; Director Wolf adopted a plan for up to 10,608 shares. The presence of 10b5-1 plans and the poor timing of the CEO's February sales — the stock nearly doubled afterward — argue against reading these as informed pessimism. I treat the pattern as routine diversification, which is why the management-quality mark rests on compensation design, the capital-return characterization and the warrant negotiation rather than on Form 4s.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 53.9% against 36.2% one-year realized volatility is rich by 17.7 points, and the September tenor is the standout at 71.3% implied with an 18.5% put/call skew (the data provider characterizes elevated skew as elevated put demand) and a ±18.5% implied move. Term structure is flat across the front and back (53.9% near versus 53.8% at six months), so the priced uncertainty is not concentrated in a single date. Contradicting the skew reading, put/call volume is 0.01 and put/call open interest 0.12 — heavily call-tilted — and the chain is thin at 31 contracts across four expirations, so I would not build a directional case on this data. The delta snapshot's put/call OI collapse from 125.25 to 0.12 is almost certainly a data artifact rather than a positioning shift.

Two takeaways survive. First, options are expensive relative to realized volatility, which makes buying downside protection a poor way to express caution here and makes covered-call overwriting the more efficient de-risking route for holders who want to stay involved. Second, 14.9% short interest with 6.1 days to cover is a meaningful squeeze constraint on any short expression, and it means the bearish view is already partially crowded.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $38 (+38%)

A10 has converted a hyperscaler relationship from one product line into two, evidenced by an equity warrant that is worthless to Microsoft absent multi-year purchasing. AI traffic is genuinely changing network requirements (higher volume, burstier, more latency-sensitive, new threat surface), and A10's carrier-grade heritage becomes an enterprise asset as the two architectures converge. Guidance has been raised twice, gross margin held at 79% through a memory squeeze, and the cash-cost operating margin expanded roughly 200bp year over year once the mechanical SBC spike is set aside. TrojAI and ThreatX give a credible AI-security roadmap. The balance sheet carries $132M net cash, the dividend is covered five times over by free cash flow, and $53.1M of buyback authorization remains available if the stock stays weak. If FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches $1.30 and the market re-rates toward 29x — still below the peer median — the stock clears $38, back to its July high.

### Bear Case → $18 (−34.5%)

Thirty-eight percent of revenue depends on the procurement cycle of one hyperscaler, with no ARR, no unbilled backlog, and no disclosed revenue ex-Microsoft to gauge the underlying business. Hyperscaler infrastructure purchasing is project-based and lumpy; the $14.7M unexplained jump in product deferred revenue and Q1's acknowledged "pull forward dynamics with your largest customer" both hint that timing, not durable run-rate, is driving part of the growth. Meanwhile the two-thirds of the world outside the U.S. is shrinking 15–22%, the head of sales left with no successor at the exact moment North American enterprise execution became the priority, and one distributor holds 57% of receivables against a $19 thousand reserve. Memory costs are a deferred margin problem once pre-bought inventory clears. The Microsoft warrant, when quantified, may reduce reported revenue by low single digits for two years. GAAP earnings are already 46x trailing and non-GAAP relies on an add-back growing faster than revenue. A single quarter of hyperscaler order digestion at 23x forward earnings resets the multiple to high teens — precisely where the stock traded nine months ago at $16.52 with 11% growth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** This breaks a sequence of three HOLDs (5.6 → 5.6 → 5.0), and the justification is disclosure that arrived with the August 6 10-Q rather than the price decline itself. The stock is 28.6% below its July 9 high of $38.49, and a lower price on unchanged fundamentals would improve risk/reward. Fundamentals are not unchanged. Since the prior review, largest-customer concentration rose 8 points sequentially in the quarterly data and 15 points year over year, top-ten concentration rose 12 points, one distributor reached 57% of gross receivables, GAAP operating income fell 12.5% on 15.5% revenue growth, non-GAAP EPS missed by a penny, the implied H2 revenue growth rate decelerated to 9.8–13.6%, and the company granted equity to its largest customer with an admittedly unquantifiable financial effect. The guidance raise is a genuine offset. Net, the information set is adverse, and the multiple did not compress enough to price it.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into strength, targeting the $29–31 area if the stock retraces there on the September quarter's early momentum; do not wait for a specific date. Holders unwilling to sell outright should consider overwriting calls given 53.9% near-dated implied volatility against 36.2% realized. New capital should not be committed at $27.49. A partial position is defensible only for investors underwriting the M&A optionality implied by the board composition and the warrant's change-of-control expiry, which is speculation rather than a thesis.

**Base case $29 (+5.5%)** — 24–25x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.19. **Bull $38 (+38%)** — $1.30 at 29x. **Bear $18 (−34.5%)** — $0.90 at 20x, versus a 52-week low of $16.52.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better (any two):**
1. A10 discloses ARR, a recurring-revenue metric, or revenue excluding its largest customer, and the ex-largest-customer growth rate is confirmed at high single digits or better.
2. Largest-customer concentration stabilizes at or below 38% for two consecutive quarters *while* total revenue growth remains above 12% — i.e., growth broadens rather than deepens.
3. The Microsoft warrant's accounting treatment is quantified at an annual P&L impact below roughly 1% of revenue.
