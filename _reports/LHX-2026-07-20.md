**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

L3Harris is the sixth-largest U.S. defense prime, built from the 2019 Harris/L3 merger and the 2023 Aerojet Rocketdyne acquisition. It generates ~$21.9B of revenue with 75% derived from the U.S. Government, and it occupies genuinely scarce niches: tactical communications and electronic warfare (a 25%+ operating-margin franchise), missile-tracking satellites for the Golden Dome program, and solid rocket motors (a domestic duopoly with Northrop). Fundamental momentum is re-accelerating: Q1 FY26 revenue rose 12% YoY to $5.74B, diluted EPS rose 33% to $2.72, operating margin expanded to 11.4%, and backlog reached a record $40.7B, providing multi-year visibility.

Two idiosyncratic catalysts differentiate LHX from the legacy-prime peer group. First, the planned 2H 2026 IPO of Missile Solutions under the "Axyv" brand, backed by a $1.0B direct Pentagon investment into Aerojet Rocketdyne via convertible preferred and warrants, a first-of-its-kind industrial-base financing structure that monetizes a capital-heavy, lower-margin asset at a scarcity premium while retaining control. Second, the July 2026 Golden Dome awards ($955M for 18 missile-tracking satellites within a $1.75B AMDT3 program), a direct growth driver for the fast-expanding Space & Mission Systems segment.

The gap between the catalyst story and the numbers keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY. The stock trades at ~33x trailing and ~24x forward earnings against near-term estimate revisions that are flat-to-negative (current-quarter EPS revised -1.6% over 90 days, next-quarter -2.4%), sits ~26% below its March 2026 high of $379, and shows a five-year cumulative TSR (178) that lags the defense peer group (238). Fixed-price contract exposure (75% of revenue) produces recurring negative EAC adjustments, and the Axyv carve-out introduces valuation and execution risk that is unconfirmed until the IPO prices. The catalyst path warrants continued ownership; it does not yet justify max conviction at $282.

## Company Overview

L3Harris positions itself as "the Trusted Disruptor," a platform-agnostic merchant supplier and prime that moves faster and cheaper than the big-five primes (Lockheed, Northrop, RTX, General Dynamics, Boeing). It employs ~45,000 people including ~18,000 engineers and scientists. Effective FY2026, the company reorganized from four segments into three:

- **Space & Mission Systems (SMS):** Q1'26 revenue $2.99B (+24% YoY), 10.5% margin. Covers ISR, space systems (including Golden Dome missile-tracking satellites), maritime, FAA air-traffic modernization, and Intel & Cyber. Largest and fastest-growing segment, with $21.1B backlog.
- **Communication & Spectrum Dominance (CSD):** Q1'26 revenue $1.86B (+3%), a 25.1% operating margin. Tactical radios/waveforms, electronic warfare (Next-Gen Jammer), night-vision and integrated vision. This is the earnings engine.
- **Missile Solutions (MSL):** Q1'26 revenue $0.99B (+18%), 12.5% margin. The former Aerojet Rocketdyne — propulsion, solid rocket motors, munitions. Being carved out for the Axyv IPO.

L3Harris has taken an unusually collaborative posture for a legacy prime, striking partnerships with Palantir, Anduril, Shield Capital, and Amazon's Project Kuiper to fuse hardware, software, and AI. This positions it to participate in the acquisition-reform wave that is lowering barriers for non-traditional entrants.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** The multi-year trajectory shows $17.1B (FY22) → $19.4B (FY23) → $21.3B (FY24) → $21.9B (FY25), roughly 3% organic in FY25 after the CAS divestiture. FY25 operating margin was 9.7% (net margin 7.3%). Diluted FY25 EPS was $8.53. The margin story rests on the completed "LHX NeXt" cost program (now folded into ongoing "e3" operations); the consolidated ~10-11% margin understates the underlying mix because it is depressed by ~$700M+/year of intangible amortization from the L3 and Aerojet deals. CSD's ~25% margin is the true value core.

**Cash flow quality is strong.** FY25 operating cash flow was $3.1B (up from $2.56B), with FCF of $2.68B (12.3% margin). The Q1 seasonal cash outflow (-$95M OCF) is consistent with historical pattern and not a red flag. FCF conversion against net income is healthy for a defense prime.

**Backlog and visibility.** Backlog grew from $38.7B (end FY25) to $40.7B (end Q1'26), with ~40% converting within 12 months. Combined with the July Golden Dome awards, forward revenue visibility is solid.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets are $41.4B, but $20.0B goodwill plus $6.3B intangibles equals ~64% of assets, the legacy of roll-up M&A. Long-term debt is ~$10.4B (~$10.8B total fixed-rate, all unsecured), laddered with $1.8B in the current portion (3.85% 2026 and 5.40% 2027 notes). There are no revolver draws, $2.2B is available, and ratings are investment-grade. The Altman Z of 1.95 ("grey zone") is an artifact of the goodwill-heavy, leveraged structure typical of defense roll-ups, not operating distress; stable government cash flows, $2.68B FCF, and the record backlog confirm this.

**Capital allocation.** The company raised its dividend for the 25th consecutive year (to $1.25/quarter, ~$0.95B/year) and repurchased $1.2B (5.1M shares) in FY25, with $296M in Q1'26 and $1.9B remaining authorization. Pension de-risking transferred $1.4B of obligations to insurers in FY25 with no cash contribution needed. Capital allocation is balanced and disciplined.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is positive at the operational level and mixed at the estimate level, and the divergence is what governs the score.

**Operational momentum is accelerating.** Q1'26 revenue +12% and EPS +33% mark a clear step-up from the ~3% organic base of FY25. SMS grew 24% on classified-program ramps and international deliveries; MSL grew 18% on munitions-replenishment demand. The July Golden Dome awards feed directly into SMS, targeting a 2028 demonstration. The structural backdrop is favorable: the GFY26 defense appropriation ($859B, +1%), the GFY27 PBR seeking a ~$1.5T national-defense topline including reconciliation, NATO's 5%-of-GDP commitments, and Ukraine/Middle East munitions replenishment. Golden Dome, munitions, and shipbuilding are explicitly cited priorities in the July 2025 reconciliation bill.

**Estimate revisions do not yet corroborate the acceleration.** Consensus next-year EPS is revised only +1.4% over 90 days. Current-year EPS sits at $11.76, next-year at $13.63. This is not the +30%-in-90-days signal that would flag a systematic underestimation; it is a company delivering solid growth against estimates that are drifting sideways. The earnings-surprise record is reassuring (four straight beats, ranging 2.4%-10.3%), so consensus is not stretched, but the absence of positive revision velocity is the single most important reason this is not a BUY. Growth is real; the market is not yet marking it up.

The MSL carve-out adds a growth-optionality layer. Management (Bedingfield, now MSL President) has guided the business to more than double sales by end of decade, funded in part by the $1B Pentagon capacity investment into solid rocket motors for PAC-3, THAAD, Tomahawk, and Standard Missile. If that materializes, it accrues to L3Harris's retained controlling stake plus a valuation-crystallization event.

## Valuation Assessment

At $282, LHX trades at ~33x trailing EPS ($8.53) and ~24x forward on the $11.76 current-year consensus, with a 1.8% dividend yield. The market cap is ~$52.8B.

**Peer context is heterogeneous and of limited use as a single anchor.** The nearest-market-cap A&D peer set spans Rocket Lab (negative earnings), Elbit (73.9x), Axon (179.4x), Northrop (17.3x), and TransDigm (39.3x), a peer-median P/E of 73.9 that is not a meaningful benchmark given the mix of hyper-growth and roll-up names. The most relevant listed comparable in this set is Northrop at ~17.3x. On that basis LHX at ~24x forward carries a premium to NOC, which is defensible given LHX's faster top-line acceleration and its two idiosyncratic catalysts, but it is not a discount that indicates mispricing.

The forward multiple sits at ~24x while next-year EPS growth is consensus'd at ~16% ($11.76 → $13.63), implying a PEG near 1.5. That is a fair-to-slightly-full multiple, not a cheap one. A sum-of-the-parts case exists: post-MSL-IPO, RemainCo skews toward the higher-margin CSD (~25%) and SMS franchises, and the Axyv monetization at a scarcity premium (only two U.S. SRM producers) could crystallize value the consolidated multiple obscures. But that value depends on an unpriced, unconfirmed IPO in 2H 2026 at an unknown valuation.

The ~26% drawdown from the March high of $379 improves the risk/reward relative to that peak, but the decline coincided with softening near-term estimate revisions rather than pure sentiment, so it is not a clean case of a lower price on unchanged fundamentals. Consensus analyst targets (mean $378, median $387, low $285, high $443) imply upside, but the mean/median cluster near the March high and predate the recent estimate softening; they read as stale on the upside and are best treated as a plausible bull path rather than a base case. The valuation earns a middling score: not a value trap, not asymmetrically cheap, with the catalyst-driven re-rating case intact but unrealized.

## Competitive Landscape

L3Harris holds defensible positions in high-demand niches. CSD's tactical comms and EW franchise commands a 25%+ operating margin, reflecting genuine pricing power and mission-critical incumbency. In solid rocket motors, MSL is one of only two domestic producers alongside Northrop's Orbital ATK, a structural scarcity that the $1B Pentagon capacity investment reinforces. In space, the Golden Dome missile-tracking satellite wins position SMS as a favored supplier in the administration's top missile-defense priority.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than clearly widening. Acquisition reform (OTAs, the "Warfighting Acquisition System") cuts both ways: it lowers barriers for non-traditional entrants (Anduril, Ursa Major, Silvus) but also rewards L3Harris's faster/cheaper merchant-supplier positioning and its partnerships with the very disruptors that threaten legacy primes. The direct DoD equity stake in Missile Solutions signals that L3Harris is a favored industrial-base partner under the current procurement regime.

The offsetting data point is the peer-lagging five-year cumulative TSR (178 vs. the peer group's 238) despite a strong 2025: the market has been slow to reward the L3/Aerojet integration, which supports the re-rating optionality but also flags that share-of-value creation has trailed peers over the measurement window. This is why the competitive-position score sits solidly above midpoint but not in high-conviction territory.

## Risk Assessment

**Fixed-price and EAC risk.** With 75% of revenue fixed-price, cost overruns and inflation hit L3Harris directly. Negative EAC adjustments are a recurring swing factor: a $31M unfavorable adjustment on a legacy naval sensor program in Q1'26, and a $38M classified Maritime hit in FY25. These are the mechanism by which a margin-expansion thesis can slip.

**Government concentration and budget risk.** 75% U.S. Government exposure means shutdown and continuing-resolution risk is real (FY25 saw the longest shutdown on record at 43 days). Defense topline growth is a tailwind, but proposed NASA/NOAA cuts threaten civil-space and AR programs within SMS.

**Carve-out execution and valuation risk.** The Axyv IPO is the largest single execution risk. The $1B Pentagon investment structure is novel (convertible preferred + warrants, DoD ending <10%, converting at 80% of IPO price plus warrants for 3% at 110%), the IPO timing and valuation are unconfirmed and forward-looking, and Hudson Institute's Bryan Clark has flagged both the irony of buying Aerojet in 2023 to spin it out ~2.5 years later and the risk that SRM demand could "whipsaw." There is also a potential competitive-fairness question versus Northrop. The 10-K risk factors now explicitly call out the planned MSL IPO, and the 10-Q adds "unexpected issues related to the Department of War investment in our subsidiary" as a named risk.

**Goodwill impairment.** With ~$26.5B goodwill+intangibles, reporting-unit-level impairments could be material. FY25 tests passed with >40% clearance post-reorg, but an $85M AR goodwill charge was taken on the Space Technology disposal group, showing the risk is live.

**Environmental liabilities.** $657M accrued (largely Aerojet legacy Superfund/PFAS sites), $482M recoverable via contract pricing, a bounded but ongoing tail.

**Management transition risk.** A first-year public-company CFO (Kenneth Sharp) is running the parent during a complex carve-out, while the prior CFO (Bedingfield) runs the IPO candidate.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 31.6% versus 25.7% one-year historical volatility, rich by ~5.9 points, pricing elevated near-term uncertainty into the upcoming earnings window (last report was April 30; the next print is the near-term catalyst). The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 31.6% > long-term 28.0%), consistent with a specific event being priced. One-week put/call skew is elevated at +3.3% (near-term put demand), though this flattens and turns modestly negative at the six-month tenor (-2.8%), suggesting the hedging is event-specific rather than a structural bearish stance. IV has eased 2.4 points since late June on a roughly flat price. The signal is one of near-term caution around the earnings print, not a durable bearish tilt, consistent with a HOLD that waits for the print and estimate reaction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Revenue acceleration (Q1 +12%, EPS +33%) proves durable as classified and international programs ramp; CSD's 25% margin franchise carries mix higher post-MSL divestiture; the Axyv IPO crystallizes a scarcity-premium valuation on the SRM duopoly and unlocks sum-of-the-parts value the consolidated ~24x multiple obscures; Golden Dome and munitions replenishment sustain SMS/MSL growth through 2028; margin targets deliver and consensus estimates begin rising, driving a re-rating toward the peer group's historical TSR. Bull target ~$400-435, roughly consistent with the analyst high end and a full multiple on rising forward EPS.

**Bear Case.** Fixed-price EAC charges recur and compound, capping margin expansion; the Axyv IPO slips, prices poorly, or draws a competitive-fairness challenge; a goodwill impairment surfaces on the disposal groups; government shutdown/CR risk and NASA/NOAA cuts pressure SMS; estimates continue to drift lower and the ~24x forward multiple compresses toward NOC's ~17.3x. Bear target ~$245-260, roughly 8-13% below the current price.

The base case sits in between: solid delivery on backlog and the Golden Dome ramp, an Axyv IPO that prices reasonably but without a dramatic surprise, and estimates that stabilize and begin to inch higher. That supports a base target of ~$320-330, but the path there depends on estimate revisions turning positive, which they have not yet done.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 6-12 months, anchored to the next earnings print (near-term), the Axyv IPO in 2H 2026 (primary catalyst), and continued Golden Dome/AMDT3 award flow.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive — next-year EPS revised up >3% over a 90-day window, confirming the operational acceleration is being marked up by consensus.
- The Axyv IPO prices at or above expectations (a scarcity-premium valuation for the SRM franchise), crystallizing sum-of-the-parts value while L3Harris retains control.
- A pullback toward $270-285 with fundamentals intact, restoring a clearer margin of safety on the ~24x forward multiple.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Two consecutive quarters of negative EAC adjustments exceeding ~$50M combined, signaling the fixed-price margin thesis is breaking.
- A material goodwill impairment on the Space Technology or MSL disposal groups.
- Axyv IPO delay beyond 2027 or a competitive-fairness ruling that impairs the monetization, combined with next-year estimates revised down >5%.

**Current-level read:** With base-case upside of ~13-17% to $320-330 and bear-case downside of ~8-13% to $245-260, the risk/reward is roughly balanced rather than asymmetric. The catalyst path is real and specific, which is why this remains a growth-oriented hold rather than a source-of-funds; the entry does not yet clear the bar for adding, and the sector carries a structural alpha headwind that demands a company-specific catalyst be confirmed, not merely anticipated.

## Conclusion

L3Harris is a high-quality defense prime with accelerating fundamentals, a record backlog, and two differentiating catalysts: the Axyv/Missile Solutions IPO with a $1B Pentagon anchor investment, and the Golden Dome satellite awards. The business is financially sound, cash-generative, and disciplined with capital. What holds it back from a BUY is the disconnect between the operational story and the estimate reality: near-term consensus is drifting sideways to lower, the forward multiple (~24x, PEG ~1.5) is fair rather than cheap, and the marquee catalyst (Axyv) is unconfirmed on timing and valuation. The recent score trajectory (6.4 → 6.0) and repeated HOLD calls reflect this stall: a company delivering results the market has not yet chosen to re-rate.

Recommendation: **HOLD (growth_hold).** Own it for the catalyst path — an Axyv IPO that crystallizes scarcity value and a Golden Dome/munitions-driven top line — but wait for estimate revisions to turn positive or for a better entry near $270-285 before adding. The near-term signal to watch is whether the next earnings print and the ensuing consensus reaction convert operational momentum into rising forward estimates.
