**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bio-Rad Laboratories is best understood as two distinct entities bolted together: a ~$2.6B-revenue life-science and clinical-diagnostics operating business, and a passive ~38% ordinary / 28% preference equity stake in the German bioprocess company Sartorius AG. The Sartorius holding was worth $5.67B at year-end 2025 and fell to $4.90B by the end of Q1 2026. Against a total market capitalization of roughly $6.6B, the stake alone represents 74–86% of BIO's equity value, meaning the market is ascribing only $1–2B (roughly 1x sales) to a diagnostics-and-tools franchise generating around $375M of free cash flow. Reported GAAP earnings are dominated by mark-to-market swings on Sartorius and carry essentially no signal about the operating business: net income has whipsawed from +$4.25B (2021) to −$3.63B (2022) to +$760M (2025), and Q1 2026 posted a −$527M net loss on a $738M Sartorius mark even as operating income rose year-over-year.

The central investment question is whether BIO's persistent net-asset-value discount ever closes. A sum-of-parts calculation (~$4.9–5.7B Sartorius plus ~$1.5B net cash and investments, less $1.2B debt) leaves the operating business valued at a steep discount, or the holding company as a whole trading well below the value of its parts. The market applies this discount for concrete reasons: BIO has no board seat at, and no access to the financials of, Sartorius (it explicitly states it "tried and failed" to obtain information for equity-method accounting); German capital-gains tax would leak on any sale; the Schwartz family's dual-class control blocks any forced unlock; and the underlying operations are deteriorating. Management's Investment Company Act disclosure that it "may modify its position in Sartorius" is the single most important sentence in the filings, and it hints at eventual partial monetization, but there is no timeline, no announced plan, and family control is the structural obstacle.

We rate BIO a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The operating business is shrinking on a currency-neutral basis (Q1 2026 revenue −4.2%, masked by a weak-dollar FX tailwind that produced +1.1% reported), consensus EPS has been cut roughly 12% for the current year over 90 days, and margins are compressing. The NAV discount limits downside, but without a catalyst to unlock it, the discount can persist indefinitely while the operating drag and falling estimates weigh on sentiment. This has been a HOLD for four consecutive reports as the score declined from 6.1 to 4.8; the new data confirms rather than reverses that trajectory.

## Company Overview

Bio-Rad is a ~75-year-old, family-controlled company headquartered in Hercules, California, with roughly 7,450 employees across 37 countries and more than 12,000 products sold through two segments:

- **Clinical Diagnostics (~60% of sales)**: reagents, instruments, software, and quality controls supporting over 300 in-vitro diagnostic tests, spanning quality control, blood typing and transfusion medicine, diabetes/HbA1c monitoring, and immunology. Revenue is largely recurring on a razor/razor-blade model — labs standardize on installed instruments and buy consumables per test. Customers include hospital, reference, transfusion, and physician-office labs. Addressable market ~$16B.
- **Life Science (~40% of sales)**: instruments and reagents for separating and quantifying proteins, cells, and nucleic acids, including process chromatography, Droplet Digital PCR (ddPCR), food safety, and western blotting. Customers are universities, government agencies, and biopharma. Addressable market ~$19B.

Geographically, revenue splits roughly 40% US / 60% international, with Europe the largest overseas region. BIO competes against far larger, better-capitalized players — Thermo Fisher, Danaher, Roche, Abbott, Siemens Healthineers, BD, Merck KGaA, QuidelOrtho, bioMérieux, and Qiagen — differentiating on niches (ddPCR, process chromatography, QC, blood typing) rather than scale.

The defining feature of the company is the Sartorius stake, which dominates reported results and, for most investors, the equity value. Owning BIO is primarily a leveraged, tax-inefficient, illiquid bet on Sartorius with an operating business attached at a low implied multiple.

## Financial Analysis

**The Sartorius stake dominates GAAP earnings.** The single-quarter decline in the holding drove the −$527M Q1 2026 net loss. GAAP net income has been essentially noise: +$4.25B (2021), −$3.63B (2022), −$637M (2023), −$1.84B (2024), +$760M (2025). Nearly all of that swing is unrealized mark-to-market on Sartorius. Trailing GAAP EPS of $27.85 and a headline P/E of ~48.8x are therefore analytically meaningless; the correct earnings gauge is non-GAAP EPS and segment operating income.

**The underlying operating business is stagnant-to-declining.** Revenue moved $2.80B (2022) → $2.67B (2023) → $2.57B (2024) → $2.58B (2025), essentially flat-to-down for three years. FY2025 grew just +0.7% reported and was roughly flat currency-neutral. Q1 2026 was weak, with Life Science down 4.3% currency-neutral on academic-research weakness and Clinical Diagnostics down 4.1% currency-neutral, hit by Middle East conflict disrupting EMEA logistics and demand.

**Margins are compressing.** Consolidated gross margin fell from 53.7% (2024) to 51.9% (2025). FY2025 GAAP operating income collapsed to $47.2M from $269M in 2024, driven by $172.8M of in-process R&D impairments — Dropworks ($81.7M) and Curiosity Diagnostics ($91.1M net), both failed/discontinued acquisitions. Non-GAAP EPS is drifting lower: H1 2025 $5.15 vs. $5.39 in H1 2024; Q2 2025 $2.61 vs. $3.11.

**The operating balance sheet is solid.** Current ratio 5.6x, debt/equity 0.42, Altman Z 2.95 (grey zone, but distorted by the enormous Sartorius asset base). Cash and short-term investments were $1.56B at 3/31/26. Debt consists of $1.2B in senior notes — a 3.3% tranche due March 2027 (now reclassified to current) and a 3.7% tranche due 2032. Management says it can repay the $400M 2027 maturity from cash but may refinance at higher rates. Free cash flow was ~$375M in 2025 (14.5% margin), with capital allocation tilted heavily to buybacks: $295.5M repurchased in 2025 (1.2M shares) and $203.6M in 2024, with $237.1M remaining authorized at Q1 2026. There is no dividend. Basic share count has shrunk from ~29.8M (2022) to ~27M (2025).

The operating business generates real cash and carries manageable leverage, but it is not growing, and its cash flow is being deployed into buybacks and serial M&A with a poor hit rate.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest part of the story. Both segments contracted currency-neutral in Q1 2026. Life Science is over-indexed to academic and government research funding, which faced 2025 US federal budget pressure, and to weak biopharma and small-biotech capex. Clinical Diagnostics, which should provide a recurring, defensive base, has been undercut by China diabetes reimbursement cuts and now EMEA/Middle East disruption that management explicitly guided to persist "through the rest of 2026."

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 28.7% over 30 days and 30.9% over 90 days; current-year EPS is down 12.3% over 90 days to $9.00; next-year EPS is down 14.3% over 90 days to $9.72. This is negative earnings momentum across every window. The earnings surprise record is mixed: after two large beats (Q2 2025 +49%, Q3 2025 +16%), the last two quarters missed (Q4 2025 −8.0%, Q1 2026 −5.7%), signaling deteriorating visibility.

The Stilla Technologies acquisition (closed June 30, 2025, $257.7M including up to $50M contingent) expands ddPCR and drove the QX Continuum and QX700 launches, but ddPCR is exposed to the same academic and biopharma softness that is pressuring the segment, and BIO's M&A track record does not inspire confidence that this bet accretes as planned. There is no visible near-term operating growth catalyst; the growth thesis, if one exists, lives entirely in the Sartorius NAV discount, not in the operating business.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is genuinely two-sided and difficult to score, because the peer P/E table is not comparable — several Life Sciences Tools peers show negative or triple-digit earnings multiples (AVTR −11.9x, CRL −50.0x, TECH 77.8x, AZTA −9.3x, MEDP 29.9x), reflecting sector-wide down-cycle distress. Applying a trailing or forward P/E to BIO is meaningless given the Sartorius mark distorts GAAP earnings.

The correct framework is sum-of-parts:
- Sartorius stake: ~$4.90B (as of 3/31/26)
- Net cash and investments: ~$1.56B cash + short-term investments, less $1.2B debt ≈ +$0.36B net, plus the €400M SHB loan asset (fair value $359M)
- Operating business: $2.58B revenue, ~$300M EBITDA, ~$375M FCF

Against a $6.6B market cap, the Sartorius stake plus net cash roughly accounts for the entire equity value, implying the market ascribes little-to-nothing incremental for a cash-generative operating business. On its face, that is a wide discount. The problem is that the discount is structurally justified and has proven persistent: BIO has no control over or insight into Sartorius, German capital-gains tax leaks on any disposal, and the Schwartz family's dual-class control prevents an outside party from forcing a wind-down. A discount with no catalyst can persist for years, and BIO's five-year TSR of ~52 versus a ~105 peer group shows the discount has widened, not closed, as the stock roughly halved relative to peers.

On the operating business alone, ~1x sales for a franchise with recurring diagnostics revenue and 50%+ gross margins looks cheap in isolation, but it is cheap for reasons: the business is shrinking currency-neutral, margins are compressing, and estimates are falling. This is the classic value-trap configuration — a low apparent multiple reflecting genuine deterioration rather than mispricing. Cheapness alone is not a reason to own it; a catalyst is required, and none is identifiable with a timeline.

Analyst price targets cluster tightly around the current price (low $275, mean $297, median $300, high $320), with sentiment degrading and the single strong-buy rating removed in the past month. There is no consensus that a re-rating is imminent. We score valuation a neutral 5.0: the sum-of-parts discount provides a floor, but the absence of a catalyst and the eroding operating trend prevent a higher mark.

## Competitive Landscape

Bio-Rad is a mid-cap operating in a scale-driven oligopoly dominated by much larger, better-capitalized competitors. It competes on niche differentiation — ddPCR, process chromatography, quality controls, blood typing — rather than breadth or cost. The 10-K flags that multi-year public tenders increasingly favor aggressive competitor pricing and local-manufacturing requirements (China, India, Russia) that BIO may not meet.

The competitive trajectory is negative. The entire Life Sciences Tools sector has been in a post-COVID down-cycle — destocking, weak biopharma capex, US academic funding pressure, and China weakness — but BIO is a relative laggard even within the weak group. Its Life Science segment is more exposed to the academic and ddPCR softness than diversified peers, and its Clinical Diagnostics recurring base has not provided the offset the model implies, undercut by China diabetes reimbursement and now EMEA/Middle East disruption. The five-year TSR gap indicates share and multiple erosion relative to the group. The prior report cut the competitive-position score from 6.5 to 4.0, and the Q1 2026 currency-neutral decline in both segments confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it. We hold the score at 4.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Sartorius concentration is the central risk.** BIO's reported earnings and its share price move on a single German stock over which it has no board seat and no access to financials. A Sartorius decline crushes GAAP results independent of operations, as the Q1 2026 mark-driven loss demonstrated. This is undiversifiable single-name equity risk embedded in the balance sheet.

**Investment Company Act (1940) risk.** Because the Sartorius stake is so large relative to the whole company, BIO could be deemed an unregistered investment company. It relies on the Section 3(b)(1) "primarily engaged in another business" exemption and has discussed the matter with SEC staff, but warns an adverse determination "would have a material adverse effect" and "could call into question the validity of all contracts." The disclosure that BIO "may modify its position in Sartorius" is both the key catalyst hint and a genuine regulatory overhang.

**The €400M SHB loan / value-appreciation right.** A Level 3, collateralized €400M loan (fair value $359M at 3/31/26, due January 2029) tied to Sartorius trust interests, plus embedded value-appreciation rights. The collateral — untradeable trust interests that convert to Sartorius ordinary shares upon trust termination around mid-2028 — may be worth less than the loan if Sartorius declines, effectively doubling down on the same single-name exposure with added valuation-model uncertainty.

**End-market softness** across US government/academic research funding cuts, weak biopharma and small-biotech, China diabetes reimbursement cuts, and Middle East conflict disrupting EMEA — the last newly emphasized in Q1 2026 as an active headwind guided to persist through 2026.

**Serial M&A/impairment risk.** The 10-K candidly admits acquisition benefits "may prove to be less than anticipated, which we have experienced." The Dropworks and Curiosity write-offs validate that; Stilla is the newest bet and raises the bar for capital-allocation credibility.

**Governance and control.** Schwartz family dual-class control (Class B one vote, Class A one-tenth vote) entrenches management, blocks a change of control, and means Class A holders elect only 25% of directors. This is the structural obstacle to any activist-driven NAV unlock.

**Debt maturity.** The $400M 2027 note is now current; liquidity is ample, but refinancing would be at rates above the 3.3% coupon in a higher-for-longer environment.

Most of these risks are known and partially priced, but the combination of single-name concentration, regulatory overhang, and a family structure that prevents unlock is not fully symmetric — the downside catalysts (further Sartorius decline, adverse Investment Company Act determination, continued operating erosion) are more identifiable than the upside catalysts (monetization). We score risk 4.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. One-month ATM implied volatility is 47.1% against 41.4% one-year historical vol (a rich 5.6-point spread), with a 65th-percentile IV reading and an elevated put/call skew of 7.6%, implying a ±12.2% move. The term structure is in backwardation (47.1% near-term versus 38.9% at six months), consistent with the market pricing an imminent event — most likely the next earnings print and continued Sartorius sensitivity. Short interest is negligible (220 shares), so this is genuine hedging demand rather than a crowded short. The elevated near-term put demand reinforces the cautious stance and argues against stepping in ahead of the next catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The wide NAV discount eventually closes through partial monetization of Sartorius. Management's Investment Company Act language signals this is on the table, and the setup — a passive stake worth roughly the entire market cap plus an operating business at ~1x sales — is precisely what attracts activists and sum-of-parts value investors. If Sartorius recovers from its recent decline and BIO begins to monetize or clarify its intentions, the discount could narrow meaningfully. The operating business, while stagnant, generates ~$375M FCF, carries a strong balance sheet, and would inflect if the sector down-cycle ends. Buybacks at a discount to NAV are accretive. A bull scenario could see the stock re-rate toward $360–400 as Sartorius recovers and/or a partial unlock is announced.

### Bear Case
Sartorius continues to decline, dragging GAAP results and sentiment lower, while the operating business keeps shrinking currency-neutral with compressing margins and falling estimates. The family control structure means no unlock ever comes, so the discount persists indefinitely, as it has for five years. Continued academic, China, and EMEA weakness pressures both segments; M&A write-offs recur; and the Investment Company Act overhang lingers. In this scenario the stock drifts toward $240–260, near the 52-week low, as estimates and Sartorius both weaken.

The base case sits between: the discount neither closes nor blows out, the operating business muddles along near flat currency-neutral, Sartorius stabilizes at a lower level, and the stock trades in a range around $280–305 with no clear directional catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence does not support a confident call in either direction. The NAV discount caps downside, but the deteriorating operating trend, falling estimates, and absence of an unlock catalyst cap upside. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the new data confirms the declining score trajectory (6.1 → 4.8) rather than reversing it — the Q1 2026 currency-neutral −4.2% decline, the 12% current-year estimate cut, and the removal of the sole strong-buy rating all validate the caution.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A concrete, announced plan to monetize or partially reduce the Sartorius stake, or resolution of the Investment Company Act question in a way that unlocks NAV.
- Currency-neutral operating revenue returning to positive growth for two consecutive quarters alongside stabilizing or rising EPS estimates.
- A Sartorius recovery combined with a demonstrated narrowing of the holding-company discount.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse Investment Company Act determination or material escalation of that regulatory risk.
- Continued currency-neutral revenue decline below −5% with further margin compression, signaling structural rather than cyclical impairment of the operating business.
- A dilutive or covenant-straining refinancing of the 2027 notes, or evidence the SHB loan collateral is impaired.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (roughly late July 2026) for operating trend and estimate direction, and ongoing monitoring of Sartorius share price and any 8-K disclosure regarding the stake or the Investment Company Act position.

## Conclusion

Bio-Rad is a holding company whose reported earnings tell you almost nothing about its operating business and whose equity value is dominated by a passive, illiquid, tax-inefficient stake in Sartorius that it neither controls nor can readily monetize. The sum-of-parts discount is wide and structurally justified, and it has proven persistent. Beneath the Sartorius overlay, the operating business is shrinking on a currency-neutral basis, its margins are compressing, and consensus estimates have been cut across every window in the past 90 days. There is a genuine value argument buried in the NAV discount, but value without a catalyst is a trap, and the family dual-class structure is the specific obstacle preventing that catalyst from arriving on any predictable schedule.

We maintain a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The discount limits downside, but the deteriorating fundamentals and absent unlock catalyst limit upside equally, and the options market is pricing near-term stress. We would turn constructive on a concrete Sartorius monetization signal or a genuine currency-neutral operating inflection, and negative on an adverse Investment Company Act outcome or further structural erosion of the core business.
