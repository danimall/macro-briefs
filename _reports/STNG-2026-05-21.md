## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers is generating earnings at a rate that makes its current valuation appear disconnected from fundamentals. With Q2 2026 pool/spot rates of $96,000/day on LR2s and $66,000/day on MRs — roughly double the Q1 averages that already produced an earnings beat — consensus estimates for 2026 have been revised materially upward. At $81.68, the stock trades at an attractive multiple to current-year earnings with a net cash balance sheet of approximately $479M (~$9.50/share based on 50.42M shares outstanding).

The structural thesis rests on three pillars: (1) Middle East conflict has materially increased ton-mile demand by forcing refined product trades onto longer routes, (2) the product tanker orderbook remains historically constrained with shipyard capacity limiting new supply through 2027-2028, and (3) management is executing a disciplined capital recycling strategy — selling older vessels at peak prices, issuing 1.75% convertible debt, and buying back equity below NAV. The $500M buyback authorization represents approximately 12% of market cap.

The primary risk is geopolitical resolution. If the Hormuz disruption normalizes, rates could compress 50-60% within quarters. Diplomatic progress has been limited, and the EIA's May 2026 outlook incorporates continued risk premium from regional disruption, though it assumes gradual traffic resumption may begin later in 2026. The balance of probabilities favors continued elevated rates through much of H2 2026, though investors should monitor diplomatic developments closely.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers operates 87 product tankers across three classes: 32 LR2s (~110,000 DWT), 41 MRs (~50,000 DWT), and 14 ice-class Handymax vessels (~38,700 DWT). The fleet averages 10.2 years of age with extensive scrubber installations that provide an estimated fuel cost advantage on compliant routes that varies with the HSFO/VLSFO spread.

The company operates through related-party commercial pools managed by Scorpio Commercial Management S.A.M. headquartered in Monaco. STNG is incorporated in the Marshall Islands and listed on the NYSE. It has diversified into VLCCs with two 300,000 DWT newbuildings ordered from Hanwha Ocean for H2 2028 delivery.

- Net cash: $395.3M
- Pro-forma (post-April convertibles, as of May 1): ~$479M net cash, $2.1B total liquidity

**Post-May 12 Capital Structure Update:** Following the May 12 additional convertible note issuance ($230M including greenshoe exercise), total convertible notes outstanding are approximately **$605M** at 1.75% coupon. The concurrent $55M share repurchase partially offsets dilution. Gross debt post-May offerings is approximately $1.17B, though the company remains in a net cash position given its $1.4B+ unrestricted cash.

**Q2 2026 Implied Economics:**
At disclosed Q2 pool/spot rates ($96K/day LR2, $66K/day MR, $61K/day Handymax), the fleet's spot-exposed vessels are generating exceptional returns. These are pool/spot-market TCE rates and do not apply to the entire fleet. The company also has vessels on time charter at significantly lower rates (LR2: $30,300/day, MR: $26,500/day, Handymax: $23,000/day) and one MR on bareboat at $12,986/day. If pool/spot rates persist through June for the spot-exposed portion of the fleet, Q2 adjusted EBITDA could reach the $350-400M range, though this remains a sensitivity estimate subject to coverage changes, voyage timing, and pool result finalization.

**Cash Flow Generation:** At current spot rates, the annualized FCF yield is substantial. Even normalizing to the Q1 rate environment ($37,700/day fleet-weighted), FCF yield is approximately 12-14%. The company is generating cash faster than it can deploy it through buybacks and dividends.

**Capital Structure:** The 1.75% convertible notes ($605M total) are extremely cheap capital. The conversion price of $100.39 is 23% above current price. If rates remain elevated and the stock appreciates to $100+, dilution occurs — but at a price where equity holders have already realized substantial gains. If rates decline and the stock stays below conversion, STNG retains near-zero cost debt.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term (Q2-Q4 2026):** Estimate revisions have been strongly positive. Current-year and current-quarter estimates have been revised significantly upward in recent months, reflecting the dramatic acceleration in Q2 spot rates. The pace of upward revisions is among the most extreme positive revision momentum in any sector.

**Structural demand drivers:**
- Middle East conflict forcing refined product sourcing from distant markets (increasing ton-miles per barrel moved)
- New refinery capacity in Middle East/India exporting to consuming regions far from production
- Product tanker orderbook/fleet ratio below historical averages
- Shipyard capacity constrained through 2027, limiting new supply response

**Fleet strategy:** Management is actively selling 15 older vessels (approximately $723M in gross sale proceeds) at peak asset values while ordering **10 newbuildings** (4 LR2s, 4 MRs, and 2 VLCCs) for 2027-2029 delivery. This is counter-cyclical fleet renewal — selling high, ordering for delivery when supply will be even tighter. Remaining newbuilding commitments total $641.5M through 2029.

**Longer-term (2027+):** The VLCC entry adds optionality if crude tanker rates recover, though it represents a meaningful capital commitment in a segment where STNG has no operating history.

## Valuation Assessment

STNG's valuation is compelling on multiple metrics, though precise forward P/E depends on which consensus estimate source is used:

**Valuation framework:**
- Price-to-book: ~1.21x ($3.41B equity / 50.42M shares outstanding = ~$67.6 book value per share). At $81.68, the stock trades at a modest premium to book.
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 4.25x on annualized Q1 EBITDA (~$856M). Using pro-forma net cash including pending vessel-sale proceeds, this compresses toward ~3.8x.
- Net cash per share: approximately $9.50 on the May 1 net cash figure of $479M and 50.42M shares outstanding.
- NAV: With $2.1B liquidity, $2.49B vessel book value (which likely understates market values given 10.2-year average fleet), and $1.17B gross debt post-May offerings, tangible book value per share is approximately $67.6. Market values of vessels at current charter rates would add meaningful upside.
- Analyst targets: Bank of America raised its price target to $100 (May 7, 2026); Jefferies initiated at approximately $84.79 (April 24, 2026). The convertible conversion price of $100.39 clusters around the same level as BofA's target.

The stock is priced for significant rate normalization that hasn't materialized — and based on current Q2 spot data, won't materialize this quarter.

**Why the market may be wrong:** Tanker stocks persistently trade at low P/Es because investors assume cyclical peaks are transitory. The difference in this cycle is that the supply constraint (low orderbook + shipyard capacity) coincides with a structural demand increase (longer voyages from conflict-driven trade rerouting). Each week that passes without resolution generates substantial cash that either gets returned to shareholders or used to buy back shares.

## Competitive Landscape

STNG operates one of the three largest publicly traded product tanker fleets globally alongside International Seaways (INSW) and Torm (TRMD). Competitive advantages:

1. **Fleet scale and diversity:** 87 vessels across 3 classes provides maximum commercial flexibility
2. **Scrubber penetration:** Majority of LR2s and newer MRs fitted with scrubbers, providing a fuel cost advantage that varies with the HSFO/VLSFO price spread
3. **Ice-class capability:** 14 Handymax vessels with 1A ice ratings access premium Baltic/Arctic trades
4. **Balance sheet strength:** Net cash position vs. most peers carrying net debt
5. **Fleet age:** 10.2-year average is below the global product tanker fleet median

The competitive position is strengthening as STNG sells older tonnage at peak values while peers hold aging fleets that will require replacement at higher newbuilding prices. The VLCC entry broadens the addressable market, though execution risk exists in a segment where STNG has no operating history.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical resolution (HIGH IMPACT, UNCERTAIN PROBABILITY):** A comprehensive Middle East peace or Hormuz reopening would likely compress rates 50-60% over 2-3 quarters. Diplomatic progress has been limited to date, but the EIA's May 2026 base forecast assumes gradual Hormuz traffic resumption beginning around June 2026 and recovery toward pre-conflict levels later in the year. This conflicts with the bull case of sustained disruption through H2 2026. Investors should treat the geopolitical premium as potentially time-limited rather than structural.

**Rate cyclicality:** Product tanker rates have historically shown mean-reversion over 3-5 year cycles. At $66-96K/day, current spot rates are 2-3x mid-cycle levels. Even at mid-cycle rates ($30-40K/day), STNG generates meaningful EPS.

**Related-party pool governance:** Revenue allocation through SCM-managed pools creates potential conflicts of interest. Investors rely on board oversight of terms that are not fully disclosed.

**VLCC execution risk:** Two $150M+ vessels in an unfamiliar segment with 2028 delivery is a meaningful capital commitment with uncertain returns.

**Convertible dilution:** $605M in converts at $100.39 conversion price = approximately **6.0M potential diluted shares (~12% dilution)** above $100, before considering the company's option for treasury-settlement. This effectively caps upside unless rates remain extraordinarily elevated for multiple years.

**Demand growth uncertainty:** The EIA projects global oil demand increasing by only 0.2M b/d in 2026, well below earlier forecasts, though a rebound of 1.5M b/d is expected in 2027. Slowing demand growth could weigh on sentiment even if ton-mile demand remains elevated from longer voyages.

**Russia's energy incentives:** While Russia's May 2026 oil-and-gas revenue was estimated up approximately 39% YoY for that single month, its January-May cumulative oil/gas revenue was reportedly down roughly one-third YoY. The incentive structure around conflict perpetuation is more complex than a single month's revenue suggests.

**G&A cost inflation:** G&A expenses rose 37% YoY (from $28.5M to $39.1M in Q1), primarily driven by compensation costs, in a quarter where the fleet shrank from 99 to 91 vessels.

### Options Market Signal

Put/call ratios indicate institutional hedging activity, likely around earnings and the recent price decline from $87 highs. The flat term structure indicates uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a specific event. Short interest remains modest and uncrowded.

## Investment Thesis

- Q2 pool/spot rates sustain at $66-96K/day through summer, producing exceptional quarterly earnings
- Conflict persists, rates remain elevated through 2027
- $500M buyback executes aggressively, retiring meaningful share count

- EPS declines to $4-5 normalized (still above historical averages due to scrubber advantage)

- VLCC newbuildings deliver into weak crude market

### Base Case ($95-100, +16% to +22%)
- Conflict persists through 2026 but rates moderate slightly from Q2 peaks
- Full-year 2026 EPS: $10-13 (range reflects uncertainty in consensus estimates)
- Buybacks reduce share count by 3-5%
- Stock converges toward analyst consensus and convertible conversion price

## Conclusion

STNG is a high-quality cyclical trading at a valuation that assumes rapid earnings normalization not yet supported by current spot rates or supply/demand fundamentals. Consecutive earnings beats, a net cash balance sheet, approximately 12% of market cap authorized for buybacks, and 10 newbuildings ordered for delivery at attractive cycle timing create a setup where the stock can appreciate meaningfully even if rates moderate from current peaks.

The geopolitical risk appears compensated at current valuation levels with ~$9.50/share in net cash. Management's "sell high, borrow cheap, buy back" strategy is intelligent capital allocation for this point in the cycle. The stock has appreciated 96% over the past year, largely justified by the earnings trajectory (3x YoY EPS growth) rather than multiple expansion. BUY with position sizing of 2-3% given commodity cyclicality and binary geopolitical risk.
