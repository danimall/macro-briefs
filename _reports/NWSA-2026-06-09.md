**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

News Corp emerged from FY2025 a structurally cleaner company than it has been in a decade. The April 2025 sale of the capital-intensive Foxtel pay-TV business to DAZN removed the segment that had long depressed margins and obscured the underlying quality of the portfolio. On a continuing-operations basis, revenue grew 2% to $8.45B, total segment EBITDA rose 14% to $1,415M, free cash flow reached $571M, and the parent carries roughly zero net debt ($1,962M of parent debt against $2.4B of cash). The growth engine is Dow Jones, where the B2B information franchise — Risk & Compliance (+15%), Energy/OPIS (+11%), and Factiva — competes against Reuters, LexisNexis, and S&P Global in sticky, high-margin enterprise subscriptions. Management has set a five-year target of $1B Dow Jones segment EBITDA (versus $588M in FY25), partly underpinned by emerging AI content-licensing revenue.

The investment case has two genuine pillars and one persistent obstacle. The pillars are the Dow Jones B2B compounder and the sum-of-the-parts argument: the 61.4% stake in ASX-listed REA Group alone is worth a large fraction of the entire $9.8B market capitalization, implying the market assigns little value to Dow Jones, HarperCollins, and News Media. The obstacle is that this discount has persisted for years because of the dual-class structure that concentrates voting control in the Murdoch Family Trust (~41% of Class B votes) and by-laws that explicitly permit directing corporate opportunities to Fox Corp. The late-2025 resolution of the family succession dispute in Lachlan Murdoch's favor removes a control-change uncertainty but entrenches the very structure that keeps the discount in place.

We rate NWSA a HOLD. Despite a sound business and credible long-term growth in Dow Jones, three concrete factors prevent a higher-conviction call: near-term consensus EPS estimates are falling (next-quarter cut 10.7% over 90 days; the data characterizes the revision trend as "negative earnings momentum"), the SOTP discount has no identifiable near-term catalyst to close, and the options market is pricing imminent stress (near-term implied volatility of 50.9% against 24.5% realized, with a steep backwardation). This has been a four-quarter BUY thesis that has produced a roughly flat share price over twelve months; the bar to continue adding should be higher than the bar to keep holding.

## Company Overview

News Corp is a diversified global media and information-services company reporting in five segments after the Foxtel divestiture (fiscal year ends June 30):

| Segment | FY25 Revenue | FY25 Seg. EBITDA | % of Revenue | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dow Jones | $2,331M | $588M | 28% | WSJ, Barron's, MarketWatch; B2B Risk & Compliance, Energy/OPIS, Factiva |
| Digital Real Estate | $1,802M | $601M | 21% | 61.4% of REA Group (Australia); 80% of Move/Realtor.com (US) |
| Book Publishing | $2,149M | $296M | 25% | HarperCollins, #2 global consumer publisher |
| News Media | $2,170M | $153M | 26% | News Corp Australia, News UK (The Sun, The Times), NY Post |
| Other (corporate) | — | $(223)M | — | Overhead + UK Newspaper Matters legal costs |

The competitive core is proprietary, trusted content and data: the Dow Jones B2B franchise sells benchmark pricing (OPIS energy) and compliance data into enterprise contracts that are difficult to displace; REA Group holds a near-monopoly position in Australian residential property portals; and HarperCollins owns a deep, monetizable backlist (64% of consumer revenue). Realtor.com is the structural weak link — a perennial #2 to Zillow operating under a perpetual-but-terminable agreement with the National Association of Realtors.

## Financial Analysis

The single most important interpretive point is that the headline revenue decline from $10.1B (FY24) to $8.45B (FY25) is almost entirely the reclassification of Foxtel to discontinued operations, not organic deterioration. On a continuing-operations basis, revenue grew 2% versus the recast $8.25B base.

Quality of continuing operations is strong:
- Total segment EBITDA up 14% to $1,415M, with margin expansion across all four operating segments.
- Net income from continuing operations up 71% to $648M.
- Operating cash flow up 9% to $978M; free cash flow $571M after $407M of capex (versus $540M in FY24).
- Reported net income to NWSA stockholders of $1,180M includes roughly $692M from the Foxtel disposal gain in discontinued operations, which inflates the headline figure and the trailing EPS basis.

Segment detail:
- **Dow Jones:** revenue +4%, EBITDA +8%; digital is 82% of segment revenue; professional information (B2B) +7%, led by Risk & Compliance +15% and Energy +11%. WSJ digital-only subscriptions +9% to 4.1M; total consumer subs +7% to 6.26M.
- **Digital Real Estate:** revenue +9%, EBITDA +18%; REA Australia +12% on price increases and depth penetration; Move/Realtor.com only +1% as US lead volumes fell 9% on elevated mortgage rates.
- **Book Publishing:** revenue +3%, EBITDA +10%, helped by audiobook/Spotify and backlist.
- **News Media:** revenue −4% (largely the transfer of UK third-party printing to the DMG Media JV) but EBITDA +15% on cost reductions, a genuine restructuring gain.

Balance sheet quality is high for a media company. Cash of $2.4B exceeds parent debt of ~$1,962M (REA debt is non-recourse to the parent), leaving roughly zero net debt at the parent. Current ratio 1.84. The Altman Z-Score of 1.87 (grey zone) is misleading: it reflects a goodwill-heavy, low-asset-turnover model (goodwill $4.4B plus intangibles $1.9B is ~40% of assets) rather than financial fragility. Two caveats matter for capital flexibility: roughly $280M of cash is trapped at separately-listed REA Group and accessible only via dividend declaration, and ~$915M total sits with foreign subsidiaries.

The earnings track record supports near-term estimate reliability: three beats and one in-line print over the last four quarters, with surprises of +12.1%, +14.2%, and +17.0% in the three beat quarters.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth story is Dow Jones B2B. Risk & Compliance and Energy pricing benchmarks grow double digits with structural pricing power, and management's five-year target of $1B Dow Jones EBITDA (from $588M) is the central long-term value driver. Digital Real Estate growth is healthy in Australia (REA +12% revenue) but soft in the US, where Realtor.com leads fell 9% under high rates and the post-NAR-settlement commission upheaval clouds demand for lead products.

The non-obvious growth lever is AI content-licensing. News Corp signed a multi-year licensing agreement with OpenAI (press-reported at up to ~$250M over five years across cash and credits; exact economics are not confirmed in the 10-K) and has reportedly added a Meta deal. This converts generative AI from a pure threat into a high-margin revenue stream and is explicitly part of the Dow Jones $1B target. The company is simultaneously litigating against AI firms that train without paying (the Dow Jones/NY Post suit against Perplexity), a deliberate "license the willing, sue the unwilling" strategy to establish that grounding on its content requires payment. The 10-K flags a real durability caveat: not all content-license agreements have renewed, and renewals are not guaranteed.

Against this, the near-term earnings momentum is negative, which is the central tension for the growth score. Consensus has cut next-quarter EPS by 10.7% over both the trailing 30 and 90 days, and current-quarter EPS by 2.8% over 30 days. Full-year revisions are marginally positive (current year +2.1%, next year +3.9% over 90 days), but the near-term cuts and the explicit "negative earnings momentum" characterization argue against extrapolating acceleration into the next two prints. The strong-revisions BUY signal (positive 90-day revisions of similar magnitude) is not present here; revisions are mixed and skewed negative at the quarterly level.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing/forward P/E of 34.3x looks rich against the publishing peer median of 24.5x, but the headline multiple is distorted. Trailing EPS of $0.84 is calculated against a share base inflated by Foxtel-driven discontinued-operations noise. On continuing-operations earnings and the forward consensus, current-year EPS of $1.08 puts the forward multiple closer to ~25x and next-year EPS of $1.29 at ~21x — broadly in line with peers (NYT 33.0x, NWS 39.6x, IAC 24.5x) rather than a clear premium.

The bull case rests on the sum-of-the-parts: the 61.4% REA Group stake is worth a very large fraction of (by some analyses exceeds) the entire $9.8B market cap, implying the market values Dow Jones, HarperCollins, and News Media at little or nothing. Analyst targets sit entirely above the current $27.06 — low $29.40 (+8.7%), median $35.00 (+29%), high $43.00 (+59%) — which supports the view that the equity is not expensive on the parts.

The problem is that this discount is durable for a structural reason, not a temporary one. The dual-class structure concentrates control in the Murdoch Family Trust, anti-takeover provisions entrench it, and by-laws permit corporate opportunities to be directed to Fox. Buybacks mechanically increase Murdoch voting concentration. The succession resolution entrenches continuity rather than removing the structure. With no identified event within the next 12 months that forces the discount to narrow (a REA spin, a Dow Jones separation, or a change in the dual-class structure), the SOTP gap is a reason to own the business at a margin of safety, not a near-term catalyst. The valuation is fair-to-mildly-attractive on forward earnings with embedded optionality, which supports holding but not a high-conviction long entry at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Dow Jones B2B occupies a structurally attractive position: financial-crime/compliance data and energy pricing benchmarks (OPIS) carry pricing power and double-digit growth, competing against LexisNexis, Reuters, Argus, and S&P. REA Group is a genuinely dominant Australian property portal with high incremental margins. HarperCollins is a consolidated #2 in a mature category, benefiting from audiobook growth.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The information segments are gaining share and widening their data moat; News Media faces secular print decline and platform-algorithm-driven traffic loss (already hitting The Sun in the UK) but has been right-sized through the DMG Media JV and cost cuts. Realtor.com is the clearest competitive weakness — a persistent #2 to Zillow, now navigating the elimination of cooperative-compensation rules from the NAR settlements, with an existential single-counterparty dependency on the NAR operating agreement. Generative AI is double-edged: it threatens content commoditization and traffic loss while creating a new high-margin licensing channel where News Corp owns the scarce input.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Generative AI commoditization.** The 10-K repeatedly warns that AI chatbots and search overviews have reduced and may continue to reduce traffic and subscriber demand. The licensing strategy offsets this but its durability is unproven (not all license deals renew).
2. **NAR operating-agreement dependency.** Realtor.com runs on a perpetual but terminable agreement; if terminated for cause, Move must hand software and advertiser/data contracts to NAR. This is a binary, single-counterparty risk for a key asset.
3. **US real estate cyclicality.** Elevated rates depressed Move leads 9%; the post-settlement commission environment adds demand uncertainty.
4. **Governance / dual-class structure.** Limited Class A voting rights, ~41% Murdoch Class B control, anti-takeover provisions, and explicit Fox conflict-of-interest by-laws. Buybacks concentrate control further.
5. **UK Newspaper Matters.** Enduring legal/contingency tail from historic UK phone-hacking litigation.
6. **FX translation.** Material AUD and GBP exposure via REA and News UK/Australia.
7. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** ~40% of assets; none taken in FY25 but a standing risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a near-term binary that fundamental data does not fully explain. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 50.9% against 24.5% one-year realized — rich by 26.4 percentage points and in the 71st percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (50.9% at one week versus 30.9% at six months), and the nearest expiry implies a ±8.9% move over eight days. Put/call open interest of 0.03 is call-heavy and the near-term skew of −32.1% is unusually call-tilted, so the positioning is not bearish hedging; rather, the market is pricing an imminent event with elevated uncertainty. Entering a fresh long ahead of an unquantified near-term catalyst while quarterly estimate revisions are falling is unfavorable risk/reward. Short interest is modest at 4.0% of float (3.4 days to cover), so squeeze dynamics are not a factor either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($35–43):** Dow Jones B2B compounds toward the $1B EBITDA target as Risk & Compliance, Energy, and AI licensing scale; REA Group continues double-digit Australian growth; the $1B buyback authorization plus continued FCF generation steadily shrinks the float; and a discount-closing event (REA monetization, Dow Jones separation, or simply consensus catching up to continuing-ops earnings power) re-rates the SOTP gap. Analyst targets of $35 (median) to $43 (high) reflect this path, implying +29% to +59%.

**Bear Case ($23):** AI traffic erosion accelerates faster than licensing revenue can offset, content-license renewals disappoint, US real estate weakness persists or the NAR agreement is challenged, and the dual-class structure keeps the SOTP discount permanently wide. The stock retraces toward its February 2026 low near $22.20–23.00, roughly 15% below current levels. The negative near-term revision trend and the options-implied stress are the early-warning signals consistent with this path.

The base case is a roughly flat-to-modestly-higher stock as the Dow Jones engine offsets News Media drag and US real estate softness, with the discount neither closing nor widening materially absent a structural catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The long-term Dow Jones B2B and AI-licensing thesis is credible and the parent balance sheet is strong, but the absence of a near-term catalyst, the negative quarterly revision momentum, and the options-implied stress prevent adding here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Next-quarter and current-quarter EPS revisions turn positive and the FY Q4 (early-August) print and FY26 guidance confirm a reacceleration in Dow Jones B2B / digital subscriptions.
- A concrete SOTP-unlocking event materializes — a REA Group monetization or partial spin, a Dow Jones structural separation, or a credible path to simplify the dual-class structure.
- Confirmation of durable, contracted AI-licensing economics (renewed/expanded OpenAI and Meta agreements with disclosed terms supporting the Dow Jones $1B target).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence that AI-driven traffic and subscriber erosion is outpacing licensing offsets, with Dow Jones digital sub growth decelerating below mid-single digits.
- Any material adverse change to the NAR operating agreement for Move.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment or a guidance cut that breaks the continuing-ops EBITDA growth streak.

**Reassessment timeline:** The fiscal Q4/full-year print in early August 2026 is the natural reassessment point, alongside the resolution of the near-term event the options market is pricing over the next two weeks.

## Conclusion

News Corp is a fundamentally improved business after the Foxtel exit: 14% continuing-ops EBITDA growth, $571M of free cash flow, near-zero parent net debt, a structurally growing Dow Jones B2B franchise, and a dominant REA Group stake that anchors a credible sum-of-the-parts argument. These strengths justify owning the stock.

They do not, however, clear the bar for adding capital today. Near-term consensus EPS estimates are falling (next-quarter −10.7% over 90 days), the trailing multiple is full and the forward multiple only in line with peers, the SOTP discount has no identifiable near-term catalyst given the entrenched Murdoch control structure that buybacks reinforce, and the options market is pricing an imminent binary with implied volatility at twice realized levels. Four consecutive bullish calls have coincided with a roughly flat share price over the past year, which raises the burden for continuing to add. The appropriate posture is to hold the long-term Dow Jones and SOTP optionality while waiting for either a revision-momentum inflection or a discount-closing catalyst before upgrading.
