## Executive Summary

Golden Entertainment completed its sale transaction on April 30, 2026, and is no longer a publicly traded company. The deal delivered approximately $30 per share in total consideration (0.902 shares of VICI Properties + $2.75 cash), representing a 41% premium to the undisturbed share price prior to the November 6, 2025 announcement. The stock has been delisted from NASDAQ.

For any investor still evaluating GDEN, the analysis is retrospective. The company was a Nevada-concentrated gaming operator with deteriorating fundamentals — revenue declined 4% and Adjusted EBITDA fell 10% in fiscal 2025 — that went private through a management buyout structured with VICI Properties as the real estate acquirer. The transaction valued the enterprise at approximately 8.5x trailing EBITDA on declining earnings, a reasonable exit price given the operational headwinds, geographic concentration, and limited growth prospects.

Former GDEN shareholders should now evaluate their VICI Properties position as a separate investment. There is no remaining tradeable interest in Golden Entertainment.

## Company Overview

Golden Entertainment operated three Nevada-focused gaming segments prior to its delisting:

- **Nevada Casino Resorts** (59% of revenue): The STRAT Hotel on the Las Vegas Strip, plus the Aquarius and Edgewater casinos in Laughlin
- **Nevada Locals Casinos** (24%): Five smaller casinos serving repeat local customers
- **Nevada Taverns** (17%): 72 branded tavern locations (PT's, Sierra Gold) offering food, beverage, and slot gaming

The company underwent significant simplification between 2023 and 2025, divesting Maryland ($260M), Montana ($109M), and Nevada distributed gaming operations ($213.5M), reducing total revenue from $1.1B to $635M while retiring $276M in senior notes. This positioned the remaining assets for the final sale-leaseback transaction with VICI.

| Operating Cash Flow | — | $92M | $83M |

*2023 net income inflated by $370M+ gain on asset sales

Revenue declined sequentially in both 2024 and 2025. EBITDA margins compressed across all three segments. Hotel occupancy at resort properties deteriorated, STRAT tower visitation declined, and the locals segment faced pressure from Station Casinos and Boyd Gaming.

### Balance Sheet

- Total debt: $435M (Term Loan B-1 $390M + $45M revolver draw)
- Debt/Equity: 1.42x
- Altman Z-Score: 1.66 (distress zone)
- Cash: $55M
- FCF yield: ~4.8% (on pre-announcement market cap)

Leverage was elevated for a gaming operator in a declining revenue environment. The Z-Score placed the company in distress territory, though the pending sale transaction rendered balance sheet metrics moot.

### Earnings Track Record

The company missed analyst estimates in all four quarters prior to closing: Q1 2025 (-28.5%), Q2 2025 (-5.3%), Q3 2025 (-367.5%), Q4 2025 (-298.4%). The magnitude of misses accelerated through the second half of 2025, confirming the deteriorating operational trend that likely motivated the management buyout.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects for the standalone business were minimal to negative:

- Revenue was declining 4-5% annually with no identified catalysts for reversal
- The STRAT occupied a disadvantaged position on the far north end of the Las Vegas Strip, competing with properties that had spent billions on renovation
- The locals market faced competitive encroachment from Station Casinos' newer facilities
- The tavern segment was mature, with new openings unlikely to offset margin compression from labor costs
- 100% Nevada concentration meant no geographic diversification to offset local softness

The company had no meaningful capex expansion program beyond maintenance at The STRAT. This was a cash-flow harvesting operation being taken private by its founder.

## Valuation Assessment

The transaction valued Golden at approximately $30/share, or roughly 8.5x trailing EBITDA on 2025's $140M figure. Peer comparisons at closing:

| Company | EV/EBITDA | P/E |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Monarch Casino (MCRI) | ~10x | 19.9x |
| Red Rock Resorts (RRR) | ~9x | 16.9x |
| Churchill Downs (CHDN) | ~12x | 16.8x |
| GDEN takeout | ~8.5x | N/A (negative earnings) |

The 8.5x takeout multiple reflects a discount to peers, justified by: (a) declining EBITDA, (b) 100% Nevada concentration, (c) small scale, and (d) limited growth. The 41% premium to the undisturbed price was adequate compensation for minority shareholders losing their equity interest in a declining business.

At the final traded price of $28.55, the stock had converged to the deal consideration value, leaving no remaining arbitrage spread.

## Competitive Landscape

Golden occupied a challenged competitive position:

- **vs. Strip operators**: The STRAT competed against MGM, Caesars, and Wynn properties with inferior location and amenities
- **vs. locals competitors**: Station Casinos (Red Rock Resorts) and Boyd Gaming had newer, better-positioned properties and stronger loyalty programs
- **Tavern advantage**: The 72-location tavern network was a genuinely differentiated asset — high-frequency, low-cost customer acquisition targeting younger demographics. This was arguably the most valuable operating asset in the portfolio.
- **True Rewards loyalty**: 560,000 active members provided a data asset, but paled in comparison to larger operators

The competitive trajectory was deteriorating as evidenced by the revenue and EBITDA declines.

## Risk Assessment

With the transaction closed, all forward-looking risks to GDEN equity holders have been resolved through receipt of the deal consideration. For completeness, the risks that existed during the transaction period:

1. **Regulatory approval risk** — Required gaming regulatory approval in Nevada (received April 2026)
2. **Shareholder vote risk** — Required majority approval (obtained March 31, 2026)
3. **Financing risk** — VICI's $1.16B real estate acquisition required capital markets access (completed)
4. **Management conflict** — CEO Sartini as both buyer and seller created structural conflict, mitigated by Special Committee oversight

All risks resolved favorably. The stock is no longer tradeable.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (Retrospective)
The bull case played out: shareholders received a 41% premium in a take-private transaction. The company's strategic simplification (divesting non-core assets, deleveraging, concentrating on Nevada) positioned it for an efficient sale-leaseback with VICI. Shareholders who held through the announcement captured approximately $8-9/share in value creation relative to pre-deal trading levels.

### Bear Case (Retrospective)
The bear case for the business — declining revenue, compressed margins, competitive disadvantage at key properties, high leverage — was accurate and likely motivated management's decision to take the company private while assets still commanded a reasonable EBITDA multiple.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The stock has been delisted and the transaction closed on April 30, 2026. There is no remaining tradeable equity interest. Shares outstanding have been cancelled and converted into merger consideration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Re-entry Criteria
There is no re-entry path. GDEN has ceased to exist as a public equity. Former shareholders now hold VICI Properties shares, which should be evaluated as a separate investment (S&P 500 triple-net gaming REIT).

### Timeline
Permanent. The company has been taken private and will operate as a family-owned business under the Sartini family with a long-term master lease to VICI.

## Conclusion

Golden Entertainment is no longer an investable public equity. The transaction closed April 30, 2026, delivering $2.75 cash + 0.902 VICI shares per GDEN share. The 8.5x EBITDA takeout represented a fair exit for a small, concentrated gaming operator with limited growth options.

Recommendation: **AVOID** — the security no longer trades. Any GDEN shares appearing in a portfolio should have already been converted to VICI consideration + cash. Investors should evaluate their VICI position independently.
