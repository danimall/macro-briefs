**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Resideo is three weeks from a structural transformation. On August 3, 2026 the company distributes ADI Global Distribution to shareholders as a standalone public company (ticker ADIG, 1 ADI share per 2 REZI shares), leaving behind a pure-play building-technologies manufacturer. That surviving entity, the Products & Solutions segment, carries a roughly 20.6% segment operating margin ($555M on $2,688M FY2025 revenue), versus the 4.4% margin of the distribution business being spun off. The market currently prices the consolidated entity as a "Trading Companies & Distributors" name, which describes precisely the business that is leaving. The central question is whether the surviving manufacturer re-rates toward building-products/technology multiples once ADI is gone.

The reported financials are misleading and require careful interpretation. FY2025 shows a net loss of −$527M, diluted EPS of −$3.77, and operating cash flow of −$1,137M. Almost none of this reflects operational deterioration. In August 2025 Resideo paid Honeywell $1,590M to permanently terminate a legacy environmental indemnification obligation that had been capped at $140M/year and running to as late as 2043; the P&L absorbed a $972M charge and cash flow absorbed $1,695M of Honeywell payments. Strip those out and the picture inverts: consolidated operating income rose to $607M (8.1% of revenue) from $520M (7.7%), gross margin expanded to 29.4% from 28.1%, and Q1 2026 returned to profitability at $38M net income / $0.17 EPS. A quantitative screen reading trailing GAAP net income, ROE, or FCF will materially mis-rank this company.

The offsetting concern is leverage and execution. The Honeywell exit was funded with roughly $1.2B of new floating-rate term debt right before the company divides, pushing total debt to ~$3.2B and lifting Q1 2026 interest expense 88% YoY to $47M. The debt split between RemainCo Resideo and ADI is not yet fully confirmed from primary filings, and the spin lands into a dual CEO transition (Tom Surran to lead Resideo, Rob Aarnes to lead ADI, with longtime CEO Jay Geldmacher departing). We rate REZI a HOLD with a positive 12-month bias. The sum-of-the-parts thesis is credible, but the swing variables (post-spin leverage allocation, standalone financial disclosure, and whether the market grants the RemainCo a manufacturer multiple) resolve over the next two to three quarters, and the risk/reward does not yet support a BUY ahead of that visibility.

## Company Overview

Resideo, spun out of Honeywell in October 2018, manufactures and distributes sensing and controls products for residential and commercial buildings, with products in more than 150M spaces globally sold primarily through roughly 100,000 professional installers. The company operates two structurally different businesses:

- **Products & Solutions (P&S)** — a building-products manufacturer covering HVAC controls and thermostats, water and air quality, smoke and CO detection (First Alert, BRK brands), security/video, and OEM components. FY2025 revenue $2,688M, segment operating income $555M (~20.6% margin). This is the higher-quality, higher-margin business and the surviving RemainCo.
- **ADI Global Distribution** — a specialty distributor of low-voltage security, AV, and fire products, selling 500,000+ SKUs from 1,000+ suppliers. FY2025 revenue $4,784M, segment operating income $212M (~4.4% margin, classic distribution economics). ADI represented roughly 64% of revenue and 35% of operating income in 2025 and is the entity being spun off.

Post-split, Resideo becomes a pure-play building-technologies company addressing a serviceable market management sizes at over $40B, while ADI becomes a standalone distributor targeting a ~$65B North American low-voltage market. The competitive moat for RemainCo rests on the installed base of 150M+ locations, deep Pro relationships (15M+ installations per year), the licensed Honeywell Home brand plus owned brands, 2,900+ patents, and low-cost manufacturing across six Mexican facilities.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Consolidated revenue grew from $6,761M in 2024 to $7,472M in 2025 (+10.5%), driven largely by the Snap One acquisition (+$446M, closed June 2024), price/mix (+$193M), volume (+$47M), and FX (+$32M). Q1 2026 revenue was $1,912M, up 8.0% YoY, though aided by extra selling days. Full-year 2026 guidance calls for mid-single-digit revenue growth. Underlying demand tracks repair/remodel activity, home sales, and rates; management guides to slow growth in U.S. residential and moderation in non-residential construction.

**Margins.** The underlying trend is favorable. Consolidated gross margin expanded to 29.4% in 2025 from 28.1%, and management cites 12 consecutive quarters of gross-margin expansion. Q1 2026 gross margin held roughly flat at 28.8%; operating income fell to $102M (5.3% of revenue) primarily on $24M of one-time ADI-separation costs and a $19M legal settlement rather than core degradation.

**The FY2025 loss, decomposed.** The net loss and negative EPS are dominated by the $972M Indemnification Agreement expense tied to terminating the Honeywell environmental obligation. Operating income before that charge was $607M. Operating cash flow and FCF reflect the $1,695M paid to Honeywell in 2025 (including the $1,590M one-time termination payment). On a normalized basis management reports greater than 85% FCF conversion in each of the last three years. This is the most important interpretive point in the analysis: the headline distress signals are one-time, not recurring.

**Balance sheet — the genuine watch item.** The termination was funded with $1,225M of new term loans, pushing total debt to roughly $3,231M at year-end 2025 from ~$1,983M of long-term debt in 2024. Debt/equity is ~1.9x and the Altman Z-score sits at 2.15 (grey zone). The Q1 2026 debt stack: $2,326M A&R Term B facility (variable, ~5.67% weighted average, maturities 2028/2031/2032), $300M 4.0% notes due 2029, and $600M 6.5% notes due 2032, with a $500M undrawn revolver. The company is in compliance with all covenants. Cash fell to $438M in Q1 2026 from $661M on seasonal working-capital use and separation costs. Interest expense nearly doubled, Q1 2026 $47M versus $25M a year earlier, on the incremental debt, and roughly 100 bps of rate movement equals ~$22M of annual interest on the floating portion. Goodwill of $3.1B plus intangibles of ~$1.1B (Snap One-heavy) make up ~$4.2B of the $8.2B asset base, with no impairments in 2023–2025.

**Capital allocation.** Capex is modest (~$116M/year, asset-light). A $150M buyback authorized in August 2023 is essentially dormant ($108M remaining, no repurchases in 2024, 2025, or Q1 2026) as deleveraging takes priority. The company pays $35M/year of cumulative dividends on the CD&R preferred.

The financial health score of 5.5 reflects this two-sided picture: healthy and growing underlying operations with strong normalized cash conversion, offset by a materially higher, largely floating-rate debt load carried into a corporate split where the RemainCo's standalone leverage is not yet confirmed.

## Growth Analysis

The earnings-surprise record supports reasonable confidence in near-term estimates: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with Q3 2025 beating by 22.9% and Q2 2025 by 19.8%, though Q4 2025 missed by 6.3%. Estimate revisions are mixed rather than cleanly positive — the current quarter and next quarter have edged up (+0.5% and +3.5% over 30 days), while current-year and next-year estimates have drifted down (−1.7% and −3.1% over 30 days). This is a modestly decelerating consensus that reflects separation costs and macro caution rather than acceleration.

The growth thesis is structural, not momentum-driven. Management's July 2026 investor-day frameworks (unaudited guidance) lay out for pure-play Resideo a 2025–2030 revenue CAGR of 4–5%, roughly 400 bps of gross-margin expansion to 43–45% by 2030, and adjusted EBITDA margin expansion of ~400 bps to 23–25%. For ADI, revenue CAGR of 4–6% to ~$6B, adjusted EBITDA CAGR above 10%, and more than $80M of run-rate cost savings by end-2027. These are targets, not commitments, and should be discounted accordingly, but the direction (margin mix-shift toward higher-value connected products and exclusive brands) is consistent with the gross-margin expansion already delivered.

The growth score of 6.0 reflects a durable mid-single-digit top line with credible margin-expansion optionality, tempered by the absence of an acceleration signal and by macro sensitivity to housing turnover and rates.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward-looking valuation cannot rely on the current trailing multiple, which is negative and meaningless given the Honeywell charge. On consensus current-year EPS of $2.85, REZI trades at roughly 12.1x forward earnings; on next-year $3.03, roughly 11.4x. At a $5.2B market cap the whole company trades well below the peer group median P/E of 31.3x for Trading Companies & Distributors (MSM 31.1x, DXPE 31.3x, WSO 31.5x, CNM 21.2x).

The sum-of-the-parts framing is where the value case lives. The surviving RemainCo carries a ~20.6% segment operating margin and is being valued inside a distributor comp set that describes the segment leaving. Building-products and connected-technology manufacturers with mid-teens-to-20% operating margins routinely command mid-teens-to-low-20s forward multiples. If the market re-bases the RemainCo toward those comps after August 3 while ADI trades on distributor economics, the combined value should exceed the current consolidated print. Analyst price targets bracket this: a low of $45 and high of $55 against a $34.54 current price, implying 30–59% upside, with a median target of $48.

Two cautions temper the score. First, the peer-relative discount is not automatically a mispricing; the market applies it partly because of the elevated post-Honeywell leverage and the execution overhang of the split, and because the RemainCo's standalone leverage is unconfirmed. A cheap multiple that reflects real structural uncertainty is not the same as a mispriced one. Second, the equity lessons here caution against treating cheapness as a buy signal absent a specific catalyst, and a catalyst does exist and is dated (August 3 spin, followed by Q2 results August 12). The valuation score of 6.2 credits the identified sum-of-the-parts catalyst and the cheapness on forward earnings, while withholding a higher mark until the post-spin capital structure is visible and the re-rating is confirmed rather than assumed.

## Competitive Landscape

Post-split the two entities compete in distinct markets. RemainCo Resideo competes in residential sensing and controls against connected-home and controls players, with the structural threat of Big Tech encroachment in the smart home and DIY/retail pressure on the Pro channel. Its defenses are its brand portfolio, its installed base, and its Pro relationships, a distribution and trust moat that is difficult to replicate but not immune to platform competition. The ~20% segment operating margin and 12 consecutive quarters of gross-margin expansion indicate the moat is currently intact and the pricing/mix trajectory is improving.

ADI operates in fragmented low-voltage distribution against other distributors, with the perennial risk that suppliers go direct or shift channels. Its differentiation is omnichannel scale and growing exclusive-brand penetration, with the Snap One integration adding scale but also ERP-integration risk the 10-K has flagged.

The most important classification point for investors: after August 3, REZI is best understood as a building-products manufacturer, not a distributor, and comps and multiples should be re-based accordingly. The competitive score of 6.0 reflects a stable-to-improving moat in the RemainCo (share-holding brands, expanding margins) against genuine long-term platform and channel threats.

## Risk Assessment

**Post-spin leverage and standalone viability (most material).** The company added ~$1.2B of debt to exit the Honeywell liability immediately before spinning off the segment contributing the majority of revenue. The 10-K explicitly warns the spin could adversely affect earnings and cash flows and jeopardize credit ratings, cost of capital, and capital-markets access. ADI is issuing its own $400M 7.125% notes due 2034 (priced June 2026), but the full debt allocation between RemainCo and ADI, and therefore each entity's pro-forma leverage, is not yet confirmable from primary filings. This is the single biggest unresolved variable in the thesis.

**Execution risk on the separation.** Complex IT and system carve-outs (ADI is still integrating Snap One's ERP), tax-free treatment contingent on an IRS ruling/tax opinion, and two simultaneous CEO transitions raise the probability of disruption.

**Tariff and Mexico exposure.** P&S manufactures the majority of U.S.-sold products in its Mexican facilities. Most is USMCA-exempt, but a 10% surcharge (effective 2/24/2026) and 15–50% metals tariffs (4/6/2026) apply to non-exempt goods, with active tariff litigation unresolved (the 10-K notes a 2/20/2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized, with remedies unresolved). Management's "not material" characterization is a claim to stress-test at the next earnings call.

**Honeywell dependency (ongoing).** The Honeywell Home trademark license is critical and terminates automatically on an unapproved change of control; the Tax Matters Agreement ($88M liability) leaves Resideo indemnifying Honeywell for spin-related and pre-2018 taxes.

**Governance concentration.** CD&R holds ~19.9% of voting power on an as-converted basis (per its Nov 2025 13-D), two board seats (Nathan Sleeper, John Stroup), consent rights over senior securities, charter amendments, and liquidation/bankruptcy, and cumulative preferred dividends. Its interests may diverge from common holders, particularly around future M&A and the mechanics of the split.

**Macro cyclicality.** Demand keys off repair/remodel, home sales, and rates, with management guiding to slow residential growth and non-residential moderation in 2026.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects that several of these risks are quantified and near-term (leverage, spin execution, CEO turnover) rather than fully priced, offset by the genuine structural de-risking of the Honeywell exit.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. ATM IV of 73.9% sits well above 60.1% one-year historical volatility (rich by 13.8 points), the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 73.9% > long-term 61.0%), and the put/call skew of 24.7% signals elevated put demand into the August spin and August 12 earnings. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±16.1%. ATM IV has collapsed from 323.3% in mid-June to 73.9% now; the extreme dislocation has normalized, but the market still prices a binary event. This confirms the fundamental read: the catalyst could move the stock materially in either direction, warranting patience rather than aggressive positioning ahead of it. Short interest of 4.7% of float (2.7 days to cover) is uncrowded, indicating no strong informed bearish consensus.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The August 3 spin unbundles a ~20%-margin building-technologies manufacturer from a thin-margin distributor, and the market re-rates the RemainCo from distributor multiples toward manufacturer multiples. The Honeywell exit permanently removes a chronic cash drag and valuation discount. Normalized FCF conversion above 85% resumes, deleveraging progresses, and the 4–5% revenue CAGR with 400 bps of margin expansion through 2030 materializes. Sum-of-the-parts closes toward the $45–55 analyst target range, implying roughly 30–59% upside.

**Bear case.** Post-spin leverage on the RemainCo proves heavier than the market expects, floating-rate interest expense compounds, credit ratings come under pressure, and the split disrupts customer relationships or the Snap One ERP integration. Housing weakness and tariff pass-through compress margins, the CEO transitions falter, and the RemainCo trades as a leveraged, cyclical small-cap rather than a re-rated manufacturer. The stock retraces toward the May 2026 lows near $26–28, roughly 19–25% downside.

**Base case.** The spin executes cleanly and the RemainCo earns a partial re-rating, but leverage and macro caution keep the multiple below full manufacturer comps for the first two to three quarters as investors wait for standalone results and deleveraging proof. The combined entities trend toward $40–42 over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The recommendation history (HOLD → HOLD → HOLD) and improving score trajectory (4.8 → 6.1 → 5.6) reflect a thesis that has been strengthening as the Honeywell overhang cleared and profitability returned, but not yet resolving into a confident BUY. The prior-report price decline is not a fundamental deterioration signal: underlying operating income rose, Q1 2026 returned to profit, and the sum-of-the-parts catalyst is now dated and imminent. What holds the call short of BUY is the unconfirmed post-spin capital structure and the event risk the options market is pricing, both of which resolve within one to two quarters.

**Reassessment timeline.** August 3, 2026 (spin distribution) and August 12, 2026 (Q2 results, post-spin), plus the availability of ADI's Form 10 with pro-forma standalone leverage for each entity.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Post-spin pro-forma RemainCo net leverage confirmed at or below ~2.5x with a clear deleveraging path, and no credit-rating downgrade through the split.
- The RemainCo trades post-spin at a discount to building-products/technology comps (below ~15x forward EPS) with confirmed mid-single-digit organic growth and continued gross-margin expansion.
- Current-year and next-year estimate revisions turn positive following clean standalone disclosure.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- RemainCo standalone leverage materially above ~3.5x net or a credit-rating downgrade around the split.
- Spin execution disruption evident in Q2/Q3 (customer loss, ERP-integration failure, guidance cut), or tariff impact shifting from "not material" to a quantified margin headwind.
- Gross-margin expansion reverses, breaking the 12-quarter streak, indicating the manufacturer moat is eroding.

## Conclusion

Resideo is a breakup story with a clean, dated catalyst. The reported financials understate the business: the FY2025 loss and negative cash flow are Honeywell-termination artifacts, and underlying operations are healthy, margin-expanding, and back to GAAP profitability in Q1 2026. On forward earnings the whole company is cheap at ~11–12x against a distributor peer group at ~31x, and the sum-of-the-parts logic argues the surviving ~20%-margin manufacturer should command a materially higher multiple once ADI departs on August 3. Analyst targets of $45–55 imply substantial upside.

The reasons to wait rather than commit are concrete: leverage roughly doubled to fund the Honeywell exit, the debt is largely floating with interest expense up 88% YoY, the RemainCo's standalone capital structure is not yet confirmed, and two CEO transitions land into the split just as the options market prices a ±16% binary event. This is a growth-oriented HOLD: the thesis is improving and the catalyst is close, but the risk/reward supports adding on confirmation of a clean post-spin balance sheet and re-rating rather than positioning blind into the event. We would move to BUY on confirmed RemainCo leverage at or below ~2.5x with the stock still below manufacturer multiples, and revisit to the downside only on genuine spin-execution failure or a materially heavier standalone debt load.
