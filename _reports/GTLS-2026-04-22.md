## Executive Summary

Chart Industries trades at $207.91, approximately $2.09 (1.0%) below the $210/share all-cash acquisition price offered by Baker Hughes, with an expected close in mid-2026. The stock has been pinned within a $206-$209 range since late 2025, reflecting near-universal market expectation that the deal closes. The implied annualized spread, assuming a Q2 2026 close, is approximately 3-5% depending on exact timing.

The fundamental question for any potential investor is whether 1% gross upside justifies the binary downside. If the deal breaks, GTLS faces a standalone reality characterized by $3.66B in debt (Altman Z-Score 1.41), no sitting CEO, $308M in annual interest expense, and a potential $508M termination liability that would consume more than the company's entire cash position. A reasonable post-break price is $130-$150, implying 25-35% downside. The expected value of this trade is negative for new capital at current levels.

The underlying business — global leader in cryogenic, heat transfer, and specialty process equipment — has genuine competitive strengths, including a record $5.89B backlog driven by LNG, carbon capture, and data center demand. Public shareholders will not participate in that backlog's conversion; it accrues entirely to Baker Hughes at the fixed $210 price.

## Company Overview

Chart Industries designs, engineers, and manufactures process technologies and equipment for gas and liquid molecule handling across four segments: Cryo Tank Solutions (14.6% of 2025 revenue), Heat Transfer Systems (29.0%), Specialty Products (25.8%), and Repair, Service & Leasing (30.6%). The company was transformed by its March 2023 acquisition of Howden for $4.4B, which roughly doubled revenue to $3.35B in 2023.

The company operates 62 manufacturing locations globally with approximately 11,800 employees. International operations account for 58% of revenue. End markets include LNG liquefaction, industrial gas, hydrogen, carbon capture, nuclear, data center cooling, marine, and water treatment.

On July 28, 2025, Chart agreed to be acquired by Baker Hughes for $210/share in cash (~$13.6B total enterprise value). Shareholders approved on October 6, 2025. The HSR waiting period expired November 6, 2025. Closing is expected mid-2026 pending remaining international regulatory reviews.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** GTLS generated $4.26B in 2025 revenue, up 2.5% from $4.16B in 2024. Organic growth was modest after the Howden acquisition anniversaried. Heat Transfer Systems was the standout, growing 19.5% on LNG backlog conversion and data center demand. The other three segments declined year-over-year.

**Profitability — Distorted by One-Time Charges:** Reported operating margin of 8.4% and net income of $40.7M (net margin 0.9%) were crushed by the $266M Flowserve termination fee. Adjusting for this and $22.8M in M&A-related SG&A, underlying operating margin was approximately 15.2%, roughly in line with 2024's 15.6%. Trailing EPS of $0.33 reflects this distortion, producing the misleading 630x trailing P/E in the data.

**Segment Economics:** Heat Transfer Systems demonstrated powerful operating leverage, with operating margins expanding from 22.5% to 29.4% on 20% revenue growth. Repair, Service & Leasing carries the highest gross margins (44.3%) but declined 5% as prior-year emergency work did not repeat. Specialty Products margins compressed to 12.2% from 15.5%, partly due to startup costs at the Theodore, Alabama facility.

**Balance Sheet — Distress Zone:** Total debt of $3.66B against $366M cash produces net debt of approximately $3.29B. The debt/equity ratio is 2.0x. Goodwill and intangible assets of $5.58B represent 57% of total assets. The Altman Z-Score of 1.41 places the company squarely in the distress zone (<1.81). Annual interest expense of $308M consumes 7.2% of revenue. The debt structure includes 9.5% unsecured notes due 2031 — expensive paper that reflects the company's credit profile.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow declined to $292.7M from $503.0M, primarily due to working capital timing. Free cash flow of $202.8M (4.8% FCF margin) is modest relative to the debt load. FCF/net income conversion is high (nearly 5x) because reported net income is artificially depressed by the termination fee.

**Orders & Backlog:** Orders of $5.68B (+13.4%) and year-end backlog of $5.89B (+21.5%) provide approximately 1.4x revenue coverage. Specialty Products orders surged $519M (+42%) on carbon capture, nuclear, and data center demand. This backlog represents substantial future value — for Baker Hughes.

**Earnings Surprise Record:** Over the last four quarters, GTLS missed estimates three times (Q1 2025 by -15.6%, Q3 2025 by -0.9%, Q4 2025 by -9.9%) and beat once (Q2 2025 by +3.2%). This pattern suggests either sell-side models were too aggressive or operational execution has been inconsistent. However, this record is largely irrelevant given the pending acquisition.

## Growth Analysis

As a standalone entity, Chart's growth profile would be compelling. The $5.89B backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility. Key secular tailwinds include:

- **LNG:** Global liquefaction capacity expansion supports Heat Transfer Systems
- **Data Centers:** Cooling demand for high-density compute is an emerging, fast-growing end market
- **Carbon Capture:** Government subsidies (IRA, EU Green Deal) driving project sanctions
- **Nuclear:** Specialty Products seeing order acceleration from nuclear restart/expansion
- **Hydrogen:** Full value chain exposure from production to storage to distribution

None of this growth accrues to public equity holders at $210/share in cash. The backlog, the secular tailwinds, the margin expansion potential from the Theodore facility ramp — all transfer to Baker Hughes upon closing. For current investors, the growth story is priced out.

## Valuation Assessment

The stock trades at $207.91 against a $210/share cash acquisition. The 1.0% gross spread, annualized to 3-5% on an expected mid-2026 close, is thin by merger-arbitrage standards and reflects high market confidence in deal completion.

**Peer comparison is irrelevant in a deal context.** The peer median P/E of 32.9x applied to normalized adjusted earnings would suggest standalone value in the $150-$180 range (depending on earnings adjustments), well below the deal price. Baker Hughes is paying a premium for Chart's strategic value in LNG and clean energy equipment.

The trailing P/E of 630x reflects the Flowserve termination fee distortion. On a normalized adjusted EPS basis (roughly $9-10), the $210 deal price represents approximately 21-23x, a modest premium to peers but reasonable given Chart's backlog growth and margin trajectory.

The forward P/E of 630x reported in the data appears to reflect the same distortion applied to forward estimates, or may reflect consensus confusion around the deal timeline.

## Competitive Landscape

Chart holds leadership positions in several defensible niches. Brazed aluminum heat exchangers are manufactured by a small number of global competitors. The company's proprietary IPSMR® LNG liquefaction technology is widely deployed. Its 50+ service centers create an aftermarket moat with high switching costs once equipment is installed.

The Howden acquisition added rotating equipment (fans, compressors) to Chart's stationary equipment portfolio, creating cross-selling opportunities and broadening the addressable market. The combined product suite covers the full lifecycle from design through installation through decades of aftermarket service.

Baker Hughes' willingness to pay $13.6B validates Chart's competitive positioning. The acquisition creates a vertically integrated LNG and clean energy equipment company with global scale.

## Risk Assessment

### Deal Break Risk (Primary)
The most significant risk is regulatory rejection of the Baker Hughes acquisition. While the HSR waiting period has expired and shareholder approval was obtained, international regulatory reviews remain pending. Baker Hughes' simultaneous divestiture of Waygate Technologies to Hexagon for $1.45B may facilitate clearance by reducing overlap concerns.

If the deal breaks, Chart faces a combined $508M liability: a $250M termination fee to Baker Hughes plus reimbursement of the $258M Baker Hughes paid to Flowserve on Chart's behalf. Against $366M in cash and $3.66B in existing debt, this liability would likely force emergency refinancing at punitive rates or asset sales. The stock would plausibly fall to $130-$150.

### Standalone Vulnerability
Without the deal, Chart has no CEO (Evanko departed January 6, 2026), and operational restrictions during the pendency period may have eroded organizational agility. Board turnover (four 8-K filings for director changes in 2025-2026) adds governance uncertainty.

### Macro/Tariff Exposure
With 58% international sales and aluminum/steel as key input materials, Chart is exposed to trade policy disruption. The 10-K explicitly flags tariff risk as material. In the current environment of elevated tariff uncertainty, this is a non-trivial standalone risk, though largely irrelevant if the deal closes.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 3.3% against 29.5% 1-year historical volatility is the defining options observation. The 26.2 percentage point discount to HV reflects extreme certainty that the stock will remain pinned near $210 until deal close. The put/call OI ratio of 0.19 is heavily call-skewed. Only 76 contracts across 3 expirations signals minimal options market activity, consistent with a near-certain merger-arb situation. The flat term structure (3.3% near-term to 3.5% far-term) shows no meaningful event premium priced in any tenor.

This options profile confirms that the market treats this as a completed deal. If an investor believed deal break risk was materially underpriced, puts would be extremely cheap relative to the potential payoff, but the low liquidity (76 contracts) makes execution difficult.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The Baker Hughes deal closes on schedule in mid-2026, shareholders receive $210/share, and the ~1% gross return is captured. The annualized return of 3-5% modestly exceeds money market rates. For existing holders who bought below $170, this is a fine outcome.

### Bear Case
Regulatory objections delay or block the deal. Chart owes $508M in combined termination liabilities against $366M in cash, has no CEO, and carries an Altman Z-Score in the distress zone. The stock falls to $130-$150, representing 28-37% downside from current levels. Refinancing the combined $4.2B+ in obligations at current rates (3.64% SOFR + elevated credit spreads for a Z-Score 1.41 borrower) would produce annual interest expense exceeding $350M, severely constraining operating flexibility.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** No specific catalyst for a short is identifiable. The bear case requires the Baker Hughes deal to fail, a low-probability binary outcome. There is no earnings miss catalyst (the company is in deal mode), no identifiable regulatory ruling date, and no specific timeline for a negative event. The stock trades within $2 of the deal price, providing minimal downside room even in a deal-closes scenario.

**Timing window:** Undefined. Regulatory reviews could extend into H2 2026, creating carrying costs for any short position.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Not available in the provided data, but the thin trading range and high institutional ownership typical of merger-arb situations create squeeze risk on any short covering.

**Borrow and options signal:** Extremely low IV (3.3%) makes put protection cheap in dollar terms, but OI of only 76 contracts makes meaningful put positions impractical. The options market is illiquid.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** If the deal breaks, $130-$150 target (28-37% downside). If the deal closes, ~1% upside loss plus borrow costs. Risk/reward ratio appears favorable on paper (28-37% vs. 1-2%), but the probability of the deal breaking is low (estimated 5-15%), making expected value negative.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis requires a low-probability binary event (deal break) with no identifiable catalyst or timeline. This is a speculative bet against deal completion, not a fundamentally-driven short. The illiquid options market makes hedged positioning impractical.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### For Current Holders (AVOID adding)
- **Base case:** Deal closes mid-2026 at $210/share. Collect the $2.09 spread (1.0% gross).
- **Upgrade trigger:** None. There is no scenario in which adding to this position at $207.91 offers attractive risk/reward.
- **Downgrade trigger:** Any regulatory objection or delay announcement. Monitor Baker Hughes' Waygate Technologies divestiture progress as a leading indicator of regulatory clearance.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Any material regulatory filing or announcement.

### Re-entry Criteria (for those not currently holding)
If the deal breaks and the stock falls to $130-$150, reassess Chart as a standalone entity. At that level, the business would trade at approximately 14-16x normalized adjusted earnings with strong secular tailwinds in LNG, carbon capture, and data centers. The key questions at that point would be (a) whether the $508M termination liability is manageable, (b) whether a new CEO is hired, and (c) whether the debt can be serviced or refinanced. If all three are resolved favorably, the stock could be a compelling recovery candidate in the $130-$140 range.

**Timeline for headwinds:** Until the deal closes or breaks, the stock is a low-volatility, low-return merger-arb instrument. If the deal breaks, headwinds from leverage and leadership vacuum would persist for 6-12 months.

## Conclusion

GTLS is a high-quality industrial franchise being acquired at a fair-to-generous premium by Baker Hughes. The underlying business has genuine competitive strengths, a record backlog, and secular growth tailwinds. For investors who bought below $170, holding through deal close is sensible.

For new capital, the arithmetic is unfavorable: 1% gross upside against 25-35% downside on a deal break. The company's standalone profile makes the asymmetry particularly poor. Even assigning only a 5-10% probability to deal failure, the expected value of initiating a new position at $207.91 is negative.

A money market fund yields more with zero downside risk. The score of 4.2 reflects strong underlying business quality offset by extreme leverage, a fully-priced acquisition, and binary deal risk with asymmetric downside.
