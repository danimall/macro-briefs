**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Madison Square Garden Sports is a pure-play trophy-asset holding company. Its principal assets are the New York Knicks (NBA) and New York Rangers (NHL), franchises that private-market appraisers value at roughly $13.5 billion combined (Sportico: Knicks ~$9.85B, Rangers ~$3.65B) against a public market capitalization of approximately $7.0 billion. That gap is the entire investment proposition. The standard valuation toolkit does not work here: trailing P/E is negative, GAAP equity is negative ($281M deficit at FY25), and an Altman Z-Score of 0.12 would flag bankruptcy for an industrial company. None of these signals describe distress. They are accounting artifacts of carrying two of the most valuable sports franchises in North America at a combined ~$330M historical cost on the balance sheet.

The catalyst that converts this static discount into a tradeable thesis is the board's February 18, 2026 authorization to explore a tax-free spin-off separating the Rangers into a standalone public company. A confidential Form 10 was filed around May 2026. The logic is to separate a championship-caliber Knicks (2026 NBA Finals run, first since 1999) from an underperforming Rangers (two consecutive playoff misses) so each trades on its own merits, easing minority-stake sales and partial monetization. The Knicks' Finals appearance also drives an estimated $140M+ in Q4 FY2026 postseason arena revenue, a near-term tailwind not reflected in the nine-month results.

We rate MSGS a HOLD. The thesis is sound and the conglomerate discount is real, but three considerations cap conviction. First, the stock has already advanced roughly 94% over twelve months and sits within 9% of its 52-week high, so a meaningful portion of the SOTP re-rating is priced. Second, the spin-off is contingent on league approval (the key gating item), a tax opinion, Form 10 effectiveness, and final board sign-off, none guaranteed and the timeline is open-ended. Third, the related-party web around MSG Networks introduces genuine counterparty-solvency risk that could erode a recurring revenue stream and trigger credit-facility consequences. This is a growth hold predicated on the spin-off completing, not a conviction buy at $360.

## Company Overview

MSG Sports owns the Knicks and Rangers, both playing at The Garden, plus two development affiliates (Westchester Knicks, NBA G League; Hartford Wolf Pack, AHL) and a Greenburgh training center. It operates as a single reportable segment with a June 30 fiscal year-end. The company converted from Delaware to Nevada incorporation in June 2025.

MSGS does not own The Garden. It leases the arena from sister company MSG Entertainment under 35-year Arena License Agreements expiring 2055, with payments escalating 3% annually. The company sits inside the broader Dolan-controlled ecosystem: MSG Entertainment (arena owner, day-of-game services, sponsorship/suite sales), Sphere Entertainment/MSG Networks (local media rights), and AMC Networks. James L. Dolan serves as Executive Chairman and CEO of MSGS, MSG Entertainment, and Sphere Entertainment simultaneously, and Non-Executive Chairman of AMC Networks.

The competitive moat is scarcity. There are a fixed number of NBA and NHL franchises, they almost never trade, and these two operate in the largest U.S. media market with near-capacity attendance. Multi-year contracted revenue (media rights, suites, sponsorships) smooths year-to-year volatility from on-court performance.

## Financial Analysis

The reported financials require franchise-holdco interpretation rather than literal reading.

**Revenue trajectory ($M):** FY22 $821 → FY23 $887 → FY24 $1,027 → FY25 $1,039 (+1% YoY). Steady top-line expansion driven by ticket pricing, suites, sponsorship, and national league distributions. The nine months ended 3/31/26 showed revenue +5% to $875M, with Q3 +2% to $432M.

**The FY2025 earnings collapse.** Operating income fell 90% to $14.8M (FY24: $146M); Adjusted Operating Income fell 78% to $38.2M (FY24: $172M); the company posted a net loss of -$22.4M against +$58.8M prior-year net income. Four drivers explain the swing: (1) the Knicks swung from an ~$12M NBA luxury-tax receipt to a $38M luxury-tax expense as a significant payer; (2) $49.1M in team-personnel transaction charges (waivers/terminations $39.2M, trades $9.8M) versus $0.8M prior; (3) team compensation rose $32.8M; (4) local media rights fell $17.9M from the MSG Networks amendment.

A large share of this earnings deterioration is the cost of fielding a championship roster. Luxury tax, contract terminations, and compensation all scale with competitive ambition. Management prioritizes long-term franchise value over period earnings, and the franchise value rose sharply even as GAAP earnings turned negative.

**FY2026 YTD.** Nine-month operating loss of -$3.3M (vs. +$37.4M prior), net loss -$20.5M, Adjusted OI down 65% to $19.2M. Drivers: higher team comp (+$37.6M), luxury tax/revenue sharing (+$26M), and $6.9M executive-transition costs. These figures exclude the 2026 playoff run.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY25 operating cash flow was $91.6M and FCF $88M; the business generates cash despite GAAP losses (large non-cash arena lease expense, working-capital timing). Cash was $107M at 3/31/26 with $433M of revolver availability. The negative -$281M stockholders' equity is normal for a sports-franchise holdco where franchises are carried at ~$102.6M indefinite-lived intangible plus $226.5M goodwill against a ~$13.5B private worth. The negative book equity and Z-Score are mechanical consequences of historical-cost accounting on assets worth multiples of their carrying value.

The November 6, 2025 refinancing amended and extended both credit facilities: the Knicks revolver upsized to $425M (from $275M) and the Rangers revolver to $250M, both now maturing November 6, 2030. This removed the prior December 2026 maturity wall and improved the liquidity runway.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is bifurcated between a depressed earnings line and an appreciating asset value, and the two move in opposite directions over the short term.

On the earnings line, the estimate-revision picture is mixed and net negative. Current-quarter EPS was revised up sharply (+144.5% over 30 days, +148.1% over 90 days, to $0.40) as the playoff-revenue tailwind comes into view. But the longer-dated revisions are deteriorating: current-year EPS revised to -$0.46 (down 28.8% over 30 days, 40.0% over 90 days) and next-year EPS to -$0.63 (down 307.4% over 30 days). The earnings momentum is negative once the one-time playoff quarter is stripped out, reflecting persistent luxury-tax exposure, eroding local media rights, and Section 162(m) tax headwinds beginning FY2028.

The genuine growth vector is asset value and the spin-off-driven re-rating. League-wide economics are improving: the new 11-year NBA national media deals with Disney/NBCUniversal/Amazon (effective 2025-26) validate rising franchise-level distributions, of which MSGS receives a pro-rata share. The spin-off is designed to let the market value each franchise at full private-comparable multiples rather than a blended conglomerate discount.

The earnings surprise track record argues for caution on near-term estimates: two beats and two misses over four quarters, with the most recent quarter (3/31/26) missing by 237% (-$0.78 actual vs. $0.57 estimate). Forward earnings visibility for a roster-cost-driven franchise is inherently low.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiples are non-informative: trailing and forward P/E are negative because the company is in a deliberate earnings trough driven by championship-roster costs. The valuation case rests entirely on the sum-of-the-parts.

The private-market SOTP is ~$13.5B (Knicks $9.85B + Rangers $3.65B). Against a ~$7.0B public market cap, the public stock trades at roughly a 48% discount to private worth (the company's prior public cap of ~$8.5B implied ~37%, and the stock has narrowed that gap modestly). The spin-off is the explicit mechanism to close the discount: separated franchises trade closer to private comps and become easier to monetize via minority-stake sales.

Peer multiples (Communication Services median P/E ~21.3) are not a useful anchor for a trophy-asset holding company with negative GAAP earnings. The relevant comparison set is franchise transaction values, where NBA and NHL teams have appreciated steadily and almost never trade below appraised value.

Analyst targets — low $295, mean ~$398, median ~$414.50, high $470 — bracket the current ~$360 price and imply roughly 11-30% upside to the mean/median, with the high-end reflecting a full SOTP realization. We treat the median around $414 as a reasonable base-case anchor: it implies a partial but not complete closure of the conglomerate discount over a 12-18 month horizon as the spin-off advances. We do not treat these targets as floors, because the re-rating thesis depends on a contingent corporate action rather than rising organic estimates, and the stock has already captured a large share of the move (up ~94% over twelve months).

The valuation score reflects asymmetric upside if the spin-off completes (the discount is identifiable and quantified), tempered by the fact that the catalyst is contingent and a portion of the gap has already narrowed during the run-up.

## Competitive Landscape

MSGS competes for the NYC sports and entertainment dollar against the Nets, Islanders, Devils, Yankees, Mets, Giants, Jets, Liberty, and broad entertainment alternatives, but the real value driver is the scarcity of NBA/NHL franchises in the largest U.S. market. These are irreplaceable brands with near-capacity attendance and decades of franchise-value appreciation.

The competitive trajectory is favorable on the asset side and mixed on the cash-flow side. League economics are improving (new national media deals), and franchise valuations continue to compound. The structural pressure is the regional-sports-network model: MSG Networks could not refinance and forced a cut to local media fees, with the term shortened to end after 2028-29. This directly erodes a recurring revenue stream and is the one clear area where the company's competitive economics are deteriorating. On balance, the trajectory is improving — the trophy assets are appreciating and the spin-off positions each franchise to capture full value — which supports a high competitive-position score, with the RSN decline as the offsetting drag.

## Risk Assessment

**MSG Networks solvency / local media rights erosion (highest-priority).** MSG Networks, a Sphere Entertainment subsidiary, had ~$510M of debt written off in a June 2025 restructuring. The amended rights agreements cut the Knicks fee 28% and the Rangers fee 18% (effective 1/1/25), eliminated escalators, and shortened the term to 2028-29; stated annual fees fall to ~$139.2M (FY26) from $162.9M (FY25). A future MSG Networks bankruptcy would eliminate a major recurring revenue stream, block borrowing under the revolvers, and trigger a Rangers credit-facility default. MSGS partially offset the distress by receiving penny warrants for 19.9% of MSG Networks equity.

**Player-cost volatility / NBA luxury tax.** Punitive luxury-tax rates began 2025-26; the Knicks are expected to be a payer again in FY26. Competitive success directly inflates costs, suppressing GAAP earnings precisely when the team wins.

**The Garden / Penn Station / zoning.** MSGS does not own its arena. The Garden's zoning special permit was renewed for only five years in September 2023 (expires ~2028), and MTA/Amtrak/NJ Transit have pushed for Penn Station redevelopment that could pressure relocation.

**Section 162(m) expansion (OBBBA), effective FY2028.** Limits executive-compensation deductions; analysts estimate ~$55M (Knicks) + ~$20M (Rangers) of incremental tax post-spin.

**Dolan control and conflicts of interest.** The Dolan family controls ~70.8% of voting power via Class B super-voting shares. MSGS is a "controlled company" and opts out of NYSE majority-independent-board rules. Overlap persons and a renounced-corporate-opportunities provision create structural conflict risk.

**Spin-off execution risk.** Completion requires league approval (the key gating item), a tax opinion, Form 10 effectiveness, and final board sign-off. Failure or indefinite delay would leave the conglomerate discount intact.

**NYC real estate tax exemption** (worth ~$43M in FY25) faces recurring political calls for repeal.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 48.9% against a 1-year historical volatility of 29.1% — rich by ~20 points, in the 83rd percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (1-week 48.9% vs. 6-month 27.8%), which typically signals an anticipated near-term event. Put/call volume ratio is elevated at 1.90, though the put/call OI ratio of 0.75 is more balanced and the skew is roughly neutral (-2.3% near term). The OI ratio rose from 0.43 to 0.75 since early May alongside an 8.7% price gain, indicating some hedging into the run-up. The elevated near-term vol is consistent with the playoff/Finals revenue outcome and ongoing spin-off headlines rather than a directional bearish bet.

### Insider Signal

Recent Form 4 activity (Feb 2026, June 2026) is intra-family trust transfers (Code "J") for estate planning, not open-market buys or sells, and carries no directional signal. The more notable governance item is James Dolan's February 25, 2026 pledge of 189,613 Class A shares (plus 33,680 from family trusts) to JPMorgan as collateral for a margin line, a leverage/governance consideration rather than a trading signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$470, +31%):** The spin-off receives league and board approval, the Form 10 becomes effective, and the Rangers and Knicks trade as separate entities at or near private-comparable valuations, collapsing the conglomerate discount. The Knicks' Finals run drives the postseason revenue and sustained pricing power, and the separation enables a partial monetization of the Knicks stake that crystallizes the ~$9.85B appraisal. Franchise values continue compounding alongside rising league-wide media economics.

**Bear case (~$295, -18%):** The spin-off stalls or fails on league or tax-opinion grounds, leaving the conglomerate discount intact. MSG Networks files for bankruptcy, eliminating recurring local-media revenue and triggering credit-facility consequences. Luxury-tax and roster costs keep GAAP earnings negative, Section 162(m) headwinds bite from FY2028, and Penn Station/zoning uncertainty around The Garden weighs on the trophy-asset narrative. The stock gives back a portion of its twelve-month gain.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For this HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The spin-off Form 10 becomes effective with confirmed league approval and a definitive timeline, materially de-risking the SOTP re-rating.
- The stock pulls back toward the low-$300s or below while the spin-off and franchise-value thesis remain intact, restoring the asymmetry the run-up has eroded.
- MSG Networks counterparty risk is resolved (refinancing, sale, or structural separation) without triggering a default or further media-rights cuts.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The board abandons the spin-off, or league approval is denied, leaving the conglomerate discount permanent with no near-term catalyst.
- MSG Networks files for bankruptcy, triggering a Rangers credit-facility default and loss of borrowing capacity.
- A material adverse zoning/Penn Station ruling forces a Garden relocation onto the agenda within the near term.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q4 FY2026 earnings (the playoff-revenue quarter, expected August/September 2026) and any public Form 10 effectiveness are the next natural reassessment points. The spin-off is the central event to monitor, with league approval the key gating item.

## Conclusion

MSGS offers a quantifiable discount to a ~$13.5B private-market sum-of-the-parts, with a board-authorized tax-free spin-off explicitly designed to close it and a Knicks Finals run providing a near-term revenue tailwind. The trophy-asset franchise economics are durable and improving, the November 2025 refinancing removed a near-term maturity wall, and the negative GAAP metrics that quantitative screens flag are accounting artifacts rather than distress.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of a contingent catalyst, a stock that has already advanced ~94% over twelve months and sits 9% off its high, and genuine related-party counterparty risk at MSG Networks. The spin-off must clear league, board, and tax-opinion hurdles on an open-ended timeline, and the longer-dated earnings revisions are deteriorating. The risk/reward is reasonable but no longer asymmetric at $360, with most of the cheap re-rating already captured. We would become more constructive on confirmed Form 10 effectiveness with league approval, or on a pullback toward the low-$300s. This is a growth hold predicated on the spin-off completing.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the discount of the ~$7.0B public cap to the ~$13.5B private SOTP is ~48% (not ~30%), implying materially more upside than the report's HOLD narrative assumed — the "most of the cheap re-rating has been captured" framing may overstate how much of the gap has closed.
