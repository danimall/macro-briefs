## Executive Summary

ZoomInfo is a cash-generative business in secular revenue decline, wrapped in a capital structure containing a $2.73B contingent claim that sits ahead of the common equity. Both halves of that sentence matter, and the market has spent eighteen months arguing about which dominates. At $4.18 the stock trades at 4.74x trailing adjusted EBITDA of $493.9M excluding the Tax Receivable Agreement, 3.7x consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.12, and a 25-32% free-cash-flow yield depending on how one normalizes 2026. Include the TRA at book value and the multiple becomes 10.3x — a fully-priced, no-growth software asset. The entire valuation debate reduces to how much of that $2.73B an equity holder should capitalize.

The August 5 second-quarter print was genuinely better than feared: revenue of $310.4M (+1.2%), adjusted EPS of $0.28 against a $0.27 consensus (a fourth consecutive non-GAAP beat), adjusted EBITDA of $119.1M with margin up 210bps to 38.4%, and FY2026 revenue guidance raised to $1.207-1.217B from the $1.185-1.205B range set in May. Management also retired $58.5M of principal on its 3.875% notes for $47.1M cash — roughly 80.5 cents — an unambiguously accretive use of capital. The stock has recovered about 50% from the $2.78-2.94 area where the company itself was buying in June and July.

What the headline obscures is the shape of the guidance. FY2025 revenue was $1,249.5M and H1 FY2026 was $620.6M, so the $1,212M midpoint implies H2 FY2026 revenue of $591.4M against $637.1M a year earlier — a 7.2% decline — and, with Q3 guided to roughly $299.5M, an implied Q4 of $291.9M, or 6.0% below the second quarter just reported. Part of that step-down is a recognition-timing artifact of the shift from per-seat subscriptions to a platform fee plus prepaid data credits beginning in Q3. Part of it is demand. Net revenue retention was 89% at both June 30, 2026 and June 30, 2025, and customers above $100K of annual contract value went from 1,882 to 1,891 (+0.5%). The installed base is contracting roughly 11% per year with no evidence of improvement, and the company's own 10-K identifies the mechanism: as AI agents make individual sellers more productive, customers achieve the same outcomes with fewer seats. We are moving from AVOID to REDUCE because the cash generation and modest 2.3x net leverage do not support a thesis of imminent equity impairment, and two quarters of delivery deserve credit. We are not moving to HOLD, because the bounce has removed the margin of safety, the credit market is marking first-lien paper at 88 cents at 1.1x first-lien leverage, and the guided trajectory is down.

## Company Overview

ZoomInfo (legal registrant name unchanged; ticker converted from ZI to GTM) sells go-to-market intelligence to B2B sales, marketing, revenue-operations, and recruiting teams through a single reporting segment. The platform stacks three layers: a proprietary contact-and-company database (contacts, firmographics, technographics, buying-intent signals), an orchestration layer that enriches and routes data into customer CRMs, and an engagement layer covering sequencing, conversation intelligence, ABM, chat, and recruiting workflows. Branded products include ZoomInfo Copilot, Sales, Marketing, Operations, Talent, the newer GTM Studio and GTM Workspace, and a freemium tier, ZoomInfo Lite.

Two structural features are specific to this business rather than generic to software. First, the database is partly self-reinforcing: paying customers and free users contribute CRM and contact data back, and the 10-K flags dependence on this contributory network as a discrete risk — slowing contribution or accelerating opt-outs degrades data freshness, which is the product. Second, the standard contract contains a data-accuracy guarantee permitting termination and fee reimbursement if accuracy falls below a specified threshold, converting data quality into a contingent financial liability.

Scale: more than 35,000 customer companies, 1,891 above $100K ACV representing over half of total ACV, 53% of contract value under multi-year agreements, and no customer above 10% of revenue or receivables. Q2 FY2026 revenue was 89% United States ($276.6M) and 11% rest of world ($33.8M), with the international share down from 12%. Compute runs predominantly on Google and Amazon infrastructure as a customer, and cost of service is guided to rise slightly as a percentage of revenue on AI inference and Copilot onboarding costs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Audited annual revenue: FY2022 $1,098.0M, FY2023 $1,239.5M (+12.9%), FY2024 $1,214.3M (−2.0%), FY2025 $1,249.5M (+2.9%). H1 FY2026 was $620.6M versus $612.4M (+1.3%). The FY2026 guidance midpoint of roughly $1,212M is a 3.0% decline from FY2025 and 3.6% below the original February guidance midpoint of ~$1,257M. The revenue base has been flat for three years and is now guided lower.

**Margins.** Gross margin remains high but is eroding: FY2025 84.0%, Q2 FY2026 82.7% versus 83.9% a year earlier, roughly 120bps of compression consistent with rising AI inference and onboarding costs. GAAP operating income has been volatile (FY2023 $259.5M, FY2024 $97.4M on SG&A of $295.3M, FY2025 $225.7M). Non-GAAP profitability is improving on cost reduction rather than volume: Q2 adjusted EBITDA of $119.1M (+7%) at a 38.4% margin versus 36.3%, H1 adjusted EBITDA of $237.3M versus $219.0M (+8%), TTM adjusted EBITDA of $493.9M. The May 2026 restructuring eliminated approximately 600 positions, roughly 20% of headcount.

**The goodwill impairment.** Q2 carried a $650.5M goodwill write-down, taking goodwill from $1,692.7M to $1,042.2M, with a May 31, 2026 measurement date and fair value estimated by equally weighting a DCF and a guideline-public-company approach. It reduced GAAP net income (a $(2.19) per-share quarterly loss), total assets, and retained earnings, and distorted the effective tax rate. It did not touch revenue, adjusted EBITDA, operating cash flow, cash taxes, or covenant EBITDA. What it does represent is management's audited statement, at a $2.78-share-price environment, that the reporting unit is worth $650M less than carried, with "a decline in planned revenues and earnings as a result of key changes in strategy" among the cited triggers.

**Balance sheet.** Two line items dominate. Deferred tax assets of $3,624.9M are 63.8% of the $5,678.5M total, arising from the pre-IPO Up-C basis step-up. The mirror item is the Tax Receivable Agreement liability of $2,726.4M, roughly 75% of the DTA. Per share on 292.3M shares: TRA $9.33, DTA $12.40, book equity $2.84. Tangible book equity is negative $406.2M (−$1.39/share), and remaining goodwill of $1,042.2M still exceeds total book equity of $828.7M. Retained earnings swung from +$436.6M to −$177.8M. Any mechanical distress screen will fail this company, driven by the DTA inflating assets and the TRA inflating liabilities; that output is not a solvency verdict, but it correctly flags that a very large non-operating claim stands ahead of common.

**Debt.** $1,270.7M principal: a $579.2M first-lien term loan (SOFR+175, 5.64% effective, February 2030), $100M drawn revolver (2028), and $591.5M of 3.875% notes (February 2029). Net leverage reproduces to 2.27x ($1,120.6M net of $150.1M cash and short-term investments, over $493.9M), first-lien net leverage 1.1x. Liquidity is $147.6M cash plus $176.0M revolver availability. Swaps fix $425M of notional at 3.280% to February 2028.

The striking disclosure is the fair-value table. The notes are marked at $479.1M on $591.5M principal (81.0 cents) and the term loan at $508.2M on $579.2M (87.7 cents), against roughly 94.0 and 99.2 cents at December 31, 2025. A first-lien loan does not fall eleven points in six months at 1.1x first-lien leverage for liquidity reasons. The plausible reading is that credit investors are discounting terminal value of the revenue base beyond the 2029/2030 maturities and, secondarily, the presence of the TRA claim. Thin secondary trading in a small, out-of-index structure could exaggerate the marks, and management's repurchase at 80.5 cents is an intelligent response to the dislocation rather than evidence of distress. Both readings can be true; the direction of the signal is unhelpful for equity.

**Cash flow.** H1 FY2026 operating cash flow of $202.0M less $41.7M capex gives $160.3M of free cash flow, against $228.1M and $191.3M a year earlier — an 11.4% OCF decline on 1.3% revenue growth. Identifiable drivers: a smaller receivables tailwind, a $13.1M unearned-revenue decline versus $5.6M, $30.5M of restructuring cash out versus $9.6M, and $8.7M more cash interest. FY2025 full-year OCF was $465.4M with $76.1M capex for $389.3M of FCF, a 31.9% yield on today's capitalization. Doubling H1 gives $320.6M (26.2% yield) as a sensitivity, not a forecast; adjusting for non-recurring restructuring and litigation cash out, seasonally front-loaded collections, and the 10-Q's explicit warning that the pricing transition shifts billings and collections to later periods, a FY2026 range of roughly $300-340M is defensible. That is a 25-28% yield.

One definitional caution: H1 booked $40.2M of income tax expense against a $574.2M pre-tax loss while receiving $5.1M net in cash refunds, with $36.9M of deferred tax added back in the cash-flow statement. The GAAP tax line here says almost nothing about cash economics.

**Capital allocation.** Share count fell from 342.0M (12/31/2024) to 307.3M (12/31/2025) to 292.3M (6/30/2026) — 14.5% in eighteen months. But repurchase spending fell as the price fell: $241.3M at a $9.86 average in H1 FY2025, $118.7M at $6.13 in H1 FY2026, and only $7.5M at $2.94 in July, against $1,111.9M of remaining authorization. The H1 FY2025 tranche would cost $102.3M today, a ~$139M arithmetic differential. In H1 FY2026 the company directed $122.4M to stock, $47.1M to its own discounted bonds, and $50.1M to debt repayment. Two readings — prudent liquidity preservation ahead of an uncertain transition, or reduced conviction in the equity — cannot be distinguished from the filings, and 10b5-1 mechanics and blackout windows constrain pacing in ways not disclosed.

The mathematical core of the bull case: $300-340M of FCF at $4.18 could retire roughly 72-81M shares, 25-28% of the count, per year. Against that, 22.37M unvested RSUs (7.65% of shares over ~1.6 years weighted) imply roughly 4.8% of annual gross dilution, plus 9.678M premium-priced CEO performance options (3.31% of shares) that should be probability-weighted rather than counted at face. Total anti-dilutive securities of 32.40M equal 11.08% of shares outstanding, or 9.98% post-issuance ownership dilution if all were issued — maximum contingent, not current, dilution.

## Growth Analysis

There is no revenue growth to analyze; there is a decline to characterize. The binding constraint is net revenue retention of 89%, unchanged year over year. That means the existing base shrinks roughly 11% annually and all reported growth must come from new logos, while enterprise logo count is effectively flat (+0.5%). Remaining performance obligations fell 6.4% in six months to $1,172.0M, though ZoomInfo's RPO definition (unearned revenue plus billings-based backlog beyond the current billing cycle) excludes renewal value, no year-earlier comparative is available, and Q4 is the seasonal bookings peak — so this is directionally consistent with the retention picture rather than independent evidence.

The mechanism behind the decline is disclosed by the company. The FY2025 10-K states that as AI agents become more sophisticated, "individual users may become substantially more productive, enabling customers to achieve similar outcomes with fewer seats," producing seat reductions at renewal even where value delivered is equal or greater. Per-seat pricing against agent-driven productivity is a structural mismatch, and 89% NRR is its observable form. This is consistent with what has happened across seat-based application software generally as enterprise technology budgets reallocate toward infrastructure and compute: the buyer is not spending less on technology, but is spending less on per-head licenses.

The response is a pricing overhaul beginning Q3 FY2026: a lower annual platform fee plus pre-purchased data credits consumed over time, converting primarily at renewal. Economically this is the correct direction — it reprices from headcount to data consumption, which should grow with agent activity rather than shrink with it. Execution is entirely ahead. Usage-based revenue was $4.8M in Q2, double the prior year but only 1.5% of total. The newly added risk factor is unusually specific: billing disputes, collectability, refunds and chargebacks, potential regulatory scrutiny of consumption billing, greater revenue-recognition complexity, a shift of billings and collections into later periods raising receivables and working-capital needs, and reduced revenue visibility with increased period-to-period variability. The transition simultaneously explains part of the guided H2 step-down and removes the ability to distinguish timing from demand for several quarters.

Estimate revisions are modestly constructive on the non-GAAP line: current-year EPS of $1.12 is up 1.6% over 30 days and 1.1% over 90 days; next year at $1.13 is up 1.5% over 30 days but down 3.1% over 90 days, and next-quarter estimates are down 4.8% over 90 days. Four consecutive non-GAAP beats of 4.8-11.9% support confidence in near-term EPS delivery. This is not the earnings-inflection signature — revisions above 30% with accelerating revenue — that would justify overriding a declining top line; EPS is being defended by cost reduction while revenue guidance was cut 5% in May and only partially restored in August.

## Valuation Assessment

Computed both ways on 292.3M shares at $4.18 ($1,221.9M market capitalization), net contractual debt of $1,120.6M, and TTM adjusted EBITDA of $493.9M:

| Basis | Enterprise value | EV/adj. EBITDA |
|---|---|---|
| Excluding TRA | $2,342.5M | **4.74x** |
| Including TRA at book ($2,726.4M) | $5,068.9M | **10.26x** |

On non-GAAP EPS the stock is 3.73x current-year consensus of $1.12 and 3.70x next year's $1.13. Trailing GAAP multiples are meaningless: the $650.5M impairment drives TTM GAAP EPS deeply negative, and the reported trailing/forward P/E of −2.32 in the data feed is inconsistent with the reported $0.38 EPS field — neither should be used.

Peer comparables in Interactive Media & Services are heterogeneous and mostly reported on GAAP: TRIP 75.6x, YELP 10.4x, QNST 10.9x, CARS 22.0x, SSTK negative, median 22.0x. The closest analogues are YELP and QNST — low-growth, cash-generative internet marketplaces at 10-11x. Applying YELP's 10.4x to GTM's $1.12 of non-GAAP EPS produces $11.65, which is below what the $15.00 high analyst target implies. That comparison is the bull case in one line, and it is also where the bull case breaks: YELP and QNST are not carrying 2.3x net leverage, negative tangible equity, a $9.33-per-share senior contingent claim, or guidance for a 7% second-half revenue decline. A substantial discount is warranted; the question is whether 4.7x versus 10.4x is the right size of it.

The TRA is the crux and deserves precision. Recipients are certain Pre-IPO Owners and Blocker Holders; payers are subsidiaries of ZoomInfo Technologies Inc.; the entitlement is 85% of cash tax benefits deemed realized from the basis step-up and pre-IPO NOLs, payable as savings are realized. No TRA payments were made in H1 FY2026 or H1 FY2025, so near-term cash drain in those periods was nil. The obligation may accelerate on certain changes of control and will accelerate on an early-termination election, at the present value of all future payments discounted at the lesser of 6.5% or SOFR+100bps, under assumptions favorable to recipients (sufficient future taxable income to use all deductions; NOLs used ratably over the shorter of statutory expiration or five years), with no clawback if the IRS disallows the underlying benefits. The 10-K states in the company's own words that this "may impair our ability to consummate change of control transactions or negatively impact the value received by owners of our common stock."

The inference — and it is inference — is that a $9.33-per-share claim capable of accelerating on a sale is a substantial deterrent to the take-private that would otherwise be the natural resolution for an asset at 4.7x EBITDA with a 26%+ FCF yield. Alternatives cannot be excluded: TRA holders are largely also equity holders and could negotiate, a transaction might be structured to avoid triggering acceleration, or the market may simply be pricing terminal decline with the TRA secondary. No filing characterizes the TRA as an absolute bar. But an equity holder should not assume the arbitrage will be closed by a buyer.

Consensus provides a useful reality check. Twenty-one analysts split 2 strong buy, 13 hold, 3 sell, 3 strong sell, with a mean target of $4.69 (+12%), a **median of $4.00 (−4% from spot)**, a low of $2.50 (−40%), and a high of $15.00 (+259%). The dispersion — a 6x spread between low and high — is the honest expression of a binary. The stock now sits above the median. Note also that on an inflection stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x multiple, consensus targets are usually stale floors; that logic does not apply here, because the revenue line is guided down and the sell-side skew is toward sells.

Valuation scores 4.0. The multiple is cheap on cash flow, but the cheapness reflects identified structural impairment — a contracting base, a self-diagnosed disruption to the pricing model, and a senior contingent claim — rather than a mispricing we can demonstrate. Cheapness without a proven recovery catalyst is the classic value-trap configuration, and this file has most of its markers.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive attack comes from three directions at once, and the filings and sell-side commentary agree on the shape.

From above, platform bundling: the 10-K names Salesforce, Oracle, Google, and Microsoft/LinkedIn. LinkedIn is the acute problem, functioning both as a free substitute for basic contact discovery and as a Microsoft-owned asset that can bundle Sales Navigator into enterprise agreements where ZoomInfo must be bought as a standalone line item.

From below, venture-funded specialists at lower price points. BTIG specifically named Apollo.io and Clay in its May 12 downgrade. This dovetails with Piper Sandler's observation of elevated churn in the software customer vertical — software companies were the best cohort in 2020-2022, are now the most cost-disciplined, and are the most capable of substituting or building.

From the side, LLM disintermediation. The 10-K's own language is the sharpest articulation: model providers "may incorporate business contact information, company intelligence and market insights directly into their models or make such data readily accessible through AI-enabled tools at substantially lower costs... or even at no incremental cost." If firmographic and contact data become a commodity feature of a general-purpose model, pricing power in the intelligence layer erodes and value migrates to orchestration and workflow, which is where GTM Studio and GTM Workspace are aimed. That migration is a plausible defense, but it is a race against a repricing.

A fourth, less-discussed vector runs through customer acquisition. The 10-K flags that AI-powered search reduces click-throughs and could "materially diminish the value of traditional SEO strategies." ZoomInfo historically acquired a large share of SMB customers through organic search, so the same technology shift compresses both revenue per customer and the funnel that replaces lost customers.

The pattern is not one bad quarter. A ~27% single-day drop followed Q2 2023; a May 2024 Piper Sandler downgrade cited SMB customer erosion with the target cut to $15 from $24; August 2024 brought a 16% drop on weak guidance plus a CFO transition; February 2026 a 6.4% drop on initial FY2026 guidance; May 2026 a 35% collapse on the guidance cut. The company deliberately moved upmarket to escape SMB churn, and the churn reappeared in enterprise software. Competitive position scores 2.5 on trajectory: share is being lost to both cheaper specialists and bundled incumbents, gross margin is compressing, and NRR is flat at a level that mathematically requires perpetual new-logo growth the company is not delivering.

## Risk Assessment

**Tax Receivable Agreement (highest-magnitude structural risk).** $2,726.4M carried, $9.33 per share, acceleration on certain changes of control at a discount rate of 6.5% or lower under recipient-favorable assumptions, no clawback. It suppresses the strategic-bid option and, in an acceleration scenario, could consume most or all of the equity value. Partially offsetting: the DTA and TRA are economically linked — a valuation allowance against the DTA would hit earnings but should reduce the TRA in parallel, and H1 FY2026 already recorded a $5.5M TRA remeasurement gain.

**Revenue durability and the pricing transition.** Guidance implies −7.2% H2 revenue and a Q4 6.0% below Q2. The transition to prepaid credits begins now, with disclosed risks spanning collectability, chargebacks, working capital, and reduced visibility. For at least two quarters, investors will be unable to separate recognition timing from demand.

**Privacy and data-broker regulation attacking the asset.** California's DROP platform went live January 1, 2026 under the Delete Act, propagating a single deletion request to all registered data brokers; New Hampshire and others are following; third-party intermediaries enable at-scale opt-outs; roughly 20 states have comprehensive privacy laws; Virginia HB638 would impose a $100,000 broker registration fee. Because the contributory network and database are the product, and the standard contract carries an accuracy guarantee with reimbursement exposure, industrialized opt-outs attack completeness rather than just the sales motion. Actual DROP opt-out volumes are undisclosed; the magnitude is unquantified.

**Litigation.** Two securities class actions (class periods 11/10/2020-8/5/2024 and 11/3/2025-5/11/2026, the latter filed 6/25/2026 alleging overstated confidence in revenue outlook, AI product growth, and "sustained improvement in net revenue retention"), four derivative suits, and four privacy/consumer-protection matters across Washington, Quebec, and Colorado. Settlement charges were $15.0M TTM and $12.2M in H1 FY2026, with $5.7M paid in cash. Lead-plaintiff deadline for the newest action was August 24, 2026. Allegations are unadjudicated; the awkward fact is that disclosed NRR did not improve year over year.

**Further impairment.** The Q2 test used a May 31, 2026 measurement date with a "sustained decrease in share price" as a stated trigger. Remaining goodwill of $1,042.2M exceeds book equity of $828.7M. The recovery to $4.18 relieves the immediate pressure; a return toward $3.00 revives it.

**Governance.** Classified board, removal only for cause with a 66⅔% vote, blank-check preferred, no written consent, exclusive Delaware forum, and a stockholders agreement giving founders board-designation rights at ≥5% voting power. Layered on TRA acceleration, the architecture is defensively oriented against exactly the outcome that would monetize the discount.

**Squeeze mechanics cut both ways.** Short interest is 17.9% of float (41.5M shares) at 3.4 days to cover. That constrains the attractiveness of an outright short and helps explain the velocity of the move from $2.78 to $4.18, but it also means a disappointing Q3 unwinds a crowded long-side bounce.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 54 contracts across eight expirations — so inference should be modest. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 96.5% against 69.5% one-year realized, a 27-point premium at the 76th percentile of the current chain, with a backwardated term structure (96.5% at five days versus 74.2% at six months). Post-print backwardation of that shape usually signals an unresolved near-term catalyst rather than a clean event; the August 24 lead-plaintiff deadline and continued post-bounce repositioning are candidates. Near-tenor put/call skew is elevated at 10.2% (five days) and 8.3% (twelve days), inverting to −7.2% at three months. Open interest and volume are overwhelmingly call-dominated (P/C OI 0.04, P/C volume 0.01), the signature of speculative upside positioning on a heavily-shorted bounce rather than institutional accumulation. The prior snapshot's 1,033.9% ATM IV reading is not credible and is disregarded.

The practical takeaway supports the recommendation: with realized volatility at 69.5% and five-day implied at 96.5%, options are expensive to own and attractive to sell against existing stock. Holders reducing exposure have a better-than-usual premium environment for covered calls; buyers of the story are paying up for optionality on an unproven transition.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $8.00 (+91%)

The cost program is working, and the pricing overhaul breaks the link between customer productivity gains and ZoomInfo revenue. Adjusted EBITDA margin of 38.4% holds or expands toward 40% on a $1.20-1.22B revenue base, producing $470-490M of EBITDA and $320-350M of FCF. Management spends the $1,111.9M authorization aggressively at these prices, retiring 20%+ of shares annually while continuing to buy back its own notes in the low 80s — each dollar of which is accretive twice, through the discount and through interest saved. Revenue stabilizes in FY2027 as credit-based contracts convert and usage revenue scales from 1.5% of total. On $480M of EBITDA at 6.5x with net debt down to ~$1.0B and shares near 268M, equity is worth roughly $7.90. Non-GAAP EPS of $1.13 at a still-discounted 7x supports the same area. The 17.9% short position provides fuel. Consensus already carries a $15.00 high target, so the sell-side upside case is more aggressive than ours.

### Bear Case — $2.00-2.25 (−46% to −52%)

Seat compression proves the dominant force and the credit transition accelerates it: customers use the renewal conversion as an opportunity to cut committed spend, and prepaid credits go unconsumed, producing both lower revenue and customer dissatisfaction. Revenue declines 8-10% in FY2027, gross margin continues compressing on AI inference costs, and cost cuts cannot keep pace after 20% of headcount is already gone. EBITDA falls toward $415M, FCF toward $230-250M as working capital absorbs the billings shift. The market applies 4x to a shrinking stream: $1.66B EV less $1.10B net debt is $560M of equity, roughly $1.92 per share. Credit marks at 81 and 88 cents suggest sophisticated debt investors already assign meaningful probability to this path. A further goodwill charge, an adverse securities settlement, or a DTA valuation allowance would each add pressure, and the TRA ensures no strategic buyer arrives to set a floor.

### Base Case — $4.50 (+8%)

Revenue declines 3-5% in FY2027 with margins roughly held at 38%, producing $460-470M of EBITDA and $290-320M of FCF. The buyback shrinks the count 8-12% net of dilution as management continues to split capital between equity, discounted debt, and deleveraging. The market holds it at 5x EV/EBITDA: $2.325B less ~$1.05B net debt is $1.275B across ~283M shares, or $4.51. Probability-weighting bear at 35%, base at 45%, and bull at 20% gives roughly $4.36 — approximately 4% above spot, with a left tail nearly six times the width of the central case's upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Expected value is roughly flat from $4.18 with a materially fatter left tail, which is a less-than-fair risk-adjusted return.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the current strength. The stock has advanced roughly 50% from the $2.78-2.94 area where the company was itself repurchasing, sits 4% above the $4.00 consensus median, and carries five-day implied volatility of 96.5% against 69.5% realized. For holders willing to retain a small position, we would cut exposure by half to two-thirds now and cap residual size at a level where a move to $2.00 is tolerable; writing near-dated calls against the remainder is well compensated at current premiums. New capital should not initiate.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.** (1) Net revenue retention printing above 92% for two consecutive quarters, which would mathematically break the 11% annual base contraction; (2) Q4 FY2026 revenue at or above $300M against the ~$291.9M implied by guidance, demonstrating the H2 step-down was recognition timing rather than demand; (3) usage-based revenue exceeding 5% of total with sequential revenue growth, evidencing that credit conversion expands rather than resets spend; (4) buyback spending returning to $60M+ per quarter, confirming management conviction at these prices; (5) senior notes recovering above 92 cents, signaling credit investors have re-underwritten terminal value.

**Downgrade triggers back to AVOID.** (1) Q3 or Q4 revenue below guidance with FY2027 revenue guided down more than 5%; (2) FCF conversion falling below $60M in a quarter as the billings shift consumes working capital; (3) a second goodwill impairment or a DTA valuation allowance without an offsetting TRA reduction; (4) any disclosure of TRA acceleration, early-termination election, or a change-of-control process that quantifies the equity haircut; (5) adjusted EBITDA margin falling below 35%, indicating cost cuts have exhausted their offset.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 FY2026 print in early November 2026 is the first quarter reported under the hybrid pricing model and the primary decision point — it is the earliest observation that separates recognition timing from demand. Interim monitoring items: the August 24 lead-plaintiff deadline and subsequent consolidated complaint, monthly Item 5 repurchase disclosures for buyback pacing, and the Q3 10-Q's debt fair-value table for credit marks.

## Conclusion

ZoomInfo presents the two most common ways to lose money in small-cap value: a low multiple that reflects a real, disclosed, structural decline, and a capital structure in which the common equity sits behind a claim larger than the market capitalization. The cash flow is genuine — $389.3M in FY2025, $160.3M in H1 FY2026, a defensible $300-340M for the full year against a $1.22B market capitalization — and 2.3x net leverage with no maturities before 2028 means the company has years of runway to prove the pricing transition works. Management is allocating capital intelligently at the margin, retiring notes at 80.5 cents and holding $1.11B of repurchase authorization.

Against that: net revenue retention of 89% flat year over year, enterprise logo count flat, guidance embedding a 7.2% second-half revenue decline and a fourth quarter 6% below the second, gross margin compressing on AI inference costs, a self-authored 10-K risk factor describing precisely how AI erodes per-seat revenue, industrialized privacy opt-outs attacking the database itself, two securities class actions, negative tangible book equity, a first-lien loan marked at 88 cents, and a $9.33-per-share Tax Receivable Agreement that accelerates on change of control and therefore removes the buyer who would otherwise close the gap between a 4.7x multiple and a 26% cash yield.

We are upgrading from AVOID to REDUCE because two quarters of non-GAAP delivery, a guidance raise, 210bps of margin expansion, and accretive debt retirement together argue the equity is not worth zero, and the prior rating implied a downside skew the cash generation does not support. We are not upgrading further because the 50% rally has consumed the margin of safety, the stock now trades above the $4.00 consensus median with 6 of 21 analysts at sell or strong sell, and the single most important verifiable metric — retention — has not moved. Composite score 3.4, essentially unchanged from prior work: better execution offsetting a worse guided revenue trajectory and a second class action. Trim into strength; revisit after the November print, when the first quarter of consumption-based revenue will show whether the diagnosis and the cure match.
