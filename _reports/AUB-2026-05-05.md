## Executive Summary

Atlantic Union Bankshares completed its acquisition of Sandy Spring Bancorp in April 2025, nearly doubling total assets to $37.6 billion and establishing itself as the largest regional bank headquartered in the lower Mid-Atlantic. On an adjusted operating basis, FY2025 EPS of $3.44 (+19% YoY) demonstrates the earnings power of the combined entity, supported by a 3.80% net interest margin and 20.4% operating ROTCE. The integration was completed in just six months, and management proactively sold $2.0 billion of CRE loans to de-risk the combined balance sheet.

However, the headline financials obscure a structural headwind: $146 million in purchase accounting accretion inflated 2025 NIM by approximately 47 basis points. As this accretion declines toward $60-80 million in 2026, core NIM reverts closer to 3.33%, compressing EPS toward the $2.90-3.10 range absent offsetting organic growth. The stock trades at 15.8x trailing GAAP EPS and ~1.73x tangible book value — a premium to the peer median P/E of 10.9x that reflects integration optimism but leaves limited margin of safety against accretion headwinds, CRE stress, and the current macro environment.

The consensus analyst target of $44 (median $45) implies 17-20% upside, and the 6-to-1 buy/hold ratio suggests Wall Street is positioned for continued earnings accretion. We rate AUB a HOLD with a growth disposition — the fundamental franchise is strong and the acquisition strategy is sound, but entry at current levels requires confidence in organic growth offsetting accretion decay and CRE credit normalization.

## Company Overview

Atlantic Union Bankshares operates through two segments: Wholesale Banking (CRE, C&I, equipment finance, wealth management, capital markets) and Consumer Banking (retail deposits, mortgage origination, brokerage). The bank operates across Virginia, Maryland, Washington D.C., North Carolina, and South Carolina, with 6.3% deposit market share in Virginia and 4.8% in Maryland.

The bank's growth has been acquisition-driven: StellarOne (2014), Xenith (2018), Access National (2019), American National (2024), and Sandy Spring (2025). This serial acquirer model has built scale but accumulated $1.73 billion in goodwill (4.6% of total assets). Management is now pivoting toward organic expansion with 10 new branches planned in North Carolina's Raleigh and Wilmington markets over three years.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** GAAP diluted EPS declined to $2.03 in 2025 from $2.24 in 2024, entirely due to $157 million in merger costs, $101 million in Day 1 CECL provisioning, and 48% share dilution from the Sandy Spring stock consideration. Adjusted operating EPS of $3.44 represents the true earnings power of the combined entity.

The $3.44 includes $146 million of purchase accounting accretion income, up from $40 million in 2024. This accretion contributed approximately 47 basis points to the reported 3.80% NIM. Stripping it out, core NIM is approximately 3.33% — essentially flat with 2024. The headline NIM improvement is transitory.

**Capital Position:** CET1 of 10.10% and total capital of 13.90% are well above regulatory minimums. TCE/TA of 7.85% is adequate but not excessive for a bank carrying $1.73 billion in goodwill. Tangible book value per share is approximately $21.67.

**Asset Quality:** NPAs rose to 0.42% of loans from 0.32%, driven by Sandy Spring PCD (purchased credit deteriorated) loans. Net charge-offs increased to 0.17% from 0.05%, primarily from two C&I relationships. Multifamily nonaccruals spiked from $132K to $45.4 million, entirely Sandy Spring-acquired. The allowance for credit losses at 1.16% of loans provides 256% coverage of nonaccrual loans.

**Dividend:** Annual rate increased to $1.48/share in 2026 (6.5% increase), yielding approximately 3.9% at $37.56. Payout ratio against adjusted operating EPS is approximately 43% — well covered.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term (2026-2027):** Consensus estimates $3.72 for FY2026 and $4.00 for FY2027. The path from $3.44 adjusted in 2025 to $3.72 in 2026 implies 8% growth, which must overcome the accretion headwind. If accretion drops by $60-80 million ($0.45-0.60/share after-tax impact on ~130M diluted shares), organic earnings growth of 15-20% is required just to grow EPS modestly. This is achievable through: cost synergies from Sandy Spring (target ~33% pre-tax cost savings), organic loan growth in North Carolina, and wealth management fee scaling.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter estimates are down 1.9% over 30 days, current year down 1.1%. This is a mild amber flag suggesting the market is marginally less confident in near-term execution, though not dramatically so.

**Organic Growth Initiatives:** The North Carolina expansion (10 branches, 86 ATMs) targets the Raleigh and Wilmington markets. Equipment finance provides nationwide diversification. Wealth management AUM expanded ~123% from the Sandy Spring combination. These are genuine growth vectors but will take 2-3 years to contribute meaningfully.

## Valuation Assessment

At $37.56, AUB trades at:
- 15.8x trailing GAAP EPS ($2.03) — inflated by merger charges
- 10.9x adjusted operating EPS ($3.44) — in line with peer median
- ~10.1x FY2026E EPS ($3.72)
- ~9.4x FY2027E EPS ($4.00)
- 1.73x tangible book value ($21.67)
- 3.9% dividend yield

**Peer Comparison:** The peer median P/E of 10.9x (HOMB 10.9x, HWC 13.6x, OZK 7.7x, ASB 9.7x, AX 12.0x) puts AUB at a modest premium when measured on trailing adjusted EPS and at a material premium on forward GAAP. The peer median dividend yield of 3.1% positions AUB's 3.9% favorably. The price/tangible book premium reflects AUB's above-peer ROTCE (20.4% adjusted) but is vulnerable to any credit deterioration that impairs goodwill.

**What needs to go right:** AUB must demonstrate organic NIM defense (loan repricing, deposit cost discipline) to offset accretion decay, sustain credit quality through the CRE cycle, and execute on North Carolina expansion. If normalized EPS grows to the $3.72-4.00 range with a stable 10-11x multiple, fair value is $40-44. If accretion declines faster than expected or CRE losses accelerate, normalized EPS could stall at $3.00-3.20, implying $33-35.

The stock is not expensive on normalized forward earnings but provides limited margin of safety at current levels. The 10.1x forward P/E is fair, not cheap.

## Competitive Landscape

AUB's position as the largest regional bank in the lower Mid-Atlantic provides meaningful advantages: pricing power on larger commercial relationships, ability to invest in technology and compliance infrastructure at lower per-unit cost, and an attractive franchise for talent acquisition. The Sandy Spring integration expands the wealth management platform substantially and deepens Maryland market presence.

Competitive pressures include: (1) Truist, PNC, and M&T competing aggressively in the same footprint with greater resources; (2) fintech platforms capturing younger deposits; (3) credit unions with structural cost advantages in consumer lending. AUB is gaining scale through M&A while maintaining community banking relationships, but it competes in a contested market without a structural moat beyond scale and relationships.

The DC Metro concentration (particularly for CRE) introduces specific vulnerability to federal government employment trends. DC unemployment rose to 6.7% from 5.3% in 2024, and federal headcount reductions could pressure the region further.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (Primary Risk):** CRE at 60% of total loans ($16.7 billion) and 275% of capital is the defining risk factor. The $1.48 billion office portfolio (5.3% of loans) includes $431 million in DC Metro, where vacancy rates are elevated nationally. In an environment of elevated long-term rates, CRE refinancing costs are significantly higher than at origination for many borrowers. The $2.0 billion proactive CRE loan sale demonstrates management awareness but doesn't eliminate the remaining concentration.

**Accretion Cliff:** The $146 million accretion income is a wasting asset. Every quarter that passes without offsetting organic growth compresses earnings relative to the 2025 adjusted base.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** AUB is modestly asset-sensitive (+7.4% NII at +300bps), which means the current elevated rate environment is supportive. However, if the economy deteriorates and rates eventually fall, NII faces pressure.

**Integration Execution:** Completing Sandy Spring systems conversion in 6 months is aggressive. Customer attrition, cultural clashes, and personnel departures remain risks for 12-18 months post-integration.

**CFO Transition:** Robert Gorman retiring after ~14 years, replaced by Alexander Dodd from TD Bank. The hire signals ambition but introduces transition risk during a complex period.

**Short Interest:** At 8.4% of float with 8.9 days to cover, short interest is moderately elevated for a regional bank. This suggests a meaningful institutional cohort is positioned for CRE deterioration or earnings disappointment. Not at squeeze levels but not negligible.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($42-46):**
- Sandy Spring synergies fully realized, generating $100M+ in annual pre-tax savings
- North Carolina expansion contributes meaningfully to deposit and loan growth by 2027
- CRE credit losses remain contained (no systemic office deterioration)
- NIM stabilizes at 3.50%+ as organic loan growth offsets accretion decay
- FY2027 EPS reaches $4.00-4.20, trading at 11x = $44-46
- Dividend growth continues at 5-7% annually, yield support limits downside

**Bear Case ($30-33):**
- Accretion income declines faster than expected (or is restated)
- CRE losses accelerate, particularly in DC Metro office and multifamily
- Integration challenges emerge (customer attrition, key personnel departures)
- Rate environment shifts — either much higher (credit losses) or much lower (NIM compression)
- Normalized EPS stalls at $2.90-3.10, multiple compresses to 10x on uncertainty = $29-31
- Goodwill impairment test approaches if stock trades below tangible book for extended period

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Price decline to $33-34 (9x FY2026E EPS), providing sufficient margin of safety against accretion headwinds
- Two consecutive quarters demonstrating core NIM stability at 3.45%+ excluding accretion
- CRE nonaccruals stabilizing or declining without significant reserve builds

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE nonaccruals exceeding 1.5% of CRE portfolio ($250M+)
- Reported NIM declining below 3.30% (indicating accretion cliff hitting faster than organic growth can offset)
- Management announcing additional dilutive capital raises or cutting the dividend
- DC Metro office losses exceeding $50M in a single quarter

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely July 2026) will provide the first full quarter with normalized accretion levels and initial North Carolina branch economics. This is the natural reassessment point.

## Conclusion

Atlantic Union Bankshares has built an impressive regional banking franchise through disciplined M&A execution, and the Sandy Spring acquisition positions it as a scale player in the attractive Mid-Atlantic market. Adjusted operating metrics (20.4% ROTCE, 3.80% NIM, 49% efficiency ratio) demonstrate the earnings power of the combined platform.

At $37.56 the stock prices in successful execution without adequate compensation for three specific risks: (1) the accretion income cliff creating a ~$0.45-0.60/share headwind annually, (2) CRE concentration at 275% of capital during a period of acute commercial real estate stress, and (3) a macro environment where elevated long-term rates directly impair CRE refinancing economics. The trailing GAAP P/E of 15.8x reflects merger-depressed earnings and is not directly comparable to the peer median of 10.9x; on adjusted operating EPS, AUB trades in line with peers at 10.9x.

We rate AUB a HOLD with a growth disposition. The 3.9% dividend yield provides income while waiting for either a valuation correction providing better entry (below $34) or fundamental evidence that core earnings momentum can sustain EPS growth without purchase accounting tailwinds.
