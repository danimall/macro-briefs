## Executive Summary

Merck reported June-quarter revenue of $16.61B (+5%) against consensus of $16.36B, raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $66.3–67.3B from $65.8–67.0B, and cut FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $2.66–2.76 from $5.04–5.16. The guidance cut is arithmetic, not operational: Merck's non-GAAP definition does not exclude in-process R&D charges from asset acquisitions, so $3.62 per share for Cidara (January 2026) and $2.31 per share for Terns (closed ~May 2026) flow directly through the reported figure. Adding both back gives roughly $8.64 of underlying non-GAAP EPS against the prior implied $8.72 and FY2025's actual $8.98. Revenue is up ~2.8% at the guidance midpoint while underlying earnings are down ~3.8%, negative operating leverage consistent with Q1 gross margin of 74.2% versus 78.0%, rising intangible amortization, and a gross-to-net deduction rate on U.S. sales that moved from ~19.8% to ~21.5%.

The valuation looks inexpensive and is the crux of the debate. At $126.425 the shares trade at 13.1x FY2027 consensus EPS of $9.65 and 14.6x FY2026 underlying earnings, against a pharmaceutical peer median P/E of 20.0. The discount is defensible only if you accept two things the evidence does not yet support. First, FY2027 consensus assumes no further asset-acquisition charges from a company that has taken $5.93 per share of them in a single year and has spent roughly $26B on three deals in nine months; normalizing $1.50–2.00 per share of annual IPR&D expense puts the effective multiple near 16x, essentially level with Bristol Myers at 15.9x, the other large-cap facing a concentrated cliff. Second, Keytruda's share of consolidated revenue is rising, not falling: 48.8% in FY2025, 49.3% in Q1 2026, 50.4% in Q2. The franchise faces two independent 2028–2029 events (U.S. compound patent expiry December 2028 with guided biosimilar entry 2028–2029, and an IRA-set price effective 1 January 2029 that management itself says will cause U.S. sales to "decline materially"). The replacement cohort — Winrevair, Ohtuvayre, Capvaxive, Welireg, Enflonsia — runs at roughly $4.0B annualized against a franchise above $32B.

Score trajectory has declined from 6.6 (February) to 5.0 (July) across four consecutive HOLDs, driven by competitive position (8.0 → 5.0), valuation (7.0 → 5.0) and management quality (7.0 → 5.5). The Q2 print does not extend that decline: the price is roughly flat versus the July report ($127.50 then, $126.425 now) while revenue guidance rose and underlying earnings guidance held within eight cents. That argues for stabilization rather than further deterioration, and the overall score ticks to 5.2. It does not argue for an upgrade. The Halozyme litigation attacking Keytruda Qlex, already producing a German injunction prohibiting sales, has a decision cluster in September–October 2026, and Merck appears to be trailing in the PD-1×VEGF bispecific class that produced the first positive overall-survival readout against a PD-1-plus-chemotherapy control at ASCO 2026. Those two unresolved binaries determine whether the bridge across the cliff holds. HOLD, classified uncertain.

## Company Overview

Merck reports two segments. Pharmaceutical generated $14,349M of Q1 2026 sales (88% of total), spanning oncology, vaccines, cardiometabolic, HIV and hospital acute care, sold through wholesalers, retailers, hospitals and government programs, including significant pediatric vaccine volume through the U.S. CDC Vaccines for Children program. Animal Health generated $1,791M (11%), growing 13% reported and 6% ex-FX, with Bravecto at $379M (+16%) and a new second-generation canine JAK inhibitor (Numelvi) approved February 2026.

The structural fact is Keytruda. Q1 2026 Keytruda plus Keytruda Qlex was $8,034M of $16,286M total revenue (49.3%) and 56.0% of Pharmaceutical sales; Q2 was $8.37B of $16.61B (50.4%). The moat is the indication estate rather than the molecule: 40+ U.S. indications across 19 tumor types plus two tumor-agnostic labels, with an expanding combination web (Padcev with Pfizer/Astellas, Trodelvy with Gilead, Lenvima with Eisai, Merck's own Welireg). Merck also books a substantial alliance-revenue line where partners are the principal — for Reblozyl, Bristol Myers sells the product and pays Merck a tiered 20–24% royalty; Merck neither manufactures nor sells it.

A data conflict merits flagging: the company profile lists last earnings as 30 April 2026, while the June-quarter results discussed here were reported on 4 August 2026. Q2 detail comes from the company release and press coverage; the Form 10-Q was not available at the information cutoff, so Q2 balance-sheet and cash-flow detail is unverified against a primary filing.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 base.** Revenue $64,972M (+1.3%), pretax income $21,067M, net income attributable to Merck $18,254M, GAAP diluted EPS ~$7.28, non-GAAP EPS $8.98 (confirmed in the proxy's pay-versus-performance table). The effective tax rate implied by $21,067M pretax against $18.25B net income is roughly 13.3% — low, and itself an exposure given open IRS examinations.

The quality-of-earnings item: operating cash flow fell from $21.5B to $16.5B (−23%) while net income rose. Management attributes this to cash taxes of $6.1B versus $3.9B and upfront collaboration payments of $3.0B versus $1.1B. Both are real cash. A Piotroski reading of 5/9 fails on OCF-below-net-income, rising leverage (long-term debt/assets 34.2% versus 29.5%), falling asset turnover (0.475 versus 0.548) and falling ROA (13.3% versus 14.6%). Three of those four are mechanical consequences of adding $19.8B of assets that generate no current revenue. The accrual-quality flag is not.

Solvency is sound despite a screening output suggesting otherwise. Recomputing Altman Z from the FY2025 balance sheet with market equity at the current capitalization yields approximately 4.2 — safe zone — and the result is insensitive to the interest-expense estimate. Any distress reading on Merck is a data-mapping artifact.

**Q1 2026 (10-Q).** Revenue $16,286M (+5% reported, +3% ex-FX). GAAP pretax loss $(3,534)M, net loss $(4,240)M, diluted EPS $(1.72); non-GAAP EPS $(1.28). R&D expense went from $3,621M to $12,592M on the $9.0B Cidara charge. Backing that charge out gives underlying non-GAAP EPS of roughly $2.34 versus $2.22 a year earlier (+5.4%).

Cash mechanics matter. The Cidara charge is added back as non-cash in the operating-cash-flow reconciliation while the $8,779M cash outflow appears in investing — standard GAAP treatment for an asset acquisition, not aggressive presentation. Q1 operating cash flow was $3,918M (versus $2,500M), free cash flow $2,927M after $991M of capex. This should not be annualized: net changes in assets and liabilities were a $1,613M drag in Q1 2026 and $3,712M in Q1 2025, so quarterly timing dominates. A normalized frame anchored on FY2025 OCF of $16.5B less capex near $5B gives roughly $11–12B of annual free cash flow.

Balance-sheet movement in one quarter was substantial. Cash and equivalents fell from $14,565M to $5,327M; cash and investments from $15,521M to $6,807M. Total equity fell from $52,662M to $45,931M on the net loss, $2,114M of declared dividends and $925M of buybacks. Total debt was broadly flat near $49.1B, so net debt rose from roughly $33.8B to $42.3B on a balance comparison. Terns is being funded with a $6.0B 364-day delayed-draw term loan at SOFR+50bp stepping to SOFR+75bp, which management intends to refinance into long-term debt, pushing pro forma net debt toward $48B before H2 cash generation. Net debt/EBITDA runs roughly 1.6x at Q1 and ~1.8x pro forma using FY2025 pretax income plus estimated interest and D&A of about $27B.

Two rate-regime consequences deserve attention. Interest expense rose from $313M in Q1 2025 to $479M in Q1 2026 (+53%); with the Terns loan drawn from May, FY2026 interest could rise versus about $1.5B (estimated) in FY2025, a drag of roughly $0.25 per share [unverified — full-year FY2026 interest figure not in sources]. The refinancing of the term loan into long-term debt is a real exposure, though the specific long-end rate environment referenced here is [unverified — not in sources]. The legacy stack is a partial offset: fair value of debt ($44.7B) is $4.4B below carrying value ($49.1B), meaning existing coupons are below current market — an economic benefit invisible in reported equity, and one that erodes as the stack rolls.

**Capital allocation is the pressure point.** Against roughly $16.5B of FY2025 operating cash flow, Merck is committed to about $8.4B of annual dividends (2,470M shares × $3.40), roughly $3.0B of 2026 buybacks, and roughly $5B of capex — about $16.4B. The ~$26B of deal spend over nine months (Verona ~$10B, Cidara $9.2B, Terns ~$6.7B) is therefore essentially entirely debt-financed. Leverage remains modest in absolute terms; the shock absorber is being consumed ahead of the cliff rather than after it.

Financial health scores 6.3: solvency and cash generation are genuinely strong, but flexibility is narrowing — cash down ~64% in a quarter, net debt up ~$14B pro forma, interest expense up 53% year-over-year in Q1, and zero organic funding capacity for M&A after shareholder returns.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is modest and guided higher. FY2026 revenue guidance implies +2.0% to +3.6% over FY2025. H1 revenue grew 5% reported. Within that:

- **Winrevair $588M (+75%)** — the fastest grower, but carrying a 22% royalty to Bristol Myers in cost of sales, so structurally low-margin.
- **Capvaxive $184M (+42%)**; its royalty steps from 7.25% to 2.5% from 2027.
- **Keytruda Qlex $463M**, up from $128M in Q1 — conversion of 5.5% of the franchise from 1.6%, a steep trajectory off a small base.
- **Animal Health $1.78B** (Q2); Q1 grew 13% reported / 6% ex-FX.
- **Lipfendra (enlicitide)** approved 16 July 2026 as the first once-daily oral PCSK9 inhibitor, with reported Phase 3 LDL-C reduction of 56–59%. This is the most commercially significant approval in the portfolio: the injectable PCSK9 class has chronically underperformed on adherence and access. Counterweights are real — a STAT commentary argued prescribing patterns will not shift quickly on formulary and access grounds, and as a product launched during the MFN Agreement term Lipfendra is subject to most-favored-nation reference pricing and is named for the direct-to-patient program.

Against that, 2026 is a genuine loss-of-exclusivity year independent of Keytruda. Januvia and Janumet lost U.S. exclusivity in May 2026, Janumet XR in July; management expects to "lose nearly all U.S. sales." Bridion lost U.S. exclusivity in July 2026 and Merck expects to discontinue U.S. sales. Combining Q1 U.S. run-rates (Januvia $252M, Janumet $68M, Bridion $427M) gives roughly $3.0B of annualized U.S. revenue disappearing within about twelve months, or ~$4.2B including international. Gardasil is already broken: FY2025 $5.23B (−39%), Q1 2026 $1,069M (−19%), China shipments suspended since February 2025 with any 2026 resumption revenue expected to be immaterial.

FY2027 consensus of $9.65 implies +11.7% growth over FY2026 underlying $8.64. The identifiable building blocks: expiry of the 2.5% worldwide Keytruda royalty after 2026 (~$825M of pretax cost-of-sales relief on ~$33B of revenue, roughly $0.29 per share after tax), the Vaxneuvance/Capvaxive royalty step-down, launch ramps, and the absence of assumed IPR&D charges. That last item is the weak link. Restructuring will not help: management states the $3.0B of savings from the 2025 Program "will be fully reinvested into strategic growth areas," and the 2024 Program requires ~$4.0B of cost to deliver ~$750M of annual savings by end-2031.

The pipeline record in Q1 2026 was two-sided. Four meaningful approvals (Welireg adjuvant RCC, Padcev MIBC, Trodelvy 1L TNBC, Idvynso HIV) sat alongside three Phase 3 misses (LITESPARK-012 on both experimental arms, KEYNOTE-975, KEYNOTE-866) and outright termination of the MK-1308A coformulation program. Late-stage attrition at this program count is statistically normal; the pattern nonetheless indicates the Keytruda label-expansion engine is hitting harder indications.

Growth prospects score 5.0. Revenue grows through 2028; underlying earnings do not, and the 2029 wall is inside a reasonable investment horizon.

The trailing 35.7x figure is an artifact of the same charge treatment and should be discarded. The comparison that matters is 13.1x FY2027 consensus versus a peer median of 20.0x, PFE at 20.0x, BMY at 15.9x, JNJ at 27.6x. A 35% discount to the median needs a reason, and there is one: ~50% of revenue faces a double LOE within roughly 30 months against a replacement portfolio one-sixth its size.

The adjustment that changes the conclusion is the IPR&D normalization. Merck has structured three deals as asset acquisitions in nine months, expensing $5.93 per share in a single year, and its non-GAAP definition — more conservative than most peers — carries those charges through. FY2027 consensus assumes zero. Deducting a $1.50–2.00 per share annual run-rate lifts the effective multiple to about 16x, which is BMY's level. On that basis Merck is priced roughly in line with its closest structural comparable rather than at a discount to it.

The multiple has also already expanded materially. A year ago at $79.86, FY2026 underlying earnings of $8.64 implied 9.2x; today 14.6x. The discount to the peer median has narrowed from roughly 55% to 35%. Price appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, but multiple expansion of that magnitude is: the re-rating from cliff-panic toward normalcy has largely occurred, which removes most of the asymmetry that existed at the low.

What remains is yield support (2.69%, covered at 39% of underlying non-GAAP EPS and roughly 73% of normalized free cash flow), the quantifiable 2027 royalty tailwinds, and a genuine option on Lipfendra and Qlex conversion. Analyst consensus sits at a mean of $135.38 and median $139, range $105–155 — implying about 7% of upside to the mean. With the shares 6.4% below the 52-week high of $135.05 and estimate revisions essentially flat (FY2027 +1.4% over 90 days, +0.6% over 30 days), the consensus target is not a stale floor here; there is no accelerating revision signal to overtake it.

Valuation scores 5.5. Cheap on the headline, appropriately priced on normalized earnings, with yield support offsetting a structurally justified discount.

## Competitive Landscape

Three developments define the trajectory, and all point the same direction.

**Class displacement risk in PD-1.** At the ASCO 2026 plenary, Akeso reported HARMONi-6 data showing its PD-1/VEGF bispecific ivonescimab plus chemotherapy delivered a statistically significant overall-survival benefit with a hazard ratio of 0.66 versus a PD-1 inhibitor plus chemotherapy in first-line squamous NSCLC. Scope limits must be respected: the sources describe the comparator generically, not as pembrolizumab, and the trial geography and enrolled population are unconfirmed. Akeso's prior work in this program was conducted substantially in China against tislelizumab. This is not a head-to-head defeat of Keytruda. What it establishes is that the class has produced a positive Phase 3 overall-survival readout against a PD-1-plus-chemotherapy control in a major lung-cancer setting — the first credible evidence that PD-1 monotherapy backbones could be superseded rather than merely genericized. Summit Therapeutics has an FDA-accepted BLA in EGFR-mutant NSCLC with a PDUFA reported for November 2026 (single lower-tier source).

**Merck appears to be behind in that class.** It licensed a PD-1×VEGF bispecific from LaNova Medicines and showed first clinical data at AACR in April 2026 described as broadly comparable to the field's leaders, while declining to discuss Phase 3 plans. A late-July report indicated Merck posted and then withdrew a trial plan for a PD-1×VEGF plus TROP2-ADC combination. Merck's own pipeline chart in the Q1 10-Q (as of 30 April 2026) lists no PD-1×VEGF bispecific in Phase 3. Competitors have paid heavily for position — Pfizer $1.25B upfront to 3SBio in May 2025.

**The primary mitigant is under legal attack.** Keytruda Qlex, the subcutaneous form, is Merck's principal tool for retaining volume after IV biosimilars arrive. Halozyme alleges its modified-hyaluronidase patents cover berahyaluronidase alfa, an ingredient. A German court granted a preliminary injunction prohibiting Merck's sales after a December 2025 hearing (appeal decision expected Q2/Q3 2026). Halozyme asserts 15 patents in New Jersey and has counterclaimed in the Dutch action extending to seven additional European countries; the Dutch hearing was held at end-July 2026 with a decision expected within three months. Merck is winning on validity — 14 PGRs instituted at the PTAB with three IPR institution decisions expected late September 2026 — while losing on the ground in Europe, where EMEA revenue was $3,886M in Q1 (24% of total). Merck's own 10-Q conditions European Keytruda SC availability on this litigation and discloses possible additional royalties on Qlex net sales.

Offsetting these: 40+ Keytruda indications and deep prescriber familiarity remain a real moat through 2028; the combination approvals with Padcev, Trodelvy and Welireg extend the estate; Animal Health is growing double digits; Lipfendra is first-in-class in a category with a demonstrated adherence problem. A Keytruda biosimilar has already launched in Argentina with further small-market launches expected in 2026 at immaterial revenue impact — a useful leading indicator of biosimilar readiness.

Competitive position scores 4.5. Concentration is rising, the class-displacement threat is credible and Merck is not the pace-setter in it, and the mitigation asset is enjoined in one major market.

## Risk Assessment

**The double cliff.** U.S. Keytruda compound patent expires December 2028; two additional composition-of-matter patents expire May and November 2029 and Merck expects them litigated. Guided U.S. biosimilar entry is 2028–2029. Independently, Merck expects Keytruda to be selected for IRA price setting in 2027 with the set price effective 1 January 2029, and states U.S. sales "will decline materially after that time." Two mechanisms — volume loss and administered price reduction — arriving within roughly a year, against half of revenue.

**Halozyme decision cluster, September–October 2026.** German appeal, Dutch decision, and three IPR institution decisions all land within a quarter. Outcomes are wide in both directions: a 14-of-15 PGR institution rate suggests genuine validity vulnerabilities in Halozyme's portfolio, and preliminary injunctions are provisional. This is the single most consequential near-term binary and it is under-weighted in most coverage.

**Policy.** The MFN Agreement (December 2025, three-year term) commits Merck to MFN reference pricing on newly launched products — precisely the cohort meant to replace Keytruda — plus an unquantified obligation to "repatriate and share with the Federal government a portion of foreign revenue received as a result of the government's successful trade policy efforts." No amount, rate, cap or mechanism is disclosed, against $7,122M of Q1 international revenue (44% of total). The trigger is conditional and may never activate; it cannot be modeled, and the correct treatment is an explicitly unquantified policy-scenario downside. Paired offset: a three-year Section 232 tariff delay. Observable gross-to-net erosion is accelerating — U.S. discounts, chargebacks and rebates rose to $2.5B from $2.1B (+19%) while net U.S. sales rose 8%.

**Vaccine policy.** The January 2026 CDC schedule restructuring (single-dose adolescent HPV, RSV/hepatitis moved to high-risk tiers, rotavirus to shared decision-making) was preliminarily enjoined by a Massachusetts federal court on 16 March 2026, now on appeal to the First Circuit. Merck is the only U.S. manufacturer of MMRV (ProQuad) and varicella (Varivax) — protection and political concentration at once. AHIP member plans committed to no-cost-share coverage of ACIP-recommended immunizations only through end-2026.

**Tax and legal.** IRS NOPAs from April 2025 propose increasing the 2017 transition tax by ~$1.3B plus ~$260M of penalties, exclusive of interest, with 2021 and 2022 also under examination against a ~13.3% effective rate. Merck carries no insurance for most product liabilities: ~735 talc/mesothelioma cases, ~135 Gardasil POTS cases (with an October 2025 proposed resolution described as not material, subject to participation thresholds, and a California state trial re-set for 27 July 2026), the Cronin securities class action on China Gardasil disclosures (motion to dismiss filed 1 May 2026), a RotaTeq antitrust class action at certification, and two DOJ False Claims Act civil investigative demands.

Risk factors score 4.5 — the binary density is high, several items (Halozyme Europe, the MFN foreign-revenue clause) are not plausibly reflected in a 13x headline multiple, and the discount already embedded in the shares is attributable primarily to the patent cliff rather than to these.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($155, +22.6%).** Underlying earnings stabilize near $8.60–9.00 and grow to $9.65+ in 2027 on the Keytruda royalty expiry (~$0.29), the vaccine royalty step-down, and launch ramps. Merck wins the September PTAB cluster and the German appeal, clearing Keytruda Qlex in Europe; Qlex conversion continues at the observed pace (1.6% → 5.5% in one quarter) toward a share of volume that survives IV biosimilar entry. Lipfendra proves the oral PCSK9 thesis, with the injectable class's adherence gap converting to meaningful share. The four-of-four earnings beat record continues and the market re-rates a de-risked cliff toward 16x — the peer-adjacent multiple — on $9.65, giving roughly $155.

**Bear case ($100, −20.9%).** The Dutch decision extends the German injunction across Western Europe, removing the SC mitigant in a region generating 24% of revenue and imposing additional Qlex royalties. Ivonescimab or a peer bispecific establishes superiority in a large NSCLC setting, and prescribers begin migrating off PD-1 monotherapy backbones before the patent cliff arrives, pulling the erosion curve forward. Merck continues taking $1.50–2.00 per share of annual IPR&D charges on unproven assets, funded with debt refinanced at potentially higher long-end yields, while the MFN foreign-revenue provision activates. Gross-to-net erosion continues at the current ~170bp annual pace. Earnings power settles near $8.75 and the multiple compresses to 11x on visible 2029 impairment, giving roughly $100 — below the street low of $105, justified by the mechanism rather than by sentiment.

**Base case ($134, +6.0%).** Revenue grows 2–4% through 2028. Underlying non-GAAP EPS moves from $8.64 in 2026 to roughly $9.30 in 2027 — below the $9.65 consensus, reflecting continued gross-to-net erosion, full annualization of the Januvia/Janumet/Bridion losses, and ~$0.25 of incremental interest, partly offset by the royalty relief. Halozyme resolves partially in Merck's favor on validity while the European commercial position stays contested. Applying 14.4x — roughly the multiple the shares carry today on FY2025 non-GAAP — gives about $134. Total return including the 2.7% dividend approaches 9%.

The distribution is close to symmetric with a modest negative skew, and the base case sits below the analyst mean of $135.38. That does not support a BUY at this price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it should be high. The justification is that the Q2 print resolved the most important open question in Merck's favor — underlying earnings guidance held within eight cents while revenue guidance rose — but did nothing to resolve the two questions that determine the terminal value, both of which have dated resolution points inside the next four months. Continuing the HOLD is a statement about unresolved binaries, not about lack of analysis.

**Reassessment timeline:** late September 2026 (PTAB IPR institution decisions), late October 2026 (Dutch Halozyme decision, three months from the end-July hearing), and the Q3 print. The German appeal decision is expected within Q2/Q3 2026 and may arrive sooner.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A favorable September PTAB cluster combined with a reversal or narrowing of the German injunction, removing the litigation overhang on Keytruda Qlex in Europe. This is the single highest-value catalyst.
2. Keytruda Qlex conversion reaching 15%+ of franchise revenue by the Q4 2026 print, with a company-disclosed eligible-patient or conversion target — evidence the mitigation is sized to matter rather than merely growing.
3. Price toward $108–112, which would put the shares near 11.5x underlying 2027 earnings of $9.30 and restore an asymmetry the current price does not offer.
4. A credible Phase 3 entry in PD-1×VEGF, or Phase 2 data establishing Merck as competitive in the class rather than trailing it.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. An adverse Dutch decision extending the Halozyme injunction across Western Europe, particularly if paired with an unfavorable German appeal.
2. A head-to-head or near-head-to-head readout demonstrating a bispecific superiority against pembrolizumab specifically in a large indication, which would pull the erosion curve forward of the 2028–2029 patent dates.
3. A fourth large asset acquisition (>$5B, expensed) financed with incremental debt, taking pro forma net debt/EBITDA above roughly 2.5x while the dividend and buyback are maintained.
4. Underlying non-GAAP EPS guidance for 2027 issued below $9.00, confirming that negative operating leverage persists rather than reversing with the royalty tailwinds.

**Position guidance for holders:** the 2.69% yield is covered at 39% of underlying earnings and roughly 73% of normalized free cash flow, so income holders are not being asked to underwrite an at-risk payout. Do not add at this level; the multiple has already expanded from 9.2x to 14.6x on FY2026 underlying earnings over twelve months, and the September–October litigation cluster is a reasonable point at which to be sized rather than over-sized.

## Conclusion

Merck delivered a clean operating quarter — a revenue beat, a raised revenue guide, and an adjusted loss narrower than consensus — with headline EPS guidance cut for reasons that are entirely definitional. On underlying earnings the company is roughly flat to modestly down: revenue up ~2.8% at the FY2026 midpoint, underlying non-GAAP EPS down ~3.8%, gross margin down 380bp, gross-to-net deductions up 170bp. That is negative operating leverage in a business whose largest product is 50% of revenue and faces both biosimilar entry and an IRA-set price within a thirteen-month window beginning in late 2028.

The valuation appears to compensate at 13.1x FY2027 consensus against a peer median of 20x.
