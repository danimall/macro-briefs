**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Exelon is the largest pure-play regulated transmission and distribution (T&D) utility in the United States, serving roughly 10.7 million electric and 4 million gas customers across six operating utilities (ComEd, PECO, BGE, Pepco, DPL, ACE) in seven regulatory jurisdictions plus FERC. Since the February 2022 spinoff of its generation arm (Constellation), Exelon owns no power plants; commodity costs pass through to customers, so wholesale power-price swings do not affect net income. The company sits directly inside the PJM data-center electricity-demand surge: a committed pipeline of ~18 GW (with ~43 GW of high-probability potential), Northern Illinois data-center demand growing ~26% CAGR, and a transmission rate base compounding ~16% annually. This supports a $41.7B 2026-2029 capital plan, 7.9% blended rate-base growth to ~$87.4B by 2029, and an adjusted EPS CAGR near the top end of 5-7%, plus a ~3.6% dividend yield growing 5% per year.

The thesis is genuine, confirmed in regulated-utility hard data (the load growth is appearing in commercial KWh, transmission RTEP awards, and capex revisions), but it is capped on two sides. First, the same data-center boom that drives Exelon's transmission opportunity is spiking customer supply bills: PJM wholesale prices rose 70%+ year-over-year in 2025, and roughly 75% of bill increases are supply-driven costs Exelon merely passes through, yet customers and politicians blame the utility. PECO's voluntary withdrawal of its 2026 Pennsylvania rate cases citing affordability, and Maryland's 2026 Utility RELIEF Act tightening, are the clearest signs the political ceiling on rate increases is real. Second, the growth is funded with negative free cash flow (-$2.28B in 2025) and continuous equity dilution (~$850M/year), which compresses per-share growth and leaves the company dependent on capital-market access through a higher-for-longer rate environment.

On balance this is a reasonable mid-single-digit total-return compounder trading at ~16.7x trailing earnings and ~16x forward, a modest discount to the electric-utility peer median (P/E 22.5) that is largely explained by higher leverage (D/E ~3.05), below-ask regulatory ROEs at ComEd (8.905%), and the affordability/dilution overhang. Analyst sentiment is improving (one strong-buy added, four buys upgraded to seven, holds cut), and the mean target of $49.33 implies ~8% upside, but there is no asymmetric entry and no fundamental mispricing the market is failing to see. We rate EXC HOLD on a growth-plus-income basis, with an upgrade path tied to constructive resolution of the ComEd Grid Plan (order expected December 2026) and stabilization of the dilution/credit picture.

## Company Overview

Exelon operates a "wires-only" regulated T&D model. The six utilities map to seven state/federal jurisdictions:

- **ComEd** (~35% of rate base) — Northern Illinois/Chicago, 4.2M electric customers; ICC and FERC; operates under a 2024-2027 multi-year rate plan.
- **PECO** (~22%) — Southeastern PA/Philadelphia, electric and gas; PAPUC.
- **PHI utilities — Pepco/DPL/ACE** (~26% combined) — DC, Maryland, Delaware, Southern New Jersey.
- **BGE** (~18%) — Central Maryland/Baltimore, electric and gas; MDPSC.

The competitive structure is the classic regulated-monopoly one: legal franchise territories with cost-of-service recovery and an allowed return on a growing rate base. Differentiators within the peer group are scale and jurisdictional diversification (no jurisdiction is more than ~30% of the business, no single capital project more than ~3% of the four-year plan), top-quartile reliability for ten consecutive years, customer rates roughly 19% below the largest US cities, and a rare position as one of only four US operators of 765kV extra-high-voltage transmission across 11,197 circuit miles. Most utilities carry revenue decoupling, making revenue largely weather-neutral; PECO and DPL-Delaware are the exceptions. The company is a PJM member, placing it at the center of the largest US wholesale market.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue rose to $25.0B (2025) from $23.1B (2024), $21.0B (2023), and $16.9B (2022). Much of the revenue line is offset pass-through (purchased-power riders), so it overstates underlying business growth. GAAP net income to common reached $2,768M (diluted EPS $2.73) from $2,460M (EPS $2.45) in 2024; adjusted operating EPS was $2.77 (2025) vs. $2.50 (2024). Margins: operating 20.6%, net 11.1%, ROE 9.6%, operating ROE (the company's compensation metric) 9.7%. The earnings track record is consistent: four straight beats over the trailing four quarters, ranging from 1.8% to 9.6%, supporting confidence in near-term consensus.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets are $116.6B, of which net PP&E is $84.3B; the business is overwhelmingly physical infrastructure. Long-term debt of $42.8B against $28.8B of equity gives D/E ~3.05. The Altman Z-Score of 0.89 ("distress zone") and the sub-1.0 current ratio (0.92) are misleading in isolation: high leverage matched against a stable, regulator-approved rate base earning an allowed return is structurally normal for a rate-regulated utility, and not a distress signal. The credit story is investment-grade (ExCorp Baa2/BBB+ stable, ComEd A1/A), but under modest pressure from the capex ramp: Moody's placed PECO under review for downgrade (April 24, 2026) and S&P cut BGE's senior unsecured rating to A- (April 30, 2026). Management targets ~14% FFO/debt, 100-200bps above downgrade thresholds.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $6.25B but capex was $8.53B, producing negative free cash flow of -$2.28B. Exelon is in a heavy investment cycle and funds the gap with debt and equity. The negative-FCF/dilution model is the structural constraint on per-share value creation: the rate base compounds, but so does the share count and the debt load. Weighted-average debt maturity is ~16 years, and the company has executed ~43% of 2026 debt needs and ~37% of 2026-2029 equity needs via ATM forward contracts, which de-risks the funding plan but does not eliminate the dilution.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $0.42/quarter for 2026 (from $0.40), targeting a ~60% payout and 5% annual growth through 2029. Equity issuance is modest relative to size (~$850M/year, under 2% of market cap), and the use of forward sales shows reasonable financing discipline. The dividend is well-covered at a ~60% payout.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is the cleanest part of the story. The four-year capital plan was raised to $41.7B (2026-2029), supporting 7.9% blended rate-base growth to ~$87.4B by 2029, and an adjusted operating EPS CAGR near the top end of 5-7% off a $2.64-$2.74 2025 base. FY2026 guidance is $2.81-$2.91 (reaffirmed at Q1), and the total-return target is 9-11% (EPS growth plus the ~60%-payout dividend).

The differentiated element is transmission. Transmission rate base is growing ~16% CAGR versus 7.9% blended, earns higher allowed ROEs (10.35-11.5% including a 50bp RTO adder, versus ~9.5% distribution), uses annual formula-rate updates that reduce regulatory-lag risk, and carries $12-17B of upside beyond the plan (largely 2030+). The Q1 2026 plan revision shifted ~$1.1B out of state-jurisdiction distribution and +$1.5B into FERC-regulated transmission, a quality upgrade and a defensive pivot away from politically sensitive distribution spend. Major projects include Brandon Shores (~$1.5B reliability, in-service 2028), Tri-County Line ($1B+, 2030), MISO LRTP Tranche 2.1 ($1B+), and $1.2B of PJM RTEP Window #1.

The data-center theme is the demand engine: ~18 GW committed (with ~45% secured via Transmission Security Agreements), ~43 GW high-probability, and Northern Illinois demand at ~26% CAGR. ComEd announced 6.5+ GW of new TSAs in January 2026. This load growth is confirmed across the sector in hard utility data, which argues against the deceleration risk being seen at the chip layer.

The constraint on growth: estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, not the accelerating pattern that would signal an inflection. Current-quarter consensus was revised -2.6% in 30 days; next-quarter -3.4% in 90 days; current-year and next-year essentially flat. This is a steady mid-single-digit compounder, not an earnings-inflection story, so the growth score reflects durability rather than acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.56, EXC trades at ~16.7x trailing EPS ($2.73) and ~16x forward (FY2026 midpoint $2.86), with a 3.6% dividend yield. Against the electric-utility peer median (P/E 22.5, yield 3.3%), EXC trades at roughly a 26% discount. Peer dispersion is wide — VST 24.7x, ETR 28.1x, WEC 22.5x, PEG 17.4x, EIX 7.9x — reflecting heterogeneous business mixes (merchant generation, wildfire exposure, growth profiles).

The discount to peers is largely explained, not a clean mispricing: EXC carries higher leverage (D/E ~3.05), a below-ask ComEd allowed ROE (8.905% with 50% equity and denial of return on the pension asset), the affordability/political overhang, and a negative-FCF/dilution model that caps per-share growth. PEG is roughly 16x / 6% ≈ 2.7, which is unremarkable for a regulated utility. A Seeking Alpha DCF marks fair value near $51.74 (~13% upside), and the analyst mean target of $49.33 (median $49.00, range $41-$58) implies ~8% upside. These are floors-not-ceilings only on inflection stocks with rising estimates and sub-30x multiples; EXC has flat-to-falling estimates, so the targets here are reasonable anchors rather than stale lowballs.

The valuation does not screen as a value trap (there is a real, dated catalyst path) nor as asymmetric upside. The market is pricing the affordability and dilution risks roughly correctly. We score valuation modestly below neutral: cheapness alone is not a buy signal absent an independent catalyst, and the catalysts here (ComEd Grid Plan, Maryland normalization) are two-sided.

## Competitive Landscape

Exelon's moat is durable but not widening dramatically. Within PJM, its footprint covers a top-3 emerging data-center market (Northern Illinois) and Mid-Atlantic load, where its 765kV transmission experience, scale, and ability to win competitive PJM RTEP and MISO LRTP awards (often partnering with NextEra and Invenergy) are the most differentiated part of the story versus pure-distribution peers. The industry-first Transmission Security Agreement structure (making large loads pay for the infrastructure they require and protecting existing customers from cost-shifting) is now being embedded in Illinois large-load tariffs (FERC-approved) and Maryland/NJ legislation, which is a genuine first-mover advantage in serving hyperscaler demand.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on the transmission side, offset by deteriorating constructiveness on the state-distribution side (Maryland tightening, PECO's withdrawn rate cases, ComEd's appealed 2024 order). The mix shift toward FERC-regulated transmission is the right strategic response and the highest-quality element of the algorithm, supporting a competitive-position score modestly above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

**Affordability backlash and political/regulatory risk — the dominant emerging theme.** The utility absorbs the political blame for the supply-driven bill increases described above. ComEd bills are rising double-digits in summer 2026, fueling bipartisan Illinois backlash against data centers. PECO's withdrawal of its 2026 PA electric and gas rate cases is unusual: a utility voluntarily forgoing a rate increase signals a real political ceiling, raising the risk of less constructive rate outcomes and capex deferral.

**Maryland regulatory tightening.** The 2025 Next Generation Energy Act barred multi-year-plan reconciliation filings after 1/1/2025; the 2026 Utility RELIEF Act prohibits forecast test years until a PSC study completes (~April 2027), scales back EmPOWER, and lowers the large-load tariff threshold to 25 MW/60% load factor. Pepco MD has pivoted to a traditional historical test year.

**Rate-case execution.** ComEd is appealing its 2024 order (8.905% ROE). Pending: Pepco MD ($119.9M, 10.5% ROE request, order ~Aug 2026) and DPL DE ($47.6M, order Q3 2027). The ComEd Grid Plan (ICC Docket 26-0047, $15.3B of 2028-2031 investment, order expected December 2026) is the single largest catalyst and risk.

**Credit pressure.** Heavy capex strains metrics; PECO is under review for downgrade and BGE was downgraded to A-. Downgrades raise borrowing costs and can trigger collateral posting on PJM/gas contracts.

**Funding dependence.** Negative FCF, a ~60% payout, and ~$850M/year equity issuance leave Exelon reliant on continuous capital-market access; a higher-for-longer rate path directly compresses the model's per-share economics.

**Data-center conversion risk.** The pipeline may ramp over up to ten years and differ from initial estimates; only ~45% is TSA-secured. Other risks: cybersecurity/physical security, storm/wildfire exposure, transformer supply-chain shortages, $6.6B goodwill (regulatory-outcome-sensitive), $325M 2026 pension contribution, and ComEd's Chicago franchise, terminable on one year's notice with a municipalization option (neither party has acted).

Two overhangs were cleared: the ComEd FERC audit settled in April 2025 for a manageable $70M disallowance, and DOE IIJA/IRA grant terminations (Oct 2025) had no material financial impact.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 21.4% versus 18.4% one-year historical vol, a fair 3.0% spread, with IV in the 13th percentile of its own chain. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.3%), and put/call volume (0.29) and OI (0.65) ratios are unremarkable. The implied one-month move is ±4.5%. The term structure shows mild backwardation (near 21.4% > long-term 19.3%), pointing to some imminent-event pricing consistent with the regulatory and rate-case calendar, but nothing extreme. The reported ATM IV jump to 391.7% in the options-delta feed is a corrupted thin-options artifact and is non-informative. Net read: the options market sees roughly average uncertainty and is not flagging a directional catalyst, consistent with the HOLD thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The data-center demand surge converts to approved, recoverable rate base; the ComEd Grid Plan clears constructively in December 2026; Maryland's regulatory study resolves toward a workable framework by April 2027; and the $12-17B of post-2029 transmission opportunity moves into the plan. Transmission's ~16% rate-base CAGR at 10.35-11.5% ROEs carries the EPS algorithm to the top of the 5-7% range, the dividend grows 5%, and the discount to peers narrows. On ~$3.20-$3.30 EPS in the bull window at ~20x (toward peer multiples), the stock approaches the high-$50s to low-$60s. Bull target ~$58, matching the high analyst target.

**Bear Case.** The affordability backlash intensifies; more rate cases are withdrawn, delayed, or decided below ask; ComEd's appealed ROE stays depressed; credit downgrades raise borrowing costs and collateral needs; and the higher-for-longer rate path makes the ~$850M/year equity issuance increasingly dilutive against negative FCF. EPS growth slips to the low end of the range or below, and the multiple compresses toward the low-distribution-utility cohort. Downside to ~$41-$42 (the low analyst target and the rough 52-week-low zone), roughly -8% to -10% from current.

The risk/reward is roughly balanced-to-modestly-favorable: ~8% to the mean target and ~27% to the bull case against ~8-10% downside, with a covered 3.6% dividend cushioning the wait. The lack of an asymmetric edge and the flat-to-negative estimate revisions keep this a hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, anchored to the December 2026 ComEd Grid Plan order, the August 2026 Pepco MD rate order, the April 2027 Maryland PSC study completion, and quarterly earnings/guidance reaffirmations.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. ComEd Grid Plan (Docket 26-0047) clears with a constructive ROE/equity ratio and broad capex approval, de-risking the largest single element of the plan.
2. Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year/next-year consensus rising rather than flat-to-down) alongside reaffirmation of the top-end 5-7% EPS CAGR, signaling the data-center load is converting to recoverable rate base faster than modeled.
3. Stabilization or improvement in the credit picture (PECO review resolved without downgrade; FFO/debt holding ~14%), reducing the dilution/funding overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A further rate-case withdrawal or a materially adverse rate order (well-below-ask ROE) that signals the affordability ceiling is forcing capex deferral and breaking the 5-7% EPS algorithm.
2. A credit downgrade at ExCorp or a second operating-utility downgrade that raises financing costs and triggers collateral requirements.
3. The 2026 EPS guidance ($2.81-$2.91) is cut, or the dividend-growth commitment (5%/year) is reduced.

## Conclusion

Exelon offers a credible mid-single-digit total-return profile: a ~3.6% covered yield growing 5% per year plus a 5-7% (top-end) EPS CAGR backed by a $41.7B capital plan and confirmed PJM data-center load. The transmission mix shift is the highest-quality element of the story and a sensible defensive response to a real affordability ceiling. The same boom that drives the opportunity is spiking customer bills and inviting political pushback (PECO's withdrawn rate cases, Maryland's tightened regime), and the negative-FCF/equity-dilution model compresses per-share growth against a higher-for-longer rate backdrop. At ~16x forward and a peer discount that is largely explained rather than mispriced, the risk/reward is balanced with no asymmetric entry. The declining score trajectory (6.4 → 6.0) reflects the regulatory and credit pressures genuinely tightening, not price action; the stock is roughly flat to its prior-report level. We rate EXC **HOLD (growth_hold)**, holding for the income-plus-compounding, and would upgrade on a constructive ComEd Grid Plan outcome and positive estimate revisions, or downgrade on an adverse rate order, further credit downgrade, or a guidance/dividend cut.
