## Executive Summary

CVR Energy produced its strongest operating quarter since 2024 and its shareholders lost money on it. Q2 2026 crude utilization reached 98.4% (versus 76.9% in the turnaround-suppressed prior year), throughput was 212,965 bpd, the Group 3 2-1-1 crack averaged $44.91/bbl against $24.02/bbl a year earlier, ammonia utilization ran 99%, and realized ammonia gate prices rose 33% year over year. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was $209 million. Earnings per share attributable to CVR Energy stockholders were negative $0.03, and negative $1.94 for the first half. Two mechanisms explain the entire gap. First, Renewable Fuel Standard compliance cost $11.16 per barrel in the quarter — more than the $9.94 per barrel of reported refining margin — because CVI's subsidiaries cannot blend the majority of their transportation fuels and must buy RINs outright. Second, CVR Energy consolidates 100% of CVR Partners' revenue, EBITDA and $550 million of secured notes while retaining roughly 37% of the LP economics; in 1H26 a $114 million consolidated net loss became a $195 million loss attributable to CVI after $81 million of income was allocated to the MLP's minority holders.

The valuation does not compensate for this. At $31.37 the stock sits above the consensus mean target of $29.60 and the median of $29.00, with an analyst distribution of zero buys, two holds, three sells and one strong sell. Enterprise value of roughly $4.20 billion is 8.1x LTM consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $517 million; adjusting to the EBITDA that actually accrues to CVI shareholders (Petroleum $248 million plus 37% of Nitrogen's $276 million, or about $350 million) against parent enterprise value of roughly $3.77 billion gives about 10.8x for a two-refinery, 206,500 bpd inland refiner during what management describes as an above-mid-cycle margin environment. Next-year consensus of $1.91 puts the stock at 16.4x against a peer median P/E of 10.8x (PARR 6.2x, PBF 10.8x, REX 15.3x). Current-year consensus was cut 49.8% in the last 30 days to $0.37.

We are downgrading to REDUCE from three consecutive HOLDs, and the overall score falls to 4.3 from 4.7. The prior improvement in scores (3.6 in May to 4.7 in July) reflected the crack-spread cycle and a competently executed February refinancing. The Q2 print tested whether that cycle converts into attributable earnings and clean cash flow, and the answer was no: adjusted EPS of $0.34 missed the $0.45 consensus by 25%, near-term estimates turned down, 1H26 operating cash flow of $371 million was substantially funded by a $336 million increase in an accrued-but-unsettled RFS liability, and Q3 guidance implies sequential price declines in fertilizer alongside a 40-day East Dubuque turnaround. The price decline from $35.13 in mid-July to $31.37 is accompanied by identifiable estimate deterioration rather than occurring against intact fundamentals.

## Company Overview

CVR Energy is a downstream holding company in Sugar Land, Texas, operating two reportable segments after the Renewables segment was eliminated in Q1 2026 (the Wynnewood renewable diesel unit was reverted to hydrocarbon service in December 2025 on unfavorable economics; the option to switch back is retained).

**Petroleum** comprises the Coffeyville, Kansas and Wynnewood, Oklahoma refineries, with 206,500 bpd of consolidated crude capacity selling into the Group 3 / PADD II market, plus crude gathering and logistics (113,627 bpd of Q2's 212,965 bpd throughput was gathered).

**Nitrogen Fertilizer** is held through CVR Partners, LP (NYSE: UAN) — Coffeyville (pet coke gasification feedstock) and East Dubuque (natural gas feedstock), producing ammonia, UAN and urea.

The ownership structure governs how the financials should be read. Icahn Enterprises and affiliates own approximately 71% of CVI common stock. CVI owns approximately 37% of CVR Partners' common units and 100% of the general partner interest, which triggers full consolidation of a partially owned entity. Public float is roughly 29.3 million shares, about $913 million at the current price. CVI relies on NYSE controlled-company exemptions and is therefore not required to maintain a majority-independent board or fully independent compensation and nominating committees.

FY2025 GAAP net income of $27 million is not a usable earnings figure. It embedded a non-cash benefit of approximately $488 million from reducing the accrued RFS liability after the EPA's August 2025 small refinery exemption decisions (which reduced Wynnewood's obligations by more than 424 million RINs), partially offset by roughly $354 million of RIN revaluation and lower RIN sales for a net favorable refining-margin contribution of $93 million. This affects margin, ROA and ROE inputs; it does not invalidate leverage or liquidity ratios, and Adjusted EBITDA (which excludes RFS mark-to-market) is the more comparable measure.

### Where the Q2 margin went

The capture-rate reconstruction is the single most decision-relevant calculation for this business. Per total throughput barrel (19,379,847 barrels in Q2):

| Q2 2026 | $/bbl |
|---|---|
| Group 3 2-1-1 benchmark | 44.91 |
| Reported GAAP refining margin ($193M) | 9.94 |
| Add back unfavorable change in RFS obligation ($73M) | +3.77 |
| Less favorable inventory valuation ($19M) | −0.98 |
| Less unrealized derivative gain ($6M) | −0.31 |
| **Realized margin ($241M)** | **12.43** |
| Add back net RIN expense ($216M) | +11.16 |
| Add back realized derivative losses ($81M) | +4.18 |
| **Margin before RFS and hedging** | **≈27.77** |

The $12.43/bbl step recomputes to management's disclosed realized margin and 28% capture rate, which validates the reconciliation. The refineries themselves capture roughly 62% of the Group 3 benchmark, a defensible outcome for an inland sub-octane refiner with a 45.4% distillate yield. RFS compliance consumed about 25 points of capture and the derivative book about 9 points, per management's own decomposition. WTI backwardation cost an additional ~$90 million in purchased crude differentials (~$4.64/bbl). Management's $216 million "net RIN expense" may partially overlap the $73 million mark-to-market add-back; it is treated as distinct here because the company describes the $216 million as excluding the change in the RFS liability.

### Cash flow quality

Reported 1H26 operating cash flow of $371 million (versus negative $19 million in 1H25) requires adjustment. The "other current liabilities" working-capital line contributed +$288 million, and the accrued RFS obligation rose from $72 million at December 31, 2025 to $408 million at June 30, 2026 — a $336 million increase in an accrued but unsettled liability. Headline 1H26 free cash flow of $280 million ($371M − $90M capex − $1M turnaround) becomes approximately negative $56 million if the accrual build is treated as a deferred cash cost. Two offsets: part of the increase is mark-to-market revaluation of previously accrued RINs rather than new volume, and a granted 2025 exemption would reverse a portion non-cash. The direction and size of the eventual cash true-up depend on an EPA decision the company does not control, and Q2's $264 million of reported free cash flow should not be annualized — it benefited from a war-driven crack spike, a post-turnaround throughput rebound, $1 million of turnaround spending (versus $190 million capitalized in 1H25), essentially zero cash taxes (a net $1 million received), and the accrual build.

### Balance sheet

The February 12, 2026 refinancing was the year's clearest management win. CVI issued $600 million of 7.500% notes due 2031 and $400 million of 7.875% notes due 2034 ($993 million net), repaid the Petroleum Term Loan in full, redeemed all $600 million of 8.500% 2029 notes at 104.25% and $217 million of the 5.750% 2028 notes at par, and upsized the ABL from $345 million to $550 million with maturity extended from June 2027 to February 2031. GAAP extinguishment loss $32 million; cash call premium $25 million; issuance costs $13 million. Derived cash interest run-rate: $87.0 million at the parent plus $33.7 million at CVR Partners ≈ $120.7 million, which reconciles to $60 million of 1H26 cash interest paid and is roughly flat against the pre-refinancing stack. Maturities extended materially at approximately neutral cost.

Remaining maturities: $183 million of 5.750% notes (Feb 2028), $550 million of CVR Partners 6.125% secured notes (Jun 2028), then nothing until 2031 and 2034. Liquidity at June 30 was approximately $1.3 billion consolidated ($737 million cash, $540 million ABL availability, $50 million CVR Partners ABL), up from $807 million at year-end, and the company was in compliance with all covenants.

The offset is parent equity erosion. CVI stockholders' equity fell from $730 million to $525 million in six months while noncontrolling interest rose from $168 million to $219 million; accumulated deficit is $982 million. Consolidated total equity of $744 million therefore flatters the parent. Market capitalization of $3,154 million is 6.0x parent book. The current ratio fell from 1.79 to 1.42 as the RFS accrual moved into current liabilities. Altman Z of 2.50 on FY2025 inputs sits in the grey zone and is in any case a weak instrument for a commodity refiner consolidating a partially owned MLP.

Interest coverage on the metric that matters — CVI-attributable EBITDA of roughly $350 million against parent cash interest of $87 million — is about 4x. Adequate, not strong, and it is measured at a cyclical high in cracks.

**Financial health: 4.5.** Genuine improvements (maturity extension, $1.3 billion liquidity, covenant compliance, interest-neutral refinancing) are offset by parent equity erosion, a 1.42 current ratio, a $408 million unsettled regulatory liability equal to 12.9% of market capitalization, and operating cash flow whose quality is materially weaker than the headline. Unchanged from prior.

## Growth Analysis

LTM Adjusted EBITDA (FY2025 less 1H25 plus 1H26) is $517 million consolidated: Petroleum $248 million, Nitrogen $276 million. The fertilizer segment out-earned refining on roughly 8% of consolidated revenue, and CVI owns ~37% of it. Cash actually received from CVR Partners was $46 million in FY2025 and $17 million in 1H26, with about $24 million declared for Q2.

The forward path is cyclical rather than structural, and the near-term direction is down:

- **Q3 guidance** is 205,000–220,000 bpd throughput with ammonia utilization of 75–80% during the ~40-day East Dubuque turnaround ($35–40 million expensed, not capitalized). Management cited prompt Q3 pricing of $650–700/ton ammonia and $325–350/ton UAN against Q2 realized gate prices of $791 and $392 — a sequential decline of roughly 15% in ammonia and 14% in UAN at the midpoints.
- **Agricultural demand mix is adverse.** USDA estimates 2026 corn acres down 3.5% and soybean acres up 5.1%; corn depletes soil nitrogen and soybeans fix it. UAN sales volumes fell 6% in 1H26.
- **Estimate revisions are two-signed and near-term negative.** Next year sits at $1.91, up 94.6% over 90 days but down 11.7% in 30 days. Current year was cut 49.8% in 30 days to $0.37. Current quarter $0.91 (−1.1% over 30 days); next quarter $0.41 (−14.7%). Coverage is thin — two analysts on near-term periods, four on next year. The velocity pattern here — a large 90-day gain that has reversed over 30 days — is deceleration, not the accelerating revision that would support a constructive growth call.
- **Growth capex is real but small and slow.** FY2026 capex guidance is $215–240 million ($76–82 million growth), with $90 million spent in 1H. Projects: Wynnewood alkylation conversion from HF to fixed-bed catalyst, $136 million, adding up to 2,500 bpd of premium gasoline from propylene currently sold as product, operational late 2027; Coffeyville flare gas recovery under the 2023 EPA consent decree, ~$53 million, operational late 2026; Coffeyville fertilizer dual-feedstock project in final engineering, subject to UAN GP board approval; East Dubuque brownfield ammonia expansion of ~5% during the August turnaround.
- **Feedstock slate widened** — Canadian crude from 581 bpd to 19,475 bpd year over year, other domestic from 32,313 to 70,165 bpd — though the 2025 comparison is distorted by the Coffeyville turnaround, during which gathered crude was sold rather than run (crude oil sales fell from $332 million in 1H25 to $23 million in 1H26).

Management is explicitly optimistic that "refining and fertilizer conditions could stay above mid-cycle levels well into 2027," and the supply-side backdrop supports part of that: net US refining capacity has declined since 2020, Middle East refining and LNG infrastructure has been damaged, Russian utilization is reduced, and cheap US natural gas disadvantages European refiners. But the 10-Q itself attributes the 2026 margin environment to a war that began February 28, 2026, including Strait of Hormuz closure, with WTI averaging $82.77 versus $64.73 in 2025. The larger and more fragile component of the year-over-year improvement is a war premium. A verified reopening of transit — the condition we would need to see sustained for 72-plus hours — would compress the crack environment that is currently doing all the work.

**Growth prospects: 4.8** (from 5.5). Downgraded on the guided sequential decline in both segments, the 30-day estimate cuts, and the war-premium dependency of the LTM improvement.

## Competitive Landscape

The industrial backdrop is constructive and the regulatory position is not.

**Structural cost disadvantage.** The 10-Q states plainly that the obligated-party subsidiaries "are not able to blend the majority of their transportation fuels with renewable fuels" and must purchase RINs or obtain exemptions. Blender-refiners generate RINs internally; CVI pays cash. RIN cost per blended barrel was $13.78 versus $6.08 a year earlier. The competitive question is not whether cracks stay wide but whether an inland non-blending refiner is economically viable at full compliance cost.

**The regulatory trajectory is deteriorating on three dated fronts.** The 2026/2027 Final Rule sets record renewable volumes and reallocates 70% of exempted volumes (petitions for review filed in the D.C. Circuit in June 2026, pending). The EPA has not ruled on Wynnewood's July 2025 SRE petition. H.R. 1346, passed by the House in May 2026, would authorize year-round nationwide E15 and bar certain small refiners including Wynnewood from SRE eligibility beginning in 2028; the Senate is weighing it alongside S. 593, which authorizes E15 without amending the small-refinery definition.

**Geographic positioning.** The 10-Q notes that at the outset of the 2026 conflict, waterborne regions elevated more rapidly than inland regions and that the dislocation "continues to be wider than historical averages"; CVI captured less of the initial price spike than coastal peers, though the gap has narrowed.

**Fertilizer is the better business, and CVI owns 37% of it.** Coffeyville's pet coke gasification ($44.94/ton in Q2, down from $56.68, largely contractually set for 2026) is largely decoupled from natural gas; East Dubuque benefits from $2.84/MMBtu gas. Coffeyville received a Verified Ammonia Carbon Intensity certification in June 2026 under The Fertilizer Institute framework, enabling "blue" ammonia marketing. Management claims completion of the dual-feedstock project would make Coffeyville the only US nitrogen facility with pet coke plus natural gas flexibility — a company claim, not independently verified.

A discipline note: the shift in EBITDA mix toward fertilizer does not establish diversification. FY2025 concentration disclosures remain narrow (largest Petroleum customer 12% of segment net sales; top two Nitrogen customers 28%), and both remain two-plant businesses in single regions. Gunvor USA is a crude supply and intermediation **counterparty**, not a customer, through January 2029, and CVI bears Gunvor's performance and credit risk.

**Competitive position: 4.0.** Defensible refinery capture before compliance; deteriorating on the regulatory axis, which is the axis that determines earnings.

## Risk Assessment

**Renewable Fuel Standard (dominant, quantified).** The accrued obligation was $408 million at June 30, 2026, representing approximately 169 million RINs at a $2.41 average mark, up from $72 million six months earlier, excluding open fixed-price commitments to purchase a net 20 million RINs. Wynnewood's 2025 obligation is ~120 million RINs absent a waiver; the company is purchasing 50% of Wynnewood's expected 2026 obligation while maintaining full-waiver eligibility. This liability is 12.9% of market capitalization and its resolution is out of the company's hands.

**Derivative book.** Net commodity derivative losses were $257 million in 1H26 and $75 million in Q2. At June 30 the company was short 7,455 thousand barrels of NYMEX diesel cracks, up from 3,120 thousand at December 31 — short exposure increased as Group 3 2-1-1 cracks rose from a $20.89 1H25 average to $33.34 in 1H26. About 4.6 million barrels of 2026 diesel and 3.2 million barrels of 2027 diesel are hedged. The new CEO stated the company may seek lower hedge authorization than its historical ~30%-of-production standard and layer hedges more cautiously. A hedge program exists to reduce variance, not maximize expected value, and part of the notional increase is normal 2027 layering, but the realized drag has been large and recurring. Separately, $159 million of gross derivative liabilities carry credit-rating-contingent provisions with $47 million of collateral posted; credit ratings were downgraded in 2H24.

**Litigation with material tails.** Exxon asserts a 1993 guaranty obligating Wynnewood Refining to defend and indemnify it against Louisiana contamination claims; deadlines are stayed until September 2026 pending mediation, and the company states an adverse conclusion "could have a material adverse effect." Personal-injury suits from the October 2025 Coffeyville ammonia release are consolidated in Fort Bend County, Texas, with an insurer seeking a declaratory judgment that it has no duty to defend or indemnify. A Kansas environmental class action lost its medical-monitoring claim in July 2026 but proceeds on compensatory and punitive damages; again the company flags potential material adverse effect.

**Controlling-shareholder risk, two-sided.** The 10-Q MD&A discloses that IEP and the company are considering strategic transactions including "the acquisition of material amounts of refining assets" and "strategic options involving CVR Partners," in which the controlling shareholder "may participate or direct." With 29% float, minority upside could be set by negotiation rather than realized in the market. The same disclosure is the mechanism by which a premium could be delivered.

**Concentration and turnarounds.** Two refineries, two fertilizer plants, one region. The Wynnewood turnaround is scheduled for 2027; Coffeyville's April 2025 turnaround required ~$210 million of cash. The 76.9% Q2 2025 utilization shows what one event does to results.

**Conditional obligations.** Under the 45Q transaction, CRNF must deliver minimum annual carbon oxide quantities or pay up to $15 million per year subject to a $45 million cap, with CVR Partners guaranteeing CRNF's and the JV parent's obligations to third-party investors.

Item 1A of the Q2 10-Q reports no material changes from the FY2025 risk factors; the substantive 2026 developments appear in Note 12 and the forward-looking statements.

**Risk factors: 3.6.** Several risks are specific, dated and asymmetric — the unruled SRE petition, the 2028 legislative elimination of eligibility, the September 2026 Exxon mediation deadline, an insurer contesting coverage on the ammonia release — against a market price above consensus targets.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $42 (+34%)

The EPA grants Wynnewood's 2025 exemption at or near 100%, relieving roughly $289 million of accrued obligation, restoring ~9 points of consolidated capture, and converting the 1H26 accrual build from a deferred cash cost into a non-cash reversal. Diesel cracks stay above mid-cycle into 2027 on structurally reduced global capacity, damaged Middle East refining, reduced Russian utilization and a persistent US natural-gas cost advantage. The new CEO reduces hedge authorization and the ~$4.18/bbl derivative drag shrinks. The $183 million of 2028 notes are retired against the $1.0 billion ex-CVR Partners leverage target, triggering the "sustainable increase" in the dividend management has signaled. Petroleum EBITDA runs near the Q2-annualized $424 million and CVI-attributable EBITDA approaches $500–550 million; at 6.5x with parent net debt reduced, equity approaches $42. A negotiated transaction involving CVR Partners or a take-private at a premium is an additional, unquantifiable path to a similar outcome.

### Bear Case — $19.50 (−38%)

Hormuz transit normalizes on a sustained basis, OPEC+ completes its supply rollback, and Group 3 cracks revert toward the low-$20s of 1H25. Petroleum EBITDA falls back to the $150–200 million range seen in FY2024–25. Fertilizer prices continue the sequential decline management guided for Q3 while corn acreage contracts 3.5%. The EPA denies or continues to sit on Wynnewood's petition, the 70% reallocation rule survives D.C. Circuit review, and the House-passed elimination of small-refiner eligibility from 2028 becomes law — making the $408 million accrual a cash obligation and permanently capitalizing the RIN cost into the asset value. Free cash flow, adjusted for the accrual, stays negative; the $0.10 dividend is suspended again as it was in October 2024. Equity retests the $19.62 52-week low.

### Base Case — $28 (−11%)

Cracks stay above mid-cycle but below Q2's $44.91 as the war premium partially decays; fertilizer prices decline as guided and Q3 volumes are constrained by the East Dubuque turnaround. The EPA continues to delay, and CVI keeps accruing at 100% while purchasing half of Wynnewood's 2026 obligation. Normalized CVI-attributable EBITDA of roughly $390–400 million (Petroleum ~$300 million, plus ~37% of ~$250 million of Nitrogen, plus GP) at 7.0–7.5x parent EV less ~$615 million of parent net debt supports roughly $2.7–2.9 billion of equity, or $27–29 per share. This is consistent with the $29.60 consensus mean and $29.00 median and with Goldman's $30 target. Probability-weighting base 50% at $28, bull 25% at $42 and bear 25% at $19.50 gives an expected return of approximately −6%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Base case $28 (−11%), bull case $42 (+34%), bear case $19.50 (−38%). Probability-weighted expected return approximately −6% over 12 months.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into strength over the next four to six weeks, using the elevated short interest and the call-tilted options structure as liquidity: rallies toward $34–36, the range from which the stock has failed three times since March, are the preferred exit zone. We would retain no more than a token position ahead of the EPA decision on Wynnewood's 2025 petition, which is the one genuinely two-sided catalyst. Do not express this as a short — 4.7 days to cover on a 29% float against a single favorable regulatory headline is unfavorable mechanics.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. EPA grants Wynnewood's 2025 SRE at 50% or more, or the D.C. Circuit vacates the 70% reallocation provision of the 2026/2027 Final Rule. Either would materially reduce the accrual and restore capture.
2. Two consecutive quarters in which operating cash flow excluding the change in the accrued RFS obligation exceeds capex plus turnaround spending — the test the 1H26 headline fails.
3. Price in the low $20s (below approximately $23, a discount to the $25 low consensus target and near our bear case), which would restore adequate margin of safety on unchanged fundamentals.
4. Retirement of the $183 million 2028 notes accompanied by a dividend increase materially above the current $0.40 annualized rate.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. Senate passage and enactment of H.R. 1346's provision barring Wynnewood from SRE eligibility from 2028, which would permanently capitalize full RIN cost into the refining asset value.
2. Group 3 2-1-1 crack sustained below $25/bbl for a full quarter — verified 72-plus hours of uninterrupted Hormuz commercial transit would be the leading indicator — combined with the guided fertilizer price decline.
3. Suspension of the reinstated $0.10 dividend, or a collateral call under the rating-contingent derivative provisions following a further downgrade.
4. An adverse determination in the Exxon guaranty dispute after the September 2026 mediation, or an insurer prevailing on no-duty-to-defend in the ammonia release litigation.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next decision points are the September 2026 expiry of the Exxon mediation stay, the Q3 print in late October 2026 (which will show the East Dubuque turnaround impact and whether the RFS accrual continues to build), and any EPA action on the 2025 petition, which is already past its statutory 90-day deadline and could land at any time.

## Conclusion

The Q2 print resolved the question the prior three HOLD ratings left open. CVR Energy's assets ran well in the best crack environment in two years, and the earnings did not reach shareholders. RFS compliance took $11.16 per barrel against a $9.94 reported refining margin, the derivative book took another $4.18, minority holders of a consolidated MLP took $81 million of income out of a consolidated loss, and the reported cash flow was substantially funded by a $336 million build in a liability that the EPA has declined to rule on. The stock trades at 16.4x next-year consensus versus a 10.8x peer median, above the consensus mean and median targets, with zero buy ratings among six analysts, and with current-year estimates cut 49.8% in 30 days.

The February refinancing was well executed and leaves no maturity until 2028, liquidity is $1.3 billion, and Icahn's persistent open-market accumulation — roughly 3.6 million shares between January 2025 and February 2026 at $16.11 to $21.41 — is the most credible bullish datapoint in the filings. But those purchases tell us what the buyer thought at $16–21, not at $31.37, and the same filings that disclose his accumulation also disclose contemplated transactions in which minority upside could be set by negotiation. Two CEOs in seven months and an interim CFO signing certifications for a company managing a $408 million mark-to-market regulatory liability, an 8.2 million barrel derivative book and a stated acquisition mandate add execution risk at the wrong moment.

The overall score of 4.3 reverses the improvement from 3.6 in May, and we are moving from HOLD to REDUCE. The improvement earlier this year was the crack cycle plus a refinancing; both are now in the price, and neither converts to attributable free cash flow at current compliance costs. Trim into strength. Revisit near the low $20s, or immediately on an EPA grant of Wynnewood's 2025 exemption.
