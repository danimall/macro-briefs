**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

United Parks & Resorts (formerly SeaWorld Entertainment) is a leveraged-recap equity wrapped around a mature, high-fixed-cost theme-park business. The operating story has been deteriorating for several years: net income fell from $291.2M (2022) to $227.5M (2024) to $168.4M (2025), and the adjusted-EBITDA proxy dropped from a $728.2M peak to $605.1M. Q1 2026 sharpened the concern, with attendance down 5.0%, revenue down 3.0%, and a $25.4M swing to an $8.5M operating loss as fixed costs rose 6.2% against falling revenue. The company carries $2.27B of debt against negative book equity (a ($557.2M) stockholders' deficit created by ~$2.08B of accumulated treasury stock), roughly $1.5B of it floating and entirely unhedged.

That is the bear file, and it is real. The question this report must resolve is whether it is already priced. The evidence says largely yes. The stock troughed at $28.77 on March 27, 2026 (the level our own downside scenario contemplates) and has since rallied ~53% to $44.11, sitting essentially at the analyst mean target of $46.20. Short interest is 31.6% of float (6.5 days to cover), so the skeptical view is heavily represented in current positioning. Consensus EPS estimates are being revised up across every forward period even after the Q1 miss was reported: +9.3% for the current year, +14.6% and +15.7% for the next two quarters. The lone sell rating on the street was removed over the past three months (1→0) while hold coverage expanded (5→8). Debt maturities cluster in 2029 (notes) and 2031 (term loan), leaving no near-term refinancing cliff.

Prior reports moved this name from HOLD to AVOID and reiterated AVOID as scores drifted from 4.7 to 3.5. On reexamination, an AVOID issued after a 50% bounce, near the mean target, on a stock with a 31.6% short base and rising forward estimates is difficult to defend; it is the classic "avoid a depressed name that then rallies" error. The balanced reading is a business in genuine operational flux offset by a valuation and positioning that already discount that flux. We rate PRKS HOLD (uncertain), with explicit upgrade and downgrade triggers rather than a directional conviction call.

## Company Overview

PRKS operates 12–13 parks under the SeaWorld, Busch Gardens, Aquatica, Discovery Cove, and Sesame Place brands, with a licensed SeaWorld in Abu Dhabi. The company's zoological collection (marine mammals, orcas) is both its differentiator and its structural liability: it draws visitors while inviting animal-welfare activism and layered regulatory oversight (APHIS, OSHA/Cal-OSHA, FWC) that ride-based competitors like Six Flags/Cedar Fair do not face.

Revenue is geographically concentrated: ~59% Florida, 16% California, 13% Virginia, which stacks hurricane and weather exposure onto discretionary-spend sensitivity (Hurricanes Milton and Debby cost 14 operating days in 2024). The competitive set includes far larger, better-capitalized operators, Disney and Universal in particular, with Universal's Epic Universe opening directly in PRKS's core Orlando market. Management explicitly concedes competitors have "substantially greater financial resources."

Two license relationships anchor the portfolio: the Busch Gardens marks (perpetual, royalty-free from Anheuser-Busch) and Sesame Street (term through 2031, and now under active litigation — see Risk Assessment). The controlling shareholder is Hill Path Capital at ~53.2% (insiders/affiliates ~55.1%), with founder Scott Ross as Chairman.

## Financial Analysis

**Operating trend.** The multi-year earnings decline is the central fact. Net income: $291.2M (2022) → $234.2M (2023) → $227.5M (2024) → $168.4M (2025). The EBITDA proxy fell to $605.1M from $700.2M in 2024. Q1 2026 (seasonally weakest quarter) showed the deterioration accelerating: net loss widened to ($34.1M) from ($16.1M), and Segment Adjusted EBITDA fell 12.5% to $78.3M. The one bright spot was in-park per-capita spending, up 5.3% to $40.62, against admission per capita down 0.5% to $45.81; the problem is gate volume, not the willingness of visitors who show up to spend.

**Balance sheet.** The stockholders' deficit worsened from ($435.8M) at year-end 2025. This is a mechanical artifact of ~$2.08B in treasury stock from years of buybacks, not a going-concern signal. The Altman Z-Score screening as "distress" (~1.05) reflects the same financial-engineering artifact. The $2.27B debt breaks down as $1.52B Term B-3 (SOFR+2.00%, matures Dec 2031), $725M 5.25% senior notes (Aug 2029), and $30M drawn on a $700M revolver. Net total leverage is 3.52x on covenant math (Covenant Adjusted EBITDA $637.6M), with ample headroom to the 6.25x first-lien covenant.

**Liquidity and capital allocation.** Cash fell sharply to $28.9M (from $99.8M at YE25) after $93.8M of Q1 buybacks, though ~$659M of revolver availability provides a real cushion. The company repurchased ~$620M in 2024, ~$157M in 2025, and ~$157M more by May 2026, shrinking weighted shares from ~55M (Q1'25) to ~49.4M (Q1'26). The aggressive stance of buying stock while depleting cash and drawing the revolver into declining operations is the sharpest financial-health concern. Interest burden runs ~$132M/yr, with ~$1.5B floating and no hedges, so a 100bps SOFR move swings interest ~$15.2M annually. This is the single most vulnerable line item in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

The financial-health score of 4.0 reflects the genuine fragility (negative equity, thin cash, unhedged floating leverage, declining EBITDA) balanced against the absence of a near-term maturity cliff and substantial revolver liquidity. This is not an insolvency setup, but it is not a resilient balance sheet either.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is negative: attendance -1.8% FY25 and -5.0% in Q1'26, revenue -3.0% in Q1'26. Management's own MD&A concedes "less than optimal" execution and staffing, acknowledging a company-specific component to the attendance weakness beyond weather and soft international visitation.

The counterweight is the estimate-revision trend, which is constructive and occurred after the Q1 miss was already reported. Current-quarter EPS was revised +14.6% over 90 days to $1.93, next quarter +15.7% to $2.31, current year +9.3% over 90 days to $4.45, and next year +6.9% to $4.72. Analysts raising the bar simultaneously post-miss is a signal that cuts against a pure structural-impairment thesis: either the summer season is setting up better than the Q1 print implied, or per-capita spending strength and buyback-driven share shrinkage are expected to carry EPS even on flattish attendance.

This does not rise to the earnings-inflection BUY signal. The revenue trend is negative rather than accelerating, and the revisions, while positive, are single-analyst-thin in places (2–3 analysts). The +9-16% revision magnitude is meaningful but is being driven partly by float reduction and per-capita pricing rather than a demand inflection. Growth prospects score 4.5: modestly negative operations offset by a genuine, if thin, positive revision trend.

## Valuation Assessment

On the $4.45 current-year EPS estimate, PRKS trades at ~9.9x forward earnings; on the $4.72 next-year estimate, ~9.3x. That is a deep discount to the leisure-facilities peer median P/E of 33.1x (OSW 33.1x, PLNT 18.6x; FUN is negative and not meaningful). The discount is partly justified: PRKS carries more leverage, negative book equity, a shrinking attendance base, and idiosyncratic litigation/governance overhangs that the peers do not. But a ~70% discount to the peer median is wide even after adjusting for those factors.

Two things temper the "cheap" read, consistent with the discipline that a low multiple is a warning as often as an opportunity. First, the trailing "P/E" of zero in the data reflects the seasonal loss quarter and buyback-distorted share math; the forward multiple is the correct anchor and it is genuinely single-digit. Second, this is a Consumer Discretionary name, a sector where selection has historically produced negative alpha, so cheapness alone does not earn a high score without a catalyst.

The specific reason the market may be under-pricing the equity is the leveraged-recap mechanics: on a ~$595M LTM EBITDA base against a ~$2.1B market cap and ~$2.27B debt, the equity is a levered call option on stabilizing EBITDA. If EBITDA holds and buybacks continue against a shrinking float, per-share value compounds quickly. If EBITDA keeps sliding, the equity de-rates fast. The current price sits between the low ($37) and mean ($46.20) analyst targets, with a $56 high, so the consensus itself frames this as roughly fairly-to-slightly-under valued rather than a clear mispricing in either direction. Valuation scores 5.5: cheap on forward earnings with a plausible catalyst (buyback-amplified per-share value on stabilization), but the cheapness carries real structural justification, so it is not a high-conviction value call.

## Competitive Landscape

PRKS's competitive trajectory is deteriorating at the margin. Universal's Epic Universe adds direct capacity into PRKS's most important market (59% of revenue). The zoological differentiation remains a genuine moat for the visitors it attracts, but it is simultaneously the source of the activist/regulatory friction (terminated ticket-reseller relationships under activist pressure, ongoing marine-mammal scrutiny) that constrains the addressable audience. Per-capita in-park spending shows pricing power over the visitors who come, but attendance declines indicate share and demand erosion at the gate.

Against far larger, better-capitalized competitors, PRKS is a scale laggard executing a self-described "less than optimal" operating program during a period of leadership turnover. Competitive position scores 4.0: a market participant losing volume share into a strengthening competitive field, partly offset by durable pricing power on captured demand and the perpetual Busch Gardens license.

## Risk Assessment

**Sesame termination suit (most material fresh development).** After a $12.6M arbitration award (paid Q4 2025) and a breach notice the company issued in September 2025, Sesame Workshop sued in the SDNY on 3/12/2026 seeking to terminate the entire Sesame License Agreement. A loss would strand the two standalone Sesame Place parks and require rebranding Sesame areas within other parks, at a moment when execution is already weak. This is a genuine, under-appreciated tail risk with a binary character.

**Leverage and rate exposure.** The unhedged floating-rate exposure directly pressures the equity's residual value in a levered-recap structure under higher-for-longer rates. Cash is thin, though revolver availability cushions near-term.

**Governance and management flux.** Hill Path controls ~53.2%, with its founder as Chairman and Compensation Committee chair, and the 10-Q disclosing that board members are "actively involved in overseeing certain key operating activities." Minority holders have limited influence and likely no control premium. Management turnover is a distinct thread: a cluster of Item 5.02 filings (7/22/25, 8/19/25, 10/28/25, 12/19/25 + amendment, 1/6/26), an Interim CFO (James Forrester) signing the Q1 10-Q, and a mid-implementation ERP flagged as a control/execution risk with a previously-remediated material weakness that could recur. CEO comp jumped ~4.5x to $4.89M in a down year while NEO "compensation actually paid" went negative and TSR underperformed peers (114.9 vs 154.7), a real alignment question.

**Buyback aggression.** Repurchasing stock while cash falls and drawing the revolver, into deteriorating operations, is the sharpest capital-allocation concern. Covenant headroom exists today (3.52x vs 6.25x), but the trajectory matters if EBITDA weakens further.

These risks are specific and several carry binary or governance character not fully priced, which is why risk factors score 4.0. What keeps the score from being lower is that the most-feared outcome (the March low near $28.77) has already been visited, and the short base means the bearish scenarios are already heavily positioned.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress: ATM IV of 64.5% is rich against 51.5% one-year historical vol, the term structure is in backwardation (64.5% near-term vs 53.7% at six months), and put/call skew is elevated at 11.7%, indicating meaningful put demand. The nearest expiry implies a ±14.4% move. ATM IV collapsed from 134.0% to 64.5% since mid-June, so the acute event premium (Q1 print) has largely bled off. The current setup reads as a market bracing for the summer-season attendance data, with put demand consistent with the short base. This confirms the two-sided read: elevated put demand and heavy short interest mean any attendance stabilization creates asymmetric squeeze risk to the upside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Attendance stabilizes into the peak summer season, per-capita spending strength continues, and rising estimates prove correct. On $4.72 next-year EPS, even a modest re-rating to ~12x plus buyback-driven float reduction pushes the equity toward the high analyst target of $56. The short base amplifies any upside surprise into a squeeze. In this scenario the leveraged-recap machine works: stable EBITDA plus shrinking float compounds per-share value.

**Bear case.** Attendance decline continues beyond weather, fixed-cost deleverage compresses EBITDA below $560M, floating-rate interest bites, and the Sesame license is lost. Covenant headroom narrows, buybacks pause, and the levered equity de-rates back toward the March low of $28-30 (-32% to -35% from current). The governance structure means minority holders have no lever to force a change of course.

The distribution is genuinely two-sided from here. The bear-case price is a level the market already reached and rejected four months ago; the bull case requires only operational stabilization, not a demand inflection.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (uncertain); the evidence does not support confident conviction in either direction at $44.11.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Attendance returns to flat-or-positive YoY on the Q2/Q3 2026 prints (the seasonally critical quarters), confirming the Q1 weakness was weather/execution rather than structural demand loss.
- LTM Adjusted EBITDA stabilizes at or above ~$595M for two consecutive quarters, and buybacks continue without covenant pressure.
- Sesame litigation resolves via settlement or license continuation, removing the binary tail.
- A pullback toward the low-$30s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a clear valuation margin of safety.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- LTM EBITDA falls below ~$560M, pushing net leverage toward covenant sensitivity and forcing a buyback pause.
- Adverse ruling in the Sesame termination suit.
- Cash falls below ~$25M with continued revolver draws while operations deteriorate — a liquidity/discipline red flag.
- Estimate revisions turn negative across forward periods.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (peak summer season), which will be the cleanest read on whether attendance is stabilizing or structurally eroding.

## Conclusion

PRKS carries a legitimate bear file: a multi-year earnings decline, a Q1 operating loss, unhedged floating-rate leverage on negative book equity, an escalating Sesame termination suit, an interim CFO amid heavy executive turnover, and a controlling shareholder running the turnaround top-down. Those risks are real and keep the overall score at 4.3.

But an AVOID at $44.11 is not defensible on this evidence. The stock already fell to the level our own bear case contemplates and has rallied to sit essentially at the analyst mean target. The skeptical view is heavily positioned; forward estimates are rising across every period even after the Q1 miss; the sole street sell rating was removed; and there is no maturity cliff before 2029. The bearish thesis is priced. The balanced reading of deteriorating operations offset by a single-digit forward multiple, rising estimates, a crowded short with asymmetric squeeze risk, and adequate near-term liquidity supports HOLD, not AVOID. We would turn constructive on attendance stabilization or a pullback into the low-$30s, and negative on a covenant-pressuring EBITDA slide or an adverse Sesame ruling. The Q2 summer-season data is the next read on which way the situation resolves.
