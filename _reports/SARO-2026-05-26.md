## Executive Summary

StandardAero is the world's largest independent, pure-play aerospace engine aftermarket services provider, operating across commercial aviation (~57%), military/helicopter (~18%), and business aviation (~25%) end markets. The company IPO'd in October 2024 at $24/share and trades today at ~$26.74, 22% below its 52-week high of $34.48. The stock has been pressured by three specific factors: persistent Carlyle Group secondary sales ($2.1B+ since January 2026), three disclosed material weaknesses in internal controls, and Q1 2026 EBITDA margin compression to 12.5% from 13.8% YoY due to LEAP ramp-up costs and contract restructuring.

The core investment thesis rests on SARO's position as the first independent LEAP-1A/1B Premier MRO provider in the Americas — a multi-decade growth platform as the CFM LEAP fleet (powering ~70% of narrowbody deliveries) enters maintenance cycles. LEAP revenues "roughly quadrupled" YoY in Q1 2026, and the program approaches profitability in H1 2026. At ~18.4x 2026E EPS ($1.45 midpoint) and ~12.2x EV/EBITDA, the stock trades at a substantial discount to the $35.89 consensus price target (34% upside). The analyst community is nearly unanimous (12 Buy/Strong Buy vs. 2 Hold, zero Sells).

This does not translate into a BUY recommendation because of the confluence of three specific, concrete risks that could delay or impair the re-rating: (1) the material weaknesses create a reasonable probability of delayed or qualified Section 404 opinions in 2027, constraining institutional ownership capacity; (2) Carlyle's ~31% stake will continue to generate secondary-offering supply pressure until they cross below 25% (likely 3-6 months); and (3) the earnings track record is only 4 quarters with 2 misses, meaning the market has insufficient confidence in management's ability to deliver on guidance — the most recent miss (Q1 2026 EPS $0.24 vs. $0.28 estimate, -15.5%) occurred less than three weeks ago.

## Company Overview

StandardAero provides maintenance, repair, and overhaul services for aerospace engines across two segments:

- **Engine Services** (~88% of revenue, $5.35B in FY2025): Full-suite MRO including overhaul, on-wing field support, asset management, and engineering solutions for commercial, military, and business aviation engines.
- **Component Repair Services** (~12% of revenue, $709M in FY2025): Engine piece-part and accessory repairs with materially higher margins (28.6% EBITDA margin vs. 13.2% for Engine Services).

The company holds exclusive or semi-exclusive OEM licenses for key platforms including Rolls-Royce RB211-535, AE series; Honeywell HTF7000; and Safran Arriel. It operates through 8,000 employees across 11 countries, serving ~5,000 global customers with approximately 80% of revenue derived from long-term agreements.

The business was majority-owned by Carlyle Group and GIC Private Limited through the IPO. Carlyle has subsequently reduced its stake from majority to ~31.4% through aggressive secondary offerings, approaching the 25% threshold at which it loses board majority designation rights (currently holding 6 of 9 seats).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:**
FY2025 revenue reached $6.063B (+15.8% YoY), with operating income of $551M (+36.7%) and net income of $277M (up from $11M in FY2024). The net income transformation was driven by $108M in reduced interest expense following IPO-related debt paydown and the absence of $39M in 2024 refinancing charges.

Q1 2026 delivered $1.63B in revenue (+13.3% organic), but EBITDA margin compressed to 12.5% from 13.8% due to LEAP learning-curve costs, military contract closeouts, and pass-through revenue burndown. Management guided full-year 2026 revenue of $6.325-$6.45B (5-6% growth) and Adj. EBITDA of $875-$905M (10-12% growth), with Adj. EPS of $1.40-$1.50.

Reported revenue growth in 2026 significantly understates organic momentum because management is deliberately removing $300-$400M of low/no-margin pass-through material revenue from restructured commercial contracts. Adjusting for this, underlying organic growth is closer to 10-12%.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet:**
- Operating cash flow: $317M (FY2025), up from $76M (FY2024)
- CapEx: $82M (modest for the revenue base)
- FCF: $234M (FY2025), guided $270-$300M (FY2026)
- Total debt: $2.25B (all maturing October 2031), weighted average rate 6.8%
- Net leverage: ~2.6x Adj. EBITDA, down from 3.1x a year ago
- Available liquidity: $1.03B ($290M cash + $736M undrawn revolver)
- Share repurchase: $450M authorized, ~$60M executed in Q1 2026

The balance sheet is serviceable given the industry context (A&D MRO companies typically operate with moderate leverage), with no near-term maturities and declining leverage trajectory. The Q1 2026 seasonal working capital build of -$134M is consistent with management's full-year FCF guidance.

**Profitability Trajectory:**
The company is navigating a margin transition period. Component Repair margins expanded from 26.1% to 28.6% (FY2024 to FY2025), but Engine Services margins remain flat at 13.2% as LEAP/CFM56 investments create near-term headwinds. Management expects these programs to reach profitability in H1 2026, setting up margin expansion in H2 2026 and beyond.

## Growth Analysis

StandardAero benefits from three structural growth drivers:

**1. LEAP Engine Aftermarket (Primary Growth Vector):**
The CFM LEAP-1A/1B powers the Airbus A320neo and Boeing 737 MAX — the two highest-volume commercial aircraft programs globally, with 6,000+ engines in service and growing. StandardAero is the first independent Premier MRO provider for LEAP in the Americas. Q1 2026 LEAP revenues "roughly quadrupled" YoY, and the company completed its first full LEAP 1A overhaul. As the installed base matures (engines enter first shop visits 5-7 years after delivery), this becomes a multi-decade revenue stream.

**2. Aging Installed Base / New Aircraft Delivery Delays:**
OEM supply chain disruptions are extending the service lives of older aircraft and their engines, driving incremental maintenance demand. The engines delivered during the 2010s build cycle are now entering prime maintenance windows simultaneously.

**3. Military and Business Aviation:**
The military segment benefits from sustained geopolitical spending. Business aviation fleet expansion supports accessory and engine maintenance volumes. The recent Unified Turbines acquisition extends capabilities on PT6A and PW100 turboprop engines.

**Growth Guidance Credibility:**
Management raised 2026 guidance following Q1 results, signaling confidence in the H2 acceleration thesis. However, the Q1 EPS miss (-15.5%) introduces execution uncertainty around the ramp timeline. The 90-day EPS revision for the current year is essentially flat (+0.2%), and the 30-day revision turned negative (-2.2%), suggesting the sell-side is not yet fully de-risking estimates after the Q1 miss.

## Competitive Landscape

StandardAero's competitive position is strong and improving:

**Moat Characteristics:**
- OEM authorizations create barriers to entry — competitors cannot service certain engines without OEM approval
- Multi-year, multi-engine customer relationships create switching costs
- LEAP authorization specifically is a scarce asset: only a handful of independents globally have achieved Premier MRO designation
- Scale advantages: 11-country footprint allows follow-the-sun service and regional proximity to customers
- Customer concentration declining (36% from top 4 OEMs in 2025, down from 43% in 2023) reduces dependency risk

**Competitive Risks:**
- OEMs continuously evaluate vertical integration into aftermarket (GE Aerospace's services revenue has grown faster than its OE revenue)
- Parts access controlled by OEMs creates structural dependency
- Other independents (MTU, ST Engineering) compete for similar authorizations
- Airlines with in-house MRO capabilities (Delta TechOps, Lufthansa Technik) serve as benchmarks

The trajectory is positive: LEAP authorization is additive, diversification from OEM dependency is improving, and the aging fleet dynamic benefits independents disproportionately (OEMs focus capacity on newer platforms).

## Risk Assessment

**1. Material Weaknesses in Internal Controls (HIGH SEVERITY):**
Three simultaneous material weaknesses identified in the FY2025 10-K relating to control environment, period-end financial reporting, and IT general controls. For a newly-public company at $9B market cap, this is a significant governance risk. It does not mean financials are incorrect, but it means there is a "reasonable possibility" a material misstatement would not be detected. Remediation is underway but incomplete. This creates three specific consequences: (a) potential for restatements, (b) inability to attract certain institutional mandates that require clean Section 404 opinions, and (c) elevated audit fees and management distraction. The company must demonstrate remediation progress by the FY2026 filing (early 2027) — failure to do so would be a thesis-breaking development.

**2. Carlyle Overhang (MODERATE-HIGH SEVERITY):**
At ~31% ownership with a demonstrated pace of selling 20M+ shares per quarter through secondaries, Carlyle will likely cross below 25% within 3-6 months. Each secondary creates 1-3 days of elevated volume and typically 3-5% stock pressure. Until Carlyle reaches single-digit ownership (12+ months), this remains a persistent technical headwind that limits sustained re-rating regardless of fundamental performance.

**3. Execution Risk on LEAP Margin Inflection (MODERATE SEVERITY):**
Management guided LEAP/CFM56 programs to profitability in H1 2026. Q1 results showed these programs still dragging margins. Q2 2026 results (expected August) become the critical validation point. If margins don't improve sequentially, the "back-half acceleration" narrative weakens materially.

**4. Variable-Rate Debt in Rising-Rate Environment:**
$2.25B at 6.8% weighted average, all floating. Each 100bps increase translates to ~$22.5M in additional annual interest expense, or approximately $0.05/share EPS headwind.

**5. Tariff Uncertainty:**
The 10-K provides unusual real-time commentary on IEEPA tariff uncertainty. While SARO's operations are "primarily located in regions where products are sold" and USMCA provides protection, timing delays in cost pass-through could compress margins 50-100bps in any given quarter.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($34-38, +27-42%):**
- LEAP programs achieve profitability in Q2 2026, demonstrating margin expansion trajectory
- Carlyle crosses below 25% in Q3/Q4 2026, removing board control and reducing selling pressure
- Organic revenue growth sustains 12%+ as LEAP fleet enters maintenance cycles
- Material weaknesses remediated by FY2026 filing, unlocking institutional demand
- Multiple re-rates from ~18x to 22-24x forward EPS on improved execution credibility
- Math: 22x × $1.50 FY2026E EPS = $33; 24x × $1.50 = $36

**Bear Case ($20-22, -18-25%):**
- Q2 2026 results show continued margin pressure, raising doubts about LEAP profitability timeline

**What Prevents a BUY Recommendation:**
The specific, falsifiable risk is the material weakness disclosure combined with the earnings miss pattern. In the system's Industrials track record, companies with governance risks and mixed execution histories at ~18-19x forward P/E have not generated positive returns on BUY recommendations. The analyst consensus is bullish (12 of 14 rate Buy/Strong Buy), but the stock's inability to sustain gains above $30 in three attempts since March 2026 — despite raised guidance — demonstrates that the market requires proof of execution (clean Q2 margins + material weakness remediation progress) before re-rating. Issuing a BUY before that proof materializes would be premature given the 2-for-4 earnings track record and the 15.5% miss magnitude in the most recent quarter.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 results (expected August 2026) show Adj. EBITDA margin ≥13.5% with sequential improvement in Engine Services segment — would confirm LEAP margin inflection
2. Carlyle ownership drops below 25% with no further secondary announced within 60 days — removes structural supply pressure
3. Company discloses "significant progress" on material weakness remediation with specific timeline for resolution

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 EBITDA margin below 12.0% (would signal LEAP margin thesis is failing)
2. Restatement of previously filed financials related to material weaknesses
3. Any OEM withdrawing or narrowing SARO's authorization scope for LEAP or other key programs
4. Net leverage exceeds 3.5x Adj. EBITDA (would signal balance sheet deterioration)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected early-to-mid August 2026) is the primary catalyst. Secondary: any Carlyle Form 4 filing showing ownership below 25%.

## Conclusion

StandardAero possesses genuine competitive advantages — exclusive OEM authorizations, scale independence, and transformative LEAP positioning — that support a multi-year growth thesis. At 18.4x 2026E EPS with 10-12% EBITDA growth and 13%+ organic revenue growth, the stock appears undervalued relative to the $35.89 consensus target and its own growth profile. The 20% upside to even the lowest analyst target ($32) suggests limited downside if execution proceeds as guided.

However, three material weaknesses, a 50% earnings miss rate over its short public life, and ongoing sponsor liquidation create concrete risks that the re-rating catalyst may be delayed 2-3 quarters. The correct posture is to hold for the growth thesis to materialize with a defined upgrade path: Q2 margin inflection + material weakness progress + reduced Carlyle overhang would justify a BUY at current valuation levels. The risk/reward is favorable but the timing evidence is insufficient to commit ahead of the August earnings print.
