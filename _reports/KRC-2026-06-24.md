**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kilroy Realty is a West Coast-concentrated office and life science REIT trading at roughly $37, up from a March low near $28, having recovered as the market began to price an operating inflection. The investment question is whether that inflection is durable. The trailing data is poor: occupancy fell from 82.8% at end-2024 to 77.6% at the end of Q1 2026, second-generation cash releasing spreads ran -16.8% in Q1, retention was 18.5%, and the company posted a Q1 GAAP net loss of $(0.16) per share on a $61.8M impairment of two Los Angeles residential assets. Set against that, forward indicators turned: Q1 2026 produced the strongest first-quarter leasing since 2017 (568,000 sq ft, more than double the prior-year quarter), management raised both full-year FFO and average-occupancy guidance, and same-property NOI swung to +3.2% after a -3.0% full-year 2025 print.

The two-speed nature of the story is genuine and is the reason this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID. A reading that stops at trailing occupancy and EPS misses the leasing-pipeline turn; a reading that anchors only on the guidance raise ignores that in-place rents remain above market across parts of the portfolio, so renewals continue to re-set lower. The balance sheet keeps the downside bounded: 95.7% fixed-rate debt at a ~4.6% effective rate, 87% unsecured, an undrawn $1.1B revolver, investment-grade ratings, and manageable near-term maturities. The optical jump in debt-to-market-cap to 58.3% was driven almost entirely by the share price falling to $28, not by added leverage.

We score KRC 5.1 overall and rate it HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The ~5.9% dividend is covered by FFO, the new CEO is recycling capital and buying back stock opportunistically, and the flight-to-quality thesis is intact. But office pricing power is still negative, tech-tenant concentration at 51% of base rent cuts both ways, the dividend is partly return-of-capital, and there is no open-market insider buying to confirm management's NAV conviction at the personal level. The re-rating from $28 to $37 has already captured the cheapest part of the move.

## Company Overview

Kilroy Realty is a self-administered REIT focused on premier office, life science, and mixed-use properties in five innovation markets: the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, San Diego, Seattle, and Austin. The company and its predecessors have developed West Coast commercial real estate since 1947, and it operates through Kilroy Realty, L.P., of which KRC owns roughly 99%.

As of December 31, 2025, the portfolio comprised 121 stabilized office and life science properties totaling roughly 16.3M rentable square feet, plus 1,001 residential units. By March 31, 2026 this grew to 123 properties and ~17.1M sq ft following the addition of KOP Phase 2. The portfolio is geographically concentrated, with only about 11 properties outside California (10 in Seattle, one in Austin), and the tenant base skews heavily to technology at 51% of base rent, followed by life science/healthcare at 19%, professional services at 9%, finance at 7%, and media at 6%. The top 20 tenants represent 53.7% of base rent.

The competitive thesis rests on owning high-quality, modern, amenity-rich, transit-proximate assets in supply-constrained submarkets, with recognized sustainability leadership (carbon-neutral since 2020, GRESB 5-Star). The development pipeline provides optionality: roughly 6M sq ft and 1,750 residential units across eight to nine future sites, including Flower Mart in San Francisco (~2.3M sq ft, $713M), KOP Phases 3 and 4, and SIX0 in Seattle, totaling an estimated $1.5–1.6B of future investment.

## Financial Analysis

The trailing fundamentals are soft, but the trajectory shows a forward turn that matters for a REIT.

Revenue was approximately $1.10B in FY2025, down from ~$1.13B in FY2024, with 98.3% from rental income. NOI of $736.2M was down 3.7% YoY, and same-property NOI fell 3.0%. The early turn is in the quarterly data: Q1 2026 NOI of $178.4M was down 1.0% YoY on a total basis, but same-property NOI rose 3.2%.

Occupancy is the central concern. It declined from 82.8% (12/31/24) to 81.6% (12/31/25) to 77.6% (3/31/26). A meaningful part of the Q1 drop is mechanical: KOP Phase 2, an ~871K sq ft life science campus only 44% leased and 5% occupied, entered the stabilized pool and pulled headline occupancy down roughly four points. Ex-KOP2, occupancy held near 81%. The releasing spreads are the cleaner read on pricing power and they remain negative: FY2025 second-generation GAAP rents were -9.3% and cash rents -18.4%; Q1 2026 all-leases GAAP was -10.6% and cash -16.8%. Retention was low at 34% in FY2025 and 18.5% in Q1 2026. In-place rents above market mean renewals continue to re-set lower.

On the bottom line, FY2025 net income to common was $276.1M, flattered by $127M of disposition gains and offset by a $16.3M impairment. Q1 2026 was a GAAP net loss of $(14.7)M, driven by the impairment on two LA residential properties (Columbia Square Living and Jardine) that sold in April 2026 for $202M. For a REIT, FFO is the operative metric: Q1 2026 FFO was approximately $108.8M versus $122.3M a year earlier, down 11% on a total basis, but management reported FFO of $0.91 per share. Operating cash flow was healthy at $150.7M in Q1, up 10% YoY.

The balance sheet is the key offset to operating weakness and supports a financial health score above the midpoint despite the office-sector backdrop. Total debt is roughly $4.6B, at an effective rate of ~4.6%, all investment-grade with staggered maturities. Cash was ~$192.9M at quarter-end, and the company is in compliance with all covenants. Near-term maturities are manageable: a $200M term loan due October 2026 (extendable) and $250M of notes due 2026, and the company has been refinancing proactively, repaying $400M of notes in 2025 and $50M of private notes in April 2026 while issuing $400M of 5.875% notes due 2035. The cost of new debt is rising (5.875% versus 4.375% retired), a slow drag on FFO over time but not a refinancing cliff.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data shows genuine upward momentum. Current-year consensus EPS rose to $0.70 from $0.47 ninety days ago (+48.1%) and from $0.56 thirty days ago (+25.0%). The next-quarter estimate moved to $0.13 from $0.07 ninety days ago (+83.3%), and next-year sits at $0.50, up 61.1% over 90 days. On the surface this clears the threshold that would normally flag a strong forward signal. Two qualifications temper it. First, GAAP EPS for a REIT is a low-quality metric distorted by impairments and disposition gains; the FFO guidance raise is the more reliable confirmation, and it is real but modest. Second, the analyst coverage behind these revisions is thin (one to four analysts per line), so the percentage swings overstate the conviction.

The durable growth driver, if it materializes, is the lease-up of recently delivered and future product into a flight-to-quality market. KOP Phase 2 at 44% leased is dilutive today and accretive as it fills. The 1900 Broadway JV in Redwood City (251K sq ft, 58% pre-leased, KRC ~97% on completion) and the broader development pipeline provide multi-year optionality, but require capital and pre-leasing in a transaction market that remains constrained. The bull framing is leverage to the AI/tech build-out in the Bay Area, where KRC is a landlord to a tenant base that is, for now, the most demand-elastic segment in the market.

Against this, the earnings surprise record is poor: three misses in the last four quarters, including a -54% Q1 2026 miss against the GAAP estimate (the impairment-driven loss). That history argues for treating consensus as a soft anchor rather than a reliable forecast.

## Valuation Assessment

KRC trades at roughly $37 with a trailing/forward P/E near 20x on reported EPS, but P/E is the wrong lens for an office REIT carrying impairments and disposition gains through earnings. On FFO, with management guiding to an annualized run-rate implied by the $0.91 Q1 print, the stock trades at a high-single-digit to low-double-digit FFO multiple, consistent with the office subsector's depressed range.

The peer set underscores how dispersed and impaired this group is. Office REIT peer medians show a P/E of 36.2x and a 4.8% yield, but the medians are distorted by negative earners (CUZ at -907x, SLG at -20.4x, DEI at -75x). The more useful read is the dividend yield comparison: KRC's ~5.9% exceeds the peer median of 4.8% and sits between HIW (6.6%) and DEI (6.1%), reflecting market skepticism about office cash flows broadly and KRC's negative releasing spreads specifically. The analyst price-target distribution (low $28, mean $37.14, median $38, high $51) places the stock essentially at consensus mean, with the median implying only modest upside.

A high valuation score requires a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock, and here the case is balanced rather than asymmetric. The bullish argument is that NAV exceeds the share price (management's buyback at $30.80 and refusal to issue ATM equity both signal a NAV-aware view that the stock is below intrinsic value), and that the operating inflection is only partially priced. The bearish argument is that the cheapest part of the re-rating, from $28 to $37, has already occurred, that the dividend is partly return-of-capital (2025 distributions exceeded taxable income), and that negative mark-to-market on expiring leases will weigh on FFO growth for several years. The cheapness here is the kind that historically functions as a value-trap warning absent a hard catalyst, and the catalyst (sustained positive releasing spreads) has not yet arrived. We score valuation at the midpoint.

## Competitive Landscape

KRC operates in the most-challenged REIT subsector but in its most-defensible niche. Hybrid work continues to depress aggregate office demand and pressure renewal rents, which is visible directly in KRC's negative spreads and low retention. The countervailing dynamic is bifurcation: trophy and Class-A assets capture a disproportionate share of the demand that remains, and KRC's modern, amenity-rich, transit-proximate portfolio is positioned for that flight-to-quality.

The trajectory is the relevant question, and it is mixed. The 568K sq ft Q1 leasing print and the +3.2% same-property NOI turn suggest the competitive position is stabilizing at the leasing-activity level. But the negative releasing spreads show the company is winning tenants by accepting lower rents, which is consistent with a market leader defending occupancy rather than expanding pricing power. Life science exposure (19%) offers diversification but carries its own oversupply concerns in some submarkets, including South San Francisco where KOP2 sits. We score competitive position slightly below the midpoint: the asset quality is genuine, but share is being defended on price, not gained, and the prior decline in this dimension reflects that reality. The current data stabilizes the trajectory without reversing it upward.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is secular: hybrid work continues to make tenants more selective, pursuing space efficiencies and taking less space. This drives the negative mark-to-market on expiring leases, where in-place rents above market re-set lower on renewal. Tech-tenant concentration at 51% is the highest-variance exposure: a tech downturn or layoff cycle would hit demand disproportionately, while the AI/tech boom is the upside scenario. Geographic concentration in California, Seattle, and Austin layers in earthquake exposure, high regulation and tax, Proposition 13 reassessment risk, and episodic media-industry strikes.

Development and lease-up risk is concrete: KOP Phase 2 is only 44% leased at stabilization, and the large future pipeline (Flower Mart) requires capital and pre-leasing in a soft market. The dividend, while FFO-covered, was partly return-of-capital in 2025, a yellow flag to monitor if leasing momentum stalls. Refinancing risk is mitigated by the fixed-rate, staggered, investment-grade debt structure and undrawn revolver, but new debt costs more. Risk factors were unchanged between the FY2025 10-K and the Q1 2026 10-Q, with the only notable additions in the 10-K being expanded cybersecurity/OT-systems and AI/data-privacy language. We score risk factors below the midpoint because the negative-spread/concentration combination represents a material, not-fully-priced downside path if the leasing inflection proves transitory, and because the impairment cadence (the $61.8M Q1 2026 residential impairment following the $16.3M FY2025 impairment) signals NAV erosion in the non-core book.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 45.7% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 28.3%, a rich 17.4-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (45.7% at one month versus 37.3% at six months), which signals an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings print. Put/call skew is modestly elevated at 3.3% near-term and notably higher at 14.8% in the three-month tenor, indicating downside hedging into the summer. The 12.7% short interest with seven days to cover corroborates institutional skepticism. IV fell 7.7 points (53.4% to 45.7%) in the most recent snapshot alongside a roughly flat price (-0.8%), suggesting some near-term uncertainty has resolved. The net read: the options market does not share the bull case, and a HOLD is consistent with that positioning, with two-sided risk into the next print rather than a clean asymmetric setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The operating inflection is real and early. As KOP2 and the development pipeline lease up into a flight-to-quality market, occupancy recovers and FFO compounds. The balance sheet is conservative enough to fund recycling without dilutive equity, the new CEO is buying back stock at NAV-accretive prices, and the AI/tech build-out drives Bay Area demand toward KRC's trophy assets. Multiple re-rates toward a normalized office FFO multiple as spreads turn positive. Bull case target ~$48–51, consistent with the high end of analyst targets and a recovery in occupancy toward the mid-80s.

**Bear Case.** The leasing print is a one-quarter pull-forward, not a trend. Negative releasing spreads persist as above-market in-place rents continue to re-set lower, FFO grows slowly or stalls, and the dividend's return-of-capital character forces a coverage debate if FFO weakens. A tech-spending pullback hits the 51% concentration, KOP2 lease-up disappoints, and additional non-core impairments erode NAV. The stock retraces toward the low end of the range. Bear case target ~$28, the March low and the analyst low target, roughly 24% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, reflecting genuine two-sided evidence rather than a confident directional view. The natural reassessment point is the next two earnings prints, where the durability of the leasing inflection and the releasing-spread trajectory will become clear.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of releasing spreads improving toward flat (cash spreads better than -10%), confirming pricing power is returning rather than occupancy being bought on price.
- Occupancy stabilizing or rising sequentially ex-KOP2, with KOP2 leasing progress above 60%.
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives, which would confirm at the personal level the NAV conviction the corporate buyback implies.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut to FFO or occupancy, or a third consecutive quarter of declining same-property NOI.
- A dividend coverage deterioration (FFO payout rising toward or above 100%) or any dividend reduction.
- Further material impairments signaling continued NAV erosion, or a tech-tenant demand shock visible in leasing volumes.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) is the first checkpoint; a full re-evaluation should follow the Q3 print to confirm whether the spread and occupancy trajectory is sustained.

## Conclusion

Kilroy owns high-quality assets in a difficult subsector, and the data now shows a credible early turn set against still-negative pricing power and a tech-concentrated, geographically concentrated portfolio. The balance sheet is conservative, the yield is FFO-covered, and management is acting like a NAV-aware capital allocator by recycling assets and buying back stock at depressed prices. The score has improved modestly over the trajectory (financial health and management quality both up half a point), which the current data confirms rather than reverses.

We rate KRC HOLD. The recovery from $28 to $37 has already captured the easiest part of the re-rating, the absence of open-market insider buying leaves the bullish conviction incomplete, and the negative releasing spreads cap upside until pricing power returns. We would not add at current levels, but the bounded downside (conservative balance sheet, covered dividend, NAV support from buybacks) and the genuine forward inflection argue against an AVOID. The thesis resolves over the next two quarters: confirmation that leasing momentum converts to positive spreads and stable occupancy would warrant an upgrade; a stalled inflection or guidance cut would warrant a downgrade.
