## Executive Summary

Oceaneering is the scale leader in a consolidated market. It operates 250 work-class remotely operated vehicles and held ROV contracts on 82 of 139 contracted floating rigs (59%) at June 30, 2026, generating 27-30% segment operating margins in Subsea Robotics. Return on net invested capital was approximately 24% in FY2025. Following the July 2026 refinancing the company is roughly net cash, with liquidity near $945M and no bond maturity until July 2034. Q2 2026 was the best quarter in more than a decade: revenue $768.2M (+10.0%), adjusted EBITDA $115M, diluted EPS $0.65 against $0.54, and a fourth consecutive earnings beat.

The problem is what the market is paying for that. At $49.25 the enterprise value is approximately $4.86bn against FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $400-440M — 11.6x the $420M midpoint. That midpoint implies +4.6% EBITDA growth versus FY2025's $401.5M, following +24.3%, +20.1% and +15.6% in the three prior years, and the low end is essentially flat. Reported trailing EPS of $3.49 is not a usable anchor: the FY2025 10-K discloses a $154M deferred-tax valuation-allowance release that produced a $67.9M tax *benefit* rather than an expense, contributing roughly $1.40-1.50 per share. Normalized at the 1H26 effective rate of 29.3%, FY2025 EPS was approximately $2.00-2.10, putting the trailing multiple near 24x rather than the 14.2x screen figure. Consensus of $2.03 for the current year and $2.28 for next year puts the forward multiples at 24.3x and 21.6x against a peer median P/E of 19.6.

Beneath the Q2 headline, 1H26 consolidated operating income fell 4.3% on 6.4% higher revenue, with margins down in four of five segments. ROV utilization was 63% versus 67%, driven by an average contracted floater count that fell from 146 (2024) to 137 (2025). Manufactured Products, the segment delivering the best margin improvement (+660bp), saw backlog fall 13% to $445M with trailing book-to-bill of 0.88, the third consecutive sub-1.0 reading. Management reversed its own February guidance for Integrity Management & Digital Solutions from "improve significantly" to "decrease significantly" within five months, despite that segment's backlog having grown 78%. On the Q2 call management described the recovery as gradual, building into early 2027, with no pronounced inflection. The current price already exceeds the high analyst target of $47.00 and sits 22% above the mean of $40.25 with a rating distribution of three holds and one strong sell. We move to REDUCE: probability-weighted expected return from $49.25 is modestly negative, and the bear-case drawdown (-31%) exceeds the bull-case gain (+22%).

## Company Overview

Oceaneering provides engineered services and products to the offshore energy industry, with a growing defense and aerospace engineering arm. It employs roughly 11,100 people across approximately 48 countries and derived 55% of FY2025 revenue from foreign operations. Five reporting segments generated $2,784.2M of FY2025 revenue and $494.1M of segment operating income, reduced to $304.6M consolidated after $189.6M of unallocated corporate expense:

| Segment | FY2025 Revenue | Op. Income | Margin | Share of segment profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Subsea Robotics | $855.2M | $257.1M | 30.1% | 52.0% |
| Manufactured Products | $569.0M | $72.5M | 12.7% | 14.7% |
| Offshore Projects Group | $616.0M | $96.1M | 15.6% | 19.4% |
| Integrity Mgmt & Digital | $284.0M | $10.7M | 3.8% | 2.2% |
| Aerospace & Defense Tech | $459.9M | $57.7M | 12.5% | 11.7% |

Subsea Robotics is the franchise. ROV services were 78% of segment revenue in FY2025, split roughly 64% drilling support and 36% vessel-based work. The fleet has been static at 250 units across year-end 2023, 2024 and 2025. Offshore Projects Group runs two owned Jones Act-compliant deepwater vessels plus five charters, none extending beyond Q1 2029. ADTech serves the US Navy, NASA and defense primes; it was named a Lockheed Martin best-in-class supplier for Artemis work and, with Kongsberg as co-awardee, received a Defense Innovation Unit contract to support extra-large unmanned underwater vehicle development.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is decelerating on the same trajectory as margins.** Revenue grew 17.4% (2023), 9.8% (2024), 4.6% (2025) and 6.4% in 1H26. Operating margin expanded from 5.4% (2022) to 10.9% (2025), then slipped to 10.0% in 1H26. Adjusted EBITDA, the company's own selected performance measure in the proxy, went $232.6M → $289.0M → $347.2M → $401.5M, growth of +24.3%, +20.1%, +15.6%. Guidance of $400-440M for FY2026 breaks that sequence.

**The guidance bridge is tighter than it looks.** 1H26 adjusted EBITDA was approximately $200.6M (operating income $146.0M plus D&A $54.6M, reconciling to the company's reported $115M for Q2 alone). Q3 is guided to $115-125M. At the $420M midpoint, implied Q4 is roughly $99M; reaching the $440M top end requires approximately $119M in Q4 with essentially no seasonal step-down from the summer activity peak. For a business weighted to North Sea and Gulf of Mexico weather windows, the midpoint-to-low-end is the more probable landing zone. A heavier Q4 vessel and installation schedule, given that OPG was guided up in Q3 on US Gulf and West Africa utilization, is the counterargument.

**Trailing earnings are distorted, and this is the single most important reconciliation in the file.** FY2025 net income was $353.8M on pre-tax income of $285.9M (operating income $304.6M + interest income $14.5M − interest expense $37.0M + equity earnings $1.0M + other $2.8M). The arithmetic only works because tax was a $67.9M benefit, driven by a $154M valuation-allowance release ($140M federal, $10M state). The implied current tax expense was approximately $72.7M, a 25.4% rate. Normalizing at the 1H26 actual 29.3% rate gives net income near $202M and EPS near $2.00; using current tax only gives $213M and roughly $2.10. Roughly 40-43% of reported EPS was a non-cash, non-recurring accounting event. Cash taxes move the other way: FY2026 cash income tax payments are guided at $95-105M.

The allowance release is economically real — it reflects an audited conclusion that US federal deferred tax assets are more likely than not recoverable, and it shields future book tax expense. What it distorts is the trailing multiple and any earnings-based screen. It also explains why FY2025 operating cash flow ($318.9M) was below net income; adjusted to $213M, cash conversion is comfortably above 100% and accrual quality is fine.

**Cash flow is back-half weighted and should not be annualized off the first half.** 1H26 operating cash flow was −$3.9M, against −$3.5M in 1H25 — a repeated seasonal pattern rather than new deterioration, driven by a $119.9M receivables and contract-asset build (AR rose to $414.4M from $308.5M at year-end). Q2 standalone generated $55.2M of operating cash flow and roughly $32M of free cash flow. FY2025 delivered $318.9M operating cash flow, $111.0M capex and $207.8M free cash flow (7.5% of revenue). As a labeled sensitivity only: if 2H26 converts at 2H25's rate, FY2026 operating cash flow lands near $300M, which against $105-115M capex guidance and $95-105M of cash taxes implies free cash flow of roughly $180-200M. That is an estimate, not guidance.

One working-capital item deserves monitoring: AR plus contract assets rose from approximately 69 days of the most recent quarter's revenue at year-end to approximately 76 days at June 30. The allowance for credit losses is $1.5M against $414.4M of receivables — 0.36%. The counterparty base (IOCs, NOCs, US Government) has historically paid, but the $3.3M Ghana VAT receivable write-off in Q1-26 and consolidated Angolan operations argue the reserve is thin.

**The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case.** At June 30: cash $629.5M, working capital $854M, long-term debt $490.2M, total equity attributable to Oceaneering $1,164.2M. In July the company issued $500M of 6.875% notes due 2034, tendered roughly $400M of 6.000% 2028 notes for approximately $415M cash, and redeemed the remainder at par plus make-whole. The revolver was upsized from $215M to $345M, extended from April 2027 to July 2031, and repriced 25bp tighter. Pro forma cash is approximately $600M against $500M of principal — roughly $100M net cash, with liquidity near $945M. Covenants (3.25x maximum net leverage, 3.0x minimum interest coverage) are unchanged and compliant. Our Altman Z on FY2025 balances and current market equity is approximately 4.6, well inside the safe zone.

Ongoing interest cost rises by approximately $4.4M annually ($500M × 87.5bp). The one-time Q3 charge comprises the ~$15M tender premium, an undisclosed make-whole premium on the ~$100M redeemed 18 months early, and write-off of unamortized issuance costs ($9.755M of total unamortized discount and issuance costs at June 30). A pre-tax charge of $25-30M, or roughly $0.18-0.21 per share after tax, is our estimate; the aggregate will only be confirmed in the Q3 10-Q. Adding roughly $300M of capitalized operating leases (ROU assets were $295.2M; undiscounted lease obligations $453.1M) gives adjusted gross debt near $800M, approximately 0.5x guided EBITDA net of cash. Three vessel charters were extended in Q3 2025 with terms beginning Q1 2026 into a tighter vessel market, so charter rates have likely reset upward, a cost headwind embedded in OPG's 2026 economics.

**Returns on capital are genuinely good.** NOPAT of $216.2M (operating income × 0.71) against invested capital of $887.8M (debt $500M + equity $1,076.7M − cash $688.9M) gives ROIC of approximately 24.4%; on a broader base of total assets less cash less non-debt current liabilities, approximately 17.8%. Capital intensity is low — $111M capex against roughly $92M of annualized depreciation, with a flat fleet count. That is the right posture at 63-66% utilization, but it also caps volume upside if the 2027 recovery management describes does arrive.

## Growth Analysis

**Subsea Robotics is trading volume for price, and the volume driver is exogenous.** ROV days utilized fell to 28,604 in 1H26 from 30,382, on identical days available (45,250) — utilization of 63% versus 67%. Revenue per ROV day utilized rose to $11,894 in Q2 from $11,265, up 5.6%, and Q2 segment EBITDA margin held flat at 35%. That is the cleanest evidence of pricing power in the file. The demand input is the contracted floater count, which fell 6.2% to a 137 average in 2025. Management stated plainly on the Q2 call that high-70s utilization is unreachable without more contracted rigs, that they expect to "creep above two-thirds," and that there is no pronounced inflection.

**The highest-margin-improvement segment is consuming backlog.** Manufactured Products margin expanded 660bp in 1H26 to 16.4%, achieved *despite* a further $7.5M theme-park-ride inventory reserve — so the core energy hardware business is performing better than segment margins indicate. Backlog fell to $445M at June 30 from $516M a year earlier and $511M at December 31, down roughly 13% both year-over-year and over six months. Trailing book-to-bill was 0.88, following 0.84 for FY2025 and 0.65 at mid-2025, against FY2026 guidance of 0.9-1.0. Three consecutive sub-1.0 readings put 2027 revenue at risk in the segment currently driving margin gains.

The counterweight is the 10-K's reproduction of a third-party projection of 306 subsea tree awards and 370 installations in 2026, versus 190 awards and 343 installations in 2025. If accurate, that is a substantial order-intake step-up flowing to Manufactured Products and Subsea Robotics with a 12-24 month lag. It is an unnamed consultancy's forecast, not a company commitment, and it has not yet shown up in Oceaneering's own book-to-bill. Resolving that tension is the most important item for the Q3 call: either the awards have not converted yet, or Oceaneering is not capturing its share.

**The defense growth is real but small and diluting margin.** ADTech revenue rose 28.0% in 1H26 with unfunded contracted backlog up 136% to $658M. Margin fell to 9.3% from 13.0%, or roughly 11.3% excluding the $6.8M contract-dispute accrual — still down approximately 170bp. ADTech contributed 10.1% of 1H26 segment operating income against Subsea Robotics' 50.4%. For the consolidated multiple to re-rate on defense, ADTech must grow *and* expand margin; at present it is doing the first while compressing on the second. Early-phase XLUUV design work legitimately carries thin margins ahead of production awards, so this may be a timing question rather than a structural ceiling, but it is not yet evidence of a re-rating.

**Estimate momentum is positive but coverage is thin and the base is modest.** Current-year consensus rose 17.2% over 90 days to $2.03; next year rose 9.3% to $2.28; the current quarter is up 22.4% to $0.60. Four consecutive beats (40.1%, 11.6%, 35.6%, 64.0%) support confidence in near-term consensus. Two caveats: only one to three analysts contribute to the quarterly and annual lines, so the revisions reflect a very narrow set of models; and the 90-day revision of +17% is below the threshold at which revision velocity has historically signalled sustained re-rating, particularly when the resulting forward multiple remains above the peer median.

**Geographic growth is rotating, not expanding.** 1H26: UK revenue $187.1M (+37%), Norway $153.7M (+27%), against Africa $197.8M (−11%) and US $596.8M (−2%). North Sea strength is funding African weakness.

## Competitive Landscape

The quantified share position is the most defensible fact in the case: ROV contracts on 82 of 139 contracted floaters (59%) at Q2-26, approximately 60% at year-end 2025. A 250-unit fleet, global shore bases and integration into drilling contractors' operations produce switching costs that showed up as 30% FY2025 segment margins sustained through a *declining* utilization environment, with day rates rising 5.6%. Drill-support ROV work is an oligopoly with materially smaller competitors.

Two qualifications. First, share is flat-to-marginally-lower (60% → 59%), so the moat is stable rather than widening. Second, the addressable base is shrinking: the contracted floater count fell 6.2% in 2025, so volume growth is capped by an exogenous driver regardless of execution quality.

Offshore Projects Group's two Jones Act-compliant owned vessels are a structural advantage in the US Gulf that foreign-flag competitors cannot replicate, but five of seven vessels are chartered, and charter rates appear to have reset upward on the three extensions beginning Q1 2026. In unmanned underwater vehicles, Oceaneering competes against Kongsberg, Boeing, Saab, Lockheed Martin, General Dynamics Mission Systems, Fugro and TechnipFMC. The DIU XLUUV award was structured as a joint contract with Kongsberg as co-awardee rather than a sole-source prime award; Lockheed Martin is a customer on Artemis, not a partner. The credible internal evidence for the defense thesis is ADTech's +28% revenue and +136% unfunded backlog, plus the hiring of a former Seventh Fleet commander to run the segment, a management upgrade for a company trying to move from subcontractor to prime.

Integrity Management & Digital Solutions is the deteriorating franchise: revenue −5.5% in 1H26 and an operating loss of $0.9M against $8.1M of income, after backlog had grown 78%.

## Risk Assessment

**Guidance credibility.** The February 2026 10-K said IMDS operating income would "improve significantly" in 2026. Five months later management said it would "decrease significantly" with low-single-digit margins, citing Middle East uncertainty and reduced West Africa activity. Those causes are exogenous and largely unforecastable, and IMDS is only 2.2% of segment profit, so the earnings impact is trivial. The confidence impact is not: it demonstrates that Oceaneering's backlog is a weak predictor of segment profitability, and it should discount the remaining 2026 segment guidance.

**Backlog definition.** The 10-K reports $2,703M of company-defined backlog plus $658M of unfunded ADTech backlog. The 10-Q reports remaining performance obligations of $395M at June 30, using expedients that exclude contracts of one year or less original duration. The two measure different things and must not be conflated. Many ROV contracts carry 30-day termination provisions. Neither figure is authoritative — backlog overstates contractual commitment; RPO understates practical visibility on recurring day-rate work.

**Customer concentration worsened at the single-name level even as end-market mix diversified.** Top-five customers were 31% of revenue in both 2025 and 2024 (36% in 2023) — flat. The US Government became the only customer above 10%, at 12% of FY2025 revenue, versus none above 10% in 2024. Appropriations, continuing-resolution and shutdown risk are explicitly flagged in the 10-K. Credit quality of that concentration improved; the degree of it rose.

**China.** The FY2025 10-K risk factors state that in September 2025 the Chinese government placed the company on its Unreliable Entity List, generally prohibiting China-related import/export activity and new investment. Management expects no material impact. We could not obtain external corroboration, and the filing's reference to sanctions on "our parent company" is anomalous for an entity with no parent. Treat the designation as a filing-disclosed fact with an open verification gap and unquantified scope.

**Recurring non-core write-downs.** A $13M theme-park-ride inventory reserve in FY2025 and a further $7.5M in Q2-26 brought total inventory reserves to $55M against $197.7M net inventory — approximately 21.8% of gross inventory reserved. MP also booked $3.5M of projected contract losses in 1H26. The filings do not state whether the line is being wound down or remediated; further charges are possible.

**Other specific items.** The Q3 refinancing charge (our estimate $25-30M pre-tax, magnitude undisclosed). $15M of capitalized ERP/cloud implementation costs in 1H26 versus $4.3M in 1H25, during a period when three of the top four roles below the CEO turned over. Reliance on bilateral letter-of-credit lines cancellable at bank discretion (mitigated by the LC sublimit rising from $100M to $150M). A consolidated 45%-owned Angolan VIE and $7.0M of Angolan government bonds. OCSLA litigation over presidential withdrawal of offshore acreage. Corporate unallocated expense rose 20.3% to $189.6M in 2025 and is guided to approximately $200M in 2026, absorbing roughly 35% of segment-level profit gains.

**Macro overlay.** Offshore activity is a lagging derivative of the oil price. Any sustained decline in crude would, with a lag, cool offshore sentiment — a downside vector for a stock priced on a 2027 recovery — while a sustained increase would support FIDs but would not lift the contracted floater count inside twelve months. ### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 42.8% against 44.4% one-year realized — fair to slightly cheap, at the 4th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in contango (42.8% at 13 days, 50.1% at 41 days, 49.0% at 160 days), with implied moves of ±7.5%, ±13.7% and ±24.9% respectively. IV compressed 7.3pp since July 23 on a flat price, the standard post-earnings vol crush. The 1m tenor shows a put/call skew of 8.4%, characterized as elevated put demand. Open interest, however, is overwhelmingly call-side (put/call OI 0.01, volume 0.06); positioning is not defensively structured in size, which is a mild counterpoint to our cautious view.

The practical read: cheap near-dated volatility at the 4th percentile makes protective puts an efficient alternative to outright sales for holders unwilling to trim into strength, particularly ahead of the Q3 print where the refinancing charge lands. Short interest of 9.2% of float and 5.3 days to cover means a positive Q3 surprise can produce a sharp squeeze; sizing matters for anyone acting on the bearish side.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Oceaneering owns a 59-60% share of a consolidated ROV market and is demonstrably raising price into declining utilization (+5.6% per utilized day). ROIC is approximately 24%, capital intensity is low, and the July refinancing removed all maturity risk to 2034 while lifting liquidity to $945M and leaving the company roughly net cash. Four consecutive beats and current-year estimates up 17% in 90 days say the operating trajectory is better than guidance implies. If the projected 306 subsea tree awards in 2026 convert (versus 190 in 2025) and the floater count turns in 2027, the 11.6x EV/EBITDA multiple would be applied to a materially higher forward base — $500M of FY2027 EBITDA at 12x supports $60. ADTech adds an option: +28% revenue growth, $658M of unfunded backlog up 136%, and a credentialed leadership hire, with margin expansion plausible once XLUUV development converts to production.

**Bear case.** The stock is priced for a recovery management explicitly declines to forecast. Guided EBITDA growth is +4.6% at the midpoint and flat at the low end. ROV utilization is down 400bp year-over-year on a shrinking contracted-floater base. Manufactured Products backlog is down 13% with book-to-bill below 1.0 for three consecutive periods, putting 2027 revenue at risk in the segment that is currently supplying the margin story. 1H26 consolidated operating income fell 4.3% on 6.4% higher revenue. Trailing EPS overstates earning power by 65-75%, so the true multiple is approximately 24x, against a peer median of 19.6x and a PEG near 2. The price exceeds every published analyst target. Capital return is 0.8% of market cap with no dividend despite roughly $600M of cash. A Q3 refinancing charge of an undisclosed size lands next quarter. If crude retreats while the rig count stays flat, both the earnings base and the multiple compress: $380M of FY2027 EBITDA at 9x is $34, a 31% decline.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Base case $48 (−2.5% from $49.25); bull case $60 (+21.8%); bear case $34 (−31.0%). Probability-weighted expected return at 45/25/30 is approximately −5%.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength now rather than waiting for the Q3 print, which carries an undisclosed refinancing charge (our estimate $0.18-0.21 per share after tax) and the first read on whether Manufactured Products book-to-bill has recovered toward the guided 0.9-1.0. Reduce exposure by roughly half at current levels; retain a residual position for the 2027 cycle option given the balance sheet and share position, and for the squeeze risk implied by 9.2% short interest. Holders unwilling to sell should consider January 2027 protection while near-dated IV sits at the 4th percentile of its own chain, recognizing the 6-month tenor prices ±24.9%.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better (any two):**
1. Manufactured Products book-to-bill at or above 1.0 with backlog stabilizing above $445M on the Q3 print — evidence the 306-tree-award projection is converting to Oceaneering orders.
2. Average contracted floating rigs rising sequentially toward 145+ with ROV utilization returning above 67%, restoring the volume leg to Subsea Robotics.
3. ADTech segment margin recovering above 13% on continued 20%+ revenue growth, which would make the defense mix shift multiple-accretive rather than dilutive.
4. Price toward $40-42, which would put the stock near the analyst mean and at roughly 9.5-10x EV/FY2027E EBITDA — a level at which the bull-case asymmetry returns.
5. FY2026 EBITDA guidance raised through $440M *and* a materially larger capital-return program (dividend initiation or buyback above $30M per quarter) that puts the ~$600M cash balance to work.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below $400M, or Q3 EBITDA below the $115M low end.
2. A second segment guidance reversal of the IMDS type, which would establish a pattern rather than an exogenous one-off.
3. ROV utilization falling below 60% on a further decline in contracted floaters, combined with revenue per utilized day flattening — the point at which pricing power stops offsetting volume.
4. Material further theme-park or contract-loss charges, or an ERP-related internal control deficiency disclosed in Q3 or Q4.
5. External confirmation that the China Unreliable Entity List designation carries quantified revenue or supply-chain impact.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next hard decision point is the Q3 2026 report (late October 2026), which should disclose the aggregate refinancing charge, the Q4 EBITDA path within the $400-440M range, and Manufactured Products book-to-bill. A secondary checkpoint is the FY2026 10-K in February 2027, which will provide the 2027 contracted-floater base and the first credible read on whether the "early 2027" recovery language has firmed.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory (6.1 → 6.7 → 5.9 → 5.8 → 5.7 → 5.6, now 5.2) has been driven almost entirely by valuation compressing from 7.5 to 3.5 while financial health improved from 6.5 to 7.0.
