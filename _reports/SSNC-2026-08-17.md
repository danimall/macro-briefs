## Executive Summary

SS&C is misclassified by its industry label. "Software – Application" implies a license-and-subscription vendor; the filings describe something different. Technology-enabled services (renamed from "software-enabled services" in Q1 2026) produced $5,211.1M of FY2025 revenue against $6,272.2M total — 83.1% of the company — and 21,300 of 28,800 employees, roughly 7,900 of them in India, sit in client support, consulting and services versus 3,900 in R&D. SS&C runs fund accounting, transfer agency, retirement recordkeeping and pharmacy claims adjudication for asset managers, fund sponsors and health plans, on platforms it built and owns, with its own largely offshore staff performing the work. Pricing scales with client AUM, account counts, transaction volumes and claim volumes rather than seats. Revenue retention has exceeded 95% in each of the last five years, and the top ten clients are about 15% of revenue with none above 5%.

That model has been compounding steadily: revenue growth of 4.2%, 6.9% and 6.6% in FY2023–FY2025, with operating margin up 130bp over three years to 22.9%. The June quarter was better than trend — revenue $1,695.7M, +10.3% (organic constant-currency +7.6%), operating margin 24.6% versus 22.4%, GAAP diluted EPS $0.97 versus $0.72 — and the stock rose about 11% on the July 23–24 release, then continued to $80.49, roughly 20% above the pre-print level and 31% above the June 30 low of $61.40.

The problem with initiating here is arithmetic that sits inside management's own raised guidance. H1 2026 revenue was $3,342.8M, +9.6%. A ~$6.75B full-year midpoint implies H2 of about $3,407.2M against H2 2025 of roughly $3,223.1M, or +5.7% — a ~400bp deceleration, and that H2 figure still carries inorganic contribution from Calastone and Curo in Q3. The Q3 revenue guide of ~$1.68B is below the $1.70B just delivered. Management attributed a couple of hundred basis points of the Q2 organic acceleration to large renewals, which carry near-zero incremental cost and therefore also explain a meaningful share of the 220bp margin gain. Underwriting 9–10% growth off the H1 print extrapolates past what the company is guiding to. At 11.4x guided adjusted EPS the stock is not expensive; at ~23x trailing GAAP EPS with a $2.0B 5.5% note maturing in September 2027, roughly 3.3x true net leverage, and no insider open-market buying through a 32% drawdown, it is not obviously cheap either. HOLD, with a preferred add zone in the high-$60s to low-$70s — below the $69.87 average at which the company itself repurchased 8.7M shares in the first half.

## Company Overview

SS&C, founded by William Stone in 1986 and still chaired and led by him, describes itself as the world's leading hedge fund and private equity administrator and mutual fund transfer agent. The principal franchises:

- **SS&C GlobeOp** — alternative fund administration, more than $2.9 trillion of alternative assets under administration. Competes against custodian banks (State Street, BNY Mellon, Northern Trust) and CITCO; the stated edge is independence from custody and prime brokerage.
- **Global Investor and Distribution Solutions (GIDS)** — global transfer agency and investor servicing on a single platform, including Retirement Solutions (TRAC, >12 million participants, ~400,000 plan sponsors) and, since October 2025, Calastone.
- **Wealth and investment technology** — Black Diamond, Geneva, Advent APX, Eze OMS/EMS, Global Wealth Platform, Genesis, Singularity.
- **Intralinks** — virtual data rooms for M&A and fundraising.
- **Blue Prism** — RPA/agentic automation, plus the internal "Customer Zero" program and the SS&C AI Gateway governance layer.
- **SS&C Health** — pharmacy and medical claims on DomaniRx; more than 600 million claims processed in FY2025.

Contracts run one to five years with monthly or quarterly billing and automatic annual renewal. Geographic mix was 67% U.S. / 33% international in FY2025, up from 31% international in the two prior years. In transfer agency and retirement, SS&C holds client cash as agent — client funds obligations of $3,799.5M at 12/31/2025 rising to about $3,984.5M at 6/30/2026 — which grosses up both sides of the balance sheet and distorts several standard screens, discussed below.

**Earnings quality and the tax base effect.** FY2025 GAAP net income grew only 4.8% while pretax income grew 9.1%. The difference is tax: the effective rate was 14.8% in FY2024, 18.1% in FY2025 and 29.0% in FY2023. FY2024 was an anomalously low base, consistent with excess tax benefits from option exercises during a rising-share-price year. The Q2 2026 rate rose to about 25.1% from 24.4%, consistent with a shrinking windfall benefit as the stock spent much of the year below more strike prices. Anyone comparing FY2025 to FY2024 on net income is comparing against a flattered base.

**Cash generation, adjusted for what the headline excludes.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,744.8M and property-and-equipment capex $80.8M, giving the commonly quoted $1,664.0M of free cash flow (26.5% of revenue, 8.8% yield). SS&C separately capitalized $221.9M of software development in FY2025 ($194.3M in FY2024, $194.9M in FY2023). Deducting it gives $1,442.1M, or 23.0% of revenue and a **7.6% FCF yield** on the current market capitalization and roughly 5.5% on enterprise value. The stricter figure is the comparable one against peers that expense development.

Two related observations. R&D expense fell 2.0% in FY2025 to $507.5M while capitalized software rose 14.2%; total gross development spend went from 12.1% to 11.6% of revenue, up only 2.4% against 6.6% revenue growth, and the incremental capitalization flattered operating income by roughly $27.6M, about 1.9% of the total. The most likely explanation is a genuine multi-year replatforming cycle (Genesis, Singularity, DomaniRx, Calastone integration) whose costs properly qualify for capitalization; no motive should be inferred from the accounting treatment. Declining R&D intensity while management argues AI leadership is worth monitoring in subsequent filings.

A second adjustment cuts the other way on quality: FCF of $1,442.1M against net income of $796.9M is 181% conversion, because D&A of $703.8M substantially exceeds cash capex plus capitalized software of $302.7M. Much of that D&A is amortization of acquired intangibles, and SS&C replaces those intangibles through M&A rather than internal capex — roughly $1.1B of acquisitions closed in 2025. If one treats even half of ongoing M&A as maintenance of the earning asset base, the sustainable cash figure is materially lower than $1.44B. That is the core of the GAAP-versus-adjusted debate.

**Leverage — three different numbers, none of which should be quoted alone.** Total debt was $7,466.9M at 12/31/2025 and $7,577.2M at 6/30/2026 against cash of $434.8M, with roughly $427.8M of annual interest payments at then-current rates and $593.7M of revolver availability. The reported covenant ratio of ~1.70x against a 5.25x maximum is a consolidated *net secured* leverage test: it counts term loans and the revolver, nets cash, and excludes roughly $2.75B of senior unsecured notes. On GAAP EBITDA of $2,140.5M (operating income $1,436.7M + D&A $703.8M) and FY2025 net debt of $7,004.8M, **net debt/EBITDA is 3.27x**. On the credit agreement's add-back-laden Consolidated EBITDA of ~$2,614.5M and Q2 net debt of $7,142.4M it is 2.73x. Interest coverage is 4.6–5.0x — adequate, not strong.

Two screen artifacts deserve correction. An Altman Z of 1.32 puts SS&C nominally in the distress zone, but substituting market equity for book equity in the fourth term lifts it to roughly 1.84, and additionally de-grossing the ~$3.8B of pass-through client funds from both sides of the balance sheet lifts it to roughly 2.38 — grey zone, not distress. The residual weakness is real: goodwill of $9,991.3M is 48.2% of total assets and exceeds book equity of $6,887.6M, so tangible book is deeply negative and the equity cushion in stress is franchise value rather than assets. Similarly, a current ratio of 1.07 is distorted by the client-funds gross-up in both numerator and denominator.

**Rate exposure runs the opposite way to intuition.** Revenue includes investment earnings on roughly $4.0B of client balances held as agent, but SS&C also carries roughly $4.9B of variable-rate debt. Per the 10-Q sensitivity disclosures, 100bp affects variable-rate interest expense by approximately $49M pretax against roughly $13M after-tax on client balances. Direction is clear and magnitudes are approximate: **rate cuts are net positive, rate increases net negative**, by roughly 3–4x. **Q2 2026 in detail.** Revenue $1,695.7M decomposes into organic +$117.6M (7.65% constant-currency), acquisitions +$35.9M and FX +$5.4M. Operating income $417.1M, 24.6% margin, +220bp. Gross margin improved only ~30bp (48.0% → 48.3%); the expansion came from operating expenses falling from roughly 25.5% to 23.7% of revenue, with the filing attributing a ~$7.9M organic opex *decline* to lower personnel costs while revenue grew 10.3%. Restructuring was $9.4M versus $17.1M. Diluted shares fell 4.0% to 242.0M. Adjusted EPS of $1.76 beat the $1.71 consensus in the supplied surprise table by 2.8% (other aggregators cite a $1.68 consensus and a 4.8% beat; the basis is non-GAAP against non-GAAP either way), the fourth consecutive beat, with the prior three at +0.3%, +3.1% and +4.5%.

The margin expansion has two candidate explanations. The automation reading — that SS&C is making a 21,300-person services organization more productive with its own agents — is supported by absolute opex declining on 10.3% revenue growth. Management's own remark that "we did have a couple of big renewals in Q2, and that's why organic revenue was up a couple of hundred extra basis points" points the other way, since zero-marginal-cost license renewals flow almost entirely to operating income. One quarter does not distinguish a durable productivity curve from renewal timing, and the Q3 guide argues that timing was the larger contributor.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying organic growth is probably 5–6%, not 7.6%. The evidence: Q2's acceleration was explicitly renewal-attributed; the Q3 guide of ~$1.68B is below both Q2 actual and consensus; and the full-year midpoint implies H2 of +5.7% including inorganic contribution. The FY2026 guide of ~$6.75B is +7.6% total revenue growth, and consensus adjusted EPS of $7.06 for the current year and $7.73 for next year implies about 9.5% EPS growth, sourced from roughly 5–6% revenue, 50–100bp of annual margin expansion, and 3–4% share-count reduction.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-quarter +2.7% over 30 days, current year +2.5% and next year +3.1% over 90 days, across nine to ten analysts. This confirms steady delivery rather than an inflection. It does not resemble the revision velocity that reliably precedes re-rating, and it should be weighted accordingly.

Where incremental growth could come from:

- **Calastone** ($1,030.0M purchase price, closed October 2025) operates a fund-transaction network connecting managers, distributors, platforms, transfer agents and custodians, with tokenized fund issuance capability. A network asset bolted onto the transfer-agency franchise has better defensibility than a book-of-business roll-up. Management characterized Calastone and Curo as performing ahead of expectations with 431 new clients added, though revenue contribution, margin profile and purchase multiple are not separately disclosed.
- **Asia-Pacific** grew 22.9% from 2023 to 2025 to $344.0M — real, but 5.5% of revenue, so not yet a needle-mover.
- **SS&C Health/DomaniRx** — the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program reportedly processed nearly 3 million claims in its first weeks, against more than 600 million claims company-wide in FY2025. New workload, small scale, and currently capital-consuming: a $75.0M mandatory JV capital call was authorized in Q2, with SS&C's roughly $60.1M share funded in July 2026 against roughly $241.7M of associated carrying intangibles.

Against those, one specific disclosed headwind: brokerage subaccounting conversion, which the 10-K says "generally results in lower revenues" because per-unit fees are lower, and which the filing states "have begun to extend to the tax-advantaged accounts (such as retirement and Section 529 accounts) we service." Retirement and 529 accounts were the stickier, higher-fee-per-unit part of the transfer-agency franchise. The magnitude is not quantified, and acquisitions mask the erosion in headline growth. Add the secular shift toward passive strategies as a risk to the hedge fund client base against more than $2.9T of alternative AUA, and the growth profile is best characterized as durable mid-single-digit with high retention, not accelerating.

The entire valuation debate is the $3.30-per-share gap between GAAP and adjusted EPS — 47% of adjusted EPS — dominated by acquired-intangible amortization and stock compensation. For a company that has made 70 acquisitions since 1995 and states that "our acquisitions have been an extension of our research and development effort," amortization of acquired technology is closer to a recurring cost of the operating model than a non-recurring item. Stock compensation is unambiguously a real cost. The honest range is therefore ~11x on the metric management guides to and ~21–26x on GAAP, and neither number alone settles it.

Peer context matters more than usual here because the label is wrong. Against the application-software cohort, 11.4x looks like a deep discount. Against the business the filings actually describe — financial-transaction processing and outsourced servicing — 11.4x adjusted and roughly 20–21x forward GAAP is approximately market. [unverified — no peer-benchmark data was available in Phase 1 (peer_benchmarks: available=false); the specific peer-median forward multiples cited previously are not supported by the sources.] SS&C is priced as a levered, mid-single-digit-growth processor rather than as software, and that pricing is defensible given 3.3x net leverage, negative tangible book, and a services model whose labor base is 74% of headcount.

What would make the market wrong? Two candidates. First, if the Q2 operating leverage is durable automation rather than renewal timing, margin expansion runs faster than the 50bp/year trend and both EPS and multiple move. Base-case fair value: **$90**, which is 11.6x next-year consensus adjusted EPS of $7.73 and roughly 21x my estimated FY2027 GAAP EPS of about $4.30 (built from FY2026 GAAP of roughly $3.85, 6% revenue growth, 50bp of margin expansion and 3% share reduction). On analyst targets as a signal: the covered targets range from a low of $82.00 to a high of $112.00, with a mean of $92.33 and median of $90.00 — all above spot, mechanically supportive, but SSNC is a steady compounder near consensus, not a cheap inflection story where targets reliably lag. The dispersion ($82 to $112) is wide enough that the mean carries limited information. Treat it as corroboration of the absence of a bear case on the street, not as independent evidence of upside.

## Competitive Landscape

SS&C does not face one competitor; it faces a different competitive structure in each of nine or ten verticals, and its relative position varies widely.

**Strongest — alternatives administration.** Against State Street, BNY Mellon, Northern Trust and CITCO, SS&C's independence argument is credible: custodians and prime brokers face conflicts when they also administer. Owning the full software stack lets SS&C capture the services margin rather than pay a vendor. Portal competitors include eFront (BlackRock) and Allvue.

**Most contested — wealth.** Envestnet, Orion, Addepar and SEI, plus custodians Schwab, Fidelity and Raymond James. The 10-K concedes competitors here are "generally smaller firms focused solely on the advisory market" — focused specialists against SS&C's breadth. Wealth and Investment Technologies reportedly rebounded to 9.8% organic growth in Q2 from roughly flat in the prior quarter; given renewal lumpiness that reads as volatile rather than as a durable inflection.

**Structurally uncomfortable — retirement and automation.** In retirement, competitors include Infosys, TCS and Wipro, which compete on the same labor-arbitrage axis SS&C uses and are aggressive AI adopters. In intelligent automation, the named competitors are Microsoft, Pega, UiPath and Appian plus emerging agentic providers. Competing with Microsoft on horizontal automation is not a winnable fight; SS&C's defense is vertical domain depth in regulated workflows, the AI Gateway governance layer, and the "Customer Zero" proof point of running its own operations on Blue Prism.

**Healthcare** is a structural-independence play mirroring the fund-administration argument: SS&C Health is not owned by or merged with a health plan, which it argues eliminates channel conflicts around steering membership to retail, mail-order or specialty pharmacy. That is a credible differentiator against PBM-owned platforms, and the unbundling thesis — health plans replacing monolithic legacy systems with modular best-in-class vendors — is the strategic bet behind DomaniRx.

Trajectory assessment: improving in alternatives and, if Calastone's network integrates, in fund distribution; stable in enterprise wealth technology; slowly eroding in transfer agency because of subaccounting fee compression; structurally exposed in retirement and horizontal automation. Revenue retention above 95% for five consecutive years and top-ten client concentration of about 15% with none above 5% cap the downside on any single loss. The mix shift — technology-enabled services rising from 81.6% to 83.1% of revenue — increases concentration in a single delivery model even as customer concentration stays low, and prior-year client-concentration data is not available, so diversification cannot be claimed as improving.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The September 2027 refinancing.** $2.0B of 5.5% senior notes mature roughly thirteen months after the latest reported quarter. Refinancing at prevailing spreads for a ~3x-levered issuer is feasible against $1.4–1.7B of annual free cash flow, and the coupon reset is modest in isolation — 100bp on $2.0B is roughly $20M pretax, about $0.06 of after-tax EPS. The coupon reset is a real earnings variable and is the most concrete explanation for why cash generation of this quality trades at 11x.

**2. AI is genuinely two-sided, and the 10-K says so.** The same filing that markets AI Gateway and agentic automation warns that "third parties may deploy AI technologies that disrupts our business models, or in a manner that reduces customer demand for, or affects the desirability, pricing or structure of, our products and services." With 74% of headcount in services and pricing set per account and per transaction, the technology that expands SS&C's margins also lowers the barrier for clients to in-source and gives competitors — including Indian IT majors — a path to undercut. The Q2 opex decline shows the opportunity side working; nothing yet shows the pricing side. Both directions remain substantially unpriced.

**3. Guidance deceleration.** The implied H2 of +5.7% versus H1 of +9.6% is the most decision-relevant number in the file. Management said it would not "pre-beat the drum" on upcoming renewals, so the guide may be conservative; the company did beat its own revenue expectation by 2.1% in Q2. The Q3 print in late October is the first test, and a stock 20% above its pre-earnings level has less room for a modest miss.

**4. Goodwill concentration.** $9,991.3M, or 48.2% of assets, exceeding book equity. An impairment would not affect cash flow or covenant compliance but would eliminate a large share of book equity and would be a hard signal on acquisition quality.

**5. Fiduciary and service-provider tail risk.** The filings note that the dollar value of transactions processed vastly exceeds the revenue derived, and that as a fund administrator SS&C "has in the past, and may in the future" face claims from investors, liquidators and regulators when an administered fund suffers losses or fraud. Item 3 Legal Proceedings was not examined in the underlying work, so active matters are an open item.

**6. FX and India.** International revenue rose to 33% and the company states it does not engage in material hedging. Q2 FX was a $5.4M tailwind and can reverse. Roughly 7,900 of 28,800 employees are in India, with disclosed exposure to tax-incentive continuation and exchange controls.

**7. Governance.** Stone beneficially owns about 14.4% with the right to nominate two directors including himself while CEO; there is no key-man insurance; the board is classified with no stockholder action by written consent and no right to call special meetings. A specific value leak exists in any takeover: under the IFDS L.P. partnership agreement, a change of control gives the other partner the option to purchase SS&C's interests at book value, which "may be substantially less than fair market value."

### Options Market Signal

Positioning does not corroborate a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV of 30.3% against 28.1% one-year realized is fair (2.2pp spread, 47th percentile within the chain); put/call skew of 1.5% is balanced; volume put/call is 0.27 and open-interest put/call 0.60 — call-tilted. The nominal backwardation (30.3% at three days versus 25.4% at six months) is an artifact of an imminent short-dated expiry rather than a signal of priced stress; the September and October tenors sit at 25.7% and 25.2%. Since the June 3 snapshot, open-interest put/call fell from 0.88 to 0.60 while the price rose 18.4%, consistent with hedges being lifted into the rally. Short interest is 4.2% of float at 3.1 days to cover — uncrowded, with no squeeze dynamic in either direction. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±2.8%, and the six-month tenor prices ±12.9%, which brackets my base and bear scenarios reasonably.

The market is pricing normal volatility and mildly bullish positioning, which argues against a near-term dislocation but also means no cushion is priced for an October guidance disappointment.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $108 (+34.2%)

Organic growth holds at 7%+ rather than decelerating to the guided 5.7%, because the Q2 renewal cohort was a genuine pull-forward of durable multi-year commitments and Calastone's network economics begin contributing incremental distribution revenue. The 220bp margin gain proves to be automation-driven: SS&C keeps absolute operating expense roughly flat while revenue compounds, taking operating margin toward 26–27% over two years as its own agents reduce the cost of a 21,300-person services organization. Adjusted EPS reaches $8.50–9.00 by FY2028 on 3–4% annual share reduction, and the September 2027 refinancing clears at a coupon inside 6.5%, removing the leverage discount and allowing the multiple to move to 13.5–14x. Retention above 95%, no client above 5% of revenue, and a mission-critical regulated workflow make the revenue base far more defensible than the "application software" comparison implies. $108 is 14.0x next-year consensus of $7.73; the high street target is $112.

### Bear Case — $66 (−18.0%)

The guided H2 deceleration to 5.7% turns out to be structural rather than conservative. Subaccounting conversion continues spreading into retirement and 529 accounts, compressing revenue per account in GIDS; passive migration erodes the hedge fund AUA base that drives GlobeOp fees; and Indian IT majors plus agentic automation platforms begin repricing outsourced recordkeeping mandates, so SS&C's own cost deflation passes through to clients as price rather than to shareholders as margin. Q2's operating leverage does not repeat, because it was renewal timing. The September 2027 refinancing clears at 7%+, adding roughly $30M of pretax interest, while roughly $4.9B of variable-rate debt is sensitive to rate moves (approximately $49M pretax per 100bp, against only ~$13M after-tax of offsetting client-cash revenue). Diluted share count re-expands as up to 15.5M previously antidilutive option shares re-enter on a treasury-stock basis — under 6.4% of the 242.0M base, but working against the buyback. The multiple compresses to 8.5x adjusted EPS, roughly where the stock traded on June 30 at $61.40. $66 is about 7% above that low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month expectation is positive — mid-single-digit revenue, 50–100bp of margin expansion, 3–4% share reduction and a 1.34% dividend compound to roughly 10% of EPS growth — but the margin of safety at $80.49 is thin after a 31% move off the June low, and management's own guide points to slower H2 revenue.

- **Base case target: $90 (+11.8%).** 11.6x next-year consensus adjusted EPS of $7.73; ~21x estimated FY2027 GAAP EPS of ~$4.30. Coincides with the analyst median.
- **Bull case target: $108 (+34.2%).** 14.0x $7.73 on durable automation-led margin expansion plus a clean 2027 refinancing.
- **Bear case target: $66 (−18.0%).** 8.5x $7.73 / ~15x GAAP, roughly 7% above the June 30 low of $61.40.
- **Probability-weighted return:** approximately 50% base / 22% bull / 28% bear gives roughly +8% price return plus 1.3% dividend — a fair, not compelling, risk-adjusted outcome.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints the late-October Q3 report (against the ~$1.68B guide) and the FY2026 close in February 2027, followed by refinancing developments through mid-2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price in the **$68–72** range on unchanged fundamentals — below the $69.87 average of the company's own H1 repurchases — which would restore roughly 25%+ upside to the $90 base case.
2. Q3 revenue at or above **$1.71B** with organic constant-currency growth at or above **7%** and operating margin held at or above **24%**, demonstrating that Q2's leverage was not renewal timing. Two consecutive quarters of absolute operating-expense discipline on high-single-digit revenue growth would move the automation thesis from hypothesis to evidence.
3. Refinancing of the $2.0B September 2027 notes announced at a coupon inside **6.5%**, or a ratings upgrade, either of which compresses the leverage discount.
4. Clustered open-market insider purchases — absent through the entire drawdown from the $91.07 high to the $61.40 low — which would be a meaningful change in the behavioral signal.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Organic constant-currency growth below **5%** in any quarter, or a full-year revenue guidance cut, confirming that the H2 deceleration is structural.
2. Operating margin retracing below **23%** on a year-over-year basis, indicating the Q2 gain was timing and that services cost inflation is outrunning automation.
3. Evidence that subaccounting conversion or AI-driven repricing is compressing revenue per account — visible as GIDS/Retirement revenue declining while account or participant counts grow.
4. Total net debt/GAAP EBITDA rising above **3.75x**, or a refinancing at a coupon above **7.25%**, or a goodwill impairment against the $9,991.3M balance.

**Reassessment timeline:** late October 2026 (Q3 report and Q4 guide) is the next natural decision point. An interim reassessment is warranted if the stock trades into the low $70s, which would convert this to a staged-entry case without any change in fundamentals.

## Conclusion

SS&C is a high-retention, low-client-concentration outsourcer with genuine cash generation — $1.44B of free cash flow net of capitalized software, a 7.6% equity FCF yield, four consecutive earnings beats, and estimates rising 2.5–3.1% over 90 days. It is also a 70-acquisition rollup with $9.99B of goodwill exceeding book equity, roughly 3.3x true net leverage that its headline covenant ratio understates by about 60%, a $2.0B maturity in September 2027, and a services-heavy cost base that AI can either compress profitably or commoditize competitively. The filings support both readings, which is why the stock carries a processor multiple rather than a software multiple.

The specific reason not to initiate at $80.49 is that the mispricing which existed at $62–67 has largely been captured. Management raised guidance and the stock responded with a 20% move, yet that same raised guidance implies H2 revenue growth of roughly 5.7% against 9.6% in H1, and the Q3 guide sits below the quarter just delivered. Base-case upside of about 12% against 18% bear-case downside, with no insider buying through a 32% drawdown and a leverage overhang that resolves in 2027 rather than 2026, is a fair-value setup. HOLD, add on weakness toward the high-$60s to low-$70s, and let the October print resolve whether the margin expansion is automation or arithmetic.
