**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CMS Energy is a Michigan-focused regulated utility holding company whose dominant subsidiary, Consumers Energy, serves roughly 1.9 million electric and 1.8 million gas customers under cost-of-service regulation by the Michigan Public Service Commission. The investment thesis is the classic regulated-utility algorithm: a $24.1B five-year capital plan through 2030 drives >8% annual rate-base growth, which management converts into a 6-8% adjusted EPS growth target and a dividend that has risen for 20 consecutive years. Q1 2026 confirmed the engine is running: net income to common of $338M (+12% YoY), diluted EPS of $1.10 (vs $1.01), and reaffirmed 2026 guidance. Estimate revisions have edged higher (+8.5% on the current quarter over 90 days, +6.5% on next quarter), and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters.

The case is balanced rather than compelling. CMS carries a genuinely idiosyncratic overhang in the J.H. Campbell coal plant standoff: a 1,407 MW complex Consumers received state approval to retire is being kept running by five sequential federal Section 202(c) emergency orders, generating roughly $180M of net compliance cost through Q1 2026 with FERC cost-recovery not yet secured (FERC rejected MISO's first compliance filing in March 2026). The 2025 electric rate case granted $277M of $387M requested at a 9.90% ROE below the 10.25% asked, illustrating the regulatory-lag compression that governs earned returns against the large capex program. The business is structurally free-cash-flow negative (Q1 capex of $1,039M against operating cash flow of $705M) and funds the gap with continuous debt and persistent equity dilution via a $1B ATM/forward program.

At $73.46, CMS trades at roughly 19x the $3.88 current-year consensus and 17.6x the $4.17 next-year estimate, a discount to the multi-utility peer median P/E of ~23.1x but appropriate given below-median earned-ROE outcomes and the Campbell uncertainty. With ~3.1% yield, 6-8% EPS growth, and a consensus mean target of ~$80 (implying ~8% upside), this is a steady total-return vehicle without the asymmetry that warrants a buy. We rate CMS a HOLD on an income basis.

## Company Overview

CMS Energy operates through three segments. The electric utility and gas utility within Consumers Energy produce essentially all operating income; the non-regulated NorthStar Clean Energy segment (~1,665 MW of independent power capacity, focused on tax-equity-financed renewables) contributes modestly but with disproportionate volatility. In Q1 2026, NorthStar swung to $41M net income from a $18M loss a year earlier, the single largest driver of the quarter's EPS growth.

The competitive moat is the standard regulated-utility one: a legally protected Lower Peninsula service territory with rates set on a cost-of-service plus authorized-ROE basis (currently 9.90% electric). Retail Open Access customer choice is capped at 10% of sales and is fully subscribed, so competitive erosion is structurally limited to fewer than 300 customers. The principal competition CMS faces is regulatory and political.

The company is executing a clean-energy transition mandated by Michigan's 2023 Energy Law (50% renewable by 2030, 60% by 2035, 100% clean by 2040). The June 2026 Integrated Resource Plan proposes an "all-of-the-above" approach: >13 GW of new renewables and storage alongside ~1,500 MW of new natural gas capacity in Bay and Genesee counties. The gas addition is a pragmatic pivot toward firm dispatchable capacity, prioritizing reliability and affordability over pure decarbonization.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 operating revenue rose 11.6% YoY to $2.73B, driven by electric and gas rate increases (+$25M electric, +$49M gas net) plus higher gas commodity cost pass-through. Segment net income: Electric $110M (down $14M YoY on storm restoration and ERP/IT costs), Gas $220M (+$7M), NorthStar $41M (vs -$18M), Corporate -$33M. The earnings bridge is textbook regulated-utility mechanics (capex flows into rate base, which produces authorized returns), with rising depreciation, property-tax, and IT-implementation headwinds partially offsetting the rate increases.

Operating cash flow fell to $705M from $1,000M a year earlier. This optically alarming decline is driven by PSCR/GCR commodity-cost under-collections and vendor payment timing, both of which reverse through the regulatory recovery mechanism rather than signaling fundamental erosion. Q1 capex was $1,039M (up from $888M), confirming the structurally negative free cash flow that defines a high-growth regulated utility. The company funds the gap with continuous debt and equity issuance.

The balance sheet is appropriately leveraged for the model. Total debt is ~$18.8B; CMS parent debt/capital sits at 0.55 against a 0.70 covenant, and Consumers at 0.50 against a 0.65 covenant, leaving comfortable headroom. A low Altman Z-Score (~0.8) is normal for a regulated utility intentionally leveraged against a stable, rate-recoverable asset base; the binding credit metrics (covenant compliance, investment-grade ratings, $1.4B+ revolver availability at Consumers) are healthy.

Equity funding runs through a $1B ATM/forward program. In Q1 2026, CMS entered forward sales for ~6.4M shares at ~$75.63 and settled 1.9M shares for $142M net proceeds. With 308.9M shares outstanding and rising, dilution is modest but persistent, the necessary cost of funding the capex program. The dividend is $0.57/quarter ($2.28 annualized, ~3.1% yield), with a ~6.5% ten-year growth rate.

The earnings track record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one narrow miss over the last four quarters, with the Q1 2026 beat (+1.9%) and a strong Q3 2025 (+8.9%). Revision momentum is positive on the near quarters but essentially flat on the full-year and next-year numbers (+0.3% and 0.0%), indicating the market sees timing shifts rather than a fundamental step-up in earnings power.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm is durable and clearly articulated, anchored by Michigan's statutory clean-energy mandate that provides a multi-decade capex runway with regulatory cost recovery. It is among the more transparent and well-supported growth stories in the regulated-utility space.

The differentiated catalyst is a >1 GW data-center load agreement signed in 2025. For a utility whose underlying demand growth is otherwise low-single-digit, incremental large-load demand materially improves the rate-base and sales-growth outlook. Management has flagged the offsetting risk in the 10-Q, stating that anticipated data-center load growth "may not materialize as expected" and citing AI-sector confidence shocks as a potential demand and financing risk. This caution is well-placed; the broader AI-infrastructure financing question is unresolved, and power-demand commitments from compute customers carry execution risk.

Additional growth and de-risking levers: the December 2025 $383M Toshiba/TAES jury verdict (plus $11M liquidated damages) on the defective Ludington pumped-storage overhaul could substantially offset Consumers' ~$350M regulatory asset, though realization is appealable and years away. The MCV Partnership 10-year PPA (up to 1,240 MW from 2030) and the Covert Generating Station bolster resource adequacy through the coal transition. Asset rationalization (sale of 13 hydro dams with a 30-year PPA buyback, HL Solar tax-equity monetization) supports capital recycling.

We score growth prospects at 6.8: solid and well-supported, but the upside on the full-year and next-year estimates is flat, the data-center catalyst is unconfirmed, and the achievable EPS growth is constrained by rate-case outcomes running below requested levels.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the multi-utility peer median P/E of ~23.1x (NiSource 23.1x, CenterPoint 26.2x, Ameren 19.7x, DTE 24.0x, Pinnacle West 19.4x), CMS trades at a discount. The discount is largely warranted: the 2025 electric rate case landed at a 9.90% authorized ROE and granted only about 72% of the requested revenue increase, and the Campbell cost-recovery overhang introduces uncertainty that peers without an analogous federal standoff do not carry. CMS sits closer to the cheaper end of the cohort (alongside Pinnacle West and Ameren) rather than the premium names (DTE, CenterPoint).

This is not an earnings-trough story, so cycle-adjusted valuation logic does not apply; CMS is a stable, slow-compounding regulated utility, and trailing/forward P/E are the right anchors. The dividend yield of ~3.1% sits modestly above the peer median of 2.7% and provides absolute support. PEG on a 6-8% growth rate at ~18x forward is roughly 2.5-3.0x, which is rich on a pure growth-adjusted basis but standard for the utility group, where the multiple reflects bond-proxy income quality more than growth.

The consensus mean target is $79.79 and the median $80.50, with a high of $88 and a low of $66. Analyst sentiment is characterized as "degrading," and the recommendation distribution (3 strong buy, 5 buy, 7 hold, 1 strong sell) skews neutral. Treating analyst targets as floors rather than ceilings does not apply here; that principle is for cheap inflection stocks, and CMS is a fairly valued steady compounder where consensus targets reflect the same modest-upside view we hold.

We score valuation at 5.5. The stock is reasonably priced for what it is, but there is no identifiable mispricing or catalyst that argues for asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

CMS's competitive position is stable-to-improving, anchored by a protected service territory and constructive (if not generous) Michigan regulation. The 2023 Energy Law provides statutory demand for the capex program, and the data-center load agreement adds a structural sales tailwind that most utilities lack. The pragmatic gas pivot in the June 2026 IRP (~1,500 MW of firm capacity despite the 100%-clean-by-2040 mandate) signals management is managing the reliability/affordability/decarbonization trilemma realistically, a likely credit positive even if it draws environmental-intervenor opposition in rate cases.

The defining industry tension is the clean-energy transition versus reliability, embodied in the Campbell standoff, where federal energy-security policy collides with Michigan's decarbonization timeline. CMS sits at the center of a multi-state MISO reliability dispute, a source of both political risk and, potentially, federal recognition of its baseload value. We score competitive position at 6.5: a wide regulatory moat with a modest, unconfirmed demand tailwind.

## Risk Assessment

**J.H. Campbell federal emergency orders (the central idiosyncratic risk).** Consumers received MPSC approval in May 2025 to retire the Campbell complex, recovering book value plus a 9.0% ROE via a regulatory asset. The U.S. Secretary of Energy instead issued a Section 202(c) order forcing continued operation, now extended through five sequential 90-day orders (the fifth in May 2026). Net compliance costs were $42M on the first order and $138M on the second (net of MISO revenue). Recovery is sought through a MISO Tariff modification at FERC; FERC granted the underlying complaint in August 2025, and a revised filing is pending. The Michigan Attorney General, Organization of MISO States, and environmental groups are challenging the orders in federal appeals court (arguments heard May 2026). The probability and timing of cost recovery remain unresolved, leaving an ongoing cash and political exposure.

**Regulatory lag and rate-case outcomes.** The 2025 electric case was complicated by a company calculation error corrected via an April 2026 MPSC Errata. A $240M gas rate case is pending (order due by October 2026). The spread between requested and authorized outcomes directly governs earned ROE and EPS achievability against the $24B plan.

**Capital-market dependence.** With persistent negative FCF, CMS relies on continuous debt and equity access; the 10-Q warns that restricted market access would force reduced capital spending, directly impairing the rate-base growth thesis. In an environment of elevated long-end rates and a flat-to-rising-rate path, this dependence raises the cost of the growth algorithm.

**Data-center demand realization.** The >1 GW agreement is a key growth pillar, but management has flagged it may not materialize.

**State tax claim.** An adverse Michigan Court of Appeals ruling (Feb 2026) on MISO sales apportionment prompted an $18M charge; an unfavorable Supreme Court outcome could force revaluation of state deferred tax liabilities.

The rising-rate environment is a sector-wide headwind for bond-proxy utilities: higher discount rates compress the present value of long-dated dividend streams and raise the cost of the debt/equity funding the capex program. This is partly priced into the modest multiple but remains a live valuation risk if the long end pushes higher.

We score risk factors at 5.5: the Campbell overhang and rate-case compression are specific, unresolved, and not fully priced, partially offset by the asymmetric Toshiba verdict upside and the comfortable covenant headroom.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** CMS executes its 6-8% EPS algorithm reliably, the gas rate case lands constructively (order by October 2026), FERC ultimately approves Campbell cost recovery, the data-center load materializes, and the Toshiba verdict offsets the Ludington regulatory asset. Multiple holds at ~21x on rising estimates. Bull target ~$88 (matching the analyst high), or ~20% above current.

**Bear case.** Campbell cost recovery is denied or repeatedly delayed, the gas rate case compresses earned ROE further, rising rates de-rate the utility group, and the data-center load fails to materialize, leaving the capex program funded by dilutive equity into a weak demand backdrop. Multiple compresses toward 16x on the FY26 number. Bear target ~$62-66, or ~10-16% below current.

The distribution is roughly symmetric, with a fair-value base case modestly above current price. That symmetry, against a ~3.1% yield, defines this as a steady total-return hold rather than an actionable long.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on a durable ~3.1% yield with 20 years of dividend growth plus a 6-8% EPS algorithm, generating a high-single-digit total return profile appropriate for income and defensive allocation, without the asymmetry to justify a buy.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FERC approves the MISO Tariff modification securing Campbell cost recovery, removing the ~$180M+ overhang and the political tail risk.
- The pending gas rate case (order by October 2026) lands at or near the $240M request with an authorized ROE at/above 10.0%, demonstrating constructive regulatory support for the capex program.
- The >1 GW data-center load is confirmed in service or contractually firmed, raising the rate-base growth ceiling, particularly if combined with a pullback toward the high-$60s that improves the entry yield above ~3.4%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A definitive FERC denial of Campbell cost recovery combined with a continued forced-operation order, locking in unrecovered compliance costs.
- A gas rate-case outcome materially below request with an authorized ROE cut below ~9.7%, signaling regulatory deterioration.
- Loss of investment-grade rating or covenant pressure forcing a cut to the capex program, which would break the rate-base growth thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the gas rate-case order (by October 2026), the next Campbell emergency-order/FERC compliance update, and Q2/Q3 2026 earnings. We would revisit on any of these.

## Conclusion

CMS Energy is a high-quality, defensive regulated utility with a transparent 6-8% EPS / >8% rate-base growth algorithm, a 20-year dividend-growth record, and constructive Michigan regulation. The score trajectory has drifted lower (6.9 in February to 6.5 in May, now 6.1), reflecting accumulating regulatory and cost-recovery pressures rather than a deterioration in the franchise; this update largely confirms that drift, with the Campbell overhang and below-request rate-case outcomes as the marginal negatives offsetting positive near-term estimate revisions and the NorthStar turnaround. The stock is fairly valued at ~19x forward against a ~23x peer median, with the discount warranted by below-median earned ROE and the federal-order uncertainty. With roughly symmetric risk/reward around a base case modestly above current price and a ~3.1% yield underpinning total return, CMS is a hold for income-oriented and defensive portfolios. We would become more constructive on FERC cost-recovery resolution, a constructive gas rate case, or a pullback toward the high-$60s that lifts the entry yield.
