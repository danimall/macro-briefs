**Research Date: August 6, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pursuit is a 19-month-old public company inside a century-old corporate shell. The former Viad Corp sold its GES exhibitions business for $535 million on December 31, 2024, relaunched as a pure-play owner-operator of sightseeing attractions and adjacent lodging, and now runs 14 attractions and 29 lodges across the Canadian Rockies, Montana, Alaska, Iceland, and Costa Rica. The investable asset is scarcity: the Banff Gondola, the Columbia Icefield Adventure, the Jasper SkyTram and the Maligne Lake Cruise sit on Parks Canada concessions and land leases with 25 to 46 years of remaining term, and no second gondola will be permitted inside Banff National Park. That shows up in the operating data — FY2025 same-store attraction effective ticket price rose 8.6% while visitor volume rose only 1.4%, and same-store hotel ADR rose 4.5% while occupancy *also* rose 1.8 points to 73.6%. Both are signatures of constrained supply.

The problem is what the numbers have done since. Same-store attraction visitors fell 4.9% in Q2 2026 and 3.6% in H1 2026, the second consecutive period of volume weakness after FY2025's +1.4%. Same-store ticket pricing decelerated from +8.6% to +5.7%. Management attributes the volume decline to unfavorable weather near several attractions, which is entirely plausible for outdoor gondolas, glacier tours and boat cruises. Two alternative explanations cannot be excluded from the filings: price elasticity resistance now that average tickets have compounded to roughly $51, and softer cross-border North American leisure demand. Growth has shifted from volume-plus-price to price-only, supplemented by acquisitions — Tabacón in Costa Rica at ~3.8x revenue, the Jasper SkyTram (where $13.5M of the $16.5M purchase price was allocated to a 27-year operating license rather than physical assets), and Eagle Wing Tours on July 14, 2026 for about US$17M.

At $50.10 the stock has appreciated 72.9% over twelve months and sits 11.3% below its June 30 high of $56.51. On pro forma numbers it trades at roughly 14.3x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $105.9M excluding noncontrolling interests, 15.0x including NCI at book, 29.8x FY2026 consensus EPS of $1.68, and a derived maintenance free cash flow yield near 3.5%. Attractions and regional resort operators historically clear at 9-11x EBITDA; a scarcity premium is defensible, but the current multiple already embeds one. Two internal anchors matter more than the peer table: Pursuit sold Flyover at approximately 14.5x that unit's Adjusted EBITDA, essentially the multiple the market assigns Pursuit itself, and the company's own last observable repurchase was in May at $41.87 with zero shares bought in June despite $57.1M of unused authorization. Maintain HOLD with a base case of $54.

## Company Overview

Pursuit reports as a single operating segment organized into destination collections: the Banff Jasper Collection (~1,220 rooms plus the Banff Gondola, Columbia Icefield Adventure and Skywalk, Lake Minnewanka and Maligne Lake cruises, Golden Skybridge, Jasper SkyTram, Open Top Touring), the Glacier Park Collection (~630 rooms in Montana), the Alaska Collection (~520 rooms plus Kenai Fjords Tours), Sky Lagoon in Iceland, and Tabacón Thermal Resort & Spa in Costa Rica (105 rooms on 570 acres, acquired July 1, 2025). Revenue splits into attractions, hospitality, transportation and other. Approximately 71% of FY2025 revenue was earned outside the United States — Canada $244.7M, Iceland $62.2M, Costa Rica $13.1M of $452.4M — and the company does not hedge translation exposure.

The reported financial history requires care. XBRL revenue tags for FY2023 ($1.239B) and FY2022 ($1.127B) are legacy Viad consolidated figures including GES. Restated continuing-operations revenue for FY2023 is $350.3M. FY2024 net income of $373.8M is almost entirely the $421.9M pre-tax gain on the GES sale inside discontinued operations, against a $51.8M continuing-operations loss. The correct revenue series is $350.3M → $366.5M → $452.4M. Any screen built on raw tags will read a 70% revenue collapse that did not occur.

Vendor data compounds this. The supplied profile shows trailing and forward P/E both at 46.02x on EPS of $0.876. That EPS figure reconciles neither to FY2025 reported diluted EPS of $0.80 nor to the sum of the last four reported quarters (approximately $1.30 depending on the Q2 basis used). Valuation work in this report therefore uses forward consensus and EBITDA, not the vendor P/E.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue of $452.4M grew 23.4%. Derived EBITDA — revenue less cost of food, beverage and retail ($34.6M), operating expenses ex-D&A ($226.1M) and SG&A ($80.1M) — was $111.6M, a 24.7% margin against 17.3% in FY2024. That arithmetic reconciles exactly to reported pre-tax income of $55.0M after D&A of $46.1M, net interest of $8.8M and other expense of $1.7M. The proxy's company-selected Adjusted EBITDA is $105.9M; the most likely bridge is the unrealized foreign-exchange gain on remeasurement of the Sky Lagoon finance lease obligation, which runs through operating expenses and was a $6.1M tailwind in H1 2025 versus a $(0.2)M loss in H1 2026. Pursuit's own non-GAAP reconciliation was not located, so treat the bridge as a hypothesis. The directional point stands: a non-cash item on a 42-year Icelandic land lease can move reported operating expenses by $6M in either direction, and it reversed in 2026.

The FY2025 margin expansion is flattered by the comparison. FY2024 absorbed the July 2024 Jasper wildfires during peak season and $47.6M of impairments, mostly at Flyover. Of the 23.4% revenue growth, roughly 7-10 points were same-store; the remainder came from Tabacón, the Jasper SkyTram, Flyover Chicago, three Glacier-area assets, and the wildfire-year lap.

Two structural items compress GAAP earnings well below the EBITDA line. First, geography of taxable income: FY2025 foreign pre-tax income was $80.3M against a U.S. pre-tax loss of $(25.3)M, and $19.56M of the $19.57M current tax provision was foreign. The effective rate was 30.0%, and it is structural rather than cyclical — $152.8M of U.S. NOLs and $65.8M of Section 163(j) deferred interest deductions sit behind a $46.7M valuation allowance. Second, minority partners: of FY2025 consolidated net income of $36.3M, $13.6M (37.6%) was attributable to noncontrolling interests, leaving $22.7M and diluted EPS of $0.80. NCI equity was $72.7M at June 30, 2026, with $5.4M of cash distributions in FY2025. The 37.6% figure is a net income share after subsidiary-level D&A, interest and tax; the NCI share of EBITDA is materially lower, but it is not zero, and enterprise-value work must add NCI.

Cash flow: FY2025 operating cash flow from continuing operations was $86.2M against $75.0M of capex, leaving $11.1M of reported free cash flow — 2.5% of revenue. That understates cash generation because capex is predominantly discretionary growth. The Q2 2026 10-Q guides FY2026 capex to $103-114M with $70-80M of that growth, implying maintenance capex near $33.5M. Applying that split to FY2025 gives maintenance free cash flow of roughly $86.2M − $33.5M − $5.4M NCI distributions = $47.3M, or $1.73 per share on 27.263M shares — a 3.5% yield at $50.10. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast: it ignores working-capital timing and does not normalize the $4.2M insurance business-interruption gain. Cash interest paid fell to $9.7M from $45.8M in FY2024, the largest single benefit of GES deleveraging.

Balance sheet at June 30, 2026: cash $33.9M, total debt $191.8M (Pursuit revolver $128.3M at 5.6%, Brewster revolver $42.2M at 4.6%, Jasper term loan $11.4M at 6.5%, a new Forest Park construction loan $10.0M at 5.5%), finance leases $45.4M, total $237.3M. Net debt of $203.4M is 1.92x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA at the seasonal peak borrowing point, or 1.49x excluding finance leases. Covenants require total net leverage below 3.0x and fixed-charge coverage above 1.25x; the company was compliant at both December 31 and June 30. The maturity ladder is benign — $0.25M due 2026, $0.27M 2027, $11.4M 2028, nil 2029, and the revolver in 2030. Liquidity totalled $160.9M. Pro forma for the $75.0M Flyover proceeds and the ~$17.0M Eagle Wing purchase, net debt is roughly $145M, or about 1.4x.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.63 and the 0.81 current ratio are artifacts of the model rather than distress signals. An attractions operator collects cash before delivering the experience, carries no meaningful receivables, and is measured at a December seasonal trough where working capital is structurally negative. Equity-to-liabilities of 1.91 and debt-to-equity of 0.52 are the more informative solvency reads.

Seasonality is the dominant financial fact. Q3 2025 revenue of $241.0M was 53.3% of the year, and Q3 pre-tax income of $105.8M was 192% of full-year pre-tax income. Q1 and Q4 each lost more than $28M pre-tax. A single adverse event inside a ten-week window can erase a year, and did in 2024. H1 2026 operating cash outflow of $(11.0)M versus $(2.8)M a year earlier is seasonal, not a deterioration signal in isolation.

## Growth Analysis

Q2 2026 revenue rose 14.3% to $133.5M and H1 rose 20.0% to $185.1M. Beneath the headline: attractions revenue +4.2% on visitors −4.9%; hospitality +23.4% with same-store RevPAR +10.0% (ADR +8.1% to $244.97, occupancy +1.3 points to 76.4%); transportation −0.6%; and "other" of $4.05M against $0.563M, driven by roughly $3.5M of Flyover film-licensing revenue that will not recur after the July 31 divestiture. Q2 pre-tax income from continuing operations doubled to $21.7M and diluted EPS was $0.54 against $0.20. Note a basis conflict worth flagging: the consensus-tracking record shows Q2 actual EPS of $0.50 against a $0.54 estimate, a 7.5% shortfall, while the 10-Q reports $0.54 of diluted EPS attributable to Pursuit. The likely reconciling item is the $3.1M Flyover disposal-group impairment recorded in the quarter. I will not call the print a beat or a miss across mismatched bases.

Consensus estimates are rising: current-year EPS $1.68 (+5.9% over 30 days, +5.7% over 90 days), next-year $1.88 (+4.6%/+7.4%), current-quarter $3.09 (+9.4%). The revision direction is positive but the magnitude is nowhere near the +30% threshold that signals an earnings inflection, and only one contributor supplies estimates — thin coverage that makes the series fragile. FY2026 revenue guidance reportedly moved from $465M (February and May) to $485M on August 5 against consensus of $467M. That figure comes from a third-party aggregator whose transaction page for this company also carries a verified error (it still shows the Flyover sale at $78.4M rather than the issuer-confirmed $75.0M), and neither the issuer release nor the transcript was retrievable. Treat the raise as unconfirmed.

Even if accurate, the composition matters. The Flyover close slipped from "spring 2026" to July 31, keeping four extra months of Flyover revenue in continuing operations; Eagle Wing added a partial period from July 14; Q2 carried the non-recurring film-licensing revenue; and translation on 71% non-USD revenue is a live variable. Organic same-store RevPAR of +10.0% genuinely outran plan. The split cannot be apportioned from available disclosure, and the distinction changes materially how bullish the raise should be read.

Forward growth drivers are identifiable and modest. Tabacón is the counter-seasonality play, and the pro forma data supports the logic: derived Tabacón revenue is roughly $26.2M in FY2024 and about $15.6M in H1 2025 alone against $13.1M in the post-acquisition second half, meaning it earns more in the first half — precisely the offset a company with 53% of revenue in Q3 needs. Denali is a discrete 2027 catalyst: the park road has been closed since 2021 and Pursuit plans to relaunch the Denali Backcountry Adventure to coincide with the anticipated reopening, entirely on a third-party government timeline. Minority buy-ins ($1.6M for the last 43.6% of Flyover Iceland, $13.0M for the last 20% of Glacier Park) increase Pursuit's claim on existing cash flow without adding assets. Against that, FY2027 revenue laps out roughly eight months of Flyover, and the attractions line — the higher-margin, more scarcity-protected half — is shrinking in units.

## Valuation Assessment

Market capitalization is $1,366M on 27.263M shares (the profile's 27.32M share count gives $1,369M; the difference is immaterial). Pro forma net debt shortly after the July 31 close is approximately $145M, giving an enterprise value near $1,511M excluding noncontrolling interests and $1,584M including NCI at book.

- On FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $105.9M: **14.3x to 15.0x**.
- Holding the FY2025 margin of 23.4% against $485M guided revenue implies roughly $113.5M, giving **13.3x to 14.0x**. This assumes flat margin despite losing Flyover's film-licensing revenue and the reversal of the FX tailwind, so treat it as the optimistic end.
- On consensus EPS: **29.8x FY2026 ($1.68) and 26.6x FY2027 ($1.88)**, against a peer median P/E of 20.6x (HGV 27.8x, TNL 20.6x, CHH 14.5x; SABR and VAC are loss-making).
- Maintenance free cash flow yield: **~3.5%**. No dividend.
- PEG on FY2027: 26.6x against 12% consensus EPS growth ≈ **2.2**.

Three internal reference points discipline this more than the peer table. First, Pursuit sold Flyover at approximately 14.5x that unit's 2025 Adjusted EBITDA (implying ~$5.17M, about 4.9% of the total), which is roughly the multiple the market assigns Pursuit itself. The divestiture's merit is portfolio quality, not multiple arbitrage — Flyover generated $47.6M of FY2024 impairments, sat in leased urban real estate with no scarcity protection, had an Icelandic minority put option fail its performance condition, and was one of two goodwill critical audit matters. Second, the company's last observable repurchase price was $41.87 in May, with $37.68 in April and zero shares in June despite the Board raising the authorization by $50M on May 1 and $57.1M remaining. A pre-earnings blackout and competing calls on cash (Eagle Wing, $67-77M of remaining FY2026 capex, peak seasonal revolver draws) are mundane explanations, but $42 remains the last price at which management demonstrably wanted the stock. Third, the CEO bought at $33.32 and $33.39 in November 2025, with directors at $34.00 and $36.77.

Four analysts cover the stock, all rated buy, with targets from $55 to $70 (mean $60.75, median $59). Analyst targets are more useful as floors on cheap, inflecting names; at 29.8x forward earnings and a PEG above 2, PRSU does not qualify, and with only four covering analysts and one supplying estimates, the target dispersion carries limited information.

Valuation score 4.8. The multiple is not stretched to the point of structural risk, and the balance sheet has improved, but there is no identified mispricing at $50.10 — the scarcity premium is priced, and the company's own capital-allocation behavior implies a lower entry.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is a permitting argument and it is genuine. Intangibles include operating contracts and licenses with a 25.4-year weighted-average remaining life ($49.0M net), an in-place lease intangible with 30.8 years remaining, Canadian and Icelandic land leases running up to 46 years, a Parks Canada lease on the Jasper SkyTram with nearly 30 years left, and finance leases through 2067. New competing infrastructure inside Banff or Jasper is effectively unpermittable. The Jasper SkyTram purchase allocation makes the economics explicit: $13.5M of a $16.5M price went to the operating license and $2.3M to physical assets. Pursuit is buying permits.

Trajectory is more mixed than level. On the lodging side it is improving — Q2 same-store RevPAR +10.0% with occupancy up, which is not achievable in a demand-constrained market. On attractions it is flat to negative: visitors −4.9% in Q2 after +1.4% for FY2025, and pricing decelerating from +8.6% to +5.7%. The 10-K's own competition language ("location, uniqueness of facilities, service, quality, and price" against locally owned independents and chains) concedes that Pursuit competes on lodging price in Montana and Alaska while enjoying near-monopoly economics only on the marquee Canadian attractions. The Flyover exit raises average asset quality and simplifies the story. Pursuit is a price-setter inside its own gates and a price-taker on macro travel demand, with roughly half of revenue Canadian and unhedged.

## Risk Assessment

**Peak-season catastrophe concentration** is the dominant risk and it is live right now — the report is being written inside the ten weeks that decide the year. The 2024 Jasper wildfires swung FY2024 pre-tax income to a $(45.4)M loss. Insurance has partially worked: Jasper-related proceeds reached $28.8M through June 30, 2026, and only the Maligne Canyon Wilderness Kitchen was physically lost. The 10-K flags climate change increasing the frequency and intensity of adverse weather.

**Same-store volume decline of unresolved cause.** Weather is management's stated explanation and reverses; elasticity resistance above a ~$51 average ticket and softer cross-border leisure demand do not. Competing explanations (elasticity resistance above ~$50 tickets, cross-border travel softness, post-pandemic demand normalization) cannot be ruled out from the filings. The Q2 transcript, which would likely resolve the question, was not retrievable.

**Currency.** 71% of revenue is non-USD and unhedged for translation; cumulative translation losses inside equity were $48.2M at June 30. Separately, the Sky Lagoon finance lease remeasurement (±$6M per year) and a $32.6M intercompany loan to the Tabacón subsidiary create income-statement volatility that is non-cash but not non-reported.

**Goodwill.** Deloitte's FY2025 critical audit matter covered goodwill at Flyover and the Glacier Park Collection. Flyover is gone; Glacier Park remains, and the CAM designation signals the auditor viewed its DCF headroom as requiring especially subjective judgment. Goodwill was $127.1M at June 30 after the Flyover held-for-sale reclassification, with $20.2M of accumulated impairment already taken. The 10-K warns further impairments may be material.

**Public-land and policy dependency.** Concessions and permits from Parks Canada, the NPS, both Coast Guards and DOT; explicit risk-factor language on government shutdowns during peak season; approximately 2,500 seasonal employees, a significant portion of them foreign nationals requiring visas.

**Contingent and legal tails.** Guarantees of $38.4M at June 30, including roughly $20.0M of GES facility leases running through 2033 that Truelink Capital has agreed to indemnify — Pursuit carries a $0.5M fair-value liability and is the backstop obligor if Truelink does not pay. The July 2020 Ice Explorer accident (three fatalities) resolved on the regulatory side in 2023 for CAD 0.5M, with victim and family claims still being defended and reserves plus insurance believed sufficient. The Columbia Icefield Adventure remains a core asset.

**Governance verification gaps.** A December 10, 2025 8-K pairs Item 5.02 (officer departure) with Item 5.03 (bylaws amendment), and the FY2025 10-K added a risk factor citing "uncertainties arising from leadership changes." Neither that 8-K nor a May 20, 2026 Item 8.01 filing was readable. The 10-K also discloses an immaterial multi-year correction misallocating unrealized FX translation between Pursuit and NCI (FY2024 comprehensive income attributable to NCI restated from $(11.7)M to $(1.4)M) — presentational, in the same area that drives the largest non-operating swings, with ICFR still assessed as effective.

**Management quality reads better than the risk profile.** Insider activity is buying-only in the retrieved Form 4 set: the CEO at $33.32/$33.39, directors at $34.00 and $36.77, no open-market sales. The Flyover exit removed the impairment-generating unit; minority buy-ins were cheap; Tabacón at ~3.8x revenue was full price but coherent counter-seasonality. Repurchases of $32.7M in H1 2026 at $37.68 and $41.87, then nothing above that, is consistent with price discipline, though the Q1 average price is not disclosed and issuances from treasury confound back-solving. Crestview's $135M convertible preferred already mandatorily converted on December 31, 2024, which is why weighted shares rose from 21.4M to 28.2M — that dilution is in the reported figures, not pending.

### Options Market Signal

The chain carries 35 contracts across three expirations and should be read with heavy skepticism; the prior snapshot recorded an ATM IV of 954%, which is a data artifact. As captured, near-term IV of 72.4% against 36.2% one-year realized volatility with a backwardated term structure (72.4% at 14 days, 40.5% at 42 days, 35.8% at 133 days) and a ±12.2% implied move for August 21 is consistent with post-earnings repricing uncertainty in an illiquid book rather than a positioned view. Put/call skew of −28.2% is described as call-heavy and put/call open interest is effectively zero, so there is no hedging bid corroborating the bear case. Short interest of 5.9% of float and 2.9 days to cover is unremarkable. Note the price basis: $50.10 is the August 5 official close, which precedes the after-close Q2 release. A gap in either direction on the print is possible and is not reflected in the arithmetic above.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($65).** The Q2 visitor decline is weather, and Q3 recovers to flat-to-positive volume on top of 5-6% pricing, validating the $485M guidance and pushing FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA to $115M+. The $75M of Flyover proceeds plus operating cash flow takes net debt below 1.2x, funding a resumed buyback into the $57.1M authorization and further minority buy-ins that lift PRSU's claim on existing cash flow. Tabacón demonstrably flattens the seasonal curve, reducing the single-event discount the market applies. Partial release of the $46.7M U.S. valuation allowance — plausible now that Flyover's Las Vegas and Chicago loss centers are gone and the OBBBA restores EBITDA-based 163(j) treatment — unlocks part of the $218.6M attribute pool and cuts the 30% effective rate. At 15x on $118M of pro forma EBITDA less $130M of net debt and $73M of NCI, equity is $1,567M, or $57.50; at 16x, $65. The analyst high of $70 requires both a multiple expansion and a volume recovery.

**Bear case ($37, −26%).** The visitor decline is elasticity, not weather. A ~$51 average ticket has outrun the North American leisure consumer, pricing decelerates below 4%, and attractions revenue growth goes to zero even with strong lodging. Q3 delivers a wildfire, smoke event, or U.S. government shutdown inside the ten-week window, and pre-tax income for the full year approaches zero as it did in 2024. FY2027 revenue laps out eight months of Flyover and the film-licensing revenue, so reported growth turns negative without further acquisitions, which require revolver capacity at 5.6%. Glacier Park goodwill impairs. At 11.5x on $105M of EBITDA less $145M of net debt and $73M of NCI, equity is $989M, or $36.30 — near where the company itself was buying and where the CEO bought.

**What resolves it.** The Q3 print in early November. It carries 53% of the year's revenue, will show three months of same-store attraction visitors, and will be the first clean quarter of guidance on a post-Flyover base.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Reassessment point: the Q3 2026 report in early November 2026, with the August 5 Q2 release and any 8-K quantifying the guidance raise as an interim check.

**Base case $54 (+7.8% from $50.10).** Two methods converge. EV/EBITDA: 14.5x on $115M of pro forma FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA gives EV of $1,668M; less NCI at book $72.7M and pro forma net debt $145M leaves $1,450M of equity, or $53.20 on 27.263M shares. Earnings: 28x FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.88 gives $52.60. Rounding to $54 allows for modest buyback-driven share reduction against the $57.1M remaining authorization.

**Bull case $65 (+29.7%)** — 16x on $118M with a volume recovery and partial valuation-allowance release.
**Bear case $37 (−26.1%)** — 11.5x on $105M with elasticity confirmed or a peak-season event.

The distribution is roughly symmetric in magnitude with a modestly positive base case, which supports holding rather than adding.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q3 2026 same-store attraction visitors flat or positive year over year, confirming the weather explanation, with same-store effective ticket price still up 5% or more.
2. Resumed buyback executed above $48, or disclosure that repurchases continued through Q3 — evidence management sees value at current prices rather than only near $42.
3. A price near $42-43, which restores roughly 25% upside to the base case and matches the company's own last demonstrated buying zone.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Same-store attraction visitors down more than 4% for a third consecutive period with pricing decelerating below +4%, which would establish elasticity rather than weather.
2. A goodwill impairment at the Glacier Park Collection, or total net leverage above 2.5x on the covenant basis without a corresponding acquisition.
3. FY2026 revenue guidance withdrawn or cut, or a Q3 pre-tax result below roughly $85M (versus $105.8M in Q3 2025) absent an identified one-time cause.

## Conclusion

Pursuit owns assets that cannot be replicated and is proving it can price them — 8.6% same-store ticket increases in FY2025 with essentially flat volume, and 10.0% RevPAR growth in Q2 2026 with occupancy rising. The Flyover divestiture removed the impairment-generating, non-scarce part of the portfolio and cut pro forma net debt to roughly 1.4x. Insider purchases are buying-only, capital allocation has been coherent, and consensus estimates are drifting higher.

None of that makes the stock cheap at $50.10. Fourteen to fifteen times Adjusted EBITDA is a full multiple for an attractions operator, roughly the multiple at which Pursuit itself sold a business, and above where management last chose to repurchase shares. The scarcity thesis is priced; what is not resolved is whether the second consecutive quarter of declining attraction volume is weather or the first evidence that a $51 average ticket has met the limits of a slowing discretionary consumer. That question is decided over the next ten weeks, inside a quarter that carries 53% of annual revenue and has an established history of single-event loss. Holding an existing position through the Q3 print is reasonable given the improved balance sheet and the modest positive base case. New capital should wait for either the volume data or a price in the low $40s.
