## Executive Summary

Arcosa is mid-execution on a multi-year portfolio transformation from a diversified industrial company into a focused U.S. infrastructure and construction materials platform. The April 2026 barge divestiture ($450M) completes the strategic pivot, leaving two high-quality segments — Construction Products (aggregates/asphalt) and Engineered Structures (utility poles/wind towers) — both positioned against durable demand from the IIJA, grid modernization, and data center power buildout.

Operationally, the company is performing well: Q1 2026 delivered a 116% EPS beat, full-year EBITDA guidance was raised to $565M, utility structure margins hit a record 21.1%, and the wind-to-utility facility conversion is running ahead of schedule. The stock has responded, rallying 47% from its August 2025 lows to $124.62.

The reason this does not warrant a BUY is threefold: (1) consensus EPS estimates are declining — current year down 9.2% over 90 days, next year down 12.1% over 30 days — indicating that the Q1 beat may be a seasonal anomaly rather than a run-rate signal; (2) the wind tower business faces a structural demand cliff post-2027 with AMP tax credit termination and PTC phase-out under the OBBBA, which also threatens the 13.6% effective tax rate; and (3) at 27x trailing P/E and ~13.4x EV/EBITDA, the current price already reflects the transformation thesis without adequately discounting these headwinds. The stock trades below the analyst consensus low target ($130) and needs another leg of earnings confirmation to justify current multiples.

## Company Overview

Arcosa, Inc. (spun off from Trinity Industries in 2018) operates as a U.S. infrastructure-focused industrial company through two continuing segments:

**Construction Products ($1.31B revenue, ~49% of continuing ops):** Natural and recycled aggregates, specialty materials, and asphalt. The company holds 1.3 billion tons of proven/probable reserves concentrated in Texas (~35%) and New Jersey (~20% post-Stavola). Arcosa is the largest U.S. recycled aggregates producer.

**Engineered Structures ($1.19B revenue, ~45% of continuing ops):** Steel and concrete utility structures ($835M), wind towers ($355M), traffic/lighting poles, and telecom structures. Combined backlog of $1.06B provides 12-18 months of revenue visibility.

The Transportation Products segment (inland barges, $383M revenue) was divested April 1, 2026 for $450M to Wynnchurch Capital.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

Revenue grew from $2.24B (2022) to $2.88B (2025), a 29% increase driven primarily by the $1.2B Stavola acquisition (closed October 2024) and volume growth in utility structures. Operating income surged 73% to $341.9M in 2025 (11.9% margin, up from 7.7% in 2024). Net income more than doubled to $208.4M ($4.26/share).

The effective tax rate collapsed to 13.6% in 2025 from 27.9% in 2024, benefiting from AMP tax credits on wind towers and statutory depletion on aggregates reserves. This represents approximately $30-40M of incremental net income that faces reversal post-2027.

For 2026, management guides $2.65B revenue (continuing operations) and $565M Adjusted EBITDA, implying ~21.3% EBITDA margins — a meaningful step-up from the ~20% achieved in 2025 on a consolidated basis.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt sits at ~$1.54B following a $156.5M prepayment, with $214.6M cash and $700M of undrawn revolver capacity. Net debt is approximately $1.33B. The D/E ratio of 0.89x is elevated relative to pre-Stavola levels but manageable given $341M operating cash flow and $450M of incoming barge sale proceeds. The 2025 Refinancing Term Loan ($698M, SOFR + 2.00%, maturing 2031) introduces variable rate exposure in a rising-rate environment.

**Cash Flow:**

Operating cash flow was $341M in 2025 (down from $502M in 2024, which benefited from working capital timing). Capex ran $166M, yielding $175M free cash flow. The company allocates virtually nothing to dividends ($10M/year) or buybacks, directing all excess cash to debt reduction and acquisitions.

**Q1 2026 Beat Context:**

EPS of $0.81 vs. $0.37 consensus (116% beat) was driven by the timing of the barge divestiture gain and seasonal utility structure strength. The magnitude of the beat relative to subsequent estimate revisions (-10-13% downward across all periods) suggests analysts view the beat as non-recurring rather than indicative of higher run-rate earnings.

## Growth Analysis

**Secular Tailwinds (Genuine):**
- Grid modernization: U.S. utilities spending to accommodate data center demand growth, EV charging, and reliability requirements. Utility structures revenue grew to $835M with record 21.1% margins.
- IIJA highway spending: TxDOT lettings at $10.9B for FY2026; NJ DOT budget $3.6B (+14.8% YoY). These programs underpin aggregates demand in Arcosa's core markets.
- Construction materials scarcity: Permitting barriers for new quarries create local monopolies. Arcosa's 1.3B tons of reserves in growth markets (Texas, NY-NJ MSA) are irreplaceable.

**Growth Concerns:**
- Wind tower demand post-2027 is uncertain. The $627.8M backlog provides visibility (42% to be recognized in 2026, 53% in 2027), but new order flow beyond existing backlog faces OBBBA headwinds.
- Residential construction weakness: Management guides flat-to-down residential aggregates volumes in 2026 due to mortgage rate affordability.
- Estimate revisions are uniformly negative: current year EPS down 9.2% (90 days), next year down 3.2% (90 days). This is a material contra-indicator.

**Capacity Conversion as Growth Lever:**

The conversion of idle wind tower facilities to utility structure production is a capital-efficient growth strategy. The Illinois conversion came online ahead of schedule, and management expects additional capacity by H2 2026. This effectively pivots manufacturing capacity from a policy-threatened market (wind) to a secularly growing one (utility/grid) using existing assets and workforce.

## Valuation Assessment

At $124.62, Arcosa trades at:
- 27.0x trailing P/E ($4.24 LTM EPS)
- 28.9x forward P/E ($4.31 current year consensus)
- ~13.4x EV/EBITDA ($565M 2026 guidance, $7.6B EV)
- 24.7x FY2027 P/E ($5.05 consensus)

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 24.3x. Arcosa's 27x is a modest premium, justified by its higher-quality asset mix (aggregates reserves, utility structure demand) relative to pure construction/engineering services firms like FLR (20.3x) or PRIM (24.3x). However, GVA trades at 34.5x, suggesting Arcosa's premium is not extreme within the peer set.

**The aggregates re-rating thesis:** Pure-play aggregates companies (MLM, VMC) trade at 15-18x EV/EBITDA. As Arcosa simplifies toward materials/utility structures and away from cyclical businesses, a re-rating toward 14-15x EBITDA is plausible — but at 13.4x with negative estimate revisions, the market appears to be pricing this fairly rather than offering a discount.

**Why the market is not wrong at $124.62:** The stock already appreciated 47% from August 2025 lows on the transformation thesis. Analyst consensus ($141 mean target) implies 13% upside, but with estimates falling, the basis for that target is weakening. The all-analyst-BUY consensus (3 buys, zero holds/sells in current month) typically represents a fully-recognized thesis rather than an undiscovered opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

**Construction Products:** Arcosa competes with Vulcan (VMC), Martin Marietta (MLM), and thousands of local operators. Its competitive position is strong in Texas and the NY-NJ MSA (post-Stavola), where quarry proximity to demand and permitting barriers create effective local oligopolies. The 1.3B tons of reserves represent decades of production at current rates.

**Engineered Structures — Utility:** Arcosa is one of a handful of domestic manufacturers capable of producing large-diameter utility poles. Customer relationships with major utilities and long-lead-time contracts create switching costs. The wind-to-utility conversion adds capacity in a supply-constrained market.

**Engineered Structures — Wind:** Arcosa is a leading North American wind tower manufacturer, but this market faces structural headwinds from OBBBA policy changes. The conversion strategy partially mitigates this but cannot fully replace a $355M revenue line if demand evaporates post-2027.

**Competitive position trajectory:** Improving. The portfolio simplification concentrates Arcosa in businesses with stronger moats (quarries, utility structures) while exiting competitive markets (barges, steel components). The Stavola acquisition adds irreplaceable NY-NJ quarry positions.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Wind Tower Demand Cliff (HIGH):** AMP tax credit termination for towers sold after 2027 + PTC phase-out under OBBBA. $355M of 2025 revenue and a significant tax benefit ($30-40M) at risk. Backlog provides bridge through 2027; post-2027 visibility is poor. Potential 5-8 percentage point increase in effective tax rate.

**2. Negative Estimate Revisions (MEDIUM-HIGH):** All estimate periods declining 8-13% over 30-90 days. The Q1 2026 beat has not translated into upward revisions, suggesting analysts view it as non-recurring. This is the single most concerning fundamental signal.

**3. Leverage Digestion (MEDIUM):** $1.5B debt from Stavola acquisition. Interest expense tripled to $109M in 2025. Variable rate exposure (SOFR + 2.00%) creates margin pressure if rates remain elevated or increase further. Barge proceeds provide offset but don't eliminate the issue.

**4. GE Vernova Concentration (MEDIUM):** 12.2% of revenue from a single customer concentrated in the wind tower segment. OBBBA changes could reduce their order volumes.

**5. Tariff/Trade Risk (MEDIUM):** 10% steel tariff on Mexican-produced utility structures; potential new tariffs on wind tower components; USMCA review July 2026 affecting ~1,225 Mexican employees.

**6. Cybersecurity Incident (LOW-MEDIUM):** December 2025 8-K filing indicating material incident. Insufficient public detail to assess impact.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($140-152, +12% to +22%):**
- Utility structure demand accelerates beyond current guidance as data center power requirements force grid investment
- Wind-to-utility conversion delivers full incremental margin contribution by H2 2026, driving positive estimate revisions
- Barge sale proceeds drive leverage below 2.0x net debt/EBITDA, triggering re-rating toward pure-play aggregates multiples (15-16x EBITDA)
- Wind tower demand sustains post-2027 as growing electricity needs override subsidy dependency
- Aggregates pricing power in Texas/NJ delivers margin expansion above 15%

**Bear Case ($95-105, -16% to -24%):**
- Estimate revisions continue declining as Q1 beat proves seasonal; full-year EPS misses $4.31 consensus
- Wind tower orders fall sharply post-backlog, revealing a $300M+ revenue hole without replacement
- Effective tax rate normalizes to 20-22% post-AMP credit expiration, reducing net income $25-40M
- Rising rates compress utility and residential construction demand simultaneously
- Tariff escalation on Mexican-produced structures erodes margins without contractual offset
- GE Vernova reduces wind tower orders as policy incentives expire

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS estimate revisions (30-day), indicating the earnings trajectory has stabilized and is inflecting upward
- Stock pullback to $108-112 (~13x forward EPS on current estimates), providing sufficient margin of safety against wind/tax headwinds
- Concrete evidence of post-2027 wind tower demand (new orders, customer commitments, or policy reversal)

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
- Full-year 2026 EPS guidance cut below $4.00, indicating organic growth insufficient to offset barge divestiture
- Utility structure backlog declining >15% in consecutive quarters (signal of demand saturation)
- Net leverage exceeding 3.5x without clear de-leveraging path

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August 2026). This will provide the first clean quarter of two-segment results post-barge sale and reveal whether the negative estimate revision trend is stabilizing.

## Conclusion

Arcosa is a well-managed company executing a sound strategic transformation. The construction materials and utility structure businesses have genuine secular tailwinds, the balance sheet is being actively managed, and the wind-to-utility conversion demonstrates management's ability to anticipate and adapt to policy shifts. The operational story deserves a 7+ score on competitive positioning and management quality.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY at $124.62 is specific and falsifiable: **consensus EPS estimates are declining across all time horizons (10-13% over 30-90 days), and the stock trades at 27x earnings with a post-2027 structural headwind (wind tower tax credits, representing $30-40M of annual net income) that the current multiple does not adequately discount.** The all-analyst-BUY consensus and 47% appreciation from the August 2025 low indicate the transformation thesis is already recognized and priced. For a BUY, I need either (a) stabilizing/rising estimates confirming the Q1 beat is sustainable, or (b) a price correction to $108-112 that provides asymmetric upside relative to the identifiable risks.
