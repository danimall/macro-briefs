**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Atmos Energy is the cleanest expression of the regulated gas-utility model in the U.S. equity market: a pure-play, monopoly-franchise distribution and intrastate-pipeline business with essentially no unregulated exposure, concentrated in Texas, running a capital-recovery construct that reimburses roughly 95% of capex within six months. The result is a rare utility that both grows earnings at a double-digit clip (net income $774M FY22 → $1,199M FY25, ~+15% YoY; diluted EPS $5.60 → $7.46) and earns close to its authorized ~9.4–9.9% ROEs because it minimizes regulatory lag. Management raised FY2026 guidance to $8.40–$8.50 (from $8.15–$8.35) and lifted the dividend 14.9% to $4.00, its 40th-plus consecutive annual increase. A new Texas infrastructure rule (7.7102) is targeted to add ~$155–165M pretax to FY2026, the single largest new earnings catalyst.

The quality is not in dispute; the problem is price and structure. At $179, ATO trades at ~21x the FY26 EPS midpoint, a premium to the ~16x gas-utility peer median that leaves little room for regulatory disappointment. The ~$26B FY26–FY30 capex program (>80% safety/reliability) that drives the growth also drives structurally negative free cash flow (–$1.5B in FY25 on $3.6B capex against $2.05B operating cash flow), which forces continuous debt and equity issuance. Share count rose from 137.8M to 158.9M basic over four years, so net-income growth overstates per-share value creation, and a higher-for-longer rate environment raises the marginal cost of the external-financing model. Moody's downgraded the senior rating to A2 in April 2025, citing this externally-financed capex intensity.

We rate ATO a HOLD (growth_hold). The company is worth owning for a durable ~7% long-term EPS CAGR (running well ahead of that near-term) plus a ~2.2% dividend, but the current entry point offers balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable risk/reward. Consensus targets (mean $189, high $206, low $170) imply modest upside, and the stock sits within ~7% of its 52-week high. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $160s or on evidence that per-share growth is durably outpacing dilution.

## Company Overview

Atmos Energy is the largest pure-play regulated natural gas utility in the U.S., headquartered in Dallas. It operates two segments:

- **Distribution (~62% of net income):** Regulated gas distribution to ~3.4 million customers across eight states (TX, KY, TN, VA, LA, MS, CO, KS) through six divisions. Mid-Tex (the Dallas/Fort Worth Metroplex, ~1.83M meters) is the crown jewel, generating $532.6M of the segment's $963.4M operating income in FY2025.
- **Pipeline & Storage (~38% of net income):** Primarily Atmos Pipeline–Texas (APT), one of the largest intrastate pipelines in Texas, with five underground storage facilities. Over 80% of segment revenue comes from APT, much of it affiliate transportation serving Mid-Tex, regulated by the Texas Railroad Commission via the GRIP mechanism.

The competitive foundation is a set of regulated monopoly franchises (1,010 franchises, 5–35 year terms) over 81,000+ miles of pipe, combined with an unusually favorable regulatory construct. Atmos has assembled rate mechanisms (GRIP, formula rates, RRM, infrastructure riders) that let it recover ~95% of capex within six months and substantially all within twelve. Weather-normalization mechanisms protect ~97% of residential/commercial revenue from weather variance, and purchased-gas-cost pass-throughs insulate operating income from commodity price swings. Roughly 75% of operations sit in Texas, a growth-friendly and historically constructive regulatory jurisdiction. This footprint is arguably the best among U.S. gas utilities.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth trajectory.** Net income compounded from $774M (FY22) to $886M (FY23), $1,043M (FY24), and $1,199M (FY25). Diluted EPS moved $5.60 → $6.10 → $6.83 → $7.46. FY2026 is tracking higher: H1 FY26 EPS was $5.92, net income $984.9M, implying ~13% full-year growth against the raised guidance. This is genuine double-digit earnings growth in a sector where mid-single-digit is the norm.

**Margins and returns.** Operating margin runs ~33%, net margin ~25.5%. ROE is ~8.8%, ROA ~4.2%. Authorized ROEs cluster at 9.4%–9.9% across jurisdictions (11.45% at APT-Texas), on equity-heavy authorized structures (~55–61% equity). Because regulatory lag is minimal, Atmos earns close to authorized returns, a structural advantage over utilities that suffer wider gaps between authorized and realized returns.

**Capital intensity is the defining feature.** FY2025 capex was $3.6B (87% safety/reliability) against $2.05B operating cash flow, producing –$1.5B free cash flow. Management plans ~$26B of capex over FY2026–FY2030 (>80% safety/reliability), with FY2026 capex guided to ~$4.2B. Every recoverable dollar invested grows the rate base on which authorized returns are earned. This negative FCF is the deliberate mechanism of the model, not a distress signal, a distinction that a naive screen (flagging a –32% FCF margin) gets wrong. The genuine caveat is that the flywheel depends entirely on regulators continuing to reimburse capital quickly.

**Balance sheet.** Equity capitalization was 60.3% at FYE25 (60.9% at Q2 FY26), deliberately low leverage to preserve capital-market access. Total debt is ~$8.9B against ~$13.6B equity, or ~40% debt/cap; the screening tool's reported D/E of 0.04 and Altman Z of 17.07 are artifacts of misclassified debt fields and should be disregarded. Credit ratings are A-/A2 (S&P/Moody's), stable outlook. Current ratio of 0.77 is normal for a utility, not a liquidity concern given ~$4.1–4.9B of committed liquidity (cash, undrawn $3.1B revolvers, $1.5B CP program, plus equity forward-sale availability).

**Dilution is the quiet cost.** Atmos funds its deficit with roughly a 60/40 debt/equity mix, issuing shares through an ATM program and forward-sale agreements. EPS still grows double-digits, but per-share growth lags net-income growth because investors fund ~40% of the capex deficit. About $1.6B of forward-sale proceeds remain unsettled, a known future dilution overhang.

**Earnings quality/visibility.** The beat/miss record is tight and clean: the last four quarters ran +1.1%, –0.4%, +5.7%, +0.3%, a well-controlled, low-variance delivery profile consistent with a regulated model. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: FY26 consensus +1.8% over 90 days to $8.44, FY27 +1.8% to $9.02, next quarter +5.8%. This is steady positive momentum, not the >30% swing that would flag a mispriced cyclical.

## Growth Analysis

Atmos's growth is unusually visible and mechanical. Three forces drive it:

1. **Rate-base growth via recoverable capex.** The $26B five-year plan, >80% directed at safety/reliability that regulators recover with near-zero lag, is the primary engine. FY2025 rate outcomes added $333.6M to annual operating income; a further $231.1M of requests were in progress at FYE25, including a $165M Mid-Tex formula-rate case ($138.5M already implemented 10/1/2025). Mississippi issued a 9.40% ROE order 11/4/2025. The rate-case pipeline is full and productive.

2. **Texas Rule 7.7102.** New Texas infrastructure-spending legislation is the single largest new catalyst and a direct dividend of the Texas concentration. This is a management-stated figure sourced from Q2 FY26 commentary; it is a step-function benefit rather than a recurring rate.

3. **Customer and volume growth.** Population and industrial growth in Texas support organic meter growth and APT through-system revenue, cited among the Q2 FY26 drivers.

The long-term algorithm management targets is ~6–8% EPS growth; recent delivery of ~13–15% reflects the additive effect of rate outcomes plus 7.7102. The durability question is per-share: net-income growth will keep outpacing EPS growth as long as the capex deficit is funded ~40% with equity. The growth is real and low-risk operationally, but the dilution drag and the eventual normalization from the 7.7102 boost mean the sustainable through-cycle rate is closer to the high-single-digits than the low-double-digits.

## Valuation Assessment

At $179 against an FY26 EPS midpoint of ~$8.45, ATO trades at ~21.2x forward earnings. The gas-utility peer set trades at a median ~16.1x P/E (UGI 12.0, NFG 10.3, NJR 16.1, OGS 17.0, with SWX an outlier at 26.7). Atmos commands a ~30% premium to the median.

That premium is defensible on quality and growth: the best regulatory-recovery mechanisms in the group, near-zero lag, a full rate pipeline, and a genuine double-digit near-term growth rate versus mid-single-digit peers. On a growth-adjusted basis, ~21x forward against ~7% durable EPS growth (higher near-term) yields a PEG in the 1.5–2.7 range depending on the growth assumption used — full, not cheap. The dividend yield of ~2.2% sits below the peer median of ~3.4%, providing less absolute yield support than lower-multiple peers.

ATO is not cheap on any measure, so there is no value-trap risk from a compressed multiple; the risk is the opposite, a premium multiple with little margin for error. Estimate revisions are positive but modest, well short of the threshold that would justify treating analyst targets as stale floors. Consensus targets (median $189.5) imply ~5–15% upside to base, with the low target only ~5% below spot — a symmetric-to-slightly-favorable band that reflects a fairly-priced quality name rather than a mispricing.

A reasonable base case: applying a 20.5x multiple to a normalized ~$9.00 FY27 EPS yields ~$185, roughly in line with the current price and mean target. The multiple is the swing factor: a higher-for-longer rate path pressures utility multiples broadly, and any Texas regulatory disappointment would compress ATO's premium disproportionately. We score valuation below neutral because the current price fully reflects the quality with no identifiable mispricing and below-median yield support.

## Competitive Landscape

The distribution business faces no direct competition within its franchise territories; it is a regulated monopoly. Indirect competition comes from electricity for heating, cooking, and water heating, where natural gas retains a meaningful cost advantage. Six of eight operating states have passed laws barring local gas bans, which materially blunts the near-to-medium-term electrification threat, though decarbonization remains a long-term structural overhang, particularly outside Texas.

Within the gas-utility peer group, Atmos stands out on three axes: (a) the highest-quality regulatory recovery mechanisms (near-zero lag), (b) a premium growth profile, and (c) Texas exposure that is a net positive given population/industrial growth and constructive regulation. The one area of eroding competitive position is Pipeline & Storage, where management notes that "several new pipelines have been completed, which has increased the level of competition in this segment." That is a modest negative confined to ~38% of net income and not yet material to results. On balance, competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in distribution and slightly deteriorating in pipeline, netting to a solidly above-average but not accelerating position.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Regulatory-lag dependency (the central thesis risk).** The entire case rests on mechanisms that permit near-real-time capex recovery. Management explicitly warns lag "could significantly increase if the regulatory authorities modify or terminate these rate mechanisms." Any adverse change, especially in Texas, would impair the model and the premium multiple simultaneously.

2. **Texas concentration (~75% of operations).** Economic, weather, and regulatory outcomes in Texas dominate results. A single unfavorable Texas Railroad Commission or legislative shift is a genuine single-point-of-failure. The same concentration that delivered the 7.7102 tailwind cuts both ways.

3. **Capital-market access dependency.** With structurally negative FCF and a $26B forward plan, Atmos must continuously tap debt and equity markets. A downgrade or dislocation raises costs or throttles growth. The April 2025 Moody's downgrade is a mild yellow flag on this axis, and a higher-for-longer rate path raises the marginal cost of every dollar of the plan.

4. **Dilution overhang.** The ~$1.6B of unsettled forward-sale proceeds and continued ATM issuance mean per-share growth will keep lagging net-income growth. This is a certain, ongoing cost rather than a tail risk.

5. **Decarbonization / electrification.** A long-term existential question for a gas-only utility, partially mitigated by anti-gas-ban laws in six of eight states.

6. **Safety / pipeline integrity.** Operating hazards near populated areas could cause large uninsured liabilities and penalties, the reason 85–87% of capex is safety-directed.

Most of these risks are structural and at least partly priced. The idiosyncratic, market-moving risks not already fully reflected are (a) a discrete adverse Texas regulatory outcome and (b) rate-driven multiple compression on the premium — neither imminent, but both carrying asymmetric downside given the full valuation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (→ ~$206, +15%).** Texas regulation stays constructive, the capex plan converts cleanly to rate base with continued near-zero lag, 7.7102 delivers at the top of its range, and the rate-case pipeline keeps adding ~$300M+ annually to operating income. EPS compounds toward $9.00+ in FY27 and the market rewards the quality with a sustained ~22–23x multiple. The dividend Aristocrat status and defensive characteristics attract a bid if rates fall, supporting multiple expansion.

**Bear Case (→ ~$155–160, –12% to –14%).** A higher-for-longer rate path compresses the utility complex and Atmos's premium disproportionately; the externally-financed model faces rising costs and a second downgrade notch; equity issuance accelerates to fund the deficit, deepening the dilution drag; or a single adverse Texas regulatory/legislative outcome (or a modification to the GRIP/formula-rate mechanisms) forces the market to re-rate the "near-zero lag" premium toward the peer median ~16x, which alone implies ~$135–145.

**Base Case (→ ~$185, +3%).** The rate pipeline and 7.7102 deliver, EPS reaches the ~$8.45 FY26 midpoint and ~$9.00 FY27, and the multiple holds around 20.5x. The stock delivers a total return roughly equal to EPS growth net of dilution plus the ~2.2% dividend — a high-single-digit total return, most of it earnings-driven rather than re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the fiscal-Q3 FY26 print (early August 2026), which will test the 7.7102 contribution and the FY26 guidance range, followed by the FY26 full-year results and FY27 guidance in November 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward the low-to-mid $160s (≤~18x forward), restoring a margin of safety and pushing implied upside above ~15%.
- Evidence that per-share growth is durably outpacing dilution — i.e., a slowdown in ATM/forward-share issuance while EPS guidance holds, or settlement of the ~$1.6B forward-sale overhang without a fresh raise.
- A constructive resolution/extension of Texas rate mechanisms (GRIP/formula rates) that de-risks the "near-zero lag" premium for the full capex cycle.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any modification, termination, or adverse ruling on the Texas cost-recovery mechanisms that materially widens regulatory lag.
- A second Moody's/S&P downgrade notch, or a step-up in equity issuance that pushes annual dilution above ~4–5% and caps EPS growth below mid-single-digits.
- FY27 guidance materially below the ~$9.00 consensus, or estimate revisions turning negative across all windows.

## Conclusion

Atmos Energy is the best-in-class regulated gas utility, and the fundamentals support the growth-quality label: a full and productive rate pipeline, a fresh Texas legislative tailwind, double-digit near-term EPS growth, a 40-plus-year dividend-increase streak, and a fortress-grade balance sheet by utility standards. None of the recent price decline from the April high reflects fundamental deterioration — estimates are rising modestly and delivery is clean.

The constraint is that all of this is priced. At a premium to the peer median with below-median yield support, structurally negative FCF requiring perpetual dilution, and a higher-for-longer rate path that raises the cost of its financing model, the risk/reward near a 52-week high is balanced at best. Consensus targets frame ~5–15% upside against ~5–14% downside, and the multiple, not the earnings, is the swing factor. We rate ATO HOLD, and would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $160s or on confirmation that per-share value creation is outrunning the dilution drag.
