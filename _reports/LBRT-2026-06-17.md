**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Liberty Energy is one of the largest North American hydraulic-fracturing and integrated-completions providers, operating roughly 40 active fleets across U.S. shale basins, Canada, and Australia. The investment case has bifurcated. The legacy frac business is in a clear cyclical down-leg: FY2025 revenue fell 7% to $4.01B, operating income collapsed from $389M (2024) to $73M (2025), and Adjusted EBITDA declined 31% to $634.1M. Free cash flow was just $14.1M for the full year, and reported net income of $147.9M was flattered by a $162.6M non-cash gain on equity investments (Oklo, Tamboran). Strip those out and core profitability was thin.

Overlaid on this is a major strategic pivot into distributed natural-gas power generation via Liberty Power Innovations (LPI), aimed at data-center, AI hyperscaler, and industrial customers. This narrative drove the stock's ~60% six-month rally into mid-2026 and allowed the company to place $1.295B of zero-coupon convertible notes at a 30-32% premium. The power pipeline (3 GW targeted by 2029, a ~1 GW Vantage development agreement, a 330 MW Texas reservation) is explicitly disclosed as not yet under binding contract. It is optionality, not backlog.

The stock has since pulled back ~21% from its 52-week/intraday high of $34.48 to about $27.17-27.35, which has compressed the narrative premium and left a roughly symmetric setup against the visible scenarios. The balance sheet is more conservative than headline debt figures suggest: net leverage is approximately 0.9x on instruments carrying zero cash interest. Forward estimate revisions are sharply positive (next-year EPS +69% over 30 days, +304% over 90 days), the company has beaten consensus in three of the last four quarters, there are no sell ratings, and the current price sits ~20% below the consensus mean target of $33.92. With genuine deterioration in core cash generation balanced against constructive forward signals and an unproven but real power option, the defensible call is HOLD.

## Company Overview

Liberty grew from a single frac fleet in 2011 to ~40 active fleets as of Q1 2026. The completions platform is vertically integrated: frac, wireline, proppant delivery, two owned Permian sand mines, CNG/natural-gas fueling, field gas processing, and proprietary data analytics (FracTrends). Its differentiation rests on next-generation equipment: digiFleets (digiFrac/digiPrime electric and hybrid pumps with roughly 25% lower CO2e than Tier IV dual-fuel), Liberty Quiet Fleet, dual-fuel DGB fleets, and wet-sand handling. This aligns with E&P customers pushing efficiency (proppant per well up roughly 4x since 2014 to ~25M lbs) and emissions reduction.

The defining strategic development is the LPI pivot into behind-the-meter and off-grid natural-gas power. Its platform includes Forte (modular generation), Tempo (power-quality management for cyclical AI loads), and Chorus (grid-co-located optimization). In March 2025 Liberty acquired IMG Energy Solutions (~$19.6M) for engineering and utility-interconnection capabilities. The company manages itself as a single reportable segment, so power-business financials are not yet disclosed separately, which limits investor visibility into the economics of the new venture.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Revenue moved from $4.15B (2022) to a $4.75B peak (2023), then declined to $4.32B (2024) and $4.01B (2025). The decline was pricing-driven, with activity holding up. Operating income fell from $760M (2023) to $389M (2024) to $73M (2025), leaving an operating margin near 1.8%. The quality of FY2025 earnings is weak: of $147.9M net income, $162.6M was a non-cash mark on equity investments, meaning operating earnings excluding marks were negative-to-marginal.

**Q1 2026 trends.** Revenue rose 4% YoY to $1.02B on higher activity and utilization, but cost of services rose 11%, compressing Adjusted EBITDA to $125.9M from $168.2M a year earlier. Management cites a tightening frac supply-demand balance (equipment cannibalization and attrition reducing effective capacity) and rising oil prices as constructive for 2026. The reported pricing recovery thesis is still prospective.

**Cash flow and capital intensity.** This is a capital-heavy business. FY2025 operating cash flow of $609.6M was nearly fully absorbed by $595.5M of capex, leaving FCF of just $14.1M (0.4% margin), down from $829M of OCF in 2024. Q1 2026 OCF dropped to $8.4M on a large working-capital build (receivables/unbilled up ~$125M) against $157M of capex, so FCF was sharply negative in the quarter, before any power-project capex ramp. The thin cash generation is the most important negative in the file.

**Balance sheet — netting cash matters.** Headline figures show long-term debt rising to ~$1.27B in Q1 2026 from ~$336M at YE2025. The relevant figure is net leverage. Cash jumped to $699M after the financings, leaving net debt of roughly $570M against ~$634M of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, approximately 0.9x net leverage. The new debt is $1.295B of 0.00% convertible senior notes ($770M due 2031 at a $34.50 conversion price / 32.5% premium; $525M due 2032 at $37.44 / 30% premium), which carry no cash interest. Liberty also spent $186.5M on capped calls (cap prices $65.10 and $72.00) to limit dilution. The revolver was paid to zero. Altman Z sits at 2.91 (grey zone), current ratio 1.22, covenant leverage cap 3.5x with senior cap 2.5x, currently in compliance. The genuine risk is not the current debt load but the trajectory: the credit agreement was amended to permit up to $600M of new bridge indebtedness by 6/30/2026 and to double the convertible basket from $300M to $600M, signaling further large raises ahead to fund the power buildout.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.09 (~$15M/quarter, ~1.28% yield). The $750M buyback authorization (expiring 7/31/2026) had $270M remaining, but repurchases were halted in Q1 2026 as capital was redirected to power. This is a deliberate regime change away from shareholder returns toward growth capex.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is genuinely two-sided, which is why this dimension does not earn a high score despite strongly positive forward revisions.

**Forward estimate revisions are sharply up over the relevant horizon.** Next-year EPS consensus was revised to $0.60 from $0.35 over 30 days (+69%) and from $(0.29) over 90 days (+304%). Current-year EPS is $0.27, up 165% over 90 days, even after a -14.6% 30-day trim. The 30-day current-year cut reflects near-term frac margin softness, while the forward-year line — the more relevant figure for a 12-month view — points to a meaningful recovery. The earnings surprise record reinforces upward bias: Q1 2026 actual EPS of $0.14 beat the $(0.13) estimate (+205.6%), Q4 2025 beat by 150.8%, and Q3 2025 beat by over 1,600%. Consensus has been systematically too low at the trough.

**Frac cyclical recovery is plausible but not yet in the numbers.** The supply-side dynamic of cannibalization and attrition shrinking effective frac capacity while next-gen replacement lags could tighten pricing if E&P activity firms. WTI averaging near $99.85/bbl in early April 2026 (per the Q1 MD&A) and elevated oil prices through mid-2026 are supportive. The constructive scenario depends on durable activity, which is uncertain in a stagflationary macro with two-sided oil risk.

**Power is the large but unproven optionality.** ERCOT projects Texas grid demand could quadruple by 2032, and the AI/data-center power demand wave is real and broad-based. But every disclosed Liberty power "deal" is explicitly non-binding: the 3 GW 2029 target is not under contract, the ~1 GW Vantage relationship is a development agreement rather than a signed end-user power contract, and the 330 MW Texas item is a preliminary reservation. Management states it "has not directly engaged in the distributed power solutions business before." This should be treated as embedded optionality, not committed revenue. If the pipeline converts on acceptable economics, the growth ceiling rises materially; if it slips, Liberty carries a larger capital base against a softening frac cycle.

## Valuation Assessment

LBRT does not screen as cheap on any near-term metric. Trailing and forward P/E both sit near 30x on $0.89 EPS. On next-year consensus EPS of $0.60, the forward multiple is roughly 45x. The peer set in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services trades at a median P/E of 19.6x (WHD 55.0, OII 11.8, TDW 12.4, AROC 19.6, INVX 38.2), so LBRT carries a premium to the median that is justified only by the power optionality the market is paying for, not by current frac earnings.

The multiple already embeds substantial enthusiasm for the data-center power theme. The valuation argument is one of balance rather than mispricing: the ~21% drawdown has removed a meaningful slice of the narrative premium, and the current price sits ~20% below the consensus mean target (median $35.00, range $21.00-$40.00) with zero sell ratings among 13 analysts (3 strong buy, 5 buy, 5 hold). Against this, the convertible conversion prices ($34.50 and $37.44) and capped-call caps ($65.10/$72.00) frame the upside the structured-financing buyers were willing to underwrite.

Cycle-adjusted, the picture is genuinely ambiguous. Forward estimates are inflecting up hard, which argues against penalizing the trailing multiple in isolation. But even on the recovered forward-year number the stock is not cheap, and the power option that justifies the premium remains pre-revenue. The valuation score reflects a fairly priced stock with two-sided risk, not an asymmetric opportunity.

## Competitive Landscape

**Frac.** Liberty is a top-tier North American pressure pumper alongside Halliburton, SLB, ProPetro, and Patterson-UTI/NexTier. The industry has endured years of pricing pressure and consolidation. Liberty's edge is its next-generation electric/low-emission fleet (digiFleets), integrated supply chain, and owned sand. The supply-tightening dynamic of older equipment attriting faster than next-gen replacement arrives favors the players with modern, efficient fleets and could support pricing recovery, modestly improving Liberty's competitive trajectory within the segment.

**Power.** This is a crowded, fast-evolving market. Liberty competes against incumbent IPPs, utilities, turbine OEMs, and other behind-the-meter providers benefiting from the same AI demand wave (GE Vernova, Caterpillar, Bloom Energy, pure-play data-center power developers). Its advantages are existing natural-gas fueling logistics, mobile-power expertise transferred from frac, and the Caterpillar supplier relationship. Its disadvantage is the absence of any operating track record in long-duration power contracts and competitors with deeper capital and established customer relationships. The Vantage arrangement is a development agreement in which Liberty is the prospective power-solutions provider; it does not own Vantage's data centers, and the end-customer contracts are not signed. The competitive trajectory in power is improving on paper but unproven in execution.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Power execution (the central risk).** Management is entering a business it has not operated before, with a largely non-binding pipeline, long sales cycles, large upfront non-refundable equipment deposits, supplier concentration for turbines/gensets, and tariff exposure. Treat power revenue as optionality.

2. **Financing and dilution.** The strategy depends on raising significant further capital: the $600M bridge facility maturing within a year, project/non-recourse debt, and potential equity. The convertibles dilute above the conversion prices, only partially offset by capped calls. If the power business is slow to generate cash, refinancing and dilution risk grows. The current balance sheet itself is not stretched (net leverage ~0.9x with zero cash coupon), so this is a forward, scaling risk, not a present-day solvency issue.

3. **Core cyclicality and pricing.** Demand tracks E&P capex and oil/gas prices; 2025 WTI averaged $65.45 vs $76.63 in 2024. Frac is a commoditizing service facing technology-driven competition.

4. **Customer concentration.** Top-5 customers were ~39% of 2025 revenue (43% in 2024); in Q1 2026 two customers were ~12% each.

5. **Tax Receivable Agreements.** Liberty owes 85% of certain tax savings to pre-IPO holders (~$66.9M liability); a change of control could trigger an accelerated lump-sum payment, potentially deterring M&A.

6. **Governance optics.** Founder Chris Wright is now U.S. Secretary of Energy. This carries policy-visibility upside and conflict-of-interest/headline risk for an oil-services and power company.

Much of this is already discounted after the drawdown, which is reflected in a mid-range risk score rather than a low one.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term ATM IV of 132.6% is concentrated in a 1-day-to-expiry tenor; the 1-month and 3-month tenors sit at ~58%, essentially in line with the 60.1% one-year historical volatility. The "imminent stress" reading is therefore overstated: this is expiry-day noise, not a structural signal of a coming blowup. The put/call OI ratio has risen sharply (0.27 → 1.68 since June 2) alongside the 13.4% price decline, and the put/call volume ratio is elevated at 5.43, indicating real hedging/bearish positioning building into the drawdown. Short interest of 10% of float (3.4 days to cover) corroborates institutional caution. This does not override the fundamental balance, but it signals the market is genuinely uncertain rather than complacent.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The frac cycle inflects as equipment attrition tightens effective capacity and elevated oil prices sustain E&P activity, restoring pricing and Adjusted EBITDA toward the $900M+ range. The LPI pipeline converts: the Vantage and Texas relationships move to binding contracts, and the 3 GW target gains commercial traction, making Liberty a hybrid completions-plus-power infrastructure company with a structural AI/data-center demand tailwind. Rising forward estimates prove conservative, the three-quarter beat streak continues, and the stock re-rates toward the $40 consensus high and the convertible conversion zone. Bull target ~$40 (+47% from $27.17).

### Bear Case
The frac recovery stalls as oil softens on a Hormuz reopening or demand weakness, keeping pricing depressed and FCF near breakeven. The power pipeline slips or converts at thin economics, while the capital base swells via the bridge and further converts, leaving a larger debt load against weak cash generation and rising dilution risk. The narrative premium fully unwinds and the stock de-rates toward the peer median multiple, implying ~$20-21 (the analyst low and a ~19.6x peer P/E applied to normalized earnings). Bear target ~$20-21 (-23% to -26% from $27.17).

The setup is roughly symmetric around the current price, which is the core reason this is a HOLD rather than a directional call. The negatives (thin core cash, pre-revenue power, capital intensity) and positives (sound net leverage, rising forward estimates, beat streak, oil tailwind, ~20% below mean target, zero sells) genuinely offset.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and any 8-K disclosing a binding power contract or further financing. The May 7, 2026 material-agreement 8-K and the 6/30/2026 bridge-facility window are near-term items to confirm. A 6-9 month horizon should reveal whether the power pipeline begins converting and whether the frac cycle is firming.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A binding, economics-disclosed end-user power contract (Vantage, the 330 MW Texas developer, or a comparable named deal), moving power from optionality to backlog.
- Sustained frac pricing recovery evidenced by Adjusted EBITDA returning above ~$200M per quarter with positive FCF, confirming the supply-tightening thesis.
- Continued forward EPS upgrades (next-year consensus holding above $0.60 and rising) alongside a pullback toward the low-$20s that restores asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Power capex scaling materially via the bridge and additional converts with no binding contracts, pushing net leverage toward the 2.5x senior covenant and FCF persistently negative.
- A guidance cut or two consecutive frac-margin misses indicating the cyclical down-leg is deepening rather than inflecting.
- A material covenant headroom problem or a forced equity raise that dilutes below the conversion prices.

## Conclusion

Liberty Energy is a high-quality, technology-differentiated frac operator caught in a cyclical down-leg, onto which management has bolted a large, capital-intensive, and unproven bet on distributed power for data centers. The frac fundamentals are genuinely weak, with operating income of $73M, FY2025 FCF of $14.1M, and net income leaning on non-cash investment gains. The power thesis driving the re-rating remains pre-revenue optionality with candid execution and financing risks.

At the same time, the bear case is materially less severe than headline figures suggest. Net leverage is roughly 0.9x on zero-coupon converts with no cash interest, forward estimates are inflecting sharply higher, the company has beaten consensus in three of the last four quarters by wide margins, there are no sell ratings, and the drawdown to $27.17 has already compressed much of the narrative premium, leaving the stock ~20% below the consensus mean. The visible scenarios — a ~$20-21 bear and a ~$40 bull around a $27 spot — are roughly symmetric.

A symmetric risk/reward, a sound (if forward-risky) balance sheet, and a binary power catalyst that is not yet resolvable add up to a HOLD. The improving score trajectory (4.2 in April to ~5.3 now) reflects fundamental support that has emerged — a stronger balance-sheet read once cash is netted and sharply rising forward estimates — without yet clearing the bar for a constructive directional call given the unproven power pivot, the elevated multiple, and the broadly challenging selection environment in energy services. The honest classification is uncertain: I do not have sufficient conviction to expect appreciation or decline over the next 12 months, and the catalysts that would break the tie (binding power contracts, confirmed frac pricing recovery) are identifiable but not yet realized.
