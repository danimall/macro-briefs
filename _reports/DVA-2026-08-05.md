## Executive Summary

DaVita reported Q2 2026 diluted EPS of $4.02 against $2.58 a year earlier, a 55.8% increase, and raised the floor of its FY2026 guidance to $14.10–$15.20 in adjusted diluted EPS. The stock fell on the print and has fallen further since. The reason is visible in the segment footnote rather than the headline. Adjusting Q2 2025 for the ~$13.5M of cybersecurity remediation charges that landed entirely within U.S. dialysis, that segment's operating income rose from $536.3M to $538.0M (0.3%) on 3.4% segment revenue growth, implying adjusted segment margin compression from 18.41% to 17.86%. The same arithmetic held for the full year 2025 (17.88% versus 18.31% in 2024). The $66.1M consolidated pretax improvement decomposes as +$31.2M from the other income line (absent Mozarc equity losses plus investment gains), +$25.7M from lower corporate administrative support, +$13.5M from the non-recurrence of cyber charges, and +$1.7M from the core dialysis business.

The more consequential disclosure is in the payor-mix footnote. U.S. dialysis commercial patient service revenue was $940.2M in Q2 2026 against $947.0M in Q2 2025, a 0.7% decline in absolute dollars, while Medicare and Medicare Advantage revenue grew 5.4%. Commercial's share of U.S. dialysis patient service revenue fell from 32.6% to 31.3%. Item 1 of the FY2025 10-K states plainly that commercial payors "generate nearly all of our profits and all of our non-hospital dialysis profits," sourced from roughly 11% of U.S. patients. The mechanism management identifies is the expiration of enhanced ACA premium tax credits at the end of 2025 flowing into January 2026 enrollment. The H1 comparison (+1.2%) versus the Q2 comparison (-0.7%) indicates a building headwind.

At $227.99 the stock trades at 15.6x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $14.65 and 13.2x next-year consensus of $17.22, against guided adjusted operating income growth of 5.1%. That is not an obviously expensive multiple in isolation, but it sits above the analyst consensus mean of $208.57 and median of $218, follows a roughly 126% advance from the January 2026 low of $101, and rests on a capital structure with a -$765.1M shareholders' equity deficit, 4.24x lease-adjusted net leverage, and $8.3B (77% of debt) maturing in 2030–2031. This is the fifth consecutive report in a declining score sequence (6.3 → 5.6 → 5.8 → 5.2 → 4.7), and the Q2 segment data supplies the specific fundamental evidence that moves the call from HOLD to AVOID rather than the modest price pullback since mid-July.

## Company Overview

DaVita operates 2,671 U.S. outpatient dialysis centers and 595 international centers across 14 countries, serving roughly 298,500 patients in total as of June 30, 2026. The business reports one segment (U.S. dialysis and related lab services, ~86% of FY2025 revenue) plus an "other" grouping covering U.S. Integrated Kidney Care (IKC), other ancillary services, and international operations. International concentration is Brazil (127 centers), Colombia (74), Malaysia (71), Poland (64), Chile (63), and Germany (46). Headcount is approximately 78,000.

Payor structure drives the economics. For FY2025, government programs supplied 68% of U.S. dialysis patient service revenue (Medicare and Medicare Advantage 57%, Medicaid 7%, other government 3%) while covering 89% of patients. Commercial payors supplied 32% including hospital services; non-hospital commercial specifically was ~26% of revenue from ~11% of patients. A patient with commercial coverage generally converts to Medicare as primary payor after 33 months at rates the company describes as "on average significantly lower." The commercial cohort therefore depletes continuously and must be replenished to hold profit flat.

The durable competitive asset is not clinic real estate (Item 1 puts the cost of a new center at roughly $2M with operating profitability in year two) but the more than 900 medical director contracts, generally ten-year terms, containing covenants not to compete or hold interests in competing centers. Medicare certification requires each center to operate under a medical director, and roughly 5,200 nephrologists refer to DaVita facilities. Thirty percent of U.S. dialysis revenue flows through joint ventures in which physicians and hospitals hold minority stakes.

Consolidated GAAP operating margin fell from 16.3% in FY2024 to 15.0% in FY2025; revenue grew 6.5% while operating income declined 2.2%. FY2025 growth was mix-driven: U.S. dialysis revenue grew 3.5% to $11,793M while ancillary grew 27.3% to $1,922M, of which international grew 37.8% to $1,346M largely through acquiring 62 centers.

Volume is the binding constraint. FY2025 U.S. dialysis treatments were 28,733,980, down 1.1%, with normalized non-acquired treatment growth of -0.8%. H1 2026 normalized non-acquired growth was +0.1% (Q1) and +0.3% (Q2). Revenue per treatment rose 4.7% to $409.56 in FY2025, but $18.24 of that increase was substantially attributable to phosphate binders entering the Medicare bundle, while patient care cost per treatment rose 5.9% to $273.34, a pass-through rather than margin expansion. Q2 2026 did deliver a genuine operating positive: patient care cost per treatment fell sequentially to $277.40 from $280.11 on productivity and fixed-cost absorption.

### Q2 2026 quality of earnings

Holding the Q2 2025 diluted share count of 77,362K constant, Q2 2026's $265.4M of attributable net income equates to $3.43 per share rather than the reported $4.02. Share repurchase therefore contributed roughly $0.59, or 41%, of the $1.44 EPS increase. Of the remaining $0.85, approximately $0.35 traces to the other income swing, $0.29 to lower corporate overhead, and $0.15 to the absent cyber charge, computed at the 25.6% attributable tax rate.

Two of these drivers deserve fair treatment. The Mozarc equity-loss reversal is durable rather than a timing item: FY2025 included $46.4M of impairment and restructuring within those losses, which by construction do not repeat. And the corporate overhead reduction may reflect real cost discipline. But the trajectory (-$41.7M in Q2 2025, -$30M in Q1 2026, -$16M in Q2 2026) and the disclosed drivers ("decreased professional fees and long-term incentive compensation") suggest a low-water mark; long-term incentive accruals mark to a share price that has risen sharply, and H1 stock-based compensation of $54.2M was already below H1 2025's $62.6M. Reversal of that tailwind is plausible.

### Balance sheet

Total DaVita shareholders' equity was a deficit of $765.1M at June 30, 2026, versus -$651.1M at year-end 2025 and +$1,056M at year-end 2023. Adding back $7,591.0M of goodwill (42.9% of total assets) and $228.8M of intangibles produces a tangible book deficit of roughly $8.6B. This is overwhelmingly a repurchase artifact rather than accumulated losses (retained earnings were positive $81.2M) but it removes any book cushion for absorbing an adverse shock.

Total debt principal was $10,847.5M at June 30, up from $10,344.6M at year-end following a $500M incremental Term Loan B-2 in June. Weighted-average effective rate 5.43%, improved from 5.51% at year-end and 5.68% in FY2024, aided by $3.5B of notional interest rate caps through 2028 and $1,750M in 2029. Unhedged floating exposure is $832.9M of Term Loan A-2 plus a $65M revolver draw against $1,435M of availability.

Credit-agreement leverage was 3.37x against a 5.00x maximum on TTM Consolidated EBITDA of $3,018M and net debt of $10,162M. Headroom is genuine: reaching the covenant would require roughly a one-third EBITDA decline. Two adjustments frame the true position: excluding the $7M of "expected cost savings" and $149M of "Other" add-backs the credit agreement permits, EBITDA is $2,862M and leverage 3.55x; adding $2,625.5M of operating lease liabilities for a lessee of 2,671 clinic sites gives lease-adjusted net leverage of 4.24x. Interest coverage on TTM operating income of approximately $2,128M against $540M of interest expense is 3.94x. Debt expense rose from $357M in FY2022 to $580M in FY2025, a $223M increase that by itself exceeded the change in operating income over the period.

The maturity profile is barbelled: $62.3M remaining in 2026, $114.0M in 2027, $160.8M in 2028, $144.6M in 2029, then $4,554.3M in 2030 and $3,764.2M in 2031. Term Loan A-2 and the revolver spring to a March 2, 2030 maturity if any of the 4.625% Notes remain outstanding 91 days before their June 2030 maturity.

### Capital allocation

Basic shares outstanding fell from ~92,992K at year-end 2022 to ~63,772K post the July 31 Berkshire settlement, a 31.2% reduction in three and a half years. FY2024 repurchases averaged $140.06, FY2025 $140.09, H1 2026 $142.77, and July 2026 $199.55. Judged against the current price, the 2024–2025 program was well executed. Remaining authorization was $1,373M at July 31, roughly 6.4M shares (~10.1% of shares outstanding) at $213 and approximately 6.0M shares (~9.4%) at $227.99.

The mechanism, however, is what produces the EPS line. FY2025 adjusted EPS of $10.78 rose only 0.8% from $10.69 despite a ~16% reduction in diluted shares, implying adjusted attributable net income fell roughly 14–15%. In FY2026 the same lever is applied to a modestly improving base, which is why guided EPS growth of 35.9% so far exceeds guided operating income growth of 5.1%.

Berkshire Hathaway held 28,697,229 shares after the July 31 settlement, approximately 45.0% of shares outstanding, with the sales executed as repurchases by the issuer under an April 30, 2024 agreement recorded in "due to related party." Berkshire's percentage has not declined while DaVita retires other shares; H1 sale-back pricing averaged $132.97 against DaVita's $154.71 open-market average, so the arrangement was not economically disadvantageous to remaining holders in that window. Free float is therefore roughly 35.1M shares, near $8.0B at the current price, which amplifies moves in both directions. The specific ownership threshold is derived from share arithmetic; the terms of the agreement in Note 18 of the FY2025 financial statements were not retrievable and should be treated as unconfirmed.

## Growth Analysis

The addressable U.S. market has stopped growing. USRDS data cited in Item 1 put the U.S. ESKD dialysis population above 557,000 in 2023, growing at a 1.8% CAGR from 2013–2023 but only 0.2% from 2018–2023 and 0.5% from 2022 to 2023. Management guided U.S. treatment volumes to approximately flat for 2026. Two forces operate: post-pandemic ESKD mortality, which is cyclical and improving (Q2 volumes beat internal expectations on lower-than-expected mortality), and GLP-1 receptor agonists plus SGLT2 inhibitors slowing CKD progression, which Item 1 names explicitly and which is secular.

Reimbursement arithmetic works against margin. The CY2026 ESRD PPS update is +2.2%; the June 2026 proposed CY2027 rule is +1.1%, inclusive of incorporating phosphate binders into the base rate as the Transitional Drug Add-on Payment Adjustment expires at end-2026. Against those administered updates, H1 2026 patient care cost per treatment rose 3.2% and clinical wage rates rose approximately 3.8% in each of 2024 and 2025. That gap is the arithmetic behind the recurring 43–55bp of annual U.S. dialysis margin compression. Management has guided FY2026 phosphate-binder impact on operating income to approximately flat; the CY2027 conversion from cost-based add-on to fixed base-rate increment shifts the cost variance to DaVita, though it also changes the incentive on binder acquisition cost in DaVita's favor. The final rule expected around November 2026 is a genuine unknown.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year consensus of $14.88 is up 5.4% over 90 days and next-year $17.22 up 5.0%, with current-quarter up 7.0%. This is directionally supportive and I weight it as such, but it is well short of the magnitude that signals a systematic underestimation of an inflection, and the underlying operating growth being revised is 5%, not 30%.

Two growth pillars carry specific fragility. IKC swung from -$19M in Q1 to +$40M in Q2 on "a net increase in shared savings," with H1 at +$21M versus -$3M. But $98.5M of H1 IKC revenue related to performance obligations satisfied in prior years, risk-based patients of 64,900 remain below the 66,000 year-end figure, non-risk arrangements shrank from 9,400 to 5,700 in six months, and CMS terminated the ETC and KCF models at end-2025 while extending CKCC only to December 31, 2027 with no announced successor. Second, expanded hemodialysis and hemodiafiltration are being positioned as a volume lever on the logic that reduced mortality extends revenue duration per patient. Item 1A frames the same technologies as a competitive risk requiring significant capital and lacking large-scale U.S. studies; Fresenius manufactures its own devices. Which way this cuts is unresolved.

Mezzanine noncontrolling interests of $1,561.4M belong in enterprise value: TTM net income attributable to NCI was $342M against $1,164M of pre-NCI net income, and TTM cash distributions to NCI were $323M, so consolidated EBITDA materially overstates what accrues to DaVita holders.

The supplied profile fields (trailing P/E and forward P/E both 21.93, EPS $9.51) are internally inconsistent for a company guiding 36% EPS growth and imply a price near $208.5 rather than the authoritative $227.99 close; I discard them and anchor on guidance and consensus, which reconcile.

Peer benchmarks are close to unusable here. The Health Care Services comparables carry a median P/E of 40.1, but two of five have negative earnings (HIMS at -494x, RDNT at -308x) and BTSG at 82.1x is a different business model. The only clean comparable, Chemed at 23.0x, is a debt-light hospice operator with materially different leverage. Sector medians provide no valid anchor.

The relevant anchors are absolute and historical. DaVita traded near $122 in early February 2026, roughly 8.3x the same FY2026 guidance now in hand. The multiple has expanded from ~8x to ~15.6x in six months. Prior appreciation is not by itself a valuation argument, and the fundamental improvement over that window was real: Q1 beat by 22.6%, guidance was raised, and the Mozarc drag was removed. But the re-rating has now carried price above the consensus mean of $208.57 and median of $218, with the analyst distribution containing two strong sells against two buys and four holds. On inflection stocks with positive revisions and cheap multiples, consensus targets function as stale floors. That framing requires the stock to be cheap relative to the growth being delivered. Here the growth being delivered at the operating line is 5%, the EPS growth above that is purchased with balance-sheet capacity, and the FCF yield and the buyback are claims on the same cash and cannot both be counted. At 8.7–9.2x EV/EBITDA for a business with flat volumes, ~50bp of annual core margin compression, administered reimbursement running below its own cost inflation, and 4.24x lease-adjusted leverage, the multiple looks fair to full rather than mispriced.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is a mature duopoly in a non-growing end market. DaVita's ~200,500 U.S. patients against the 2023 USRDS base imply roughly 36% share, an approximation given the period mismatch. Fresenius Medical Care is the principal competitor, and DaVita concedes the structural asymmetry in its own filing: Fresenius manufactures its own supplies and equipment, which "may... provide cost advantages." DaVita's answer was partnership rather than integration through the Mozarc device joint venture, which recorded $46.4M of impairment and restructuring charges in FY2025. The disappearance of those losses is a meaningful 2026 earnings tailwind and simultaneously an admission that the capability was not acquired.

Unit-level entry barriers are low (approximately $2M of capital, roughly a year from lease to opening, profitability in year two) and Item 1A cites the "relative ease of entry." Competition includes private-equity-backed entrants and individual nephrologists or former medical directors opening their own units. The real barrier is the local medical director relationship, which is precisely what the FTC's April 2024 civil investigative demands examine, covering "restrictive covenants, such as non-competes, with physicians" from January 2016 forward. DaVita argues its covenants do not restrict practice, patient choice, or rounding rights at competitor facilities, which may position them favorably. If curtailed, the transmission channel would be gradual medical-director attrition rather than a step change.

Two industry-specific legal doctrines push in the same direction as the observed commercial revenue decline. *Marietta Memorial Hospital v. DaVita* permits group health plans to impose uniform limits on outpatient dialysis benefits, enabling restrictive plan design. Separately, removal of objective time-and-distance network adequacy standards for outpatient dialysis weakens DaVita's leverage in commercial contract negotiations.

Where DaVita's position is genuinely strong: claimed CMS Quality Incentive Program leadership for the eleven most recently reported years and Five-Star leadership for ten, against a QIP that can reduce Medicare payment by up to 2%. That is a real, if modest, economic advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial mix erosion (highest impact, now measurable).** The company attributes the Q2 commercial revenue decline to enhanced ACA premium tax credit expiration at end-2025. The FY2025 10-K states non-hospital commercial patients as a share of total patients was "relatively flat," so patient-count concentration has not deteriorated while revenue mix has. That pattern is consistent with rate pressure or plan-design change rather than patient loss, and rate pressure can be recovered in later contract cycles. Rising government revenue against a shrinking commercial dollar base is mix deterioration in profit terms, not diversification.

**Phosphate binder reimbursement transition.** TDAPA expires at end-2026; the proposed CY2027 rule offers +1.1% inclusive of base-rate incorporation. The final rule around November 2026 is the near-term binary.

**Regulatory and litigation stack.** Recorded accruals for legal and regulatory matters were "immaterial" at both June 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025 against four open government investigations: the FTC industry investigation into physician non-competes; a D.C. Attorney General antitrust CID on the American Kidney Fund; California Department of Insurance subpoenas on patient insurance communications and AKF premium assistance; and a U.S. Attorney (D.N.J./E.D. Pa.) inquiry into physician joint ventures and Corporate Integrity Agreement compliance, with the related *U.S. ex rel. Bayne* False Claims Act qui tam surviving a motion to dismiss on April 29, 2025. California's AB 290, which would cap reimbursement for patients receiving charitable premium assistance, is stayed on appeal. One favorable development: class certification was denied on June 10, 2026 in the no-poach follow-on civil litigation.

**Noncontrolling interest puts.** $1,561.4M of NCI subject to put provisions sits in temporary equity, of which roughly $1,474M was presented as contractually exercisable in the 2026 column of the FY2025 obligations table, at the partners' discretion. A ±1.0x change in the weighted-average EBITDA multiple moves estimated fair value by ~$225M. Historical exercise has been modest ($18.6M in H1 2026), so a mass put event is not a base case, but this is a partner-controlled call on ~$1.5B of liquidity competing directly with the buyback.

**Cybersecurity.** The April 2025 incident involved PII and PHI exfiltration, produced ~$25.2M of FY2025 charges excluding business interruption, and impaired both collections and, per the filing, DaVita's "ability to accept new patients." This was the second event in fourteen months following the February 2024 Change Healthcare vendor breach.

**Labor.** Clinical wage rates rose ~3.8% in each of 2024 and 2025 against a skilled-personnel shortage, with union petitions filed at a number of California clinics at varying stages of election and legal challenge.

**Balance sheet fragility.** No book equity cushion (-$765.1M, -$8.6B tangible), 4.24x lease-adjusted leverage, 3.94x interest coverage, and $8.3B of 2030–2031 maturities with a springing March 2030 acceleration on Term Loan A-2 and the revolver. Debt is not priced as distressed: the 6.875% and 6.75% notes were marked near 102.9% of par at June 30, and near-term amortization is negligible.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress that the disclosed fundamentals do not explain. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 74.7% against 40.2% one-year historical volatility, a 34.5-point premium at the 91st percentile of the current chain, with a pronounced backwardation (74.7% at 15 days, 52.8% at 43 days, 42.3% at 106 days) and an implied move of ±12.7% on the August 21 tenor. IV rose 26 points between July 19 and August 5, from 48.7%, while the price fell 3.8%. This is unusual two weeks *after* an earnings report, which normally crushes near-dated vol.

Two related observations. Put/call open interest is 0.31 (call-tilted, and rising only from 0.12) with skew balanced at one month and mildly negative at three and six months. So the vol bid is not obviously a directional put bid. And the frozen snapshot records an extended-hours last trade of $208.10 against the $227.99 official close, a -8.7% diagnostic divergence flagged alongside a market-date mismatch. I cannot attribute either the vol spike or the pre-market print to any disclosed event, and I will not speculate on the cause. Both are consistent with the view that the risk/reward at $227.99 is unfavorable, and both mean a portion of the downside in my base case may already be realizing. The high near-dated vol also makes buying downside protection expensive.

Short interest of 13.7% of float with 5.3 days to cover sits against a free float of only ~35.1M shares once Berkshire's ~28.7M are excluded, so bearish positioning is more crowded relative to tradable supply than the headline suggests.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($270, +18%)

Q2's flat adjusted core segment income marks the trough of the ACA subsidy hit rather than a trend rate. H2 2026 carries 158.0 normalized treatment days against 154.5 in H1, a ~2.3% mechanical volume tailwind, plus Q4's typically favorable commercial deductible and co-insurance position, so guidance implying H2 adjusted operating income of $1,089–$1,189M against H1's $1,061M is achievable. Patient care cost per treatment already fell sequentially on productivity. Lower ESKD mortality extends revenue duration per patient, and expanded HD/HDF rollout reinforces it. Commercial contract negotiations currently underway recover rate. IKC risk-based patients grew sequentially to 64,900 with annualized risk spend rising from $5.4B to $5.8B, and most IKC revenue derives from commercial and Medicare Advantage C-SNP contracts rather than CKCC, so the 2027 sunset is survivable. The remaining $1,373M authorization retires another ~9% of shares, and 2024–2026 repurchases at $140–$143 averages now look well-timed. At 15.6x guidance with a 7.7% FCF yield and estimates rising, 17x FY2027 consensus of $17.22 supports $290; the analyst high of $270 is the disciplined version.

### Bear Case ($150, -34%)

Commercial revenue continues declining in absolute dollars as the subsidy expiration works through 2026 and 2027 enrollment. Because commercial supplies "nearly all" non-hospital profit from ~11% of patients, each point of mix loss carries disproportionate profit consequence; the observed 130bp mix decline is consistent with the 55bp of segment margin compression. The CY2027 final rule converts phosphate binders to a fixed base-rate increment inside a +1.1% total update against 3.2% patient-care cost inflation and 3.8% wage inflation, extending the annual 43–55bp compression. Corporate administrative support normalizes upward from a $16M quarterly low-water mark as long-term incentive accruals reset. CKCC expires December 31, 2027 with no successor, removing the IKC shared-savings vehicle whose prior-year true-ups already supply a large share of reported segment profit. Debt-funded buybacks ($504M net issuance against $751M of repurchase in H1) meet a stalling EBITDA base, and leverage above 4.5x lease-adjusted narrows the program. FY2027 EPS lands near $15 and the multiple compresses toward 10x, its early-2026 zone. $150.

### Base Case ($208, -8.8%)

Guidance is met near the midpoint; H2 delivers on the treatment-day tailwind. FY2027 EPS growth decelerates to high-single digits as buyback capacity is consumed and the binder base-rate conversion offsets volume. Fourteen times the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $14.65 gives $205; 12.5x FY2027 consensus of $17.22 gives $215. The midpoint of those two, $208, is also approximately the consensus mean of $208.57.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Three dated events. The CY2027 ESRD PPS final rule, expected around November 2026, sets the phosphate-binder base-rate mechanics against a proposed +1.1% total update. The Q3 2026 print, expected late October or early November, tests the H2 ramp required by guidance ($1,089–$1,189M of adjusted operating income and $604–$854M of FCF against H1's $1,061M and $396M). January 2027 open-enrollment data reveals whether commercial mix erosion continues past the first subsidy-expiration year. Each is specific and dated, but none is a solvency or covenant event: leverage of 3.37x against a 5.00x maximum requires roughly a one-third EBITDA decline to bind.

**Timing window.** Three to six months to the Q3 print and the final rule. That is acceptable carry duration.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 13.7% of float short, 5.3 days to cover, 4.50M shares. The complication is float: Berkshire's ~28.7M shares are ~45% of the 63.8M outstanding, leaving ~35.1M shares of tradable supply. Shorts are covering against a structurally thin float, which amplified both the 2025 decline and the 2026 recovery. The remaining $1,373M repurchase authorization is an additional buyer of ~9% of shares.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market does not confirm a bearish consensus. Put/call open interest is 0.31 (call-tilted) and skew is balanced at one month and only -2.0% and -3.6% at three and six months. Near-term IV of 74.7% at the 91st percentile makes put protection expensive; a ±12.7% implied move on 15 days means an at-the-money August put requires a large realized move simply to break even. High near-dated vol also raises the effective cost of any hedged expression.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear $150 (-34%), base $208 (-8.8%), bull $270 (+18%). Measured on extremes the ratio is 34:18, or 1.9:1. Measured on the more probable base case it is 8.8:18, clearly unfavorable. ## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** I would revisit constructively on any of the following, all measurable from disclosed data:

1. **Commercial revenue stabilization** — U.S. dialysis commercial patient service revenue flat or growing year-over-year in absolute dollars for two consecutive quarters (Q3 2026 baseline: $940.2M was the Q2 figure; the Q3 2025 comparator should be checked in the 10-Q payor-source footnote). That is a 23% decline from the current basis.
4. **CY2027 final rule** — a final ESRD PPS rule materially better than the +1.1% proposal, or DaVita quantifying a neutral-to-positive phosphate-binder economic outcome under base-rate mechanics.
5. **Insider behavior** — clustered open-market purchases by two or more C-suite executives, reversing the current pattern of five senior sellers and no buyers.

**Timeline for headwinds.** The ACA subsidy effect works through at least the 2027 enrollment cycle, so through H1 2027. The phosphate-binder transition begins January 2027. Expect two to four quarters before the commercial mix trajectory is resolvable, with the November 2026 final rule and the Q3 2026 print as the next natural reassessment points.

**Downgrade to a shortable posture would require** a guidance cut at the Q3 print, credit-agreement leverage rising above 4.0x, or a material adverse development in the FTC non-compete investigation, any of which would convert the multiple-erosion thesis into a discrete catalyst.

## Conclusion

DaVita's reported results are improving and its guidance is credible. The problem is composition. The 85%-of-revenue core segment produced 0.3% adjusted operating income growth on 3.4% revenue growth in Q2, extending a multi-year pattern of roughly 50bp of annual margin compression driven by administered reimbursement updates running below the company's own 3.2% patient-care and 3.8% wage cost inflation. The highest-margin revenue line, commercial, which the filing states supplies nearly all profit from ~11% of patients, declined in absolute dollars year-over-year, with the H1-to-Q2 acceleration indicating a building rather than fading headwind. Guided FY2026 EPS growth of 35.9% against 5.1% operating income growth is the arithmetic of a 14.6% reduction in share count, a 350bp lower tax rate, and the removal of the Mozarc drag, funded in part by net debt issuance against a -$765.1M equity deficit.

None of this makes DaVita a broken business. It generates $1.0–$1.25B of guided free cash flow, holds wide covenant headroom, faces negligible maturities before 2030, and has an operator with a demonstrably well-timed repurchase record. What has changed is price. At $227.99 the stock trades at 15.6x guidance and 8.7–9.2x EV/EBITDA after a roughly 126% advance from the January low, above both the consensus mean of $208.57 and median of $218, with two strong sells in an eight-analyst distribution, 13.7% short interest against a ~35M-share float, and near-dated implied volatility at 74.7% in steep backwardation signaling risk the disclosed record does not explain. Downgrading to **AVOID** with a base case of $208 (-8.8%), bear $150 (-34%), and bull $270 (+18%). Not shortable: the thesis erodes a multiple over quarters rather than producing a break, and the float mechanics plus expensive options make an active short unattractive.
