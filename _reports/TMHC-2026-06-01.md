**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The investment case for Taylor Morrison reset on May 31/June 1, 2026, when Berkshire Hathaway agreed to acquire the company in an all-cash transaction at **$72.50 per share**, valuing the equity at roughly $6.8B and the enterprise at ~$8.5B including debt. The price represents a ~24% premium to the May 29 unaffected close of $58.50 and is the first major acquisition under new Berkshire CEO Greg Abel. Management expects the deal to close in the second half of 2026, subject to antitrust review and a shareholder vote. Financing is not a constraint given Berkshire's ~$400B cash position.

This reframes TMHC from a fundamental homebuilder analysis into a risk-arbitrage situation. At the post-announcement price of ~$71.61, the stock trades roughly $0.89 (about 1.2%) below the cash consideration. The entire prospective return for a new equity buyer is that gross spread, which annualizes to a low-single-digit IRR over a six-month close. The asymmetry is unfavorable for a directional equity position: the residual upside is capped at $72.50, while a deal break would likely re-expose the unaffected ~$58 level, a roughly 19% decline. The market is implicitly pricing a deal-closure probability near 94%.

We rate TMHC **HOLD**. Existing holders are reasonable to retain the position and collect the spread given the high probability of closure, the all-cash certainty, and the modest antitrust overlap (Berkshire's Clayton Homes is predominantly manufactured housing versus TMHC's site-built focus). New equity capital is poorly compensated: there is no independent appreciation thesis remaining, and the binary completion risk dominates the return profile. This is a confident-but-thin merger-arb position, not a fundamental conviction call.

## Company Overview

Taylor Morrison is the sixth-largest U.S. homebuilder, operating across 12 states under the Taylor Morrison and Esplanade brands, with a build-to-rent business (Yardly) and an ancillary financial-services arm covering mortgage (TMHF), title (Inspired Title), and insurance (TMIS). The company closed roughly 13,000 homes in 2025 across 341 active selling communities with approximately 3,000 employees.

The operating structure comprises three homebuilding segments (East, Florida-heavy: Tampa, Orlando, Sarasota, Atlanta; Central: Texas, Colorado, Indiana; and West: California, Arizona, Nevada, Pacific Northwest) plus Financial Services. The buyer mix spans entry-level, move-up, and resort-lifestyle customers, a deliberate diversification management presents as cycle-resilient.

A defining strategic feature is the land-light model: TMHC increasingly controls lots through options, joint ventures, and land-banking arrangements rather than outright ownership, with 27% of lots acquired in 2025 and approximately $3.4B of off-balance-sheet land-option and land-banking obligations. The company has been named "America's Most Trusted Home Builder" for 11 consecutive years by Lifestory Research, a brand attribute Abel cited explicitly in the deal rationale.

The standalone fundamentals describe a decelerating but still highly profitable business that Berkshire is acquiring into clear cyclical softness.

Revenue has plateaued near $8.1B with home closings essentially flat year-over-year (12,997 versus 12,896 units). The most important fundamental concern is margin compression: home-closings gross margin fell 190 bps to 22.5% (23.0% adjusted), driven by higher incentives and discounts, closing product mix, and inventory impairments that rose to $28.8M from $5.0M in 2024. Q1 2026 gross margin reportedly fell further to ~20.6%, which management characterized as above guidance. Interest expense increased sharply to $47.0M from $13.3M as land-banking communities grew 48% year-over-year, generating non-capitalizable interest.

The balance sheet is healthy. Operating cash flow nearly quadrupled to $817M as land and inventory spend declined, generating roughly $777M of free cash flow. Net homebuilding debt-to-capital improved to 17.8% from 20.0%, total liquidity sits near $1.8B, the Altman Z-score is 2.78 (grey zone but normal for a capital-intensive homebuilder), and debt-to-equity is 0.56. In December 2025 the company issued $525M of 5.75% senior notes due 2032 and redeemed its 2027 notes, terming out maturities at favorable rates with a modest $13.3M extinguishment loss.

Financial Services is a relative bright spot: revenue rose 5%, pre-tax income increased 17.6% to $117.3M, and originations were high-quality (average FICO 751, 96% agency). The company repurchased 6.5M shares for $381M in 2025 and authorized a new $1.0B buyback on February 11, 2026, now superseded by the acquisition.

## Growth Analysis

The forward operating picture is the weak point of the standalone story and is precisely what allows Berkshire to buy at what it views as a trough. The order book deteriorated materially through 2025:

- Net sales orders fell **9.6%**
- Backlog collapsed **40.6%**, to 2,819 units from 4,742
- The cancellation rate jumped to **13.2%** from 9.5%

These trends reflect elevated mortgage rates, affordability strain, and buyers' inability to sell existing homes. Management has been reducing required deposits to stimulate orders, which itself raises cancellations. Q1 2026 showed a sequential backlog recovery (+23% q/q) and an EPS beat ($1.12 versus $0.87 estimate, +28.6%), but the year-over-year trajectory remains negative.

Consensus estimate revisions confirm the deceleration. Over the past 90 days, current-year EPS estimates were cut 20.9% (to $5.29 from $6.69) and next-year estimates 19.3% (to $6.42 from $7.95). The current-quarter estimate fell 13.0% over the last 30 days alone. The earnings-surprise record is mixed (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters), consistent with a business at a cyclical turn where visibility is poor.

For an equity holder, standalone growth is now largely moot: the deal fixes the exit value at $72.50 regardless of whether the housing cycle turns. Berkshire's purchase is an explicit counter-cyclical bet on pent-up demand and an eventual cycle recovery, but that upside accrues to the acquirer, not to TMHC shareholders.

The expected-value math: at a 94% closure probability, EV ≈ 0.94 × $72.50 + 0.06 × $58.00 ≈ $71.63, essentially the current price. The residual return is thin relative to the ~19% downside on a break. This supports a HOLD rather than a BUY: there is no asymmetric upside, and the downside is real if the deal fails.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. homebuilding sector has been in a prolonged downturn driven by elevated mortgage rates and affordability pressure, visible directly in TMHC's order, backlog, and cancellation figures. Within that environment, TMHC retains genuine competitive assets: a top-six national scale position, an 11-year "most trusted" brand designation, a diversified buyer mix, and a land-light balance sheet that reduces capital intensity and downside exposure to land write-downs.

Berkshire's strategic logic centers on vertical integration. Abel signaled intent to unify TMHC's site-built operations with Berkshire's existing housing ecosystem: Clayton Homes (manufactured housing), building-products subsidiaries, and Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices brokerage. The combined site-built footprint would rank approximately fourth nationally. The product-market distinction between manufactured (Clayton) and site-built (TMHC) homes limits direct overlap, which both supports the strategic fit and reduces antitrust concentration concerns. Among peers, TMHC was characterized as a relative Q1 standout, but its order book deteriorated in line with the broader sector, so the competitive trajectory is best described as stable-to-modestly-positive within a declining industry.

## Risk Assessment

Post-announcement, the risk profile is dominated by deal-completion risk rather than operating risk. Standalone risks now matter primarily as the downside scenario if the transaction breaks.

1. **Deal-completion risk (primary).** Antitrust review is the most plausible source of delay. Berkshire already owns Clayton Homes, and the combined site-built entity would rank fourth, but the manufactured-versus-site-built product distinction limits direct overlap. A shareholder vote and customary closing conditions apply; financing is not a concern. If the deal fails, the stock likely returns toward the unaffected ~$58 level (~19% downside).

2. **Standalone demand deterioration.** The cyclical softness Berkshire is underwriting, captured in the order, backlog, and cancellation figures above. This is the bear case if the equity is left standalone.

3. **Margin pressure.** Reliance on rate buydowns and discounts, tariff-driven input-cost inflation on lumber/steel/aluminum, and rising non-capitalizable interest as land-banking communities expand.

4. **Off-balance-sheet land obligations.** The $3.4B in lot-option and land-banking commitments depends on land-bank investor capital remaining available.

5. **Geographic and legal.** Heavy Western U.S./California exposure with wildfire-insurance availability risk; legal accruals rose to $53.3M from $26.2M in 2023, with a $64.7M Florida club-fee judgment precedent.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility collapsed from 39.7% (May 18, pre-deal) to 8.9% (post-deal), a 30.7-point decline that reflects the price certainty introduced by the all-cash offer. With the stock pinned near the deal price, residual volatility is low. The term structure is in backwardation (8.9% near-term versus 4.6% at six months), which in a deal context reflects near-dated event/timing carry around the closing process and arbitrageur hedging rather than systemic stress. The put/call volume ratios (1.88 near-term, 3.64 at three months) are consistent with merger-arb hedging activity rather than informed directional bearishness. Short interest at 8.2% of float (4.4 days to cover) is unremarkable for an arb situation. The options market broadly corroborates a high-probability, near-term resolution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$72.50, +1.2%)
The Berkshire deal closes on schedule in 2H 2026. Antitrust clearance is straightforward given the limited manufactured-versus-site-built overlap, the shareholder vote passes given the 24% premium during a cyclical trough, and holders collect the $72.50 cash consideration. The position behaves like a short-duration, high-probability fixed-income instrument with a low-single-digit annualized return.

### Bear Case (~$58.00, -19%)
The transaction breaks, most plausibly on an unexpected antitrust challenge or a regulatory remedy demand. The stock re-rates to its unaffected level around $58, where the standalone fundamentals (orders down ~10%, backlog down ~41%, cancellations at ~13%, margins compressing toward 20%, current-year estimates cut 21%) reassert themselves. The 8.7x standalone multiple was depressed for cause, and absent the deal premium the equity has limited near-term catalysts.

The asymmetry of risking ~19% to capture ~1.2% is the defining feature. The trade is only rational for investors who underwrite a deal-closure probability comfortably above ~94%, which is defensible but leaves thin margin for surprise.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The natural resolution point is deal close, targeted for 2H 2026. Interim milestones include the antitrust review period (HSR filing and any second-request developments) and the shareholder vote.

**Upgrade triggers (to a more constructive arb position):**
- Early antitrust clearance or expiration of the HSR waiting period without a second request, which would compress the implied break-risk and could narrow the spread toward zero.
- A shareholder-vote record date and proxy timeline confirming a near-term close, improving the annualized IRR on the residual spread.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID/exit):**
- Any antitrust second request, regulatory remedy demand, or litigation challenging the combination, which would widen the spread and materially raise break probability.
- Public indication of a financing or material-adverse-change dispute (low probability given Berkshire's cash position, but monitor).
- Deal termination, which would crystallize the move back toward the ~$58 unaffected level; the stock would then be re-evaluated as a standalone cyclical homebuilder with deteriorating orders and negative estimate revisions.

For existing holders, retain the position to collect the spread but be prepared to exit if antitrust friction emerges. For new capital, the reward does not justify the binary risk unless deployed within a diversified merger-arb book sized for the asymmetry.

## Conclusion

Taylor Morrison's standalone fundamentals (a top-six builder with a healthy balance sheet but decelerating orders, a collapsing backlog, rising cancellations, compressing margins, and negative current-year estimate revisions) are now secondary to the pending Berkshire Hathaway acquisition. The deal converts the equity into a merger-arbitrage instrument with ~1.2% upside to the deal price and ~19% downside on a break, a payoff that compensates only investors assigning very high closure probability. Berkshire's all-cash certainty, ample financing, and the modest antitrust overlap support a high-probability close, but the residual spread is thin and the situation is binary.

We rate TMHC **HOLD**: existing holders are reasonable to retain and collect the spread, while new equity capital is poorly served by the capped upside and real binary downside. The prior score trajectory (5.5 → 6.0, improving on fundamentals) is superseded by the deal, which simultaneously locks in a premium exit and introduces completion risk; our overall score of 5.7 reflects the loss of independent appreciation potential rather than fundamental deterioration. The outcome now depends on antitrust clearance and the shareholder vote in the second half of 2026.
