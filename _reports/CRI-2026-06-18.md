**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carter's is North America's largest branded children's apparel company, and the FY2025 numbers describe a profit recession layered on a secular demand headwind: operating margin compressed 400bps to 5.0%, net income fell 50.5% to $91.8M, diluted EPS halved to $2.53, and free cash flow margin shrank to 2.4%. The proximate cause was roughly $110M of incremental tariff cost, of which ~$60M flowed through COGS, compounded by CEO-transition and restructuring charges in SG&A. A quality franchise is being squeezed from both ends: a shrinking newborn customer base and an import-cost shock it cannot fully pass through.

The operating line is inflecting at the consumer level. U.S. Retail comps were +10.5% in Q1 FY2026, the fourth consecutive positive quarter, with growing customer counts. Operating cash flow swung from −$48.6M to +$6.4M year over year. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 6.5% over 90 days, the current-quarter estimate moved from −$0.16 to +$0.06, and Q1 delivered a 207.6% EPS surprise. The top-line turn is real; the bottom-line recovery lags it because wholesale gross margins are still being crushed by tariff-inflated average unit costs.

Two asymmetric upside contingencies sit on top of the operating story. Carter's has filed a ~$130M IEEPA tariff refund claim (≈8% of the $1.6B market cap) following a Supreme Court ruling invalidating those tariffs, accounted for as an unrecognized gain contingency, with substitute Section 122 tariffs a live risk. And in September 2025 the Board adopted a poison pill "in response to the rapid accumulation of a significant amount of shares … by a third party," then installed a proven turnaround operator (former Build-A-Bear CEO Sharon Price John) as permanent CEO on June 15, 2026. At ~13x CY consensus, with the mean analyst target ($42.67) modestly above spot and the prior reports' own low-$30s re-entry math sitting just below the forward multiple, the payoff is roughly balanced and tilts un-shortable. That combination argues for HOLD rather than AVOID. I lack conviction in either direction until the profit recovery follows the consumer turn, so this is an uncertain hold.

## Company Overview

Carter's (founded 1865) is the largest branded apparel company in North America focused exclusively on babies and young children (newborn–size 14). Core brands are Carter's and OshKosh B'gosh (acquired 2005). It uniquely owns exclusive mass-channel brands at all three large retailers simultaneously: Child of Mine (Walmart), Just One You (Target), and Simple Joys (Amazon). Emerging lines include Skip Hop (baby gear), Little Planet (organic), and Otter Avenue (toddler, launched 2025).

FY2025 segments:
- **U.S. Retail** — $1.47B (50.6% of sales); ~800 stores plus eCommerce/app.
- **U.S. Wholesale** — $1.00B (34.5%); ~19,500 doors, anchored by Walmart, Target, Amazon.
- **International** — $431M (14.9%); Canada (191 stores), Mexico (73 stores), plus licensees in 90+ countries.

The moat is genuine but eroding: Carter's reaches a very high share of U.S. newborn wardrobes through unmatched multichannel reach. The structural headwinds (declining birth rates, value-seeking consumers, mass-merchant share gains, and tariff inflation) are sector-wide, but Carter's margin compression has been deeper than peers'.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year deterioration from the 2022 peak is the central financial story:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 (53 wks) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3.21B | $2.95B | $2.84B | $2.90B (+1.9%) |
| Operating income | $379M | $323M | $255M | $144M |
| Operating margin | 11.8% | 11.0% | 9.0% | **5.0%** |
| Net income | $250M | $233M | $186M | **$91.8M** |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $5.12 | **$2.53** |

FY2025 specifics: gross margin fell 260bps to 45.4% on the incremental tariff cost in COGS; SG&A rate rose 230bps to 41.0% on CEO transition, restructuring, consulting, and incentive comp. Operating cash flow fell 59% to $122M and FCF was only $69M as tariff-inflated inventory consumed working capital.

The balance sheet is adequate but no longer cheap to fund. Cash is $487M against $575M of total debt (a single tranche of 7.375% senior notes due 2031). D/E is ~1.77x and the current ratio a healthy 2.51x, with no ABL drawings and ~$744M available at year-end. The November 2025 refinancing replaced $500M of 5.625% 2027 notes with $575M of 7.375% 2031 notes, extending maturity but adding ~$3M/quarter of interest (Q1 FY26 interest expense +50% YoY). Altman Z of 2.52 places the company in the grey zone; this is not a solvency story, but the cost of capital moved the wrong way.

Q1 FY2026 shows the inflection and its limits. Net sales rose 8.1% to $681M with U.S. Retail comps positive and customer counts growing. But gross margin still fell 310bps to 43.1% (~$50M tariff hit), U.S. Wholesale operating income fell 33.5% as tariff-driven average unit costs outpaced price recovery, and net income was $14.3M versus $15.5M a year earlier — adjusted EPS fell ~41%. Operating cash flow turned positive at +$6.4M (from −$48.6M). The top-line turn is ahead of the bottom-line turn, and the gap is entirely tariff-driven wholesale margin.

## Growth Analysis

The genuine secular constraint is declining U.S. birth rates, explicitly cited in the 10-K as a structural drag on the customer funnel. This is a permanent, not cyclical, pressure on the addressable base, and it caps the long-run organic growth rate well below what the franchise enjoyed historically.

Against that backdrop, the near-term direction is positive. Four consecutive quarters of positive U.S. Retail comps, capped by +10.5% in Q1 FY26 with growing customer counts, indicate the pricing/assortment/marketing stabilization plan is gaining traction. The estimate-revision data confirms it: current-year EPS rose +6.5% over 90 days (to $3.26), next-year +1.8% (to $3.65), and the current-quarter estimate swung from −$0.16 to +$0.06 — a +140.2% revision off a depressed base. These are the signatures of a trough-and-recovery, not a collapse.

The recovery is concentrated in retail and is being offset in wholesale. Simple Joys (Amazon) demand is declining as Carter's deliberately shifts its Amazon assortment toward flagship brands, and that pressure is expected to continue into FY2026. The strategic reset — cutting SKUs 20–30%, compressing the product calendar to ~3 months, barbell pricing (premium plus opening price point), and ~150 store closures FY25–28 with a 15% office headcount cut (~$35M savings) — is a credible brand-revitalization playbook, and it is precisely the type of program new CEO Sharon Price John ran at Build-A-Bear. Execution risk is real, but the framework is coherent.

## Valuation Assessment

At $41.32, CRI trades at ~12.7x CY consensus EPS ($3.26) and ~11.3x NY EPS ($3.65). On a trailing basis it is ~16.4x reported EPS of $2.53. Because FY2025 EPS reflects a tariff-depressed trough rather than steady-state earnings power, the forward multiple is the relevant anchor, and at low-double-digits it is undemanding for a franchise with a recovering consumer business.

Peer context is messy. The same-sector median P/E of 32.1x is distorted by Capri (32.1x) and several names with negative earnings (Under Armour, Oxford). The cleanest comparable, G-III Apparel, trades at 12.5x — essentially in line with CRI's CY multiple. So Carter's is not obviously mispriced against the most analogous peer; it is priced about fairly for a structurally challenged apparel name with a margin-recovery option.

The market's own read reinforces "roughly fair." The consensus mean target ($42.67) is modestly above spot, the median ($41.50) roughly at spot, with a $30–$53 range. That distribution implies the sell side sees balanced risk/reward, not a clear discount. Where the bull case lives is in the un-modeled optionality: the IEEPA refund and the contested-control situation, neither of which is in consensus estimates. I am not treating the stock as cheap on fundamentals alone — it is fair-to-slightly-cheap on forward earnings, with two catalysts that are not in the price. That nets to a 5.5 valuation score: an undemanding multiple, but no clean catalyst I can underwrite, and the "cheap apparel name" setup has historically been a value trap absent an independent catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Carter's competitive position is strong in level but deteriorating in trajectory. It remains the dominant U.S. children's apparel brand with unmatched reach and the unusual asset of owning exclusive brands across all three mass retailers. But the franchise is being pressured by a fragmented, intensely competitive market that includes its own wholesale customers' private label, by mall-traffic erosion, and by value-conscious spending, and its five-year TSR (41.57) materially lagged its proxy peer group (68.73), evidence the erosion is company-specific as well as sector-wide.

Customer concentration is a structural vulnerability: top-5 wholesale customers are ~30% of sales, Walmart 11.0% and Target 10.3%, with ~83% of gross receivables sitting with the top 10. The deliberate de-emphasis of Simple Joys at Amazon trades near-term wholesale revenue for brand control, defensible strategically but a headwind to reported wholesale results. The retail comp is the clearest evidence the brand still has pull with consumers when the assortment and pricing are right; whether that translates to share gains or merely stabilizes a structurally shrinking base is unresolved.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs — the dominant near-term swing factor.** ~75% of FY2026 sourcing is from Vietnam, Bangladesh, Cambodia, and India, with ~60% of fabric from China. The Supreme Court invalidated the IEEPA tariffs and Carter's filed its refund claim via CBP's CAPE process, but it is a gain contingency, timing and amount uncertain, and the MD&A flags possible substitute Section 122 tariffs. This cuts both ways: it is the single largest upside optionality and a continuing margin drag if substitute tariffs replace it.

**Secular birth-rate decline** is a permanent drag on the customer funnel, not a cyclical one.

**Customer concentration and the Simple Joys decline** pressure wholesale into FY2026.

**Impairment trip-wire:** the Canada reporting unit's fair value exceeds carrying value by only ~8% (goodwill $38M); triggering-event tests were run twice in FY2025 on stock-price declines. OshKosh tradename already took a $30M FY24 impairment.

**Execution risk** on the store rationalization, headcount reduction, and operating-model overhaul, executed through a leadership transition.

**Governance instability:** two CEO changes in ~14 months (Palladini in April 2025, out after ~12 months effective April 28, 2026; CFO/COO Westenberger interim; Sharon Price John permanent June 15, 2026).

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly cautious but not alarming. Near-term ATM IV (43.0%) is well below 1-year historical volatility (55.1%) — IV is cheap, in the 12th percentile of its own chain, which suggests the market is not pricing an imminent shock and makes protective puts relatively inexpensive. The put/call skew of 9.9% shows elevated put demand, and the 11.0% short interest (2.9 days to cover) confirms meaningful institutional bearish positioning. The term structure is in contango (near-term 43.0% < long-term 50.1%), a normal/complacent shape. (The options-delta feed shows a ~1,805% ATM IV reading that is an obvious data artifact and is disregarded.) The net signal: bearish positioning exists, but the low days-to-cover and the enumerated upside catalysts mean any positive surprise — a refund recognition or a control event — could force covering. This supports the NOT SHORTABLE conclusion below.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$53).** The retail comp momentum sustains; tariff costs roll off as the IEEPA refund is recognized and/or substitute tariffs stay modest; Sharon Price John repeats her Build-A-Bear playbook and restores operating margin toward high-single-digits; and the contested-control situation resolves via an activist value-creation program or a takeout. On normalized $3.65+ NY EPS re-rating toward 14–15x, plus refund optionality, the stock approaches the $53 analyst high (+28% from spot).

**Bear case (~$28–30).** Wholesale margin pressure persists, substitute Section 122 tariffs replace the IEEPA relief, the refund claim stalls, birth-rate decline keeps the top line structurally soft, and the turnaround under a fourth leadership configuration in two years stumbles. The Canada goodwill cushion trips an impairment. On a continued ~5% margin and stale ~$3.00 EPS at 9–10x, the stock returns to the low-$30s/high-$20s (−27% to −32%).

The bear and bull are roughly symmetric in magnitude, and the upside contingencies (refund, control event) are real options the bear case cannot dismiss. That symmetry, plus rising estimates and improving cash flow, defines a balanced HOLD rather than a structural-impairment AVOID. The legitimate bear points — 5.0% operating margin, birth-rate decline, tariff dependence, thin Canada cushion — offset the bull case; they do not dominate it.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** There is no dated negative catalyst. The next earnings report could disappoint on wholesale margin, but estimates are rising and the consumer trend is positive — the recent surprise pattern (207.6% Q1 beat) cuts against a near-term miss thesis.

**Timing window.** None identifiable. The two live catalysts (IEEPA refund recognition, control event) are upside-skewed.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 11.0% of float with only 2.9 days to cover — elevated, but easily covered. A positive surprise (refund recognition, takeout) would force rapid covering.

**Borrow and options signal.** Put skew is elevated (9.9%) but near-term IV is cheap (43.0% vs 55.1% HV, 12th percentile) — the options market is not pricing a collapse, and there is no rich-IV confirmation of a bearish catalyst.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear ~$28–30 (−27% to −32%) against bull ~$53 (+28%) plus refund/control optionality. With upside roughly matching downside and two un-modeled positive contingencies, the asymmetry does not favor the short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** No specific catalyst, rising estimates, improving cash flow, and live takeout/refund optionality. This is a name to size carefully if owned, not to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Reassessment horizon is the next two earnings reports (roughly through early FY2027) and the September 21, 2026 poison-pill expiry, which will clarify whether the contested-control situation resolves.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Recognition of the IEEPA refund (or clear, quantified visibility on substitute Section 122 tariffs being immaterial), removing the gross-margin overhang.
- Wholesale operating income inflecting positive year-over-year, confirming the bottom-line recovery is following the consumer turn, with operating margin tracking back toward high-single-digits.
- A defined control/activist outcome (board accord, strategic review, or bid) that creates a clear value-realization path; or open-market insider buying by the new CEO/board.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- U.S. Retail comps turning negative, breaking the four-quarter streak, indicating the consumer turn has stalled.
- Substitute tariffs replacing IEEPA relief while the refund claim is denied/delayed, locking in the 300bp+ gross-margin drag with no offset.
- A Canada (or OshKosh) goodwill impairment, signaling the thin cushion has tripped.

**Reassessment timeline:** next quarterly report; CEO Sharon Price John's first full quarter under her strategy; and the September 2026 pill expiry.

## Conclusion

I am moving Carter's from AVOID to **HOLD (uncertain)**. The prior bearish stance correctly identified a profit recession and a structural demand headwind, but it under-weighted an actively improving operating trajectory — four consecutive positive U.S. Retail comps, a swing back to positive operating cash flow, +6.5% current-year EPS revisions over 90 days, and a 207.6% Q1 surprise — alongside an undemanding ~12.7x CY / ~11.3x NY forward multiple that sits at the prior reports' own re-entry zone. With the consensus mean target modestly above spot, the sell side sees roughly fair value, not a short.

The deciding factor is asymmetry. The bear case (~$28–30, −27% to −32%) is roughly matched by the bull case (~$53, +28%) and is then exposed to two upside contingencies — the tariff refund and a live contested-control situation with a proven turnaround CEO newly installed — that the bear case cannot dismiss. A name that is "avoid owning" but demonstrably "not shortable" is more honestly a balanced hold. The legitimate concerns (5.0% operating margin, declining birth rates, tariff dependence, thin Canada goodwill cushion, governance instability) offset rather than dominate the recovery case. I hold pending evidence the bottom-line recovery follows the consumer turn, and I would upgrade on tariff-overhang removal plus wholesale margin inflection, or downgrade on a negative comp print plus denied refund.
