**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Voya Financial is a capital-light workplace-benefits, retirement-savings, and asset-management company that emerged from ING's 2013 IPO and has since shed its variable-annuity and individual-life exposure. The investment case today is dominated by a single development: on June 1, 2026, TOMS Capital Investment Management, one of Voya's largest shareholders, sent a public letter to the board demanding a formal review of strategic alternatives including a sale of the company. Bloomberg, Reuters, and the Financial Times confirmed the letter. The stock has re-rated roughly 35% over the trailing twelve months and trades near its 52-week high of $93.05, up from approximately $65 in early March 2026, with most of the move concentrated in the weeks following the activist disclosure.

The underlying business is genuinely improving. FY2025 adjusted operating earnings before tax grew 19% to $1,038M, the Employee Benefits segment's stop-loss loss ratio improved from 94.0% to 83.7%, and Q1 FY2026 adjusted operating EPS rose 13% year-over-year to $2.26 with ROE above 18%. The company beat consensus in three of the last four quarters. At roughly 9.5x current-year consensus EPS of $9.49, Voya carries a discount to its retirement and asset-management peers that the activist argues is anomalous and self-inflicted. The bull thesis rests on a sale or break-up unlocking that gap.

The setup is binary and the easy money has largely been made. The stock now embeds meaningful takeout optionality, and consensus targets sit essentially at spot ($91.83 mean versus $90.15). The activist's own thesis flags real fundamental complications: Full Service retirement net outflows widened to -$9.1B in 2025 (from -$1.25B), the Employee Benefits margin recovery was partly engineered by shrinking the book, excess capital fell to roughly $0.4B after the OneAmerica deal, and Allianz's 24% stake in the asset-management arm complicates a clean break-up. We rate VOYA a HOLD. A confirmed sale process with a credible bidder would push the stock toward the activist's higher targets; a stalled or rejected review reverts it toward standalone fair value in the low-$70s. With the reward and risk roughly symmetric from current levels, we see no asymmetric edge at $90.

## Company Overview

Voya operates three reporting segments. Retirement is the largest profit driver at $959M of FY2025 adjusted operating earnings, anchored by a top-5 U.S. defined-contribution recordkeeping franchise with approximately 10 million participant accounts across roughly 45,000 employers spanning corporate 401(k), education 403(b), healthcare, and government 457 plans. Investment Management contributed $291M including non-controlling interest ($226M excluding), running $360B in AUM ($422B including AUA) with strength in institutional fixed income and private credit; Allianz owns 24% of the IM holding company and distributes Voya IM products internationally. Employee Benefits contributed $152M, a sharp rebound from $40M, and includes group life, disability, voluntary benefits, the Benefitfocus administration platform, and a stop-loss business that ranks third-largest in the U.S. with roughly $1.6B of in-force premium.

Total client assets reached $1.1 trillion at year-end 2025. The competitive set is concentrated and well-capitalized: Fidelity, Empower, TIAA, and Principal in retirement; Invesco, Franklin, and T. Rowe Price in asset management; MetLife, Unum, Sun Life, and Cigna in benefits. Voya's strategic distinction is its capital-light posture following the divestiture of capital-intensive insurance blocks, which supports a higher excess-capital-generation profile than legacy life insurers.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue rose 1.7% to $8,189M from $8,050M. GAAP net income to common declined slightly to $613M from $626M, while the cleaner operating gauge, adjusted operating earnings before tax, grew 19% to $1,038M from $870M. Q1 FY2026 adjusted operating EPS rose 13% year-over-year to $2.26, trailing-twelve-month EPS reached $9.11 (up 20%), and ROE exceeded 18%.

The segment detail is where the quality questions sit. Retirement adjusted earnings rose $139M to $959M, but the growth was acquisition-driven: total DC net flows of +$28.2B were almost entirely the OneAmerica recordkeeping book acquired January 2025, while Full Service organic net flows deteriorated to -$9.1B from -$1.25B. Employee Benefits adjusted earnings nearly quadrupled, with the stop-loss loss ratio improving from 94.0% to 83.7% and segment margin rising from 4.1% to 13.6%. That recovery was achieved partly by shrinking the book: stop-loss sales fell from $607M to $365M as management repriced and exited unprofitable business. Corporate losses widened by $100M to -$305M on higher performance-comp accruals, pension assumption changes, and OneAmerica integration costs.

On capital structure, financial leverage excluding AOCI improved to 27.0% from 30.3% and debt-to-cap stood at 29.8% on $2.1B of total financial debt. The combined insurance-subsidiary RBC ratio of 413% sits comfortably above the 375% target. The watch item is excess capital, which fell to roughly $0.4B at year-end 2025: Voya generated approximately $775M of excess capital in 2025 but deployed it across the OneAmerica acquisition, $174M of dividends, and $200M of buybacks (down from $803M of total return in 2024), with $562M of buyback authorization remaining. Ratings are solidly investment-grade and stable (holding company A-/Baa2/BBB+; insurance subs A+/A2/A+). The general-account investment portfolio of roughly $38B carries a weighted-average A quality with a deliberate 41% BBB tilt toward private credit. Federal NOLs of $5.2B are a real asset but carry Section 382 change-of-control limitations that become directly relevant in a sale scenario.

The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the most recent quarter (Q1 FY2026) beating by 8.0%, partly offset by an 8.5% miss in Q4 2025.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is mixed and warrants careful weighting. Adjusted operating earnings growth of 19% is genuine, but it leans heavily on two non-recurring or non-organic drivers: the OneAmerica integration inflating Retirement assets, and a stop-loss turnaround that came partly from deliberately contracting the book. Strip those, and the underlying organic engine is mid-single-digit at the franchise level.

Estimate revisions tell a two-speed story. Over the trailing 90 days, current-quarter consensus fell 7.0%, next-quarter fell 4.0%, current-year fell 2.6%, and next-year fell 2.3% — modest downward drift. Over the trailing 30 days, estimates have ticked back up: current quarter +0.7%, next quarter +1.8%, current year +1.2%. The recent stabilization is constructive, but it does not meet the threshold of a strong inflection signal: the 90-day trend is still negative, and the company is not a cyclical trough recovery. Current-year consensus EPS sits at $9.49 and next-year at $11.04, implying roughly 16% forward earnings growth, much of it from continued buybacks and full-year OneAmerica accretion rather than organic flow generation.

The durable growth drivers are the sticky DC recordkeeping franchise, which benefits from structural retirement-savings inflows, and the institutional fixed-income/private-credit IM platform, which the activist notes outperformed peers and benchmarks on 78% of assets over three years and 82% over ten. The drag is Full Service retirement retention and the question of whether the core franchise is quietly losing share beneath the acquisition headline. We score growth as solid but not exceptional.

## Valuation Assessment

At $90.15, Voya trades at approximately 9.5x current-year consensus EPS and roughly 8.2x next-year EPS. Against the financials peer median P/E of 13.6, that is a meaningful discount, and the activist frames it as anomalous — below even where Voya would trade as a capital-intensive life insurer, despite its capital-light transformation and mid-teens ROE.

The peer set provided is heterogeneous (a regional bank, a data vendor, an asset manager, two payment-processing/fintech names), so direct read-across is imperfect. More relevant is the activist's comparison to retirement and asset-management peers — Principal, Great-West, Franklin, Invesco, Unum, Sun Life — against which Voya's discount is wider than its historical norm. Closing even part of that gap supports the bull case.

We are deliberately cautious about rewarding the cheapness alone. A low multiple on a financials name with deteriorating organic retention and thin excess capital can be a value trap absent a catalyst, and the historical record favors treating "cheap" as a warning rather than a buy signal. Here, there is a specific catalyst: the public sale campaign, which distinguishes this from a passive value trap. The problem is that the catalyst is largely priced. Consensus targets ($91.83 mean, $91.50 median) sit at spot, and the stock has already moved from $65 to $90 since the activist began accumulating. The $74 low target effectively maps the standalone-no-deal floor; the $117 high target maps a successful full-company sale. The current price is roughly midway, a fair reflection of an uncertain binary outcome rather than a mispricing. We score valuation modestly above neutral — cheap on standalone fundamentals, but with much of the re-rating already captured.

## Competitive Landscape

Voya competes in three consolidating, fee-pressured industries, and its competitive trajectory is broadly stable-to-improving. In retirement recordkeeping, scale matters and Voya's top-5 position with 10 million participant accounts generates sticky, structural inflows; the OneAmerica acquisition extended that scale. The risk is that organic Full Service net flows turned sharply negative, suggesting share leakage in the core book that the acquisition masks.

In asset management, the IM platform's documented outperformance (78% of assets beating benchmark over three years) is a genuine quality marker, and its conservative private-credit posture, avoiding the aggressive yield reach now pressuring parts of the industry, is defensible, though it caps near-term yield upside. The Allianz distribution relationship is an asset internationally but a structural constraint on a clean sale of the segment.

In Employee Benefits, the third-largest stop-loss position is a strong franchise, but the margin recovery came partly from contraction, and the activist's allegation that management privately floated divesting the business after publicly defending it points to internal strategic ambiguity rather than confident moat-widening. The franchise quality is real, but the trajectory questions — organic retirement retention, stop-loss strategic direction — keep this from a higher score. We apply competitive position as a secondary discriminator and require confirmation; the evidence here is genuinely two-sided.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is that the activist process resolves unfavorably. If no credible bidder emerges, if the board resists a full review, or if a Section 382 NOL complication and the Allianz IM stake deter acquirers from a clean transaction, the stock reverts toward standalone fair value. At a 12-13x multiple on current-year EPS, that is roughly $114-123 in a successful sale, versus low-$70s if the takeout premium evaporates and the stock reprices on standalone fundamentals near its historical 8-9x.

Company-specific risks beyond the event:
- **Stop-loss medical-trend risk.** The 2025 earnings swing demonstrates how leveraged the segment is to morbidity and medical inflation; the book is repriced annually but vulnerable to gene-therapy and chronic-condition severity mis-estimation.
- **Full Service retirement outflows** raise organic-retention concerns masked by the OneAmerica headline.
- **Thin excess capital** constrains near-term capital return absent stronger generation, even with a robust RBC ratio.
- **OneAmerica contingent consideration** of up to $160M tied to plan persistency is due in 2026.
- **Benefitfocus** remains a focal point of activist criticism as a dilutive, poorly-fitting acquisition with reduced disclosure; peers have written down similar assets.
- **Section 382 NOL limitation** — the $5.2B federal NOLs could be impaired by a change of control, a factor any acquirer prices and a structural complication for the sale thesis.
- **Allianz's 24% IM stake** ties international distribution to the Allianz relationship and impedes a clean sum-of-parts break-up.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute event stress. Near-term ATM IV of 32.5% is modestly rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 27.3%, but sits at only the 15th percentile within the current chain, and the put/call skew is balanced at -0.8%. The put/call volume ratio of 0.30 and OI ratio of 0.31 lean call-heavy, and the term structure is flat (32.5% near versus 31.5% far), indicating uncertainty is spread evenly rather than concentrated on a near-dated binary. IV has fallen 2.9 points (from 35.3% to 32.5%) even as the stock rose 10.4% since late May, consistent with the market treating the activist situation as a slow-developing catalyst rather than an imminent resolution. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.7%. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float, 21,457 shares), so there is no squeeze dynamic and no crowded bearish positioning. Options sentiment confirms the slow-burn, event-driven character of the setup without flagging an imminent re-rating in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($114-123, with upside to $117 high target)

The activist campaign forces a formal strategic review, and a credible acquirer — one of the asset managers TOMS cites as having signaled M&A appetite, or a strategic buyer for the retirement/IM franchise — emerges. Voya's discount to retirement and IM peers (under 9.5x forward versus a low-double-digit peer range) closes as the market prices a takeout premium. A whole-company sale at 12-13x forward EPS, or a sum-of-parts realization of the high-quality Retirement and IM segments (89% of ex-Corporate earnings), drives the stock to the activist's higher targets. Underlying operating momentum (adjusted earnings +19%, ROE >18%, three of four recent beats) underwrites a fundamental floor independent of the deal.

### Bear Case (low-$70s, roughly -20% from current)

The board defers a full review, or the structural impediments — Section 382 NOL erosion on change of control, the Allianz 24% IM stake blocking a clean break-up, and a thin $0.4B excess-capital position — deter a clean transaction. The takeout premium embedded in the $65-to-$90 move unwinds, and the stock reprices on standalone fundamentals toward its historical 8-9x multiple. Organic Full Service retirement outflows prove to be share leakage rather than noise, and the stop-loss margin recovery normalizes as the book stops shrinking, compressing the recently-quadrupled segment earnings. The stock returns to the low-$70s, near the $74 analyst low target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** The setup is event-driven and binary, with reward and risk roughly symmetric from $90.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) The board publicly initiates a formal strategic review or retains advisors for a sale process; (2) a credible bidder is reported, particularly a strategic acquirer willing to absorb the Section 382 and Allianz complications; (3) the stock pulls back toward the low-$70s on no-deal disappointment while operating fundamentals remain intact, restoring asymmetric upside to standalone fair value plus optionality.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) The board explicitly rejects a strategic review and the activist disengages, removing the catalyst while the stock holds near $90; (2) Full Service retirement net outflows accelerate beyond -$9.1B, confirming organic share loss; (3) a stop-loss loss-ratio reversal back above 90% or a Benefitfocus impairment signals deteriorating segment quality; (4) RBC ratio falls toward the 375% target alongside continued excess-capital depletion below $0.4B.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the board's formal response to the TOMS letter (near-term) and the Q2 FY2026 earnings release, which will update organic flow trends, stop-loss margins, and any strategic-review commentary. The activist situation could resolve over 6-12 months.

We classify this as an uncertain hold rather than a growth or income hold. The 2.1% dividend yield is below the peer median and does not anchor the case, and the appreciation thesis hinges entirely on a binary corporate event that is already substantially priced. There is insufficient evidence to make a confident directional call at $90.

## Conclusion

Voya is a higher-quality franchise than its 9.5x forward multiple suggests, and the operating story — 19% adjusted operating earnings growth, mid-teens ROE, a credible institutional IM platform, and improving Employee Benefits margins — supports the activist's contention that the standalone discount is wide. The trajectory of our scores (5.9 in March, 6.6 in May) reflects genuine fundamental improvement, and the latest data confirms rather than reverses that trend. The 35% twelve-month re-rating, concentrated since the TOMS campaign became public, has converted a value opportunity into a priced event. The stock sits roughly midway between a no-deal floor in the low-$70s and a successful-sale ceiling above $114, with consensus targets pinned at spot. That symmetry, combined with real fundamental complications (organic retirement outflows, engineered stop-loss margins, thin excess capital, NOL and Allianz break-up frictions), leaves no asymmetric edge. We rate VOYA a HOLD and would become buyers on a no-deal pullback toward the low-$70s or on confirmation of a credible sale process, and would step aside if the board forecloses the strategic review.
