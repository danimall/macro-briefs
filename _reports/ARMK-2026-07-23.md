**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Aramark has become a cleaner business since its September 2023 spin-off of the Vestis uniform segment. What remains is a pure-play global food services and facilities management operator generating ~$18.5B in FY25 revenue, split roughly 70/30 between the US and international. The operating story has improved: FY25 continuing-operations revenue grew 6.4%, FY26 organic growth is guided to the high end of a 7-9% range, client retention exceeds 98%, and Q2 FY26 revenue rose 14.7% (though ~3-4 points of that came from a calendar shift and ~2.4 points from FX). International is the standout, up 20.8% in Q2. The company has termed out and repriced its debt, so interest expense is falling ($342M FY25 vs $367M FY24) even as gross debt sits near $6.1B.

The problem is price. The stock has appreciated ~29% over the trailing twelve months and now trades at roughly 42x trailing and ~25x forward EPS ($2.25 consensus for the current year). That is a full multiple for a business with structurally thin margins (net margin ~1.8%, operating margin ~4.3%), a heavily leveraged balance sheet, and limited ability to pass through food and labor inflation on two-thirds of its contract book. The re-rating has correctly rewarded the post-Vestis cleanup and accelerating growth, but it has also priced most of the good news. Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-positive rather than a large positive move that would justify chasing at these levels.

We maintain a HOLD. The growth trajectory is real and the deleveraging-into-growth dynamic is favorable, which is why this is a growth hold rather than an income hold (the 0.85% dividend yield is immaterial). At ~25x forward earnings on a low-margin, levered outsourcing business, the risk/reward is balanced. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$40s where forward multiple compression restores asymmetry, or on evidence that Nexus and international momentum are driving durable margin expansion rather than revenue growth alone.

## Company Overview

Aramark provides food services and facilities management across two reportable segments. FSS United States (~70% of revenue) spans Business & Industry, Education, Healthcare, Sports/Leisure & Corrections, and Facilities. FSS International (~30%) delivers the same range across 15 countries, largest in the UK, Chile, Canada, China, Germany, Spain and Ireland. Roughly 85% of global revenue is food services and 15% facilities.

The business is contract-based. About two-thirds of revenue comes from "profit & loss" contracts where Aramark bears cost and revenue risk, and one-third from management-fee/cost-plus arrangements. This is a scale-driven, low-margin outsourcing model where competitive advantage derives from purchasing power, client relationships, and breadth of service. Primary competitors are the larger Compass Group and Sodexo, regional players, and the persistent alternative of clients self-operating their dining and facilities.

The portfolio reshaping matters for interpreting the numbers. The 2023 Vestis spin-off was a tax-free distribution, and the company also monetized non-core equity stakes: the AIM Services (Japan) 50% interest for $535M and its San Antonio Spurs holding for ~$199M. Reported revenue figures around the spin are distorted; on a continuing-operations basis, growth has been consistent mid-to-high single digits.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Continuing-operations revenue moved from $16.1B (FY23) to $17.4B (FY24) to $18.5B (FY25). FY26 has accelerated: Q2 revenue rose to $4.9B and H1 rose 10.3% to $9.74B, though the headline overstates underlying momentum given the calendar shift and FX tailwinds. Q2 net income jumped 64.8% to $102M ($0.38 diluted vs $0.23), with H1 net income up 18.5% to $198.6M.

**Margins.** Structurally thin and the central constraint on this equity. Cost of services runs 91-92% of revenue, with personnel (~42%) and food (~28%) the largest buckets. Because two-thirds of the book is P&L contracts, food and wage inflation cannot always be passed through, and faster new-business wins carry start-up costs that pressure near-term margins. Revenue growth at this company does not translate one-for-one into earnings leverage.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY25 operating cash flow was $921M against $489M capex, yielding ~$432M FCF (roughly a 3% FCF yield on the current market cap). The company raised its dividend to $0.12/quarter and has a $500M buyback authorization with ~$306M remaining as of April 2026. Cash flow is highly seasonal: H1 is cash-negative on working-capital build (H1 FY26 operating cash was -$382M) with an H2 catch-up from Higher Ed prepayments. FCF should be judged on a full-year basis.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** This is the defining feature. Total debt rose from ~$5.4B at FY25-end to ~$6.1B by April 2026, partly a seasonal working-capital increase including a $625M receivables-facility draw. Against ~$3.3B equity, this is a levered structure. The covenant picture is comfortable: TTM Covenant Adjusted EBITDA of ~$1.57B (up from $1.46B FY25), Consolidated Secured Debt Ratio of 2.65x against a 5.125x covenant, and interest coverage of 4.57x against a 2.0x minimum. Management has aggressively refinanced (Term B-10 loans due 2030 repriced to SOFR+1.75%, €400M 4.375% notes due 2033, all 2025 maturities repaid) and roughly $2.5B of variable exposure is hedged via swaps. This is a deleveraging-into-growth credit, not a distressed one, but the leverage leaves little room for an operational stumble.

(The automated Altman Z-Score of 100 and ROA of 128% in the raw data are clearly scaling artifacts and should be disregarded.)

## Growth Analysis

The growth vectors are credible. Both segments are expanding adjusted operating income, and international is running well ahead of the US, reflecting new wins in the UK, Spain, Chile and Germany. Business & Industry has posted +20-23% growth recently, benefiting from return-to-office trends that increase on-site dining density.

Aramark Nexus, launched in May 2026, is a premium hospitality and food-services offering for workforce communities at hyperscale AI data centers. In July 2026, Nexus was selected by a leading AI data-center colocation provider to serve workers across multiple sites. This gives a traditionally slow-growth food-services name a tie to the AI infrastructure buildout, with the caveat that Aramark is the services vendor to data-center operators, not an AI or data-center company. The demand for on-site feeding and hospitality at remote hyperscale sites is a new end-market, but it is early, unquantified, and not yet disclosed as material revenue.

The counterweight to the growth story is that estimate revisions are flat. Current-year EPS consensus of $2.25 has moved +1.1% over 90 days and -0.0% over 30 days; next-year is +2.9% over 90 days. This is stable, modestly positive earnings momentum, not a large positive revision velocity that would signal systematic underestimation. The earnings track record is mixed: two in-line quarters and two misses over the trailing four, including a -14.4% miss in the September 2025 quarter. Growth is real, but earnings momentum is muted in the revision data even as revenue accelerates, which is consistent with the thin-margin reality: top-line strength is not fully converting to EPS beats.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$56.63, ARMK trades at ~42x trailing EPS ($1.22 reported, distorted by the Vestis transition and seasonality) and ~25x forward EPS. Even on next-year's $2.69 consensus, the forward multiple is ~21x. For a business with a 1.8% net margin, mid-to-high single-digit organic growth, and material leverage, this is a full valuation.

The peer comparison is muddled by the fact that Aramark is classified alongside distributors (GPC, POOL, LKQ) that are not close operational comps. Its true peers are Compass Group and Sodexo, larger and higher-margin food-services operators. Against the listed distributor set (peer median P/E 17.6), ARMK carries a substantial premium, though that comparison is weak given the different business models. The more relevant point is that Compass and Sodexo are larger and higher-margin; Aramark's thinner margins and narrower geographic footprint are structural disadvantages relative to those global leaders.

The stock is not cheap, and the valuation dimension does not offer asymmetric upside at the current price. On a growth-adjusted basis, ~25x forward P/E against high-single-digit organic growth is unattractive unless margin expansion or Nexus materially inflects the earnings power. Analyst targets bracket the current price (low $40.00, mean $58.94, median $59.75, high $70.50), with the stock trading essentially at the mean. On a premium-multiple, non-inflecting name like this, proximity to consensus is not a floor; it reflects the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price. The appreciation over twelve months has been earned by fundamental improvement, but the re-rating has now captured most of the good news. We would need a pullback toward the low-$40s (roughly 18x forward) to see genuinely asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Aramark operates in a global oligopoly with Compass Group and Sodexo, both larger and higher-margin, plus regional players and the ever-present alternative of client self-operation. Structural tailwinds favor large multi-service providers: the secular outsourcing trend and preferred-vendor consolidation reward breadth and scale. Against this, Aramark carries a narrower geographic footprint than some competitors, a disadvantage on multinational bids.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than clearly gaining share. The retention rate and accelerating international wins suggest the competitive position is holding and expanding at the margin, and Nexus positions the company early in a new data-center end-market. The company is not the scale leader, its margins trail the leaders, and structural risks (offsite food-delivery aggregators disrupting on-site dining, rising ESG table-stakes requirements, and the persistent self-operation alternative) cap the upside on this dimension. This is a solid but not widening moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** The top risk. Debt near $6.1B with variable tranches, and covenants that restrict dividends, buybacks and acquisitions. The covenant cushion is comfortable today (2.65x secured leverage vs 5.125x), but a material EBITDA deterioration would compress it and could force refinancing at worse terms. The swap-hedged variable exposure remains a live consideration.

**Contract economics.** Two-thirds of revenue is P&L contracts with limited pass-through; labor at ~42% of costs makes minimum-wage increases and tight labor markets a direct hit. Faster growth pressures near-term margins via start-up costs.

**Client retention.** Contracts are often terminable on short notice and renewals frequently come at less favorable terms. Retention is the single number to watch.

**Supplier concentration.** Sysco distributes ~43% of US/Canada food and non-food purchases, a genuine single-point-of-failure dependency.

**Vestis litigation.** A securities class action was amended in November 2024 to add Aramark and CEO Zillmer as defendants; the motion to dismiss was denied September 30, 2025, so the case proceeds. This is a specific, active overhang with headline and settlement risk that is not sized as material by management but is worth monitoring.

**Cyclical exposure.** Sports, leisure and B&I revenue is tied to event attendance, corporate spending and return-to-office trends, swing factors that would be exposed by any macro deterioration in consumer and mid-market discretionary spending.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 33.8% is rich versus 26.0% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is in backwardation (33.8% near-term vs 27.1% at six months), signaling the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty around the next earnings print. The implied one-month move is ±8.2%. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.2% near-term, drifting to -3.2% at six months), and P/C open interest is low at 0.18, not a bearish positioning tell. ATM IV has risen from 24.9% to 33.8% since early June, consistent with an upcoming catalyst. The options market is not signaling directional bearishness, but it is pricing elevated event uncertainty, which argues against paying a full multiple immediately ahead of the print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$70).** International growth sustains above 15%, Nexus scales into a disclosed, material revenue line riding data-center capex, return-to-office continues to lift B&I density, and organic growth holds at the high end of the guide while margins expand modestly as new-business start-up costs anniversary. Interest expense keeps falling, deleveraging accelerates, and the market pays ~28-30x a rising EPS base approaching $2.70+. This aligns with the $70.50 street high.

**Bear case (~$40).** A cyclical downturn hits sports/leisure and B&I attendance, food and labor inflation compresses the thin margin further, a client-retention slip below 98% surfaces, or the Vestis litigation produces an adverse settlement. On a levered, low-margin business, even a modest EBITDA disappointment de-rates the multiple sharply. This maps to the $40.00 street low, roughly a 29% decline from current levels.

The balance between these cases is roughly symmetric. The growth is credible but muted in the revision data; the leverage is well-managed but leaves no room for error; and the valuation has already captured the re-rating. That is the definition of a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the fiscal Q3 FY26 earnings print (likely August 2026), where the options market is pricing a ±8% move. Full-year FCF and organic-growth delivery against the high-end guide are the key data points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$40s (~18x forward EPS), restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Evidence of durable margin expansion (operating margin trending above ~4.5% with organic growth intact) rather than revenue growth alone, or Nexus disclosed as a material, quantified revenue line.
- EPS estimate revisions inflecting to +5% or more over a rolling 90 days, signaling the market is underestimating earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Client retention slipping below 97%, or a guidance cut to the low end of the organic range.
- Secured leverage rising toward 3.5x on EBITDA deterioration, or a covenant-cushion compression that constrains buybacks/dividends.
- An adverse development in the Vestis litigation (unfavorable ruling or material settlement reserve).

## Conclusion

Aramark is a materially better business than it was two years ago: a cleaner pure-play with accelerating organic growth, strong retention, falling interest expense, and an early foothold in the AI data-center services market via Nexus. The score trajectory (4.9 in March to ~5.5 in June) reflects that improving picture, and this report confirms rather than reverses that trend, with Growth Prospects and Risk Factors having genuinely firmed.

The obstacle is price. At ~25x forward earnings on a 1.8%-net-margin, ~$6.1B-levered outsourcing business, the twelve-month re-rating has captured the good news, and estimate revisions are flat rather than inflecting. The cyclical portions of the revenue base remain exposed to any deterioration in consumer and mid-market discretionary spending. This is a growth hold: worth owning through the deleveraging-into-growth story if already positioned, but not a name to chase at a full multiple ahead of an earnings print the options market is bracing for. We maintain HOLD and would re-engage constructively on a pullback toward the low-$40s or on confirmed margin expansion.
