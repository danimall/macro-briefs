**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Public Service Enterprise Group is a predominantly regulated New Jersey utility holding company that pairs a stable rate-base growth engine (PSE&G) with a ~3,500 MW merchant nuclear fleet selling into PJM. The structural setup is attractive on paper: PJM faces a projected ~6 GW capacity shortfall by 2027 as AI data-center load outruns interconnection capacity, which is tightening capacity-market prices and raising the value of always-on, carbon-free nuclear baseload. PSEG owns scarce assets on the supply-favored side of this energy-market dynamic, and New Jersey's April 2026 lift of its new-nuclear moratorium opens long-dated optionality.

The complication is that the same dynamic that benefits PSEG Power makes PSE&G the focus of affordability anger. New Jersey electricity rates rose ~16.9% in 2025, among the worst nationally; a new state administration issued January 2026 executive orders directing the BPU to mitigate increases; a new law prohibits summer disconnections (raising bad-debt expense); and the BPU's affiliate/management audit remains unresolved. Layered on top are FERC actions ordering a transmission cost re-settlement back to 2015 and a new formula-rate audit. The regulatory compact that powers the 6.0-7.5% rate-base CAGR is under more pressure than it has been in years. Near-term estimates are drifting lower (current-quarter and next-quarter EPS revised down ~5% over 90 days), and the stock has spent the last twelve months in a 76-91 range with no net progress.

We rate PEG a HOLD on an income basis. The ~3.3% well-covered dividend, IG-stable balance sheet, robust operating cash flow (~$1.27B in Q1 2026), and nuclear optionality justify owning the shares. The appreciation case requires either a signed data-center PPA (none exists as of the Q1 2026 10-Q) or a clean resolution of the affordability/regulatory overhang, neither of which is dated. At ~17.6x trailing earnings against a peer median near 22.5x, the discount partly reflects these unresolved risks rather than a clean mispricing.

## Company Overview

PSEG operates through two reportable segments:

- **PSE&G (regulated utility)** — Transmits electricity and distributes electricity and natural gas to ~2.3 million electric and ~1.9 million gas customers in New Jersey, regulated by the NJ Board of Public Utilities on distribution and FERC on transmission. This segment is the primary value driver, generating $577M of the company's $741M net income (78%) in Q1 2026.
- **PSEG Power & Other** — A pure-play merchant nuclear business following the 2022 exit from fossil generation. Operates ~3,500 MW of nuclear capacity (Salem 1 & 2, Hope Creek, plus an interest in Peach Bottom) selling into PJM, backed by a federal nuclear Production Tax Credit floor (2024-2032, up to ~$15/MWh) that provides downside price protection. Also includes PSEG Long Island, which operates LIPA's T&D system under a fee-based contract recently extended through 2030 at ~$43M/year.

The strategic positioning is a regulated utility with a nuclear option: stable rate-base growth plus optionality on the nuclear fleet tied to surging data-center power demand within PJM.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 results were strong on a reported basis. Operating revenue rose to $3,848M from $3,222M (+19%), net income to $741M from $589M (+26%), and diluted EPS to $1.48 from $1.18. Operating cash flow rose to $1,271M from $1,049M (+21%).

A meaningful portion of the YoY net-income improvement came from a smaller mark-to-market hedging drag ($30M after-tax loss vs. $135M in Q1 2025) rather than purely from operations. This MTM line is a recurring non-cash distortion; analysts should focus on operating (non-GAAP) earnings. The MTM value-at-risk spiked to $314M (95% confidence) in Q1 2026 versus $71M for full-year 2025, signaling that commodity-price volatility in the portfolio will keep injecting reported-earnings noise.

The balance sheet carries leverage typical of a capital-intensive regulated utility: total assets $57.9B, net PP&E $42.4B, long-term debt $22.7B plus $1.6B short-term and $425M current maturities, against $17.3B of equity. The low Altman Z-Score (~0.95) that screening tools flag is structural for the sector, not a distress signal: operating cash flow is robust, the dividend is well-covered, and credit ratings are solidly investment grade with stable outlooks (PSEG Baa2/BBB; PSE&G A1/A; PSEG Power Baa2/BBB). One contained liquidity tail: a two-notch downgrade of PSEG Power below IG would trigger ~$691M in additional collateral postings, manageable against $3.5B of available credit and not imminent given the stable outlook.

Capital allocation is overwhelmingly directed to rate base. The dividend was raised to $0.67/quarter ($2.68 annualized, ~3.3% yield). The buyback is modest and exists solely to offset equity-comp dilution (865,000 shares in Q1 2026), not as a return lever. Like most regulated growth utilities, PSEG funds its capital program partly through external financing, so per-share growth runs below rate-base growth.

The earnings-surprise record is consistent: three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating by 6.9% ($1.55 vs. $1.45 estimate). This supports reasonable confidence in delivery against full-year guidance even as near-term quarterly estimates soften.

## Growth Analysis

Management guides to a $22.5-25.5B regulated capital program for 2026-2030, driving a 6.0-7.5% rate-base CAGR off a ~$36B base at end-2025 (rate base grew from ~$34B in 2024 to ~$36B in 2025). The total capital program is $24-28B. The upper end of the range is tied to incremental investments addressing continued demand growth, which is where the data-center theme intersects the regulated business.

The growth thesis has three components of differing quality:

1. **Regulated rate-base growth** is the durable, high-confidence engine, but it converts to per-share EPS growth at a lower rate because of external equity/debt financing of the program. Full-year consensus EPS is essentially flat-to-stable: current-year $4.38 (vs. $4.37 ninety days ago) and next-year $4.70 (vs. $4.72). This translates to mid-single-digit EPS compounding, consistent with the long-term utility growth algorithm.

2. **Merchant nuclear in a tightening PJM** is the upside lever. PJM's December 2025 auction cleared at the FERC-approved $333.44/MW-day cap, with PJM stating the uncapped price would have been $529.80. PSEG cleared 3,500 MW at $270, $329, and $333/MW-day across the next three delivery years, a multi-year tailwind to PSEG Power margins.

3. **The data-center PPA optionality** is the highest-variance component. FERC's 2025 co-location order and PJM's February 2026 compliance filing build the rule-making framework, and New Jersey's April 2026 nuclear-moratorium lift opens a long-term avenue. As of the Q1 2026 10-Q, no data-center PPA has been signed. This is optionality, not contracted growth, and the report's appreciation case should not capitalize a deal that does not yet exist.

The near-term estimate trend is negative: current-quarter EPS revised to $0.82 from $0.86 (-4.9% over 90 days) and next-quarter to $1.15 from $1.22 (-5.5%). This is the opposite of the positive revision velocity that would justify aggressive forward weighting, and it tempers the growth score.

PSEG's closest analytical comparison is to a regulated utility (justifying a multiple toward the peer median) carrying a merchant nuclear option that the market is not fully crediting. The discount to median is therefore partly justified by the affordability/regulatory headwind, the higher leverage, and the dilution that caps per-share conversion of rate-base growth, and partly an unpriced nuclear-scarcity option.

We do not treat the discount as a clean mispricing. A cheap multiple in a regulated utility with intensifying regulatory pressure is as likely to reflect those risks as to signal undervaluation; the catalyst (a signed PPA, a constructive base-rate-case outcome, a clean BPU audit resolution) is required before the discount closes. The analyst price-target band of $75 low / $89 median / $97 high implies modest upside to consensus, with the current price near the bottom of the range. With near-term estimates drifting lower rather than rising, we do not treat consensus targets as floors here; that principle applies to stocks with rising estimates, which PSEG is not on a quarterly basis.

A reasonable base case applies ~18-19x to ~$4.55 normalized forward EPS, yielding roughly $82-86, modestly above the current ~$79.40, with the bulk of total return coming from the dividend plus mid-single-digit EPS compounding rather than multiple expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

PSE&G is a regulated monopoly with a historically constructive framework, and the nuclear fleet provides >80% of New Jersey's carbon-free electricity. The competitive trajectory is favorable on the supply side and stable-to-pressured on the regulated side.

PSEG operates entirely within PJM, the largest U.S. grid (65M people, 13 states), which is in a structural supply/demand squeeze. PJM projects a ~6 GW shortfall by 2027 with 800+ projects in a revamped interconnection queue. Data-center load has driven a ~76% surge in PJM power prices per the market monitor, which warns the impact on customers is not reversible. As an owner of scarce, always-on, carbon-free baseload, PSEG Power is a structural winner from tight capacity markets, and the cleared capacity prices through three delivery years lock in part of that benefit.

The competitive risk is not displacement (a regulated monopoly does not lose customers) but compression of the regulatory compact. The 16.9% 2025 rate spike has made PSE&G a political target, and a sustained period of mitigation orders, slowed capital approvals, or reduced allowed ROEs would erode the rate-base growth algorithm at the margin. The trajectory is therefore bifurcated: improving on the merchant-nuclear side, modestly deteriorating on the regulated-political side. On balance the moat is intact and the structural tailwinds are real, supporting a moderately above-average competitive score, but the regulatory pressure prevents a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Affordability backlash / regulatory-political risk (the #1 risk).** A newly elevated 10-Q risk factor cites resource-adequacy challenges that could prompt policymaker responses. A new law bars disconnections for non-payment June 15-Aug 31 annually (bad-debt allowance rose to $259M from $234M). This could compress allowed returns or slow capital-program approvals, directly threatening the rate-base CAGR.

2. **PJM capacity-market dysfunction / collection risk.** High capacity prices benefit PSEG Power but flow through to PSE&G customers via BGS auctions, worsening affordability. A January 2026 13-governor statement of principles and a planned September 2026 reliability backstop auction introduce uncertainty around who funds new capacity.

3. **FERC transmission disputes (newly active).** A March 2026 FERC order directs PJM to recalculate transmission cost responsibility and resettle back to 2015. A separate intervenor complaint targets Roseland-Pleasant Valley cost recovery (already carrying a $6.6M penalty), and an April 2026 FERC audit of PSE&G's formula rate is underway. Loss of the 50bp PJM ROE adder would cut net income ~$40M/year.

4. **Nuclear PTC guidance uncertainty.** The IRA nuclear PTC hinges on still-undefined Treasury guidance on the "gross receipts" phase-out threshold; ZEC refund adjustments could be material.

5. **Environmental Superfund liabilities (large, open-ended).** Lower Passaic River cleanup totals ~$2.85B across PRPs with only ~$66M accrued; additional MGP, Newark Bay, Hackensack River, and CCR exposures are "likely material in the aggregate."

6. **Litigation.** A July 2025 putative Sherman Act class action against 26 nuclear generators including PSEG alleges nuclear-worker wage-fixing and seeks treble damages.

The cluster of regulatory and FERC items is what pushes the risk-factor score below the midpoint: these are specific, active, and only partially priced, with several resolving on uncertain timelines.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm. Near-term ATM IV is 19.3% against 18.9% one-year historical vol (fairly priced, 2nd percentile within the current chain), with a flat term structure and a small put/call skew of 3.2% indicating mild put demand. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±4.6%, and the put/call volume ratio of 0.14 is light. The reported spike in the options-delta snapshot (ATM IV "233.5%") is a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. The market is not pricing a near-term binary, consistent with a low-beta utility whose key catalysts (base-rate case, BPU audit, possible PPA) are not on a fixed near-term clock. The longer-dated tenors show modestly elevated put activity (6m P/C OI 2.83), which fits a market hedging the affordability/regulatory overhang into the back half of the year.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95-97).** PSEG signs a Constellation/Talen-style nuclear PPA with a hyperscaler at premium pricing, crystallizing the merchant-nuclear option; the BPU audit resolves benignly; the next base-rate case lands constructively; PJM capacity tailwinds compound; and the market re-rates the regulated business toward the peer median (~22.5x) while crediting the nuclear scarcity value. Power uprates at Salem 1 & 2 and 20-year license extensions extend the runway. This requires the regulatory overhang to clear and a deal to be consummated.

**Bear Case ($66-70).** NJ political pressure forces mitigation that compresses allowed ROEs and slows the capital program, cutting the rate-base CAGR below 6%; the FERC transmission re-settlement and ROE-adder loss reduce net income; affordability-driven bad-debt expense rises; no data-center PPA materializes; and a higher-for-longer rate path compresses the multiple while raising the cost of the external financing the capital program depends on. This represents roughly 12-17% downside from current levels.

**Base Case ($82-86).** Rate base compounds at the low-to-middle of the guided range, EPS grows mid-single-digits to ~$4.55 normalized forward, the dividend rises with earnings, capacity tailwinds offset some regulatory drag, and the multiple holds near 18-19x. Total return is dominated by the ~3.3% yield plus EPS growth, with limited multiple expansion absent a PPA catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, anchored to (a) the next NJ base-rate-case milestones, (b) the BPU management-audit determination, (c) the September 2026 PJM reliability backstop auction, and (d) any 8-K disclosure of a data-center PPA.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A signed, disclosed nuclear PPA with a hyperscaler (verified via primary 8-K), materially de-risking the merchant-nuclear option.
- A constructive base-rate-case outcome or benign BPU audit resolution that confirms the 6.0-7.5% rate-base CAGR survives intact.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions turning positive (current-quarter and next-quarter trend reversing from the current ~-5% 90-day drift) alongside a multiple still below ~18x.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A BPU/political outcome that explicitly compresses allowed ROEs or forces a capital-program slowdown that cuts the rate-base CAGR below ~5%.
- Loss of the 50bp PJM ROE adder plus an adverse transmission re-settlement, combining for a material (>$60M/year) net-income hit.
- A PSEG Power credit-outlook deterioration toward the downgrade threshold that activates collateral-posting strain.

This is an income hold: own it for the well-covered ~3.3% yield, the IG balance sheet, and embedded nuclear optionality, while recognizing the appreciation case is gated on catalysts that are not yet dated.

## Conclusion

The score has trended down (6.8 in February to 6.2 in May), and the new data largely confirms rather than reverses that drift. Financial health remains solid on cash-flow and dividend coverage but is appropriately marked in the mid-5s for structural leverage and reliance on external financing in a higher-for-longer rate environment. The regulatory and FERC risk cluster has if anything intensified since the prior reads (the March 2026 transmission re-settlement order, the April 2026 formula-rate audit), and near-term estimates are falling, both of which weigh on the growth and risk dimensions. Offsetting this, the merchant-nuclear scarcity value and the New Jersey nuclear-moratorium lift are genuine, underpriced positives that prevent a lower mark.

We maintain HOLD with an income rationale and an overall score of 5.85. The stock offers a reasonable total-return profile dominated by yield and mid-single-digit EPS growth, trading at a justified-to-modest discount to peers. We would turn more constructive on a signed data-center PPA or a clean resolution of the affordability overhang, and more cautious if NJ political pressure converts into compressed allowed returns or a slowed capital program. At ~$79.40, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.
