## Executive Summary

Eagle Bancorp is a $800M market cap community bank headquartered in Bethesda, Maryland, operating exclusively in the Washington, D.C. metropolitan area with heavy concentration in commercial real estate lending. The company lost $138M in 2025 (EPS -$4.55) due to catastrophic credit losses on its CRE office portfolio, driven by structural declines in DC-area office valuations and accelerated loan dispositions. Q1 2026 marked a return to profitability ($14.7M net income, $0.48 EPS), NIM expanded to 2.47%, and CRE concentration fell below the 300% regulatory threshold for the first time.

However, the recovery thesis faces material ongoing risks that the market is correctly pricing. Nonperforming assets rose $22M in Q1 2026 to $130.8M despite the turnaround narrative, with $61.6M in new NPA inflows. The current administration's explicit policy to reduce the federal government's real estate footprint represents a structural headwind unique to EGBN's collateral base. An undisclosed DOJ investigation carries unquantified enforcement risk, the CEO has announced retirement by December 2026 without a named successor, and the options market is pricing extreme near-term stress (84% IV in steep backwardation). At 0.70x book value, the discount reflects specific, ongoing risks rather than an opportunity the market is missing.

## Company Overview

Eagle Bancorp operates EagleBank, a Maryland-chartered community bank with twelve branch offices and four lending centers across Suburban Maryland, Washington D.C., and Northern Virginia. As of Q1 2026, the bank held $10.5B in total assets, $7.3B in loans, and $9.1B in deposits.

The loan portfolio composition reveals the source of the crisis:
- Income-producing CRE: 46% ($3.35B)
- Owner-occupied CRE: 22% ($1.60B)
- Commercial (C&I): 18% ($1.34B)
- Construction: 12% ($0.90B)
- Office CRE specifically: ~$576M (7.9% of total loans)

This is a CRE-concentrated bank in a single metro area experiencing structural demand destruction in its core collateral type.

## Financial Analysis

**The Credit Crisis (2024-2025):**

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 (Ann.) |
|--------|------|------|------|----------------|
| Net Income | $100.5M | -$47.0M | -$138.1M | $58.8M |
| EPS | $3.31 | -$1.56 | -$4.55 | $1.92 |
| Provision | $31.5M | $66.4M | $293.1M | $53.6M |
| NCOs | $18.9M | $38.6M | $248.2M | $104M |
| NCOs/Avg Loans | 0.24% | 0.48% | 3.22% | 1.46% |

The 3.22% annual charge-off rate in 2025 is extreme for any bank and typically associated with existential stress. Management accelerated this through a "revised strategy for resolving criticized and classified loans" that front-loaded losses.

**Capital Adequacy:** CET1 of 13.80% provides a 730bps buffer above the 6.5% regulatory minimum. This is the primary bull case for survival — the bank can absorb another $300-400M in losses before capital becomes constrained. Total book value sits at $37.56/share.

**Funding:** The bank eliminated all FHLB advances and reduced brokered deposits (36% at year-end 2025, declining). However, 25% of deposits ($2.3B) remain uninsured, and the company issued $76.4M in 10% senior notes in September 2024, evidence of how distressed the market perceived the company at that time.

**Pre-Provision Net Revenue:** PPNR of $27.7M in Q1 2026 annualizes to ~$110M, which could cover provisioning needs if credit quality stabilizes. Management guides NIM of 2.6%-2.8% for 2026 (vs. 2.47% in Q1), suggesting further improvement.

**Earnings Trajectory:** Consensus estimates project $1.70 EPS for 2026 and $2.62 for 2027. At $26.38, this implies ~15.5x 2026E and ~10.1x 2027E earnings. The forward P/E of -6.4x on trailing losses is meaningless; the relevant metric is the forward multiple on normalized earnings, which appears reasonable if normalization occurs.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis depends on three elements:

1. **NIM Expansion:** Management guides 2.6%-2.8% NIM for 2026, implying 13-33bps of expansion from Q1's 2.47%. Drivers include declining wholesale funding costs and loan repricing.

2. **C&I Diversification:** C&I loans grew 5.2% QoQ in Q1 2026. The strategy to reduce CRE concentration below regulatory thresholds and grow the commercial book is directionally correct but early-stage.

3. **Expense Discipline:** Guidance for flat to -4% noninterest expense combined with 15-25% noninterest income growth would produce meaningful operating leverage.

Against these positives: the bank is shrinking its loan book (total loans declining as CRE runs off faster than C&I grows), deposit franchise value has been impaired by the crisis, and the competitive position in the DC market has weakened through reputational damage and management turnover.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current year EPS estimates up 7% over 90 days, next year up 3%. Directionally encouraging but does not reach the +30% threshold that would constitute a strong signal.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | EGBN | Peer Median |
|--------|------|-------------|
| P/E (Forward) | 15.5x (2026E) | 13.0x |
| P/B | 0.70x | ~0.9-1.0x |
| Dividend Yield | 0.15% | 3.7% |

The 0.70x book value appears cheap versus peers at face value. However, several factors justify the discount:

1. **Book value may be overstated.** With $576M in remaining office CRE and NPAs still rising, additional impairments could reduce tangible book. If the bank takes another $100-150M in cumulative losses (plausible given remaining classified assets of $738M), book would compress to ~$33-34/share, making current P/B closer to 0.78-0.80x.

2. **No dividend income.** Peers yield 3.4-4.4%. EGBN's $0.01/quarter dividend is essentially zero, eliminating the income component that supports community bank valuations.

3. **Earnings quality is uncertain.** The path from -$4.55 to +$1.70 depends on provisioning normalization. If NPAs continue rising (as they did in Q1 2026), provisions may exceed the $13.4M Q1 level, compressing actual EPS below consensus.

4. **The 10% senior notes** cost ~$7.6M annually and mature in 2029. This is an ongoing drag that peers don't carry.

The stock is not obviously cheap when adjusted for these factors. The market is pricing approximately $340M in additional expected losses beyond current reserves — given $738M in classified assets and a DC office market facing federal government withdrawal, this is a defensible estimate.

## Competitive Landscape

Eagle Bancorp's competitive position has deteriorated through the crisis:

- **Reputational damage:** KBRA downgrade with Negative Outlook; public losses of this magnitude reduce counterparty/depositor confidence
- **Management turnover:** At least 6 executive departure filings in 2025-2026, with CEO retiring without successor
- **Dividend elimination:** Removes a key attraction for the community bank investor base
- **Geographic concentration:** 100% DC metro, with the specific economic headwind of federal government downsizing

Positives:
- The 12-branch lean model remains operationally efficient
- C&I growth (+5.2% QoQ) demonstrates the franchise retains relationship value
- $4.3B in available liquidity (195% of uninsured deposits) provides safety

The trajectory is concerning: a challenger bank model that has become a problem-asset workout vehicle, competing for new C&I relationships against institutions that didn't experience public credit losses.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Ongoing CRE Office Deterioration (CRITICAL):** NPAs rose $22M in Q1 2026 with $61.6M in new inflows. The performing office coverage ratio was *reduced* from 12.89% to 7.39% as overlays were released. If DC office values decline further (probable given federal government withdrawal), the released reserves may prove premature.

**2. Federal Government Real Estate Reduction (STRUCTURAL):** The 10-K explicitly identifies "actions to reduce the real estate footprint of the federal government" as a risk. For a bank whose collateral base is DC metro office/CRE, this is a uniquely adverse structural headwind with no comparable peer risk.

**3. DOJ Investigation (UNQUANTIFIED):** $10M provision may be insufficient. The investigation is by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Middle District of Pennsylvania. Open enforcement risk without visibility on timeline or magnitude.

**4. CEO Succession (EXECUTION):** Susan Riel retiring by December 2026, no public successor named. Executing a turnaround without continuity of leadership during the most critical phase creates meaningful execution risk.

**5. Elevated Short Interest (10.2%, 8.3 days to cover):** Institutions are positioned for further downside. The days-to-cover ratio suggests any negative catalyst could produce sustained selling pressure.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing extreme near-term stress: **84% implied volatility vs. 47.6% historical vol** (a 36.4% spread), with the term structure in **steep backwardation** (84% near-term vs. 44.3% at 6 months). This pricing structure implies the market expects a binary event or material news within weeks. The 8.7% put skew confirms elevated demand for downside protection. A 3-month implied move of ±13.4% suggests the market assigns meaningful probability to the stock reaching either ~$23 or ~$30 by mid-June.

This options structure is inconsistent with a benign recovery thesis and consistent with unresolved credit/legal risks creating potential for another sharp negative event.

**Verdict: SHORT CANDIDATE** — Specific catalysts (Q2 earnings, DOJ resolution), manageable squeeze risk, options market confirming stress, and 2:1+ risk/reward. Suggested position: 1-2% of portfolio with stop-loss at $30 (52-week high $29.26 provides natural resistance). The trade works best as a 3-6 month position spanning Q2 earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (conditions that would change the AVOID recommendation):**
- Three consecutive quarters of declining NPAs (not achieved — Q1 2026 showed increase)
- DOJ investigation resolved with costs within accrual
- Named CEO successor with relevant turnaround experience
- DC metro office vacancy rates stabilizing (currently still rising)
- Stock trading below 0.55x tangible book ($20.50 or lower) — at that level, capital buffer provides sufficient margin of safety

**Timeline:** Structural headwinds (federal government real estate reduction, DC office weakness) persist for 12-24+ months minimum. The bank may eventually normalize earnings, but the path is longer and more uncertain than consensus estimates imply.

## Conclusion

Eagle Bancorp presents a value trap at current levels. The 0.70x P/B multiple reflects specific, ongoing risks that have not resolved: rising NPAs despite the turnaround, structural demand destruction in DC office collateral from federal workforce reductions, an open DOJ investigation, imminent CEO departure without succession, and options markets pricing extreme near-term stress. The bank's 13.8% CET1 ratio means it will survive, but survival and attractive investment returns are distinct outcomes. At ~15.5x 2026E earnings with no dividend, no insider buying, and 10% short interest, the risk/reward skews negatively.
