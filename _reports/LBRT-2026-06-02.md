**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Liberty Energy is one of North America's largest hydraulic fracturing and completions providers, operating roughly 40 active frac fleets across all major U.S. shale basins plus Western Canada and Australia. The company is in the middle of a strategic transformation: it is using its existing natural-gas fueling and generation capability (Liberty Power Innovations, or LPI) to build a behind-the-meter power business targeting AI/data-center demand, with a stated goal of deploying ~3 GW of power projects by 2029. The investment case has shifted within a single year from a low-debt cyclical frac compounder to a leveraged build-out of a speculative power platform funded by 0% convertible notes.

The current setup is genuinely two-sided. On the constructive side, consensus EPS estimates have moved sharply upward — current-year estimates moved from -$0.46 ninety days ago to +$0.31 today, and next-year estimates from -$0.31 to +$0.35. The frac business appears to have bottomed in late 2025, oil is sitting elevated on the Strait of Hormuz disruption (WTI averaged ~$99.85 through April 20 per the Q1 filing), and Q1 2026 showed revenue stabilization (+4% YoY to $1.02B) with management guiding to sequential growth in Q2. The power optionality is a call option on a structural demand theme, with a Vantage Data Centers agreement (≥1 GW over five years, 400 MW reserved for 2027) anchoring it.

On the cautionary side, the underlying economics are weaker than the headline suggests. 2025 operating income collapsed 81% to $73M and reported net income of $148M was flattered by a $162.6M non-cash gain on equity investments (Oklo, Tamboran) that has since reversed. The balance sheet levered up ~$1B in eight weeks, with $600M more bridge debt authorized through June 30, 2026. A flagship 330 MW Texas power project was already terminated in March 2026. The stock has run 148% off its 52-week low and trades at ~32x trailing earnings against $73M of true operating income. We rate LBRT a HOLD with a growth lean, anchored to the Q2 2026 sequential-growth catalyst and the estimate-revision turn, but we cannot justify a BUY at this multiple with these execution and balance-sheet risks.

## Company Overview

Liberty operates a single-segment completions-services business with meaningful vertical integration: it owns two Permian sand mines, runs proppant logistics (PropConnect, wet-sand handling), and holds proprietary fracture-design databases and ~500 patents. Its differentiated equipment includes dual-fuel DGB fleets and next-generation digiFleets (digiFrac electric and digiPrime hybrid pumps), which the company markets as ~25% lower CO2e than Tier IV DGB. Top-5 customers were 39% of 2025 revenue, with Occidental and XTO (ExxonMobil) each above 10%.

The defining strategic story is LPI's evolution from an internal fueling/power unit into a standalone behind-the-meter power business. LPI's platform — Forte (modular generation), Tempo (power quality for AI load swings), and Chorus (grid optimization) — targets hyperscaler demand at a moment when transmission and interconnection bottlenecks favor on-site natural-gas generation. ERCOT projects Texas grid demand could quadruple by 2032, which Liberty cites as supportive of both its frac and power theses. The March 2025 IMG Energy Solutions acquisition (~$19.6M) added engineering and interconnection capability.

A critical clarification: the power generation business, equipment orders, and customer contracts belong to Liberty/LPI. The Vantage agreement is a development partnership, not a binding power-supply contract with the data-center end user. Liberty must still secure end-user contracts on acceptable terms, a point it flags in its own risk disclosure.

## Financial Analysis

The core frac business is in a cyclical downturn that appears to have bottomed in late 2025.

| Net income ($M) | 400 | 556 | 316 | 148 |

2025 was a sharp decline year. Revenue fell 7%, but operating income collapsed 81% ($389M → $73M) and Adjusted EBITDA fell 31% ($922M → $634M), driven almost entirely by pricing erosion — activity was flat-to-modestly up. The mechanism is straightforward: frac is bid-based and price-sensitive, WTI averaged $65.45 in 2025 vs $76.63 in 2024, and lower oil compressed completions pricing while costs proved sticky. Reported net income of $148M overstates operating health, because $162.6M of it was a non-cash mark-to-market gain on equity stakes; the Oklo position actually reversed to an $11.9M loss in Q1 2026. Screening on net income materially overstates fundamental health here.

Free cash flow nearly evaporated in 2025 (~$14M) because Liberty kept capex elevated (~$595M) to fund digiFleet upgrades and the power build-out while EBITDA fell. Q1 2026 showed stabilization, not recovery: Adjusted EBITDA fell to $126M (from $168M in Q1 2025) as pricing headwinds fully landed and winter weather hurt. Management cited "record pumping efficiencies and high fleet utilization."

The balance sheet transformation is the most important recent development, and one that standardized data pulls (still showing the 12/31/25 sheet) would miss. As of 12/31/25, debt was modest (~$307M total, D/E 0.71, $27.6M cash). In February–March 2026, Liberty raised ~$1.295B in 0% convertible senior notes ($770M due 2031 at $34.50 conversion / 32.5% premium; $525M due 2032 at $37.44 / 30% premium), netting ~$1.26B, and spent ~$186.5M on capped calls to push effective conversion caps to $65–72. Cash jumped to $699M at 3/31/26; total debt is now ~$1.32B. The revolver was fully repaid, and the credit agreement was amended to permit up to $600M of additional bridge debt (must be incurred by 6/30/26) and to double the convertible basket to $600M, signaling further capital-raising for the power business. Liberty explicitly intends to fund the power expansion with "debt, project financing including non-recourse debt, and co-investments or equity."

The 0% coupon structure is a genuine financing positive: Liberty is effectively borrowing $1.3B at zero cash interest, with equity upside to lenders only above ~$34.50/$37.44 and capped-call protection to $65–72. It is opportunistic and shareholder-conscious, but it front-loads conversion and refinancing risk into 2031–2032, and the bridge-debt option could accelerate near-term revolver maturity triggers.

On capital allocation: a $750M buyback authorization runs through July 2026, but only $24M was repurchased in 2025 and zero in Q1 2026 as capital was redirected to the power build-out. The quarterly dividend was maintained/raised to $0.09/share (~1.2% yield). A legacy TRA liability (~$67M) continues to consume cash ($7.9M paid in Q1 2026).

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two distinct engines. The first is the cyclical frac recovery. Estimate revisions have turned hard: current-quarter EPS moved from -$0.08 (90 days ago) to +$0.08 (+199.7%), next quarter from -$0.03 to +$0.11 (+462.4%), current year from -$0.46 to +$0.31 (+168.4%), and next year from -$0.31 to +$0.35 (+214.4%). The magnitude (>30% over 90 days across every period) is a meaningful positive signal. The earnings-surprise record reinforces it: three of the last four quarters beat, with Q1 2026 delivering +$0.14 against a -$0.13 estimate. Elevated oil supports completions pricing if it holds, and a tightening frac-fleet supply/demand balance (equipment cannibalization and attrition shrinking effective capacity) is a structural tailwind into 2026.

The caveat is that this turn is recovering off a deeply depressed base. Current-year consensus EPS of $0.31 implies ~$50M net income on a $5.1B market cap — the revisions are real, but they reflect a move from losing money to barely profitable, not a return to the 2023 earnings power ($556M net income). Much of the oil-driven optimism is contingent on a conflict premium that the broader energy complex has repeatedly seen reverse; if Hormuz reopens and WTI normalizes toward $70-75, the pricing recovery would be slower and shallower than the current revision velocity implies.

The second engine is the power pivot, a call option on a structural theme priced more on aspiration than backlog. The 3 GW by 2029 target and the Vantage agreement are headline figures, not booked revenue. The March 2026 termination of the 330 MW Texas project is a concrete reminder that this pipeline is fragile. Management openly admits its team "has not directly engaged in the distributed power solutions business before," and sales cycles are long and uncertain. The demand is real; execution and returns are the open questions, and Liberty competes against NextEra, grid utilities, and distributed-power players with deeper capital, established customer bases, and fewer regulatory hurdles.

## Valuation Assessment

LBRT trades at ~32x trailing/forward earnings on $0.89 EPS, against a peer median P/E of 18.2x in oil & gas equipment and services. Trailing P/E is the wrong lens at a cyclical trough, so the relevant question is forward earnings power. On current-year consensus EPS of $0.31, the stock trades at ~95x; on next-year $0.35, ~84x. These are not the multiples of a cheap stock by any reasonable forward measure: the earnings recovery is real but the price has already discounted a substantial portion of it and a meaningful chunk of the power optionality on top.

The peer set is heterogeneous, with KGS (88.4x) and WHD (54.5x) carrying power/gas-infrastructure premiums while pure frac/services names trade at 11-18x. LBRT's multiple sits above the pure-services cohort because the market is pricing the power pivot, which is the correct framing, but it also means a large portion of the valuation rests on a business with one already-terminated deal, no booked power offtake, and management's stated inexperience. On a sum-of-parts: a recovering frac business at ~$600M EBITDA deserves a ~5-6x multiple (~$3.0-3.6B EV), leaving the remaining ~$2.6B of enterprise value (net of ~$620M net debt) to be carried by power optionality that is targets-not-backlog.

Analyst price targets span $21 (low) to $40 (high), with a $33.85 mean and $35.00 median. At the current $29.37, the stock sits between the mean target and the low. We do not treat the consensus targets as floors here, because at ~84-95x forward earnings the stock is at a premium multiple where targets reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price. The downside to the low target ($21) is ~28%; the upside to the high ($40) is ~36%. The valuation score reflects a multiple that is stretched against true operating earnings, with the offsetting case being the estimate-revision turn and the power call — neither sufficient to clear a high valuation score at this level.

## Competitive Landscape

In frac, Liberty competes against Halliburton (largest), Patterson-UTI, ProFrac, and ProPetro in a bid-based, price-sensitive market. Liberty differentiates on technology (digiFleets), efficiency, safety, and integrated supply chain (owned sand mines, proppant logistics). The industry dynamic is constructive on a 12-18 month view: pricing pressure plus equipment cannibalization and attrition is shrinking effective frac capacity, which sets up a tighter supply/demand balance. Liberty's technology lead and vertical integration give it durable share within its niche, and its competitive position is improving relative to the trough.

In power, the picture is harder. Liberty is a new entrant in a crowded, capital-rich field, competing against NextEra, grid utilities, and other distributed-power providers chasing the same hyperscaler demand. Liberty's own disclosure concedes competitors have "greater access to capital, more established customer bases, deeper vendor relationships and fewer regulatory hurdles." Equipment (gas turbines/engines) is supply-constrained and subject to tariffs. Liberty's natural adjacency — it already buys and operates gas gensets and supplies CNG — is real, but it does not confer the kind of moat that the frac business has built over a decade. The competitive trajectory is positive in frac and unproven in power, which nets to a moderate score.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile has concentrated meaningfully since the convertible financing. The central risk is execution on the power pivot: the 3 GW target is not under contract, Vantage is a development partner rather than an offtake counterparty, and management admits inexperience. The March 23, 2026 termination of the 330 MW Texas project (8-K Item 7.01, corroborated by news) validates the disclosed sales-cycle risk in real time and is a yellow flag on pipeline firmness.

Second, leverage and financing dependence: net debt swung from near-zero to ~$620M in one quarter, with $600M more bridge debt authorized. Power projects require large upfront, often non-refundable equipment deposits and take-or-pay commitments before any revenue arrives.

Third, frac cyclicality: the core business is at trough margins, and recovery depends on sustained higher oil and tighter fleet supply. A Hormuz reopening that normalizes WTI toward $70-75 would slow the recovery the revisions are pricing.

Fourth, customer concentration: OXY and XTO each above 10%, top-5 at 39%; E&P consolidation could pressure pricing.

Fifth, convertible dilution above $34.50/$37.44, mitigated by capped calls only up to $65-72.

Sixth, regulatory exposure in both businesses, including new power-business oversight (FERC, NERC, ERCOT/PUCT, the RTC+B Texas market design). The Q1 2026 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K, but the 10-K's extensive new distributed-power risk section is itself the major recent addition.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no directional edge. Near-term ATM IV is 60.5% against 59.7% one-year historical vol — fairly priced with a 0.8% spread, and IV percentile sits at the 22nd percentile within the current chain (relatively cheap). Put/call skew is 2.9% (balanced), put/call OI ratio is 0.27 (call-heavy), and put/call volume ratio is just 0.07. The term structure is flat. Intraday, ATM IV compressed from 77.1% to 60.5% on June 2, consistent with event-premium unwinding rather than informed positioning. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.2%. Nothing in the options data confirms or challenges the fundamental thesis; it reads as a stock with evenly distributed uncertainty and no concentrated informed bet either way, which is appropriate for a two-sided setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$40).** The frac recovery accelerates as oil holds elevated and effective fleet capacity tightens, pushing Adjusted EBITDA back toward $750-850M and validating the steep estimate revisions. The power pivot converts at least one major end-user offtake (beyond the Vantage development agreement), de-risking the 3 GW target and justifying a re-rating toward power-infrastructure multiples. The 0% converts prove to be cheap financing for a business that compounds, and the stock approaches the $40 high target (~36% upside).

**Bear Case (~$21).** Hormuz reopens and WTI normalizes toward $70-75, slowing the frac pricing recovery and undercutting the revision velocity. The power pivot stalls — a second deal slips or terminates, the team's inexperience shows in returns below modeled assumptions, and the $1.3B-plus of debt becomes a drag with no offsetting power EBITDA. The market re-rates LBRT back toward the pure-frac peer cohort (5-6x trough-recovery EBITDA), and the stock falls toward the $21 low target (~28% downside).

The base case sits in between: frac stabilizes and grows modestly through 2026, the power business generates headlines and incremental contracts but remains targets-not-backlog, and the stock churns in the high-$20s to low-$30s as the market waits for power offtake evidence. We place base-case fair value around $30-32, essentially in line with the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean).** The specific thesis: consensus EPS estimates have turned from negative to positive across all forward periods (+168% to +462%), the frac business has bottomed with a dated Q2 2026 sequential-growth catalyst from management, and the power pivot is a call option on AI/data-center demand. The reason this is HOLD rather than BUY is valuation — at ~84-95x forward earnings on a recovery base, with one power deal already terminated and ~$1.3B of fresh debt, the risk/reward is roughly balanced (28% downside to the low target vs 36% to the high), not the asymmetric skew a BUY requires.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A binding power-supply/offtake contract with a hyperscaler end user (not just a development agreement), materially de-risking the 3 GW pipeline.
- Q2/Q3 2026 Adjusted EBITDA returning toward an $180-200M quarterly run-rate, confirming the frac recovery is durable rather than oil-premium-dependent.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $20s that restores asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second power deal termination or material Vantage slippage, signaling the pipeline cannot convert.
- WTI normalizing below $70 with frac pricing rolling over, reversing the estimate-revision trend.
- Bridge debt drawn near the $600M cap without corresponding power-project bookings, signaling the balance-sheet build outrunning revenue.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026), which will test both the sequential-growth guidance and any power-contract progress. The June 30, 2026 bridge-debt incurrence deadline is a second near-term marker of management's financing intentions.

## Conclusion

Liberty Energy is a high-quality frac franchise at a cyclical trough, layering a debt-financed and unproven power business on top. The constructive evidence is concrete: a sharp upward estimate turn, a Q2 sequential-growth catalyst, elevated oil, tightening fleet supply, and a director's $250K open-market purchase at $25.79 near the lows. The cautionary evidence is equally concrete: 2025 operating income of just $73M against headline net income flattered by reversed equity gains, ~$1.3B of debt added in eight weeks with $600M more authorized, an already-terminated 330 MW power deal, and management's own admission of inexperience in the new business. The score trajectory has improved steadily (4.2 → 5.0) on the turn in growth and competitive position, and the current price decline from the April high to $29.37 reflects no fundamental deterioration, but it also does not restore enough valuation cushion to upgrade. At ~32x trailing and ~84-95x forward earnings, the stock prices in both the recovery and a generous slice of the power optionality. We rate LBRT HOLD with a growth lean and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$20s or on a binding power offtake.
