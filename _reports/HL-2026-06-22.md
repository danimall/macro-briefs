**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hecla Mining has executed a clean balance-sheet transformation at an opportune moment. Over roughly 18 months, the company moved from ~$550M net debt to debt-free (final $263M of 7.25% Senior Notes redeemed April 9, 2026), sold its Casa Berardi gold mine in Quebec, and exited Mexico to become a pure-play silver producer concentrated in the US and Canada. This was funded by a price-driven earnings explosion: Q1 2026 sales from continuing operations doubled to $411.4M, net income from continuing operations rose to $164.7M from $24.3M, and operating cash flow hit $182.9M, almost entirely because realized silver prices rose to $82.70/oz from $33.59. Liquidity now stands near $765.6M plus an undrawn $225M revolver, against stockholders' equity of $2.57B.

The entire investment case is now a leveraged bet on the silver price holding. Silver spiked to a reported $118/oz in January 2026 and settled near $80; the stock peaked at $34.17 in late January and has retraced to $15.96, a ~53% drawdown from the high. Production volumes were flat-to-down (3.9M oz silver vs 4.1M), so essentially all of the earnings surge is price, not output. Near-term consensus EPS estimates are falling (current-quarter -24.8% over 30 days, current-year -15.3% over 90 days) even as the longer-dated estimates rose, reflecting both the realized price retreat and the loss of Casa Berardi's contribution. The Q1 EPS came in below consensus (-10.1% miss) after three consecutive beats.

We rate HL a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The balance sheet is genuinely strong and the jurisdictional positioning is best-in-class among silver peers, which supports the floor. But the forward case offers no asymmetric edge: it depends on silver staying elevated, the company's own hedges cap participation in further upside, management has explicitly warned of "outright halts" and possible care-and-maintenance at Keno Hill between 2027 and 2030, and ~54% of the Casa Berardi proceeds are deferred or contingent on a smaller counterparty. At ~23x trailing earnings on cyclical-peak metals prices, the stock prices in continued strength without offering protection if prices revert.

## Company Overview

Hecla, founded in 1891, is North America's largest and oldest primary silver producer, responsible for roughly 37% of all silver mined in the US and Canada in 2025. Following the March 25, 2026 Casa Berardi sale and Mexican-subsidiary divestiture, the company operates three segments:

- **Greens Creek (Alaska)** — the flagship polymetallic massive-sulfide mine producing silver, gold, zinc, and lead. Q1 2026 gross profit was $168.6M. Rich gold and base-metal by-product credits drive a negative silver AISC (-$8.39/oz in Q1 2026), placing it among the lowest-cost silver mines globally.
- **Lucky Friday (Idaho)** — a deep silver-lead-zinc mine using Hecla's patented Underhand Closed Bench method to manage seismicity. Q1 2026 gross profit $60.6M, AISC $23.78/oz. A surface cooling project is ~81% complete, targeting mid-2026.
- **Keno Hill (Yukon)** — acquired via the 2022 Alexco deal, high-grade but still in ramp-up and not yet at declared commercial production. Q1 gross profit $24.3M, but constrained by permitting and infrastructure bottlenecks.

Silver is the designated primary product; gold, zinc, lead, and copper function as by-product credits offsetting cash costs. Consolidated AISC after by-product credits was $8.17/oz in Q1, an unusually low figure flattered by high gold and base-metal prices.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture combines record operating performance with a misleading GAAP headline. The reported net loss applicable to common of $(19.2)M is an accounting artifact. It reflects a $183.7M loss from discontinued operations, including a $192.5M non-cash book loss on the Casa Berardi disposal. Underlying continuing operations were strongly profitable. Investors keying on the headline loss would misread the quarter, but the cyclical earnings volatility this masks is real — full-year net income was $321.7M in 2025, $35.8M in 2024, and a net loss of $84.2M in 2023.

The balance-sheet transformation is the most durable positive. As of April 9, 2026, Hecla is debt-free, having redeemed the remaining Senior Notes for $265.8M. Cash stands at $587.6M (up from $241.6M at year-end), marketable securities ~$178M, plus the undrawn revolver. FCF generation is strong at peak prices but inherently cyclical; FY2026 capex guidance is $204-223M with ~$55M of exploration. This earns a high financial-health score — the most reliable downside-filter dimension in our framework, and Hecla clears the test on cash flow, leverage, and liquidity.

Capital returns remain conservative. The silver-price-linked dividend component was removed in February 2025, leaving only a token $0.015/share annual dividend (~0.09% yield). The 20M-share buyback authorization (19.1M remaining) has been dormant since June 2014. Management is prioritizing balance-sheet repair and growth optionality over shareholder returns, which is defensible but offers no income support to the thesis.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed and warrants a below-average score. Reported revenue and earnings growth is spectacular but almost entirely a metals-price phenomenon, not a volume story. The 2026 silver guidance of 15.1-16.5M oz, with a stated longer-term pathway to 20M+ oz, depends materially on Keno Hill ramping toward its 440 tpd permitted capacity. That ramp is gated by Yukon permitting outside the company's control, and management itself warns of curtailed production or outright halts between 2027 and 2030.

Estimate revisions reinforce the caution. Near-term consensus is falling sharply, with next-quarter EPS revised -30.0%. The longer-dated estimates rose (next-year +25.1% over 90 days), but the dominant near-term signal is negative momentum. We do not see the +30% sustained estimate-revision signal here — the 90-day current-year trend is negative, and what positive revision exists is concentrated in lower-conviction out-year numbers. Growth prospects rest on a price assumption rather than volume expansion, which limits durability.

## Valuation Assessment

At $15.96, HL trades at ~23x trailing earnings and a similar forward multiple, against a Materials peer median P/E of 21.2. The peer set (Element Solutions, Crown, Westlake, Avery Dennison, Eastman) is heterogeneous chemicals/packaging rather than mining, so the comparison is imperfect, but it establishes that HL is not screening as obviously cheap. The trailing earnings reflect cyclical-peak silver prices. A 23x multiple on peak earnings is a demanding valuation if prices mean-revert toward the $80-100 base cases banks model, and punishing if they revert further.

Our framework treats a low or moderate multiple as a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst, and here the catalyst would have to be silver moving structurally higher. The analyst price-target range ($20-32, mean $25.53) sits well above the current price, which on an inflection stock could be a floor. But HL is not a clean inflection: estimates are falling near-term, the sentiment trend is degrading, and H.C. Wainwright already cut its target to $26.75 from $36.50 post-Casa Berardi. We treat the target spread as reflecting silver-price optimism rather than independent confirmation of mispricing. The ~53% retracement from the January high improves entry relative to the peak, but the decline tracks the silver-price retreat and falling estimates — a fundamental change, not pure volatility. We score valuation roughly neutral: there is no specific reason to believe the market is mispricing the stock today, and the multiple offers limited margin of safety against a commodity reversion.

## Competitive Landscape

Hecla's competitive position is solid and arguably improving on the jurisdictional axis. It competes with Coeur Mining, First Majestic, Pan American Silver, and others. Its differentiators are genuine: scale as the largest North American silver producer, an exclusively US/Canada asset base that carries lower political risk than Mexico- and Latin America-heavy peers, very low by-product-adjusted costs at Greens Creek, and a now-debt-free balance sheet that few peers can match. The exit from Mexico and Casa Berardi sharpens the pure-play silver focus, which has thematic appeal given silver's dual monetary and industrial (solar, electronics) demand.

The offsetting concern is that the company's growth lever — Keno Hill — is structurally impaired by factors outside its control, which caps the share-gain trajectory. The competitive moat is real and stable on cost and jurisdiction, but not clearly widening. We score it above average but not high.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is heavier than the clean balance sheet suggests, which keeps the risk score at neutral.

**Keno Hill permitting and infrastructure cliff** is the standout company-specific risk. Management's own 10-Q language is unusually candid: the mill is permitted to 440 tpd but runs ~276 tpd, tailings Phase 2E reaches capacity ~October 2026, total tailings space is exhausted by late 2028, the waste-rock limit is hit ~mid-2027, and the required Quartz Mining License and Water License amendments need a ~12-month YESAB review with full completion ~mid-2029. Management explicitly warns it is "likely there will be times of curtailed production, if not outright halts" between 2027 and 2030, with possible care and maintenance. Power curtailments by Yukon Energy (16 days in December 2025, 5 in January 2026) and unresolved First Nation wealth-sharing provisions compound this. The asset acquired for growth may contribute little for stretches of the next several years.

**Casa Berardi contingent consideration** introduces collectibility risk: ~54% of the $601.7M headline price is deferred or contingent (deferred cash, production royalties up to $211M, permit/gold-price payments), and fair value was booked at $385.7M. Realization depends on Orezone Gold's solvency and execution. Hecla also holds 65.8M Orezone shares (~$106M) and indemnifies Orezone for penalties from the May 2023 HM3 dam slip.

**Hedging drag** is an underappreciated headwind. In Q1 2026, collar losses of $22.3M plus ~$10.2M of net derivative losses in sales suppressed realized gains. Collars cap silver upside (max strikes $58-76), and the company holds 10M oz of silver puts at $50. In a melt-up, the hedges cap participation; investors buying HL as a silver-price call are partly hedged against themselves.

**Metals-price dependence** is now total. **Customer concentration** is meaningful (top three customers 62% of 2025 revenue), and the China market is effectively closed since March 2025. **Legacy CERCLA/reclamation liabilities** total $126.4M in continuing provisions plus multiple Superfund exposures.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 76.8% sits rich to 1-year historical vol of 72.8%, with the term structure in backwardation (76.8% near vs 68.7% long-dated) — the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty rather than complacency. The 1-week put/call skew of 6.2% indicates elevated put demand, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.7%. The options market sees more two-sided risk than a clean balance sheet alone would imply. Reported short interest is negligible (1,311 shares), so squeeze dynamics are not a factor in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($24-28):** Silver holds in the $90-100 range or pushes higher on industrial (solar/electronics) and monetary demand. Hecla's debt-free balance sheet and low Greens Creek costs convert that price into large free cash flow, Keno Hill ramps without major interruption, and the longer-dated estimate upgrades (next-year +25%) prove accurate. At sustained high prices, the analyst mean target of $25.53 is achievable and the multiple re-rates on a clean, growing pure-play. This is roughly the H.C. Wainwright $26.75 Buy case.

**Bear case ($9-11):** Silver mean-reverts toward $50-60 as the January spike unwinds, collapsing earnings (recall the 2023 net loss). Keno Hill is placed on care and maintenance during the 2027-2030 permitting gap, removing the growth lever. The Casa Berardi contingent payments disappoint on Orezone weakness. Falling estimates continue and the multiple compresses on peak-cycle earnings, implying ~40% downside.

**Base case ($16-19):** Silver grinds in the $75-90 band. Hecla generates solid cash flow, stays debt-free, and muddles through Keno Hill with intermittent disruption. The stock tracks the silver price with the hedges capping upside, leaving it range-bound near current levels. This is the most likely outcome and underpins the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the balance sheet supports the floor, but there is no asymmetric directional edge over a 12-month horizon.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Silver establishes a durable floor above ~$95/oz with the current-year EPS estimate trend turning positive over 90 days.
- Keno Hill secures the Quartz Mining License and Water License amendments (or Phase 2W tailings approval), removing the production-halt overhang ahead of schedule.
- Initiation of a meaningful buyback or restoration of the silver-linked dividend, signaling confidence in sustained cash flow.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Silver breaks below ~$60/oz on a sustained basis, pointing toward a return to loss-making operations.
- Confirmation that Keno Hill is placed on care and maintenance, or a material write-down of the Casa Berardi contingent consideration on Orezone distress.
- Operating cash flow turns negative in a quarter on volume disruption stacked on price weakness.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, with the silver price and Keno Hill permitting milestones (Phase 2W approval expected mid-2026) as the key interim data points.

## Conclusion

Hecla has done the hard work of becoming a debt-free, US/Canada-focused pure-play silver producer at the top of a price cycle, and that structurally improves its risk profile. The new clarity of the business model also clarifies the bet: at ~$16, HL is a leveraged call on silver staying elevated, with the company's own hedges blunting the upside, near-term estimates falling, a candid management warning of production halts at Keno Hill through 2027-2030, and meaningful contingent-consideration risk on the Casa Berardi exit. The drawdown from the January peak tracks a real silver-price retreat and falling estimates, not noise, so it is not a clean dip-buy. We maintain HOLD. The stock is ownable for investors who want clean silver exposure and can tolerate commodity volatility, but it does not offer the asymmetric upside required for a BUY, nor the structural impairment that would justify an AVOID. Watch the silver floor and the Keno Hill permitting clock.
