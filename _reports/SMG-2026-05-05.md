## Executive Summary

Scotts Miracle-Gro has completed a multi-year operational recovery from its Hawthorne cannabis segment collapse, returning to profitability in FY2025 with $145M net income and generating $274M in free cash flow. The company's Q2 FY2026 results (April 29, 2026) demonstrated continued progress: adjusted EPS of $4.53 beat estimates by 11.5%, gross margins expanded 240bps to 41.8%, and net leverage declined to 3.71x — crossing below the 4.0x threshold that unlocks unrestricted shareholder returns.

The stock has sold off ~18% from its February 2026 high of $72.35 to $59.39, driven by the Q2 earnings sell-the-news reaction (down ~$6 on the report day despite a beat) and broader market concerns. The decline creates a more reasonable entry point at 13.7x FY2026 adjusted EPS, well below the Materials peer median P/E of 41.7x. However, the balance sheet remains structurally impaired: $2.1B in debt against negative $500M equity, a $250M senior note maturity in December 2026, and an Altman Z-Score of 1.76 (distress zone). Combined with declining near-term EPS estimates (-11.4% over 30 days for the current quarter), elevated short interest (10.8% of float), and consumer spending headwinds, the risk/reward does not favor an aggressive position in either direction.

The holding thesis rests on three specific catalysts over the next 12 months: (1) commencement of the $500M buyback program, which at current prices could retire ~14% of shares outstanding; (2) the December 2026 debt maturity refinancing at potentially lower rates given the improved leverage profile; and (3) the Hawthorne divestiture completing within management's stated 12-month window, removing the final legacy drag.

## Company Overview

Scotts Miracle-Gro is the dominant branded marketer of consumer lawn and garden products in North America, with approximately $3.4B in FY2025 revenue. The U.S. Consumer segment (~88% of sales) houses iconic brands including Scotts Turf Builder, Miracle-Gro, Ortho, and Tomcat. The company also serves as exclusive marketing agent for Monsanto/Bayer's consumer Roundup products, earning a 50% commission on Program EBIT.

The company's remaining Hawthorne segment (indoor/hydroponic gardening) was classified as held for sale in Q1 FY2026 and now reports as discontinued operations. Revenue from Hawthorne collapsed from $467M (FY2023) to $166M (FY2025), with cumulative restructuring charges exceeding $300M.

Two retail customers — Home Depot and Lowe's — account for 52% of consolidated net sales. The Hagedorn family controls approximately 23% of outstanding shares through Hagedorn Partnership, L.P.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Recovery**

The operational turnaround is genuine and quantifiable:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q2 FY2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|-----------|
| Revenue | $3,551M | $3,553M | $3,413M | $1,460M (+5% YoY) |
| Gross Margin | 18.5% | 23.9% | 30.6% | 41.8% |
| Operating Income | $(174M) | $209M | $359M | — |
| Adj. EPS | — | — | $2.47 | $4.53 (Q2 alone) |

Gross margin expansion of ~2,300bps from the FY2023 trough to Q2 FY2026 reflects lower material costs (urea, resins), reduced warehousing/transport expenses, improved inventory management, and mix optimization. Restructuring charges normalized from $466M (FY2023) to $3M in Q1 FY2026, removing the primary source of below-the-line earnings destruction.

**Balance Sheet Concerns**

Despite operational recovery, the balance sheet remains structurally impaired:
- Total debt: $2,049M (FY2025 end)
- Total equity: negative $500.6M (Q1 FY2026)
- Altman Z-Score: 1.76 (distress zone)
- Net leverage: 3.71x (Q2 FY2026, down from 4.41x prior year)

The negative equity results from cumulative operating losses ($438M in FY2022 alone) compounded by $872.5M of historical treasury stock repurchases. The company maintains $1.5B revolving credit capacity with covenants at 5.0x maximum leverage (well above current 3.71x) and 3.0x minimum interest coverage (current ~5.0x).

Critical near-term obligation: $250M of 5.250% Senior Notes mature December 15, 2026. Given the improved credit profile (S&P upgraded outlook to "positive" in August 2025), refinancing appears manageable but represents execution risk.

**Cash Flow**

Free cash flow of $274M in FY2025 and guided $275M for FY2026 provides adequate debt service capacity (~$108M annualized interest) and dividend coverage ($153M annually at $2.64/share). FCF conversion relative to adjusted net income ($143M) exceeds 100%, indicating high-quality earnings generation from the core lawn and garden business.

The company's $750M accounts receivable sales program reduces reported debt metrics — $254.6M was sold in Q1 FY2026 alone, meaning reported leverage is modestly better than underlying economic reality.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic growth is modest.** Management guides low-single-digit U.S. Consumer net sales growth for FY2026, consistent with a mature category. The 5% sales increase in Q2 FY2026 was driven partly by favorable timing and retailer inventory building ahead of spring selling season.

**Growth vectors:**
1. **Category expansion:** Management's 2030 strategic vision targets $1B in incremental sales, implying ~6% revenue CAGR. This relies on new product innovation and adjacent category penetration.
2. **Margin expansion:** Adjusted gross margin target of 32%+ for FY2026 (already at 41.8% in Q2, though seasonal peak). Full-year margin recovery from restructuring completion provides earnings growth even on flat revenue.
3. **Share buyback:** The $500M authorization at current prices (~$59) could retire approximately 8.5M shares (~14% of outstanding). This converts low revenue growth into meaningful EPS growth via denominator reduction.

**Headwinds:**
- Consumer spending under pressure from macroeconomic uncertainty
- The lawn and garden category, while recession-resilient, is not recession-immune
- Revenue has declined from $3,924M (FY2022) to $3,413M (FY2025), and management only guides "low-single-digit" growth — the business is still smaller than four years ago on an absolute basis

**Estimate revisions are concerning.** Current quarter EPS estimates fell 11.4% over 30 days (from $2.85 to $2.53), and next quarter estimates fell 10.6%. Full-year FY2026 estimates are marginally positive (+1.3% over 30 days to $4.34), suggesting the beat in Q2 was partially offset by lower expectations for H2. FY2027 estimates declined 2.4% to $4.72.

## Valuation Assessment

At $59.39, SMG trades at:
- 13.7x FY2026E adjusted EPS ($4.34)
- 12.6x FY2027E adjusted EPS ($4.72)
- 17.0x trailing EPS ($2.47 — distorted by H1 seasonality)
- ~4.4% dividend yield

Against Materials sector peers (median P/E 41.7x), SMG appears inexpensive. However, this comparison is misleading — peers like Hawkins (41.7x), Materion (48.2x), and Avient (40.7x) are specialty chemicals/materials companies with different growth profiles and balance sheets. The more relevant comparison is H.B. Fuller at 20.7x, which has similar characteristics (leveraged, mature-industry). SMG's discount to Fuller (13.7x vs 20.7x) is partially justified by SMG's negative equity, higher leverage, and consumer cyclicality.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $74.50, low $67.00, high $80.00. At $59.39, the stock sits 25% below consensus mean — unusual for a company that just beat earnings. JPMorgan's March downgrade to Neutral with a $67 target represents the most recent directional call.

The valuation is neither compelling enough to override balance sheet risk nor expensive enough to warrant an outright bearish call. At 13.7x earnings with a 4.4% dividend yield, the stock offers fair compensation for a leveraged turnaround in mid-cycle, but the market is pricing genuine risks (leverage, consumer headwinds, seasonal uncertainty) that are not trivially dismissed.

## Competitive Landscape

SMG's competitive position is the strongest element of the thesis:
- **Dominant branded market share** in a category with limited scaled competition
- **Exclusive Roundup marketing agreement** with Monsanto/Bayer provides a protected revenue/earnings stream
- **Retailer relationships** deeply embedded through exclusive programs at Home Depot and Lowe's
- **~90% domestic sourcing** provides meaningful insulation from tariff escalation relative to competitors with international supply chains
- **80% of FY2026 commodities locked** by Q2 close, providing near-term margin visibility

The competitive moat is stable but not widening. Private label remains a persistent threat in consumer staples categories, though SMG's brand strength has historically limited penetration. The Roundup agreement carries structural risk if Bayer decides to decommission the brand (SMG receives $375M termination fee) or if Program EBIT falls below $50M (termination right for either party).

The Hawthorne divestiture removes the primary source of competitive deterioration — that business was being destroyed by cannabis oversupply, falling from $467M (FY2023) revenue to $166M (FY2025).

## Risk Assessment

**Balance Sheet Risk (HIGH):** $2.1B debt on negative equity with $250M maturity December 2026. While operational cash flows are adequate and covenants have significant headroom, the company operates with minimal margin of safety against unexpected shocks. A severe recession, weather-related demand destruction, or loss of a major retail customer could rapidly compress the covenant cushion.

**Consumer Spending Deterioration (MODERATE-HIGH):** Lawn and garden is somewhat discretionary — consumers may defer or reduce purchases. The ~76% revenue concentration in Q2-Q3 means a bad spring season has limited recovery opportunity.

**Estimate Revision Momentum (MODERATE):** The pattern of declining H2 estimates typically precedes further downside unless management provides offsetting positive commentary. The Q2 beat did not arrest the decline.

**Customer Concentration (MODERATE):** 52% of sales through two retailers. No long-term contracts. Any shift in shelf space allocation or inventory management practices could materially impact revenue.

**Securities Litigation (LOW-MODERATE):** Consolidated class action and derivative suits alleging misstatements during 2021-2023. While the company states claims lack merit, potential settlement costs represent an unquantifiable liability.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($72-80):**
- Buyback program commences in H2 FY2026, providing accretive per-share growth
- December 2026 debt refinancing executes smoothly at lower rates, improving interest coverage
- Hawthorne divestiture completes, removing drag and generating cash proceeds
- Lawn and garden demand benefits from spring/summer normalization
- Leverage declines toward 3.0x, enabling credit rating upgrades and further debt cost reduction
- 16.5x FY2027E EPS of $4.72 = $78 target

**Bear Case ($45-50):**
- Consumer spending deterioration reduces spring/summer demand by 5-10%
- Rising rates increase refinancing cost for December 2026 maturity
- Hawthorne sale at distressed price below carried value triggers additional write-down
- Estimate revisions continue negative trajectory, H2 misses guidance
- Credit concerns resurface if leverage stalls above 3.5x
- 10.5x FY2026E EPS of $4.34 = $46 target

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $50-52 (11.5-12.0x FY2026E), providing adequate margin of safety given balance sheet risk
2. Buyback program launches with $100M+ in initial purchases, demonstrating commitment and conviction
3. December 2026 maturity refinanced at ≤5.5% with credit rating upgrade to BB-/Ba3

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Leverage rises above 4.0x, re-triggering restricted payment limitations
2. H2 FY2026 revenue declines >3% YoY (would indicate consumer spending impairment)
3. Current quarter (Q3 FY2026) EPS misses consensus by >10%

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 earnings (expected late July/August 2026) — the peak seasonal quarter will either confirm or refute the recovery trajectory. Secondary checkpoint: December 2026 debt maturity refinancing execution.

## Conclusion

Scotts Miracle-Gro presents a genuine turnaround story with identifiable forward catalysts (buyback, debt refinancing, Hawthorne exit) that support a growth HOLD rather than a defensive position. The 3.71x leverage and $275M FCF generation demonstrate operational recovery is real. However, $2.1B of debt on negative equity, declining near-term estimates, 10.8% short interest, and consumer spending headwinds create a risk profile that prevents conviction in either direction at $59.39.

The stock is worth holding for shareholders who entered at lower prices, as the 12-month path to $72-78 (16-17x FY2027E EPS) is plausible if execution continues. New positions should wait for either a price pullback to $50-52 or clarity on the buyback timing and December debt maturity refinancing.
