## Executive Summary

Figma delivered an objectively strong Q1 2026: revenue accelerated to 46% YoY ($333M), net dollar retention hit 139% (the highest in 2+ years), and free cash flow reached $89M (27% margin). The stock rallied ~13% post-earnings on May 14.

The problem is structural. Figma faces a triple overhang: (1) an existential competitive threat from AI-native design tools — most critically from Anthropic, the same company whose Claude models power Figma's AI features; (2) ~139 million shares unlocking between May 18 and August 2026, representing roughly 26% of fully diluted shares hitting the market at prices far below where insiders would prefer to sell; and (3) a 30.7% short interest that, combined with no insider buying and concentrated insider selling, signals informed bearishness. The Q1 beat was necessary but insufficient to resolve these structural issues.

At $23.41, the stock trades at ~6.2x forward revenue — seemingly cheap for a 46% grower — but this multiple reflects rational market pricing of the probability that AI compresses Figma's addressable market, margins, or both. Until the AI competitive picture stabilizes and the supply overhang clears, the risk/reward is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Figma is the dominant collaborative design platform for software product teams, enabling real-time multiplayer design workflows entirely in the browser. The company IPO'd on August 1, 2025 (NYSE: FIG) at $33/share after Adobe's $20B acquisition attempt was blocked by regulators in 2023.

The platform has expanded from a single product (Figma Design) to nine products: Design, FigJam (whiteboarding), Dev Mode, Slides, Make (AI prompt-to-prototype), Sites (design-to-website), Buzz (marketing assets), Draw (vector illustration), and Weave (multi-model AI canvas via acquisition). Revenue is subscription-based with per-seat pricing across Starter (free), Professional, Organization, and Enterprise tiers.

As of Q1 2026, Figma had ~690,000 paid customers (+54% YoY), with 15,218 customers above $10K ARR (+37%) and 1,525 customers above $100K ARR (+48%). Revenue is geographically diversified: 47% US, 53% international. No customer exceeds 10% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $333.4M in Q1 2026, up 46% YoY — an acceleration from 40% in Q4 2025 and 38% in Q3. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to $1.422-1.428B (~35% growth), with Q2 guided at $348-350M (~42-43% growth). The deceleration implied by full-year guidance relative to Q1's pace suggests management expects the growth rate to moderate through H2.

**Profitability (the dual narrative):**
- GAAP operating loss: $(137.4)M, driven by $169M in stock-based compensation (51% of revenue)
- Non-GAAP operating income: $52.1M (16% margin)
- Free cash flow: $88.6M (27% margin)

The GAAP picture is distorted by IPO-triggered RSU vesting. SBC will remain elevated (~$340M annually based on $1.2B in unrecognized compensation over 3.5 years) but is declining as a percentage of revenue as revenue scales.

**Gross Margin Deterioration:** GAAP gross margin compressed from 91% (Q1 2025) to 79% (Q1 2026). The driver is AI inference costs: cost of revenue increased 253% YoY, with $33.7M in incremental AI hosting expense. This is the central tension — Figma is investing heavily in AI capabilities that currently dilute margins without proven long-term monetization.

**Balance Sheet:** $1.64B in cash/securities, zero debt. The balance sheet is a clear strength. However, $85M is allocated to Bitcoin and crypto — an unusual choice that adds unnecessary treasury volatility ($20M in combined losses in Q1).

**Key Operating Metrics:**
- Net Dollar Retention: 139% (expanding — strong signal of enterprise upsell)
- Remaining Performance Obligations: $682.3M
- Deferred Revenue: $627.7M (+54% YoY)
- Customers >$100K ARR: 1,525 (+48% YoY)

## Growth Analysis

The Q1 data provides genuine evidence that Figma's multi-product strategy and AI investments are generating revenue pull-through:
- Pro team conversions grew 150%+ YoY
- Customers using the MCP server grew seats 70% faster than non-users
- 60% of $100K+ customers used Figma Make weekly (up from 50% last quarter)
- Pro teams with AI credit add-ons showed 3x average annualized spend

These are real demand signals. The growth concern is not Q1 performance but the trajectory of competition. Two specific threats emerged in the 60 days preceding this report:

1. **Anthropic's Claude Design (April 2026):** Generates polished designs, prototypes, and marketing collateral from conversational prompts. This targets Figma Make, Figma Slides, and Figma Buzz simultaneously — and comes from the same company whose Claude models power Figma's own AI features.

2. **Google Stitch (March 2026):** Google explicitly announced it is "evolving Stitch into an AI-native software design canvas," directly competing with Figma's core collaborative design product.

The growth question for the next 12-24 months is whether Figma's network effects (established team workflows, design system libraries, developer handoff integrations) are strong enough to withstand AI-native tools that enable non-designers to produce acceptable design output without specialized collaboration tooling.

## Valuation Assessment

At $23.41 with $1.42B in guided 2026 revenue, Figma trades at approximately:
- 6.2x EV/forward revenue (using ~$10.4B market cap less $1.64B cash = ~$8.8B EV; $8.8B / $1.425B = ~6.2x)
- Negative trailing P/E (GAAP losses)
- ~87x FY2026 non-GAAP EPS ($0.27 consensus)

For context: Comparable high-growth SaaS companies (35-45% revenue growth, 25%+ FCF margins) have historically traded at 10-15x forward revenue in favorable environments. The current 6.2x multiple implies the market is discounting Figma's growth durability by 40-60% relative to historical comps.

The bull case valuation argument: if Figma sustains 30%+ growth and the competitive picture stabilizes, re-rating to 10-12x revenue produces $32-38/share — the median analyst target of $34 reflects this scenario.

The bear case: if AI competition accelerates seat compression and NDR declines, the multiple contracts further to 4-5x, implying $14-18/share.

Goldman Sachs recently cut its target from $35 to $30 (applying 10x EV/sales on compressed peer multiples), maintaining Neutral. The analyst consensus (9 Holds, 2 Buys, 1 Strong Buy) reflects widespread uncertainty about the right multiple in an AI-disrupted environment.

At current prices, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric: ~45% upside to median target vs. ~25-40% downside to bear case. This asymmetry is insufficient to justify a BUY given the concentration of specific near-term risks.

## Competitive Landscape

Figma's competitive position has deteriorated meaningfully since its IPO:

**Moat elements (intact):**
- Network effects: real-time collaboration requires your team to be on the same platform
- Design system libraries: enterprises have invested significantly in Figma-native component systems
- Developer handoff workflow: Dev Mode integrates into engineering workflows at scale
- Brand/muscle memory: Figma is the default tool for product design teams

**Moat elements (under threat):**
- AI-native alternatives bypass the need for specialized design tooling entirely
- Per-seat pricing is vulnerable if AI reduces headcount or enables single-seat agents to do multi-seat work
- Design-to-code workflows are being solved by AI tools that don't require a design file as input
- The 10-Q explicitly acknowledges "AI agents and extensions may use one session to do the work of multiple seats"

The Anthropic paradox is particularly concerning: Figma's primary AI model provider is simultaneously building a competitive product. This creates both supply-chain concentration risk and competitive risk from the same entity. If Anthropic deprioritizes Figma's API access or preferentially routes innovation into Claude Design, Figma's AI features could lag.

## Risk Assessment

**Supply Overhang (Immediate):** ~61.1M shares were freed from lock-up on May 18, 2026 (two days before this analysis). Another ~77.7M shares release by August 2026. Combined, this is ~139M shares (~26% of fully diluted count) entering the market at prices 70-84% below where most employees' RSUs vested. The incentive to sell into any strength is substantial.

**Short Interest:** 30.7% of float is short, with 3.8 days to cover. This creates both downside momentum risk (structural short thesis is well-populated) and potential squeeze upside on any dramatic positive catalyst.

**AI Competitive Disruption:** The existential question is whether "prompt-to-product" tools make collaborative design platforms less valuable. Figma's Q1 data suggests the answer is "not yet" — but one quarter of strong results does not refute a multi-year competitive thesis.

**Insider Activity:** CTO Rasmussen sold $5.3M at $28-30 in March 2026. CEO Field, CFO Melwani, and CRO Voskanian all adopted or modified selling plans. Zero open-market purchases by any insider. The signal is unambiguous: insiders are preparing to sell, not buying.

**CEO Governance:** Dylan Field controls 72.3% of voting power through 15:1 Class B shares. The company has $85M in cryptocurrency on its balance sheet. These are governance red flags that limit accountability.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The primary near-term catalyst is the lock-up release. With insiders sitting on positions that are 70-84% underwater from IPO-day highs (and many RSUs vested at $60-$100+ equivalent), the selling incentive on any rally is extreme. Secondary catalyst: Q2 2026 earnings (expected ~August 2026) will be the first quarter fully reflecting both AI credit monetization friction and competitive response to Claude Design/Google Stitch.

**Timing Window:** May-August 2026 for lock-up-driven selling pressure. Q2 earnings (August) for competitive deterioration evidence.

**Short Interest and Squeeze Risk:** At 30.7% SI with 3.8 days to cover, the trade is crowded. Squeeze risk is real — another earnings beat or positive AI announcement could trigger short covering that pushes the stock to $28-32 before fundamentals reassert. This is the primary risk for short positions.

**Borrow and Options Signal:** Call-heavy options positioning (P/C 0.44) could amplify any squeeze. IV is rich (116.6% vs 89% HV), making put-based short expressions expensive.

**Downside Target and Risk/Reward:** Bear case target: $16-18 (lock-up selling + competitive deterioration + multiple compression). Upside risk: $30-32 (squeeze + beat + sentiment shift). At current $23.41: downside ~$6 (25%), upside risk ~$8 (35%). Risk/reward is approximately 0.7:1 — unfavorable for an outright short at current levels.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** at current price. The crowded short positioning plus post-earnings momentum and call-heavy options create meaningful squeeze risk. The stock would need to rally to $28-32 (approaching lock-up holder cost basis) before offering favorable short entry. At that level, lock-up selling + competitive headwinds + crowded exits create better asymmetry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($34-40, +45-70%):**
- AI features expand TAM by enabling non-designers to participate in Figma workflows
- NDR continues expanding as multi-product adoption deepens
- Lock-up selling creates a technical bottom, followed by gradual re-rating
- Network effects prove durable against AI-native competitors
- Non-GAAP margins expand from 16% toward 25%+ as AI costs leverage

**Bear Case ($14-18, -25-40%):**
- Claude Design / Google Stitch gain traction, NDR declines by H2 2026
- Lock-up releases create sustained selling pressure through August
- AI credit monetization friction accelerates customer churn
- Gross margins continue compressing as AI inference costs grow faster than AI revenue
- Per-seat pricing collapses as AI agents reduce team sizes

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry Criteria:**
- NDR sustained above 130% for two consecutive quarters post-lock-up expiry (confirms competitive durability)
- Gross margins stabilize or rebound above 80% (confirms AI cost leverage)
- Lock-up overhang fully absorbed (post-August 2026)
- Competitive response data: Claude Design / Google Stitch usage metrics show limited enterprise penetration vs. Figma
- Price target: $16-18 range would offer better risk/reward given current risks

**Timeline:** The lock-up overhang persists through August 2026. Competitive clarity around AI-native alternatives requires 2-3 quarters of data. The earliest reasonable re-evaluation point is Q3 2026 earnings (likely November 2026), when both the supply overhang and initial competitive response data will be visible.

## Conclusion

Figma's Q1 2026 results demonstrate that the core business remains healthy: 46% revenue growth, 139% NDR, and 27% FCF margins are genuinely strong metrics for a ~$10B market cap company. The stock is not cheap on an absolute basis (87x non-GAAP EPS, 6.2x revenue), but the growth rate could justify re-rating if sustained.

The structural problems are unresolved. The Anthropic paradox (model provider as competitor), ~139M shares unlocking over 3 months, 30.7% short interest with zero insider buying, 72% voting concentration in a founder with crypto on the balance sheet, and gross margin compression from AI costs — these are active, ongoing risks with specific catalysts that could materially impair the stock over the next 3-6 months. One earnings beat does not resolve them.

I am maintaining an AVOID recommendation. Despite strong revenue growth, the concentration of immediate supply risk (lock-up), existential competitive risk (AI-native tools from Figma's own model provider), and governance concerns (founder control + crypto treasury) outweigh the growth thesis on a risk-adjusted 12-month basis. The bar to upgrade from AVOID to HOLD is NDR stability + gross margin stabilization + lock-up clearance + competitive clarity.
