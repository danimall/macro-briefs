**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Marathon Petroleum is in the middle of a cyclical earnings recovery. Diluted EPS rose 31% to $13.22 in 2025 from $10.08 in 2024, and the recovery has accelerated into 2026: Q1 EPS of $1.65 beat the $0.75 consensus by 118.6%, refining capture reached 99%, and the company repurchased $750M of stock in the quarter. Consensus estimates have moved sharply higher: current-year EPS estimates are up 82% over 90 days (to $29.77), next-year up 64% (to $24.29), and 15–16 analysts have lifted the current-quarter number 95.1% over the same window. At $242.94, the stock trades around 8.2x the current-year consensus and offers roughly 10% upside to the mean analyst target of $268. On the surface, this is the profile of an inflection BUY.

Two things hold the call at HOLD rather than BUY, and both are sourced from the company's own filings rather than any external macro scenario. First, the inflection is the refining margin cycle itself. MPC discloses that R&M EBITDA moves roughly $1,125M for every $1.00/bbl change in its blended crack spread. That sensitivity drove net income from $14.5B in 2022 to $3.4B in 2024 and is now driving it back up. Buying refiners when crack spreads and estimate revisions are both rising is buying near the favorable part of a cycle that has historically reverted hard. Second, the 2025 base is flattered. Approximately $897M of asset-sale gains, $253M of legal settlements, and a $57M credit added roughly $1.2B of pre-tax income that does not recur, against just $151M of comparable items in 2024. EPS also benefited disproportionately from a 10% reduction in share count: net income rose only 17% while EPS rose 31%.

The durable part of the story is MPLX. MPC owns the general partner and ~64% of the units; the midstream segment generated $6.75B of adjusted EBITDA in 2025, exceeding R&M's $6.14B, and it grew through the refining downcycle. MPLX distributed $2.56B to MPC in 2025, funding the dividend and a meaningful share of buybacks, and MPC's stake is worth ~$34.6B, roughly half its market cap. The investment case is refining optionality wrapped around a fee-based midstream cash stream. That mix justifies owning the name but does not yet justify paying up for the cyclical peak in refining margins, which is why the recommendation is a growth-leaning HOLD.

## Company Overview

Marathon Petroleum is one of the largest independent U.S. refiners, with ~3.0 million bpd of crude capacity across 13 refineries in three regions: Gulf Coast (1,248 mbpcd), Mid-Continent (1,186 mbpcd), and West Coast (552 mbpcd). The business runs in three reportable segments:

- **Refining & Marketing (R&M)** — the operating core. 2025 net throughput was 2,989 mbpd at 94% utilization, on a roughly 50/50 sour/sweet crude slate. MPC sells wholesale and spot, through ~7,882 Marathon-branded jobber outlets across 40 states and ~1,162 ARCO-branded direct dealers concentrated in Southern California. Gulf Coast export logistics moved 401 mbpd in 2025.
- **Midstream (MPLX)** — MPC owns the GP and ~64% of this large-cap MLP, which gathers and processes natural gas and NGLs (Marcellus, Utica, Permian) and provides fee-based crude and product logistics to R&M. This is now the larger and more stable EBITDA contributor.
- **Renewable Diesel** — the Dickinson, ND facility plus the 50/50 Martinez JV with Neste; currently a small EBITDA drag (-$110M in 2025).

Structural advantages include scale, refinery interconnection that lets the company shuttle intermediates to optimize yields, Gulf Coast export access, heavy/sour processing complexity that captures feedstock discounts, and a captive fee-based midstream business that smooths refining cyclicality. The company was spun out of Marathon Oil in 2011, acquired Andeavor in 2018, and sold Speedway to 7-Eleven in 2021 for $21.4B, the windfall that funded a multi-year mega-buyback now largely exhausted.

The financials describe a cyclical downcycle off a 2022 peak that stabilized and began recovering in 2025.

**Segment adjusted EBITDA (2025):** R&M $6,138M, Midstream $6,750M, Renewable Diesel -$110M.

Several points define the financial picture:

- **Earnings rebounded despite falling revenue.** Operating income rose to $8,291M from $6,796M as R&M margins recovered ($16.87/bbl vs $16.01/bbl in 2024) and roughly $1.0B of margin tailwinds (higher crack spreads, narrower differentials) came through. Layered on top were ~$1.2B of pre-tax one-offs ($897M of asset-sale gains, $253M of legal settlements, a $57M credit) versus just $151M in 2024. The underlying improvement was real, but the headline jump overstates it.

- **The buyback drove the per-share story and is now decelerating.** Net income rose 17% while EPS rose 31%, the gap explained by the 10% share-count reduction. Since 2012 MPC has authorized $60.05B and repurchased $55.67B, with $4.38B remaining. The annual pace fell from $11.6B (2023) to $9.1B (2024) to $3.4B (2025) as the Speedway cash was spent. With Q1 2026 at $750M, the per-share tailwind is moderating from the levels that flattered 2023–2025 EPS growth.

- **Leverage is rising and equity is thinning.** Total debt/finance leases reached $33.31B at YE2025 (including $26.01B at MPLX), long-term debt rose to $30.5B from $24.4B, and stockholders' equity fell to $17.3B. That puts D/E at 3.46x and equity-to-assets at 20.6%. The buybacks mechanically erode book equity while MPLX debt funds midstream acquisitions. The Altman Z-Score of 2.14 ("grey zone") is a direct artifact of this (thin equity and low EBIT/assets) rather than a sign of distress. Ratings remain solid investment grade (Moody's Baa2, S&P/Fitch BBB, all stable).

- **Cash flow is healthy but FCF margin is thin.** OCF was $8.25B, capex $3.49B, FCF $4.77B (3.6% margin). The 2026 capital outlook is modest at ~$4.2B ($1.5B MPC + $2.7B MPLX). MPLX distributions of $2.56B substantially fund MPC's $1.14B dividend and a portion of buybacks, and MPC's ~647M MPLX units were worth ~$34.6B at year-end.

The earnings beat record supports confidence in the near-term direction: three beats and one miss in the last four quarters, with surprises of +118.6% (Q1 2026), +40.0% (Q4 2025), and +19.2% (Q2 2025) against a single -5.3% miss in Q3 2025. This pattern argues that current estimates, while rising fast, are not stretched relative to delivery.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal here is unambiguous and current. EPS estimate revisions over 90 days are extraordinary: +95.1% for the current quarter, +90.5% for next quarter, +82.0% for the current year, and +64.0% for next year. Thirty-day revisions remain positive (+6.1% to +9.6% across periods), so the trend is intact rather than rolling over. A revision velocity of this magnitude, combined with the Q1 beat, is among the stronger forward signals available and ordinarily favors continued outperformance as consensus catches up.

The key analytical question is the source of the growth. It is the refining margin cycle. R&M EBITDA grew on a $0.86/bbl improvement in realized margin and the ~$1.0B of crack-spread and differential tailwinds management cites; the same $1,125M-per-$1.00/bbl sensitivity that is lifting estimates today is what cut them in 2023–2024. Management's medium-term framing is constructive: global demand growth expected to outpace net refining capacity additions through the end of the decade, U.S. gasoline and distillate inventories at or below five-year averages, and structural advantages for U.S. refiners (cheap natural gas, feedstock access, export logistics). That backdrop can keep margins firm for longer than a typical cycle, but the earnings inflection driving the estimate revisions is cyclical rather than structural.

The structural growth lever is MPLX. The midstream segment grew through the refining downcycle to $6.75B of EBITDA, and 2025 M&A — Northwind ($2.4B), the remaining 55% of BANGL ($703M plus earnout), Whiptail ($235M) — concentrates the franchise on the Permian-to-Gulf Coast NGL and gas value chain, with $2.4B of MPLX growth capital in 2026 pointed at Permian gas processing and long-haul pipelines. This is the part of the business that compounds. The renewable diesel segment is currently a drag and not a growth contributor.

## Valuation Assessment

At $242.94 the stock carries a trailing P/E of 15.8x on $13.22 of 2025 EPS, but trailing multiples are the wrong lens given the estimate inflection. On the current-year consensus of $29.77, MPC trades at ~8.2x; on next year's $24.29 (which embeds some normalization), it is ~10.0x. Both sit well below the 30x / PEG-1.5 threshold above which rising estimates fail to justify a re-rating, and the consensus mean target of $268 (median $267.5, range $186–$344) sits ~10% above the current price.

Against refining peers, the stock is mid-pack rather than cheap. Peer P/Es are PSX 17.6x, VLO 18.6x, DINO 10.6x, PBF 10.8x, with a peer median of 17.6x on a trailing basis. On forward earnings, MPC's ~8.2x is comparable to the smaller, less diversified independents and at a discount to the integrated-scale refiners. The dividend yield of 1.6% is below the peer median of 2.8%: MPC has historically returned capital through buybacks rather than yield, and that channel is decelerating.

The case for and against a higher valuation score both rest on the same fact: the current multiple is cheap because it is being measured against cyclically elevated earnings. If 2026 margins hold near the current run-rate, ~8x is genuinely inexpensive and the analyst target of $268 is a floor that consensus will chase higher as estimates rise. If crack spreads mean-revert toward mid-cycle (the more likely path over a 12–18 month horizon given the $1,125M/bbl sensitivity and the absence of any structural reason margins should stay at the high end) then the next-year $24.29 estimate itself is at risk, and the "cheap" multiple is a cyclical artifact. The 2025 base is additionally flattered by the one-offs, so normalized earnings power is below the headline. A high valuation score requires confidence that the market is wrong about something specific; here the market is largely pricing a cyclical peak correctly, which limits the asymmetry. I score valuation moderately positive: there is identifiable near-term mispricing if margins hold and the consensus target is a reasonable anchor, but the cyclical normalization risk caps the upside case.

## Competitive Landscape

This is a commodity business with limited pricing power, but MPC's competitive position is solid and arguably improving through the midstream pivot. The company competes with integrated majors that hold captive crude supply and can better weather margin troughs, and with independents like Valero and Phillips 66. MPC produces none of its own crude, a structural feedstock-cost disadvantage versus integrated peers in downturns. It offsets this with scale, refinery interconnection, heavy/sour processing complexity that captures feedstock discounts, and Gulf Coast export logistics.

The genuine differentiator is the ~64% MPLX stake. Unlike pure-play refiners, MPC carries a growing, fee-based midstream cash stream that smooths cyclicality and funds capital returns. MPLX EBITDA now exceeds R&M EBITDA and is being concentrated on the Permian-to-Gulf Coast NGL chain through disciplined M&A. The West Coast remains the swing factor: the LA refinery is the largest on the coast, exposed to the most aggressive regulatory regime but also the most lucrative crack spreads ($22.13 ANS 3-2-1 in 2025).

Management's relative TSR record supports the competitive read: cumulative TSR reached 448.4 versus a peer group at 329.4 in 2025, beating peers consistently across the five-year window. The competitive position is stable-to-improving on the midstream trajectory, tempered by the structural feedstock disadvantage and the commodity nature of refining.

## Risk Assessment

**Refining margin volatility is the dominant risk.** The disclosed sensitivity — ~$1,125M of R&M EBITDA per $1.00/bbl change in blended crack spread, plus ~$520M each per $1.00 sour/sweet differential — is the specific variable that would invalidate the current estimate trajectory. The cycle currently runs in MPC's favor; the same lever works in reverse. This is a permanent feature of the business.

**Earnings quality.** The 2025 base includes non-recurring pre-tax items. Normalized earnings power sits below the headline, which matters when valuing the stock on a "cheap forward multiple" thesis built on cyclically elevated, partly one-off-flattered numbers.

**Rising leverage / thin equity.** D/E of 3.46x and 20.6% equity-to-assets reflect deliberate buyback-driven financial engineering plus consolidated MPLX debt. A sustained margin downturn would pressure the ability to sustain both buybacks and the dividend. Ratings are stable investment grade, so this is a flexibility risk rather than a solvency risk.

**California regulation — recently two-sided.** SB X1-2 authorizes a maximum gasoline refining margin and penalty plus CEC control over turnaround timing; AB X2-1 mandates minimum inventories. The framework remains, but in August 2025 the CEC said it would not act on a margin cap for at least five years and offered a potential exemption, a near-term de-risking. Separately, the 2026 federal rollback of the Endangerment Finding and EV mandates is favorable for liquid-fuel demand, though both developments are under court challenge.

**MLP structural risks.** Loss of partnership tax treatment would be materially adverse; MPLX cash flows depend on third-party producer drilling activity MPC does not control; GP conflict-of-interest exposure; and MPLX's $26B debt load.

**Other.** Acquisition integration (Northwind, BANGL), RINs cost volatility ($1.33B in 2025, up 24% YoY, a hidden and unpredictable margin swing), operational hazards including Gulf Coast hurricane exposure, PFAS/AFFF liability, and climate litigation.
