**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Affiliated Managers Group is a strategic partner to independent boutique investment firms, taking equity interests in ~24 partner-owned managers ("Affiliates") in exchange for a contractual share of revenue while leaving each firm operationally autonomous. The story over the past two years has been a deliberate mix shift out of structurally-declining long-only equities and into private markets and liquid alternatives, which carry higher fee rates and performance fees. Q1 2026 validated the pivot: AUM grew 24% YoY to ~$882B, aggregate fees rose 50% to $1,909.9M, equity-method income (net) jumped 96% to $147.4M, and Economic EPS — management's primary metric — grew 58% to $8.23.

The nuance that governs the entire investment case is that a single equity-method Affiliate is now disclosed as "significant" under Reg S-X, with its revenue leaping from $189.3M to $441.9M and net income from $82.0M to $305.6M in one year. This one firm, almost certainly a large liquid-alternatives manager, is driving a disproportionate share of the performance-fee upside. That is simultaneously the bull case (genuine operating leverage in a strong year) and the bear case (concentration plus performance-fee mean reversion). Layered on top is one of the more aggressive buyback programs in the sector — a ~30% share-count reduction in 3.5 years — which manufactures a meaningful chunk of the per-share growth.

At $358.29, roughly 6% below the 52-week high of $382.75, AMG trades at ~14x on a current-year Economic EPS base that is being revised upward (+7.3% over 90 days). That is a discount to the peer median 19.5x, but the discount partly reflects the earnings lumpiness and legacy-equities drag that the headline numbers mask. Two directors bought stock in the open market near all-time highs, a mild positive, but the CEO was a net seller and a senior C-suite executive departed in February. We rate AMG a HOLD with a growth bias: the alternatives franchise is real and compounding, but the setup near the highs into a July 30 earnings print — with the market pricing an ~11% implied move — does not offer the asymmetry required for a fresh buy.

## Company Overview

AMG's model is distinct from both traditional asset managers and pure alternatives GPs. It acquires minority or majority economic interests in partner-owned firms via "structured partnership interests," contributing growth capital, product development, U.S. wealth and global distribution, and succession-planning support in return for a contractual share of Affiliate revenue (or revenue-less-expenses). Management teams retain operating autonomy and significant equity; the pitch to founders is liquidity and succession without selling to a strategic acquirer or PE roll-up.

As of March 31, 2026, AUM stood at ~$882B across four buckets: Private Markets ($148B), Liquid Alternatives ($261.5B), Equities ($297.8B), and Multi-Asset & Fixed Income ($174.7B). Alternatives now represent roughly half of AUM and the vast majority of incremental earnings. The Affiliate count rose to 24 equity-method firms in Q1 2026 from 22 at year-end 2025.

A critical accounting point: some Affiliates are consolidated (their revenue and expenses flow through consolidated revenue, ~$545M in Q1'26) while the higher-growth alternatives managers are increasingly reported via a single "equity method income" line reflecting only AMG's net share. This bifurcation is why headline consolidated operating margin looks distorted: the true earnings engine sits in the equity-method line and in aggregate fees, not in consolidated revenue. Any analysis anchored on consolidated revenue or GAAP operating margin will misread the business. The relevant metrics are Economic EPS, Adjusted EBITDA, and aggregate fees.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 (10-Q filed 5/7/2026) was an exceptional quarter across every relevant line:

- AUM +24% YoY ($712B → $882B); average AUM +24%.
- Aggregate fees +50% YoY to $1,909.9M — asset-based fees +31%, with a large jump in performance-based fees concentrated in liquid alternatives.
- Consolidated revenue +10% to $544.9M (the least informative line, given the equity-method bias).
- Equity method income (net) +96% to $147.4M — the dominant profit driver.
- Net income (controlling interest) +52% to $110.4M; diluted GAAP EPS $3.84 vs $2.20.
- Economic EPS +58% to $8.23 (vs $5.20); Economic net income +42% to $224.6M; Adjusted EBITDA +39% to $317.3M.

The earnings surprise record supports confidence in the near-term consensus: four straight beats, including a 6.2% beat in Q4 2025 and a modest 0.9% beat in the March 2026 quarter. Estimate revisions are broadly positive — current-year Economic EPS consensus of $35.89 has risen 2.9% over 30 days and 7.3% over 90 days, with next-year estimates up 12.1% over 90 days. This is genuine forward momentum, though below the +30% threshold that would flag a durable acceleration.

Quality of earnings checks out. Operating cash flow was $299.3M in Q1'26, underpinned by $294.4M of actual cash distributions received from equity-method Affiliates. This is collected cash, not accrued income, which lends credibility to the equity-method line that dominates the story.

**Capital allocation** is aggressive and shareholder-oriented. Basic shares fell from ~38.5M in 2022 to ~26.4M in May 2026, a ~31% reduction in ~3.5 years. Q1'26 alone retired 0.6M shares at an average $307, and 5.6M shares remain authorized. The dividend is a token $0.01/quarter (yield effectively zero); buybacks are the entire return vehicle. In December 2025–January 2026, AMG redeemed its 5.15% junior convertible trust preferreds for ~$514.6M cash, eliminating ~1.7M dilutive shares but triggering a ~$56M cash tax liability and ~$18.5M of forward-sale expense, a deliberate anti-dilution move.

**Balance sheet:** total debt ~$2.92B at 3/31/26, up from $2.69B at year-end due to a $565M revolver draw. AMG is investment-grade (A3 Moody's / BBB+ S&P), with very long-dated debt (weighted-average maturity ~22 years; nearest note $350M in June 2030) and $1.22B of perpetual-like junior subordinated notes (2059–2064). Cash fell to $376.1M from $586M after the convertible settlement and $242M of new Affiliate investments (BBH Credit Partners, Garda, HighBrook). The financing profile is comfortable, but the combination of a drawn revolver, reduced cash, and continued buybacks means EPS growth is increasingly debt-funded.

**Watch item:** two consecutive quarters of mutual-fund intangible impairments — $70M aggregate in Q1'25 and $43M in Q1'26 — on indefinite-lived acquired client relationships in declining legacy equity funds. Roughly $4.2B of the $9.2B balance sheet is intangibles, and equity-method investments ($2.97B) are also largely intangible in substance. These write-downs confirm the legacy-equities drag is secular, not cyclical.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three legs. First, the alternatives mix shift is working: liquid alternatives and private markets generated the inflows and the performance fees, while equities bled $9.1B of net outflows in Q1'26. Alternatives carry higher fee rates and performance fees that legacy long-only products do not. Second, AMG continues to deploy capital into the pivot — Q1'26 added BBH Credit Partners (taxable fixed-income/credit), an incremental stake in Garda (liquid-alt relative value), and HighBrook (real estate private markets), ~$242M deployed. Third, the share-count denominator keeps shrinking, which converts even modest aggregate-earnings growth into faster per-share growth.

The private-markets and alternatives demand backdrop is favorable at the manager level even as the private-credit complex shows liability-side stress at the fund level. AMG's economics are tied to fee-earning AUM and performance across a diversified set of strategies rather than to any single evergreen redemption vehicle, which insulates it somewhat from the redemption-gate dynamics affecting parts of the alternatives complex.

The overhang on growth durability is that a large share of the Q1'26 result is performance-fee driven, and performance fees are non-recurring by nature. Roughly 27% of AUM (40% of equity-method AUM) can earn performance fees. The spectacular Q1'26 print sets a high comparison bar. If the significant Affiliate's performance fees normalize, aggregate-fee and equity-method growth will decelerate sharply on a reported basis even if the underlying franchise is healthy. Consensus already builds in this reversion — current-year Economic EPS implies a growth rate well below the +58% Q1 pace, and next-year consensus of $28.22 sits below current-year, reflecting the expected mean reversion of an outsized performance-fee year.

## Valuation Assessment

At $358.29 and ~26.4M shares, market cap is ~$9.5B. On the reported ~14x P/E (which the data expresses on Economic EPS) AMG trades at a meaningful discount to the peer median of 19.5x (SEIC 15.0x, JHG 10.2x, VCTR 19.5x, with IVZ and CRBG distorted by GAAP artifacts). On a forward basis, the current-year Economic EPS consensus puts the stock at ~10x, and even the conservative next-year $28.22 (which prices in performance-fee reversion) implies ~12.7x.

The discount is partly justified and partly a mispricing. Justified: the earnings base is lumpier than a traditional manager's, single-Affiliate concentration is now disclosed, and legacy equities are in secular outflow with recurring impairments. Mispricing: the market is applying a traditional-manager discount to a business that is now half alternatives, with a durable, cash-backed distribution stream (the $294.4M of cash received supports the equity-method line) and a relentless buyback that compounds per-share value. The estimate-revision trend — rising in every window — argues the consensus target of $412 (median $421) is more likely to be revised up than down over the coming quarters.

We do not treat proximity to the $382.75 high or the run-up as evidence of overvaluation; price appreciation driven by rising Economic EPS and estimate revisions is not a valuation argument. The reason we do not score valuation higher than 6.0 is that the "cheap" multiple sits on an Economic EPS base inflated by a performance-fee year that consensus itself expects to partially reverse next year. A ~14x multiple on peak-flavored earnings is less cheap than it appears; the honest forward comparison is the ~12.7x on the reversion-adjusted $28.22, which is fairer but not screaming value. The analyst target range ($330 low, $469 high, $412 mean) brackets the stock with modest upside to the mean and meaningful upside to the high, a favorable but not asymmetric setup.

## Competitive Landscape

AMG occupies a differentiated niche. On the buy side of its own model, it competes for boutique stakes against PE firms, sovereign wealth funds, insurers, and other consolidators, many with a lower cost of capital. Its edge is the promise of preserving Affiliate independence and autonomy, which some founders value over a higher headline bid from a strategic acquirer. That model has proven durable and continues to source deals (three new investments in Q1'26).

On the underlying-strategy side, the picture is bifurcated. Legacy equities face secular fee compression and passive/ETF share gains — visible in AMG's own equity outflows and the recurring mutual-fund impairments; only 41% of equity AUM beat benchmark over three years. The alternatives franchise, by contrast, is performing well (liquid alts 92% ahead of benchmark; private markets 84–86%) and gaining share of both AUM and economics. Net trajectory is improving as the mix shifts toward the winning side, but the company is still a market participant in a challenged active-management industry rather than a pure alternatives GP with the multiple that comes with it. The 10-K's expanded AI language flags both a competitive threat to active fees and a cyber vector, a slow-building headwind the market has not fully weighed.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is **single-Affiliate concentration**. The 10-K explicitly warns that problems or key-person departures at the significant Affiliate "could have a disproportionately adverse impact." This is a genuine, quantified, binary-flavored risk that is not fully priced; a bad year or a founder departure at that one firm would materially dent results.

**Performance-fee volatility** compounds this. The Q1'26 result is heavily performance-fee driven; those fees are non-recurring and mean-reverting, and the high comparison bar makes future YoY comparisons harder. **Structured-partnership expense exposure** means that in revenue-share-less-expenses deals (a growing share as alternatives grow), AMG bears downside if Affiliate expenses rise, often without distribution priority and with limited control. **Intangible impairment risk** on a ~45%-intangible balance sheet is recurring, with two straight quarters of write-downs. **Contract terminability** (most contracts terminable on ≤60 days' notice; change-of-control fragility for mutual-fund contracts) is a structural feature of the model.

On governance, the February 2026 departure of Thomas Wojcik (former President & COO, Separation and Release Agreement dated 2/10/2026, confirmed by 8-K Item 5.02 filings) is a senior C-suite exit, meaningful because AMG has no executive employment agreements and depends heavily on key personnel to source deals. Dava Ritchea is CFO and signs the 10-Q; Horgen remains CEO.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 51.7% versus 32.6% trailing realized — rich, at the 84th percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (51.7% near-term vs 41.4% at six months). The market is pricing an imminent ~11% implied move, consistent with the July 30 earnings date. Put/call skew is balanced (1.7%) and put/call volume and OI ratios are near zero, so there is no directional bearish tilt; the elevated near-term IV reflects event risk around the print rather than a negative positioning bias. The prior-snapshot IV of 211.6% collapsing to 51.7% is a data-quality artifact of a thin chain and should be read as a normalization rather than a signal. Entering immediately ahead of a print with an 11% implied move and rich IV is unfavorable timing; a post-earnings IV crush would offer a better entry.

Short interest is modest at 5.3% of float, 2.8 days to cover — no squeeze dynamics either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$450–470).** The alternatives pivot is a structural re-rating story that the market is still valuing as a traditional manager. Economic EPS compounds mid-teens as fee-earning AUM in alternatives grows, the buyback keeps shrinking the denominator ~5–7% annually, and the significant Affiliate's performance sustains at a high level rather than reverting sharply. A re-rating toward the alternatives-manager cohort plus rising estimates carries the stock to the analyst high of $469. Directors buying in the open market near highs (Cates +1,500 at $305.83; Franqui +750 at $304.88) support this read.

**Bear case (~$275–300).** The significant Affiliate's performance fees mean-revert, aggregate fees decelerate hard on tough comparisons, and the market recognizes that a large slice of recent growth was a one-time performance year plus denominator shrinkage. Legacy equities keep bleeding, impairments recur, and the debt-funded buyback looks less attractive with cash drawn down and the revolver at $565M. Multiple compresses back toward the low-teens on reversion-adjusted earnings, taking the stock toward $275–300, roughly 16–23% downside from current.

**Base case (~$395–410).** Alternatives momentum continues but performance fees normalize partway; Economic EPS lands near the current-year consensus with continued buyback support; the multiple holds around the current level and rising estimates drag the stock toward the analyst mean of $412. This is roughly 10–14% upside.

The risk/reward is skewed modestly favorable (base +12%, bull +31%, bear −20%), but not the 2:1+ asymmetry that would justify a fresh buy near the highs into a binary earnings event.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth bias over a 12-month horizon, anchored to the alternatives-pivot thesis and the compounding buyback.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- A pullback toward the low-$300s (near the level where directors bought and roughly 12–15% below current), which would restore the risk/reward asymmetry.
- Confirmation at the July 30 print that the significant Affiliate's performance is broadening across multiple Affiliates (reduced single-Affiliate concentration) rather than resting on one firm.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +30% over 90 days on the forward Economic EPS base, which would flag a durable acceleration rather than a single strong quarter.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- A material reversal in performance fees at the significant Affiliate, or disclosure of a key-person departure there.
- Aggregate fees turning to YoY declines, or equity-method income contracting on normalized comparisons.
- Cash falling further with continued debt-funded buybacks against a shrinking cash cushion, signaling capital-allocation strain.

**Reassessment timeline:** The July 30, 2026 Q2/1H earnings report is the immediate catalyst and the natural reassessment point, given the ~11% implied move and the concentration question.

## Conclusion

AMG's alternatives pivot is delivering real, cash-backed earnings growth, and the ~31% share-count reduction in 3.5 years compounds the per-share economics. At ~14x on a rising Economic EPS base and a discount to the peer median, with two directors buying in the open market, the fundamentals justify ownership. But the headline strength is heavily dependent on performance fees at a single now-"significant" equity-method Affiliate that consensus itself expects to partially reverse, and a senior C-suite executive departed in February. Near the 52-week high, into a July 30 print with rich near-term IV pricing an ~11% move, the risk/reward is favorable but not asymmetric enough to buy. We rate AMG a **HOLD** with a growth bias, and would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-$300s or evidence that the earnings engine is broadening beyond one Affiliate.
