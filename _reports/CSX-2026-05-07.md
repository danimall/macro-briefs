## Executive Summary

CSX Corporation is emerging from three consecutive years of declining profitability (operating income down 25% from 2022 to 2025) with Q1 2026 marking a clear operational turn: operating margin expanded 560bps to 36.0%, EPS grew 26% YoY, and the company beat consensus by 9.2%. The operational turnaround is being driven by efficiency improvements (velocity +7%, dwell -7%), normalization of Hurricane Helene costs, and the completion of the Howard Street Tunnel enabling double-stack intermodal service on a critical East Coast corridor.

The fundamental improvement is real, but the stock has already rerated substantially — up 60%+ from its May 2025 lows to $44.86, now trading at ~28x trailing and ~23.6x FY2026 consensus earnings ($1.90). Estimate revisions are modestly positive (+2.9% over 90 days for FY2026) but lack the velocity that would signal systematic under-appreciation of the recovery. The UP-NS merger application (resubmitted April 30, 2026) represents a binary risk that could materially impair CSX's competitive position if approved.

At current prices, the market is pricing in operational recovery but not offering meaningful margin of safety against the merger risk or a potential macro slowdown in freight volumes.

## Company Overview

CSX operates approximately 20,000 route miles of rail network across 26 states east of the Mississippi River, connecting over 70 ocean, river, and lake ports. The business is diversified across Merchandise (62% of revenue), Intermodal (15%), Coal (13%), and Trucking/Quality Carriers (6%). The company employs approximately 22,200 workers, of whom ~75% are unionized.

CSX's primary rail competitor is Norfolk Southern, with additional competition from trucking, barges, and pipelines. The company's strategic value lies in its dense Eastern network connecting major manufacturing, agricultural, and port facilities with consumer markets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Revenue has contracted from $14.85B (2022) to $14.09B (2025), a 5% decline driven primarily by coal pricing weakness (-15% in 2025, or -$347M) and Quality Carriers underperformance. Q1 2026 revenue of $3.48B (+2% YoY) signals stabilization, with intermodal volumes up 6% and merchandise flat.

**Margin recovery:** The critical metric. Operating margin collapsed from 40.5% (2022) to 32.1% (2025) due to Hurricane Helene ($470M), Howard Street Tunnel disruption costs ($53M), goodwill impairments ($164M in 2025), and labor inflation. Q1 2026's 36.0% margin demonstrates that removing one-time items and executing efficiency improvements can drive meaningful reversion. However, reaching prior peak margins of 40%+ would require both volume growth and continued cost discipline.

**Cash flow:** FY2025 FCF of $1.71B (12.1% margin) was depressed by hurricane capex. Q1 2026 generated $793M in FCF, annualizing to ~$3.2B, which represents the normalized earning power with capex at the guided <$2.4B level. This is a FCF yield of approximately 3.8% on the current $83B market cap.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $18.9B with D/E of 2.32x is typical for Class I railroads. Investment-grade ratings (A-/A3/BBB+, all stable) confirm adequate coverage. Total future interest payments of $13.9B on existing debt represent a meaningful fixed obligation. The Altman Z-Score of 1.33 is structurally low for capital-intensive railroads and does not indicate genuine distress.

**Shareholder returns:** $1.4B in buybacks (2025), ~$1.04B in dividends (annually). The 8% dividend increase to $0.14/quarter marks 22 consecutive annual increases. Dividend yield of 1.2% is below the peer median of 1.7%.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term catalysts:**
1. **Howard Street Tunnel (completed May 5, 2026):** Enables double-stack freight service on a shorter East Coast-Midwest route, directly improving intermodal competitiveness vs. trucking. This is a multi-year revenue catalyst that should support continued intermodal volume growth.
2. **Truck-to-rail conversion:** Rising diesel costs (driven by geopolitical factors) and tighter trucking capacity create favorable competitive dynamics for rail. CSX management has explicitly cited this as a revenue growth driver.
3. **Margin normalization:** With hurricane costs behind, Howard Street Tunnel disruptions eliminated, and the depreciation study providing ~$40M annual tailwind, the path to 37-38% operating margins in FY2026 appears achievable.

**Medium-term growth rate:** Consensus estimates project EPS of $1.90 (FY2026, +23% vs. FY2025's $1.54) and $2.15 (FY2027, +13%). The FY2026 jump reflects recovery from depressed levels rather than structural acceleration. Revenue growth of 2-4% annually is the realistic trajectory given coal's secular decline offsetting intermodal gains.

**Headwinds:** Coal revenue is structurally declining (energy transition, lower global benchmark prices). Quality Carriers is operating at a loss ($202M revenue vs. $207M expenses in Q1 2026). Macro softness could compress freight volumes in H2 2026.

The 23.6x forward P/E offers modest upside if margins expand toward 38%+ and volumes accelerate, but provides limited cushion if the macro environment deteriorates or the UP-NS merger is approved. The stock has moved from $28 (trough) to $45 (current) on the operational recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

**The UP-NS merger is the defining competitive variable.** If approved by the STB, a merged UP-NS would create a transcontinental railroad projecting 244,000 annual loads captured from CSX and 303,000 from BNSF. CSX's logical response — a merger with BNSF — appears blocked by Berkshire Hathaway's stated disinterest. This would leave CSX as the only major standalone Eastern railroad, potentially disadvantaged on coast-to-coast traffic.

**Mitigating factors:** The STB already rejected the initial application in January 2026 on procedural/completeness grounds. Historical STB decisions have been very conservative on railroad consolidation (the last major merger, CPKC, was approved only with significant conditions). There is meaningful probability (perhaps 50-60%) that the merger is ultimately blocked or requires conditions that substantially reduce its competitive impact on CSX.

**Current competitive dynamics are favorable:** Rising diesel costs drive truck-to-rail conversion. The Howard Street Tunnel gives CSX a meaningful service improvement in a key corridor. Q1 2026 volume growth of 3% (with intermodal at 6%) demonstrates CSX is gaining ground in the current environment.

**Five-year TSR of 128 vs. peer group 190** reflects the market's lingering concern about CSX's competitive position — a discount that may narrow if the merger is blocked but could widen if approved.

## Risk Assessment

**1. UP-NS Merger (HIGH — binary, partially unpriced):** If approved (40-50% probability), CSX faces secular competitive deterioration. If blocked (50-60%), CSX benefits from status quo and potential re-rating. The stock likely does not fully price in either outcome.

**2. Coal secular decline (MEDIUM):** 13% of revenue with structurally declining volumes and prices. Each 10% decline in coal revenue = ~$180M headwind. Partially offset by new chemical/intermodal volumes but represents a persistent drag.

**3. Macro/recession risk (MEDIUM):** Freight volumes are GDP-sensitive. A macro slowdown in H2 2026 could cause volumes to disappoint relative to Q1's strong trends.

**4. Quality Carriers (LOW-MEDIUM):** Operating at a loss with fully impaired goodwill. Either requires a turnaround plan or divestiture. At ~$800M revenue, it's a meaningful drag on consolidated margins.

**5. Tariff/trade disruption (MEDIUM):** CSX's intermodal business connecting ports to inland markets is exposed to trade volume reductions from elevated tariff regimes. Export coal is also trade-dependent.

**6. Natural disaster recurrence (LOW-MEDIUM):** Self-insured retention is $200M per occurrence. Climate change is increasing frequency/severity of events on CSX's southeastern network.

## Conclusion

CSX is executing a legitimate operational recovery from a depressed 2025, with Q1 2026 demonstrating that efficiency improvements and normalized costs can restore margins toward historical levels. The Howard Street Tunnel completion is a real catalyst for intermodal growth, and the CEO's $1M open-market purchase at $40.27 in March signals insider confidence in the trajectory.

However, at $44.86 the stock has already discounted much of this recovery, trades at consensus analyst targets, and faces a binary risk from the UP-NS merger. At ~23.6x FY2026 earnings with low-single-digit revenue growth and a 1.2% dividend yield, the risk/reward is balanced. The correct posture is to hold for existing owners with an upgrade contingent on either a valuation pullback to $38-40 or a favorable STB ruling on the merger.
