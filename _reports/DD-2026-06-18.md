**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

DuPont has finished a multi-year breakup. The entity trading today is roughly half the size of the 2024 company: it spun off its semiconductor/interconnect business as Qnity Electronics on November 1, 2025, and closed the sale of its Aramids (Kevlar/Nomex) business to an affiliate of TJC LP on April 1, 2026. What remains are two segments — Healthcare & Water Technologies (FY25 sales $3.23B, +9% YoY, ~30% Operating EBITDA margin) and Diversified Industrials (FY25 sales $3.62B, −3% YoY, ~22% margin). The breakup transaction sequence used Qnity's $4.1B pre-separation cash distribution to retire roughly $4.0B of senior notes, cutting total debt from $7.2B to $3.2B and pushing the balance sheet firmly into investment-grade territory (S&P BBB+, Moody's Baa1, Fitch BBB+, all Stable).

The headline FY2025 net loss of −$779M is misleading and should be discarded as a valuation input. It is driven almost entirely by discontinued operations, principally a $768M Aramids goodwill impairment. The continuing business earned $200M of pretax income in 2025 and produced a clean first quarter in Q1 2026: revenue $1,681M (+4%, +2% organic), continuing-operations diluted EPS $0.36 (up from $0.19), net income $164M, and operating cash flow of $232M versus $77M a year earlier. The trailing P/E of 151x reported by data feeds is a discontinued-operations artifact; on the current-year consensus of $2.39 the forward multiple is ~20x, and on next-year $2.62 it is ~18x.

The recommendation is HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The 12-month thesis rests on three identifiable catalysts: resolution of the NJ PFAS settlement (judicial hearing scheduled June 24, 2026, which removes an overhang), margin re-rating as the 2026 restructuring program eliminates stranded costs left behind by Qnity and Aramids, and continued double-digit-margin growth in Healthcare & Water. This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because of valuation and the absence of an asymmetric mispricing: at ~20x forward earnings on a low-double-digit grower facing an unbounded PFAS tail, the risk/reward is balanced rather than skewed, and the stock has already re-rated substantially off its 52-week low without insider conviction buying to confirm the move.

## Company Overview

The "New DuPont" is a focused specialty materials company with two reporting segments after the Q4 2025 realignment.

**Healthcare & Water Technologies** (~47% of revenue) is the growth engine. It includes Tyvek medical packaging, Tychem protective garments, medical-device components built through the 2023 Spectrum Plastics ($1.78B) and 2024 Donatelle ($365M) acquisitions, and water technologies — FilmTec reverse-osmosis membranes, Amberlite ion-exchange resins, and ultrafiltration. End markets are biopharma, medical packaging, and municipal/industrial water and desalination, all with secular demand support and regulatory qualification moats.

**Diversified Industrials** (~53% of revenue) is more cyclical. Building Technologies (Tyvek housewrap, Styrofoam, Corian) is tied to a soft construction market; Industrial Technologies (Vespel, Molykote, Betaforce adhesives, Cyrel printing plates) serves aerospace (strong), automotive, and packaging. This is the contested, lower-margin half of the portfolio.

The competitive base rests on entrenched branded materials with high switching costs, regulatory qualification barriers in medical and water applications, and a century-old R&D platform. The portfolio is now cleaner and more comparable to medtech-materials and water peers than to legacy diversified chemicals.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** FY2025 continuing-operations revenue was $6,849M, +2% YoY against recast 2024 of $6,719M, with +3% volume (Healthcare & Water +7%) offset by −1% price/mix. Continuing-ops pretax income was $200M at a distorted 51% effective tax rate inflated by separation costs. The reported net loss reflects discontinued-operations items — the Aramids impairment and separation effects — and does not reflect the operating business.

Q1 2026 is the first clean read. Healthcare & Water grew 6%, Diversified Industrials 3% (flat organic). The most recent quarter beat consensus by 10.4% ($0.55 reported vs $0.50 estimate), extending a track record of three beats and one in-line print over four quarters.

**Balance sheet.** Cash is $715M, stockholders' equity $13.9B, and the current ratio improved to 2.1:1 in Q1 2026. Interest expense fell from $366M (2024) to $313M (2025) and to a $40M Q1 run-rate. The financial covenant (debt/cap ≤0.60) is comfortably met. The Altman Z-Score of 2.52 ("grey zone") is mechanically depressed by near-zero transition-year EBIT/assets and a goodwill-heavy balance sheet; it is not a genuine distress signal given investment-grade ratings.

**Asset-quality concern.** Goodwill ($7.9B) plus intangibles ($2.9B) equal roughly half of total assets ($21.5B). The 10-K specifically flags the Protection reporting unit for impairment risk, and a soft Diversified Industrials/construction backdrop raises that risk. The −$24.3B accumulated deficit is a legacy DowDuPont purchase-accounting artifact, not operational.

**FCF and capital allocation.** FY25 operating cash flow was $560M against $333M capex, leaving positive free cash flow. A $2B buyback was authorized in November 2025; the Q4 2025 ASR retired 12.2M shares at an average $40.89, and a $275M ASR was announced in May 2026, leaving ~$1.5B on the authorization. The dividend was reset to $0.20/quarter post-spin (from $0.41), now yielding ~1.7%. A 1-for-2 to 1-for-4 reverse stock split is being put to shareholders at the 2026 annual meeting.

Financial health scores a 6.5: deleveraging is real and substantial, free cash flow is positive, and ratings are stable, but the goodwill concentration and transition-year earnings distortion cap the score below the level a clean fortress balance sheet would earn.

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth case lives in Healthcare & Water Technologies, which grew Operating EBITDA 15% in FY25, with Q1 2026 organic growth of +3% led by medical packaging and biopharma. These end markets — biopharma device components, sterile medical packaging, and water purification/desalination — carry demographic, regulatory, and reshoring tailwinds. PFAS-driven water-treatment demand is a growth vector for the FilmTec and Amberlite franchises.

Diversified Industrials is the offset: flat organic in Q1 2026, with aerospace and auto strength canceled by construction and printing/packaging weakness. This segment will track the broader industrial and construction cycle and is unlikely to be a growth contributor near-term.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising: current-year EPS consensus is +3.8% over 90 days and flat over 30 days, next-year +3.1% over 90 days. This is steady positive revision, not the +30% velocity that signals a systematic underestimate. Growth here is durable and mid-single-digit at the company level — Healthcare & Water compounding faster, dragged by the cyclical industrial half. The 2026 restructuring program ($100–150M of charges through 2028) targets stranded costs left by the divestitures; successful execution could lift the consolidated margin toward the H&W level and provide an EPS tailwind independent of revenue. Growth prospects score 6.0 — solid and segment-bifurcated, without acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E reflects the discontinued-operations net loss, not the continuing business. The correct frame is forward earnings: ~20.2x on current-year consensus and ~18.3x on next-year.

Against Specialty Chemicals peers, the median P/E is 24.0 (IFF 24.0, RPM 20.7, with ALB and LYB at negative earnings and SOLS at 69x). DuPont at ~20x forward sits modestly below the profitable-peer median, which is defensible given the cyclical Diversified Industrials drag and the PFAS overhang but does not represent a clear discount on a quality-adjusted basis. The peer set is heterogeneous and includes loss-making commodity names, so the comparison is loose.

Analyst targets bracket the stock above the current price: low $52, mean $56.81, median $56.50, high $64. The 13 buy/strong-buy ratings against 4 holds and zero sells indicate the sell side sees upside. Treating these as floors would require the stock to look cheap relative to rising estimates with a PEG below 1.5; at ~20x forward on a mid-single-digit consolidated grower (PEG well above 1.5), that condition is not met, so the targets should be read as fair-value anchors rather than stale floors.

The valuation score is 4.5. There is no identifiable catalyst-driven mispricing that makes the current price asymmetrically attractive. The forward multiple is reasonable but not cheap, the growth does not justify a premium to peers, and the PFAS tail is unbounded above accruals. On forward earnings versus growth and risk, the stock is fairly priced.

## Competitive Landscape

The repositioned portfolio has stronger competitive characteristics than legacy diversified chemicals. Tyvek holds a near-monopoly position in sterile medical packaging with regulatory qualification barriers; FilmTec and Amberlite are leaders in membrane and ion-exchange water treatment; the Spectrum and Donatelle acquisitions gave DuPont scaled medical-device contract manufacturing. These are improving or stable competitive positions in structurally growing niches.

Diversified Industrials is more contested. Building Technologies competes in commoditizing construction materials exposed to a cyclical downturn, and the segment's FY25 revenue decline and flat organic Q1 reflect that pressure rather than share gains. The China SAMR antitrust investigation into the Tyvek business (opened April 2025, currently suspended) is a competitive/regulatory watch item, though China is only ~10% of sales and Tyvek-to-China just ~1%.

On balance the trajectory is mixed-to-improving: the high-quality half is gaining ground in secular markets while the cyclical half is holding rather than advancing. Competitive position scores 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**PFAS legacy liabilities** are the dominant idiosyncratic risk. Under the 2021 MOU with Chemours and Corteva, eligible PFAS costs are shared up to a $4B aggregate cap (DuPont's portion ~$1.4B; ~$730M paid through Q1 2026). The August 2025 New Jersey Settlement ($875M over 25 years, DuPont NPV share $186M) is pending judicial approval. Roughly 11,900 AFFF personal-injury cases remain in the South Carolina MDL — DuPont never manufactured AFFF and is named largely on fraudulent-transfer theory, but the filing explicitly states possible losses could exceed accruals. This tail is more bounded than it was (the MOU cap and the Qnity 44%/DuPont 56% allocation help) but it is not closed.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment** risk is elevated given the ~50%-of-assets concentration and the flagged Protection reporting unit, in a weak construction environment.

**Tax risk** on the spin/distribution structure exists — if Qnity, DowDuPont, or N&B distributions were deemed taxable, liabilities could be large, though mostly indemnified/shared.

**Counterparty reliance** on Qnity, Chemours, Corteva, and Dow to honor shared-liability obligations is a structural dependency.

**Stranded costs** from the divestitures are a margin watch item; the 2026 restructuring program's execution will determine whether margins re-rate.

Risk factors score 5.0. The PFAS tail is the swing variable: bounded by the MOU but explicitly capable of exceeding accruals, with a near-term dated event (June 24 hearing) that is two-sided.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Healthcare & Water continues compounding at high-single-digit-plus revenue with ~30% EBITDA margins; the 2026 restructuring eliminates stranded costs and lifts consolidated margins; the June 24 NJ hearing approves the settlement and removes a key PFAS overhang; the $2B buyback and reverse split tighten the share count; and the cleaner, investment-grade specialty profile earns a re-rating toward the profitable-peer median of 24x. On $2.62 next-year EPS at 24x, that points to ~$63, near the Street high. Bull target $62–64.

**Bear case.** Construction weakness deepens and drags Diversified Industrials; a Protection reporting-unit goodwill impairment lands; the PFAS tail expands beyond accruals (AFFF MDL or an adverse NJ ruling); and stranded costs prove sticky, holding margins down. The forward multiple compresses to ~15x on a haircut $2.30 EPS, implying ~$35. Bear target ~$35, roughly −27% from current.

**Base case.** Mid-single-digit consolidated growth, modest margin improvement from restructuring, and the NJ settlement approved without escalation. ~21x on $2.55 blended forward EPS gives a ~$54 base-case fair value, roughly +13% from $47.95 plus the ~1.7% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month growth-hold. The thesis rests on the restructuring-driven margin re-rate, continued Healthcare & Water growth, and PFAS overhang resolution.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NJ settlement approved at/after the June 24 hearing with no new material PFAS escalation, removing the dominant overhang.
- Two consecutive quarters of consolidated Operating EBITDA margin expansion toward the H&W ~30% level, confirming stranded-cost removal.
- Clustered open-market insider buying — none has appeared despite the stock trading well above the $40.89 ASR price, and its emergence would meaningfully strengthen conviction.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Protection (or other) reporting-unit goodwill impairment, or estimate revisions turning negative beyond −5% over 90 days.
- An adverse PFAS development materially above accruals (NJ ruling rejection, AFFF MDL expansion, or MOU cap breach risk).
- Diversified Industrials organic revenue declining beyond −5% YoY on deepening construction weakness.

**Reassessment timeline:** The June 24, 2026 NJ PFAS hearing is the next discrete event, followed by the Q2 2026 earnings release and the annual-meeting reverse-split vote.

## Conclusion

DuPont has executed a credible transformation: a halved debt load, a focused two-segment portfolio anchored by a genuinely high-quality Healthcare & Water franchise, and a clean first post-spin quarter that confirms the continuing business is profitable, growing, and cash-generative. The reported net loss and trailing P/E are accounting noise from discontinued operations and should be ignored.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because of the absence of asymmetry. At ~20x forward earnings on a mid-single-digit consolidated grower carrying an unbounded PFAS tail, the stock is fairly valued against profitable peers, the Street targets are anchors rather than stale floors, estimate revisions are steadily positive but not inflecting, and insiders have not added personally despite a substantial move off the low. The growth-hold thesis is supported by Healthcare & Water compounding, restructuring-driven margin upside, and PFAS resolution as a near-term catalyst on June 24, but it is balanced by cyclical drag in Diversified Industrials, goodwill-impairment exposure, and the PFAS swing factor. Maintain the position; upgrade on settlement clarity and confirmed margin expansion, downgrade on impairment or adverse PFAS developments.
