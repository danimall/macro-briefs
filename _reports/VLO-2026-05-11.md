## Executive Summary

Valero Energy is the world's largest independent petroleum refiner, currently operating 14 petroleum refineries with approximately 3.0 million barrels per day of throughput capacity following the April 2026 idling of the Benicia refinery. The company is predominantly positioned along the U.S. Gulf Coast. The stock has appreciated approximately 98% over the past year, driven by the Iran conflict/Strait of Hormuz closure that has pushed refining crack spreads to multi-year highs. Q1 2026 reported EPS of $4.22 beat consensus significantly, and forward estimates have been revised sharply upward, among the most aggressive upward revision trajectories in the Energy sector.

At $248, Valero trades at a discount to peer forward multiples. The company has retired 22% of its shares outstanding over the past three years, generates approximately $3.9–4.0B in annual free cash flow (FY2025 operating cash flow of $5.8B less ~$1.9B capital investments), and maintains investment-grade ratings with $10.8B in total liquidity as of Q1 2026. The central question is duration: how long does the Hormuz disruption sustain elevated crack spreads? With oil prices near or above $100 and diplomatic efforts repeatedly failing — most recently with Trump rejecting Iran's latest proposal on May 11 — the author's base case assumes continued elevated margins through at least H2 2026.

Key risks include the Port Arthur refinery explosion (March 2026) creating Q2 throughput headwinds, the California exit removing capacity, renewable diesel segment losses (-$156M in FY2025), and the binary nature of geopolitical resolution. The stock's 98% appreciation reflects substantial repricing, but with forward P/E at sub-9x on some consensus estimates, the market may still be pricing relatively rapid margin normalization that appears inconsistent with the conflict duration outlook.

## Company Overview

Valero operates across three segments: Refining (approximately 95% of 2025 external revenue, 14 operating refineries, ~3.0M bpd post-Benicia idling), Renewable Diesel (~2%, via 50/50 DGD JV with Darling Ingredients, ~1.2B gal/year), and Ethanol (~3%, 12 plants, ~1.7B gal/year). The company is headquartered in San Antonio, TX with approximately 9,800 employees.

The refinery network is concentrated on the U.S. Gulf Coast (Port Arthur 435K bpd, Corpus Christi 370K, St. Charles 340K, Texas City 260K, Houston 255K), with one U.K. refinery (Pembroke, 270K bpd) and one Canadian facility. This geographic positioning provides structural access to discounted domestic crude (WTI Midland/Houston) while serving export markets in Latin America — a competitive advantage magnified during periods of global crude supply disruption when Atlantic Basin refineries with access to cheap feedstock earn outsized margins.

Unlike integrated peers (ExxonMobil, Chevron) or partially diversified downstream operators (Phillips 66, Marathon Petroleum), Valero is a pure-play refiner with no upstream production, no retail networks, and limited chemicals exposure. This creates maximum leverage to crack spread cycles — higher earnings torque in strong margin environments, but also greater vulnerability when margins compress.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Growth Drivers (2026-2027):**

1. **Iran conflict / Hormuz closure sustaining elevated crack spreads.** The author's base case assumes sustained elevated refining margins through at least H2 2026, given repeated diplomatic failures, sustained oil near $100, and no operational pathway to Hormuz reopening in the near term.

2. **Estimate revision velocity is exceptional.** Analyst consensus estimates have been revised sharply upward over the past 90 days. This pace suggests analysts may be systematically underestimating the margin duration, a common pattern in supply-disruption cycles where consensus lags reality.

3. **Port Arthur restart.** According to Valero's Q1 2026 10-Q, the refinery resumed operations at reduced capacity following the March 23 fire, though efforts to assess damage and plan repairs remain ongoing. The company guided Q2 Gulf Coast throughput lower to reflect anticipated Port Arthur reductions. Full diesel hydrotreater rebuild timing and cost remain uncertain.

4. **California exit reduces drag.** Benicia (170K bpd) idling was completed in April 2026, eliminating high-cost, regulation-intensive operations. While capacity is lost, the margins on that capacity were structurally inferior to Gulf Coast operations. Valero continues to evaluate strategic alternatives for its remaining Wilmington refinery (135K bpd).

**Medium-Term Structural Supports:**
- Global refinery closures accelerating (including Valero's own, Phillips 66 closures, European shutdowns)
- Russian refinery damage from Ukrainian strikes reducing global supply
- New capacity additions delayed in emerging markets
- Jet fuel demand growing fastest among transport fuels
- EPS accretion from continued share buybacks

**Headwinds:**
- Renewable diesel segment: $156M loss in FY2025, triple headwinds from tariffs, tax credit changes, and EPA regulatory uncertainty
- Potential for rapid margin normalization if geopolitical resolution occurs
- Long-term EV adoption (decades-long timeline)

## Competitive Landscape

Valero's competitive position is strengthening for several reasons:

1. **Gulf Coast crude access advantage amplified by Hormuz closure.** With Middle Eastern crude largely unavailable, European and Asian refiners face feedstock cost pressure while Valero accesses discounted WTI Midland/Houston. The widening of crude differentials directly benefits Valero's margin structure.

2. **Capacity reductions improve industry structure.** Valero's own California exit, combined with Phillips 66 closures and European shutdowns, tightens the global refining system. The company is consolidating toward its highest-return assets.

3. **Scale creates cost absorption advantages.** At ~3.0M bpd, Valero can spread regulatory compliance, maintenance, and corporate costs across the industry's largest independent throughput base.

4. **DGD feedstock flexibility is a differentiator.** While the renewable diesel segment currently loses money, the DGD JV's ability to process 100% waste feedstocks (achieving lowest carbon intensity scores) positions it favorably as North American feedstock sourcing requirements under OBBB take effect. This could become a medium-term competitive advantage as foreign waste-oil competitors are restricted.

5. **Pure refining leverage to crack spreads and crude differentials.** The current geopolitical environment creates precisely the conditions — widening product-to-crude spreads and favorable Gulf Coast differentials — under which Valero's concentrated refining exposure generates maximum earnings.

Competitive risks include Marathon Petroleum's stronger diversification (midstream assets, retail operations), Phillips 66's chemicals/midstream earnings stability, and potential for new capacity additions in the Middle East (post-conflict) or India reducing structural tightness.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geopolitical Resolution Risk (HIGH impact):**
If the Iran conflict resolves and Hormuz reopens, crack spreads could compress 40-60% within 2-3 months. Oil falling to $85-95 would reduce the crude differential advantage. The author assesses this as a lower-probability near-term outcome based on repeated diplomatic failures (most recently Trump's May 11 rejection of Iran's latest proposal), but the magnitude of potential earnings decline makes it the single largest risk factor.

**2. Port Arthur Refinery Restart / Litigation:**
Per the Q1 2026 10-Q, the refinery has resumed operations at reduced capacity, but cause determination, damage assessment, and repair planning remain ongoing; timing and amounts are uncertain and not reasonably estimable. The company expects additional 2026 capital expenditures related to the incident, likely covered by insurance subject to retention. Potential litigation and regulatory risks remain.

**3. Renewable Diesel Structural Headwinds:**
The DGD JV swung from +$507M (FY2024) to -$156M (FY2025) in one year. Tariffs on imported feedstocks, tax credit restructuring (BTC to CFPC), and EPA RFS Set II proposals create ongoing uncertainty. Recovery requires feedstock cost normalization and regulatory clarity — neither is imminent.

**4. California Exit Execution:**
$1.1B impairment already taken on both Benicia and Wilmington. Valero continues to evaluate strategic alternatives for Wilmington (135K bpd). Residual asset retirement obligations, environmental liabilities, and potential future regulatory actions (the $3.25M air quality fine in April 2026 illustrates ongoing exposure) remain. The total potential capacity removal of 305K bpd (~10% of the pre-exit system) assumes both refineries are ultimately closed, though only Benicia has been completed to date.

**5. Regulatory/Political Risk:**
New York and Vermont "climate superfund" legislation could create strict liability for fossil fuel companies. Potential windfall/margin taxes in various jurisdictions. California SBx1-2 enforcement continues.

## Conclusion

Valero remains a BUY at $248 with a base case target of $275 over 6-9 months. The forward valuation remains well below peer medians despite maximum geopolitical tailwinds, and the company's Gulf Coast positioning, aggressive buyback program, and balance sheet strength provide structural support.

The stock has appreciated substantially, and the initial repricing gains are behind us. The incremental return from here depends on margin duration — specifically, whether the Hormuz disruption persists as the author's base case suggests. With diplomatic efforts repeatedly failing and oil sustained near $100, the risk/reward favors the long side, though with more modest expected returns and meaningful binary risk.

Position sizing should reflect the geopolitical binary: a 2-3% allocation is appropriate, with a hard stop if operational Hormuz reopening is confirmed. The Trump-Xi summit (May 14) represents a near-term binary event that could either reinforce the thesis (export control escalation → more supply disruption) or create headline risk (comprehensive Iran pressure → partial resolution pathway).

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrections to FCF ($3.9–4.0B vs. originally stated $5.1B), post-Benicia capacity (~3.0M bpd vs. 3.2M), and the acknowledgment that geopolitical probability assumptions are the author's scenario estimates rather than market-validated facts collectively reduce the quantitative support for the price target and buyback accretion projections, though the directional thesis remains intact.
