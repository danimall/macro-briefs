**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Bunge Global completed its transformative $10.6B acquisition of Viterra in July 2025, creating the largest publicly traded pure-play agribusiness company with $70B+ in revenue and operations spanning both hemispheres. The deal nearly tripled grain merchandising volumes and significantly expanded Canadian canola origination capacity, positioning Bunge directly into the structural demand for biofuel feedstocks driven by EPA blending mandates.

However, the stock at $122 has already appreciated 70% from its July 2025 low of $71.60, pricing in much of the recovery thesis. At a trailing P/E of 31.7x on depressed $4.93 EPS (reflecting the trough and integration costs), and ~15x on management's $7.50-8.00 FY2026 guidance, the valuation is not obviously cheap given $14.6B in total debt, goodwill impairment risk (Grain Merchandising only 9% above threshold), and the elevated interest expense now running at $724M annualized. Q1 2026 delivered a massive earnings beat ($1.83 vs. $0.90 estimate) but underlying quality was mixed — Tropical Oils benefited from mark-to-market gains while Grain Merchandising posted a $76M loss driven by the Iran conflict-related ocean freight cost surge.

The fundamental trajectory is improving (full-year estimates revised up 11.7% over 90 days), but the margin of safety at current levels is insufficient to recommend accumulation. We maintain HOLD pending evidence that: (a) integration synergies are materializing in reported segment margins, (b) the $15 EPS by 2030 trajectory is tracking, and (c) debt reduction begins in earnest.

## Company Overview

Bunge Global SA operates as a vertically integrated agribusiness connecting farmers to end consumers across four segments: Soybean Processing & Refining (53% of segment EBIT), Softseed Processing & Refining (22%), Grain Merchandising & Milling (20%), and Tropical Oils & Specialty Ingredients (5%). Post-Viterra, the company processes approximately 80M metric tons of oilseeds and grains annually, operating a global network of crushing plants, refineries, export terminals, and grain elevators.

The company's competitive positioning is distinguished from pure commodity traders by its processing orientation — crushing soybeans and canola into meal and oil generates more stable margins than pure merchandising. The Bunge-Chevron Ag Renewables JV positions the company as a feedstock supplier to the expanding renewable diesel sector.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings Trajectory:**

Revenue grew 32% to $70.3B in FY2025, almost entirely driven by the Viterra half-year contribution. Underlying organic growth was negligible. Net income declined 28% to $816M ($4.91 diluted EPS), continuing the normalization from the 2023 peak of $2.24B ($13.66 EPS).

Q1 2026 revenue of $21.9B (vs. $11.6B Q1 2025) reflects the first full-quarter Viterra comparison. Reported EPS of $0.35 was depressed by mark-to-market effects, but adjusted EPS of $1.83 beat consensus of $0.90 by 103%. This beat was driven primarily by Tropical Oils ($110M EBIT vs. $5M prior year) benefiting from rising palm oil prices and biofuel demand.

**Balance Sheet Concerns:**

Total debt of $14.6B represents a debt/equity ratio of 2.8x. Interest expense is running at $181M quarterly ($724M annualized), up 74% YoY. While $9.7B in unused credit facilities and $13.4B in readily marketable inventories provide liquidity, the leverage ratio leaves limited room for margin compression or working capital stress.

Goodwill of $3.1B+ carries specific impairment risk: the Grain Merchandising reporting unit is only 9% above its impairment threshold, and Global Cotton is only 15% above. Given that grain trading margins have been normalizing and the Iran conflict has driven freight costs higher (turning Q1 2026 Grain EBIT negative at -$76M), a goodwill charge within 12-18 months is a non-trivial probability.

**Cash Flow:**

Operating cash flow was negative $541M in Q1 2026, reflecting the seasonal inventory build during South American harvest — typical for the business but amplified by the much larger post-Viterra asset base. Full-year 2025 operating cash flow of $844M (vs. $1.9B in 2024) was reduced by integration costs and working capital expansion. Capex guidance of $1.5-1.7B for 2026 means free cash flow will remain constrained until operating margins stabilize.

## Growth Analysis

**Biofuels Structural Tailwind:**

The EPA's increased renewable volume obligations for 2026 create structural demand for vegetable oils as biodiesel/renewable diesel feedstocks. Bunge's expanded oilseed crushing capacity (both legacy and Viterra-contributed) directly benefits. Biofuel blending mandates provide visibility into demand growth independent of food consumption trends, forming the core growth thesis.

**$15 EPS by 2030 Target:**

Management's Investor Day framework implies compounding EPS at ~15% annually from the $7.75 midpoint of 2026 guidance to $15 by 2030. This requires: (1) Viterra synergies of $250-350M fully realized, (2) biofuels volumes growing 15-20% annually, (3) no sustained period of compressed crush margins. While plausible in a constructive commodity environment, it assumes everything goes right simultaneously.

**Estimate Revisions:**

Current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 14.7% over 30 days and 11.7% over 90 days following the Q1 beat and guidance raise. This positive momentum reflects a single quarter's outperformance; sustainability needs to be confirmed in Q2.

## Valuation Assessment

At $122, Bunge trades at:
- 31.7x trailing EPS ($4.93) — artificially elevated by the earnings trough
- ~15.7x FY2026E EPS ($7.75 midpoint of management guidance)
- ~12.9x consensus FY2026E EPS ($9.43)
- ~11.2x FY2027E EPS ($10.89)

Against peers: the Consumer Staples peer median P/E is 15.6x, making BG appear in-line on management guidance and cheap on consensus FY2026 and FY2027 estimates. However, the peer comparison is imperfect — BG's nearest true comparables are Cargill (private) and Louis Dreyfus (private). Among public analogs, ADM trades at ~12x forward earnings with similar structural characteristics.

The analyst consensus target median of $145 implies 19% upside, with a range of $116-150. At $122, the stock is already 5% above the low analyst target — limited downside protection from the street.

The critical valuation question: is the market correctly pricing the probability that $7.50-8.00 EPS (management guidance) represents a trough or a normalized state? If biofuel demand and Viterra synergies drive EPS toward $10+ by 2027, the stock is inexpensive at 11-12x. If grain trading margins remain compressed and interest costs persist at $700M+, the $7.50-8.00 range could represent fair-weather earnings that deteriorate further in a global recession.

## Competitive Landscape

Post-Viterra, Bunge occupies a uniquely strong position within the ABCD oligopoly as the largest publicly traded pure-play agribusiness. Key competitive advantages include:

1. **Processing orientation** — crush margins are more stable than pure trading margins, providing earnings visibility
2. **Hemispheric diversification** — year-round sourcing as harvests rotate between continents
3. **Scale in canola** — Viterra's Canadian operations make Bunge a dominant canola originator/processor, critical as canola oil displaces soybean oil in certain biofuel applications
4. **Biofuels integration** — the Chevron JV and expanded refining capacity provide direct exposure to mandated demand growth

The trajectory score decline from 7.5 to 6.5 in prior reports reflected integration uncertainty; with Q1 2026 showing the combined entity operating (albeit with grain merchandising stress), I'm moving this back to 7.0 as the fundamental franchise value of the combined entity is confirmed.

## Risk Assessment

**Tier 1 — Material Risks:**
- **Goodwill impairment:** If grain trading margins don't recover in H2 2026, a write-down ($500M-1B potential) is likely within 12-18 months.
- **Interest expense escalation:** At $724M annualized (31% of segment EBIT based on Q1 2026 annualized), the debt burden materially compresses equity returns. Any rate increases further squeeze.
- **Integration execution:** $467M already spent; remaining IT, compliance, and cultural integration is multi-year. Operating synergies ($250-350M targeted) not yet visible in reported results.

**Tier 2 — Moderate Risks:**
- **Geopolitical exposure:** Iran conflict directly impacting freight costs (visible in Q1). Ukraine operations (<2% of assets but headline risk). US-China tariff uncertainty affecting soybean trade flows.
- **Concentrated shareholders:** Glencore (~17%) + CPP (~14%) collectively hold ~34% with board nomination rights. Strategic decisions may not prioritize minority shareholder returns.
- **Spruce Point short report** (March 2026) characterizing BG as "non-economic roll-up" — creates periodic headline risk, though the thesis hasn't been substantiated by subsequent earnings.

**Tier 3 — Monitoring:**
- Mark-to-market volatility ($2.3B in unrealized derivative losses Q1 2026) obscures underlying performance
- Weather risk (La Niña/El Niño cycle impacts South American production)
- Biofuel mandate policy reversals under future administrations

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($140-150, +15-23%):**
- Viterra synergies begin appearing in H2 2026 results, segment EBIT margins expand
- Biofuel mandates drive sustained crush margin improvement, FY2026 achieves high end of $8.00 EPS or approaches consensus of $9.43
- Iran conflict resolution drives freight costs lower, Grain Merchandising returns to profitability
- Share repurchases resume ($3.2B authorization provides ~14% of market cap)
- Stock re-rates to 15x FY2027 EPS of $10.89 = $163

**Bear Case ($90-100, -18-26%):**
- Grain trading margins remain compressed; Grain Merchandising goodwill impairment of $500M-1B
- Interest expense stays at $700M+ with no meaningful debt reduction
- Biofuel mandate growth disappoints or gets rolled back under policy shifts
- Global recession compresses commodity volumes; Q2-Q4 2026 earnings miss
- Stock de-rates to 10x depressed earnings = $75-80 (extreme case)

**Base Case ($125-135, +2-11%):**
- FY2026 hits guidance midpoint of $7.75 EPS (or approaches consensus $9.43)
- Gradual integration progress but no step-function improvement until FY2027
- Some debt reduction via non-core asset sales (European margarines at ~$239M)
- Stock consolidates at 14-15x forward, reflecting partial credit for 2030 path

2. FY2026 guidance lowered below $7.00 EPS
3. Dividend cut or suspension of buyback authorization permanently

## Conclusion

Bunge Global is a structurally stronger franchise post-Viterra, with legitimate biofuels tailwinds and a dominant position in global oilseed processing. The Q1 2026 earnings beat and guidance raise confirm positive near-term momentum, and estimate revisions are trending favorably.

However, at $122, the stock has already captured much of the recovery from its $71.60 low. The forward ~15.7x on guided $7.75 is in-line with (not discounted to) peers. Material risks — $14.6B in debt, thin goodwill cushions, concentrated shareholders, and integration execution — warrant a higher required rate of return than the 2-11% base case upside provides.

The prior score trajectory showing decline from 6.3 to 5.5 was driven by deteriorating financial health and management quality scores during peak integration uncertainty. The Q1 beat and guidance raise justify stabilization at current levels, with competitive position recovering slightly as the combined entity demonstrates operational continuity. However, insufficient evidence exists to confirm a definitive recovery — the fundamental test comes in Q2-Q3 2026 when synergies either appear or don't.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Wait for either: (a) a meaningful pullback to $100-105 providing valuation cushion, or (b) two quarters of demonstrated synergy realization confirming the $15-by-2030 pathway.
