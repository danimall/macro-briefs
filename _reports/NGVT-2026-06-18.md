**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ingevity is a specialty chemicals company in the middle of a deliberate portfolio simplification into a two-business model: Performance Materials (automotive activated carbon, mid-50s% EBITDA margins) and Pavement Technologies (warm-mix asphalt additives, high-teens margins). The single most important fact about NGVT is the gap between its alarming screening metrics and its healthy underlying cash engine. Trailing GAAP EPS of -$4.15 and a negative trailing P/E, an Altman Z-score of 0.7, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 54.6x all flag distress. Those figures are artifacts of $293.1M in non-cash impairments in 2025 (APT goodwill, road-markings long-lived assets) that gutted retained earnings to a razor-thin $38.9M equity base. The operating business is not in distress: 2025 adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations was $373M (stable versus $376.5M in 2024 and $353M in 2023), free cash flow was $273.5M at a 23.4% margin, credit-agreement net leverage is 2.5x against a 4.0x covenant ceiling, and interest coverage is 6.2x.

The investment case rests on three legs: (1) the market re-rating "New Ingevity" as a cleaner, higher-margin pair of franchises once the APT divestiture closes by year-end 2026; (2) the resolution of the $113.2M BASF litigation (paid April 1, 2026), removing a multi-year overhang worth roughly 4.5% of market cap; and (3) a favorable mix shift as consumers migrate back toward hybrid vehicles, which use more and higher-value activated carbon than pure ICE vehicles. Management has reaffirmed 2026 guidance of $370-395M adjusted EBITDA and $4.70-5.20 adjusted EPS, and the company beat Q1 2026 estimates by 45% on adjusted EPS.

Against this, the near-term picture is muddier than the strategic story. Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS estimates have been revised down 5-7% over the past 30 days, the recovery in Pavement margins (after road-markings divestiture) is unproven, the 2028 Senior Notes refinancing is an unaddressed milestone that springs the revolver maturity forward, and the company has cycled through notable C-suite and board turnover under activist pressure. At ~$73 against a consensus mean target of $85.5, the asymmetry is positive but not wide enough to clear a BUY bar in a sector where company selection has historically struggled to beat the index. This is a HOLD with a constructive 12-month growth thesis.

## Company Overview

Ingevity was spun out of WestRock in May 2016 and is headquartered in North Charleston, SC. It currently reports three segments, but the portfolio is being actively reshaped:

- **Performance Materials (PM)** — the core franchise. Hardwood-based activated carbon for automotive gasoline-vapor emission control (ICE and hybrid vehicles), plus filtration. 2025 sales of $606.9M at a 53.8% segment EBITDA margin. This is a regulation-driven, high-barrier oligopoly competing against Kuraray/Norit and Chinese producers. North America is the most profitable region given stringent life-of-vehicle emission standards. Top-10 customers represent roughly 90% of segment sales (PHINIA, MAHLE, Kayser, Korea Fuel-Tech).
- **Performance Chemicals (PC)** — Pavement Technologies (Evotherm warm-mix asphalt, 2025 sales $302.6M) is the keeper. Road Markings ($97.9M of 2025 sales) was divested to PPG on April 15, 2026 for ~$65M. Pavement is tied to government infrastructure spending and is highly seasonal (70-75% of revenue April-September).
- **Advanced Polymer Technologies (APT)** — Caprolactone polymers from a single UK plant in Warrington. 2025 sales of $160.2M at a 17.2% EBITDA margin (Q1 2026). Being sold; management targets announcing a transaction before end of 2026.

The competitive moat rests on entrenched technical know-how, IP (Evotherm, caprolactone chemistry), and the regulatory "low-risk supplier" status of its automotive carbon, which locks in incumbents in a recall-averse OEM supply chain.

## Financial Analysis

**Reconciling GAAP losses with cash generation is the central analytical task.**

Revenue from continuing operations declined from $1,692M (2023) to $1,406M (2024) to $1,167.6M (2025). The bulk of that decline reflects the Performance Chemicals repositioning, reclassification of industrial specialties to discontinued operations, and genuine APT weakness from Chinese competition and tariffs, rather than core PM erosion.

The 2025 GAAP net loss of -$167.1M was driven by a $183.8M APT goodwill impairment and a $109.3M road-markings long-lived asset impairment. The 2024 loss of -$430.3M included a $306.6M PC goodwill impairment and large discontinued-ops charges. Goodwill is now nearly fully written off ($4.3M). These are non-cash charges; they do not reflect cash burn.

Adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations has been steady: $353M (2023), $376.5M (2024), $373M (2025). Operating cash flow was $331.2M in 2025 and free cash flow $273.5M, supported by modest maintenance capex of $40-60M annually. This is a high-conversion, low-capital-intensity cash engine.

Q1 2026 reinforced the operating picture: sales of $258M (+4% YoY, all price/FX), continuing-ops net income of $23.4M, adjusted EBITDA of $92M (35.5% margin), and adjusted EPS of $1.15 (+14% YoY), a 45% beat versus the $0.79 estimate. The quality of that quarter requires care: PM margin spiked to 59.2% on an estimated ~$6M inventory-build benefit ahead of a planned Q2 outage, which reverses, normalizing PM toward the mid-50s. PC margin collapsed to 1.0% on road-markings underutilization, but that drag is now divested. The 2026 margin progression will be noisy from these inventory swings and ~$12-18M of stranded divestiture costs.

**Balance sheet — read carefully.** Total debt is ~$1.2B: $550M of 3.88% Senior Notes due 2028, ~$518M revolver, $98.5M finance leases, and $38.7M of AR securitization. Stockholders' equity is $38.9M at Q1 2026, which is what produces the headline 54.6x D/E and 0.7 Altman Z. Credit-agreement net leverage is only 2.5x (versus 4.0x maximum) with 6.2x interest coverage (versus 3.0x minimum), and the company is in full covenant compliance. The revolver was amended on March 26, 2026, downsized to $750M and extended to 2031 (springing to 2028 if more than $250M of the 2028 notes remain outstanding 91 days before maturity). A naïve quant screen reads NGVT as near-bankruptcy; the filings show a self-help restructuring that is largely working.

The earnings surprise record is mixed but recently strong: +45.0% (Q1 2026), -10.2% (Q4 2025), +7.2% (Q3 2025), +17.2% (Q2 2025) — three beats and one miss over four quarters.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is qualitative and structural rather than a clean revenue-acceleration story, which tempers the score. Headline revenue is declining because the company is shedding businesses. The relevant question is the earnings power and margin profile of the remaining two franchises.

Three tailwinds support the forward case. First, the hybrid-vehicle resurgence is a favorable mix shift for Performance Materials, and the expiration of US EV tax credits in late Q3 2025 has accelerated consumer migration back toward hybrids. This de-risks the long-term EV bear case in the near term. Second, the divestiture of low-margin, cyclical road markings lifts the Performance Chemicals margin profile into the high teens. Third, management is extending activated-carbon expertise into filtration (food/beverage, medical, water) and exploring battery/energy-storage applications as a long-term hedge against secular ICE decline.

Against this, the near-term estimate revision trend is negative. Current-quarter EPS estimates have fallen 7.2% over 30 days (and 6.1% over 90 days), and next-quarter estimates are down 5.6% over 30 days. The full-year picture is more constructive — current-year EPS revised +4.6% over 90 days and next-year +8.0% over 90 days — but the most recent 30-day moves have turned modestly negative across most periods. This is not the +30% revision-velocity signal that flags an earnings inflection; it is a company in transition with choppy quarterly visibility. The 2026 guidance implies roughly flat-to-modestly-up earnings on a smaller revenue base, with EBITDA margin expansion offsetting divested volume.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here given the impairment-driven GAAP loss. The right lens is forward adjusted EPS and EV/EBITDA. At ~$73 on the consensus current-year estimate of $4.93, NGVT trades at roughly 14.8x forward earnings; on next-year's $5.75, ~12.7x. On EV/EBITDA, with ~$2.5B equity plus ~$1.2B net debt against ~$380M of 2026 EBITDA, the multiple is roughly 9.7x.

Peer comparison is mixed and the specialty-chemicals set is heterogeneous. Peer median P/E is 21.3x (MTX 14.9x, IOSP 18.8x, AVNT 21.3x, FUL 22.0x, with KWR distorted at 581.6x). NGVT's ~14.8x forward sits at a discount to the median, which is partially justified by higher leverage, customer concentration in PM, the secular EV question, and the unproven margin recovery; a clean two-franchise "New Ingevity" with mid-50s% PM margins and 23% FCF conversion arguably warrants narrower than the current discount once APT closes and Pavement margins prove out.

The analyst consensus range of $75 (low) to $92 (high), with a mean of $85.5 and median of $87.5, implies roughly 17-20% upside to the mean from current levels. That is meaningful but not extreme, and the negative near-term revision trend argues against treating those targets as floors. I am not assigning a high valuation score because the discount-to-peers thesis depends on execution milestones (APT close, margin normalization) that have not yet been delivered, and because a compressed multiple on a transition story carries genuine value-trap risk until the simplification is complete. A low absolute multiple alone is not a buy signal; the catalyst here exists but is not yet realized.

## Competitive Landscape

Performance Materials enjoys the most defensible position: a regulation-driven, technically entrenched oligopoly where OEMs are recall-averse and reluctant to switch qualified suppliers. The main threat is technological and secular (long-term EV adoption, sealed gas-tank technology) rather than share loss to direct competitors. The hybrid resurgence is a near-term positive for mix and volume.

Pavement Technologies competes with Nouryon, Arkema, and Zydex, differentiating on consultative civil-engineering-plus-chemistry selling and Evotherm warm-mix IP. It benefits from infrastructure spending and recycling/sustainability trends but is exposed to government budget cycles and weather seasonality.

APT, being exited, competes with Daicel, Hunan Juren, and BASF in caprolactone, and is pressured by Chinese entrants in paint protective film, a key reason for the divestiture. The competitive trajectory of the remaining two franchises is stable-to-improving once the dilutive, contested APT line is removed.

## Risk Assessment

1. **EV/secular transition risk to Performance Materials** — the core long-term thesis risk. Near-term this is a tailwind (hybrid shift), but a faster EV transition or sealed-tank adoption would erode automotive carbon demand. Management's filtration and energy-storage diversification is early-stage.
2. **Customer concentration in PM** — top-10 customers represent ~90% of segment sales on a purchase-order basis, with limited contractual recourse if a customer departs.
3. **APT divestiture execution** — the sale may not close, may fetch less than hoped, or may trigger further impairment or stranded-cost adjustments. APT also remains exposed to Chinese competition.
4. **2028 Senior Notes refinancing** — the revolver maturity springs forward to 2028 if more than $250M of the notes remain outstanding near maturity. This is the most concrete balance-sheet milestone to monitor.
5. **Pavement government/infrastructure dependence, seasonality, and weather sensitivity.**
6. **Single-site production risk** — caprolactone only at Warrington (UK), honeycomb only at Waynesboro (GA); plus third-party utility dependence and shared environmental liability at Covington.
7. **Leadership churn** — CFO transition (Phillip Platt succeeding Mary Dean Hall), multiple Item 5.02 filings, and a 2025 proxy contest with Vision One Fund ($8.2M of costs). Activist pressure is consistent with the value-surfacing strategy, but governance instability is a real overhang.

The BASF litigation is now resolved and paid, removing a binary risk but creating a Q2 2026 cash outflow that is excluded from FCF guidance.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and provides no contradicting signal to the fundamental view. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 29.9% against 41.8% one-year historical volatility — IV is cheap (spread -11.8%, 3rd percentile within the current chain), suggesting the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst and that any directional position can be expressed cheaply via options. Put/call skew is balanced (2.3%) and the put/call OI ratio collapsed to 0.04 (from 1.08 in early May), indicating bearish hedging has unwound. The term structure is in contango (29.9% near-term versus 33.3% six-month), a normal/complacent shape. Short interest is low at 3.8% of float (3.7 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The implied ±7.0% move on the nearest expiry frames the near-term range.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The portfolio simplification completes: APT sells by year-end 2026 at a reasonable value, Pavement margins normalize to the high teens, and PM holds mid-50s% margins on a favorable hybrid mix. The market re-rates a clean two-franchise company generating ~$380M EBITDA and ~$230M FCF (ex-BASF) toward the peer median multiple. Buybacks ($245.6M remaining authorization, $300M targeted through 2027, executed at $52-67 below current levels) shrink the share count meaningfully against a 34.8M share base. Estimate revisions turn positive as the simplified model proves out. Bull target ~$92 (consensus high), roughly +27% from current.

**Bear case.** The APT sale slips or closes at a disappointing value, triggering further impairment. Pavement margin recovery underwhelms, near-term estimate cuts continue, and the 2028 notes refinancing becomes a financing-cost headwind in a higher-for-longer rate environment. The EV/sealed-tank secular question reasserts itself, compressing the PM multiple. Customer concentration produces a volume air-pocket. Bear target ~$58-60, roughly -18% to -20%, back toward the multiple compression seen post-Q1.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-18 month story tied to the completion of the portfolio transformation.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) APT sale announced and closed at a value that confirms the SOTP thesis; (2) two consecutive quarters of positive EPS estimate revisions alongside Pavement margins demonstrably in the high teens; (3) a clear refinancing path for the 2028 Senior Notes that removes the revolver maturity spring.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) APT sale terminates or triggers a further material impairment; (2) covenant net leverage rises toward 3.5x or interest coverage falls below 4.5x; (3) loss of a top-3 PM customer or a guidance cut below the $370M EBITDA floor; (4) PM volumes contract on an accelerated EV shift not offset by hybrid mix.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (which will show the BASF cash outflow and normalized PM margin), followed by any APT transaction announcement and the year-end 2026 transformation completion target.

## Conclusion

Ingevity is a self-help restructuring whose screening metrics understate its operational health, and whose strategic simplification offers a credible path to a re-rating. The cash engine is intact, the BASF overhang is resolved, the hybrid mix shift is a near-term tailwind for the core franchise, and the buyback is being executed below current prices. The constraints on conviction are the negative near-term estimate revisions, the unproven Pavement margin recovery, the pending (not closed) APT sale, the 2028 refinancing milestone, and governance churn under activist involvement. At ~$73 against an $85.5 consensus mean, the risk/reward is positive but not asymmetric enough to clear a BUY threshold, particularly in a sector where company-specific selection has historically failed to generate alpha and where cheap multiples on transition stories have repeatedly proven to be value traps absent a realized catalyst. Recommendation: **HOLD** with a constructive bias. Upgrade on a confirmed APT close plus stabilizing estimates; revisit at Q2 earnings.
