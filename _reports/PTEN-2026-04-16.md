## Executive Summary

Patterson-UTI Energy is the product of a 2023 merger with NexTier Oilfield Solutions and acquisition of Ulterra, creating one of the largest U.S. land drilling-and-completion platforms. The combined company generated $4.83B in revenue in fiscal 2025, down 10.2% year-over-year, with the U.S. active rig count falling from 112 rigs/day to 100 — and further to 90 rigs as of March 2026. Adjusted EBITDA declined 22.3% to $918M. The business is cash-generative ($372M FCF in 2025) but GAAP-negative ($94M net loss), with depreciation consuming 19.5% of revenue and an $885M goodwill impairment in 2024 acknowledging the NexTier acquisition was overpriced relative to current economics.

The stock has rallied 80%+ from its October 2025 lows near $5.10 to approximately $9.92, driven by a natural gas price spike to $7.72/MMBtu in January 2026, a Q4 earnings beat, and a 25% dividend increase. At $9.92, the stock trades above the sell-side consensus target of $10.29 (mean) and well above the $8.55 average of aggregated targets. Forward estimates project continued losses through FY2027 (-$0.31 current year, -$0.14 next year), and the rig backlog has eroded 65% over three years to $291M. Directors have been liquidating shares aggressively — one exited his entire position — with zero open-market purchases at any point during the downturn. The risk/reward at current levels is unfavorable.

## Company Overview

Patterson-UTI operates three segments: Drilling Services (32% of 2025 revenue, $1.56B), Completion Services (60%, $2.89B), and Drilling Products (7%, $344M, the Ulterra PDC drill bit business). The company runs 152 marketed land-based drilling rigs, 137 of which are Tier-1 super-spec, concentrated in West Texas (70 rigs), Appalachia (21), Rockies (18), and Oklahoma (15). International exposure is limited to Colombia (7 rigs), Ecuador (1 rig), and a recently signed multi-year lease for 2 rigs in Argentina's Vaca Muerta.

The 2023 merger strategy was to create a vertically integrated drilling-plus-completions platform. Integration is largely complete ($1M residual costs in 2025, down from $98M in 2023). The strategic logic of offering bundled services at lower total cost to E&P operators is sound in theory but has been tested by a falling rig count environment that compresses pricing across all segments simultaneously.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is negative.** Revenue peaked at $5.38B in 2024 (the first full year of NexTier consolidation), then fell 10.2% to $4.83B in 2025. Drilling Services revenue declined 9.3% on 12 fewer average rigs/day (100 vs. 112). Completion Services revenue declined 11.6% despite flat pumping hours, meaning the entire revenue decline was from pricing deterioration — the most bearish possible decomposition of a revenue miss. Average WTI crude declined from $76.62 to $65.39.

**Profitability is structurally challenged under current activity levels.** GAAP operating income was -$41M in 2025 (-0.3% margin), reflecting $940M of D&A on a $4.83B revenue base (19.5% of revenue). Adjusted EBITDA of $918M represents a 19% margin, down from 22% in 2024. Completion services adjusted gross margin compressed from 17.8% to 14.9%. Drilling services margins held better at 37.3% (down from 40.4%), and drilling products at 42.9% (down from 45.7%).

**Cash generation remains the bull case anchor.** Operating cash flow of $961M and free cash flow of $372M demonstrate the business generates real cash despite GAAP losses. FCF yield on the current $3.95B market cap is approximately 9.4%. The company returned $187M to shareholders in 2025 ($122M dividends + $65M buybacks), representing 50% of adjusted FCF.

**Balance sheet is adequate but carries impairment risk.** Cash of $421M, total debt of $1.23B (investment-grade rated), $495M undrawn revolver, total liquidity of $916M. Debt maturities are staggered ($483M at 3.95% due 2028, $345M at 5.15% due 2029, $400M at 7.15% due 2033). Annual interest expense of $70.5M is well covered by OCF. However, remaining goodwill of $487M (allocated to Ulterra) had only an ~8% cushion in the Q2 2025 impairment test — a 100bp increase in discount rate or decrease in revenue growth would push fair value below carrying value.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are poor on a 12-month horizon. Every leading indicator points to continued contraction:

**Backlog erosion:** U.S. drilling backlog has declined from $830M (Dec 2022) to $291M (Dec 2025) — a 65% decline. Only 9% extends beyond 2026. Average term contract rigs decline from 49 in Q1 2026 to 27 for full-year 2026, exposing the majority of the fleet to spot pricing.

**Rig count decline:** The U.S. rig count fell from 131 in Q1 2023 to 93 in Q4 2025, and further to 90 in March 2026. Management acknowledges activity is below levels needed to hold U.S. production steady — a structurally bullish argument for the eventual cycle turn, but one that provides no near-term earnings recovery.

**Estimate trajectory:** Forward EPS estimates are improving directionally (current year revised to -$0.31 from -$0.37 over 90 days, next year to -$0.14 from -$0.16). The 90-day improvement trend is real but the absolute level remains negative through FY2027. A return to profitability requires either a substantial oil price increase or a reversal in rig count trends — neither of which is base-case.

**Natural gas as potential catalyst:** The January 2026 spike to $7.72/MMBtu is a genuine positive for gas-directed activity in the Haynesville and Appalachia, where PTEN has meaningful rig exposure. If gas prices sustain above $5/MMBtu, incremental gas-directed drilling could add 5-10 rigs to the company's count. This is the single most credible near-term growth catalyst, but natural gas prices are volatile and the current elevated level is partly conflict-driven.

## Valuation Assessment

At $9.92, PTEN trades at:
- **Negative trailing and forward P/E** (meaningless for comparison)
- **~4.3x EV/EBITDA** on 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $918M (EV ~$3.95B equity + $1.23B debt - $0.42B cash = $4.76B)
- **~9.4% FCF yield** on 2025 FCF of $372M
- **~0.73x debt/equity**
- **~11% below 52-week high** of $11.75

Against peers: HP trades at a negative P/E (-9.0x), Valaris at 3.6x earnings, and Noble Corp at 20.9x. The peer median P/E of 20.9x is not applicable given PTEN's negative earnings. EV/EBITDA is the relevant metric, and 4.3x is optically cheap for an asset-heavy cyclical.

The FCF yield of 9.4% and 3.8% dividend yield provide real value support. However, at $9.92 the stock trades above the sell-side mean target of $10.29 (3.7% upside) and above the median of $11.00 (10.9% upside). The low target of $7.00 implies 29% downside. The risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside at current levels — the stock has already discounted most near-term positive catalysts (Q4 beat, dividend raise, nat gas spike).

## Competitive Landscape

PTEN is one of the top 2-3 U.S. land drillers by rig count, competing with Helmerich & Payne and Nabors in drilling, and with Halliburton, Liberty Energy, and ProPetro in completions. The company's 137 Tier-1 super-spec rigs represent a modern, competitive fleet, and the Ulterra drill bit business provides differentiated vertical integration.

However, the competitive dynamics are harsh. Completion services pricing deteriorated throughout 2025 despite stable demand (flat pumping hours), indicating excess capacity and aggressive pricing behavior by competitors. E&P consolidation (Exxon/Pioneer, Chevron/Hess) concentrates buying power. The company's top 10 customers represent 57% of revenue, and its largest single customer accounts for ~12%.

The Vertex automated completions system and Cortex rig operating system represent genuine technology differentiation, but these advantages are incremental in a market where the primary decision variable for E&P customers is price-per-foot drilled.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity price dependency is the dominant risk.** The entire business model requires sustained E&P capital spending, which requires sustained oil and gas prices. WTI averaged $65.39 in 2025, and prolonged sub-$60 pricing would further compress rig counts and pricing. Current oil prices above $100 (driven by the Iran conflict) could eventually translate into higher U.S. activity, but E&P operators have demonstrated discipline in not increasing drilling in response to elevated spot prices — the traditional supply response function is broken, which limits PTEN's upside capture.

**Goodwill impairment risk is tangible.** The remaining $487M of goodwill (Ulterra) has only an ~8% valuation cushion. Any deterioration in the drill bit market could trigger a material write-down.

**Completion services margin compression has no clear floor.** The 290bp margin decline in 2025 occurred in a flat-activity environment. If completion activity declines, margins will compress further on fixed cost deleveraging.

**Insider selling is a significant warning signal.** Director Robert Drummond sold his entire position (~$5M) over February-March 2026. Director James Carl Stewart sold over 798,000 shares ($4.7M+) through December 2025. No insider purchased shares at any point during the downturn. The absence of any open-market buying — even when the stock traded at $5-6 — indicates insiders do not view the equity as undervalued at materially lower prices than today.

**Tariff risk is newly elevated.** The 10-K explicitly identifies 2025 trade policies as contributing to market deterioration. Steel tariffs increase drill pipe and rig component costs. This is a cost headwind with no pricing offset in a weak demand environment.

## Investment Thesis

Oil prices remain elevated above $80, gradually lifting rig counts. Completion services pricing stabilizes as competitors exit. The 9.4% FCF yield and 3.8% dividend yield provide a valuation floor. EV/EBITDA rerates to 5.5-6.0x on improved earnings visibility, implying $12-13 per share.

The stock retraces to its October 2025 lows in the $5-6 range.

**Base Case ($8-9):** Rig counts stabilize around 88-92. Completion margins bottom near 13-14%. FCF generation continues but declines to $250-300M. The stock drifts back toward the $8-9 range as the conflict premium fades and forward losses persist through FY2027.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The most identifiable catalyst is oil price normalization as the Iran conflict de-escalates. PTEN's rally from $5.10 to $9.92 is substantially conflict-driven — elevated oil and gas prices are the primary reason investors anticipate an eventual activity recovery. If oil returns to $70-80 (still elevated vs. 2025 average), the rig count recovery thesis weakens materially. Secondary catalyst: Q1 2026 earnings (likely late April/early May) may disappoint if the March rig count of 90 translates into weaker-than-expected revenues and continued margin compression.

**Timing window:** Q1 2026 earnings in late April/early May is the nearest discrete event. Oil price normalization is harder to time — conditional on geopolitical developments.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 11.5% of float with 3.0 days to cover. Elevated but not crowded. Institutional ownership is meaningful. The stock has already squeezed 80%+ off lows, which reduces near-term squeeze risk — shorts positioned at $5-6 have largely been flushed.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options IV is extremely rich (102% near-term), which makes protective calls expensive for short positions. Thin options liquidity (41 contracts across 4 expirations) limits hedging ability. Balanced put/call skew provides no directional confirmation.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $5.50-7.00 implies 29-45% downside from $9.92. Upside risk to $12-13 implies 21-31% upside. Risk/reward is approximately 1.5:1 to 2:1 depending on scenario weighting. Options cost and conflict-driven tail risk make the short mechanically difficult despite favorable fundamental setup.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental bear case is sound, but the stock is driven by oil prices in an active conflict environment with significant upside tail risk (Hormuz escalation → $130+ oil → rapid rig count recovery narrative). Actively shorting into a hot war environment with oil above $100 carries unacceptable left-tail risk. Avoid owning the stock, but do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** Consider upgrading to HOLD or BUY if: (1) U.S. rig count stabilizes above 95 for two consecutive quarters, (2) completion services adjusted gross margins recover above 17%, or (3) the stock retreats to $6.50-7.00 (representing 5-6x EV/EBITDA on trough EBITDA of ~$750M), which would provide adequate margin of safety for a cyclical trough entry.

**Timeline:** The headwinds (falling rig counts, completion pricing pressure, negative EPS) are expected to persist through at least Q3 2026. A turn in activity levels requires either sustained commodity price improvement or a sharp decline that forces marginal producers to sell assets and reduce supply.

## Conclusion

Patterson-UTI Energy is a well-managed, scaled oilfield services platform with genuine technological advantages and meaningful cash generation. However, the stock at $9.92 has already priced in the Q4 earnings beat, the dividend raise, and a significant portion of the commodity price uplift from the Iran conflict. Forward earnings remain negative through FY2027. The backlog is eroding rapidly. Completion services pricing is under sustained pressure. Insiders are liquidating aggressively with no open-market purchases. The stock trades above sell-side consensus with limited upside to the high target of $13.00 (31%) against meaningful downside to the low target of $7.00 (29%) and the bear case of $5.50 (45%).

Oilfield services stocks in structurally declining activity environments with negative earnings, heavy insider selling, and above-consensus valuations do not offer favorable risk/reward. AVOID.
