## Executive Summary

Houlihan Lokey reported fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) revenue of $511M, down 15.5% from $605M, with GAAP diluted EPS of $1.15 versus $1.42 and adjusted EPS of $1.35 against a $1.74 non-GAAP consensus — a 22.2% miss and the second consecutive shortfall after an 11.7% miss in the March quarter. The stock fell 7.7% on July 30 (close $139.08 to $128.34, with an intraday low of $112.83 that set the 52-week low) and now sits 39.8% below the September 2025 high of $211.78.

The mechanism matters more than the magnitude. Corporate Finance revenue fell 24% ($303M from $398M) while closed transactions rose 2% (127 from 125). The implied average fee per closed CF transaction fell from $3.18M to $2.39M, a 25% decline. Management attributes this to transaction mix and states it "does not represent a short-term trend." That is an assertion by a self-interested narrator, and the company's own Item 1A discloses active fee-based share competition: "a number of independent investment banks... have emerged, with several showing rapid growth... there could be pricing pressures... some of our competitors may seek to obtain increased market share by reducing fees." One quarter of data cannot separate the two explanations. Compounding the problem, the countercyclical hedge failed to hedge: Financial Restructuring revenue fell 8% with closed transactions down 34%, and FR is now 20% of revenue versus 27% in FY2024.

This is genuine fundamental deterioration rather than a valuation-driven drawdown, and it justifies a downgrade from the prior BUY. Consensus FY2027 adjusted EPS has been cut from $8.53 to $6.99 (-18.0% over 90 days, -14.9% in 30 days); FY2028 is down 7.3% to $8.83. Sequential adjusted EPS has declined four consecutive quarters: $2.14, $1.84, $1.94, $1.63, $1.35. Offsetting this, the business remains structurally sound: zero funded debt, $797M of liquid assets, FY2026 free cash flow of $681.8M on a 26.0% margin, a compensation ratio held at 64.3% through the revenue decline, and a CFO who bought $500,570 of stock in the open market four days after the 10-Q. At 20.5x trailing GAAP EPS against a 23.0x peer median and 14.5x the FY2028 consensus, the shares are no longer priced for growth. With no forward revenue visibility and no catalyst before the late-October print, the call is HOLD.

## Company Overview

Houlihan Lokey is an advisory-only investment bank founded in 1972, headquartered in Los Angeles, with more than 1,900 financial professionals (approximately 2,800 total employees) across thirty-plus offices, serving more than 2,000 clients annually. Three segments:

- **Corporate Finance** — mid-cap M&A (self-defined as sub-$1B transaction value) plus Capital Solutions. FY2026 revenue $1,744.6M (66.7% of total), segment profit $581.2M (33.3% margin). 251 Managing Directors at 3/31/26, 260 at 6/30/26.
- **Financial Restructuring** — debtor/creditor advisory, liability management. FY2026 revenue $528.7M (20.2%), segment profit $179.1M (33.9%). 59 MDs at 3/31/26, 58 at 6/30/26.
- **Financial and Valuation Advisory** — fairness opinions, illiquid-asset valuation, dispute resolution (partly billed hourly). FY2026 revenue $344.2M (13.1%), segment profit $93.6M (27.2%). 44 MDs at 3/31/26, 47 at 6/30/26.

The firm conducts no lending, trading, or research. The 10-K presents this as a conflict-avoidance advantage and, in Item 1A, as a competitive disadvantage against balance-sheet competitors "who are able to, and often do, provide financing or market making services that are often a crucial component of the types of transactions on which we advise." Both statements sit in the same filing.

Approximately 850 employee shareholders own roughly 21% of equity, none more than 2% individually. Class B shares (15.27M at 3/31/26) carry ten votes and are held by the HL Voting Trust: ~22% economic interest, ~74% of voting power. HLI is a controlled company under NYSE rules, though a majority of directors are independent and the three key committees are fully independent.

**Share-count note.** At 6/30/26 there were 54.2M Class A and 15.7M Class B shares outstanding, 69.9M total, with identical economic rights. At $127.58 that is an $8.92B equity value. The supplied $6.94B market cap divided by price implies ~54.4M shares — Class A only, a float-based convention. Per-share metrics (P/E, dividend yield) are unaffected; any enterprise-value or price-to-book screen built on $6.94B understates HLI's valuation by roughly 22%. Separately, the supplied forward P/E equals the trailing P/E (21.43x on EPS of $6.22, implying a $133.26 price) and is not a genuine forward figure; on the $127.58 basis trailing GAAP P/E is 20.5x.

### Cash flow and balance sheet

FY2026 operating cash flow was $704.1M against $424.2M of net income (1.66x conversion) but down from $848.6M in FY2025 despite higher earnings. Two identified causes: unbilled work-in-progress consumed $115.1M of cash versus a $33.8M contribution in FY2025 (balance $157.8M to $271.2M, a 72% increase against 10% revenue growth, partially unwinding to $255M by 6/30/26), and cash taxes paid doubled to $187.2M from $93.3M on a flat 24.6% effective rate. FY2026 free cash flow was $681.8M after $22.3M of capex (down from $39.7M and $66.7M as an office build-out cycle completed), a 26.0% FCF margin and a 7.6% yield on the $8.92B equity value.

Quarterly cash flow cannot be annualized. Q1 FY27 OCF was negative $336M (Q1 FY26 negative $132M) because the May bonus payment lands in Q1: accrued salaries and bonuses fell $481M from $1,077M to $589M. Cash fell from $1,189M to $745M and securities from $170M to $52M. This is working-capital seasonality.

At 6/30/26: total assets $3,717M, total liabilities $1,352M, redeemable non-controlling interest (temporary equity) $110M, stockholders' equity $2,255M. No principal outstanding under the $150M Bank of America revolver (maturing 8/19/2030, SOFR + 0.95%, 2.00x leverage covenant, compliant). The reported 0.79x debt/equity consists of operating items — accrued compensation $1,077M at year-end, operating leases $492M, payables $136M — not borrowings. Genuine long-duration obligations are the leases: $644.8M undiscounted, ten-year weighted-average term at 5.5%. An Altman Z-Score of 2.53 is not decision-relevant here; the working-capital component computes to zero because the balance sheet is unclassified, and the model's inputs assume inventory and funded debt.

Two balance-sheet items deserve weight. Goodwill of $1,395.9M plus $204.2M of other intangibles equals 37% of assets and 68% of stockholders' equity, with $1,128.0M of the goodwill in Corporate Finance, the segment whose revenue just fell 24%. The 10-K lists "a prolonged decline in our stock price and market capitalization" among interim impairment triggers, which a 39.8% drawdown makes live. Separately, the Audere Partners acquisition (closed February 2026, controlling interest in a France-based advisory firm) was consolidated with only $2.539M of net cash out; the redeemable NCI was recorded at $98.846M fair value and remeasured to $110.554M within roughly two months (+13.4%), with the increase charged to additional paid-in capital. The remainder is subject to put/call features exercisable over approximately three years. This is a real and growing cash obligation of ~$110M that appears in neither total liabilities nor permanent equity. Put/call structures are standard for European advisory partnership acquisitions and function as seller-partner retention; the redemption formula and total cash required at full exercise are not disclosed.

## Growth Analysis

The two-year record is strong: total revenue up 36.7% from FY2024 to FY2026, CF up 57.6%, international revenue from $570.1M to $842.2M (32.2% of total). CF Managing Directors went from 240 at 3/31/25 to 260 at 6/30/26; CF goodwill rose $110.0M in FY2026 alone. Because HLI does not disclose acquisition revenue contribution, organic versus acquired growth cannot be decomposed; Waller Helms (December 2024) and Audere (February 2026) plus $84.995M and $21.072M of shares issued as acquisition consideration in FY2025 and FY2026 indicate a meaningful inorganic component.

Forward visibility is effectively zero, a structural feature rather than a disclosure gap. Fees are milestone-contingent: "for advisory engagements that do not result in the successful consummation of a transaction, we are not paid a fee other than the reimbursement of certain out-of-pocket expenses and, in some cases, a modest retainer." HLI uses the practical expedient not to disclose remaining performance obligations for contracts under one year, and RPO for longer contracts "was not material." Deferred income was $38.9M at 3/31/26 and $43M at 6/30/26. None of these figures constitute backlog. This is why one quarter's mix shift produced a 16% revenue decline.

## Competitive Landscape

HLI's stated niche is sub-$1B sell-side M&A, "an attractive segment that is underserved by bulge-bracket investment banks," with a claimed leadership position in sub-$1B sell-side volume sourced to LSEG league tables the company states it has not independently verified. FVA's fairness- and solvency-opinion franchise is genuinely defensible and grew 13% through the downturn on a 9% increase in Fee Events.

Three pressures on trajectory:

**Fee competition is named by the company.** Item 1A's language about newly emerged independents seeking share by reducing fees is unusually direct for a risk factor, and it lands in the same filing that attributes a 25% average-fee decline to mix. The filing does not resolve which applies.

**The restructuring hedge is thinner and did not work.** FR fell from 27% of revenue in FY2024 to 20% in FY2026, and in Q1 FY27 declined alongside CF rather than offsetting it. Even a full restructuring upcycle now covers a smaller share of any CF shortfall than it did two years ago.

**Structural inability to finance.** HLI concedes twice that financing and market making are "often a crucial component" of the transactions it advises on. Capital Solutions intermediates third-party private credit instead, so HLI depends on relationships rather than capital commitments.

The AI risk factor is more pointed than boilerplate for a firm whose product is analytical labor and whose FVA segment bills partly by the hour. The 10-K discloses competitor AI adoption as a risk without any offsetting disclosure of HLI's own deployment.

Retention is the binding constraint. With 354 MDs and no captive balance sheet, defection is the primary durability risk, and the 10-K notes state and federal erosion of restrictive-covenant enforceability "including California," where HLI is headquartered. Unrecognized equity compensation was $497M at 6/30/26 (from $436M a year earlier) amortizing over ~2.0 years — roughly $250M annually of committed expense that doubles as the retention mechanism. A share price 40% off its high weakens that mechanism precisely when competitors recruit.

## Risk Assessment

**Second consecutive miss.** With no backlog and milestone-contingent fees, quarterly revenue can move 15%+ on mandate timing. The next report (late October) carries a genuine two-sided risk, and consensus has been cut 18.5% for the current quarter in 30 days without yet establishing a floor.

**Corporate Finance goodwill impairment.** $1,128M of CF goodwill against a segment with revenue down 24%, with the company's own trigger list including prolonged share-price and market-capitalization decline plus key-personnel attrition. A charge would be non-cash and would not affect operating cash flow, the dividend, or the covenant, but it would hit reported equity and GAAP EPS and would supply a headline that validates the bear case.

**Fee compression versus mix.** If durable, roughly a quarter of CF revenue per deal is permanently gone and both the base and bull cases require rebuilding on a lower fee base.

**MD attrition.** Amplified by the drawdown and by weakening restrictive-covenant enforceability in California.

**FR-specific clawback.** Bankruptcy courts and U.S. Trustees can challenge fees and require repayment from clients that subsequently file. Net bad debt expense was $7.7M in FY2026 and $9.3M in FY2025; the allowance ended FY26 at $21.2M with $7.0M written off.

**International and tax concentration.** 32.2% of FY2026 revenue and $199.0M of $562.3M pre-tax income were international. A 10% USD depreciation would have moved other comprehensive income by ~$114M at 6/30/26. Germany and the UK alone accounted for $54.2M of $187.2M of FY2026 cash taxes. $546M of the 6/30/26 cash sat in foreign subsidiaries. Four GBP forwards ($151M notional) plus one EUR forward were open at 6/30/26 versus none a year earlier.

**Governance.** Class B is at ~22% of shares against the 20% Final Conversion Date threshold, declining mechanically as Class B converts on transfer (1.36M in FY26, 1.70M in FY25, 1.94M in FY24). Crossing the threshold shifts voting power from ~74% to ~22% for the Voting Trust with no change in economic ownership; the 10-K frames the consequences as increased vulnerability to hostile takeovers or activists, while a reasonable investor could read it as a governance improvement. The December 30, 2025 Amended and Restated Voting Trust Agreement replaced the 2015 agreement in its entirety; the trustees may vote "in their sole and absolute discretion... without fiduciary duties of any kind." What specifically changed is unverified. Two Item 5.02 8-Ks within four months (2025-10-06 and 2026-01-29) are unexamined.

**Closed regulatory matter.** The August 8, 2023 SEC off-channel-messaging settlement carried a $15M civil penalty. Resolved, but it establishes prior enforcement exposure.

**Unverified items** that should not be treated as established: FY2026 CEO total compensation (proxy extraction failed); the "Mellum" transaction reported by news outlets in January 2026 and absent from every filing section reviewed; the Audere put/call redemption formula and total cash at full exercise; earnings-call content and any forward guidance (no transcript retrievable). Client concentration is not disclosed anywhere, so no claim about diversification is supportable in either direction — geographic and segment mix shifts establish only geographic and segment mix shifts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($180, +41.1%)

The June quarter was a shortage of large-fee closings inside a franchise that grew CF revenue 57.6% over two years. Closed CF transactions rose 2%, so client activity did not decline — only the fee mix. MD count is still expanding (CF 251 to 260, FVA 44 to 47), FVA grew 13% through the weakness, and international revenue reached 32.2% of total. The compensation ratio held at 64.3% through a 16% revenue decline, capping downside earnings leakage. FY2026 free cash flow of $681.8M on no funded debt yields 7.6%, and $180.1M of buyback authorization remains available at a price well below the FY2026 average repurchase price of $179.42. The CFO bought $500,570 of stock at $124.52 four days after the 10-Q. Restructuring conditions cited by management remain supportive — elevated interest rates, record corporate leverage, and software-sector disruption named as an FR driver — and sponsor liquidity pressure argues for accelerating mid-cap exits in 2027. On FY2028 consensus of $8.83, the stock trades at 14.5x, and a re-rating to the 23.0x peer median on normalized earnings supports the bull value.

### Bear Case ($96, −24.8%)

The average-fee decline reflects competitive fee compression that the company itself disclosed as an active dynamic, and a durable shift toward smaller mandates. Adjusted EPS has fallen four consecutive quarters, from $2.14 to $1.35, and consensus has cut FY2027 by 18% in 90 days without stabilizing. FR provides no offset: revenue down 8% and closed transactions down 34% in a period when the restructuring backdrop should have been supportive, with FR now only 20% of revenue. There is no contracted backlog — deferred income of $43M and immaterial RPO — so a second weak quarter cannot be ruled out on any evidence currently available. A CF goodwill impairment against $1,128M of carrying value would follow a prolonged price decline that the company names as its own trigger, hitting 68%-of-equity intangibles. A 40% drawdown reduces the retention value of $497M of unvested awards precisely as restrictive-covenant enforceability weakens in California. Buybacks executed at $179.42 (FY26) and $143.77 (Q1 FY27) destroyed roughly $225M of relative value, and the share count is still rising: basic weighted shares 64.34M, 65.72M, 66.55M across FY24-FY26, with 1.15M shares granted in FY26 and 1.4M in Q1 FY27 exceeding open-market retirement. At $6.00 of compressed adjusted EPS and 16x, the equity is worth $96.

## Conclusion

Houlihan Lokey remains a high-quality advisory franchise with a debt-free balance sheet, a 26% free-cash-flow margin, genuine employee ownership (~850 holders, ~21% of equity), and a variable-compensation structure that demonstrably absorbed a 16% revenue decline with only a 13% decline in operating income. Those attributes justify holding the shares and support the 7.5 financial-health score.

They do not currently support adding. The June quarter delivered the first material break in the growth record — Corporate Finance revenue down 24% on flat deal count, meaning the entire shortfall came from a 25% decline in fee per transaction — in a business with no contracted backlog and therefore no way to verify management's mix explanation before late October. Consensus has cut FY2027 by 18% in 90 days, adjusted EPS has declined four consecutive quarters, and the restructuring segment that is supposed to hedge fell alongside M&A. The CFO's $500,570 open-market purchase at $124.52 is a credible, directionally positive single-officer signal; it is not a substitute for a second quarter of fee data, and it sits against buybacks executed roughly $225M above the current price with a share count that is still rising.
