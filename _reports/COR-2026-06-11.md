**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cencora is one of three U.S. pharmaceutical distributors that handle the overwhelming majority of drug volume in the country, a high-volume logistics business with structurally tiny margins (GAAP net margin ~0.5%) that functions as a working-capital float machine. The economically relevant metric is adjusted EPS, which compounded from $9.26 in FY21 to $16.00 in FY25 (~15% CAGR) while GAAP net income stayed roughly flat near $1.5–1.7B. The gap is almost entirely non-operating: PharmaLex goodwill write-offs ($418M FY24, $724M FY25), opioid charges, ~$550M/year acquisition intangible amortization, and litigation swings. On adjusted forward earnings the stock trades at ~15.8x FY26 and ~14.2x FY27 consensus, well below the 23.4x health-care-distributor peer median and McKesson's 20.2x.

The shares fell from a November 2025 high of $377 to an intraday low of $244.82 on the May 6 earnings session, a peak-to-trough drawdown above 35%, and have since recovered to ~$281. The decline followed a Q2 FY26 EPS miss ($4.75 vs. $4.86), a revenue shortfall driven by faster-than-expected biosimilar conversion, and rising attention to customer concentration. A >30% drawdown places the burden of proof on the bull case, and here the sellers are reacting to a genuine, specific risk: roughly 25% of revenue and ~38% of net accounts receivable are tied to Walgreens Boots Alliance, which Sycamore Partners took private in August 2025 and is now shrinking through ~1,200 announced store closures.

The valuation discount, the ~15% adjusted-EPS compounding, a fresh $2.0B buyback, an open-market director purchase at $266, and an orderly CFO succession constitute a coherent recovery thesis toward the $350 analyst consensus. That thesis is gated by the Walgreens/Sycamore concentration, the active biosimilar headwind, a debt increase to $12.4B, and negative H1 FY26 operating cash flow. Those are specific and falsifiable concerns, and until they resolve, the appropriate posture is HOLD with a constructive lean.

## Company Overview

Cencora (formerly AmerisourceBergen; rebranded 2023) sits between drug manufacturers and healthcare providers and is typically the primary supplier to its customers. Generics, roughly 90% of U.S. prescription volume, carry higher margins than brand drugs and are central to profitability. The company reorganized its segments in Q1 FY26 into:

- **U.S. Healthcare Solutions (~88% of revenue):** human-health distribution (specialty, brand, generic) plus a growing specialty physician/MSO platform: RCA (retina) and OneOncology (oncology).
- **International Healthcare Solutions (~10%):** Alliance Healthcare (European wholesale), World Courier (specialty cold-chain), Innomar (Canada), PharmaLex.
- **Other (being wound down/sold):** MWI Animal Health (held-for-sale), Profarma (Brazil), U.S. Consulting Services (divested April 2026).

The competitive advantage rests on scale, one of the lowest cost structures in distribution, the Columbus, OH national distribution center, specialty/oncology depth, and the Walgreens generic-sourcing alliance (WBAD). The strategic direction is a deliberate move up the value chain into higher-margin specialty distribution and physician services, funded by shedding lower-growth units.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY22 $238.6B → FY23 $262.2B → FY24 $294.0B → FY25 $321.3B (+9.3%). Growth came from unit volume, specialty, and GLP-1 drugs, where sales rose 26.9% in FY25 (~$7.7B incremental) at low margins.

**Margin structure:** FY25 gross margin ~3.6%, operating margin ~0.8%, GAAP net margin ~0.5% (net income $1.55B). The model funds itself through negative working capital: $54.7B trade payables support $20.5B inventory and $25.2B receivables. A current ratio of 0.9 is normal for this float model, and asset turnover of 4.2x drives an Altman Z of 4.26 (safe zone).

**Adjusted EPS:** $9.26 (FY21) → $11.03 → $11.99 → $13.76 → $16.00 (FY25). Consensus is $17.76 for FY26 and $19.79 for FY27, implying ~11% forward growth, a deceleration from the trailing ~15% pace.

**Cash flow and leverage — the near-term concern.** FY25 operating cash flow was $3.9B and FCF $3.2B (~5.8% FCF yield on the current ~$55B cap), but H1 FY26 operating cash flow was **negative $1.0B** versus +$632M a year earlier, driven by a $2.2B payables drawdown. This is timing-driven within a float-dependent model rather than structural, but it coincides with a debt increase from $7.7B (9/30/25) to **$12.4B (3/31/26)** to fund the $4.65B cash portion of the OneOncology acquisition. Net interest expense rose 61% YoY in H1 FY26, and the equity cushion is thin. The combination of negative near-term operating cash flow plus a doubling of leverage is the main reason the financial-health profile, otherwise resilient, scores below average.

**Earnings quality and the surprise record.** The four-quarter beat/miss record is mixed: +3.2% (Jun-25), +0.4% (Sep-25), in-line (Dec-25), and **−2.3% (Mar-26)**, the first miss after a streak of beats. Q2 FY26 GAAP diluted EPS of $8.40 was inflated by a one-time $1.1B remeasurement gain on the previously held 34.9% OneOncology stake, which is non-cash and non-recurring. The recent miss tempers confidence in near-term consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The structural demand backdrop is supportive: IQVIA projects ~8.4% U.S. pharma sales CAGR through 2029, GLP-1 volume remains a tailwind, and the specialty/oncology mix carries better economics than brand distribution. The MSO pivot (OneOncology, RCA) is the primary growth lever, moving Cencora from pure distribution into physician-services economics where it can capture more value per script.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive — current-year +0.8% over 90 days, next-year +0.8%, next quarter +3.8% — which confirms positive momentum but is far from the magnitude that signals a fundamental acceleration. Offsetting the tailwinds, management flagged faster-than-expected brand-to-biosimilar conversion as a Q2 revenue headwind, and the company lost an oncology customer (June 2025) and a grocery customer. Forward growth of ~11% on consensus reflects both the specialty mix shift and these headwinds. The growth profile is solid and durable for a distributor, but decelerating and partly demand-mix-dependent.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline trailing P/E of ~21.5x on $7.96 GAAP EPS is misleading because GAAP earnings are depressed by impairments, opioid charges, and amortization. On the metric that drives the business and management compensation:

- $281 / $17.76 FY26 adjusted = **15.8x**
- $281 / $19.79 FY27 adjusted = **14.2x**

Against an 11–15% adjusted-EPS growth rate, that implies a PEG near 1.0–1.3. Relative to peers, COR trades at a clear discount: the distributor peer median P/E is 23.4x, Cardinal Health 31.5x, McKesson 20.2x. Even allowing for COR's first earnings miss and its concentration profile, a >20% discount to McKesson, its closest comparable, is wide for a business growing adjusted EPS at a similar pace.

Analyst consensus reflects the same view: mean target $350.58, median $350, against a current price of $281 — roughly 25% upside. The low target sits at $280, essentially the current price, and the high at $412. The clustering of the low target at spot indicates the bear case is "no further downside from here," while the median implies the recent derating overshot.

The valuation argument for a higher score is specific: the stock derated more than 30% on a 2.3% EPS miss plus concentration fears, compressing the forward multiple to a level that prices in materially weaker growth than consensus expects. The reason the valuation score is not higher still is that the discount is partly warranted: the Walgreens/Sycamore credit and renegotiation overhang and the biosimilar headwind are real reasons a buyer demands a lower multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

The Big-3 oligopoly (McKesson, Cardinal, Cencora) provides pricing discipline and high entry barriers, and Cencora's scale and low-cost structure are durable. The trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, the specialty and MSO pivot widens the addressable margin pool and differentiates COR from a pure-volume distributor. On the negative side, the company lost two named customers in the past year, biosimilar conversion is compressing brand economics faster than planned, and the customer base is consolidating in ways that shift bargaining power toward buyers.

The structural threats span all sides of the channel: manufacturer direct-to-consumer models that bypass wholesalers, Most-Favored-Nation pricing (EO 14297), IRA Medicare Part D negotiation effective 2026, 340B contract-pharmacy disputes, and CMS reimbursement and bona-fide-service-fee rule changes. None is an immediate cliff, but collectively they pressure the distributor compensation model. The competitive position is stable with an improving mix offset by a deteriorating customer-concentration and pricing-reform backdrop.

## Risk Assessment

**Walgreens/Sycamore concentration — the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** Walgreens and Boots represent ~25% of revenue and ~38% of net accounts receivable; Evernorth (Cigna) adds ~13%; the top 10 customers are ~66%. This concentrates two risks in one now-more-leveraged relationship: contract renegotiation at the 2029 (US/WBAD generic sourcing) and 2031 (Boots) renewals, and credit risk on roughly a third of receivables from a PE-owned entity executing a major restructuring. The 10-K flags this as a modified risk factor. A WBA credit event or a renewal on materially worse terms would be a falsifiable, material hit.

**Opioid liability.** $4.3B accrued, payable over 13 years (~$416M/year). The DOJ's Controlled Substances Act civil suit is in discovery through 2027, and the 4th Circuit vacated/remanded the favorable West Virginia verdict in October 2025. The Maryland Supreme Court vacated the Baltimore verdict in the company's favor in April 2026, a partial offset.

**Drug-pricing and channel reform.** MFN pricing, IRA negotiation, DTC manufacturer models, and reimbursement rule changes are a persistent structural overhang.

**Integration risk.** OneOncology ($7.4B) and RCA expand Cencora into the more heavily regulated corporate-practice-of-medicine and MSO-billing space, which the company has not historically carried. The full PharmaLex goodwill write-off ($724M) shows prior M&A in life-sciences consulting disappointed.

**Near-term cash conversion and leverage.** Negative H1 FY26 operating cash flow and debt at $12.4B require monitoring against full-year cash recovery.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 27.9% versus 31.3% trailing realized, so options are slightly cheap (IV percentile 10% within the current chain), and the term structure is flat (near 27.9% ≈ far 28.3%), so the market is not pricing an imminent event. A positive near-term put/call skew of ~8–9% at the front indicates elevated downside hedging demand, with a −4.4% skew at the 6-month tenor reflecting a balanced longer-dated view. Since the May earnings session, ATM IV has fallen from 37.7% to 27.9% as the stock rose 12%, and the put/call OI ratio dropped from 1.40 to 0.66, consistent with post-event de-hedging and stabilization. Short interest is low at 3.2% of float (2.4 days to cover), so this is not a crowded bear trade. The options configuration is consistent with a stock that has stabilized but where hedgers still see near-term tail risk, aligned with the Walgreens concentration overhang.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (toward $350–412).** The biosimilar headwind is transitory mix noise, Walgreens contracts renew without material concession and the receivables prove money-good, and adjusted EPS compounds ~11–13% toward $19.79+ in FY27. A re-rating to even 18x FY27 adjusted earnings (still below the peer median) implies ~$356; the $412 high target assumes ~21x and continued specialty-mix margin gains. The $2B buyback, a 9% dividend increase, the director's open-market purchase at $266, and orderly CFO succession to Eva Boratto support the floor and the recovery path.

**Bear Case (toward $230–245).** Walgreens/Sycamore renegotiates distribution terms lower at the 2029 renewal or experiences a credit event affecting ~38% of receivables; biosimilar conversion continues to outrun plan and compresses revenue and brand economics; the leverage taken on for OneOncology weighs on FCF as interest costs climb and integration underdelivers, echoing PharmaLex. In that scenario the multiple stays compressed near 13x FY26 adjusted, putting the stock back toward the May low of $245 or modestly below, roughly 13–18% downside from current levels.

The asymmetry is mildly favorable — ~25% to consensus versus ~13–18% to the bear case — but the bear case turns on a specific unresolved event (Walgreens) rather than a low-probability tail, which is why this is a constructive HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY26 earnings in early August 2026 is the next natural checkpoint, covering revenue trajectory (biosimilar pace), H2 operating cash-flow recovery, buyback execution, and any commentary on the Walgreens relationship.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Evidence the Walgreens/Boots relationship is stable — a renewal or affirmation of terms, or Walgreens receivables aging and credit metrics holding clean through two quarters — combined with the stock still below ~15x forward adjusted EPS.
2. FY26 full-year operating cash flow recovering to the $3.5B+ range, confirming the H1 negative print was a working-capital timing swing, with leverage beginning to step down.
3. Adjusted-EPS estimate revisions re-accelerating (next-year revisions turning positive at a >2% 90-day pace) as biosimilar headwinds annualize and the MSO platform contributes.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A Walgreens/Sycamore contract renegotiation at materially worse terms, a store-closure pace that cuts COR volume guidance, or a receivables impairment.
2. Q3 FY26 revenue decelerating further on biosimilar conversion with a second consecutive adjusted-EPS miss.
3. Operating cash flow staying negative through Q3 alongside rising leverage or a credit-rating action.

## Conclusion

Cencora is a high-quality, scaled distributor compounding adjusted EPS at a low-to-mid-teens rate, now trading at a genuine discount to the peer median and to McKesson. The capital-return program, the director's open-market buy near current prices, and an orderly leadership transition argue the May derating overshot. The reason this is not a BUY is specific and falsifiable: the Walgreens Boots Alliance concentration is now PE-owned and shrinking, which creates concurrent renegotiation and credit exposure that the market is actively repricing; this stacks on the biosimilar headwind, the debt jump, negative H1 FY26 operating cash flow, and the first earnings miss after a beat streak. Those concerns are why the forward discount exists and why the bear case carries a defined trigger rather than a tail probability.

The recovery thesis is real and the risk/reward is mildly favorable, which supports holding with a constructive lean and accumulating into evidence that the Walgreens relationship and cash conversion hold. Recommendation: **HOLD** (growth-oriented), with an upgrade to BUY contingent on the triggers above.
