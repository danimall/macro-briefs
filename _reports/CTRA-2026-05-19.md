## Executive Summary

Coterra Energy ceased to exist as an independent company on May 7, 2026, when its all-stock merger with Devon Energy closed. Each CTRA share converted into 0.70 shares of DVN common stock, meaning the $32.56 CTRA price implies a ~$46.51 DVN share price. Any investment thesis on CTRA is now functionally an investment thesis on the combined Devon Energy.

The combined entity operates approximately 1 million Boe/day of production across premier U.S. basins, with particular concentration in the Delaware Basin. Management has targeted $1B in annual pre-tax synergies by year-end 2027 and announced an $8B buyback plus a 33% dividend increase post-close. Coterra's operational track record — PVI-10 of 1.78 vs. 1.50 target, below-budget drilling costs — now resides within a larger, more complex organization.

The fundamental concern is valuation relative to peers. At ~15x forward earnings, the implied DVN multiple sits at a meaningful premium to peer medians (10.9x). This premium existed pre-merger for Coterra given its dual-basin diversification, but the combined entity must now justify it through synergy delivery and operational outperformance. Integration risk, activist pressure from Kimmeridge, and a recent pattern of earnings misses (3 of last 4 quarters) collectively argue for patience rather than conviction.

## Company Overview

Coterra Energy was formed through the 2021 merger of Cabot Oil & Gas and Cimarex Energy, creating a differentiated E&P with exposure to both oil (Permian Basin) and natural gas (Marcellus Shale). The company expanded aggressively in January 2025, spending $4.0B on Franklin Mountain Energy and Avant Natural Resources assets in the Delaware Basin.

On February 1, 2026, Coterra announced an all-stock merger with Devon Energy. The deal received shareholder approval on May 4, 2026 and closed May 7, 2026. The combined company trades as DVN on NYSE with former Coterra shareholders owning approximately 46% of the new entity. Tom Jorden (former Coterra CEO) serves as Non-Executive Chairman; Shannon Young (former Coterra CFO) is now Devon's CFO.

## Financial Analysis

**Standalone Coterra (Final Period):**

Revenue declined from a $9.5B peak in 2022 to $5.5B in 2024 before recovering to $7.3B in 2025, driven primarily by the FME/Avant acquisitions rather than organic growth. Operating cash flow reached $4.0B in 2025, generating $1.7B in free cash flow against $2.3B in capex — a 23% FCF margin.

Q1 2026 (final standalone quarter) showed revenue of $2.4B, net income of $466M ($0.61/share), and operating cash flow of $1.6B (up 44% YoY). Production reached 771 MBoe/day with oil volumes up 16% YoY. These operational results were obscured by $434M in derivative mark-to-market losses as oil prices surged well above Coterra's collar ceilings ($56-$80 range) during the U.S.-Iran conflict.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt stood at $3.5B against $15.1B in stockholders' equity (19% debt/capitalization). Investment-grade rated. The company had $485M cash and $2.0B in revolver availability. Conservative leverage for E&P.

**Capital Returns:** The $0.88/share annual dividend (2.7% yield) plus residual buyback capacity provides modest but reliable shareholder returns. Post-merger, Devon announced a 33% dividend increase to $0.32/quarter and an $8B buyback authorization.

**Earnings Quality Concern:** LOE/Boe increased 20% to $3.38 in Q1 2026, and workover expense doubled to $56M, largely attributable to the acquired properties. This suggests integration costs are real and weighing on per-unit economics.

## Valuation Assessment

At $32.56 (implying ~$46.51 for DVN), CTRA/DVN trades at approximately 15x trailing EPS ($2.17), but roughly 10.9x on the $3.00 forward estimate. The peer median P/E is 10.9x.

The stock is fairly valued vs. peers on forward estimates. The premium that CTRA historically commanded (multi-basin diversification, gas optionality) may partially justify a slightly elevated multiple, but the combined Devon entity has greater complexity and integration risk that argues against expanding the premium.

**FCF yield:** At ~7% (based on standalone $1.7B FCF and $24.7B market cap), the stock offers reasonable yield support but isn't particularly attractive relative to peers offering 8-10%+.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus median $38.50 (18% upside); range $28-$46. The $28 low target implies downside risk if commodity prices revert.

The stock appreciated 33% over the trailing year, with the bulk of the move occurring between January 2026 (merger announcement at ~$25) and March 2026 (peaking at $36.88). The pullback from the 52-week high to $32.56 reflects a combination of deal-close selling pressure and the broader energy pullback from Iran peace signals.

## Competitive Landscape

The combined Devon-Coterra entity is now one of the largest independent U.S. E&P companies, competing directly with ConocoPhillips, EOG Resources, Diamondback Energy, and others for the title of premier Permian Basin operator.

**Strengths:** Inventory depth in the Delaware Basin is exceptional. The multi-basin portfolio (Marcellus, Anadarko, Eagle Ford, Powder River, Williston) provides diversification that pure-play Permian operators lack. The natural gas position is becoming more valuable as LNG exports grow and data center power demand accelerates.

**Challenges:** The consolidation mega-cycle means competitors are also scaling rapidly (ExxonMobil/Pioneer, ConocoPhillips/Marathon). The combined entity's operational complexity increases relative to focused operators. Kimmeridge's activist pressure for non-core asset sales suggests the market may not fully value portfolio breadth.

**Operational differentiation:** Coterra's PVI-10 of 1.78 demonstrates capital efficiency above most peers. Whether this culture survives within a larger Devon is uncertain and worth monitoring through combined entity results.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration Execution (Primary Risk):** $1B synergy target by end-2027 is ambitious for a merger of this scale. Q1 2026 cost pressure from acquired FME/Avant assets (LOE +20%, workovers doubled) already demonstrates the challenge. Scaling further with Devon's assets introduces additional complexity.

**Earnings Miss Pattern:** Three misses in four quarters signals potential overoptimism in management projections. The Q4 2025 miss of -20.6% is particularly concerning. Forward estimates may still embed too much optimism despite positive revision trends.

**Commodity Price Risk:** The stock's recovery from $22.33 to $32.56 was driven substantially by the U.S.-Iran conflict pushing oil prices higher. If diplomatic resolution occurs, the commodity tailwind reverses. Coterra's hedges at $56-$80 suggest management didn't anticipate sustained elevated oil, implying it may not persist.

**Activist Pressure:** Kimmeridge's involvement creates governance uncertainty and could force capital allocation decisions that prioritize short-term returns over long-term value creation.

**EPA Enforcement:** Outstanding 2023 Clean Air Act violations referred to DOJ represent an unquantified liability.

**Reassessment timeline:** Combined Devon's first full quarterly report (likely early August 2026 for Q2 results) is the natural assessment point. Synergy progress update and combined production guidance will provide first real data on execution.

## Conclusion

CTRA is no longer an independent investment — it is a claim on Devon Energy shares at a fixed 0.70 ratio. The combined entity has strong operational foundations, premier acreage, and a credible synergy pathway. However, at 15x trailing earnings (roughly in line with peers on forward estimates), the valuation doesn't offer asymmetric upside. The recent earnings miss pattern, integration complexity, and activist uncertainty create sufficient near-term headwinds to warrant a HOLD posture pending execution evidence from the combined entity.

The declining score trajectory (6.6 → 6.1 over recent reports) reflects appropriately increasing concern about valuation and risk as the stock appreciated from $24 to $33+. With the merger closed and the investment now functionally a Devon position, the evaluation framework has changed. Wait for Q2 2026 combined results before reassessing.
