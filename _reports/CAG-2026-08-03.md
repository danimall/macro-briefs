## Executive Summary

Conagra's fiscal 2026 (ended May 31, 2026) was a reset year in the literal accounting sense: $2,929.6M of pre-tax goodwill and brand impairments, a reported loss of $4.00 per share, removal from the S&P 500 in late June, a CEO change effective around June 1, and a 50% dividend cut announced July 15. The operating business underneath is smaller and less profitable than the reported line suggests. Reported net sales of $11,281.6M fell 2.9%, but the bridge decomposes to +1.8pp from a 53rd week, −4.6pp from divestitures, +0.3pp FX and −0.4% organic. Adjusted operating margin fell 274bp to 11.3%; FY27 guidance takes it to 10.0–10.5%, against roughly 16% "a few years ago" by management's own framing. Adjusted EPS goes from $2.30 (FY25) to $1.72 (FY26) to a guided $1.40–$1.50 (FY27).

The central analytical problem is that the price decline has not outrun the earnings decline. A year ago the stock closed at $18.26; today it is $14.51 against a guided $1.45. The share price is down 20.5% over twelve months while the forward multiple has expanded from roughly 8.3x to 10.0x. Against a packaged-food peer median of 11.2x, Conagra's discount is about one turn — narrow compensation for negative tangible book equity of −$3.6B, net leverage guided *higher* in FY27 (~4.0x versus 3.83x actual) against a 4.5x covenant, $762.5M of notes maturing in October 2026 whose refinancing terms are not verifiable from available filings, and a Refrigerated & Frozen reporting unit written down to fair value with effectively zero remaining goodwill cushion.

What keeps this from being a short is cash and alignment. FY26 free cash flow was $978.7M (14.1% yield on the current market cap); a conservative FY27 reconstruction using the guided ">90%" conversion produces roughly $626M, still a ~9% yield, and the halved dividend consumes only $335M of it — 1.9x coverage. New CEO John Brase bought 35,000 shares in the open market at $14.5895 on July 17, two days after announcing the impairment and the dividend cut, and every other recent executive disposition on the Form 4 record is tax withholding on vesting rather than discretionary selling. Options positioning is call-heavy (put/call open interest 0.40) with balanced skew, which does not corroborate the 13.3% short interest. We maintain HOLD with a 3.9 composite: the asymmetry is roughly a 7% expected total return against a 24% bear case, which is not enough to buy and not deteriorating fast enough to short.

## Company Overview

Conagra manufactures and markets branded packaged food across four reportable segments. FY26 net sales and adjusted operating profit: Grocery & Snacks $4,610.1M / $885.0M (19.2% margin, −156bp); Refrigerated & Frozen $4,641.8M / $485.6M (10.5%, −351bp); International $913.9M / $134.4M (14.7%, −35bp); Foodservice $1,115.8M / $114.3M (10.2%, −172bp); corporate expense of $340.6M. Total adjusted operating profit $1,278.7M on an 11.3% margin.

The brand portfolio — Birds Eye, Marie Callender's, Healthy Choice, Banquet, Duncan Hines, Reddi-wip, Slim Jim, Angie's BOOMCHICKAPOP, Hebrew National, PAM — holds category positions in center-store and freezer aisles that are structurally low-growth. Management disclosed roughly 5,500 SKUs on the July 15 call and named complexity reduction as a priority.

Two structural features matter for how the earnings should be read. First, the Ardent Mills flour joint venture is equity-method accounted and contributed $150.3M of adjusted equity earnings in FY26 (down 22.8% from $189.6M), with FY27 guided to ~$140M. That is roughly 11% of guided FY27 pre-tax adjusted profit generated with none of Conagra's own capex, and FY26 cash distributions approximately matched earnings — high quality, but not controlled. Second, customer concentration is extreme: the FY26 10-K discloses top-ten customers at approximately 60% of net sales and Walmart alone at approximately 29% — the highest Walmart figure in the years reviewed. That concentration collides directly with the FY27 plan, which requires taking roughly 3 points of price while simultaneously rationalizing SKUs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue quality.** Q4 reported net sales rose 3.6% to $2,882.1M; the bridge is +7.7pp from the 14th week, −4.6pp M&A, +0.5pp FX, and organic exactly flat. FY27 must lap a −1.8pp reported headwind from the extra week alone. Organic volume for FY26 was −1.4% (Q4 −1.6%), with Grocery & Snacks −2.4%, International −4.2%, Foodservice −2.2% and Refrigerated & Frozen the only positive at +0.3%. FY27 guidance embeds organic net sales of −3% to −1%, which management decomposed as volume down mid-single digits offset by price/mix near +3%, with declines "weighted to frozen." Taking those two figures at face value, the plan implies a price elasticity near 1.7 — revenue is being held roughly flat by destroying units.

**Margins.** Adjusted gross margin fell 175bp in FY26 to 24.0%, and 130bp in Q4 to 24.5% despite roughly $6M of tariff refunds and the benefit of the extra week. Adjusted SG&A rose 99bp as a percentage of sales for the year on higher incentive compensation and advertising. FY27 guidance holds gross margin only "roughly flat," meaning the 3 points of pricing are fully consumed by guided COGS inflation of 5–6% net of productivity above 4%, plus a $40M tariff headwind over-indexed to Q1. The 175bp of FY26 gross margin loss is not recovered. Q1 FY27 adjusted operating margin was guided to "high single digits" — the trough.

**The impairments.** FY26 pre-tax impairments of $2,929.6M ($2,382.4M goodwill, $547.2M other intangibles) produced an after-tax charge of $2,731.4M, or $5.69 per share. Reconciling Q3 FY26 segment goodwill balances against the FY26 balance sheet establishes that 100% of the goodwill charge sat in Refrigerated & Frozen — the Pinnacle-era assets — bringing cumulative R&F goodwill impairment to roughly $3.05B. Two features are decision-relevant. The charges generated only $198.2M of tax benefit on $2,929.6M pre-tax, a 6.8% effective shield, confirming ~93% non-deductibility and no cash tax offset. And the trigger was partly the share price itself: the Q3 10-Q attributes the Q2 charge to "a sustained decline in market capitalization and stock price" alongside a 150bp discount-rate increase and lower industry multiples, and the July 15 release attributes the Q4 $2.0B charge "primarily" to the same cause. Investors should not treat the write-down as independent new information about operations; it is largely the accounting system catching up to the equity de-rating. The consequence is that goodwill of $8,119.3M now equals 47.0% of total assets and exceeds stockholders' equity of $6,357.6M, leaving tangible book value at approximately −$3,592M.

Remaining cushion is thin by construction. R&F was written to fair value and is disclosed as the only reporting unit with ≤10% excess fair value over carrying value, tested at an 11.0% discount rate with 1.5% long-term growth, where +50bp on the discount rate reduces fair value by $120.0M. A further $902.1M of brands carry less than 10% cushion, where a 100bp royalty-rate reduction cuts fair value by $215.8M.

**Cash flow.** FY26 operating cash flow $1,402.1M less capex $423.4M gives free cash flow of $978.7M, down 24.9% from $1,302.6M, at 118.9% conversion of adjusted net income — the third consecutive year above 115%. FY27 conversion guidance drops to ">90%." Using the guided $1.45 EPS midpoint on ~480M diluted shares (~$696M adjusted net income) at 90% conversion gives roughly $626M of FCF, with implied operating cash flow near $1,176M against guided capex of ~$550M. Label this a sensitivity rather than a forecast: conversion is guided with no upper bound and working-capital timing can move it materially. Both FY26 and FY25 contain distortions in opposite directions — FY26 included a $144.9M inventory release and a net −$76.6M litigation drag; FY25 benefited from $173.8M of accelerated receivables collection versus $35.0M in FY26. Neither year is a clean run-rate.

**Capital structure.** Total debt fell to $7,268.4M from $8,067.6M; net debt to $7,050.4M from $7,999.6M. Company-stated net leverage is 3.83x, implying adjusted EBITDA of $1,840.8M, which reconciles to $1,840.7M when rebuilt independently from the release's EBITDA bridge. The $2.0B revolver (undrawn, maturing June 27, 2030) requires EBITDA/interest ≥3.0x and net debt/EBITDA ≤4.5x; the company reports compliance. Interest coverage is 4.81x. Holding net debt constant, a 4.5x breach would require EBITDA below $1,567M, a 14.9% decline. A reconstruction of FY27 from guided margin and sales — organic base $11,024.1M × 0.98 ≈ $10,804M revenue, × 10.25% ≈ $1,107M adjusted operating profit, plus ~$430M D&A, ~$140M equity earnings and ~$25M pension income ≈ $1,702M — implies a 7.5% decline, consuming roughly half the covenant cushion in one guided year. This is a sensitivity, not a covenant calculation: the contractual EBITDA definition was not read and may permit add-backs that widen headroom.

**Two derived findings deserve emphasis.** First, FY26's deleveraging was mostly asset sales. Net debt fell $949.2M; internal sources after dividends and buybacks were $978.7M − $669.7M − $15.3M = $293.7M, while divestiture proceeds of $648.9M (principally Chef Boyardee at $607.0M net) plus $38.9M of PP&E sales contributed $687.8M. Roughly 70% of the 11.9% net debt reduction was funded by selling businesses, and Chef Boyardee carried $385.9M of prior-year segment sales that do not come back.

Second, halving the dividend does not deleverage the company in FY27 — it prevents leverage from worsening. Both figures are issuer-stated: 3.83x at FY26 year end, guided to ~4.0x at FY27 year end. The cut saves roughly $335M annually ($0.70 versus $1.40 on ~479M shares against FY26 dividends paid of $669.7M), which management framed as ~$1B over three years. But EBITDA falls faster than debt. After the dividend, FY27E discretionary cash is roughly $291M against ~$6.8–7.0B of net debt. Reaching the stated 3.0x long-term target from 4.0x requires roughly $1.7B of net debt reduction, or approximately six years at that internal rate absent EBITDA recovery or further asset sales.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis for the next twelve months, and management is not offering one. FY27 guidance is negative on organic sales, negative on margin, and negative on EPS. The estimate revision picture confirms that consensus has capitulated rather than resisted: current-quarter EPS is $0.28, down 23.8% in 30 days and 27.9% in 90; current-year $1.45 (exactly the guidance midpoint), down 14.6% in 90 days; next-year $1.55, down 13.4%. The earnings-surprise record is unremarkable — three small beats and one small miss over four quarters, the largest being +15.8% on a $0.34 estimate — which tells us guidance is roughly achievable, not that it will be exceeded.

The reinvestment plan is coherent and unquantified. Advertising and promotion rises ~$40M (+14%, from $279.4M or 2.5% of sales to roughly $320M or 2.9%) — a level that remains thin for branded food and is consistent with the view that brand support had been under-funded to protect reported cash metrics. Capex rises ~$125M to ~$550M (5% of sales), but roughly $100M of that step-up is attributed to fried chicken and protein insourcing, a specific vertical-integration project rather than remediation of chronic under-investment; FY26 depreciation of $396.0M was only modestly below capex of $423.4M, so the asset base was not visibly starved. "Project Catalyst" was named with no disclosed savings target, cost, or timeline, deferred to an Investor Day in early 2027.

Structural demand headwinds are now in the risk factors rather than in analyst speculation: GLP-1 adoption, health-and-wellness perception of processed food, the MAHA Commission and FDA synthetic dye phase-out with associated reformulation cost, extended producer responsibility packaging laws, PFAS, and — most directly relevant to value-tier brands such as Banquet — "declining benefits or changing eligibility requirements under government food assistance programs." Whether trade-down into value packaged food materializes as an offset is unverified in the available sources and has not yet appeared in Conagra's volumes.

Growth Prospects scores 2.5, unchanged from the prior review. The guidance itself, not the risk case, embeds mid-single-digit volume decline.

## Competitive Landscape

The evidence supports category position but not pricing power. In Q4 the company reported volume share gains in frozen single-serve meals, frozen multi-serve meals, frozen vegetables, meat snacks, seeds and pudding — while total organic volume fell 1.6%. Gaining share in shrinking categories is a materially different and less fixable problem than losing share in growing ones.

The two large segments are running opposite and equally unsuccessful plays. Refrigerated & Frozen held volume roughly flat (+0.3% FY26) but at negative price/mix (−1.0% FY26, −0.8% Q4). Grocery & Snacks took +4.0% price/mix in Q4 and gave up −3.5% volume. Neither produced profit growth; R&F adjusted margin fell 351bp and G&S fell 156bp. FY27 volume declines are guided "weighted to frozen," which is where volume had been holding — implying management now expects the price/volume trade to be forced in the segment that had been defending units.

Guided FY27 adjusted operating margin of 10.0–10.5% is low for branded packaged food, and management is explicitly choosing reinvestment over margin defense. Their own framing — 16% "a few years ago" to 10.25% guided — is an admission that the prior earnings level was not sustainable at the prior level of brand and supply-chain support.

Competitive Position scores 3.5, unchanged. Margin, mix and volume are all moving the wrong way simultaneously.

## Risk Assessment

**Price/volume execution against a 29% customer.** The FY27 plan requires realizing ~3 points of price while cutting SKUs, both negotiated with Walmart. If pricing under-delivers, revenue and the flat-gross-margin assumption fail together.

**Cost inflation versus productivity.** Guided COGS inflation of 5–6% against productivity above 4%, plus a $40M tariff headwind over-indexed to Q1, with tinplate steel for cans specifically cited. Q1 is the trough quarter; the current-quarter consensus of $0.28 already reflects it.

**Further impairment.** R&F carries roughly $2.5B of goodwill written to fair value — zero cushion by construction — and $902.1M of brands sit below 10% cushion. Disclosed sensitivities show how little deterioration is required. These charges are non-cash, but a second consecutive year of write-downs would raise questions about the covenant EBITDA definition and about carrying values in the remaining segments.

**Refinancing and leverage.** $762.5M matures in October 2026 ($500M at 5.30%, $262.5M at 7.125%, a 5.93% blended coupon). Two 424B5 supplements dated July 21–22 and an 8-K on July 28 under Items 1.01/2.03/8.01 establish that a registered offering and a new direct financial obligation occurred, but size, coupon, maturity and use of proceeds are unverified. The FY27 guide of ~$360M net interest (down from $382.6M) leaves limited cushion if refinancing occurs at a materially higher coupon than the ~5.0% weighted-average existing coupon. No current credit rating or agency action was located, which is itself a gap for a 4.0x-levered issuer. [unverified — the 10-year Treasury level and characterization of the long end are not in the provided sources]

**PAM cooking spray liability, now uninsured.** The company paid $141.1M in FY26 through February plus $25M in FY25, agreed to a further $44.3M, and the related insurance receivable fell from $81.8M to $1.6M. A Q3 jury returned a $25M compensatory verdict in *Esparza* (diacetyl exposure), under appeal. Offsetting this, the total litigation accrual fell from $204.5M to $47.8M, suggesting management believes most known exposure is resolved. Future settlements are self-funded.

**New litigation theory.** The FY26 10-K discloses pending litigation alleging certain products should be considered ultra-processed and cause negative health impacts — a named theory rather than boilerplate product liability, and an industry-level tail with no quantifiable exposure.

**Contractual inflexibility.** $2.72B of unconditional purchase obligations, $1.63B due in FY27, plus approximately $826M of warehousing agreements running up to ten years. These constrain the response if volumes fall faster than the mid-single-digit plan.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 36.7% against 30.0% one-year realized — rich by 6.6 points — with a backwardated term structure (36.7% at one week, 35.9% at one month, 32.2% at six months). Backwardation without an imminent earnings date (Q1 FY27 reports in roughly two months) points toward the October maturity and refinancing window as the priced event. Skew is balanced (−0.0% aggregate; +1.4% to +2.4% at near tenors, −2.4% at six months) and open interest is call-heavy at 0.40 put/call, with a put/call volume ratio of 0.63.

The meaningful signal is a divergence: short interest of 13.3% of float represents genuine institutional bearishness, yet options positioning is not corroborating it. Call-heavy open interest on a 4.8% yielder is consistent with covered-call income writing rather than directional conviction, so it should not be read as a bullish vote. What it does argue is that no dislocation is priced into the near term, and that with 2.0 days to cover, the short base is liquid enough to unwind quickly on any positive surprise — a modest squeeze risk that argues against shorting more than it argues for owning.

Risk Factors scores 3.5. Much of the deterioration is priced (28.6% below the 52-week high, consensus targets at spot), but the refinancing gap, the zero-cushion impairment position, and the covenant math consuming half its headroom in one guided year are live and not obviously reflected.

## Management Quality

This is the one dimension where new evidence is favorable, and it is the dimension we weight most heavily as a forward discriminator.

CEO John Brase started around June 1, 2026 following Sean Connolly's departure ($8.1M of separation costs in Q4 corporate expense; 8-K filed June 23). Brase purchased 35,000 shares in the open market at $14.5895 on July 17 for $510,632, two days after announcing a $2.0B impairment, a halved dividend and reduced guidance. His post-transaction direct holding was 35,000 shares, indicating he held little or no direct common stock beforehand. He received 205,953 RSUs on July 22 (~0.043% of shares outstanding). Across the recent Form 4 record, every other executive disposition is Code F — shares withheld for taxes on vesting — with no discretionary open-market selling. A new CEO deploying personal capital immediately after delivering bad news is a clean alignment signal, with the caveat that $511K is modest against a large-cap pay package and one purchase is not a valuation opinion.

Capital allocation decisions since the transition are defensible. The dividend cut was sized to fund capex and A&P and protect covenant headroom rather than to defend a 9.4% pre-cut yield that the market had already flagged as unsustainable. Buybacks have effectively stopped ($15.3M in FY26 versus $64.0M in FY25, against $837.6M of remaining authorization), which is appropriate given leverage, though it also means no capital was deployed at multi-year-low prices. Net dilution is negligible — basic weighted average shares rose 0.1%. The July 28 reorganization eliminates the COO role (Tom McGough retires September 4), moves the two largest segment presidents to direct reports, and elevates Burke Raine to Chief Growth Officer — consistent with the stated simplification agenda, and notable because Brase himself held the COO seat before promotion. The CFO seat appears stable; David Marberger's absence from the July 15 Q&A is not corroborated by any filing as a transition.

The prior regime's record is poor and is why this score is 5.5 rather than higher: the 2018 Pinnacle acquisition has now produced roughly $3.05B of cumulative R&F goodwill impairment, and proxy TSR shows $100 invested growing to $78.60 by FY25 year end versus $113.33 for the peer group. To the compensation committee's credit, the structure transmitted that loss — Connolly's FY25 "compensation actually paid" was negative $2,249,801 against $13.14M of reported compensation. (Note that the proxy's incentive-plan "Adjusted EPS" of $2.02 for FY25 is a different measure from the $2.30 reported in the July 15 release; they are not interchangeable.)

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($19.00, +31%).** The FY27 guide is a deliberate investment trough and 4.0x is peak leverage. A&P at 2.9% of sales and $550M of capex begin to stabilize frozen volumes by FY28; Project Catalyst is quantified at the early-2027 Investor Day (savings target not yet disclosed); the ~$100M of fried chicken insourcing capex delivers vertical-integration margin; gross margin recovers part of the 175bp lost in FY26. On a stabilized FY28 adjusted EPS at a peer-median multiple, the stock re-rates toward $19; add a 4.8% yield and the total return approaches 36%. A supporting variant: at 7.6x EV/EBITDA with snacking assets transacting elsewhere in the sector, Conagra becomes a more plausible seller of segments, and asset sales at 1.4x sales (the Chef Boyardee comparable) would accelerate deleveraging faster than the ~$291M internal rate.

**Bear case ($11.00, −24%).** Price/mix under-delivers against Walmart, volume declines exceed mid-single digits, and FY27 adjusted EPS lands below guidance of $1.40–$1.50. EBITDA falls toward $1.55–1.60B, taking net leverage above 4.3x and putting the 4.5x covenant within one bad quarter. R&F, with zero cushion, takes a further impairment, reinforcing the reflexive loop in which the equity de-rating produces the accounting loss that reinforces the de-rating. Ultra-processed food litigation gains traction, food-assistance benefit reductions hit Banquet volumes, and GLP-1 adoption compounds category decline. The stock falls toward $11, near the January 2026 lows and above the $12.00 low analyst target only because the dividend is covered.

**Base case ($15.50, +6.8% price / ~11.6% total return).** FY27 lands within guidance at roughly $1.45–1.50, leverage peaks near 4.0x, the October maturities refinance without a material interest step-up, and the multiple holds near 10.0–10.5x on next-year consensus of $1.55. The dividend is maintained and covered 1.9x by free cash flow. Probability-weighting these outcomes at roughly 45% base / 30% bear / 25% bull produces an expected total return near 7% — approximately the cost of equity, with a wide distribution.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 FY27 earnings, expected late September or early October 2026, is the first hard test — it is the guided trough quarter (adjusted operating margin "high single digits," current consensus $0.28) and will reveal whether +3% price/mix is being realized at retail. The October 2026 maturity settlement is the second. The early-2027 Investor Day, where Project Catalyst is to be quantified, is the first potential re-rating catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q1 FY27 organic volume decline better than −4% with gross margin flat or higher year over year, and FY27 adjusted EPS guidance of $1.40–$1.50 reaffirmed — evidence that pricing is sticking at a 29%-of-sales customer.
2. Project Catalyst quantified at a substantial annualized savings target with a defined timeline, or FY28 net leverage guided below 3.75x.
3. Price in the $11.50–12.50 range (near the $12.53 52-week low) without fundamental deterioration, which would put the stock near 8x guided FY27 EPS with a covered ~5.7% yield and a 12%+ free-cash-flow yield.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Any reduction of FY27 adjusted EPS guidance below $1.40, or adjusted operating margin guided below 10.0%.
2. Net leverage reported or guided above 4.25x, or October refinancing on terms that push FY27 net interest above $390M against the ~$360M guide.
3. A further impairment above $500M accompanied by downward revisions to segment projections — the second year of the reflexive loop, which would also raise covenant-definition questions.
4. Any change to the newly reset $0.175 quarterly dividend, or resumption of buybacks ahead of deleveraging.

**On the score trajectory:** the composite has moved 2.9 → 3.7 → 3.5 → 3.9 → 4.0 → 3.9, with competitive position falling from 5.5 to 3.5 and valuation from 6.5 to 5.0. The valuation decline alongside a falling share price is not a contradiction: earnings power fell 37% from FY25 to guided FY27 while the price fell 20.5%, so the forward multiple expanded. Since the July 31 review, the only incremental information is the July 28 leadership reorganization (mildly positive) and the still-unverified July debt offering (a gap, not a datapoint). The price basis is unchanged at $14.51. We therefore maintain the 3.9 composite and the HOLD, classified as uncertain rather than as a growth or income thesis — the yield at 4.8% is below the closest peer's 6.8%, and there is no twelve-month appreciation thesis we can underwrite.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Conagra is a fairly priced business in structural decline with a competent new management team, adequate near-term liquidity, and a balance sheet that does not improve in the year ahead. The reset was honest: guidance is internally consistent (a bottom-up reconstruction from FY26's $1.72 through margin, equity earnings, interest and tax lands exactly on the $1.45 midpoint), the dividend was cut to a level free cash flow covers 1.9x, and the CEO put his own money in at approximately today's price.

What prevents a BUY is that the forward multiple offers roughly one turn of discount to peers for a company carrying negative tangible equity, leverage guided from 3.83x to 4.0x against a 4.5x covenant, zero goodwill cushion in its largest impaired segment, an unverified October refinancing, and a sixth consecutive year of volume decline embedded in its own guidance. What prevents an AVOID is a ~9% forward free-cash-flow yield, a covered dividend, a clean insider signal, consensus targets already at or below spot, and 13.3% short interest with 2.0 days to cover in a name where options positioning prices no near-term dislocation. Expected total return in the base case is approximately 11.6% against a 24% bear case — insufficient asymmetry to buy, insufficient deterioration velocity to short.

Existing holders should collect the $0.70 and wait for the Q1 FY27 print. New capital should wait for either evidence that price/mix is holding with stable gross margin, or a price in the $11.50–12.50 range.
