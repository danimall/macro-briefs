**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BXP is the largest publicly traded U.S. office REIT, owning ~50.4M rentable square feet of Class-A "premier workplace" assets concentrated in six gateway markets (Boston and New York together generate ~67% of rental revenue). The company is in the middle of a deliberate, management-defined turnaround announced at its September 2025 Investor Day: cut the dividend ~29% (from $0.98 to $0.70/quarter), retain cash, sell non-core assets (~$1.2B of proceeds through May 2026), and self-fund a ~$2.3B remaining development pipeline while reducing leverage. This is a rational response to a multi-year FFO/share decline — $7.53 in 2022 falling to $6.85 in 2025 — driven by rising interest expense and soft occupancy outpacing contractual rent growth.

The shares have re-rated sharply. BXP's Q1 2026 10-Q anchors to a $51.90 close on March 31, 2026; the stock now trades around $68, a ~31% recovery that reflects improving office capital-markets sentiment, on-plan deleveraging, and leasing wins at flagship developments (343 Madison drawing McDermott Will & Schulte and others). That recovery is the crux of the current call: the deep-value entry that existed at $52 has largely closed. At $68 the stock trades near the $72 consensus mean/median target, at a full multiple on flat-to-declining FFO, with a reset ~4.1% dividend yield that no longer offers a wide margin of safety.

We rate BXP a HOLD (uncertain rationale). The turnaround is real and executing, and the supply backdrop (new office construction slowed to a halt) is a genuine multi-year tailwind for institutional-quality assets. But the operational trend remains negative: NOI fell 1.1% in Q1 2026, FFO declined year-over-year, blended cash re-leasing spreads were −3.2%, and $1.0B of 2.75% notes due October 2026 must be refinanced at materially higher rates. Estimates are drifting lower (current-year EPS revised −3.1% over 90 days), there is no open-market insider buying at the lows, and CEO compensation nearly doubled to $23.0M in a year of declining FFO and a dividend cut. The evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction at the current price.

## Company Overview

BXP (formerly Boston Properties) develops, owns, and manages modern, amenitized, well-located Class-A office buildings it brands "premier workplaces," across Boston, New York, San Francisco, Washington DC, Los Angeles, and Seattle. As of March 31, 2026 the portfolio comprised 164 properties totaling ~50.4M rentable square feet: 143 office, 14 retail, 6 residential, 1 hotel, plus six properties under construction or redevelopment (~3.4M sq ft).

The company operates as an UPREIT: BXP, Inc. is the general partner of Boston Properties Limited Partnership (BPLP), owning ~89.4%, with the remaining ~10.6% held by limited partners via OP/LTIP units. Several trophy assets sit in consolidated joint ventures (767 Fifth Avenue / the GM Building at 60%, 601 Lexington at 55%).

BXP's stated moat is its commitment to premier CBD office at a time when many competitors divested. Roughly 90% of its share of annualized rent comes from CBD premier workplaces, which were 89.9% occupied and 93.4% leased at Q1 2026, a spread better than the broader office market. The bull thesis rests on "flight to quality": that trophy space outperforms on occupancy, rents, and absorption while new supply is scarce. Boston (~36% of Q1 rental revenue) and New York (~31%) are the franchise core; San Francisco (~15%) and DC (~14%) round out the meaningful markets, with LA and Seattle small.

## Financial Analysis

The defining operational metric is FFO per share, and its direction is the problem. Using the company-selected measure from the pay-versus-performance disclosure (diluted FFO/share): 2021 $6.56 → 2022 $7.53 → 2023 $7.30 → 2024 $7.12 → 2025 $6.85. Peak was 2022; every year since has eroded as higher interest expense and softer occupancy outweighed contractual rent bumps.

Q1 2026 results confirm the trend continued. Total revenue was $872.1M, up 0.8% year-over-year. Net income attributable to BXP was $101.6M ($0.64/share) versus $61.2M ($0.39/share), but the improvement was driven almost entirely by $54.7M of gains on asset and JV sales (Gateway Commons, 7750 Wisconsin Avenue), not operations. NOI declined 1.1% to $506.0M. Same-property NOI rose 1.4%, but that was flattered by $12.8M of one-time lease-termination income (versus $0.2M a year earlier); strip it out and underlying operations were roughly flat-to-soft. Diluted FFO (BPLP total) fell to $280.5M from $289.5M year-over-year.

The reported earnings beats in the surprise history are, therefore, largely optical. Q1 2026 EPS of $0.64 versus a $0.34 estimate (+89%) and Q4 2025 EPS of $1.56 versus $0.58 (+171%) reflect asset-sale gains and lease-termination income rather than a step-change in operating earnings. The −177% miss in Q3 2025 similarly reflects impairment charges. FFO, not GAAP EPS, is the metric that matters for a REIT, and FFO is declining.

Occupancy is stabilizing at a soft level: total in-service portfolio 87.4% (up 70 bps sequentially from Q4 2025), leased 90.9%. The ~350 bps gap between leased and occupied (~1.6M sq ft signed but not commenced) provides some forward visibility into 2026 occupancy gains. Leasing volume was healthy — 1.15M sq ft signed in Q1 — but the change in second-generation cash rents was negative 3.18% blended, with steep declines in LA (−47.6%), NY (−9.5%), and DC (−7.6%), partly offset by San Francisco (+15.4%) and Seattle (+2.1%). Rent roll-downs remain a real headwind outside a few tightening submarkets.

The balance sheet carries the central risk. Consolidated debt was $15.6B at Q1 2026 (down from $16.6B at YE 2025 after repaying $1.0B of 3.65% notes with cash in February 2026); BXP's share including JVs is ~$15.3B. Consolidated debt/market cap was 62.9% computed off the depressed $51.90 quarter-end price; at ~$68 that ratio is materially lower, but leverage remains high for the asset class. The debt book is a legacy advantage: 90.7% fixed-rate at a weighted-average ~3.9% stated rate, well-laddered, with only ~$1.4B effectively variable after swaps. The near-term wall is refinancing risk: $1.0B of 2.75% notes due October 1, 2026 (plus a $100M extendable term loan) will reprice from ~2.75% to ~6%+, adding roughly $30M+ of annual interest expense on that tranche alone. Refinancing a decade of cheap debt at current rates is the structural mechanism pressuring FFO regardless of occupancy.

Cash flow reflects the capital intensity. Q1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $156.5M from $210.0M, weighed by $105.5M of leasing costs/TI spend in the quarter. Development requires ~$2.3B of remaining funding against ~$3.7B total investment across six projects. Liquidity is adequate: ~$464M cash plus ~$1.5B available on the revolver as of May 1, 2026. The ~29% dividend cut, reducing the annualized payout to ~$2.80, retains cash to help fund this program without over-relying on high-cost debt or dilutive equity.

## Growth Analysis

BXP's growth case is a turnaround, not a secular story. Management's multi-year plan targets earnings growth from two levers: (1) occupancy gains, supported by the 1.6M sq ft of signed-but-not-commenced leases and improving CBD absorption, and (2) development deliveries. The near-term development set is bifurcated: 290 Binney Street (Cambridge life sciences, 100% pre-leased to AstraZeneca) delivers Q2 2026 and is a clean win; 343 Madison Avenue (Manhattan, Grand Central-adjacent, 930K sq ft) was only 29% pre-leased as of May 1, 2026, with management guiding to ~56% including pending leases — meaningful lease-up and timing risk against ~$1.6B still to invest.

The estimate trend argues against near-term earnings acceleration. Consensus current-year EPS was revised down 3.1% over 90 days (to $2.01) and next-quarter estimates ticked down 1.4% over 90 days; the broad direction is negative earnings momentum. This is the opposite of the +30% revision-velocity inflection signal that would justify aggressive positioning. FFO is expected to remain flat-to-soft in the near term as refinancing headwinds offset occupancy gains.

The genuine multi-year tailwind is supply. New office construction has effectively stopped across BXP's markets, which improves the long-run supply/demand balance for institutional-quality assets as legacy Class-B/C space is converted or obsolesced. Capital-markets sentiment toward office is improving (more transactions, better debt/equity availability), which supports both BXP's asset-sale program and eventual cap-rate compression. Management's own framing is instructive: it needs "consistent incremental absorption to constitute a macro recovery," and the recovery is submarket-specific, not broad. Applying the sector-selection discipline here, Real Estate BUYs are roughly market-neutral on alpha, and office is not a standout property type; this is a relative-value/self-help story, not a thematic growth vehicle. Growth prospects are best characterized as a plausible stabilization with a positive multi-year skew, gated by refinancing drag.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$68 the stock trades at a trailing/forward P/E around 34x on GAAP EPS, a metric distorted by depreciation and impairments and not the right lens for a REIT. On FFO, the more relevant basis, BXP trades at roughly 10x 2025 FFO/share, a multiple that is not obviously cheap given the FFO is declining. The reset dividend yield is ~4.1%, roughly in line with the office-REIT peer median of 4.4% and below higher-yielding peers like KRC (5.6%) and ARE (5.5%).

Peer comparison is muddied by the sector's earnings distortions: ARE and CUZ show negative or nonsensical GAAP P/Es from impairments, VNO trades at 10.5x, KRC at 21.1x, CDP at 25.0x. The cleaner read is that BXP no longer trades at a distressed discount. The 52-week range is $49.72 to $79.33; at $68 the stock sits mid-range, well off the March low and above the $58 consensus low target while below the $72 mean and $91 high. The current price is essentially at consensus, and with estimates drifting lower, the consensus targets are not acting as reliable floors; the estimate-revision guardrail cuts against treating analyst targets as upside anchors on a name with negative revision momentum.

The valuation dimension deserves explicit skepticism. A superficially "cheap" 10x FFO multiple is the kind of reading that has historically marked value traps rather than opportunities, and cheapness alone is not a buy signal without an independent catalyst. BXP has a partial catalyst — deleveraging plus 343 Madison lease-up — but it also carries a genuine structural drag (refinancing) that caps the upside. The deep-value case that existed at $52 (63% debt/market cap, ~5.4% yield) has substantially closed after the ~31% re-rate. At $68, the risk/reward is roughly balanced: upside requires occupancy recovery and cap-rate compression to outrun refinancing; downside is a stall in absorption or a rate back-up that reprices office. We score valuation at 4.5 — no longer a compelling discount, with a real risk that the compressed FFO multiple partly reflects structural impairment rather than mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

BXP's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving within a still-challenged property type. Its premier-CBD portfolio is outperforming the broader office market: CBD assets were 89.9% occupied / 93.4% leased versus the total portfolio's 87.4% / 90.9%, and tightening is visible in Midtown Manhattan, Boston Back Bay, Reston Town Center, SoMa, and Santa Monica. The scarcity of new supply and BXP's institutional scale, balance-sheet access, and development capability give it a structural edge in capturing the highest-quality tenant demand as it consolidates toward premier space.

The offsetting reality is that the trajectory is not clearly improving in aggregate. Blended −3.2% cash re-leasing spreads mean BXP is still re-signing space below expiring rents in most markets, and 87% occupancy is soft in absolute terms. The "flight to quality" thesis is directionally supported but not yet strong enough to offset sector weakness outside a handful of submarkets. This is a market leader defending and slowly improving its position rather than a challenger rapidly gaining share, a position that maps to a mid-range score. We rate competitive position 5.5, holding roughly where prior reports placed it after the earlier de-rating from 7.0, reflecting a durable franchise with a slowly stabilizing but not accelerating share trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing/leverage** is the dominant risk. The debt book's ~3.9% weighted-average rate is a wasting advantage; the October 2026 notes and subsequent low-coupon maturities reprice into a ~6% environment, structurally pressuring FFO. In a higher-for-longer rate regime, this drag persists and could widen.

**Negative rent spreads and soft occupancy** mean the operational recovery is not yet self-sustaining. A stall in CBD absorption would leave FFO stuck or declining.

**Impairments appear ongoing, not one-time.** JVs recognized Gateway Commons (−$425.8M) and Safeco Plaza (−$319.5M) impairments in 2025, BXP took a $145.1M OTTI on Gateway Commons, and a new Item 2.06 Material Impairment 8-K appears to have been filed around May 2026 — after the FY2025 10-K — indicating asset values are still being marked down as the asset-sale plan forces mark-to-market.

**Development lease-up risk** is concentrated in 343 Madison.

**Litigation** includes the Brammer Bio (Thermo Fisher) construction suit over 290 Binney, where an injunction could delay AstraZeneca delivery and trigger penalties (management flags potential material adverse effect); a NYC acquisition-fee dispute (~$25M+ claimed); and a NYPD Paid Detail wage-and-hour class action.

**Dilution lever:** BXP renewed/upsized its ATM to $1.0B in March 2026 "for general business purposes… including debt reduction." No shares issued yet, but issuance at depressed prices would be dilutive.

Most of these risks are partially priced given the FFO multiple, the dividend cut already taken, and the stock's history. The unpriced tails are ongoing impairments and a refinancing-driven FFO step-down that the market may be underweighting after the recovery rally. We score risk factors 4.5 — specific, identifiable downside catalysts exist (October 2026 refi, further impairments, 343 Madison lease-up) with limited asymmetric recovery cushion at the current price.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 34.3% sits rich versus 28.6% one-year historical volatility (a 5.7pp spread) and at the 64th percentile of the current chain, with a ±7.5% implied move on the nearest expiry. Put/call skew is elevated at 4.9% in the front month, indicating incremental demand for downside protection, though the term structure is flat and the six-month tenor actually shows negative skew (−2.8%), suggesting the hedging concern is near-term rather than structural. ATM IV rose 3.3pp (31.0% → 34.3%) alongside the 5.9% price gain since the prior snapshot, while P/C open interest fell from 0.84 to 0.50 — a mix consistent with the market pricing a catalyst (likely earnings/refinancing) into a stock that has run. Net read: the options market confirms two-sided risk and does not offer a cheap-entry timing signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($80–91).** Deleveraging executes on plan, asset sales continue at firm cap rates, and improving office capital-markets sentiment compresses cap rates on premier CBD assets. Occupancy climbs toward 90%+ as the signed-not-commenced space commences and 343 Madison leases toward and past management's ~56% target. 290 Binney delivers cleanly to AstraZeneca. FFO stabilizes and inflects back to growth by 2027 as occupancy gains outrun refinancing drag, and the reset dividend proves comfortably covered with room to grow. The stock re-rates toward the $91 high target on a normalized FFO multiple as office regains investor favor.

**Bear case ($52–56).** The October 2026 refinancing and subsequent maturities reprice ~$1B+ of debt into 6%+ coupons, driving another leg down in FFO/share. CBD absorption stalls, negative rent spreads persist, and 343 Madison lease-up slips, forcing capitalized-interest and timing pressure. Further JV impairments continue as asset values mark lower, and BXP taps the ATM at depressed prices, diluting shareholders. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low as the FFO multiple compresses on a still-declining earnings base.

**Base case (~$70).** Occupancy grinds higher toward 88–89%, offsetting most but not all of the refinancing drag, so FFO stabilizes near $6.7–6.9/share with muted growth. Asset sales and the retained dividend keep the deleveraging plan on track. The reset yield is well-covered, and the stock trades roughly in line with fundamentals near the ~$72 consensus, delivering total return dominated by the dividend rather than capital appreciation over the next 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — insufficient evidence for a confident directional call at $68 after the recovery. The prior report sequence (AVOID → AVOID → AVOID → HOLD, scores drifting 4.7 → 4.65) reflects a franchise whose deep-value entry has closed while the operational turnaround remains unproven. New data confirms rather than reverses that assessment: FFO still declining, estimates still drifting lower, no insider buying, ongoing impairments.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (late July / Q2 2026, which should confirm 290 Binney delivery and updated 343 Madison pre-leasing) and, critically, the October 1, 2026 note maturity/refinancing terms.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Blended cash re-leasing spreads turn positive and total occupancy sustains above 89%, signaling operational inflection.
- 343 Madison pre-leasing reaches or exceeds ~70%, de-risking the largest development.
- Clustered open-market insider buying emerges at current or lower prices, OR estimate revisions turn positive over a two-quarter window.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The October 2026 (or subsequent) refinancing prints at coupons that drive a visible FFO/share step-down below ~$6.5, with management guiding lower.
- Occupancy declines or negative rent spreads worsen materially, indicating the CBD recovery has stalled.
- The company issues meaningful equity via the ATM at depressed prices, or files further material impairments signaling continued asset-value deterioration.

## Conclusion

BXP is a best-in-class office REIT executing a credible, self-help turnaround — dividend cut to fund deleveraging and the development pipeline, on-plan asset sales, a low-coupon fixed-rate debt book, and tightening premier-CBD submarkets against a halt in new supply. Those positives are real. But the operational trend is still negative (FFO declining, NOI −1.1%, blended rent spreads −3.2%, occupancy 87.4%), estimates are drifting lower, ongoing impairments signal asset-value resets in motion, and $1B+ of cheap debt must reprice into a 6%+ environment. The re-rate off the March low has removed the deep-value margin of safety; at ~$68 the stock trades near consensus with roughly balanced risk/reward.

Management's capital allocation is defensible in substance but carries a governance flag — CEO pay nearly doubled to $23.0M in a year of declining FFO and a dividend cut — and there is no open-market insider buying at the lows, which would have been the strongest bullish tell. The new leverage-ratio pay metric for 2026 is a constructive alignment step, but it does not change the near-term earnings math.

We rate BXP a **HOLD**. The dividend is now covered and the ~4.1% yield provides a floor, but this is not a confident income hold given the FFO trajectory, nor a confident growth hold given negative revisions and refinancing drag. It is a wait-for-evidence position: own it for the reset yield if already held, but the case for adding capital requires confirmation that occupancy recovery and 343 Madison lease-up are outrunning refinancing headwinds — evidence that does not yet exist.
