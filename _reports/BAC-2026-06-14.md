**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bank of America is delivering a net-interest-margin expansion that the market is recognizing only partially. Q1 2026 EPS of $1.11 was up 25% year-over-year, ROTCE reached 16.0% (from 13.97% a year ago), revenue grew 7% to $30.27B, and net income hit $8.58B. The mechanism is visible in the average-balance data: interest-bearing deposit costs fell 43bps YoY (2.42% → 1.99%) as Fed cuts flow through, while the large fixed-rate securities and loan book reprices slowly upward, lifting net interest yield from 1.99% to 2.07%. With roughly $529B of noninterest-bearing deposits anchoring funding costs, the cheap-deposit advantage is reasserting after the 2021-era low-yield asset overhang that suppressed margins.

The defining debate on BAC is the $81.2B of gross unrealized losses sitting in the held-to-maturity securities book ($514.8B amortized cost vs $433.6B fair value), which is invisible on the balance sheet and equals roughly 27% of the $300.7B total equity. This is the same dynamic that felled Silicon Valley Bank, and it is the primary reason BAC trades at a discount to JPMorgan on tangible book. BAC's $2.04T deposit base is large, sticky, and insurance-heavy, making a forced realization far less plausible than at a wholesale-funded regional. The losses widened marginally QoQ ($80.3B → $81.2B) as long-end yields stayed elevated, so this is not yet reversing, but it represents a large embedded call option on book value that turns positive whenever the long end falls.

At $56.06 the stock trades at 12.5x the current-year consensus EPS of $4.47 and 11.1x next year's $5.05, in line with the peer median P/E of 12.7 despite a higher-quality, more diversified franchise than most of the comparable set. With four consecutive earnings beats, stable-to-rising estimates, improving credit, management guiding ~15% YoY Q2 trading growth, and a ~4.5% reduction in share count over the past year, the risk/reward is asymmetric. The principal restraints — the HTM overhang and a rising 2027 G-SIB surcharge that may slow buybacks — are known and substantially priced. I rate BAC a BUY.

## Company Overview

Bank of America is one of the largest U.S. diversified banks and a Global Systemically Important Bank, with roughly $3.5 trillion in total assets, ~$2.04 trillion in deposits, ~212,000 employees, and ~3,540 financial centers. The franchise spans four top-tier segments:

- **Consumer Banking** (~36% of net income): ~$973B deposits, ~$103B card balances, ~50M digital users. Q1 2026 net income $3.06B, efficiency ratio 52.8%, ROAC 27%. The low-cost, sticky deposit base (total deposit spread 3.01%, ~$529B noninterest-bearing) is the franchise's core asset.
- **Global Wealth & Investment Management** (Merrill + Private Bank): ~$4.57T client balances, ~$2.12T AUM, Q1 2026 revenue $6.71B, net income $1.33B. Fee-rich and capital-light.
- **Global Banking** (corporate/commercial lending, treasury, IB): Q1 2026 net income $2.09B, ROAC 16%.
- **Global Markets** (FICC + equities): Q1 2026 net income $2.0B; sales & trading revenue $6.39B (FICC $3.55B, equities $2.84B).

The competitive moat rests on scale, the enormous low-cost deposit base, a leading consumer digital platform, and breadth across consumer, wealth, markets, and banking. Only JPMorgan is comparably positioned across all four. Goodwill of $69B reflects the legacy Merrill Lynch, Countrywide, and FleetBoston acquisitions.

## Financial Analysis

The earnings trajectory is strong and accelerating:

| Net interest income | $14.44B | $15.75B | $15.75B |
| Total revenue | $28.25B | $28.37B | $30.27B |
| Net income | $7.36B | $7.65B | $8.58B |
| Diluted EPS | $0.89 | $0.98 | $1.11 |
| ROTCE | 13.97% | 13.97% | 16.00% |
| Efficiency ratio | 62.9% | 61.5% | 61.2% |

FY2025 net income was $30.5B, up from $27.0B in 2024. The Q1 2026 result was a clean beat across drivers: NII +9% YoY, Global Markets revenue +8% YoY, and investment banking fees recovering to $1.84B.

The earnings surprise record reinforces confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats (Q1'26 +8.6%, Q4'25 +1.3%, Q3'25 +10.3%, Q2'25 +3.0%). This is a franchise that consistently delivers slightly above consensus, which warrants treating the current $4.47 FY26 / $5.05 FY27 estimates as reliable anchors.

**Capital and share count.** Buybacks are driving EPS — diluted shares fell from ~7.77B (Q1'25) to ~7.42B (Q1'26), a ~4.5% reduction. The dividend was raised to $0.28/quarter (~2.0% yield). Book value per share is $38.66 and tangible book value per share $28.84, both rising. CET1 stands at 11.2% (Standardized, binding), comfortably above requirements but down ~60bps YoY as RWA grew and buybacks continued. SLR is 5.5%.

**Credit quality is a tailwind.** Total net charge-offs were $1.41B (0.48% of loans) in Q1 2026. The credit card NCO rate improved to 3.64% from 4.05% a year ago; CRE NCOs fell sharply (1.24% in Q2'25 → 0.33% in Q1'26); criticized commercial exposure declined to 3.21% from 4.12%. Allowance coverage is $13.1B (1.09%). Provisioning was modest at $1.34B. This supports near-term earnings durability against the broader consumer-stress backdrop.

**The HTM overhang.** The single most important balance-sheet item is primarily agency MBS marked down ~$67.8B and Treasuries ~$12.6B. Two facts temper this: the loss is a paper figure only relevant in a forced-sale scenario that BAC's sticky deposit base and liquidity make unlikely, and the AFS book ($371B amortized vs $369B fair value, ~$2.5B net loss) does flow through AOCI (total AOCI –$11.0B), so the market already sees and discounts the smaller, recognized piece.

Earnings are high-quality and recurring; net margin is ~27%, ROA ~1.0%, ROE ~12% — solid for a megabank, structurally below JPMorgan.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three legs. First, the NIM expansion: deposit costs are repricing down faster than asset yields. The cheap-funding advantage reasserts as Fed cuts continue and the fixed-rate book slowly rolls into higher yields. This is the dominant driver of the 25% YoY EPS growth and is durable as long as the rate environment stays in a Fed-cutting / elevated-long-end posture, which is structurally favorable to BAC's margin.

Second, capital markets recovery. Management guided to ~15% YoY trading revenue growth in Q2 2026 (Moynihan, Bernstein conference, May 27), building on Q1's sales & trading print and recovering IB fees. Given rate-path uncertainty and elevated equity-market volume, this guidance has credible support.

Third, buyback-driven EPS accretion. The ~4.5% annual share-count reduction directly adds to per-share growth. This leg has a ceiling — the G-SIB surcharge rises to 3.5% from 3.0% in 2027 and CET1 has drifted down to 11.2%, so the buyback pace may have to moderate, which is the main risk to the EPS-growth runway beyond 2026.

Estimate revisions are stable to modestly positive: current-year EPS +3.1% over 90 days, next-year +1.5% over 90 days. This is not the >30% revision velocity of an inflection stock, and I am not treating it as such — BAC is a steady compounder with a margin tailwind, not a deep-cyclical recovery story. The direction is positive and the beat record argues the estimates are conservative.

At $56.06, BAC trades against the diversified-banks peer set in line to slightly cheap:

Peer median P/E is 12.7. BAC trades at the median despite owning a more diversified, higher-return franchise (16% ROTCE) than most of the peer group, with the second-best universal-bank model in the U.S. after JPMorgan. The mispricing is specific and identifiable: the market applies a tangible-book discount because of the HTM overhang and assigns no premium for the diversification and capital-markets optionality. On price-to-tangible-book (~1.94x on $28.84 TBVPS), the stock is not cheap on an absolute basis, but TBVPS itself is depressed by the same HTM drag — book value rises mechanically as those losses pull to par over time, whether through rate declines or simple maturity roll-down.

The case for the market being too cautious: at a 12.5x multiple with rising estimates, four consecutive beats, and improving credit, the consensus mean target of $63.16 (median $62.50, range $57.50–$71.00) implies ~13% upside, and these targets typically lag a bank delivering 25% EPS growth. The valuation does not require multiple expansion to work — flat 12.5x on the $5.05 FY27 estimate yields $63, and any normalization of the tangible-book discount as the HTM drag eases adds a further leg.

BAC sits below the threshold where the estimate-revision and target-as-floor logic applies, so the steady positive revisions are constructive rather than a contrarian warning.

## Competitive Landscape

BAC competes directly with JPMorgan (the clear profitability leader), Wells Fargo, and Citigroup in universal banking, plus Goldman and Morgan Stanley in markets and wealth. Its competitive position is stable-to-improving: it is holding share in a consolidating industry, the digital platform (~50M users, ~42M mobile) is a durable consumer moat, and GWIM's client balances provide a fee-rich, capital-light annuity.

The structural threat the 10-K names explicitly is financial disintermediation — fintech lenders, low-fee trading platforms, real-time payments, and the emerging stablecoin/digital-asset ecosystem that could siphon deposits. This is a slow-moving threat, not a near-term earnings risk, and BAC's scale and deposit stickiness provide meaningful insulation. The current rate environment (short rates falling, long end elevated) is a tailwind specific to BAC's slow-repricing asset book.

## Risk Assessment

**HTM unrealized loss (~27% of equity).** The central risk. It is a paper loss relevant only in forced-liquidation, which BAC's deposit base makes unlikely, but it caps tangible-book valuation and would convert to realized capital damage in a severe deposit-flight scenario. It widened slightly QoQ, so the relief vector (falling long-end yields) has not yet engaged.

**Regulatory: December 2024 OCC AML consent order.** A specific, BAC-flagged overhang on its BSA/anti-money-laundering/sanctions programs, with the 10-K noting other regulators are requesting similar information, raising the prospect of follow-on actions. Remediation is costly and can constrain M&A and growth.

**Rising G-SIB surcharge to 3.5% in 2027** raises the required capital floor and may force buybacks to moderate, removing an EPS tailwind. Basel endgame RWA proposals expected in 2026 add uncertain incremental capital demands.

**Litigation cluster:** Zelle fraud liability, cash-sweep deposit-rate settlements, card marketing/representment fee practices, and COVID-era unemployment-benefit processing. Individually manageable, collectively a recurring cost-and-headline drag.

**Rate sensitivity cuts both ways:** the NIM tailwind reverses if the Fed cuts deeply and the long end follows, compressing the asset-repricing benefit. Office CRE and consumer card remain credit watch items even as both currently improve.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing is benign and offers no warning against the thesis. Near-term ATM IV of 24.0% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 21.7% (a 2.3pp spread), and the IV percentile within the current chain is just 8% — implied vol is cheap, indicating no near-term event premium and a reasonable entry from a timing standpoint. The term structure is flat (24.0% near vs 25.4% far), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than front-loaded. Put/call skew of 5.5% shows modestly elevated put demand at the front, and the OI put/call ratio of 1.09 is mildly defensive, consistent with normal hedging rather than informed bearish positioning. Unusual activity is call-heavy ($57 and $58 June calls with large volume), which leans constructive. Short interest is negligible (2,704 shares). The options market is neither confirming a strong directional view nor flagging hidden stress.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($66–71)
NIM continues to expand as deposit costs reprice down and the fixed-rate book rolls into higher yields; Q2 trading growth lands near the ~15% guide; credit stays benign; the HTM drag begins to reverse as the long end eventually falls, lifting tangible book and compressing the peer discount. Apply 13–14x to FY27 EPS of $5.05+ with the tangible-book discount narrowing → $66–71. The mean analyst target of $63 and high of $71 bracket this outcome.

### Bear Case ($46–48)
Long-end yields rise further, widening the HTM loss and pressuring tangible book; a hard consumer cliff lifts card and CRE charge-offs; deep Fed cuts compress the asset-repricing benefit and reverse the NIM tailwind; the OCC AML order escalates to follow-on actions. The stock re-rates toward 9.5–10x on a flat-to-lower $4.80 EPS → $46–48, roughly the 52-week low region (-14% to -17% from current).

The bear-case downside of ~14-17% is at the edge of, but acceptable within, a megabank position given the asymmetry against a base case ~13% above current and a bull case ~18-27% higher, with a 2% dividend cushion and negligible short interest.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $63.** 12.5x (unchanged peer-median multiple) on a blended ~$5.00 forward EPS run-rate, consistent with the consensus mean target of $63.16 and median $62.50. This requires no multiple expansion — only continued estimate delivery.

**Bull case target: $70** (13.9x on $5.05 FY27 EPS with tangible-book discount narrowing).

**Bear case target: $47** (~-16% from $56.06; ~9.8x on a flat-to-lower EPS as the HTM drag and credit pressure re-rate the stock toward its 52-week low).

**Upside/downside from current ($56.06):** +13% to base, +25% to bull, -16% to bear.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the July 2026 Q2 earnings (testing the ~15% trading guide and NIM trajectory) and the Fed path through H2 2026.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Net interest yield declines QoQ for two consecutive quarters (signaling the NIM expansion has stalled or reversed).
2. CET1 falls below ~10.7% or management signals a material buyback pause ahead of the 2027 G-SIB surcharge, removing the EPS-accretion leg.
3. Credit card NCO rate rises back above 4.0% or criticized commercial exposure reverses higher above ~4.0%, signaling the credit tailwind has turned.

## Conclusion

Bank of America offers a steady, mid-teens-ROTCE megabank delivering a real NIM expansion at a peer-median 12.5x forward multiple, with improving credit, four consecutive beats, and management guiding ~15% Q2 trading growth. Why this is a BUY rather than a HOLD despite the overhang: the HTM loss is a paper figure unlikely to be realized given a sticky deposit base, it is already the reason the stock trades at the peer median rather than a JPMorgan-like premium, and it converts to a tailwind on book value when the long end falls. The valuation does not require multiple expansion; flat 12.5x on the FY27 estimate alone yields ~13% upside, and the consensus targets lag a bank growing EPS 25%. The known restraints (HTM, rising G-SIB surcharge, AML order) are specific and substantially priced. Against a base case ~13% above current, a bull case ~25% higher, and a bear case ~16% below, the asymmetry favors ownership.

**Recommendation: BUY.** Accumulate at current levels, with the understanding that the buyback-pace ceiling and the long-end-rate dependence of the HTM reversal cap the conviction below a top-tier rating.
