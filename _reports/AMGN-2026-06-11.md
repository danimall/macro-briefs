**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Amgen is in the middle of a portfolio transition that management has labeled a "springboard year." FY2025 product sales rose 10% to $35.1B, driven by Repatha (+36%), EVENITY (+34%), TEZSPIRE (+52%), and BLINCYTO (+28%), while legacy assets eroded (ENBREL –33%) or moved past patent (Prolia, XGEVA, both off-patent February 2025). The fundamental question for the equity is binary at the operating level: can the growth portfolio outrun the simultaneous hit from denosumab biosimilar erosion (~$6.5B of 2025 sales now exposed) and IRA/MFN price-setting on ENBREL (effective Jan 2026) and Otezla (effective 2027). Q1 2026 (+6% revenue, raised guidance) and a four-quarter beat streak suggest growth is winning so far, but the price-setting drag deepens through 2027.

Two discrete, largely independent binaries dominate the risk/reward, and neither is comfortably resolved. First, the IRS dispute: the agency seeks roughly $10.7B before interest (~$3.6B for 2010–2012, ~$5.1B plus ~$2.0B penalties for 2013–2015) over U.S./Puerto Rico profit allocation, with a U.S. Tax Court decision expected no earlier than H2 2026 and audits of later years (2016–2018, then 2019–2022) that could cascade. Against only $8.66B of book equity, an adverse ruling would be material to financial flexibility even though it does not threaten solvency. Second, MariTide: early Phase 2 data showing durable second-year weight maintenance on monthly/quarterly dosing is constructive, but the asset enters an obesity market where Lilly (retatrutide cleared Phase 3 May 2026) and Novo are extending efficacy and dosing leads. MariTide is upside optionality, not a base-case certainty.

At $337.73 (down from $347.94 at the snapshot date, and ~14% off the $391 high) the stock trades at ~23.5x trailing EPS of $14.23, a premium to the biotech peer median of ~20.3x. The premium is defensible on a more diversified, higher-growth portfolio and the MariTide option, but it leaves little margin for an adverse IRS ruling or for accelerating biosimilar erosion to outrun new launches. With estimate revisions roughly flat (current-year EPS $22.41, +0.1% over 90 days), no insider buying, and options pricing imminent stress (near-term IV 36.6% vs 27.3% HV, backwardated term structure, 20.1% put skew), the setup is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate AMGN HOLD.

## Company Overview

Amgen is one of the largest independent biotechnology companies, founded ~1980, ~31,500 employees, operating across ~100 countries in a single reportable human-therapeutics segment. Its competitive edge is biologics manufacturing scale and reliability, reinforced by newer, smaller-footprint U.S. facilities (Holly Springs NC, New Albany OH, Puerto Rico) cited as cost and speed advantages, plus Section 232 tariff relief (~3 years, granted Dec 2025) in recognition of U.S. manufacturing investment.

The portfolio splits cleanly into growth engines and eroding legacy. Growth: Repatha (PCSK9, $3.0B, with the VESALIUS-CV primary-prevention readout broadening the label), EVENITY ($2.1B), TEZSPIRE ($1.5B, AstraZeneca collaboration), BLINCYTO ($1.6B), IMDELLTRA (full approval Nov 2025), plus PAVBLU, UPLIZNA, and WEZLANA biosimilar. "Other products" grew +29% to $7.3B. Eroding/at-risk: ENBREL ($2.2B, Medicare Part D price set effective Jan 2026), Prolia ($4.4B, flat, patent expired Feb 2025), XGEVA ($2.1B, –6%, patent expired), Otezla ($2.3B, CMS price-setting from 2027, already impaired $1.2B).

Distribution concentration is high but typical for U.S. pharma: McKesson, Cencora, and Cardinal Health together account for ~77% of gross revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth and profitability are strong.** Revenue progressed from $26.3B (2022) to $28.2B (2023) to $33.4B (2024, first full year of Horizon) to ~$36.75B (2025). FY2025 net income was $7.71B (+89% YoY, but 2024 was depressed by Horizon inventory step-up amortization), diluted EPS $14.23, operating margin 24.7%, net margin ~21%. The 2025 volume/price split (+13% volume, –3% net price) is the recurring biopharma dynamic that will intensify as biosimilars and IRA price-setting bite.

**Cash flow quality is high.** Operating cash flow was $9.96B and FCF ~$8.1B (a 22% FCF margin), comfortably covering $5.1B of dividends. Capex steps up to ~$2.6B in 2026 for U.S. manufacturing. The four-quarter beat record (6.1% to 11.6% surprises) lends confidence to near-term estimates.

**The balance sheet is the central tension.** The Altman Z-Score of 0.97 ("distress zone") is misleading read naively; it is a structural artifact of the $27.8B debt-funded Horizon acquisition (Oct 2023), not operating distress. Long-term debt of $50.0B (plus $4.6B current) sits against stockholders' equity of only $8.66B, producing D/E of 10.46x and an optically flattering 89% ROE on the thin equity base. Retained earnings show a –$25.1B deficit, the cumulative result of buybacks and dividends exceeding earnings over years; the company states this does not impair its ability to operate, pay dividends, or service debt.

Mitigants are real: investment-grade ratings (S&P BBB+, Moody's Baa1, Fitch BBB+), active deleveraging ($6.0B debt retired in 2025, $4.5B in 2024), and declining net interest expense ($2.76B vs $3.16B prior). The company describes itself as "substantially returned to its pre-acquisition capital structure." The leverage is being managed down with ample FCF, but it constrains flexibility for the next large acquisition and amplifies sensitivity to a cash-flow shock. The IRS case is the most concrete such shock, which is why the thin equity base matters beyond the accounting artifact.

**Capital allocation is dividend-first.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $2.52 (+6%) for Q1 2026, continuing a consistent ~6% annual cadence and supporting the ~2.9% yield. Buybacks have been minimal ($200M in 2024, none in 2023 or 2025) while deleveraging takes priority; $6.8B of authorization remains. We score Financial Health 5.5 — high-quality cash generation and IG ratings offset by genuinely elevated leverage and a balance sheet unusually exposed to a single adverse cash event.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on whether the new-and-growing portfolio can bridge over the legacy erosion. The evidence through Q1 2026 is constructive, and the growth assets are not speculative: Repatha, EVENITY, TEZSPIRE, BLINCYTO, and IMDELLTRA all have label expansions or recent data supporting durable momentum. The Repatha VESALIUS-CV primary-prevention readout (25% MACE reduction) and the August 2025 broadened label materially expand the addressable population for the fastest-growing legacy asset.

Estimate revisions, however, are roughly flat rather than rising: current-year consensus EPS sits at $22.41, next-year $23.48 (+0.9% over 90 days). This revision velocity does not signal a growth acceleration the market is underestimating; it reflects the offsetting forces — growth products advancing while Prolia/XGEVA biosimilar erosion accelerates and ENBREL/Otezla price-setting deepens. Management has guided to "accelerated sales erosion" on the denosumab franchise in 2026.

MariTide is the swing factor for the equity's long-term value. The differentiated thesis is dosing convenience (monthly/quarterly vs weekly) to improve the poor real-world GLP-1 adherence (Amgen's own data: ~49% persistence at one year). Early Phase 2 Part 2 data showed weight loss largely maintained for a second year on lower-frequency dosing, with low nausea/vomiting and no new safety signals; a separate Phase 2 in T2D showed meaningful HbA1c and weight reduction. Six global Phase 3 studies are underway (obesity ± T2D, CV disease, heart failure, OSA). This is upside optionality entering a market where Lilly's tirzepatide dominates and retatrutide just cleared Phase 3. We score Growth Prospects 6.5 — durable, broad-based product growth with a clear bull/bear fork and a large embedded option, tempered by flat near-term revisions and intensifying pricing headwinds.

## Valuation Assessment

At $337.73 the stock trades at ~23.5x trailing EPS and ~15.1x current-year consensus EPS, with a ~2.9% dividend yield. Against the Biotechnology peer median P/E of 20.3 (GILD 17.6, VRTX 26.5, REGN 15.5, BIIB 21.0, UTHR 20.3), Amgen sits at a modest premium on trailing earnings and roughly in line on forward.

The premium is defensible on portfolio diversification, higher near-term growth (+10% sales vs flat-to-declining at several peers), and the MariTide option, which none of the listed peers carry to the same degree. The forward multiple of ~15x is not stretched in absolute terms. But the valuation does not offer asymmetric upside: flat revisions provide no rising-estimate tailwind to re-rate the multiple, the IRS ruling is an under-priced binary against thin equity, and pricing erosion deepens through 2027. The analyst dispersion captures this directly — Guggenheim Neutral at $340 (citing accelerating biosimilar erosion) versus Piper Sandler Overweight at $427 (MariTide and ~16 potential blockbusters). The consensus mean and median both sit at $352.5, roughly 4% above current price, with a wide $200–$427 range.

We do not treat the analyst mean as a floor here because the stock is at a premium-to-peer trailing multiple with flat revisions; the conditions under which targets lag reality (cheap stock, rising estimates) do not apply. Valuation scores 5.5: fairly priced for the visible growth and quality, with the upside option offset by the downside binary and no clear catalyst for multiple expansion in the next two quarters.

## Competitive Landscape

Amgen's manufacturing moat in biologics is genuine, and its diversified portfolio is a structural advantage over single-franchise peers. The competitive picture by franchise:

- **Obesity** is the defining battleground. MariTide must demonstrate competitive efficacy and tolerability and a differentiated dosing/adherence profile to take share from Lilly's tirzepatide. The bar is rising fast: Lilly retatrutide cleared Phase 3 (May 2026), Lilly and Novo are pushing high-dose and oral options, and Viking has an oral candidate. Convenience is a real wedge given GLP-1 adherence problems, but it is unproven at scale.
- **Cardiovascular**: Repatha competes with Novartis Leqvio and Regeneron/Sanofi Praluent; the broadened primary-prevention label is a TAM expansion that strengthens position.
- **Immunology**: Otezla faces IL-23/TYK2 agents (Skyrizi, Tremfya, Sotyktu); ENBREL faces a crowded field plus its own pricing collapse (340B-driven –36% net price).
- **Bone**: Prolia/XGEVA now face denosumab biosimilars head-on.

Five-year cumulative TSR of 166.6 trailed the peer group's 192.4, reflecting market skepticism about the post-LOE growth bridge that 2026 is meant to resolve. Competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on the growth franchises and clearly eroding on legacy. We score Competitive Position 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**IRS tax dispute (the largest discrete near-term binary).** The dispute is partially offset by ~$0.9B and ~$2.2B repatriation tax already paid. Trial concluded January 2025; a Tax Court decision is expected no earlier than H2 2026. Audits of 2016–2018 and the start of 2019–2022 (H1 2026) mean an adverse ruling could cascade. Against $8.66B of book equity, a large cash judgment is material to financial flexibility.

**U.S. drug-pricing/policy pressure (structural, intensifying).** The December 2025 decision to comply with the Administration's "July MFN Letter" (most-favored-nation pricing, direct-to-patient via AmgenNow/TrumpRx) has an uncertain net effect on pricing and margins. Colorado's PDAB declared ENBREL "unaffordable" with an Upper Payment Limit (≥2027); Washington selected ENBREL for review.

**Loss of exclusivity.** Prolia and XGEVA RANKL patents expired Feb 2025 (US), with multiple biosimilars launched and "accelerated sales erosion" guided for 2026.

**Pipeline setbacks.** Bemarituzumab first-line gastric cancer abandoned (Feb 2026); rocatinlimab collaboration with Kyowa Kirin terminated (Jan 2026); and the TAVNEOS standoff — FDA requested voluntary withdrawal (Jan 16, 2026) over re-adjudication and hepatotoxicity concerns; Amgen refused (Jan 28, 2026) and is contesting. The standoff signals management confidence in the data but adds regulatory-relationship and headline risk on a ~$460M product.

**Manufacturing concentration.** Commercial manufacturing concentrated in Puerto Rico (hurricane/grid risk); 30+ inherited CMOs, many single-source, including KRYSTEXXA drug substance produced in Israel (Middle East conflict exposure).

The clustering of two material binaries (IRS ruling, MariTide Phase 3) plus structural pricing erosion, against a thin equity base, justifies a Risk Factors score of 4.5. The IRS case in particular is an under-appreciated near-term event against limited book equity.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress that is not reflected in the flat estimate trend. Near-term ATM IV is 36.6% versus 27.3% one-year HV (rich, 9.3pp spread, 67th percentile), and the term structure is in backwardation (1-week 36.6% > 6-month 32.8%), which the market typically uses to signal an expected near-term event. The put/call skew is elevated at 20.1%, and ATM IV rose 6.8pp (29.8% to 36.6%) even as the stock rose 5.1% since the prior snapshot, with P/C OI ratio falling from 1.05 to 0.64. The mix — rising IV and elevated put skew alongside a price rally and falling put OI — reads as event-hedging into a known catalyst window (the IRS ruling timeline and ongoing obesity-competition data) rather than as outright bearish positioning. Short interest is low at 2.2% of float (4.4 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. For a HOLD, the signal reinforces caution: the market is paying up for near-term protection, consistent with the binary-risk framing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** 2026 confirms the springboard: growth products (Repatha post-VESALIUS, EVENITY, TEZSPIRE, BLINCYTO, IMDELLTRA, PAVBLU) outrun legacy erosion and price-setting, sustaining high-single-digit revenue growth. The IRS ruling is favorable or settles for a manageable sum. MariTide Phase 3 data confirms the second-year-maintenance, convenient-dosing profile, opening a credible obesity franchise that the market is not pricing into a ~15x forward multiple. EPS compounds toward and beyond consensus $23.48 next year, and the multiple re-rates toward the higher end of the peer band. Piper Sandler's $427 target frames this scenario.

**Bear case.** Denosumab biosimilar erosion and ENBREL/Otezla price-setting outpace new launches, flattening revenue growth; the IRS Tax Court rules adversely with a multi-billion cash judgment that, against thin equity, forces a re-rating and constrains capital allocation; MariTide disappoints on efficacy or tolerability relative to retatrutide and oral GLP-1s, eliminating the obesity option from the valuation. The TAVNEOS standoff and further pipeline setbacks add headline drag. Guggenheim's $340 Neutral (and the $200 low target) frames the downside.

The two cases are roughly balanced, with the IRS ruling acting as a near-term swing event that could move the stock materially in either direction before MariTide Phase 3 data fully arrives.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The growth inflection is real but already reflected in a premium-to-peer trailing multiple, and two unresolved binaries cap the asymmetry. This is a confident hold on the business and an uncertain hold on the equity's 12-month direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable IRS Tax Court ruling or a settlement materially below the claimed amount, removing the largest discrete overhang — combined with current-year EPS estimate revisions turning positive (>+5% over 90 days).
- MariTide Phase 3 obesity data confirming competitive efficacy and the differentiated dosing/adherence profile, supporting an obesity franchise the current multiple does not price.
- A pullback toward ~$300 (≈13x current-year EPS) that restores asymmetry while fundamentals remain intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse IRS ruling with a multi-billion cash judgment that forces a dividend or deleveraging trade-off.
- Two consecutive quarters where revenue growth decelerates below ~3% YoY as biosimilar/price-setting erosion outpaces launches, with falling estimate revisions.
- MariTide Phase 3 efficacy or tolerability disappointment relative to retatrutide/oral GLP-1s.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings, and specifically the H2 2026 Tax Court decision window, are the natural reassessment points. MariTide Phase 3 readouts over the following 12–24 months will determine the long-term call.

## Conclusion

Amgen is executing a credible portfolio transition with high-quality cash generation, a four-quarter beat streak, raised 2026 guidance, and a large embedded obesity option. The equity sits at a premium-to-peer trailing multiple with roughly flat estimate revisions, a balance sheet thinned to $8.66B of equity by the Horizon acquisition, and two unresolved binaries — the IRS dispute (Tax Court decision expected H2 2026) and MariTide's competitive position against an accelerating Lilly/Novo lead. The options market is paying up for near-term protection, consistent with that binary-risk profile. With the upside option offset by the downside event risk and no clear near-term catalyst for multiple expansion, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate AMGN HOLD and would look to upgrade on a favorable IRS resolution, confirmatory MariTide Phase 3 data, or a pullback toward $300.
