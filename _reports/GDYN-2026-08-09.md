## Executive Summary

Grid Dynamics is a 4,838-person digital engineering firm that bills 92.9% of revenue on time-and-materials, delivers 62% of its labor out of Central and Eastern Europe at a median employee cost of ~$52,400, and sells 68.5% of that labor to U.S. clients. At $7.58 the entire enterprise is valued at roughly $316.6M after netting $298.4M of cash against zero drawn debt — 0.70x FY2026 guided revenue and 5.9x FY2025 company-defined EBITDA of $53.8M. Cash alone is $3.68 per share, 48.5% of the quote. On assets and on a mid-single-digit growth business, that is a low price.

The reasons it is low are documented rather than mysterious. GAAP operating income has been negative for four consecutive fiscal years (−$21.0M, −$5.6M, −$2.1M, −$1.9M) and −$2.4M in H1 2026; every dollar of pretax profit in FY2023-FY2025 came from below the operating line, principally interest on the cash pile plus contingent-consideration mark-ups. That earnings source is now shrinking mechanically: other income fell 45.9% in H1 2026 as cash went from $342.1M to $298.4M funding $28.8M of buybacks, $14.1M net for Ekumen and $2.1M of earnouts, driving H1 net income down 83.2% to $1.379M despite revenue growth and improved operating income. Revenue growth decelerated from +17.5% in FY2025 to +5.3% in H1 2026, and the growth that remains is concentration: the largest customer supplied 66.9% of H1 incremental revenue, the top five supplied 141.7% (so revenue outside the top five fell 3.5%), and revenue from customers below the top 20 fell 8.7% in absolute dollars against a flat customer count of 213.

Q2 was the better print of the year — revenue $108.2M (+7.0%) above the company's own guided high end, gross margin +250bps to 36.6%, EBITDA margin +100bps to 13.6%, and positive quarterly GAAP operating income of $1.276M — and revenue per head rose 10.9% year over year on a 3.5% headcount decline, which is the most objective evidence available that AI tooling is currently helping this firm rather than deflating its billable base. Against that, the maintained $435-465M FY2026 range requires H2 revenue of $222.7-252.7M versus $210.3M a year ago; the $450M midpoint implies Q4 at ~$124.7M, or +17.5% year over year and +10.4% sequentially against an actual Q3→Q4 2025 sequential of +1.9%, with management explicitly naming "anticipated contributions from the M&A pipeline" as a pillar of that confidence. One week after that reaffirmation, EPAM — the closest listed comparable and one of only two firms Grid Dynamics itself designates a "reference company" — cut its annual revenue forecast and fell more than 18%. The stock has meanwhile risen 15% since the print. That combination leaves a thin margin of safety into a well-defined November risk event, and supports HOLD rather than accumulation.

## Company Overview

Grid Dynamics became public in March 2020 via a SPAC combination with ChaSerg Technology Acquisition Corp.; the operating business dates to 2006. It markets itself as an AI transformation partner for the Fortune 1000. Structurally it is a single reportable segment with no discrete profitability disclosed by vertical, client or geography; the CEO is the chief operating decision maker and only revenue is disaggregated below the consolidated line. That is a real analytical constraint: an investor cannot see which accounts or verticals earn money.

The model is labor arbitrage with an AI-delivery overlay. Q2 2026 revenue split $100.5M time-and-materials (92.9%) versus $7.6M fixed-fee (7.1%). Headcount at June 30, 2026: Europe 3,011 (62.2%), Americas 863 (17.8%), rest of world 964 (19.9%), with U.S. staff at 7.8% of total per management. Long-lived assets sit in Poland ($5.9M), Ukraine ($2.3M), Serbia ($2.1M), the U.S. ($1.6M), Armenia ($1.4M) and Moldova ($1.3M). H1 2026 revenue by country: U.S. $145.4M (68.5%), U.K. $26.1M, India $8.9M, Poland $7.6M, other $24.3M.

H1 2026 verticals (with prior-year comparison, and the 10-Q discloses prior-period reclassifications): TMT $65.1M (+33.5%), Retail $56.4M (−6.0%), Finance $49.2M (−2.4%), CPG/Manufacturing $23.3M (+4.0%), Healthcare & Pharma $4.3M (−14.1%), Other $13.9M (−6.5%). The rise in TMT from 24.2% to 30.7% of revenue and the rise in top-one concentration from 14.7% to 17.3% are the same event described two ways; TMT growth was attributed in the 10-Q to "strong demand from our largest technology customers."

Differentiation claimed by management is the "GAIN" AI-native engagement model, a target to certify 90% of engineers on an AI software development lifecycle by end-October 2026, and partner-influenced revenue of 19.1% trending toward 25-30%. There is no proprietary IP moat evident in the filings: capitalized software development cost is $34.2M gross but only $11.4M net on a two-year amortization life, consistent with internal tooling. Goodwill has grown from $45.5M (YE2022) to $91.7M (6/30/2026) through tuck-ins including JUXT, Mobile Computing (Oct 2024) and Ekumen (May 2026).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2022 $310.5M → FY2023 $312.9M (+0.8%) → FY2024 $350.6M (+12.0%) → FY2025 $411.8M (+17.5%) → H1 2026 $212.3M (+5.3%), Q2 2026 $108.2M (+7.0%). Three-year CAGR to FY2025 was 9.9%. The 2023 flat year and the 2026 re-deceleration frame this as a discretionary-spend cyclical, not a compounder.

**Where the profit comes from.** The table below is the single most important thing in the financials:

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating income | −$21.0M | −$5.6M | −$2.1M | −$1.9M | −$2.4M |
| Other income, net | +$0.6M | +$10.4M | +$13.2M | +$17.6M | +$6.4M |
| Pretax income | −$20.5M | +$4.8M | +$11.1M | +$15.7M | +$4.0M |
| Net income | −$29.2M | −$1.8M | +$4.0M | +$9.7M | +$1.4M |

In Q2 2026, other income of $3.187M was 2.5x operating income of $1.276M. Other income fell 57.1% in Q2 and 45.9% in H1 as the interest-bearing asset was spent. Part of "other income" is non-cash earnout remeasurement — a $1.183M fair-value gain in H1 2026 — so the pure interest sensitivity is smaller than the headline swing. The practical implication stands: capital returns and M&A at this company reduce reported GAAP EPS roughly one-for-one with the interest income foregone, even as per-share metrics improve. Company-defined EBITDA and free cash flow are the right lenses; GAAP net income is the wrong one.

**Margins.** Gross margin declined 38.8% → 36.2% → 36.2% → 34.6% across FY2022-FY2025, then rebounded to 36.6% in Q2 2026 from 34.1% (H1 35.7% vs 35.5%). Non-GAAP EBITDA was $52.5M (15.0% margin) in FY2024 and $53.8M (13.1%) in FY2025 — $1.3M of EBITDA growth on $61.3M of incremental revenue, an incremental margin near 2%. H1 2026 EBITDA was $27.242M versus $27.354M, down $112K on $10.75M more revenue, though Q2 margin improved to 13.6% from 12.6%. Management has committed to 300bps of margin expansion via a shift toward fixed-bid work; with fixed-fee at 7.1% of revenue, that transition is barely begun and moves estimation and delivery risk onto Grid Dynamics' own P&L.

**Cash flow quality deteriorated in H1.** Operating cash flow $14.493M versus $23.731M; capex $7.538M versus $7.511M; derived FCF $6.955M (3.3% margin) versus $16.220M (8.0%). FY2025 full-year FCF was $25.3M (6.1%). The driver is receivables: the AR change consumed $15.379M versus $8.755M. Trade receivables rose 20.2% to $95.562M against 5.3% revenue growth; derived DSO went from ~68.8 days (Q4 2025) to ~80.4 days (Q2 2026), or ~79.1 days adjusting out Ekumen's acquired receivables. Bad debt expense doubled to $855K from $399K and the allowance rose 20.2% to $4.471M. The most probable explanation is mix shift toward very large enterprise accounts with longer payment terms — a direct consequence of the concentration trend rather than a credit event — but the June-versus-December comparison is not seasonally clean and the June 30, 2025 balance is not available, so this is a monitoring item for Q3 rather than an established deterioration. H1 stock-based compensation of $12.883M was ~9.3x GAAP net income; the majority of reported operating cash generation is an add-back of equity dilution.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $298.429M (of which $234.3M in money market funds, $50.4M held outside the U.S.), zero debt drawn, a $30.0M undrawn JPMorgan revolver maturing March 15, 2028 with accordion to $50M and in compliance with its leverage covenant, current ratio 7.2x, working capital $353.4M, total liabilities $80.4M against $519.9M equity. Solvency risk is effectively absent. A Z''-score computed on this balance sheet exceeds 10, but essentially all of it comes from the working-capital and equity-to-liabilities terms; both earnings-power terms are slightly negative. This is a fortress balance sheet attached to a barely-profitable operating business, and the two facts should be held separately.

**Tax.** Effective rates of 136% (FY2023), 63.4% (FY2024), 38.4% (FY2025) and 65.7% (H1 2026) reflect consolidated GAAP operating losses coexisting with taxable profits in foreign delivery entities plus large non-deductible equity compensation. Cash taxes of $3.863M in H1 2026 versus $6.556M a year earlier indicate the book rate overstates cash drag, but GAAP net income will remain heavily and erratically taxed.

## Growth Analysis

The reported +5.3% H1 growth decomposes badly. Derived from the 10-Q concentration note: the largest customer grew from $29.536M to $36.732M, contributing +$7.196M or 66.9% of the $10.754M increment; the top five grew +$15.235M, or 141.7% of total growth, meaning revenue outside the top five declined $4.481M (−3.5%); revenue below the top 20 declined $4.790M (−8.7%). Concentration rose at every tier: top-one 14.7%→17.3%, top-five 36.5%→41.8%, top-ten 56.9%→60.4%, top-twenty 72.8%→76.4%. Customer count was 213 versus 211.

Management's framing is vendor consolidation — Grid Dynamics displacing smaller, less technically capable suppliers at premier accounts. That is a coherent and, in IT services, historically durable form of share gain, and it typically produces exactly this concentration pattern. But it does not constitute diversification, and the simultaneous 8.7% contraction of the long tail says the wins are not broad-based.

The second decomposition is the AI split. Company-reported (unaudited, undefined in the filings) AI revenue was 30.7% of Q2 revenue, +54.6% year over year. Deriving from disclosed totals, AI revenue was ≈$33.2M in Q2 2026 versus ≈$21.5M in Q2 2025, implying non-AI revenue of $74.96M versus $79.62M, or −5.9%. Management confirmed the direction in Q&A, arguing growth in agentic and physical AI "substantially exceeds the decline in legacy business segments." That is candid, and it reframes the security: roughly 69% of the business is shrinking mid-single-digits, and consolidated +5.3% is the net score of a transition race. Because 92.9% of revenue is T&M labor, the AI metric plausibly reflects project reclassification as much as new demand, in which case both the growth rate and the implied legacy decline are overstated and the underlying business is more uniformly mid-single-digit.

The bullish counter-evidence is productivity. Derived revenue per head annualized: $89.4K in Q2 2026 versus $80.7K in Q2 2025, +10.9%, with headcount down 3.5% (Europe −229) and gross margin up 250bps. That is consistent with management's stated shift toward experienced senior talent over volume staffing. It cost $4.293M of H1 restructuring versus $0.863M, and the 10-Q does not split headcount by billable status, so the assertion that reductions were confined to non-billable roles is unverified.

**The guidance arithmetic is the near-term crux.** FY2026 maintained at $435-465M against H1 actuals of $212.264M implies H2 of $222.7-252.7M versus $210.317M in H2 2025:

| Scenario | Implied H2 | H2 YoY | Implied Q4 | Q4 YoY | Q3→Q4 seq. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $435M | $222.7M | +5.9% | $109.7M | +3.3% | −2.9% |
| $450M | $237.7M | +13.0% | $124.7M | +17.5% | +10.4% |
| $465M | $252.7M | +20.2% | $139.7M | +31.6% | +23.7% |

Actual Q3→Q4 2025 sequential was +1.9%. The midpoint requires roughly five times that seasonal step and an acceleration from +7.0% in Q2 to +17.5% in Q4, with management naming M&A pipeline contribution as one of three pillars and stating acquisitions "will be a component of achieving the higher end" of the range. The low end looks comfortably achievable; the midpoint requires either a demand inflection or unannounced deals. On August 6, one week after the reaffirmation, EPAM cut its annual revenue forecast and fell more than 18% (headline-level source only; magnitude and stated causes unverified). Grid Dynamics is roughly one-eighth EPAM's size and could genuinely be taking share, and its Q2 did exceed its own guided high end, but the burden of proof on the H2 ramp has risen.

Consensus estimate revisions are mildly constructive on the current year and mildly negative beyond: current quarter $0.13 (+4.0% over 90 days), current year $0.47 (+5.0% over 90 days), next year $0.52 (−2.4% over 90 days). This is not the >30% revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection; it is a stable, modestly rising non-GAAP number in a decelerating business.

## Valuation Assessment

Derived from the frozen $7.58 basis:

- Market cap $615M; cash $298.4M; no debt → **EV ≈ $316.6M**
- Cash per share **$3.68** (48.5% of price); ex-cash operating business ≈ **$3.90/share**
- **EV/revenue:** 0.77x FY2025 actual, **0.70x** on the $450M FY2026 midpoint
- **EV/EBITDA (company-defined):** 316.6 / 53.8 = **5.9x** FY2025
- **Non-GAAP P/E:** 7.58 / $0.47 current-year consensus = **16.1x**; 7.58 / $0.52 next year = 14.6x
- **GAAP P/E:** not meaningful (63.6x on FY2025 net income; >200x annualizing H1 2026)
- **FCF yield on EV:** 8.0% on FY2025 FCF of $25.3M; 4.4% on H1 2026 annualized

Two adjustments matter. First, the consensus non-GAAP EPS embeds meaningful interest income; stripping the pretax interest component out lowers ex-interest non-GAAP EPS materially, so the operating business at $3.90 per share trades at a higher multiple than the headline non-GAAP P/E implies. Second, non-GAAP excludes stock-based compensation (H1 2026 SBC was $12.883M) against a company that grants 8.4% of shares in unvested awards and has 14.4% of the current base in outstanding-plus-available equity (12.6% post-issuance ownership dilution if fully issued). Adjust for that and the economic-earnings multiple is higher still.

Peer context is heterogeneous and of limited use: the supplied same-industry set shows ASGN at 15.1x, VYX 23.8x, DXC 88.2x, ADEA 29.9x, TDC 7.5x — median 23.8x, but these span staffing, IP licensing and legacy outsourcing with different economics. The relevant comparables are EPAM, Globant and Endava, all of which have derated hard on the same demand and AI-pricing questions; EPAM's guidance cut is the live datapoint. Against those, Grid Dynamics' 0.70x EV/revenue is a discount that reflects subscale, worsening concentration, zero contracted revenue and a 16.6% Chinese-listed holder with two board seats and registration rights that plausibly deters strategic and activist interest.

Is this a mispricing or a discount for cause? The honest answer is mostly the latter, with a partial cushion. The cushion is real: net cash equal to half the market cap, a $50M buyback authorization with $19.2M remaining, executed at a ~$6.53 H1 average that is 14% below today's price, and disciplined price sensitivity (2.26M shares bought at ~$6.58 in May, only 345K at ~$7.00 in June). The cause is also real: no RPO, no backlog, negative GAAP operating margin, growth entirely from one to five accounts, a company-defined AI metric doing the narrative work, and governance that shareholders have already rejected once at the ballot.

**Scenario framework:**

- **Bear ($5.90, −22%):** EPAM read-through confirms sector demand weakness; Q3 guidance trimmed and FY2026 lands at or below $435M; largest-customer spend normalizes; FY2027 flat. EBITDA ~$48M at 5x EV/EBITDA = $240M EV plus ~$285M cash. Cash and buyback limit downside below this.
- **Base ($8.00, +5.5%):** FY2026 lands $437-445M, low half of range; Q2 gross-margin gain partially holds; FY2027 revenue ~$465M with 13.5-14% EBITDA. 0.8x EV/revenue on ~$440M = $352M EV plus ~$290M cash = $8.00.
- **Bull ($10.75, +42%):** H2 ramp delivers $455M+, the fixed-bid shift begins landing the promised 300bps, AI revenue growth outruns the legacy decline decisively, FY2027 revenue ~$490M at 15% EBITDA = $73M, 8x = $584M EV plus $300M cash.

Probability-weighting base 42% / bull 25% / bear 33% gives an expected return near +5-6%, with wide dispersion and low confidence given the absence of any contracted-revenue anchor. Analyst targets ($7.00 low, $9.20 mean, $9.00 median, $12.00 high; 5 Buy/Strong Buy of 6) sit above the base case but were largely set before the EPAM cut and should be treated as stale rather than as independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Grid Dynamics is subscale against EPAM, Globant, Endava and Accenture. Its plausible edge is agility: a 4,838-person organization can re-tool delivery to an AI-native model far faster than a 50,000-person incumbent, and $18M tuck-ins such as Ekumen (Buenos Aires robotics and physical-AI engineering, ROS expertise; $16.8M cash plus $1.6M contingent consideration at fair value against a $2.5M maximum; $10.4M of customer-relationship intangibles on an eight-year life; contribution described as de minimis) are immaterial to larger peers but can add a capability line here. The Doosan Robotics arrangement announced on the Q2 call is characterized as a partnership with no disclosed economics and no filing confirmation; it should not be modeled as revenue.

The plausible vulnerability is the structure of the market it serves. Fortune 1000 clients rationalizing toward two or three vendors is currently working in Grid Dynamics' favor at its largest accounts — the vendor-consolidation story, consistent with the +33.5% TMT growth and 66.9%-of-increment top-one contribution. The same dynamic is working against it in the tail, which shrank 8.7%. A 213-customer firm with 76.4% of revenue in its top 20 has minimal cushion if one displacement win reverses.

The structural question is whether AI compresses billable hours faster than scope expands. With 92.9% of revenue billed by the hour, hour deflation is a direct revenue risk. Current evidence points the other way — revenue per head +10.9%, gross margin +250bps, headcount −3.5% — which is the strongest single argument for the bull case. But that evidence is two quarters old, cost $4.3M of restructuring, and the offsetting capture mechanism (fixed-bid contracting) is at 7.1% of revenue. Competitive trajectory is therefore genuinely improving at the top of the client roster and genuinely deteriorating in the tail, which nets to roughly stable.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Customer concentration, worsening on every tier, with zero contractual visibility.** All performance obligations met a practical expedient at June 30, 2026, so no RPO is disclosed at all; deferred revenue is $1.508M. A 20% spend reduction at the largest client alone removes ~3.5 points of company revenue with no backlog cushion.
2. **H2 guidance dependent on unannounced M&A** and a Q4 sequential step roughly 5x the prior-year pattern, into a peer guidance cut. Q3 (early November) is the resolution point.
3. **Legacy revenue declining ~5.9%** on the derived split; the investment case rests on a transition race with no disclosed timeline.
4. **Geopolitical delivery concentration.** The 10-Q language escalated, flagging risk that Russia could seize Ukrainian assets of U.S.-registered companies and identifying situations in Armenia, Serbia and a possible invasion of Moldova — all locations with long-lived assets. Asset values are small ($1.3-2.3M each); the at-risk delivery capacity of 3,000+ people generating ~68% U.S.-billed revenue is not. Geographic reorganization/exit expenses ran $712K in H1.
5. **FX.** 49.1% of Q2 cost of revenue plus operating expenses is non-USD against ~68.5% U.S.-billed revenue; hedging is limited to ≤6-month PLN forwards. Disclosed sensitivities (~$0.5M PLN, ~$0.5M INR, ~$0.4M RSD per 10% move) are material against ~$1.3M of quarterly operating income.
6. **Related-party revenue.** $8.7M in Q2 and $16.3M in H1 2026 from companies affiliated with board members — 7.7% of H1 revenue, growing 14.8% versus the company's 5.3%, contributing ~19.5% of total growth. Grid Dynamics is the service provider receiving payment; board-affiliated entities are the paying customers. Counterparties are unnamed, and the pricing and arm's-length character cannot be assessed. Related-party billed receivables rose from $4.6M to $6.5M.
7. **Ownership overhang.** Beijing Teamsun Technology (Shanghai-listed) beneficially owns 13,889,183 shares (16.6%) through four intermediate entities, with two affiliated directors on a nine-member board and up to three demand registrations plus piggyback rights. No CFIUS action, inquiry or client loss attributable to this appears in the record; the risk operates through potential-seller, strategic-buyer-deterrence and enterprise-procurement channels. This is plausibly part of why a debt-free company at 0.70x EV/revenue with half its cap in cash has drawn no strategic or activist interest.
8. **Governance.** Say-on-pay support collapsed to ~57% from 98%. FY2025 PSUs paid 206% of target — 175% on revenue growth, 200% (capped) on contribution margin, a −10% relative-TSR modifier for a −28.17% TSR ranking in the 27th percentile of the Russell 2000, more than offset by a +20% relative-revenue-CAGR modifier that duplicates the primary revenue metric. Livschitz vested 658,240 shares against a 320,000 target. Three CEO family members are employed with expected 2026 increases of 53% and 60%. Only three executive officers; classified board; board met four times in 2025; double-trigger change-in-control cost ~$50.0M, roughly 16% of EV. Two responsive remediations were made (removal of the option-repricing provision, enhanced PSU disclosure) after engaging holders of ~40% of shares.
9. **Working capital and receivables drift**, with bad debt expense doubling, requiring confirmation in Q3.
10. **Evidence gaps.** Items 1, 1A, 7 and 8 of the FY2025 10-K were not retrievable, so the official risk-factor set and audited FY2025 notes are absent; two Item 5.02 8-Ks (November 10 and December 23, 2025) covering director/officer changes are unread; the AI-revenue metric is company-defined and unaudited; the EPAM cut is headline-level only. These gaps argue for wider scenario dispersion rather than a directional adjustment.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility fell from 85.8% to 74.8% between July 30 and August 9 while the price rose 15%, and the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 0.11 to 0.03 — an unusually call-tilted book with almost no downside protection outstanding. The options market has taken risk off after the print and is pricing complacency into the next quarter. That sits awkwardly against a guidance midpoint requiring a Q4 sequential ramp 5x the prior-year pattern and a peer that cut on August 6. Short interest of 9.7% of float at 1.9 days to cover is moderate: enough to produce a squeeze on good news, not enough to make the bear case crowded. The setup favors patience over adding at $7.58.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($10.75, +42%).** Q2 marks the operating inflection: first positive quarterly GAAP operating income in the recent record, gross margin +250bps, EBITDA margin +100bps, revenue per head +10.9% on a smaller headcount. The vendor-consolidation dynamic is genuine and Grid Dynamics is displacing weaker suppliers at premier technology and financial accounts, which historically compounds. AI revenue at 30.7% growing 54.6% eventually outruns a legacy base declining 5.9%, and the fixed-bid shift delivers the committed 300bps. Ekumen and the Doosan relationship give a credible physical-AI franchise that larger peers are not yet contesting at this size. On $490M of FY2027 revenue at 15% EBITDA, 8x EV/EBITDA plus $300M of cash supports $10.75. An operating business priced at $3.90 per share with no debt is a plausible take-private candidate if the Teamsun stake ever clears.

**Bear case ($5.90, −22%).** EPAM's cut is the leading edge of a demand air pocket in CEE-delivered digital engineering. Grid Dynamics trims Q3 guidance in November, FY2026 lands at or below $435M, and the H2 ramp that never depended on organic strength fails to materialize because the M&A that was supposed to fund it does not close. The largest customer, having supplied two-thirds of H1's incremental revenue, normalizes spend, and there is no backlog to absorb it. The AI metric proves to be reclassification, revealing a uniformly mid-single-digit-declining business. Interest income keeps shrinking as cash is spent, GAAP earnings stay near zero, the tax rate stays punitive, and multiple compression to 5x EBITDA takes the equity to $5.90, with the $3.68 of cash per share and the remaining $19.2M of buyback authorization as the practical floor.

**Base case ($8.00, +5.5%).** FY2026 lands in the low half of guidance around $437-445M; the Q2 margin gain partly holds; FY2027 grows high single digits to ~$465M at 13.5-14% EBITDA. At 0.8x EV/revenue plus net cash that is $8.00, roughly fair value from $7.58.

The decisive disconfirming evidence for the bull case is that FY2025 — a "record revenue" year at +17.5% — produced $1.3M of EBITDA growth, and H1 2026 EBITDA was down $112K in absolute dollars on $10.75M more revenue. Operating leverage has not shown up at any volume level yet, and one improving quarter is not a proven inflection.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +5-6% over 12 months, with a −22% bear tail at meaningful probability and no contracted-revenue anchor to raise confidence. Existing holders own a business with half its market cap in cash, no debt, an active and well-executed buyback, and a first quarter of positive operating income. New capital has no edge at $7.58, 15% above the pre-print level and going into a Q3 report where the guidance arithmetic and a peer's cut both point the same way.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 report, expected late October/early November 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
- Q3 revenue at or above ~$113M *with* FY2026 guidance narrowed to the upper half on organic grounds, i.e. without an acquisition supplying the difference.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive GAAP operating income together with fixed-fee revenue rising above ~12% of total, evidencing the 300bps margin program.
- Revenue outside the top five customers returning to growth, or DSO retracing to ≤72 days with bad-debt expense normalizing.
- Price toward $6.25-6.50 with the above fundamentals intact, which would restore roughly 25-30% upside to base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
- FY2026 guidance cut, or Q4 guided to sequential decline, confirming the EPAM read-through.

## Conclusion

Grid Dynamics is inexpensive on assets and defensible on solvency: $298.4M of cash, no drawn debt, a 7.2x current ratio, and an operating business valued at roughly $316.6M — 0.70x guided revenue and 5.9x company-defined EBITDA.
