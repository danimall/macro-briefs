## Executive Summary

Ellington Financial is an externally managed mortgage REIT with an unusual second engine: Longbridge Financial, a wholly owned reverse-mortgage originator, servicer, and Ginnie Mae HMBS issuer. At $13.33 the stock trades at 0.98x the $13.61 preliminary June 30 book value estimate and yields 11.70%. The operating story has improved on several measurable axes. Net interest income rose 39.3% in FY2025 to $190.0M on 18.9% interest-income growth, credit-portfolio net interest margin widened from 2.83% to 3.25%, repo haircuts on reverse-mortgage collateral fell from 15.4% to 8.4%, total cost of funds fell to 4.70% from 5.18%, and Longbridge's Q1-2026 segment net income of $57.5M exceeded its entire FY2025 segment result. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters, and consensus for the current year has risen 4.1% over 90 days.

Three things keep this from being a buy. First, the reported earnings metric on which the yield case rests overstates cash available to shareholders. Consensus "EPS" for EFC tracks Adjusted Distributable Earnings — the Q1-2026 estimate of $0.44 versus the $0.55 ADE actual confirms the basis — and ADE adds back the incentive fee paid to the external manager. That fee was $0 in FY2023, $0 in FY2024, $4.5M in FY2025, and $19.2M in Q1-2026 alone. Charging Q1's fee against Q1 ADE reduces coverage from 1.41x to 1.01x. Second, the hurdle on which that fee is calculated was redefined effective April 1, 2025 from weighted-average gross proceeds per share since inception to GAAP common equity of the operating partnership — a base of roughly $13.16 versus additional paid-in capital per share of $16.93 at YE2025, lowering the annual hurdle by approximately $0.34/share. Third, book value per share is $13.61 against $13.99 at YE2023; the two-year 2024–2025 economic return of 16.6% (about 8.0% annualized) was earned while the book shrank and dividends were partly funded by accretive equity issuance.

Net of the fee, the distribution is being earned, but with negligible cushion in the most recent reported quarter and no discount to book to absorb an error in a balance sheet that is 96.3% Level 3. Base case twelve-month total return is roughly 15%, essentially all of it the dividend. Risk/reward from $13.33 is $2.17 of upside to a $15.50 bull case against $2.58 of downside to a $10.75 bear case — insufficient asymmetry for new capital at book. HOLD for income.

## Company Overview

EFC operates two reported segments. The Investment Portfolio segment holds residential and commercial mortgage loans, RMBS/CMBS, CLOs, ABS, consumer loans, forward MSR-related investments, and equity and debt stakes in loan-origination companies — $6.38B of assets against $2.15B of segment equity at YE2025. The Longbridge segment originates, services, and invests in reverse mortgages — $12.78B of assets against only $282M of segment equity, the leverage supplied almost entirely by non-recourse HMBS obligations.

Total assets were $19.35B at YE2025, up 18.6% year over year. Reverse mortgage loans of $12.33B represent about 64% of the balance sheet, matched against $10.41B of non-recourse HMBS-related obligations. This gross-up is why headline total debt-to-equity of 9.1x is not the operative leverage measure: recourse debt-to-equity was 1.9:1 at YE2025 and 2.0:1 at March 31, 2026, which is conservative for the sector. Standard distress screens (Altman Z, Piotroski) misfire on this structure — the former is calibrated to industrials, and the latter penalizes the operating-cash-flow classification of held-for-sale loan production. Neither should be read as solvency evidence here.

The company is externally managed by an affiliate of Ellington Management Group. PwC has audited since 2007 with clean opinions on FY2025 financials and internal control.

## Financial Analysis

**Spread expansion is real.** Interest income grew from $416.0M (FY2024) to $494.5M (FY2025) while interest expense grew only from $279.6M to $304.5M, lifting net interest income 39.3% to $190.0M. Management attributes this to credit-portfolio NIM widening to 3.25% from 2.83% as funding costs fell faster than asset yields. The Q1-2026 10-Q corroborates: weighted average repo cost declined, term extended to 263 days with 41% beyond 364 days, and aggregate amount at risk was $585.3M across 21 counterparties with none exceeding 10% of equity (versus one counterparty at 11.7% at YE2024).

**Per-share earnings did not follow.** Net income to common was $117.8M in FY2024 and $118.7M in FY2025 — a $0.9M increase — while weighted average shares including convertible units rose 14.6% from 87.7M to 100.5M. GAAP EPS therefore fell from $1.36 to $1.19. Share count rose again in Q1-2026, from 113.1M to 124.6M (a 10.17% increase in shares outstanding, equivalently 9.23% ownership dilution for pre-existing holders), on 11.5M shares issued for $154.6M net, or $13.43/share — above the $13.16 YE2025 book and therefore accretive to book per share, but a persistent drag on per-share earnings growth.

**Cash flow requires interpretation, not alarm.** Operating cash flow was −$925.5M in FY2025, but the dominant driver was −$946.6M of originations, purchases, and fundings of loans held for sale, which mortgage-banking GAAP routes through operating activities. Financing provided $5.12B (including $1.54B of HMBS issuance and $302M of net equity) and investing consumed $4.07B. The substantive observation is narrower: the $158.3M of FY2025 common dividends was funded predominantly from capital-markets access and portfolio runoff rather than operating cash generation, which is normal for a levered mortgage REIT and simultaneously means the distribution depends on continuous HMBS, securitization, and repo access.

**Book value, the correct anchor.** $13.99 (YE2023) → $13.51 (YE2024) → $13.16 (YE2025) → $13.56 (3/31/2026) → $13.61 (6/30/2026 preliminary estimate). The 2026 recovery is genuine: Q1 economic return was ($13.56 − $13.16 + $0.39) ÷ $13.16 = 6.0%. But Q2 decelerated to approximately ($13.61 − $13.56 + $0.39) ÷ $13.56 = 3.2%, or roughly 13% annualized against a dividend representing 11.5% of book — enough to grind book higher by perhaps $0.20/year, not enough to compound it.

**Earnings quality in Q1-2026.** Reported EPS of $0.78 included a $17.0M Longbridge litigation settlement (counterparty undisclosed) and a $21.2M unrealized gain on EFC's own fair-valued borrowings arising from its credit spreads widening — a paper gain that reverses if spreads tighten. Together roughly $0.31, or 40% of the quarter's GAAP EPS. To management's credit, both are excluded from ADE.

**The coverage recalculation that matters most.** ADE of $0.55 in Q1-2026 covers the $0.39 quarterly dividend 1.41x as reported. The $19.222M incentive fee equals $0.156/share on ~123.0M weighted shares. ADE net of the fee is $0.394 against $0.39 — 1.01x. For FY2025 the same adjustment is benign ($1.825 versus $1.56, 1.17x), so this is a Q1-2026 condition rather than a chronic one. A supporting signal points the same direction: return of capital rose from 14.8% of distributions in FY2024 to 32.5% in FY2025, and EFC has distributed in excess of GAAP earnings for three consecutive years ($62.5M, $21.8M, $37.9M), widening accumulated deficit to −$413.0M. Fair-value/tax timing differences, TRS income outside REIT taxable income, and NOL utilization are all legitimate innocent explanations for rising ROC, and ROC is tax-deferred for holders. It is a caveat, not proof of shortfall.

**Debt maturities and preferred.** Unsecured principal totals $647.75M: $37.8M of 6.00% notes due August 1, 2026 (essentially at hand), $210.0M of 5.875% notes due April 1, 2027 (callable at par since April 1, 2026), $400.0M of 7.375% notes due September 30, 2030, and $15.0M of trust preferred. The 7.375% coupon on the 2030 issue versus 5.875% on the maturing paper quantifies the refinancing cost at roughly 150bp — a direct consequence of the long-end repricing described below. The preferred stack after February's Series A redemption is $230.0M of liquidation preference; Series B ($120.5M, 6.250%) resets January 30, 2027 at five-year Treasury plus 4.99%. At a 4.0% five-year Treasury that implies roughly 9.0% and about $3.4M of incremental annual cost, though redemption is a plausible alternative given the Series A precedent. *(The five-year Treasury input is an estimate, not a filing fact.)*

## Growth Analysis

The business is growing; per-share economics are not, and the gap is the analytical crux.

**Longbridge and proprietary reverse are the genuine growth engine.** Longbridge Q1-2026 segment net income of $57.5M compares to −$1.0M in Q1-2025 and exceeds the $34.7M FY2025 segment total. Segment ADE was $25.4M versus $6.0M. Originations of $515.4M rose 52% year over year. The mix shift is favorable: securitized proprietary (non-FHA) reverse loans grew from $606.8M to $1,400.7M of fair value in FY2025 and to roughly $1.78B UPB by March 31, 2026, and proprietary loans carry a 9.63% weighted average coupon versus 5.88% on HECM collateral backing HMBS. Industry data corroborates the tailwind — proprietary reverse volume reportedly rose 118% in 2025 to a 22% share, and proprietary volume ($953M) exceeded HECM ($875M) for the first time in Q1-2026 — though these figures are externally reported and not verified in EFC filings.

**Non-QM volume forecasts are supportive but competitive.** A projected rise in non-QM originations to roughly $175B in 2026 from ~$108B in 2025 would be a supply tailwind for a platform that closed fifteen non-consolidated EFMT securitizations in 2025 plus four proprietary reverse deals and five co-sponsored deals. Against that, Redwood's Aspire platform is reportedly targeting ~10% of the non-QM market backed by an $8B capital partnership, and Angel Oak is scaling. Volume growth in this asset class historically compresses gain-on-sale and spread rather than expanding it.

**Consensus sees no per-share growth.** Current-year consensus of $1.94 and next-year of $1.93 imply a flat trajectory; 30-day revisions are zero on all four horizons and 90-day revisions are +0.9% to +4.1%. That is a stable, not accelerating, estimate picture — well short of the inflection threshold that would justify overriding valuation concerns. Longbridge's earnings are also demonstrably lumpy: GAAP segment income *declined* slightly in FY2025 ($37.3M → $34.7M) even as segment ADE rose from $26.8M to $49.5M and segment interest income more than doubled.

Three structural leaks convert business growth into flat per-share growth: continuous equity issuance (14.6% weighted-share growth in FY2025, 10.2% share-count growth in Q1-2026 alone), the incentive fee on the reset hurdle, and total FY2025 payments to the manager of $62.5M ($25.4M base fee, $4.5M incentive, $32.6M uncapped expense reimbursement) against roughly $1,357M of average common equity — about 4.6%, of which base-plus-incentive was ~2.2%. The reimbursement covers personnel an internally managed REIT would pay directly, so 4.6% overstates the excess, but the fee-only load is real.

## Valuation Assessment

**Reconciliation first.** The supplied trailing and forward P/E of 7.81 does not reconcile with the supplied EPS of $1.19414, which implies 11.2x at $13.33. On consensus current-year $1.94 the multiple is 6.9x. Because that consensus series is ADE-based, the honest translation is that EFC trades at 6.9x price-to-ADE and 11.2x trailing GAAP EPS. Net of an incentive fee running at $0.30–0.60/share annualized, comparable cash earnings would be $1.34–$1.64, i.e. 8.1x–9.9x, with dividend coverage of 0.86x–1.05x. That range, not the 6.9x headline, is what a buyer is underwriting.

**Price to book is the operative metric.** At $13.33 against the $13.61 preliminary June 30 estimate, EFC trades at 0.979x book; against audited March 31 book of $13.56, 0.983x. Mortgage REITs generally clear between roughly 0.75x and 1.0x book, and EFC sits at the top of that band — which is precisely the stated rationale for BTIG's June 17, 2026 downgrade to Neutral on "premium valuation," reportedly alongside upgrades of MITT and MFA to Buy. That is a relative-value call rather than a fundamental deterioration call, and it is consistent with EFC's documented five-year TSR of $167.05 per $100 invested versus $114.13 for its proxy peer group (~10.8%/yr versus ~2.7%/yr) having compressed its discount away.

**The peer table supplied is not usable as a valuation anchor.** EVTC, HOPE, DCOM, CASH, and STBA are banks and a payments processor with a median P/E of 13.2 and a 2.5% yield. Comparing a 9.0x-total-leverage, fair-value mortgage REIT with an 11.7% yield to a community-bank median produces no signal. The relevant comparison set is levered mortgage credit vehicles valued on price/book and coverage.

**Where the margin of safety would come from — and doesn't.** At 0.98x book there is no cushion for an error in a book that is 96.3% Level 3 ($17.57B of $18.25B of fair-valued assets; 97.7% of fair-valued liabilities), where PwC's sole Critical Audit Matter is precisely those valuations. Disclosed unobservable inputs show HECM loans discounted at a 4.2% weighted average yield with a 7.6% CPR and non-performing residential loans at a 7.0% yield with 84.6% recovery over 18.4 months. Separately, $79.6M of loan-originator equity stakes are carried at a 2.2x weighted average price-to-book (range 0.4x–2.4x); remarking to 1.0x would cost roughly $43.4M, or $0.35/share — 2.6% of book.

**Absolute yield support is the real valuation argument.** An 11.70% distribution covered approximately 1.0x on an all-in basis, on a balance sheet with 2.0x recourse leverage and improving funding terms, is defensible in a 4.67% ten-year environment. Analyst targets bracket $14.00 to $16.00 with a $15.00 median — every published target sits above spot, though the composition has degraded (strong buys 4 → 3 over one month), and the BTIG action carried no target. Valuation scores 5.3: the yield is genuine, the discount is absent, and the fee structure is a forward claim on returns that consensus may not fully model.

## Competitive Landscape

EFC's differentiation is structural rather than asset-specific: an owned reverse-mortgage originator/servicer producing origination and servicing economics rather than pure spread; a non-QM sourcing network built on equity stakes in the originators themselves (LendSure at 63.1% total ownership, carried at $52.6M); and an active derivatives hedging program with average interest-rate swap notional of $11.50B in FY2025 versus $6.26B in FY2024.

The hedging claim has a same-quarter, same-asset-class test. In Q1-2026 — ten-year up 15bp to 4.32%, CDX IG +13bp, HY +69bp, MOVE up roughly 50% — Angel Oak (AOMR) reported a GAAP net loss on unrealized valuation changes while EFC earned $0.78 GAAP and grew book 3.0%. Two caveats: EFC's quarter contained ~$0.31 of non-recurring items, and EFC is far more diversified than a pure non-QM vehicle, so the comparison partly reflects business-mix breadth. Still, the disclosed rate sensitivity (−$3.8M or −0.20% of equity at −50bp; −$12.4M or −0.63% at +100bp) supports the view that hedging discipline is durable.

Trajectory is mixed. Reverse is improving — falling haircuts on reverse collateral (15.4% → 8.4%) evidence rising lender comfort, and proprietary product is where both industry growth and fatter coupons sit. Non-QM is getting more crowded, with two well-capitalized entrants targeting share. Competitive position scores 6.0: differentiated and improving in reverse, facing intensifying competition in the collateral class that supplies the Investment Portfolio segment.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit is deteriorating modestly, and reported ratios understate it.** Residential 90+ day non-accruals rose to $276.6M UPB, or 7.40% of residential UPB, from 6.48% — worse on both an absolute and ratio basis. Commercial 90+ non-accruals rose in absolute terms ($56.5M across 4 loans → $64.8M across 7) while the ratio "improved" from 15.24% to 9.99% purely because the commercial book grew 75%. REO went from 117 properties/$46.7M to 177/$75.5M at YE2025 and to 199/$101.2M by Q1-2026, with $99.4M transferred in from loans during FY2025 and realized disposition losses growing each year ($5.2M → $5.5M → $7.7M). FY2025 total REO drag was roughly $16.9M, about 11% of net income. In Q1-2026 three commercial loans entered foreclosure versus zero at YE2025 while headline non-performing counts fell — consistent with migration into REO rather than resolution, though faster foreclosure throughput can also reflect better servicing.

**Commercial concentration sits in the two hardest US markets.** At YE2025 commercial collateral was 24.3% Florida and 19.9% New York; including allocable JV share at Q1-2026, New York 26.6% and Florida 22.9%, with multifamily ~61% and hotel ~12% of property type. Weighted average commercial loan life is 1.18 years, so refinancing risk crystallizes quickly, with New York facing rent-regulation-driven valuation pressure and Florida facing insurance-cost and condo-reserve pressures.

**Rate risk is disclosed as a parallel shift.** The sensitivity table shows near-neutrality to parallel moves (−$3.8M or −0.20% of equity at −50bp; −$12.4M or −0.63% at +100bp). It does not surface curve steepening. Separately, EFC's fixed-payer swap book pays 3.42% weighted average and receives 3.87% floating, generating $36.955M of realized periodic settlement gains in FY2025 (~$0.37/share). That carry persists only while short rates exceed ~3.42%. Any aggressive easing that threatened it would simultaneously lower repo costs across $2.66B of repo and lift asset values, so the net effect is not one-directional.

**Longbridge structural obligations.** Unfunded commitments to fund future reverse draws were $2.3B at YE2025 against $201.9M of cash and $136.3M restricted, funded through HMBS tail securitizations — a dependency on continuous Ginnie Mae market access, not an imminent gap. Mandatory MCA repurchases out of HMBS pools ran $159.8M UPB in FY2025 and $55.5M in Q1-2026 alone versus $31.6M a year earlier, with timing largely outside company control. Two leading indicators are deteriorating quickly: inactive reverse loans rose 34% to $520.1M UPB and unpoolable HECM loans rose 104% to $59.1M UPB. Unpoolable loans consume balance-sheet capacity and cash until HUD claims resolve. Emerging fee-transparency scrutiny of proprietary reverse origination fees is externally reported commentary rather than a proposed rule, but it targets EFC's fastest-growing profit pool.

**Related-party architecture is the risk with no peer analogue.** Joint Entity repo facilities held $872.6M of aggregate debt at YE2025, of which EFC's segregated silo was $383.2M (roughly $1,005M/$441.0M by Q1-2026), leaving approximately $489M of affiliate debt sharing collateral pools. The filing states that on default "all of the assets of each respective Joint Entity, including those beneficially owned by any non-defaulting owners… could be used to satisfy the outstanding obligations." No party has defaulted, silos are contractually segregated, and shared facilities plausibly deliver better pricing than EFC could obtain standalone. Separately, $2.1B of residential loans and REO sit in a trust owned by an Ellington related party and consolidated by EFC as primary beneficiary; guarantees of $15.0M and $15.5M are outstanding as caps with nothing drawn. LendSure generated $33.0M of FY2025 gains — 21.9% of net income — while functioning simultaneously as investee, loan-supply counterparty, borrower under a $5.0M line at 15%, and guaranteed party.

**Unresolved items.** A signed agreement to acquire a residential mortgage loan servicer is disclosed pending regulatory approval, with target, consideration, and timing undisclosed. The $17.0M settlement counterparty is undisclosed. A May 29, 2026 8-K carrying Items 5.02 and 5.07 is consistent with routine annual-meeting director activity but unconfirmed. Covenant thresholds on senior notes, warehouse lines, and the consumer-ABS facility are not disclosed, and several facilities with 2026 maturities have unconfirmed renewal status. A $62.6M valuation allowance fully reserves TRS deferred tax assets against $321.1M of NOLs — either a future tailwind on release or an indication management does not project sustained TRS taxable income.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 32.5% against 17.8% one-year realized is rich (14.7-point spread, 74th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation with the six-month tenor at 24.6%. That is standard positioning into the August 6 earnings release rather than a directional signal: put/call skew is balanced at −0.3%, put/call open-interest ratio is 0.36 (call-heavy), and put/call volume is effectively nil at 0.02. The reported implied move figures are internally inconsistent (±7.7% for the nearest expiry versus 19.0% in the one-month row), and the chain is thin at 20 contracts across five expirations, so inference quality is low. Short interest of 6.2% of float with 4.2 days to cover is unremarkable. Net read: the options market is pricing an event, not a direction, and it does not contradict the fundamental view. For an existing holder, rich near-dated volatility makes covered-call overwriting into the print a reasonable way to monetize the event premium.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $15.50 (+16.3% price, +28.0% total return)

Longbridge's Q1 result proves through, with proprietary reverse origination compounding as the product takes share from HECM and carrying a 9.63% coupon against 5.88% on HMBS collateral. Reverse-collateral haircuts at 8.4% and total cost of funds at 4.70% expand return on equity without adding recourse leverage. Economic return sustains 12–14%, exceeding the 11.5% dividend-on-book, so book compounds to roughly $14.35 within twelve months. Incentive fees, while large, are self-limiting — 25% of adjusted net income above a hurdle of the greater of 9% or 3% plus the ten-year Treasury — so a large fee mechanically implies strong pre-fee returns for shareholders. The pending servicer acquisition adds fee-based, less rate-sensitive income. Non-QM origination volume rising toward $175B feeds a securitization platform that closed fifteen deals in 2025. The multiple re-rates to 1.08x book on demonstrated coverage. This case aligns with the $15.00 analyst median and $16.00 high.

### Bear Case — $10.75 (−19.4%)

The long end continues to steepen, marking down fixed-rate credit assets in a way the parallel-shift sensitivity table does not capture. Residential 90+ non-accruals extend beyond 7.40% as the H2 consumer cliff materializes; New York and Florida multifamily/hotel collateral, roughly half the commercial book with 1.18-year weighted average life, requires refinancing into that environment, accelerating REO migration beyond the current 199-property, $101.2M balance. The $21.2M own-credit gain reverses if spreads tighten, or is followed by further widening that raises the cost of refinancing $247.8M of unsecured paper. Incentive fees persist near Q1 levels while economic return decelerates from 6.0% to 3.2% and lower — the specific danger signal, because it means fees are being earned on marks that do not persist. Coverage falls below 1.0x, the monthly dividend is trimmed, and the multiple compresses to 0.85x a $12.65 book. A remark of originator equity from 2.2x to 1.0x book removes another $0.35/share.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income)**

**Base case target: $13.80 (12 months).** Math: Q2-2026 annualized economic return of roughly 13% against a dividend equal to 11.5% of book supports book growth of approximately $0.20/year, taking the $13.61 June 30 estimate to about $13.80; applying the current 1.0x multiple yields $13.80. That is +3.5% in price and +15.2% total return including $1.56 of distributions.

**Bull case target: $15.50** — 1.08x a $14.35 book on 12–14% sustained economic return (+16.3% price).

**Bear case target: $10.75** — 0.85x a $12.65 book after credit and mark deterioration (−19.4%).

**Reward-to-risk from $13.33:** $2.17 upside versus $2.58 downside, roughly 0.84:1 on price. Including the dividend the total-return asymmetry improves to approximately 1.4:1, which supports holding for income but not adding at book.

**Reassessment date: August 6, 2026** — Q2 results, which will disclose Q2 ADE, the Q2 incentive fee, credit metrics, and audited book.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. ADE *net of the incentive fee* of $0.47/share or better (≥1.20x coverage) for two consecutive quarters with reported book value at or above $13.75.
2. Price at or below approximately 0.88x reported book (roughly $12.00) with residential 90+ non-accruals stable or improving — this restores a margin of safety against a 96% Level 3 balance sheet.
3. Residential 90+ non-accruals falling below 6.5% of UPB with sequential declines in REO property count, confirming resolution rather than migration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Book value declines in two consecutive quarters, or economic return falls below the dividend for two consecutive quarters while the incentive fee remains above $10M per quarter.
2. Monthly dividend cut below $0.13, or return of capital exceeding roughly 45% of distributions in the FY2026 tax characterization.
3. Any default or margin event inside the Joint Entity repo facilities, or a material markdown of the $79.6M originator equity book (LendSure in particular, given it supplied 21.9% of FY2025 net income).
4. Failure to renew the 2026-maturing warehouse, HECM tail, or consumer-ABS facilities, or a sustained impairment of HMBS issuance access (monthly HMBS issuance was reported at $463M in July, described as weak).

## Conclusion

The score trajectory — 6.1 in May, 6.3 in June, 5.5 in early August — reflects a discount that closed rather than a business that broke, and the current 5.35 continues that path for the same reason. Fundamentals have not deteriorated: net interest income grew 39.3%, funding costs and haircuts improved materially, Longbridge inflected, four consecutive quarters beat consensus, and book value recovered $0.45 from its YE2025 trough. What changed is the price relative to book and the emergence of a fee structure that now captures a meaningful share of incremental returns. At 0.98x a Level 3 book value, the buyer of EFC today receives the 11.70% distribution and very little else, and receives it with roughly 1.0x all-in coverage in the most recently reported quarter.

The governance findings deserve weight without overstatement. The hurdle-basis change from gross proceeds per share to GAAP common equity lowered the annual hurdle by approximately $0.34/share, worth roughly $9–11M/year of additional fee capacity at a 25% participation rate; the magnitude is approximate because the contractual calculation uses FIFO attribution and a retained-earnings term not fully reproducible from disclosure. The change was disclosed, a GAAP-equity base is arguably the more standard convention, and EFC's returns genuinely improved, so no motive should be inferred. But shareholders should model incentive fees off the new basis, and they should stop treating the co-CIO's Form 4 code-J acquisitions as insider buying — those 33,877 and 161,934 share increases reconcile to incentive-fee stock paid to the manager, which is a cost to shareholders. Genuine open-market insider purchases over the reviewed period total one transaction: a director's 5,544 shares at $13.56, about $75,000. Alongside zero repurchases in FY2025 against a $45.1M authorization, while issuing equity above book, that is consistent with a manager optimizing scale rather than per-share value.

Hold for the distribution. The August 6 print is the falsifiable test: if the incentive fee stays near Q1's $19.2M on a Q2 economic return that decelerated to 3.2%, the yield thesis weakens materially and the downgrade triggers above come into play. If ADE net of fee clears $0.47 with book above $13.75, the case for adding improves. New capital should wait for one of those two resolutions, or for a price near $12.00.
