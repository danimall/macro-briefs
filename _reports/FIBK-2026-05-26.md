## Executive Summary

First Interstate BancSystem is a $26.4B-asset regional bank operating across 12 western and midwestern states, currently executing a strategic transformation that prioritizes profitability over asset growth. The company has shed approximately $5B in earning assets over two years through branch divestitures, product exits, and intentional runoff of non-relationship credits. This deliberate shrinkage has produced meaningful profitability improvements: net interest margin expanded from 3.02% in 2024 to 3.41% in Q1 2026, the efficiency ratio improved to 59.2%, and EPS grew 34% in FY2025 (though partially boosted by a $62.7M branch sale gain).

The stock trades at $35.78, or approximately 12.2x trailing earnings ($2.94 EPS), 1.62x tangible book value, and offers a 5.3% dividend yield with ~6% annualized share count reduction from buybacks. This total capital return profile of ~10%+ is compelling for income-oriented investors. However, the bull case faces meaningful headwinds: the loan book continues to shrink with no clear turn toward organic growth, CRE concentration at 53.6% of loans creates vulnerability in a rising-rate environment, agricultural credit stress is emerging, and 17.2% short interest suggests institutional skepticism about the sustainability of profitability improvements without top-line growth.

The KBW downgrade to Market Perform in May 2026 and consensus analyst targets ($33-$41, median $36.50) suggest limited upside from current levels. FIBK is best characterized as an income-generating hold rather than a capital appreciation opportunity.

## Company Overview

First Interstate BancSystem operates ~289 banking offices across Colorado, Idaho, Iowa, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, Oregon, South Dakota, Washington, and Wyoming. The bank holds dominant market positions in Montana (#2, 16.6% share) and Wyoming (#1, 13.3% share), providing competitive insulation in these lower-competition markets.

The business model is traditional community banking: lending, deposit-gathering, wealth management ($8.7B AUM), and payment services. Revenue is heavily interest-income dependent. The company underwent a significant strategic pivot under CEO James Reuter beginning in 2025, including:

- Sale of 12 Arizona/Kansas branches (October 2025, $641.6M deposits)
- Pending sale of 11 Nebraska branches (Q2 2026, $303.5M deposits)
- Discontinuation of indirect lending and outsourcing of consumer credit cards
- Organizational redesign from regional layers to a flatter State President model
- $300M share repurchase authorization (partially deployed)

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability Trajectory

The NIM expansion story is the core of the investment thesis. The progression from 3.02% (2024) → 3.30% (2025) → 3.41% (Q1 2026) reflects two drivers: (1) elimination of $2.6B in average FHLB borrowings (from $2.6B in 2023 to zero by Q4 2025), and (2) declining deposit costs as rate cuts flow through. Interest expense dropped from $480.9M (2024) to $352.6M (2025).

However, net interest income grew only 0.5% in FY2025 ($825.4M vs. $821.6M) because the earning asset base shrank faster than margins expanded. This is the fundamental tension: margin improvement is genuine, but the denominator is contracting.

### Capital Position and Returns

CET1 at 14.30% is exceptionally well-capitalized, providing ample capacity for continued buybacks and dividends. Operating cash flow of $305.6M and free cash flow of $277.8M comfortably cover the $188M annual dividend burden (payout ratio 63.7%). The bank carries zero FHLB borrowings and zero brokered deposits — a strong liquidity position.

Share repurchases have been aggressive: ~6.04M shares retired since August 2025 ($201.6M deployed), with $159.4M remaining through March 2027. At current prices, this represents roughly another 4.5M shares, which would reduce the count to ~92.5M from the current ~97.1M.

### Credit Quality: Lumpy Improvement

Net charge-offs improved dramatically: 0.57% annualized (2024) → 0.24% (2025) → 0.06% (Q1 2026). However, non-performing assets rose $24.2M in Q1 2026 to $162.5M (0.61% of assets), driven by a single $20.2M relationship. Criticized loans remain elevated at $1.03B (6.8% of total loans). Agricultural non-performing loans jumped from $5.3M to $27.1M in real estate and from $20.9M to $24.6M in agricultural production.

The $443.2M in unrealized losses on investment securities (AFS + HTM) is manageable given the bank's intention to hold to maturity and its strong liquidity position, but represents a latent capital constraint.

### Q1 2026 Results

EPS of $0.61 beat consensus by 3.6%, but revenue missed by 2.7%, reflecting the ongoing balance sheet shrinkage. Management cited a "strong commercial pipeline" but acknowledged "weaker than anticipated loan production." Loans held for investment stood at $14.7B, down from approximately $16.7B a year earlier.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are constrained in the near term. Consensus estimates project FY2026 EPS of $2.62 and FY2027 EPS of $2.95, implying 12-13% growth. The drivers are:

1. **Continued NIM expansion** (perhaps 5-10bp remaining upside)
2. **Share count reduction** (~6% per annum at current pace)
3. **Efficiency gains** from organizational redesign

Against this, loan balances continue declining. Construction loans fell 39% YoY. Management has not provided a timeline for when organic loan growth resumes. The Nebraska branch sale (Q2 2026) will further reduce assets.

Estimate revisions are modestly negative: current year estimates down 0.5% over 30 days, next year up 0.3%. There is no earnings momentum signal. The 90-day revision for the current quarter (+2.0%) is modestly positive but insufficient to indicate a turn.

## Valuation Assessment

At $35.78, FIBK trades at:
- 12.2x trailing EPS ($2.94, which includes branch sale gains)
- ~13.7x forward FY2026 EPS ($2.62 consensus)
- ~12.1x FY2027 EPS ($2.95 consensus)
- 1.62x tangible book value ($22.06)
- 1.05x book value ($33.99)
- 5.3% dividend yield

Versus peer medians (P/E 13.0x, dividend yield 3.3%), FIBK's forward P/E is slightly above the peer group while its dividend yield is meaningfully higher. The premium is modest and arguably justified by the NIM expansion trajectory and aggressive capital return program.

The consensus analyst target range is $33-$41, with a median of $36.50 — essentially at current prices. The stock is 9% below its 52-week high of $39.26.

The valuation is neither compelling nor stretched. At 13.7x forward earnings with flat estimate revisions, the market is pricing in the current profitability run-rate without significant growth premium or discount.

## Competitive Landscape

FIBK's competitive advantages are primarily geographic: dominant positions in Montana and Wyoming where competition is structurally limited. These markets have low population density and limited appeal for large national banks or fintech entrants. The 23.9% non-interest-bearing deposit share (though declining) and fully core-funded balance sheet provide cost-of-funds advantages.

Competitive position is stable rather than improving. The company explicitly acknowledges being "not willing to assume the same level or type of risk as certain more aggressive competitors" in commercial lending, which protects credit quality but constrains market share gains. The potential Regulation II interchange fee reduction (from 21 cents to 14.4 cents) would pressure payment services revenues.

The strategic transformation — exiting non-core markets and products — is a defensive refocusing on markets where FIBK has structural advantages.

## Risk Assessment

### CRE Concentration (High)
At 53.6% of loans in commercial real estate (77.1% including all real estate), FIBK is highly exposed to any CRE downturn. A single $15.8M CRE charge-off occurred in Q4 2025. Multi-family lease-up has been "slower than anticipated." In a prolonged high-rate environment where CRE refinancing stress intensifies, this portfolio could produce elevated losses.

### Loan Growth Stagnation (Medium-High)
The current trajectory — loans declining ~12% annually — cannot continue indefinitely without impairing the franchise. If the commercial pipeline does not convert to actual originations, EPS growth will stall once the NIM expansion tailwind exhausts and buyback capacity depletes.

### Agricultural Stress (Medium)
Agricultural NPLs jumped 5x YoY. With commodity prices pressured and tariff uncertainty affecting export markets, this segment could produce additional credit deterioration in FIBK's core Rocky Mountain markets.

### Short Interest (Elevated)
At 17.2% of float with 8.6 days to cover, institutional bearish positioning is significant. This creates both overhang (sellers waiting) and potential squeeze dynamics if a catalyst emerges.

### Scott Family Overhang (Low-Medium)
The founding family's large holdings and periodic sales (Jonathan Scott's $1.9M sale on April 30) create regular supply pressure. This is a structural feature rather than a fundamental concern.

### Options Market Signal
ATM implied volatility at 30.6% is roughly in line with historical volatility (28.8%), suggesting the market is not pricing unusual uncertainty. Put/call skew of 3.0% indicates mildly elevated put demand. The flat term structure (30.6% near-term vs. 32.3% far-term) shows no specific event being priced. The low OI put/call ratio (0.25) reflects limited options activity overall — this is a thinly-traded options name where signals carry less weight.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($40-$41, +12-15% upside)
- NIM expansion continues toward 3.50%+ as fixed-rate assets reprice and deposit costs decline further
- Organic loan growth resumes in H2 2026 as the pipeline converts
- Share count reduction to ~92M by early 2027 boosts EPS toward $3.00+
- Short squeeze potential if credit quality sustains and loan growth turns positive
- Attractive total return: 5.3% yield + 6% buyback + 5-10% EPS growth = mid-teens annualized

### Bear Case ($28-$30, -16-22% downside)
- CRE credit cycle worsens: criticized loans ($1.03B) produce elevated charge-offs through 2026-2027
- Loan growth fails to materialize; NIM expansion peaks and reverses if funding costs rise again
- Agricultural portfolio deterioration accelerates with commodity/tariff stress
- Goodwill impairment risk if stock trades persistently below book value
- The "shrink to grow" strategy reaches its mathematical limit without revenue growth

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net loan growth turns positive for two consecutive quarters
- Stock pulls back to $30-31 (1.36-1.40x TBV) while NIM holds above 3.35%
- CRE criticized loans decline below $800M

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net charge-offs exceed 0.40% annualized for two consecutive quarters
- NIM declines below 3.20% (signaling deposit cost pressure winning)
- Dividend cut or suspension of buyback program
- CRE non-performing assets exceed 2.0% of CRE loans

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026) — this will reveal whether the Nebraska branch sale is accretive and whether the commercial pipeline is converting to actual originations.

## Conclusion

FIBK is a well-managed regional bank executing a credible profitability improvement strategy, generating attractive income through a 5.3% dividend yield and aggressive buybacks. The NIM expansion is real and has room to continue modestly. Capital levels are strong, liquidity is robust, and the geographic franchise in Montana and Wyoming provides competitive durability.

However, the stock at ~13.7x forward earnings with flat estimate revisions, a shrinking asset base, elevated CRE concentration, and 17.2% short interest does not offer sufficient asymmetry for a BUY recommendation. The transformation story is priced in: the stock has already appreciated 34% from its 52-week low. Further upside requires loan growth to resume — something management has guided toward but not yet demonstrated.

For existing holders, the ~10% annualized total capital return (yield + buyback) provides adequate compensation for holding while the strategic transformation plays out. For new money, better entry points would emerge at $30-31 or upon demonstration of organic loan growth.

**Recommendation: HOLD (Income)**
