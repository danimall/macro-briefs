**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ABM Industries is an ~$8.7B-revenue integrated facility-services company (janitorial, engineering, parking, aviation, and technical/mission-critical infrastructure) trading at ~$39.35, down roughly 25% over the trailing twelve months and within a few percent of its 52-week low of $36.96. At ~10x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted-EPS guidance ($3.85–$4.15), the multiple compresses the market's concerns about three real headwinds: structurally low and eroding gross margin (13.1% in FY2023 to 11.6% in Q1 FY2026), elevated office vacancy pressuring the largest segment, and a 2026 squeeze from interest-rate-swap roll-off and lapsed federal hiring tax credits.

The central analytical task is to judge whether the bear case is already in the price, not to debate whether ABM is a high-quality compounder. ABM is a thin-margin, labor-intensive, acquisition-financed services business with ~54% of assets in goodwill and intangibles. On balance, the bear case is largely priced in. The stock has derated to a trough multiple, consensus estimates are stable rather than collapsing (current-year down 0.8% and next-year down 0.2% over 90 days), the entire analyst panel sits above spot (low target $45), and the most important operational development of the past two quarters is positive: operating cash flow swung +$168M year-over-year as the ERP-driven working-capital crunch unwound. Management is buying back stock aggressively ($91.1M in Q1 FY2026 at ~$44) and has paid a dividend for 23+ consecutive years.

The offsetting reality is that ABM has missed EPS in four straight quarters, gross margin direction is still negative, Technical Solutions margin collapsed 452 bps in Q1, and full-year guidance now depends heavily on a second-half ATS recovery that management attributes to weather and timing. The result is a roughly symmetric risk/reward: a credible path to $50+ if mission-critical mix-shift and restructuring savings prove out, against $30–33 if margin erosion continues and H2 execution disappoints. That symmetry, combined with a depressed multiple and a cash-generative balance sheet, defines a HOLD. There is neither asymmetric downside that would justify shorting nor a confirmed catalyst that would justify a high-conviction long.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1909, ABM provides facility services across five reportable segments to 20,000+ clients, with ~92% of revenue U.S.-based and the remainder in the UK and Ireland. It employs ~113,000 people, 45% of whom are union-represented under 300+ collective bargaining agreements.

Segment structure (FY2025 revenue / operating margin):
- **Business & Industry (B&I):** $4,126M / 7.7% — the largest segment, most exposed to hybrid-work and office-vacancy risk.
- **Manufacturing & Distribution (M&D):** $1,619M / 9.4% — highest-margin core segment, includes data-center facilities; one client ≈ 32% of segment revenue.
- **Aviation:** $1,119M / 5.8% — lowest margin; two clients ≈ 27% of segment revenue.
- **Education:** $922M / 7.3%.
- **Technical Solutions (ATS):** $961M / 9.0% — the growth engine; microgrids, UPS, EV charging, and mission-critical/data-center infrastructure anchored by the 2022 RavenVolt acquisition; one client ≈ 30% of segment revenue.

No single client exceeds 10% of consolidated revenue, but segment-level concentration is meaningful. ABM is running two overlapping transformation programs: ELEVATE (a multi-year ERP modernization started in 2021) and a Q4 FY2025 restructuring program targeting ~$35M of annualized savings.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is solid and accelerating.** FY2025 revenue reached $8,745.9M, up 4.6% (organic +3.8%, acquisitions +0.8%). Q1 FY2026 revenue grew 6.1% to $2,243.5M, with organic growth of 5.5% — the strongest organic pace since Q4 2022, and all five segments grew. This argues against any "structural revenue decline" framing.

**Profitability is the key tension.** FY2025 net income of $162.4M (+99.6%) is largely optical: FY2024 carried a non-recurring $95.7M RavenVolt contingent-consideration fair-value charge. Normalizing, underlying earnings were roughly flat-to-modestly-up, and FY2023 net income was actually higher at $251.3M. This argues against paying up for the "doubled earnings" headline, not against owning a cash-generative business at a trough multiple.

Gross margin has eroded every year: 13.1% (FY23) → 12.4% (FY24) → 12.3% (FY25) → 11.6% in Q1 FY2026 (down 69 bps YoY), driven by strategic pricing on B&I rebids, M&D mix, and weather. Net margin is structurally low (~1.9%), typical of a business where direct labor is 68% of revenue. In Q1 FY2026, Education shone (+54% operating profit, partly aided by weather-related school closures), but ATS operating margin collapsed from 8.2% to 3.7% (-452 bps) on adverse project mix and weather delays, and M&D margin fell 139 bps to 8.6%. Q1 net income declined to $38.8M ($0.64 diluted) from $43.6M ($0.69); adjusted EPS was $0.83.

**The cash-flow inflection is the most under-appreciated positive.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $234.4M and FCF $155.1M. Q1 FY2026 OCF swung to +$62.0M from -$106.2M a year earlier — a $168M improvement as the ERP-related invoicing/collections disruption from H1 FY2025 unwound. This is a transient working-capital drag reversing, not structural decline. It supports continued buybacks and deleveraging and undercuts any "fundamentals are deteriorating" thesis.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets of $5.27B include $2.59B goodwill + $0.24B intangibles (~54% of assets), reflecting an acquisition-built model. Stockholders' equity is $1.79B; debt/equity ~1.95. The Feb 2026 WGNSTAR acquisition added a $255M incremental term loan, pushing total debt to ~$1.7B. The Altman Z-Score of 2.78 ("grey zone") reflects goodwill-heavy assets and leverage, not operational distress — covenants (max net leverage 5.0x, flexing to 5.5x post-acquisition) are comfortably met, and the weighted-average borrowing rate is 5.44%. A genuine 2026 headwind: all interest-rate swaps (~$620M notional fixed at low-1-to-3% rates) mature in June 2026, rolling off hedge protection into a higher-rate environment.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $0.29/quarter (FY2025 declared $1.06/share). Buybacks were aggressive: $121.3M in FY2025 and $91.1M in Q1 FY2026 alone (2.07M shares at avg $44.13), with $92.0M remaining authorized. Share count fell from 62.2M to 58.6M YoY. Management bought back stock while levering up for WGNSTAR, then pivoted to prioritize deleveraging — a sign of conviction the stock is cheap, though the FY24/FY25 buyback prices (~$47.86, ~$47.35) are now underwater.

## Growth Analysis

ABM's growth profile is bifurcated. The legacy B&I core (offices, parking) faces a structural headwind from elevated office vacancy and hybrid work, which compresses both base demand and high-margin supplemental work-order revenue. Management is managing this defensively via strategic pricing on rebids — accepting lower margin to retain volume — and has flagged the anticipated exit of a large London/UK contract that will pressure B&I organic growth in H2 FY2026.

The growth engine is Technical Solutions and mission-critical infrastructure: data-center power/UPS/microgrid services, semiconductor-fab support, and advanced manufacturing. ATS grew 18.7% in FY2025 and 13.6% in Q1 FY2026. ABM has deliberately pivoted capital here (RavenVolt 2022, Quality Uptime 2024, WGNSTAR 2026), positioning it as a partial AI/data-center-infrastructure beneficiary — a theme with broad, multi-company demand confirmation. The caveats: ATS is ~11% of revenue, its margin is project/mix/weather-sensitive, and roughly two-thirds of its operating profit is back-half-weighted, making FY2026 guidance heavily dependent on H2 execution.

FY2026 guidance is organic growth 3–4% (total 4–5% with M&A) and adjusted EPS $3.85–$4.15. Management maintained full-year guidance despite the weak Q1, attributing ATS softness to weather and timing. Consensus estimate revisions are stable rather than falling: current-year EPS consensus is $3.96 and next-year $4.34 — a "no significant directional shift" signature, not the rapidly-falling-estimate profile of a deteriorating business. This stability is a meaningful counterweight to the four-quarter miss streak, which itself ranged from a narrow -1.1% to a more concerning -19.8%.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$39.35 against FY2026 adjusted-EPS guidance of $3.85–$4.15, ABM trades at roughly 9.5–10.2x forward adjusted earnings. Reported trailing P/E is ~15.5x on $2.59 GAAP EPS. The peer set in Environmental & Facilities Services is heterogeneous and largely uninformative for direct comparison — GEO at 11.4x, HCSG at 21.8x, CLH at 38.0x, and CWST at a distorted 748.8x produce a peer median of 38.0x that does not map cleanly onto a thin-margin, labor-intensive integrator like ABM. ABM has historically traded in the low-to-mid teens on forward earnings, so the current ~10x sits at the low end of its own range.

The valuation question is whether ~10x reflects a value trap or a trough-multiple cash generator. Three points favor the latter: (1) revenue is growing organically, not declining; (2) the OCF inflection is real and reversing a transient drag; (3) estimates are stable. The offsetting concern is that the multiple is being paid on adjusted EPS that has consistently come in below estimates, and gross-margin erosion is genuine. The market is right to apply a discount; the question is whether the discount is excessive. With the entire analyst panel above spot (low $45, median $50, mean $51.4, high $68), consensus implies the discount is overdone, and on a name with stable rather than collapsing estimates, that panel cannot simply be dismissed as stale.

A defensible base case is a modest re-rating toward 11–11.5x on ~$4.00 adjusted EPS, or roughly $44–46, contingent on H2 ATS recovery and gross-margin stabilization. The bull case ($50+) requires restructuring savings ($35M annualized), mission-critical mix shift, and margin recovery to compound. The bear case ($30–33) assumes continued gross-margin erosion, a failed H2 ATS recovery, and the full bite of the interest/tax-credit squeeze. The payoff is roughly symmetric around the current price, consistent with a fairly-valued name rather than an asymmetric opportunity in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

The facility-services industry is fragmented, price-competitive, and low-barrier. ABM competes against regional and local operators with lower cost structures and against in-house self-performance. Its differentiation is scale, national/multinational footprint, integrated multi-service bundling, and vertical specialization. The competitive trajectory is mixed: the legacy B&I core is in defensive share-retention mode (visible in flat B&I operating profit and the strategic-pricing margin give-up), while ATS is gaining position in a structurally growing mission-critical/data-center niche.

Relative performance has been weak and is acknowledged by the company's own compensation system: 5-year TSR of 136.4 versus a peer group at 191.3, and CEO comp-actually-paid fell to $6.24M from $11.6M, reflecting that underperformance. The competitive position is best characterized as stable-to-slightly-improving in the high-margin growth segments and stable-to-eroding in the legacy core — a net wash that does not yet justify either a moat upgrade or a structural-decline downgrade.

## Risk Assessment

1. **ERP/systems execution.** The new ERP go-live disrupted invoicing and drove the H1 FY2025 working-capital spike, flagged as a material change to internal control over financial reporting. Remaining segments migrate over several years, posing continued execution risk to controls, collections, and costs, though the Q1 cash-flow recovery suggests the worst has passed.
2. **Office/hybrid-work exposure.** B&I depends on commercial-office occupancy; the anticipated large London/UK contract exit will pressure H2 organic growth.
3. **ATS margin volatility.** The growth and valuation story is project/mix/weather-driven, with two-thirds of operating profit back-half-weighted.
4. **RavenVolt earn-out.** Up to $205M cash contingent consideration possible in 2026; ABM expects to pay $32.5M in May 2026. The FY2024 $95.7M fair-value swing shows the earnings disruption potential.
5. **Leverage + WGNSTAR integration + hedge roll-off.** ~$264M debt-financed acquisition raised leverage just as ~$620M of swaps mature in June 2026, a real interest-expense headwind.
6. **Tax-credit lapse.** WOTC/Federal Empowerment Zone credits expired Dec 31, 2025 and have not been renewed, a tax-rate headwind.
7. **Self-insurance reserves.** $660M in claim liabilities; a 10% change in projected ultimate losses moves net income ~$40M; FY2025 had $23.3M adverse development.
8. **Segment-level client concentration.** Single clients represent ~32% of M&D, ~30% of ATS, and two clients ~27% of Aviation.

Most of these risks are disclosed, quantified, and at least partly reflected in the depressed multiple. The combination of the interest/tax-credit squeeze and the H2-dependent guidance is what keeps the risk score balanced rather than high.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 47.3% versus 28.7% historical volatility (rich by 18.6 points), with the term structure in backwardation (47.3% near-term vs 41.9% longer-dated). Put/call skew is elevated at +13.6% (heavy near-term put demand), and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.49 to 0.79 over the prior week as the stock drifted -1.3%. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.0%. This signals the market expects an imminent catalyst — likely the next earnings print and clarity on H2 ATS recovery — with downside skew. The signal is consistent with the symmetric-to-cautious framing and argues against a high-conviction long entry ahead of the event; it does not, by itself, indicate terminal impairment.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50+, +27% to +40%):** The cash-flow inflection proves durable, the $35M restructuring savings flow through, ATS margin recovers in H2 as weather/timing reverse, and the mission-critical/data-center mix shift (RavenVolt, Quality Uptime, WGNSTAR) lifts blended margin and growth. Gross margin stabilizes, deleveraging proceeds, and the stock re-rates from ~10x toward 12–13x forward adjusted EPS as the AI/data-center exposure is recognized. The analyst panel (mean $51.4) becomes the realistic target.

**Bear Case ($30–33, -16% to -24%):** Gross-margin erosion continues as B&I rebids price down further, the London/UK exit weighs on organic growth, ATS H2 recovery disappoints, and the interest-swap roll-off plus WOTC lapse compress EPS below the guidance midpoint. A fifth consecutive miss in a depressed-sentiment tape pushes the multiple toward 8x on ~$3.85 EPS. Self-insurance or earn-out surprises add downside.

The base case sits between these at $44–46 (~11x on ~$4.00 adjusted EPS), a modest re-rating contingent on H2 execution. The payoff distribution is roughly symmetric, which is why this is a HOLD and not a directional bet.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the H2 ATS recovery is the pivotal data point), with the June 2026 swap roll-off and the May 2026 RavenVolt earn-out payment as interim watch items.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or expands for two consecutive quarters (arresting the 13.1% → 11.6% slide), confirming strategic-pricing dilution has bottomed.
- ATS operating margin recovers back above ~7–8% in H2, validating the weather/timing attribution rather than structural deterioration.
- Sustained free-cash-flow generation funds visible deleveraging (net leverage trending down) alongside continued opportunistic buybacks, with consensus EPS revisions turning positive.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fifth consecutive EPS miss accompanied by a guidance cut and falling consensus estimates.
- Gross margin breaks below ~11.0% with no offsetting mix benefit, signaling pricing power has collapsed.
- Leverage breaches comfortable covenant headroom, or a material self-insurance/earn-out charge impairs the balance sheet.

## Conclusion

ABM is a low-margin, acquisition-built facility-services compounder that the market has marked down to a trough ~10x forward multiple near its 52-week low. The bearish narrative — office exposure, gross-margin erosion, an ATS margin air-pocket, and a 2026 interest/tax-credit squeeze — is real, but it is already substantially reflected in the price. Against it sit a genuine and under-appreciated cash-flow inflection (+$168M YoY), stable rather than collapsing estimates, organic revenue growth, an active $91M buyback, a 23-year dividend, and an analyst panel sitting above spot. There is no evidence of terminal or structural impairment: the Altman grey-zone reading reflects goodwill weighting, covenants are comfortably met, and the business generates cash.

The result is balanced risk/reward — a credible path to $50+ against a $30–33 downside, with an options market pricing near-term event risk. That symmetry, combined with the lack of a confirmed catalyst to underwrite a high-conviction long and the absence of asymmetric downside to justify a short, places ABM in the HOLD category. The H2 ATS recovery and gross-margin trajectory are the swing variables, and they will not be resolved until the next one or two prints. Investors should hold existing positions, accumulate only on weakness toward the low-$30s where the bear case is fully discounted, and wait for confirmation of margin stabilization before treating ABM as a long.
