**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Aon is the world's second-largest insurance broker, an asset-light professional-services compounder that converts a high share of earnings into free cash flow and has historically returned nearly all of it through buybacks. The operating engine remains intact: Q1 2026 delivered 5% organic revenue growth, GAAP operating margin expansion to 34.1% from 30.9%, and adjusted diluted EPS of $6.48, up 14% year over year. The company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters.

The stock trades at ~$326.69, down roughly 14% from its July 2025 high of $381 and near the lower half of its 52-week range, at about 17x forward earnings. That is a discount to the insurance-broker peer median (P/E ~20.7) and to Aon's own multiple history. Three things explain the discount. First, the industry-wide turn in the property & casualty pricing cycle: brokerage commissions move with premium rates, and softening P&C pricing is a sector-wide organic-growth headwind that the market is now pricing into all the major brokers. Second, Aon's buyback runway is nearly exhausted — only ~$0.8B remains on the $27.5B authorization, which removes a structural support for EPS growth until re-authorization. Third, governance: CEO total compensation jumped to $73.7M in FY2025 from $26.2M against a 5-year TSR that lagged the peer group (174 vs. 203), and senior leadership is reshuffling.

The setup is a quality business at a reasonable but not compelling valuation, facing a cyclical organic-growth deceleration and a near-term capital-return air pocket. The estimate-revision picture is flat (current-year consensus $19.12, essentially unchanged over 90 days), which argues against treating this as a story that justifies a premium call. We rate AON a HOLD with a modest positive 12-month bias, looking for the buyback re-authorization and Vesttoo litigation paths to clarify before upgrading.

## Company Overview

Aon operates in 120+ countries with roughly 60,000 colleagues and is incorporated in Ireland, a structural feature with implications for capital distribution (buybacks and dividends require Irish-law "distributable profits," currently ample at ~$29B) and for Pillar Two global-minimum-tax exposure. Since 2024 the firm reports two segments:

- **Risk Capital (~70% of revenue):** Commercial Risk Solutions (insurance and specialty brokerage, risk consulting, captives, Affinity programs) and Reinsurance Solutions. Q1'26 revenue $3.50B, adjusted operating margin ~42.0%.
- **Human Capital (~30%):** Health Solutions and Wealth Solutions (retirement consulting, pension administration, investments). Q1'26 revenue $1.54B, operating margin 28.8%.

The model is recurring, advisory-driven revenue with high retention, no meaningful single-client concentration, and a data-and-analytics layer ("Aon United" / "3x3 Plan" via Aon Business Services). Principal competitors are Marsh McLennan, Willis Towers Watson, Arthur J. Gallagher, Brown & Brown, and Lockton. The April 2024 NFP acquisition (~$13B) extended distribution into the U.S. middle market; Aon subsequently divested NFP's wealth business for $2.3B in October 2025, booking a $1.2B pre-tax gain that flatters FY2025 GAAP earnings.

## Financial Analysis

Reported revenue has risen from $12.48B (2022) to $13.38B (2023), $15.70B (2024), and $17.18B (2025), the 2024–25 step-up driven largely by NFP. Underlying organic growth has run mid-single-digit consistently. Q1 2026 revenue was $5.03B (+6% reported; 5% organic, +4% FX tailwind, −3% net divestitures). Risk Capital grew +10% (Commercial Risk organic +7%); Human Capital was flat, dragged by the NFP Wealth divestiture even as Health organic grew +4%.

Profitability is the bright spot. Adjusted operating margin reached 39.1%. The AAU restructuring program contributed $25M of net savings in the quarter and is targeting ~$450M of annualized savings by end-2027 ($295M realized to date), though the program's cost estimate was raised from $1.0B to $1.3B. FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS was ~$17.07; trailing EPS reported here is $17.02.

Cash generation supports the compounder thesis. Q1'26 operating cash flow was $430M (vs. $140M; Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter) and FCF was $363M. The dividend was raised to $0.82/quarter (declared April 2026), a ~1.0% yield. The capital-return engine is the issue: ~176.4M shares / $26.7B repurchased to date leaves only ~$0.8B on the authorization. Buybacks have been the primary EPS-growth amplifier, and that lever is nearly spent until re-authorization.

The balance sheet screens look alarming and are misleading. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.07 ("distress") and D/E of ~4.4x reflect ~$18.9B of fiduciary assets and liabilities — client premiums in transit — that gross up the balance sheet without representing economic leverage. Stockholders' equity was negative in 2022 (−$529M) and 2023 (−$826M) as a direct result of aggressive buybacks, not operating losses, and has since recovered to $9.83B (Q1'26) with retained earnings turning positive (+$307M). Real leverage is total debt of ~$14.7B against investment-grade ratings (S&P A−/stable, Moody's Baa2/positive, Fitch BBB+/stable), with full covenant compliance. Near-term maturities are being handled with operating cash: the $2B term loan was fully repaid, $750M notes matured December 2025, and $593M notes were redeemed February 2026. The financial-health filter that flags this name as distressed is a screen artifact; the genuine financial-health considerations are the thin buyback runway and the ~$21.5B of goodwill/intangibles (≈43% of assets) from NFP, which carries an unrealized impairment risk.

## Growth Analysis

The durable component of the growth story is mid-single-digit organic revenue compounding plus margin expansion plus capital return. Q1'26's 5% organic print and Commercial Risk's +7% suggest net-new-business and retention are offsetting the early stages of P&C rate softening. The earnings-beat record is consistent (four straight beats, magnitudes of 0.3% to 3.7%), which lends reasonable confidence to current consensus.

The complication is that the tailwind is fading. The defining 2026 industry dynamic is the turn in the P&C pricing cycle, with brokers reporting sharper property-rate declines across the board. Because commissions track premium rates, softening pricing is a sector-wide headwind, not Aon-specific. Estimate revisions reflect this: current-quarter consensus has drifted down 1.7% over 90 days, next-year consensus is off 0.6%, and current-year is flat at $19.12. There is no estimate-revision velocity here that would signal an underappreciated acceleration — the strongest forward indicator is neutral-to-slightly-negative.

Forward EPS power is real but moderating: consensus of $19.12 for the current year and $21.40 for next year implies low-double-digit EPS growth, of which a meaningful slice has historically come from share-count reduction now constrained by the near-exhausted authorization. Strip the buyback amplifier and the organic-plus-margin engine produces roughly mid-single to high-single-digit EPS growth, which is a solid compounder rather than a re-rating catalyst. The growth prospects score reflects a quality engine running into a cyclical headwind with a temporarily reduced capital-return assist.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$326.69 and ~17x forward EPS (~$19.12), AON trades below the insurance-broker peer median P/E of 20.7. The peer set shows wide dispersion: AJG 35.7x, Marsh McLennan 21.0x, Brown & Brown 19.2x, Erie 20.7x, and WTW 15.2x. Aon sits between WTW and the median. The discount to Marsh McLennan (the most direct, larger peer at 21.0x) is the cleanest comparison and is partly justified by Aon's higher leverage, NFP integration noise, the thinner buyback runway, and the Vesttoo tail.

The case for the discount being too wide rests on Aon's superior margin profile (~40% ROE, adjusted operating margins near 39%) and its consistent organic growth. The case against treating "cheap" as a buy signal is that a compressed multiple here reflects identifiable headwinds rather than a clean mispricing: the P&C cycle turn is real and broad-based, the buyback lever is constrained, and estimate revisions are not rising. A low multiple without a positive catalyst and against decelerating estimates is the classic value-trap profile, and we are deliberately skeptical of rewarding cheapness in this sector.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range — low $298, mean $382, median $389, high $436, with KBW at $404 (Outperform) and Morgan Stanley recently trimmed to ~$370 (Overweight). With flat-to-down estimates and a stock at a premium-quality but not premium-multiple level, we do not treat the high targets as floors; the Street's range reflects disagreement about how much the cyclical deceleration matters. A defensible base case is ~19x on $19.12, or roughly $363 — about 11% above current. That return is attractive enough to hold but not sufficiently asymmetric, given the cyclical and capital-return overhangs, to warrant a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

Aon's moat is producer and client relationships, data and analytics scale, and recurring advisory revenue with high retention. The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-pressured. On the positive side, Commercial Risk organic growth of +7% and continued bolt-on M&A (ShoreOne coastal/flood broker closed March 2026; Griffiths & Armour UK in January 2025) show the franchise is taking share and adding capability even as rates soften. On the negative side, the people-driven nature of brokerage makes talent retention the operative competitive variable, and the post-period defection of Aon Canada's CEO to a rival (June 2026), layered on top of the departure of long-time President Eric Andersen, is a watch item. Marsh McLennan remains the larger, higher-multiple leader; Brown & Brown and Gallagher are aggressive consolidators. Aon's position is not eroding, but the combination of a softening cycle and senior-leadership turnover argues against an improving-trajectory score.

## Risk Assessment

**Vesttoo litigation (the standout idiosyncratic risk).** Following 2023 allegations of fraudulent letters of credit arranged via Vesttoo, Aon took a $197M charge in Q4 2023. The Clear Blue lawsuit was dismissed with prejudice (Sept 2025), a favorable development, but the Vesttoo bankruptcy liquidating trust filed a complaint against Aon in August 2025; Aon filed a motion to dismiss in November 2025. The $197M reserve "may be substantially recoverable," so resolution is a two-sided swing factor. Bermuda and other regulators could still act.

**Buyback air pocket.** With the authorization nearly spent, the EPS-growth cadence will slow until re-authorization. Given Aon's history of buyback-driven EPS, this is a near-term de-rating risk the market is partly pricing.

**NFP integration and goodwill.** Integration costs run through Q2 2026; the NFP goodwill/intangibles balance carries impairment risk if Risk Capital or Health growth disappoints, though none has been taken.

**FX reversibility.** Roughly half of revenue is non-USD; the Q1'26 FX tailwind (~$0.35–0.36/share) reverses if the dollar strengthens.

**Other:** E&O coverage layers have been materially depleted with some self-insurance, the 2016 Bolivia aviation litigation remains, and Pillar Two tax uncertainty applies to the Irish domicile. The Q1'26 10-Q makes no material changes to the 10-K risk factors.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 28.1% sits modestly above 1-year historical volatility of 23.7% (a 4.3% rich spread), but at only the 15th percentile of the current chain, so the options market is not pricing acute event risk. Put/call skew is balanced (1.5%) and the put/call open-interest ratio is low at 0.36, both consistent with neutral-to-mildly-constructive positioning rather than hedging distress. The elevated put/call volume ratio (2.58) is noisy against the low OI ratio and does not signal informed bearish positioning. The "340.6% IV" reading in the options-delta feed is a corrupted thin-options artifact and is disregarded. The options market corroborates the fundamental read of a stable, not stressed, situation — implied move of ±3.5% near-term, with no binary catalyst priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$390–410):** The P&C softening proves shallow and Aon's net-new-business / retention machine keeps organic growth at 5–6%. Margins continue to expand on AAU savings. The board re-authorizes a sizeable buyback, restoring the EPS amplifier, and the Vesttoo trust suit is dismissed or the reserve recovered. Multiple re-rates toward the peer median (~20–21x) on ~$21+ next-year EPS. This is roughly the KBW $404 / median $389 zone.

**Base case (~$363):** Organic growth decelerates modestly with the cycle to 4–5%, margins expand at a slower pace, buyback re-authorization comes but capital return is more measured during deleveraging. ~19x on $19.12 current-year EPS yields ~$363, about 11% above current.

**Bear case (~$280–290):** P&C pricing softens faster than expected, organic growth drops toward 2–3%, the Vesttoo trust suit advances unfavorably, an NFP-related goodwill charge surfaces, and the buyback gap weighs on EPS while the multiple compresses toward WTW's ~15x. ~15x on ~$19 implies ~$285, about 13% below current.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced-to-slightly-favorable but not the 2:1+ upside that would justify a BUY at this point in the cycle. The governance flag (CEO pay tripling against lagging TSR, plus a special PSU grant and leadership reshuffle) reduces our confidence in management alignment and weighs on the call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented).** Aon remains a high-quality compounder worth holding for moderate appreciation, but the cyclical organic-growth deceleration, the buyback air pocket, and the governance overhang prevent a higher conviction call until they resolve.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July / early August), which will reveal the trajectory of P&C-driven organic deceleration, AAU savings progress, and any update on the buyback authorization and Vesttoo litigation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Board announces a meaningful new repurchase authorization (restoring the EPS amplifier) AND organic growth holds at ≥5% despite softening rates.
2. Favorable Vesttoo resolution — dismissal of the bankruptcy-trust complaint or confirmed substantial recovery of the $197M reserve.
3. Current-year EPS estimates begin rising again (positive 90-day revisions) alongside multiple compression below ~16x forward, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Organic growth falls below ~3% YoY for two consecutive quarters, confirming the cycle is biting harder than priced.
2. A material NFP-related goodwill impairment, or an adverse Vesttoo development that expands the liability beyond the existing reserve.
3. Adjusted operating margins compress sequentially for two quarters, indicating the cost-savings program is not offsetting revenue pressure.

## Conclusion

Aon is a durable, cash-generative brokerage compounder whose Q1'26 results confirm the operating engine is working. At ~17x forward earnings the stock is below its peer median, but the discount is explained by a real, broad-based P&C pricing-cycle turn, a near-exhausted buyback authorization that removes a structural EPS support, NFP integration noise, the Vesttoo tail, and a governance flag in CEO compensation. The flat estimate-revision picture argues against treating this as a story that justifies paying up. The 11% base-case upside to ~$363 is real but not asymmetric enough, against balanced downside, to warrant a BUY at this stage of the cycle. We rate AON a HOLD with a positive 12-month bias, and would upgrade on a meaningful buyback re-authorization, a favorable Vesttoo resolution, or a return of positive estimate revisions.

This continues a declining-score, post-upgrade trajectory (7.3 in February to 6.8 in May, with prior softening in management quality, competitive position, and growth). The current 5.85 reflects further modest deterioration in management quality (CEO pay surge against lagging TSR, plus the leadership reshuffle) and confirms, rather than reverses, the cyclical-deceleration concern. The stock's drift lower since the prior report is consistent with these fundamental developments rather than a mispricing the market has missed, so we do not interpret the lower price as a strengthened buy case here.
