**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Harley-Davidson is an iconic American heavyweight motorcycle manufacturer operating through three segments: the core Harley-Davidson Motor Company (HDMC), the captive finance arm Harley-Davidson Financial Services (HDFS), and the majority-owned electric brand LiveWire. The business is in multi-year volume decline: consolidated revenue fell from $5.84B (2023) to $4.47B (2025), net income from $707M to $339M, and US heavyweight (601cc+) market share from 37.3% to 34.5% in a market that is itself shrinking. The company is mid-turnaround under CEO Artie Starrs, who took over October 1, 2025, with a new "Back to the Bricks" plan unveiled alongside Q1'26 results.

The investment case is two-sided, which is why the prior four-quarter AVOID stance no longer cleanly holds. On the constructive side: Q1'26 worldwide retail sales rose 8.1% (US +15.8%) and dealer inventory fell roughly 22% year-over-year, suggesting the destocking that crushed wholesale shipments and margins is working; the CEO bought 15,000 shares (~$287K) on the open market at $19.10 on March 9, 2026 and Director Lori Flees bought 4,000 shares at $18.89 the same day; the balance sheet is cleaner after the 2025 HDFS receivables restructuring; and on normalized next-year earnings (~$1.85 consensus) the stock trades around 14x versus a peer-group median near 32x, with a covered 2.9% dividend. The options market is not pricing material downside: near-term IV is cheap at 23.5% against 40.8% trailing realized vol, with balanced skew.

The bearish counterweight is equally real. 2026 HDMC operating result is guided to a range of –$40M to +$10M, so the core motorcycle business may lose money this year. The customer base is aging and the affordability pivot (Sprint 2026, Sportster return 2027) is unproven. The brand is under simultaneous attack from a renewed culture-war boycott campaign and competitor (Indian/Polaris) attack ads at the exact moment a consumer-goods outsider CEO is trying to broaden the customer base. A $140–450M brake-hose recall contingency sits unaccrued, credit ratings are at the bottom of investment grade with negative outlooks, and the heavyweight-motorcycle category is exposed to consumer-confidence and financing-rate sensitivity that would hit big-ticket discretionary purchases directly. We rate HOG HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the bad news is substantially priced after a roughly 50% recovery off the $17.09 low, insiders are buying, but there is insufficient evidence for a confident directional call until the turnaround demonstrates traction.

## Company Overview

Harley-Davidson (founded 1903, public since 1986) designs and manufactures heavyweight motorcycles (mostly 600cc–2,000cc) plus parts, accessories, apparel (MotorClothes), and licensed products, sold through roughly 1,174 independent dealer points globally (554 in the US). Manufacturing is US-centric (Milwaukee and Tomahawk WI, York PA) plus facilities in Thailand and Brazil.

The three segments:

- **HDMC** — the core. FY2025 revenue $3.58B (down from $4.12B in 2024 and $4.84B in 2023). Mix is roughly 74% motorcycles, 17% parts and accessories, 6% apparel.
- **HDFS** — captive finance (wholesale floorplan plus retail consumer loans), financing 68.4% of new US Harley retail sales in 2025. FY2025 revenue $869M. This segment was structurally reset in 2025 (see Financial Analysis).
- **LiveWire** — separately public, majority-owned electric brand. FY2025 revenue $25.7M, chronically loss-making (Q1'26 operating loss $17.7M). Management now expects powersports EV adoption far later than originally anticipated and will not invest beyond an existing $75M term loan.

The company's moat rests on the Bar & Shield brand and premium pricing, the H.O.G. owner community, the dealer network, and captive financing. The strategic tension that defines the equity is that the same iconic brand commanding premium pricing now needs to attract a younger, more price-sensitive rider base without alienating its aging core.

## Financial Analysis

The headline numbers require careful unpacking because a major 2025 financing restructuring distorts year-over-year comparisons.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidated revenue | ~$5.8B | $5.84B | $5.19B | $4.47B |
| Net income (attrib.) | $741M | $707M | $455M | $339M |

**Revenue is in structural decline.** HDMC revenue fell roughly 26% over two years ($4.84B → $3.58B), driven by both a shrinking US 601cc+ market (registrations 256K in 2023 → 238K in 2025) and Harley losing share within that shrinking market.

**Margin compression is acute.** Q1'26 HDMC operating margin collapsed to 1.8% from 10.8% a year earlier, with operating income down 83.7% to $18.9M. Drivers: lower volume, ~$45M of tariff costs in the quarter, unfavorable manufacturing leverage (fixed costs over fewer units), heavy incentives to clear Grand American Touring dealer inventory, and one-time severance and recall costs. The destocking and several of these cost items are transitory; the volume deleverage is not.

**2026 guidance is the central concern.** Full-year HDMC operating result is guided to –$40M to +$10M, LiveWire loss of $70–80M, and full-year tariff cost of $75–90M. The core motorcycle business may run at a loss this year.

**The HDFS reset is widely misread.** In 2025 the company restructured HDFS via investments from KKR and PIMCO and a Forward Flow Agreement to sell roughly two-thirds of future retail loan originations to two counterparties through 2030; those counterparties took a 9.8% non-controlling interest in HDFS convertible to HOG stock starting Q4 2032 at ~1.75x HDFS equity carrying value. The mechanical effects: total assets fell from $12.4B (Q1'25) to $7.2B (Q1'26), long-term debt from ~$6.8B to $2.1B, and HDFS operating income is guided down to $45–60M in 2026 versus $490M in 2025 (the 2025 figure flattered by gains on the receivables sale). HDFS Q1'26 interest income was $50M versus $209M a year earlier. Management targets HDFS recovery to ~$125–150M by 2029 as it rebuilds the held-for-investment book. This is a financing-model change rather than an operating collapse, and the apparent deleveraging is largely VIE deconsolidation, not organic balance-sheet repair.

**Cash flow signals require the same care.** Q1'26 operating cash flow was –$228M versus +$142M a year earlier, but this is distorted by the new accounting geography: originations of held-for-sale retail loans now run through operating activities under the Forward Flow Agreement. Underlying FCF (FY2025 ~$415M) remains positive but pressured.

**Liquidity is adequate:** ~$1.8B cash plus ~$1.4B facility availability, totaling roughly $3.2B at Q1'26. Credit ratings sit at the bottom of investment grade (Baa3/BBB-/BBB) with negative outlooks (S&P CreditWatch Negative, Fitch negative), the key financial-health watch item. The company redeemed €700M of 6.36% notes early in March 2026 and amended/extended its credit facilities (two $650M facilities maturing 2029/2031) in April 2026.

**Capital allocation:** The company abandoned its $1B buyback target (having completed $726M), repurchased $128M in Q1'26 including an ASR settlement, and maintains a $0.1875 quarterly dividend. Diluted shares fell from ~124M to ~110M year-over-year, flattering EPS even as net income fell.

The earnings-surprise record is volatile: Q1'26 beat by 11.7% ($0.30 vs $0.27), but Q4'25 missed by 131.7% (–$2.44 vs –$1.05 estimate), Q3'25 beat by 100.2%, and Q2'25 missed by 9.0%. Visibility is low.

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth picture is negative. US 601cc+ registrations have fallen ~7% over two years, the core rider demographic is aging, Europe is a structural weak spot (3.4% share, down from 5.0%), and worldwide shipments continue to decline. LiveWire is being contained rather than grown.

Against this, the Q1'26 data showed the first genuine operational green shoots in some time. The wholesale-shipment and margin weakness is partly a deliberate destocking that, once complete, should let shipments re-align with healthier retail demand. Estimate revisions are positive over 90 days (current-year EPS +36.2% to $0.38, next-year +21.4% to $1.85), though the percentage moves are flattered by depressed bases and the 30-day trend has stalled (current quarter –0.7%, current year –1.8%). The combination of declining revenue and collapsing margins distinguishes this from a clean earnings-inflection profile; the rising estimates reflect a low-base recovery hope, not accelerating fundamentals.

The "Back to the Bricks" plan sets concrete targets: ≥$150M cost reduction versus the 2025 base (fully realized by 2028), a total HDMC profitability benefit of ≥$225M, mid-single-digit retail-sales CAGR over 3–5 years, HDMC gross margin of 25–30%, and >$350M HDMC EBITDA in 2027. These are credible on paper but unproven, and the prior "Hardwire" plan ended with declining sales. New product (Sprint in 2026 and the Sportster return in 2027) targets younger, more price-sensitive riders, a segment where Harley has historically had weaker credibility against smaller-displacement and adventure competitors.

The growth thesis hinges entirely on whether the destocking-driven retail recovery is durable and whether the affordability pivot restores volume without diluting brand premium. There is not yet proof of either.

## Valuation Assessment

Headline multiples are misleading. Trailing P/E of 13.2x rests on $2.78 EPS that includes 2025 HDFS receivables-sale gains. On current-year consensus EPS of $0.38, the stock is at ~67x, meaningless because 2026 is a guided trough/loss year for the core. The relevant anchor is normalized earnings.

Next-year consensus EPS of $1.85 puts the stock at roughly 13.9x at $25.64. The Consumer Discretionary peer median P/E is 32.1x (OSW 33.1x, PENN negative, WHR 15.0x, PATK 22.0x, CPRI 32.1x; wide dispersion, so the median is a rough reference rather than a tight comp). HOG also offers a 2.9% dividend yield against a 0.0% peer median. On normalized earnings and the >$350M HDMC EBITDA 2027 target plus HDFS rebuilding to $125–150M by 2029, there is a genuine value argument: the market is discounting the stock well below peers on recovered earnings power.

The discount is partly earned. It reflects warranted skepticism about a fifth turnaround attempt, secular volume decline, active brand-damage campaigns, the unaccrued recall tail, and bottom-of-IG credit on negative watch. We do not view ~14x normalized as a clear mispricing so much as a fair-to-modestly-cheap multiple for a structurally challenged, high-execution-risk turnaround. The analyst range is wide ($15 low, $32 high, $26 median), itself reflecting the bimodal outcome distribution.

The stock has recovered roughly 50% from its $17.09 52-week low to $25.64 (and sits ~18% below the $31.25 high). That recovery means the deeply asymmetric entry available near the lows, where the CEO was buying, has compressed. At current prices the risk/reward is closer to neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

Harley remains the dominant US heavyweight brand at 34.5% share, but the trajectory is negative: share fell from 37.3% in 2024, in a market shrinking on both cyclical (high rates, weak consumer confidence) and secular (aging riders) drivers. Europe is structurally weak at 3.4%.

Indian Motorcycle (Polaris) is the principal domestic rival and is on the offensive, using both product and aggressive marketing (including attack ads in June 2026 mocking CEO Starrs's restaurant background) to peel off Harley's traditional customers. The affordability/younger-rider pivot pushes Harley into segments where it has weaker historical credibility. HDFS financing 68% of US retail sales remains a real competitive weapon, even with the temporary earnings hollowing.

The one positive data point is the Q1'26 US retail figure (+15.8%), which suggests dealer-level stabilization even as reported share slipped. Whether that reflects destocking-driven incentive pull-forward or genuine demand recovery is the key open question.

## Risk Assessment

- **Turnaround execution.** The single risk factor added/modified in the Q1'26 10-Q is explicitly about the new "Back to the Bricks" plan and new CEO. There is no proof yet the plan or the affordability pivot will land.
- **Secular demand erosion.** Declining 601cc+ registrations and an aging core demographic; the pivot to younger/affordable riders is an admission the traditional base is shrinking.
- **Brand boycott and competitor attack.** Robby Starbuck renewed a boycott campaign attacking executive hires (early June 2026), and Indian ran attack ads. For a brand whose core customer skews toward the demographic targeted by these campaigns, this is a genuine, company-specific demand risk, not boilerplate.
- **Brake-hose recall contingency.** An unresolved NHTSA matter on model-year 2008+ motorcycles, with potential cost of $140–450M and zero accrued, based on management's expectation of favorable inconsequentiality determinations. A large unaccrued tail.
- **Credit and funding.** Bottom-of-IG ratings with negative outlooks; 30-day managed delinquency rose to 4.63% from 4.47% and the company weighted its credit-loss forecast toward more pessimistic scenarios. Funding-cost risk if ratings deteriorate.
- **Tariffs.** $75–90M FY2026 estimated cost; EU motorcycle tariffs (a 2018–2021 retaliation target at up to 31%) remain a live exposure, partly offset by a ~$41M Q1'26 refund from a favorable appeal.
- **Macro.** Heavyweight motorcycles are discretionary big-ticket purchases highly sensitive to consumer confidence and financing rates, both of which the company cites as pressuring demand.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating an imminent-downside thesis. Near-term ATM IV is in the 1st percentile of its own chain, with a balanced put/call skew (0.7%). The implied nearest-expiry move is ±3.8%, put/call OI ratio 0.85, and the term structure is in contango (near-term 23.5% versus long-term 48.1%): a complacent rather than fearful structure. The 6-month skew is –6.8% (call-side), and the P/C OI ratio has risen from 0.09 to 0.85 over the past month as the stock recovered. This argues much of the bad news is priced and materially weakens any active short case. The 20.4% short interest with 5.4 days to cover, combined with insider buying and recovering retail momentum, creates a meaningful squeeze setup against, rather than for, the bears.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($32):** 2026 is a transitory trough, accounting-driven on HDFS and tariff/destocking/one-time-cost-driven on HDMC, and normalized earnings power (~$1.85+ next year, >$350M HDMC EBITDA in 2027) is well above the trough. The Q1'26 retail recovery and 22% inventory destocking prove demand is stabilizing as the channel clears. Cost savings ramp toward $150M by 2028, gross margin recovers toward the 25–30% target, HDFS rebuilds, and the affordability products (Sprint, Sportster) expand the addressable rider base. The CEO's open-market buy at $19.10 marks a floor that proves prescient, and at ~14x normalized versus a 32x peer median the stock re-rates toward the $32 analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$15):** The retail "recovery" was incentive-driven pull-forward, demand resumes its secular slide, and the affordability pivot dilutes the brand premium without restoring volume. The 2026 HDMC loss materializes at the low end of guidance, the brake-hose recall accrues toward the high end of the range, the boycott and competitor pressure bite, a consumer pullback hits big-ticket discretionary demand, and credit is downgraded out of investment grade, raising HDFS funding costs. The stock revisits the lower end of the analyst range near $15 (–41% from current).

The wide gap between these scenarios, with no near-term resolution, is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2'26 earnings (expected early August 2026), which will test whether the Q1 retail recovery and destocking benefit carry into margins, followed by Q3'26 for confirmation. The "Back to the Bricks" cost-savings ramp and any movement on the brake-hose NHTSA matter are the medium-term watch items.

**Specific evidence supporting HOLD rather than AVOID (the validation question):**
- **Dated insider conviction:** CEO Starrs's and Director Flees's open-market purchases on March 9, 2026 were ~25% below the current price, the strongest insider signal type.
- **Dated operational inflection:** Q1'26 (reported May 5, 2026) worldwide retail +8.1%, US retail +15.8%, dealer inventory down ~22% YoY, and an EPS beat of 11.7%.
- **Valuation support:** ~14x normalized next-year EPS versus a 32.1x peer median, plus a covered 2.9% dividend, with the stock having already recovered ~50% off its low, limiting incremental downside.
- **Options corroboration:** balanced skew, cheap IV, and a complacent term structure indicate the market is not pricing imminent collapse; 20.4% short interest creates squeeze risk against an active short.

These specifics justify holding rather than avoiding, but I classify the rationale as uncertain because the offsetting structural risks (loss-year 2026 core, secular decline, active brand attacks, recall tail) are equally concrete, and I lack conviction for a directional view.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY-equivalent conviction):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive HDMC retail sales growth AND HDMC operating margin recovering above ~6%, confirming the destocking benefit is durable rather than incentive-driven.
- Resolution of the brake-hose NHTSA matter with a favorable inconsequentiality determination (removing the $140–450M tail).
- Credit-outlook stabilization to neutral from negative.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- HDMC 2026 operating result tracking toward the –$40M low end with retail sales rolling back over.
- A credit downgrade out of investment grade, raising HDFS funding costs.
- Material brake-hose recall accrual at the high end of the range, or evidence the boycott/competitor pressure is denting US retail momentum.

## Conclusion

Harley-Davidson sits in an uncomfortable middle. The four prior AVOID ratings were appropriate to a deteriorating-fundamentals, falling-price setup; that setup has shifted. The stock has recovered ~50% off its low, insiders are buying with open-market conviction, the destocking is visibly working at the dealer level, the balance sheet is cleaner, and the options market is not pricing downside, making an active short unattractive given the 20.4% short interest and squeeze risk. On normalized earnings the stock is below peers with a covered dividend.

At the same time, this remains a structurally declining, brand-dependent business with a 2026 core guided to roughly breakeven-to-loss, an aging customer base, eroding US share, an active brand-boycott and competitor offensive, an unaccrued recall tail, bottom-of-IG credit on negative watch, and an unproven fifth turnaround under a new CEO, into a category where discretionary big-ticket demand is sensitive to consumer confidence and rates. The valuation discount is largely earned by these risks, and at the current price the deeply asymmetric entry available near the lows has compressed.

The honest assessment is HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the operational green shoots and insider buying are real enough to keep us from avoiding, but there is insufficient evidence for a confident directional call until the turnaround demonstrates margin traction in the Q2/Q3 2026 prints. We are not buyers at $25.64, given that the sector and macro argue against adding big-ticket discretionary exposure and the turnaround is unproven, but we are not active sellers or shorts either.
