**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Loews Corporation is a diversified holding company controlled by the Tisch family, misclassified by data vendors as a "multi-line insurer." It is a sum-of-the-parts conglomerate: CNA Financial (~92% owned, commercial P&C) drives 81% of revenue and roughly $1.17B of 2025 net income; Boardwalk Pipelines (100%) contributes 12.6% of revenue and $444M; Loews Hotels and a ~53% equity stake in Altium Packaging round out the portfolio. The company's defining feature is capital allocation: the Tisch family has spent decades upstreaming subsidiary cash and recycling it into share repurchases, retiring roughly 14% of the share count in three years (243.3M diluted shares in 2022 to 206.1M as of February 2026). Diluted EPS reached $7.97 in 2025, up from $6.41, with a meaningful portion of that growth manufactured through share-count reduction rather than operating expansion.

On one side of the investment debate, the per-share compounding machine remains intact: CNA's combined ratio improved to 94.7% (91.8% underlying), A.M. Best upgraded CNA to A+ in December 2025, and Boardwalk is transitioning from a maintenance-capex pipeline into a ~$3B expansion cycle backed by a >$19B firm-revenue backlog tied to LNG exports and data-center power demand, with an earnings inflection dated to 2029. Parent liquidity is strong: $3.9B net cash, A/A3 ratings, no parent guarantees of subsidiary debt. On the other side, the stock sits near its 52-week high of $121.01 after a ~32% twelve-month run, trades above book value (ex-AOCI book value per share ~$94), and management itself throttled buybacks in Q3 2025, explicitly noting that all-time-high prices made it "difficult to pull the trigger."

We rate Loews a HOLD. The buyback discipline that built the track record is now working against near-term upside: when the company's own opportunistic repurchaser says the stock is expensive, that is a signal to weigh. The most compelling reason to hold rather than trim is the Boardwalk optionality, which is buried inside the "insurance" wrapper and whose earnings contribution is still ahead. The genuine idiosyncratic risk is CNA's $13.4B long-term-care run-off block and escalating mass-tort reserve development, not leverage (the Altman Z-Score and consolidated D/E are accounting artifacts of insurance reserves).

## Company Overview

Loews operates through four segments:

- **CNA Financial (~92% owned, publicly traded separately):** Commercial P&C across Specialty, Commercial, and International lines. 81.2% of consolidated revenue, $1,173M net income in 2025.
- **Boardwalk Pipelines (100%):** ~14,275 miles of natural gas/NGL pipelines plus ~199.5 Bcf of storage, Gulf Coast-centric and FERC-regulated, with ~87% of revenue under firm contracts. 12.6% of revenue, $444M net income.
- **Loews Hotels & Co (100%):** 27 hotels, heavily concentrated in Florida and Universal Orlando (59% of rooms). ~5% of revenue, $31M net income.
- **Altium Packaging (~53%, equity method):** Rigid plastic packaging; posted a $28M equity loss in 2025 owing to its own leverage.

The parent's function is to allocate capital. In 2025 Loews received $1.5B in subsidiary distributions ($954M from CNA including a $497M special dividend, plus $500M from Boardwalk) and deployed $806M into buybacks versus only $52M in dividends. Third-generation leadership took over in January 2025 when Benjamin J. Tisch (43), son of Chairman James S. Tisch, became President & CEO; cousin Alexander Tisch runs the hotels segment.

## Financial Analysis

**Top line and earnings.** Consolidated revenue grew from $14.0B (2022) to $18.5B (2025), a ~9.7% CAGR. Net income to Loews rose $1,012M → $1,434M → $1,414M → $1,667M. Operating cash flow was $3.28B and free cash flow approximately $2.7B in 2025.

**The buyback engine.** This is the mechanical core of the per-share story. Diluted shares fell from 243.3M in 2022 to 209.1M in 2025, with only 206.1M outstanding as of February 6, 2026. A material share of EPS growth is deliberate share-count reduction rather than pure operating leverage, which is the intended design of the Loews model.

**Holdco liquidity.** Parent cash and investments net of receivables/payables stood at $3.9B at year-end 2025. Q1 2026 distributions from subsidiaries were expected at $616M from CNA and $75M from Boardwalk. The parent is rated A (S&P) / A3 (Moody's) and does not guarantee subsidiary debt.

**Segment quality.** CNA's 2025 net investment income was $2,557M on a 4.9% fixed-income yield; Moody's carries a positive outlook. Boardwalk generated $1,174M of EBITDA (+8%) with a $15.6B contracted backlog plus $3.8B from Texas Gateway, roughly $19.4B of projected firm revenues, 87% from investment-grade counterparties. Hotels are harvesting years of Orlando/Arlington investment.

**On the distress screens.** The Altman Z-Score of 0.51 and consolidated D/E of 3.57 are false signals. The Z-Score model is not applicable to an insurance holding company whose balance sheet carries $27B of claim reserves and $27B of future-policy-benefit reserves, which inflate liabilities and depress the model's ratios. Any standardized screen will badly misread this business.

The earnings-surprise history is uninformative here: no consensus EPS estimates were captured against the reported quarterly prints ($1.63, $1.94, $2.43, $1.87), so we cannot calibrate management's beat/miss tendency from the supplied data.

## Growth Analysis

Growth at Loews is a blend of modest organic segment expansion and per-share compounding through buybacks. The organic story is bifurcated.

CNA operates in a cyclical commercial P&C market where rate momentum is decelerating: overall rate up ~3% (Commercial +5%), but management-liability lines are under pricing pressure and social inflation is weighing on Commercial and auto-liability lines. Net investment income provides a durable tailwind as the fixed-income book rolls into higher yields, but underwriting-margin expansion is closer to plateauing than accelerating.

Boardwalk is the genuine growth optionality, under-appreciated because it sits at 12.6% of revenue inside a conglomerate framed as insurance. U.S. gas demand is at all-time highs (>110 Bcf/d, +45% since 2015), driven by LNG export FIDs (~8 Bcf/d YTD) and data-center power demand (data centers ~4% of U.S. electricity, potentially 7–12% within three years). Pipeline capacity has lagged demand (gas demand +64% since 2010 versus capacity +35%), giving Boardwalk pricing power on recontracting. The company is moving from ~$200–300M/year maintenance capex into a ~$3B expansion cycle: Texas Gateway ($1.2B, 155-mile new-build, 20-year IG anchor, 2029 in-service) and Kosci Junction ($1.0B), targeting low-to-mid-teens unlevered returns. The 2029 earnings inflection does not support near-term multiple expansion; it supports holding the position through the cycle.

Hotels are reaping the Epic Universe-adjacent JV openings but remain a small, geographically concentrated contributor.

The estimate-revision data required to identify an inflection signal was not provided; on the fundamentals available, this is a mid-single-digit organic compounder with a longer-dated Boardwalk lever, not an accelerating growth story. We score Growth Prospects at 5.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At $119.22, Loews trades at ~15.2x trailing earnings and above book value (total BVPS $88.39, ex-AOCI ~$94.00 as of Q3 2025). The peer set for multi-line insurance (AIZ 13.3x, AFG 12.6x, AIG 13.2x, LNC 4.3x, GNW 17.0x; median P/E 13.2x) shows Loews at a modest premium to the median. But peer comparison is only loosely applicable: Loews is a holdco whose sum-of-the-parts value depends on CNA's implied value (public), Boardwalk's private infrastructure value, hotels, and parent cash, not on a blended insurance multiple.

The most important valuation tell is internal: management, whose entire identity is opportunistic repurchase, slowed buybacks in Q3 2025 to ~600K shares/$56M because the stock hit all-time highs and it was "difficult to pull the trigger." When a serial repurchaser with better information than any outside analyst pauses at these levels, that is a self-imposed valuation ceiling, even as the family maintains the stock remains below intrinsic value. The countervailing signal is director Dino Robusto's ~$1.05M of open-market purchases in May 2026 at ~$105, below today's price, which cuts against the "fully valued" chorus. These two data points are in tension, and we weight them roughly evenly.

A name that screens "cheap" (here on a headline 15x, below GNW and near peers) is not automatically attractive, and cheapness has been a poor standalone predictor in prior scoring cycles. Loews trades above book with management flagging the price, and there is no identified near-term catalyst to re-rate the sum-of-the-parts. The Boardwalk 2029 inflection is real but too distant to justify paying up today. We score Valuation at 4.5: the multiple is not stretched to danger, but the asymmetry is unfavorable and the clearest informed party (management, via its buyback pace) is telling you so.

## Competitive Landscape

CNA competes in a crowded, cyclical commercial P&C market against larger carriers, insurtechs, and MGUs. Its position is stable-to-modestly-improving: the A+ Best upgrade strengthens the franchise, the combined ratio is respectable, and investment income is a structural tailwind. But management-liability pricing pressure and social inflation are competitive headwinds, and CNA is a mid-tier player, not a share-gaining leader.

Boardwalk holds the strongest competitive position in the portfolio: advantaged Gulf Coast/Haynesville positioning into a structural supply-demand imbalance that confers recontracting pricing power. This is where the moat is widening.

Hotels face OTA commission leverage, Airbnb, and theme-park-adjacent supply; the Universal Orlando concentration is both an advantage (proximity to a growing ecosystem) and a single-point risk.

Net trajectory: stable, with Boardwalk improving and CNA plateauing. We score Competitive Position at 6.0. This dimension has been a weak recent predictor in our framework, so we weight it lightly.

## Risk Assessment

**Long-term care run-off — the real tail.** CNA carries $13.4B of LTC reserves in run-off with only $1.5B of statutory margin. A 5% increase in morbidity would cut pretax income by $620M; the block is highly sensitive to cost-of-care inflation and to the discount rate applied to reserves. This is a slow-burn, hard-to-model exposure that could periodically dent CNA, and therefore the dividends Loews depends on.

**Mass-tort reserve development.** CNA took a $106M after-tax charge in 2025 (up from $62M in 2024) on legacy abuse-reviver reserves, and P&C swung to $51M of unfavorable prior-year development versus $31M favorable in 2024. Sexual-abuse reviver statutes, PFAS, opioids, and social inflation make this an intensifying, unpredictable line.

**A&EP cushion nearly exhausted.** The 2010 Berkshire/NICO loss-portfolio transfer had a $4.0B limit; $3.9B is already ceded, so further asbestos/environmental deterioration flows back to CNA.

**Boardwalk execution and concentration.** ~$3.3B growth capex through 2030 with no cash flow until 2029 in-service dates, exposed to construction/tariff/labor inflation. One customer exceeds 10% of revenue; top-10 counterparties are 66% of backlog. FERC rate-review risk applies.

**Hotels concentration.** 64% of rooms in Florida, 59% at Universal Orlando: high leverage to one theme-park ecosystem and Florida hurricane risk.

**Newer disclosures.** A Q4 2025 CNA vendor data breach affecting employee health data, expanded tariff/trade-policy language at Boardwalk (steel) and Hotels, and extensive AI-related underwriting/claims risk language.

**Governance.** Tisch family control cuts both ways: a long-term, per-share-value orientation and a flat 1.2x CEO-to-NEO comp ratio (signaling low entrenchment premium), but limited outside check. Third-generation succession is a watch item, though the transition has proceeded without distress events.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that is not obvious in the fundamentals. Near-term ATM IV of 23.6% is rich versus 16.3% one-year historical vol, the put/call skew is elevated at 6.3%, the put/call volume ratio is 3.50, and the term structure is in backwardation (23.6% near versus 18.8% at six months), the classic pattern of the market pricing an imminent event, likely the next earnings print. Short interest is low at 2.6% of float (4.8 days to cover), so this is hedging demand rather than a crowded directional short. For a HOLD, the takeaway is defensive: the market is paying up for near-term downside protection near all-time highs, consistent with our view that the risk/reward is balanced-to-unfavorable at this price.

We score Risk Factors at 5.0. The LTC and mass-tort exposures are specific, quantified, and only partially priced, and they are the reason risk sits below neutral rather than the misleading distress screens.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$140).** The buyback engine keeps compounding per-share value at a sub-206M and falling share count; CNA sustains a low-90s combined ratio while investment income keeps rising; Boardwalk's backlog and 2029 expansion cycle begin to be recognized in the sum-of-the-parts; and the parent's cash funds continued opportunistic repurchase on any pullback. Re-rating toward a fairer conglomerate valuation gets the stock to the high-$130s to $140.

**Bear case (~$95, ~20% downside).** LTC morbidity assumptions deteriorate or a mass-tort reserve charge materially exceeds the $106M 2025 run-rate; CNA underwriting margin compresses as management-liability pricing softens and social inflation bites; Boardwalk capex pressures distributions in 2027–2029, throttling the cash Loews depends on; and the stock de-rates back toward book value in the mid-$90s, near where director Robusto bought.

The base case sits between: mid-single-digit per-share compounding with the buyback lever intact, no near-term catalyst to force a re-rating, and management's own buyback discipline capping upside until either the stock pulls back or the Boardwalk inflection draws closer.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the reason to own it is the multi-year per-share compounding plus the Boardwalk 2029 optionality, not near-term appreciation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, with particular attention to CNA prior-year development and the pace of buybacks. Boardwalk project milestones (Texas Gateway/Kosci Junction FIDs and construction updates) are the longer-dated checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward book value (low-$100s or below), where the sum-of-the-parts offers margin of safety and management resumes aggressive repurchase.
- Evidence of accelerating buyback pace at current-or-higher prices, signaling management sees value where they recently paused.
- Confirmation that Boardwalk's expansion contributions are being recognized in earnings/guidance ahead of the 2029 in-service date.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material LTC reserve strengthening (morbidity-driven charge approaching the disclosed $620M sensitivity) or a mass-tort development charge that materially exceeds the $106M 2025 run-rate.
- CNA combined ratio deteriorating above ~100% on sustained underwriting-margin compression.
- Boardwalk capex forcing a cut to Loews-directed distributions, breaking the capital-return loop.

## Conclusion

Loews is a well-run, A-rated holdco whose per-share value compounds through a disciplined and shareholder-aligned buyback program, backed by an improving CNA franchise, an under-appreciated Boardwalk expansion cycle, and $3.9B of parent cash. The distress screens should be ignored; they are insurance-accounting artifacts. But the stock trades near all-time highs above book value, the options market is pricing near-term stress, and the most informed party (management, through its throttled buyback pace) is signaling that the price is full. The LTC and mass-tort reserve tails are the real, only-partially-priced risks. With the Boardwalk earnings inflection still four years out and no near-term catalyst to re-rate the sum-of-the-parts, the risk/reward is balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable at $119. We maintain a HOLD, consistent with the three prior reports, and would become more constructive on a pullback toward book value where the family's buyback machine re-engages.
