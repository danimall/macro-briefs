**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

LTC Properties is a $1.9B-cap health-care REIT executing a deliberate transformation from a pure triple-net landlord and mortgage lender into an operator-economics business via the RIDEA structure and a new Seniors Housing Operating Portfolio (SHOP) segment. The SHOP segment, launched in Q2 2025, already represents 25.7% of net carrying value ($508M) and is guided to reach roughly 45% of gross investments and ~40% of annualized NOI by year-end 2026. The mechanism: instead of collecting fixed rent from a tenant, LTC owns the real estate and captures the operating margin (and bears the operating risk) of senior-housing communities run by third-party managers under a taxable REIT subsidiary.

The headline financial picture is misleading on a quick screen. FY2025 Nareit FFO fell 38% to $77.3M, but the decline is almost entirely a non-cash $41.5M write-off of previously accrued effective interest on the Prestige mortgage loan, tied to a favorable modification that raised the loan to its full 11.14% rate and added a penalty-free early-payoff option. The forward run-rate differs: Q1 2026 Core FFO was $0.69/share (up from $0.65), and management reaffirmed FY26 Core FFO guidance of $2.75–$2.79 and Core FAD of $2.82–$2.86. At $36.40, the stock yields ~6.2% on a $2.28 monthly dividend covered at roughly 80% of Core FAD.

This is an income hold. The dividend is covered and the balance sheet is conservative for a REIT (34% debt/gross-asset value, 4.8x interest coverage). Insider conviction is a genuine positive: the CIO bought ~$348K of stock in the open market at ~$34.7 in June 2026, and a director added at $38.00 and $38.41. But the central variable is execution of an operating-economics pivot by a 25-person team, funded by steady equity dilution (share count up roughly 14% over two years), with a thin dividend cushion if SHOP occupancy or margins disappoint. We do not yet have proof the higher-growth profile is being delivered, which caps the appreciation case relative to the yield case.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1992 and based in Westlake Village, California, LTC invests in seniors housing (SH) and skilled nursing facilities (SNF). The portfolio spans roughly 186–190 properties — about 8,300 SNF beds and 8,000 SH units — across 23 states with 30 operators. Capital is now deployed across five vehicles:

- **Triple-Net Portfolio** — 35.0% of net carrying value ($693M)
- **SHOP** — 25.7% ($508M), new in Q2 2025
- **Mortgage loans** — 19.3% ($382M)
- **Financing receivables** (sale-leasebacks with purchase options) — 18.1% ($359M)
- **Notes receivable & JVs** — ~1.9%

The defining shift is the adoption of the RIDEA structure. Under triple-net, LTC collected fixed rent and offloaded operating risk to tenants. Under RIDEA/SHOP, LTC owns the real estate and takes the operating economics — occupancy, rate growth, labor and food costs — with third-party operators running day-to-day operations under management agreements. By Q1 2026, SHOP was 29% of gross investments. Concurrently, skilled nursing fell from 46% of gross investments at year-end 2024 to 33% by Q1 2026 as LTC divests SNFs. Management frames the pivot as a move toward higher growth and a better risk-adjusted return as the 80+ demographic cohort accelerates.

Leadership transitioned in 2025: long-time CEO Wendy Simpson became Executive Chairman, with Clint Malin and Pamela Shelley-Kessler now Co-Presidents and Co-CEOs. The company runs lean — 25 employees, average tenure over 12 years.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is real but composition-driven.** Revenue rose from $175M (2022) to $197M (2023) to $210M (2024) to $263M (2025), a 25% jump in 2025 driven almost entirely by SHOP ($72M of resident fees). Rental income actually fell from $132M to $116M as leases converted to the SHOP structure, so the top-line growth is a reclassification of where the same assets show up, plus genuine new acquisition activity. Q1 2026 revenue nearly doubled YoY to $95.4M (from $49.0M), reflecting the full SHOP run-rate ($49.6M of resident fees in the quarter).

**Net income was inflated by gains.** Net income to common rose from $90.4M (2024) to $117.3M (2025), but the increase was driven by a $77.8M gain on real estate sales versus $8.0M the prior year. Excluding that, the underlying franchise looks softer than the GAAP headline.

**FFO fell, but the decline is a one-time artifact.** Nareit FFO dropped from $125.7M (2024) to $77.3M (2025), driven by the non-cash Prestige write-off (Q3 2025). Adjusted EBITDAre was $169.2M for 2025, and Q1 2026 Core FFO confirms the earnings power is intact. A naive screen on FY25 FFO would badly misprice this name.

**Balance sheet is conservative for a REIT.** Debt/equity is 0.84, fixed-charge coverage 4.8x. Total debt is roughly $842M: a $253M revolver at 4.40%, term loans of $198M at 4.77%, and senior unsecured notes of $391M at 4.12%. LTC upsized its credit facility to $800M (July 2029 maturity, $1.2B accordion) and laddered $200M of term loans with matched interest-rate swaps. Liquidity was roughly $650M at year-end 2025 (~$540M proforma after subsequent borrowings). The Altman Z-Score of 1.57 ("distress zone") is a false signal — an artifact of REIT accounting with near-zero working capital and 0.13 asset turnover. The coverage ratios and 34% leverage indicate sound credit health.

**Dividend coverage is adequate but thin.** The monthly $0.19 dividend ($2.28 annualized) represents roughly 80% of Core FAD at the guidance midpoint — covered, but without much margin for error. The dividend has been flat, which management's own risk factors flag.

**Capital allocation requires monitoring.** LTC is funding SHOP growth partly through equity: 2.8M shares for $100.6M under the ATM in 2025, plus $43.4M in Q1 2026 and $107M of net revolver borrowings post-year-end. Share count rose from ~43.7M (2024) to ~48.5M (YE2025) to ~49.8M (Q1 2026). Funding a transformation with dilutive equity near a sub-$37 price is a recurring drag on per-share metrics if NAV is not clearly above the issuance price.

**Earnings surprise track record is volatile.** Over the last four quarters: a 69% beat (Q4'25, gain-driven), a 242% miss (Q3'25, the Prestige write-off), a 36% miss (Q2'25), and a 10% miss (Q1'26). The misses largely reflect the transition's non-cash noise rather than operational deterioration, but the volatility lowers confidence in any single forward estimate.

## Growth Analysis

The forward trajectory is the actual investment story. FY26 guidance calls for EPS of $1.80–$1.84. SHOP NOI is guided to $65–77M, implying 14% growth on the core 27-property pool, with occupancy rising 150 bps from 89.7% and REVPOR up 5% against EXPOR up 2.5% — operating leverage if delivered.

The SHOP build-out is aggressive: a $108M Georgia three-property acquisition (Q1'26, 7% year-one cap, low-teens IRR), a $9.2M Illinois SHOP (April, 9% cap), and roughly $250M of additional SHOP acquisitions expected to close in Q2 2026, plus ongoing triple-net-to-SHOP conversions in TX, GA, and SC. Total 2026 SHOP acquisitions are projected near $600M against roughly $266M of SNF divestitures.

The demographic backdrop is genuinely favorable. The 80+ cohort is accelerating, which supports senior-housing occupancy and rate growth, and SHOP lets LTC capture that operating upside rather than fixed rent. The neutralization of the CMS minimum-staffing mandate (vacated by courts plus a 10-year moratorium under the July 2025 legislation) removes a major cost threat to SNF operators.

Estimate revisions are mixed and modest. Current-year consensus EPS is roughly flat over 90 days (+0.6% over 30 days, -3.9% over 90 days), and next-year estimates are down ~5.7% over 90 days. There is no estimate-revision pattern of the magnitude that would signal a structural earnings acceleration. Growth here is acquisition-and-execution-dependent, not driven by a market that is rapidly upgrading its forward view.

## Valuation Assessment

At $36.40, LTC trades at ~14.2x trailing/forward earnings and roughly 13.1x the midpoint of FY26 Core FFO guidance ($2.77). The 6.2% dividend yield provides absolute support and is near the high end of the peer set.

Against Health Care REIT peers, the picture is two-sided. The peer median P/E is 29.3 and median yield 6.4%. LTC's P/E is well below peers (SBRA 29.3, CTRE 23.4, UHT 31.4; MPT and HR show negative earnings), which on a headline basis looks cheap. But the relevant multiple for a REIT is price-to-FFO, and at ~13x forward Core FFO LTC sits at a justified discount: it is a sub-$2B name with a higher cost of capital, a less-proven SHOP platform than scaled peers like CTRE and SBRA, and a transformation that has not yet demonstrated the targeted NOI ramp. The yield is roughly in line with the peer median, which argues the market is pricing LTC fairly on income.

The valuation does not offer the kind of asymmetric mispricing that warrants a high score. The dividend yield supports the downside, and the insider buying suggests management sees value near current levels. But there is no identified catalyst that re-rates the multiple absent SHOP execution coming through in reported NOI over multiple quarters. A cheap-looking P/E here reflects the genuine uncertainty of the operating-model transition rather than a market error. We score valuation at the midpoint — supported on yield, capped on the absence of a re-rating catalyst and the dilution headwind to per-share growth.

Analyst targets bracket $38 (low) to $45 (high), with a $41 mean and $40 median; the stock sits ~12% below the mean target, consistent with a name the Street views as fairly-to-modestly-undervalued but not a high-conviction long.

## Competitive Landscape

LTC competes against far larger health-care REITs — Welltower, Ventas, Omega, Sabra, CareTrust, NHI — that enjoy structurally lower cost of capital. LTC's stated edge is a relationship-driven, single-asset and small-portfolio sourcing strategy with regional operators, deliberately avoiding broadly-marketed large portfolios where pricing compresses yields below its hurdles. That is credible for a niche player and explains the high-single-digit cap rates on recent deals, but it inherently limits scale.

The competitive concern is trajectory. LTC is building a SHOP operating platform with a 25-person team at the same time Welltower and others have already scaled sophisticated RIDEA platforms with dedicated operational infrastructure. LTC is a relative latecomer assuming operating risk (labor, occupancy, capex) without the operating depth of larger peers. Operator concentration compounds this: roughly 25.3% of lease-plus-interest revenue came from three operators in 2025, with Prestige alone at 11.9% of revenue (and now repaying its loan, removing a high-yield asset that must be redeployed). The competitive position is stable rather than improving — LTC is defending a niche while taking on a more demanding business model, which keeps this dimension at the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

**SHOP operating risk (new, top-listed in the rewritten 10-K).** Under RIDEA, LTC now bears direct exposure to occupancy swings, labor/food/energy inflation, professional and general-liability claims, and capex — risks it previously offloaded to triple-net tenants. It is structurally dependent on third-party operators' competence and accurate reporting, with limited day-to-day control. This is a genuine change in the risk profile, not boilerplate.

**Operator concentration.** Three operators contributed ~25.3% of lease-plus-interest revenue in 2025. Top relationships: ALG Senior Living ($297M financing receivables in NC/SC), Prestige ($268M, 11.9% of revenue), Encore, HMG, Carespring.

**Reimbursement and regulation.** SNF exposure ties LTC to Medicare/Medicaid. The CMS minimum-staffing rule was neutralized (a tailwind), but a January 2026 CMS final rule tightening state provider-tax use of Medicaid funding is a fresh headwind to monitor. The 10-K also adds emphasis on pending federal/state proposals to limit REIT ownership of nursing homes — a sector-specific overhang.

**Dilution and dividend coverage.** The flat $2.28 dividend at ~80% Core FAD payout, funded alongside steady ATM issuance, works if SHOP NOI ramps as guided. If occupancy or margins disappoint, the cushion is thin and further equity raises near current levels would compound dilution.

**Credit overhangs are clearing, with a trade-off.** Prestige is repaying in full, Genesis assets were sold to a new sponsor and rent is current, and Anthem's 12 communities converted to SHOP. This de-risks the legacy book but removes high-yield mortgage income (Prestige) that must be redeployed at lower spreads.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-constructive. Near-term ATM IV is 22.2% versus 18.6% historical volatility — modestly rich but at only the 27th percentile within the chain. The put/call skew is -3.9% (call-heavy), the put/call volume ratio is 0.23, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.13 to 0.06 over the prior six weeks as the stock declined 7% to $36.37. There is no elevated put demand or downside hedging despite the pullback; the options market is not pricing a binary downside catalyst, consistent with an income name in transition. The flat term structure (near 22.2% ≈ far 21.0%) indicates uncertainty is evenly distributed, not concentrated around a near-term event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** SHOP NOI ramps as guided, the ~$600M of 2026 acquisitions deploy at high-single-digit caps with low-teens IRRs, and the senior-housing demographic wave drives REVPOR ahead of cost growth. Core FFO grows from the $2.77 midpoint toward $3.00+ in 2027, the multiple re-rates toward scaled-SHOP peers, and the dividend resumes growth as FAD coverage improves. The neutralized staffing rule de-risks the residual SNF book. Insider buying near $35–38 proves prescient. Upside toward the $45 high analyst target, roughly +24% plus the ~6% yield.

**Bear Case.** SHOP operating risk materializes — labor inflation outpaces rate growth, occupancy stalls below the 89.7% base, or a managed community underdelivers — and the 25-person team proves under-resourced for an operating-economics platform. The dividend cushion erodes below comfortable coverage, forcing either a cut or further dilutive equity issuance near a depressed price. Prestige's payoff and SNF divestitures shrink high-yield income faster than redeployment replaces it. The stock drifts toward the 52-week low of $33.64, roughly -8%, with the yield as the floor.

The base case sits in between: LTC delivers FY26 guidance roughly as stated, the dividend holds, and the stock tracks within the analyst range as the market waits for multiple quarters of proven SHOP NOI before re-rating. This is a covered-yield holding with optionality on execution, not a conviction long.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on income rationale, with a 12-month reassessment horizon anchored to the SHOP NOI ramp.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of reported SHOP NOI tracking the upper half of the $65–77M FY26 guide, with same-community occupancy above 91% and REVPOR growth sustained above EXPOR by 200+ bps.
- Core FAD payout falling below ~75% (improved dividend cushion), signaling room for dividend growth and reduced reliance on dilutive equity.
- A halt or sharp reduction in ATM equity issuance, indicating self-funded growth at accretive spreads.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- SHOP occupancy declining below ~88% or SHOP NOI guidance cut, indicating the operating-model bet is impairing rather than enhancing returns.
- Dividend cut, or Core FAD payout exceeding ~95%.
- A new operator credit event among the top three concentrations (ALG, Prestige successor, or a SHOP manager) that threatens revenue continuity.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which should report the ~$250M of expected Q2 SHOP closings and an updated read on SHOP NOI versus the full-year guide.

## Conclusion

LTC is a financially sound small-cap health-care REIT mid-transformation, with a conservative balance sheet, a covered ~6.2% monthly dividend, and reaffirmed FY26 Core FFO guidance. The headline 38% FFO decline is a one-time non-cash artifact of the favorable Prestige modification and should not drive the assessment. Genuine open-market insider buying by the CIO and a director near current prices is a constructive signal, and the demographic backdrop for the SHOP pivot is favorable.

The constraint on a stronger call is that the entire appreciation thesis depends on executing an operating-economics business model — assuming labor, occupancy, and capex risk — with a 25-person team that is a relative latecomer to RIDEA platforms peers have already scaled, funded by steady equity dilution against a thin dividend cushion. The valuation is supported on yield but offers no clear re-rating catalyst until SHOP NOI is proven over multiple quarters. We rate LTC a HOLD for income, with a path to upgrade if the SHOP ramp delivers as guided and a path to downgrade if occupancy or coverage deteriorate.
