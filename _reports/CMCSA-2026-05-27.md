## Executive Summary

Comcast trades at $25.61, representing a 30% decline from its 52-week high of $36.66 and approximately 7-8x normalized earnings (adjusting for the $9.4B Hulu gain that inflated FY2025 reported EPS to $5.39). The stock offers a 5.2% dividend yield and the company generates $22B in annual free cash flow, providing meaningful downside support. The January 2026 Versant spinoff removed declining cable networks, leaving a cleaner portfolio of connectivity, streaming, sports, and theme parks.

The fundamental challenge is that Comcast's core broadband franchise is experiencing accelerating subscriber losses (711K net lost in 2025 vs. 411K in 2024) as T-Mobile/Verizon fixed wireless and AT&T fiber overbuilds erode the cable broadband pseudo-monopoly. Analyst estimates are declining: current year EPS has been revised down 4.1% over 90 days, and next year down 4.4%. The stock's decline from $33+ to $25 reflects this competitive deterioration, and the burden is on the bull case to explain what the market is missing.

While wireless growth (9.3M lines, +1.5M in 2025) and Epic Universe theme park performance (24% Q1 2026 revenue growth) provide partial offsets, these businesses are insufficient to reverse the trajectory of the broadband core. The investment case requires conviction that broadband losses stabilize and the low multiple provides sufficient margin of safety even if they don't.

## Company Overview

Following the January 2026 spinoff of Versant Media Group, Comcast operates through two primary segments:

**Connectivity & Platforms (~65% of revenue):** Broadband ($25.8B), wireless ($5.0B), video ($26.4B — declining), advertising ($3.7B), international connectivity ($5.0B), and business services ($10.2B). This segment serves 31.3M domestic broadband customers across 65M homes passed, with a 47.6% penetration rate that is trending lower.

**Content & Experiences (~35% of revenue):** NBC/Telemundo broadcast, Peacock streaming (44-46M paid subscribers), Sky Sports, NBCUniversal film/TV production, and Universal theme parks (including the newly opened Epic Universe in Orlando).

The company generated $123.7B in FY2025 revenue, $37.4B in adjusted EBITDA, and $21.9B in free cash flow. Market capitalization stands at approximately $91B on 3.56B diluted shares outstanding.

At $25.61 per share:

- Normalized P/E: ~7.3x (on ~$3.53 current year estimate)
- Forward P/E: ~6.7x (on $3.81 next year estimate)
- EV/EBITDA: ~5.1x (EV ~$180B post-Versant / estimated post-Versant EBITDA ~$35B)
- FCF yield: ~24% (on $21.9B FCF / $91B market cap; post-Versant FCF likely ~$18-20B)
- Dividend yield: 5.2%

**Peer Comparison:** The provided peer group (WBD, EA, TTWO, LYV, OMC) is poorly matched — these are entertainment/gaming companies, not cable operators. More relevant peers would be Charter Communications (CHTR, ~6x EBITDA) and AT&T (T, ~7x EBITDA). Against actual cable/telecom peers, Comcast's valuation is at the lower end but not radically mispriced.

**Bull Case Valuation:** If broadband losses stabilize in H2 2026 and wireless/theme parks continue their growth trajectory, a re-rating to 8x forward earnings on $3.80 EPS yields $30.40 (19% upside). At 6x EV/EBITDA on ~$35B, EV = $210B less $89B net debt = $121B equity / 3.56B shares = $34.

**Bear Case Valuation:** If broadband losses accelerate to 1M+/year and ARPU pressure intensifies, normalized EPS could compress to $3.00-3.20. At 6x earnings (appropriate for a structurally declining utility), stock = $18-19 (25-30% downside).

The valuation is cheap on absolute terms. The central uncertainty is whether 7x earnings appropriately discounts a business facing secular subscriber erosion in its core segment. The 5.2% dividend yield and double-digit FCF yield provide genuine downside support — Comcast is unlikely to cut its dividend given coverage of ~4x from FCF.

## Competitive Landscape

**Broadband — Deteriorating:**
Comcast's broadband moat is eroding from three directions simultaneously:
1. **Fixed Wireless Access (FWA):** T-Mobile and Verizon added over 4M combined FWA subscribers in 2025, many within Comcast's footprint. At $25-50/month with no contract, FWA undercuts Comcast's typical $60-80/month broadband pricing.
2. **Fiber Overbuilds:** AT&T's 2M+ fiber passings per year, plus smaller operators receiving BEAD federal funding, bring symmetrical gigabit service that cable's DOCSIS architecture cannot match without expensive upgrades.
3. **Regulatory/Subsidy Risk:** Federal broadband programs (BEAD) may subsidize new entrants within Comcast's existing service territory.

Management's response — free wireless lines, rate locks, DOCSIS 4.0 deployment — addresses symptoms but doesn't restore the structural monopoly. The company is transitioning from pricing power to a retention/bundling model with inherently lower margins.

**Streaming — Sub-Scale:**
Peacock at 44-46M paid subscribers competes against Netflix (285M+), Disney+ (150M+), and Amazon Prime. Its sports-centric strategy (NFL, NBA, Olympics, EPL) is expensive but provides differentiation. However, Peacock lost ~$1.1B in 2025 and widened losses in Q1 2026. Profitability depends on sustained subscriber growth and eventual scale economics that remain unproven.

**Theme Parks — Strengthening:**
Epic Universe creates the first genuine three-gate competitor to Disney in Orlando. Early results are strong (24% Q1 2026 revenue growth). Planned expansions (UK park 2031, Universal Kids Texas 2026, Horror Unleashed venues) suggest management views parks as the highest-return growth investment.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**
1. **Broadband subscriber acceleration risk:** If losses increase from 711K to 1M+ annually, the core business enters structural decline rather than managed deceleration. Fixed wireless capacity improvements and fiber deployment pace are external factors Comcast cannot control.

2. **Sports rights inflation:** The $93.8B in programming obligations (dominated by NBA 11-year deal, NFL, EPL) represent enormous fixed-cost commitments. If viewership declines or ad markets weaken, these become albatross obligations.

3. **Peacock's negative contribution:** At $1.1B+ annual losses, Peacock consumes ~5% of FCF. If subscriber growth stalls below 50-60M, the path to profitability extends indefinitely, creating a potential write-down scenario.

4. **Dual-class governance:** Brian Roberts' Class B stock provides permanent 33⅓% voting control regardless of economic ownership. This structure prevents activist intervention or hostile takeover, removing a potential value-realization mechanism.

5. **Rate sensitivity:** $98.9B in debt, while long-duration, eventually refinances. In the current elevated rate environment (3.8% average coupon vs. 5%+ market rates), refinancing of near-term maturities will increase interest expense.

**Options Market Signal:**
ATM IV at 33.2% is modestly rich versus 28.4% historical volatility (4.9pp spread). The put/call OI ratio of 0.58 indicates mildly call-heavy positioning — the options market is not pricing material downside fear at current levels. The contango term structure (near 33.2%, far 38.4%) reflects normal expectations without event-driven concern. IV rose 3pp from 30.2% to 33.2% as the stock declined 8.3% from $27.94 to $25.61 — proportionate to the move, not indicating informed put-buying. Short interest at 2.7% is minimal, suggesting no institutional short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($30-34, 17-33% upside):**
- Broadband losses stabilize in H2 2026 as Xfinity plan overhaul (rate locks + unlimited data + free wireless) takes effect
- Wireless growth to 12-15M lines by 2027 creates meaningful revenue contributor ($7-8B)
- Epic Universe drives sustained 15-20% theme park revenue growth through 2027
- Post-Versant clarity drives multiple re-rating from 7x to 8-9x forward earnings
- Capital returns ($11-12B/year) provide steady share count reduction of 5-6% annually
- At current levels, 5.2% yield and 24% FCF yield limit downside through yield support

**Bear Case ($18-20, 22-30% downside):**
- Broadband losses accelerate to 1M+ annually as FWA capacity improves and fiber deployment accelerates
- ARPU compression from free wireless and rate locks offsets any volume stabilization
- Peacock losses widen rather than narrow, requiring continued capital infusion
- Sports rights costs escalate while linear ad market shrinks
- Rising rates increase refinancing costs on near-term debt maturities
- Multiple compresses to 5-6x as market treats Comcast as a structurally declining utility

3. Peacock losses widening to >$2B annually without subscriber growth above 55M

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). This will be the first full quarter reflecting the Xfinity plan overhaul impact and Epic Universe summer ramp. Broadband subscriber trajectory in this quarter will be the most important single data point.

## Conclusion

Comcast presents a value trap/deep value ambiguity that the market has not resolved. The stock is quantifiably cheap at 7x normalized earnings with a 5.2% dividend yield and 24% FCF yield, backed by real infrastructure assets and $22B in annual cash generation. However, the cheapness reflects rational discounting of a business whose core broadband franchise is experiencing accelerating competitive erosion from fixed wireless and fiber — threats that management cannot fully neutralize.

The declining score trajectory (6.5 → 6.0 → 5.9 → current 5.63) reflects this ongoing deterioration. The Versant spinoff and Epic Universe opening are positive structural developments, but they have not arrested the broadband subscriber decline that drives the majority of earnings and cash flow. Estimate revisions are negative across all timeframes.

At current levels, the dividend yield provides a floor, and the FCF generation allows continued buybacks even in a declining revenue environment. This is a mature business whose competitive advantages are narrowing. The appropriate posture is HOLD with close monitoring of broadband subscriber trends, recognizing that stabilization would make this a compelling BUY, while continued acceleration of losses would justify exiting.
