**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Okta is a profitability and cash-flow story more than a growth story. Over three fiscal years the company swung from a -$812M GAAP operating loss (FY2023) to +$149M operating income (FY2026), lifted net income to +$235M, and generated $875M of free cash flow at a 30% margin — all while revenue growth decelerated from 22% to roughly 12%. That transformation was engineered by cost discipline: revenue grew 12% in FY26 while operating expenses grew about 2%, and every major opex line fell as a share of revenue. The balance sheet is clean ($2.59B cash and short-term investments against a single $350M convertible now settled in cash), and in January 2026 the board authorized the company's first-ever buyback, $1B, with $680M remaining.

The engine of Okta's historical value creation — existing-customer expansion — has been running down. Dollar-based net retention slid from 111% (FY24) to 107% (FY25) to 106% (FY26), stabilizing to 107% in Q1 FY27. Growth has matured into the low-teens. The bull case now rests on re-accelerating expansion through new product categories, principally identity for AI agents and non-human identities, a narrative that drove a 30%+ single-day rally on the May 28 print and carried the stock 41% higher over the trailing year. That narrative has not yet converted into dollar retention, which is the metric that would validate it.

The stock trades at roughly 99x trailing GAAP earnings and about 36x the $3.84 current-year consensus EPS — a full price for 12% revenue growth and eroding retention, even accounting for a high-quality cash-generation profile. The re-rating from a deeply de-rated, breach-overhung base was warranted as fundamentals mended; the incremental move now prices in an AI re-acceleration that remains a hypothesis. We rate OKTA a HOLD. The quality is real and the balance sheet is a genuine asset, but at the current multiple the risk/reward is balanced and the decisive evidence — a durable NRR inflection — is not yet in hand.

## Company Overview

Okta positions itself as the leading independent identity provider, the neutral alternative to Microsoft in an identity market that is consolidating around two poles. Because Okta is not tied to a cloud or productivity suite, it markets itself on neutrality and breadth (over 7,000 integrations in the Okta Integration Network). The business runs on two platforms: the Okta Platform for workforce identity (SSO, MFA, identity governance, and — via the September 2025 Axiom acquisition — privileged access management), and the Auth0 Platform for customer identity, embedded by developers into their own applications.

The model is roughly 98% subscription revenue on multi-year contracts invoiced annually in advance, which generates a large deferred-revenue float ($1.75B at Q1 FY27). International is about 20% of revenue, and the company reports a single segment. Management is deliberately shifting professional-services delivery to partners, which reduces services revenue but improves mix.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is the strongest part of the story. Our Financial Health assessment has climbed from 6.0 to 7.5 across recent reports as the profitability inflection matured.

**Revenue and margin.** FY26 revenue of $2,919M grew 12% versus 15% in FY25 and 22% in FY24, a monotonic deceleration. Against that, the cost discipline is striking: S&M fell from 46% of revenue to 35% over three years, R&D from 29% to 22%, and G&A from 20% to 15%. GAAP operating income went from -$74M in FY25 to +$149M in FY26. Subscription gross margin improved to 80%.

**Cash generation.** FY26 operating cash flow was $884M and free cash flow was $875M, a 30% margin, on just $9M of capex, an asset-light profile. FCF far exceeds net income, driven by the deferred-revenue float, the SBC add-back, and low capex. The financial-health screen shows an Altman Z of 3.04 (safe zone), a current ratio of 1.43, and equity at 72% of assets.

**Stock-based compensation.** SBC remains the principal quality-of-earnings caveat at $544M in FY26 (about 19% of revenue), though it is declining from $684M in FY24. GAAP net income is genuine, but a fully-diluted cash view is less flattering than the headline, and $874M of unrecognized SBC remains. The new buyback ($241M executed in Q1 FY27, 3.0M shares at an average of $79.56) is meaningfully offsetting dilution for the first time.

**Balance sheet and debt.** Total debt is essentially the $350M 2026 convertible (0.375% coupon), which management elected to settle in cash at the June 15, 2026 maturity, a funded, known event. The prior $510M 2025 notes were settled in cash in September 2025. Deleveraging is complete. Goodwill is 56% of assets ($5.5B), almost entirely from the 2021 Auth0 deal; there has been no impairment, but it is a large intangible base to monitor.

**Earnings quality and taxes.** The company maintains a full valuation allowance on U.S. deferred tax assets and has flagged a potential future release given sustained profitability. Such a release would create a one-time non-cash earnings benefit and would signal management's confidence in durable profitability, though it would not change cash economics.

**Q1 FY27 (April 2026).** Revenue was $765M (+11%), subscription +11%, operating income $56M (up from $39M), net income $74M ($0.42 diluted). cRPO grew 12% to $2,499M and total RPO grew 16% to $4,719M. The earnings-surprise record is consistent: four straight beats of roughly 4-6%, which supports confidence in the current consensus path.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is where the story is weakest and where the burden of proof for the bull case sits. Revenue growth has decelerated to 11-12% and net retention has ground lower for three consecutive years. NRR of 111%→107%→106%, with a first-stabilization tick to 107% in Q1 FY27, is the single most important number in the model, because Okta's historical value creation came from expanding within its installed base. Management attributes the erosion to macro-driven slower expansion by existing customers.

The re-acceleration thesis rests on new product categories: identity governance, privileged access management (the Axiom tuck-in versus incumbent CyberArk), and the centerpiece, identity for AI agents and non-human identities. The framing is that AI agents constitute a new "workforce" inside every organization, each requiring governed and secured identity, expanding the addressable market well beyond human seats. The products are in early access. This is a plausible and potentially large TAM expansion, but it is not yet visible in dollar retention, and the estimate-revision data confirms the market has not yet moved on it: current-year EPS estimates are flat over 30 days and up only 1.4% over 90 days; next-year up 1.2%. There is no outsized revision velocity here. This is a stable, low-teens grower, not an inflecting one, which places it outside the profile where rising estimates justify a re-rating.

The structural backdrop is mixed. On the tailwind side, enterprises continue replacing legacy on-prem IAM (customers with ACV >$100K grew to 5,180). On the headwind side, mature NRR signals the core expansion motion has run its course, and re-acceleration is entirely a function of landing new categories at scale, an unproven proposition. The February 2025 go-to-market reorganization ("further specializing our sales force") is a plausible source of near-term sales-cycle friction and is likely connected to the elevated executive turnover discussed below.

## Valuation Assessment

Okta trades at roughly 99x trailing GAAP earnings and about 36x the $3.84 current-year consensus EPS. On free cash flow it is more defensible — a $23.0B market cap against $875M FCF is roughly 26x FCF — but that still embeds continued mid-single-digit-plus FCF growth. The company pays no dividend.

Against Application Software peers, the picture is nuanced. The peer set (FICO 37.6x, WDAY 40.7x, PTC 11.4x, TYL 40.8x, DT 74.8x; median 40.7x P/E) shows wide dispersion, and OKTA's ~36x forward is below the peer median. But the peers carry that context: several are growing faster or carry cleaner earnings quality, and the median is skewed by DT's 74.8x. The more relevant comparison is growth-adjusted. At 12% revenue growth and eroding retention, a mid-30s forward multiple is not obviously cheap; the PEG on revenue growth is unattractive, and the implied premium requires the AI-agent re-acceleration to materialize.

The valuation dimension deserves particular skepticism. A "reasonable relative to peers" multiple has historically marked value traps rather than opportunities; cheapness relative to a de-rated software cohort is not an independent buy catalyst. The prior appreciation off the April low ($62.66) is not itself a reason to be bearish; the fundamental improvement was real. But the current multiple already discounts the mended fundamentals and a good portion of the AI optionality. What would make valuation attractive is a specific, falsifiable mispricing, and the strongest candidate, a valuation-allowance release, is a one-time non-cash accounting benefit, not a change in cash earnings power. We score valuation at 4.5: the multiple is full for the delivered growth, and the discount to a heterogeneous, de-rated peer set is not a sufficient reason to reward it.

## Competitive Landscape

Our competitive-position read has declined from 7.0 to 5.5 across recent reports, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend. Okta's moat rests on independence and neutrality plus its 7,000+ integrations and embeddedness in customers' security stacks. Those are real switching costs.

The persistent threat is Microsoft, which the 10-K names explicitly as the principal competitor. Microsoft bundles Entra ID into E3/E5 enterprise agreements at effectively zero marginal cost, the classic bundling squeeze; the 10-K warns that competitors may sell "at zero or negative margins." Okta's counter is best-of-breed neutrality, but the NRR erosion suggests the bundling pressure is showing up in slower expansion within accounts. The metric that matters — share and expansion within the installed base — has flattened at best. The AI-agent identity opportunity could reset the competitive frame in Okta's favor if it lands, because non-human identity governance is a greenfield category where neutrality is an advantage. But that is prospective. On current evidence, the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating at the expansion motion, which the score reflects.

## Risk Assessment

**Cybersecurity incidents — the asymmetric tail.** Okta is a high-value target because it sits in customers' security software supply chain, and it has already suffered two publicized incidents (a January 2022 third-party provider issue and the October 2023 breach of a third-party-hosted customer support system). The 10-K is unusually candid, stating management expects continued adverse impact and that any future incident, even minor, could damage the business. For a company that sells trust, a breach is the single risk most capable of impairing the thesis, and it is not fully priceable.

**Net-retention erosion.** The three-year NRR decline is a genuine fundamental risk to the growth algorithm, not a boilerplate concern. If the Q1 stabilization proves temporary, the low-teens grower becomes a high-single-digit grower and the multiple compresses.

**Governance and turnover.** The dual-class structure gives insiders (including CEO McKinnon) about 32% of voting power, with a 10-year sunset arriving April 12, 2027, after which control disperses. More immediately, there has been an elevated cadence of Item 5.02 executive/director-change 8-Ks across 2025-2026, plus a Transition and Separation Agreement with Larissa Schwartz (April 2026), coincident with the go-to-market reorganization. Individually routine; in aggregate a soft yellow flag on execution stability.

**Goodwill.** At $5.5B (56% of assets), the Auth0 goodwill base is large; no impairment to date, but worth watching if growth disappoints.

**Public-sector exposure** via FedRAMP and partners adds sensitivity to government budget cycles and shutdowns.

On balance, the known risks are meaningful but partly priced, and the breach tail is genuinely asymmetric and not fully reflected. We score risk factors at 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 61.5% against 55.1% one-year historical vol — modestly rich (6.4-point spread) but only in the 19th percentile of the current chain, so not extreme. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.8%, the term structure is in normal contango (near-term 61.5% below the 64.5% six-month), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.8%. The put/call OI ratio has risen from 0.37 to 0.76 since late May, alongside a 50% price gain, indicating some hedging into strength but nothing that signals directional conviction. Short interest is low at 5.1% of float with 2.2 days to cover; this is not a crowded or contested name. The options market is not pricing a binary catalyst; it reads as complacent-to-neutral, consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Okta is a durable, asset-light cash compounder with an 80% subscription gross margin, 30% FCF margin, a fortress balance sheet, and a newly initiated buyback that offsets dilution. If the AI-agent / non-human-identity category lands, it expands the TAM well beyond human seats, re-accelerates NRR back toward the mid-teens, and re-rates the stock as a growth-plus-profitability name. A U.S. valuation-allowance release adds a one-time reported-earnings boost and signals durable profitability. Bull target ~$175, matching the high end of the analyst range and implying ~26% upside, on renewed double-digit-plus growth at sustained margins.

**Bear case.** NRR erosion continues, the AI-agent narrative fails to convert into dollars, Microsoft's bundling grinds down expansion, and OKTA settles into a high-single-digit grower. At that point ~36x forward earnings is unsustainable and the multiple compresses toward the low-20s, and a new security incident is an ever-present tail that could accelerate the de-rating. Bear target ~$75 (the analyst low), implying ~46% downside.

**Base case.** Low-teens revenue growth persists, NRR stabilizes near 106-108% without meaningfully re-accelerating, margins hold, and buybacks support the share count. The stock is roughly range-bound as the market waits for evidence on the AI thesis. Base target ~$127, near the current price and slightly below the consensus mean, reflecting that the current price already discounts the mended fundamentals.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY27 print (late August/early September 2026), where the NRR reading and any commentary on AI-agent product traction will be decisive.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Dollar-based net retention re-accelerates to ≥110% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the expansion motor is turning back on.
- AI-agent / non-human-identity products move from early access to disclosed, quantified revenue contribution or bookings.
- A pullback toward the mid-$90s to low-$100s (roughly 25-27x forward EPS) that restores asymmetric upside on the existing cash-flow profile.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NRR breaks below 105% or revenue growth decelerates toward high single digits, signaling structural maturity at a premium multiple.
- A new material cybersecurity incident that impairs customer trust.
- Goodwill impairment on the Auth0 base, or a marked acceleration in executive departures signaling deeper GTM execution problems.

## Conclusion

Okta has turned a cash-burning hypergrowth machine into a profitable, asset-light, cash-generative business with a clean balance sheet and, for the first time, a shareholder-return program. That justifies the improvement in our Financial Health score to 7.5 and the constructive drift in the overall rating. But the recommendation history — four consecutive HOLDs — sets a higher bar for change, and the current data does not clear it in either direction. Growth has decelerated to the low teens, net retention has eroded for three years and only just stabilized, and the stock has re-rated on an AI-agent-identity thesis that is real in concept but absent from the dollar-retention numbers and from estimate revisions. The competitive threat from Microsoft's bundling and the asymmetric tail of a security incident round out a balanced picture.

We reiterate HOLD, classified as uncertain: the evidence supports neither a confident buy at this multiple nor an avoid on a business of this quality. The decisive data point is net retention. If it inflects up and the AI category begins producing quantifiable dollars, this becomes a BUY; if it slips further, the premium is unsupportable. Until then, own the quality with modest expectations and wait for either a better entry price or proof that the AI narrative is translating into revenue.
