**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TeraWulf is a vertically integrated digital-infrastructure operator that is converting a Bitcoin-mining power footprint into AI/high-performance-computing (HPC) data center capacity leased to hyperscale and neocloud tenants. The first quarter of 2026 marked the operational shift: HPC leasing revenue reached $21.0M (62% of total) and surpassed Bitcoin mining ($13.0M, 38%) for the first time, against a year-ago quarter that was 100% mining. The thesis is straightforward: control utility-scale land, interconnection rights, power, and cooling, then lease turnkey capacity under 10-to-25-year contracts. The execution required to deliver it is hard.

The stock has risen roughly 630% over twelve months, from $3.40 to $26.49, on the AI-pivot narrative, a Jefferies Buy initiation (May 14, 2026), and a string of capacity announcements (Hawesville KY, Muskie KY >1 GW). The fundamentals supporting that move are real at the unit level: both segments posted ~$10.2M of segment profit in Q1 and adjusted EBITDA was near breakeven at $(4.1)M. But the GAAP picture is severe: a $(427.7)M net loss, negative stockholders' equity of $(77.6)M, $5.725B of on-balance-sheet principal debt, and a further $1.3B at the off-balance-sheet Abernathy JV. The headline loss is dominated by non-cash and non-operating items, principally a $216.3M mark-to-market loss on Google warrants and $101.4M of stock compensation. The better the stock performs, the larger the GAAP warrant loss becomes.

This is a leveraged infrastructure-development bet funded by serial capital raises. The model works if leases commence on schedule and project financing stays open; it breaks if construction slips past contractual thresholds (which can let customers terminate without triggering the Google backstop) or if capital markets close. At $26.49 against a ~$13B market cap, with 26.2% short interest and a wide analyst target range ($27 low to $52 high), the binary outcome is not asymmetrically priced. We rate WULF a HOLD with an "uncertain" classification: the evidence does not support a confident call in either direction at the current price.

## Company Overview

TeraWulf (Nasdaq: WULF), headquartered in Easton, Maryland and led by Chairman/CEO Paul Prager, owns, develops, and operates U.S. digital infrastructure. The company is mid-transformation from a pure-play Bitcoin miner into an AI/HPC data center landlord. Its operating philosophy is "infrastructure-first": secure utility-scale land (owned or via long-term ground leases), interconnection rights, electrical and cooling systems, and increasingly on-site power generation, then lease high-density capacity to AI/hyperscale tenants under long-dated contracts. Management emphasizes in-house power and grid engineering as its differentiator, a capability reinforced by the May 2025 acquisition of Beowulf E&D (94 power-infrastructure employees brought in-house).

The asset base:

- **Lake Mariner (Barker, NY)** — the flagship, a repurposed coal plant on low-carbon NYISO Zone A power. As of 3/31/26: 145 MW legacy mining plus 60 critical-IT MW of HPC energized; near-term ~500 MW gross, up to 750 MW potential pending NYISO approval. 438 MW of HPC capacity is contracted here across two tenants.
- **Abernathy (TX)** — 168 MW, 100% pre-leased to Fluidstack on a 25-year lease, developed through a 50.1%-owned JV (TeraWulf paid $450M cash); targeted 2H 2026. The JV issued $1.3B of 7.25% Senior Secured Notes due 2030, accounted for under the equity method (off balance sheet).
- **Hawesville (KY)** — acquired February 2026 ($301.9M), up to 480 MW gross; construction 2026, phased through 2027; no customer signed.
- **Muskie (Eastern KY)** — announced ~May 26, 2026, >1 GW capacity, delivery from 2028.
- **Cayuga (Lansing, NY)** — up to 320 MW critical IT in the development pipeline.

The two HPC tenants are **Core42** (60 MW, all commenced, 10-year term plus two 5-year options) and **Fluidstack** (an independent AI cloud platform, not a Google entity), which contracted 378 MW at Lake Mariner and the full 168 MW at Abernathy, none of which had commenced as of 3/31/26.

## Financial Analysis

WULF is a heavy-capex buildout where GAAP earnings are deeply negative but segment-level economics have turned positive. Reading the income statement at face value materially overstates operating deterioration.

**Revenue.** Q1'26 total revenue was $34.0M, flat against $34.4M a year earlier, but the composition changed entirely. Mining revenue fell from $34.4M to $13.0M (curtailment, buildings repurposed for HPC, lower BTC price), offset by $21.0M of new HPC leasing revenue. Mining mined 168 BTC in Q1'26 (versus 372 in Q1'25) at a negative cash cost of $(381) per BTC, driven by $14.1M of demand-response credits during a Northeast cold snap. The company holds only 18 BTC and sells production immediately; mining is now an opportunistic cash-cow byproduct, not the core thesis.

**Profitability.** Q1'26 net loss was $(427.7)M, or $(1.01) per share, versus $(61.4)M a year earlier. FY2025 net loss was $(661.4)M. The Q1 loss is dominated by non-cash and non-operating items: a $216.3M mark-to-market loss on Google warrants (a function of the rising stock price), $101.4M of stock compensation (including ~$62.7M of PSU expense), $67.1M of interest, $28.5M of depreciation (including $11.9M accelerated), and $25.7M of impairment. Adjusted EBITDA was $(4.1)M, near breakeven, and both segments were profitable at the segment level (Digital Mining $10.2M; HPC Leasing $10.2M). The gap between the headline loss and segment profitability is the single most important nuance in valuing this business.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets reached $7.01B, roughly 9x the ~$788M at YE2024. Cash plus restricted cash was $3.09B ($2.63B unrestricted). But stockholders' equity is now negative: a total deficit of $(77.6)M and an accumulated deficit of $(1.42)B. Total principal debt is $5.725B, excluding the $1.3B at the Abernathy JV.

**Capital structure** (the central feature of this story):
- $3.2B 7.75% Senior Secured Notes due 2030 (effective rate 8.7%) — funds Lake Mariner HPC.
- $500M 2.75% Convertibles due 2030 (conversion price $8.48, now in the money and classified short-term).
- $1.0B 1.00% Convertibles due 2031 (conversion price $12.43, became convertible April 2026).
- $1.025B 0.00% Convertibles due 2032 (conversion price $19.94).
- $1.3B 7.25% JV Notes due 2030 (Abernathy, equity-method/off balance sheet).
- New $250M Morgan Stanley revolver (May 2026); $100M Hawesville bridge drawn and repaid April 2026 ($7.3M extinguishment loss flows to Q2).

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $(17.6)M (Q1'25 was +$56.5M, flattered by $90M of prepaid rent). Capex was $523M; total investing outflow was $712.8M (including $201M for Hawesville). The model is funded by continuous capital markets access — a $1.04B April equity raise plus $190.9M of ATM sales (program now exhausted) on the equity side, layered on the debt stack above.

The distress screens (Altman Z in the ~0.3 distress zone, negative book equity) are technically accurate but reflect a deliberately leveraged growth phase backed by $2.6B of cash and contracted, credit-enhanced revenue, rather than operational collapse. The financial-health risk is real but is a funding-and-execution risk, not an imminent-liquidity risk.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is the strongest part of the WULF story, and the estimate-revision data confirms it. Next-year consensus EPS swung from $0.01 to $0.18 over 30 days (the 90-day revision is +56.8%), reflecting the market beginning to model the transition from cash-burning buildout to lease-revenue harvest. The current-quarter estimate improved +26.2% over 30 days. The next-year revisions are genuine signals, but the magnitude (a +2,212% headline) is partly an artifact of a near-zero base, so the absolute number — a swing toward modest GAAP profitability — matters more than the percentage.

Capacity is the growth engine. Management reaffirmed a target of 250-500 MW of new contracted capacity annually. The contracted pipeline already includes 438 MW at Lake Mariner (Core42 + Fluidstack) plus 168 MW at Abernathy, with Hawesville (up to 480 MW), Muskie (>1 GW), and Cayuga (up to 320 MW critical IT) representing the development bench. Lake Mariner alone carries $1.03B of commenced minimum operating lease payments, and the larger Fluidstack and Abernathy capacity is not yet in that figure.

The AI infrastructure demand backdrop is broad-based: a clear cohort of Bitcoin miners is successfully repricing power footprints into AI capacity (Jefferies initiated WULF, Cipher, Hut 8, and Riot at Buy on exactly this thesis). The structural demand for the product is not the question. The question is whether TeraWulf can build it on time and finance it without diluting equity holders into the ground.

The growth durability is high at the sector level but company-specific execution is the binding constraint. Construction, power interconnection, supply chain, and permitting all need to go right across four simultaneous mega-projects. The Hawesville community petition (~1,100 signatures) is a reminder that permitting is not guaranteed. We score growth prospects at 7.5: the demand and contracted backlog are real, but the conversion from contracted MW to commenced revenue is unproven at this scale.

## Valuation Assessment

WULF cannot be valued on trailing earnings — they are deeply negative and dominated by non-cash items. Trailing and forward P/E both read -10.5x, which is meaningless. The relevant frame is a forward EV/EBITDA on contracted-and-ramping lease economics, and that requires a leap of faith on timing.

At $26.49 and ~495.5M shares (5/5/26 count), market cap is ~$13B. Add net debt — $5.725B principal less $2.63B unrestricted cash, plus the off-balance-sheet $1.3B JV obligation — and enterprise value sits in the ~$17-18B range, before accounting for the dilution embedded in three in-the-money convertible series (conversion prices of $8.48, $12.43, and $19.94, all below or near the current price) and 80.9M warrants (mostly Google penny warrants) plus 47.5M RSUs. Fully diluted, the share count and EV are materially higher than the headline figures.

The bull framework values WULF on stabilized lease revenue once Lake Mariner, Abernathy, and subsequent sites commence. If the company reaches several hundred MW of commenced critical IT at data-center lease economics, the multiple compresses rapidly as adjusted EBITDA scales from breakeven into the hundreds of millions. The Jefferies thesis and the wide analyst band (low $27, mean $34.79, median $32, high $52) reflect this: the consensus mean implies ~36% upside, but the dispersion is enormous because the outcome is binary on execution timing.

The bear framework notes that the equity already capitalizes a substantial fraction of the unbuilt pipeline. With negative book equity, debt at effective rates near 8.7% on the senior secured notes, and a funding model that depends on continued capital markets access, any slippage in lease commencement or any tightening in financing markets would force further dilution at lower prices. The convertibles are a one-way ratchet: in the money, they dilute; if the stock falls, the debt becomes a refinancing problem.

On balance, the valuation is not asymmetrically attractive at $26.49. The contracted backlog provides a floor of sorts, but the gap between current EV and the EBITDA that would justify it is wide and contingent on flawless multi-site execution over 2026-2028. We score valuation at 5.0: neither a clear mispricing on the upside nor a clear value trap on the downside, but a fairly-priced binary.

## Competitive Landscape

WULF sits in a well-defined cohort of Bitcoin miners repurposing secured power footprints into AI/HPC capacity. Jefferies' Buy initiation on WULF, Cipher, Hut 8, and Riot frames the entire group around "converting power footprints into AI capacity." Within that cohort, WULF's differentiation rests on three points: (1) low-carbon NY power diversified across Texas and Kentucky; (2) in-house power engineering from the Beowulf E&D acquisition; and (3) the Google credit backstop standing behind Fluidstack, which has unlocked investment-grade-style project financing rare in this sector.

The competitive risk is that hyperscalers and well-capitalized neoclouds are competing for the same power, GPUs, and interconnection queue positions. Power availability is the binding constraint across the entire AI data-center buildout, and WULF's secured-and-permitted megawatts are a genuine asset, but so are those of every other miner-turned-landlord, and the hyperscalers have far deeper balance sheets. The other structural risk, explicitly flagged in the 10-K, is AI model efficiency: more efficient models could reduce the high-density compute demand the entire thesis depends on. This is a low-probability but high-impact tail.

The competitive trajectory is improving: WULF is gaining contracted capacity, has secured marquee credit enhancement, and is diversifying geographically. But it is a challenger in a capital-intensive race against the best-funded companies in the world. We score competitive position at 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are substantial and several are not fully priced.

**The backstop is narrower than the headline.** Google's credit support of Fluidstack's lease obligations only becomes effective at lease commencement and does not cover all termination events. If construction is delayed past contractual thresholds, the customer can terminate without triggering the backstop. This concentrates the execution risk on TeraWulf's own buildout completion — the risk the market may be underweighting given the "Google-backed" framing.

**Customer concentration is acute.** 100% of current HPC revenue is from a single customer (Core42), and the growth thesis hinges overwhelmingly on Fluidstack across Lake Mariner and Abernathy. If Fluidstack's leases fail to commence on schedule, or if Fluidstack itself encounters financial difficulty before the backstop is effective, the revenue ramp stalls.

**Leverage plus negative equity plus serial dilution** is the central tension, funded by continuous capital raises. Share count rose from ~425M (3/31) to 495.5M (5/5), and three in-the-money convertible series plus the 80.9M warrants and 47.5M RSUs sit on top of that. The $2.6B cash cushion buys time but does not eliminate the dependence on open capital markets.

**Execution risk** spans four simultaneous mega-projects (Lake Mariner, Abernathy, Hawesville, Muskie): construction, interconnection, supply chain, tariffs, and permitting (note the Hawesville community opposition).

**Governance and related-party density.** The Beowulf E&D acquisition was from an entity controlled by management; the Lake Mariner and Cayuga ground leases are with related parties; the company funds an employee trust equal to 2% of annual HPC capex. These were vetted by an independent committee, but the concentration of insider relationships under a dominant Chairman/CEO warrants ongoing scrutiny.

On the positive side, the August/October 2024 short-seller chapter is closed: the SEC inquiry into the company's zero-carbon energy claims was closed with no enforcement on January 31, 2025. We score risk factors at 3.5, reflecting the cluster of specific, material, and partially under-priced binary risks.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 107.2% against one-year historical volatility of 99.6% — rich, with IV in the 88th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 107.2% versus six-month 93.1%), which signals the market is pricing imminent event risk, consistent with a stock that moves sharply on each capacity announcement and financing event. The put/call skew is balanced (0.3% near-term) and put/call volume (0.27) and open-interest (0.18) ratios are call-heavy, so despite the short interest the options market is not positioned for a directional collapse. The implied near-term move is ±6.3%. The combination of high IV, balanced skew, and call-tilted positioning suggests the market views this as a high-variance, two-sided situation rather than a clear short, which aligns with our HOLD.

### Insider Signal

The insider picture leans cautionary but is partly mechanical. All four named executives converted large PSU tranches in April-May 2026 as stock-price hurdles were met. Beyond tax withholding, CEO Prager conducted substantial open-market selling across March ($16-17), April ($20-21), and May ($24-27), and CFO Fleury sold ~600k shares in March (~$9.7M, taking one account to zero). Director Bucella made small offsetting open-market purchases (~$25k each in mid-March) around $15-16. Against rising next-year estimates and an operational shift, isolated executive selling during the rally is largely routine wealth management and is low-signal on its own. But the magnitude and breadth of CEO/CFO selling into the rally, set against only token director buying and a $39.4M CEO comp package (up from $6.9M a year earlier), is a governance-and-alignment caution. We score management quality at 4.5.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The AI data-center demand backdrop is structural and broad-based, and WULF is a confirmed beneficiary with contracted backlog, marquee credit enhancement, and a secured, permitted power footprint. The Q1 crossover (HPC > mining) is the first hard proof of the transition. Both segments are profitable at the segment level and adjusted EBITDA is near breakeven before the buildout fully commences. If Lake Mariner's 438 MW, Abernathy's 168 MW, and subsequent Hawesville/Muskie capacity commence on schedule, adjusted EBITDA scales from breakeven into the hundreds of millions over 2026-2028, the multiple compresses, and the equity re-rates. Next-year consensus EPS has already turned positive, and the analyst high target of $52 captures this outcome — roughly double the current price.

### Bear Case

The equity already capitalizes a large fraction of the unbuilt pipeline. With negative book equity, debt at near-8.7% effective rates on the senior notes, three in-the-money convertible series, and a model dependent on continuous capital raises, any slippage in lease commencement or tightening in financing markets forces dilution at lower prices. The Google backstop does not protect against construction-delay terminations, the exact risk concentrated on WULF's own execution. Customer concentration is extreme. The headline GAAP losses, while non-cash-heavy, reflect a warrant liability that grows with the stock price and a dilution cadence that erodes per-share value. In a bear scenario where one or two large leases slip and the financing window narrows, the stock retraces toward the analyst low of $27 or below as the market re-prices execution risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale: the binary execution outcome is not asymmetrically priced at $26.49, and we lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Fluidstack leases at Lake Mariner commence on schedule (commencement converts contracted MW to recognized revenue and activates the Google backstop), with management confirming on-time delivery.
- Abernathy commences in 2H 2026 as targeted, and a customer is signed for Hawesville's 480 MW.
- Adjusted EBITDA turns sustainably positive (two consecutive quarters) without a fresh equity raise, demonstrating the model can self-fund incremental capacity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any disclosed construction delay past a contractual threshold that gives Fluidstack a termination right outside the backstop, or any Fluidstack counterparty stress.
- A forced equity raise at a price meaningfully below the current level, signaling deteriorating financing access.
- Next-year consensus EPS reverting to negative on falling estimate revisions, indicating the inflection is slipping.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August), with interim monitoring of Abernathy commencement progress and any Hawesville customer announcement. The 2026-2028 buildout window is the period over which the thesis resolves.

## Conclusion

TeraWulf has executed the most important step in its transformation: HPC leasing revenue now exceeds Bitcoin mining, and both segments are profitable at the segment level. The demand backdrop is structural, the contracted backlog is real, and the Google-backstopped Fluidstack relationship has unlocked financing that peers cannot match. Those are genuine positives that justify the company's place in the AI-infrastructure cohort.

But the equity at $26.49 prices in a substantial fraction of an unbuilt pipeline, funded by $5.7B+ of debt, negative book equity, and continuous dilution, with the headline GAAP losses obscuring — and partly inflated by — the underlying near-breakeven operating economics. The Google backstop is narrower than the marketing implies, customer concentration is acute, and the multi-site buildout carries real execution and financing risk. The options market prices high two-sided variance, short interest sits at 26.2%, and insiders have been net sellers into the rally. None of this resolves to a confident long or short at the current price.

We rate WULF a **HOLD**. The risk/reward is symmetric: the path to the $52 high target runs through flawless multi-site execution, while the path to the $27 low runs through any single lease slippage or financing hiccup. We would become buyers on confirmation that the Fluidstack leases commence on schedule and adjusted EBITDA turns sustainably positive, and sellers on a forced dilutive raise or a disclosed construction-delay termination risk.
