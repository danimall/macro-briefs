## Executive Summary

REX American Resources is a pure-play ethanol producer trading at $49.93 per share, having appreciated 137% from its 52-week low of $19.44 over the past year. The rally was driven by a record Q4 FY2025 earnings report (EPS $2.50 full-year vs. $1.65 prior year) and the recognition of $28.1M in 45Z Clean Fuel Production Tax Credits. The company maintains a fortress balance sheet with $375.8M in cash/investments against essentially zero debt, and is executing two major capital projects: a 33% capacity expansion at One Earth (150M → 200M gallons/year) and a carbon capture & sequestration (CCS) facility that could unlock up to $1.00/gallon in additional tax credits.

The fundamental question for REX at current levels is whether the stock's 20x trailing P/E (28-30x on normalized, non-45Z-boosted earnings) adequately prices in the binary CCS permitting outcome and the inherent volatility of ethanol crush spreads. The company's cash position represents roughly 23% of its market cap, providing downside protection, but the commodity-dependent earnings stream and policy-contingent upside create a wide distribution of outcomes that makes conviction difficult at current prices. The EPA Class VI permit decision has been pushed to September 2026, extending the catalyst timeline.

## Company Overview

REX American Resources is headquartered in Dayton, Ohio and operates through equity investments in three ethanol production entities: One Earth Energy (76.1% owned, Gibson City, IL), NuGen Energy (99.7% owned, Marion, SD), and Big River Resources (10.3% equity method, multiple Iowa locations). In aggregate, REX's effective ownership represented approximately 294 million gallons of ethanol shipped in fiscal year 2025 (ended January 31, 2026).

Revenue is derived from ethanol (~77%), dried distillers grains (~14%), distillers corn oil (~8%), and modified distillers grains (~1%). The company is explicitly undiversified — its SEC filings identify this concentration as a primary risk factor. REX previously operated in refined coal (ceased 2021) and retail (sold 2007).

## Growth Analysis

REX's growth thesis rests on three pillars with varying probability of delivery:

**1. One Earth Capacity Expansion (High Probability, Moderate Impact):**
Nameplate capacity increasing from 150M to 200M gallons/year. $107.6M spent, nearing completion, expected operational in FY2026. At $0.45-$0.50/gallon crush spread (recent levels), incremental 50M gallons = ~$22-25M pre-tax income addition. This is relatively well-priced into the stock.

**2. 45Z Tax Credits (High Probability Through 2029, Significant Impact):**
REX earned $0.10/gallon in 45Z credits in FY2025 ($28.1M total). On 200M gallons at One Earth alone, this represents ~$20M annually going forward. The credits were established through 2029 via OBBBA. Key risk: legislative repeal or regulatory modification.

**3. Carbon Capture & Sequestration (Low-to-Medium Probability, Transformative Impact):**
If CCS is successfully permitted and operational, REX could reduce its carbon intensity score by 30-35 points, potentially qualifying for up to $1.00/gallon in 45Z credits. On 200M gallons = $200M/year — more than the current market cap relative to trailing earnings. However:
- EPA Class VI permit decision pushed from June to September 2026
- Illinois imposed CO2 pipeline moratorium until July 2026+
- NuGen's CCS depends on Summit Carbon Solutions, whose North Dakota permits were voided in March 2026
- $58.4M already invested, creating write-off risk if unsuccessful

The CCS optionality is the core bull case. At current prices, the market appears to assign 15-25% probability to full CCS success, which seems roughly appropriate given the regulatory obstacles.

## Valuation Assessment

At $49.93 per share with 32.9M shares outstanding, REX's market cap is ~$1.64B.

**Trailing metrics:**
- P/E: 20.0x on $2.50 reported EPS
- Adjusted P/E: 28-30x on normalized EPS of ~$1.65-$1.80 (excluding Q4 45Z credit catch-up timing benefit)
- EV/EBITDA: ~10.5x (enterprise value reduced by net cash)
- Price/Book: 2.7x

**Peer comparison:**
The peer median P/E is 10.6x (PARR at 6.5x, PBF at 11.0x, DINO at 10.6x). REX trades at nearly 2x the peer median P/E on reported earnings. The premium reflects: (1) zero debt vs. levered peers, (2) CCS optionality, (3) 45Z credit positioning, and (4) growth capex nearing completion. Whether a 100% premium is justified depends heavily on CCS outcomes.

**Cash-adjusted valuation:** Stripping out $375.8M in cash, enterprise value is ~$1.27B against ~$88.6M net income (including 45Z) = ~14.3x ex-cash P/E. Against normalized earnings of $54-60M (excluding 45Z timing and using normalized tax rates), ex-cash P/E is 21-24x. Still expensive for an ethanol producer.

The 137% appreciation over 12 months was driven by genuine fundamental improvement (record earnings, 45Z credits, expansion nearing completion). However, at current multiples, the stock has priced in much of the visible upside and is now dependent on CCS permitting for the next leg higher.

## Competitive Landscape

REX operates in a structurally challenged industry — U.S. ethanol production capacity of ~18.5 billion gallons exceeds mandated volumes of 15.0 billion gallons by ~3.5 billion gallons. This overcapacity creates persistent margin pressure during demand downturns.

REX's competitive advantages:
- Among the lowest carbon intensity scores in the industry via Energy Attribute Certificates
- 22 consecutive profitable quarters (5.5+ years), demonstrating operational discipline
- Zero debt enables investment through downturns while levered competitors retreat
- Management claims "top" operational performance metrics
- Strong corn oil yields and pricing power (+23% price realization in FY2025)

Competitive threats:
- Large integrated grain companies (ADM, Cargill) with superior scale and diversification
- EV adoption poses structural demand headwind to ethanol by mid-2030s
- If CCS fails to materialize, competitors with successful CCS programs gain permanent cost advantage via higher 45Z credits
- Trade risks: 36% of U.S. ethanol exports go to Canada; tariff retaliation could impact export markets

## Risk Assessment

**Binary CCS Risk:** $58.4M invested in a project facing multiple regulatory delays. Illinois moratorium, EPA timeline slippage, and Summit Carbon Solutions' legal setbacks in the Dakotas create meaningful probability of partial or full write-off.

**Crush Spread Volatility:** Corn represents 74% of COGS with only ~4 months of forward visibility on pricing. A $1/bushel corn price increase reduces margins by ~$0.35/gallon across 200M+ gallons = $70M+ annual impact. REX cannot hedge this risk beyond a single quarter.

**IRS Audit Exposure:** ~$82.7M in contested tax credits ($58.2M refined coal + $24.5M R&E). While management is defending vigorously, adverse resolution could materially reduce cash balances.

**45Z Policy Risk:** The $28.1M annual credit stream depends on legislation remaining intact through 2029. Political changes or Treasury regulatory interpretations could reduce or eliminate this income source.

**EV Structural Headwind:** California and multiple states banning ICE vehicles by mid-2030s. While not immediate, this creates a declining terminal value framework for ethanol assets.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($65-70, +30-40%):**
- CCS receives EPA Class VI permit in September 2026, enabling qualification for $0.50-$1.00/gallon in enhanced 45Z credits
- One Earth expansion to 200M gallons ramps smoothly, driving 25-30% volume growth
- Crush spreads remain favorable with low corn prices and elevated oil prices supporting ethanol pricing
- Buyback accelerates post-capex completion, reducing share count meaningfully
- Market re-rates from commodity P/E to "green fuel" P/E as CCS optionality de-risks

**Bear Case ($32-35, -30-35%):**
- CCS permit denied or indefinitely delayed; $58.4M write-off; competitive disadvantage
- 45Z credits reduced or eliminated through legislative/regulatory action
- Crush spreads compress: corn prices rise while ethanol prices fall
- Canadian tariffs implemented on U.S. ethanol exports (36% of export market)
- IRS audit resolves adversely ($82.7M exposure)
- Stock reverts to peer-comparable 10-12x P/E on ~$1.65 normalized EPS = $16.50-$20/share (plus cash of ~$11.40/share = $28-31)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $38-40 range (25-30x normalized, reducing to 15-18x if 45Z becomes recurring), providing better risk/reward into September CCS decision
- EPA signals positive momentum on Class VI permit (public comment period completion, draft approval)
- Crush spreads widen further with corn falling below $4.00/bushel while ethanol holds >$2.00/gallon

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CCS permit formally denied or delayed beyond 2027
- 45Z credits legislatively repealed or materially reduced in scope
- Crush spreads compress below $0.30/gallon for 2+ consecutive quarters
- Stock reaches $55+ without fundamental catalyst advancement (pure multiple expansion into binary outcome)

**Reassessment timeline:** September 2026 (EPA permit decision) is the primary catalyst date. Q4 FY2026 earnings (expected March 2027) will reveal expansion contribution.

## Conclusion

REX American Resources is a well-managed ethanol producer with an exceptionally clean balance sheet and meaningful optionality in CCS-enhanced tax credits. The company's execution on its expansion program and positioning for 45Z credits has been sound. However, at $49.93 the stock trades at a significant premium to ethanol peers (20x trailing vs. 10.6x peer median), pricing in success scenarios that face meaningful regulatory and political hurdles. The remaining upside is concentrated in the binary CCS outcome with a September 2026 decision timeline.

The options market's rich IV, backwardated term structure, and elevated put demand confirm uncertainty about sustainability at current levels. With limited analyst coverage and limited institutional following, price discovery is driven by retail flows and binary catalyst positioning.

For investors seeking ethanol exposure with CCS optionality, the risk/reward improves materially at $38-42 (where downside is protected by cash + normalized earnings and upside captures CCS at a more favorable entry). At current prices, HOLD is appropriate — the balance sheet protects against permanent capital loss while the CCS timeline plays out, but the margin of safety is insufficient for new capital deployment.
