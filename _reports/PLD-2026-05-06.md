## Executive Summary

Prologis is the global leader in logistics real estate with 1.3 billion square feet of owned and managed space across 20 countries. Q1 2026 delivered record leasing (64 million square feet in logistics plus $1.3B in data-center build-to-suit starts), a beat-and-raise quarter ($1.50 Core FFO vs. $1.48 estimate), and $1.3B in new data center development starts that signal a material strategic expansion. The stock trades at $142.55, approximately 23x the midpoint of 2026 Core FFO guidance ($6.07-$6.23), which represents a premium to industrial REIT peers but one justified by the company's superior growth profile, balance sheet, and data center optionality.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) an 18% embedded lease mark-to-market that delivers 4-7% annual same-store NOI growth for the next 3-5 years without requiring any further market rent appreciation, (2) a data center development pipeline with a 5.6 GW power pipeline (1.7 GW secured, 3.9 GW advanced-stage) that could add an estimated $200-400M in incremental annual NOI by 2028-2029 at yields superior to logistics, and (3) a balance sheet rated A/A2 with approximately 8.5-year average debt maturity and 3.2% weighted average cost that provides a structural cost-of-capital advantage. The primary risk is that ~23x forward FFO leaves limited margin of safety if market rents turn materially negative or if the data center pivot faces execution delays.

## Company Overview

Prologis operates through two segments: Real Estate (owning and operating ~2,968 consolidated logistics properties encompassing 645M sq ft) and Strategic Capital (managing $62.4B in co-investment ventures with institutional partners). Revenue for FY2025 was $8.79B, up 7.2% YoY, with Real Estate contributing approximately 93% and Strategic Capital approximately 7%. The company's top customers (consolidated portfolio) are Amazon (6.3% of net effective rent), Home Depot (2.8%), FedEx (1.8%), UPS (1.0%), and Walmart (0.8%) — well-diversified with the top 10 representing only 16.3%.

The company's strategic pivot into data center development, announced over the past 18 months and now accelerating with $1.3B in Q1 2026 starts alone, leverages Prologis's existing land bank (sites with power access near population centers), development capabilities, and capital recycling model. Management has disclosed a 5.6 GW power pipeline across multiple sites including a massive Georgia campus (4.3M sq ft, nine buildings).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and NOI Growth:**
FY2025 total revenue of $8.79B grew 7.2% from FY2024. Real Estate NOI expanded 8.2% to $6.19B, driven by 50.1% net effective rent change on rollover and 95.6% occupancy. Same-store NOI growth was 4.7% net effective / 5.7% cash in Q4 2025. Q1 2026 continued this trajectory, with management raising full-year same-store growth guidance to 6.25-7.0% cash / 4.75-5.50% net effective.

**Core FFO:**
FY2025 Core FFO was $5.81/share (+4.5% YoY from $5.56/share). Core FFO excluding Net Promote Income was $5.86/share (+6.0% from $5.53/share), reflecting the absence of meaningful promote revenue in FY2025. Q1 2026 came in at $1.50/share (+5.6% YoY), beating consensus by $0.02. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised to $6.07-$6.23/share, implying 4.5-7.2% growth off the headline FY2025 figure. The deceleration from FY2025's Core FFO growth reflects: (a) rising interest expense (up 15% in FY2025 to $1.02B as new debt issued at 4.2% vs. portfolio average of 3.2%), and (b) normalization of development contribution margins.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total consolidated debt of $35.0B carries an approximately 8.5-year weighted average maturity and 3.2% weighted average interest rate — among the lowest in REITs globally. Total equity is approximately $57.8B. The company is rated A (S&P) / A2 (Moody's) with stable outlook. Approximately $1.8B in consolidated debt matures in 2026, easily manageable with $7.6B in liquidity. The multi-currency debt structure (35% EUR, 44% USD, 8% JPY) provides natural hedging for the global portfolio.

**Cash Flow and Capital Allocation:**
Operating cash flow of $5.01B (FY2025) well-exceeds the approximately $3.87B total dividend and distribution obligation ($4.04/share). The payout ratio versus headline Core FFO of $5.81/share is approximately 69.5%, providing ample retained cash for development. Development value creation remains compelling: 2025 stabilizations yielded 6.7% on cost with 25.1% margin — meaning historically, each $1B deployed in stabilizations at these economics creates approximately $250M in value, though future margins may vary (the year-end development portfolio had an estimated 18.2% margin, while Q1 2026 starts had a 32.0% estimated margin).

**Earnings Surprise Pattern:**
Three beats in the last four quarters on a Core FFO basis, with the miss (Q2 2025, -4.7%) being modest. The Q4 2025 GAAP EPS beat of 117.6% reflects timing of disposition gains and promotes rather than recurring operations; Q4 Core FFO was $1.44/share, down from $1.50 in Q4 2024. The Q1 2026 Core FFO beat of $0.02/share ($1.50 vs. $1.48 estimate) demonstrates continued execution and supports the positive estimate revision trend.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Engine — Embedded Mark-to-Market:**
Average in-place rent is approximately $6.48/sq ft; market rents are approximately $7.70/sq ft. With ~13-15% of the portfolio rolling annually (average lease term ~5 years), even zero market rent growth delivers 4-6% annual same-store NOI growth for 3-5 years.

**Development Pipeline:**
$5.57B in development TEI at year-end 2025 (Prologis share), 55.8% pre-leased, with an estimated stabilized yield of 6.3% and approximately $323M in annualized estimated NOI at stabilization. Q1 2026 added $1.78B in Prologis-share starts (approximately $1.23B data centers + approximately $550M logistics). The land bank of $37.3B TEI (consolidated) provides a multi-decade runway.

**Data Center Upside:**
This is the key forward growth variable not fully reflected in consensus estimates. Data center cap rates in the market are generally tighter than logistics, meaning assets developed at attractive yields on cost create significant value. Based on analyst assumptions regarding conversion rates, development costs per MW, and stabilized yields, the data-center pipeline could add an estimated $200-400M in incremental annual NOI by 2028-2029, representing an estimated $0.30-$0.60/share in annual Core FFO contribution. These figures represent scenario analysis based on assumed deployment pace and yields, not company-guided amounts, and actual results will depend on LOI conversion, power availability, construction timelines, and market conditions.

**Estimate Revisions:**
Core FFO estimate revisions have trended positively in recent months, with the Q1 2026 guidance raise supporting continued upward revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $142.55, PLD trades at:
- 23.1x midpoint 2026E Core FFO ($6.15)
- ~2.8% current dividend yield
- ~3.1% trailing cap rate on total enterprise value

Relative to industrial REIT peers (EGP at 36.0x, REXR at 37.6x, STAG at 29.5x, FR at 23.8x trailing P/E), PLD's 35.3x trailing P/E (which reflects GAAP EPS including depreciation) appears in-line. However, trailing P/E is inappropriate for REITs; the FFO multiple comparison is more relevant. At 23x forward FFO, PLD commands approximately a 15-20% premium to FR (the closest comparable on FFO basis) but a discount to REXR and EGP on an FFO-adjusted basis.

The premium is justified by: (1) unmatched global scale, (2) A/A2 balance sheet enabling lower cost of capital, (3) data center optionality that is not present in any peer, and (4) superior 18% mark-to-market providing multi-year growth visibility. The discount to where PLD traded in 2022 (27-28x FFO) reflects the higher rate environment and modest market rent deceleration.

The stock is approximately 2% below its 52-week high of $145.44 and has appreciated 33% over the past 12 months. Analyst consensus target is $149.40 (median $152), representing 5-7% upside. Near-term upside appears constrained on a 6-month basis, but the 12-18 month outlook incorporating data center NOI contribution and continued rent rollover supports a higher fair value range of $155-165 (25-27x 2027E FFO).

The specific mispricing: consensus 2027 estimates do not fully incorporate the data center contribution ($1.3B in BTS starts at attractive yields = incremental NOI not yet in models for stabilization in 2028). This represents additional FFO growth additive to the base case.

## Competitive Landscape

Prologis's competitive position is exceptional and improving. The company operates at a scale no competitor can match. This scale provides:

- **Cost of capital advantage:** A/A2 rating yields borrowing costs 50-100bps below smaller peers.
- **Customer access:** Large tenants requiring global logistics networks can only be served by PLD.
- **Development pipeline:** The $37B land bank in infill locations is irreplaceable.
- **Data center conversion potential:** Existing sites with power, fiber, and permitting relationships give PLD a differentiated position in logistics-to-data-center conversion.

Customer retention of ~70%+ and 95.6% occupancy confirm that tenants view PLD's assets as mission-critical. The 50.1% rent change on rollover reflects genuine pricing power that smaller peers cannot consistently replicate.

The competitive trajectory is improving due to the data center pivot. Specialized data center operators (Equinix, Digital Realty) have deep operating, interconnection, and customer capabilities that PLD does not yet possess; however, they generally lack PLD's land bank, power-positioning pipeline, and logistics-scale development expertise. Other industrial REITs lack the scale to justify a dedicated data center platform. PLD occupies a differentiated position at the intersection of logistics and data center development, though it faces meaningful competition from both specialized data center developers and hyperscaler self-builds.

## Risk Assessment

**Market Rent Deceleration (MODERATE):** Management acknowledges "recent quarters of lower, or in some cases negative, market rental growth." If rents decline 5%+ from current levels, the mark-to-market compresses toward 10-12%, reducing the growth runway from 3-5 years to 2-3 years. This would warrant multiple compression from 23x to 20-21x. Probability: 25-30% over the next 12 months.

**Rising Interest Costs (MODERATE):** New debt at 4.2% vs. portfolio average of 3.2% creates gradual headwind. With only ~$1.8B in consolidated debt maturing in 2026, this is a slow-moving risk, but interest expense could grow at 8-12% annually for the next 3 years, consuming 100-150bps of same-store NOI growth. Fully priced at current FFO guidance.

**California Concentration (MODERATE):** 30.6% of consolidated assets and 31.9% of NOI in a single state. Regulatory, tax, or economic shock specific to California could materially impact results. Diversification is occurring but slowly.

**Data Center Execution (MODERATE):** Construction cost overruns, permitting delays (Georgia was "contentious"), or power procurement challenges could delay stabilization and reduce margins. Competition from specialized operators with deeper data-center operating expertise adds risk. However, build-to-suit structures (60.9% of starts) substantially mitigate demand risk.

**Tariff-Related Demand Shock (LOW):** Record Q1 2026 leasing suggests customers are adapting to trade uncertainty rather than reducing real estate commitments. Near-shoring trends could actually be net positive for U.S. logistics space demand.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($155-165, +9-16%):**
Data center development starts sustain at elevated levels through 2026-2027, consistent with company guidance of $4.5-$5.5B in total owned & managed starts (with data centers representing a substantial portion). Market rents stabilize and begin modest recovery as limited new industrial supply (elevated construction costs + higher rates discourage speculative development) tightens available inventory. Core FFO reaches $6.70-$7.00 by 2027, and the market re-rates PLD toward 24-25x on demonstrated data center execution. Dividend grows 5-7% annually. Total return: 12-18% annualized over 18 months.

**Bear Case ($120-125, -12-16%):**
Market rents decline 5-8% as tariff-related demand destruction hits logistics volumes. Data center LOIs convert at lower rates than expected due to power procurement delays or competitive pressure. Core FFO growth stalls at 2-3% annually. Multiple compresses from 23x to 20x on slower growth outlook. 30Y rates remain above 5%, capping REIT re-rating broadly.

**Base Case ($152-158, +7-11%):**
Same-store NOI grows 6-7% in 2026 per guidance. Data center pipeline stabilizes on schedule, contributing modestly by late 2027. Core FFO of $6.35-$6.50 for 2027 (above current consensus). Multiple holds at 23-24x as data center optionality becomes better understood. Dividend growth of 5%. Total return: 10-14% including the 2.8% yield.

**Base case target:** $155 in 12-18 months. Derived from 24x 2027E Core FFO of $6.50 = $156.

**Bull case target:** $165 (25x × $6.60 2027E FFO, reflecting full data center premium). +16% from current.

**Bear case target:** $122 (20x × $6.10 2026E FFO if growth disappoints). -14% from current.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: (1) Q2/Q3 2026 earnings confirming same-store NOI at 6.5-7.0%, (2) Additional data center start announcements through 2026, (3) Market rent stabilization data from major markets (tracked in CBRE/JLL quarterly reports).

**Upside/downside:** +9% to base, +16% to bull; -14% to bear. Risk/reward of approximately 1.2:1 to base case, improving to 1.5:1 including the 2.8% dividend yield on a 12-month basis.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Same-store NOI growth declines below 4% for two consecutive quarters (would indicate mark-to-market exhausting faster than expected)
2. Data center LOI conversion rate falls below 50% or management reduces development start guidance by >25%
3. Occupancy drops below 93% (would indicate demand shock)

## Conclusion

Prologis offers a compelling risk-adjusted return at current levels, driven by visible organic growth (18% embedded mark-to-market), a strategic data center expansion not fully in consensus estimates, and a balance sheet that provides a durable cost-of-capital moat. At 23x forward FFO, near-term upside is constrained by proximity to analyst targets, but the 12-18 month path to $155+ is supported by specific, measurable catalysts (data center stabilizations, continued 6-7% same-store growth, estimate revisions). The 2.8% dividend yield provides income support while waiting for re-rating.

The key differentiation from a HOLD is the data center optionality: $1.3B in Q1 2026 starts with a 5.6 GW power pipeline represents a growth vector that consensus has not fully modeled, and that peer industrial REITs cannot replicate. This creates a specific, identifiable source of upward earnings revisions over the next 12-18 months that justifies an active overweight rather than neutral positioning.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The data-center NOI contribution estimates ($200–400M / $0.30–$0.60 per share) are analyst scenario assumptions, not company-guided figures; if actual conversion and stabilization materially lag these assumptions, the incremental upside supporting the overweight recommendation would be diminished.
