**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Somnigroup International (formerly Tempur Sealy, renamed February 2025) is the world's largest bedding company and, since the February 2025 close of the ~$5.1B Mattress Firm acquisition, a vertically integrated manufacturer-retailer. The reported financials are dominated by purchase accounting rather than organic performance: FY25 revenue rose 51.6% to $7.48B almost entirely from consolidating ~$3.5B of Mattress Firm stub-period sales, while GAAP diluted EPS fell 14.8% to $1.84 on share dilution (178M → 209M) and a near-doubling of interest expense to $267.9M. The business is best valued on adjusted EBITDA ($1.31B FY25) and FCF ($633M), not GAAP EPS.

The investable thesis over the next 12 months rests on three measurable items: synergy capture (Tempur Sealy North America gained 620bps of synergy margin in Q1 2026, driving adjusted EPS +20.4% to $0.59), deleveraging (credit-facility leverage improved from 3.21x at YE25 to 3.07x at Q1'26 against a 5.0x covenant, with management targeting the 2.0–3.0x range in 2026), and the Leggett & Platt backward-integration deal that cleared the HSR waiting period June 3, 2026. These concrete catalysts are why this is a hold rather than an avoid.

The offsets keep the risk/reward balanced. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut over the trailing 90 days (current quarter -21.4%, current year -4.4%, next year -4.6%) with no recovery in the last 30 days, the single most important forward signal pointing the wrong way. The stock trades at ~24x current-year adjusted EPS ($3.15) and ~30x trailing GAAP EPS, well above the 16.4x peer median, while organic demand is soft (Mattress Firm gross margin slipped 140bps on promotional pressure and Q1 revenue missed). Goodwill plus intangibles of ~$7.2B sit against ~$3.1B of equity, and management is stacking a second large integration onto the first. We rate SGI HOLD.

## Company Overview

Somnigroup operates in over 100 countries across three segments:

- **Mattress Firm** — U.S. retail, 2,161 stores as of Q1 2026, 100% direct channel, ~33% gross / ~5% operating margin.
- **Tempur Sealy North America** — manufacturing/distribution/retail (Tempur-Pedic, Sealy, Stearns & Foster), ~$2.7B FY25 sales at ~51% gross / ~20% operating margin.
- **Tempur Sealy International** — Europe (including Dreams in the UK), APAC, LatAm, ~$1.3B FY25 sales at ~49% gross margin.

The strategic logic is end-to-end value-chain control: owning the most recognized bedding brands, the largest U.S. specialty retail footprint, and, pending the Leggett & Platt close, a major components supplier. More than 60% of global sales are now direct-to-consumer, with no customer exceeding 5% of sales. Majority-U.S. manufacturing of U.S.-sold product limits tariff exposure.

## Financial Analysis

**The headline growth is M&A, not demand.** Reported revenue reflects consolidating Mattress Firm (Feb 5–Dec 31), partly offset by elimination of ~$976M of intercompany TSNA→Mattress Firm sales. Net income was essentially flat at $384.1M versus $384.3M in 2024. The GAAP EPS decline is mechanical: ~34.2M new shares plus the interest expense increase. Adjusted EPS rose 5.9% to $2.70.

**Margins compressed on mix.** Consolidated operating margin fell 280bps to 10.1% as lower-margin retail diluted the blend and acquisition charges hit ($263.8M of FY25 operating-income adjustments). Net margin dropped to 5.2% from 7.8%.

**Cash generation is the better story.** FY25 operating cash flow was $800M and FCF $633M (8.5% FCF margin). Q1 2026 OCF jumped to $246.5M from $106.4M a year earlier. FCF conversion against GAAP net income exceeds 100%, supported by the negative-working-capital retail model (Piotroski 4/5, with OCF > NI as a quality signal).

**The balance sheet is the watch item.** Goodwill reached $4.60B and intangibles $2.59B (the Mattress Firm trade name alone is a $1.88B indefinite-lived intangible). Total debt is ~$4.7B. The Altman Z-Score of 1.43 reads as "distress," but this is largely structural: a lease-laden, goodwill-heavy retailer with negative working capital by design (–$271M at YE25, –$360M at Q1'26, driven by $2.35B of operating lease obligations). The mechanism is benign: retailers collect cash from customers before paying suppliers, producing a sub-1.0 current ratio (0.83) without implying insolvency. The company is comfortably covenant-compliant.

**Deleveraging is on schedule.** Against the 5.0x covenant maximum, management targets 2.0–3.0x in 2026 and ≥50% of FCF to dividends/buybacks. The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.17 from $0.15; ~$774.5M remains on the buyback authorization, though no open-market repurchases occurred in 2025 or Q1'26 because capital is going to debt paydown.

**Q1 2026 detail.** Revenue rose 12.3% to $1.80B (full-quarter Mattress Firm vs. stub prior year), GAAP EPS swung to $0.49 from a –$0.17 loss. TSNA gross margin surged on synergy capture and intercompany elimination. Mattress Firm gross margin slipped on promotional investment and product-mix pressure, and revenue came in below consensus, drawing a negative market reaction.

## Growth Analysis

The 12-month growth driver is synergy realization, not volume. The 620bps of synergy margin captured at TSNA is a measurable, repeatable mechanism: manufacturing scale and intercompany efficiency flowing through as the company integrates Mattress Firm. Adjusted EPS growth in a quarter with a revenue miss demonstrates that the margin lever is doing the work. The Leggett & Platt deal, if it closes by year-end, adds a second margin lever by capturing supplier markup on innersprings, foam, and components SGI already buys.

The countervailing evidence is the estimate-revision trend, the most reliable forward indicator and currently negative. Consensus has been cut with no recovery in the trailing 30 days. Consensus is marking down the top line while leaving the margin path intact. Management characterizes the bedding industry as "challenged" through Q1 2026, and the Mattress Firm margin erosion is the clearest read-through of a weak big-ticket consumer pressured by high rates and low housing turnover.

For FY26, consensus adjusted EPS of $3.15 implies modest growth driven by synergies and L&P contribution rather than industry recovery. A genuine demand recovery would require lower mortgage rates feeding housing turnover and discretionary big-ticket spending, a macro dependency outside management's control and currently absent.

The premium to peers requires a growth or quality justification. SGI's scale, brand portfolio, and synergy runway support a premium, but the negative estimate revisions and soft organic demand do not support a 45–80% premium to the peer median. Because estimates are being cut rather than raised, the argument for valuing on a depressed trailing base does not apply here. The 0.9% dividend yield offers little downside support relative to peers yielding 2–7%.

The bull anchor is the analyst price-target range of $76–115 (mean $97.25), which implies the Street sees synergy and deleveraging upside not yet in the numbers. But targets on a stock with negative revisions deserve skepticism; the same analysts cutting EPS have left price targets elevated, a divergence that usually resolves toward the estimates. We view fair value near the current price, with the upside requiring synergy capture to outrun demand softness and the L&P deal to close cleanly.

## Competitive Landscape

SGI's competitive position is the strongest dimension. It owns the most recognized bedding brands, controls the largest U.S. specialty retail chain, and is moving to control component supply, a degree of value-chain integration no competitor matches. This is why the FTC imposed a remedy.

The trajectory is improving on share and integration depth, which supports a 7.0. Two structural constraints temper it. First, the FTC merchandising commitment legally requires SGI to maintain 43% of premium ($1,500+) floor slots for third-party mattresses in Mattress Firm stores, a binding cap on the central synergy of selling more of its own product through its own stores. Second, the 10-K flags ongoing threats from online DTC entrants and "the influx of low-end, imported beds." The moat is wide and widening at the high end; the low end remains contested.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Integration and leverage stacking (dominant risk).** SGI is integrating Mattress Firm while having signed a second large deal (Leggett & Platt). A substantial portion of debt is variable-rate; a 100bp rate rise reduces pretax income ~$28.7M. Covenants restrict dividends/buybacks if leverage rises.
2. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** ~$7.2B of goodwill+intangibles against ~$3.1B equity, with the company having switched to a qualitative annual impairment test in 2025. A deterioration in the soft bedding market could trigger a large non-cash impairment of the Mattress Firm unit or the $1.88B trade name.
3. **FTC floor-slot remedy.** The 43% third-party premium-slot commitment structurally limits a key acquisition synergy and carries regulatory/litigation risk on non-compliance.
4. **Soft end-market demand.** Mattress Firm margin compression and the Q1 revenue miss confirm consumer weakness; the growth story currently leans on synergies rather than volume.
5. **Multi-employer pension exposure.** Several plans are in the Red Zone (<65% funded), with potential withdrawal-liability cash calls.
6. **CEO pay optics.** 2025 CEO compensation spiked to $45.9M total / $91.8M comp-actually-paid (vs. ~$10.9M in 2024) on a one-time Mattress Firm transaction bonus. Largely equity-aligned and offset by long-run TSR (348 vs. peer 129), but a governance watch item.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 84.4% versus 1-year historical volatility of 37.7% (a 46.7pp spread, 90th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (84.4% near-term vs. 48.8% six-month). IV jumped from 44.2% to 84.4% over the past month as the stock rose 20.8%, indicating the market expects a near-term binary event. Put/call skew is elevated (4.9% at one month) and short interest sits at 10.8% of float (5.4 days to cover). This is consistent with a market positioned for downside around a near-term catalyst, likely the next earnings print or L&P closing milestone. The signal reinforces a balanced stance rather than fresh long exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($95–115)
Synergy capture continues at the TSNA pace, deleveraging reaches the 2–3x target in 2026, the Leggett & Platt deal closes by year-end and begins capturing supplier margin, and a 2027 bedding-demand recovery on lower rates lifts volumes. Adjusted EPS compounds toward $3.91 (next-year consensus) and beyond, and the multiple holds. This is roughly the upper analyst target.

### Bear Case ($55–60)
Soft demand persists, Mattress Firm margins erode further, a goodwill/intangible impairment crystallizes against the thin equity base, L&P integration strains a stretched management team, and the multiple compresses toward the peer median. The stock revisits its 52-week low near $60.39, roughly -19% from current.

### Base Case ($75–82)
Synergies offset demand softness, deleveraging proceeds, L&P closes, but negative revisions and a full multiple cap upside. Fair value sits near the current price with modest appreciation as adjusted EPS grows mid-single to low-double digits.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, anchored to quarterly synergy/margin disclosure, the L&P close (targeted year-end 2026), and an Investor Day where updated synergy and leverage targets are expected.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive — current-year consensus revised upward by 5%+ over a 90-day window, signaling demand stabilization.
- Mattress Firm gross margin re-expands (recovers the 140bps lost) alongside positive same-store sales, confirming the consumer is recovering rather than the company discounting to hold volume.
- Leverage reaches the 2.5x or lower range and buybacks resume, demonstrating the deleveraging path is ahead of schedule.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill or trade-name impairment is announced, or leverage rises back above ~3.5x on weaker EBITDA.
- The Leggett & Platt deal fails the L&P shareholder vote or closes on materially worse terms.
- Current-year EPS estimates are cut another 10%+ with continued revenue misses, indicating demand deterioration outpacing synergy capture.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report and the announced Investor Day.

## Conclusion

SGI is a roll-up in progress, and the case for owning it rests on management executing two integrations into a structurally low-growth market while deleveraging. The synergy lever is real and measurable, which gives a defensible 12-month growth thesis and lifts this above an avoid. The offsets are equally concrete: negative estimate revisions, a multiple 45–80% above peers, ~$7.2B of goodwill/intangibles against ~$3.1B of equity, soft consumer demand confirmed in Mattress Firm margins, and an options market pricing imminent stress. The risk/reward is balanced. We rate SGI **HOLD** and would look to upgrade on positive estimate revisions plus margin re-expansion, or downgrade on impairment or a failed L&P vote.
