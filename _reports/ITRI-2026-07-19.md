**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Itron has delivered one of the cleaner operational turnarounds in the grid-edge/IIoT space: operating income moved from a $7M loss in 2022 to $313M in 2025, gross margin expanded roughly 330bps to 37.7%, and free cash flow reached $383M (16.2% of revenue). GAAP diluted EPS grew 26% to $6.50 while non-GAAP EPS reached $7.13. The margin story is genuine, funded by favorable mix, manufacturing efficiency, and the deliberate exit of low-margin hardware. The balance sheet carries $1.02B of cash (year-end 2025), an undrawn $750M revolver, and $1.61B of essentially zero-coupon convertible debt that was recently termed out to 2030 and 2032.

The problem is what carried the earnings. Revenue fell 3% in 2025 to $2,367M and slipped another 3% in Q1 2026 to $587M, with the core Networked Solutions franchise down 13% in the quarter on deployment timing. Q1 bookings were soft at $476M (book-to-bill below 1.0), total backlog has drifted from $4.7B to $4.4B, and consensus estimates are falling across every window — current-quarter EPS revised down 13.1% over 90 days, next-year down 4.6%. EPS grew 26% while revenue shrank, and the durability of that dynamic depends on bookings reaccelerating, which the most recent data does not yet show.

Layered on top is a ~$1.4B acquisition-funded pivot (Urbint plus Locusview) that built a brand-new Resiliency Solutions segment from scratch and pushed goodwill and intangibles (~$1.97B) above book equity ($1.61B). The strategic logic of shifting the revenue mix toward high-margin recurring SaaS as AI/data-center-driven electricity demand pressures utilities to modernize the grid is sound. But it concentrates integration and impairment risk in an unproven segment while the core hardware business temporarily contracts. With the stock down 37% from its 52-week high, no open-market insider buying, and a buyback executed at ~$95 (above today's ~$86), the setup is a HOLD: the earnings are high quality, but the growth thesis is unresolved and estimate momentum is negative.

## Company Overview

Itron, incorporated in 1977, is a global leader in grid-edge intelligence and IIoT infrastructure for utilities and municipalities. It sells smart meters, communications networks, grid-edge devices, and increasingly software and analytics that help utilities manage electricity, gas, and water infrastructure. As of FY2025 the company reports four segments:

- **Networked Solutions** (~66% of revenue, $1,557M FY25): AMI/AMR smart-metering systems, network infrastructure, DER management, and smart-city applications. The core franchise.
- **Device Solutions** ($447M): standalone meters and hardware without Itron communications; lower margin, actively rationalized.
- **Outcomes** ($360M, +15% YoY): high-growth recurring SaaS/managed services and data analytics — the strategic growth engine.
- **Resiliency Solutions** (new in Q4 2025): AI-based worker safety, damage prevention, emergency response, and digital construction management, built almost entirely through acquisition.

The moat rests on an interoperable, transport-agnostic IIoT platform, deep long-cycle utility relationships, scale in end-to-end deployments, and a growing partner ecosystem. Primary competitors are Landis+Gyr, Ericsson, Advanced Energy Industries, and Xylem. Customer concentration is low: no customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and the top ten collectively account for ~32%. CEO Thomas Deitrich has led since 2019; CFO Joan Hooper since 2017.

## Financial Analysis

Itron is a margin-driven turnaround, not a top-line growth story, and the two facts should be held simultaneously.

**Revenue trajectory:** $1,796M (2022) → $2,174M (2023) → $2,441M (2024) → $2,367M (2025, –3%). The 2025 decline was driven by Networked Solutions (–6%) on tough 2024 comps (supply-chain catch-up), deployment timing, and planned EMEA meter runoff. Q1 2026 continued the pattern, with Networked Solutions down 13% and Outcomes up 22%.

**Profitability:** Operating income swung from a $7M loss in 2022 to $313M in 2025 (+19% YoY), net margin 12.8%, GAAP diluted EPS $6.50 (+26%), non-GAAP EPS $7.13. Gross margin reached 40.3% in Q1 2026 (up ~450bps YoY). Adjusted EBITDA of $374M was up 16%. Every segment improved margins.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:** Operating cash flow was $406M and free cash flow $383M (up from $208M in 2024) on light capex (~$23M), an ~10% FCF yield on the current market cap. Cash fell to $713M after the Q1'26 acquisition and note repayment. The current ratio is 1.8 and D/E ~1.15. ROE ran 17.7%. The Altman Z-score sits at 2.34 (grey zone), a function of the goodwill-heavy asset base rather than distress; Piotroski indicators are healthy.

**Earnings quality caveats:** The 2025 result benefited from ~$48M of interest income on convert proceeds. As that cash is deployed into acquisitions, the interest tailwind fades: Q1'26 interest income roughly halved YoY, pressuring GAAP EPS (which fell to $1.18 from $1.42 even as non-GAAP rose to $1.49 on higher amortization). This is a mechanical headwind to reported GAAP earnings independent of operating performance.

**Recurring revenue inflection:** ARR reached $368M in Q4'25 (vs $306M a year earlier) and $414M in Q1'26 (vs $324M), up ~28% YoY. Management deliberately switched its headline KPI from endpoints under management to ARR, signaling the mix shift toward software. This is the genuinely positive forward signal in the numbers, though a meaningful chunk of the increase is inorganic (Urbint/Locusview).

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated and, on balance, weak enough to be the binding constraint on the score.

On the negative side: revenue is contracting, the core Networked Solutions hardware business fell 13% in Q1, bookings were soft, and total backlog has eroded. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative, with next-quarter EPS down 4.1% alongside the current-quarter and next-year cuts. This is negative earnings momentum, the mirror image of the revision velocity that would flag an inflection buy. There is no cycle-adjusted trough case here: the trailing period is peak margins on a shrinking top line, not depressed earnings recovering.

On the positive side: Outcomes grew 22% in Q1, ARR is compounding ~28%, and the secular backdrop is favorable. AI/data-center-driven electricity demand, electrification, and DER integration all pressure utilities to modernize the grid and buy edge intelligence and planning software, exactly where Itron is repositioning. The Resiliency pivot is designed to lift recurring, high-margin software revenue and reduce dependence on lumpy hardware deployments. Resiliency posted $16M revenue at ~27% adjusted operating margin in Q1'26, an early but encouraging print at 73–76% gross margin.

The unresolved question is timing. Utility capital spending is inherently lumpy, driven by multi-year bid cycles and regulatory approvals; the Q1 book-to-bill below 1.0 and eroding backlog could be timing noise or the start of a demand air pocket. Until bookings recover above 1.0 for a couple of quarters and estimate revisions turn, growth prospects warrant a below-average score. Weighting growth heavily as the most reliable winner-selection dimension, the current evidence pulls the score down rather than up.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$86, ITRI trades at ~13.7x trailing and forward EPS, a steep discount to the Electronic Equipment & Instruments peer median P/E of 29.5 (OSIS 26.5x, BMI 29.5x, with MIR and PI distorted by near-loss earnings and VNT the low outlier at 10.1x). On free cash flow the discount is starker, at a ~10% FCF yield against a light-capex model.

The discount reflects negative revenue growth, falling estimates, and skepticism about an acquisition-heavy pivot. Cheapness on a name with contracting revenue and eroding backlog is the classic value-trap configuration, and the discipline here is to treat the low multiple as a warning flag rather than a buy signal absent an independent catalyst. There is no identifiable near-term catalyst: the next earnings report is more likely to confirm the bookings softness than reverse it, and management's buyback at ~$95 has not established a floor.

Analyst targets (low $112, median $130, high $145, mean $126.7) sit 30%+ above the current price, implying the sell side still believes in a re-rating. But those targets are not the floor-not-ceiling case that applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates; here estimates are falling, so the targets are more likely to be trimmed than raised if bookings stay soft. The 5-year TSR of 96.83 versus a peer group at 110.21 confirms the market has been discounting Itron despite the operational turnaround.

A defensible base case anchors on ~$5.97 current-year consensus EPS at a modest re-rating to ~16–17x (a discount to peers justified by lower growth but a premium to today's multiple, reflecting FCF quality and the ARR mix shift), yielding roughly $95–100 in modest upside contingent on a bookings recovery. If revenue stagnation becomes the narrative and estimates keep sliding, ~11–12x on trimmed earnings supports the low-$60s/mid-$60s. The valuation is not stretched, but the discount is earned; a mid-range score reflects real cheapness offset by the absence of a catalyst and a legitimate impairment overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

Itron's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the level that matters for scoring, which is trajectory over level. The core Networked Solutions moat (interoperable platform, deep utility relationships, end-to-end scale) is intact, and the secular tailwinds (grid modernization, electrification, AI/data-center load growth) are real and confirmed across the utility complex. Low customer concentration is a genuine structural strength.

But the near-term evidence points to share/timing pressure rather than share gains: Networked Solutions revenue fell 13% in Q1, bookings were below 1.0x, and backlog is shrinking. Against Landis+Gyr, Ericsson, and Xylem, Itron is defending rather than extending in hardware while trying to open a new front in resilience software. The Outcomes segment (+22%) and the Resiliency build are the offensive moves, and if executed they widen the moat into higher-value, stickier software. The risk is that integrating two acquisitions into a brand-new segment while the core temporarily contracts is a demanding execution task, and the 5-year TSR lag suggests the market is not yet giving credit for the pivot. Net: a modestly below-neutral trajectory, appropriate for a market leader defending share in its core while betting on an unproven adjacency.

## Risk Assessment

**Growth/backlog softness** is the primary idiosyncratic risk not fully priced by the cheap headline multiple. If book-to-bill stays below 1.0 and backlog keeps eroding, the margin story loses its cover and top-line stagnation becomes the narrative, pressuring the multiple further.

**Goodwill impairment** is the sharpest binary risk. The acquisition spend pushed goodwill to $1.70B and total goodwill+intangibles above book equity, concentrated in the brand-new Resiliency segment. Segment underperformance or a sustained market-cap decline could trigger a non-cash impairment that would dent GAAP earnings and sentiment.

**Convertible dilution overhang.** The converts are currently anti-dilutive (stock ~$86 vs conversion prices $123.77 and $131.24), and capped calls raise the effective dilution threshold to ~$190. The 2021 warrants (3.7M shares at $180 strike) expire October 2026 and are dilutive only above $180. On a coupon basis the debt is nearly free and the maturity ladder is clean, a genuine positive, but a re-rating reintroduces dilution.

**Other risks:** utility capex cyclicality and long bid cycles (permanently priced but real), sole-source semiconductor/supply-chain and tariff exposure with limited repricing ability on fixed-price backlog, ~27% non-USD (euro-heavy) revenue and European pension concentration, FCC spectrum dependence, and cybersecurity given the connected-device footprint. No new risk factors were added in the Q1'26 10-Q.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 56.9% is rich versus 41.8% one-year historical vol (a 15.1pp spread), the term structure is in backwardation (56.9% near vs 53.7% at six months), and the put/call OI ratio of 2.24 with an 8.0% put skew signals elevated hedging demand. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±14.3%. Combined with 20.2% short interest and 9.2 days to cover, positioning is defensively bearish into the next print. For a HOLD this reinforces caution on timing rather than contradicting the thesis: the market expects a catalyst (likely the next earnings/bookings update) and is bracing for downside. The rich IV also means the stock is an expensive place to be long optionality.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$130–145):** Bookings reaccelerate as utility grid-modernization budgets ramp on AI/data-center electricity demand; book-to-bill moves back above 1.0 and backlog rebuilds. Outcomes and Resiliency drive ARR through $450M+, blended margins hold near 40%, and the market re-rates the recurring-revenue mix toward peer multiples (mid-20s P/E on ~$6.50+ EPS). The software pivot proves accretive, goodwill is never impaired, and the FCF machine (~$400M) supports continued buybacks below intrinsic value.

**Bear case (~$60–65):** The Q1 book-to-bill miss is the start of a demand air pocket in utility capex; revenue stagnates or declines through 2026, estimates keep falling, and the margin tailwind exhausts as low-margin exits complete and interest income fades. Resiliency underperforms, triggering a goodwill impairment against equity, and the multiple compresses to ~11x on trimmed earnings. No insider buying and a buyback executed above the current price offer no floor.

The base case sits between: modest re-rating toward ~$95–100 on a bookings stabilization, but with meaningful two-sided risk and negative near-term estimate momentum that keeps the reward/risk closer to balanced than asymmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The earnings quality is real, but the growth thesis is unresolved and estimate revisions are pointing the wrong way. The trajectory of scores (6.2 → 6.8 → 6.1 → 5.8 → 5.7) and the shift from BUY to three consecutive HOLDs reflects a fundamental narrative deterioration — declining revenue, eroding backlog, and falling estimates — not merely a lower price. The current data confirms rather than reverses that trend; the score holds roughly flat.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Book-to-bill returns above 1.0 for two consecutive quarters with total backlog stabilizing or growing above $4.5B.
- Consensus EPS revisions turn positive (30-day revisions flip from negative to +5% or better), confirming the negative momentum has broken.
- Organic (ex-acquisition) ARR growth demonstrably accelerates, showing the software pivot is compounding beyond the inorganic step-up.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment charge against the Resiliency segment, or Resiliency margins/revenue materially below the ~27% adjusted operating margin run-rate.
- Revenue declines accelerate (>5% YoY) with book-to-bill staying below 1.0 for a third consecutive quarter.
- Gross margin rolls over below ~37% as the low-margin-exit tailwind exhausts, removing the EPS cover for the shrinking top line.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (bookings, backlog, book-to-bill, and ARR are the key data points), then the following print to confirm any inflection. The October 2026 warrant expiry is a minor calendar item; the operational data cadence is the real clock.

## Conclusion

Itron is a well-run turnaround with high-quality cash generation, a clean and cheap capital structure, and a coherent strategy to shift toward recurring high-margin software as the grid modernizes under AI-driven electricity demand. Those are genuine strengths, and the ~13.7x multiple and ~10% FCF yield are undemanding on their face. But the multiple is cheap for a reason: revenue is contracting, backlog is eroding, book-to-bill is below 1.0, estimates are falling across every window, and a ~$1.4B acquisition-funded pivot has concentrated goodwill above book equity in an unproven segment. Cheapness without a catalyst on a shrinking-revenue business is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, and the negative estimate momentum is the most reliable forward indicator working against the name.

The stock's 37% drawdown from its high partly reflects this deterioration rather than pure volatility, and the options market is bracing for further near-term stress. Until bookings recover and estimate revisions stabilize, the reward/risk is closer to balanced than asymmetric. **HOLD**, with a clear path to upgrade on a book-to-bill recovery and estimate stabilization, and a path to downgrade on impairment or accelerating revenue decline.
