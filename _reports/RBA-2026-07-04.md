**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RB Global operates the largest global auction/marketplace for commercial assets (Ritchie Bros.) alongside a leading digital salvage-vehicle marketplace (IAA, acquired 2023). The business is a two-sided network monetizing a take rate on ~$16.2B of annual gross transaction value (GTV), producing ~76% recurring-style service revenue, strong free cash flow ($719M FY2025, 15.7% FCF margin), and improving returns on capital (adjusted ROIC 11.2% TTM vs. 10.0% prior). After a soft 2025 in the construction/used-equipment cycle, Q1 2026 delivered a clear turn: GTV +13%, revenue +11%, net income +20%, and CC&T (construction, trucks, ag) GTV +27% as deferred seller supply released. Operating cash flow rose 43% year over year.

The investment question is whether the current price offers asymmetric upside. At $112.81, RBA trades at ~55x trailing GAAP EPS ($2.04) and ~26x forward GAAP consensus ($4.42 CY, $4.88 next year), a premium to the peer median (P/E 19.3). The trailing multiple overstates expensiveness because FY2025 GAAP earnings absorbed roughly $90M+ of one-time items — $53.7M in CEO-arbitration/executive-transition costs, $17M restructuring, a $16M divestiture loss, plus $282M of IAA intangible amortization that is non-cash. As those one-time items roll off, GAAP EPS should converge upward toward adjusted EPS of $4.00, a real earnings-quality tailwind. But the market has already re-rated toward this convergence, and consensus estimate revisions are essentially flat (+1.9% CY EPS over 90 days, +0.9% next year), so the "cheap on forward earnings" case is only partially supported.

We rate RBA a HOLD (growth_hold) with a 12-month base-case target of $124. The score trajectory has improved steadily (5.6 → 5.9) on strengthening financial health and a modestly better valuation read, and Q1 2026 confirms the cyclical-recovery thesis rather than reversing it. The bar for upgrading to BUY is a pullback that restores asymmetry (low-$100s) or an acceleration in estimate revisions confirming the CC&T recovery is durable rather than a one-quarter supply catch-up.

## Company Overview

RB Global is an omnichannel marketplace and transaction-solutions provider bridging sellers (insurers, fleets, dealers, OEMs, governments) and buyers across ~170 countries. Incorporated in Ontario, operationally headquartered in Westchester, Illinois, ~8,000 employees (68% U.S.). The company reports as a single operating segment under CEO Jim Kessler, who is the chief operating decision-maker.

The portfolio spans two flagship brands and a set of data/services assets:
- **Ritchie Bros.** — the world's largest auctioneer of heavy commercial equipment (construction, trucks, earthmoving, ag, energy).
- **IAA** — a leading digital salvage/total-loss vehicle marketplace serving auto insurers; the source of the ~$4.67B goodwill and ~$2.46B intangibles on the balance sheet from the ~$7B 2023 acquisition.
- **Supporting assets** — Rouse (equipment-valuation data and benchmarks, a genuine data moat), SmartEquip (parts procurement), VeriTread (heavy-haul transport, now 100%-owned after the Q1 2026 minority buyout), plus GovPlanet, IronPlanet, Marketplace-E, and Boom & Bucket.

Revenue divides into service revenue (~76%; seller commissions, buyer fees, marketplace services) and inventory sales (~24%; assets purchased and resold on a principal basis). GTV is the key volume metric; reported revenue is a take rate on GTV. A mix shift from commission to inventory contracts inflates revenue growth without a proportional profit uplift, so GTV and service revenue are the cleaner gauges.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (vs. FY2024):**
- Revenue $4,591M, +7% (service +4% to $3,502M; inventory +18% to $1,089M — the mix effect flatters the headline).
- GTV $16.2B, +2% (automotive +5%, CC&T −2% on a soft used-equipment cycle, Other +3%).
- Operating income $713M, −6% — depressed by executive-transition costs, higher amortization, restructuring, and a divestiture loss.
- Net income $428M, +4%; GAAP diluted EPS $2.04, +1%.
- Adjusted diluted EPS $4.00, +15%; Adjusted EBITDA $1,400M, +7%.
- Operating cash flow $978M (+5%); FCF $719M (15.7% margin — strong for the sector).

**Q1 2026 — the turn:**
- Revenue $1,235M, +11%; GTV $4.3B, +13% (all regions/sectors positive; CC&T +27%).
- Net income $135.6M, +20%; GAAP diluted EPS $0.66, +20%; adjusted EPS $1.01, +13%; Adjusted EBITDA $363M, +11%.
- Operating cash flow $224M, +43%.

Management attributes CC&T strength to release of pent-up supply as sellers deferred through 2025, plus stabilizing used-equipment values and a friendlier rate environment. Q1 2026 also carried a non-recurring ~$11.2M benefit from the retroactive repeal of Canada's Digital Services Tax, which should be netted out of the underlying trend.

**Balance sheet and leverage:** Total debt $2.60B (Q1'26), cash $667M, adjusted net debt/adjusted EBITDA ~1.4x — comfortably de-levered from ~2.9x post-IAA. The company refinanced in April 2025 on improved terms (revolver expanded to $1.3B, term-loan A cut, maturity extended to 2030, spread −85bps). The debt stack: term-loan A (~$985M), 6.75% secured notes ($550M due 2028), 7.75% unsecured notes ($800M due 2031). The Altman Z-Score of 1.78 ("distress zone") is a false signal — it penalizes the goodwill-heavy, low-asset-turnover (0.38x) marketplace structure. Actual credit health is solid: in covenant compliance, $1.0B undrawn revolver, Piotroski signals uniformly positive (profitable, OCF > NI, current ratio 1.1x).

**Earnings quality:** The GAAP-to-adjusted wedge (EPS $2.04 vs. $4.00 in FY2025) is unusually wide but mostly non-recurring. It comprises $282M of IAA intangible amortization (a real acquisition cost, but non-cash) plus one-time items concentrated in 2025 ($53.7M executive transition, $17M restructuring, $16M divestiture loss, $4M debt refinancing). As those one-time items roll off, GAAP earnings should trend up toward the adjusted figure into 2026–2027.

**Capital allocation:** Quarterly dividend $0.31/share (~$230M/yr common + ~$27M preferred); a new $500M NCIB buyback (10M shares) was authorized in Q1 2026 — the first meaningful repurchase since the IAA deal, marking a transition from de-leveraging to capital return. Tuck-in M&A is being funded from FCF and the revolver.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two engines. The cyclical engine is the CC&T recovery: after −2% GTV in 2025, CC&T turned to +27% in Q1 2026 on deferred supply release. Management frames the demand backdrop around data-center buildout, reshoring/manufacturing, and civil-infrastructure projects — secular tailwinds now showing up in volumes. The durability question is whether Q1 reflects a one-time supply catch-up (sellers who deferred in 2025 clearing the queue) or a sustained re-acceleration. The 2026 guide of 5–8% GTV growth implies management does not expect +27% to persist; Q1 is running well ahead of the full-year target.

The structural engine is an M&A-led consolidation of the fragmented heavy-asset/ag auction market plus international salvage expansion. Four acquisitions closed or signed in roughly 12 months — J.M. Wood ($213.6M, closed Jul 2025), Smith Broughton (Western Australia, Nov 2025), Blackmon Auctions (~$60M GTV, Apr 2026), and BigIron ($350M, U.S. online ag marketplace, HSR-cleared, expected Q2 2026 close) — plus the VeriTread minority buyout. Internationally, IAA won Suncorp Australia (~65,000 units/yr; Australia revenue quadrupled YoY to $71M in Q1'26), expanded the U.S. GSA fleet contract, and added alliances in the UAE, Panama, Azerbaijan, and Guatemala. These deals are small relative to the balance sheet and self-fundable; integration is the watch item.

Estimate revisions are the moderating data point. Consensus CY EPS rose only +1.9% over 90 days and next-year +0.9% — stable, not accelerating. This falls well short of the revision velocity that would flag a systematic underestimation of a turn. The earnings-beat record is consistent (four straight beats, +9.7% and +12.6% in two recent quarters, though only +0.7% most recently), which supports confidence in consensus but suggests the beats are narrowing as the recovery gets priced. We score growth prospects above the midpoint on the confirmed cyclical turn plus the M&A and international optionality, but stop short of a high score given flat revision momentum and management's own decelerating full-year guide.

## Valuation Assessment

At $112.81, RBA trades at ~55x trailing GAAP EPS ($2.04) and ~26x forward GAAP consensus ($4.42 CY). On adjusted EPS of $4.00, the trailing multiple is ~28x. Against the peer median P/E of 19.3 (Diversified Support Services: LDOS 11.2, CPRT 19.3, CACI 21.7, AMTM 36.8, SAIC 12.9), RBA carries a premium. The most relevant comparable is Copart at ~19x — a larger, higher-margin salvage incumbent — which frames RBA as expensive on a like-for-like basis unless the market rewards the omnichannel diversification and CC&T recovery.

The trailing P/E overstates the case because FY2025 GAAP earnings were depressed by ~$90M of one-time items and $282M of non-cash amortization. On forward GAAP earnings the multiple compresses to ~26x, and the GAAP-toward-adjusted convergence provides a mechanical earnings tailwind that peers on cleaner books do not have. That said, this convergence is well understood, and the modest estimate revisions argue the market has already priced most of it. The forward multiple is not cheap relative to a mid-teens adjusted-EPS growth rate — PEG sits near 1.6–1.8, which is fair, not asymmetric.

The dividend yield of ~1.1% provides limited absolute support, and the analyst target set (mean $127.7, median $130.0, low $103, high $150) implies ~13% upside to the mean. On a stock at a reasonable-to-full multiple, analyst targets can lag; but at ~26x forward the multiple is high enough that targets partly reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price, so we treat them as a soft floor rather than a hard confirmation. Our valuation score sits at the midpoint: the business quality and the convergence catalyst prevent a value-trap read, but the current price offers no clear mispricing to exploit. Historically, a compressed multiple in this sector has not been a reliable buy signal on its own, and here the multiple is not even compressed.

## Competitive Landscape

**Automotive salvage.** IAA's principal competitor is Copart — larger, higher-margin, and more U.S.-centric. Insurers can also disintermediate by remarketing directly. The battleground is service level and net returns to insurers, and RBA's competitive edge is international share gains (Suncorp Australia, Middle East alliances) where Copart is less established. This is a defensible but not dominant position; RBA is the challenger against the category leader.

**CC&T / heavy equipment.** Highly fragmented; Ritchie Bros. is the scale leader competing against physical/online auctioneers, OEM/dealer trade-ins, rental firms, and private sales. The moats are real: network effects from deep buyer liquidity, irreplaceable physical yard infrastructure combined with digital reach, and a proprietary data/analytics ecosystem (machine-vision damage scoring, Rouse benchmarks). The M&A cadence is consolidating share in a fragmented market.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving. Prior reports scored competitive position higher (7.0 declining to 6.5); the moat is intact and international share is being won, but the head-to-head against Copart in the higher-margin salvage segment caps the ceiling. Segment-level data-center/reshoring/infrastructure demand is a genuine secular tailwind translating into volume, but competitive positioning has decayed from a firm secondary signal to a supporting one in our framework, so we anchor it with financial health and growth rather than lean on it alone.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Supplier/customer concentration in salvage.** ~23% of consolidated revenue comes from the three largest supplier customers (auto insurers), on contracts cancellable at 30–90 days' notice. Loss of a major insurer — or an insurer self-remarketing — is the single largest structural risk, sharpened by direct competition with Copart for these accounts.

2. **CRA tax dispute — material, unreserved contingency.** The Canada Revenue Agency assessed C$79.1M (~$57M) for 2010–2015, asserting a former Luxembourg subsidiary was Canadian-resident. RBA has paid a C$39.5M refundable deposit, filed a Notice of Appeal, and carries no reserve (management deems its position more likely than not to prevail). CRA is also probing 2016–2020 on the same issue, an unquantified parallel exposure. A loss would be a discrete hit and a modest reputational issue.

3. **Cyclicality of both engines.** CC&T depends on the construction/used-equipment cycle (negative in 2025, turning in 2026); automotive salvage depends on accident frequency, miles driven, total-loss ratios, and the repair-cost-vs-used-value spread — mild weather and fewer catastrophes reduce volumes.

4. **Environmental liabilities inherited via IAA.** Named at the Lower Duwamish Waterway Superfund Site (Seattle) and Pyrite Canyon Plume (Jurupa Valley, CA); potential liability "cannot be estimated."

5. **Inventory/principal risk.** Guarantee and inventory contracts expose RBA to losses if realized proceeds fall below appraised/guaranteed values (~2.7% of IAA vehicles bought outright; $166M guaranteed at Q1'26).

6. **Preferred overhang.** Series A Senior Preferred ($482M carrying) pays a 5.5% dividend stepping up to 7.5% after Feb 2027 and floating to ≥10.5% after year 9, plus participation rights — a growing fixed claim ahead of common (conversion price $71.58) that the market may under-appreciate.

7. **Governance/jurisdiction.** Ontario incorporation limits enforceability of U.S. civil liabilities; a 20% poison pill and advance-notice bylaws entrench against takeovers.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contradiction to a constructive fundamental read. Near-term ATM IV of 26.7% is essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility (27.1%), with an IV percentile of just 3% within the current chain — the market is pricing low near-term uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced (0.8%), the put/call volume ratio is 0.50 (call-leaning), and the term structure is in normal contango (26.7% near-term vs. 32.4% at six months). The implied one-month move is ±4.7%. There is no elevated put demand or unusual positioning signaling informed bearish flow. Short interest is moderate at 9.0% of float with a high 12.8 days to cover, which creates some squeeze mechanics but is not itself a directional signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($150).** The CC&T recovery proves durable rather than a one-quarter supply catch-up; data-center, reshoring, and infrastructure demand sustain double-digit GTV growth. GAAP EPS converges toward the ~$4.00 adjusted figure as 2025 one-time items and amortization roll off, and the M&A pipeline (BigIron, ag consolidation) plus international salvage (Australia, Middle East) add share. The $500M buyback plus dividend signal FCF confidence. At sustained mid-teens adjusted-EPS growth, a ~30x forward multiple on ~$5.00 adjusted EPS supports the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear Case (~$92, −18%).** Q1's +27% CC&T is a one-time supply release; underlying growth reverts toward the 5–8% guide. A major insurer contract loss to Copart, an adverse CRA ruling (including the unquantified 2016–2020 exposure), or a used-equipment-value rollover compresses take rates. The preferred step-up to 7.5% in early 2027 raises the fixed claim ahead of common. At the peer-median ~19x on ~$4.42 forward EPS, the multiple derates to the mid-$80s to low-$90s.

The stock has re-rated toward the convergence thesis without the estimate-revision acceleration that would confirm a systematic underestimation, leaving the risk/reward roughly symmetric near current prices.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward the low-$100s (below ~$105) restoring asymmetry, with fundamentals intact.
- CY EPS estimate revisions accelerating meaningfully over a 90-day window, confirming the CC&T recovery is durable rather than a supply catch-up.
- Two consecutive quarters of CC&T GTV growth above the 5–8% full-year guide, with used-equipment values stable or rising.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Loss of a top-three insurer supplier account, or evidence of insurer disintermediation reducing IAA volumes.
- An adverse CRA ruling on the 2010–2015 assessment, or a material quantified expansion of the 2016–2020 probe.
- CC&T GTV reverting negative on a year-over-year basis, indicating the Q1 turn was a one-time supply release.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the natural checkpoint to test CC&T durability and the BigIron integration. The February 2027 preferred step-up is a dated cost catalyst to monitor.

Base-case target: **$124** (~28x forward GAAP EPS of ~$4.42, in line with the convergence-adjusted quality of the franchise), implying ~10% upside. Bull case $150 (~+33%); bear case $92 (~−18%). Timeframe 12 months.

## Conclusion

RB Global is a high-quality marketplace operator with real network and data moats, strong free cash flow, and a confirmed cyclical turn in its equipment business. The Q1 2026 acceleration validates the recovery thesis, and the roll-off of 2025's one-time costs plus IAA amortization should push GAAP earnings up toward the ~$4.00 adjusted figure over 2026–2027.

The constraint is price. At a premium to the peer median, with flat estimate revisions and management guiding to a decelerating 5–8% full-year GTV, the current level offers limited asymmetry. The stock has moved ~7% since the prior review on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, consistent with a well-owned quality name rather than a mispricing to exploit. We maintain HOLD with a $124 base-case target and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$100s or on evidence that the CC&T recovery is durable enough to lift consensus estimates materially. The steady score improvement (5.6 → 5.9) reflects a franchise getting healthier; the fourth consecutive HOLD reflects a valuation that has largely caught up to that improvement.
