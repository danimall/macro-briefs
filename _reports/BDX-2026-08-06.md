## Executive Summary

Becton Dickinson is a different company than the one described in its last annual report. On February 9, 2026 BD completed a Reverse Morris Trust separating its Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions business into Waters Corporation. BD shareholders received Waters stock representing roughly 39.2% of the combined company; BD the corporation retained no Waters equity and received $3.857B of net cash from a spinco-funded distribution. Management deployed that cash roughly 50/50: ~$2.12B of principal retired through February tender offers (booking a net $122M gain on discounted long bonds) and $2.0B of accelerated share repurchase at an average February price of ~$171.69. Gross debt fell $1.9B to $17.28B and diluted shares fell from a 288.4M prior-year average to 275.5M outstanding.

The residual business is a four-segment consumables and devices franchise generating $9,200M of six-month revenue (+4.4%) at 36.4% aggregate segment margin. The problem is what sits underneath the headline: Q2 revenue growth of +5.2% decomposed to volume +2.4%, price +0.2%, FX +2.6%, and international revenue *fell* 1.4% FX-neutral. Every one of the four segments showed year-over-year margin compression on a six-month basis, and total segment income declined to $3,345M from $3,403M on 4.4% higher revenue. Management guides FY2026 adjusted EPS of $12.52-$12.72 on low-single-digit revenue growth, and consensus sits at $12.56 for FY2026 and $13.46 for FY2027 — roughly 7% EPS growth, a meaningful share of it from the buyback rather than volume.

Our prior four reviews carried HOLD ratings with a declining score (6.1 → 5.8 → 5.0 → 5.2). The score holds at 5.2, but the composition changed. Operational delivery has been marginally better than expected — three beats in the last four quarters (average surprise +2%), guidance raised on May 7, ChloraPrep shipments resumed May 29 after a 23-day hold — while the entry price has deteriorated sharply. BDX has recovered 27.8% from its June 22 low of $140.11 and now trades 3.4% below its post-spin high of $185.39 (February 20), at the $177.50 analyst median. The valuation argument that supported patience in June has largely been consumed by price. What remains is a 13.3x forward multiple against 2-3% organic growth, four open FDA enforcement matters including a repeat-observation warning letter that challenges the authority of BD's Quality Unit, and $1.7B of legal accruals with material unaccrued exposure. That is a justified discount, not an identified mispricing.

## Company Overview

BD sells high-volume, single-use consumables and the capital equipment they attach to, into hospitals and increasingly into alternate-care settings. Effective October 1, 2025 the company reorganized into four reportable segments, reported as follows for fiscal Q2 2026 (quarter ended March 31, 2026):

| Segment | Q2 FY26 revenue | YoY | FX-neutral | Segment income | Margin (prior year) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medical Essentials | $1,647M | +4.7% | +1.7% | $574M | 34.8% (39.3%) |
| Connected Care | $1,120M | +4.9% | +3.2% | $347M | 31.0% (33.0%) |
| BioPharma Systems | $590M | +2.5% | −1.8% | $225M | 38.1% (38.9%) |
| Interventional | $1,357M | +7.3% | +5.3% | $579M | 42.7% (44.7%) |

Three sources of durable advantage are visible in the filings rather than merely asserted. First, installed-base lock-in: at March 31, 2026 BD disclosed approximately $2.3B of remaining performance obligations for service contracts and pending equipment installations plus approximately $2.0B of unsatisfied minimum consumable purchase commitments in Medication Management and Medication Delivery. Both figures are explicitly limited to contracts with original expected duration exceeding one year, and BD does not define them as total backlog — they are a floor on contracted future revenue. Second, regulatory barriers cut both ways: the FDA regime currently penalizing BD also raises entry costs in infusion pumps and sterile drug-device combinations. Third, BioPharma Systems is a genuinely differentiated container-closure franchise, with double-digit growth in prefillable syringes for biologics led by GLP-1 delivery products, partly offset by lower vaccine demand.

The strategic direction is consistent with the activist thesis Starboard Value advanced in early 2025. The separation was announced July 13, 2025 and closed February 9, 2026; BD now competes more directly with Baxter, ICU Medical, Teleflex and Medtronic in vascular access and infusion, and with Masimo and Philips in patient monitoring — the latter comparison sharpened by hiring Masimo's former COO to run Connected Care in July 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue quality.** Reported revenue grew from $18,870M (FY2022) to $21,840M (FY2025), but FY2025's +8.2% decomposed to volume/other +3.2%, pricing −0.3%, FX +0.1%, the Advanced Patient Monitoring acquisition +4.8%, other +0.4%. Underlying growth was roughly 3% with negative price. FY2026 to date is similar in character with FX doing more of the work.

**The GAAP/non-GAAP gap is unusually wide and must be respected in both directions.** FY2025 GAAP operating income was $2,579M on $21,840M (11.8% margin) against segment margins of 34-43%, because BD recorded $2,951M of pre-tax specified items — purchase accounting $1,898M (including a $336M APM inventory step-up), product/litigation/other $548M, restructuring $275M, integration $127M, separation $97M. Management quantified the FY2025 diluted EPS impact at $8.59 against GAAP continuing-operations EPS of $5.82, implying ~$14.41 adjusted on the pre-spin perimeter. That derivation checks out: $2,477M of after-tax specified items ÷ $8.59 = 288.4M shares, matching the disclosed diluted count. Comparing FY2026 guidance of $12.62 to $14.41 as a 12% decline would be wrong — the difference is dominated by moving B&DS earnings into discontinued operations. We could not locate a recast FY2025 continuing-operations adjusted EPS, so the split between perimeter change and operational change remains unquantified.

**Q2 FY2026 GAAP results were poor and mostly explicable.** Operating income fell to $93M from $383M on $533M of integration, restructuring and transaction costs including a $450M non-cash impairment spanning all four segments (PP&E $238M, developed technology $134M, ~$78M other) taken on committing to exit projects misaligned with the new strategy. Below the line, a $76M tax provision on $39M of pre-tax income (193.1% effective rate) reflected a deferred tax liability recognized on a decision to distribute prior-year earnings of foreign subsidiaries as part of an intercompany restructuring. That is transaction-driven rather than run-rate, but it anticipates cash repatriation and associated cash taxes.

**The underlying margin trend is the more important signal and it is negative.** Six-month segment margins: Medical Essentials 35.2% vs 38.8%, Connected Care 31.0% vs 32.2%, BioPharma Systems 35.8% vs 36.7%, Interventional 42.4% vs 44.6%. Selling and administrative expense rose to 25.7% of revenue from 24.9%. Q2 gross margin of 45.7% versus 41.5% looks like expansion but the filing decomposes it as specified items +4.9pts, FX +0.3pt, operating performance −1.0pt. Management cited tariffs, FX and labor, and told investors on May 7 it plans pricing actions to offset high oil and resin costs — relevant given crude's roughly 20% monthly gain into July and the resulting resin pass-through.

**Cash flow requires an adjustment.** Six-month operating cash flow was $1,328M versus $489M, and capex $233M, for FCF of $1,095M versus $270M. Roughly three-quarters of that improvement is timing: the change in operating assets and liabilities was −$417M versus −$1,070M, a $653M favorable swing out of an $839M OCF improvement. BD also transferred $834M of receivables during the six months versus $560M a year earlier (+49%), with the balance yet to be collected and remitted at $381M versus $289M; supplier finance payables moved the other way ($215M versus $234M), which argues against a coordinated window-dressing effort. We are not annualizing the half-year figure — BD's cash flow is back-half weighted, cash restructuring payments remain, and the repatriation decision implies future cash tax. As a labeled sensitivity only, doubling gives ~$2.2B against FY2025's $2.67B on a larger perimeter. FY2025 OCF of $3,430M was itself down ~11% from $3,844M while revenue rose 8%.

**Balance sheet.** Deleveraging is real but the maturity profile shifted toward the short end:

| | 9/30/2025 | 3/31/2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash + ST + restricted | $859M | $1,018M |
| Current assets / current liabilities | $9,255M / $8,313M | $8,012M / $8,507M |
| Current ratio | 1.11 | 0.94 |
| Current debt | $1,559M | $2,573M |
| Commercial paper | $855M | $1,183M |
| Total debt | $19,181M | $17,279M |
| Goodwill | $25,964M | $25,955M |
| Total equity | $25,390M | $24,133M |

Goodwill plus intangibles of $34,304M equals 67.5% of total assets against $24,133M of equity, implying roughly −$10.2B of tangible equity. Cash is "primarily held outside the United States." Offsetting: an undrawn $2.75B revolver to September 2030 with a ≤4.25x leverage covenant, weighted average cost of debt of 3.3%, and investment-grade ratings from all three agencies (S&P BBB, Moody's Baa2, Fitch BBB, all Stable). Interest coverage of $2,579M / $613M = 4.21x for FY2025; adding back Q2's $533M of charges gives $626M / $149M = 4.20x, essentially unchanged. Approximating adjusted EBITDA at ~$6.0B post-spin (adjusted net income ~$3.5B grossed up for tax and net interest, plus tangible depreciation) puts net leverage near 2.7x — comfortable for the rating but not a covenant calculation, since the credit-agreement EBITDA definition and its add-backs are not disclosed.

A screening tool flagged an Altman Z of 1.31 with a −1.8% operating margin. Those inputs are irreconcilable with the audited statements. Recomputed with FY2025 balance-sheet data and market equity at the current price, Z is approximately 1.98 — the grey zone. Neither figure is informative: Altman Z structurally penalizes a goodwill-heavy acquirer through asset turnover of 0.39. The ratings and 4.2x coverage are better solvency evidence.

## Growth Analysis

Management reaffirmed low-single-digit FY2026 revenue growth and raised the adjusted EPS range on May 7. Consensus FY2026 of $12.56 sits at the low end of guidance; FY2027 of $13.46 implies +7.2% EPS growth. Estimate revisions are stable-to-marginally-positive: current year +0.1% over 30 days and +0.6% over 90 days; next year +0.5% and −0.4%. This is nowhere near the revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection, and it should not be read as one.

The composition of that 7% is important. With revenue growing 2-3% and segment margins compressing, a meaningful portion comes from the ~4.5% share count reduction and from cost actions funded by the $557M of six-month restructuring charges. Ex-buyback, operating EPS growth is plausibly in the 3-4% range.

Genuine growth pockets exist. Advanced Patient Monitoring grew 13.6% (+12.0% FX-neutral) to $292M in Q2 while legacy Medication Management grew 2.2% (+0.4% FX-neutral) to $829M — the $3.914B Edwards Critical Care acquisition is working commercially, and is also the reason purchase-accounting amortization and leverage are elevated. BioPharma Systems saw double-digit prefillable syringe growth on GLP-1 delivery, though total segment revenue grew only 2.5% (−1.8% FX-neutral) as vaccine demand fell. PureWick grew double digits but faces CMS's proposed expansion of competitive bidding to DMEPOS devices. Emerging markets grew 2.6% in FY2025 with China volume-based procurement pressuring price.

The counterweight is structural. BD's own filings describe GPO and IDN concentration driving "downward pricing pressure," distributors that are themselves manufacturers, and a named risk that migration to non-acute settings "may also place financial pressure on hospitals... that could result in less demand." FY2025 pricing of −0.3% is the numerical footprint. BD also devotes a risk factor to AI and to nontraditional technology entrants, conceding that failure to integrate AI into differentiated products could impair its competitive position.

The most immediate growth question is the one this report cannot answer. Fiscal Q3 covers April 1 – June 30, 2026 and therefore contains the 23-day U.S. ship hold on ChloraPrep and PurPrep (May 6 to May 29). BD does not disclose product-level revenue, so the headwind is directional only. The earnings feed shows the most recently reported quarter at $3.23 against a $3.17 estimate (+1.8%), and the reference price is 4.95% above the prior close on the scheduled reporting date. The reasonable inference is that Q3 landed slightly ahead and was received well; we could not verify Q3 revenue, segment detail or any guidance revision, and the quarterly rows in the estimate feed do not reconcile with the annual consensus, so we place no weight on them. An alternative explanation for the move is broader healthcare tape strength — the sector has had an active M&A bid, and BDX also gained 4.7% on July 28 on above-average volume for reasons we could not attribute.

The absolute multiple is low for medtech and the yield is the highest in the peer set. The nearest business analogue, GE HealthCare — also low-growth, tariff- and litigation-exposed — trades at 15.5x trailing, so BDX's discount is roughly 15% against the closest comparable rather than the 32% suggested by the full peer median (which is inflated by IDXX and EW at 41-47x).

Two things prevent us scoring this as a mispricing. First, adjusting for growth removes most of the apparent cheapness: 13.3x against ~7% EPS growth is a PEG near 1.85, and on organic earnings growth of 3-4% the figure is worse. A 13x multiple on a business with negative-to-flat pricing power, compressing segment margins, and four open FDA matters is a fair clearing price, not an error.

Second, the drawdown that created the opportunity has closed. The 52-week high of $213.08 is pre-separation and not comparable — shareholders received Waters stock on February 9, so the price adjusted mechanically (CEO Polen terminated his 10b5-1 plan on February 10 explicitly citing the post-spin price adjustment). The valid reference is the post-spin path: $185.39 high on February 20, a decline to $140.11 on June 22 spanning the El Paso warning letter, the ChloraPrep hold and the Q2 GAAP loss, then a 27.8% recovery to $179.11. The stock is now 3.4% below its post-spin high, 0.9% above the analyst median target of $177.50, and 1.5% below the mean of $181.83, on FY2027 estimates that are 0.4% *lower* than 90 days ago. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, but the round trip means the price now embeds the recovery that a June buyer was paid to underwrite, and further upside requires multiple expansion on a low-growth base.

We note the brief's central correction — that the supplied $179 price overstated market cap by ~14% versus a Form 4-verified $157.13 on July 27 — has been superseded by the tape. The price history confirms $158.92 on July 27, $170.66 on August 5, and $179.11 intraday August 6. The $179 figure is a genuine subsequent move, and market capitalization of $49.4B is correct on 275.5M shares.

Because the reference price is an intraday trade 4.95% above the prior close on a reporting date, the closing level is not yet established. Scenario arithmetic below uses $179.11; a close nearer $170 would improve each upside figure by roughly 5 percentage points.

## Competitive Landscape

BD's position is stable in structure and mildly deteriorating in trajectory. It retains scale, an installed base with contractually committed consumable purchases, and a differentiated injectable-delivery franchise. Against that: pricing was negative in FY2025 and only +0.2% in Q2 FY2026 as management pushed price to recover input costs; international revenue declined 1.4% FX-neutral; margins compressed in all four segments; and the customer side is consolidating faster than the supplier side.

The quality-system record is the most consequential competitive variable, because FDA enforcement translates into growth constraints through withheld export certificates and blocked supplements listing affected sites. BD is operating under four concurrent postures: the amended CareFusion 303 consent decree on Alaris infusion pumps (final independent-expert audit report delivered to FDA in January 2026; penalties up to $15,000/day capped at $15M/year, no accrual); a Specimen Management warning letter open since January 11, 2018; a Dispensing (Pyxis) warning letter from November 22, 2024 carrying a $68M accrual; and the April 30, 2026 El Paso warning letter citing inadequate deviation and OOS investigation, insufficient laboratory controls supporting sterility assurance, contamination-control and facility-design shortcomings, terminal sterilization issues, repeat observations, and challenges to the authority and effectiveness of the Quality Unit. An eight-year-old unresolved letter plus repeat findings at a second site plus a Quality-Unit-authority citation is better read as a systemic quality-management weakness than as plant-specific bad luck, though BD's site count is very large and it did obtain nine clearances under the 2018 letter and restored ChloraPrep shipments within 23 days.

Layered on top, BD is recalling specific lots of its Intraosseous Vascular Access System Needle Sets (issuer notice via FDA, July 30-31, 2026) after reports of difficulty removing the obturator. One trade outlet reported the recall followed four patient deaths; the issuer/FDA text we retrieved confirms the recall and the defect but not the death count, so we treat that figure as externally reported and unconfirmed.

## Risk Assessment

**El Paso.** BD states a loss is probable but not reasonably estimable. FDA is likely to withhold export certificates for drug and drug-led combination products from the site and to withhold approval of applications listing it. ChloraPrep and PurPrep are lead infection-prevention products inside Interventional's Surgery business ($1,572M of FY2025 revenue, +7.4% in Q2). Management raised guidance one day after the hold began, which suggests they judged it manageable, but the FDA review continues.

**Litigation concentrated where the goodwill is.** Aggregate product liability and certain other legal accruals were ~$1.7B at March 31, 2026. Hernia repair: ~6,995 claims, up from 6,905, with most pre-existing claims settled in FY2024 and payments spread over years. Implantable ports: ~3,250 claims in an Arizona MDL, first trial commenced April 2026 with the **next trial scheduled for August 2026** — the nearest dated binary. Ethylene oxide (Covington, Georgia): ~430 suits, a May 2025 $20M compensatory verdict, a $50M punitive finding set aside with a mistrial on specific intent, appellate oral argument set May 14, 2026, no judgment entered and **no amount accrued**; punitive damages are capped at $250,000 per claimant unless specific intent is proven, making that single legal question the pivotal variable across ~440 plaintiffs. Also open: TELA Bio antitrust (motion to dismiss filed), DOJ civil investigative demands from April 2019 (CareFusion/VA contracts, False Claims Act, subsequently expanded) and April 2023 (Genesis containers), pelvic mesh and IVC filters. Interventional carries $12,755M of the $25,955M goodwill total (49%) and houses nearly every one of these matters; the last goodwill test (July 1, 2025, no impairment) predates both the segment reorganization and the El Paso letter. Interventional's 42.7% margin and +7.3% growth support the carrying value, and impairment turns on projected cash flows rather than claim counts.

**Other identified exposures.** Loss of well-known seasoned issuer status upon the FY2025 annual report filing, tied to a December 2024 SEC Order, removes automatic shelf registration for an issuer with $17.3B of debt, $2,573M of current debt and $1,183M of commercial paper. Tariffs are expected to hit FY2026 operating expense with "limited mitigation opportunities," and BD has recorded a receivable for tariff exemption claims whose collectability may be affected; a Commerce Section 232 investigation into medical consumables and equipment adds policy uncertainty. EtO sterilization compliance was pushed to April 2028 by a July 2025 executive order but EPA's occupational exposure limits phase in over ten years. GLP-1s are two-sided: named as a substitution risk for device-delivered therapies, yet currently a double-digit growth driver in prefillable syringes.

**Governance items we could not verify** and that should not be treated as established: the purpose of the Series D Junior Participating Redeemable Preferred Stock certificate of designation filed February 5, 2026 (a shareholder rights plan is one reading; a Section 355(e) tax-protective structure following the RMT is an equally plausible one); the officer change and Reg FD disclosure in the July 22, 2026 8-K; and the terms of the May 20, 2026 8-K disclosing a material agreement creating a direct financial obligation.

**Management alignment.** Across roughly 25 Form 4 filings from January through July 2026 there were no open-market purchases by any officer or director. Selling was small and plan-driven (Feld 74-75 shares monthly; Garrison 1,100 shares twice in June at $151.48 and $145.66; Polen exercised 20,209 SARs at $126.16 and sold 2,764 at $146.35, retaining 110,163). The magnitude is immaterial and does not constitute a bearish signal, but the absence of any buying through a 23% post-spin drawdown is a missing confirmation. Executive churn is heavy: two CFO changes in six months, with Vitor Roque named permanent CFO on May 7, 2026 holding 8,231 shares; Interventional president Byrd retiring after 25 years with Menziuso appointed; a new General Counsel; a new Chief Revenue Officer title for Feld. Pay-versus-performance is functioning — Polen's FY2025 granted compensation of $17.13M produced $3.48M actually paid, down from $29.77M in FY2023 — but the underlying five-year TSR is the indictment: 89.25 indexed versus a peer index of 130.17, with BDX trailing in four of five years.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($215, +20%).** Post-spin BD is a simpler, higher-margin consumables business whose 13.3x forward multiple reflects the pre-separation conglomerate discount plus a quality-system penalty that remediation closes. The Alaris final audit report went to FDA in January 2026 and the U.S. installed-base replacement should be substantially complete within a year; El Paso resolves as the Dispensing letter has been progressing; the 2018 Specimen Management letter clears. Pricing actions announced in May recover resin and oil costs, restoring the 200-400bp of segment margin lost year over year. APM continues compounding at low-double-digits, GLP-1 prefillable syringes keep BioPharma Systems growing, and $2.0B+ of annual capital return on a 275M share base compounds EPS at high single digits. Re-rating to GEHC's 15.5x on FY2027 EPS of $13.46 plus continued buyback gets to the mid-$200s within 18-24 months; the $225 high target is achievable on that path.

**Bear case ($148, −17%).** Organic growth stays at 2-3% with negative price, and the four-front FDA record proves systemic — a fifth action, an El Paso escalation, or export-certificate restrictions constrain new-product cadence in the segments that matter. The August ports trial produces an adverse verdict; the Georgia appellate court permits a specific-intent retrial, opening uncapped punitive exposure across ~440 EtO plaintiffs against zero accrual. Segment margin compression continues as tariffs and resin costs outrun pricing. Cash flow disappoints as the working-capital and factoring tailwinds exhaust and restructuring and repatriation cash taxes land. A goodwill impairment in Interventional — 49% of a $25.96B balance last tested before the reorganization and the El Paso letter — resets a book value that already carries −$10.2B of tangible equity. The multiple compresses to 11x and the stock retests the June range.

**Our view.** The bull case requires a re-rating that the last five years of relative performance argue against, and it depends on quality-system remediation that has repeatedly taken longer than expected (the 2018 letter is still open). The bear case requires a specific adverse event from a stack of them with reasonable probability. Between those, the base case is that BD delivers guidance, buys back stock, and holds a low-teens multiple: 14.0x $13.46 = $188, a 5% gain plus a 2.35% yield. That is not a compelling risk/reward at 17% downside to the bear case.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (fifth consecutive). Base case $188 (+5.0%), bull $215 (+20.0%), bear $148 (−17.4%) from the $179.11 reference. The bar for continuing an unchanged HOLD is higher after four prior reviews, and the reason it is met is that the two inputs moved in opposite directions: operational delivery improved modestly (three beats in four quarters, guidance raised, $1.9B of debt retired, ~4.5% of shares repurchased) while the entry price worsened by 17% since the July review, netting to an unchanged 5.2.

**Upgrade to BUY if:**
1. The price returns to $150-158 with FY2027 consensus at or above $13.40 — that combination restores a 11-12x multiple with a ~2.7% yield and 20%+ upside to base case, the setup that existed in June.
2. FDA closes either the El Paso or the 2018 Specimen Management warning letter, or the Alaris consent decree is lifted following the January 2026 final audit report. Any one of these removes a growth constraint and a tail liability simultaneously.
3. Two consecutive quarters of positive FX-neutral pricing combined with year-over-year segment margin expansion in at least three of four segments — the direct test of whether the announced pricing actions are recovering input costs.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:**
1. A fifth FDA enforcement action, or an El Paso escalation to import alert, consent decree or withheld export certificates for material product lines.
2. An adverse implantable-ports verdict in the August 2026 trial with damages that force a material accrual increase above the current ~$1.7B, or a Georgia appellate ruling permitting a specific-intent retrial on EtO (uncapped punitive exposure across ~440 plaintiffs, currently unaccrued).
3. A goodwill impairment in Interventional, or full-year FCF (once reported) below ~$1.8B, which would indicate the six-month improvement was entirely working capital and factoring and would put the ~$1.16B dividend plus buyback ambitions in tension.
4. FY2027 consensus revised below $12.75 (a ~5% cut), which would signal the low-single-digit growth guide is not holding.

**Reassessment timeline.** Immediate re-review once the fiscal Q3 release is available in full — specifically Q3 revenue with the FX/volume/price decomposition, segment margins, any quantification of the ChloraPrep ship-hold impact, updated FY2026 guidance, and year-to-date free cash flow. Second checkpoint is the August 2026 ports trial outcome. Third is the fiscal Q4 report and FY2027 guidance in November 2026, which will provide the first clean full-year post-spin base and a recast comparison.

## Conclusion

BD has executed the structural change the market asked for: the Waters separation closed with a favorable IRS ruling, $3.857B of net proceeds were split evenly between retiring $2.12B of discounted long-dated debt (at a $122M net gain) and $2.0B of buyback, gross debt fell $1.9B, and the share count is down 4.5%. Investment-grade ratings are intact across all three agencies with 4.2x interest coverage.

What has not changed is the operating profile. Organic growth is 2-3% with pricing that only recently turned marginally positive, segment margins compressed year over year in all four segments, more than half of Q2's reported growth was currency, and international revenue declined FX-neutral. Three-quarters of the striking six-month cash flow improvement was working-capital timing and a 49% increase in receivables transferred.
