## Executive Summary

Neogen Corporation is a food and animal safety company carrying the consequences of a value-destructive acquisition. The September 2022 Reverse Morris Trust merger with 3M's Food Safety Division (FSD) doubled Neogen's size but resulted in $1.059B of goodwill impairments through FY2025, persistent material weaknesses in internal controls (year three and counting), and negative free cash flow. The company generated only $58M of operating cash flow in FY2025 against $902M of total debt and $72M of annual interest expense. Revenue declined 3.2% in FY2025 to $895M, and gross margins compressed 310bp to 47.1%.

A turnaround narrative is forming under new CEO Mike Nassif (appointed August 2025). FY2026 guidance was raised to $845-855M in revenue and $175M in adjusted EBITDA, Q2 FY2026 showed sequential improvement, and the C&D business divestiture for $130M simplifies the portfolio and reduces debt. The stock surged 31% on the January 2026 earnings report. However, at ~$1.9B market cap and ~$2.7B enterprise value, the stock trades at approximately 15.4x guided EBITDA — a multiple that requires successful execution on the incomplete Petrifilm manufacturing transition, further margin improvement, and no additional goodwill impairments on the remaining $1.065B balance. In a stagflation environment with rising input costs, tariff exposure on 50% international revenue, and an auditor's adverse opinion on internal controls, the risk-reward skews negative.

## Company Overview

Neogen operates in two segments: **Food Safety** (~71% of FY2025 revenue, $638M) and **Animal Safety** (~29%, $257M). Food Safety manufactures diagnostic test kits, Petrifilm microbial detection plates (the crown jewel acquired from 3M), Clean-Trace sanitation testing, culture media, and pathogen detection systems. Animal Safety spans veterinary instruments, pharmaceuticals/vaccines, rodent/insect control, cleaners/disinfectants, and GeneSeek genomics services (six global labs).

International revenue reached 50.2% of total in FY2025, up from 48.4% in FY2023. The company operates in 29 countries with no single customer exceeding 10% of revenue. Headquarters are in Lansing, Michigan, where a new manufacturing facility for Petrifilm is under construction.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue of $895M declined 3.2% YoY, driven by $24.3M in FX headwinds, $3.9M from discontinued products, and $1.4M of organic decline. Food Safety fell 3%; Animal Safety fell 5%. The decline occurred across veterinary instruments, genomics (down from $107M in FY2023 to $97M in FY2025 from customer insourcing), and sample collection products (production disruptions from the 3M manufacturing relocation).

Gross margin compressed from 50.2% to 47.1% — a 310bp decline caused by lower volumes, manufacturing inefficiencies from the sample collection relocation, elevated inventory write-offs ($16.5M reserve vs. $12.4M prior), tariff impacts, and $4.4M restructuring charges. Petrifilm, historically a high-margin consumable, should be margin-accretive once manufacturing transitions to Neogen's own facility. Until that transition is complete, margins remain structurally depressed.

Reported operating income swung to a ($1.061B) loss from $58.7M income, entirely driven by the $1.059B goodwill impairment ($461M in Q2, $585M in Q4 for Food Safety plus $13M for Animal Safety). Stripping out the impairment, operating income was approximately ($2M) — still a real deterioration from $59M the prior year.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt stands at $902M: $450M term loan (SOFR + 1.75%, maturing April 2030), $350M in 8.625% Senior Notes due 2030, $100M drawn on a $250M revolver, plus minor finance leases. Cash was $129M at fiscal year-end. Net debt of ~$773M represents approximately 13x trailing operating cash flow — a heavy burden.

The C&D divestiture in July 2025 brought $130M in gross proceeds for debt reduction, which would lower net debt to approximately $643M. The April 2025 refinancing reduced the term loan spread by 60bp (from 2.35% to 1.75%), generating modest annual interest savings.

Remaining goodwill of $1.065B ($997M Food Safety, $68M Animal Safety) constitutes 31% of total assets. Intangible assets total $1.41B, of which $1.04B is customer relationships. These assets, acquired at peak valuations, face impairment risk if discount rates rise (as they are in the current rate environment) or if performance misses targets.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow improved to $58.2M from $35.3M, driven by working capital normalization. Capex of $104.6M (including $35M+ for the Lansing Petrifilm plant and 3M integration) resulted in negative free cash flow of approximately ($46M). FY2026 capex guidance of ~$50M (roughly half FY2025 levels) should bring FCF positive, but the improvement depends on EBITDA actually reaching the $175M target.

### Earnings Surprise Pattern

The last four quarters show a mixed record: two beats (Q1 FY2026 at +96%, Q2 FY2026 at +78%) and two misses (Q4 FY2025 at -35%, Q3 FY2025 at -11%). The recent beats suggest early turnaround traction, but the absolute earnings levels are extremely thin — Q2 FY2026's "beat" was $0.10 vs. $0.06 expected. Small absolute earnings magnitudes make percentage surprise metrics unreliable.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is structurally challenged in the near term. FY2026 guided revenue of $845-855M is approximately 5% below FY2025's $895M, though this reflects the C&D divestiture rather than organic decline. Core revenue growth turned positive in Q2 FY2026, with indicator testing, pathogens, and biosecurity offsetting declines in genomics and veterinary instruments.

The medium-term growth thesis rests on three pillars:

1. **Petrifilm manufacturing transition completion** — Once Neogen's Lansing facility is operational, the company captures manufacturing margin currently going to 3M under the transition agreement. This is both the most important catalyst and the highest execution risk.

2. **Animal Safety cyclical recovery** — Management describes the segment as "at or near cyclical lows" with potential restocking upside. If animal safety markets normalize, $257M of revenue could stabilize or grow modestly.

3. **Margin expansion from restructuring** — FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA of $175M implies a ~20.5% margin, up from the mid-teens implied by FY2025 operations ex-impairment. This improvement requires volume recovery, manufacturing efficiency gains, and lower restructuring costs.

Working against these, genomics faces a structural headwind from customer insourcing, international revenue (50%) faces tariff and FX exposure in the current macro regime, and food production volumes remain depressed from cumulative inflation. Revenue growth above low-single-digits requires all three pillars to execute simultaneously.

Analyst consensus targets range from $10 to $13, with a median of $12. Only four analysts cover the stock (1 strong buy, 1 buy, 2 hold), providing limited independent validation. The targets imply 15-50% upside from current levels, but these targets were likely set closer to the post-earnings January 2026 enthusiasm when the stock was trading above $10.

## Competitive Landscape

Neogen's competitive position:

**Strengths:** The Petrifilm brand is globally recognized with deep customer penetration in food production facilities. The breadth of Neogen's product portfolio spanning both food and animal safety is unique — no single competitor matches the full range. Customer relationships, once established, are sticky due to regulatory validation requirements for switching diagnostic platforms.

**Weaknesses:** Neogen competes against larger, better-capitalized companies (bioMérieux in pathogen detection, major veterinary firms in animal safety) and nimble specialists. Genomics faces structural insourcing pressure. The 3M integration disruptions — particularly ERP-related fulfillment failures in FY2024 — caused customer service failures that may have permanently shifted some volume to competitors. With $902M in debt and negative FCF, Neogen lacks the financial resources to match R&D spending by larger rivals.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration execution risk (Critical):** The Petrifilm manufacturing transition is the primary risk. Products are still being manufactured in 3M plants under a transition agreement. Any delay, quality issue, or cost overrun at the Lansing facility directly threatens the margin expansion thesis. The company's track record on integration — three years of disruption, ERP failures, lost market share — does not inspire confidence.

**Material weaknesses in internal controls (High):** BDO USA issued an adverse opinion on internal controls as of May 31, 2025. This is year three of material weaknesses covering "substantially all" business and financial reporting processes. While no material misstatements have been identified, the adverse opinion limits institutional investor participation, increases regulatory risk, and raises questions about financial reporting reliability.

**Goodwill impairment risk (Moderate-High):** $1.065B of goodwill remains, representing 31% of assets. In the current rising-rate environment, higher discount rates mechanically increase impairment risk. Any performance shortfall vs. management projections could trigger additional write-downs.

**Macro/tariff exposure (Moderate-High):** With 50% international revenue and U.S.-based manufacturing, tariffs create both input cost pressure (imported materials) and export competitiveness pressure. Management acknowledged inability to "fully mitigate" tariff impacts. In the current stagflation environment with oil at $116, transportation and input cost inflation adds further margin pressure.

**Debt service burden (Moderate):** $72M annual interest expense absorbs the majority of operating cash flow. The 8.625% Senior Notes are expensive fixed-rate debt. With 39% of total debt at variable rates, each 75bp rate increase costs ~$2.6M. In an environment where the Fed may hike rates (35-40% probability in world model), this sensitivity works against them.

**Genomics structural decline:** Revenue has declined three consecutive years ($107M → $102M → $97M) from customer insourcing. This trend appears structural rather than cyclical.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
New CEO Nassif executes the turnaround: Petrifilm manufacturing transitions smoothly to Lansing, capturing manufacturing margins; core revenue growth accelerates to mid-single digits; EBITDA reaches $200M+ by FY2027; material weaknesses are remediated; debt reduction from C&D proceeds and improving FCF creates balance sheet flexibility. Animal safety markets recover from cyclical lows. At $200M EBITDA and 14x, EV of $2.8B implies equity value of ~$9.90/share — modest upside but with potential for multiple expansion if execution proves consistent.

### Bear Case
Petrifilm manufacturing transition encounters delays or quality issues, disrupting the company's most important product line. Stagflation-driven food production volume declines persist, keeping organic revenue growth negative. Tariff escalation compresses margins further. Rising interest rates increase discount rates, triggering additional goodwill impairments on the remaining $1.065B. Material weaknesses persist into year four, limiting institutional ownership and increasing regulatory scrutiny. EBITDA stalls at $150M. At 11x EBITDA and $150M, EV of $1.65B implies equity value of ~$4.60/share — 47% downside.

### Probability-Weighted Assessment
The bull case requires successful execution on multiple simultaneous workstreams (manufacturing transition, margin improvement, revenue stabilization, internal controls remediation) in a hostile macro environment. The bear case requires only one or two of these to go wrong. Asymmetry favors the downside.

## Conclusion

Neogen's turnaround narrative under new CEO Nassif is genuine — FY2026 guidance was raised, core growth inflected positive, and the C&D divestiture was a sensible portfolio simplification. However, the stock at $8.70 already reflects substantial turnaround optimism, having doubled from its April 2025 low of $3.87. The remaining path to value creation requires flawless execution on a Petrifilm manufacturing transition with no precedent at this company, remediation of internal control weaknesses that have persisted for three years, and margin expansion in a stagflation environment with tariff headwinds on 50% of revenue.

At 14.5x guided EBITDA with negative trailing FCF, ongoing material weaknesses, $1.065B of at-risk goodwill, and $900M in debt, the risk-reward profile is unfavorable. The company is improving at the margin, but the balance sheet is fragile and the macro environment works against the thesis. Per calibration guidance (score ≤5.0 → presumption of AVOID; "is this company getting worse or merely bad?"), Neogen is improving from a deeply impaired position, but the pace and magnitude of improvement required to justify the current multiple makes this an AVOID.

**Recommendation: AVOID**
