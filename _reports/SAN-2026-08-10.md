## Executive Summary

Santander has delivered five consecutive years of double-digit attributable-profit growth off the 2020 loss: €8,124m (2021) → €14,101m (2025), a 14.8% annual compound rate, verified from the company's own XBRL-tagged 20-F filings. Earnings per share grew faster still, at roughly 19.6% a year, because the share count fell about 4% annually — approximately one quarter of the EPS growth came from retiring stock rather than from earning more money. The group has simultaneously reshaped its footprint: it sold ~49% of Santander Bank Polska to Erste for €6.8bn (completed 9 January 2026), closed the TSB acquisition in the UK in Q2 2026, and closes the $12.3bn Webster Financial acquisition on 20 August 2026 after receiving OCC, ECB and Federal Reserve approvals in sequence.

The problem is price. The ADR has risen 61.9% over twelve months and trades at $14.705 against a 52-week high of $14.93 and a 52-week low of $8.99. On the brief's reconstruction, the clean trailing multiple — adjusting out the ~€1.9bn Poland disposal gain booked in Q1 2026 — is 13.5-13.8x, versus 5.7x on FY2024 earnings and 5.1-6.6x through 2021-2024. Price to total book is roughly 1.65x, and price to tangible book attributable to the parent is materially higher than that (total equity of €112,748m includes non-controlling interests, AT1 instruments, goodwill and intangibles). Consensus mean target sits at $13.56, below the current price; the median is $15.60 and the high $15.78. Coverage is thin (one to three analysts) and includes a fresh sell rating.

Two unquantified exposures sit against the earnings path. Santander UK declined to publish its scheduled Q3 2025 results citing measurement uncertainty over the FCA motor-finance redress scheme, then took an additional £183m provision at FY2025; the scheme was finalised at £9.1bn industry-wide (~12.1m agreements, ~£829 average) in April 2026, and parts are now suspended pending a judicial review brought by a consumer group arguing the redress is too small, with proceedings expected in December 2026. The skew on that liability runs against Santander. Separately, Santander Brasil — one of the group's largest profit centres — reported Q2 2026 recurring net income of BRL 3.0bn at 12.5% ROAE, reported profit down 17.6% year on year and roughly 23% below the BRL 3.9bn LSEG consensus, on tighter spreads and higher provisions. We rate the shares HOLD: the operating trajectory is intact and the distribution is substantial, but the entry price offers no margin of safety against two live binaries.

## Company Overview

Santander is a retail-led commercial banking group headquartered in Madrid, structured as a network of locally capitalized, largely self-funded national subsidiaries in Spain, the UK, Portugal, the US, Mexico, Brazil, Chile, Argentina and Uruguay, overlaid with five cross-border product lines: Retail & Commercial Banking, Digital Consumer Bank (auto and consumer finance plus the Openbank digital deposit platform), Corporate & Investment Banking, Wealth Management & Insurance, and Payments (Cards and PagoNxt).

On the segment revenue series available (aggregator-sourced, with definitional caveats noted below), Retail & Commercial Banking generated €31,216m of roughly €58,559m FY2025 total income — over half the group. Digital Consumer Bank contributed €13,015m, CIB €8,488m, Wealth Management & Insurance €4,239m, Cards €4,640m and PagoNxt €1,373m. Management stated the group added 12 million net new customers year on year as of Q2 2026, with TSB contributing more than four million UK customers.

The subsidiary-autonomy funding model limits contagion between geographies but has two costs that matter for equity holders: a material portion of group profit accrues to non-controlling interests in listed subsidiaries (Brasil, Chile, México, plus a residual Polska stake), and consolidated CET1 includes capital that is not freely fungible across the group because upstreaming dividends from Latin America is subject to local capital rules and withholding tax.

A note on sourcing: Santander is a foreign private issuer, filing 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A or Form 4. Direct retrieval of the FY2025 20-F narrative was blocked, so the business description, risk factors, MD&A-equivalent and financial statement notes were not read. Attributable profit and total equity are filing facts from the SEC XBRL API; revenue, segment revenue, EPS, DPS and share count are third-party aggregator figures or derivations from them. Where the two conflict, we flag it rather than smoothing it. Three standard screening artefacts should be discarded outright: absent Altman Z / Piotroski scores (the source form does not exist, and Z-Score is not meaningful for a deposit-funded bank), reported "free cash flow" of −€20.7bn and an "FCF margin" of −35.3% (loan growth is an operating outflow under IFRS while its wholesale funding is a financing inflow), and zero insider transactions (no Form 4 obligation exists).

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings (filing facts, € millions):** 2021 8,124 → 2022 9,605 (+18.2%) → 2023 11,076 (+15.3%) → 2024 12,574 (+13.5%) → 2025 14,101 (+12.1%). H1 2024 6,059; H1 2025 6,833.

**Return on equity (derived):** attributable profit over average total equity gives 11.0% (FY2023), 11.9% (FY2024), 12.8% (FY2025). This is not comparable to management's reported RoTE — the numerator is parent-only profit while the denominator includes minorities, AT1 and intangibles — so treat the 11.0% → 12.8% path as a directionally consistent internal trend rather than a level.

**Capital.** CET1 was reported at 14.4% at Q1 2026 with an efficiency ratio of 42.8% (third-party summary of the Q1 call, not filing-confirmed). Management guided to €6.9bn of 2026 buybacks equal to approximately 114bp of CET1, which back-solves to risk-weighted assets of roughly €600-611bn and CET1 capital of roughly €86-88bn. A 14.4% ratio sits well above any plausible SREP requirement plus buffers for this institution, and it is why the group can fund €6.9bn of repurchases and a $12.3bn acquisition in the same year. The proximate source of that headroom is the Poland disposal.

The gap in this picture is material: no source quantifies the CET1 consumption or goodwill created by Webster, and the post-TSB, post-Webster ratio is unknown. The €6.9bn buyback, the TSB integration and the Webster purchase all draw on the same Poland-generated capital release.

**Composition of 2025's growth is the weakest point in the financials.** Attributable profit rose 12.1% while total income moved €58,384m → €58,559m, up 0.3%. Retail & Commercial Banking revenue fell €1,158m (−3.6%), the only major division to decline, while CIB (+1.8%), Wealth Management & Insurance (+11.5%), Cards (+10.0%) and PagoNxt (+10.7%) grew. So 2025's earnings growth came from costs, provisions, tax or below-the-line items rather than franchise expansion. Two candidate explanations for the retail decline — genuine NII compression as ECB and Latin American policy rates normalized and deposit costs caught up, versus presentational effects from reclassifying Santander Bank Polska ahead of the January 2026 completion — cannot be adjudicated without the 20-F segment note. The second would substantially reduce the significance of the decline. The same revenue series shows an implausible +37% jump from FY2022 to FY2023, so its absolute levels are unreliable even where the 2024→2025 year-on-year comparison is internally consistent.

**Q2 2026 is basis-dependent and should be reported as such.** Reported net profit €3.52bn (+3% YoY) against Visible Alpha consensus of €3.53bn — in line. Underlying profit €3.77bn (+17% YoY) — a company-described record, and the basis on which one wire reported a beat. Sequentially, reported profit fell 36% because Q1 included the Poland gain. Total income €15.68bn (+9%) against €15.70bn expected. The quarter carried €250m of TSB integration charges. The ADR EPS surprise data supplied (−4.6%: $0.23 actual vs $0.24 estimate) is directionally consistent with the reported-vs-reported reading; it does not reconcile arithmetically to €3.52bn of attributable profit on our derived ~14,979m share count, which suggests the per-share series is on a different basis (AT1 coupon treatment, or underlying rather than reported). We do not treat the −4.6% figure as evidence of operational deterioration.

**Liquidity, flagged not concluded.** The aggregator series shows "cash & investments" falling from €220.3bn (FY2023) to €192.2bn (FY2024) to €152.3bn (FY2025) to €138.0bn (June 2026) — roughly €82bn, or 37%, over 30 months. The likeliest explanations are eurozone-wide central-bank reserve normalization and TLTRO III repayment, redeployment into loans and amortized-cost securities, and the January 2026 Polska deconsolidation. We do not read it as a liquidity problem, but it is unverified against the 20-F LCR and NSFR disclosures and should not be assumed benign either. Total debt was broadly flat: €325.5bn (FY2024) → €324.4bn (FY2025).

**Consistency note on supplied fields.** The profile reports trailing and forward P/E as identical at 14.59 alongside trailing EPS of $0.936; 14.705 ÷ 0.936 = 15.7x, so the two fields are not reconcilable. We therefore work from the estimate series and the brief's derivations rather than the packaged multiple.

## Growth Analysis

Three drivers carry the forward case, in descending order of verifiability.

**1. Webster Financial (closes 20 August 2026).** From Santander's own release: OCC approval 12 June, ECB 21 July, Federal Reserve 4 August, with Webster shareholders having approved on 27 May. Most of Webster's businesses fold into Santander Bank, N.A. Management guided to US RoTE of approximately 18% by 2028, approximately 7-8% EPS accretion and approximately 15% return on invested capital, all by 2028. Webster's appeal, per third-party analysis, is a low-cost deposit base anchored by HSA Bank — a direct fix for the funding-cost disadvantage that has kept Santander's US franchise the group's weakest on returns while over-weighted to subprime auto. Fed approval arrived 129 days after application, fast for a large foreign-owned bank acquisition. The 7-8% accretion guidance is specific and testable; two years to 2028 also leaves room for slippage, and the capital cost is undisclosed.

**2. The 2028 target.** Management has publicly targeted more than €20bn of net profit in 2028. From the €14,101m FY2025 base that requires 12.4% annual growth for three years — in line with the delivered 12-15% range of the last four years, but on a base that now includes a shrinking retail revenue line, a weakening Brazil and an unquantified UK redress tail. Management reaffirmed that target on 22 July, one week before Santander Brasil missed consensus by 23% — a credibility commitment with real downside if missed.

**3. Share retirement.** Roughly −4% a year, ~−15% cumulative 2021-2025, funded by excess capital and executed while the stock traded below book. €6.9bn is planned for 2026 (≈114bp of CET1), with a €1.8bn ECB-approved tranche announced 22 July and a €10bn cumulative goal on track. Half the Poland proceeds are committed to repurchases.

**Estimate momentum supports the near term but rests on very thin coverage.** Current-year ADR EPS consensus is $1.14 (two analysts), revised +1.2% over 30 days and +5.0% over 90. Next year is $1.43 (one analyst), revised +9.2% over 30 days and +14.6% over 90. The direction is positive and the 90-day next-year revision is large, but a +14.6% revision from a single contributor is not a broad-based estimate inflection that carries predictive weight, and the stock already trades at 10.3x that number. We treat it as corroborating rather than decisive.

**The offsets are concrete.** FY2025 revenue was flat. Q2 2026 reported profit grew 3%. Retail & Commercial Banking, over half of group income, shrank. Brazil's 12.5% ROAE sits below the group's derived 12.8% return on total equity and far below Chile's 31% — the largest Latin American contributor is currently the weakest, and Reuters attributed the deterioration to both spread compression and higher provisions, which is a harder combination to dismiss as timing than provisions alone. Without NPL ratios and stage migration we cannot separate credit cycle from lumpy single-name provisioning.

## Valuation Assessment

**Derived inputs.** Implied shares ≈14,979m (from aggregator net debt per share); implied EUR price ≈€12.42 (cross-checks to $14.705 at 1.183 EUR/USD); implied market capitalization ≈€186bn (≈$220bn), consistent with the characterization of Santander as the largest eurozone bank by market cap. These are reverse-engineered from an internally consistent ratio set, not filing facts, and every multiple below inherits that uncertainty.

**Trailing.** €12.42 ÷ FY2025 EPS €0.90 = 13.8x. On TTM the headline is 11.8x, but TTM includes the ~€1.9bn Poland disposal gain in Q1 2026; removing it gives TTM EPS of ~€0.92 and a clean trailing multiple of ~13.5x. The honest trailing range is 13.5-13.8x, not 11.8x.

**Forward.** $14.705 ÷ $1.14 (FY2026, two analysts) = 12.9x. $14.705 ÷ $1.43 (FY2027, one analyst) = 10.3x. If management delivers >€20bn in 2028 and the ~4% annual share retirement continues, 2028 share count ≈13,254m, 2028 EPS ≈€1.51 (≈$1.79), and the 2028 multiple at today's price is ≈8.2x.

**Book.** €186bn ÷ €112,748m total equity = ≈1.65x. Price to tangible book attributable to the parent is materially higher, because total equity includes minorities, AT1, goodwill and intangibles that we could not size. We deliberately do not publish a P/TBV number, but the direction is unambiguous and it matters: at 1.65x total book, Santander is not trading at a European-bank discount to book on any reasonable adjustment.

**The re-rating is the central valuation fact.** Aggregator P/E history: 6.6x (FY2021), 5.1x (FY2022), 5.8x (FY2023), 5.7x (FY2024), 11.1x (FY2025), ~13.5x clean now. The multiple has roughly doubled in eighteen months on a broadly unchanged growth rate. That is sector-wide — European banks spent 2015-2023 priced as structurally uninvestable on negative rates, fragmentation and capital demands, and 2024-2026 unwound that view. Santander participated fully. The consequence is that the case has shifted from discount closure to delivery: incremental return now depends on the 2028 target and Webster accretion, not on further multiple expansion.

**Distribution yield, corrected.** €6.9bn of 2026 buybacks on ≈€186bn of market cap is 3.7%. A cash dividend base of order €3.5bn adds ≈1.9%, for a total distribution yield of roughly 5.5-5.6% — not the high single digits sometimes quoted. The ADR's reported cash yield of 1.44% is below the €0.24 FY2025 DPS translated at current rates, likely a payment-timing artefact. A 5.5% total distribution yield is a genuine valuation support and the main reason we do not rate the shares REDUCE.

**Where consensus sits.** Mean target $13.56 is 7.8% *below* the current price; median $15.60 is 6.1% above; high $15.78 is 7.3% above; low $9.30 is 37% below. The recommendation mix moved to 1 strong buy / 1 hold / 1 sell, with a strong-sell converting to a sell. The general observation that analyst targets act as floors on inflection stocks applies to names trading cheap against rising estimates with broad coverage; here the stock trades above the mean target on two-to-three-analyst coverage after a 62% twelve-month run, so we treat the target dispersion as evidence of genuine disagreement rather than stale lag.

Valuation score of 5.0: neither stretched nor asymmetric. The 8.2x 2028 arithmetic is real optionality if delivered; the 13.5x clean trailing and ~1.65x total book, with P/TBV higher, remove the margin of safety that made this a compelling name in 2024.

## Competitive Landscape

Santander's competitive trajectory is genuinely improving in structure and mix, and flat-to-deteriorating in its largest engines.

*Improving:* it exited a sub-scale country position (Poland) and redeployed into density in two markets where it already held a licence and a subscale franchise (UK via TSB, US via Webster). That is the operating mode of European banking in this cycle — funding density in core markets by selling sub-scale geographies — and Santander is executing it more aggressively than most peers. Product factories are gaining: Wealth Management & Insurance +11.5%, Cards +10.0%, PagoNxt +10.7% in FY2025, a mix shift toward fee-based, capital-light income. Twelve million net new customers year on year.

*Not improving:* Retail & Commercial Banking, still over half of group income, shrank 3.6% in FY2025. In Spain, mortgage and deposit spread pressure appears real as eurozone rates normalized. In the UK, the franchise is materially larger post-TSB but the market is intensely competitive on mortgages and carries the redress overhang; Santander UK's public framing of the FCA scheme as a threat to UK jobs and growth is unusually confrontational for a regulated subsidiary and signals how material management judges the cost. In Brazil, 12.5% ROAE on tighter spreads and higher provisions indicates competitive pricing pressure alongside credit normalization. The US has historically been the group's weakest franchise on returns, and Webster is an explicit attempt to fix funding cost and business mix rather than an extension of existing strength.

One claim we decline to make: growing CIB, wealth and payments revenue does not establish that the group has diversified away from retail rate sensitivity. Revenue mix change and reduced concentration are different claims, and Retail & Commercial still represents over half of income.

## Risk Assessment

**1. UK motor finance redress — the largest unquantified contingent liability.** The disclosure trail: FCA consultation on an industry-wide redress scheme covering 2007-2024 agreements (7 October 2025); Santander UK declined to publish scheduled Q3 2025 results citing uncertainty over scope, methodology and timing (29 October 2025); additional £183m provision at FY2025 results (4 February 2026); scheme finalised at £9.1bn industry-wide, ~12.1m agreements, ~£829 average (April 2026); Santander agreed to participate; consumer group Consumer Voice launched a judicial review arguing the redress short-changes claimants (22 April 2026); FCA confirmed parts of the scheme are suspended pending legal challenge with proceedings expected December 2026 (2 July 2026). A listed subsidiary declining to publish a scheduled quarterly result indicates management judged the provision range too wide to disclose reliably. That is a statement about measurement uncertainty; conservative IAS 37 recognition discipline is an equally available reading, and we infer no motive. What matters for the equity is the direction of the tail: the live challenge argues redress is too *small*, so a December 2026 ruling that expands scope or methodology raises the provision. The verifiable cumulative provision is the £183m taken in FY2025; Santander's share of the £9.1bn is not disclosed in anything available.

**2. Brazil credit and spread deterioration.** The weakest quarter on profitability since late 2023, on tighter lending spreads and higher provisions. Group "on track" messaging on 22 July preceded this print on 29 July. Brazilian provisioning is lumpy and a single large exposure or model recalibration can produce a one-quarter step-up, so this is not yet established as a portfolio-wide credit turn. It is the single most likely source of a 2027 estimate cut.

**3. Webster execution and undisclosed capital cost.** The group's largest US acquisition, closing 20 August 2026, into the franchise with its weakest historical returns, with no publicly quantified CET1 consumption or goodwill. Accretion guidance runs to 2028.

**4. Buyback permission risk.** On 23 April 2026 Santander temporarily suspended a €5bn buyback to comply with US regulation — the literal mechanism being that US banking rules constrain an acquirer's distributions while a merger application is pending before the Federal Reserve. It resumed in tranches with ECB approval for €1.8bn announced 22 July. Anyone underwriting the roughly 4 percentage points a year of EPS growth from share retirement should model it as a permissioned, interruptible flow contingent on two regulators and on M&A timing, not as policy. (Single secondary source, not filing-confirmed.)

**5. Structural frictions.** Minority interests in listed subsidiaries mean attributable profit understates operating earnings while overstating cash available to the parent; upstreaming from Brazil, Mexico and Chile faces local capital rules and withholding tax; consolidated CET1 includes non-fungible capital. Argentine hyperinflation accounting and Latin American FX translation can move OCI and reported equity materially. The status and quantum of the Spanish banking levy in 2026 could not be established and no figure should be assumed.

**6. Governance and disclosure gaps.** No SEC-filed proxy or Form 4 exists, so executive compensation alignment, board independence and related-party transactions are unassessed — empty rather than negative. The FY2025 20-F narrative was not read, so we cannot report which risk factors were added or modified. Reported H1 2026 attributable profit, the current CET1 ratio, the €1.9bn Poland gain, the TSB purchase price and the €82bn decline in liquid assets all remain unverified.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 28.1% sits below one-year realized volatility of 33.4% (spread −5.2%), at the 18th percentile of the current chain, with a contango term structure (28.1% near, 31.9% long-dated). The market is not pricing a near-term event, despite Webster closing on 20 August, inside the 10-day tenor. Near-term put/call skew of 8.5% is described as elevated put demand and the put/call volume ratio is 2.14, but open-interest put/call is only 0.51 and the 3-month and 6-month skews are negative; the chain carries just 40 contracts across three expirations, so the volume-based readings are low-confidence noise. Short interest is negligible at 0.7% of float. The composite read: cheap near-term optionality with no positioning consensus. For a holder concerned about the December 2026 redress proceedings, longer-dated protection is priced at a modest premium to realized volatility rather than at a stress level.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $18.50 (+26%)

Webster closes on schedule and delivers the guided 7-8% EPS accretion and ~18% US RoTE, converting the group's worst-returning franchise into a contributor and validating management's redeployment of Poland proceeds. TSB integration completes within the €250m-per-quarter charge cadence and the UK franchise scales into a larger deposit base. The €6.9bn 2026 buyback executes in full and continues at similar scale, sustaining ~4% annual share retirement. Brazil's Q2 proves to be lumpy provisioning rather than a credit turn, and Chile's 31% ROAE shows what the Latin American book can produce. The FCA scheme survives the December 2026 challenge broadly as finalised, capping Santander's incremental exposure near existing provisions. On that path, management's >€20bn 2028 net profit is reachable, implying 2028 EPS near €1.51 (~$1.79); a 10.5x multiple on that gives ~$18.75, and the shares reach $18.50 within 18-24 months while paying a ~5.5% total distribution yield along the way.

### Bear Case — $10.75 (−27%)

The December 2026 judicial review expands the FCA scheme's scope or methodology, and Santander's share of a larger industry liability requires provisions materially above the £183m already taken, with payouts slipping into 2027. Brazil's spread compression and provisioning prove cyclical rather than one-off, pulling group cost of risk higher and 2027 estimates down. Webster integration consumes more capital and management attention than guided, and the CET1 ratio — currently unverified post-close — constrains the buyback that supplies roughly 4 percentage points of EPS growth. Spanish retail spread compression continues as eurozone deposit repricing works through, and European macro deterioration raises credit costs across the eurozone book. In that scenario FY2027 ADR EPS lands nearer $1.15 and the multiple compresses toward 9x — a European-bank multiple appropriate to single-digit growth — giving $10.35. We use $10.75 as the bear target, still above the $8.99 52-week low.

### Base Case — $15.50 (+5.4%)

Webster closes and delivers partial accretion by 2027, TSB integration proceeds, the buyback continues at reduced pace during integration, Brazil stabilizes at low-teens ROAE without a step-change in cost of risk, and the UK redress resolves within a range that requires incremental but not transformative provisioning. FY2027 ADR EPS of $1.35 — between the single-analyst $1.43 and a haircut for Brazil and redress — at 11.5x gives $15.53. Add roughly 1.4% of cash dividend and the twelve-month total return is approximately 7%.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives an expected price return of roughly +2%, or approximately +4% including the cash dividend. That is below what we would require to add capital and above what would justify trimming a position held for the distribution.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**

2. The December 2026 judicial review resolves without expanding the FCA scheme's scope or methodology, and Santander quantifies its total motor-finance exposure at a level requiring no material incremental provision beyond amounts already taken.
3. Santander Brasil returns to ROAE above 15% for two consecutive quarters with a stable or falling cost of risk, confirming Q2 2026 was provisioning noise rather than a credit turn.
4. Price near $12.25-12.75 on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore roughly 15-20% upside to our base case and put the shares back near 9x the FY2027 estimate.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any incremental UK motor-finance provision above roughly €1bn, or an adverse December 2026 ruling expanding the redress scheme's scope.
2. A cut, suspension or material reduction in the 2026-2027 buyback programme for reasons other than short procedural M&A constraints — this removes the mechanical component of EPS growth.
3. Brazilian ROAE below 11% for two consecutive quarters, or a group cost-of-risk increase of more than 15bp year on year.
4. Withdrawal or downward revision of the >€20bn 2028 net-profit target, or the Webster 7-8% EPS accretion guidance.
5. Consensus current-year ADR EPS revised below $1.05, reversing the current +5.0% 90-day trend.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Webster close on 20 August 2026 (watch for disclosure of purchase accounting and the pro-forma CET1 impact), Q3 2026 results in late October (first quarter with a full TSB contribution and the first with any Webster contribution; also the first read on whether Brazil's Q2 was cyclical), and the December 2026 FCA judicial-review proceedings. We would revisit the rating formally after Q3 results and again after the December ruling.

## Conclusion

The operating record is real and verifiable from Santander's own filings: attributable profit compounding at 14.8% a year since 2021, five consecutive years of double-digit growth, 12.8% return on total equity in FY2025, a 14.4% CET1 ratio that funds both a €6.9bn buyback and a $12.3bn acquisition, and roughly 15% of the share count retired over four years. The portfolio reshaping — out of sub-scale Poland at €6.8bn, into the UK via TSB and the US via Webster — is coherent and, in the Webster case, addresses the group's clearest structural weakness with a low-cost HSA-anchored deposit base.

What has changed is the price paid for that record. The clean trailing multiple is 13.5-13.8x against 5.7x on FY2024 earnings; price to total book is ~1.65x with tangible book coverage materially thinner; the ADR is up 61.9% over twelve months and sits at 98.5% of its 52-week high, above the $13.56 consensus mean. The two-analyst FY2026 estimate implies 12.9x and a single-analyst FY2027 implies 10.3x, which is not demanding, but the coverage is too thin to treat rising estimates as a broad signal, and the FY2027 number does not yet reflect the Brazil miss.

Set against that: an unquantified UK motor-finance liability whose live legal challenge argues redress is too small, resolving in December 2026; the group's largest Latin American contributor earning 12.5% ROAE on compressing spreads and rising provisions; a $12.3bn US integration with undisclosed capital cost; and an EPS engine that depends 4 percentage points a year on repurchases requiring ECB permission, which were briefly suspended in April 2026. A roughly 5.5% total distribution yield and a defensible 2028 path justify holding. Neither justifies paying $14.705 for a base case of $15.50 with a $10.75 bear case. HOLD, with the entry we would want at $12.25-12.75 or after the December ruling clears.
