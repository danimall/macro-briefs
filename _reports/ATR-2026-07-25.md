**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AptarGroup is a global leader in drug and consumer-product dosing, dispensing, and protection systems, anchored by a differentiated Pharma franchise (~46% of sales, ~69% of segment EBITDA, 35% Pharma EBITDA margin) that rides secular tailwinds in injectable components for GLP-1s and biologics, emergency/CNS nasal delivery, and eye care. The quality of that Pharma business would support a premium multiple in isolation. The problem is that the consolidated company is not currently growing organically, and its profitability is moving the wrong way. FY2025 core sales grew just 2% (reported +5% flattered by 2% FX and 1% M&A); Q1 2026 core sales were flat, with reported +11% almost entirely FX (+8%) and acquisitions (+3%). Operating margin slipped to 13.3% in FY2025 from 13.8%, then compressed sharply to 10.9% in Q1 2026 from 12.8%, with all three segments deteriorating (Pharma 33.3%, Beauty 11.1%, Closures 13.1%).

Reported earnings are also being flattered. FY2025 net income of $392.8M and EPS of $5.89 included a $26.5M one-time BTY remeasurement gain (~$0.40/share); adjusted EPS declined to $5.74 from $5.81, and Q1 2026 adjusted EPS fell to $1.19 from $1.30. The clean underlying trend is margin compression. Consensus estimate revisions confirm this: next-quarter EPS has been cut 6.1% over 90 days and next-year 1.8%, with Zacks flagging a YoY earnings decline on higher revenue for Q2 2026. This is the opposite of the positive-revision setup that would justify chasing.

At ~$132.36 the stock trades at ~22x trailing and ~24x forward EPS on falling estimates — a premium to the peer median of ~16.4x that is only partly defensible by Pharma mix. Analyst targets ($140–$220, mean $165) sit above the current price, but those targets embed a margin recovery that the trailing and forward data do not yet confirm. We rate ATR a HOLD. The Pharma franchise, fortress-adjacent balance sheet, and 32-year dividend-growth record make it worth owning through a cycle, but the flat organic engine, compressing margins, the ARS/neffy litigation overhang, and Beauty goodwill risk leave no asymmetric entry at today's price. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the low-$110s or a confirmed margin/revision inflection.

## Company Overview

Founded in the late 1940s making aerosol valves and headquartered in Crystal Lake, Illinois, AptarGroup is now a ~$3.8B-revenue, ~14,000-employee company operating in roughly 20 countries. It is heavily international: ~49% of sales are Europe-shipped, ~31% domestic, and ~20% Asia/Latin America, with most production outside the U.S. The customer base is diversified across ~5,000 customers, none exceeding 4% of sales.

The company runs three reporting segments:

- **Pharma (~46% of sales, ~69% of segment Adjusted EBITDA):** Nasal spray pumps, metered-dose inhaler valves, injectable elastomeric components (PremiumCoat coated stoppers/plungers), active material science, and digital health. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA margin 35.0% (up from 34.6%). Sub-markets: prescription drug 51%, consumer healthcare 20%, injectables 19%, active material science 10%. This is the value driver, with regulatory co-development know-how creating multi-year switching costs.
- **Beauty (~35% of sales, ~18% of segment EBITDA):** Fragrance, skincare, and cosmetics pumps and airless systems. The weakest business, with EBITDA margin declining to ~12% and to 11.1% in Q1 2026, exposed to prestige-fragrance cyclicality, weak North American indie skincare, tariffs, and low-cost Asian competition.
- **Closures (~19% of sales, ~13% of segment EBITDA):** Dispensing closures for food, beverage, and personal care, a steady ~16% EBITDA-margin business that softened to 13.1% in Q1 2026.

The moat is strongest in regulated Pharma (FDA combination-product expertise, injectable components for biologics/GLP-1/vaccines, years of qualification) and thinnest in Beauty and Closures, where the company faces price competition from Silgan, Berry, Albéa, Sonoco, and Asian suppliers. In injectables specifically, it competes against West Pharmaceutical and Datwyler.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Annual revenue progressed $3.32B (2022) → $3.49B (2023) → $3.58B (2024) → $3.78B (2025). The FY2025 +5% headline decomposes into +2% core, +2% FX, +1% acquisitions. Q1 2026 revenue rose 11% reported to $982.9M but core sales were flat, with +8% FX and +3% acquisitions doing all the work. The organic engine is idling.

**Profitability and margins.** Net income rose to $392.8M in 2025 from $374.5M and EPS to $5.89 (+7%, aided by buybacks), but operating margin slipped to 13.3% from 13.8% on adverse mix, operational inefficiencies, higher D&A, and tariff drag in Beauty. Q1 2026 compression was driven by tough emergency-medicine comps in Pharma, lower tooling margins, and higher D&A. Volumes are still growing, so this is a mix/cost-timing story rather than a demand collapse, but the direction across all three segments is unfavorable.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $570M (down from $643M on tax-payment timing) and FCF roughly $300M (~8% margin) against capex near 7% of sales. The company returned $485.8M to shareholders in 2025 ($120.8M dividends + $365M buybacks), well above FCF, with the gap funded by debt. It raised the quarterly dividend to $0.48, marking a 32nd consecutive year of increases, and authorized a fresh $600M buyback in February 2026 ($500M remaining after Q1). Capex is guided up to $310–320M in 2026, which will pressure FCF and the buyback pace if margins do not recover.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt rose to $1.48B in 2025 from $1.03B, driven by the November 2025 issuance of $600M 4.75% senior notes due 2031, which replaced cheaper 3.6% notes and raised the average cost of debt (interest expense +$8.8M in 2025, +$5.6M in Q1 2026). Leverage remains conservative: consolidated leverage of 1.43x against a 3.50x covenant, interest coverage ~13.7x, current ratio 1.62. The Altman Z of 2.22 ("grey zone") overstates risk here; it reflects a capital-intensive industrial with moderate leverage, not credit stress. Financial health is solid, but the above-FCF return policy meeting a rising cost of capital and rising capex is a watch item, not a near-term concern.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story bifurcates sharply. Pharma injectables (+11% core in 2025, +20% core in Q1 2026) is a genuine secular winner tied to GLP-1 and biologics fill-finish demand, and emergency medicine/CNS nasal delivery adds a second growth vector. This is the part of the portfolio the market, and management's increasing presence at healthcare conferences, wants to reframe as a healthcare compounder.

At the consolidated level, however, organic growth is flat. Beauty is a structural drag: soft prestige fragrance, weak North American indie skincare, tariff cost pass-through, and Asian competition are compressing margins and offsetting Pharma's momentum. Closures is steady but low-growth. The reported top-line strength is currency and small bolt-ons (Sommaplast, the BTY stake increase, the Goldrain minority interest), none of which represent durable organic acceleration.

Estimate revisions reinforce the caution. Current-year EPS consensus is $5.49 (down 0.5% over 30 days, roughly flat over 90); next-quarter is $1.46, cut 1.4% over 30 days and 6.1% over 90; next-year is $6.25, down 1.8% over 90 days. This is broadly falling earnings momentum, the opposite of the positive-revision profile that flags a strong buy. Management guided Q2 2026 adjusted EPS to $1.32–$1.40 (assuming €1.18 and a 22.5–24.5% tax rate), and consensus expects a YoY earnings decline on higher revenue.

Longer-term, regulatory pressure on plastics (EU PPWR, PFAS bans, tethered-cap mandates) cuts both ways: it raises reformulation costs but also creates demand for Aptar's recyclable/mono-material designs. The HFC propellant phase-down is a manageable headwind for MDIs given the pharma exemption. Net, the growth durability rests on Pharma; the rest of the portfolio caps blended organic growth in the low single digits.

## Valuation Assessment

At $132.36, ATR trades at ~22x trailing EPS and ~24x forward on the $5.49 current-year consensus, a meaningful premium to the peer median P/E of 16.4x (Silgan 15.1x, Ball 16.4x, Greif 21.5x; O-I is loss-making). The dividend yield of ~1.5% is below the peer median of 2.1%.

The Pharma mix justifies some premium: a 35%-EBITDA-margin, regulatorily-moated drug-delivery business is worth more than a commodity container maker. But this is not a cycle-adjusted-cheap situation. Trailing earnings are not a trough; adjusted EPS is declining, estimate revisions are negative, and margins are compressing. The cycle-adjusted-valuation and analyst-targets-as-floors principles that apply to inflection stocks (positive revisions, accelerating revenue) run the other way here; estimates are falling, so consensus targets embed a margin recovery that the data have not yet delivered. On falling estimates, those targets are as likely to be revised down as up.

Treating a below-peer-implied "quality premium" as automatically cheap is the value-trap pattern to avoid: the multiple is elevated, not compressed, and there is no identified near-term catalyst forcing a re-rating. A reasonable base case applies ~22x to a normalized ~$6.00 of earnings power (Pharma reinflecting, Beauty stabilizing), yielding roughly $132, right at spot, implying the stock is fairly valued rather than mispriced. The asymmetric entry exists lower, in the low-$110s, where the multiple would sit near ~19x forward and the dividend/downside support firms up. We score valuation 4.5: not a value trap in the impairment sense, but a full multiple on decelerating fundamentals with no independent catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Aptar's competitive position is stable-to-improving in Pharma and stable-to-eroding in Beauty. In regulated drug delivery (injectable elastomer components, nasal pumps, MDI valves) the moat is durable: high-precision molding to 1/1000-inch tolerances, clean-room manufacturing, FDA combination-product qualification, and multi-year switching costs. Injectables growth confirms share gains into the GLP-1/biologics wave, where it competes with West and Datwyler. Digital health and active material science add optionality.

In Beauty and Closures the position is weaker. Beauty is the only reporting unit that required a quantitative goodwill test in 2025, with margins declining from 13.0% to 12.1% to 11.1% amid Asian low-cost competition and soft demand. This is the segment where competitive pressure is visible in the numbers.

The ARS Pharmaceuticals/neffy dispute complicates the Pharma moat narrative. As the device supplier and plaintiff in the trade-secret misappropriation case (SDNY), Aptar received a favorable procedural signal when the court denied ARS's motion to dismiss in substantial part on March 30, 2026, and ARS filed no counterclaims. But ARS's separate antitrust suit (S.D. Cal., alleging Aptar refused to sell components) shows a key customer relationship turned adversarial and puts the trade-secret-based moat under legal test. Net, we hold competitive position at 5.5: improving where it matters most (injectables) but capped by Beauty erosion and the litigation overhang.

## Risk Assessment

1. **ARS/neffy litigation (most idiosyncratic and material).** Two intertwined cases: Aptar's trade-secret claim against ARS (favorable procedural posture, no counterclaims) and ARS's antitrust claim against Aptar (motion to dismiss/transfer pending). This cost $8.4M in special items in FY2025 and continues in 2026. The outcome affects both a live customer relationship and the antitrust exposure; the company states it cannot estimate possible loss.

2. **Beauty goodwill impairment.** The $370.8M Beauty carrying value passed the 2025 quantitative test (fair value exceeded carrying value, and a 10% haircut would not have changed the conclusion), but margins are deteriorating. Further weakness in prestige fragrance/skincare or intensifying Asian competition is the most plausible negative accounting catalyst.

3. **Margin compression.** The clearest near-term risk is that mix/cost pressures persist rather than reverse, extending the Q1 2026 operating-margin decline into H2.

4. **FX dependence.** With ~49% Europe exposure, much of recent reported "growth" is currency; a dollar reversal would flip the tailwind to a headwind.

5. **Tariffs/trade policy.** A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidated IEEPA-based tariffs (Aptar is pursuing refunds as an unrecorded gain contingency), but a new ≥10% Section 122 surcharge was imposed; net effect uncertain.

6. **Nemera IP litigation** (ophthalmic; German trial June 2026, French January 2027) and customs assessments (~$10–11M principal plus ~$21–22M interest/penalties, contested), manageable but live.

7. **Capital-allocation/leverage.** Above-FCF returns funded by 4.75% debt against rising capex; sustainable near-term at 1.43x leverage but reduces flexibility if margins stay soft.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 39.5% is rich versus 26.5% one-year historical vol (13-point spread, 66th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (39.5% near-term vs 29.8% at six months), signaling the market is pricing imminent event stress consistent with the Q2 2026 earnings print. IV rose 9.2 points as the stock climbed 10.3% since late June, so the market is not complacent into the report. The put/call volume ratio of 1.50 and modestly negative six-month skew (-3.6%) indicate some downside hedging, but skew is not extreme. Short interest is low at 3.3% of float (3.8 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The elevated near-term IV argues against chasing ahead of earnings; a better entry likely follows the post-print vol crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$165, +25%).** Pharma injectables sustains ~20% core growth into the GLP-1/biologics fill-finish wave, emergency-medicine comps normalize, Beauty margins stabilize, and consolidated operating margin recovers toward 14%+. FX stays a tailwind, the ARS trade-secret case resolves favorably (validating the moat), and the market re-rates ATR as a healthcare/pharma compounder at ~26x. Consensus targets are hit as estimates turn up.

**Bear case (~$105, -21%).** Margin compression persists (Q1 2026's 10.9% operating margin proves a run-rate rather than a trough), Beauty deteriorates into a goodwill impairment, the FX tailwind reverses, and the ARS antitrust case creates a material overhang or damages the neffy supply relationship. Estimates continue falling, and the premium multiple compresses toward the ~16x peer median on flat organic growth, taking the stock to the low-$100s.

**Base case (~$132, flat).** Organic growth stays low-single-digit, Pharma offsets Beauty weakness, margins bottom near current levels and recover modestly, and the stock earns its dividend plus mid-single-digit EPS growth. ~22x on normalized ~$6.00 earnings power ≈ $132, right at spot.

The distribution is roughly symmetric-to-slightly-favorable but lacks a near-term catalyst to force the upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (guidance $1.32–$1.40 adjusted EPS), which will confirm whether operating margin bottomed in Q1 or continues compressing. Beyond that, the German Nemera trial (June 2026) and progress on the ARS cases.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Operating margin reinflects above 13% for two consecutive quarters, or core sales growth accelerates to mid-single-digits ex-FX.
- Consensus EPS revisions turn positive (30-day and 90-day both green), reversing the current -6.1% next-quarter trend.
- Price pullback toward the low-$110s, where forward P/E compresses to ~19x and risk/reward turns asymmetric.
- Favorable resolution/dismissal of the ARS antitrust suit removing the overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Beauty goodwill impairment charge, or Beauty EBITDA margin falling below 10%.
- Q2/Q3 2026 operating margin deteriorating further below Q1's 10.9%, confirming compression is structural not transient.
- An adverse ARS antitrust ruling, or loss/material deterioration of the neffy supply relationship.
- Estimate cuts accelerating (next-year revision worse than -5% over 90 days) while the multiple stays above 22x.

## Conclusion

AptarGroup owns a high-quality Pharma/injectables franchise and a fortress-adjacent balance sheet with a 32-year dividend-growth record, but the consolidated business is not currently growing organically, margins are compressing across all three segments, and reported earnings are being flattered by a one-time gain and FX. The stock trades at a full multiple on falling estimates, a premium to peers that the current fundamentals only partly justify. There is no identified catalyst forcing a near-term re-rating, and the options market is pricing event stress into the next print.

We rate ATR a **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale. This is a company worth owning through a cycle for the Pharma engine and dividend, but the flat organic growth, negative revision momentum, ARS litigation overhang, and Beauty goodwill risk leave no asymmetric entry at $132. We would upgrade on a confirmed margin/revision inflection or a pullback toward the low-$110s, and downgrade on a Beauty impairment, continued margin deterioration, or an adverse ARS antitrust outcome. The score trajectory (6.0 → 4.8 over four months, with the June AVOID) reflected the same deterioration; the modest price recovery since does not change the underlying picture, as margins and estimates remain the swing factors, and both are still pointing down.
