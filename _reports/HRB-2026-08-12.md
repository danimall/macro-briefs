**Research Date: August 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

H&R Block reported FY2026 (year ended June 30, 2026) revenue of $3,945.4M, up 4.9%, EBITDA of $1,056.9M, up 8.3% with 83bp of margin expansion to 26.8%, operating cash flow of $838.7M, up 23.2%, and free cash flow of $756.1M after $82.6M of capex. FY2027 guidance of $4.11–4.16B revenue, $1.11–1.14B adjusted EBITDA and $6.04–6.24 adjusted diluted EPS sits roughly 6% above the $5.79 consensus for next year. The company retired 10.5 million shares — 7.9% of the count — for $500.3M at an average $47.48, raised the dividend 10% to $0.46 quarterly (ninth consecutive increase), and guided to a further ~$400M of repurchases in FY2027. Four consecutive quarterly EPS beats support confidence in the guide.

The arithmetic of the equity story is straightforward and does not require a growth inflection. At the authoritative close of $46.67, HRB trades at 7.6x the guided FY2027 adjusted EPS midpoint of $6.14, 5.6x EV/guided adjusted EBITDA, and a 13.1% free cash flow yield on equity, against a specialized-consumer-services peer median P/E of 22.2x. A 3.94% dividend plus ~6% annual share shrink delivers roughly a 10% cash return before any multiple change, and my reconstruction of the FY2027 bridge (adjusted EBITDA $1,125M less ~$80M adjusted D&A less ~$80M interest, taxed at the guided 23%, divided by the $6.14 midpoint) implies ~121.0M weighted diluted shares versus 128.87M in FY2026 — internally coherent, with roughly 8% adjusted net income growth and ~6% share shrink producing ~15.6% EPS growth.

What the print does not resolve is the reason the stock traded at $28.16 in February 2026. Total system assisted volume was flat for the season; company-owned assisted volume rose ~2.7% because the company bought ~160 franchise offices, transferring returns from the franchise ledger (royalties fell 3.9%; franchise offices fell from 2,013 to 1,814) to the company ledger. DIY online paid volume fell 4.2% in the very season the free IRS Direct File alternative was withdrawn, with revenue held flat only by ~3.9% pricing. Short interest is 22.0% of float at 9.2 days to cover. The bear thesis is that general-purpose AI progressively absorbs the simple-to-moderate filing tier; this data is consistent with it, and one season does not adjudicate it. We rate the shares ACCUMULATE on the authoritative price basis, with an explicit caveat on entry: the shares were indicated 13.2% above the official close in extended hours following the release, and at ~$53 the margin of safety is largely consumed.

## Company Overview

H&R Block is a single-segment provider of individual and small-business tax preparation, organized in Missouri in 1955 and operating through two channels plus attached financial products. FY2025 filings disclose 11.3 million U.S. assisted returns and 3.8 million paid DIY online returns, contributing to 22.8 million total returns filed by or through the company across the U.S., Canada and Australia. As of March 31, 2026 the network comprised 6,802 company-owned and 1,814 franchise offices, a net contraction of 98 units year over year alongside a deliberate shift from franchise to company ownership.

FY2026 revenue mix: U.S. assisted preparation $2,560.9M (64.9% of total, +6.1%); U.S. DIY $384.6M (9.7%, +0.2%); royalties $185.4M (−3.9%); Refund Transfers $145.1M (+5.5%); financial services $99.5M (−2.3%); international $265.4M (+7.4%); Wave small-business SaaS $122.7M (+12.3%). The U.S. tax-preparation bundle is 87.6% of revenue, and it is overwhelmingly human-delivered.

The structural feature that dominates the financial statements is seasonality: substantially all revenue and cash flow are generated February through April, with losses in the first two fiscal quarters, and peak seasonal headcount of roughly 70,100 against ~4,300 regular full-time associates. Business is not asset-intensive — FY2026 EBIT of $934.5M on $3,256.4M of total assets is a 28.7% pre-tax return on assets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** FY2022 $3,463.3M → FY2023 $3,472.2M → FY2024 $3,610.3M → FY2025 $3,761.0M → FY2026 $3,945.4M. Growth has modestly accelerated across three years (+4.0%, +4.2%, +4.9%). Operating margin computed as (revenue less total operating expenses)/revenue improved from 22.0% to 23.0%.

One quality-of-earnings item deserves attention: marketing and advertising expense fell $8.0M to $277.8M, against an $80.6M EBITDA increase, so roughly 10% of the EBITDA gain came from lower marketing spend in a year DIY units declined. Q4 marketing was up 7.4%, so the decline was concentrated in the first three fiscal quarters and looks more like reallocation than retrenchment. At $8M on a $3.9B base I would not overweight it, but it is spend reduction rather than operating leverage.

**Cash flow.** OCF of $838.7M exceeded net income of $733.6M despite a $97.1M cash outflow tied to income tax receivables and reserves associated with the IRS settlement. The $84.1M discrete tax benefit recognized in Q3 FY2026 ($0.65 of FY2026 EPS) was accretive to reported GAAP earnings but cash-negative in the period; cash taxes paid fell to $202.1M from $226.8M as reserves unwound. The OCF improvement is therefore not an artifact of the tax benefit.

Interim cash flow cannot be annualized here. Nine-month OCF was $586.7M against $838.7M full-year, with Q4 contributing $252.0M. Receivables ran $63.6M (6/30/25) → $297.6M (3/31/26) → $58.2M (6/30/26), a $234M intra-year build and release driven by unbilled assisted fees recoverable from client refunds.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $3,256.4M, total liabilities $3,138.9M, stockholders' equity +$117.5M, cash $958.7M, long-term debt $1,491.5M with zero current portion, revolver undrawn. Net debt $532.8M equals 0.50x FY2026 EBITDA; interest coverage is 13.1x on $80.6M of interest expense. Debt sits in three tranches (2.500% due July 2028 at $500M, 3.875% due August 2030 at $650M, 5.375% due September 2032 at $350M) with no maturity inside two years. The August 2025 refinancing of $350M of 5.250% October 2025 notes into 2032 paper cost 12.5bp of coupon for seven years of extension; total interest expense rose only 3.2%. The $1.5B revolver runs to July 2030 with a debt/EBITDA covenant of 3.50x at March/June/September quarter-ends and 4.50x at December quarter-ends, and a 2.50x coverage minimum. Headroom is very wide.

Book equity is near zero by construction and swings deeply negative intra-year: +$88.9M (7/1/25) → −$550.9M (9/30/25) → −$823.1M (12/31/25) → −$24.4M (3/31/26) → +$117.5M (6/30/26). The company discloses this as structural. Any screen keyed to book equity or run off a non-June quarter-end will misprice this business — a recomputed Altman Z using market rather than book equity and correct EBIT gives 3.48 on FY2026 data, in the safe zone, and the current ratio improved from 0.90 to 1.13 as the prior current portion of debt was refinanced. That said, the underlying feature the screens grope toward is real: capitalizing $610.9M of operating leases lifts EV/EBITDA from ~6.0x to ~6.6x, and the Baa3/BBB ratings sit one notch above high yield at Moody's, a less benign view than 0.50x reported net leverage alone implies.

**Capital allocation.** FY2026 returned $711.3M — 94.1% of free cash flow — via $500.3M of repurchases and $211.0M of dividends, plus $57.6M of franchise acquisitions. The repurchase cadence was lumpy: ~$400M at ~$50.81 average in Q1 (July–September 2025), essentially nothing in January–March 2026, and ~$100M at roughly $38 in Q4. That pattern reflects cash availability — the company has no cash to deploy during the working-capital build — and it means the buyback cannot be opportunistic. Buying $400M at $61.10 average in FY2025 was, in hindsight, poorly timed; buying nothing at the February low of $28.16 was a missed opportunity the seasonal cash cycle made structural rather than discretionary.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 4.9% decomposes into price, mix and franchise internalization, with essentially no net unit growth. Three volume measures must not be conflated. For the nine months, company-owned assisted volume rose 2.7% while total assisted volume (company-owned plus franchise) rose 0.6%; for the full season through April 30, 2026, total assisted volume was flat. Net average charge rose 4.0%, and per secondary reporting of the August 11 call, the full-year 4.1% NAC gain decomposed into roughly 3% price and 1% mix. The gap between company-owned and system volume is the franchise-to-company transfer, which the filings state explicitly increases tax preparation revenue and decreases royalties.

This is legitimate value capture — converting a ~30% royalty into full revenue economics — but it is finite and management is already slowing it, guiding to 100–125 franchise buybacks in FY2027 versus ~160 in FY2026. At that pace the remaining 1,814 franchise offices represent perhaps 12–18 years of runway, and the mechanical drag on royalties continues.

DIY is the clear weak spot. Paid online volume fell 4.2% for the season, revenue was flat at $384.6M for the year and declined 2.2% in Q4. The FY2026 season is the cleanest natural experiment available: multiple secondary reports indicate the IRS Direct File program was shut down and unavailable for 2026 after roughly 300,000 users in 2025. A named strategic risk was removed and the paid DIY funnel still shrank. Direct File was small enough (300,000 returns against 3.8M paid DIY returns) that its removal was never going to be decisive, so the two facts may be unrelated, but the negative unit trend stands on its own, and I cannot identify from the available data who gained the share.

Management's counter-evidence is real but is engagement and mix data rather than share data: assisted conversion improved 200bp and retention 190bp (the largest single-year conversion gain the company has recorded, per the call), the $50,000–$200,000 AGI cohort rose to 50% of clients from 38% over several years, Second Look drove >600bp higher return rates among new clients receiving it, and AI Tax Assist supported 4.2 million client interactions, nearly double prior year. A favorable AGI mix does not establish that the DIY units lost were low-value; both should be reported and neither used to neutralize the other.

FY2027 guidance implies revenue +4.2% to +5.4%, adjusted EBITDA +5.0% to +7.9%, and adjusted EPS +13.8% to +17.5%, with the low end assuming maintained assisted share and the high end assuming share growth, continued low-single-digit pricing, and stable unemployment supporting filings while slower job growth limits new filings. Roughly 45% of the guided EPS growth is share-count shrink. Guidance excludes an ~$8.3M Q1 FY2027 severance charge from the shift to year-round office leadership, so reported GAAP EBITDA will run below the adjusted range.

Estimate revisions are mixed and partly stale: FY2026 consensus rose 3.2% over 90 days to $5.16 against a $5.31 actual, while FY2027 consensus slipped 0.5% to $5.79 — well below the $6.14 guided midpoint. That gap should close upward over the next several weeks. This is a modest positive revision setup rather than a large inflection.

The peer set is heterogeneous but instructive: Frontdoor 24.1x, Bright Horizons 23.2x, Mister Car Wash 21.5x, Service Corp 22.2x, ADT 10.6x, median 22.2x. HRB at 7.6x forward trades at roughly a 65% discount to the median and a 28% discount to ADT, the structurally closest comparable (leveraged, low-growth, recurring). The discount is defensible — HRB's unit volumes are flat to declining and its largest channel faces a credible substitution threat — but a 65% discount prices terminal decline rather than the mid-single-digit revenue growth and 19.2% FCF margin actually delivered.

The specific case that the market is mispriced: at 7.6x forward with 94% of free cash flow returned, an investor does not need unit growth. The dividend (3.94%) plus guided repurchases (~$400M against a ~$5.8B cap, ~6-7% of shares) produce a ~10% cash return, and low-single-digit pricing on flat units plus franchise internalization sustains mid-single-digit revenue growth. Guidance above consensus with four consecutive beats is the near-term catalyst for consensus to move toward $6.14.

The constraints on that case are equally specific. Roughly $600M remains of the $1.5B authorization, funding about 18 months at the guided pace before the Board must renew (it consistently has, retiring more than 48% of shares since 2016). The buyback also becomes progressively less accretive as the multiple re-rates — repurchasing at $52 creates materially less value than the ~$38 executed in Q4 FY2026.

Fair value framework:
- **Base $55** — 9.0x FY2027 guided adjusted EPS of $6.14, a discount to HRB's own 10–12x pre-derating range that reflects confirmed DIY erosion. Cross-checks: 6.5x EV/FY2027 adjusted EBITDA and an 11.1% FCF yield at that price, both still undemanding.
- **Bull $66** — ~10.7x $6.14, requiring DIY unit stabilization, assisted share gain at the high end of guidance, and multiple normalization toward the ADT-like end of the peer range; 22% short interest at 9.2 days to cover is mechanical fuel.
- **Bear $33** — ~6x FY2027 EPS revised toward $5.50 on accelerating DIY decline and assisted unit erosion; matches the low analyst target of $33 and sits above the February 2026 low of $28.16.

From $46.67 that is +17.8% to base, +41.4% to bull, −29.3% to bear. Probability-weighting 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives roughly +10.7% expected return. Analyst targets span $33–$50 with a $47 median, and the $50 high was set before this guide; on a stock at 7.6x forward with guidance above consensus, those targets are stale and should be treated as a floor rather than a ceiling. At the ~$52.85 extended-hours indication the base-case upside compresses to +4.1% and the expected return turns roughly flat; the entry price, not the franchise, is the binding constraint.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is a barbell: a fragmented, labor-intensive assisted market (CPAs, independent preparers, accounting firms) and a concentrated, software-driven DIY market, with free offerings from governments, volunteer organizations and competitors pressing from below in both. The filings name governmental organizations, including the IRS and state and foreign tax authorities, as direct competitors.

In assisted, the evidence supports stability. Flat system volume with conversion up 200bp and retention up 190bp in a season management characterized as moderating industry growth is share maintenance, and the franchise internalization program converts royalty economics into full revenue at a cost of assuming operating expense. Trademark assets are described in the filings as of material importance, with perpetually renewable registrations, and the ~8,600-office network is genuinely hard to replicate.

In DIY, the position is deteriorating. Paid units fell 4.2% with pricing up 3.9%, and Q4 DIY revenue declined. DIY is only 9.7% of revenue so the near-term P&L impact is contained, but it is the channel with the lowest marginal cost, greatest scalability, and highest exposure to AI-native substitution. Losing units in the lowest-cost channel while defending revenue with price is the pattern that precedes margin pressure if it migrates upmarket.

The AI question cannot be resolved from one season. Management's position — that AI automates collection and entry so professionals focus on advice, and that complex filings require accountability a model cannot provide — is coherent, and 87.6% of revenue is human-assisted where switching to a model is a higher-trust decision. The bear position is that general-purpose assistants absorb the simple-to-moderate tier and the 4.2% paid-unit decline is the leading edge. Both are consistent with the FY2026 data. Competitive trajectory scores 4.5: stable in the majority channel, eroding in the strategically important one.

## Risk Assessment

**Pathward bank-partner expiry (most specific and most datable).** Pathward, N.A. is the sole originator/issuer for Refund Transfers, Emerald Advance, Refund Advances, Emerald Card and Spruce accounts. The Program Management Agreement was extended effective October 18, 2024 for two years to **June 30, 2027** — roughly eleven months after the FY2026 balance sheet date. Associated revenue is $244.6M, or 6.2% of FY2026 revenue, and Refund Transfers are structurally embedded in how many assisted clients pay their preparation fee out of the refund. The filings state plainly that a replacement vendor may not be available on acceptable terms, and note heightened federal and state scrutiny of bank partnership arrangements. Neither the FY2027 guidance discussion nor any coverage I reviewed addresses this expiry. Mitigating: the relationship has been renewed repeatedly, bank partner fees were only $32.8M in FY2026, and the PMA may already have been extended in a filing not reviewed.

**Credit risk retained by H&R Block, not Pathward.** Pathward originates Emerald Advance loans; H&R Block purchases participation interests at par — $283.7M in the nine months, 87% of origination volume — and therefore bears the credit loss, with a $17.6M allowance against $61.9M of balances and $19.7M of gross charge-offs on FY2025 vintages. The company separately guarantees up to $18.0M of pre-acceptance Refund Advance loans against a $2.2M accrual. FY2026 bad debt expense was $75.9M. The exposure is declining (allowance $33.5M → $19.7M → $17.6M) as the company shrinks the product.

**Season concentration.** Substantially all annual profit is earned in a ten-week window. A disrupted season — severe weather, IRS service degradation amid federal staffing reductions, or a filing-deadline change — is unhedgeable and compresses an entire year's earnings.

**Litigation.** Ongoing governmental inquiries, class actions and mass arbitrations relating to the IRS Free File Program and DIY practices including pixel use, with subpoenas from multiple state attorneys general. Total litigation accrual was only $13.3M at March 31, 2026 (up from $6.2M), and reasonably possible losses in excess of accrual are described as not material where estimable; small accruals suggest management does not expect a large settlement, but the company also states this is not maximum loss exposure.

**Key-person and execution.** CEO (Campbell, effective January 1, 2026), CFO (Mason, September 2024) and Chief Accounting Officer (Wasleski, October 2025) all changed within roughly fifteen months, alongside three new directors in January 2026, while the company executes a new growth strategy whose failure the filings flag as a named risk. Disclosure controls and ICFR were reported effective with no material changes, so I read this as continuity risk rather than a control concern. Campbell's background (TaxAct CEO 2018–2023, Intuit product leadership 2014–2017) is unusually well matched to the DIY problem, and is also his first public-company CEO role.

**Insider and alignment.** No open-market purchases by any executive or director in the reviewed Form 4 window. Departing CEO Jones monetized roughly $13.9M of options in November 2025 while retaining 861,313 shares, a pattern most consistent with a compressed post-termination exercise window rather than a directional signal. Campbell holds roughly 43,000 shares — normal early in a vesting cycle, but small dollar alignment against a ~$5.8B capitalization. Incentive plan measures are EBITDA, revenue, pre-tax earnings and relative TSR; the buyback, which drives roughly 45% of guided EPS growth, is not directly incentivized.

**Macro.** Fixed-rate debt with no maturity before July 2028 insulates HRB from the higher-for-longer rate path better than most levered consumer names. Consumer stress cuts both ways: filing is mandatory, which limits volume cyclicality, but trade-down toward free and low-price DIY alternatives and higher Emerald Advance credit losses are the transmission channels if household finances deteriorate.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 61.2% against 42.2% one-year realized — rich, at the 87th percentile within the chain — with a pronounced backwardation to 46.9% at three months and 39.3% at six months and an ±8.6% implied move on the eight-day tenor. This is event pricing around the FY2026 release rather than a directional statement; ATM IV rose 10.1 points since July 23 while the price rose 16.8%. Put/call open interest of 0.52, down from 0.57, is call-tilted, which is unusual against 22.0% short interest at 9.2 days to cover and is consistent with positioning for upside continuation or short-side hedging. Skew at the near tenor is described as balanced. The useful takeaway for a fundamental investor is the six-month tenor: 39.3% implied against 42.2% realized means longer-dated optionality into the FY2027 filing season is not expensive, offering a defined-risk way to hold the thesis through the January–April window that determines the year. Near-term premium should be sold or avoided rather than bought.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($66, +41% from $46.67).** HRB is a 19% FCF-margin, 0.5x-levered cash generator being shrunk toward higher per-share value. FY2027 guidance of $6.04–6.24 is above the $5.79 consensus and the company has beaten four consecutive quarters. Assisted conversion (+200bp) and retention (+190bp) improvements, the AGI mix shift toward $50K–$200K households, and the consultative pilot expanding to a full designated market area for the 2027 season indicate the assisted franchise is being upgraded rather than harvested. Franchise internalization adds 100–125 offices of revenue transfer in FY2027. At 7.6x forward with ~$600M of remaining authorization and $400M of guided repurchases, a shareholder earns roughly 10% in cash annually before multiple change; a move to 9–11x forward, in line with a discounted ADT rather than the 22x peer median, produces the balance. Twenty-two percent short interest at 9.2 days to cover means any consensus upgrade cycle is amplified mechanically.

**Bear case ($33, −29% from $46.67).** System-wide assisted units were flat and DIY paid units fell 4.2% in the season the free government competitor was withdrawn. Revenue growth is price (+3%), mix (+1%), and moving returns from the franchise ledger to the company ledger — none of which is client acquisition, and the internalization lever is finite and already slowing. Roughly 10% of FY2026 EBITDA growth came from lower marketing spend in the same year DIY units fell. Roughly 45% of guided EPS growth is share shrink, funded by a buyback that becomes less accretive as the price rises and that the seasonal cash cycle prevents from being opportunistic. The Pathward agreement covering 6.2% of revenue expires June 30, 2027 with no disclosed renewal. Book equity is negative for eight months of the year and the credit rating sits one notch above high yield. If AI substitution migrates from DIY into the simple end of assisted, the terminal-value question dominates and the multiple compresses toward the ~6x forward the market paid in February 2026.

**Staged-entry plan.** The case above rests on the authoritative $46.67 close. Accordingly: add on the official-close basis or into any pullback to **$44–47**; do not initiate above **$50** on this thesis. **Upgrade to BUY if:** (1) FY2027 first-half disclosures show DIY paid volume flat or better with NAC still rising, breaking the erosion pattern; (2) the Pathward PMA is extended on disclosed terms with no material economic concession; and (3) the shares are available below $45 while FY2027 consensus has converged to $6.10 or higher.

**Downgrade to HOLD if:** consensus FY2027 EPS fails to move above $6.00 within two months of the guide; or FY2027 repurchases run materially below the guided $400M; or the shares hold above $55 without a corresponding upward revision in the guidance range.

**Downgrade to REDUCE/AVOID if:** (1) the 2027 season shows total assisted volume declining more than 1% with NAC unable to offset; (2) the Pathward agreement lapses or is renewed on terms that materially reduce Refund Transfer or financial services economics; (3) adjusted EBITDA margin compresses year over year, indicating price-led revenue growth has exhausted itself; or (4) the Board declines to renew the repurchase authorization when the remaining ~$600M is exhausted.

**Next reassessment points:** the FY2026 Form 10-K (expected mid-August 2026, providing audited figures, FY2026 volume statistics and any risk-factor changes); Q1 FY2027 results in November 2026 (a seasonal loss quarter, but the first read on early-season DIY positioning and the $8.3M severance charge); and the January–April 2027 season update, which is the only data that meaningfully tests the AI-substitution thesis.

## Conclusion

The FY2026 print resolved the near-term earnings question favorably and left the structural question open. Cash generation is high-quality and the balance sheet is genuinely conservative once the seasonal book-equity distortion is set aside: $756.1M of free cash flow, 0.50x net leverage, 13.1x interest coverage, no maturity before July 2028, and a recomputed Altman Z of 3.48. Guidance is internally coherent and above consensus, and the company is retiring 6–8% of its shares annually while raising the dividend. At 7.6x guided FY2027 EPS and a 13.1% FCF yield, that combination is mispriced relative to a 22.2x peer median even after discounting heavily for the growth profile.

The discount exists for a defensible reason. System-wide assisted units were flat, DIY paid units fell 4.2% in the season the free government alternative was withdrawn, and essentially all revenue growth traces to price, mix and moving returns between company and franchise ledgers. That is the profile of a well-run cash annuity with a live substitution threat, not a compounder. The rating is ACCUMULATE rather than BUY because the expected return is positive but the bear case is 29% below the authoritative price and rests on a thesis this print did not disprove; and because the actionable price following the release is roughly 13% above the basis on which the arithmetic is built. Buy it on the close basis or on weakness into the mid-$40s; do not chase it above $50.
