## Executive Summary

Karman Space & Defense is a Tier-2/Tier-3 supplier of mission-critical subsystems (fairings and payload protection, interstages, propulsion components and solid rocket motor hardware) sold to more than 80 prime contractors across 130-plus programs. The operating trajectory is strong and improving. Q2 FY2026 revenue rose 58.2% to $182.1M with company-stated organic growth of 24.4%; gross margin reached 43.0% versus 37.6% in FY2023; backlog grew roughly 65% from year-end to ~$1.3B on approximately $500M of quarterly bookings, implying a book-to-bill near 2.7x. FY2026 guidance was raised to $730–745M of revenue and $215.0–222.5M of adjusted EBITDA. The end markets (hypersonics, tactical munitions, submarine composites, space launch) are the fastest-growing lines in the U.S. defense budget, and the pending ~$94M Walker Precision acquisition extends the platform into European missile programs.

The problem is what investors are paying for that trajectory and what the trajectory has cost. At $61.47 the enterprise value is approximately $8.95B ($8.15B equity plus $807M net debt), or 40.9x the guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA midpoint, 50.0x LTM adjusted EBITDA and 12.1x guided revenue. That multiple is being applied to an EBITDA figure that has not converted to cash: FY2025 produced $145.3M of adjusted EBITDA against **negative $22.1M of operating cash flow and negative $42.5M of free cash flow**, Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow was $0.2M against $44.8M of adjusted EBITDA, and reconstructed H1 FY2026 operating cash flow sits near zero against $99.4M of adjusted EBITDA. Receivables plus contract assets equal 187 days of trailing revenue, marginally worse than the 182 days at year-end. Growth has been funded by lenders: total debt rose from $441.0M (12/31/24) to $859.1M (6/30/26) across four credit agreement amendments, with tangible book equity around negative $404M.

The governance and assurance profile raises the required margin of safety rather than lowering it. Material weaknesses in internal control were disclosed as of December 31, 2025 and the Q1 10-Q reported **no ICFR changes during the quarter**, with disclosure controls "not effective." As an emerging growth company there is no auditor attestation. EDGAR shows Item 4.01 auditor-change filings in June 2025 and June 2026, and the reason for the second could not be verified. Insider activity is one-directional: roughly $70M of 2025 executive and director sales, 2,025,000 shares under Rule 10b5-1 plans including 1,000,000 for the chairman, two secondary offerings from which the company received no proceeds, and no open-market purchases by any officer or director. We rate KRMN **REDUCE**: base case ~$60, bull ~$92, bear ~$38, a roughly flat probability-weighted return against 38% downside in a security with 72% realized volatility.

## Company Overview

Karman was assembled by Trive Capital as a roll-up of nine acquired businesses, converted to a Delaware corporation and listed on February 14, 2025 at $22.00 per share (26.5M shares, of which only 8.4M were primary; net company proceeds ~$147.3M). It operates from 17 locations across eight U.S. states with roughly 1,400 employees including ~300 engineers, holds 23 issued patents, and is non-unionized.

FY2025 revenue of $471.5M split across three end markets: Tactical Missiles & Integrated Defense $171.7M (36.4%), Hypersonics & Strategic Missile Defense $150.0M (31.8%), Space & Launch $149.8M (31.8%). The February 2026 Seemann acquisition added Maritime Defense Systems, which contributed $33.6M (18.4%) of Q2 FY2026 revenue.

The business model matters for how to read management's demand commentary. Karman sells components and subsystems to primes; the primes hold the government contracts. Management cited ">$90 billion in recent prime contractor awards for THAAD and PAC-3" and ">$76 billion for new Columbia and Virginia class submarines." Those are awards to the primes. Karman's participation is as a content supplier, and no filing quantifies its dollar share. The strategy is explicitly a second-source play ("winning alternative supplier positions on new programs and in new content areas"), which is what primes want when scaling interceptor and munitions output against capacity constraints, and which the 10-K itself notes is exposed to the primes' own make-versus-buy decisions once volumes justify insourcing.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin structure.** Revenue compounded from $280.7M (FY2023) to $345.3M (FY2024) to $471.5M (FY2025), with H1 FY2026 at $333.3M, +54.9%. Karman quantified organic growth for the first time in Q2 FY2026 at +24.4% against +58.2% total, implying roughly 58% of growth came from acquisitions. No organic figure has been published for FY2025 or Q1 FY2026, and the definition is undisclosed. Within Q2, Tactical Missiles grew 55.4% and Hypersonics 24.2%, but **Space & Launch grew only 6.3%**, attributed to shifting launch schedules — the visible soft spot in roughly a quarter of the business.

Gross margin expanded 540bp since FY2023 to 43.0% in Q2 FY2026. Yet adjusted EBITDA margin has gone nowhere and is now declining: 30.0% in Q2 FY2026 versus 30.7% a year earlier, 29.8% in H1 versus 30.5%, and the FY2026 guidance midpoint ($218.75M on $737.5M) implies 29.7% versus 30.8% in FY2025 — about 110bp of erosion.

The arithmetic sits below the gross line. FY2025 G&A rose 92.8% ($44.4M → $85.7M) against 36.6% revenue growth. Stripping the items management adds back, residual G&A was approximately $60.2M or 12.8% of revenue versus ~10.5% in FY2024, roughly 230bp of underlying overhead build, with professional fees rising from $8.2M to $21.8M and doubling again year over year in Q1 FY2026. Two readings are available: the acquired businesses carry lower EBITDA margins than the legacy platform and/or public-company and integration overhead is structural; or the Maritime mix shift is deliberately lower-margin and higher-volume. Neither can be resolved because Karman declined to publish pro forma results for MTI, ISP, Five Axis or Seemann, and there is a single reportable segment.

**Cash conversion is the decisive fact.** The FY2025 bridge is reproducible from the filed cash flow statement: net income $17.4M plus D&A $42.7M plus deferred taxes $10.5M plus smaller items gives $71.2M before working capital; working capital and acquisition accruals consumed $93.3M, driven by contract assets −$49.1M, receivables −$18.4M and payables/accruals −$16.9M. Result: operating cash flow −$22.1M, free cash flow −$42.5M. A cleaner way to see the gap: $145.3M adjusted EBITDA less $44.8M cash interest, $16.9M cash taxes and $20.3M capex would leave ~$63.2M of theoretical free cash flow; actual was −$42.5M, a ~$106M shortfall attributable to working capital and cash transaction costs excluded from adjusted EBITDA.

Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow was $0.2M against $44.8M of adjusted EBITDA. The Q2 press release contained no cash flow statement; reconstruction from the $17.8M cash increase, reported Q1 flows and an assumed $15–20M of Q2 cash capex implies H1 operating cash flow of roughly −$1M to +$3M against $99.4M of adjusted EBITDA. That reconstruction is an estimate, not a filing fact, and should be replaced when the Q2 10-Q is available. Working capital intensity has not improved: receivables plus contract assets were $302.1M at 6/30/26 against $589.6M of trailing revenue, or 187 days, versus 182 days at 12/31/25.

The benign explanation is real and should not be dismissed: this is the working-capital signature of milestone-billed, over-time revenue recognition on programs ramping hard, compounded by funding acquired companies' receivables post-close. If growth decelerates toward the guided H2 rate, the absorption should stop and free cash flow could inflect sharply. But six consecutive quarters without cash generation is a pattern, and it is being valued at 41x EBITDA.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt at 6/30/26 was $807.3M ($859.1M total debt including $102.1M of finance leases, less $51.7M cash). That is 3.69x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA and 4.51x LTM adjusted EBITDA of $179.1M. Pro forma for Walker at ~$94M, if debt-funded, net debt approaches $901M or ~4.1x guided EBITDA before any Walker contribution (Walker's EBITDA is undisclosed).

The refinancing work has been genuinely good. The all-in rate fell from 11.01% (12/31/24) to 7.50% (12/31/25) to 6.42% (3/31/26); the February 2026 repricing cut 75bp and management guided ~$4M of annual interest savings. Term amortization is ~$5.05M/year with a bullet in 2032, so there is no near-term maturity wall. Interest coverage improved from 1.64x (FY2025) to 2.28x (Q2 FY2026).

Two structural points cut the other way. The sole financial covenant is a **springing** 6.50x first-lien net leverage test applied only if revolver borrowings exceed a threshold; with zero drawn it was untested at both 12/31/25 and 3/31/26, and the Fourth Amendment raised revolver commitments to $150M while removing the cap on further incremental revolving commitments. Near-term default risk is remote, as is any lender-imposed brake on further leveraging. And essentially the entire ~$769M term loan floats: a +100bp move adds roughly $7.7M of annual pre-tax interest, meaningful against $56.3M of H1 operating income.

Asset composition is thin. Goodwill plus intangibles of $824.9M equal 57.1% of total assets and 196% of stockholders' equity, leaving tangible book equity near negative $404M. Goodwill has been tested only qualitatively (step 0) against a **single reporting unit**, so any future impairment test is all-or-nothing at the consolidated level and cannot isolate an underperforming acquisition.

**Earnings quality flags.** The Q2 "beat" was $0.14 adjusted versus $0.13 non-GAAP consensus, against GAAP diluted EPS of $0.11; the gap is mostly transaction, integration, lender-fee and share-based-comp add-backs, and transaction and integration costs are cash and recurring for a company doing two to three deals a year. On the GAAP tape, the four-quarter record is one beat and three misses (−12.2%, −4.8%, −10.1%, +5.8%). The ISP earnout — a $5.0M cash contingency tied to a specified adjusted EBITDA threshold for the twelve months ended December 31, 2025 — **was not achieved**, the liability was written to zero, and the reversal was booked to other income, flattering FY2025 pre-tax results. It is a small number, but it is the only public, objective test of a recent acquisition's performance, and the acquisition failed it within roughly seven months of closing.

A further item requires verification: goodwill rose $58.9M and net intangibles fell $73.7M between 3/31/26 and 6/30/26 with the term note essentially flat and no evident acquisition cash outflow, consistent with a large measurement-period reallocation of the preliminary Seemann purchase price from amortizable intangibles into non-amortizable goodwill. Measurement-period adjustments within twelve months are standard under ASC 805 and no motive should be attributed, but the economic effect would be to reduce future amortization (raising GAAP earnings) while moving value into the asset subject only to a qualitative, single-reporting-unit test.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is the strongest part of the case. Karman's four end markets map to priority defense lines: hypersonics and strategic missile defense (NGI qualification phases named in the 10-K), tactical munitions (GMLRS rate increases, loitering munitions, counter-UAS), naval and submarine composites, and space launch. The FY2025 10-K cites approximately $150B of incremental OBBBA defense funding through FY2029, weighted to hypersonics, missiles and munitions. Walker adds content on more than 25 EU tactical missile and air-defense programs at a moment of elevated European defense spending.

Order intake corroborates it. Backlog progressed $428.7M (FY2023) → $579.8M (FY2024) → $801.1M (FY2025) → $1,026.9M (Q1 FY2026) → ~$1.3B (Q2 FY2026), with the Q2 step supported by disclosed bookings of nearly $500M including a large multi-year space and launch agreement. Two definitional cautions apply. Karman renamed "funded backlog" to "backlog" in FY2025 with no change to historical amounts, and the definition is broad, including purchase orders, long-term agreements and "contractual authorization to proceed." Backlog is materially larger than ASC 606 remaining performance obligations: RPO was $550.6M against $801.1M backlog at 12/31/25 and $723.3M against $1,026.9M at 3/31/26, with 33.9% of RPO at 3/31/26 extending beyond 2027 — a longer conversion tail than the FY2025 profile. Much of the Q1 backlog increase arrived with Seemann rather than from organic orders.

The pipeline disclosure requires more discipline still. The "~$3 billion active pipeline" (as of May 25, 2026) is issuer-provided, caveated by the company as estimates "developed by individual team members" with "inherent variability," and the accompanying end-market breakdown sums to roughly $1.55B — about half the headline — with no reconciliation. Named pursuits are described as "secured **or** currently negotiating." The "more than $1 billion" of three additional long-term agreements cited on the Q2 call refers to unsigned agreements. None of this is backlog.

Consensus estimates tell a more sober story than the bookings. Current-year EPS consensus is $0.58 versus $0.66 ninety days ago (−11.2%) and current-quarter $0.15 versus $0.18 (−14.9%); next year is $0.93 versus $0.98 (−4.7%). The 30-day trend has stabilized and turned marginally positive (+1.2% current year, +1.2% next year) following the Q2 print. The accurate read is a material downgrade cycle over the last quarter that has just begun to stabilize, not the accelerating upward revision pattern that would support paying a premium multiple. Guidance itself beat consensus by a slim margin: the raised $737.5M revenue midpoint against roughly $732M of analyst consensus is well under 1%.

H2 guidance implies $396.7–411.7M of revenue (+19.0% to +23.5% sequentially over H1) at a 29.2–29.9% adjusted EBITDA margin — continued strong top-line delivery with further margin erosion embedded.

The analyst mean target of $90.20 sits 46.7% above spot with 10 of 11 ratings positive. On a stock at 41x EBITDA, those targets are not independent confirmation; they embed the same growth extrapolation as the price. The single sell rating and the $37 low target define the range of disagreement.

## Competitive Landscape

Karman's structural argument is credible. The supply base for qualified, integrated subsystems is fragmented among piece-part machine shops, while few suppliers can deliver a qualified system; gross margins of 40–43% for a build-to-print aerospace supplier support the claim, as do 10–24 year customer-relationship intangible lives assigned by third-party valuation and program lifecycles measured in decades. More than 130 programs with no single program above 12% of FY2025 revenue is real program-level diversification. Second-source qualification is precisely what primes are buying today as they scale interceptor and munitions output.

Two ceilings constrain the position. First, the 10-K names the primes' make-versus-buy decisions as a competitor: content won as a second source can be insourced once volumes justify it, and Karman does not qualify for SBA small-business set-asides that can steer awards to smaller rivals. Second, customer concentration is worsening at the top even as end-market mix broadens. The largest customer's revenue share rose from 23.5% (FY2023) to 27.8% (FY2024) to 28.5% (FY2025); the top three were 51.5% in FY2025 versus 50.8% in FY2024. Two customers were 40.7% of receivables at 12/31/25, and one supplier was 23.8% of accounts payable. Adding a fourth end market broadens program exposure; it does not establish customer diversification, and the direct concentration data points the other way. Q1 FY2026 showed only two customers above 10%, which may indicate a turn, but one quarter is not a trend.

Gross margin durability rests on holding qualified positions rather than on pricing power over customers who are large, few and — on cost-plus and time-and-materials work — have visibility into Karman's costs.

## Risk Assessment

**Internal control and assurance.** Material weaknesses were disclosed as of December 31, 2025. The Q1 FY2026 10-Q concluded disclosure controls "were not effective at a reasonable assurance level" and stated there were **no changes in ICFR during the quarter** — no reported remediation progress. As an EGC, Karman has no auditor attestation under SOX 404(b). Revenue is recognized over time using cost-based estimates-at-completion, and contract assets are roughly a third of revenue, so the control weakness sits on the most judgment-intensive figures in the accounts. Observable data-quality slips reinforce the concern: a mislabeled leverage ratio in the FY2025 capitalization table; Seemann consideration described as $210.0M cash plus $10.0M stock in the 10-K versus $215.9M cash plus $17.0M stock ($232.9M total) in the Q1 10-Q for a deal that closed before the 10-K was filed; an irreconcilable opening share count in the Q1 equity statement; and a formally "CORRECTED and REPLACED" May 2026 operational-data release. None changes a material subtotal, and serial acquirers routinely produce presentation errors while scaling, but collectively they are consistent with the disclosed weaknesses manifesting in published figures.

**Auditor changes.** EDGAR shows Item 4.01 filings on June 6, 2025 and June 11, 2026. The successor firm, whether the second change was a dismissal or resignation, and whether any Item 304(a) disagreements were disclosed could not be verified. A benign reading — upgrading firms ahead of losing EGC status and filing a first SOX 404 management report at ~$750M of revenue — is entirely plausible, as is a reading connected to the unremediated weaknesses. This should be resolved before committing capital.

**Leverage and financing dependence.** Every acquisition since the IPO has been funded with incremental term loans, not internally generated cash, against approximately zero cumulative operating cash flow over FY2025–H1 FY2026. Net leverage is ~3.7x guided and ~4.5x LTM, rising toward ~4.1x guided pro forma for Walker. The covenant structure imposes almost no discipline. An unretrieved August 6, 2026 8-K carrying Items 1.01 and 2.03, filed the same day as earnings, is plausibly Walker financing given the pattern, but the linkage is unconfirmed.

**Program and policy risk.** Contracts are terminable for convenience and subject to competitive re-bid; fixed-price development work and undefinitized contract actions create margin risk; classified programs limit visibility. CMMC cybersecurity certification is expected on nearly all new contracts by 2026, and non-compliance would bar bidding. The FY2025 10-K notes the February 2026 Supreme Court invalidation of a significant portion of IEEPA tariffs, creating refund and re-imposition uncertainty, with Walker adding UK and Poland cross-border and export-control exposure. Two institutional holders (Carillon, Alger) flagged defense budget "noise" as a driver of sector multiple compression in Q2.

**Governance and alignment.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director appear in the Form 4 record. Realized 2025 sales approximate $70M across the then-CEO, CFO, COO, CGO and a director; six insiders adopted 10b5-1 plans in Q1 FY2026 covering 2,025,000 shares including 1,000,000 for Chairman Stinnett. Trive sold 23.6M shares at $49.00 and distributed 50.45M in kind to zero direct holding. A second secondary of up to 15.525M shares (~11.7% of shares outstanding) was launched in May 2026 with no company proceeds. Post-IPO diversification by holders of low-basis stock is normal and 10b5-1 plans are the compliant mechanism, so no negative view of the business should be inferred; the informational content is the absence of any buying signal at these prices. Executive compensation alignment cannot be assessed — DEF 14A pay-versus-performance fields returned null, most likely due to EGC exemptions — which is a real gap given a fresh 11.5M-share plan (~8.0% ownership dilution if fully issued over time, from a currently trivial base) and a new CEO whose incentive metrics are unknown. Two of the top five executive roles turned over within roughly two months; Jon Rambeau's arrival from L3Harris reads as a capability upgrade for a company scaling toward $1B of revenue.

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 75.9% against 72.2% one-year realized is modestly rich (3.7-point spread) at the 65th percentile of the current chain, with the nearest expiry pricing a ±10.7% move and a flat term structure (75.9% near, 74.5% far). Near-term skew is balanced at −0.2%, but skew steepens with tenor to −1.8% at three months and −3.4% at six months, and the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.61 against a put/call volume ratio of 0.10 indicates recent flow has been call-dominated while the standing book carries more downside protection at longer dates. The longer-dated skew is consistent with the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($92, +49.7%)

Defense demand in Karman's four end markets is the most durable spending in the federal budget, with roughly $150B of incremental OBBBA funding weighted to hypersonics, missiles and munitions through FY2029. Karman is the qualified second source primes need to scale interceptor and munitions output, evidenced by 43% gross margins, 130-plus programs, no program above 12% of revenue, and roughly $500M of Q2 bookings against $182M of revenue. Backlog at ~$1.3B is 1.8x FY2026 guided revenue and growing 65% year-to-date. Organic growth of 24.4% is genuine, not acquisitive. Walker is management-described as immediately accretive to revenue, backlog, EBITDA, EPS and cash flow, and extends the platform into European missile programs where spending is rising. The working-capital drain is the arithmetic signature of a business ramping production and funding acquired receivables; as growth decelerates toward the guided H2 rate, contract assets stop absorbing cash and free cash flow inflects sharply, at which point 12x revenue looks defensible on a business compounding EBITDA above 25%. The refinancing sequence cut the all-in rate from 11.01% to 6.42% and saves ~$4M annually, the maturity wall is 2032, and coverage improved to 2.28x. A new CEO with large-cap operating experience, plus potential segment disaggregation, improves both execution and transparency.

### Bear Case ($38, −38.2%)

The market is capitalizing an adjusted EBITDA number that has not become cash for six consecutive quarters, and the working-capital intensity has not improved (187 days versus 182). Adjusted EBITDA margin is declining year over year and guidance embeds further erosion, because underlying overhead has built roughly 230bp and the acquired mix appears less profitable — a hypothesis that cannot be tested because acquisition-level results are not disclosed and there is one reportable segment. The one acquisition with an objective public performance test, ISP, missed its earnout, and the reversal flattered FY2025 earnings. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 11.2% in ninety days and three of the last four quarters missed on GAAP. Leverage doubled in eighteen months under a covenant structure with no practical brake; tangible book equity is negative $404M; goodwill plus intangibles equal 196% of equity and are tested only qualitatively against a single reporting unit, so an impairment signal would arrive late and all at once. Material weaknesses remain unremediated with no 404(b) attestation and two auditor changes in twelve months whose cause is unverified, directly over the estimates-at-completion that drive both revenue and a $187M contract-asset balance. If defense budget timing slips, Space & Launch stays soft, or growth decelerates, the multiple normalizes toward the 25x EV/EBITDA at which cash-generative aerospace peers trade, which alone produces a high-30s share price. The 63% peak-to-trough drawdown already recorded in eighteen months demonstrates how fast this multiple compresses.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Trim exposure into the current strength rather than waiting for a catalyst.

- **Base case: $60** (32x FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of ~$275M less ~$900M net debt), −2.4% from $61.47.
- **Bull case: $92** (44x on $300M FY2027E EBITDA), +49.7%.
- **Bear case: $38** (25x on $230M FY2027E EBITDA), −38.2%.
- **Probability-weighted return**: approximately 0% at 25/45/30 weights.
- **Timeframe**: 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q2 FY2026 10-Q (cash flow statement, Seemann purchase-price footnote, ICFR status), the Walker close and its financing disclosure in Q3, and the FY2026 10-K with the first SOX 404 management report.

**De-risking plan.** Reduce position size now, in the $58–65 range, retaining at most a residual holding for investors who want optionality on the defense demand cycle. The stock has recovered roughly 37% from its July low, which is a favorable exit window relative to the fundamental picture; do not wait for the analyst mean target of $90.20, which requires the current 41x multiple to expand rather than normalize.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE** — any two of the following:
1. The Q2 10-Q shows H1 operating cash flow positive and receivables plus contract assets falling below 170 days of trailing revenue for two consecutive quarters.
2. Documented ICFR remediation progress, specifically a disclosure that controls were effective or that remediation steps were implemented during the period, plus clarification of the June 11, 2026 auditor change confirming no Item 304(a) disagreements.
3. Adjusted EBITDA margin stabilizing at or above 30.0% for two consecutive quarters, reversing the 110bp of guided erosion.
4. EV/forward adjusted EBITDA compressing below approximately 30x — roughly $45–48 per share on FY2027 estimates — with the operating case intact.

## Conclusion

Karman is a well-positioned supplier into the fastest-growing lines of defense procurement, and the Q2 order book is among the strongest operating data points in the mid-cap aerospace group. The issue is price relative to the quality of the earnings behind it. At $61.47 the market pays 40.9x guided adjusted EBITDA and 12.1x revenue for a company that has generated approximately zero operating cash flow across six quarters, has levered from $441M to $859M of debt to fund acquisitions, carries negative $404M of tangible book equity, has unremediated internal-control material weaknesses with no auditor attestation and two auditor changes in twelve months, and whose current-year consensus EPS has been cut 11% in ninety days. Insiders and the former sponsor have been consistent net sellers with no offsetting purchases, and the ISP earnout failed.

The base case sits essentially at the current price; the bear case, requiring only multiple normalization toward cash-generative aerospace peer levels, is 38% lower and coincides with both the low analyst target and a level below the 52-week low. That asymmetry does not justify holding a full position. **REDUCE.** Revisit if free cash flow inflects positive with working-capital days improving, if the control environment and auditor question resolve cleanly, or if the forward EBITDA multiple compresses below roughly 30x.
