**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Jabil is one of the world's largest contract electronics manufacturers, and it is in the middle of a business-mix transformation. Its Intelligent Infrastructure segment — server and rack integration, high-speed networking, liquid cooling, and data-center power — has grown from 41% of FY2025 revenue to 48% in the third quarter of FY2026, expanding ~41% over nine months while the segment's operating margin (6.1%) now runs above the corporate average. Management responded to the June 17, 2026 quarter by raising FY2026 revenue guidance to ~$35B and its AI-related revenue guide to $13.6B, and guiding FY2027 core operating margin above 6%. Next-year consensus core EPS has been revised up ~15% in 90 days (from $14.59 to $16.75). Jabil is a legitimate AI-infrastructure beneficiary trading, on forward core earnings, at a discount to both its own history and its EMS peers.

The reasons to stop short of a full endorsement are structural, not cyclical. Jabil earns a sub-10% gross margin in a business the company's own 10-K describes as having no meaningful proprietary protection, competing against Foxconn, Flex, Celestica and customers' in-house lines. The growth engine is concentrated in a single ~16% customer (almost certainly Amazon/AWS, tied to the December 2024 warrant), and the AI ramp is being partly financed by $2.8B of customer-controlled consignment components, payables stretched to 135 days, and $12.7B of receivables sold in nine months — an efficient structure that would reverse sharply if hyperscaler demand cooled. Jabil is a leveraged, low-margin expression of the durability of hyperscaler AI capex, the most-debated open question in the current market.

The stock has fallen roughly 20% from its late-June closing highs near $385 to ~$303 on no fundamental deterioration; the June quarter was a clean beat-and-raise. On the "price decline without thesis deterioration" logic, that improves the setup. But with a 4.5/10 risk profile driven by concentration and working-capital fragility, a commodity business model, and an options market pricing near-term stress (front-month IV of 80% in backwardation, elevated put skew), the call is a constructive HOLD with a clear path to an upgrade, rather than a clean BUY at current levels.

## Company Overview

Jabil provides contract engineering, manufacturing, and supply-chain services across ~100 sites in 30 countries with ~135,000 employees and ~$29.8B in FY2025 revenue. It operates a decentralized, single-customer "business unit" model with highly automated continuous-flow manufacturing, and has been pushing up the value chain into design, thermal/liquid cooling, power management, and procurement analytics.

Effective September 1, 2024, the company reorganized into three segments. FY2025 mix → Q3 FY2026 mix:
- **Intelligent Infrastructure** (AI/cloud/data center, capital equipment, networking): 41% → 48%. The growth engine.
- **Regulated Industries** (auto/transport, healthcare & packaging, renewables): 40% → 36%.
- **Connected Living & Digital Commerce** (warehouse automation/robotics, digital commerce): 19% → 16%. Shrinking, partly by design.

Structural characteristics that frame everything else: the top five customers are ~36% of revenue, ~78 customers make up 90%, and one customer ("Customer A," reported in Intelligent Infrastructure) rose to 16% of revenue in FY2025 from under 10%. Roughly 74-76% of revenue is foreign-sourced, with significant China manufacturing. R&D is only ~0.1% of revenue because customer-specific engineering runs through cost of goods sold. This is a high-asset-turnover (revenue-to-assets ~1.6x), working-capital-intensive, thin-margin pass-through business.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2022 $33.5B → FY2023 $34.7B → FY2024 $28.9B → FY2025 $29.8B (+3.2%). The FY2023-to-FY2024 "decline" is not organic; it reflects the December 2023 divestiture of the Mobility Business (Chengdu/Wuxi consumer electronics) to BYD Electronic for ~$2.2B, which also produced a one-time $942M pre-tax gain that inflated FY2024 GAAP net income to $1,388M. FY2026 is inflecting upward: nine-month revenue $25.3B (+17.6% YoY), Q3 revenue $8.75B (+11.8%), with Intelligent Infrastructure nine-month revenue up ~41% (cloud/data center +31%).

**Margins and earnings quality.** Gross margin is thin but rising on AI mix: 8.3% (FY23) → 9.3% (FY24) → 8.9% (FY25) → 9.5% in Q3 FY2026. Core (non-GAAP) operating income has been stable — $1,733M (FY23), $1,588M (FY24), $1,620M (FY25) — and is tracking higher in FY2026 (nine-month core operating income $1,394M vs $1,101M prior year). Core operating margin runs ~5.5%, with management guiding FY2027 above 6%. GAAP FY2025 net income fell to $657M mainly on the divestiture-gain comp base, plus $181M restructuring, a $97M Italy divestiture loss, and a $46M preferred-stock impairment. A naive trailing-P/E screen (37.9x on GAAP EPS of $5.92) badly misreads this business; the correct anchor is core EPS: $8.63 (FY23) → $8.49 (FY24) → $9.75 (FY25), with nine-month FY2026 core EPS of $8.70 versus $6.48 a year earlier.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow is steady (~$1.6-1.7B/yr); FY2025 adjusted FCF was $1,318M and nine-month FY2026 OCF was $1,269M, up year over year. Buybacks are the defining story: $6.6B repurchased FY2016-FY2025, including $2.5B in FY2024 and $1.0B in FY2025, cutting diluted share count from ~144M (FY22) to ~107M (Q3 FY2026), a ~26% reduction. The dividend is a token ~$0.08/quarter (~$36M/yr). Capex is guided to just 1.0-1.5% of revenue in FY2026.

**Balance sheet — read past the optics.** Stockholders' equity is only ~$1.3B (May 2026) against ~$23.8B assets, producing a headline D/E near 11x and a grey-zone Altman Z of 1.99. This is a buyback artifact, not distress: treasury stock is ~$8.8B, retained earnings are $7.0B, funded debt is a modest ~$3.4B of senior notes (net debt ~$2.0B) against $1.6B+ core operating income, and the company is in full covenant compliance. On an EBITDA basis, leverage is comfortable. Liquidity is ample: $1.4B cash, $4.4B of unused facilities, a $700M ABS program, and multi-billion receivables-sale capacity. In January 2026 Jabil issued $500M 4.20% notes (2029) and $500M 4.75% notes (2033), retiring the $500M 1.70% 2026 notes.

The one genuine financial-health caution is the working-capital structure funding the AI ramp: inventory rose to $5.9B, customer-controlled consignment components hit $2.8B (from $1.1B), payables stretched to 135 days, and the cash-conversion cycle compressed to five days. That flatters reported OCF and keeps capital light, but it is favorable-and-fragile: a demand reversal or a tightening of receivables-factoring capacity would expose the model to inventory write-downs (a recurring theme in the filings).

**Earnings track record.** Four consecutive beats: +11.5% (Sep-25), +4.5% (Dec-25), +6.2% (Mar-26), +0.9% (Jun-26). The narrowing surprise magnitude in the June quarter suggests consensus is catching up, though estimates continue to rise.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is straightforward and confirming. Intelligent Infrastructure is now the largest and fastest-growing part of the company, and it carries above-corporate margins. The M&A program is deliberately building capability into the highest-demand parts of the data-center stack: Hanley Energy ($752M, closed January 2026) for data-center power and rack-level infrastructure, Mikros for liquid cooling, and Rebound Technologies for supply-chain/shortage management. An announced (but not yet closed) strategic-alliance intent with India's Adani Enterprises to build AI data-center manufacturing capacity adds optionality; treat its scale as unconfirmed.

The estimate-revision picture confirms momentum: current-quarter EPS +9.3% (30d) / +9.5% (90d), next-quarter +6.4% / +6.6%, current-year +3.3%, and next-year +14.8% / +15.0%. This falls short of the extreme +30% inflection threshold, and revenue growth of ~12-18% is not the >40% acceleration seen at cyclical troughs, so I treat this as durable positive momentum rather than a maximum-conviction inflection signal. The AI-infrastructure demand underpinning it is broad-based: data-center load is now visible in hyperscaler capex, networking gear, power generation, transmission rate base, electrical equipment, and cooling. That breadth argues against a near-term deceleration at the infrastructure layer, even as the semiconductor layer flashed a warning (Broadcom's cut).

The durability caveat: hyperscaler/AI demand is lumpier and more concentrated than Jabil's legacy diversified base. A single customer at 16% of revenue, sitting inside the growth segment, means the entire growth thesis carries idiosyncratic tail risk that the diversified legacy business did not.

## Valuation Assessment

Using core EPS as the correct denominator: at ~$303, Jabil trades at ~23.7x current-year consensus EPS ($12.76) and ~18.1x next-year ($16.75). Against the EMS peer group — TTMI 101.8x, FLEX 65.2x, SANM 52.3x, FN 50.0x, PLXS 42.1x (median 52.3x on trailing GAAP) — Jabil's forward multiple is a clear discount, even allowing that peer trailing figures are inflated. The relative-value case is genuine rather than a screen artifact: Jabil's mix is shifting toward higher-margin data-center work while the multiple sits below the cohort's.

Analyst targets reinforce the point. The consensus range runs $365 (low) to $482 (high), mean $441, median $450. At $303, the stock trades ~17% below the most bearish target. On a stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x forward multiple, consensus targets tend to act as floors rather than ceilings — analysts have not yet fully caught up to the raised AI guide.

Two things keep me from a 7+ valuation score. First, "cheap" is not by itself a buy signal; a compressed forward multiple on a no-moat, thin-margin, single-customer-exposed business can persist if AI capex sentiment sours, and the market's willingness to re-rate the whole EMS group is itself cyclical. Second, the near-term options market is pricing meaningful stress (see Risk Assessment), suggesting the discount reflects real uncertainty about the durability of the AI ramp rather than pure mispricing. On balance the valuation is attractive but not asymmetric enough to override the concentration and cyclicality risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Jabil operates in a structurally low-margin, high-competition industry against Foxconn/Hon Hai, Flex, Celestica, Sanmina, Benchmark, and customers' own in-house manufacturing. The 10-K is candid that the principal competitive factors are cost, time-to-market, scale, global footprint, and advanced technology, and that Jabil's processes are "generally not subject to significant proprietary protection." There is no durable moat; the company competes on execution, footprint, and increasingly on engineering value-add.

The trajectory, however, is improving. The mix shift into Intelligent Infrastructure is favorable, and the capability acquisitions in liquid cooling (Mikros) and data-center power (Hanley) position Jabil in the highest-demand, higher-value parts of the AI build-out rather than in commodity box-assembly. Winning and growing share with a leading hyperscaler is evidence of competitiveness, even as it concentrates risk. Level of moat is weak; direction of positioning is positive. That combination supports a mid-range score.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific and material, which is why this is the lowest-scoring dimension.

1. **Single-customer concentration in the growth engine.** Customer A is 16% of revenue and sits in Intelligent Infrastructure. Cloud customers are described in the filings as "cyclically different," and customers generally do not commit beyond one quarter. A single hyperscaler pausing capex, insourcing, or reallocating sourcing would hit the fastest-growing, now-largest segment disproportionately.

2. **Working-capital fragility.** The $2.8B of consignment components, 135-day payables, five-day cash-conversion cycle, and $12.7B of receivables sold make reported cash flow sensitive to a demand reversal and to continued factoring capacity. A cooling AI cycle would risk inventory write-downs.

3. **Thin margins and pass-through economics.** Sub-10% gross margin leaves little cushion; any inability to pass through component or tariff costs quickly compresses profit.

4. **Geopolitics and tariffs.** Substantial China manufacturing and ~74% foreign-sourced revenue. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking IEEPA tariffs (with refunds now flowing) reduces one tail, and management expects to pass tariff costs through, but the exposure remains live.

5. **M&A integration and goodwill.** A step-up in acquisitions (Hanley $752M, Pii $309M, Rebound $133M, Mikros $63M) has more than doubled goodwill and intangibles, raising integration and impairment risk.

6. **Hyperscaler-capex hostage.** Jabil is a leveraged, low-margin expression of AI infrastructure demand. If the demand-side questions being debated around hyperscaler capex (usage plateaus, financing sustainability) prove correct, JBL de-rates faster than the median IT name.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Front-month ATM IV is 80.4% versus 43.6% one-year historical volatility — richly priced, in the 84th percentile of the current chain — with a 14.6% near-term put skew and a put/call OI ratio of 1.39. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 80.4% > six-month 57.2%), a signal of a market-anticipated near-term move. IV has collapsed from 159.9% around the June 17 earnings event to 80.4%, but the residual backwardation and put-heavy near-term positioning indicate the market remains cautious. The six-month skew is negative (-4.6%), so longer-dated positioning is more balanced-to-call-heavy; the fear is concentrated in the near term. For a name I am rating constructively, the elevated near-term put demand is a divergence worth flagging: it argues for patience on entry rather than chasing a bounce. Short interest is low (3.7% of float, 2.0 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic and no crowded-bear thesis to lean on.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Jabil is a reasonably priced, execution-proven beneficiary of the confirmed data-center build-out. AI revenue guided to $13.6B and FY2027 core margin above 6% mean the mix shift is both a growth and a margin story. At ~18x next-year core EPS the stock is cheap versus its EMS peers (median 52x trailing) and ~17% below the lowest analyst target. Continued hyperscaler capex, execution on Hanley/Mikros liquid-cooling and power capabilities, and further buybacks against a light-capex model drive core EPS toward $18-19 in FY2027. A re-rating to 22-24x forward would put the stock at $400-450. Bull target ~$450.

### Bear Case
The growth is a leveraged bet on one hyperscaler's capex and on a working-capital structure that can reverse quickly. If AI capex decelerates (the Broadcom warning generalizes, or Customer A pauses/insources), Jabil faces inventory write-downs, a compressing cash-conversion cycle, and estimate cuts against a thin-margin base with no moat. The forward multiple compresses to ~14x on lowered estimates, taking the stock to $210-230, roughly 25-30% below current. The uniform insider selling with no open-market buying, and the options market's near-term stress pricing, are consistent with limited near-term upside conviction.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented), constructive with a defined upgrade path. Timeframe: 12 months.**

- **Base case target: ~$335** (≈20x FY2027 core EPS of $16.75, or ≈26x FY2026 core EPS of $12.76). That is ~11% above the current ~$303, reflecting continued AI-mix execution without a full peer re-rating.
- **Bull case target: ~$450** (~24-27x FY2027 core EPS on rising estimates and a group re-rating; consistent with the $441 mean / $482 high analyst targets). ≈+49% from current.
- **Bear case target: ~$220** (~14x cut FY2027 estimates on an AI-capex reversal and working-capital unwind). ≈-27% from current.

**Upgrade to BUY if:** (1) a second consecutive quarter confirms the AI ramp with sustained Intelligent Infrastructure growth >30% and segment margin holding ≥6%; (2) the stock pulls back toward the low-$270s, widening the discount to the $365 analyst floor and improving risk/reward past 2:1; or (3) next-year core EPS estimates continue rising toward $18+ while the forward multiple stays below 20x.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:** (1) Customer A/hyperscaler demand shows signs of pausing (Intelligent Infrastructure sequential revenue declines, a lowered AI-revenue guide, or a canceled/reduced order), (2) the consignment/payables structure reverses — cash-conversion cycle expanding materially with inventory write-downs — or (3) FY2027 core-margin guidance of >6% is walked back.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal-quarter print (late September 2026, FQ4 FY2026, which sets the FY2026 close and FY2027 framing) is the natural checkpoint; monitor hyperscaler capex commentary in the interim.

## Conclusion

Jabil is executing well on a genuine AI-infrastructure transformation, and after a ~20% pullback from June highs on confirming rather than deteriorating fundamentals, the risk/reward has improved. The forward valuation (~18x FY2027 core EPS) is reasonable and discounted to peers, estimate revisions are positive, and the analyst target floor sits ~17% above the current price. Information Technology is also the one sector where careful stock selection has historically earned durable outperformance, which supports a constructive lean.

What holds me back from a clean BUY is the risk profile: a no-moat, sub-10%-margin pass-through model whose growth is concentrated in a single ~16% hyperscaler and financed by a favorable-but-fragile working-capital structure, precisely the setup that de-rates fastest if AI capex durability disappoints. The options market's near-term stress pricing and the uniform insider selling reinforce caution on entry. The overall score of 6.05 rises modestly from the prior 5.8, reflecting improving risk/reward, but the concentration and cyclicality keep the rating at a growth-oriented HOLD. A further pullback toward the low-$270s or a second quarter of AI confirmation would flip this to BUY.
