**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HCI Group is a Tampa-based property & casualty holding company that writes predominantly Florida homeowners' insurance (~91% of gross premium) through two carriers, augmented by a majority-owned publicly traded insurtech subsidiary (Exzeo, NYSE: XZO), two consolidated policyholder-owned reciprocal exchanges (CORE and Tailrow), and a newly licensed Cayman captive reinsurer (Fortex). The equity trades at $178.83, roughly 15% below its 52-week high of $210.50 but 29% above its 12-month trough, with a market cap of about $2.3 billion.

The investment case balances an unusually strong balance sheet and capital-return posture against structural concentration risk. HCI holds roughly $1 billion in cash and $1.1 billion in conservative investments against only ~$68 million of drawn debt, is executing an $80 million buyback authorized in March 2026 (already ~$41.5 million completed at an average near $159, below the current price), and carries a fresh Zacks upgrade and analyst targets clustered $225-255, 26-43% above spot. Management's five-year total shareholder return of 406 versus a peer group of 132 is genuine outperformance, and compensation actually paid swings hard with the stock (–$23.9 million in the 2022 loss year), evidencing real alignment.

The countervailing fact dominates the risk assessment: FY2025's record $320 million net income and Q1 2026's 56.9% net combined ratio reflect an absence of major landfalling hurricanes, not a durable structural improvement. Trailing EPS of $22.72 produces a headline P/E of 7.8x, but that number embeds catastrophe luck. Consensus forward EPS of ~$18.45 (2026) and ~$18.79 (2027), against which the stock trades near 9.7x, is the more honest anchor and already incorporates a normalized catastrophe load. We rate HCI a BUY with disciplined sizing: the valuation is attractive on normalized earnings and the sum-of-the-parts is real, but the position is a bet that Florida catastrophe experience stays manageable and that reinsurance costs remain contained.

## Company Overview

HCI operates a multi-entity structure that requires careful disaggregation to see the economics that actually accrue to shareholders. The core is **Insurance Operations**: Homeowners Choice Property & Casualty (HCPCI) and TypTap Insurance (TTIC), with a third carrier, perRisk (Arizona surplus lines), not yet writing. Policies in force reached ~297,800 in Q1 2026, up from ~278,400 a year earlier, driven by organic growth and opportunistic assumption of policies from state-backed Citizens Property Insurance Corp. through depopulation programs (13,917 policies / $35.8 million annualized premium assumed in Q1 2025).

**Exzeo Group (XZO)**, 82.5%-owned as of Q1 2026, completed its own IPO in November 2025. It provides proprietary underwriting, claims, and operational software for the P&C ecosystem, generating ~$27.6 million pre-tax segment income on ~$58 million revenue in Q1 2026, a higher-margin, higher-multiple business embedded inside an insurer valued at insurance multiples. The **reciprocal exchange operations** (CORE, launched 2024; Tailrow, launched 2025) are policyholder-owned reciprocals in which HCI holds no equity but consolidates as variable interest entities while earning management fees as attorney-in-fact. HCI also owns ~$103 million of commercial real estate.

The business grew as a Florida "take-out" specialist and now positions itself as a well-capitalized, technology-enabled survivor of a market that shed multiple carriers to insolvency. The 2022-2023 Florida tort and litigation reforms materially improved profitability for the survivors, and HCI's roughly 56% combined ratios in a benign year screen as best-in-class.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory and its interpretation.** Annual net income moved from –$55 million (FY2022) to +$89 million (FY2023), +$128 million (FY2024), and +$320 million (FY2025) on revenue of ~$901 million. The 2022 loss and the 2025 record both trace primarily to hurricane activity rather than operational change. The FY2025 result is not a run-rate.

**Q1 2026.** Total revenue was $242.9 million (+12.2% YoY), with net premiums earned of $222.2 million (+10.7%). Net income was $85.0 million, or $73.4 million after noncontrolling interests. Diluted EPS was $5.45 versus $5.35 a year prior, while basic EPS fell to $5.62 from $6.47, a function of a larger share count and more income allocated to Exzeo minority holders after the XZO IPO. The net combined ratio was 56.9% (loss ratio 29.5%, expense ratio 27.4%), with a gross loss ratio of just 20.1%, again reflecting no catastrophe losses in the quarter. Q1 also showed favorable prior-year reserve development, consistent with adequate-to-conservative reserving.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets were $2.61 billion against total equity of $1.17 billion (stockholders' equity $1.09 billion plus $84 million NCI). Cash and equivalents of roughly $1.0 billion plus $1.1 billion of total investments constitute extraordinary liquidity for a ~$2.3 billion market-cap insurer. The investment portfolio is conservative: 72% AA-rated, weighted-average duration 5.1 years. Leverage is minimal: only $36 million drawn on a $150 million revolver plus $32 million in promissory notes, with interest expense falling sharply after convertible notes converted to equity. Reserves totaled $566.8 million, of which $506 million is IBNR.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was ~$149 million in Q1 2026. The quarterly dividend is $0.40/share, and the buyback was already ~52% executed by early May. Buying back stock at $159 while it trades at $179 is a disciplined use of excess capital and a management signal on perceived value.

A note on screening tools: any Altman Z-Score for HCI should be disregarded, as the model is not built for insurers and produces misleadingly low readings because of insurance balance-sheet structure.

The financial health picture supports a score of 7.5. Liquidity, low leverage, and favorable reserve development are all strong. What holds it below an 8 is that the earnings quality is catastrophe-dependent, and the "phantom" consolidation layers (VIE reciprocals plus Exzeo minority) mean reported net income overstates per-share economics.

## Growth Analysis

Policy-count growth (~7% YoY) and net-premium-earned growth reflect genuine share gains in a recovering Florida market, plus fee income from the capital-light reciprocal-exchange model that grows premium without consuming HCI's underwriting equity. The Exzeo platform is a real growth vector with a differentiated margin profile, and the CORE/Tailrow reciprocals extend the franchise on a fee basis.

Forward estimate revisions are essentially flat: current-quarter consensus is –2.1% over 30 days, next-quarter –1.5%, full-year +1.0%, and next-year +1.7%. There is no accelerating positive revision velocity here; consensus has stabilized after the post-Q1 re-rating rather than continuing to climb. Forward EPS implies modest year-over-year growth off a normalized base, which is consistent with a mature hard-market carrier whose top line grows mid-to-high single digits while underwriting margins revert from a benign-season peak. The four consecutive earnings beats (Q3 2025 +114%, Q4 2025 +70%, Q1 2026 +4.8%) are almost entirely a function of catastrophe experience running below reserved expectations, not a sustainable beat cadence.

The growth score is 6.0: real, durable premium and fee growth, plus Exzeo optionality, offset by decelerating estimate momentum and the fact that reported earnings growth is inflated by weather.

## Valuation Assessment

Headline metrics understate the nuance. Trailing P/E of 7.8x looks extraordinarily cheap, but that EPS embeds catastrophe luck and is not a defensible base. On forward EPS, the stock trades at ~9.7x. That still compares favorably to the P&C peer median of 16.1x (STC 14.6x, PLMR 16.1x, PRA 19.6x, SAFT 16.9x; UFCS is the outlier at 9.6x).

A discount to peers is warranted for HCI's geographic concentration, since a single-state, single-peril book earns a lower multiple than diversified carriers, but the current gap looks wider than the concentration alone justifies, for three reasons. First, HCI's balance sheet is stronger than most peers, with near-zero net leverage. Second, the Exzeo stake is a genuine, separately market-priced insurtech asset embedded at insurance multiples; on a sum-of-the-parts basis, the higher-multiple XZO income is under-credited in a blended P&C multiple. Third, the buyback below market and analyst targets (mean $238) both point to intrinsic value above spot.

The equity lessons here warrant caution: within Financials, "cheap" has been a persistent value-trap signal, and a low multiple alone is never a buy. The distinction for HCI is that the cheapness comes with an identifiable, funded catalyst: an active buyback executing below market, a real SOTP asset, and a management incentive structure targeting $200 (200,000 RSUs vest only if the stock holds $200 for 30 consecutive trading days). That combination is why I score valuation 6.5 rather than treating the low P/E as a trap. I do not credit the trailing 7.8x; the honest anchor is ~9.7x forward, and the upside case depends on the peer discount narrowing modestly plus the SOTP being recognized, not on the trailing multiple re-rating.

The prior report's valuation score of 7.0 declined from an earlier 8.0; I take it a notch further to 6.5, not because the stock fell (it has actually risen since the June read) but because the estimate-revision momentum that would justify a higher score has flattened, and the normalized-earnings math leaves a good-but-not-exceptional discount once concentration risk is priced.

## Competitive Landscape

HCI's edge over commodity Florida homeowners writers is threefold. The Exzeo technology stack provides proprietary risk selection and claims efficiency that supports superior loss ratios. The reciprocal-exchange structure (CORE, Tailrow) grows premium and fee income without consuming HCI's own underwriting capital, a capital-light lever most peers lack. And the fortress balance sheet lets HCI opportunistically assume Citizens depopulation policies and expand while weaker competitors are capital-constrained.

The competitive trajectory is improving within a favorable structural backdrop: the 2022-2023 Florida litigation reforms reduced fraudulent and litigated claims, lifting profitability for surviving carriers, and HCI is a prime beneficiary as one of the stronger performers in a ~32-company P&C peer set. The offsetting reality is that the moat is narrow geographically, with the entire franchise levered to one state's regulatory and catastrophe environment, and the reforms that boosted the market can be reversed by future legislation. Competitive position scores 6.5: clearly improving share and margin position, with technology and capital advantages, capped by single-market exposure.

## Risk Assessment

**Catastrophe and geographic concentration.** Substantially all assets are in Florida. A single major hurricane can swing results from record profit to loss, as FY2022 demonstrated. The combined ratios are luck-dependent and will not persist through an active season. This is the dominant risk and the primary reason the risk-factor score is low.

**Reinsurance dependence.** HCI cedes ~32% of gross premium, renewing every June 1. Roughly 68% of recoverables sit with six reinsurers (including the Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund), a counterparty concentration. The new Fortex captive partially hedges rising third-party costs, but reinsurance pricing remains a direct margin swing factor.

**Reserve adequacy.** The IBNR share of reserves is judgment-heavy, though recent development has been favorable rather than adverse.

**Regulatory and rate risk.** Rates and forms require FLOIR approval, and subsidiary dividends to the holding company are restricted ($44.5 million paid up in Q1 2026). Regulatory change cuts both ways.

**Interest-rate risk.** A 300bp rate rise would cut ~$128 million (–14%) from the fixed-maturity portfolio's fair value through OCI; AOCI already swung to –$4.5 million in Q1 2026.

**Governance items to monitor.** The December 2025 Exzeo employment agreement formalizes CEO Patel's dual role at parent and public subsidiary, creating a potential attention-split/conflict dynamic between HCI and XZO shareholders. A small (~$1.0 million/year) related-party reinsurance contract with Oxbridge Reinsurance, whose Chairman/CEO is an HCI director, is fully collateralized and market-rate but worth watching.

The risk-factor score is 4.5. The catastrophe binary, reinsurance counterparty concentration, and single-state exposure are specific, material, and only partly priced. This is the score that most constrains the overall rating.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility of 41.5% sits well above one-year historical volatility of 32.0% (a 9.5-point rich spread, 70th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (41.5% near-term versus 30.5% at six months); the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with hurricane-season seasonality and the June/summer reinsurance-and-storm calendar. Put/call skew is modestly elevated at 3.1% near-dated, flipping to negative (call-favored) at three and six months, suggesting hedging demand is concentrated in the immediate term rather than reflecting a structurally bearish view. IV compressed sharply from 58.5% to 41.5% since mid-June as the Q1 event passed. Any position taken here carries embedded event risk into the Atlantic hurricane season; this does not contradict the fundamental thesis but argues for sizing that can absorb a catastrophe drawdown. Short interest is low at 4.6% of float (2.5 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Florida's post-reform hard market persists, catastrophe experience stays manageable, and HCI compounds premium and fee income mid-to-high single digits while returning capital aggressively. The buyback below intrinsic value shrinks the share count, the forward multiple re-rates toward the peer median as the SOTP value of Exzeo is recognized, and the stock clears the $200 management-incentive hurdle. Analyst targets are realized. Bull target ~$255 (analyst high, roughly the peer-median forward multiple applied plus SOTP credit), ~43% above spot.

**Bear case.** A major hurricane makes landfall in the Florida book, driving the combined ratio from ~57% toward or above 100%, cutting a season's earnings by hundreds of millions and pressuring book value. Reinsurance costs spike at the June renewal, compressing margins further. The NCI leakage from Exzeo and the reciprocals continues to dilute per-share economics, and the market re-anchors on cat-loaded earnings. Bear target ~$135-140 (near the 52-week low, ~10x a stressed normalized EPS with a concentration discount), ~22-25% below spot.

**Base case.** Benign-to-average catastrophe experience, mid-single-digit premium growth, continued buyback, and a modest multiple expansion toward ~11x normalized forward EPS. Base target ~$205, roughly the prior high, ~15% above spot.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$205.** ~11x normalized forward EPS of ~$18.50 plus partial SOTP credit for the Exzeo stake, ~15% above the current $178.83.

**Bull case target: ~$255.** Peer-median forward multiple recognition plus full SOTP credit and clearing the $200 incentive hurdle, ~43% upside.

**Bear case target: ~$135-140.** A material Florida catastrophe season plus reinsurance-cost pressure re-anchoring the market on cat-loaded earnings, ~22-25% downside.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to the June 1 reinsurance renewal, the June-November Atlantic hurricane season, and quarterly earnings that will reveal the pace of buyback execution and Exzeo/NCI dynamics.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- A major hurricane landfall driving the reported quarterly net combined ratio above 100%, or FLOIR-approved rate inadequacy that structurally impairs underwriting margin.
- June 2026 reinsurance renewal at materially higher cost (ceded premium ratio rising well above the ~32% level) that compresses forward margins.
- A halt to the buyback or a shift to dilutive issuance, which would reverse the capital-return signal underpinning the valuation case.
- Adverse prior-year reserve development, reversing the favorable pattern and signaling under-reserving in the judgment-heavy IBNR book.

## Conclusion

HCI is a BUY with disciplined sizing. The stock trades at ~9.7x normalized forward earnings against a peer median of 16.1x, carries near-zero net leverage, is buying back stock below market, and holds a genuine, separately priced insurtech asset (Exzeo) that a blended insurance multiple under-credits. Management alignment is demonstrated rather than assumed: five-year TSR of 406 versus 132 for peers, compensation that swung to –$23.9 million in the 2022 loss year, and a CEO retaining deep-in-the-money option shares. Analyst targets and a fresh Zacks upgrade corroborate the mispricing thesis.

The rating is constrained, not driven, by the low multiple. The trailing 7.8x is inflated by benign hurricane seasons; the durable anchor is forward earnings that already load a normalized catastrophe cost. The dominant risk is a single-state, single-peril concentration that makes the P&L binary on Florida weather, and the near-term options market is pricing that seasonal stress. The recommendation continues a multi-report BUY, but the bar for that continuation is met specifically by the funded buyback below market and the SOTP optionality; the appreciation case does not depend on the trailing multiple re-rating. Size the position to absorb a catastrophe-season drawdown, and treat the June renewal and hurricane-season losses as the live tests of the thesis.
