## Executive Summary

The January 7, 2026 close of the $21.8B Calpine acquisition added roughly 23 GW of dispatchable gas capacity across ERCOT, CAISO and PJM, geothermal, batteries and a competitive retail platform serving ~62 TWh annually, on top of the largest US nuclear fleet (~179 million MWh of expected 2026 output). Total assets went from $57.2B to $96.9B in a single quarter; equity from $14.5B to $33.5B; goodwill from $0.4B to $11.5B. Segment reporting expanded to six segments. Nothing about the pre-2026 financial history is directly comparable.

The stock is 36.3% below its October 15, 2025 high of $412.70 and 14.9% above its July 1, 2026 low of $228.63. That drawdown is not explained by earnings deterioration. FY2026 adjusted operating EPS guidance of $11.00-$12.00 was affirmed on the May 11 print; Q1 adjusted operating EPS of $2.74 beat consensus by 5.7% while absorbing roughly $660M of gross unfavorable purchased-power cost from the January weather event, a $350M Illinois CMC drag, and 11 additional refueling outage days. Current-year consensus has risen 0.6% over 90 days. What has changed is the multiple, and the reasons are identifiable: FERC's December 2025 draft order finding PJM's tariff unjust as to co-located load truncates the behind-the-meter nuclear premium that drove the 2025 re-rating; PJM's RPM price collar (cap ~$325/MW-day, accepted by FERC in April 2026) caps capacity upside; and 50 million shares were issued at $354.58, with roughly 25 million unlocking on June 30, 2026 into a weak tape.

The judgment is that this is a high-quality growth asset trading at a fair rather than cheap multiple, with an unusually dense near-term binary calendar. At $262.75 the stock is 22.4x current-year consensus of $11.71 and 19.6x next-year $13.38, modestly below the 23.8x peer median, but that comparison flatters CEG because the peer figures are trailing and regulated peers cluster nearer 17-19x forward. The September 4 DOJ divestiture deadline is five weeks away with the Jack A. Fusco asset unsigned as of the last filing, PJM has not filed its co-located-load compliance tariff, out-year estimates are drifting lower, and the options market prices a ±7.3% move on a three-day expiry. **HOLD**, with a base case of $300 (+14.2%), bull $370 (+40.8%), bear $210 (-20.1%).

## Company Overview

Constellation describes itself as the largest private-sector power producer in the world, with 55 GW of capacity across nuclear, gas, oil, geothermal, hydro, wind and solar, serving approximately 2.5 million customer accounts including 80% of the Fortune 100. It was separated from Exelon in February 2022.

Merger consideration for Calpine was $21,835M: 50 million newly issued CEG shares valued at $17,603M (struck at the January 6, 2026 close of $354.58) plus $4,342M cash, less $96M of stock subject to post-combination vesting and $14M for pre-existing relationships. Constellation also assumed ~$12.6B of Calpine debt at fair value ($12,551M recognized), split roughly $7.6B corporate and $5.0B project financings.

The strategic logic is threefold and largely verifiable from the filings. First, the retail and load book naturally offsets generation length, converting a price-taking merchant nuclear operator into an integrated generator/retailer. Second, existing interconnection at operating gas sites ("powered land") is the scarcest input in the AI build-out; the CyrusOne agreements are direct monetization of it. Third, geographic diversification away from PJM concentration. One caution: the segment disclosures establish revenue-mix diversification across ISOs, but there is no direct customer-concentration disclosure for the combined retail and wholesale book beyond the derivative-counterparty table (one counterparty above 10% of net derivative exposure, $457M of $2,413M). Changing geographic mix does not by itself establish customer diversification.

On the commercial side, roles matter. Constellation signed a new 380 MW agreement with CyrusOne to connect and serve a data center adjacent to the Freestone Energy Center in Texas, with an exclusive agreement covering a further 380 MW phase 2; these follow 400 MW of Calpine-CyrusOne agreements at Thad Hill announced in 2H 2025. CyrusOne pays Constellation for power, grid connectivity and site infrastructure and develops and owns the data centers. Constellation is the energy and infrastructure provider. Management framed the economics on the Q1 call at roughly $0.20-$0.50 of EPS per 1,000 MW of powered land.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP earnings are structurally uninformative for this company, in both directions.** FY2025 GAAP net income to common fell 38% to $2,319M ($7.40 diluted EPS) while adjusted operating earnings rose 7.6% to $2,944M ($9.39 adjusted EPS, +8.3%). Two mechanisms explain the gap. The IRA nuclear PTC functions as a floor that phases out as annual gross receipts rise through roughly $26.00-$44.75/MWh; recognized PTC revenue fell from approximately $2.08B in 2024 to approximately $0.32B in 2025 because energy and capacity revenue exceeded the phase-out band, a consequence of higher realized prices, not a subsidy cut. The Q1 2026 10-Q confirms the mechanism directly, stating no material PTC benefit was recorded in either Q1 2026 or Q1 2025 as full-year gross receipts exceeded the phase-out for most units. The second driver is unrealized mark-to-market on economic hedges, favorable in FY2024 and unfavorable in FY2025.

The reverse distortion appeared in Q1 2026: GAAP diluted EPS of $4.49 was 64% above adjusted operating EPS of $2.74, inflated by $721M of after-tax unrealized fair-value gains and $174M of decommissioning items. Trailing GAAP EPS of $7.40 against $262.75 produces a 35.5x multiple that describes nothing. The practical consequence is that net-income-dependent screening components (GAAP margin trend, ROA change) are unreliable for CEG specifically, while leverage, liquidity and cash-flow components remain informative.

**Q1 2026 operating detail.** Revenues $11,122M vs $6,788M; operating income $2,332M vs $451M; adjusted operating earnings $972M vs $673M (+44.4% in dollars, +28.0% per share on a 12.7% higher share count). Calpine contributed $1,126M of RNF on $2,395M of segment revenue in under three months. Legacy segments were roughly flat on RNF (Mid-Atlantic $812M vs $809M; Midwest $854M vs $850M; New York $409M vs $401M; ERCOT $209M vs $214M) despite day-ahead prices rising 43-81%, because of the CMC clawback and the January weather event.

**Leverage is manageable and lower than screener outputs suggest.** Total debt = $17,364M long-term (including current) + $5,102M short-term = $22,466M; less $800M cash gives net debt of approximately $21,666M. Debt/equity is 0.67x. A "D/E" near 2.9x sometimes reported is total liabilities/equity computed on the pre-close balance sheet and includes $11.8B of trust-funded nuclear decommissioning AROs and $5.4B of Regulatory Agreement Unit payables; neither is financial debt. Against reverse-engineered 2026E adjusted EBITDA of approximately $8,336M (see Valuation), net debt/EBITDA is roughly 2.6x, and pro forma for $5.0B of divestiture proceeds against the associated EBITDA loss, plausibly 2.2-2.4x. S&P BBB+ and Moody's Baa1 were both affirmed in January 2026 after the close, a three-notch cushion to sub-investment grade.

**Liquidity is adequate but the downgrade trigger consumes most of it.** $15.05B of aggregate bank commitments, $6.7B available capacity plus $0.8B cash at March 31; the revolver was upsized from $4.5B to $7.0B. All covenants in compliance. A downgrade below investment grade would require approximately $3.0B of incremental collateral, coverable by $7.5B of liquidity, but it would absorb 40% of it.

**Do not annualize the quarter's cash flow.** Q1 operating cash flow was $425M against $1,275M of capex, distorted by a $1,377M accounts-payable outflow, a $191M pension contribution, the $235M Exelon TMA remittance, and approximately $450M of state-program refunds relating to 2024 PTCs, partly offset by $249M of collateral inflow and a $323M receivable decrease. FY2025 GAAP operating cash flow was $4,237M on $2,949M of capex. Management guides free cash flow before growth of approximately $8.4B cumulatively for 2026-2027 and $11.5-13.0B for 2028-2029.

FCFbG is a planning metric, not free cash flow: it excludes growth capex and after-tax asset-sale proceeds. Against roughly $5.7B of 2026 and $4.7B of 2027 capex, of which approximately $3.9B across the two years is growth capex (Crane restart, uprates, co-location infrastructure, license renewals), true two-year free cash flow after growth capex is closer to $8.4B − $3.9B ≈ $4.5B, or ~$2.25B annually, a **2.4% FCF yield** on $93.8B, before divestiture proceeds. That is thin, and it is the single most important qualifier on the financial-health score. The offsets are real: BBB+/Baa1 affirmed, 0.67x debt/equity, $7.5B liquidity, $5.0B of near-term divestiture cash, and an incentive plan that includes cash-flow-from-operations/debt as a performance measure. Financial health scores **6.4**.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has one high-confidence contractual leg and one lower-confidence market/regulatory leg.

**The contractual leg: Illinois CMC roll-off.** The CMC is a two-way contract-for-differences under which the IPA subtracts energy and capacity index prices from a bid price of $33.50/MWh; when negative, Constellation pays ComEd. The average realized CMC price was negative $26.18/MWh in Q1 2026 versus negative $2.04/MWh a year earlier, because ComEd-zone day-ahead power averaged $50.71/MWh (+43.6%) and ComEd capacity cleared at $269.92/MW-day (+833%). The Midwest segment recorded $350M of unfavorable CMC revenue in the quarter alone. Management's modeling shows CMC volumes of 53 million MWh in 2026 falling to 23 million in 2027 and zero from 2028, consistent with the program's delivery-year structure ending May 31, 2027. Re-pricing the 30 million MWh released in 2027 from ~$34.09 to ~$45.75 adds approximately $350M pre-tax; the residual 23 million MWh released in 2028 re-pricing from ~$34.50 to ~$48.88 adds approximately $331M. Combined ~$681M pre-tax, ~$504M after tax at 26%, ~$1.40/share on 361M shares. This is roughly 12% of the 2026 midpoint delivered by contract expiry rather than by a price bet, and it is the highest-confidence component of the plan. Two caveats: it is already embedded in the base framework and is therefore not upside to guidance, and the released units are then market-exposed with the PTC floor as protection only below the phase-out band. The FY2026 NI Hub forward reference near $44/MWh sits close to the threshold, so ComEd-zone units carry less cushion than PJM-West units.

**The market/regulatory leg.** Management's 2029 framework specifies base adjusted operating EPS of $11.40-$11.90 with "enhanced" earnings at 30-35% of total, implying a total of roughly $16.3-$18.3 (arithmetic on management's disclosed inputs, not a company-published figure). Base EPS CAGR from the 2026 base midpoint of $6.70 to the 2029 base midpoint of $11.65 is 20.3%; total adjusted EPS CAGR 2026→2029 implied is approximately 14.5%. Consensus is more conservative and closely tracks the near-term path: current year $11.71, next year $13.38 (+14.3%).

Supporting evidence: Crane Clean Energy Center (835 MW, 20-year Microsoft PPA) with the Eddystone-to-Crane capacity interconnection rights waiver reportedly granted by FERC on or about June 2, 2026 (externally reported and corroborated by an Item 8.01 8-K whose text was not read; the scope of contingent-upgrade relief is unverified, and PJM's Phase I study had identified upgrades with in-service dates as late as December 2030); ~5,000 MW of new capacity submitted into PJM processes; Pin Oak Creek (460 MW ERCOT peaker) commercial in April 2026 with a 25% interest sold in May; Pastoria Solar (105 MW) commissioned April 2026 with a co-located 80 MW/320 MWh battery under a 15-year PG&E PPA.

**Estimate revisions cut against the momentum framing and must be weighted.** Current quarter consensus fell 5.3% over 90 days to $2.40; next quarter fell 3.4% to $3.62; next year fell 1.6% to $13.38. Only the current year rose (+0.6% to $11.71). This is not the accelerating-revision pattern that precedes re-rating. The pattern is consistent with analysts trimming quarterly phasing while holding the annual number, but it is negative at the margin and argues against paying up. Growth prospects score **7.0**, down from the peak levels of earlier this year, reflecting a durable multi-year ramp with a large contractual component but no upward revision momentum and regulatory gating on the highest-value optionality.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is the fleet. Constellation owns the largest US nuclear fleet, and nuclear is the only large-scale always-on carbon-free resource, which is why hyperscalers contract for it directly. Management's claim that fleet replacement cost exceeds 3x enterprise value (implying >$350B, or >$6,400/kW blended) is an unaudited assertion from the earnings call; it is plausible for a one-third-nuclear fleet but not independently verified.

The binding constraint on the data-center thesis is regulatory, not demand. FERC's December 2025 draft order found PJM's tariff unjust and unreasonable as to co-located load, directed PJM to specify terms and require transmission customers serving co-located load to choose among four service options, and found behind-the-meter generation rules no longer appropriate. **As of the May 11, 2026 filing, PJM had not filed its final compliance tariff revisions, and the form and timing remain subject to stakeholder process and FERC review.** This is the gating item for behind-the-meter nuclear deals of the Crane-Microsoft type and remains unresolved. Separately, FERC accepted PJM's extension of the RPM price collar (floor ~$175/MW-day) for the 2028/29 and 2029/30 Base Residual Auctions in April 2026, truncating capacity upside from data-center scarcity while providing a floor. Management's expected sequence — PJM filing around June 2026, Phase 1 bilateral contracting from about September 2026, central procurement around March 2027 — is a plan, not a schedule the company controls.

The realized price backdrop is strongly favorable in Eastern PJM: Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025 day-ahead ATC of PJM West $97.16 vs $53.69 (+81%), NYISO Central $112.23 vs $75.31 (+49%), ComEd $50.71 vs $35.31 (+44%), ERCOT North $40.65 vs $31.39 (+30%), and CAISO NP15 $29.01 vs $40.96 (−29%). PJM EMAAC and ComEd capacity both cleared at $269.92/MW-day.

Competitive position trajectory is mixed rather than improving: the Calpine integration widens the addressable set (powered land, retail offsets, ERCOT/CAISO reach) and the CyrusOne agreements convert it to contracted revenue, while the co-located-load proceeding and the capacity collar administratively cap the highest-value monetization path. Score **7.0**.

## Risk Assessment

**1. September 4, 2026 DOJ divestiture deadline (five weeks out, unsatisfied).** The DOJ resolution requires definitive agreements to divest five PJM assets, one ERCOT asset, and Calpine's Gregory interest within 240 days of closing. Gregory closed in January. The five PJM plants (Bethlehem 1,130 MW, Hay Road 1,130 MW, York 2 828 MW, Edge Moor 725 MW, York 1 565 MW; 4,378 MW total) are under a March 2026 agreement with LS Power for $5.0B pre-adjustments, subject to DOJ and FERC review. The remaining ERCOT asset, Jack A. Fusco (609 MW), had no signed agreement as of May 11 and is entangled in project-finance collateral: CCFC may require lender consent to release Fusco as guarantor, and the York centers secure the CDHI Revolver. Assets held for sale carried $5,735M at March 31 against acquisition-date fair value less costs to sell of $5,603M, with no impairment recognized, so the LS Power sale should produce little accounting gain or loss while delivering ~$5.0B of cash (≈$1,142/kW). A slip past September 4 would push toward a DOJ extension or a divestiture trustee; a trustee-run sale would likely realize less than carrying value. The consent-decree terms were not reviewed. Do not treat the outcome as settled.

**2. Goodwill concentration on preliminary accounting.** Calpine goodwill of $11,107M is 34% of total equity, is not expected to be tax-deductible, and **has not yet been assigned to reporting units**. The measurement period runs to January 2027, so PP&E, intangibles, AROs, contingencies and deferred taxes remain provisional with revisions flowing to goodwill. The goodwill was struck against a $354.58 share price; the 50 million shares issued would be worth ~$13.1B at $262.75 rather than $17.6B. This does not mechanically imply impairment — testing is against reporting-unit fair value, not the parent's share price — but no reporting-unit baseline has been established, which is unusual six months post-close.

**3. Level 3 derivative concentration.** Of $2,493M of net commodity derivative assets, $2,173M is Level 3, up from $140M at year-end: $1,290M from contracts acquired at the acquisition date plus $848M of gains recognized in the quarter, $568M unrealized. Level 3 forward power price inputs span $2.43-$181/MWh. These marks are excluded from adjusted operating earnings, which limits the earnings-quality concern, but the balance-sheet concentration in unobservable valuations is material.

**4. Credit trigger.** A downgrade below investment grade requires approximately $3.0B of incremental collateral against $7.5B of liquidity. Three notches of cushion make this remote near-term, but the debt load ($22.5B) and $5.7B of 2026 capex leave the balance sheet sensitive to a persistently higher cost of capital.

**5. Weather and load-serving asymmetry.** Despite being long generation, the January 2026 event produced $350M of net unfavorable purchased-power cost in Mid-Atlantic and $310M in Midwest. A large retail and wholesale book plus 62 TWh of acquired Calpine load means extreme weather can be net negative for a long-generation portfolio.

**6. Operational and fuel.** Nuclear capacity factor 92.3% vs 94.1% (99 vs 88 outage days). Approximately 35% of uranium concentrate requirements for the remainder of 2026 through 2031 come from three suppliers, including contracts sourced from Russia, with non-performance flagged as potentially material. Illinois CEJA could force Zion Energy Center (503 MW) to shut by January 2030 absent relief.

**7. Share supply.** The second 50% tranche of Calpine consideration shares (~25 million, ~6.9% of outstanding) unlocks June 30, 2027, with registration rights in place. The first tranche unlocked June 30, 2026, one day before the 52-week low — a plausible contributor, but no Form 4, 13D/G or secondary-offering evidence of actual selling was located, so the causal link is a hypothesis.

**8. Litigation.** Winter Storm Uri tort litigation continues; the Texas Supreme Court denied plaintiffs' mandamus petitions in all five bellwether appeals in March 2026, with rehearing sought. Accrued litigation liabilities were $136M.

Risk factors score **5.0**. Item 1A was unchanged in the first post-close quarter, but the number of live, dated binaries — DOJ September 4, PJM compliance filing, unassigned goodwill through January 2027, a 2027 lock-up — is high relative to what a 22.8x forward multiple discounts.

Out-year estimates keep drifting lower and 2028 market exposure — where Item 3's hedge protection expires — proves less favorable than the base framework assumes. Reporting-unit goodwill assignment in late 2026 establishes a baseline with limited cushion on gas assets. Multiple compresses to ~16x next-year $13.38.

### Base Case — $300 (+14.2%)

22.5x next-year consensus of $13.38 = $301. Guidance is delivered near the upper half of $11.00-$12.00 (Q1's $972M was 23.4% of the implied full-year figure while absorbing weather cost and the CMC drag, and management affirmed rather than raised — though Q1 is seasonally strong for a winter-peaking Northeast book and Calpine's ERCOT/CAISO assets are summer-weighted, so this is not by itself evidence of conservatism). ## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive and catalyst-identified, but the entry price does not compensate for the binary density.

**Trajectory note.** Scores have moved 6.8 → 6.1 → 6.2 → 5.9 → 6.5 → 6.4, with growth prospects down 1.7 points and competitive position down 1.3 from earlier peaks. This holds at 6.35. The price is 4.2% lower than at the last review while guidance is unchanged and current-year consensus has risen 0.6% over 90 days — improving risk/reward on unchanged fundamentals, which argues against cutting scores. Offsetting that, out-year consensus has fallen 1.6% and next-quarter 3.4%, and the September 4 DOJ deadline has moved from distant to imminent without the Fusco agreement being signed. Those two changes are why the recommendation does not upgrade despite the lower price. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the specific conditions that would break the pattern are below.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **PJM files a co-located-load compliance tariff that preserves economics for behind-the-meter nuclear service**, or FERC accepts terms that permit Crane-type structures without material incremental network charges. This is the largest single unpriced swing factor.
2. **Price toward $230-240** with guidance intact — approximately 17.5x next-year $13.38, at which point the base case offers 25%+ upside against a bear case near the July low.
3. **Fusco definitive agreement signed and LS Power cleared by DOJ/FERC before September 4**, combined with disclosed buyback execution using divestiture proceeds at prices below $270. Confirmation that ~$5B of cash is being deployed below the issuance reference would materially improve the per-share arithmetic.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **September 4 passes without a Fusco agreement** and DOJ moves to a divestiture trustee, or LS Power clearance is denied.
