**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Chord Energy is the scaled, low-cost consolidator of the Williston Basin, the largest acreage holder (1.30 million net acres) and top producer (277 MBoe/d, 56% oil) in the Bakken/Three Forks. The business is built for free-cash-flow harvest rather than production growth, and the cash figures confirm it: 2025 operating cash flow held at $2.04B (down only 3% YoY) and free cash flow reached $693M (14.2% margin) even as realized crude fell ~15% to $62.78/bbl. The GAAP headline of net income down 95% to $44.5M is a bookkeeping artifact, not an operational failure. Roughly 92% of that decline is a $539.3M non-cash goodwill impairment (goodwill written to zero) plus an acquisition-driven DD&A step-up. Q1 2026 confirmed the recovery, with net income of $108.6M and realized crude back to $70.05/bbl following the late-February Middle East escalation.

The investment case is a value/return-of-capital story: ~7.5x forward earnings, a projected >$1.5B of 2026 FCF (roughly a mid-teens-to-high-teens FCF yield on the ~$7.2B market cap), ~0.4x net leverage, and $881M remaining on a $1.0B buyback authorization. Management has pivoted from variable dividends to buybacks, signaling it views the equity as undervalued at depressed prices. That pivot is shareholder-friendly on a per-share basis but reduces the headline yield (base dividend cut from $10.15 total in 2024 to $5.20 in 2025).

We rate CHRD a HOLD. The quality of the cash engine and the balance sheet are not in dispute, but three factors prevent an asymmetric BUY at $128: (1) the stock trades ~52% above its 52-week low and near the middle of its 52-week range, with no discount to the buyback zone that would improve entry; (2) forward estimate revisions have turned negative across the near-term windows (current-year down 9% in 30 days, next-quarter down 26%) even as the reported quarter beat sharply; and (3) the entire earnings power is a two-sided function of the unresolved Hormuz oil binary: a verified reopening pulls crude toward $85-95 and compresses realizations, while a failure spikes it but also risks Bakken-specific differential and takeaway stress. This is a business to own on weakness toward the low-$100s where the FCF yield and buyback support turn the risk/reward asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Chord Energy is a pure-play independent E&P formed from the 2022 Oasis/Whiting merger and scaled through the May 2024 Enerplus acquisition and the October 2025 XTO/Williston bolt-on ($542.2M). Operations are ~100% concentrated in the Williston Basin across North Dakota and Montana, with limited non-operated Marcellus gas interests.

Key operational facts from the FY2025 10-K:
- **1.30 million net leasehold acres**, essentially all held-by-production (only ~1,600 net acres expire in 2026 — negligible expiry risk).
- **917.5 MMBoe proved reserves** (56% oil, 69% proved developed); PV-10 of $9.1B, Standardized Measure $7.5B.
- 2025 production averaged **276,620 Boepd** at ~78% average operated working interest; 89% of proved reserves are operated.
- Competitive cost structure: LOE $9.73/Boe, gathering/processing/transportation $2.88/Boe, production taxes $2.89/Boe.
- Only 676 employees for a $7B-cap producer, headquartered in Houston.

The strategic identity is the Bakken consolidator: acquire contiguous acreage, deploy 3-4 mile laterals and multi-bench pad drilling, and compound FCF per share. The Williston is a mature, cash-harvest basin with a lower-growth profile than the Permian, where the thesis is durability of cash returns rather than volume expansion.

## Financial Analysis

**Cash generation is the real signal, and it is strong.** Revenue was $4.88B in 2025, down 7% YoY, driven by a $10.89/bbl (~15%) drop in realized crude to $62.78/bbl and a $435M reduction in low-margin purchased-oil-and-gas pass-through revenue. Underlying production grew (100,966 MBoe vs. 85,182 the prior year on Enerplus, XTO, and organic additions). Operating cash flow fell only 3% to $2.04B; against $1.35B of capex, that produced $693M of FCF at a 14.2% margin, a self-funding model even at ~$63 WTI.

**The headline earnings collapse is non-cash.** Net income fell to $44.5M from $848.6M, but ~92% of the drop is attributable to the $539.3M Q2 2025 goodwill impairment (share-price-triggered; goodwill zeroed) plus a higher DD&A rate ($14.12/Boe, +$1.82) from acquisition purchase-price step-ups. The 81.7% effective tax rate is itself an impairment artifact. Excluding the impairment, pre-tax profitability was solid.

**The balance sheet is a genuine strength.** Nothing is drawn on the $2.75B borrowing base ($2.0B elected commitments, reaffirmed May 2026). Debt is $1.5B in senior notes (6.0% 2030s, 6.75% 2033s) with the $400M 6.375% 2026 notes retired, leaving no near-term maturity wall. D/E is 0.62 and net leverage sits under 0.5x. The Altman Z of 2.27 ("grey zone") is a structural artifact of asset-heavy E&P accounting (large PP&E, high DD&A depressing EBIT/assets) rather than distress; a Piotroski of 5/5 on available signals corroborates the underlying strength.

**Q1 2026 confirmed the recovery.** Net income was $108.6M, operating cash flow $507M, production 275,615 Boepd (57% oil), and realized crude jumped to $70.05/bbl on the WTI spike. Reported net income was suppressed by a $241.5M non-cash mark-to-market derivative loss (rising futures curve); Chord's hedges are not designated as hedges for accounting, so MTM swings hit the P&L (they added a $127.6M gain in FY25). Investors should look through to cash-realized settlements, not GAAP derivative line items.

**Capital return.** In 2025 Chord returned ~$667M ($302.5M base dividends + $364.5M buybacks) and shifted from variable dividends to repurchases. $881M remains on the $1.0B authorization. External projections put 2026 FCF above $1.5B, which at the current market cap implies a high-teens FCF yield if the price deck holds.

## Growth Analysis

Chord is not a growth story in the volume sense. The Williston is a mature basin; the model is FCF-per-share compounding funded by disciplined acquisitions and share retirement. Organic production is roughly flat-to-modestly-higher, with the 2026 program guiding $1.35-1.45B capex across 135-165 wells and full-year oil guidance raised ~2,000 bopd at Q1 with no capex increase, a modest capital-efficiency positive.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and net-negative near-term, which weighs against a stronger growth score:
- Current quarter: $6.57, revised +8.9% over 30 days and +38.8% over 90 days, a large positive that reflects the crude spike flowing through realizations.
- Next quarter: $4.22, revised **-25.7%** over 30 days, indicating the market expects the price spike to partially reverse.
- Current year: $19.59, revised **-9.0%** over 30 days (though still +15.1% over 90 days).
- Next year: $14.73, revised **-6.8%** over 30 days.

The 90-day current-quarter revision exceeds +30%, which is normally a strong forward signal. Here it is a commodity-price artifact rather than a durable operational change; the -25.7% next-quarter and -9.0% current-year cuts confirm the market reads the Q1 strength as a Hormuz-spike phenomenon that mean-reverts. The overall characterization is negative near-term earnings momentum on flat volumes. Growth durability is capped by the commodity, not by execution, and the current oil binary makes the forward path two-sided rather than trending.

A structural watch item on the growth/inventory front: the depletion rate has risen from $12.30 to $14.12 to $15.20/Boe across 2024/2025/Q1'26, and proved-developed reserves are declining sequentially. This is the mechanical cost of an acquisition-led model, and it puts a premium on continued disciplined bolt-ons to sustain inventory runway.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$128, CHRD trades at roughly 7.5x forward earnings and offers a high-teens FCF yield on projected 2026 cash generation. Against peers, the picture is nuanced. Peer P/Es range widely (MTDR 13.8x, RRC 10.1x, MGY 15.8x, SM 32.5x, MUR 65.8x) with a peer median of 15.8x. On trailing GAAP earnings CHRD screens as anomalous (negative, distorted by the impairment), so trailing multiples are meaningless here. On forward and normalized earnings, CHRD is clearly cheaper than the peer median, and the discount is defensible given the return-of-capital profile and clean balance sheet.

The valuation dimension does not clear a high score, for two reasons. First, a low multiple in a mature single-basin oil producer with negative near-term estimate revisions is the classic setup where cheapness is the market's assessment, not a mispricing. The multiple is compressed partly because forward earnings are a levered bet on a crude price that consensus expects to soften (the -25.7% next-quarter cut). Second, and more important, the equity carries no asymmetric entry at the current price. The market's own analysts anchor a low target of $129 (essentially the current price), a mean of $165.80, and a high of $209. The absence of any target below the current price is supportive, but the low end sitting right at spot indicates limited downside cushion from the sell-side floor. A verified Hormuz reopening, the most probable de-escalation outcome per the current oil scenario set, pulls crude toward $85-95, which compresses realizations and forward earnings and would widen the effective forward multiple.

The buyback provides a genuine valuation backstop: management retiring stock at depressed prices with $881M of authorization is accretive to per-share metrics and signals the board sees value. But the buyback economics are most attractive in the $90s-low-$100s where the shares traded for much of the past year, not at $128. We would become materially more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$100s, where the FCF yield expands and the buyback support turns the risk/reward asymmetric. Base fair value sits near the analyst mean in the mid-$160s under a stable-to-firm crude deck; the bear case in a verified-reopening scenario clusters in the low-$100s.

## Competitive Landscape

Chord's moat is scale and contiguity in a mature basin. Being the largest acreage holder in the Williston enables long-lateral economics (3-4 mile laterals), multi-bench pad development, shared infrastructure, and in-house marketing, advantages that compound as the company rolls up adjacent acreage. The XTO bolt-on added contiguous acreage with no goodwill recorded (clean fair-value allocation), and 89% of proved reserves are operated, giving Chord control over pace and capital.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving: cost structure is competitive (LOE under $10/Boe), and the consolidator role gives Chord a structural edge in a basin where the marginal acreage seller is increasingly a private operator or a major shedding non-core assets. The limiting factor is that the Williston is lower-growth and more mature than the Permian, and depletion is climbing, so the moat sustains cash returns rather than driving share gains. Against the world's oil-scenario backdrop, Chord's fully unhedged-to-lightly-hedged exposure means its competitive economics track the crude cycle directly rather than a differentiated cost or resource advantage that would insulate it from price. That places the competitive position as solid but not widening in a way the market is mispricing.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity price leverage and impairment risk.** With goodwill zeroed, the next impairment domino is the $10.6B net carrying value of proved oil & gas properties. Management states no impairment is "reasonably likely" at current prices, but a 10% cut in the SEC price would reduce PV-10 by $1.8B and reserves by ~30 MMBoe. The 2025 goodwill charge demonstrated the model's sensitivity to price and share-price declines.

**Single-basin, single-commodity concentration.** ~100% Williston, 56% oil. There is no geographic diversification to cushion a Bakken-specific differential blowout or takeaway disruption. Differentials were a manageable -$2.02/bbl in 2025, but the explicitly flagged **Dakota Access Pipeline (DAPL) shutdown risk** would widen them materially if it materialized.

**Two-sided Hormuz binary.** The current oil scenario set is genuinely two-sided: a verified, held reopening pulls crude toward $85-95 (compressing realizations and forward earnings), while a decisive failure spikes it toward $150-160 (boosting near-term cash but raising the risk of demand destruction and differential/takeaway stress). The most probable outcome is a $100-120 grind. Chord's earnings power is a direct function of which tail resolves.

**Acquisition integration and M&A appetite.** Two large deals in 18 months. Integration/synergy realization and the temptation to overpay in further consolidation are live risks, though management now signals "more disciplined M&A ahead."

**Rising depletion / inventory runway.** Proved-developed reserves are declining sequentially against a climbing depletion rate, a signal to monitor on inventory longevity.

**Minor controls flag.** The Q1 2026 $41.8M out-of-period tax correction on the Enerplus-era impairment provision is immaterial by management's assessment but is a reminder the goodwill/tax accounting has had at least one estimation error.

Overall, the idiosyncratic risks (concentration, DAPL, depletion, the commodity binary) are meaningful and only partly priced into the compressed multiple, warranting a middling risk score.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly constructive and does not confirm a bearish view. Near-term ATM IV is 44.4% versus 38.4% one-year historical vol, rich by 6 points, in the 72nd percentile of the current chain, pricing an implied move of ±10.9% into the next monthly expiry. The put/call skew is -3.1% (call-heavy) and the put/call volume ratio is a strikingly low 0.04, indicating no meaningful put demand or downside hedging. The term structure is flat (44.4% near vs. 44.8% far), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. The elevated IV reflects the two-sided oil binary rather than a directional bearish tilt; the call-heavy skew is consistent with participants positioning for the upside tail of an oil spike. For a prospective buyer, the richness of IV argues against paying up for calls here and favors waiting for a price pullback.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Chord is a fortress-balance-sheet, low-cost Williston consolidator generating >$1.5B of FCF in 2026 at a high-teens yield, with ~0.4x net leverage and $881M of buyback authorization retiring stock at attractive prices. If crude grinds in the $100-120 range or a Hormuz failure spikes it higher, realizations expand, the 2026 FCF projection proves conservative, and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median of ~15.8x forward. Consensus targets cluster in the mid-$160s (mean $165.80) to $209 high, implying ~30-60% upside. The buyback compounds per-share value at any price the market offers, and management's pivot from variable dividends to repurchases signals board-level conviction the equity is undervalued. Bull case: **$200-210**.

### Bear Case
A verified, held Hormuz reopening — the current base-case de-escalation path — pulls crude toward $85-95, compresses realizations, and drives the near-term estimate cuts deeper. Single-basin concentration offers no cushion; a DAPL disruption would widen differentials; rising depletion erodes inventory runway and pressures returns per barrel. At $85-90 WTI, FCF contracts and the market re-rates the equity back toward the low-$100s where it traded for most of the past year. The proved-property carrying value ($10.6B) becomes an impairment watch if the SEC price deck falls. Bear case: **~$100-105** (roughly -20% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive on the FCF-compounding-plus-buyback machinery, but the current price offers no asymmetric entry and forward estimates are being cut near-term. This is a name to accumulate on weakness rather than chase at $128.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward the low-$100s (roughly $105-115), where the FCF yield expands above ~20% and the buyback support turns the risk/reward asymmetric.
- A verified, sustained oil regime settling in the $100-120 grind band that stabilizes forward estimates, combined with a turn in the current-year revision trend back to positive.
- Evidence of accretive, disciplined bolt-on M&A at attractive multiples that extends inventory runway without leverage expansion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified Hormuz reopening pushing WTI decisively below $80 while management leans into further large-scale, higher-multiple M&A that stresses the balance sheet.
- A proved-property impairment or a DAPL shutdown event that materially widens Bakken differentials.
- Depletion rate continuing to climb toward the high-$16s/Boe with proved-developed reserves declining and no offsetting bolt-on activity, a genuine inventory-runway problem.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the associated oil-guidance update, plus any resolution of the Hormuz binary (72+ hours of sustained, verified commercial transit through the strait). The next borrowing-base redetermination and any 8-K M&A activity are secondary checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Chord Energy is a high-quality, low-cost Williston consolidator with a strong cash engine and a fortress balance sheet, obscured by a headline GAAP earnings collapse that is 92% non-cash. The 2025 impairment and DD&A step-up are bookkeeping, not operations; operating cash flow barely moved and Q1 2026 confirmed the recovery. The equity is a legitimate return-of-capital value play at ~7.5x forward earnings with a high-teens FCF yield and an active buyback.

What prevents a BUY today is the entry point and the commodity dependence. At $128 the shares sit near the middle of the 52-week range with the sell-side low target essentially at spot, near-term estimates are being cut, and the entire forward earnings profile is a levered bet on an oil price the market expects to soften if de-escalation holds. The buyback and balance sheet cap the downside, but the asymmetry that would justify a BUY sits in the low-$100s. We rate CHRD a **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale, and would move to a BUY on a pullback toward $105-115 or a durable firming of the crude regime and forward estimates.
