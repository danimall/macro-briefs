## Executive Summary

Churchill Downs Inc. operates at the intersection of gaming, horse racing, and entertainment with a differentiated business model centered on Historical Racing Machines (HRMs), a regulatory arbitrage that delivers casino-like economics under pari-mutuel classification. The company generates $1.2B in Adjusted EBITDA on $2.93B in revenue, owns irreplaceable brand assets (Kentucky Derby, soon Preakness Stakes), and is investing aggressively in growth projects (Victory Run, Rockingham Grand). At $85.76 and ~15.6x forward earnings, the stock sits 28% below its 52-week high of $118.45.

The bull case is straightforward: a high-quality, asset-light-at-the-margin growth business temporarily mispriced due to macro headwinds affecting consumer discretionary names and legitimate refinancing concerns around $1.3B of debt maturing in 2027-2028. The bear case centers on that leverage ($5.15B total debt, 6.4x D/E), the legal fragility of the HRM classification (Louisiana's unconstitutionality ruling provides precedent), and a softening consumer environment with gasoline at elevated levels reducing discretionary gaming spend.

The 12-analyst consensus is unanimously bullish (3 Strong Buy, 9 Buy, 0 Hold/Sell) with a median target of $139, implying 62% upside. While this unanimity warrants skepticism, the fundamental case at current prices — 15.6x forward P/E for a business growing EBITDA mid-single-digits with multiple capital projects in the pipeline — is reasonable. The refinancing overhang and consumer spending uncertainty prevent a more aggressive stance.

## Company Overview

Churchill Downs has transformed over the past decade from a horse racing company into a diversified gaming operator generating nearly $3B in annual revenue across three segments:

- **Live and Historical Racing** (49% of revenue, 53% of segment EBITDA): The Kentucky Derby, 10,190 HRMs across 17+ venues in Kentucky, Virginia, and New Hampshire, and traditional racing operations.
- **Gaming** (36% of revenue, 37% of EBITDA): Ten casino properties across eight states plus equity investments in Rivers Casino Des Plaines (61.3%) and Miami Valley Gaming (50%).
- **Wagering Services and Solutions** (18% of revenue, 14% of EBITDA): TwinSpires online wagering platform, Exacta Systems (HRM technology), and retail sports betting.

The HRM business is the primary growth engine. These devices function like slot machines but are classified as pari-mutuel wagering on previously-run horse races, allowing CDI to operate in states that permit horse racing wagering but restrict traditional casino gaming.

## Growth Analysis

CDI's growth comes from three vectors:

1. **HRM Expansion**: Marshall Yards opened February 2026 (225 HRMs). Rockingham Grand Casino (825 HRMs, 32 table games) opens mid-2027 in Salem, NH — a greenfield in an underserved Northeast market. Virginia operations continue to ramp.

2. **Premium Product Investment**: Victory Run at Churchill Downs ($280-300M) adds premium suites and box seating, expected to significantly increase per-attendee revenue starting with the 2028 Derby.

3. **Preakness Acquisition**: $85M for the second leg of the Triple Crown creates unique media rights optionality with NBC's contract expiring after 2026, plus immediate $5.8M+ annual licensing income (~7% yield on investment).

Analyst estimates project $6.87 EPS for FY2026 and $7.30 for FY2027, representing 29% and 6.3% growth respectively. Current-year estimates have been revised up +3.2% in the past 30 days; next-year estimates have been revised down -7.8% over 90 days. The divergence likely reflects confidence in near-term execution but uncertainty about the gaming segment trajectory and higher interest costs post-refinancing.

The Gaming segment ($483M EBITDA) declined 4.7% in FY2025 and remains under pressure from increased competition (Rivers Des Plaines), lost markets (Louisiana HRM shutdown), and regional softness. This segment is a drag on group growth.

## Competitive Landscape

CDI occupies a unique competitive position that no peer replicates:

**Moat Components:**
- **Kentucky Derby**: Irreplaceable 150+ year brand asset. Cannot be built or acquired.
- **Preakness Stakes IP (pending)**: Unites first two legs of Triple Crown, creating strong media rights negotiating leverage.
- **HRM regulatory classification**: First-mover advantage in states that have legalized HRMs. CDI controls the technology through Exacta Systems, creating a vertically integrated position.
- **Vertical integration**: Content creation (live racing) → distribution (TwinSpires, simulcasting) → venue operations (HRM casinos).

**Competitive Threats:**
- iGaming legalization could cannibalize HRM revenue in overlap states (Maine already authorized)
- Louisiana Supreme Court ruling creates precedent for HRM legal challenges in other states
- Rivers Des Plaines facing increased competition from other Illinois casinos
- Traditional casino portfolio faces secular headwinds from regional gaming saturation

**Trajectory**: The competitive position is stable-to-improving on the HRM expansion front but faces emerging structural risk from iGaming and legal challenges. The Preakness acquisition strengthens the media/brand moat.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Leverage and Refinancing (CRITICAL)**
$5.15B in total debt with $1.3B maturing in 2027-2028. Refinancing will likely cost 6.5-7.5% for gaming credits, adding $20-40M in annual interest expense and reducing EPS by $0.20-0.40.

**2. HRM Legal Fragility (HIGH)**
Louisiana's unconstitutionality ruling demonstrates that the HRM regulatory arbitrage can be challenged. CDI's Live & Historical Racing segment generates $637M in EBITDA primarily from HRMs. A Virginia or Kentucky court ruling against HRMs would be existential for the growth thesis.

**3. Consumer Spending Sensitivity (MODERATE-HIGH)**
Regional gaming operations face volume pressure in a softening consumer environment. The Gaming segment is already declining. HRM venues in Virginia and Kentucky serve a middle-market consumer who is sensitive to fuel costs.

**4. Execution Risk on Capital Projects ($560-585M committed)**
Victory Run, Rockingham Grand, and Preakness acquisition total over $550M in committed capital while the company simultaneously manages upcoming maturities. Any construction delay or cost overrun would strain an already-leveraged balance sheet.

**5. Maryland Preakness Acquisition Risk**
Maryland is "seriously exploring" acquiring the Preakness brand itself for $85M — potentially competing with CDI's deal. If Maryland outbids or blocks the deal, CDI loses the Triple Crown consolidation thesis.

## Conclusion

Churchill Downs is a high-quality, asset-rich franchise temporarily available at a discount to intrinsic value and peer multiples. The stock prices in legitimate concerns about leverage and consumer spending but undervalues the unique asset portfolio and free cash flow generation.

The declining score trajectory (6.4 → 6.2 → 6.12) reflects the macro environment's increasing hostility to levered consumer discretionary names, not fundamental deterioration — Q1 2026 was a record quarter with EBITDA up 5% and current-year estimates have been revised upward. The options market's put-heavy positioning creates near-term headwind risk.

This is a growth-oriented HOLD. The assets are excellent and the valuation is reasonable, but the leverage overhang in a rising-rate environment combined with consumer headwinds prevents a higher-conviction call. The stock needs either a cheaper entry point or a demonstrated refinancing path to warrant aggressive positioning.
