## Executive Summary

Archrock Inc is the largest provider of outsourced natural gas compression services in the United States, operating 4.8 million available horsepower across 5,319 compression units. The company has executed a growth strategy through two acquisitions (TOPS for $869M in September 2024, NGCS for $349M in May 2025) and sustained organic pricing gains, tripling net income from $44M in 2022 to $322M in 2025. Operating at 95% fleet utilization with Caterpillar equipment lead times stretched to 160 weeks, Archrock possesses genuine pricing power that competitors cannot easily replicate.

The stock has appreciated 52% over the trailing twelve months and 43% year-to-date, reaching all-time highs near $40 in early May 2026 before pulling back to $37.25. At 20.3x forward earnings with consensus expecting ~15% EPS growth in FY2027, the valuation is reasonable but not compelling for a new position. The Q1 2026 earnings miss (EPS $0.41 vs. $0.47 consensus) and concentrated CFO selling of $10.9M in shares near highs introduce near-term uncertainty, though full-year guidance was reaffirmed and the miss was driven by one-time SG&A items rather than operational deterioration.

The specific concern preventing a BUY recommendation at this level: AROC trades at 20.3x forward P/E on $1.83 trailing EPS, with consensus FY2026 EPS at $2.03 (implying 18.3x current-year earnings). Against the peer median of 58.4x, this appears cheap — but peers KGS and NOV have dramatically different business profiles and their elevated multiples reflect trough earnings rather than comparable franchise value. The more relevant comparison is to WFRD at 17.2x, making AROC's premium modest but not discounted. With 2% upside to the analyst consensus low target ($38) and only 24% to the high ($46), the risk/reward from $37.25 is symmetric rather than skewed to the upside. A pullback to the $32-34 range would provide asymmetric entry.

## Company Overview

Archrock operates as a fee-based infrastructure company that designs, owns, installs, operates, and maintains natural gas compression equipment for midstream companies and E&P producers. Compression is essential to move gas through gathering systems, processing plants, and transmission pipelines, making this a "must-run" service that generates revenue regardless of commodity prices.

**Revenue composition:**
- Contract Operations: 85% of revenue (~$1.32-1.36B guided for FY2026)
- Aftermarket Services: 15% of revenue (~$200-220M guided for FY2026)

**Geographic concentration:** ~75% of operating horsepower deployed in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford formations. Operations span 41 states with ~300 customers, no single customer exceeding 10% of revenue.

**Key structural advantage:** The 160-week (3+ year) Caterpillar engine lead time effectively freezes new fleet supply growth across the industry, creating a pricing environment where existing fleets appreciate in value and utilization remains elevated.

## Growth Analysis

Archrock's growth is driven by three concurrent forces:

**1. Secular demand for natural gas compression:**
- U.S. natural gas production at 107.4 Bcf/d in 2025, forecast to grow 1%/year through 2027
- LNG exports growing 9% in 2026, 10% in 2027 (EIA forecasts)
- Pipeline exports to Mexico increasing
- AI data center power demand explicitly cited by management as emerging catalyst
- Declining reservoir pressures in maturing wells require progressively more compression over time

**2. Supply-constrained pricing power:**
- 95% fleet utilization (near all-time highs)
- 160-week Caterpillar lead times prevent meaningful new supply
- Contract Operations revenue per HP increasing both sequentially and YoY
- This dynamic is likely to persist for 2-3 more years given current order backlogs

**3. Acquisition-driven scale:**
- TOPS ($869M, Sept 2024) and NGCS ($349M, May 2025) added substantial fleet
- Acquisitions are inherently value-accretive given the inability to buy new equipment for 3+ years
- Integration appears smooth; margins expanding post-close

**FY2026 Guidance:** EBITDA of $865-915M represents a digestion year after two major acquisitions relative to FY2025's $901M. FY2027 consensus EPS of $2.34 implies 15% growth from FY2026's $2.03.

**Growth capex allocation:** $250-275M in growth capex for FY2026, implying continued fleet expansion despite lead time constraints (likely ordering far in advance and deploying recently-ordered units).

## Competitive Landscape

Archrock's competitive position is strong and improving:

**Scale dominance:** Largest large-HP compression fleet in the U.S. Competitors include USA Compression Partners (USAC) and Kodiak Gas Services (KGS, which absorbed CSI Compressco). The TOPS and NGCS acquisitions have widened the fleet gap.

**Supply constraints as moat:** While the 10-K acknowledges "highly competitive with low barriers to entry," the 160-week Caterpillar lead time creates an enormous practical barrier. The theoretical cost to replicate Archrock's fleet exceeds $2.4B even if equipment were available — which it isn't for 3+ years.

**Pricing trajectory:** Contract Operations adjusted gross margin expansion from 67% to 73% in a single year demonstrates that Archrock is exercising pricing power without sacrificing utilization. This is consistent with a supply-constrained market where the incumbent has leverage.

**Customer stickiness:** Average equipment deployment duration of ~6 years, combined with switching costs (physical relocation, downtime, operator training) creates substantial customer retention. The 30-day cancellation clause after initial terms is a theoretical risk but rarely exercised given the difficulty of sourcing replacement equipment.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Oil/Gas Price Sensitivity (Moderate):**
While Archrock's fee-based model provides partial insulation, a severe decline in oil/gas prices would reduce drilling activity, slow new compression demand, and eventually pressure utilization. The current Hormuz uncertainty cuts both ways — elevated oil prices support E&P activity and compression demand, but a rapid resolution could reduce the urgency of production growth.

**2. Leverage at Scale (Moderate):**
$2.4B in debt is manageable at 2.6x EBITDA with no near-term maturities, but creates amplified downside if utilization drops below 90%. Interest expense of $165M/year represents a fixed cost burden that constrains cash flow flexibility in a downturn.

**3. Q1 Miss Creates Credibility Overhang (Near-term):**
The 13.4% Q1 EPS miss creates a "show me" dynamic for Q2 results. Consensus must be rebuilt. The stock's 8% pullback from highs reflects this uncertainty.

**4. CFO Insider Selling (Monitoring):**
CFO Doug Aron sold ~295,000 shares for $10.9M in March-May 2026 near all-time highs. While this follows large equity grants (90,232 shares in Feb 2026) and represents partial diversification rather than liquidation, the magnitude is notable. Combined with SVP selling, this suggests management views current prices as fair-to-full rather than cheap.

**5. Tariff/Input Cost Inflation (Emerging):**
Steel, lube oil, and imported parts cost increases may pressure margins with a lag, as fixed monthly fee contracts create pass-through delays.

**6. Geographic Concentration (Structural):**
75% of horsepower in Permian/Eagle Ford creates basin-specific risk from pipeline constraints, regulatory changes, or localized production declines.

## Conclusion

Archrock occupies an enviable competitive position: the largest fleet of essential infrastructure equipment in a supply-constrained market, generating 95% utilization and expanding margins while secular demand grows from LNG, AI power, and declining reservoir pressures. The business quality is high and improving.

The stock's valuation, however, reflects this quality at $37.25. At 20.3x forward earnings with 2% upside to the analyst consensus floor, the margin of safety for a new position is insufficient. The Q1 earnings miss, CFO selling near highs, and broader uncertainty around oil prices from potential Hormuz de-escalation create a reasonable probability of a pullback to the $32-34 range where the risk/reward becomes more compelling. Existing holders should maintain positions for the secular growth thesis and 2.4% yield; new capital should wait for a better entry.
