**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Option Care Health is the largest independent provider of home and alternate-site infusion services in the United States, running roughly 198 locations across all 50 states. The investment case turns almost entirely on a single event: on April 30, 2026, the stock fell about 24-25% in one session after management cut full-year revenue guidance from $5.8-6.0B to $5.675-5.775B and disclosed that the gross-profit headwind from chronic inflammatory disease (CID) dynamics (IRA price negotiation on Stelara plus biosimilar conversion plus patient attrition) had grown from a $25-35M estimate at Q4 (late February) to roughly $55M roughly 65 days later. The shares now trade near $21, down about 34% over the trailing twelve months and near the $18.01 fifty-two-week low.

The cleanest read on this file is that two things are true simultaneously. First, the balance sheet is sound (Altman Z-Score 3.02, FCF positive, well-laddered debt recently refinanced at a lower spread, revolver upsized to $850M), and the valuation has reset to roughly 11x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS and ~7x adjusted EBITDA, genuinely cheap versus the company's own history. Insiders reinforced this: the CEO, CFO, and two directors made open-market purchases in early May at ~$20-21, the strongest contrarian signal in the file. Second, profitability has been eroding for two full years: net income peaked at $267M in 2023 and fell each year since to $208M in 2025 despite revenue growing ~31% over the same span. The April event accelerated a deterioration that was already underway, and the speed of the guidance reversal has drawn four securities-fraud investigations.

The unresolved analytical question is whether CID attrition is the one-time "reset" management describes or the leading edge of structural margin erosion driven by IRA negotiation and biosimilar economics. Until the Q2 and Q3 census data resolve that, the asymmetry is not clear enough to underwrite either an active long or a short. We rate OPCH HOLD, with specific upgrade and downgrade triggers tied to the next two prints.

## Company Overview

Option Care Health contracts with managed-care organizations, payers, hospitals, and physicians to deliver infused pharmaceuticals and complex compounded solutions in patients' homes and in ambulatory infusion suites. Per the Q1 2026 10-Q, the network comprises 87 full-service pharmacies (73 with ambulatory infusion suites) and 111 stand-alone ambulatory infusion suites, with over 8,000 team members including more than 5,000 clinicians. It reports as a single segment (infusion services).

The portfolio splits into acute therapies (anti-infectives, nutrition support: shorter duration, referral-driven, lower drug cost) and chronic therapies (immunoglobulin, neurological disorders, and chronic inflammatory disease including the Stelara franchise, plus rare/orphan drugs: higher revenue per patient but increasingly mix-pressured). The structural tailwind is the long-running site-of-care shift from hospital to home, supported by aging demographics and an expanding biologic pipeline. The structural tension is that the same specialty-drug launches that drive volume eventually flip to biosimilars and price negotiation, which compress economics. Stelara is the textbook case: it faces a 66% IRA list-price cut effective January 2026 and biosimilar conversion at the same time.

OPCH's principal competitive distinction is that it is independent, not owned by a PBM, insurer, distributor, or retail pharmacy chain. That independence is a double-edged attribute. It positions OPCH as a neutral partner to health systems and payers, but it also means vertically integrated competitors hold drug-cost advantages OPCH cannot fully match.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has grown steadily: $3.94B (2022) → $4.30B (2023) → $5.00B (2024) → $5.65B (2025), a 13.0% increase in the most recent year, helped by mid-teens acute growth that benefited from competitor disruption diverting volume to OPCH.

The central financial concern is margin compression, and it predates the April event. Gross margin fell from 20.3% in 2024 to 19.3% in 2025 and was 19.4% in Q1 2026. Operating margin slipped from 6.4% to 6.0%; net margin from 4.2% to 3.7%. The mechanism is therapy mix (higher-cost rare/orphan drugs in the chronic book) and biosimilar adoption, which carries lower per-unit economics. The consequence shows up cleanly in the net-income line, which declined for two consecutive years ($267M in 2023 → $212M in 2024 → $208M in 2025) even as revenue rose ~31% over the same period. A business growing the top line at low-double-digits while net income falls is converting revenue into progressively less profit, which is the fundamental issue beneath the April headline.

Cash flow remains positive but softened. 2025 operating cash flow was $258M (down from $323M in 2024, partly from strategic inventory builds), with free cash flow of $217M (3.8% FCF margin). The balance sheet is sound: Altman Z-Score 3.02 (safe zone), positive net income, OCF above net income, current ratio 1.53. Total debt is ~$1.18B: a $674.6M First Lien Term Loan (SOFR+1.75%, due 2032) and $500M of 4.375% Senior Notes (due 2029), with D/E ~1.6x. Maturities are well-laddered, and the September 2025 Fourth Amendment cut the term-loan margin from SOFR+2.25% to +1.75%. In March 2026 OPCH expanded its revolver from $400M to $850M, materially increasing liquidity. The one balance-sheet flag is goodwill of $1.61B, roughly 47% of assets, which creates impairment exposure if cash flows deteriorate further.

Q1 2026 itself was a deceleration, not a collapse: revenue $1.35B (+1.3% YoY, down sharply from +13% in 2025), GAAP net income $45.3M (-3.0%), adjusted EBITDA $104.8M (-6.3%), GAAP diluted EPS $0.29, adjusted diluted EPS $0.40 (flat YoY). Operating cash flow was negative $12.1M on seasonal working-capital and incentive-comp timing. The earnings surprise record remains decent (three beats and one miss over four quarters, most recently a 5.5% beat in March 2026), but that record is now less reliable as a forward guide given the guidance credibility damage.

Capital allocation has been disciplined and defensively timed. Share count fell from ~181M (2022) to ~157M (Q1 2026); the company repurchased $310M in 2025 and $253M in 2024, and the board doubled the authorization to $1.0B in January 2026 (~$675M remaining at Q1). OPCH then cut buybacks sharply in Q1 2026 ($17.5M versus $100M in Q1 2025) while upsizing the revolver, both consistent with management bracing for a tougher year and preserving optionality. The ~$675M remaining authorization deployed near $20-22 would be meaningfully accretive.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture has deteriorated, and the estimate-revision data confirms it rather than contradicting it. Consensus next-year EPS has been revised down 3.7% over 90 days, and current-quarter and next-quarter revisions are slightly negative over 30 days (-0.7% each). The longer-dated current-year and 90-day prints remain marginally positive (+3.5% current year, +8.0% next quarter on a 90-day basis), but the trend since the April cut points down. This is the opposite of the +30% upward revision velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection, and there is no inflection BUY signal present here. Revenue growth decelerated from +13% in 2025 to +1.3% in Q1 2026, and FY2026 guidance implies low-single-digit full-year growth at the midpoint.

The growth question decomposes into a fixable operational component and a secular pricing component. CID census volatility is partly operational: chronic patients require annual benefit reverification and plan-design resets, which in early 2026 produced higher-than-expected attrition. To the extent that attrition reflects execution and one-time plan resets, it can recover. The secular component, IRA price negotiation (the 66% Stelara list cut) and biosimilar conversion, is permanent and recurring as additional drugs enter negotiation rounds. Distinguishing the two is the central analytical question, and it cannot be resolved with the data in hand. Acute therapy growth remains a genuine bright spot, having grown mid-teens in 2025 on share gains from competitor disruption, but acute carries lower revenue per patient and does not offset chronic mix pressure on the margin line.

We score growth prospects at 4.5. The top line is still growing, but profit growth has been negative for two years and the forward guide implies continued margin pressure with no near-term catalyst to reverse it.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$21 with ~157M shares (~$3.3B market cap) and FY2026 guidance of $1.82-1.92 adjusted EPS, the stock trades at roughly 11x guided adjusted EPS, ~10.2x forward (provider) earnings on the consensus blend, and ~7x adjusted EBITDA. That is cheap versus the company's own history and reflects a credibility discount plus litigation overhang rather than a balance-sheet problem.

The peer set provided (BBIO, a development-stage biopharma with negative earnings) is not a meaningful comparable, so the valuation case rests on absolute metrics and the company's own trajectory. Three things temper the "cheap" read. First, the cheapness reflects a genuine reduction in earnings quality: margins have compressed for two years, so a lower multiple on lower-quality earnings is partly justified, not purely a sentiment discount. Second, analyst targets have already reset down ~28% (mean ~$29 from ~$41), and Goldman initiated at Neutral with a $22 target in June 2026, essentially calling the stock fairly valued post-reset. With the shares at $21 and the lowest target at $22, the consensus is signaling limited downside but also limited identified upside; the targets here behave more like a fair-value band than a floor, because the inflection conditions that would make targets stale (rising estimates, accelerating growth) are absent.

Third, the asymmetry is real but contingent. If CID stabilizes and the headwind proves one-time, the company earns roughly $1.85-1.92 adjusted in 2026 with a path back toward $2.00+ in 2027, and an 11x multiple is too low for a scale leader generating positive FCF; a re-rating to 13-14x would imply $24-27. If the headwind is structural and 2027 estimates continue to drift lower, the multiple is appropriate or generous. The valuation score of 6.5 reflects a cheap absolute multiple offset by deteriorating earnings quality and the absence of an identified catalyst to close the gap on a defined timeline.

## Competitive Landscape

OPCH is the scale leader in independent home and alternate-site infusion, a structurally growing niche. Its 2025 acute strength was partly a share gain from competitor disruption, and its national footprint, health-system JVs, and payer relationships constitute a durable but not impregnable moat. The trajectory of that moat is the relevant question. On the acute side, the company is gaining share. On the chronic/CID side, the combination of biosimilar economics and IRA pricing is compressing the margin profile of its highest-revenue-per-patient business, and vertically integrated competitors (PBM-, insurer-, and distributor-owned infusion arms) hold drug-cost advantages OPCH cannot match. Independence is a competitive asset in payer/health-system partnerships and a cost-structure liability in drug procurement.

On balance, competitive position is stable-to-modestly-eroding: leadership and scale intact, share gains in acute, but a structural cost disadvantage versus integrated rivals in exactly the therapy category facing the most pricing pressure. We score it 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk that already materialized is the most instructive. Management reaffirmed guidance in late February, then cut it ~65 days later when the CID gross-profit headwind grew to ~$55M. This prompted securities-fraud investigations from at least four plaintiff firms (Kirby McInerney, Levi & Korsinsky, Rosen, Glancy Prongay). No lawsuit has been filed, but the headline and litigation overhang is real, and the Q1 10-Q's statement of "no material changes to risk factors" is precisely what the plaintiff firms are probing.

The forward risks, in order of materiality:
1. **CID/biosimilar/IRA structural pressure.** The Stelara IRA list-price cut is permanent and recurring, and biosimilar conversion compresses economics on the same franchise. The swing variable for 2026-2027 is the size and stickiness of the CID headwind.
2. **Guidance credibility.** Having missed Q4 (reported $0.37 vs ~$0.46 expected on Feb 24) and reversed guidance within 65 days, management's forward estimates now carry a lower confidence weight than the historical beat record would suggest. A new VP of Investor Relations was appointed in June 2026 to rebuild the function.
3. **Payer concentration.** 88% of revenue from MCOs/commercial/PBMs, largest single payer ~14%; periodic competitive bidding can compress price.
4. **Goodwill impairment.** $1.61B (47% of assets) is exposed if cash flows deteriorate further.
5. **Floating-rate debt.** ~$675M of term debt is floating, partially hedged by a $300M interest-rate cap expiring late 2026, a modest headwind in a higher-for-longer rate environment.
6. **Supplier concentration** (~67-68% of purchases from four vendors, with newly flagged tariff exposure) and a legacy BioScrip escheatment liability.

We score risk factors at 4.5. The combination of a structural pricing headwind whose magnitude is still uncertain, active securities investigations, and damaged guidance credibility represents specific, not generic, downside risk that is only partly priced.

### Options Market Signal

No listed options sentiment data was provided, but short interest is informative: 11.0% of float short with 2.5 days to cover. This is elevated bearish positioning, consistent with institutional skepticism about whether the CID reset is one-time, but the low days-to-cover means squeeze risk is limited and the short thesis is not crowded enough to mechanically lift the stock on good news. The short interest reinforces that the bear case is well-represented and that any genuine census stabilization in Q2-Q3 would need to surprise that positioning to drive a re-rating.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$27-31).** CID attrition was a one-time reset tied to early-2026 plan reverification, the $55M headwind does not recur or grow, acute share gains continue, and 2026 lands at the upper end of guidance (~$1.92 adjusted EPS) with a clear path toward $2.00+ in 2027. Management's open-market buying at $20-21 proves prescient, the ~$675M buyback authorization is deployed accretively at depressed prices, and the multiple re-rates from ~11x to 13-14x as credibility is rebuilt. The securities investigations resolve without a material filing. This implies $27-31.

**Bear Case (~$16-18).** The CID headwind is structural rather than one-time, biosimilar and IRA pressure broadens to additional drugs in subsequent negotiation rounds, 2027 estimates continue to drift down, and a goodwill impairment becomes a live question if FCF erodes. A securities lawsuit is filed and adds a settlement liability and management distraction. The multiple holds at ~9-10x on lower forward earnings, implying $16-18, roughly 14-24% below the current price.

The base case sits between these. The most likely outcome is that 2026 lands in the guided range, the CID census partially stabilizes but margin recovery is gradual, and the stock grinds in a $20-25 band until the Q2 and Q3 prints resolve the structural-versus-cyclical debate. Insider buying and the cheap absolute multiple provide a reasonable floor; the absence of a near-term catalyst to lift the multiple caps the upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 2026 earnings report (expected late July/early August) is the first critical checkpoint, followed by Q3 (early November). These two prints will show whether CID census is stabilizing and whether the $55M headwind holds or grows.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CID census stabilization confirmed for two consecutive quarters, with gross margin holding at or above ~19.4% and the CID gross-profit headwind not exceeding the ~$55M reset figure.
- Positive next-year EPS estimate revisions resuming (next-year consensus revised up rather than the current -3.7%/90d), signaling the margin erosion has bottomed.
- Securities investigations resolving without a material filing, removing the litigation overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The CID gross-profit headwind expanding beyond ~$55M on the Q2 or Q3 call, or a further full-year guidance cut, confirming structural rather than one-time erosion.
- A goodwill impairment charge, or FCF margin falling below ~2%.
- A filed securities lawsuit with quantified exposure, or further C-suite departures beyond routine succession.

**Reassessment point:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August 2026).

## Conclusion

OPCH presents a genuine two-sided setup. The balance sheet is sound, the valuation has reset to a cheap ~11x adjusted EPS and ~7x EBITDA, and insiders, including the CEO and CFO, bought stock in the open market at the lows, the most credible contrarian signal in the file. Against that, net income has fallen for two consecutive years despite ~31% cumulative revenue growth, the forward estimate trend is negative, the IRA/biosimilar pressure on Stelara is structural and recurring, and the speed of the guidance reversal has drawn four securities-fraud investigations and damaged management's forward credibility. The decisive question, whether the CID census deterioration is a one-time reset or the start of structural erosion, cannot be answered with the data currently available and will be resolved by the Q2 and Q3 prints.

That genuine uncertainty, combined with a valuation that consensus targets and Goldman's $22 Neutral peg as roughly fair post-reset, argues against both an active long and a short. The risk/reward to the bear case (~14-24% downside) is not asymmetric enough versus the bull case to justify positioning ahead of the census data. We rate OPCH **HOLD** (uncertain), and would revisit the call after Q2 2026 earnings clarify whether margin erosion has bottomed.
