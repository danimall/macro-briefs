## Executive Summary

The single most consequential fact about Tootsie Roll's valuation is a share-count error embedded in most screening data. The Form 10-Q cover page for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 lists 42,981,466 shares of Common Stock (NYSE: TR) and 32,090,182 shares of unlisted Class B Common Stock — 75,071,648 shares in total. Class B converts into Common one-for-one at the holder's option (Note 4 documents 10,000 shares converted in H1 2026), participates equally in the 3% annual stock dividend, and receives the identical $0.09 quarterly cash dividend. The quoted market capitalization of $1.68bn equals 42.98M × $39.23 — it omits 42.7% of the company's economic shares. At $39.74, total equity value is 75,071,648 × $39.74 = **$2,983M**. That converts TR from an apparent 17x-earnings staple into a **29.8x** one.

Against that corrected multiple, the Q2 2026 10-Q is a material negative development that postdates our prior review. Net product sales fell 0.8% to $151.9M. Adjusted operating earnings (excluding the deferred-compensation distortion) fell 37.7% to $14.3M, with adjusted operating margin down 5.6pp to 9.4%; GAAP earnings from operations were **negative $1.6M**. For H1, adjusted operating earnings fell 24.3% and adjusted operating margin fell 3.5pp to 10.7%. The drivers per management's own MD&A are significantly higher cocoa and chocolate unit costs charged immediately to cost of sales under LIFO, freight/delivery/warehousing up 18.2% to 10.2% of sales on fuel surcharges and energy, resin-based packaging inflation, advertising nearly doubled, deliberately increased trade promotions that suppress reported net sales, and declining international operating income.

Two genuine offsets deserve weight. Q3 is historically TR's largest quarter on pre-Halloween sales, and management explicitly attributes part of the Q2 shortfall to sales timing between Q2 and Q3. And management states that cocoa costs, while still above historical levels, have retreated and should flow through as lower supply-chain cost in H2 2026 and into 2027. Those support a margin recovery toward FY2025 levels — they do not create growth. Normalized earnings power on FY2025's adjusted operating margin, $730M of sales and roughly $21M of investment income is approximately **$1.33-$1.40 per share**, essentially where FY2025 landed. Paying 29-30x for stable, non-growing normalized earnings with a 0.9% cash yield, against a peer median of 21.5x and 4.0%, is a negative-expected-return proposition. We move to REDUCE from HOLD.

## Company Overview

Tootsie Roll manufactures and sells candy and reports as a single segment. Note 1 of the 10-Q states the CODM — the CEO — assesses performance on consolidated net income, a figure that includes investment income from a ~$443M bond portfolio.

The brand portfolio is weighted toward sugar confectionery and novelty candy rather than premium chocolate: Tootsie Roll, Tootsie Pops, Charms Blow Pops, Child's Play, Caramel Apple Pops, Charleston Chew, Dubble Bubble, Razzles, Cella's, Dots, Junior Mints, Sugar Daddy, Sugar Babies, Andes, Fluffy Stuff, Nik-L-Nip, Frooties and Tutsi Pop. Chocolate/cocoa exposure concentrates in Andes, Junior Mints, Charleston Chew and the core Tootsie Roll chew.

Manufacturing spans Chicago (headquarters and largest plant, hourly workforce unionized under the Bakery, Confectionery, Tobacco Workers and Grain Millers union), Cambridge MA, Covington TN, Delavan WI, Concord Ontario, Mexico City and Barcelona (97%-owned). Roughly 2,000 employees. Distribution runs through brokers, distributors and wholesalers into mass, dollar/variety, club, drug, convenience, vending, fundraising, military and export channels.

Two non-candy income streams matter. TR is a commercial-property lessor — leased property at cost of $51.2M against $20.7M accumulated depreciation, producing $3.7M of H1 2026 lease income; total rental and royalty revenue was $7.8M in FY2025. And it holds $572.9M of cash plus short- and long-term investments at June 30, 2026, principally investment-grade corporate bonds with a stated three-to-five-year average maturity.

FY2025 revenue remains 5.1% below the 2023 peak. The 2021-2023 surge was price-led inflation recovery; 2024 gave back most of it. Adjusting out trade-promotion accrual releases that flowed through the top line ($2.7M in 2025, $5.665M in 2024) yields FY2025 growth of +1.71% versus +1.28% reported — the accruals did not flatter the growth comparison, though trade-promotion accruals were the auditor's critical audit matter and remain a material judgment area.

### FY2025 earnings growth came from the bond portfolio, not candy

Reported FY2025 EPS of $1.37 (on the then-outstanding share count) was up 16.1%. The decomposition, from MD&A:

- Adjusted operating earnings: $117.29M vs $116.83M = **+0.4%**
- Non-repeat of FY2024's $11.01M deferred-tax-asset write-off: +$11.01M
- AFS investment income: $20.19M vs $9.60M = +$10.59M pre-tax
- Foreign exchange: −$2.22M

The candy business delivered 0.4% operating earnings growth. Product gross margin did genuinely improve (33.08% in 2023 → 34.59% in 2024 → 34.85% in 2025), and the effective tax rate was approximately 27%. A further $3.2M pre-tax of FY2025 income (~2% of EPS) was an out-of-period catch-up on previously unaccreted bond discount, corrected in Q3 2025 — an AOCI-to-net-income reclassification that leaves total comprehensive income and equity unchanged.

An 8-15% decline in adjusted operating earnings, partially cushioned by investment income (AFS income $10.5M in H1 2026 vs $6.9M, +51%), implies FY2026 EPS of roughly **$1.14-$1.20**, or 33-35x the $39.74 price.

### Balance sheet: fortress, correctly measured

At June 30, 2026: cash $62.1M (down from $127.2M at year-end on the seasonal Halloween inventory build of $44.5M plus $41.6M of AFS purchases, not deterioration); restricted cash $0.4M; current investments $79.0M; non-current investments $431.8M; aggregate cash and investments $572.9M. Total debt is **$8.3M** — the $7.5M industrial development bond now current on its June 2027 maturity plus $0.8M of Spanish borrowings at 5.5%. Total liabilities $317.8M; total equity $951.8M.

Of the $572.9M, $130.7M is trading securities held as an economic hedge of deferred-compensation liabilities of up to $135.4M — earmarked, and under-covering the liability by roughly $4.7M. Excluding them: $442.2M of cash and AFS securities less $8.3M debt = **$433.9M net financial assets = $5.78 per share, 14.5% of the current price.**

A common screening artifact is worth correcting: reported debt-to-equity of 1.33 is the equity multiplier (FY2025 total assets $1,254.0M ÷ equity $940.6M = 1.333). Actual total liabilities to equity is 0.333; interest-bearing debt to equity is 0.009. TR carries no refinancing cliff and no covenant risk.

Similarly, the 3% annual stock dividend charges fair value to retained earnings each year — retained earnings fell from $65.5M at 12/31/25 to $3.4M at 3/31/26 on a $73.2M stock dividend, recovering to $9.9M by 6/30/26, with roughly $915M of cumulative earnings sitting in capital in excess of par. This mechanically depresses the retained-earnings term in an Altman Z-Score and mechanically fails the share-count test in a Piotroski F-Score every year regardless of fundamentals. Recomputed Z-Score is approximately 6.8 on the full share count — solvency risk is negligible either way.

### Cash flow and capital allocation

FY2025: operating cash flow $130.6M, capex $34.3M, FCF $96.4M — operating cash flow exceeded net income by 31%, a mark of clean earnings quality. FCF yield on $2,983M equity value is **3.23%**.

Capex is inflecting. The 10-Q discloses a plant expansion at one U.S. facility totaling **$75-85M**, with most building-construction spend concentrated in 2026 and early 2027 and $18.2M incurred to date. H1 2026 capex was $20.6M versus $10.4M. If FY2026 capex lands at $50-60M, FCF compresses toward $70-80M on flat operating cash flow — an FCF yield of **2.3-2.7%**. H1 operating cash flow of −$10.9M should not be annualized; H1 is seasonally negative on the Halloween build.

Capital returned in FY2025 was $26.1M of dividends (26% payout) plus modest buybacks — roughly one-third of FCF. The remainder went into corporate bonds; non-current investments grew from $332.3M to $437.1M. H1 2026 buybacks were $3.8M, all in June, at an average $38.36. There is no formal repurchase program; the board authorizes dollar amounts periodically.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth engine here. Volume is flat, revenue growth has been price-led and has reversed once already, and the company has no presence in the categories driving confectionery growth (better-for-you, premium chocolate, functional gummies). Innovation cadence is minimal, and trade-spend scale is a fraction of Mars/Wrigley, Hershey, Mondelez or Ferrara.

What growth exists is mix and rate. Foreign net product sales grew 25.1% in Q2 while domestic fell 2.5% — but domestic is still 92.2% of sales, so the geographic mix shift is not yet material to consolidated growth. Investment income is a real tailwind, and H1 AFS income rose 51%. But bond coupons are not a franchise, and capitalizing them at 30x is precisely the error this multiple embeds.

The $75-85M plant expansion is the most constructive forward datum in the filings — management is finally committing balance-sheet capacity to capacity rather than to bonds. It is also a program expected to take place over the next seven years, with no disclosed volume or return target, and it compresses near-term FCF.

Cocoa relief is a margin recovery, not a growth event. It restores FY2027 earnings toward FY2025's level. On our normalized build — $730M sales, FY2025-level adjusted operating margin, ~$21M investment income, 27% tax — normalized EPS is approximately **$1.33-$1.40**. There is no path in the disclosed evidence to materially above that within the forecast horizon.

Offsetting the demand picture, the synthetic-dye phase-out is a growth risk specific to TR. The 10-Q carries an expanded risk factor: the FDA intends to phase out certain synthetic dyes "which the Company uses in many of its products," several states have passed restrictions, and TR states natural colors "will likely result in higher costs" with potentially inadequate supply. TR's growth-relevant SKUs — Tootsie Pops, Dots, Razzles, Blow Pops, Fluffy Stuff, Frooties — are brightly colored novelty candy where color is the product identity. Reformulation risks both cost and consumer rejection.

Note that the data feed's own EPS of $1.33 is computed on the full 75.07M share count while its share count and market cap are listed-Common only; the quoted trailing P/E of 31.28 implies an EPS of $1.271 and reconciles to neither. The internally consistent figure from primary filings is **29.8x** trailing.

Stripping the balance sheet does not rescue the multiple. Ex-cash price is $39.74 − $5.78 = **$33.96**. FY2025 after-tax adjusted operating earnings are $117.29M × 0.73 = $85.6M = $1.14 per share. That is **29.8x** for the candy-plus-real-estate business ex-cash. On the FY2026 sensitivity (adjusted operating earnings −12%, after-tax ~$1.00 per share), ex-cash is **34x**.

Against peers: median P/E 21.5x, median yield 4.0%. Dispersion is wide (JJSF 35.6x, DMC 43.6x, FLO 21.5x/6.9%, FDP 19.9x/4.1%, BRBR 8.1x), so the median is a rough anchor. TR trades at a 39% premium to the median P/E with declining earnings and a **0.91%** cash yield — under a quarter of the peer median. The 3% annual stock dividend is economically a split and adds nothing to shareholder yield; screens that treat it as a distribution overstate TR's income profile.

TR earned some premium over the past five years: the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows $100 invested at end-2020 grew to $151 by end-2025 versus $98 for the peer group. That outperformance is the reason the multiple is where it is, not a reason it should stay there.

Cycle-adjustment cuts against the bull case rather than for it. Trailing EPS of $1.33 is not trough earnings — it already contains a peak investment-income contribution of roughly $0.20 per share after tax and roughly $0.03 of prior-period bond-accretion catch-up. Normalized EPS of $1.33-$1.40 puts the stock at 28-30x mid-cycle. There is no trough-multiple argument available.

Valuation score: **2.5**. The multiple is stretched against normalized earnings power with no growth to justify it and no identified catalyst for re-rating higher. The offsetting supports — $5.78 per share of net financial assets, zero leverage, zero equity-comp dilution — bound downside but do not create upside.

## Competitive Landscape

TR competes against materially larger, better-capitalized multinationals. Its durable advantages are narrow and real: century-old brand recognition at very low absolute price points, dominance in specific novelty niches (Blow Pops, Dubble Bubble, Fluffy Stuff), and a low-cost, self-funded manufacturing base. What it lacks is trade-spend scale, innovation cadence and presence in growth categories.

Trajectory is the relevant test, and it is flat to deteriorating. Management's own MD&A states it is "mindful of the effects and limits when passing on the above-discussed higher input costs to our customers as well as to the final consumers" — a direct admission of constrained pricing power. Trade promotions were deliberately increased, and management acknowledges this suppressed reported net sales. Spending more to hold volume flat at compressing margins is share defense.

Customer concentration has not improved. As a percentage of net product sales: Walmart 22.0% (2025) vs 23.2% (2024); McLane 19.7% vs 20.7%; Dollar Tree 13.1% vs 12.6%. These cannot be summed — the 10-K notes some Walmart and Dollar Tree sales route through McLane. The defensible statement is that Walmart plus Dollar Tree represent 35.1% of FY2025 net product sales with McLane at 19.7% partially overlapping. Receivables concentration improved (37.8% of trade receivables from the top three, down from 41.9%); sales concentration did not.

One genuine puzzle: TR's portfolio skews toward sugar confectionery, which should make it *less* cocoa-exposed than Hershey or Lindt, yet H1 margin compression was 3.5pp. Either cocoa content is higher than the brand mix implies, or TR's subscale procurement and weaker leverage with Walmart, Dollar Tree and McLane produce worse terms. The MD&A's pass-through language supports the second reading, which is the less favorable one.

Industry consolidation is accelerating around TR — the proxy notes Kellanova's acquisition by Mars and TreeHouse Foods' acquisition by Investindustrial, both removed from the compensation peer group (as described in TR's proxy; terms not independently verified). TR is the kind of asset a strategic buyer would want. But the change-in-control definition in the executive agreements requires an acquirer's voting power to exceed the Gordon family's, which makes an unsolicited transaction effectively impossible. Any takeover-optionality premium in the price rests on a door the family controls.

## Risk Assessment

**Deferred compensation inverts GAAP operating income.** Executive deferred comp is invested in notional accounts hedged by $130.7M of trading securities. Gains on those securities book to Other income, while the corresponding liability increase is charged to COGS and SG&A inside operating earnings. In Q2 2026 deferred-comp charges within earnings from operations were $15.95M — larger than the entire $14.33M of adjusted operating profit — producing a GAAP operating loss. Any screen keyed to GAAP operating margin or EBIT will misread this company. The liability is concentrated: per the proxy, approximately $73.6M is owed to CEO Ellen Gordon and ~$26.5M to CFO Howard Ember. Both the 10-K and 10-Q state these amounts will not be deductible when paid, structurally elevating the effective tax rate (Q2 26.9%, H1 25.9%). The current portion rose from $3.1M at 12/31/25 to $13.7M at 6/30/26 — plausibly the start of a scheduled executive distribution, plausibly a routine change in distribution elections. The filings do not say.

**Multi-employer pension, with a September 2027 decision point.** The Bakery and Confectionery Union fund has been in critical and declining status since 2015. Headline withdrawal liability was $102.8M (2025), but the actuary believes actual liability would likely be capped at twenty annual payments of $2.7M, present value $32.9-35.4M — 1.1-1.2% of equity value. The mass-withdrawal tail is $47.8-56.8M. Funded percentage is deteriorating on an actuarial basis (47.0% → 45.2% → 41.0%), and the actuary states it "remains unclear if the Plan can remain solvent through the targeted date of 2051." TR is obligated to participate through the union contract's September 2027 expiration. That expiry is both an opportunity (crystallize ~$33-35M, eliminate escalating surcharges) and a labor risk at the largest plant.

**Cocoa unhedged, transmitted through LIFO.** Item 3 confirms TR hedges "certain commodities (primarily sugar)," with commodity derivative notionals of only $7.2M at 6/30/26. Cocoa is managed through annual supply contracts, not derivatives. Under LIFO, current costs hit cost of sales immediately in both directions — which is why relief should arrive, but with a supply-chain lag TR has not quantified.

**Governance and controls.** The audit committee has no designated financial expert by board determination; a single 73-year-old officer serves as both principal financial officer and principal accounting officer; and FY2025 contained an out-of-period correction of $4.5M cumulative unaccreted bond discount. ICFR was reported effective with a clean Grant Thornton opinion, but the combination is a real control-environment concern rather than boilerplate.

**Key-person and succession.** The CEO is 94 and still signs the 10-Q certifications. Succession has been initiated — Karen G. Mills (former SBA Administrator, HBS faculty) appointed President June 2025, Henry G. Mills appointed COO January 2025 — not completed. Three of six board seats are held by Gordon family members.

**Spain.** The 97%-owned Barcelona subsidiary lost $2.24M in 2025 versus $0.61M in 2024. Management is conducting an "in-depth evaluation" of the "best course of action," expects continued 2026 losses, and anticipates likely needing to provide additional cash. Non-controlling interests are negative $(0.46)M. Immaterial financially; an exit would likely produce a one-time charge.

**Tariffs — a modest tailwind.** FY2025 incremental tariff cost was approximately $3.7M. Cocoa tariffs were rescinded in Q4 2025. Following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize the 2025 tariffs, management estimates it could recoup up to $1.3M, recorded only when received. Only $1.3M of the $3.7M was paid directly by TR; the balance was embedded in supplier prices and is not recoverable. Management characterizes H1 2026 tariff impact as nominal.

**Channel exposure to a consumer slowdown.** With 35%+ of sales through Walmart and Dollar Tree, TR is levered to mass and dollar-channel traffic. TR's low absolute price points provide partial trade-down insulation.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 35.8% against 27.0% one-year realized — rich by 8.8 points — with the term structure in backwardation (35.8% at 8 days versus 32.9% at 127 days) and an implied ±9.0% move on the August 21 tenor. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (−2.8% near term, −0.6% at six months). Confidence in these readings is low: the chain carries only 46 contracts across three expirations with a put/call open-interest ratio reported as 0.00, and the 10-Q was filed August 7 with no imminent scheduled catalyst. The reading is best interpreted as illiquidity premium in a thin chain rather than informed positioning. Notably, options are not corroborating a bearish thesis with put demand.

Short interest is 8.5% of float with **12.6 days to cover** and 2.35M shares short. On a genuinely tradable float far smaller than the 75.07M economic share count, that is squeeze-prone. This materially argues against expressing the negative view through a short position.

Risk score of **4.5** reflects genuine offsets — no leverage, no refinancing cliff, no covenant risk, negligible bankruptcy probability, zero equity-comp dilution — against a cluster of unquantified idiosyncratic exposures (dye reformulation, the 2027 union expiry, key-person, unhedged cocoa under LIFO, control-environment gaps) sitting behind a multiple that provides no cushion.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $45 (+13.2%)

Cocoa spot prices have retreated and flow into LIFO cost of sales during H2 2026, restoring adjusted operating margin toward the FY2025 level. Q3 Halloween sell-through captures the sales management says shifted out of Q2, and the increased advertising and trade promotion converts to volume. Freight normalizes as energy markets ease. FY2027 EPS recovers to roughly $1.50 on modest price and volume, and the market applies 30x — the multiple it has paid repeatedly over the past year — for $45. Investment income continues to compound on a $442M ladder. The $433.9M net financial asset position and $75-85M plant expansion signal that capital finally moves from bonds toward capacity. Zero leverage and zero equity dilution mean shareholders capture all of it.

### Bear Case — $30 (−24.5%)

Cocoa relief arrives later and smaller than management implies, while the freight and packaging inflation driving Q2's 18.2% distribution-cost increase persists on elevated energy markets. Increased advertising and trade promotion fails to lift volume, so the H1 pattern — flat sales, compressing margin — extends into H2, and FY2026 EPS lands near $1.14. Synthetic-dye reformulation costs land in 2027 with either margin compression or consumer rejection on TR's most color-dependent SKUs. Capex of $50-60M pushes FCF yield to 2.3-2.7%. The market re-rates toward the packaged-food median: 26x on $1.14-1.20 is $30-31, roughly 10% below the 52-week low of $33.43, with the $5.78 per share of net financial assets providing the floor beneath that.

### Base Case — $35.50 (−10.7%)

Halloween timing recovers part of the H1 shortfall and cocoa relief partially offsets persistent freight, producing FY2026 EPS near $1.17 and FY2027 recovery to roughly $1.32 — normalized, not growing. A 27x multiple on $1.32 is $35.64. Probability-weighting base 50%, bear 30%, bull 20% gives an expected price return of approximately **−10%**, roughly −9% including the 0.91% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength. The stock has risen 8.3% since our last review ($36.24 to $39.74) while H1 adjusted operating earnings fell 24.3% and the Q2 print showed a GAAP operating loss — price and fundamentals moved in opposite directions, which worsens risk/reward rather than improving it. Investors holding for the dividend should recognize that a 0.91% cash yield against a 4.0% peer median provides negligible income support at this multiple. We would reduce to no more than a residual position ahead of the Q3 print in late October / early November 2026, which is the year's decisive quarter (Halloween) and the first clean test of both the sales-timing claim and cocoa relief. We would not short: 12.6 days to cover on a small tradable float makes squeeze risk asymmetric against the position, and the options chain is not corroborating the bearish view.

**Upgrade triggers (back to HOLD or better).**
1. Q3 2026 adjusted operating margin recovers to ≥14% *with* net product sales growth ≥3% — confirming both the Halloween timing catch-up and cocoa pass-through, rather than one at the expense of the other.
2. Price at or below **$32** — approximately 24x normalized EPS of $1.33, roughly parity with the peer median once adjusted for $5.78 per share of net financial assets and zero leverage.
3. A step-change in capital return: a special dividend, or a formally authorized repurchase program of meaningful scale, deploying the $442M portfolio. This would convert the standing capital-efficiency discount into realized shareholder return and is the single most plausible re-rating catalyst available to management.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).**
1. FY2026 full-year adjusted operating margin below 11% with no cocoa relief visible in Q4 or Q1 MD&A commentary — indicating the compression is structural pricing-power loss rather than input-cost timing.
2. Synthetic-dye reformulation cost quantified above roughly 150bps of net product sales, or evidence of SKU delistings on color changes.
3. Union contract mishandling at the September 2027 expiration — a Chicago work stoppage, or withdrawal crystallizing above the $35.4M actuarial cap.
4. A further out-of-period accounting correction or an ICFR material weakness, given the absence of a designated audit committee financial expert and the combined PFO/PAO role.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 10-Q (late October / early November 2026), then the FY2026 10-K in February 2027. Monitor 8-K Item 5.02 filings continuously given the CEO's age; note that any CEO transition would not by itself open the company to an unsolicited transaction, because the change-in-control definition requires an acquirer to exceed Gordon-family voting power.

## Conclusion

**REDUCE.** Overall score 4.3, down from 5.3, driven by the Q2 2026 10-Q rather than by price action. Three findings carry the decision. First, the correct share count is 75.07M, not 42.98M, which puts the equity at $2,983M and 29.8x trailing net income — roughly 30x ex-cash after-tax operating earnings, and 33-35x the FY2026 sensitivity range.
