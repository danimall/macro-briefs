**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Unum Group is a top-tier provider of workplace financial-protection benefits — group and individual disability, group life, and voluntary/worksite products through Colonial Life — operating in the U.S., U.K., and Poland. The core business is healthy: Q1 2026 premium income grew 3.4% to $2,794M with growth in all three operating segments, Unum US sales rose 20.8%, and after-tax adjusted operating EPS reached $2.14, up roughly 10% year-on-year. The headline GAAP earnings collapse in 2025 (net income $738.5M vs. $1,779M in 2024) was a non-operating event tied to the third-quarter long-term-care reserve assumption update and the Fortitude Re reinsurance transaction; underlying operating EPS held near $8.

The investment case has shifted toward capital return. Buybacks doubled year-on-year to $402.4M in Q1 2026 under a fresh $1.0B authorization, the diluted share count fell about 8%, and the dividend was raised 10% to $0.46/quarter. Statutory capital is robust (U.S. RBC ~460%), holding-company liquidity is $1,726M, and the Fortitude Re cession (~21% of Closed Block LTC reserves) marks genuine progress dismantling the legacy tail. Much of the operating-EPS growth, however, is share-count driven rather than organic — premium grew only 3.4%, and several core segments softened in the quarter.

The stock has appreciated roughly 17% since our prior review to a fresh 52-week high near $92, leaving it at ~10.5x current-year adjusted operating EPS — cheap in absolute terms but at a discount that has persisted for years because of the LTC overhang. With consensus mean and median targets at $97.6 and $100 (only ~6–9% upside), no fresh dated re-rating catalyst, an LTC block that is now a recurring quarterly drag, and emerging disability-claim softness across U.S. and U.K. books, the asymmetry that supported a BUY at ~$80 has compressed. We move to HOLD on the back of the capital-return floor and 2% yield, while flagging the legacy tail as the dominant risk.

## Company Overview

Unum sells workplace financial-protection benefits through the employer/worksite channel via brokers and a captive agent force (Colonial Life). The product set spans group and individual disability, group life and AD&D, voluntary/supplemental products (accident, critical illness, cancer), dental/vision, and leave-management services. Five reportable segments structure the business:

- **Unum US (~66% of premium)** — the profit engine. Group disability, group life & AD&D, and supplemental/voluntary. Q1 2026 segment adjusted operating income (AOI) $337.9M.
- **Colonial Life (~17%)** — voluntary worksite products via independent agents; high-margin (Q1 benefit ratio 46%, AOI margin 27%). AOI $127.8M.
- **Unum International (~10%)** — Unum UK and Unum Poland. AOI $30.9M, with the UK weak this quarter.
- **Closed Block** — runoff long-term care (LTC) and other discontinued lines; the company's defining legacy risk.
- **Corporate** — holding-company costs and net interest expense.

Unum is a scale leader in U.S. group disability and the voluntary/worksite channel. Its moats are distribution depth (broker relationships, the Colonial agent force), integrated leave-management and fee-based services that support group persistency, and disability underwriting/claims expertise. Financial-strength ratings are solid and stable: A (AM Best/Fitch/S&P), A2 (Moody's). Market capitalization is approximately $14.6B on 159.8M shares.

## Financial Analysis

**Operating earnings are the right lens, not GAAP.** The 2025 net income drop was almost entirely the third-quarter LTC reserve assumption update plus Fortitude Re transaction losses. The company-selected adjusted operating EPS measure was $8.13 in 2025 versus $8.44 in 2024 — a modest dip — while GAAP net income halved. The trailing GAAP P/E of ~20x shown in screening data is built off depressed GAAP EPS of $4.27 and overstates the true earnings multiple; on adjusted operating EPS the stock trades closer to ~11x trailing.

**Q1 2026 (most recent quarter):**
- Net income **$232.0M ($1.41/dil)** vs. $189.1M ($1.06) a year ago, up 22.7%.
- After-tax adjusted operating income **$352.5M ($2.14/dil)** vs. $348.8M ($1.95). The per-share figure is up ~10%, boosted heavily by buybacks — 164.4M diluted shares vs. 178.9M, down 8%.
- Premium income +3.4% to $2,794M, with growth in all three core segments.
- Sales: Unum US +20.8%, Unum International +14.1%, Colonial Life +0.9%.

**Segment detail:**
- Unum US benefit ratio improved to 59.5%; group life AOI surged on lower mortality (benefit ratio 61.8% vs. 69.3%).
- Soft spots: Unum US group disability AOI −10.6% (pricing actions plus higher paid-family-leave incidence); supplemental/voluntary AOI −17.4% (higher individual-disability claim size/incidence, plus lapping a one-time recapture gain); Unum UK AOI −30.8% in local currency on adverse disability claims (benefit ratio jumped to 72.9% from 67.1%).
- The Closed Block swung to a **$145.3M adjusted operating loss** (vs. +$8.0M) on a $95.2M remeasurement loss and a 97.6% LTC net premium ratio (up from 94.7%).

**Balance sheet and capital:**
- Total assets $62.7B; equity $10.9B; investment portfolio $42.8B (98% investment-grade fixed maturities, only 2.9% below-IG).
- Net unrealized loss on fixed maturities widened to $2.2B (from $1.7B) on higher Treasury rates and wider spreads — an AOCI item, not a credit problem.
- U.S. RBC ratio ~460%, well above action levels; holding-company liquidity $1,726M; ~$631M of ordinary U.S. subsidiary dividend capacity in 2026.
- Operating cash flow $340.8M in Q1; long-term debt $3.76B (manageable for an insurer).

The quality flag: adjusted operating EPS growth is materially supported by the shrinking float. That is durable while the LTC block behaves and statutory dividend upstreaming continues, but it is not the same as organic earnings expansion. The earnings-surprise record reinforces caution — over the last four quarters, one beat and three misses (Q4'25 missed by 10%), so forward consensus carries less reliability than the screening multiple alone would suggest.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth picture is mixed. Premium income rose only 3.4%, and the headline ~10% adjusted operating EPS gain is roughly the sum of low-single-digit operating progress plus an 8% reduction in share count. Sales momentum is genuinely strong (Unum US new sales +20.8%, International +14.1%), and persistency improved (group LTD persistency 92.2%, group life 92.6%), which supports a multi-year premium-growth glidepath in the core segments. The voluntary/worksite secular tailwind — employers shifting cost to employee-paid voluntary products — favors both Unum US supplemental and Colonial Life.

Against that, three operating lines deteriorated in the quarter: U.S. group disability (paid-family-leave incidence), U.S. individual disability (claim size/incidence), and a sharp Unum UK disability spike. Whether this is noise or the start of a claims-experience trend is the key open question for the operating thesis. The Closed Block is now a structural quarterly drag, and management has reclassified it out of adjusted operating income, which improves the optics but does not change the cash and reserve reality.

Estimate revisions do not signal acceleration. Current-year consensus is $8.77 (+0.7% over 30 days, −0.1% over 90 days) and next-year $9.74 (+0.5% over 30 days, −1.1% over 90 days). These are essentially flat — well short of the revision velocity that would mark an accelerating earnings story. The growth case is steady compounding via buybacks layered on a soft-but-stable operating base.

## Valuation Assessment

At $91.62, Unum trades at ~10.4x current-year adjusted operating EPS ($8.77) and ~9.4x next-year ($9.74). On a forward adjusted basis this is inexpensive in absolute terms. The peer table shows a median P/E of 20.0, but that median is distorted — JXN (−18.2) and BHF (−21.6) carry negative GAAP P/Es from variable-annuity hedging noise, and CNO (20.0) and PRI (11.7) span a wide range. The more useful read is that life/health insurers generally trade in the high-single to low-double-digit forward multiple range, and Unum sits at the lower end.

The cheapness is not a new signal. Unum has traded at a discount for years specifically because of the LTC overhang, and a low multiple anchored to a structural overhang is not evidence of mispricing absent a catalyst that resolves the overhang. The Fortitude Re cession was that kind of event, but it closed in July 2025 and has largely been digested — the indexed cumulative TSR roughly doubled the peer group (398.5 vs. 201.0 over the proxy measurement window) and the stock just ran 17% to a fresh 52-week high. There is no further dated catalyst to force a re-rating from here; the next major LTC event (the third-quarter annual assumption review) is a downside risk, not an upside trigger.

Analyst targets bound the asymmetry tightly: mean $97.6 (~7% upside), median $100 (~9%), low $81 (~12% downside), high $117 (~28%). With the stock at the high end of its range, the realized appreciation has consumed most of the gap to consensus. We score valuation moderately — the forward multiple offers downside support and the 2% dividend plus buyback yield is real, but at this price the cheap multiple is more a function of a permanent discount than an identifiable, soon-to-close mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Unum competes with MetLife, The Hartford, Lincoln Financial, Guardian, and Principal. Its differentiation rests on leave-management and fee-based services, integrated worksite enrollment technology, and Colonial Life's agent-driven voluntary distribution — all of which support persistency. The voluntary/worksite secular shift toward employee-paid products is a structural tailwind for both Unum US supplemental and Colonial Life.

Management explicitly notes the group disability market "remains competitive," which pressures pricing and renewals — visible in the group disability AOI decline as pricing actions and claims incidence weighed on the line. The competitive position is stable rather than improving: Unum is holding its scale advantages and growing sales, but margin trajectory in the core disability lines is currently flat-to-soft, and the legacy LTC block historically capped the multiple relative to peers. The Fortitude Re deal addresses that overhang directly, which is the most constructive structural development.

## Risk Assessment

**Long-term care is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and is worsening at the margin.** Despite the Fortitude Re cession, the Closed Block still carries roughly $24B of LTC future-policy-benefit reserves. Future benefits nearly exhaust future premiums, and some cohorts are in loss recognition, generating a $49.1M pre-tax hit in Q1 2026 alone. Q1 also showed higher-than-expected group LTC policy terminations and claim incidence. Reserve adequacy depends on volatile morbidity, mortality, claim-resolution, persistency, and rate-increase assumptions. The annual third-quarter assumption review is a recurring earnings-risk event; it caused the 2025 GAAP collapse and will recur each year. The market cannot fully price the magnitude of this binary ahead of time, which warrants a below-average risk score.

**Reinsurance counterparty concentration** is a new dependency: $11.3B of reinsurance recoverable now includes ~$3.6B from Fortitude Re, a single A-rated Bermuda reinsurer (collateralized trust). **Interest-rate sensitivity is two-sided** — higher rates lifted new-money yields and reduced reserves ($0.6B reserve reduction in Q1) but inflated the $2.2B unrealized investment loss; falling rates pressure LTC reserve discount rates. **Disability claim experience** softened across U.S. group, U.S. individual, and U.K. lines this quarter. The investment portfolio carries $1.44B of NAV-volatile private-equity partnership exposure and $2.07B of CRE mortgages (well-underwritten, 82.5% at LTV ≤65%, office only ~14%). Per the Q1 10-Q, there were no material changes to risk factors from the 2025 10-K.

The buyback engine itself is a soft risk: continued aggressive repurchases depend on uninterrupted statutory dividend upstreaming, which the LTC block could disrupt in an adverse assumption year.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is rich at 44.3% (rising to ~50.2% in the latest snapshot) against 1-year historical volatility of 25.1%, a 19-point spread, with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 44.3% > long-term 27.2%). The market is pricing imminent stress or a near-dated event. Skew is balanced (−1.2%) and put/call ratios are low (volume 0.13, OI 0.11), so there is no pronounced directional fear — the elevated near-term vol reflects expected movement rather than a clear bearish tilt. Short interest is light at 2.7% of float (2.6 days to cover), indicating no crowded bearish position. The rich near-term IV is a mild caution on entry timing; it does not contradict the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- Capital return compounds shareholder value: 8% annual share-count reduction, 10% dividend growth, $601M remaining on the $1.0B authorization, RBC ~460% supporting continued upstreaming.
- The Fortitude Re cession and the February 2026 halt of new group LTC enrollments steadily shrink the legacy tail; management commits to "continue to explore and execute" further reinsurance/structural options, with the prospect of another de-risking transaction that re-rates the multiple toward peers.
- Strong core sales momentum (US +20.8%) and improving persistency support a multi-year premium glidepath; the voluntary/worksite secular shift is a durable tailwind.
- Forward multiple of ~10.4x with a 2% yield and large buyback provides downside support; bull case to the analyst high of $117 (~28%) on a successful further LTC transaction plus stable claims experience.

### Bear Case
- The LTC block deteriorates further: a third-quarter assumption review again forces a reserve charge, and higher-than-expected terminations/incidence persist. Closed Block losses recur quarterly.
- Disability claim softness across U.S. group, U.S. individual, and U.K. proves to be a trend rather than noise, pressuring the core margin engine.
- Adjusted-EPS growth that is largely buyback-driven decelerates if statutory dividend capacity is constrained by an adverse LTC year.
- The stock de-rates back toward the analyst low of $81 (~−12%) as the post-Fortitude re-rating fades without a fresh catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are Q2 2026 earnings (late July) and the third-quarter 2026 LTC annual assumption review (Q3 report), which is the single largest annual swing factor.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Announcement of a further LTC reinsurance/structural transaction materially reducing the ~$24B Closed Block reserve, removing the multiple-capping overhang.
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilized disability claims experience (Unum US group disability benefit ratio holding/improving, Unum UK benefit ratio recovering below ~68%) alongside CY/NY EPS estimate revisions turning positive (>+3% over 90 days).
- A pullback toward the high-$70s/low-$80s that restores double-digit upside to consensus targets while fundamentals hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A third-quarter LTC assumption update forcing another large reserve charge with the net premium ratio pushing toward/above 100%, signaling structurally inadequate reserves.
- Confirmation that disability claims deterioration is a multi-quarter trend (group disability AOI declining for three consecutive quarters).
- Curtailment of the buyback or dividend coverage strain driven by constrained statutory dividend capacity.

## Conclusion

Unum is a well-managed, scale leader in workplace benefits with a robust capital position, disciplined capital return, and a clear strategy to dismantle its long-term-care tail. The prior BUY at ~$80 was rewarded. At ~10.4x current-year adjusted operating EPS the valuation is inexpensive, but cheapness has been the perennial state given the LTC overhang, the further-de-risking catalyst is not yet announced or dated, and the post-Fortitude re-rating has largely played out. Operating-EPS growth is substantially buyback-driven on top of a 3.4% premium base, several core lines softened this quarter, and the LTC block is now a recurring quarterly drag with a 97.6% net premium ratio and an annual assumption review that remains a downside event.

With only ~7–9% upside to consensus mean/median targets, an emerging-but-unconfirmed disability-claims softness, and the legacy tail worsening at the margin, the risk/reward has compressed at the high. We rate UNM **HOLD** (income_hold), supported by the capital-return floor and 2% dividend, and would revisit a more constructive stance on a further LTC transaction, confirmed claims stabilization, or a pullback that restores asymmetry.
