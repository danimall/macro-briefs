**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Carlisle has completed a multi-year transformation from a diversified industrial conglomerate into a pure-play building-envelope products company. Following the 2024 divestiture of Carlisle Interconnect Technologies for ~$2.0B net, the business now runs two segments: Carlisle Construction Materials (CCM, ~74% of revenue, ~27% operating margin) and Carlisle Weatherproofing Technologies (CWT, ~26% of revenue, ~8% operating margin). CCM is one of only four major U.S. single-ply commercial roofing manufacturers, and roughly 70% of its demand is non-discretionary re-roofing. That structural insulation, combined with a lean operating culture that sustains best-in-class margins, is the durable part of the thesis.

The cyclical part is less favorable. Revenue has plateaued near $5.0B (2023 $4.59B, 2024 $5.00B, 2025 $5.02B), 2025 organic revenue fell 2.9%, and operating margin compressed from 22.8% to 20.0% on fixed-cost deleveraging. Q1 2026 revenue declined 4.0% YoY, and management explicitly expects "challenging market conditions to continue into the first half of 2026." The defining corporate action is an aggressive, partly debt-funded buyback that has retired roughly 20% of the share count in two years (52.5M diluted shares in 2022 to 41.1M by Q1 2026), funded by ~$1B/year of free cash flow plus $1B of new 5.25%–5.55% senior notes. This is a bet on both the company's own stock and the eventual construction recovery, and it has raised leverage (D/E ~1.0x and rising) and nearly doubled interest expense YoY.

At $336, down 22.9% from the 52-week high of $435.92, CSL trades at ~16x consensus 2026 EPS of $20.63 and ~14.4x 2027 EPS of $23.28, a meaningful discount to industrial-conglomerate peers at ~35x, though that peer set is a poor comparable. The forward EPS recovery mechanism (margin snap-back on volume plus a shrunken share count) is real and the balance sheet is sound. But estimate revisions have turned modestly negative over the past 30–90 days, there is no dated near-term catalyst, and near-term options positioning is flashing stress (P/C OI ratio 7.11, IV in backwardation at 50.8% vs 45.1% long-dated). The risk/reward is balanced. We rate CSL HOLD.

## Company Overview

Carlisle now operates as a focused building-products manufacturer serving the commercial and residential construction envelope. The two segments:

- **Carlisle Construction Materials (CCM)** — $3.72B revenue in 2025. Single-ply commercial roofing membranes (EPDM, TPO, PVC), polyiso insulation, and architectural metals under the Carlisle SynTec, Versico, WeatherBond, and Hunter Panels brands. Segment operating margin ~26.8%, adjusted EBITDA margin ~29%. This is a top-tier position in a consolidated, rational four-manufacturer U.S. single-ply roofing oligopoly. The moat rests on a distribution/contractor network with high switching costs, long-dated warranties (5–40 years) that lock in product specification, and the Carlisle Operating System (COS) lean/six-sigma culture. About 70% of CCM's demand is re-roofing replacement of aging non-residential roofs (>70% of U.S. non-residential stock is >25 years old), which creates a recurring, non-discretionary maintenance stream.

- **Carlisle Weatherproofing Technologies (CWT)** — $1.30B revenue in 2025. Waterproofing, air/vapor barriers, spray polyurethane foam, EPS insulation, and roofing underlayments. Materially lower quality: 7.8% operating margin (down from 13.4%) and 17.3% adjusted EBITDA margin, with more residential exposure and greater M&A dependence.

The company employs ~5,900 people and is targeting continued portfolio focus and bolt-on M&A under its "Vision 2030" framework.

## Financial Analysis

**Cash flow and balance-sheet resilience are genuine strengths.** 2025 operating cash flow was $1.10B (up from $1.03B), free cash flow $970.6M on modest capex of $131M, and an FCF margin of 19.3%. Operating cash flow exceeds net income of $743M, signaling clean, high-quality earnings. Altman Z-Score of 3.1 sits in the safe zone; the current ratio is 3.09.

**Revenue and margin trend reflect a cyclical trough, not structural decline:**

| Year | Revenue | Op. Income | Op. Margin | Net Income (cont. ops) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $4.59B | $983M | 21.4% | $767M |
| 2024 | $5.00B | $1.14B | 22.8% | $865M |
| 2025 | $5.02B | $1.00B | 20.0% | $743M |

2025 revenue was essentially flat (+0.3%): organic -2.9%, acquisitions +3.2%. The margin compression is almost entirely a volume-absorption story: gross margin fell from 37.7% to 35.7% as fixed costs were spread over lower volume. This works both ways: margins should snap back sharply when volumes recover, but they remain exposed if the trough extends. CWT bore the brunt, with operating margin collapsing from 13.4% to 7.8%.

**Q1 2026 demonstrated the cost-discipline thesis.** Revenue of $1.05B fell 4.0% YoY (organic -5.0%), yet operating margin expanded 30 bps to 17.1% and adjusted EBITDA margin rose 50 bps to 22.3%, driven by COS execution and an 11.4% cut in S&A. CCM adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 27.4%. The Q1 operating cash use of -$44.7M was distorted by a $125M post-year-end settlement of an accrued tax liability; underlying working capital improved.

**The capital structure is being deliberately re-engineered.** Carlisle repurchased $1.3B of stock in 2025 and $1.6B in 2024, retiring ~20% of the float in two years. This is partly debt-funded: long-term debt jumped from $1.89B to $2.88B after $1B of new notes (5.25% 2035s, 5.55% 2040s) in August 2025. Stockholders' equity has been reduced to $1.65B against $6.37B of accumulated treasury stock. Interest expense in Q1 2026 was $28.3M, up 91% YoY. Debt/equity is ~1.0x and rising as equity shrinks. This leverage level is manageable for a stable-cash-flow building-products business, but funding buybacks with high-coupon debt while equity contracts is a deliberate escalation of financial risk. That tempers the financial-health score below the top tier.

The dividend was raised to $1.10/quarter (~$181M paid in 2025), an aristocrat-caliber raise history, at a modest 1.34% yield with ample coverage.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is soft. Revenue is stuck near $5B, organic growth was negative in both 2025 (-2.9%) and Q1 2026 (-5.0%), and management expects challenging conditions into H1 2026. Both segments are levered to commercial construction, with CWT additionally exposed to residential housing affordability. Weather is a swing factor: Q1 2026 was hurt by unfavorable winter weather limiting contractor days on the roof.

The forward earnings recovery mechanism is the crux of the bull case. It is an EPS story built on three levers: (1) operating margin snap-back as volumes recover and fixed-cost absorption improves, (2) a share count that has fallen ~20% and continues to shrink (6.6M shares remaining under authorization after Q1, another 7.5M authorized in Sept 2025), and (3) continued bolt-on M&A. Consensus captures this: current-year EPS of $20.63 and next-year of $23.28 imply ~13% EPS growth off a depressed 2025 base.

The estimate revision picture is the caution. Over the trailing 90 days, next-quarter estimates rose +2.3%, current-year +1.8%, and next-year +1.1%. But the trailing 30 days have turned negative across every window (current quarter -1.2%, next quarter -2.8%, current year -1.5%, next year -0.7%). This is deceleration, not durable momentum, and it does not clear the bar for a strong inflection signal. Growth is directionally intact on a 12–18 month view but lacks the accelerating estimate velocity that would justify weighting growth heavily today. R&D was up 33% in 2025 and new products (ThermaThin 7 polyiso) are gaining recognition, but these are incremental, not needle-moving.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of 19.2x is on trough earnings and understates the cyclical-recovery upside embedded in forward estimates.

The peer benchmark is misleading. The supplied comparables — MMM (30.5x), HON (35.3x), CSW (41.0x) — are classified as industrial conglomerates but bear little resemblance to a focused building-envelope manufacturer. On absolute terms, ~16x forward earnings for a business generating ~19% FCF margins, ~27% CCM operating margins, and mid-teens forward EPS growth off a trough is reasonable-to-modestly-cheap, but not a screaming asymmetric setup.

The case for the stock being mispriced rests on cycle-adjusted earnings power. If commercial construction recovers and CCM margins normalize back toward 22–23% while the share count sits ~20% below 2022 levels, mid-cycle EPS power is plausibly in the $24–26 range. At a market-average ~18–20x on normalized earnings, that supports a $430–500 range, the top of the analyst target band. That is the bull mechanism.

The offsetting factors keep the valuation score balanced rather than high: estimate revisions have turned negative over 30 days, there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating, two customers represent a third of revenue, and the equity is being levered up. Cheapness alone, absent a catalyst, is a weak signal; value screens have persistently been the wrong way to sort winners in this environment, and CSL is cheap-ish rather than distressed-cheap. Analyst consensus targets (median $420, mean $403, high $450) sit well above the current price, but on a stock at a cyclical trough with negative near-term revisions those targets are anchored to a recovery that has not yet begun, so we treat them as a directional floor rather than confirmation of imminent upside. Valuation scores 5.5 — modest upside, no obvious mispricing to exploit today.

## Competitive Landscape

CCM's position is the anchor. It sits alongside Holcim's Elevate (former Firestone), GAF/Standard Industries, Johns Manville (Berkshire), and Sika in a rational oligopoly that supports high, stable margins. The re-roofing demand base is non-discretionary and driven by aging building stock, and energy-efficiency/decarbonization megatrends support long-run insulation and envelope demand. Long-dated warranties and specification lock-in create real switching costs.

The trajectory is where the concern lies. The 10-K notes ongoing consolidation among roofing distributors, which increases buyer power, directly tied to the fact that two customers represent 33% of consolidated revenue. This is a market leader whose channel is consolidating around it, giving those buyers pricing leverage over time. CWT is the weaker franchise, and the Henry trade name ($219M carrying value) cleared its impairment test by less than 10%, a specific signal that competitive/demand pressure in that segment is real. Net, competitive position is stable-to-slightly-defensive rather than clearly improving; the score of 6.0 reflects a strong incumbent with a durable moat but no evidence of share gains or moat widening, offset by distributor concentration risk. This is consistent with the trajectory (competitive position declined from 7.0 to 6.0 across prior reports), and the current data confirms the downgrade rather than reversing it.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration is the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** Two customers equal 33% of total consolidated revenue (per the 10-K). Loss of either "could have a material adverse effect." These are consolidating roofing distributors with growing pricing leverage. For a company this size, this concentration is unusually high and underappreciated.

**Cyclical trough exposure.** Both segments are levered to commercial construction, with CWT additionally exposed to residential. Management expects weakness into H1 2026. The margin snap-back that powers the bull case only materializes if the cycle turns; if the trough extends, fixed-cost deleveraging continues to pressure margins.

**Raw-material and oil sensitivity.** Raw materials plus inbound freight equal ~66% of COGS, with inputs (MDI, polyol, TPO/EPDM polymer, asphalt, styrene) petrochemical-linked. Q1 2026 management flagged oil-driven cost inflation tied to Middle East conflict, requiring price increases and freight surcharges, a margin-timing risk that is live given the current energy backdrop.

**Financial leverage and buyback risk.** Funding buybacks with high-coupon debt in a higher-for-longer rate environment raises interest expense and reduces the equity cushion. If the cycle stays depressed, the levered-equity bet amplifies downside.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** $1.54B goodwill + $1.43B intangibles = ~47% of assets. The Henry trade name cleared impairment by <10%, a plausible near-term impairment if CWT demand doesn't recover.

**Open governance item.** A November 2025 Item 5.02 + Reg FD 8-K could not be verified (SEC/news access blocked). This warrants confirmation as a possible senior management or succession event that is not yet fully characterized.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 50.8% is rich versus 38.5% one-year historical volatility (a 12.3-point spread) and sits at the 88th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (50.8% near-term vs 45.1% at six months), which typically signals an imminent event or elevated uncertainty. The put/call open-interest ratio has spiked from 0.77 to 7.11 over the past month while the price fell 6.4%, indicating heavy put accumulation. Skew is balanced near-term (-0.1%) but steepens to -4.4% at six months, indicating growing downside hedging demand further out. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11%. This does not override the fundamental view, but it corroborates a cautious, balanced stance rather than a conviction buy at these levels; the market is bracing for a move, likely around the next earnings print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($430–500):** The construction cycle troughs in H1 2026 and re-roofing resilience plus new-construction recovery drive CCM volumes higher. Fixed-cost absorption reverses, pushing operating margins back toward 22–23%. Against a share count ~20% below 2022, EPS recovers toward $24–26 mid-cycle. The Q1 2026 margin expansion despite a 4% revenue decline demonstrates the cost discipline that would amplify this recovery. At ~18–20x normalized EPS, the stock reaches the mid-$400s to $500, in line with the analyst high target of $450 and consensus median of $420.

**Bear Case ($260–290):** The trough extends through 2026 into 2027 as commercial and residential construction stay weak. Fixed-cost deleveraging continues to compress margins, CWT margins remain depressed and trigger a Henry trade-name impairment, and one of the two 33%-concentration customers renegotiates or defects. Rising interest expense on the newly issued high-coupon debt compounds the earnings pressure while the reduced equity base amplifies downside. The stock derates toward ~13x depressed earnings, roughly 15–20% below current levels.

**Base Case ($370–390):** Modest H2 2026 stabilization, continued buyback support, and margin defense keep EPS on its ~$20.60 → ~$23.30 consensus path. The stock re-rates toward ~16–17x forward EPS as the cycle bottoms, implying ~10–15% upside over 12 months plus the ~1.3% dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the forward earnings-recovery mechanism is genuine but there is no dated near-term catalyst, estimate revisions have turned modestly negative, and the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth turns positive (>0% YoY) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the cycle has troughed
- Estimate revision velocity turns positive again (30-day revisions flip from negative to +3% or better on the current-year number)
- A pullback toward the low-$300s / high-$290s (near the 52-week low of $293), which would push forward P/E below 15x and widen the margin of safety

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material Henry trade-name (or other CWT) goodwill/intangible impairment
- Loss or renegotiation of one of the two customers representing 33% of revenue
- Interest coverage deterioration or a covenant concern as debt-funded buybacks continue against a depressed earnings base
- Confirmation that the unverified November 2025 8-K represents a disruptive management transition

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the near-term options stress and IV backwardation point to a move around this print) and confirmation of the November 2025 governance item. Reassess the cycle-trough thesis against H2 2026 construction data.

## Conclusion

Carlisle is a high-quality, focused building-envelope compounder with a durable re-roofing demand base, a rational four-player oligopoly in its core CCM segment, best-in-class margins, and ~$1B/year of free cash flow being converted into aggressive share reduction. The forward earnings-recovery arithmetic — margin snap-back on a volume recovery times a share count ~20% smaller than three years ago — is real and could drive outsized EPS upside when the construction cycle turns.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are equally concrete: revenue is stuck near $5B, organic growth is negative, margins are compressing on volume deleveraging, estimate revisions have rolled over in the last 30 days, the equity is being deliberately levered up with high-coupon debt, and two customers account for a third of the company. Near-term options positioning (P/C OI 7.11, IV backwardation) signals the market is bracing for stress. At ~16x forward EPS with no dated catalyst, the stock offers modest upside to base case with symmetric downside if the trough extends. This is consistent with the recent trajectory — the recommendation has moved BUY → HOLD as the cyclical picture softened, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that shift. We rate CSL **HOLD** and would turn constructive on a pullback toward the low-$300s or on confirmation that construction volumes and estimate revisions have inflected positively.
