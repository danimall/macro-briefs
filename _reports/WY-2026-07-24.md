**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Weyerhaeuser owns approximately 10.4 million acres of U.S. timberland plus a leading wood-products manufacturing footprint, structured as a REIT. It is a high-quality, hard-asset business trading through a cyclical trough. The stock at $23.64 sits about 15% below its 52-week high of $27.75 and, per third-party estimates, at roughly 66% of net asset value. The debate is whether the market is under-pricing the embedded optionality (forest carbon and carbon capture within the Climate Solutions line, incremental engineered-wood capacity, and long-run timberland appreciation) or correctly discounting an earnings base that remains levered to a housing recovery that keeps getting pushed out.

The near-term operating picture is weaker than the headline suggests. Q1 2026 net earnings of $156M ($0.22 EPS) looked like a strong beat, but roughly $86M of that was non-recurring: a $58M gain on the Virginia timberland sale and a $28M product-remediation insurance recovery. Net earnings before special items were $77M, down from $83M a year earlier. Wood Products adjusted EBITDA fell 56% year over year on a 26% drop in OSB realizations, and Strategic Land Solutions EBITDA more than doubled entirely on a single $94M conservation easement sale. Operating cash flow was $52M against $112M of capex and $151M of dividends paid; adjusted funds available for distribution (FAD) was negative $58M. The dividend is currently funded by asset sales and balance-sheet cash, not operations.

We maintain a HOLD. The asset value and balance sheet limit downside, and management is high-grading the portfolio while making a rare growth bet on the $300M Monticello engineered-wood plant. But near-term estimate revisions are falling (current-quarter consensus cut 24.5% over 30 days), the variable portion of shareholder return is effectively switched off until FAD recovers, and there is no dated catalyst to close the NAV discount. The prior three reports were all HOLDs; nothing in the current data supports upgrading to a directional call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Weyerhaeuser controls one of the largest private timberland bases in the world, concentrated in the supply-constrained West (Oregon, Washington) and the higher-supply South, with additional acreage in Maine and managed acreage in Canada. As reclassified effective Q1 2026, the company reports three segments:

- **Timberlands** — logs (domestic and export), stumpage, and recreational leases. This is the economic core, resting on an appreciating biological asset that management can flex against price cycles by adjusting harvest timing.
- **Strategic Land Solutions** (renamed from "Real Estate, Energy & Natural Resources") — higher-but-for-use land sales, minerals/aggregates/gas royalties, and Climate Solutions (conservation and mitigation banking, renewable-energy leases, forest carbon credits, and carbon capture and sequestration). This is the highest-margin, highest-optionality, and lumpiest segment.
- **Wood Products** — structural lumber, OSB, engineered wood products (TimberStrand, I-joists), plywood, MDF, and distribution. The most cyclical, pure-commodity segment.

The structural advantage is the irreplaceable land base and the Western position's pricing power. The structural vulnerability is that Wood Products and, to a lesser degree, Timberlands earnings are direct functions of U.S. housing starts and repair/remodel activity, which the company cannot control.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory frames the entire investment case. Revenue fell from roughly $10.2B in the 2022 peak to about $6.9B, and net income collapsed from $1.88B (2022) to $839M (2023), $396M (2024), and $324M (2025). The stock's depressed level reflects this decline.

Q1 2026 detail:
- Net sales $1,727M vs. $1,763M a year prior (–2%).
- Net earnings $156M ($0.22) vs. $83M ($0.11); net earnings before special items were $77M vs. $83M, flat-to-down operationally.
- Wood Products adjusted EBITDA $71M vs. $161M (–56%), driven by a 26% OSB realization decline and 4–5% lumber price declines.
- Timberlands adjusted EBITDA $120M vs. $167M on softer Western log prices (–10%) and volumes.
- Strategic Land Solutions EBITDA $193M vs. $82M, inflated by one $94M conservation easement sale.

The cash-flow quality is the central concern. Operating cash flow was $52M (down from $70M). After $112M capex, adjusted FAD was negative $58M. The company paid $151M in common dividends, funded by $192M of timberland-sale proceeds and balance-sheet cash (which fell from $464M to $299M). This is survivable at a trough for a company with strong liquidity, but the dividend is out-earning operating free cash flow, and it caps the variable portion of shareholder return.

The balance sheet supports patience. Total debt is roughly $5.4B, with about $4.1B fixed at a 5.03% average and $1.35B variable ($800M swapped to a fixed 3.414%). Liquidity is ample: $299M cash, an undrawn $1.75B revolver (maturity 2030), and a $1.75B commercial-paper program established in Q4 2025. The company is in covenant compliance. As a REIT, most REIT-level income is untaxed; the taxable REIT subsidiary bears roughly $15M/quarter. The pension carries a $489M net loss in AOCI, sensitive to rates and returns.

Capital allocation targets returning 75–80% of adjusted FAD via base dividend plus a variable supplement. The $1B buyback authorized in May 2025 has $928M remaining, but only $10M was repurchased in Q1 2026; buybacks were throttled given negative FAD. Share count is drifting down (721M vs. 726M year over year), and the base dividend held flat at $0.21/quarter.

The earnings-surprise record shows four consecutive beats, but the magnitudes (124%, 33%, 187%, 8%) are exaggerated by the tiny estimate base near trough earnings and by non-operating items. High beat percentages on cents-per-share estimates are not evidence of durable operating momentum here.

## Growth Analysis

There are two distinct growth vectors, one cyclical and one structural, and both are longer-dated than the current price wants.

The cyclical lever is a U.S. housing recovery. Q1 2026 starts ran around 1.4M SAAR, and new single-family home sales fell 17.2% in January 2026 on affordability pressure from ~6.4% mortgage rates. The recovery timing keeps slipping. Management's long-term thesis (favorable home-buying-age demographics, a decade of underbuilding, aging housing stock at a median 46 years supporting repair/remodel) is credible over a multi-year horizon but does not resolve the near-term affordability headwind. Within Q1 the lumber composite did rise from $385 to $470/MBF on Q4 2025 curtailments and lean dealer inventories, a positive supply-side signal; OSB rose more modestly ($230 to $265/MSF) because capacity remains ample.

The structural lever is Climate Solutions and engineered wood. Forest carbon and CCS are WY-owned opportunities rather than partnership dependencies, and the $300M Monticello TimberStrand plant (start-up targeted 2027) adds high-margin capacity and shifts mix away from commodity/Canadian exposure. These are real value drivers, but Climate Solutions revenue is lumpy; the Q1 EBITDA doubling rested on a single easement sale that should not be extrapolated to a run-rate.

Estimate revisions temper the growth score. Current-quarter consensus was cut 24.5% over 30 days (though up 42.8% over 90 days off a very low base), next-quarter is down 16.8% over 90 days, and both current-year ($0.29) and next-year ($0.67) estimates were trimmed over the past 30 days. The 90-day upturn signal is present in the current quarter, but it is not corroborated across windows or by revenue growth (Q1 sales were down), so it does not meet the bar for a standalone bullish read.

## Valuation Assessment

WY trades at a 42.9x trailing/forward P/E on $0.44 trailing EPS, which is nearly meaningless at a trough. On next-year consensus of $0.67, the multiple is roughly 36x, still elevated for a cyclical. Earnings multiples are the wrong lens here; the relevant frame is asset value and mid-cycle earnings power.

On net asset value, third-party estimates put the stock at about 66% of NAV, the clearest support for the shares and the core of the bull case. Timberland is an appreciating hard asset, and the discount embeds a full cyclical trough plus little credit for Climate Solutions/CCS optionality. The 3.5% dividend yield provides some absolute support, though the negative adjusted FAD qualifies its durability at the trough.

Against Real Estate peers, WY's headline P/E is not comparable: the peer median P/E of 31.0 reflects stabilized-income REITs (KIM, INVH, HST, WPC, ESS) with very different earnings drivers, and WY's 3.5% yield sits modestly below the 4.0% peer-median yield. The more relevant comparison set is timber and lumber peers (Rayonier, PotlatchDeltic, West Fraser, Canfor), where WY is the scale leader with the deepest HBU/Climate Solutions optionality.

The valuation is value support with no catalyst rather than an asymmetric buy. Cheapness relative to NAV is real, but a cheap-on-assets stock without an independent catalyst tends to stay cheap; the NAV discount has persisted precisely because the earnings recovery keeps slipping and FAD is negative. We score valuation modestly above neutral to reflect the asset backing and yield support, tempered by the absence of a dated re-rating trigger and falling near-term estimates. Analyst targets ($27 low, $30 median, $38 high) sit roughly 14–61% above the current price, but these are stabilized targets on an expected cyclical recovery, not a catalyst we can date.

## Competitive Landscape

Weyerhaeuser is the scale leader among timber REITs, with the most diversified segment mix and the deepest optionality set. Its Western timberland is supply-constrained, conferring pricing power that its Southern acreage (ample supply, prices down ~3.5% year over year) lacks. The company is actively high-grading: selling non-strategic and higher-cost assets (108,000 Virginia acres for $192M, the Princeton BC mill for ~$85M) while investing in higher-margin engineered wood. The OBBBA's increase in the TRS asset cap from 20% to 25% (effective 1/1/2026) is a modest structural positive for expanding Wood Products and HBU activity.

The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding in trajectory terms. Five-year relative TSR is indexed at 86.4 versus a peer group at 102.2, consistent with the NAV-discount underperformance. The prior report cut this dimension from 7.0 to 6.0, and we hold at 6.0: the moat (irreplaceable land, scale, Western pricing power) is intact, but there is no evidence of share gains, and commodity segments have no pricing power by definition. Trade policy (Canadian softwood duties, 2025 tariff escalation, retaliatory tariffs) is a two-sided competitive variable; China lifting its U.S. log-import suspension in Q4 2025 is a modest tailwind WY is re-establishing exposure to.

## Risk Assessment

Company-specific risks that could move the stock beyond what is already priced:

1. **Housing/lumber cycle duration.** The entire earnings base is levered to a recovery that keeps slipping. This is largely priced (the NAV discount reflects it), but a further leg down in starts on higher-for-longer rates would pressure FAD and the variable dividend.
2. **Commodity price exposure with no pricing power.** A 26% OSB swing flows nearly directly to EBITDA. This cuts both ways; the within-Q1 lumber recovery to $470/MBF is the positive mirror.
3. **CBA expiration May 31, 2026.** Roughly 1,200 union workers (IAM) across four lumber mills and Western timberlands are covered by an agreement that expires at end of May; the prior cycle triggered a Sept–Oct 2022 work stoppage. This falls in peak building season and is an underappreciated near-term operational risk.
4. **Dividend not guaranteed.** The board conditions dividends on cash flow. With adjusted FAD negative, the variable supplement/buyback is effectively off; the base dividend is being sustained by asset sales.
5. **Trade policy.** Canadian softwood duties and 2025 tariff/retaliation dynamics create two-sided export/import risk.
6. **Physical climate risk.** Fire, pests, and drought threaten the timber base, which the company does not insure.
7. **Governance transitions.** A cluster of Item 5.02 director/officer 8-Ks (Nov 2025, Jan–Feb 2026) appears routine but warrants a confirmatory read; CEO Stockfish remains in seat.

On balance, the known risks are well-understood and substantially priced, but the CBA expiration and negative FAD are specific, near-dated items that keep the risk score at neutral rather than higher.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no directional edge. Near-term ATM IV of 26.6% is essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 26.1% (fair), sitting at the 22nd percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (–2.1%), and the term structure is in normal contango (26.6% near vs. 31.2% long-dated), reflecting complacency rather than stress. ATM IV collapsed from 54.7% in early June to 26.6%; the prior event premium (likely earnings-related) has fully deflated, so the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst. The implied ±6.2% move on the nearest expiry is modest. Short interest is low at 3.9% of float (4.0 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded bearish view nor squeeze fuel. Options data is consistent with a range-bound, cycle-dependent stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($30–38).** The stock trades at ~66% of NAV on an irreplaceable, appreciating hard asset. A housing recovery, supported by demographics, a decade of underbuilding, and aging stock, restores mid-cycle Wood Products and Timberlands earnings; lumber has already begun recovering within Q1 ($385 to $470/MBF); and the variable dividend/buyback resume as FAD turns positive. Climate Solutions/CCS and the Monticello EWP plant add margin and optionality the market is not paying for. Convergence toward NAV plus mid-cycle earnings power supports the $30 median-to-$38 high analyst range.

**Bear Case ($19–21).** Higher-for-longer rates keep mortgage rates near 6.4%+ and push the housing recovery further out; OSB and Southern log oversupply persist; adjusted FAD stays negative, forcing continued reliance on asset sales to fund a flat dividend and keeping buybacks off. A summer 2026 CBA work stoppage disrupts peak-season output. The NAV discount persists or widens as the trough extends, taking the stock toward the low $20s and below.

**Base Case (~$26).** A gradual, back-half-weighted lumber/log price recovery lifts operating earnings off the trough while the housing recovery remains slow. FAD improves toward breakeven over the next 12 months, the base dividend holds, and the stock partially closes the NAV gap. This implies roughly $26, or about 10% above the current price, plus the 3.5% yield: a reasonable but unspectacular total return that does not clear the bar for a directional call given the falling near-term estimates and lack of a dated catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction over the next 12 months. The stock is cheap on assets but the earnings recovery is undated, and near-term estimates are falling.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted FAD turns clearly positive on a trailing basis, restoring the variable dividend/buyback (buyback activity meaningfully above the $10M/quarter Q1 pace).
- Two consecutive quarters of positive estimate revisions across current-year and next-year windows, alongside sustained lumber composite above ~$450/MBF and OSB recovery.
- A dated, quantifiable Climate Solutions/CCS monetization framework (recurring carbon/CCS revenue, not one-off easement sales) that lets the market credit the optionality.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A prolonged CBA work stoppage in summer 2026 that materially disrupts peak-season Wood Products output.
- Housing starts declining below ~1.2M SAAR on a sustained basis, pushing the recovery beyond the current horizon and forcing a base-dividend reassessment.
- Adjusted FAD remaining negative for multiple additional quarters while the balance-sheet cash used to fund the dividend continues to erode.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release and the June 25, 2026 Reg FD guidance disclosure (Q2 EBITDA guidance), with the May 31, 2026 CBA expiration as an immediate operational watch item.

## Conclusion

Weyerhaeuser is a high-quality, hard-asset business at a cyclical trough, trading at roughly two-thirds of net asset value with a strong balance sheet and genuine embedded optionality in Climate Solutions/CCS and engineered wood. Those attributes cap downside and reward patience. But the Q1 2026 beat was almost entirely non-operating, underlying Wood Products EBITDA fell 56%, adjusted FAD is negative while the dividend is funded by asset sales, and near-term estimates are being cut. The asset value is real; the catalyst to close the discount is not yet dated. We maintain HOLD at an overall score of 5.35, essentially flat with the prior 5.2, and would look to upgrade on a confirmed FAD inflection or a durable estimate-revision turn, and downgrade on a CBA disruption or a further leg down in housing.
