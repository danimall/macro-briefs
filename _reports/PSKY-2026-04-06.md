## Executive Summary

Paramount Skydance Corporation is a newly formed media conglomerate (August 2025) created through the merger of Paramount Global and Skydance Media, now controlled entirely by the Ellison family through non-voting/voting Class A/B share structure. The company operates CBS, Paramount Pictures, Nickelodeon, MTV, Paramount+ (78.9M subscribers), and other legacy media assets generating ~$29.4B in pro forma 2025 revenue and ~$3.08B adjusted OIBDA. The core business had mixed results: Paramount+ turned profitable, but linear TV advertising fell 11%, FCC licenses were reclassified to finite-lived (a tacit admission of structural decline), and GAAP results were distorted by $900M+ in restructuring charges.

The primary risk is the pending ~$110B acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, which would layer ~$57.5B in new debt financing onto PSKY's existing ~$15B in debt, creating a combined entity with approximately $79B in net debt. Fitch has already downgraded PSKY to junk. Analyst estimates are falling across every time horizon — current year EPS consensus has dropped 27% in 90 days. The WBD shareholder vote is scheduled for April 23, 2026 with a targeted July close. Public Class B shareholders have zero voting rights and cannot influence this or any strategic decision. At $9.52, the stock trades 54% below its 52-week high and below the analyst low target of $10. The risk/reward profile is dominated by binary merger outcomes and credit deterioration.

## Company Overview

PSKY operates three segments: **TV Media** (~58% of revenue) encompassing CBS, cable networks, and owned stations; **Direct-to-Consumer** (~28%) including Paramount+ and Pluto TV; and **Filmed Entertainment** (~14%) through Paramount Pictures and Skydance's production capabilities. The company holds significant sports rights (NFL, NCAA, UEFA Champions League, PGA) with $32B in off-balance-sheet programming commitments.

The corporate structure stands out for its governance design. All voting Class A shares are held by Harbor Lights Entertainment, controlled ~77.5% by Lawrence and David Ellison. Public investors hold exclusively non-voting Class B shares. The company avails itself of controlled company exemptions from Nasdaq governance requirements. David Ellison serves as Chairman and CEO; Jeffrey Shell (former NBCUniversal CEO) is President; Dennis Cinelli was appointed CFO in January 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is negative.** Pro forma FY2025 revenue of ~$29.4B declined 3% from $30.3B in 2024. The decline was concentrated in advertising (-11%, driven by loss of Super Bowl and lower political spending) and theatrical (-23%, weaker film slate). Affiliate and subscription revenue grew 4%, partially offsetting declines through streaming growth.

**Profitability is thin and distorted.** Combined FY2025 adjusted OIBDA of ~$3.08B was essentially flat versus $3.12B in 2024. GAAP operating results show a $(95)M loss in the Successor period (Aug 7-Dec 31) after $650M restructuring, $157M FCC license impairments, and inflated D&A from pushdown accounting ($590M in 5 months versus $392M for full FY2024). Free cash flow was ~$353M ($649M operating cash flow minus $296M capex), pressured by $459M in restructuring/transformation cash costs.

**The balance sheet is already stretched before WBD.** Total debt stands at $14.98B (face value) against $3.27B cash, producing ~$11.7B net debt. The weighted average interest rate is 5.17%. Near-term maturities are manageable ($433M in 2026, repaid; $584M in 2027; $1.0B in 2028), but $1.6B in junior subordinated debentures reset to floating rates in early 2027 (SOFR + ~3.9%), which at current SOFR levels would increase the coupon by 100-200bp. The credit facility covenant requires a maximum 4.5x leverage ratio from March 2026; the company met its 4.75x test at year-end.

**2026 cash flow will deteriorate.** Management guided for up to ~$800M in transformation costs during 2026 (severance, IT migration, professional fees). Combined with ongoing restructuring and the pending WBD transaction costs, free cash flow could approach zero or turn negative.

**Earnings estimates are in freefall.** Current year EPS consensus: $0.77, down 25% in 30 days and 27% in 90 days. Next year: $0.94, down 16% in 90 days. Next quarter estimates collapsed 50% in 30 days ($0.32 → $0.17). The trailing EPS is -$0.73. These revisions reflect both the operational challenges and the WBD deal uncertainty.

**Earnings surprise record is poor.** Two of the last four quarters missed estimates, including a massive Q4 2025 miss (-$0.12 actual vs. -$0.01 estimate) and Q3 2025 (-$0.12 vs. +$0.39 expected). The pattern shifted from modest beats in H1 2025 to large misses in H2 2025, suggesting deteriorating visibility coinciding with the Skydance integration.

## Growth Analysis

**Streaming profitability is the single bright spot.** Paramount+ generated $230M combined adjusted OIBDA in 2025 versus a $(497)M loss in 2024, a $727M swing. Subscriber growth to 78.9M (+2.8M YoY) with 20% revenue growth demonstrates the streaming business can scale. However, at 78.9M subs versus Netflix's 300M+, the platform lacks the scale advantages of larger competitors.

**Linear TV is in structural decline.** The FCC license reclassification from indefinite to finite-lived (30-year amortization) creates a permanent $82M+ annual amortization drag and constitutes management's own acknowledgment that broadcast television's useful life is limited. Cable affiliate revenue erosion and advertising declines will continue as cord-cutting accelerates.

**The WBD acquisition is the growth thesis, but it depends entirely on leverage rather than organic improvement.** The combined entity would control HBO, Warner Bros., DC Comics, CNN alongside CBS, Paramount Pictures, Nickelodeon — one of the deepest content libraries in entertainment. Management targets $6B in cost synergies over three years. If achieved, this would meaningfully improve OIBDA. But the growth case depends entirely on synergy execution at unprecedented scale while managing ~$79B in debt during a period of elevated interest rates and economic uncertainty.

**Sports rights provide near-term revenue visibility but long-term cost risk.** $32B in programming commitments are largely non-cancellable, providing distribution leverage but fixing costs regardless of viewership trends.

## Valuation Assessment

At $9.52 with trailing EPS of -$0.73, the trailing P/E is meaningless (-13x). Forward P/E on consensus $0.77 current-year EPS is ~12.4x, but this estimate has been falling rapidly and the H2 2025 miss pattern raises questions about achievability. On a next-year basis ($0.94 consensus, also declining), the forward P/E is ~10.1x.

Against peers: Disney trades at 16.7x, Netflix at 37.1x, TKO at 92.5x. PSKY's discount reflects the leverage overhang, binary deal risk, and governance structure. On an EV/EBITDA basis, with ~$21.4B enterprise value ($10.7B market cap + $11.7B net debt, pre-WBD) against ~$3.1B OIBDA, the multiple is ~6.9x — cheap on a standalone basis but irrelevant if the WBD deal closes and transforms the capital structure entirely.

The analyst consensus target is $13.07 (median $12), with a range of $10-$20. Seven of twenty analysts have Sell/Strong Sell ratings; only two rate it Buy. The stock already trades below the analyst low target of $10.

The 2.1% dividend yield provides marginal income support, but dividend sustainability is questionable given negative GAAP earnings, deteriorating FCF, and the potential WBD close.

## Competitive Landscape

PSKY holds competitive assets worth noting by name: CBS (#1 US broadcast network), Paramount Pictures (112-year legacy studio), blue-chip sports rights, and iconic franchises (Top Gun, Mission: Impossible, Sonic, SpongeBob, Star Trek, Transformers). Paramount+ streaming has reached viable scale with a profitability turn.

The challenge is that every larger competitor — Netflix ($390B market cap), Disney ($170B), Amazon, Apple, Comcast — has substantially more financial resources and/or tech-platform advantages. Netflix's 300M+ subscriber base generates network effects and content ROI advantages that 78.9M Paramount+ subscribers cannot match. Disney's parks/experiences provide diversified revenue PSKY lacks.

The WBD combination would create a combined entity that addresses the scale gap, bringing together HBO (premium content quality leader), Warner Bros. (film studio depth), DC (IP franchise pipeline), and CNN (news) with PSKY's existing portfolio. The strategic logic for combination is sound; the execution risk at $79B in net debt is severe.

## Risk Assessment

**WBD Acquisition — Primary Risk.** The $110B deal would create ~$79B in net debt on a combined entity generating perhaps $10-12B in adjusted OIBDA (before synergies). That's 6.5-8x leverage. Fitch's junk downgrade is the first institutional credit action. The $7B regulatory termination fee if the deal fails on antitrust grounds creates a binary outcome: close (massive leverage, credit deterioration, potential equity dilution) or fail ($7B liability on a $10.7B market cap company). DOJ subpoenas have been reported. The WBD shareholder vote on April 23 is the near-term catalyst.

**Governance Disenfranchisement.** Class B shareholders cannot vote on anything. The Ellison family controls all board appointments and strategic decisions, including the WBD acquisition. The $300M Oracle Cloud deal signed months after close — with Larry Ellison's Oracle as counterparty — illustrates the related-party conflict potential. Investors in PSKY are pure economic passengers with no governance recourse.

**Integration Complexity.** The Skydance-Paramount merger closed just 8 months ago. Now a second, far larger integration is being layered on. $650M in restructuring charges in 5 months, plus $800M targeted for 2026, signals enormous organizational disruption. Three-way integration (Paramount legacy + Skydance + WBD) across studios, networks, streaming, distribution, and corporate functions has no close operational precedent.

**Linear TV Structural Decline.** Advertising down 11%, FCC licenses reclassified to finite-lived, cable subscriber erosion ongoing. These trends are accelerating.

**Credit Deterioration.** $1.6B in junior subordinated debentures reset to floating rates in 2027. At current SOFR levels plus 3.9% spread, coupons could reach 7.5%+. The leverage covenant at 4.5x leaves thin margin for error.

**Macro Context.** Per the world model, the current stagflation environment with credit cascade probability at 60-68% and potential rate hikes is hostile to highly leveraged media companies with discretionary consumer exposure. Oil at $107+ increases consumer budget pressure, reducing spending on streaming subscriptions and entertainment.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- WBD deal closes successfully, creating one of the deepest content libraries in entertainment
- $6B cost synergies achieved, lifting combined OIBDA to $16-18B range
- Lawrence Ellison's personal financial guarantee and ~$24B in Gulf sovereign wealth fund commitments provide capital certainty
- Paramount+ streaming profitability proves sustainable and scales further
- Combined HBO+Paramount+ achieves 130M+ subscriber scale, approaching Netflix viability
- Stock re-rates from distressed levels toward peer multiples as integration progresses

### Bear Case
- WBD deal closes but creates an overleveraged entity ($79B net debt) entering a recession/stagflation environment with tightening credit markets
- $6B synergy target proves unachievable — cost synergies at this scale require eliminating tens of thousands of positions across union-heavy creative workforces
- DOJ blocks or imposes onerous conditions; $7B termination fee becomes a near-existential liability
- Linear TV decline accelerates faster than streaming growth can offset
- Credit cascade scenario (60-68% probability per world model) triggers refinancing crisis for junk-rated debt
- Sports rights renewals escalate costs while audience fragmentation reduces advertising CPMs
- Class B shareholders bear all economic risk with no governance input as Ellison family makes all strategic decisions
- Estimates continue falling — the 27% decline in current-year consensus over 90 days may accelerate

## Conclusion

Score trajectory: 5.2 → 4.3, now updated to 3.78, driven by further estimate deterioration (27% decline in 90 days), the Fitch junk downgrade, and advancing WBD deal risk. This update confirms the prior AVOID recommendation.

PSKY combines several elements that each independently warrant caution and collectively point to avoidance: (1) a controlled company structure where public shareholders have zero voting rights, (2) a leveraged acquisition that will create ~$79B in net debt during a stagflation/credit-tightening environment, (3) falling estimates across every time horizon, (4) a core business in structural decline (linear TV), and (5) a 7.1% short interest with 4.6 days to cover.

The streaming profitability turn is real and the content assets are valuable. But at $9.52 — already below the analyst low target — the price does not compensate for the magnitude of deal execution risk, credit risk, and governance risk. The WBD shareholder vote on April 23 is the next binary catalyst. Even successful deal completion creates a junk-rated, $79B-net-debt entity navigating the tightest credit environment since 2008. Recommendation: AVOID.
