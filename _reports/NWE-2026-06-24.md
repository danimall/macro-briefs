**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Since the August 18, 2025 announcement of an all-stock "merger of equals" with Black Hills Corporation (BKH), NWE is best analyzed as a merger-arbitrage instrument rather than a standalone regulated utility. NWE holders receive a fixed 0.98 BKH shares per NWE share, with no collar, meaning the entire downside of Black Hills' stock price flows directly to NWE holders. Both companies' shareholders approved the deal in April 2026 and the HSR antitrust waiting period has expired. The remaining gating items are Montana (MPSC) and South Dakota (SDPUC) regulatory approvals plus resolution of a FERC rehearing motion on the Colstrip Puget cost-based rates. Nebraska approved in May 2026, South Dakota settlements were reached in June 2026, and management guides to a 2H 2026 close.

At $70.95, NWE trades at a modest discount to implied deal value. With Black Hills near recent levels (BofA's June 2026 $78 price target implies roughly $76 of NWE value at the 0.98 ratio), the spread reflects residual approval and timing risk rather than a fundamental discount. The return on offer is low-single-digit spread capture if the deal closes on schedule, against meaningful downside if Montana or South Dakota approvals derail it and NWE reverts to standalone value.

The standalone fundamentals underneath the arb are mixed. Revenue grew 6.4% in FY2025 and 6.6% in Q1 2026, but net income fell in both periods, driven by a $30.9M YCGS regulatory disallowance, merger costs, rising interest expense, unrecovered Colstrip operating costs, and mild Montana weather (the company has no revenue decoupling). The data-center load-growth story in Montana — agreements with Sabey, Atlas, and Quantica scaling toward ~1,500 MW by 2030 — is a core part of the merger rationale, but it accrues largely to the combined company, not standalone NWE. We rate the stock a HOLD: own it for the arb spread if you are comfortable with the deal-break tail, but there is no independent appreciation case.

## Company Overview

NorthWestern Energy is a vertically-integrated regulated electric and natural gas utility serving approximately 850,300 customers across Montana, South Dakota, Nebraska, and Yellowstone National Park. Montana operations, covering roughly 73% of the state's land area, run through the NorthWestern Corporation subsidiary; South Dakota and Nebraska through NorthWestern Energy Public Service. The business splits into two regulated segments, electric (~79% of revenue) and natural gas (~21%), with a rate base of approximately $5.57B.

The generation fleet is about 52% carbon-free, anchored by 470 MW of owned Montana hydro (FERC-licensed, 100% carbon-free) plus wind, balanced by coal (Colstrip, Big Stone) and natural gas. Authorized Montana electric ROE is 9.65% on a 47.84% equity layer; gas authorized ROE is 9.60%. The competitive moat is regulatory: a monopoly franchise with constructive cost-tracking mechanisms (PCCAM for power costs, gas and property-tax trackers).

The merger with Black Hills will form "Bright Horizon Energy," headquartered in Rapid City, South Dakota. Black Hills is the legal and accounting acquirer under ASC 805; NWE becomes a wholly-owned subsidiary. NWE holders will own approximately 44% of the combined company and hold 5 of 11 board seats, ceding control to Black Hills designees.

## Financial Analysis

The financial pattern is revenue growth paired with earnings decline, a signature of cost pressure outrunning rate recovery:

- **FY2025**: Revenue $1,610.6M, up 6.4% from $1,513.9M. Net income fell to $181.1M from $224.1M, a 19% decline. Operating income was essentially flat ($325.8M vs. $323.3M). Non-GAAP utility margin rose 11.2% to $1,200.8M, indicating underlying franchise growth.
- **Q1 2026**: Revenue $497.6M, up 6.6%. Net income fell to $63.5M from $76.9M; diluted EPS $1.03 vs. $1.25.

The earnings drag is dominated by identifiable, largely non-recurring items: the $30.9M non-cash YCGS capital disallowance booked in Q4 2025, merger costs ($9.3M in 2025 and $3.4M in Q1 2026), rising interest expense ($150.4M in 2025 vs. $131.7M), higher depreciation, ~$48M/year of incremental Colstrip O&M not yet in base rates, and mild Q1 weather (Montana 26% warmer year-over-year). Because NWE has no revenue decoupling, weather translates directly into the income statement.

On the balance sheet, total assets are $8.46B against $2.89B of equity and ~$3.18B of long-term debt, for a D/E of ~1.93 and a current ratio of 0.72. This leverage is normal for a capital-intensive regulated utility. The Altman Z-Score of 1.04, which screens as "distress," is structurally uninformative here: capital-intensive utilities carry huge fixed PP&E, low asset turnover (~0.15), and high regulated leverage by design. Investment-grade ratings (Fitch BBB issuer/Stable, S&P BBB-/Positive) and reliable rate-regulated cash flows are the relevant solvency signals.

Cash flow is the genuine constraint. Operating cash flow ran roughly $394M in 2025 against capex of $524M, with capex rising to $683M (2026E), $643M (2027E), and $667M (2028E). This free-cash-flow deficit is funded by debt and, from 2027, equity issuance for South Dakota generation. The dividend (~$0.67/quarter, ~$161M/year) targets 60–70% of EPS, and the debt/cap target is 50–55%. The $452.2M of non-expiring federal NOLs means minimal cash taxes into 2029, a modest cushion. Earnings beat consensus in three of the last four quarters (most recent quarter +6.9%), so forward estimates carry reasonable confidence, though the prior quarter missed by 2.5%.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth is the standard regulated-utility rate-base compounding story, in the mid-single digits, supplemented by a secular tailwind in data-center load. NWE has development agreements with Sabey, Atlas, and Quantica totaling ~150 MW by late 2027, scaling to ~1,500 MW by 2030, and filed a Large New Load tariff rule in March 2026 to prevent cost-shifting to existing ratepayers. South Dakota also faces SPP-mandated capacity needs by 2030, driving the ~$300M Aberdeen gas plant and a 33 MW unit online in 2026. This is the same data-center electricity-demand dynamic now confirmed across the regulated-utility complex, where commercial load is surging at multiple operators.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed. Near-term quarterly estimates rose sharply (current quarter +26.1% over 90 days, next quarter +15.1%), but full-year and next-year estimates drifted slightly negative (current year -2.0%, next year -0.8% over 90 days). The near-term jumps reflect weather and timing normalization, not a structural acceleration; this is below the >30% full-year revision velocity that would mark an earnings inflection. Growth Prospects score modestly above the dimension's recent flat reading, reflecting the data-center optionality, but the bulk of that upside accrues to Bright Horizon Energy post-merger, not to standalone NWE.

## Valuation Assessment

On standalone metrics, NWE trades at roughly 24x trailing EPS (current price $70.95 against $2.94 trailing EPS); on consensus FY EPS of $3.73 that is ~19x forward. The peer group in Multi-Utilities trades at a median P/E of 19.3 (MDU 22.7, AVA 16.7, BKH 18.5, OGE 21.1, PNW 19.3). On forward earnings NWE is in line with peers; on trailing earnings it sits at a premium that reflects the depressed 2025 base. The 3.8% dividend yield is modestly above the peer median of 3.6%.

The valuation that matters is the deal math, not the standalone multiple. At the fixed 0.98 ratio, NWE value tracks Black Hills' stock price, implying a spread of approximately 7% to the $76 BofA-implied value before the deal closes. The analyst price-target spread ($54 low to $78.50 high, $74 median) brackets this dynamic: the high targets reflect deal completion at BKH levels, the low target reflects standalone reversion if the deal breaks.

I am scoring valuation near neutral. A cheap-looking multiple is not a buy signal absent a specific catalyst, and the catalyst here is the merger closing rather than any standalone re-rating. The arb spread is positive but thin relative to deal-break downside, and the standalone fundamentals (falling net income, full forward multiple, negative FCF) offer no independent margin of safety. Utility BUYs have historically delivered negative alpha versus the market, reinforcing caution on treating any apparent discount as edge.

## Competitive Landscape

NWE operates regulated monopolies, so competition is limited to large-customer self-generation, distributed solar, and fuel-switching. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the demand side: data-center load growth is a structural tailwind the company is actively positioning for via development agreements and a Large New Load tariff designed to insulate existing ratepayers. The 52% carbon-free portfolio is above the U.S. average and reduces transition risk relative to coal-heavy peers, though the $0 Colstrip acquisitions cut the other way.

The regulatory relationship in Montana is the swing variable. Two Montana commissioners individually intervened at FERC against the Colstrip Puget rates, and the MPSC disallowed YCGS capital costs on prudence grounds. This is a contentious commission, which tempers the competitive-position score even though the franchise itself is secure. The temporary suspension of the PCCAM 90/10 sharing mechanism (effective February 1, 2026) currently favors shareholders by removing supply-cost variance exposure that cost $8.2M pretax in 2025, but it is temporary and pending MPSC review.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger completion risk dominates.** The fixed 0.98 exchange ratio with no collar means NWE holders bear Black Hills' share-price risk in full. The FERC rehearing motion on Colstrip Puget rates carries a Q2 2026 ruling expectation; Montana remains the harder of the two outstanding state jurisdictions. A deal break would likely send NWE back toward standalone value, with a $100M termination fee receivable or payable depending on cause. The force-the-vote and no-shop provisions limit topping-bid optionality.

**Colstrip cost recovery and coal exposure.** NWE took its Colstrip stake to 55% (814 MW) for $0 cash, absorbing the unrecovered O&M offset only partially by power-sales contracts through late 2027. MATS rule compliance could require $350–665M (industry estimate, NWE's proportionate share). The coal supply contract runs to 2033 and depreciable life to 2042, a long-dated environmental and stranded-asset tail. Management deliberately housed the Puget interest in a FERC-regulated subsidiary, signaling intent to monetize via wholesale/large-load contracts rather than the Montana rate base near-term.

**No revenue decoupling** leaves earnings highly weather-sensitive, visible in the weak Q1 2026. **Labor risk** is elevated, with all union collective-bargaining agreements expiring in 2026 and under renegotiation. **Wildfire liability**, by contrast, has been structurally reduced: Montana HB490 (2025) and South Dakota SB36 (2026) curb strict-liability claims and create a rebuttable presumption of reasonableness if an approved mitigation plan is followed, a differentiator versus California-style inverse-condemnation exposure.

The earnings decline is largely noise rather than deterioration, dominated by the one-time items above while underlying utility margin grew double digits. Normalized earnings power appears intact. Higher-for-longer rates remain a generic headwind to the negative-FCF financing model.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contradicting signal. Near-term ATM IV is 23.7% against 23.0% trailing historical vol, fairly priced, with IV in only the 19th percentile of its own chain. The put/call skew is balanced (3.0%), the put/call OI ratio is extremely low at 0.04, and the term structure is flat (near 23.7% ≈ far 23.3%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. ATM IV ticked up 3.8 points since the prior snapshot (23.6% → 27.4%), modestly reflecting approaching merger-approval dates, but nothing in the options market suggests informed positioning for a deal break. Short interest is low at 4.1% of float (4.6 days to cover), consistent with the market treating this as a likely-to-close arb rather than a contested situation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($76–78):** The merger closes on schedule in 2H 2026 with Black Hills near or above current levels. Montana and South Dakota approvals come through, the FERC rehearing motion is resolved favorably, and NWE holders convert at the 0.98 ratio into Bright Horizon Energy stock worth ~$76 or more if Black Hills re-rates on the data-center value-creation thesis BofA cites. Spread capture plus any BKH appreciation drives mid-to-high single-digit returns over six months.

**Bear Case ($54–58):** Montana regulatory friction derails or materially conditions the merger. The MPSC, already contentious (YCGS disallowance, FERC intervention), imposes conditions Black Hills rejects, or the deal slips well into 2027. NWE reverts to standalone value, where falling net income, no decoupling, a full forward multiple, and a negative-FCF financing model offer no support. The stock trades back toward the low-$50s 52-week low and the analyst floor of $54.

**Base Case (~$74):** The deal closes in 2H 2026 at roughly the median analyst target, delivering low-single-digit spread capture from the current price, plus the ~3.8% dividend in the interim. This is the most-likely outcome given Nebraska approval, the South Dakota settlement, and management's reaffirmed timeline, but the return is modest relative to the deal-break tail.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the position is worth holding for arb spread capture if you are comfortable with the deal-break downside, but it offers no standalone appreciation case and the spread is thin.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Montana MPSC merger approval and favorable resolution of the FERC Colstrip rehearing motion, removing the dominant remaining gating risk while the spread remains 5%+.
- Black Hills re-rates higher (e.g., data-center value creation confirmed in BKH results), widening the implied NWE value above $78 at the 0.98 ratio.
- A standalone scenario emerges where forward EPS estimates inflect upward (full-year revisions turning positive >5%) on confirmed data-center rate-base additions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Montana or South Dakota regulators impose conditions Black Hills publicly rejects, or either party signals deal termination.
- The merger timeline slips materially beyond 2H 2026 with no clear path, leaving NWE exposed to standalone fundamentals at a premium multiple.
- A standalone re-emergence coincides with deteriorating credit metrics or a dividend-coverage shortfall as the FCF deficit widens.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoints are the Q2 2026 FERC rehearing ruling, the Montana MPSC and South Dakota SDPUC merger decisions (expected through 2H 2026), and the next quarterly earnings release. At close, the position converts to a Black Hills (Bright Horizon Energy) holding that requires fresh analysis on the combined company.

## Conclusion

NorthWestern Energy is a merger-arbitrage situation rather than a utility holding. With both shareholder votes secured, HSR cleared, Nebraska approved, and South Dakota settled, the probability-weighted path points to a 2H 2026 close at roughly the median analyst target, delivering modest spread capture plus the dividend. The downside if Montana balks reverts to a standalone business with falling net income, no decoupling, a full forward multiple, and a negative-FCF financing model that offers no independent margin of safety. The thin spread against that tail, combined with the structural negative-alpha history of utility positions, keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY. Investors already holding for the arb can stay the course while monitoring the Montana and FERC milestones; new capital has little reason to chase the spread at current levels. The marginal score improvement (5.1 → 5.2) is consistent with this reading: the deal de-risking through Nebraska and South Dakota offsets the standalone earnings softness, leaving the overall picture roughly stable.
