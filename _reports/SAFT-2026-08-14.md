## Executive Summary

Safety Insurance stopped trading on its fundamentals on July 23, 2026, when it signed a definitive agreement to be acquired by MAPFRE U.S.A. Corp. for $105.00 per share in cash. The stock gapped from $72.94 to $103.20 on July 24 and has traded in a $103.00-$103.75 band for fifteen consecutive sessions on declining volume. Every valuation question about SAFT now reduces to one: what is the probability the deal closes, and is 1.42% of gross spread plus a $0.92 quarterly dividend adequate compensation for a roughly 21-30% break?

The answer, on my arithmetic, is no. At $103.53 the gross spread is $1.47. Assuming three quarterly dividends over a nine- to ten-month regulatory review, total return on completion is $4.23, or 4.09% — about 4.9% annualized. A regulatory break returns the stock toward the $72.94 unaffected level, cushioned by MAPFRE's $111.8M reverse termination fee (roughly $7.62/share pre-tax, ~$6.02 after a 21% tax) plus accrued dividends, which plausibly supports something near $80. Probability-weighting an 88% completion gives 0.88 × $107.76 + 0.12 × $82.76 = $104.76, a +1.2% expected total return over nine months, or roughly 1.6% annualized. The spread does not clear a plausible cash yield, let alone compensate for a binary tail. [unverified — no cash/T-bill rate or funds-rate figure appears in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

The reason the spread is 1.42% rather than 0.3% is specific and does not resolve quickly. MAPFRE is named in Safety's own 10-K as its principal competitor in all three major lines. The combination pairs the #1 and #4 Massachusetts private passenger auto writers (18.3% + 9.4% = 27.7%), the #1 and #2 commercial auto writers (13.0% + 11.8% = 24.8%), and the #1 and #3 homeowners writers (10.6% + 7.0% = 17.6%). Closing requires both HSR expiration and Massachusetts Form A change-of-control approval. Federal screens probably place the deal in "moderately concentrated" territory rather than presumptive illegality — ΔHHI of +344 in PPA and +307 in commercial auto, with post-merger shares under the 30% threshold — but state insurance holding company competitive-standard tests carry materially lower market-share triggers, and both parties exceed them by wide margins in PPA. The negotiated fee asymmetry (a 7.25% reverse fee expressly triggered in part by failure to obtain timely regulatory approvals, versus a 3.0% company fee) is the strongest market-implied evidence that counsel on both sides identified real approval risk. A large holder acted on the same view: SRB Corp, a >10% beneficial owner, sold approximately 1.24 million shares on July 27 at $103.05-$103.09, taking about 98% of deal value in cash rather than carrying the remaining $1.40 plus dividends.

Underneath the deal, the standalone business is weakening in a way that makes the break scenario worse than a simple return to $72.94 implies. H1 2026 direct written premium fell 0.5%, private passenger auto policy counts fell 8.8%, homeowners counts fell 4.0%, and the Massachusetts PPA rate-filing cadence decelerated from +5.3%/+5.1% in 2025 to +1.3%/+3.3% in 2026. H1 net income was $20.2M against $50.8M a year earlier. Trim into strength.

## Company Overview

Safety underwrites property and casualty insurance exclusively in Massachusetts, New Hampshire and Maine through four licensed subsidiaries, with roots in the Massachusetts market since 1979. FY2025 direct written premium of $1,278.6M breaks down as private passenger auto $701.4M (54.9%), homeowners $322.2M (25.2%), commercial auto $194.1M (15.2%), business owners $32.5M (2.5%), and the remainder in dwelling fire and umbrella. Geographic concentration is extreme: Massachusetts generated $1,204.8M, or 94.2% of DWP. New Hampshire ($57.7M) and Maine ($16.1M) remain immaterial to the consolidated result after the out-of-state expansion (NH since 2008, ME since 2016).

Distribution is the franchise. Safety sells only through independent agents (797 agents across 1,063 offices) in a state where independent agencies hold 68.8% of the personal lines market versus roughly 39% nationally. Account density supports the model: 60.9% of PPA exposures carry a non-auto policy and 81.2% of homeowners policyholders carry a matching auto policy. Both metrics have declined three consecutive years (auto cross-sell 64.5% → 61.3% → 60.9%).

Two regulator-conferred assets bear on the valuation. Safety is one of only four CAR-appointed servicing carriers for the Massachusetts commercial auto residual market, reappointed for a further five-year term effective January 1, 2027, which partly underpins its #1 commercial auto ranking. Massachusetts is a heavily administered market: rates require Division of Insurance review, CAR sets residual-market rates, and insurers may charge MAIP policyholders only the lower of the MAIP rate or their own voluntary rate. That structure caps upside but also constrains destructive price competition and rewards incumbent expertise. A.M. Best cites this expertise as a positive and Massachusetts PPA concentration as an offsetting negative; it affirmed the A (Excellent) financial strength rating on July 15, 2026, eight days before the merger announcement.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported trajectory.** Revenue grew from $797.6M (2022) to $1,263.7M (2025); net income moved $46.6M → $18.9M → $70.7M → $99.3M, with combined ratios of 97.2%, 107.7%, 101.1% and 99.0%. The 2023 trough reflected $41.2M of catastrophe losses plus auto severity inflation.

**Underwriting quality is weaker than the headline.** Two tailwinds flatter reported combined ratios.

First, prior-year reserve releases: $47.4M (2023), $51.9M (2024), $44.6M (2025), $21.1M (H1 2026). Decomposing FY2025 — current-accident-year incurred losses of $841.7M against ~$1,139.0M of net earned premium is a 73.9% current-year loss ratio, which with a 29.0% expense ratio implies an ex-development combined ratio near 102.9%. The same math on FY2024 gives roughly 106.2%. H1 2026 reported 104.5%; adding back 3.62% of releases gives ~108.1%, and removing $42.7M of Q1 winter-storm losses (7.33% of NEP) leaves ~100.8%. Q2 2026 standalone: 95.7% reported, ~99.3% ex-development.

The counter-argument is substantive and I weight it. All ten prior accident-year cohorts (2015-2024) show cumulative redundancies of $44.6M to $159.8M, and reserves are carried above the actuarial midpoint at both dates — $612.3M within a $573.5M-$640.4M range at 12/31/2025 (58.0% of range width) and $657.6M within $616.5M-$685.4M at 6/30/2026 (59.6%). If initial loss picks are systematically conservative, the ex-development figure overstates true accident-year cost. A reasonable normalized combined ratio sits between the two readings, roughly 100-102%. On that basis, pre-tax earnings are carried primarily by investment income, not underwriting.

Second, catastrophe experience. MD&A reported no material catastrophe losses in 2024 or 2025. That reversed immediately: two January-February nor'easters produced $42.7M of losses and a Q1 2026 net loss of $(14.3)M. Effective first-event exposure is larger than headline retention — reinsurers take 85% of the 2026 first layer, so Safety retains its $80M attachment plus 15% of a $120M layer, approximately $98M, or ~11% of 6/30/2026 equity of $876.7M.

**Investment portfolio.** Invested assets of $1,687.5M at 12/31/2025: fixed maturities $1,315.5M (77.9%), equities $221.0M (13.1%), other invested assets (private credit and equity limited partnerships) $151.0M (9.0%). Net investment income rose 12.6% to $62.7M at a 4.0% net effective yield. Management sold below-investment-grade bank loans and bought investment-grade CLOs during 2025, lifting the investment-grade share to 91.4% from 83.2%. The ratings migration helps regulatory capital, but asset-backed instruments are now 52.6% of fixed maturities and "other ABS" grew from $63.7M to $169.5M — risk was transformed as much as reduced, since senior CLO tranches behave differently from corporates under liquidity stress. AOCI was −$28.8M at 6/30/2026 (unrealized bond losses).

**Capitalization — and a screen to discard.** Standardized screens misread this company badly. An Altman Z-Score of 1.36 with zero working-capital and EBIT components is a model-misapplication artifact; Altman was calibrated on manufacturers and has no valid mapping to an insurance balance sheet. The screened 1.77 debt/equity is total liabilities over equity, dominated by $761.7M of gross loss reserves and unearned premium. Actual borrowings are $50M drawn on a Citizens Bank facility at SOFR + 1.25%, upsized from $50M to $100M and extended to June 9, 2031 on June 9, 2026 — debt/equity of 5.6% and debt/total capital of 5.3%. Statutory surplus was $833.4M with $83.1M of statutory net income, all IRIS ratios within normal range for 2023-2025, and all subsidiaries above every RBC action level.

The genuine balance-sheet exposures are insurance-specific and quantified: catastrophe accumulation (~$98M first-event, ~11% of equity), reserve adequacy, and $169.6M due from CAR at 12/31/2025 — roughly 19% of equity — where Safety also bears a share of pool deficits allocated by voluntary market share and can be reallocated an insolvent member's share.

**Trailing multiple reconciliation.** The quoted trailing P/E of 22.21 does not reconcile to the quoted $6.70 EPS at $103.53 (that would be 15.5x). The $6.70 is FY2025 GAAP EPS; the 22.21 uses trailing twelve months. Reconstructing: FY2025 net income $99.3M less H1 2025 $50.8M gives $48.5M for H2 2025, plus H1 2026 $20.2M equals $68.7M TTM, or roughly $4.68 per share on 14.68M shares — $103.53 ÷ $4.68 = 22.1x. That reconciles the field and clarifies what buyers are paying today: 22x depressed trailing earnings against a P&C peer median of 15.1x.

## Growth Analysis

The most decision-relevant fundamental finding is that all of Safety's premium growth is rate, the unit base is shrinking, and the rate lever is weakening.

Total direct written exposures were 790,817 in 2025, down 1.1%, after +9.8% in 2024 and +12.3% in 2023. PPA exposures fell 2.2%, homeowners 0.1%, business owners 3.7%; only commercial auto grew (+2.8%). With DWP up 7.2% against exposures down 1.1%, implied average premium per exposure rose approximately 8.4% (1.072 ÷ 0.989 − 1).

The trend accelerated in 2026. H1 DWP was −0.5%, with PPA policy counts −8.8% and homeowners −4.0%, partly offset by commercial auto +2.7%. Massachusetts PPA rate filings decelerated from +5.3% (Jan 2025) and +5.1% (Jul 2025) to +1.3% (Jan 2026) and +3.3% (Jul 2026).

The earned-premium lag is about to bite. NEP grew 12.7% in 2025 (~$1,010.8M to ~$1,139.0M) because 2024's +20.4% written growth was still earning through. H1 2026 NEP was $582.6M; as a labeled sensitivity rather than a forecast, doubling that gives ~$1,165M for 2026, only +2.3%. With written premium now flat to declining and rate decelerating, 2027 earned premium declines absent renewed pricing.

Management attributes part of the count decline to deliberately cancelling underperforming agency relationships, which Item 1 confirms is an annual practice. Intentional culling and competitive share loss produce identical reported numbers with opposite implications for future margin, and the H1 2026 data cannot separate them. In a break scenario, an investor is left holding a shrinking book with an unresolved question about whether the shrinkage is strategic.

Growth prospects score 3.5: flat-to-declining earned premium, decelerating rate, and shrinking units, with the acquisition rendering standalone growth largely academic.

## Valuation Assessment

**What MAPFRE is paying.** Using 6/30/2026 balances (equity $876.7M, 14,683,137 shares, goodwill $17.1M, intangibles $6.3M): BVPS $59.71, TBVPS $58.11. At $105.00 that is 1.76x book, 1.81x tangible book, 1.70x ex-AOCI book, 15.7x FY2025 GAAP EPS of $6.70, and 18.4x FY2025 non-GAAP operating EPS of $5.71. The $1.54B headline is equity value ($105.00 × 14,680,482 = $1,541.5M); adding $50M of drawn debt gives EV near $1.59B.

That is a full price for a business earning roughly 11% ROE in its best recent year with a normalized combined ratio at or slightly above 100%. It is most consistent with MAPFRE paying for strategic assets — agency appointments, market share, CAR servicing-carrier status, and expense-ratio leverage against its existing Massachusetts platform (Safety's expense ratio was 29.0% in 2025 and 30.2% in 2024) — rather than for a standalone earnings stream.

**What the buyer of the stock today is paying.** At $103.53: 1.73x book, 1.78x tangible book, 22.1x TTM earnings against a peer median of 15.1x (UFCS 10.1x, HCI 8.1x, STC 15.1x, PRA 19.6x). The premium to peers is entirely the deal. There is no fundamental valuation case at this price; the ceiling is contractual.

**Arbitrage arithmetic (all inputs shown).**

- Gross spread: $105.00 − $103.53 = $1.47 = 1.42% of price
- Quarterly dividend $0.92, declared August 5, 2026, record September 1, payable September 15

| Scenario | Gross return | Approx. annualized |
|---|---|---|
| Close ~6 months, 2 dividends ($1.84) | ($1.47 + $1.84) ÷ $103.53 = **3.20%** | ~6.4% |
| Close ~10 months, 3 dividends ($2.76) | ($1.47 + $2.76) ÷ $103.53 = **4.09%** | ~4.9% |

Break downside: return toward the $72.94 unaffected close is −29.6% from $103.53. The reverse fee adds $111.8M ÷ 14.68M = $7.62/share pre-tax, ~$6.02 after tax at 21%, plus accrued dividends — supporting a post-break price plausibly near $80, or −22.7%.

**Implied completion probability.** Solving single-period, ignoring discounting:

- No dividends, no fee: p × 105 + (1−p) × 72.94 = 103.53 → p = 30.59 ÷ 32.06 = **95.4%**
- Three dividends in both branches: p × 107.76 + (1−p) × 75.70 = 103.53 → p = 27.83 ÷ 32.06 = **86.8%**
- Dividends plus after-tax reverse fee in the break branch: p × 107.76 + (1−p) × 81.72 = 103.53 → p = 21.81 ÷ 26.04 = **83.8%**

The market is discounting roughly 84-95% odds. My own estimate is near the middle — call it 85-90%. Massachusetts approved MAPFRE's 2008 Commerce Group acquisition, MAPFRE has a $111.8M incentive to push the approval through, and state regulators may impose conditions rather than block outright. Against that, this is a horizontal combination of the top two commercial auto writers in a single state, where the merged entity would hold more than twice the share of the next competitor, and no sell-side work exists on the regulatory path.

At p = 0.88 over nine months with three dividends: 0.88 × 4.09% + 0.12 × (−22.7%) = **−1.12%**. At p = 0.92: +0.75%. At p = 0.95: +2.15%. The position needs very high completion confidence to be attractive on a risk-adjusted basis, and that level of confidence is not supportable given an unread Massachusetts competitive-standard statute, an unfiled merger proxy, no confirmed outside date, and a fee structure that allocates regulatory risk to the buyer at 2.4x the target's fee.

Valuation score 3.2: bounded upside of 1.4% plus carry against an open 21-30% tail, at a multiple that only the deal justifies.

## Competitive Landscape

Level is respectable; trajectory is not. Safety ranks #4 in Massachusetts PPA (9.4%), #1 in commercial auto (13.0%, including its residual-market servicing share), and #3 in homeowners (7.0%). It sits behind Progressive (12.1%) and GEICO (11.5%) in PPA — both national direct writers that have taken meaningful share in an independent-agency state. Item 1 is candid that since 2008 several entrants have arrived "including some that would be able to sustain significant losses in order to acquire market share."

The deteriorating markers are internal and consistent: three consecutive years of declining cross-sell in both auto and homeowners, PPA counts down 8.8% in H1 2026, and rate deceleration. The durable advantages are the CAR servicing-carrier appointment (renewed through the five-year term effective January 1, 2027), the agency density strategy of being top-two in each appointed agency, and 44 profitable years in 45. The April 2024 FAIR Plan restructuring into a stand-alone risk-bearing entity removed a source of homeowners residual-market volatility from future results, a modest quality-of-earnings improvement.

Competitive position 4.5, unchanged from the prior review. The structural assets are real; the direction of the account base is unfavorable, and trajectory carries more weight than level.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Regulatory non-approval — the dominant risk.** Both HSR expiration and Massachusetts Form A approval are closing conditions. ΔHHI of +344 (PPA), +307 (commercial auto) and +148 (homeowners) follow directly from disclosed shares. Federal screens likely fall short of a structural presumption; the state competitive-standard test is a separate and lower-threshold hurdle, and the operative Massachusetts statutory text has not been verified. Absolute pre- and post-merger HHI levels depend on assumptions about unlisted carriers and should be treated as indicative.

**2. Break downside exceeds a simple return to $72.94.** Standalone fundamentals have deteriorated since the unaffected price was set: DWP −0.5%, PPA counts −8.8%, H1 net income $20.2M versus $50.8M. Offsetting, the reverse fee adds ~$6/share after tax to book, and the deal itself establishes that at least one strategic acquirer valued the franchise at 1.76x book, which would set a partial floor.

**3. Single-state, single-line concentration.** 94.2% of DWP in Massachusetts, 54.9% in PPA. A single adverse rate decision, CAR/MAIP rule change, or auto tort shift affects essentially the whole book.

**4. Catastrophe retention above headline.** ~$98M first-event exposure equals ~11% of equity; Q1 2026 demonstrated the mechanism.

**5. Reserve-release dependence.** Sub-100% reported combined ratios rely on 3.6-3.9% of NEP in favorable development. A decade of redundancies and reserves above the actuarial midpoint argue the process is conservative rather than opportunistic, but a decade of releases also implies the cushion is being drawn down.

**6. CAR counterparty concentration.** $169.6M due from CAR, ~19% of equity, with reallocation of an insolvent member's deficit share to survivors.

**7. Merger-pendency operating constraints.** No-shop and operating covenants during a review in which the incumbent market leader is the acquirer create agent and employee attrition risk. Inferred from the 10-Q's merger description; full covenant text unread.

**8. Portfolio composition.** Asset-backed instruments at 52.6% of fixed maturities, plus $151.0M in less liquid equity-method partnerships with valuation lag.

### Options Market Signal

Liquidity is thin — 36 contracts across three expirations — so read this with low confidence. Near-term ATM IV of 13.3% on a six-day tenor sits well below one-year realized volatility of 39.6% (which is dominated by the July 24 gap), and the term structure is labeled backwardated, falling to 8.5% at one month and 5.1% at three months. In a pending all-cash merger, collapsing longer-dated implied volatility is the expected signature: the stock is being priced as a bond-like claim on $105 rather than as an equity. The near-term 13.3% and the ±4.9% implied move on the six-day tenor are more likely artifacts of wide quotes on illiquid strikes than a genuine near-term event signal, and I do not read them as evidence of imminent stress.

The more informative datum is put/call open interest of 0.01 — essentially no put protection outstanding. Nobody is paying for downside insurance against a break, which means the options market offers no corroboration of a bearish deal-risk view and also no cheap hedge for holders who wish to stay. Short interest of 4.0% of float with 1.1 days to cover is consistent with routine event-driven positioning. Price moved from $70.53 to $103.60 between snapshots (+46.9%), entirely the announcement.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($108, +4.3% including dividends).** Massachusetts approves the Form A with conditions rather than blocking it, HSR clears without a second request, and the deal closes within six to nine months. Holders collect two to three $0.92 dividends plus the $1.47 spread. MAPFRE has $111.8M of contractual incentive to push the approval, has been a Massachusetts insurance owner since 2008, and can offer the Division of Insurance concrete commitments on rates, agent appointments and jobs. A well-capitalized international acquirer may be viewed as stabilizing for a market that has lost share to national direct writers. Total return 3.2-4.3%; a topping bid is possible but improbable, since MAPFRE is the highest-synergy buyer and any rival faces the same overlap problem in reverse.

**Bear case ($80, −22.7%).** The Division of Insurance applies a competitive-standard test under which both parties exceed market-share triggers by wide margins in private passenger auto, and either denies the application or extracts conditions MAPFRE will not accept. The deal terminates, MAPFRE pays the $111.8M reverse fee (~$6.02/share after tax), and the stock resets toward 1.2-1.3x a book value of $59.71 plus the fee cushion. Standalone earnings power is the problem: earned premium flat in 2026 and declining in 2027, a normalized combined ratio of 100-102%, and pre-tax income resting on $62.7M of net investment income against a portfolio carrying −$28.8M of AOCI. A second consecutive active winter would compound it — Q1 2026 showed $42.7M of storm losses turning a quarter negative.

**Base case ($105, +1.4% price, +4.1% total return over ten months).** The deal closes but the review runs long, and total return annualizes to a modest single-digit figure. Probability-weighting an 88% close: 0.88 × $107.76 + 0.12 × $82.76 = $104.76, a +1.2% expected total return.

**What decides it.** Two documents. The merger proxy (DEFM14A, not yet filed) will disclose the outside date, the regulatory-efforts covenant, the dividend covenant, management projections and the fairness opinion. The Form A filing and any Division of Insurance hearing notice will reveal how the competitive-standard question is being framed. Until both exist, the position is an unpriced bet on an unread statute.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim now, into the tight spread and the thin volume that follows deal announcements. Existing holders who bought below $80 have captured essentially all the strategic premium; the remaining $1.47 plus carry does not compensate for a 21-30% regulatory tail on a horizontal merger of the #1 and #4 auto writers in a single state. Sell 60-75% of the position at current levels and retain a residual tail position only if the mandate specifically tolerates merger-arb risk. There is no cheap hedge available — put/call open interest of 0.01 means the put market is effectively empty at this size.

**Base case target: $105 (+1.4% price; +4.1% total return with three dividends).** Bull case $108 (+4.3%) on an early close. Bear case $80 (−22.7%), derived as 1.25x BVPS of $59.71 ($74.6) plus ~$6.02/share of after-tax reverse fee.

**Timeframe.** Six to ten months to resolution, though the outside date is unconfirmed — no merger proxy has been filed and no company-guided closing timeline is available.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. HSR waiting period expires without a second request, and the Massachusetts Form A is accepted for review with no competitive-standard objection raised — this would justify pricing completion above 95% and make the carry adequate.
2. Merger proxy discloses a hell-or-high-water or best-efforts regulatory covenant obligating MAPFRE to accept remedies, together with an outside date within six months.
3. Price weakness to $99-100 on procedural noise rather than substantive regulatory opposition, which would restore a 5-6% gross spread and roughly 8-9% total return — an adequate premium for the tail.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. A second HSR request, a Division of Insurance hearing set on competitive grounds, or public opposition from the Attorney General or agent associations.
2. Any MAPFRE statement declining to accept divestiture or rate-commitment remedies, or an extension of the outside date without a corresponding fee increase.
3. Suspension of the quarterly dividend during pendency, which would remove the carry and leave only $1.47 of spread against the full tail.

**Reassessment timeline.** Immediately upon the DEFM14A filing (the single largest information gap), and at the Q3 2026 10-Q, which should update reserve ranges, policy counts and any regulatory-process disclosure. Absent those, no new information should change the position.

## Conclusion

Safety Insurance is a well-capitalized, conservatively reserved, narrowly concentrated Massachusetts underwriter that has agreed to sell itself at 1.76x book — a full price for a franchise generating roughly 11% ROE on a normalized combined ratio of 100-102%, with units shrinking 8.8% in auto and rate decelerating. Management deserves credit for the outcome: it repurchased 262,370 shares at ~$76.23 nine months before a $105 takeout (worth ~$0.51 per remaining share), it ties incentive pay to combined ratio with demonstrated downside sensitivity (CEO comp actually paid fell 74% in the 107.7% combined-ratio year), and it extracted a 44% premium plus a 7.25% reverse termination fee. Selling the company was the right capital-allocation decision available to it.

For an investor at $103.53, none of that is investable. The upside is contractually capped at $105 plus dividends — 1.4% of price appreciation, 3.2-4.1% of total return depending on timing — while the downside on a regulatory failure is 21-30%. The specific facts (a horizontal merger combining the #1 and #4 PPA writers and the #1 and #2 commercial auto writers in one state, a dual federal-and-state approval structure, a 2.4:1 fee asymmetry expressly tied to regulatory failure, an unread state competitive-standard statute, and an unfiled merger proxy) do not support the very high completion confidence the position requires to be attractive. A >10% holder reached the same conclusion and monetized 1.24 million shares four days after the announcement.

The score trajectory has declined from 6.0 to 5.2 to 4.5. The earlier declines reflected competitive and valuation deterioration in the standalone business; this one reflects a changed instrument — a bounded-upside claim with an unhedged binary tail — layered on top of continued unit attrition. REDUCE: monetize into the spread, and revisit only if the regulatory path clears or the price falls to $99-100.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report's cash/T-bill hurdle (~3.6% yield, 3.50-3.75% funds rate) and the 10-year yield of 4.63% are not supported anywhere in the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed/flagged as unverified; the probability-weighted expected-return arithmetic was also internally inconsistent (base case used the $80/−22.7% bear price in one place and $82.76/−21.1% in another) and has been reconciled to the report's own $80 bear-case level. The core "spread does not clear cash" argument relies on an unverified hurdle rate.
