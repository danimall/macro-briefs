**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Church & Dwight is a high-quality consumer-staples franchise whose reported top line understates the health of the underlying business. Q1 2026 net sales were essentially flat at $1,469.3M (+0.2% YoY), but the composition tells a different story: organic volume grew +5.3%, offset almost entirely by a self-inflicted −8.1% drag from deliberate business exits (Spinbrush, Flawless, Waterpik showerheads) and the VMS/gummy-vitamin divestiture. Gross margin expanded 140 bps to 46.4% on productivity and favorable mix. The company is being reshaped under a new, finance-led CEO (Richard Dierker, former CFO, who took over in March 2025) toward higher-growth, higher-margin, less tariff-exposed brands. On the fundamentals, this is a company executing a coherent strategy competently.

The problem is price. At ~$97.64, CHD trades at roughly 32x trailing and ~26x FY2026 consensus EPS ($3.77), a substantial premium to household-products peers Kimberly-Clark (19.6x), Clorox (15.9x), and Reynolds (15.0x). The peer-median P/E is 19.0x. That premium historically reflected CHD's superior organic-growth track record and disciplined M&A, but the growth algorithm is now mid-single-digit organic with a headline masked by portfolio surgery, and the durability of the premium brands is under scrutiny, most acutely at Waterpik, whose $644.7M trade name carried fair value at only 117% of book at the last annual test, down from 135% a year earlier. A modest miss to management's aggressive Waterpik projections would produce a material non-cash impairment charge.

We rate CHD a HOLD. The business quality is real, the balance sheet is sound, and organic volume momentum is genuine, but the valuation offers no margin of safety and there is no identified catalyst to expand the multiple from here. Consensus already sits near the current price (mean target ~$104, low $77, high $115). We would become constructive on a pullback into the low-to-mid $80s (roughly peer-adjusted fair value) or on evidence that the premium-brand portfolio, particularly Waterpik and oral care, has stabilized. This is our fourth consecutive HOLD, and the case for continuing it rests on a valuation gap that the market has been unwilling to pay through, not on deteriorating operations.

## Company Overview

Church & Dwight develops, manufactures, and markets household, personal-care, and specialty products, generating roughly $6.2B in annual revenue. The business runs three segments:

- **Consumer Domestic (~76% of sales):** Laundry, deodorizing, cleaning, and personal care. Q1 2026 sales $1,117.7M.
- **Consumer International (~19%):** Primarily personal care, growing ~4.6%. Q1 2026 sales $273.9M.
- **Specialty Products (~5%):** Sodium bicarbonate, animal nutrition, industrial chemicals. Q1 2026 sales $77.7M.

The strategic core is seven "power brands" that generate roughly 70% of net sales and profit: ARM & HAMMER, OXICLEAN, BATISTE (dry shampoo), WATERPIK (water flossers), THERABREATH (oral care), HERO (acne treatment), and TOUCHLAND (hand sanitizer). The portfolio is built as a barbell: value/staple franchises (ARM & HAMMER baking soda, cat litter, and laundry) that benefit from consumer trade-down, paired with premium, higher-margin acquired brands (TheraBreath, Hero, Zicam, Touchland) that drive above-category organic growth. The company's genuine low-cost advantage sits in the sodium-bicarbonate franchise, backed by a captive sodium-mineral supply partnership committing 240,000 tons per year.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** The Q1 2026 net-sales bridge is the most important single data point in the quarter: organic volume +5.3%, price/mix −0.3%, FX +1.1%, acquisitions (Touchland) +2.2%, and business exits/divestitures −8.1%, for a headline of +0.2%. Stripping the deliberate pruning, the underlying business is compounding volume at a healthy mid-single-digit rate. The flat headline is a portfolio decision, not demand weakness.

**Margins.** Gross margin was driven by productivity (+150 bps) and favorable business-exit/Touchland mix (+110 bps), partially offset by higher manufacturing costs including tariffs (−180 bps). Operating margin was 19.8%, down 40 bps, as SG&A rose 160 bps to 17.1% on Touchland integration and growth investments in e-commerce and international. The margin structure is healthy, and the tariff headwind is quantified rather than open-ended.

**Profitability and cash flow.** Q1 2026 net income was $216.3M ($0.91 GAAP diluted; $0.95 adjusted). FY2025 net income was $737M. The effective tax rate improved to 20.7%. Operating cash flow was $174.8M in Q1 (down from $185.7M on working-capital timing), with modest capex (~$130M planned for 2026). FCF conversion remains strong.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $9.0B; long-term debt $2.21B (all fixed-rate senior notes staggered 2027–2052); cash $503M; equity $4.19B. Leverage is modest and well-suited to a staples business with stable cash flows. A new $2.0B revolver (July 2025, maturing 2030) backs a $2.0B commercial paper program. The one caveat: ~$6.1B of the $9.0B balance sheet is goodwill and intangibles, an acquisition-driven structure that leaves reported book equity sensitive to brand underperformance.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised 4.2% in January 2026 to $0.3075/quarter (~$291M/year), extending decades of increases. Buybacks are muted: $228.9M remains on the 2021 $1B authorization, and there were zero open-market repurchases in Q1 2026 as cash was directed to Touchland (~$656M at close plus a $158.7M earnout paid April 2026). The current yield is only ~1.3%, well below the peer median of 3.9%; CHD is not an income name.

**Earnings track record.** The company has beaten consensus for four straight quarters (Q2 2025 +8.6%, Q3 2025 +8.8%, Q4 2025 +1.8%, Q1 2026 +1.0%). The beat magnitude has decelerated, with the earlier ~8–9% beats narrowing to ~1%, consistent with consensus catching up and less upside visibility.

## Growth Analysis

The genuine growth engine here is organic volume of roughly +5%, above staples-category norms, supported by the premium brands and international expansion. Management has staked its long-term compensation on three explicit growth pillars in the January 2026 "2030 Strategy Grant": ARM & HAMMER net-sales CAGR, international net-sales CAGR, and oral-care (TheraBreath) net-sales CAGR, a clear signal of where the company sees durable expansion and a useful alignment tool.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating. Current-year consensus EPS is $3.77 (+0.5% over 30 days, +0.6% over 90 days), next-year $4.05 (−1.1% over 30 days, +1.0% over 90 days). The current-quarter estimate was cut −7.7% over 90 days to $0.90, and Q2 2026 consensus (~$0.89) implies a ~5.3% YoY decline. There is no positive-revision inflection to lean on. This is a mid-single-digit EPS grower, not a re-accelerating one. The estimate-revision signal is neutral, which removes any basis for treating the stock as an inflection story.

The self-inflicted exit drag will annualize through FY2026, so the reported top line will look better in 2027 once the comparison base cleans up. That mechanical improvement is not the same as accelerating demand and does not by itself justify multiple expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

CHD trades against a household-products peer set trading at a median of 19.0x:

| Ticker | Name | P/E | Div Yield |
|--------|------|-----|-----------|
| KMB | Kimberly-Clark | 19.6 | 5.0% |
| CLX | Clorox | 15.9 | 5.0% |
| REYN | Reynolds | 15.0 | 3.9% |
| IPAR | Interparfums | 19.0 | 3.3% |
| WDFC | WD-40 | 38.0 | 1.8% |
| **CHD** | **Church & Dwight** | **~26x fwd** | **1.3%** |

Even granting CHD a deserved premium for its superior organic-growth track record and cleaner portfolio, ~26x forward for a mid-single-digit EPS grower is demanding. The implied PEG (roughly 26x / ~6% growth ≈ 4.3) offers no cushion. WD-40 at 38x is the only peer above CHD, and it is a narrow, unusually high-return niche business.

The forward P/E is not being depressed by trough earnings; this is not a cyclical-inflection setup where trailing multiples are misleading. FY2026 earnings represent normalized, growing earnings, and the multiple sits comfortably above the growth rate. A high valuation multiple on a slow-growth base, with no identified catalyst and a live impairment overhang, is a value trap in reverse: the market is paying full price for quality that is already well understood. Historically, "cheap-looking" staples that trade at premiums to their group without a growth differential tend to underperform as the premium mean-reverts.

Analyst targets (mean ~$104, median $105, low $77, high $115) cluster around the current price, offering roughly 6% upside to the mean. On a stock at 26x forward with stable-to-declining near-term estimates, analyst targets are not a "floor"; they reflect the same full-multiple optimism embedded in the price. The valuation score is 4.0: the multiple is stretched relative to the growth it delivers, with no specific mispricing to exploit.

## Competitive Landscape

CHD competes in mature, slow-growth categories against far larger peers (P&G, Colgate, Unilever, Reckitt) and increasingly against private label. Its differentiation is the barbell strategy: value brands that gain in a trade-down environment, paired with premium brands that grow above category. The current inflationary, trade-down environment helps ARM & HAMMER and XTRA but pressures discretionary premium lines, most visibly Waterpik.

Two competitive developments warrant flagging. First, the 10-K discloses that in 2025 some of the company's largest customers launched private-label brands competing directly with CHD products, an escalation of retailer private-label pressure, compounded by consumer trade-down. Second, Walmart represents ~23%+ of sales, a concentration that amplifies the private-label threat. On the positive side, the portfolio pruning is deliberately concentrating the mix in defensible, higher-margin franchises, and the sodium-bicarbonate cost advantage in the value tier is durable. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-pressured: the value franchises hold their moat, but the premium/discretionary end faces real headwinds (Waterpik distribution losses, private-label entry). We score competitive position 5.5, a solid business whose trajectory is flat rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Waterpik impairment (the live, specific risk).** Management's impairment model assumes an 8.0% discount rate, mid-single to low-double-digit revenue growth, and ~25% EBITA margins, aggressive assumptions given Waterpik oral-care sales were declining in Q1 2026, with distribution losses, trade-down, tariff pressure, and competition all cited. A modest miss to those projections could trigger a material non-cash charge. The next annual test (Q4 2026) is the key date. This is a non-cash event and would not impair the operating business, but it would be a negative headline and a validation of the bear case on premium-brand durability.

**China/tariff exposure.** CHD imports substantially all Waterpik flossers from China. In response to tariffs, it has ceased importing substantially all Waterpik flossers from China into the U.S. and is relocating manufacturing while using "surgical pricing." Tariffs already cost ~180 bps of gross margin in Q1 2026.

**Portfolio-repositioning execution.** The company is simultaneously exiting/divesting weaker businesses and integrating Touchland under a new CEO. "Strategic alternatives" reviews create both opportunity and disruption risk.

**Intangible-heavy balance sheet.** ~$6.1B goodwill + intangibles vs. $4.2B equity leaves the firm sensitive to brand underperformance, of which Waterpik is the clearest example.

Most of these risks are at least partially priced; the stock's ~3% trailing-52-week underperformance vs. the S&P 500's +19% reflects some skepticism. But the Waterpik impairment is a specific, dated, asymmetric event not fully discounted at a 26x multiple. Risk score 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 30.9% against 1-year historical volatility of 22.8% — options are modestly rich (8.1 pp spread), indicating the market is pricing some elevated uncertainty into the Q2 print (expected ~7/31/2026). The put/call skew is a mild 3.7% (slightly elevated put demand) at the 1-month tenor, flipping negative (−3.8%) at 6 months, so there is no strong directional bearish positioning further out. ATM IV collapsed from 69.4% to 30.9% since the mid-June snapshot; the prior reading was likely a data artifact, and the current 30.9% is the reliable figure. The implied ±7.0% move for the nearest expiry frames the earnings binary. Short interest is low at 5.1% of float (5.2 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The options picture is consistent with a fundamental HOLD: some earnings-event caution, no conviction directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$115).** The portfolio pruning annualizes and reported revenue re-accelerates toward the ~5% organic rate in 2027; Touchland and TheraBreath sustain above-category growth; margin expansion continues on productivity; Waterpik stabilizes and avoids impairment. The premium multiple holds or expands modestly as the quality of the reshaped portfolio becomes clearer. At ~28x on FY2027 EPS of ~$4.05, the stock reaches ~$113–115. This is the analyst high target and requires both operational execution and multiple maintenance.

**Bear Case (~$80).** Waterpik takes a material non-cash impairment in the Q4 2026 test, validating premium-brand-durability concerns; private-label entry by large customers pressures share; the premium multiple mean-reverts toward the peer group. At ~21x (still a premium to the peer median) on FY2026 EPS of $3.77, the stock trades to ~$79. This is roughly the analyst low target ($77) and represents ~18% downside.

The base case sits in between: a well-run business fully valued, drifting with earnings. At ~24x FY2026 EPS the stock is worth roughly $90, modestly below the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The business is sound and the operational execution is competent, but the valuation offers no asymmetry and there is no catalyst to close the peer premium. This is our fourth consecutive HOLD; the declining score trajectory (6.3 → 5.8 → 5.5) reflects the erosion of the growth/valuation premium justification, not operational deterioration. The current-quarter estimate cut and the narrowing beat magnitude reinforce a "fully valued, drifting" characterization.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pullback into the low-to-mid $80s (roughly 22–23x forward, still a premium to peers but with margin of safety), particularly if organic momentum holds.
- Waterpik impairment test cleared (Q4 2026) with fair-value coverage stabilizing or improving above ~120%, removing the single largest specific overhang.
- Positive estimate-revision inflection: current-year consensus rising >5% over 90 days alongside sustained ~5%+ organic volume growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material Waterpik impairment combined with evidence of broader premium-brand deterioration (TheraBreath or Touchland decelerating).
- Organic volume growth falling below ~2%, signaling the underlying engine is stalling and the portfolio surgery is not translating into share gains.
- Multiple expanding further (>30x forward) on stable earnings, which would worsen the risk/reward.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 report (~7/31/2026), which will test the ~5.3% YoY EPS-decline consensus and refresh organic-volume trends. The Q4 2026 annual impairment test is the second key date.

## Conclusion

Church & Dwight is a well-run consumer-staples franchise with a durable barbell portfolio, healthy ~5% organic volume growth, expanding gross margins, a sound balance sheet, and a management team aligned via the 2030 Strategy Grant. The issue is that at ~26x forward earnings the market already pays full price for these attributes, against a peer group at ~19x and a mid-single-digit EPS growth rate that does not justify a PEG north of 4. Layered on top is a specific, dated impairment risk in the Waterpik trade name that a full multiple does not discount.

We maintain a HOLD. We are not sellers of a quality compounder at a modest premium with genuine organic momentum, but we see no basis for buying at a valuation that offers no cushion and no catalyst. A pullback into the low-to-mid $80s, or a clean Waterpik impairment test with premium-brand stabilization, would change the calculus. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced.
