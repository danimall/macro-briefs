**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Comstock Resources is a pure-play Haynesville/Bossier natural gas producer carrying a leveraged balance sheet into a period of falling earnings estimates and a capital-intensive strategic pivot it cannot fund from internal cash flow. The stock has fallen 43% over twelve months to $13.42, sitting near its 52-week low of $12.84 and roughly 35% below the $19-22 range where insiders transacted in early 2026. The decline coincides with sharply negative estimate revisions: current-year EPS consensus has been cut 42% over 90 days, current-quarter EPS cut 78%, and the company missed its most recent quarter by 42% (Q1 2026: $0.15 actual vs. $0.26 estimate).

The bull case rests entirely on the Western Haynesville data-center power-hub thesis — a Department of Commerce-selected "Texas Natural Gas-Fired Power Generation Hub" and a NextEra partnership to supply gas for up to 8 GW of generation feeding hyperscaler demand. The structure is strategically differentiated, but none of it is contracted. The 5.2-GW hub and ~1 Bcf/d-by-2031 figures are management projections, not binding offtake agreements. The company is FCF-negative ($-450M in 2025, negative again in Q1 2026), holds $14.8M in cash against ~$3.0B debt, faces a $440M Pinnacle redemption in H1 2026, and plans $1.4-1.5B of D&C capex in 2026. The credit facility bars dividends and buybacks. Funding the vision requires sustained $4+ gas, additional asset sales, JV capital, or equity issuance.

We rate CRK AVOID with an overall score of 4.1. The data-center angle may prove transformative, but an equity holder is being asked to underwrite multi-year execution risk on a thin balance sheet, during a period of declining estimates and shrinking production, with no current cash return and hedges that cap near-term upside. The risk/reward at $13.42 is unfavorable until either gas prices sustain above strip levels or the power-hub framework converts to firm contracts.

## Company Overview

Comstock operates ~1.07 million gross acres (802,769 net) in the Haynesville and Bossier shales of North Louisiana and East Texas, one of the premier U.S. gas basins by virtue of proximity to Gulf Coast LNG export terminals, petrochemical demand, and emerging data-center power load. As of year-end 2025 the company held 7.0 Tcfe of proved reserves (PV-10 of $4.5B at SEC prices, $5.2B at year-end strip), roughly 99% natural gas, 41% developed, with a ~16-year reserve life. It runs 1,724 producing wells (959.7 net) and operates ~99% of its proved reserve base, giving it strong control over cost and timing.

The company's distinguishing feature is operational and locational rather than financial. It drills the longest laterals in the basin (41 wells at ≥15,000 ft from 2021-2025), uses "horseshoe" well designs, owns the Pinnacle midstream system serving its Western Haynesville exploration play, and carries MiQ methane certification. The strategic centerpiece is converting Western Haynesville (Anderson County, TX) into a vertically integrated gas-to-power hub: Comstock supplies gas and hosts the site, while partner NextEra develops the electrical generation that serves data-center customers. Comstock is the upstream gas supplier, not the power developer or data-center operator.

Comstock is controlled by Jerry Jones (Dallas Cowboys owner) family entities following a 2018 change of control. The CEO/Chairman (M. Jay Allison, since 1988) and President/CFO (Roland Burns, since 1990) are long-tenured and founder-aligned.

## Financial Analysis

Comstock's results swing violently with Henry Hub. Revenue moved from $3.63B (2022) to $1.57B (2023) to $1.25B (2024) and back to $2.22B (2025). Net income tracked accordingly: $1.14B, $211M, a $230M loss, and a $396M profit. The 2024 loss occurred at a $1.98/Mcf realized price; the 2025 recovery was driven almost entirely by price ($1.98 → $3.17, +60%), not volume. Gas production fell 15% to 450 Bcf (1.2 Bcf/d) in 2025.

The 2025 net income figure is flattered by non-recurring items: a $291.9M gain on the Shelby Trough/Cotton Valley divestitures ($432M net proceeds) and a $62M unrealized derivative gain. Stripping these, the underlying earnings power at $3.17 gas is considerably thinner than the headline suggests.

Q1 2026 showed net income of $112.5M ($0.38/sh per the filing, though the reported EPS surprise data shows $0.15 actual against a $0.26 estimate, a 42% miss). Realized gas rose 19% YoY to $4.27/Mcf, but production again fell 15% YoY to 97.9 Bcf on winter weather. Operating cash flow was $272M against $405M of capex, leaving the company FCF-negative for the quarter. Hedging turned into a drag: realized hedge losses of $80.4M as spot prices rose above the $3.51 swap level.

The balance sheet is the central concern. Total debt is approximately $3.0B (6.75% notes due 2029: $1.62B; 5.875% notes due 2030: $965M; revolver $397M at 3/31/26). Cash is just $14.8M. The current ratio is 0.49, debt/equity ~1.5, and the Altman Z-Score of 1.48 sits in the distress zone. The Z-Score overstates true bankruptcy risk for a reserve-rich E&P with $4.5B PV-10 and covenant compliance (leverage <3.5x, current ratio ≥1.0 adjusted), but it correctly flags a leveraged, capital-intensive equity highly exposed to gas-price troughs. Liquidity is ~$1.27B against a $2.0B borrowing base and $1.5B commitments.

The 2026 plan calls for $1.4-1.5B of D&C capex plus $100-150M of midstream, drilling 66 operated wells (59.7 net). Management states an intent to fund within cash flow "while protecting our balance sheet," but Q1 already outspent. The $440M Pinnacle/Quantum-Cactus Class B redemption due in H1 2026 adds funding pressure on top of the capex program. There is no dividend or buyback — both are restricted by the credit facility.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth story and the actual production trajectory point in opposite directions. Gas volumes fell 15% in both FY2025 and Q1 2026. The bull case rests on a future Western Haynesville ramp tied to power-hub demand, not on any demonstrated volume trend.

Estimate revisions are decisively negative, which matters more than any single data point for forward direction. Current-quarter EPS has been cut 78% over 90 days (to $0.04 from $0.17), next-quarter cut 42% over 90 days (to $0.13 from $0.23), and current-year cut 42% over 90 days (to $0.56 from $0.95). Only the next-year estimate ($1.22) has held relatively stable, down 6% over 90 days. The combination of a 42% Q1 miss followed by collapsing near-term consensus is the opposite of the positive revision velocity that would support a constructive view. Estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days are a strong buy signal; CRK is showing the inverse, with broad-based negative revisions across every near-term period.

The Western Haynesville data-center thesis is the only genuine growth catalyst, and its specifics deserve precise framing. The Department of Commerce selected the site to host a Texas Natural Gas-Fired Power Generation Hub (March 2026). NextEra is the announced partner to build generation; trade press describes a 5.2-GW hub with on-site gas ramping toward ~1 Bcf/d by 2031. These are management projections and framework arrangements, not contracted volumes. The structure — owned acreage, owned midstream, federal backing, behind-the-meter demand outlet — is differentiated, and if it converts to firm offtake it could de-risk Western Haynesville volumes and improve realized economics. But the company has turned only 30 Western Haynesville wells to sales through 2025, having spent $490M of exploratory capex in 2025 on the basin's deepest and highest-pressure wells (10,500-19,200 ft). The repeatability and economics are not yet proven at scale.

The world model's AI-infrastructure theme supports the underlying demand premise: data-center power load is real and broad-based. But the demand being real does not make this particular leveraged upstream developer the right way to express it, particularly when the offtake is uncontracted and the funding is uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

CRK trades at 6.37x trailing/forward P/E on $1.33 EPS, which optically screens cheap against the peer median of 15.9x. That cheapness is the wrong frame on two counts. First, the trailing EPS is inflated by ~$354M of non-recurring divestiture and derivative gains; normalized earnings power at current strip is meaningfully lower. Second, the relevant comparison is to the forward estimate, which is collapsing — the $0.56 current-year consensus implies a far higher forward multiple than the trailing figure, and that estimate is still being cut.

Peers present a mixed picture: CNX trades at 4.6x, MGY at 15.9x, MUR at 61.8x, with two peers (CRGY, CRC) at negative trailing P/E. The wide dispersion reflects differing gas/oil mixes, leverage, and hedge positions. CRK's discount to the median is explained by its leverage (D/E ~1.5 vs. better-capitalized peers), its FCF deficit, its lack of any cash return, and the binary nature of its forward thesis. A low multiple here reflects genuine risk, not mispricing.

Asset-based valuation provides some downside support: $4.5B PV-10 (SEC prices) and $5.2B at strip against a ~$4.0B market cap and ~$3.0B debt imply the equity is not trading at a deep discount to PV-10 net of debt. PV-10 is itself highly price-sensitive: PUD reserves swung from 2.2 Tcf (2023) to 1.0 Tcf (2024) to 4.2 Tcf (2025) purely on price revisions, and the future development cost obligation ballooned to $4.7B. A gas-price decline could de-book reserves and trigger impairments, as the $29.1M Eagle Ford impairment in 2025 illustrates.

We do not see a specific mispricing the market is missing. The low headline multiple is a value trap signature: compressed because forward earnings are falling, leverage is high, and the growth catalyst is unfunded and uncontracted. We score valuation 4.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Comstock holds a defensible operational and locational position in one of the best U.S. gas basins. The Haynesville benefits structurally from Gulf Coast LNG export growth, Mexico exports, petrochemical demand, and now AI/data-center power load. CRK's combination of owned acreage, owned Pinnacle midstream, federal power-hub designation, and the NextEra generation partnership is a vertically integrated angle few gas pure-plays can match. Its long-lateral drilling and completion design give it a genuine cost edge.

The competitive position is improving on the strategic dimension (the power-hub vision), but the company competes for acreage and services against far larger, better-capitalized operators (majors, Expand Energy/Chesapeake-Southwestern). CRK's edge is operational and locational; in a capital-intensive business during a leveraged stretch, the financial disadvantage matters. Customer concentration is notable (Enterprise 18%, Venture Global 11%, Shell 10%) but mitigated by a liquid gas market. We score competitive position 5.5, reflecting a real but financially constrained position with an improving strategic trajectory offset by capital disadvantage.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the interaction of gas-price leverage with a thin balance sheet. With ~$3B debt and a structurally FCF-negative reinvestment program, a return to 2024-level prices ($1.98) would wipe out earnings, as it did in 2024. The Pinnacle redemption, capex plan, and minimal cash balance create a funding gap that must be closed by sustained high gas prices, asset sales, JV capital, or dilutive equity. The 2024 precedent — a $100.5M private placement of 12.5M shares to Jones-controlled entities — shows the controlling shareholder can backstop, but also that dilution is a live tool.

Hedges currently cap upside rather than protect it. The 2026 book (88 Bcf swapped at $3.51, 126.5 Bcf collared $3.50/$4.35) produced an $80M realized loss in Q1 at $4.27 spot. If the data-center demand thesis spikes gas prices, near-term participation is limited.

Additional risks: Western Haynesville execution (deepest/highest-pressure wells in the basin, only 30 wells to sales); reserve/PUD price sensitivity and impairment risk; ~$740M federal and $1.2B state NOLs expected to expire unused under the Section 382 limitation; controlling-shareholder governance with combined Chairman/CEO and an aging board (63-83); and related-party transactions (operating partnerships owned by the majority stockholder, marketing/hedging fees). We score risk factors 3.0 — the binary funding and execution risks, on top of price leverage, are not fully discounted.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute near-term stress, which sits in tension with the equity's slide to 52-week lows. Near-term ATM IV is 58.6% against 59.1% historical volatility (fair, -0.4% spread), with the IV percentile at the 5th percentile of the current chain — implied vol is low relative to its own recent range. The term structure is in contango (58.6% near vs. 64.2% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. Put/call skew is balanced near-term (+0.2%) but turns negative at six months (-4.2%), indicating downside hedging demand building further out. Put/call OI ratio rose to 1.14 from 0.95. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.4%. The signal is consistent with a market that sees no imminent binary event but is accumulating longer-dated downside protection. This does not contradict the AVOID; it suggests the deterioration is viewed as a grind rather than a cliff.

The very high short interest (27.3% of float, 8.3 days to cover) is a genuine two-sided consideration: it confirms widespread bearish conviction but creates squeeze risk on any positive surprise, most plausibly a binding data-center contract.
