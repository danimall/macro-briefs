## Executive Summary

PPG Industries is a $25B global coatings company that has undergone meaningful portfolio transformation through 2024 divestitures (U.S./Canada architectural, silicas) and is now positioned as a more technology-focused, internationally oriented business. The stock trades at ~14.2x forward 2026 EPS ($7.90 midpoint guidance) with a 2.7% dividend yield and 54 consecutive years of dividend increases. Q1 2026 showed modest organic improvement (+1% organic, +6% adjusted EPS growth), and the company has announced up to 20% price increases to offset tariff-driven raw material inflation.

The fundamental challenge is that PPG's adjusted EPS actually declined in 2025 ($7.58 vs. $7.87 in 2024) despite organic growth, and 2026 guidance ($7.70-$8.10) implies only modest recovery. Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, with next-quarter estimates down 4.9% over 90 days. The stock has underperformed its peer group by approximately 51 percentage points over the proxy measurement period, and while valuation is no longer stretched, the growth trajectory doesn't support a re-rating catalyst in the near term.

PPG is a solid franchise with defensive income characteristics, appropriate for portfolios seeking quality industrial exposure with yield. It lacks the growth inflection or catalyst needed to generate meaningful capital appreciation from current levels.

## Company Overview

PPG Industries operates across three segments: Global Architectural Coatings ($3.8B, 24% of sales), Performance Coatings ($5.5B, 35%), and Industrial Coatings ($6.5B, 41%). Total 2025 revenue was $15.9B, down from $18.2B in 2023 due to divestitures rather than organic decline. Approximately 70% of sales originate outside the United States, creating significant FX and geopolitical exposure.

The company's competitive strengths center on aerospace coatings (high barriers, $315M backlog), automotive OEM technology integration (on-site services at factory facilities), and sustainability-advantaged formulations (43% of 2025 sales). Key competitors include Sherwin-Williams (dominant in North American architectural), AkzoNobel, Nippon Paint, and Axalta.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
- FY2025 revenue: $15.9B (+0.6% from continuing operations)
- Organic growth: +2% (price +1%, volume +1%, FX +1%, divestitures -3%)
- GAAP net income: $1.58B (up from $1.12B due to non-recurrence of restructuring charges)
- Adjusted EPS: $7.58 (down 4% from $7.87 in FY2024)

The adjusted EPS decline in a year of organic growth is concerning. Unfavorable product mix (lower-margin industrial volumes growing faster than high-margin performance coatings) and cost inflation offset restructuring savings and pricing.

**Cash Flow:**
- Operating cash flow: $1.94B (+39% YoY)
- Capital expenditure: $778M (elevated vs. ~$400M depreciation run rate)
- Free cash flow: ~$1.16B
- Capital returned: $1.42B ($790M buybacks + $628M dividends)

Cash generation is a clear strength, though the working capital quality signal is mixed. DSO expanded from 51 to 59 days, and working capital as a percentage of sales rose from 15.6% to 17.6%.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Long-term debt: $6.6B (up from $4.9B after $1.6B+ issuance)
- Cash: $2.2B
- Debt/equity: 1.76x
- Total indebtedness/capitalization: 47% (vs. 60% covenant limit)
- Goodwill + intangibles: $8.1B (exceeds equity of $7.9B)
- Current ratio: 1.62x

The leverage increase warrants monitoring. PPG issued significant new debt in 2025, likely funding share repurchases and the elevated capex program. While the covenant headroom is adequate (47% vs. 60% limit), the $6.6B debt load on a company generating $1.16B in free cash flow gives net debt/FCF of approximately 3.8x. For a specialty chemicals company in a rising-rate environment, this is manageable but not conservative.

**Q1 2026 Results:**
- Revenue: $3.9B (+7% YoY)
- Adjusted EPS: $1.83 (+6% YoY, beating consensus of $1.79)
- Segment EBITDA margin: >19%

## Growth Analysis

PPG's growth profile is modest and challenged by several structural factors:

**Positive drivers:**
- Aerospace: Double-digit growth, $315M backlog, debottlenecking investments underway. This is the highest-quality growth vector.
- Automotive OEM outperformance: +300bps vs. global market in Q1 2026, suggesting genuine share gains.
- Protective & marine: Double-digit organic growth from sustainably-advantaged products.
- Restructuring savings: ~$50M incremental in 2026, with $175M annualized target by 2027.

**Headwinds:**
- European industrial weakness: Soft demand with no clear recovery catalyst.
- Automotive refinish structural decline: Collision avoidance technology reducing accident frequency. Claims down high-single-digits for much of 2025.
- Organic growth guidance: Only flat to low-single-digit for FY2026.
- Estimate revisions: Next-quarter EPS estimates down 4.9% over 90 days; current-year down 1.9% over 90 days.

The estimate revision pattern is mildly negative and doesn't meet any threshold for a growth inflection signal. Consensus FY2026 at $7.87 and FY2027 at $8.62 implies approximately 10% EPS growth next year, a rate that modestly exceeds the dividend yield but remains below historical compounding rates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $111.53 with consensus FY2026 EPS of $7.87:
- Forward P/E: ~14.2x (vs. the reported trailing P/E of ~16.0x using $6.92 LTM GAAP EPS)
- Dividend yield: 2.7%
- Free cash flow yield: ~4.6% ($1.16B FCF / $25B market cap)

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 55.0x is heavily distorted by DuPont at 157.9x and losses at ALB and LYB. Among profitable specialty chemicals companies, PPG's 14-16x is reasonable for a company growing EPS low-single-digits. IFF trades at 25.7x with a 2.3% yield, suggesting some discount is warranted given PPG's lower growth. Against its closest fundamental comparable Sherwin-Williams (which trades at ~30x+), PPG's discount reflects the lower organic growth rate and more international/cyclical exposure.

**Analyst targets:** Median $125 (+12% upside), mean $122.90, range $108-$135. With 23 analysts covering (2 Strong Buy, 8 Buy, 13 Hold), the distribution leans neutral-to-positive but without strong conviction.

The valuation is fair: not cheap enough to represent a compelling entry given the growth rate, but not expensive enough to warrant avoidance. The 2.7% yield provides a floor of sorts for this Dividend Aristocrat.

## Competitive Landscape

PPG's competitive position is its strongest attribute:

1. **Aerospace coatings:** One of a handful of qualified suppliers globally. High certification barriers, multi-year qualification processes, and safety-critical applications create durable moat. The backlog signals demand well beyond current capacity.

2. **Technology differentiation:** 43% of 2025 sales from "sustainably-advantaged" products. The transition to PFAS-free and BPA-free packaging coatings creates switching costs as competitors scramble to reformulate.

3. **Global scale with local manufacturing:** The "zero Chinese suppliers among top 30 for U.S. businesses" positioning is valuable in the current tariff regime. Local production reduces cross-border exposure.

4. **Customer integration:** On-site services at automotive OEM facilities create relationship stickiness and information advantages.

5. **Weakening area — architectural:** After divesting the U.S./Canada business, PPG relies on COMEX (Mexico) and international brands where market positions are strong but not dominant.

PPG outperformed its end markets by 300+ basis points in Q1 2026 automotive OEM, suggesting active share gains rather than passive market growth. The protective & marine segment's double-digit growth on sustainability-driven reformulations also indicates competitive momentum.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Tariff/input cost inflation (HIGH probability, MODERATE impact):** Mid-single-digit COGS inflation in 2026 driven by tariffs and geopolitical disruption. PPG announced up to 20% price increases but expects "low single digit" actual realization initially. If price-cost neutrality takes longer than guided ("months, not years"), margins compress further.

2. **European macro deterioration (MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact):** PPG derives significant revenue from European architectural and automotive OEM markets that remain weak. Further deterioration would pressure the Industrial Coatings segment most directly.

3. **Automotive refinish secular decline (HIGH probability, LOW-MODERATE impact):** Collision avoidance technology adoption is accelerating. PPG management acknowledges this segment "will never be our highest-growth business." The risk is managed through diversification, but refinish remains a meaningful contributor to Performance Coatings margins.

4. **Working capital deterioration (MODERATE probability, LOW impact):** If DSO expansion reflects customer credit stress rather than administrative timing, it could impair cash conversion.

5. **CFO transition (LOW probability of disruption):** CFO Vince Morales departing. Leadership transitions add execution uncertainty.

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term IV at 37.9% is significantly rich to historical volatility of 28.6% (+9.4pp spread), with the term structure in backwardation (37.9% near-term vs. 30.0% at 6 months). This typically signals the market pricing near-term event risk, likely related to the post-earnings price adjustment still digesting. The put/call OI ratio at 0.31 is notably low (call-heavy), suggesting options positioning is actually somewhat constructive, which contradicts the IV signal. Net assessment: elevated uncertainty but not bearish positioning.

3. Dividend coverage falling below 2.0x (currently ~1.9x on FCF basis, which is already tight for a Dividend Aristocrat)

## Conclusion

PPG Industries is a well-managed global coatings franchise with genuine competitive advantages in aerospace, automotive OEM, and sustainability-driven formulations. The portfolio transformation (2024 divestitures + restructuring) positions the company for higher-quality earnings over time, and the 54-year dividend growth streak provides income stability.

However, the growth trajectory is insufficient to drive meaningful capital appreciation at current levels. Adjusted EPS declined in 2025 despite organic growth; 2026 guidance implies only modest recovery; estimate revisions are flat-to-negative; and significant macro headwinds (tariffs, European weakness, energy cost inflation) create margin uncertainty. The stock's 51-point TSR underperformance versus peers over the proxy measurement period reflects the market's correct assessment that PPG lacks a growth catalyst.

At ~14x forward earnings with a 2.7% yield, PPG is fairly valued for a low-growth industrial compounder with income characteristics. The risk/reward from current price is approximately symmetric (12% upside to median target vs. 10-15% downside in bear case). This warrants a HOLD with income orientation: own it for the dividend and optionality on margin recovery, but don't expect re-rating without a demonstrable acceleration in organic growth or a significant price dislocation.
