## Executive Summary

HCI Group is a Florida-focused property and casualty insurer that generated $22.72 in diluted EPS during 2025 — a no-major-hurricane year that demonstrated the company's extraordinary earnings leverage. At $154/share, the stock trades at 6.8x trailing earnings, roughly half its P&C peer median of approximately 15.8x. The discount reflects Florida hurricane concentration risk, but several structural factors argue the market is too pessimistic: (1) Florida's 2022 tort reform has reduced claim/litigation frequency, potentially making sub-25% gross loss ratios more achievable than history suggests, though the 2025 result benefited from zero named hurricane landfalls; (2) HCI's approximately 82.5% stake in Exzeo, which IPO'd at a ~$1.9B implied valuation, represents significant embedded value; (3) the balance sheet holds $1.2B in cash against just $68M in financial debt, with $80M in active buybacks.

The critical analytical question is whether the 2025 result represents normalized earnings power or a one-off. Historical context (2022 loss of $59M, 2024 hurricane impact of $128M) demonstrates the severity of the downside. But the combination of comprehensive reinsurance, Florida legislative reform, and the embedded Exzeo value creates a margin of safety at current prices that justifies a BUY recommendation with explicit recognition of the binary catastrophe risk.

## Company Overview

HCI Group operates as a diversified holding company headquartered in Tampa, Florida. The core insurance business (HCPCI and TypTap) writes homeowners policies primarily in Florida, with ~313,400 policies in force as of December 2025. The company's growth engine is the assumption of policies from Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, Florida's state-backed insurer of last resort — a legislatively mandated depopulation program through which HCI assumed 173,485 policies representing $756.6M in annualized gross premiums over 2023-2025.

Beyond insurance, HCI owns approximately 82.5% (inclusive of unvested restricted stock) of Exzeo (NYSE: XZO), an insurtech platform that completed its IPO in November 2025 at $21/share, raising $168M. HCI also manages two reciprocal exchanges (CORE for commercial residential, Tailrow for homeowners) and holds commercial real estate investments in Florida.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** Revenue grew from $500M (2022) to $901M (2025), a 22% CAGR. Q1 2026 gross premiums earned of $326M (vs. $300M prior year) indicate continued momentum. The growth is driven by Citizens policy assumptions and rate adequacy improvements.

**Profitability — Exceptional in 2025:** The 2025 gross loss ratio of 19.6% (vs. 34.6% in 2024 and 33.3% in 2023) reflects zero catastrophe losses — no named hurricanes made landfall in Florida in 2025. The net combined ratio of 56.3% produced $320M in consolidated net income ($299M after noncontrolling interests) and a consolidated net margin of 35.6%. Q1 2026 maintained this trajectory with a 20.1% gross loss ratio and $5.45 diluted EPS.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $444M in 2025 (up from $332M in 2024) with negligible capex ($3.6M) yields free cash flow of approximately $441M — a 49% FCF margin on revenue. At HCI's $154 share price and approximately 13.0M shares outstanding, this implies a free cash flow yield well above typical P&C peers.

**Balance Sheet:** $1.21B in cash, $790M in investments, total assets of $2.53B, stockholders' equity of $1.04B ($80.13/share book value as of year-end 2025). Financial debt is minimal: $36M revolver plus $31.9M in long-term debt. The $172.5M convertible notes were fully converted to equity during 2025, reducing interest expense from $13.3M to $9.2M annually. Q4 2025 quarterly interest expense was approximately $1.0M, annualizing to roughly $4.0M based on the remaining debt balance.

**Capital Return:** $80M buyback authorized March 2026; $37.5M deployed through April 30 at ~$157/share average. Quarterly dividend of $0.40/share (~1% yield). The company is prioritizing buybacks at these valuations — the correct allocation decision given the discount to intrinsic value.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has three legs:

1. **Citizens policy assumptions:** The primary driver historically. Citizens' book has been shrinking — from approximately 1.23M policies in 2023 to an estimated 738,000 by year-end 2025 — but remains substantial relative to private market capacity. Competition for remaining takeout policies may intensify, and future assumptions depend on legislative/regulatory decisions.

2. **Rate adequacy and retention:** With tort reform reducing litigation costs, HCI can retain more premium while maintaining competitive rates. The structural improvement in loss ratios means each policy generates more earnings than historically.

3. **Exzeo and reciprocal exchanges:** Exzeo's ability to sell technology services to third-party carriers and the management fees from CORE and Tailrow represent nascent but scalable revenue streams. If Exzeo captures even a modest share of the insurtech market, HCI's stake becomes a significant value driver.

Consensus estimates project $16.66 in EPS for FY2026 and $18.07 for FY2027. These figures are substantially below the $22.72 earned in 2025, implying analysts are embedding meaningful cat-loss provisions. The 90-day revision trend is +5.8% for current year EPS — modestly positive momentum from a low base.

## Valuation Assessment

At $154/share, HCI trades at:
- 6.8x trailing EPS ($22.72)
- 9.2x FY2026 consensus EPS ($16.66)
- 8.5x FY2027 consensus EPS ($18.07)
- 1.92x verified year-end 2025 book value ($80.13)

The peer median P/E of approximately 15.8x (based on broad P&C comparables, though Florida-concentrated insurers may warrant a lower multiple given catastrophe exposure) would imply $263/share on trailing earnings and $150-165 on normalized (analyst-embedded-cat-loss) earnings. Analyst consensus targets as of early 2026 ranged from $235 to $255, with a mean of $245, implying 53-66% upside.

**Exzeo embedded value analysis:**
Exzeo had 90.9M shares outstanding at year-end 2025. At its $21 IPO price, implied equity value is approximately $1.91B. HCI's approximately 82.5% ownership stake implies a value of roughly $1.58B, or approximately $121/share on HCI's 13.0M share count. However, this value is already partially reflected in HCI's consolidated earnings — Exzeo reported $217M revenue and $82.8M net income in 2025, of which HCI's proportional share (~$68M) flows through consolidated results. A proper assessment of whether Exzeo is undervalued in HCI's stock price requires comparing:
- HCI's market cap ($2.0B)
- The market value of HCI's Exzeo stake (~$1.58B at IPO price)
- The residual value attributed to non-Exzeo insurance operations (~$420M implied)
- Non-Exzeo earnings (consolidated EPS minus Exzeo contribution, estimated at ~$17/share in 2025)

At the residual implied value of ~$420M for non-Exzeo insurance operations that earned approximately $17/share (or ~$221M after removing Exzeo's contribution), the core insurance business would be trading at roughly 2x those earnings — suggesting the market may indeed be applying a steep discount, though the degree depends on the appropriate multiple for Florida-concentrated cat-exposed earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

HCI operates in a structurally favorable competitive environment. National carriers (State Farm, Allstate) have been reducing Florida exposure for years, ceding market share to specialists. Several smaller Florida-focused insurers went insolvent in 2022-2023, further concentrating opportunity among survivors. Key competitors include Universal Insurance Holdings (UVE) and Heritage Insurance (HRTG).

HCI's competitive advantages include:
- **Proprietary technology (Exzeo):** Enables superior risk selection, claims processing, and operational efficiency
- **Scale:** ~313,400 policies provide data density for underwriting refinement
- **Citizens relationship:** Established track record as an assumption partner
- **Bermuda captive reinsurer:** Optimizes risk retention and reduces external reinsurance costs
- **Balance sheet strength:** $1.2B cash vs. peers that often operate with tighter capital positions

The structural tailwind from Florida's 2022 tort reform (reducing assignment-of-benefits abuse and litigation frequency) benefits all Florida writers but disproportionately aids larger operators like HCI that had invested in technology to manage the prior high-litigation environment. S&P Global reported that lawsuits against Florida insurers fell 23.8% year over year in 2024 and 42.6% from the 2021 peak.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Hurricane Catastrophe Concentration (Critical):** A direct Category 4/5 hit on Tampa or southeastern Florida would generate substantial losses before reinsurance — illustratively in the range of $300-500M based on HCI's policy concentration and exposure, though specific probable maximum loss figures are not publicly disclosed. Even after reinsurance recovery, residual losses and reinstatement premiums could produce a net loss year comparable to or worse than 2022 (-$59M). The company mitigates through reinsurance (33.5% of gross premiums ceded), but the tail risk remains substantial and unhedgeable.

**2. Geographic/Regulatory Concentration:** The company's insurance revenue derives predominantly from Florida, though it operates in 13 states. Adverse regulatory changes — modifications to the depopulation program, rate caps, or moratoriums on non-renewals — could impair the business model.

**3. Growth Deceleration:** Consensus estimates below 2025 actuals suggest the market expects reversion to loss-normalized results. Citizens' shrinking book means the depopulation runway is narrowing and competition for remaining takeouts may intensify.

**4. Reinsurance Market Dynamics:** Reinsurance costs ($414M in 2025) represent the largest expense after losses. A further hardening cycle or capacity withdrawal from Florida would compress margins.

**5. Clean-Year Loss Ratio Normalization:** The 2025 gross loss ratio of 19.6% benefited from zero named hurricane landfalls in Florida. HCI's own CEO letter attributes the low ratio in part to the absence of catastrophe losses. While tort reform has structurally reduced litigation costs, investors should not assume 19-20% represents a normalized baseline without explicitly modeling hurricane/catastrophe provisions.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of approximately 68.5% vs. 33% historical volatility represents extreme richness. The steep backwardation in the term structure signals the market is pricing an imminent catalyst — likely related to hurricane season onset (June 1). The call-heavy skew and low short interest suggest speculative long positioning rather than protective activity. This IV richness is a seasonal phenomenon for Florida insurers entering hurricane season and does not indicate fundamental deterioration.

## Conclusion

HCI Group presents a compelling valuation case at 6.8x trailing earnings, supported by minimal debt, aggressive buybacks, and an approximately 82.5% Exzeo stake with a public market valuation of ~$1.9B. The structural improvement from Florida tort reform appears durable, with lawsuits declining over 40% from 2021 peaks. The stock has declined 27% from its October 2025 highs, with fundamentals improving (Q1 2026 record results, book value growing to approximately $84/share).

The binary hurricane risk cannot be diversified away within this single position. Position sizing should reflect this: 2-3% maximum allocation, with willingness to add on any cat-loss-driven drawdown that doesn't impair the reinsurance structure. At current prices, risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside assuming average hurricane seasons.

**Recommendation: BUY** with 2-3% portfolio allocation and recognition that hurricane season (June-November) represents a defined risk window.
