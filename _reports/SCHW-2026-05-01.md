## Executive Summary

Charles Schwab reported Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.43 (beating $1.42 consensus), grew client assets to $11.77 trillion on $140 billion in net new assets, and expanded pre-tax margins to 49%. The stock subsequently dropped ~8% on concerns that JPMorgan's planned AI-driven cash management tool could accelerate client cash sorting away from Schwab's low-rate sweep deposits. At $92.63, the stock trades at 15.4x FY2026E adjusted EPS of $6.02, below its peer median of 17.0x and below growth-adjusted fair value given the NIM expansion trajectory still underway.

The core thesis has strengthened since the prior BUY was issued at ~$100: bank supplemental funding is 94% eliminated ($5.1B remaining from $80B peak), NIM has expanded from 2.74% to ~3.0% with room to reach 3.2-3.4% through FY2026, and consensus estimates are rising across every forward period (current year +2.7% over 30 days, next year +2.9%). The stock is now cheaper than it was when the prior BUY was issued, while the earnings trajectory is stronger. This is the specific material change justifying continuation of the BUY recommendation: the price has overreacted to a competitive concern that does not fundamentally impair the business model.

The primary risk is interest rate sensitivity in a stagflationary environment. If rates rise sharply, Schwab's HTM/AFS portfolio would see further unrealized losses, potentially constraining capital returns. If rates fall, NIM compression is possible though manageable. The JPMorgan AI cash sorting concern, while headline-worthy, faces the structural reality that Schwab's sweep cash at 0.19% is embedded in a zero-commission, full-service ecosystem that clients value holistically.

## Company Overview

Charles Schwab is the largest U.S. retail brokerage and RIA custodian, managing $11.9 trillion in total client assets across 38.5 million active brokerage accounts. Revenue splits roughly 49% net interest income, 27% asset management and administration fees, 17% trading revenue, and 7% other (including 4% bank deposit account fees and 3% other). This revenue mix gives Schwab characteristics of a brokerage, a bank, and an asset manager, with bank-like NII sensitivity dominating the earnings profile.

The company operates two segments: Investor Services (~79% of revenue) serving retail clients, and Advisor Services (~21%) providing custody and technology to ~7,000+ independent RIAs. The completed TD Ameritrade integration added approximately $2.0 billion in annual run-rate cost synergies, and TD Bank's full exit from its SCHW equity position in February 2025 removed a significant share overhang.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 marked a recovery year.** Total net revenue grew 22% to $23.9 billion, net income surged 49% to $8.9 billion, and diluted EPS rose 56% to $4.65. Pre-tax margin expanded 870 basis points to 47.9%. These results reflect the near-complete elimination of expensive supplemental funding that had depressed margins since mid-2023.

The central financial dynamic is NIM expansion. Net interest revenue grew 28% to $11.75 billion as Schwab reduced bank supplemental funding from $49.9 billion to $5.1 billion during 2025, cutting interest expense 41% from $6.4 billion to $3.75 billion. Average funding costs fell from 1.49% to 0.88%. NIM expanded from 2.12% to 2.74% for the full year, reaching approximately 3.0% in Q1 2026. Management's long-term NIM target range of 3.2-3.4% implies another 20-40 bps of expansion ahead as the remaining $5.1B in supplemental funding rolls off.

**Balance sheet health is strong for a bank-model business.** Tier 1 Leverage Ratio stands at 9.3% (minimum: 4%), CET1 at 30.4% (minimum: 4.5%), LCR at 131%, and NSFR at 129%. The AOCI drag from unrealized securities losses improved to -$11.0 billion from -$18.1 billion at year-end 2023, and will continue to recover as securities mature (portfolio duration: 3.7-3.9 years).

Capital return is substantial: $7.3 billion in share repurchases plus $2.0 billion in common dividends plus $2.5 billion in Series G preferred redemption in FY2025, with $14.5 billion remaining on the $20 billion buyback authorization. The dividend was raised 19% to $0.32/quarter in January 2026.

**Free cash flow of $8.8 billion** (37% FCF margin) provides ample coverage for both capital returns and organic investment. Operating cash flow of $9.3 billion well exceeds total capital deployment.

**Earnings beat/miss record:** 3 beats and 1 miss over the last 4 quarters, with the miss (Q4 2025, -2.3%) modest in magnitude. The Q1 2026 beat was narrow (+0.5%) but accompanied by stronger-than-expected client asset growth. This pattern suggests manageable but not exceptional forecasting visibility.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic growth metrics are accelerating.** Q1 2026 delivered $140 billion in core net new assets and 1.3 million new brokerage accounts. Full-year 2025 net new assets of $519.4 billion represented 5.1% organic growth, up 42% from the prior year. Total client assets reached $11.77 trillion in Q1 2026.

Estimate revisions are uniformly positive: current-quarter EPS estimates rose 0.9% over 30 days, next-quarter +2.0%, current-year +2.7%, and next-year +2.9%. Eighteen analysts cover the current year, and the upward revision breadth across all periods signals genuine earnings momentum rather than one-time adjustments. FY2026E EPS of $6.02 represents 29% growth over FY2025's $4.65, and FY2027E of $6.99 implies 16% further growth.

Three specific growth vectors deserve quantification:

1. **NIM expansion runway:** Each 10 bps of NIM improvement on Schwab's ~$430B interest-earning asset base adds roughly $430 million in annual NII, or approximately $0.18 per share after-tax. The remaining ~20-40 bps to management's target represents $0.36-0.72 in incremental EPS.

2. **Client engagement surge:** Daily average trades rose 31% to 7.7 million in FY2025; margin lending balances grew 34% to $112.3 billion. Trading revenue increased 28% to $4.1 billion. This engagement is both cyclically and structurally driven as Schwab's platform advantages attract active traders.

3. **Strategic expansion:** The Forge acquisition ($660M) opens private markets to Schwab's 39M accounts. Schwab Crypto launches mid-2026 for spot Bitcoin/Ethereum trading. Both initiatives expand addressable wallet share within the existing client base.

The growth trajectory has moderated from the prior score of 8.0 to 7.0 for a specific reason: the easiest NIM recovery gains are behind us (supplemental funding went from $80B to $5B), and the remaining upside, while material, is incremental. The 29% EPS growth expected in FY2026 will decelerate to ~16% in FY2027 as the base normalizes.

The market requires additional proof quarters of sustained NIM expansion before re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Schwab's competitive position is its strongest dimension. The company operates at 0.12% expenses as a percentage of client assets, a structural cost advantage that zero-commission competitors cannot easily replicate without similar scale. The RIA custody franchise ($6.0 trillion in advisory assets, ~7,000+ RIA relationships) creates high switching costs: migrating a custodial relationship requires regulatory filings, client notifications, and months of operational disruption.

The TD Ameritrade integration is complete, with $2.0 billion in realized annual cost synergies. The combined platform has nearly 2x the active brokerage accounts of the next-largest competitor.

**JPMorgan's AI cash management tool** is the most-discussed competitive threat. The concern is that AI-driven optimization would alert clients to the low yield on Schwab's sweep deposits (0.19% in Q1 2026), accelerating cash sorting. The counter-argument: Schwab's sweep cash functions as the embedded cost of a zero-commission, full-ecosystem platform. Clients who moved to higher-yielding alternatives during 2022-2023 have already done so; cash as a percentage of client assets has declined from 10.5% to 9.7%, limiting incremental sorting risk. The remaining sweep cash is held by clients who value convenience over optimization.

New competitive vectors (Forge private markets, Schwab Crypto) expand Schwab's addressable market into areas where Fidelity, Coinbase, and Robinhood currently have advantages, leveraging Schwab's 39M account distribution channel.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** Schwab estimates +100 bps parallel shift produces +4.1% NII benefit, while -100 bps produces -4.4% NII drag. The most likely rate path is sustained higher rates, which on balance benefits Schwab's earning asset yields. However, a sharp move above 5% on the 10Y would deepen AOCI losses on the ~$196B securities portfolio and potentially constrain capital returns.

**Margin Lending Concentration:** $112.3 billion in margin loans (up 34% YoY, 16% in Q4 alone) represents concentrated tail risk during a severe equity correction. The 10-K added new language flagging this as a "materially increasing" risk. A 20% market drawdown could trigger cascading margin calls and write-offs.

**Cash Sorting — Structural vs. Cyclical:** The 2022-2023 episode demonstrated that clients will sort cash when rates provide meaningful alternatives. While 90% of the supplemental funding is eliminated, a renewed cash sorting wave (triggered by competitive pressure or further rate increases) would require Schwab to either raise sweep rates (compressing NIM) or accept deposit outflows.

**Regulatory Risk:** If total assets reach $700B, Schwab triggers Category II designation requiring AOCI inclusion in regulatory capital. Current assets are $491B, but strong organic growth plus expanding lending books could bring this threshold closer over 3-5 years.

**Crypto Launch Risk:** Schwab Crypto introduces operational, custody, and regulatory risks. Digital asset custody involves private key management where loss is irreversible. While the opportunity set is real, execution risk is non-trivial for a firm without existing crypto infrastructure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($125-131, 35-41% upside)

NIM reaches 3.3-3.4% by year-end 2026 as the final supplemental funding rolls off and organic deposit growth continues. FY2026 adjusted EPS comes in at $6.20-6.40 (above current consensus). FY2027 EPS of $7.00-7.50 at 18x yields $126-135. Schwab Crypto and Forge add incremental revenue streams. Buyback of $8-10B annually reduces share count by ~5% per year, providing mechanical EPS support. The stock re-rates to 19-20x forward as the market gains confidence in sustainable earnings power above $6.00.

### Bear Case ($79-85, 8-15% downside)

A sharp rate increase drives 10Y above 5%, deepening AOCI losses and constraining buybacks. JPMorgan's AI tool or competitor actions accelerate cash sorting, requiring Schwab to either raise sweep rates or accept deposit outflows. Margin lending losses materialize during an equity correction. NIM expansion stalls at 3.0% rather than reaching 3.3%+. FY2026 EPS comes in at $5.50-5.70, and the stock trades at 14-15x. The 52-week low of $79.47 provides a floor reference.
