## Executive Summary

Xylem's 2026 results have split cleanly along one axis: margins are beating and volumes are missing. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.46 exceeded the $1.34 adjusted consensus by 9.2% and beat management's own April guide of $1.31–$1.36; adjusted EBITDA margin of 23.3% came in 80–130bp above the guided 22.0%–22.5%. Reported revenue growth of +1.5% missed the guided +2% to +3%, organic growth was +1.3%, and management cut FY2026 organic guidance to 2%–3% from 2%–4% (total to ~2% from 1%–3%). Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion of 150bp on organic volume growth of roughly one percent is a restructuring-and-price story, and the restructuring program that funds it is quantified and finite: 1,463 position reductions completed in 2025, ~$80–120M of annual net savings beginning in 2026 with the majority landing this year.

The most important analytical correction concerns the order book. Reported Q2 orders of $3,086M (+42.0%, +40.9% organic) and a 15.2% year-to-date backlog build were widely read as a demand inflection, and several outlets attributed the surge to AI/data-center demand. The primary evidence points elsewhere: on the April 28 call management described a single $850M, 20-year outsourced-water contract with an existing specialty-chemical customer, 75% service / 25% capital, with ~10% of value recognized in 2026. Strip that order out and Q2 organic orders grew roughly 1.3%, indistinguishable from organic revenue, and book-to-bill falls from 1.32x to about 0.96x. That is consistent with FY2025's 0.99x book-to-bill and 9% backlog decline, and with Water Infrastructure organic orders of −3.9% in Q2. Data-center demand is real and accelerating (Q1 2026 data-center orders exceeded all of FY2025), but it sits in Applied Water, the smallest segment at 20% of revenue, and has not yet produced margin: Applied Water's Q2 adjusted operating margin rose 10bp and its adjusted EBITDA margin *fell* 50bp.

The stock has de-rated accordingly: down 16.1% over twelve months, 22% below the October 2025 high of $154.27, and trading at 19.4x next-year consensus EPS of $6.19 versus a historical multiple near 30x. That compression is a rational response to organic growth falling from ~5% to ~1%, not a sentiment overshoot. It also leaves the shares priced more sensibly than at any point in the last two years. My base case of $134 (21.6x FY2027 consensus) implies +11.4% plus a 1.4% dividend; the bear case of $102 implies −15%. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly 10–11% against a ~4.6% ten-year yield and a negative-alpha Industrials backdrop is positive but thin, and the upside depends on two unverified items: management's commitment to exit 2026 with MCS "well in excess of 25%" EBITDA margin (versus 21.1% in Q2), and the abatement of a self-described 200bp "80/20" walk-away drag in 2027. HOLD, with a defined accumulation zone below $110.

## Company Overview

Xylem is a pure-play water technology company: FY2025 revenue of $9,035M across roughly 150 countries and ~22,000 employees, organized in four segments.

| Segment | FY2025 Revenue | % Total | FY2025 Adj. Op. Margin (YoY) | Q2 2026 Adj. Op. Margin (YoY) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water Infrastructure | $2,636M | 29% | 22.1% (+430bp) | 24.6% (+510bp) |
| Water Solutions & Services | $2,464M | 27% | 17.1% (+140bp) | 18.6% (+170bp) |
| Measurement & Control Solutions | $2,086M | 23% | 16.9% (−60bp) | 16.3% (−180bp) |
| Applied Water | $1,849M | 20% | 18.4% (+240bp) | 19.6% (+10bp) |

Water Infrastructure is Flygt transport pumps (59% of segment) plus treatment brands. Applied Water sells building pressure-boosting, HVAC and fire-protection pumps and heat exchangers, the segment where data-center content resides, as a component supplier to builders and contractors rather than as a cooling-architecture owner. MCS is the Sensus smart-metering franchise (83% of segment) operating FlexNet on FCC-licensed 900 MHz spectrum in the U.S., plus analytics. WSS is largely the acquired Evoqua platform, including the outsourced-water model that converts equipment sales into multi-decade service contracts.

Geographic mix has narrowed toward the U.S.: 58% of FY2025 revenue versus 54% in FY2023, with Emerging Markets falling from 16% to 14%. Q2 2026 Emerging Markets revenue declined 6.5% to $286M. Customer concentration is genuinely low: no individual customer exceeded 5% of consolidated revenue in 2023, 2024 or 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The FY2023–24 step-up from $5,522M (FY2022) to $8,562M was the Evoqua merger. Underlying momentum has faded: organic growth of ~5% in FY2025 fell to +0.5% in H1 2026 (+1.3% in Q2). Management attributes ~200bp of the 2026 organic drag to peak-year "80/20" portfolio walk-away actions, weighted to the first two-to-three quarters, and roughly 1% of full-year headwind to China, where Q1 revenue fell ~30% and management decomposed the decline as one-third market contraction, one-third competitor actions, one-third voluntary exit. Taking the ~2% total guide against the $9,035M base implies FY2026 revenue near $9,216M, requiring H2 revenue of ~$4,755M, or +6.6% versus H1. Seasonality is genuinely H2-weighted, so that is achievable, but it is a back-half-loaded plan.

**Margins.** Adjusted operating margin went 16.0% (FY2024) → 17.8% (FY2025) → 17.7% H1 2026 → 19.1% in Q2. The Q2 bridge in the 10-Q is legible: +680bp favorable operations (350bp productivity, 240bp price, 80bp mix) against −520bp unfavorable (270bp inflation, 160bp negative volume leverage), plus 180bp from lower special charges and amortization. On H1 revenue of $4,461M, 390bp of productivity is roughly $174M of gross savings against roughly $125M of inflation cost, so most of the restructuring benefit is offsetting inflation rather than compounding on top of it. Cumulative program cost is $144M, of which $128M was incurred through Q2, with $16M and roughly 524 planned position reductions remaining.

The internal tension is MCS, which has now compressed year-over-year for three consecutive reported periods (−60bp FY2025 on 11.5% revenue growth, −120bp H1 2026, −180bp Q2 2026) as lower-margin energy metering displaces water metering. Adjusted EBITDA margin there fell 200bp to 21.1%. Management's Q1 commitment to exit 2026 "well in excess of 25%" MCS EBITDA margin, aided by the international metrology divestiture, requires more than a 400bp step-up in two quarters. It is the single most testable claim in the story and it is not yet corroborated by any filing.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 OCF $1,241M (against net income of $957M, a positive quality signal), capex $331M, FCF $910M, or 10.1% of revenue. FCF declined ~3% year over year while adjusted EPS rose 19%. H1 2026 FCF was $219M versus $169M. H1 must not be annualized: H2 2025 produced 81% of FY2025 free cash flow. As a labeled sensitivity, if H2 2026 repeats the H2 2025 dollar contribution, FY2026 FCF lands near $1.01B, a 3.6% yield on $28.1B of market value, still below management's "low double-digit FCF margin" framework ($1.10–1.29B on ~$9.2B of revenue).

Two working-capital items are structural rather than timing-driven. Long-term receivables consumed $104M in H1 2026 (versus $70M), explicitly tied to long-term outsourced-water projects, and contract assets rose to $585M from $510M while contract liabilities fell to $272M from $286M. The outsourced-water model front-loads capital and receivables and back-loads the service annuity; the $850M contract's capital build lands in 2027, which should intensify the drag before it reverses.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt rose from $1,942M at year-end to $2,926M at June 30 following the May 29 issuance of $500M 5.200% notes due 2033 and $500M 5.450% "Blue Notes" due 2036. Net debt went from $463M to $1,650M; against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $2,065M that is 0.80x, at the top of management's stated 0.5x–1.0x policy band and far inside the 3.50x revolver covenant. Liquidity is ~$2.3B with the $1B revolver undrawn and both commercial paper programs at zero.

Two caveats. First, reported interest expense *fell* to $11M in H1 2026 from $17M despite $984M of incremental debt, because cross-currency swaps designated as net investment hedges (notional $4,291M, up from $3,171M) recognized $28M of income within interest expense. Gross interest before swap income was roughly $39M in H1; cash interest paid was $29M. The interest line is not a read on cost of capital, and the $500M 3.250% note maturing October 2026 most plausibly refinances near 5%, adding ~$10M annually. Second, goodwill ($8,256M) plus other intangibles ($2,150M) total $10,406M against total equity of $10,421M: tangible common equity is effectively nil, and roughly 60% of the $17.3B asset base is acquisition intangibles. At 0.8x net leverage this is not a solvency question, but the equity carries no asset cushion and a goodwill charge would be an equity event. The Altman Z-Score of 2.94 sits at the top of the grey zone principally because asset turnover (0.51) and GAAP EBIT/assets (6.9%) are mechanically depressed by ~$220M of annual purchase-accounting amortization; it is a fair flag on asset quality and a poor read on near-term solvency.

**Capital returns.** The Board authorized $1.5B of repurchases on February 25, 2026. H1 execution was 10.5M shares for $1,243M at an average of $118.38 (Q2 average $114.30), with $453M remaining. Diluted shares fell 3.0% year over year to 236.6M. The dividend rose ~8% to $0.43/quarter ($1.72 annualized, 1.42% yield).

## Growth Analysis

Consensus is modestly rising and the beat record supports it: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with an average surprise of +5.0%. Current-year consensus of $5.66 is up 2.2% in 30 days and 2.4% in 90 days; next year $6.19 is up 1.5%/1.7%. These are respectable but nowhere near the +30% revision velocity that signals an earnings inflection; the appropriate read is steady, low-magnitude upward drift driven by cost execution.

H1 adjusted EPS of ~$2.57 against a $5.66 full-year consensus implies H2 of $3.09, closely matching the quarterly estimates ($1.45 Q3, $1.65 Q4). Given the beat pattern, $5.80–5.90 is achievable. The composition matters: FY2026 EPS growth of ~11% on ~2% revenue growth is roughly 500bp of margin expansion plus 3% of share-count reduction. FY2027 consensus growth of 9.4% requires the same mix or a volume recovery.

The volume recovery case rests on three items, in descending order of verifiability:

1. **The 80/20 drag abates.** If the 200bp walk-away offset is genuinely voluntary and peaks in 2026, underlying demand growth is nearer 4–5% and reported organic reaccelerates in 2027 without any market improvement. The complication is that management's own China decomposition assigns one-third of a 30% decline to "competitor actions," which means the walk-away and share-loss categories overlap and are not separable from disclosed data.
2. **Data-center content scales in Applied Water.** The Q2 10-Q cites data-center project growth as the driver of Applied Water organic growth. But this is the smallest segment, and the margin has not followed. Management expects data-center projects to ship at higher margin; that remains a forward expectation.
3. **The water AMI replacement cycle.** Management's own timing pushes this out: water AMI ran "five to seven years behind" the 2008–09 electric wave, so the refresh picks up "as we exit this decade going into 2030." This is not a 2026–2027 revenue driver.

Against these, the observable order data is flat-to-negative. FY2025 book-to-bill was 0.99x with backlog down 9% to $4,615M. Q2 2026 book-to-bill ex-the-$850M contract was ~0.96x; approximate ex-contract backlog of ~$4,515M is down modestly year to date and roughly 10% year over year. The 10-Q states outright that backlog declined in both MCS and Water Infrastructure because revenue outpaced orders.

The $850M contract is a high-quality win that validates the WSS outsourced-water annuity model, and management indicated additional pipeline. Its revenue phasing, derived from management's disclosed percentages, is ~$85M in 2026, ~$127M of remaining capital in 2027, and ~$638M of service spread from 2028 across roughly 18 years, averaging ~$35M annually, or ~1.4% of WSS FY2025 revenue. It is a durable asset and a small near-term growth contributor.

Growth Prospects: **5.2**. Earnings growth of 9–11% is real and cost-driven; revenue growth is stalled and the order book does not yet support reacceleration.

On trailing GAAP, Xylem at 30.7x is above the median; on forward consensus at 19.4x it is below every peer except Otis. The GAAP-to-adjusted gap of roughly $1.45 per share is dominated by ~$220M of annual acquisition amortization, which is non-cash but also non-reversing given the Evoqua/Sensus asset base.

The relevant question is whether 19–21x forward is a mispricing or a fair re-rating. Xylem traded near 30x forward when organic growth was mid-single-digit and the Evoqua integration promised mid-teens EPS compounding. Organic growth is now ~1%, and the EPS bridge is carried by a finite restructuring program plus buyback arithmetic. PEG on next-year consensus growth of 9.4% is roughly 2.1, which is not cheap. The 3.2–3.6% FCF yield provides support but no margin of safety at a ~9% cost of equity.

Consensus targets are meaningfully above my base: mean $153.24, median $159.00, range $129–$180. Even the low target implies +7.2%. I do not treat that dispersion as independent confirmation. A $153 mean requires roughly 24.7x FY2027 consensus, which embeds both the MCS bridge and a 2027 organic reacceleration. My base case of $134 uses 21.6x FY2027 consensus $6.19, a modest multiple recovery consistent with the MCS bridge landing and 2027 organic guidance of 3–4%, without assuming a return to the pre-2026 multiple.

Valuation: **5.6**. The multiple is defensible rather than compelling. Cheapness relative to the stock's own history is largely explained by a genuine deterioration in organic growth, which is the classic setup for a value trap if the MCS bridge fails.

## Competitive Landscape

Two competitive advantages are supported by the filings. Customer diversification is genuine (no customer above 5% of revenue for three consecutive years), and FlexNet operates on FCC-licensed 900 MHz spectrum in the U.S., a real switching-cost advantage versus unlicensed alternatives, with the corollary that the 10-K flags non-renewal as an impairment trigger.

Trajectory is mixed by segment. Water Infrastructure is earning a 24.6% adjusted operating margin, up 510bp, partly by shrinking its Treatment base through walk-aways, which is value-accretive and simultaneously a growth ceiling, with organic orders at −3.9%. WSS has demonstrated it can win multi-decade outsourced-water mandates at scale. MCS is losing margin while winning lower-value energy metering volume, with backlog shrinking and book-to-bill explicitly below 1.0 in Q1, a pattern consistent with competing on price in a well-capitalized specialist market. China is the clearest negative datapoint, with management itself attributing a third of a ~30% revenue decline to competitor actions.

The secular demand case is strong and long-dated: roughly one in five gallons lost to non-revenue water in the U.S., ~4% of global electricity consumed moving and treating water, U.S. infrastructure graded C− to D+, and management's cited ~$1.5 trillion of U.S. need over the next decade. None of that is visible in Xylem's 2026 order book. On the Q1 call the CEO reported meeting ~15 utility CEOs with "no indication of any meaningful funding pullbacks," which sits awkwardly next to −3.9% Water Infrastructure organic orders and secondary characterizations of Q2 as industrial demand offsetting utility weakness. I could not reconcile those from disclosed data.

Competitive Position: **6.0**, down from 6.5. Strong franchise, improving in WSS and WI margin, deteriorating in MCS and China, with rising U.S. geographic concentration.

## Risk Assessment

**MCS margin bridge (highest-impact near-term).** Exiting 2026 above 25% EBITDA margin from 21.1% in Q2 is a >400bp step-up in two quarters. Failure means roughly a quarter of revenue is structurally lower-margin than the market assumes, and the FY2027 consensus of $6.19 becomes vulnerable.

**Order-book deterioration.** Ex-the-$850M contract, book-to-bill is below 1.0 and backlog is down ~10% year over year. Backlog arithmetic here is directional only: the 10-Q notes that beginning backlog plus orders minus revenue will not tie to ending backlog because of contract adjustments and FX, but the direction is corroborated by the filing's own language and by FY2025's 0.99x.

**Goodwill against zero tangible equity.** Roughly $9B of goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles are flagged as an impairment-risk concentration; the Q4 2025 test produced no impairment. With TCE near zero, any charge flows straight through book value.

**Contingent obligations.** The Idrica put right allows holders to require Xylem to purchase the remaining equity at a fixed €168M strike after December 10, 2027 (redeemable NCI carried at $242M; Idrica is loss-making, contributing $6M of H1 net loss attributable to NCI). Surety bonds, bank guarantees and letters of credit total $899M, up from $822M, consistent with a growing long-term project book. The Swedish Tax Agency matter (SEK 837M, ~$86M, including penalties and interest) has been decided in Xylem's favor twice but is under appeal again with **no unrecognized tax benefit recorded**; an adverse final outcome would be a discrete charge.

**Refinancing and rate exposure.** $500M of 3.250% notes mature in October 2026; the swap income currently suppressing reported interest expense is rate- and currency-dependent rather than contractual. [unverified — the ten-year Treasury level and long-end supply conditions are not in the provided sources.]

**Tariff accounting.** The Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 affirmation that many IEEPA tariffs exceeded presidential authority produced $4M of refunds and a $12M receivable in Q2, credited to cost of revenue (~68bp of the 41.2% GAAP gross margin) but classified within special charges and therefore **excluded from adjusted results**. The $1.46 adjusted EPS does not contain the windfall while GAAP does. This is conservative and correctly disclosed, and it argues the adjusted margin expansion is real rather than flattered.

**Verification gaps that a reader should not treat as established:** the specific revised FY2026 adjusted EPS and EBITDA margin ranges (only the revenue revision is confirmed in the 10-Q; the prior April guide was $5.35–$5.60 EPS and 22.9%–23.3% margin); the $850M contract's counterparty and terms, which rest on oral management commentary; and the MCS exit-rate commitment. No Q2 earnings call transcript was available.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $158 (+31%)

The 200bp 80/20 walk-away drag is genuinely voluntary and peaks in 2026, so 2027 reported organic growth returns to 4–5% with no market improvement. MCS delivers the promised exit rate above 25% EBITDA margin as water metering backlog rebuilds and the international metrology divestiture removes dilution, and Applied Water's data-center content ships at the higher margin management expects, taking group adjusted EBITDA margin toward 24%. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $6.30–6.50, aided by the remaining $453M of buyback plus a refreshed authorization reducing share count another 2%. The $850M contract's service tail begins in 2028 and management converts additional outsourced-water pipeline, re-establishing the annuity narrative. At 25x $6.30 the shares reach $158, roughly the consensus median of $159. The dividend, raised 8% and covered ~2.5x by FCF, adds 1.4% annually.

### Bear Case — $102 (−15%)

The walk-away label proves partly cosmetic and China share loss continues, holding organic growth at 1–2% through 2027. MCS misses its bridge, as it has in each of the last three reported periods, and the mix shift toward energy metering proves structural rather than cyclical, capping group margin near 23%. Restructuring savings fully lap by mid-2027 with no successor program, removing the EPS engine, while the October 2026 refinancing and the outsourced-water working-capital build (long-term receivables already consuming $104M in H1, intensifying as the $850M capital phase lands in 2027) keep FCF margin at ~10% rather than the low-double-digit target. FY2027 EPS settles near $5.90 and the multiple holds at 17.5x, implying $102, 3% below the May 2026 low of $105.29.

### Base Case — $134 (+11.4%)

FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at $5.70–5.85 (consensus $5.66 plus the historical ~5% beat pattern), FY2027 near $6.19. MCS improves but arrives short of the "well in excess of 25%" commitment; organic growth recovers to 3% in 2027 as the walk-away drag partially abates. The multiple recovers modestly to 21.6x FY2027 consensus, giving $134. Total return including the 1.42% dividend is roughly 12.8% over twelve months. Probability-weighting base 50% / bull 25% / bear 25% yields an expected price return near 9.2%, or ~10.6% total: positive, but not enough margin of safety to justify initiating a full position ahead of the MCS proof point.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (fourth consecutive). Scores have declined 6.4 → 6.3 → 5.9 → 5.7. The Q2 print confirms the direction of that trajectory rather than reversing it: the margin dimension improved (adjusted EBITDA margin +150bp, EPS beat 9.2%, estimates up 2.2% over 30 days), while the growth dimension weakened further (organic guidance cut, ex-contract book-to-bill below 1.0, backlog down ~10% year over year). The 8% price appreciation since the June report against ~2% estimate growth means the multiple expanded roughly 6%, which is why the valuation score ticks down from 5.7 to 5.6 despite the stock remaining 22% below its high.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (late October) is the first hard test of the MCS exit-rate bridge and of the H2-weighted revenue plan requiring +6.6% sequential-half growth. February 2027 guidance is the test of whether the 80/20 drag abates.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. MCS adjusted EBITDA margin above 23% in Q3 and tracking toward 25%+ in Q4, confirming the bridge.
2. Ex-large-contract organic order growth above 4% for two consecutive quarters with book-to-bill above 1.05x, evidencing demand rather than one lumpy award.
3. Price below $110 (roughly 17.8x FY2027 consensus and 8% below management's $118.38 H1 buyback average) with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $6.15; that combination restores a double-digit margin of safety to the $134 base and would move me to ACCUMULATE.
4. FY2026 free cash flow at or above $1.05B (11.4% margin), demonstrating that outsourced-water working capital is manageable.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. MCS adjusted EBITDA margin below 21% in Q3, or explicit withdrawal of the "well in excess of 25%" exit-rate commitment.
2. FY2027 organic revenue guidance below 3%, indicating the walk-away drag was not the principal explanation for 2026 deceleration.
3. Any goodwill or indefinite-lived intangible impairment against effectively zero tangible common equity.
4. FY2026 free cash flow below $900M, which would put the H1 working-capital build in the structural rather than seasonal category.

**Position guidance:** existing holders retain exposure; the 1.42% dividend is well covered and leverage at 0.80x provides ample flexibility. New capital should wait for the $105–110 zone or for Q3 confirmation of the MCS bridge. There is no case for buying at 19.4x forward with organic orders flat.

## Conclusion

Xylem is executing well on the levers it controls and poorly on the one it does not. Productivity and price delivered 590bp of gross operating-margin benefit in Q2 against 270bp of inflation and 160bp of negative volume leverage, producing a 9.2% adjusted EPS beat and a modest raise to profit guidance while revenue guidance was cut. That is a high-quality cost story on a stalled top line, and it is being achieved without help from the $16M of tariff refunds, which are correctly excluded from adjusted results.

The market has already priced much of this. The multiple has compressed from roughly 30x to 19.4x next-year consensus, and the twelve-month decline of 16.1% is a defensible response to organic growth falling from ~5% to ~1% rather than a sentiment dislocation to be arbitraged. What remains unpriced in either direction is the MCS margin bridge, which requires a step-up of more than 400bp in two quarters from a segment that has compressed year-over-year for three consecutive periods. If it lands, and if the 200bp walk-away drag genuinely abates in 2027, $158 is reachable. If it fails, $102 is the floor implied by 17.5x on a flat-organic EPS path.

Investors underwriting Xylem as an AI-infrastructure derivative on the strength of the +42% order print should recalibrate. The order surge is a $850M, 20-year specialty-chemical outsourced-water contract that averages roughly $35M of annual service revenue from 2028, a good asset and a small growth contributor. Data-center demand is genuinely accelerating, but it lives in the 20%-of-revenue Applied Water segment whose adjusted EBITDA margin declined 50bp in Q2. Ex-contract, the order book grew about 1.3% and book-to-bill was below one.

HOLD at $120.32, base case $134, with an accumulation zone of $105–110 and a Q3 print in late October as the decisive checkpoint.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: Q2 2026 adjusted consensus per Phase 1 was $1.35 (surprise +8.1%), not $1.34/+9.2% as stated throughout the report and used to characterize the "beat"; the dividend yield at $1.72/$120.32 is 1.43%, and at $0.43/quarter the January raise is +7.5% (not ~8%); these do not move the core thesis but touch figures repeated in the Executive Summary, Conclusion, and return math.
