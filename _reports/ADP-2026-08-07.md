## Executive Summary

ADP closed fiscal 2026 (ended June 30, 2026) at the top of its own guidance range: revenue of $21,947.4M (+7% reported, +6% organic constant currency), adjusted EBIT margin of 26.8% (+80bp), and adjusted diluted EPS of $11.12 (+11%). Reconstructing the fourth quarter by subtraction from the nine-month 10-Q gives revenue of $5,473.8M (+6.8%), adjusted EBIT margin of 25.06% (+140bp) and adjusted diluted EPS of $2.64 (+16.8%), figures that reconcile with same-day third-party reporting of the release. This was a clean print from a business with 1.1 million clients, no client above 2% of revenue, and approximately 13-year average client tenure in Employer Services.

The complication is what sits underneath the 7%. Zero-margin PEO benefit pass-throughs added $318.3M of revenue at no profit (1.5 points of growth) and client-funds interest added $165.7M (0.8 points). Stripping both, core revenue was $15,985.3M versus $15,082.8M, or +6.0% reported and roughly 5% organic constant currency. US pays per control grew 1%; PEO average worksite employees grew 2%. PEO segment EBIT *fell* 2% to $936.1M, and on the base that actually carries margin (revenue ex pass-throughs) PEO EBIT margin compressed roughly 244bp, from 39.58% to 37.14%. The FY2027 workers' compensation premium ADP Indemnity paid in July 2026 rose 17.9% to $327.8M from $278M, which suggests the PEO cost drag persists.

At $273.51 (up 45% from the April 10 low of $188.16 and 11.8% below the 52-week high of $310.08) the shares trade 22.3x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $12.26, 20.4x FY2028 consensus of $13.40, 18.6x EV/adjusted EBIT, and a 4.8% free cash flow yield. Estimates are rising modestly: +0.9% for the current quarter and +1.2% for next year over 90 days. The 12-month base case of roughly $288 is about 5% above spot; the bear case, driven by multiple compression on a genuine labor contraction, is near $215. That asymmetry supports holding, not adding. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on the name; the post-print rally has removed the entry discount that the prior report was waiting for rather than validated a stronger call.

## Company Overview

ADP is a payroll and human capital management outsourcer founded in 1949. FY2026 scale disclosures: more than 42 million workers paid globally, approximately 26 million in the United States (roughly one in six US workers), operations in more than 140 countries, approximately 67,000 associates, and more than $3.5 trillion of client funds moved during the year.

Two segments:

**Employer Services** — FY2026 revenue $14,831.4M, EBIT $5,436.8M, margin 36.7% (+60bp). Payroll, benefits administration, workforce management, compliance (SmartCompliance), retirement and insurance services, plus global payroll. RUN serves more than 980,000 small businesses; Workforce Now more than 90,000 mid-market and larger North American clients; Lyric HCM targets global enterprises. New business bookings +6%; client revenue retention 92.1%. Derived Q4 ES margin was 34.39% versus 33.51%, roughly +88bp.

**PEO Services (TotalSource)** — FY2026 revenue $7,128.1M, EBIT $936.1M, margin 13.1% (−110bp). More than 19,000 clients and 770,000 worksite employees under a co-employment model. ADP is the largest IRS-certified PEO and is ESAC-accredited. Derived Q4 PEO margin was 12.20% versus 13.21%, with dollar EBIT down 1.3%.

One disclosure convention matters for modeling. ADP states in Item 1 that anticipated future revenue under contract "is not a significant portion of our expected future revenue, is not a meaningful indicator of our future performance." Deferred revenue and any RPO-style figure should not be read as backlog for this company. The correct forward operating metrics are ES bookings growth (+6%), ES retention (92.1%), pays per control (+1%), and PEO worksite employee growth (+2%).

Three-year revenue CAGR is 6.8%; net earnings CAGR 8.9%; reported EBIT margin expanded roughly 150bp. Effective tax rate 23.0%. Adjusted EBIT was $5,874.6M (26.8%) and adjusted EPS $11.12.

Q4 GAAP EBIT margin *contracted* 25bp to 22.98% while adjusted margin expanded 140bp. The entire bridge is a new $91.1M business alignment charge ($89.1M severance, $2.0M strategic project costs), described in the 10-K as corporate-led organizational streamlining. At 0.4% of revenue the charge is small and its exclusion from adjusted results is conventional, but it is a new item, absent from prior years, taken in the same quarter management guided to further FY2027 margin expansion. The most plausible reading is that ADP is pre-positioning its cost base ahead of a decelerating revenue year. An alternative and equally consistent reading is AI-enabled service-delivery efficiency being harvested; both point the same direction for FY2027 margins.

One margin caveat cuts against extrapolation. Stock-based compensation expense fell 9.7% for the first nine months of FY2026 ($182.9M versus $202.5M) while revenue grew 7%. ADP grants cash-settled awards to non-US employees, and those liabilities mark to the share price, which fell from roughly $260 in January to $196 in April. If mark-to-market explains the decline, part of FY2026's margin expansion is non-repeating and reverses as the shares recover toward $273.

### The float: exposure to the level of rates, not to near-term changes

Average client funds balances rose 7.4% to $40.4B at an average earned rate of 3.4% (from 3.2%), producing $1,354.8M of client-funds interest revenue. Average corporate cash of $10.4B generated $371.0M of corporate interest income. The extended investment strategy is funded partly with commercial paper (average daily balance $4.2B at 4.0%, average maturity approximately three days) plus $3.6B of reverse repurchase balances at 4.1%. Applying those disclosed balances and rates gives a financing cost of roughly $316M, so net client-funds interest is approximately $1,039M, or 18.1% of reported EBIT. Adding corporate interest income, rate-and-float-dependent income totals roughly $1,410M, about 24.6% of pretax earnings. (Cross-check: $316M plus $171.5M of contractual senior-note interest due within a year is $487M against reported total interest expense of $459.3M, the gap consistent with the 2036 notes being outstanding for only part of the year.)

The important nuance is duration. ADP discloses that a 25bp move across both short and intermediate rates changes pretax earnings by approximately $21.0M over twelve months; short-term-only, approximately $10.0M. The portfolio is laddered to five years (extended) and ten years (long). ADP therefore has high exposure to the multi-year *level* of rates and low exposure to near-term *changes*. A one-year rate-sensitivity screen materially overstates near-term risk while understating the multi-year dependence. Illustratively, if the average earned rate eventually reset from 3.4% to 2.5% on a $40.4B balance, gross client-funds interest would fall roughly $364M pretax, about $0.70 of adjusted EPS at a 23% rate, but the reset would take years and financing costs would fall in parallel.

### Cash flow, balance sheet, and one screening artifact to discard

Operating cash flow was $5,441.2M against capital expenditures of $196.6M, giving free cash flow of $5,244.6M: a 23.9% FCF margin, 1.19x net income, and a 4.9% yield on $107.4B. Cash was $4,230.1M; long-term debt $4,964.1M; short-term debt zero at year end (versus $4,769.5M of commercial paper a year earlier). Net corporate debt of $734M is 0.12x adjusted EBIT. Interest coverage is 12.8x on total interest expense and roughly 34x against senior-note interest alone. Committed undrawn facilities total $11.7B, with an $11.7B commercial paper program rated A-1+/P-1/F1+ and nothing drawn at year end.

Two adjustments deserve mention. Reported capex of 0.9% of revenue is extraordinarily light, but the 10-K discloses "R&D investment" of $1.405B against an income-statement R&D expense of $1,028.8M, a $376M gap containing capitalized and acquired software. Treating the entire gap as capital spend reduces FCF to roughly $4,869M, a 4.5% yield. That is an upper-bound adjustment since part relates to acquisitions. Separately, ADP disclosed approximately $2,226.8M of purchase commitments for software licenses and services at March 31, 2026, with $1,042.7M in FY2027–FY2028, roughly 5% of annual revenue committed, reflecting the cloud and AI infrastructure build. MD&A cites +$74.4M of incremental hosting, cloud and software-license expense in FY2026.

A caution on standardized screens: an Altman Z-Score of 0.62 and total-liabilities-to-equity of 9.48x can be generated for ADP because $44,415.5M of the $57,162.1M in total liabilities is client funds obligations, matched by $43,957.8M of funds held for clients. Excluding client funds from both sides, adjusted liabilities/equity is 2.11x and corporate debt/equity is 0.82x. A reported 1.9% operating margin is likewise irreconcilable with the filings, where revenue less cost of revenue less SG&A is $5,779.0M (26.3%). None of those screen outputs are facts about ADP. The one genuine leverage exposure is not solvency but the short-dated funding of a long-dated client-funds portfolio.

### Capital allocation

ADP returned $4.7B in FY2026 ($2.6B dividends, $2.1B buybacks), roughly 90% of free cash flow. It repurchased 8.6M shares at an average $242.92, versus 4.4M at $289.11 in FY2025: roughly double the shares at a 16% lower average price. Monthly Q3 disclosures show execution at $260.08 in early January, $222.18 in February and $213.29 in March, and the Board raised the authorization to $6.0B on January 14, 2026, near the start of the drawdown. Approximately $5,490.2M remained at March 31, which at $273.51 would retire about 20.1M shares, roughly 5.0% of shares outstanding: total remaining capacity, equal to roughly 2.6 years at the FY2026 pace, not an annual rate. The quarterly dividend of $1.70 annualizes to $6.80, a 2.49% yield at 61% of adjusted EPS. Weighted diluted shares fell 1.3%; dilution from outstanding awards is approximately 0.25% of basic shares.

## Growth Analysis

The gap between reported and underlying growth is the central analytical issue. Headline revenue +7% decomposes into roughly 5% core organic constant currency, plus 1.5 points of zero-margin pass-through growth (largely health-benefit cost inflation), plus 0.8 points of float interest, plus roughly a point of FX. Management's own compensation plan uses "Revenue ex-ZMPT Growth" as a performance measure, which indicates the distinction is recognized internally.

Volume is the weak leg. US pays per control grew 1%, below the 2–3% typical of a healthy US labor expansion, and PEO worksite employees grew 2%. Two explanations are at least equally plausible: AI-driven substitution of white-collar headcount, which the FY2026 10-K now names explicitly ("including in employment levels (as a result of AI or otherwise)"), or a broadly soft US labor market and slower small-business hiring. The pays-per-control figure cannot distinguish them. Growth is being carried by pricing, ES bookings (+6%) and float, not by employment volume.

FY2027 guidance reported by secondary sources (revenue growth 5–6%, adjusted EPS growth 9–11%, client funds balance growth 3–4% "as wage growth cools off") is single-sourced and should not be treated as established; the Wall Street Journal confirmed only the directional statement. The ranges are internally coherent, however: 5.5% revenue growth plus roughly 70bp of adjusted margin expansion (worth about 2.6–2.8% of EPS on a 26.8% base) plus roughly 1.3% share-count reduction gives 9.4–9.6% adjusted EPS growth. That composition matters: two-thirds of the EPS growth comes from margin and buyback rather than revenue.

Consensus is aligned and rising slowly: FY2027 $12.26 (+0.5% over 30 days, +0.9% over 90) and FY2028 $13.40 (+0.6%/+1.2%). Four consecutive beats, but small ones (0.7%, 1.7%, 0.8%, 1.1%), consistent with tight guidance discipline rather than accelerating momentum. This is a rising-estimate stock; the revision magnitudes are two orders below the threshold at which revisions become a standalone buy signal.

The PEO drag is the clearest identifiable headwind. Ex-pass-through PEO margin compressed 244bp, driven by higher workers' compensation and state unemployment insurance costs (+$37.1M cited in MD&A) and less favorable actuarial development at ADP Indemnity (a $2.8M pretax gain versus $8.8M). Development became *less favorable*, not adverse; a single year of reserve normalization is not a trend. But the 17.9% increase in the FY2027 workers' compensation premium to $327.8M is a forward cost datapoint that argues the compression continues into the current year. ADP Indemnity held $824.4M of assets against $774.3M of estimated unpaid losses, a cushion of roughly 6.5%.

## Competitive Landscape

ADP's 10-K competition section names no competitors, listing categories instead (outsourcers, ERP vendors, cloud HCM providers, financial institutions, and clients' own in-house functions) and places price last among competitive factors, consistent with 150bp of EBIT margin expansion over three years.

Three moat elements are specific enough to verify and hard to replicate. ADP Trust Company, N.A. holds a 2019 OCC national trust bank charter and is sole trustee of the ADP Client Trust holding US client funds, under ongoing OCC oversight. ADP has implemented GDPR Binding Corporate Rules, which it states few companies have. And it maintains direct integrations with "tens of thousands of government entities, tax authorities, and banking institutions" across 140+ countries, alongside registrations spanning FINRA, the SEC and DOL (ERISA fiduciary), New Hampshire banking regulators, FINTRAC and the Bank of Canada, and BSA/AML obligations for the Wisely paycard. The barrier is assembling that regulatory perimeter, not writing payroll software.

Trajectory is mixed rather than uniformly improving, which is why the competitive score sits at 6.0 rather than higher. ES is gaining: bookings +6%, retention 92.1%, Q4 margin +88bp. PEO is not: profit dollars down 2%, margin down 244bp on the profitable base, worksite employees +2%. The market debate is live and unresolved. A Seeking Alpha piece on July 15, 2026 argued high margins invite competition as AI lowers entry barriers and simplifies the regulatory complexity ADP monetizes; the Mairs & Power Balanced Fund noted AI fears hurt the position in Q1 but reaffirmed it. ADP's counter-position, that AI accelerates regulatory fragmentation and increases demand for a trusted orchestration layer, is plausible but unproven in the volume data, where pays per control at +1% is the only hard evidence and points the wrong way.

Management reportedly cited ADP Assist reaching more than 3 million active users, 12 million conversations, 45,000 compliance issues flagged, and 48% internal deployment of an AI service platform. Those figures are not in the filings and are unconfirmed.

## Risk Assessment

**Employment levels, with AI named as a driver.** The FY2026 10-K states that negative changes in employment levels "(as a result of AI or otherwise)" may hurt the business, and that contracts blend base fees with per-employee fees. Pays per control at +1% makes this concrete rather than hypothetical. This is the single most consequential exposure and the one least resolvable from disclosure.

**Linked float-and-employment risk.** The client-funds portfolio, laddered to five and ten years, is funded partly with ~three-day commercial paper. ADP's own risk factor states that reduced financing availability could force sales of available-for-sale securities at a loss, and separately that declining employment shrinks client funds obligations and could force the same outcome while also lowering interest income. Net unrealized pretax AFS losses were $459.2M at June 30, 2026, up from $425.7M. Client funds obligations of $44,415.5M exceed funds held of $43,957.8M by $457.7M, essentially equal to the unrealized loss, an accounting identity from carrying assets at fair value against a par obligation, not a shortfall if held to maturity, but it quantifies what a forced liquidation would crystallize.

**Multi-year rate reset.** Roughly 24.6% of pretax earnings is float and corporate interest income. Near-term sensitivity is trivial ($21M per 25bp across the curve), but a sustained reset in the average earned rate is worth roughly $0.70 of adjusted EPS per 90bp on the current balance.

**PEO co-employment and CPEO liability.** As a certified PEO, ADP may be liable for worksite employee wages, taxes and benefits even if a client fails to remit, magnified in banking stress. The filing also flags that no definitive judicial or legislative interpretation of "employer" exists for PEOs under ERISA; an adverse determination on TotalSource plans could require a business-model change plus penalties. A putative ERISA class action filed in May 2020 regarding the TotalSource Retirement Savings Plan remained open at March 31, 2026, with no estimable loss disclosed. Six years unresolved and unsized warrants monitoring.

**Legislative risks with no hedge.** Reductions in withholding amounts or a shortening of the collection-to-remittance interval would directly reduce average client fund balances. Tax Credit Services depends on renewal of federal, state and local credit statutes.

**Mitigants worth weighting.** No client or affiliated group exceeded 2% of FY2026 revenue across 1.1 million clients, genuinely low concentration versus enterprise-software comparables. No goodwill impairment, with reporting-unit fair values stated to "substantially exceed" carrying values and a 100bp WACC increase insufficient to trigger impairment. No restatements or non-reliance filings.

### Options Market Signal

The 160.1% near-term ATM IV is an artifact of a one-day expiry and should be discarded; the 35.8% → 163.8% jump in the delta snapshot is the same mechanic. The informative part of the term structure is flat and unremarkable: 29.4% at one month, 26.0% at three months, 27.3% at six months, against 26.7% one-year realized volatility. Options are priced roughly at fair value with no regime premium. One-month skew is call-heavy at −6.4%, and put/call open interest is 0.92 with a volume ratio of 0.53, showing no accumulation of downside protection. The single unusual print is 1,106 August 14 $270 calls against 142 open interest, near-the-money and small. Short interest at 4.0% of float with 5.0 days to cover is uncrowded. The options market is not corroborating either the AI-disruption bear case or a squeeze setup; it is pricing a slow-moving, fully-discovered large cap. That is consistent with a HOLD and offers no timing edge in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($335, +22.5%)

The AI debate resolves in ADP's favor: regulatory fragmentation accelerates, compliance complexity rises, and the OCC trust charter plus BCRs plus 140-country tax integration become more valuable rather than less. ES momentum continues, with bookings compounding at 6%+, retention holding above 92%, and Q4's 88bp of margin expansion sustained by AI-enabled service delivery. Pays per control recovers toward 2% as the labor market stabilizes, restoring a volume leg to growth. PEO workers' compensation and state unemployment costs normalize, and the 244bp of ex-pass-through compression reverses. The $5.49B remaining authorization retires 5% of shares at prices management has shown willingness to press. Multiple re-rates to 25x on FY2028 $13.40.

### Bear Case ($215, −21.4%)

Pays per control turns negative as AI-driven white-collar displacement broadens from tech opex to the mid-market ADP serves. Per-employee fees decline, the float shrinks with employment, and ADP realizes losses on AFS sales at the same time interest income falls, the linkage its own risk factor describes. PEO compression proves structural rather than cyclical, with the 17.9% workers' compensation premium increase the leading edge. Wage growth cools, reducing pricing power. FY2028 estimates are cut 5–8% and the multiple compresses to 17x, back toward where the April low implied. Part of FY2026's margin expansion reverses as cash-settled stock compensation re-marks upward with the share price.

### What tips the balance

The bull case requires a volume recovery that the hard data does not yet show. The bear case requires a labor contraction that has not yet reached ADP's revenue, with pays per control still at +1%. In between sits the base case: 5–6% revenue growth, 9–11% adjusted EPS growth two-thirds sourced from margin and buyback, and a roughly unchanged multiple. At $273.51 the market is paying close to that base case already.

## Conclusion

ADP remains one of the highest-quality recurring-revenue businesses in the industrials universe: 1.1 million clients with none above 2% of revenue, approximately 13-year ES tenure, $5.24B of free cash flow converting at 1.19x net income, net corporate debt of $734M, $11.7B of undrawn committed facilities, and 90% of free cash flow returned to shareholders. Management's capital allocation in FY2026 was better than mechanical: doubling repurchased shares at a 16% lower average price, raising the authorization near the start of the drawdown, and holding dilution to 0.25% of basic shares. Financial health and management quality are the two dimensions where this name earns its premium.

The constraint is arithmetic. Core organic growth is roughly 5%, not 7%; pays per control is +1%; PEO profit dollars declined; and the FY2027 EPS growth bridge leans two-thirds on margin expansion and buybacks. At 22.3x FY2027 and 20.4x FY2028 consensus, a premium to the 21.9x peer median for growth that is not superior, the base case sits about 5% above spot against roughly 20% bear-case downside. The prior report's plan was to enter after the print; the print was good and the stock rallied 11% to consume the discount. Own it, collect the 2.5% dividend and the 5% of shares the remaining authorization can retire, and add in the $235–245 range or on evidence that pays per control has inflected.
