**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Arcus Biosciences is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company that, over the five months from December 2025 to April 2026, lost essentially its entire anti-TIGIT franchise — the program for which Gilead paid $725M in option fees and which formed the historical centerpiece of the investment case. STAR-221 (1L gastric) was discontinued for futility in December 2025, and STAR-121 (1L NSCLC) followed on April 20, 2026. What remains is a single-asset story built around casdatifan, a once-daily oral HIF-2α inhibitor for clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) that directly challenges Merck's approved belzutifan (Welireg). The equity value is now overwhelmingly levered to the pivotal PEAK-1 trial (casdatifan plus cabozantinib in IO-experienced ccRCC) and the broader casdatifan combination program.

The setup is more constructive than a franchise collapse would suggest. At a ~$2.8B market cap against ~$876M cash, the market is valuing casdatifan plus residual Gilead/Taiho optionality at roughly $2B, having largely written off TIGIT. The stock at $22.12 sits near the low end of the recent insider-transaction range and roughly 23% below its 52-week high of $28.72, while consensus EPS estimates have risen meaningfully over 90 days (+12.7% current year, +19.7% next quarter), reflecting a declining burn profile and partner-funded pipeline expansion. The June 3, 2026 non-exclusive clinical collaboration with Bristol Myers Squibb — adding casdatifan arms to the BMS-sponsored pumitamig (PD-L1/VEGF-A bispecific, jointly developed by BioNTech and BMS) ROSETTA trial — restores a 1L TKI-free combination path that was dented when the AstraZeneca eVOLVE-RCC02 study paused on potential immune-mediated adverse events. Three large-pharma partners (Taiho, AstraZeneca, BMS/BioNTech) validating casdatifan combinations is a genuine external signal.

We rate RCUS a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The catalyst path over the next 12 months is concrete: PEAK-1 full enrollment by year-end 2026, updated casdatifan + cabozantinib and initial early-line data this year, and a planned 1L Phase 3 initiation. But this is a binary, single-asset biotech with ~$550M annualized burn (declining), a sole-source China manufacturer exposed to the BIOSECURE Act, a disengaging anchor partner, and a 15.3% short interest. The risk/reward does not yet clear our bar for a long, but the priced-in bear case and rising estimates keep us from an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Founded in 2015 by Terry Rosen (CEO) and Juan Jaen (President), Arcus is headquartered in Hayward, California, and built on internal small-molecule discovery. It operates as a single reportable segment with no approved products and no product revenue. All revenue to date derives from collaboration and license arrangements, primarily with Gilead (≈88% of Q1 2026 revenue) and secondarily with Taiho.

The portfolio historically spanned four mechanisms developed in intra-portfolio combinations: anti-TIGIT (domvanalimab), anti-PD-1 (zimberelimab, in-licensed from WuXi Biologics), CD73 (quemliclustat), and HIF-2α (casdatifan). With the TIGIT Phase 3 program now effectively dead — only PACIFIC-8 in unresectable Stage 3 NSCLC remains, solely funded by Arcus — the strategic center of gravity has shifted entirely to casdatifan. Arcus owns casdatifan globally except Japan/Asia-ex-China (licensed to Taiho in Q4 2025 for a $15M option payment). Management's plan is to position it as a backbone therapy across all lines of ccRCC: 1L TKI-sparing, 1L TKI-inclusive, 2L on top of cabozantinib, and late-line. Quemliclustat (Phase 3 PRISM-1 in pancreatic cancer, with FDA orphan designation from June 2025) is a secondary value driver.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement (Q1 2026 vs. Q1 2025):** Total revenue fell 39% to $17M from $28M, reflecting winding-down Gilead development services; this is collaboration revenue, not product sales. R&D was roughly flat at $122M, G&A rose 4% to $29M, and the net loss widened to $(128)M from $(112)M, with EPS of $(1.02) versus $(1.14). FY2025 net loss was $(353)M, and the accumulated deficit reached $1.6B by Q1 2026.

**Balance sheet (3/31/2026):** Cash and marketable securities of $876M, down from $1,010M at year-end 2025. Total stockholders' equity was $524M against total assets of $997M. Debt consists of $100M drawn on a $250M Hercules term facility at an effective rate of ~12.85%, maturing September 2030, interest-only to September 2028, secured by substantially all assets. A minimum-cash covenant begins July 2027, conditionally waived if market cap exceeds a threshold.

**Cash flow and runway:** Operating burn was $(138)M in Q1 2026 (~$550M annualized but guided to decline as domvanalimab studies wind down). Management guides cash runway into 2H 2028, expects to end 2026 with ~$600M in cash, and guides FY2026 GAAP revenue of $50–65M. During 2025, the company raised ~$438M gross via two equity offerings (29.4M shares) plus a $50M Hercules draw. Shares outstanding grew from ~92M at year-end 2024 to ~125.5M at Q1 2026, a 36% increase — a material dilution history that is the defining financial risk for the equity.

Standard distress screens (Altman Z ~1.0) are not informative for a pre-commercial biotech; they penalize negative earnings and retained earnings by construction. The binding constraints are cash runway and clinical success. Liquidity is adequate into 2H 2028, but the company will need to raise again — through equity or partnership economics — before it generates product revenue. A $200M ATM is in place.

The earnings surprise record is mixed and of limited signal for a pre-revenue name: three beats and one miss across the last four quarters, with the most recent (Q1 2026) a 14% miss ($(1.02) vs. $(0.89) estimate). For a company with no product sales, the relevant "earnings" signal is burn discipline and runway, both of which are trending favorably.

## Growth Analysis

For a clinical-stage biotech, "growth" means pipeline advancement and the probability-weighted value of pivotal readouts, not revenue trends. On that basis the 12-month catalyst path is the substance of the thesis:

- **PEAK-1 full enrollment by year-end 2026** (casdatifan + cabozantinib vs. cabozantinib in IO-experienced ccRCC) — the company-defining pivotal trial.
- **Updated casdatifan + cabozantinib data and initial early-line ccRCC data expected in 2026.**
- **A 1L Phase 3 in metastatic ccRCC planned to initiate by year-end 2026.**
- **The new BMS ROSETTA collaboration** adds casdatifan arms to a BMS-sponsored Phase 1/2 trial of pumitamig on a non-exclusive, supply-based footing. Arcus pays little, retains all casdatifan rights, and gets a 1L combination readout funded substantially by BMS. This is capital-efficient pipeline expansion and a direct response to the eVOLVE-RCC02 pause.

The estimate-revision trend supports the constructive read: next-year EPS estimates have risen +6.2% over 90 days, with all four horizons net positive. For a pre-revenue biotech, this primarily reflects a declining burn trajectory (R&D guided lower as domvanalimab winds down) rather than accelerating revenue. The +30% revision threshold that would flag a strong buy signal is not met here, and the underlying driver is cost reduction rather than a revenue ramp, so we weight it as confirmatory rather than decisive.

The growth case is real but narrow. With TIGIT dead and quemliclustat a secondary bet, value creation depends on casdatifan demonstrating a profile competitive with or superior to belzutifan in Phase 3. Multiple partners pursuing casdatifan combinations raises the option value but does not change the binary nature of the pivotal data.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiples are meaningless: RCUS has negative earnings and no product revenue, so trailing/forward P/E (-6.84) and the peer P/E median (18.1) are not usable comparables. The biotech peers shown (HRMY, ADMA, CPRX, VCEL, SRPT) are commercial-stage companies with earnings; they do not map to a pre-revenue, single-catalyst clinical asset.

The appropriate frame is a sum-of-parts on enterprise value. At ~125.8M shares and netting cash against debt gives net cash of ~$776M, implying an enterprise value of roughly $2.0B attributed to the pipeline — predominantly casdatifan, plus residual Taiho/Gilead optionality and the quemliclustat free option. The TIGIT write-off is rational given two Phase 3 futility discontinuations.

The question is whether ~$2.0B for casdatifan is asymmetric. The bull view: ccRCC is a large, growing market; HIF-2α is a validated mechanism (belzutifan is approved); casdatifan's once-daily oral profile and early-data claims (deep, durable inhibition) plus three independent large-pharma combination partnerships suggest perceived best-in-class potential; and a successful PEAK-1 could support a multi-line backbone franchise worth multiples of the current EV. The bear view: ~$2.0B is a full price for a single asset whose pivotal data are not yet in, against a competitor with first-mover approval and a 1L combination path that already hit immune-mediated AEs in one study.

Analyst targets bracket this tension: low $22.0 (essentially current price), median $34.5, mean $35.75, high $47.0. The 10-buy / 3-hold split skews bullish, and the median implies ~52% upside over the $22.66 reference price. On a high-momentum name with rising estimates these targets would normally read as floors, but for a pre-revenue biotech the targets are themselves probability-weighted pivotal-readout scenarios — they are not independent of the binary risk and should be discounted accordingly. We score valuation at 5.5: the bear case appears substantially priced in, but the upside is gated entirely on data that have not yet read out, which is the opposite of a clean asymmetric setup.

## Competitive Landscape

The TIGIT class has collapsed industry-wide. Roche, GSK, and BeOne retreated earlier; domvanalimab (Fc-silent) was among the last standing, and its repeated Phase 3 failures confirm the class's poor track record. Only AstraZeneca's rilvegostomig (TIGIT/PD-1 bispecific) remains meaningfully in play. Arcus's residual TIGIT shot is PACIFIC-8, now solely company-funded.

HIF-2α in ccRCC is the battleground. Merck's belzutifan (Welireg) is the approved first-mover. Arcus's differentiation pitch rests on deeper, more durable HIF-2α inhibition with high response rates and low primary progression in early studies — company claims not yet Phase 3-validated. The breadth of partnerships now validating casdatifan combinations (Taiho in Asia, AstraZeneca until the eVOLVE pause, and now BMS/BioNTech) is a positive external signal of the asset's perceived potential, and the industry push toward TKI-sparing 1L regimens (PD-L1/VEGF-A bispecifics) is a favorable structural trend.

The competitive trajectory is mixed: Arcus is gaining external validation for casdatifan while having decisively lost its TIGIT positioning, and it faces an entrenched approved competitor. We score competitive position at 5.5 — improving on the casdatifan vector, deteriorated on TIGIT, and unproven against belzutifan until pivotal data arrive.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Single-asset binary risk.** With TIGIT dead and quemliclustat secondary, equity value is heavily levered to casdatifan and PEAK-1. A PEAK-1 failure would be company-defining.
2. **eVOLVE-RCC02 safety signal.** Arcus and AstraZeneca agreed in March 2026 not to re-open enrollment following potential immune-mediated adverse events in the casdatifan + volrustomig 1L combination. This may be attributable to AZ's CTLA-4-containing bispecific rather than casdatifan, but it is a specific safety flag on a casdatifan combination to monitor as more data emerge.
3. **Competitive risk vs. belzutifan.** Casdatifan must demonstrate a profile competitive with an already-approved drug.
4. **Single-source China manufacturing + BIOSECURE.** WuXi Biologics is the sole manufacturer of zimberelimab and domvanalimab. The U.S. BIOSECURE Act (enacted December 2025) could disrupt supply or raise costs if WuXi were designated a company of concern, despite a 5-year grandfathering window.
5. **Funding/dilution.** Declining burn, reliance on the $200M ATM and milestone income, and a Hercules covenant package (minimum-cash covenant from July 2027) restricting flexibility. A raise before product revenue is likely.
6. **Gilead disengagement.** Gilead returned etrumadenant (2025), declined the $100M 6th-anniversary payment (April 2026, with the option period ending July 14, 2026), and surrendered early-stage inflammation options (CCR6, CD89, CD40L), retaining only AB801, AB598, AB102, and a TNF inhibitor. This narrows future non-dilutive option/milestone income (each retained option carries a $150M exercise fee) and signals reduced conviction from a 25% holder, though Arcus regains control of de-optioned programs.
7. **Governance/personnel.** Founder-led with explicit key-person risk; COO Jennifer Jarrett signed a separation agreement (March 20, 2026) amid the strategic pivot. Gilead holds ~25% equity and 3 board seats via the Investor Rights Agreement (a takeover deterrent and significant influence); classified board and Delaware anti-takeover provisions are in place.

We score risk factors at 4.5 — below the midpoint because of the concentrated binary exposure, the crystallized safety signal, and the newly material China manufacturing overhang, partially offset by the fact that much of the bad news (TIGIT, Gilead) has already materialized and is reflected in the price.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a major upcoming event. ATM implied volatility surged from 32.0% to 159.0% (+127pp) between early May and mid-June 2026, while the underlying fell 8.6% — consistent with hedging into a near-term binary catalyst rather than directional complacency. The put/call open-interest ratio of 0.67 (down slightly from 0.73) is not heavily put-skewed, so options positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the short interest. The rich IV makes long-premium entry expensive and confirms that the market expects a volatility-resolving data point; it does not contradict the fundamental view but reinforces that this is a setup to size cautiously around defined-risk structures rather than a clean directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($35–47):** PEAK-1 enrolls on schedule and subsequent casdatifan ccRCC data (2L on cabozantinib, early-line, and the BMS/BioNTech 1L combination) confirm a best-in-class HIF-2α profile that differentiates from belzutifan. Casdatifan becomes a credible multi-line backbone franchise, the partner validation (Taiho, BMS/BioNTech) converts into milestone economics, burn declines on plan, and the equity re-rates toward analyst high targets. Net cash plus a de-risked lead asset supports a $4–6B enterprise value.

**Bear Case ($10–14):** PEAK-1 or early casdatifan data disappoint, or the immune-mediated AE signal recurs in combination settings. With TIGIT already dead, a casdatifan setback removes the core of the equity value; the stock compresses toward net cash (~$6/share) plus a deeply discounted pipeline, implying a fall to the low-to-mid teens. Continued dilution and a BIOSECURE-driven supply disruption would compound the downside.

**Base Case (~$24–28):** PEAK-1 enrollment completes on time and interim casdatifan data are encouraging but not yet definitive. The stock holds within its recent range as investors await pivotal readouts, supported by net cash and rising estimates but capped by binary risk. This is consistent with the analyst median of $34.5 being a probability-weighted target that the market discounts pending data.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is year-end 2026 (PEAK-1 full enrollment, planned 1L Phase 3 initiation, and updated casdatifan + cabozantinib / early-line data), with an interim checkpoint at the July 14, 2026 expiry of the Gilead option period and subsequent quarterly cash-runway updates.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Positive casdatifan combination data (response rate / durability) that clearly differentiates from belzutifan in 2L or early-line ccRCC, or a positive PEAK-1 interim signal.
- A new monetizing partnership or milestone that extends runway beyond 2H 2028 without materially diluting equity (e.g., casdatifan ex-territory deal economics, or BMS/BioNTech/Taiho milestone capture).
- Confirmation that the eVOLVE-RCC02 immune-mediated AE signal is attributable to AZ's CTLA-4 agent and not casdatifan, removing a key overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- PEAK-1 futility or discontinuation, or casdatifan combination data showing the immune-mediated AE signal recurring with casdatifan as the likely cause.
- A dilutive equity raise materially below the current price absent offsetting clinical de-risking, or a covenant/liquidity stress as the July 2027 minimum-cash covenant approaches.
- WuXi Biologics designated a BIOSECURE "company of concern" with a credible supply-disruption path for zimberelimab/domvanalimab.

## Conclusion

RCUS is a casdatifan-centric, single-catalyst biotech that has shed a failed TIGIT franchise and a disengaging anchor partner, with both setbacks substantially reflected in the current market cap and enterprise value. The 12-month path is concrete and catalyst-rich, and consensus EPS estimates are rising across all horizons as burn declines. That is a genuine growth-hold thesis, not a default neutral stance.

It does not yet clear our bar for a long. The equity is a binary bet on data that have not read out, against an approved competitor, with a single-source China manufacturer exposed to BIOSECURE, a documented immune-mediated AE signal in one combination, near-certain future dilution, and options markets pricing a volatility event. The asymmetry that would justify a BUY requires either favorable casdatifan data or a runway-extending non-dilutive deal — both of which are upgrade triggers we are watching rather than facts in hand. We rate RCUS a **HOLD (growth_hold)**, with conviction to upgrade on positive casdatifan de-risking and to downgrade on a PEAK-1 setback or a recurring safety signal.
