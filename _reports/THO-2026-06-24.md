**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

THOR Industries is the world's largest manufacturer of recreational vehicles, holding roughly 48% of the North American motorhome market, 36-39% of NA towables, and 26% of European motorcaravans. Market position is dominant and stable; the investment question is where the company sits in a brutal post-COVID cycle, and whether the bottom is in. The data argues the bottom is not in. Revenue is down ~41% from the FY2022 peak and net income down ~77%, and the most recent quarter showed the largest segment (NA Towable) collapsing 24.6% with backlog down 39.1% year over year. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut 16-17% across all forward periods in the last 90 days, and management cut FY2026 guidance to $3.30-$3.80 from $3.75-$4.25 on the June 3 print.

The stock has de-rated from above $114 in January 2026 to $72.85, a decline of roughly 36% from the high and ~16% since the prior report. The bull view holds that this is a deeply cyclical company trading near trough earnings with a clean balance sheet, a growing 2.9% dividend, founder open-market buying, and demographic tailwinds that recover when rates ease. That view has merit on a multi-year horizon. The near-term reality is a worsening industry forecast (RVIA cut CY2026 wholesale shipments to ~314,000 units, -8.2% YoY, reversing an earlier growth call), dealer destocking, FX-flattered reported revenue masking flat-to-declining constant-currency volume, and a thin goodwill cushion that creates non-cash impairment tail risk.

We rate THO a HOLD on income-hold grounds. The dividend is well-covered and the balance sheet can absorb the downturn, but there is no identifiable catalyst to inflect demand within the next 12 months, and the trajectory of estimate revisions remains negative. We would not add at current levels and would not chase the cheap headline multiple, which reflects a cycle that is still deteriorating rather than a recovery the market is missing.

## Company Overview

THOR operates a decentralized house-of-brands model across three reportable segments. North American Towable (~40% of sales) includes Airstream, Jayco (now incorporating Heartland), Keystone, and KZ. North American Motorized (~23%) includes Thor Motor Coach, Tiffin, Jayco, and Airstream motorhomes, where the company holds a dominant ~48% share. The European segment (~32%) is the Erwin Hymer Group (Hymer, Dethleffs, Bürstner, Laika, and others across nine production sites). A components arm (Airxcel, Postle) supplies parts and aluminum extrusions to OEMs and the aftermarket.

Products are sold almost entirely through independent, non-franchise dealers (~2,400 in North America, ~1,100 in Europe). THOR does not finance dealers directly but provides standby repurchase agreements to floor-plan lenders, a commitment of roughly $3.7B at April 30, 2026. The business is highly cyclical and seasonal, with low barriers to entry and intense competition from Forest River (Berkshire-owned), Winnebago, Trigano, and Knaus Tabbert. The fiscal year ends July 31.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature of THOR's financials is a severe, multi-year cyclical downturn that has not yet bottomed:

| Metric (FY, July 31) | FY2022 (peak) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $16.3B | $11.1B | $10.0B | $9.58B |
| Net income | $1.14B | $374M | $265M | $259M |
| Gross margin | 17.2% | 14.4% | 14.5% | 14.0% |

FY2025 revenue fell 4.6% to $9.58B with gross margin of 14.0% and EPS of ~$4.84. The FY2025 effective tax rate fell to 13.4% on a non-recurring foreign deferred-tax revaluation, which flattered reported EPS and does not repeat.

FY2026 is deteriorating, not recovering. Nine-month revenue (ended 4/30/26) was $7.30B, up 3.4% YoY, but approximately 8.5 points of that came from euro strength, leaving constant-currency revenue roughly flat. Gross margin compressed to 12.7% from 13.7%. In Q3 alone, revenue fell 3.9% to $2.78B and diluted EPS dropped to $1.86 from $2.77 a year earlier, a 33% decline. Gross margin fell to 12.8% from 15.3%, and adjusted EBITDA fell 28% YoY. The segment story is bifurcated: NA Towable sales fell 24.6% in Q3, the largest segment weakening fastest, while NA Motorized recovered modestly off a depressed base and European growth was almost entirely FX.

Cash flow quality has weakened materially. Nine-month operating cash flow collapsed to $77M from $319M a year earlier, driven by a working-capital build (inventory and chassis up ~$172M, receivables up ~$166M). Cash fell from $587M to $372M. FY26 pretax income has also been propped up by non-operating items (gains on property/plant sales of ~$35M, largely from the Heartland restructuring; warrant and investment mark-ups; and deferred-comp swings) that mask weaker core operating earnings. The FY26 effective tax rate normalized up to ~28-29% from the FY25 low, an additional EPS headwind.

The balance sheet remains the clearest source of resilience. Equity is ~$4.3B; total debt ~$883M (a $500M 4.00% senior note due 2029, a term loan due 2030); the $1B asset-based revolver is essentially undrawn with ~$998M available. D/E is 1.65 and the current ratio 1.75. Altman Z of 2.79 sits in the grey zone, reflecting cyclical earnings compression rather than distress, and the company continues to deleverage. FCF was $574M in FY25. The balance sheet supports the dividend and buyback through the trough; the concern is earnings, not solvency.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture is negative across every measurable indicator, and this is the dimension that carries the most weight in our framework.

Estimate revisions are uniformly falling and accelerating downward. Current-quarter EPS estimate fell 35.4% in 30 days and 36.9% in 90 days. Current-year estimate fell 15.8% (30d) and 17.1% (90d); next-year fell 16.3% and 17.1%. These are deteriorating estimates on a deteriorating business, the inverse of the inflection signal we look for. The most recent quarter broke a streak of large beats (831% and 593% surprises in the two prior quarters, which were against trivially low estimates) with a 3.4% miss.

Leading indicators confirm the deterioration. Total backlog at 4/30/26 was $2.51B, down 12.3% YoY, with NA Towable backlog down 39.1%, signaling that the core segment's weakness extends into FY27. THOR's North American dealer inventory was down 13.7% YoY as dealers destock. RVIA's June 2026 forecast cut CY2026 wholesale shipments to ~314,000 units, -8.2% YoY, down from an earlier forecast of growth to ~349,300 units.

The long-term demand case is intact but not actionable on a 12-month horizon. Demographic and lifestyle demand for the outdoors, plus a large base of pandemic-era first-time buyers becoming repeat purchasers, supports a recovery, but the trigger is lower interest rates and improved consumer affordability, neither of which is visible in the near term. The affordability that drives both dealer floor-plan economics and consumer financing remains constrained by elevated interest rates. The Heartland-into-Jayco restructuring is a sensible cost response to the towable downturn but is defensive, not growth-generating.

## Valuation Assessment

THO trades at ~14.7x trailing P/E on FY25 EPS of $4.84, and roughly 20-21x on the cut FY26 guidance midpoint of ~$3.55, or ~15.5x on the next-year consensus of $4.71. The headline multiple looks cheap against the Leisure Products peer median P/E of 25.5, but that comparison is misleading. Two of the closest market-cap peers (Polaris, Brunswick) show negative trailing P/Es, indicating the entire RV/powersports cohort is in a trough; the elevated multiples on YETI (25.5x) and Acushnet (35.1x) reflect different, more stable end markets. The relevant comparison is to THOR's own normalized earnings power, and there the case is two-sided.

The cycle-adjustment discipline cuts against the stock here rather than for it. The cycle-adjustment rule rewards stocks with rising estimates and a forward multiple cheap relative to improving fundamentals. THO has the opposite profile: falling estimates (-17% over 90 days), a deepening backlog decline, and a freshly cut industry forecast. A low multiple on falling estimates is a value-trap signature, not an inflection. The 2.9% dividend yield provides absolute support and a floor, and on truly normalized mid-cycle earnings of perhaps $6-7 of EPS the stock would look cheap at $72.85. But the market is correctly pricing the risk that mid-cycle earnings are still one to two years away, and that the trough could be deeper than current guidance implies.

We also flag the earnings-quality issue for valuation: a growing share of FY26 pretax income comes from asset-sale gains and investment mark-ups rather than core RV operations, so the cleaner operating earnings power is below the reported figure. The analyst dispersion captures the uncertainty, with targets ranging from a low of $59 to a high of $138, a mean of $93 and a median of $90. On a 12-month view, our base case is closer to the lower-middle of that range. The cheapness is real on normalized numbers but the catalyst to realize it is absent.

## Competitive Landscape

THOR's competitive position is stable to mildly improving in relative terms even as absolute demand falls. The company is the clear category leader, competing against Winnebago and privately held Forest River in North America and Trigano/Knaus Tabbert in Europe. Structural advantages include scale, brand breadth across price points, a flexible North American cost structure that can be ramped down quickly, and a vertically integrated component arm. These let THOR pull cost levers in a downturn that smaller competitors cannot.

The pressure points are mix and pricing. The shift toward lower-priced entry-level units (travel trailers, special-edition motorcaravans) plus heavier discounting is compressing gross margin, and dealer consolidation (FreedomRoads/Camping World represents ~14% of FY25 sales and 12-14% of receivables) increases buyer bargaining power and concentrates repurchase-agreement exposure. The moat is genuine but this is a low-margin, low-barrier-to-entry industry; THOR's leadership does not confer pricing power in a buyer's market. Trajectory is best described as holding share while the pie shrinks, which is neither a clear positive nor a clear negative for the competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclical trough deepening.** The single largest risk is that the downturn extends. The RVIA forecast cut, the towable backlog decline, and dealer destocking all point to FY27 weakness, not recovery. This is partly priced via the drawdown, but consensus may still be too optimistic.

**Tariffs.** The most significant new and escalating risk versus the prior year. Tariffs imposed since early 2025 (some under IEEPA) are increasingly pressuring material costs as FY26 progressed. A February 2026 Supreme Court opinion on tariff legality prompted THOR to file refund claims with U.S. Customs; recovery amounts and timing are uncertain. THOR often imports indirectly via vendors, limiting both visibility and its ability to pass costs through without hurting demand. This creates open-ended estimate uncertainty in both directions: a cost headwind now, possible refund optionality later.

**Goodwill impairment.** $1.87B of goodwill sits on the balance sheet. At the May 2025 annual test, reporting units representing ~75% of consolidated goodwill had fair value exceeding carrying value by less than 25%, a thin cushion. With the demand forecast cut and discount-rate pressure, a material non-cash impairment is a real FY26/FY27 tail risk, explicitly foreshadowed by added macro-trigger language in the 10-Q. The impairment would not affect cash or covenants but would be a sentiment and book-value event.

**FX dependency.** Roughly 32-36% of sales are non-USD. Recent reported "growth" is FX-flattered; a dollar reversal would expose underlying volume weakness and could turn the reported top line negative.

**Customer concentration.** FreedomRoads at ~14% of sales concentrates both demand and the standby repurchase exposure to floor-plan lenders.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is moderate and broadly two-sided. Near-term ATM IV is 44.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 38.0%, modestly rich (6.5-point spread), with an IV percentile of 34% within the current chain — the market is pricing some uncertainty but not extreme stress. The put/call skew of 2.5% is balanced, and the term structure is flat (44.5% near vs. 44.7% far), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. ATM IV fell sharply from 69.0% to 44.5% since the June 1 snapshot as the post-earnings uncertainty around the June 3 print resolved, and the market is no longer pricing an imminent binary. The put/call OI ratio of 1.10 and short interest at 11.7% of float (7.0 days to cover) reflect meaningful but not crowded bearish positioning. Net read: the options market corroborates the fundamental view of a stressed-but-not-collapsing business, with no near-term catalyst priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** THOR is a dominant, well-capitalized cyclical trading near trough earnings. The balance sheet (net debt of only ~$511M against ~$4.3B equity, an undrawn $1B revolver) lets it weather the downturn while paying a growing dividend and repurchasing stock. Founder/director Peter Orthwein has made open-market purchases on the way down ($85.40 and $88.25), and management is buying back stock. When interest rates ease and affordability recovers, the demographic base of new RV owners drives a wholesale-shipment recovery, mid-cycle EPS normalizes toward $6-7, and the stock re-rates. On that math, $72.85 is a cheap entry. Loop Capital's $96 Buy target and the $138 high target frame the upside. Bull case target ~$120 (16x on normalized ~$6.50 EPS plus a recovery multiple) on a 2-3 year horizon.

**Bear case.** The cycle is deeper and longer than guidance implies. The towable backlog decline and the RVIA forecast cut signal FY27 weakness, not recovery. FX-flattered revenue masks declining volume; earnings quality is deteriorating as one-time gains prop up pretax income; the tax rate normalized higher; and a goodwill impairment hits book value. Falling estimates continue, dividend coverage tightens as FCF compresses, and the stock drifts toward the analyst low of $59. Bear case target ~$59-62, roughly 15-19% below current.

**Base case.** The trough persists through FY26 with no near-term catalyst, but the balance sheet and dividend hold and the stock trades in a $68-85 range. Base case target ~$78, ~7% above current, dominated by the dividend rather than appreciation. This sits between the bear ($59) and bull ($120) scenarios.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-18 month income-hold with optional multi-year cyclical upside that requires a rate/affordability catalyst not currently visible.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NA Towable backlog stabilizes or turns positive YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling the core segment has bottomed.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive — the clearest inflection signal.
- RVIA revises its CY2026/2027 wholesale shipment forecast upward, or interest-rate relief visibly eases dealer floor-plan and consumer financing costs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material goodwill impairment that signals management has written down the recovery thesis.
- FY26 EPS guidance cut a second time below the current $3.30-$3.80 range.
- Dividend coverage deteriorates such that FCF no longer comfortably funds the ~$110M annual payout, or a cut is contemplated.
- Operating cash flow remains depressed (sub-$150M annual run-rate) into FY27, indicating the working-capital build is structural rather than cyclical.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q4 FY26 / full-year print (expected around September 2026, consistent with the FY25 10-K filing cadence), which will set FY27 guidance and reveal whether the towable backlog has begun to stabilize.

## Conclusion

THOR is a high-quality leader in a weak cyclical moment. The franchise, balance sheet, and capital-return policy are durable, and on truly normalized earnings the stock is cheap. But the dimension that matters most for sorting winners from losers — forward growth — is uniformly negative: falling estimates, a collapsing towable backlog, a cut industry forecast, and reported revenue flattered by FX. A low multiple on falling estimates is the textbook value-trap setup, and we will not reward cheapness without a catalyst. The drawdown has improved the risk/reward, but at this magnitude the burden is on the bull case to identify what the sellers are missing, and the sellers are correctly pricing a cycle that is still deteriorating.

We rate THO a **HOLD** on income-hold grounds. The dividend is well-covered, the founder is buying, and the balance sheet can carry the company through the trough — reasons to hold rather than sell. But there is no near-term path to appreciation, and we would not add until backlog and estimate revisions stabilize. The score trajectory (5.0 → 5.8 → 5.6, now 4.7) reflects that the modest fundamental improvement priced earlier in 2026 has reversed: the June guidance cut and the towable collapse are genuine fundamental deterioration, not merely a lower price on unchanged facts, and the score moves down accordingly.
