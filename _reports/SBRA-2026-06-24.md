**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sabra Health Care REIT is a self-managed healthcare REIT mid-way through a deliberate, multi-year transformation away from its skilled-nursing roots toward managed senior housing. The portfolio of 361 properties plus loans and equity investments now generates more than half its revenue from private-pay sources for the first time, a milestone management framed as a "material change." That mix shift improves revenue quality and reduces dependence on government reimbursement, but it trades the contractual stability of triple-net leases for direct operating exposure to labor and food cost inflation in the senior-housing-managed (SHOP) segment, which now represents roughly 28% of undepreciated investment value and is growing fastest.

The financial profile is solid for the sub-sector: FY2025 AFFO of $1.47/share (up from $1.43), Q1 2026 AFFO of $0.39 (vs. $0.37 a year prior), net debt/adjusted EBITDA of 5.04x, ~$1.2B of liquidity, and a well-laddered debt stack with no near-term maturity wall. The $1.20 annualized dividend is covered roughly 1.3x by AFFO at a ~77% payout ratio, supporting the ~6.6% yield that is the core of the investment case. The Altman Z-Score of 1.43 flagging "distress" is a model artifact common to asset-heavy REITs and should be disregarded given covenant compliance and the liquidity position.

The reasons this is a hold rather than a buy are specific and current. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised down 3.6–5.6% across all forward periods over the past 90 days, and the company missed GAAP EPS in three of the last four quarters, including a 51.7% miss in Q3 2025. The growth model is equity-funded (share count rose ~8% YoY and ~24M forward shares remain at roughly $19), so accretion depends entirely on deploying capital at spreads above a cost of capital that is rising in a higher-for-longer rate environment. At ~12.4x annualized AFFO and roughly $19.30 on a stock that has round-tripped from a $21.28 high, there is no asymmetric entry. The yield is durable; the appreciation case is not yet established.

## Company Overview

Sabra (Nasdaq: SBRA), headquartered in Tustin, CA, operates through an UPREIT structure (Sabra Health Care LP) and reports as a single segment. As of March 31, 2026, the portfolio comprised 361 properties held for investment, 13 loans receivable, 5 preferred equity investments, and 2 unconsolidated JVs across the U.S. and Canada. The company is run lean, with just 58 full-time employees, under long-tenured leadership: Chairman/CEO Rick Matros (40+ years in the sector), CFO Michael Costa, and CIO Darrin Smith.

By undepreciated investment value, the mix is: Skilled Nursing/Transitional Care ~47.1% (the historical base, down from ~96% at inception), Senior Housing-Managed ~28.3% (the growth engine), Behavioral Health ~12.9%, Senior Housing-Leased ~7.6%, and Specialty Hospitals & Other ~3.8%. Sabra runs two operating models: triple-net leases (271 properties, 7-year weighted-average remaining term, CPI escalators capped 1.0–5.0%), where tenants bear operating costs; and the senior-housing-managed structure, where Sabra owns operations and recognizes resident fees and operating expenses directly.

Diversification is genuine. Texas is the largest state at ~15.8% of beds; no state exceeds 16%, and no single tenant relationship represents 10% or more of revenue across 61 operator relationships. The strategic identity is being a relationship-driven capital source to small and mid-sized operators through off-market deals; management cites that 100% of its SNF deals bypass broad auctions, which it credits for better pricing.

## Financial Analysis

The right lens for Sabra is FFO/AFFO, not GAAP net income, which is depressed by rising depreciation and amortization from the expanding managed portfolio (GAAP EPS was $0.16 in Q1 2026 vs. $0.17 a year prior).

Revenue is shifting from rent toward resident fees. FY2025 total revenue was ~$774.6M (rental $374.1M; resident fees $356.9M; interest/other $43.6M). Resident fees grew 25% YoY in FY25 and 51% YoY in Q1 2026 (to $116.7M), driven by acquisitions, lease-to-managed transitions, and occupancy/rate gains. Rental revenue is flat-to-down (-2% FY25; -1% Q1'26) as facilities transition to managed or are sold. Q1 2026 total revenue was $221.8M, up 20.9% YoY.

On earnings quality: FY2025 AFFO was $361.6M, up from $1.33/share in 2023. Q1 2026 AFFO was $100.1M, with FFO at $0.37. Operating cash flow was strong at $348.6M in FY25 and $98.4M in Q1'26 (vs. $80.3M). The $0.30 quarterly dividend against the ~$1.56 annualized AFFO run-rate implies the ~77% payout ratio and roughly 1.3x coverage.

The balance sheet is sound for the sub-sector. Total debt was ~$2.69B at 3/31/26 ($1.25B senior notes at 3.20–5.38%, ~$1.04B term loans, $355M revolver, $43.5M secured), against ~$2.79B equity for D/E of ~0.95x. Liquidity of ~$1.2B comprises $116.5M cash, $645M revolver availability, and ~$451M of forward equity. 2027 maturities are modest ($100M notes plus revolver), with $537.7M term loans in 2028 and the bulk in 2029–2031. Term loans are swapped to fixed at effective ~4.18% USD / 3.84% CAD.

Two cautions temper the financial-health read. First, the earnings-surprise track record has deteriorated: three GAAP misses in the last four quarters (-5.3%, -37.6%, -51.7%) against one large beat (+51.3% in Q2 2025), which reduces visibility into forward estimates. Second, diluted shares rose from ~236M (2024) to ~256M (Q1'26), with ~24M forward shares still outstanding at roughly $19. This is standard for a growth-oriented REIT but means per-share accretion depends on disciplined spread investing, and in a higher-for-longer rate environment the cost of equity capital is rising against deal yields in the high-6% range (the post-quarter Maryland SNF disposition was at a 6.8% yield).

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is the deliberate pivot to managed senior housing, where favorable demographics (75+ population CAGR ~10.1% through 2035), constrained SNF supply, and slowed new senior-housing construction support occupancy and rate recovery. The 51% YoY resident-fee growth reflects this, though much of it is acquisition-driven rather than purely organic.

The investment pipeline is robust: $400M closed or awarded YTD as of the Q1 call, with another ~$200M (managed senior housing plus SNF) expected to close in Q2, and management guiding to "materially exceed" 2025's total investments. Deals are largely off-market with existing partners. Management maintained 2026 guidance but flagged it may be revisited upward in Q2, a tentatively positive signal.

Set against that, the estimate-revision data runs the other way, and this is the dominant near-term forward indicator. Consensus has cut current-quarter EPS by 4.8%, next-quarter by 5.6%, current-year by 3.6%, and next-year by 5.3% over the trailing 90 days, with the same revisions appearing over the trailing 30 days — falling estimates with no sign of stabilization. Growth prospects are where this system's evidence shows the most reliable winner-selection signal, and Sabra screens negatively: revisions are falling, not rising, and the magnitude is nowhere near the +30% threshold that would flag a structural earnings re-rating. The realistic forward profile is mid-single-digit AFFO-per-share growth funded by spread investing, partially diluted by equity issuance. Crossing 50% private-pay revenue improves revenue quality and may, over time, support a modest re-rate toward SHOP-oriented peers, but it also introduces labor-cost volatility (SHOP operating expenses rose 22% in FY25 and 45% in Q1'26) that makes earnings less predictable than a pure net-lease book.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$19.30 (recently $18.63), SBRA carries a trailing/forward P/E around 29.6x, but the meaningful metric is the multiple on AFFO: roughly 12.4x the annualized Q1 run-rate of ~$1.56. The ~6.6% dividend yield, well-covered at the ~77% payout, is the clearest valuation support.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. The Health Care REIT peer median P/E is 23.4x with a 4.7% yield. SBRA's yield is well above that median, but several of the closest comparables are distorted (MPT and HR show negative GAAP P/Es), making AFFO yield the better gauge. Higher-quality, lower-cost-of-capital peers such as CareTrust (23.4x P/E, 4.1% yield) and the larger Welltower/Ventas complex trade at premiums that reflect superior cost of capital and execution, while LTC (14.5x, 6.1%) sits closer to Sabra's profile. Sabra's discount to the premium names is partly justified by its SNF concentration, equity-funded growth, and operating exposure in SHOP.

The evidence base argues against treating a cheap-looking yield as a buy signal. Across this system's calibration, the valuation dimension has been reliably anti-predictive — names that screen cheap have underperformed, and the effect is strengthening, particularly in Real Estate and Utilities where compressed multiples and high yields are typically beta rather than edge. Sabra lacks an independent catalyst that would force a re-rating: estimates are falling; there is no near-term event that closes a clear NAV gap; and the equity-funded model caps multiple expansion when the cost of equity is rising. The post-quarter disposition above book ($33.2M book → $79.4M gross sale) suggests conservative carrying values and supports NAV, which is a floor argument, not an asymmetric upside catalyst. I score valuation at 5.0 — fairly priced for the risk, with the yield as support but no mispricing to exploit.

## Competitive Landscape

Sabra's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving. It competes against larger healthcare REITs with structurally lower cost of capital (Welltower, Ventas, Omega, CareTrust, National Health Investors). Its differentiated niche is serving as a relationship-driven capital source to small and mid-sized operators through off-market deals, which management credits for avoiding competitive auctions and securing better pricing — a real, if modest, edge in deal sourcing.

The mix shift is a genuine repositioning that improves the durability of the franchise: crossing 50% private-pay revenue reduces government-reimbursement dependence, and SNF rent coverage at "all-time highs" of 2.46x EBITDAR/rent indicates the remaining SNF book is healthier than the sector reputation implies. The CMS repeal of federal minimum staffing standards effective 2/2/2026 removes a major operator cost overhang, supporting coverage. The offsetting factor is that Sabra's higher cost of capital relative to the premium REITs limits how aggressively it can compete for the highest-quality assets, and the SHOP pivot, while improving mix, exposes it to operating volatility its net-lease peers avoid. Competitive position is a useful secondary discriminator but its recent predictive strength runs at roughly half its all-time level, so I weight the improving trajectory but do not treat it as conclusive — score 6.0, consistent with last read after confirming the underlying drivers persist.

## Risk Assessment

The largest single exposure is SNF reimbursement and policy risk: roughly 31–36% of revenue derives from SNFs dependent on Medicare/Medicaid. The FY2026 Medicare rate increase was +3.2%, but the FY2027 proposed rule (issued 4/2/2026) is a smaller +2.4%, signaling decelerating rate growth, and Medicaid faces state-budget and potential federal-cut pressure. The CMS minimum-staffing repeal is a meaningful offset on the cost side.

Operating exposure in SHOP is a structural risk that grows with the portfolio: Sabra directly bears labor, food, and utility inflation across a rising share of its investment value, making earnings more volatile and labor-sensitive than a net-lease book. Tenant/operator credit and transition risk remains (non-cash rent write-offs of $7.8M in FY25), though the 2.46x SNF coverage mitigates near-term concern. Leverage and refinancing risk is moderate given the well-laddered stack, but a portion of borrowings carries variable rates (~4.7%), and a higher-for-longer path raises refi costs on 2027–2028 maturities. Equity-funded growth introduces ongoing dilution. The 10-K flags proposed federal/state laws that could restrict REIT ownership of nursing homes, a newer, idiosyncratic risk worth monitoring. Key-person risk is concentrated in CEO Matros.

I score risk factors at 5.0, holding the prior level. The known risks (SNF reimbursement, SHOP operating volatility, dilution) are largely priced and there are no fresh binary catalysts surfacing, but the falling estimates and recent miss streak indicate forward visibility is deteriorating rather than improving, which offsets the favorable staffing-rule and coverage developments. The 8-K "officer departure" flag was benign — a CIO retention agreement, not an exit.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing shows mild near-term caution. Near-term ATM IV of 30.4% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 20.9% (a 9.5-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 30.4% > long-term 28.2%), which the market typically reads as pricing imminent stress, likely the next earnings print and the Q2 guidance revision. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.4%) and put/call volume is negligible, so this is not a directional bearish bet. More notable is the short-interest profile: 16.0% of float short with 12.6 days to cover — elevated institutional bearish positioning that, combined with the recent ~6.8% price decline since the prior snapshot, suggests skepticism on the equity-funded growth model and rate sensitivity. The elevated short interest does create modest squeeze potential on any upside surprise (a Q2 guidance raise), but the days-to-cover figure tempers that. None of this overrides the fundamental read; it confirms a stock the market views as fairly valued with two-sided near-term risk into earnings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$23–25)

The mix shift to managed senior housing accelerates organic growth as occupancy and rates recover into favorable 75+ demographics and constrained supply. Management executes the ~$600M H1'26 pipeline at accretive spreads, raises 2026 guidance in Q2 as flagged, and the stock re-rates toward higher-quality SHOP peers as private-pay revenue durability is recognized. SNF coverage stays at all-time highs, the staffing-rule repeal lifts operator margins, and the elevated short interest unwinds. AFFO grows to ~$1.65+ and the multiple expands toward 14x, plus the ~6.6% dividend. This aligns with the high end of analyst targets at $25.

### Bear Case (~$15–16)

A higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of equity capital above achievable deal yields (high-6%), making the equity-funded model dilutive rather than accretive. Estimate revisions continue lower, the GAAP miss streak extends, and SHOP labor inflation compresses managed-segment margins. Medicaid cuts under federal budget pressure hit SNF operators, and an operator transition triggers impairments. The yield premium widens to compensate, pushing the stock toward a ~7.5% yield on the held dividend (~$16). This is roughly 17–22% downside from current levels.

### Base Case (~$19–20)

AFFO grows mid-single digits to ~$1.55–1.60, the dividend holds and is covered ~1.3x, and the stock trades sideways-to-modestly-higher on its yield, delivering a total return dominated by the ~6.6% distribution. The multiple stays near ~12.5x AFFO absent a re-rating catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income-oriented hold with a 12-month reassessment horizon, anchored to the Q2 2026 earnings/guidance update and subsequent quarterly prints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive and stabilize for two consecutive quarters, ideally alongside a Q2 guidance raise that confirms accretive pipeline deployment.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $17s that pushes the AFFO yield and dividend yield to a level (≥7%) offering asymmetric income-plus-upside, with fundamentals intact.
- Demonstrated organic SHOP margin expansion (managed-segment NOI growth outpacing operating-expense growth) showing the operating model is working rather than just buying scale.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend coverage deterioration below ~1.1x AFFO, or any signal the dividend is at risk.
- A material adverse SNF reimbursement outcome (Medicaid cuts beyond the proposed +2.4% Medicare deceleration) combined with operator-coverage erosion below ~2.0x.
- Continued GAAP/AFFO misses with widening estimate cuts, indicating the equity-funded model is dilutive rather than accretive.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release and the flagged guidance revision, followed by the FY2027 final SNF rate rule.

## Conclusion

Sabra is a competently run healthcare REIT executing a genuine and constructive repositioning toward private-pay revenue, with a well-covered ~6.6% dividend, moderate leverage, ample liquidity, and no near-term refinancing wall. The score trajectory has improved (5.7 → 6.2 over the prior two reads, driven by competitive position and risk), and the underlying business changes — crossing 50% private-pay, all-time-high SNF coverage, the staffing-rule repeal — support that improvement. The recent ~6.8% price decline reflects rate sensitivity and skepticism on the equity-funded model rather than fundamental deterioration.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the absence of an asymmetric setup. Estimates are falling across forward periods, the company has missed GAAP EPS in three of four quarters, the growth model depends on spread discipline that a higher-for-longer rate path is squeezing, and the valuation offers yield support but no identifiable mispricing or catalyst to drive re-rating. In a sub-sector where cheap-screening yields have been reliably anti-predictive and Real Estate BUYs carry negative alpha, the bar for a BUY is a positive estimate-revision turn or a meaningfully lower entry price, neither of which is present. Own it for the durable, well-covered income; revisit for appreciation if Q2 guidance is raised and revisions turn positive, or if the price falls into the low-to-mid $17s with fundamentals intact. Recommendation: HOLD (income hold). Analyst targets span $19.00 to $25.00, with a $22.00 median.
