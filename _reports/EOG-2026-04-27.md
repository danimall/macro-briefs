## Executive Summary

EOG Resources is one of the highest-quality independent E&P operators in the U.S., currently positioned at a favorable intersection of structural and cyclical catalysts. FY2025 results reflected margin compression from lower crude realizations ($65.63/bbl vs. $77.40 in 2024), but Hormuz-related disruption and global oil supply tightening have driven a sharp reversal in 2026 commodity pricing. Analyst consensus for FY2026 EPS has surged 54% over 90 days to $14.79, and the current-quarter estimate of $3.16 has risen 39% in the same period. At ~9x forward earnings, EOG trades at a material discount to its peer median (32.6x) even adjusting for the higher quality of its asset base and balance sheet.

The $5.7B Encino acquisition, closed in August 2025, expanded EOG's Utica position to 1.1M+ net acres and is ramping from 55 to 85 net well completions in 2026. This adds meaningful gas-weighted production as natural gas prices recover, and EOG has a 10-year Brent-linked gas contract commencing 2027. The balance sheet absorbed the acquisition, moving debt-to-capital from 14% to 21%, which remains conservative relative to peers. Operating cash flow of $10B in 2025 (a down year for crude prices) underpins a 70%+ return-of-capital commitment and a $10B buyback authorization. The primary risk is EOG's limited hedging exposure: a sustained crude price decline below $60 WTI would compress returns materially. At current prices, the risk/reward profile is asymmetrically favorable.

## Company Overview

EOG Resources is one of the largest independent crude oil and natural gas exploration and production companies in the U.S. by market capitalization (~$71B). Headquartered in Houston, EOG operates primarily across the Delaware Basin (Permian), South Texas (Eagle Ford and Dorado), Appalachian Basin (Utica), and Rocky Mountain regions, with smaller operations in Trinidad and nascent exploration in Bahrain and the UAE.

Total 2025 production reached 1,232.2 MBoed, up 16% from 1,062.1 MBoed in 2024, driven by both organic growth and 5 months of Encino-acquired Utica volumes. Proved reserves stood at 5,514 MMBoe at year-end 2025, up 766 MMBoe. The production mix is 35% crude oil/condensate, 27% NGLs, and 38% natural gas, shifting gradually toward gas with the Utica and Dorado ramp-ups.

EOG differentiates through proprietary drilling technology (downhole motors, self-sourced sand, extended laterals), a decentralized organizational structure, and a ~$50 WTI breakeven that positions the company as one of the lowest-cost U.S. producers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** Revenue declined from $23.4B in 2024 to $22.6B in 2025, reflecting a 15% drop in crude oil realizations that overwhelmed 6% crude volume growth and 17% NGL volume growth. Natural gas was the bright spot, with revenue up 80% on 39% higher prices and 30% higher volumes. Operating margin compressed to 28.3% from 34.6%, and net margin fell to 22.1% from 27.4%, driven by lower crude prices, higher DD&A ($4.46B vs. $4.11B), increased GP&T costs ($2.13B vs. $1.72B from Utica ramp), $100M in Encino acquisition-related G&A, higher interest expense ($235M vs. $138M), and $843M in impairments.

Per-unit cost discipline remains strong: total costs per BOE actually declined to $20.72 from $21.12, with lower lease/well costs ($3.72 vs. $4.04) and lower DD&A per BOE ($9.34 vs. $10.04) partially offsetting higher GP&T ($4.74 vs. $4.43).

**Balance Sheet:** The Encino acquisition meaningfully shifted the balance sheet. Long-term debt nearly doubled from $4.2B to $7.9B, funded by $4.47B in new senior notes (4.4%-5.95% coupons). Cash dropped from $7.1B to $3.4B. Debt-to-capital rose from 14% to 21%. These levels remain conservative for large-cap E&P; the $3.0B undrawn revolver provides total liquidity of ~$6.4B.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $10.0B in 2025, down from $12.1B in 2024 on lower realizations. Organic capex was approximately $6.1B, generating organic FCF of ~$3.9B. EOG returned ~$4.8B to shareholders ($2.2B dividends + $2.6B buybacks), supplementing FCF with balance sheet cash. The current annualized dividend of $4.08/share provides a 3.1% yield.

**Share count reduction:** Basic shares declined from 583M (2022) to 543M (2025), a 6.9% cumulative reduction. The $10B buyback authorization (expanded from $5B in November 2024) provides significant remaining capacity.

**Price Sensitivity:** Each $1/bbl change in crude prices impacts net income by ~$174M and pretax OCF by ~$223M. Each $0.10/Mcf change in natural gas affects net income by ~$64M and OCF by ~$83M.

## Growth Analysis

The growth trajectory for EOG is driven by three vectors:

**1. Commodity price recovery.** FY2026 EPS estimates have risen from $9.62 to $14.79 over 90 days (54%), reflecting the oil price environment created by Hormuz disruptions and tightening global supply. The current-quarter estimate of $3.16 is up 39% in 90 days, and the next-quarter estimate of $4.18 has nearly doubled (+88% in 90 days). Sell-side models are still catching up to the current commodity tape.

**2. Encino/Utica ramp.** EOG plans to increase Utica completions from 55 to 85 net wells in 2026, a 55% increase. The acquisition added 675,000 net acres and ~200+ MBoed of production. Management has identified $150M in synergy targets, and integration is on track with the opening of a Columbus, Ohio division. A 50% well cost reduction in the Utica has been achieved relative to pre-acquisition economics.

**3. Dorado (South Texas) gas development and 10-year Brent-linked gas contract.** Commencing in 2027, EOG will sell 180,000 MMBtud of domestic gas production linked to Brent pricing. This converts a portion of gas production into oil-price-correlated revenue, adding significant value if Brent remains elevated. It also provides a natural hedge for the shifting production mix toward gas.

Total company production grew 16% in 2025, and guided 2026 capex of $6.3-6.7B supports continued growth in the low-to-mid single digits organically, supplemented by Utica ramp contributions.

**Earnings beat record:** 4 of 4 quarters beaten, with an average surprise of 4.7% and a 10.4% beat in Q3 2025. This track record suggests current estimates may still be conservative, particularly given the accelerating revision trend.

## Competitive Landscape

EOG occupies a premium competitive position among independent E&P operators. Its ~$50 WTI breakeven is among the lowest in the U.S., providing a structural cost advantage that enables positive returns through downturns and outsized returns in elevated commodity environments.

The Encino acquisition made EOG the dominant Utica Shale operator. The 50% well cost reduction achieved post-acquisition demonstrates EOG's ability to apply its proprietary technology and operational discipline to acquired assets, positioning the Utica as a meaningful growth lever over the next 3-5 years.

EOG's competitive advantages include: (1) proprietary drilling technology and self-sourced sand that reduces well costs relative to peers on comparable formations; (2) a decentralized organizational structure that empowers field-level decision-making; (3) multi-basin diversification across Delaware, Eagle Ford, Utica, and Rockies, reducing single-basin concentration risk; and (4) internally generated prospect inventory rather than dependence on acquisitions.

The U.S.-global crude price divergence, where domestic producers benefit from structurally lower input costs while selling into globally elevated product markets, provides an additional competitive tailwind. EOG's domestic production base (~99% U.S.) positions it to capture this divergence more fully than international peers.

Competitive risks include the ongoing production mix shift toward natural gas (lower-margin) and the potential for well productivity to plateau as tier-1 inventory is gradually consumed across the Delaware Basin.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Commodity Price Exposure (Primary Risk).** EOG has minimal hedging in place for 2026: natural gas swaps cover only ~460 MMBtud against 2,500+ MMcfd production, with no apparent crude oil hedges. This provides full upside exposure but creates significant downside vulnerability. A sustained move below $60 WTI would compress earnings to the $7-8 range and pressure the return-of-capital commitment.

**2. Encino Integration and Leverage.** The acquisition introduced execution risk on the Utica ramp. The $7.9B debt load at 4.4-5.95% coupons adds ~$400M in annual interest expense.

**3. Declining ROCE.** Return on Capital Employed declined from 35.8% (2022) to 18.7% (2025). While largely commodity-driven, this trajectory warrants monitoring. If ROCE continues to decline even as crude prices recover, it would signal structural margin pressure from higher costs or lower-return development.

**4. Production Mix Shift.** Oil and NGL production fell from 72% to 68% of U.S. volumes in 2025. The Utica and Dorado ramp-ups are gas-weighted, which will continue this trend. Gas is lower-margin than crude, creating potential for flat or declining margins per BOE even with volume growth.

**5. Impairment Risk.** FY2025 impairments totaled $843M (vs. $391M in 2024), including Barnett Shale and Woodford Oil Window write-downs. Additional impairments are possible if commodity prices moderate in legacy basins.

## Conclusion

EOG Resources is a best-in-class E&P operator trading at a compressed multiple during a period of sharply rising earnings estimates. The combination of lowest-cost production economics, an acquisition now in ramp phase, and the structural tailwind from Hormuz-related oil supply disruptions creates a favorable risk/reward profile. The 3.1% dividend yield provides a floor, while the $10B buyback authorization and 70%+ return-of-capital commitment ensure shareholders participate meaningfully in the commodity upside. The primary risk, EOG's lack of hedging, is also the primary source of upside optionality at current oil prices. At ~9x FY2026 EPS with estimates still rising, the stock offers meaningful value relative to both historical multiples and peers. BUY with a base case target of $155.
