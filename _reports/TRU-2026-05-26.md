## Executive Summary

TransUnion is a global consumer credit bureau operating in an oligopolistic industry structure with Equifax and Experian, generating $4.58B in 2025 revenue across credit reporting, analytics, and identity solutions. The stock has declined 29% from its 52-week high of $99.39 to $70.57, driven by macroeconomic concerns about consumer lending volumes and broader market weakness. Q1 2026 revenue grew 13.7% YoY — the fastest rate in years — driven by U.S. Financial Services (+24%), as rate cuts in late 2025 began catalyzing mortgage refinancing activity.

The investment case centers on an operational transition: a $373M technology transformation completed in December 2025 is now generating $130M in annualized savings, CapEx is declining from 7.1% to ~6% of revenue, and the company raised full-year 2026 guidance to $4.68-$4.75 adjusted EPS (8-9% organic revenue growth). At ~15x forward earnings, the stock appears meaningfully below intrinsic value relative to a business with 36% EBITDA margins, high barriers to entry, and accelerating growth.

However, near-term estimate revisions are negative (-2.2% current quarter, -2.8% next quarter over 30 days), management destroyed capital through poorly-timed buybacks ($302M at $84.45 average), leverage remains elevated ($5.1B debt, 2.6x net leverage), and macro sensitivity to lending volumes creates binary exposure to rate direction and consumer health. The analyst consensus mean target of ~$90 implies 28% upside, but the negative revision trend and macro headwinds warrant patience before upgrading.

## Company Overview

TransUnion operates as one of three national consumer reporting agencies in the United States, maintaining credit files on approximately 200 million U.S. adults. The business extends into analytics, identity verification, fraud prevention, and marketing solutions across 30+ countries.

**Segment breakdown (2025):**
- U.S. Markets: $3.58B (78% of revenue) — Financial Services ($1.68B), Emerging Verticals ($1.32B), Consumer Interactive ($575M)
- International: $1.01B (22%) — India ($264M), UK ($270M), Canada ($167M), Latin America ($135M), Asia Pacific ($101M), Africa ($74M)

The company completed its acquisition of Trans Union de Mexico in Q1 2026, gaining a leading position in Latin America's second-largest economy. A pending acquisition of RealNetworks' mobile division adds AI/ML capabilities for real-time analysis of text, multimedia messages, and phone calls, augmenting fraud prevention.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:**
Revenue accelerated from 3.3% growth (2023) to 9.2% (2024) to 9.4% (2025), and reached 13.7% YoY in Q1 2026. The 2023 trough reflected suppressed lending volumes from aggressive Fed tightening; the recovery tracks the gradual normalization of consumer credit activity.

**Profitability:**
- Adjusted EBITDA: $1.65B (2025), 36.0% margin
- GAAP operating margin: 18.7% (2025), up from 15.9% (2024) and 3.4% (2023, impaired by UK goodwill write-down)
- Adjusted diluted EPS: $4.30 (2025), up 10% from $3.91 (2024)
- 2026 guidance: $4.68-$4.75 adjusted EPS

**Cash flow:**
- Operating cash flow: $988M (2025), up from $833M (2024) and $645M (2023)
- FCF: ~$662M (2025), implying ~14.5% FCF margin
- CapEx declining from 7.1% to target ~6% of revenue in 2026

**Balance sheet:**
- Total debt: $5.1B; net debt: $4.25B
- Net leverage: 2.6x adjusted EBITDA (down from 3.0x in 2024 and 3.6x in 2023)
- Interest expense: $236M (2025), declining from $288M (2023)
- 75.5% of debt hedged via swaps at 3.07%-3.99% fixed rates
- Goodwill + intangibles: $8.36B (75% of total assets) — Altman Z-Score 1.72 (technically distress zone, but misleading for this business model given stable recurring revenues and strong cash generation)
- Revolver expanded to $1.0B (February 2026); $599M available

**Capital allocation — a weakness:**
Management repurchased $302M of stock in 2025 at an average price of $84.45. The stock currently trades at $70.57, representing 16% value destruction on those buybacks. While $700M remains authorized and future buybacks at current levels could be value-creating, the historical pattern suggests poor timing discipline. Total shareholder returns in 2025 included $302M in buybacks and $90.5M in dividends ($0.46/share, ~0.65% yield).

## Growth Analysis

1. Mortgage refinancing wave: U.S. Financial Services grew 24% in Q1 2026 as the 75bp of rate cuts in late 2025 catalyzed refi activity. Each mortgage origination generates multiple credit pulls across the three bureaus.
2. Transformation savings: $130M annualized now fully realized, supporting margin expansion.
3. Mexico consolidation: Full ownership provides revenue and margin uplift.
4. OneTru platform migration: Enables faster product development and cross-selling; first U.S. credit customers migrated in 2025.

**Medium-term drivers:**
- Emerging Verticals penetration (insurance, public sector, collections)
- International expansion (India credit penetration, Latin America growth post-Mexico)
- AI/ML product development via RealNetworks acquisition

**Growth headwinds:**
- India regulatory tightening slowed growth to -1.9% in 2025 after +23.1% in 2024
- Consumer Interactive declined 2.3% in 2025
- Near-term estimate revisions are negative (-2.2% current quarter, -2.8% next quarter)
- Macro uncertainty from tariffs could suppress lending volumes

The negative revision trend is the primary near-term concern. While full-year 2026 estimates are essentially flat (-0.1% 30-day revision), the quarterly trajectory suggests analysts are becoming incrementally cautious about the pace of recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

TransUnion operates in a regulated oligopoly with structural barriers:
- Decades of accumulated credit data on 200M+ consumers
- Regulatory requirements for multi-bureau pulls in mortgage origination
- Deep integration into customer decision workflows
- Network effects (more data → better models → more customers → more data)

The competitive position is improving through:
- OneTru platform creating analytical differentiation vs. legacy systems at competitors
- Mexico acquisition extending Latin American lead
- Insurance vertical growth (unique driver violation data)
- AI/ML investment (fraud prevention, identity resolution)

Threats include FHFA evaluation of allowing mortgage origination with fewer than three credit reports (could reduce volumes per bureau if implemented), free credit alternatives from banks/fintechs eroding Consumer Interactive, and alternative data scoring models.

TransUnion's competitive position relative to Equifax and Experian is stable-to-improving operationally, but the stock's massive TSR underperformance vs. peers (88.6 vs. 194.2 over the measurement period per the proxy) indicates the market has penalized TRU's higher leverage and execution missteps (2023 UK impairment, 2025 data breach).

## Risk Assessment

**1. Macroeconomic Sensitivity (HIGH):**
37% of revenue is directly tied to financial services lending volumes. If tariff uncertainty or rate re-escalation suppresses consumer credit activity, the Q1 acceleration reverses quickly. The business amplifies both economic upswings and downturns.

**2. Leverage (MODERATE, IMPROVING):**
Net leverage at 2.6x is manageable and declining, but $5.1B in absolute debt against $8.36B in goodwill/intangibles creates impairment risk in a downturn. The UK precedent ($414M write-down in 2023) demonstrates this is not theoretical.

**3. Regulatory (MODERATE):**
CFPB enforcement authority was authorized in July 2024 following a March 2024 NORA letter regarding dispute handling practices; engagement subsequently "paused" due to leadership changes — resumption is uncertain. Colorado AI Act (effective June 2026) and EU AI Act (applying starting August 2026) create compliance costs. FHFA single-bureau evaluation is a low-probability, high-impact threat.

**4. Data Breach Liability (LOW-MODERATE):**
July 2025 breach affecting 4.4M consumers has generated class actions and regulatory inquiries. While management characterized it as "not material," the litigation environment (class actions at 10-year highs) creates contingent liability.

**5. Capital Allocation (MODERATE):**
Multiple executive departures in late 2025/early 2026 (three 5.02 filings in approximately 4 months) introduce management continuity risk.

## Conclusion

TransUnion presents a compelling valuation case: 15x forward earnings for a business with oligopolistic positioning, 36% EBITDA margins, accelerating growth, and a completed transformation generating $130M in savings. The 29% drawdown has created meaningful distance between price and intrinsic value, with the analyst consensus implying 28% upside.

The recommendation remains HOLD rather than BUY for three specific, falsifiable reasons: (1) near-term estimate revisions are negative, indicating momentum has not yet confirmed despite Q1's strong print; (2) management demonstrated poor capital allocation discipline on 2025 buybacks, raising questions about shareholder alignment; and (3) the business carries $5.1B in debt with 75% of assets as goodwill/intangibles, creating meaningful downside convexity in a recession scenario where the 37% Financial Services revenue stream is directly exposed to lending volume compression — a plausible outcome given current macro uncertainty around tariffs and consumer spending deterioration.

The stock upgrades to BUY on either: (a) estimate revisions stabilizing with Q2 confirmation of sustained >10% growth, or (b) price reaching $65 or below, where the risk/reward becomes asymmetric regardless of near-term momentum.
