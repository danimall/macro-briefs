## Executive Summary

CAVA's Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended 07/12/2026) was operationally strong and cleanly reported: revenue $368.4M (+31.3%), same-restaurant sales +9.0% split +5.3% traffic and +3.7% price/mix, 17 net new units to 476, trailing AUV $3,088, restaurant-level profit $93.8M at a 25.7% margin, net income $23.0M, diluted EPS $0.19, adjusted EBITDA $54.7M (+30.0%). Positive traffic of that magnitude, in a category that external coverage describes as ceding occasions to grocery and convenience, is share capture rather than a rising tide, and it is the single most valuable fact in the file. The balance sheet reinforces it: $322.8M cash plus $112.8M of fixed-income securities, **zero borrowings**, and a revolver upsized to $150M and extended to March 2031 in the March 2026 amendment, undrawn but for $0.9M of letters of credit.

Three things keep this from being a buy. First, price. On the $60.81 basis the equity is ~$7.10B, enterprise value ex-leases ~$6.67B, which is 4.85x TTM revenue of $1,374.0M, **36.6x TTM adjusted EBITDA of $182.3M** (39.4x lease-inclusive), 35.8x the FY2026 guided EBITDA midpoint of $186M, and ~107x trailing GAAP earnings. On the tradeable $71.65 print those become 5.8x sales and ~43.5x EBITDA. FY2027 consensus EPS of $0.75 puts the stock at 81x forward at the basis price and 96x at the tradeable price; PEG on the ~36% implied FY26→FY27 EPS growth is roughly 2.2x. There is no version of this multiple that qualifies as mispriced-cheap.

Second, management's own guidance describes an H2 slowdown. Reaffirmed (not raised) FY2026 same-restaurant sales of 4.5-6.5% against H1 actual of +9.4% implies, on a week-weighted approximation, H2 comps of roughly **-1% to +3%**, and the prior-year H2 base is soft (FY2025 full-year comps +4.0% against H1 +6.6% implies ~+1% in H2). The reaffirmed restaurant-level margin guide of 23.7-24.3% sits below the 25.3% H1 actual, which makes the sales guide look like a coherent internal view rather than pure conservatism. Third, the 10-Q adds a new disclosure that a July 2026 multistate cyclosporiasis outbreak tied to iceberg lettuce, with no CAVA ingredient implicated, has "adversely impacted our revenue during the third quarter to date," unquantified.

Scores move from 6.2 to 5.95, a modest decline driven by identifiable new information rather than price: the live Q3 demand impairment, a Delaware derivative action filed 07/22/2026 seeking disgorgement of alleged insider trading profits, and guidance reaffirmed rather than raised alongside a margin guide below the H1 run rate. The prior improving trajectory (valuation 3.0→5.5, financial health 5.5→7.0) was earned largely at prices in the low $60s; an 18% post-print re-rating consumed most of the gap to fair value in a single session.

## Company Overview

CAVA operates a wholly company-owned (no franchising) fast-casual Mediterranean chain built on a customizable bowl-and-pita assembly line. Founded in Bethesda in 2011, holding company formed in Delaware 2015, headquartered in Washington, D.C. Unit count went from 367 (FY2024) to 439 (12/28/2025) to **476 in 29 states and D.C.** at 07/12/2026, with more than 15,000 team members.

Two operating segments exist, CAVA (restaurants) and CAVA Foods (centralized dip/spread production plus retail CPG), but CAVA Foods falls below quantitative thresholds, so restaurants are the single reportable segment and CPG sits in "Other." "Other" produced $3.0M of Q2's $368.4M revenue (0.8%) and a **segment loss of $1.77M** in the quarter ($4.11M for 28 weeks). CPG is a brand-awareness and vertical-integration asset; it should not be valued as a second growth engine.

Vertical integration comprises production facilities in Laurel, MD and Verona, VA (opened Q1 FY2024), a distribution center in Edison, NJ, and support offices in Brooklyn, Manhattan and Plano. All restaurants are leased on typical 10-year initial terms with renewal options generally excluded from the lease term. Digital mix reached **39.0%** in Q2 (37.3% prior year; 37.9% FY2025), with substantially all delivery, including orders placed on CAVA's own app, fulfilled by third-party couriers. FY2026 investments include drive-thru pick-up lanes ("a meaningful portion" of openings, explicitly disclosed as carrying higher capex and real-estate cost), Digital and Hybrid Kitchens for catering, and AI tooling for ingredient-depletion monitoring.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and units.** FY2023 $728.7M → FY2024 $963.7M (+32.3%) → FY2025 $1,179.7M (+22.4%) → H1 FY2026 $806.7M (+31.7% on the 28-week comparable). Net new openings: 58, 72, and 37 in H1 FY2026 against 75-77 guided for the year, so the build is back-half weighted as in FY2025 (24 of 72 net new came in Q4).

**The comp cycle is the dominant variable.** Quarterly same-restaurant sales: +2.1% (Q2 FY25), +1.9% (Q3), **+0.5% (Q4)**, then **+9.7% (Q1 FY26)** and **+9.0% (Q2 FY26)**. Restaurant-level margin over the same sequence: 26.3%, 24.6%, 21.4%, 25.1%, 25.7%. The Q4 21.4% should not be treated as a run rate: the 10-K discloses Q1/Q4 seasonality (colder weather, holidays) against fixed occupancy, Q4 carried the heaviest opening cadence at 24 net new units that run worse in their first six months, and the chicken shawarma LTO raised food cost as a percentage of revenue in Q3-Q4. A concept that swung 13 points of comp peak-to-trough inside eight quarters does not deserve a stability premium.

**Margins.** Restaurant-level margin went 25.0% (FY2024) → 24.4% (FY2025) → 25.7% in Q2 FY2026, each period down ~60bps year over year. Management names the causes and calls them deliberate: Pomegranate Glazed Salmon (launched 04/20/2026), higher third-party delivery mix, ~3% higher average hourly wages including expansion of the Assistant General Manager role, tariff-driven costs on olive oil, sugar, rice, beef, lamb and paper, and menu pricing held **below inflation**. Salmon and delivery are dilutive to margin *rate* and accretive to margin *dollars* through higher checks: restaurant-level profit dollars grew 19.7% in FY2025 and 28.1% in Q2 FY2026, and traffic went from +1.6% (FY2025) to +6.1% (H1 FY2026). The trade is working. It is also a reason four-wall percentage margin may not return to 25%+, which the FY2026 guide of 23.7-24.3% concedes.

G&A leverage is the clearest structural offset: 12.5% of revenue (FY2024) → 11.7% (FY2025) → **10.8% in Q2 FY2026** (9.3% excluding equity comp and executive transition costs). Part of the quarterly improvement is timing (prior-year CAVA Connect conference, incentive accrual phasing), so the underlying gain is smaller than 50bps, but the multi-year direction is unambiguous.

**Consolidated.** FY2025 operating income $55.3M (4.7% margin) vs $43.1M (4.5%); adjusted EBITDA $152.8M (12.9%) vs $126.2M (13.1%); Q2 FY2026 adjusted EBITDA $54.7M (14.9%), H1 $116.5M (14.4%).

**Do not read FY2025 as an earnings collapse.** FY2024 GAAP net income of $130.3M included an **$80.1M net benefit from releasing the deferred-tax valuation allowance**; on management's comparable adjusted-net-income bridge, FY2024 $50.2M → FY2025 $63.7M, **+26.9%**. The corollary matters more going forward: discrete excess tax benefits on equity vesting fell from $12.4M (H1 FY2025) to $4.2M (H1 FY2026), and the effective rate moved from 0.0% to 23.2%. That mechanically compresses EPS growth below pre-tax growth in FY2026, and it is pro-cyclical with the share price: a lower stock means smaller future benefits.

**Cash flow.** H1 FY2026 operating cash flow $134.5M (from $98.9M), capex $89.7M, **free cash flow $44.8M**. FY2025: OCF $184.8M, capex $158.7M, FCF $26.1M, a **2.2% FCF margin** on $1.18B of revenue. Earnings quality is high in the sense that cash is not receivable-financed (trade receivables $10.1M against $368M of quarterly revenue), and FY2025 OCF/net income was 2.90x on $73.7M of D&A and $18.1M of equity comp. But H1 FCF should not be annualized: openings are back-half weighted, Q1 is a 16-week quarter, and management attributes part of H1 OCF to favorable working capital "primarily associated with performance-based incentive compensation," a reversing timing item. Construction in progress rose from $42.2M to **$64.0M** and accrued capital purchases from $9.5M to $15.0M between 12/28/2025 and 07/12/2026, both consistent with an accelerating H2 build. With FY2026 capex disclosed only as "higher than fiscal 2025's $158.7M" and drive-thru formats costing more, FY2026 FCF plausibly lands in the $30-60M range against a $7.1B equity value. CAVA is a self-funding growth compounder, not a cash-returning one.

**Balance sheet, correctly read.** At 07/12/2026: cash $322.8M plus securities $112.8M = **$435.6M liquid**, **zero borrowings**, total liabilities $658.5M of which **$520.7M is operating lease liabilities**, equity $841.3M, total assets $1,499.8M. The March 2026 Amendment No. 3 extended maturity from 03/11/2027 to **03/20/2031** and doubled commitments to $150M at Term SOFR + 100-225bps; $149.1M available, all covenants met. Screening artifacts (an Altman Z of 2.85, D/E of 0.74) reflect ASC 842 lease capitalization and $437.9M of right-of-use assets, not funded leverage; solvency is not a live question. The real exposure is **operating** leverage: $624.9M of undiscounted lease commitments as of 12/28/2025, largely non-cancellable, against occupancy of 6.3% of revenue in Q2. Comfortable now; unflexible if traffic turns.

**Dilution is immaterial**, which is unusual for a 2023 IPO. Diluted shares 118.4M vs 116.8M outstanding; 1.776M options (WAEP $18.91), 0.645M unvested RSUs, 0.075M PSUs at target (0.150M at 200% max), roughly 2.57M shares, ~2.2% of outstanding, leaving existing holders at ~97.8% of proportional ownership if fully issued. Unrecognized comp expense $41.0M over 2.0-2.6 years; Q2 equity comp $5.7M, 1.5% of revenue.

## Growth Analysis

The unit engine is credible. New FY2025 vintages are characterized by management as trending above $3.0M AUV, at or above system average, which is the best available evidence of concept portability, though it is a management characterization rather than an audited cohort disclosure. Unit count grew 19.6% year over year; 476 units in 29 states leaves substantial domestic whitespace, and Las Vegas and the Bay Area are reported as next markets (externally sourced, not yet in a filing).

Costs of that growth are rising. Pre-opening cost per net new unit: FY2025 $266K ($19.1M ÷ 72); FY2026 guided ~$293K ($22.25M ÷ 76), **+10%**. The Zoes Kitchen conversion pipeline is exhausted, so all site selection is now organic into markets where CAVA has no operating experience, and the 10-K states permit, construction and tariff-driven equipment costs "have caused, and are continuing to cause, a delay in opening restaurants." Cannibalization in the dense D.C./Maryland/Virginia core is acknowledged.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than confirming. Current quarter $0.19 (+10.3% over 90 days) but **next quarter $0.13, revised -3.2% over 30 days and -8.6% over 90 days**; current year $0.55 (+3.8%/90d, -0.4%/30d); next year $0.75 (+2.7%/90d, -0.4%/30d). The negative next-quarter revision is the estimate-level footprint of the cyclospora drag and the guided H2 deceleration. This is not the >+30% revision velocity that signals a systematic underestimate of an inflection; and at 81-96x forward earnings, rising near-term estimates would not by themselves justify a re-rating.

The forward algorithm therefore looks like: ~14-16% unit growth, 4-6% comps in a normal year, modest four-wall margin rate erosion offset by G&A leverage, EBITDA margin drifting from 12.9% toward 13.5%, and EPS growth throttled by a normalizing tax rate. FY2026 revenue lands near $1.50-1.52B with guided EBITDA of $181-191M; FY2027 plausibly $1.78-1.83B of revenue and $225-245M of EBITDA. Strong, durable, decelerating.

## Competitive Landscape

CAVA is the definitional operator in an emerging category rather than a share-taker in an established one, and the 10-K is unusually candid that this cuts both ways: "our competitors will likely grow in number as the Mediterranean food category grows, and we may face the risk that new or existing competitors will mimic our business model, menu offerings, marketing strategies, and overall concept." Defenses are brand, real estate, assembly-line operational complexity, vertically integrated dip/spread production and a 39% digital mix, not patents (no material registered IP outside the U.S.) and not franchise agreements.

The named competitive set includes grocery stores, convenience stores, meal subscription services, delivery kitchens, and the delivery marketplaces themselves. That last point is the sharpest structural tension in the file: aggregators are simultaneously CAVA's largest fulfillment channel and platforms merchandising substitutes, and the 10-K flags aggregator consolidation as a source of future pricing leverage against CAVA. Delivery mix has been cited as a margin-rate headwind in three consecutive disclosures (FY2025, Q1 FY2026, Q2 FY2026) while digital mix climbed 36.0% → 36.4% → 37.9% → 39.0%. CAVA's offset is delivery-specific pricing, which sits awkwardly beside a stated policy of pricing menu below inflation.

Trajectory is favorable. Q2 traffic rose 5.3% while external coverage described fast casual ceding occasions to grocery and convenience, and management's own risk factors describe "a softening labor market," consumers shifting to lower-cost alternatives, and the possibility that trading down becomes permanent. Gaining traffic against that backdrop is evidence of share capture, which supports a competitive-position score above the sector average while stopping short of a durable-moat rating.

## Risk Assessment

**Live, unquantified Q3 demand shock.** The 10-Q adds a new "Additional Factors Affecting Our Business" paragraph: a July 2026 multistate cyclosporiasis outbreak associated with iceberg lettuce, with "no ingredients in our supply chain... implicated," yet "broader consumer concerns related to the outbreak have adversely impacted our revenue during the third quarter to date," moderating but of uncertain duration. Because CAVA is not the source, recovery should track news-cycle decay rather than remediation, historically weeks. No magnitude was disclosed, and "moderating" is management-sourced. A produce-forward menu makes this a recurring exposure class; the 10-K already documents a multi-month grape-tomato substitution in 2023-24.

**Guidance embeds deceleration.** The restaurant-margin guide of 23.7-24.3% versus 25.3% H1 actual corroborates a genuinely weaker internal H2 view. Externally reported bearish commentary — a short-seller's public criticism of the implied comp slowdown, and a sell-side-adjacent "growth borrowed from the future" piece — targets exactly this line; neither is verified against a primary source and no positions are confirmed.

**Governance.** Five Item 5.02 8-Ks in under twelve months (09/26/2025, 01/12/2026, 04/21/2026, 06/24/2026, 08/03/2026), a COO departure in September 2025 carrying $832K of transition costs, a CLO separation agreement executed 05/15/2026, and $545K of Q1 FY2026 executive transition costs. The individual filings were not readable, so who joined versus left in each case is unverified; a bench upgrade for scale is the most plausible reading but remains an inference. On 07/22/2026 the Cleveland Bakers and Teamsters Pension Fund filed a **derivative action in Delaware Chancery** against certain current and former officers and directors alleging breach of fiduciary duty and unjust enrichment and seeking "disgorgement of alleged improperly obtained trading profits." The complaint does not seek damages from the Company; exposure is D&O/indemnification cost and management attention. No link to any specific Form 4 is established.

**Cost and regulatory exposure.** Named single- or limited-source dependencies for olive oil and steak, heavy chicken purchasing, no long-term supply pricing agreements, and disclosed tariff pass-through on olive oil, sugar, rice, beef, lamb and paper. Labor at 25.3% of Q2 revenue with wages +~3%, plus California AB 1228 and its Fast Food Council, Fair Workweek/predictive scheduling rules including NYC, and NYC just-cause termination law.

**Tax-asset fragility.** The FY2024 valuation-allowance release was maintained for FY2025, but a sustained downturn could put partial re-establishment back on the table, a large non-cash GAAP hit. Deferred income taxes fell from $65.4M to $51.6M, which is normal use of the DTA (H1 deferred expense $13.9M), not impairment.

**Shareholder-base composition.** The 10-K notes the stockholder base is "increasingly comprised of retail (or non-institutional) investors, which may create additional stock price volatility." With 11.7% of float short (12.87M shares, 3.9 days to cover), the tape is structurally two-sided: the 18% post-print gap likely includes short covering, and the same mechanics work in reverse on a Q3 miss.

**Insider record — genuinely mixed.** Selling clustered 06/15-06/18/2026 at $89-90: pre-IPO holder **Artal Participations sold 3.0M shares at $90.30** (leaving 6.51M, ~5.6% of outstanding), CEO Schulman 33,174 at $89.43 (retaining ~799K), CFO Tolivar 4,969, co-founder Xenohristos 3,252 (retaining ~325K), CPO Costanza 15,360, and director Karen Kochevar sold 10,000 at $90.00 leaving **3,074 shares**, a near-complete exit. Against that, two newly seated executives bought in the open market at prices bracketing today's level: **COO Douglas Thompson 6,500 shares at $77.90-79.45 (~$513K)** and **CLO Joseph Kadow 2,000 shares at $70.00-79.00 (~$149K)**. Discretionary purchases by sitting officers are the higher-quality signal; much of the June selling is exercise-and-sell of deep-in-the-money legacy options and possibly pre-scheduled (the 10-Q discloses only that no 10b5-1 plan was adopted or terminated in the twelve weeks ended 07/12/2026). The uncomfortable facts remain the tightness of the June date range at what proved a local high, the director's near-exit, and the subject-matter overlap with the derivative suit. This meets the threshold for treating insider activity as a caution rather than noise, which is why Management Quality sits at 5.0 despite genuinely good capital discipline (no equity issuance, no debt, revolver upsized and extended, first-ever PSU grants in Q1 FY2026, RSU vesting shortened from four years to three). The unaddressed design flaw is that incentive metrics are Adjusted EBITDA and Revenue with **no ROIC, new-unit-return or restaurant-margin measure**; for a company whose entire case rests on returns against ~$2.5-3.0M per new box, that rewards building units over earning on them.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case (→ $90, +48% from $60.81 / +26% from $71.65)

Traffic, not price, is driving the comp reacceleration: +6.8% in Q1 and +5.3% in Q2 against a category losing occasions to grocery. New units are opening at or above the ~$3.1M system AUV, so the 75-77 unit annual cadence adds revenue at full economics rather than diluting the average. Restaurant-level profit dollars grew 28.1% in Q2 even as the rate fell 60bps, so the salmon/delivery trade is compounding checks and traffic. G&A leverage from 12.5% to 10.8% of revenue in two years is structural and continues as the base scales. The FY2026 guide is beatable: it embeds an unquantified cyclospora drag that management already says is moderating, and laps an H2 FY2025 base of roughly +1%. The balance sheet needs nothing — zero debt, $435.6M liquid, $149.1M undrawn to 2031, ~2.2% total equity overhang — so growth is fully self-funded with no dilution or refinancing risk. Two newly seated executives bought stock in the $70s. If FY2027 EBITDA reaches ~$255M and the market keeps paying 40x for a 15% unit grower with 20%+ revenue growth, the equity is worth ~$90.

### Bear case (→ $42, -31% from $60.81 / -41% from $71.65)

The comp series has swung 13 points in eight quarters, and management's own guidance says the swing is turning again: -1%-to-+3% implied H2 comps against an easy base, plus a margin guide 100bps below the H1 run rate. The reacceleration may be promotion-dependent — grilled steak, chicken shawarma, salmon in successive periods — in which case comps require a continuous cadence of higher-cost proteins, and the first quarter without a launch reveals the underlying rate. Delivery mix at 39% is a structural margin-rate drag with aggregators consolidating and gaining pricing leverage, while CAVA prices menu below inflation; those two policies collide. Growth capital intensity is rising (pre-opening per unit +10%, drive-thrus costing more, construction in progress up 52% in 28 weeks) so FCF stays near 2-4% of revenue and the 0.37% FCF yield offers no valuation support. The equity-comp tax shield is shrinking ($12.4M → $4.2M of discrete benefit), pushing the effective rate from 0% to 23.2% and capping EPS growth below pre-tax growth. Governance is noisy: five Item 5.02 filings in a year, a derivative suit over alleged trading profits, a director exiting 77% of her stake and the pre-IPO sponsor selling 3.0M shares at $90.30. At 43.5x EV/EBITDA on the tradeable price, a single quarter of sub-2% comps compresses the multiple to the mid-20s, which is $42-48.

## Conclusion

CAVA is a high-quality growth restaurant with a fortress funding position and the best operating datapoint in its category right now: 5.3% traffic growth while peers cede occasions to grocery. Zero borrowings, $435.6M of liquid assets, a $149.1M undrawn revolver to 2031, ~2.2% total equity overhang and positive free cash flow mean the 75-77 unit annual build needs no external capital. That earns high marks on financial health and solid marks on growth and competitive trajectory.

The stock does not offer asymmetric upside from here. On the authoritative $60.81 close it trades at 36.6x TTM adjusted EBITDA, 107x trailing earnings and 81x FY2027 consensus, with a 0.37% FCF yield; on the ~$71.65 post-print print those become 43.5x, 126x and 96x. Layer on five executive-departure filings in twelve months, a Delaware derivative action seeking disgorgement of alleged trading profits, a 3.0M-share sponsor sale at $90.30 and a director exiting 77% of her stake — partially offset by two newly seated executives buying in the $70s — and the risk and management dimensions cap the overall score at 5.95, down modestly from 6.2 on specific new information rather than price action.

The fifth consecutive HOLD is warranted, but the reasoning has shifted: previously the call was quality at a fair price awaiting comp confirmation; now the comps have confirmed and the price has moved to meet them. Own it, do not chase the gap, and let the Q3 print resolve whether the guided H2 slowdown was conservatism or the leading edge of the next comp downcycle.
