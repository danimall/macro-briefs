## Executive Summary

VICI Properties is an S&P 500 experiential net-lease REIT owning 93 gaming and entertainment properties across 26 US states and Canada, including trophy Las Vegas Strip assets (Caesars Palace, MGM Grand, The Venetian). The stock has declined from $34.01 to $28.11 over the past year, a 17% drawdown driven almost entirely by deteriorating fundamentals at Caesars' regional casino portfolio, where rent coverage has compressed to approximately 1.0x on a lease representing ~25% of VICI's NOI.

The Caesars Regional Master Lease is a legitimate risk that warrants significant analytical weight. VICI management has acknowledged "preliminary discussions" with Caesars about the lease, and a restructuring involving rent concessions, property transfers, or term extensions is the most probable near-term outcome. However, the stock at 10.8x trailing earnings (a 62% discount to the 28.4x REIT peer median P/E) and a 6.4% dividend yield with 73% AFFO payout coverage prices in a meaningfully negative resolution. The 39.6-year weighted-average lease term, 100% occupancy, contractual CPI-linked escalators on 90% of the portfolio (over the full term), investment-grade balance sheet, and diversification into non-gaming experiential properties provide structural durability that the valuation fails to reflect.

This is a BUY with a base case target of $33 over 12 months, contingent on a Caesars lease resolution that does not materially impair dividend coverage. The 6.4% yield provides meaningful carry while waiting for the catalyst.

## Company Overview

VICI Properties was spun out of the Caesars Entertainment bankruptcy in 2017. The company operates as a pure landlord, collecting triple-net rent where tenants bear all property costs, with only 28 employees and G&A expenses of $65M (1.6% of revenue). The portfolio spans ~127 million square feet, ~60,300 hotel rooms, and 500+ restaurants, bars, and nightclubs.

The business model is contractual: 100% occupancy with built-in annual rent escalators (42% CPI-linked in 2025, ramping to 90% over the portfolio's life, subject to caps). Revenue was $4.01B in 2025, growing 4.1% YoY. AFFO per share grew 5.1% to $2.38.

Key tenant concentration is high: Caesars Entertainment (~39% of annualized rent) and MGM Resorts (~35%) together represent 74% of leasing revenue. Las Vegas Strip properties generated approximately 49% of total 2025 revenue. The company has been diversifying through debt investments with conversion rights into non-gaming experiential properties (Chelsea Piers, Canyon Ranch, Great Wolf Resorts, Cabot golf), but gaming remains dominant.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** Revenue grew from $2.60B in 2022 to $4.01B in 2025 (15.5% CAGR), driven by the MGM Grand/Mandalay Bay acquisition and organic rent escalators. Net margin was 70.4% in 2025, reflecting the capital-light triple-net model. G&A has remained extraordinarily lean at $65M across 28 employees with minimal variable costs.

**AFFO — the core REIT metric:** AFFO was $2.53B in 2025, or $2.38/share, up 5.1% from $2.26 in 2024. Management guided 2026 AFFO up to $2.625B, implying 4-5% growth. Analyst consensus sits at $2.88 EPS for FY2026 and $2.95 for FY2027, with modest positive revisions of +0.5% and +1.0% over 30 days. Two analysts covering the name is thin, but the direction is positive.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $2.51B, growing 5.4% YoY. Investing cash flow was $(905M), primarily from $887M in loan portfolio fundings. Financing outflows of $(1.57B) reflect $1.85B in dividends partially offset by $1.28B in note issuances and $376M in ATM equity proceeds. The dividend of $1.80/share is well-covered at 73% of AFFO ($2.38), providing $0.58/share of cushion.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $17.1B against $46.7B in total assets. Debt-to-equity of 0.67x. Investment-grade ratings from all three agencies with stable outlooks. Available liquidity of $3.2B ($563M cash, $2.36B revolver, $243M forward equity). The weighted-average interest rate rose modestly from 4.34% to 4.46% as older debt was refinanced at higher rates. Interest expense of $844M consumed 33% of revenue, manageable for a net-lease REIT but a rising proportion as refinancing continues in a higher-rate environment.

**Debt Maturities:** $1.75B in 2026, $1.5B in 2027, $2.0B in 2028, $1.75B in 2029, $6.95B thereafter. The near-term maturities are manageable given the liquidity position and consistent capital markets access, but refinancing at higher rates will compress NII margins progressively.

**Credit Loss Allowance:** Rose 40% to $177.9M in 2025 from $126.7M, reflecting deteriorating tenant credit metrics and higher CECL reserves on new loan originations. A 10% increase in probability of default would add ~$92M; a 10% increase in loss-given-default would add ~$110M. This deserves monitoring as a leading indicator of tenant stress.

**Earnings Surprise Record:** 1 beat, 1 in-line, 2 misses over the last four quarters. The Q4 2025 miss (-18.3%) and Q1 2025 miss (-26.3%) are concerning and reduce confidence in near-term consensus. The beaten-up expectations and thin analyst coverage (2 analysts) mean the current estimates may be achievable but carry less conviction than a company with a consistent beat pattern.

## Growth Analysis

VICI's growth comes from three sources:

**1. Contractual rent escalators (4-5% organic):** The built-in escalators provide a durable floor. Currently 42% of the portfolio has CPI-linked escalators; this rises to 90% over time. With CPI running above 3%, the portfolio's organic growth rate should remain at or above the guided 4-5% range. The PENN lease modification, introducing a performance-contingent escalator, may preview a shift toward conditional escalators in future lease negotiations, which would slightly reduce the contractual certainty of this growth channel.

**2. Acquisitions:** The $1.16B Golden Entertainment acquisition (7 Nevada properties, $87M initial rent, 30-year term, 2% escalators) is on track after regulatory approvals and expected to close mid-2026. The CAD$200.6M Canadian portfolio adds international diversification. However, deal volume has slowed from the aggressive 2022-2023 pace, and Mizuho cited this deceleration in their downgrade. At current share price levels, equity-funded acquisitions are dilutive, limiting VICI's primary capital markets advantage.

**3. Embedded pipeline:** Extensive ROFR/ROFO/put-call rights, including a call on the Caesars Forum Convention Center (through Dec 2028) and ROFRs on multiple Las Vegas Strip Caesars properties (Flamingo, Paris, Planet Hollywood, Bally's, LINQ). These provide optionality but are discretionary and dependent on capital availability and pricing.

**4. Debt investment portfolio ($2.58B):** The lending platform generates investment income ($218M in 2025 vs. $134M in 2024) and potential conversion to owned properties. However, the portfolio carries meaningful construction/development risk, particularly the $450M One Beverly Hills mezzanine loan (PIK, maturing March 2026) and the $510M North Fork Casino loan (tribal sovereign land, unique enforceability risks). Future funding commitments of $623M are mostly due in 2026.

Growth is real but moderate: mid-single-digit AFFO/share growth is the reasonable expectation for 2026-2027. The Golden Entertainment acquisition and continued escalator income support this trajectory, but the Caesars lease situation caps the upside potential until resolved.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.11, VICI trades at:
- **10.8x trailing P/E** — a 62% discount to the 28.4x REIT peer median
- **~9.8x forward AFFO** (using consensus $2.88 for FY2026)
- **6.4% dividend yield** vs. 4.4% peer median
- **17% below 52-week high** of $34.01
- **~18% below median analyst target** of $34.00
- **Analyst price targets:** $29.00 (low) to $39.00 (high), with a mean of $34.35

The peer group includes IRM (169.5x), EXR (28.4x), CCI (35.3x), SBAC (19.7x), and AVB (24.5x). Even the cheapest peer (SBAC at 19.7x) trades at nearly double VICI's multiple. Some discount is warranted given the tenant concentration risk that no other name in this peer set faces, but a 62% discount implies the market is pricing either a significant rent reduction on the Caesars Regional Master Lease or a broader deterioration in tenant credit quality.

A scenario analysis on the Caesars lease restructuring illustrates the valuation range:

- **No restructuring needed:** AFFO stays at $2.38+, growing 5%; fair value at 13-14x AFFO = $33-35. Probability: 20%.
- **Moderate restructuring (10-15% rent reduction on regional lease):** AFFO declines ~$0.10-0.15/share; fair value at 12-13x = $28-33. Probability: 45%.
- **Significant restructuring (20-25% rent reduction + property transfers):** AFFO declines ~$0.20-0.30/share; fair value at 11-12x = $23-26. Probability: 25%.
- **Caesars default/bankruptcy:** Parent guarantee tested; extended legal process; AFFO at risk. Fair value $18-22. Probability: 10%.

The probability-weighted fair value is approximately $29-31, suggesting the current price of $28.11 is at or modestly below fair value even incorporating the full range of Caesars outcomes. With the 6.4% carry, total return to the probability-weighted midpoint exceeds 10% over 12 months.

## Competitive Landscape

VICI is the dominant gaming-focused REIT alongside Gaming and Leisure Properties (GLPI). VICI's competitive advantages include:

- **Trophy asset quality:** Caesars Palace, MGM Grand, The Venetian are irreplaceable Las Vegas Strip assets with decades of brand equity and location value
- **Investment-grade rating + S&P 500 membership:** Provides lower cost of capital than nearly all competitors, enabling accretive acquisitions when the equity trades at or above fair value
- **Scale:** 93 properties, $4B revenue, $3.2B liquidity — few counterparties can absorb deals of this size
- **Experiential diversification:** The push into fitness (Chelsea Piers), wellness (Canyon Ranch), golf (Cabot), and waterparks (Great Wolf) reduces long-term gaming dependency

The primary competitive risk is structural: VICI's business depends on the health of its tenants' operations. Prediction markets, flagged as a new risk in the 10-K, could create competitive headwinds for regulated gaming tenants who bear licensing, taxation, and compliance costs that prediction market operators may avoid. This is an emerging but real threat.

## Risk Assessment

### Caesars Regional Master Lease (HIGH)
The defining near-term risk. Coverage at ~1.0x on ~25% of NOI is unsustainable without intervention. Management's disclosure of "preliminary discussions" suggests a negotiated outcome is likely. The Caesars parent guarantee provides a structural backstop, but the 10-K explicitly flags the risk that in a Caesars bankruptcy, the 2020 additional rent could be recharacterized as disguised financing. Resolution is the primary catalyst for the stock.

### Tenant Concentration (HIGH)
74% of revenue from two tenants is an irreducible structural risk. Diversification through experiential acquisitions and lending will take years to materially change the ratio. The PENN lease modification and Northfield Park severance lease show incremental progress but don't move the needle.

### Interest Rate / Refinancing Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH)
With $17.1B in debt and rates elevated above 4.5%, each refinancing cycle incrementally compresses margins. In the current rate environment, where hike probability exceeds cut probability, VICI faces a sustained headwind. Each 50bp increase in refinancing rates on a $1.75B maturity adds ~$9M to annual interest expense.

### Development Loan Portfolio (MEDIUM)
The $450M One Beverly Hills mezzanine loan (PIK, maturing March 2026) and $510M North Fork Casino loan represent concentrated bets on specific development outcomes. Mezzanine positions are structurally subordinate. The 40% increase in credit loss allowances signals the company's own models are recognizing deterioration.

### Las Vegas Concentration (MEDIUM)
49% of revenue from properties on one geographic corridor. Water stress from Colorado River/Lake Mead depletion is a long-tail risk that receives increasing attention. Extreme heat events and tourism demand sensitivity to economic cycles are more immediate concerns. In a recession (probability now elevated), Las Vegas leisure spending would likely contract, putting additional pressure on tenant coverage ratios.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 29.8% is meaningfully rich versus 16.5% historical volatility, an 80%+ premium. The term structure is in backwardation (29.8% near-term vs. 20.5% at 3-6 months), consistent with the market pricing event risk in the very near term. The 1-week expiry shows a 7.24 put/call volume ratio, heavily skewed toward puts. The 6-month tenor shows a 4.56 P/C volume ratio, indicating institutional hedging extends well beyond any immediate event date.

This options positioning confirms the fundamental concern: the market is pricing a near-term catalyst, most likely a Caesars lease restructuring announcement or further deterioration news. The elevated put demand does not invalidate the fundamental thesis (2.7% short interest is low and squeeze risk minimal), but it signals that informed participants are hedging for a downside event. The 10.2% put skew is elevated. Position sizing should account for the possibility of a sharp move lower on adverse restructuring terms.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($35-37, +25-32%)
- Caesars lease restructuring resolves favorably: minor rent reduction (5-10%) with term extension, preserving the vast majority of cash flow
- Golden Entertainment acquisition closes mid-2026 and contributes $87M in incremental rent immediately
- Rate environment stabilizes or improves, allowing refinancing at comparable or lower rates
- Experiential diversification strategy gains traction, with call right exercises on Caesars Forum and/or additional non-gaming acquisitions
- Multiple re-rates toward 13-14x AFFO as the Caesars overhang lifts
- 6.4% dividend yield provides total return floor while waiting

### Bear Case ($22-25, -11-22%)
- Caesars regional operations continue deteriorating, requiring a 20-25% rent reduction on the regional master lease
- Caesars financial health weakens further, raising bankruptcy risk and testing the parent guarantee
- Rising rates increase refinancing costs, compressing AFFO margins by $0.10-0.15/share annually
- Development loans (One Beverly Hills, North Fork) require loss recognition
- Consumer spending contraction (record-low Michigan sentiment, recession probability 45-50%) reduces gaming volumes broadly, putting pressure on MGM coverage ratios as well
- Multiple compresses further to 9-10x AFFO

2. AFFO per share guidance for 2027 comes in below $2.60, suggesting the combined effect of lease concessions and refinancing costs is eroding the earnings base faster than organic growth can offset

## Conclusion

VICI Properties presents a compelling risk/reward at $28.11. The Caesars Regional Master Lease overhang is real, well-documented, and the primary reason the stock trades at a deep discount to REIT peers. The market is pricing this risk aggressively: the 6.4% yield, 73% payout ratio, 39.6-year weighted-average lease term, 100% occupancy, investment-grade balance sheet, and zero insider selling collectively suggest the situation is containable. The most probable outcome, a moderate lease restructuring with some rent concessions offset by term extensions, would remove the overhang while preserving the vast majority of cash flow.

The stock is a BUY for investors willing to accept the binary risk around the Caesars resolution. Position sizing should reflect the concentrated tenant exposure and elevated options-implied uncertainty: this is appropriate at 2-3% of a diversified portfolio, not a maximum conviction allocation. The 6.4% yield provides meaningful income while waiting for the catalyst.
