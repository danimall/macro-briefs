## Executive Summary

Shell's Q2 2026 result was the strongest quarterly print since mid-2022: adjusted earnings of $9,836m, adjusted EBITDA of $20,710m, cash flow from operations of $21,432m and reported free cash flow of $17,524m. The quarter did two things. It repaired a balance sheet that had been quietly deteriorating — net debt had climbed from $43,216m in Q2 2025 to $52,606m in Q1 2026 as the company distributed more cash than it generated at roughly $70 Brent — and it demonstrated that the LNG trading book, not the barrels, is the earnings engine. Integrated Gas adjusted earnings rose $872m sequentially to $2,691m *while segment production fell 31%*, because trading, optimisation and realised prices contributed $1,359m against a $907m volume drag. Very few competitors can absorb the loss of a third of a segment's output and still grow that segment's earnings.

The problem for a buyer at $89.95 is that the quarter described a price environment that had already changed before it was reported, and consensus has not fully absorbed that. Current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 8.6% over 90 days to $11.07 per ADS, implying H2 2026 adjusted earnings of roughly $14.6bn versus H1's $16.75bn — only a modest step down despite management guiding Q3 to *zero* output from Qatar and warning that chemical spreads have begun to soften. Next-year consensus has moved the other way, down 6.9% over 90 days to $8.84 per ADS. Rising near-year estimates alongside falling out-year estimates is the fingerprint of a commodity peak, not an earnings inflection, and it is the opposite of the setup in which rapid upward revisions reliably precede further re-rating.

We rate Shell HOLD on an income basis. The company is genuinely cheap on trailing and near-year numbers — approximately 5.2x EV to FY2025 adjusted EBITDA, 9.7x trailing IFRS earnings on a matched per-ADS basis, 8.1x current-year consensus — but the multiple compresses to 10.2x on FY2027 consensus and to roughly 14x if realised prices revert toward FY2025 averages. At 5.2% below the 52-week high after a 26% twelve-month advance, with the sell-side mix drifting from 1 strong buy / 7 buy / 8 hold to 0 / 6 / 10, there is no identifiable mispricing to underwrite. What there is: a 3.47% declared dividend yield, a $4.2bn buyback programme to complete by the Q3 results, roughly $4.8bn of announced-but-unreceived divestment proceeds, and a discrete, datable recovery in Qatar. That combination supports holding; it does not support adding at this price.

## Company Overview

Shell reports in five segments: Integrated Gas, Upstream, Marketing, Chemicals & Products, and Renewables & Energy Solutions. The structural fact that distinguishes it from asset-heavy peers sits inside Integrated Gas. In Q2 2026 the segment liquefied 7.73 million tonnes of LNG and **sold 17.96 million tonnes** — sales at roughly 2.3x equity liquefaction. Shell sources the balance from third parties and monetises route, timing and quality optionality across the largest flexible LNG book in the industry. Segment production was 631 thousand boe/d in the quarter, down 31% sequentially.

Upstream produced 1,824 thousand boe/d in Q2, of which 1,367 thousand b/d liquids, weighted toward deepwater (Brazil, Gulf of America, Nigeria). Group production was 2,455 thousand boe/d, down from 2,682 a year earlier and 2,752 in Q1 — the decline is concentrated in Qatar rather than in base decline.

Two structural additions are in progress. LNG Canada shipped its first cargo around mid-2025, has delivered more than 100 cargoes and reached full capacity in Q2 2026; H1 2026 liquefaction rose 17% year-on-year to 15.60Mt from 13.32Mt *despite* losing Qatari volumes. The pending acquisition of ARC Resources (Montney shale, British Columbia and Alberta) supplies the feedstock complement to that chain, with a Phase 2 FID targeted for 2027-28.

One methodological note that materially affects how this issuer should be read: Shell is a UK-domiciled foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F and Form 6-K. There is no 10-K, no 10-Q, no 8-K, no DEF 14A and no Section 16 insider reporting. Standard financial-health screens (Altman Z, Piotroski F), proxy compensation data and Form 4 insider transactions do not exist for this security. Absence of insider-buying evidence here is a structural artefact and should not be read as an adverse signal in either direction.

**Derived share count and market metrics.** Q2 income attributable of $10,821m divided by basic EPS of $1.94 implies 5,578m ordinary shares; the adjusted line cross-checks at 5,589m ($9,836m ÷ $1.76). At 2,789m ADS-equivalent and $89.95, market capitalisation is approximately **$251bn**, and enterprise value with $41,754m of net debt is approximately **$293bn**.

**Cash-flow quality is the most important nuance in the quarter, and it cuts both ways.** Q2 operating cash flow of $21,432m included working-capital *inflows* of $3.4bn and $1.3bn of net inflows from the timing of emission-certificate and biofuel payments. Stripping those $4.7bn of timing items puts underlying Q2 free cash flow near **$12.8bn** — still exceptional, but 27% below the headline. The mirror image is Q1, where free cash flow was only $2,927m. For the half year, working capital was a $7.7bn *headwind* against $2.6bn of emission timing inflows, so H1 reported free cash flow of $20,451m arguably *understates* underlying generation. Both statements hold simultaneously. Neither quarter is a run rate: Q2 flatters, Q1 understates, and a price spike followed by a collapse within a single quarter is close to the ideal working-capital pattern for a physical optimisation book.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt of $73,076m fell year-on-year from $75,675m; net debt of $41,754m implies roughly $31.3bn of cash. Gearing peaked at 23.2% in Q1 and never reached a level that constrained anything. Management indicated on the Q2 call that net debt is roughly $12bn excluding leases, implying approximately $30bn of lease liabilities — plausible for a company chartering LNG carriers, drilling rigs and operating retail forecourts, and if accurate it makes the *financial* balance sheet very conservative against $56bn of trough-year EBITDA. That $12bn figure comes from a third-party call summary and is not verified against a primary filing; treat it as indicative.

**The durability question the windfall obscures.** From roughly mid-2025 through Q1 2026, Shell distributed more cash than it generated and funded the difference with debt. Buybacks ran at $3.5bn per quarter through Q4 2025 before being cut to $3.0bn at Q1 2026; against roughly $2.2bn of quarterly dividends and $1.2bn of interest, quarterly outflow to capital providers was near $6.8bn versus Q1 free cash flow of $2,927m. Part of that gap is self-liquidating working capital that reversed in Q2. Part of it is a payout pace that was not self-funding at $70 Brent. The available disclosure does not permit a clean split, and we do not infer intent — through-cycle smoothing is a legitimate policy. But an investor underwriting the current shareholder yield should assume it is a peak-price yield, not a trough-price yield.

**Distributions and the policy gap.** Q2 distributions of $5.2bn ($3.0bn buyback plus $2.2bn dividends) against $21,432m of CFFO is a 24% payout, well under the stated 40-50% through-cycle policy. The release explains it: the buyback was temporarily suspended in connection with the ARC agreement, with only $1.8bn of the programme completed, and the new programme is $3.0bn plus $1.2bn of catch-up, to complete by the Q3 results. Applying 40-50% to H1 CFFO of $27,495m would imply $11.0-13.7bn of H1 distributions against a materially lower actual. If the policy is applied to the windfall base there is room for a step-up in Q3/Q4; if management applies it to a normalised base, no catch-up is owed. No primary statement resolves this, and it is the single cleanest upside catalyst available.

**One data inconsistency worth flagging.** The market feed reports trailing and forward P/E both at 19.65x with EPS of $3.00 — figures that reconcile to neither the ADS price nor any Shell earnings basis. Trailing four quarters of IFRS income attributable derive to approximately $25,972m (H2 2025 of $9,457m plus H1 2026 of $16,515m), or $9.31 per ADS, putting the shares at **9.7x trailing GAAP**. The reported 19.65x appears to divide the ADS price by ordinary-share EPS.

## Growth Analysis

Shell's volume growth is real but modest and back-loaded, and it is currently masked by an outage.

**Confirmed and in the numbers.** H1 liquefaction rose 17% year-on-year to 15.60Mt on the LNG Canada ramp, achieved while Qatari volumes were absent. Record refinery utilisation of 102% in Q2, with runs steered toward middle distillates during a high-margin window. Chemicals turned free-cash-flow positive with the Pennsylvania Petrochemicals Complex at its best result to date — against a base where Q4 2025 Chemicals & Products was guided *below break-even*.

**Announced and pending.** ARC Resources — CAD 8.20 cash plus 0.40247 Shell ordinary shares per ARC share, approximately USD 13.6bn equity value, approved by 99.54% of votes cast, with Investment Canada Act clearance the remaining condition and a Q3 2026 expected close. Management expects ARC to add roughly $1.5bn of annual free cash flow and to lift production growth to approximately 4% per year to 2030 from about 1% (call summary; unverified against a filing). Dilution is manageable: at an inferred ~568m ARC shares, the exchange ratio implies roughly 229m new ordinary shares, a 4.1% increase in share count leaving existing holders with 96.1% of the enlarged company, i.e. approximately 3.9% ownership dilution. Cash consideration is roughly USD 3.4bn. The ARC share count is a derivation from the stated USD equity value and an inferred FX rate, so treat the dilution figure as ±0.3pp.

**Working against growth.** Group production fell to 2,455 kboe/d from 2,682 a year earlier. Q3 guidance assumes zero Qatar output. Pearl GTL repairs are targeted for Q1 2027. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $8.84 per ADS is 20% below FY2026's $11.07 — the out-year decline is price-driven, not volume-driven, but it is the number a twelve-month holder is buying into. Underlying opex actually *rose* 2.6% year-on-year in H1 ($17,026m vs $16,598m), which is consistent with $5.8bn of cumulative structural cost reductions since 2022 being offset by inflation and mix rather than producing absolute cost decline. Management flagged 5-6% inflation across project costs, with deepwater rigs and FPSOs "particularly expensive" — directly on the critical path for a growth pipeline weighted to Brazil, the Gulf of America, Bonga SW and Zabazaba.

Growth Prospects scores 5.5. The LNG chain and ARC are genuine, durable additions to volume and free cash flow, but the earnings trajectory into 2027 on consensus is negative, production is declining, and the largest single growth claim (4% per annum to 2030) rests on a secondary source.

The valuation case is a question of which earnings base is right. On FY2026 consensus the stock is 8.1x, which for an integrated major with a genuine LNG franchise looks inexpensive. But that consensus embeds H2 2026 adjusted earnings of roughly $14.6bn against a quarter guided to zero Qatari output and softening chemical spreads, which means it assumes realised prices remain near war-elevated levels. That assumption is a source-dependent one: the most recent verified price datapoint in the research base is ~$73/bbl Brent as of early July 2026, after a US–Iran interim peace deal, and the reversal from >$120 had already occurred before the quarter was reported — but current spot levels and transit conditions are not established in the sources available for this assessment.

On FY2027 consensus of $8.84 per ADS the multiple is 10.2x. Note what that estimate implies: $8.84 per ADS equates to roughly $4.42 per ordinary share, or about $25.7bn of adjusted earnings on a post-ARC share count — *above* FY2024's $23,716m and 39% above FY2025's $18,529m. Consensus is not modelling reversion to 2025 conditions; it is modelling elevated prices plus LNG Canada plus ARC. If realised prices instead revert toward FY2025 averages, adjusted EPS lands nearer $6.30-6.50 per ADS and the shares are at approximately 14x. That is the range in which the "cheap" characterisation stops holding.

Three things do support the current price. First, absolute yield: $3.1248 per ADS declared, up 9.1% year-on-year, and buyback capacity at the current pace — combined shareholder yield in the mid-single-digit area at peak cash generation. Second, the announced-but-unreceived divestment pipeline: Jiffy Lube $1.3bn (completed 30 June), Na Kika/Coulomb $1.7bn and Solenergi/Sprng $1.8bn, roughly $4.8bn against only $0.8bn of proceeds recognised in H1 — a funding tailwind that broadly offsets the ARC cash outflow. Third, the Qatar hole is discrete and datable rather than a diffuse impairment.

Valuation scores 5.5. Cheap on headline metrics, fairly priced on a normalised base, and lacking a specific reason the market is wrong. Analyst targets are not treated as a floor here: at a cyclical peak with sentiment already degrading, the $98 mean is a consensus extrapolation of current prices, not independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Shell's competitive trajectory in gas is improving and its position in the rest of the portfolio is stable to narrowing by choice.

The LNG trading book is the durable differentiator, and Q2 was a controlled experiment: equity supply fell 31% and segment earnings rose 48% sequentially. Management frames Trading & Supply as contributing 2-4 percentage points to ROACE and states it is currently at the top of that range — which is both the bull case and its own limitation. A quarter containing a price spike and a collapse is close to ideal for physical optimisation; a flat market is not. Trading earnings are also less transparent and less capitalisable than production earnings, which is a legitimate reason for the market to apply a lower multiple to them.

The Qatar disruption is a sector event, not a Shell event — Shell and TotalEnergies both declared force majeure after the 2 March 2026 QatarEnergy shut-in, and Qatar plus the UAE reportedly lost close to 80% of LNG output across four months. Relative position depends on who held non-Qatari flexible supply, and Shell's H1 liquefaction rose 17% while the Gulf was down. Shell's Qatari exposure is also asymmetric in a favourable way: its 30% interest in QatarEnergy LNG's fourth Ras Laffan train (2.4Mtpa equity) continued operating throughout, while the concentrated loss sits in 100%-owned Pearl GTL. The segment-liquids line is the evidence — Integrated Gas liquids fell from 115 kb/d in Q1 to 37 kb/d in Q2, the fingerprint of a GTL/NGL plant going offline, while liquefaction fell only 2% sequentially.

Bargaining power in LNG is shifting toward buyers as Asian and European counterparties seek lower prices and supply guarantees from Gulf sellers on higher insurance costs. As a Qatari equity holder Shell loses from that; as the largest flexible portfolio seller it gains, because reliability and route flexibility are exactly what Gulf-sourced supply cannot currently offer.

The strategic logic of ARC is the cleanest available for a large gas acquisition by a major in 2026: Shell owns the top of the chain (LNG Canada Phase 1 at full capacity, Phase 2 FID targeted 2027-28) and is buying the feedstock underneath it. Against that, management volunteered that a portion of roughly $45bn of capital is underperforming, concentrated in low-carbon, with CCS and Holland Hydrogen I expected to begin delivering returns only from 2027 and a >10% segment return targeted by end of decade. The $1.8bn Solenergi/Sprng sale is a complete exit from Indian renewables and is consistent with acting on that admission.

Competitive Position scores 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Qatar and Hormuz are two separate risks with different resolution paths.** Pearl GTL's damaged sections are targeted for return by Q1 2027; unaffected units could restart sooner if shipping lanes reopen. Q3 guidance assumes nothing. Even a repaired plant with no insurable export route remains impaired — the QatarEnergy-linked carrier Al Rekayyat was struck in the waterway, and the Al Areesh's 30 July transit was the first in three weeks. Engineering resolves the first risk; only sustained verified transit resolves the second.

**Price direction is genuinely two-sided for Shell, more so than for pure-play E&Ps.** A verified Hormuz reopening restores Qatari volumes and eventually Pearl, but compresses realisations, trading spreads and product cracks simultaneously. Sustained disruption keeps earnings elevated but leaves Pearl offline and raises the tail risk of retaliation against Gulf energy infrastructure. There is no configuration in which Shell is unambiguously long or short the conflict, which is a reason to hold rather than to size up in either direction.

**Chemicals fragility.** The segment moved from below break-even in Q4 2025 to roughly $1.93bn of adjusted earnings in Q1 2026 and positive free cash flow in Q2. Management flagged spreads softening into Q3. A segment that swings that far in two quarters can swing back.

**Fiscal risk scales with the growth story.** Brazil oil export levies cost Upstream $242m in Q2 2026, and Brazil is where Shell posted record production. The UK Energy Profits Levy produced a $509m charge in H1 2025. Growth is concentrated in jurisdictions that tax windfalls, and the current environment is a windfall.

**Unresolved disclosure items.** The release discloses that Integrated Gas H1 operating cash flow was reduced by "a payment relating to a legal case of $635 million" without identifying the matter, counterparty or residual exposure. FY2025 20-F content — risk factors, proved reserves, reserve replacement, contingent liabilities, remuneration — was not accessible, so reserves and reserve-life metrics normally central to an E&P assessment are absent from this analysis. H1 identified items were a $2.0bn net loss versus $1.2bn in H1 2025, including a $1.5bn year-on-year swing in Integrated Gas derivative fair-value movements that is a hedge-accounting timing artefact rather than economic loss — which is why IFRS income and adjusted earnings should never be compared against a single consensus figure.

**Capex timing.** Management reaffirmed 2026 cash capex of $24-26bn including roughly $4bn tied to ARC, against H1 actual of $8,439m. Ex-ARC that implies $11.6-13.6bn in H2, a 38-62% step-up in half-yearly run rate. The most likely explanation is the ARC cash consideration plus consolidation of ARC capex from close, with some genuine H2 acceleration; the alternative is guidance conservatism, in which case free cash flow beats. Either way this is a timing question, not evidence of discipline breaking down.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 33.3% against 22.5% one-year realised is rich by 10.9 points, with the term structure in backwardation (33.3% at one week versus 25.9% at six months). The market is pricing near-term event risk — consistent with the Hormuz binary; the nearest-expiry implied move is ±2.9%. Positioning, however, is not bearish: put/call skew is −6.1% (call-heavy), put/call volume ratio 0.09 and open-interest ratio 0.35, the most call-tilted readings in our energy coverage. Short interest is 1.8% of float at 4.4 days to cover, effectively nil. The six-month tenor prices an implied move of ±11.1%.

The read: participants are buying near-dated upside and paying for it, rather than hedging downside. For a stock 5.2% below its 52-week high after a 26% twelve-month advance, that is complacency about the reopening scenario rather than confirmation of the bull case. For an existing holder it argues that selling covered upside into the elevated near-term vol is better compensated than adding equity here.

Risk Factors scores 5.5 — sector risk is priced, but the concentration of earnings direction in a single unresolved geopolitical variable, plus an unidentified $635m legal item and inaccessible reserves disclosure, leaves more unpriced idiosyncratic risk than the option market's positioning implies.

## Management Quality

No Form 4 data, no proxy, no compensation structure, no board composition and no insider transactions are available for this issuer, and none of that is an adverse finding — it follows from foreign private issuer status. What can be assessed is behaviour.

Communication has been notably non-promotional in three specific instances: Q3 guidance was set at zero Qatari output even though management believes unaffected units could restart earlier, i.e. below its own expectation; chemical spread softening was volunteered immediately after a strong Chemicals quarter; and roughly $45bn of underperforming capital was disclosed alongside an admission that the fix "will take several years." Capital-allocation mechanics have been shareholder-friendly in detail — the buyback was cut from $3.5bn to $3.0bn when prices were low rather than defended with debt indefinitely, was paused mid-programme to fund ARC, and the unspent $1.2bn was explicitly credited back into the new programme rather than dropped. Divestment execution has been brisk and priced sensibly ($4.8bn across three transactions), with the Jiffy Lube exit retaining the lubricants supply relationship into the divested business.

Against that: the FY2025 buyback pace was not self-funding, financed partly by a $9.4bn rise in net debt; the ARC premium was 27%; and the stated $5.8bn of structural cost reductions since 2022 sits in unresolved tension with management's characterisation of being "roughly halfway" to a $5-7bn target, while underlying opex rose 2.6% year-on-year.

Management Quality scores 6.3 — credible, candid and disciplined on the evidence available, capped by the absence of any insider or compensation-alignment data and by a payout pace that required a windfall to validate.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($115, +27.8%).** Hormuz transit remains impaired through H2 2026, keeping Brent elevated and European gas tight on below-normal storage; the LNG trading book stays at the top of its 2-4 point ROACE contribution; Pearl GTL returns in Q1 2027 and Qatari equity volumes restart, adding earnings without the price giving back; ARC closes in Q3 and delivers the claimed ~$1.5bn of incremental free cash flow; management applies the 40-50% CFFO policy to the windfall base and takes the buyback back to $3.5bn per quarter. Adjusted EPS holds near $11 per ADS into 2027 and the multiple stays at 10-11x. Equates to 13.0x FY2027 consensus $8.84.

**Base case ($96, +6.7%; ~10% total return with dividend).** Partial de-escalation. Qatar restarts progressively through H1 2027; ARC closes and contributes from Q4; trading normalises toward the middle of its ROACE range; chemical spreads soften as guided. Adjusted EPS lands near consensus FY2027 of $8.84 per ADS and the shares hold 10.5-11x.

**Bear case ($70, −22.2%).** Verified sustained Hormuz transit plus a broader easing in oil prices compresses realisations, product cracks and trading spreads together; working capital that inflated Q2 cash flow unwinds; chemical spreads revert to the Q4 2025 below-break-even base; Brazil export levies persist. Adjusted EPS falls to $6.00-6.50 per ADS and the stock trades at 11x — $66-72, against a 52-week low of $68.63. The buyback is cut again and net debt resumes rising.

Probability-weighting base 50% / bull 25% / bear 25% gives roughly +5% expected price return and approximately +8.5% total return including the dividend over twelve months. That is fair compensation, not asymmetric upside, and it is the arithmetic behind the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income basis). Reassessment at the Q3 2026 results (late October 2026).**

The holding case rests on cash returned rather than expected re-rating: a $3.1248 per ADS declared dividend (3.47% yield, raised 9.1% year-on-year) plus $4.2bn of buyback to complete by the Q3 results, against a balance sheet at 18.7% gearing with roughly $31.3bn of cash and $4.8bn of divestment proceeds still to land. Existing owners are adequately paid to wait through the Hormuz binary. New capital should wait.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of the following occur:**
1. Price falls to **$76-80** (roughly 8.6-9.0x FY2027 consensus $8.84 per ADS, a 4.3-4.6% dividend yield) with FY2027 estimates stabilising rather than continuing to fall.
2. Q3 results disclose a buyback step-up beyond the announced $4.2bn — evidence that the 40-50% CFFO policy is being applied to the windfall base rather than a normalised one.
3. Pearl GTL restart is confirmed earlier than Q1 2027 *and* sustained commercial transit through Hormuz is verified for 72+ hours, resolving the volume side without the full price give-back.
4. ARC closes and the first consolidated quarter demonstrates free cash flow tracking toward the claimed ~$1.5bn annual run rate.

**Upgrade to BUY** requires the price in the low $70s alongside FY2027 consensus holding above $8.50 per ADS — a forward multiple below 8.5x on stabilising estimates.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following:**
1. FY2027 consensus EPS falls below **$7.50 per ADS** (a further 15% cut from $8.84) while the share price remains above $88 — the multiple would then exceed 11.7x on falling estimates.
2. Q3 or Q4 adjusted earnings fall below **$5.5bn** *and* the quarterly buyback is cut below $3.0bn — the combination that signals the payout is again outrunning generation.
3. Net debt rises back above **$50bn** post-ARC close on anything other than identifiable working-capital timing.
4. Chemicals & Products returns to below break-even, reversing the two-quarter turnaround.

**Downgrade to AVOID** on identification of a material undisclosed liability arising from the unexplained $635m legal payment, or on an escalation that damages Shell's Qatari LNG equity interests (as distinct from Pearl GTL) or its Brazilian production base.

**Monitoring checklist:** Q3 2026 results (late October) for Qatar restart timing, the buyback decision and H2 capex against the $24-26bn guide; Investment Canada Act clearance on ARC; verified sustained Hormuz commercial transit; European gas storage builds into winter; the FY2025 20-F for reserves, reserve replacement and contingent liabilities not available for this assessment.

## Conclusion

Shell has done the operational work that matters. LNG Canada is at full capacity and lifted H1 liquefaction 17% while the Gulf was shut in. The trading book proved in Q2 that it can absorb a 31% loss of segment production and still grow segment earnings. Chemicals turned free-cash-flow positive from a below-break-even base. ARC gives the Canadian chain its feedstock at approximately 3.9% ownership dilution against a claimed $1.5bn of incremental annual free cash flow. The balance sheet is back to 18.7% gearing, and the dividend rose 9.1% year-on-year.

What the price does not offer is a margin of safety. At $89.95 the shares are 5.2% below their 52-week high after a 26% twelve-month advance, on 8.1x a current-year consensus that assumes war-elevated realisations persist through a quarter guided to zero Qatari output, and 10.2x an FY2027 consensus that itself implies adjusted earnings above FY2024's level. Current-year estimates rising 8.6% while next-year estimates fall 6.9% is the signature of a commodity peak.
