**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Pursuit Attractions & Hospitality is the operating business that emerged from Viad Corp after the December 2024 sale of its GES exhibitions unit. It is a pure-play owner-operator of irreplaceable destination assets: 17 attractions and 29 lodges (~2,890 rooms) concentrated in the Canadian Rockies, Alaska, Montana's Glacier region, Iceland, and Costa Rica. The franchise quality is genuine. Most of these assets sit on or adjacent to national parks under concession arrangements with Parks Canada and the U.S. National Park Service, so the locations cannot be replicated. That scarcity, combined with the post-COVID shift toward experiential travel, has produced theme-park-like pricing power, with same-store effective ticket prices up 8.6% and same-store RevPAR up 7.1% in FY2025 on an ADR-led basis.

The fundamentals are improving and the data screens are misleading. A standardized pull shows revenue falling from ~$1.24B (2023) to ~$452M (2025) and net income spiking to $369M then collapsing to $23M — both artifacts of the GES divestiture, not business deterioration. The continuing business grew 23.4% in FY2025 and 37.4% in Q1 2026 (acquisition-aided by Tabacón). Estimate revisions are broadly positive (+16% to current-year EPS over 90 days), management reaffirmed 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $123M–$133M, and the CEO and a director bought stock in the open market near $33 in November 2025. The portfolio is being high-graded: the impaired, manufactured Flyover venues are being sold to Brogent for ~$78.4M, and the proceeds fund higher-return natural-asset growth.

The problem is price, not business. The stock has appreciated 86% over twelve months and now trades at 44.7x trailing earnings, above the peer median of 27.8x, against a consensus mean target of $52.25 — roughly 2.6% above the current $50.94. Free cash flow is thin (2.5% margin in FY2025) because growth capex and an aggressive buyback are consuming operating cash, and the revolver balance jumped to $159.7M at quarter-end from $58.5M three months earlier, narrowing the cushion against a 3.0x net-leverage covenant. The business is also structurally exposed: 79% of revenue lands in Q2/Q3, and the assets carry realized disaster risk (2024 Jasper wildfires, the 2020 Icefield rollover). At this valuation, the risk/reward has flattened. We initiate at HOLD with a positive growth bias, recommending accumulation on pullbacks rather than chasing the current level.

## Company Overview

Pursuit operates a portfolio of attractions, lodging, food & beverage, retail, and transportation organized into geographic collections:

- **Banff Jasper Collection** (Canadian Rockies, ~1,220 rooms): Banff Gondola, Columbia Icefield glacier tours, Maligne Lake Cruise, Golden Skybridge, Jasper SkyTram — the crown jewels.
- **Alaska Collection** (~520 rooms): Denali/Kenai Fjords wildlife and glacier cruises plus remote lodges.
- **Glacier Park Collection** (Montana, ~630 rooms): historic lodges and rafting.
- **Sky Lagoon** (Iceland): high-margin geothermal lagoon with extended seasonality.
- **Tabacón** (Costa Rica, 105 rooms): eco-luxury thermal resort acquired July 2025.
- **Flyover Attractions** (Vancouver, Reykjavik, Las Vegas, Chicago): immersive flying-theater venues being divested to Brogent.

The stated strategy is "Refresh, Build, Buy": renovate existing assets for ADR uplift, build new experiences, and acquire high-return assets in perennial-demand destinations. The competitive advantage is scarcity. Most assets occupy locations inside or adjacent to national parks that new entrants cannot access. Following the GES sale, Pursuit operates as a single segment splitting reporting into Attractions and Hospitality.

The structured financial data obscures the underlying business. The apparent "revenue cliff" from $1.24B to $452M is the deconsolidation of GES (discontinued operations); the $452M figure is the continuing attractions and hospitality business, which is growing. The 2024 net income of $368.5M is dominated by the one-time gain on the GES sale; 2024 continuing operations were actually a pre-tax loss of -$45.4M, inflated by a $47.6M Flyover impairment.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 continuing operations:**
- Revenue $452.4M, +23.4% YoY (vs. $366.5M in 2024). Attractions +23.6% to $257.5M; Hospitality +26.1% to $180.4M.
- Net income $22.7M; operating margin 12.2%; net margin 8.0%.
- Operating cash flow $86.2M (up from $56.9M); capex $75.0M; free cash flow only $11.1M (2.5% FCF margin).
- Balance sheet: total assets $965M, PP&E $649M, goodwill ~$150M, equity $582M, long-term debt $155M, cash $31M.

Growth came from three sources: recovery from the 2024 Jasper wildfire disruption, pricing power (same-store effective ticket +8.6%, same-store RevPAR +7.1%), and acquisitions (Tabacón, Jasper SkyTram, Flyover Chicago). The pricing power is the most durable element; it reflects the scarcity moat directly.

The free cash flow figure is the central financial tension. Operating cash flow of $86.2M is healthy, but $75.0M of capex leaves only $11.1M of FCF, and 2026 growth capex is guided to ~$103M–$114M. This is a capital-intensive business in an active build-and-buy phase, so thin FCF reflects deliberate reinvestment rather than weak economics. But it does mean the company is funding buybacks and acquisitions partly with debt.

**Q1 2026 (10-Q):** Revenue $51.6M, +37.4% YoY, with Tabacón contributing roughly $10M of the increase. Q1 is structurally a loss quarter (net loss $24.9M vs. -$31.1M prior year) because of the Q2/Q3 revenue concentration. Same-store growth was more modest (ticket +4.7%, RevPAR +6.5%), confirming the headline number was acquisition-aided. Management characterized this as record first-quarter results.

**Leverage trajectory bears watching.** The revolver drawn balance climbed from $58.5M (12/31/25) to $159.7M (3/31/26), driven by seasonal working capital, $25.2M of Q1 buybacks, growth capex, and the Tabacón debt funding. The company was in compliance with its net-leverage covenant at quarter-end and the Flyover proceeds (expected to close around May 2026) provide relief. But the combination of front-loaded capex, accelerating buybacks, and Q1 cash burn (-$29.5M operating cash flow, seasonal) means the leverage cushion is thinner than the headline D/E of 0.52 implies.

Health diagnostics are mixed-but-acceptable: Altman Z 2.63 (grey zone), Piotroski 4/5, current ratio 0.81. The sub-1.0 current ratio is normal for a seasonal attractions operator that collects deposits as contract liabilities and carries little inventory.

A quiet structural drag: a full U.S. valuation allowance ($46.7M) means the company pays cash tax in Canada, Iceland, and Costa Rica while U.S. losses go unrelieved, keeping the FY2025 effective rate at 30% and depressing reported net income relative to EBITDA.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has three legs.

First, **pricing power on irreplaceable assets**. Renovated rooms (Forest Park, Grouse Mountain) are delivering +22% ADR uplift. This is the "Refresh" lever and it carries high incremental margins because the assets already exist.

Second, **acquisition-led expansion that also de-risks seasonality**. Tabacón is strategically larger than its size suggests. Costa Rica generates revenue more evenly across the calendar year, partially offsetting the company's summer-peak concentration. Tabacón contributed ~$20M in its half-year of FY2025 ownership and was the main Q1 2026 growth driver.

Third, **portfolio high-grading**. Selling the impaired, manufactured Flyover venues (which had already taken a $41.5M impairment) and redeploying into natural-destination assets improves return on capital and concentrates the business in its actual moat. The press coverage describing Flyover launching a global content-licensing division reflects Brogent's plans as the acquirer, not Pursuit's go-forward strategy.

Estimate revisions support the momentum: current-year EPS estimates rose +16.1% over 90 days and next-year +12.5%, with the current quarter up +17.1%. The earnings surprise record is favorable (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters), though the sample is thin and dominated by the seasonal pattern. These revisions are constructive, but the magnitude is below the +30% threshold that would flag a structural mispricing, and the stock is not at a cyclical trough; it has already re-rated substantially.

The growth thesis is genuine but capacity-constrained. The company can only grow same-store pricing so fast before it tests demand elasticity, and meaningful unit growth requires either capex-heavy builds or debt-funded acquisitions. The 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guide implies roughly mid-single-digit to low-double-digit growth over an implied FY2025 base, decelerating from the wildfire-recovery-aided 23% headline.

## Valuation Assessment

At $50.94, PRSU trades at 44.7x trailing earnings against a peer median of 27.8x (Hilton Grand Vacations, Vail Resorts, Travel + Leisure). Two of the closest comparables on multiple — MTN at 31.2x and HGV at 27.8x — are slower-growing, more mature operators, so a growth premium is defensible. But 44.7x is a substantial premium even adjusting for growth.

Forward metrics offer some relief but not enough to call the stock cheap. Consensus current-year EPS of $1.59 implies a forward P/E of ~32x; next-year EPS of $1.85 implies ~27.5x. The PEG on a ~12-15% forward growth rate is roughly 2.0-2.5, not the sub-1.5 territory that signals mispricing. The proper way to value this business is on EBITDA, given the heavy D&A from $649M of PP&E and the U.S. tax drag suppressing net income. At the guided Adjusted EBITDA range, an enterprise value of roughly $1.52B (market cap $1.39B plus ~$129M net debt) puts the stock around 12x EV/EBITDA, a full but not extreme multiple for an irreplaceable-asset operator with double-digit pricing power.

The analyst price targets frame the upside ceiling: consensus mean $52.25, median $52.50, high $55.00, low $49.00. Current price sits 2.6% below the mean. Normally I treat analyst targets as floors on cheap, accelerating stocks, but that principle applies when the stock is cheap relative to rising estimates. Here the stock has rallied to consensus, the multiple is above peers, and the four covering analysts have not raised targets to create new headroom. The targets here function closer to a ceiling than a floor.

The valuation score reflects this. The business quality is high and the growth is real, but at 44.7x trailing / ~12x EV/EBITDA with the stock pressed against consensus targets after an 86% run, there is no identifiable mispricing to exploit and the asymmetry has flattened.

## Competitive Landscape

Pursuit's moat is the strongest part of the story. Its assets occupy locations inside or adjacent to Banff, Jasper, Glacier, Denali, and Kenai Fjords national parks under concession arrangements that cannot be replicated by new entrants. This is closer to a toll-road or theme-park franchise than to a hotel business, which is why the company sustains above-inflation ticket and ADR increases. Reported relative TSR (93.12 vs. peer group 73.94 in 2025) confirms outperformance against the comparable set.

The trajectory is improving, not merely stable. The Refresh program is generating measurable ADR uplift, the Tabacón acquisition extends the footprint into a counter-seasonal geography, and the Flyover divestiture removes the lowest-quality, most-commoditized part of the portfolio. The competitive position is strengthening through deliberate capital reallocation toward the scarcest assets.

The competitive constraint is the same one that creates the moat: concession dependency. Operations rely on Parks Canada and NPS regulation. The Denali Park Road has been closed since a 2021 landslide, suspending Pursuit's Denali Backcountry Adventure until at least 2027, a multi-year revenue loss entirely outside the company's control.

## Risk Assessment

**Seasonality and single-peak concentration** is the defining risk. With 79% of revenue earned in Q2/Q3, any adverse event during the narrow summer window — weather, wildfire, a demand shock — is disproportionately damaging. Tabacón mitigates this at the margin but does not eliminate it.

**Realized disaster exposure.** The July 2024 Jasper wildfires evacuated the park, destroyed the Maligne Canyon Wilderness Kitchen, and hit peak-season visitation; Pursuit has collected $24.0M in insurance to date. Wildfires (Canada/Montana), volcanic activity (Iceland), and hurricanes (Costa Rica) are recurring tail risks for geographically concentrated, irreplaceable assets. The July 2020 Columbia Icefield Ice Explorer rollover killed three people and litigation is ongoing, a standing liability and brand risk inherent to adventure attractions.

**FX translation.** Roughly 71% of revenue is non-USD (CAD, ISK, CRC) and unhedged. Cumulative translation loss in equity was $43.3M at 3/31/26. Earnings and book equity swing with these crosses.

**Leverage and capital cadence.** The Q1 revolver drawdown against the 3.0x net-leverage covenant narrows the margin for error if a peak season disappoints; the Flyover proceeds should relieve it.

**Concession and integration.** The Denali road closure shows how regulatory/infrastructure factors outside the company's control can suspend revenue for years. The acquisition-led model carries ongoing integration and impairment risk (Flyover already took a $41.5M write-down).

**Macro.** As a discretionary, experiential-travel operator, Pursuit is fully exposed to a consumer-spending slowdown. Peak-season visitation in 2026 carries macro risk that the current valuation does not discount.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 43.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 38.2%, so options are modestly rich (5-point spread). The term structure is in backwardation (43.2% near-term vs. 35.4% three-month), indicating the market is pricing imminent event risk, consistent with the run into mid-June and the approach of the seasonally critical Q2 print. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±10.5%. The reported delta showing ATM IV spiking to 954% is a corrupted thin-options data artifact and should be disregarded. Short interest is moderate at 7.2% of float with 5.6 days to cover, neither a squeeze setup nor a crowded bearish position. The net read: the options market expects volatility around the next earnings window but is not signaling directional conviction either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($60–65):** Peak-season 2026 delivers, validating the EBITDA guide at the high end. Tabacón's counter-seasonal revenue smooths the earnings profile and the market awards a higher multiple for reduced volatility. The Flyover proceeds delever the balance sheet, freeing capacity for accretive acquisitions in the "Buy" pipeline. Continued double-digit same-store pricing demonstrates the scarcity moat justifies a premium to slower-growing lodging peers, and the stock re-rates toward 14-15x EV/EBITDA. The November 2025 insider buying at ~$33 looks prescient.

**Bear Case ($36–40):** A wildfire, weather event, or consumer pullback hits the narrow summer window and 2026 EBITDA lands below guidance. The thin FCF and elevated revolver balance leave little cushion; leverage approaches the 3.0x covenant and the buyback stops. The multiple compresses toward the peer median of 27.8x on a disappointed forward number, and the stock retraces a meaningful portion of its 86% run. FX moves against the company on a stronger dollar, compounding the translation drag.

**Base Case ($50–54):** The company delivers near the midpoint of guidance, same-store pricing stays mid-to-high single digits, and the Flyover sale closes on schedule to relieve leverage. The stock tracks roughly sideways to modestly higher, consistent with the $52.25 consensus mean, as the high-quality but fully-valued business compounds earnings without further multiple expansion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q2 2026, the seasonally decisive peak-season print) and the Flyover close confirmation.**

This is a genuinely good business at a price that no longer offers asymmetric upside. The quality justifies owning it; the 86% run and the proximity to consensus targets argue against chasing it here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the high-$30s to low-$40s (roughly 15-25% lower), which would restore asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals and approach where insiders bought.
- Q2/Q3 2026 results that track the high end of the EBITDA guide AND a clear demonstration that Tabacón is materially smoothing the seasonal profile (counter-seasonal revenue rising as a share of the mix).
- Confirmation that the Flyover proceeds delevered the revolver below ~$80M, restoring acquisition capacity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- 2026 EBITDA guidance cut, or a peak-season miss driven by a disaster/demand event.
- Revolver leverage breaching or pressing the 3.0x covenant alongside a continued buyback, signaling capital-allocation strain.
- Same-store pricing decelerating below ~3% YoY, indicating the pricing-power moat is hitting demand elasticity.

## Conclusion

Pursuit is a high-quality, scarcity-moated operator of irreplaceable destination assets with demonstrated pricing power, disciplined portfolio high-grading, and a constructive insider signal from open-market buying near the lows. The fundamental story is sound and improving, and the standardized data badly understates the business by conflating it with the divested GES unit. None of that is in dispute.

What gives me pause is the price. At 44.7x trailing earnings and ~12x EV/EBITDA after an 86% twelve-month run, the stock sits at consensus, above peer multiples, with thin free cash flow, a rapidly drawn revolver, and structural seasonal/disaster concentration that the current valuation does not adequately discount. The growth is decelerating from wildfire-recovery-aided levels toward a still-respectable but more modest base. The asymmetry that existed when insiders bought at $33 has compressed. I recommend HOLD with a positive growth bias: own the quality, monitor the leverage and the peak-season print, and accumulate on weakness rather than at the high.
