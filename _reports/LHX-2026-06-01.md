**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

L3Harris is a tier-one U.S. defense prime with roughly $21.9B of fiscal 2025 revenue, 75% of it from the U.S. Government, positioned across communications/electronic warfare, space and mission systems, and missile propulsion. The fundamental picture improved meaningfully in Q1 FY2026: revenue rose 12% reported (≈15% organic) to $5.74B with all three segments growing, diluted EPS jumped 33% to $2.72, and backlog reached a record $40.7B (+13% YoY). Management raised FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance to roughly $11.40–$11.60. The defense demand backdrop (Golden Dome missile defense, munitions replenishment, shipbuilding, FAA air-traffic modernization, and NATO's 5%-of-GDP commitments) maps directly onto L3Harris's franchises and supports multi-year revenue visibility.

The central value-creation thesis is the planned carve-out IPO of the Missile Solutions (MSL/Aerojet Rocketdyne) segment, reinforced by an unusual $1.0B Department of War equity investment into the AJRD subsidiary announced April 17, 2026, and a $1.27B Virginia solid-rocket-motor capacity build. Solid rocket motors are a supply-constrained chokepoint dominated by an Aerojet/Northrop duopoly, so the strategic scarcity is real, and the government-as-shareholder structure signals national priority on the propulsion industrial base.

The constraint is price. At $315 the stock trades at roughly 27x the FY26 EPS midpoint and ~23x next-year consensus of $13.63, against reported revenue growth in the mid-single digits and consensus estimates that are essentially flat over the trailing 90 days. The catalyst set is genuine but partially priced; the stock has already re-rated (+29% over the trailing year, recovering from a ~17% drawdown off the March high). Fixed-price contract exposure, a goodwill-heavy balance sheet, and emerging political scrutiny of the DoW equity stake cap the asymmetry. We rate LHX HOLD with a constructive growth lean.

## Company Overview

L3Harris was formed by the 2019 Harris/L3 merger and expanded materially through the July 2023 Aerojet Rocketdyne acquisition (propulsion/missiles) and the Tactical Data Links purchase. The company employs roughly 45,000 people, including 18,000 engineers and scientists. Revenue is ~75% U.S. Government (directly or as a subcontractor, including foreign military sales) and ~22% international across more than 100 countries, with no single international country exceeding 5% of revenue.

Effective fiscal 2026, L3Harris collapsed four segments into three:
- **Communication & Spectrum Dominance (CSD)** — tactical radios/waveforms, electronic warfare, night vision, sensors. The margin engine: Q1'26 revenue $1,855M (+3%), 25.1% operating margin.
- **Space & Mission Systems (SMS)** — satellites/payloads, missile warning and defense, ISR, maritime, FAA mission networks. Q1'26 revenue $2,990M (+24% YoY), 10.5% margin.
- **Missile Solutions (MSL)** — Aerojet Rocketdyne propulsion, munitions, fuzing, hypersonics. Q1'26 revenue $990M (+18%), 12.5% margin.

The reorganization isolates MSL as a standalone unit, preparing it for separation. L3Harris positions itself as a platform-agnostic "merchant supplier/flexible prime," competing with Lockheed, Northrop, RTX, General Dynamics, Boeing, BAE, and Thales, while partnering with non-traditional players (Palantir, Anduril, Amazon Kuiper) to hedge against AI-enabled disruptors.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $17.1B (FY22) → $19.4B (FY23) → $21.3B (FY24) → $21.9B (FY25). Reported FY25 growth was a modest +3%, depressed by the CAS divestiture (~$459M revenue headwind). The Q1 FY2026 acceleration was +12% reported and ≈15% organic per management, with growth across all three segments.

**Margins and profitability:** FY25 operating margin was 9.7%, net margin 7.3%, gross margin ~25.7%. The margin story is bifurcated: CSD's ~25% operating margin subsidizes SMS (10–12%) and the still-maturing MSL (aided by a $39M legacy-asset monetization gain). FY25 Aerojet margin compressed to 9.5% from 11.9%, hurt by an $85M goodwill impairment on the Space Technology disposal group. ROE of 8.2% and ROA of 3.9% are modest, weighed down by the acquired-asset base. The FY25 effective tax rate rose to 16.9% (from 5.3%) on a state R&D credit valuation allowance, the CAS divestiture, and tax-law changes; Q1'26 ETR was a lower 13.1% helped by FDII export deductions.

**Cash flow quality is the strongest part of the story.** FY25 operating cash flow was $3.1B (up from $2.56B), free cash flow $2.68B (12.3% FCF margin) on only $424M of capex. Cash conversion is healthy, with OCF above net income. Q1 is seasonally negative (–$95M OCF) per normal pattern. The LHX NeXt cost program completed its implementation phase in FY2025 with cumulative savings targeted near $1.2B, now embedded as standard practice, providing a structural margin lever.

**Balance sheet is the key caution.** Total assets of $41.2B include ~$20.0B goodwill plus $6.5B intangibles — roughly 64% of assets are acquisition-derived, leaving tangible equity thin. Long-term debt is $10.4B (total fixed-rate debt $10.9B), D/E ~1.1. The Altman Z-Score of 1.95 sits in the "grey zone," but this reflects a goodwill-heavy, post-merger structure rather than operating distress; Piotroski signals are healthy (positive net income, OCF > net income, current ratio 1.19). Management repaid net $825M of long-term debt in FY25 and de-risked the pension via a $1.4B annuity buyout, moving to a net pension asset position of $1.2B.

**Earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates.** Four consecutive beats: Q1'26 +5.9%, Q4'25 +2.4%, Q3'25 +3.8%, Q2'25 +10.3%. The beats are consistent but modest in recent quarters, consistent with a maturing defense compounder rather than an accelerating business.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs. First, the demand backdrop: U.S. defense topline near $859B for GFY26, a stated GFY27 PBR targeting ~$1.5T national defense, the NATO 5%-of-GDP pledge, and program themes (Golden Dome layered missile defense, munitions replenishment, shipbuilding, FAA ATC modernization) that map onto CSD, SMS, and MSL. Second, backlog: $40.7B (+13% YoY), with SMS backlog at $21.1B, providing multi-year visibility (~40% converting within 12 months). Third, the missile/propulsion franchise, where solid rocket motors are a structurally scarce chokepoint validated by both the $1B DoW equity injection and the Virginia capacity build.

The tension is between the strong organic momentum in Q1 and the flat estimate revision picture. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $11.70 (+0.6% over 30 days, –0.7% over 90 days); next-year at $13.63 (+1.4%/+0.5%). Current-quarter and next-quarter estimates have ticked down slightly (–1.5% and –1.6% over 30 days). This is a stable-to-modestly-positive revision profile, not the +30% earnings revision velocity that signals a mispricing. The Q1 acceleration appears partly driven by easier comparisons (the CAS divestiture annualizing) and program timing, so we treat the implied two-year EPS CAGR of roughly 8–10% as the durable growth rate rather than extrapolating the 33% Q1 EPS jump.

## Valuation Assessment

At $315, LHX trades at roughly 27x the FY26 GAAP EPS midpoint ($11.50) and ~23x the FY27 consensus of $13.63. Against a peer set in Aerospace & Defense, the picture is mixed: General Dynamics at 21.6x and Northrop at 17.3x trade meaningfully cheaper, TransDigm at 38.7x and Axon at 157x trade richer. The peer median P/E of 38.7x is distorted by the high-growth software/components names; the more relevant comparables (NOC, GD) are pure defense primes trading at 17–22x. On that basis, L3Harris carries a premium to its closest peers that the market is justifying on the MSL IPO optionality and the recently accelerated organic growth.

The valuation case for upside hinges on a sum-of-the-parts unlock. If the MSL carve-out crystallizes the value of a fast-growing, supply-constrained propulsion franchise at a standalone multiple, "RemainCo" L3Harris becomes a higher-margin, communications/space-weighted business that could re-rate. That is a real source of optionality, and it is the reason the analyst consensus target sits at $382 mean / $390 median with a high of $443 — well above the current price. But two facts temper this. The DoW preferred converts at 80% of the IPO price (an effective 20% discount), plus warrants for ~3% of the IPO company, embedding real dilution into the value-creation event. And the timing and political reception of the IPO are uncertain (see Risk Assessment).

We score valuation at 5.0. The multiple is full relative to a mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit durable growth rate, estimate revisions are flat rather than rising, and the catalyst that would justify a higher score (a successful MSL IPO at an attractive multiple) is not yet executed and carries embedded dilution. The consensus targets are credible as a directional anchor, but at ~27x current-year earnings the stock is not cheap relative to the growth it is delivering, so we do not treat those targets as a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

L3Harris occupies a differentiated merchant-supplier/flexible-prime niche, deliberately platform-agnostic to ride multiple OEMs' programs. Its competitive trajectory is positive in the franchises that matter most: CSD's ~25% margins reflect genuine pricing power in tactical communications and EW, and the Aerojet propulsion business sits inside a structurally scarce SRM duopoly with Northrop. Backlog growth of +13% YoY indicates share retention and program wins rather than erosion.

The headwind is the acquisition-reform shift toward OTAs, commercial solutions, and the "Warfighting Acquisition System," which lowers barriers for non-traditional entrants like Anduril, Ursa Major, and Silvus. L3Harris's response — partnering with Palantir, Anduril, Shield Capital, and Amazon Kuiper — is a sensible hedge, but the risk-factor language explicitly flags AI-enabled startups potentially out-innovating legacy primes. On balance, the competitive position is stable-to-improving in the near term, with a genuine multi-year disruption question that the market has only partially digested.

## Risk Assessment

**Government dependence and budget volatility.** With 75% of revenue from the U.S. Government and FY25 marked by a record 43-day shutdown, funding flows in fits through CRs and reconciliation. NASA funding cuts are a specific headwind for AR's space propulsion.

**Fixed-price contract exposure.** 75% of FY25 revenue is fixed-price, placing cost-overrun and inflation risk on L3Harris. EAC adjustments were net favorable $47M in FY25, but individual programs took hits ($31M unfavorable on a naval sensor program in Q1'26; $38M on a classified Maritime program in FY25). This is a recurring swing factor that can compress margins program-by-program.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** With ~$20B of goodwill, reporting-unit deterioration could trigger material write-downs; FY25 already booked an $85M impairment. Reporting units cleared FY26 tests by >40%, providing cushion.

**MSL IPO execution and political scrutiny.** A newly elevated risk factor names the planned IPO and DoW equity investment as sources of uncertainty (carve-out complexity, tax treatment, indemnification, distraction). The House Armed Services Committee issued a draft bill expressing concern that the DoW equity stake comes "at the expense of other suppliers" (notably Northrop), injecting regulatory/political risk into the IPO narrative. This is developing and corroborated by a single trade-press source, but it is a credible thread that could delay or complicate the value-unlock event.

**Environmental and hazardous-materials liabilities.** $657M of environmental liabilities (Aerojet legacy/Superfund sites, partially recoverable $482M via contract pricing), plus catastrophic-incident risk from energetic propellant handling.

**Management transition.** A CFO change (Kenneth Sharp, offer letter Feb 2026) and multiple officer/director 8-Ks in early 2026 coincide with the carve-out — continuity risk to monitor, though no governance red flags surfaced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 33.6% is rich versus 24.0% one-year historical volatility (a 9.7pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (33.6% near-term vs 27.3% at six months), which typically signals an anticipated event. The 1-week implied move is ±2.9% with put/call skew of 4.8%, indicating modest elevated put demand. However, the put/call OI ratio has fallen from 0.91 to 0.52 and IV cooled from 37.1% to 33.6% as the stock rose 2.6%, a net easing of bearish positioning. Short interest is negligible at 1.6% of float (1.6 days to cover), so there is no crowded bearish position. The signal is consistent with the market pricing event risk around the MSL IPO/Q2 results rather than a directional bearish thesis. The backwardation and rich front-end IV are reasons to be patient on entry rather than evidence of fundamental deterioration.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($390–$443)
The MSL IPO executes successfully in 2H'26, crystallizing the value of a scarce, fast-growing propulsion franchise at a standalone multiple while RemainCo re-rates as a higher-margin communications/space business. Defense budgets ratchet higher (GFY27 PBR toward $1.5T, NATO 5% pledge), backlog continues compounding double-digits, CSD margins hold near 25%, and the LHX NeXt savings flow through to operating leverage. EPS reaches the high end of the consensus path (~$13.63 next year, growing further), and the SOTP unlock plus continued buybacks ($1.9B remaining authorization) drive the stock toward the $390 median target and the $443 high. This requires the IPO to clear without political derailment and organic growth to sustain.

### Bear Case ($250–$270)
The MSL IPO is delayed or priced poorly amid HASC political scrutiny and a soft IPO window, removing the near-term catalyst. Fixed-price program EACs deteriorate, compressing margins. A budget CR or reconciliation impasse slows program funding. The stock de-rates from ~27x toward the 17–22x where NOC and GD trade, implying roughly $250–$270 on FY26 earnings (a 15–20% decline). The goodwill-heavy balance sheet limits downside support if a reporting unit weakens.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced: ~24–40% upside to the analyst target range versus ~15–20% downside to the de-rating bear case. That is not the limited-downside, substantial-upside profile required for a high-conviction buy, particularly with estimates flat and the stock already re-rated.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean). Reassessment timeline:** Q2 FY2026 earnings (late July 2026) and any S-1 amendment / IPO pricing for Missile Solutions, plus resolution of the HASC concern in the NDAA process.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. MSL IPO prices or files a public S-1 at a valuation implying a clear SOTP discount in the current LHX price (i.e., the standalone propulsion multiple exceeds the implied multiple embedded in LHX today).
2. Forward EPS estimate revisions turn positive by +5% or more over 90 days alongside sustained ≥10% organic revenue growth — confirming the Q1 acceleration is durable rather than comp-driven.
3. The stock pulls back toward $270–$285 (≈23–25x FY26 EPS) without fundamental deterioration, restoring an asymmetric entry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. MSL IPO is shelved or materially delayed due to HASC/political opposition, removing the value-unlock catalyst.
2. Two consecutive quarters of net-unfavorable EAC adjustments or a margin guide-down, indicating fixed-price execution stress.
3. A goodwill impairment of meaningful size, or backlog growth decelerating below mid-single digits.

## Conclusion

L3Harris is a well-run defense prime with accelerating organic growth, a record backlog, robust free cash flow, and a credible value-crystallization catalyst in the Missile Solutions carve-out, anchored by a government equity stake that signals strategic priority. The fundamental trajectory is constructive and supports continued ownership for existing holders. At $315 — roughly 27x current-year earnings against high-single-digit durable growth and flat estimate revisions — the valuation is full and the catalyst is both partly priced and subject to political and execution risk. The DoW preferred conversion at an 80% discount embeds real dilution into the unlock event, and the options market is pricing near-term event stress. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric, and the appropriate posture is HOLD with a growth lean, with a clear path to upgrade on either an attractive IPO valuation print, a positive estimate revision inflection, or a pullback toward $270–$285.
