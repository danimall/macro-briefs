**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Leonardo DRS is a mid-tier U.S. defense technology company built around two segments: Advanced Sensing & Computing (EO/IR sensors, AESA tactical radars, electronic warfare, counter-UAS, rugged edge computing) and Integrated Mission Systems (naval electric power and propulsion, including the Columbia-class ballistic missile submarine, plus force protection and ground-vehicle integration). The company is delivering against a strong demand backdrop: FY25 revenue grew 12.8% to $3,648M, net income rose 30.5% to $278M, gross margin expanded 100bps to 23.8%, and the company has beaten consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters, most recently by ~26% in Q1 2026. Management raised FY26 guidance to $3.9–3.975B in revenue and $1.26–1.30 adjusted EPS, and a director made an open-market purchase of $1.07M of stock at $42.77 in May.

The structural setup is favorable. The Administration's FY27 budget request (~$1.15T base national defense plus $350B reconciliation) supports the top line, and DRS sits in the highest-priority budget areas — naval power, tactical radar, counter-drone systems. Roughly 45% of remaining performance obligations are Navy electric power/propulsion recognized over up to 11 years, providing rare multi-year revenue visibility. The Columbia-class franchise is a genuine moat.

The problem is price. At ~$47, DRS trades at roughly 37x the midpoint of FY26 adjusted EPS guidance and ~33x FY27 consensus ($1.44), against low-double-digit revenue growth and high-single-digit EPS growth in the out-year. That is a premium to comparably positioned primes (GD ~21x, NOC ~18x, LMT ~26x) and prices flawless execution. Two specific frictions argue against paying up: bookings are decelerating (total bookings -10.7% in Q1, ASC bookings -35.9%, backlog flat at ~$8.4B for over a year), and the equity carries a unique FOCI/proxy-control structure — Leonardo S.p.A. holds ~71% of voting power, leaving a thin true float, constrained M&A, and overhang risk. The quality is real, but it is in the price. We rate DRS a HOLD.

## Company Overview

DRS is a 55+ year defense electronics and systems supplier with ~7,300 employees (7% unionized) and ~80% of revenue from the U.S. government (Navy 36%, Army 36%). It operates as prime contractor on 39% of revenue and subcontractor on 61%, typically as a subsystem supplier to platform primes. No single contract exceeds 10% of revenue, which the company frames as a diversification advantage relative to platform-concentrated peers.

The two reporting segments:

- **Advanced Sensing & Computing (ASC)** — FY25 revenue $2,355M (65% of total). EO/IR sensors, next-generation AESA tactical radars, electronic warfare, signals intelligence, space sensing, counter-UAS, and rugged AI-enabled edge/network computing.
- **Integrated Mission Systems (IMS)** — FY25 revenue $1,307M (35%). Leading provider of naval electric power and propulsion to the U.S. Navy, including the Columbia-class submarine (the first modern U.S. electric-drive submarine), plus force protection, short-range air defense, and ground-vehicle integration.

The defining structural feature of the equity is ownership. DRS is ultimately controlled by Leonardo S.p.A. (Milan-listed Italian aerospace/defense group), which through Leonardo US Holding holds ~71% of DRS voting power. Because the Italian state owns ~30.2% of Leonardo S.p.A., DRS is deemed "foreign-government controlled" by U.S. regulators and operates under an amended FOCI mitigation proxy agreement with the DoD that expires in March 2030. This governs everything from board composition to M&A consent rights and is addressed in detail under Risk Assessment.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has accelerated and is high-quality:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 2,693 | 2,826 | 3,234 | 3,648 |
| Gross margin | 21.4% | 22.9% | 22.8% | 23.8% |
| Operating earnings ($M) | 561* | 231 | 293 | 348 |
| Net income ($M) | 405* | 168 | 213 | 278 |
| Diluted EPS | — | 0.64 | 0.80 | 1.03 |

*2022 operating/net income was inflated by a one-time RADA merger-related gain and is not a baseline.*

Operating cash flow was $366M against capex of $139M (up >60% YoY for capacity expansion, including a new 140,000-sq-ft naval power facility in South Carolina), yielding FCF of $227M (6.2% FCF margin). Internal R&D rose ~40% to $129M, reinvesting into sensing and power-dense propulsion, the areas the budget is funding.

The balance sheet is sound. Cash was $647M at year-end against total debt of ~$347M, with equity of $2,730M. Headline ratios — D/E of 1.64, Altman Z of 2.32 in the "grey zone" — overstate distress risk because they reflect large contract liabilities (customer advances) and $1,238M of goodwill rather than financial leverage. For a government prime with an $8.4B backlog and minimal net debt, bankruptcy risk is genuinely low. The accumulated deficit of -$2.3B is a legacy of pre-IPO write-offs, not current operating losses.

Q1 2026 (reported 5/5/26) extended the trend: revenue $846M (+5.9%), gross margin 25.1% (+240bps), operating earnings $77M (+30.5%), GAAP EPS $0.23 and adjusted EPS $0.26. Both segments expanded margins — ASC from 4.9% to 7.2%, IMS from 11.7% to 12.5% on improving Columbia-class execution. Cash fell to $328M after the company repaid and terminated its $191M 2022 term loan in January and entered a new $500M unsecured JPMorgan revolver (maturing 2031); Q1 operating cash flow of -$66M is a normal seasonal pattern.

Two financial caveats temper the optimism. First, the 2025 gross-margin expansion was partly driven by a one-time quantum cascade laser license, so the underlying margin trajectory is less steep than the headline. Second, negative estimate-at-completion (EAC) adjustments grew to -$59M in 2025 (2% of revenue) from -$25M in 2024 and -$23M in 2023, driven by a legacy ground-surveillance program charge and germanium cost inflation. On 88% fixed-price revenue, rising EAC charges signal some execution stress and warrant monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

The demand environment is durable and broad-based. DRS is concentrated in the FY27 budget priority categories: naval electric power and propulsion (Columbia-class, multi-decade), tactical radar and counter-UAS/SHORAD (a direct beneficiary of drone-warfare lessons from Ukraine and the Middle East), and electronic warfare. The defense theme has multiple confirming signals — Leidos, V2X, and BWX also raised 2026 guidance — which raises the durability of the topline thesis above any single-company story.

Revenue visibility is unusually good for a mid-tier name, given the long-dated Navy electric power/propulsion RPO. The four-quarter beat streak (6.5%, 2.9%, 12.1%, 25.9% surprises) supports confidence in current estimates, and consensus is rising modestly: current-year EPS revised +3.5% over 90 days, next-year +2.9%. These are positive but not the +30%-type revisions that signal a systematically underestimated earnings recovery — DRS is a steady compounder, not a trough-to-recovery story.

The clearest yellow flag sits in bookings. FY25 bookings rose only 4.1% (book-to-bill ~1.16) with ASC bookings down 5.7%. In Q1 2026, the ASC decline ran against a tough prior-year comp that included a large multi-year ground-vehicle-sensing award. With revenue still growing 6–13%, this is not yet alarming — multi-year awards distort individual quarters — but sustained sub-1.0 book-to-bill in ASC would eventually constrain the growth rate the market is paying for. This is the single most important fundamental metric to watch over the next two to three quarters.

## Valuation Assessment

Against trailing EPS of $1.03 the multiple is ~46x. This is a premium to the defense prime cohort:

| Company | Approx. forward P/E | Revenue growth profile |
|---|---|---|
| DRS | ~33–37x | Low-double-digit |
| LMT | ~26x | Low-single-digit |
| GD | ~21x | Low-double-digit |
| NOC | ~18x | Mid-single-digit |
| RTX | ~33x | Mid-single-digit |

DRS does merit a growth premium — its revenue is growing faster than most primes, its margin trajectory is positive, and its exposure is weighted to the fastest-growing budget lines. But the premium is already substantial. On a PEG basis, ~33x forward against ~10% EPS growth (FY26-to-FY27 adjusted EPS midpoint implies ~12%, but the out-year consensus growth is closer to high-single to low-double-digit) yields a PEG well above 2.0 — expensive for the durability on offer, particularly with bookings decelerating.

The cycle-adjusted argument that supports premium defense multiples elsewhere (trough earnings recovering) does not apply cleanly here: DRS earnings are not at a trough, they are compounding off a normal base, so trailing-P/E discounting is not the right lens, but neither is there a hidden earnings inflection that forward P/E understates. The estimate revisions are positive but modest, not the +30% magnitude that would justify overriding a rich multiple.

Analyst targets bracket the stock: low $47, mean $52.9, median $53.5, high $59. At ~$47 the stock sits at the analyst low. On a premium-multiple name where targets and price both reflect the same growth extrapolation, proximity to the low end is not strong evidence of undervaluation — it reflects that the stock has already re-rated toward fair value after the Q1 beat-and-raise (the stock rose from ~$40 to ~$48 in May). The valuation score reflects that the quality is real and largely priced: there is no specific mispricing to exploit at current levels, and downside to a de-rate toward the prime cohort is material.

## Competitive Landscape

DRS's competitive position is stable-to-improving. Its moat rests on incumbent sole- or dual-source positions on long-lived programs — Columbia-class naval power is the clearest example, a multi-decade franchise where DRS supplies the first modern U.S. electric-drive submarine propulsion. The company's domain depth in EO/IR sensing and power-dense propulsion, combined with agility relative to larger primes, has translated into share gains in counter-UAS and tactical radar.

As a mid-tier player, DRS competes against larger primes with greater R&D scale and pricing flexibility, and against agile non-traditional entrants, while frequently teaming with the very primes it competes against (61% of revenue is subcontractor work). International revenue is modest and volatile — 8% in FY25, down from 13% in FY24 as Ukraine-related and legacy program revenue rolled off. Margin expansion across both segments and rising IR&D investment support a constructive read on competitive trajectory. The bookings deceleration is the one signal that complicates the share-gain narrative and bears watching.

## Risk Assessment

**FOCI / proxy-agreement structure (the defining risk).** Under the amended FOCI mitigation proxy agreement with the DoD, independent U.S.-cleared proxy holders vote Leonardo's shares and the parent retains consent rights over major M&A, debt above target leverage, mergers, and dividends. Implications: (a) a thin true public float (~29%) with limited minority-shareholder influence; (b) constrained M&A in a consolidating industry, since CFIUS treats DRS as a foreign person; (c) overhang risk from potential Leonardo secondary sales (registration rights exercised once, Nov 2023); and (d) a tail risk that proxy non-renewal or breach could jeopardize classified contracts. The 10-K notes DRS "has at times been found to not have strictly complied" (never sanctioned). This is a permanent structural feature that caps the multiple the market should rationally pay.

**Customer concentration.** ~80% of revenue from the U.S. government correlates DRS to appropriations cycles, shutdown risk, and procurement priority shifts.

**Fixed-price execution.** 88% of revenue is firm-fixed-price; DRS bears cost-overrun risk. Rising negative EAC adjustments signal incremental program stress.

**Supply chain.** Documented shortages of germanium, rare earths, castings, and microelectronics, plus exposure to foreign export controls and tariff dynamics.

**Israel operations.** Material manufacturing and R&D presence in a conflict zone, with workforce-call-up and logistics risk.

**Bookings/backlog softness.** Discussed above — the live fundamental yellow flag.

**CEO transition.** William Lynn III retired after 14 years; John Baylouny (former COO) became CEO around January 1, 2026. The transition was orderly and internally promoted, but it is a leadership change to monitor.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a near-term event without directional bearishness. Near-term ATM IV is 49.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 39.5% — rich by ~9.6 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (49.1% near-term vs. 41.6% long-term), indicating the market expects imminent stress around the June expiry. Positioning is call-heavy: put/call skew is -4.4%, put/call volume ratio 0.12, and put/call OI ratio 0.31. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.8%. The combination of rich near-term IV with light put demand suggests event hedging and upside positioning rather than informed bearish bets, consistent with a name that just printed a beat-and-raise and re-rated higher. Short interest is negligible at 1.3% of float (3.5 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic on either side.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$59, +24%):** DRS sustains book-to-bill above 1.0 in both segments as FY27 budget dollars flow, ASC bookings normalize off the tough comp, margins continue expanding on Columbia-class execution and operating leverage, and the company delivers toward the high end of guidance with continued beats. The market holds the premium multiple given best-in-class growth among defense names, and FY27 EPS reaches ~$1.50+, supporting a ~39x multiple toward the analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$36, -24%):** Bookings deceleration persists into a sustained sub-1.0 ASC book-to-bill, the one-time margin tailwinds do not recur and EAC charges continue rising, growth decelerates toward the prime-cohort norm, and the multiple compresses toward 24-26x forward (GD/LMT territory). A Leonardo secondary sale or proxy-agreement friction adds an overhang. On ~$1.40 FY27 EPS at ~26x, the stock revisits the mid-$30s — a level it traded at as recently as December 2025.

The base case sits between: DRS is a high-quality compounder delivering steady mid-teens earnings growth, but at ~33-37x forward the risk/reward is balanced. The upside requires the premium to hold while bookings reaccelerate; the downside requires only a modest de-rate toward peers. With neither tail clearly dominant and no specific mispricing to exploit, the position is a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (early August 2026), with the bookings trajectory the key metric. The FY27 budget appropriations process through H2 2026 is a secondary catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Book-to-bill returns above 1.0 in ASC for two consecutive quarters with backlog inflecting upward from ~$8.4B, demonstrating the bookings dip was a comp artifact rather than a trend.
- A pullback toward the high-$30s/low-$40s that compresses forward P/E below ~28x while estimate revisions stay positive — improving risk/reward without fundamental deterioration.
- Evidence the FY27 budget dollars are converting into firm DRS awards (multi-year naval power or tactical radar bookings), confirming the growth durability the multiple requires.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Sustained sub-1.0 book-to-bill in ASC for 3+ quarters with backlog declining, signaling the growth narrative is breaking.
- EAC charges expanding beyond 2-3% of revenue, indicating fixed-price execution is deteriorating materially.
- A Leonardo secondary sale, proxy-agreement breach finding with contract consequences, or guidance cut.

## Conclusion

Leonardo DRS is a genuinely good business — accelerating revenue, expanding margins, a four-quarter beat streak, a multi-decade Columbia-class naval-power annuity, and exposure to the fastest-growing parts of the defense budget. The director open-market purchase and the broad-based defense demand confirmation across peers reinforce the constructive read on fundamentals. None of that is in dispute.

The dispute is valuation. At ~$47 the stock has re-rated to ~33-37x forward EPS on a low-double-digit grower, a premium to GD, NOC, and LMT that prices in flawless execution at exactly the moment bookings are decelerating and backlog has gone flat. The FOCI/proxy-control structure adds a permanent governance discount. Estimate revisions are positive but modest, not the magnitude that would justify overriding the multiple. The risk/reward is symmetric — roughly +24% to the analyst high against -24% to a peer-multiple de-rate — and that symmetry, on a name where the quality is already well understood and priced, defines a HOLD rather than a buy. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$30s/low-$40s or on confirmed ASC bookings reacceleration.
