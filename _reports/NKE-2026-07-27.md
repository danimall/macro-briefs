**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nike sits at $41.70, down 44.7% over the trailing year and roughly 48% below its 52-week high of $80.17, in the second year of a deliberate, management-orchestrated reset. The company is intentionally sacrificing near-term revenue — cutting supply of oversaturated franchises (Air Force 1, Dunk, Jordan lifestyle), pulling back on discounted digital selling, and cleaning up bloated inventory in China and Converse — to restore full-price brand equity. The strategy is coherent, but it is slow, non-linear by management's own admission, and it collides with a softening discretionary consumer. Forward EPS estimates have fallen across every window: current-year consensus is down 7.2% over 90 days to $1.73, and next year is down 8.5% to $2.25.

The headline financials overstate the underlying business. FY26 net income of $3.1B and reported diluted EPS of $2.10 were materially inflated by a one-time $986M IEEPA tariff refund recognized in cost of sales after the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling. In Q4 alone, this added 52 cents to EPS (reported 72 cents vs. 20 cents adjusted) and lifted Q4 gross margin roughly 890bps to 49.2%. Stripping out the refund, the underlying business is still contracting, with gross margins far below the 44.6% FY24 level and EBIT margin near 8%. Any valuation anchored to the trailing 19.9x P/E is anchored to inflated earnings.

The most compelling contrarian signal is the insider-buying cluster. CEO Elliott Hill purchased roughly $2M across two tranches (~$61 in December, ~$42.27 in April); directors Tim Cook and Robert Swan each bought at both ~$59 and ~$42, doubling down as the price fell. This is open-market buying by people with unusually good information, at multi-year lows, and it is the clearest reason to give the downside the benefit of the doubt. Conviction from insiders does not create a near-term catalyst, and the fundamental picture — China down through FY27, Converse in freefall, digital traffic falling, estimates declining — does not support a BUY. We rate NKE a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Nike is the world's largest designer, marketer, and distributor of athletic footwear, apparel, and equipment, headquartered in Beaverton, Oregon. It sells through NIKE Direct (owned stores plus digital, $17.7B in FY26) and Wholesale ($27.5B), across four geographies: North America ($20.5B), EMEA ($12.6B), Greater China ($5.8B), and Asia Pacific & Latin America ($6.2B), plus the wholly-owned Converse subsidiary ($1.2B). Product mix runs footwear ($29.5B, ~65%), apparel ($13.4B), and equipment ($2.2B). The Jordan Brand generated $7.0B, down from an $8.7B FY24 peak.

The moat rests on brand equity, athlete and team endorsements (a $15.5B forward contractual obligation), asset-light scale sourcing through outsourced contract manufacturing (only $240M goodwill, minimal PP&E), and product innovation. Governance is founder-controlled: Swoosh, LLC (the Knight family entity) owns roughly 79% of Class A stock, and Class A holders elect three-quarters of the board.

The current business is defined by a two-year earnings collapse (net income down ~45% from FY24) and a stabilization attempt led by Elliott Hill, a Nike veteran who returned as President & CEO in late 2024 to run the "Sport Offense" turnaround.

## Financial Analysis

The trajectory of the core numbers tells the story:

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $51.4B | $46.3B | $46.4B (flat; -2% cc) |
| Gross margin | 44.6% | 42.7% | 42.9% |
| EBIT margin | 12.7% | 8.2% | 8.3% |
| Net income | $5.7B | $3.2B | $3.1B |
| Diluted EPS | $3.73 | $2.16 | $2.10 |
| Operating cash flow | — | $3.70B | $2.87B |

Revenue is down roughly 10% from the FY24 peak. FY26 revenue was flat only because a wholesale rebound (+6%) offset a NIKE Direct decline (-6%, driven by digital -12% on reduced traffic).

The earnings-quality flag is material. Excluding the tariff refund, the FY26 gross-margin "improvement" to 42.9% largely disappears, and underlying profitability remains weak. As of quarter-end, Nike had collected only ~$302M of the $986M, leaving a ~$684M receivable (substantially collected shortly after). The refund does not repeat in FY27, which is one reason forward estimates sit well below reported trailing EPS.

The balance sheet is solid but no longer a fortress. Current ratio is 1.96, cash plus short-term investments about $9.0B, long-term debt $5.9B. Reported D/E of 2.58 looks elevated but reflects the asset-light model and years of buybacks that shrank equity — retained earnings turned slightly negative (-$155M). Both S&P and Moody's downgraded Nike's credit in 2025 to A+/A2, which raises borrowing costs at the margin. The Altman Z-Score of 2.36 sits in the grey zone, driven by depressed EBIT/assets, not by distress. ROIC declined to 18.7% from 20.2%.

Capital allocation shifted defensively. Nike paused share repurchases in Q1 FY26, buying just $122M all year against billions historically, and returned roughly $2.5B mostly via dividends. Operating cash flow fell to $2.87B from $3.70B. The pause is prudent given the earnings decline, but it removes a historical support for per-share metrics.

The earnings-surprise record is superficially strong — four consecutive beats, including a 50.2% beat on the June 2026 quarter (20 cents actual vs. 13 cents estimated). But the bar was set low: consensus had modeled a severely depressed number, and much of the beat traces to the tariff refund rather than operational outperformance. The beats reflect a heavily reset estimate base, not accelerating fundamentals.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to speak of at the top line, and the near-term direction is down. Management guided H1 FY27 revenue to decline low-to-mid single digits, with Q1 FY27 gross margin "slightly positive" and EPS "flattish." Management explicitly warned the turnaround is non-linear and flagged decelerating consumer trends beginning mid-April 2026, with sportswear and Jordan streetwear sell-through "remaining challenged."

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration. The 30-day next-quarter cut of -9.1% shows the negative momentum is not abating. This is the opposite of the positive-revision turn that would signal a fundamental recovery. The strong estimate-revision buy signal — positive revisions plus accelerating revenue — is absent; the data point the other way.

Within the mix, there are real bright spots and real disasters:

- **Running** was up double digits for a fifth straight quarter, adding roughly $1B in sales. This is the performance/innovation core regaining traction against Hoka, On, and New Balance, and it is the strongest evidence the "Sport Offense" strategy is working where it matters.
- **Greater China** fell 13% cc to $5.8B with EBIT down 20%, and digital there fell 29%. Because China has historically been Nike's highest-margin geography, its decline is disproportionately profit-dilutive. Management expects negative China impact to continue through FY27.
- **Converse** collapsed 32% cc with EBIT down 93% to $18M — near breakeven — prompting open analyst discussion of divestiture.
- **Digital** fell 12% on lost traffic as Nike deliberately repositions Nike.com as a full-price platform and re-leans into wholesale, refreshing 15,000+ wholesale doors and elevating 150+ DTC stores. This is a strategic reversal of the 2020-2023 DTC-first push.

The World Cup provides a potential multi-quarter brand tailwind — Nike's "Rip the Script" campaign exceeded 1.5B views and outpaced Adidas on engagement, though Nike is not an official sponsor. This is a marketing edge, not a revenue guarantee.

The honest read: revenue growth returns no earlier than FY27's back half in a good scenario, and Telsey argues weak sportswear/DTC/international trends persist until FY2028.

## Valuation Assessment

Nike trades at 19.9x trailing earnings, but that multiple is on inflated earnings. On the falling forward number of $1.73 for the current fiscal year, the effective forward P/E is closer to 24x, and on next year's $2.25 it is about 18.5x. Neither figure is cheap for a business with declining revenue and no near-term earnings growth.

Against peers in Apparel, Accessories & Luxury Goods, Nike screens roughly in line to slightly rich on a growth-adjusted basis. The peer median P/E is 26.0 (TPR 46.8, RL 26.0, VFC 27.3, KTB 16.2, LULU 9.8). Nike's trailing 19.9x is below the median, but the peer set is heterogeneous, and LULU at 9.8x — also a wounded athletic-lifestyle name — is the more relevant comparable than TPR or RL. Nike does not deserve a premium to that cohort while it is guiding revenue lower and cutting estimates.

A below-median multiple is not a buy signal here. The compressed multiple reflects genuine structural pressure — China decline through FY27, Converse near breakeven, digital erosion — not a temporary trough with a visible recovery catalyst. That is the profile of a value trap absent an identifiable turn, and the discipline of demanding a specific mispricing thesis before rewarding cheapness applies directly. The 3.9% dividend yield provides some absolute support and is well-covered by cash flow, which is a floor argument, not an appreciation argument.

The analyst price-target spread is unusually wide — low $23, high $94, mean $51.12, median $47 — reflecting the sharp bull/bear split. On stocks with rising estimates, consensus targets act as floors, but Nike's estimates are falling, so the mean target of $51 is more likely to compress toward the median $47 than to be revised upward in the near term. The degrading sentiment trend (buy ratings falling from 15 to 11 over three months, hold count rising to 25) reinforces that.

Our fair-value read centers around $46-48 on normalized FY27 earnings power, which is roughly 10-15% above spot — not enough margin of safety to force a BUY, but consistent with a HOLD supported by the dividend and downside protection from insider conviction.

## Competitive Landscape

Nike remains the dominant global athletic brand, but its competitive trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. The moat — brand equity, endorsement scale, sourcing, and innovation — is intact, but it has been eroding at the edges. Hoka, On Running, and New Balance have taken meaningful share in running and lifestyle, and Nike's own share losses in the DTC/digital channel were partly self-inflicted through over-saturation of hero franchises and heavy discounting.

The competitive picture is genuinely two-sided. On the positive side, running growth and the reinvestment in wholesale doors rebuild marketplace breadth and suggest the innovation engine is functioning where product credibility matters most. On the negative side, sportswear and Jordan lifestyle — the high-margin, high-velocity franchises that drove the peak — remain challenged, China is losing to local and international competitors, and Converse has effectively lost its cultural relevance.

The FY26 10-K added substantial new risk language on AI-driven shopping discovery (agentic commerce) and rising endorsement competition from athlete-led ventures. These signal management sees structural threats to both how consumers discover products and the cost structure of its endorsement model. The competitive-position score sits at 4.5 — the company is defending a leading position while share erodes in key lifestyle categories, offset by genuine gains in performance running. That trajectory does not warrant a score above the midpoint, and the prior report's downgrade of this dimension (from 7.5 to 5.0) correctly recognized the deterioration.

## Risk Assessment

**Earnings quality.** The single most important risk is that trailing profitability is overstated by the non-recurring tariff refund. FY27 will not have this benefit, which is why forward estimates sit below trailing EPS. Investors anchoring to the 19.9x trailing multiple are underestimating the effective forward multiple.

**China structural decline.** The FY26 declines (revenue -13% cc, EBIT -20%, digital -29%) are expected to continue through FY27, and are disproportionately profit-dilutive given China's historically high margins.

**Converse.** Near breakeven at $18M EBIT, a divestiture would remove drag and distraction but crystallize a loss and complicate the reported base.

**Consumer softness and turnaround execution.** Nike's turnaround is running into a weakening spending backdrop, which raises execution risk against the non-linear path management has flagged.

**Balance sheet defense.** Credit downgrades to A+/A2, buyback pause, negative retained earnings, and rising effective leverage reflect a company playing defense — prudent, but a departure from the historical capital-return machine.

**Litigation overhang.** Multiple securities class action and shareholder derivative lawsuits tied to the stock-price drop create an investor-facing legal overhang.

**Governance.** Knight-family control of ~79% of Class A and three-quarters of board seats limits minority-shareholder say, mitigated by a high-caliber independent board that is putting personal capital at risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options market prices elevated but not extreme uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 45.1% is rich versus 36.3% historical vol (an 8.8pp spread), and IV sits in the 67th percentile of its own chain. The put/call skew is balanced at -1.5%, and the put/call OI ratio of 1.22 is mildly defensive but not alarming. The term structure is flat, with the 1-week and 6-month IVs both near 45%, and a positive skew of 5.8% at the 3-month tenor around the September expiry — the market is pricing some downside protection demand into the next quarterly print. The implied ±4.1% weekly move and ±19.4% six-month move indicate the market expects continued volatility without a strong directional bet. IV rising 3.1pp since late June against a roughly flat price suggests the market is pricing an upcoming catalyst (the September quarter) rather than resolving uncertainty. For a HOLD, this is consistent: no options-implied conviction in either direction, and rich IV that argues against paying up for the stock via options.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The turnaround is real and the bottom is in. Running growth proves the innovation core works; gross margin and inventory recover before revenue (the "quality-before-quantity" thesis); wholesale reinvestment rebuilds marketplace breadth; China and Converse are cleaned up through FY27; and the World Cup provides a brand tailwind. The heavyweight CFO hire (David Denton, formerly of Pfizer, Lowe's, and CVS) signals a serious capital-allocation and operating-discipline pivot. The CEO and two operating-CEO directors bought stock at ~$42, doubling down from ~$59 — a coordinated vote of confidence at cyclical lows. On a return to normalized $2.75-3.00 EPS by FY28 at a 22x multiple, the stock is worth $60-66. Bull case: **$65**.

### Bear Case

The turnaround stretches to FY2028 (Telsey's view), China keeps declining, Converse forces a distracting divestiture or write-down, and the softening consumer deepens the revenue decline beyond the low-to-mid single-digit guide. Forward estimates keep falling, the dividend gets squeezed if cash flow deteriorates further, and the stock re-rates toward the low end of the analyst range. On depressed $1.70 EPS at a 16x trough multiple, plus continued estimate cuts, the stock revisits the low-to-mid $30s. Bear case: **$32** (roughly -23% from spot).

### Base Case

Revenue stabilizes but does not grow through FY27; margins recover gradually as full-price selling returns; the tariff-refund tailwind rolls off; and normalized earnings power settles around $2.25-2.50 for FY27. At a 19-20x multiple on that base, fair value is roughly $46-48. Base case: **$47** (~+13% from spot, plus a 3.9% dividend).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The insider conviction and brand quality support the downside, but falling estimates, a declining revenue guide, and the absence of a near-term earnings catalyst prevent a stronger call. This is an honest uncertain hold, not a growth or income conviction.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q1 FY27 print (approximately late September 2026), which will show whether gross margin turns "slightly positive" as guided and whether China/Converse deterioration is moderating. The September options skew suggests the market views this print as the key catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS estimate revisions (reversing the current -7% to -9% trend), signaling the fundamental turn has arrived.
- Confirmed gross-margin expansion on an ex-tariff-refund basis, plus stabilizing or growing NIKE Direct/digital revenue.
- China revenue decline moderating to flat or better on a constant-currency basis ahead of the FY27 guidance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- H1 FY27 revenue falling worse than the guided low-to-mid single-digit decline, indicating the turnaround is failing against the consumer backdrop.
- A dividend cut or further balance-sheet deterioration (additional credit downgrade, sustained negative retained earnings, cash-flow erosion below $2.5B).
- Cluster insider selling by the same executives who recently bought, which would reverse the strongest bullish signal.

## Conclusion

Nike is a franchise-quality brand executing a coherent but slow and non-linear turnaround into a weakening discretionary consumer. The FY26 earnings are lower quality than the headline suggests — flattered by the non-recurring tariff refund — and every forward estimate window is falling, which removes the estimate-revision turn that would justify a BUY. The valuation is not cheap on forward earnings despite the ~48% drawdown, and a below-median trailing P/E reflects structural pressure rather than a temporary trough with a visible catalyst.

Against that, the open-market insider-buying cluster is genuinely compelling: a sitting CEO and two operating-CEO directors each doubled down at ~$42. That conviction, combined with a well-covered dividend and demonstrable strength in the running category, supports the downside and argues against shorting or avoiding. Conviction is not a catalyst, and the fundamental data — China down through FY27, Converse near breakeven, digital erosion, falling estimates — does not support paying up here.

The score trajectory has crept up from the low 4s to 5.5 over recent reports as the balance sheet and growth outlook stabilized, and the recommendation moved from AVOID to HOLD. New data neither confirms a further improvement nor reverses it: the insider signal strengthens the floor while the estimate cuts and the tariff-refund roll-off cap the ceiling. We hold at an overall 5.0, reiterating HOLD, and would revisit constructively on a genuine estimate-revision turn at the September print or a pullback toward the mid-$30s that widens the margin of safety.
