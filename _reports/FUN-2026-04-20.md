## Executive Summary

Six Flags Entertainment is North America's largest regional amusement park operator, created through the July 2024 merger of Cedar Fair and the former Six Flags. The combination has underperformed on every dimension that matters: EBITDA margins compressed 610 basis points to 27.1% in FY2025, the company recorded a $1.52 billion goodwill impairment just 15 months after closing, total shareholder return trailed peers by 58 percentage points, and the CEO was replaced. The stock has fallen 48% from its 52-week high of $38.47 to approximately $20 today.

The balance sheet is the binding constraint. Net debt of $5.1 billion at roughly 6.5x Adjusted EBITDA leaves minimal margin for error. Cash interest expense of $320–330 million annually consumes approximately 40% of operating cash flow. Free cash flow was negative $152 million in FY2025. The January 2026 refinancing eliminated near-term maturity risk but raised the coupon on $1 billion of notes from ~5.4% to 8.625%. Covenant step-downs to 4.5x net first lien leverage by end-2027 require substantial EBITDA improvement that has not yet materialized.

Jana Partners is publicly pushing for a full company sale, and the board has been reconstituted under pressure from three separate activist campaigns in 12 months. While a takeout scenario provides optionality, the $5.2 billion debt load makes a leveraged acquisition structurally difficult. Analyst estimates for current-year EPS have fallen from +$0.60 to -$0.43 over the past 90 days, and next-year estimates have dropped from +$1.10 to +$0.13. This is a deeply levered consumer discretionary business facing margin pressure, management instability, and deteriorating earnings momentum in a macro environment of record-low consumer sentiment and rising energy costs.

## Company Overview

Six Flags operates 26 amusement parks, 15 separately gated water parks, and 9 resorts across 16 U.S. states, Canada, and Mexico. The portfolio includes iconic properties such as Cedar Point, Six Flags Great Adventure, Kings Island, and Knott's Berry Farm. Revenue splits approximately 51% admissions, 35% food/merchandise/games, and 14% accommodations and premium products. The business is highly seasonal, with roughly 70% of attendance and revenue concentrated in Q2 and Q3.

The company holds exclusive U.S./Canada/Mexico theme park rights for Warner Bros./DC Comics animated characters and exclusive U.S./Canada Peanuts character rights. It also manages Six Flags Qiddiya City in Saudi Arabia. In March 2026, Six Flags sold 7 lower-performing parks for approximately $342 million to Enchanted Parks/EPR Properties as part of a portfolio rationalization effort. Six of those parks closed in early April 2026.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Attendance

FY2025 revenue reached $3.10 billion, up 14.4% from $2.71 billion in FY2024. However, this growth is almost entirely attributable to the former Six Flags parks being included for a full 12 months versus only 6 months post-merger in 2024. On a like-for-like basis, attendance declined approximately 2.1 million visits. Total attendance was 47.4 million versus 41.6 million, a 13.8% increase driven by the accounting effect of the merger.

Per capita spending grew only 1.0% for the full year ($61.90 vs. $61.31), though Q4 showed encouraging acceleration at 8% ($66.41). The Q4 improvement was driven by pricing optimization, premium product adoption (Fast Lane/Flash Pass), and food and beverage initiatives.

### Profitability

Adjusted EBITDA declined $83 million to $792 million despite the revenue increase. Modified EBITDA margin compressed 610 basis points from 33.2% to 27.1%. The core issue is that Cedar Fair's legacy parks ran at meaningfully higher margins than the acquired Six Flags parks, and integration has not closed the gap. Lower attendance at existing parks combined with a high fixed-cost structure produced significant margin erosion.

The company recorded a $1.52 billion goodwill impairment in Q3 2025, triggered by what management described as "revenue and earnings not meeting expectations" and a "sustained decline in share price vs. peers." This write-down reduced equity from $2.04 billion to $550 million.

### Balance Sheet

Net debt stands at $5.11 billion, representing approximately 6.5x Adjusted EBITDA. Total debt is $5.2 billion before issuance costs. The debt/equity ratio has expanded to 14.2x following the impairment. Current ratio is 0.69x, meaning current liabilities exceed current assets. Cash on hand is $91 million with $532 million available on the revolving credit facility.

The debt structure following January 2026 refinancing includes:

- $1,481M term loan (SOFR + 200bps, May 2031)
- $1,000M 8.625% senior notes (January 2032, new)
- $300M 6.50% notes (October 2028)
- $500M 5.25% notes (July 2029)
- $800M 7.25% notes (May 2031)
- $850M 6.625% secured notes (May 2032)
- $272M revolver borrowings

The refinancing eliminated 2027 maturity walls but raised the blended cost of debt. Annual cash interest is projected at $320–330 million.

### Cash Flow

FY2025 operating cash flow was $327 million, down 12.4% from $373 million in 2024. Capital expenditure totaled $480 million, producing free cash flow of negative $152 million. FY2026 capex guidance is $400–425 million. Even with the $342 million in park sale proceeds, FCF generation in FY2026 will be marginal at best. The company has no dividend capacity under current covenants (pro forma leverage exceeds the 5.50x threshold).

### Earnings Trajectory

The earnings surprise record is volatile: a massive Q4 2025 miss (-291.7%, actual -$0.91 vs. estimate -$0.23) and a significant Q2 2025 miss (-70.6%, actual $0.26 vs. estimate $0.89), partially offset by a strong Q3 2025 beat (+47.7%). This inconsistency undermines forward estimate confidence.

The consensus has gone from expecting a profitable FY2026 to expecting a loss, and the FY2027 outlook has collapsed from $1.10 to $0.13.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth prospects are limited. Attendance has declined on a like-for-like basis. The company's stated strategy centers on three levers:

1. **Per capita spending optimization**: Q4 2025 results (+8% per capita) suggest this can work when executed properly. Premium products (Fast Lane, Flash Pass, VIP tours), food and beverage upgrades, and dynamic pricing are the mechanisms.

2. **Cost reduction and operating efficiency**: Management targets significant cost synergies from the merger, including corporate overhead reduction and procurement savings. The 7-park sale removes lower-margin assets. FY2026 capex guidance ($400–425M) is reduced from FY2025 ($480M).

3. **Fewer operating days, higher quality**: The elimination of winter holiday events at 4 parks and the general shift toward fewer but higher-revenue operating days is a margin-improvement strategy rather than a growth strategy.

All three levers target efficiency rather than expansion. In a consumer environment characterized by record-low sentiment (Michigan 47.6), rising energy costs feeding through to discretionary budgets, and elevated inflation reducing real wages, volume growth for a regional theme park operator is structurally difficult. The company's guidance for FY2026 revenue was not explicitly quantified in the available data, but the dramatic downward revision in EPS estimates implies flat to declining revenue.

## Valuation Assessment

The stock trades at $19.97, implying a market capitalization of approximately $2.0 billion and an enterprise value of roughly $7.1 billion. Against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $792 million, EV/EBITDA is approximately 9.0x.

The trailing P/E is negative (-1.3x) due to the impairment-driven net loss, and the forward P/E is also negative given consensus expects FY2026 EPS of -$0.43. The stock does not screen well on earnings-based metrics.

Relative to peers, the picture is mixed. The peer median P/E is 34.9x (Planet Fitness at 27.3x, OneSpaWorld at 34.9x), but FUN cannot be compared on P/E given negative earnings. United Parks & Resorts (PRKS), the closest comparable at $2 billion market cap, also lacks a P/E but has experienced a similar 35% drawdown from 52-week highs.

On EV/EBITDA, 9.0x is reasonable for a regional park operator if margins stabilize. If EBITDA declines further in FY2026 on continued attendance erosion, the multiple would expand on lower earnings. If EBITDA recovers to $900 million (management's implied target with synergies and park sales), EV/EBITDA would compress to approximately 7.5x. That recovery requires execution that has not yet been demonstrated.

The analyst consensus target is $23.50 (mean) with a range of $14.00 to $29.00. The $14 low target implies 30% downside from current levels. Oppenheimer's recent cut from $40 to $26 (maintaining Outperform) reflects the trend of declining expectations even among bulls.

## Competitive Landscape

Six Flags is the largest regional amusement park operator in North America by number of parks, giving it geographic diversification and procurement scale. Key competitive advantages include iconic park brands (Cedar Point, Great Adventure, Kings Island), exclusive character licensing (DC Comics, Peanuts), and substantial owned real estate ($4.3 billion net PP&E).

Competitive disadvantages are meaningful:

1. **Integration execution gap**: Former Six Flags parks operate at structurally lower margins than Cedar Fair parks, and the merger has not closed this gap after 18 months.
2. **Leverage handicap**: Competitors do not carry comparable debt loads, giving them more flexibility for capital investment and marketing.
3. **Management instability**: Three activist campaigns, a CEO change, and a chairman change in 12 months create execution risk during the most critical period of the integration.

Regional parks compete primarily on location, ride quality, pricing, and atmosphere. The threat from destination parks (Disney, Universal) is moderate — different price points and travel distances serve different occasions. The more relevant competitive threat is entertainment alternatives broadly: dining, sports, streaming, and travel. In an environment where consumers are trading down, theme parks represent a relatively expensive family activity ($250–400+ per visit for a family of four).

## Risk Assessment

### Financial Risk: Severe
The covenant step-downs to 4.5x by end-2027 require approximately $200 million of annual EBITDA improvement over two years. If FY2026 EBITDA comes in below $850 million, the deleveraging trajectory becomes questionable. The negative FCF position means the company is consuming cash, not generating it. The 7-park sale for $342 million provides a one-time buffer but does not solve the structural cash flow problem.

### Operational Risk: Elevated
The seasonal concentration (70% in Q2–Q3) means one bad weather quarter can materially damage full-year results (documented in Q2 2025). Rising tariffs increase ride and equipment costs. Labor cost inflation persists. The partnership obligations related to Six Flags Over Texas and Six Flags Over Georgia create unusual complexity and potential forced transactions.

### Governance Risk: Elevated
Three separate activists (Dendur, Sachem Head, Jana Partners) now have board representation or influence. Jana is publicly demanding sale exploration. CEO and chairman have both been replaced within 6 months. This level of instability creates risk of strategic whiplash. No C-suite insider has made open-market purchases despite the stock's 48% decline from its 52-week high.

### Macro Risk: High
Consumer discretionary spending is contracting. Michigan sentiment at 47.6 is a record low. Rising energy costs compress household budgets. Regional theme parks are early victims of consumer belt-tightening because visits are deferrable and substitutable.

### Impairment Risk: Ongoing
The company still carries $2.07 billion of goodwill as of December 31, 2025. The 10-K explicitly warns of "further goodwill and/or trade name impairment" risk. Continued underperformance in FY2026 could produce additional impairments.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 82.2% is 18.8 percentage points above 63.4% historical volatility, placing IV at the 85th percentile of its chain. The term structure is in backwardation (82.2% near-term vs. 74.6% at 6-month), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress or a near-term catalyst. However, the OI put/call ratio of 0.69 suggests accumulated positioning is more call-heavy, potentially reflecting speculative takeout bets or short covering expectations. This options structure is consistent with a stock facing binary outcomes: takeout speculation on one side, fundamental deterioration on the other.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($26-29)

Successful integration under new management (CEO Reilly) stabilizes margins. Cost synergies from the merger materialize in FY2026. Per capita spending growth (demonstrated in Q4 2025 at 8%) offsets attendance softness. The $342 million park sale proceeds reduce debt meaningfully. EBITDA recovers to $900 million+, reducing leverage toward 5.5x. Jana Partners' sale push leads to a legitimate strategic review, and the company's real estate and brand value attract a buyer (EPR-type REIT or infrastructure investor) at 8–9x EBITDA. At 8.5x on $900M EBITDA, EV = $7.65B, minus $5.1B debt = $2.55B equity value, or roughly $25 per share.

### Bear Case ($10-14)

Consumer spending deterioration worsens through 2026 amid rising energy costs and falling confidence. Attendance declines accelerate. EBITDA falls to $700 million or below. Leverage approaches 7.5x. Covenant compliance becomes threatened. Debt trades at stressed levels. Additional goodwill impairment is recorded. The stock approaches the $12.51 52-week low and potentially breaks below it. At 8.5x on $700M EBITDA, EV = $5.95B, minus $5.1B debt = $850M equity = ~$8.30/share. With distressed multiple compression (7x), equity could approach zero.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: Two near-term catalysts could drive the stock materially lower: (1) Q1 2026 earnings report (expected late May 2026) — Q1 is historically the weakest quarter (seasonal loss), and consensus at -$2.74 EPS may still be too optimistic given the trend of misses; (2) ongoing negative estimate revisions — if the 2026 operating season starts with weather disruptions or attendance shortfalls, estimates could deteriorate further through summer.

**Timing window**: Q1 earnings in late May 2026 and early Q2 attendance data (July–August) provide the nearest catalysts. The thesis should resolve within 3–5 months.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest at 23.9% of float with 11.3 days to cover is very high. This creates meaningful squeeze risk, particularly given Jana Partners' public sale push, which could produce headline-driven rallies at any time. The 16.3 million share spike in volume on October 21, 2025 (stock up 22% in a single session on unspecified news) demonstrates the stock's capacity for violent short squeezes.

**Borrow and options signal**: The 82.2% near-term IV makes put protection expensive. The backwardation structure means near-term puts are priced at a significant premium. The put/call volume ratio of 2.50 indicates active put buying is already occurring — the bearish options trade is becoming crowded. Borrow costs are likely elevated given 23.9% SI.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Base case downside to $14–16 (roughly $14 analyst low target) represents 20–30% from current levels. Upside risk to $26–29 on takeout speculation represents 30–45% against the short. Risk/reward is approximately 0.7:1 to 1.0:1 — unfavorable for an active short.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental thesis is correct — the company faces severe leverage, deteriorating earnings, and uncertain execution. However, the 23.9% short interest with 11.3 days to cover creates unacceptable squeeze risk. Jana Partners' active sale push introduces headline risk that could trigger sharp rallies. The risk/reward ratio is unfavorable for new short positions. The right positioning is to avoid owning the stock.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: A more constructive view would require at least two of the following:
1. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA exceeding $850 million (demonstrating margin recovery)
2. Net leverage declining below 5.5x on a trailing basis
3. Two consecutive quarters of positive like-for-like attendance growth
4. A concrete, publicly announced strategic review with identified bidders

**Timeline**: The headwinds are structural and likely persist through at least Q3 2026 (end of peak season). The consumer spending environment is unlikely to improve meaningfully while inflation exceeds 4% and sentiment remains depressed. Reassess after Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026), which will capture the peak operating season and provide the first real read on whether new management's initiatives are gaining traction.

## Conclusion

Six Flags is a deeply levered consumer discretionary business in active restructuring, with negative free cash flow, deteriorating earnings estimates, management instability driven by multi-activist campaigns, and a macro environment hostile to discretionary spending. The merger has failed to deliver on its value proposition: margins compressed, attendance declined, and $1.52 billion in goodwill was written off.

The takeout optionality via Jana Partners' campaign provides a floor scenario, but the debt load makes acquisition structurally difficult, and the stock's 23.9% short interest already reflects significant bearish positioning.

At $20 per share, the stock offers asymmetric downside: the bear case ($10–14) represents 30–50% downside, while the bull case ($26–29) offers 30–45% upside — but the bull case requires operational execution that has not been demonstrated and a consumer recovery that is unlikely in the current macro environment. No C-suite insider has made open-market purchases.

**Recommendation: AVOID.** The fundamental deterioration, leverage risk, and hostile consumer environment outweigh the takeout speculation. Revisit if and when operational evidence of a turnaround emerges.
