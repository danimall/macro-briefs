**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

S&P Global is one of the highest-quality franchises in financial services: a credit-ratings oligopolist (with Moody's and Fitch), the owner of the S&P 500 and DJIA index brands, and a large multi-asset data/analytics business, generating operating margins that run from ~34% in Market Intelligence to 68% in Ratings and 72% in Indices. On July 1, 2026 the company completed the tax-free spin-off of its Mobility segment (now Mobility Global, NYSE: MBGL), leaving a leaner RemainCo of Ratings, Indices, Market Intelligence, and Energy — roughly 95% high-margin recurring and benchmark businesses. Mobility was the lowest-margin unit (~21%), so the pro-forma company is structurally more profitable and should command a higher multiple.

The stock has declined about 14% from its August 2025 high of $579 to $451 (and touched the low-$390s in February 2026), and consensus EPS estimates have been falling: next-quarter revisions -9.8%, current-year -5.3%, next-year -8.7% over 90 days. Some of this reflects the mechanics of the Mobility separation (estimates being recast) rather than pure demand deterioration, but the direction is negative and coincides with a genuine secular debate. The 10-K devotes unusually extensive new language to AI as a two-sided force: AI-related debt issuance boosted investment-grade Ratings revenue in Q1'26 (billed IG issuance +41%), while the same 10-K warns at length that AI tools could make data collection "relatively inexpensive or free" and let clients and competitors "supplant our products." The central multi-year question for the equity is whether proprietary benchmarks become more valuable as reference/training data or the data moat is commoditized.

A distinctive bullish tell is a cluster of open-market purchases in late April/early May 2026 by three separate top insiders (CEO Martina Cheung ~$998K, S&P DJI CEO Catherine Clay ~$1.08M, and Director Robert Moritz ~$500K), all near $430 and ahead of the value-unlocking spin. That is the strongest form of insider conviction and it argues the sell-off is overdone. On balance we rate SPGI a HOLD (growth-lean), with an upgrade to BUY contingent on the estimate-revision trend turning and the July 28 standalone guidance confirming the RemainCo growth and margin profile.

## Company Overview

Post-spin, S&P Global operates four reportable segments:

- **S&P Global Ratings** — Independent credit ratings, research, and analytics; ~68% operating margin, roughly $1.3B quarterly revenue. Transaction revenue is tied to debt-issuance volume (cyclical with rates and spreads); a growing non-transaction/surveillance base (~45% of Ratings revenue) provides recurring ballast. Includes the Crisil subsidiary.
- **S&P Dow Jones Indices** — Licensing of the S&P 500, DJIA, and thousands of other indices; ~72% operating margin. Revenue is driven by asset-linked fees (66%), exchange-traded derivatives royalties, and data subscriptions. Structured as a JV in which CME Group holds 27% (a ~$4.9B redeemable noncontrolling interest with contingent put features).
- **Market Intelligence** — The largest segment by revenue (~$1.3B/qtr) but lowest core margin (~34% ex-items); Capital IQ Pro, RatingsXpress, RatingsDirect, and enterprise workflow tools. The most contested competitive arena and the one most exposed to the AI-substitution debate.
- **Energy** (formerly Commodity Insights) — Platts benchmarks, price assessments, CERAWeek, and energy/commodity data; ~44% margin.

Revenue is roughly half subscription, giving high recurring visibility ($5.7B remaining performance obligations at Q1'26). The moat rests on entrenched benchmarks (S&P 500, Platts oil assessments), the regulatory barriers protecting the ratings oligopoly, and mission-critical data embedded in client workflows.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 (pre-spin, five segments):**
- Revenue $4,171M, +10% YoY, with growth across all segments. Indices +17% (AUM-driven), Ratings +13% (strong IG issuance, including AI-related and M&A activity), Market Intelligence +8%.
- Operating profit $2,002M (48% margin), but this included a $175M one-time gain on the EDM/thinkFolio disposition. Ex-items, operating profit grew ~12%.
- Net income attributable to SPGI $1,395M; diluted EPS $4.69 vs. $3.54 (+32%), materially flattered by the disposition gain and buybacks. Normalizing, underlying operating growth was closer to ~12%.
- Free cash flow $919M (+13% YoY), with consistently strong conversion.

The headline +32% EPS growth overstates the underlying trajectory; the durable engine is high-single-digit revenue growth converting to low-double-digit operating profit growth, supplemented by share-count reduction.

**Multi-year trajectory:** Net income grew from $3.26B (2021) to $4.82B (2025); adjusted diluted EPS from $13.43 to $18.20, reflecting IHS Markit integration and steady organic compounding.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt $13.3B against total equity $31.3B — modest leverage for a business of this cash generation. The sole credit-facility covenant (debt/cash flow ≤ 4:1) has never been breached. Goodwill of $36.4B plus intangibles of $16.0B totals $52.4B, or 86% of total assets, a legacy of the IHS Markit deal that creates a real, if not imminent, impairment tail if a segment underperforms. Cash was $1.8B; the company is solidly investment-grade. Interest expense is rising (~$96M/qtr, +24% YoY) on the December 2025 senior-note issuance and commercial paper.

**Capital allocation** is aggressive and self-funding: $1.0B of buybacks in Q1'26 alone (ASR at $426.70 avg), a 30M-share (~10%) authorization approved in November 2025, share count down from 307.3M to 297.3M YoY, and a raised dividend ($0.97/qtr) with a 50-plus-year record of increases. Bolt-on M&A (Enertel AI, ORBCOMM AIS, With Intelligence) is FCF-funded, paired with portfolio pruning (EDM/thinkFolio to STG, OSTTRA to CME, Energy geoscience software to SLB). This supports a Financial Health score of 7.5; the balance sheet is a reliable downside filter here, with the only caveat being the goodwill carrying value and rising interest cost.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth picture is solid but the forward signal has weakened. Q1'26 revenue grew 10% with broad-based contribution, and the earnings track record is respectable (three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with a +5.2% beat in Q3'25 offset by a -2.6% miss in Q4'25). Estimate revisions are broadly negative: current quarter -0.8% / -1.1% (30d/90d), next quarter -9.8% / -10.0%, current year -5.3%, and next year -8.7% / -8.1%. Part of this is mechanical, as consensus is being recast to strip out Mobility ahead of the standalone guidance due July 28, but the magnitude of the next-year cut is larger than a clean segment removal would imply and coincides with genuine cyclicality risk in Ratings transaction revenue and the AI overhang on Market Intelligence.

The growth case rests on three legs. First, Indices benefits from a multi-decade passive-investing tailwind; ETF AUM rose 25% YoY to $5.385T (though -2% QoQ on market depreciation, underscoring beta sensitivity of the highest-margin segment). Second, the post-spin RemainCo is structurally higher-margin and more focused, which should support both organic growth mix and multiple. Third, management is actively rotating the portfolio toward AI/high-growth data (Enertel AI power-price forecasting) while shedding commoditized assets. Offsetting these, the estimate-revision velocity is the strongest near-term signal, and it points down. We do not have the accelerating +30% revisions that would flag a buy. Growth Prospects score: 6.0.

## Valuation Assessment

At $451 the stock trades at roughly 28.9x trailing EPS ($14.66) and approximately 24x the current-year consensus of $18.59 (though that consensus is mid-recast; the standalone RemainCo figure will differ). Peer medians in Financial Exchanges & Data are 26.1x P/E: CME 22.4x, ICE 20.2x, NDAQ 26.1x, MCO 31.7x, MSCI 34.0x. SPGI sits roughly in line with the median and at a discount to its closest ratings/index-quality comps (MCO, MSCI), which is defensible given SPGI's larger scale and the post-spin margin uplift.

Two considerations pull in opposite directions. In its favor, the RemainCo is higher-margin than the pre-spin composite, so a forward multiple applied to a cleaner earnings base is arguably conservative; analyst targets (mean $517.6, median $523, high $555, low $444) all sit above the current price, implying ~15% upside to the mean. Against it, the estimate-revision trend is falling; a "cheap-looking" multiple against declining forward estimates is a weaker setup than a stable or rising one, and cheapness against negative revisions has historically been a warning rather than a signal. The valuation is not stretched, but it is not offering clear asymmetry either: the ~22% drawdown from the $579 high reflects a real debate (AI moat erosion, Ratings cyclicality) rather than a pure sentiment overshoot, and the burden of proof for "the market is wrong" is not fully met until standalone guidance is in hand. We score Valuation 6.0 — fair, mildly constructive, but not a clear entry.

## Competitive Landscape

SPGI's moat is wide but its trajectory is the relevant question, and that trajectory is now mixed. In Ratings, the S&P/Moody's/Fitch oligopoly is protected by regulatory barriers and NRSRO status; the moat is stable to improving on the recurring-surveillance base. In Indices, the S&P 500 brand is effectively irreplaceable, and asset-linked fees rose 18% YoY; the moat is stable-to-widening on passive flows, tempered by market-beta sensitivity and competition from MSCI, FTSE Russell, Nasdaq, and CME.

Market Intelligence is where the competitive trajectory is under most pressure. It competes with Bloomberg, LSEG/Refinitiv, FactSet, and Moody's Analytics, and the 10-K explicitly warns that competitors — "many of whom are our clients" (banks, asset managers) — are adding data/analytics capabilities. The AI-substitution risk is concentrated here: off-the-shelf models lower the barrier for new entrants and could let clients replicate insights that today they buy. Energy's Platts benchmarks face Argus and ICIS but retain regulatory-benchmark protection under IOSCO/EU/UK rules.

The competitive-position score has been declining in prior reports (9.0 to 7.5). We move it modestly lower to 6.5, reflecting that the AI-driven contestation of Market Intelligence is a real deterioration of trajectory in one meaningful segment, even as Ratings and Indices remain fortress-quality. This dimension has been an unreliable recent predictor across our work, so we anchor the overall view on growth and financial health rather than lean on competitive position alone.

## Risk Assessment

1. **AI as a double-edged sword — the central secular risk.** The 10-K's expanded language flags that AI could make data collection "relatively inexpensive or free," lower entry barriers via off-the-shelf models, and let third parties "use our data with AI tools to create their own insights and potentially supplant our products." The Q1'26 bidirectional dynamic (AI debt issuance lifting Ratings while AI fears cut software/tech leveraged-loan ratings) shows the effect is live, not hypothetical. This is not fully priced and is the primary reason the risk score is below neutral.
2. **Ratings cyclicality and regulation.** Transaction revenue tracks debt-issuance volume; a rate/spread shock would compress the segment. Heavy regulation (Dodd-Frank, SEC NRSRO, ESMA, FCA) plus a still-open Australian CDO-ratings class action are structural.
3. **CME redemption feature.** CME can require SPGI to repurchase at least 20% of its 27% Indices stake at fair value at any time, and 100% on a change of control — a contingent call on capital and an underappreciated structural feature.
4. **Goodwill/intangible impairment.** The $52.4B carrying value creates a non-trivial impairment tail if Market Intelligence or Energy underperform.
5. **Spin-execution and guidance uncertainty.** With standalone guidance not yet issued, the pro-forma growth/margin profile is not fully confirmed; the falling next-year estimates partly reflect this transition. AWS concentration is a named operational dependency, and telegraphed executive turnover (CLO retirement end-2026) is orderly but worth monitoring.

Risk Factors score: 5.5 — the AI-substitution overhang and the contingent CME liability are specific, material, and not obviously priced, offsetting the otherwise clean, oligopolistic risk profile.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 27.7% sits just below one-year historical volatility of 29.6% (a -1.8pp spread, effectively fair), with IV at the 1st percentile of the current chain; the market is not pricing acute near-term stress into the front week. The put/call skew is elevated at 14.8% in the one-week tenor (some downside hedging), but the put/call OI ratio has fallen sharply from 1.81 to 0.90 as the stock rallied ~12% off its June lows, indicating positioning has turned less bearish. Term structure is in normal contango (near 27.7% < long 30.0%). The one-month IV spikes to 43.4% around the July 31 window, consistent with the July 28 earnings/standalone-guidance event being the key near-term catalyst. The options market is complacent near-term but flags the earnings print as the binary. Short interest is negligible (1.2% of float, 1.4 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$575).** The post-spin RemainCo re-rates toward its highest-quality peers as standalone guidance confirms a cleaner, faster-growing, higher-margin profile. Passive-flow AUM growth and IG-issuance strength sustain double-digit operating profit growth; self-funded buybacks compound EPS; and the proprietary-benchmark/reference-data thesis wins the AI debate, with SPGI's data becoming more valuable, not less. The insider cluster at $430 proves prescient.

**Bear Case (~$360).** AI-driven substitution accelerates in Market Intelligence — clients and competitors replicate insights, pricing power erodes, and the data moat compresses faster than the market expects. A rate/spread shock knocks Ratings transaction revenue; the falling next-year estimate revision proves to be the leading edge of a demand slowdown rather than a spin artifact. Goodwill impairment surfaces, and the multiple derates toward the low end of the peer group on decelerating growth.

The base case (~$510) sits between these: ~26x a normalized standalone EPS in the ~$19.5-20 range post-spin, consistent with the analyst mean of $517.6 and the peer-median multiple applied to a higher-margin RemainCo.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-lean). Timeframe: 12 months, anchored to the July 28 standalone guidance and subsequent quarterly prints.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Next-quarter and next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (or the standalone recast shows organic growth ≥8% with margins above the pre-spin composite) at or after the July 28 print.
- Standalone guidance confirms RemainCo operating margin expansion and Ratings recurring-base resilience, with Market Intelligence organic growth holding ≥6% despite AI competition.
- A pullback toward the low-$400s (the insiders' ~$430 entry or below) with fundamentals intact, restoring clearer asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence that AI substitution is materially cutting Market Intelligence pricing/retention (segment organic growth turning negative, or explicit management commentary on client cannibalization).
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or a rate/spread shock that pushes Ratings transaction revenue down double digits.
- Continued estimate-revision deterioration beyond what the Mobility recast explains (next-year consensus falling below ~$19 on standalone basis).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 earnings and standalone guidance on ~July 28, 2026.

At $451, upside to the base case (~$510) is ~13%, to the bull case (~$575) is ~28%, and downside to the bear case (~$360) is ~20%. The risk/reward is roughly balanced-to-favorable but not asymmetric enough for a BUY ahead of guidance confirmation. The insider-buying cluster and fortress balance sheet keep this a conviction growth-hold; the falling estimate revisions and unpriced AI overhang keep it short of BUY.

## Conclusion

S&P Global remains a wide-moat compounder with a fortress balance sheet, self-funded capital return, and a newly cleaner, higher-margin post-spin structure. The insider-buying cluster near $430 by the CEO, the Indices CEO, and a director is a genuine conviction signal that argues the pullback from the high is overdone. Weighing against that are falling consensus estimates and a specific, credible AI-substitution threat to the Market Intelligence data moat that the market has not fully priced. Until the July 28 standalone guidance confirms the RemainCo growth and margin trajectory and estimate revisions stabilize, the appropriate posture is HOLD (growth-lean), with a path to a BUY upgrade on a revision turn or a re-entry into the low-$400s. Overall score: 6.5.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the drawdown from the $579 high is ~22%, not ~14% (the ~14% figure is the vendor's period return, not the peak-to-current decline), which somewhat overstates how "overdone" the sell-off is; and the low-$390s were touched in February 2026, not June — neither changes the thesis mechanics but both bear on the "overdone pullback" framing supporting the HOLD (growth-lean) stance.
