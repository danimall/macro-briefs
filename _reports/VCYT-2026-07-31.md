**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Veracyte reported Q2 2026 on July 30: total revenue $150.3M (+15% y/y), testing revenue $145.7M (+19%), GAAP net income $25.5M versus a $1.0M loss a year earlier, adjusted EBITDA $44.0M at a 29.2% margin, and raised full-year guidance to $590–596M total / $576–582M testing revenue. Non-GAAP diluted EPS of $0.54 beat the non-GAAP consensus of $0.43 by 25% — the fourth consecutive beat. The company also launched two new products in the quarter (Prosigna as a US laboratory-developed test, and TrueMRD in muscle-invasive bladder cancer) and secured Medicare coverage for TrueMRD at launch, which is unusual timing in molecular diagnostics.

The stock closed at $59.78 on July 30 and trades at $46.28, a decline of roughly 23% in the following session. I could not identify a disclosed cause. That gap is the central analytical problem in this report. A 23% single-session move the day after guidance is raised is not consistent with the reported quarter; it is consistent with a forward-looking item surfaced on the call or in the 10-Q. The two candidates that fit both the magnitude and the calendar are (a) commentary on the CY2027 PAMA reimbursement reset — the first triennial reporting window for CPT 81546 (Afirma), 81542 (Decipher Prostate) and 0016U (Decipher Bladder) closed July 31, 2026, with new rates effective January 1, 2027 — and (b) the implied second-half margin path embedded in a reaffirmed ">26%" adjusted EBITDA guide against a 29.2% Q2 print and an 81% year-over-year R&D increase. Both are inferences, not established facts, and I flag them as such.

Scores have improved across four prior reviews (5.8 → 6.8 → 6.6 → 6.5), driven mainly by financial health as the company crossed into durable GAAP profitability and cash generation. That trend is confirmed, not reversed, by Q2: operating cash flow of $81.0M in 1H26 versus $39.0M in 1H25, cash and short-term investments of $485.2M against total liabilities of $98.0M and no debt. Risk/reward at $46.28 is better than at $59.78 on the same disclosed fundamentals. I am nonetheless leaving this at HOLD for a fifth time, with the composition of the hold changed: the valuation objection has largely been removed by the decline, and what remains is a dated binary (preliminary CY2027 CLFS rates, ordinarily published by CMS in late September) plus an unexplained 23% repricing. Buying into both without the transcript or the 10-Q would be underwriting an information gap rather than a thesis.

## Company Overview

Veracyte sells genomic classifier tests for cancer diagnosis and prognosis, run predominantly as CLIA laboratory-developed tests in its own laboratories (South San Francisco for thyroid, San Diego for urologic, Austin for sample intake), with a smaller international in-vitro diagnostic kit business. It reports one operating segment and one goodwill reporting unit.

Two products are effectively the company. In Q2 2026, Decipher (Prostate plus Bladder) generated $91.9M and Afirma $51.2M — $143.1M of $145.7M in testing revenue, or 98%. Decipher Prostate is described in the FY2025 10-K as the only gene-expression test included in the 2026 NCCN prostate cancer guidelines, supported by Simon Level IB evidence, >115 peer-reviewed studies, and coverage reaching payers representing more than 215 million enrollees. Afirma addresses indeterminate thyroid nodules — roughly 650,000 US fine-needle aspirations annually — with reported 97% sensitivity / 99% negative predictive value from a 13-study meta-analysis and coverage above 275 million enrollees. Medicare Clinical Laboratory Fee Schedule rates are $3,873 for Decipher Prostate, $3,600 for Afirma (unchanged since 2018), and $3,469.83 for Decipher Bladder.

Newer franchises are pre-revenue by management's own statement: the 10-K says Prosigna LDT and TrueMRD are not expected to contribute meaningfully to 2026 revenue, and the raised testing guidance explicitly excludes them. A structural detail worth holding: Afirma cytopathology is performed by Thyroid Cytopathology Partners, a Texas professional association paid a monthly per-FNA fee, disclosed as a sole-source service dependency.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year record is a clean inflection. Revenue went $296.5M (FY2022) → $361.1M → $445.8M → $517.1M (FY2025), a 20.4% three-year CAGR. Operating income moved from a $41.1M loss to $57.8M positive; net income from a $36.6M loss to $66.4M. Operating cash flow nearly doubled in FY2025 to $136.3M against $9.7M of capex, producing $126.6M of free cash flow — a 24.5% FCF margin on $517.1M of revenue, which is atypical for a diagnostics company at this scale.

Three adjustments matter for earnings quality, and they run in a less negative direction than a screen would suggest. FY2025 general and administrative expense contained a **negative $15.294M** contingent-consideration revaluation (a credit) versus a $2.167M charge in FY2024 — a $17.5M favorable swing. Against that, FY2025 absorbed a **$20.5M non-cash impairment** and **$11.9M of incremental professional fees** tied to the French subsidiary wind-down. Normalizing out the credit and adding back the impairment gives roughly **$63.0M** of operating income, slightly better than the $57.8M reported. The downward contingent-consideration mark does imply lower expected probability or later timing on C2i/nCounter milestones; that is a fair-value estimate about milestone timing and should not be read as a statement about program merit or management intent.

Second, the tax line is not economically normal. FY2025 income tax provision was ~$1.8M on $68.2M of pre-tax income because a valuation allowance still sits against deferred tax assets. Federal NOLs were $561.9M at year-end 2025. The 10-K flags that sufficient positive evidence may emerge within twelve months to release part of the allowance, producing a one-time book benefit. That is an accounting event and should not be capitalized as recurring earnings power. The economically relevant point runs the other way: cash taxes will rise from near zero over time, which is why consensus 2027 EPS of $1.89 sits only 5% above 2026's $1.80 despite mid-teens revenue growth.

Third, R&D stepped up hard. GAAP R&D was $29.4M in Q2 2026 versus $16.3M (+81%), and 1H26 R&D of $56.5M is already ~80% of full-year FY2025 R&D. The most plausible explanation is funding the Prosigna and TrueMRD launches plus evidence generation (OPTIMA, ENZAMET). Adjusted EBITDA margin still expanded to 29.2%, so the step-up is being absorbed — but 2027 models should not assume reversion, and I cannot resolve durability without the call transcript.

Gross margin improvement (69.0% → 72.2% GAAP in Q2) is partly mechanical rather than pure operating leverage. Cost of biopharmaceutical revenue fell from $3.572M to $0.207M as that revenue fell from $4.303M to $0.802M following the deconsolidation of Veracyte SAS (the former HalioDx business, placed into Marseille commercial court proceedings in Q2 2025, with the contract-manufacturing portion sold to Helio Diagnostics SAS effective August 1, 2025). A roughly break-even-to-negative-margin line was removed. That also explains the gap between guided total revenue growth (14–15%) and testing revenue growth (17–18%) — arithmetic drag from an exited business, not deterioration in the core.

The balance sheet is the strongest score input. At 6/30/26: total assets $1,486.4M, total liabilities $98.0M (essentially operating leases), equity $1,388.4M, cash and short-term investments $485.2M, no debt disclosed. Current ratio ~8.2x; Altman Z computed off the FY2025 filing is ~10.3. The one item that requires monitoring is **goodwill of $767.2M — 52% of total assets and 55% of equity — carried in a single reporting unit**, meaning the whole balance is tested against enterprise fair value with no internal diversification. No impairment was recorded in 2023, 2024 or 2025, and at a ~$3.2B enterprise value the cushion is wide. There is no dividend and no disclosed buyback; cash is accumulating at roughly $80M per half-year, which is mild capital inefficiency offset by the option value of self-funding.

On cash flow, I am deliberately not annualizing 1H26's $81.0M of operating cash flow. Diagnostics collections are lumpy, receivables rose $9.7M in 1H26, and cash taxes are artificially low. As a labelled sensitivity: doubling gives $162M; growing FY2025's actual $136.3M with guided revenue growth gives roughly $156M. The disclosed facts are $136.3M (FY2025) and $81.0M (1H26).

## Growth Analysis

Growth is volume-led, which is the more durable form in this industry and also the reason there is no pricing cushion.

FY2025 blended testing revenue per test was $2,906 versus $2,932 in FY2024 — approximately **–0.9% blended ASP** against 19% volume growth. At the product level in Q2 2026, implied Decipher ASP was ~$3,094 (+2.6% y/y) and Afirma ~$2,753 (+7.3%). These are derived from disclosed revenue against "approximately" volume figures, with Decipher aggregating Prostate and Bladder, so treat them as ±2–3% estimates. The Afirma step-up could be single-quarter payer mix rather than a structural rate gain; one quarter does not separate the two.

Volume itself decelerated modestly: total diagnostic volume +13% in Q2 (50,967 tests) versus +19% for FY2025. Decipher volume +17%, Afirma +10%. Afirma growing 10% in a roughly fixed ~650,000-FNA US market implies continued share or utilization gain, most likely driven by the July 2024 MolDX local coverage determination for thyroid nodule risk stratification.

Guidance was raised on both lines, and the testing range excludes the two newly launched products — a structurally conservative construction from a management team that has beaten four consecutive quarters. Against that, the reaffirmed ">26%" adjusted EBITDA margin implies a meaningful second-half step-down. Using Q2's $44.0M and assuming a comparable Q1, 1H adjusted EBITDA against a full-year floor of ~$154M leaves the second half implying lower margins than the 29.2% Q2 print on roughly $300M of revenue. That is a derived estimate resting on an assumed Q1 figure I do not have, and ">26%" is a floor rather than a point estimate. But it is consistent with the consensus pattern: current-quarter EPS estimates are up only 0.9% over 30 days, next-quarter estimates are down 3.0% over 90 days, and 2027 consensus EPS growth is only +5%.

Estimate revisions are two-speed. Current-year consensus rose from $1.65 to $1.80 over 90 days (+9.4%) and next year from $1.77 to $1.89 (+6.4%) — solidly positive. Over 30 days both drifted down slightly (–0.9%, –0.7%). This is not the >30% revision velocity that flags a mispriced inflection; it is a business whose 2026 numbers were marked up during the spring and have since flattened.

Longer-dated growth optionality sits in three places: Decipher's expansion into metastatic/castration-resistant prostate cancer (Medicare coverage effective retroactive to October 2024), Prosigna's US entry into a ~225,000-patient HR+ breast market, and TrueMRD in MIBC with Medicare coverage secured at launch. None is in the 2026 guide.

But on earnings growth the stock screens poorly — 25.7x forward for +5% consensus EPS growth into 2027 is a PEG well above 2, because rising cash taxes and the R&D step-up absorb revenue growth. I am not treating consensus targets as a floor here.

Score: 5.8. Moderately attractive after the drawdown, with a specific negative-skewed catalyst (PAMA) sitting directly on top of the 2027 numbers that support the multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is mixed by franchise, which is why this dimension sits near 6 rather than 7.

**Prostate (~61% of Q2 testing revenue) — improving but with a new-modality threat.** Decipher grew revenue 20% on 17% volume growth and holds a stated exclusivity as the only gene-expression test in the 2026 NCCN prostate cancer guidelines. The 10-K itself names a threat that sits outside that category: "companies that combine traditional pathology methods and new technologies such as AI and digital pathology, such as the ArteraAI Prostate Test." A digital-pathology test can in principle be added to guidelines without displacing a gene-expression exclusivity. The item to monitor is the addition of non-genomic prognostic tools to NCCN prostate, not the removal of Decipher. Myriad and MDxHealth remain direct genomic competitors.

**Thyroid — improving.** 18% revenue and 10% volume growth in a fixed-procedure market implies share and utilization gains against Interpace and ThyroSeq (Sonic Healthcare), with expanded MolDX coverage the likely driver. This is the closest thing Veracyte has to monopoly economics.

**Breast — entering from far behind.** Veracyte's own 10-K states Exact Sciences "currently commands a substantial majority of the market." Prosigna's US LDT launch is supported by new OPTIMA predictive evidence, but this is a challenger entry into an installed base.

**MRD in MIBC — the most consequential open question.** The 10-K names Natera as primary competition. External mid-2026 reporting indicates Natera's Signatera achieved FDA companion-diagnostic authorization with atezolizumab (referencing IMvigor011), NCCN recognition in a bladder guideline update, EU IVDR Class C certification, and Japanese PMDA approval. Critically, the NCCN item traces to a Natera press release that could not be retrieved, and the CDx claim traces to an interview with a Natera executive; neither is verified against a primary FDA or NCCN source. Whether NCCN recommends ctDNA-MRD as a *modality* (a tailwind TrueMRD can ride) or names a specific technology (a durable barrier) changes the conclusion in opposite directions. Veracyte's offsets are real and company-disclosed: Medicare coverage for TrueMRD at launch, an existing urology call point through Decipher Prostate and Bladder, and no direct genomic competitor cited in the Decipher Bladder prognostic indication.

**Payer position — deteriorating.** Medicare rose 31% → 31% → 33% of revenue across FY2023–25 and UnitedHealthcare 11% → 13% → 14%; combined 42% → 44% → 47%. Product-line diversification (Prosigna, TrueMRD) does not diversify payer concentration, and the two should not be conflated. The company also concedes it is out-of-network with some commercial payers and that such claims "are often denied upon submission."

## Risk Assessment

**1. PAMA reset of Afirma and Decipher rates (highest-impact, dated).** The FY2025 10-K discloses that the first triennial PAMA reporting period for CPT 81546, 81542 and 0016U was expected May 1 – July 31, 2026, using January–June 2025 data, with rates potentially resetting January 1, 2027 through December 31, 2029 at the volume-weighted median of private commercial payer payments. The current rates were set by gapfill ($3,873; $3,469.83) or frozen since 2018 ($3,600) — they were never benchmarked to private medians. Medicare was 33% of FY2025 revenue (~$171M). A 10% CLFS reduction is ~$17M (3.3% of revenue); 20% is ~$34M (6.6%) — near-100% incremental margin dollars, so the EBITDA effect is proportionally larger. Some commercial contracts index to Medicare, which would amplify. These are sensitivities constructed from disclosed concentration, not forecasts; the same 10-K states management "believes that PAMA and its implementing regulations are generally favorable to us," and the filing notes Congress revised the PAMA review cycle in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026. Only the timing and mechanism are established. Preliminary CY2027 rates are ordinarily published in late September.

**2. The unexplained 23% decline.** The stock went from $59.78 (July 30 close, up 7.3% on the day of the release) to $46.28. I found no disclosed cause. Given that the earnings release itself was a raise, the residual explanations are call commentary, the 10-Q, or a third-party competitive/reimbursement development. A third-party summary of the call describes more "launch-execution and workflow scrutiny (EMR integration, reimbursement pathing)" and "more explicit volume sensitivity discussion in Decipher low-risk" — that source could not be opened in full and is an unverified secondary characterization, though Q2 Decipher volume of +17% does not on its face indicate a volume problem.

**3. Goodwill concentration.** $767.2M in a single reporting unit, 52% of assets. Protected at a $3.2B enterprise value; not diversified within the test.

**4. LDT regulatory tail.** The FDA's May 2024 LDT rule was vacated March 31, 2025, but FDA retains enforcement authority and legislative revival would apply premarket-review costs to essentially 100% of testing revenue. Low probability, high severity.

**5. Undisclosed transaction activity.** 1H26 non-GAAP adjustments include $3.0M of "expenses related to the assessment of licensing and strategic investments," and the cash flow statement shows a new $1.6M "issuance of loan receivable." No 8-K has been filed and no counterparty disclosed. Against $767M of existing goodwill and a compensation plan keyed to revenue and margin with no capital-efficiency or ROIC measure, an acquisition is the most plausible route to a negative surprise from an otherwise clean balance sheet. It could equally be evaluated-and-abandoned opportunities or a routine platform license.

**6. New legal expense.** The FY2025 10-K Item 3 states no material legal proceedings; 1H26 added back $1.7M of "expenses related to legal proceedings." The nature and counterparty are unverified. Candidates range from IP disputes in liquid biopsy (which the 10-K flags as a litigious area, and which is precisely the technology launched in Q2) to California PAGA employment exposure, which the filing calls out specifically.

**7. Operational and supply concentration.** Afirma genomic testing occurs only in South San Francisco (seismic/wildfire exposure); urologic testing only in San Diego. Prosigna depends on nCounter components from Bruker Spatial Biology (the former NanoString, acquired out of a distressed process). The MRD platform's technical team and assets are in Israel.

**8. Section 382.** An ownership-change event could limit use of the $561.9M federal NOL.

ATM IV collapsed from 81.1% (July 19) to 50.4% (July 31) as the earnings event passed, and near-term IV of 50.4% now sits below one-year realized volatility of 58.1% (spread –7.7%), at the 13th percentile within the current chain. The market is pricing materially more uncertainty into December than into the next two months, which is consistent with the CY2027 reimbursement calendar and the Q3 print rather than with anything imminent. Short interest at 9.9% of float and 4.8 days to cover is moderate and not crowded. For an investor who ultimately wants to own this, cheap near-dated vol against rich six-month vol argues for expressing entry through options rather than paying for December optionality.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $68 (+47%)

Two guideline-anchored franchises compound on volume in markets where coverage keeps broadening: Decipher at +20% revenue with NCCN exclusivity among gene-expression tests and a new metastatic indication; Afirma at +18% in a fixed-procedure market, implying share gain. PAMA lands neutral or favorable because commercial in-network rates negotiated since 2021 sit at or above the gapfill rates, removing the largest overhang and re-rating the terminal value. Prosigna and TrueMRD — both excluded from 2026 guidance, with TrueMRD already Medicare-covered at launch — begin contributing in 2027, taking revenue toward $690M with adjusted EBITDA near $200M. At 23–25x EV/adjusted EBITDA plus ~$650M of accumulated net cash, that supports $65–70. A partial valuation-allowance release adds a one-time book benefit and clarifies the tax path. Four consecutive beats and a guidance construction that excludes new products argue management is setting a floor, not a ceiling.

### Bear Case — $31 (–33%)

PAMA resets Afirma and Decipher toward private-payer medians below administratively-set rates, phasing 15–20% reductions from January 2027 into 33% of revenue at near-100% incremental margin, with commercial contracts that index to Medicare amplifying the hit. 2027 revenue stalls near $620M and adjusted EBITDA margin compresses toward 22% as the R&D step-up proves permanent, giving ~$136M. At 15x EV/EBITDA plus net cash, that is roughly $31. Payer concentration keeps worsening (Medicare + UnitedHealthcare at 47% and rising), ArteraAI-style digital pathology enters NCCN prostate outside Decipher's category exclusivity, Prosigna fails against Exact Sciences' installed base, TrueMRD is gated by a competitor's companion-diagnostic label, and management deploys the $485M cash pile into an acquisition under a comp plan that rewards revenue growth without any capital-efficiency measure — adding to $767M of goodwill in a single reporting unit.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Own it if you own it; do not add before the two open items resolve.

**Base case: $51 (+10% from $46.28).** 2027 revenue of ~$664M (+12% off the $593M 2026 midpoint, assuming PAMA is modestly negative and offset by volume), adjusted EBITDA at 27% = $179M, valued at 20x EV/adjusted EBITDA = $3.58B enterprise value, plus ~$600M of net cash at end-2027 (allowing a buffer for M&A), divided by ~83M diluted shares. Cross-check: 24x on 2027 non-GAAP EPS of roughly $2.10 gives $50.

**Bull case: $68 (+47%).** As above — 23–25x EV/adjusted EBITDA of ~$200M with PAMA neutral and new products contributing.

**Bear case: $31 (–33%).** 15x on ~$136M of 2027 adjusted EBITDA after a phased 15–20% CLFS reduction and permanent R&D step-up.

**Timeframe:** 6–12 months, gated on two specific dates — CMS publication of preliminary CY2027 CLFS rates (ordinarily late September 2026) and the Q3 2026 print (early November 2026).

**Upgrade triggers (any two would move this to BUY):**
1. Preliminary CY2027 CLFS rates for 81546 / 81542 / 0016U flat-to-higher, or an aggregate implied revenue impact under 3%.
2. Second-half 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin of 28% or better, demonstrating the 81% R&D increase is absorbed rather than structural.
3. Clustered open-market insider purchases at or near current levels — the July 31 decline creates a clean test of conviction that the March–June sell-and-withhold pattern did not provide.
4. Disclosed volume or revenue traction for TrueMRD and Prosigna in the Q3 or Q4 releases, converting excluded optionality into a modelable line.

**Downgrade triggers (any one would move this to AVOID):**
1. Preliminary PAMA rates implying reductions above 15% on Afirma or Decipher Prostate.
2. Testing revenue growth guidance cut below 15%, or Decipher volume growth decelerating below 10%.
3. An acquisition above $500M, particularly equity-funded, given the existing $767M goodwill balance and a comp plan with no capital-efficiency metric.
4. A coverage-policy change at UnitedHealthcare or a MolDX retrenchment on thyroid or metastatic prostate.
5. 30-day consensus EPS revisions turning down more than 5%, which would confirm that the July 31 decline reflected a fundamental disclosure rather than positioning.

## Conclusion

Veracyte is a better business than its five-year share performance suggests — the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows cumulative TSR indexed to 86.02 at FY2025 against a peer group at 124.75, over a period in which revenue went from $219.5M to $517.1M and net income from a $75.6M loss to $66.4M of profit. Some of that gap is the mechanical effect of a 2020 measurement date at the peak of the diagnostics valuation cycle. Some of it is the market assigning a lower terminal value to LDT-based Medicare revenue streams than the current P&L implies, which is a defensible position given that 33% of revenue is priced off administratively-set rates about to be benchmarked to private-payer medians for the first time.

At $46.28, the stock is 24% below its 52-week high, trades at 5.4x EV on guided 2026 revenue and roughly 21x EV/adjusted EBITDA, carries $485M of cash and no debt, and generates a 24.5% free-cash-flow margin. That is a reasonable price for a franchise growing testing revenue 17–18% on volume with guideline-anchored coverage. The decline improves risk/reward relative to the prior review, and I have not lowered financial health, growth or competitive scores on account of price action.

What prevents an upgrade is the combination of an unexplained 23% single-session repricing the day after a raise, and a dated reimbursement reset whose direction the company's own filing declines to predict — the 10-K contains both a specific warning and a statement that PAMA is "generally favorable to us," which is itself informative. Risk factors move down to 4.6 not because the stock fell but because the calendar advanced into the reset window and because a material unknown now sits between me and the tape. Preliminary CY2027 rates and the Q3 print resolve most of this within roughly 100 days. Until then, hold.
