## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is a pure-play Delaware Basin E&P company trading at ~8.7x FY2026E earnings ($6.77), with production scaling to record levels (207,070 BOE/d in 2025, +21% YoY) and estimates surging upward — current-year EPS revisions rose 51% in 30 days and 47.5% over 90 days, reflecting the sharp improvement in oil pricing. The company carries meaningful leverage ($3.4B total debt, 2.07x D/E), but this is partially offset by non-recourse San Mateo midstream debt ($883M), $1.8B of undrawn revolver capacity, a Fitch upgrade to BB, and $883M of free cash flow generated in 2025 despite realized oil prices averaging just $64.99/Bbl.

The investment case centers on three elements: (1) a deeply discounted valuation relative to the current oil price environment (~$102 WTI), where Matador's unhedged 2H 2026 production (only ~50% of 2026 is hedged) captures significant upside; (2) the Hugh Brinson Pipeline coming online Q3-Q4 2026, which resolves the Waha gas pricing problem and management quantifies at ~$90M incremental annual revenue per $0.50/MMBtu improvement; and (3) an insider buying pattern that is among the most bullish in U.S. E&P — 78 open-market purchases with zero sales, including C-suite buying as recently as March 2026. At current oil prices, this stock should be trading materially higher.

The principal risks are commodity price reversal (a de-escalation of the Iran conflict would pull oil back toward $80-90, compressing the EPS estimate surge), the hedge book capping upside on 50% of 2026 oil production (collars with ~$66 ceilings), and balance sheet leverage that leaves limited margin for error in a downturn. At $58.77, the stock prices in roughly $70-75 WTI — a substantial discount to the current $102 spot price and a reasonable margin of safety even in a de-escalation scenario.

## Company Overview

Matador Resources, founded in 2003 by Joseph Wm. Foran, is an independent E&P company focused exclusively on the Delaware Basin (western Permian Basin) in Southeast New Mexico and West Texas. The company holds approximately 354,600 gross (212,500 net) acres targeting the Wolfcamp and Bone Spring formations. With 5,568 gross (1,852 net) identified drilling locations and a current pace of ~130 net wells/year, Matador has roughly 14 years of drilling inventory.

The company operates a vertically integrated model through its 51% ownership in San Mateo Midstream (Five Point Energy holds 49%), which provides natural gas processing (720 MMcf/d capacity), oil gathering, and produced water disposal. This midstream integration provides flow assurance, incremental revenue, and operational control that most mid-cap E&Ps lack.

Matador completed its portfolio simplification in Q1 2025 by divesting its remaining Eagle Ford assets ($22.2M). The company now derives ~99% of proved reserves from the Delaware Basin, making it a pure-play on the highest-quality U.S. shale acreage.

Key metrics: 483 employees, 58% oil / 42% gas production mix (by BOE), ~$7.3B market cap, NYSE-listed.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

2025 revenues of $3.66B grew just 3% YoY despite 21% production growth, because realized oil prices fell 14% ($64.99 vs. $75.89/Bbl) and gas prices declined 13% ($2.08 vs. $2.38/Mcf). Net income declined 14% to $759M ($6.09/share diluted). Adjusted EBITDA held flat at $2.295B, demonstrating that production growth offset commodity headwinds, but only barely.

Operating margin of 33.5% and net margin of 23.5% are strong for a mid-cap E&P. ROE of 15.2% reflects reasonable capital efficiency, though ROA of 7.4% is dragged down by the asset-heavy nature of the business.

The critical financial dynamic going forward is oil price leverage. At $102 WTI (current spot), unhedged barrels generate roughly $37/Bbl more than the 2025 average realization. On Matador's ~120,000 Bbl/d oil production, the incremental revenue from current prices versus 2025 realizations is approximately $4.4M/day or $1.6B annualized. This explains the estimate surge: FY2026E EPS of $6.77 (up 51% in 30 days) and FY2027E of $8.00 (up 26%).

However, approximately 50% of 2026 oil production is hedged with costless collars featuring ~$53 floors and ~$66 ceilings. This means half the production cannot benefit from oil above $66. The unhedged second half of production captures the full commodity upside.

### Cash Flow Generation

Operating cash flow reached $2.43B in 2025 (up 8% YoY), while capex of $1.54B produced free cash flow of $883M (24.1% FCF margin). This FCF funded dividend payments (~$172M at the raised $1.50/share rate), $55.8M in buybacks, and debt reduction.

2026 guidance calls for $1.45-1.55B total capex, approximately flat with 2025 but with D/C/E capex down ~11%. Management guides for ~3% oil production growth on reduced capital, reflecting improving capital efficiency, with well cycle times shortened 13% and D&C costs guided down 6% to $785-805/lateral foot.

At $102 oil and current cost structure, 2026 operating cash flow could approach $3.5-4.0B, producing free cash flow of $2.0-2.5B ($16-20/share). This would represent a 27-34% FCF yield at current prices.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is the primary risk factor. Total debt of approximately $3.43B includes $2.15B in senior notes (well-laddered: $500M at 6.875% due 2028, $900M at 6.50% due 2032, $750M at 6.25% due 2033), $398M drawn on the revolving credit facility, and $883M on the San Mateo credit facility (non-recourse to Matador).

Debt-to-equity stands at 2.07x. Cash on hand is just $15M, meaning the company operates with essentially no cash buffer and relies on its $1.8B undrawn revolver for liquidity. The current ratio of 0.79 (below 1.0) reflects this aggressive working capital posture.

Mitigating factors: (1) the San Mateo debt is non-recourse; (2) the revolver provides ample liquidity; (3) debt covenants (current ratio ≥1.0 on adjusted basis, Debt/EBITDA ≤3.50x) are comfortably met; (4) Fitch upgraded the credit to BB from BB- in 2025; (5) annual interest expense of ~$237M is well-covered by $2.4B+ OCF. At current oil prices, Net Debt/EBITDA is likely in the 1.0-1.2x range.

The 8-K filed March 5, 2026 (material agreement / new financial obligation) suggests recent capital markets activity, likely refinancing or addressing the 2028 maturity.

### Earnings Quality

The Piotroski score of 4/5 indicates strong earnings quality: positive net income, positive ROA, positive operating cash flow, and OCF exceeding net income. FCF conversion is healthy — $883M FCF on $759M net income (116% conversion ratio), indicating that reported earnings are backed by cash generation.

Lease operating expenses per BOE increased from $4.83 (2023) to $5.50 (2025), a 14% rise that bears monitoring for signs of inflation passthrough. DD&A at $15.82/BOE reflects the higher asset base post-Ameredev acquisition.

## Growth Analysis

### Production Trajectory

Matador's production growth has been exceptional: from approximately 38,000 BOE/d in 2022 to 207,070 BOE/d in 2025. The 2024 Ameredev Acquisition drove much of the step-change, but organic development continues at pace.

2026 guidance targets ~3% oil production growth on ~11% less capital, reflecting the transition from acquisition-driven hypergrowth to disciplined organic development. This is the correct strategic pivot for a company with this leverage profile, and should be viewed positively.

### Estimate Momentum

The estimate revision data is among the strongest in mid-cap E&P:

- Current quarter EPS: $1.33 (up 51.3% in 30 days, up 30.9% in 90 days)
- Next quarter EPS: $1.94 (up 84.3% in 30 days, up 93.4% in 90 days)
- FY2026 EPS: $6.77 (up 50.8% in 30 days, up 47.5% in 90 days)
- FY2027 EPS: $8.00 (up 25.9% in 30 days, up 28.0% in 90 days)

All 17-19 covering analysts are revising upward. 30-day revisions exceed 90-day revisions for the current and next quarters, meaning estimates are being raised at an increasing pace. This is a direct function of the oil price surge driven by the Hormuz blockade and dual supply compression from Iranian and Russian waiver non-renewals.

### Specific Catalysts

1. **Hugh Brinson Pipeline (Q3-Q4 2026):** 500,000 MMBtu/day of capacity connecting Permian gas to Gulf Coast markets. Management quantifies the revenue sensitivity at ~$90M annually per $0.50/MMBtu improvement in basis differentials. The Waha-Henry Hub basis averaged ($2.96)/MMBtu in 2025, with spot as wide as ($5.11). If the pipeline narrows this by $2-3, the incremental annual revenue is $360-540M (pre-tax).

2. **Unhedged 2H 2026 oil exposure:** With only ~50% of 2026 production hedged and current oil at $102, each $10/Bbl above the hedge ceiling on unhedged barrels generates approximately $220M of incremental annual revenue.

3. **2026 production beat potential:** Management has beaten production guidance in 4 consecutive quarters. Q4 2025 came in 2% above guidance midpoint. The consistent beat pattern on a 4-for-4 EPS surprise record (averaging +6.8% surprise) builds confidence that FY2026E of $6.77 may prove conservative.

4. **Ongoing deleveraging:** At current oil prices, FCF of $2.0-2.5B allows rapid debt reduction, accelerated buybacks, or further dividend increases.

### Drilling Inventory Depth

14 years of identified drilling locations at current pace, with 76% expected to be high-value 2-mile+ laterals. This provides long-term visibility and eliminates near-term concerns about inventory exhaustion that affect some shorter-reserve E&Ps.

## Competitive Landscape

Matador operates in the most competitive oil basin in North America. At $7.3B market cap, it is a mid-cap competing against significantly larger operators (Diamondback, Devon, ConocoPhillips) for acreage, rigs, and services.

Competitive advantages:
1. **San Mateo Midstream integration** — provides operational control, flow assurance, and incremental revenue that most mid-cap E&Ps lack
2. **Delaware Basin concentration** — pure-play focus on the highest-quality U.S. shale play
3. **Operational efficiency** — 483 employees for $3.7B revenue; 13% improvement in well cycle times; D&C costs declining
4. **14-year drilling inventory** — eliminates pressure to make dilutive acquisitions for inventory replacement
5. **Multi-zone development capability** — multiple stacked pay zones (Wolfcamp A-D, Bone Spring, Brushy Canyon, Avalon) across the acreage position

Competitive disadvantages:
1. **Scale** — significantly smaller than Permian majors/super-independents, limiting bargaining power on service costs
2. **Customer concentration** — three purchasers at 72% of revenue
3. **Federal land exposure** — 33% of acreage subject to BLM permitting, creating regulatory uncertainty
4. **Leverage** — higher debt load than most peers following Ameredev acquisition

## Risk Assessment

### Commodity Price Risk (High)
The dominant risk. At $102 WTI, the stock is deeply undervalued. At $70 WTI, the investment case weakens materially. The hedge book ($53 floors / $66 ceilings on ~50% of 2026 oil) limits downside but also caps upside on hedged volumes. A de-escalation of the Iran conflict pulling oil back to $80-90 would compress the estimate surge by roughly 30-40%.

### Waha Basis Risk (Moderate, Declining)
The Waha-Henry Hub differential destroyed gas economics in 2025 (Q4 realizations of $0.91/Mcf). The Hugh Brinson Pipeline (Q3-Q4 2026) is the structural fix. Until then, Matador mitigates through Houston Ship Channel sales and NGL processing. This risk is time-limited and has a clear resolution path.

### Balance Sheet Risk (Moderate)
Interest expense of $237M/year is manageable at current prices but becomes a meaningful burden below $55/Bbl oil. The non-recourse nature of San Mateo debt reduces effective leverage at the parent level.

### Regulatory Risk (Low-Moderate)
33% federal land exposure creates permitting risk. The current political environment is favorable for oil/gas development, but administrations change and NEPA litigation is ongoing.

### Options Market Signal

MTDR's options market shows significant stress pricing: near-term ATM IV of 67.5% versus 39.5% historical volatility represents a 28 percentage point premium. The term structure is in steep backwardation (67.5% near-term → 43.2% at 6 months), consistent with the broader energy sector's pricing of near-term geopolitical uncertainty.

The put/call OI ratio of 0.23 is extremely call-heavy, indicating speculative positioning for upside. The put/call volume ratio of 0.32 confirms this directional bias. The 1-week implied move of ±3.8% ($56.84-$61.00) reflects elevated near-term uncertainty from the Iran conflict.

The rich IV suggests that options are expensive — a holder of the underlying equity is better positioned than an options buyer at current levels. For new positions, the high IV makes covered-call strategies attractive to monetize the volatility premium.

### Short Interest

Short interest at 9.3% of float with 4.5 days to cover is elevated but not crowded. This represents potential fuel for a short squeeze if oil prices remain elevated and earnings continue to beat estimates. The short interest also reflects genuine skepticism about sustainability — shorts are betting on mean reversion in oil prices.

## Conclusion

Matador Resources is a well-run, pure-play Delaware Basin E&P trading at a 50% discount to peer median P/E with the strongest insider buying pattern in the sector, surging estimate revisions, and a specific near-term catalyst in the Hugh Brinson Pipeline. The balance sheet carries meaningful leverage, but at current oil prices, FCF generation is robust enough to deleverage rapidly.

The stock at $58.77 prices in roughly $70-75 WTI. Even accounting for hedge-book limitations on 50% of production, the upside from unhedged barrels and the gas economics improvement from Hugh Brinson justify a significantly higher valuation. Management's insider buying (including C-suite purchases in November 2025 near the $37 low and again in February-March 2026 in the low $50s) provides a high-conviction directional indicator.

The risk is straightforward: oil prices. A rapid de-escalation of the Iran conflict would pull oil back toward $80-90, compressing the estimate surge. At $70 oil, the stock is roughly fairly valued at current levels. Below $65, the leverage becomes a concern. The hedge book provides a $53 floor on half the production, establishing a downside cushion.

At the current setup — deeply discounted valuation, accelerating positive estimate revisions, specific catalysts with timelines, and exceptional insider alignment — MTDR rates as a **BUY** for investors with tolerance for commodity price volatility and a 6-12 month horizon.
