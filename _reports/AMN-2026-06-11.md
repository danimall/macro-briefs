**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AMN Healthcare is the largest diversified healthcare workforce-solutions company in the U.S., operating across nurse/allied staffing (~60% of revenue), physician and leadership solutions (~26%), and technology and workforce solutions (~14%). The business is two years into a severe post-COVID normalization: revenue fell from $5.24B in 2022 to $2.73B in 2025 (~48% peak-to-2025), and the company posted GAAP net losses in both 2024 (–$173M) and 2025 (–$101M), each driven heavily by goodwill impairments. The defining near-term distortion is a wave of large-scale hospital labor disruptions (strikes) that pushed Q1 2026 revenue to $1.378B (+100% YoY), of which roughly $722M was non-recurring strike work. Management's own Q2 2026 guidance of ~$627.5M (down ~54% sequentially, roughly flat-to-down YoY) confirms the surge is transitory and that the core business remains near a depressed ~$650M/quarter baseline.

The stock has rallied from a January 2026 low of $15.10 to $30.68, a ~103% move, now within 6% of its 52-week high of $32.52, almost entirely on the Q1 strike print and a building expectation that strike work recurs. The bear thesis is well-evidenced and largely disclosed: the strike revenue is non-recurring per management, the core is in a multi-year trough, the leverage covenant was loosened to 5.25x through Q1-2027, the refinancing pushed the marginal coupon from 4.625% to 6.500%, Kaiser is ~22% of revenue, and the company has impaired goodwill three years running. But each of those items is in the filings and in guidance, and the offsetting facts are also real: AMN generated $234M of FCF in FY2025 (8.6% margin) despite the GAAP loss, is not in liquidity distress, carries a low 0.438 beta, and has uncrowded short interest (8.4% of float, 2.0 days to cover).

The setup is two-sided. Normalized earnings power supports a fair value in the high-teens to low-$20s on the core; the strike-revenue optionality and an eventual bill-rate re-inflection support the sell-side mean of $28.14 and high of $36. At $30.68, the downside to a normalized core valuation and the upside to a strike-plus-recovery scenario roughly bracket the current price. That is a HOLD, not a directional call in either direction.

## Company Overview

AMN provides technology-enabled healthcare workforce solutions and staffing to acute and sub-acute hospitals and other facilities, primarily in the U.S. The three reportable segments:

- **Nurse and Allied Solutions (~60% of revenue):** Travel nurse, allied health (PT/OT/respiratory/lab/radiology), international nurse permanent placement, rapid-response/crisis nursing, labor-disruption (strike) staffing, local staffing, and revenue-cycle solutions. Delivered substantially through managed services programs (MSP), with ~$3.3B of spend under management.
- **Physician and Leadership Solutions (~26%):** Locum tenens (largest specialty CRNAs), interim leadership, executive search, and physician permanent placement.
- **Technology and Workforce Solutions (~14%):** AMN Language Services (interpretation), SaaS vendor management systems (ShiftWise Flex, Medefis, b4health), workforce optimization, and RPO.

AMN is a market leader in nurse/allied and locum tenens staffing and a leading provider of VMS/MSP and healthcare language services. Its competitive position rests on national scale, breadth of "total talent solutions," MSP relationships, Joint Commission certification, and a tech ecosystem (AMN Passport with 340K+ registered users, WorkWise, and an Event Management System purpose-built for strike-response). Principal competitors include Aya Healthcare, CHG, Cross Country Healthcare, Ingenovis, Jackson Healthcare, Medical Solutions, and HealthTrust. Strategically, management is steering toward more recurring, less cyclical MSP/VMS/tech revenue; recent portfolio moves include the Nov-2023 MSDR acquisition and the Jul-2025 sale of Smart Square scheduling software.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory (annual):**
- 2022: $5.24B revenue, +$607M net income, gross margin 33.0% (2023)
- 2023: $3.79B, +$284M
- 2024: $2.98B, –$173M (included $222M goodwill impairment), GM 30.8%
- 2025: $2.73B (–8% YoY), –$101M, GM compressed to 28.3%

The three-year decline of ~48% reflects normalization of COVID-era bill rates and traveler volumes. In FY2025, nurse/allied average travelers fell 14% and bill rates –2%. The 2025 net loss included a $109.5M goodwill impairment (physician & leadership) and an $18.3M intangibles impairment (revenue-cycle), partly offset by a $39.1M gain on the Smart Square sale.

**Cash generation is the genuine bright spot.** Despite the GAAP loss, FY2025 operating cash flow was $269M and FCF $234M (8.6% FCF margin), aided by receivables release (DSO improved to 47 from 70 in 2023). The losses are dominated by non-cash impairments and D&A; working capital releases cash as the business shrinks. This is why AMN is not in financial distress despite negative reported earnings, and why the Altman Z of 1.89 understates resilience: it is depressed by impairment-driven negative EBIT, not by liquidity stress.

**Balance sheet / leverage:** Total debt ~$767M against ~$642M equity (D/E ~2.26x). The October 2025 refinancing redeemed $500M of 4.625% 2027 notes, issued $400M of 6.500% 2031 notes, retained $350M of 4.0% 2029 notes, and extended/downsized the revolver to $450M maturing 2030. Lenders loosened the net-leverage covenant to 5.25x through Q1-2027 (Fourth/Fifth Amendments). The covenant accommodation and the higher marginal coupon both signal that AMN was managing toward covenant pressure as EBITDA fell, and that debt service is now a stiffer earnings headwind.

**Q1 2026 (10-Q, filed May 2026) requires careful reading.** Headline revenue was $1.378B (+100% YoY), GAAP EPS $1.59, net income $62M, management-cited adjusted EBITDA $166M, adjusted EPS $2.10. The driver was ~$722M of labor-disruption (strike) revenue versus $39M a year prior, a +$683M non-recurring swing, with strike work ~52% of total Q1 revenue. The underlying core remained soft: travel nurse $241M, allied $151M, locum $131M (–7%), VMS –18%, language services –8% on pricing pressure. The $560M cash balance and the $513M "client deposits" current liability are strike-event prepayments reconciling in Q2–Q3, not a structural improvement; Q1 operating cash flow of $562M is similarly inflated by these deposits.

**On earnings momentum, the picture is mixed rather than uniformly negative.** Current-year EPS consensus rose +30.3% over 90 days to $2.65, and the current-quarter estimate was revised +161.4% over 90 days to $0.19. The current-year lift is mechanically Q1-driven, but the sharp forward-quarter upgrade indicates the Street has built some recurring strike contribution and modest core firming into near-term estimates, not purely a one-time Q1 mark. The next-year estimate of $1.07 fell ~4.9% over 90 days, and 30-day revisions are flat-to-negative across most horizons. The net read: positive near-term momentum tied substantially to strike capture, but no durable upgrade to the through-cycle earnings base.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces lumpiness: +53.6% (Q2'25), +93.1% (Q3'25), –21.5% (Q4'25), +28.6% (Q1'26). Three beats and one miss, with surprise magnitudes swinging widely, consistent with a business whose results now hinge on unpredictable strike timing.

## Growth Analysis

The honest framing is that AMN has no clear near-term organic growth driver. Core volumes and bill rates only just stabilized exiting 2025 after multi-year declines, and Q2 guidance confirms a soft, roughly flat baseline. Hospitals continue to reduce contingent-labor reliance, build internal float pools and "internal travel" programs, and (per management) increase insourcing, partly enabled by AI-driven internal staffing tools. VMS and language services, the segments meant to provide recurring, less-cyclical revenue, were down 18% and 8% respectively in Q1, undercutting the diversification narrative in the near term.

The long-term secular demand case is intact and credible: the AAMC projects an up-to-86,000 physician shortage by 2036, the WHO projects a 4.5M global nurse shortfall by 2030, ~900K RNs are expected to exit the workforce by 2027, and U.S. health spending is projected to grow ~5.3–5.6% annually (Medicare ~7–9%). An aging population supports contingent-labor demand over a multi-year horizon. The unresolved question is timing: when does contingent-labor demand and bill-rate growth re-inflect off the current trough, and AMN's three consecutive years of goodwill impairment ($0 in 2023, $222M in 2024, $109.5M in 2025) reflect repeated downward revisions to forward forecasts, suggesting management has consistently been early on the recovery call.

A more constructive growth wrinkle: labor-disruption staffing is emerging as a differentiated, higher-margin niche where AMN's Event Management System and scale confer an advantage. Q1 2026 demonstrates the franchise can capture enormous revenue during strike waves. I do not treat this as zero forward contribution. A reasonable through-cycle assumption is that strike work contributes more than the pre-2026 ~$39M/quarter baseline given an elevated organized-labor environment in healthcare, perhaps $100–200M annually in a normal year, with episodic spikes well above that. This raises normalized revenue and margin modestly relative to a strict "strike = zero" view and is part of why a high-conviction bear call is not warranted.

## Valuation Assessment

At $30.68 the forward P/E is ~28.25x on the $1.07 next-year EPS estimate. On the current-year $2.65 estimate, which is strike-inflated by Q1, the multiple is ~11.6x, but that denominator is not a clean run-rate. The valuation hinges entirely on what one assumes for normalized earnings power.

**Normalized core scenario:** If the business settles near a ~$650M/quarter baseline with gross margins around 28% and a modest strike contribution, normalized EPS lands roughly in the $1.00–$1.30 range, close to the $1.07 next-year consensus. At 14–16x (a fair multiple for a deleveraging, cyclical-but-cash-generative staffing leader with secular tailwinds), that supports ~$15–$21. This is the basis for a fair-value zone in the high-teens to low-$20s on the core alone.

**Strike-plus-recovery scenario:** If strike waves recur at an elevated cadence and core bill rates re-inflect, normalized EPS could move toward $1.50–$2.00, and the multiple could expand. That supports the sell-side mean of $28.14 and high of $36.

The reconciliation the situation demands: consensus sits ~50%+ above a strict normalized-core value not because the Street has missed the strike fade (Q2 guidance is public and analysts have it) but because the sell-side is assigning real option value to recurring strike capture and to an eventual demand re-inflection that the secular shortage data makes plausible. I do not dismiss those targets as mere "caution signals." They reflect a defensible, if optimistic, weighting of the same two-sided distribution I am describing. The disagreement with my central estimate is about probability weighting, not about a fact the market is missing. This is why this is a HOLD: the bull and bear are arguing about the odds on an uncertain demand recovery, and at $30.68 the price sits between the two regimes rather than clearly inside either.

Peer comparison is of limited use here: the only same-sector comparable supplied (BridgeBio, a clinical-stage biopharma with a negative P/E) is not a relevant staffing analog. Closer operational peers (Cross Country Healthcare, CHG, Aya) face the same cyclical down-cycle: AMN's scale and MSP/VMS franchise are advantages versus regional players, but its still-staffing-heavy mix leaves it exposed to the same headwinds.

The valuation score is set at 4.5: the multiple is not clearly cheap on a clean run-rate, the rally has already captured much of the strike optionality, but the downside is bounded by genuine FCF and a real secular floor, so it is not a value trap either.

## Competitive Landscape

AMN's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-eroding in the near term. National scale, ~$3.3B of MSP spend under management, Joint Commission certification, and the tech ecosystem are durable advantages versus regional competitors. The Event Management System gives AMN a structural edge in large-scale strike response, a niche that is becoming a differentiated, higher-margin capability. These are real moats.

Against that, the segments meant to be the recurring, sticky core showed weakness in Q1 (VMS –18%, language –8% on pricing pressure), and management explicitly names AI-enabled internal staffing tools and client insourcing as demand threats: hospitals getting better at staffing themselves directly reduces agency need. The trajectory is therefore a market leader holding share in a shrinking pool while its highest-value recurring segments face pricing pressure. That nets to a competitive-position score of 5.5: clear leadership and a defensible niche, offset by softening in the parts of the portfolio that were supposed to de-cyclicalize the business.

## Risk Assessment

**Kaiser concentration (~22% of consolidated revenue; ~32% of nurse/allied).** AMN's single most important idiosyncratic risk. No other client exceeds 5%. The relationship is a stabilizing anchor but a single-point-of-failure; any material reduction would be a meaningful adverse event. This cuts both ways and is not currently flashing distress, but it caps the risk score.

**Structural demand normalization and insourcing.** The core driver of the multi-year decline; bill rates have only just stabilized. The timing of re-inflection is the dominant swing variable and is genuinely uncertain.

**Leverage and rising cost of debt.** $767M debt, covenant loosened to 5.25x through Q1-2027, marginal coupon up to 6.500%. EBITDA must recover to avoid renewed covenant strain after the accommodation lapses. This is a real constraint but not an imminent liquidity problem given $234M FY2025 FCF.

**Strike-revenue volatility.** A swing factor that can double quarterly revenue but is "unpredictable" by management's own description. It distorts comparability and earnings quality, and it is as much an upside tail as a downside risk.

**Impairment risk.** $756M goodwill remains after $547M cumulative impairments. Disclosed WACC sensitivity is tight (a 50bp change moves the physician unit's excess fair value ~14%), so further impairments are plausible if forecasts soften. These are non-cash but pressure equity and reflect a pattern of over-optimistic forecasting.

**Regulatory and other:** Independent-contractor classification of locum tenens, corporate-practice-of-medicine rules, immigration/visa retrogression for international nurse, ACA rollback risk, and persistent California/Federal wage-and-hour class actions (the largest litigation accrual category).

The risk score of 4.5 reflects that the principal risks (strike fade, leverage, Kaiser) are largely disclosed and partially priced after the move down from COVID highs, but the impairment cadence, covenant clock, and concentration keep idiosyncratic downside live.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (toward $36)
Strike waves recur at an elevated cadence in an active organized-labor environment, and AMN's Event Management System captures outsized, higher-margin revenue each time. Core bill rates and volumes re-inflect off the trough as hospital float pools prove insufficient against the documented 900K-RN exit and physician-shortage backdrop. EBITDA recovers comfortably clear of the 5.25x covenant before Q1-2027, removing the leverage overhang. Normalized EPS moves toward $1.50–$2.00, and a ~18–20x multiple plus strike optionality drives the stock toward the high-$30s.

### Bear Case (toward $18–$20)
Strike activity normalizes back toward the pre-2026 baseline, Q2's ~$627.5M run-rate persists, VMS/language pricing pressure continues, and insourcing plus AI staffing tools structurally shrink the contingent-labor pool. A fourth consecutive goodwill impairment dents equity, EBITDA bumps against the covenant after Q1-2027, and the market re-rates the stock back toward normalized-core value at 14–16x on ~$1.00–$1.10 EPS, roughly $15–$21. The rally fully reverses as the strike optionality the market is now paying for fails to materialize.

The two cases are roughly symmetric around the current $30.68. The bear catalyst (strike fade) is already disclosed in guidance and partially priced, while the bull catalyst (strike re-inflation) is uncontrollable and can recur without warning. Neither side has a decisive informational edge, which is the defining characteristic of a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The setup is two-sided at $30.68, with downside to ~$18–$20 (–35% to –41%) and upside toward $36 (+17%) bracketing the current price. There is no asymmetry sufficient to justify either a BUY or an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters showing core (ex-strike) revenue stabilizing and growing YoY, confirming a demand re-inflection rather than a strike-only story.
- Evidence that elevated strike capture is recurring (a second large strike-revenue quarter in 2026) combined with EBITDA tracking comfortably below the 5.25x covenant.
- A pullback into the high-teens/low-$20s (~12–14x normalized EPS) that restores asymmetry on the core alone.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A fourth consecutive goodwill impairment alongside a downward revision to forward EBITDA, signaling the recovery forecast is again being pushed out.
- Any material reduction or loss signal in the Kaiser MSP relationship.
- Core run-rate deteriorating below the ~$627.5M Q2 baseline with no offsetting strike capture, pressuring the covenant headroom as the 5.25x accommodation approaches expiry (Q1-2027).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), which will reveal the post-strike run-rate and management's tone on core demand and strike pipeline. The Q1-2027 covenant step-down is the key structural date to monitor.

## Conclusion

AMN is a cash-generative, market-leading healthcare staffing franchise caught in a deep post-COVID demand trough, where a non-recurring strike-revenue wave has lifted the stock ~103% off its January low to near its 52-week high. The bear thesis is real and well-documented: transitory strike revenue, a soft core confirmed by Q2 guidance, a higher debt coupon, a loosened covenant, 22% Kaiser concentration, and three straight years of impairments. But every one of those items is disclosed and at least partially reflected in a stock that still trades below its COVID-era levels, and the offsetting facts are equally concrete: $234M of FY2025 FCF, no liquidity distress, a low beta, uncrowded short interest, a genuine secular shortage backdrop, and a differentiated strike-response capability that creates a real, uncontrollable upside tail. The forward-quarter estimate was revised up +161% over 90 days, indicating the Street has built recurring strike contribution into near-term numbers rather than treating Q1 as purely one-time.

A strict normalized-core valuation supports the high-teens to low-$20s; the strike optionality and demand-recovery case support the sell-side mean of $28.14 and high of $36. At $30.68 the price sits between those regimes, and the bull and bear are arguing about probabilities on an uncertain demand recovery rather than about a fact the market has missed. That is the profile of a balanced HOLD: monitor the core run-rate, the strike cadence, the Kaiser relationship, and the covenant clock, and revisit on either a confirmed core re-inflection (upgrade) or a renewed impairment/demand deterioration (downgrade).
