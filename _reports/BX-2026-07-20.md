**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Blackstone is the world's largest alternative asset manager, with $1.30 trillion in total AUM and $937.6 billion in fee-earning AUM as of March 31, 2026. The Q1 2026 print was operationally strong: distributable earnings of $1.996B (+23% YoY), fee-related earnings of $1.548B (+23%), management and advisory fees of $2.13B (+13%), and $52.7B of total net inflows. Net accrued performance revenues — the "carry bank" of future realizations — rose to $7.0B from $6.4B. On a fundamental basis the franchise is compounding, and the AI/data-center secular tailwind (BIP infrastructure AUM $80.6B, 19% net return since inception) sits at the center of the bull case.

The stock nonetheless trades at $124.05, down roughly 35% from its $190 September 2025 high, while consensus estimates are broadly falling (current-year EPS revised -3.9% over 90 days, current-quarter -7.7%). The market is repricing a specific overhang: BCRED, the retail perpetual private-credit vehicle that powered recent fee growth, went into net outflows in Q1 2026, and management stated in unusually direct language that "net flows are likely to continue to be negatively impacted." The equity of alternatives managers is being derated on evergreen/incentive-fee and redemption-gate risk, even where the committed-capital management-fee base is insulated.

We rate BX a HOLD. The recurring FRE base tied to $539.7B of perpetual capital is durable and largely insulated from the redemption dynamics the bears cite, but the retail perpetual-credit engine that drove the last leg of fee-rate growth is cooling in real time, estimate momentum is negative, and the stock still trades at roughly 21x forward distributable earnings — a premium to most listed alternatives peers that is hard to defend while the flow trend is deteriorating and the exit market for carry realizations remains soft. This is our fifth consecutive HOLD; the score has drifted down from 5.9 to 5.8, and the fundamental data confirms rather than reverses that drift. We would upgrade on stabilization of BCRED/BREIT retail flows or a re-entry below ~$110; we would downgrade on a credit-spread-driven credit conversion or a quantifiable step-up in direct-lending defaults.

## Company Overview

Blackstone operates a capital-light, fee-driven model across four segments:

- **Credit & Insurance ($457.5B AUM)** — the largest segment, spanning BXCI private corporate credit (BCRED, BXSL BDCs), liquid credit/CLOs, infrastructure/asset-based credit, and a large insurance-capital platform of SMAs and sub-advisory mandates.
- **Private Equity ($429.9B AUM)** — corporate PE (BCP), infrastructure (BIP/BXINFRA), secondaries (Strategic Partners), GP stakes, tactical opportunities, life sciences, growth, and the retail vehicle BXPE. This is the fastest-growing segment.
- **Real Estate ($315.3B AUM)** — BREP opportunistic funds, Core+ perpetual vehicles (BREIT, BPP, BEPIF), and real estate debt (BREDS, BXMT). Blackstone is the largest real estate investor globally and a dominant data-center owner.
- **Multi-Asset Investing ($101.4B AUM)** — BXMA, the world's largest discretionary hedge-fund allocator, which crossed $100B in Q1 2026.

Revenue is management fees (recurring), performance revenues (realization-dependent carry and incentive fees), and principal investment income. The economic moat is scale, brand, and perpetual capital: $539.7B of total AUM is perpetual, providing durable management fees that do not cycle with fund vintages. The firm uses an Up-C structure — Blackstone Inc. is the sole GP of the Blackstone Holdings partnerships, with senior managing directors holding roughly 445M exchangeable partnership units, which is why GAAP net income is heavily split with non-controlling interests. Stephen Schwarzman controls the company via Series II preferred stock; Jonathan Gray is President and COO.

## Financial Analysis

Consolidated GAAP figures are misleading in isolation because Blackstone consolidates funds, VIEs, and Holdings partnerships. The correct lens is segment distributable earnings and attributable amounts.

**FY2025 (attributable/parent basis):** Revenue $9.05B (+11% YoY from $8.15B); net income attributable to Blackstone Inc. roughly $3.0B; pretax $7.17B; operating cash flow $4.66B and FCF $4.55B (capex minimal, confirming the capital-light model); total assets $47.7B; Blackstone Inc. stockholders' equity $8.67B; total equity including NCI $20.5B.

**Q1 2026 (period ended 3/31/26):** Total segment DE $1.996B (+23% YoY); FRE $1.548B (+23%); total segment revenue $3.43B (+24%); management and advisory fees $2.13B (+13%); adjusted EBITDA $2.13B (+23%); GAAP EPS $0.83 vs $0.80. Net accrued performance revenues rose to $7.0B.

The balance sheet carries $13.3B of long-dated, investment-grade senior notes (weighted toward low-coupon 2020-2021 issuance, maturing through 2052) plus a $4.325B revolver, of which $900M was drawn at quarter-end and an additional $700M was drawn in May 2026 for routine liquidity management. Liquidity is ample: $2.4B cash plus $7.8B of mostly liquid other investments. The Altman Z-Score and consolidated debt/equity ratios are not meaningful for this business — they gross up fund VIE assets ($5.8B) and NCI ($7.2B+), and BX is investment-grade with negligible refinancing risk.

Capital allocation runs roughly 85% of DE as dividends (Q1 2026 dividend $1.16/share; FY2025 $4.74/share, a ~3.8% yield at current price). Buybacks are modest — $24.4M in Q1 2026 against $1.7B remaining on the $2.0B authorization — largely offsetting the recurring equity-comp dilution ($561M in Q1 2026, a real cost excluded from segment DE that investors should carry).

The earnings-surprise record is solid but softening: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with Q4 2025 beating by 10.7% and Q3 2025 by 20.3%, but Q1 2026 missing by 1.9% ($1.36 vs $1.39 estimate). The blended annualized base management fee rate compressed to 0.84% from 0.86% YoY as growth skewed toward lower-fee credit and perpetual vehicles — a gradual but real mix headwind.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine has two distinct components with diverging trajectories. The recurring base is healthy: FRE +23% YoY, management and advisory fees +13%, and $52.7B of total net inflows in Q1 2026 (Credit & Insurance $37B, PE $20B). The $7.0B carry bank is a forward indicator of DE upside once exit markets normalize — carry funds currently below their thresholds (BREP IX, BREP Europe V/VI, BREP Asia II) will not generate carry until they recover, but the accrued balance argues for latent earnings power.

The concern is the retail perpetual-credit channel. In Q1 2026, "heightened press and market attention around private credit, as well as concerns about decelerating performance, drove a material increase in BCRED redemption requests," and BCRED turned to net outflows. Management's forward warning is direct. BREIT also continued to see outflows. Retail perpetual vehicles were the growth-and-fee-rate story of the last several years — institutional and insurance demand offset the retail softness this quarter, but the marginal-growth vector is impaired.

Consensus reflects this. Current-year EPS has been revised down 3.9% over 90 days to $5.88, next-year down 3.0% to $7.44, and current-quarter down 7.7% to $1.33. The revisions are modest in magnitude but consistently negative across every window — the opposite of the accelerating positive-revision signal that would support a BUY. The AI/data-center tailwind is genuine and central (BIP $80.6B AUM, 19% net return since inception), and infrastructure/data centers were cited as the top performance driver. The same AI theme cuts against BX in its software-equity and software-credit holdings: Q1 2026 included a $385M unrealized principal-investment loss partly tied to software valuations, and management repeatedly flags that "valuations of select software companies have been and may be negatively impacted."

## Valuation Assessment

At $124.05 the stock trades at roughly 32x trailing GAAP EPS of $3.87, but the more relevant metric is forward distributable earnings: on the $5.88 current-year and $7.44 next-year DE consensus, BX trades at ~21x current-year and ~16.7x next-year. The dividend yields ~3.7%, well above the peer median of 1.6%, providing absolute support.

Against peers, the picture is genuinely mixed. KKR trades at 33.2x P/E, APO at 84.8x (distorted by insurance accounting), while BNY Mellon (16.9x), Ameriprise (11.2x), and State Street (16.7x) are traditional asset managers on different economics. Within the alternatives cohort, BX is not obviously cheap — KKR and BX are broadly comparable on forward multiples, and BX's flow-trend overhang argues for a discount, not a premium, until BCRED stabilizes. The analyst price-target set (low $114, mean $139.95, median $137, high $184) puts current price near the low end, implying ~13% upside to the mean.

Our discipline here is to treat cheapness as a warning rather than a signal. The stock's ~35% drawdown from its high, on a franchise whose fundamentals are largely intact, could read as a mispricing, but at that magnitude the burden of proof shifts to the bull. The specific thing the sellers appear to be pricing is not a myth: the retail perpetual-credit engine is cooling, and management's own guidance confirms the flow deterioration will persist. A premium multiple on a franchise with a deteriorating marginal-growth vector and falling estimates does not offer asymmetric upside. We score valuation 5.2 — the yield support and the reasonable ~17x next-year DE multiple offset the negative revision momentum and the absence of a clear near-term catalyst to close the gap. We would find the risk/reward clearly attractive below ~$110, where the forward DE multiple compresses toward 15x and the yield approaches 4.3%.

## Competitive Landscape

Blackstone's competitive position is strong but no longer improving at the pace the prior scoring assumed (competitive position has drifted from 9.0 to 7.0 across recent reports). Its differentiation is scale, perpetual capital, and retail/private-wealth distribution. The structural shift of lending from banks to private markets remains a durable tailwind, and Blackstone is one of the largest credit/CLO managers globally. Insurance capital — permanent, liability-matched AUM in SMAs terminable only for long-term underperformance or cause — is a genuine sticky growth engine, and institutional and insurance demand offset the retail credit softness in Q1 2026.

The data-center/AI infrastructure vector is where Blackstone's combined real estate and infrastructure scale gives it a real edge, and this is the strongest secular differentiator. Against that, the retail private-credit franchise that distinguished BX from institutional-only peers is the very channel now under stress, so the moat's most recently-added layer is the one being tested. On balance the franchise remains best-in-class, but the trajectory is stable-to-modestly-eroding rather than widening, which supports a 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that are not fully priced cluster around the credit franchise:

1. **BCRED/retail perpetual-credit redemptions** — the marquee risk. The retail engine is cooling in real time and warrants close monitoring each quarter.
2. **Rising direct-lending defaults into falling base rates** — management states defaults "have risen and we expect will continue to rise from a historically low level," while floating-rate returns "are likely to be lower" as base rates fall. Credit is the largest segment.
3. **AI-driven software valuation risk** — the $385M unrealized principal loss and repeated software-valuation flags in both PE and Credit.
4. **Realization/exit-market cyclicality** — Middle East conflict slowed realizations and the IPO pipeline; carry funds below thresholds generate no carry until recovery.
5. **Clawback exposure** — $8.4B after-tax performance allocations subject to clawback in a remote worst case ($7.6B Holdings' potential liability).
6. **Governance/structure** — founder control via Series II preferred, and roughly $2.0B undiscounted owed to pre-IPO owners/SMDs under the tax receivable agreement, a structural cash leakage over ~15 years.

The Kentucky Retirement System / BLP litigation continues after a $82.5M settlement was terminated when the court declined approval; an accrual has been taken and it is not expected to be individually material.

The reported "Credit segment DE fell 26% YoY" is not a credit blow-up — it is largely a tough comp against the Q1 2025 one-time Bistro software-sale gain plus CLO losses; underlying credit FRE and fundraising were healthy. The real red flag is the BCRED flow trend, not the segment number.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 62.8% versus 35.0% one-year historical vol — rich, at the 87th percentile within the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (62.8% near-term vs 42.2% six-month). The market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with an earnings date and the private-credit overhang. Near-term put/call skew is roughly balanced at -2.5%, but the put/call OI ratio of 1.62 and a six-month -3.6% put skew indicate hedging demand further out. The implied one-week move is ±5.4%. The combination of rich, backwardated near-term vol with elevated further-out put open interest confirms the fundamental read that the market is pricing a specific, near-dated risk rather than generalized volatility. Short interest is low at 3.1% of float (4.0 days to cover), so this is not a crowded-short setup; the selling is real-money repricing, not a squeeze dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($155-175).** The BCRED redemption episode proves transient, retail flows stabilize by H2 2026, and institutional/insurance demand continues to more than offset it. The $7.0B carry bank converts as exit markets normalize (aided by a de-escalation reopening the IPO pipeline), FRE compounds mid-teens, and the AI/data-center infrastructure vector drives above-plan performance. The stock re-rates back toward 22-24x forward DE as the private-credit fear fades, and the ~3.7% yield plus low-teens DE growth delivers a low-20s total return.

**Bear case ($95-105, ~18-25% downside).** BCRED and BREIT redemptions accelerate into broader private-credit stress, direct-lending defaults step up as base rates fall, gating spreads and forces asset-side liquidation at marks, and the software-valuation drag compounds. Credit spreads widen and the equity-credit disconnect converts. Fee-rate compression continues past 0.84%, FRE growth decelerates to high-single digits, and the multiple compresses to 14-15x forward DE on a franchise the market now treats as cyclically exposed rather than structurally advantaged.

**Base case ($130-138).** Retail credit flows remain soft but do not cascade; institutional inflows carry AUM growth; DE compounds low-double-digits on the $5.88/$7.44 estimate path; the multiple holds around 17-18x next-year DE. The stock grinds back toward the analyst mean ($139.95) as estimate revisions stabilize, delivering high-single-digit price appreciation plus the ~3.7% yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The recurring FRE base is durable and the franchise is best-in-class, but the specific overhang on the retail perpetual-credit engine is deteriorating in real time, estimates are falling across every window, and the ~21x current-year / ~17x next-year forward DE multiple does not offer asymmetric upside while that trend runs. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD; the current fundamental data confirms the modest downward drift in the score rather than justifying either a break higher or a downgrade to AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- BCRED and BREIT return to net inflows (or clearly decelerating outflows) in the Q2 or Q3 2026 print, with management removing the "net flows likely to continue to be negatively impacted" language.
- Consensus current-year DE estimate revisions turn positive over a 30- and 90-day window.
- Re-entry below ~$110, compressing forward DE toward 15x with a ~4.3% yield.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Credit spreads widen materially and the private-credit cascade converts, or a quantifiable step-up in direct-lending defaults appears in segment disclosures.
- Fee-rate compression accelerates below ~0.80% alongside FRE growth decelerating to single digits.
- BCRED/BREIT redemptions force gating or asset-side liquidation at distressed marks.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July) is the natural checkpoint — the flow trend in BCRED/BREIT and any change in management's forward flow language are the decisive data points. Credit-spread moves are the macro monitor between prints.

## Conclusion

Blackstone is a best-in-class, capital-light compounder with record $1.30T AUM, +23% YoY distributable earnings and FRE, a $539.7B perpetual-capital base, and a genuine AI/data-center secular tailwind. The recurring management-fee engine tied to committed and perpetual capital is largely insulated from the redemption dynamics the bears cite. The specific vector under stress — BCRED retail perpetual credit — is the very channel that distinguished BX's recent growth, and management's unusually direct warning that outflows will persist, combined with falling estimates across every window and a still-premium ~21x forward DE multiple, leaves the risk/reward balanced at $124. The ~35% drawdown from the high is the market pricing a real, management-confirmed deterioration in the marginal-growth vector against a franchise whose durable base remains intact. We rate BX a HOLD, with a clear path to a more constructive stance on flow stabilization or a re-entry below ~$110.
