## Executive Summary

Sanmina has undergone a genuine business transformation via its October 2025 acquisition of ZT Systems from AMD for $1.62B, effectively doubling revenue and repositioning the company from a diversified EMS provider into a major AI infrastructure manufacturer. Q2 FY2026 results demonstrated this: revenue of $4.01B (+102% YoY), with cloud/AI infrastructure now representing 69% of the mix (up from 37%). IMS margins improved from 7.7% to 8.5% despite the integration, the contingent earnout increased to $170M from $111M (signaling ZT is outperforming deal-model assumptions), and the company generated $577M in operating cash flow in H1 FY2026.

The bull case is straightforward: AI infrastructure demand is structural, the AMD NPI partnership provides differentiated access to hyperscale customers, margins are improving despite the revenue mix shift, and estimates are rising (+11.1% FY26, +7.5% FY27 over 90 days). The bear case centers on: the stock at $261 has appreciated 210% from its 52-week low of $81.69, the forward P/E of ~23x on $11.20 FY26 consensus is above historical EMS multiples, next-quarter guidance of $3.35B disappointed, customer concentration has risen materially (top 2 customers >20% of revenue), and the CEO/insiders are selling into strength with no open-market purchases. At this price, the stock is trading above all four analyst price targets (consensus high: $234), and the 9% short interest reflects meaningful skepticism about sustainability.

The 72-hour assessment: SANM's transformation thesis is real and the growth trajectory is compelling, but the market has largely priced the known upside. A HOLD is appropriate because the current price exceeds every analyst target, insider selling is concentrated, and the next data point (Q3 FY2026 results, likely July/August) will test whether the $3.35B guidance represents deceleration or conservatism.

## Company Overview

Sanmina is a global EMS company headquartered in San Jose, California, operating from approximately 20 countries with ~39,000 employees. The company operates in two segments:

**Integrated Manufacturing Solutions (IMS, ~90% of revenue):** PCB assembly, test, high-level assembly, direct order fulfillment, and warranty services. This segment now includes ZT Systems' AI and general-purpose computer infrastructure manufacturing.

**Components, Products & Services (CPS, ~10% of revenue):** Advanced PCBs, optical/RF components, Viking memory/storage products, SCI defense & aerospace, and 42Q manufacturing software. Higher-margin, more specialized business.

The ZT Systems acquisition closed October 27, 2025, fundamentally reshaping the company. ZT designs and manufactures rack-scale AI infrastructure for hyperscale cloud operators. As part of the deal, AMD designated Sanmina as its preferred U.S.-based New Product Introduction manufacturing partner for rack and cluster-scale AI solutions.

## Competitive Landscape

**Pre-ZT:** Sanmina was a mid-tier EMS player competing with Foxconn, Jabil, Flex, and Celestica on cost and manufacturing footprint. Differentiation was modest — advanced PCBs, defense electronics, and the 42Q software platform provided some moat, but the core business was commoditized contract manufacturing.

**Post-ZT:** The competitive positioning has materially improved. The AMD NPI partnership creates a differentiated go-to-market channel that competitors cannot easily replicate. ZT's hyperscale customer relationships (likely including major cloud providers) provide sticky revenue streams that require deep engineering integration. The U.S. manufacturing footprint (71% of Americas revenue) is a genuine advantage in the current tariff environment.

**Competitive Risks:** Celestica has executed a similar AI infrastructure pivot and trades at elevated multiples. Quanta Computer and Wistron (Taiwan ODMs) are formidable competitors in rack-scale infrastructure. If AMD loses AI compute share to NVIDIA or if hyperscale customers diversify manufacturing sources, Sanmina's positioning weakens.

The trajectory is clearly positive — Sanmina has moved from a commoditized manufacturer to a strategically positioned AI infrastructure partner. The key uncertainty is durability of this positioning.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Customer Concentration (HIGH RISK):** Top 10 customers now represent ~72% of revenue, with two exceeding 10% each. Given ZT's customer base likely includes 2-3 hyperscale operators, losing a single large customer could reduce revenue by $2-4B annually. No long-term purchase commitments exist. This risk was significantly amplified by the acquisition.

**2. Integration Execution (MODERATE-HIGH):** ZT represents 47% of revenue and is excluded from internal controls assessment for FY2026. IT systems, operational processes, and control environments are still being integrated. The next 2-3 quarters represent the critical integration window.

**3. Leverage and Interest Cost (MODERATE):** Debt increased from $300M to $2.2B. Annual interest expense runs ~$130M, consuming approximately 10-12% of operating income. While cash flow generation is strong, a demand slowdown would compress coverage ratios toward covenant limits.

**4. AMD Dependency (MODERATE):** The NPI partnership is core to ZT's strategic value. If AMD loses data center share or if the partnership terms change, Sanmina's AI infrastructure positioning degrades. This is a single-vendor risk embedded in the growth thesis.

**5. Quarterly Variability (MODERATE):** Guidance of $3.35B for next quarter (vs. $4.01B actual) represents significant lumpiness. Hyperscale purchasing patterns are binary — a single customer delaying an order by one quarter creates headline risk at premium multiples.

**6. Insider Selling (NOTABLE):** CEO sold $27M in stock (May 6, 2026). Director Licata sold $5.6M. Multiple executives adopted 10b5-1 plans. No open-market purchases. While diversification explains part of this, the concentrated pattern at elevated prices warrants monitoring.

**7. IRS Tax Dispute:** $503M worthless stock deduction from 2009 challenged. If disallowed, could reduce NOL carryforwards and create material tax liability.
