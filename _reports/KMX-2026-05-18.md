## Executive Summary

CarMax is a deteriorating franchise navigating simultaneous headwinds across its retail operations and finance arm. Revenue has declined 13% from the FY2023 peak ($29.7B → $25.9B), net income collapsed 50.6% in FY2026, and the company executed a CEO change in March 2026 after admitting its strategic direction was failing. The new CEO has articulated no concrete turnaround plan beyond margin cuts to drive volume — a strategy that compresses near-term earnings with uncertain payoff.

The auto finance segment (CAF), which represents CarMax's key competitive differentiator, is showing accelerating credit deterioration: net charge-offs rose to 2.34% in FY2026 (annualizing at 2.54% in Q3), delinquencies hit 5.43%, and recovery rates are falling. Management is simultaneously expanding into lower-credit tiers — a bet that will either prove prescient or compound losses if the consumer environment continues to weaken. With macroeconomic headwinds facing the consumer, the latter outcome appears more probable.

At $37.38 (down 48% from 52-week highs), the stock is cheap on adjusted metrics (~13x adjusted FY2026 EPS) but expensive on GAAP (~22x) and facing negative estimate revisions across all periods. Four Strong Sell ratings from Wall Street — unusual for a $5B market cap name — signal institutional conviction that further downside exists. The median analyst target of $39 offers minimal upside, and the consensus low of $19 implies potential for another 49% decline.

## Company Overview

CarMax operates 256 used vehicle retail stores across 110 U.S. television markets, representing approximately 85% of the U.S. population. The company sold 780,684 used vehicles and 538,203 wholesale vehicles in FY2026 (ended February 2026). Its vertically integrated model spans vehicle acquisition, reconditioning, retail/wholesale sales, financing (CarMax Auto Finance), and extended protection plans.

CarMax Auto Finance (CAF) originated loans on 42.4% of retail sales, managing a $16.4 billion auto loan portfolio servicing approximately 1 million customer accounts. The company's no-haggle pricing model and omni-channel approach (81% of transactions digitally enabled) differentiate it from the fragmented dealer market (18,000+ franchised dealers). Market share stands at 3.6% of 0-10 year old used vehicles — the largest single retailer but still a small fraction of the approximately 20 million-unit addressable market.

The FY2026 result includes $141.3M in goodwill impairment (Edmunds write-off) and $49.8M in restructuring charges (CEO severance, workforce reduction). Excluding these, adjusted EPS of $2.91 still represents an 11% decline year-over-year.

**Q3 FY2026 marked the sharpest deterioration:** comparable store used unit sales plunged -9.0%, SG&A consumed 98.5% of gross profit, and management acknowledged the pricing strategy was failing.

**Balance Sheet:**

Corporate-only debt (excluding $15.8B non-recourse securitization) is approximately $2.0B against $5.9B equity — a 0.34x ratio that is manageable. However, the revolver is drawn $840M of $2B capacity (highest since pre-pandemic), cash has been halved to $123M, and a $200M senior note maturity in April 2026 required refinancing. The share buyback pause signals management awareness of balance sheet strain.

**CAF Credit Quality — The Key Risk:**

- Net credit losses: 1.93% (FY2024) → 2.03% (FY2025) → 2.34% (FY2026), annualizing at 2.54%
- Delinquencies: 5.43% (Nov 2025) vs. 4.85% (Feb 2025)
- Recovery rates declining: 53.0% → 47.2% → 45.5% over three years
- Problem concentrated in 2022-2023 vintage loans (originated at peak vehicle prices to stressed borrowers)

Management claims post-April 2024 originations are performing "in line with expectations," but is simultaneously expanding Tier 2 penetration targets from <15% to ~30% — a contradictory signal that introduces new vintage risk.

**Capital Allocation Destruction:**

CarMax repurchased $642.8M of stock in FY2026 at an estimated average of $55-60/share. At $37.38 today, this represents approximately $200M+ in destroyed shareholder value. The buyback is now paused, and the remaining $1.31B authorization sits dormant while leverage exceeds targets.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are materially negative across all measurable dimensions:

- Revenue has declined three consecutive years
- Comparable store units: -9.0% in Q3 FY2026, with management deliberately sacrificing margins to stabilize volumes
- Market share declining: 3.6% vs. 3.7% prior year
- Estimate revisions negative across all periods: current year -9.0% (30d), next year -17.1% (30d)

The theoretical recovery path involves: (1) $200M annualized SG&A savings by end of FY2027, (2) margin cuts driving unit volume recovery, (3) CAF Tier 2 expansion adding $10-12M lifetime pre-tax income per penetration point, and (4) EPP redesign contributing $35/unit margin improvement.

However, these initiatives face headwinds from the macro environment. With consumer discretionary spending under pressure, the demand for $26,000 average-priced used vehicles is structurally impaired. CarMax's target demographic (mass-market consumers needing auto financing) is precisely the cohort most affected by current economic conditions.

The tariff environment creates additional uncertainty: if new vehicle tariffs push prices higher, used vehicle demand could theoretically benefit, but affordability constraints already limit buyers at current price levels.

## Competitive Landscape

CarMax's competitive position is eroding on multiple fronts:

**Carvana:** The digital-native competitor has recovered from near-bankruptcy and is reportedly exceeding expectations on revenue, profit, and efficiency. Carvana's lower fixed-cost structure (fewer physical locations, lower reconditioning infrastructure) enables more aggressive pricing. CarMax's historical advantage — physical presence combined with digital — loses relative value as consumer comfort with fully online auto purchasing increases.

**Franchised Dealers:** 18,000+ franchised dealers with manufacturer-backed "certified pre-owned" programs compete on trust and warranty. Their access to manufacturer subvented financing rates provides a structural advantage CarMax cannot match.

**Data/Platform Competition:** Online platforms increasingly enable smaller dealers to price effectively, reducing CarMax's information advantage in vehicle acquisition and pricing.

The direction of competitive positioning is negative: market share declining, comparable store sales collapsing, and the Edmunds acquisition (intended to strengthen digital positioning) has been written off as value-destructive.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks (not yet fully priced):**

1. **CAF credit loss acceleration in a recession.** With consumer credit under pressure, the 2.34% loss rate could materially exceed the 2.78% allowance. Management's sensitivity analysis shows a 10% worsening scenario adds $51.8M in losses — a recession could produce 20-30% worsening.

2. **Turnaround execution failure.** New CEO Keith Barr has been in seat since March 16, 2026 — approximately two months. No concrete strategy has been articulated beyond margin cuts and cost reduction. The company's own filing states "it is clear CarMax needs change" without specifying what that change involves.

3. **Consumer environment deterioration.** Elevated input costs compress dealer margins on reconditioning and parts. Affordability constraints and elevated energy costs directly reduce demand for $26,000 vehicles.

4. **Securities litigation overhang.** Class action filed November 2025 covering June-November 2025 period. Even if ultimately meritless, creates legal expenses and management distraction during critical turnaround period.

5. **Refinancing risk.** $200M senior notes due April 2026 required refinancing in a higher-rate environment. Revolver at $840M drawn with rising rates increases interest expense.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term backwardation (68.5% 1-month vs. 50.4% 6-month) confirms imminent event risk. The put/call volume ratio of 2.05 and 12% short interest reinforce bearish institutional positioning.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Consumer earnings establishing broad consumer weakness narrative; KMX's next quarterly report (likely July 2026) where management's margin-cut strategy must show volume results or the thesis collapses further. If CAF Q1 FY2027 loss rates spike above 2.5% annualized, credit concerns become acute.

**Timing window:** 2-4 months. The market will evaluate whether margin cuts produced volume recovery in Q1 FY2027. If comparable store sales remain negative despite $300/unit GPU sacrifice, the stock reprices lower.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 12.0% with 4.1 days to cover. Elevated but not extreme. No obvious squeeze catalyst (no activist buying, no M&A speculation at these levels, institutional positioning bearish). Manageable risk.

**Borrow and options signal:** The options market confirms the bearish thesis (89th percentile IV within chain, backwardation structure). Elevated IV means put protection is expensive; shorting common stock is the cleaner expression if borrow is available.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $22-25 vs. current $37.38 represents 33-41% downside. Upside risk to $48-50 on turnaround evidence (28-34% upside). Reward:risk approximately 1.2:1 at current levels — below the 2:1 threshold for active shorting.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE at current levels.** The reward:risk has compressed because the stock has already fallen 48% from its highs. The bearish thesis is correct but largely reflected in price. The 4 Strong Sells and 12% short interest indicate the institutional community has already positioned. Remaining downside is real but not sufficiently asymmetric versus squeeze/turnaround risk given the new CEO variable.

## Conclusion

CarMax faces a structural challenge that a new CEO and cost cuts cannot quickly resolve. The company's competitive moat — scale, data, and financing — is being eroded by Carvana's lower-cost model and improving execution. The macro environment is hostile to a $26,000 average-ticket consumer discretionary purchase requiring financing. CAF credit losses are accelerating into what may become a consumer recession.

The stock's 48% decline from 52-week highs partially reflects these challenges, but at 22x trailing GAAP EPS (or ~16x forward on declining estimates), it remains expensive relative to peers trading at 7-11x. Estimates have fallen further, the earnings miss in Q3 was severe, and the macro backdrop has intensified. Until the new CEO demonstrates measurable progress on both volume recovery and credit stability — neither of which is likely before fall 2026 at the earliest — the stock lacks a credible catalyst for sustained recovery.
