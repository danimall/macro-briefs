**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Texas Roadhouse is one of the few casual-dining operators still generating genuine traffic growth in a value-seeking consumer environment. Q1 2026 delivered same-store sales of +7.1% (traffic +4.5%, check +2.6%) and EPS of $1.87 (+9.6% YoY), with restaurant margin holding roughly flat (16.3% vs 16.6%) despite ~6.2% beef inflation. This reverses the FY2025 story, where 6.1% commodity inflation outran ~2% net pricing and collapsed restaurant margin from 17.1% to 15.5%, pushing net income down 6.5% and EPS down 5.8% to $6.10 even on +9.4% revenue. The Q1 result demonstrates the core thesis: when comps run high, volume-driven labor leverage can offset beef cost inflation without resorting to value-eroding price hikes.

The dominant variable for 2026-27 is exogenous and not fully in TXRH's control. The New World Screwworm outbreak has reached Texas for the first time in roughly 60 years, the US has closed its border to Mexican cattle, the domestic herd sits at a 75-year low, and ground beef hit a record +13% YoY ($7.06/lb) in May 2026. Beef is ~50% of TXRH's food costs, sourced from four suppliers, and only 45-65% of 2026 beef is locked. Management's 6-7% commodity inflation guide already assumes elevated beef; the screwworm shock raises the probability of the high end or worse. This is the swing factor behind the stock's drawdown from a 52-week high of $197 to $168 and the recent surge in put demand.

At ~26x forward EPS ($6.41 current-year consensus) with the dividend yielding only ~1.8%, the stock offers no obvious margin of safety against either the beef shock or the broader H2 consumer-spending risk facing the discretionary sector. The fundamentals are improving and the business is high-quality, but the asymmetric near-term risk on its single most important input keeps this at HOLD with a constructive growth bias. I would upgrade on confirmation that the margin recovery holds through Q2-Q3 against contained beef costs, or on a pullback that compresses the multiple below 22x.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1993 by the late W. Kent Taylor, Texas Roadhouse operated 822 system-wide restaurants as of Q1 2026 across three concepts: Texas Roadhouse (657 company + franchise units, ~$8.7M AUV), Bubba's 33 (56 company units, ~$6.3M AUV), and Jaggers (17, still nascent). The model is overwhelmingly company-operated (723 of 822 units), so commodity and labor inflation flow directly through the P&L rather than being buffered by asset-light royalties, which run under 0.5% of revenue.

The competitive moat rests on four pillars: a value-for-money positioning (generous portions, free peanuts and rolls, moderate price points) that has driven sustained traffic where peers rely on check growth; an owner-operator "managing partner / market partner" structure under which operators post refundable deposits and earn a share of restaurant pre-tax income, creating an ownership culture; dinner-focused operations (weekday dinner-only at most TR units) that maximize throughput; and deliberate avoidance of national advertising in favor of local-store marketing.

TXRH is also systematically reacquiring its franchise units (20 for $107.6M in 2025 and 5 more for $71.7M in Q1 2026), converting royalty streams into full P&L ownership. It holds contractual rights to acquire 32 of 37 remaining domestic franchise units at pre-set formulas. This is accretive but increases the company's direct exposure to the same commodity and labor cost lines.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth is robust and accelerating: FY2023 $4.63B → FY2024 $5.37B (which included a 53rd week worth +$114.7M) → FY2025 $5.88B (+9.4%). Q1 2026 revenue was $1.63B, up 12.8% YoY.

The central financial dynamic is margin. FY2025 net income fell to $405.6M and diluted EPS fell to $6.10 despite the revenue gain, because restaurant margin compressed 160bps on commodity inflation (beef) and 3.7% labor inflation against net menu pricing. Food and beverage costs rose to 35.0% of sales from 33.4%; operating margin fell from 9.6% to 8.1%. Roughly 4 points of the EPS decline is the lost 53rd week, so underlying momentum was healthier than the headline suggested.

Q1 2026 margin held nearly flat against essentially identical beef inflation because strong volume leverage lowered labor to 32.9% of sales from 33.3%. To-go reached 14.6% of sales. The difference between the FY2025 margin collapse and Q1 2026 stability was traffic acceleration, not pricing or beef relief.

Cash flow and the balance sheet are conservative. FY2025 operating cash flow was $730M; FCF was $342M (5.8% margin) after a record $388M of capex. Equity is $1.46B with essentially no long-term debt at year-end; the company drew $50M on its $450M revolver (expandable to $700M, maturing 2030) in Q1 2026 to fund franchise acquisitions while continuing buybacks and dividends, with OCF up 9% to $259M in the quarter. The screening metrics that look distressed (Altman Z 2.94, current ratio 0.5, D/E 1.42) are artifacts of the restaurant model. Sales are cash, receivables and inventory are minimal, and a large slice of current liabilities is deferred gift-card revenue (a favorable float, not a true obligation). Real leverage is near zero, and ROE of ~28% and ROA of ~12% are genuinely strong.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly: FY2025 returned $180M in dividends plus $150M in buybacks; the dividend was raised 10% to $0.75/quarter (over a decade of consecutive raises), and a $500M buyback authorized in February 2025 has $351.8M remaining. FY2026 capex is guided to ~$400M.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four reported quarters, including a -16.7% miss in Q4 2025. The Q1 2026 beat was modest (+1.0%). This argues for some caution on consensus visibility, even with the favorable margin trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm has three legs: unit growth (store-week growth guided 5-6% for 2026), comp growth (+7.1% in Q1 vs +4.9% for FY2025), and franchise reacquisition (accretive conversion of royalty to owned P&L). The traffic-led nature of the comp growth (+4.5% traffic) is the most important qualitative signal: it indicates real demand and brand health rather than price-masked declining visits, which is what several peers are showing.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating: current-year consensus is $6.41 (+2.2% over 90 days), next-year $7.65 (+0.1% over 90 days), current-quarter $1.82 (+5.5% over 90 days). This is steady single-digit upward drift consistent with a healthy but mature compounder, not the +30% revision velocity that flags a fundamental shift. The forward growth thesis is solid (high-single-digit revenue growth, EPS resuming growth after the FY2025 dip) but not explosive, and it is conditioned on beef costs not spiking beyond the guided 6-7%.

The structural growth runway remains long: the concept is under-penetrated nationally, Bubba's 33 and Jaggers provide secondary vehicles, and to-go/off-premise execution is improving as a defensive channel. The 1.9% Q2 2026 menu price increase is deliberately restrained against 6-7% commodity inflation, prioritizing traffic and value perception over near-term margin; this strategy builds long-term share but caps the pace of EPS recovery in a high-inflation year.

## Valuation Assessment

At $168 (current) against $6.41 current-year and $7.65 next-year consensus EPS, TXRH trades at ~26x current-year and ~22x next-year. The dividend yields ~1.8%. Peer medians in the restaurant set are distorted by high-growth concepts (CAVA 170x, BROS 103x, WING 38x) and low-growth value names (DPZ 18x, EAT 15.5x); the median P/E of 38.3x is not a clean comp. TXRH's premium to the value-end peers (DPZ, EAT) is justified by its superior traffic-led comp growth and unit economics; its discount to the growth-concept peers reflects its larger, more mature base.

The cycle-adjusted-valuation framework applies only weakly here. TXRH is not at a cyclical earnings trough: FY2025 EPS dipped 5.8% (partly optical from the lost 53rd week) and is already recovering, with Q1 +9.6%. Estimate revisions are positive but only +2.2% over 90 days, well short of the +30% threshold that would mandate a forward-only lens and override the multiple. So ~26x trailing/forward is a reasonable read of the actual multiple, not a trough artifact.

A low P/E here is not the issue; TXRH is not cheap. At 26x forward for a business growing EPS high-single to low-double digits (PEG roughly 2.5-3.0), the multiple is full for the growth on offer. There is no identifiable catalyst that the market is mispricing to the upside; if anything, the market is correctly pricing the beef-cost tail risk via the put-heavy options structure. The analyst mean target of $198 (median $200, range $168-234) implies ~18% upside, but that consensus largely predates full appreciation of the screwworm shock and the degrading sentiment trend in the analyst data (hold count remains high at 14 of 28). I treat the consensus target as optimistic rather than a floor: this is a quality compounder at a full multiple with a specific near-term margin risk.

Fair value on 23x next-year EPS ($7.65) is ~$176, roughly in line with the current price. That is the basis for a HOLD: the price is approximately fair, with the risk skewed to the downside on beef.

## Competitive Landscape

TXRH is the recognized share-gainer in casual-dining steakhouse. Its +4.5% Q1 traffic stands against peers like Bloomin' Brands' Outback, which shows check-driven (not traffic-driven) comps and weaker margins. Darden's LongHorn is the other share-gainer; both are winning the value-seeking diner with stronger value propositions. The owner-operator partner model and disciplined under-pricing relative to inflation reinforce the value moat and explain the traffic outperformance: TXRH is willing to accept margin compression in high-inflation years to protect traffic, a durable competitive choice even if it caps near-term earnings.

Competitive threats include fast-casual and QSR for the value diner, grocery (food-at-home) as beef inflation pushes menu prices up, and the GLP-1 consumer-preference shift that the 10-K explicitly names as a risk for a portion-centric steakhouse. There is no current evidence of GLP-1 impact (traffic is accelerating, not declining), but it is a structural watch item over a multi-year horizon.

The competitive trajectory is improving, which supports a 7.0 on competitive position: the company is gaining share, its value positioning is structurally advantaged in a value-seeking environment, and its traffic-led model is qualitatively healthier than its check-reliant peers.

## Risk Assessment

**Beef cost concentration is the dominant risk, and it is now amplified by an exogenous shock.** Only 45-65% of 2026 beef is locked. The screwworm outbreak, closed cattle border, and 75-year-low herd raise the probability that commodity inflation lands at or above the high end of the 6-7% guide. The Q1 2026 result shows TXRH can offset ~6% beef inflation via volume leverage; it does not prove the company can offset a 10%+ spike if the herd shortage and border closure persist into 2027. This is a specific, falsifiable downside catalyst and the principal reason for the HOLD rather than a BUY.

**Consumer Discretionary cyclical risk.** The broader environment is one of negative real wages, mid-market consumer cracking, and a meaningful probability of an H2 2026 consumer-spending cliff (housing wealth effect, rising claims, two-thirds of households cutting spending). TXRH's value positioning is relatively defensive within discretionary (trade-down behavior can benefit it), but a genuine consumer pullback would pressure traffic, the single variable that has been carrying margins.

**Geographic concentration.** ~21% of company restaurants are in Texas and Florida, the states most exposed to both screwworm and hurricane risk.

**Management/governance.** A dense cadence of executive-change 8-Ks reflects a leadership reshuffle. The CFO seat revolved through interim occupancy for much of 2023-2025 before Michael Lenihan joined in December 2025; the new hire is experienced, and CAO Humpich provides continuity, but the instability is a modest negative. Insider activity is routine post-vest selling with no open-market buying (neutral-to-slightly-cautious). A small related-party note (an officer holding 2% interests in two reacquired franchise units) is worth flagging but immaterial in scale.

**Mitigants:** the locked beef position cushions part of the spike, the balance sheet is near-debt-free with a fully available revolver, and the demonstrated ability to lever volume against beef inflation provides a buffer. The miss-prone recent earnings record (3 of 4) tempers confidence in consensus, however.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, near-dated stress. Near-term ATM IV is 64.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 28.3%, a 36-point spread that places IV in the 75th percentile of its own chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (64.5% near-term vs 31.0% three-month), and the put/call OI ratio has surged from 0.35 to 1.29 over the past month while the stock fell only 1.7%. Put/call skew of 73.2% confirms elevated put demand. This is consistent with the market hedging the screwworm/beef-cost overhang and diverges from a bullish fundamental read. Informed positioning is defensive into the near-term catalysts, and the rich near-term IV makes this a poor moment to chase the stock. It does not invalidate the longer-term quality thesis (three-month skew at 3.3% is not pricing structural collapse), but it argues against a BUY at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($210-234).** The Q1 margin recovery holds through 2026: traffic stays at mid-single digits, volume leverage continues to offset ~6-7% beef inflation, and EPS resumes high-single to low-double-digit growth toward $7.65+ in FY2027. Screwworm proves contained and manageable (Mizuho's defended view), Mexican beef exports partially backfill US supply, and the locked position cushions the worst of the spike. Franchise reacquisitions add accretive owned units. On 27-30x a recovering ~$7.65 next-year EPS, the stock re-rates toward $210-234, matching the high end of the analyst range. RBC's May 2026 upgrade to Outperform reflects this thesis.

**Bear Case ($130-145).** Screwworm and the herd shortage push beef inflation into double digits and beyond the 35-55% of the book that is unlocked, overwhelming volume leverage as it did in FY2025. A broader H2 2026 consumer cliff hits casual-dining traffic, removing the labor-leverage offset. Margin compresses another 150-200bps, EPS stalls or declines, and the multiple de-rates to ~20x on falling estimates, taking the stock to $130-145 (down ~14-23% from current). The miss-prone recent earnings record and the put-heavy options structure suggest this tail is being actively priced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The business is high-quality and the margin trough is likely behind it, but at ~26x forward with a specific, dated beef-cost shock on its single largest input and a Consumer Discretionary backdrop facing an H2 cliff, the risk/reward is approximately balanced. Fair value on 23x next-year EPS is ~$176, roughly the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 and Q3 2026 reports confirm restaurant margin holding flat-to-up YoY against contained commodity inflation (i.e., 6-7% guide not breached), demonstrating the Q1 recovery is durable rather than a one-quarter event.
- A pullback that compresses the forward multiple below ~22x (roughly $141 on current-year EPS) without fundamental deterioration, restoring a margin of safety.
- Confirmation that screwworm is contained and 2027 beef-cost inflation is decelerating (USDA herd data, border reopening, supplier contract renewals).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Commodity inflation guidance raised above 7%, or a reported quarter showing restaurant margin compressing more than 100bps YoY on beef.
- Comps decelerating below +3% (loss of the traffic-driven labor leverage that is carrying the model), or traffic turning negative.
- A guidance cut or a second consecutive double-digit EPS miss.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (early August 2026), which will show whether the Q1 margin stabilization persisted through a quarter of full screwworm-driven beef pressure and a 1.9% Q2 price increase. Monitor USDA cattle/beef data and border-status news in the interim.

## Conclusion

Texas Roadhouse is among the best-run operators in casual dining: traffic-led comps, a near-debt-free balance sheet, disciplined capital allocation, and a value moat that is structurally advantaged in a value-seeking consumer environment. The Q1 2026 margin recovery is genuine and reverses the FY2025 compression story. The scores have improved across financial health, growth, and risk over the past several reports, and that trajectory is supported by current data.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY is concrete and falsifiable. The screwworm outbreak, the depleted US herd, and record ground-beef prices create a specific, near-term risk to the single input that is ~50% of food costs and only 45-65% locked for 2026. The options market is pricing this directly via near-term IV and the surge in put open interest. At ~26x forward EPS with a ~1.8% yield, the stock offers no cushion against either that beef shock or the broader H2 consumer-spending risk facing the discretionary sector, and recent earnings have missed in 3 of the last 4 quarters. Fair value sits near the current price. I would turn buyer on confirmation the margin recovery holds against contained beef costs through Q2-Q3, or on a pullback below ~22x forward.
