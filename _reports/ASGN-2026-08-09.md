## Executive Summary

The security requested as ASGN no longer trades under that symbol. On April 24, 2026 the company legally changed its name from ASGN Incorporated to Everforth, Inc. and began trading as NYSE: EFOR (CIK unchanged at 890564). All operating and filing data below refer to the same entity. The authoritative price snapshot for this report failed validation (no official close was returned), so all arithmetic uses the daily-history close of **$31.57 for market date 2026-08-09** as the action basis. That figure implies a ~56% advance from the $20.20 price on the most recent insider Form 4 (June 11, 2026) and warrants re-verification against a live quote under the EFOR symbol before any transaction. If the true executable price is materially lower, near the low $20s where thirteen insiders bought in late April, the conclusion below inverts toward ACCUMULATE.

At $31.57 the arithmetic is unforgiving. Market capitalization is approximately $1.29B on 40.9M shares outstanding; net debt at 6/30/2026 was $1,298.9M, giving an enterprise value near **$2.59B**, or **6.4x** my TTM adjusted EBITDA build of $405M (6.2x on the ~$416M lender-defined figure implied by the covenant ratio) and **0.65x** TTM revenue of ~$3.97B. On forward adjusted EPS of roughly $3.00-3.30 the multiple is ~10x; on GAAP EPS, which is tracking near $1.05-1.20 for FY2026, it is ~26-30x. The spread between those two numbers is ~$58M of annual stock compensation, ~$70M of intangible amortization, and acquisition, integration and "strategic planning" expense that has now run for two consecutive years and is rising, not falling.

The operating trend has not turned. Reported revenue fell 1.3% in Q2 2026, but Quinnox ($290M purchase price, closed March 2026) is in the quarter and absent from the comparative; adjusting on a 2-3x revenue multiple puts organic decline at roughly 4-6%. Consolidated gross margin fell 40bps in Q2 and 70bps in H1, driven by a 120bps Commercial decline on collapsing permanent placement and unhedged Mexican peso delivery cost. GAAP operating income fell 31.0% to $41.1M and diluted EPS fell from $0.67 to $0.35. Federal funded backlog is $403.9M, down 25.7% from YE2023, on a 0.8x TTM book-to-bill. Against that, three things genuinely improved since my last review: the July 7 credit amendment upsized the revolver to $600M, extended maturity to July 2031 and retired term loan A; Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin of 9.6% came in above the company's own guidance with Q3 guided to 9.6-10.3%; and Commercial IT consulting book-to-bill was 1.4x. Those improvements justify moving from AVOID to REDUCE. They do not justify paying 6.4x EBITDA for a business whose EBITDA is falling and whose equity is roughly 50% of enterprise value.

## Company Overview

Everforth is a US-centric IT services business assembled by acquisition on a staffing foundation, consolidating six legacy brands (Apex Systems, Creative Circle, CyberCoders, ECS, GlideFast and TopBloc) under a single identity, with Quinnox added in March 2026. Two reportable segments:

**Commercial** — 70.1% of FY2025 revenue ($2,790.2M), split between Consulting ($1,290.1M) and Assignment, i.e. traditional staffing ($1,500.1M). Roughly 90% of Commercial revenue is time-and-materials. Delivery is predominantly US-based with two near-shore centers in Mexico and a growing India center.

**Federal Government** — 29.9% of FY2025 revenue ($1,190.2M), all consulting, across Defense & Intelligence ($526.0M), National Security ($310.0M), Federal Civilian ($224.9M) and Other ($129.3M). The segment holds roughly 1,000 cybersecurity consultants (~500 cleared) and ~900 cleared AI/data professionals.

The 10-K's differentiation claim is operationally specific and credible: the company does not carry a large bench for most IT consulting work, assembling teams instead from a proprietary talent database. That lowers fixed delivery cost in a downturn. It is also a thinner moat than proprietary IP, and the filing concedes the industry is "highly competitive and fragmented with limited barriers to entry."

One distinction matters for how the business is described. Everforth partners with AWS, Microsoft, Google, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday, CrowdStrike, Databricks and Snowflake to co-sell and co-deliver. It is an implementation and services partner to those vendors: TopBloc implements Workday software, GlideFast implements ServiceNow. The vendors are alliance counterparties supplying referrals, not customers; the paying customers are Everforth's enterprise and federal clients. The risk factors flag that losing an alliance causes "a direct and immediate loss of referrals."

Management's ~$680B addressable-market claim is an unsourced internal estimate. Revenue has declined for three consecutive years and four consecutive half-years against that alleged TAM, so it carries no analytical weight here.

Revenue is down 13.1% from 2022; operating income is down 43.8%. Margin is eroding roughly three times faster than revenue. The Q2 2026 operating income decline of 31.0% on a 1.3% revenue decline reflects severe operating deleverage: cost reductions are not keeping pace and corporate overhead is growing.

The FY2025 earnings decline decomposes cleanly against the segment footnote: Commercial operating income –$38.4M (286.5 → 248.1), Federal –$1.9M (95.2 → 93.3), corporate SG&A –$33.8M (77.3 → 111.1). The three sum exactly to the $74.1M consolidated decline. **Roughly 46% of the FY2025 profit decline came from corporate overhead, not from client-facing operations** — SG&A rose 43.7% in a year revenue fell 2.9%, including $26.5M of acquisition, integration and strategic-planning cost and a $4.4M write-off of capitalized software that will never be placed in service. That pressure intensified in H1 2026: corporate SG&A of $64.6M versus $56.7M, including $22.6M of acquisition/integration/strategic-planning expense versus $11.6M.

This is the crux of the bull case and I take it seriously: if these costs are genuinely transitional (a rebrand, two integrations and a back-office outsourcing transition running concurrently) then reversing $35-45M of annualized overhead adds roughly 100bps to operating margin. The problem is that they have now risen for two consecutive years and the filings warn the company "may fail to prevent the return of costs eliminated in these efforts." FY2027 corporate SG&A is the test.

Gross margin has turned negative, which is new. FY2025 was flat at 28.9%; Q2 2026 was 28.3% versus 28.7%, and H1 2026 was 27.9% versus 28.6%. Commercial drove it, down 120bps in H1, on lower permanent-placement contribution and Mexican peso strength. Permanent placement is the highest-margin, most cyclically sensitive piece of the portfolio and its weakness reads directly on client hiring confidence. The peso exposure is cost-side only (foreign operations were ~2% of revenue but ~6% of cost of services in H1) so peso appreciation compresses margin with no revenue offset and is structurally unhedged. Federal gross margin improved 30bps in H1 on portfolio management, achieved despite an adverse mix shift toward cost-reimbursable work (H1 cost-reimbursable revenue +13.8% to $203.0M; higher-margin T&M –12.8% to $221.6M).

For a serial acquirer of asset-light services businesses this is normal and not itself a solvency signal, but it removes any asset-value floor under the equity.

The disclosed covenant ratio of 2.17x at 6/30/2026 is **senior secured** debt to lender-defined TTM EBITDA and excludes the $550M of unsecured notes. Senior secured debt reconciles to $901.8M (revolver $318.0M + term loan A $97.5M + term loan B $486.3M), which matches exactly the $901.8M variable-rate balance in the interest-rate sensitivity disclosure. That implies lender EBITDA of ~$415.6M, and therefore total gross debt at **~3.49x** and net debt at **~3.13x**. My independent build cross-checks it: TTM adjusted EBITDA at 6/30/2026 ≈ $405.1M (FY2025 $422.6M less H1 2025 $197.8M plus H1 2026 $180.3M), with the ~$10M gap explained by lender-permitted pro-forma acquisition add-backs.

The July 7, 2026 amendment is constructive and, in my view, the most important development since my last review: the revolver went from $500M to $600M, maturity extended from February 2028 to July 2031, and term loan A was repaid and terminated. The trade-off is a tightening covenant schedule: maximum leverage stepping to 3.50x from the quarter ending June 30, 2027 and 3.25x from June 30, 2028. Holding senior secured debt at $901.8M, the 3.25x test breaches only if lender EBITDA falls below ~$277M, roughly 33% below current levels. That is real cushion, not imminent distress, and it is why the balance sheet no longer supports an AVOID.

What it does not solve is the **$550M of unsecured notes at 4.625% maturing in 2028**. Refinancing a ~3.1x-levered services borrower with declining revenue at prevailing spreads is likely to cost materially more. As a sensitivity: at 7.5% the incremental pre-tax interest is $15.8M, roughly $0.29 per share after tax at 24% on 41M shares; at 9%, $24.1M and ~$0.45 per share. Against H1 2026 diluted EPS of $0.48, either is material. I do not want to underwrite the assumption that 2028 spreads will be friendly for a revenue-declining, ~3x-levered borrower.

## Growth Analysis

**Reported stabilization is substantially purchased.** Neither filing discloses acquired-revenue contribution or ASC 805 pro-forma revenue. TopBloc ($340M, closed 3/4/2025) sat in Commercial consulting for ~10 months of FY2025; at a 2-3x revenue multiple that implies roughly $94-142M of FY2025 revenue against the +$161.9M reported increase in Commercial consulting, leaving organic growth of roughly +2% to +6% versus the +14.4% reported, and consolidated FY2025 organic revenue nearer **–5% to –6%** versus –2.9% reported. The same arithmetic on Quinnox ($290M, closed March 2026) puts Q2 2026 organic revenue down roughly **4-6%** versus the 1.3% reported. Corroborating detail: four of five Commercial industries declined in Q2 and only TMT grew (+$7.7M). The multiples are my estimate, not disclosed; platform consultancies can transact above 3x, which would make the acquired revenue smaller and the organic decline milder, while the absence of pro-forma disclosure suggests management judged the deals immaterial to revenue, arguing the low end. This is the highest-priority verification gap in the file.

**Commercial bookings are the genuine positive.** Commercial IT consulting book-to-bill was 1.4x in Q2 2026 (1.3x in Q2 2025) and 1.2x TTM; FY2025 Commercial consulting bookings were $1,522.8M versus $1,281.3M (+18.9%). Consulting is now ~50% of Commercial revenue. The caveats come from the company: underlying contracts are terminable on short notice with little penalty, bookings involve estimates, there are no independent standards governing the calculation, and there is no assurance they convert. Bookings are the right leading indicator to track; they are self-defined and unauditable.

**Federal is contracting and the forward indicators are worse than revenue.** Funded backlog: $543.5M (YE2023) → $529.0M → $492.9M → **$403.9M** (6/30/2026), down 25.7% from 2023. Total contract backlog $2,670.7M, –8.7% year over year. TTM awards $926.2M, down 32%. TTM book-to-bill 0.8x. Q2 revenue confirms it (DoD/Intel –$9.0M, Federal Civilian –$6.5M, National Security +$6.9M). Definitional discipline matters: contract backlog includes unexercised options and unfunded negotiated awards, only 15% is funded, contracts are accounted for as short-term so no remaining performance obligation is disclosed, and contract liabilities were just $41.6M. This backlog is not RPO and not a government obligation.

Management's claim that federal work provides countercyclical support did not hold in this cycle: Federal revenue declined in all three years (1,276.2 → 1,231.0 → 1,190.2) because the shock was political (DOGE contract losses, a shutdown) rather than economic. Peers faced the same disruption; a 0.8x book-to-bill says Everforth is not taking share through it.

**Secondary-sourced guidance:** management reported Q2 revenue above $1 billion and adjusted EBITDA margin of 9.6%, both above the high end of guidance, and guided Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin to 9.6%-10.3% on roughly $1.01B of revenue. I could not retrieve the press release, so the "beat" framing is externally sourced and compares non-GAAP results to non-GAAP guidance. I can reconcile the 9.6% margin arithmetically from the 10-Q ($41.1M operating income + $13.2M segment D&A + $17.3M intangible amortization + $14.6M stock compensation + $9.8M acquisition/integration = $96.0M, 9.53% of revenue, with corporate depreciation bridging). The add-back stack is ~$54.9M per quarter, making adjusted EBITDA 2.3x GAAP operating income, and $14.6M of it (~$58M annualized, ~1.5% of revenue) is stock compensation, a real cost to shareholders.

Also structural: the Work Opportunity Tax Credit was not renewed, driving the H1 effective tax rate to 37.1%. It was worth $1.7M annually in each of 2023-2025, small in absolute terms but permanent for a large-scale employer of contract labor.

## Valuation Assessment

Build, at the $31.57 basis:

- Shares outstanding 40.9M (diluted 41.0M) → market capitalization ~**$1.29B**
- Net debt 6/30/2026 → **$1,298.9M**
- Enterprise value → **~$2.59B**
- TTM adjusted EBITDA $405.1M → **6.4x** (6.2x on lender-defined ~$416M)
- Adjusted EBITDA less stock compensation (~$347M) → **7.5x**
- TTM revenue ~$3.97B → **0.65x**
- FY2026 GAAP EPS tracking ~$1.05-1.20 → **~26-30x**
- FY2026 adjusted EPS ~$3.00-3.30 (H1 adjusted ≈ $1.50 grossed for H2 seasonality) → **~10x**
- FY2026 FCF $130-235M on $1.29B equity → **10-18% equity FCF yield**

The 10x adjusted P/E and double-digit FCF yield are why this security has bid. Two offsets keep me from treating cheapness as a buy signal. First, the equity is only ~50% of enterprise value, so the FCF yield is a leverage artifact: unlevered FCF plus after-tax interest of roughly $190-290M on a $2.59B EV is a 7-11% unlevered yield, respectable but not extraordinary for a business shedding 5% of organic revenue a year. Second, leverage cuts both ways in the multiple. At ~3.2x net debt to EBITDA, a 10% EBITDA decline at a constant multiple takes the equity down roughly 26%. That is the mechanism that produced the ~60% drawdown from January to April, and nothing about the capital structure has changed it.

Scenario framework (EV/EBITDA on forward EBITDA, less net debt, on ~41.3M shares):

- **Bull $46** — 7.25x on $440M EBITDA. Requires corporate SG&A reversing $35-45M in FY2027, Commercial consulting bookings converting to revenue growth, gross margin stabilizing, and the buyback resuming against a $922.5M authorization on a $1.29B cap. +46%.
- **Base $29** — 6.25x on $395M EBITDA, roughly flat to modestly down, federal continuing to shrink and Commercial offsetting. **–8% from spot.**
- **Bear $19.50** — 5.75x on $360M EBITDA if organic decline persists at 5-6%, permanent placement stays depressed, and the 2028 notes reprice at 7.5-9% (a $0.29-0.45 EPS hit). –38%.

Probability-weighting 25% / 45% / 30% gives roughly $29.65, about 6% below spot with a fat left tail. That is the definition of a REDUCE: negative expected return and unfavorable asymmetry.

Peer context is unhelpful in the direction bulls would like. The staffing and IT services complex is in an extended downcycle, and legacy IT services names are derating on AI-disintermediation risk rather than re-rating on it. A 6.4x EBITDA multiple is a discount to Everforth's own history of 8-10x, but that history belongs to a company with growing revenue, 8-9% operating margins and 2x leverage. Applying pre-downcycle multiples to a post-downcycle business is the standard error in this sector. ## Competitive Landscape

Everforth sits between two better-defended cohorts. Above it are global systems integrators and offshore-heavy IT services firms (Accenture, Infosys, TCS, Cognizant) with far larger delivery pyramids, deeper industry IP and scale in AI transformation programs; Everforth's offshore footprint is a growing India center plus two Mexico near-shore sites, with foreign operations at only ~6% of cost of services. Quinnox, which brings India-based digital engineering, is explicitly designed to close that gap, and the strategic logic is sound. Below it are pure-play staffing firms carrying less overhead. The declared strategy is migration from the second cohort toward the first; the bookings data suggest partial progress, from a shrinking base and with corporate cost rising.

The federal franchise is the genuine differentiator. Roughly 500 cleared cybersecurity consultants, ~900 cleared AI/data professionals, 1,400+ certifications and facility clearances cannot be replicated quickly. The offsetting reality is that this asset shrank through a political shock and is winning at 0.8x book-to-bill; the relevant peer set (CACI, Booz Allen, Leidos, SAIC, ICF) faced the same DOGE disruption, and a 0.8x TTM book-to-bill suggests Everforth is not winning share through it.

On diversification, one distinction is worth holding firmly. The industry mix is genuinely balanced (H1 2026 Commercial: $454.8M Consumer & Industrial, $282.9M TMT, $259.3M Financial Services, $233.0M Healthcare, $147.2M Business Services, the largest ~23% of consolidated revenue). But counterparty concentration in the federal government is flat to slightly worse: 28.7% (2023), 30.0% (2024), 29.9% (2025), 30.3% (H1 2026), with direct prime contracts at 26% of FY2025 revenue and DoD/Intelligence alone at 13.2%. The consulting mix shift changes what the company sells; it has not reduced who pays.

## Risk Assessment

**AI as demand destruction.** The risk factor is unusually candid: clients "may develop their own internal AI-related capabilities, which could lead to reduced demand for our services or solutions," and separately, if AI value fails to materialize at scale, clients may cut AI spending. For a business billing hours of human technical labor, client-side AI productivity is a direct volume threat, and the 27.8% two-year collapse in Assignment revenue (2,078.9 → 1,500.1) is at minimum consistent with it. This is the risk I regard as least priced, and it is the same mechanism repricing seat-based software and legacy IT services more broadly.

**Impairment.** $2,280.4M of goodwill and $305.2M of indefinite-lived trademarks sit against equity market value of ~$1.29B. Deloitte named the Creative Circle trademark its sole Critical Audit Matter, citing subjective judgment, fair-value specialist involvement and "the recent financial performance," Creative Circle being the most discretionary, most cyclical brand in the portfolio. For the 2025 test the company performed only a qualitative assessment of both goodwill and trademarks. Its own policy lists "a substantial sustained decline in the market capitalization of our stock" as a triggering event, and the shares fell ~60% from the January 2026 grant price of $46.66 to $20.20 in June, yet the Q2 10-Q states there were no fair-value adjustments for non-financial assets in H1. An impairment at or before the October 31, 2026 annual test is a live possibility. Three necessary qualifications: goodwill is tested at reporting-unit level on discounted cash flows and market approaches, not against market capitalization; the charge would be non-cash, would not consume cash, and would not breach the leverage covenant since lender EBITDA excludes it; and the recovery in the shares since June reduces the triggering-event pressure. I flag elevated probability, not inevitability, and I draw no inference about intent from the choice of a qualitative test.

**Refinancing.** $550M at 4.625% due 2028, against a 5.3% weighted-average cash interest rate in H1 2026. Quantified above at $0.29-0.45 per share.

**Covenant path.** Ample headroom today (breach requires lender EBITDA below ~$277M, ~33% down), but the ratio tightens to 3.50x from Q2 2027 and 3.25x from Q2 2028 while EBITDA is falling. The amendment simultaneously improved liquidity and maturity, which is what a confident lender group does; the step-down may be standard for the vintage rather than borrower-specific.

**Single-vendor outsourcing dependency.** A newly disclosed risk factor states the company depends on "a single third-party vendor relationship" for outsourced back-office functions, with $217.2M of non-cancelable purchase obligations ($81.3M due 2026, $52.0M 2027, $44.1M 2028, $26.0M 2029, $13.8M 2030). The filing states reduced control over quality and the possibility that "unexpected costs or failure to achieve projected savings may negate the intended benefits." This is the engine of the promised cost-out and the likely source of any FY2027 SG&A relief; it is also a large fixed commitment layered onto declining revenue, running against the flexible-cost-structure advantage management touts. The vendor is unnamed.

**Rebrand execution.** Six brands with decades of client and agency recognition (Apex Systems and ECS in particular) are being folded into a name with no market history, while $305.2M of indefinite-lived trademark value is predicated on the legacy names. The risk factors do not address brand-equity loss from the rebrand, which is a notable omission.

**Governance and legal.** A classified board, board-only vacancy filling, advance-notice provisions and 1.0M authorized blank-check preferred shares materially impede any activist or strategic outcome for a company at ~6x EBITDA with negative tangible book. Standing wage-and-hour collective and PAGA actions are endemic to employer-of-record staffing; none is quantified.

**Data-integrity risk specific to this report.** The valuation snapshot failed validation on six checks, including a missing authoritative price and market timestamp. The ticker migration from ASGN to EFOR is a plausible cause of feed breakage. The $31.57 basis should be re-verified before acting.

## Investment Thesis

## Conclusion

Base case sits below spot; the bear case is 38% lower and is reached by extending the trend of the last four half-years.
