## Executive Summary

Molson Coors is a cash-generative, investment-grade brewer priced by the market for structural decline, and the FY2025 and H1 2026 filings largely justify that pricing. Consolidated financial volume has fallen from 83.772m hectolitres in 2023 to 72.810m in 2025 (−13.1%), with a further −4.4% in the first half of 2026. The FY2025 GAAP loss of $2,139.6M (−$10.75 per diluted share) was overwhelmingly non-cash — $3,645.7M of Americas goodwill impairment plus $273.9M of brand impairments and $28.7M of restructuring — and operating cash flow held at $1,784.4M with free cash flow of $1,067.8M. Adding the identified charges back to the reported $(2,336.9)M operating loss gives roughly $1,611M of adjusted operating income, down about 8% from FY2024's $1,753.2M. Solvency is not the question here.

The question is whether the earning power that management wrote down has stabilized. Q2 2026 gives a split answer. Volume decline moderated to −5.4% from FY2025's −8.6%, non-GAAP EPS of $1.58 beat the $1.52 consensus by 3.7%, and full-year guidance was reaffirmed. But net sales fell 3.3% [unverified — Q1 2026 standalone net sales were not isolated in the sources; the six-month figure was net sales $5,447.6M (−1.0%)], price/mix contribution compressed to +1.8% from +3.8% in FY2025 with EMEA&APAC premiumization partly offset by increased promotional activity, reported gross margin fell 570bp to 34.3%, and EMEA&APAC posted a six-month pretax loss of $13.8M against +$45.6M a year earlier. Roughly 45% of the Q2 margin collapse is a $98.0M unrealized commodity mark-to-market swing sitting in the Unallocated segment; excluding it, adjusted gross margin was ~37.5% versus ~39.8% — a roughly 230bp underlying contraction driven by ~$40M of Midwest Premium aluminum surcharge, materials and logistics inflation, adverse premiumization mix, and volume deleverage.

At 8.9x FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.75, 8.6x FY2027's $4.92, ~5.5x EV to adjusted EBITDA, and a 4.56% dividend covered ~3.0x by free cash flow, the absolute yield support is real. It is also roughly where the rest of the structurally challenged staples cohort trades — Campbell's at 11.1x with a 6.9% yield, Conagra at a 4.7% yield with its own impairment-driven negative P/E. The discount reflects cohort membership rather than isolated mispricing. Probability-weighting a $45.50 base, $58 bull and $33 bear against $42.13 plus the $1.92 dividend yields roughly a 5-10% expected total return over twelve months — approximately fair, with a wide distribution and a fat left tail. HOLD, held for the covered yield rather than for an appreciation thesis.

## Company Overview

Molson Coors operates two reporting segments. Americas (U.S., Canada, LatAm) generated $8,712.8M of FY2025 net sales on 53.507m hl, roughly 78% of consolidated net sales, and posted a $(2,343.6)M pretax loss after impairment. EMEA&APAC generated $2,455.7M on 19.310m hl and a $(13.1)M pretax loss. Consolidated FY2025 net sales were $11,140.8M, down 4.2%.

Brand concentration is unusually high for a company of this size. Item 1A discloses that Coors Light and Miller Lite together represented approximately 55% of Americas segment net sales in 2025, and that Carling, Staropramen, Coors, Madrí, Ožujsko and Bergenbier were roughly 45% of EMEA&APAC net sales. Both flagship U.S. brands sit in light lager, the segment where the filing acknowledges "lower share performance."

A material portion of the portfolio consists of licensing and distribution rights rather than owned trademarks: Simply Spiked and Topo Chico Hard Seltzer under authorization from Red Tree Beverages, LLC, Fever-Tree under license from Fevertree Drinks plc (alongside an $88.1M equity stake now carried at $116.9M), Heineken brands in Canada, and Beck's, Stella Artois, Löwenbräu, Hoegaarden, Leffe and Corona under arrangements with AB InBev in Central Europe. ZOA Energy is a 51% consolidated stake. Non-renewal of these arrangements is disclosed as a material adverse risk.

Structural assets include consolidated packaging joint ventures with Ball Corporation and Owens-Brockway ($223.4M of assets at June 30, 2026), equity-method interests in the Canadian distribution JVs Brewers Retail Inc. ("The Beer Store") and Brewers Distributor Ltd. ($145.3M combined), and protected U.S. distributor territories under the three-tier system.

Control sits with two family blocs. Pentland Securities (Molson) and the Adolph Coors, Jr. Trust together hold more than 90% of Class A common and Class A exchangeable shares under a combined voting trust. If the two blocs disagree on any stockholder matter other than director elections, the trustees are required to vote all deposited Class A shares against the matter, with no deadlock-resolution mechanism. Certain transformational actions additionally require two-thirds Board authorization. Public Class B holders own economics without any path to force a control event.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue architecture.** The FY2025 bridge was volume −8.6%, price/sales mix +3.8%, currency +0.6%, netting to −4.2%, with net sales per hectolitre up 4.8%. Roughly 3 percentage points of the Americas volume decline came from the deliberate exit of contract brewing, so underlying branded decline was closer to −6%. For the first half of 2026: net sales $5,447.6M (−1.0%), volume −4.4%, price/mix +2.3%, currency +1.1%. Q2 alone: net sales $3,096.5M (−3.3%), volume −5.4%, price/mix +1.8%.

The pricing deterioration matters more than the volume moderation. Volume decline improving from −8.6% to −5.4% is progress. Price/mix falling from +3.8% to +1.8% removes the mechanism that was offsetting it. Management's stated plan of a 1-2% 2026 price increase is roughly equal to the ~$130M of guided Midwest Premium cost measured against $8,712.8M of Americas net sales (~1.5%).

**Margins.**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $11,627.0M | $11,140.8M | $5,504.9M | $5,447.6M | $3,200.8M | $3,096.5M |
| Gross profit | $4,533.4M | $4,274.6M | $2,132.8M | $1,960.5M | $1,281.9M | $1,063.3M |

Q2 COGS per hectolitre rose 12.1%. The decomposition is decision-relevant: the Unallocated segment's cost line swung from +$7.0M to −$91.0M on unrealized commodity derivative positions, a $98.0M adverse move that MCBC's stated policy reclassifies to the exposed segment when realized. Adding it back gives Q2 adjusted gross margin of ~37.5% (derived: (1,063.3 + 98.0) ÷ 3,096.5) against ~39.8% adjusted prior year. The unrealized loss reflects adverse forward curves on aluminum, Midwest Premium and diesel that will convert into realized segment cost, but it pulls economics forward rather than creating them. The six-month picture, where the mark-to-market swing was only $27.5M against a 270bp margin decline, is the better read on genuine compression.

**Cash generation.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $1,784.4M was down $125.9M, with management citing lower non-cash-adjusted net income, a $60.6M final Keystone litigation payment, and higher interest paid, offset by ~$80M of lower cash taxes. Capex paid was $716.6M, giving FY2025 FCF of $1,067.8M — 9.6% of net sales. First-half 2026 OCF of $820.4M less $335.2M of capex gives $485.2M, but that includes the $107.5M swap settlement receipt and cycles the prior-year Keystone payment. Stripping the swap gain leaves ~$378M of H1 FCF. A normalized full-year 2026 figure meaningfully below FY2025's $1,067.8M is a reasonable planning assumption once the H2 interest step-up and the back-half weighting of Midwest Premium cost are absorbed.

**Balance sheet and the refinancing.** FY2025 gross debt of $6,299.5M less $896.5M cash gives net debt of ~$5,403M, or ~2.33x my adjusted EBITDA estimate of $2,323M — comfortably inside the 4.00x revolver covenant, though the covenant definition itself was not available. The FY2025 10-K disclosed $2,424.7M of 2026 maturities with explicit "no assurance" refinancing language. That contingency was cleared: on May 27, 2026 MCBC issued $500M of 4.9% notes due 2031, $1.0bn of 5.5% notes due 2036, and CAD 500M of 4.3% notes due 2033; the CAD 500M 3.44% notes were repaid June 18 and the $2.0bn 3.0% notes at maturity July 15.

The coupon step-up is real but modest in aggregate. Retired USD coupons totalled $60.0M annually on the $2.0bn 3.0% notes; new USD coupons total $79.5M ($55.0M + $24.5M), less ~$10.8M of annual amortization benefit from the $107.5M realized swap gain, and against ~$500M less gross debt outstanding since ~$500M of the repayment was funded from cash. On a like-for-like rate basis the refinanced USD stack carries ~220–250bp higher coupons. The full interest drag is visible in H2 2026 and 2027 but not thesis-determining. Ratings are unchanged at BBB/Baa1/BBB, all stable, with ~$1.8bn of revolver capacity available after ~$0.2bn of commercial paper at August 6.

The Altman Z of 1.24 and Piotroski of 2 that appear on screens are artifacts. EBIT/assets of −0.103 becomes +0.071 using adjusted operating income; working capital/assets of −0.104 reflects $2,434.1M of soon-refinanced debt sitting in current liabilities at the balance sheet date. The screens are wrong about bankruptcy risk and directionally right that liquidity buffers are thin: a negative-working-capital staples model with a 0.55 year-end current ratio depends on uninterrupted cash conversion.

**Capital allocation.** Weighted diluted shares fell from 217.3M (2023) to 199.1M (2025) to 187.7M in Q2 2026. Total economic share count is ~186.45M including Class A common, Class B common, and the Canadian exchangeable shares. The Board increased the Class B repurchase authorization by $2.0bn to $4.0bn on February 9, 2026 and extended it to 2031, leaving $2,352.6M of capacity. Execution moved the other way: Q2 2026 repurchases were $42.8M (1,016,224 shares at an average $41.77) against $249.7M in Q2 2025, with monthly average prices declining through the quarter from $43.75 to $39.91. Competing uses in the same six months were the $275M Monaco acquisition and ~$500M of cash-funded debt repayment; a blackout around the May issuance is also a plausible explanation. Whatever the cause, the company bought fewer shares at lower prices, which is the inverse of value-accretive timing. The quarterly dividend rose from $0.47 to $0.48, annualizing to ~$358M on economic shares.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis in the filings. Volume declined in every region in FY2025 and again in H1 2026: U.S. core and value brands, the U.K., and Central and Eastern Europe. Cumulative volume decline is 13.1% over two years with a further mid-single-digit decline in progress. Item 1A concedes that "most of our major markets are mature, so growth opportunities may be more limited to us than to our global competitors who may already be in higher growth or emerging markets" — the company describing itself as geographically constrained relative to peers.

The substitution set named in the risk factors is broad: above-premium flavored malt beverages, RTDs and spirits-based drinks; non-alcoholic; health-and-wellness trends "including the use of glucagon-like peptide (GLP-1) agonists"; and explicitly legal cannabis, delta-9, hemp derivatives and online gambling as competing claims on discretionary spend. The asymmetry is that MCBC must win as a challenger in above-premium and RTD while defending an incumbent position in light lager where it is losing share.

The beyond-beer build-out is serial, sub-scale and has one completed failure. Blue Run Spirits, acquired in Q3 2023, was fully impaired for $75.3M in Q3 2025 — roughly two years — with the 10-K citing a challenging macro environment for full-strength spirits. Fever-Tree carried ~$30M of 2025 integration fees management describes as recoverable over three years. Monaco Cocktails (Atomic Brands) closed April 1, 2026 for $275M, allocated ~$65M to a 15-year brand intangible and ~$200M to goodwill, with no pro forma presented on materiality grounds. A ~73% goodwill allocation flowing into the Americas reporting unit that management says has under 15% impairment cushion raises the stakes on integration. A further unnamed Americas brand was exited in Q2 2026, triggering $8.1M of accelerated amortization.

Consensus captures this: FY2026 $4.75 and FY2027 $4.92, implying 3.6% EPS growth driven by buybacks and cost savings rather than revenue. Revision momentum is mildly negative — current quarter −2.1% over 30 days, next year −1.3% over 30 days and −1.9% over 90 days. This is the opposite of the estimate-revision pattern that would justify paying up for a cyclical trough.

The genuine offset is cost. Footprint rationalization is sustained and real: contract brewing exits, craft divestitures, the Chippewa Falls and 10th Street Milwaukee closures, the Americas Restructuring Plan (cumulative $33.8M, substantially complete at the low end of the $35-50M range), a new Americas supply-chain program ($10.1M of accelerated depreciation in H1 with $10-15M more expected), and an EMEA&APAC program including a small U.K. brewery closure by end-2026. The corollary risk is disclosed: excess capacity from either efficiency gains or continued volume decline results in asset under-utilization.

That supports a valuation score of 5.5: genuine absolute yield support, no identified catalyst for re-rating, and a peer cohort that says the discount is earned.

## Competitive Landscape

The category is contracting in absolute terms, not merely rotating share. The FY2025 MD&A cites "increased consumer and economic uncertainty" and macroeconomic pressure that "has resulted in heightened competitive activity and associated reduction in market share of our products in certain segments." The Q2 2026 10-Q escalates slightly to "lower consumer confidence and heightened competitive activity resulting in market share reductions of our products in certain regions and segments."

MCBC's own disclosures describe a relative disadvantage in three places. On scale: competitors "may have greater financial, marketing, production and distribution resources" and may be "more diverse in terms of their geographies and brand portfolios." On geography: mature markets only, with no exposure to growth beer markets. On route-to-market: "Our unique portfolio may require more brand building than our competitors, and distributors may not invest the time and other resources to effectively support these brands." That last admission, in a system where nearly all U.S. volume moves through independent distributors with statutorily protected exclusive territories, is a candid statement of structural weakness.

I obtained no quantified third-party share data for Coors Light or Miller Lite. The competitive assessment therefore rests on MCBC's own volume series and its qualitative admissions, which is a genuine limitation. Customer concentration is stable and low — no customer exceeded 10% of consolidated net sales in Q2 2026 or Q2 2025, and the largest Americas customer was flat at ~$0.6bn of six-month net sales in both years, which is roughly 11% of a shrinking base. Mix shift toward beyond-beer does not by itself constitute customer diversification, and the single-customer dollar figure has not moved.

Channel structure is changing in Canada. Under Ontario's Early Implementation Agreement, licensed beer, wine and RTD sales expanded to all convenience stores from September 2024 and eligible grocery and big-box stores from October 2024. Store-count floors protecting The Beer Store expired, and from January 1, 2026 TBS has "sole and absolute discretion" to close locations. MCBC holds an equity-method interest in BRI/TBS. A new Ontario wholesale markup structure was expected to be announced in Q1 2026 for April 1, 2026 effect; the Q2 10-Q says only that management "continue[s] to evaluate the impact," leaving the outcome unresolved.

## Risk Assessment

**Second Americas goodwill impairment.** The highest-signal disclosure in the filings. After the $3,645.7M write-down, management states the Americas reporting unit's fair value exceeds carrying value by less than 15% and the unit "continues to be at a heightened risk of future impairment," with the August 31, 2025 test using a discount rate that included an additional risk premium "assessed on the reporting unit based on the current industry environment." Management further concedes that the growth targets embedded in the forecast "were inherently at risk given that the strategies are still in progress." Remaining Americas goodwill is $2,144.8M after Monaco added $202.8M; cumulative Americas impairment is $5,159.0M, and EMEA&APAC goodwill was written to zero in 2020. The annual test date is August 31, with results disclosed in the Q3 10-Q. A second charge would be non-cash but would confirm that the long-range plan supporting the current carrying value did not hold.

**Midwest Premium.** A tariff-derived regional aluminum delivery surcharge, escalating from ~$35M in FY2025 to ~$40M in Q2 2026 alone, ~$70M in H1, and ~$130M guided for full-year 2026 — a ~3.7x increase. Management states hedging is "costly" because of "opaque pricing and limited liquidity." This is policy-contingent and mean-reverted after the 2018 imposition and partial 2019 lifting, so it is not necessarily a permanent margin reset, but it is not under management's control.

**Regulatory escalation on alcohol.** The filing catalogues the EU's Europe Beating Cancer Plan contemplating mandatory warnings, the January 2025 U.S. Surgeon General advisory recommending a cancer-risk label, proposed Canadian warning and standard-drink bills, and enacted Irish legislation, noting advocates "are becoming increasingly organized and coordinated on a global basis."

**ERP implementation.** A newly added risk factor. The multi-year global ERP replacement began in Q3 2025, with Item 1A warning of possible delays and cost overruns and impairment of forecasting, manufacturing, procurement, order fulfillment, revenue recognition, receivables collection and internal controls, plus elevated cyber exposure from running legacy and new systems in parallel. Given the March 2021 cybersecurity incident that the filing repeatedly cites as having caused production, shipment and financial-reporting delays, this carries weight. Controls were effective at June 30, 2026.

**EMEA&APAC deterioration.** Six-month pretax loss of $13.8M versus +$45.6M, a $59.4M adverse swing, of which $20.3M is newly initiated restructuring charges that are non-recurring by design. Staropramen was partially impaired $198.6M and reclassified from indefinite-lived to a 50-year definite life, with management citing "prolonged weakness in consumer demand... expected to continue into the future." H1 is seasonally weak and Q2 standalone remained profitable at $37.9M pretax, so the full-year picture will differ.

**Labor and unread filings.** Approximately 27% of Americas and 25% of EMEA&APAC workforces are union or works-council represented, with the 2022 Montréal/Longueuil and 2024 Fort Worth strikes cited as having materially hurt results. Two 8-Ks — May 22, 2026 (Item 8.01) and June 24, 2026 (Item 5.02, departure or election of officers/directors) — were identified but not read. An unread executive-change filing about eight months after the CEO transition completed in October 2025 is an open item, as are the DEF 14A compensation structure and the non-GAAP reconciliation bridging Q2 GAAP EPS of $1.23 to the externally reported $1.58.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating the bearish positioning visible elsewhere. Near-term ATM IV of 30.7% sits modestly above 27.7% one-year realized vol, at only the 24th percentile within the current chain, and has fallen 6.4 points from 37.1% on July 22 — the standard post-earnings vol crush. Term structure is in contango (30.7% near, 33.5% at six months), with no near-term dislocation priced. Skew is call-heavy at −4.7% on the front week, put/call volume is 0.11, and put/call open interest fell from 1.33 to 1.04.

That sits awkwardly against 25.9% short interest and 9.0 days to cover, plus three strong-sell ratings among 21 analysts. Two readings are available and I cannot distinguish them from the data: either the short base is expressing structural conviction the options market has not joined, or a meaningful portion of reported short interest is technical — the Canadian exchangeable shares (TSX: TPX.A/TPX.B) carry substantially equivalent economics and voting rights to the U.S. Class A/B shares and support a cross-listing arbitrage that shows up as short interest without directional view. For a $7.9bn staples name, 25.9% is extraordinary on a purely fundamental interpretation. Practical implication for a long: the six-month implied move of ±16.2% is cheap optionality relative to the scenario spread, and any evidence of volume stabilization would meet a crowded short base with 9 days to cover.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($58, +37.7%)

The FY2025 loss was ~$3.9bn of non-cash charges against $1,784.4M of operating cash flow. The 2026 maturity wall that the 10-K flagged with "no assurance" language was pre-funded three months later on investment-grade terms, with an eight-year-old forward-starting swap paying off $107.5M to cushion the coupon step-up. Volume decline is moderating (−8.6% → −5.4%), footprint rationalization is genuine and sustained, and roughly 45% of the Q2 gross-margin print is derivative mark-to-market that reverses into segment results. Midwest Premium is a tariff artifact that mean-reverted after 2019 and could do so again without any management action, removing ~$130M of cost. At 8.9x forward earnings with a 4.56% dividend covered ~3.0x and $2,352.6M of buyback authorization running to 2031, flat EBITDA plus 5-6% annual share shrinkage produces a low-double-digit total return without any re-rating. A 25.9% short base with 9 days to cover amplifies any positive volume surprise.

### Bear Case ($33, −21.7%)

Management, using independent valuation specialists and its own updated long-range plan, concluded the Americas business is worth $3.6bn less than carried and that the remainder has under 15% cushion. The mechanism that was offsetting volume decline is failing: price/mix contribution halved from +3.8% to +1.8%, with promotional activity explicitly cited in EMEA&APAC. The announced 1-2% price increase is consumed entirely by one input cost line. Substitution pressure is multi-directional and named in the filings — RTDs, spirits, non-alcoholic, GLP-1 agonists, cannabis and online gambling — and MCBC must win as a challenger in the growing segments while defending share it is losing in light lager. The beyond-beer record already contains a full write-off within two years of purchase, and Monaco put ~73% of its consideration into goodwill inside the impairment-sensitive unit. Estimate revisions are negative across every window. The dual-class voting trust means no bidder can arbitrage the cheap multiple. If volumes re-accelerate downward, the same impairment logic recurs, and the screen that flagged distress will have been early rather than wrong.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income).** Existing holders are compensated by a 4.56% dividend covered ~3.0x by FY2025 free cash flow, with the maturity wall cleared and ratings stable. Prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal: base-case appreciation of 8.0% against bear-case downside of 21.7% is not adequate margin of safety, and the expected return is dominated by the coupon rather than by any identified re-rating catalyst.

This is the third consecutive HOLD following two AVOIDs, and the overall score has drifted from 5.2 in June to 4.8 in July to 4.7 now. The Q2 print is what keeps this from being a downgrade: a 3.7% non-GAAP beat, reaffirmed full-year guidance, moderating volume decline, and a completed refinancing that removed a disclosed contingency. What keeps it from being an upgrade is that Q2 also delivered a net sales decline of 3.3%, price/mix compression to +1.8%, a six-month EMEA&APAC pretax loss, and negative estimate revisions. The price is essentially unchanged from the prior review ($41.04 → $42.13), so the risk/reward is unchanged as well.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Americas volume decline better than −3% for two consecutive quarters *with* price/mix at or above +2.5% — the combination, not either alone, would establish revenue stabilization.
2. Buyback execution returning to $200M+ per quarter at prices below $45, demonstrating that management's own valuation view matches the disclosed authorization.
3. Evidence the Midwest Premium is reversing — either U.S. aluminum trade policy change or Q4 COGS/hl inflation below +4% — removing $100M+ from the 2027 cost base.
4. Price at $34-36, which would push the dividend yield above 5.3% and the forward multiple below 7.5x, widening margin of safety enough to compensate for the structural decline.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Disclosure of a triggering event or a second Americas goodwill impairment at the August 31, 2026 test, or language indicating the cushion has narrowed further.
2. FY2026 guidance cut, or FY2027 consensus falling below $4.50 from the current $4.92.
3. Price/mix turning negative in any quarter, indicating promotional escalation has replaced premiumization.
4. Buyback suspension alongside a halt to dividend growth — the combination would signal internal cash-flow stress that the current ~3.0x coverage does not suggest.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 10-Q, expected early November 2026, which will disclose both the August 31 annual goodwill test outcome and the first quarter to carry the full refinanced interest run-rate. The Ontario wholesale markup decision and the contents of the unread June 24, 2026 Item 5.02 filing are secondary items to resolve before that.

## Conclusion

Molson Coors is priced roughly where the evidence says it should be. The balance sheet is investment-grade with ~2.3x net leverage, the FY2025 loss was ~$3.9bn of non-cash charges against $1.78bn of operating cash flow, the refinancing risk disclosed in the annual report was cleared in May on reasonable terms, and the 4.56% dividend is covered ~3.0x. Against that, volume has fallen 13.1% over two years with no inflection, the price/mix offset has halved, one aluminum surcharge line consumes the entire announced price increase, management's own goodwill test leaves under 15% cushion on the segment representing 78% of net sales, and the voting trust removes any takeover mechanism that would ordinarily close a 5.5x EV/EBITDA gap. The forward multiple of 8.9x is a discount to the broad staples median but sits in the middle of the structurally challenged cohort where Campbell's trades at 11.1x with a higher yield.

Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 5-10% over twelve months, most of it the dividend, against 21.7% downside in the bear case, describes a fairly priced security rather than an opportunity. Hold for the yield; require demonstrated stabilization in the volume-and-price combination, or a price in the mid-$30s, before adding.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: FY2025 FCF dividend coverage was overstated as "2.5-3.0x" but is ~3.0x on the $1.92 annualized dividend; EV/adjusted EBITDA was overstated at 5.7x and is ~5.5x (net debt ~$5.4bn, not ~$5.85bn implied); the Q1 2026 +2.0% net sales figure and "sequential deterioration" framing could not be sourced (Q1 standalone was not isolated in the brief); and the licensor of Simply Spiked/Topo Chico is Red Tree Beverages, LLC, not The Coca-Cola Company. None materially alters the HOLD, but the valuation looked marginally cheaper and the sequential-momentum narrative marginally weaker than the sources support.
