## Executive Summary

Victoria's Secret & Co. has produced the operating inflection the bulls wanted. FY2025 (ended January 31, 2026) delivered comparable sales +5% and adjusted operating income of $403M against $373M the prior year, despite an ~$85M net tariff hit and a ~$14M operating-income cost from the May 2025 security incident. Q1 FY2026 was stronger still: net sales $1.56B (+15%), comparable sales +13%, adjusted gross margin 37.6% (+240bp), and adjusted operating income up 153% to $80M. Management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $4.35–4.60 from $3.20–3.45, and consensus for the current fiscal year has risen 33.1% in ninety days. The company executed its first-ever buyback, retiring 2.2M shares for $100M at roughly $45, and two directors bought stock on the open market at ~$46 during the contested proxy solicitation.

The problem is what the market has already paid for that inflection. The stock is up 354% over twelve months and sits at its 52-week high. At $102.27 it trades at 22.1x current-year consensus EPS of $4.62, 18.7x next-year consensus of $5.46, and 34x trailing adjusted EPS of $3.00, against an apparel-retail peer median of 14.8x (Gap 8.1x, Abercrombie 10.8x, Urban Outfitters 14.8x, Boot Barn 20.7x). Two facts complicate the earnings quality: management attributed only $55M of the $120M operating-income guidance raise to underlying business strength, with $65M coming from more favorable net tariff assumptions (10% through end-July, 20% thereafter, per management's stated assumption); and the growth mix is unusual for a 22x multiple, with company-operated selling square footage down 2% in FY2025, the U.S. fleet down 16 net stores, and the direct/digital channel — 31% of revenue — roughly flat.

We rate VSXY **REDUCE**. Base case $95 (17.4x FY2027 consensus $5.46), bull $125, bear $62. From $102.27 that is -7% to base, +22% to bull, -39% to bear, and a probability-weighted expected return of roughly -8%. The proximate catalyst is the Q2 FY2026 print, where consensus of $0.77 sits above management's own $0.65–0.75 guide and the options market prices a ±20% move by the September tenor. Existing holders should take money off the table into strength; new capital has no margin of safety here.

## Company Overview

Victoria's Secret & Co. operates three brands — Victoria's Secret, PINK, and Adore Me — as a single reporting segment. At January 31, 2026 the footprint was 858 company- and joint-venture-operated stores (766 U.S. Victoria's Secret/PINK, 24 Canada, 65 China JV, 3 Adore Me) plus 562 partner-operated stores across roughly 70 countries under franchise, license and wholesale arrangements at royalty rates disclosed as generally low-double-digits to low-teens percent of partner retail sales. Total store count rose to 1,420 from 1,387, with 111 openings against 78 closures — the net addition came entirely from partner-operated units while the company-operated fleet shrank.

FY2025 revenue of $6,553M split 54% North America stores ($3,544M), 31% direct/digital ($2,042M), and 15% international ($967M). Roughly 33,000 associates, 77% in stores. Merchandise sourcing runs through ~335 vendors with the largest at ~13% of spend; intimates and apparel production is concentrated in Southeast Asia and beauty manufacturing and distribution near the Columbus, Ohio headquarters.

The "Path to Potential" strategy has four pillars: supercharge bra authority, recommit to PINK, fuel beauty growth, and evolve brand projection. The senior team is entirely new — CEO Hillary Super since September 2024, CFO/COO Scott Sekella since January 2025, CMO Elizabeth Preis since May 2025 — and the 10-K carries a dedicated risk factor on leadership turnover.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 GAAP income statement understates the underlying business, and the reconciliation is worth stating precisely.

| $M | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 6,344 | 6,182 | 6,230 | **6,553** |
| Gross margin | 35.6% | 36.3% | 36.7% | **36.4%** |
| Operating income (GAAP) | 478 | 246 | 310 | **271** |
| Operating margin (GAAP) | 7.5% | 4.0% | 5.0% | **4.1%** |
| Net income | 348 | 109 | 165 | **161** |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | — | — | 2.05 | **1.93** |

Reported operating income fell $39M while gross profit rose $100M and comps rose 5%. The gap sits in one-time items: a $120M impairment of Adore Me long-lived tangible and intangible assets, $36M of inventory reserves and severance from restructuring Adore Me and DailyLook fulfillment, $20M of organizational and executive restructuring, $19M of intangible amortization and $6M of acquisition accounting, offset by a $69M gain from resolution of credit-card interchange litigation in which the company was a plaintiff. On that basis adjusted operating income was $403M versus $373M (+8.0%) and adjusted diluted EPS $3.00 versus $2.69 (+11.5%).

**Store productivity is improving on a shrinking base.** U.S. sales per average selling square foot rose 6% to $624 and sales per average store rose 7% to $4,305K, while total selling square footage fell 2% to 5,315K and the U.S. store count declined from 782 to 766. Growth came from higher average unit retail and better conversion, with total traffic slightly down and comparable traffic up. This is a low-capital-intensity path to earnings growth with a finite runway — you cannot harvest productivity from a rationalized fleet indefinitely.

**Cash generation is adequate, not exceptional.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $499M against $187M capex produced $312M of free cash flow, 4.8% of sales. That figure includes the $69M pre-tax interchange recovery; the 10-K does not disclose when the cash was received, so if the full amount landed in FY2025, underlying free cash flow was closer to $243M. We flag this rather than assert it. FY2026 capex is guided to $220–240M, so a reasonable FY2026 free-cash-flow range on guided EBITDA of roughly $810M (guided adjusted operating income $565M midpoint plus ~$245M D&A) less ~$60M cash interest, ~$120M cash taxes and $230M capex is $330–400M before working-capital change — a 4.1–4.9% free-cash-flow yield on the current $8.13B market capitalization. No dividend has been paid since the 2021 spin-off.

**The balance sheet screens worse than it is.** Total debt of $975M comprises a $379M Term Loan B due August 2028 (6.49% at year-end after a December 2025 repricing to Term SOFR + 2.75%) and $596M of 4.625% notes due July 2029. Cash was $518M, so net financial debt of $457M is 0.71x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $641M. The $750M ABL is undrawn with $589M available and was extended to May 2030 with reduced pricing. Interest coverage on adjusted operating income is 5.8x, all covenants were in compliance, and both Moody's (Ba3) and S&P (BB-) carry stable outlooks. The widely quoted 4.79 debt-to-equity figure is total liabilities over equity, dominated by capitalized operating leases — undiscounted future lease obligations are $2,452M with $416M due within one year. The correct read is operating-lease-intensive rather than financially levered, which is precisely why the ABL covenant is written on debt/EBITDAR rather than debt/EBITDA. Lenders cutting spreads twice in twelve months is the more informative signal than an Altman score distorted by ASC 842.

Two working-capital items warrant attention. Inventories rose 12.1% to $1,071M against 5% sales growth — management attributes this to international and European DC growth plus tariff-inflated unit costs, which is coherent, but the inventory-to-sales ratio moved the wrong way ahead of a year in which the entire margin thesis rests on selling at regular price. And the disclosed sensitivity that a one-percentage-point change in the gift-card breakage rate moves net sales and operating income by ~$18M is a non-trivial estimate-driven swing factor on a $565M guided operating-income base.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth data is unambiguously strong. Q1 FY2026 comps of +13% marked a fourth consecutive positive quarter; February was the first February growth in eight years; bras, PINK and beauty each grew low double digits; international sales rose 45% (36% adjusting for the reclassification of EU digital fulfillment). Adjusted gross margin expanded 240bp on what management calls a "promo detox" — more regular-price selling, fewer promotions, mid-single-digit AUR growth — achieved despite ~90bp of incremental net tariff pressure.

Guidance was raised to $7.03–7.13B in net sales (+7–9%), $550–580M adjusted operating income (+36–44%), and $4.35–4.60 adjusted EPS (+45–53%). The seasonality check holds: Q1's $80M plus the Q2 guide of $90–100M implies H1 of $170–180M, or 29–33% of the full-year guide, leaving $370–410M for H2 against a Q4 that is normally about a third of sales and the most profitable quarter. That does not require heroic acceleration.

Three qualifications temper how much of this should be capitalized.

**First, composition.** Management attributed $55M of the $120M operating-income raise to underlying business strength and $65M to more favorable net tariff impacts, working off an assumption of 10% tariffs through end-July and 20% thereafter. Just over half the raise is a policy-dependent, non-durable earnings stream that deserves a lower multiple than the operational component. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court decision striking down certain IEEPA tariffs, followed by a new 10% Section 122 tariff, is genuinely two-sided: refunds of previously paid IEEPA tariffs would be unquantified upside the guidance excludes, while Section 122 is statutorily time-limited and could be replaced by something higher.

**Second, channel mix.** Direct/digital, 31% of revenue, was flat at $2,042M in FY2025 — roughly +2% adjusting for the $44M EU reclassification out of Direct and the ~$20M security-incident cost. The international build came substantially from "International Wholesale, Royalty and Sourcing" (+$78M), which is asset-light royalty revenue on partner-operated sales the company does not control. The bull case therefore rests on physical-store productivity plus partner royalties rather than on owned digital growth, at a time when the direct-to-consumer competitors taking share are digital-native.

**Third, the mechanism.** Margin expansion is coming from price and mix rather than volume: in FY2025 North America store growth came from AUR with units per transaction flat; in Q1 FY2026 the gross-margin gain came from regular-price mix and mid-single-digit AUR. This is the highest-quality form of retail margin expansion and also the most vulnerable to consumer trade-down. Management's own commentary that new-customer growth was strongest in households earning under $50,000 and over $200,000 describes a barbell in which the mid-income core is not yet re-engaged.

The estimate-revision data is a real positive: current-year consensus +33.1% and next-year +33.2% over ninety days, with four consecutive earnings beats (96.7%, 7.8%, 7.8%, 54.8%). Revision velocity of that magnitude usually precedes further upward drift. But the guardrail matters here — this is a mid-single-digit-revenue-growth retailer, not a 30%+ top-line inflection, and the multiple has already expanded from roughly 13x forward in April to 22x today. Consensus of $4.62 for the current year sits at the very top of management's $4.35–4.60 range, and the current-quarter estimate of $0.77 sits above the $0.65–0.75 guide. The cushion that made the last four beats easy has been consumed.

Against apparel-retail peers — Gap 8.1x, Abercrombie 10.8x, Urban Outfitters 14.8x, Boot Barn 20.7x, median 14.8x — VSXY carries a 50%+ premium on any consistent basis. (Note the peer table's separate "VSCO" line at $4B market cap and 28.5x P/E is a stale duplicate entry for the same company under the prior ticker; the profile's 39.77 forward P/E field is also internally inconsistent, equalling the trailing figure and reconciling to neither the stated EPS nor the current price. We rely on the guidance and consensus figures.)

A premium to Gap and Abercrombie is defensible on comp momentum and margin trajectory. A premium to Boot Barn is harder, because Boot Barn grows units. VSXY's company-operated square footage is shrinking, its digital channel is flat, and its unit growth is asset-light royalty. The correct comparison set for a retailer whose earnings growth comes from AUR, mix and fleet productivity is the mid-teens, not the low twenties.

The PEG arithmetic looks less demanding — 22.1x current-year against 18% expected FY2027 earnings growth is a PEG of roughly 1.2 — but that FY2027 growth assumes the tariff assumption normalizes favorably, comps stay positive, and promo discipline holds through a weakening consumer. Strip the $65M tariff component out of the FY2026 raise and the operational earnings base is closer to $4.10–4.15 in adjusted EPS, on which the stock trades at roughly 25x.

The analyst distribution is informative in the other direction from the usual momentum read. Mean target $91.40, median $90.00, high $110.00, low $73.00. The current price is 11.9% **above** the mean and 13.6% above the median, and only 7.1% below the single highest published target. On a cheap inflection stock we would treat consensus targets as stale floors; when a stock has already traded 12% through the mean target while the same sell-side community has been publicly skeptical through the entire rally — a target cut after the Q4 print, upgrades only to "cautious hold" — the more likely reading is that price has run ahead of underwritten fundamentals rather than that eleven analysts are uniformly asleep.

**Base case $95.** 17.4x FY2027 consensus $5.46. This awards a two-to-three turn premium to the peer median for demonstrated comp momentum and balance-sheet quality, and assumes earnings roll forward as guided. That is 7% below the current price.

**Bull case $125.** FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$6.00 (sustained double-digit comps, international compounding, a favorable tariff resolution including partial IEEPA refunds) at 21x. +22%.

**Bear case $62.** 13.5x FY2026 consensus $4.60 — the multiple the stock actually carried in April 2026 on the pre-revision estimate base — reflecting a comp deceleration into a weakening discretionary consumer, a step-up to 20% tariffs in the back half, or a Q4 execution stumble. -39%.

Probability-weighted at 50/25/25: roughly -8% expected return.

## Competitive Landscape

Scale is the durable advantage and it is real. Roughly 1,420 stores across ~70 countries, in-store bra-fitting expertise the MD&A names as a key differentiator, and a beauty/fragrance franchise management calls its "secret weapon" are assets no DTC challenger can replicate quickly. The Q1 evidence that the brand is regaining relevance is concrete: the "Angels Among Us" search drew over 100,000 applicants and ~1.7 billion media impressions, the Hailey Bieber and TWICE campaigns generated ~2 billion impressions, and comps of +13% comfortably exceed anything in the peer set.

The trajectory is improving from a low base, which is the relevant framing. Aerie built its position on inclusive sizing and body positivity, Skims on shapewear and celebrity distribution, and ThirdLove and Cuup on fit-focused premium DTC, while Abercrombie and Gap extended into adjacencies. Victoria's Secret lost share for most of a decade. The company's own proxy shows cumulative TSR at index 94 versus a peer index of 144 as of January 31, 2026 — even after a strong FY2025 recovery, more than four years of cumulative underperformance.

Two structural facts cap how far the competitive score can go. Company-operated selling square footage declined 2% and the U.S. fleet shrank by 16 net stores, so unit growth is entirely partner-operated — capital-light and royalty-bearing, but ceding execution control over 562 stores whose conduct affects brand equity, a risk Item 1A names explicitly. And the direct channel, where the share losses originated, is flat. The turnaround is being won in stores and in international royalties, not in the channel where the competitive threat lives.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer discretionary macro.** A margin model built on eliminating promotions and lifting AUR mid-single-digits is among the most exposed configurations in retail to a consumer trade-down. The disclosed new-customer barbell (under $50K and over $200K households) suggests the mid-income cohort most sensitive to that squeeze has not yet returned. [Specific macro indicators — mortgage rates, mortgage application demand, payroll data, and metro-level housing clearance — are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed.]

**Tariff assumption.** Guidance embeds 10% through end-July and 20% thereafter. FY2025's realized net cost was ~$85M, roughly 130bp of margin. A worse outcome compresses the back half; a refund of previously paid IEEPA tariffs, which the 10-K says explicitly may or may not be obtainable, is unquantified upside. Two-sided, and roughly half the guidance raise depends on it.

**Consensus above guidance.** Current-quarter consensus of $0.77 exceeds management's $0.65–0.75 range, and full-year consensus of $4.62 sits at the top of the $4.35–4.60 guide. A print inside management's own range would be read as a miss.

**Concentration of remaining goodwill.** Adore Me's identifiable intangibles went from $95M to zero via the $120M impairment, and total FY2025 Adore Me-related charges were $162M pre-tax, but $367M of goodwill remains, tested quantitatively in Q4 FY2025 (50% DCF / 50% guideline public-company multiples) without impairment. Because the company reports a single segment and the 10-K references "our reporting unit with goodwill," the allocation cannot be determined from the filing. A downturn or reporting-unit reallocation could expose it.

**Governance overhang.** All nine directors were re-elected in June 2026, with Chair Donna James at over 83%, but Mariam Naficy chose not to stand for re-election and a replacement search is underway. The Rights Plan adopted in response to BBRC's accumulation had a one-year term expiring May 18, 2026 with no evidence of renewal or lapse — an unresolved question. The allegations exchanged during the solicitation, including the company's espionage claim and BBRC's rebuttal, are unadjudicated and should not be treated as fact in either direction.

**The informed marginal seller.** BBRC International reduced its holding from 10,310,631 to 7,810,631 shares between June 2 and July 28, 2026 — exactly 2,500,000 shares for ~$212.4M at a weighted-average $84.94, with final trades at $89.08–$90.60. The remaining ~7.81M shares are roughly 9.8% of outstanding, just below the 10% Section 16 threshold, so the sales may be structurally motivated rather than a price judgment, and concentration reduction after losing a proxy vote is an equally plausible explanation. But the holder with the deepest engagement and the longest accumulation history monetized a quarter of its position 12–16% below the current price, and a ~9.8% residual stake is a supply overhang. Any thesis resting on a strategic bid is weakened, because the most acquirer-adjacent holder is a net seller.

**Buyback support is nearly exhausted.** The $100M executed at ~$45 was well-timed, retiring 2.8% of shares. The remaining $150M of authorization buys only ~1.47M shares, or ~1.8% of outstanding, at current prices — no longer a meaningful floor.

**Verification gap.** Q1 FY2026 figures, including the $55M/$65M split of the guidance raise, come from external reporting of the June 2 call and press coverage rather than from the 10-Q text, which could not be retrieved directly. The FY2025 10-K figures are filing-grade; the Q1 figures should be treated as high-confidence but unconfirmed against the filed document.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 67.7% sits essentially on top of 69.0% one-year realized volatility — options are fairly priced, not signalling stress, and IV is at the 7th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew of 1.7% at the near tenor is characterized as balanced, and put/call volume of 0.54 is call-tilted while open-interest put/call of 1.10 is mildly put-heavy — the mix of existing protection against fresh call activity typical of a stock at highs. The term structure is in contango, 67.7% near versus 72.7% at six months, which prices rising rather than imminent uncertainty. The decision-relevant number is the implied move: ±9.5% on the August 21 expiry and ±20.1% on the September 18 tenor spanning the Q2 print. The market is pricing a genuine binary at the next report, consistent with our view that the setup carries substantial two-sided event risk against a consensus bar set above management's own guide. Short interest of 19.0% of float with 5.9 days to cover cuts both ways: it has almost certainly amplified the move up and remains squeeze fuel, but it also means the incremental buyer at $102 is not a short covering cheaply, and a disappointing print would face thin natural demand.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (to $125, +22%).** The turnaround compounds rather than merely inflecting. Comps stay double-digit as the brand-relevance work converts, promo discipline holds and AUR keeps rising, international compounds off China digital and partner royalties at 30%+, and beauty scales. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $6.00. Tariffs resolve favorably, and a partial refund of previously paid IEEPA duties lands as an unmodelled one-time benefit against FY2025's $85M net cost. Net financial leverage of 0.7x and an undrawn $750M revolver give management room to reinvest and repurchase. Nineteen percent short interest forces continued covering. At 21x FY2027 the stock reaches $125, and consensus targets — currently a mean of $91 — migrate upward behind the price.

**Bear case (to $62, -39%).** The consumer squeeze arrives in the back half. A weakening discretionary consumer pushes the mid-income customer back toward promotion-seeking behavior, and the promo-detox margin gain reverses fastest because it was never volume-driven — units per transaction were already flat in FY2025. Tariffs step to 20% as assumed or worse, removing $65M of the guidance raise. Q4, roughly a third of sales, misses. Inventory that grew 12.1% against 5% sales growth has to be cleared through markdown. The multiple compresses to the 13.5x the stock carried in April, and $4.60 of earnings supports $62. The 9.8% BBRC residual becomes supply into that decline, and the remaining $150M authorization is too small to matter.

**Base case (to $95, -7%).** Guidance is met at the midpoint, comps decelerate to mid-single digits as the AUR runway shortens and comparisons harden, FY2027 earnings land near $5.46, and the multiple settles two to three turns above the 14.8x peer median at 17.4x. The stock does approximately nothing for twelve months while earnings grow into the current price.

The asymmetry decides the rating. Upside requires the multiple to hold at a premium to a peer group where no comparable retailer sustains 20x+ without unit growth. Downside requires only that one of three things happen — a comp deceleration, an unfavorable tariff outcome, or a Q4 stumble — none of which is remote.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength. We would reduce roughly half of a full position now, ahead of the Q2 FY2026 report where consensus of $0.77 sits above management's $0.65–0.75 guide and the September options tenor prices a ±20% move. Retain the remainder only if the position is held with an explicit stop; a residual holding is defensible on operating momentum and a 0.7x-net-levered balance sheet, but not at a full weight against a base case 7% below spot. Do not add.

**Targets and returns from $102.27.** Base $95 (-7%), bull $125 (+22%), bear $62 (-39%). Probability-weighted expected return at 50/25/25 is approximately -8%. Timeframe: twelve months, with the decisive checkpoints at the Q2 print (late August/early September 2026), the Q3 print (late November), and the Q4/FY2026 report in March 2027 that will confirm or break the H2-weighted guide.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.**
1. A pullback to $80–85 — approximately 15x FY2027 consensus — with comps still positive and FY2026 guidance intact. That restores a defensible entry.
2. Two consecutive quarters of comparable sales above +8% with adjusted gross margin holding at or above 37.5% **and** direct/digital returning to at least mid-single-digit growth ex-reclassification. Digital re-acceleration would materially change the growth-mix argument against the multiple.
3. Confirmed recovery of IEEPA tariff refunds, quantified in a filing, plus FY2027 guidance built on a tariff assumption at or below 10%.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. Comparable sales decelerating below +3%, or adjusted gross margin contracting year-over-year in any quarter — either would indicate the promo detox is unwinding.
2. FY2026 adjusted operating income guidance cut below $520M, or a Q4 guide that requires more than 70% of full-year operating income from H2.
3. Inventory growth exceeding sales growth by more than 10 percentage points for a second consecutive quarter, or a goodwill impairment against the retained $367M balance.
4. BBRC reducing below approximately 5% of outstanding, or a Schedule 13D amendment indicating a full exit — which would remove any residual strategic-interest support while adding supply.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q2 FY2026 report, expected late August or early September 2026. That print resolves whether the company can clear a consensus bar set above its own guidance, and it is the first quarter in which the assumed tariff step-up to 20% begins to affect cost of goods.

## Conclusion

The operating story here is legitimate. Four consecutive quarters of positive comps, 240bp of adjusted gross-margin expansion driven by regular-price selling, a fleet generating 6% more revenue per square foot on 2% less square footage, net financial leverage of 0.7x with an undrawn revolver and lenders voluntarily cutting spreads twice in a year, a first-ever buyback executed at roughly 44% of the current price, and two directors putting personal capital in at ~$46 during the worst of the governance fight — this is a company doing the right things and being rewarded for it.

The valuation has moved past the story. At $102.27 the stock capitalizes 22.1x current-year and 18.7x next-year consensus, a 50%+ premium to an apparel-retail peer median of 14.8x, on a business whose company-operated square footage is shrinking and whose 31%-of-revenue digital channel is flat. Just over half of the guidance raise that drove the re-rating is tariff relief rather than operating outperformance. Consensus now sits at the top of management's ranges for both the current quarter and the full year, removing the cushion behind four easy beats. The price is 12% above the mean sell-side target. And the 10% holder with the deepest engagement and the strongest stated views on capital allocation sold 2.5M shares at an average $84.94, retaining a ~9.8% stake that constitutes supply rather than support.

None of that makes VSXY a short — the balance sheet is sound, the momentum is real, 19% short interest with 5.9 days to cover makes the borrow crowded, and the tariff-refund optionality is genuine unmodelled upside. It makes the risk/reward unfavorable at this price. **REDUCE.** Revisit at $80–85, or on evidence that digital growth and comp momentum can carry the multiple rather than the tariff line.
