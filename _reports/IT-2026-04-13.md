## Executive Summary

Gartner, the world's dominant research and advisory franchise, has repriced down 63% from its 52-week high of $451.73 to $154.70. The decline reflects a convergence of operational deterioration and structural concern. Wallet retention fell below 100% for the first time in both GTS (96%) and GBS (99%), U.S. federal contract value was nearly halved (from ~$230M to $126M) under DOGE-driven cuts, and the company's advisory model faces a genuine existential question from AI-powered alternatives. Management responded with what CEO Gene Hall called "more change than we've ever done at Gartner" in 21 years, including launching AskGartner, divesting Digital Markets, and restructuring content creation.

The balance sheet reflects aggressive financial engineering that now constrains flexibility. Stockholders' equity collapsed from $1.36B to $320M in 2025 as the company spent $2.0B on buybacks against $729M in net income. Gross debt rose to $2.98B. While cash flow generation ($1.18B FCF) services this debt comfortably today, the company has eliminated its balance sheet cushion at the exact moment the business faces its most significant operational and competitive challenges. The Altman Z-Score of 1.3 is partly a mechanical artifact of the buyback strategy, but it correctly identifies a structure with no room for error.

At ~11x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS of $12.30 and ~15x trailing GAAP, Gartner appears optically inexpensive against its history. But estimate revisions are negative across all periods (-2.5% current year, -3.0% next year over 90 days), contract value growth has decelerated to 1%, and the company's own 10-K acknowledges that LLMs could reduce demand for its core products. The trajectory is deteriorating, and no concrete catalyst exists for stabilization. This is an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Gartner operates as the leading global research and advisory company, serving over 13,000 enterprises across approximately 90 countries. The business model centers on subscription-based research (Insights, 78% of 2025 revenue), supplemented by destination conferences (10%) and project-based consulting (9%). The former Digital Markets segment (3%) was divested in February 2026 for $110M following a $150M goodwill impairment.

The company's competitive moat rests on its proprietary frameworks — the Magic Quadrant and Hype Cycle — which have become de facto industry standards influencing billions in enterprise technology purchasing decisions. The scale of Gartner's data collection (200,000+ client conversations on AI alone in 2025, 6,000+ AI research documents) creates a flywheel where more clients generate more data, improving research quality and attracting additional clients.

Revenue reached $6.50B in 2025, growing 3.7% YoY but only 3% on an FX-neutral basis, decelerating from 6.1% in 2024 and 7.9% in 2023.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings

Gartner's revenue trajectory shows clear deceleration:

| Year | Revenue | YoY Growth | Operating Margin | Diluted EPS |
|------|---------|------------|------------------|-------------|
| 2022 | $5.48B | — | 20.1% | $9.97 |
| 2023 | $5.91B | +7.9% | 20.9% | $11.07 |
| 2024 | $6.27B | +6.1% | 18.5% | $16.00 |
| 2025 | $6.50B | +3.7% | 15.8% | $9.65 |

The 2025 EPS decline requires context: 2024 was inflated by a $300M insurance gain and a $162M one-time IP transfer tax benefit. Adjusting for these, the underlying earnings trajectory is still positive but flattening. Adjusted EPS was $13.17 for FY2025; management guided to "at least $12.30" for FY2026, implying a 6.6% decline in adjusted earnings.

The operating margin compression from 20.9% (2023) to 15.8% (2025) reflects both the Digital Markets impairment and deliberate investment spending. Management set 23.5% adjusted EBITDA margin as a "new baseline" for 2026, down from ~24.8% in 2025. This is a conscious margin sacrifice to fund transformation initiatives — a reasonable strategy if it stabilizes growth, but a margin cut during revenue deceleration creates earnings compression from both directions.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow was $1.18B in 2025, down from $1.48B in 2024 (the 2024 figure included $300M in insurance proceeds). Guided FCF of "at least $1.135B" for 2026 suggests further modest compression. Capital expenditures remain minimal at $115M, reflecting the asset-light model. On a normalized basis, the business generates approximately 18% FCF margins.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is the product of a deliberate financial engineering strategy that has reached concerning extremes:

- Total debt: $2.98B (up from $2.46B in 2024)
- Cash: $1.72B (down from $1.93B)
- Stockholders' equity: $320M (down from $1.36B)
- D/E ratio: 24.3x
- Altman Z-Score: 1.3

The equity destruction is almost entirely attributable to $2.0B in share repurchases in 2025 (7.0M shares, ~8% of outstanding). The company simultaneously issued $800M in new senior notes to fund these buybacks. Gross leverage at 1.9x EBITDA is manageable, and all debt carries fixed rates, insulating from rate increases. But the near-elimination of the equity cushion means any meaningful revenue disruption would quickly create covenant stress. The current ratio of 1.0x is partially obscured by deferred revenue ($2.2B+ of the current liabilities don't require cash outflows), but the reported figure provides no margin of safety.

## Growth Analysis

### Contract Value — The Leading Indicator

Contract value (CV) is Gartner's most important forward metric because subscription revenue is recognized ratably over contract terms. CV grew only 1% FX-neutral to $5.2B at year-end 2025. Excluding U.S. federal, CV grew 4% — better but still a sharp deceleration from ~8% in 2024.

The wallet retention deterioration is the single most concerning operational datapoint:
- GTS wallet retention: 102% → 96% (6-point drop)
- GBS wallet retention: 106% → 99% (7-point drop)

Sub-100% wallet retention means the existing client base is contracting its spending. Client retention held relatively stable (85-86%), so clients are staying but spending less. This breaks the "land and expand" model that has driven Gartner's long-term growth. If wallet retention doesn't recover above 100%, CV growth will depend entirely on new client acquisition — a more expensive and less predictable growth vector.

### Revenue Guidance

FY2026 guidance of "at least $6.455B" implies approximately 2% FX-neutral growth, the slowest in recent history. The guidance assumes the federal headwind abates (management states the "vast majority" of affected contracts have now either renewed or churned) and the Insights transformation begins to bear fruit. Both assumptions carry execution risk.

### AI: Threat and Opportunity

The AI dynamic is Gartner's most significant strategic uncertainty. The company's own 10-K risk factors explicitly state that "LLMs and AI chatbots could reduce demand for our research products." If executives can get "good enough" technology guidance from AI tools trained partially on leaked or publicly available Gartner frameworks, the value proposition of a $50K+ annual subscription erodes.

The countervailing force is AskGartner, launched August-October 2025, which showed "substantially higher renewal rates" among licensed users. This suggests AI can increase engagement with proprietary datasets rather than substitute for them. But it's early, and the competitive landscape is evolving rapidly. Every major consulting firm and AI company is building enterprise advisory products.

At $154.70, Gartner trades at:
- ~11.7x FY2026E adjusted EPS ($13.17 current consensus)
- ~15x trailing GAAP EPS ($9.65)
- ~14.9x forward P/E (per Yahoo Finance)
- ~10.6x FY2026E FCF ($1.135B guidance / $10.9B market cap)

Against peers:
| Metric | IT | ACN | CTSH | IBM | EPAM | Peer Median |
|--------|-----|-----|------|-----|------|-------------|
| P/E | 14.9x | 17.1x | 18.2x | 26.6x | 30.5x | 26.6x |

Gartner trades at a 44% discount to its peer median P/E of 26.6x. Historically, Gartner has commanded a premium to this peer group given its higher-margin, more recurring revenue profile and superior TSR (314% 5-year vs. 159% peer group). The current discount reflects the market's assessment that the growth and competitive dynamics have structurally shifted.

On a DCF basis, $1.14B in guided FCF at a 10% discount rate with 2% terminal growth implies roughly $14.3B enterprise value, or ~$160/share after netting $1.26B in net debt — approximately in line with the current price. The market is pricing in low-single-digit perpetual growth, consistent with the guided trajectory.

The valuation would be compelling if the trajectory were flat or improving. With negative estimate revisions, sub-100% wallet retention, and an unresolved AI disruption threat, the discount appears to reflect genuine fundamental deterioration rather than temporary mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Gartner's competitive position has two layers. The first is the brand moat: the Magic Quadrant, Hype Cycle, and related frameworks are deeply embedded in enterprise procurement processes. Technology vendors pay significant premiums to achieve favorable Gartner ratings, and CIOs use Gartner research as career-risk mitigation ("nobody got fired for following Gartner's advice"). This moat has been durable for decades.

The second layer is the data flywheel: 13,000+ enterprise clients, 200,000+ annual AI conversations, and thousands of practitioner data points create insights that no competitor can easily replicate. This data advantage is real but potentially perishable if AI tools can aggregate and synthesize equivalent information from public sources.

Direct competitors (Forrester, IDC, Everest Group) are smaller and less diversified but are investing aggressively in AI-native products. Indirect competition from AI-native information services (ChatGPT Enterprise, Perplexity, specialized vertical AI) is harder to quantify. The 10-K's explicit acknowledgment that clients may be loading proprietary Gartner content into LLMs — despite contractual prohibitions — suggests the moat is already experiencing seepage.

## Risk Assessment

### Federal Government Exposure (Acute, Materializing)
U.S. federal CV dropped ~45% in 2025 to $126M. DOGE-related contract terminations specifically targeted advisory firms. Management claims the worst is past, but further government austerity remains possible given the current fiscal environment.

### AI Disruption (Strategic, Accelerating)
The core risk: can Gartner's subscription model survive when LLMs can produce synthesized technology assessments? AskGartner is the defensive response, but the bar for maintaining premium pricing rises every quarter as AI capabilities improve.

### Balance Sheet Fragility (Financial, Self-Inflicted)
The current structure works if FCF stays above $1B. A recession-induced decline in enterprise spending or accelerated churn due to AI competition would quickly stress this balance sheet given the minimal equity cushion.

### Wallet Retention Trajectory (Operational, Active)
If sub-100% retention persists for 2-3 more quarters, cumulative CV erosion will create a negative revenue cycle that is difficult to reverse.

### Macroeconomic Sensitivity
Enterprise IT advisory spending is discretionary. In a recessionary environment — probability currently estimated at 45-50% — companies cut advisory budgets among the first austerity measures. Gartner's revenue deceleration from 7.9% to 3.7% to guided 2% occurred during modest GDP growth. A recession would likely push growth negative.

### Options Market Signal

Options markets are pricing elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 65.2% sits 12.4 percentage points above 52-week historical volatility of 52.8%, placing IV at the 62nd percentile. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 65.2% vs. 6-month 53.0%), characteristic of imminent event risk or unresolved fundamental uncertainty. The 9.6% put skew in the weekly expiry confirms elevated demand for downside protection. Short interest at 12.7% of float with 5.0 days to cover indicates meaningful institutional bearish positioning. The options market is consistent with the fundamental thesis: ongoing deterioration with no resolution catalyst priced in the near term.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$190-210)
- Federal headwind proves transitory: remaining contracts stabilize, CV excluding federal returns to 5-6% growth
- AskGartner drives wallet retention back above 100% by Q3 2026, demonstrating AI is an engagement accelerator
- Margin reset at 23.5% EBITDA proves conservative; expansion to 25%+ by 2027
- Aggressive buybacks at depressed levels ($1.2B authorized) generate significant EPS accretion
- The brand moat proves durable against AI disruption, similar to how Bloomberg survived the internet information revolution

### Bear Case (~$100-120)
- Wallet retention remains sub-100% for multiple quarters, causing CV to contract and revenue to decline
- AI disruption accelerates: ChatGPT Enterprise and vertical AI tools erode the advisory value proposition, client churn rises above 20%
- Recession hits enterprise IT budgets, compounding the organic deceleration
- FCF declines toward $800M, limiting further buybacks and forcing deleveraging
- Federal exposure stabilizes but doesn't recover, creating a permanent ~$100M revenue hole

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: No single clean catalyst with a specific date. The next earnings report (likely early May 2026 for Q1) is the nearest point of fundamental revelation, specifically around Q1 contract value growth and wallet retention. If wallet retention remains sub-100% and management cuts guidance, shares could break below the $139 52-week low. However, the February 2026 earnings already delivered the shock (stock dropped from ~$200 to ~$160 on report day). The next leg lower requires incremental deterioration, not a repetition.

**Timing window**: Q1 2026 earnings (early May) is the next major data point. A 3-6 month window captures two earnings cycles and allows the AI competitive dynamic to develop further.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: 12.7% SI with 5.0 days to cover is elevated. A sharp reversal — for example, a buyout rumor or a dramatic CV recovery — could trigger a squeeze. The 63% drawdown from highs means a substantial portion of the bearish thesis has already been expressed in the stock price.

**Borrow and options signal**: IV at 65.2% makes protective puts expensive. The backwardated term structure suggests the market expects elevated volatility to persist. Borrow cost would be meaningful given the elevated short interest.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Bear case of $100-120 represents 22-35% downside from $154.70. Bull case of $190-210 represents 23-36% upside. The risk/reward is approximately 1:1, which fails to clear the 2:1 threshold for active shorting.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE**. The 63% decline has already captured much of the deterioration thesis. The remaining downside requires either a recession or AI disruption acceleration — both plausible but without specific timing triggers. Short interest at 12.7% creates meaningful squeeze risk, and rich IV makes hedging expensive. The correct expression of a bearish view is to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria** (conditions that would change the AVOID to BUY):
1. **Wallet retention recovers above 100%** in both GTS and GBS for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the "land and expand" model is functioning again
2. **Contract value growth accelerates to 5%+ FX-neutral**, excluding federal, signaling that the Insights transformation is driving organic demand recovery
3. **Stock reaches $110-120** (~8-9x forward earnings), providing sufficient margin of safety to compensate for the structural risks. At that level, even a modestly negative growth scenario would be priced in.

**Timeline**: The headwinds are likely to persist for at least 2-3 quarters. The AI competitive dynamic is a multi-year overhang with no resolution date. The federal headwind should largely pass by Q2 2026. Wallet retention is the swing factor — two quarters of data (Q1 and Q2 2026 reports) will determine whether the business has stabilized.

**Reassessment point**: Q1 2026 earnings (early May 2026) for first read on post-guidance CV trends. Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August 2026) for confirmation.

## Conclusion

Gartner is a high-quality franchise facing a convergence of cyclical and structural headwinds. The cyclical factors — federal contract losses, enterprise spending caution, recession risk — are manageable given the business model's inherent resilience. The structural factor — AI-driven commoditization of research and advisory services — is harder to dismiss and has no clear timeline for resolution.

The balance sheet amplifies these risks. By spending $2.0B on buybacks in 2025 while earning $729M in GAAP net income, management destroyed $1.04B in equity during a period of accelerating operational challenges. The resulting $320M equity base against $2.98B in debt provides no buffer if the business deteriorates beyond current expectations.

At ~12x forward adjusted earnings, the stock is cheaper than at any point in its modern history as a public company. This cheapness is directionally warranted: estimate revisions are negative, wallet retention is below 100% in both segments, and the AI disruption thesis has not been disproven. Until contract value growth re-accelerates and wallet retention stabilizes above 100%, the correct posture is to avoid owning the stock despite the apparently attractive multiple.
