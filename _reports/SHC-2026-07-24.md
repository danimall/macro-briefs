**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sotera Health is the only vertically-integrated global gamma sterilization provider, combining outsourced terminal sterilization (Sterigenics, 65% of revenue), Cobalt-60 supply (Nordion, 16%), and lab testing/advisory (Nelson Labs, 19%). The business is essential and non-discretionary: single-use medical devices are legally required to be sterilized, ~90% of Sterigenics revenue sits under multi-year contracts, and the top-10 customers have renewed for 5+ years at a 100% rate. The economics reflect that position, with a consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin near 51% and 2025 free cash flow of $149M. The company serves 40+ of the top 50 device makers and 9 of the top 10 global pharma companies across 62 facilities. Its principal competitor in outsourced sterilization is STERIS's Applied Sterilization Technologies, forming an effective duopoly protected by >$50M facility build costs and multi-year regulatory licensing.

Two structural events reshaped the equity story in 2026. First, legacy PE sponsors Warburg Pincus and GTCR sold their entire ~20% combined stakes across March–May at roughly $15.17/share, taking public float to ~90% and unwinding the board-designation rights that had governed the company since the IPO. Second, Alton Shader, an outside medical-industry executive, succeeded IPO-era CEO Michael Petras as CEO effective May 26, 2026. The overhang is gone and governance is reset, but SHC now trades entirely on public-market sentiment with no anchor holder. Separately, the defining ethylene-oxide (EtO) mass-tort litigation is de-risking: Willowbrook is essentially settled, per-tranche settlement sizes have fallen from a $408M lump to $30–34M increments, and annual litigation cost dropped to $46.2M in 2025, but Georgia (~755 claims), California, and New Mexico cases remain live, insurance now excludes EtO, and a single large adverse verdict can trigger appellate-bond and covenant-default consequences.

At $17.49 the stock has risen ~48% off its 52-week low, sits near the middle of analyst targets ($16 low / $24 high, $20 median), and trades at ~43x trailing earnings against essentially flat estimate revisions (current-year EPS $0.97, revised -0.6% over 30 days). The fundamental trajectory is positive and the balance-sheet story is better than the screens suggest, but the multiple already reflects the improving litigation and governance narrative. We rate SHC a HOLD (growth_hold), with a base case that captures modest low-double-digit total return over 12 months and specific triggers that would move us to a BUY.

## Company Overview

Sotera Health operates three reportable segments:

- **Sterigenics** ($755.8M revenue in 2025, +8.3%; segment margin 54.6%) — outsourced terminal sterilization via gamma irradiation (23 facilities), ethylene oxide (17 facilities), and E-beam (8 facilities). No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. Switching costs are high because each product/facility combination is validated with regulators.
- **Nordion** ($187.6M, +8.2%; segment margin ~57%) — the leading global supplier of Cobalt-60, the input for gamma sterilization, plus gamma irradiation systems and medical Co-60 for oncology (Gamma Knife). Nordion has built more than 120 of the roughly 200 large-scale irradiators worldwide.
- **Nelson Labs** ($220.2M, -3.9%; segment margin ~33%) — microbiological/analytical chemistry lab testing and advisory. This is the weakest and only shrinking segment, dragged by a ~10% decline in advisory/consulting revenue.

The vertical integration of Sterigenics and Nordion is the structural differentiator: SHC controls both the sterilization capacity and the Co-60 feedstock that gamma sterilization depends on. This is protected by regulatory licensing, capital intensity, and customer validation lock-in.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded steadily: $1,003.7M (2022) → $1,049.3M (2023) → $1,100.4M (2024) → $1,163.6M (2025, +5.7%). Q1 2026 revenue grew 10.0% reported (6.5% constant currency) to $280.0M. Growth is consistently split across price and volume/mix, the hallmark of a business with pricing power in a regulated, non-discretionary end market.

Profitability is the standout. 2025 adjusted EBITDA was $593.8M (+8.2%) at a 51.0% margin. Operating cash flow of $287.2M in 2025 exceeded net income of $77.9M, a favorable earnings-quality signal, and FCF was $149.2M (12.8% margin). Q1 2026 net income of $26.6M reversed a prior-year $13.3M loss, and adjusted EBITDA rose 10.5% to $134.7M. Nordion's Q1 constant-currency growth of 25.8% was driven by cobalt-harvest timing.

The central financial nuance is leverage, and it is materially misread by standard screens. Total debt is approximately $2.24B (a $1,419.5M term loan, $750M of 7.375% secured notes due 2031, and finance leases). The structured financial feed shows a long-term debt figure near $94M, which is incomplete; the correct figures come from the 10-K and Q1 release. Net debt is $1.92B and net leverage is 3.2x and improving. The reported Altman Z-score of ~0.9 ("distress zone") and debt/equity of ~4.4x are artifacts of PE-legacy leverage and negative retained earnings (-$532M, accumulated from historical litigation charges and buyouts), not operating distress. The business covers roughly $135–145M of 2026 guided interest with ~$287M of operating cash flow.

Liquidity is solid: $346M cash at year-end 2025 plus an undrawn $600M revolver (extended to 2030) gives more than $900M of availability. Interest expense is declining after the September 2025 repricing (Amendment No. 6) cut the term-loan spread to SOFR+2.50%, bringing 2026 interest guidance to $135–145M from $156M in 2025. Approximately $1.42B of floating-rate term debt means a 1% SOFR move adds ~$10.2M of annual interest, a manageable but real sensitivity in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

Capital allocation: no dividend; cash goes to debt paydown ($75M voluntary term-loan repayment in 2025), capex ($138M in 2025, guided $175–225M in 2026, including ~$51M for EtO environmental controls), and disciplined M&A. Heavy environmental capex is a recurring drag on FCF conversion that will persist.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth algorithm is mid-single-digit organic revenue growth translating to slightly faster adjusted EBITDA growth via price/mix, supported by structural demand for regulation-mandated sterilization of single-use devices. 2026 guidance reaffirmed at the Q1 print calls for revenue of $1.233–1.251B (5.0–6.5% cc), adjusted EBITDA of $632–641M (5.5–7.0% cc), adjusted EPS of $0.93–1.01, and interest of $135–145M.

The earnings-surprise record is constructive: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with beats of 14.5% (Q2 2025), 17.8% (Q3 2025), and 6.2% (Q4 2025) before an in-line Q1 2026. This is not an acceleration story. Estimate revisions are essentially flat to slightly negative: current-quarter EPS revised -0.5% (30d) / -2.5% (90d), current-year -0.6% (30d) / +0.2% (90d), and next-year -0.3% (30d) / +1.3% (90d). The revision trend does not carry the velocity that would flag a strong forward catalyst; consensus sees steady compounding, not acceleration.

Segment dispersion matters for the growth thesis. Sterigenics and Nordion are the engines; Nelson Labs (-3.8% cc in Q1 2026, segment income down 11.5%) is a persistent drag. Nordion's Q1 outperformance was timing-driven and should normalize. Whether new management addresses the Nelson Labs advisory softness is a swing factor worth monitoring but not one we underwrite in the base case.

## Valuation Assessment

At $17.49 the stock carries a trailing P/E near 43x on GAAP EPS of ~$0.27 (depressed by non-operating litigation and interest charges). On adjusted EPS the picture is more reasonable but still not cheap: guidance of $0.93–1.01 implies ~18x current-year adjusted earnings, and next-year consensus of $1.06 implies ~16.5x. EV/EBITDA is the cleaner lens given the leverage: ~$5.05B equity + $1.92B net debt = ~$6.97B EV against ~$636M of guided 2026 adjusted EBITDA, or roughly 11x forward EV/EBITDA. That is a fair multiple for a high-margin, high-barrier, contracted business with a legal tail, neither a bargain nor stretched.

The peer set is heterogeneous and of limited use: Health Care Services median P/E of 43.9 blends money-losers (RDNT at -308x), high-growth diagnostics (VCYT at 43.9x), and slow compounders (CHE at 23x, CON at 19.9x). SHC's economics justify a premium to the low-multiple names but the litigation and leverage warrant a discount to the highest-growth peers.

We are deliberately cautious on treating the "low GAAP earnings, high trailing multiple" combination as evidence of hidden cheapness: the trailing multiple is not depressed by a cyclical earnings trough that will spring back, but by structural interest and litigation costs that recur. Estimate revisions are not rising fast enough to argue that analyst targets are stale floors; with revisions flat, the median target likely reflects fair value rather than a lagging anchor. The sponsors who knew the business best sold at ~$15.17, roughly 13% below the current price, which caps the mispricing argument on the upside. On balance the multiple already discounts the improving litigation and governance narrative, so we score valuation as balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position is the strongest dimension and continues to improve. SHC and STERIS's Applied Sterilization Technologies dominate outsourced medical-device sterilization, protected by facility costs, multi-year licensing, and product-specific regulatory validation that makes switching prohibitively costly. Sterigenics' segment margin and 100% top-10 customer renewal over 5+ years are consistent with a wide moat. Nordion's control of the Co-60 value chain and its installed base reinforce the position on the feedstock side.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving: mid-single-digit organic growth in the core segments, pricing power evident in every quarter, and no single-customer concentration. The structural threats are gradual: X-ray and non-Co-60 sterilization modalities and FDA-encouraged EtO alternatives (vaporized hydrogen peroxide approved in 2024) could pressure the mix over a multi-year horizon, and Nelson Labs is losing ground in advisory. None of these is a near-term share-loss event. The removal of the sponsor overhang and the independent board refresh (Rich Kyle, Feb 2026; Ken Krause, a veteran public-company CFO, March 2026) modestly strengthen the governance foundation of the competitive franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**EtO mass-tort litigation** is the defining idiosyncratic risk and the main reason the risk score is not higher. Cook County produced a $358.7M verdict ($320M punitive) in one 2022 case. The remaining Cobb County, GA docket comprises ~450 personal-injury plus ~305 property claims, with additional suits in California, Illinois, and New Mexico (an AG action with a preliminary injunction on the Santa Teresa facility). The credit agreement and indenture treat an unstayed judgment exceeding $162.6M or 30% of EBITDA for 60 days as an event of default, which links a single large adverse verdict directly to liquidity. The settlement trend is favorable (smaller tranches, declining annual cost), but the tail is not closed.

**Co-60 supply concentration** is the second material risk: ~20% of long-term Co-60 comes from Russian reactors, spiking to ~50% in some years on outage timing. Sanctions, license renewals (2026–2028), and reliance on a single licensed ocean carrier for radioactive goods create genuine supply fragility. Mitigants include the Westinghouse/PSEG U.S. Co-60 program and the Darlington (Ontario) project nearing completion.

**EtO regulatory tightening** (EPA 2024 NESHAP, FIFRA Interim Decision) requires costly controls; a July 2025 White House proclamation granted a 2-year exemption, but a January 2026 lawsuit seeks to invalidate it, which could accelerate compliance and capex. **Leverage and floating-rate exposure** compound in a higher-for-longer path, though net leverage is falling and interest expense is declining after repricing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 46.1% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 37.0% (a 9.1-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (46.1% near-term vs 44.0% at six months), signaling the market is pricing an imminent catalyst — most likely the next earnings print and ongoing litigation headlines. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±10.5%. Put/call skew is balanced (0.8%), so there is no directional bearish tilt in positioning; the elevated IV reflects event uncertainty rather than a one-sided bet. ATM IV rose 3.0 points over the prior five sessions on a flat price, consistent with rising event-hedging demand. For a fundamental holder, the rich near-term IV argues against chasing and favors patience on entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The EtO litigation resolves in an orderly, cheaper cadence with no further covenant-threatening verdict; the market re-rates the equity as the tail risk fades and the leverage screens are recognized as artifacts. Steady 5–7% organic EBITDA growth plus ongoing deleveraging (interest expense falling, net leverage below 3x) drives adjusted EPS toward the high end of guidance and beyond. New management stabilizes Nelson Labs and reallocates capital efficiently. On ~$1.06+ next-year adjusted EPS at a ~22–23x multiple (justified by the duopoly and margins) the stock reaches ~$23–24, matching the top analyst target.

**Bear case.** A large adverse Georgia, California, or New Mexico verdict — or an unstayed judgment triggering appellate-bond or covenant consequences — reignites the liquidity concern the balance sheet screens flag. The EtO exemption is invalidated, pulling forward compliance capex and pressuring FCF. Rates stay high, keeping floating-rate interest elevated. Nelson Labs continues to shrink and modality shift accelerates against the Co-60/EtO franchise. Multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range and the stock retraces toward the ~$13–14 lows and the sponsors' exit price of ~$15.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is steady compounding with an improving balance sheet and a de-risking legal tail, but the current multiple and flat estimate revisions leave the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A definitive resolution or global settlement of the remaining Georgia/California/New Mexico EtO claims that removes the covenant-default tail (litigation cost trending below ~$30M/year with no active large-verdict exposure).
- Estimate-revision inflection — current-year adjusted EPS revised up meaningfully (positive 30-day revisions) alongside confirmed net leverage below 3.0x.
- A pullback toward the mid-teens ($14–15, the sponsors' exit zone), which would restore asymmetric upside to the $20 median target.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new adverse EtO verdict above the covenant threshold ($162.6M or 30% of EBITDA) or any appellate-bond requirement that strains liquidity.
- Invalidation of the EtO regulatory exemption forcing accelerated compliance capex that meaningfully compresses FCF.
- A Co-60 supply disruption (Russian sanctions/license lapse) that impairs Nordion volumes, or a guidance cut on the core segments.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026 print) for guidance/segment trajectory and litigation updates; ongoing monitoring of the Georgia docket and the January 2026 exemption lawsuit.

**Base case target: $19.50** — ~10.7x forward EV/EBITDA on ~$636M 2026 adjusted EBITDA, or ~18x next-year adjusted EPS of ~$1.06, roughly +11% from $17.49 and consistent with sitting just below the $20 median analyst target.
**Bull case target: $24** (+37%) — successful litigation resolution and re-rating to ~22–23x forward adjusted EPS.
**Bear case target: $13.50** (-23%) — adverse verdict / covenant scare and multiple compression toward the lows.

## Conclusion

Sotera Health is a high-quality, wide-moat compounder whose reported balance-sheet screens materially overstate its financial fragility: net leverage of 3.2x and falling, with declining interest expense and >$900M of liquidity, is a genuinely improving picture behind an Altman Z of 0.9 that reflects PE-legacy leverage and litigation-driven negative equity. The competitive position is the clearest strength: a protected duopoly with 51% adjusted EBITDA margins, ~90% contracted revenue, and structural demand. The 2026 sponsor exit and outside-CEO transition remove a long-standing overhang and reset governance in the company's favor.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the improving narrative is largely priced. The stock has run ~48% off its low, trades at ~43x trailing / ~16.5x forward adjusted earnings and ~11x forward EV/EBITDA, and estimate revisions are flat. The EtO litigation is de-risking but not closed, and its covenant linkage to a single large verdict is a real asymmetric tail that insurance no longer covers. We would move to a BUY on a definitive litigation resolution, a positive revision inflection with confirmed sub-3x leverage, or a pullback toward the mid-teens where the sponsors themselves exited.
