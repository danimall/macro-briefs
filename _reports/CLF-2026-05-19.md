## Executive Summary

Cleveland-Cliffs is a vertically integrated North American steel producer trading at $10.35 with a $5.9B market cap, $7.3B in long-term debt, and a trailing net loss of $1.4B. The company is attempting to execute a turnaround driven by 50% steel tariffs, elimination of an unprofitable slab contract, and cost restructuring after the poorly-timed $2.5B Stelco acquisition. Q1 2026 showed early signs of improvement (positive EBITDA of $95M vs. -$179M a year earlier), and management projects positive free cash flow from Q2 2026.

However, the bull case requires sustained HRC prices above $900/ton, automotive production recovery, and continued tariff protection — all while servicing $594M in annual interest on 7.5%+ coupon debt. EPS estimates are declining sharply across all periods (current year down -313% over 90 days). The CEO sold his entire 3M common share position in February 2026, and the CFO is the CEO's son. These governance signals, combined with an Altman Z-Score of 1.81 (distress boundary), elevated short interest (14.9%), and 3 misses in the last 4 earnings quarters, create a risk profile incompatible with an investment recommendation at current levels.

## Company Overview

Cleveland-Cliffs operates as the most vertically integrated steel producer in North America, spanning iron ore mining (20.1M long tons annual pellet capacity), HBI production, cokemaking, scrap processing, and primary steelmaking through 7 blast furnaces and 4 EAFs with ~20M net tons of annual raw steel capacity. Downstream operations include finishing, stamping, tooling, and tubing.

Revenue breakdown: Automotive (~30% direct), Infrastructure & Manufacturing (29%), Distributors & Converters (28%), and Steel Producers (13%). The company is the dominant supplier of automotive-grade steel in the U.S. and a leading producer of electrical steels (GOES/NOES) for power grid transformers and EV motors. The November 2024 completion of the Stelco acquisition added what management characterizes as the lowest-cost flat-rolled capacity in North America.

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory from 2022 to 2025 reflects severe deterioration:

- Revenue declined from $23.0B (2022) to $18.6B (2025), a 19% contraction
- Operating income swung from +$1.9B (2022) to -$1.6B (2025)
- Adjusted EBITDA collapsed from $3.2B (2022) to $37M (2025)
- Net loss of $1.4B in FY2025 (-$2.90/share)
- Free cash flow was -$1.0B in 2025

**Balance Sheet:** $7.3B long-term debt (up from $3.1B in 2023), $57M cash, $3.2B ABL availability. Debt-to-equity of 2.24x. Interest expense runs $594M annually at 7.5-7.625% blended coupon rates. The Altman Z-Score of 1.81 sits at the boundary of the distress zone (below 1.81 indicates high default probability).

**Q1 2026 showed improvement:** Revenue $4.9B (+6.3% YoY), adjusted EBITDA $95M, adjusted EPS -$0.40 (vs. -$0.92 prior year). HRC averaged $980/ton (+24% YoY). However, the quarter was pressured by $80M in unexpected winter energy costs.

The capital structure is precarious. At $594M annual interest, CLF needs roughly $2.4B in annual EBITDA just to achieve a 4x interest coverage ratio — a level the company last achieved in 2023 ($1.9B EBITDA) and hasn't exceeded since 2022. Current run-rate EBITDA (annualizing Q1's $95M = $380M) covers interest expense only 0.6x.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis depends on several converging factors:

**Positive catalysts:**
1. 50% Section 232 tariffs have reduced finished steel imports to 18% of U.S. consumption (from 23% in 2024)
2. Unprofitable slab contract expiration (Dec 9, 2025) removes ~1.5M tons of loss-making volume
3. HRC prices averaging $980/ton in Q1 2026, up 24% YoY
4. Automotive tariffs (25% on vehicles and parts) should boost domestic production
5. Cost cuts from facility closures ($300M in charges taken) flowing through in 2026

**Negative factors:**
1. EPS estimates in freefall: current year estimate moved from +$0.20 to -$0.43 over 90 days (-313%)
2. Next year estimates fell from $0.76 to $0.46 (-39%) over 90 days
3. Auto production remains 10% below pre-COVID levels at 15.3M units
4. Only 1 beat in 4 recent quarters, with the most recent quarter missing by -7.9%
5. New EAF capacity from NUE/STLD adding domestic supply pressure

The estimate revision data is unambiguously negative. Current quarter estimates fell from +$0.11 to -$0.20 over 90 days. The one outlier — next quarter estimates up 2032% over 30 days — reflects a revision from near-zero ($0.01) to $0.11, a small absolute move. Revenue growth of 6.3% in Q1 is primarily price-driven (HRC +24%) on essentially flat-to-lower volumes.

## Valuation Assessment

At $10.35, CLF trades at approximately:
- 0.31x trailing sales
- ~1.0x book value
- Negative forward P/E (-4.6x on trailing losses)
- EV/EBITDA is not meaningful on $37M trailing EBITDA

Against peers: Commercial Metals (CMC) trades at 15.7x P/E with 1.1% yield; Worthington Steel at 16.1x with 1.6% yield. CLF pays no dividend and is unprofitable. The peer median P/E of 32.8x is not applicable given CLF's negative earnings.

The stock trades at a distress-level multiple. At $10.35, the market is pricing roughly $1.5-2.0B in normalized EBITDA (implied EV/EBITDA ~8-9x on a ~$13B enterprise value). This pricing requires HRC prices sustained above $900/ton AND demand recovery AND successful deleveraging.

The valuation is neither compelling nor egregious. It reflects genuine uncertainty about whether the company can service its debt load through a potential economic slowdown. Analyst consensus targets show a median of $10.00 (below current), mean of $10.67 (roughly in line), suggesting limited institutional conviction in upside.

## Competitive Landscape

CLF's competitive position within flat-rolled steel is genuinely differentiated:

**Strengths:**
- Self-sufficient in iron ore pellets, insulating from volatile raw material markets
- Decades of automotive qualification infrastructure that requires years for competitors to replicate
- Only HBI producer in the Great Lakes region
- Rising scrap prices hurt EAF competitors (NUE, STLD) while benefiting CLF's blast furnace model
- Electrical steel production addresses structural grid investment demand

**Weaknesses:**
- Massively underperformed peers on TSR (91.2 vs. peer group 324.78)
- Higher cost structure than Nucor/STLD due to blast furnace model (energy-intensive)
- Stelco integration still in early stages
- Fixed-price contracts (~35-40% of volume) prevent full capture of spot price gains

The competitive moat in automotive steel is real. EAF producers struggle to match the surface quality and formability specifications required by automotive OEMs. This advantage becomes more valuable under tariff protection that limits Japanese/Korean imports. However, the moat has not translated into superior shareholder returns versus simpler EAF-focused competitors.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical risks:**

1. **Leverage/Solvency (Severe):** Z-Score 1.81 at distress boundary. If HRC drops to $800/ton or auto demand weakens, the company faces covenant pressure and potential restructuring discussions. Only $57M cash on hand.

2. **Tariff Dependency:** The entire thesis assumes permanence of 50% steel tariffs. Any exemptions, trade deals (USMCA joint review begins 2026), or policy reversal would immediately destroy the pricing support that makes CLF's cost structure viable.

3. **Estimate Trajectory:** Three misses in four quarters. Forward visibility is poor.

4. **POSCO Deal Uncertainty:** Management is "no longer in a hurry." This deal was the primary deleveraging mechanism. Without it, the company must deleverage through operating cash flow alone — a slower, riskier path.

5. **Labor:** Multiple major contracts expiring in 2026 across critical facilities.

6. **Macro environment:** With the current stagflationary backdrop, demand for steel (particularly automotive) faces cyclical headwinds. Elevated rates increase CLF's refinancing risk when maturities come due in 2029+.

## Investment Thesis

- Slab contract expiration adds ~$200-300M in incremental EBITDA
- Auto production recovers toward 16M units in 2027
- POSCO deal eventually closes, enabling $2-3B deleveraging
- Annualized EBITDA reaches $1.5-2.0B, stock re-rates to 6-7x EV/EBITDA
- Cost restructuring gains accelerate in H2 2026
- Probability: 20-25%

**Bear Case ($5-7):**
- HRC weakens below $850/ton on demand destruction or tariff rollback
- Auto production disappoints, remains sub-15.5M units

- Interest expense consumes all EBITDA improvement

- HRC stabilizes at $900-950/ton
- Modest EBITDA improvement to $800M-1.0B annualized

- Stock trades range-bound reflecting uncertainty
- Probability: 30-35%

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Next earnings report (likely July 2026 for Q2 results). Management has promised positive FCF starting Q2 — failure to deliver would likely trigger a 15-20% selloff given the leveraged balance sheet and collapsing estimates. Any material weakening in HRC spot prices (currently ~$980/ton, vs. needed $900+ for viability) would serve as a continuous negative catalyst.

**Timing window:** 2-3 months. Q2 earnings confirmation/failure is the cleanest binary event.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** At 14.9% SI and 4.7 days to cover, this is moderately crowded. The stock rallied 89% from its May 2025 low of $5.63 to October highs of $16.70, demonstrating capacity for violent short squeezes on tariff news. This is a meaningful risk for shorts.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options show modest IV premium (76.9% vs. 71.3% HV) but no extreme put skew (-2.5%), suggesting the options market is not pricing a near-term collapse. Borrow costs are likely elevated given SI levels. The lack of extreme put demand is slightly inconsistent with an imminent collapse thesis.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $5-7 (30-50% downside from $10.35) vs. potential squeeze to $14-15 (35-45% upside risk). Reward:risk is approximately 1.2:1 to 1.5:1 — below the 2:1 threshold for compelling shorts.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is correct (overleveraged company with deteriorating estimates and poor governance) but squeeze risk is meaningful given demonstrated rallies on tariff news, and the reward:risk ratio is insufficient. The proper expression is avoidance, not active shorting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow AND sustained HRC above $950/ton
2. POSCO deal closure or equivalent deleveraging event reducing net debt below $5.5B
3. CEO purchasing common shares at meaningful levels (>$1M open-market buy)
4. Estimate revisions turning positive for at least 2 consecutive months

**Timeline:** The headwinds are likely to persist 6-12 months minimum. Even the bull case requires multiple quarters of execution. The balance sheet will not be right-sized before mid-2027 at the earliest under any realistic scenario.

## Conclusion

Cleveland-Cliffs presents a classic overleveraged turnaround situation with a narrow path to success and multiple failure modes. The company's competitive position in automotive steel is genuine, the tariff environment is supportive, and Q1 2026 showed the first positive EBITDA in over a year. These are real positives.

However, the weight of evidence tilts decisively negative: the CEO sold 100% of his common shares (the single most informative insider signal), EPS estimates are collapsing at -313% over 90 days, the company has missed 3 of 4 recent quarters, leverage sits at distress-zone levels with $594M in annual interest against sub-$400M trailing EBITDA, and the governance structure (CEO's son as CFO, $19M CEO comp on a $1.4B loss year) suggests misaligned incentives.

The analyst consensus median target of $10.00 — below the current price — combined with 9 of 12 analysts at Hold, confirms that institutional conviction in the turnaround is thin. At current levels, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside: the bear case ($5-7, 40-50% probability) carries higher probability than the bull case ($14-15, 20-25% probability). Recommend avoidance until balance sheet repair is demonstrated through multiple quarters of positive FCF and verifiable deleveraging.
