**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Par Pacific is a Houston-based independent downstream refiner whose competitive position rests on geographic isolation rather than scale. Its four refineries (219 Mbpd combined) serve supply-constrained Hawaii and an inland corridor from Washington to Wyoming where import competition is structurally limited. The Hawaii facility is the only refinery in the state. These attributes give Par higher and more stable capture rates than coastal merchant refiners, which is the durable foundation of the investment case.

The near-term setup is two-sided and unusually high in variance. On the positive side, Singapore 3-1-2 crack spreads spiked to roughly $72/bbl in April 2026 versus a 2025 average near $16, driven by Persian Gulf export reductions and Asian refinery run cuts. With no crack hedges in place, Par has direct leverage to this super-spike, and consensus has responded: current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 228% over 90 days to $14.06, and next-year estimates up 142% to $10.00. The Hawaii Renewable Fuels Facility, which achieved on-spec renewable diesel in late April 2026, adds an earnings ramp in H2 2026. On the negative side, the company missed Q1 2026 EPS by 13.9% (the second miss in two quarters), the stock fell ~11-13% on the print, a 30-45 day Hawaii hydrocracker turnaround caps Q3 capture, and a large slice of 2025's headline earnings came from a one-time $199.5M Small Refinery Exemption benefit that cannot be repeated.

We rate PARR a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The 12-month thesis is specific: capture the unhedged Q2 crack windfall and the renewables ramp against the headwinds of trailing-earnings overstatement and turnaround drag. At $52.53 (current), trading at ~7.3x reported trailing EPS but a more meaningful ~5.3x next-year consensus of $10.00, the stock is not expensive on normalized power, but the earnings base is unusually difficult to anchor and the variance is wide enough that we do not see the asymmetric reward required for a BUY in this sector.

## Company Overview

Par Pacific operates three segments. Refining is the earnings engine: Kapolei, Hawaii (~84 Mbpd); Billings, Montana (~52 Mbpd, acquired June 2023); Tacoma, Washington (~39 Mbpd); and Newcastle, Wyoming (~13-17 Mbpd). Logistics (pipelines, terminals, storage) contributes a steady ~$120-150M annual adjusted gross margin, largely intersegment. Retail (gas stations and convenience stores in Hawaii and the Pacific Northwest) generates roughly $75M of operating income.

The competitive advantage is structural. The Hawaii refinery's monopoly position gives it pricing power and a state energy-security role that management explicitly factors into turnaround scheduling. The inland Rockies refineries access discounted Canadian and Bakken crude (WCS Hardisty traded ~$11/bbl below WTI in 2025). Par also holds an equity stake in Laramie Energy (natural gas E&P) and minority interests in refining/logistics JVs inherited from the Billings deal.

## Financial Analysis

Extreme cyclicality defines the financials. Net income swung from +$728.6M (2023) to -$33.3M (2024) to +$369.4M (2025) on relatively stable ~$7.5-8.2B revenue. This is a margin-driven business, not volume-driven.

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $8.23B | $7.97B | $7.46B |
| Operating income | $680M | $48M | $539M |
| Net income (to PARR) | $728.6M | -$33.3M | $369.4M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $696M | $239M | $634M |
| Operating cash flow | $579M | $84M | $445M |
| Refining Adj. GM/bbl | $16.01 | $9.05 | $14.60 |

Two adjustments matter for anyone valuing the company on trailing numbers. First, 2025's rebound was materially flattered by the SRE benefit recognized in August 2025 for 2019-2024 compliance years. Stripping it out, refining adjusted GM/bbl was $11.64 versus the reported $14.60, and the SRE drove $202.6M of the $394.8M adjusted EBITDA improvement. Second, 2023's headline net income was inflated by a $277.7M deferred-tax valuation-allowance release. Both years require normalization before they can serve as an earnings anchor.

The balance sheet is healthy and de-risked. The Altman Z-Score of 3.57 sits in the safe zone. Long-term debt fell from $1.11B (2024) to $798M (2025) on $332.5M of net repayments. Liquidity stands at $914.6M ($164M cash plus $750M ABL availability), with a current ratio of 1.61. Reported D/E of 1.51 overstates true leverage because it includes inventory-financing obligations under Citi/Wells Fargo intermediation agreements rather than traditional term debt; gross term debt was only $638M in Q1 2026, below the low end of management's leverage targets. A December 2025 amendment cut the Term Loan margin by 50 bps.

The Q1 2026 print is the most important recent data point. Adjusted EBITDA was $91M, adjusted EPS $0.78 (versus -$0.94 a year ago), revenue $1.82B (+4.5% YoY). The headline miss versus the $0.91 estimate was driven primarily by a $125.5M adverse Hawaii price-lag headwind when product prices spiked. Hawaii sales are contractually priced on prior-month/prior-week averages, so a rapid product-price rise produces a timing loss that reverses in declining-price periods. This is a real, recurring volatility source rather than an economic loss, and management expects partial Q2 reversal. The company also set a Q1 conventional throughput record (184 Mbpd).

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data warrants explicit treatment:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Rev. |
|--------|-------------|---------|----------|
| Current Year | $14.06 | $4.28 | +228.4% |
| Next Year | $10.00 | $4.14 | +141.5% |
| Next Quarter | $3.27 | $1.90 | +72.1% |

Revisions of this magnitude normally constitute one of the strongest forward signals available. The guardrail here is that the signal applies cleanly to inflection stocks recovering from a depressed base and trading cheaply relative to the improving estimates. Par qualifies on the cheapness dimension (~5.3x next-year consensus), and the inflection is real (cracks moved from $16 to $72). What tempers the weighting is the source: the revision surge is overwhelmingly a function of the Asian crack super-spike, which is a commodity-price event Par neither controls nor hedges. Cracks have already begun converging back toward global parity, and the Hawaii Q3 turnaround caps how much of the summer window Par captures. The estimate momentum is genuine, but its durability is tied to a volatile external variable rather than a structural step-change in the business.

There are two more durable growth levers. The Hawaii Renewable Fuels Facility (63.5% Par / 36.5% Alohi, with Alohi being a Mitsubishi/ENEOS vehicle) reached on-spec renewable diesel in late April 2026 and is transitioning to SAF mode, with an earnings ramp expected H2 2026 after the Hawaii turnaround. The excess RIN asset position, where Par has monetized less than half of its SRE-related RINs, provides a multi-quarter working-capital tailwind that GAAP earnings understate.

The track record on delivering against estimates is mixed: two beats and two misses across the last four quarters, with the most recent two being misses (-9.9% in Q4 2025, -13.9% in Q1 2026). This argues for treating the rising consensus with some skepticism on near-term delivery, since visibility into the price-lag swing factor is poor.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.53, PARR trades at ~7.3x reported trailing EPS of $7.16 and ~5.3x the next-year consensus of $10.00. Against the peer set, the picture is nuanced:

| REX | Rex American | 15.3 | — |
| CVI | CVR Energy | neg. | 1.3% |
| **Peer median** | | **10.8** | **2.5%** |

On a headline basis, Par trades at a discount to the peer median P/E, but the discount is partly earned. Par's trailing earnings are overstated by the SRE windfall, so the true normalized multiple is higher than 7.3x suggests; on the ex-SRE refining margin of $11.64/bbl, the trailing earnings power is closer to peer-comparable. The company also pays no dividend versus a 2.5% peer median, removing the yield support that anchors peer valuations.

The cleaner question is whether the next-year multiple of ~5.3x represents asymmetric upside. If the $10.00 consensus holds, the stock is meaningfully cheap and analyst targets ($60 low, $80 high, $75 mean) imply 14-52% upside. But that $10.00 embeds continued elevated cracks; in a normalized crack environment closer to 2024 levels, earnings would compress materially toward the 2024 trough. The valuation is a function of where in the crack cycle one normalizes, and the wide dispersion in plausible outcomes is why we do not assign a high valuation score. A low headline multiple driven by an overstated and cyclically-peaking earnings base is the value-trap profile to be cautious about, and the deteriorating analyst sentiment trend (one buy-to-hold shift and a downgrade in the hold count in recent revisions despite higher targets) reinforces caution.

We do not penalize the stock for having appreciated sharply over the trailing year; that move reflects the genuine fundamental recovery from the 2024 trough. But proximity to consensus targets here is not a floor we treat as conservative, because at this point in the cycle the targets and the estimates both extrapolate the same elevated crack environment.

## Competitive Landscape

Par competes against integrated majors and large independents (Marathon, Phillips 66, HF Sinclair) that enjoy crude-production integration, scale, and stronger balance sheets. Par offsets these disadvantages through geographic insulation rather than scale. Its island and inland markets limit import competition, supporting structurally higher and more stable capture rates.

The 2026 industry backdrop favors surviving refiners. Global refining capacity rationalization (aging Asian refineries running at minimum rates, Persian Gulf export reductions, protectionist trade policy) is tightening product supply and widening cracks, particularly in distillate and jet, where Par carries an above-average ~15% jet yield. Par's Hawaii operations are indexed to Singapore, making it a direct beneficiary of Asian product tightness. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in the current environment, though not in a way that compounds independently of the crack cycle.

The counter-trend is long-term structural demand risk: EV adoption, state low-carbon fuel standards (Washington CCA, Hawaii renewable mandates), and ESG-driven capital flight. These are gradual rather than imminent threats but do cap the terminal value a buyer should assign.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile carries several specific, quantified items that are not fully reflected in the headline multiple:

1. **Crack-spread volatility with no hedges.** A single year (2024) of compressed cracks turned the company unprofitable. The deliberate choice to run unhedged captures upside but leaves earnings fully exposed to a commodity variable Par cannot control.
2. **RIN/RFS regulatory dependency.** The 2025 beat hinged on EPA SREs, which are inherently political. The 10-K explicitly warns the administration's posture on SREs and RVO levels is uncertain, and that fewer waivers or higher RVO would raise RIN costs. Environmental compliance cost rose $98.4M in 2025 on current production.
3. **Single-asset concentration.** The Wyoming refinery was idled 66 days in 2025 after a February operational incident, demonstrating how one event at a small asset swings comparability. The Hawaii Q3 2026 hydrocracker turnaround is a known near-term drag.
4. **Labor.** United Steelworkers agreements covering ~22% of the workforce expired January 31, 2026 and are on rolling 24-hour extensions, an unresolved strike risk.
5. **Hawaii price-lag.** The Q1 headwind illustrates a recurring timing-driven swing factor that makes any single quarter a poor read on underlying profitability.
6. **Environmental remediation overhang** (~$15.8M Wyoming, ~$11.6M wastewater, ~$8.6M Billings) and Renewable Fuels Facility execution/credit-pathway risk.

A May 14, 2026 8-K disclosed a new material financing agreement (Items 1.01/2.03) that is not yet detailed and warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 80.6% versus 55.6% historical volatility (rich by 25 points), at the 88th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (80.6% near-term versus 54.4% long-term), the put/call skew is elevated at 25.7%, and the put/call volume ratio is 2.98. ATM IV jumped 20.4 points (60.2% to 80.6%) while the price fell 11.4% since the prior snapshot. This configuration signals the market expects a near-term move and is paying up for downside protection, consistent with the wide-variance crack/turnaround setup. Short interest is also elevated at 13.5% of float (3.8 days to cover), confirming institutional bearish positioning. For a stock we rate HOLD, this divergence reinforces caution rather than conviction: the options market is not corroborating the bullish estimate-revision story.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Unhedged exposure to the Asian crack super-spike drives an exceptional Q2 2026, the Hawaii price-lag headwind reverses, the Renewable Fuels Facility ramps in H2 2026, excess RINs provide a cash tailwind, and the next-year $10.00 consensus proves achievable. At 7-8x that figure, the stock reaches the $70-80 range. Continued opportunistic buybacks (the company repurchased >14M shares — about 20% of shares outstanding — at an average ~$25 versus current ~$52) compound per-share value.

**Bear case.** Cracks converge back toward global parity (already underway), the Q3 Hawaii turnaround caps summer capture, the SRE benefit does not recur and RIN economics tighten under an uncertain EPA posture, and earnings revert toward a normalized base well below the elevated 2025/2026 prints. A repeat of 2024-type crack compression would push earnings toward breakeven. In that scenario the stock retraces toward the high-$30s, near where it traded in late 2025.

The base case sits between: Q2 is strong but the turnaround and crack convergence moderate the full-year figure to somewhere between the elevated consensus and a normalized mid-cycle level, leaving the stock range-bound around current levels with upside if cracks hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale.** The specific 12-month thesis: the unhedged Q2 2026 crack windfall plus the Renewable Fuels Facility H2 ramp provide identifiable catalysts for appreciation, offset by trailing-earnings overstatement, the Q3 turnaround, and crack-convergence risk. We do not see the asymmetric reward needed for a BUY, nor fundamental deterioration sufficient for an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 results (early August) confirm the crack windfall flowed through with adjusted EBITDA materially above the ~$91M Q1 level, AND management indicates cracks are holding rather than collapsing.
- Renewable Fuels Facility demonstrates a quantified, repeatable earnings contribution (e.g., >$50M annualized adjusted EBITDA run-rate) in H2 2026.
- The stock pulls back toward the low-$40s on no fundamental change, restoring asymmetric upside to analyst targets.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Singapore/consolidated cracks revert toward 2024 trough levels and management guides to a normalized refining margin near $9-10/bbl.
- A USW strike or unplanned outage at Hawaii or Billings materially disrupts throughput.
- The May 14 financing agreement, once detailed, reveals leverage or covenant deterioration; or NOL/Section 382 issues surface.
- A third consecutive EPS miss with the price-lag or RIN dependency proving structurally negative rather than timing-driven.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (early August) is the decisive near-term checkpoint, as it captures the crack super-spike before the Q3 turnaround drag.

## Conclusion

Par Pacific is a well-run, balance-sheet-healthy independent refiner with a genuine moat in geographic isolation and a credible, value-accretive buyback record. The current setup is unusually high in variance: a powerful crack-spread tailwind feeding rising estimates, against a trailing-earnings base inflated by a non-recurring SRE windfall, a known Q3 turnaround drag, and a commodity-price driver the company neither controls nor hedges. The options market is pricing imminent stress and short interest is elevated, both signaling that sophisticated investors are not corroborating the bullish revision story.

On forward earnings the stock is not expensive, but the difficulty of anchoring a normalized earnings base, combined with two consecutive misses and degrading analyst sentiment, means the reward is not asymmetric enough to recommend purchase in a refining sector where company-specific selection skill has historically been hard to come by. We rate PARR a HOLD, with Q2 2026 results as the catalyst that should resolve the thesis in either direction.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: trailing P/E was overstated as cheaper than reality (~7.3x, not ~5.8x, on EPS of $7.16), and the peer-discount framing ("roughly half the peer median") was overstated — Par trades at a discount but not half; the valuation case is somewhat less attractive than originally presented.
