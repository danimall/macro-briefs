## Executive Summary

Zoetis is the largest animal health company in the world and, until roughly nine months ago, one of the more reliable premium-multiple compounders in health care. That status has been dismantled by a demand break in a single business line. U.S. companion animal revenue — $4,220M in FY2025, or 44.6% of consolidated revenue — declined 11% in Q1 2026 and 11% again in Q2 2026. Because that segment carries the highest gross margin in the company (82.2% in Q1 2026 versus 70.9% for International), the revenue decline is compounding into margin compression. FY2026 adjusted cost of sales guidance was raised from ~28.5% to ~29.0% of revenue after three consecutive years of improvement (30.0% → 29.4% → 28.2% GAAP).

The guidance sequence is the decisive data series. Revenue guidance has gone from $9,825–10,025M in February to $9,680–9,960M in May to $9,120–9,320M on August 6 — a $705M, 7.1% cut from the original midpoint, with organic operational growth reset from +3% to +5% down to −3% to −1%. Adjusted EPS guidance fell 10.5% at the midpoint in one quarter, from $6.85–7.00 to $6.15–6.25. Against 1H 2026 adjusted EPS of $3.40, the new full-year guide implies 2H 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.80 versus $3.18 in 2H 2025, a 12% decline — so the reported 1H "growth" of +7% inverts sharply in the back half. That 1H growth was in any case a financing outcome: Q2 adjusted net income fell 1.26% while diluted shares fell 6.24%, which arithmetically produces the reported +5% adjusted EPS with zero operating contribution.

Three things keep this a HOLD rather than an AVOID. First, the balance sheet is sound — net debt of roughly $7.1B against ~$4.1B of TTM EBITDA is 1.7x versus a 3.50x covenant, interest coverage is ~16x, bonds trade near 95% of par on rates rather than credit, and FY2025 free cash flow of $2,283M equals 24.1% of revenue and a 7.0% yield on today's market cap. Second, at 12.5x guided 2026 adjusted EPS and 9.6x EV/2026E adjusted EBITDA, with a 2.74% dividend at a 34% payout, the multiple already embeds a business in decline. Third, the market's response to the August 6 cut — the worst of the three — was a 4.0% gain from the prior close, which suggests positioning was already below guidance. Against that, consensus current-year EPS of $6.87 sits 10.8% above the company's own $6.20 midpoint, so a mechanical wave of negative revisions is imminent, and I can identify no dated catalyst that would establish a floor in U.S. companion animal before the November print. I am not willing to pay for stabilization that has not begun.

## Company Overview

Zoetis was carved out of Pfizer (incorporated 2012, IPO 2013) and retains a patent and know-how license under which Pfizer controls prosecution and enforcement of certain licensed patents. It sells medicines, vaccines, diagnostics, biodevices and genetic tests across eight species and seven product categories.

FY2025 revenue of $9,467M split into a U.S. segment of $5,097M (54%; 83% companion animal), an International segment of $4,254M (45%; 56% companion animal), and $116M of contract manufacturing and human health. By species group, companion animal was ~70% of revenue ($6,587M, +5%) and livestock ~29% ($2,764M, −5% reported). Forty-two percent of revenue was denominated in non-USD currencies.

Concentration is central to the current problem. Simparica/Simparica Trio is approximately 16% of revenue and Apoquel/Apoquel Chewable approximately 12%; adding Cytopoint, Librela and the ceftiofur line, the top five product lines are ~42% of revenue and the top ten ~57%. Sales to the largest customer, an unnamed U.S. veterinary distributor, were approximately 16% of 2025 revenue. The demand signal Zoetis observes is therefore filtered through both distributor inventory decisions and clinic traffic, which is one reason the deterioration appeared abruptly rather than gradually.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2022 $8,080M → FY2023 $8,544M (+5.7%) → FY2024 $9,256M (+8.3%) → FY2025 $9,467M (+2.3%) → FY2026 guidance midpoint $9,220M (−2.6%). The FY2025 deceleration was already low-quality: management's own bridge attributes it to price +4%, other in-line volume +1%, key-franchise volume +1%, MFA divestiture −3%, FX −1%. Essentially all of 2025's growth was price, and price is precisely the lever management now says is unavailable, citing "macro-driven price sensitivity" and lower clinic visits.

**Where the damage sits.** U.S. companion animal: FY2025 $4,220M (+4%); Q1 2026 $865M (−11%); Q2 2026 $1,044M (−11%); 1H 2026 $1,909M versus $2,149M (−11%). At 44.6% of the FY2025 base, an 11% decline is roughly a 5-point consolidated drag, which is essentially the entire gap between the original +3–5% guide and the current −3% to −1%. Management attributes it to softer end-market demand, price sensitivity and heightened competitive pressure in dermatology and Simparica Trio, generic competition on Cerenia and Convenia, and lower Librela sales. Read against the concentration disclosure, the pressured franchises are the largest ones.

**The offsets are real but lower quality.** Global livestock 1H 2026 revenue was $1,451M versus $1,278M (+13.5% reported, +11% organic operational), with U.S. livestock up 23% in Q2. International Q2 revenue of $1,173M was +8% reported but only +5% operational, and 1H was +12% reported versus +7% operational — roughly 40% of reported International growth is currency, struck at July 21, 2026 rates. The Q2 U.S. livestock drivers are episodic by the company's own description: beef cattle "supply timing" and poultry vaccine sales "tied to disease outbreak activity." Separately, the elimination of the one-month international reporting lag effective January 1, 2026 shifted the timing of annual price increases into Q1 2026 and pushed delayed December 2025 orders into 2026, with prior periods recast (Q1 2025 revenue $2,220M → $2,198M). Organic operational growth is the only clean comparison, and it is −1% for the half.

A mix shift caused by the highest-margin business shrinking is not diversification. Customer concentration (16%, one distributor) and product concentration (top five = 42%) are unchanged.

**Margins.** Q2 2026 adjusted COGS was 27.1% of revenue versus 26.7%; 1H was 27.6% versus 27.4%; FY2026 guidance is ~29.0%, raised from ~28.5% three months earlier. The mechanism is arithmetic before any pricing effect: shrinking an 82%-gross-margin U.S. segment while growing a 71%-margin International segment compresses consolidated margin. Some portion is likely fixed-cost under-absorption on lower U.S. volumes; the company has not decomposed it.

**The cut flows almost entirely to earnings.** Against a $600M revenue midpoint reduction from May to August, adjusted SG&A guidance was cut $20M and adjusted R&D $15M. Reconciling: ~$432M of gross profit at ~72% margin, plus ~$46M from the 50bp COGS-ratio deterioration, less $35M of opex relief ≈ −$397M pretax; at the guided 20.5% rate that is ~−$315M, which ties exactly to the adjusted net income guidance change of $2,910M → $2,595M. There is no cost offset in the current plan. A "comprehensive cost and productivity program" is underway — $75M of employee termination costs in Q2 2026 alone, with FY significant items and deal costs guided up from ~$100M to ~$150M — but expected savings have not been quantified, which makes the 2027 bridge unmodelable from disclosure.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $2,904M (FY2024 $2,953M) against $621M of capex, giving FY2025 FCF of $2,283M, or 24.1% of revenue. OCF/net income was 1.09x, down from 1.19x. Q1 2026 OCF was $401M versus $515M with capex of $110M; Q1 is seasonally weakest and includes annual incentive payouts, and the calendar change shifted order timing, so I will not annualize it. As a labeled sensitivity only: if 2026 OCF declines in line with the ~8% adjusted net income decline, OCF ≈ $2.7B and FCF ≈ $2.1–2.2B, a 6.5–6.8% yield on the current market cap.

Debt principal was $9,150M at 12/31/2025 with maturities of $750M (2027), $1,350M (2028), $2,000M (2029 convertibles), $750M (2030) and $4,300M thereafter — no near-term wall. Net debt was ~$7,104M at 3/31/2026. Net debt/EBITDA on ~$4.1B TTM EBITDA is ~1.7x against a 3.50x covenant; FY2025 EBIT of $3,582M over $222M of interest is ~16x coverage; the current ratio was 3.15x. Long-term debt fair value of $8,635M against $9,045M carrying is consistent with rate levels, not credit stress. Moody's A3 stable (January 2025) and S&P BBB+ stable (April 2025) both predate the 2026 deterioration and should be treated as stale.

One data point in the screening layer needs discarding: an Altman Z-score of 1.44 is mis-specified (it reported EBIT/assets of 0.002 for a company with a ~38% operating margin and substituted book equity for retained earnings and market value). Recomputing the standard public-manufacturer model on FY2025 as-reported figures gives Z ≈ 4.59, and the 3/31/26 balance sheet with current market cap gives ≈ 4.7 — safe zone. Reported debt/equity of 2.7–3.6x is likewise an artifact of $11,288M of treasury stock having compressed book equity to $3,233M. This is an operating and competitive problem, not a solvency problem.

**Reconciling the supplied multiples.** The market-data feed shows forward P/E equal to trailing P/E at 12.18 on EPS of $6.02, which is a trailing computation duplicated into the forward field rather than a genuine forward multiple. Using company guidance: at $77.37, adjusted 2026 P/E is 12.5x ($6.20 midpoint) and GAAP 2026 P/E is 13.8x ($5.60 midpoint).

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to underwrite in the next four quarters. Consolidated revenue is guided down 2.6% and organic operational growth down 1–3%. Adjusted EPS of $6.20 compares to approximately $6.37 in FY2025 (1H $3.19 plus $1.70 and $1.48), so adjusted EPS declines ~2.7% despite a ~6% reduction in share count — meaning adjusted net income falls roughly 8%. The back half is worse than the reported half: implied 2H adjusted EPS of $2.80 versus $3.18 is a 12% decline.

Consensus has not caught up. Current-year consensus of $6.87 is 10.8% above the guidance midpoint, and current-quarter consensus of $1.85 is inconsistent with a 2H total of $2.80 split across two quarters. The "estimates stable" reading in the revision data (−0.1% over 30 days, −2.3% over 90 days for the current year) reflects the August 6 release not yet being incorporated. Investors should expect current-year and next-year cuts of roughly 8–11% within weeks. That is the single most important near-term mechanical driver, and it argues against treating the current 12.5x on guidance as though it were 11.3x on consensus.

The credible growth vectors, ranked by verifiability:

1. **Livestock, U.S. screwworm.** New World screwworm is confirmed in the United States. Zoetis holds the first and only FDA conditional approval for prevention and treatment of screwworm myiasis in beef cattle (Dectomax-CA1, 2025), and Dectomax plus Dectomax-CA1 received emergency use authorization across swine, sheep, deer, horses, and dairy cattle categories. This is a genuine, company-specific U.S. catalyst. The Q2 release lists it under "Investments in Growth" rather than as a Q2 revenue driver, which suggests it is prospective.
2. **Long-acting osteoarthritis pain monoclonals.** Lenivia (izenivetmab, dogs) and Portela (relfovetmab, cats) — three-month dosing — are approved in the EU and Canada, launched in Q2 2026, and approved in Great Britain and Switzerland. No U.S. approval appears in any filing. U.S. Librela sales are declining, so the successor cohort has not yet reached the troubled market. Note the ambiguity: a three-month product priced at less than 3x a monthly product cannibalizes revenue even while defending share.
3. **Diagnostics.** VPG (2025), VitalRADS teleradiology (July 2026), and the pending Neogen animal genomics acquisition (reported at $160M by a single secondary source; the 10-Q confirms a definitive agreement with expected 2H 2026 close subject to regulatory approvals, and an Australian competition review has reportedly been flagged). Directionally counter-cyclical, since labs and imaging capture spend when drug volumes fall. But diagnostics was $117M in Q1 2026 — about 5% of revenue — and cannot offset an 11% decline in a $4.2B business for several years.
4. **Cost program.** $75M of Q2 termination costs implies meaningful headcount action, but with no quantified savings this is a 2027 possibility rather than a modelable input.

Management claims "more than 12 potential blockbusters" (≥$100M annual sales) across chronic kidney disease, oncology, cardiology, anxiety and obesity, plus a commitment to a significant approval in a major market annually. No phase status, timelines or probabilities are disclosed, so this is aspiration, not forecast. R&D is being cut in absolute dollars ($735–745M → $720–730M) while rising as a share of revenue to 7.9% from 7.4% — the cut is modest, but it is not leaning in.

## Competitive Landscape

The animal health industry has been structurally attractive because it is predominantly self-pay (no reimbursement squeeze), veterinarians both prescribe and dispense, individual product markets are too small to attract scaled generic entrants, and brand loyalty persists after exclusivity loss. Three of those four supports are weakening in the U.S. simultaneously.

**Generic entry has arrived and is moving upmarket.** The 10-K's own data refutes the brand-loyalty claim in at least two cases: Draxxin U.S. sales are down 66% since 2021 generic entry and Rimadyl chewable down 39%. Cerenia's active-ingredient patent has expired everywhere with a U.S. generic injectable approved; Convenia generic competition appears in Q1 and Q2 2026 disclosure but not in the 10-K's patent-expiry list, suggesting it is recent. The ceftiofur line (Excede/Naxcel), a top-five product line, has no remaining active-ingredient protection and formulation patents running only to September 2026 in Japan and August 2027 in Brazil, with generics already present in Brazil, Mexico and Russia.

**Branded competition has reached the crown jewels.** Management now uses "heightened competitive pressure" for both the dermatology franchise (Apoquel, Cytopoint) and Simparica Trio in consecutive quarters, without naming competing products. Apoquel was a first-in-class JAK inhibitor approved in 2013; Simparica Trio was a first triple-combination parasiticide in the U.S. in 2020. Second entrants against near-monopoly positions change the pricing environment in a step function rather than gradually. Notably, the 10-K discloses no expiry dates for sarolaner or oclacitinib — the active ingredients of the two franchises that together are 28% of revenue.

**Self-pay cuts both ways.** With no insurance intermediary, pet-owner discretionary income is the demand curve. Management's own language is "lower clinic visits and pet owner price sensitivity." Price contributed +4 points of FY2025's +2.3% total growth; that lever now appears exhausted. This is consistent with the broader consumer picture — mortgage rates at one-year highs and a negative housing wealth effect pressure exactly the discretionary categories where a $250 clinic visit competes with other household spending.

Named competitors are Boehringer Ingelheim, Merck Animal Health, Elanco and IDEXX. In diagnostics — where Zoetis is investing hardest — IDEXX is the entrenched incumbent and Zoetis's base is ~5% of revenue. The 10-K also flags online retailers pressuring margins, and the 16%-of-revenue distributor sits precisely where clinic-channel disintermediation would first appear.

Trajectory, not level, drives the competitive score. Zoetis remains the scale leader with 21 owned manufacturing sites, ~3,900 sales and technical staff, >5,500 granted patents and ~1,700 R&D personnel. But the direction in its largest and most profitable market is clearly negative, and the erosion is now inside the top two franchises rather than the mid-tier. Score: 3.5, continuing the decline from 8.5 in February.

## Risk Assessment

**Product and customer concentration.** Top two products at 28% of revenue, top five at 42%, top ten at 57%, and 16% of revenue through a single unnamed U.S. distributor. Both concentrations are unchanged despite the revenue-mix shift.

**Undisclosed exclusivity timelines on 28% of revenue.** Sarolaner and oclacitinib expiry dates do not appear in the patent section. This is a disclosure gap rather than a finding, but it prevents modeling the largest single risk to the franchise.

**Securities litigation.** Multiple plaintiff firms have announced a class action with a July 27, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline, alleging misrepresentation of weakening veterinarian adoption and prescription trends. One firm cites a $23.91 per-share loss on the final disclosure. The FY2025 10-K Item 3 is boilerplate and describes materiality as "remote," consistent with the litigation post-dating the filing. Separately, plaintiff releases frame the claim as concealing "safety" deterioration in Librela; I found no FDA action, label change or company disclosure corroborating a safety event, and Zoetis attributes lower Librela sales to competitive and demand factors. Treat the safety allegation as unverified.

**IRS deemed-repatriation dispute.** For 2017–2018 the IRS has asserted approximately $450M of additional tax excluding interest and penalties on the one-time deemed repatriation tax, against total uncertain tax positions of $223M plus $56M of interest and penalties. The reserve is not sized to the full assertion. At ~1.4% of market cap, this is a real but non-existential contingent cash exposure.

**Guidance credibility.** Three cuts in six months, each larger than the last, with the August cut absorbed almost entirely into earnings. The probability that $6.20 itself holds through the November print is not high given that neither the demand driver nor the competitive driver has been quantified or bounded.

**Inventory and under-absorption.** Inventories of $2,430M at 12/31/2025 against FY2025 COGS of $2,666M implies roughly 333 days. Long biologics and vaccine cycles make high inventory normal in this industry and I have no multi-year series, so I make no trend claim. But elevated inventory into a deteriorating U.S. demand environment raises write-down and fixed-cost under-absorption risk.

**Execution risk.** A multi-year ERP replacement ($29M of 2025 costs, $11M in 1H26) is running concurrently with a headcount-reduction program and a demand shock. The Q1 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors from the 10-K even as its own MD&A describes an increasingly competitive landscape — legally defensible if generic and competitive risk factors already existed, but it means Item 1A is not the place to look for the current problem.

**Currency.** Forty-two percent of revenue is non-USD, guidance is struck at July 21, 2026 rates, and roughly 40% of reported 1H International growth is FX. A dollar rally would remove a visible portion of the offset.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.4% is essentially in line with 38.1% one-year realized (spread 1.3%), and IV has fallen from 47.6% on July 19 to 39.4% — the post-earnings volatility crush following the August 6 release. The term structure is in contango, with 1-month IV at 39.4%, 3-month at 36.4% and 6-month at 43.0%, so the market prices more uncertainty five to six months out than immediately ahead. That is consistent with the view that resolution of the U.S. companion animal question sits in the November and February prints rather than the next few weeks. Put/call open interest of 1.40 and put/call volume of 1.86 indicate a put-tilted book, and open interest has moderated from 1.86 to 1.40 since mid-July. Near-term skew is reported as balanced (−0.4%); the six-month figure of −6.0% is larger in magnitude, and given the contango, the fair reading is that the options market is pricing elevated but not acute uncertainty concentrated in the December tenor. Options are not cheap enough to make long-dated calls an attractive substitute for equity, and the ±6.6% implied move on the August 21 expiry does not suggest a near-dated binary.

Short interest of 5.1% of float with 2.8 days to cover is uncrowded. The bearish thesis is not consensus-positioned, which cuts both ways: more room for shorts, but also no sign that informed capital is pressing it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $105 (+35.7%)

The de-rating from 30x+ to 12.5x has already discounted a multi-year impairment of the U.S. companion animal franchise, and the deterioration proves cyclical rather than structural. Pet owners deferring visits during a period of elevated rates and negative housing wealth effects return as the rate cycle turns; U.S. companion animal declines narrow from −11% toward −3% by mid-2027. The cost and productivity program — $75M of Q2 termination costs implies a real program — delivers $250M+ of annualized savings into 2027, restoring adjusted COGS toward 28%. Screwworm EUA products convert into U.S. livestock revenue in 2027; Lenivia and Portela obtain U.S. approval and defend the osteoarthritis pain franchise; the Neogen genomics and VitalRADS assets build a diagnostics business that captures clinic spend independent of drug volumes. Management redirects the remaining $1.8B authorization at 12.5x instead of 20x. 2027 adjusted EPS reaches $7.00 and the multiple re-rates to 15x on evidence of stabilization. Three independent directors, including a former Pfizer CFO, bought ~$886K on the open market at $75–78 within a week of the Q1 collapse, which is at least a data point that informed insiders saw value at these levels.

### Bear Case — $59 (−23.7%)

The competitive entry into Apoquel/Cytopoint dermatology and Simparica Trio is permanent, and second-entrant pricing pressure compounds rather than stabilizes. The 10-K's own evidence — Draxxin −66%, Rimadyl chewable −39% — becomes the template for how the top-five lines behave post-exclusivity, and the ceftiofur line's last formulation patents lapse in 2026–2027. Price, which delivered all of FY2025's growth, remains unavailable because the constraint is pet-owner income rather than competitive positioning. The mix shift toward 71%-margin International keeps compressing consolidated gross margin. Consensus cuts 10% within weeks and a fourth guidance cut arrives at the November print, taking 2026 adjusted EPS below $6.00 and 2027 to $5.40. FX reverses, removing the 3–5 points flattering International. Elevated inventory at 333 days forces write-downs and under-absorption charges. Credit outlooks, both stale, move to negative, constraining the buyback. 11x on $5.40 is $59.

### Base Case — $82 (+6.0%)

U.S. companion animal declines moderate but do not reverse; livestock and International offsets persist at a lower FX contribution; the cost program delivers enough to hold 2027 adjusted EPS roughly flat at $6.30. Consensus resets to guidance, the stock trades in a $70–90 range through two more prints, and total return is the 2.7% dividend plus modest multiple stability. This is the most likely path and it does not justify new capital at a 12.5x multiple that is not demonstrably below fair value for a business with no growth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment point: Q3 2026 results, expected early November 2026.** That print is the first opportunity to observe whether U.S. companion animal decline is narrowing, whether adjusted COGS is tracking to the ~29.0% guide, and whether the cost program is quantified.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following:**
1. U.S. companion animal decline narrowing to better than −5% year over year in a reported quarter (from −11% in each of the last two).
2. Management quantifying annualized savings from the cost and productivity program at $250M or more, with a stated 2027 phasing.
3. U.S. regulatory approval for Lenivia and/or Portela, which would put the three-month OA franchise into the market where Librela is declining.
4. Clustered C-suite open-market purchases (currently only independent directors have bought), or a price near $62–65, which would put the stock at ~10x the guided 2026 number with an 8%+ FCF yield.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of the following:**
1. A fourth consecutive guidance cut, or FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance reduced below $6.00.
2. U.S. companion animal decline worsening beyond −12% in a reported quarter, or the decline broadening into International companion animal.
3. FY2026 adjusted COGS guided above 29.5% of revenue, or a material inventory write-down disclosed.
4. Credit outlook revision to negative by Moody's or S&P, or continued buyback execution while net debt/EBITDA rises above 2.5x.
5. An FDA action or label change on Librela that converts the unverified safety allegation in the securities litigation into a disclosed fact.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** hold. The dividend is covered at a 34% payout, the balance sheet is investment grade at 1.7x net leverage, and the stock sits $6 above its 52-week low with the worst guidance cut already public. Do not add. New capital should wait for either evidence of U.S. stabilization or a price in the low $60s.

## Conclusion

**HOLD. Overall score 4.7, continuing a trajectory from 7.2 in February.**

The score decline is warranted by new information, not by the price decline. Since the last review, Zoetis cut FY2026 revenue guidance by a further $600M at the midpoint and adjusted EPS guidance by 10.5%, guided 2H adjusted EPS down 12% year over year, raised adjusted COGS guidance by 50bp, and reported a second consecutive 11% decline in the segment that represents 45% of revenue and the highest gross margin in the company. Competitive position drops to 3.5 from 4.5 because branded competition has now reached the two franchises that constitute 28% of revenue and the company's own disclosures show 39–66% post-generic erosion in comparable products. Growth prospects drop to 3.0 from 4.0 because revenue and adjusted net income are both guided to decline and the only quantified cost offset is $35M against a $600M revenue reduction.
