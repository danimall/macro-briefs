**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

H2O America (formerly SJW Group, renamed May 2025) is a regulated water and wastewater utility serving roughly 409,000 connections and 1.6 million people across California, Connecticut, Maine, and Texas. The investment case rests on the standard regulated-utility mechanics: invest capital into rate base, earn a state-authorized return on equity, and compound a dividend that has now risen for 58 consecutive years. Structural industry tailwinds are genuine: aging pipe replacement, PFAS/emerging-contaminant mandates that drive recoverable capex, and Sun Belt population migration toward the Texas corridor where the pending $540M Quadvest acquisition expands the footprint into metro Houston.

The problem is that the growth is not reaching per-share results. Net income rose 15% in Q1 2026, yet diluted EPS was flat at $0.49 because the share count has ballooned from roughly 35M (2025 average) toward 41.8M outstanding, with a 7.55M-share forward sale agreement still to settle and an ATM program running. Fully-diluted share count is heading toward ~49M. The company realized a ~6.7% ROE against authorized returns of 9.3%–10.88%, a persistent gap driven by regulatory lag, capex not yet in rate base, and continuous equity issuance. New debt is being placed at 5.83%–6.70% against a legacy book cost near 4.4%, and S&P moved the outlook to Negative in July 2025 over Quadvest. The cost of capital is rising as the balance sheet expands.

At $63.92, HTO trades at ~22x earnings, essentially in line with the water-utility peer median of 22.1x, near the top of its 52-week range ($43.75–$66.05), and within a few dollars of the consensus mean target of $65.43. The stock has run ~27% over the trailing year on multiple expansion and rate-driven revenue, but the forward return profile is capped by dilution and a rate-path headwind to the financing model. This is a well-run income vehicle with a strong dividend record rather than a mispriced growth story. We rate it HOLD on an income basis, with a preference to add at lower prices where the yield and rate-base compounding offer better entry economics.

## Company Overview

H2O America is a holding company operating four principal regulated subsidiaries:

- **San Jose Water Company (SJWC)** — the largest and oldest (est. 1866) subsidiary, serving ~232,000 connections and ~1 million people in the San Jose/Silicon Valley area under CPUC jurisdiction. This is the economic core of the company.
- **Connecticut Water Company (CWC)** and **Maine Water Company (MWC)** — acquired via the 2019 CTWS merger, serving ~143,000 connections and ~465,000 people (PURA and MPUC).
- **The Texas Water Company (SJWTX)** — ~30,000 connections in the fast-growing San Antonio–Austin corridor (PUCT), the highest-growth geography and the launch pad for the Quadvest expansion.

Water Utility Services is the sole reportable segment. The business earns rates set by state regulators designed to recover operating costs plus an authorized return on invested capital. The moat is statutory: certified service territories function as regulated monopolies, and no competing investor-owned utility can enter without demonstrating existing service is inadequate. The primary competitive dynamics are (a) municipalization/eminent-domain risk on the downside and (b) competition among consolidators for tuck-in acquisitions of fragmented municipal systems on the upside.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is steady and rate-driven, not volume-driven.** Revenue advanced from $620.7M (2022) to $670.4M (2023), $748.4M (2024), and $800.6M (2025), roughly +7% in 2025. The 2025 build decomposes as +$44.1M from authorized rate increases, +$23.3M from pass-through water costs, only +$1.5M from customer growth, and –$7.2M from lower usage. Customer counts are essentially flat (~400,128 in 2025 vs. 397,087 in 2023). This is a rate-case and capital-recovery engine, with volume a modest drag as conservation reduces consumption.

**Profitability is respectable in absolute terms but weak against authorized returns.** 2025 net income was $102.6M (+9% YoY), GAAP diluted EPS $2.92 ($2.99 adjusted). Operating margin ran ~22% and net margin ~12.8%. ROE of 6.7% and ROA of 2.0% sit well below the 9.3%–10.88% authorized ROE range. The company chronically under-earns its allowed return, a function of regulatory lag, heavy capex sitting outside rate base, and equity dilution. Q1 2026 shows the tension directly: revenue $183.3M (+9%), net income $19.0M (+15%), yet flat diluted EPS because of the larger share count.

**The core tension is capital intensity.** Operating cash flow was $244.8M in 2025 against company-funded capex of $489.6M. The business does not self-fund its growth. The five-year plan calls for ~$2.57B in capex, including ~$400M for PFAS treatment to meet EPA MCL compliance by 2029. This makes HTO a perpetual external-capital story, issuing both debt and equity every year. In 2025 alone, the company raised $185M of new long-term debt and $122.8M of equity.

**Leverage is normal for the model but the cost trajectory is unfavorable.** Total debt is ~$1.95B against equity of $1.54B at year-end 2025 (long-term debt $1.87B). The weighted-average cost of long-term debt rose from 4.12% (2024) to 4.38% (2025), with new 2025 issuances printed at 5.83%–6.70%. Current ratio of 0.7 is normal for a utility and not a distress signal. Goodwill of $640M represents 12% of assets (largely the 2019 CTWS deal), carrying impairment risk if regulatory outcomes or performance deteriorate. The Piotroski F-score of 4 is solid, and the Altman Z-score is not meaningful for a leveraged regulated-utility balance sheet.

**Dividend is the standout durable feature.** 58 consecutive years of increases and 330 consecutive quarters paid, with a 2025 payout ratio of ~57% of net income and a Q1 2026 dividend of $0.44/quarter (~2.75% yield). The dividend is well-covered on earnings, but growth is partly financed by continuous equity issuance, which dilutes the per-share value of that stream.

I am holding the Financial Health score at 4.5. The persistent ROE gap, negative free cash flow requiring continuous external funding, rising marginal debt cost, and the S&P Negative outlook collectively outweigh the strong dividend coverage and stable investment-grade profile. This is a capital-inefficiency and cost-of-capital flag, not a distress flag, and the market is already partly pricing it.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is durable and mechanically driven by rate base expansion. The pending rate cases are the near-term levers: CWC general rate case (+$26M, decision ~February 2027), MWC general rate case (+$9.5M, ~Q2 2027), and the TWC second SIC amendment (+$5M). Texas HB 2712 (effective 9/1/2025), which permits future test years beginning 9/1/2026, is a structural positive for capital recovery in the fastest-growing state. The Maine unified tariff (approved January 2026) and the Connecticut PFAS surcharge mechanism (WQTA, July 2025) both improve cost recovery and simplify future filings.

The Quadvest acquisition ($540M total: $483.6M regulated retail + $56.4M wholesale) is the single largest growth catalyst. PUCT set the ratemaking rate base at the full purchase price, avoiding a write-down to a lower appraised value, and the STM application was deemed administratively complete on April 14, 2026, with an expected close in 2H 2026.

The gating issue for the growth score is that this earnings growth is being diluted at the per-share level. Estimate revisions confirm the muted trajectory: current-year consensus of $2.74 is essentially flat (–0.1% over 30 days, +0.4% over 90 days), and next-year consensus of $2.75 has drifted slightly lower (–0.2%/30d, –0.7%/90d). The estimate trend is flat-to-negative on the out-year, which does not support a growth-lean thesis. The 7.55M-share forward (settling by March 2028 at ~$51.28) means Quadvest accretion spreads across a much larger base, and investors should model share count near ~49M rather than the ~35M implied by trailing figures. Net income can grow high-single-digits while EPS stays flat, which is what Q1 2026 delivered.

I score Growth Prospects at 5.0, down from the prior 7.0 several months ago. The revenue and rate-base growth is real and durable, but the per-share dilution and flat out-year estimates mean the growth does not translate into shareholder-level compounding at an attractive rate.

The absence of a mispricing, combined with a rising cost of capital that pressures utility valuations broadly and the dilution overhang, argues against rewarding the multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

HTO's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving. The regulated-monopoly structure provides statutory protection within certified territories, and the moat is durable by design. The consolidation runway is genuine: the U.S. water industry is fragmented and municipally dominated, favoring well-capitalized acquirers. HTO's strategy mirrors larger peers (American Water, Essential Utilities, California Water): grow rate base organically plus via fair-market-value acquisitions.

Texas is the bright spot: the FMV statute (purchase price equals rate base) and the new future-test-year law make the state structurally attractive, and Quadvest positions the company in high-growth metro Houston. Against that, HTO is a mid-cap consolidator competing for the same assets as much larger, lower-cost-of-capital peers like AWK ($24B market cap) and WTRG ($10B), which can outbid on scale. HTO's rising marginal debt cost (5.8–6.7%) versus larger investment-grade peers is a competitive disadvantage in the acquisition market.

Operationally, the California water-supply concentration is a structural constraint rather than an advantage: SJWC depends on Valley Water (contract to 2051), which is raising purchased-water prices ~10%/year while calling for 15% voluntary conservation because Anderson reservoir is offline for seismic retrofit through 2026. This simultaneously raises costs and suppresses volumes, mostly (not fully) offset by decoupling/balancing mechanisms (FCBA, WCMA), the latter carrying a 20bp ROE penalty.

I score Competitive Position at 5.5: stable moat, credible growth-by-acquisition runway in Texas, offset by scale disadvantages against larger consolidators and the California supply constraint.

## Risk Assessment

**Quadvest execution and financing risk** dominate the near-term profile. The deal is funded largely by the March 2026 equity offering, adding dilution and integration risk in a new geography. There is a $21M termination fee payable under certain regulatory-approval-failure scenarios, and the S&P Negative outlook is a credit-quality warning to monitor.

**Equity dilution is material and ongoing.** Beyond the outstanding-share and forward-sale figures noted above, $55M remains on the ATM. Fully-diluted count is heading toward ~49M+, structurally capping per-share growth. This is the single most under-appreciated dynamic relative to a standardized data pull.

**California supply and inverse-condemnation exposure.** Beyond the Valley Water conservation/cost dynamic, SJWC owns ~6,400 acres of Santa Cruz Mountain watershed; under California inverse-condemnation doctrine, it could be liable for property damage from its facilities even absent negligence, with no guarantee of rate recovery. The 2025 captive insurance subsidiary (H2O America Risk Solutions) is a response to a hardening wildfire/pollution insurance market.

**PFAS is genuinely two-sided.** The mandated capex is a cost, but HTO is also a plaintiff recovering from manufacturers ($25.1M received to date, $5.3M more in April 2026, plus 8 years of future 3M payments), recorded as a regulatory liability benefiting customers. California (WCLMA) and Connecticut (WQTA) have created dedicated cost-recovery mechanisms. Net PFAS exposure is more manageable than the headline capex implies.

**Regulatory lag** is the fundamental earnings risk: the entire model hinges on timely rate approvals in an inflationary, rising-rate environment. **Litigation:** CWC faces an October 2023 putative class action alleging water contaminants (Connecticut Superior Court), no loss estimate provided. **Governance:** frequent Item 5.02 filings across 2024–2026 reflect a major leadership transition (Walters CFO→CEO→Chairman; Kelly to CFO; Hauk President/COO), including a June 12, 2026 departure; the finance bench is credible, but stability bears watching.

On the constructive side, Atlas Infrastructure Partners / GIP Atlas Holdings, a 10% owner holding ~4.6M shares, has been actively accumulating (+50,385 shares at $59.06 on 4/10/2026; +2,784 at $62.03 on 7/7/2026), a supportive signal from a sophisticated infrastructure investor buying even after the price run.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that is not present in the fundamentals. Near-term ATM IV of 37.3% is rich versus 23.0% one-year historical vol (a 14.3-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (37.3% near-term vs. 28.1% at six months), consistent with positioning ahead of the July 27 Q2 print. Put/call skew of 22.2% signals elevated put demand, and short interest is high at 17.3% of float with 11.7 days to cover. IV has collapsed from 78.0% to 37.3% since the mid-June snapshot as the price rose 13.3%, so much of the acute event-hedging has already unwound. The elevated short interest and put skew reflect the dilution/valuation concerns discussed above rather than a distinct new catalyst; they argue for patience on entry timing (implied ±8% move into earnings) rather than a change in the fundamental read.

I score Risk Factors at 5.0. The Quadvest financing/integration, dilution overhang, California inverse-condemnation tail, and S&P Negative outlook are real and specific, but they are largely known and partly priced, and PFAS recovery mechanisms plus balancing accounts provide offsets.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
HTO closes Quadvest on favorable rate-base terms in 2H 2026, the CWC (+$26M) and MWC (+$9.5M) rate cases resolve favorably in early-to-mid 2027, and Texas future test years accelerate capital recovery. The ROE gap narrows as capex enters rate base, EPS growth reaccelerates once dilution is digested, and the 58-year dividend record supports a premium income multiple. Atlas/GIP accumulation at $59–62 anchors the floor. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target of $70 (~9% upside) with the dividend on top, and continued rate-base compounding supports mid-single-digit annual total returns thereafter.

### Bear Case
Rising marginal debt cost and continuous equity dilution keep realized ROE stuck near 6.7%, well below authorized. The forward and ATM push diluted share count toward ~49M, keeping EPS flat despite net-income growth. S&P follows through on its Negative outlook with a downgrade, raising the cost of capital further into the $2.57B capex cycle. A higher-for-longer rate environment compresses utility multiples across the sector, and California supply/conservation and inverse-condemnation risks surface. The stock derates toward the low analyst target of $60 (~6% downside), or toward the low-$50s if the rate/dilution pressure compounds.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on the ~2.75% well-covered dividend, the 58-year increase record, and durable rate-base compounding, not on near-term appreciation. The stock is fairly valued against peers, near its 52-week high, and within ~2% of the consensus mean target, with flat out-year EPS estimates that do not support a growth-lean case.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pulls back to the low-to-mid $50s, restoring a yield above ~3.3% and a more attractive entry against forward EPS.
- Out-year EPS consensus turns decisively positive (e.g., FY+1 revisions >+3% over 90 days) as Quadvest accretion and rate-case wins begin flowing to per-share results.
- S&P returns the outlook to Stable and realized ROE demonstrably closes toward 8%+, signaling the regulatory-lag/dilution drag is easing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- S&P downgrades the credit rating, raising the cost of capital mid-cycle.
- Quadvest approval fails or the deal terms deteriorate materially (write-down below the $483.6M rate base), triggering the $21M termination fee and stranding integration spend.
- A goodwill impairment (against the $640M balance) or a material adverse ruling in the CWC contaminant class action.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 print on July 27, 2026, followed by the Quadvest close (expected 2H 2026) and the CWC rate-case decision (~February 2027).

## Conclusion

H2O America is a well-run, investment-grade regulated water utility with a strong dividend record and genuine structural tailwinds from mandated infrastructure and PFAS capex, Sun Belt expansion via Quadvest, and a consolidation runway in a fragmented industry. The problem is that these positives are not translating into per-share value at an attractive rate: realized ROE sits well below authorized, continuous equity dilution flattens EPS even as net income grows high-single-digits, and rising marginal debt cost plus an S&P Negative outlook pressure the financing model in the middle of a $2.57B capex cycle.

At a multiple in line with the peer median, near the top of its range, and within a few dollars of consensus targets, the stock is fairly valued to slightly rich; there is no mispricing to exploit, and the yield is below the peer median. The score has declined from 6.3 to the current 4.85, reflecting the growing recognition that dilution and cost-of-capital pressure are the binding constraints, not the revenue growth. New data confirms rather than reverses that trend: flat out-year estimates, the forward overhang, and rising debt costs all remain in place. We rate HTO a HOLD on income grounds. Own it for the dividend and the low-beta ballast, and add on weakness toward the low-to-mid $50s where the yield and entry economics improve, but do not chase it near the 52-week high for capital appreciation that the current setup does not support.
