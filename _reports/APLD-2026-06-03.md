**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Applied Digital is a Dallas-based data-center developer transforming from a Bitcoin-mining hosting company into an AI/HPC "AI Factory" landlord. The equity has appreciated 372% over the past twelve months to roughly $47.86, carrying a ~$12.7B market capitalization on ~$353M of trailing nine-month revenue, much of which has only recently come online. The investment question is whether common shareholders, sitting at the bottom of a capital stack containing ~$2.7B of senior secured debt and ~$0.9B of Macquarie preferred with a 1.80x minimum-MOIC liquidation preference, are adequately compensated for the subordination and execution risk embedded in a buildout that is only about one-sixth energized. AI data-center demand is confirmed across the hyperscaler capex complex; the open question is the compensation for subordination, not the demand.

The fundamental picture is inflecting, and this matters for the rating. Q3 FY26 (quarter ended 2/28/2026) revenue rose 139% YoY to $126.6M; the HPC segment produced its first full quarter of ELN-02 revenue at $71M; and Adjusted EBITDA jumped to $44.1M from $6.3M a year earlier. Consensus current-year EPS estimates have been revised from -$0.64 to -$0.33 over 90 days, a +48.4% move. The March 2026 CoreWeave lease restructuring, which initially read like a tenant pulling back, functioned as a credit enhancement: the ELN-02/ELN-03 leases were moved into a CoreWeave SPV that obtained an investment-grade A3 rating, with unconditional springing guarantees from CoreWeave Inc. and a $50M letter of credit. Backlog continues to grow with a 200 MW Polaris Forge 2 lease (signed 10/22/2025) and a second 15-year hyperscaler lease announced in April 2026.

I rate APLD a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The case against owning the common — leverage, refinancing dependency, an unremediated material weakness in accounting for complex instruments, extreme valuation, and customer concentration — is real, but it is also largely reflected in a 29.9% short interest and an equity that has nonetheless tripled. The case for the common rests on a backlog that requires years of flawless construction and continued access to expensive capital before it self-funds. With the bearish thesis substantially priced and the operating data inflecting upward rather than deteriorating, neither an actionable short nor a conviction long is supportable. HOLD captures the balance.

## Company Overview

Applied Digital, founded in 2021, designs, builds, and operates data centers across three reported segments:

**Data Center Hosting (legacy crypto)** — 286 MW across Jamestown, ND (106 MW) and Ellendale, ND (180 MW), both at full capacity hosting Bitcoin miners. This is the most capital-efficient piece of the business: ~$37.5M revenue and $13.9M segment operating profit in Q3 FY26 on roughly $120M of deployed assets, the highest return on assets in the company. It is also down to effectively one customer: 93% of FY25 segment revenue from a single miner under a five-year contract with ~2.5 years remaining that can be unilaterally reduced.

**HPC Hosting (the growth engine)** — Build-to-suit AI data centers leased to hyperscalers and neoclouds under ~15-year leases, branded "Polaris Forge," using proprietary waterless direct-to-chip liquid cooling. The anchor is CoreWeave at Polaris Forge 1 (Ellendale, 400 MW). ELN-02 (100 MW) went fully operational in CY2025 and produced $71M of revenue in Q3 FY26 (its first full quarter: $44.1M base rent, $18.9M fit-out, $8.1M power pass-through). ELN-03 (150 MW) and ELN-04 (150 MW) are under construction for CY2026/2027.

**Cloud Services (being divested)** — A money-losing GPU-rental business, ~$53M revenue over nine months of FY26, being spun into ChronoScale Corporation via a reverse merger with EKSO Bionics (Nasdaq: EKSO → CHRN), with APLD retaining ~97%. Expected to close Q4 FY26.

Management's stated ambition is to exceed $1 billion of net operating income within five years, against a power pipeline of roughly 1 GW being marketed of which management says only about one-sixth of long-term potential is contracted.

## Financial Analysis

Growth is explosive while GAAP losses persist with heavy non-cash distortion. Nine-month FY26 GAAP net loss was $101.9M, with the Q3 net loss to common of $100.9M (-$0.36) distorted by a $59.7M non-cash write-down of the Cloud business (it ceased to qualify as held-for-sale) and $48.9M of Q3 stock-based comp. Excluding Cloud, Q3 Adjusted net income was $33.2M ($0.09). The HPC platform is beginning to show operating leverage as buildings energize.

The balance sheet at 2/28/2026 is liquid today but heavily levered. Cash and equivalents stood at $1.73B plus ~$0.38B restricted cash, roughly $2.1B total. Total debt was ~$2.69B; total liabilities $3.68B. Property and equipment was $3.0B, including ~$1.5B of construction-in-progress. Reported equity of $1.58B is flattered by the exclusion of $923M of redeemable noncontrolling interest (Macquarie preferred, classified as temporary equity, effectively a senior claim ahead of common).

The cash-flow statement is where the core tension lives. Nine-month FY26 operating cash flow was -$42.9M; investing was -$1.59B (capex $1.58B); financing was +$3.62B. The model only functions through continuous, large external financing. In nine months the company raised approximately $2.5B of debt, $900M of Macquarie NCI contribution, $740M of preferred, and $196M via common ATM. This is a capital-markets-dependent business by design.

The cost of capital reflects the credit risk the market assigns:
- 9.250% Senior Secured Notes due 2030 (~$2.35B, Polaris Forge 1)
- 6.750% Senior Secured Notes due 2031 ($2.15B, issued at 98%, proceeds in escrow pending an electric service agreement, subject to special mandatory redemption if conditions are not met by approximately mid-2026)
- 2.75% Convertible Notes due 2030 ($450M)
- Macquarie preferred units accruing 12.75%+ (escalating) with a 1.80x minimum-MOIC liquidation preference sitting senior to common

Altman Z-Score screens deep in the distress zone. For a pre-stabilization, debt-funded real-estate developer this is partly structural, but it correctly signals that leverage and refinancing risk are the dominant financial-statement risks. The eventual goal of refinancing project debt down from 9%+ as assets stabilize is plausible but not yet demonstrated.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is strong and broad-based, and I weight it heavily. Three independent vectors point the same direction: revenue up 139% YoY in Q3; Adjusted EBITDA inflecting roughly +600% YoY ($6.3M→$44.1M) as ELN-02 reached its first full quarter; and consensus current-year EPS revised +48.4% over 90 days. The next-year estimate ($-0.95 from -$0.90) reflects the cost of bringing additional capacity online rather than a deterioration in the unit economics of energized buildings. This is a normalization-from-trough and energization-ramp profile, and that distinction is central to why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID.

Backlog growth is the durable part of the thesis. The CoreWeave anchor at Polaris Forge 1 (400 MW), the 200 MW Polaris Forge 2 lease, the second 15-year hyperscaler lease announced ~4/23/2026, and the Delta Forge 1 groundbreaking (430 MW campus, ~300 MW critical IT, online ~mid-2027) collectively represent multi-year, ~15-year-duration revenue under contract. The mechanism is straightforward: each campus that energizes converts construction-in-progress into base rent that flows to Adjusted EBITDA, and ELN-02 demonstrated the magnitude ($44.1M base rent in one quarter from 100 MW). If the buildout executes, the operating-leverage story is credible.

The growth risk is that energization is gated by power availability, equipment lead times, and tariff exposure on electrical gear, and that the revenue requires the capital that funds the construction to remain accessible at tolerable cost. Two of the three large customers and the Polaris Forge 2 and April 2026 hyperscaler tenants are undisclosed; the "investment-grade hyperscaler" label is management's characterization, not independently verifiable from filings.

## Valuation Assessment

The company has no positive earnings, so trailing and forward P/E are meaningless (-103.9x reported). The appropriate frame is forward-looking on backlog conversion and NOI, which is inherently speculative at this stage of buildout. At ~$12.7B market cap on trailing nine-month revenue, much of it just turned on, the equity is priced as an option on the full buildout, not on the in-place asset base.

The cycle-adjusted framing argues against penalizing APLD purely for a high trailing multiple, and I accept that. The estimate-revision signal and revenue growth >40% are the inflection markers that warrant forward valuation. But the guardrail also applies: the equity sits below the senior secured debt and the preferred, so common shareholders capture the residual after a large senior stack is satisfied. On management's $1B+ NOI ambition, a stabilized data-center REIT might fetch 15-20x NOI, implying a substantial enterprise value, but that requires netting ~$3.6B of senior claims (and accreting preference) and assumes flawless execution, full energization, and successful refinancing of 9%+ debt down to stabilized rates. The market is paying close to the bull-case present value today.

Analyst targets (mean $64.59, median $66.00, low $40.00, high $106.00) with 11 of 11 rating Buy/Strong Buy and zero holds or sells imply 35%+ upside to the mean. I treat these as informative but not independent confirmation: they extrapolate the same backlog-conversion thesis that the price already reflects, and on a pre-profitability, capital-structure-complex name, sell-side unanimity tends to discount subordination and refinancing risk that the credit market prices explicitly via the 9.25% coupons. I do not treat the consensus target as a floor here, because the stock is not cheap relative to improving estimates in any conventional sense: the estimates remain negative and the valuation rests on multi-year NOI not yet earned. The valuation score of 4.0 reflects asymmetry skewed against the common given the senior stack, balanced against a genuine inflection that prevents a lower mark.

## Competitive Landscape

APLD's competitive edge is access to low-cost stranded/remote power (North Dakota), construction speed, and direct-to-chip liquid cooling, which lets it sign long-duration leases that management likens to annuity streams backed by strong credits. Management claims it operates one of the only 100 MW direct-to-chip liquid-cooled data centers online today. The moat is power access plus speed, not proprietary technology.

The competitive reality, candidly acknowledged in the 10-K, is that APLD is a sub-scale, highly leveraged developer competing against hyperscalers building their own capacity and far larger data-center REITs and developers with materially cheaper capital. The filing states it "does not have the resources to compete with larger providers." The trajectory is improving (share of a structurally growing market, backlog expanding, tenant credit enhanced via the CoreWeave A3 SPV), but the position is one of a fast-moving challenger dependent on continued capital access rather than an entrenched leader. The CoreWeave restructuring cuts both ways: it strengthened tenant credit, a genuine positive for APLD's landlord position and its bondholders, while crystallizing how tightly APLD's fate is bound to a single, itself-leveraged neocloud's balance sheet.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for this specific company:

1. **Refinancing / capital-access dependency.** The 10-K explicitly warns it must raise "substantial additional capital" and may be unable to refinance debt at maturity. The growth plan is funded by serial debt and dilutive equity. Any tightening of AI-infrastructure capital markets is an existential risk.

2. **Extreme customer concentration.** Nine-month FY26 revenue: Customer A 52%, Customer B 33%, Customer C 15%. HPC is anchored almost entirely on CoreWeave; legacy hosting is down to one miner. The A3 SPV restructuring and $50M LC mitigate the CoreWeave credit risk meaningfully but do not eliminate concentration.

3. **Material weakness in internal controls.** An unremediated material weakness in accounting for complex financial instruments, directly relevant given the labyrinth of convertibles, warrants, preferred series, and VIE/NCI structures. Remediation is "in process" with no assurance of completion.

4. **Dilution.** The Series G PEPA expanded from $150M to $1.6B; $740M of preferred converted into ~49M shares in nine months; share count grew from ~225M (5/31/25) to ~285M (2/28/26). Nine-month SBC was $94.7M versus $10.2M a year earlier.

5. **Execution / construction risk.** Speculative builds, long-lead equipment, tariff exposure on electrical gear, permitting, and power availability (the 2031 notes' escrow hinges on an electric service agreement). December 2023 power outages at Ellendale/Garden City materially hurt FY24 revenue.

6. **Related-party web and litigation.** APLD guaranteed Base Electron's ~1.2 GW Babcock & Wilcox design-build agreement ($50-100M termination-fee exposure); a 2023 securities class action (McConnell v. APLD) was stayed in September 2025 pending a motion to dismiss.

These risks are largely known and at least partly priced: the 29.9% short interest, the 9.25% senior coupons, and the distress-zone Z-Score all reflect them. The risk score of 3.5 reflects that several of these (refinancing dependency, material weakness, subordination) carry genuine binary downside that is not fully neutralized by the inflection.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 117.6% sits rich to one-year historical volatility of 106.6% (81st percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (117.6% near-term vs 102.7% long-term), which the vendor flags as the market pricing imminent event stress. Put/call skew is balanced (-2.2%) and put/call volume (0.59) and OI (0.51) ratios are call-tilted, so the options market is not expressing a directional bearish lean despite the heavy short interest. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.9%. The read: this is an expensive-volatility, high-uncertainty name where the options market is hedging a binary outcome rather than confirming a directional thesis. For a HOLD, the rich near-term IV argues against initiating either leg via options at current pricing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** AI data-center demand is structurally confirmed across the hyperscaler complex; APLD has secured ~15-year leases on a growing backlog (CoreWeave 400 MW, Polaris Forge 2 200 MW, a second hyperscaler lease, Delta Forge 1 430 MW) with tenant credit enhanced by the CoreWeave A3 SPV and $50M LC. As campuses energize, Adjusted EBITDA compounds; ELN-02 alone added $44.1M of quarterly base rent. If the buildout executes and project debt refinances down from 9%+ as assets stabilize, the $1B+ NOI ambition supports an enterprise value well above today's. Eleven of eleven analysts rate Buy with a $66 median target. Bull case ~$80-106.

**Bear case.** Common equity is the most junior claim on a thinly-capitalized, debt-heavy asset base. The model requires continuous external financing; any AI-infrastructure capital-markets tightening, a CoreWeave stumble, a construction or power delay, or a re-rating of the speculative buildout multiple compresses the equity sharply. The unremediated material weakness in the very instruments that dominate the balance sheet, the nine-month SBC, a $122M CEO PSU grant against persistent losses, and steady director selling compound the concern. Bear case ~$24-30 (toward where the senior stack and energized-asset cash flow can be more concretely underwritten).

The risk/reward is two-sided, which is why this resolves to HOLD. The operating data is inflecting, not deteriorating, so an AVOID would issue a bearish actionable call on a name whose fundamentals are recovering and whose bear case is already heavily reflected in a 29.9% short position. At the same time, the subordination, refinancing dependency, and valuation prevent constructive conviction. I cannot underwrite either leg at current pricing.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q4 FY26, expected mid-2026), which will reveal whether the 6.750% 2031 notes' electric-service-agreement escrow condition is satisfied (special mandatory redemption risk by ~mid-2026), the ChronoScale/Cloud divestiture closes, and whether ELN-03 energization proceeds on schedule.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Operating cash flow turns sustainably positive and the company demonstrates the buildout can self-fund without further large dilutive equity or preferred issuance.
- Project debt is refinanced down from 9%+ toward stabilized data-center rates, materially de-risking the capital stack and lifting residual value to common.
- The equity de-rates to a level (roughly low-$30s or below) where the price offers a margin of safety against the ~$3.6B of senior claims and the energized, contracted NOI alone supports the valuation.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID / actionable bearish):**
- A capital-markets tightening or a specific financing failure (e.g., 2031 notes special mandatory redemption, a failed equity/preferred raise) that breaks the funding model.
- Material adverse change in CoreWeave's credit or a customer non-renewal/reduction, given 52% revenue concentration.
- Evidence of construction or energization slippage that pushes EBITDA inflection out by more than two quarters, combined with deteriorating estimate revisions.

## Conclusion

Applied Digital is a leveraged AI data-center developer in genuine operating inflection. The growth signal is real and broad-based — Adjusted EBITDA roughly +600% YoY, HPC revenue 0→$71M, current-year EPS estimates +48.4% in 90 days — and the CoreWeave A3 SPV restructuring strengthened tenant credit rather than weakening it. These facts argue against treating this as a deteriorating, terminally-impaired name, and against an actionable bearish posture on an equity that has tripled and carries 29.9% short interest with the bear case substantially priced.

At the same time, common equity sits behind the senior secured debt and the preferred, the entire plan depends on continuous external financing into a backlog only ~1/6 energized, there is an unremediated material weakness in accounting for complex instruments, and the valuation prices close to the bull-case present value of a multi-year buildout. I cannot underwrite the buildout outcome with conviction, and I cannot construct a favorable short given crowded positioning and the operating momentum.

The call is HOLD, rationale uncertain. The thesis flips constructive if the equity de-rates toward the low-$30s (offering margin of safety against the senior stack) or if the buildout demonstrably begins to self-fund and refinances down from 9%+ project debt. It flips actionably negative if the funding model breaks, CoreWeave's credit deteriorates, or energization slips materially. Until one of those resolves, the risk/reward is balanced.
