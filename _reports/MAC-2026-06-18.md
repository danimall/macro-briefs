**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Macerich owns a genuinely high-quality portfolio of Class A regional malls in affluent, supply-constrained markets, and the operating business is improving on every metric that matters for a mall REIT. Trailing-twelve-month tenant sales reached $899/sq ft portfolio-wide ($941 for go-forward centers), occupancy is 93.4% (94.5% go-forward), leasing spreads have been positive for 17 consecutive quarters (+6.7% in FY2025), and adjusted FFO grew 8.7% to $397M with Q1 2026 FFO of $0.34 beating the $0.31 consensus. The forward story is anchored by a quantified signed-not-open (SNO) lease pipeline that management expects to generate roughly $116M of incremental rent at MAC's share through 2028, bridging to the promised NOI acceleration in 2027-2028.

The equity is binary because the balance sheet, not the operating business, is the central issue. Pro-rata debt sits at ~$6.45B, net debt/adjusted EBITDA is 7.76x, the weighted-average maturity is only 3.23 years, and the weighted-average rate is 5.27%. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, every refinancing rolls below-market loans into 5.5-7%+ coupons, and the Path Forward Plan explicitly contemplates handing underwater properties back to lenders (Santa Monica Place, Twenty Ninth Street, formerly South Plains). The non-recourse structure limits contagion, but persistent GAAP net losses (−$197M in 2025) and recurring impairments are features of this model. The GAAP net loss is driven by ~$471M of annual depreciation, not operating cash burn, so FFO is the relevant gauge.

The stock has appreciated roughly 45% over twelve months, from a ~$15.48 low to a recent $25.72 high before pulling back to $23. At the current price, the leasing momentum and deleveraging progress are increasingly recognized, while the leverage risk remains live. We rate MAC a HOLD with a growth bias. The 12-month thesis is real and specific (SNO conversion plus continued net-debt/EBITDA reduction), but the asymmetry that justified accumulation at $16-18 has compressed at $23 into a near-consensus price. We would upgrade to BUY on either a pullback toward $19-20 or further evidence that net-debt/EBITDA is moving decisively below 7x.

## Company Overview

Macerich is a self-managed REIT owning interests in 39 retail centers totaling approximately 41 million square feet of GLA as of Q1 2026. The portfolio is concentrated in California (~24% of go-forward NOI), Arizona/Phoenix-Scottsdale (~21%), New York (~17%), the Mid-Atlantic corridor (~19%), and Oregon (~7%). Trophy assets include Tysons Corner Center, Queens Center, Scottsdale Fashion Square, Broadway Plaza, Kings Plaza, Santa Monica Place, and Washington Square.

The company differentiates itself within a structurally challenged sector by productivity. Class A malls in affluent markets are seeing landlords regain pricing power as limited new supply meets steadier retailer demand, while B/C malls continue to decline. Macerich's strategy is to shed the latter and concentrate ownership of the former (the "Go-Forward Portfolio"). Many flagship assets are held in 50%-owned joint ventures (Tysons, Scottsdale, Broadway Plaza, Chandler, Deptford, FlatIron), which makes pro-rata reporting essential and complicates the GAAP statements. The company also holds mixed-use densification optionality (residential at FlatIron, hotel/office at Scottsdale and Tysons).

A note on the data: standardized financial pulls for MAC capture only the Management Companies' fee-revenue line (~$68M) and mislabel it as total revenue. Actual GAAP revenue is ~$960M+ annually, with Q1 2026 leasing revenue alone at $226M. This report relies on 10-K MD&A and quarterly supplement figures.

## Financial Analysis

The operating trajectory is improving off a low base while GAAP remains loss-making for structural reasons.

**FFO and NOI.** Adjusted FFO grew 8.7% to $397.0M in FY2025 from $365.3M in 2024. Q1 2026 adjusted FFO was $0.34/share, flat YoY but ahead of the $0.31 consensus. Go-Forward Portfolio NOI (excluding lease-termination income) rose 1.8% in 2025 and 1.2% in Q1 2026, with management guiding to ≥3% go-forward NOI growth in 2026 and accelerating growth in 2027-2028 as the SNO pipeline opens.

**GAAP losses.** Net loss was −$197M in 2025, −$194M in 2024, and −$274M in 2023, driven by ~$471M of annual depreciation/amortization on a pro-rata basis plus recurring impairments, not operating cash burn. This is typical for a depreciation-heavy mall REIT. Reported EPS of −$0.78 and the resulting negative trailing/forward P/E (−31.9x) are not meaningful for valuation; FFO is the operative metric.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** The headline EPS line has missed in each of the last four quarters (−26% in Q1 2026, −403% in Q4 2025, −321% in Q3 2025), but these are GAAP EPS figures distorted by impairment and depreciation timing. The metric the company and the Street actually track, adjusted FFO, beat in Q1 2026. GAAP EPS is the wrong gauge here.

**Leasing momentum.** Seventeen consecutive quarters of positive base-rent leasing spreads; FY2025 releasing spread was +6.7% ($65.39 → $69.77/sq ft). Leasing volume rose 85% in square footage in 2025. Management reports the "leasing speedometer" at 83% toward an 85% mid-year target with all 30 anchor repositionings committed.

**Balance sheet — the central issue.** Total pro-rata debt is ~$6.45B (Q1 2026), down from $6.65B, 91% fixed-rate. Liquidity is ~$780M including ~$650M available on the upsized $900M revolver, with ~$182M cash. Operating cash flow rose +$38M in 2025. The dividend is held at $0.68 annualized (~2.9% yield), a deliberately conservative ~50% FFO payout that retains cash for deleveraging and redevelopment.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is quantified and time-bound, which separates MAC from a generic "mall recovery" story.

The SNO pipeline is the measurable bridge. Management has already signed leases that, as tenants open and begin paying rent through 2028, drive the promised NOI/FFO acceleration. The leading indicators to track are the leasing speedometer and anchor repositioning conversion. If these openings hit schedule, FFO growth re-rates the stock; if they slip, the thesis stalls.

The redevelopment pipeline adds incremental yield: ~$297-330M (pro rata) across FlatIron Crossing residential densification (6.75-7.75% yield), Green Acres Mall grocery/anchor redevelopment (10-11%), and the Scottsdale Fashion Square Nordstrom luxury wing (17-18%). These are attractive incremental returns if executed on time and on budget.

Management has also resumed offense, acquiring Crabtree Mall (Raleigh, $290M, June 2025) and Annapolis Mall ($260M plus a $12M Sears parcel, April 2026), with Annapolis described as accretive to 2028 FFO targets. Acquiring while deleveraging at 7.76x is an aggressive posture that signals confidence but warrants scrutiny.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive on the FFO-relevant metrics: current-year EPS estimates rose 11.8% over 90 days, next-quarter +40%, and next-year flipped from −$0.02 to +$0.03 (a +250% revision off a near-zero base). These are stable over the trailing 30 days, so there is positive momentum but not the +30% acceleration velocity that would signal a forced re-rating. The estimates remain small absolute numbers on a GAAP basis, so they confirm direction rather than magnitude.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard P/E metrics are not usable given GAAP losses. On an FFO basis, MAC's adjusted FFO run-rate is roughly $1.36/share, putting the stock at ~17x FFO at $23. That is not cheap for a 7.76x-levered mall REIT, but it is defensible against the quality of the assets and the embedded SNO growth.

Peer P/E comparisons (KRG 21.8x, PECO 45.3x, NNN 22.4x, SKT 37.6x, ADC 40.4x, median 37.6x) are of limited use because those peers are net-lease and open-air shopping center REITs that report positive GAAP earnings, while MAC is a mall REIT with a different capital structure and the only one carrying a balance-sheet turnaround. The closest comparable is Simon Property Group, which trades at a premium FFO multiple justified by ~5-6x leverage. MAC's discount to Simon is the appropriate frame, and it largely reflects the leverage gap rather than a mispricing.

Analyst price targets bracket the current price closely: low $19, median $24, mean $23.81, high $30, against the current $23. The stock is trading essentially at the consensus median. Because MAC carries elevated leverage and persistent GAAP losses rather than a clean inflection profile with a sub-30x forward multiple, analyst targets here function as a genuine band rather than a stale floor that will be revised up on the next print. The 2.9% dividend yield provides only modest support and trails the 4.0% peer median.

The honest read: at $16-18, the asymmetry was attractive because the deleveraging optionality and SNO pipeline were not priced. After a 45% twelve-month run, the price now reflects a meaningful share of the good news while the leverage risk remains unresolved. We score valuation at 5.0 — neither a clear bargain nor clearly stretched, with the upside dependent on execution rather than multiple expansion. A cheap-looking REIT in turnaround is not by itself a buy signal; the catalyst (SNO conversion and net-debt reduction) has to deliver.

## Competitive Landscape

Macerich's competitive position is improving within its tier. Class A malls in affluent markets are experiencing a genuine reversal of the prior decade's dynamic: retailers are competing for limited high-productivity space, supporting positive releasing spreads and rising occupancy. MAC's portfolio productivity sits well above weaker mall peers, and the deliberate shedding of B/C assets (~$980M of dispositions in 2024-2025) while consolidating ownership of core JVs is sharpening the portfolio.

The distinguishing weakness versus Simon Property Group is the balance sheet, which raises MAC's cost of capital and constrains flexibility. The distinguishing strength is the embedded SNO rent pipeline and the high-yield redevelopment slate. Against the broader sector, the bifurcation between top-tier and lower-quality malls works in MAC's favor as it concentrates in the productive segment, but secular e-commerce pressure and tariff-driven tenant cost inflation remain structural counterweights that apply to all physical retail landlords. The trajectory is positive; the moat is real but narrower than the sector leader's because of the financing disadvantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and refinancing (the dominant risk).** 7.76x net-debt/EBITDA against a ~3.2-year weighted-average maturity means continuous refinancing into higher rates. Each maturity is a discrete event. A higher-for-longer rate path is a direct headwind to financing-heavy real estate.

**Strategic mortgage defaults.** Santa Monica Place defaulted on a $300M loan and transitioned to a receiver; Twenty Ninth Street ($76.5M pro rata) was in default as of February 2026; South Plains was in default in November 2025 and cured via a February 2026 extension. The non-recourse structure limits contagion, and this is rational capital allocation, but it reflects genuine asset-value impairment and guarantees continued GAAP losses.

**Tenant bankruptcies and co-tenancy.** 2025 saw 8 tenant bankruptcies (67 leases, ~873k sq ft, ~$12.9M annual revenue at MAC's share), with Saks Global and others added in early 2026. Anchor closures can trigger co-tenancy rent reductions.

**Geographic concentration** in CA, NY, and AZ exposes MAC to localized regulatory and natural-disaster risk; CA earthquake policies carry 5% deductibles and a $100M aggregate cap.

**Secular and macro headwinds:** e-commerce pressure and tariff-driven tenant cost inflation pressure tenant sales and rent-paying capacity.

**Execution risk on the Path Forward Plan.** The entire equity thesis rests on hitting deleveraging and FFO "launch point" targets over the next 2-3 years.

Mitigants: the recently upsized ($650M → $900M) and re-priced revolver, extended to March 2030, is a credible lender endorsement; the non-recourse debt structure caps downside per asset; and ~$780M of liquidity provides runway.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility is extreme and distorted. The 1-week ATM IV reads 172.6% versus 31% one-year historical volatility, but this is a thin, near-dated artifact (the 1-month tenor drops to 36.9% and 3-month to 44.4%). The term structure is in backwardation, which typically signals an imminent event, and the put/call skew is −10% (call-heavy) with a low put/call OI ratio of 0.12. ATM IV jumped from 33.9% to ~141% over six weeks while the price rose 9.7%. The net read is that positioning is call-leaning and not flagging a bearish setup; the elevated near-term vol reflects the stock's self-aware meme/short-interest character (the 10-K explicitly discusses short squeezes) more than informed bearish hedging. Short interest is moderate at 6.1% of float with 3.6 days to cover. This is consistent with a sentiment-driven name where technical factors can dominate fundamentals near-term, and it argues for patience on entry rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($30).** The SNO pipeline converts on schedule, driving go-forward NOI growth above 3% in 2026 and accelerating into 2027-2028; net-debt/EBITDA falls decisively below 7x as dispositions and FFO growth compound; high-yield redevelopments (Scottsdale 17-18%, Green Acres 10-11%) deliver; and the market re-rates MAC's FFO multiple toward Simon's as the leverage gap narrows. The dividend, currently a ~50% payout, gains room to grow. At ~22x a rising FFO base, the stock reaches the $30 analyst high.

**Bear case (~$19, ~−17% from current).** Rates stay higher for longer, refinancings roll into 6-7%+ coupons that consume the FFO growth, a consumer slowdown pressures tenant sales and triggers co-tenancy reductions, additional strategic defaults impair NAV, and SNO openings slip. The multiple compresses and the stock retraces toward the analyst low of $19, with risk toward the prior $16-17 base if the thesis breaks.

The base case sits between these: SNO conversion and deleveraging proceed broadly on plan but the rate environment caps multiple expansion, leaving the stock range-bound around current levels with modest FFO-driven appreciation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth bias on a 12-18 month horizon. The thesis is specific: the SNO pipeline converts to NOI/FFO acceleration through 2028, and net-debt/EBITDA moves below 7x. The reason it is a HOLD rather than a BUY at $23 is that the risk/reward has compressed after a 45% run, with the price now near the consensus median while the leverage risk remains unresolved.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Net-debt/EBITDA confirmed moving below ~7.0x in a quarterly supplement, demonstrating deleveraging is ahead of plan.
- SNO pipeline conversion accelerating (leasing speedometer above the 85% mid-year target and anchor openings on schedule), with go-forward NOI guidance raised above 3%.
- A pullback toward $19-20 that restores asymmetry without any fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net-debt/EBITDA rising rather than falling, or a forced equity raise to address a refinancing wall.
- Two or more additional strategic defaults on go-forward (not non-core) assets, signaling NAV impairment in the core.
- Go-forward NOI growth turning negative or the SNO pipeline conversion stalling (speedometer declining, anchor openings slipping into 2028+).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the Q2 2026 supplement, with the mid-year leasing speedometer target the key data point), and any 8-K disclosing a material refinancing.

## Conclusion

Macerich is a leveraged Class A mall turnaround with a genuinely improving operating business and a quantified, time-bound growth thesis. Rents, occupancy, sales productivity, and FFO are all rising; the SNO pipeline provides a measurable bridge to 2027-2028 NOI acceleration; and management is making visible deleveraging progress validated by lenders upsizing and re-pricing the revolver. The offsetting risk is concrete and live: 7.76x net-debt/EBITDA against a ~3.2-year maturity wall in a higher-for-longer rate world, with strategic defaults a recurring feature of the model.

We rate MAC a HOLD with a growth bias. The 12-month appreciation case is real and specific, but after a 45% twelve-month advance the stock trades near the consensus median ($24) at ~17x FFO, with the leverage overhang unresolved. The asymmetry that made this attractive in the high teens has narrowed. We would move to BUY on confirmed deleveraging below 7x, accelerating SNO conversion, or a pullback toward $19-20. We would move to AVOID on rising leverage, a forced equity raise, or strategic defaults migrating into the core portfolio.
