**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

EPR Properties is the leading diversified "experiential" net-lease REIT, owning and financing venues where consumers spend discretionary leisure time: theatres, eat-and-play (Topgolf), attractions, ski resorts, fitness/wellness, and gaming. The investment case has improved materially over the past year. A post-COVID turnaround has restored dividend coverage, FFOAA per share grew 5% to $5.12 in FY2025, leverage sits at a conservative 5.0–5.2x Net Debt/Adjusted EBITDAre, and management is deploying capital again (the $315M Six Flags attraction portfolio closing in Q2 2026). The monthly dividend was raised to $0.90/month (~$3.60 annualized, ~5.7% yield at $63.53), covered at roughly 69% of FFOAA. Estimates are drifting higher, guidance has been raised twice in 2026, and the box office is tracking up double digits year-over-year, easing the long-running "theatres are dying" bear thesis.

The catch is price. EPR has re-rated approximately 32% off its 52-week low of $48.11 to $63.53, near the 52-week high of $63.69 and above the analyst mean target of $61.50. The consensus range of $55–$70 places the current price above the mean and median, implying limited upside beyond the dividend. The turnaround narrative that drove the appreciation (coverage restoration, deployment resumption, box-office recovery) is now largely priced. What remains is a well-covered high yield, offset by a top-3 tenant concentration of ~39% (Topgolf 14.1%, AMC 13.9%, Regal 10.3%) that leaves the dividend hostage to a handful of operators, one of which (Regal/Cineworld) already went through Chapter 11 in 2022.

We rate EPR a HOLD on an income basis. The company is a legitimate, well-run, conservatively financed REIT paying a covered ~5.7% monthly yield, and it deserves to be owned for that income. After the re-rating, the equity is priced for its improved fundamentals rather than mispriced, and the balance of catalysts and risks from $63.53 is roughly symmetric. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s or on evidence that the acquisition engine is compounding FFOAA faster than the ~5% run-rate.

## Company Overview

EPR is a self-administered Maryland REIT formed in 1997 and headquartered in Kansas City. It owns and finances a portfolio of experiential real estate spread across 43 states and Canada, held predominantly through long-term triple-net leases and mortgage notes. The asset-light structure (only 54 employees) pushes operating costs, taxes, insurance, and maintenance onto tenants, leaving EPR to collect contractual rent and interest.

As of Q1 2026, total investments were approximately $7.1B, split 94% Experiential / 6% Education. The Experiential book spans 148 theatres, 61 eat-and-play venues (Topgolf, Andretti, entertainment districts), 26 attractions (water and amusement parks), 11 ski properties, 4 experiential lodging assets, 28 fitness-and-wellness locations, one gaming property (the Resorts World Catskills ground lease), and one cultural property. Occupancy runs at approximately 99%. The Education segment (46 early-childhood centers and 9 private schools) is a legacy portfolio being wound down and recycled into experiential assets.

The strategic direction is clear and consistent: shrink theatre exposure, and grow ski, attractions, eat-and-play, fitness/wellness, and gaming. Theatre investment spending was just $8.2M in 2025 versus $159M deployed into fitness and wellness, and the Six Flags acquisition reinforces the pivot. EPR's competitive edge lies in a niche most diversified REITs avoid, giving it access to non-brokered build-to-suit deals, cross-default provisions across multi-property tenants, and mortgage-to-ownership conversion options.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue rose to $718.4M in FY2025 (+3% YoY), with Q1 2026 at $181.3M (+3.6%), driven by acquisitions and developments (+$11.5M minimum rent) plus higher percentage rent. Reported net income to common jumped to $250.8M from $121.9M in 2024, but that headline overstates operating momentum: the 2024 base was depressed by $51.8M of real estate impairments and $28.2M of joint-venture impairments, both of which were zero in 2025. The honest growth read is FFOAA per share, up 5% year-over-year; Q1 2026 FFOAA per share was $1.26, up 5.9%. AFFO was approximately $398M for FY2025.

Cash flow generation is solid. Operating cash flow was $421M in FY2025. The monthly dividend of $3.52/share in FY2025 is covered at roughly 88% of AFFO, a comfortable cushion and a meaningful improvement from the post-COVID dividend-cut era. The earnings surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats in the last four quarters, including +30% in Q2 2025, +10.2% in Q4 2025, and +11.0% in Q1 2026, with one small 2% miss in Q3 2025.

The balance sheet is conservative for a REIT. Total debt is $2.93B, 99% unsecured, at a weighted-average rate of 4.40%, with no revolver balance drawn ($1.0B undrawn capacity). Net Debt/Gross Assets is 39%. The company is in compliance with all covenants. A note on the data: screening tools reporting an Altman Z of 15.0 and D/E of 0.04 are artifacts of REIT debt misclassification; actual leverage is moderate and appropriate for a net-lease REIT.

The mortgage note book (approximately $615M at a ~9.1% weighted-average yield, individual rates 7.15%–12.69%) is a high-return complement to the owned portfolio, with two collateral-dependent notes reserved (~$6.4M fully reserved). Near-term refinancing is manageable: approximately $629.6M of 2026 maturities, including $179.6M of Series B private notes due 8/22/26 at 4.56%, covered by the undrawn revolver, the ATM program, and the November 2025 issuance of $550M of 4.75% notes due 2030.

## Growth Analysis

EPR is a mid-single-digit FFOAA compounder, not a high-growth story. FFOAA per share grew 5% in 2025, and Q1 2026 ran at 5.9%. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year EPS estimates rose 3.9% over 90 days to $3.10, next-year estimates rose 2.3% to $3.11, and next-quarter estimates rose 6.2%. These are healthy but well short of the +30% revision velocity that would flag accelerating growth. Citizens upgraded the stock in June citing deployment upside.

The growth levers are external acquisitions funded by accretive capital and internal escalators embedded in the net leases. The Six Flags deal (seven attraction parks, closing Q2 2026) is the largest recent deployment and a clean diversification-away-from-theatres move. The Q1 2026 conversion of a $70M Tennessee experiential-lodging mortgage note into an owned, triple-net-leased property is another example of the mortgage-to-ownership optionality. The $400M ATM launched in December 2025 (forward sale of 797K shares at $59.52 in Q1, $352.5M remaining) provides the equity to fund the pipeline.

The key swing factor for a spread-investing net-lease REIT is cost of capital. The stock re-rating and a stabilizing rate environment would lower EPR's cost of equity and re-open accretive external growth. The near-term tailwind is a recovering box office, which supports AMC/Regal rents and percentage-rent upside. This is a durable-but-modest growth profile, appropriate for an income-oriented holding rather than a total-return compounder.

## Valuation Assessment

At $63.53, EPR trades at roughly 12.4x FY2026 FFOAA (using the $5.12 base plus ~5% growth) and about 19x trailing earnings. Against peers in the specialized-REIT group, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 22.4 with a median yield of 4.5%. EPR's ~5.7% dividend yield is well above the peer median, reflecting both its higher risk profile (tenant concentration, theatre exposure) and its stronger income orientation. GLPI, the most comparable gaming/experiential net-lease name, trades at 15.1x with a 6.8% yield, suggesting EPR is priced at a modest premium to the highest-yielding comparable.

The core valuation issue is that the discount is gone. A year ago, EPR traded near $48 with unresolved theatre and coverage concerns; today, near its 52-week high, it prices in the turnaround. The analyst median target is $61.00, below the current price, with a low of $55. The current price sits above mean and median consensus, which for a mid-single-digit grower signals that the re-rating has run its course rather than that targets are stale. This is a stabilized income vehicle trading at fair value, not a stock with rapidly rising estimates and a compressed multiple.

We assign a valuation score of 5.0 (neutral). The stock is neither a value trap (the fundamentals are genuinely improving and the yield is covered) nor an obvious bargain (the price fully reflects the improved outlook). The covered yield provides absolute return support, but there is little identifiable mispricing to reward. On our base case, the stock offers total return roughly equal to its dividend plus modest FFOAA growth, with limited multiple expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

EPR occupies a defensible niche. Its experiential focus attracts limited direct competition from the broader net-lease universe, which tends to favor generic retail, industrial, or office. That specialization enables exclusive build-to-suit relationships, cross-default protections, and mortgage-conversion optionality that generalist competitors cannot easily replicate. The deliberate remix is actively improving diversification.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than clearly widening. The new CIO, Ben Fox (ex-Ares Net Lease MD, ex-Realty Income EVP of Asset Management), signals a potential professionalization and acceleration of the acquisition engine. The near-term box-office recovery strengthens the theatre tenants, and the Six Flags deal broadens the attractions exposure. Against this, the fundamental competitive vulnerability is structural: net-lease REIT returns depend on cost-of-capital spreads, and EPR's higher-risk asset mix keeps its cost of capital elevated versus lower-risk net-lease peers. The moat is real but narrow, and the trajectory is incremental improvement. We score competitive position at 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Tenant concentration** is the single largest fundamental risk. The top three tenants represent roughly 39% of revenue (Q1 2026). A default or restructuring by any one could pressure the dividend. Topgolf now warrants the closest watch: it is the largest tenant, and its parent, Topgolf Callaway, has been exploring a separation, which introduces credit-quality uncertainty at EPR's biggest income source.

**Theatre secular risk** remains despite the near-term recovery. EPR still owns 148 theatres, exposed to studio consolidation, compressed theatrical windows (~45 days versus ~75 historically), and streaming. Management is actively de-risking, but this is the largest single property-type exposure and the driver of the multi-year bear thesis.

**Refinancing risk** is manageable but rate-dependent: the 2026 maturities are covered by the undrawn revolver, ATM, and prior issuances, but any refinancing occurs at higher rates than the maturing 4.40%-average book. **Discretionary-spend sensitivity** ties all experiential categories to consumer health, and macro pressures (recession, inflation, tariffs) flow through to tenant sales and percentage rent. Smaller tail risks include gaming regulation (Resorts World Catskills), coastal/climate exposure (the St. Pete Beach hurricane-destroyed JV lodging assets, still being exited), development funding risk, and key-person risk given only 54 employees.

We score risk factors at 5.0. The concentration and theatre risks are well-known and substantially priced, but they are not fully de-risked: the Topgolf separation and any single-tenant restructuring remain live, quantifiable threats to a dividend that is otherwise well-covered.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no strong directional signal. Near-term ATM IV is 23.6% versus 22.9% historical volatility, a fair 0.7-point spread indicating no elevated event premium. Put/call skew is balanced at -2.8%, and the put/call volume and OI ratios (0.18 and 0.10) are call-tilted, consistent with the recent upward price momentum. IV rose 3 points as the stock climbed 9.8% since the prior snapshot, and the P/C OI ratio fell sharply from 0.44 to 0.10, indicating bullish repositioning rather than hedging. The term structure is flat, distributing uncertainty evenly. Against this, short interest is elevated at 14.1% of float with 12.2 days to cover, reflecting the institutional bearish view on concentration and theatre risk; the high days-to-cover creates some squeeze potential on positive catalysts, but the crowded short also validates the cautionary thesis. Net read: options are neutral-to-mildly-bullish, while short interest keeps a bearish counterweight in play.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($70).** The acquisition engine accelerates under the new CIO, deploying the ATM capital accretively into attractions, eat-and-play, and gaming; FFOAA growth accelerates above the ~5% run-rate toward high single digits. The box office continues its recovery, lifting AMC/Regal rents and percentage rent, and de-risking the theatre exposure faster than the market expects. Cost of capital falls as rates stabilize, re-opening spread-positive external growth. The covered yield plus mid-to-high-single-digit FFOAA growth and modest multiple expansion delivers total returns toward the high-$60s/$70.

**Bear case ($48–52).** A top-three tenant stumbles (most plausibly Topgolf amid the parent separation, or a renewed theatre downturn hitting AMC/Regal), forcing a coverage reassessment. A consumer spending contraction pressures percentage rent and tenant health across the experiential book. Higher-for-longer rates raise refinancing costs on the 2026 maturities and compress the acquisition spread, stalling growth. The stock de-rates back toward its 52-week low, a ~20% decline from current levels, with the dividend intact but the equity offering only its yield.

**Base case ($64–66).** EPR delivers ~5% FFOAA growth, maintains the covered dividend with a modest annual raise, and closes the Six Flags deal without incident. The multiple holds around 12–12.5x FFOAA. Total return approximates the dividend plus low-single-digit price appreciation, roughly 7–9% over twelve months, driven almost entirely by income rather than re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income hold. EPR is a fairly-valued, well-covered ~5.7% monthly yield with a conservative balance sheet and modest growth, worth owning for the income, but with the re-rating largely complete and limited price upside from $63.53.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FFOAA-per-share growth accelerates above 7% on accretive deployment, or estimate revisions turn to +10% or more over 90 days.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s that restores a meaningful discount to intrinsic value and pushes the yield above 6.5%.
- Resolution of the Topgolf parent separation on terms that de-risk the largest tenant (e.g., an investment-grade standalone operator or lease affirmation).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A top-three tenant default, restructuring, or lease rejection that impairs dividend coverage below AFFO.
- FFOAA-per-share growth turning negative, or a dividend that ceases to be covered by AFFO.
- Refinancing of the 2026 maturities at rates materially above the current 4.40% average that visibly compress the acquisition spread and stall external growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) for FFOAA delivery, Six Flags deal close confirmation, and updated tenant-concentration and guidance data.

## Conclusion

EPR Properties has earned its re-rating. The turnaround worked: coverage was rebuilt, the dividend restored and raised to a covered ~5.7% monthly yield, leverage held at a conservative ~5x, and the company is on offense again with the Six Flags acquisition, note-to-ownership conversions, and twice-raised 2026 guidance. Total shareholder return of 212.46 versus a 137.53 peer index confirms the market has rewarded the recovery. This is a legitimate, shareholder-friendly, well-run REIT.

The problem for new capital is that the appreciation reflects the improved fundamentals rather than a persistent mispricing. Near the 52-week high and above both the mean ($61.50) and median ($61.00) analyst targets, the stock is priced for its ~5% growth and ~5.7% yield, with limited room for further multiple expansion and a tenant concentration (~39% top-three) that keeps the dividend hostage to Topgolf, AMC, and Regal. The score trajectory (5.1 → 6.6 → 5.6, AVOID → HOLD → HOLD) reflects genuine fundamental improvement now offset by a higher price; the current data confirms the improving fundamentals but does not support upgrading past the valuation.

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** Own EPR for its covered monthly yield and conservative balance sheet, not for price appreciation. Accumulate on weakness toward the low-to-mid $50s, where the yield exceeds 6.5% and the risk/reward turns asymmetric, or on evidence the acquisition engine is compounding FFOAA faster than the mid-single-digit run-rate. At current levels the balance of catalysts and risks is roughly symmetric.
