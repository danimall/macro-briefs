**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Somnigroup International is the world's largest bedding company, the former Tempur Sealy renamed in February 2025 after the ~$5.4B acquisition of Mattress Firm converted a wholesale-dependent manufacturer into a vertically integrated manufacturer-plus-retailer. The deal changed the economics of the business: the Direct channel jumped from 24.9% of sales in 2024 to 63.5% in 2025, and no single customer now exceeds 5% of revenue, eliminating the wholesale-concentration risk that cost the company $26.7M in 2024 when a distributor's acquisition foreclosed OEM distribution. FY25 revenue rose 51.6% to $7.48B, almost entirely from consolidating Mattress Firm, while organic bedding demand stayed soft.

The near-term picture is a GAAP-versus-adjusted divergence resolving in the company's favor. GAAP EPS fell 14.8% to $1.84 on ~$264M of acquisition charges and a 34M-share issuance, but adjusted EPS rose 5.9% to $2.70, and Q1'26 provided the cleanest evidence yet that the merger is working operationally: Tempur Sealy North America gross margin expanded 2,390bps YoY, of which ~620bps was explicitly synergy-driven, and GAAP operating income jumped to $187.1M from $13.2M as one-time deal costs rolled off. Adjusted EPS grew 20.4% to $0.59. Credit-facility leverage improved to 3.07x from 3.21x, well inside the 5.0x covenant, and FCF was $633M in FY25 (8.5% margin).

The stock has fallen roughly 29% from its 52-week high of $98.56 to ~$70, and the drivers are identifiable rather than diffuse: consumer-discretionary demand weakness in a big-ticket deferrable category, dilution and integration load from a second major acquisition (the ~$2.5B all-stock Leggett & Platt deal signed April 13, 2026 and cleared antitrust June 4), and negative estimate revisions (current-year consensus down 4.3% over 90 days, current-quarter down 19.1%). At ~22x current-year consensus EPS of $3.15 the stock is not expensive for a scaled category leader realizing synergies, and sell-side targets sit at $97-105 against a $70 price. What holds this at a growth HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of two overlapping integrations, a still-soft consumer, and the FTC-imposed structural constraint on the vertical-integration thesis. The trajectory of prior scores (4.8 → 5.5 over four months) reflects genuine de-risking of the balance-sheet and valuation concerns, and current data confirms that trend rather than reversing it.

## Company Overview

Somnigroup operates three reporting segments. **Mattress Firm** (~$3.5B FY25 sales) is the largest U.S. mattress specialty retailer with ~2,161 stores, 62 distribution centers, and e-commerce, and it sells competitor brands (Purple, Serta, Beautyrest, Nectar) alongside Somnigroup products. **Tempur Sealy North America** (~$2.7B) manufactures and distributes owned brands (Tempur-Pedic, Sealy, Stearns & Foster, and the Sleepy's private label) plus OEM output. **Tempur Sealy International** (~$1.3B) covers Europe, APAC, and LatAm, including Dreams (the UK's leading bedding retailer, 200+ stores) and SOVA in Sweden.

The company owns brands 130-175 years old, more than 1,000 patents, roughly 70 manufacturing facilities, and four R&D centers, positioning itself in a ~$120B global sleep market. CEO Scott Thompson, who architected the Tempur Sealy-to-Somnigroup transformation, holds ~4.0M shares. The retention of Mattress Firm's CEO Steven Rusing is a constructive integration signal. Third Point (Dan Loeb) is reported to hold SGI as a top position.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY25 net sales came almost entirely from the ~11-month Mattress Firm stub period ($3.5B), offset by the elimination of $976M of previously intercompany wholesale sales. GAAP operating margin compressed to 10.1% from 12.9%; adjusted operating margin was 13.6%. Interest expense nearly doubled to $267.9M from $134.8M, the direct cost of acquisition leverage.

**The GAAP/adjusted gap is a timing and accounting artifact, not economic deterioration.** The GAAP EPS decline is almost fully explained by the acquisition/transaction charges and the 34.2M-share issuance (diluted count rose from 178M to 209M). Q1'26 corroborates this: net sales +12.3% to $1.80B, and the year-earlier $13.2M GAAP operating income reflected $114M of deal costs and a GAAP loss. TTM net income reached $521.4M. The earnings-surprise record is solid but not spectacular — three beats and one narrow miss over four quarters (Q3'25 +9.6%, Q1'26 +1.9%, Q4'25 -1.0%) — consistent with a company delivering roughly in line while the consumer stays weak.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is $4.58B (down from $4.72B), with goodwill of $4.59B and intangibles of $2.58B now dominating the $11.5B asset base after the Mattress Firm purchase (goodwill $3.49B plus $1.88B indefinite-lived trade name). The Altman Z-Score of 1.43 nominally places SGI in the "distress zone," but this is misleading for an asset-light, lease-heavy retailer. The score is depressed by (a) a deliberate working-capital deficit (-$271M at YE25, -$360M at Q1'26) driven by ~$273M of short-term operating-lease liabilities across Mattress Firm's ~2,150 leased stores, and (b) the goodwill/intangible balance suppressing equity-to-assets. Management states it will "generally operate with a working capital deficit," which is normal for the model. The functional metrics matter more: credit-facility leverage of 3.07x versus a 5.0x covenant, FCF of $633M (operating cash flow $800M), and a Piotroski F-score of 4/5. The company targets a return to 2.0-3.0x leverage in 2026.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $0.17/quarter (from $0.15), a ~1.0% yield. $774.5M remains on the buyback authorization, but no open-market repurchases occurred in FY25 or Q1'26, with cash directed to deleveraging. Management plans to allocate ~50% of FCF to dividends plus buybacks once back in the target leverage range, so a resumption of repurchases would signal that leverage discipline has been restored.

Rate sensitivity is a live consideration: ~$2.87B of variable-rate debt means a 100bp move is ~$28.7M pretax. In the current higher-for-longer environment, deleveraging is both financially and strategically the right priority.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth story is muted. Management repeatedly flags a bedding industry "challenged in 2025 due to macroeconomic pressures on the consumer," with the mid-to-entry-level tier under "outsized pressure," prompting the 2025 Sealy Posturepedic relaunch (PrecisionFit coils) and a 2026 all-new Stearns & Foster premium line. Mattresses are big-ticket, deferrable purchases tied to housing turnover and consumer confidence, both soft in the current cycle.

The growth thesis therefore rests on three internal levers rather than end-market recovery. First, **synergy realization**: the ~620bps of synergy-driven gross-margin lift in Q1'26 NA is tangible and recurring, and full Mattress Firm synergy capture is a multi-year runway. Second, **the Leggett & Platt acquisition**, an all-stock deal (including LEG debt) that extends vertical integration upstream into innersprings and components, potentially improving cost control and value-chain capture. It requires LEG shareholder approval but not SGI's, and targets a year-end 2026 close. Third, **channel mix**: at 63.5% Direct, more of each sale is captured, and DTC economics can offset a soft top line.

Estimate revisions temper enthusiasm. Next-year EPS consensus fell 4.8% over 90 days to $3.90; the current quarter is roughly flat over the last 30 days at +0.7%, suggesting the cuts have stabilized. Estimates are drifting lower on consumer weakness even as synergies flow through, which is the tension that keeps this a HOLD. The next-year figure implies ~24% EPS growth off the current-year base, driven by synergy capture and LEG accretion, but that depends on execution and a stabilizing consumer.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$70, SGI trades at ~18x next-year $3.90. That is a premium to the Home Furnishings peer median P/E of 16.4x (MHK 16.4x, LZB 19.5x, LEG 6.6x, ETD 14.0x), but the peer set is a poor comparison — those are pure component manufacturers or smaller furniture retailers, not a scaled, vertically integrated bedding leader realizing multi-year synergies. The premium is defensible on the growth differential (~24% forward EPS growth versus flat-to-declining peers) and the durable market-leadership position.

The sell-side gap is the central valuation observation. Consensus targets sit at a $97.25 mean / $98.5 median (KeyBanc Overweight $105 post-antitrust clearance; Seeking Alpha Buy $100), with a $76 low and $115 high, implying ~40% upside to the mean if the thesis executes. On stocks with reasonable multiples, analyst targets tend to lag reality, but the caution here is that the estimate revisions are moving the wrong way, so those targets could compress rather than get chased higher if consumer weakness persists. This is not the clean setup where cheapness plus rising estimates equals a floor.

The lessons from recent scoring history argue against rewarding a "cheap" print at face value: cheapness has been anti-predictive absent an independent catalyst, and Consumer Discretionary carries a structural alpha deficit. Here the catalysts (synergy realization, LEG close) are real but the consumer overhang and negative revisions blunt the asymmetry. The prior score history moved valuation from 3.5 to 5.0 as the balance-sheet distress narrative was debunked and the multiple compressed on price weakness; current data supports holding valuation around 5.5, with the stock fairly priced to modestly cheap on forward earnings if execution holds, but not a mispricing wide enough to override the demand and integration risks. I set a base case of $82 (~26x the $3.15 current-year EPS, a modest premium justified by synergy visibility and category leadership).

## Competitive Landscape

SGI is the scaled leader in a consolidating bedding market. On the manufacturing side the U.S. is concentrated (SGI, Serta Simmons, Purple, Sleep Number, plus DTC entrants like Saatva); on the retail side international markets remain fragmented. The Mattress Firm acquisition was a defensive-and-offensive vertical-integration move that secures ~2,150 premium retail doors and a 63% direct mix, insulating the company from the wholesale channel risk that materialized in 2024. The Leggett & Platt deal extends integration upstream into springs and components. The "own the value chain" thesis (manufacturing plus components plus the largest U.S. retailer) is the core structural advantage, and the competitive trajectory is improving: SGI is the entity driving consolidation rather than defending against it.

The material offset is the **FTC-imposed 43% floor-slot commitment**: to win Mattress Firm approval, SGI must reserve 43% of premium ($1,500+) horizontal floor slots for third-party premium mattresses, monitored by an independent consultant (in compliance as of 12/31/25). This directly caps how much SGI can monetize its retail control by pushing its own Tempur-Pedic and Stearns & Foster products, and the recurring "floor model transition" costs relate to reconfiguring merchandising to meet it. The 10-K also notes competitors are themselves vertically integrating and pushing private label and DTC. The competitive position is strong and improving, but the FTC constraint prevents the full monopolistic monetization the bull case might imagine, which is why I score this a 6.5 rather than higher.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration risk, now doubled.** The 10-K describes Mattress Firm integration as "complex and time-consuming," and internal controls over Mattress Firm are still being incorporated (to be assessed in the FY2026 10-K). SGI is layering a second large integration (Leggett & Platt) before the first is fully digested, the single most important execution risk.

**Antitrust merchandising constraint.** The 43% floor-slot commitment is a company-specific structural limit with legal/regulatory downside if compliance lapses.

**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** ~$2.87B variable-rate debt (~$28.7M pretax per 100bp); dividend/buyback restrictions above 3.50-3.75x leverage. Deleveraging progress (3.07x, targeting 2.0-3.0x) is the key monitor.

**Consumer demand.** Big-ticket deferrable category tied to soft housing turnover and confidence; weakness in the broader discretionary complex is a direct headwind. The mid-to-entry tier is under the most pressure.

**Tariffs and supply chain.** Adjustable bases depend on China-origin components; management believes 2026 impact is immaterial (majority of U.S. product is U.S.-made) but the exposure is open-ended.

**Multi-employer pensions.** Participation in several "Red Zone" (<65% funded) plans creates withdrawal-liability exposure.

The idiosyncratic risks that are not fully priced — a second integration mid-first-integration, the FTC constraint on the vertical thesis, and negative estimate revisions in a soft consumer — justify a risk score of 5.0. These are real and specific rather than sector truisms, and while none are near-term binary blowups, they collectively cap the risk/reward asymmetry.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning corroborates near-term caution. Near-term ATM IV of 58.2% is rich versus 38.4% one-year historical vol (an 87th-percentile reading), and the term structure is in backwardation (58.2% near-term vs 52.6% long-term), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress, most likely the mid/late-July Q2 print. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±12.9%. Put/call skew is elevated at 5.2%, and the 6-month put skew of -3.2% indicates hedging demand into the LEG-close window. ATM IV has fallen sharply from 84.4% to 58.2% since mid-June while the price fell 8.5%, so some of the event premium has already deflated. Short interest of 9.3% of float (5.3 days to cover) is moderate, not a crowded bearish setup. Rich IV, put skew, and backwardation into an earnings binary argue for patience on entry rather than chasing, and confirm the fundamental read that the next quarter carries genuine two-sided risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-115).** Synergy realization continues (the NA gross-margin lift compounds across the segment), the consumer stabilizes or recovers, LEG closes and adds accretive vertical integration, leverage falls into the 2.0-3.0x range and buybacks resume, and the multiple re-rates toward the sell-side $97-105 range on higher forward EPS. At the high end this reaches the $115 high analyst target.

**Bear Case ($50-55).** Consumer weakness deepens through H2 2026, mattress demand contracts further, dual-integration execution stumbles (control weaknesses, synergy shortfalls), estimate revisions continue lower, and the market applies a peer-median ~16x multiple to a cut ~$3.00 EPS, taking the stock to ~$48-50. This represents ~28-30% downside from the current price and is the scenario the options market is partly hedging.

**Base Case ($82).** Synergies flow as guided, the consumer stays soft but does not collapse, LEG closes on schedule, and the market applies ~26x to $3.15 current-year EPS as forward visibility improves, yielding ~$82 (~17% upside). The primary swing factor between base and bull is the consumer; between base and bear it is integration execution.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD (growth_hold)**. The 12-month growth thesis is intact — synergy capture plus LEG accretion supports ~24% forward EPS growth — but the soft consumer, negative near-term revisions, and dual-integration load keep this short of a BUY. Timeframe: 12 months, keyed to the mid/late-July Q2 print, the LEG close by year-end 2026, and the FY2026 10-K assessment of Mattress Firm internal controls.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year consensus stabilizes and rises for two consecutive quarters), signaling consumer stabilization plus synergy flow-through.
- Leverage reaches 2.0-3.0x and management resumes open-market buybacks, a concrete signal that balance-sheet risk has cleared and capital return has restarted.
- LEG closes cleanly with purchase accounting confirming accretion, or a pullback toward $60 (mid-teens forward P/E) widens the risk/reward.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Q2 or subsequent revenue growth turns negative YoY with synergy realization decelerating, indicating the consumer cliff is materializing faster than synergies can offset.
- Material weakness or control failure surfaces in the FY2026 10-K Mattress Firm integration assessment.
- Leverage rises back above 3.5x or LEG integration produces a synergy shortfall/impairment.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the mid/late-July Q2 2026 earnings report, followed by the LEG-close window in Q4 2026.

## Conclusion

Somnigroup is a fundamentally improved business trading at a reasonable forward multiple, with tangible evidence that its Mattress Firm acquisition is delivering synergies and a balance sheet whose headline "distress" score is an accounting artifact rather than genuine solvency risk. The ~29% de-rate from the 52-week high reflects identifiable concerns — consumer-demand weakness, dilution and integration load from the second all-stock acquisition, and negative estimate revisions — rather than a broken thesis, and the sell-side sees ~40% upside to the mean target.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because the setup lacks the clean asymmetry a buy requires. Estimates are drifting lower, not inflecting higher; the company is running two large integrations simultaneously; the FTC 43%-floor commitment structurally caps the vertical-integration monetization thesis; and the options market is pricing genuine two-sided risk into the next print. The trajectory of the score (4.8 → 5.5) correctly reflects the de-risking of the balance-sheet and valuation narratives, and I maintain that direction. For existing holders, the growth thesis justifies patience through the July print and the LEG close. New capital is better deployed on confirmation — either a positive revision turn or a pullback toward $60 — given the consumer overhang and the structural alpha headwind in Consumer Discretionary.
