**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CarMax is the largest used-vehicle retailer in the U.S., and it is emerging from a clean, well-defined earnings trough. Fiscal 2026 (year ended Feb 28, 2026) was the low point: net income fell 50.6% to $247M and diluted EPS collapsed to $1.68 from $3.21, dragged down by a $141.3M non-cash goodwill impairment (writing off all Edmunds goodwill), $49.8M of restructuring charges tied to a CEO change and workforce cuts, SG&A deleverage, and rising CarMax Auto Finance (CAF) credit losses. Adjusted EPS fell a milder 11% to $2.91. The board fired CEO Bill Nash effective December 2025 and hired outside operator Keith Barr (former IHG Hotels CEO) as President & CEO in March 2026, who has laid out a four-pillar turnaround plan with a full strategic update promised for late fall 2026.

Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended May 31, 2026) delivered the first evidence the trough is behind the company. Revenue returned to growth at +6.2% YoY to $8.01B, the EPS decline shrank to −5% ($1.31 vs $1.38 prior year) against a large consensus beat ($1.31 vs $0.99 estimate), SG&A per unit fell 6.8% restoring operating leverage, and CAF loan penetration rose 150bps to 43.3% while net credit losses improved to 1.80% from 1.96%. A cluster of open-market insider purchases in June — including the new CEO buying ~$500K of stock alongside four independent directors near the post-earnings low — signals that management believes the trough is in.

The catch is price. The stock has rallied roughly 90% off its November 2025 low of $30.26 and now trades at ~$58.70, above the consensus mean analyst target of $53.15 and near the top of its recent range. Barclays' July upgrade to Equal Weight came with a $61 target, barely above spot. The "trough is in" re-rating appears largely captured. Further upside requires proof of both durable volume growth and margin/credit stabilization, and that proof is being bought with deliberately lower gross profit per unit (GPU), higher advertising spend, and an intentional move down the credit spectrum via CAF Tier-2 expansion into a consumer showing late-cycle stress. We rate KMX a HOLD.

## Company Overview

CarMax sold 780,684 retail used vehicles in FY2026 across 256 stores in 110 TV markets covering ~85% of the U.S. population. The company operates two reportable segments:

**CarMax Sales Operations (~79% of revenue)** — retail used-vehicle sales, wholesale auctions (~538K vehicles, ~99% sell-through), extended protection products (ESP/GAP), Edmunds, and service. The differentiators are no-haggle pricing, nationwide inventory transfer (~38% of sales are transferred at customer request), and an omni-channel platform that handled 81% of transactions digitally in FY26. The company sourced ~987K vehicles directly from consumers in FY26 through instant appraisal and its MaxOffer dealer channel.

**CarMax Auto Finance (CAF)** — a captive lender that financed 42.4% of retail units in FY26 and services a ~$16.4B loan portfolio across ~1M accounts. CAF generated ~$563M of income in FY26 and is a distinct, meaningful profit engine that also shapes the company's balance-sheet optics.

Despite being #1, CarMax holds only ~3.6% of the 0–10-year-old used-vehicle market (down from 3.7%) in a highly fragmented ~39M-units/year market. Its structural competitors are 18,000+ franchised dealers with certified pre-owned (CPO) advantages, online disruptors led by Carvana, independent dealers, and private sellers.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year trajectory shows a business that peaked in the 2021–2022 used-car boom and has since normalized down, with FY26 marking an acute profit trough:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $29.7B | $26.5B | $26.4B | $25.9B |
| Net income | $485M | $479M | $501M | $247M |
| Diluted EPS | ~$3.03 | $3.02 | $3.21 | $1.68 |
| Adj. EPS | — | $2.70 | $3.27 | $2.91 |
| Used GPU | $2,288 | $2,288 | $2,311 | $2,253 |

The FY26 GAAP decline was amplified by non-recurring items: the Edmunds goodwill write-off was non-deductible, which spiked the effective tax rate to 35.5%, and restructuring covered CEO severance, workforce reductions, and an Edmunds lease abandonment. SG&A as a percent of gross profit jumped to 87.4% from 84.0% as gross profit fell 3.2% while costs stayed roughly flat — the operating deleverage that Barr's "Run Lean" pillar targets.

The most important fundamental thread is CAF credit. Net credit losses rose to 2.34% of average loans in FY26 from 2.03% in FY25 and 1.93% in FY24; the provision jumped to $391M from $335M. The driver was 2022–2023 vintage loans originated when car prices were peak-inflated, with recovery rates falling to 45.5% from 53% in FY24. Management tightened underwriting after April 2024, and the newer vintages are performing in line.

Q1 FY2027 detail:
- Used units roughly flat (+0.04%); wholesale units +8.4%.
- SG&A per total unit down to $1,619.
- Deliberate margin sacrifice for volume: used GPU dropped $230 to $2,177 as management cut prices. Full-year guidance calls for used margin down ~$200/unit.
- CAF interest margin 6.7%; allowance ratio ticked up to 2.95% on Tier 2 expansion and seasonal mix.

**The balance-sheet "distress" flag is a false positive.** The Altman Z-Score of 1.6 (distress zone) and D/E of 3.48 are artifacts of CAF's business model. Of ~$18.1B total debt, ~$16.1B is non-recourse securitization/warehouse debt legally isolated in funding vehicles — investors have no recourse to CarMax's operating assets. Ex-CAF recourse debt is modest (~$2.3B). The company is in covenant compliance, has $1.11B of undrawn revolver and $3.24B of unused warehouse capacity, refinanced a $500M term loan in June 2026 (maturing 2029), and runs a current ratio of 2.2. The real financial risk is earnings/credit cyclicality and securitization-market access, not solvency.

**Capital allocation** turned defensive. Buybacks were paused in Q4 FY26 as leverage sat "slightly above targeted range"; $1.31B remains authorized. FY26 buybacks totaled $643M; Q1 FY27 was essentially zero. Capex is guided down to ~$400M in FY27 from $541M. Share count has fallen from 158M (FY24) to ~142M — meaningful prior reduction, but the pause removes a near-term per-share tailwind.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the turnaround converting into durable unit and share gains. The near-term evidence is encouraging but not yet conclusive. Estimate revisions have moved the right way: current-year EPS consensus rose to $2.71 from $2.39 ninety days ago (+13.2%), and next-year to $3.11 from $2.99 (+4.1%). The current-quarter revision is essentially flat (−1.6% over 30 days, +8.4% over 90 days), so the momentum is real but decelerating at the margin.

The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward numbers: three beats in the last four quarters (June +32.4%, March +46.8%, December +34.5%), with the one miss (September −40.1%) coinciding with the disclosures that preceded the CEO ouster. The pattern is consistent with a company that hit bottom and is now beating a reset bar.

The quality of the growth is the open question. Used units were essentially flat in Q1 even as revenue grew, and the volume that did materialize was purchased with a GPU cut and higher advertising. If price cuts do not durably lift units and market share, both the top and bottom lines suffer. Management conceded share was "pressured in the second half of calendar 2025" even as it gained in the first half, consistent with a competitive squeeze from Carvana. The CAF penetration gain (toward a 50% goal) is a cleaner growth lever, capturing finance economics on each additional financed unit, but that lever is intertwined with the credit risk discussed below.

We do not observe the strongest inflection signal — 90-day EPS revisions above +30% combined with revenue growth above 30% — that would force a cycle-adjusted BUY. Current-year revisions of +13.2% on +6.2% revenue growth are positive but well short of that threshold.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$58.70, KMX trades at roughly 22x consensus current-year EPS of $2.71 and ~19x next-year EPS of $3.11. On trailing GAAP EPS of $1.68 the multiple is ~35x, but that trailing figure is depressed by the goodwill impairment and restructuring, so the forward multiples are the relevant lens.

The peer set is instructive and cautionary. Automotive-retail comparables trade at a median P/E of 10.8: Lithia 10.8x, AutoNation 10.6x, Asbury 7.1x. Even the higher-multiple names (Murphy USA 21.5x, Valvoline 50.5x) are structurally different businesses. On a forward basis, KMX at ~19–22x commands a substantial premium to the franchised-dealer group. Part of that is justified: CarMax's captive finance arm, direct-to-consumer sourcing, and national footprint are genuine differentiators that the pure dealer comps lack, and CAF earnings recovery gives the forward number a distinct driver. But a doubling-plus of the peer median requires the turnaround to deliver, and that outcome is not yet proven.

The more direct valuation constraint is analyst targets and price action. The stock sits above the consensus mean target and the median of $50.00 (range $33–$96). On our reading of the valuation lessons, prior appreciation alone is not a reason to mark a stock down, and analyst targets can lag on cheap inflection names. But KMX is not cheap on a peer-relative basis, and the stock now trades above the mean target with the analyst distribution clustered at Hold/Equal-Weight (13 holds, 3 buys, 3 strong sells in the current period). At this level, the "trough is in" re-rating appears substantially priced.

We assign valuation a 4.5. The multiple is not stretched in absolute terms for a business inflecting off a trough, but it offers no margin of safety versus peers, sits above consensus targets, and requires proof of both volume and margin/credit stabilization to justify further upside. Consistent with the broader value-trap dynamics in this sector, a "reasonable" forward multiple on a Consumer Discretionary name is not itself a buy signal without an independent catalyst, and the next dated catalyst (the late-fall strategic update) is several months out.

## Competitive Landscape

CarMax's structural edges remain intact: the largest physical footprint in used-car retail, a recognized brand built on no-haggle pricing, a captive finance arm, an omni-channel platform, and consumer-direct sourcing. The turnaround thesis is precisely that these assets were under-leveraged because of a clunky digital experience, uncompetitive pricing, and bloated costs — all of which Barr's four-pillar plan targets.

The competitive trajectory is the concern. Market share of the 0–10-year-old used-vehicle market slipped, and management acknowledged share pressure in the second half of calendar 2025. Carvana is the key disruptor; the market treats KMX and CVNA as linked on used-car margin and subprime-credit fears, evidenced by both falling together on the June 17 print (KMX closed down ~9% and Carvana fell ~8%). Franchised dealers retain a CPO advantage and manufacturer relationships that CarMax cannot fully match (it lacks manufacturer authorization for recall repairs). Longer term, EV and autonomous-vehicle secular change is an unquantified overhang on the used-car model.

The trajectory is roughly stable-to-slightly-negative on share, offset by a credible strategic reset and improving digital/pricing execution. We score competitive position at 5.0 — a market leader working to reverse modest share erosion, with the outcome still to be proven.

## Risk Assessment

Several company-specific risks that are not fully priced warrant attention:

**CAF credit and the Tier-2 expansion (the key idiosyncratic risk).** Credit losses drove much of the FY26 profit decline. Management is deliberately expanding Tier 2 lending (target ~30% of Tier 2 volume in FY27, up from <15%) to capture finance economics — each point of penetration adds an estimated $10–12M of lifetime pre-tax income but also raises the provision. This is a calculated bet that amplifies downside if the consumer weakens. A 10% downside macro scenario would add ~$52M to the allowance. Moving down the credit spectrum precisely as the company laps elevated 2022–2023 vintage losses, and against a backdrop of consumer stress (rising used-car prices, elevated rates, affordability strain), is the single most important thing to monitor. Q1's improved loss rate is a positive early data point, but a full quarter of Tier-2 seasoning has not occurred.

**Volume-vs-margin tradeoff.** The turnaround explicitly trades GPU for units. If price cuts do not durably lift volume and share, both lines suffer and the higher ad spend becomes a permanent drag on the "Run Lean" thesis.

**Securities litigation (new).** *Jason Cap v. CarMax* (D. Md., filed 11/3/2025, amended 3/31/2026) alleges §10(b)/§20(a) violations for the class period 6/20/2025–11/5/2025, tied to disclosures preceding the CEO ouster. A motion to dismiss has been filed; loss is not yet estimable. This is an overhang, not yet a quantifiable liability.

**Leadership and activism risk.** The CEO transition to an outside hire brings execution risk during a critical reset, and the 10-K devotes an unusually prominent risk factor to activism/proxy contests, suggesting management anticipates or has faced activist pressure. Continued Item 5.02 8-K churn through 2026 indicates ongoing executive/board turnover.

**Securitization-market sensitivity.** With ~$16.1B of non-recourse funding, CAF depends on continued access to the securitization market at reasonable spreads. Rising funding costs would compress CAF's interest margin.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not confirming an imminent directional move. Near-term ATM IV is 41.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 57.8% — options are cheap on a realized-vol basis (−16.4% spread), sitting at the 2nd percentile of the current chain. This follows an IV collapse from 210.1% pre-earnings on June 17 to 41.4% now, as the earnings catalyst resolved. The term structure is in contango (near-term 41.4% < 6-month 48.3%), a normal, complacent structure. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (1.9% near-term), and the put/call open-interest ratio fell to 0.36 from 0.59, indicating reduced hedging pressure. Short interest is elevated at 10.1% of float with 3.0 days to cover — meaningful bearish institutional positioning but not squeeze-prone at that days-to-cover level. The market is pricing resolved near-term uncertainty and a ±9.5% implied move on the nearest expiry, with no acute directional bet in either direction. Cheap near-term vol favors patient entry over chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75–96).** The trough is confirmed and the turnaround compounds. Barr's four-pillar plan drives durable unit growth and market-share recovery, SG&A leverage sticks, GPU stabilizes near the guided level rather than deteriorating further, and CAF Tier-2 expansion delivers finance economics without a credit blowup as tightened-underwriting vintages season well. Buybacks resume once leverage normalizes, adding a per-share tailwind. Next-year EPS reaches the low-to-mid $3s and the stock re-rates on the finance-plus-retail hybrid quality. The insider-buying cluster proves prescient.

**Bear Case ($33–40).** Volume growth proves ephemeral and was simply bought with margin — units stall, GPU keeps sliding, and ad spend becomes a structural cost. The CAF Tier-2 bet coincides with a genuine consumer cliff; credit losses re-accelerate as the deliberate move down the credit spectrum meets a weakening subprime borrower. The securities litigation and activism pressure distract management during a fragile reset. The stock gives back most of its rally toward the low end of the analyst range as the peer-relative premium compresses.

**Base Case (~$58–62).** Modest continued improvement — revenue grows mid-single digits, EPS recovers toward $2.71 this year and low-$3s next year, CAF stabilizes but without dramatic upside — leaves the stock roughly range-bound near current levels. The re-rating from trough is done; further appreciation waits on the late-fall strategic update to prove the volume-and-margin combination.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an `uncertain` rationale. The turnaround is genuine but its quality is unproven, and the price already reflects the initial re-rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters showing used-unit growth of ≥5% YoY *with* used GPU holding at or above the mid-$2,100s — proving volume is not purely bought with margin.
- CAF net credit losses stabilizing below ~2.0% of average loans through a full Tier-2 expansion cycle, with the allowance ratio flattening rather than climbing.
- A pullback into the low-to-mid $40s (below the $50 median target), which would restore an attractive entry into an improving fundamental story.
- A late-fall strategic update that quantifies credible unit/margin/CAF targets the market can underwrite.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Used GPU falling below ~$2,000 alongside stalling units — evidence the volume-for-margin trade is failing.
- CAF net credit losses re-accelerating above ~2.5% of average loans as Tier-2 seasons, or a securitization-market disruption widening funding spreads.
- An adverse development in the *Jason Cap* securities litigation or renewed executive turnover disrupting the reset.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the Q2 FY27 print (September 2026) and the promised late-fall 2026 strategic update, which together should reveal whether the volume-and-margin combination is durable.

## Conclusion

CarMax has done the hard, visible part of a turnaround: it took the FY26 kitchen-sink quarter (impairment, restructuring, CEO firing), installed fresh outside leadership, and produced a Q1 print with real green shoots — revenue growth, restored SG&A leverage, improving CAF penetration and losses, and a large EPS beat. The insider-buying cluster, with a new CEO putting his own money to work alongside four directors near the post-earnings low, is the strongest single bullish tell and lifts the management-quality read materially. The balance-sheet distress flag is a misread of non-recourse CAF debt, not a solvency concern.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of price and unproven quality. The stock trades above the consensus mean target with analysts clustered at Hold, on a forward multiple roughly double the franchised-dealer peer median. The volume it is generating is being purchased with lower GPU and higher ad spend, and the CAF Tier-2 credit expansion is a deliberate move down the risk spectrum into a consumer showing late-cycle strain. The turnaround thesis is credible, but the initial re-rating is priced, and further upside requires proof of both durable volume growth and margin/credit stabilization that will not be visible until the fall. The score trajectory (5.1 → 4.3 → 3.9 → 5.1) captures this — a recovering but still balanced setup where the risk/reward is roughly two-sided at current levels. We would become constructive on a pullback into the low-to-mid $40s or on hard evidence that volume and margin can rise together.
