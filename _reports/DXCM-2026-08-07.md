## Executive Summary

Dexcom's second-quarter 2026 report resolved the central operating question that has hung over the stock since FY2024: whether the manufacturing and yield problems that cost 270bp of gross margin in 2024 and another 40bp in 2025 were structural or fixable. They were fixable. Q2 non-GAAP gross margin reached 64.1% against 59.5% GAAP a year earlier (63.4% GAAP this quarter), cost of sales grew 2.2% on 13.1% revenue growth, and non-GAAP operating margin expanded 590bp. Management raised FY2026 guidance on revenue and all three margin metrics. The mechanism is identifiable and not a matter of interpretation: the G7 15 Day sensor replaces a 10-day sensor at unchanged monthly reimbursement, cutting per-patient-month sensor consumption by roughly 35% (1 − 10/15.5). Fourth consecutive earnings beat; consensus FY2026 EPS is up 3.5% over 90 days and FY2027 up 2.7%.

The problem is what management is guiding, not what it delivered. The FY2026 revenue range of $5.18-5.25B implies 2H26 growth of roughly 8.5-11.4% (midpoint ~10.6%) against 1H26's 14.0%. The margin guide is more explicit: 1H26 non-GAAP operating margin was 23.7% ($592.7M on $2,500.3M), and the FY midpoint of 23.75% implies 2H at approximately 23.8%. Management is presenting the step-up as achieved and held, not compounding. Against that, the stock rose ~12% on July 31 and a further ~4.6% into August 3's $87.31 intraday high, while consensus estimates moved only ~3%. The move was almost entirely multiple.

At $83.02 the shares trade at 31.2x FY2026 consensus EPS of $2.66, 26.5x FY2027's $3.13, 18.5x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, and 5.9x guided revenue on an enterprise value of $30.6B. That is a defensible price for a mid-to-high-teens EPS grower with net cash and 32% EBITDA margins, but it is not a mispricing. Our base case is $95 (+14.4%), bull $115 (+38.5%), bear $63 (−24.1%); a 50/25/25 weighting yields roughly +11% expected return with no margin of safety and a structural terminal-value question — the 2028 Medicare competitive-bidding reset — that Dexcom's own filings say will reduce reimbursement and that cannot be sized from public disclosure because Medicare revenue percentage is not disclosed. This is the third consecutive HOLD; the justification is not inertia but that the price advanced 12% toward the base case while the company simultaneously guided to slower revenue growth and a margin plateau.

## Company Overview

Dexcom sells continuous glucose monitoring systems — wearable biosensors measuring interstitial glucose every five minutes — and reports as a single operating segment. Economics run through the disposable sensor. The portfolio is G7 (10-day, ages 2+), G7 15 Day (cleared April 2025, launched late 2025, 15.5-day wear, adults 18+, disclosed MARD of 8.0% with 94.2% of readings within 20% of comparator), legacy G6 (manufacturing discontinuation planned, customer transition targeted by end-2026), Dexcom ONE/ONE+ in Europe (also phasing out by end-2026), and Stelo, the over-the-counter biosensor launched August 2024 for prediabetes and non-insulin-using Type 2.

FY2025 revenue was $4,662.0M, +15.6%, on approximately 600,000-700,000 net customer additions worldwide excluding Stelo. Manufacturing is concentrated in Mesa, Arizona and Penang, Malaysia, with Athenry, Ireland under construction. Headcount was approximately 11,100 at year-end 2025. Channel structure matters for the risk section: Q2 2026 distributor revenue was $1,102.4M of $1,308.4M (84.3%), with U.S. distributors alone at 68.3%. Medicare business has been routed through distributors rather than billed directly since fall 2020.

Coverage is broadly won in the U.S.: Medicaid in approximately 48 states, contracted rates with all eight largest private payors, and the April 2023 Medicare LCD extending coverage to all insulin users plus non-insulin patients with problematic hypoglycemia. The remaining volume vectors are the >25 million U.S. non-insulin-using Type 2 population, international markets (Q2 +19% reported, +16% organic versus U.S. +11%), and the consumer channel.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory:**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1H26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 3,622.3 | 4,033.0 | 4,662.0 | 2,500.3 |
| YoY | +24.5% | +11.3% | +15.6% | +14.0% |
| Gross margin | 63.2% | 60.5% | 60.1% | 63.2% |
| Operating margin | 16.5% | 14.9% | 19.6% | 22.9% |
| Net income ($M) | 541.5 | 576.2 | 836.3 | 448.6 |

Three-year revenue CAGR FY2022-FY2025 is 17.0%. FY2024 is the break in the series — growth halved and gross margin fell 270bp — and that period is the subject of the first securities class action. FY2025 re-accelerated on volume but gross margin slipped again on what the 10-K describes as supply-availability inefficiencies, build configurations that lowered yield, and replacement costs. The 2026 recovery is therefore a return toward FY2022's 64.7% gross margin rather than a new high.

**Q2 2026 specifics** (all filing/issuer facts): revenue $1,308.4M versus $1,157.1M, +13.1% reported and +12% organic after $9.9M of FX and $0.2M of acquired non-CGM revenue. GAAP gross margin 63.4% versus 59.5%; non-GAAP 64.1% versus 60.1%. GAAP operating margin 24.3% versus 18.4%. Adjusted EBITDA $421.3M (32.2% margin) versus $327.6M (28.3%). GAAP diluted EPS $0.64; non-GAAP $0.70 versus $0.48. Operating expense provided part of the leverage — R&D +3.2% on project timing, SG&A +9% against 13% revenue growth, total opex from 41% to 39% of revenue — but gross margin is the larger lever.

**A qualification on the margin quality.** Gross margin improved *despite* sharply higher inventory reserve charges: $45.6M in Q2 2026 versus $8.8M in Q2 2025, and $84.2M in 1H26 versus $37.0M in 1H25. The 10-Q attributes these to quality control data, the planned G6 manufacturing discontinuation, forecasted demand, risk-exposure analysis, and product innovation. The incremental $47.2M is roughly 1.9pp of 1H26 revenue; absent it, 1H gross margin would have been near 65.1%. That cuts both ways. The most likely reading is a controlled platform transition — building G7 finished goods ahead of the G6 shutdown while reserving obsolete stock — which implies the reserves have a visible end. The alternative reading is less comfortable: finished-goods inventory rose 45.1% ($266.5M to $386.7M) in six months while raw materials *fell* ($257.6M to $232.7M) and revenue grew 13%. A 45% finished-goods build against 13% growth is also consistent with demand landing below plan. The disclosure does not distinguish these, and the inclusion of "quality control data" in the reserve rationale alongside an unresolved FDA warning letter means quality-driven scrap cannot be excluded as a contributor. This is the single most important thing to check in the Q3 print.

**Cash flow — strong but not annualizable.** 1H26 operating cash flow was $794.8M versus $486.8M, capex $161.3M, so 1H free cash flow was $633.5M. Doubling that would be wrong. The 10-Q shows a $258.0M increase in payables and accrued liabilities and only a $47.5M receivables drag, versus a $333.5M receivables drag in 1H25 — the company attributes the swing to timing of sales and collections. The quarterly detail makes the point: Q2 2026 OCF was $269.2M, *down 11% year over year*, even as Q2 net income rose 39%. Implied Q1 2026 OCF was approximately $525.6M. Quarterly OCF has ranged from roughly $184M to $526M over four quarters.

A normalized frame: FY2025 OCF/net income was 1.72x, inflated by rebate-accrual growth. If FY2026 GAAP net income lands near $960M-$1,000M (consistent with the guided operating margin, roughly flat net other income and a ~21.8% tax rate) and the conversion ratio compresses toward 1.5x, FY2026 OCF would be $1.44-1.50B. Against capex of $330-350M, FY2026 FCF plausibly falls in the $1.10-1.16B range, versus FY2025 actual of $1,077.2M (23.1% margin). That is a 3.5-3.7% FCF yield on the current market cap.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash and equivalents $1,105.0M plus short-term marketable securities $842.0M equals $1,947.0M; total debt is a single instrument, the $1,250.0M 0.375% convertible due May 2028 with a $162.41 conversion price. Net cash is $697.0M, or $1.85 per share. The $200M revolver is undrawn with $8.7M of letters of credit and matures October 13, 2026 — a renewal to monitor but not a funding constraint given net cash. 1H26 interest expense of $6.1M against $573.6M of operating income is roughly 94x coverage; the cash coupon is $2.4M per half-year. The 2025 Notes ($1.21B) matured in November 2025 and were repaid entirely in cash.

Two definitional notes worth carrying. First, a book-equity Altman Z of 2.63 ("grey zone") is not the operative read; recomputed with market equity and June 2026 balances the classic public-firm Z is approximately 7.3. Second, $1,652.0M of the $2,191.5M "accounts payable and accrued liabilities" line is accrued rebates — contra-revenue obligations to payors, not supplier credit — with trade payables only $423.8M. Working-capital distress ratios therefore read more adversely than the creditor position warrants.

**Contingent dilution, stated correctly.** The 2028 Notes convert into 7,696,375 shares, 2.04% of shares outstanding and 2.00% post-issuance ownership dilution. The price-based conversion trigger requires the stock at or above $211.13 for 20 of 30 trading days; the company may settle in cash, stock or a combination at its discretion, and 2028 capped calls (cap $212.62) offset dilution to that cap. At $83.02 — 49% below the conversion price — this is not part of current capitalization in any meaningful sense, though GAAP diluted shares (390.1M in Q2) already include the if-converted shares while the non-GAAP count (382.4M) strips them out.

## Growth Analysis

The forward picture separates cleanly into a decelerating revenue line and a faster-growing earnings line.

**Revenue.** FY2026 guidance of $5.18-5.25B represents 11-13% growth. The 1H26 actual was +14.0%, so the guide requires 2H26 growth of roughly 8.5% (low end) to 11.4% (high end). Management has beaten four consecutive quarters, so the guide is more likely conservative than not; even so, the direction of travel is decelerating from FY2025's 15.6% and FY2023's 24.5%. Geographic mix is the healthier component — international at 28.7% of Q2 revenue versus 27.3%, growing 19% reported against U.S. +11%.

**Earnings.** Consensus FY2026 EPS of $2.66 is up 2.9% in 30 days and 3.5% in 90; FY2027's $3.13 is up 1.7% and 2.7%. Those are positive but ordinary revisions — nowhere near the >30% velocity that flags a genuine estimate inflection. The FY2027 figure implies 17.7% EPS growth off FY2026, most of it revenue-driven since the margin step is guided as complete. Note the one dissonant line in the revision table: next-quarter consensus fell 2.1% in 30 days, consistent with the guided 2H deceleration.

**The G7 15 Day arithmetic and its limit.** Extended wear reduces sensor consumption per patient-month by roughly 35% at unchanged monthly reimbursement — that is the ~400bp gross-margin lever. Critically, it is a one-time step per converted patient, not a rate of improvement. A secondary source reports management targeting roughly 50% U.S. conversion by year-end 2026, which would imply a similar increment available in 2027; that figure is unconfirmed in any filing and should not be underwritten. What *is* confirmed is that the FY2026 margin guide implies no further sequential expansion in 2H26.

**The genuine new growth vector is CONNECT.** The announced randomized controlled trial showed clinically meaningful and statistically significant glucose-control improvement from Dexcom CGM in Type 2 patients *not using insulin*. That is the evidence base required for payor coverage expansion into the >25 million-person U.S. population the 10-K identifies as its primary target. Coverage decisions follow evidence with a lag measured in quarters to years, so this is a 2027-2029 revenue story rather than a 2026 one, but it is the most important positive item in the file.

**Stelo is not yet visible.** On the only channel line where it would plausibly appear, U.S. Direct revenue was $39.9M in Q2 2026 versus $41.0M a year earlier — a 2.7% decline — with 1H26 at $75.3M versus $70.9M. Dexcom's net-add disclosure explicitly excludes Stelo customers, and Stelo revenue is not broken out. U.S. Direct includes non-Stelo sales, so this does not prove Stelo is flat; it does establish that the FY2026 growth story rests on prescription G7/G7 15 Day plus international, not the consumer product.

**A gap that matters.** Dexcom held a 2026 Investor Day and introduced a new long-term financial outlook through 2030. The content of that outlook is not in our possession. For a company whose valuation debate is fundamentally about the 2028-2030 terminal margin under CMS bidding, that is the largest single unresolved input.

## Competitive Landscape

The CGM category is a duopoly at the therapeutic end with a widening consumer front.

**What is improving.** Dexcom's disclosed MARD of 8.0% on G7 15 Day is a specific, quantified accuracy claim and the company describes the product as the most accurate CGM cleared by the FDA. The 2024 Abbott settlement produced a worldwide, royalty-free, fully paid-up cross-license and a covenant not to sue until December 20, 2034 — eight more years without patent litigation against the primary rival, with the P&L benefit already visible in $87.2M of lower FY2025 legal expense. Sensor-wire manufacturing is vertical. Reimbursement infrastructure (48 Medicaid states, contracted rates with all eight largest private payors) is a real and hard-to-replicate asset. CONNECT extends the clinical evidence base into the largest untapped population.

**What is deteriorating.** The 10-K itself discloses that Abbott has FDA clearance to integrate Libre sensors into automated insulin delivery systems and is pursuing third-party pump integrations. AID integration was the mechanism that made Dexcom sensors sticky inside the pump ecosystem; if pump makers can dual-source, that stickiness weakens. Note the literal relationship — pump companies are integration counterparties, not Dexcom customers in a reseller sense, so the exposure is to sensor pull-through rather than to a reseller contract. Medtronic spun its diabetes business into MiniMed, converting a division inside a conglomerate into a focused competitor with its own equity currency and the disposable Simplera sensor. The 10-K names emerging Chinese low-cost entrants. Most structurally, FDA CDRH guidance issued January 6, 2026 broadens the "general wellness device" category to include wearables reporting blood glucose, which lowers the regulatory barrier for consumer-electronics entrants — a direct threat to Stelo's core differentiation as the first *cleared* OTC glucose biosensor. Dexcom's stated response is to pursue development partnerships to be the sensor layer inside third-party consumer experiences.

**GLP-1s** are framed in the 10-K as companion rather than substitute. The plausible mechanism is positive — they pull more Type 2 patients into active metabolic management, and CONNECT supplies the outcome evidence — but better pharmacological control reducing perceived monitoring need is a coherent bear reading that the filings do not resolve.

**Score rationale: 6.0, unchanged.** Product trajectory is improving (accuracy, extended wear, clinical evidence); moat trajectory is eroding at both ends (AID dual-sourcing, wellness pathway). Those roughly offset, which is why the earlier markdown from 8.0 stands rather than reversing.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CMS competitive bidding — the terminal-value question (highest weight).** In late 2025 CMS extended DMEPOS competitive bidding to Dexcom's CGMs and receivers, with contracting beginning 2027 and payment changes effective January 1, 2028. Ten contracts are anticipated. Pricing will be set at the **75th percentile of winning bids** rather than the maximum winning bid — a deliberate downward shift. CMS reclassified CGMs as items requiring frequent and substantial servicing and will phase in monthly rental payments bundling the receiver into the supplies payment. Contracts recompete at least every three years, with national rollout or non-CBA price matching required. Whether the structure is a single nationwide Remote Item Delivery area or regional areas is undetermined. The company's own conclusion is verbatim: it expects Medicare reimbursement for its CGM systems to decrease beginning in 2028. The bid also includes a country-of-origin question, non-trivial given Penang and Athenry manufacturing.

Two things about sizing. Dexcom does **not** disclose Medicare as a percentage of revenue, and since 2020 has sold to Medicare through distributors, so Medicare revenue is embedded in the 84% distributor channel. The 2028 impact cannot be quantified from public filings, and any number offered would be invented. Uncertainty runs both directions — winning a large share of ten national contracts at lower unit price could raise Medicare volume. There is also a partial structural hedge: under a fixed monthly payment, a 15.5-day sensor accrues its consumption saving to Dexcom's gross margin. The equally plausible alternative is that CMS sets the monthly rate knowing extended-wear economics and captures the saving for the program. Both are hypotheses; the bid math is not public.

**2. Unresolved FDA warning letter, now echoed in civil court.** A March 2025 warning letter followed inspections of San Diego (Oct-Nov 2024) and Mesa (June 2024), citing deficiencies in Dexcom's Form 483 response letters and observed non-conformities in manufacturing processes and the quality management system. It does not restrict production, marketing, distribution, or 510(k) clearances, and does not require recall. It was unresolved as of the February 2026 10-K, and the Q2 10-Q added no update — absence of an update is not evidence of resolution. The QMSR replaced the QSR effective February 2, 2026, changing the compliance standard mid-remediation.

**3. Litigation load, unaccrued.** Twelve matters: two securities class actions with distinct class periods (the first covering April 2023-July 2024 revenue statements, procedurally favorable so far with multiple dismissal-stage rulings granting leave to amend; the second, *Prime* in S.D.N.Y., covering July 2024-September 2025 statements about G7 accuracy, reliability and manufacturing, with opposition to Dexcom's MTD due July 31, 2026); four derivative actions, all stayed; and six consumer putative class actions, five consolidated May 15, 2026 in S.D. Cal., alleging G6/G7 devices were "adulterated or misbranded under federal law." A separate risk factor concedes that Class II classification "is likely to weaken our ability to rely on federal preemption of state law claims" — the defense that would otherwise blunt those claims. Dexcom states it cannot reasonably estimate outcomes and discloses no accrual. Nothing here supports a specific damages estimate.

**4. Rebate estimate sensitivity.** Accrued rebates were $1,652.0M at June 30, up from $1,487.6M at year-end — 35% of FY2025 revenue. The 10-K discloses that a 1% change in the proportion of products subject to rebate equates to roughly ±$50.1M of revenue. Both the FY2025 and Q2 disclosures cite rebate eligibility as a partial offset to revenue growth. This is an estimate-heavy revenue line with material sensitivity to payor-mix drift.

**5. Concentration and single points of failure.** Distributor channel 84.3% of Q2 revenue, U.S. distributor alone 68.3%; the 10-K states certain distribution agreements exceeded 10% of total revenue without quantifying. Single-source suppliers include the transmitter ASIC, applicator seals, and certain sensor-membrane polymers. Geographic mix is diversifying modestly, but customer concentration is a separate measure and has not been shown to improve.

**6. Medicaid policy.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) carries CBO estimates of roughly $1 trillion in reduced Medicaid spending and 11.8 million additional uninsured by 2034, with work requirements, cost sharing and six-month redeterminations. Dexcom has Medicaid coverage in ~48 states, so exposure is direct.

**7. Governance.** Approximately ten Item 5.02 filings between March 2025 and March 2026, an Item 4.01 change of certifying accountant on March 25, 2025 (the same month as the warning letter; the outgoing/incoming auditors and the reason are unverified — we draw no inference), three directors whose May 2026 post-grant holdings equal their grants (i.e., recent appointments), and the Chief Legal Officer's title expanded to include Compliance between May and June 2026. The most coherent reading is a board-supervised leadership and control-environment refresh following FY2024's miss; management concluded disclosure controls were effective at June 30, 2026 with no changes in internal control over financial reporting.

**8. Sector read-across.** Insulet cut 2026 revenue guidance in early August, with Tandem and Dexcom declining in sympathy. Insulet is an insulin-delivery integration partner; softer pump adoption could reduce pump-attached sensor demand. Dexcom's own raised guidance predates the Insulet cut and no Dexcom disclosure addresses it, so the transmission mechanism is a hypothesis.

**Score rationale: 4.5.** The cluster of an unsized 2028 price reset, an unresolved quality citation, twelve unaccrued legal matters, and an unexplained finished-goods build constitutes specific, identifiable downside catalysts that the current 26.5x forward multiple does not obviously discount.

### Options Market Signal

ATM vol collapsed sharply after the July 30 print (the pre-earnings snapshot showed 180.7% falling to 107.6% on the comparable measure), which is normal event decay. The term structure is in contango: 33.7% at 13 days, 31.6% at 41 days, and 40.9% at 132 days, with the December tenor pricing a ±21% move. That shape says the market sees little near-term catalyst risk but meaningful event risk into year-end — consistent with the litigation calendar and the timing of CMS bid-structure decisions.

Put/call volume ratio of 1.72 against open-interest ratio of 0.49 means recent flow is put-tilted while the standing book remains call-heavy; the 13-day tenor shows a 5.0% put skew while 41-day and 132-day skews are negative. Short interest is 5.7% of float at 3.9 days to cover — uncrowded, with no squeeze mechanics either way.

The practical takeaway for a holder: near-dated protection is cheap relative to realized volatility and to the longer-dated curve, which makes hedging the Q3 print inexpensive. It does not contradict the fundamental view and is not independent evidence for it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to $115, +38.5%)

The margin recovery is real, mechanically explained, and roughly half-complete. G7 15 Day cuts sensor consumption per patient-month by ~35% at unchanged reimbursement; if U.S. conversion runs from its current level toward saturation through 2027, a comparable increment to the 400bp already realized remains available and the FY2026 guide's implied plateau proves conservative — as four consecutive beats suggest guidance tends to be. CONNECT provides the randomized evidence needed to unlock payor coverage in the >25 million-person U.S. non-insulin Type 2 population, the single largest volume vector Dexcom has ever addressed. The 2028 CMS monthly-rental structure, counterintuitively, favors an extended-wear supplier: at a fixed monthly payment, longer wear accrues to gross margin. International is compounding at 16-19% and rising as a share of mix. The balance sheet is a weapon — $697M net cash, one 0.375% bond, 94x interest coverage, and a formal policy committing at least 50% of operating cash flow net of capex to repurchases, with $600M deployed in Q2 at an average of $69.88. Net income has more than tripled since 2021 while the stock is down 28% over five years; a return to even a 33x forward multiple on an upgraded FY2027 EPS gets to $115.

### Bear Case (to $63, −24.1%)

Management's own guidance is the bear case's primary exhibit. Revenue is guided to decelerate to 8.5-11.4% in 2H26 from 14.0% in 1H, and the margin guide implies 2H operating margin of ~23.8% against 1H's 23.7% — the step-up is done. Finished-goods inventory rose 45.1% in six months against 13% revenue growth while raw materials fell, and inventory reserves more than doubled to $84.2M in 1H with "quality control data" cited as a driver alongside an unresolved FDA warning letter; a demand shortfall reading of that data cannot be excluded. Dexcom's own filing says Medicare reimbursement will decrease beginning in 2028, pricing set at the 75th percentile of winning bids, recompeted every three years, with Medicare exposure undisclosed and therefore unhedgeable by an outside investor. Abbott now has AID clearance, dissolving the integration moat; MiniMed is a focused standalone competitor; the January 2026 wellness guidance opens the consumer channel to companies with vastly larger distribution. Stelo is invisible in the revenue lines that would show it. Twelve litigation matters carry no accrual, and Class II classification weakens the preemption defense against the consumer claims. The stock traded at $54.11 nine months ago; a return to 22x a trimmed FY2027 estimate is not a tail scenario.

## Conclusion

Dexcom delivered the quarter the bulls needed. The gross-margin recovery is real, its mechanism is arithmetic rather than narrative, and the balance sheet — net cash, one 0.375% bond, 94x interest coverage, a formalized policy directing at least half of free cash flow to repurchases, and $600M actually deployed in Q2 at an average price 16% below today's — is among the cleanest in medical devices. Management bought stock aggressively at prices that have since proven good, which is the strongest capital-allocation evidence in the file.

The reasons to wait are equally concrete and mostly come from the company's own guidance. Revenue is guided to slow to roughly 10.6% in the second half from 14% in the first, and the margin guide explicitly holds 2H operating margin flat against 1H. The finished-goods build of 45% against 13% revenue growth, with more than doubled inventory reserves citing quality-control data, is an unresolved ambiguity in the middle of the very metric that drove the re-rating. The 2028 CMS reset is disclosed by Dexcom as a reimbursement *decrease*, priced at the 75th percentile of winning bids, and cannot be sized by anyone outside the company because Medicare revenue is not disclosed. Twelve litigation matters carry no accrual, and the preemption defense is conceded as weak. Governance has churned heavily, incentive metrics contain no quality or compliance measure while the company operates under an unresolved warning letter, and there has been no open-market insider buying — though the sales that did occur were at $59.91-$76.32, below today's price, so they say nothing about insiders viewing the current level as excessive.

At $83.02 the shares offer roughly 14% to a defensible base case against 24% downside, with no margin of safety and a structural pricing question in 2028 that neither the company nor the market can currently quantify. That is a fair price for a good business, which is what HOLD means. The prior BUY at a substantially lower price was the right call on the same fundamentals; the arithmetic that supported it returns near $72-75, or earlier if the October print shows the margin step-up compounding rather than plateauing.
