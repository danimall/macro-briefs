**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ARMOUR Residential REIT is an externally managed agency mortgage REIT that owns roughly $21.5 billion of Agency MBS, funds it with short-term repo borrowing at ~8x leverage, and earns the spread between asset yields (~4.9%) and repo costs (~3.8%). The economically meaningful story today is a cyclical tailwind: as the Fed eased to a 3.50–3.75% target range, ARR's funding cost fell to 3.84% from 4.51% a year earlier while asset yields held near 4.9%, widening net interest spread to 1.04% from 0.49% and driving net interest income up 95% YoY to $70.7M in Q1 2026. Estimated REIT taxable income — the figure that governs the dividend — rose to $73.6M from $52.8M, and the $0.24/month distribution ($2.88 annualized, ~17% forward yield) is currently covered on a per-share basis.

The reasons the market prices ARR at roughly book value (~0.95–1.0x, stock at $16.80 against an estimated ~$17.4 book) are governance, not fundamentals. ARR routes ~43% of its repo through BUCKLER Securities, a broker-dealer controlled by external manager ACM in which ARR holds only 10.8%; ARR committed an on-demand $275M subordinated loan to BUCKLER secured by its own securities, yet collected $0 of promised financing-profit-sharing in both Q1 2026 and Q1 2025. Within a 90-day window the manager terminated its ~$6.6M/year fee waiver (Dec 2025) and then extended its own contract to 2033 with a 4x-trailing-fee termination penalty (March 2026). Cumulative Total Economic Return has been negative in four of the last five years, and TSR lags the peer index by roughly 50 points. Shares roughly doubled in 15 months through ATM and public issuance near or below book.

This produces a balanced setup. The cyclical carry and covered distribution offset the governance defects and the inherent rate/spread gearing, and the bearish governance case is already embedded in the ~1.0x book multiple and ~17% yield. We rate ARR a HOLD on an income basis. The covering analyst base (2 buy / 4 hold / 0 sell; price targets $18.00–$19.00, all above the current price) sees modest upside, and there is no fundamental deterioration — no guidance cut, no covenant breach, no dividend cut, with the most recent quarter beating on distributable EPS — that would justify an outright avoid. The reasonable expectation is total return delivered primarily through the yield near book value, with limited downside absent a rate/spread shock.

## Company Overview

ARMOUR is a Maryland-incorporated (2008) agency mREIT based in Vero Beach, FL. The model is commoditized and capital-intensive: buy Agency MBS guaranteed by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and Ginnie Mae; fund with short-term repurchase agreements; hedge rate risk with interest-rate swaps, swaptions, futures, and TBAs. The portfolio is a single reportable segment of ~$21.5B in assets, 95%+ Agency MBS plus some Treasuries, concentrated in higher-coupon production (5.0%–6.5% coupons ~64% of MBS).

ARR has no employees. It is externally managed by ARMOUR Capital Management LP (ACM); CEO Scott Ulm, CFO Gordon Harper, and co-CIOs Sergey Losyev and Desmond Macauley are all ACM employees. Repo financing is heavily intermediated by BUCKLER Securities LLC, a FINRA broker-dealer controlled by ACM, which provided 43.4% of repo borrowings at Q1 2026 (47.0% at YE2025). ARR has no durable moat — it competes against far larger peers (Annaly, AGNC) for the same MBS and the same repo. Its stated edge is portfolio selection, hedging, and BUCKLER-facilitated financing access.

## Financial Analysis

**Look past GAAP entirely.** ARR designates its full MBS book as trading securities and uses no hedge accounting, so all MBS and derivative fair-value moves run through earnings. FY2025 GAAP net income was +$322.7M against –$14.4M in FY2024; Q1 2026 swung to a –$54.9M loss (–$0.49/share) as a late-quarter rate spike produced a $192M unrealized MBS loss only partly offset by $83M of derivative gains. These are mark-to-market artifacts, not operating results.

**The operating story is net interest income, and it is improving.** The most recent quarter beat on the economically relevant distributable metric: Q1 2026 actual $0.76 against a $0.75 estimate.

**Balance sheet.** Stockholders' equity was $2.34B at Q1 2026, up from $1.36B at YE2024, but almost entirely from issuance rather than retained earnings. Debt/equity is 7.90x (implied leverage 8.21x including TBAs), normal for an agency mREIT within its 6–10x target. The repo book is $18.5B at a 3.80% weighted rate with a 21-day average maturity — extreme rollover dependence by design. Liquidity is $1.12B; average haircut a thin 2.61%. The hedge book is $12.9B notional pay-fixed swaps (2.52%, 50-month avg term) plus $600M TBAs.

**Dilution is the central financial concern.** Common shares went from 62.4M (YE2024) to 111.9M (YE2025) to 123.6M (Q1 2026) — nearly doubling in ~15 months — via ATM and a public offering, much of it near or below book (the August 2025 offering at $16.14). Issuing below book at an mREIT erodes existing holders' book value. Cumulative distributions of $2.76B sit against a $573M accumulated GAAP deficit, structurally normal for mREITs (which distribute taxable income excluding unrealized hedge losses) but a reminder that the business has not compounded GAAP book. Offsetting this, ARR carries a $257M indefinite NOL and $1.27B of capital-loss carryforwards that shelter future gains and support tax-advantaged return-of-capital distributions.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** Over four quarters: 1 beat, 1 in-line, 2 misses (Q4 2025 –9.3%, Q2 2025 –7.5% on distributable EPS), with the most recent quarter a slight beat. Visibility is modest; quarterly distributable EPS swings with funding cost and prepayment dynamics rather than holding a clean trend.

## Growth Analysis

"Growth" here means earnings power per share, not balance-sheet expansion. The +95% NII growth is substantially a bigger book, not better per-share economics — equity nearly doubled to fund it. The genuine improvement is the spread normalization: lower front-end rates widen the gap between MBS yields and repo cost, and that mechanism lifts the entire agency mREIT cohort, not ARR specifically. Consensus EPS estimates have drifted modestly lower (current year $2.93 vs. $3.05 ninety days ago, –3.9%; next year $3.01 vs. $3.14, –4.1%), with 30-day revisions roughly flat. There is no estimate-revision tailwind to lean on; the durable forward driver is whether the spread holds as the curve normalizes, which is a rate call rather than a company-specific growth thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

ARR trades at roughly 0.95–1.0x book. Trailing P/E of ~7.7x is largely meaningless given GAAP volatility; the relevant anchors are price-to-book and dividend coverage. Within the mortgage REIT peer set, dispersion is wide and the comparison is imperfect — BXMT at 29.9x and ABR at 12.9x are commercial mREITs with different risk profiles. On yield, peer median is ~11.0%; ARR's ~17% sits at the top of the range alongside PMT (16.2%), reflecting both a real distribution and elevated cut-risk pricing.

The valuation is neither cheap nor stretched. A ~1.0x book multiple already reflects the governance discount and the market's skepticism about per-share book durability — the bearish case is in the price. There is no specific catalyst arguing the market is materially wrong: estimates are flat-to-down, dilution is ongoing, and the manager has entrenched itself. There is also no structural impairment that would justify a deep discount to book — the dividend is covered, leverage is by-design, and the spread is widening. The covered ~17% yield provides absolute return support near book. We score valuation modestly above neutral: the asymmetry is limited in both directions, with return most likely delivered through the distribution rather than multiple expansion. The all-analyst-target range implies ~7–13% price upside, but on a name at premium yield those targets are best read as confirmation that the distribution is seen as sustainable, not as an aggressive re-rating thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

The agency mREIT sub-sector is rate- and spread-driven, and currently benefits from falling repo costs and a normalizing curve — a sector tailwind that lifts ARR, AGNC, and NLY alike. ARR's specific competitive position is weak relative to peers: it is mid-cap (~$2.1B) against far larger, more liquid, lower-cost competitors, and its external-management-plus-affiliated-broker-dealer structure is comparatively shareholder-unfriendly. The ~50-point TSR gap versus the peer index is consistent with a structurally disadvantaged cost and governance configuration. The differentiators ARR claims (BUCKLER financing access, hedging skill) are, on the evidence, net negatives or unproven — BUCKLER has delivered $0 of financing-profit-sharing to ARR shareholders for two consecutive comparable quarters. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-eroding rather than improving, even as the cyclical environment helps the absolute numbers.

## Risk Assessment

**Governance / related-party concentration.** The standout idiosyncratic risk. The $275M subordinated loan to BUCKLER (effective Feb 2026 through 2029) is collateralized by ARR's own securities and usable by BUCKLER as regulatory capital with re-pledge rights. ARR shareholders bear the concentration and conflict while collecting none of the promised financing economics. The fee-waiver termination followed by the contract extension to 2033 entrenches ACM and limits any shareholder remedy. These defects are real and legitimately cap the multiple, which is why a constructive BUY would be wrong; but they are visibly priced into ~1.0x book and have not impaired the covered distribution.

**Rate / spread risk (core mREIT exposure).** Company sensitivity tables (3/31/2026): +100bp parallel shock cuts equity ~9.9%; +25bp MBS spread widening cuts equity ~10.6%. The Q1 2026 loss demonstrated this live. Book value is highly geared.

**Funding / rollover risk.** A 21-day average repo maturity means ARR continuously refinances its ~$18.5B repo book; a repo disruption, margin calls on collateral declines, or counterparty exit would force sales at depressed prices. The Fed's December 2025 conclusion of QT adds front-end funding uncertainty.

**Macro / GSE reform.** Management flags persistent rate volatility tied to the Feb 2026 Middle East conflict and 2025 tariff regime; a ceasefire was announced April 8, 2026, but management expects volatility to persist. Renewed discussion of ending Fannie/Freddie conservatorship is a tail risk to the Agency MBS market that constitutes ARR's entire asset base.

**Dividend sustainability.** This is the risk the high yield prices, and the data argues coverage holds for now: per-share taxable income roughly tracks the $0.72/quarter payout, the dividend is held and guided through July 2026, and the spread is widening. A cut becomes likely only on a sustained spread compression or a forced de-leveraging — not the current trajectory. The ~17% yield is partly a structural feature of a leveraged, near-book mREIT and partly a risk premium, not self-evidently a cut signal.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 19.5% versus 24.2% one-year historical vol — options are cheap (spread –4.7%, 7th percentile within the chain), and IV has fallen 6.0 points since late May. The term structure is in contango (19.5% near vs. 23.9% long), a normal/complacent shape. Put/call skew is modestly elevated (5.5%) and the 1-month put/call volume ratio is 2.16, indicating some downside hedging, but OI ratios remain call-tilted (0.29) and short interest is effectively zero (0.0% of float). The market is not positioned for a near-term collapse; it is pricing modest hedging against rate-driven book volatility, consistent with the income-hold framing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$18.5–19.0).** The Fed holds or eases further, the curve normalizes, repo cost falls further while MBS spreads stay contained, net interest spread holds above 1.0%, taxable income continues to comfortably cover the dividend, and the stock re-rates modestly above book toward the $18–19 analyst targets while collecting a ~17% yield. Tax-loss carryforwards support continued tax-advantaged distributions.

**Bear case (~$14, roughly –17%).** A rate/spread shock (the company's own +25bp spread sensitivity is –10.6% to equity) compresses book value, the dividend is trimmed to preserve capital, and continued below-book issuance dilutes per-share value, pushing the stock toward ~0.80x book.

**Base case (muddle-through near book, ~$16–17.5).** Spread stays positive but volatile, the dividend is held and covered, dilution continues at the margin, governance keeps the multiple capped at ~1.0x book, and total return is delivered primarily through the distribution. This is the most probable outcome and the reason the name is an income hold rather than either a buy or an avoid — the positive cyclical carry and covered yield roughly cancel the governance drag and rate/spread risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and monthly dividend declarations; near-term Fed policy path is the dominant variable. Reassess each quarter on spread and taxable-income trends.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back toward ~0.80x book (~$14) with the dividend still covered and net interest spread holding above 1.0% — restoring genuine asymmetry to the yield.
- A credible governance improvement: meaningful unwind or de-risking of the BUCKLER arrangement, reinstatement of a fee concession, or a manager realignment that narrows the cost/TSR gap.
- Sustained positive estimate revisions (+5%+ on forward distributable EPS) confirming durable per-share earnings power rather than balance-sheet-driven growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut, or taxable income falling below the payout for two consecutive quarters.
- Net interest spread compressing back below ~0.6% on a rate/curve reversal.
- A material adverse development in the BUCKLER relationship (e.g., funded subordinated loan with capital strain) or evidence the financing structure is impairing liquidity.
- Continued aggressive issuance materially below book that visibly erodes per-share book value.

## Conclusion

ARR is a balanced setup. The cyclical engine is working, and there is no fundamental deterioration to bet against. The offsetting reality is structural: an entrenched external manager locked in through 2033, a conflicted affiliated broker-dealer consuming the financing economics, chronic TSR underperformance, and serial below-book dilution — defects that are real but already embedded in the ~1.0x book multiple and ~17% yield. With the analyst base at 2 buy / 4 hold / 0 sell and all targets above the current price, and with a bear case of only ~–17% against a covered double-digit carry, the profile is an income-oriented HOLD: own it for the distribution near book value, with a defined buy-the-dip level near $14 and clear downgrade triggers around dividend coverage and spread compression. Investors who do not need the yield and are unwilling to accept the rate/spread gearing have little reason to add here; those holding for income are adequately compensated for now.
