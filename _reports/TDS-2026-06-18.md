**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Telephone & Data Systems is no longer the integrated wireless-and-wireline telecom that standardized data feeds still describe. On August 1, 2025 its 81.9%-owned subsidiary (formerly UScellular, renamed Array Digital Infrastructure, NYSE: AD) sold its wireless operations and select spectrum to T-Mobile for ~$4.29B ($2.63B cash + $1.67B debt assumed). What remains is a holding company with three value drivers: (1) TDS Telecom, a 100%-owned fiber/broadband operator (~1.06M connections, ~$1.04B revenue) in a heavy-capex growth phase; (2) an 81.9% stake in Array, which now owns 4,452 towers, minority interests in Verizon/AT&T-managed wireless partnerships generating ~$176M/year of equity-method income, and ~$1.58B book value of retained spectrum being sold off in tranches; and (3) a substantial cash and capital-return position. The headline financials (revenue down from $4.96B in 2024 to $1.23B in 2025, a GAAP operating loss, and a distress-level Altman Z of 1.32) are mechanical artifacts of moving the wireless business into discontinued operations. The actual balance sheet is strong: $1.37B cash at 3/31/2026, long-term debt cut from $4.05B to ~$673M, and adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations of $529M (+18% YoY).

The investment question is a corporate-action and sum-of-the-parts question, not an operating one. The near-term catalysts are well-defined: the pending ~$1.0B Verizon spectrum sale (expected Q2/Q3 2026, likely funding another Array special dividend), the May 7, 2026 proposal for TDS to absorb Array's ~18% public float in an all-stock deal at 0.86 TDS shares per Array share, a $524M buyback authorization, and continued spectrum monetization against a $4.2B market cap. Q1 2026 printed $1.09 diluted EPS, flattered by a $150.9M book gain on the AT&T spectrum sale that closed January 13, 2026.

This is a HOLD rather than a BUY because the structural-simplification thesis has been substantially repriced. The stock has roughly doubled relative to its peer group (proxy TSR 260.75 vs. peer 123.65), Raymond James downgraded to Market Perform on May 11, 2026 citing "light" Q1 operating results and the proposed structure collapse, and the most easily-monetized assets (AT&T spectrum, T-Mobile wireless) are already sold. The remaining catalysts are real but incremental, and they sit against a controlled-company governance structure (Carlson family voting trust) that introduces conflict-of-interest risk on the Array buy-in. The asymmetry that justified owning this name through the breakup has narrowed.

## Company Overview

TDS is a Chicago-based diversified telecom holding company controlled by the Carlson family through a dual-class structure (Series A super-voting shares held in the TDS Voting Trust). As of mid-2026 it operates two reportable segments plus a small printing subsidiary:

**TDS Telecom (100%-owned).** A fiber, broadband, video, voice, and small MVNO wireless provider serving ~1.06M connections across 30 states, concentrated in small-to-mid-sized urban, suburban, and rural markets. The strategy is fiber expansion: 1.9M service addresses (+5% YoY), 50% of incumbent addresses now served with fiber, 79% of footprint offered at 1Gig+. This is the long-term operating core. It is currently a cash user, with capex guidance of $550-600M for 2026 (vs. $436M in 2025) and a 2026 target of 200,000-250,000 new fiber addresses. Legacy declines persist (Q1 2026: voice -16%, video -10%, copper broadband -25% YoY), partially offset by fiber connection growth.

**Array Digital Infrastructure (81.9%-owned, NYSE: AD).** Post-divestiture, Array is (a) a tower company with 4,452 owned towers in 19 states; (b) holder of noncontrolling interests in Verizon/AT&T-managed wireless partnerships generating significant equity-method income; and (c) holder of ~$1.58B book value of retained spectrum (primarily C-Band, plus high-band 28/37/39 GHz) being sold to Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile.

For any reader using a standard screen, the apparent revenue collapse and distress score reflect the deconsolidation of wireless into discontinued operations, not a deteriorating business. The remaining company is asset-rich and cash-rich.

## Financial Analysis

**Continuing operations.** 2025 revenue was $1.23B with a GAAP operating loss of $97.4M, driven by D&A of $352M, spectrum impairment, and strategic-review costs. Net income from continuing operations was nonetheless $151M, supported by $176M of equity-method partnership earnings, a $62M tax benefit, and $69M of imputed spectrum lease income. Adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations grew to $529M (+18% YoY); the underlying cash earnings power is healthy despite the headline operating loss.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue was $309M (+7% YoY); net income attributable to common shareholders was $127M ($1.09 diluted EPS), boosted by a $150.9M book gain ($114.7M net) on the AT&T spectrum sale. The earnings-surprise record is noisy and dominated by these one-time gains: Q1 2026 actual $1.09 vs. $-0.44 estimate (+347.5%), Q4 2025 $0.33 vs. $0.08 (+298.6%). These beats reflect transaction timing, not recurring operating outperformance, and should not be extrapolated into forward confidence.

**Balance sheet.** Cash rose to $1.37B (from $766M at YE2025) after collecting $1.02B from the AT&T sale. Long-term debt was cut from $4.05B (2024) to ~$823M (YE2025) to ~$673M (Q1 2026); TDS repaid $781M of term loans, a $300M secured loan, and a $150M EDC facility across 2025-26. Stockholders' equity is ~$4.9B. Credit ratings (S&P BBB-, Moody's Ba1, Fitch BB+, all stable) were reissued in Aug/Sep 2025 and sit comfortably within the 3.5x leverage / 3.0x interest-coverage covenants.

**Cash flow.** Reported FCF is negative (-$52M in 2025, -$81M Q1 2026), entirely a function of heavy fiber capex. This is investment funded from cash, operating cash flow, and Array dividends. The negative FCF is discretionary growth spend that could be moderated, not a structural shortfall.

**Capital returns.** Array paid special dividends of $23.00/share (Aug 2025) and $10.25/share (Feb 2026; TDS pro-rata share $725.6M). A further special dividend is expected on the Verizon spectrum close. TDS repurchased $108M of stock in 2025 and the board authorized an additional $500M buyback in November 2025 (~$524M now available). The TDS common dividend was cut from $0.185/qtr (2023) to $0.04/qtr as a post-divestiture reset; current yield is ~0.4% and future dividend policy is explicitly described as uncertain. This is not an income vehicle.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story bifurcates. The operating core (TDS Telecom) is a volume-driven fiber penetration story: fiber connections growing double-digits while legacy copper/video/voice declines drag the blend, and residential revenue per connection up only ~1%. Growth is footprint expansion funded by heavy capex and E-ACAM subsidies (through 2038), supplemented by tuck-in M&A; the $25.4M Granite State Communications acquisition (NH fiber, closing Q3 2026) is the template. This is a multi-year, capital-intensive compounding effort with no near-term earnings inflection.

The corporate-action layer is where 12-month value creation concentrates: the Verizon spectrum sale and likely associated special dividend, continued monetization of the remaining spectrum book, the Array minority buy-in (which simplifies the structure and could narrow the conglomerate discount), and ongoing buybacks. Estimate revisions are mixed and low-signal here: the current-year EPS revision of +284.5% over 90 days is driven by the spectrum-gain accounting rather than a fundamental demand inflection, while the next-year estimate was cut -41.2%, consistent with the gains being non-recurring. I do not treat the headline current-year revision as a signal that warrants aggressive scoring; it is a transaction artifact.

Weighing the durable operating growth (modest, capex-constrained) against the one-time corporate-action value (real but largely realized), growth prospects are middling. The score reflects a business whose recurring growth engine is years from contributing to per-share earnings while its near-term upside is event-dependent and partially priced.

## Valuation Assessment

Reported P/E of ~23.6x on $0.39 trailing EPS is meaningless given the transactional distortions. The proper frame is sum-of-the-parts:

- **Cash:** $1.37B, with ~$1.0B more incoming from Verizon.
- **Remaining spectrum:** ~$1.58B book value, with the caveat that high-band marks have been impaired ($47.7M in Q3 2025, $136.2M in 2024) on suppressed pricing and weak demand, so realizable value is below book for the high-band tranche.
- **Array tower portfolio + partnership interests:** the partnerships alone generate substantial cash distributions on top of their equity-method income, a meaningful and under-followed value source.
- **TDS Telecom fiber business:** valued on EBITDA and footprint, currently a cash user.

Against the $4.2B market cap, the gross asset value materially exceeds the equity value, which is the conglomerate-discount argument. The problem is that the discount is structural and persistent at controlled companies, and the most liquid assets (the cash and the priced spectrum sales) are the ones least subject to mispricing. The peer table is of limited use given how heterogeneous Communication Services is — WMG 33.7x, CNK 26.4x, IRDM 50.4x, TGNA 16.3x, UNIT 3.1x — none is a clean comp for a telecom holdco mid-restructuring.

The valuation score sits at the midpoint. There is a genuine asset-value cushion supporting the downside, but the catalyst that would close the discount (the Array buy-in plus completed spectrum monetization) is already partially reflected after the stock's near-doubling and the Raymond James downgrade. I treat the analyst targets (mean ~$50.5) as informative but stale; they predate the May downgrade and the post-Q1 pullback to ~$39.44, and the cleaner forward signal is the Market Perform reset. A cheap-on-assets setup with the easy catalysts already realized is not an asymmetric mispricing, and the lessons of this environment caution against rewarding cheapness without a fresh, unpriced catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

**Towers (Array).** Array is now a sub-scale tower operator (4,452 towers) against American Tower, Crown Castle, and SBA. Its economics rest on long-term escalating leases, but the tenant base is thin and the trajectory is negative. Reported tenancy fell from 1.03 (YE2025) to 0.96 (Q1 2026) in a single quarter. T-Mobile leases ~1,800 towers on an interim, cancellable basis (up to 30 months), and management explicitly expects early termination and revenue decline. DISH Wireless claimed in September 2025 that its tower obligations are "excused," has stopped paying, and is now recognized on a cash basis only with $2.9M of contract assets written off; DISH was ~$6.5M of 2025 revenue with a contract nominally running to 2035. The tower revenue quality is less durable than the headline 15-year Master License Agreement (2,015-tower minimum) implies.

**Fiber/broadband (TDS Telecom).** Competing against cable incumbents and larger fiber overbuilders in small and rural markets, where scale disadvantages on procurement, marketing, and capex are real. The thesis works only if fiber penetration gains outpace legacy declines over a multi-year horizon, which requires sustained heavy investment. TDS repeatedly flags its scale disadvantage in filings.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating on the tower side (falling tenancy, tenant defaults, interim-lease churn) and stable-to-modestly-improving on fiber (footprint growth offset by scale disadvantage and legacy attrition). This nets to a below-average competitive position score, weighted toward the negative tower trajectory, which is the more immediate revenue risk.

## Risk Assessment

- **T-Mobile tenant concentration and interim-lease churn (Array):** management expects ~1,800 interim leases to terminate early; tenancy already slipped to 0.96.
- **DISH default (escalating):** non-payment, cash-basis recognition, contract-asset write-off, contractual commitment to 2035 now in doubt.
- **Spectrum monetization risk:** ~$1.58B book must be sold opportunistically into a soft pricing environment (high-band already impaired); Verizon, T-Mobile, and remaining sales are subject to FCC approval and could be delayed or fail.
- **Minority buy-in conflict of interest (new):** the May 7, 2026 all-stock proposal (0.86x) is under review by an Array independent special committee, but the Carlson voting trust controls both entities, so the fairness of the exchange ratio is a live governance question. The 10-Q added this as a new risk factor.
- **Regulatory/USF dependence:** material reliance on E-ACAM support (through 2038) with build obligations, plus exposure to USF reform.
- **Legacy litigation:** ongoing False Claims Act litigation (Advantage Spectrum reinstated by the D.C. Circuit Sept 2025; King Street concluded favorably Jan 2026) and a 2025 shareholder derivative suit over 2022 UScellular statements.

These risks are partly offset by the asset cushion: at a controlled company sitting on net cash with monetizable spectrum, the downside is buffered. The risk score is balanced rather than low, reflecting that the most material risks (tower revenue erosion, buy-in fairness) are idiosyncratic and not fully priced, while balance-sheet strength limits tail severity.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly cautious but not alarmed. Near-term ATM IV is 32.2% vs. 1-year HV of 27.9% (rich by 4.3 points, but only 20th percentile within the current chain), with a 6.3% put/call skew indicating modestly elevated put demand. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.2%. Term structure is in contango (32.2% near vs. 36.7% long-dated), pointing to greater uncertainty around the later-2026 catalysts (spectrum closes, buy-in resolution) than near-term. The P/C OI ratio collapsed from 0.25 to 0.04 since the May snapshot as the price fell from $45.38 to $39.44, consistent with bearish positioning being closed rather than added (neutral-to-mildly-supportive for the stock). Short interest is negligible at 0.1% of float. None of this contradicts the HOLD; it reflects a market awaiting the next corporate-action data point rather than positioning for a directional break.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Verizon spectrum sale closes for ~$1.0B and funds another large Array special dividend; remaining spectrum is monetized above the impaired marks; the Array buy-in closes on terms that simplify the structure and narrow the conglomerate discount; and the $524M buyback is deployed at depressed prices. The asset value (cash + spectrum + partnerships + towers + fiber) is realized closer to gross, pulling the stock toward the prior ~$50.5 analyst mean. Bull target ~$50.

**Bear case.** Tower revenue erodes faster than expected as T-Mobile cancels interim leases and DISH's default becomes permanent; remaining spectrum sells below book or sales are delayed by FCC review; the Array buy-in stalls or is litigated on fairness grounds; and the fiber business's heavy capex continues to suppress consolidated FCF. With the easy breakup gains already realized, the stock drifts back toward the low end of its 52-week range ($32.60). Bear target ~$33, roughly -16% from current.

Both legs have narrowed since the divestiture. The bull case requires execution on catalysts that are real but incremental and partially priced; the bear case is cushioned by net cash. This is a name to hold through the catalyst path, not one offering compelling asymmetry at ~$39.44.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is the sum-of-the-parts catalyst sequence: ~$1.0B Verizon spectrum proceeds and associated special dividend (Q2/Q3 2026), resolution of the Array minority buy-in, and continued buyback execution against a market cap trading below gross asset value. These are specific, dated, and capable of moving the equity; they justify holding rather than exiting.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Array buy-in finalized on terms accretive to TDS minority holders (e.g., exchange ratio at or better than 0.86x with an independent fairness opinion), removing the conflict overhang.
- Verizon spectrum sale closes at or above ~$1.0B with proceeds returned via buyback at prices below estimated SOTP value.
- Tower tenancy stabilizes above ~0.95 (vs. 0.96 in Q1) with the DISH and T-Mobile interim-lease tails clarified, de-risking the durable cash flow.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Tower tenancy continues falling below ~0.90 with confirmed permanent loss of DISH and accelerated T-Mobile interim cancellations.
- Verizon spectrum sale fails or is materially delayed/repriced by FCC review, or further high-band impairments signal the spectrum book is significantly overstated.
- Array buy-in is restructured at terms that transfer value from TDS minority holders to the controlling family, or triggers litigation.

**Reassessment timeline:** next earnings release and the expected Q2/Q3 2026 Verizon spectrum close, whichever comes first. The Array special-committee decision is the single most important interim data point.

## Conclusion

TDS is a cleanly executed restructuring story whose easy gains have already been captured. The company slashed debt by ~$3.4B, built a $1.37B cash position, retains $1.58B of monetizable spectrum and ~$176M/year of partnership income, and trades at a market cap below its gross asset value. That asset cushion supports the downside and is why the stock is not an AVOID despite a deteriorating tower trajectory and a controlled-company governance structure. But the structural-simplification catalyst that drove the near-doubling versus peers is substantially priced, the remaining catalysts are incremental and event-dependent, and the controlling-family conflict on the Array buy-in is unresolved. The fiber operating core is years from contributing to per-share earnings and currently consumes cash. HOLD, with the Verizon spectrum close and the Array special-committee decision as the key reassessment points.
