**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Paramount Skydance is not analyzable as a conventional operating company. Since the August 2025 combination of Skydance Media and Paramount Global, it has functioned as a control-investment vehicle for the Ellison family and RedBird Capital, mid-transformation, attempting an ~$80.9B equity / ~$110B+ enterprise acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD). Pro forma for that deal, total debt would balloon to roughly $85 billion, financed by $54B of commitments including a $49B bridge, against an entity whose primary EBITDA engine (linear TV) is in secular decline. The equity story for public holders is dominated by deal mechanics, leverage, dilution, and integration risk rather than by operating fundamentals.

The standalone business is genuinely improving at the margin. Q1 2026 revenue grew 2% to $7.35B, Paramount+ reached 79.6M subscribers with ARPU up 14% to $8.30, and direct-to-consumer turned profitable ($251M adjusted EBITDA). But the headline "+59% adjusted EBITDA" is substantially an artifact of pushdown accounting that wrote down programming assets and lowered future amortization, not pure operating leverage; operating cash flow was a thin $185M. Cash fell from $3.27B to $1.94B in a single quarter, drained by a $2.8B Netflix termination fee paid on WBD's behalf, with $2.15B drawn on the revolver to fund it.

The single largest overhang, DOJ antitrust review, cleared in June 2026, and Chinese regulators followed. That removed a genuine binary risk and is why the stock has held above its 52-week low. But the clearance is politically loaded (top DOJ officials reportedly cleared the deal before career antitrust staff could formally object), leaving residual fragility to state AG action, and it does nothing to resolve the structural problem for Class B holders: they are non-voting, will be heavily diluted by the equity syndication (~39–42% of post-merger Class B going to syndication parties), and own a claim on a ~$85B-debt balance sheet whose deleveraging path depends on integrating a company that just lost $2.9B in a quarter. We rate PSKY AVOID, consistent with the prior three reports, and see the current price as offering no asymmetric reward against these risks.

## Company Overview

Paramount Skydance Corporation (Nasdaq: PSKY) was formed on August 7, 2025, combining Skydance Media and Paramount Global under a new holding company. The portfolio includes Paramount Pictures, CBS, CBS News/Sports, Nickelodeon, MTV, BET, Comedy Central, Showtime, Paramount+, Pluto TV, and the Skydance studios (Animation, Film, TV, Interactive/Games).

Beginning in 2026 the company reorganized into three reporting segments:
- **TV Media** (linear — CBS network/stations, cable nets): Q1 revenue $3.67B, adjusted EBITDA $1,055M. The profit engine, structurally declining (ad revenue -6%, affiliate -6% on a recast basis).
- **Direct-to-Consumer** (Paramount+, Pluto TV, BET+, Showtime): revenue $2.40B, adjusted EBITDA $251M. Newly profitable, the growth story. Paramount+ at 79.6M subscribers (+1.8M YoY), ARPU $8.30 (+14% YoY), revenue +17%.
- **Studios** (CBS Studios, Paramount Pictures, Skydance, Miramax): revenue $1.28B, adjusted EBITDA $164M.

The controlling structure is central. The Ellison family holds roughly 77.5% of voting Class A stock; the publicly listed PSKY Class B shares are non-voting. Public holders cannot influence governance and are not guaranteed a control premium. CEO David Ellison chairs the board, with President Jeff Shell (ex-NBCUniversal), COO/Chief Strategy Officer Brandon Gordon, and CFO Dennis Cinelli. The Chief Legal Officer is Makan Delrahim, former head of the DOJ Antitrust Division under the first Trump administration, a hire whose relevance to the politically scrutinized WBD approval is hard to overlook.

## Financial Analysis

Standardized data feeds misrepresent PSKY because of the Successor/Predecessor split created by pushdown accounting. The combination revalued Paramount Global's net assets to roughly $11.7B fair value as of August 7, 2025, inflating quarterly D&A to $362M from $88M on intangible amortization, and rendering period-over-period comparisons non-comparable on a GAAP basis. The headline trailing EPS of -$0.73 and the reported "FY2025" net loss of -$586M are transition artifacts. The 10-Q is the reliable source.

**Q1 2026 (Successor) vs. Q1 2025 (Predecessor):**
- Revenue $7.35B, +2% (Paramount+ +17% and Skydance inclusion offsetting linear declines)
- Operating income $616M, +12%
- Net earnings to Parent $168M, $0.15 diluted EPS (down from $0.22, reflecting dilution from ~1,110M shares post-merger vs. ~672M prior)
- Adjusted EBITDA $1,161M, +59%, but a meaningful portion of the content-cost reduction comes from the pushdown revaluation writing down programming assets, lowering future amortization, rather than operating improvement
- Operating cash flow $185M, thin, and the figure that best reflects real cash generation

**Balance sheet at 3/31/26:**
- Cash $1.94B, down from $3.27B
- Total debt $14.8B long-term + $0.66B current; face value $16.78B (carried net of a ~$1.30B fair-value haircut). The bonds trade below carrying value (fair value $11.4B vs. $13.33B carrying), reflecting market caution on the credit pre-WBD
- Equity $11.7B; leverage covenant max 4.50x, currently in compliance
- Revenue mix: affiliate & subscription 48%, advertising 33% (weak linear ad market), licensing 17%, theatrical 2%

The earnings track record is volatile: 2 beats and 2 misses over four quarters, including a -131% surprise in Q3 2025 and a -1,279% surprise in Q4 2025, against a 51.8% beat in Q1 2026. This is not a business with stable forward visibility.

The standalone trajectory is real but modest. Cash operating leverage is far smaller than the headline EBITDA suggests, liquidity is being managed tightly (revolver expanded to $5.0B in April 2026), and the EBITDA anchor, linear TV, is in secular decline.

## Growth Analysis

The credible growth vector is direct-to-consumer. Paramount+ added 1.8M subscribers YoY to 79.6M, ARPU rose 14% to $8.30, segment revenue grew 17%, and the segment turned profitable at $251M adjusted EBITDA. This is the most defensible part of the bull case.

It is set against a declining linear base. TV Media remains the largest EBITDA contributor at $1,055M but advertising fell 6% and affiliate revenue fell 6% on a recast basis. The structural mechanism is well established: cord-cutting reduces affiliate subscribers and linear ad inventory value, compressing the segment that funds content. Streaming growth is partially cannibalizing the higher-margin linear business rather than purely adding to it.

The forward estimate picture is deteriorating, which weighs heavily here. Consensus EPS revisions are broadly negative: current-quarter -42.4% over 90 days, next-quarter -49.0%, current-year -17.4%, next-year -10.8%. The 30-day trends remain negative for the next quarter (-25.8%) and full year (-15.3%). Consensus is marking the standalone earnings power down, not up.

The dominant growth question is WBD. The deal would combine Paramount's CBS/Paramount+/Paramount Pictures with WBD's Warner Bros. studio, HBO/HBO Max, CNN, DC, and content library, creating scale to compete with Netflix and Disney. The strategic logic is coherent. But WBD reported a $2.9B net loss in Q1 2026, the merger terms require PSKY to acquire WBD's troubled Streaming & Studios business and condition closing on WBD not spinning that unit off, and synergy realization is unproven. Growth from here is a turnaround-plus-integration story layered on a debt-heavy close, not organic momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are negative and uninformative given the merger accounting. The relevant frame is forward operating earnings against the capital structure and the deal outcome, and on that frame the case is poor rather than cheap.

The peer set trades at a median P/E of 26.3 (Netflix 26.3, Disney 16.0, TKO 79.4, Live Nation negative). PSKY cannot be cleanly placed in that comparison because its earnings are distorted and its enterprise value is about to change by an order of magnitude. Current-year consensus EPS of $0.64 against a $9.94 price is ~15.5x, but those estimates are falling, the figure is heavily affected by pushdown amortization, and it precedes a transaction that adds a large increment of incremental debt (pro-forma debt of ~$85.2B vs. ~$15.5B currently). A low headline multiple here reflects structural complexity and balance-sheet risk, not mispricing.

Analyst targets bracket the price widely: low $7.00, median $12.00, mean $12.64, high $20.00, current $9.94. The spread reflects genuine uncertainty about the deal's value to non-voting holders, not a consensus that the stock is undervalued. The recommendation mix has deteriorated: strong-buy ratings went from 2 to 0 over the most recent period while sells rose from 4 to 5, with 11 holds. On a beta-stripped basis, a depressed multiple that reflects real leverage and governance impairment is a value trap, not an entry point.

The one identifiable source of potential asymmetric value for public holders is the 10-year warrant distribution (one per share, strike set at the syndication price within a $12–$16.02 collar) offered in lieu of a rights offering. That instrument is the cleanest mechanism by which a Class B holder could participate in upside if the deal closes and deleverages successfully. It is not enough to offset the dilution and leverage risk in the common.

We assign a valuation score of 4.0, below neutral because the compressed multiple reflects structural impairment and a falling-estimate backdrop rather than an identifiable mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

On scale, the combined entity would be a credible challenger to Netflix and Disney, with one of the deepest content libraries in the industry. The strategic rationale for scale in a streaming margin race is sound, and bundling Paramount+ with HBO Max content and the Warner Bros. studio output could improve unit economics over time.

The trajectory is the concern. Linear TV, still the profit engine, is in secular decline, and the company is trying to fund a streaming buildout and a debt-laden acquisition while its highest-margin revenue erodes. Paramount+ is gaining subscribers but remains subscale relative to Netflix (~$326B market cap, ~26x P/E) and is competing in a market where Netflix and Disney have larger content budgets and better-established profitability. WBD's own troubles mean the acquisition is a turnaround, not a bolt-on of healthy assets.

Industry pushback adds friction: a Hollywood financier op-ed (May 2026) called the deal anti-competitive and warned of debt-driven job and synergy cuts, and a private antitrust suit by five streaming consumers (April 2026) seeks to block it. Competitive position is improving in DTC but the overall trajectory is mixed at best, warranting a below-neutral 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the dominant feature of this equity, and it is concentrated rather than diffuse.

1. **Leverage.** Pro forma for WBD, total debt would be ~$85.2B against a business with thin standalone operating cash flow and a declining EBITDA core. Risks include credit downgrades, refinancing risk, dividend suspension, and forced content-spend cuts. This is the central financial risk.

2. **Deal mechanics and termination fees.** If antitrust fails, PSKY owes WBD a $7.0B termination fee (funded by the Ellison parties at $16.02/share); a ticking fee accrues if not closed by 9/30/2026, with an end date of March 4, 2027 (extendable to June 2027).

3. **Antitrust residual fragility.** Reporting indicates top officials cleared the deal before career antitrust staff (reportedly leaning toward a lawsuit) could formally object, and multiple state AGs have issued subpoenas/CIDs. The risk is reduced, not extinguished, and remains susceptible to state-level action.

4. **Dilution.** Equity syndication parties will receive ~39–42% of post-merger Class B; warrants dilute further.

5. **Governance and conflicts.** Non-voting Class B; Ellison family holds 100% of voting power. Related-party concentration includes a 6-year, $300M Oracle cloud agreement (Larry Ellison chairs Oracle), a $169M property lease from an Ellison-controlled entity, and RedBird serving as M&A advisor on WBD for an $80M fee plus 20% of any termination fee.

6. **Sovereign-wealth funding.** Saudi PIF, Qatar QIA, and an Abu Dhabi vehicle are in the equity syndication, optically and politically notable for an entity that owns CBS News and would own CNN, with plausible future editorial-independence and foreign-ownership scrutiny.

7. **Legacy liabilities.** ~18,050 pending asbestos claims (Westinghouse predecessor) with a ~$1.4B fair-valued contingent liability.

Given the concentration of binary and structural risks not fully resolved by the antitrust clearance, and the non-voting, heavily-diluted nature of the public claim, risk factors score a low 2.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV sits at 47.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 64.2%, making short-dated options screen cheap (IV percentile 10th within the chain). But the term structure is steeply upward-sloping (1-week IV 47.2%, 1-month 85.6%, 3-month 104.6%) and the 1-month tenor shows a sharply negative put/call skew of -33.2% with a put/call volume ratio of 1.12, pricing meaningful downside protection demand around the expected Q3 deal-close window. The 1-week put/call skew is balanced (-2.8%), so the immediate complacency is offset by elevated medium-term hedging. The single notable unusual trade is a September $9 call (4.0x volume/OI), a modestly bullish position into the close timeline. The ATM IV reading swung from 356.4% to 47.2% between snapshots, indicating thin, unstable options data that should be weighted lightly. The options market is calm in the next week but is pricing substantial uncertainty into the deal-resolution window, consistent with the fundamental view.

Short interest is elevated at 13.9% of float (74.6M shares, 7.6 days to cover), indicating bearish institutional positioning, but not at a level (>20%) that creates acute squeeze risk against a non-voting controlled stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** DOJ and Chinese clearances are secured, removing the largest binary risk. The deal closes in Q3 2026 on schedule, creating a streaming/studio scale player with one of the deepest content libraries in media. Paramount+ momentum continues, DTC profitability scales, and synergies plus content-library monetization drive deleveraging from ~$85B over several years. The warrant distribution gives public holders a leveraged participation in a successful close. WBD's troubled assets are turned around under new management. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the analyst high of $20 over an extended horizon.

**Bear Case.** Integration of two large, troubled studios and overlapping streaming platforms proves harder and slower than modeled; WBD's losses persist; synergy realization disappoints. The ~$85B debt load, into a higher-for-longer rate environment and a weak linear ad market, forces a dividend suspension and content-spend cuts, weakening the competitive position the deal was meant to strengthen. Falling consensus estimates continue. State AG litigation or the private antitrust suit creates delay and cost. Heavy syndication dilution caps any per-share recovery. The non-voting common drifts toward the analyst low of $7.00, roughly 30% below the current price.

The asymmetry favors the bear case. The upside requires near-flawless execution of a debt-heavy turnaround that public holders cannot influence, while the downside is structural and partly already in motion (falling estimates, tightening liquidity, secular linear decline).

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated events are the expected Q3 2026 deal close and the 9/30/2026 ticking-fee threshold. A close on heavy leverage with confirmed dilution terms could pressure the common, but the antitrust risk that would have been the cleanest negative catalyst has already cleared. There is no single dated event with high probability of driving a sharp leg down; the bear case is a slow-grind leverage and integration story, not a binary collapse.

**Timing window.** Deal resolution is expected Q3 2026, three to four months out, with an end date as far as June 2027. That is at the edge of a reasonable short horizon and exposes the position to carry over a multi-month window with an uncertain trigger.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** SI is 13.9% of float with 7.6 days to cover, elevated and bearish, but below the >20% threshold that creates acute squeeze risk. The crowded-but-not-extreme positioning means much of the bearish view is already expressed.

**Borrow and options signal.** Medium-term options confirm bearish positioning (1-month -33.2% skew, P/C volume 1.12), but near-term IV is cheap and the data is unstable. There is no obvious cheap, well-timed put structure that isn't already pricing the deal-window risk.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$7.00 (~30% downside) vs. a squeeze/successful-close upside toward $12–20 (a 20–100% upside risk). With no clean dated catalyst and meaningful upside risk from a successful close or Ellison-family support, the asymmetry does not clear a 2:1 reward/risk bar for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is structural weakness in a controlled, leveraged vehicle, not an actively collapsing situation. The largest negative catalyst (antitrust) has already resolved favorably, short interest is already elevated, and the upside risk from a successful close or controlling-shareholder backstop is too large relative to the downside. Avoid owning; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an AVOID. The headwinds (~$85B pro-forma leverage, heavy dilution, non-voting governance, falling consensus estimates, and integration risk on a turnaround acquisition) are expected to persist through and beyond the Q3 2026 close and into a multi-year deleveraging period.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- The WBD deal closes and management provides a credible, milestone-based deleveraging path with confirmed synergy capture, evidenced by sequential debt reduction and stabilizing free cash flow over two or more quarters.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive on a 30- and 90-day basis (currently -15% to -49%), signaling the standalone and combined earnings power is being marked up rather than down.
- Governance/conflict overhang is materially reduced (clarity on warrant value realization for Class B holders and resolution of related-party concerns) and DTC profitability scales with continued ARPU and subscriber growth.
- Alternatively, a price low enough to fully discount the leverage and dilution (toward or below the $7 analyst floor) with the deal de-risked could create a value entry, but only with a specific deleveraging catalyst in hand.

**Timeline.** Headwinds persist through the expected Q3 2026 close and well into the post-close deleveraging period (12–36 months). The next natural reassessment points are the Q2 2026 earnings print and confirmation of deal timing/terms.

## Conclusion

Paramount Skydance is an event-driven, controlled-company situation whose equity outcome for public holders is dominated by deal mechanics, leverage, dilution, and integration risk rather than by the modestly improving standalone business. The DOJ clearance removed the cleanest binary risk and explains why the stock has held above its 52-week low, but it leaves intact the structural problems: non-voting Class B shares, ~39–42% dilution to syndication parties, ~$85B of pro-forma debt against a declining linear EBITDA base, falling consensus estimates, and an acquisition target that just lost $2.9B in a quarter. The headline +59% EBITDA growth is substantially an accounting artifact; cash operating leverage is thin and liquidity is being managed tightly.

There is no asymmetric upside at $9.94. The downside is structural and partly already unfolding, while the upside requires near-flawless execution of a leveraged turnaround that public shareholders cannot steer. This is the fourth consecutive AVOID; new data (antitrust clearance) reduces one specific risk but does not change the structural case, and the deteriorating estimate trend reinforces it. We maintain **AVOID**.
