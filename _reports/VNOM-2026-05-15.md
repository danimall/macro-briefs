## Executive Summary

Viper Energy is the dominant publicly traded mineral and royalty interest company in the Permian Basin, controlling approximately 96,000 net royalty acres with no drilling or LOE burden, zero capex obligations, and 90%+ EBITDA margins. The business model is structurally differentiated from E&P companies: Viper collects royalty payments from operators drilling on its acreage while operators (primarily Diamondback Energy at ~55% of royalty income, ExxonMobil at ~14%) bear all drilling, completion, and operating expenses. Cash operating costs of $3.52/BOE mean the company retains ~91% of realized revenue as pre-tax cash flow.

The stock trades at approximately 20x forward EPS ($2.37 consensus for FY2026) with a 5.6% dividend yield, approaching a capital return inflection point where management has signaled distributions could exceed 100% of cash available. The primary risk is commodity price reversal, combined with Diamondback's significant governance influence creating asymmetries and periodic secondary selling pressure.

## Company Overview

Viper Energy was created as a subsidiary of Diamondback Energy to own and acquire mineral and royalty interests in the Permian Basin. The company has no employees — all personnel are Diamondback secondees. Its asset base consists of perpetual mineral rights that generate royalty income whenever operators drill and produce on its acreage, regardless of cost environment.

Following the $4.0B Sitio Royalties acquisition (August 2025) and the $3.6B Diamondback Drop Down (2025), VNOM approximately doubled its production base to 95,126 BOE/d average in FY2025, with a Q4 exit rate of ~134,000 BOE/d. The February 2026 Non-Permian Divestiture ($617M) sharpened the portfolio to pure Permian Basin exposure.

The company recently announced the Riverbend acquisition ($337M in cash plus approximately 3.7M Class A shares), adding approximately 3,064 net royalty acres with expected next-12-month production of approximately 4,000 BOE/d. Post-Q1 2026, the company updated standalone 2026 production guidance to 126-130 MBOE/d (excluding Riverbend contributions). With 406,035 MBOE in proved reserves (78% PDP, 48% oil), the asset base supports multi-decade production.

## Growth Analysis

Production grew 91% in FY2025, primarily acquisition-driven. Organic growth comes from operator drilling activity on VNOM's acreage — as of March 31, 2026, 88 gross rigs were operating on its acreage, 13 of which were Diamondback-operated.

The updated 2026 standalone production guidance of 126-130 MBOE/d (excluding Riverbend) implies modest sequential growth from the Q4 2025 exit rate of ~134 MBOE/d as the Non-Permian divestiture removed some volume. The Riverbend acquisition is expected to add approximately 1,000 bo/d to the midpoint of standalone 2026 oil guidance.

The critical growth variable is commodity pricing. At FY2025 average oil prices of $63.27/Bbl, realizations were depressed relative to 2024's $75.48/Bbl. VNOM's near-zero cost structure means any improvement in oil prices translates directly to revenue and cash flow. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $2.37 reflects 90-day estimate revisions of +63.3%, capturing partial recovery expectations.

The minerals consolidation pipeline remains active. The Permian Basin minerals market is "highly fragmented" per management, with VNOM's scale, data capabilities, and Diamondback relationship providing sourcing advantages for accretive bolt-on acquisitions.

## Competitive Landscape

VNOM is now the largest publicly traded Permian Basin mineral company after absorbing Sitio Royalties in 2025. The competitive position is strong and improving:

- **Scale advantage:** 96,003 net royalty acres across the most prolific basin in North America
- **Operator diversification:** 88 gross rigs as of March 31, 2026, though Diamondback accounts for approximately 55% of royalty income and operates ~38% of net royalty acreage — representing meaningful concentration
- **Zero operating leverage:** Immune to service cost inflation, supply chain disruptions, labor shortages — all borne by operators
- **Perpetual assets:** Unlike leasehold positions that expire, mineral rights have infinite duration
- **Consolidation platform:** Largest acquirer in a fragmented market, with demonstrated execution ($4.0B Sitio, $3.6B Drop Down, Riverbend)

The primary structural risk to competitive position is that Diamondback reduces Permian Basin activity, which would slow production growth on VNOM's acreage. However, at current oil prices, Diamondback's capital discipline framework still supports substantial drilling programs.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Commodity Price Exposure (HIGH IMPACT):**
WTI averaged $64.73 in 2025, down from $75.76 in 2024. Every $1/Bbl change materially impacts royalty income. OPEC+ policy, tariffs, and global demand are uncontrollable factors. VNOM's very low cost structure provides significant downside protection — management has indicated the company's royalty model supports continued cash generation even in materially lower price environments — but equity valuation would compress with lower oil prices.

**2. Diamondback Governance/Secondary Sales (MODERATE):**
In March 2026, a secondary offering of approximately 17.4M shares (at a public offering price of $45.90) was completed by selling stockholders including Diamondback and other holders; Viper received no proceeds. Post-offering, Diamondback's beneficial ownership declined to approximately 38.9% of fully diluted shares/units (from 42.1% at YE2025). Diamondback retains significant governance rights — including board designation rights and approval over non-Diamondback-seconded executive officers — while it holds at least 25% ownership. Further monetization creates technical selling pressure. The governance structure allows Diamondback to transfer assets at prices that may not maximize minority shareholder value (demonstrated by the 2025 Drop Down triggering immediate impairments).

**3. Ceiling Test Impairments (LOW — Cosmetic):**
Management noted another material impairment is "reasonably likely" in Q1 2026 under full-cost accounting rules. These are non-cash and do not affect distributable cash flow, but they distort GAAP metrics and may confuse less sophisticated investors.

**4. Share Dilution:**
Fully diluted shares approximately doubled from ~180M (2023) to ~368M (YE2025) through equity-funded acquisitions. Per-share metrics remain constructive but further dilution is possible for large acquisitions. The Riverbend acquisition adds approximately 3.7M Class A shares.

**5. Waha Gas Pricing:**
Natural gas realized only $1.08/Mcf in FY2025 due to Permian pipeline constraints. Gas represents ~26% of reserves. Hedges lock in -$1.61 differential for 2026, limiting near-term upside from any gas price improvement.

**6. Post-Riverbend Leverage:**
Pro forma for the Riverbend acquisition (expected to close in Q3 2026), net debt is expected to increase to approximately $1.8B at $55 WTI, temporarily moving leverage above the stated $1.5B target and potentially delaying the full capital return inflection.

## Conclusion

VNOM represents a structurally advantaged royalty business at reasonable valuation with a potential capital return inflection ahead. The low-cost model, investment-grade balance sheet approaching target leverage, expanding buyback authorization, and 5.6% dividend yield create a compelling income + growth combination. The 90-day estimate revision of +63.3% confirms fundamental improvement from the expanded asset base, though 30-day revisions have turned negative (-3.4% current year) reflecting commodity price uncertainty.

The primary risk — commodity price weakness — carries meaningful impact given VNOM's direct exposure to oil realizations. Diamondback governance influence and secondary selling create ongoing friction. The Riverbend acquisition temporarily increases leverage above the net debt target. These risks are real but quantifiable and partially compensated by the dividend yield floor.

With 17 analysts unanimously rating BUY/Strong BUY, a median target of $58 (+20%), approaching capital return inflection, and a structurally advantaged business model, VNOM offers asymmetric risk/reward from current levels.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The valuation case is less compelling on a fully diluted basis (~13.5x OCF vs. originally stated ~6x), and pro forma Riverbend leverage (~$1.8B net debt) delays the capital return inflection; these corrections moderate the magnitude of near-term upside but do not invalidate the constructive thesis.
