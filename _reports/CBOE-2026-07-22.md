**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cboe Global Markets is the most derivatives-concentrated of the large exchange operators. Roughly 68% of net revenue-less-cost-of-revenue derives from the Options and Futures segments, dominated by the exclusively-licensed SPX complex and the proprietary VIX franchise. Index options carry a revenue-per-contract roughly 14x that of multi-listed equity options, and the Options segment posted a 72.7% operating margin in 2025. This is a genuine near-monopoly in U.S. equity-index volatility products, protected by exclusive S&P 500 licensing, proprietary VIX intellectual property, and liquidity network effects. Net revenue grew 17% in 2025 to $2,429M and adjusted diluted EPS rose 24% to $10.67, driven by a 25% surge in options ADV led by the zero-day-to-expiry (0DTE) boom.

The forward setup is defined by two crosscurrents. On the positive side, consensus EPS estimates are rising steadily — current-year up 11.1% and next-year up 13.3% over 90 days — and the stock has fallen roughly 24% from its May 2026 high near $371 to ~$282, compressing the forward multiple to ~20x on a $13.98 current-year and ~19x on a $15.02 next-year figure. The launch of "Cboe Predicts" in June 2026, offering binary options on the Mini-S&P 500 distributed through Interactive Brokers and Charles Schwab, converts a disclosed competitive threat (Kalshi/Polymarket) into a growth vector that leverages the existing index IP. On the cautionary side, 2025 was a record-volatility year that sets a difficult 2026 comp, the entire C-suite has turned over under a new CEO, there is no open-market insider buying, and the near-term options market is pricing elevated stress (43.7% ATM IV vs 31.4% historical, backwardation, 6.5% put skew).

We rate CBOE a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.5, consistent with the prior report's trajectory (7.4 in early May declining to 6.6 in mid-June). The de-rating has improved the risk/reward relative to the spring highs, and the estimate-revision momentum is real, but the combination of a peak-volume comp, a management transition the company itself flags as an execution risk, and a modest premium to the durable recurring-revenue base keeps this short of a high-conviction buy. We would accumulate on further weakness toward the low-$240s (near the 52-week low and analyst floor).

## Company Overview

Cboe operates the largest U.S. options exchange, the third-largest U.S. equities exchange operator, one of Europe's largest equities exchanges, a leading pan-European clearinghouse (Cboe Clear Europe), and cash/derivatives venues in Canada and Australia. As of January 1, 2025 it reports five segments — Options, North American Equities, Europe & Asia Pacific, Futures, and Global FX — aggregated for revenue management into three product groupings: Derivatives markets (55% of net revenue), Data Vantage/market data (26%), and Cash & spot markets (19%).

The economic engine is the proprietary index franchise. SPX options trade on an exclusive S&P 500 license; VIX options and futures use Cboe's own volatility methodology. Multi-listed equity options are intensely price-competitive with an RPC of $0.066, while index products earn $0.924 per contract. That 14x differential is the moat expressed in numbers.

The business is undergoing a deliberate focus-and-simplify strategy. Following a 2025 strategic review, Cboe wound down Cboe Japan equities (~$25M impairment), initiated sale processes for Cboe Australia and Cboe Canada ($17.7M Canada impairment), wound down CEDX (pan-European derivatives), and discontinued U.S./European corporate listings. Total 2025 impairment charges were $46.7M. Simultaneously, the company is expanding into prediction markets.

The drivers of the 17% net revenue growth: options ADV +25% (60.8M contracts/day), index options ADV +21%, multi-listed ADV +24% (the 0DTE boom on SPX); European equities matched ADNV +31%; Cboe Clear Europe cleared trades +19%; Data Vantage +10% (recurring, non-volume-linked). GAAP net income was flattered by roughly $97M of non-operating investment gains (the 7Ridge/Trading Technologies stake sale of $90.8M plus PYTH token gains). Adjusted diluted EPS of $10.67 (+24%) is the cleaner growth figure.

Margins and returns are elite: 60.4% GAAP operating margin (65.6% adjusted), net margin ~45% of net revenue, ROE 21.4%, ROA 11.8%, FCF margin 35.7%.

The balance sheet is effectively net-cash. Total debt of ~$1.44B across three senior unsecured note tranches (2027/2030/2032) sits against $2.22B cash and $5.14B equity, with A-/A2 investment-grade ratings, a 1.87 current ratio, and 0.81 debt/equity. The Altman Z-Score of 2.81 ("grey zone") is a false signal: the balance sheet is inflated by $1.6B+ of clearinghouse margin deposits and clearing funds passing through Cboe Clear Europe, which are offsetting current assets/liabilities rather than genuine operating leverage. Applying a manufacturing-oriented distress model to an exchange/clearinghouse balance sheet mis-reads it. Real leverage is minimal.

Capital allocation shifted in 2025. Dividends of $284M rose, but share repurchases fell 68% to $65.3M from $205M in 2024, despite $614.5M remaining authorization and cash rising to $2.2B. The most likely read is that the new CEO is preserving dry powder for M&A or the strategic pivot. Reduced buybacks remove a per-share support that peers rely on, and a large acquisition would introduce integration and valuation risk.

The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one narrow miss over the last four quarters, with the most recent print (Q1 2026) beating by 10.3% ($3.70 vs $3.36).

## Growth Analysis

Two growth threads run in parallel. The durable one is Data Vantage — recurring, high-margin market-data revenue growing ~10% independent of volume, which anchors the valuation regardless of trading activity. The cyclical one is derivatives volume, which surged in 2025 on the 0DTE phenomenon and now faces a difficult comp.

The forward-looking catalyst is Cboe Predicts: binary option contracts on the Mini-S&P 500. Two points matter here. First, these are Cboe's own listed products — the brokers provide retail order flow, but Cboe owns the franchise and the economics. Second, prediction markets (Kalshi and Polymarket ran combined monthly volume near $24B in April 2026, up from under $5B a year earlier) were flagged as a competitive threat in the 10-K, and Cboe is now entering the space as the regulated, exchange-listed player leveraging its existing SPX/index IP and 0DTE momentum. The ramp and regulatory posture (CFTC/SEC treatment of binary/event contracts) are the swing factors on how large this becomes.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum: current-quarter EPS revised +14.5% over 90 days to $3.49, next-quarter +10.7%, current-year +11.1%, next-year +13.3%. These are meaningful but fall short of the +30% threshold that would flag a strong standalone buy signal, and they reflect a normalization of a record year rather than recovery from a trough. The central uncertainty is whether the 0DTE/volume boom sustains at 2025 levels or whether 2026 faces a tough comp as volatility mean-reverts. The recurring data base and prediction-markets optionality provide downside cushion, but the derivatives volume comp is the dominant near-term variable.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$282, CBOE trades at ~23.4x trailing EPS of $10.42, but the cleaner lens is forward: ~20.2x the current-year consensus and ~18.8x the next-year estimate. The price decline occurred against rising, not falling, estimates, which improves the risk/reward rather than signaling deterioration.

Against peers in Financial Exchanges & Data, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 26.1 (NDAQ 26.1, MSCI 34.0, COIN 56.0, with FDS at 15.2 and MORN at 18.1 as the cheaper data-centric names). CBOE at ~23x trailing / ~19x forward sits below the transaction-exchange peers, a discount that is partly justified by the difficult 2026 volume comp and the single-product concentration risk, and partly by the market's uncertainty about whether the 2025 volume surge repeats. On growth-adjusted terms, with next-year EPS growth in the low double digits and a forward multiple in the high teens, the PEG sits near 1.4-1.5 — reasonable, not cheap.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $258, median $307, mean $311.64, high $375. The stock trades below the median, implying ~9% upside to consensus, but the low target of $258 is only ~8% below current price, which caps how much of a floor the analyst range provides. We do not treat the median target as a strong "floor" here because the multiple already reflects the market's discounting of the 0DTE comp risk, and the sell-side distribution (10 holds, 2 sells, 1 strong sell against 3 strong buys and 1 buy) is split.

Applying the equity-lessons discipline: a low or compressed multiple alone is not a buy signal, and cheapness has been anti-predictive absent a specific catalyst. Here the catalyst — Cboe Predicts plus the estimate-revision momentum — is real but not yet quantified, and the recurring-revenue quality supports the multiple rather than making it a bargain. We assign a valuation score of 6.2: modestly constructive on the de-rating and forward growth, but not an asymmetric value setup.

## Competitive Landscape

Cboe occupies a structurally advantaged niche. Its index-options and volatility franchise is protected by exclusive licenses (S&P 500, Russell 2000) and proprietary IP (VIX methodology) with genuine pricing power. The equities matching business is a race-to-zero commodity where Cboe's U.S. equities exchange market share fell to 10.0% from 11.4% and net capture dropped 32% in 2025. The company is right to be shedding sub-scale international equities/derivatives units and concentrating on the derivatives/data core.

Industry tailwinds favor the franchise: structural growth in short-dated options (0DTE), retailization of options and outcome-based trading, and recurring high-margin data demand. Cboe Predicts positions the company to capture the retail prediction-market shift with a regulated product rather than ceding it to Kalshi, Polymarket, or new entrants reportedly including Meta.

The competitive risks are concrete. Off-exchange venues continue to gain equities share. Tokenization and atomic settlement could, over time, disintermediate traditional clearing. A competitor developing economically similar volatility products, or a change in S&P 500 license exclusivity terms, would strike at the core. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in the high-margin core (data and index products) but eroding in the commoditized equities business, which nets to a moderately positive 6.8, down from prior reports' higher marks as the equities share loss and clearing-disintermediation risks have become more concrete.

## Risk Assessment

**Revenue concentration is the defining risk.** The 68% net-revenue dependence on SPX and VIX means loss, non-renewal, or de-exclusivization of the S&P 500 license would be materially damaging — a single point of failure a diversified financials name would not carry.

**Customer concentration compounds it.** Top-10 customers represent ~57% of revenues, and in options clearing three OCC clearing members account for ~71% of transaction/other fees. These customers have no lock-up agreements; loss of a major market-maker could disrupt options liquidity.

**Regulatory/market-structure risk.** The SEC's adopted tick-size/access-fee-cap rule is flagged as likely to pressure equities transaction-fee revenue. Potential changes to Rule 611 are under examination. UK recognition of OCC as a third-country CCP has a December 31, 2027 deadline; failure would raise capital charges for UK participants.

**Volume-comp risk.** If 2026 volatility mean-reverts, the derivatives volume that drove the surge compresses, pressuring the transaction-fee line — the single most important variable for the year.

**Execution/continuity risk from the C-suite reset** (detailed below).

The Cboe Clear Europe €1.2B liquidity facility that terminated June 26, 2026 appears to have been refinanced (8-K dated 2026-06-26), which resolves the near-term concern the 10-K flagged. On balance, the concentration and volume-comp risks are the ones not fully priced, warranting a below-neutral risk score of 5.5 — unchanged from the prior report's markdown from 7.0.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options market is pricing meaningful stress. ATM IV of 43.7% sits well above the 31.4% one-year historical vol (a rich 12.3pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (43.7% near-term vs 34.6% at six months), which markets typically read as an imminent-catalyst signal — most likely the upcoming earnings print testing the 2026 volume comp. Put/call skew of 6.5% at the near tenor indicates elevated put demand, though it flattens to negative at longer tenors. ATM IV jumped 7.4pp in a single day (36.3% to 43.7%) even as the stock rose 2.7%, consistent with hedging ahead of a known event rather than directional bearishness. The implied one-week move is ±2.5%, but the one-month is ±7.3%. This confirms the fundamental read that the next earnings report is a genuine binary on whether the 0DTE momentum sustains, and argues for patience on entry rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($340-375)

The 0DTE boom is structural, not cyclical, and derivatives volume holds near 2025 levels. Cboe Predicts ramps materially as retail prediction-market demand routes through the regulated, exchange-listed channel, adding a new high-margin revenue stream built on existing index IP. Data Vantage continues compounding ~10% independent of volume. Estimate revisions continue rising, consensus re-rates the multiple back toward the high-20s on the growth acceleration and the new CEO's deal-oriented strategy delivers accretive capital deployment from the $2.2B cash pile. At ~24x the next-year EPS the stock reaches ~$360, near the $375 street high.

### Bear Case ($215-240)

2026 volatility mean-reverts, the derivatives volume comp turns negative, and transaction fees compress. Cboe Predicts fails to scale meaningfully or draws adverse CFTC/SEC treatment. The SEC tick-size/access-fee-cap rule pressures the equities book. The C-suite transition disrupts execution during the strategic pivot, and a large, richly-priced acquisition dilutes returns. On flat-to-declining EPS and a de-rating to ~16x, the stock retraces to the low-$240s or below, roughly 15-24% downside to the 52-week low of $227.

The base case rests between: recurring data revenue and the index franchise anchor the floor while the volume comp and management transition cap the upside near-term, with prediction-markets optionality as the swing factor.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $300.** At ~20x the next-year consensus EPS, reflecting a modest re-rating from ~19x as estimate revisions continue and prediction-markets traction is confirmed, but short of a full return to peer-median transaction-exchange multiples given the volume-comp overhang. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios above.

**Bull case target: $360** (~24x next-year EPS on sustained 0DTE volume + prediction-markets ramp).

**Bear case target: $230** (~16x on a negative volume comp and stalled prediction-markets), roughly 18% below the current ~$282.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the next 2-3 earnings prints, which will reveal whether 2026 volume holds against the record 2025 comp and how quickly Cboe Predicts contributes.

**Upside/downside from current (~$282):** +6% to base case, +28% to bull case, -18% to bear case.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 prints showing derivatives ADV holding flat-to-up YoY (volume comp fear resolved), OR estimate revisions accelerating past +20% on a forward-year basis.
- Cboe Predicts producing disclosed, quantifiable revenue contribution with a favorable regulatory posture.
- A pullback to the low-$240s or below on unchanged fundamentals, restoring clear asymmetric upside.
- Open-market insider buying by the new C-suite, which is currently absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any material adverse change to the S&P 500 license exclusivity or a competitor launching economically similar index-volatility products.
- Derivatives ADV declining >15% YoY, confirming the volume-comp bear case.
- A large, richly-priced (>4x revenue) acquisition that dilutes the return profile, or evidence of execution failure from the management transition.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (the near-term options market is pricing this as a genuine binary), then the Q3 2026 print for the prediction-markets ramp.

## Conclusion

Cboe remains a high-quality, elite-margin franchise with a genuine near-monopoly in equity-index volatility products, a net-cash balance sheet, and a credible growth extension in Cboe Predicts. The recent de-rating occurred against rising estimates, which improves the risk/reward and argues against reading the price decline as thesis deterioration. This is a growth hold rather than a buy: the derivatives franchise faces a difficult 2026 volume comp against a record volatility year, the near-term options market is pricing an imminent earnings catalyst, the entire C-suite has turned over under a new CEO with execution risk the company itself flags, there is no open-market insider buying, and buybacks were cut while cash accumulated. At ~19x forward on low-double-digit growth the valuation is reasonable but not asymmetric.

We rate CBOE HOLD with an overall score of 6.5, essentially confirming the recent trajectory. We would move to BUY on a pullback toward the low-$240s, on confirmation the 0DTE volume sustains through the difficult comp, or on quantified prediction-markets traction — and would grow more cautious on a negative volume comp, a change to the S&P 500 license, or a poorly-priced acquisition.
