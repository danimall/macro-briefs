## Executive Summary

Oceaneering International has delivered an extraordinary 12-month stock performance, more than doubling from ~$17.75 to ~$37.54. The move reflects three overlapping drivers: (1) a structural recovery in subsea services demand, (2) the emerging ADTech defense franchise, and (3) a $154M one-time deferred tax valuation allowance release that inflated FY2025 EPS to $3.49. On normalized earnings of approximately $2.00/share, the stock trades at roughly 19x — a narrower discount to its peer median of 23.5x than the headline 11x P/E suggests.

The company's competitive position is strong: ~60% ROV drill support market share, $2.7B funded backlog, $689M cash against $487M debt, and a defense business growing 35% YoY with unfunded backlog up 136% to $658M. The hire of Vice Admiral Merz to lead ADTech and the Freedom AUV/ROV platform for the Defense Innovation Unit represent credible dual-use pivots.

The counterweights are meaningful. Analyst consensus targets sit at $25-$35, entirely below the current price. Every insider with recent transactions sold shares, with two officers liquidating their entire positions. OPG revenue is guided to decline "significantly" in 2026. ROV utilization has fallen to 65% from 67%. And the Manufactured Products backlog deteriorated 15% to $511M. With the energy side benefiting from the current commodity environment, the stock has already priced substantial optimism. This is a hold: the ADTech growth thesis is intact and warrants patience, but the risk/reward at $37.54 is unattractive for new capital.

## Company Overview

Oceaneering International provides engineered services, products, and robotic solutions across five segments serving two end markets: energy (83% of revenue) and defense/aerospace (17%). The company operates the world's largest fleet of work-class ROVs (250 systems) and commands an estimated 60% share of ROV drill support on the 136 contracted floating rigs globally.

**Segment Breakdown (FY2025):**

| Segment | Revenue | Op. Income | Margin | % Revenue |
|---------|---------|------------|--------|-----------|
| Subsea Robotics (SSR) | $855M | $257M | 30.0% | 31% |
| Manufactured Products | $569M | $72M | 12.7% | 20% |
| Offshore Projects Group (OPG) | $616M | $96M | 15.6% | 22% |
| IMDS | $284M | $11M | 3.9% | 10% |
| ADTech (Defense) | $460M | $58M | 12.6% | 17% |
| **Total** | **$2.78B** | **$305M** | **10.9%** | **100%** |

The company operates across approximately 48 countries, with ~55% of revenue generated internationally. Its Jones Act-compliant vessels provide protected positioning in the U.S. Gulf. The technology transfer between subsea energy robotics and undersea defense applications (pipeline monitoring, cable protection, autonomous underwater vehicles) creates a differentiated competitive moat.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings Trajectory

Revenue grew from $2.07B in FY2022 to $2.78B in FY2025, a 10.4% CAGR. Operating income expanded at a materially faster 40% CAGR ($111M to $305M) as operating margins recovered from 5.4% to 10.9%. The company's cost structure is substantially fixed (ROV fleet, vessels, facilities), so incremental revenue carries high margins.

The critical distinction in the P&L: FY2025 net income of $354M ($3.49/share) includes a $154M deferred tax valuation allowance release. This converts an expected ~$78M tax provision into a $(68M) tax benefit. Adjusted for this one-time item, normalized net income is approximately $200-220M ($2.00-$2.15/share). The valuation allowance release is a positive signal — management determined that accumulated deferred tax assets are now realizable given sustained profitability — but it should not be extrapolated. Going forward, cash taxes of $95-105M annually imply a 25-30% effective rate.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Free cash flow improved from ~$96M in FY2024 to $208M in FY2025 (7.5% FCF margin). The balance sheet has shifted to net cash: $689M cash and short-term investments against $487M in 6.00% Senior Notes due February 2028. The current ratio stands at 1.99x, the revolver ($215M) is undrawn, and Altman Z-Score is 4.33.

Q1 2026 showed a seasonal working capital build producing negative OCF of $(59M) and FCF of $(77M), drawing cash to $607M. This is typical for the company's seasonal pattern and not concerning given the full-year EBITDA guidance of $390-440M.

Capital allocation has been conservative: $40M in share repurchases in FY2025, no dividend, and capex guided to $105-115M for 2026. The company retains ample financial flexibility.

### Q1 2026 Results

Revenue of $692M exceeded consensus ($671M), and EPS of $0.36 exceeded the $0.32 consensus estimate by 11.6%, though results included an ADTech contract dispute accrual that compressed margins. Adjusted EBITDA of $83.7M was down 13% YoY. The standout metric was $1 billion in new order intake, including $300M+ in SSR and $175M in ADTech. Full-year guidance was reaffirmed.

## Growth Analysis

### ADTech: The Primary Growth Engine

ADTech revenue grew 17% in FY2025 to $460M and accelerated to 35% YoY growth in Q1 2026. Unfunded contracted backlog surged from $279M to $658M (+136% YoY). The hire of Vice Admiral William Merz (36-year Navy veteran, former Commander of U.S. Seventh Fleet) to lead the segment signals strategic commitment. The Freedom AUV/ROV platform, developed for the Defense Innovation Unit, positions Oceaneering at the intersection of subsea energy automation and undersea defense — a market gaining urgency as NATO focuses on critical infrastructure protection.

The defense growth vector provides genuine diversification from energy cyclicality. At a 13% operating margin with accelerating revenue, ADTech could contribute $600-700M in revenue by FY2027 if the current trajectory holds.

### Energy Segments: Mixed

Subsea Robotics (30% margins, $855M revenue) is the crown jewel but faces near-term headwinds: ROV utilization declined from 67% to 65%, and the floating rig count fell 6.2% YoY. Management expects improvement in the second half as pricing gains and new contracts take effect.

Manufactured Products backlog declined 15% to $511M with book-to-bill of 0.84x. Subsea tree awards are projected to increase from 190 to 306 in 2026, which should eventually replenish the backlog, but the lag creates a near-term revenue gap.

OPG revenue is guided to decline "significantly" in 2026 due to reduced U.S. Gulf and West Africa activity. IMDS full-year operating income guidance was lowered due to Middle East conflict disruptions.

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year (FY2026) consensus EPS of $1.84 has risen 3.3% over 90 days — positive but modest momentum. Next-year (FY2027) estimates of $2.12 have declined 5.7% over the same period, suggesting analysts see 2026 as a potential peak energy year before normalization. The divergence between rising near-term and falling out-year estimates warrants attention.

## Valuation Assessment

On normalized FY2025 earnings of ~$2.00-2.15 (excluding the one-time tax benefit), the trailing multiple is approximately 17-19x.

On forward estimates of $1.84 (FY2026E), the stock trades at 20.4x — below the peer median of 23.5x but reflecting a tighter discount than the headline figure suggests. On FY2027E of $2.12, the forward multiple is 17.7x.

Analyst consensus sits at $31.50 mean / $33.00 median, with a range of $25-$35. The current price of $37.54 trades **above the consensus high target of $35.00**. Zero out of 3-4 covering analysts see upside from current levels. The recent recommendation mix (0 Buy, 2 Hold, 1 Strong Sell) reinforces this.

On an EV/EBITDA basis, using the midpoint of 2026 guided EBITDA ($415M) and enterprise value of approximately $3.5B (market cap $3.7B minus net cash ~$200M), the stock trades at 8.4x EV/EBITDA — reasonable for an oilfield services company with defense exposure, but not compellingly cheap after a 111% rally.

The stock trades at 93.6% of its 52-week high ($40.12), leaving limited technical upside buffer before the prior peak.

## Competitive Landscape

Oceaneering's competitive position is its strongest dimension. The company maintains approximately 60% market share in ROV drill support, built over decades through fleet scale, technology investment, and global operational infrastructure. Deploying and servicing 250 work-class ROVs across 48 countries requires supply chain, personnel, and maintenance capabilities that cannot be replicated quickly.

The dual-use technology bridge between subsea energy and undersea defense is increasingly valuable. Competitors like TechnipFMC and Subsea 7 lack equivalent defense credentials. In ADTech, Oceaneering competes against numerous defense engineering firms but brings unique subsea domain expertise that defense-pure-play firms cannot match.

Manufactured Products faces competition from Aker Solutions and TechnipFMC in the subsea hardware market. The 15% backlog decline may reflect market share pressure or cyclical order timing; the projected increase in subsea tree awards (306 in 2026 vs. 190 in 2025) should provide clarity.

The competitive trajectory is positive: expanding defense positioning, maintained ROV market share, and proprietary autonomous technology (Freedom platform) create structural advantages that peer multiples may not fully reflect. However, at 19-20x normalized earnings, a material share of this positioning appears priced.

## Risk Assessment

### Identified Risks

1. **Oil Price Cyclicality (HIGH):** OII's 83% energy revenue exposure makes it highly sensitive to commodity prices. Management explicitly stated 2026 energy results will be back-half weighted as the oil market "remains oversupplied through the early part of the year." The IEA projects a record oil oversupply of ~4 million bpd in 2026 as OPEC+ output rebounds. If oil prices weaken materially, offshore FID delays could push energy segment results below guidance.

2. **Valuation Above All Analyst Targets (HIGH):** Trading above the consensus high of $35 with a Strong Sell rating in the mix creates asymmetric downside risk if results disappoint.

3. **ADTech Contract Dispute (MODERATE):** The unresolved dispute depressed Q1 2026 margins to 6% (vs. 13% normalized) and could produce additional charges. It is not currently in litigation, which limits worst-case scenarios, but introduces earnings unpredictability.

4. **China Unreliable Entity List (LOW-MODERATE):** In September 2025, China placed OII on its "Unreliable Entity List," effectively banning import/export and new investment in China. Management states it "does not currently expect a material impact," but it permanently closes the Chinese market.

5. **Insider Selling (MODERATE):** Broad-based selling with two officers liquidating entire positions is a qualitative negative. While vesting-related, the complete absence of any purchases and the extent of dispositions warrants monitoring.

6. **OPG Revenue Decline (MODERATE):** Guided "significant" decline adds downward pressure on consolidated results that ADTech growth must offset.

7. **U.S. Government Spending / DOGE Risks (MODERATE):** Approximately 12% of consolidated revenue comes from the U.S. Government (U.S. Navy, NASA). Budget uncertainty, continuing resolutions, or spending caps could impact the ADTech segment.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($42-48; +12% to +28%)

ADTech accelerates to $600M+ in FY2027 at 13-15% margins, offsetting energy normalization. Sustained high oil prices keep offshore activity robust, ROV utilization recovers to 68-70%, and the Manufactured Products backlog rebuilds as subsea tree awards materialize. The company generates $250M+ in annual FCF, initiates a dividend, and re-rates to 22-24x normalized earnings (~$2.50+ FY2027E) as the market assigns a defense premium. The stock trades to $45-48.

### Bear Case ($22-26; -30% to -41%)

Oil prices decline significantly, triggering offshore FID deferrals and reducing ROV utilization to 60%. Energy segment revenue declines 10-15%. The ADTech contract dispute escalates, consuming margin. The Manufactured Products backlog continues to deteriorate. Normalized EPS falls to $1.40-1.60, and the market applies a 16-18x multiple appropriate for a cyclically weakening OFS name. The stock trades to $22-28.

### Base Case ($35-40; -7% to +7%)

ADTech grows 20-25% but cannot fully offset moderate energy weakness. Full-year EBITDA comes in at the midpoint of guidance ($415M). Normalized EPS of $1.84 (consensus FY2026E) is achieved. The stock consolidates in the $35-40 range as the market debates whether the defense pivot justifies a premium multiple. Share repurchases provide modest support.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- ROV utilization drops below 60% in any reported quarter
- ADTech unfunded backlog declines below $500M
- FY2026 EBITDA guidance lowered below $360M

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which will provide second-half energy ramp visibility, ADTech contract dispute resolution progress, and updated Manufactured Products backlog trajectory.

## Conclusion

Oceaneering is a well-run company with a genuine competitive moat and a credible defense growth vector. The balance sheet is fortress-like, the ROV market position is dominant, and the ADTech pivot under Vice Admiral Merz's leadership has real substance — backed by $658M in unfunded defense backlog and 35% Q1 revenue growth.

The problem is price. At 19-20x normalized earnings after a 111% twelve-month rally, with the stock above every analyst target, the discount to peers is narrow. Broad-based insider selling, including two officers liquidating entirely, adds a qualitative negative. Energy segments face headwinds from declining OPG revenue, falling ROV utilization, and a shrinking Manufactured Products backlog.

The ADTech thesis warrants continued ownership for those already positioned. For new capital, the risk/reward at $37.54 is balanced at best. A pullback to $28-30 would create an attractive entry. **HOLD.**
