## Executive Summary

Range Resources delivered a strong Q1 2026 with adjusted EPS of $1.52, beating consensus by 19%, driven by elevated natural gas realizations ($4.84/mcfe including hedges), record NGL premiums ($4.41/bbl above Mont Belvieu), and the company's best gas basis differential in over a decade. Net debt fell 32% sequentially to $834M — the lowest level in the company's modern history — after redeeming $600M of 8.25% senior notes in January 2026. The balance sheet transformation is effectively complete.

The stock trades at approximately 9.6x forward earnings on consensus of $4.28 for FY2026 and $4.63 for FY2027, with current-year estimates revised upward 24% over the past 90 days. At ~1.0x the standardized measure of discounted future net cash flows ($9.6B, calculated using $3.39/mcf average NYMEX — materially below Q1 2026 realized prices), the market is offering the reserve base at a discount to intrinsic value in the current commodity environment. The primary risks are geographic concentration (100% Pennsylvania Marcellus), commodity price cyclicality, and $5.9B in take-or-pay transportation commitments that represent a large fixed cost base.

With U.S. LNG export capacity expanding meaningfully in 2026-2027 (EIA projects LNG exports growing from approximately 15.1 Bcf/d in 2025 to 18.6 Bcf/d in 2027, roughly 23% growth), Middle East supply disruptions creating structural tightness in global gas markets, and Range's cost structure enabling profitable production at gas prices well below current levels, the risk/reward skews positively from current levels.

## Company Overview

Range Resources is a pure-play Appalachian natural gas, NGL, and condensate producer focused exclusively on the Marcellus Shale in southwestern Pennsylvania. The company operates 1,579 gross producing wells (1,499 net) across approximately 879,000 gross acres with average daily production of 2.24 Bcfe/day (approximately 69% natural gas, with the remainder primarily NGLs and a small amount of oil). Proved reserves total 18.1 Tcfe (65% natural gas, 34% NGLs, 1% oil) with 71% proved developed. The company's asset base has an anticipated productive life in excess of 50 years based on total inventory depth, though proved reserve life at current production rates is approximately 22 years.

Range was an early mover in Marcellus development and has leveraged its single-basin focus to achieve some of the lowest unit costs in the industry ($0.13/mcfe direct operating expense, $2.07/mcfe total cash costs). The company markets its production through a diversified portfolio of pipeline and processing agreements that access Midwest, Gulf Coast, Southeast, Northeast, and international markets.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

Natural gas, NGL, and oil sales recovered from their 2024 trough to approximately $2.82B in 2025 as NYMEX averaged $3.43/mcf vs. $2.27 in 2024. (Note: GAAP total revenues and other income for 2025 were $3.12B, which includes derivative fair-value changes and other items beyond product sales.) Q1 2026 natural gas, NGL, and oil sales of $1.01B (+28% YoY) reflect the continuation of the commodity price recovery. Net income followed a similar path: $266M in 2024, $658M in 2025, and on a trajectory to exceed prior years at current commodity prices.

**Cash Flow Generation:**

Operating cash flow reached $1.17B in FY2025 (approximately 39% OCF margin) and $619M in Q1 2026 (including a $79.5M working-capital benefit; pre-working-capital cash flow was $545M). Free cash flow after capital expenditure ($139M in Q1) was approximately $480M in the quarter on a GAAP OCF basis — enabling simultaneous debt reduction ($384M), buybacks ($27M), and dividends ($24M). Annualizing Q1 results (which benefited from elevated Q1 gas prices), the company could generate approximately $1.5–1.8B in annual FCF, though this is sensitive to commodity-price seasonality. EIA's April 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook forecasts Henry Hub averaging $3.67/MMBtu for full-year 2026 and $3.59 for 2027, which would imply lower annualized FCF than Q1's run rate.

**Balance Sheet:**

The de-leveraging trajectory is striking. Net debt declined from approximately $1.7B in 2024 to $1.22B at year-end 2025 to $834M at Q1 2026 end. Remaining debt consists of $500M in 4.75% senior notes due 2030 and $334M drawn on the credit facility (borrowing base $3.0B, maturity October 2030). Interest expense dropped 36% YoY in Q1 to $0.09/mcfe. Total liabilities-to-equity stands at approximately 0.72x (net debt-to-equity is approximately 0.19x). Liquidity of approximately $1.5B on the undrawn revolver provides substantial cushion.

The retained earnings deficit of -$909M reflects legacy losses from prior commodity downturns but is closing rapidly given $2.98B in cumulative net income over 2022-2025.

**Capital Allocation:**

Management is deploying cash across three priorities: (1) debt reduction (largely accomplished), (2) share repurchases ($1.5B remaining authorization, representing ~15% of current market cap), and (3) a modest but growing dividend ($0.36/share, 0.9% yield, increased 12.5% in 2025). Capital expenditure guidance for 2026 is $650–700M, implying material excess free cash flow for shareholder returns if commodity prices remain supportive.

## Growth Analysis

Production guidance for 2026 is 2.35-2.40 Bcfe/day, representing 5-7% growth versus 2025. Range is not pursuing aggressive volume expansion but rather optimizing returns from its existing inventory of 27 million lateral feet of remaining drilling locations.

The primary growth driver is price, not volume. Three structural tailwinds support the commodity backdrop:

1. **U.S. LNG export capacity expansion:** EIA projects U.S. LNG exports growing from approximately 15.1 Bcf/d in 2025 to 17.0 Bcf/d in 2026 and 18.6 Bcf/d in 2027 — approximately 23% cumulative growth. This creates incremental demand for domestic gas that Appalachian producers are well-positioned to serve via Gulf Coast pipeline access.

2. **Global supply disruption:** Middle East conflict has damaged global LNG supply infrastructure, creating a structural supply gap that U.S. gas must fill. Infrastructure restoration takes years, making this a multi-year rather than transient dynamic.

3. **NGL pricing strength:** Range's NGL realizations hit record premiums in Q1 2026. Management raised NGL differential guidance to MB +$1.25-$2.50/bbl (from +$0.00-$1.00), implying approximately $160M in additional annual cash flow versus prior expectations. However, the full-year guidance range is well below Q1's $4.41/bbl premium, suggesting seasonal normalization is expected.

Current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 24% over 90 days ($3.45 → $4.28), reflecting analyst catch-up to the improved commodity environment. Next-year estimates rose 9.2% over the same period to $4.63.

The growth limitation is the inherent maturity of the asset base — Range won't deliver 20%+ volume growth. But given the operational leverage to pricing (37% cash margin expansion in Q1 YoY on modest production growth), the earnings growth trajectory is compelling in the current commodity environment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $41.17, RRC trades at approximately 9.6x forward P/E on consensus $4.28 FY2026E and 8.9x on $4.63 FY2027E. Against peer medians of 16.4x P/E, the stock carries a significant discount, partially justified by its gas-weighted production mix (more volatile than oil-weighted peers) and geographic concentration.

Several factors suggest the discount is too large:

- **Standardized measure discount:** The standardized measure of discounted future net cash flows was $9.6B at year-end 2025, calculated using 12-month average NYMEX of $3.39/mcf. Range's pre-tax PV-10 was $11.6B. At Q1 2026 realized prices (~$4.97/mcf NYMEX average), the intrinsic value of reserves would be higher, though a precise estimate requires sensitivity analysis accounting for future development costs, hedges, basis differentials, and taxes. The market cap of $9.7B trading at approximately 1.0x the standardized measure provides a useful reference point, though PV-10 and standardized measure are reserve valuation metrics rather than direct equity-value measures.

- **FCF yield:** At Q1 annualized FCF of approximately $1.5–1.8B (commodity-price dependent), the implied 15–19% FCF yield is among the highest in the peer group, though sustainability depends on gas prices remaining near Q1 levels. At EIA's lower forward price assumptions (~$3.67/MMBtu for 2026), annualized FCF would be meaningfully lower.

- **Buyback support:** $1.5B remaining repurchase authorization (15% of market cap) provides a floor and EPS accretion mechanism.

- **EV/EBITDA:** Using Q1 2026 annualized EBITDA of approximately $2.4B and current EV of approximately $10.5B ($9.7B market cap + $0.8B net debt), the stock trades at ~4.4x EV/EBITDA — inexpensive by historical and peer standards.

The forward P/E of 9.6x combined with 24% upward estimate revisions over 90 days and a 19% Q1 earnings beat represents an attractive entry point. Analyst consensus targets suggest approximately 14% upside from current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Range's competitive advantages are durable and difficult to replicate:

1. **Cost leadership:** Total cash costs of $2.07/mcfe are among the lowest in the Appalachian basin, providing profitable production at gas prices well below current levels. The 100% drilling success rate over three consecutive years reflects operational maturity.

2. **NGL premium pricing:** Consistently realizing premiums above Mont Belvieu on NGLs suggests superior product quality and market access. The record $4.41/bbl premium in Q1 2026 reflects a structural advantage in market positioning, though management's full-year guidance of MB +$1.25-$2.50/bbl implies normalization from Q1 peaks.

3. **Market access diversity:** The $5.9B in transportation commitments, while a fixed cost burden, secure pipeline capacity that competitors lack. This infrastructure backbone enables premium pricing to multiple demand centers.

4. **Reserve depth and inventory:** 27 million lateral feet of remaining inventory and an asset base with an anticipated productive life in excess of 50 years provide development optionality without the need for acquisitions.

Against peers: EQT is larger but has execution variability. Antero Resources (AR) trades at 19.2x P/E — a significant premium to RRC — with a similar Appalachian focus but less efficient operations. SM Energy (5.5x P/E) is cheaper but oil-weighted and smaller. Matador (10.3x) is a close comparable in valuation terms.

Range's competitive position is stable-to-improving as LNG export capacity additions create structural demand growth for Appalachian gas, where Range has both the lowest-cost production base and the best-positioned marketing infrastructure.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (High Impact, Moderate Probability):**
Revenue ranged from approximately $2.3B to $5.3B over 2022-2024 depending on pricing. A reversion of NYMEX to $2.50/mcf would compress earnings by 50%+ and reduce FCF materially. EIA's April 2026 STEO forecasts Henry Hub averaging $3.67/MMBtu in 2026 and $3.59 in 2027, with Q2/Q3 2026 expected around $3.10/MMBtu — materially below Q1 2026 levels. The structural demand backdrop (LNG exports, Middle East disruption) supports above-average prices, but sustained $4.50+/mcf gas is not guaranteed by official forecasts.

**Geographic Concentration (Moderate Impact, Low-Moderate Probability):**
100% Pennsylvania exposure creates vulnerability to state-specific regulation. The Cecil Township 2,500-ft setback ordinance (challenged by RRC) and the statewide citizen petition for expanded setbacks (accepted for rulemaking review in December 2025) represent escalating regulatory risk. A statewide hydraulic fracturing ban would be existential, though this scenario remains low-probability given Pennsylvania's economic dependence on the industry.

**Transportation Commitments (Moderate Impact, Low Probability of Disruption):**
$5.9B in total commitments represent a large fixed cost obligation. Actual 2025 transportation, gathering, processing, and compression expense was $1.223B ($1.50/mcfe), with 2026 guidance of $1.55–$1.60/mcfe. At current production and pricing, these are serviceable and provide valuable market access. The risk materializes if production declines significantly or pipeline access is disrupted for extended periods.

**Third-Party Processing Dependency:**
Range relies on third-party processing plants for NGL extraction. Disruption of these facilities could materially affect the company's ability to market and deliver natural gas production, with no control over restoration timeline. This is mitigated by diversified marketing but remains a concentration risk.

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term ATM IV of 34.0% is approximately in line with 1-year historical vol (32.5%), suggesting the options market is pricing normal uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced (0.5%), and the P/C OI ratio declined from 0.44 to 0.20 over the past week — a mild bullish shift as put holders closed positions. Unusual put activity at the $39 strike for June 2026 (5.3x volume/OI) suggests some institutional hedging of the downside, but the overall options profile is benign. IV at the 12th percentile of its range indicates options are cheap relative to recent history.

## Conclusion

Range Resources is a well-managed, low-cost gas producer at an attractive valuation (9.6x forward P/E, ~1.0x standardized measure, compelling FCF yield at current commodity prices) benefiting from structural demand growth in U.S. LNG exports and temporary supply disruption in global gas markets. The balance sheet transformation is essentially complete ($834M net debt, down from $1.7B+ in 2024), freeing capital for shareholder returns via a $1.5B buyback authorization.

The improving fundamental trajectory over the past three months reflects genuine operational and pricing improvement (Q1 beat, debt reduction, estimate revisions) rather than multiple expansion. The stock's appreciation from its 52-week low of $32.60 is modest relative to the fundamental improvement, and the forward P/E remains at a significant discount to the peer median.

The primary near-term risk is commodity price sensitivity to geopolitical developments. If global energy supply disruptions moderate, gas and LNG prices could decline, compressing the short-term earnings outlook. EIA's April 2026 forward outlook ($3.67/MMBtu for 2026, $3.59 for 2027) is materially below Q1 2026 realized prices, and the bull case depends on prices remaining above these official forecasts. Range's cost structure ($2.07/mcfe cash costs) ensures profitability even at significantly lower commodity prices, and the LNG export infrastructure buildout is a multi-year structural tailwind independent of any single geopolitical event.

I reiterate BUY with a base case target of $50 (21% upside), appropriate position sizing of 2-3% given commodity sensitivity, and clear exit criteria tied to gas pricing and estimate revision direction.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The valuation case relies on commodity prices remaining above EIA's April 2026 forward outlook ($3.67/MMBtu for 2026); if gas prices revert toward EIA forecasts, the FCF yield and earnings trajectory supporting the target price would be materially lower.
