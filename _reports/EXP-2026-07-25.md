**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eagle Materials is a U.S.-only, low-cost producer of cement, gypsum wallboard, aggregates, and recycled paperboard operating 70+ facilities across 21 states. The business earns exceptional returns for a cyclical materials company (roughly 18% net margin and ~29% ROE), anchored by owned, long-life raw-material reserves located near plants, which minimizes input and freight costs, and by the regional oligopoly economics of cement (expensive to transport, so local plants hold pricing power). The current investment debate centers on a two-speed end market: infrastructure-driven cement demand (volumes +7-9% on IIJA and state DOT spending) is partly offsetting a residential-led decline in wallboard (volume -8%, price -3%), the segment that historically carried ~38-40% operating margins.

The stock trades at ~16.2x the current-year consensus EPS of $12.88 and ~16.2x trailing EPS of $13.16, a wide discount to the aggregates peers Martin Marietta (35x), Vulcan (33x), and CRH (19x). That gap is partly justified: Eagle's mix carries meaningful residential exposure the pure-aggregates names lack, its pricing power is currently muted (cement net price flat-to-down while volumes grow, wallboard price falling), and it just levered up 43% year-over-year with a $750M November 2025 notes issuance. Segment operating margins compressed on both sides (cement 30%→28%, wallboard 40%→38%) as cost inflation outran price. The near-term earnings direction is soft: consensus estimates have drifted down 1.0-1.2% over the past 30 days even as the 90-day trend remains modestly positive.

Our recommendation is HOLD. The balance-sheet resilience is genuine (investment-grade, zero revolver draw, $418M cash, full covenant compliance; the "distress zone" Altman Z-Score is a mechanical artifact of a capital-intensive asset base), and the buyback is shrinking the share count meaningfully. But there is no dated catalyst to close the valuation gap, wallboard weakness has an uncertain recovery timeline tied to mortgage rates, and the higher-for-longer rate path that pressures housing also raises the cost of Eagle's newly larger debt stack. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the mid-$180s, confirmed wallboard price/volume stabilization, or a productive deployment of the cash reserve.

## Company Overview

Eagle operates two sectors across four reportable segments:

**Heavy Materials.** *Cement* is the largest and most profitable segment: eight wholly-owned plants plus a 50% JV (Texas Lehigh, Buda, TX), two slag grinding facilities, and 30+ distribution terminals concentrated in the U.S. heartland (TX, CA, CO, IL, MO, NV). *Concrete & Aggregates* comprises 25+ readymix plants and seven aggregates plants (~9M tons capacity) and is the company's acquisition-led growth vehicle.

**Light Materials.** *Gypsum Wallboard* runs five plants distributed nationally (ex-Northeast). *Recycled Paperboard* is a single mill supplying Eagle's own wallboard operations plus third parties under long-term agreements.

The moat is a low-cost position built on owned raw-material reserves (limestone, gypsum, aggregates) near the plants, which compresses both input and freight costs. Because cement is costly to ship, local plants enjoy regional pricing power. Eagle has been public since 1994 and has a long record of disciplined capital return, having repurchased roughly 52.6M of 55.9M authorized shares over three decades. The fiscal year ends March 31, so the most recent 10-K covers FY2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale and profitability.** FY2026 revenue annualizes to roughly $2.3B (nine-month revenue $1.83B, +2% YoY). Net margin sits near 18% and ROE near 29%, elite figures for a cyclical materials producer and evidence the low-cost, pricing-disciplined model is intact even in a soft patch. Nine-month FY2026 net earnings were $363.6M (-8% YoY), diluted EPS $11.21 versus $11.75 a year earlier. Operating cash flow was $512M for nine months (+5% YoY), comfortably funding capex and buybacks.

**Segment divergence — the central story.**
- *Cement:* volumes +7-9% YoY on infrastructure spending, revenue +7% to $1.05B (9mo), but operating margin compressed from 30% to 28% on higher freight, purchased raw materials, and labor/fixed costs. Net sales price was roughly flat (-1%).
- *Gypsum Wallboard:* revenue -10%, volume -8%, price -3% (9mo); operating earnings -18% to $221M, margin down from ~40% to 38%. Weak residential construction (elevated mortgage rates, builder pullback) is the driver, and management explicitly flags the recovery timing as uncertain.
- *Concrete & Aggregates:* revenue +21% (largely from acquisitions), operating earnings surged off a small base; the strategic growth engine.
- *Recycled Paperboard:* revenue down but margins up (24% in Q3) as recycled fiber (OCC) input costs fell ~$13.9M for nine months, a natural hedge within Light Materials.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt rose from $1.25B (Mar 2025) to $1.785B (Dec 2025) following a $750M 5.000% senior notes issuance on November 13, 2025. Debt-to-cap is 54.4%, net-debt-to-cap 47.8%. The low Altman Z-Score (~1.7) that screens flag as "distress" is a false positive: it is driven by the capital-intensive asset base (low asset turnover mechanically depresses the Z-Score) and a deliberate leverage-up. Eagle is investment-grade, was in full compliance with covenants (leverage ratio ≤3.5x, interest coverage ≥2.5x), had zero drawn on its $750M revolver, and held $418M cash at December 31, 2025. The company raised the notes but is sitting on the cash, a posture pointing to pre-funding the capex program, a potential acquisition, or continued buybacks (or refinancing the $750M 2.5% notes due 2031).

**Interest expense is the item to watch.** Net interest expense rose 14% for nine months. The new 5% notes and larger term loan add ~$30M+ of annual interest versus two years ago. Interest capitalization tied to the Mountain Cement project is currently masking part of the P&L impact; that reverses once the project is placed in service. The $285M term loan is floating-rate, so a 100bp move adds ~$2.9M of annual expense.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks totaled $310M over nine months (1.4M shares at ~$221 average), cutting the count from ~33.3M to 31.6M. The dividend is modest ($0.25/quarter, ~0.5% yield). Capex is guided to $430-450M for FY2026, nearly double the prior ~$200M pace, funding the Mountain Cement (Laramie, WY) expansion and the Oklahoma wallboard modernization.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth profile is bifurcated and, on a consolidated basis, modest. Nine-month revenue grew only 2% and net earnings fell 8% as wallboard weakness offset cement volume gains. The durable growth levers are: (1) IIJA and state DOT infrastructure spending, which still has substantial unspent funds and supports cement volumes into FY2027; (2) tuck-in aggregates M&A (Bullskin Stone & Lime ~$149.9M in Jan 2025; Northern Kentucky aggregates ~$24.9M in Aug 2024), which is compounding the Concrete & Aggregates line at 20%+; and (3) the buyback, which provides a durable per-share tailwind, with the ~5% annual share-count reduction cushioning flat-to-down net earnings into EPS growth.

The swing factor is wallboard. A residential recovery on falling mortgage rates would flip the largest current drag into a tailwind, given wallboard's high incremental margins. But with housing demand indicators soft and mortgage rates elevated, management offers no timeline. The structural excess nameplate capacity in the U.S. wallboard industry caps how much pricing power returns even when volumes recover.

Estimate revisions are consistent with this balanced read. The current-year consensus of $12.88 is up 1.9% over 90 days but down 1.0% over 30 days; next-year $14.51 is up only 0.5% over 90 days and down 1.2% over 30 days. This is well below the >30% revision velocity that would signal a re-rating; the business is grinding sideways with a soft near-term tilt. The Q4 FY26 print (period ending March 31, 2026) beat by 20.6%, but the prior two quarters missed by 3.8% and 7.8%, a 2-beat, 2-miss track record over four quarters that argues for measured confidence in forward estimates rather than a durable beat pattern.

## Valuation Assessment

At $213.55, EXP trades at ~16.2x trailing EPS ($13.16) and ~16.6x the current-year consensus ($12.88), or ~14.7x the next-year $14.51 estimate. That is a wide discount to the Construction Materials peer set: Martin Marietta 35.4x, Vulcan 33.2x, Knife River 29.6x, CRH 19.4x, peer-median 33.2x. The dividend yield of ~0.5% roughly matches MLM/VMC but the capital return runs primarily through buybacks.

The discount is partly warranted and partly a value opportunity, and distinguishing the two is the crux of the call. Warranted: the aggregates pure-plays (MLM, VMC) carry no residential-wallboard exposure and have demonstrated more consistent pricing power, justifying a structural premium; Eagle's current margin compression (both cement and wallboard down 2pp) and flat-to-negative pricing signal that its own pricing power is temporarily muted; and the 43% debt build raises the equity's rate sensitivity. Not warranted: at 14.7x next-year earnings for a ~29% ROE, ~18% net-margin business with a durable buyback, the absolute multiple is undemanding, and the analyst consensus mean target of $226 (median $225) sits ~5-6% above spot with a $240 high.

We resist over-rewarding the low multiple. The equity lessons here are pointed: cheapness in Materials has been a persistent value trap in this scoring regime, and the sector carries structurally negative alpha, so a low P/E is not itself a buy signal. What would justify a high valuation score is an identified catalyst to close the gap (a wallboard recovery, a pricing recovery, or an accretive deployment of the cash reserve), and none of those is dated. We land at a mid valuation score: cheap enough to limit downside, without a mispricing thesis strong enough to argue for a re-rating today. Consensus targets function as a soft ceiling rather than a floor here, because Eagle's estimates are drifting down near-term rather than rising.

## Competitive Landscape

Eagle's position is stable-to-slightly-improving in Heavy Materials and stable-to-pressured in Light Materials. In cement, the regional oligopoly and low-cost reserve base insulate it from larger global players (Holcim, Heidelberg, CRH) and from imports; +7-9% volume growth suggests it is at least holding regional share as infrastructure demand rises, though flat pricing indicates it is not pushing price aggressively. In aggregates, the acquisition strategy is expanding the footprint at attractive tuck-in prices, a positioning gain from a small base.

In wallboard, the picture is defensive. Structural industry overcapacity means competition is largely on price, and Eagle's -3% price with -8% volume reflects the whole industry's residential exposure rather than idiosyncratic share loss. The recycled-paperboard integration is a modest cost advantage that is currently a bright spot as OCC prices fall. On balance, the moat (owned reserves, low-cost freight-advantaged network) is intact, but the pricing power that historically converted cost inflation into stable margins is temporarily muted, a trajectory to monitor rather than a structural erosion.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Residential/wallboard cyclicality** — the most active near-term risk. Wallboard has been a ~38-40% margin contributor; continued volume and price weakness is the key earnings swing factor, with an uncertain recovery tied to mortgage rates.
2. **Wallboard oversupply** — structural excess nameplate capacity caps pricing power on any recovery.
3. **Cement margin compression** — costs (freight, purchased raw materials, labor) are outpacing pricing; whether Eagle reasserts price in FY2027 is unresolved.
4. **Leverage and rate sensitivity (newly elevated)** — debt up 43% YoY to $1.785B; the floating-rate term loan and rising interest expense are a headwind, and interest capitalization currently masks part of the P&L drag until Mountain Cement is in service.
5. **Infrastructure funding dependency** — a large share of cement demand rides on IIJA and state DOT appropriations, subject to shutdown/budget risk.
6. **Capital-project execution** — capex nearly doubling for Mountain Cement and Oklahoma raises overrun/commissioning risk.
7. **GHG/environmental regulation** — cement kilns are CO2-intensive and could be disproportionately affected by future regulation.
8. **Governance** — classified board, no action by written consent, blank-check preferred, and Delaware forum-selection are entrenchment features, partly offset by the strong capital-return record.

The cash reserve ($418M with zero revolver draw) both de-risks the balance sheet and introduces capital-deployment uncertainty: a large, richly-priced acquisition would be the negative surprise, while continued disciplined buybacks or an accretive tuck-in would be positive.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 42.0% versus 34.9% one-year historical volatility, rich by ~7pp, at the 64th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (42.0% near-term vs 38.4% at six months), which typically signals the market is pricing an imminent event; the next earnings print is the likely driver. Put/call skew is balanced (0.6% near-term, turning modestly negative -3.1% to -3.3% at 3-6 months), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.5%. IV has climbed 6.2pp since mid-June while the stock fell ~4.9%, consistent with rising uncertainty into the next report rather than directional put demand. Short interest is moderate at 8.3% of float (4.1 days to cover), not a crowded or squeeze-prone name. The net read: the options market prices event risk around the coming quarter but is not signaling a strong directional bet, aligning with our balanced fundamental view. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors patience for a post-print entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$255-290).** Mortgage rates ease, residential construction recovers, and wallboard volumes and price recover, flipping the largest current drag into a high-margin tailwind. Cement pricing reasserts as infrastructure demand tightens the market, reversing the 2pp margin compression. The cash reserve funds an accretive cement acquisition (plants come available infrequently) or continued buybacks at attractive prices, and Mountain Cement/Oklahoma projects come online lifting capacity and returns. On mid-cycle earnings power of ~$16-18 and a re-rating toward 15-16x, the stock reaches $255-290. This is roughly consistent with the sector premium narrowing modestly rather than closing.

**Bear case (~$150-165, ~23-30% downside).** Housing weakness persists into FY2027 and wallboard price/volume continue to fall; cement volume growth decelerates as IIJA funds are exhausted and appropriations tighten; margin compression continues as cost inflation outruns muted pricing. Rising interest expense (compounded once capitalization reverses) and a higher-for-longer rate path pressure both earnings and the multiple. A large, richly-priced acquisition dilutes returns. Earnings drift toward ~$11-12 and the multiple compresses to ~13-14x, taking the stock to $150-165.

**Base case (~$225).** Cement volume strength and the buyback offset wallboard drag; consolidated earnings grow low-single-digits with per-share support from a shrinking count. The stock holds a mid-teens forward multiple, tracking the analyst consensus mean/median of $225-226. This reflects roughly $14.50 of next-year EPS at ~15.5x plus modest buyback accretion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The setup is genuinely balanced: an investment-grade, high-return compounder with a durable buyback and cement tailwind, offset by wallboard weakness, muted pricing power, rising interest expense, and no dated catalyst to force a re-rating. The stock is neither cheap enough to demand ownership nor impaired enough to avoid.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Wallboard price and volume stabilize or turn positive for two consecutive quarters, or mortgage rates fall materially with confirming builder-demand data.
- Cement net sales price re-accelerates to +3% or better while volumes hold, reversing the margin compression.
- A pullback toward the mid-$180s (roughly 13x next-year EPS) restoring asymmetric upside, or a clearly accretive deployment of the cash reserve at sensible multiples.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Wallboard volume decline accelerates beyond -10% or a further leg down in price, signaling a deeper residential downturn.
- Consolidated operating margin compresses another 200bp+ as pricing power fails to reassert, alongside a large debt-funded acquisition at >12x EBITDA.
- A credit-rating downgrade or covenant pressure raising borrowing costs materially.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly earnings release (fiscal Q1 FY27, expected late July/early August) is the natural checkpoint; the options market is pricing a ±9.5% move into it, and the segment cadence (wallboard trajectory, cement pricing, capex execution) will resolve much of the current uncertainty.

## Conclusion

Eagle Materials is a high-quality, low-cost construction-materials producer earning ~29% ROE and ~18% net margins, valued at a substantial discount to its aggregates peers (14.7x next-year earnings versus a 33x peer median). That discount reflects real residential-wallboard exposure, currently muted pricing power compressing segment margins, and a deliberate 43% debt build, set against a balance sheet that is investment-grade and cash-rich despite screening as "distressed" on a mechanical Z-Score. The buyback and infrastructure-driven cement volumes provide durable support, but near-term estimate revisions are drifting down and no dated catalyst exists to close the valuation gap. We rate EXP a HOLD, biased to accumulate on weakness toward the mid-$180s, on confirmed wallboard stabilization, or on an accretive use of the cash reserve. The score history (5.6 → 6.7 → 5.7) reflects this oscillation around a fair-value zone; today's data (soft 30-day revisions, ongoing wallboard weakness, and rich near-term IV) confirms the balanced posture rather than pointing to a decisive move.
