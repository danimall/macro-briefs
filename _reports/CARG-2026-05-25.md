## Executive Summary

CarGurus is the #1 most-visited automotive shopping site in the U.S. (per Similarweb), operating a capital-light, two-sided marketplace that connects consumers with dealers. After completing the wind-down of its money-losing CarOffer wholesale business in December 2025, CarGurus is now primarily a marketplace platform generating 93% gross margins, 35% adjusted EBITDA margins, and ~29% FCF margins on 14-15% revenue growth. The stock trades at $28.24, down 28% from its 52-week high of $39.42, creating meaningful valuation asymmetry against a business that generated $266M in FCF in 2025 (using the company's definition, which subtracts both PPE and capitalized website development costs).

The sell-off was triggered by post-Q1 2026 earnings pressure (stock fell from $38 to $28 in two weeks despite beating on revenue and EBITDA), driven by a $19.7M impairment charge (primarily a lease impairment on the company's 121 First Street office), guidance implying margin compression from AI investments, and rapidly declining cash balances from aggressive buybacks. At current levels, the stock trades at approximately 9.5x trailing FCF and 11.3x forward P/E (on FY26 estimates of $2.51), which is below what a 15%-growth, 93%-gross-margin marketplace business warrants. Management has retired 29% of outstanding shares since December 2022, and a director made a $1M open-market purchase in March 2026 at $32.50—both signaling conviction that the stock is undervalued.

Key risks include AI-driven search disruption (Google/LLMs potentially disintermediating automotive marketplaces), the absence of a permanent CFO for over a year, and macro sensitivity if tariffs or interest rates materially reduce vehicle transactions. These risks are real but appear largely priced at the current multiple.

## Company Overview

CarGurus was founded in 2006 by Langley Steinert (TripAdvisor co-founder) and operates the largest U.S. online automotive marketplace by traffic. The platform uses proprietary pricing algorithms (Instant Market Value) to rate deals for consumers, creating a trust-based marketplace that drives high engagement and repeat visits.

**Revenue model:** Dealer subscription fees (tiered Listings packages), advertising from OEMs, and financing partnerships (revenue from funded loans). The business is subscription-based with month-to-month contracts and 30-day cancellation terms.

**Key metrics (Q1 2026):**
- 34,596 total paying dealers (+7% YoY)
- Consolidated QARSD (quarterly average revenue per subscribing dealer): $6,647 (+8% YoY)
- 36.6M average monthly unique users in the U.S. (+25% YoY)
- Revenue: $244M (+15% YoY)

**Geographic mix:** ~90% U.S., ~10% international (U.K. and Canada), with international growing faster (QARSD +19% YoY).

### Balance Sheet

The low cash balance is a function of aggressive capital return, not financial stress. Annual FCF of ~$266M replenishes cash rapidly, and the revolver provides ample liquidity.

### Capital Return
The buyback program is the dominant capital allocation story:
- Total repurchased since December 2022: ~29% of shares outstanding
- 2025: $350M at avg $32.65/share
- Q1 2026: $175M under the $250M 2026 authorization ($75M remaining as of March 31, 2026)
- New $250M authorization in February 2026; however, only $75M remained at quarter-end, and the program does not obligate the company to repurchase any minimum amount
- At current prices ($28), management is buying back stock below their recent average purchase price

This intensity signals management's view that the stock is materially undervalued. However, the pace of Q1 buybacks is unlikely to be sustained at an annualized rate without a new authorization. If a new program is approved, the buyback yield would remain a significant component of total shareholder returns.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term (2026):** Management guided 10-13% revenue growth with 1.5-2.5% margin decline (deliberate investment in AI products and growth). This translates to ~$1.0B revenue and ~$296-305M adjusted EBITDA.

**Medium-term drivers:**
1. **QARSD expansion** — U.S. ARPU has grown from $6,532 to $7,938 (+22%) in two years, and management continues to release new products (LeadAI, PriceVantage, Digital Deal) that create upsell opportunities
2. **International growth** — International QARSD growing 19% with dealer count +17% represents a faster-growing but smaller segment with significant runway
3. **New products** — CG Discover (GenAI shopping assistant), Dealership Mode, and Audience Targeting represent incremental monetization of existing traffic
4. **Advertising** — OEM advertising at scale against 36.6M monthly U.S. uniques is underpenetrated

**Long-term TAM:** U.S. used vehicle transactions are ~40M annually; new vehicle transactions ~16M. Even at current penetration, revenue per transaction facilitated remains well below traditional advertising spend per vehicle sold ($500-1,000).

Consensus estimates show $2.51 EPS for FY2026 and $2.92 for FY2027 (+16% growth). The estimate revision trend is modestly positive: FY2027 estimates have risen +3.1% over 90 days.

## Valuation Assessment

At $28.24 with approximately 90M total common shares outstanding (75.7M Class A + 14.2M Class B as of March 31, 2026), the market cap is approximately $2.54B.

**Absolute metrics:**
- Forward P/E (FY26): 11.3x ($2.51 consensus)
- Forward P/E (FY27): 9.7x ($2.92 consensus)
- EV/EBITDA (trailing): ~7.7x ($319M adj. EBITDA, EV ≈ $2.54B - $72M cash = $2.47B)
- Price/FCF (trailing): ~9.5x ($266M company-defined FCF)
- Price/Revenue: ~2.8x

**Peer comparison:**
The peer median P/E is 10.7x, but this cohort (Yelp at 9.9x, ZoomInfo at 8.9x, QuinStreet at 10.7x) represents lower-growth, lower-margin businesses. CarGurus at 11.3x forward P/E with 15% growth and 29% FCF margins is trading in line with companies growing at 0-5% with significantly lower margins. The appropriate peer set for a 93% gross margin marketplace growing 15% annually is closer to vertical SaaS companies trading at 20-30x forward earnings.

**Why the market is wrong:** The stock is being valued as if CarOffer's legacy still weighs on economics. Post-wind-down, CarGurus is a fundamentally higher-quality business than what existed 18 months ago. The market also appears to be pricing meaningful AI disruption risk at a discount that overstates the near-term probability of search disintermediation in a considered-purchase category (cars are not impulse buys that will shift to AI chatbots immediately).

At 11.3x forward P/E for a 15%-growth, debt-free, 29% FCF margin business with consistent execution, the stock offers clear upside to any reasonable intrinsic value estimate.

## Competitive Landscape

CarGurus holds the #1 position in U.S. automotive marketplace traffic (per company-cited Similarweb data), which creates compounding network effects: more consumers attract more dealers, which creates more inventory, which attracts more consumers. This flywheel is difficult to disrupt incrementally. The company also claims the largest inventory selection and dealer network among major online automotive marketplaces (based on third-party datasets including Joreca and YipitData).

**Key competitive dynamics:**
- vs. Cars.com: CarGurus has meaningfully higher traffic and a differentiated consumer value proposition (deal ratings/transparency vs. vanilla listings)
- vs. AutoTrader/Cox: Privately held with integrated solutions; less price-transparent model
- vs. Amazon Autos: New entrant with massive distribution but limited automotive-specific trust/data; unclear monetization model with dealers
- vs. AI search: The genuine medium-term threat — if LLMs can aggregate automotive inventory and pricing without sending traffic to CarGurus

The competitive position is stable to slightly improving based on traffic growth (+25% YoY U.S. unique users in Q1 2026) and dealer count expansion. International growth at 14-17% demonstrates the model replicates well in new geographies.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. AI Search Disruption (Moderate-High, 2-3 year timeline):**
If Google AI Overviews or competing LLMs begin providing car recommendations with pricing and inventory data directly in search results, CarGurus' traffic acquisition model is threatened. The company is investing in its own AI (CG Discover), but the risk is structural. Mitigant: car purchasing remains a high-consideration, multi-step process where trust and comprehensive inventory matter more than quick answers.

**2. Macro/Auto Industry Cyclicality (Moderate):**
Management explicitly excluded "macro-level industry issues" from 2026 guidance. Rising interest rates (mortgage-level auto loan rates) and potential tariff-driven vehicle price increases could reduce transaction volumes. However, CarGurus may paradoxically benefit from price-conscious consumer behavior (more comparison shopping = more platform usage).

**3. Missing CFO (Governance Gap):**
CEO Jason Trevisan has served as interim principal financial officer since March 2025—over 14 months. This is atypical for a $2.5B public company and creates key-person risk. The lack of a dedicated CFO may signal either poor recruiting outcomes or intentional cost avoidance.

**4. Impairment Charge ($19.7M in Q1 2026):**
The Q1 2026 10-Q discloses $19.2M in operating-expense impairments related to the company's 121 First Street lease (which it intends to sublease), comprising $14.7M for the operating lease right-of-use asset and $4.5M for leasehold improvements and furniture/fixtures due to lower anticipated rental recovery rates. An additional $0.5M in cost-of-revenue impairments related to capitalized website development costs for technology the company decided to stop investing in. These are non-cash charges and do not reflect operational deterioration.

**5. Buyback Authorization Constraints:**
Only $75M remained on the 2026 repurchase authorization as of March 31, 2026. Continuation of aggressive buybacks would require a new board authorization, which is not guaranteed.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($37-43, +31% to +52%)
- Market re-rates post-CarOffer to reflect true marketplace economics (20x forward P/E on $2.51 = $50; even 15x = $38)
- Buybacks provide mechanical EPS growth acceleration (assuming new authorization)
- AI products drive QARSD expansion above the 8% run rate
- International growth accelerates as unit economics proven in U.K./Canada
- CFO hire removes governance overhang

### Bear Case ($22-24, -15% to -22%)
- AI search disruption materializes faster than expected, with Google/Amazon capturing automotive search intent
- Auto industry macro shock (tariff-driven transaction volume decline, dealer distress, cancellation waves)
- QARSD growth decelerates as dealers consolidate or push back on pricing
- Cash burn from buybacks at higher prices proves poorly timed if business deteriorates
- Permanent multiple compression if market views AI disruption as existential

## Conclusion

CarGurus at $28.24 represents a high-quality marketplace business trading at a cyclical-value-company multiple. The post-CarOffer clean business generates 93% gross margins, ~29% FCF margins, and grows at 14-15% annually with minimal capital requirements. A sophisticated board member (Stephen Kaufer, TripAdvisor co-founder) made a $1M open-market purchase at $32.50—$4.26 above today's price.

The stock was rejected from $38-39 on post-earnings selling driven by a non-cash lease impairment charge and margin guidance that reflects growth investment, not deterioration. The 28% drawdown from the 52-week high creates a specific mispricing: the market is treating AI disruption risk and a missing CFO as severe impairments to a business that just delivered 15% growth, beat EBITDA estimates, and continues to take traffic share. At ~9.5x trailing FCF for a growing, debt-free, #1 market position with buyback support, the asymmetry favors buyers. Position sizing should be moderate (2-3%) given the AI disruption tail risk and uncertainty around future buyback authorizations.
