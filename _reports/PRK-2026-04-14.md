## Executive Summary

Park National Corporation is a 116-year-old Ohio-based community banking franchise that has delivered strong operating results: 22% YoY EPS growth through Q3 2025, NIM expansion to 4.70%, and efficiency improvement to 57%. The February 2026 acquisition of First Citizens Bancshares (Tennessee) adds $2.6B in assets and expands the franchise into a fifth state, but pushes Park over the $10B asset threshold, triggering Durbin Amendment interchange caps, CFPB supervision, and higher FDIC assessments.

The stock trades at ~15.6x trailing earnings vs. an 11.5x peer median — a 35% premium. Superior profitability metrics (ROATE 16.3%, efficiency ratio 57%) have supported this premium, but the $10B regulatory cliff will erode $10-20M+ of annual pre-tax income beginning in 2026-2027, directly narrowing the performance gap that justifies the valuation spread. A nonperforming loan spike to 1.13% of loans, driven by a $22.2M downgrade in a niche non-bank consumer finance portfolio, raises questions about credit quality in a deteriorating macro environment.

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress (50.3% near-term IV vs. 25.5% HV, in backwardation), likely reflecting earnings uncertainty and integration risk. Analyst consensus is uniformly Hold with a tight $171.50-$183.00 price target range. The risk/reward is balanced but skews modestly negative given the structural headwinds.

## Company Overview

Park National Corporation operates through Park National Bank, serving small and medium-sized communities across Ohio (24 counties), North Carolina (5 counties), South Carolina (4 counties), Kentucky (1 county), and — following the February 2026 acquisition — Tennessee. The company operates 100+ branches with 1,731 associates.

**Business segments:**
- Commercial banking: $3.45B (42.9% of loans) — CRE ($2.2B), C&I ($1.2B), aircraft financing via Scope Aircraft Finance ($339M), and a $274M national non-bank consumer finance lending book
- Consumer banking: $1.8B (22.6%) — dominated by $1.6B in indirect auto lending
- Residential RE & construction: $2.8B (34.5%)
- Wealth management: ~$9.3B average AUM, $33.9M fee income (9M 2025)

Post-acquisition total assets exceed $10B, deposits approximately $10.5B, and loans approximately $9.6B. Park issued ~2.0M new shares (12% dilution) to fund the all-stock First Citizens deal valued at $324.1M.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings trajectory through Q3 2025:**

| Metric | 9M 2025 | 9M 2024 | Change |
|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| Net Interest Income | $324.4M | $294.6M | +10.1% |
| Other Income | $88.5M | $91.5M | -3.3% |
| Net Income | $137.4M | $112.8M | +21.8% |
| Diluted EPS | $8.48 | $6.95 | +22.0% |

Full-year 2025 net income reached $180.1M, recovering strongly from a 2023 trough of $126.7M. The NIM expanded 33bp YoY to 4.70% as average loan yields rose 20bp to 6.32% while cost of interest-bearing deposits fell 26bp to 1.74%. The efficiency ratio improved 440bp to 57.0%.

CET1 at 13.91% sits well above the 6.50% well-capitalized threshold — nearly 750bp of excess capital. Park eliminated all $190M of subordinated debt in September 2025, saving ~$9.4M in annual interest expense. Available liquidity stands at $1.98B. Noninterest-bearing deposits comprise 31.2% of total deposits — a structural advantage over peers with higher funding costs.

Operating cash flow of $140.1M (9M 2025), up 8.1% YoY. Full-year FCF approximately $192M. The $4.28 annualized dividend ($1.07/quarter) is comfortably covered at a roughly 43% payout ratio.

The Q4 2025 earnings miss (-5.9% vs. consensus) broke a streak of three consecutive beats (Q1 +19.6%, Q2 +20.2%, Q3 +5.7%). This deteriorating beat trajectory could signal either one-time noise or early signs that the NIM expansion cycle is plateauing.

## Growth Analysis

Park's organic growth prospects are modest. Loans grew 2.2% YoY through Q3 2025, deposits 2.3%. These are community-banking-pace growth rates in small Ohio markets with limited population growth.

The First Citizens acquisition provides a step-function increase: approximately $1.6B in loans and $2.2B in deposits, or roughly 20% incremental to Park's existing base. Cost synergies should emerge as system integration progresses, though management has not publicly quantified targets.

Growth headwinds are substantial:

1. **Durbin Amendment interchange cap** will reduce debit card fee income. Park earned $19.3M in debit card fees (9M 2025), annualizing to ~$25.7M. Industry data suggests Durbin reduces interchange revenue by 30-50% for banks crossing $10B, implying $8-13M in annual revenue loss.

2. **Higher FDIC deposit insurance assessments** under the large-bank pricing methodology could add $3-5M annually in costs.

3. **CFPB compliance infrastructure** — staff, systems, reporting — will require several million in incremental annual expense.

4. **NIM expansion may have peaked.** The Fed is holding rates in a hawkish stance with inflation above target. If rates stay elevated, asset repricing benefits moderate while deposit competition remains fierce. If rates rise further, Park's own sensitivity analysis shows a modest 1.1% increase in net income — limited upside.

In aggregate, the $10B regulatory burden could cost $15-20M+ pre-tax annually, equivalent to roughly 8-11% of 2025 pre-tax income. This structural margin compression is not yet reflected in the earnings run rate.

Wealth management ($9.3B AUM) provides a modest fee income growth avenue, and Scope Aircraft Finance ($339M) offers a differentiated niche, but neither is large enough to offset the regulatory headwinds.

## Valuation Assessment

PRK trades at ~15.6x trailing EPS ($11.11) and ~15.6x forward P/E. The peer median for similar-sized regional banks is 11.5x — a 35% premium.

| Metric | PRK | Peer Median |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| P/E | 15.6x | 11.5x |
| Dividend Yield | 2.5% | 3.3% |

The premium has been justified historically by Park's superior ROATE (16.3% vs. typical community bank 10-14%), strong efficiency ratio (57% vs. industry 60%+), and clean balance sheet. Whether these advantages persist post-$10B is the central question.

Simple illustration: if the $10B regulatory burden reduces pre-tax income by $15M (midpoint estimate), that is roughly $11.5M after tax, or approximately $0.60/share on a post-dilution share count of ~20M. This reduces trailing EPS from $11.11 to approximately $10.50, pushing the effective P/E to ~16.5x on current price — an even wider premium to peers.

Analyst consensus targets range from $171.50 to $183.00 with a median of $180.00, implying 4% upside from current levels. All three covering analysts rate PRK a Hold.

The stock is trading at 96% of its 52-week high ($179.48), having rallied 21.6% over the past year. At these levels, the valuation assumes continued earnings growth that the $10B headwinds make difficult to deliver.

## Competitive Landscape

Park's competitive advantages are real but incremental:

- **Deep community relationships:** 116-year history, 89% associate-shareholder participation, 9.7-year average employee tenure — these produce stable, low-cost core deposits
- **Niche lending verticals:** Scope Aircraft Finance and national non-bank consumer finance lending differentiate Park from pure community bank peers
- **Geographic diversification:** Five-state footprint reduces single-market concentration
- **Noninterest-bearing deposit mix:** 31.2% — structurally superior funding cost

Against these strengths, Park competes with national banks that have greater technology budgets, credit unions with tax advantages, and fintechs increasingly penetrating small-market banking. The company's digital capabilities (ParkDirect mobile banking) are adequate but not differentiating.

The First Citizens acquisition extends Park into Tennessee, a higher-growth market than eastern Ohio. Integration success — retaining customers, employees, and the Tennessee community bank culture — will determine whether this creates lasting value or becomes a multi-year distraction.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit quality deterioration.** NPLs surged to 1.13% of loans ($90.6M) at Q3 2025, driven by a single $22.2M downgrade in the non-bank consumer finance portfolio. This niche national lending business ($274M) is secured by sub-prime borrowers' cash flows, the collateral most vulnerable in a recession. Management acknowledges these loans present "a higher level of risk." In a moderate recession scenario (Park's own 50%-weighted CECL scenario), reserves would need to increase by $30.2M.

**$10B threshold costs.** As detailed in Growth Analysis, $15-20M+ in annual pre-tax income erosion from Durbin, FDIC, CFPB, and Volcker Rule compliance is a structural headwind beginning in 2026-2027.

**CRE concentration.** $2.1B in CRE loans including $286M in non-owner-occupied office — the most stressed commercial real estate segment nationally. Management says the portfolio is "not currently exhibiting signs of stress," but office CRE deterioration tends to manifest with a lag.

**DOJ Consent Order.** Active through 2028, requiring specific investments in majority-Black/Hispanic communities, dedicated lending staff, and ongoing compliance reporting. Not material financially (~$9M total commitment) but requires continuous management attention.

**Integration risk.** Absorbing $2.6B in assets from a Tennessee bank into an Ohio-based community banking model, with different markets, customers, and potentially different credit cultures, is a multi-quarter execution challenge.

**Macro sensitivity.** Record-low consumer sentiment, elevated energy costs, and rising recession probability (45-50%) create headwinds for a bank with $1.6B in indirect auto loans and exposure to small-to-medium businesses.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.3% is nearly double the 25.5% historical volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (50.3% near-term vs. 28.9% at 6 months). This suggests the market is pricing a specific near-term catalyst — most likely the upcoming Q1 2026 earnings report, which will be the first to include First Citizens integration impacts and post-$10B regulatory disclosures. The 0.12 put/call OI ratio indicates low put positioning relative to calls, partially offsetting the elevated IV signal: the stress pricing appears driven by uncertainty rather than directional bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($185-195)
- First Citizens integration proceeds smoothly, adding $1.6B in loans with minimal credit friction
- Tennessee franchise proves higher-growth than Ohio core, accelerating loan and deposit growth to 5-7% annually
- NIM remains elevated as the rate environment supports asset yields while deposit costs stay controlled
- $10B regulatory costs come in at the low end of estimates ($10M) and are offset by acquisition synergies
- Wealth management AUM grows toward $12-13B, diversifying fee income
- Valuation re-rates to ~17x on proven acquisition execution

### Bear Case ($140-150)
- $10B regulatory burden hits the high end ($20M+), compressing EPS by $1.00+
- Credit deterioration spreads from the non-bank consumer finance portfolio into CRE and indirect auto as recession takes hold
- First Citizens integration suffers customer and employee attrition, with Tennessee loans requiring higher provisions
- NIM compresses 20-30bp as deposit competition intensifies or rates decline
- The Q4 2025 earnings miss becomes a trend, with Q1-Q2 2026 also disappointing
- Valuation de-rates toward peer median (11.5x) on $9.50-10.00 EPS = $109-115

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. Stock pulls back to $150 or below, bringing the P/E toward 14x — closing the peer premium gap to levels justified by superior ROATE
2. First Q1 2026 earnings report shows acquisition accretion and manageable $10B regulatory impact (Durbin cost <$8M annualized)
3. NPL ratio declines below 0.90% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the Q3 spike was idiosyncratic

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. NPLs exceed 1.50% of loans or a second large non-bank consumer finance downgrade occurs
2. $10B regulatory costs confirmed at $20M+ annually with no offsetting synergies
3. NIM contracts below 4.30% for two consecutive quarters

### Reassessment Timeline
Next natural reassessment point is the Q1 2026 earnings release (expected late April 2026), which will provide the first integrated financial statements post-acquisition and the first look at regulatory cost impacts.

## Conclusion

Park National is a high-quality community bank with proven operating discipline — 16%+ ROATE, sub-57% efficiency ratio, zero subordinated debt, and 750bp of excess capital above well-capitalized thresholds. These metrics justify some valuation premium over regional bank peers.

However, at $173 the stock already prices in strong operating performance, trading at a 35% premium to peers while facing a structural earnings headwind from crossing the $10B asset threshold. The resulting $15-20M+ annual pre-tax income erosion ($0.60-0.80 in EPS) narrows the profitability advantage that justifies the premium.

Credit quality trends bear watching: the NPL spike and specific exposure to non-bank consumer finance lending represent meaningful risk in a weakening economic environment. The Q4 2025 earnings miss, though modest, ended a streak of strong beats and may signal peak earnings momentum.

The risk/reward profile is neutral. Hold.
