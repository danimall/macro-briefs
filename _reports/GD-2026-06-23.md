**Research Date: June 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

General Dynamics is delivering one of the cleaner growth-plus-margin stories in large-cap defense, and the operating data confirm it. FY2025 revenue rose 10.1% to $52.6B, EPS rose 13.4% to $15.45, and total backlog jumped 30% to $118B, then extended to $130.8B in Q1 2026 on a $15.4B Columbia-class award. Defense book-to-bill hit 2.2x in the quarter. Cash conversion is high quality: 2025 operating cash flow was 122% of net earnings and free cash flow converted at 94%. The two economic engines that define GD, the near-irreplaceable Navy nuclear-submarine franchise (Marine Systems) and the margin-inflecting Gulfstream fleet cycle (Aerospace), are both working at the same time, which is unusual.

The stock has rerated alongside the multi-front defense demand environment, up roughly 22% over the trailing twelve months to $343. At ~21x forward EPS of $16.45-16.55, GD trades at a meaningful discount to the A&D peer median (~39x) and below Lockheed (26.6x), but at a premium to Northrop (17.3x). The valuation gap is real but partly justified: GD's growth is locked in by backlog while its margin profile is mixed. Marine Systems, the largest segment at 32% of revenue, earns only ~7.0% and its profitability, not its growth, is the swing factor. The bull case rests on Marine margin stabilization (early evidence is positive: Q1'26 Marine margin improved 30bp) and continued Aerospace margin expansion (15.0% in Q1'26, up 70bp). The bear case is the one open contingency GD refused to characterize as immaterial: the Sherman Act no-poach class action against the shipyards, reinstated by the Fourth Circuit in May 2025 with a cert petition pending.

We rate GD a HOLD with a constructive bias, classified as a growth hold. The franchise quality, backlog visibility, and reasonable multiple support ownership and accumulation on weakness, but the stock sits below its prior 52-week high of $369.70 having round-tripped a January rally, estimate revisions have stalled (+2.3% current-year over 90 days, flat over 30 days), and the risk/reward at the current price is balanced rather than asymmetric. The cleaner entry is on a pullback toward $300-310.

## Company Overview

General Dynamics operates ten business units across four reporting segments, straddling two distinct economic models: high-margin commercial business jets and capital-intensive government defense work.

**Marine Systems (32% of 2025 revenue, $16.7B; 7.0% margin)** is the structural anchor. Electric Boat is prime/lead designer on every U.S. Navy nuclear submarine program: the 12-boat Columbia-class ballistic-missile program (Navy program of record exceeding $125B, first delivery 2028) and the Virginia-class attack subs (14 in backlog through 2034). Bath Iron Works builds Arleigh Burke destroyers; NASSCO builds auxiliaries. This is a near-irreplaceable position in the defense industrial base, and also GD's lowest-margin segment.

**Technologies (26%, $13.5B; ~9.5% margin)** combines GDIT federal IT services with Mission Systems defense electronics. No single contract is material, but the segment is the most exposed to federal IT-spending efficiency cuts.

**Aerospace (25%, $13.1B; 13.3% margin and rising)** is the profit and margin driver: Gulfstream business jets plus Jet Aviation services. GD delivered 158 aircraft in 2025 (+16%), with an all-new fleet (G700, G800 already certified; G400 and super-midsize G300 in development) clearing the low-margin early-production phase.

**Combat Systems (17%, $9.2B; 14.4% margin)** holds the highest defense margin: sole-source Abrams tanks, Stryker, European Land Systems, and Ordnance & Tactical Systems munitions.

Customer mix is 68% U.S. government, 15% U.S. commercial, 17% non-U.S. The contract base is roughly 51% fixed-price / 44% cost-reimbursement. CEO Phebe Novakovic has chaired the company since 2013.

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory is strong across growth, margins, and cash conversion.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 39.4 | 42.3 | 47.7 | 52.6 (+10.1%) | 13.5 (+10.3%) |
| Operating margin | 10.7% | 10.0% | 10.1% | 10.2% | 10.5% |
| Net income ($B) | 3.39 | 3.32 | 3.78 | 4.21 | 1.13 |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $13.63 | $15.45 (+13.4%) | $4.10 |

The headline is backlog, which carries total estimated contract value of $188.4B. The drivers were $20.1B of combined Navy submarine awards, $9.2B of international combat-vehicle awards, and Aerospace book-to-bill of 1.2x. Roughly 55% of backlog converts to revenue by year-end 2027, giving GD multi-year visibility most industrials cannot match.

Cash quality is high. Q1'26 operating cash flow swung to +$2.16B from −$148M a year earlier as Combat Systems working capital unwound. Aerospace margin expanded as the G800 cleared early production and post-certification R&D rolled off. Marine inched to 7.0%/7.3%, the key watch item.

On the balance sheet, total debt of ~$8.0B against equity of $26.1B and $3.7B cash leaves net debt modest. The screening "grey-zone" Altman Z-Score of 2.28 is a model artifact, not a distress signal: the Z-Score penalizes asset-heavy, working-capital-funded contractors carrying large goodwill ($21B) and customer advances (~$10.8B of pre-funded working capital, a favorable feature). Actual financial leverage is conservative investment-grade, and the 122% cash conversion is the truer signal. Capital allocation tilts toward reinvestment: the 29th consecutive annual dividend increase (to $1.59/qtr), repurchases deliberately limited to offsetting dilution ($637M in 2025), and capex up ~30% to $1.2B to expand submarine and munitions capacity. ROIC improved each year from 12.3% to 14.2%. The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one narrow miss over the last four quarters, including an 8.2% Q1'26 beat ($4.10 vs. $3.79).

## Growth Analysis

GD's growth is among the most visible in the sector because it is contracted, not forecast. The Navy submarine super-cycle, moving toward one Columbia plus two Virginias per year, carries the highest national-priority funding rating (DPAS) and bipartisan support. The constraint is industrial-base capacity, not demand, which is why GD is pushing capex up to build it. European rearmament from the Russia-Ukraine conflict supports durable international demand for wheeled/tracked vehicles and munitions. Within Aerospace, 38% of aircraft backlog is now non-U.S.

Aerospace adds a margin-led growth vector independent of defense budgets. The completed new-fleet cycle (G400/G500/G600/G700/G800/G300) is a multi-year competitive and margin tailwind as new models mature down the cost curve, supported by a 1.2x book-to-bill.

The growth caveat is that revisions have stalled. Current-year EPS estimates rose only +2.3% over 90 days and were flat over 30 days; next-year is up +0.7% over 90 days. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here: the +30%-over-90-days threshold that would flag a strong buy is far away. Management's FY2026 EPS guide of $16.45-16.55 (against current-year consensus of $16.67) is consistent with the company's typical modest conservatism and implies high-single-digit EPS growth. Combat Systems faces a U.S. Army recapitalization headwind: the M10 Booker program was terminated and Stryker volumes are down, with U.S. military vehicle revenue falling in both 2025 and Q1'26, partly offset by XM30, international demand, and munitions. Growth is durable and broad, but it is steady compounding, not acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $343, GD trades at ~21x forward EPS of ~$16.50 and ~20.7x the FY26 guide midpoint. Against A&D peers, the picture is mixed:

| HWM | 61.4 | 0.2% |
| TDG | 39.3 | 7.4% |
| LMT | 26.6 | 2.6% |
| NOC | 17.3 | 1.8% |
| **GD** | **~21** | **1.8%** |
| Peer median | 39.3 | 1.8% |

GD sits well below the peer median P/E and below Lockheed, but above Northrop. The discount to TDG/HWM is appropriate: those are higher-margin, higher-multiple aftermarket and commercial-aero franchises. The relevant comparison is to the prime peers (LMT, NOC), where GD's growth profile (10% revenue growth, 30% backlog growth, dual defense-plus-commercial engines) is superior to Lockheed's stalling franchise and supports a multiple above Northrop's. On that basis, GD looks fairly-to-modestly-cheaply valued, not deeply mispriced.

Cycle-adjustment is not the issue here: GD is not a trough-earnings inflection story; earnings are growing steadily off a healthy base. The market is wrong only at the margin: it discounts Marine's structurally low ~7% margin and the open litigation tail, while underweighting the Aerospace margin inflection and the visibility that 2.2x defense book-to-bill provides. Analyst targets (low $313, mean $393, median $398, high $444) bracket meaningful upside, with the mean ~13% above the current price. We treat these as informative but not as a floor: GD is not a sub-30x-with-rapidly-rising-estimates name where consensus systematically lags. With estimate revisions stalled, the consensus targets reflect a constructive-but-divided view (Jefferies upgraded to Buy at $400 on Marine growth; a competing DCF pegged fair value at ~$335 on Marine margin concerns). The valuation supports a HOLD with accumulation appeal on weakness, not a clear BUY at spot.

A note on our framework: a low headline multiple alone does not earn a high valuation score, and GD's discount is real but partly structural (low Marine margin, government concentration). The score reflects genuine relative cheapness against prime peers offset by the absence of a near-term catalyst that closes the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

GD's moat is among the widest in defense, concentrated in irreplaceable franchise positions. In Marine, the only meaningful competitor is Huntington Ingalls, with which GD simultaneously partners on Virginia-class and subcontracts to on Columbia, a deeply interdependent duopoly. The submarine demand is multi-decade and bipartisan; switching costs are effectively infinite. Combat Systems holds sole-source positions on Abrams and benefits from durable NATO rearmament demand. Gulfstream competes at the top of the business-jet market against Bombardier and Dassault, with flagships holding 350+ city-pair speed records and a completed fleet refresh that should sustain share and margin.

The trajectory caveat that warrants a notch below maximum: Technologies is the most commoditized segment (versus Leidos, Booz Allen, SAIC) and the most exposed to federal IT-efficiency cuts, and the U.S. Army vehicle recapitalization is a genuine, if partly offset, headwind in Combat Systems. The competitive position is improving in Marine and Aerospace and softening at the edges in Technologies and U.S. land vehicles: net stable-to-improving, which supports a strong but not elite score.

## Risk Assessment

**Marine Systems margin/execution — the primary idiosyncratic risk.** The submarine ramp is straining a shipbuilding supply chain hit by post-pandemic labor and demographic shortages. These are largely fixed-price programs; 2024 margins were dented by supplier cost growth. Growth is contractually guaranteed; profitability is the variable. The Q1'26 Marine improvement and the ~7.3% full-year guide are early evidence the stabilization is working, but this is the central swing factor.

**Sherman Act no-poach litigation — the one tail risk GD won't size.** A putative class action (filed October 2023) alleges the shipyards conspired not to solicit naval architects and marine engineers. The Fourth Circuit reversed dismissal in May 2025; defendants petitioned the Supreme Court in September 2025. GD states it "cannot estimate" the loss and that an adverse outcome "could be material." This is the single contingency GD declined to call immaterial: a genuine, unquantifiable tail.

**Government concentration and budget risk (~70% of revenue).** GD disclosed 2025 impacts from federal cost-cutting (staff reductions, contract modifications/terminations, award delays), but these were largely confined to GDIT/IT services — the lowest-multiple, most-impairment-exposed segment — while the submarine, Gulfstream, and munitions franchises are insulated by national-priority funding. Full-year FY26 appropriations were enacted in early February 2026.

**Other monitorables.** A single large international tracked-vehicle contract carries ~$1.3B of net unbilled receivables where realized variable consideration "could be less than our estimate," with a potential "material unfavorable impact." Technologies holds $14.5B of GD's $21B goodwill with $1.8B accumulated impairment and only ~25% fair-value cushion at the last quantitative test (2022), the most impairment-vulnerable unit given federal IT pressure. Single-source supply (semiconductors, an Israel-based mid-cabin airframe supplier) caused Aerospace delays and a 30bp tariff hit in 2025. Two unexplained December 2025 officer/director 8-Ks could not be retrieved and warrant follow-up, though current evidence suggests routine succession given Novakovic's 13-year tenure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options price modest stress that is most likely event-driven rather than a directional warning. ATM IV of 28.4% sits rich to 21.9% one-year historical vol, with the term structure in backwardation (28.4% near vs. 20.3% at six months): the market is pricing imminent vol. Put/call skew is slightly elevated (3.8%), but put/call volume (0.27) and OI (0.47, falling from 0.58) ratios are call-leaning, not bearish. Short interest is negligible at 1.1% of float (2.0 days to cover). The net read is benign: low conviction on the short side and no informed bearish positioning, with near-term IV reflecting a near-term event rather than a thesis-level concern.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Marine margins stabilize and inch toward the high-7s/low-8s as labor and supply chain normalize, Aerospace sustains 15%+ margins as the new fleet matures, and the defense book-to-bill converts a record backlog into accelerating revenue. EPS compounds at low-double digits toward ~$18.20 in FY27. A multiple rerating toward Lockheed's 26x on superior growth, plus the no-poach matter resolving favorably or being settled at an immaterial sum, drives the stock to the high-$400s. Target ~$400-444.

**Bear case.** Marine margins stall at ~7% under fixed-price cost pressure, the no-poach litigation produces a material adverse outcome or a Technologies goodwill impairment surfaces, federal IT cuts deepen in GDIT, and the U.S. Army vehicle recapitalization headwind worsens. EPS growth decelerates to mid-single digits, the multiple compresses toward Northrop's 17x, and the stock falls to ~$290-313. The analyst target floor of $313 anchors the moderate-bear scenario.

The bull/bear axis is precise: growth is locked in by backlog; Marine margin and the litigation tail are the variables.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold). Reassessment timeline: next quarterly (Q2 FY26) earnings.**

This is GD's fourth consecutive HOLD, and the score has drifted lower (7.1 in April to 6.6-6.7 now), driven by reduced risk and growth scores rather than fundamental deterioration. The downgrades reflect the no-poach litigation reinstatement (a genuine new tail) and stalled estimate revisions, not a price-driven thesis change: the stock is below its January high but fundamentals are intact and improving. The bar for continuing the HOLD is appropriately higher after three prior holds, and the current data clear it: the business is strong but the multiple and risk/reward at $343 are balanced, not asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward $300-310, restoring asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals (this is the cleanest path).
- Two consecutive quarters of Marine margin above 7.5% with continued backlog conversion, confirming the margin inflection.
- Favorable resolution or immaterial settlement of the Sherman Act no-poach matter, removing the one unquantifiable tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse no-poach litigation outcome or a Technologies goodwill impairment.
- Marine margin reverting below 7.0% on supplier cost growth, signaling the stabilization has failed.
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turning negative alongside a guidance cut.

**Base-case fair value: ~$385** (~21x the FY27 consensus of ~$18.20, holding the current multiple as growth compounds), roughly 12% above current. Bull case ~$400-444 (~24-26x rerating); bear case ~$290-313 (-9% to -16%). Reassess at the next quarterly print, with the Q2 Marine margin and any Aerospace delivery-cadence update the decisive data points.

## Conclusion

General Dynamics offers a high-quality, visible-growth defense franchise at a reasonable price: a near-monopoly Navy submarine position, a margin-inflecting Gulfstream cycle, a record backlog, and strong defense book-to-bill, all backed by high cash conversion and rising ROIC. At ~21x forward EPS the stock is fairly-to-modestly cheap against prime peers, and the analyst mean target implies ~13% upside. The reasons to hold rather than buy at $343 are concrete: estimate revisions have stalled, the stock has round-tripped its January rally, Marine margin remains the unresolved swing factor, and the Sherman Act no-poach matter is a genuine tail GD itself won't size. We rate GD a HOLD with a constructive accumulation bias, with the cleanest entry on a pullback toward $300-310 and the next quarterly earnings print as the next reassessment point.
