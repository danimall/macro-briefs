**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Halliburton trades at $39.71, approximately 99% above its May 2025 lows ($19.44) and 9% below its 52-week high ($43.59). The stock's rally has been driven by WTI recovering from $55 to the $85-95 range on Strait of Hormuz disruptions, plus improving operating fundamentals post the $831M impairment-heavy 2025. Q1 2026 delivered $0.55 EPS (beating $0.50 consensus by 9.2%), and operating income recovered to $679M from $431M in the year-ago quarter, confirming that 2025's margin compression was largely non-recurring.

The 12-month growth thesis centers on three elements: (1) the VoltaGrid data center power collaboration backed by $1B in Blackstone/HAL equity, with a 7.2 GW pipeline targeting 2027-2029 delivery — a materially new revenue stream not fully modeled by consensus; (2) continued estimate revisions upward (current-year estimates rose +7.0% in the past 30 days); and (3) international revenue stabilization/growth as Middle East tensions resolve and NOC spending resumes. At ~22.7x trailing P/E but ~16.7x FY26 consensus ($2.37), valuation is not compelling but also not prohibitive if the VoltaGrid adjacency begins to be priced.

However, material risks — concentrated insider selling without any offsetting purchases, a $640M IRS contingent liability, structural North American activity headwinds, and binary oil price sensitivity — prevent a BUY recommendation. The stock warrants holding for the VoltaGrid optionality and estimate momentum, with clear upgrade and downgrade triggers.

## Company Overview

Halliburton is one of the world's three largest oilfield services companies (alongside SLB and BKR), operating in 70+ countries with 46,000+ employees. Revenue splits roughly 58/42 between Completion & Production (C&P) and Drilling & Evaluation (D&E), with geographic exposure of 41% North America, 26% Middle East/Asia, 18% Latin America, and 15% Europe/Africa/CIS.

The company's competitive differentiation centers on its North America pressure pumping leadership (Zeus electric frac fleet — 50% converted), proprietary directional drilling systems (iCruise), and digital platforms (LOGIX automation, Landmark software). A strategic pivot into behind-the-meter data center power through VoltaGrid represents the most significant adjacency expansion in the company's recent history.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Revenue peaked at $23.0B in 2023, declined to $22.9B in 2024, and fell to $22.2B in 2025 as WTI averaged $65/bbl. Q1 2026 revenue of $5.4B was flat YoY, with international growth (+3%) offsetting North America decline (-4%).

**Margin recovery:** 2025 operating margin collapsed to 10.2% (from 16.7% in 2024) due to $831M in impairments, $154M SAP migration costs, and $89M tariff charges. Excluding these items, underlying operating margin was approximately 14.5%. Q1 2026 operating income of $679M on $5.4B revenue implies a 12.6% margin, confirming recovery.

**Balance sheet:** Debt/equity of 1.39x is moderate for the sector. $2.2B cash plus $3.5B undrawn revolver provides ample liquidity. No financial covenants. BBB+/A3 credit ratings. Altman Z-Score of 2.48 is typical grey-zone for capital-intensive OFS companies but presents no near-term distress risk.

**Cash generation:** 2025 FCF of ~$1.6B on $2.9B operating cash flow against $1.3B capex. Capital returns of $1.6B ($1.0B buybacks + $579M dividends) reflect shareholder alignment, though aggressive at trough earnings. Share count reduced from 899M (2023) to 853M (2025). Capex guided to ~$1.1B in 2026 — capital discipline in a softening market.

**Earnings quality:** Three beats in four quarters (9.2%, 25.2%, 14.9% surprises) with one marginal miss (-0.9%). This track record supports confidence in forward estimates, though the magnitude of beats has been declining.

## Growth Analysis

**Traditional OFS growth (flat to declining):** North America rig counts continue falling (669 → 580 → 546 from 2023-2025). E&P capital discipline is structural — companies prioritize shareholder returns over production growth. International spending is more resilient but decelerated in 2025 and faces headwinds from OPEC+ production cuts.

**VoltaGrid — the differentiated catalyst:** The $1B Blackstone/HAL equity investment into VoltaGrid (May 2026) targets the AI data center power buildout. VoltaGrid's 7.2 GW generation pipeline for 2027-2029 leverages HAL's manufacturing capabilities for modular natural gas power systems. HAL has secured capacity for 400 MW of Eastern Hemisphere deliveries beginning 2028. If VoltaGrid captures even 20% of its pipeline, it could represent $2-4B in cumulative manufacturing revenue for HAL over 2028-2030 — material relative to $22B annual revenue.

**Estimate revisions confirm improving trajectory:** Current-year EPS estimates rose +7.0% in 30 days (from $2.21 to $2.37). Next-year estimates rose +6.3% (from $2.73 to $2.90). This is positive momentum but below the +30% threshold that would constitute a strong signal. The revision direction is clearly positive; the magnitude is moderate.

**LNG capacity expansion:** Natural gas demand strengthening through 2026-2027 as LNG export capacity comes online benefits HAL's frac and completion services.

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.71, HAL trades at:
- 22.7x trailing EPS ($1.75 reported, distorted by impairments)
- 16.7x FY26 consensus EPS ($2.37)
- 13.7x FY27 consensus EPS ($2.90)
- ~7.6x EV/EBITDA (estimated on normalized EBITDA of ~$4.5B)

Versus peer medians: The peer set median P/E is 15.0x. HAL at 16.7x FY26 represents a slight premium. However, the peer comparison is imperfect — EQT (11.0x, pure E&P) and FTI (27.1x, subsea engineering) are not direct OFS comparables. Against SLB (~14x forward) and BKR (~15x forward), HAL trades at a modest premium.

The VoltaGrid optionality is not priced into consensus models, which provides potential upside. At the current price, HAL offers ~10% upside to consensus mean target ($43.52) and ~38% to the high ($55.00). Downside to $31 (analyst low) represents 22% loss — the risk/reward is not asymmetrically favorable.

The 99% rally from the 52-week low was driven by fundamental improvement (oil recovery + margin normalization), not multiple expansion alone. At current levels, the stock is pricing in continued oil strength and execution — valuation is fair, not cheap.

## Competitive Landscape

Halliburton maintains the leading position in North American pressure pumping, a differentiator being eroded by structural rig count decline. The Zeus electric frac fleet (50% converted) provides cost advantages and ESG positioning. iCruise directional drilling and LOGIX automation maintain technology leadership.

Against SLB (broader integrated portfolio, stronger international) and BKR (energy technology pivot, LNG leadership), HAL is the most North America-weighted of the Big 3, which has been a disadvantage in the 2024-2025 downturn. HAL's 5-year TSR has underperformed its peer group (163.8% vs. 181.7%).

The VoltaGrid initiative could reposition competitive standing if the data center power adjacency scales. No other OFS company has made a comparable commitment to this market. However, this remains speculative until manufacturing deliveries begin in 2028.

## Risk Assessment

**Oil price binary risk (HIGH):** HAL's operating leverage to WTI is extreme. At $65 (2025 average), the business generates ~$1.30 EPS. At $85+, it generates $2.40+. If Hormuz resolution reopens the strait and oil drops to $75-85, estimates face downward pressure of $0.15-0.25/share.

**IRS contingent liability ($640M):** While manageable (22% of annual FCF, 6% of equity), this creates a multi-year overhang and potential surprise negative event. HAL states resolution will take "substantial time."

**Insider selling pattern:** CEO Miller sold $6.3M (March 2026); EVP Beckwith sold $8.2M (May 2026). No insider purchases observed. While likely routine compensation-related, the absence of any open-market buying during a period of apparent fundamental improvement is a cautionary signal.

**North America structural headwind:** E&P capital discipline shows no signs of reversing. NA revenue expected to decline further in 2026. HAL is stacking "uneconomic fleets" — a capacity destruction signal.

**Tariff uncertainty:** $89M impact in 2025 with potential for additional tariff-related costs depending on Supreme Court rulings and trade policy evolution.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing elevated uncertainty: near-term IV of 43.8% exceeds 1-year historical vol of 36.3% (7.5pp rich). The term structure is in backwardation (43.8% near-term vs. 40.9% at 6 months), indicating the market expects near-term catalysts or stress. Put/call skew of 4.4% shows elevated put demand. The $38 put for June 18 (507 contracts vs. 104 OI = 4.9x Vol/OI) suggests fresh downside protection being established, consistent with concern about oil price reversal on Hormuz resolution. The options market is pricing material near-term risk, not confirming a bullish thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50-55, +26-38%):**
- VoltaGrid manufacturing revenue begins to be modeled by consensus, adding $2-4B revenue/year optionality by 2028-2030
- WTI sustains above $85 through Hormuz uncertainty, driving continued estimate upgrades
- International activity rebounds as NOCs resume spending post-geopolitical resolution
- Restructuring benefits flow through to 18%+ operating margins
- Re-rating from OFS to "energy infrastructure" multiple (20-25x forward)

**Bear Case ($28-31, -22-29%):**
- Hormuz resolution drops WTI to $70-75; NA activity declines further
- VoltaGrid pipeline delays or fails to convert to HAL manufacturing revenue
- IRS rules against HAL on $640M tax dispute
- Continued insider selling signals management concern about forward fundamentals
- Peer compression as OFS sector de-rates on lower commodity prices

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. VoltaGrid secures 1+ GW of firm customer commitments with specific delivery dates, confirmed in HAL 8-K or earnings call
2. WTI sustains above $90 for 60+ days AND North America rig count stabilizes/increases for 2 consecutive months
3. Any insider open-market purchase by C-suite executives

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY26 EPS estimates decline below $2.10 (currently $2.37), indicating commodity or activity deterioration
2. WTI falls below $70 for 30+ consecutive days
3. IRS rules against HAL on the $640M tax matter and HAL announces liability recognition

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (July 2026) is the natural reassessment point. Q2 results will reveal whether Q1 margin recovery sustained and provide VoltaGrid update. Hormuz resolution timeline (30-60 days from deal if confirmed) represents the oil price risk window.

## Conclusion

Halliburton is too dependent on oil price for a clean growth story, but the VoltaGrid adjacency provides identifiable upside optionality over 12-24 months. The estimate revisions (+6-8%) are directionally positive but moderate. Q1 margin recovery confirms 2025 was trough. Insider selling, $640M IRS risk, and options market stress signals argue against conviction at current levels.

The stock is a HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis: the VoltaGrid 7.2 GW pipeline, if even partially realized, shifts the company's growth narrative from cyclical OFS to energy infrastructure. First manufacturing deliveries in 2028 create a visible catalyst. However, oil price binary risk (Hormuz resolution could take WTI from $90 to $75 rapidly) and absence of insider buying prevent an upgrade to BUY. Monitor for the specific triggers identified above.
