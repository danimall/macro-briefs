## Executive Summary

Archrock is the largest outsourced natural gas compression services provider in the United States, operating 4.8 million available horsepower across 5,300+ units. The business benefits from a rare combination of structural demand growth (LNG exports adding 14 Bcf/d through 2030, AI data center power demand, natural reservoir pressure decline) and constrained supply (Caterpillar engine lead times at ~160 weeks creating a 3-year ordering cycle that locks out smaller competitors). These dynamics support continued pricing power and high utilization (~95% industry-wide).

The company has executed well: revenue grew 29% in 2025 (including acquisitions), contract operations margins expanded to 72-73%, leverage declined from 3.2x to 2.6x in a single year, and cash available for dividend covers the current payout 3.5x. The Q1 2026 EPS miss ($0.41 vs. $0.47 consensus) was entirely driven by non-operational SG&A items (stock-price-driven compensation and a one-time $3.7M retention expense), not fundamental weakness.

The challenge for a new buyer at $38.29 is that the market has already repriced much of this thesis. The stock has appreciated 81% from its August 2025 low of $21.17, now trading at 20.4x forward earnings — a premium to adjusted comps like WFRD at 16.8x and TDW at 13.7x. The stock sits near the analyst consensus low target ($38), suggesting limited near-term upside unless estimates rise materially. With the Q1 miss creating some earnings uncertainty and the CFO transition underway, the risk/reward at current levels favors patience for a pullback rather than immediate entry.

## Company Overview

Archrock provides outsourced natural gas compression services under long-term contracts (12-60 months initial terms, average 6-8 years on-site). The company owns, operates, and maintains the compression equipment at customer locations, charging fixed monthly fees. Customers supply the fuel, insulating Archrock from direct commodity price volatility.

**Business segments:**
- Contract Operations (85% of revenue): Fixed-fee compression services
- Aftermarket Services (15%): Parts, maintenance, overhauls for customer-owned equipment

**Fleet:** 5,319 units, 4.8M available HP. 74% of operating HP is large (>1,000 HP/unit). Standardized around Caterpillar engines. Average fleet age: 10 years.

**Geography:** ~75% of operating HP in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford. Q1 2026 showed geographic diversification in new bookings (only 35% Permian, with growth in Northeast, Mid-Continent, South, Rockies).

**Customers:** ~300 customers, top 5 representing 35% of revenue (no single customer >10%).

## Growth Analysis

Archrock has four structural growth drivers operating simultaneously:

1. **LNG export demand:** ~2 Bcf/d of additional capacity coming online in 2026, with ~14 Bcf/d sanctioned through 2030. Each Bcf of export requires compression infrastructure throughout the supply chain.

2. **AI/data center power demand:** Natural gas-fired generation meeting rapidly growing electricity demand from hyperscaler data centers increases gas production requirements and compression needs.

3. **Permian Basin associated gas:** Mid-single-digit gas production growth in 2026, with 4.6 Bcf/d of new takeaway capacity supporting continued development.

4. **Natural pressure decline:** Existing wells require incrementally more compression over time as reservoir pressure falls — a structural, non-cyclical demand source.

**Supply constraint as growth enabler:** 160-week Caterpillar engine lead times mean the industry cannot rapidly expand supply. Only companies with Archrock's balance sheet strength and customer visibility can commit capital this far in advance. This creates pricing power (Q1 2026 pricing up high-single digits YoY) and protects returns on invested capital.

**Estimate revisions:** Modestly positive (+9.6% current quarter over 90 days, +10.1% next quarter). Not the magnitude to trigger a strong BUY signal per inflection criteria, but directionally supportive.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.29, AROC trades at:
- **20.4x forward P/E** (based on $1.83 trailing EPS and $2.03 current year estimate)
- **~7.8x 2026E EBITDA** (using $890M midpoint / ~$6.7B enterprise value)
- **2.35% dividend yield** with 16% YoY growth and 3.5x coverage

**Peer comparison:** The 36.5x peer median P/E is distorted by NOV (82.9x) and KGS (99.3x). More relevant comps: WFRD at 16.8x, TDW at 13.7x. Archrock commands a premium due to its contracted revenue model, lower commodity sensitivity, and higher margin profile — but 20.4x represents a full premium already embedded.

**Analyst targets:** Low $38, median $41, high $46. The stock currently sits at the consensus low target, meaning the sell-side sees 7% upside to median and 20% to high. This limits downside protection from analyst support while capping near-term upside to modest levels unless targets are revised higher.

**Price appreciation context:** AROC has risen ~50% from its ~$25 average in mid-2025 and 81% from its $21.17 low. This appreciation is fundamentally justified (earnings grew, leverage improved, demand outlook strengthened), but the upside math at $38+ differs materially from $25. At $25, you were paying ~12.3x forward earnings; at $38, you're paying ~18.9x (based on $2.03 current year estimate). The growth story is the same, but the entry multiple has re-rated significantly.

**Why this prevents a BUY:** The specific concern is asymmetry. The expected 12-month return to median target ($41) is +7%. The bear case (cyclical slowdown, oil price weakness reducing associated gas growth, multiple compression to 16-17x) implies $33-35, or -8% to -13% downside. This creates roughly symmetric risk/reward, which is insufficient for BUY given the Q1 miss, CFO transition, and energy sector's elevated macro uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

**Market position:** Archrock is the clear scale leader in U.S. outsourced compression by total HP. The market is consolidating (TOPS acquisition 2024, NGCS 2025), and Archrock has $1.4B of dry powder for additional deals.

**Competitive advantages:**
1. Scale drives geographic density, reducing operating costs per unit
2. Balance sheet strength enables 160-week advance ordering (smaller competitors cannot)
3. Fleet depth provides service reliability that mid-market customers value
4. Standardized fleet reduces parts inventory and maintenance complexity
5. Customer switching costs are high in practice (average 6-8 year site duration despite month-to-month terms)

**Competitive threats:**
- Kodiak Gas Services (KGS) is pursuing similar scale-driven strategy
- Electric motor drive adoption could eventually shift competitive dynamics
- Private equity-backed roll-ups in compression space

The competitive position is strong and improving. The supply constraint environment disproportionately benefits the largest, best-capitalized operators.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Cyclical oil/gas downturn (moderate probability):** If oil prices decline significantly, Permian development slows, reducing compression demand. The current environment over-supports activity levels; the risk is a sharp reversal.

2. **Q1 miss pattern (watch):** Two misses in the last four quarters (Q1 2026 -13.4%, Q3 2025 -2.8%) creates uncertainty about management's ability to deliver consistently. The Q1 miss explanation (SG&A) is credible but reduces visibility.

3. **CFO transition:** Doug Aron's retirement removes a key strategic architect. His $9.3M of stock sales ($35-37 range) over March-May 2026 are understandable given retirement planning but notable in magnitude.

4. **Supply chain concentration:** Caterpillar is the primary engine supplier. Any disruption to Cat's production (tariffs on steel/components, supply chain issues) could constrain Archrock's growth capex program.

5. **Leverage ($2.4B):** While declining, this debt load amplifies downside in a cyclical trough. At 2.6x leverage with no maturities until 2032, this is manageable but not negligible.

6. **Tariff risk:** Steel and imported component tariffs could increase fleet expansion costs. Pass-through to customers has a timing lag.

2. Leverage exceeds 3.5x due to acquisition or revenue shortfall
3. Industry utilization drops below 90% (currently ~95%)

## Conclusion

Archrock is a high-quality business with structural demand tailwinds, supply constraints that protect pricing power, and competent management executing a clear capital allocation strategy. The thesis is sound and improving. However, at $38.29 after a 50%+ appreciation, the stock has re-rated to a level (20.4x forward, at analyst consensus floor) where the risk/reward is approximately symmetric rather than asymmetric. The Q1 EPS miss (-13.4%), CFO transition, and position at the bottom of analyst target range create sufficient near-term uncertainty to warrant patience. A pullback to $33-35 would restore the asymmetry that justified prior BUY recommendations. Hold for existing positions; new capital should wait for better entry.
