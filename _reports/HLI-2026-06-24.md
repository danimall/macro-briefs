**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Houlihan Lokey is a leading independent investment bank built around three balanced advisory franchises: Corporate Finance (67% of revenue), Financial Restructuring (20%), and Financial & Valuation Advisory (13%), with no lending, trading, or research to create conflicts. The model is asset-light and cash-generative: FY26 (year ended March 2026) revenue grew 9.5% to $2.62 billion, net income reached $425.7 million, free cash flow was $682 million (a 26% FCF margin), and the balance sheet carries zero drawn debt against roughly $1.36 billion of liquidity. The firm raised its quarterly dividend ~17% to $0.70 alongside a record full year.

The stock has fallen from the ~$211 September 2025 high and the ~$170-185 range where the company was actively buying back shares to ~$140 today, an ~34% drawdown from the high and ~20% off the levels where management itself deployed capital. The decline is driven by softening near-term estimates (current-year consensus EPS cut 7.1% over 90 days), a Q4 FY26 miss (-11.7%), the higher-for-longer rate environment compressing the multiples of all capital-markets-sensitive financials, and HLI's status as a Q4 detractor in the PGIM Jennison Financial Services Fund. None of this reflects franchise impairment: FY26 was a record, the Q3 print was strong across all three segments, and the restructuring hedge ran in reverse (FR revenue fell 3% as M&A boomed), meaning the year was carried by a single engine with embedded upside optionality if the credit cycle turns.

We rate HLI a BUY with disciplined sizing. At ~14.8x next-year EPS ($9.44) the stock trades at roughly a 36% discount to its independent-advisory peer cohort, supported by a net-cash balance sheet, a ~2% dividend yield raised aggressively, and a #1-by-volume M&A franchise. The principal reasons we stop short of high conviction are negative near-term estimate revisions, contingent-fee-driven quarterly lumpiness, and a dual-class control structure that limits public-shareholder influence. The sector lesson, that Financials BUYs have historically carried negative alpha, also tempers position size; this thesis rests on a specific company-level mispricing, the gap between record fundamentals and the drawdown, not on sector beta.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1972 and headquartered in Los Angeles, Houlihan Lokey is a global independent advisory bank with ~2,800 employees, 1,900+ financial professionals, 354 Managing Directors, 2,000+ clients, and 30+ offices. It deliberately avoids balance-sheet activities to position itself as a conflict-free advisor. The three segments serve complementary cyclical roles:

- **Corporate Finance ($1.74B, 67%):** M&A and capital solutions, with the firm "consistently selling more companies under $1 billion than any competitor." This is the growth engine and the most cyclically exposed line, levered to the M&A recovery.
- **Financial Restructuring ($529M, 20%):** One of the largest restructuring and liability-management practices in the industry. It is a countercyclical hedge: restructuring demand rises when M&A falls and shrinks when credit conditions ease, as it did in FY26 (FR down 3%, segment profit down 14%).
- **Financial & Valuation Advisory ($344M, 13%):** Fairness/solvency opinions, valuation, tax, and disputes, a highly recurring, defensible franchise.

The combination of a leading mid-cap M&A franchise with one of the industry's largest restructuring practices is the structural differentiator versus pure M&A-advisory peers like Moelis or Evercore. In any given year, one engine cushions the other.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is high-quality and resilient. Revenue compounded from $1.81 billion (FY23) to $1.91 billion (FY24), $2.39 billion (FY25), and $2.62 billion (FY26), tracking the M&A market recovery. Net income attributable to the firm rose 6.5% to $425.7 million, with diluted EPS through nine months running $4.74 versus $4.05 a year earlier.

Margins are stable and the compensation ratio, the single most important margin lever for any advisory firm, held steady at 64%. Operating margin sits near 20%, net margin 16.2%, ROE 18.1%, and ROA 9.8%. Cash conversion is excellent: FY26 operating cash flow was $704 million and FCF $682 million on negligible capex (~$22M), a 26% FCF margin that maps to roughly a 9% FCF yield at the current $7.6 billion market cap.

The balance sheet is effectively net-cash: $1.19 billion of cash plus ~$170 million of investments against zero drawn debt and only a $150 million undrawn revolver maturing 2030. The reported 0.79 debt/equity ratio is an artifact of operating-lease liabilities (~$497M) and accrued compensation, not financial leverage. The Altman Z-Score of 2.53 ("grey zone") is a false alarm: the model penalizes asset-light advisory firms with large goodwill and minimal working capital and is not designed for this business; genuine bankruptcy risk is negligible given the net-cash position and recurring cash flows.

Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly: FY26 financing outflows of $493 million via dividends and buybacks, an ~17% dividend increase to $0.70/quarter, and ~$280 million remaining on the buyback authorization. The company repurchased shares at $179-185 in late 2025, roughly 30% above today's price, which establishes a clear management reference point on intrinsic value.

A quality-of-earnings watch-item: management reclassified contingent-consideration revaluation (e.g., the Waller Helms earnout rising to $47.9M from $30M) to a dedicated P&L line starting Q1 FY26, inflating corporate expenses ~30% in nine months. These are non-cash, judgment-heavy fair-value swings that will add reported-earnings volatility as the firm does more bolt-on deals (Audere, Waller Helms), though they do not affect cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

The medium-term growth story is intact but the near-term momentum has rolled over, which is the central tension in this name. Three observations frame it:

First, the secular driver is the M&A recovery. HLI's mid-cap sell-side franchise and #1 deal-volume ranking position it to capture rising volumes, and CEO Scott Adelson is leaning into the rebound with an aggressive Managing Director hiring spree. MD count (354 and growing) is the cleanest leading indicator of revenue capacity, and the firm is adding senior bankers while California's curbs on non-compete enforceability make talent both an opportunity and a risk.

Second, the restructuring hedge ran in reverse in FY26, with CF rising ~14%. This means the record year was carried by a single engine, leaving genuine upside optionality if the credit cycle turns and FR re-accelerates. Given the world-model backdrop of a developing private-credit cascade and elevated recession odds, this optionality is more valuable than the market currently credits.

Third, the near-term estimate trend is negative and must be respected. Current-quarter consensus has been cut 5.0% over 30 days and 12.3% over 90 days; current-year EPS is down 7.1% over 90 days to $8.21; next-year is down 3.9% to $9.44. The Q4 FY26 print missed by 11.7% ($1.63 vs $1.85). This is a negative estimate-revision signal, the primary reason Growth Prospects scores a 6 rather than higher. The earnings surprise record is still 3 beats in 4 quarters (including a +26% beat in June 2025), so visibility is lumpy rather than deteriorating, consistent with the contingent-fee revenue model where Completion Fees are earned only on close.

Weighting growth prospects heavily as the most reliable winner-selection dimension, the honest read is mixed: durable multi-year capacity expansion (MD hiring, FR optionality) against falling near-term consensus. That balance lands at a moderate score.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$140, HLI trades at ~22.6x trailing EPS ($6.22) and ~14.8x next-year consensus EPS ($9.44), with a ~17.1x current-year multiple ($8.21). The peer table is instructive but heterogeneous: Piper Sandler 19.7x, BGC 30.4x, Moelis 23.2x, StoneX 23.0x, Stifel 13.9x, with a peer median of 23.0x. On a forward basis HLI sits at roughly a 36% discount to the median and to the closest pure independent-advisory comp (Moelis at 23.2x), despite arguably a stronger and more balanced franchise.

The mispricing case rests on three legs. First, the multiple compression is sentiment- and rate-driven, not fundamental: the firm delivered a record FY26 and a strong Q3, yet the stock fell 34% from its high. Second, management's own capital deployment frames intrinsic value above the current price: buybacks executed at $179-185 and the dividend raise are revealed-preference signals that insiders viewed the stock as cheap 30% higher. Third, absolute support exists via a ~2% dividend yield (well-covered, growing) and an ~9% FCF yield on a net-cash balance sheet.

I am deliberately disciplined here. The estimate-revision trend is negative, which argues against treating analyst targets (low $155, mean $172.5, high $200, all above the current price) as reliable floors; on a stock with falling estimates, targets can lag downward as readily as upward. And the calibration history is clear that "cheap" sorts the wrong way absent a catalyst: a low multiple alone is a value-trap warning, not a buy. The catalysts that justify the high valuation score are specific: (1) the FR re-acceleration optionality, which the world-model credit backdrop makes more probable than consensus assumes; (2) a normalization of M&A activity that has already produced a record year and a beat-heavy surprise record; and (3) the management buyback reference point. Net, this is a mispricing with identifiable catalysts rather than a stretched multiple or an impaired-business value trap, supporting a 6.8.

## Competitive Landscape

HLI's competitive position is improving on the dimensions that matter. It holds the #1 ranking by deal volume in M&A advisory and is the most prolific mid-cap sell-side advisor globally, a niche underserved by bulge-bracket banks whose senior bankers do not engage on sub-$1-billion deals. Independence (no balance-sheet conflicts), deep sector expertise, and broad employee ownership (~850 employee-shareholders own ~21% of equity) reinforce the moat and align retention incentives.

The trajectory is positive: an aggressive MD hiring spree expands future revenue capacity, and the firm is integrating bolt-on acquisitions (Audere Partners, a prominent French corporate finance firm formerly known as Natixis Partners, closed February 2026; continued Waller Helms integration) to add sector and geographic reach. The structural disadvantage is on deals requiring financing, where bulge-bracket competitors with balance sheets have an edge, and the independent-advisory field (Evercore, Moelis, PJT, Lazard, Perella Weinberg) is crowded. The talent dynamic cuts both ways: with California curbing non-competes and HLI both poaching and being poached, MD retention is simultaneously the moat and the risk. On balance, a leading franchise gaining share with a balanced cyclical model earns a 7.

## Risk Assessment

The risks are largely structural and known rather than acute, but several deserve weight relative to the current valuation:

- **Negative near-term estimate momentum and contingent-fee lumpiness.** The Q4 miss and falling consensus mean another soft quarter is plausible. Completion Fees earned only on deal close make results hard to forecast quarter to quarter.
- **Goodwill/intangibles (~$1.6B, ~37% of assets)** create impairment risk and earnings volatility from contingent-consideration revaluation.
- **Talent retention** is the franchise's foundation; non-compete erosion in California and other states weakens a key retention tool just as the firm leans into hiring.
- **Dual-class "controlled company" structure:** the HL Voting Trust controls ~74% of voting power on ~22% economic interest. Class A holders have limited influence, and HLI relies on NYSE controlled-company exemptions. A latent "Final Conversion" trigger (if insider ownership slips below 20%, currently ~22%) would collapse insider voting power and could introduce activist/M&A dynamics, a two-sided overhang.
- **International exposure (32% of revenue)** introduces material FX volatility (a ~$34M positive OCI swing in nine months versus -$20M prior).
- **Regulatory/litigation:** fairness-opinion work carries inherent litigation risk; the $15M SEC off-channel-messaging penalty (Aug 2023) is a reminder of compliance exposure.

I treat sector cyclicality as permanently priced rather than as a novel risk. The risk score of 5.5 reflects mostly-known risks with a couple of genuinely two-sided overhangs (dual-class conversion, estimate momentum) that are not fully resolved.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 37.7% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 26.4% (an 11.3-point spread, 64th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation, with near-term vol (37.7%) exceeding longer-dated (32.5%), which typically signals an anticipated catalyst or event. Put/call skew is balanced near term (+1.4%) but turns more negative at 3- and 6-month tenors (-3.0% and -5.4%), and ATM IV ticked up 2.7 points as the price fell 1.4% over the latest snapshot. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.4%. The read: the market expects above-average movement and is paying up for downside protection at longer tenors, consistent with the negative estimate trend. For a BUY thesis with a multi-quarter horizon, rich near-term IV argues against chasing and for scaling in, with the next earnings print the obvious resolution point. Short interest is low (3.3% of float, 2.2 days to cover), so this is not a crowded-short setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175-200).** M&A activity continues to recover, CF compounds, and the restructuring engine re-accelerates as the credit cycle turns, both engines firing simultaneously rather than offsetting. The MD hiring spree converts to revenue capacity, the multiple re-rates toward the peer median (~23x), and the stock returns to the $170-185 range where management itself bought back shares, with the high analyst target at $200. The dividend raise and continued buybacks underpin total return.

**Bear Case ($110-120).** A recession arrives, M&A volumes stall, and the FR hedge cannot fully offset because credit stress takes time to convert into restructuring mandates. Near-term estimates keep falling, contingent-consideration revaluations add reported-earnings noise, MD attrition rises as non-competes weaken, and the multiple compresses further toward the low-teens. A bear case in the $110-120 range implies roughly 14-21% downside from current levels.

**Base Case ($168).** M&A normalizes without a sharp recession, FR stabilizes and provides modest optionality, and the multiple recovers partway toward the peer cohort. ~17.8x on next-year EPS of $9.44 supports a ~$168 base case, broadly in line with the analyst median of $169 and implying ~20% upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $168** (17.8x next-year EPS of $9.44, in line with analyst median; ~20% upside from $140).
**Bull case target: $185** (~19.6x next-year EPS, the level where management repurchased stock; ~32% upside).
**Bear case target: $115** (~12x next-year EPS in a recession scenario; ~18% downside).

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, tied to the M&A activity cycle. Near-term catalysts include the next two quarterly prints (the first being the natural resolution of the current estimate-revision and options-implied-stress overhang) and any inflection in restructuring activity as credit conditions evolve.

**Upside/downside from current ($140):** +20% to base, +32% to bull, -18% to bear. The asymmetry is favorable given the net-cash balance sheet and dividend support limiting downside.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of declining year-over-year Corporate Finance revenue, signaling the M&A recovery has stalled.
2. The compensation ratio rising sustainably above 66%, indicating talent costs are outrunning revenue (margin erosion).
3. A guidance or estimate trajectory implying full-year EPS below ~$7.50, which would mean the negative revision trend has become structural rather than cyclical.
4. Net MD attrition (count declining over multiple quarters), which would undercut the core revenue-capacity thesis.

## Conclusion

Houlihan Lokey offers a quality independent-advisory franchise at a price that reflects negative sentiment rather than franchise deterioration. The firm delivered a record FY26, runs a net-cash balance sheet with a 26% FCF margin, raised its dividend ~17%, and trades at ~14.8x next-year earnings, roughly a 36% discount to its independent-advisory peers and ~20% below the levels where management deployed buyback capital. The restructuring hedge running in reverse this year means the record was achieved on a single engine, leaving real optionality if the credit cycle turns.

The case has blemishes: near-term estimates are falling, a Q4 miss reminds us the contingent-fee model is lumpy, options markets are pricing near-term stress, and the dual-class structure limits public-shareholder influence. These keep us from high conviction and argue for disciplined position sizing, particularly given that Financials BUYs historically require a distinct company-level catalyst to overcome negative sector alpha, which here is the specific gap between record fundamentals and the drawdown. We rate HLI a **BUY** with disciplined sizing, a base-case target of $168, and a clear set of invalidation triggers to monitor at the next two earnings prints.
