**Research Date: June 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Costco is one of the highest-quality businesses in global retail, and the financial data confirms it: ~28% ROE, ~$7.8B free cash flow, $20B in cash and short-term investments against just $5.7B of long-term debt, and a negative-working-capital model that lets the company collect from members and sell inventory before paying suppliers. The membership format generates high-margin, recurring, deferred-revenue cash flows that are still benefiting from the September 2024 fee increase (the first since 2017), which contributed roughly 35% of fiscal-YTD 2026 fee growth and continues to flow through ratably. None of this is in doubt.

The debate is entirely about price. At ~$961 and a forward P/E near 48x (trailing 48x), Costco trades at roughly double the consumer-staples multiple and a premium to Walmart's ~40x. That premium is contingent on the durability of membership growth, and the data shows that growth moderating: total paid-member growth has slowed from a historical 6-7% to ~4.8%, and worldwide renewal slipped to 89.7% (U.S./Canada 92.2%) from 89.8%/92.3% a year earlier. The Q3 FY26 reaction — the stock fell despite +10% comps and +11.6% revenue — demonstrated how little room the valuation leaves for anything short of reacceleration. Three of the last four quarters have narrowly missed consensus EPS.

The offsetting positives are real. The Executive tier, ~73.6% of worldwide sales and the bulk of fee revenue, is growing ~9% — the most loyal, highest-spending cohort is accelerating even as digitally-acquired younger members churn at higher rates. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (current-year +0.7%, next-year +0.9% over 90 days). But these are incremental data points, not a thesis that closes the gap between a 48x multiple and mid-single-digit member growth. We maintain HOLD. The business deserves ownership; the entry price does not offer asymmetric upside, and the multiple is the dominant risk.

## Company Overview

Costco operates 928 membership warehouses (as of the quarter ended May 10, 2026): 637 in the U.S./Puerto Rico, 115 in Canada, and 176 across 13 other countries. The operating model is built on limited assortment — fewer than 4,000 SKUs per warehouse versus roughly 120,000 at Walmart — which drives high volume per item, rapid inventory turns, and the ability to operate profitably at ~11% gross margins, far below conventional retailers.

The economic engine is the membership fee, not merchandise. Costco sells goods at thin margins to preserve pricing authority and earns the bulk of its profit on recurring, high-margin fees. At Q3 FY26: 82.9 million paid members and 148.5 million cardholders, with Executive members ($65 upgrade on top of the $65 Gold Star fee) representing ~73.6% of worldwide sales and earning a 2% reward. The moat rests on scale purchasing power, the Kirkland Signature private label (higher margin, rising penetration, loyalty driver), member renewal rates above 92% in the core U.S./Canada market, and an unusually low-turnover workforce (~94% one-year-plus U.S./Canada employee retention, ~$32/hr average hourly wage).

Leadership is deep and long-tenured. CEO Ron Vachris (since January 2024) is a 25-year-plus Costco veteran; CFO Gary Millerchip joined from Kroger in 2024. Recent management changes (Russ Miller's retirement, Caton Frates's promotion) are orderly succession events, not red flags.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Total revenue grew from $227.0B (FY22) to $275.2B (FY25), an 8% FY25 increase. The clean operating net income figure for FY25 is ~$8.1B (+10%), $18.21 diluted EPS, per MD&A; an XBRL figure of ~$10.8B appears to include a discrete tax item and should not be used as the operating baseline. Q3 FY26 (ended May 10, 2026) delivered total revenue +11.6% to $70.5B, net income of $2,192M ($4.93 diluted EPS, up from $4.28), comps +10% (+7% ex-gas/FX), and digitally-enabled sales +21%. The 36-week FY26 figures: revenue $207.4B, net income $6,228M ($14.01 EPS).

**Membership fees.** The high-margin recurring line grew from $4,580M (FY23) to $5,323M (FY25, +10%), and reached $1,373M in Q3 FY26 (+11%). The September 2024 fee increase drove ~40% of FY25 fee growth, and because fees are recognized ratably over 12 months, the lift continues to flow through deferred revenue. Costco historically raises fees roughly every five to six years, so this lever is far from exhausted.

**Margins.** Gross margin is ~11.1% (FY25 11.12%, +20bps), operating margin ~3.8%, net margin ~2.9%. In a model this thin, SG&A leverage (9.25% of sales in FY25) is the key swing factor; small basis-point moves translate to material EPS effects.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $13.3B in FY25 (up from $11.3B), with FCF of $7.8B; 36-week FY26 OCF reached $11.1B (vs. $9.5B), helped by faster inventory turns and improved supplier payment terms. Altman Z-score is 4.93 (deep safe zone), ROE 27.8%, ROA 10.5%. The negative-working-capital model means accounts payable ($22.4B) exceed inventory ($19.4B). Long-term debt is just $5.7B against $33.5B equity and $20.0B cash plus short-term investments, with senior notes laddered 2027-2032 at coupons of 1.375%-3.0%. The D/E of 1.64 reflects operating liabilities (payables, deferred fees), not financial leverage. This is one of the strongest balance sheets in retail, and we score Financial Health 8.5.

**Capital allocation.** FY26 capex is guided to ~$6.5B (35 planned openings plus depot/digital). The dividend was raised from $1.16 to $1.30 (April 2025) to $1.47 (April 2026, +13%), supplemented by periodic special dividends (a $15/share, ~$6.7B payout in FY24). Buybacks are modest (~$0.6-0.9B/yr; $1.36B remaining on a $4B authorization expiring January 2027), leaving share count essentially flat at ~444M. Coverage of the dividend by FCF is comfortable.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two divergent currents. The headline is moderating: renewal rates slid roughly 70bps over FY25. Because membership fees underpin both profitability and the valuation, this is the variable that matters most.

The cause is a mix shift rather than a value-proposition failure. Digitally-acquired younger members are now roughly half of new sign-ups (versus ~5% in 2019), and they churn at higher rates — they join for a single big-ticket online purchase or delivery convenience. The headline renewal decline therefore partly reflects successful acquisition of a different, larger cohort, not erosion of the core. Management's stabilization initiatives (auto-renewal pushes, expiration reminders, a $10/month Instacart credit, Executive-only shopping hours, in-warehouse auto-renewal incentives, and a standalone-gas-station pilot to attract younger members) have moderated the quarterly declines to roughly 10bps.

The healthier sub-metric sits beneath the soft headline: the Executive tier is growing ~9% over two consecutive quarters versus ~5% total. The most loyal, highest-spending members are accelerating even as the digital long tail turns over. This quality-over-quantity dynamic supports the durability of fee revenue.

Other growth vectors: digitally-enabled sales extend Costco beyond the in-warehouse SKUs into big-ticket discretionary (mattresses, electronics, fitness equipment), and international openings — particularly in Asia, where opening-day member sign-ups run 5-10x a U.S. opening — remain a member-acquisition engine, though the slower pace of high-growth-market openings is itself a drag.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year EPS revised to $20.58, next year to $22.67, with broad-based small upticks across 27-32 analysts. This is well short of the +30% revision velocity that would warrant a re-rating thesis. It signals stable-to-improving momentum, not acceleration. We score Growth Prospects 6.0 — durable, high-quality growth, but at a moderating mid-single-digit rate that is decelerating where it matters most (member count).

## Valuation Assessment

This is the crux. At ~$961, Costco trades at a forward P/E near 48x (on the $20.58 current-year and ~$22.67 next-year estimates) and a trailing P/E of 48x. That is roughly double the consumer-staples sector multiple (~25x) and a premium to Walmart's ~40x. The dividend yield is just 0.61%, providing no valuation support.

The premium is real and partly defensible: Costco's recurring, deferred-revenue membership economics behave more like a subscription business than a grocer, its returns on capital are exceptional, and its balance sheet is pristine. But a ~48x forward multiple on a business growing earnings in the low-double-digits with members growing ~4.8% leaves no margin for error. The 10-K itself flags this explicitly: "the price of our stock currently reflects high market expectations... Any failure to meet... could cause the price of our stock to decline." The Q3 FY26 selloff is the live demonstration. Three of the last four quarters narrowly missed consensus EPS (Q3 FY26 -1.9%, Q2 -1.5%, Q4 FY25 -0.8%; only Q1 FY26 beat, by 3.3%), which means even strong operating results are being priced as disappointments against the embedded bar.

The cycle-adjusted argument does not rescue the multiple here. Costco is not a trough-earnings story; it is a stable, premium-multiple compounder. The estimate-revision-as-floor logic applies to cheap inflection names, not to a stock at ~48x forward with single-digit member growth. At this multiple, analyst targets (mean $1,083, median $1,100, range $740-$1,315) reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation. The mean target implies ~13% upside, but the dispersion to a $740 low (-23%) captures the asymmetry: the downside from a multiple compression event is larger than the typical staples name precisely because the multiple is elevated.

We score Valuation 3.5. The business quality is undeniable, but there is no identifiable catalyst to argue the market is mispricing the stock upward from here, and the premium multiple offers poor asymmetry. A "cheap" valuation score would require a mispricing thesis; this is a fully-valued, error-intolerant multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

Costco's competitive position is improving on trajectory even as the membership headline softens. The Kirkland Signature private label continues to gain penetration, driving both margin and loyalty, and the negative-working-capital model is a structural advantage: improved supplier payment terms in Q3 FY26 show scale leverage is still expanding, funding growth and dividends without leverage. The U.S./Canada renewal rate and ~94% employee retention reinforce a self-reinforcing loyalty/cost advantage that competitors (Sam's Club, BJ's, Target, Kroger, Amazon) cannot easily replicate.

The relative weakness is delivery speed versus Amazon and Walmart, which is more an opportunity than a threat if addressed, and the consumer bifurcation backdrop helps Costco's value-seeking traffic even as it pressures discretionary baskets. The digital channel extends the assortment into big-ticket categories where the limited-SKU warehouse cannot go.

The one genuine watch-item is brand reputation. The Kirkland tequila labeling suits (alleging the "100% de Agave" claim is false) strike at the exact quality perception that makes Kirkland — and Costco's pricing authority — work. Financially immaterial, but the brand is so central to margins and loyalty that any sustained erosion of the quality narrative would matter. On balance, the trajectory is a widening moat and gaining share in value retail, with one reputational vector to monitor. We score Competitive Position 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Valuation-expectation risk** is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and it is partly, but not fully, priced. At the current multiple, any quarter that fails to show member or renewal reacceleration is treated as a disappointment. The downside in a multiple-compression scenario (toward Walmart's 40x, or the staples 25x in a severe de-rating) is the single largest threat to the stock and is asymmetric to the upside.

**Membership-growth deceleration** is the structural concern, mitigated by the mix-shift explanation and the accelerating Executive tier, but unresolved — management's fixes have moderated, not reversed, the renewal decline.

**Geographic concentration**: U.S./Canada is 86% of sales and 84% of operating income; California alone is 26% of U.S. sales, with HQ and centralized systems in earthquake-prone regions.

**Tariffs**: management states higher tariffs are more likely to hurt than help results, and the March 2026 IEEPA tariff-refund class actions (members suing over passed-through tariffs) are a novel reputational/precedent vector worth monitoring, though likely immaterial financially.

**FX**: international is 27% of sales and 34% of operating income; a -$97M net-income hit in FY25, a tailwind in Q3 FY26 — a real but manageable swing.

**Litigation**: wage-and-hour class actions, pixel-tracker privacy suits, opioid MDL, and the Kirkland tequila labeling suits. Management deems none individually material; risk factors were unchanged in the Q3 10-Q.

The earnings track record modestly lowers confidence in forward estimates and reinforces the error-intolerance problem at this multiple. We score Risk Factors 5.0 — the balance sheet is pristine, but the valuation-driven downside and the unresolved membership-growth question are specific, near-term, and only partially priced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing modestly elevated near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 26.7% versus 18.8% one-year historical volatility (rich by ~7.9 points), and the term structure is in backwardation (26.7% near-term vs. 21.4% six-month), which typically signals an imminent event or lingering post-earnings caution. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.93 to 1.22 over the past month as the stock fell ~6.5%, indicating increased hedging/bearish positioning. Put/call skew is balanced near-term (0.6%) but turns negative (-3.6%) at the six-month tenor, consistent with the market pricing more downside protection over the medium term. This confirms that positioning sees more two-sided risk than complacency, consistent with our HOLD and the elevated-multiple risk framing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$1,185-$1,315):** The membership slowdown proves to be a benign mix shift, not core erosion; the Executive tier's ~9% growth and a stabilizing renewal rate (declines moderating to ~10bps/quarter) reassure the market that fee growth is durable. The September 2024 fee increase continues to flow through, digital sales sustain 20%-plus growth, international openings accelerate member acquisition, and another fee increase becomes visible on the horizon. EPS compounds in the low-double-digits and the market sustains the ~48x multiple, pushing the stock toward BofA's $1,185 and beyond. This is the "membership engine is intact, premium is justified" outcome.

**Bear Case (~$740-$800):** Member-count growth decelerates further below 4.8%, renewal rates resume declining, and the market concludes the digital-cohort churn is a structural drag on the fee base. With three of four recent quarters already missing consensus, a clean miss or soft guidance triggers multiple compression toward Walmart's 40x or lower. At 38x on $20.58 EPS, the stock is ~$782; a de-rating toward the analyst low of $740 implies ~23% downside. Tariff pass-through litigation or a Kirkland brand-trust event amplifies the de-rating. This is the "premium was a bubble, growth doesn't support ~48x" outcome.

**Base Case (~$1,030):** Membership growth stabilizes around mid-single digits without reacceleration, the Executive tier keeps the fee base healthy, EPS lands near the $20.58 current-year / $22.67 next-year consensus, and the multiple holds roughly flat-to-slightly-compressed (~45x on next-year EPS = ~$1,020). The stock grinds modestly higher in line with earnings, but the lack of member reacceleration caps the re-rating. This is the most likely outcome and underpins the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the FY26 Q4 print (late September 2026), with membership/renewal trends and the FY27 capex/opening cadence as the key data. Interim membership data and any fee-increase signal are continuous monitoring items.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Total paid-member growth reaccelerates above 6% AND worldwide renewal stabilizes or rises (>89.7%) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the digital-cohort churn is contained.
- A confirmed announcement of a new membership fee increase (the lever is roughly on its five-to-six-year cycle), which would add a multi-year, low-risk earnings tailwind.
- A meaningful pullback (toward ~$820-$850, ~40-41x forward) that restores asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Member-count growth falls below 4% AND renewal rates resume declining (>15bps/quarter), signaling structural fee-base erosion.
- A clean EPS miss combined with negative estimate revisions, breaking the broadly-rising consensus trend.
- The Kirkland brand-trust or tariff-pass-through litigation escalates into a quantifiable, material liability or a sustained sales/loyalty impact.

We are explicit that this is an "uncertain" hold rather than a confident one: the business merits ownership and would be a clear BUY at a materially lower multiple, but at ~48x forward with member growth moderating, neither the bull nor bear case has enough decisive evidence to warrant a stronger call. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it is met by the same unresolved valuation/growth tension; the call would change quickly on either a re-rating pullback or a confirmed member reacceleration.

## Conclusion

**Recommendation: HOLD (Overall score 6.1).** Costco is a structurally superior business: pristine balance sheet, ~28% ROE, a recurring high-margin membership engine, a widening private-label moat, and an accelerating Executive-member tier that anchors fee durability. The fundamentals are improving on most quality dimensions and estimate revisions are modestly positive. The problem is exclusively price: at ~48x forward earnings, roughly double staples peers and above Walmart, the stock prices in flawless execution against a membership-growth rate that has slowed to ~4.8% and renewal rates that have softened. Three of the last four quarters missed consensus, and the Q3 FY26 selloff on strong comps illustrates how unforgiving the multiple is.

The risk/reward is balanced. The base case implies modest upside roughly in line with earnings; the bear case (multiple compression toward Walmart's level) carries ~20%+ downside with a specific, plausible trigger. We would own Costco enthusiastically below the high-$800s, and we would upgrade on confirmed member reacceleration or a new fee increase. At the current price, holders should stay long for the quality and compounding, but the absence of asymmetric upside and the error-intolerant valuation argue against adding. HOLD.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report repeatedly stated forward P/E "near 46x," but Phase 1 data shows forward P/E of 48.34x (equal to trailing); the slightly higher multiple modestly reinforces (does not undercut) the valuation-driven HOLD. Note also that one upgrade trigger cited a "~$820-$850, ~36-38x forward" pullback, but $820-$850 on $20.58 forward EPS is ~40-41x, not 36-38x — corrected accordingly.
