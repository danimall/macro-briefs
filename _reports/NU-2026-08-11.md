## Executive Summary

Nu Holdings operates the largest branchless consumer bank in Latin America, and the operating economics are exceptional by any global banking standard. Trailing-twelve-month net income of $3.19B on average assets of $65.8B is a 4.84% return on assets, roughly three to four times what large US universal banks earn, and at 6.2x accounting leverage it produces a 29-30% return on equity. Fixed-asset intensity is close to nil: FY2025 capital expenditure was $7.2M against $6,991M of net revenue, and net property and equipment at March 2026 was $75M against $77.5B of assets. Gross revenue (the sum of the six disclosed revenue lines) grew 37.0% in FY2025 to $15.8B, and loans grew 53.1% year over year to $31.2B against deposits of $42.4B.

The stock is nonetheless 27.1% below its January 29, 2026 high of $18.98, having recovered 23.7% from the June 3 low of $11.20. Nothing in the reported numbers explains the de-rating: EPS rose every quarter from $0.11 in Q1 2025 to $0.18 in Q1 2026, and Q1 2026 net margin of 44.0% was the highest in the dataset. What the market appears to be discounting is a set of forward risks that reported results have not yet absorbed. Consensus EPS has been revised down in every window (current quarter -6.2% over 90 days, next year -1.3% over 30 days); the last two quarters missed by 4.4% and 12.7%; a seven-year CFO handed over to an external hire in July, prompting BofA to cut to Underperform with a $10 target; and roughly $1,011M of the $4,943M five-quarter equity build came from favorable BRL translation rather than earnings, which means reported growth and reported book value would compress simultaneously on a currency reversal.

The decisive gap is asset quality. On a loan book compounding 53% year over year with 12.5% growth in a single quarter, delinquency ratios are mechanically flattered because the denominator expands faster than new vintages can season. No NPL, coverage, provision-expense or cost-of-risk figure was obtainable from any source available for this analysis — Nu is a foreign private issuer filing 20-F and 6-K, and the underlying documents were not retrievable. That is not a reason to assume the worst; it is a reason not to underwrite a 30% ROE as durable. Sizing the sensitivity: each 100bp increase in cost of risk on $31.2B of loans is roughly $218M after tax, or about 5.4% of the $0.81 current-year consensus. A 300bp normalization, well within the historical range for Brazilian unsecured consumer credit through a downturn, takes EPS to roughly $0.68 and, at a compressed 14x, the stock to $9.50-10.00. That is the bear case, and it is the same number BofA published. **HOLD** at $13.84, base case $16.50 (+19%), bull $21.00 (+52%), bear $10.00 (-28%).

## Company Overview

Nu Holdings is a Cayman-incorporated holding company operating from São Paulo, listed on the NYSE since December 2021, SIC 6199, large accelerated filer, December fiscal year end. As a foreign private issuer it files Form 20-F annually and Form 6-K for interim results — there is no 10-K, no 10-Q and no DEF 14A. That structurally reduces disclosure granularity relative to a domestic bank: no quarterly MD&A in US form, no proxy-based compensation or related-party disclosure, and no US-style credit-quality tables in the sources accessible here.

The operating business, Nubank, earns money across six revenue lines. FY2025 (USD millions): lending interest income $4,784, credit card interest income $4,598, interest on other assets at amortized cost $2,285, interest and gains on financial instruments at fair value $1,406, fee and commission income $2,340, and interest on other receivables $362 — $15,775M in aggregate gross revenue. Net revenue, the measure after funding and (possibly) credit costs, was $6,991M. The two bases differ by 2.26x in FY2025 versus 2.09x in FY2024, and the composition of that bridge is not visible; this matters, because whether the widening reflects funding cost or credit cost determines whether it mean-reverts with the Selic cycle or does not.

Three markets: Brazil (core), Mexico (scaling, bank license authorized July 2026), Colombia (immaterial to revenue on external accounts). A derivation from reported Q1 2026 ARPAC of $15.90/month implies roughly 104 million active customers, up about 24.5% year over year, with ARPAC up 23% — the two multiply to +53.1% against actual gross revenue growth of +53.0%, which closes the arithmetic to within 0.1pp. Roughly half the growth is customer acquisition and half is monetization of the existing base. Treat the 104M figure as a derivation rather than a disclosed number.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Net revenue progressed $850M (FY2021) → $1,839M → $3,707M → $5,513M → $6,991M (FY2025), with net income moving from a $165M loss in FY2021 to $2,869M in FY2025 (+45.5%). Net margin on net revenue expanded from 27.8% (FY2023) to 35.8% to 41.1%, and to a record 44.0% in Q1 2026. That is genuine operating leverage: FY2025 net income grew 45.5% on 26.8% net revenue growth.

Quarterly net revenue: $1,378M (Q1'25), $1,626M, $1,920M, $2,060M, $1,981M (Q1'26). Reported year-over-year growth swung from +10.7% to +47.5% in three quarters. No operating story explains a 37-percentage-point swing in that timeframe. The translation reserve corroborates the currency driver — accumulated other comprehensive income moved from -$828.2M at December 2024 to +$183.3M at March 2026, a $1,011M favorable swing. Nu reports in USD and earns almost entirely in BRL and MXN, so BRL appreciation through 2025 inflated USD-reported growth just as depreciation in late 2024 suppressed it. Neither the FY2025 +26.8% nor the Q1'26 +43.8% figure should be extrapolated. Note also that Q1 2026 net revenue fell 3.8% sequentially while margin still expanded.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $77.5B at March 2026 (from $49.9B at December 2024). Deposits $42.4B (+34.5% YoY). Net loans $31.2B (+53.1% YoY, +12.5% in the March quarter alone). Shareholders' equity $12.59B, book value per share $2.56, tangible $2.34. Equity/assets 16.3%, up from 15.3% — comfortable, though this is an accounting ratio, not CET1 for the Brazilian or Mexican bank subsidiaries, which were not obtainable.

Two trends inside those numbers deserve weight. First, the loan-to-deposit ratio rose from 60.9% (December 2024) to 73.4% (March 2026). The absolute level is conservative and deposits still exceed loans by $11.3B, but the direction is toward higher balance-sheet intensity. Second, long-term debt reached $4,504M versus $1,707M a year earlier, +164%, taking debt/equity to 0.36x. Nu is progressively substituting market funding for the near-zero-cost retail deposits that are the foundation of its cost advantage. A benign reading is pre-funding for the Mexican bank launch and term diversification; the less benign reading is that deposit gathering is no longer keeping pace with loan origination. Both are consistent with the data.

**Cash flow.** Quarterly operating cash flow ran -$1,214M (Q1'26), +$831M, -$971M, +$2,555M, +$1,085M; FY2025 OCF was $3,500M against TTM $1,201M. For a deposit-funded lender under IFRS, operating cash flow absorbs loan-book and deposit movements — Q1 2026 loans grew $3,467M while deposits grew $523M, which alone accounts for the negative quarter. No annualization of any single quarter of OCF or "free cash flow" is meaningful here, and FCF-margin screens will misprice this company badly. The one cash-flow fact that is informative is capex at 0.10% of net revenue.

**Equity quality.** Equity rose $4,943M from December 2024 to March 2026: retained earnings +$3,921M, AOCI +$1,011M, additional paid-in capital +$9M. So about 20% of the build was currency translation, and only $9M came from share issuance — realized dilution has run roughly 0.3% per year (implied share count 4,930M in FY2024 to 4,947M in FY2025), unusually low for a fintech. Total outstanding options and RSUs were not obtainable, so maximum contingent dilution is unknown; 0.3% is realized dilution only.

**Earnings delivery.** Two beats then two misses: +4.2% (Q2'25), +3.6% (Q3'25), -4.4% (Q4'25), -12.7% (Q1'26). The deterioration in delivery coincides with the estimate-cut sequence. That combination lowers confidence in the $0.81 current-year consensus more than the absolute growth rate would suggest.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is real, decelerating in local terms, and rotating in composition.

Customer growth of roughly 24.5% and ARPAC growth of 23% are both substantive, and they multiply cleanly into reported revenue growth. Gross revenue growth has stepped down each year: +69.6% (FY2023), +41.7% (FY2024), +37.0% (FY2025). That is normal maturation at this scale.

The composition shift is the more consequential development. Lending interest income overtook credit card interest income for the first time in FY2025 ($4,784M vs $4,598M) and again in Q1 2026 ($1,603M vs $1,556M), reversing a ratio that stood at 1.53x in favor of cards in FY2023. Lending interest income grew 59% year over year in Q1 2026. Fee and commission income was 14.8% of FY2025 gross revenue and 14.4% of TTM gross revenue — flat to slightly down. The growth engine is therefore balance-sheet lending, which consumes capital and adds credit and duration exposure. Whether this raises or lowers per-unit risk depends on secured versus unsecured mix, and the portfolio breakdown was not obtainable. This is product rotation within essentially one market and one customer base; it should not be described as diversification.

A second composition point that is underappreciated: interest income from other assets at amortized cost — the treasury and securities book earning Brazil's high Selic policy rate — grew from $1,279M to $2,285M, +78.6%, versus +37.0% for total gross revenue. That line contributed disproportionately to FY2025 growth. If Selic normalizes downward, this compresses faster than the rest of the book. The offsetting effect is lower funding cost and better consumer credit performance, so the net rate sensitivity is genuinely ambiguous — but the revenue-line sensitivity is one-directional and material. Whether the +78.6% was yield or portfolio volume could not be separated.

Forward optionality is real but unpriceable from here. Nu Mexico received authorization to begin bank operations (disclosed July 10, 2026), which supports the stated ambition of becoming Mexico's largest digital bank; license scope, conditions and any required capital injection are unknown, and the shares traded lower the following session. A single secondary source claims Mexico has reached break-even, which would be material if confirmed. US entry is signalled by Cristina Junqueira's title change to "US CEO & Chief Growth Off." between March and April 2026 and by press coverage of a US plan, but no charter application, partner-bank arrangement or acquisition has been verified. The relevant industry datapoint is the OCC's August 7, 2026 denial of Dutch neobank Bunq's national bank charter on "significant supervisory and compliance concerns" — that concerns Bunq, not Nu, but it establishes a currently restrictive chartering environment for foreign digital banks.

Consensus embeds $0.81 for the current year (+40% on FY2025's $0.58) and $1.07 for next year (+32%). Those are aggressive numbers against falling revisions and two consecutive misses; I model $1.00 for FY2027 in the base case.

## Valuation Assessment

**A share-count conflict must be resolved first.** The supplied market capitalization of $52.8B implies 3.81B shares at $13.87. Three independent routes converge on ~4.90-4.92B shares: TTM net income $3,186.2M ÷ TTM diluted EPS $0.65 = 4,902M; common equity $12,589M ÷ BVPS $2.56 = 4,917M; net cash $18,612M ÷ net cash per share $3.79 = 4,911M. The supplied trailing P/E of 21.35 is only reconcilable with the larger count ($13.84 ÷ $0.65 = 21.3x). The $52.8B figure is almost certainly a Class A float calculation excluding Class B founder shares. I use ~4.91B shares and a ~$68.0B market capitalization. This matters: it is the difference between 21.3x and 16.6x trailing.

On that basis:

| Metric | NU at $13.84 |
|---|---|
| P/E, TTM EPS $0.65 | 21.3x |
| P/E, current-year consensus $0.81 | 17.1x |
| P/E, next-year consensus $1.07 | 12.9x |
| PEG (12.9x ÷ 32.1% EPS growth) | 0.40 |
| P/B, BVPS $2.56 | 5.41x |
| P/TBV, TBVPS $2.34 | 5.92x |
| TTM ROE / ROA | 30.1% / 4.84% |

At 5.41x book with a 30% ROE, the implied earnings multiple is 18x — internally consistent. The 12.9x next-year multiple against 32% expected EPS growth is the bull case in a single ratio, and a PEG of 0.40 is the kind of number that normally signals mispricing.

I do not treat it as one, for three reasons. First, the cheapness is conditional on an unverifiable input. A 30% ROE built on a 4.84% ROA in high-yield emerging-market unsecured consumer credit is not the same asset as a 1.2% ROA at 11x leverage in a US universal bank; the ROE is higher because the assets are riskier. If cost of risk normalizes 300bp on a $31.2B book, after-tax earnings fall roughly $650M, EPS drops toward $0.68, and 12.9x becomes 20x on a decelerating number. Second, the estimates are moving the wrong way — down 1.0% and 1.3% in 30 days for current and next year, down 6.2% for the current quarter over 90 days — and delivery has slipped to two consecutive misses. Falling estimates plus a compressed multiple is the classic configuration in which the multiple is right and the estimates are wrong, not the reverse. Third, roughly a fifth of the recent book-value build is currency translation, so the 5.41x P/B denominator is itself exposed to BRL reversal at exactly the moment a weaker BRL would stress consumer credit; those exposures are correlated, not independent.

Book-value compounding gives a cleaner anchor than multiple re-rating. Nu pays no dividend and retains everything, so at a 28-30% ROE, BVPS should compound from $2.56 toward $3.90-4.20 by end-2027 absent FX reversal. At 4.0x book that is $15.60-16.80, which brackets my $16.50 base case. Cross-check on earnings: 16.5x a haircut FY2027 EPS of $1.00 = $16.50. The two methods agree.

Analyst consensus sits at mean $17.98, median $18.00, high $22.00, low $10.00 — a $12 dispersion on a $13.84 stock, which is an unusually honest representation of the actual uncertainty. Note that the sell-side mix has drifted marginally negative over three months (from 5 strong buy / 15 buy / 1 hold / 0 strong sell to 5 strong buy / 13 buy / 3 hold / 0 sell / 1 strong sell). At 12.9x forward the "analyst targets lag reality" logic would ordinarily apply, but it requires rising estimates; here estimates are falling, which removes the mechanism.

## Competitive Landscape

The cost structure is the moat. Capex at 0.10% of revenue and $75M of net PP&E on a $77.5B balance sheet means Nu carries essentially none of the branch overhead borne by Itaú, Bradesco, Banco do Brasil and Santander Brasil. That gap is what allows a 4.84% ROA on a customer base skewed to low- and middle-income Brazilians whom incumbents historically underserved. Roughly 104 million derived actives makes Nu larger by customer count than most US banks.

Trajectory is improving on scale and mixed on quality. Positive: ARPAC +23% demonstrates pricing and cross-sell power inside the installed base, not just account growth; the Mexican bank license converts a payments-and-cards operation into a deposit-funded balance sheet in a second large market; deposits still fund loans with an $11.3B surplus, meaning the funding advantage is intact in absolute terms.

Less positive: the rotation toward balance-sheet lending is a shift into a more capital-intensive, more replicable business. Any bank can lend; not every bank can acquire tens of millions of customers a year at near-zero marginal cost. Fee and commission income flat at ~14.5% of gross revenue suggests the capital-light layer is not scaling with the balance sheet. Brazil's Pix instant-payment rail and open-banking regime commoditize exactly the transactional layer where Nu's early advantage was widest, and incumbents have had five years to build digital front ends. The loan-to-deposit and wholesale-funding trends indicate the cost-of-funds edge is narrowing at the margin even as it remains large.

Zacks groups Nu with SoFi as a digital-banking disruptor; that framing is directionally useful for multiple context but not for underwriting, since the credit books and regulatory regimes are not comparable.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Unmeasured asset quality (the crux).** Loans +53.1% YoY, +12.5% in one quarter, with no NPL 15-90, NPL 90+, coverage, provision expense or cost-of-risk data available. Rapid growth mechanically suppresses reported delinquency ratios. External commentary references "rapid credit expansion and higher provisions," which I could not verify. Sensitivity: 100bp of cost of risk ≈ $218M after tax ≈ 5.4% of current-year consensus EPS; 300bp ≈ 16%. This single unknown determines whether the 30% ROE is a franchise characteristic or a vintage artifact.

**2. Brazil single-country concentration.** The overwhelming majority of revenue is Brazilian. A market-unfriendly macro or political shock hits the currency, the rate path and consumer credit simultaneously. This risk is shared with other Brazilian financials and is only partially priced in a stock that has already de-rated 27%.

**3. FX translation, quantified.** $1,011M AOCI swing, ~20% of the five-quarter equity build, and a 37pp range in reported YoY revenue growth across five quarters. BRL depreciation compresses reported revenue, reported EPS and reported book value at once — the three inputs to every valuation ratio above.

**4. Rate-cycle sensitivity in the treasury book.** Treasury interest income grew 2.1x as fast as total revenue in FY2025. Selic normalization compresses that line faster than the rest of the book. Partially offset by lower funding costs and better credit performance, but the revenue-line effect is direct.

**5. Funding trajectory.** LDR 60.9% → 73.4% in five quarters; wholesale debt +164% YoY. Levels remain comfortable; the direction erodes the core cost advantage.

**6. Execution concentrated in time.** A new Mexican bank build-out, an unspecified US entry, and Colombia — under a CFO who started July 13, 2026 after a seven-year predecessor moved to special adviser. BofA downgraded to Underperform with a $10 target on June 2 explicitly citing the transition. Q2 2026 will be the first reported quarter under the new CFO.

**7. Disclosure architecture.** FPI status means no 10-Q, no proxy, and no regulatory capital ratios in the sources available here. A dual-class structure is confirmed by Form 4 references to Class A shares; a secondary source cites 85% founder voting power, unverified. Twelve Form 3s filed simultaneously on March 18, 2026 alongside continued 20-F/6-K filing raises an unresolved question about Section 16 status that I decline to interpret. Practically, this means less frequent and less granular credit disclosure than a US-listed domestic bank of comparable size.

**8. US charter environment.** The OCC's August 7, 2026 rejection of Bunq's application demonstrates the current bar for foreign digital bank charters. Any US-entry optionality should be valued at close to zero until a specific vehicle is disclosed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 88.2% against 37.4% one-year realized — an 89th-percentile reading within the chain, with a pronounced backwardation (88.2% at 2 days, 53.5% at 16 days, 46.6% at 37 days, 42.9% at 128 days). The ±7.3% implied move on the August 14 expiry, combined with the absence of any Q2 financial-statement filing as of August 11, places the second-quarter print inside that window. Near-term skew is balanced (-0.5%) and near-term put/call volume is light at 0.59, so the market is pricing magnitude rather than direction into the event.

The informative signal sits further out. The December 18 tenor carries a put/call volume ratio of 3.89 against 0.14 at one month and 0.31 at three months, and the single flagged unusual trade is 3,009 contracts of the November 20 $11 put against 187 open interest (16.1x). Concentrated put activity in the November-December tenors, roughly 21% below spot, is consistent with positioning around a macro-political catalyst rather than around the earnings print. That corroborates my framing of the principal risk as macro and credit-cyclical rather than event-specific.

Short interest is 4.1% of float with 2.1 days to cover — uncrowded, meaning neither squeeze support nor confirmation from dedicated short capital.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $21.00 (+52%)

Q2 delivers stable-to-improving delinquency and coverage, resolving the disclosure gap in Nu's favor. Cost of risk holds near current levels because the mix shift toward lending is deliberate movement into secured and payroll-deductible products, which would invert the risk read. Mexico confirms break-even and turns accretive, adding a second deposit-funded balance sheet. Selic cuts lower funding costs and revive Brazilian credit demand faster than they compress treasury income. BRL holds or firms. FY2027 EPS reaches $1.07-1.15; a 30% ROE compounder at 18-19.5x forward earnings is $20-21, close to the $22 street high and to a 4.7x multiple on end-2027 book of roughly $4.20.

### Bear Case — $10.00 (-28%)

Provisioning normalizes as the 2025-26 vintages season. 300bp of additional cost of risk on $31.2B takes FY2027 EPS to roughly $0.68. BRL weakens, simultaneously cutting reported revenue, reported EPS and reported book value, and worsening consumer credit. Treasury income compresses as Selic falls. The multiple contracts to 14x on the lower number, or to 3.0-3.5x a book value that FX has stopped inflating. Both routes land at $9.50-10.00 — the BofA target. This is not a tail scenario; it is the reasonable consequence of the risks I cannot currently measure.

### Base Case — $16.50 (+19%)

Growth decelerates in local currency to the mid-20s while cost of risk drifts modestly higher, producing FY2027 EPS of about $1.00 against $1.07 consensus. FX is roughly neutral. Retained earnings compound BVPS to $3.90-4.20 by end-2027. At 16.5x FY2027 EPS, or 4.0x book, the stock is $16.50. Probability-weighting base 45% / bull 25% / bear 30% gives roughly +13% expected return over twelve months — positive, but not the asymmetry that justifies initiating a full position two days before a print on a name whose central risk variable is undisclosed.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD. Horizon: 12 months.** Existing holders should carry the position through the Q2 print; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $13.84.

**Upside/downside from $13.84:** base $16.50 = +19.2%; bull $21.00 = +51.7%; bear $10.00 = -27.7%.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires any two of:**
1. Q2 2026 disclosure showing 90+ day NPL flat or lower sequentially with coverage maintained, plus a cost-of-risk figure that does not exceed the prior four-quarter average by more than ~50bp.
2. Consensus current-year EPS revisions turning positive, or a beat of at least 5% on the current-quarter $0.19 estimate — the revision trend reversing is the single cleanest confirmation available.
3. Price at or below $11.50-12.00 with fundamentals intact, which takes next-year consensus to under 11.5x and P/B under 4.7x and restores the margin of safety.

**Upgrade to BUY requires** the credit disclosure in (1) *plus* either confirmed Mexico profitability with disclosed segment contribution, or evidence that BRL and Brazilian rate risk has meaningfully resolved, with the stock still under 14x next-year consensus.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. Cost of risk or 90+ day NPL rising materially with coverage falling — the specific configuration that converts the bear case from hypothetical to reported.
2. Loan-to-deposit rising above roughly 85%, or wholesale debt exceeding ~$7B, either of which would indicate the deposit-funding advantage is no longer keeping pace with origination.
3. A third consecutive EPS miss, or current-year consensus cut below $0.75 (a further ~7% reduction).
4. Sequential decline in active customers or ARPAC, which would break the acquisition-plus-monetization engine that drives the entire growth model.

**Downgrade to AVOID** if a credit-cost shock coincides with BRL depreciation of more than ~15% and an equity/assets ratio falling below ~13%, or if a capital raise is required to fund the Mexican bank build-out.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediately upon the Q2 2026 6-K (expected inside the August 14 options expiry window).

## Conclusion

Nu Holdings is a genuinely superior operating business trading at a multiple that would be obviously too low if its asset quality were verifiable. Twelve-point-nine times next-year consensus for a franchise earning a 4.84% ROA with 24% customer growth, 23% ARPAC growth and effectively zero physical capital intensity is not a demanding price. The problem is that the 27% drawdown is not the market being careless. Falling estimates in every window, two consecutive misses, a CFO transition, 20% of the recent equity build sourced from currency translation, a treasury income line growing at twice the rate of revenue into a probable rate-cut cycle, and a loan book that grew 53% in a year without any published delinquency data together constitute a coherent, falsifiable bear case that I cannot currently rule out.

The correct posture is to hold what you own and let the Q2 disclosure resolve the one variable that matters. If delinquencies and coverage hold, the stock is materially undervalued and the upgrade path is short. If provisioning is normalizing, $10 is a reasonable destination and there will be no shortage of time to buy. At $13.84, roughly 19% to base case against 28% to bear case with the central input unmeasured, the asymmetry does not favor initiating. **HOLD.** Analyst targets: low $10.00, high $22.00.
