## Executive Summary

Ameris Bancorp is a $28.5 billion-asset, five-state Southeastern bank with a differentiated liability structure (noninterest-bearing deposits at 30.0% of total) and three non-bank lending divisions — warehouse lending, premium finance and equipment finance — that together produced roughly 30% of first-half segment net income on minimal operating expense. Core profitability is strong: management's adjusted Q2 2026 metrics show 1.53% ROA, 14.08% return on tangible common equity and a 50.4% efficiency ratio, with the 3.88% net interest margin carrying no purchase-accounting accretion. Tangible book value per share has compounded 10.9% to 14.7% annually for five consecutive years.

The problem is price and trajectory rather than franchise quality. At $87.73 the stock trades at 1.94x tangible book value per share of $45.10 and roughly 13.2x current-year consensus EPS of $6.64 and 12.5x next-year $7.03, against a regional-bank peer median trailing multiple of 11.5x and a peer dividend yield of 3.4% versus Ameris's 0.91%. Consensus estimates have been cut in every period over both 30 and 90 days (current year -1.6%/-2.0%; next year -1.8%/-1.6%), the ratings mix has shifted from three strong-buys to one with holds doubling to four, and adjusted Q2 EPS of $1.60 came in about 3.6% below the non-GAAP consensus near $1.66.

Two disclosures from the 10-Q filed August 7 sharpen the case for patience. First, the $82.5 million pre-tax accrual for the *Byrne v. Ameris Bank* verdict — a jury finding of malice, oppression or fraud in a dispute over how the acquired Balboa Capital long-term incentive plan calculated earnings before taxes — arose from a methodology that reportedly applied to roughly 150 transferred employees, with plaintiff's counsel stating it is preparing additional claims. That tail is unaccrued and unquantified, and Item 1A of the 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors. Second, first-half loan growth of $664.3 million ran roughly three times ending-deposit growth of $211.6 million; the gap was funded by more than doubling other borrowings from $558.0 million to $1,250.0 million and lifting brokered CDs to about 6.7% of deposits, pushing loans/deposits from 96.15% to 98.19% — the fourth consecutive quarterly increase. We rate the shares HOLD with a base case of $93 (+6%), bull $107 (+22%) and bear $68 (-22%).

## Company Overview

Ameris Bank operates 163 branches across Georgia, Alabama, Florida, North Carolina and South Carolina, with total assets of $28.49 billion, loans held for investment of $22.18 billion and deposits of $22.59 billion at June 30, 2026. Four reportable segments produced $161.9 million of first-half net income: Banking Division $113.4 million, Retail Mortgage $23.7 million, Warehouse Lending $12.8 million and Premium Finance $12.1 million.

The economics of the specialty divisions are the most distinctive structural feature. Warehouse Lending generated $12.8 million of half-year net income on $1.5 million of total noninterest expense, essentially a pure spread business against mortgage originators' pipelines. Premium Finance produced $12.1 million on $7.0 million of expense. Retail Mortgage services a $9.48 billion third-party portfolio, with MSRs carried at $125.9 million against fair value of $165.1 million.

The loan book is concentrated in commercial real estate. Commercial and farmland real estate is $9.24 billion (42% of loans), residential $4.33 billion (20%), C&I $3.45 billion (16%), construction and development $1.70 billion (8%), premium finance $1.53 billion (7%) and mortgage warehouse $1.35 billion (6%). Measured on Federal Reserve Call Report definitions, investor CRE excluding owner-occupied was $8.93 billion, or 40% of loans and 261% of Tier 1 capital plus allowance against a 300% internal limit; construction and development sat at 49% against a 100% limit. Within investor CRE, office is disclosed at $1.11 billion (down from $1.26 billion at year-end), with Atlanta alone at $470 million.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 baseline.** Net income $412.2 million, diluted EPS $6.00, ROA 1.54%, ROE 10.52%. Tax-equivalent net interest income rose 10.3% to $940.7 million entirely on funding relief — interest expense fell 13.5% to $457.6 million while interest income was $1.398 billion — lifting NIM to 3.79% from 3.56%.

**First-half 2026.** Net income $161.9 million and diluted EPS $2.40, versus $197.8 million and $2.87. The entire decline traces to the litigation accrual. Adding it back at the first-half effective tax rate of 21.7% gives $64.6 million after tax, or approximately $0.96 per diluted share, for an adjusted first-half figure near $3.36. Backing Q2 GAAP EPS of $0.77 out of the half implies Q1 GAAP EPS of $1.63 and net income of $110.5 million, a record quarter. The pre-charge earnings path is upward: FY2025 ran $1.27, $1.60, $1.54, $1.59 by quarter; 2026 has delivered $1.63 and an adjusted $1.60.

**Margin composition is the key operating disclosure.** Tax-equivalent NIM was 3.88% in both Q2 and first-half 2026, up from 3.77% and 3.75%. But earning-asset yield *fell* to 5.61% from 5.64% and loan yields to 5.81% from 5.85%, while the cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 28bp to 2.66%. All margin expansion came from liability repricing. That source is nearly exhausted, and the forward indicator is clear: Q2 loan production carried a weighted-average yield of 6.20% versus 6.76% a year earlier, a 56bp decline; for the half, 6.17% versus 6.80%. Management has guided to a few basis points of quarterly NIM erosion and flagged rising deposit costs.

**Funding.** The mechanism runs: loan growth above deposit growth → wholesale substitution → higher marginal funding cost → NIM compression beyond guidance, unless deposit gathering accelerates. First-half interest-bearing deposits *declined* $145.2 million while noninterest-bearing grew $356.7 million (+5.6%). Other borrowings rose $692.0 million, consisting largely of FHLB advances including $875 million of fixed-rate advances maturing in July and August 2026 at 3.76%–3.86% plus a $342 million daily-rate credit at 3.88%. Those maturities must be rolled or replaced in the current quarter at prevailing rates.

Two mitigants are real. The category that grew is the highest-quality one, consistent with deliberate runoff of rate-sensitive balances rather than franchise erosion, and average deposits grew about 4.4% annualized in Q2 against the weaker ending figure. The available evidence does not permit distinguishing deliberate liability management from competitive loss, which is the central open question.

Liquidity backstops are substantial: $2.46 billion available at the FHLB and $2.37 billion at the discount window against $1.25 billion drawn, plus $1.20 billion of cash. Estimated uninsured deposits fell to $10.34 billion, of which $3.20 billion is collateralized municipal money, leaving roughly $7.1 billion genuinely at-risk.

**Credit.** Net charge-offs annualized 0.21% in the half (Q2 0.20%), inside the 20–25bp guide but above 0.18% for FY2025. Allowance for credit losses on loans is 1.62% ($359.5 million) plus $53.4 million for unfunded commitments. Nonaccruals rose to $120.5 million from $109.1 million, but $33.7 million is serviced GNMA-guaranteed paper where credit risk sits with the guarantor; excluding that, nonaccruals were $86.8 million versus $84.8 million — effectively flat, and NPAs ex-GNMA near 0.35% of assets. Charge-offs are concentrated outside CRE: first-half gross charge-offs were C&I $19.2 million, consumer $8.9 million, premium finance $4.3 million and CRE only $1.6 million. Consumer charge-offs rose 4.7x from $1.9 million on a $157 million book, a visible but non-threatening underwriting miss tied partly to medical lending. Special mention loans rose 54% to $61.0 million, the leading indicator to watch, and the Q2 provision increase was driven partly by raising the office qualitative factor, management reserving forward while shrinking office exposure by $143 million.

**Capital.** CET1 fell to 12.84% from 13.17% and Tier 1 leverage to 11.25%, reflecting RWA growth, $103.3 million of buybacks, $27.0 million of dividends and the accrual. Tangible common equity of $3,026.6 million equals 11.04% of tangible assets. Securities marks are not a capital issue: AFS gross unrealized losses of $29.3 million on $2.48 billion amortized cost and HTM losses of $15.4 million on only $208.2 million. Holding-company debt is limited to $135.3 million of trust preferreds at roughly 8.3%; the $110 million of 3.875% subordinated notes were redeemed October 1, 2025 before the coupon would have floated to SOFR + 3.753%. Contingent equity dilution is immaterial at roughly 0.3%, and there is no preferred outstanding.

**Rate positioning.** The July 1, 2026 simulation shows near-neutral 12-month sensitivity (+200bp → +2.8% NII; -200bp → -1.6%) but material 24-month asymmetry: +200bp → +8.9%, -200bp → -9.9%, -300bp → -14.8%. A sustained easing cycle is the more dangerous tail for this balance sheet than a hiking cycle.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance is mid-single-digit loan and deposit growth for 2026, charge-offs of 20–25bp, and slight ongoing NIM compression. First-half loans annualized about 6.2% while ending deposits annualized about 1.9%. Sixty-four percent of first-half loan growth ($425 million of $664 million) came from warehouse lending and premium finance, which grew to $1.35 billion from $1.15 billion and $1.53 billion from $1.31 billion respectively.

Fee income is moving the other way. Mortgage banking income fell to $32.5 million in Q2 from $39.2 million, on production of $1.15 billion versus $1.27 billion and gain-on-sale spread of 2.04% versus 2.22%. The open pipeline closed Q2 at $609.3 million against $719.1 million a year earlier and $701.9 million at year-end, down on both comparisons and pointing to continued fee pressure. Management is retaining more mortgages on balance sheet, converting fee income into future NII.

Consensus captures this: current-year $6.64 and next-year $7.03 imply 5.9% growth, and both have been revised down. Loan pipeline of $2.7 billion, production up 24% year over year, and the announced Nashville entry support the mid-single-digit guide, but nothing in the data supports a growth re-rating. Positive operating leverage is real — adjusted revenue +6% against adjusted expenses +3% — and shrinking the diluted share count 2.5% year over year adds roughly two points to per-share growth. The composite is high-single-digit EPS growth rather than the acceleration that would justify a premium multiple. The estimate-revision picture is the opposite of the accelerating-revisions pattern that signals systematic underestimation.

## Valuation Assessment

Derived from the 10-Q balance sheet: 67,106,587 shares outstanding, book value $60.96 per share (1.44x at $87.73), tangible book $45.10 per share (**1.94x**). Trailing four-quarter GAAP diluted EPS of $5.53 gives 15.9x; the quoted 15.85x trailing multiple uses FY2025's $6.00.

There is a genuine mechanical distortion worth naming: every trailing-GAAP screen includes the $82.5 million charge, so it understates earnings power. Annualizing Q1 GAAP $1.63 plus Q2 adjusted $1.60 implies roughly $6.45–6.55 of FY2026 adjusted EPS, or about 13.4x — and that sensitivity itself embeds a non-recurring $7.4 million Visa Class B-2 conversion gain. Consensus at $6.64 sits just above the sensitivity.

The distortion argument would matter far more if the stock were cheap on the corrected number. It is not. At 13.2x current-year and 12.5x next-year consensus, Ameris trades above the peer median trailing P/E of 11.5x (ASB 10.1x, OZK 8.4x, HOMB 11.5x, AUB 16.8x; FLG's -67.9x is uninformative). Adjusting for basis differences narrows but does not eliminate the premium. On tangible book, 1.94x against roughly 14% adjusted ROTCE prices the franchise at the upper end of the mid-cap regional range. Dividend support is thin: $0.80 annualized is a 0.91% yield on a roughly 12% payout of adjusted earnings, against a 3.4% peer median. Investors here are paid in buybacks and retained compounding rather than cash.

Base case: 13.2x next-year consensus $7.03 = $92.8. Cross-check on book: tangible book of $45.10 compounding at 12–13% reaches roughly $48.50 by mid-2027; 1.9x gives $92.2. Base **$93** (+6.0%), consistent with the $93.50 consensus median and below the $94.17 mean.

Bull case requires the deposit engine to reaccelerate so NIM holds near 3.88%, plus partial reversal of the punitive award on appeal (worth $0.50–0.75 per share after tax if half the $62.9 million were vacated), plus a renewed buyback authorization. 15x $7.15 = $107, or 2.1x forward tangible book near $50 = $105. Bull **$107** (+22%).

Bear case: deposit competition forces faster NIM compression, special-mention migration converts to substandard, and provisioning moves toward the disclosed severe-scenario sensitivity of $82.4 million (roughly 20% of a year's normalized earnings). 11x $6.20 = $68; 1.45x tangible book near $47 = $68. Bear **$68** (-22%), just above the 52-week low of $65.91.

Probability-weighting 60/20/20 across base/bull/bear gives roughly +3.6% price return plus the 0.9% yield, approximately fair with a slight negative skew. Analyst targets span $88 to $100 with a $94.17 mean; the price sits above the low end of the range and above the pre-verdict Piper Sandler ($90), DA Davidson ($87) and Truist ($80) targets. On a bank at 12.5x forward with falling estimates and a degrading ratings mix, consensus targets function as a ceiling rather than a lagging floor.

## Competitive Landscape

The durable advantages are specific. Noninterest-bearing deposits at 30.0% of the base support a NIM well above the regional median with zero purchase-accounting accretion. The footprint — metro Atlanta, Tampa, Jacksonville, Orlando, Charleston, the Carolinas — sits in high-migration MSAs. The specialty divisions generate earning-asset growth that does not depend on branch-level competition and, in warehouse lending's case, at almost no operating cost. Five-year TSR of 208.03 against a peer index of 152.71 reflects execution, not just beta.

The trajectory is more mixed. New-money loan yields down 56bp year over year while deposit costs bottom is a sector-wide squeeze visible in Ameris's own numbers, and its markets are contested by super-regionals (Truist, Regions, Synovus, Pinnacle, South State, First Horizon) plus a long tail of community banks. Interest-bearing deposits shrank in the half while brokered CDs rose; the data cannot say whether this is pricing discipline or share loss. The retail mortgage pipeline is contracting on both comparisons.

The specialty mix should not be read as diversification at the borrower level. Revenue mix has shifted, but CRE remains 40% of loans and the CRE-to-capital ratio barely moved (262% to 261%). The 25 largest relationships totaled $2.766 billion of committed exposure at year-end 2025 with zero nonaccrual, roughly 12% of loans, and cannot be trended on the available comparatives. Warehouse lending is also cyclically linked to the same origination environment pressuring Ameris's own mortgage pipeline, and premium finance carries $8.3 million of substandard loans and $4.3 million of half-year gross charge-offs.

Early adoption of ASU 2025-08 on January 1, 2026 — a standard whose purpose is eliminating day-one credit-loss expense on purchased loans — is administratively useful to an acquirer, but no transaction is announced or rumored and this should not be treated as evidence of a pending deal.

## Risk Assessment

**Litigation, including an unquantified tail.** *Byrne v. Ameris Bank* (C.D. Cal.) produced a June 12, 2026 verdict for the plaintiff on all counts: $16.525 million of economic, non-economic and statutory damages plus approximately $62.9 million of punitive damages, with judgment entered July 27, 2026. Ameris recorded $82.5 million of expense and intends to appeal. Prejudgment interest at 10% annually accrues on roughly $9 million of past economic loss from September 16, 2024, and attorneys' fees are not yet fixed, so the accrual could prove modestly insufficient. Punitive-to-compensatory ratios near 3.8x are frequently reduced on appeal, making partial reversal a plausible positive catalyst, though appellate outcomes are genuinely uncertain and this is a general inference rather than case-specific analysis. The larger issue is that the disputed incentive-plan calculation reportedly applied to roughly 150 transferred Balboa employees, and plaintiff's counsel states it is preparing additional claims. No such action has been filed on the record reviewed and no company filing discloses related claims; characterizations of internal conduct come from a single plaintiff-sourced, advocacy-oriented outlet and are unconfirmed. Treat this as a possible contingency rather than a liability. The 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors despite a judgment equal to roughly 20% of prior-year net income and 2.7% of tangible common equity.

**CRE concentration.** Investor CRE at 261% of Tier 1 plus allowance, C&D at 49% of the same base, in a portfolio where current performance is excellent (investor CRE past-dues around one basis point) but which would dominate any recession scenario. Management raising the office qualitative factor while past-dues are negligible reads as caution; it can also be read as visibility into specific credits that have not yet migrated.

**Funding and deposit competition.** Loans/deposits at 98.19% after four consecutive quarterly increases, $875 million of near-dated FHLB maturities to reprice, brokered CDs at 6.7% of deposits. The FY2025 10-K names stablecoins alongside fintechs and internet banks as deposit alternatives.

**Rate-path asymmetry.** Near-neutral over 12 months, but -200bp over 24 months costs 9.9% of NII. Both a sustained easing cycle and a renewed hiking cycle are live tails in different directions.

**Reserve model.** Substituting a 96th-percentile downside scenario for the current 50/50 baseline/S-2 weighting would raise the quantitative allowance by approximately $82.4 million, about 2.7% of tangible common equity.

**Capital return mechanics.** The $200 million authorization has $65.4 million remaining and expires October 31, 2026. First-half repurchases of $103.3 million (1,282,260 shares at roughly $80.60 average) equal 1.89% of year-end shares, accretive at 13x forward earnings, dilutive to tangible book at nearly 2x, and executed while capital ratios fell 33bp and loans/deposits rose.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 49.4% against one-year realized volatility of 23.6% — a 25.7-point premium — with the term structure in backwardation (49.4% at 11 days versus 29.4% at six months) and a call-heavy skew of -15.1%. Implied volatility rose from 32.4% to 49.4% between July 20 and August 9 while the shares fell 5.0%. Rising implied volatility on a falling price after earnings have already been reported is unusual and typically signals an anticipated near-term event; the call-heavy configuration argues against interpreting it as accumulated downside protection. The chain is thin, however — 71 contracts across four expirations — so this is low-confidence positioning data that should not carry weight against the fundamentals. Taken at face value, it prices a ±6.5% move by August 21 with an upside tilt, which is consistent with either post-judgment legal developments or corporate-action speculation. Neither is verifiable from the filings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($107, +22%)

Core earnings power is materially better than GAAP shows, and the market's screens display the distorted number. Adjusted Q2 delivered 14.08% ROTCE, 50.4% efficiency and 1.53% ROA with no purchase-accounting accretion, and Q1 was a record $1.63 GAAP quarter. Noninterest-bearing deposits grew 5.6% in the half; the highest-quality funding category expanded while only rate-sensitive balances shrank, supporting management's characterization of deliberate high-cost runoff. If deposit gathering reaccelerates to the guided mid-single digits in the second half, NIM holds near 3.88%, the guided few basis points of quarterly erosion is absorbed by 6% loan growth and 2.5% annual share shrinkage, and 2027 EPS reaches $7.15–7.30. Reserve coverage of 1.62% plus $53.4 million on unfunded commitments — roughly 1.86% of loans-plus-commitments, or about nine years of current charge-offs — absorbs normalization. Partial vacatur of the punitive award adds $0.50–0.75 per share. Tangible book compounding at 12–14% supports 2.1x on a rising base. A renewed and enlarged buyback authorization in October would signal confidence; early adoption of acquisition-friendly accounting keeps optionality live.

### Bear Case ($68, -22%)

The margin engine has been running on liability repricing that is now exhausted while asset yields decline: new production at 6.20% versus a 5.81% back book leaves little cushion, and Q2 earning-asset yield already fell year over year. Deposit growth annualizing near 2% against mid-single-digit loan growth means the loan/deposit ratio keeps climbing, $875 million of FHLB advances reprice this quarter, and marginal funding shifts further to brokered CDs, compressing NIM faster than guided. Consensus is already moving that way, cut in all four periods. Special mention loans up 54% in six months and consumer charge-offs up 4.7x are early migration; a severe macro scenario adds $82.4 million of provisioning. Investor CRE at 261% of capital with $1.11 billion of office including $470 million in Atlanta is the exposure that determines the downside. Follow-on Balboa claims, if filed, are unaccrued. At 1.94x tangible book, 12.5x next-year earnings and a 0.91% yield versus a 3.4% peer median, there is no valuation cushion and no income support; a multiple compression to 11x and 1.45x tangible book takes the shares to the 52-week low area.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $93 (+6.0% from $87.73), bull $107 (+22%), bear $68 (-22%). Probability-weighted expected return of roughly 4–5% including the dividend is approximately fair for a bank of this quality, with margin of safety insufficient for ACCUMULATE and no thesis impairment justifying REDUCE. The bar for a change in either direction is set out below.

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints in Q3 2026 (mid-to-late October) and Q4 2026 (late January 2027).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Ending deposits growing at an annualized mid-single-digit rate in Q3, with loans/deposits stabilizing at or below 98.2% and other borrowings not exceeding $1.25 billion, resolving the funding question in favor of deliberate liability management.
2. Q3 tax-equivalent NIM at 3.83% or better with new-production yields no longer falling sequentially, confirming that margin erosion stays inside the guided few basis points per quarter.
3. Price in the $76–79 range on unchanged fundamentals (approximately 11x next-year consensus and 1.65x forward tangible book), which would restore a double-digit expected return to base case.
4. Buyback authorization renewed at $200 million or more before the October 31, 2026 expiry, alongside CET1 stabilizing at 12.8% or higher.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Loans/deposits above 100%, or other borrowings above $1.6 billion, in the Q3 10-Q, indicating wholesale dependence becoming structural.
2. Q3 NIM below 3.78% (more than 10bp of sequential compression), or net charge-offs above the 25bp guidance ceiling.
3. Special mention loans exceeding $85 million, or substandard loans above $200 million, indicating migration rather than one-off normalization.
4. A filed follow-on Balboa incentive-plan action, or an appellate ruling affirming the full $62.9 million punitive award plus a material attorneys'-fee award, which would move the litigation from accrued to open-ended.
5. Next-year consensus cut below $6.75 (a further ~4%), which would take the forward multiple above 13x on deteriorating estimates.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings, expected mid-to-late October 2026, together with the buyback authorization decision due before October 31, 2026.

## Conclusion

Ameris is a well-run bank with a better-than-average deposit franchise, three profitable non-bank lending businesses, heavy reserve coverage and five years of double-digit tangible book compounding. The trailing GAAP multiple overstates its valuation because it includes the $82.5 million litigation charge, and investors who correct for that see roughly 13x forward earnings rather than 16x trailing.

That correction is not enough to make the shares attractive. Corrected for the charge, the stock still trades at a premium to a peer group whose median sits near 11.5x, at 1.94x tangible book, on a 0.91% yield, with consensus estimates falling in every measured period, the analyst mix shifting toward hold, adjusted Q2 earnings 3.6% short of expectations, and the margin's entire source of expansion — liability repricing — now spent. The funding gap is the variable that decides the next four quarters, and the filings do not permit a confident read on whether shrinking interest-bearing deposits reflect pricing discipline or competitive loss. Until the Q3 balance sheet answers that, roughly 6% base-case upside against 22% bear-case downside argues for holding existing exposure and waiting for either a better price near the mid-to-high $70s or confirmation that deposits are keeping pace with loans.
