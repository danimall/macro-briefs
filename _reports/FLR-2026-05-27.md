**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Fluor Corporation presents an unusual situation: a $6.6B engineering and construction firm with $3.2B in cash, a net-cash balance sheet, and an aggressive buyback program ($1.4B target in 2026), yet delivering near-zero segment profit margins and a 1-for-4 quarterly earnings beat record. The company's successful divestiture of its NuScale Power stake ($2.43B in total proceeds) eliminated a source of volatility and funded a 17% share count reduction over 15 months. This financial engineering provides a quantifiable path to EPS growth even if operating margins remain flat.

The core tension is between capital return mechanics (clearly positive; shares could reach 125-130M by year-end from 169M at start of 2025) and operating execution (weak; Q1 2026 total segment profit was $8M on $3.66B revenue). Fluor also faces concentrated litigation exposure: Santos appeal in July 2026, LOGCAP post-trial motions in Q3 2026, a securities class action, and four derivative suits. Any single adverse ruling could absorb a meaningful portion of the buyback benefit.

The stock trades at 21x trailing (distorted by losses) and ~18x FY26E earnings of $2.58. Peers trade at 16.5x-49x with a median of 27x. On a forward P/E basis, FLR is not cheap relative to its thin margins and legal overhang, though the buyback mechanics provide a specific pathway to $3.30 EPS in FY27 that would bring the forward multiple down to ~14x. The hold thesis rests on this mechanical accretion timeline.

## Company Overview

Fluor operates through three segments: Urban Solutions (67% of Q1 2026 revenue; life sciences, mining, infrastructure, data centers), Energy Solutions (19%; refinery, chemicals, LNG), and Mission Solutions (14%; DOE/DOD government contracts). Total backlog stands at $25.7B as of March 31, 2026, with 82% on a reimbursable basis, significantly reducing fixed-price execution risk relative to the company's troubled history.

The company completed a multi-year turnaround under Executive Chairman David Constable, transitioning to CEO James Breuer in May 2025. The turnaround's capstone was the complete divestiture of NuScale Power (a nuclear small modular reactor developer), which generated $2.43B in proceeds and eliminated a source of significant mark-to-market earnings volatility.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Margins:** FY2025 revenue was $15.5B, essentially flat versus FY2023 ($15.5B) after a modest peak of $16.3B in FY2024. Q1 2026 revenue declined 8% YoY to $3.66B. The margin profile remains structurally thin: even excluding the $96M LOGCAP legal charge, Q1 2026 segment margins would have been approximately 2.8%, below the 3.3% achieved in Q1 2025. Energy Solutions delivered a strong 10.5% margin on project closeouts, but Urban Solutions was negligible at 0.2% and Mission Solutions was negative.

**Balance Sheet:** Cash of $3.19B against total debt of $1.07B yields a net cash position of approximately $2.1B. Total liquidity of $4.1B (including $903M undrawn credit facility) provides substantial cushion. This is the best balance sheet Fluor has had in over a decade.

**Capital Return:** Since Q4 2024, Fluor has repurchased 32M shares for $1.4B, reducing the share count from 169M to approximately 140M (17% reduction). The company targets approximately $1.4B in total buybacks for 2026. If completed, share count could reach 125-130M by year-end, representing approximately 25% total accretion to per-share metrics from early 2025 levels.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow was negative $387M, primarily due to the $649M Santos litigation payment and working capital builds. Q1 2026 showed improvement at +$110M. The $473M NuScale final payment received in April 2026 provides continued buyback funding. Normalized underlying operating cash flow (ex-litigation) appears to be in the $200-400M annual range.

**Convertible Note Overhang:** $575M in 1.125% convertible notes due 2029 carry a conversion price of $45.37. With the stock at $47.34, these are in-the-money, creating potential dilution that partially offsets the buyback benefit above the conversion threshold. The company holds capped calls to hedge but the net dilution above $45.37 remains a structural headwind.

## Growth Analysis

**Backlog:** New awards of $2.69B in Q1 2026 declined 54% from $5.81B in Q1 2025. However, backlog remained stable at $25.7B due to $1.1B in scope increases on existing projects. The book-to-bill below 1.0x is a concern; if sustained, it implies future revenue declines.

**Growth Vectors:** Fluor has exposure to data center construction (won a "large-scale" U.S. project with limited notice to proceed in Q1 2026), energy transition, mining & metals, and DOE nuclear work. These are real tailwinds, but none has yet translated into meaningful above-trend revenue growth. The company's guidance range was reportedly lowered at the Q1 2026 call, suggesting near-term revenue visibility has softened.

**EPS Growth via Buyback:** If Fluor achieves $2.58 EPS in FY26 on ~135M average shares, the same earnings on 125M shares in FY27 would yield ~$2.79 per share, an 8% improvement from financial engineering alone. Analyst consensus for FY27 is $3.30, implying they expect both margin improvement and continued buyback accretion.

**Estimate Revisions:** Next-quarter estimates have risen 34.4% over 90 days ($0.63 → $0.85), suggesting analysts see margin recovery in the back half. Current-year estimates declined 6.7% in the past 30 days (from $2.76 to $2.58), reflecting Q1 weakness. The net pattern is mixed: near-term revision momentum is positive but full-year confidence has weakened.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.34, Fluor trades at approximately:
- 18.3x FY26E EPS of $2.58
- 14.3x FY27E EPS of $3.30
- 0.43x revenue
- ~$6.6B market cap less $2.1B net cash = ~$4.5B enterprise value on $15.5B revenue

Peer multiples range from 16.5x (TTEK) to 49.2x (MYRG), with a median of 27.0x. On a forward P/E basis, FLR appears modestly below the peer median. However, Fluor's margins are among the lowest in the group (sub-3% segment margins vs. peers typically earning 5-10%), which justifies a discount.

The bull case from Seeking Alpha pegs fair value at $58-62, assuming buyback completion and normalized FCF of $350-400M (8-10% FCF yield at current price). This would require segment margins recovering to the 3.5-4.0% range, which is plausible but not yet demonstrated under the new leadership.

The bear case: if margins remain at Q1 levels and litigation absorbs cash, normalized EPS is closer to $2.00, making the stock expensive at 24x.

## Competitive Landscape

Fluor's competitive position has stabilized but not improved. The shift to 82% reimbursable backlog reduces execution risk but also caps margin upside: reimbursable contracts typically earn 2-4% margins versus 5-8%+ on fixed-price (when executed well). The company's 5-year TSR of 248% trails its peer group at 388%, suggesting the market continues to assign a discount for historical execution failures.

Positive factors: diversified end-market exposure across data centers, life sciences, mining, LNG, and government; large-scale project capability few competitors can match; improved risk management discipline.

Negative factors: thin margins suggest limited pricing power; the 54% decline in new awards raises questions about competitive positioning in the current cycle; management turnover (multiple executive departures in 2024-2026) creates continuity risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Litigation Concentration (Severe):** Five concurrent major legal matters: Santos appeal (July 2026, potential for additional losses if appeal fails and insurance coverage is denied), LOGCAP post-trial (treble damages could exceed the $96M already reserved), securities class action, NuScale class action, and derivative suits. This density of active litigation is unusual and creates binary downside risk.

**Margin Sustainability:** Q1 2026's 0.2% segment margin (even excluding charges) suggests the operating business remains fragile. A single cost overrun on a large project (as demonstrated by the $37M mining JV charge) can erase an entire quarter's profit.

**Backlog Replenishment:** Book-to-bill below 1.0x in Q1 2026. If new awards don't recover, revenue will decline in 2027 regardless of buyback-driven EPS growth.

**Convertible Dilution:** $575M in convertibles with a $45.37 conversion price create incremental dilution above current stock price levels, partially neutralizing buyback benefits.

**Key Person Risk:** Constable's transition to consulting role and establishment of a selling plan suggest the turnaround architect has completed his work. New CEO Breuer has limited track record in the top role.

### Options Market Signal

The market has priced out the post-earnings event premium and views near-term vol as relatively benign. Unusual activity in September $55 calls (1,151 contracts at 3.7x vol/OI) suggests some participants expect the stock to recover toward pre-earnings levels within 4 months. The flat term structure (50.6% near vs. 52.4% far) indicates no specific binary event is being priced beyond normal operations.

The options market is moderately constructive, consistent with a hold-with-upside-bias thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($55-60, 16-27% upside):**
- Buyback reduces share count to 125-130M by year-end 2026, mechanically driving EPS toward $3.30 in FY27
- Santos appeal resolves favorably (July 2026) or insurance coverage is confirmed, reducing litigation overhang
- Data center and LNG project awards accelerate in H2 2026, restoring book-to-bill above 1.0x
- Segment margins recover to 3.5-4.0% as project closeouts and new profitable work ramp
- Multiple re-rates toward peer median of 27x as litigation overhang clears

**Bear Case ($37-40, 16-22% downside):**
- Santos appeal fails and insurance recovery is denied, resulting in an additional $200-400M charge
- LOGCAP treble damages significantly exceed $96M reserve
- Operating margins remain sub-2% as cost overruns persist
- New awards stay depressed, signaling market share losses
- Convertible dilution + litigation cash drain limits buyback completion

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 segment margins above 3.5% demonstrating operational recovery without closeout tailwinds
2. Santos appeal resolved favorably (expected July 2026) removing the single largest litigation overhang
3. New awards exceeding $5B in any quarter (restoring book-to-bill above 1.0x)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Santos appeal ruled against Fluor with insurance coverage denied, consuming substantial cash and limiting buyback completion
2. Two consecutive quarters of segment margins below 2.0% excluding legal charges
3. Management announces buyback slowdown or suspension due to cash requirements

**Reassessment timeline:** July 2026 (Santos appeal resolution) is the key date. The Q2 2026 earnings report (likely August) will provide the next operational data point on margin recovery.

## Conclusion

Fluor presents a specific, identifiable 12-month catalyst: mechanical EPS accretion of 10-15% from continued share repurchases funded by the NuScale divestiture windfall. The $1.4B buyback target for 2026, partially funded by $473M in April NuScale proceeds, is being executed aggressively (11M shares in Q1 alone at ~$46 average). On an FY27 basis at $3.30 consensus, the stock trades at 14.3x, a discount to peers that narrows as the litigation calendar clears.

However, the operating business has not yet demonstrated the margin profile that would justify a higher-conviction recommendation. A 1-for-4 earnings beat record, declining new awards, and concentrated litigation exposure create enough uncertainty to prevent upgrading from HOLD. The thesis is specific (buyback-driven EPS growth + litigation resolution timeline) but operating execution risk and the legal tail remain unresolved. HOLD with a positive 12-month skew, pending July 2026 Santos resolution and Q2 margin data.
