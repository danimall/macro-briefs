**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Toll Brothers is the largest US luxury homebuilder and, by balance-sheet quality, the best-capitalized name in a cyclically challenged group. The investment debate is narrow and specific: FY2025 revenue was flat at $10.97B while net income fell 14% to $1.35B, and the deterioration accelerated into FY2026 — Q2 (ended April 30, 2026) home-sale revenue fell 7%, deliveries fell 14%, and net income fell 26% to $260.6M. The mechanism is gross-margin compression. Home-sales cost of revenue has moved from 73.4% of revenue in FY2024 to 74.4% in FY2025 to 76.1% in Q2 FY2026, driven by higher sales incentives to defend pace in soft demand and a deliberate mix shift toward lower-margin spec (quick move-in) and "affordable luxury" homes. Backlog, the cleanest leading indicator, was down 22% in units at fiscal year-end 2025 and still down 11% in units at mid-year 2026. The company is defending its revenue base through footprint expansion (446 → 459 communities) even as per-community sales pace declines.

Against that earnings downtrend sits a genuinely strong balance sheet and a disciplined capital-return model. Debt-to-total-cap is 0.25x, there are no revolver borrowings against $2.24B available, and roughly $1.03B of FY2025 free cash flow funded $651M of buybacks (reducing share count ~15% over three years) plus a dividend raised during the downturn. Management extended revolver and term-loan maturities to 2031 in February 2026, leaving a $450M 4.875% note due March 2027 as the nearest maturity. The stock trades at ~11.3x trailing and ~11.4x forward EPS — a discount to the peer P/E median of 15.8x, though that peer set is heterogeneous and several comparables carry their own cyclical distortions.

We rate TOL a HOLD. The valuation is reasonable and the balance sheet provides real downside support, but forward earnings are declining, the margin compression may be partly structural rather than purely cyclical, and there is no identified near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple higher. A completed CEO transition (Karl Mistry in, Douglas Yearley to Executive Chairman) adds strategic-continuity uncertainty. Consumer Discretionary has been a persistently negative-selection-alpha sector, which raises the bar for any bullish call here; we do not clear it.

## Company Overview

Toll Brothers designs, builds, markets, sells, and finances single-family detached, attached, master-planned, active-adult, and urban high-rise homes. At fiscal year-end (October 31, 2025) it operated in 24 states plus DC, sold from 446 communities (up from 408 a year earlier), and controlled roughly 76,100 home sites (33,000 owned, 43,100 optioned). It delivered 11,292 homes in FY2025.

The company's differentiation within homebuilding rests on four pillars. First, an affluent customer base: average delivered price ~$960K, average price in backlog ~$1,182K, with management noting buyers "have higher incomes and greater accumulated wealth," which partially insulates TOL from the affordability crunch hitting entry-level builders. Second, vertical integration across architectural, engineering, mortgage, title, land development, insurance, and smart-home subsidiaries. Third, a deliberate broadening into lower price points ("affordable luxury"), more geographies, and a higher spec-home mix, which grows volume but carries lower gross margins and faster downside price sensitivity. Fourth, five geographic segments (North, Mid-Atlantic, South, Mountain, Pacific), with Mountain now the largest revenue region and Pacific the highest ASP at ~$1.6M.

The FY2026 fiscal year ends October 31, 2026. The strategic direction has simplified: in September 2025 TOL announced an exit from its for-rent apartment/rental-JV business, selling roughly half the portfolio and its operating platform to Kennedy Wilson for ~$380M gross (~$330M net), substantially completed in H1 FY2026. In May 2026 it made a tuck-in acquisition of Buffington Homes of Arkansas (9 communities, ~1,500 home sites).

## Financial Analysis

The core financial story is that earnings are declining despite flat revenue and a record community count — a margin problem, not a top-line problem.

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 10.28 | 9.99 | 10.85 | 10.97 |
| Operating income ($B) | 1.51 | 1.72 | 2.04 | 1.72 |
| Net income ($B) | 1.29 | 1.37 | 1.57 | 1.35 |
| Home-sales cost of revenue (% rev) | — | 73.6% | 73.4% | 74.4% |
| Diluted shares (M) | 118.0 | 111.0 | 104.7 | 99.8 |

FY2024 benefited from a one-time $175M pre-tax land-parcel gain in Northern Virginia that did not recur, which flatters the year-over-year decline somewhat. H1 FY2026 SG&A rose to 11.8% of home-sales revenue from 10.9% as new communities opened, layering operating deleverage on top of gross-margin erosion. Land impairments and write-offs are also rising: $65.9M in FY2025, and $44.2M in H1 FY2026 versus $26.2M in the prior-year H1, concentrated in Pacific and Mid-Atlantic. A separate $57.8M of other-than-temporary impairments hit the remaining Rental Property JVs in H1 as the multifamily wind-down proceeded.

The balance sheet is the primary reason this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID. D/E is ~0.75, equity $8.27B, cash $1.26B. The Altman Z of 2.74 sits in the grey zone, which reflects the inventory-heavy homebuilder model rather than distress. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.11B and free cash flow ~$1.03B. Capital allocation is shareholder-return-heavy: $651M repurchased in FY2025 (plus $627M in FY2024), a ~15% share-count reduction over three years, and a dividend raised to $0.26/quarter in March 2026.

One risk-monitoring item on the financing side: the interest-rate swaps expired in October 2025, so the entire revolver and term loan now floats. A 1% rate move changes interest incurred by ~$8.3M/year. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, that is a modest but non-trivial headwind, and it compounds the demand-side sensitivity of buyer mortgage rates.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the weakest part of this story, and the leading indicators point down before they point up. Backlog fell 15% in value at October 31, 2025 ($5.49B, 4,647 homes), and dollar backlog recovered nominally to $6.32B at April 30, 2026. Because roughly 98% of the October-2025 backlog is expected to deliver by October 2026, the backlog decline mechanically foreshadows lower FY2026 deliveries and revenue. The rising spec-home mix reinforces this: spec homes build and turn faster, but they expose TOL to more rapid price cuts to avoid carrying finished inventory, so a higher spec share both accelerates the timing and deepens the margin risk of a soft market.

Estimate revisions are consistent with a flat-to-soft near-term profile. Consensus for the current year sits at $12.77, unchanged over both 30 and 90 days; next-year sits at $14.15, also flat. The current-quarter estimate has been cut 15.6% over 90 days (to $2.93), while the next quarter has been revised up 10.6% (to $5.05), which nets to a stable full-year picture with quarter-to-quarter timing noise. This is not the >30% positive revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection; it is a company being estimated to earn roughly flat consolidated EPS while the mix and margin composition shift. The earnings beat record is reassuring on execution (3 beats, 1 miss over the last four quarters, most recently +4.9%) but does not change the direction of the underlying trend.

The long-term bull framework — structural US housing undersupply, favorable demographics, growth in upper-income households — is real and is management's stated thesis. But it is a multi-year story that requires either a demand recovery or a stabilization of incentives to translate into earnings growth, and neither is visible in current data. Community-count growth defends the revenue base but comes at the cost of SG&A deleverage while per-community pace declines. We score Growth Prospects at 4.0.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$145–149, TOL trades at ~11.3x trailing EPS ($13.49) and ~11.4x forward EPS (consensus current-year $12.77). Against the homebuilding peer median P/E of 15.8x, TOL looks cheap on the surface. But the peer set is heterogeneous and the comparison requires care:

|--------|------|---------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| NVR | NVR, Inc. | $17B | 15.8 | — | -27% |
| BLD | Topbuild | $10B | 23.0 | — | -37% |
| LEN | Lennar | $17B | 13.6 | 2.2% | -43% |
| TMHC | Taylor Morrison | $7B | 10.7 | — | -0% |
| IBP | Installed Bldg Prods | $6B | 22.1 | 0.8% | -38% |

TOL's ~11.4x forward sits above Taylor Morrison (10.7x) and below Lennar (13.6x) and NVR (15.8x); BLD and IBP are products/installation companies that command structurally higher multiples. On its own five-year history, TOL's multiple is not stretched, but the cheapness is not evidence of mispricing — it is consistent with an earnings base that consensus expects to be roughly flat-to-down, in a sector that has structurally traded at low-single-digit-to-mid-teens multiples through cycles precisely because earnings are cyclical and mean-reverting.

Cheapness in cyclicals is not a buy signal by itself, and here there is no identified catalyst to close the gap. Revenue growth is roughly zero, margins are compressing, and estimate revisions are flat. The cycle-adjusted argument cuts against a re-rating rather than for one: trailing EPS of $13.49 sits above the forward consensus of $12.77, meaning the trailing period reflects a mild peak, not a trough. A stock earning down toward mid-cycle should not command a peak-cycle multiple.

Analyst price targets bracket the stock: low $122, high $187, mean $168.2, median $175 — implying meaningful upside to consensus. On a stock with rising estimates and a sub-30x forward multiple, we would treat those targets as floors that lag reality. Here, estimates are flat rather than rising, so the targets carry less weight as forward indicators; they reflect a view that demand normalizes and the multiple holds. The realistic base case is a return to fair value in the low-$160s (roughly 12.5–13x on a normalized ~$12.75–13.00 earnings base), rather than the median target. The absolute dividend yield (~0.7%) provides negligible valuation support. We score Valuation at 5.0 — reasonable but not asymmetric, with the value-trap risk of a cyclical whose earnings are still declining offsetting the peer-relative discount.

## Competitive Landscape

TOL's competitive position is stable-to-mildly-eroding rather than clearly improving or deteriorating. Homebuilding is highly competitive and fragmented; TOL competes with national/regional builders and the resale market. Its durable edge is the luxury brand, scale, balance-sheet stability, and vertical integration — advantages that hold up better than most in a soft cycle because its affluent buyers are less rate- and affordability-constrained than the first-time buyers that pressure D.R. Horton and Lennar.

To grow, TOL has pushed down into "affordable luxury" and spec homes, precisely the segments now seeing the most price and margin pressure. Segment data confirms the tension: South and Mountain (the more affordable, higher-volume regions) posted the sharpest pretax-income declines and mix-driven margin erosion, while premium Pacific ASPs rose even as Pacific backlog units fell 31%. The company is defending share and revenue through footprint and price-point expansion, but at the cost of margin quality — a trajectory that is neutral at best for the moat. Community count still growing is a positive on the revenue base; declining per-community pace (~2% YoY) and the rising spec share are offsets. We score Competitive Position at 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

The company-specific risks that matter most, beyond the sector-level cyclicality that is permanently priced:

1. **Structural vs. cyclical margin compression.** If the incentive intensity and lower-margin spec/affordable mix are the new baseline rather than a cyclical trough, then normalized earnings power is lower than the trailing figure implies, and the stock is less cheap than it looks. This is the central risk and the primary reason we cannot rate the stock higher.

2. **CEO transition.** Karl Mistry is now CEO (confirmed by his SOX certification on the Q2 FY2026 10-Q); Douglas Yearley, the long-tenured prior CEO, moved to Executive Chairman by June 2026, corroborated by a cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks (December 2025 through May 2026). Leadership succession of an influential CEO introduces strategic-continuity uncertainty, and the risk factors flag several senior executives as retirement-eligible. Early press framing suggests Mistry's agenda continues luxury expansion, but the trade-press content could not be fully verified, and execution is unproven.

3. **California / Pacific concentration.** A significant portion of revenue and above-average gross margin comes from California, exposing TOL to regulatory, wildfire/earthquake, and affordability risk. Pacific backlog units fell 31% YoY.

4. **Floating-rate exposure post-swap expiry.** The entire revolver and term loan now floats after the October 2025 swap expiration; a 1% move is ~$8.3M/year of interest. Demand is also directly rate-sensitive through buyer mortgage affordability.

5. **JV guarantee exposure.** ~$417M maximum estimated exposure under guarantees of unconsolidated-entity debt, plus ~$122.5M of guarantees retained after selling the rental JVs to Kennedy Wilson (partly backstopped by a reimbursement agreement).

6. **Impairment trajectory.** Rising land and JV impairments signal that management is marking down softer assets, concentrated in Pacific and Mid-Atlantic.

The nearest debt maturity is manageable given $1.26B cash, ~$1B FCF, and $2.24B of undrawn revolver capacity, and the February 2026 maturity extensions pushed the revolver and a portion of the term loan out to 2031. Insider activity is mildly negative but routine: option-exercise-and-sell transactions by Yearley (~$12.2M in June 2026, ~$11.5M in February 2026) and COO Parahus ($1.1M in June 2026) tied to deep-in-the-money options struck at $31.61 and RSU vesting — diversification, not distress. There is no open-market insider buying to lean on as a bullish signal.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mixed and does not strongly confirm either direction. Near-term ATM IV is 38.6% versus 34.0% one-year historical vol — modestly rich (4.6pp spread), with the term structure in backwardation (38.6% near-term vs 34.9% long-term), which typically prices imminent event stress. The put/call skew is call-heavy (-4.9%) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.30, both leaning slightly bullish on flow, but the put/call open-interest ratio is elevated at 1.34, and IV has collapsed sharply since the prior snapshot (62.1% → 38.6%, -23.5pp), reflecting the passage of the May earnings event. The net read: the market is pricing a ±8.9% move at the nearest expiry with no decisive directional lean, consistent with our HOLD. The absence of a clear put-skew warning is a mild positive for the balance-sheet-supported floor thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** TOL is the highest-quality name in homebuilding, with a fortress balance sheet, disciplined capital return, and an affluent buyer base more resilient than entry-level peers. At ~11.4x forward EPS and a discount to the peer median, the stock is inexpensive. If demand stabilizes and incentives normalize — supported by structural housing undersupply, favorable demographics, and growth in upper-income households — earnings re-accelerate toward the $14+ next-year consensus, the multiple holds or expands modestly, and the stock returns to the low-$180s, near the analyst high target. The multifamily exit simplifies the business toward higher-ROE for-sale operations, and continued buybacks compound per-share value even in a flat-earnings scenario. Bull case ~$185 (roughly 13x on a $14.15 next-year base).

**Bear Case.** The margin compression proves structural rather than cyclical. The deliberate mix shift into spec and affordable-luxury homes permanently lowers gross margin (the cost ratio holds near 76%+), backlog erosion translates into falling FY2026 deliveries and revenue, and higher-for-longer rates keep buyer affordability constrained while raising floating-rate interest cost. Normalized earnings power is meaningfully below trailing, so the "cheap" multiple is a value trap. Impairments accelerate in Pacific and Mid-Atlantic, and the CEO transition introduces execution missteps. The stock derates toward the analyst low of $122 (roughly -16% to -18% from current levels), buffered somewhat by the balance-sheet floor and ongoing buybacks. Bear case ~$120–125.

The realistic base case sits in between: roughly flat consolidated earnings near consensus, a stock that trades within a $130–165 band, and a return driven more by buyback-supported per-share arithmetic than by multiple expansion. That is a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (hold_rationale: uncertain — the evidence does not support a confident directional call; earnings are declining and there is no identified catalyst, but the balance sheet and valuation prevent a downgrade to AVOID).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Home-sales gross margin stabilizes or improves — the cost ratio holding below ~75% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the incentive/mix pressure is cyclical rather than structural.
- Backlog units turn positive year-over-year, or net signed contracts (orders) accelerate, indicating demand recovery ahead of deliveries.
- Estimate revisions turn positive by >5–10% on the current and next fiscal year, combined with a pullback that pushes forward P/E below ~10x (a clearer margin of safety).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The home-sales cost ratio deteriorates further toward or above ~77–78%, confirming structural margin impairment.
- Deliveries and revenue decline accelerate (e.g., deliveries down more than ~15% YoY for two consecutive quarters) alongside rising land/JV impairments.
- A dividend cut or a pause in buybacks — a signal that FCF is no longer comfortably funding the shareholder-return model.
- Evidence of strategic disruption from the CEO transition (material shift in land-spend discipline or leverage policy).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the fiscal Q3 FY2026 earnings release (expected around late August 2026), which will show whether the margin erosion and backlog decline stabilized or worsened. The $450M note maturity (March 2027) is a monitoring item but not an imminent risk given liquidity.

## Conclusion

Toll Brothers is a well-run, well-capitalized company caught in an earnings downtrend. The quality is real and provides genuine downside support. But quality alone does not make the stock a buy when net income is falling 14–26%, gross margins are compressing over the reported periods, backlog is down double digits, and consensus estimates are flat rather than inflecting. The ~11.4x forward multiple is reasonable and modestly below peers, yet it prices a mildly-above-mid-cycle earnings base that consensus expects to hold flat, with no catalyst to re-rate it. The completed CEO transition adds continuity uncertainty. In a Consumer Discretionary sector where security selection has been a persistent alpha drag, the burden of proof for a bullish call is higher than usual, and TOL does not clear it. We rate the stock HOLD, with a modestly negative bias on growth and a balance-sheet floor limiting downside; the trajectory of the gross-margin ratio at the next earnings release is the single variable that would move us to BUY (stabilization) or AVOID (further deterioration).
