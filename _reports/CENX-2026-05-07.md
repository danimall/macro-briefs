## Executive Summary

Century Aluminum is the largest domestic primary aluminum producer in the United States, operating approximately 770,000 tonnes per year of capacity across facilities in Kentucky, South Carolina, and Iceland. The company is experiencing a major earnings inflection: the Midwest Premium tripled from $427/t in 2024 to $1,295/t in 2025 due to Section 232 tariff increases (now 50%), and global aluminum prices have surged 46% YoY to $3,578/t. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $215-235M annualizes to approximately $900M, up from $42M in 2025 net income.

The stock has appreciated approximately 280% over the past year, reflecting this earnings power transformation. Forward EPS estimates have risen 76% (current year) and 133% (next year) over 90 days, confirming the inflection is real and accelerating. At approximately 6x 2026E EBITDA, the valuation is not egregious for the earnings power being delivered. However, three factors constrain conviction: (1) the earnings inflection is almost entirely policy-dependent (Section 232 tariffs), not operational, (2) every C-suite executive and Glencore (36.4% owner) sold aggressively in Q1 2026 totaling $343M, and (3) a material weakness in internal controls and financial restatement reduce confidence in reported figures.

The company holds genuine strategic assets — including a $500M DOE-backed Oklahoma smelter project, low-carbon Icelandic production, and $1.54B in federal NOL carryforwards — but the commodity cyclicality and policy dependency create a risk profile where substantial downside exists if tariffs are modified or aluminum prices revert. The current price reflects much of the near-term upside while leaving meaningful downside risk inadequately compensated.

## Company Overview

Century Aluminum operates three primary aluminum smelters: Sebree (Kentucky, 220,000 tpy), Mt. Holly (South Carolina, 230,000 tpy), and Grundartangi (Iceland, 320,000 tpy). Total capacity is ~770,000 tonnes, though 2025 production was reduced to 638,000 tonnes due to a transformer failure at Grundartangi and partial Mt. Holly curtailment. The company also owns 55% of Jamalco (Jamaica), a bauxite mining and alumina refinery providing vertical integration, and a carbon anode facility in the Netherlands.

Glencore holds 36.4% of outstanding shares and accounts for 54% of consolidated revenue, creating an unusually concentrated dual relationship as both dominant customer and controlling shareholder. The company's entire U.S. production benefits from 50% Section 232 tariffs on imported aluminum, while its Icelandic production serves the EU market (also tariff-protected).

Century is developing a 750,000 tpy greenfield smelter in Inola, Oklahoma through a 60/40 JV with Emirates Global Aluminium (EGA), supported by a $500M DOE cooperative agreement. No final investment decision has been made, and the project requires finalizing a power supply agreement with Public Service Company of Oklahoma.

## Growth Analysis

Century's growth catalysts are both near-term and structural:

**Near-term (6-12 months):**
- Grundartangi restart (end of April 2026): restores ~210,000 tpy of idled capacity
- Mt. Holly expansion: first hot metal in April 2026, full production by June 2026, adding ~57,500 tpy (10% increase in U.S. primary aluminum output)
- Sustained high aluminum prices ($3,578/t as of April 2026) and Midwest Premium (~$1,300/t)
- Section 45X production tax credits (10% of eligible costs)

**Long-term (3-10 years):**
- Oklahoma smelter JV (40% of 750,000 tpy): potentially $300-400M in proportional EBITDA contribution by decade-end
- Structural demand growth from EVs, grid buildout, aerospace
- Low global aluminum inventories + challenged supply growth
- Carbon regulations favoring Century's low-carbon Icelandic production ("Natur-Al" brand)

**Estimate revision velocity confirms the inflection.** Current year EPS estimates rose +76.1% over 90 days and next year rose +132.6% over 90 days. Next quarter estimates are up +65.9% over 90 days. This magnitude of upward revision across all periods confirms the market is systematically catching up to the earnings reality.

However, this is a commodity cycle. Volume growth beyond the near-term capacity restarts depends on the Oklahoma project that hasn't received a final investment decision, doesn't have a power supply agreement, and requires $4B in total capital. The bull case for volume-driven growth is years away.

## Valuation Assessment

At $63.23, CENX trades at:
- ~149x trailing P/E (based on $0.42 trailing EPS — heavily distorted by Grundartangi outage)
- ~6.0x forward P/E on $10.51 FY2026E consensus EPS
- ~6.1x EV/2026E EBITDA (per Seeking Alpha analysis)
- ~74x 2025 FCF ($85M on $6.3B market cap)

The trailing metrics are meaningless given the earnings inflection. The relevant comparison is forward P/E of ~6.0x against peers at a median of 15.5x. This apparent discount exists because: (1) the market recognizes the cyclicality and tariff dependency, (2) the forward estimates assume sustained aluminum at $3,500+ and tariffs at 50%, and (3) the company has a 4-quarter consecutive miss record, suggesting execution risk.

Analyst consensus targets range from $75 to $86, with a median of $77 — implying 19-36% upside. All 3 covering analysts rate the stock Buy. The targets were likely set when the stock was lower and may not fully reflect the price appreciation to $63.

At 6x forward earnings, the stock looks cheap if you believe the earnings are sustainable. But "sustainable" requires: (1) aluminum prices staying above $3,000/t (vs. 2015-2020 average of ~$1,800), (2) Section 232 tariffs remaining at 50% (policy risk), and (3) no operational disruptions (against a track record that includes Grundartangi failure and Hawesville curtailment). The market is implicitly discounting forward earnings by assigning a 6x multiple to a cyclical commodity company at peak pricing — this is standard practice for cyclical peaks, not mispricing.

Versus peers: SEE at 12.2x, AXTA at 16.2x, EXP at 15.5x all have less commodity cyclicality and more diversified revenue streams. The 6x multiple CENX commands is appropriate for the risk profile.

## Competitive Landscape

Century's competitive position has materially strengthened over the past 12 months:

**Structural advantages:**
- Only large-scale domestic aluminum producer in a market that imports ~90% of primary aluminum needs
- 50% tariff wall creates $1,300/t Midwest Premium that flows directly to margin
- $500M DOE support + bipartisan political backing (national security framing)
- Duty-free access to both U.S. and EU markets (the two shortest-supply aluminum markets globally)
- Low-carbon production from Iceland serves ESG-focused industrial buyers at premium pricing
- Vertical integration through Jamalco reduces alumina supply chain vulnerability

**Vulnerabilities:**
- The competitive "moat" is overwhelmingly a government tariff, not operational superiority
- Without Section 232, Century's U.S. operations are marginal producers that have historically idled capacity (Hawesville curtailed 2022-2026)
- Global competitors (Rusal, Norsk Hydro, Alcoa) operate at lower cost curves in regions with cheaper power
- Customer concentration (Glencore 54%) creates a single point of failure

The competitive position trajectory is positive — from a struggling producer intermittently curtailing capacity to a fully utilized operator with government backing and a greenfield expansion project. The improvement is legitimate. But the source of the advantage (tariff protection) is inherently fragile relative to structural moats like technology or network effects.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Policy Risk (HIGH).** The entire Midwest Premium explosion — adding ~$560M in annualized U.S. revenue — is a direct function of 50% Section 232 tariffs. The Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling against presidential tariff authority under IEEPA, while not directly affecting Section 232, introduces legal uncertainty. Any tariff reduction (even from 50% to 25%) would immediately cut the Midwest Premium by hundreds of dollars per tonne and crater margins.

**2. Commodity Price Cyclicality (HIGH).** Aluminum prices are at cyclical highs ($3,578/t vs. long-term average ~$2,000-2,500). The Iran conflict and supply disruptions are supporting prices. If geopolitical conditions normalize, aluminum could retreat 20-30% rapidly. Century has essentially zero ability to offset price declines operationally.

**3. Insider Selling (MODERATE-HIGH).** The CEO sold $15.5M in two transactions (Jan/Mar 2026). The CFO sold $845K. Every named SVP sold. Glencore sold 6.3M shares for $327M. Total insider/insider-adjacent selling: ~$343M in Q1 2026. While post-vesting sales are common, the universality and magnitude during a period of strong earnings deserve weight. Those with the most information about tariff sustainability and aluminum price outlook chose to de-risk at these levels.

**4. Material Weakness and Restatement (MODERATE).** The March 2026 8-K regarding non-reliance on previously issued financial statements (Jamalco consolidation methodology) introduces reporting reliability risk during a period when investors are relying on forward guidance.

**5. Operational Risk (MODERATE).** The Grundartangi transformer failure (Sept-Oct 2025) idled two-thirds of the facility for 6+ months, demonstrating the fragility of smelter operations. Recovery timeline extended multiple times before being moved forward.

**6. Oklahoma Project Execution (LOW — near-term).** The $4B smelter is optionality that adds no near-term earnings risk but creates potential for future dilution, cost overruns, or write-offs if the project fails.

**7. Section 45X Credit Phase-out (MODERATE — long-term).** The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (signed July 2025) removed the critical minerals exemption from the Section 45X phase-out. Credits will begin reducing by 25%/year in 2031 and reach 0% in 2034, removing a meaningful earnings tailwind over the medium term.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75-86, representing analyst target range; +19% to +36%):**
- Aluminum prices remain elevated ($3,000-3,500/t) on structural supply deficit, geopolitical disruption, and de-stocking
- Section 232 tariffs sustained at 50% through 2028+, maintaining $1,200-1,500/t Midwest Premium
- Grundartangi restart + Mt. Holly expansion bring production to 770,000+ tpy (full capacity)
- FY2026 EPS achieves or exceeds $10.51 consensus
- Oklahoma project advances to FID, de-risking long-term growth
- Forward multiple re-rates from 6x to 8-10x as earnings sustainability becomes evident

**Bear Case ($30-40, representing -37% to -52%):**

- Aluminum prices revert toward $2,500/t on geopolitical normalization or global demand weakness

- 4-quarter consecutive miss pattern continues (execution risk)
- Glencore further reduces stake, signaling waning commitment to the relationship

## Conclusion

Century Aluminum represents a genuine earnings inflection — the combination of 50% Section 232 tariffs, rising global aluminum prices, and capacity restarts has transformed a marginal producer into a highly profitable operator. The estimate revision velocity (+76% current year, +133% next year over 90 days) confirms the inflection is real and broad-based.

However, this is a cyclical commodity company at peak pricing whose earnings power is overwhelmingly driven by government tariff policy rather than operational differentiation. The approximately 280% appreciation over 12 months has already priced in substantial improvement. The universal insider selling ($343M in Q1 2026 across CEO, CFO, all SVPs, and Glencore) is the strongest available signal about how those with the most information view risk/reward at current levels. The material weakness and restatement add reporting uncertainty.

At ~6x forward P/E, the stock is not expensive if the cycle persists. But cyclical commodity companies trade at low forward multiples near peaks precisely because the market recognizes the earnings are transitory. The risk/reward skews modestly negative from $63: analyst targets suggest 19-36% upside while the bear case (tariff modification or aluminum price reversion) presents 37-52% downside. The probability-weighted expected return — considering tariff policy uncertainty, commodity price cyclicality, and universal insider de-risking — does not justify a BUY at current levels. Hold for existing positions with tight monitoring of tariff developments and Q1 execution.
