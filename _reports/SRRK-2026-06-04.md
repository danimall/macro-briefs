**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Scholar Rock is a late-stage rare-disease biopharmaceutical company whose entire near-term value rests on a single asset, apitegromab, a myostatin-inhibiting antibody positioned as the first muscle-directed adjunct therapy for spinal muscular atrophy (SMA). The drug met its primary endpoint with statistical significance in the pivotal Phase 3 SAPPHIRE trial, which removed the efficacy question. The investability of the stock now turns on a manufacturing problem rather than a science problem. In September 2025 the FDA issued a Complete Response Letter rejecting the apitegromab BLA, citing cGMP deficiencies at a third-party fill-finish facility (Catalent Indiana, now part of Novo Nordisk) that subsequently received a Form 483, an Official Action Indicated classification, and a November 2025 Warning Letter. The drug substance manufacturer was not implicated, and the CRL raised no efficacy or safety concerns.

The story has materially improved since the CRL. Scholar Rock resubmitted the BLA with a dual fill-finish strategy (Catalent Indiana plus a second US site), the FDA accepted the resubmission and set a hard PDUFA action date of September 30, 2026, the agency re-inspected Catalent Indiana, and the second facility is expected to have commercial supply early in Q3 2026. Management reinforced the balance sheet to roughly $480M in cash via a $100M Blue Owl debt draw and $98M of net ATM equity proceeds in Q1 2026. The remaining binary is whether the Catalent re-inspection produces a satisfactory re-classification and whether the EU's mutual-recognition arrangement with the FDA delays a positive CHMP opinion expected mid-2026.

We rate SRRK a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The 12-month thesis is anchored to a specific, dated catalyst path that could re-rate the stock if approval lands, and the stock trades at roughly $45.69, about 5% below the lowest published analyst target of $48 and well below the $59 mean. We stop short of a BUY because the central event remains a genuine binary regulatory decision dependent on a third party's GMP remediation, out-year EPS estimates are being revised lower, and cash burn is running near $100M per quarter ahead of any revenue. The bull case requires the approval to land cleanly; the bear case is a second CRL or EU contagion that resets the equity sharply lower.

## Company Overview

Scholar Rock is built around selective inhibition of growth-factor activation, with a leadership position in myostatin biology, the protein system that regulates skeletal muscle mass. The lead asset, apitegromab, is a fully human monoclonal antibody that selectively binds the pro- and latent forms of myostatin in skeletal muscle. It is designed as an adjunct to existing SMN-targeted SMA therapies (Biogen's nusinersen/Spinraza and Roche's risdiplam/Evrysdi), addressing residual muscle weakness rather than competing for the same mechanism. Apitegromab carries Orphan Drug, Fast Track, and Rare Pediatric Disease designations in the US and PRIME/Orphan status in the EU.

The pipeline beyond the lead indication provides optionality that a standard screen would miss. Apitegromab is advancing into a Phase 2 trial in facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy (FORGE, initiating mid-2026, addressing roughly 30,000 patients in the US/EU) and a Phase 2 in younger SMA patients (OPAL). A subcutaneous autoinjector formulation has completed Phase 1. SRK-439, a novel subcutaneous myostatin inhibitor engineered to avoid GDF11/Activin A binding, has Phase 1 topline expected in H2 2026 and is positioned for the muscle-preservation opportunity alongside GLP-1 weight-loss drugs (apitegromab was tested with tirzepatide in the EMBRAZE obesity proof-of-concept). SRK-181, an earlier-stage anti-latent TGFβ1 immuno-oncology asset, has been de-emphasized.

The leadership team is a meaningful qualitative positive for an unproven commercial launch. CEO and Chairman David Hallal is the former CEO of Alexion Pharmaceuticals, with a rare-disease commercialization pedigree, and President of R&D Akshay Vaishnaw is a former Alnylam R&D leader.

## Financial Analysis

Scholar Rock has the standard pre-revenue clinical-stage profile, with the relevant questions being runway to launch and the binary approval event rather than conventional solvency ratios.

**Income statement.** FY2025 net loss was $377.9M, up roughly 53% from $246.3M in FY2024, driven by commercial build-out and launch-readiness costs ahead of approval. The accumulated deficit reached $1.3B at year-end 2025. Q1 2026 net loss was $105.5M versus $74.7M in Q1 2025. The composition is informative: R&D was nearly flat ($51.8M versus $48.7M) while G&A nearly doubled to $50.2M (from $28.4M), confirming the spend is shifting toward commercial infrastructure (sales, marketing, market access). There is no product revenue in any period.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Cash, equivalents, and marketable securities rose from $367.6M at 12/31/2025 to $479.9M at 3/31/2026. At a roughly $100M quarterly burn, this provides a cushion of approximately four to five quarters before further financing, which dovetails with the September 2026 PDUFA but leaves little margin for delay. On 2/27/2026 the company terminated its Oxford Finance facility and entered a new $350M senior secured facility with Blue Owl Capital. Total liabilities rose to $259M (3/31/2026) from $159M (12/31/2025), with long-term liabilities up to $200M. The debt carries covenants and acceleration risk for a still-pre-revenue company.

**Capital structure and dilution.** Weighted shares rose from roughly 111.8M in Q1 2025 to roughly 127.3M in Q1 2026, about 14% dilution YoY, with an active Jefferies ATM ($91.7M of stock sold in 2025), roughly 17.4M pre-funded warrants, and roughly 12.2M options/RSUs/PSUs outstanding. Continued dilution to fund launch is essentially certain.

The financial picture is bridge financing built deliberately ahead of a binary event. Management stacked capital (a larger debt facility plus ATM usage) while the stock was elevated and before the approval decision, prudent but dilutive and leveraging.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is entirely event-driven rather than the steady revenue ramp typical of a maturing company. There is no commercial revenue today; the 12-month growth thesis is the probability-weighted value of a first SMA launch against a defined catalyst calendar.

The catalysts are crisp and dated:
- Catalent Indiana re-inspection facility classification, expected within roughly 90 days of the re-inspection. This is the key remaining binary; the Warning Letter/OAI status must be satisfactorily resolved for approval.
- Second fill-finish facility commercial apitegromab supply, expected early Q3 2026.
- FDA PDUFA action date, September 30, 2026, with approval possible at any time before then.
- EU CHMP opinion anticipated mid-2026, with a planned EU launch in H2 2026 starting in Germany.
- SRK-439 Phase 1 topline in H2 2026, a free option on the larger cardiometabolic muscle-preservation market.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and warrants caution. The current-quarter EPS estimate improved 8.9% over 90 days (to -$0.83) and the current-year estimate improved 1.9%, but the next-year estimate deteriorated 23.6% over 90 days and 8.4% over the last 30 (to -$2.53). The deteriorating out-year estimate reflects the longer, costlier path to commercialization the CRL imposed, with launch economics pushed out and burn front-loaded. The estimate trend does not support treating this as a clean re-rating; it is a binary launch story with heavier near-term spend.

The earnings track record is weak on visibility, with one beat and three misses across the last four quarters, including a 44.6% miss in Q2 2025. For a pre-revenue biotech this is largely noise around launch-spend timing rather than a demand signal, but it argues for not over-relying on near-term consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples are not meaningful for a pre-revenue biotech with negative EPS (-$3.48 trailing, forward P/E of -13.0). The valuation question is whether the roughly $5.5B market capitalization reasonably reflects the probability-weighted value of an SMA launch plus pipeline optionality, net of the binary regulatory risk.

The analyst posture is unusually one-sided: 18 of 18 covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, with zero Hold or Sell ratings. The target range runs from a low of $48 to a high of $70, with a mean of $59.3 and median of $58. At $45.69, the stock trades roughly 5% below the lowest published target and roughly 21% below the median. On a stock with positive near-term catalysts and an all-Buy analyst base, this gap suggests the market is pricing meaningful residual approval risk that the sell side discounts.

The stock has round-tripped meaningfully over the past year, ranging from a $27.07 low to a $52.19 high. The drawdown from the highs around the CRL and the subsequent recovery to the mid-$40s reflect the swing in approval odds, not a change in the underlying drug. The current level prices in a substantial probability of approval already; it does not reflect a deeply distressed asset. We assign a valuation score of 5.5 because the discount to targets is real and the catalyst path is concrete, but the binary nature of the central event means a single negative outcome (a second CRL or unfavorable Catalent re-classification) could compress the equity by 30-40%. This is asymmetric in both directions rather than cleanly mispriced to the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Apitegromab's commercial thesis rests on a differentiated, complementary mechanism. It is the only muscle-directed therapeutic clinically validated in SMA, dosed on top of the entrenched SMN-targeted standard of care (Spinraza, Evrysdi, Zolgensma) to address residual muscle weakness. This expands rather than fights for the existing market, which is a genuine competitive advantage and the core of the pricing thesis. Orphan and rare-pediatric designations support premium pricing.

The principal future competitive threat is Roche's GYM329 (RG6237), another anti-myostatin antibody in SMA development, which represents the most direct mechanistic competition over a multi-year horizon. Other myostatin programs (Regeneron, Lilly, Veru, Biohaven) target obesity/sarcopenia, the arena Scholar Rock's SRK-439 addresses. Rising interest in muscle preservation alongside GLP-1 drugs makes the myostatin platform strategically valuable and a potential source of future partnering optionality.

The trajectory of the competitive position is improving on the strength of being first to validate the muscle-directed mechanism in SMA, which supports a competitive score of 7.0. The main caveat is that the adjunct positioning requires convincing payers and physicians to add an expensive therapy on top of already-costly SMN treatments, a real commercial execution hurdle for a company that has never sold a product.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by one idiosyncratic, recently realized risk rather than generic biotech boilerplate.

**Manufacturing/CRL risk (the defining issue).** The remaining binary is whether the Catalent re-inspection produces a satisfactory re-classification. The mitigation, a second US fill-finish site, is itself new and unproven at commercial scale.

**Concentrated third-party manufacturing dependence.** Scholar Rock owns no manufacturing and historically relied on a single source for both drug substance and fill-finish. The CRL is the direct manifestation of that concentration. Catalent is now part of Novo Nordisk, adding dependence on a contractor undergoing ownership integration.

**EU regulatory contagion.** Because the EMA has a mutual-recognition arrangement with the FDA, the OAI classification could delay or trigger a negative CHMP opinion on the EU MAA.

**Commercial execution and reimbursement.** A first-ever launch built from scratch in the US and EU, with extensive new risk-factor language on US drug-pricing policy (MFN executive orders, proposed GLOBE/GUARD Medicare models, Section 232 pharma tariff investigation) and EU HTA/reference-pricing hurdles. As an add-on orphan therapy, apitegromab faces cost-effectiveness scrutiny.

**Financial/leverage risk.** The new $350M Blue Owl secured debt adds covenants and acceleration risk, with runway only "into 2027" before the Q1 raises and ongoing dilution essentially certain.

We assign a risk score of 4.5 to reflect the binary regulatory decision and concentrated manufacturing dependence that are not fully resolved, partially offset by the clean (non-efficacy) nature of the CRL and the verifiable de-risking steps taken.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing acute near-term distress. Near-term ATM IV is 49.4% versus one-year historical volatility of 64.5%, a -15.2% spread that places implied vol cheap relative to realized and at the 4th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in contango (near-term 49.4% rising to 63.8% at six months), the normal/complacent shape, with longer-dated vol building toward the September PDUFA window. The put/call OI ratio of 0.84 and a modestly call-heavy near-term skew (-3.7%) indicate positioning is not defensively tilted into the near term. The longer-dated vol build and 16.4% short interest (11.5 days to cover) frame a stock where bearish institutional positioning is meaningful but the binary is priced into the back of the curve rather than the front. Elevated short interest with high days-to-cover creates squeeze potential on a favorable Catalent re-classification.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The drug works (Phase 3 SAPPHIRE positive), the CRL was a fixable third-party manufacturing problem, and management has converted a single point of failure into redundancy with an accepted BLA, a hard PDUFA date, and a second supply site online early Q3. A satisfactory Catalent re-classification plus a positive CHMP opinion leads to a US (and Germany-first EU) launch in H2 2026, executed by an Alexion-pedigreed commercial team. Approval re-rates the equity toward the analyst target range of $58-70 (+27% to +53% from current), with SRK-439 Phase 1 data and the FSHD/obesity optionality providing further upside. The 16.4% short interest amplifies the upside on a clean approval. Bull target $70.

**Bear Case.** The Catalent re-inspection fails to satisfy the agency, producing a second CRL or further delay, or the EU mutual-recognition arrangement triggers a negative CHMP opinion. With burn near $100M per quarter, a delay forces additional dilutive financing into weakness, and the next-year EPS estimate deteriorates further. In that scenario the stock retraces toward its 52-week low near $27 (-41% from current). Bear target $28-30.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale on a 6-12 month horizon tied to the PDUFA calendar.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable Catalent Indiana facility re-classification disclosed following the re-inspection, removing the central manufacturing overhang.
- Confirmation that the second fill-finish site has commercial apitegromab supply on schedule in early Q3 2026, establishing redundant supply.
- A positive CHMP opinion mid-2026 that removes the EU contagion risk.

Any two of these, particularly the Catalent re-classification, would shift the risk/reward to clearly asymmetric and justify a BUY ahead of the September 30 PDUFA.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second CRL or any FDA communication indicating the Catalent deficiencies remain unresolved.
- A negative or delayed CHMP opinion.
- A dilutive equity raise on materially worse terms (priced significantly below market) that signals constrained financing options.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Catalent re-classification (within ~90 days of re-inspection), the mid-2026 CHMP opinion, the early-Q3 second-site supply confirmation, the September 30, 2026 PDUFA, and SRK-439 Phase 1 topline in H2 2026.

## Conclusion

Scholar Rock is a binary, catalyst-rich rare-disease story whose central question has narrowed from "does the drug work" (answered yes by SAPPHIRE) to "does a third party's manufacturing remediation satisfy the FDA." The company has taken concrete, verifiable steps to de-risk that question, including a dual fill-finish strategy, an accepted BLA, the September 30, 2026 PDUFA date, and a reinforced balance sheet. The stock trades roughly 5% below the lowest published analyst target with a unanimous Buy posture across 18 analysts, which signals the market prices residual approval risk the sell side discounts.

We rate SRRK a HOLD. The growth thesis is specific and dated: a clean Catalent re-classification, second-site supply on schedule, a positive CHMP opinion, and approval would plausibly re-rate the equity toward the $58-70 target range, and the short interest would amplify the move. We stop short of BUY because the central event remains a genuine binary dependent on a contract manufacturer's GMP remediation, the next-year EPS estimate is deteriorating sharply, cash burn is heavy and front-loaded ahead of revenue, and a negative outcome carries roughly 41% downside toward the 52-week low. This is a position to hold into the catalyst window and upgrade on confirmed manufacturing resolution, not to size up before the binary resolves.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the discount to the lowest analyst target was overstated (the stock trades ~5% below the $48 low target, not ~25%), which materially weakens the "discount to targets" support for the thesis.
