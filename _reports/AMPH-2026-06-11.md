**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Amphastar is a vertically integrated specialty/generic pharmaceutical company whose competitive edge rests on manufacturing technically difficult injectable, inhalation, and intranasal dosage forms in-house. That moat is real, but the financial picture over the last two reported quarters shows a deterioration that the flat revenue headline obscures. Q1 2026 revenue was essentially unchanged at $171.2M, yet gross margin fell roughly 890 basis points to 41.1% from 50.0%, gross profit dropped 18%, and GAAP net income fell 75% to $6.4M from $25.3M. Adjusted EPS of $0.42 missed the $0.72 consensus by 41.6%, following a 22.4% miss the prior quarter. The company is buying revenue growth with margin: high-margin branded products (BAQSIMI, glucagon) are eroding on net pricing, while lower-margin "other products" carry the top line.

The company's largest acquired asset, BAQSIMI — bought from Eli Lilly for ~$591M in 2023 and ~19-26% of revenue — has become a drag rather than a driver. Management cut its BAQSIMI outlook to flat-to-low-single-digit growth, citing higher rebates, 340B discounts, and suspected duplicate 340B claims. Stacked on top are a newly disclosed FDA "Official Action Indicated" classification at the South El Monte facility (April 2026), structural erosion in glucagon and epinephrine vials, founder pledged-share margin-call risk into a stock down ~34% from its 52-week high, and a web of related-party transactions with the founders' family. Consensus EPS for the current year has been cut 17.5% in 90 days and 18.9% for the current quarter.

The stock looks cheap at 6.5x forward earnings, but that multiple is calculated on a consensus estimate ($2.68 FY) that is itself falling fast, and the prior misses suggest even the cut estimate carries downside risk. This is a value trap signature: a low multiple on impaired and still-deteriorating earnings power, with no identifiable near-term catalyst to restore the ~50% gross margin the business historically earned. The genuine pipeline optionality (2027 insulin aspart biosimilar, GLP-1 ANDA) is real but too far out to anchor a 2026 thesis. We rate AMPH AVOID.

## Company Overview

Amphastar (HQ Rancho Cucamonga, CA; ~1,976 employees) develops, manufactures, and markets 25+ prescription products plus one OTC product across injectable, inhalation, and intranasal formats, along with insulin and heparin APIs. The moat is manufacturing difficulty: these are complex formulations and dosage forms that are hard to genericize, not commodity orals. Manufacturing spans California (Rancho Cucamonga, South El Monte), Massachusetts (Canton), France (Éragny-sur-Epte; insulin/heparin API), and China (Nanjing/ANP). The company runs one reportable segment.

Q1 2026 revenue mix:
- **BAQSIMI** (intranasal glucagon rescue, ~$32M/qtr): acquired from Lilly in 2023; the transition services agreement ended January 2025. Manufactured by a single third-party CMO. Carries up to $450M in contingent earn-out to Lilly on sales milestones (none triggered).
- **Primatene MIST** (OTC epinephrine inhaler, ~$30M/qtr): stable, brand- and OTC-insulated; long-term HFC propellant phase-down risk under the AIM Act.
- **Epinephrine** (~$19M/qtr): growing on pre-filled syringe demand offsetting vial erosion.
- **Lidocaine** (~$13M/qtr): stable.
- **Glucagon injection** (~$9M/qtr): collapsing (down 56% YoY), cannibalized in part by BAQSIMI.
- **Other products** (~$67M/qtr, +34% YoY): the growth engine — albuterol, iron sucrose, teriparatide, dextrose, plus the April 2026 launch of generic ipratropium bromide HFA (first and only generic on market).

The business is founder-controlled: co-founders and spouses Dr. Jack Zhang (CEO/CSO) and Dr. Mary Luo (COO) own ~26% directly, with insiders ~29% total, a classified board, and anti-takeover provisions.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature is a sharp, broad-based margin and earnings deterioration despite roughly flat revenue.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $644M | $732M | $720M | $171.2M (flat YoY) |
| Net income | $137.5M | $159.5M | $98.1M | $6.4M (vs $25.3M) |
| Gross margin | — | — | — | 41.1% (vs 50.0%) |

The margin compression has multiple independent drivers, which makes it harder to dismiss as a one-quarter aberration: (1) price erosion on the highest-margin products — BAQSIMI net pricing down ~15% on rebates/340B/channel mix, glucagon down 56%, plus phytonadione and epinephrine multi-dose vials; (2) higher manufacturing costs from the Rancho Cucamonga facility expansion; (3) R&D up 33% YoY (including a $2.0M Hanxin license payment); (4) G&A up 13% on legal and ERP implementation. FY2025 net income had already fallen 38%, driven by the loss of the high-margin BAQSIMI net-revenue arrangement after the Lilly TSA ended.

Cash generation and the balance sheet remain the relative strengths. Q1 2026 operating cash flow rose to $47.8M from $35.1M, and cash plus short-term investments stand at ~$292M. Total debt is ~$620M: $345M of 2.0% convertible notes due March 2029 (conversion price $62.96, deeply out of the money with the stock near $20), a $250M Wells Fargo term loan due June 2028 (variable, swap-hedged, secured by substantially all assets), and a ~$25M ICBC China facility. The company is in compliance with all covenants. The 2029 converts being far out of the money removes near-term dilution and refinancing pressure; the secured term loan due 2028 is the item to watch if cash flow degrades further.

Capital allocation has been aggressive on buybacks: $29.5M in Q1 2026 (~3% of shares), $75M+ in FY2025, with the Board adding $50M to authorization in March 2026 (total $485M authorized). Management frames this as dilution-offset rather than opportunistic. The effective tax rate jumped to 36% in Q1 2026 from 18% on discrete items, which amplified the GAAP EPS drop. No dividend.

The earnings-surprise record is the clearest warning. After two beats (Q2 2025 +12.5%, Q3 2025 +10.6%), the company missed by 22.4% in Q4 2025 and 41.6% in Q1 2026. A streak-break of that magnitude signals deteriorating forecast visibility, which is corroborated by the estimate revisions: current-year EPS cut from $3.31 to $2.68 over 90 days (-19.1%), current-quarter from $0.79 to $0.64 (-18.9%). The 30-day current-year revision (-17.5%) versus the 90-day (-19.1%) shows the cutting may be moderating but has not stopped.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth story is hollow. Revenue is flat, and the only growing line — "other products," +34% YoY — carries structurally lower margins than the branded portfolio it is replacing. Management maintained corporate mid-to-high-single-digit sales growth guidance, but that guidance now leans heavily on the April 2026 launch of generic ipratropium (no competitor as of early May), albuterol, iron sucrose, and teriparatide. Hitting the number in 2026 depends materially on ipratropium ramping before competition arrives.

The legacy erosion is structural rather than cyclical. Glucagon "has not reached the bottom yet" per management; epinephrine vials and phytonadione are under pressure; and BAQSIMI was cut to flat-to-low-single-digit growth. BAQSIMI's problem is net pricing (rebates/340B), not demand — unit demand reportedly +8% YoY — but for a margin thesis, that is the worse of the two problems because it compresses profitability without volume offset.

The genuine optionality sits in 2027: the AMP-004 insulin aspart biosimilar targets a >$1B market, and a GLP-1 ANDA is in development. The biosimilar is the most material catalyst, with interchangeability status (uncertain) determining ramp speed. There is also earlier-stage proprietary peptide work and AMP-110, an in-licensed synthetic ACTH analog (Phase 1 targeted 2027, no FDA alignment yet). These are real, but back-end loaded. None of them helps 2026 earnings, and none offsets the near-term margin trajectory.

The estimate-revision picture is the inverse of a buy signal: estimates are falling double digits across every horizon while the business is being repositioned from high-margin branded to lower-margin generic. Growth prospects score low.

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.54, AMPH trades at 6.5x forward earnings and 12.4x trailing. On screen, this is cheap. The problem is what the forward multiple is built on. The $2.68 FY consensus is still falling, and the company has missed the last two quarters by 22% and 42%. A 6.5x multiple on an estimate that may itself prove too high is not a margin of safety; it is the market discounting an impaired and uncertain earnings stream.

Peer comparison is thin in the provided set — the single same-sector comparable (BBIO) is a loss-making clinical-stage name with a negative P/E, not a useful read-across for a profitable specialty generic. More instructive is the company's own history: it earned ~50% gross margins and ~$137-160M net income in 2023-2024, versus a $98M FY2025 and a Q1 2026 run-rate that annualizes far lower. The stock is not cheap against normalized earnings power because the central question — whether ~50% gross margin returns — is unanswered and trending the wrong way. This is the opposite of a cyclical trough where forward earnings are understated; here the trailing period flatters the forward outlook, and consensus is racing to catch down.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $19, mean $22.83, median $21.50, high $30. The all-HOLD analyst posture (7 holds, 0 buys, 0 sells, down from 1 strong buy / 2 buys three months ago) and the downward target migration reflect the same deterioration. With the stock near the low end of the target range and estimates still being cut, the targets are more likely to follow earnings lower than to act as a floor. We do not treat consensus as a floor here because this is an impaired, not an inflecting, name.

The buyback at ~3% of shares per quarter provides some technical support and signals management confidence, but buybacks into falling earnings can destroy value if the impairment proves durable. Valuation scores at the midpoint: genuinely cheap on the optics, but the cheapness reflects real impairment with no catalyst to crystallize a re-rating in the relevant horizon.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat — difficulty of manufacturing complex injectables and inhalation products in-house — is intact and is the reason erosion has been gradual rather than cliff-like. The inhalation franchise is the bright spot: generic ipratropium launched with no generic competition, and Primatene MIST is insulated by OTC status and a 60-year brand.

The trajectory, though, is deteriorating in the products that matter most to profitability. Glucagon is losing to Viatris/Cipla/Lupin and to the broader shift toward ready-to-use rescue products, including Amphastar's own BAQSIMI (partial self-cannibalization). BAQSIMI's 340B/rebate pressure is, per management, an industry-wide gross-to-net dynamic, meaning it is not easily fixed by company-specific action — the 3% list-price increase and the outside firm hired to validate 340B claims are remediation attempts with uncertain payoff. The portfolio mix is migrating toward lower-margin generics where pricing power is structurally weaker. A market leader in specialty injectables losing margin in its highest-value products scores below the midpoint on trajectory even with a defensible moat at the franchise level.

## Risk Assessment

This is the lowest-scoring dimension, and several of the risks are specific, recent, and not yet fully priced.

**FDA Official Action Indicated at South El Monte (most important new risk).** The Q1 2026 10-Q disclosed that the FDA inspected South El Monte in December 2025, the company responded to Form 483 observations in January 2026, and in April 2026 the FDA classified the facility as OAI. OAI is a serious status that can delay product approvals and precede enforcement. Management says corrective actions are underway and does not anticipate material interruption, but this is a red flag that a standard data pull would miss, and it raises the probability of approval delays for the very pipeline the forward thesis depends on.

**BAQSIMI net-pricing erosion.** A ~26%-of-FY2025-revenue asset has flipped to a drag. Suspected duplicate 340B discounts are being investigated by an outside firm (engaged May 2026), but the timing and magnitude of any recovery are unknown, and the up-to-$450M Lilly earn-out plus single-CMO dependency add tail risk.

**Founder pledged-share margin-call overhang.** Zhang and Luo have pledged 5.7M shares to secure personal loans, including a $15M UBS facility callable unilaterally for any reason. With the stock down to roughly a third of prior highs, a forced sale or margin call could pressure the stock independent of fundamentals. The company tightened its pledging policy in 2026 (cap: lower of 60% of holdings or 15% of total shares), which mitigates but does not eliminate the exposure.

**Related-party web.** Hanxin (AMP-110 licensor and CMO partner) is 11.5%-owned by Amphastar and chaired by the CEO's son, Henry Zhang, with the founders holding stakes via an affiliate. Letop, Genreach, and Chengong are also family-affiliated. Aggregate milestone exposure to family-related entities runs up to ~$89M. Individually immaterial today, collectively a governance concern that warrants scrutiny.

**Customer concentration.** Top 3 distributors (Cencora 21%, McKesson 20%, Cardinal Health 19%) are ~60-65% of revenue.

**Structural legacy erosion** in glucagon, epinephrine vials, and phytonadione, with management explicitly flagging glucagon has not bottomed.

### Options Market Signal

No options chain data was provided, but the short-interest profile is informative: 17.8% of float short, 3.7M shares, 4.9 days to cover. This is elevated institutional bearish positioning. The moderate days-to-cover (4.9) means squeeze risk is real but not extreme — a positive surprise could force covering, but the float is liquid enough that a short is not trapped. For an AVOID thesis, the elevated SI is a double-edged consideration: it confirms sophisticated investors share the bearish view, but it also caps the remaining easy downside and creates squeeze potential on any good news (e.g., a clean South El Monte re-inspection or BAQSIMI 340B recovery).

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Revenue holds flat-to-up because the ipratropium launch ramps faster than competition arrives, albuterol/iron sucrose/teriparatide keep the "other products" line growing 30%+, and the BAQSIMI 340B duplicate-discount investigation recovers meaningful net pricing. The South El Monte OAI is remediated without approval delays. Gross margin stabilizes and begins recovering toward the mid-40s as facility-expansion costs anniversary. The 2027 insulin aspart biosimilar and GLP-1 ANDA come into view, re-rating the stock on pipeline optionality. Buybacks shrink the share count into a recovery. At a normalized $3.00+ EPS and a modest 10x, the stock approaches the $30 high target.

**Bear case.** BAQSIMI net pricing keeps eroding as an industry-wide 340B/rebate dynamic with no company-specific fix; glucagon and epinephrine vials continue structural decline; mix shift toward low-margin generics holds gross margin in the low-40s. The South El Monte OAI escalates to a warning letter, delaying pipeline approvals and pushing the 2027 biosimilar catalyst right. Consensus continues cutting and prints another miss. The founder pledged-share overhang triggers forced selling on a further decline. On a $2.30-2.50 EPS at 6-7x, the stock revisits the $16-17 area (the 52-week low was $16.65), roughly 17-19% below current.

The asymmetry favors the bear in the relevant horizon. The bull case requires several things to go right simultaneously (340B recovery, OAI resolution, ipratropium ramp, margin stabilization), while the bear case requires only a continuation of trends already in motion. The genuine upside optionality is real but lives in 2027.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The next earnings report (likely early August 2026 for Q2) is the primary dated catalyst. With consensus still being cut and two consecutive double-digit misses, a third miss or another guidance walk-down on BAQSIMI/margin is plausible. A secondary catalyst is escalation of the South El Monte OAI to a warning letter, which has no fixed date. Neither is a clean, high-conviction binary.

**Timing window.** Q2 print in ~8 weeks (early August). The OAI is open-ended. The 2027 pipeline catalysts are far enough out that they are not a near-term short risk.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 17.8% of float short, 4.9 days to cover. This is crowded enough to create meaningful squeeze risk on any positive surprise (340B recovery, clean re-inspection, ipratropium beat). At 17.8%, much of the bearish thesis is already expressed, which limits incremental downside and amplifies upside risk into the print.

**Borrow and options signal.** No options data was provided to confirm rich put skew or elevated borrow cost. The elevated SI alone suggests borrow is not free. Absent an options confirmation of the bearish thesis, the conviction for an active short is lower.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$16-17 (-17% to -19% from $20.54); the 52-week low of $16.65 is the obvious support test. Upside risk on a squeeze or 340B/OAI good news is plausibly +20-25% given the crowded short. That is roughly 1:1 reward/risk, below the 2:1 bar required for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental thesis is bearish, but at 17.8% short interest with a near-equal reward/risk and no clean dated catalyst, the stock is already widely sold. The downside is largely expressed in the price, and squeeze risk on any positive surprise is meaningful. This is an AVOID to own, not an active short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would change the view to constructive):**
- Two consecutive quarters of gross margin stabilizing or recovering toward the mid-40s, confirming the 41.1% Q1 print was a trough rather than a new baseline.
- The South El Monte OAI resolved (Voluntary Action Indicated or no-action re-classification) without pipeline-approval delays.
- BAQSIMI net pricing recovering — concrete evidence the 340B duplicate-discount investigation yielded recoupment, with the line returning to growth.
- Consensus EPS revisions turning positive (the current -17.5% 90-day current-year cut reversing to flat or up) for at least one cycle.
- The 2027 insulin aspart biosimilar reaching FDA acceptance/filing milestones that de-risk the >$1B opportunity.

**Timeline.** The near-term headwinds (margin compression, BAQSIMI pricing, OAI overhang) are likely to persist through at least the next two quarters (H2 2026). The pipeline catalysts that could materially change the earnings trajectory are 2027 events. We would not expect a fundamental re-rating before mid-2027 at the earliest, and only if the margin trajectory inflects.

**Reassessment point.** Q2 2026 earnings (early August) is the next checkpoint — specifically for the gross-margin trend, the BAQSIMI net-pricing commentary, the ipratropium ramp, and any update on the South El Monte OAI status.

## Conclusion

Amphastar's moat in complex injectables and inhalation products is genuine, its cash generation is solid ($47.8M Q1 operating cash flow, ~$292M cash/investments), and its 2027 biosimilar/GLP-1 pipeline carries real optionality. None of that resolves the immediate problem: a ~890bp gross-margin collapse to 41.1%, a 75% drop in GAAP net income on flat revenue, two consecutive earnings misses (-22% then -42%), and consensus EPS cut ~18-19% across every horizon with the cutting not yet fully stabilized. The crown-jewel acquisition, BAQSIMI, has become a net-pricing drag, legacy products are in structural decline, and a newly disclosed FDA OAI at South El Monte threatens the pipeline the bull case depends on. Layered on top are founder pledged-share margin-call risk into a depressed stock and a web of family related-party transactions.

The 6.5x forward multiple is cheap only if you trust an estimate that is falling and that the company has repeatedly missed. This has the signature of a value trap — a compressed multiple reflecting real impairment, not a cyclical trough. With no near-term catalyst to arrest the margin decline and the meaningful upside living in 2027, the risk/reward does not support ownership. We rate AMPH **AVOID**, while noting it is not an active short given crowded 17.8% short interest, a near-1:1 reward/risk, and the stock already trading near its 52-week low.
