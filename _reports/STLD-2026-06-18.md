**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Steel Dynamics is one of North America's largest and lowest-cost steel producers, built on an electric-arc-furnace (EAF) model that recycles scrap and integrates vertically into metals recycling, fabrication, and a new recycled-aluminum operation. The investment story today turns on the collision of two facts. First, the steel cycle has turned upward: Q1 2026 steel-segment operating income rose 143% to $555M as steel selling prices climbed roughly $193/ton against $10/ton scrap inflation, expanding the metal spread 30%, with record quarterly shipments of 3.6M tons and a fabrication backlog up 38% extending into Q3. Consensus estimates have followed, with current-year EPS revised up 14.8% over 90 days to $15.90 and next-year to $17.91. Second, on June 18, 2026, STLD issued downside Q2 guidance including a $16M asset writedown and fell about 7.5%, on the same day Nucor, its closest peer, guided up. That divergence implies the near-term softness is company-specific, tied to the loss-making aluminum startup and fabrication spread compression, rather than a sector-wide pricing problem.

The stock has appreciated 103% over the past year and trades near $270 against a 52-week range of $119.89 to $288.74. On a trailing basis it screens at ~29x, but on rising forward consensus it sits closer to 15-17x next-year EPS, reasonable for a quality cyclical at a spread-recovery point. The aluminum operation in Columbus, Mississippi is the swing factor. It lost $173M at the operating line in 2025 and a worsening $64.6M in Q1 2026, with a "temporary pause in operations" acknowledged during startup. The bull case rests on aluminum converting from cash drain to counter-cyclical earnings diversifier; the bear case is that startup losses persist and overhang the multiple while the steel spread recovery normalizes.

We rate STLD a HOLD. The fundamental trajectory in steel is genuinely improving and the forward multiple is reasonable, but the June 18 guidance divergence against Nucor is a specific, recent signal of execution friction at the moment the company should be capturing the cycle. With aluminum unproven at scale, no insider conviction buying, and the stock having more than doubled, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would upgrade on evidence the aluminum ramp is reaching breakeven, and downgrade if steel spreads roll over while aluminum losses widen.

## Company Overview

Steel Dynamics operates roughly 16 million tons of annual steelmaking and coating capacity through four segments:

- **Steel Operations (72% of FY25 sales):** EAF flat-roll mills (Butler, Columbus, Sinton) and long-products mills (Structural & Rail, Engineered Bar, Roanoke, Steel of West Virginia), plus coating and processing assets including the newly acquired New Process Steel. Capacity is roughly 9.4M tons flat-roll and 4.6M tons long-products, with about 70% of steel and fabrication sales value-added.
- **Metals Recycling (Omni, 11% of sales):** The largest internal scrap supplier — 65% of its ferrous scrap went to STLD's own mills in 2025. This integration lowers working capital intensity and stabilizes input costs.
- **Steel Fabrication (New Millennium, 8% of sales):** Joists and deck for non-residential construction, roughly one-third of the domestic joist/deck market. High-margin but currently in spread compression.
- **Aluminum Operations (2% of sales):** A new 650,000-metric-ton recycled aluminum flat-rolled mill in Columbus, MS plus two satellite slab centers, targeting a mix of ~45% can sheet, ~35% automotive, ~20% industrial at full run-rate. The mill is a 94.4% JV with Unity Aluminum.

The structural advantages are real and durable: a low-cost EAF cost structure with a lower carbon footprint than blast-furnace competitors, vertical scrap integration, geographic proximity to scrap and customers that reduces freight, a diversified value-added mix, and a performance-based incentive culture where over 60% of plant compensation and over 85% of senior leadership compensation is at risk.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant theme is cyclical trough-and-recovery. Earnings peaked in 2022 and bottomed in 2025; Q1 2026 shows a sharp rebound.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 vs Q1'25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $21.5B | $18.8B | $17.5B | $18.2B | $5.20B vs $4.37B (+19%) |
| Operating income | $5.09B | $3.15B | $1.94B | $1.48B (−24%) | $538M vs $275M (+96%) |
| Net income | ~$3.86B | $2.45B | $1.54B | $1.19B (−23%) | $403M vs $217M (+86%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $9.84 | $7.99 | $2.78 vs $1.44 |
| Gross margin | 28.5% | 21.5% | 16.0% | 13.2% | 14.7% (Q1'26) |

The spread mechanics explain the swing. The 2025 weakness was metal-spread compression: steel selling prices fell roughly $14/ton while scrap stayed flat (steel spread −2%), and fabrication spreads compressed 17%. In Q1 2026 that reversed, with the steel metal spread up 30% (detailed in the Executive Summary).

The balance sheet remains conservative but leverage is creeping up. Long-term debt rose from $2.8B (2024) to $4.18B (2025) following over $1.0B of senior note issuance (5.25% '35s, 5.75% '55s, 4.0% '28s) used partly to refinance maturing low-coupon notes. Net interest in Q1'26 was $33M, up 174% year-over-year, a real rising headwind as the company refinances cheap legacy debt at 4-5.75% coupons. Even so, the credit profile is comfortable: debt/cap 31.5%, interest coverage 13.7x against a 2.5x covenant, current ratio 3.06, and an Altman Z of 3.2 in the safe zone.

Cash flow reflects the trough. FY25 operating cash flow was $1.45B (down from $1.8B), and FCF was $502M after $948M of capex (down from $1.9B in 2024 as the aluminum build winds down). Capital allocation has been aggressive: roughly $900M of buybacks in 2025 (about $2.1B over 2024-25), cutting share count from ~166M (2023) to ~144M, with the dividend raised 9% in 2025 and another 6% to $0.53/quarter in Q1 2026. About $687M remains on the $1.5B February 2025 authorization. The buybacks were funded partly by new debt, which is a measured rather than alarming shift given coverage.

The earnings surprise record is mixed: 2 beats and 2 misses across the last four quarters, including a 2.1% miss in Q1 2026. This is not a company that reliably clears the bar, which matters for how much credence to give rapidly rising estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth signal is positive and broad-based. Estimate revisions have been strong: current-quarter EPS up 14.6% over 90 days to $4.16, next-quarter up 32.0% to $4.92, current-year up 14.8% to $15.90, and next-year up 12.9% to $17.91. Combined with Q1 revenue growth of 19% and a fabrication backlog up 38% year-over-year extending into Q3, this is the profile of a cyclical re-accelerating off a trough.

The +32% 90-day revision on next-quarter estimates clears the threshold typically associated with meaningful earnings inflection, and it co-occurs with double-digit revenue growth. I weight that as a positive for growth prospects. The caveat that holds me back from treating it as a clean BUY trigger: the June 18 downside Q2 guidance landed after most of these revisions, and the company's own 2-of-4 miss record argues for discounting the optimism somewhat. Estimate momentum is real, but the most recent management signal cut against it for the very near term.

Longer-term growth has three legs. First, the steel spread recovery, which management expects to persist "through 2026 and into the following years" on tariff support, onshoring, infrastructure funding, and supply-chain regionalization, with imports having declined in 2H25. Second, the New Process Steel acquisition (full control taken December 1, 2025), expanding value-added downstream distribution and processing. Third, the aluminum option: the Columbus mill shipped 15,000 MT in 2H25 and 22,500 MT in Q1 2026 (+54% sequentially), achieving can, automotive, and industrial qualifications. At full run-rate the 650,000-MT operation could be a material counter-cyclical earnings contributor. Today it is the opposite, a cash and earnings drain that is widening even as volumes ramp.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$270, STLD's trailing P/E near 29x is misleading because 2025 was a trough year. The relevant lens is forward earnings power. On current-year consensus of $15.90, the stock trades at ~17x; on next-year $17.91, ~15x. Against the steel peer median P/E of 26.8x (Nucor 25.9, Reliance 26.8, Commercial Metals 17.4), STLD on forward earnings looks reasonable to modestly cheap rather than stretched. The PEG on near-term revision velocity is well below 1.0.

The reason I do not score valuation high despite the optical cheapness: in steel, "cheap on forward earnings" frequently marks a cyclical peak rather than a discount, because consensus extrapolates current-quarter spreads forward. The forward multiple looks low precisely because the Street is now modeling spreads near the high end of the cycle. If steel spreads normalize toward mid-cycle, the $17.91 next-year number compresses and the trailing-looking multiple proves the more honest one.

Set against that, the absolute valuation support is thin — a 0.77% dividend yield offers little floor — and the stock has already captured most of the cyclical re-rating with its 103% advance. The analyst range runs $221 low to $310 high, mean $268.82, median $270, with the current price essentially at the median. On a high-momentum name with a reasonable multiple, I would normally treat consensus as a floor, but the June 18 guidance divergence and the aluminum drag argue the targets are fairly placed rather than stale. I score valuation at 5.0, neither a clear buy nor clearly overvalued, with the cyclical-peak risk offsetting the forward-multiple comfort.

## Competitive Landscape

STLD's competitive position is solid and improving at the structural level. It is among the lowest-cost domestic producers via the EAF model, with vertical scrap integration (65% of Omni's ferrous scrap consumed internally) that few peers match at scale. The value-added mix (70% of steel/fab sales) and geographic proximity to customers provide pricing and freight advantages. The current backdrop favors domestic producers: Section 232 tariffs, onshoring, infrastructure funding, and declining imports in 2H25.

The trajectory question is sharpened by the June 18 divergence, when Nucor, the closest comparable, guided up while STLD guided down. Steel pricing and spreads were still expanding in Q1, so this points to STLD-specific friction in the aluminum startup and fabrication spread compression rather than a sector pricing problem. That divergence is a near-term competitive negative against the one peer that matters most, even if it does not impair the long-run cost-position moat. The aluminum entry, if it succeeds, widens the moat by adding a counter-cyclical product and capturing aluminum-scrap synergies. If it stumbles, it is a multi-year distraction and capital sink that Nucor and Reliance do not carry. I score competitive position at 6.5, a strong cost moat in steel tempered by the unproven aluminum extension and the recent peer-relative miss.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Aluminum execution — the largest idiosyncratic swing factor.** Beyond the operating losses detailed above, the 10-K explicitly cites the company's "little experience" in the aluminum industry and that delays "have adversely affected and may continue to adversely affect" results. Aluminum revenue is also concentrated among a small customer group. This is the single most important determinant of whether STLD re-rates higher or disappoints, and the risk is not fully priced.
2. **Metal-spread cyclicality.** Profitability hinges on the spread between selling prices and scrap, both volatile and not always correlated. 2025 demonstrated how fast spreads compress. The current Q1 strength is a cyclical high point, not a permanent level.
3. **Global overcapacity and trade-policy dependence.** Chinese/Asian overcapacity and reliance on Section 232 tariffs; management explicitly flags that relaxation of tariffs would pressure prices. The current regime is a tailwind that could reverse.
4. **Scrap availability.** As industry-wide EAF capacity grows, ferrous and aluminum scrap demand rises, potentially squeezing the cost side of spreads.
5. **Rising interest expense.** Net interest up 174% YoY in Q1 as low-coupon debt is refinanced at 4-5.75%. Coverage remains comfortable at 13.7x, so this is a headwind, not a solvency concern.

Risk factors were unchanged between the FY25 10-K and the Q1 2026 10-Q. I score risk factors at 5.0, consistent with the prior report. The June 18 downside guidance and ongoing aluminum losses are specific, near-term negative catalysts that are only partially reflected in a stock trading near its 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$330-360):** The steel spread recovery proves durable through 2026 as management guides, fabrication spreads recover off the current trough, and the aluminum mill reaches breakeven and then profitability as volumes scale past current 22,500-MT quarterly run-rates with can/automotive/industrial qualifications in hand. Next-year EPS lands above the $17.91 consensus, and the multiple holds at the peer median of ~26x on normalized earnings power. Continued buybacks compound per-share growth off a 144M share base. At ~20x on $17.50-18 of through-cycle EPS, the stock reaches the low-to-mid $350s.

**Bear Case (~$185-200, roughly -27% to -32%):** Steel spreads normalize toward mid-cycle in 2H26 as the cyclical bounce fades, fabrication stays compressed, and aluminum losses persist or widen with further writedowns. The June 18 guidance divergence proves to be the leading edge of company-specific operational problems rather than a one-quarter air pocket. Forward EPS gets revised back toward $13-14, and the multiple compresses on a cyclical name losing momentum versus Nucor. The stock retraces toward the low $200s and below.

**Base Case (~$280):** Steel spreads hold near current strong levels through 2026 but do not expand further; aluminum continues to lose money at a narrowing rate without yet reaching breakeven; fabrication recovers modestly. EPS lands near consensus of $15.90 this year and $17.91 next year, and the multiple stays at ~15-16x forward. Buybacks provide a per-share tailwind. The stock trades roughly sideways to modestly higher around current levels, consistent with the analyst median of $270.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $285.** Approximately 16x next-year consensus EPS of $17.91, held flat with the current forward multiple, plus a modest buyback tailwind. This is ~6% above the current $270 and sits between the bear and bull scenarios.

**Bull case target: $350** (+30%). ~20x normalized through-cycle EPS of ~$17.50 if aluminum reaches breakeven and spreads hold.

**Bear case target: $195** (−28%). Spreads normalize and aluminum losses persist; forward EPS revised toward $13-14 with multiple compression.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the Q2 2026 print (the first test of whether the June 18 guidance was a one-quarter issue), the 2H26 trajectory of steel spreads, and quarterly aluminum-segment loss progression.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Aluminum-segment operating loss narrows materially (toward breakeven, e.g., loss below ~$25M/quarter) with sustained sequential volume growth above 30,000 MT/quarter.
- Q2 print confirms steel-spread durability and the June guidance proves a transient air pocket, with estimate revisions resuming upward.
- A meaningful pullback toward the low $200s on unchanged steel fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Steel metal spreads compress sequentially in 2H26 (selling prices falling faster than scrap), signaling the cyclical bounce has peaked.
- Aluminum losses widen beyond ~$70M/quarter or a further material writedown is taken, indicating the startup is structurally impaired rather than ramping.
- Next-year EPS estimates revised down more than 10% from the current $17.91.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July), with the aluminum-segment loss line and steel metal-spread direction as the two metrics that decide the call.

## Conclusion

Steel Dynamics is a high-quality, low-cost EAF steelmaker with genuine operating leverage to a steel spread recovery that is already underway, with Q1 2026 steel operating income up 143%, record shipments, backlog up 38%, and rising estimates across all forward periods. On a forward basis the ~15-17x multiple is reasonable to modestly cheap versus a 26.8x peer median. Those are real positives.

Three things keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. The June 18 downside Q2 guidance with a $16M writedown, issued the same day Nucor guided up, flags company-specific friction at the point in the cycle when STLD should be capturing strength. The aluminum operation remains a multi-year, cash-burning option that is widening losses as it ramps and is the dominant variable for whether the stock re-rates or disappoints. And the apparent cheapness on forward earnings carries the cyclical-peak risk that consensus is extrapolating high-end spreads forward. After a 103% advance with the stock at the analyst median and no insider conviction buying, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate STLD HOLD and would reassess at the Q2 print, watching the aluminum loss trajectory and the direction of steel metal spreads above all else.
