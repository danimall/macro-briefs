**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Galaxy Digital is converting itself from a cyclical crypto-trading and asset-management firm into a hybrid business that adds an AI/HPC data-center landlord franchise on top of its existing digital-asset operations. The transformation centers on the Helios campus in West Texas, a former Bitcoin-mining site Galaxy is leasing to CoreWeave under a 15-year contract signed in April 2025. Lease revenue recognition only began in April 2026. The entire AI premium embedded in the stock is, as of this report, promissory: the first revenue dollar has just started flowing, and it flows from a single tenant.

The financial picture underneath the narrative is volatile and capital-hungry. FY2025 produced a net loss of roughly $(241)M against a $753.7M digital-asset impairment, and adjusted EBITDA collapsed more than 95% to ~$33.7M from $715.4M in FY2024. Operating cash flow was negative ~$(317)M for FY2025, with ~$2.39B of financing inflows funding both the business and the Helios build. The operating businesses (Digital Assets at +$246.8M adjusted EBITDA and Asset Management) remain profitable on an adjusted basis; the headline losses come from mark-to-market swings on Galaxy's own balance-sheet crypto and venture holdings in the Treasury & Corporate segment. GAAP results are therefore poor predictors of franchise health, and the equity trades partly as a leveraged crypto-beta vehicle layered onto an unproven infrastructure story.

We rate GLXY a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The thesis to hold is specific and datable: the Helios Phase I ramp completing by end of Q2 2026 and Phase II deliveries in H1 2027, layered on accelerating institutional validation (BlackRock validator selection, State Street tokenized cash fund, NY BitLicense). These are concrete catalysts within a 12-month window. We stop short of a BUY because the value rests on one tenant whose own funding and capex trajectory are exogenous, the company is not self-funding (consuming operating cash and raising via dilution and debt), and the wide analyst valuation range ($22 to $57) shows that this is a binary, execution-dependent story rather than an asymmetric mispricing we can underwrite with confidence.

## Company Overview

Galaxy Digital (Nasdaq: GLXY) is a diversified digital-asset financial services and infrastructure firm founded and controlled by CEO Michael Novogratz, who holds approximately 49% of voting power through a dual-class Up-C structure. The company uplisted from the Toronto Stock Exchange to Nasdaq in May 2025 and completed its TSX delisting in Q1 2026, making Nasdaq its sole listing. Market capitalization is approximately $5.4B at ~$29 per share, with ~191.5M Class A shares outstanding.

The business spans three areas:

1. **Digital Assets — Global Markets:** Institutional OTC trading and market-making, derivatives, lending (loan book historically ~$1.4–1.8B), crypto investment banking and M&A advisory, and capital markets. This is the core operating profit engine.

2. **Digital Assets — Asset Management & Infrastructure Solutions:** ETFs, staking and validation, tokenization, and custody (including the GK8 self-custody technology). Q1 2026 ended with ~$5.0B AUM and ~$3.2B assets under stake. Recent franchise wins include BlackRock selecting Galaxy as an approved validator for the iShares Staked Ethereum Trust ETF, and a State Street/Galaxy tokenized cash-management fund launched in May 2026.

3. **Data Centers (Helios, West Texas):** Galaxy is converting the site into AI/HPC capacity leased to CoreWeave under a 15-year, expandable lease. The company also launched GalaxyOne, a retail fintech platform (October 2025), through partners (Cross River Bank, Paxos, DriveWealth), and obtained a NY BitLicense and Money Transmission License in May 2026 for GalaxyOne Prime NY institutional services.

One precision point: CoreWeave is Galaxy's tenant and customer at Helios. Galaxy owns and operates the real estate and power, not CoreWeave's compute business. Similarly, BlackRock and State Street are clients using Galaxy's validation and tokenization services; the ETFs and funds are their products, not Galaxy's. The investment case is one of infrastructure and service provision, not direct ownership of the end products.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is volatile and crypto-price-driven.** FY2025 net loss reversed +$347M in FY2024 and +$229M in FY2023. The swing was driven by lower BTC/ETH prices, the digital-asset impairment (+127% year-over-year), and ~$87–95M of Helios mining-conversion impairments. The FY2025 segment split is instructive: Digital Assets adjusted EBITDA was a healthy +$246.8M, but Treasury & Corporate dragged $(215.8)M on unrealized losses on the firm's own crypto and venture holdings.

**Q1 2026 confirmed continued pressure but narrowing losses.** Net loss of $(216)M (EPS $(0.49)) was substantially narrower than the $(482)M a year earlier and beat consensus of roughly $(0.59), even as total crypto market cap fell ~20% in the quarter. Adjusted EBITDA was $(188)M and adjusted gross loss $(88)M, with Treasury & Corporate again the culprit at $(167)M EBITDA. The operating businesses held up: Digital Assets generated +$49M adjusted gross profit (down only 4% year-over-year), Asset Management +$18M, and trading volumes held flat while industry volumes fell — a relative-share positive. The loan book shrank ~20% quarter-over-quarter to ~$1.4B on price depreciation and client deleveraging.

**The balance sheet relies on capital markets, not operations.** Total assets reached ~$11.3B in FY2025 (+59%), with total liabilities ~$8.3B (+69%) and equity ~$3.0B. Q1 2026 showed ~$2.6B cash and stablecoins and ~$2.8B equity. The most important quality-of-earnings flag is cash flow: operating cash flow was negative ~$(317)M in FY2025 (worse than FY2024's -$205M), with financing inflows funding the business and the data-center build. The company is consuming operating cash and funding growth through dilution and debt.

**The debt stack totals ~$3.0B contractually:** 2026 Notes ($500M at 3.0%), 2029 Notes ($402.5M at 2.5%), 2031 Exchangeable Notes ($1.3B at 0.5%, issued October 2025, carrying ~74M shares of dilution risk), and a $1.4B Deutsche Bank senior secured Helios term loan ($878M drawn at year-end 2025). On capital allocation, large 2025 dilution (a June offering of 26.4M shares at $19.00 and an October private placement at $36.00) was partly reversed by a $200M buyback authorized in February 2026, of which $65M (3.2M shares) was repurchased in Q1 2026 — management states this more than offset 2025 stock-based compensation dilution.

The earnings-surprise record is consistent with the volatility: one beat against three misses over the trailing four quarters, including a +573% surprise in Q3 2025 and a -108% miss in Q4 2025. This pattern reflects mark-to-market noise rather than predictable operating cadence, and argues for low confidence in any single quarter's GAAP print.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is concentrated in two areas with genuinely different durability profiles.

**Helios/CoreWeave is the swing factor and the source of the AI re-rating.** The mechanism is direct: Galaxy converts power-rich Texas real estate into HPC capacity, leases it to CoreWeave on a long-dated contract, and converts a depreciated mining asset into a contracted infrastructure annuity. ERCOT has approved more than 1.6 GW at the site; Phase I (133 MW) was targeted for substantial completion by end of Q2 2026, with the first data hall already delivered, and Phase II (260 MW) deliveries are slated for H1 2027. The contracted-versus-uncontracted split matters: roughly 830 MW remains uncontracted, which is speculative upside, not committed revenue. With revenue recognition having begun in April 2026, the next two to four quarters will provide the first hard data on the ramp.

**The asset-management and staking franchise is showing accelerating institutional validation.** The BlackRock validator selection, the State Street tokenized cash-management fund, and the NY BitLicense are real franchise positives that partially de-risk this leg even while the trading book is cyclically pressured. These are recurring-revenue, lower-volatility businesses building on blue-chip distribution relationships, and they represent the most defensible part of the growth story.

The estimate-revision data shows a near-term shift in consensus: current-quarter EPS revised +94.7% over 30 days and current-year EPS +69.8% over 30 days, with the consensus loss narrowing materially. We weight this carefully rather than mechanically. The same table shows next-year EPS revised sharply lower (-85.8% over 30 days, -90.4% over 90 days), and the 90-day current-quarter revision is deeply negative (-397.6%). This is a volatile, two-sided revision pattern in which near-term loss-narrowing coexists with material downward revisions to forward expectations, not the clean, broad-based shift that precedes a durable re-rating. The combination signals analysts struggling to model a business whose results are dominated by crypto mark-to-market. We do not treat this as a strong-buy revision signal, because the revision velocity here reflects estimate noise on a mark-to-market book rather than confirmed operating acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

GLXY does not screen cleanly on any conventional metric. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (-18.4x) because the company is loss-making on GAAP. EPS of $(0.60) trailing makes earnings-based multiples meaningless. The dividend yield is zero. The stock has appreciated ~57% over the trailing twelve months but sits ~38% below its 52-week high of $45.92, having round-tripped from ~$18 to ~$46 and back to ~$29.

The valuation is almost entirely a sum-of-the-parts and optionality exercise. The operating digital-asset businesses generate positive adjusted gross profit (~$67M combined in Q1 2026 across Digital Assets and Asset Management, an annualized run-rate north of $260M), the AUM and staking franchise carry recurring-revenue value, and the ~$2.6B cash/stablecoin position is real. Against this sit the ~$3.0B contractual debt stack, the embedded crypto-beta of the Treasury book, and the Helios optionality whose value depends entirely on execution.

The analyst range frames the problem precisely. BTIG (Buy, $50) and Rosenblatt (Buy) emphasize Helios/CoreWeave optionality and the staking franchise; a Seeking Alpha analyst rates it Hold with a $22–26 probability-weighted fair value implying 15–28% downside, arguing the data-center pivot is unproven and the trading business is commoditizing. The published price-target distribution is wide: low $28, mean ~$41.6, median $40.5, high $57. At ~$29, the stock sits at the bottom of that range, near the low target.

We score valuation at 5.0 — neither asymmetric upside nor a clear short. The gap between $22 and $57 is not analytical disagreement at the margin; it is a binary outcome on Helios execution. If the CoreWeave ramp proceeds and uncontracted capacity gets leased, the stock is cheap. If CoreWeave wobbles or milestones slip, the AI premium evaporates and the equity reverts toward the value of a cyclical crypto trading book minus the debt load. We cannot identify a specific reason the market is mispricing this today with the confidence required for a high valuation score; the market is correctly pricing a wide distribution of outcomes. Consensus targets here are not "floors" in the inflection-stock sense — the stock is loss-making with no forward P/E to compress below a growth rate, so the standard "analyst targets lag rising estimates" framework does not apply.

## Competitive Landscape

Galaxy competes in two distinct arenas. In crypto financial services, it competes with Coinbase, Cantor and other prime brokers, and crypto-native trading desks. Its differentiation is institutional breadth — trading, lending, asset management, custody, and advisory under one roof — combined with blue-chip partnerships. The relative-share gain in Q1 2026 is a constructive signal that the franchise is taking share during a downturn.

In AI/HPC data centers, the competitive set is entirely different: CoreWeave itself, Crusoe, hyperscalers, and other former-miners-turned-AI-landlords such as Core Scientific, IREN, and TeraWulf. Galaxy's edge is its West Texas power position (>1.6 GW ERCOT-approved) and an anchor tenant. Its disadvantage is stark: zero operating track record as a data-center operator and complete dependence on a single tenant. The competitive trajectory is improving in digital assets (share gains, institutional wins) but unproven in data centers, where Galaxy is a first-time entrant against established operators.

The overall competitive position is stable-to-improving, weighted toward the credible digital-asset franchise. We do not credit the data-center leg with competitive strength yet because there is no operating history to assess.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-customer concentration in data centers.** CoreWeave is the only data-center tenant and can terminate on missed milestones. The entire AI valuation premium rests on one counterparty whose own credit and capex trajectory are exogenous and must be monitored. This is the single largest idiosyncratic risk.

**Execution risk on the pivot.** Galaxy has no prior data-center operating history. Phase I completion is targeted end-Q2 2026; Phase II deliveries not until H1 2027. The uncontracted ERCOT-approved capacity is speculative, not committed.

**Crypto-price leverage through the Treasury & Corporate book.** The firm's own balance-sheet crypto and venture holdings generate large unrealized swings that have driven recent losses, making GAAP results poor predictors of operating health and tying the equity to crypto direction.

**Legal and regulatory overhang.** The NY AG LUNA settlement (March 2025) requires $160M payable over four years (~$151M accrued) over 2020–2022 LUNA trading and statements, plus an Ontario class action naming the company and Novogratz. Ongoing digital-asset securities-classification risk persists across SEC/CFTC/FINRA.

**Counterparty and custody risk.** Approximately $2.1B of digital assets are held at venues without SOC reporting, and ~$1.2B of exposure sits in DeFi protocols.

**Structural and dilution risk.** The Up-C structure includes a Tax Receivable Agreement paying 85% of tax benefits to legacy LPs; the exchangeable notes carry ~74M shares of dilution risk; and stock-based compensation creates ongoing dilution.

**Key-person risk.** Heavy dependence on Novogratz, who controls voting and carries a high public profile.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, two-sided uncertainty with a call-heavy tilt. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 113.3% against 86.6% one-year historical volatility — rich, in the 82nd percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is in backwardation (113.3% near-term versus 82.0% long-term), which signals the market expects a near-term catalyst. The skew is call-heavy (-12.5% near-term), and put/call volume (0.20) and OI (0.73) ratios lean toward calls, not the put-protection demand one would expect ahead of a feared decline. Unusual activity in $35 July calls (Vol/OI 8.7x) reinforces a positioning that leans toward upside speculation around the Helios ramp. This call-tilted positioning sits in contrast to the elevated 14.1% short interest (4.3 days to cover), creating two-sided event risk: a clean Helios data point or franchise win could squeeze, while any CoreWeave or crypto-price disappointment validates the shorts. The rich near-term IV makes this a poor moment to pay up for directional optionality.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Galaxy executes the Helios ramp on schedule, CoreWeave honors and potentially expands the lease, and the uncontracted capacity gets leased to additional tenants, converting a depreciated mining asset into a long-dated, contracted infrastructure annuity at scale. The institutional franchise compounds — BlackRock, State Street, and additional staking/tokenization mandates build a recurring-revenue base that re-rates the asset-management leg. Crypto prices recover, flipping the Treasury & Corporate drag into gains, and the trading book's relative share gains translate into profitability through the cycle. In this scenario the stock approaches the high end of the analyst range (~$50–57), roughly +72% to +96% from current levels.

### Bear Case

CoreWeave experiences funding or demand stress and slips milestones, the Helios ramp disappoints, and the uncontracted capacity stays uncontracted, so the AI premium evaporates. Crypto prices decline further, deepening Treasury & Corporate losses and shrinking the loan book, while the debt stack and negative operating cash flow force additional dilutive capital raises. The LUNA settlement and regulatory overhang consume cash and attention. In this scenario the stock reverts toward the Seeking Alpha probability-weighted fair value of $22–26, roughly -10% to -24% from current levels, with a tail toward the low-$20s/high-teens seen as recently as April 2026.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)** over a 12-month horizon, anchored to the Helios execution timeline.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CoreWeave lease revenue ramps as guided over the next two quarters (Phase I 133 MW substantially complete by end-Q2 2026) with no milestone disputes, AND a second data-center tenant is contracted against the uncontracted capacity.
- Operating cash flow turns toward breakeven or positive, demonstrating the business is self-funding rather than dependent on dilutive financing.
- Sustained quarter-over-quarter growth in AUM/assets-under-stake (above ~$5.0B/$3.2B) confirming the institutional franchise is compounding independent of crypto direction.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any CoreWeave milestone dispute, lease termination, or public signal of CoreWeave funding/demand stress.
- Helios Phase I slips materially past Q2 2026, or an additional dilutive equity raise is required to fund the build.
- A renewed quarter of large operating cash outflow combined with a fresh material digital-asset impairment, indicating the cash-consumption problem is structural rather than cyclical.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report, which will contain the first full quarter of CoreWeave lease revenue and confirm or refute the Phase I completion timeline. A second checkpoint is H1 2027 for Phase II deliveries.

## Conclusion

Galaxy Digital is a high-volatility hybrid: a profitable-but-cyclical crypto franchise plus an unproven, capital-intensive AI data-center optionality bet that depends on a single tenant. The operating businesses (Digital Assets, Asset Management) are sound on an adjusted basis, and the institutional validation from BlackRock, State Street, and the NY BitLicense de-risks the asset-management leg. Against this, the company runs deeply negative operating cash flow funded by external capital, carries a ~$3.0B debt load, and concentrates its entire AI re-rating on CoreWeave, whose lease only began generating revenue in April 2026.

We rate GLXY a HOLD with a growth rationale because the thesis to hold is specific and datable: the Helios Phase I ramp through Q2 2026 and Phase II in H1 2027, layered on a compounding institutional franchise, are concrete catalysts within a 12-month window that justify maintaining a position. We do not rate it a BUY because the central value driver is binary on single-tenant execution, the business is not self-funding, and the wide analyst range reflects genuine, unresolved uncertainty rather than an asymmetric mispricing we can underwrite. The next two quarters of Helios revenue data will determine whether this transitions to a BUY or an AVOID; until then, the risk/reward is balanced and the appropriate posture is to hold existing positions and monitor the named triggers rather than add aggressively at current levels.
