**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Acuity Inc. is the leading North American non-residential lighting company executing a deliberate pivot into higher-margin building-technology software and pro-audio-visual control. The company generates strong free cash flow, runs a clean balance sheet, and has used the cash from its mature lighting franchise to fund the $1.24B QSC acquisition (closed January 2025) while simultaneously deleveraging and raising the dividend. The headline numbers look excellent: FY2025 revenue +13.1%, H1 FY2026 EPS +19.1%, consolidated gross margin reaching 49.3% in the most recent quarter. The repositioning thesis is coherent and the execution is competent.

The complication is that the reported growth is almost entirely acquired. QSC contributed $428.6M to FY2025 revenue; core Acuity Brands Lighting (ABL), about 78% of sales, grew roughly 1.1% organically in FY2025 and is now shrinking modestly (Q2 FY2026 ABL sales –2.8%). The Acuity Intelligent Spaces (AIS) segment is carrying the company, up 44.7% in Q2 FY2026, but that figure laps the QSC close and will decelerate as the acquisition anniversary passes in early 2026. Consensus EPS estimates have been falling across every forward period (current year –1.3% over 90 days, next year –1.8%), the opposite of the signal that would justify a higher growth score. The 30-day revision picture is roughly flat, so the deterioration is gradual rather than abrupt, but it is consistently negative.

At ~14x next-year consensus EPS ($19.49 current-year, $21.25 next-year against a $298.69 price), Acuity is reasonably valued for a quality industrial with a credible margin-mix story, but the multiple does not offer asymmetric upside given decelerating organic growth and falling estimates. The balance sheet improvement and the spring 2026 director open-market buying are genuine positives. The near-term options market is pricing imminent stress (backwardation, 46.5% near-term IV vs 34% historical vol, ±9.4% implied move) ahead of the Q3 FY2026 print due late June. We rate AYI a HOLD, consistent with the prior four reports, and classify the rationale as uncertain: the bull and bear cases are roughly balanced, and the next catalyst (the Q3 report and the resolution of the QSC-lapping growth question) should clarify direction.

## Company Overview

Acuity Inc. (renamed from Acuity Brands, Inc. in March 2025) operates two segments with a fiscal year ending August 31:

- **Acuity Brands Lighting (ABL) — ~78% of revenue.** The dominant North American commercial lighting franchise, comprising luminaires plus electronics (drivers, the nLight/SensorSwitch controls platform). Brands include Lithonia Lighting, Holophane, Gotham, Juno, and eldoLED. Distribution runs through independent sales agencies, electrical distributors, direct/corporate accounts, retail, and OEM channels. FY2025 ABL revenue was $3.61B at a 16.4% operating margin and roughly 45% gross margin.
- **Acuity Intelligent Spaces (AIS) — ~22% and growing fast.** The smart-building growth engine: Atrius (cloud spatial-intelligence software), Distech Controls (building management/HVAC controls), and QSC/Q-SYS (a full-stack audio-video-control platform acquired January 2025). Sold through system integrators into education, airports, sports venues, hospitality, and enterprise campuses. FY2025 AIS revenue was $764M (+162% y/y), at structurally higher gross margins of roughly 55-59%.

The strategic logic is to use a leading installed base and controls platform in a mature, GDP- and construction-driven lighting category to fund a mix-shift into higher-growth, higher-margin software and pro-AV. The company holds roughly 1,700 patent assets and operates 18 manufacturing plants, heavily weighted toward Mexico. CEO Neil Ashe, a digital/technology executive (ex-Walmart eCommerce, CBS Interactive), embodies the repositioning. Market capitalization is approximately $9.2B on roughly 30.1M shares.

## Financial Analysis

The automated health-score and structured-proxy tools returned insufficient data and the XBRL feed was stale, so the figures here are drawn directly from the FY2025 10-K MD&A and the Q2 FY2026 10-Q. The missing standardized scores reflect a data-availability gap, not a fundamental weakness.

**FY2025 (ended 8/31/2025):**
- Net sales $4.35B, +13.1%, with QSC contributing $428.6M. Core ABL grew approximately 1.1% organically.
- Gross margin expanded to 47.8% from 46.4% on favorable AIS mix.
- Operating profit $563.9M, a 13.0% margin (down 140 bps), pressured by a 20.9% jump in SD&A (QSC integration, intangible amortization, deal costs) and $29.7M of special charges.
- Net income fell 6.2% to $396.6M; diluted EPS $12.53 (–6.8%). The decline was driven by a $30.9M non-cash pension settlement charge, special charges, and a swing to net interest expense of $22.0M (from $4.5M net interest income) following the QSC term-loan borrowing.

**H1 FY2026 (most recent):**
- H1 net sales $2.20B (+12.3%); H1 operating profit $293.4M (+20.5%); H1 diluted EPS $6.91 (+19.1%).
- Q2 FY2026: revenue $1.06B (+4.9%), gross margin 49.3% (+280 bps), diluted EPS $3.09 (+26.1%).
- The growth-quality split is now clear: ABL is shrinking (Q2 –2.8%, H1 –0.8%) while AIS carried the company (Q2 +44.7%, H1 +106%, lapping QSC).

**Balance sheet and cash flow:**
- Total debt cut from $896.8M (Aug 2025) to $697.1M (Feb 2026); the company voluntarily repaid $400M of the $600M QSC term loan. Remaining: a $200M SOFR-based term loan maturing June 2027 plus $500M of 2.150% notes due 2030.
- Cash $272.5M (Feb 2026); roughly $865.9M total liquidity including the revolver; in compliance with all covenants.
- H1 FY2026 operating cash flow $229.9M (+20%); capex light at roughly $42M, supporting strong free-cash-flow conversion.
- Goodwill plus intangibles total $2.55B of $4.56B total assets (56%), reflecting the intangible-heavy post-QSC balance sheet. Equity $2.84B.

**Capital allocation:** Dividend raised roughly 18% to $0.20/quarter; approximately $105M of buybacks in H1 FY2026 (3.0M shares, roughly 10% of float, still authorized). Management prioritized deleveraging after the deal, a disciplined and shareholder-friendly sequence.

The financial-health profile is solid. Net debt of roughly $425M against $866M of liquidity and strong free cash flow, with most remaining debt locked at a cheap 2.15% fixed rate through 2030, places little stress on the capital structure. The earnings track record is consistent: four straight beats over the trailing four quarters (surprises of 2.5%, 1.2%, 6.4%, and 14.5%), though the magnitude of the beats has compressed sharply from the 14.5% beat a year ago to 2.5% most recently. Financial health scores a 7.3, tempered by the intangible-heavy balance sheet and the pension mark-to-market volatility that can produce large non-cash Q4 charges.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the standardized data is most misleading and where the analysis must lean hardest on the segment detail. Headline growth looks robust, but essentially all of it is QSC and Distech. Core lighting is flat-to-declining, reflecting soft non-residential construction and renovation activity, which is sensitive to interest rates, credit availability, and vacancy rates — all currently unfavorable given the higher-for-longer rate environment.

The decisive question is what happens after the QSC anniversary laps in early 2026. Once the inorganic boost rolls off, consolidated growth should decelerate toward the underlying low-single-digit ABL trend unless AIS organic momentum (Distech, Q-SYS) sustains. The bull view is that AIS is a genuine secular growth franchise in a fragmented building-technology and pro-AV market, and that its higher gross margins (55-59% vs 45% for lighting) will structurally re-rate the company's margin profile and multiple. The Q2 FY2026 gross margin expansion even as ABL softened is real evidence for this thesis.

The estimate revision data cuts against treating this as an inflection story. Consensus EPS has fallen across all forward periods: current quarter $5.19 (–1.6% over 90 days), next quarter $5.53 (–3.1%), current year $19.49 (–1.3%), next year $21.25 (–1.8%). This is negative earnings momentum, not the +30% revision velocity that signals a genuine inflection. The 30-day picture is roughly flat (current quarter +0.5%, others within a percentage point), so the deterioration is mild and may be stabilizing, but the trend is consistently down. Analyst sentiment is degrading: a hold downgrade in the recent recommendation set moved the hold count from 3 to 4.

Growth prospects score a 5.5. The mix-shift thesis is real and the margin trajectory is favorable, but the organic core is stagnant, the acquired growth is about to lap, and estimates are falling rather than rising. This is the dimension that most reliably sorts winners from losers, and Acuity does not present the durable, accelerating organic growth signature that would warrant a higher mark.

## Valuation Assessment

At $298.69, Acuity trades at roughly 15.3x current-year consensus EPS and 14.1x next-year. On trailing GAAP EPS of $12.53 the trailing P/E is approximately 23.8x, but that trailing figure is depressed by the pension charge and QSC deal/amortization costs, so the forward multiple is the cleaner gauge. The analyst price target set runs $295 low to $435 high, with a $352.5 mean and $342.5 median, placing the stock below the median target with roughly 18% upside to the mean.

For a quality industrial-technology company with a leading franchise, improving margins, and a clean balance sheet, ~14-15x forward earnings is not expensive. The question the valuation framework requires is whether there is a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock. The case for mispricing rests on the AIS margin re-rating: if the software/AV mix continues to lift consolidated gross margin and the market begins valuing Acuity as a building-technology platform rather than a lighting company, the multiple could expand meaningfully. The case against is that organic growth is low-single-digit, estimates are falling, and the multiple already sits modestly above a pure lighting comparable, which suggests the market is partially crediting the re-rating thesis.

This is not a deep-value situation where a low multiple flags a buy. The valuation is fair rather than cheap, and the falling estimate revisions argue against treating proximity to analyst targets as a floor — those targets are stale on the downside, not the upside, given negative revision momentum. The IEEPA tariff-refund optionality flagged in the 10-Q (Supreme Court ruling February 20, 2026, and the Court of International Trade ruling in March 2026) offers a modest, uncertain upside catalyst, but timing and amount are unknown.

Valuation scores a 5.6: reasonable and not stretched, but lacking the asymmetric upside or specific identified catalyst that would justify a higher mark. The prior report cut this dimension from 6.5 to 5.5, reflecting the recognition that organic growth no longer supports a growth premium; we hold it at roughly that level.

## Competitive Landscape

Acuity is the market leader in North American non-residential lighting, but that is a mature, commoditizing category facing import and startup price competition, global electrical conglomerates (Signify, Eaton/Cooper, Hubbell), and GDP- and construction-driven replacement demand. The lighting franchise is a cash engine with a defensible installed base and controls platform, but its competitive trajectory is flat-to-eroding, as the modest organic ABL decline confirms.

The competitive upside lies in AIS. Distech in building management and Q-SYS in pro-AV operate in fragmented, faster-growing markets where Acuity is a credible scaled participant rather than a dominant incumbent. The 10-K warns explicitly of vertically integrated global technology and building-solution providers bundling offerings, and of AI/software entrants; these are genuine threats in the segment that is supposed to drive the equity story. The 1,700-patent portfolio and the nLight/Atrius/Distech controls stack provide some integration moat, but Acuity is competing from a challenger position in AIS, not a leadership one.

The trajectory is mixed: deteriorating in the core lighting business, improving in AIS but from a smaller base and against stronger competitors. Competitive position scores a 6.0, consistent with the prior report's downgrade from 6.5. The trajectory matters more than the level, and the net trajectory is roughly neutral: a leading position in a flat market plus a developing position in a growing one.

## Risk Assessment

The most important idiosyncratic risk is Mexico manufacturing concentration and tariffs. Roughly 55% of finished products are manufactured in Mexico (76% of total manufactured/sourced is from outside the US, with 15% from Asia). The business depends on Maquiladora duty-free status and USMCA preferential treatment. Tariffs are already compressing ABL gross margin — the 10-Q explicitly cites higher tariff costs as a Q2 and H1 FY2026 headwind. Any USMCA renegotiation, loss of Maquiladora status, or new Mexico/steel/aluminum/copper tariffs would hit COGS materially. This risk cuts both ways near-term given the IEEPA refund optionality, but the structural exposure is a persistent overhang.

The QSC integration and impairment risk is meaningful. The deal generated $394.6M of goodwill and $713.9M of intangibles (including $434M of Q-SYS developed technology). Purchase accounting finalized in Q2 FY2026. If Q-SYS revenue or synergy assumptions disappoint, non-cash impairment is a real tail risk. AIS operating margin already compressed from roughly 14.9% to 10.0% in FY2025 on amortization and fair-value step-ups.

Cyclical end-market exposure to non-residential construction is a current headwind, visible in ABL's organic decline. Core lighting commoditization, AI/technology disruption (newly emphasized in the 10-K), and pension mark-to-market volatility round out the risk set. There is also an unconfirmed May 13, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01 and 2.03) creating a direct financial obligation — most plausibly a refinancing of the June 2027 term loan, but the document text was inaccessible and the interpretation should be verified.

Risk factors score a 5.5. The Mexico/tariff concentration and QSC impairment tail are specific, material, and not fully priced. These are real binary-leaning exposures rather than generic sector truisms.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 46.5% versus 34% one-year historical volatility — rich, with a 12.6% spread and an IV percentile of 75% within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (46.5% near-term vs 33.8% long-term), signaling the market expects a near-term catalyst, almost certainly the Q3 FY2026 earnings report due in late June. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.4%. Put/call skew is balanced (0.8%) and the put/call volume ratio is low (0.15), so the positioning is event-driven rather than directionally bearish. The P/C open-interest ratio rose from 0.50 to 0.72 in the most recent snapshot. For a holder, the rich near-term IV makes this a poor moment to add ahead of a binary print; for a fundamental view, the balanced skew suggests the market is not positioned strongly against the stock, only braced for volatility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The AIS mix-shift is a genuine structural re-rating story. Higher-margin software and pro-AV continue to lift consolidated margins, as the Q2 FY2026 gross margin already demonstrates. Distech and Q-SYS sustain double-digit organic growth as the QSC anniversary laps, and the market begins valuing Acuity as a building-technology platform rather than a lighting company, expanding the multiple toward the high end of the analyst range. Aggressive deleveraging ($400M of $600M term loan repaid in 14 months) plus ongoing buybacks and the dividend hike compound per-share value. The IEEPA tariff refund provides a modest upside surprise. Bull case target: $400 (roughly 19x the $21.25 next-year EPS on a platform re-rating, near the analyst high of $435), approximately 34% above current.

**Bear case.** The QSC anniversary laps and consolidated growth decelerates to the low-single-digit ABL trend, exposing that the company is mostly a flat-to-declining cyclical lighting business with a developing but sub-scale software segment. Falling estimates continue, non-residential construction weakens further in the higher-for-longer rate environment, tariffs keep compressing ABL margins, and a Q-SYS synergy shortfall triggers a non-cash impairment. The multiple compresses toward a lighting-comparable 12-13x. Bear case target: $255 (roughly 12x next-year EPS, near the 52-week low of $257), approximately 15% below current.

**Base case.** Acuity executes competently, AIS grows but decelerates from its lapped peak, margins hold their gains, organic growth runs low-single-digit, and estimates stabilize. The stock trades in a range as the market waits to see whether AIS organic momentum is durable enough to justify a platform multiple. Base case target: $325 (roughly 15.3x next-year EPS, near the current consensus mean trajectory), approximately 9% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The bull and bear cases are roughly balanced, and the key swing variable — whether AIS organic growth is durable enough to offset core lighting stagnation once QSC laps — will not resolve until the next two to three quarterly prints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AIS organic growth (ex-QSC anniversary effect) sustains double-digits in Q3/Q4 FY2026, demonstrating the platform thesis is real rather than acquisition-driven.
- Consensus EPS revisions turn positive (current-year and next-year estimates rising rather than falling for two consecutive quarters), confirming an inflection rather than a deceleration.
- Consolidated gross margin holds above 49% with ABL stabilizing, signaling the mix-shift re-rating has structural legs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q-SYS non-cash impairment or a guided-down AIS growth outlook, signaling the QSC deal is underperforming its price.
- Material adverse tariff/USMCA development that compresses ABL gross margin by more than 200 bps without offsetting pricing.
- ABL organic decline accelerating beyond mid-single-digit declines, indicating a deeper non-residential construction downturn.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q3 FY2026 report (due late June 2026, consensus ~$5.19 EPS / ~$1.18B revenue) is the immediate catalyst, with the ±9.4% implied move signaling the market expects a meaningful reaction. The QSC anniversary fully laps by early-to-mid 2026, so the Q3 and Q4 FY2026 prints together will reveal the true underlying organic trajectory. Next natural reassessment is the Q3 print.

## Conclusion

Acuity is a well-run, cash-generative lighting leader executing a credible and disciplined pivot toward higher-margin building-technology software and pro-AV. The balance sheet is clean, deleveraging has been aggressive and shareholder-friendly, and spring 2026 open-market director buying near the lows is a constructive insider signal. These are genuine strengths that support a defensible holding.

The bull thesis depends entirely on the AIS mix-shift proving to be a durable organic growth and margin re-rating story rather than an acquisition mirage. The current evidence is mixed: margins are improving (a positive), but core lighting is shrinking, the QSC-driven headline growth is about to lap, and consensus estimates are falling across every forward period (negatives). At ~14-15x forward earnings the valuation is fair but offers no asymmetric upside or identified near-term catalyst large enough to warrant a buy. The near-term options market is braced for a binary earnings reaction, making this a poor moment to add. We maintain the HOLD that has now stood across five reports, classify it as uncertain given the balanced risk/reward, and look to the Q3 FY2026 print and the post-QSC organic growth picture to break the tie.
