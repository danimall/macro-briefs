**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

U.S. Bancorp is the fifth-largest U.S. bank by assets (~$701B at 3/31/2026), and it has put together one of the cleanest operating turnarounds in the large-cap bank group. Q1 2026 marked the seventh consecutive quarter of positive operating leverage (440 bps in the quarter), with the efficiency ratio falling to 58.2% from 60.8% a year earlier, EPS of $1.18 up 14.6% YoY, and ROTCE of 17.0%. The thesis is straightforward: expense discipline is now "embedded," revenue is growing 4-6%, and the gap between revenue and expense growth flows directly to earnings even in a soft-revenue environment.

The stock trades at roughly 11.4x the current-year consensus EPS of $5.10 and ~12.5x trailing, a discount to the diversified-bank peer median of 13.6x (PNC), despite USB carrying a fee-income mix (~41% of net revenue) closer to a money-center bank than a spread-lending regional. The valuation gap exists for a specific, documented reason: USB's five-year cumulative TSR (142) has materially lagged its peer group (196), a pattern persistent across multiple years. The market is in "show me" mode. The case for closing that gap rests on a near-term, identifiable catalyst: a buyback ramp as USB completes a deliberate capital build (only ~$200M repurchased in 1Q26 against a $4.1B remaining authorization) ahead of a transition to Category II, supported by a proposed Basel III finalization that delivers ~5% RWA relief.

We rate USB a BUY. This is a Financials name, a sector where selection has historically been difficult, so the recommendation rests on company-specific evidence rather than sector beta: durable operating-leverage delivery, a four-for-four earnings beat record, a discounted multiple on a 17%-ROTCE bank, a high-quality NDFI book that is a relative strength amid sector-wide anxiety, and a capital-return catalyst with a defined timeline. The base case offers ~14% upside to $66 over 12 months, against a bear case near $48 (-17%).

## Company Overview

U.S. Bancorp operates through three reportable segments. Wealth, Corporate, Commercial & Institutional Banking is the largest profit contributor ($1.43B net income in 1Q26, +19% YoY) and spans core/specialized lending, capital markets, asset management, and corporate trust. Consumer & Business Banking ($616M, +3%) covers branch/digital consumer banking, small business, and consumer lending. Payment Services ($231M) includes credit/prepaid cards, corporate/purchasing cards, and Elavon merchant processing across the US, Canada, and Europe.

The structural differentiator is the fee-income franchise. Payments, merchant acquiring, corporate trust, and wealth produce higher fee diversity and higher through-cycle ROTCE than a typical spread-lending regional. The franchise is geographically concentrated in the Midwest and West (26 states, ~2,000+ branches), with the MUFG Union Bank acquisition (closed Dec 2022) giving USB the #4 California deposit position ($76B+ deposits, 560+ locations), now growing 1.2-2.2x the franchise rate.

Leadership transitioned mid-2025 to Gunjan Kedia (President & CEO), the first woman to lead USB, with predecessor Andy Cecere moving to Executive Chairman. The succession was internal and orderly, with continuity in strategy and a sharper productivity focus.

## Financial Analysis

The earnings trajectory tells the recovery story. Net income to USB went from $7.96B (2021) to a $5.43B trough (2023, reflecting MUFG integration charges, FDIC special assessments, and balance-sheet repositioning), then recovered to $6.30B (2024) and $7.57B (2025), with 2026 tracking to a new high.

**Q1 2026 detail:**
- Net income to USB $1.945B, +13.8% YoY; diluted EPS $1.18, +14.6%.
- Net interest income (TE) $4.29B, +4.1%; NIM 2.77% (+5 bps YoY), aided by fixed-asset repricing and improved earning-asset mix.
- Fee revenue +6.9%, led by capital markets (+29%), trust/investment management (+9.6%), merchant processing (+5.1%).
- ROA 1.15%, ROTCE 17.0%, ROE 12.6%.

The mechanism behind the operating-leverage run: revenue grew ~4.7% while expense growth was held below 1% YoY, and management states the discipline is structural rather than a one-time cost program. That is the lever that should sustain earnings growth even if the revenue environment stays soft.

**Capital and balance sheet:** CET1 stands at 10.8% (stable); Tier 1 at 12.3%; Total RBC at 14.2%. Including AOCI, CET1 is 9.3% — the 1.5-point gap quantifies the embedded mark on the securities book. Tangible book value per share is $29.56, up 15.3% YoY, with TCE up 31% since 1Q24. Deposits of $528B fund ~75% of assets; LCR is 107.9% with $301B total available liquidity.

The AOCI overhang is the principal balance-sheet caveat: $4.7B of unrealized AFS losses ($3.5B net-of-tax) plus $9.4B of unrealized HTM losses (fair value $66.1B vs $75.4B amortized cost, on an 8.1-year WAM, 2.37%-yield book). As a Category III bank, USB currently excludes most AOCI from regulatory capital. The capital-return story is deliberately throttled during this build phase, with $4.1B remaining on the $5B authorization, plus a $0.52/quarter dividend (+4% YoY) for a ~3.5% yield.

**Earnings quality:** Four beats in four quarters (1Q26 +2.1%, 4Q25 +4.9%, 3Q25 +7.4%, 2Q25 +2.8%). This consistency supports confidence in the FY26 consensus of $5.10 and the FY27 figure of $5.65.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance calls for total net revenue +4-6% versus the $28.7B FY25 base, with 200+ bps of positive operating leverage and medium-term (2026-27) targets of ROA 1.15-1.35%, high-teens ROTCE, and a mid-to-high-50s efficiency ratio. Guidance excludes BTIG, which management expects to add ~$200M in quarterly net revenue and be slightly EPS-accretive in 2026 post-close (expected Q2 2026).

Several discrete growth drivers underpin the revenue line:
- **Amazon Small Business co-brand credit card** — USB is the exclusive issuer; converts Q3 2026, expanding payments reach. (USB issues/owns the program; Amazon is the distribution partner.)
- **California/MUFG synergies** — CA is growing 1.2-2.2x the franchise rate off the #4 deposit position.
- **Payments transformation** — new account acquisition +18% YoY, merchant processing fee growth accelerating to 5.1% YoY.
- **Two additional partnerships**, each ~$75-85M revenue/quarter (one capital-markets, one consumer co-brand), embedded in guidance.

I do not see the +30%/90-day estimate-revision inflection signal here — current-year EPS revisions are modest (+1.5% over 90 days, flat over 30 days) and next-quarter estimates ticked down slightly (-1.9% / 30d). This is steady, mid-single-digit revenue growth converting to low-double-digit EPS growth via operating leverage and buybacks. Growth is durable rather than explosive, which is why the Growth Prospects score sits in the mid-6s rather than higher.

## Valuation Assessment

At $57.91, USB trades at ~11.4x current-year consensus EPS of $5.10 and ~12.5x trailing $4.62. The diversified-bank peer set provides the relevant benchmark:

|--------|-----|-----------|
| PNC | 13.6 | 2.9% |
| TFC | 12.5 | 4.2% |
| C | 17.2 | 1.8% |
| WFC | 12.7 | 2.2% |
| BAC | 13.7 | 2.1% |
| **Peer median** | **13.6** | **2.2%** |
| **USB** | **~12.5** | **3.5%** |

USB trades at a discount to the peer median P/E while generating a 17% ROTCE — top-half profitability in the group — and offering a 3.5% dividend yield that exceeds every peer except TFC. The proxy data documents a specific, falsifiable reason for the gap (multi-year TSR underperformance, 142 vs 196 over five years), and there is an identifiable mechanism to close it. As USB completes its CET1 build and Basel III finalization delivers RWA relief (concentrated in residential mortgage and IG commercial), management has explicitly framed a move to Category II with ~10% pro-forma CET1 as the gateway to "resuming long-term capital returns." A buyback ramp from the current ~$200M/quarter pace would mechanically lift EPS and is the most identifiable near-term re-rating trigger.

If USB re-rates to a 13x multiple on the FY27 estimate of $5.65 — still below where peers like Citi (17.2x) and BAC (13.7x) trade — that implies ~$73, close to the Street high of $74. A more conservative base case of 12.5x on $5.30 (a blend of FY26/FY27 plus modest buyback accretion) yields ~$66. The valuation score of 6.8 reflects a genuine discount-to-quality with a defined catalyst, tempered by the "show me" history: the multiple has been compressed for years and may stay compressed until buybacks accelerate.

On the historical pattern that cheap-looking financials can be value traps: USB is not a structurally impaired bank trading at a deserved discount. It posts 17% ROTCE, grows TBV at double digits, and beats estimates every quarter. The discount reflects sentiment and capital-return timing, not deteriorating earning power.

## Competitive Landscape

USB competes with money-center banks (JPM, BofA, Wells, Citi), large regionals (PNC, Truist, Fifth Third), and fintechs in payments. Its edge is the payments/merchant-acquiring + corporate trust + wealth fee engine, which produces a higher fee mix (~41%) and higher ROTCE than pure spread-lending regionals.

Two competitive-trajectory points matter. First, the NDFI book — a sector-wide concern after credit blowups elsewhere — is a relative strength for USB. Its $50B portfolio carries an average internal rating of A+ (better than its core BBB+ C&I book), a criticized rate below 1% (vs 2.1% for core C&I), only ~2% in BDCs, with subscription lines (~30%) and CLOs/ABS (~27%) the dominant categories and strong structural protections. As peers disclose messier NDFI exposure, USB's quality differential is a competitive advantage in the current environment. Second, the regulatory backdrop is turning supportive (streamlined capital proposals, ~5% Basel III RWA relief, higher heightened-standards thresholds), which disproportionately benefits a bank positioned to step up capital returns.

The competitive position is improving at the margin — fee-engine momentum, California synergies, payments account growth — but USB is a scale incumbent, not a share-gaining challenger, so the score sits at 6.8 rather than higher.

## Risk Assessment

**TSR underperformance is the structural risk.** The 2024 reading was 122 vs 148 for the peer group. The bull case requires the operating-leverage story to finally translate into a re-rating, and the multi-year laggard pattern means patience may be required.

**Interest-rate/AOCI sensitivity.** A migration to Category II would force AOCI into regulatory capital, partly offset by the Basel III RWA relief. This is the single most important item to stress-test in any valuation; the headline 10.8% CET1 becomes 9.3% including AOCI.

**Commercial real estate.** CRE is $50B (12.5% of loans); office (9% of CRE) drives elevated CRE nonperformers (1.04%, improving from 1.61% YoY). CRE net charge-offs were net recoveries (-0.07%) in 1Q26, but office occupancy remains monitored.

**Consumer credit.** Card NCOs were 3.96% (down from 4.47% YoY but seasonally rising). The payments tilt means more sensitivity to consumer stress and to any credit-card interest-rate-cap legislation. Against a possible H2 consumer slowdown, this bears watching, though USB's card book quality has been improving.

**BTIG integration.** First sizeable deal since MUFG; execution and cost risk are real but the deal is small (up to $1B) relative to USB's scale.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 26.3% sits modestly rich to 22.3% one-year historical vol (a 4-point spread), with an IV percentile of just 22% within the current chain — the options market is not pricing acute near-term stress. The put/call skew of 4.3% indicates somewhat elevated put demand, and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.10 with a P/C OI ratio of 0.16, both consistent with benign, call-tilted positioning rather than defensive hedging. The term structure is in contango (near-term 26.3% < longer-dated 37.2%), the normal/complacent shape. The flagged jump in the longer-dated ATM IV reading (to ~60.6% in the delta snapshot) appears to be a thin-options artifact and is treated as non-informative. The options market is neither confirming acute downside fear nor pricing a binary near-term catalyst, consistent with a steady-fundamental, catalyst-on-a-timeline thesis. Short interest is negligible (26,017 shares), removing any squeeze dynamic from the picture.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$73, +26%):** Operating leverage continues (eighth-plus consecutive quarter), efficiency ratio reaches the mid-50s, and ROTCE holds high-teens. Basel III relief and the Category II transition unlock a buyback ramp toward normalized deployment, mechanically lifting EPS and finally closing the TSR gap. BTIG and the Amazon co-brand add incremental fee revenue. USB re-rates to ~13x on FY27 EPS of $5.65, with the Street high at $74.

**Bear Case (~$48, -17%):** A consumer-credit deterioration in H2 2026 lifts card NCOs, CRE office stress reaccelerates, and the AOCI mark deepens on a back-up in long rates, forcing a slower capital build. The multiple stays compressed at ~10x on flattish EPS as the "show me" discount persists. The TSR laggard pattern continues into a fifth and sixth year.

The asymmetry favors the long side: the bull/bear span is roughly +26%/-17% from current levels, with a base case of ~$66 (+14%), and the bear case is bounded by a 3.5% dividend yield, top-half profitability, and a discounted starting multiple that already prices skepticism.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $66 (+14% from $57.91).** Reasoning: ~12.5x on a $5.30 blended forward EPS (FY26 $5.10 plus partial-year buyback accretion and BTIG contribution toward FY27's $5.65), a multiple still below the 13.6x peer median for a bank generating 17% ROTCE.

**Bull case target: $73 (+26%).** 13x on FY27 EPS of $5.65 — re-rating to mid-pack on a peer-relative basis as buybacks accelerate.

**Bear case target: $48 (-17%).** ~10x on flat EPS as credit normalizes and the capital build stalls.

**Timeframe: 12 months.** Key dated catalysts: 2Q26 earnings (and BTIG close commentary), Q3 2026 Amazon co-brand conversion, and confirmation of Basel III finalization terms / Category II positioning that would unlock larger buybacks.

**Upside/downside:** +14% to base, +26% to bull, -17% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Operating leverage turns negative for two consecutive quarters (revenue growth falling below expense growth), signaling the cost discipline is unwinding.
2. Card net charge-offs rise above ~4.5% or CRE office NPLs reaccelerate above ~1.6%, indicating credit normalization is overshooting.
3. Management defers the buyback ramp through year-end 2026 (buybacks remaining below ~$500M/quarter), signaling the capital-return catalyst is not materializing on the expected timeline.

## Conclusion

U.S. Bancorp offers a discounted multiple (~11.4x forward, below the peer median) on a bank generating 17% ROTCE with seven consecutive quarters of positive operating leverage, an above-average fee-income franchise, a high-quality NDFI book that stands out favorably against sector concerns, and a 3.5% dividend yield. The persistent TSR underperformance that has kept the stock cheap is the central risk, but it is also the source of the opportunity: there is a specific, dated catalyst — a buyback ramp enabled by Basel III RWA relief and the Category II transition — that could close the valuation gap, against a downside bounded by yield support and top-half profitability.

This is a Financials BUY, a sector where selection has historically required a distinct company catalyst rather than sector beta. USB clears that bar on company-specific grounds: documented operating-leverage delivery, a perfect recent beat record, a defined capital-return catalyst, and a discount-to-quality that is a mispricing rather than a structural impairment. We rate USB **BUY** with a 12-month base-case target of $66.

**On the validation flag:** the prior HOLD-with-a-6.2-score inconsistency is resolved here in favor of a BUY at a 6.6 overall. The specific reasons no longer rise to the level of a HOLD: the valuation discount has an identifiable catalyst (buyback ramp on a defined timeline), the operating-leverage trend is confirmed by seven quarters of data plus FY26 guidance of 200+ bps, and the NDFI/CRE/AOCI risks are quantified and either improving (CRE recoveries, card NCOs down YoY) or manageable (AOCI offset by RWA relief). The residual concern — multi-year TSR underperformance — is a reason for measured position sizing, not for withholding the recommendation, given the asymmetry favors the upside.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: USB's trailing P/E is ~12.5x (not ~12.1x), so the discount to the 13.6x peer median is somewhat narrower than the report's "~11%/~12% below median" framing implied; base-case upside from the current $57.91 price is ~14% (not ~13%).
