## Executive Summary

Halliburton has rallied 78% from its April 2025 low of ~$19.22 to $38.15, driven almost entirely by the Iran-Israel conflict's impact on oil prices and the associated geopolitical risk premium. The stock now trades at approximately 25x trailing earnings on a reported EPS of $1.50 — a multiple that embeds substantial optimism about the durability of elevated oil prices. Underlying fundamentals are weaker: 2025 revenue declined 3% to $22.2B, operating income fell 41% to $2.3B (10.2% margin vs. 16.7% in 2024), and the company booked $831M in impairments and restructuring charges, including $276M in severance for a major workforce reduction.

Near-term EPS estimates are falling, with the current quarter revised down 1.9% in the past 30 days and next quarter down 2.6%. The earnings beat/miss record is mediocre — two beats and two misses over the last four quarters. Insiders are uniformly selling: CEO Miller sold $6.3M in stock at $40 in late March, and multiple executives adopted 10b5-1 selling plans in August 2025. No insider has made an open-market purchase.

The fundamental case for HAL requires sustained oil above $85-90 to stabilize North American rig counts and justify current pricing. With oil now crashing below $83 on an Iran ceasefire, the geopolitical premium that drove this rally is directly threatened. The company's restructuring actions (capex cut 30%, $400M annualized cost savings) are appropriate defensive moves, but they confirm management's own assessment that the activity environment is weak absent geopolitical disruption. At 25x trailing and ~17x forward FY2026E — a premium to peer medians — the stock prices in a recovery that the fundamental data does not yet support.

## Company Overview

Halliburton is the world's second-largest oilfield services company by revenue, operating across two segments: Completion and Production (C&P, ~58% of 2025 revenue) and Drilling and Evaluation (D&E, ~42%). The company operates in over 70 countries with approximately 47,000 employees (down significantly from 2024 after restructuring). Geographic revenue split: North America 41%, Middle East/Asia 26%, Latin America 18%, Europe/Africa/CIS 15%.

Key technology differentiators include the Zeus electric fracturing platform (which reduces emissions and fuel costs in completions), iCruise rotary steerable systems for directional drilling, and Landmark digital software. The company has also entered a strategic collaboration with VoltaGrid to manufacture 400 MW of modular natural gas power systems for data center applications, with delivery targeted for 2028.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability Deterioration

2025 marked a clear cyclical downturn. Revenue fell from $22.9B to $22.2B (-3%), but profitability deteriorated far more severely:

- Operating income: $2.3B (10.2% margin), down from $3.8B (16.7%) in 2024
- Net income: $1.3B (5.8% margin), down from $2.5B (10.9%)
- Reported EPS: $1.50, down from approximately $2.80 in 2024

The $831M in impairment and restructuring charges explain much of the gap. Adjusting for these, segment operating margin was approximately 16% — still down 300bps from 2024's 19%. Q3 2025 was particularly weak, with net income of just $18M on a 90.9% effective tax rate driven by a $125M FTC valuation allowance.

### Balance Sheet and Liquidity

The balance sheet is adequate but not a source of strength:

- Cash: $2.2B; long-term debt: $7.16B; debt/equity: 1.39x
- Altman Z-Score: 2.48 (grey zone — not distressed but not comfortably safe)
- Credit ratings: BBB+/A3 (investment grade, stable outlook)
- Total liquidity: ~$5.7B including $3.5B undrawn revolver (no financial covenants)
- Current ratio: 2.04x

Free cash flow was $1.6B in 2025 ($2.9B operating cash flow minus $1.3B capex). The company returned $1.6B to shareholders ($1.0B buybacks, $579M dividends), consuming essentially all FCF. The dividend appears secure at ~$580M annualized, and $2.0B in buyback authorization remains. Share count has declined 5.6% since 2022, from 904M to 853M — a meaningful contributor to EPS support.

### Forward Estimates and Revision Trends

Current consensus for FY2026 is $2.21 EPS, with FY2027 at $2.73. At $38.15, this implies approximately 17.3x FY2026E and 14.0x FY2027E.

Current quarter estimates fell 1.9% in the last 30 days, next quarter fell 2.6%, and current year estimates are essentially flat despite the oil price recovery. Only FY2027 estimates have risen (+2.0% over 30 days, +2.8% over 90 days), suggesting analysts view any recovery as back-end loaded and contingent on sustained higher oil. The earnings surprise record — 2 beats, 2 misses, with the two beats coming in the second half of 2025 at deeply depressed levels — provides limited confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term: Weak Activity Environment

Management's own commentary is direct: "Absent geopolitical disruptions, we expect commodity prices are unlikely to rise" in 2026. North America rig count fell from 786 (2024 average) to 736 (2025 average). International rigs declined from 1,162 to 1,080. U.S. production continues to hit all-time highs despite falling rig counts, reflecting efficiency gains that reduce per-unit service demand.

The company is cutting capex by 30% in 2026 (to ~$1.0-$1.1B) and has implemented $400M in annualized cost reductions. These are appropriate defensive measures, but they confirm that the organic growth trajectory is flat to negative.

### Medium-Term: Data Center Power Optionality

The VoltaGrid collaboration for 400 MW of modular gas power systems targeting 2028 delivery is a genuine medium-term opportunity. Data center power demand is structural and growing. However, 2028 delivery means no earnings contribution for approximately two years, and the revenue potential remains unquantified. This is optionality, not a growth catalyst at current prices.

### Long-Term: LNG Capacity Expansion and International Deepwater

LNG capacity additions and deepwater project sanctioning provide longer-duration tailwinds. These are real but diffuse — they support the OFS sector broadly rather than providing HAL-specific differentiation. HAL's international revenue declined alongside the sector in 2025, and recovery depends on sustained commodity pricing that incentivizes incremental project sanctioning.

HAL trades at a 44% premium to peer median trailing P/E. For a company with declining revenue, compressing margins, and falling near-term estimates, this premium requires a view that the activity environment improves materially. The peer set includes E&P companies (DVN, EQT, EXE) with more direct commodity leverage and lower multiples, as well as FTI at 31.7x — the only peer comparably expensive.

On a forward basis, 17.3x FY2026E is reasonable if $2.21 EPS is achievable, but if oil prices settle into the $75-85 range consistent with the current ceasefire scenario, that estimate likely needs to come down 10-15%, pushing the effective multiple to 19-20x.

Analyst consensus targets range from $29.00 to $46.48, with a median of $40.00 — only 5% above the current price. The mean target of $38.86 is essentially at the current price. The consensus already reflects much of the upside case.

## Competitive Landscape

Halliburton competes primarily with Schlumberger (SLB) and Baker Hughes (BKR) in a mature oligopoly. HAL has historically held a dominant position in North American completions, particularly pressure pumping, but this segment faces structural headwinds from efficiency gains and the Zeus e-frac platform transition (which reduces fleet requirements per unit of production).

The company's strategic pivot toward international markets and higher-technology services is sound directionally but hasn't yet offset North American weakness. International D&E revenue remains the most durable revenue stream, but growth has stalled as Saudi Arabia and other OPEC+ producers curtail activity.

The chemical business divestiture (Multi-Chem, resulting in a $200M impairment) signals portfolio rationalization but also reduces revenue diversity.

## Risk Assessment

### Discrete Risks

**IRS Termination Fee Dispute:** The potential $640M+ cash tax liability (plus interest) from the IRS challenge to HAL's $3.5B deduction of the termination fee paid in connection with the failed 2016 Baker Hughes merger is the most significant discrete risk. Management states it would not impair capital returns even if they lose, but $640M represents approximately 40% of 2025 free cash flow. Resolution timeline: potentially years.

**PEMEX Credit Exposure:** Mexico's primary customer represented 11% of total receivables as of Q3 2025 (up from 7% at year-end 2024), with $592M in credit default swaps related to financing arrangements. PEMEX's financial condition is well-known to be precarious.

**Venezuela Receivables:** The $805M allowance for credit losses is primarily comprised of Venezuelan receivables (likely PDVSA). Recovery remains uncertain.

**Tax Structure Change:** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act's FTC rule changes forced a $125M valuation allowance, structurally impairing HAL's international tax position and raising the go-forward effective tax rate from ~22% toward 27%.

### Structural Risks

**Oil Price Dependence:** The entire re-rating from $19 to $38 was driven by geopolitical oil price premium. A sustained period of oil in the $70-80 range would pressure rig counts further and make current estimates unachievable.

**Efficiency Paradox:** U.S. production hitting all-time highs with fewer rigs means the industry needs less service work per barrel produced. This is a secular headwind for OFS companies that no cyclical oil price recovery can reverse.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 69.2% versus 37.4% historical volatility indicates extreme options richness — IV is nearly double HV. The term structure is in sharp backwardation (69.2% near-term vs. 38.3% at 6-month), meaning the market is pricing imminent event risk. The 1-week implied move of ±1.2% seems modest relative to the IV level, suggesting elevated kurtosis/tail expectations rather than linear moves. Put/call OI ratio of 0.55 (call-heavy) and notable unusual activity on $39 May calls (14.2x volume/OI) suggest speculative upside positioning — likely tied to geopolitical oil scenarios. The elevated IV makes initiating new long equity positions expensive on a risk-adjusted basis, as the options market is pricing in substantial uncertainty not reflected in the spot equity price.

## Investment Thesis

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The most identifiable catalyst is the Iran ceasefire's potential extension into a framework agreement, which would pull oil below $80 and pressure rig count expectations. The 10-day ceasefire window provides a specific near-term timeline (resolving by late April 2026). Q1 2026 earnings (likely late April) could also disappoint if the oil price recovery arrived too late to flow through to service activity.

**Timing window:** 2-6 weeks. Ceasefire resolution and Q1 earnings report are the near-term catalysts.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI of 5.1% of float with 1.8 days to cover is manageable — low squeeze risk. The short is uncrowded, suggesting limited consensus on the bearish view.

**Borrow and options signal:** The extreme IV richness (69.2% near-term vs. 37.4% HV) makes put purchases expensive. However, the call-heavy OI (0.55 P/C ratio) and unusual call activity at $39 suggest speculative bullish positioning that could unwind quickly on peace-driven oil weakness. Borrow should be readily available given the low SI%.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Base bear target of $28-30 (implied by 13-14x on $2.10-$2.20 EPS in a sub-$80 oil environment) represents 22-27% downside from $38.15. Upside risk to $42-44 on ceasefire failure represents 10-15% upside. Risk/reward: approximately 2:1 to 1.8:1 in favor of the short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** While the risk/reward is borderline favorable, the thesis depends on a specific geopolitical outcome (ceasefire extending) that has a 25-30% probability, and the stock has strong technical momentum. The 78% rally reflects real geopolitical risk premium that may persist through repeated ceasefire-and-failure cycles. The better expression is avoiding the name and monitoring for deterioration in activity data. Shorting HAL into an active Middle East conflict — even during a ceasefire — carries unacceptable left-tail risk on any escalation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Oil sustained above $90/bbl for 8+ weeks with corresponding rig count stabilization (Baker Hughes weekly data showing US Land rigs above 580)
- FY2026 EPS estimates revised upward by 5%+ over a 60-day period
- Valuation compresses to 12-13x forward earnings (stock at ~$27-29 on current estimates)
- Insider open-market purchases by C-suite executives

**Timeline:** The AVOID thesis persists for 3-6 months minimum. If the ceasefire produces a framework deal and oil settles below $80, near-term estimates will need to be cut substantially, validating the thesis. If the ceasefire fails and oil returns above $95, the thesis weakens and should be reassessed at next quarterly earnings.

## Conclusion

Halliburton has repriced from $19 to $38 on geopolitical oil premium, but the underlying business faces declining revenue, compressing margins, falling near-term estimates, and uniform insider selling. At 25x trailing and 17x forward — a substantial premium to energy peers — the stock prices in a recovery that depends on sustained $85+ oil, now directly threatened by the Iran ceasefire. The company's own defensive actions (30% capex cut, $400M cost reductions, workforce cuts, asset divestitures) confirm that management does not expect organic activity growth. The $640M IRS dispute and PEMEX credit exposure add to the unfavorable asymmetry. The VoltaGrid data center power opportunity is genuine but 2+ years from revenue contribution. At current prices, the risk/reward is unfavorable.
