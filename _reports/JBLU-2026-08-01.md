## Executive Summary

JetBlue's June 2026 quarter contained the first genuinely encouraging operating data in three years. Revenue rose 14.5% to $2,697M on 3.2% more capacity, meaning unit revenue climbed 10.9% with yield up 9.6% *and* load factor up 0.8 points — price gains taken without volume sacrifice. Unit costs excluding fuel grew 2.4%, a sharp deceleration from FY2025's 6.2% and FY2024's 6.6%. Management reinstated full-year guidance, quantified JetForward at $470M of cumulative incremental EBIT delivered against a $1.2B 2028 target, and published a 2028 EPS target of at least $1.00. The revenue improvement is externally corroborated: Aviation Week's analysis of 339 former Spirit routes concludes nearly half of that carrier's capacity remains unfilled, which is consistent with JetBlue growing Fort Lauderdale capacity 40% while raising FLL unit revenue 11%.

The problem is that none of this reaches the equity holder yet, and much of it is already in the price. The company reported a $141M operating loss and a $247M net loss in the quarter because fuel rose 80.7% to $4.23/gal. TTM operating margin is -5.9%, TTM EBITDA is approximately $146M against roughly $584M of interest expense and $490M of cash interest — coverage of 0.25x. Six-month operating cash flow was -$35M; capex was $348M. The gap is being funded by pledging aircraft: encumbered assets rose from 61% to 67% of owned property, equipment and intangibles in six months, and a new $500M aircraft-secured facility signed in April was fully drawn by June 30. Shareholders' equity fell from $2,120M to $1,587M in two quarters, putting equity/assets at 9.7%.

Critically, the market is not missing the inflection. FY2027 consensus of -$0.41 per share implies roughly a $155M net loss; adding back ~$584M of interest and assuming near-zero cash tax (there are $4.8B of federal NOLs) implies consensus already embeds approximately $430M of 2027 operating income, or about a 4% margin, versus a TTM operating loss of $565M. At $6.07 against a $5.77 consensus mean and $6.00 median, the stock prices a substantial portion of the turnaround while the company runs completely unhedged on fuel. This is a two-sided levered call on fuel reversion, not an underpriced recovery. We move to HOLD from prior AVOID recommendations on genuine fundamental improvement, but we would not commit new capital above $5.00-5.25, and at 26.7% short interest with 2.9 days to cover it is not shortable either.

## Company Overview

JetBlue operates a single reportable segment (air transportation including the TrueBlue loyalty program), positioning itself as a low-fare, high-service carrier anchored in New York, Boston, Fort Lauderdale, Los Angeles, Orlando and San Juan, serving over 100 destinations. The fleet at June 30, 2026 was 296 aircraft — 286 owned, only 10 on operating leases — averaging 12 years of age. The Embraer E190 fleet was fully retired during 2025, with remaining airframes sold in H1 2026 for a $30M net gain.

The high owned-aircraft proportion is the central structural fact of the balance sheet: it is the collateral base against which the company borrows, and the pace at which that collateral is being pledged is the real measure of runway.

Geographic mix in Q2 2026: Domestic & Canada $1,659M, Caribbean & Latin America $865M (32%), Atlantic $173M. Roughly 40% of revenue derives from ASMs touching outside the US and Canada across 32 countries — a more international, more leisure-weighted mix than most US low-cost peers.

The turnaround framework, "JetForward," comprises four priorities executed through specific initiatives: BlueFirst domestic first class (sales launch fall 2026, majority of retrofits by end-2027, management projecting ~5% RASM uplift); the Blue Sky commercial arrangement with United (reciprocal loyalty benefits from October 2025, expanded Mosaic/MileagePlus benefits in Q2 2026, Paisly distributing United car rentals from July 2026); premium ground product (BlueHouse lounge at JFK T5 opened December 2025, Boston opening August 2026); loyalty and co-brand card monetization; and LEO-satellite connectivity via Amazon Leo from 2027.

What JetBlue does not have is a cost advantage. FY2025 CASM ex-fuel was 11.20¢, up 6.2% after +6.6% in 2024 from 9.89¢ in 2023 — roughly 13% two-year unit cost inflation against a shrinking revenue base. Its durable assets are constrained slot positions at JFK, LaGuardia and Boston; the Mint premium product (J.D. Power's #1 North American first/business-class ranking for a second year); and the TrueBlue program, which was pledged in an August 2024 financing that raised ~$2.8B.

## Financial Analysis

### The multi-year record

| $M | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 9,158 | 9,615 | 9,279 | 9,062 |
| Operating income | (298) | (230) | (684) | (368) |
| Net income | (362) | (310) | (795) | (602) |
| Operating cash flow | — | 400 | 144 | (94) |
| Total equity | 3,563 | 3,337 | 2,641 | 2,120 |

Four consecutive annual net losses, with FY2025 revenue below FY2022 in nominal terms. A framing correction matters here: FY2024's $795M GAAP loss included a $532M Spirit merger-termination write-off, so on management's own adjusted basis the net loss *widened* from $245M in FY2024 to $593M in FY2025, and adjusted operating margin deteriorated from -1.0% to -3.7%. Adjusted loss per share went $0.71 to $1.64. Any screen reading GAAP year-over-year records improvement where the operating reality degraded materially. The proxy's own disclosure shows five consecutive years of negative pre-tax margin: -4.4%, -4.8%, -3.5%, -9.7%, -8.5%.

Isolating fuel: Q2 gallons of 215M repriced at the prior-year $2.40 would have cost $516M versus $911M actual, a $395M delta. Held static, the $141M operating loss becomes roughly $254M of operating income, a +9.4% margin. That arithmetic establishes that fuel more than fully accounts for the year-over-year margin decline. It is not a forecast — industry-wide cheap fuel historically invites capacity growth and fare competition, so JetBlue would not retain a $237 average fare in a $2.40 fuel world. The narrow, defensible conclusion is that commercial and cost execution improved while the P&L deteriorated for an exogenous reason.

### Cash, coverage and collateral

This is where the investment case is decided.

- Six-month 2026 operating cash flow: **-$35M**, against $348M of capex plus $27M of pre-delivery deposits, partly offset by $71M of flight-equipment sale proceeds. Free cash flow is deeply negative. FY2025 FCF was -$1,172M.
- Cash and restricted cash fell to $1,919M from $2,370M a year earlier. Company-stated liquidity: $2.2B unrestricted cash and investments plus a $600M undrawn revolver, down from $2.5B plus $600M at year-end 2025.
- Cash interest paid: $246M in each six-month period, roughly **$490M annually**.
- Derived TTM through 6/30/26: revenue $9,503M; operating loss -$565M (-5.9% margin); net loss -$886M; D&A $711M implying **EBITDA of approximately +$146M**; total debt including finance leases $8,478M. That is **Debt/EBITDA near 58x and EBITDA/interest of 0.25x**.
- Equity fell $533M in six months to $1,587M; equity/assets 9.7% versus 12.8% at year-end. Retained earnings stand at $151M and turn negative during H2 2026 on the H1 run rate.
- **Pledged assets: $8.1B net book value, 67% of owned PP&E and intangibles, up from 61% at year-end 2025.** A $500M facility secured by owned A321/A320/A220 aircraft was signed April 14, 2026 and fully drawn by June 30, with up to $250M of additional availability.

The maturity ladder is not the near-term problem: $236M in the remainder of 2026, $478M in 2027, $582M in 2028, then **$1,835M in 2029** (coinciding with the TrueBlue term loan and the $460M 2.50% convertible notes). The binding constraint is the shrinking pool of unencumbered aircraft, which is the mechanism funding sub-break-even operations.

Two important counterweights. First, the working capital deficit of $1,377M and 0.74 current ratio overstate distress — $2,000M of current liabilities is air traffic liability, extinguished by flying passengers rather than paying cash. Negative working capital is structurally normal for airlines. Second, and more substantively: **the fair value of JetBlue's debt was $8,320M against $8,478M carrying value at 6/30/26 (~98% of par), versus $7,829M against $8,498M at 12/31/25 (~92% of par)**. Credit spreads tightened materially over six months. Bond investors, who sit senior and are underwritten against the same collateral, are pricing meaningfully lower distress probability than an Altman Z-Score of 0.65 implies. When equity screens and credit markets disagree this sharply on solvency, the credit market is generally the better-informed party.

A new item worth flagging: the Q2 effective tax rate collapsed to 8.7% (6.7% for six months) because the company recorded a valuation allowance against certain deferred tax assets. Losses no longer generate meaningful tax benefits, so GAAP losses now flow through at close to pre-tax magnitude — this explains part of the gap between the -5.2% operating margin and -9.2% net margin. It is non-cash and does not impair the underlying $4.8B of federal NOLs, which remain available subject to Section 382.

**Financial Health: 2.5/10.** Zero earnings coverage of interest, negative operating cash flow, eroding equity, and a liquidity model dependent on progressively encumbering the fleet. The credit-market signal and the light near-term maturity ladder prevent a lower score.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line inflection is real and has an identifiable external cause. Spirit Airlines declared bankruptcy and ceased operations in May 2026, with nearly 100 aircraft in desert storage. Aviation Week's June 16, 2026 analysis of 339 former Spirit routes concludes Frontier and JetBlue are the largest beneficiaries but that nearly half of the ULCC's former capacity remains unfilled. JetBlue's own data corroborates this: FLL RASM +11% while FLL capacity grew 40%. Adding 40% capacity into a market while raising unit revenue 11% is only arithmetically possible if net supply fell.

Specific growth vectors:

- **Fort Lauderdale**: more than 125 daily departures to 55+ nonstop destinations by July 2026, the largest schedule ever, with a structured bank schedule for connectivity and management targeting 150+ daily flights and its largest-ever FLL Mint schedule by December.
- **LaGuardia slots**: SDNY Bankruptcy Court approved the §363 transfer of 22 LaGuardia operating authorizations to JetBlue for $58.5M on July 22, 2026, supporting up to 12 daily roundtrips. Closing remains subject to regulatory approval with conditions to be satisfied no later than October 31, 2026 — not yet consummated, and should not be modeled as secured.
- **BlueFirst**: sales launch fall 2026, management projecting ~5% RASM contribution.
- **Blue Sky/United**: DOT completed its review and permitted the arrangement to proceed. Paisly began distributing United car rentals in July 2026, with hotels and insurance expected in Q4. United is a commercial counterparty, not a customer or an owner; we found no verification of any equity, warrant or slot-exchange component and treat such claims as unsupported.
- **JetForward**: $470M cumulative incremental EBIT through June 2026, targeting $850-950M annualized by end-2027 and $1.2B in 2028.

Estimate revisions are strongly positive across every window: current quarter +33.6% over 90 days, next quarter +40.6%, current year +19.4%, next year +42.9%. The earnings surprise record is mixed — two beats and two misses in four quarters, with Q2's $(0.66) beating a $(0.72) consensus by 8.9% on a like-for-like basis (adjusted equalled GAAP in the quarter, with no special items).

Two framing disciplines apply to these revisions. First, they represent losses narrowing, not positive earnings rising — there is no forward P/E against which to capitalize the improvement, which is precisely the condition under which the estimate-revision signal is weakest. Second, and more decisively, the revisions have already run: FY2027 consensus of -$0.41 per share on ~377M shares implies a $155M net loss, which after adding back ~$584M of interest and near-zero cash tax implies roughly $430M of operating income and a ~4% operating margin. Consensus already assumes a swing of approximately $1.0B in operating income from the TTM base. The street is not skeptical of JetForward; it has substantially underwritten it.

**Growth Prospects: 5.5/10** (from 5.0). Upgraded on the Q2 beat, guidance reinstatement, the independently sourced capacity-gap evidence, and the LaGuardia slot award. Capped by the fact that growth is price-driven off a competitor's liquidation, ASM growth is only "modest," and the durability of the supply gap is a 2-3 year proposition — Frontier is expanding into the same routes and a Spirit brand revival has been reported as possible.

## Valuation Assessment

There is no meaningful P/E: TTM EPS is -$1.66 and consensus is negative through 2027. Peer medians (P/E 22.1 across ALGT, SNCY, SKYW, ALK, AL) are not usable comparables for a loss-making carrier.

The workable anchors:

- **EV/revenue**: net debt of roughly $6,278M plus $2.29B market cap gives EV near $8.57B against TTM revenue of $9,503M — **0.90x**, or ~0.99x including approximately $908M of operating lease liabilities.
- **Price/book**: book value per share of $4.21 ($1,587M / 377.3M) puts the stock at **1.44x book**, on a book that fell $533M in six months and is likely near $3.60-3.80 by year-end 2026 on consensus H2 losses.
- **Consensus targets**: mean $5.77, median $6.00, low $3.50, high $8.00, against a price of ~$6.07. The stock trades above the mean and at the median. Sell-side distribution is 1 buy, 10 hold, 3 sell, 3 strong sell, with one hold moved to strong sell in the past month.

On the 2028 target: $1.00 EPS on ~377-395M shares implies roughly $380-395M of net income against a TTM net loss of $886M — a swing of about $1.27B. The bridge closes with modest slack ($1.2B of incremental JetForward EBIT on a roughly -$93M FY2024 adjusted EBIT baseline gives ~$1.05B absolute EBIT, less ~$584M interest, roughly $470M pre-tax and untaxed given the NOLs). The arithmetic is not fantastical. But the baseline year against which the $1.2B is measured is not confirmed by the company, and the chain requires fuel reverting to roughly $2.50-2.90/gal, full delivery of the remaining ~$730M of EBIT, no material step-up from four simultaneous labor negotiations, and no adverse litigation outcome.

Discounting the bull path: $1.00 of 2028 EPS at 10x is $10, discounted two years at 15% gives roughly $7.55. Weighted against a distressed outcome near $3.00-3.50, a probability-weighted value lands close to $5.50-5.75 — approximately where consensus sits and modestly below spot.

The valuation is neither a bargain nor obviously stretched. What it is not is a mispricing: the identifiable catalysts (Spirit supply withdrawal, JetForward, BlueFirst) are in consensus estimates, and the stock has appreciated 35% over twelve months and roughly 13-15% from its pre-print level. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument; the argument is that the specific improvement has been incorporated into the forward estimates against which the stock is now priced.

**Valuation: 4.5/10.** Fair value roughly at spot with a wide, fuel-dependent distribution. New capital deserves a discount to compensate for 0.25x interest coverage.

## Competitive Landscape

JetBlue sits structurally between ULCCs it cannot match on cost (CASM ex-fuel 11.12¢) and legacies it cannot match on network breadth, corporate contracts or loyalty scale. Its strategic response — moving up-market via Mint, BlueFirst, lounges and a premium co-brand while harvesting Spirit's abandoned leisure geography — is coherent and mirrors the playbook Delta executed successfully. The difference is that Delta ran it from balance-sheet strength.

Trajectory is clearly improving, which matters more than level. Evidence: the FLL data (+11% RASM on +40% capacity), system RASM +10.9% on +3.2% ASMs, the 22 LaGuardia slots, DOT clearance for Blue Sky (notable given courts blocked the Spirit merger and forced the American alliance unwind), and Mint's second consecutive J.D. Power #1 ranking. On-time performance improved to 78.2% in Q2 from 74.3% for FY2025.

Constraints on the trajectory: unfilled capacity attracts entrants, Frontier is the obvious competitor for the same leisure demand, and a Spirit brand revival has been publicly floated. The FAA's temporary 10% slot relief at New York airports expires October 2026, which would force JetBlue either to operate slots it may not want or to risk forfeiture. Pratt & Whitney GTF reliability remains a capacity and cost constraint — an average of 9 aircraft on ground during 2025, with 200-300 day shop-visit turnarounds and FY2025 maintenance expense up 26% to $791M. The July 27, 2026 supplemental support agreements with Pratt & Whitney provide credits of up to $105M usable on qualifying purchases through December 31, 2027, covering disruptions only through December 31, 2025, and obtained in part by waiving certain claims. That is non-cash, capped, time-limited compensation against a materially larger disruption cost.

**Competitive Position: 5.0/10** (from 4.0). Improving trajectory on verifiable data, held back by a structurally uncompetitive cost base and the finite duration of the Spirit windfall.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Unhedged fuel.** The Q2 10-Q discloses **no fuel hedges outstanding** at June 30, 2026, and that a 10% fuel price increase raises annual fuel expense by $309M — 3.3% of TTM revenue, or roughly 3.3 points of operating margin per 10% move, with no derivative buffer. (Note the disclosure implies a ~$3.09B annual base, or roughly $3.70/gal, below Q2's $4.23 spot; treat $309M as a floor at current prices.) For a company at -5.9% TTM operating margin, this converts oil into the determinant of solvency. Reuters (July 28, 2026) attributes the Q2 fuel spike to ongoing fuel headwinds tied to the Middle East war; running unhedged is a deliberate posture that management has not explained in the filings.

**2. Interest against near-zero EBITDA.** ~$584M of interest expense and ~$490M of cash interest against $146M TTM EBITDA. Each sub-break-even quarter is funded by cash drawdown or new pledges; encumbrance moved 6 points in six months.

**3. Four simultaneous labor negotiations.** 50% of active FTE crewmembers are unionized. The ALPA pilot contract has been amendable since February 2025 with talks open since May 2024. The TWU inflight contract becomes amendable December 13, 2026. TWU flight-instructor initial-contract talks have run since April 2024. On February 26, 2026, dispatchers and air traffic/system controllers voted to join TWU, with negotiations not yet begun. The 2024 pilot deal delivered ~9% wage-rate increases; pilots receive a 17% non-elective 401(k) contribution. Negotiating from negative margins is historically the weakest bargaining position.

**4. NEA litigation.** Four consolidated putative antitrust class actions in the EDNY have completed fact discovery, and **plaintiffs filed a class-certification petition in early July 2026**, which JetBlue is opposing. Class certification transforms settlement economics in antitrust litigation. Separately, American Airlines sued JetBlue on April 28, 2025 in Tarrant County, Texas, alleging breach of the NEA revenue-sharing agreement for April 2022 through July 2023. No loss estimate is possible on either.

**5. Section 382.** With a $2.29B market cap and an activist holder trading around a >5% position, an inadvertent ownership change impairing $4.8B of NOLs is a non-trivial risk. We found no evidence of a protective NOL rights plan.

**6. Regulatory and closing conditions.** New York slot relief expires October 2026; the LaGuardia slot purchase must satisfy conditions by October 31, 2026; the DOT assessed a civil penalty in January 2025 for chronically delayed 2022-2023 flights.

**7. Consumer exposure.** Roughly 40% of revenue touches outside the US/Canada with a leisure-heavy Caribbean/LatAm mix (32% of Q2 revenue), exposed to hurricane season and regional macro. A domestic discretionary-spending slowdown would hit leisure yields first.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 59.5% sits slightly below one-year realized volatility of 62.6% and in the 21st percentile of the current chain, with a normal contango term structure (59.5% at 1m rising to 62.6% at 6m). Options are cheap relative to realized movement in a stock that has traded a $3.87-$6.50 range — unusual complacency for a name with this event density, and favorable for anyone wanting to express a view via options rather than shares. Put/call skew of 10.2% indicates elevated put demand, concentrated in the 1m-3m tenors (5.0% at 1m, 7.1% at 3m) and inverting to -3.8% at 6m, which reads as near-dated hedging around the October catalysts rather than a structural bearish position. Open interest remains heavily call-weighted (P/C OI 0.19) even as put volume ran 1.05x calls, and the only unusual print was a 998-contract buy of August 14 $6 calls against 330 open interest. Combined with 26.7% short interest and 2.9 days to cover, the structure suggests positioning skewed toward an upside squeeze on any fuel relief — which is a reason not to short, not a reason to own. (The two options feeds report inconsistent ATM IV levels; we rely on the term structure qualitatively rather than the absolute level.)

**Risk Factors: 2.5/10.** The unhedged fuel exposure alone justifies a low score; the labor cycle, class-certification timing and encumbrance clock compound it.

## Management Quality

The incentive design is well-matched to the problem. FY2025 performance measures were Operating Margin, Controllable Costs, Customer NPS, Absolute Pre-Tax Margin, EBIT Improvement and Relative TSR, with Pre-Tax Margin as the company-selected measure. Realized pay has been marked to shareholder outcomes: CEO compensation granted was $5,191,318 while compensation actually paid was $971,222, an 81% reduction; NEO averages were $3,223,253 granted versus $1,615,317 paid. Leadership is stable with no Item 5.02 departure filings in the recent window. Capital allocation shows discipline: aircraft commitments were reduced from $5.7B/86 aircraft to $5,471M/80 aircraft with deliveries deferred in favor of A320 life-extension modifications, and the $325M 0.50% convertible was repaid in full on April 1, 2026 from a pre-funded escrow.

Against that, the record is poor and the revealed preference is negative. Indexed five-year TSR: $100 became **$31 for JBLU versus $85 for the airline peer group** — roughly 2.7x underperformance versus direct comparables, isolating the problem as company-specific. Five consecutive years of negative pre-tax margin.

On insider activity: across the roughly 20 most recent Form 4s (February-June 2026) there were **no open-market purchases** by any officer or director. Director Thomas Winkelmann sold his entire 35,473-share position on May 1 at $4.70-4.71, going to zero. Director Vivek Sharma cut holdings ~44% on May 28 at ~$5.40. The Principal Accounting Officer sold 18,193 shares at $5.00, to zero. New 10b5-1 sell plans were adopted April 30, 2026 for the CFO (up to 108,368 shares) and CDTO (24,000). Carl Icahn reportedly reduced his active stake to 5.55% after selling 4,331,821 shares between June 1 and June 9, 2026.

The benign reading — diversification and tax management by executives with concentrated exposure and depressed realized compensation, plus a board retirement — is plausible and we do not weight the selling heavily on its own. What carries more weight is the *absence* of a single officer open-market purchase near book value in the same window management published a $1.00 2028 EPS target. Note also that the April 22, 2026 RSU grants were very large in share terms (CEO 446,428; CFO, President and COO 274,725 each), which is standard mechanics when the share price is depressed but means insiders now hold substantial upside-levered exposure struck near the bottom without having committed cash.

The decision we weight most is the choice to run zero fuel hedges with 0.25x interest coverage. That is a risk-management judgment, not an accident, and it materially widens the distribution of outcomes for equity holders.

**Management Quality: 3.5/10.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $9.50-10.00 (+57% to +65%)

Brent retreats and jet fuel falls to $2.90-3.10. On unchanged Q2 volumes that recovers $250-300M of quarterly fuel cost. JetForward delivers the remaining ~$730M of incremental EBIT; BlueFirst launches on schedule and contributes its projected ~5% RASM uplift; the 22 LaGuardia slots close by October 31 and add 12 daily roundtrips in the most slot-constrained US market; Spirit's unfilled capacity persists through 2027 keeping leisure yields elevated; labor settlements land near inflation rather than at 2024-pilot-deal levels. FY2027 operating income reaches $430-500M, 2028 EPS approaches $1.00, and $4.8B of NOLs shield the recovery from tax. With 85.5M shares short against 2.9 days to cover and call-heavy open interest, the re-rating would be violent. At 10x $1.00 discounted back, $9.50-10.00.

### Bear Case — $3.00-3.25 (-46% to -51%)

Jet fuel stays above $4.20. The $309M-per-10% sensitivity consumes the entire JetForward benefit. FY2026 losses land at or below the -$2.20 consensus; H2 burns another $250M+ of net loss; book value falls toward $3.60 and retained earnings turn negative. Encumbrance passes 75% of owned PP&E, and the next financing comes at a wider spread or requires equity. One or more labor contracts settle at industry-leading rates, adding structural CASM. Antitrust class certification is granted, forcing a reserve and altering settlement economics. Frontier and a possible Spirit revival refill the FLL and LGA capacity gap. At roughly 0.85x a $3.70 book, $3.00-3.25.

### Base Case — $5.50-5.75 (-5% to -9%)

Jet fuel remains elevated through 2027. JetBlue delivers the ~3.5-point H2 operating margin improvement and finishes FY2026 near a -3% operating margin, roughly the midpoint of the reported -2% to -5% range. FY2027 operating income lands at $300-430M, below the level required for the 2028 EPS target but consistent with "sustained operating profitability." Net income remains negative through 2027 (consensus -$0.41), book value bottoms near $3.60-3.80, and the equity trades at 1.45-1.55x that book. Weighted against the bull and bear tails, roughly $5.50-5.75 — approximately the $5.77 consensus mean, modestly below spot.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline**: the Q3 2026 report (late October 2026) is the decisive checkpoint, and it coincides with three dated items — the October 31, 2026 deadline for LaGuardia slot closing conditions, the October 2026 expiry of FAA New York slot relief, and a likely ruling window on NEA class certification.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Positive operating cash flow for two consecutive quarters** with pledged assets stable at or below 67% of owned PP&E and intangibles. This is the single condition that converts JetBlue from a financing story into an operating story.
2. **Jet fuel realized below $3.20/gal** for a full quarter, or the initiation of a meaningful hedge program. Either removes the 3.3-point-per-10% margin exposure that currently governs the outcome.
3. **A price near $4.75-5.00** — approximately 1.25-1.30x projected year-end book — which would restore adequate compensation for 0.25x interest coverage. At that level we would upgrade on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Q3 CASM ex-fuel growth re-accelerating above 4%**, or any labor settlement adding more than 100bp to unit costs. Either would break the cost-control plank that made Q2 credible.
2. **Pledged assets exceeding 75% of owned PP&E and intangibles**, or liquidity falling below $1.8B. That would compress the runway from multi-year to roughly 12-18 months.
3. **Grant of class certification in the EDNY NEA litigation, or an adverse ruling in the American Airlines Tarrant County case** with a quantified reserve.
4. Failure to satisfy LaGuardia slot closing conditions by October 31, 2026, which would remove the clearest near-term network catalyst.

**What would make us wrong in the other direction**: a sustained decline in crude that pulls jet fuel toward $2.90-3.10. That would deliver most of the bull case mechanically, and with 26.7% of the float short the equity response would be disproportionate. That is why we do not short this and why we treat the position as two-sided rather than negative.

## Conclusion

JetBlue has produced the first quarter in three years where both the commercial and cost programs showed measurable traction — RASM +10.9% on +3.2% capacity, CASM ex-fuel growth cut from 6.2% to 2.4% — and the revenue improvement is corroborated by an independent source rather than resting on management assertion. Credit spreads tightened materially over six months, from 92% to 98% of par, which is the most reliable signal in the file that solvency risk is lower than the equity screens suggest. Those developments justify the improved score and the continuation of a HOLD rather than a return to AVOID.

They do not justify buying the equity at ~$6.07. The stock trades above the $5.77 consensus mean, and FY2027 consensus of -$0.41 already embeds roughly $430M of operating income — a $1.0B swing from the TTM base. The improvement is in the price. What is not compensated is the risk structure: $584M of interest against $146M of TTM EBITDA, negative free cash flow, book value falling $533M in two quarters, collateral encumbrance rising 6 points in six months, four simultaneous labor negotiations, a pending antitrust class-certification decision, and zero fuel hedges against a $309M annual exposure per 10% fuel move. Management's decision to carry that exposure unhedged, combined with the absence of a single officer open-market purchase near book value while publishing a 2028 EPS target, is the reason the management score stays at 3.5.

**HOLD.** Existing holders have a legitimate levered call on fuel reversion with 85.5M shares short providing upside torque; new capital should wait for $4.75-5.00, evidence of positive operating cash flow, or the initiation of a hedge program. Given 26.7% short interest, 2.9 days to cover and cheap near-term implied volatility, this is not a short.
