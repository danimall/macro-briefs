**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Victoria's Secret & Co. has delivered a credible operational turnaround under CEO Hillary Super's "Path to Potential" strategy. FY2025 (fiscal year ended January 2026) revenue rose 5% to $6.55B with comparable sales up 5% (versus flat the prior year), U.S. sales per square foot up 6% to $624, and the international channel up 27% to $967M. Adjusted operating income improved to $403M from $373M and adjusted diluted EPS to $3.00 from $2.69, even as GAAP results were depressed by a $120M Adore Me impairment and $36M of restructuring charges. The Q1 FY2026 print (reported June 2, 2026) accelerated the story: net sales +15% to ~$1.56B and adjusted EPS ~$0.60 against ~$0.32 consensus, driving the stock's best single day ever (~+40%) and subsequent record highs.

The problem for a new buyer is that the market has recognized this. The stock has risen from a 52-week low of $17.53 to $81.45, a ~4.6x move, and now trades at roughly 13x forward EPS ($3.48 current-year consensus, $4.17 next-year). That forward multiple is not stretched relative to the business's re-accelerating growth, and it sits at a modest premium to a mixed peer set. But the trailing multiple of 28x reflects a company still normalizing off one-time charges, current-quarter estimates have flattened after a +276% 90-day revision (mostly a mechanical reset off a depressed base), and the two most powerful swing factors, tariffs and discretionary consumer demand, are unresolved and two-sided. Short interest at 22.5% of float signals a large cohort of investors who see either continued squeeze fuel or structural deterioration ahead.

We rate VSCO a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The turnaround is real and the balance sheet is healthier than screening metrics suggest, but at record highs the asymmetry that existed at $18–$40 is gone. The appropriate posture is to own on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s (near the consensus median target and where forward P/E compresses to ~15x on the following year), or to add on confirmation that Q2/Q3 comps sustain the momentum. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the bar for continuing it is met because the price has moved to fair value, not because fundamentals have deteriorated.

## Company Overview

Victoria's Secret & Co. is the world's largest intimate-apparel company, spun from L Brands in 2021 and headquartered in Reynoldsburg, Ohio. It operates a brand portfolio: the flagship Victoria's Secret intimates brand (bras are the strategic and highest-margin core), PINK (a lifestyle intimates/apparel brand skewed to younger women), a fragrance-led Beauty business (Bombshell the signature scent), and Adore Me, a DTC lingerie brand acquired in early 2023 along with the DailyLook subscription styling service.

The footprint comprises ~790 company-operated stores (766 U.S., 24 Canada), a China JV (65 stores), and 562 partner-operated stores across roughly 70 countries. FY2025 channel mix was North America stores $3.54B (54%), Direct/digital $2.04B (31%), and International $967M (15%). The competitive assets management emphasizes are leading intimates market share, in-store bra-fitting expertise, a large loyal customer base, an engaged social community, and the revived Victoria's Secret Fashion Show (relaunched October 2025).

The turnaround thesis rests on re-establishing "bra authority," the highest-margin, most defensible category that anchors customer acquisition and pulls buyers into adjacent categories (sport, swim, sleep, beauty). International and beauty are the diversification engines away from a mature, competitive North American mall-based store base.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The multi-year trajectory shows genuine re-acceleration: FY2023 $6.34B → FY2024 $6.18B → FY2025 (Jan'25) $6.23B → FY2026 10-K year (Jan'26) $6.55B (+5%). Comparable sales turned to +5% from flat, and comp store sales to +4% from -2%. International led at +27% to $967M, driven by China, franchise royalties, wholesale/sourcing, and the shift of EU direct sales to a Netherlands distribution center. U.S. store productivity improved on lower selling square footage (sales/sq ft $624, +6%; sales/avg store $4.3M, +7%; selling area -2%), the operating-leverage signature of a working turnaround.

**Margins.** Gross margin slipped modestly to 36.4% from 36.7%, a reasonable outcome given ~$85M of net tariff drag and $36M of Adore Me/DailyLook fulfillment restructuring, partly offset by reduced promotion and more full-price selling. The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap is the crux of the FY2025 story. GAAP operating income fell to $271M (4.1% margin) from $310M (5.0%), but this reflects a -$120M Adore Me long-lived asset impairment, -$36M restructuring, and a +$69M interchange fee litigation gain. Stripping those, adjusted operating income rose to $403M from $373M. GAAP net income was $161M (EPS $1.93) versus adjusted $250M (EPS $3.00). The effective tax rate was an unusually low 9.2% (from 23.6%) on a $24M China JV deferred-tax valuation allowance release, which flatters GAAP EPS and is not repeatable.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $499M (+$74M YoY); capex $187M; free cash flow $312M (4.8% margin). FY2026 capex is guided up to $220–240M for the store program plus tech/logistics. Cash grew to $518M from $227M. The ABL facility is undrawn with $589M availability. Total debt is ~$975M ($379M Term Loan B due 2028; $600M 4.625% notes due 2029; ABL matures 2030), and a December 2025 term-loan amendment cut the rate spread, bringing interest expense to $70M. Debt-to-cap improved to 53% from 60%. Credit ratings are Moody's Ba3 / S&P BB-, both stable, sub-investment grade.

The screening leverage looks alarming (D/E 4.79, equity/assets 17.1%, Altman Z 1.94 grey zone) but is distorted by the operating-lease-heavy retail model (~$2.45B of future lease obligations) against modest book equity ($856M). Coverage is comfortable: OCF/capex 267%, FCF $312M, all covenants in compliance, undrawn ABL. A standardized data pull flags distress that the cash-generation profile does not support. That said, sub-IG ratings and a ~$975M debt load in a higher-for-longer rate environment keep the financial-health score below the "fortress" tier: the business carries real leverage that is well-serviced, not eliminated.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is the most encouraging part of the story. FY2025 comps of +5% and Q1 FY2026 net sales +15% with adjusted EPS roughly double consensus indicate the bra-authority reset is acquiring new customers, including in the 18–24 demographic and across income cohorts, with those customers buying into adjacent categories. Management raised full-year sales guidance on the Q1 print, citing strong customer acquisition and lower-than-expected tariff costs.

Estimate revisions are broadly supportive but require careful reading. Current-year EPS has been revised +14.5% over 90 days to $3.48, and next-year +9.7% to $4.17. The current-quarter +275.7% 90-day revision (to $0.32) is largely a mechanical reset off a depressed $0.08 base and has flattened over the last 30 days (-0.9%), so it is not the clean, sustained signal it appears to be at first glance. The more durable signals are the +9.7% next-year and +14.5% current-year revisions on a stock trading at ~13x forward; that combination of moderate positive revision velocity plus a reasonable forward multiple supports the growth case without triggering the strongest signal, which requires >30% revisions sustained across windows alongside >30% revenue growth. VSCO's revenue growth is mid-single-digit to mid-teens, not the >40% territory of a true trough re-acceleration.

Growth durability rests on three legs: international (+27%, the clearest structural expansion runway via China, franchise, travel-retail beauty), digital (31% of revenue), and full-price mix normalization. The North American store base is mature and mall-dependent, so it is a productivity story (sales per square foot) rather than a unit-growth story; the company closed 32 stores and opened 16 in FY2025. We score growth prospects at 6.3: positive and improving, with international and beauty providing real diversification, but tempered by a mature core, mid-single-digit comps that are cyclically sensitive, and a discretionary end-market facing a plausible H2 consumer slowdown.

## Valuation Assessment

At $81.45, VSCO trades at ~28x trailing (distorted by one-time charges and the low tax rate) and ~13x forward EPS ($3.48 current-year). On next-year consensus of $4.17, the multiple is ~19.5x, reflecting the growth being priced with some skepticism given the cyclical and tariff risks.

Peer context is mixed. The apparel-retail median P/E is 11.4 (ANF 8.7, AEO 11.4, BKE 10.3, URBN 14.9, BOOT 23.8). VSCO's 13x forward sits modestly above the median but below the higher-multiple growth names (BOOT, URBN). The premium to ANF/AEO/BKE is defensible given VSCO's re-accelerating comps, international runway, and adjusted-EPS growth, but it is not a screaming discount. The peer group also carries a value-trap caution: apparel retail is precisely the sector where cheap multiples have historically been anti-predictive, and where a low P/E often signals a structurally challenged, mall-dependent business rather than a bargain. VSCO's forward multiple is not the source of the investment case; the growth trajectory is.

Applying the cycle-adjusted framework: the trailing 28x is meaningless because FY2025 GAAP earnings were depressed by the Adore Me impairment. The relevant metric is forward P/E on the improving consensus, which is reasonable at ~13x current-year. The prior-appreciation guardrail cuts the other way here: the stock is not expensive simply because it ran 4.6x, but the re-rating has already captured the turnaround recognition. The consensus median target of $67 sits ~18% below the current $81 price, and the mean is $65.78, with the high at $81 essentially at spot. When the entire analyst target range brackets or sits below the current price, the "targets are floors" principle does not apply: this is a stock trading at or above where sell-side analysts see fair value, which is a caution signal rather than confirmation of further upside. One July analyst view explicitly argues the turnaround is "complete and fully priced in."

We score valuation at 5.2. The forward multiple is reasonable and not stretched, but the stock trades above the consensus mean/median target, the easy re-rating is behind it, and the sector's history of cheap-multiple value traps warns against rewarding the discount-to-BOOT/URBN framing. There is no identified catalyst for further multiple expansion beyond continued comp beats, which are increasingly priced in.

## Competitive Landscape

VSCO holds leading intimates market share, and the bra category is the most defensible, highest-margin anchor. In-store fitting expertise, a large loyalty base, and an engaged social community are genuine assets, and the revived Fashion Show is a brand-relevance investment. The Q1 data showing bras acquiring new customers who then buy adjacent categories is the clearest evidence the moat is being rebuilt.

The competitive position is stabilizing-to-improving after years of erosion, but the trajectory is not unambiguously positive. VSCO is more mall-dependent than many peers, a structural disadvantage as traffic shifts online, and it competes against department stores, specialty chains, discounters, and DTC pure-play lingerie disruptors. The Adore Me impairment ($120M, with intangibles written to $0 from $95M) confirms that the company's attempt to buy DTC capability was a poor deal, a negative mark on the moat-expansion strategy. International and beauty are the credible offense, but the core North American story is defensive productivity rather than share gains against a growing market. We score competitive position at 5.5: improving trajectory off a low base, real category authority in bras, but offset by structural mall dependence and a failed DTC acquisition. Consistent with our general caution, competitive-position signals have shown weak recent predictive value, so we weight this modestly.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs — material and two-sided.** FY2025 carried ~$85M net operating-income drag from Southeast Asia sourcing. The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs, replaced by a new 10% Section 122 global tariff, creates hard-to-model optionality: possible refunds on amounts already paid versus uncertain future levels. Q1 FY2026's beat was partly driven by lower-than-expected tariff costs, so this swings both ways and is the single largest exogenous variable.

**Discretionary consumer exposure.** Beta 2.25 reflects high cyclicality. Intimate apparel has shown relative resilience (retail traders dubbed it "war proof, tariff proof" after Q1), but a broad discretionary slowdown would pressure comps and compress the multiple simultaneously.

**Activist and governance overhang.** BBRC (Brett Blundy, ~13% stake) ran a proxy contest; all nine company nominees were re-elected June 16, 2026 (Chair Donna James won 83% support), but director Mariam Naficy declined to stand, a partial activist win. The poison pill expired May 2026, so BBRC can re-engage. VSCO publicly alleged its second-largest shareholder "misappropriated" trade secrets, an unusually adversarial escalation with litigation and continued-overhang risk. Blundy endorsed CEO Super, so the fight is about board/capital-allocation legacy, not the turnaround leader.

**Cybersecurity.** A May 2025 incident took the website down for days, costing ~$20M net sales / ~$14M operating income, a realized, recurring-category risk for a 31%-digital business.

**Capital allocation.** Zero buybacks against a $250M authorization and no dividend; cash directed to debt paydown and reinvestment. This is prudent given sub-IG ratings, but a capital-return announcement is a likely near-term pressure point/catalyst under activist scrutiny.

**Leverage.** ~$975M debt plus ~$2.45B lease obligations at sub-IG ratings in a higher-for-longer rate environment. Well-serviced today, but a downturn compresses the coverage cushion.

We score risk factors at 4.8. Tariff and consumer-cyclical exposure are large and unresolved, the activist overhang persists, and the 22.5% short interest signals meaningful disagreement: these are specific, material risks not fully settled at the current valuation.

### Options Market Signal

No direct options-chain data was provided for VSCO. The most informative positioning signal is short interest at 22.5% of float with 7.2 days to cover, a large and crowded short book. It creates two-sided risk: continued comp beats or a capital-return announcement could fuel a squeeze, while the sheer size of the short base indicates a well-informed cohort betting on structural deterioration or mean-reversion after the ~40% single-day surge. Given the stock trades at record highs above consensus targets, we read the elevated short interest as a genuine warning that a material set of investors expects the momentum to fade, and we weight it as a caution rather than a squeeze opportunity to chase.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($95–105).** Q2/Q3 comps sustain high-single-digit-plus growth; international continues compounding above 20%; tariff refunds materialize as a windfall; bra authority keeps acquiring younger customers who trade into adjacent categories; and a capital-return announcement (buyback activation against the unused $250M authorization) plus a short squeeze on the 22.5% float drive the multiple toward the high end of the peer growth cohort. Adjusted EPS approaches $4.50+ and the stock re-rates.

**Bear case ($50–55, ~35–38% downside).** A broad discretionary consumer slowdown arrives, comps decelerate to low-single-digits or flat, tariff costs re-escalate under the new 10% regime with no refund, and the "fully priced in" thesis plays out as the momentum crowd rotates. The short base is vindicated, the multiple compresses toward the apparel-retail median (~11x), and next-year EPS estimates stall near $4.00. The stock reverts to the analyst low target of $50 and the mid-cycle earnings power gets re-rated as cyclical rather than secular.

**Base case ($67–72).** Comps normalize to mid-single-digits, international remains the growth engine, tariffs net out modestly negative-to-neutral, and the stock settles near the consensus median target as the re-rating consolidates. Forward EPS of ~$4.17 next year at ~16–17x yields ~$67–72, ~11–17% below the current price. This is why we do not rate the stock a BUY at $81: our base case sits below spot.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (uncertain rationale). The turnaround is genuine and the balance sheet is sturdier than screens suggest, but at record highs above the consensus target range, the risk/reward is roughly balanced, and the two dominant variables (tariffs, discretionary demand) are unresolved and two-sided.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s (near the $67 median target), where forward P/E compresses to ~15x next-year and the base case offers meaningful upside.
- Q2 FY2026 (reporting ~late August/September) confirming comps sustained at high-single-digits or better with international growth holding above 20%, plus a capital-return announcement (buyback activation).
- A clarified tariff outcome that resolves as neutral-to-favorable (refund realization).

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Q2/Q3 comps decelerating to flat-to-low-single-digits, signaling the momentum has peaked and demand is weakening.
- A guidance cut or a re-escalation of tariff costs with no refund path.
- Renewed activist escalation or litigation with BBRC that reintroduces a material governance discount.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2026 earnings report (~late August/September 2026), where the durability of the Q1 result will be tested. A secondary checkpoint is any resolution of the IEEPA tariff-refund question.

## Conclusion

Victoria's Secret has done the hard part: after two soft years, comps inflected to +5%, adjusted EPS grew to $3.00, international scaled 27%, and the Q1 FY2026 print confirmed the bra-authority reset is acquiring customers and driving full-price sales. The balance sheet, despite alarming screening optics, is healthy on a cash-flow and coverage basis. The management team, endorsed even by its activist challenger, is executing.

The issue is price. From a 52-week low of $17.53, the stock has re-rated to $81.45, above the consensus mean ($65.78) and median ($67) targets, with the analyst high at $81 essentially at spot. Our base case ($67–72) sits below the current price, the bull case ($95–105) requires sustained comp beats plus tariff windfalls plus a squeeze, and the bear case ($50–55) is a ~35–38% drawdown if the discretionary consumer weakens. The 22.5% short interest and the "fully priced in" analyst caution reinforce that the easy money has been made. We rate VSCO a HOLD and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s or on Q2 confirmation that the momentum is durable rather than a single-quarter spike.
