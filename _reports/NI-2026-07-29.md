**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NiSource is a pure-play regulated multi-utility serving ~4 million customers across six states through natural gas distribution (Columbia) and a combined gas-plus-electric footprint in northern Indiana (NIPSCO). The company's defining feature is a large-scale electric generation build-out — roughly $9.25-9.75B for existing Amazon and Alphabet data-center contracts — executed through a financing structure that isolates data-center capex and returns from existing ratepayers while defending its investment-grade balance sheet with Blackstone minority co-investment. The single largest regulatory overhang, the IURC's approval of the Amazon settlement, resolved favorably on June 23, 2026, with the utility positioning ~$1.4B of savings for existing NIPSCO customers. That is a genuine de-risking event that a standardized filing pull would still flag as pending.

The fundamental case rests on above-peer EPS growth (Adjusted EPS $1.37 in 2021 → $1.90 in 2025, a mid-single-digit-plus CAGR) driven by rate-base expansion and data-center generation, with RBC initiating Outperform at a $52 target in early July. The tension is on the funding side: NiSource outspends operating cash flow by a wide margin (Q1 2026 operating cash flow $442M against $805M of capex) and plans $5.2-5.6B of 2026 capex rising to ~$6.3B by 2028-29, financed with debt, a $1.5B ATM equity program, and Blackstone minority capital. This is the negative-FCF, dilution-dependent regulated-utility model, and its per-share economics are exposed to a higher-for-longer rate environment.

We rate NI a HOLD with a constructive growth lean. The stock has recovered ~11% from its July 2025 lows to $45.73, trades at ~22x forward EPS (a premium to the multi-utility peer median of ~20x), and the recurring re-rating catalyst — the Amazon approval — has largely played out. The next natural reassessment point is the Alphabet IURC/FERC order expected in Q3 2026 and the July gas-meter hearing. We would upgrade to BUY on positive estimate-revision velocity plus a clean Alphabet approval, or on a pullback toward the low-$40s where the risk/reward on a durable compounder improves.

## Company Overview

NiSource operates two reportable segments under the Public Utility Holding Company Act:

- **Columbia Operations** (~56% of assets, ~$16.2B): regulated natural gas distribution across Ohio, Pennsylvania, Virginia, Kentucky, and Maryland, serving ~2.44M customers. Q1 2026 operating income was $472.3M.
- **NIPSCO Operations** (~$18.5B of assets): the only combined gas-and-electric utility in the portfolio, serving ~497K electric and ~879K gas customers in northern Indiana. Q1 2026 operating income was $348.5M.

Substantially all operating income is rate-regulated, with cost-recovery trackers (GCA, FAC, TDSIC, CEP, SAVE) passing through commodity and much capital cost. The strategic differentiator is the electric generation build-out for hyperscale data centers, executed through a "Pool Resource Asset" model in which NiSource retains dispatch discretion over a shared generation pool. This lets the company serve multiple hyperscalers flexibly while insulating residential and commercial ratepayers from data-center costs. Northern Indiana sits within MISO and is regarded as a premier Midwest data-center market on land, transmission access, and constructive regulation.

The generation build-out is more merchant-like and contract-based than a typical wires-and-pipes utility, but is anchored by long-dated take-or-pay-style contracts and Blackstone's 19.9% co-investment in Generation Holdings II (GenCo). NiSource consolidates these VIEs as the controlling manager.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue was ~$6.52B (+23% YoY), driven almost entirely by rate cases and capital-recovery mechanisms rather than volume. Q1 2026 revenue rose 8.2% to $2,363M, operating income grew 7.9% to $819M, and diluted EPS increased to $1.06 from $1.00. Q1 2026 was slightly warmer than the prior-year period, so the growth is weather-neutral and reflects the durable rate-case-and-capital-recovery algorithm.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets stand at $36.6B against $15.46B of long-term debt and $11.9B of total equity (including $2.27B of Blackstone noncontrolling interest). The debt-to-cap covenant ratio is 51.5% versus a 70% maximum, comfortable headroom. Credit ratings are solidly investment grade (BBB+/Baa2/BBB at the parent, Baa1 at NIPSCO). Maintaining IG status is an explicit strategic constraint given the capex program, and a below-IG downgrade would trigger ~$158.5M of collateral posting. The Altman Z-score (~22) and near-zero D/E readings from screening tools are extraction artifacts for a regulated utility; the economically meaningful metric is ~1.6x LT-debt/equity, normal for the sector.

**Cash flow and capital intensity — the central tension.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $442M (from $686M, mostly working-capital and gas timing) against $805M of capex. NiSource funds the gap with commercial paper (limit raised to $2.5B in March 2026), the $1.5B ATM program (~$1.25B remaining, forward sales priced at $41.84 and $45.63), and Blackstone contributions. The 2026 capex guide of $5.2-5.6B rising to ~$6.3B by 2028-29 means the company will run negative free cash flow for the foreseeable horizon, with base business at $3.7-5.1B/yr and GenCo (data center) at $1.3-2.4B/yr.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend rose to $0.60 (from $0.56), a ~2.6% yield. Dividend coverage is adequate on adjusted EPS (~$1.90 vs $2.40 annualized dividend; the payout ratio is elevated but standard for a utility funding growth partly through equity). The financing structure is deliberately de-risked: off-corporate-balance-sheet capacity via Blackstone minority interests, plus fixed Amazon capacity charges that begin in 2027 regardless of termination.

The financial-health score of 5.5 reflects the reliable regulated cash-flow base and IG rating against the structurally negative FCF, the dilution embedded in the ATM program, and the sensitivity of a debt-and-equity-funded growth model to a higher-for-longer rate path. This model is capital-inefficient by necessity: the returns are regulated and contracted, but per-share compounding is diluted by the financing.

## Growth Analysis

NiSource has a credible above-peer growth algorithm. Adjusted EPS compounded from $1.37 (2021) to $1.90 (2025), and consensus points to $2.06 for the current year and $2.25 for next year — mid-single-digit-plus growth consistent with the ~6-8% utility target. The data-center pipeline extends this: the AWS (Amazon) contract expanded from 2,400 MW to 2,800 MW by end-2032, and the new 15-year Alphabet agreement ramps to 300 MW by 2030. The GenCo CCGT build is mobilizing mid-2026, with ~400 MW plus 100 MW BESS progressing and the 167 MW Tipton BESS converting to a build-transfer arrangement.

The demand backdrop supports durability. Data-center load growth is now visible across the regulated-utility complex, and the DOE Section 202(c) emergency orders forcing the retired R.M. Schahfer coal plant to stay online (through June 21, 2026) are hard evidence of northern-Indiana grid tightness — the same scarcity that makes new NiSource generation valuable. The EPA's February 2026 rescission of the 2009 Endangerment Finding is a modest tailwind, potentially saving ~$675M of IRP costs.

The offset for scoring is estimate momentum. The current-year and next-year consensus estimates have been essentially flat over 90 days (+0.2% and +1.1%), and the current-quarter estimate was cut 30.6% over 30 days (to $0.16 from $0.23) — a seasonal-quarter artifact given the gas-heavy earnings profile, but not the accelerating-revision picture that would justify a growth-hold upgrade. The growth thesis is durable and broad-based, but the market is not yet re-rating on rising estimates. Growth prospects earn a 6.5: genuine, multi-year, tailwind-backed growth, held below a 7 by the absence of positive revision velocity and by dilution that dampens the per-share translation.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.73, NiSource trades at ~22x forward EPS ($2.06-2.25 consensus), a premium to the multi-utility peer median of ~20.1x (CMS 20.1x, AEE 19.5x, PNW 19.3x, DTE 24.0x, CNP 26.1x). The premium is partly justified by above-peer EPS growth and the data-center optionality, but it is a premium nonetheless, and the dividend yield of ~2.6% sits below the peer median of ~3.1%. This is not a cheap stock on either earnings or yield.

Analyst targets bracket the range: consensus mean $51.17, median $52, high $55, low $43, with RBC's $52 Outperform anchoring the bull view. On a stock trading at ~22x forward with a growth premium already embedded, we treat consensus targets as fair reference points rather than floors; a utility at a peer-premium multiple is not a setup where targets lag reality. The ~14% implied upside to the median target is real but not asymmetric given the funding-model overhang.

We assign a valuation score of 5.5. The valuation dimension has historically sorted the wrong way ("cheap" has been a value-trap signal), but NiSource is not cheap; it is fairly-to-slightly-richly priced for its growth. The score reflects a balanced setup: the growth premium is defensible but not obviously mispriced, and the negative-FCF/dilution model plus a higher-for-longer rate path cap the multiple. We would need a pullback toward the low-$40s (near the peer median multiple and near the $43 low target) to see asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

NiSource's regulated monopoly franchises with broad cost-recovery trackers are a durable moat. The trajectory is improving on the electric side: the Pool Resource Asset model gives it a first-mover position in Midwest data-center power, and the June 23 IURC approval converted the largest pending regulatory risk into a done deal while strengthening the regulatory relationship through a ratepayer-friendly savings narrative. The 202(c) coal orders underscore the regional supply scarcity that makes its new generation valuable, and the pending Alphabet approval (expected Q3 2026) would extend the pipeline.

The differentiator versus wires-and-pipes peers is that NiSource is taking on more contract-based generation risk, partially offset by long-term contracts, fixed capacity charges beginning 2027 regardless of termination, and Blackstone co-investment. The competitive position is stable-to-improving, warranting a 6.5 — improving trajectory in a favorable jurisdiction, held below a 7 because the model concentrates counterparty and construction risk in ways a pure distribution utility does not, and because competitive_position has been a weak forward discriminator in recent data, so we lean on trajectory evidence rather than level.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Data-center execution and concentration (dominant risk).** ~$9.5B committed to build CCGT plants and battery storage largely to serve two counterparties (Amazon/AWS and Alphabet). Construction/cost overruns, financing-cost pressure, customer termination or capacity reduction, and MISO capacity-accreditation changes are all live. Recovery for Pool Resource Asset customers is contractual, not traditional rate-base, a departure from the low-risk utility model.
2. **Financing and IG-rating risk.** The capex scale could pressure the balance sheet; a below-IG downgrade triggers ~$158.5M of collateral posting. The higher-for-longer rate path directly raises the cost of a negative-FCF model funded with debt and equity.
3. **Regulatory dependence.** Every major initiative requires IURC/FERC approval. The Amazon approval de-risked the largest item; the Alphabet approval (Q3 2026 order expected) and the R.M. Schahfer cost-recovery litigation at FERC/MISO ($65.9M deferred) remain open.
4. **NIPSCO gas-meter accuracy investigation.** IURC opened an investigation (Nov 2025) into a latent issue with a small percentage of gas-meter indexes; hearing July 2026. A potential reputational/refund issue to monitor.
5. **Labor.** NIPSCO locked out United Steelworkers employees April 2-24, 2026; contracts ratified but with incremental cost, unusual friction for a regulated utility.
6. **Environmental.** CCR (coal ash), MGP (41 sites, $74.3M reserve), and shifting federal GHG policy remain in the background.

**Options Market Signal.** Options positioning has turned defensive. Near-term ATM IV is 28.2% versus 18.4% historical volatility (rich, +9.8 points), with a 6.5% put/call skew, a put/call volume ratio of 2.33, an OI ratio of 2.13, and term structure in backwardation (28.2% near-term vs 23.3% long-term). The P/C OI ratio jumped from 0.00 to 2.13 since the prior snapshot. The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the July gas-meter hearing, the Q3 Alphabet order, and the near-term seasonal-quarter estimate cut. For a HOLD, this argues against chasing at current levels and favors waiting for the vol crush and event resolution before adding.

Short interest is low at 3.1% of float and 2.4 days to cover — no crowded-short dynamic, and no informed bearish consensus.

Risk factors score 5.5: the dominant data-center concentration and financing risks are real and specific, but the largest binary (Amazon approval) resolved favorably, which is why we hold the score at the midpoint rather than the 5.0 of the prior report. This reverses part of the recent downtrend in the risk dimension.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($52-55).** The Alphabet IURC/FERC order clears in Q3 2026, extending the de-risked pipeline; GenCo construction stays on schedule and budget; estimate revisions turn positive as data-center earnings begin flowing in 2027; the utility executes its 6-8% EPS growth while defending IG ratings via Blackstone capital. At ~24x forward EPS on rising estimates, the stock reaches RBC's $52 target and the $55 high, ~14-20% above current.

**Bear Case ($40-42).** A higher-for-longer rate path raises financing costs on a deeply negative-FCF model; the ATM program dilutes per-share growth; the gas-meter investigation produces a refund/reputational hit; construction cost overruns or a MISO accreditation change pressure the data-center returns; and the growth premium compresses toward the peer-median ~20x. The stock drifts to the low-$40s, ~8-12% below current, near the $43 analyst low.

**Base Case ($48-49).** NiSource delivers its ~6-8% EPS algorithm, the Alphabet approval clears without incident, and the multiple holds around 22x forward on $2.25 next-year EPS. A ~$48-49 fair value plus the ~2.6% dividend implies a high-single-digit total return over 12 months, a solid but unremarkable utility outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean). Reassessment horizon: 12 months, keyed to the Q3 2026 Alphabet order and the August earnings print.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Positive estimate-revision velocity — current-year or next-year consensus rising >3% over 90 days as data-center earnings contribution firms.
- Clean Alphabet IURC/FERC approval in Q3 2026 with no adverse cost-recovery conditions.
- A pullback toward the low-$40s (near peer-median ~20x forward and the $43 low target), where the durable compounder turns asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A ratings action toward the IG floor, or a materially adverse financing development that forces a larger-than-guided equity issuance.
- A GenCo construction cost overrun or customer-capacity reduction that impairs the data-center return economics.
- An adverse gas-meter investigation outcome with material refund exposure, or a regulatory reversal on the data-center recovery framework.

**Next natural reassessment:** August 2026 earnings, the July 2026 gas-meter hearing, and the Q3 2026 Alphabet regulatory order.

## Conclusion

NiSource is a well-run regulated multi-utility with a genuine, tailwind-backed growth story in Midwest data-center power, now materially de-risked by the June 23 Amazon approval and structurally insulated for existing ratepayers through the Pool Resource Asset and Blackstone financing model. The score trajectory has declined over the past two reports (6.7 → 6.1 → 5.7), driven by the recognition that the negative-FCF, dilution-dependent capex model is a headwind in a higher-for-longer rate environment. This report holds the overall at 5.85, marginally reversing the downtrend, because the largest regulatory binary resolved favorably and the risk profile improved accordingly — a change in fundamentals, not a reaction to the ~11% price recovery since July 2025.

The stock is fairly-to-slightly-richly priced at ~22x forward with a peer premium and a sub-peer yield, the recurring re-rating catalyst has largely played out, and options positioning signals near-term stress. We maintain HOLD with a constructive lean: own it for durable ~6-8% EPS and dividend compounding, but wait for positive estimate-revision velocity, a clean Alphabet approval, or a pullback toward the low-$40s before treating it as a BUY.
