## Executive Summary

STERIS is a high-quality healthcare equipment and services company with a dominant position in infection prevention, contract sterilization, and life sciences process solutions. The business generates 78% of revenue from recurring sources (services + consumables), operates a near-duopoly in contract sterilization through its AST segment (44.8% operating margins), and has dramatically improved its balance sheet, reducing debt-to-capital from 33.7% to 23.6% in a single fiscal year. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $1.50B grew 9.5% YoY and beat consensus, with all three segments contributing positively.

Despite strong fundamentals, several factors prevent a BUY recommendation at current levels. First, Q3 FY2026 missed EPS consensus by 1.1%, breaking a three-quarter beat streak, and estimate revisions have turned negative across all forecast periods (current quarter -0.9% over 90 days, next quarter -1.7%). Second, the options market is pricing acute near-term stress: 1-week implied volatility of 46.8% vs. 24.4% historical volatility, in steep backwardation, with elevated put demand. Third, EO litigation risk extends beyond the $48.2M Cook County settlement — other jurisdictions and future regulatory action represent an unquantified tail risk to the company's highest-margin segment.

At $214.46, the stock trades at approximately 21x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($10.20 consensus) and 19.4x FY2027E EPS ($11.08). The 30% upside to the median analyst target of $291 is compelling over 12-18 months, but the negative estimate revision trajectory and near-term event risk suggest patience will be rewarded. The thesis upgrades to BUY if estimates stabilize and the options market normalizes.

## Company Overview

STERIS operates through three segments serving the healthcare sterilization and infection prevention ecosystem:

**Healthcare (71% of FY2025 revenue, $3.88B):** Sterile processing equipment, surgical tables, endoscopy accessories, cleaning chemistries, instrument repair, and outsourced reprocessing services. Segment operating margin: 25.0%.

**AST — Applied Sterilization Technologies (19%, $1.04B):** Network of 60+ contract sterilization and laboratory testing facilities offering ethylene oxide, gamma radiation, E-beam, and X-ray sterilization. Segment operating margin: 44.8%.

**Life Sciences (10%, $542M):** Products and services for biopharmaceutical and medical device manufacturing, particularly aseptic environments. Segment operating margin: 42.3%.

The company's strategic moat is its integrated offering: customers using STERIS sterilizers also buy STERIS consumables, STERIS service contracts, and outsource to STERIS's AST facilities. This creates embedded switching costs and predictable revenue streams.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory** has been consistently positive: FY2022 $4.59B → FY2023 $4.96B → FY2024 $5.14B → FY2025 $5.46B. Organic constant-currency growth was 6.2% in FY2025, with the critical revenue mix shifting toward recurring streams (services +9%, consumables +12%) while capital equipment declined 6%. FY2026 guidance calls for 8-9% revenue growth and 7-8% organic CER growth.

**Profitability is expanding.** Gross margins improved 80bps YoY to 44.0%. Healthcare segment margins expanded from 24.1% to 25.0%. Life Sciences margins jumped from 38.7% to 42.3%. AST margins compressed slightly (46.1% to 44.8%), likely reflecting investment in alternative sterilization modalities.

**Cash flow generation is strong and improving.** FY2025 operating cash flow: $1.15B (+18% YoY). Free cash flow: $787M (14.3% FCF margin). This supports the 1.19% dividend yield (19 consecutive increases), $200M in buybacks, and significant debt reduction — all while maintaining $370M in capex (6.8% of revenue, supporting AST capacity expansion).

**Balance sheet transformation.** Total debt fell from $3.21B to $2.04B in one year — a 36% reduction. Debt-to-capital dropped from 33.7% to 23.6%. Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 1.5x. The $1.05B unused revolving credit facility provides substantial optionality for M&A or accelerated returns. Investment-grade ratings (BBB/Baa2) are well-supported.

**Backlog of $452.9M** (up 6.5% YoY) indicates continued demand visibility, though this is weighted toward the more cyclical capital equipment business.

## Growth Analysis

STERIS's growth is driven by several secular tailwinds: aging populations requiring more medical procedures (particularly colonoscopies and endoscopies), increasing complexity of surgical instruments requiring sophisticated reprocessing, outsourcing trends from hospitals preferring to contract sterile processing rather than manage it internally, and expanding biopharmaceutical manufacturing requiring contamination control.

FY2026 guidance of $10.15-$10.30 adjusted EPS implies approximately 12-14% growth over FY2025 levels. Consensus for FY2027 at $11.08 implies a further 8.6% growth — a reasonable medium-term trajectory for this type of business.

However, estimate revisions have turned negative. Current quarter estimates have fallen 0.9% over 90 days; next quarter estimates have declined 1.7%. Full-year FY2026 estimates are essentially flat, while FY2027 estimates have drifted 0.5% lower. This is modest deterioration, but it contrasts with the beat-and-raise cadence investors expect from a compounder. The Q3 FY2026 EPS miss of 1.1% may signal margin pressure from tariff-related input cost inflation or slower capital equipment recovery.

The restructuring of the European surgical business (eliminating ~300 positions, impairment of an internally developed X-ray accelerator) should contribute ~$25M in annual savings beginning FY2026. This provides a modest earnings tailwind but also signals that not all R&D bets have paid off — the X-ray accelerator impairment specifically suggests challenges in diversifying away from EO sterilization technology.

## Valuation Assessment

At $214.46, STERIS trades at:
- 21.0x FY2026E adjusted EPS ($10.20)
- 19.4x FY2027E adjusted EPS ($11.08)
- ~29.7x trailing GAAP EPS ($6.16, which includes non-recurring items)
- Forward P/E of ~29.7x per vendor data

Against peer medians: the Health Care Equipment peer group trades at a median 27.5x P/E. STERIS at ~29.7x forward P/E (per vendor data, which may reflect trailing rather than adjusted forward earnings) is trading roughly in line with this peer median. On an adjusted forward earnings basis (~21x), the stock appears cheaper than Hologic (31.3x), Penumbra (71.9x), and Dexcom (27.5x). Only GE HealthCare (13.1x) and Zimmer Biomet (20.8x) are cheaper, but GE HealthCare carries higher revenue cyclicality and Zimmer faces orthopedic market share concerns.

The analyst consensus target of $279 (mean) / $291 (median) implies 30-36% upside from current levels. The low target of $231 is 8% above the current price, suggesting even the most bearish analyst views the stock as undervalued.

However, there is a specific reason for the discount: the Q3 earnings miss and declining estimates. With negative earnings momentum, the market has appropriately derated STERIS from its January 2026 highs near $269. The valuation is not stretched at 21x forward adjusted earnings for a business growing 8-9% with 14% FCF margins — but it's also not cheap enough to provide a strong margin of safety against further estimate cuts.

The stock has fallen 20% from its 52-week high of $269.44 to the current $214.46. Given that fundamentals remain broadly intact (revenue still growing 9.5%, guidance maintained), this represents genuine valuation compression. The key uncertainty is whether estimates stabilize or continue to drift lower.

## Competitive Landscape

STERIS occupies a structurally advantaged position across all three segments:

**Healthcare:** The installed base of sterilizers, washers, and surgical tables creates ongoing consumable and service revenue. Hospital procurement cycles are long, switching costs are high (training, validation, integration with existing workflows), and the move toward outsourcing sterile processing further embeds STERIS into customer operations. Competitors include Getinge, Stryker (surgical tables), and Olympus (endoscopy), but STERIS's breadth across the entire sterile processing workflow is unmatched.

**AST:** This is effectively a duopoly with Sotera Health (Sterigenics). The barriers to entry are extraordinary: building new sterilization facilities requires regulatory permits that are increasingly difficult to obtain (particularly for EO), customer qualification processes that take 12-18 months, and capital intensity. Existing operators benefit from grandfathered regulatory positions. EO risk simultaneously threatens existing operations AND prevents new competition — existing players with permits have a moat that cannot be replicated.

**Life Sciences:** The aseptic manufacturing segment benefits from biologics growth and cell/gene therapy expansion, both requiring validated contamination control solutions. Customer qualification processes in GMP environments are rigorous, creating high switching costs.

Revenue concentration risk is low: no single customer exceeds 10% of any segment's revenue. The patent portfolio (607 U.S., 2,315 international) protects specific product innovations, though the broader competitive moat derives from operational execution and customer relationships rather than IP alone.

## Risk Assessment

**EO Litigation & Regulatory Risk (HIGH):** The $48.2M Cook County settlement resolves claims in one jurisdiction but explicitly does not eliminate risk from other jurisdictions or future regulatory action. The EPA's 2016 carcinogenicity determination remains operative, and community opposition to EO facilities continues in multiple states. A restrictive EPA rule on EO emissions could reduce AST capacity utilization or require significant capital investment in emissions controls. This risk is partially mitigated by STERIS's investment in E-beam and X-ray alternatives, but these technologies cannot sterilize all device types. AST represents 19% of revenue but contributes disproportionately to profitability given its 44.8% margins.

**Tariff & Trade Policy Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** STERIS's FY2025 10-K explicitly flagged tariff changes as a potential inflation driver. The company purchases stainless steel, electronics, chemicals, and plastics — all exposed to current tariff regimes on various countries. The Irish domicile adds complexity: legislation targeting non-U.S. domiciled companies for U.S. government healthcare contracts could create an existential issue for the government customer base.

**Tax Structure Risk (LOW-MODERATE):** Section 7874 reclassification or OECD Pillar Two implementation could reduce the tax advantage of the Irish structure. The effective rate of 23.2% already suggests limited arbitrage remaining, but any upward revision would directly impact EPS.

**Goodwill Concentration (MODERATE):** $5.9B in goodwill and intangibles on a $10.1B balance sheet (58%) reflects acquisitive history. Impairment risk exists if any segment underperforms expectations materially.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility of 46.8% is nearly double the 24.4% one-year historical volatility — a 22.4pp premium. The term structure is in steep backwardation (46.8% 1-week vs. 26.0% 6-month), indicating the market expects a specific near-term event to resolve. Put demand is elevated (8.1% skew). This could reflect positioning ahead of the next earnings report or concern about a specific risk catalyst (tariff impact, EO regulatory development, or further estimate cuts). The implied ±6.2% move for the nearest expiry suggests the possibility of a move to $201 on the downside or $228 on the upside within days.

This options positioning directly supports a cautious stance: even if the fundamental thesis is constructive over 12 months, entering during a period of acute implied stress creates unnecessary near-term downside risk.

## Conclusion

STERIS is a well-managed, structurally advantaged business with strong recurring revenue, expanding margins, and a dramatically improved balance sheet. At 21x forward adjusted earnings versus a 27.5x peer median, the stock appears reasonably discounted — reflecting legitimate near-term concerns around estimate revisions, tariff exposure, and EO litigation tail risk rather than fundamental impairment.

The specific factors preventing a BUY recommendation today are: (1) negative estimate revision trajectory with no catalyst for stabilization identified before next earnings; (2) the options market pricing a specific near-term event with IV nearly 2x historical in backwardation; and (3) the recent earnings miss breaking the pattern of consistent beats that would support confidence in forward estimates. These are measurable, time-bound concerns that should resolve within 1-2 quarters, making this a growth-hold with a clear path to upgrade.
