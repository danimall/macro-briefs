**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ameren is a pure-play regulated electric and gas utility holding company serving monopoly territories in Missouri and Illinois, with essentially no merchant/unregulated generation exposure. FY2025 was a strong year operationally: net income to common rose 23% to $1,456M and diluted EPS climbed $0.93 to $5.35, driven by an April 2025 Missouri electric base-rate order (+$355M revenue requirement), a $0.32/share excess-deferred-tax benefit, and favorable weather. The company is entering a multi-year rate-base supercycle: a $30.5–33.1B 2026–2030 capex plan, since framed as a ~$70B pipeline through 2035, anchored by Missouri data-center/large-load demand (2.2 GW of executed service agreements, 3.4 GW of construction agreements) and a competitive transmission buildout earning FERC formula rates at a 10.48% ROE.

The business is sound, but the price already reflects most of the good news. At $108.92 the stock trades at ~18.8x forward and ~19.6x trailing earnings, below the utility peer median P/E of 24.0, but that comparison is distorted by a low-multiple outlier (EIX at 7.9x) and does not adjust for Ameren's heavier equity-dilution load. The stock has appreciated 18.7% over the trailing twelve months and sits within 6% of its 52-week high. Near-term estimates are drifting down (current quarter −3.1% over 90 days), the dividend yield is modest (~2.75% on the new $3.00 annual rate), and there is no open-market insider buying. Funding the capex program requires ~$4B of equity issuance 2026–2030 against structurally negative free cash flow (−$775M in 2025), a real and ongoing dilution headwind.

The recommendation is HOLD with a growth orientation. The durable thesis of mid-single-digit EPS and dividend growth driven by rate-base expansion is intact and worth owning for income-and-growth investors. But the entry point is not asymmetric: the bull case and the bear case both run through the same capex program, and at the current multiple the reward-to-risk does not clear the bar for a BUY. I would add on a pullback toward the high-$90s/low-$100s or on confirmation that the Missouri large-load tariff translates into approved CCNs without affordability backlash.

## Company Overview

Ameren operates four reportable segments:

- **Ameren Missouri** — vertically integrated regulated electric (generation, transmission, distribution) plus gas distribution. The growth engine: 2025 net income to common of $747M (~51% of total) and the largest share of the capex plan.
- **Ameren Transmission** (Ameren Illinois Transmission + ATXI) — $415M net income, FERC formula rates at a 10.48% allowed ROE (includes a 50bp RTO incentive adder).
- **Ameren Illinois Electric Distribution** — $281M net income, operating under a CEJA Multi-Year Rate Plan with an 8.72% allowed ROE.
- **Ameren Illinois Natural Gas** — $158M net income.

The competitive moat is the classic regulated-utility structure: monopoly service territories, constructive and recently improving regulatory frameworks (PISA, RESRAM, FAC trackers in Missouri; MYRP and formula rates in Illinois; FERC formula rates for transmission), and a low cost of capital (beta 0.49, investment-grade ratings). What distinguishes Ameren from peers is the rate-base growth visibility tied to data-center demand in the Missouri territory, where the vertically integrated model lets the company earn a return on generation rather than only on wires.

| Net income ($M) | 1,075 | 1,152 | 1,184 | 1,487 |

The headline 17% revenue jump in 2025 substantially overstates underlying growth. Roughly $452M of the Missouri revenue increase is a pass-through wash: the MISO summer capacity auction price spiked from $30/MW-day (2024) to $667/MW-day (2025), inflating both off-system sales revenue and fuel/purchased-power expense with limited net earnings impact (Missouri bears only 5% of FAC variance). Normalized for this, the real earnings drivers were the +$249M Missouri electric base-rate increase, the $0.32/share excess-deferred-tax revaluation, +$0.22/share from weather/volume, and the absence of the 2024 Rush Island litigation charge (+$0.17/share). Investors should treat 2025 EPS as flattered by one-time items.

Margins are healthy and consistent with a well-run regulated utility: operating margin ~23%, net margin ~17%, ROE ~11.1%. The earnings-quality signals are positive: operating cash flow of $3,353M exceeded net income, and the Piotroski signals point to strengthening fundamentals.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Ameren is capital-intensive and leveraged by design: D/E ~3.6x, total debt ~$18.9B, equity $13.4B, net PP&E $39.3B. The Altman Z-Score of 0.84 ("distress zone") and the 0.66 current ratio are artifacts of the regulated-utility model: a large regulated asset base, low asset turnover (~0.18), and heavy debt with regulator-supported recovery. Credit ratings are solidly investment grade (Moody's Baa1 / S&P BBB+; Ameren Missouri secured debt A2/A), with $2.5B of available liquidity under credit agreements now extended to December 2030 ($3.2B capacity). The capital structure should not be mistaken for credit risk.

Free cash flow was −$775M in 2025, structural and intentional. Capex of $4,128M exceeded operating cash flow of $3,353M, and management states explicitly that cash needs (capex plus dividends) will exceed operating cash flow for several years, funded by debt and equity. Interest charges rose $113M to $776M in 2025 on higher rates and balances, a number that will keep climbing as debt-funded capex flows through.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on rate base, and the visibility is genuinely strong. The 2026–2030 plan supports a multi-year rate-base growth trajectory (Ameren Missouri alone $20.4–22.2B), which utilities typically convert to comparable EPS growth. The demand driver is concrete: Missouri executed its large-load service agreements in February 2026 under a new MoPSC-approved large-load tariff (75+ MW customers, 12-year terms plus ramp, 80% minimum demand charges, exit fees, collateral requirements). The 2025 Preferred Resource Plan assumes load growth of 1.5 GW by 2032 and 2.5 GW by 2040.

The transmission segment offers the highest-return growth: MISO long-range transmission plan Tranche 1 (~$1.8B to Ameren) begins construction spring 2026, Tranche 2 (~$1.3B assigned) has FERC CWIP incentives, and $4.4B of competitive bids remain open through 2026. At a 10.48% FERC formula ROE, this is the most attractive incremental capital deployment in the portfolio.

Recently improved regulatory mechanics support the cash economics of the plan. Missouri's PPRA (Aug 2025) allows CWIP in rate base for new gas generation and future test years for gas, improving recovery timeliness; Illinois CRGA (Jan 2026) raises energy-efficiency spending caps. Recent rate wins are stacking up: July 2025 Missouri gas (+$32M), November 2025 Illinois gas (+$79M), and Big Hollow gas (800 MW) plus battery (400 MW) CCN approvals (Feb 2026).

The countervailing signal is near-term estimate momentum. Current-year EPS is flat-to-down. Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $5.25–$5.45 on the Q1 print, and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters, so the near-term softness looks like cost-timing noise rather than a thesis crack. But it does not meet the threshold of accelerating positive revisions. Growth here is durable and visible rather than accelerating: a mid-single-digit compounder, not a re-rating story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $108.92, AEE trades at 18.75x forward and 19.59x trailing earnings. The utility peer median P/E of 24.0 makes Ameren look cheap on the surface, but that median is heavily influenced by composition: DTE 24.0x, CNP 26.1x, FE 25.4x, ATO 20.5x, and EIX an outlier at 7.9x (reflecting wildfire-liability discount). Against the cleaner-comparable, higher-quality names trading 20–26x, Ameren's ~18.8x forward is a modest discount, partly explained by its larger equity-dilution requirement and its tightened 2.25% PISA recovery cap.

Two considerations keep me from rewarding cheapness here. First, the stock trades within 6% of its 52-week high after its twelve-month move; the easy re-rating has happened. Second, the equity-issuance program (with 6.4M forward shares to settle in 2026, shares outstanding up from 258.4M in 2022 to 270.5M in 2025) is real ongoing dilution that caps per-share growth and offsets part of the rate-base growth. The MD&A flags an $0.08/share dilution drag in 2025 alone.

On dividend support, the new $3.00 annual rate yields ~2.75%, below the peer median of 3.2% and below most quality peers. That yield offers downside support but is not a compelling absolute-value anchor.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $105, mean $119.87, median $121, high $136. The median implies ~11% upside, a reasonable 12-month total-return setup (price plus dividend) for a utility but not asymmetric. With near-term estimates drifting down rather than up, the conditions under which analyst targets act as "floors" (rising estimates, cheap-to-growth multiple) are not present. A fair value in the high-$110s is consistent with mid-single-digit EPS growth on a ~20x multiple, roughly where the stock should be rather than meaningfully below it.

## Competitive Landscape

Ameren occupies the most favored sub-segment of regulated utilities: Midwest operators with available power capacity, constructive regulation, and data-center load tailwinds. The MISO capacity price surge signals a tightening regional supply-demand balance, which favors utilities that can add rate-based generation. Ameren is unusually well-positioned because Missouri's vertically integrated model lets it earn on generation, and recent state legislation (PPRA) improved generation cost-recovery economics.

The competitive position is stable-to-improving rather than rapidly strengthening. The data-center demand is a genuine multi-year catalyst, and the large-load tariff is well-structured to protect other ratepayers (12-year terms, 80% minimum demand, exit fees, collateral, plus an earnings-sharing mechanism that defers 65% of ROE above 9.74% to customers). That sharing mechanism caps the upside: above a 9.74% ROE, 65% of incremental returns flow back to customers, so the data-center boom raises rate base more than it raises returns. The transmission franchise is the clearest source of moat extension, with the caveat that a pending FERC rulemaking could eliminate the 50bp RTO incentive adder (~$19M annual net-income headwind).

## Risk Assessment

**Financing and dilution.** The capex plan against structurally negative FCF requires sustained equity issuance. This makes Ameren sensitive to its own share price and to interest rates, and it is the single most underappreciated headwind to per-share growth.

**Regulatory recovery caps.** Missouri's PISA mechanism carries an annual revenue-requirement increase cap, recently lowered from 2.5% to 2.25% (PPRA, Aug 2025). Given accelerating capex, incremental PISA deferrals could exceed the cap and be permanently excluded from recovery. Illinois MYRP carries a 105% reconciliation cap above which costs are not recovered.

**Illinois ROE litigation.** Ameren Illinois has appealed multiple ICC orders to the Illinois Appellate Court over the 2024–2027 electric distribution ROE (a low 8.72%), exclusion of an OPEB-related asset from rate base, and a $75M cut to planned gas capital. Outcomes are uncertain and open-ended in timing, and the Illinois segments are ~30% of earnings.

**Contingent large-load demand.** Ameren added a risk factor noting forecasted data-center loads may not materialize; customers can terminate or reduce capacity, and exit fees "may not fully mitigate" under-recovery. Affordability/political friction is a forward risk as rates rise to fund the buildout.

**Generation-transition execution.** Plans include coal retirement by 2042, 1,600 MW gas by 2030, 3,200 MW renewables by 2030, 1,000 MW storage by 2030, and 1,500 MW new nuclear by 2040, all dependent on CCN approvals, supply chain, and tax credits altered by the OBBBA. Concentration risks compound this: a single nuclear unit (Callaway) and ~96% of Missouri coal from the Powder River Basin.

**Leadership transition (open thread).** An unusual cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks (Oct 2025–Feb 2026), a new finance voice on the Q1 2026 call, and new subsidiary officer grants suggest a senior management refresh that could not be fully confirmed. Worth verifying before relying on continuity assumptions.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Missouri data-center supercycle drives rate base higher than currently modeled; the large-load tariff and PPRA cash-recovery improvements support timely earnings recognition; transmission Tranches 1 and 2 plus open competitive bids add high-ROE growth; and EPS compounds at a healthy mid-single-digit-or-better pace. A constructive regulatory record and turning-positive analyst sentiment (JPMorgan to Overweight at $126, Truist Buy initiation) carry the multiple toward the peer group. Target ~$130–136.

**Bear case.** The capex program forces dilutive equity issuance at a faster pace than rate base monetizes; the 2.25% PISA cap and Illinois ROE adverse rulings clip recovery; large-load demand under-delivers or triggers affordability pushback; and rising interest expense compresses the spread on debt-funded growth. The multiple de-rates toward the mid-teens and the stock drifts to ~$95–100.

The asymmetry is balanced rather than favorable: both cases pivot on the same capex plan, and at ~18.8x forward the market is paying a fair price for a known compounder. The conditions that would justify an aggressive long — a cheap-to-growth multiple, rising estimates, or insider conviction buying — are absent.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on a growth basis: a durable mid-single-digit EPS and dividend compounder worth owning for income-and-growth mandates, but without the entry asymmetry for a BUY at the current price.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Price pulls back toward the high-$90s/low-$100s (~18x forward implies ~$97 on $5.37 FY26 EPS), restoring a clear discount-to-growth entry.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year and next-quarter consensus rising rather than falling) alongside reaffirmed or raised FY2026/FY2027 guidance.
- Missouri large-load agreements convert into approved generation CCNs without affordability/regulatory backlash, de-risking the rate-base step-up.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse Illinois Appellate Court rulings that materially cut the electric-distribution ROE below 8.72% or deny OPEB rate-base treatment.
- A credit-rating downgrade or a meaningfully larger-than-planned equity issuance that signals financing stress.
- Cancellation or material reduction of executed large-load agreements, or a PISA-cap breach that permanently excludes recovery of incremental capital.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings report (FY guidance confirmation and capex-plan updates), Illinois Appellate Court rulings (no fixed deadline), and the FERC RTO-adder rulemaking outcome.

## Conclusion

Ameren is a financially sound, investment-grade regulated utility entering a multi-year rate-base supercycle driven by Missouri data-center demand and transmission buildout, supported by recently improved regulatory frameworks. The fundamentals are strengthening and the dividend is well-covered. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: at the current multiple, within 6% of its 52-week high, near-term consensus estimates are drifting down, the equity-issuance program through 2030 imposes ongoing per-share dilution against negative free cash flow, and there is no open-market insider buying or compressed-to-growth multiple to mark a mispricing. The median analyst target implies ~11% upside, a respectable 12-month total return for a utility but not the asymmetry that warrants a BUY. The case improves materially on a pullback toward the high-$90s/low-$100s or on confirmation that the large-load pipeline converts to approved, recoverable rate base.
