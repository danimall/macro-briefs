**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PPG emerged from its 2024 portfolio reshaping as a pure-play global coatings company, having divested the U.S./Canada architectural business and the silicas products line, and exited Russia and Argentina. The result is a cleaner business with ~70% of revenue generated outside the United States, a strong balance sheet, and a 126-year uninterrupted dividend record (54 consecutive annual increases). The franchise quality in aerospace coatings, automotive refinish, and protective/marine is real and, in aerospace specifically, structurally advantaged. None of this is in dispute.

What is in dispute is whether the equity has a catalyst. The reported 41% jump in 2025 net income to $1.576B is largely the absence of 2024's one-time charges ($239M restructuring, $146M Russia impairment, $110M Argentina FX loss); the cleaner read is that adjusted EPS declined ~4% to $7.58 and aggregate segment income fell ~3%. Q1 2026 revenue grew 6.7% to $3.93B, but roughly six of those seven points came from FX, with volumes flat-to-down and Industrial Coatings segment income falling 10%. Management's 2026 organic guide is flat to low-single-digit positive. This is a self-help-and-pricing story driven by margin actions rather than end-market demand.

The central tension is captured in the proxy: PPG's five-year cumulative TSR sits at ~78 against a peer-group TSR of ~129, a wide and persistent lag that explains why the stock trades at a discount to specialty-chemical peers despite its quality. The 2026 swing factor is a price initiative: up-to-20% list increases customer-by-customer to recover mid-single-digit COGS inflation tied to the Middle East energy shock. If the price sticks, it is a margin tailwind; if it triggers volume loss or share shift, it is a risk. We rate PPG a HOLD on income, with the ~2.4% yield and balance-sheet durability anchoring the position while we wait for evidence the transformation converts to earnings growth.

## Company Overview

PPG operates three reportable segments across nine operating units:

- **Industrial Coatings (~$6.5B, ~41% of sales)** — the largest segment, comprising automotive OEM coatings, general industrial coatings, packaging (beverage/food can) coatings, and specialty products (folded into this segment in Q1 2026). Lower-margin, more cyclical, exposed to global industrial production and index-based contract pricing.
- **Performance Coatings (~$5.5B, ~35%)** — the highest-margin segment (~21%) and the growth engine: automotive refinish, aerospace coatings, protective & marine, and traffic solutions.
- **Global Architectural Coatings (~$3.8B, ~24%)** — now an ex-North America business (EMEA, Latin America, Asia Pacific) following the U.S./Canada divestiture. Mexico is a key growth market; EMEA demand remains soft.

The structural consequence of the 2024 reshaping is that the U.S. dollar's direction is now a primary earnings driver, with heavy euro and Mexican peso exposure. PPG uses ~$4.0B of euro-denominated debt as a net-investment hedge. The competitive moats are concentrated in technology-advantaged products: the aerospace transparencies/sealants franchise (a roughly century-old position) and digital tools in auto refinish (PPG LINQ, Moonwalk).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Reported revenue moved $17.65B (2022) to $18.25B (2023) to $15.85B (2024) to $15.88B (2025). The 2023-to-2024 drop of ~$2.4B is almost entirely divestiture-driven, not organic deterioration; underlying 2025 organic sales grew ~2%.

**Profitability.** The YoY net income jump flatters reality because 2024 absorbed heavy one-time charges. GAAP continuing-ops EPS was $6.92 versus $5.72. Aggregate segment margins fell ~60 bps YoY on weak Mexican architectural spending in H1 and auto refinish volume declines in H2. Net margin is ~10%; cost of sales runs ~58.7% of revenue.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** 2025 operating cash flow was $1.94B, up 39% YoY (aided by lower incentive-comp payouts and working-capital timing). Capex was $778M (4.9% of sales), guided down to $650–700M in 2026, a free-cash-flow tailwind. PPG paid $628M in dividends and repurchased $790M in stock in 2025, with ~$2.0B of authorization remaining. At a $26.4B market cap, FCF after capex of roughly $1.16B implies a ~4.4% FCF yield, adequate but not compelling.

**Balance sheet.** 2025 total assets were $22.1B, equity $7.9B, long-term debt $6.6B plus $0.7B short-term. Total Indebtedness/Total Capitalization of 45–47% sits comfortably below the 60% covenant; current ratio ~1.6; no revolver drawn. Goodwill ($6.1B) plus intangibles (~$2.0B) total ~$8.1B, exceeding total equity, a legacy of 50+ acquisitions over a decade and a source of impairment exposure if reporting-unit cash flows weaken. The balance sheet is investment-grade and sound. (The automated Altman Z of 75 and ROA of 390% are mis-scaled artifacts and should be disregarded.)

**Q1 2026.** Net sales rose 6.7% to $3.93B, but ~6 points came from FX and ~1 from price, with volumes flat-to-down. Adjusted EPS was $1.83 (+6.4%). Architectural and Performance segment income rose; Industrial Coatings income fell 10% on lower index-based pricing and unfavorable China auto mix. Operating cash was only $33M, a seasonal working-capital build.

**Earnings track record.** Four trailing quarters show two beats and two misses (Q1 2026 +2.4%, Q4 2025 -5.5%, Q3 2025 +0.9%, Q2 2025 -1.4%). Visibility is mediocre; surprises cluster within ±5.5%, with no consistent conservatism to bank on.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is thin on an organic basis. The quality of recent reported growth is weak once currency is stripped out. Estimate revisions confirm the stall: current-year consensus EPS slipped to $7.87 from $8.00 ninety days ago (-1.6%), next-quarter estimates fell 4.7%, and next-year sits at $8.67 (essentially flat). Revision velocity is negative, the opposite of the +30% signal that would warrant aggressive re-rating.

Three identifiable growth levers exist:

1. **Aerospace coatings** is the genuine structural story: double-digit organic growth, a ~$315M backlog, demand outstripping capacity, with PPG investing in debottlenecking and greenfield expansion against ~5% forecast passenger-traffic growth in 2026. This is the most durable piece of the portfolio.
2. **The cost program** (announced Oct 2024) targets ~$175M annualized pre-tax savings; ~$75M was realized in 2025, with ~$50–60M incremental guided for 2026.
3. **The price initiative** is the single most important 2026 lever and is binary on execution: if it holds, margins expand; if it costs volume, it offsets itself.

The combination supports EPS growth toward the $8.67 next-year consensus, but that is a self-help glide path, not a demand inflection. Set against a Materials sector that has shown a structural alpha deficit and a European/industrial demand backdrop that remains soft, growth prospects score below average.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$117–118, PPG trades at ~15x current-year EPS and ~13.6x next-year, with a ~2.4% dividend yield. Against the Specialty Chemicals peer set (median P/E 24.0, median yield 2.1%), PPG screens cheap on the multiple. But the peer median is distorted: LYB and ALB carry negative trailing P/Es, DD sits at 147.7x on depressed earnings, and the only clean comparable, RPM, trades at 20.7x. PPG's discount to RPM is real but defensible: RPM delivers more consistent organic growth, while PPG carries a persistent TSR lag and a flat earnings trajectory.

A low multiple here is not a buy signal on its own. The compressed P/E reflects the market's accurate read that underlying earnings are not growing and that the transformation has not yet converted into shareholder returns. For the valuation to re-rate, the price initiative needs to stick and aerospace needs to keep compounding, both plausible, neither yet proven in the numbers. The analyst consensus targets bracket this neatly: low $108, mean $123, median $125, high $140. At ~15x with flat estimates, the targets are fair-value markers rather than floors; there is no rising-estimate dynamic to make consensus stale.

We score valuation modestly below neutral. The yield and FCF provide downside support, but absent a catalyst, "cheap" here describes a stock the market has priced correctly for its growth, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

PPG competes in a consolidated global coatings market against Sherwin-Williams, Akzo Nobel, Axalta, and RPM. Its strongest positions:

- **Aerospace coatings** — structurally advantaged, demand-constrained, double-digit growth. The clearest moat in the portfolio.
- **Protective & marine and packaging coatings** — taking share, with packaging aided by expanding European regulation favoring PPG chemistries.
- **Auto refinish** — defending share via the bundled digital/equipment model (LINQ, Moonwalk), but facing a secular headwind from declining U.S. collision-claim frequency as ADAS and safer vehicles reduce accident rates.

The trajectory is mixed. Aerospace and protective/marine are improving; auto refinish faces structural claim-frequency erosion; auto OEM is squeezed by index-based contract pricing and China regional mix. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving in the high-value niches and stable-to-eroding in the cyclical ones. The prior score of 7.0 was generous given the refinish and OEM headwinds; we mark it down to 6.5, reflecting that the franchise quality is genuine but the trajectory is not uniformly positive.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Middle East cost shock and the price gamble (near-term swing factor).** The ability to hold price without volume loss determines 2026 margins. A resolution of the energy disruption would partially reverse the cost pressure; a prolonged disruption sustains it.

2. **~70% international exposure / FX.** Post-divestiture, dollar direction is a primary earnings driver. Currency can swing the headline materially in either direction. Heavy euro and peso exposure.

3. **Di Gregrio (Brazil) contingency — a genuine tail risk.** A Brazilian court judgment against PPG (nominal defendant) could total ~$350–700M with interest and fees. Westlake/Axiall contractually assumed this liability in the 2013 chemicals spin and owes uncapped indemnification, but Westlake has refused to post a bond or pay, and PPG is litigating to enforce. PPG calls loss "remote" and expects a Delaware Chancery ruling in 2026. The magnitude relative to ~$1.6B annual net income makes the indemnity-enforcement outcome worth tracking closely.

4. **Goodwill/intangibles concentration.** ~$8.1B versus $7.9B equity. Acquisition-heavy history creates impairment exposure if reporting-unit cash flows weaken, a risk amplified by soft European/industrial demand.

5. **Environmental.** ~$195M reserves plus reasonably-possible unreserved exposure of $100–200M (New Jersey Chrome, glass/chemical legacy sites); asbestos reserves $43M.

6. **End-market structural risks.** Auto refinish collision-frequency decline; auto OEM EV transition and price-index compression; soft architectural EMEA.

7. **Executive turnover.** A cluster of five-plus Item 5.02 8-Ks since August 2025 signals leadership-team movement during the restructuring. The specific causes could not be confirmed from primary filings (archive pages were access-blocked), leaving open whether this reflects routine post-divestiture reorganization or instability.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 35.2% runs rich to 1-year HV of 29.0% (a 6.2pp spread), with elevated put skew of 10.5% on the front month; the market is paying up for downside protection into 2026. The term structure is flat (1m 35.2% ≈ 6m 34.9%), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a single catalyst. Put/call volume and OI ratios are extremely low (0.04 and 0.01), indicating thin two-sided positioning rather than directional conviction. The options-delta feed shows a flagged ATM IV jump to 169.7%, which we treat as a thin-options data artifact and disregard. Net read: modest elevated downside hedging consistent with the macro energy/COGS overhang, not a strong directional signal either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The price initiative sticks, recovering mid-single-digit COGS inflation and expanding margins; aerospace keeps compounding double-digit; the cost program delivers its incremental $50–60M; FX stays a tailwind; and EPS climbs toward $8.67+ next year. Capex steps down to $650–700M, lifting FCF, and the ~$2.0B buyback authorization plus the dividend record provide a steady floor. The TSR gap begins to close as the market rewards the completed transformation. At 17x $8.67, the stock reaches ~$147, roughly the analyst high of $140 plus a year of compounding. Bull target ~$140–150.

**Bear case.** The 20% price push triggers volume loss and share shift to Sherwin-Williams/Akzo/Axalta; the energy shock sustains COGS inflation without full recovery; FX reverses to a headwind; European/industrial demand stays weak; and a goodwill impairment or an adverse Di Gregrio/Westlake indemnity outcome surfaces. Adjusted EPS slips again, estimates keep drifting lower, and the multiple compresses to ~13x trough earnings near $7.50, reaching ~$98. Bear target ~$98–100, roughly -16% from current.

**Base case.** Pricing partially holds, aerospace carries the mix, cost savings land, and EPS grinds to ~$8.20–8.50, with the multiple roughly stable at 14–15x. The yield and buyback cushion total return. Base target ~$125, in line with consensus median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** The thesis rests on the ~2.4% yield, FCF durability, and balance-sheet quality, with self-help optionality, rather than a clear appreciation catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive organic volume growth alongside evidence the 20% price initiative is holding without share loss (segment income growth ex-FX).
- Current-year EPS estimates turning positive and revising upward (+5% or more over 90 days), confirming the cost/pricing program is converting to earnings.
- A favorable Delaware Chancery ruling on the Westlake indemnity that removes the Di Gregrio tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence the price increases are costing material volume (segment volume declines accelerating below -3% YoY) or a guidance cut to flat/negative organic for 2026.
- A goodwill impairment charge or an adverse Di Gregrio/Westlake outcome that PPG cannot offset via indemnity.
- Continued executive departures suggesting instability rather than reorganization.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which will show the first full quarter of price-initiative traction and the FX/volume split. The Delaware Chancery decision (expected 2026) and any resolution of the energy disruption (which would relieve the COGS overhang) are the other dated catalysts.

## Conclusion

PPG is a high-quality, financially sound coatings franchise trading at a defensible discount to peers, with a 126-year dividend record and a genuine structural growth engine in aerospace. Its underlying earnings are flat-to-down, its recent reported growth is largely currency, and its five-year TSR lags peers by roughly 50 points, a gap that explains the multiple rather than contradicting it. The 2026 story hinges on a 20% price initiative whose outcome is binary and not yet evident in the numbers.

We see neither a compelling entry nor a reason to exit. The yield, FCF, and buyback provide downside support; the absence of organic growth and rising-estimate momentum caps the upside. We rate PPG a HOLD on income, with a base-case fair value of ~$125 (consensus median), upside to ~$140–150 if pricing and aerospace deliver, and downside to ~$98 if the price push costs volume or the Di Gregrio tail materializes. Reassess after Q2 earnings for the first real read on whether the transformation converts to earnings.
