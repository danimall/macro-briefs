**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Interparfums is a specialist licensed-fragrance house that designs, produces (through third-party fillers), and distributes prestige perfumes under long-dated licenses from luxury fashion and jewelry houses: Jimmy Choo, Coach/Tapestry, Montblanc/Richemont, GUESS, Lacoste, DKNY, Ferragamo, Van Cleef & Arpels, and others. It is an asset-light, cash-generative operator with a strong long-run track record (5-year TSR of 197 versus a peer group at 147) and a founder-controlled governance structure that has historically aligned management with shareholders. The screener label of "Consumer Staples / Household Products" is a misclassification; the correct frame is discretionary prestige beauty, which carries more geopolitical and channel cyclicality than a staples tag implies, though the fragrance category itself has been one of beauty's more durable pockets.

The central issue is that 2026 is, by management's own framing, a transition and investment year. Consolidated revenue growth decelerated from +10% in 2024 to +2% in 2025, and H1 2026 was again +2% reported — of which roughly +3 points was FX, leaving organic growth near +1% (about +4% in Q2 ex-Middle East). Operating margin has compressed for three consecutive years (19.1% → 18.9% → 18.2%) as SG&A rises ahead of a slate of 2027-2028 launches (Longchamp, Off-White, David Beckham, Nautica) that are not yet proven. The dividend was held flat for 2026 after a 2025 raise, a signal of caution. The stock has round-tripped: from ~$134 in mid-2025 down to a $77.21 52-week low in November, then back to ~$120, a recovery of roughly 55% off the low that has restored the multiple to ~23x forward EPS, above the household-products peer median but broadly consistent with the prestige-beauty cohort.

We rate IPAR a HOLD. The business quality and balance sheet are genuine, and the prestige-fragrance category remains healthy (US operations grew +18% in Q2). But the near-term earnings picture is muted, estimate revisions are flat, valuation offers no discount to fair value, and two governance flags — an unremediated material weakness at year-end 2025 and an auditor change filed in May 2026 — raise the reporting-quality bar. The bull case rests entirely on the unproven 2027-2028 pipeline. Own the stock only if you are underwriting management's launch cadence, which the current data does not yet confirm.

## Company Overview

Interparfums, founded in 1982, is one of the few pure-play licensed fragrance operators. Its economic engine is licensing brand equity: it pays royalties to brand owners, designs juice and packaging, contracts manufacturing to third parties, and distributes to roughly 120 countries. The model is asset-light with clean cash conversion.

The company runs two segments:

- **European operations**, conducted through its 72%-owned, separately Euronext-listed subsidiary Interparfums SA, generate roughly 68% of sales. The 28% minority interest is material: net income attributable to IPAR is meaningfully below consolidated net income, and a noncontrolling interest of ~$231M sits on the balance sheet. Any valuation must work from attributable earnings and cash flow.
- **US operations**, wholly owned US and Italian subsidiaries, generate roughly 32% of sales.

Brand concentration is high: the top seven brands are about 77% of sales. IPAR owns a handful of trademarks outright (Lanvin, Rochas, Solférino, Goutal) but the revenue base is dominated by licensed brands. Competitive positioning rests on decades-long relationships with brand owners and a reputation as a reliable license operator, evidenced by continued wins and renewals (GUESS extended to 2048, Coach to 2031, Van Cleef to 2034, plus 20-year Authentic Brands licenses for David Beckham effective 2028 and Nautica effective 2030).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Consolidated sales moved from $1,317.7M (FY2023) to $1,452M (FY2024, +10%) to $1,488.5M (FY2025, +2%). The deceleration is the defining recent trend. H1 2026 revenue was $686M, +2% YoY, but roughly +3 points came from FX, implying organic growth near +1% after the Middle East war drag. Q2 2026 organic was approximately +4% ex-Middle East, +2% reported.

The segment divergence is stark. US operations grew +18% in Q2 and +10% in H1, rebounding off a weak 2025 base (innovation gaps, tariff-driven supply disruption): GUESS +11% H1, DKNY +12% H1, Ferragamo +17% H1. European operations fell -4% in Q2 and -1% in H1 against difficult comps, with the Middle East war and Eastern European weakness weighing: Lacoste -16% H1 (against a +44% prior-year comp), Coach -8% Q2 (against a +42% comp). Brand-level Q2 detail shows Jimmy Choo rebounding +23% on the Man Parfum launch and Roberto Cavalli -9% on Middle East exposure.

**Profitability.** Gross margin was ~63.6% in FY2025 and improved to ~65.1% in Q1 2026 on mix and FX. Operating margin has compressed each of the last three years — 19.1% (2023), 18.9% (2024), 18.2% (2025) — driven by rising SG&A (43.6% of sales versus 41.6% in Q1'26), higher royalty expense from brand mix, and a deliberate overhead build ahead of new licenses. Net income attributable to IPAR rose modestly: $152.7M (2023), $164.4M (2024), $168.4M (2025), producing diluted EPS of ~$5.24.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was ~$214.9M and free cash flow ~$190.5M (12.8% FCF margin), representing FCF conversion above net income, a mark of high earnings quality. Cash plus short-term investments were ~$295M at December 2025, falling to ~$237M by Q1'26 after dividend and working-capital outflows. Total debt is modest at ~$176M; the reported D/E of ~1.8 is inflated by the NCI/equity structure rather than signaling distress. Current ratio is ~2.99. Altman Z-Score of 3.13 places the company in the safe zone; the Piotroski F-Score of 5/9 is middling, consistent with decelerating growth and margin compression. One working-capital item worth monitoring: DSO rose to 73 days from 66 and earlier 62, meaning receivables are building faster than sales, a possible tell on channel or retailer health given US department-store fragility.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised to $3.20/share for 2025 (from $3.00) but held flat for 2026, a caution signal in a transition year. Capital otherwise flows to acquiring and renewing licenses and IP (Goutal, Off-White, Longchamp, Beckham, Nautica).

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one miss over the trailing four quarters: Q1'26 +10.0%, Q4'25 +14.5%, Q3'25 +2.7%, and a Q2'25 miss of -12.3%. The company tends to beat, but the single miss came in the segment (Europe) now under geopolitical pressure, warranting some caution on near-term visibility.

The financial health score of 7.0 reflects genuinely strong cash conversion, modest leverage, and ample liquidity, tempered by three straight years of margin compression, a middling Piotroski score, and rising DSO.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is muted in the near term and dependent on a pipeline that is real but unproven. Organic growth has decelerated to roughly +1% in H1 2026. The bull framing is that 2026 optics understate the earnings power arriving in 2027-2028, when a series of new launches (Longchamp 2027, Off-White 2027, Beckham 2028, Nautica 2030) and the recently signed long-dated licenses convert into revenue. Management is deliberately carrying elevated SG&A now to support this, which explains both the margin compression and the flat dividend.

The bear framing is that IPAR is paying today for growth that is not guaranteed, in a period when its largest segment (Europe) is contracting and its US strength is partly a rebound off a weak comp rather than a step-change in underlying demand. The category itself appears healthy — US +18% in Q2 supports the view that the 2026 slowdown is a comp/transition and geopolitical issue rather than a structural category problem — but consensus estimates are not pricing acceleration. Current-year EPS consensus of $4.86 and next-year of $5.58 imply roughly +15% forward growth, and estimate revisions are flat: current-year +0.1% over 30 days and -0.2% over 90 days; next-year +0.1% and +0.5%. This is the opposite of the revision velocity that flags an earnings acceleration. Growth prospects score 5.3: directionally positive on the pipeline and category durability, but with no near-term momentum and heavy contingency on 2027+ execution.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$120, IPAR trades at ~22.7x trailing and ~23x forward EPS (on the $4.86 FY estimate). Against the "Household Products" peer median P/E of 15.0, the stock looks expensive, but that comparison is misleading because the peer set (WD-40 at 38x, Reynolds at 15x, Central Garden at 13.5x, Energizer at 7.1x, Edgewell negative) is a mismatched staples group, not the prestige-beauty cohort IPAR actually belongs to. WD-40 at 38x is the closest quality analog and sits well above IPAR. Against prestige-beauty and specialty-branded consumer peers, ~23x forward for a mid-teens grower with a 2.6% dividend yield and clean cash flow is roughly fair, not cheap.

Analyst price targets bracket the current price awkwardly: mean $112.33, median $111.50 — both below the current $122.46 print — with a high of $141 and a low of $85. The stock trades above consensus mean, which under our framework is a caution signal rather than a floor: the "analyst targets are floors" principle applies only to acceleration stocks with positive revisions and cheap multiples, and IPAR has neither rapidly rising estimates nor a discounted multiple. When a stock trades above the mean target with flat revisions, the market has already priced the recovery.

IPAR is not "cheap" in any sense that would earn a high valuation score, and even if it were, cheapness alone has been an unreliable buy signal — it more often marks a value trap absent an independent catalyst. Here there is no near-term catalyst: the 2027-2028 launch story is 12-24 months out, revisions are flat, and the multiple has already re-rated back near its historical range after the rally off the November low. The prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark the stock down (the round-trip reflects the collapse and recovery of sentiment around the transition year), but the current price offers no asymmetric upside. Valuation scores 4.8: fairly to fully valued, with the risk skewed toward multiple compression if the 2027 pipeline slips.

## Competitive Landscape

IPAR competes as a licensee against Coty's licensed portfolio, Puig, L'Oréal Luxe, Estée Lauder's fragrance brands, and the in-house fragrance operations of some luxury houses. Its edge is being the trusted specialist operator that brand owners hand marquee licenses to, a reputation reinforced by its ability to keep winning new 20-year licenses (Beckham, Nautica via Authentic Brands) and renew existing ones on long terms.

The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the margin. The prior-report trajectory marked this dimension down from 7.0 to 6.0, and the current data supports a stable read rather than a further cut: the moat (license relationships, portfolio diversity, launch cadence) is intact, but two watch items pressure the trajectory. First, brand concentration remains high in the top seven, so the loss of any single marquee license would be material; the Dunhill exit already dented 2025. Second, a new customer-concentration flag emerged: Macy's crossed 10% of sales for the first time (~12% in Q1'26), tying IPAR more tightly to a structurally challenged US department-store channel. The offsetting positive is genuine portfolio diversity — US strength is offsetting European weakness in real time, which is precisely the resilience management leans on. Competitive position scores 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Licensed-brand dependency (the defining risk).** IPAR does not own most of the brands driving revenue. A lost, non-renewed, or repurchased license removes a large revenue block at once. This is partly mitigated by recent long-dated renewals, but it is a permanent structural feature.

**Concentration.** Top-seven brands ~77% of sales; Macy's now >10% of sales in a fragile US department-store channel.

**Internal-control material weakness.** A material weakness in internal control over financial reporting was identified in FY2024 (risk assessment, monitoring, documentation, IT general controls), partially remediated in FY2025, but a material weakness tied to the risk-assessment process remained unremediated as of December 31, 2025. This is the most serious quality flag in the file.

**Auditor change.** An Item 4.01 8-K dated May 13, 2026 records a change in certifying accountant, shortly after the material-weakness disclosures and the annual-report cycle. The specific reason and successor firm could not be independently confirmed from accessible filings. An auditor change coinciding with an open material weakness deserves scrutiny: it is an open diligence item, not disqualifying, but it raises the reporting-quality bar.

**Geopolitical and geographic.** The Middle East war was a ~2-3 point revenue headwind in H1'26 (Roberto Cavalli's largest market is the Middle East; Eastern Europe weakness hit Lacoste). Asia/Pacific softened on Korea/India distribution changes.

**Tariffs.** As an importer of French-made product into the US, IPAR incurred ~$12.8M in tariff cost in 2025 (~0.9% of sales) and management flagged a larger 2026 headwind, a direct exposure to US tariff policy.

**Minority-interest complexity.** The 72% ownership of a separately listed French subsidiary complicates valuation and diverts a meaningful share of earnings and cash flow to outside holders.

The combination of an unremediated material weakness and a subsequent auditor change, layered on top of high concentration and a tariff/geopolitical drag, justifies a below-average risk score. These are specific, not-fully-priced idiosyncratic risks. Risk factors score 4.8.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing elevated uncertainty. ATM IV of 36.3% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 31.3% (rich by ~5 points), the term structure is in backwardation (36.3% near-term versus 29.9% at six months), and the put/call skew is elevated at 10%, signaling relative demand for downside protection into the August 4-5 earnings/call. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.4%, consistent with a market bracing for a binary reaction to full Q2 financials and management's forward commentary. ATM IV has collapsed from 63.0% (mid-June) to 36.3% as the stock rallied +26% over the same window, so the current backwardation reflects event risk rather than a structural distress signal. The elevated put skew is a mild divergence against a constructive fundamental read, directionally consistent with our HOLD stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** 2026 is a transition year that understates true earnings power. The prestige-fragrance category is durable, IPAR keeps winning and renewing 20-year licenses, and the deliberate SG&A build-out positions a large 2027-2028 launch slate. If those launches deliver and Europe normalizes as Middle East/Eastern Europe headwinds fade, revenue growth reaccelerates toward high-single/low-double digits, operating margin recovers toward 19%+, and EPS pushes higher. On a 24-25x forward multiple that could approach the $141 analyst high. Clean FCF, modest leverage, founder alignment, and a covered 2.6% dividend underpin the downside.

**Bear case.** The company is paying now for growth that is not guaranteed while its largest segment contracts. Organic growth is ~1%, margins have compressed three years running, the dividend was frozen, DSO is rising, and estimate revisions are flat. The material weakness plus auditor change signal reporting-quality risk. If a major license is lost, tariffs bite harder than the ~$12.8M 2025 level, or the 2027 launches underwhelm, EPS stagnates and the multiple compresses, pointing toward the $85 analyst low, roughly -30% from current levels. Founders sold into the drawdown (Madar ~$91 in April 2026, Benacin ~$83 in December 2025) with no open-market buying, which is not the pattern seen when insiders view shares as deeply undervalued.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the risk/reward is roughly balanced near consensus targets, and the decisive variable (the 2027-2028 pipeline) is not yet observable in the data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth reaccelerates above +5% ex-FX for two consecutive quarters, particularly a return to growth in the European segment.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive by more than +5% over 90 days, confirming the market is pricing the 2027 launch power.
- The material weakness is fully remediated AND the auditor-change reason is disclosed as routine (no disagreement/dismissal), removing the reporting-quality overhang.
- A pullback toward the low-$90s (~17-18x forward) that restores a valuation discount to fair value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Loss or non-renewal of a top-seven license.
- Evidence the auditor change involved a disagreement, a restatement, or an expanded material weakness.
- Operating margin compresses below 17% with no revenue reacceleration, indicating the SG&A build is not converting to growth.
- Macy's or department-store channel weakness materially cuts US growth, or tariff costs exceed ~2% of sales.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 full financials and earnings call on August 4-5, 2026, where management guidance on 2026 margins and the 2027 launch cadence, plus any color on the auditor change, will materially inform the thesis. A second checkpoint is FY2026 results, when the first 2027 launches (Longchamp, Off-White) begin to appear in forward guidance.

## Conclusion

Interparfums is a well-run, cash-generative prestige-fragrance specialist with a durable category, long-dated marquee licenses, and founder alignment demonstrated over decades. Those qualities support the balance sheet and cap the downside. But the investable case today is caught between a muted present and a contingent future. Organic growth is near +1%, margins have compressed for three straight years, revisions are flat, the dividend is frozen, and the stock has already re-rated back above consensus targets after a strong rally off its low. Layered on top are two governance flags, an unremediated material weakness and an auditor change of unconfirmed cause, plus real concentration and tariff/geopolitical exposure.

None of this is disqualifying, and the 2027-2028 launch pipeline could genuinely reprice the stock higher. But that thesis is 12-24 months out, unproven, and not yet visible in estimates or insider behavior. At ~23x forward EPS with no near-term catalyst and no valuation discount, the risk/reward is symmetric. We rate IPAR a **HOLD** and would want either a valuation reset toward the low-$90s or hard evidence that the pipeline and European normalization are arriving before upgrading. The overall score of 5.6 reflects a high-quality business at a full price in a transition year with elevated reporting-quality scrutiny.
