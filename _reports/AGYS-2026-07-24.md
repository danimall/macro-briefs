**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Agilysys is a pure-play hospitality software company executing an accelerating shift from perpetual-license/hardware revenue to recurring subscription revenue. Fiscal 2026 (year ended March 31) produced $319.3M in revenue (+15.9%), with subscription-and-maintenance revenue reaching $205.9M (64.5% of the mix, +21.1%) and total subscription revenue up 30.2%. The recurring base carries 79.4% gross margins, and the company generates cash strongly: $70.0M operating cash flow on $1.8M of capex yields $68.1M of free cash flow, a 21.3% FCF margin, against a balance sheet with zero long-term debt and $116.9M of cash. This is the core of the bull case, and it is genuine.

The stock has fallen roughly 16% since our prior report (from ~$113 to $95) and sits about 35% below its 52-week high of $145.25. On the evidence available, the decline is multiple compression driven by broad software/growth-stock sentiment and by two January 2026 print events that spooked the market, not by fundamental deterioration. Forward estimates are rising: current-year consensus EPS moved from $2.17 to $2.45 (+12.6%) over 90 days, and next-year moved from $2.80 to $3.19 (+13.8%). A director made repeated open-market purchases between January and March 2026 at $67–$85 into the decline. The combination of a lower price on improving estimates is, mechanically, a stronger risk/reward than it was three months ago.

We are holding rather than upgrading conviction for three reasons. First, at $95 the stock trades at roughly 39x forward EPS and ~39x FCF, a demanding multiple that requires sustained high-teens revenue growth and continued margin expansion to be justified. Second, FY26's reported operating margin of 13.5% was flattered by ~$7.7–9.1M of non-recurring CARES Act employee-retention credits; normalized operating margin is closer to ~11%, so the headline ~90% operating-income growth is not a run-rate. Third, the current-quarter estimate was cut 8.9% over 30 days and the earnings track record is choppy (two beats, two misses over the last four quarters), with a binary Q1 FY27 print due July 27, 2026 that options markets are pricing for a ±17.5% move. This is a good business at a full price, and the setup rewards patience.

## Company Overview

Agilysys (fiscal year ending March 31) is a specialized leader in hospitality software with a 45+ year corporate history, refocused exclusively on hospitality after divesting its legacy IT/retail businesses through 2014. It operates as a single reportable segment across three product ecosystems:

- **Food & Beverage:** InfoGenesis POS (the flagship), self-service kiosks, mobile ordering, Agilysys Pay (a PCI-P2PE-validated payment gateway), and analytics.
- **Hospitality & Leisure:** Property-management systems spanning large/complex properties (LMS, up to 7,000+ rooms), mid-size (Versa), and cloud-native SaaS (Stay), plus booking, self-check-in, Book4Time (spa/wellness), sales & catering, and loyalty.
- **Inventory & Procurement:** Eatec and Stratton Warren System.

The company is deeply entrenched in casinos/gaming, resorts, cruise lines, and managed foodservice. Reference customers include Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Caesars, MGM, Boyd Gaming, Royal Caribbean, Marina Bay Sands, Vail Resorts, and Compass Group. Management estimates a ~$16B annual-recurring-revenue TAM, of which Agilysys captures a small fraction. Its competitive edge is an integrated end-to-end platform (POS + PMS + inventory on a shared data foundation) versus fragmented point solutions, with unique casino-management-system integrations and a large, low-cost India R&D base (~70% of ~2,413 employees). Primary competitors are Oracle Hospitality (OPERA/Simphony), Shiji, Infor, Maestro, and in-house PMS at large hotel chains.

Agilysys sits in the Information Technology / Application Software category, the one sector where our selection framework has shown durable positive alpha. That is a favorable backdrop, but the framework also requires company-specific evidence of outperformance rather than sector beta, and it flags premium software multiples as vulnerable to derating, both directly relevant here.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trend is one of steady top-line growth and improving profitability:

| FY (Mar 31) | Revenue | Op. Income | Op. Margin | Net Income | FCF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $198.1M | $12.9M | 6.5% | $14.6M | — |
| 2024 | $237.5M (+19.9%) | $15.8M | 6.6% | $86.2M* | — |
| 2025 | $275.6M (+16.0%) | $22.6M | 8.2% | ~$23.2M | ~$52M |
| 2026 | $319.3M (+15.9%) | $43.0M | 13.5% | $38.8M | $68.1M |

*FY24 net income was inflated by a $65.5M non-cash deferred-tax valuation-allowance release, not operations.*

**Revenue mix (FY26):** Subscription & maintenance $205.9M (64.5% of revenue, +21.1%); professional services $72.2M (+12.4%); products (hardware + perpetual licenses) $41.2M (flat, structurally declining). The high-margin recurring engine is compounding while the low-quality product line intentionally shrinks.

**Margins.** Blended gross margin was 62.6%. Subscription gross margin is excellent at 79.4% and expanding. Professional-services gross margin is weak at 27.3% and declining, a function of front-loaded hiring and low implementation utilization. Read charitably, that is investment ahead of subscription conversions; read skeptically, persistent weakness would signal execution or pricing problems. Products margin fell to 40.9%.

**The margin caveat that matters.** FY26 operating income of $43.0M (13.5% margin) benefited from a ~$7.7M net gain in "other charges," driven largely by ~$9.1M of CARES Act employee-retention credits recognized in the first nine months. Adjusting for that, underlying operating margin is closer to ~11%. The headline doubling of operating income overstates the run-rate. The underlying direction — subscription mix shift plus cost discipline — is still positive, but a scoring model that extrapolates the reported ~90% operating-income growth would be wrong.

**Cash flow and balance sheet are the strongest part of the story.** OCF of $70.0M on $1.8M capex produced $68.1M of FCF, a 21.3% FCF margin. The business is asset-light. Cash is $116.9M; the $50M revolver drawn for the Book4Time acquisition was fully repaid by July 2025, leaving zero long-term debt and effective net cash. Deferred/contract liabilities grew to $92.5M (Dec 2025) from $70.7M, a positive forward-bookings indicator. R&D (product development) runs ~$72.7M, or ~22.8% of revenue, a heavy sustained platform investment.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.22 ("grey zone") is misleading here. It is depressed by a goodwill-heavy balance sheet (post-Book4Time) and by a liabilities base dominated by deferred revenue rather than debt. With no long-term debt and net cash, bankruptcy risk is negligible. Financial health is the highest-conviction dimension in this report, and it is the single most reliable winner/loser discriminator in our framework, so a strong score here carries weight.

**Earnings track record.** Over the last four quarters, two beats and two misses: +24.4% (Mar 2026), -9.6% (Dec 2025), +2.1% (Sep 2025), -10.7% (Jun 2025). The visibility is choppier than the growth narrative implies, which argues for tempered confidence in any single forward quarter.

## Growth Analysis

The subscription transformation is broad-based and durable. Recurring revenue at 64.5% of the mix growing 21%+ (30%+ including Book4Time), at 79% gross margins, is a high-quality compounding engine. The addressable market (~$16B ARR TAM) leaves a long runway, and structural hospitality tailwinds — the shift from on-prem/perpetual to cloud SaaS, self-service and mobility adoption, and demand for guest-data analytics — support continued high-teens top-line growth.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum, with a meaningful nuance. Over 90 days, current-year EPS was revised from $2.17 to $2.45 (+12.6%) and next-year from $2.80 to $3.19 (+13.8%). Those are healthy upward moves. However, the current-quarter estimate was cut from $0.44 to $0.40 (-8.9%) over 30 days. The pattern — near-term trimmed, full-year and out-year raised — is consistent with a modest timing shift rather than a demand problem, but it tempers the "rapidly accelerating" read. The revision magnitude here (~13% at the year level) falls short of the +30% threshold that would flag an unambiguous buy signal, and it is layered on a stock already at ~39x forward earnings, where the guardrails caution that rising estimates alone do not justify a premium-multiple buy because beats are already expected.

The Book4Time acquisition (closed August 2024, $145.8M net) added the global spa/wellness SaaS leader and contributed ~$21.3M of subscription revenue in FY26, deepening the multi-amenity resort suite and raising solutions-per-customer. That is the primary inorganic growth lever and it is working. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act's restoration of immediate U.S. R&D expensing should generate future cash-tax savings, a modest incremental tailwind.

Growth prospects earn a solid score, but the recent softening in near-term revisions and the choppy beat/miss cadence keep it out of top-tier territory. In our framework, growth is a directional winner-selector whose recent edge has thinned, and it should be confirmed by the stronger financial-health signal, which it is here.

## Valuation Assessment

At $95.01, market cap is ~$2.67B on ~$319M of FY26 revenue (~8.4x sales), ~$68M FCF (~39x FCF), and ~39x forward EPS on the $2.45 current-year consensus (~30x on the $3.19 next-year figure). The trailing P/E of ~69x is not the relevant metric given the earnings inflection, but even the forward figures are demanding.

Peer comparison is muddy because the application-software cohort is heterogeneous. The peer-median P/E is 21.0 (RNG 40.3, CALX 80.2, RAMP 16.9, ALRM 19.3, DLB 21.0), but those names span very different growth and quality profiles. Against that median, Agilysys carries a clear premium, justified in part by 30% subscription growth, 79% subscription gross margins, and net cash, but not fully. On a PEG basis, ~30x next-year EPS against ~14% forward EPS growth gives a PEG above 2.0, which is not cheap. Growth-adjusted, the stock is priced for continued flawless execution.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $100, median $127.5, high $159, mean $127.33. The framework's "targets are floors" principle applies to cheap inflection stocks with PEG <1.5 and forward P/E <30x; Agilysys sits at the boundary (30x next-year) but with a PEG above that threshold, so we treat the target range as informative rather than as a floor. The median implies ~34% upside, which is real if growth holds, but the multiple leaves little margin for error if it does not.

Two structural points cut against a high valuation score. First, our own selection history shows "cheap" screens as anti-predictive value traps and, conversely, that premium multiples do not protect stocks in a derating regime; the 2025–2026 software derating produced 40–50% drawdowns on names where the sector thesis was correct but the multiple was full. Agilysys's own 52-week range ($61.50–$145.25) illustrates that sensitivity. Second, the FY26 margin was ERC-flattered, so the "cheap on improving margins" argument is partly built on a non-recurring benefit.

Net, the ~16% decline since our last report and the rising full-year estimates have improved the entry point, moving valuation modestly higher in direction than the 5.5 recorded previously but landing at 5.5 here: the price is better, but the multiple remains full enough that we do not see asymmetric upside at $95. We would turn more constructive toward the high-$70s to low-$80s, where the stock traded as recently as late May, or on confirmation that normalized (ex-ERC) margins continue to expand.

## Competitive Landscape

Agilysys competes against much larger Oracle Hospitality (OPERA/Simphony), plus Shiji, Infor, and Maestro, and against in-house PMS at large chains. Its differentiation is verticalized, integrated depth — particularly in gaming/casino and multi-amenity resorts — where the shared POS/PMS/inventory data foundation and unique casino-management-system integrations (Book is described as the only booking engine integrating with core gaming systems) create switching costs. The customer base is fragmented and sticky, and the India R&D engine provides a structural cost advantage.

The trajectory is stable-to-improving: 30%+ subscription growth outpacing the broader hospitality-software market implies share gains and rising solutions-per-customer, aided by Book4Time. The key competitive uncertainty is AI. Management's own risk language now warns of an AI "arms race," that AI tools could "bypass or reduce reliance on traditional application-layer workflows" and "disrupt traditional subscription-based software models." Agilysys is positioning offensively — its June 2026 "AI-Driven Hospitality Strategy" describes its own embedded features (guest analytics, demand forecasting, automation) — but the application-layer moat is the central long-term debate for any vertical SaaS name. We score competitive position as solidly positive on current trajectory, with the AI question as the principal open risk to the moat rather than a present impairment. Our framework treats competitive position as a low-weight, recently unreliable dimension, so we anchor it on trajectory evidence and do not lean on it heavily.

## Risk Assessment

1. **India workforce concentration (~70% of employees, core R&D in Chennai).** Exposes the company to U.S. immigration/visa policy, geographic catastrophe risk, wage inflation, and rupee FX. A known step-up in India taxes is coming: the SEZ subsidiary pays only 50% of regular India tax through FY27, then full rates thereafter, a future margin/cash-tax headwind not yet in the run-rate.

2. **Hospitality end-market cyclicality.** Revenue is entirely tied to travel/leisure/gaming spend, sensitive to recession, tariffs, and travel disruption. MD&A explicitly flags macro conditions pressuring customer spending, particularly outside the U.S. Customer consolidation (a merged casino/hotel could drop Agilysys) and customer insolvency are additional vectors. Given the world-model H2 consumer-cliff concern (45–55%), a hospitality-exposed name carries genuine cyclical risk that a single-vertical software company cannot diversify away.

3. **AI disruption to the application layer**, both a competitive and a cost risk (rising AI input costs pressuring gross margin if not passed through).

4. **Goodwill/intangible impairment risk**, ~$200M combined ($134M goodwill + $66M intangibles), largely Book4Time. A hospitality downturn that impaired the acquisition would be a non-cash but sentiment-negative event.

5. **The one-time ERC benefit** obscures the true margin run-rate; the FY27 comparison will face a ~$7.7–9.1M headwind that a naive year-over-year read will misinterpret.

6. **Unverified June 22, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02 — Departure/Election of Directors or Officers).** This filing could not be retrieved and no corroborating news exists. An executive departure during a period of choppy near-term results would be a governance flag; an appointment would be neutral. This is an open item that should be verified before any position change.

7. **Concentrated ownership and demonstrated volatility.** The FY26 trading range ($65.76–$141.12) and the January 2026 drop after the December print show how sharply the multiple reacts to sentiment.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. ATM implied volatility is 76.5% against 53.5% one-year historical volatility (a rich 23-point spread, 82nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (76.5% near-term vs. 58.7% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent catalyst in the July 27 Q1 FY27 print. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±17.5%. Put/call skew is balanced (2.4%) and put/call open interest is low at 0.10, so positioning is not directionally bearish; the market is pricing a large binary move rather than a one-sided decline. Rich near-term IV argues against buying premium and favors waiting for the post-print volatility crush before adding, consistent with the hold stance.

Short interest is moderate at 7.5% of float with 4.1 days to cover, neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (→ $135–$159).** The subscription transformation continues at 20%+ (30%+ with Book4Time), recurring revenue climbs toward 70% of the mix, normalized operating margin expands past the ~11% ex-ERC level toward the mid-teens as professional-services utilization improves, and FCF compounds off a net-cash base with negligible capex. Rising full-year and next-year estimates prove to be the leading edge of a multi-year re-rating as the market rewards durable recurring-revenue quality. The ~$16B TAM and hospitality's cloud/self-service tailwinds support a long runway. On $3.19+ next-year EPS re-rated toward the mid-40s multiple the market has previously awarded, the stock reaches the $135–$159 analyst-high zone.

**Bear Case (→ $65–$75, ~21–32% downside).** A consumer/hospitality slowdown (the world-model H2 cliff) pressures customer travel/gaming spend, decelerating bookings and subscription growth into the low teens. The FY27 comparison absorbs the ~$9M ERC headwind, and professional-services margins stay depressed, so normalized margin fails to expand. AI-native entrants raise the perceived risk to the application-layer moat, and the ~39x forward multiple compresses toward the peer median. The stock revisits the low-$70s it traded at as recently as late May 2026, near the level where the director was buying.

**Base Case (→ $110).** Revenue grows ~14–16% in FY27 with subscription revenue up ~20%+, normalized margins expand modestly despite the ERC headwind, and FCF stays above 20% of revenue. Estimates continue drifting higher but at a decelerating pace. Applying ~34x to the $3.19 next-year EPS gives roughly $108–$110, in line with the analyst median area and implying ~16% upside from $95 over twelve months, attractive but not sufficiently asymmetric at the current multiple to warrant a high-conviction add ahead of a ±17.5% binary print.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth hold. The reassessment horizon is the July 27, 2026 Q1 FY27 earnings print, with a fuller review over the following 12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q1 FY27 revenue growth sustained at ≥15% with subscription revenue ≥20% and evidence of normalized (ex-ERC) operating-margin expansion above ~11%.
- A pullback into the high-$70s to low-$80s that restores asymmetric upside to the $110+ base case (PEG toward or below 1.5 on next-year EPS).
- Professional-services gross margin inflecting upward, confirming the "investment ahead of implementations" reading rather than an execution problem.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Subscription revenue growth decelerating below ~12% or contract-liability/deferred-revenue balances declining sequentially, signaling a bookings slowdown.
- A guidance cut, a hospitality-demand-driven revenue miss, or a Book4Time goodwill impairment.
- Confirmation that the June 22 Item 5.02 filing represents a material senior-executive departure (CEO/CFO), particularly if paired with weakening results.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 FY27 earnings (July 27, 2026) is the immediate checkpoint; the ERC-headwind-adjusted margin trajectory across the next two prints is the key medium-term data set.

## Conclusion

Agilysys is a high-quality, net-cash, high-FCF vertical software compounder mid-way through a genuine subscription transformation, operating in the one sector (IT/Application Software) where selection has historically added alpha. The fundamentals justify the improving score trajectory recorded over the past several reports: financial health is excellent (8.0), growth is durable (7.0), and management has shown alignment (equity-heavy comp, exceptional multi-year TSR, and a director buying in the open market into the decline).

We stop short of maintaining a high-conviction BUY for reasons of price and setup rather than business quality. At $95 the stock trades at ~39x forward EPS and ~39x FCF, a full multiple that FY26's ERC-flattered margins partly disguise and that leaves little cushion against the H2 hospitality-demand risk or a Q1 FY27 disappointment. The ~16% decline since our last look and rising full-year estimates have improved the entry, but the risk/reward is symmetric-to-modestly-favorable, not asymmetric. We rate AGYS a HOLD (growth_hold), with a bias to add on a pullback toward the high-$70s to low-$80s or on confirmation of continued normalized margin expansion at the July 27 print. Overall score: 6.6.
