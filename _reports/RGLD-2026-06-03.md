**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Royal Gold is a precious-metals royalty and streaming company (a financier of mines rather than an operator) currently posting the strongest results in its history. FY2025 revenue rose 43% to $1.03B, Q1 2026 revenue more than doubled to $469M, and Q1 EPS reached $3.30 versus $1.72 a year earlier. The model is structurally attractive: operating margins near 62%, net margins near 46%, near-zero operating capex, no exposure to mine cost inflation, and a 26-year record of dividend increases. The balance sheet is conservative (D/E ~0.33, current ratio 3.1x, Altman Z 3.25), and management has paid the post-Sandstorm revolver balance down from $900M to $525M while authorizing a new $500M buyback.

The complication is that almost all of the headline growth comes from two transient or non-recurring sources. The first is an extraordinary gold price — Q1 2026 averaged roughly $4,873/oz versus $2,860 a year earlier — which flows almost entirely to margin because Royal Gold's purchase costs are largely fixed. The second is the $4.148B Sandstorm/Horizon acquisition, funded with ~18.6M new shares (roughly 29% dilution) plus ~$500M cash. Strip out the gold spike and the dilution, and organic per-share growth is far more modest than the +43% revenue line suggests. Consensus is signaling this: current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 9.5% over 30 days and 13.0% over 90 days, and the company has missed in two of the last four quarters (-24.3% in Q4 2025, -9.1% in Q1 2026).

At ~$219, RGLD trades at roughly 26x trailing EPS of $6.69 and ~19x current-year consensus of $11.63, but that forward figure embeds a gold price near all-time highs. We rate RGLD a HOLD. The franchise quality, balance sheet, and buyback floor are real, but the stock is priced on peak-cycle earnings with falling estimates, and the asymmetry that would justify a higher-conviction call is absent at this price.

## Company Overview

Royal Gold provides capital to mining operators in two forms. Streams (~67% of revenue) involve upfront deposits in exchange for the right to purchase a fixed portion of a mine's output at a deeply discounted price, then resell at spot. As of Q1 2026 the company held 17 producing and 5 development-stage streams. Royalties (~33% of revenue) are passive interests in a percentage of revenue or metal; the company held 62 producing, 25 development, and 258 exploration-stage royalties. Revenue is roughly 71–78% gold, 12–16% silver, and 7–10% copper, with approximately 85% generated outside the U.S., principally Canada, the Dominican Republic, Chile, and Zambia.

The business carries minimal headcount and no direct exposure to mine operating or capital costs (the lone exception being a 30% equity JV in the Hod Maden development project in Türkiye). When gold rises, the spread between Royal Gold's fixed purchase cost and spot expands directly into margin. When gold falls, that leverage runs in reverse. The principal competitors are Franco-Nevada and Wheaton Precious Metals, both larger; together the three form the streaming oligopoly.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is genuinely high-quality, but the trajectory must be decomposed.

**Revenue:** $603M (2022) → $606M (2023) → $719M (2024) → $1,030M (2025, +43%). Q1 2026 revenue of $469M more than doubled the $193M of Q1 2025, annualizing toward ~$1.8B if sustained. The doubling reflects the Sandstorm and Kansanshi assets layered onto a gold price roughly 70% higher year-over-year.

**Earnings:** Net income $466M in 2025 versus $332M in 2024; diluted EPS $6.69. Q1 2026 EPS $3.30 (adjusted $3.11) versus $1.72.

**Margins and cash flow:** Operating margin 61.9%, net margin 45.8%. Operating cash flow reached $705M in 2025 (vs. $529M) and $294M in Q1 2026 (vs. $136M). FCF conversion is strong because capital is deployed through deposits classified as investing rather than operating capex.

**Balance sheet:** Total assets jumped from $3.4B to $9.5B on the acquisitions; equity rose from $3.1B to $7.4B (Q1 2026). The company took on its first debt in years — $0 (2024) → $895M (2025) — then paid it down rapidly to $600M by Q1 2026 and $525M by the 10-Q filing date. D/E of ~0.33, current ratio 3.1x, and Altman Z of 3.25 place the company firmly in the safe zone. The revolver was expanded to $1.4B with a $600M accordion, giving up to $2.0B of potential capacity.

**Capital allocation:** A 26th consecutive annual dividend increase (to $1.90/share, +6%) and a first-ever-scale $500M share repurchase authorization (May 4, 2026). For a serial acquirer, this pivot toward returning capital is a meaningful signal.

Two caveats weigh on earnings quality. First, the effective tax rate was just 8.3% in Q1 2026 (aided by a $33.7M discrete foreign-tax benefit) and 17.8% for FY2025 — both below the underlying statutory level and partly non-recurring. Second, the recent earnings record is mixed: beats of 21.5% and 5.0% in mid-2025 gave way to misses of -24.3% and -9.1% in the last two quarters, with Mount Milligan deliveries softening on lower grade, recovery, and throughput.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question is whether the current run-rate is durable. We separate the drivers.

The Sandstorm/Horizon acquisition is permanent and adds a broad development and exploration portfolio, but it was financed with ~29% share dilution, so its accretion on a per-share basis is materially smaller than its revenue contribution. The Kansanshi gold stream ($1.0B advance to First Quantum) and Warintza stream add genuine new cash-flow streams, though several of these "gold streams" are referenced to copper production, introducing base-metal price and operating sensitivity into a vehicle marketed as precious-metals exposure. Copper has risen to ~10% of Q1 2026 revenue.

The gold price is the larger but more fragile driver. A 10% move in gold equates to roughly a $37M quarterly revenue swing. With gold near $4,800/oz in Q1 2026 versus a multi-year average far below that, a reversion would compress the optics sharply. The development-stage pipeline (Hod Maden, Warintza, the Sandstorm exploration assets) provides multi-year organic GEO growth, but much of it is years from delivery and depends on third-party operators advancing projects.

Consensus estimate revisions corroborate the deceleration view. Current-quarter EPS has been cut 9.5% in 30 days and 13.0% in 90 days; current-year EPS is down 2.6% (30d) and 5.7% (90d). Only next-year EPS shows a positive 30-day revision (+4.4%), and even that is down 5.1% over 90 days. This is negative near-term earnings momentum, which argues against treating the gold-flattered run-rate as a base case.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward multiple looks reasonable in isolation, but it rests on a gold price near record highs; normalized for a lower gold deck, the implied multiple is meaningfully higher.

Peer context is mixed because the Materials peer set provided (IFF, DuPont, International Paper, Reliance, CF Industries) is heterogeneous and not directly comparable to a royalty/streamer — peer median P/E is 24.9, but those are chemicals, paper, and steel businesses with different return and margin profiles. The more relevant comparables are Franco-Nevada and Wheaton, which typically command premium multiples to RGLD on superior asset purity and a longer dividend-growth track record. RGLD's 2025 TSR of 190.8 (indexed) lagged its own peer benchmark of 290.2 — material underperformance during a gold bull market, plausibly reflecting market skepticism about the dilutive Sandstorm deal and the drift into copper-referenced and development-stage assets.

Analyst targets cluster well above spot — mean $331.67, median $335.50, range $246 to $375, with UBS initiating Buy at $325 on April 14, 2026. We treat these targets cautiously rather than as a floor. With estimates falling and the forward multiple supported by a peak-cycle gold price, the targets reflect a continuation of extreme gold prices rather than independent confirmation of mispricing. The $500M buyback and 0.85% dividend yield support the downside, but neither creates the asymmetric upside that would justify a higher valuation score. We score valuation 5.0 — fairly valued on normalized earnings, with the multiple reasonable only if the current gold price persists.

## Competitive Landscape

Royal Gold sits in a structurally attractive oligopoly. The streaming model captures gold-price upside without bearing operating-cost inflation, and the company's scale, cost of capital, and 25-plus-year track record are durable advantages. The Sandstorm acquisition was a direct scale response to Franco-Nevada and Wheaton, broadening the development pipeline and closing part of the size gap.

The competitive trajectory is stable to modestly improving, but with offsetting pressures. The 10-K explicitly flags that competitors are larger with greater financial resources and that the number and size of streaming companies has grown, intensifying competition for deals and potentially compressing returns. The copper creep (Kansanshi, Warintza, Horizon, Mount Milligan) dilutes the pure-precious-metals positioning that has historically earned the big three premium multiples. The peer-lagging TSR suggests the market currently views RGLD as the relatively weaker of the three franchises despite its scale gains.

## Risk Assessment

**Asset and counterparty concentration.** Five properties generated ~53% of 2025 revenue: Mount Milligan (22%), Pueblo Viejo (13%), Andacollo (8%), Cortez (7%), Kansanshi (3%). Mount Milligan deliveries already softened in Q1 2026. The $1.0B Kansanshi stream ties Royal Gold's largest recent deployment to First Quantum's operational and credit health, with acceleration options structured around FQM credit metrics — a live counterparty watch item.

**Operator dependence and transparency.** Royal Gold controls no operating decisions and relies on operator disclosures it cannot independently verify. It does not report reserves/resources under S-K 1300, a structural transparency limitation.

**Sovereign exposure.** ~85% of revenue originates abroad (Zambia, Dominican Republic, Chile, Ecuador, Türkiye), exposing the company to expropriation, royalty/tax changes, and currency controls.

**Integration and contract structure.** Sandstorm purchase accounting was still being finalized in Q1 2026, with $1.08B of deferred tax liabilities booked. Sliding-scale rate reductions at Andacollo, Kansanshi, and Pueblo Viejo trigger after delivery thresholds, so past revenue is not indicative of future. The ~2.47M oz of deferred Pueblo Viejo silver is effectively a non-asset (management states recovery is "uncertain and may never occur").

**Hod Maden JV.** The one off-model asset requires ongoing cash calls funded via shareholder loans to a Turkish entity, with conversion to a stream/royalty uncertain.

**Gold-price leverage.** The dominant risk is a gold reversion, which would compress the current earnings optics far more than the operating picture would suggest.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 46.3% sits rich to 1-year historical volatility of 38.9% (a 7.4pp premium), with the term structure in backwardation (46.3% near-term versus 41.1% at six months). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.4%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-0.7%), and the put/call OI ratio has risen from 0.45 to 0.85 since early May as the stock fell 11.4%, suggesting accumulating hedging demand rather than directional conviction either way. The backwardation and rich IV are consistent with a market bracing for a gold-price-driven swing around the next data points. This reinforces a wait-and-see posture rather than fresh long exposure at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$325, UBS target)
Gold remains elevated or rises further on continued safe-haven demand and macro stress, sustaining the doubled revenue run-rate toward ~$1.8B+. Sandstorm and Kansanshi prove accretive as integration completes, the development pipeline (Hod Maden, Warintza) delivers organic GEO growth, the buyback shrinks the share count at prices management considers below intrinsic value, and the multiple re-rates toward Franco-Nevada/Wheaton levels as the deal's value creation becomes visible. In this scenario the stock returns toward its 52-week high of ~$306 and beyond.

### Bear Case (~$160–175, -20% to -27%)
Gold reverts from extreme levels, compressing the spread-driven earnings that flatter current results. The current-quarter estimate cut extends as Mount Milligan grade/recovery weakness persists and First Quantum/Kansanshi counterparty issues surface. The market continues to penalize the dilutive deal and copper drift with a relative discount, and the stock retraces toward the 2025 lows near $150–175. The peak-cycle 26x trailing multiple proves to be exactly that.

### Base Case (~$220–240)
Gold holds near current elevated levels but does not advance materially; organic growth and the buyback partly offset estimate erosion and the non-recurring tax tailwinds rolling off. The stock trades range-bound around current levels with the dividend and buyback providing a floor and the gold-price ceiling capping upside. This is the most likely path over the next 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The franchise quality and balance sheet are real, but the stock is priced on peak-cycle, gold-flattered earnings with falling consensus estimates, and the asymmetry needed for a higher-conviction call is not present at ~$219.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the company last reported May 6, 2026; the next print is the natural checkpoint), plus the monthly gold price trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year and current-quarter EPS estimate revisions inflect positively for two consecutive months (currently -2.6% and -9.5% over 30 days), indicating the organic earnings base holds independent of the gold spike.
- A pullback toward the low-$180s/high-$170s that compresses the forward multiple below ~15x on normalized earnings, restoring downside cushion.
- Confirmation that the Sandstorm integration is accretive on a per-share basis (organic GEO growth offsetting the dilution) and that Kansanshi/First Quantum counterparty risk is contained.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sustained gold reversion below ~$3,500/oz combined with continued downward estimate revisions, which would expose the peak-cycle multiple.
- Material operational deterioration at the top-five assets (further Mount Milligan grade/throughput declines or a First Quantum credit/operational event at Kansanshi).
- Evidence the buyback is being funded at the expense of balance-sheet discipline or that further dilutive M&A resumes before the Sandstorm deal proves value-creative.

## Conclusion

Royal Gold is a high-quality, high-margin, conservatively financed royalty/streamer enjoying record results from an extraordinary gold price and a transformational acquisition. The pristine balance sheet, dividend record, and a credible buyback at prices management views as undervalued provide genuine downside support. The offsetting reality is that the headline growth is heavily flattered by a near-record gold price and partly non-recurring tax benefits, the Sandstorm deal diluted shareholders while the stock lagged its streaming peers, consensus EPS estimates are being cut across near-term periods, and the company has missed two of the last four quarters. The options market is pricing near-term stress, and the rising put OI on the decline signals hedging rather than conviction.

At ~$219 — roughly 26x trailing and ~19x a gold-inflated forward number — the risk/reward is balanced. There is upside if gold holds and the buyback and pipeline deliver, and comparable downside if gold reverts and estimate erosion continues. We rate RGLD a HOLD and would become more constructive on a positive inflection in estimate revisions or a pullback that restores a margin of safety on normalized earnings.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Q1 2026 EPS actual was $2.72 (a -9.1% miss vs. $2.99 est.), not $3.30 — the report's $3.30/$1.72 figures conflict with the Phase 1 earnings-surprise data, and the genuine Q1 EPS is materially lower, which makes both the trailing earnings base and the "doubling" narrative weaker than stated; the report's HOLD rests on this earnings picture and may be more fragile than presented.
