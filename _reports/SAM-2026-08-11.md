## Executive Summary

Boston Beer is a debt-free, cash-generative business in a shrinking category, and the two facts are now pulling in opposite directions with roughly equal force. On the constructive side: $265.5M cash, zero borrowings, an undrawn $150M revolver, FY2025 free cash flow of $215.6M, gross margin up ~410bp in FY2025 to 48.5% and a further 60bp to 50.4% in Q2 2026, and owned-brewery production rising from 74% of domestic volume in FY2024 to 89% in H1 2026 against a >90% FY2026 target. On the other side: depletions have declined for five straight years and are worsening (-4% FY2025, -5% year-to-date 2026, **-6% in Q2 2026**), with Twisted Tea — the brand that carried the company through the Truly collapse — now declining alongside Truly, Samuel Adams, Hard Mountain Dew and Dogfish Head.

The critical analytical correction in this file is the share count. The supplied market cap of $1.43B captures Class A only. Total shares at June 27, 2026 were 8,224,038 Class A plus 2,068,000 Class B = 10,292,038, both classes participating fully in economics. At $178.33 that is **$1,835M of equity value**, not $1.43B — corroborated independently by a $1.82B reference at $174.51 on July 23. On the correct count, SAM trades at **18.8x** the $9.50 midpoint of FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance and **17.0x** next-year consensus of $10.51, against a Consumer Staples peer median P/E of 19.9x where those peers also pay a 4.1% median dividend and SAM pays none. The valuation is supportive on cash flow (FY2025 FCF yield 11.8% on market cap, 13.7% on the $1,570M enterprise value; EV/EBITDA ~6.7x) and unremarkable on earnings.

Three developments since the last review argue against the prior AVOID while stopping well short of a constructive case. First, the May 26 amended final judgment in the Ardagh matter set pre-judgment interest at $15.5M versus the $36.5M Q1 estimate, a $21.1M favorable pre-tax reversal, and the $192.6M accrual is covered 2.2x by $415.5M of cash-plus-revolver liquidity, which makes this a valuation and capital-allocation problem rather than a solvency problem. Second, management held the non-GAAP EPS range at $8.50-$10.50 and raised the gross-margin low end to 48.5%. Third, 23.8% of the float is short with 4.8 days to cover, and options open interest has flipped from put-heavy (P/C OI 4.05 in June) to call-tilted (0.82), which makes the bearish expression mechanically crowded. Against that, guidance was held by cutting planned A&P growth by $20M and capex by $10M, consensus for the current and next fiscal year has been cut 5.6% and 5.2% in 30 days, and the last two quarters missed by 25.1% and 17.5%. Probability-weighted, I get roughly **-7%** over twelve months from $178.33, which supports REDUCE rather than AVOID.

## Company Overview

Boston Beer operates as a single reportable segment across a "beyond beer" portfolio: Twisted Tea (hard iced tea, historically the largest brand), Truly Hard Seltzer (in multi-year decline), Sun Cruiser (vodka-based RTD launched 2024, the clearest growth brand), Samuel Adams, Angry Orchard cider, Dogfish Head (declining; its trademark was written down $42.6M in Q4 2024), PepsiCo-licensed Hard Mountain Dew, and Sinless vodka cocktails.

Route to market is wholesale through 300+ distributors supported by a 550+ person sales force, the largest craft field organization in the U.S. and a genuine structural asset. Q2 2026 revenue mix: 94% domestic distributors, 5% international, 1% company-owned retail across nine locations.

Governance is dual-class. Class B carries effectively all voting power and is held by founder Jim Koch, who resumed the CEO role on August 15, 2025 and has elected to take no salary, bonus or equity. That is unusual alignment; it also means the chief executive holds no contractual retention incentive at a company that has cycled through three CEOs in roughly 30 months.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and volume.** Shipment volume fell to 7,140 thousand barrels in FY2025 from 7,493 thousand (-4.7%), and to 3,607 thousand in H1 2026 from ~3,821 thousand (-5.6%). Net revenue was $1,965.0M in FY2025 (-2.4%) and $1,002.3M in H1 2026 (-3.8%); Q2 2026 net revenue was $568.3M (-3.3%). Net revenue per barrel rose 2.4% in FY2025 to $275.21 and 1.9% in H1 2026, so pricing is offsetting roughly half the volume decline. Distributor inventory was ~4.5 weeks at June 27, 2026, unchanged year over year, so the Q2 gap between -6% depletions and -4.5% shipments is not obviously an inventory-loading artifact.

A definitional note matters for screens: XBRL `Revenues` of $2,087.3M for FY2025 includes $122.3M of excise taxes. Margins computed on the gross figure understate the company's own basis (operating margin 6.9% gross versus 7.4% on net revenue).

**Gross margin is real; the operating-line benefit is not.** Gross margin moved 44.4% → 48.5% → 50.4% (Q2 2026). FY2025 COGS per barrel fell 5.1% to $141.79, itemized as contract renegotiation and recipe work +$37.4M, brewery efficiencies +$34.1M, lower obsolescence +$10.3M, lower third-party production +$9.6M, against -$36.8M of inflation including $10.1M of tariffs.

Advertising, promotional and selling expense absorbed most of it. A,P&S was 27.43% of net revenue in FY2024 ($552.1M) and 31.04% in FY2025 ($610.0M), a 361bp increase against a 410bp gross-margin gain, roughly 88% consumed. H1 2026 ran 32.5% (Q2 alone 32.7%). Adjusting FY2024 operating income for the $42.6M Dogfish Head impairment puts clean FY2024 operating margin near 5.9% versus FY2025's 7.4%, so the underlying improvement is closer to 150bp than the headline 360bp. One verification gap: I could not confirm whether FY2024's $26.1M Rauch contract settlement sat in COGS. If it did, roughly 130bp of the FY2025 gross-margin expansion is non-recurrence rather than operational.

**Earnings.** FY2025 net income $108.5M, diluted EPS $9.92 on 10.937M weighted diluted shares, a clean year with no impairment or litigation charge, implying trailing ~18.0x. H1 2026 swung to a $(93.7)M loss, $(8.99) per share, entirely litigation-driven; non-GAAP H1 EPS was $5.28, with Q2 non-GAAP $3.65 versus GAAP $4.96 (Q2 GAAP benefited $1.31/share from the favorable interest revision).

**Cash flow, with a caveat.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $270.2M against $54.6M of capex, giving $215.6M of FCF, or $19.71 per diluted share. OCF exceeded net income by $161.7M via D&A $90.4M, stock compensation $21.8M, impairments $7.0M and a $47.0M working-capital inflow, roughly half of which was inventory reduction ($117.2M → $92.5M). That is a balance-sheet drawdown, and H1 2026 reversed it (inventory a $25.8M use, receivables $43.4M). H1 2026 OCF was $117.6M versus $128.4M. Applying FY2025's seasonal shape (H1 = 47.5% of full-year OCF) implies **~$248M FY2026 OCF and ~$178M FCF** after the $70M capex midpoint, a labeled sensitivity rather than a forecast, and one that assumes no cash payment on the judgment.

**Balance sheet.** At June 27, 2026: cash $265.5M, zero debt, undrawn $150M revolver maturing December 16, 2027, no disclosed covenant issues. Total assets $1,255.1M, liabilities $540.5M, equity $714.6M. The frequently cited Altman Z of 4.77 and current ratio of 1.65 are FY2025, pre-verdict figures. Recomputed on the Q2 balance sheet, the current ratio is $515.8M/$493.6M = **1.045**; excluding the contested $192.6M accrual it is 1.71. Equity/total liabilities fell from 2.44 to 1.32. Liquidity of $415.5M against a $192.6M accrual accruing post-judgment interest at ~3.79% on $191.0M (~$7.2M/year) is 2.2x coverage.

Retained earnings deserve monitoring: $421.6M (FY2023) → $240.3M (FY2024) → $147.7M (FY2025) → **$5.2M** at June 27, 2026, driven by buyback retirements plus the H1 loss. With implied H2 non-GAAP net income of only ~$43M and repurchases continuing, retained earnings likely go negative in H2 2026. With $709.9M of additional paid-in capital and no dividend, this is largely presentational, but it interacts with a buyback program that has only **$174M remaining** on the Board's $1.6B limit.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth to underwrite. Depletions: +22% (2021), -5%, -6%, -2%, -4%, and -5% year-to-date 2026 with Q2 at -6%. The company's own 10-K states U.S. beer shipments have fallen for roughly ten consecutive years, with 2025 declines *accelerating* to about -4% in volume. SAM is therefore tracking at or slightly below an already-declining category, which is share loss, and Koch's Q2 language about "improving market share trends" concedes the direction.

Guidance for FY2026 is depletions and shipments down low-to-mid single digits, price +1% to 2%, gross margin 48.5%-50%, tariffs $20-30M (from $11M in FY2025; H1 2026 already $12.1M), and non-GAAP EPS $8.50-$10.50. The midpoint of $9.50 is flat-to-down versus FY2025's $9.92 **despite roughly 10% fewer shares and ~150bp of clean margin expansion**, because volume decline, the tariff step-up, freight and a higher tax rate absorb it.

Estimate revisions confirm the direction: current-year consensus $9.01 (-5.6% in 30 days, -9.4% in 90 days), next-year $10.51 (-5.2%, -7.8%). The next-quarter line improved 47.7%, but from $(1.58) to $(0.83), a smaller seasonal loss rather than growth. The last two prints missed by 25.1% and 17.5% after two beats, which argues for discounting rather than trusting the current consensus.

The growth optionality that exists is unverifiable from filings. Sun Cruiser is described as growing rapidly by management and by outside holders, and Angry Orchard grew in Q2, but Boston Beer reports one segment with no brand-level revenue. Whether Sun Cruiser can offset Twisted Tea's decline cannot be established, and that is a structural disclosure limitation rather than a research failure. A second, smaller positive: the 10-K discloses federal restrictions on hemp-derived beverages taking effect after November 2026, which would remove an unregulated share-taker from the RTD shelf; cannabis reclassification from Schedule I to Schedule III in December 2025 cuts the other way over a longer horizon. Neither is quantified anywhere.

The per-share offset is the one real growth lever: weighted basic shares fell 15.7% from 12.317M (FY2022) to 10.387M (Q2 2026), so net revenue per diluted share rose roughly 6% while absolute net revenue fell about 6%. That lever now has $174M of authorization behind it against a $1,835M capitalization.

**Growth prospects: 3.2.**

| EV/EBITDA (FY2025 op income $144.9M + D&A $90.4M = $235.3M) | 6.7x, or 7.5x including the accrual |

| FY2026 sensitivity FCF (~$178M) yield | 9.7% |

Against peer medians of 19.9x P/E and 4.1% yield, SAM is at a small P/E discount with no income and a mid-single-digit volume decline; peers such as Flowers Foods and Energizer are not growing either, but they distribute cash. The genuine argument for mispricing is the cash-flow gap: D&A of $90.4M against guided capex of $60-80M means free cash flow structurally exceeds net income, and a 9.7-11.8% FCF yield with zero debt is not what the market usually pays for a business that will still be generating $150M+ of FCF in three years. On EV/EBITDA at 6.7-7.5x, the stock is cheap in absolute staples terms.

The counter-argument is that most of the cheapness is compensation for a broken demand thesis rather than an oversight. Two specific reasons not to score valuation highly:

1. **The earnings that support the multiple are being held up by discretionary spending cuts.** The company kept non-GAAP EPS guidance intact by removing $20M of planned A&P growth and $10M of capex in a business where management's own incentive plan uses depletions growth as the company-selected performance measure. Management offered a plausible alternative reading — Q4 A&P declines partly reflect a comparison against elevated Q4 2025 production spend for 2026 programming — which should be tested against Q4 depletions. Either way, EPS held by reducing brand investment in a share-losing portfolio is lower-quality than EPS held by operating leverage.
2. **Margin expansion is finite and nearly exhausted.** Gross margin has moved ~700-800bp off the 2022 trough and is guided to 48.5%-50% against 49.9% in H1 2026. Once it plateaus, a 5% volume decline flows straight to operating income.

Analyst dispersion tells the same story: $160 low, $195 median, $199.85 mean, $295 high, roughly 84% between low and high around a $178 price, with recent cuts at Jefferies ($230 → $195), Deutsche Bank ($187 → $176) and Roth ($315 → $295). Ratings are 1 buy / 11 hold / 2 sell. There is no consensus on either the appeal outcome or the terminal volume trajectory, and the targets should not be treated as independent confirmation in either direction.

**Valuation: 4.8** — not stretched, meaningfully supported by cash flow and enterprise-value multiples, but not an identified mispricing with an adequate margin of safety given falling estimates and a shrinking revenue base.

## Competitive Landscape

Boston Beer's structural advantage is distribution: 550+ salespeople across 300+ distributors, which is what made Twisted Tea and Sun Cruiser launchable at scale. It is also a fixed-cost asset spread over declining volume, which is why A,P&S has climbed to 32.5% of net revenue.

The insourcing program is the clearest execution win: 74% of domestic volume in owned breweries in FY2024, 86% in FY2025, 89% in H1 2026, targeting >90%, with City Brewing's share falling from 20% of H1 2025 domestic volume to 11% in H1 2026 and COGS per barrel down 5.1%. Residual third-party obligations are modest and disclosed: ~$20M of expected future shortfall fees ($5M in the remainder of 2026, $15M thereafter), ~$25M total if third-party volume went to zero, and Rauch quarterly minimums of $4.1M annually even at zero volume through 2031.

The competitive set is getting harder. The 10-K names Anheuser-Busch InBev, Molson Coors, Constellation (Modelo/Corona), Heineken and Mark Anthony (White Claw/Mike's), and additionally names **Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Monster and Arizona** as non-alcohol companies entering alcohol. Those entrants bring far larger marketing budgets and DSD leverage into exactly the flavored-RTD niche where Twisted Tea and Sun Cruiser compete. Trajectory, which is what matters here, is negative: the two largest brands are declining, share is being ceded within a declining category, and defense costs are rising as a share of revenue.

**Competitive position: 3.5.**

## Risk Assessment

**Ardagh judgment (dominant near-term item).** Ardagh Metal Packaging USA — the aluminum can **supplier and plaintiff** — sued on December 31, 2022 alleging Boston Beer failed to purchase contractual minimum volumes for 2021-2026. Jury verdict for Ardagh April 6, 2026; amended final judgment May 26, 2026 set damages at $175.5M with pre-judgment interest of $15.5M. Cumulative pre-tax impact through June 27, 2026 is $198.1M ($175.5M + $17.2M net interest + $5.4M legal fees), against a $192.6M accrual in current liabilities. Post-judgment interest accrues on $191.0M at ~3.79%. Boston Beer denies breach, filed a post-trial motion June 8 (briefing completed July 23) and has a Notice of Appeal held in abeyance; it states it cannot estimate when or if damages will be paid.

Scale: $175.5M is 9.6% of market cap. If the judgment stands, ~$191M is paid, and it is fully deductible at a ~25% combined effective rate, the after-tax cash cost is roughly $143M, or ~7.8% of market cap, a sensitivity with both deductibility and appellate outcome unresolved. Liquidity coverage of 2.2x means this is a value transfer rather than a going-concern question. The origin appears to be over-commitment to can supply during the 2021 seltzer boom followed by the Truly collapse; the contract's minimum-volume terms may equally have been genuinely ambiguous, which is consistent with the company's continued denial and appeal.

**Tariffs and input costs.** $11M in FY2025, guided $20-30M for FY2026, $12.1M already in H1. H1 material inflation of $31.7M was mainly aluminum ($29.2M, including $9.8M of tariffs). The FY2025 10-K notes its tariff estimates predate a February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling on tariff authority, and July guidance reflects only "tariffs currently being charged by suppliers." This is a live, unquantified two-sided exposure: relief is as plausible as escalation and neither is in the filings.

**Governance and key-man.** Koch holds 100% of Class B voting power and is named as a key-person risk in the 10-K, with no disclosed Class B succession mechanism. Three Item 5.02 officer/director filings landed in a five-week span (February 10, February 17, March 16, 2026), the substance of which I could not resolve. An **Item 5.01 "Changes in Control of Registrant" 8-K filed June 1, 2026 could not be retrieved** (EDGAR returned HTTP 403; three searches returned nothing). Triangulation establishes only that no merger or acquisition occurred: Koch signed the July 23 10-Q as Chairman/President/CEO, Class B shares outstanding were unchanged at 2,068,000 between December 27, 2025 and June 27, 2026, buybacks continued through July 17, and the stock trades on live analyst targets rather than at a deal price. A technical transfer of the Class B holder of record (trust or family entity) is a plausible inference; an index-classification artifact is equally consistent. This should not be treated as established, and it is the single largest unresolved governance item in the file.

**Incentive impairment.** 237,370 options outstanding at a weighted-average strike of $317.69 (44% above spot); 151,766 exercisable at $363.50 (51% above spot); 211,007 non-vested shares/RSUs at $237.52 grant-date fair value; 53,206 options forfeited or expired in H1 2026. The company attributes its higher ex-litigation effective tax rate (32.3% H1 2026 versus 29.2%) primarily to non-deductible stock compensation, so underwater equity is costing real cash. Maximum contingent dilution from these awards is 448,377 shares, 4.36% of shares outstanding and 4.17% post-issuance ownership dilution, but 6,930 options and 62,024 RSUs are performance-based with conditions unmet, and essentially all options are far out of the money and would not be exercised at $178, so realistic near-term dilution is well below the maximum.

**Other disclosed items.** Single-source suppliers for certain flavorings, crowns and labels; unhedged Euro-denominated hop and apple contracts; total contractual obligations of $222.7M at June 27, 2026 with $138.1M falling in the remainder of 2026, explicitly excluding tariffs.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 38.9% sits essentially on top of 38.0% one-year realized volatility (spread 0.9%), at the 30th percentile within the chain, with a flat term structure (38.9% near, 37.7% at six months). The market is pricing no near-term event premium despite a pending post-trial ruling and appeal. Skew is call-heavy (-18.8% at the nine-day tenor, -1.6% and -3.6% further out), and put/call open interest has collapsed from 4.05 in mid-June to 0.82, so positioning has rotated from defensive to modestly call-tilted. Combined with 23.8% short interest and 4.8 days to cover, that configuration argues the bearish view is crowded and that any favorable surprise — an appeal win, a depletions inflection, an expanded authorization — would be amplified. It does not change the fundamental read, and the flat, fairly-priced term structure means there is no cheap options expression of either side.

**Risk factors: 4.2** — the litigation is accrued and covered, and the 5-year drawdown means much is priced, but a binary appeal, an unquantified tariff step-up, opaque governance filings and near-zero retention value in outstanding equity are a dense cluster of unresolved items.

## Management Quality

Koch's decision to take no salary, bonus or equity is genuine alignment and is the strongest positive here. Against it:

- **Capital allocation timing.** FY2025 repurchases averaged $222.17/share (896,521 shares, $199.2M); 2026 year-to-date averaged $205.97 (262,665 shares, $54.1M), with disclosed monthly averages peaking at $240.33 in the March 29-May 2 window before falling to $175.08 by late June. Mark-to-market against $178.33, the combined shortfall is roughly $46M, about 2.5% of market cap. The offsetting reading is legitimate: retirement is permanent, share count is down 15.7% since FY2022, and net revenue per diluted share rose ~6% while absolute net revenue fell ~6%. Both are true. With $174M of authorization left and retained earnings at $5.2M, the pace almost certainly slows.
- **Pay versus performance, from the company's own proxy (filed April 10, 2026).** $100 invested at FY2020 year-end was worth **$19.42** at FY2025 year-end versus **$127.18** for the peer group, roughly 107 percentage points of cumulative underperformance. NEO average total compensation rose to $6,800,711 in FY2025 from $1,906,263, roughly 3.6x, with Spillane's partial-year FY2025 total at $8,892,835 and a large October 2025 grant to COO Philip Hodges (13,186 RSUs plus 88,268 options). The FY2020 starting point was the seltzer-bubble peak, which exaggerates operational deterioration; the relative gap remains severe. The proxy extract reports $0.00 PEO compensation for 2021-2023, almost certainly a multi-PEO column artifact, so those figures should not be relied on.
- **Turnover.** Three CEOs in roughly 30 months; Mariah Calagione retired in 2025; three additional Item 5.02 filings in early 2026.
- **Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director across the Form 4 record from roughly May 2025 through May 2026, with the stock 51% below the average exercisable strike. The only acquisition for value was Cynthia Fisher's April 2, 2026 option exercise of 1,560 shares at $157.58. Otherwise: routine March 1, 2026 RSU grants and same-day withholding dispositions at $226.78, May 2026 director grants, and Spillane's post-departure exercise/sale of 3,120 shares at $205-206 in November 2025. Founder-controlled companies often have thin insider-buying culture and windows may have been restricted through the trial period, so I read this as neutral-to-mildly-negative rather than a strong bearish signal.

**Management quality: 4.0.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$215, +21%)

The appeal succeeds or the judgment is materially reduced, releasing roughly $14/share of after-tax value from the accrual and removing the overhang that has kept generalists away. Depletions stabilize toward -2% as Sun Cruiser scales past Twisted Tea's decline, helped by the federal hemp-beverage restrictions effective after November 2026 removing an unregulated RTD share-taker. Gross margin holds at the upper half of the 48.5%-50% guide as owned production passes 90% and Q4 shortfall fees fall. Tariffs are relieved rather than escalated. Non-GAAP EPS reaches $10.50-$11.00 in FY2027 on a share count 3% lower, and the multiple re-rates to 19-20x, near where the peer group trades, as the shrinking-forever narrative is challenged. Short interest of 23.8% with 4.8 days to cover amplifies the move.

### Bear Case (~$120, -33%)

Depletions worsen from -6% toward -8% as Twisted Tea's decline steepens and Coke, Pepsi, Monster and Mark Anthony contest the flavored shelf with far larger budgets. Gross margin plateaus and then compresses as aluminum, energy and freight inflation outrun a cost program that has already captured most of its gains, with tariffs landing at or above the $30M top of the guided range. The A&P cut fails to hold: the company either restores spending (EPS falls) or does not (volumes fall faster). Non-GAAP EPS drops to $7.00-$7.50. The judgment stands and ~$143M leaves in cash, the $174M authorization is exhausted with retained earnings negative, and the buyback support disappears. A 15x multiple on $7.25 gives ~$109; call it $120 with net cash.

### Base Case (~$165, -7%)

Depletions run -4% to -5% in FY2027, pricing recovers roughly half, gross margin plateaus near 49-50%, and non-GAAP EPS lands $9.00-$9.50 with a 2-3% share-count reduction partially offsetting. Apply 17-18x and adjust for a 60-70% probability that ~$14/share of after-tax litigation cash is paid: **$165**. Probability-weighted (50% base, 25% bull, 25% bear): **-6.8%** over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE. Horizon: 12 months.**

**Base case $165 (-7.5% from $178.33) | Bull $215 (+20.6%) | Bear $120 (-32.7%) | Probability-weighted ~-7%.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength rather than into weakness. The stock has traded $158.68-$264.46 over 52 weeks and has twice bounced off the mid-$160s in 2026; use rallies toward $195-$205 — the analyst median and the 2026 buyback average — to reduce. Retain no more than a residual position pending the post-trial ruling. Do not add on the FCF yield alone: the 9.7-11.8% yield is real but is attached to a revenue base declining 3-4% with the margin lever nearly spent, and the buyback that has been converting flat cash flow into rising per-share metrics has $174M of authorization left against a $1,835M capitalization.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD (any two).**
1. Depletions improve to -2% or better for two consecutive quarters, confirming that Sun Cruiser and Angry Orchard are offsetting Twisted Tea, and that the A&P reduction did not cost volume.
2. A favorable post-trial ruling or appellate outcome that reverses or materially reduces the $175.5M damages award, releasing the accrual.
3. Full-year consensus stops falling — the current-year and next-year estimates flat-to-up over a 90-day window after four consecutive negative windows (currently -9.4% and -7.8%).
4. Board expansion of the repurchase authorization beyond $1.6B combined with disclosed repurchases below $180/share.

**Upgrade trigger to ACCUMULATE.** All of the above plus gross margin sustained at 50%+ with A,P&S stabilizing at or below 31% of net revenue, evidencing that the margin gain can be banked rather than reinvested.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID (any one).**
1. Depletions deteriorate below -8% in any quarter, or FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance is cut below $8.50.
2. Gross margin falls below 47% in any quarter, indicating the insourcing benefit has reversed under aluminum/energy/tariff pressure.
3. The post-trial motion and appeal fail and the ~$191M is paid while depletions remain worse than -5%, which removes both the balance-sheet cushion and the volume case.
4. Resolution of the June 1, 2026 Item 5.01 filing reveals a substantive change in Class B control, or a Koch departure without a disclosed succession mechanism.
5. Tariff costs guided above $35M for FY2027.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 print (late October) is the first hard test: it should show whether the $20M A&P reduction cost volume, and management has guided Q3 shipments down low-to-mid single digits with the most meaningful margin improvement pushed to Q4. The district court ruling on the June 8 post-trial motion (briefing completed July 23) is the higher-impact and less predictable catalyst and could land at any time.

## Conclusion

The prior three reviews carried AVOID on a declining score path (4.9 → 4.3 → 4.0). The evidence in this cycle argues for a modest upgrade to REDUCE, and the reason is not the price — at $178.33 the stock is essentially where it stood at the June review ($176.11). The change is in the assessment of tail risk and downside support. The May 26 amended judgment reduced pre-judgment interest by $21.1M; the $192.6M accrual is covered 2.2x by $415.5M of cash-plus-revolver liquidity with zero borrowings; the balance-sheet issue is a value transfer of roughly 8% of market cap rather than a solvency question; and at 6.7-7.5x EV/EBITDA with a 9.7-11.8% FCF yield, the enterprise-value support is more substantial than a 4.0 score implied.

That does not make it ownable. The operating picture deteriorated in this quarter: depletions worsened to -6%, the second consecutive EPS miss (-25.1%) followed a -17.5% miss, current-year and next-year consensus were cut 5.6% and 5.2% in 30 days, and the non-GAAP guidance range was preserved by removing $20M of planned brand investment and $10M of capex from a portfolio in which the two largest brands are both declining. At 18.8x the guided non-GAAP midpoint, SAM trades at roughly the Consumer Staples peer median without the peers' 4.1% dividend, and the corrected 10.29M share count removes about a quarter of the apparent cheapness that a Class-A-only capitalization implies. Management quality and competitive trajectory are both weak here: no open-market insider buying with the stock 51% below the average exercisable option strike, $46M of mark-to-market on buybacks executed 15-35% above spot, three CEOs in 30 months, an unretrievable change-in-control filing, and a five-year total shareholder return of -80.6% against a peer group at +27.2% disclosed in the company's own proxy.

Existing holders should use rallies toward $195-$205 to reduce. Prospective buyers should wait for two consecutive quarters of depletions at -2% or better, or a favorable appellate outcome, before treating the cash-flow yield as a reason to own rather than a reason the downside is bounded. The 23.8% short interest and call-tilted options positioning also make this a poor place to be short.
