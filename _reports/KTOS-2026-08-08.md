## Executive Summary

Kratos is delivering the revenue growth its shareholders were promised and none of the earnings. Q2 FY2026 revenue rose 30.5% to $458.8M, with 19.1% organic growth, and total remaining performance obligations reached $2.084B, up roughly 30% organically year over year. The same quarter produced a **GAAP operating loss of $1.6M**, versus $3.7M of operating income a year earlier. First-half GAAP operating income fell to $3.1M from $10.3M despite revenue up 26.9%. Q2 pretax income of $7.0M is entirely explained by $10.2M of net interest income on the $1.44B cash balance raised in February; subtracting it leaves $(3.2)M. Free cash flow was $(137.4)M in FY2025 and is guided to a use of $(85)–(105)M in FY2026, with management stating the investment program continues "at least into" FY2027.

The stock has round-tripped violently: $134 in January, $43.09 on July 30, and $60.77 now — a 41% gain in six sessions following the August 4 print and guidance raise. That rally has consumed the margin of safety that existed three weeks ago. Enterprise value of $10.10B ($11.40B market cap less $1,437.6M cash plus $139.9M of finance lease liabilities) equals **5.7x** guided FY2026 revenue, **57.9x** guided Adjusted EBITDA, and **89.8x** Adjusted EBITDA net of the $60–64M of guided stock compensation. Consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.12 for FY2027 puts the shares at **54x** forward earnings on a measure that adds back stock compensation and depreciation in a business capitalizing $125–135M annually.

The decisive near-term issue is arithmetic. FY2026 guidance midpoints less H1 actuals less Q3 guidance midpoints imply Q4 revenue of ~$480M, Adjusted EBITDA of ~$55.1M (an 11.5% margin versus 8.3% actual in Q2), GAAP operating income of ~$13.4M (more than four times all of H1), and an H2 operating cash flow swing of roughly $117M. Management's explanation — H2 receipt of long-lead customer-funded hardware and funding normalization after the late-2025/early-2026 shutdown and continuing resolution — is credible for a fixed-price hardware business with genuine Q4 concentration. It also leaves no room for slippage. We move from HOLD to **REDUCE**: the business is real, the backlog is real, the budget tailwind is real, and the price now embeds all of it plus option value on programs Kratos does not yet hold.

## Company Overview

Kratos operates two reportable segments across roughly 4,300 employees in about 14 countries. **Kratos Government Solutions (KGS)** was 82.8% of Q2 revenue ($379.7M) and comprises five businesses: Defense and Rocket Support Services, Microwave Electronics, Space Training & Cyber, C5ISR/Modular Systems, and Kratos Turbine Technologies. **Unmanned Systems (KUS)** was 17.2% ($79.1M), down from 21.7% of FY2025 revenue — the segment that anchors the equity narrative is shrinking as a share of the business.

The franchises are genuine. Kratos holds sole- or single-source positions on the USAF BQM-167 (production year 21), USN BQM-177 (year 7) and Army MQM-178 (year 13) aerial targets. Its Israel-based microwave electronics content is designed into Iron Dome, Arrow, Patriot, THAAD, IBCS and IFPC. It is prime on MACH-TB 2.0, valued at up to $1.45B *if all options are exercised* over five years, and holds a $579M single-award SATCOM IDIQ from the Space Force. OpenSpace is a credible virtualized satellite ground software platform. The GEK800/GEK1500 turbofan work proceeds under a formal teaming agreement with GE Aerospace.

The strategic model matters more than the product list. Kratos deliberately funds development, capital equipment and in some cases finished production units ahead of contract award — over $310M cumulatively in unmanned systems since 2013, ~$315M in space/satellite/cyber since 2019. FY2025 capex included $38.4M for two Valkyrie production lots built before any contract existed; FY2026 allocates $23–24M more. This is the source of both the company's speed-to-market differentiation and its persistently negative free cash flow. The 10-K discloses the risk in its own words: if "the anticipated contract award does not materialize... our investment in the equipment or materials might be at risk if we cannot efficiently resell them."

The bridge from 30.5% revenue growth to a GAAP operating loss is identifiable: stock compensation rose 90% to $16.3M in the quarter (81% to $31.3M in H1), intangible amortization rose to $10.1M from $2.8M on the Nomad and Orbit acquisitions, and SG&A expanded to 16.0% of revenue from 15.4%.

Adjusted EPS of $0.21 beat a $0.13 non-GAAP consensus by 51.6%. Non-GAAP adjusted net income of $39.4M was roughly nine times GAAP net income of $4.4M, bridged by $10.1M intangible amortization, $12.0M depreciation, $16.3M stock compensation, $2.4M capitalized development amortization and $0.8M acquisition items. Two of those five — stock compensation and depreciation — are recurring economic costs at a company paying a rising share of compensation in equity and capitalizing heavily. The company itself cautions that investors should not infer these costs are unusual or non-recurring.

Kratos defines free cash flow as OCF less capex *plus* proceeds from asset sales, which produces $(62.0)M for H1 rather than $(75.5)M. Those proceeds ($13.5M in H1) come principally from selling company-owned Valkyries built with prior-period capex — monetization of capitalized spending rather than operating cash generation.

The H1 working-capital drag was concentrated in unbilled receivables, which consumed $104.3M of operating cash; the balance rose to $405.4M from $292.4M, the difference reflecting balances acquired with Orbit and Nomad. DSO improved to 114 days from 124 at year end but remains well above the 104 days at December 2024.

### Balance sheet

Cash of $1,437.6M against zero funded debt, total equity of $3,426.8M, current ratio 4.06, Altman Z-Score 4.72. The $177.5M Term Loan A was repaid in July 2025 with equity proceeds, and the 2022 facility was replaced in February 2026 with an undrawn five-year $300M revolver at SOFR + 100–200bp.

Two qualifications. First, "zero debt" excludes **$139.9M of finance lease liabilities** at present value, against $212.8M of total contractual payments, a 14.62-year weighted-average term and a 6.12% discount rate — with **$42.3M of new obligations recognized non-cash in H1 alone**. These are long-dated fixed facility obligations and belong in enterprise value; they carry no covenant or refinancing risk comparable to term debt, but they are not nothing. Second, goodwill of $871.2M plus intangibles of $220.8M equals **26.7% of total assets** at a company with $253.3M of cumulative historical goodwill impairments already embedded in the rollforward.

Financial health scores 5.5: liquidity and solvency are genuinely strong and the company is fully funded for its 2026–2027 investment program, but earnings quality is poor, cash conversion is negative for a second consecutive year, and the funding source has been equity issuance rather than internal generation.

## Growth Analysis

The growth is real and broadening. Q2 organic growth by business line: Defense & Rocket Support **+50.2%**, Turbine Technologies **+43.3%**, Microwave Products **+29.5%**, Space/Training/Cyber **+8.7%**, Unmanned Systems **+8.1%**. Total RPO reached $2.084B (funded $1.572B), up from $1.414B a year earlier; stripping ~$243.4M of acquired backlog leaves ~30.2% organic growth. LTM book-to-bill was 1.3x on $1.990B of bookings.

The budget backdrop is as favorable as it has been in a generation. The FY2026 Consolidated Appropriations Act produced roughly $1 trillion of national security spending, and the April 2026 FY2027 request totals $1.5 trillion with named priorities — Golden Dome, space/SATCOM, unmanned/AI, missiles, radars, air defense, nuclear triad — that map almost exactly onto Kratos' portfolio. The Secretary of War's stated intent to expend the entire $156B of OBBBA defense funding within FY2026 supports near-term order flow.

Three qualifications temper the Growth Prospects score at 6.5 rather than higher.

**Mix.** Roughly $155M of FY2026 guided revenue (~8.7%) comes from Nomad and Orbit. Q2 growth was 30.5% total, 19.1% organic. The fastest-growing lines are rocket support, turbines and microwave — not tactical drones.

**KUS is flat where it matters.** Unmanned Systems backlog was $374.6M at Q2 versus $375.4M at Q1, a 1.0x quarterly book-to-bill, with 8.1% organic growth. Management raised FY2026 KUS organic growth expectations to ~10% and targets ~40 Valkyries annually from the start of 2028, plus 3,000 small jet engines in 2027. Those are 2027–2028 events, not current run-rates.

**Estimate revisions are positive but modest.** Current-year consensus rose 6.2% over 90 days to $0.82 and next-year 2.7% to $1.12. Next-quarter estimates actually fell 2.1%. This is constructive earnings momentum, not the +30% revision velocity that signals a systematically underestimated inflection. Four consecutive non-GAAP beats (10.1%, 12.0%, 16.8%, 51.6%) support confidence in the adjusted numbers; they say nothing about GAAP conversion, which has moved the other way.

On consensus non-GAAP EPS, the shares trade at **74.1x** current year ($0.82) and **54.3x** next year ($1.12). Trailing GAAP P/E on FY2025 net income of $22.0M is roughly 518x.

Peer context is genuinely ambiguous. The sector screen shows median P/E of 46.4x, with DRS at 46.4x, HXL 64.7x, SARO 29.5x and HII 19.6x. KTOS at 54x FY2027 *non-GAAP* is not wildly out of line with DRS at 46x — except that DRS generates positive free cash flow and reports meaningful GAAP earnings, while KTOS' non-GAAP measure adds back roughly $0.32/share of stock compensation plus depreciation on a $125–135M annual capex program. On EV/sales, KTOS at 5.7x FY2026 and ~4.8x on an assumed $2.1B FY2027 sits above DRS at roughly 3.4x, with materially lower margins.

What does the multiple require? Reverse-engineering: on FY2027 revenue of ~$2.1B at 4.6x EV/sales — a premium to DRS reflecting faster growth — enterprise value is $9,660M. Adding ~$1.11B of projected year-end 2027 cash (after two more years of guided burn and Prometheus funding) and subtracting leases gives roughly **$56 per share on ~191M shares**. Getting to today's $60.77 requires about 5.0x forward sales. Getting materially above requires either a step-change in margins that H1 has not evidenced, or a CCA/Valkyrie award that Kratos does not currently hold.

Three points on framing. First, prior appreciation is not a valuation input, and we are not penalizing the stock for having risen 41% off $43.09 — we are noting that at $47.87 three weeks ago the same guidance implied ~4.5x EV/sales and a visible margin of safety, and at $60.77 it does not. Second, the estimate-revision signal here is real but modest, and it arrives on a business already at a premium multiple — the guardrail applies: at 54x forward non-GAAP with negative FCF, beats are expected and misses are punished asymmetrically. Third, analyst targets provide no anchor: the published range runs $60 to $150 with a mean of $107.14, and within a two-week window in early August Piper Sandler upgraded to Overweight with a **$75** target while Stifel cut to **$115** and B. Riley held **$128**. A $75-to-$128 spread on the same stock in the same fortnight is a statement about how little consensus exists on the appropriate multiple, not independent confirmation of upside. Nineteen of twenty-one ratings are Buy or Strong Buy; that consensus has been in place through a 54.6% drawdown from January.

Valuation scores **3.0**. There is no identifiable mispricing in the company's favor at this price, and cheapness is not the issue — the issue is that a 1.9% GAAP-operating-margin business consuming $95M of cash annually is capitalized at 5.7x sales on the strength of option value.

## Competitive Landscape

The FY2025 10-K names, unusually explicitly, a set of venture-backed entrants alongside the traditional primes: **Anduril, XBow, Shield AI, Castelion, Ursa Major and Bee Hive**. These are the companies attacking precisely Kratos' historical differentiation — affordable, mass-producible hardware delivered fast — and they now have access to capital on terms Kratos cannot match without dilution.

The clarifying event was CCA Increment 1. In June 2026 the Air Force awarded first production contracts to **General Atomics (YFQ-42A) and Anduril (YFQ-44A)**; Northrop Grumman's self-financed offering was not selected. Kratos was not an Increment 1 participant. Anduril's first YFQ-44A rolled off the Arsenal-1 line on July 28, 2026 and the type live-fired an AMRAAM on July 15. Kratos' CCA-adjacent exposure runs through Northrop Grumman, which won the Marine Corps MUX TACAIR award combining Northrop's autonomy with Kratos' Valkyrie air vehicle. Kratos is the supplier; Northrop controls the program and its economics.

CCA Increment 2 is the live catalyst — nine companies initially selected in February 2026, more than twenty still eligible, and a down-select reportedly planned before October 2026. **Kratos' participation is not confirmed by any filing or company statement**, which matters because the US reporting unit's goodwill valuation explicitly assumes "the development of the high performance UCAS product is successful and we are awarded future contracts for new tactical unmanned aircraft systems." That is $124.8M of goodwill contingent on an award, inside a $871M goodwill balance at a company that has already written off $253.3M.

Procurement reform cuts both ways, and the 10-K says so. Emphasis on commercial products and non-FAR vehicles such as Other Transaction Authority "has facilitated, and may continue to facilitate, participation by new and emerging market entrants" with "lower cost, more agile operating structures and access to capital and talent." OTA awards also carry limited protest rights and shift development-cost risk onto the contractor. Kratos was the beneficiary of speed-and-affordability procurement for a decade; the same reforms now admit better-capitalized competitors.

Against that: the target drone franchises are durable sole-source positions measured in decades, MACH-TB prime status is a real asset, and Microwave Products growing 29.5% organically on Iron Dome/Patriot/THAAD content is a defensible high-margin franchise. The GE Aerospace collaboration has generated **inception-to-date revenue of $34.5M against cost of sales of $34.5M — zero cumulative gross margin** — which is unremarkable at the development stage of a shared risk/reward arrangement but is not yet a profit franchise.

Competitive position scores **5.5**: stable-to-improving in rocket motors, turbines and microwave; deteriorating in the tactical-drone lane that carries the valuation.

## Risk Assessment

**Q4 concentration is the dominant near-term risk.** Reconciling guidance against actuals: FY revenue midpoint $1,780M less H1 $829.8M leaves $950.2M for H2; Q3 guidance of $470M leaves ~$480M for Q4. FY Adjusted EBITDA midpoint $174.5M less H1 $76.9M less Q3 $42.5M leaves **~$55.1M for Q4, an 11.5% margin versus 8.3% actual in Q2** — a ~320bp step-up in two quarters. FY GAAP operating income midpoint $19M less H1 $3.1M less Q3 $2.5M leaves **~$13.4M in Q4**, more than four times all of H1. FY OCF guidance of $30–50M against H1 of $(38.4)M implies **+$78M in H2**, a ~$117M swing, while capex must run ~$93M in H2 versus $37.1M in H1.

**Goodwill impairment risk is explicitly award-contingent** (above), with goodwill plus intangibles at 26.7% of assets and FY2027 scheduled amortization of $40.2M suppressing GAAP income regardless.

**Acquisition quality.** Of the $501.3M spent on Orbit ($352.7M cash) and Nomad ($148.6M), **$458.2M (91%) landed in goodwill and intangibles**, including a $58.7M trade-name intangible at Orbit equal to 16.6% of its purchase price. Both units posted Q2 GAAP operating losses (Nomad $(1.7)M, Orbit $(3.5)M). Pro forma H1 FY2026 as if both had been owned from December 2024 shows a **net loss of $(4.8)M** against reported net income of $16.3M — though with pro forma revenue only $10.5M above reported and pretax income swinging $21.1M, the adjustment is dominated by full-period amortization and removal of interest income on deployed cash, so it should be read partly as a purchase-accounting artifact. Orbit's implied sequential swing from roughly +$3.4M of Q1 operating income to $(3.5)M in Q2 is unexplained in the disclosure and is a specific question for the Q3 call.

**Israel.** KGS foreign subsidiary assets rose to $705.1M from $300.5M; long-lived assets in Israel are $75.2M (~16% of consolidated); over 700 personnel. Management cited Shekel strength as an active margin drag twice in the August 4 release and as tempering Q3 margin expansion. Regional conflict exposure is disclosed in the risk factors and is live.

**DCAA exposure with no accrual.** The 10-Q states DCAA "is closely examining and questioning certain of the established and disclosed practices that it had previously audited and accepted," with no estimable range of loss and **no recorded liability**. An adverse outcome produces retroactive cost adjustments and refunds.

**Two new policy risks.** An executive order that "may limit corporate distributions, share repurchases, and executive compensation incentives during periods of defense contractor underperformance" is a genuine constraint at a company whose CEO pay is heavily TSR-linked. The DoW "Go Direct-to-Supplier" initiative is a potential source of non-dilutive capital or of government influence at a company already 69% dependent on U.S. government revenue.

**Concentration.** U.S. Navy 17.7% and U.S. Air Force 12.6% of FY2025 revenue; four named programs "could represent a large portion of our future revenues," including **Sentinel/GBSD**, whose current cost and schedule status we could not verify from a current source and which we therefore flag as an unresolved exposure rather than a quantified one.

**Backlog coverage.** $2.084B against $1,780M of guided revenue is 1.17x forward coverage; funded backlog alone is 0.88x. The $1.45B MACH-TB figure, the $579M SATCOM IDIQ and the $15.0B bid pipeline are opportunity measures, not backlog — only funded task orders enter the latter.

**Section 382.** The 10-K notes a 2010 ownership change limiting NOL use and that future equity offerings or stock-funded acquisitions "could also cause an 'ownership change.'" Kratos has since completed two large offerings plus a stock-funded acquisition against a $625.6M accumulated deficit; whether these aggregate toward a new limitation is undisclosed.

Risk factors score **4.0** — a dense set of specific, near-dated binaries against a fortress balance sheet.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is positioned for continuation, not stress. Near-term ATM IV of 62.9% sits **below** 1-year realized volatility of 72.2% (a −9.4% spread) at the 5th percentile within the current chain, term structure is in contango (62.9% near versus 68.7% at six months), put/call skew is balanced at 1.6%, and put/call open interest is **0.42** — call-tilted. Since the July 22 snapshot, ATM IV collapsed from 81.9% to 62.9% while the price rose 26.9% and P/C open interest fell from 1.03 to 0.42. That is post-event vol crush plus a decisive shift in positioning toward calls.

Two implications. Complacency is the risk: the market is not pricing a Q4 execution failure or an adverse Increment 2 outcome, and the nearest expiry implies only ±7.0%. Cheap near-term vol also means downside protection is inexpensive for holders who want to retain exposure through the October down-select and the Q3 print rather than sell outright. Short interest of 6.4% of float and 1.8 days to cover means there is no crowded-short dynamic to squeeze; the recovery has been genuine buying.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $95 (+56%)

Q4 delivers the guided ~$480M of revenue at an 11.5% Adjusted EBITDA margin, validating that H1 margin weakness was long-lead-hardware and shutdown timing rather than structural fixed-price erosion. H2 operating cash flow swings positive by ~$78M as unbilled receivables convert. Kratos appears in the CCA Increment 2 down-select before October, resolving the option value that underpins the multiple and de-risking the US reporting unit's goodwill. The 2027 ramps arrive on schedule: 3,000 small jet engines for cruise missile applications, Prometheus Energetics rocket motor production at Crane, Indiana, and ~40 Valkyries annually from the start of 2028. FY2027 revenue reaches ~$2.2B with Adjusted EBITDA margin at 11–12%, and the market pays 6.0–6.5x forward sales on a company that has finally proven it can convert growth into cash. Microwave Products continues 25%+ organic growth as global air defense demand persists across three active conflict theaters. B. Riley's $128 and the $150 high target become reachable if a CCA award lands.

### Bear case — $40 (−34%)

Q4 slips. Long-lead hardware arrives in Q1 FY2027, revenue lands near $430M and Adjusted EBITDA near $45M, FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA misses the $173M low end and GAAP operating income comes in near zero. Operating cash flow does not swing positive, FCF use exceeds the guided $(105)M, and the cash balance funds a third consecutive year of burn. Kratos is absent from the Increment 2 down-select; the US reporting unit's explicit goodwill assumption fails and a portion of $124.8M is written off, reviving the memory of $253.3M in prior impairments. Fixed-price margin erosion proves structural as skilled-labor costs continue outrunning contract pricing, and the Shekel keeps compressing the Israeli microwave business. The multiple compresses to 3.0x FY2027 sales — still a premium to HII at 19.6x earnings — and the shares revisit the July low. Anduril and General Atomics consolidate the CCA franchise; Kratos' Valkyrie remains a subcontracted air vehicle on a Northrop-controlled Marine Corps program.

### Base case — $58 (−4.6%)

Q4 delivers most but not all of the implied step-up: FY2026 revenue lands near the guidance midpoint, Adjusted EBITDA near the low end, GAAP operating income in the high single digits to low teens of millions, and FCF use near $(100)M. FY2027 revenue reaches ~$2.1B on continued rocket support, turbine and microwave momentum, with KUS growing 10–12%. Increment 2 resolves ambiguously — participation without a production award, or exclusion offset by the Northrop MUX TACAIR position. Free cash flow remains negative into FY2027 as guided. The market continues paying roughly 4.5–5.0x forward sales, and the shares trade in a wide band around today's price while the equity story waits on 2027–2028 ramps.

Probability-weighting base 50% / bull 25% / bear 25% produces an expected return of roughly **+3%** with a ±45% dispersion. That is approximately fair value with materially unfavorable near-term asymmetry immediately after a 41% six-session rally.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** This breaks a four-report HOLD streak, and the reasons are specific and new since the July 22 assessment: (1) Q2 produced a GAAP operating loss on 30.5% revenue growth, with H1 operating income down 70% year over year — margin conversion deteriorated rather than stalled; (2) the price is 27% higher on guidance that was raised modestly but concentrated almost entirely into the December quarter; (3) the base case now sits at or slightly below spot, versus a visible margin of safety at $47.87 three weeks ago.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength. Holders with a position established below $50 should reduce by roughly half now while near-term implied volatility is cheap relative to realized (62.9% vs 72.2%), retaining a residual position through the two catalysts below rather than exiting entirely — the defense budget environment and 30% organic backlog growth are real, and the balance sheet removes solvency risk. Prospective buyers should wait. Cheap near-term options make protective puts through the November tenor a reasonable alternative to outright sale for holders with large embedded gains.

**Base/bull/bear from $60.77:** base $58 (−4.6%), bull $95 (+56.3%), bear $40 (−34.2%).

**Timeframe:** two decision points inside five months. The CCA Increment 2 down-select is reported for before **October 2026**. The Q3 FY2026 print (early November) tests the Q3 guidance of ~$470M revenue and ~$42.5M Adjusted EBITDA and, critically, whether H2 operating cash flow has begun the required ~$78M swing. The Q4/FY2026 print in late February 2027 is the terminal test.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. Q3 delivers ≥$470M revenue with Adjusted EBITDA margin ≥9.0% **and** quarterly operating cash flow positive — evidence the unbilled receivable balance ($405.4M) is converting.
2. Confirmed inclusion in the CCA Increment 2 down-select, or any named prime award for Valkyrie or a Valkyrie derivative that resolves the US reporting unit's goodwill assumption.
3. A price near $47–50 with FY2026 guidance intact, which restores roughly 4.5x forward EV/sales and a genuine margin of safety.
4. Any open-market purchase cluster by three or more C-suite insiders — there have been none in the Form 4 window through August 5, 2026.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**

## Conclusion

Kratos is executing on revenue and backlog while failing to convert either into GAAP earnings or cash. Management raised $1.38B at $84.00 in February and insiders have sold in one direction since, with the CEO expanding his 10b5-1 program by 87.5% ten weeks after adopting it — much of it mechanical given $59.1M of H1 tax withholding on vesting, but uniformly one-way.
