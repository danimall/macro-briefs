**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Maximus is a government business-process outsourcer whose stock has fallen from a 52-week high of $100 to roughly $56, a drawdown of about 44%. The trailing and forward P/E both sit near 8.4x, and the current price implies roughly 6.2x the $9.07 consensus next-year EPS estimate, a steep discount to the 18.2x peer median for data-processing/outsourced-services names. The headline picture is a company trading like a structurally impaired business while reporting rising EPS and expanding margins.

The reality underneath is more two-sided. Federal Services, 56% of revenue and the margin engine, lifted operating margin to 15.3% in FY25 and 17.6% in Q2 FY26, driven by efficiency gains in the VA medical-disability-examination (MDE) franchise and a mix shift away from low-margin FEMA disaster work. Management has raised EPS guidance even while cutting the FY26 revenue guide to ~$5.28B, a year-on-year decline. The market's distrust of this combination is rational: H1 FY26 revenue fell 4.1%, operating cash flow turned negative $54.9M, days sales outstanding spiked to 78 days (102 days excluding a $350M receivables-sale facility), and accounts receivable ballooned from $898M to $1.11B in six months on payment delays tied to a large federal contract. Debt rose $200M to $1.55B as the revolver bridged the working-capital gap and funded buybacks.

This report downgrades MMS to HOLD from the prior BUY. The reversal rests on specific fundamental deterioration since the last review: confirmed revenue decline in the FY26 guide, negative operating cash flow, a DSO spike, and consensus EPS estimates that have begun ticking down across all forward periods. Offsetting these are conviction open-market insider buying by the CEO and CFO at $75–79 (well above today's price), a deeply discounted multiple, ample covenant headroom (1.75x leverage vs a 4.0x ceiling), and a genuine but distant OBBBA-driven catalyst for the state-side business from ~2027. The swing factor is whether H2 FY26 cash collections normalize as management asserts. Until that resolves, the evidence is genuinely balanced.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1975 and incorporated in Virginia, Maximus provides tech-enabled administration of government health and human-services programs to over 100M citizens across the US, UK, Canada, and the Middle East, with roughly 37,200 employees plus ~9,300 contingent workers. The business runs in three segments (FY25 revenue):

- **U.S. Federal Services (56%, $3.07B)** — the crown jewel. Clinical services (VA MDEs), CMS contact centers (1-800-MEDICARE, ACA marketplace), Federal Student Aid loan servicing, FEMA disaster support, and IT modernization. Operating margin expanded to 15.3% in FY25 and management targets ~17.5% in FY26.
- **U.S. Services (32%, $1.76B)** — state/local Medicaid/CHIP eligibility and enrollment (the largest US provider), ACA exchanges, TANF, child support, and long-term-care clinical assessments. In structural decline following the post-pandemic Medicaid redetermination surge.
- **Outside the U.S. (11%, $0.60B)** — UK functional assessments for DWP, the Restart employment program, Canada, and Middle East. Being deliberately shrunk via divestitures (Australia/Korea sold Dec 2024; U.S. child-support sold Dec 2025).

The competitive moat rests on decades of program expertise, security clearances and compliance know-how, scaled clinical/MDE platforms, incumbency, and government-wide acquisition-contract eligibility. Competitors include GDIT, Deloitte, Leidos, IBM, Accenture, and Booz Allen on the federal side; Conduent, Acentra, and PCG on the state side.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from $4.63B (FY22) to $5.43B (FY25, +2.4%), but the trend inflected in FY26. H1 FY26 revenue fell 4.1% YoY to $2.65B, and the full-year FY26 guide of ~$5.28B implies a decline, roughly 0.8% below consensus.

The defining feature is shrinking revenue alongside expanding margins and EPS. FY25 operating margin was 9.7% (up from 9.2%), net income $319M, diluted EPS $5.51 (adjusted $7.36). Q2 FY26 operating margin reached 11.4% with gross margin at 26.2% (up from 24.9%), and H1 FY26 adjusted diluted EPS rose to $3.92 from $3.60. Almost all of the margin gain comes from Federal Services (17.6% Q2 operating margin) through MDE technology efficiencies and the exit from low-margin FEMA work. ROE of 19.1% and ROA of 7.8% are healthy.

Cash flow is the yellow flag and the most important near-term item. FY25 operating cash flow was $429M and FCF $366M, both down from $515M/$401M in FY24. H1 FY26 operating cash flow was negative $54.9M, worse than the prior year's negative $37.3M. The cause is payment delays on a large federal contract: DSO rose from 62 days, and receivables grew $210M in six months. Maximus is bridging the gap with its revolver and an expanded receivables-purchase program; management expects H2 FY26 recovery. If federal payment dysfunction persists rather than reverses, debt continues rising and the buyback and dividend become harder to fund.

The balance sheet carries enough headroom to absorb this in the near term. Altman Z is 2.9 (grey zone) and D/E 1.43. Debt rose from $1.35B (Sept 2025), with $225M drawn on the revolver. Credit-agreement leverage is 1.75x against a 4.0x covenant ceiling, and interest coverage is 8.84x against a 3.0x minimum. The effective interest rate is 5.0% with roughly half of debt hedged, and the next large maturity ($892M principal) is in FY2029. Goodwill ($1.78B) plus intangibles (~$0.5B) total ~57% of assets, with ~$380M of intangibles tied to the VA MDE franchise, an impairment risk if a major anchor contract is lost.

Capital allocation has been aggressive: $447M of buybacks (5.8M shares) in FY25 and $142M in H1 FY26 at an average near $76, cutting share count from ~61M to ~52.5M. The dividend was raised to $0.33/quarter and the buyback authorization refreshed to $400M in May 2026. The combination of debt-funded repurchases against a shrinking top line, executed at prices now well above the current $56, is the weakest element of the capital story.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the dimension that most cleanly distinguishes winners from losers in this peer group, and for Maximus it currently points the wrong way. Revenue is contracting (−4.1% H1 FY26), the FY26 guide is a decline, and consensus EPS estimates have edged lower across every forward period: current quarter −3.3%, next quarter −1.7%, current year −0.9% (to $8.44), next year −0.5% (to $9.07). These are modest cuts, not a collapse, but the direction is negative and there is no positive estimate-revision velocity to lean on.

EPS growth is real but low-quality in composition; it is being manufactured by margin harvest and share-count reduction rather than organic revenue expansion. The Federal margin story (MDE efficiency, FEMA exit) has limits; management itself notes ~54% of revenue is performance-based and 13% fixed-price, leaving wage inflation it cannot fully escalate.

The asymmetric growth thread is OBBBA. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025) mandates 6-month rather than annual Medicaid redeterminations and adds work/community-engagement requirements (effective ~December 2026) for the adult-expansion population. Health plans are barred from administering these requirements, channeling the work to states, Maximus's exact customer base. SNAP error-rate penalties (FY2028) similarly push states toward outsourcing. Management explicitly expects a "return to organic growth towards the end of the current fiscal year." The offsetting risk is that OBBBA also cuts federal Medicaid funding, and states may pre-emptively narrow program scope; management calls the net timing and magnitude "still uncertain." This is a credible re-acceleration catalyst for U.S. Services, but it is a 2027 story with material policy uncertainty, not a near-term driver that supports a confident growth thesis today.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$56, MMS trades at ~8.4x trailing EPS, ~6.6x the $8.44 current-year estimate, and ~6.2x the $9.07 next-year estimate. Against a peer median P/E of 18.2x, the discount is roughly 54%. The dividend yield is ~2.4%.

A multiple this low for a business with 15%+ federal segment margins and 19% ROE invites a "deep value" read, and the prior review scored valuation at 8.5 on that basis. The more disciplined interpretation is that cheapness here is a function of identifiable concerns rather than a clean market error: revenue is declining, operating cash flow is negative, federal payment dysfunction is unresolved, and EPS quality is buyback-inflated. A compressed multiple that reflects genuine cash-flow and top-line deterioration is a different proposition from one that reflects a temporary sentiment dislocation.

The peer set itself is heterogeneous and the comparison is imperfect: CNXC trades at a negative multiple, VRRM at 5.5x and an 82% discount to its high, G at 9.8x. Several of these are themselves trading at distressed multiples, so the "discount to median" framing overstates the relative cheapness. The more apt comparison is to other federal-services names trading at single-digit-to-low-teens multiples on similar procurement-disruption concerns.

There is a real asymmetric case: if H2 FY26 collections normalize and OBBBA work begins flowing from 2027, the $9+ EPS base re-rates materially even to a conservative 12–14x, implying $110–125. The insider purchases at $75–79 quantify management's view of fair value well above the current price. But the catalyst is contingent and distant, and the cash-flow deterioration is current. The valuation is attractive enough to preclude an AVOID, not clean enough to anchor a BUY. I score valuation 6.0, better than fair, with the discount partly earned by genuine risk rather than purely a mispricing.

The two-analyst consensus targets ($85 low, $125 high, $105 mean) sit far above the current price and appear stale; with the stock at $56 and estimates falling, these have not been revised to reflect the FY26 guide cut and cash-flow deterioration. Treat them as lagging indicators, not as a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

Maximus's competitive trajectory is mixed. The Federal franchise is improving on the dimension that matters most for profitability: margins are expanding, and the VA MDE platform (expanded by the Honoring our PACT Act) is a durable, scaled, high-margin moat where Maximus leads. Incumbency, clearances, and compliance know-how create real switching costs in government contracting.

Against that, the state-side U.S. Services business is in structural decline post-redetermination and recently absorbed a $6.9M capitalized-software impairment following a client decision, a small but indicative softening signal. The international segment swung to operating losses in H1 FY26 (−2.3% Q2 margin) and is being deliberately shrunk. Larger competitors (Accenture, Deloitte, Leidos) bring greater resources, and AI is flagged in the 10-K as both an efficiency enabler for Maximus and a barrier-lowering threat that could let smaller competitors bid for work.

The current federal "efficiency"/DOGE-style scrutiny is genuinely double-edged. Near-term it drives procurement delays, contract re-scopings, shutdown risk, and the payment slowdowns now hitting cash flow. Longer-term it could lower barriers to private-sector participation in government services, a tailwind that also invites more competition. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving on margin but pressured on revenue and concentration, supporting a mid-range score.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer and contract concentration** is the dominant idiosyncratic risk. Roughly 60% of revenue comes from the top 10 contracts and ~20% from a single federal agency (effectively the VA MDE franchise). Loss of one anchor contract could trigger a revenue cliff and goodwill/intangible impairment, with ~$380M of intangibles tied to that franchise. Termination-for-convenience clauses sit on nearly all contracts.

**Cash collection / DSO deterioration** is the most acute current operational risk. Negative H1 OCF on a single contract's payment delays, DSO at 102 days ex-financing, and rising revolver use create a self-reinforcing dynamic if federal payment dysfunction persists.

**OBBBA** is both the asymmetric upside thread and a funding-cut risk; the net effect on revenue is timing-dependent and uncertain.

**Federal procurement turbulence** under the current administration's efficiency focus is explicitly flagged in the 10-K as driving delays, cancellations, and re-scopings.

**MOVEit litigation** is a genuine tail risk: Maximus is a named bellwether defendant in a Massachusetts MDL arising from the 2023 Progress Software zero-day breach that exposed SSNs/PHI of a significant number of individuals; roughly half the claims survived a July 2025 motion to dismiss and are in discovery. A reserve is accrued but the outcome is unpredictable.

Many of these risks are at least partially priced into the 44% drawdown. But the binary tails (anchor-contract loss, MOVEit) and the unresolved cash-flow situation are not fully discounted, supporting a below-midpoint risk score.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term stress. The put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 2.46 to 4.59 over the past month, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term ATM IV 42.5% vs 40.0% at six months), indicating the market expects an imminent catalyst, most likely the next earnings print and continued federal-payment news flow. Near-term IV at 42.5% is rich relative to 33.3% trailing realized volatility, implying a ±8.8% move on the nearest expiry. The put/call skew is balanced (1.8%), so the positioning is more about overall uncertainty than directional bearishness, but the heavy put OI accumulation alongside 11.3% short interest (3.9 days to cover) confirms institutional caution. This corroborates rather than challenges the cautious fundamental stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The market is over-discounting a temporary working-capital problem. Federal payment delays normalize in H2 FY26, restoring positive cash flow and de-risking the buyback/dividend. The cleaner, higher-margin earnings base (Federal at 17.6%, divested volatility) compounds, and OBBBA's bar on health plans administering Medicaid work requirements channels a large, sticky new revenue stream to states from ~2027, returning U.S. Services to organic growth. At 6–8x forward EPS with 19% ROE and a durable VA MDE moat, even a partial re-rating to 12–14x on a $9+ EPS base implies $110–125. The CEO and CFO buying in the open market at $75–79, rare in this name, quantifies insider conviction at levels well above today's price. Bull target: $115.

**Bear Case.** Revenue decline is structural, not cyclical. Federal "efficiency" scrutiny continues to delay payments and re-scope contracts, keeping cash flow negative and forcing debt higher to fund buybacks and the dividend. EPS growth proves to be buyback-engineered atop an eroding franchise, and the OBBBA upside fails to materialize at scale (or arrives offset by Medicaid funding cuts). A MOVEit adverse outcome or anchor-contract loss triggers goodwill/intangible impairment. The multiple stays at 7–8x because the market correctly distrusts the earnings quality, and falling estimates compress the EPS base. Bear target: $44 (−21% from current).

These cases are genuinely balanced and hinge on a single near-term variable: H2 FY26 cash collections, which is not yet resolved.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This reverses the prior BUY. The downgrade is driven by specific fundamental deterioration since the last review: a confirmed FY26 revenue-decline guide, negative H1 operating cash flow, a DSO spike to 102 days ex-financing, and negative estimate revisions across all forward periods, not by the share-price decline itself. The deep discount and insider buying preclude an AVOID; the unresolved cash flow and contracting top line preclude a BUY. The classification is uncertain because the controlling variable (cash collection normalization) is unknown and management's reacceleration thesis is contingent.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- H2 FY26 operating cash flow returns clearly positive and DSO falls back toward 70 days or below, confirming the payment delays were timing rather than structural.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive, or management confirms the late-FY26 return to organic revenue growth with a specific OBBBA contract win.
- Federal Services revenue stabilizes (flat-to-positive YoY) alongside maintained 17%+ segment margins.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Operating cash flow remains negative through Q4 FY26 with DSO above 90 days ex-financing, forcing further revolver draws and threatening the dividend/buyback.
- Loss or material re-scoping of the VA MDE or another top-10 contract, which would risk goodwill/intangible impairment.
- An adverse MOVEit MDL development that materially exceeds the accrued reserve.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly earnings print (Q3 FY26) is the natural checkpoint, given backwardated options pricing flags it as the key near-term catalyst. The OBBBA work-requirement effective date (~December 2026) and FY27 federal appropriations are the medium-term checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Maximus offers a deeply discounted multiple, expanding federal margins, conviction insider buying, and a credible long-term OBBBA catalyst, set against declining revenue, negative operating cash flow, a deteriorating receivables position, debt-funded buybacks executed well above the current price, and falling estimates. The cheapness is real but partly earned by genuine cash-flow and top-line concerns rather than a clean mispricing. The bull and bear cases hinge on the same unresolved variable: whether H2 FY26 federal payment delays normalize. Until that resolves and estimate revisions turn, the appropriate posture is to hold and monitor rather than add. The recommendation is HOLD, with the cash-flow print at the next earnings release as the decisive data point that would move this toward BUY (collections normalize) or AVOID (delays persist).
