## Executive Summary

Viper Energy is a Permian mineral and royalty owner with no employees, no drilling capital, and one reportable segment. The economics are genuinely attractive at the asset level: FY2025 operating cash flow of $1,053M against $1,395M of total operating income implies roughly 75% conversion because there is no development capex to fund, net debt of $1.618B sits at 0.6-0.9x EBITDA and 13.6% of total capitalization against a 65% covenant, and the notes do not mature until 2030 and 2035. Two of three agencies rate it investment grade. Management raised the base dividend 32% to $2.00 per Class A share effective Q3 2026 and repurchased 3.0M Class A shares for $132M during Q2.

The problem is what the Q2 print revealed about how much of a commodity upcycle actually reaches a Class A holder. Realized oil was $98.28/Bbl, up 54% year over year and 55% above the FY2025 average of $63.27. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA was $642M and consolidated adjusted net income per share was $1.78. Class A attributable adjusted EPS was $0.76 — an 8.9% miss against the $0.83 consensus. The gap between $1.78 and $0.76 is the structure: depletion stepped up 48% to $17.48/BOE after the 2025 acquisitions, cash taxes are guided at 27-30% of pre-tax income attributable to the Company, and Class A owns only 191.4M of 356.2M total units. Class A holders receive roughly 80% of the per-unit OpCo distribution (Q4 2025: $0.52 versus $0.65). A record commodity quarter converted into a consensus miss, and consensus has responded — current-quarter estimates are down 8.6% over 30 days and next-year EPS of $2.42 now sits below current-year $2.60.

The valuation follows from that. The frequently cited 6.5x EV/EBITDA annualizes a single quarter of $98 oil. Normalizing to $80-85 WTI produces consolidated EBITDA nearer $1.9-2.1B and an EV/EBITDA of 8.0-8.8x on the current $16.8B enterprise value. That is a fair multiple for a perpetual, capex-free royalty stream with an investment-grade balance sheet, not a discounted one. We continue at **HOLD**, with the score drifting to 5.5 from 5.7 in July, and classify it as an income hold: the $2.00 base dividend is covered roughly twice at $70 WTI, and normalized cash available for distribution supports an 8-9% yield. What is missing is a margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Viper owns mineral and royalty interests underlying approximately 4.46M gross royalty acres, with 90,212 net royalty acres as of July 1, 2026 (down from 96,003 at year-end 2025 after the February non-Permian divestiture and the July Riverbend addition). Interests are leased to working-interest owners who bear all drilling and operating cost. Proved reserves were 406,035 MBOE at 12/31/2025, 48% oil, 78% proved developed producing, audited by Ryder Scott. FY2026 guidance was raised on August 3 to 66.0-67.25 MBO/d of oil and 132.5-135.0 MBOE/d total.

Three structural features determine how the equity behaves and are systematically misread by screens.

**The Up-C structure.** The quoted $8.1-8.4B market capitalization reflects Class A only. At 6/30/2026 there were 191.4M Class A and 164.8M Class B shares (each paired with an OpCo Unit, exchangeable 1-for-1). Total units of 356.2M at $42.50 is $15.14B of equity value; adding $1.618B of net debt gives an enterprise value of roughly **$16.76B**, about 2.1x the headline figure. Consolidated EBITDA, revenue and assets are reported at 100%, so any multiple built on the Class A market cap is internally inconsistent.

**The Diamondback relationship.** Diamondback held 42.1% of fully diluted voting power at 12/31/2025, falling to 38.9% at 3/31/2026 after selling roughly 12.9M converted shares at $45.69 for approximately $589M gross. Diamondback operated about 35% of net royalty acreage but generated 55% of FY2025 royalty income, because average net royalty interest on Diamondback-operated horizontal wells is 6.3% versus 1.6% on third-party wells. Viper has no employees; the CEO and CFO are Diamondback secondees, the Midland office is owned by Diamondback, and the board may not appoint an executive who is not a Diamondback secondee without either Diamondback's consent or an 80% supermajority stockholder vote.

**The sector tag is wrong.** The supplied industry classification ("Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation") and the accompanying peer table — Antero Midstream, DT Midstream, Kinetik at a 19.3x median P/E — describe fee-based tariff businesses. Viper books no fee-based contracts and owns no midstream assets. Revenue is approximately 84% oil royalties and effectively 100% commodity-linked. Benchmarking against midstream multiples misstates both beta and the appropriate multiple.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 royalty income of $1,346M rose $492M year over year, which management decomposes as +$701M from 91% production growth less $209M from lower realized prices. Roughly 46% of the production growth came from the May 2025 Endeavor drop-down and 32% from Sitio. Substantially all of 2025's growth was purchased.

Per-BOE FY2025 economics: revenue $38.77; production and ad valorem taxes $2.71; cash G&A $0.81; interest $2.76; depletion $17.48. Total cost of $23.76 against $38.77 realized is a thin GAAP cushion at $63 oil and a wide one at $98. The depletion rate rose 48% from $11.77/BOE in 2024, reflecting the cost basis of the 2025 acquisitions. The equity's GAAP earnings are now materially more price-levered than before the acquisition spree, and that is the mechanical reason a record realized price still produced a consensus miss.

The FY2025 GAAP net loss of $206M is a full-cost ceiling-test artifact — a $768M non-cash impairment, with accumulated impairment of $888M at year end. It does not affect covenants, cash flow, or debt capacity. It does distort the trailing EPS of ‑$0.48 and the ‑145x "forward P/E" in the supplied data, both of which should be disregarded. The 10-K states directly that a further material non-cash impairment was "reasonably likely" in Q1 2026 given SEC price movements; that is a book-value event, not a cash event, though it further compresses the accumulated-deficit cushion that Delaware law requires for dividend payment (accumulated deficit $278M at 12/31/2025).

**Cash flow and capital structure.** Because Viper funds no drilling, operating cash flow approximates free cash flow before acquisitions: $1,053M in FY2025 on $1,395M of operating income. The offsetting reality is that 2025 investing outflows were $2,424M and financing inflows $1,357M, funded by $1.6B of notes, $1.2B of equity and a $500M term loan, against $745M of distributions and $194M of buybacks. This is a serial acquirer that distributes most operating cash flow and funds growth with new units and debt, which makes per-unit accretion the only meaningful test.

Debt is now cleaner than at year end. The $617M February 2026 non-Permian divestiture retired the $500M term loan and $90M of revolver; the remaining revolver was repaid in Q1. Funded debt reverted to $500M of 4.90% notes due 2030 and $1.1B of 5.70% notes due 2035, with the revolver used since for Riverbend cash consideration. Net debt/total capitalization of approximately 13.6% against a 65% covenant is extraordinary headroom. Net debt/EBITDA is 0.63x on Q2 annualized, 0.72x on H1 annualized, and roughly 0.85x on our normalized $80-WTI estimate. Ratings: S&P BBB-, Fitch BBB-, Moody's Ba1.

The hedge book is appropriate for a royalty owner: oil protection is deep out-of-the-money puts only (40,000 Bbl/d at $51.75 in Q1 2026; 20,000 at $48.13 in Q2), which expired worthless in a $98 quarter at the cost of premium while preserving all upside. Gas exposure is managed through Waha basis swaps at ‑$1.61 for 2026, the profitable leg given realized gas of $0.05/Mcf in Q2. There are no fixed-price oil swaps capping upside and no interest-rate hedges, though floating exposure is now limited to revolver drawings.

**Financial health score: 7.0.** This is an increase from the 6.0 carried in July, and the divergence from the declining overall score is deliberate. The February divestiture retired the term loan, funded debt is back to $1.6B of long-dated notes, leverage is under 1x, revolver availability is roughly $1.9B, and two agencies rate the credit investment grade. Offsetting: minimal retained liquidity by design, unhedged floating revolver, an accumulated deficit that constrains Delaware dividend surplus, and growth funded by continuous unit issuance.

## Growth Analysis

Absolute growth in 2025 was dramatic — production up 91%, royalty income up 58% — and entirely purchased. The relevant test is per unit.

Q4 2025 net production of approximately 134,000 BOE/d against 368.06M fully diluted units is 364 BOE/d per million units. FY2026 guidance midpoint of 133.75 MBOE/d against 356.2M units at 6/30/2026 is 376 BOE/d per million units, roughly +3%. The comparison is imperfect in both directions: Q4 2025 included the non-Permian assets divested in February (~4,750 BO/d) and excluded Riverbend (~2,000 bo/d added July 1). Net royalty acres per million units fell from approximately 261 to 253, though the divested acreage was lower-quality non-Permian and the $617M of proceeds retired debt.

So an enterprise that roughly doubled in 2025 delivered low-single-digit per-unit volume growth into 2026. Per-share value creation is coming from commodity price, deleveraging, and buybacks rather than volume accretion. The counterargument, which we take seriously, is that the acquired inventory is development-weighted — Sitio contributed 25,300 Permian NRA and Endeavor 24,446 NRA including 394 gross DUCs and 116 completed wells — so per-unit volumes could inflect as operators convert it. Rig count on the acreage rose to 106 gross (12 Diamondback-operated) from 98 (8 Diamondback) at year end, which is a supportive leading indicator.

Organic reserve replacement was 179% gross and 126% net of the year's 18,570 MBOE of downward revisions (‑11,481 from prices, ‑4,722 from PUD downgrades, ‑2,367 from performance). Forward proved R/P is 8.3 years on 2026 guidance. For a perpetual mineral estate, SEC proved reserves capture only what operators are reasonably certain to drill within five years, so 8.3 years understates economic life — but the corollary is that value beyond a decade depends entirely on drilling decisions Viper cannot control or fund. The 10-K explicitly notes that during 2025 Diamondback "continued to exercise capital discipline by using the majority of their excess cash flow for debt repayment and/or return to their stockholders rather than expanding its drilling programs."

**Estimate revisions cut both ways and net negative.** Current-year consensus of $2.60 is up 11.4% over 90 days but down 2.2% over 30; current-quarter is up 15.3% over 90 days and down 8.6% over 30. Next-year consensus of $2.42 is down 2.5% over 90 days and 2.7% over 30, and sits below the current year. The 90-day improvement is the oil spike working through models; the 30-day deterioration is the Q2 miss and the forward strip. There is no +30% revision inflection signal here — the more recent direction is down, and consensus itself models earnings declining in 2027.

**Growth score: 5.0.** Volumes grow, per-unit volumes barely do, and next-year earnings are expected to fall.

## Valuation Assessment

Enterprise value at $42.50: 356.2M units × $42.50 = $15,139M equity + $1,618M net debt = **$16,757M**.

| Basis | Consolidated EBITDA | EV/EBITDA |
|---|---|---|
| Q2 2026 annualized ($98.28 realized oil) | $2,568M | **6.5x** |
| H1 2026 annualized | $2,244M | **7.5x** |
| Normalized $85 WTI (est.) | ~$2,100M | **8.0x** |
| Normalized $80 WTI (est.) | ~$1,913M | **8.8x** |
| Normalized $65 WTI (est.) | ~$1,460M | **11.5x** |

The normalization is built bottom-up: 66.6 MBO/d of oil at a realized price tracking WTI closely (Q1 2026 realized $73.16, Q2 $98.28), plus gas and NGL scaled from FY2025's $215M on 95 MBOE/d to current volumes, less 7% production and ad valorem taxes and ~$40M of cash G&A.

This is the central valuation point. The stock does not trade at 6.5x; it trades at roughly 8.0-8.8x on oil in the low-to-mid $80s, which is a defensible multiple for a capex-free perpetual royalty with sub-1x leverage but not a cheap one. Pure-play mineral peers have historically cleared 7-9x. The 6.5x figure requires $98 oil to persist.

Class A per-share metrics: $42.50 against current-year consensus EPS of $2.60 is 16.3x, and 17.6x next year's $2.42. Price to book per unit is approximately 1.47x ($10,363M equity ÷ 358.0M units = $28.95). Price to after-tax standardized measure is 2.35x ($6,647M ÷ 368.06M = $18.06), calculated at SEC prices of $64.80/Bbl — and that measure excludes all unproved acreage, against which $4,910M of unevaluated properties sits on the balance sheet. Those two figures are not additive (one is an after-tax DCF, the other historical cost), but together they explain why 2.35x standardized measure is not prima facie expensive: a large undeveloped inventory is simply outside the calculation.

**Distribution yield is the strongest bull argument and it needs qualification.** The base dividend of $2.00 per Class A share from Q3 2026 is 4.71% at $42.50, described by management as roughly 50% of CAD at $70 WTI. Q2 CAD to Class A was $262M, or $1.37/share — a 12.9% annualized rate, on $98 oil. Our normalized estimate at $80-85 WTI is CAD of roughly $3.60-4.10 per Class A share, an 8.5-9.6% yield. That is a real return on a perpetual asset with almost no leverage.

The qualification: management removed the prior commitment to return a minimum of 75% of CAD when it raised the base dividend. The variable component is now fully discretionary. Q2 capital deployment shows where it goes — $132M of buybacks at a $44.34 average, and a new related-party drop-down of 933 NRA for approximately 3.7M OpCo Units (roughly $157M at $42.50). Buybacks at a 9% normalized CAD yield are accretive. Unit-funded acreage purchases at approximately $174k/NRA are less clearly so.

**On analyst targets.** Nineteen analysts are at Buy or Strong Buy with zero holds, mean target $56.37 and median $57 — 33% above spot. That unanimity is not independent confirmation, and it is internally inconsistent with the same analysts' estimate cuts: $56 on next-year EPS of $2.42 is 23x, against a normalized EV/EBITDA near 11x. The low target of $46 (+8.2%) is the more defensible end of the range and is close to our base case.

**Valuation score: 5.5.** Fairly valued on normalized oil with genuine yield support and real undeveloped optionality, but no identified mispricing and a headline multiple that flatters.

## Competitive Landscape

The royalty model's advantages are structural and durable: no development capital, minimal operating expense ($1.01/BOE of all-in G&A including the $17M related-party services fee), and a perpetual interest in a basin running 244 horizontal rigs at year end, 46% of total U.S. onshore horizontal activity. Post-Sitio scale in what Viper repeatedly calls a "highly fragmented minerals market" gives it a sourcing advantage over smaller aggregators.

The trajectory is less favorable than the level. Operator concentration did not improve despite the M&A: Diamondback's share of royalty income was 61% in 2023, 54% in 2024, and 55% in 2025 — the Sitio deal broadened the base and the 69%-Diamondback-operated drop-down pulled it back. A changed revenue mix is not reduced counterparty concentration, and here the concentration measure is flat to slightly worse. Item 1A is candid on the bidding disadvantage: "because we have fewer financial and human resources than many companies in our industry, we may be at a disadvantage in bidding for these and other oil and natural gas properties."

Concentration is also stratigraphic. Post-divestiture the portfolio is essentially all Permian, with most proved reserves in the Wolfberry play of the Midland Basin. The 10-K details Texas Railroad Commission produced-water disposal restrictions near Midland and Odessa, including permit suspensions, which "could make drilling activity in the affected parts of the Permian Basin less economical." Realized natural gas of $0.05/Mcf in Q2 illustrates chronic Waha takeaway constraint — though gas and NGL are 52% of proved reserve volumes and only 16% of royalty income, so volume-weighted reserve statistics overstate gas's economic weight and understate oil beta.

**Competitive position score: 5.8.** High-quality asset, capital-efficient model, flat-to-slightly-deteriorating per-unit and concentration trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Operator dependence concentrated in the parent.** Viper cannot drill. Development is at operators' discretion, Diamondback generated 55% of FY2025 royalty income and operates 61% of PUD locations, and the 10-K documents Diamondback prioritizing debt repayment and shareholder returns over program expansion. A single third party's capital allocation is the largest driver of forward volumes.

**Class B conversion and registration overhang.** All Class A shares issuable on exchange of OpCo Units held by Diamondback, Sitio, TWR IV and Morita Ranches holders are registered for resale. Against a 191.4M Class A float sits 164.8M convertible units, and the parent demonstrated in March 2026 that it will convert and sell — 12.9M shares for approximately $589M at $45.69, cutting fully diluted voting power from 42.1% to 38.9%. This is a recurring, mechanical supply headwind on the Class A line specifically, and it is the risk we consider least well reflected in the price.

**Commodity beta at an elevated starting point.** Q2 realized oil of $98.28 reflects a market the Q1 2026 10-Q described as having "shifted from a supply-demand surplus to a deficit." A normalization of that supply-demand balance would take realizations materially lower and cut EBITDA from the Q2 run-rate; a demand-led move to $65 WTI cuts consolidated EBITDA to roughly $1.46B by our estimate. [unverified — the Hormuz/OPEC+ scenario specifics, midterm fuel-price intervention, and the "three active attack zones" framing are not supported by the research brief.]

**Distribution discretion.** The base dividend is not contractually required — only a $20,000 quarterly aggregate Class B preferred dividend is. Removal of the 75% minimum CAD-return commitment increases discretion at the same moment the OpCo payout percentage is disclosed as subject to quarterly change. Management's claim that the $2.00 base is protected down to roughly $30/bbl WTI does not reconcile with a bottom-up sensitivity on consolidated base distributions of roughly $795M, and we treat it as unverified.

**Related-party mechanics.** The certificate of incorporation waives corporate opportunity; Diamondback owes Viper no duty to offer assets and may compete. Viper's own filings describe the drop-down incentive plainly: Diamondback "may continue to be incentivized to sell properties to us, as doing so may enhance Diamondback's economic returns by monetizing long-lived producing properties while potentially retaining a portion of the resulting cash flow through dividends on Diamondback's controlling interests in us." Genuine offsets exist — the 2025 drop-down required and received a majority-of-minority stockholder vote, the audit committee is fully independent, and the board declined controlled-company governance exemptions even when available.

**Verification gaps.** Two 8-Ks were not retrievable: an Item 5.02 filing around 2/19/2026 (departure or election of directors/officers) and an Item 1.01/2.03 filing around 6/15/2026 that we infer relates to a revolver increase from $1.5B toward $2.0B, consistent with the reported ~$1.9B of availability. Neither is confirmed. No Q2 earnings call transcript was available, so there is no management Q&A on the removal of the minimum-return commitment.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 37.4% against 28.6% one-year realized is rich by 8.8 points, at the 64th percentile within the chain, with a flat term structure (37.4% near versus 35.7% at six months). Implied volatility rose 6.1 points between July 19 and August 4 while the stock fell 1.6% — and it remained elevated *after* the August 3 print, which is atypical. The market is pricing continued oil and event risk rather than company-specific uncertainty. Open interest is call-tilted at a 0.21 put/call ratio, and near-term skew is described as balanced. For a holder, rich near-term volatility favors selling upside calls over buying protection; for a buyer, it argues against paying up for entry timing. Short interest of 4.4% of float and 4.3 days to cover shows no crowded bearish positioning.

**Risk factors score: 4.5.** The conversion overhang, the parent's demonstrated selling, the shift to fully discretionary variable distributions, and an elevated commodity starting point are specific and not fully priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Oil sustains near current elevated levels, with the supply-demand deficit the Q1 10-Q described persisting. Realized oil holds near $95, consolidated EBITDA runs above $2.5B, CAD to Class A approaches $5.00-5.50 per share, and the board directs the discretionary portion into buybacks at a 12% CAD yield. Operators convert the Sitio and Endeavor undeveloped inventory — 106 rigs on the acreage, up from 98 — lifting per-unit volumes above the +3% guided for 2026. Leverage under 1x and $1.9B of revolver availability let Viper buy back units through any volatility. The stock re-rates to 9x on higher EBITDA: **$58**, +36% from $42.50, plus distributions.

**Bear case.** The supply-demand balance normalizes back toward surplus and WTI settles in the low $60s. Realized oil near $58 takes consolidated EBITDA to roughly $1.46B. The $2.00 base dividend consumes nearly all CAD, the variable goes to zero, and the market reprices a fully discretionary distribution to a 6.5-7% base yield. Diamondback continues converting and selling into a weaker tape while capital discipline holds volumes flat. **$32**, ‑25%.

**Base case.** Oil averages the mid-$80s WTI across the next four quarters. Consolidated EBITDA of roughly $2.1B, CAD to Class A of $3.90-4.10, base dividend covered about twice. At 9x normalized EBITDA: EV $18.9B less $1.7B net debt, divided by 356.2M units = **$47**, +10.6%, or a total return near 15% including the $2.00 base dividend. Cross-check: an 8.1% CAD yield on $3.90 gives $48.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $47** (+10.6% from $42.50). Derivation: 9.0x EV/EBITDA on normalized consolidated EBITDA of ~$2.1B at mid-$80s WTI, less $1.618B net debt, across 356.2M total units. Corroborated by an 8.1% CAD yield on normalized Class A CAD of $3.90.

**Bull case target: $58** (+36.5%). Sustained elevated oil, EBITDA above $2.5B, 9x, discretionary cash into buybacks.

**Bear case target: $32** (‑24.7%). WTI low-$60s, EBITDA ~$1.46B, base dividend consuming CAD, multiple compression on discretionary distribution.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (early November, first quarter of the $2.00 base dividend and the first read on post-spike realizations), the Q4 print and 2027 guidance in February 2027, and the annual base-dividend and buyback-authorization review that has historically accompanied the February board meeting.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Price at or below **$36-37** with normalized CAD intact — that is a 10.5%+ normalized CAD yield and roughly 7.5x normalized EBITDA, which restores asymmetry.
2. Two consecutive quarters in which per-unit production growth exceeds 8% annualized without new unit issuance, evidencing operator conversion of the Sitio and Endeavor undeveloped inventory rather than purchased growth.
3. Reinstatement of a formulaic minimum return-of-capital percentage, or disclosure that the discretionary portion is being directed predominantly to buybacks below $45 rather than to unit-funded drop-downs.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. A drop-down or acquisition funded with OpCo Units at above roughly $190k/NRA without a majority-of-minority vote, or any related-party transaction that raises Diamondback's fully diluted stake back above 45%.
2. Next-year consensus EPS falling below $2.10 (currently $2.42) alongside a base-dividend reduction or a quarter in which base distributions exceed CAD.
3. Diamondback secondary sales exceeding a cumulative 25M Class A-equivalent shares within any twelve-month period, which would signal the parent monetizing rather than compounding its interest.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report in early November, which will be the first quarter to show realized prices from a post-spike strip against the higher $17+/BOE depletion base and the new $2.00 base dividend.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory has declined from 6.9 in May to 5.7 in July and now to 5.5, and the Q2 print confirms rather than reverses the direction of the specific dimensions that drove it. Financial health has genuinely improved since February — the term loan is retired, funded debt is $1.6B of 2030/2035 notes, leverage is under 1x, and two agencies rate it investment grade — and we have marked it up accordingly. Growth, governance and risk have not. Per-unit volume growth of roughly 3% followed a doubling of the enterprise. Next-year consensus EPS is below current-year and falling. The 75% minimum cash-return commitment was withdrawn in the same release that raised the base dividend. The controlling holder sold roughly $589M of stock in March at $45.69 and is simultaneously selling acreage into the vehicle for units. Class A adjusted EPS missed by 8.9% in a quarter with the highest realized oil price in the company's history, because depletion and cash taxes now absorb more of the upside than consensus modeled.

None of that is an impairment thesis. This is a low-leverage, capex-free, perpetual Permian mineral estate paying a $2.00 base dividend covered roughly twice at $70 WTI, with an 8.5-9.5% CAD yield at normalized prices and a large undeveloped inventory excluded from the standardized measure. It is a legitimate income holding. What it is not, at $42.50 and approximately 8.0-8.8x normalized EV/EBITDA, is a position with asymmetric upside — base-case appreciation of 11% against bear-case downside of 25%, in a sector where our own selection record has been persistently negative on an alpha basis. Hold for the distribution; add materially only in the mid-$30s or on demonstrated per-unit volume inflection.
