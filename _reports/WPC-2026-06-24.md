**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

W. P. Carey is one of the largest diversified net-lease REITs, owning 1,703 single-tenant, triple-net properties (~185M sq ft, 374 tenants, 98.1% occupancy, 12.1-year WALT) with $1.58B of annualized base rent. After a deliberate 2023-2024 reset that spun out its office assets into NLOP and exited self-storage operations, the company is now demonstrating the intended payoff: revenue re-accelerated to +8.4% in FY2025 and +10.9% YoY in Q1 2026, AFFO grew ~6% to $1.098B in 2025 and +12% YoY in Q1 2026, and management raised FY2026 AFFO/share guidance to $5.16-$5.26. The dividend, rebased after the office spin, has resumed a steady climb to $0.94/quarter.

The fundamentals are clean once you strip the non-cash noise. GAAP optics are depressed by a $103M unrealized mark on the legacy Lineage cold-storage stake and ~$98M of FX remeasurement losses, neither of which affects distributable cash. The frequently-flagged "distress" Altman Z-score of 1.14 is a known artifact of applying an industrial-firm model to an asset-heavy, high-leverage REIT; the investment-grade BBB+/Baa1 ratings, 96% fixed-rate debt at a ~3.2% blended cost, and ~$2.5B of liquidity describe the real balance sheet. The volatile quarterly EPS "misses" (a -74% surprise in Q2 2025, -54% in Q4 2025) reflect the timing of sales-type lease proceeds and mark-to-market swings rather than operating deterioration; AFFO/share is the metric that matters and it beat in Q1 2026 at $1.30.

The investment case is constrained by valuation and the macro backdrop, not by company-specific deterioration. At $72.30 the stock trades at roughly 14x the midpoint of guided 2026 AFFO and offers a ~5.2% yield with mid-single-digit growth. That is a reasonable income proposition but not a mispricing. WPC is a spread investor, and a higher-for-longer rate path compresses the gap between acquisition cap rates and its cost of capital, which is the engine of accretive external growth. Combined with ~40% non-US ABR exposing reported results to euro translation and Eastern European geopolitical risk, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate WPC a HOLD (income hold) with a base case fair value of ~$78.

## Company Overview

W. P. Carey (founded 1973, REIT-converted in 2012) owns operationally-critical, single-tenant commercial real estate leased on long-term triple-net terms, with built-in rent escalators (many CPI-linked). The portfolio is deliberately diversified across property type, geography, tenant, and industry, and is managed as a single reportable segment.

Portfolio composition as of 3/31/2026 (pro rata): property mix of Industrial 37.9%, Warehouse 25.4%, Retail 22.7%, and Other 14%, giving a combined industrial/warehouse weight of ~63%, favorable given structural logistics demand and distinct from retail-heavy net-lease peers. Geographically, the U.S. is ~60% of ABR, Europe ~33%, with the balance in Canada, Mexico, and Japan; the principal FX exposure is the euro. Tenant diversification is genuine: the largest single tenant (Extra Space Storage) is just 2.7% of ABR and the top 10 sum to ~18%. The largest industry concentrations are Packaged Foods & Meats (9.5%) and Food Retail (8.9%), both defensive.

The 2023-2024 reset defines the strategic context. WPC spun off its office assets into Net Lease Office Properties (NLOP) and exited self-storage operating properties (the final 11 self-storage assets sold in Q1 2026). This explains the apparent revenue dip from $1.74B (2023) to $1.58B (2024), a deliberate de-risking rather than deterioration. The company retains a residual, winding-down investment-management relationship as advisor to NLOP and CESH.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue progression was $1,479M (2022), $1,741M (2023), $1,583M (2024), and $1,716M (2025). The 2025 figure represents +8.4% growth driven by $2.0B of accretive acquisitions plus contractual rent escalations. AFFO, the relevant cash-earnings measure for a net-lease REIT, was $1,036M in 2024 and $1,098M in 2025, with Q1 2026 AFFO of $288.7M versus $257.8M a year earlier, translating to $1.30/share against estimates.

**GAAP optics versus underlying cash.** FY2025 GAAP net income of $466M is depressed by the non-cash Lineage mark and FX remeasurement losses. Operating cash flow appears to have fallen from $1.83B (2024) to $1.28B (2025), but 2024 included $807M of one-time sales-type lease proceeds (U-Haul/Andalusia portfolios) versus only $200M in 2025; underlying operating cash generation is healthy and rising. The earnings-surprise record (one beat, three misses over four quarters, with two surprises beyond -50%) is a function of these timing items and mark-to-market swings, not a deterioration in run-rate cash flow. We weight AFFO over EPS accordingly.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets of $18.2B, total debt ~$8.75B, and stockholders' equity of $8.34B as of 3/31/2026 yield consolidated debt/gross assets of ~43% and debt/equity of ~1.0x, appropriate leverage for a net-lease REIT and well within investment-grade norms. Debt is now 96% fixed-rate (up from 92%) at a ~3.2% weighted-average cost, with FX-denominated debt serving as a natural net-investment hedge against European assets. The company carries BBB+/Baa1 ratings, ~$1.9B undrawn on a $2.0B revolver, and ~$650M of available forward-equity proceeds. Near-term maturities are modest: ~$353M remaining in 2026 and $585M in 2027.

**Capital markets execution.** Early 2026 activity demonstrates capacity to fund growth on attractive terms even in a higher-rate environment: a €1.0B senior-notes issuance (3.25% due 2031, 3.75% due 2035), repayment of €500M of 2.25% notes, a 6.9M-share forward equity offering (~$497M gross), and a credit-facility amendment adding a C$347M term loan with 5bps better revolver pricing. The net effect pushed out maturities and locked in still-attractive long-dated fixed rates.

The financial-health picture is genuinely strong, and we score it 7.0. The balance sheet is the cleanest part of the story: low fixed-cost debt, investment-grade ratings, ample liquidity, and well-laddered maturities. The main qualifier is the REIT structural constraint: the 90% distribution requirement limits retained cash and forces reliance on external capital markets, which links growth durability to the cost-of-capital environment.

## Growth Analysis

AFFO growth has resumed after the reset trough, and consensus estimate revisions corroborate the recovery. Current-year EPS estimate has been revised to $3.02 from $2.62 ninety days ago (+15.1%), and next-year to $3.46 from $2.79 (+24.1%). Note the divergence between the data feed's EPS line ($3.02 current year) and management's guided AFFO/share of $5.16-$5.26: the latter is the operative number on a full-AFFO basis, and the directional revision trend is what matters: estimates are broadly higher.

The +24% next-year revision combined with double-digit revenue growth would, on the surface, register as a strong recovery signal. We temper that read here for two reasons. First, this is not a depressed-trough cyclical recovering to a much higher normalized earnings power; it is a stable income REIT growing AFFO at a mid-single-digit underlying rate, where the large percentage revisions partly reflect the absence of last year's one-time drags and a single contributing analyst (low estimate breadth). Second, the durability of external growth depends on the spread between acquisition cap rates and WPC's cost of capital, which a higher-for-longer rate path pressures.

The growth runway is real and broad-based at the portfolio level. Management lifted the investment-volume target to $1.5B-$2.0B, citing pipeline strength and lower expected rent loss. Q1 2026 acquisitions of $514.7M (Poland warehouses, Canadian retail, U.S. and German industrial) plus ~$92M in April demonstrate deployment is on track. The net-lease sector backdrop has improved: after the 2022-2024 spread compression, cap rates have adjusted upward as rate expectations stabilized, restoring positive investment spreads, which is why peers' AFFO and WPC's guidance are both rising. The internal growth engine, contractual escalators on the 12.1-year WALT portfolio, provides a low-single-digit base regardless of acquisition pace. We score growth 6.2: durable mid-single-digit AFFO compounding with upside from deployment, but not the high-velocity recovery that the headline revision percentages might suggest.

## Valuation Assessment

At $72.30, WPC trades at roughly 14x the midpoint of guided 2026 AFFO/share ($5.21), with a ~5.2% dividend yield. The trailing P/E of ~30.9x is not the relevant lens; GAAP net income is suppressed by non-cash marks, and the forward AFFO multiple is the appropriate metric for a net-lease REIT.

Against same-sector peers, the comparison is mixed. Peer P/E medians (35.2x) are distorted by GAAP earnings noise across the group and are not directly comparable. On yield, WPC's ~5.2% sits below the peer median of 5.6% (EPRT 4.2%, LXP 5.3%, ALEX 6.7%, GNL 8.0% on a name with negative earnings), placing WPC in the middle of the range: a modest premium to higher-quality EPRT and a discount to distressed/higher-yield names. The yield premium WPC historically carried over Realty Income and the highest-quality net-lease names has compressed as the post-reset recovery has played out and the stock recovered from the low-$60s to the low-$70s.

The valuation framework here resists treating cheapness as a buy signal, and there is no specific mispricing to point to. The forward AFFO multiple of ~14x is reasonable for a mid-single-digit grower with an investment-grade balance sheet, but it is not a discount that signals the market is wrong. Analyst targets (mean $78.42, median $77.50, range $73-$85) sit modestly above the current price, implying ~8% upside to consensus mean plus the yield. We do not treat these targets as floors here because WPC is not a sub-30x-forward-P/E recovery name with a PEG well under 1.5; it is a stable yield vehicle where consensus targets are a fair anchor rather than a lagging indicator. The stock recovered ~12% over the trailing year and now sits ~6% off its 52-week high of $76.97 after a mid-June pullback from ~$76 to ~$71-72.

We score valuation 5.7. The price is fair, the yield is supportive, and there is single-digit upside to consensus, but the asymmetric mispricing required for a higher score is absent. The prior report's 6.8 valuation score reflected a lower entry price (low-$70s after a wider discount); the stock has since closed much of that gap, which justifies stepping the score back toward neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

WPC's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving on a cleaner, industrial-tilted portfolio. Its differentiation rests on three pillars: (1) scale and a self-originated sale-leaseback platform sourcing deals directly with corporate tenants; (2) a meaningful European footprint (~33% of ABR) that most U.S. net-lease peers, Realty Income excepted, lack, allowing larger sale-leaseback transactions at wider cap rates in less-competitive markets; and (3) long-duration leases with contractual escalators, many CPI-linked. The ~63% industrial/warehouse weighting positions WPC favorably against retail-heavy peers given structural logistics demand.

The European sourcing edge is a genuine differentiator, but it is double-edged: it brings FX translation volatility and EU regulatory/geopolitical complexity that pure-U.S. peers avoid. The core peer set (Realty Income, NNN REIT, Agree Realty, Broadstone) competes for the same investment-grade-tenant sale-leaseback flow, keeping cap rates competitive in the U.S. WPC's relative TSR of 124.4 trails the peer-group index of 137.5 over the measurement window, consistent with the dilution and reset overhang the company is still working through. The trajectory is improving (occupancy 98.1%, AFFO re-accelerating, guidance raised), but WPC is not demonstrably taking share from the highest-quality domestic compounders. We score competitive position 6.3: a durable, scaled platform with a distinctive European angle, improving from the reset trough, but not widening its moat against the best peers.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest-rate / cost-of-capital risk (most material).** As a spread investor, WPC's external growth depends on investing at cap rates above its blended cost of capital. A higher-for-longer rate path compresses investment spreads (cap rates lag rate moves) and raises refinancing cost. Interest expense rose $13.9M in 2025 and $9.7M YoY in Q1 2026 despite the low blended rate. This risk is partly priced into the REIT complex but remains the dominant variable.

**FX / European exposure (~40% of ABR).** Euro translation directly swings reported revenue. Eastern European assets (Poland, Croatia) carry incremental geopolitical risk given the Russia-Ukraine conflict, and EU tax changes (Pillar Two) and GDPR add regulatory complexity.

**Lease rollover and tenant credit.** Roughly 19% of leases by ABR expire within five years; re-leasing purpose-built single-tenant properties can require concessions, capex, or result in vacancy. Concrete recent stress includes the Hellweg (German DIY) lease restructuring with rent abatement, Q3 2025 lease terminations, and $40M of Q1 2026 impairments tied to 2027 lease expirations and dispositions. These are manageable against 98%+ occupancy but are the most concrete fundamental watch-items.

**Lineage stake overhang.** The legacy ~5.5M-share cold-storage position (fair value ~$157M) is subject to mark-to-market volatility (-$103M in 2025, -$10M in Q1 2026) and settlement at Lineage's discretion over a three-year window, adding GAAP noise.

The frequently-cited Altman Z-score of 1.14 is a false distress signal: the model misreads asset-heavy, high-leverage REITs, and should not be weighted. On balance, the risks are real but largely visible and partly priced. We score risk factors 6.0: no binary catalyst threatens a sharp downside, the principal risks (rates, FX) are well-understood and embedded, and the balance sheet provides cushion.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contrarian warning. Near-term ATM IV of 18.7% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical vol of 17.2% (a fair 1.5pp spread), the put/call skew is balanced at -1.0%, and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.14 and falling (down from 0.22 a month ago). The term structure is flat (near 18.7% versus far 20.0%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed with no near-term event premium. IV has eased slightly even as the price dipped ~2.3%, consistent with an orderly pullback rather than stress. For an income name, the lack of elevated put demand supports the view that the market is not pricing a left-tail event; it also means options are not flagging an underpriced catalyst in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$85).** The net-lease recovery extends, cap rates stay wide enough to preserve positive investment spreads, and WPC deploys the upper end of its $1.5B-$2.0B target accretively. AFFO/share reaches the high end of guidance and grows mid-single-digits beyond, the dividend continues climbing, and the yield premium versus peers fully compresses as the reset overhang fades. A stabilization or decline in the long end re-rates the AFFO multiple toward the high-teens. At ~16x $5.30+ AFFO plus the dividend, the stock reaches ~$85, matching the Street high.

**Bear case (~$62, ~14% below current).** A higher-for-longer rate path compresses investment spreads, slowing accretive deployment to a trickle and capping AFFO growth at the escalator-only base. FX losses recur, 2027 lease expirations resolve with concessions and downtime, and incremental tenant stress (beyond Hellweg) pressures occupancy. The yield re-widens toward 6%+ to clear, pulling the stock back toward the 52-week low near $61.

**Base case (~$78).** Mid-single-digit AFFO growth holds, the dividend rises modestly, and the multiple stays near ~14-15x forward AFFO. The stock delivers a ~5.2% yield plus low-single-digit price appreciation, converging on consensus mean of ~$78 over twelve months. Total return in the high-single-digits, driven by income.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an income-hold rationale: the return is anchored by a well-covered ~5.2% dividend (~70-75% of AFFO payout) plus modest AFFO-driven appreciation, not by a re-rating catalyst.

- **Reassessment timeline**: Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (AFFO/share versus the full-year guide and any guidance revision) and the trajectory of long-end rates / Fed guidance over the next two to three quarters.
- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY)**: (1) a pullback toward the high-$60s that restores a yield above ~5.6% and a forward AFFO multiple below ~13x, re-establishing asymmetric upside; (2) evidence that investment spreads are widening — acquisition cap rates rising faster than cost of capital — with deployment at the high end of the target; or (3) a sustained decline in the long end that supports multiple expansion across the net-lease group.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID)**: (1) AFFO/share guidance cut below the $5.16 floor; (2) occupancy falling below ~96% on a wave of 2027 lease-expiration vacancies or new tenant defaults beyond Hellweg; (3) a dividend coverage deterioration (payout rising toward ~90% of AFFO) or any dividend cut; or (4) forced equity issuance well below NAV to fund maturities, signaling cost-of-capital stress.

## Conclusion

W. P. Carey has executed its strategic reset and is now compounding AFFO at mid-single-digit rates on a cleaner, industrial-tilted, investment-grade portfolio with a strong balance sheet. The GAAP optics that make the stock screen poorly (depressed net income, a "distress" Z-score, a -54% EPS surprise) are artifacts of non-cash Lineage marks, FX remeasurement, and one-time lease-proceeds timing; the underlying AFFO and dividend are clean and rising. Management's raised 2026 guidance and continued dividend hikes signal the trough is past.

The constraint is valuation and macro, not the company. At ~14x forward AFFO and a ~5.2% yield with single-digit upside to consensus, the entry is fair rather than asymmetric, and WPC's reliance on positive investment spreads ties its growth durability to a rate environment that is biased higher-for-longer. The score trajectory has improved (5.7 → 6.4 → 6.2 here), and the prior valuation upgrade reflected a wider discount that the stock's recovery has since closed. We rate WPC a HOLD for income, with a base case fair value of ~$78, and would turn constructive on a pullback toward the high-$60s or clear evidence of widening investment spreads.
