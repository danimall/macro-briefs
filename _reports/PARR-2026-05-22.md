## Executive Summary

Par Pacific Holdings operates four petroleum refineries with 219,000 bpd combined capacity across logistically advantaged western U.S. markets, most notably its near-monopoly Hawaii refinery (~49% of throughput). The stock has appreciated 194% from its 52-week low of $19.28, driven by a cyclical recovery in crack spreads — the Hawaii Index surged to $31.11/bbl in Q1 2026 from $8.13 a year earlier. Current consensus EPS for FY2026 stands at $12.92, implying a forward P/E of ~4.4x, but this reflects peak-cycle conditions that analysts broadly expect to mean-revert.

Estimate revisions have been dramatic: current-quarter EPS revised +525% over 90 days. This reflects the mechanical nature of refining earnings leverage rather than a structural business improvement. The company reported a $0.04 miss in Q1 2026 and a $0.13 miss in Q4 2025, breaking what had been a strong beat streak. Combined with clustered insider selling in March 2026 and a new $500M debt issuance at 7.375%, the risk/reward at $57.45 is balanced rather than skewed positively. The stock's 18% pullback from its $70.39 high reflects emerging uncertainty about crack spread sustainability.

## Company Overview

Par Pacific Holdings is a Houston-based energy company operating four petroleum refineries serving niche western U.S. markets:

- **Hawaii (Kapolei):** ~84-90 Mbpd capacity; near-monopoly island market; 49% of throughput
- **Montana (Billings):** ~50-57 Mbpd; acquired June 2023; inland Rockies market with crude discounts
- **Washington (Tacoma):** ~23-39 Mbpd; Pacific Northwest; increasing regulatory burden
- **Wyoming (Newcastle):** ~13-18 Mbpd; smallest facility; niche inland market

Complementary operations include 13 million barrels of logistics storage, retail fuel stations (Hele brand in Hawaii, nomnom convenience stores in PNW), a 46% stake in Laramie Energy (natural gas E&P), and a 63.5% interest in a Hawaii Renewables joint venture (with Mitsubishi/ENEOS) that commenced commercial operations in April 2026 with ~61 million gallons/year capacity for renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel.

## Growth Analysis

Growth for PARR is primarily a function of crack spread recovery and structural positioning rather than organic volume expansion:

**Near-term earnings leverage:** Current-quarter EPS estimate of $6.88 reflects the Singapore 3.1.2 crack at ~$36/bbl and Hawaii Index at $31+/bbl. These are exceptional conditions. For context, the FY2024 Combined Index averaged $9.37/bbl vs. the current ~$19/bbl environment. Each $1/bbl change in Combined Index produces approximately $80M in annual EBITDA.

**Renewables Optionality:** The Hawaii Renewables JV (63.5% PARR) could contribute $30-50M in annual EBITDA at favorable renewable fuel credit spreads. Startup execution risk and feedstock cost uncertainty persist.

**Capacity growth is limited.** PARR's 219,000 bpd capacity is essentially fixed. Growth comes from margin environment, operational reliability, and per-share effects from buybacks.

**Estimate Revision Context:** The +525% 90-day revision for the current quarter reflects crack spread recalibration rather than business transformation. Forward estimates embed sustained elevated cracks: FY2026 consensus of $12.92 EPS requires margins to remain well above mid-cycle. Next-year consensus of $8.25 already implies meaningful margin normalization, suggesting analysts expect reversion.

## Competitive Landscape

PARR's competitive positioning is genuinely differentiated within the refining universe:

**Hawaii (structural moat):** The sole major refinery serving the Hawaiian islands. Competitors would need billions in capital and years of construction to challenge this position. The island market creates a natural barrier that translates to margin premiums reflected in the Hawaii Index consistently exceeding mainland benchmarks. Record throughput of 89.8 Mbpd in Q1 2026 demonstrates operational maturity.

**Montana/Wyoming (logistical advantage):** Inland Rockies markets where pipeline constraints create crude discounts (WCS Hardisty -$11.34 to WTI, Bakken -$2.52 to WTI). Montana margins compressed in Q1 2026 ($4.84/bbl Index vs. $7.07 year-ago), indicating this advantage is variable.

**Industry tailwinds:** Western U.S. refining capacity continues declining (California closures), tightening regional supply. PARR's 5-year TSR of 251% vs. 179% for the peer group reflects successful strategic execution (Billings acquisition, debt paydown, operational improvement).

**Competitive threats:** Washington state's CCA (cap-and-invest) program and clean fuel standard increasingly burden the Tacoma refinery, which saw throughput decline to 23 Mbpd from 39 Mbpd year-ago. Regulatory costs are eroding Washington's economics.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Crack Spread Mean Reversion (High probability, High impact):**
The Hawaii Index at $31/bbl is 283% above year-ago levels. Historical mid-cycle is closer to $12-15/bbl. Each $5/bbl decline in Combined Index reduces annual EBITDA by ~$400M. Analyst consensus already prices some reversion (FY2027E of $8.25 vs. FY2026E of $12.92), but the pace and magnitude remain uncertain.

**2. New Debt Increases Financial Risk:**
At 7.375% on $500M, annual interest expense increases by $37M. In a margin downturn (per FY2024), this debt burden becomes material — FY2024 operating income was only $48M against a pre-existing ~$50M+ interest expense.

**3. SRE Non-Recurrence:**
The $199.5M FY2025 windfall is unlikely to repeat at similar magnitude. The current administration has not committed to 2025 compliance year SREs. Loss of this benefit removes ~$4/share from normalized earnings.

**4. Insider Selling Pattern:**
CEO Monteleone sold ~$5.9M in March 2026 at $53-55 (net reducing holdings by ~48K shares). Director Clossey and SVP Pitkin also sold. While partial exercises of expiring options are common, no insiders purchased shares, and the clustered pattern near all-time highs warrants attention.

**5. Labor Relations:**
22% of workforce is unionized with CBAs expired and on 24-hour extensions. A work stoppage at any facility would immediately reduce throughput and earnings.

## Conclusion

PARR is a well-run refiner with genuine competitive advantages — particularly the Hawaii near-monopoly — operating in a favorable crack spread environment amplified by geopolitical supply disruptions. The 194% rally from 52-week lows accurately reflects the dramatic earnings improvement. The current price of $57.45 appropriately discounts elevated but likely-unsustainable peak-cycle margins. The forward P/E of 4.4x on FY2026E looks optically cheap but overstates intrinsic value given the cyclical nature of these earnings.

The combination of insider selling, two consecutive quarterly EPS misses, increased financial leverage, and an options market pricing sustained uncertainty (flat term structure with elevated IV) suggests the stock is fairly priced at current levels. The analyst consensus target of $72 (25% upside) reflects optimistic margin assumptions that depend on geopolitical developments and global refining supply/demand dynamics.

HOLD is appropriate. The franchise quality is high, but the entry point at $57 does not offer sufficient margin of safety for a cyclical business with binary geopolitical dependencies. A pullback toward $42-45 would create more compelling risk/reward.
