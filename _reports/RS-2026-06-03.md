**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Reliance is the largest metals service center company in North America, distributing and lightly processing carbon steel, aluminum, stainless and specialty metals across roughly 310 locations. The business has been profitable every year since its 1994 IPO, supported by extreme diversification (100,000+ products, 125,000+ customers, largest customer 0.6% of sales), a small-order spot-market model that passes price changes through quickly, and a fortress balance sheet (net debt/total capital ~17% against a 60% covenant ceiling). FY2025 was a cyclical trough — revenue $14.29B, EPS $13.98, down 10% — but the decline was largely a LIFO accounting artifact: FIFO gross margin rose 80bp to 29.5% and FIFO profitability rose ~14.5%. Q1 2026 confirmed a sharp recovery, with revenue +15.5%, EPS $5.10 (+36.4%), and operating margin +120bp to 9.1%.

The recovery is being driven by Section 232 steel and aluminum tariffs, raised to 50% in June 2025 with Canada/Mexico exemptions removed. Aluminum is at record prices because of the tariff, and RS's own 10-K states that if Section 232 is "removed or substantially lessened — whether through legal challenge, legislation, executive action or otherwise — imports would likely increase and U.S. metal prices would likely fall," materially hurting revenue, margins and cash flow. The earnings recovery and the central tail risk are the same variable. The recovery is policy-dependent rather than demand-driven.

I rate RS a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The business quality is genuine and the multi-year volume share gains (outperforming industry shipments by 7-8 points in both 2025 and Q1 2026 while the industry contracted) are the most underappreciated durable strength here. But at $391, near the 52-week high of $394.92 and a ~26x trailing multiple, the stock already discounts the tariff-supported earnings recovery, near-term estimate revisions have turned slightly negative, and the dominant earnings driver is binary and policy-controlled. The asymmetry does not favor a long at this price.

## Company Overview

Reliance operates as a distributor and first-stage processor, not a steel mill. It buys metals primarily from domestic mills, performs value-added processing (cutting, leveling, sawing, machining, electropolishing) on roughly half its orders, and distributes via next-day delivery. The model rests on three structural features. First, extreme diversification: average order size of ~$3,120, roughly 4.6 million orders in 2025 (~18,110/day), with over 90% of orders from repeat customers and no customer concentration. Second, a spot-market posture with minimal long-term contracts, which enables rapid price pass-through in both directions. Third, scale in a fragmented industry: RS holds roughly 17% of U.S. service-center tons (up from 15% in 2024) against approximately 8,400 competing companies, most of them single-location operators.

The company runs a decentralized structure under roughly 80 acquired trade names (EMJ, Metals USA, Phoenix Metals, Precision Strip) with local pricing authority, reported as one segment. It owns a fleet of roughly 1,800 mostly leased trucks, a meaningful edge given persistent driver shortages. End markets are cyclical: non-residential construction (largest), aerospace, semiconductors, energy, automotive and heavy equipment.

CEO Karla Lewis, a long-tenured insider, leads a stable executive team (COO Stephen Koch, CFO Arthur Ajemyan). The company has paid dividends for 67 consecutive years, raised them 33 times since IPO, and never cut.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is cyclical, tracking metals prices more than volume. Revenue moved from $17.03B (2022) to $14.81B (2023) to $13.84B (2024) to $14.29B (2025), with operating margin compressing from 14.7% to 7.1% over the same span. Net income to RS fell from $1.84B (2022) to $739M (2025), and diluted EPS from $22.64 (2023) to $13.98 (2025).

The 2025 GAAP decline overstates operational deterioration. The year was hit by a $258M adverse LIFO swing — from +$144.4M income in 2024 to -$113.7M expense in 2025 — driven by aluminum prices reversing from falling to rising. A standardized screen flagging "deteriorating earnings" would misread the underlying improvement; the operational story in 2025 was margin expansion masked by an accounting timing item.

Q1 2026 results: tons sold at a record (+2.7%), average selling price +12.6% on tight carbon steel supply and record aluminum prices, pretax income +33.2%, and operating cash flow of $151.4M.

The balance sheet is among the most conservative in the materials sector. Altman Z of 4.4 (safe zone), current ratio 4.88, D/E 0.45, and net debt/total capital of 16.9% against a 60% covenant maximum. Total debt is ~$1.7B at a 4.19% weighted average rate, investment-grade rated, with maturities staggered ($400M term loan 2028, $500M 2.15% notes 2030, $250M 6.85% notes 2036). Goodwill ($2.17B) plus intangibles ($0.96B) total roughly 30% of assets, with no goodwill impairment ever recorded.

Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly. Share count fell from 60.6M (2022) to ~51.0M (April 2026), a ~16% reduction, with $594M repurchased in 2025 and $529M of authorization remaining. FY2025 returns to shareholders totaled $848.8M ($594M buybacks + $255M dividends). One modeling nuance matters: RS's free cash flow falls when business improves, because rising prices and volumes build receivables and inventory, and rises in downturns. FY2025 operating cash flow dropped $598M to $831M despite a healthy business, purely on working-capital build. Declining cash flow in an up-cycle is not a red flag here.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth story is share capture, not the price cycle. RS grew tons sold +6.2% in 2025 against an industry decline of -1.0% (MSCI), a 7-point outperformance, and repeated it in Q1 2026 (industry -5.1%, RS gaining roughly 8 points). Outperforming a contracting industry by 7-8 points two years running is strong evidence the scale, service and diversification model is structurally winning share. The value-added mix has risen from ~40% of orders a decade ago to ~50% today, lifting sustainable gross margins from below 27% to ~29%+. Capex at historic highs ($329M in 2025, $431M in 2024) supports the claim that smaller competitors lack the capacity to match processing investment.

Estimate revisions present a mixed picture that argues against treating this as a clean recovery BUY. Over 90 days, consensus EPS has risen across all periods (current quarter +15.8% to $5.35, current year +15.3% to $19.58, next year +12.6% to $20.86), reflecting the tariff-driven recovery. But over the trailing 30 days, momentum has stalled or turned negative: current quarter +0.1%, next quarter -2.4%, current year -0.6%. The 90-day revision velocity exceeds +15% only when measured from a depressed prior base; the more recent 30-day trend is flat-to-negative. The earnings beat record is also uneven — a 7.3% beat in Q1 2026 followed a 17.8% miss in Q4 2025 — indicating lower visibility than the headline recovery suggests.

The unconfirmed "$3 billion government contracts" claim from a single transcript snippet, if real, would be an incremental demand driver, but it cannot be verified against the 10-Q or an 8-K and should not be underwritten. M&A, historically a core growth engine (76 deals since IPO), paused entirely in 2025, freeing ~$362M of cash but removing a lever.

## Valuation Assessment

At $391, RS trades at ~26x trailing EPS of $13.98 and roughly 20x the FY2026 consensus of $19.58. The peer median P/E is 27.9 (Steel Dynamics 27.9, Commercial Metals 17.1, Warrior Met Coal 36.2; Cleveland-Cliffs and Alpha Metallurgical at negative earnings). On a headline trailing basis RS sits below the peer median, and on forward earnings it looks reasonable for a best-in-class operator.

The problem is what those forward estimates embed. FY2026 consensus of ~$19.58 assumes the tariff-elevated pricing environment persists through the year — aluminum at record prices, carbon steel tight, both supported directly by 50% Section 232 tariffs. This is peak-of-policy-cycle earnings, not normalized mid-cycle earnings power. Normalizing toward a post-tariff environment (closer to the 2024-2025 EPS range of $13.98-$15.56) would put the multiple at 25-28x on more durable earnings, with the stock trading near a 52-week high. The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside because the upside case requires both continued tariff support and continued share gains, while the downside case requires only a policy reversal that RS explicitly warns about.

The stock has run ~30% over the past year and sits within 1% of its 52-week high. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation negative, and on fundamentals the multiple is defensible against a real recovery. But the absence of a margin of safety, the dependence of forward estimates on a single policy variable, and analyst targets that now sit below the current price (mean $354.86, median $350, high $390 against a $391 print) all indicate the market has already priced the recovery. With the analyst high target below spot, consensus is treating the current price as fully valued or stretched rather than as a floor. I score valuation 4.5: the multiple is not egregious, but there is no identifiable mispricing in RS's favor, and the earnings base supporting the multiple is policy-contingent.

## Competitive Landscape

RS's competitive position is improving, which is the strongest pillar of the thesis. In an industry of ~8,400 mostly single-location competitors, RS's ~17% share and demonstrated ability to take 7-8 points of relative share annually reflect a widening moat built on diversification, processing scale, and next-day delivery logistics. Service centers are structurally more resilient than mills because they pass through price changes and turn inventory rapidly. The rising value-added mix has structurally lifted gross margins. The decentralized trade-name model preserves local pricing intelligence while consolidating purchasing scale.

The principal long-term competitive risk is substitution — lightweighting in autos toward composites, aluminum and carbon fiber, accelerated by high metal prices. This is a slow-moving headwind rather than a near-term threat. The Q1 2026 internal reporting-unit realignment (goodwill reallocated, no impairment, single segment unchanged) is financially a non-event but worth monitoring as a possible operational reset under CEO Lewis.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by one structural feature and one policy variable, and the two interact to make the risk asymmetric.

First, metals price volatility is the dominant earnings driver — management states pricing has "a much more significant impact on results than customer demand." RS does not hedge and holds few long-term supply contracts. When prices rise for non-demand reasons (tariffs), RS cannot always fully pass through costs, which is what compressed aluminum margins in 2H2025.

Second, and most important, Section 232 tariffs are simultaneously the bull case and the central tail risk. The entire 2026 recovery rests on tariff-elevated pricing, and any rollback through legal challenge, legislation or executive action would likely send prices and margins down sharply. The 10-K notes the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that the President cannot impose tariffs under IEEPA, but states this does not directly touch Section 232 — a nuance investors may conflate, and the policy thread to monitor most closely. The risk is binary and outside management's control.

Other risks are secondary: cyclical end-market exposure (soft commercial aerospace and semiconductor aluminum demand in 2025), ~$3.1B combined goodwill/intangibles (30% of equity, never impaired), serial-acquisition execution dependence (no deals in 2025), and ~$950M of variable-rate SOFR debt (~$6.8M earnings sensitivity per 100bp).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is benign and offers no edge in either direction. Near-term ATM IV of 26.3% sits essentially in line with 25.4% one-year realized volatility (0.9% spread, 8th percentile within the current chain), so the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst. The term structure is flat (26.3% near, 27.7% far), distributing uncertainty evenly. Put/call OI of 0.14 and volume of 0.22 are call-heavy, not bearish, though the 7.4% near-term put skew indicates modestly elevated demand for downside protection. IV has fallen 3.8pp since early May as the stock rose 4.7%, consistent with resolved post-earnings uncertainty rather than rising risk. Short interest is low at 2.2% of float (2.5 days to cover). The cheap IV and absence of stress pricing reflect a market that views the recovery as priced and uneventful, consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Section 232 tariffs hold at 50% through 2026, sustaining record aluminum and tight carbon steel pricing. RS continues taking 7-8 points of relative volume share, value-added mix keeps lifting margins, and the company resumes accretive M&A from a fortress balance sheet with $529M of buyback authorization remaining. FY2026 EPS reaches the ~$19.58 consensus and FY2027 ~$20.86, and the market awards a low-20s multiple on durable share gains. This supports a price toward $430-450 (~21-22x FY2027 EPS), roughly 10-15% upside.

**Bear Case.** Section 232 is challenged, lessened, or unwound. Imports increase, U.S. metal prices fall, and RS's selling price and margin tailwind reverses. Earnings revert toward the 2024-2025 trough range ($14-$16 EPS) on a non-demand-driven price decline, and the multiple compresses to the high-teens as the policy premium evaporates. This implies $260-290 (~18-19x trough EPS), roughly 25-35% downside. The 52-week low of $260.31 sets a recent reference for this scenario.

The risk/reward is unfavorably skewed: roughly 10-15% upside in the bull case against 25-35% downside in the bear case, with the swing factor being a binary policy variable the company cannot control and explicitly flags. That asymmetry, combined with stalling near-term estimate revisions and a price above all analyst targets, is the concrete reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY despite a genuinely high-quality business. The validation concern that a 6.25 score warrants a BUY is rebutted by a specific, falsifiable condition: the FY2026 consensus that justifies the current multiple is contingent on Section 232 remaining at 50%, and the bear-case downside (-25% to -35%) far exceeds the 15% threshold required for a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) and any developments on Section 232 — legislative, executive, or judicial.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward $300-320 (high-teens multiple on FY2026 estimates) without fundamental deterioration, restoring a margin of safety and bringing bear-case downside under 15%.
- Confirmation that volume share gains and ~29%+ FIFO gross margins are sustaining independent of tariff-driven pricing (i.e., demand-driven rather than policy-driven earnings), evidenced by two consecutive quarters of price-neutral tons growth above industry.
- 30-day estimate revisions turning decisively positive again (current quarter and next quarter both >+3%), confirming the 90-day uptrend is not stalling.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A confirmed legal, legislative, or executive action removing or materially reducing Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs.
- A reversal of the share-gain trend (RS tons growth falling below MSCI industry shipments for two consecutive quarters).
- A goodwill impairment charge or a Q2 EPS miss exceeding 10% signaling that the recovery has stalled.

## Conclusion

Reliance is a high-quality, well-managed, fortress-balance-sheet operator that is genuinely winning share in a fragmented, contracting industry. The Q1 2026 recovery is real, and the 2025 "decline" was largely a LIFO optical effect that masked operational margin expansion. None of that is in dispute. The dispute is price and dependency: at $391, near the 52-week high and above every published analyst target, the stock discounts a FY2026 earnings recovery that rests almost entirely on Section 232 tariffs remaining at 50% — an asymmetric, binary, policy-controlled variable that management itself identifies as the central risk. With near-term estimate revisions stalling and bear-case downside (25-35%) materially exceeding bull-case upside (10-15%), the risk/reward does not justify a long at this level.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** This is a name to own on weakness toward $300-320, or once durable, demand-driven earnings power can be separated from the tariff premium. Existing holders are well-compensated by a 67-year dividend streak and a disciplined buyback, but new capital should wait for a better entry or for confirmation that the share-gain franchise can carry earnings without the policy tailwind.
