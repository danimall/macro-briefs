## Executive Summary

Domino's Pizza trades at $335, down 33% from its 52-week high of $499 and now below the lowest Wall Street price target of $340. The stock has repriced from ~26x forward earnings to ~16.9x FY2026E EPS ($19.83), reflecting three consecutive quarterly earnings misses, Q1 2026 U.S. same-store sales of just +0.9% (vs. +2.7% consensus), and a broader macro environment of consumer retrenchment and food cost inflation. The sell-side consensus still sees significant upside (median target $470), but the earnings revision trajectory is negative and management has guided down to "low-single-digit" U.S. comps for 2026.

The underlying franchise business model remains structurally excellent: 99% franchised, 13.6% FCF margins, $672M in annual free cash flow, an Altman Z-Score of 4.96, and three consecutive years of operating margin expansion. At 16.9x forward earnings, much of the bad news appears priced in, but the Q1 miss and negative estimate revisions suggest the earnings base itself may still be declining. The $4.8B ABS debt load with $1.34B maturing in 2027 adds refinancing risk in a higher-rate environment.

This is a HOLD. The structural quality of the business argues against AVOID at this valuation, but active deterioration in same-store sales, insider selling (no purchases), and the consumer spending environment make it premature to recommend accumulation.

## Company Overview

Domino's is the world's largest pizza company by store count, operating over 22,100 locations across 90+ markets. Approximately 99% of stores are franchise-owned, creating an asset-light model where the company earns through three channels: franchise royalties and fees (20.6% of revenue), supply chain sales to franchisees (60.5%), and a small but shrinking base of 262 company-owned stores (7.6%). An advertising fund contributes the remaining 11.3%.

Fiscal 2025 consolidated revenue was $4.94B, but global system retail sales totaled $20.1B — the 4:1 ratio between system sales and reported revenue underscores how much economic value flows through the franchise network. Digital ordering exceeds 85% of U.S. retail sales, and the company operates 22 regional supply chain centers in the U.S. plus 5 in Canada, providing vertical integration that smaller competitors cannot replicate.

The "Hungry for MORE" strategic framework targets same-store sales growth, net unit expansion (776 units added in FY2025), and margin improvement through operational leverage. International operations represent 68% of total store count (14,956 stores) but only 6.9% of consolidated revenue, as the company earns only royalties on these stores.

The margin expansion story was intact through FY2025. Gross margin improvement reflects the increasing share of high-margin franchise royalties (zero cost of sales) relative to lower-margin supply chain revenue. Supply chain gross margin itself improved 40bps to 11.5%. Company-owned store margins declined 240bps to 14.3% on food basket cost increases (+3.5%) and rising insurance costs, but these stores are a diminishing portion of the business.

**Q1 2026 marked a meaningful deterioration.** Revenue of $1.15B (+3.5% YoY) missed expectations by $10-20M. EPS of $4.13 missed the $4.27 consensus by 3.3%. U.S. same-store sales of +0.9% represented a sharp deceleration from +3.0% in FY2025 and missed the +2.7% Street estimate by 180bps. A $30M pre-tax DPC Dash write-down further weighed on results.

**Earnings Track Record:** 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters. This is a statistically significant pattern of over-estimation by the Street and/or operational underperformance.

**Capital Structure:** Total long-term debt is $4.81B, entirely in ABS notes backed by franchise royalty and supply chain cash flows. Stockholders' equity is -$3.9B, reflecting cumulative buybacks and dividends exceeding retained earnings. The weighted average borrowing rate is 3.8%, favorable but rising — the September 2025 refinancing replaced ~$1.14B at ~4% with $1B at 4.93-5.22%. The maturity wall is real: $1.34B due in 2027 and $836M in 2028. Each 100bps increase in refinancing cost on the 2027 maturities adds ~$13.4M to annual interest expense.

**Capital Return:** FY2025 saw $355M in buybacks and $237M in dividends ($592M total, 88% of FCF). A new $1B buyback authorization was announced with Q1 2026 earnings. The post-Q1 dividend rate of $7.96 annualized implies a 2.4% yield at $335.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture has deteriorated across multiple dimensions:

**Same-Store Sales:** The deceleration from +3.0% (FY2025) to +0.9% (Q1 2026) is the primary concern. Management attributed weakness to winter weather and consumer sentiment, then guided for "low-single-digit" full-year 2026 U.S. comps — effectively acknowledging the prior ~3% target is off the table. International same-store sales turned negative, contributing to global SSS of -0.4% in Q1. The promotional environment is intensifying ("Best Deal Ever" and similar campaigns), which risks margin compression to chase traffic.

**Unit Growth:** Net 776 store additions in FY2025 is solid, though international closures were notable at 349 stores. Management's stated target of 1,100+ global net openings per year appears ambitious given the international closure rate and macro headwinds.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates have moved from $19.60 (90 days ago) to $19.83 (today) — a modest +1.2% increase — but the 30-day direction has turned negative (-0.2%). Next-year estimates are declining on both 30-day and 90-day horizons (-0.3% and -0.6% respectively). The positive 90-day revision for FY2026 predates the Q1 miss and likely reflects stale optimism. Given the 3-of-4 miss pattern, current consensus of $19.83 may still be too high.

**Structural Growth Drivers:** Digital ordering penetration (85%+), continued refranchising (reducing capital intensity), and international unit expansion in underpenetrated markets remain valid long-term growth drivers. Near-term execution against a weakening consumer is the binding constraint.

## Competitive Landscape

Domino's competitive advantages remain intact:

1. **Scale-driven supply chain:** 22 U.S. regional supply chain centers provide procurement efficiency and logistics advantages that smaller pizza chains cannot match. Supply chain gross margin improved to 11.5%.

2. **Technology leadership:** 85%+ digital ordering penetration, proprietary apps, and delivery logistics optimization. The company's technology stack is a genuine moat in QSR pizza.

3. **Brand and franchisee economics:** 22,100+ stores, 99% franchised, with a brand that generates $20.1B in global retail sales. Franchisee unit economics are well-documented and support continued expansion.

4. **Delivery infrastructure:** Unlike competitors who rely heavily on third-party aggregators, Domino's controls its own delivery fleet in most markets, giving it better customer data and margin control.

However, competitive trajectory shows some weakening:

- Third-party aggregator partnerships (now embraced after years of resistance) dilute the proprietary channel advantage and carry lower store-level profitability as acknowledged in the 10-K.
- The promotional environment is intensifying across QSR, pressuring Domino's to spend more to maintain traffic.
- GLP-1 weight-loss drugs are now explicitly listed as a risk factor — the company itself acknowledges this could shift consumer preferences.
- International closure rates (349 in FY2025) suggest some markets are not generating adequate franchisee returns.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Consumer Spending Deterioration (HIGH — ACTIVE):** U.S. consumer sentiment is at multi-year lows. The Q1 SSS miss is the clearest manifestation, and management's guidance downgrade confirms they see this persisting.

2. **Refinancing Risk (HIGH — APPROACHING):** The September 2025 refinancing showed a ~100-120bps increase in borrowing costs. The next refinancing on the 2027 maturities could add meaningful annual interest expense, directly compressing EPS.

3. **Earnings Estimate Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** Three misses in four quarters, negative 30-day revision momentum, and management guidance suggesting below-consensus growth. FY2026 consensus of $19.83 may still be 3-5% too high.

4. **Food Cost Inflation (MODERATE):** Cheese (~25% of food basket), combined with energy-driven logistics costs, could pressure both company-owned store margins and franchisee economics. Franchisee margin compression eventually flows back to Domino's through reduced unit growth willingness and potential closures.

5. **GLP-1 Secular Risk (LOW-MODERATE, LONG-TERM):** Explicitly acknowledged by management. Impact will unfold over years, not quarters, but the direction is negative for pizza demand.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($400-$470, 19-40% upside):**
- Q1 was weather-affected and represents the trough in U.S. SSS growth. Q2 and Q3 benefit from easier comparisons and promotional effectiveness.
- The franchise model's FCF generation and new $1B buyback provide mechanical EPS support through share count reduction.
- At 16.9x forward earnings, a reversion to even 20x on current estimates implies $397; any earnings recovery pushes this higher.
- International unit growth reaccelerates, and the DPC Dash write-down was a one-time charge.
- 19 of 33 analysts rate BUY/Strong BUY, with median target of $470.

**Bear Case ($278-$310, 7-17% downside):**
- Consumer weakness intensifies through 2026 as inflation erodes discretionary spending. U.S. SSS turn flat or negative.
- Promotional intensity to maintain traffic compresses operating margins, unwinding the 100bps gain from FY2023-2025.
- 2027 refinancing occurs at 5.5-6.0%, adding $20-30M in annual interest expense.
- FY2026 EPS comes in at $18.00-$18.50, making the current multiple 18-19x — still not cheap enough to attract value buyers.
- International closures accelerate as master franchisees face similar consumer headwinds globally.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of U.S. SSS growth ≥2.0%, demonstrating consumer resilience and promotional effectiveness.
2. Price decline to $278-$300 range (14-15x FY2026E), where the FCF yield exceeds 5% and downside is mechanically limited by buyback support.
3. 2027 refinancing completed at ≤5.5% weighted average rate, removing the maturity wall overhang.

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of negative U.S. SSS growth, indicating structural demand impairment.
2. FY2026 EPS tracking below $18.00 through H1 2026 actuals.
3. Franchisee unit economics deterioration evidenced by net U.S. closures exceeding net openings for any quarter.

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026) will reveal whether Q1 was weather-affected or the beginning of a sustained deceleration. Also monitor the 2027 refinancing timeline — management will need to begin this process by late 2026.

## Conclusion

DPZ at $335 presents a balanced risk/reward profile that does not support either a BUY or AVOID recommendation. The business model is structurally sound, with dominant competitive positioning in QSR pizza through technology and supply chain advantages. At 16.9x FY2026E, the stock has repriced meaningfully from the 25-26x multiple of early 2025, and trading below the lowest Wall Street target ($340) historically signals oversold conditions.

Against this, three consecutive quarterly misses, negative estimate revision momentum, management's own guidance downgrade, no insider buying, elevated short interest, and continued consumer spending deterioration all argue for patience. The appropriate posture is HOLD — monitor Q2 SSS trends and the refinancing process before committing capital in either direction. A decline to the $278-$300 range (14-15x forward) would create a compelling entry point where the FCF yield and buyback support provide adequate margin of safety.
