## Executive Summary

Southwest Gas Holdings completed a multi-year corporate transformation in 2025, divesting its Centuri utility infrastructure services subsidiary for ~$1.3B in proceeds and emerging as a pure-play regulated natural gas distribution utility. The company now serves 2.28 million customers across Arizona, Nevada, and California, monopoly positions in two of the fastest-growing states in the US. The Centuri proceeds were used to eliminate ~$1.6B in net debt, taking the balance sheet from leveraged conglomerate to conservatively capitalized utility.

The forward growth story centers on the $1.7 billion Great Basin Expansion Project, which secured binding shipper agreements in December 2025 for 800 MMcf/day of new pipeline capacity serving northern Nevada (likely data center demand). This single project could nearly triple Great Basin's rate base. Combined with consistent 1.6% annual customer growth and pending rate cases in Arizona and Nevada, management guides 12-14% EPS CAGR through 2030, front-loaded in 2028-2029 when the expansion enters service.

The stock trades at 27.3x trailing EPS ($3.31), a premium to gas utility peers at 17-20x. However, trailing EPS is depressed relative to forward trajectory: 2026 guided EPS of $4.17-$4.32 implies a forward P/E of ~21x, and if the 12-14% CAGR materializes, 2029 EPS would reach $6.00-$6.50. The material weakness disclosure (state tax apportionment error, January 2026) and executive turnover create near-term governance overhang, but the discrete nature of the issue and ongoing remediation limit systemic risk. At $90.46, the risk/reward favors accumulation given the visibility of the earnings ramp and continued analyst consensus (7 of 8 Buy ratings).

## Company Overview

Southwest Gas Holdings operates as a single-segment regulated natural gas distribution utility through its wholly-owned subsidiary Southwest Gas Corporation. The company distributes natural gas to 2.281 million customers: 1.224 million in Arizona, 849,000 in Nevada, and 208,000 in California. Over 85% of operating margin comes from residential and small commercial customers. Full revenue decoupling in all three states insulates margin from weather variability and conservation effects.

The company also owns Great Basin Gas Transmission, a FERC-regulated interstate pipeline serving northern Nevada and California, which is the vehicle for the expansion project. Arizona law prohibits municipalities from banning or restricting natural gas use, providing structural regulatory protection against electrification risk.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is unusually strong for a gas utility:

**Near-term (2026):** Guided EPS of $4.17-$4.32 represents 28-33% growth over 2025 continuing EPS of $3.25. Drivers include full-year benefit of Arizona rate increases, new Nevada rate case (expected effective October 2026), and continued customer growth.

**Medium-term (2027-2030):** Management targets 12-14% EPS CAGR, front-loaded to 2028-2029 when Great Basin Expansion enters service. The math: $4.25 (2026 midpoint) growing at 13% annually = ~$6.15 by 2029 and ~$6.95 by 2030.

**Customer growth:** ~37,000 new meter sets annually (1.6% growth), driven by population migration into Arizona and Nevada and data center development in northern Nevada.

**Great Basin Expansion ($1.7B):** Binding precedent agreements for 800 MMcf/day secured December 2025. Construction begins Q4 2027, in-service late 2028. This nearly triples Great Basin's rate base (currently ~$191M post-settlement). At FERC's authorized 9.76% pre-tax return, the expansion could generate $60-80M in incremental pre-tax income by 2029.

**Rate base growth:** Total rate base growing from ~$3.9B currently (per Arizona filing) toward $6-7B by 2030 across all jurisdictions.

## Competitive Landscape

SWX occupies a structurally advantaged competitive position:

1. **Monopoly franchises** in two of the three fastest-growing states (Arizona and Nevada), with population growth outpacing the national average by 2-3x.

2. **Regulatory protection against displacement.** Arizona explicitly prohibits gas bans. Nevada's SB 281 reinforces natural gas in resource planning.

3. **Full revenue decoupling** eliminates volumetric risk — the company earns its authorized return regardless of weather or conservation trends.

4. **Data center demand tailwind.** Northern Nevada's emergence as a major data center corridor (Reno/Sparks) drives the Great Basin Expansion. This is structural demand growth that didn't exist 5 years ago.

5. **First-mover on pipeline infrastructure.** Great Basin's precedent agreements secured the only large-scale pipeline expansion serving this demand pocket. Alternatives would take 3-5+ years to develop.

The competitive position is improving. Customer growth is accelerating from data center demand. The regulatory environment in Arizona and Nevada is constructive, with recent rate cases delivering outcomes at or near filed amounts. California exposure (12% of margin) is the only territory with deteriorating competitive dynamics (cap-and-trade, potential allowance reallocation).

## Risk Assessment

**Material Weakness (High, but Discrete):** The January 2026 material weakness disclosure related to state tax apportionment calculations following the Centuri divestiture is concerning for governance but appears technically bounded. It affects deferred tax calculations, not cash taxes or operating performance. Remediation is in progress. The risk is that additional errors surface or that remediation takes longer than expected, creating continued market uncertainty.

**Great Basin Execution Risk (Moderate-High):** FERC approval still required. The 10-K risk factor explicitly mentions "limited availability of contract labor and critical materials (steel pipe, valves, compression equipment)." Cost overruns or delays would compress returns and delay the EPS inflection. Binding shipper agreements de-risk the demand side.

**Capital Needs (Moderate):** The $6.3B five-year capex plan funded at negative current FCF will require ~$2-3B in external capital (equity + debt issuance). Dilution from ATM equity issuance could partially offset EPS growth. Current authorized ROEs of 9.5-9.84% must be earned to justify continued investment.

**Executive Turnover (Moderate):** Multiple executive departures in 2025-2026. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges this could "limit or delay ability to deploy on plans." CEO Karen Haller remains in place.

**Regulatory Risk (Low-Moderate):** Arizona and Nevada are constructive. California is challenging but represents only 12% of margin. Unfavorable rate case outcomes would compress the growth trajectory.

## Conclusion

Southwest Gas Holdings combines structural growth (12-14% EPS CAGR), monopoly positioning in high-growth territories, and a recently cleaned-up balance sheet. The Centuri divestiture removed conglomerate discount overhang, and the Great Basin Expansion provides a specific, high-visibility growth catalyst differentiating SWX from peers trading at 13-20x earnings.

The 27x trailing valuation premium reflects depressed trailing earnings rather than excessive forward expectations. At 21x 2026E and 15x 2029E, the stock is reasonably priced for a utility delivering double-digit earnings growth. The material weakness and executive turnover create governance overhang that will take 1-2 quarters to resolve.

With 7 of 8 analysts at Buy, binding shipper agreements supporting the guided growth rate, constructive regulatory jurisdictions, and a clean balance sheet positioned to fund $6.3B in rate-base investment, SWX offers a favorable risk-adjusted return for investors willing to look past near-term governance uncertainty toward the 2028-2029 earnings ramp. The 2.9% dividend yield provides modest income while waiting.
