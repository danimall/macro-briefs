**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TXNM Energy is trading as a merger-arbitrage instrument rather than as a utility. On May 18, 2025, the company (the former PNM Resources) signed a definitive all-cash agreement to be acquired by Blackstone Infrastructure at $61.25 per share. With the stock at $57.36, the market is pricing a gross spread of roughly 6.8% to deal value. That spread is wide for a transaction with four of six required approvals already secured (HSR, FCC, FERC, PUCT), and it reflects residual risk concentrated almost entirely in the final and most difficult approval, the New Mexico Public Regulation Commission (NMPRC), along with the routine NRC sign-off.

The risk profile changed materially in June 2026. NMPRC hearing examiners issued a recommended decision finding that the May 2025 $400M private placement of TXNM stock to Blackstone violated New Mexico law by closing without prior commission approval under the state's change-of-control threshold. The examiners recommend reversing the stock sale, imposing roughly $300,000 in fines, and effectively restarting the acquisition process. The decision is not final — commissioners must rule after a comment period — but it is a negative signal from the precise regulator whose approval is still outstanding, and from one with a track record of rejecting utility mergers (NMPRC killed the Avangrid/Iberdrola acquisition of PNM in 2021). The monetary penalty is trivial; the procedural and timing damage is the real issue.

We rate TXNM HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The arbitrage is genuine but binary: if the deal closes, holders capture ~6.8% gross plus the ~3% dividend yield over the holding period; if NMPRC forces a restart or rejection, the stock likely re-rates toward standalone value in the low-$40s to $50, implying 13% to 25%+ downside. We do not have a defensible edge on the probability or timing of NMPRC's final ruling, and standalone fundamentals — a 2025 EPS collapse to $1.48 from $2.67, structurally negative free cash flow, and an aggressive capital plan funded by continuous equity issuance — do not support owning the equity absent the deal. The expected value is roughly balanced; we would neither initiate nor short here.

## Company Overview

TXNM is a New Mexico–incorporated utility holding company serving approximately 842,000 customers through two regulated electric utilities:

- **PNM (Public Service Company of New Mexico):** A vertically integrated electric utility regulated by the NMPRC and FERC. PNM owns generation, including a 7.3% interest in the Palo Verde nuclear station, a Four Corners coal interest slated to exit by 2031, gas plants, and 3,244 MW of solar, storage, wind, and geothermal capacity. It operates under New Mexico's Energy Transition Act, which mandates 100% zero-carbon generation by 2045; the company targets carbon-free by 2040 and coal-free by 2031.
- **TNMP (Texas-New Mexico Power):** A transmission-and-distribution-only utility in Texas, regulated by the PUCT within ERCOT. TNMP carries no commodity or generation risk and benefits from a constructive Texas regulatory framework with rider-based cost recovery (TCOS, DCRF) and favorable 2023/2025 legislation enabling faster recovery. This is the growth engine.

The strategic story is a deliberate shift of rate base toward Texas (TNMP) and FERC transmission, which now exceed half of consolidated rate base. This moves the company toward jurisdictions with more constructive and timely recovery, and away from the historically contentious NMPRC, which remains the central obstacle to the Blackstone deal closing.

## Financial Analysis

The 2025 results show a sharp earnings deterioration that is largely, though not entirely, non-recurring.

- **Revenue:** $2.17B in 2025 versus $1.97B in 2024, up 9.9%, driven by TNMP rate relief and load growth plus a PNM 2025 rate change.
- **Net earnings to TXNM:** Fell to $151.4M ($1.48 diluted EPS) from $242.2M ($2.67) in 2024. The drivers: a $58.8M pension settlement charge at PNM, an unfavorable ~$41.4M swing in capacity arrangements at PNM, milder weather, higher interest charges, and ~$15.4M of unallocated merger costs at Corporate. Diluted share count also rose to 102.4M from 90.6M on heavy equity issuance, compounding the per-share decline.
- **Segment divergence:** TNMP earnings rose $20.8M on Texas growth; PNM earnings fell $104.6M on the items above. The Texas wires business is performing; the New Mexico generation business is the drag and the more volatile leg.
- **Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $584M against capex of $1.20B produced free cash flow of roughly negative $611M, a FCF margin near negative 28%. This is structural and intentional: a utility in a heavy build cycle with an $11.1B 2026–2030 capital plan ($10.2B of construction). Funding depends on continuous capital-markets access.
- **Leverage:** D/E around 2.5x, long-term debt of $5.1B, total capitalization 61.3% debt / 38.5% equity. These figures are normal for a rate-base-funded regulated utility. Screening tools flag an Altman Z of 0.89 ("distress") and a current ratio of 0.55, but these are false positives for this business model: ratings are investment grade across the structure (TXNM Baa3/BBB-; PNM Baa2/BBB; TNMP Baa1/BBB+), with stable outlooks, and both S&P and Moody's characterized the Blackstone transaction as credit-supportive.

The financial health score of 4.5 reflects this duality: operationally sound and investment-grade, but with structurally negative FCF, rising leverage into a higher-for-longer rate environment, and a 2025 earnings base depressed by both recurring (interest, share dilution) and non-recurring (pension, weather, capacity) factors. The screen "distress" reading is noise; the genuine concern is the equity-dilution-and-debt funding model under elevated rates, consistent with our cautious stance on financing-heavy regulated utilities generally.

## Growth Analysis

The standalone growth story rests on TNMP. Texas data-center load grew 70.5% in 2025, the system hit a new peak in September 2025, and Permian Basin reliability investments are backed by ERCOT mandates and favorable PUCT/legislative cost recovery. PNM also serves a Meta data center under a renewable-energy rate rider, and PNM industrial load grew 14.3% in 2025, largely data-center driven. This load-growth profile is precisely what an infrastructure private-equity buyer covets and explains the strategic rationale for the Blackstone bid.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and ultimately not a meaningful input here. Current-year EPS consensus rose to $3.00 from $2.93 ninety days ago (+2.6%), and the current-quarter estimate rose 15.8% over 90 days, but next-quarter and next-year revisions are flat-to-negative, and the company has missed EPS in four consecutive quarters (most recently March 2026 at $0.21 versus a $0.31 estimate, a 31.4% miss). The +15.8% current-quarter revision is a small absolute move on a low base, set against a falling-to-flat broader revision trend and a clean four-quarter miss streak. We do not treat this as a meaningful positive growth signal.

For a stock trading on deal value, growth prospects matter only in the break scenario, where TNMP's load growth supports a respectable standalone trajectory but PNM's regulatory lag and generation volatility cap the consolidated rate. The growth score of 4.0 reflects a genuine but uneven underlying growth engine, an EPS base that fell sharply in 2025, and a recommendation context where standalone growth is the downside scenario, not the thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

The only valuation metric that matters is the deal spread. Layering the ~3% dividend yield onto the gross spread, total return to a clean close is in the high single digits over a holding period likely measured in several months to a year. That spread is wide relative to the 1–3% typical of a near-close utility deal with most approvals secured, and the width is the market's explicit pricing of NMPRC and $400M-stock-sale risk.

On trailing fundamentals, the headline multiple is uninformative. Trailing P/E of ~42x sits on a 2025 EPS base ($1.48) depressed by non-recurring charges. On normalized earnings power — consensus current-year EPS of $3.00 — the stock trades around 19x, in line with the electric-utility peer median of 19.7x (POR 22.4x, IDA 23.6x, OTTR 13.3x, MGEE 19.7x, HE 17.9x). The dividend yield of ~3.0% is modestly above the peer median of 2.5%. None of this argues for owning the equity on fundamentals; it argues that the standalone value, absent the deal, would be roughly peer-consistent, which is where the stock would gravitate in a break.

The analyst price targets (low $61.00, high $61.25, mean $61.15) cluster tightly at the deal price, confirming the Street is modeling the stock as merger arb rather than as a fundamental utility. These targets are not floors in the usual sense; they are the deal value, and they collapse to standalone value if the transaction fails.

The valuation score of 4.5 captures the asymmetry: limited upside (capped at $61.25 plus dividends), genuine downside to the low-$40s/$50 standalone range, and a spread that compensates for the risk but does not represent a mispricing we can confidently arbitrage given the NMPRC overhang. We deliberately avoid rewarding the "cheap on normalized earnings" framing, because for a deal-contingent name that normalized multiple only matters in the scenario where the holder is sitting on a 13–25% loss.

## Competitive Landscape

TNMP is a high-quality asset: a wires-only T&D utility in deregulated ERCOT, with explosive load growth, no commodity risk, and a constructive recovery framework. Its competitive position is improving, with data-center and Permian load as structural tailwinds and regulatory mechanics that support timely cost recovery. PNM is the weaker leg: vertically integrated, exposed to commodity and weather volatility, operating under an aggressive decarbonization mandate, and regulated by an unpredictable commission.

The deliberate rate-base tilt toward Texas and FERC transmission is a sound strategic response to NMPRC risk and has improved the overall franchise quality, supporting a competitive-position score of 6.0 — up from earlier in our coverage and consistent with the trajectory. The franchise quality is why Blackstone is paying a premium, and the same NMPRC dynamic that makes the Texas tilt attractive is the obstacle to monetizing that quality through the deal.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by the merger; standalone risks are secondary unless the deal breaks.

**Merger risks (primary):**
1. **NMPRC approval is the gating item.** Four approvals are complete — HSR expired, FCC granted, FERC approved February 20, 2026 (explicitly rejecting data-center/private-equity/cross-subsidization objections), and the PUCT approved a unanimous settlement February 6, 2026. Outstanding: NMPRC and the NRC. NMPRC is the hardest, having rejected the Avangrid/Iberdrola–PNM deal in 2021.
2. **The June 2026 hearing-examiner finding** recommends reversing the stock sale, imposing fines, and restarting the acquisition process. The decision is not final, but the procedural and timing damage outweighs the trivial fine, and this is the single most important item in the analysis.
3. **Termination fee of $210M** payable by TXNM under certain circumstances; the deal carries no financing condition.
4. **Change-of-control debt mechanics.** Signing the merger triggered a change-of-control event of default under TXNM/TNMP debt; the company executed extensive 2025 refinancings (TNMP tendered/prepaid $1.5B of first mortgage bonds, drew and repaid a $1.08B backstop term loan, amended covenants). PNM debt is not triggered.

**Standalone risks (relevant in a break):** regulatory lag on the $10.2B capex plan amid affordability pressure; wildfire liability in both the arid Southwest and the Texas Gulf Coast; water/drought risk for PNM generation; nuclear operational and decommissioning exposure; coal-mine reclamation costs above recovery caps (PNM took unrecoverable San Juan reclamation charges in 2025); and the OBBBA acceleration of IRA clean-energy credit phase-outs.

The risk score of 3.5 reflects the asymmetric, binary, and currently-elevated nature of the deal risk, recently sharpened by the examiner finding: a specific, dated, falsifiable catalyst with material downside not fully priced at a 6.8% spread.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility fell from 27.7% on May 28 to 19.6% on June 24, an 8.1-point decline, even as the stock fell 3.4% over the period. Falling IV through a price decline is unusual and suggests the options market is pricing reduced near-term event volatility, consistent with a view that the NMPRC final ruling and close are not imminent, or that the examiner finding, while negative, did not raise the probability of a sudden binary resolution. The 7.0% short interest (4.7 days to cover) is modest and does not indicate a crowded directional bet either way. Net, options positioning is not flagging an imminent catalyst, which aligns with the timing slippage implied by the examiner's restart recommendation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($61.25)
NMPRC commissioners decline to adopt the examiners' restart recommendation, treat the $400M stock-sale issue as a discrete, fineable procedural matter, and approve the merger (potentially with conditions/concessions) in H2 2026 or shortly after. The NRC approval follows as routine. Holders capture the ~6.8% gross spread plus accrued dividends. Blackstone's strong incentive to close (no financing condition, $210M reverse exposure dynamics) and the credit-supportive ratings posture support a constructive base case for completion.

### Bear Case (~$43–$50)
NMPRC adopts the examiners' recommendation, forcing a reversal of the stock sale and a process restart, or rejects the transaction outright as it did with Avangrid/Iberdrola in 2021. The deal collapses or is delayed indefinitely. The stock re-rates to standalone value. Pre-deal PNM Resources traded in the low-$40s to ~$50; against a depressed 2025 EPS base, structurally negative FCF, ongoing equity dilution, and a higher-for-longer rate path that raises the cost of the build-cycle financing model, the standalone equity is worth meaningfully less than current price, implying 13% to 25%+ downside.

The expected value is roughly balanced. The probability-weighted upside (high-single-digit total return on a clean close) is comparable in magnitude to the probability-weighted downside (a sharp re-rating on a break), with the June examiner finding having shifted the distribution toward the bear tail relative to where it stood before.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — we lack a defensible edge on the binary NMPRC outcome, and the standalone fundamentals do not support owning the equity independent of the deal.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NMPRC commissioners reject the examiners' restart recommendation and signal a path to approval (e.g., adopting the $400M issue as a discrete fineable matter), narrowing the spread risk to timing only.
- A definitive NMPRC approval (with or without conditions) plus the routine NRC sign-off, at which point the residual spread becomes near-riskless carry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NMPRC commissioners adopt the examiners' recommendation to reverse the stock sale and restart the process, or any commission action materially raising the probability of rejection.
- Either party signals willingness to terminate, or merger-protection mechanics begin unwinding.
- A break, after which the name should be evaluated purely on standalone fundamentals — where the negative FCF, dilution, and rate-environment headwinds argue against ownership at the prevailing standalone price.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the NMPRC comment period and commission ruling on the examiners' recommended decision, with the H2-2026 close target at clear risk of slipping. Each NMPRC procedural filing is an event to monitor.

## Conclusion

TXNM should be evaluated as merger arbitrage, not as a utility. The ~6.8% gross spread plus a ~3% dividend offers high-single-digit total return on a clean close, set against 13–25%+ downside to standalone value on a break. The decision variable is the NMPRC's final ruling, freshly complicated by the June 2026 hearing-examiner finding that the $400M Blackstone stock placement violated state law and should be reversed — a self-inflicted procedural problem at the one regulator that has killed a PNM merger before. With four of six approvals secured and Blackstone strongly incentivized to close, completion remains the more likely outcome, but the examiner finding has widened the risk distribution.

We rate TXNM HOLD. We would not initiate a long here given the asymmetry and the genuine NMPRC uncertainty, and the borrow/squeeze mechanics and lack of an imminent negative catalyst date do not make it an attractive short. Existing holders awaiting the cash-out have a reasonable risk/reward but should treat the NMPRC ruling as the decisive checkpoint.
