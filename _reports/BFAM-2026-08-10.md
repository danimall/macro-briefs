## Executive Summary

Bright Horizons has delivered a genuine post-COVID earnings recovery: GAAP net income rose from $74.2M in FY2023 to $193.1M in FY2025, adjusted EPS from $3.47 (FY2024) to $4.55 (FY2025) to a guided $5.05–5.15 for FY2026, while the equity has lost roughly 41% from its August 2025 high and 79 percentage points of cumulative five-year total return against its own proxy peer group ($58.58 versus $137.95 on $100 invested at end-2020). The market is not disputing the earnings recovery. It is applying a materially lower multiple to it, and the reasons are identifiable.

Three of them matter. First, revenue growth has decelerated from +19.7% (FY2023) to a guided +5.3% to +6.4% for FY2026, with the implied H2 range of +4.0% to +6.0% below H1's +6.7%. Second, the segment mix is uncomfortable: Full Service center-based care is 71% of revenue but produced only 21% of FY2025 segment operating income and ran a GAAP operating loss in H2 2025, while Back-Up Care at 25% of revenue produced 70% of operating income. The equity value effectively rests on one quarter of the revenue base. Third, and most acutely, Ofsted served a Welfare Requirements Notice on June 22, 2026 covering all 247 Bright Horizons nurseries in England, citing "significant weaknesses in organisational safeguarding leadership, governance, oversight and practice" after finding breaches at 69 settings across 172 inspections. The compliance deadline was August 1. As of this writing there is no disclosed resolution, and the Q2 10-Q filed August 6 reported no material changes to risk factors and no material legal proceedings.

Against that unresolved backdrop, management borrowed. Total debt went from $947.2M to $1,287.4M between December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026, additional paid-in capital was depleted to zero, total equity fell from $1,339.3M to $943.5M, and $473.2M of stock was repurchased in six months at roughly $71.70 per share, or 2.89x H1 free cash flow of $163.7M. Net leverage rose from 1.66x to 2.22x TTM adjusted EBITDA. The buyback is not obviously mistimed on price (13–15x adjusted earnings), and it continued through July: share count in current market data (48.64M) sits about 609,000 below the June 30 balance-sheet count of 49.24M. But the financing raises the equity's sensitivity to a U.K. or Australian impairment at the moment those risks are live. We rate the stock REDUCE: the forward multiple is not stretched, the expected return from here is modestly positive on a probability-weighted basis, and the asymmetry runs the wrong way.

## Company Overview

Bright Horizons operates three segments. **Full Service** center-based child care ran 988 centers with capacity for approximately 112,500 children at June 30, 2026 (down from 1,010 and ~115,000 at year-end 2025) across the U.S., U.K., Netherlands, Australia and India. FY2025 segment revenue was $2,081.1M (+6.1%), of which tuition was $1,895.7M and employer management fees/subsidies $185.4M. Roughly 75% of centers operate on a "P&L" model where Bright Horizons bears enrollment risk and keeps tuition; a mature P&L center generates $1.8–2.8M of annual revenue at 20–25% gross margin and takes 12–24 months to break even. The remaining 25% are cost-plus, with the employer funding costs plus a capped management fee. Personnel is ~70% of center operating cost.

**Back-Up Care** (FY2025 revenue $728.0M, +19.3%, 30.4% operating margin) delivers employer-paid emergency care through a network of more than 5,500 providers with minimal owned fixed assets. **Educational Advisory & Other** ($124.5M, +9.1% in FY2025) administers tuition assistance and workforce education.

The employer-side franchise is real: 1,450+ clients including 220+ Fortune 500 companies, largest client ~1% of revenue, top ten ~8%, and roughly 95% client retention averaged over ten years. The revenue decomposition matters: parent-paid tuition of $1,895.7M is 64.6% of consolidated revenue, while employer-paid streams total approximately 35%. Despite the employer-benefits framing, roughly two-thirds of revenue is directly consumer-elastic, at disclosed average U.S. monthly tuition of $2,765 (infants), $2,565 (toddlers) and $2,175 (preschool).

## Financial Analysis

Consolidated trajectory:

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2,418.3M | $2,686.0M | $2,933.6M | $1,491.4M |
| Growth | +19.7% | +11.1% | +9.2% | +6.7% |
| GAAP operating income | $171.2M | $246.6M | $314.7M | $144.8M |
| GAAP operating margin | 7.08% | 9.18% | 10.73% | 9.71% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $352.1M | $409.3M | $487.4M | $226.2M |
| Net income | $74.2M | $140.2M | $193.1M | $74.7M |

The two-year margin expansion of 365bp through FY2025 is genuine. H1 2026 shows the first give-back at the GAAP line, driven by $19.1M of Q2 impairments and a 37.0% effective tax rate versus 27.2%.

**Segment economics are the analysis.** FY2025 segment operating income sums exactly to consolidated operating income ($66.1M + $221.6M + $27.0M = $314.665M), confirming corporate overhead is fully allocated and making segment margins comparable. On that basis:

| Segment | H1 2025 op. inc. | H2 2025 (derived) | H1 2026 op. inc. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full Service | $73.5M (7.0% margin) | –$7.4M (–0.7%) | $62.0M (5.6%) |
| Back-Up Care | $67.3M (23.1%) | $154.3M (~35.3%) | $75.9M (22.4%) |
| Ed Advisory | $7.5M (13.6%) | $19.5M (~28.0%) | $7.0M (12.6%) |

Full Service ran a GAAP operating loss in H2 2025. Most of that is impairment timing, but it establishes how thin the core segment's cushion is. As-reported Full Service margin has been 2.7% (FY2024), 3.2% (FY2025) and 5.6% (H1 2026), against the 20–25% mature-center gross margin the 10-K describes. The gap is operating leverage trapped in under-enrolled centers, and 47% of the same-center cohort still sits below 70% occupancy.

**Quality of earnings.** Full Service impairment and net lease-termination charges were $29.8M (FY2024), $47.0M (FY2025) and $19.1M in Q2 2026 alone, approximately $96M over ten quarters, all excluded from adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS. Against 56 center closures in 2024, 29 in 2025 and a net 22-center decline in H1 2026, with management expecting additional closures in 2026 and "actively monitoring" Australia, these charges are better modeled as a recurring economic cost of the build-and-prune center model than as episodic items. The FY2024-to-FY2025 escalation argues against the alternative reading, that this is the tail of a one-time post-COVID capacity reset, though the program's endpoint is not disclosed. The FY2025 GAAP-to-adjusted bridge is a 35% uplift ($261.5M adjusted versus $193.1M GAAP), built from $47.5M of impairment/lease termination, $30.6M of stock compensation and ~$4.0M of other items, less tax.

One offsetting point deserves airing: excluding the Q2 impairment, Full Service operating margin was approximately 7.9% versus 7.5% a year earlier ($25.06M + $19.14M over $557.3M of segment revenue). Underlying Full Service performance improved; the reported line did not.

**Cash flow conversion is deteriorating at the margin.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $350.7M against $92.2M of capex, for $258.5M of free cash flow (8.8% margin). H1 2026 OCF was $202.8M versus $220.4M, down 8.0%, against +6.7% revenue growth and +9% adjusted EBITDA growth. Cash taxes of $50.3M and cash interest of $28.2M are the identifiable drivers, alongside working-capital timing. This is one half-year in a business with large seasonal parent prepayments and should not be annualized. As a labeled sensitivity: persisting H1 conversion implies roughly $325M of FY2026 OCF; holding FY2025's 71.9% OCF/adjusted-EBITDA conversion against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $505.7M implies roughly $364M. The Q3 filing resolves it.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $163.7M, total debt $1,287.4M, operating lease liabilities $767.4M, equity $943.5M with additional paid-in capital at zero. Tangible book equity is negative $1,066.8M (equity less $1,818.9M goodwill less $191.4M intangibles); goodwill plus intangibles equal 53% of total assets. Working capital is negative $506.6M, but $287.7M of that is deferred tuition, which is structural float, not a liquidity gap. The Altman Z-Score of 1.68 is not diagnostic here: negative working capital is prepaid tuition, $767.4M of ASC-842 lease liabilities inflate the denominator in a model calibrated on pre-842 manufacturers, and $1.82bn of goodwill mechanically depresses the EBIT/assets and revenue/assets terms. The direction the score picks up, leverage up 56bp of turns and equity down 30% in six months, is correct even where the level is not.

Debt structure: Term Loan B $450M maturing August 2032 (SOFR+1.75%), a new $375M Term Loan A issued June 1, 2026 maturing April 2030, and $465.95M drawn on an upsized $1.0bn revolver ($206.0M classified current). Amortization is light, $4.7M for the rest of 2026, rising to $328.1M in 2030, so there is no near-term wall. The rate exposure is the issue: effectively all funded debt is floating, the blended rate rose from 4.42% (FY2025) to 4.87% in H1 2026 and is guided to 5.25–5.50%, and hedged notional falls from $550M to $300M after October 31, 2026 (roughly 23% of floating debt). A 100bp move on the ~$991M unhedged portion is $9.9M pretax, about $0.14 per share after tax. That is modest in isolation, but it compounds a rate path that has already lifted the blended cost.

**Return on capital.** FY2025 NOPAT of $225.2M over invested capital of $2,146.4M is 10.5% ROIC; capitalizing $813.1M of operating leases takes it to 7.6%. For a 2.2x-levered consumer services company, the lease-adjusted figure sits at or slightly below a plausible cost of capital, consistent with $1.82bn of goodwill that has not compounded. The headline ROE of 14.4% will print near 19–20% on unchanged earnings simply because equity has shrunk 30%. That would be a buyback artifact, not improved returns on capital.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance of $3.09–3.12bn revenue and $5.05–5.15 adjusted diluted EPS implies +5.3% to +6.4% revenue growth. Decomposing it, implied H2 revenue of $1,598.6–1,628.6M against H2 2025's $1,536.5M is +4.0% to +6.0%, slower than H1. Implied H2 adjusted EPS of $2.97–3.07 against $2.71 is +9.6% to +13.3%.

The gap between decelerating revenue and double-digit EPS growth is share count. On TTM adjusted net income of $266.2M, adjusted EPS at the pre-buyback base of ~55.9M shares would be roughly $4.76; after ~$13.0M of incremental after-tax interest on the $340M of new debt and using ~49.9M shares, it is roughly $5.07, accretion of about 6.5%, less than half the 11.5% share reduction, because interest absorbs a third of the benefit. Consensus of $5.11 for the current year and $5.89 for next year (+15%) is similarly buyback-weighted. Organic adjusted EPS growth is running in the mid-single digits.

Segment-level growth quality is mixed. Back-Up Care remains the engine but is decelerating: +19.3% (FY2025) to +19.0% in Q2 2026 to +16.1% for H1, with the H1 margin slipping from 23.1% to 22.4% even as Q2 improved from 25.2% to 26.0%. Whether that margin profile reflects a durable network-density advantage or a post-COVID utilization windfall that has yet to normalize is the single most consequential open question in the model, because this segment carries the valuation. Ed Advisory has stalled outright, from +9.1% in FY2025 to +0.4% in H1 2026 and –1.2% in Q2, with margin roughly halving to 12.6%, in a market where Guild Education and InStride compete directly. At 4.2% of revenue it does not move the valuation, but it removes an option-value leg from the multi-service platform case.

Full Service growth depends on occupancy, not units: center count is falling and capacity is down 2,500 children in six months. Q2 pricing was +4% against +3% wage growth, a 100bp spread on a segment earning 5.6% operating margin. The 10-K is explicit that teacher hiring shortfalls force the company to constrain enrollment or close classrooms to meet ratios, so labor scarcity caps revenue directly, not only cost.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: +1.9% for the current year and +2.5% for next year over 90 days, on nine covering analysts. This is consensus tracking a 4-beats-in-4-quarters record of non-GAAP beats (+4.4%, +1.1%, +0.5%, +16.6%), not the >30% revision velocity that would signal an underappreciated inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

At $73.245 on approximately 48.64M current shares, market capitalization is roughly $3.56bn. Adding net debt of $1,123.7M gives enterprise value of approximately $4.69bn ex-leases, or $5.45bn including operating lease liabilities.

| Metric | BFAM | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E, guided FY2026 adjusted EPS ($5.10) | 14.4x | 21.5x |
| P/E, next-year consensus ($5.89) | 12.4x | — |
| P/E, TTM GAAP diluted ($3.15) | 23.3x | — |
| EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA (ex-leases) | 9.3x | — |
| EV/EBITDAR (lease-adjusted) | ~7.8x | — |
| Dividend yield | 0.0% | 2.9% |

Two things are true simultaneously. On the company's adjusted basis, the stock is cheap in absolute terms and against a peer set (MCW 21.5x, FTDR 24.1x, ADT 10.6x, HRB 8.2x) whose median is 21.5x. On a GAAP basis it is 23.3x trailing, above the peer median, because the adjusted bridge is a 35% uplift and roughly $40–50M per year of that bridge is impairments tied to an ongoing closure program.

Treating impairments as recurring is the honest middle position. Deducting a $40M annual run-rate at a 28% tax rate is about $0.59 per share, putting economic EPS near $4.51 and the multiple near 16.2x. That is a discount to peers, but a much smaller one, and it is the number to underwrite against.

Is the market wrong? The identifiable case for mispricing rests on Full Service occupancy. If the 47% of centers currently below 70% enrolled migrate toward the 20–25% mature-center gross margin the 10-K describes, Full Service operating margin has several hundred basis points of latent leverage on a $2.1bn revenue base, and none of it requires new capital or new centers. Applied against Back-Up Care's standalone worth — at 12x FY2025 segment operating income of $221.6M, roughly $2.66bn, or 74% of market cap; at 15x, $3.32bn, or 92% — the market is ascribing very little to 988 centers carrying $574M of net fixed assets.

The counter-case is that a near-zero equity value for Full Service is defensible: the segment carries $1,570.8M of goodwill, absorbed ~$96M of impairments in ten quarters, ran a GAAP operating loss in H2 2025, and is where the U.K. and Australia risks sit. A segment multiple applied inside a 2.2x-levered parent is an assumption, not a market observation.

Sell-side targets bracket the range widely — low $68 (below spot), mean $89.44, median $93, high $99 — with ratings split 2 strong buy / 3 buy / 3 hold / 1 sell and no movement over four months. On a stock at 14x forward with decelerating revenue and a live regulatory action, those targets are not independent confirmation of anything; the $68 low and the $99 high describe the same disagreement we are describing.

Our scenario set:

- **Bull $98** — Ofsted remediation confirmed without registration conditions, occupancy cohort improvement continues on a clean like-for-like basis, Back-Up holds mid-teens growth at 26%+ Q margins, no Australia charge, buyback completes remaining $328.7M authorization. 16x FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$6.10 on a further-reduced share count. +34%.
- **Base $82** — Ofsted resolved through remediation with unquantified cost, revenue grows ~5%, a modest Australia charge lands, market applies 14x to next-year consensus $5.89. +12%.
- **Bear $52** — U.K. escalation (registration conditions, additional suspensions, parental withdrawal) and/or a material Australia goodwill impairment; Full Service occupancy stalls; the market derates to ~11.5x economic EPS of ~$4.50 with leverage amplifying. –29%.

Probability-weighted at 30/45/25 the expected return is roughly +8%, below a reasonable cost of equity for a consumer-services name with all-floating debt. At 32/48/20 it is roughly +11%, approximately fair. The result is highly sensitive to the Ofsted assumption: the binary is unresolved and unresolvable from available evidence.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is real and narrow. For employer-sponsored on-site and near-site child care at scale, the 10-K names only KinderCare Education in the U.S. and Busy Bees in the U.K. Winning a Fortune 500 on-site center is a multi-year, high-switching-cost sale, consistent with ~95% ten-year client retention, 220+ Fortune 500 relationships, and roughly one-third of clients buying more than one service. This is a B2B contracting moat, and it is improving on the Back-Up side, where 5,500 providers, 30.4% margins and employer relationships originated by Full Service create a network that is hard to replicate quickly. IAC/Care.com is the only named peer of consequence.

The moat does not extend to enrollment. Once a center exists it competes for individual families against Learning Care Group, Goddard, Primrose, G8 Education, Goodstart, Guardian, Affinity, Partou, KidsFoundation, CompaNanny and Kids Planet. Because 64.6% of consolidated revenue is parent-paid tuition, the majority of the revenue base sits on the less-protected side.

Trajectory by segment: Back-Up improving strongly, Full Service stable-to-eroding (center count down, occupancy still weak, structural work-from-home headwind explicitly named as a risk factor and consistent with 85 closures across 2024–25 plus a net 22 in H1 2026), Ed Advisory losing to Guild and InStride. The U.K. regulator's shift from reactive to systematic — 3,000 additional unannounced nursery inspections per year in England from September 2026 — cuts both ways. It raises the compliance cost base and the frequency of individual-center suspension events for every operator, with large branded chains most exposed because one incident triggers group-level scrutiny, as the estate-wide WRN demonstrates. It could also advantage a well-capitalized operator with centralized safeguarding infrastructure if BFAM remediates and smaller operators cannot afford to. Which effect dominates is the crux of the U.K. thesis and is unresolved.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The Ofsted Welfare Requirements Notice (dominant).** Following a serious safeguarding incident reported to Ofsted in September 2025, the regulator conducted 172 inspections, site visits and engagements between October 2025 and June 2026, identified breaches at 69 of 247 English settings, and on June 22, 2026 served a WRN across the entire English estate. Separately, the nursery at Furness General Hospital was suspended for six weeks on July 22 on reasonable belief that children may be at risk of harm, and a former employee at a north-west London nursery was convicted of 56 sexual offences, with some parents pursuing judicial review.

The structural problem is disclosed in the company's own 10-K: in the U.K. the license to operate is regulated nationally, so suspension or revocation risk exists at both center and country-wide level. Sizing the exposure requires estimation: the U.K. accounts for 9,250 of 13,750 non-North American employees, which applied to $842.7M of non-NA revenue implies roughly $565M, or ~19% of consolidated revenue. That is an employee-count proxy, not a disclosed figure. Also relevant: 49% of net fixed assets ($282.4M of $574.2M) sit outside North America, and Full Service goodwill is $1,570.8M.

Two readings are available and we cannot choose between them. The 10-Q filed August 6, five days after the deadline, reported no material risk-factor changes and no material legal proceedings, which is consistent with remediation inside existing risk language and with Ofsted's own statement that the majority of settings continue to meet requirements (69 of 247 is 28%). It is equally consistent with a matter management judges administratively significant but financially immaterial. What we can say is that an investor relying only on SEC filings would materially underweight this issue, and that the post-deadline outcome is undisclosed.

**2. Australia.** The 10-Q cites "more challenging enrollment trends and operating conditions" in Australia, states the company is actively monitoring and may close centers there, and quantifies a 1 percentage-point drag on Q2 Full Service tuition growth. Only About Children was acquired in 2022 with $106.5M of deferred consideration paid in January 2024. Against $1,570.8M of Full Service goodwill and a $6.3M goodwill impairment already taken elsewhere in the segment in Q2, Australia is the most likely candidate for the next charge. No Australia-specific revenue or exposure is disclosed.

**3. Leverage into a rising-rate cost regime.** Net leverage 2.22x against a 4.25:1.00 first-lien covenant leaves headroom, and there is no maturity wall before $328.1M in 2030. But all funded debt is floating, hedged notional drops to ~23% after October 31, 2026, and the blended cost is guided to 5.25–5.50% from 4.42%. Tangible book is negative $1.07bn, so the equity carries no asset support against an impairment.

**4. Consumer elasticity.** Roughly 65% of revenue is parent-paid tuition at $2,175–2,765 per child per month. The transmission is direct: a second earner losing a job removes both the need for center care and the ability to pay for it. This is a discretionary consumer exposure, and the burden of proof on any long position here is higher than the multiple alone suggests.

**5. Reflexive tax drag.** Q2 effective tax rate was 38.2% versus 27.4%; H1 37.0% versus 27.2%, driven by a $3.0M stock-compensation shortfall expense versus a $1.3M prior-year benefit. With the price below grant-date values, vesting now generates tax expense rather than windfall benefits, roughly $0.08 per share. This affects GAAP net income and the effective rate only, not operating income, adjusted metrics or cash flow, and reverses if the stock recovers.

**6. Insurance and litigation structure.** A captive subsidiary underwrites part of general liability and the 10-K flags risk to the availability of abuse coverage, which is the mechanism by which reputational events convert into P&L. A March 2026 report of a surrendered Manhattan permit and city settlement is single-sourced and uncorroborated by any filing; we note it only because it is directionally consistent with the 10-K's acknowledgment of recent incidents in both the U.S. and U.K.

**7. Government policy, two-sided.** Universal pre-K expansion and public child-care benefits are named as potentially reducing demand or capping tuition; international subsidy reductions and tuition caps are also flagged. The cap variant is a direct threat to a model whose primary margin lever is pricing.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a near-term event to the downside. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 69.2% against 46.1% one-year realized, a 23-point spread at the 71st percentile of the current chain, with a sharply backwardated term structure (69.2% at 10 days versus 40.5% at 38 days and 43.0% at 129 days). Put/call volume ratio is 5.00, open-interest ratio 2.83, and put/call skew is 23.4% in the elevated-put-demand direction as characterized by the data. The nearest expiry implies a ±8.5–10% move.

Backwardation of this magnitude on a stock that reported earnings eleven days ago is positioning around something other than earnings, and the only dated catalyst in the window is the Ofsted matter. Short interest at 5.7% of float and 2.9 days to cover indicates the bearish expression is being taken in options rather than in borrow, which limits squeeze risk in either direction. The longer-dated tenors, where the skew reading flips sign and IV compresses to 43%, price no persistent stress, consistent with a binary that resolves rather than a structural derating. This corroborates the risk assessment rather than driving it: the market is paying up for near-term protection on a name where the fundamental multiple is not stretched.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($98, +34%)

The stock trades at 14.4x guided adjusted EPS and 12.4x next-year consensus against a 21.5x peer median, for a business with 95% client retention, 220+ Fortune 500 relationships, and a 30%-margin Back-Up Care franchise growing mid-to-high teens with a competitive set of essentially one named peer. Back-Up alone at 12–15x segment operating income accounts for 74–92% of the current market capitalization, implying the market values 988 centers and $2.1bn of Full Service revenue at close to nothing. Underlying Full Service performance is in fact improving — ex-impairment Q2 margin of ~7.9% versus 7.5% a year earlier, adjusted income from operations +11% in H1 — and 47% of centers below 70% occupancy is a multi-year operating-leverage reservoir requiring no new capital. The Ofsted WRN is a compliance notice, not a revocation; Ofsted itself noted most settings meet requirements, and the routine path is remediation, after which a higher compliance bar advantages the best-capitalized operator. Management is buying stock aggressively at $65–83 with $328.7M of authorization remaining (roughly 9% of market cap), providing a mechanical bid, and leverage at 2.22x sits well inside a 4.25x covenant with no maturity wall before 2030. Four consecutive beats and rising estimates support consensus.

### Bear Case ($52, –29%)

Adjusted earnings overstate economics. Approximately $96M of impairments over ten quarters, escalating from $29.8M to $47.0M annually with more closures explicitly expected, is a recurring cost of the center model, not an exclusion; deducting it puts the real multiple at ~16x, and adding back stock compensation puts it near GAAP's 23x, above peers. Revenue growth has decelerated from +19.7% to a guided +5.3–6.4%, with H2 slower than H1, and per-share growth is financed rather than earned: $340M of new floating-rate debt bought 11.5% of the shares for 6.5% accretion while depleting additional paid-in capital to zero and taking tangible book to negative $1.07bn. Operating cash flow fell 8% in H1 while adjusted EBITDA rose 9%. The core segment earns 5.6% operating margins, is shrinking in units, competes for enrollment against a dozen unbranded operators on 65% parent-paid revenue, and faces a permanent hybrid-work headwind the company itself lists as a risk factor. Roughly 19% of revenue and half of non-U.S. fixed assets sit behind a single national regulator with an open estate-wide enforcement action whose resolution is undisclosed, alongside an Australian business management says may require closures against $1.57bn of Full Service goodwill. Compensation is measured on Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EPS and Revenue Growth — no return-on-capital metric and no operational-quality or safeguarding metric — while lease-adjusted ROIC of 7.6% sits at or below cost of capital and no insider has bought stock on the open market through a 41% decline.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the current print rather than waiting for the Ofsted outcome, which is a two-sided binary the options market is charging 69% implied volatility to hedge. Existing holders should reduce to a below-weight residual position sized so that the bear case ($52, –29%) is tolerable, and should not add on weakness until either the U.K. matter is resolved in disclosure or the price reaches the low $60s, where the base case would offer ~30% upside against a narrower bear gap. New capital has no reason to enter here: expected return of roughly +8–11% probability-weighted is below the cost of equity for an all-floating-debt consumer-services name, and the asymmetry (+12% base versus –29% bear) is unfavorable.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. Disclosed confirmation that Ofsted has judged the English estate compliant without registration conditions or further suspensions, with no material remediation cost taken to the P&L.
2. Q3 2026 operating cash flow that restores nine-month OCF to at least flat year-over-year, demonstrating the H1 8% decline was timing.
3. Back-Up Care revenue growth held at or above 15% with segment operating margin at or above 23% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the network-density reading over the utilization-windfall reading.
4. A clean like-for-like occupancy comparison (Q3 2026 cohort versus the same cohort a year earlier) showing the share of centers above 70% enrolled rising without cohort attrition doing the work.
5. Price near $60–62 with fundamentals unchanged, which would put the base case at roughly +33% against a bear gap of ~15%.

**Avoid triggers:**
1. Ofsted escalation — registration conditions imposed, additional multi-site suspensions, or any action affecting the U.K. license at group level.
2. An Australia goodwill or long-lived asset impairment exceeding $75M, or disclosed closure of a material portion of the Only About Children estate.

## Conclusion

Bright Horizons is a reasonably good business at a reasonable price with an unresolved regulatory binary and a balance sheet that was deliberately made less able to absorb it. The Back-Up Care franchise is genuinely attractive — 30% margins, mid-to-high teens growth, thin competition, minimal fixed assets — and at 14.4x guided adjusted earnings against a 21.5x peer median, the market plausibly assigns close to zero equity value to 988 centers and $2.1bn of Full Service revenue. If Full Service occupancy normalizes and the U.K. matter is remediated, the base case supports $82 and the bull case $98.

Against that, the discount has earned causes. Revenue growth has halved and per-share growth is substantially financed: $340M of new floating-rate debt bought 11.5% of the shares for 6.5% accretion, took net leverage from 1.66x to 2.22x, depleted additional paid-in capital to zero and left tangible book equity at negative $1.07bn, executed in the same six months that a national regulator served a Welfare Requirements Notice on the entire English estate. Adjusted earnings exclude roughly $96M of impairments over ten quarters from a closure program with no disclosed endpoint. Operating cash flow fell 8% while adjusted EBITDA rose 9%. Lease-adjusted ROIC of 7.6% sits at or below cost of capital. No insider has bought stock on the open market through a 41% decline, and compensation carries no return-on-capital or safeguarding metric. The near-term options market is paying 69% implied volatility against 46% realized, in backwardation, with put volume at five times calls.

The expected return from $73.245 is modestly positive and the downside asymmetry is not. **REDUCE.** Trim now, revisit at Q3 or in the low $60s.
