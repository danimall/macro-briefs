## Executive Summary

SunCoke Energy is the largest independent producer of metallurgical coke in the Americas, operating under long-term take-or-pay contracts with major steel producers. The company's 2025 was defined by the Algoma Steel contract breach ($90.1M impairment), the leveraged acquisition of Phoenix Global mill services ($296M), and a resulting net loss. At $7.32 per share ($627M market cap), the stock trades at ~5.1x EV/2026E EBITDA — optically cheap, but this discount reflects a business whose core product faces permanent secular demand destruction from the electric arc furnace (EAF) transition.

The Phoenix Global acquisition represents a strategic pivot toward services that serve all steelmakers (not just blast furnace operators), and insider buying in late 2025/early 2026 signals management's confidence. However, the earnings picture is deteriorating rapidly: estimates have been slashed 64% for the current year and have turned negative for next year. The company missed earnings 3 of the last 4 quarters. The Granite City contract (590K tons, ~17% of capacity) expires December 2026 with no renewal confirmation.

The structural headwind of EAF adoption combined with accelerating estimate downgrades and unresolved contract renewal risk creates an asymmetric risk profile skewed to the downside over the next 12 months.

## Company Overview

SunCoke Energy operates five domestic cokemaking facilities with ~3.7M tons of annual nameplate capacity, utilizing proprietary heat-recovery technology that captures waste heat for steam and electricity generation. The company holds 102 U.S. patents and is the only entity to have built greenfield coke facilities in the U.S. in over 35 years.

Post the August 2025 Phoenix Global acquisition, SXC reports two segments: Domestic Coke (~88% of revenue) and Industrial Services (~12%, growing), which includes logistics terminals and mill services (slag removal, metal recovery, scrap preparation) across 15 sites in 4 countries.

Key customers: Cleveland-Cliffs (contracts through 2028-2035), U.S. Steel (Granite City, December 2026 expiry), and formerly Algoma Steel (breached Q3 2025).

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Deterioration:**
Revenue declined from $2.06B (2023) to $1.84B (2025). Operating income swung from $152M profit in 2024 to a $44M loss in 2025, driven by $90.1M in impairment charges, $10.7M in transaction costs, and volume/pricing losses from the Algoma breach. Adjusted EBITDA fell from $273M to $219M.

**Balance Sheet Stress:**
Long-term debt increased from $492M to $686M to fund Phoenix Global. Net leverage stands at approximately 2.7x on 2025 EBITDA (($686M - $89M) / $219M ≈ 2.7x), rising to 2.5-2.6x on 2026 guidance midpoint. Cash fell from $190M to $89M. The Altman Z-Score of 1.88 places the company in the "grey zone." Remaining revolver availability is $132M, providing adequate liquidity but limited cushion.

**2026 Guidance ($230-250M Adj. EBITDA):**
Management reaffirmed this range after Q1 2026 ($56.5M quarterly EBITDA vs. ~$58M/quarter run-rate needed). The guided operating cash flow of $230-250M implies significant FCF generation (~$130-160M after capex), yielding approximately 21-25% on market cap. This is the bull case core argument.

**Q1 2026 Results:**
Revenue $455M (+$19M YoY from Phoenix contribution). Adj. EBITDA $56.5M vs. $59.8M prior year. Operating cash flow of $72.7M was strong (vs. $25.8M in Q1 2025). However, Domestic Coke EBITDA per ton dropped to $41.92 from $55.57 — a 25% decline that management attributed to weather and turbine failure.

**Earnings Track Record:**
The company has missed earnings estimates 3 of the last 4 quarters, including a -265% miss in Q4 2025 and a -54% miss in Q1 2026. This pattern indicates either poor analyst modeling or deteriorating operational visibility.

## Growth Analysis

**Secular Demand Decline (Core Business):**
The fundamental growth problem is that SXC's addressable market is shrinking. EAFs now produce ~70% of U.S. steel. Each blast furnace closure permanently removes coke demand. Algoma's conversion is the first direct casualty for SXC, but the trend is industry-wide. Coke sales guidance for 2026 is already down to ~3.4M tons from 3.67M in 2025 and higher levels previously.

**Phoenix Global as Growth Engine:**
The acquisition adds ~$100M+ annualized EBITDA and serves both blast furnace and EAF steelmakers. This diversification is strategically sound but insufficient to offset the core business decline at current scale. Industrial Services EBITDA surged to $26.2M in Q1 2026 (from $13.7M without Phoenix), confirming the contribution.

**Estimate Revisions Signal Deterioration:**
Current year EPS estimates have fallen from $0.56 to $0.20 over 90 days (-64%). Next year estimates have gone from $0.56 to -$0.19 (-134%). Only one analyst covers the stock, limiting the signal's breadth, but the magnitude and direction are unambiguous.

## Valuation Assessment

At $7.32 per share:
- **EV/EBITDA:** ~$1.22B / $240M (2026E midpoint) = 5.1x
- **P/E:** Effectively meaningless (negative trailing; ~23x on $27M guided net income midpoint)
- **FCF Yield:** ~21-25% on market cap (using guided $130-160M FCF)
- **Dividend Yield:** ~6.6%
- **Peer Comparison:** Peer median P/E is 33.7x; SXC's negative P/E is not directly comparable

The 5.1x EV/EBITDA appears cheap in absolute terms. However, this multiple accurately reflects: (1) a structurally declining core business, (2) elevated leverage post-acquisition, (3) sub-investment-grade credit rating, and (4) concentrated customer risk with a major contract renewal in 7 months. Companies facing secular demand decline routinely trade at 4-6x EBITDA regardless of near-term cash flow generation.

The analyst consensus target of $9.50 (range $9-$10) implies 30% upside. With only 2 analysts covering the stock and estimates cratering, these targets likely reflect stale assumptions.

## Competitive Landscape

SXC controls ~38% of U.S. blast furnace coke market capacity and operates the only heat-recovery coke plants in the Americas. No competitor has built new coke capacity in 35+ years, creating a near-monopoly in independent coke supply. However, this competitive moat is being eroded by the shrinking addressable market as EAF adoption accelerates.

The Phoenix Global mill services business competes in a fragmented global market with a small number of players. Its competitive position is less differentiated than the coke business but addresses a larger and more durable market.

Cleveland-Cliffs represents concentration risk that is difficult to mitigate. As SXC's largest customer by far, any strategic shift by Cliffs toward reduced blast furnace operations would be existential.

## Risk Assessment

**1. EAF Transition (Structural/Existential):** This is a permanent, accelerating secular shift. Algoma's breach is the proof case. The direction is clear; the uncertainty is timing.

**2. Granite City Contract Expiry (Near-Term, December 2026):** 590K tons (~17% of capacity) at risk. U.S. Steel's potential acquisition by Nippon Steel adds uncertainty. Non-renewal or unfavorable repricing would materially reduce 2027 EBITDA guidance.

**3. Customer Concentration:** Post-Algoma, Cleveland-Cliffs is the dominant customer. SXC has no diversification strategy within its coke segment.

**4. Leverage:** At ~2.7x net leverage, the company has limited capacity to absorb further negative surprises. Credit ratings at BB-/B1 reflect this risk.

**5. Estimate Trajectory:** Next year estimates at -$0.19 EPS suggest the market expects meaningful deterioration. If realized, dividend coverage ($0.12/share quarterly, ~$0.48 annually) would come under pressure.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Granite City contract expiry (December 2026) without renewal announcement; Q2-Q3 2026 earnings that validate deteriorating estimate trajectory (next report likely late July 2026). Cleveland-Cliffs capital allocation announcements could also serve as negative catalysts.

**Timing window:** 6-12 months. The Granite City contract resolution should become visible by Q3-Q4 2026.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest at 3.3% of float with 1.1 days to cover is very low. This is an uncrowded short with no squeeze risk — but informed short sellers have not found this attractive, which may reflect limited borrow availability on a small-cap name or suggest the bearish thesis is less consensus than it appears.

**Borrow and options signal:** 43% IV is elevated but declining. No unusual put activity documented. The options market is not strongly confirming a bearish thesis at current levels.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $4.50-$5.50 (-25-38% from $7.32) vs. upside risk to $9.00-$10.00 (+23-37%). Risk/reward is approximately 1:1, which does not meet the 2:1 threshold for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The structural thesis is correct but the stock is already down significantly from its 52-week high of $9.08, the insider buying creates a near-term floor risk, and the risk/reward ratio is insufficiently asymmetric for an active short position. The correct posture is to avoid owning the stock, not to actively bet against it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Granite City contract renewed on favorable terms (establishes volume floor through 2029+)
- Two consecutive quarters of earnings beats (establishing credible estimate base)
- Cleveland-Cliffs publicly commits to maintaining blast furnace operations through 2030+
- EV/EBITDA compressed below 4x with dividend maintained (would imply price ~$5.00-$5.50)

**Timeline:** The structural headwinds are multi-year in nature. Revisit after Granite City contract resolution (expected late 2026) and after 2027 guidance issuance.

## Conclusion

SunCoke Energy presents a textbook value trap: optically cheap on near-term cash flow metrics, but facing irreversible secular decline in its core product market. The Algoma breach was the first visible manifestation of the EAF transition that will progressively shrink SXC's addressable market.

Management's Phoenix Global acquisition is strategically sound as a long-term pivot, and insider buying at $5.50-$5.85 suggests genuine conviction. But the combination of structural demand decline, elevated leverage, concentrated customer risk, and collapsing estimates creates a risk profile that does not warrant capital commitment at current levels. The 6.6% dividend yield is not reliable given the negative EPS trajectory for next year.

**Recommendation: AVOID.** Wait for clarity on Granite City renewal and stabilization of earnings estimates before reconsidering.
