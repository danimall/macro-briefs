## Executive Summary

Noble's second-quarter 2026 results changed the character of the investment case. Revenue fell 15% year over year to $719.7M, operating income fell 77% to $30.5M, and the company reported a GAAP net loss of $(36.7)M, or $(0.23) per diluted share. The mechanism is specific and documented in the 10-Q: total operating days fell 18% to 1,986 from 2,429, and floater utilization fell to 59% from 70%. Realized dayrates actually rose (floaters $412,650 versus $400,802; jackups $197,380 versus $176,503), so the earnings damage is idle-time absorption against a largely fixed cost base of roughly $446M per quarter in contract drilling costs rather than price erosion. Management narrowed FY2026 revenue guidance to $2.8-2.9B and cut adjusted EBITDA guidance to $850-925M from $940M-$1.02B, roughly a $93M reduction at the midpoint, while simultaneously raising capital expenditure guidance for the second time this year to $615-665M from $590-640M. (Guidance figures are from third-party coverage of the July 27-28 call, not a primary filing.)

The consensus response has been severe. FY2026 EPS estimates have been cut 50.8% over 90 days to $0.50, the next-quarter estimate stands at $0.02 against $0.23 ninety days ago, and even FY2027 has been cut 16.9% to $2.14. Three of the last four quarters have missed, the most recent by 94.6% on a non-GAAP basis. Against that, the stock at $40.77 trades at roughly 8.9x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA on an enterprise value of about $7.94B, and roughly 19x a falling FY2027 consensus. That is not distressed pricing, and it is not obviously cheap for an offshore driller with 62% of 2027 days committed and 47% of 2028.

The balance sheet is the genuine bright spot and it deserves credit. The June 2026 refinancing issued $800M of 6.25% notes due 2034, retired $300M of 8.0% 2030 notes and all remaining $495M of 8.5% Diamond second-lien notes, discharged that indenture and released the associated liens, and the revolver was upsized to $650M and extended to May 2031 with nothing drawn. Total debt fell to $1,888.4M, cash stands at $456.2M, and there are no maturities before April 2030. That removes solvency from the debate. What it does not remove is the earnings and cash-flow problem: 1H 2026 free cash flow of $109.1M (operating cash flow $417.4M less capex $308.4M) covered only two-thirds of the $163.2M paid in dividends, with the gap bridged by $206.4M of asset-disposal proceeds. We move from HOLD to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Noble Corporation plc is a UK-incorporated offshore drilling contractor operating a single reportable segment, Contract Drilling Services. The fleet stood at 30 rigs (24 floaters, 6 jackups) at June 30, 2026, excluding rigs operating under bareboat charter, down from 31 rigs (25 floaters, 6 jackups) at the FY2025 10-K and from a peak of 27 floaters plus 13 jackups following the September 2024 Diamond Offshore acquisition. The reshaping has been deliberate: eleven floaters acquired via Diamond, then near-total exit from jackups (six sold for $424.0M total consideration, comprising $274.0M cash plus $150.0M of seller notes) and disposal of several lower-specification floaters.

Strategically Noble targets ultra-deepwater floaters and ultra-harsh-environment jackups. Its commercial structures are distinctive and matter for the current cycle. The ExxonMobil Commercial Enabling Agreement covers four drillships with awarded term to February 2029, but dayrates reprice semi-annually on January 1 and July 1 to projected market rate, subject to a scale discount plus performance bonus. The Aker BP framework agreement for two Norwegian ultra-harsh jackups resets annually. Performance-based contracts with Shell and TotalEnergies assume roughly 40% realization of available performance revenue in backlog. Roughly a fifth of backlog therefore carries a semi-annual market reset, which transmits softening dayrates into revenue without waiting for a contract expiry.

Customer concentration is extreme and has worsened. At June 30, 2026, five counterparties accounted for 74.8% of backlog (ExxonMobil 19.8%, Shell 18.1%, BP 13.7%, TotalEnergies 12.7%, Aker BP 10.5%), up from 72.0% across four at December 31, 2025. Geographically, backlog at year-end 2025 was 35.3% US Gulf, 23.7% Guyana, 12.9% Suriname, 11.4% North Sea.

**The multi-year trend inverted in 2025 and broke in 2026.**

FY2025 revenue rose 7% while operating income fell 31% and net income fell 52%. The 10-K attribution is instructive: contract drilling revenue rose $188M, of which $626.2M came from Diamond-acquired floaters, offset by $347.9M from rigs with net changes in operating days. The pre-existing floater base therefore contributed roughly $1,942M in 2025 against $2,350M in 2024, an approximate 17% organic decline (our arithmetic from the 10-K's disclosed Diamond contribution, ignoring intra-fleet contract reallocation). Costs rose 14%, D&A rose 37% on the stepped-up asset base, $82.7M of impairments were recorded against zero in 2024, and interest expense rose to $162.4M from $94.2M.

**Q2 2026 is the sharpest datapoint in the series.** Operating days 1,986 (-18% y/y), floater utilization 59% (from 70%), revenue $719.7M (-15%), operating income $30.5M (-77%), net loss $(36.7)M. Roughly $43.0M of the floater revenue shortfall is attributable to temporary operational suspensions of two drillships in Brazil, Noble Faye Kozack and Noble Courage, which the 10-Q now names explicitly in its forward-looking-statements list. External reporting attributes the suspensions to Petrobras and to Brazilian regulator ANP flagging non-conformities; neither is confirmed in Noble's filings and the substance is unverified.

The 2026 jackup utilization figures (83% Q2, 81% 1H) are not comparable to prior periods: per the 10-Q footnote they include rigs sold to Borr Drilling operating under bareboat charter, on a smaller contracted-only denominator. The apparent jackup improvement should not be read as market strength.

FY2025 free cash flow of $432.2M comfortably covered the $320.4M dividend. In 1H 2026 free cash flow fell $54M short of dividends paid, and the gap was bridged by $206.4M of disposal proceeds, with roughly $5.1M received on the Ocean Apex in July and an expected ~$64M sixth-jackup closing in Q3. One half-year is a poor annualization base: offshore drillers carry lumpy shipyard, survey and mobilization timing, and construction in progress nearly doubled from $215.6M to $403.9M on contract-mandated rig upgrades that convert to revenue days later. On the FY2025 operating cash-flow base of $951.7M less the $665M high end of capex guidance, coverage would still exist at roughly $287M against a ~$320M dividend, which is approximately break-even with no room for repurchases. The honest read is that the dividend is currently funded at the margin by asset sales, and that the buyback has been switched off despite $370M of remaining board authorization.

**Balance sheet.** Post-refinancing debt of $1,888.4M against $456.2M of cash gives net debt of roughly $1,432M, or about 1.6x the midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, which is comfortable for an asset-heavy cyclical. Equity was $4,549M at year-end 2025, roughly $28.5 per share, so the stock trades at about 1.43x book. Debt/equity 0.66, current ratio 1.67, Altman Z 2.46 (grey zone, computed on FY2025 data and therefore not reflecting the refinancing or disposals). Q2 interest expense fell to $36.2M from $40.0M; the refinancing cost $40.7M of extinguishment premiums plus $17.4M of issuance costs against roughly $17-18M of annualized coupon savings, so the real benefit is maturity runway and lien release rather than earnings.

**Two data reconciliations worth flagging.** The company profile lists EPS of $1.35, which is the FY2025 GAAP figure, while the quoted trailing P/E of 43.5x implies trailing-twelve-month EPS of approximately $0.94. Reported 1H 2026 net income of $84.0M is itself flattered by an $89.5M pre-tax jackup disposal gain and depressed by a $42.3M Ocean Apex impairment; excluding both, 1H operating income of roughly $208M compares with roughly $318M in the prior-year comparable, a decline near 36%. GAAP EPS is a poor proxy for earning power here in either direction, partly because a $(20.4)M pre-tax Q2 loss still carried a $16.3M tax provision.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze in the near term; the question is the shape and timing of a recovery.

**Negative near-term facts.** FY2026 revenue guidance implies a 12-15% decline from FY2025's $3,286M. Adjusted EBITDA guidance was cut roughly 9% at the midpoint one quarter after being set. Estimate revisions are among the worst we have seen in the sector: current quarter -27.4% over 30 days, next quarter -90.9%, current year -48.8% over 30 days and -50.8% over 90 days, next year -12.6% and -16.9%. Seven to nine analysts contribute, so this is not a single outlier.

**Backlog is stable in headline and thin in the out-years.** Backlog was $7.0B at December 31, 2025 and $7.1B at June 30, 2026, roughly 2.5 years of guided revenue. But committed-days coverage at June 30 was 63% for the remainder of 2026, 62% for 2027, 47% for 2028, 20% for 2029 and 11% for 2030. Backlog is a contracted-revenue estimate embedding CEA repricing, Aker BP resets, Petrobras adjustment mechanisms and ~40% performance-revenue realization; it is not comparable to deferred revenue, which is only $92.7M. The 10-Q also discloses concrete negative re-sequencing: Noble Stanley Lafosse's anticipated end date pulled forward from July 2027 to January 2027, and the Noble Viking option is no longer expected to be exercised in Q4 2026.

**The plausible recovery mechanism.** Noble's filings describe an industry shifting toward multi-year contracts, with the specific downside that contracts commencing a year or more into the future "can present near-term utilization inefficiency due to challenges with filling interim availability." That is a precise description of 59% floater utilization coexisting with $7.1B of backlog. Construction in progress doubling to $403.9M is capex ahead of contracted revenue days. If Brazil resolves and the deferred contracts commence, operating days should step up in 2027, which is the arithmetic behind the FY2027 consensus of $2.14 versus $0.50 this year. The problem is that roughly 38% of 2027 days remain uncommitted in a market where the company itself says dayrates "have decreased moderately since recent highs in 2023 and 2024," and the ExxonMobil CEA reprices to market twice a year on four of its best assets.

Supply discipline is genuine and is the strongest structural argument. The 10-K notes global rig supply has come down from historic highs as contractors retired less capable and idle assets, and that "the incoming supply of newbuild offshore drilling rigs has diminished materially." Noble has been an active participant, removing at least twelve rigs since the start of 2024. That supports mid-cycle dayrates. It does not fix 2026-2027 utilization.

Growth prospects score 3.2: revenue and earnings are contracting, revisions are steeply negative on both current and next year, and the recovery depends on filling roughly 38% of 2027 days at market rates that are softening.

## Valuation Assessment

**Enterprise value.** $6.51B equity + $1.888B debt − $0.456B cash = approximately $7.94B.

**On guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA** of $850-925M (midpoint $887.5M), EV/EBITDA is 8.9x. That is a full multiple for an offshore driller, even acknowledging trough conditions.

**On FY2027 consensus.** EPS of $2.14 puts the stock at 19.1x. Building an EBITDA bridge from that EPS — roughly $342M of net income plus ~$135M interest, ~$120M cash-and-book taxes given a structurally high effective rate, and ~$600M of D&A — implies FY2027 EBITDA near $1.2B and EV/EBITDA near 6.6x. Offshore drilling has historically cleared at 5-7x mid-cycle EBITDA, so 6.6x on a recovery year that requires filling 38% of days is fair value at best. Working backward, the current price implies roughly 8x an FY2027 EBITDA of $1.0B, or roughly 19x consensus EPS.

**Book value.** At $28.5 per share of year-end equity, the stock trades at 1.43x book. Offshore drillers do not reliably hold premiums to book in downcycles, and the Ocean Apex realized roughly $5.1M net after a $42.3M write-down, which is the clearest available price signal for the bottom tier of the global fleet and a reminder that carrying values on lower-specification assets are not floors.

**Peers.** The supplied comparable set (VAL 6.3x P/E, PTEN -36.8x, HP -10.4x, median 6.3x) is of limited use because two of three have negative earnings and the mix spans land and offshore. Directionally it does not support a premium for Noble.

**Yield.** The $2.00 annualized dividend yields 4.9%. In a downcycle, yield support is only as good as coverage, and 1H 2026 free cash flow covered 67% of dividends paid. The stock's floor is therefore softer than the headline yield implies.

**Analyst targets.** Mean $48.09, median $50.00, low $35.00, high $60.00, with the recommendation mix improving to 1 strong buy / 4 buy / 6 hold from 1/2/7 three months ago. These targets are stale relative to a 50.8% cut in current-year estimates and a 9% EBITDA guidance reduction. Analyst targets function as forward anchors when estimates are rising and the multiple is compressed against growth; here estimates are falling on both the current and next year and the multiple is not compressed. We treat the $48-50 consensus as likely to be revised down rather than as a floor.

Valuation score 4.2. The stock is not egregiously expensive, but there is no identified mispricing to exploit: the multiple is fair-to-full on both trough and recovery earnings, the yield is not covered by current free cash flow, and the earnings estimates supporting the forward multiple are being cut faster than the price is falling.

## Competitive Landscape

Noble's post-Diamond fleet is scaled and skewed toward the specification tier customers actually want; the 10-K states customer focus on "our highest specification floaters, which represents the majority of our floater fleet." It has anchor relationships with five supermajors and large independents, has exited jackups at what appear to be reasonable prices (an $89.5M pre-tax gain on five rigs), and has better financing terms and maturity runway than most of the sector after the June refinancing.

Three competitive negatives carry more weight right now.

First, the market is bifurcating and Noble sits on both sides of the divide. Its own filing warns that customer focus on the highest-specification floaters "could result in lower utilization for our lower specification drillships and our semi-submersibles." Midwater semisubmersible demand is characterized as brownfield, infield and plug-and-abandonment work, which is baseload rather than growth. Four impairments across FY2025 and Q2 2026 totalling $125M, plus three rigs held for sale at a combined $96.8M carrying value, quantify the tail of the fleet.

Second, contract structure transmits weakness faster than a fixed-rate book would. The ExxonMobil CEA (19.8% of backlog) reprices semi-annually to projected market rate; Aker BP (10.5%) resets annually; certain Petrobras contracts carry adjustment mechanisms. This is an advantage in a rising market and a liability now.

Third, concentration is worsening on direct measures: five customers at 74.8% of backlog versus four at 72.0% six months earlier. The revenue mix has changed a great deal with the jackup exit and Diamond addition, but that mix change has not produced diversification. Geographic exposure clusters in exactly the theatres where the trouble is: US Gulf, Guyana, Suriname and Brazil, with the North Sea (11.4% of backlog, 16.9% of FY2025 revenue) facing UK policy headwinds the 10-K says "have reduced oil and gas exploration and development activities."

We were unable to obtain current utilization, dayrate or backlog data for Transocean, Valaris or Seadrill, so we cannot establish whether 59% floater utilization is better or worse than peers. Competitive position score 5.0, unchanged in substance from the prior read: a good fleet in a bifurcating market with concentration moving the wrong way.

## Risk Assessment

**Brazil suspensions (live, unquantified).** Two drillships were temporarily suspended. Noble says both are "currently operating in Brazil" but that "the outcome of discussions regarding proposed administrative solutions ... remain uncertain and actual revenues earned by the rigs may differ from the backlog amounts and periods presented." External reporting attributes the suspensions to Petrobras and to ANP non-conformity findings, neither of which is confirmed in the filings and the substance of which is paywalled and unverified. Remediation cost, duration and any contractual consequence are unknown. This is the single largest near-term swing factor.

**Dividend sustainability.** Consolidated retained earnings fell from $411.2M to $286.6M to $207.8M across the last three balance sheet dates, with capex guidance raised while EBITDA guidance was cut. As an English plc, Noble may distribute only out of statutory distributable reserves; consolidated retained earnings are not that test, so we cannot conclude capacity is constrained, but the direction warrants monitoring. A cut would remove the yield support currently underpinning the price.

**Borr Drilling vendor-financing credit exposure.** Noble lent $150.0M to Borr against the $360M five-jackup sale, financing about 42% of its own sale price. The note is subordinated secured, matures January 2032, carries a rate escalating from 7.5% to 15.0%, permits payment-in-kind through the third interest payment at the borrower's election, and is collateralized by three of the five rigs sold. Carried at $150.0M with no impairment, and generating $7.4M of 1H interest income. Vendor financing is common and often rational, but the escalating coupon and PIK toggle are structures associated with elevated credit risk, and a Borr credit event would leave Noble holding subordinated claims on used jackups in the market it just exited. We performed no independent assessment of Borr's balance sheet.

**Insurance gaps.** Noble carries no loss-of-hire insurance on any rig and has primarily self-insured US Gulf rigs for named-windstorm property damage. The US Gulf is 35.3% of backlog. This is an unhedged seasonal tail.

**Tax fragility.** FY2025's $56.4M provision netted $134.0M of valuation-allowance releases and $60.2M of uncertain-tax-position benefits against $250.6M of recurring accruals; 1H 2026 repeated the pattern with $37.7M of discrete benefits against $103.4M of recurring accruals. Uncertain tax position reserves are $119.1M. Reported EPS will remain lumpy and can be worse than pre-tax results imply.

**Further impairments.** Four impairments in six quarters, three rigs held for sale, and a realized Ocean Apex price of roughly $5.1M net after a $42.3M write-down. Impairment inputs are Level 3 and the 10-K states they "may have a material effect."

**Estimate-revision risk to the forward multiple.** With current-year consensus down 50.8% in 90 days and next-quarter consensus at $0.02, the FY2027 figure of $2.14 that anchors the forward multiple is the least-tested number in the model.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.2% sits fractionally below one-year realized volatility of 41.9%, having compressed from 47.3% on July 24 through the earnings event, which is the standard post-catalyst volatility crush, with the chain now pricing no imminent second event. Term structure is flat (39.2% near, 39.1% far), consistent with uncertainty distributed across the year rather than concentrated. The put/call skew is elevated at both the one-month (10.3%) and six-month (11.5%) tenors, indicating persistent demand for downside protection, while the three-month tenor shows the opposite sign (-3.3%). Put/call volume is light at 0.20 and open-interest ratios are not usable in this chain, so positioning conclusions should be drawn cautiously from a 79-contract sample.

Combined with 11.9% short interest (13.0M shares, 6.9 days to cover), the market is carrying a meaningful bearish position and paying for six-month protection at fair-to-slightly-cheap volatility. That corroborates rather than contradicts our fundamental view, and it also means a positive Brazil resolution or a Q3 utilization beat could produce a sharp squeeze, which is one reason we recommend trimming rather than shorting.

Risk factors score 3.6: several specific, dated or unresolved items (Brazil, dividend coverage, Borr credit, windstorm self-insurance, impairment pipeline) with material downside and limited evidence they are priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $54 (+32%)

Offshore supply discipline is real: rig supply has fallen from historic highs, newbuild deliveries have essentially stopped, and few stranded shipyard rigs remain. Noble's $7.1B backlog covers 2.5 years of guided revenue with five supermajor counterparties, and 2026's utilization collapse is a timing air pocket created by multi-year contracts commencing later, evidenced by construction in progress doubling to $403.9M on contract-mandated upgrades. If Brazil resolves without lasting contractual damage and deferred contracts commence on schedule, operating days step up materially in 2027. On $1.2B of FY2027 EBITDA and capex normalizing toward $400M after the upgrade cycle, free cash flow of roughly $500M would represent a 7.7% yield on today's market cap, comfortably covering the dividend and restarting the $370M buyback authorization at depressed prices. At 8.5x $1.2B EBITDA less $1.43B net debt, equity is worth roughly $55. Dayrates rising in Q2 despite collapsing utilization supports the pricing-discipline half of this argument.

### Bear Case — $27 (−34%)

Utilization stays in the low 60s through 2027 because roughly 38% of 2027 days must be filled into a softening dayrate market, and the ExxonMobil CEA reprices four of the best drillships to that softer market every six months. Brazil non-conformities require capital remediation or cost contract term. Capex guidance rises again while EBITDA lands at or below the $850M low end. The dividend, already funded at the margin by asset sales that run out once the sixth jackup closes, is cut, removing the yield floor and triggering income-holder selling. Further impairments on lower-specification floaters follow the Ocean Apex precedent, and the $150M Borr note requires a reserve. At 5.5x $850M EBITDA less net debt, equity is worth roughly $20; at 0.95x year-end book, roughly $27. We use $27 as the bear case, near the 52-week low of $26.20, on the view that asset backing and the clean maturity profile prevent a full slide to the multiple-based low.

### Base Case — $40 (−1.9% price, roughly +3% total return with dividend)

Brazil resolves over two to three quarters with modest lingering cost, FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands near the $885M midpoint, and FY2027 EBITDA recovers to roughly $1.0B as deferred contracts commence but interim availability remains partly unfilled. At 8x $1.0B EBITDA less $1.43B net debt, or 18.7x the FY2027 consensus $2.14, equity is worth roughly $40. The dividend is maintained but not raised and buybacks stay dark through 2026. Probability-weighting bull 25% / base 45% / bear 30% gives an expected price of about $39.6, or roughly -3% before dividends and approximately +1% including an expected dividend haircut for cut risk. That is below a fair risk-adjusted return for a high-beta cyclical with 11.9% short interest.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Expected risk-adjusted return is below fair, and the downside case (-34%) is larger than the base-case upside (-2% to +3% total return) with meaningful probability attached.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength rather than dumping at $40.77, which already sits 26% below the May 20 high of $54.98 and only 16% above the low end of the analyst target range ($35.00). Reduce position size by roughly half now, and complete the reduction on any rally into the $46-50 band, the level at which senior management itself sold in April and May 2026. For holders who want residual exposure to the 2027 day-count recovery, retaining a one-third position through the Q3 print (late October 2026) is defensible, sized to survive a dividend cut.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD.**
1. Noble Faye Kozack and Noble Courage returned to full contracted dayrate with the administrative resolution disclosed and quantified in a filing, and no material remediation capex.
2. Two consecutive quarters of floater utilization at or above 68% with operating days stabilizing above 2,200 per quarter.
3. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA delivered at or above the $925M high end, with FY2027 consensus EPS stabilizing (no further negative 90-day revision).

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE.** Any of the above plus either 2027 committed-days coverage rising above 75% on new fixtures at dayrates at or above $400,000 for floaters, or a share price at $30-32 (roughly 1.05-1.12x year-end book and about 6.5x $1.0B of FY2027 EBITDA) with fundamentals otherwise intact. Resumption of buybacks against the $370M authorization, funded by operating cash flow rather than disposals, would also be a substantive signal.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. A dividend cut or suspension, which would confirm that operating cash flow cannot support the distribution once asset-sale proceeds are exhausted.
2. FY2026 capex guidance raised a third time, or FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guided below $850M.
3. An impairment or credit reserve taken against the $150M Borr note, or evidence of Borr payment-in-kind election beyond the contractual window.
4. Brazil suspensions recurring, or contract term reduced on either rig.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural decision point is the Q3 2026 report in late October 2026, which will show whether Q2's 1,986 operating days was a Brazil-driven trough or a new run rate, whether 2H capex tracks the raised guidance, and whether the Q3 dividend (declared July 27, payable September 24) is followed by a fourth declaration at $0.50. The sixth jackup closing, expected in Q3 for roughly $64M, is the last scheduled disposal proceed of size.

## Conclusion

Noble is a better-capitalized company than it was six months ago and a worse-earning one. The June 2026 refinancing pushed the first maturity to April 2030, discharged the Diamond second-lien indenture and released its liens, upsized an undrawn revolver to $650M, and cut roughly $17-18M of annual interest. Solvency is not the issue. The issue is that operating days fell 18% year over year, floater utilization fell eleven points to 59%, adjusted EBITDA guidance was cut 9% one quarter after being set, capex guidance was raised for the second time, consensus current-year EPS has been cut in half in ninety days, and the $2.00 dividend was covered only two-thirds by 1H free cash flow with the balance funded by asset sales that are nearly exhausted.

At $40.77 the market is paying 8.9x guided trough EBITDA, roughly 19x a falling FY2027 estimate and 1.43x book for that combination. There is no identifiable mispricing here to underwrite: the price already embeds a 2027 day-count recovery that requires filling roughly 38% of 2027 days into a softening dayrate market, on a book where nearly a fifth of backlog reprices to market every six months. Management's own revealed behaviour reinforces caution: broad, one-directional insider selling between February and June 2026 at $43-53 with no open-market purchases, and zero buybacks against $370M of authorization while the stock fell 26% from its highs. We cannot establish which sales were plan-driven and draw no inference about motive, but the absence of any offsetting purchase after the drawdown, coupled with collapsing consensus estimates, means this selling is corroborated rather than contradicted by the fundamentals.

Our overall score of 4.35 marks a decline from 5.0 in late July, driven by the Q2 print rather than by the price move: the guidance cut, the first quarterly loss in the series, the 50.8% current-year estimate reduction and the dividend coverage shortfall are new fundamental information, not sentiment. This is our fourth consecutive non-BUY on the name and the first at REDUCE since the initial AVOID. Existing holders should trim, prioritizing sales into any rally toward the mid-$40s. Prospective buyers of the 2027 recovery should wait for either the Brazil resolution in writing or a price in the low $30s, where the asset backing does the work that today's multiple cannot.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the trailing-P/E/EPS reconciliation claim ("consistent with FY2025 net income of $217M less 1H 2025 and plus 1H 2026's $84.0M") was internally inconsistent and unverifiable and has been removed; and the de-risking plan's "8% above the low end of the analyst target range" was arithmetically wrong ($40.77 is ~16% above $35.00) — neither changes the valuation math underpinning REDUCE, but both were stated with false precision.
