**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AMD has transformed from a CPU share-gainer into the primary second source for AI accelerators, validated by two off-take deals with OpenAI (up to 6 GW of GPUs, October 2025) and Meta (up to 6 GW, February 2026). The fundamental data is unambiguous: FY25 revenue grew 34% to $34.6B, Q1 FY26 revenue grew 38% to $10.25B, gross margin reached 53%, Data Center revenue grew 57% year-over-year, and management guided Q2 to roughly $11.2B (+46%). Consensus estimates are rising fast — next-year EPS revisions are +18% over 30 days and +20.5% over 90 days — and the balance sheet carries net cash of roughly $9B. This is one of the cleaner fundamental inflection stories in large-cap semiconductors.

The complication is price. At $510 (≈$849B market cap), AMD has appreciated roughly 345% over twelve months and trades at approximately 69x current-year consensus EPS ($7.37) and ~39x next-year EPS ($13.08). The valuation prices in flawless execution of the OpenAI/Meta ramp. That bull case carries two embedded liabilities the headline numbers obscure: the two 160M-share, $0.01 warrants represent up to ~320M shares of prospective dilution (~20% of the current 1.63B share count), and the company has converted its historically asset-light model into one carrying $25.7B of unconditional purchase commitments ($18.3B due in the remainder of FY26), $4.4B of pending data-center leases, and $4.1B of lease guarantees on commercial partners' data centers booked as credit derivatives.

The fundamentals support a constructive long-term view, and the estimate-revision velocity is genuinely strong. The question for a BUY is asymmetry, not direction. At ~69x current-year earnings with a bear case that runs more than 15% below spot — driven by AI-buildout timing, warrant dilution, and multiple compression — the risk/reward at $510 is balanced rather than skewed favorably. We maintain HOLD with a positive growth bias and would become buyers materially lower.

## Company Overview

AMD is a fabless semiconductor company organized into three reportable segments after the Client and Gaming segments were merged in Q1 FY25:

- **Data Center** ($16.6B FY25, +32%; $5.78B Q1 FY26, +57% YoY) — EPYC server CPUs, Instinct AI GPU accelerators (MI350 shipping, MI450 next), Pensando DPUs/AI NICs, Xilinx FPGAs, and the "Helios" rack-scale platform. This segment is the entire bull thesis.
- **Client and Gaming** ($14.6B FY25, +51%; $3.6B Q1 FY26, +23%) — Ryzen desktop/mobile CPUs, Radeon GPUs, and semi-custom console SoCs. FY25 client ASPs rose 31%.
- **Embedded** ($3.5B FY25, −3%; $873M Q1 FY26, +6%) — legacy Xilinx FPGA/SoC franchise, highest-margin but cyclical.

AMD is the clear #2 in data-center AI accelerators behind Nvidia (which holds roughly 81% accelerator share) and the consistent share-gainer against Intel in x86 server and client CPUs. Its moat rests on a leadership CPU roadmap (5th Gen EPYC), an annual Instinct GPU cadence, and the ROCm software ecosystem, though ROCm remains structurally behind Nvidia's CUDA, a gap management explicitly acknowledges. Manufacturing is outsourced to TSMC (≤7nm) and GlobalFoundries (larger nodes), with assembly/test through Tongfu JVs in China.

## Financial Analysis

The financial inflection is real and broad-based:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $22.7B | $25.8B | $34.6B (+34%) | $10.25B (+38% YoY) |
| Gross margin | 46% | 49% | 50% | 53% |
| Operating income | $0.40B | $1.90B | $3.69B | $1.48B |
| Net income | $0.85B | $1.64B | $4.34B | $1.38B |
| Diluted EPS | — | ~$1.00 | ~$2.65 | $0.84 |

The dominant story is margin expansion, driven by favorable Data Center mix and recovery through a FY25 headwind of ~$440M in MI308 China export write-downs (of which $360M reversed in Q4 after licenses were granted). R&D spending is enormous and rising — $8.1B in FY25 (+25%, ~23% of revenue) and $2.4B in Q1 FY26 (+39%) — which funds the AI roadmap but compresses near-term operating leverage.

Cash flow quality is solid and improving. Operating cash flow was $6.5B in FY25 (continuing ops) versus $3.0B in FY24, and Q1 FY26 OCF was $3.0B versus $0.9B a year earlier. Capex remains light at roughly $1B/year under the fabless model. The balance sheet is a clear strength: $12.3B in cash and short-term investments against only $3.25B total debt (net cash ~$9B), $64.5B of equity, and undrawn $3B revolver plus $3B commercial paper capacity.

Two items temper the quality read. First, goodwill and intangibles total ~$41.5B (≈52% of assets), the legacy of the Xilinx (~$49B, 2022) and Pensando deals, with acquisition-intangible amortization running ~$2.2–2.3B/year and ~$16.2B still to amortize through 2031. Second, the effective tax rate was unusually low in FY25 (−2.5%, aided by an $853M release of uncertain tax positions); a normalized ~15% rate matters for forward EPS modeling.

Capital return has decelerated: AMD repurchased only $221M in Q1 FY26 versus $1.3B in FY25, with $9.2B remaining on the authorization, as cash is redirected to supply prepayments. There is no dividend.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on Data Center, where the company is converting AI-accelerator demand into a credible multi-year revenue trajectory. The OpenAI and Meta off-take agreements anchor the bull thesis and signal that the hyperscaler/AI-lab ecosystem wants AMD as a second source to reduce Nvidia dependency. Q2 guidance and the 57% Data Center growth in Q1 confirm the ramp is underway, not speculative.

Estimate revisions reinforce this. Current-year consensus EPS has moved to $7.37 (+9.6% over 30 days, +11.4% over 90 days). The acceleration in the 30-day windows reflects consensus revising upward faster as the OpenAI/Meta ramp comes into view. The earnings-surprise record (three beats, one miss in the last four quarters, including a 14.8% Q4 beat) supports confidence in forward estimates.

The growth signal here is strong, and we weight it heavily in the Growth Prospects score (8.0). The standard inflection-stock framework — estimate revisions exceeding +20% over 90 days combined with revenue growth above 30% — is partially present (revisions on next-year, revenue at 38%). The qualifier is that next-year revisions of +20.5% pair with a forward multiple well above the threshold where rising estimates alone justify a BUY. At ~39x next-year EPS, the multiple already embeds continued beats, which weakens the asymmetry even as it confirms the growth is real.

There is also a quality caveat to the growth: a portion of the headline revenue trajectory is effectively being "paid for" with equity through the warrants (discussed below). The growth is genuine, but reported EPS growth is flattered by warrant costs not yet recognized.

## Valuation Assessment

At $510, AMD trades at roughly 69x current-year consensus EPS and ~39x next-year EPS. Against peers, the picture is mixed:

| Ticker | Name | Mkt Cap | P/E |
|--------|------|---------|-----|
| TSM | Taiwan Semiconductor | $905B | 36.3 |
| MU | Micron | $1,176B | 45.7 |
| TXN | Texas Instruments | $265B | 52.2 |
| QCOM | Qualcomm | $247B | 27.4 |
| INTC | Intel | $555B | neg. |

Peer median P/E is 45.7. On a forward basis, AMD's ~39x next-year is within shouting distance of the cohort, and its growth rate exceeds most peers, so the multiple is not absurd for the growth if the OpenAI/Meta ramp executes. Applying the cycle-adjusted framework, trailing P/E (~170x reported) is not the right lens given the earnings inflection; forward and normalized metrics are the correct measure.

The problem is that even on forward metrics, the asymmetry is unfavorable at this price. Three points argue against a high valuation score:

1. **The warrant overhang is not in the share count.** Up to ~320M shares can be issued at $0.01 as the OpenAI/Meta warrants vest. None had vested as of Q1 FY26, so today's per-share metrics understate fully-diluted economics. On a fully-diluted ~1.95B share basis, next-year EPS power compresses and the effective multiple rises.
2. **The most directly comparable peer is cheaper.** Bernstein and other analysts frame Nvidia as cheaper on a forward basis despite faster growth and dominant accelerator share. If the second source trades at a premium to the dominant incumbent on growth-adjusted forward earnings, the burden of proof on the AMD valuation is heavy.
3. **Analyst targets are not a floor here.** The principle that consensus targets lag reality on cheap inflection stocks does not apply at ~69x current-year / ~39x next-year. At these multiples, the target ($482.5 median, $479.8 mean — both below the current $510) and the price reflect the same growth extrapolation. The stock trades above the median target, consistent with consensus optimism already being fully priced rather than lagging.

The market is pricing flawless execution of the AI ramp. We assign a Valuation score of 4.0 — the multiple is defensible only if every element of the bull case lands, and the embedded dilution plus the more-attractive Nvidia comparison make the risk/reward unfavorable at $510.

## Competitive Landscape

AMD's competitive trajectory is improving, which is what the Competitive Position score (7.0) reflects. The company is executing a coherent full-stack #2 strategy: an annual Instinct cadence (MI350 → MI450), networking via Pensando, and rack-scale systems via Helios/ZT. The OpenAI, Meta, and Oracle wins validate AMD as the second source the ecosystem wants. Against Intel, AMD continues to take x86 server and client share.

The constraints on the score are real. Nvidia remains dominant at ~81% accelerator share with roughly $62B/quarter in Data Center revenue and an intact CUDA software moat. The Q1 10-Q added the September 2025 Nvidia–Intel partnership as a new competitive/pricing threat not present in prior filings, and at least one analyst flags Nvidia's Grace/Vera CPU ambitions threatening the EPYC franchise. Intel is mounting a credible AI-server-CPU comeback. The trajectory is positive and share is being gained, but AMD remains a challenger in accelerators against an incumbent with superior software lock-in and balance-sheet firepower.

This name sits on the favorable side of the AI-infrastructure bifurcation: it builds the silicon AI workloads run on, joining the hardware cohort confirming demand rather than the application-software layer facing displacement.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that are not fully reflected in the valuation drive the Risk Factors score (4.5):

1. **Warrant dilution.** The two warrants (OpenAI + Meta) vest on both GPU purchases and AMD stock-price targets, are accounted for as a liability until equity classification, and will generate contra-revenue/share-based charges as tranches vest. As of Q1 FY26, none had vested, so the entire overhang is prospective and underappreciated in headline metrics.

2. **Purchase commitments and lease guarantees.** Unconditional commitments ballooned from $12.2B in the 10-K to the current level. A demand air-pocket could trigger inventory write-downs and guarantee payouts. This converts the asset-light model into one carrying counterparty and demand-forecasting risk.

3. **Customer concentration and AI-customer financing health.** A small number of hyperscalers/AI labs drive a large share of Data Center revenue. The 10-Q adds explicit new language that customers "may be unable to secure capital to fund their required AI infrastructure and may request alternative financing or deferred-payment arrangements" — a disclosure tying AMD's growth to the financing health of a handful of AI buyers.

4. **China export controls.** MI308 was blocked then partially re-licensed; MI325 licenses (Feb 2026) require U.S. inspection and a 25% import tariff; a 15% revenue cut on licensed China sales has been floated but not enacted. China is a large but capped, low-visibility swing factor.

5. **Memory shortage.** A new disclosure of an industry-wide memory shortage with rising prices is a direct gross-margin headwind for GPU/server products.

6. **Inventory build.** Inventories rose to $8.0B (from $5.7B) to support the advanced-node Data Center ramp, elevating obsolescence risk if AI demand timing slips.

The balance sheet means bankruptcy risk is negligible; the risk here is to the share price, concentrated in dilution, demand timing, and multiple compression rather than solvency.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 83.5% versus 63.5% one-year historical vol (rich, 88th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (83.5% near-term vs. 71.3% long-term), which typically signals an expected near-term catalyst or elevated event risk. Put/call skew is balanced (−2.1%) and the put/call OI ratio is low (0.47), so positioning is not bearish, but the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6% and unusual activity is concentrated in short-dated calls around $510–$530 (a $515 6/12 call traded 10x open interest). The reading is that the market expects a sharp move and is leaning slightly long into it, with rich premiums favoring sellers over buyers. For a holder, the elevated near-term IV makes covered-call overwriting attractive; for new capital, the backwardation argues for patience rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
AMD executes the OpenAI/Meta ramp cleanly, Data Center compounds at 40%+ for multiple years, gross margin holds in the low-to-mid 50s, and next-year EPS lands at or above the $13.08 consensus with continued upward revisions. The warrants vest in a way the market rewards (because the stock rose to hit the price targets), and the second-source thesis solidifies AMD as the structural #2 with 15–20% accelerator share. On ~$15–16 of EPS power 18–24 months out at ~40x, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range, ~$665, roughly +30% from $510.

### Bear Case
AI-buildout timing slips on power, construction, or customer financing constraints; the $25.7B in commitments and $8.0B inventory force write-downs; warrant vesting dilutes ~20% just as growth decelerates; and the multiple compresses toward the cohort as the market re-rates the second source below the incumbent. The memory shortage pressures margins and China optionality is capped by the threatened revenue tithe. The stock de-rates to ~30x a diluted ~$12 EPS, reaching the low analyst target of ~$225 — roughly −56% from $510, though a more probable bear path lands around $400 (−22%) on multiple compression alone with intact fundamentals.

The bear case runs well past the 15% downside threshold, which is the core reason this is not a BUY at $510.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive growth bias (growth_hold). The 12-month thesis is constructive, but the entry price does not offer the asymmetry required for a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward $380–420 (roughly the early-May pre-OpenAI/Meta-print-acceleration zone) on unchanged or improving fundamentals, which would compress the forward multiple toward ~30x next-year EPS and restore asymmetry.
- Warrant-vesting clarity that quantifies dilution as smaller or more back-loaded than the ~320M-share maximum, removing the overhang.
- A second consecutive quarter of Data Center growth above 50% with gross margin holding ≥53% and next-year EPS revisions continuing above +15%, confirming the ramp is durable rather than front-loaded.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut or Data Center sequential deceleration that signals an AI-buildout air-pocket.
- Material inventory write-downs or lease-guarantee payouts that confirm the demand-forecasting risk in the commitment stack.
- Gross-margin compression below ~50% from the memory shortage or China tariff/tithe effects, paired with falling estimates.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY26, expected early August 2026), which will test the guidance and reveal the trajectory of warrant vesting and inventory.

## Conclusion

AMD is a high-quality business at an inflection point: 38% revenue growth, 53% gross margins, net cash, rising estimates, and a credible second-source position in the most important demand cycle in semiconductors. The Growth, Financial Health, and Competitive Position scores reflect that. The recommendation is HOLD rather than BUY for a specific, falsifiable reason: at $510 (above the median analyst target), the valuation prices flawless execution while the bull case embeds up to ~20% prospective dilution from the OpenAI/Meta warrants and converts the model into one carrying $25.7B of purchase commitments and $4.1B of lease guarantees. The bear case runs more than 15% below spot (and plausibly to −22% on multiple compression alone), so the risk/reward is balanced, not skewed. We would upgrade to BUY on a pullback toward $380–420 or on warrant-dilution clarity, and we hold existing positions with the option of covered-call overwriting given the rich near-term IV.
