## Executive Summary

Occidental Petroleum has undergone a material transformation over the past six months. The January 2026 sale of OxyChem to Berkshire Hathaway for $9.7B funded $5.4B in debt repayment, converting OXY into a pure-play E&P/midstream company with simplified financials. Concurrently, oil prices surged from ~$65/bbl to ~$108 Brent due to Strait of Hormuz disruption, driving the stock from $38.80 (December 2025 low) to $58.83 — a 52% gain in five months.

The core question is whether this repricing is complete. Consensus EPS estimates for FY2026 have risen from $1.41 to $5.34 over 90 days (+278%), reflecting the oil price regime change. At $58.83, the stock trades at approximately 11x forward FY26 EPS — optically reasonable for an E&P. But OXY carries $15B in debt, zero commodity hedges, a pending $2.3B tax contingency, and is transitioning CEOs during what may be peak oil pricing. The stock is a HOLD with a specific growth catalyst (debt target completion unlocking buybacks), but the risk profile prohibits a BUY recommendation given the binary commodity exposure and the possibility that current prices represent cycle peak.

## Company Overview

Occidental Petroleum is the third-largest U.S. oil producer with dominant positions in the Permian Basin (786 Mboe/d, ~10% of basin output), the DJ Basin, and the Gulf of Mexico (4th largest deep-water producer). International operations span Oman, Algeria, UAE, and Qatar. Total 2025 production was 1,434 Mboe/d.

Following the OxyChem divestiture, the company operates two segments: Oil & Gas (~97% of segment income) and Midstream & Marketing (including the ~43% Western Midstream Partners stake and Oxy Low Carbon Ventures/STRATOS direct air capture facility). Berkshire Hathaway owns approximately 28% of common equity plus $10B in preferred stock yielding $679M annually.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings:** Revenue declined from $26.7B (2024) to $21.6B (2025) as WTI averaged $64.81 versus $75.72 the prior year — a 14% decline. However, the Q1 2026 earnings report (May 5, 2026) delivered $1.06 EPS versus $0.60 consensus — a 77.5% beat — reflecting the oil price surge and post-CrownRock integration benefits. The company has now beaten estimates in four consecutive quarters by an average of 51%.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow from continuing operations was $9.6B in 2025 (on lower prices). At $108 Brent, annualized OCF likely approaches $12-14B based on OXY's oil price sensitivity (~$1.5B per $10/bbl move). FCF margin was 19% in 2025; it should be materially higher in 2026.

**Balance Sheet:** The critical metric is debt reduction trajectory. Long-term debt stood at $20.6B at year-end 2025, reduced to ~$15B following the OxyChem proceeds and additional paydowns. Management targets $14.3B, after which buybacks restart ($1.2B remaining on $3B authorization). Near-term maturities are minimal: $24M in 2026, $48M in 2027. The $4.15B undrawn revolver provides liquidity comfort.

**Operating Efficiency:** Lease operating expense declined to $8.94/Boe in 2025 from $10.48 in 2023, reflecting CrownRock synergies and operational discipline. This cost improvement is structural and adds $1-2/Boe to margins versus two years ago.

**Key Concern — Interest Burden:** Annual interest expense of ~$1.1B consumes approximately $1.10/share of EPS at current share count. Completing the debt reduction to $14.3B would save an additional ~$30-40M annually — helpful but not transformational.

## Growth Analysis

OXY's growth story has two dimensions:

**1. Production Growth (Moderate):** Permian Resources volumes are growing organically. CrownRock added ~170 Mboe/d. 2026 production guidance implies low-single-digit growth. Management is prioritizing FCF over volume.

**2. Carbon Management (Long-Duration Optionality):** STRATOS (world's largest DAC facility) represents a differentiated asset but faces construction delays and uncertain economics. The 45Q tax credit provides revenue support, but the DOE funding situation remains politically contingent. NET Power (40.3% stake) adds further optionality. These assets do not contribute meaningfully to 2026-2027 earnings.

**Estimate Revisions:** The +278% 90-day revision in FY2026 EPS ($1.41 → $5.34) is almost entirely a function of oil price appreciation, not operational improvement. The revision velocity represents commodity beta, not fundamental discovery by analysts. If Brent falls 20% from $108 to $86, approximately $2.00/share of the $5.34 estimate disappears.

## Valuation Assessment

At $58.83, OXY trades at:
- **~11x FY2026 consensus EPS ($5.34)** — below peer medians of 13-14x
- **~16x FY2027 consensus EPS ($3.61)** — above peer medians, reflecting expected oil price normalization
- **~44x trailing EPS ($1.35)** — reflecting trough trailing period

The discount to EOG (13.8x) and Devon (13.1x) reflects two rational factors: (1) OXY's higher leverage ($15B debt vs. near-zero for EOG), and (2) the complete absence of hedging, which means OXY's "E" in the P/E ratio is maximally commodity-sensitive.

The analyst consensus target of $64.50 (median) implies 10% upside, while the high target of $75 implies 28% upside. At $55, the low target implies 6% downside. This range is narrow relative to OXY's actual commodity sensitivity.

**Valuation fair range at current oil prices:** $55-65 (7-12x forward earnings). The stock at $58.83 sits in the middle. There is no obvious mispricing requiring urgent action in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

OXY's competitive position is stable to slightly improving:

- **Permian dominance maintained:** 2.9 million net acres, declining cost per barrel, CrownRock integration on track. No evidence of market share loss.
- **EOR differentiation:** 50+ years of CO₂ flood expertise provides incremental barrels from mature fields that peers cannot replicate at scale. This extends reserve life and provides natural integration with carbon capture.
- **Carbon management first-mover:** No peer has commercial-scale DAC. The market is not pricing either long-term value or capital sink with conviction.
- **Berkshire backing:** The 28% ownership + preferred relationship provides governance discipline and a valuation floor. Berkshire added 8M shares in early 2026.

OXY's structural disadvantage relative to peers is leverage. EOG and Devon operate near-zero debt. ConocoPhillips maintains investment-grade with comfortable coverage. OXY's $15B debt means it must allocate $1.1B annually to interest before any capital return — a permanent drag until debt reaches target levels.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Commodity Price Binary (Critical):** OXY holds zero hedges. Every dollar of oil price movement translates directly to earnings. At $80 Brent (a 25% decline from current), FY2026 EPS would compress to approximately $2.50-3.00, and the stock at $58.83 would trade at 20-24x forward — expensive for an E&P. A geopolitical resolution that reopens the Strait of Hormuz could compress oil prices 20-30% within weeks.

**2. Tronox Tax Contingency ($2.3B):** This inherited Anadarko liability remains unresolved before the U.S. Tax Court. An adverse ruling would require a cash outflow equal to approximately 70% of one year's current-rate FCF. Resolution timing is uncertain but could occur in 2026.

**3. CEO Transition:** Hollub retires June 1, 2026. While Jackson (COO successor) represents operational continuity, leadership transitions during cyclical peaks create strategy risk. Will Jackson maintain capital discipline or chase growth?

**4. STRATOS Execution:** Construction delays confirmed in May 2026. The project has consumed significant capital with no proven returns yet.

**5. Retained Environmental Liabilities:** Environmental expenditures grew to $874M in 2025. The Diamond Alkali Superfund Site alone required a $925M reserve increase in 2024. These are recurring cash drains that are difficult to model.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($70-75, 19-28% upside):**
Oil prices sustain above $100 through 2026 due to continued Hormuz disruption and structural supply deficit. OXY reaches $14.3B debt target by Q3 2026, restarting buybacks (~2% of market cap). FY2026 EPS reaches $5.50+. Multiple expands to 13x (peer parity) on deleveraging completion. Tronox resolves favorably. STRATOS begins operations successfully.

**Bear Case ($40-45, 24-32% downside):**
Geopolitical resolution reopens Hormuz, compressing Brent to $80-90. FY2026 EPS falls to $2.50-3.00. At 13-14x normalized earnings, the stock trades to $35-42. Tronox adverse ruling triggers $2.3B outflow, delaying buyback restart. STRATOS delays persist, consuming additional capital.

**Base Case ($62-66, 5-12% upside):**
Oil prices remain elevated ($95-110 Brent) but experience periodic 10-15% drawdowns on peace rhetoric. FY2026 EPS of $4.50-5.50. OXY trades at 11-12x forward, reflecting leverage discount to peers. Debt target reached by year-end 2026. Dividend raised modestly. CEO transition uneventful.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines below $48 (9x FY26E) while oil remains above $100 — creates sufficient margin of safety for the leverage risk
2. Debt reaches $14.3B target AND management announces specific buyback timeline — removes the primary discount driver
3. Tronox ruling in OXY's favor — eliminates $2.3B contingent liability, worth $2.30/share in avoided cash outflow

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Oil (Brent) falls below $90 sustained for 4+ weeks while OXY remains above $55 — creates valuation-fundamental disconnect
2. Tronox adverse ruling confirmed — immediately impairs balance sheet and extends deleveraging timeline by 12+ months
3. New CEO signals strategic pivot toward M&A or accelerated spending (abandoning capital discipline framework)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next catalyst is Q2 2026 earnings (likely August 2026), which will reflect a full quarter at elevated oil prices and provide updated debt trajectory guidance from new CEO Jackson. Geopolitical developments (Hormuz resolution signals) should trigger interim reassessment within 48 hours given OXY's zero-hedge profile.

## Conclusion

OXY is a competent E&P operator benefiting enormously from elevated oil prices, with a specific near-term catalyst (debt target completion → buyback restart) that justifies holding over a 12-month horizon. The stock at $58.83 trades at a rational discount to peers given its leverage and unhedged profile. If oil stays above $95 Brent and the company executes on deleveraging, the stock should trade to $62-66 as the leverage discount narrows and capital returns resume.

However, the risk profile prevents a BUY recommendation. Zero commodity hedges create binary downside on geopolitical resolution. The $2.3B Tronox contingency is unquantifiable in timing. The CEO transition introduces uncertainty. At 11x forward earnings that assume sustained $100+ oil, there is limited margin of safety if the current geopolitical premium in oil partially unwinds. The position is worth holding for investors already exposed, but new capital should wait for either a price pullback to $48-50 or a de-risking event (Tronox resolution, debt target completion).
