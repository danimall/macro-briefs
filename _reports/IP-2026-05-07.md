## Executive Summary

International Paper is simultaneously integrating its $9.9 billion DS Smith acquisition, implementing an aggressive 80/20 operational restructuring, divesting non-core assets, and preparing to spin off its EMEA business into an independent publicly traded company. The result is a company generating negative GAAP earnings ($-5.61 EPS), negative free cash flow in 2025 (-$159M), and carrying $9.8 billion of debt — yet trading at 7.9-8.6x forward EV/EBITDA with $4.3 million of recent insider buying at prices near or above the current stock price.

The core investment question is whether IP's capacity rationalization thesis (10% of North American containerboard capacity removed) translates into sustained pricing power in H2 2026 and 2027. Management's guidance depends on a $650 million EBITDA step-up from H1 to H2 2026, requiring price increases to stick, cost savings to materialize, and demand to remain at least flat. In a macro environment where recession probability is elevated, consumer spending is weakening, and the stock has already fallen 40% from its 52-week high of $56.13, the burden of proof is on the bull case. Insiders are buying, but the fundamental trajectory remains negative.

## Company Overview

International Paper operates as one of the world's largest fiber-based packaging companies with two primary segments: Packaging Solutions North America (PS NA) and Packaging Solutions EMEA (PS EMEA). Following the January 2025 DS Smith acquisition, the company operates approximately 11 million tons of annual containerboard capacity in North America across 170 corrugated packaging plants, plus 14 containerboard mills and 148 corrugated plants in EMEA.

Revenue reached $23.6 billion in 2025, though the organic business was effectively flat — the entire growth was attributable to DS Smith's $7.8 billion contribution. The company announced in January 2026 that it will separate into two independent companies (North America and EMEA) within 12-15 months, retaining approximately 20% of the EMEA entity.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Deterioration:** The GAAP picture is grim. Net loss of $3.5 billion in 2025 (vs. +$557M in 2024), driven by $2.47B goodwill impairment on EMEA, $958M accelerated depreciation, and $626M restructuring charges. Even on an adjusted basis, operating EPS was -$0.20 in 2025.

**Cash Flow Strain:** Operating cash flow was $1.7 billion (flat vs. 2024 despite a much larger business), while capital expenditures nearly doubled to $1.86B, producing negative free cash flow of -$159M. FCF is guided at $300-500M for 2026, but this requires the back-half EBITDA ramp.

**Balance Sheet Leverage:** Total debt of $9.8 billion with debt-to-equity of 1.96x. $992M matures in 2026 and $2.58B in 2027 — a total of $3.6B over the next 18 months. The company maintains BBB/Baa2 investment-grade ratings (stable outlook), but approximately $4B of debt has rate step-up provisions on credit downgrades.

**Dividend Sustainability:** At $1.93/share annually (~5.8% yield), the dividend costs approximately $975M per year. This exceeds 2026 guided FCF ($300-500M), creating a structural gap currently bridged by GCF sale proceeds ($1.5B). Without meaningful EBITDA improvement, dividend sustainability becomes questionable by late 2027.

**Earnings Track Record:** Three consecutive quarterly misses (Q2-Q4 2025), with Q3 representing a -189% miss and Q4 a -131% miss. Q1 2026 was essentially in-line (+3.4% surprise), representing stabilization but not recovery.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. Pricing Power from Capacity Rationalization:** Approximately 3.5-4 million tons of North American containerboard capacity (~10% of total) have been permanently removed since 2023. IP announced $40/ton (March) and $30/ton (April) price increases, with a second wave effective June 1, 2026. If these stick, analysts project $50-70/ton cumulative benefit in 2026.

**2. 80/20 Cost Program:** IP actioned ~$710M of full run-rate cost savings in 2025 through mill closures, plant rationalizations, and headcount reductions. The benefits are expected to accelerate through 2026, contributing approximately $150M of the $650M H2 step-up.

**3. Volume Outperformance:** North American box volumes grew +2.5% per-day in Q1 2026 vs. industry decline of -0.3%. IP has outperformed the market for three consecutive quarters, suggesting market share gains from competitive exits.

**Counterarguments:** Operating rates remain at ~91.2%, below the ~95% threshold typically needed for sustained pricing power. Industry demand is guided "approximately flat" for 2026 (revised down from flat-to-+1%). In a recessionary scenario, demand destruction could offset capacity closures entirely. Estimate revisions are uniformly negative: current-year EPS estimates have fallen 23.7% over 90 days.

## Valuation Assessment

At $33.50 with guided 2026 EBITDA of $3.2-3.5B, enterprise value (~$27.5B: $17.7B market cap + $9.8B debt) implies EV/EBITDA of 7.9-8.6x. Peer median P/E is 27.6x (vs. IP's negative trailing P/E). If IP achieves $3.5B EBITDA and re-rates to 9x, implied equity value is approximately $38-40/share (+13-19% upside).

The consensus analyst target of $39.36 (median $39.00) reflects this view. The low target of $32.00 is essentially current price; the high of $45.00 assumes successful execution and pricing.

For 2027, if normalized earnings reach $2.68/share (current consensus) and the stock trades at 14-16x (packaging industry typical), the stock could be worth $37-43. This assumes no further estimate cuts, yet the trend is uniformly downward.

The valuation is not obviously cheap given the execution risk. A 7.9-8.6x EV/EBITDA on uncertain EBITDA numbers with $9.8B of debt is a leveraged bet on cost savings and pricing sticking in a weakening demand environment.

## Competitive Landscape

IP's competitive position within North American containerboard is structurally improving. The company has been a primary driver of capacity rationalization, closing three mills and multiple plants in 2025. Combined with Georgia-Pacific and Smurfit Westrock closures, the supply discipline is real and coordinated (all three announced June 2026 price increases within days of each other).

IP's integrated model (75% internal conversion) provides insulation from spot market pricing pressure. The 80/20 system is designed to focus on the most profitable customer/product combinations while exiting low-margin business — a strategy that has worked well for other industrial companies (Danaher, Roper Technologies).

However, the EMEA segment is clearly struggling (goodwill impaired 73% within 11 months of acquisition), and the separation announcement itself suggests management recognizes the strategic combination was misguided. IP's 5-year TSR of 104 (essentially flat) significantly underperforms the peer group's 127.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution Risk (Severe):** No industrial company in recent memory has simultaneously integrated a $9.9B acquisition, implemented a comprehensive operational restructuring, completed a major divestiture, and prepared a spinoff within 2-3 years. Management bandwidth is a real constraint.

**Demand Risk:** The capacity rationalization thesis requires demand to remain at least flat. In a macro environment with weakening consumer sentiment and tariff uncertainty, sustained industrial demand contraction is a plausible scenario. If demand falls 3-5%, operating rates could remain below 90% despite closures.

**Refinancing Risk:** $3.6B of debt matures in 2026-2027. At current BBB/Baa2 ratings, refinancing is available but expensive (investment-grade spreads have widened). Any credit downgrade would trigger step-up provisions on ~$4B of outstanding debt.

**Dividend Cut Risk:** The mathematical gap between FCF guidance and dividend cost creates a non-trivial probability of dividend reduction. A cut would likely drive 15-20% additional downside given yield-focused shareholders.

**Antitrust Litigation:** Sherman Act class action alleging containerboard price-fixing conspiracy (since Nov. 2020) creates tail risk of material damages.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($42-45):** Capacity rationalization thesis plays out in H2 2026. Price increases of $50-70/ton flow through to EBITDA. 80/20 cost savings reach full run-rate. 2027 earnings reach $2.68 and the stock re-rates to 14-16x. EMEA separation unlocks value through focused management and cleaner capital structure. Insider buying is proven correct. *Probability: 25-30%.*

**Bear Case ($24-28):** Demand deteriorates in recession, price increases partially rolled back. H2 2026 EBITDA step-up underdelivers by $200-300M. Dividend cut announced. EMEA separation delayed or restructured unfavorably. Estimates continue falling. Credit downgrades trigger step-up provisions. *Probability: 30-35%.*

**Base Case ($33-37):** Pricing partially sticks but demand remains tepid. EBITDA reaches $3.2-3.3B (low end of guidance). Dividend maintained but under pressure. Stock trades at 7.5-8.5x forward EBITDA with gradual recovery into 2027. *Probability: 35-40%.*

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Specific near-term catalysts for further downside include: (1) Q2 2026 earnings (likely late July) where current-quarter estimates have collapsed from $0.29 to -$0.04 over 90 days — a miss or further guidance cut is plausible; (2) dividend cut announcement if Q2 FCF disappoints; (3) potential credit rating review if EBITDA underperforms and refinancing conditions tighten.

**Timing window:** Q2 earnings report (likely late July 2026) is the next catalyst. The dividend sustainability question becomes acute by Q4 2026 if FCF doesn't ramp.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 15.2% SI with 6.4 days to cover — moderately elevated but not extreme. This level suggests meaningful bearish conviction from institutions but creates some squeeze risk on positive surprises (price increase confirmation, better-than-feared demand data).

**Borrow and options signal:** IV is rich (60.7% vs. 41.7% HV), making put purchases expensive. The backwardated term structure implies near-term resolved uncertainty would reduce put premiums. Short borrow is likely available given institutional ownership patterns of large-cap industrials.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case of $24-28 represents 16-28% downside from $33.50. Upside risk to $38-40 represents 13-19% squeeze/improvement potential. At best, this offers approximately 1.5:1 reward-to-risk on the short side, below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict:** **NOT SHORTABLE** — The thesis is a challenged transformation company with execution risk, but insider buying ($4.3M from CEO and directors at $31-40), 5.8% dividend yield providing downside support, and credible capacity rationalization story reduce the probability of a clean downward move. The risk/reward is insufficient for an active short. Avoid owning, but don't short.

2. Management confirms full-year EBITDA guidance of $3.4B+ (upper half of range) at Q2 report
3. North American operating rates reach >93% (reported by industry sources like RISI/Fastmarkets)
4. Stock reaches $28-30 with dividend intact (creates >6.5% yield floor with clearer path to FCF coverage)

**Timeline:** Reassess after Q2 2026 earnings (late July). The H2 2026 EBITDA step-up is the key falsification test. If Q2 shows minimal sequential improvement from Q1's $740M run-rate, the $3.2B annual target becomes implausible and the stock likely tests new lows.

## Conclusion

International Paper presents a deeply levered industrial transformation where the upside thesis is plausible but unproven, while the downside risks are immediate and quantifiable. The negative earnings trajectory (3 of 4 quarters missed, estimates falling 24% over 90 days), elevated debt with near-term maturities, unsustainable dividend math, and macro headwinds to demand all argue against ownership at current levels.

The insider buying is genuine — $4.3M from CEO and directors at prices near or above current levels represents strong conviction from those with maximum information. In a different macro environment, or with two quarters of positive execution proof, this would warrant a constructive view. Today, with recession probability elevated, consumer spending weakening, and the entire thesis dependent on a $650M EBITDA step-up in H2 2026 that has yet to be demonstrated, the prudent approach is to wait for evidence before commitment.

**Recommendation: AVOID.** Revisit after Q2 2026 earnings provide the first test of the pricing/cost thesis, or if the stock reaches $28-30 where dividend yield and asset backing provide a more defensible floor.
