## Executive Summary

S&P Global is a collection of quasi-monopoly franchises in credit ratings, benchmark indices, and commodity price assessments, generating 42% operating margins and $5.1B in annual free cash flow. The stock has declined approximately 27% from its 52-week high of $579 to ~$424, creating a valuation gap: shares trade at 21.6x forward earnings versus peers trading at forward multiples in the mid-to-high 20s. This compression is attributable to broader macro selloff (geopolitical risk, rate uncertainty) rather than fundamental deterioration — Q1 2026 revenue grew 10.4% YoY and adjusted EPS of $4.97 beat consensus of $4.82 by approximately 3.1%.

Three senior executives — CEO Martina Cheung, Director Robert Moritz, and Indices CEO Catherine Clay — collectively invested approximately $2.6M in open-market stock purchases in a 3-day window (April 29 – May 1, 2026). This concentrated insider buying at the current depressed price constitutes the strongest alignment signal available for a company of this quality.

The mid-2026 Mobility spin-off removes a 22%-margin, $1.7B-revenue segment from a parent averaging 42%+ margins, which should improve the remaining entity's reported profitability and clarify the valuation framework. With the Investor Day on May 12, the catalyst timeline is defined and near-term.

## Company Overview

S&P Global operates five segments following the $44B IHS Markit merger (closed February 2022):

- **Market Intelligence** (32% of revenue): Capital IQ Pro, enterprise data feeds, credit & risk analytics — 20% operating margin, suppressed by $588M/year intangible amortization from IHS Markit
- **Ratings** (31%): Credit ratings for debt issuance — 64% operating margin, regulated oligopoly with Moody's and Fitch
- **Energy** (15%): Platts price assessments embedded in physical commodity contracts — 41% margin
- **Indices** (12%): S&P 500, DJIA benchmarks with linked ETF AUM — 69% gross margin
- **Mobility** (11%): CARFAX, automotive data — 22% margin, scheduled for mid-2026 spin-off

Revenue mix is 51% subscription, 13% non-transaction surveillance fees, 8% asset-linked, and 21% non-subscription/transaction revenue. This makes approximately 79% of revenue recurring or semi-recurring in nature, though some variable components within the recurring categories fluctuate with market activity.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Growth (FY2026):** Management guides 6-8% organic revenue growth and $19.40-$19.65 adjusted EPS (~7% growth vs. FY2025's $18.20 adjusted). Consensus for FY2026 sits at $19.63, essentially at the midpoint of guidance. For FY2027, consensus expects $22.21 (+13% YoY), reflecting normalization of Ratings issuance activity and continued index AUM growth.

**Secular Growth Drivers:**

1. **Passive investing shift:** ETF AUM linked to S&P DJI indices has continued to grow strongly, with index-linked asset fees rising 15% in 2025 driven by higher AUM for ETFs and mutual funds. Asset-linked revenue (8% of total) compounds with this structural trend regardless of issuance cycles.

2. **Private markets expansion:** The $1.8B With Intelligence acquisition (November 2025) and Cambridge Associates partnership (March 2026) position SPGI to serve the rapidly growing private capital ecosystem. S&P Global has noted that alternative assets are expected to approach $40T by the end of the decade, creating substantial demand for data, ratings, and benchmarks.

3. **Post-spin margin re-rating:** Removing Mobility's 22%-margin revenue base should improve consolidated margins for the remaining entity and simplify the equity story, though the realized benefit will depend on stranded costs and post-spin reporting adjustments. Management expects to update guidance after the separation is completed.

4. **Buyback compounding:** Recent net share-count reduction has been approximately 2-3% annually (diluted shares fell from 311.9M in 2024 to 305.1M in 2025). With the new 30M-share repurchase authorization, the pace could accelerate at current depressed prices, providing additional EPS compounding above revenue growth.

**Estimate Revisions:** Consensus estimates are flat to slightly declining (-1.1% over 90 days for the current quarter, -1.6% for FY2026). This does not constitute a positive revision signal. However, the stability of estimates (no significant downward revisions despite ~27% stock decline) means forward valuation has compressed purely on multiple contraction, not earnings deterioration.

At peer-median ~26x on $19.63 EPS, fair value is approximately $515. At a modest premium to peers (warranted by franchise quality and 64%/69% segment margins), fair value approaches $540-570. The current multiple represents a significant discount to where SPGI has historically traded in the post-IHS Markit era.

FCF yield of 4.0% compares favorably given SPGI's growth rate (consensus +13% EPS in FY2027) and track record of dividend growth.

The discount to intrinsic value appears driven by: (1) broad macro risk-off in Financials, (2) elevated rate uncertainty suppressing Ratings issuance expectations, and (3) general multiple compression from geopolitical risk premium. None of these represent permanent impairment to the franchise.

## Competitive Landscape

SPGI's competitive advantages are among the strongest in public markets:

**Ratings:** The NRSRO regulatory designation, combined with issuer-pay economics and "Big Three" oligopoly structure (S&P, Moody's, Fitch), creates near-impassable barriers to entry. No new globally recognized rating agency has emerged in decades. Switching costs are extreme — issuers need S&P ratings because investors require them.

**Indices:** The S&P 500 is the global equity benchmark. Network effects compound: more AUM linked → more liquidity → more recognition → more AUM linked. The brand is essentially impossible to replicate.

**Platts/Energy:** Price assessments embedded in physical commodity contracts (oil, gas, metals) function identically to Ratings — contractual reference creates lock-in.

**Emerging Risks to Position:** AI-enabled data scraping could theoretically commoditize some data products in Market Intelligence. The 10-K devotes new, extensive language to this risk. However, the highest-margin businesses (Ratings, Indices) derive value from regulatory moats and brand authority, not data difficulty-of-collection. Market Intelligence (where AI poses the most disruption risk) carries the lowest margins in the portfolio.

Competitive Position scores slightly lower than prior reports (previously 9.0) because the AI disruption risk is real for 30%+ of revenue, but the core Ratings/Indices franchise remains unassailable.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Credit cycle sensitivity (Ratings issuance):** Transaction revenue (16% of total) correlates with debt markets activity. A sustained freeze in corporate bond issuance (possible in stagflation scenario with rates >5%) would pressure growth. However, surveillance fees (non-transaction) provide a floor.

2. **AI disruption of data products:** Generative AI could reduce the value of collected/structured data over time, particularly affecting Market Intelligence. Timeline is 3-5 years but trajectory is unclear.

3. **CME Group put option on Indices JV:** CME can force SPGI to purchase their ~27% stake at fair value (~$5B). While unlikely near-term given strong business performance, this is a contingent cash obligation.

4. **Mobility spin-off execution:** Tax-free qualification requirements, dis-synergies, stranded costs, and management distraction create near-term operational risk.

5. **Regulatory expansion:** EU AI Act, DORA, ESG Ratings Regulation all adding compliance costs. Quantification is difficult but immaterial to current earnings given scale.

6. **Geopolitical/macro:** Broader tariff and geopolitical uncertainty has impacted segment-level guidance. Broader economic slowdown would reduce Ratings issuance and corporate data subscriptions.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($540-570, +27-34%)

- Peer multiple re-rating: 27-29x on $19.63 FY2026E = $530-570
- Mobility spin-off clarifies remaining portfolio as pure toll-bridge collection; RemainCo trades at 30x+ (MSCI comps)
- Q2-Q4 2026 earnings demonstrate continued 10%+ revenue growth, validating FY2027 $22+ EPS
- Rates stabilize → Ratings issuance accelerates → transaction revenue surges
- Private markets expansion provides visible multi-year growth vector
- Buybacks at depressed prices accelerate EPS compounding

### Bear Case ($380-400, -6% to -10%)

- Stagflation deepens: rates above 5.5% freeze corporate bond issuance for 2+ quarters
- Credit cascade materializes, paradoxically reducing Ratings new-issuance revenue even as credit stress benefits CDS/research products
- AI disruption accelerates faster than expected in Market Intelligence, compressing margins
- Mobility spin creates dis-synergies or fails to qualify for tax-free treatment
- Stock revisits February 2026 lows ($382)

### Base Case ($500-515, +18-21%)

- FY2026 delivers guided $19.40-$19.65 EPS; FY2027 consensus of $22.21 remains achievable
- Multiple re-rates from 21.6x toward 25-26x as macro fear premium moderates
- Mobility spin completes cleanly in H2 2026, unlocking RemainCo valuation premium
- Insider buying proves prescient as has historically been the case for this management team

## Conclusion

SPGI at ~$424 represents a high-quality franchise trading at a meaningful discount to peers and its own history, with defined near-term catalysts (Mobility spin, insider alignment, earnings normalization) and a 4% FCF yield providing downside support. The ~27% drawdown from highs was driven by macro fear (geopolitics, rates) rather than fundamental erosion — Q1 2026 beat estimates, margins continue expanding, and management's personal capital deployment confirms internal conviction.

The Financials cap concern is noted, but SPGI's business model (subscription data + regulatory moat + index licensing) has minimal correlation to traditional financial sector risk factors (credit losses, interest rate sensitivity). It belongs in the portfolio as a capital-efficient compounder that benefits from capital markets activity regardless of direction — volatility, issuance, ETF flows, and commodity trading all feed its revenue lines.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Peer-median forward P/E is closer to ~26x (not 27.5x), which lowers the peer-based fair value from ~$540 to ~$515 and compresses the base-case upside; the Mobility spin-off margin uplift is subject to stranded costs that could reduce realized benefit.
