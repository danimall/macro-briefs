**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Medtronic is the world's largest pure-play medical device company, generating $36.36B in FY2026 revenue (fiscal year ended April 24, 2026) across Cardiovascular, Neuroscience, and Medical Surgical, with a Diabetes/MiniMed unit now being separated. The FY26 print showed the strongest headline revenue growth in roughly a decade (+8.4%), led by a 12% Cardiovascular gain and a 17% surge in Cardiac Rhythm & Heart Failure driven by the pulsed field ablation (PFA) portfolio. That is a genuine improvement over the 2-5% growth the franchise has delivered for years, and it is the crux of management's attempt to close a well-documented performance gap.

Roughly 1-2 points of the FY26 growth came from FX tailwinds and a ~$129M favorable year-over-year swing in the Italian payback accrual, so underlying organic growth is closer to mid-single digits. Consensus estimates have not followed the "acceleration" narrative upward: current-year EPS was revised down 1.9% over 90 days and next-year down 2.0%, with next-quarter estimates cut 5.8%. The company also guides to a $250M pre-tax net tariff hit in FY27, offset by an unquantified potential IEEPA refund. This is a quality franchise generating $5.43B of free cash flow, but the earnings power is not inflecting the way the revenue headline suggests.

We rate MDT a HOLD (income-oriented). At $83.20 the stock trades at ~15x non-GAAP EPS and ~22x trailing GAAP EPS, offers a 3.4% dividend covered by FCF, and sits well below the analyst mean target of $97.84. The downside is cushioned by the yield and franchise durability; the upside requires PFA share gains against Boston Scientific's Farapulse, a clean Diabetes separation, and margin expansion to actually materialize in the estimates. Absent open-market insider buying, positive revision velocity, or a de-risking of the Medical Surgical goodwill and Puerto Rico tax overhangs, there is no catalyst to force a re-rating over the next 6-12 months.

## Company Overview

Medtronic is an Irish-domiciled (post-2015 Covidien inversion) medical technology company headquartered in Galway, serving 150+ countries with 95,000+ employees. Following the Diabetes carve-out, the business reports in three segments:

- **Cardiovascular ($13.98B, +12% YoY)** — the growth engine, comprising Cardiac Rhythm & Heart Failure (CRHF, +17% to $7.5B), Structural Heart & Aortic (+7%), and Coronary & Peripheral Vascular (+5%). Flagship products include Micra leadless pacemakers, the Aurora EV-ICD, the Affera/PulseSelect PFA platform, and CoreValve/Evolut TAVR.
- **Neuroscience ($10.29B, +4%)** — Cranial & Spinal Technologies (Mazor robotics, AiBLE ecosystem), Specialty Therapies (neurovascular, ENT, pelvic health), and Neuromodulation (Inceptiv closed-loop SCS, Percept DBS with BrainSense).
- **Medical Surgical ($8.82B, +5%)** — Surgical & Endoscopy (LigaSure, stapling, the Hugo robot, GI Genius AI) and Acute Care & Monitoring (Nellcor, McGRATH).
- **Diabetes ($3.11B, +13%)** — being spun off; MiniMed IPO'd ~10% on Nasdaq in March 2026, with Medtronic retaining ~90% pending full separation.

The moat rests on scale and category breadth, brand strength with physicians, hospital/GPO relationships, and a large installed base that produces recurring consumable and lead revenue. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** $31.2B (FY23) → $32.4B (FY24) → $33.5B (FY25) → $36.4B (FY26, +8.4%). The trend is genuinely improving, but the FY26 headline includes FX and a favorable Italian payback accrual swing (a +$39M FY26 accrual vs. a −$90M FY25 drag), so the reported acceleration overstates organic momentum.

**Profitability.** GAAP operating income was $6.47B (17.8% operating margin), net income $4.80B (13.3% margin), and GAAP diluted EPS $3.73. Non-GAAP EPS was $5.53 vs. $5.49 in FY25 — essentially flat despite the revenue growth, because $1.77B of intangible amortization plus restructuring, litigation, and tax items separate the two. The near-flat non-GAAP EPS is the number that matters for the thesis: revenue accelerated but per-share earnings did not, reflecting tariff cost pressure (COGS included $185M of higher tariffs/duties) and mix.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow of $7.33B against $1.90B capex yields $5.43B FCF (~15% FCF margin), a ~5.1% FCF yield on the ~$106.5B market cap. OCF comfortably exceeds net income, a marker of high-quality earnings.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets of $93.0B include $42.6B goodwill and $10.1B intangibles (~57% of assets intangible, a legacy of acquisition-heavy history). Total debt is $28.0B against $49.5B equity (D/E 0.87), current ratio 2.13, and A/A3 ratings. Liquidity is solid at $1.9B cash + $7.3B current investments + a $3.5B undrawn revolver. Altman Z of 2.22 sits in the grey zone, dragged by low asset turnover (0.39) and the intangible-heavy base rather than by any distress signal; ROA of 5.2% and ROE of 9.8% are modest for the same structural reason.

**Capital allocation.** Only 10M shares were repurchased in FY26 (vs. 38M in FY25) at an average $93.25 — above today's $83.20, which is a mild negative on execution. About $1.2B remains on the $5B authorization. The buyback slowed as cash was redirected to debt repayment and tuck-in M&A (CathWorks $525M, Scientia Vascular ~$550M up front, SPR Therapeutics ~$650M pending). The dividend is well covered by FCF and anchors the income case.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one narrow miss across the last four quarters, with surprises in the ±0.4-2.4% range. The most recent quarter (June 2026) missed by $0.007. This is a company that hits consensus tightly, not one that structurally beats, which limits the upside optionality that estimate-beating names carry.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is now concentrated in Cardiovascular, and within it, pulsed field ablation. CRHF's 17% growth is the standout, and PFA (PulseSelect + Affera Sphere-9/Sphere-360) is the fastest-growing electrophysiology category. Structural Heart (TAVR) and the neuromodulation/cranial-spinal robotics franchises provide a mid-single-digit backbone. Diabetes, the fastest-growing segment at +13%, is being divested — a deliberate portfolio move to raise consolidated margins and let Cardiovascular set the growth narrative, at the cost of shedding an accelerating asset.

Regulatory momentum in FY26 supports the story: Hugo RAS FDA clearance for urology (Dec 2025), a CMS National Coverage Determination for Symplicity Spyral renal denervation (Oct 2025), Affera Sphere-360 CE Mark (Jan 2026), and Stealth AXiS robotic navigation approvals. These broaden the addressable market over a multi-year horizon.

The counterweight is the estimate trend. Consensus current-year EPS fell to $5.95 from $6.06 ninety days ago (−1.9%), next-year to $6.40 from $6.54 (−2.0%), and next-quarter EPS was cut 5.8%. Growth Prospects is the dimension we weight most heavily as a winner-selector, and here the signal is flat-to-negative. Revenue is accelerating while EPS estimates drift lower, which points to margin and tariff pressure eating the top-line gains. This is a mid-single-digit organic grower with a compressing earnings bridge, not the +30% estimate-revision inflection that would justify a growth-driven BUY. We score Growth Prospects 5.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At $83.20, MDT trades at ~15x non-GAAP EPS ($5.53) and ~22x trailing GAAP EPS ($3.73), with forward P/E on the $5.95 current-year non-GAAP estimate of ~14x. The peer set in Health Care Equipment carries a median P/E of 41.0 (SYK 35.4x, ISRG 50.2x, BSX 19.7x, EW 46.5x, IDXX 41.0x). On the surface Medtronic looks deeply discounted to that group.

The discount is largely warranted rather than a mispricing. Those peers grow faster (ISRG, EW), carry cleaner balance sheets, or command scarcity premiums; Medtronic's mid-single-digit organic growth, decade of TSR underperformance (5-year TSR of 97.82 vs. peer 148.62), and 57%-intangible balance sheet earn a structurally lower multiple. Boston Scientific at 19.7x is the more apt comparison, and even that trades above MDT despite MDT's superior FCF, reflecting BSX's faster growth and PFA leadership. Cheapness relative to peers is not, by itself, evidence the market is wrong: this is a mature large-cap where a low multiple has persisted for years for identifiable reasons.

The valuation is not stretched, and there is downside support. The 3.4% dividend is covered by $5.43B FCF, the ~5.1% FCF yield provides an absolute floor, and analyst targets (mean $97.84, median $100.00, low $78.00, high $121.00) sit well above the current price. We do not treat those targets as forward-looking floors in the inflection sense, because the estimate revisions are negative — analysts are more likely to trim than raise near-term. But they do frame a reasonable base case in the low-$90s if PFA momentum and the Diabetes separation execute. We score Valuation 5.5: cheap enough to limit downside, not cheap enough versus its own fundamentals and trajectory to be an asymmetric buy.

## Competitive Landscape

The thesis hinges on two battlegrounds. In **PFA**, Medtronic's Affera system differentiates on combined mapping plus dual RF/PFA energy, and CRHF's 17% growth confirms real traction. But Boston Scientific's Farapulse is the current PFA share leader, with J&J also competing; Medtronic is gaining in a fast-growing category rather than dominating it. Execution here is the single largest swing factor for Cardiovascular and, by extension, the equity.

In **surgical robotics**, Hugo secured FDA urology clearance (Dec 2025) with general and gynecologic clearances pending. This is credible optionality against Intuitive Surgical's da Vinci, but the consensus view — which we share — is that da Vinci's installed-base moat is very difficult to displace. Hugo is a long-dated call option rather than a near-term needle-mover, and bariatric/advanced stapling volumes are simultaneously pressured by GLP-1 drugs shrinking the surgical funnel.

Broader dynamics — hospital/GPO consolidation, the shift of procedures to lower-cost ambulatory surgery centers, and China volume-based procurement tenders — apply pricing pressure across the portfolio. Medtronic's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving in Cardiovascular, stable in Neuroscience, and pressured in Medical Surgical. The trajectory has been marked down from prior reads (competitive position fell 8.0 → 6.5 over recent quarters) as the PFA share picture stayed contested and GLP-1 headwinds became explicit. We score Competitive Position 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill impairment.** The Medical Surgical reporting unit's fair value exceeds carrying value by only ~12% while holding $20.0B of goodwill — the thinnest cushion in the company. A 5% cut to projected cash flows would narrow that to ~7%. Separately, management explicitly warns of possible impairment on the retained MiniMed stake, whose stock has declined post-IPO. Two live impairment threads.

**Diabetes/MiniMed separation.** A dedicated new risk category. With ~90% still owned, the separation carries delay, stranded-cost, dis-synergy, tax, and MDT-share-volatility risk around each step.

**Puerto Rico IRS transfer pricing.** The FY2005-06 dispute was remanded to the Tax Court in September 2025, still unresolved after ~16 years, with an adverse outcome flagged as potentially material — relevant given the company's ~17% non-GAAP tax rate depends on its Irish/Puerto Rico structure.

**Tariffs.** A $250M FY27 pre-tax net hit, partly offset by an unquantified potential IEEPA refund following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling. Genuinely two-sided but a near-term margin drag.

**GLP-1 disruption.** Explicitly named as pressuring U.S. bariatric surgery volumes, a Medical Surgical headwind.

**China (~6% of revenue).** VBP price compression, IP-protection weakness, and technology export restrictions.

Risk Factors was marked down from prior reads (6.5 → 4.5) as the separation risk section, the thin MedSurg cushion, and the IRS remand crystallized. We score it 5.0: the risks are real and partially unpriced, but they are gradual/probabilistic rather than acutely binary, and the balance sheet absorbs them.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 24.8% against 23.2% one-year historical vol — fairly priced, in the 3rd percentile of the current chain, indicating little event premium. IV compressed 3.3pp over the past month as the price rose 4.9%, consistent with resolved earnings uncertainty. Put/call skew of 10.1% (elevated near-term put demand) and a low P/C OI ratio of 0.40 point to modest hedging rather than aggressive bearish positioning. The 6-month term shows a −2.7% skew (call-leaning further out), which is mildly constructive. The options market is not pricing a near-term catalyst in either direction, consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($100-110).** PFA share gains against Farapulse sustain CRHF's high-teens growth; Hugo clearances expand beyond urology; the Diabetes separation completes cleanly and lifts consolidated margins; the IEEPA tariff refund materializes; and consensus revisions turn positive, re-rating the stock toward the BSX multiple and the ~$100 median analyst target. Non-GAAP EPS reaches ~$6.40 (next-year consensus) and the multiple expands to ~16-17x.

**Bear case ($68-72).** The Medical Surgical goodwill cushion (~12%) triggers an impairment; the Puerto Rico IRS case resolves adversely; GLP-1 headwinds deepen in bariatrics; PFA momentum stalls as competition intensifies; tariff drag exceeds the $250M guide with no refund; and the near-flat non-GAAP EPS trend persists, compressing the multiple toward ~12x on stagnant earnings. This is roughly 13-18% downside from $83.20.

The base case sits between: a mid-single-digit organic grower with a 3.4% covered yield, drifting in the low-$80s to low-$90s as PFA and Diabetes-separation progress is weighed against tariff, impairment, and tax overhangs, with tightly-hit but non-rising estimates capping the re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income-oriented), 6-12 month horizon.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus current-year and next-year EPS estimates turn positive over a rolling 90-day window (currently −1.9% and −2.0%), especially if paired with PFA-driven CRHF growth sustaining above 15%.
- Clustered open-market insider buying, which is currently absent — the strongest bullish signal is not present.
- A clean, completed Diabetes separation with quantified accretion to consolidated margin, plus resolution or favorable narrowing of the Medical Surgical goodwill cushion.
- Price re-entry toward the high-$70s that widens the FCF yield above ~5.5% with fundamentals intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Medical Surgical or MiniMed goodwill impairment charge.
- An adverse Puerto Rico IRS ruling with material tax liability.
- Organic growth decelerating back below 3% or a guidance cut, confirming the FY26 acceleration was transitory.
- A dividend coverage deterioration (FCF falling below the dividend obligation) — not currently a risk.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (fiscal Q2 FY27, roughly November 2026), the anticipated full Diabetes separation, and any 8-K disclosing an impairment or IRS development.

## Conclusion

Medtronic delivered its best revenue growth in a decade, and the Cardiovascular/PFA engine gives the franchise a credible story it has lacked for years. But the improvement is partly flattered by FX and accrual timing, non-GAAP EPS was essentially flat, estimate revisions are drifting lower, and the equity carries fresh separation, impairment, and tax overhangs on top of a decade of TSR underperformance. Insiders offer no open-market conviction, and the buyback has slowed after repurchasing shares above the current price.

At ~15x non-GAAP EPS with a 3.4% FCF-covered dividend and a ~5.1% FCF yield, the downside is cushioned and the shares are reasonable to hold for income and PFA/Hugo optionality. There is no catalyst to force the re-rating that would justify a BUY. We reiterate HOLD, classified as an income hold, and would upgrade on positive estimate-revision velocity, insider buying, or a de-risked separation and goodwill picture, and downgrade on an impairment charge or an adverse IRS outcome.
