## Executive Summary

Tyson is two businesses trading as one. Chicken (10.5% nine-month segment margin), Prepared Foods (12.7%), Pork (3.2%) and International (7.2%) generated $2,604M of segment operating income in the first nine months of FY2026 and carry roughly three-quarters of the $35.6B balance sheet. Beef — 39% of nine-month sales, under 10% of assets after its goodwill was fully written off in FY2025 — lost $701M over the same period and is guided to an adjusted loss of $500-650M for the full year. Cattle costs rose $1,975M over nine months on top of roughly $1,840M in FY2025. Because Tyson owns no cattle-raising operations and buys fed cattle on the spot market, herd scarcity transfers value directly to ranchers and feedyards with no offsetting hedge.

The 3 August print changed the character of the Beef problem. Q3 volume fell 15.9% while average price rose 12.1%. Prior years were spread compression; this is spread compression plus demand destruction, and management said so plainly: footprint optimization "delivered as expected, however, it was more than offset by USDA margin compression," and the 24 August Mexican feeder-cattle border reopening (roughly 5% of U.S. harvest, six-month-plus lag, "close to a year before you see the positive impact") will not close the gap. Consensus responded: current-quarter EPS estimates are down 7.4% in 30 days, current-year FY2026 down 2.1%, next-year FY2027 down 2.5% over 90 days. This is the inverse of the estimate-revision signal that would support adding capital.

The offset is that the price already reflects much of this. At $56.58 against ~352M total Class A and Class B shares, equity value is ~$19.9B and enterprise value ~$27.3B, or 7.6x an FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of roughly $3.6B and 14.3x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.95. Net leverage has improved from 3.05x to roughly 2.7x, liquidity is $3,990M against a single interest-coverage covenant, and the $2.05 annualized dividend absorbed 58% of nine-month free cash flow. That combination supports holding for yield and for the option on a cattle cycle that USDA's July inventory report suggests has bottomed. It does not support a purchase at a price where the base case offers single-digit appreciation and the litigation and impairment tails are genuinely open.

## Company Overview

Tyson processes and brands protein: approximately 133,000 employees, sales into roughly 140 countries, and brands including Tyson, Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm, Ball Park and ibp. From Q1 FY2026 the company reports five segments (Beef, Pork, Chicken, Prepared Foods, International) and no longer allocates corporate expense or amortization to them, a change that removed roughly $642M of corporate expense and $162M of amortization from nine-month segment results. Any comparison of FY2026 segment margins against FY2025 10-K segment margins on the old allocated basis will flatter FY2026; the figures used here are on the recast basis throughout.

The segment structures differ in a way that matters more than the aggregate. Chicken is fully integrated from breeding stock (wholly-owned Cobb-Vantress) through feed mills, contract grow-out, processing and further processing; feed ingredients were roughly 53% of the cost of growing a live domestic chicken in FY2025, so feed relief accrues to Tyson rather than to third-party growers. Beef is spot procurement of live cattle converted into boxed beef, a pure spread business with no structural protection against scarcity. Prepared Foods is branded value-added product using internally and externally sourced meat, which means high cattle and hog values raise its input costs at the same time they crush Beef.

Walmart accounted for approximately 18.7% of FY2025 consolidated sales across all segments, and customer agreements are described as generally short-term.

**Data note.** The supplied market capitalization of $15.8B captures Class A only. Class B (70M shares, 99.987% held by Tyson Limited Partnership) converts share-for-share and participates economically; total shares are approximately 352M. Every multiple in this report uses ~$19.9B equity value and ~$27.3B enterprise value. The supplied trailing and forward P/E of 33.79 are identical and reflect FY2025 GAAP EPS of $1.33, a charge-laden year; they are not usable as forward metrics.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Revenue has been flat at $53-54B for four years while operating income fell from $4,410M in FY2022 to $1,098M in FY2025, a 2.0% operating margin and 0.9% net margin. FY2025 sales growth of 2.1% came entirely from price (+3.3%) with flat volume, and was reduced by $698M of legal accruals recorded against revenue.

Nine-month FY2026: sales $41,834M (+3.1%), operating income $1,099M (+16.9%), net income to Tyson $527M, diluted EPS $1.49 versus $1.20. Q3: sales $13,868M (−0.1%), operating income $362M versus $260M, EPS $0.52 versus $0.17. On the adjusted basis analysts track, Q3 came in at $0.99 against a $1.00 consensus, the first miss in four quarters, following beats of 36.5%, 2.1% and 10.4%.

| Segment (9M FY26) | Sales | Op income | Margin | Prior-yr margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beef | $16,367M | ($701M) | (4.3)% | (4.4)% |
| Pork | $4,768M | $151M | 3.2% | (1.3)% |
| Chicken | $12,753M | $1,344M | 10.5% | 10.5% |
| Prepared Foods | $7,741M | $982M | 12.7% | 13.8% |
| International | $1,760M | $127M | 7.2% | 9.0% |

Two segment reads deserve correction and one deserves emphasis. First, the widely reported Chicken deceleration (Q3 operating income $389M versus $475M) is largely a $98M Broiler-litigation accrual recorded as a reduction of Chicken sales. Adding it back gives roughly $487M on $4,353M, an 11.19% margin against 11.26% a year earlier, flat rather than down 220bp. That add-back assumes the entire accrual sits in the Chicken line as the 10-Q attributes it, and there is no company-published adjusted segment bridge confirming the allocation; it also comes at a favorable feed-cost point ($60M of nine-month relief), so flat margin is stability rather than proven pricing power.

Second, Prepared Foods is the more concerning line. Q3 margin fell from 15.5% to 12.2% and nine-month from 13.8% to 12.7%. Disclosed raw-material inflation of $30M in Q3 on $2,557M of sales explains roughly 120bp of a ~330bp decline; the 10-Q does not bridge the remainder. This is an unresolved gap, and it sits directly on top of the brand-impairment sensitivity discussed under risk.

Third, Pork swung from a $58M loss to $151M of profit, a genuine, under-discussed positive worth roughly $209M year over year.

**Cash flow.** Nine-month operating cash flow fell 9.3% to $1,469M while operating income rose 16.9%. The reconciling item is a −$390M working-capital and other swing, including roughly $461M of cash legal settlement payments (pork $245M, beef $193M, broiler $23M) against $269M of new accruals; accrued current legal contingencies fell from $712M to $488M. Underlying cash generation is therefore better than the headline decline suggests, because roughly $0.46B of outflow settles liabilities charged in prior periods. The qualification is that $269M of new accruals in nine months shows the balance is being replenished, and the accrual roll-forward does not fully tie ($712 + $269 − $461 = $520M versus $488M reported).

Nine-month free cash flow of $913M (OCF $1,469M less capex $556M) is roughly flat year over year and covers dividends of $529M and buybacks of approximately $123M. Three items reduce its quality: capex has run below depreciation for two years ($978M versus $1,093M in FY2025, guided to $0.7-0.9B in FY2026); cold-storage facility sales contributed $252M in FY2025 and $44M in nine-month FY2026, creating offsetting long-term service purchase commitments that convert depreciation into future operating expense; and supplier finance program obligations inside accounts payable rose from $52M to $198M, meaning part of the $240M payables inflow reflects third-party-financed term extension. None of these is distress: sale-leaseback of non-core logistics is standard, and $198M is only 7% of the $2,761M payables balance. But reported FCF is flattered relative to core earnings.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt $8,006M against cash $740M is net debt of $7,266M and 0.44x book equity. Rolling LTM EBITDA to roughly $2,657M puts net leverage near 2.7x, down from 3.05x at FY2025, achieved through both debt reduction and EBITDA growth. Liquidity is $3,990M (cash plus a $2,500M revolver to April 2030 and a $750M revolving term facility to December 2028), with a single minimum interest-coverage covenant in compliance and BBB/Baa2 ratings. Debt is 99% fixed at a 4.9% weighted average, so the live issue is refinancing cost, not rate sensitivity: the $1,300M 3.550% notes due June 2027 were reclassified into current liabilities at quarter-end, and press reports on 10-11 August describe cash tender offers of up to roughly $1.2B alongside a $500M 5.100% new issue. I could not retrieve those 8-Ks; treat the size, structure and pro-forma effect as unconfirmed. The direction is clear: sub-4% paper is being replaced near 5.1%, worth roughly $20M of incremental annual interest on $1.3B.

Reconstructing the screening metrics: the Altman Z of 2.69 reproduces using book equity in the fourth term; substituting market equity gives roughly 3.05, at or above the "safe" threshold for the public-company formula. A full nine-signal Piotroski computation for FY2025 produces 7/9, with the three lost points (ROA, current ratio, gross margin) all traceable to the FY2025 charge load and the notes reclassification. Solvency is not the near-term question.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story here at present. Four years of flat revenue, FY2025 growth entirely from price, total Q3 volume up 1%, Beef volume down 12.0% over nine months and 15.9% in Q3. Consensus has FY2026 adjusted EPS at $3.95 and FY2027 at $4.48, both revised down over 30 and 90 days.

The credible growth vector is cyclical, not structural. Three data points support a Beef bottom: USDA's July Cattle Inventory showed total cattle numbers nudging higher for the first time in the cycle, off the smallest herd since 1951; carcass weights at roughly 962 lb are ~30 lb heavier year over year and ~65 lb above the 2020-2024 average, so fed beef production fell only 5.4% against an 8.6% slaughter decline, which helps per-head fixed-cost absorption; and the Choice cutout, after falling from $396.53 in late June to $362.81 in early August, gained nearly $4/cwt in the most recent week. Against that, industry fed-cattle plant utilization is roughly 78% (cow plants 55-60%), packer margins ran −$50 to −$300/head through 2026, and the Mexico border reopening is a 2027-and-beyond item on management's own timeline.

Quantifying the option: if Beef's adjusted loss narrows from the guided −$575M midpoint to −$200M, that is $375M of pretax recovery, roughly $0.80 of after-tax EPS on 352M shares. Layer that onto FY2027 consensus and normalized earnings power approaches $5.25-5.30. That is the bull arithmetic, and it requires two to three years plus a herd rebuild that management declines to date.

Tyson has done the self-help. The $241M network optimization program is 99% recognized: Lexington, Nebraska (4,500 head/day nameplate, running ~3,650) closed in January 2026 and Amarillo, Texas moved from ~5,500 to ~2,800 head/day, over 7,000 head/day of capacity removed from an over-supplied system, which is what industry analysts say the sector needs. The trade-off is that capacity cuts permanently reduce Tyson's earnings leverage to an eventual rebuild.

FY2026 guidance of $2.1-2.3B adjusted operating income implies Q4 adjusted operating income of roughly $464-664M against a Q3 adjusted figure near $533M, a modest sequential decline to modest improvement, helped by FY2026 being a 53-week year. That bridge is my construction from disclosed add-backs, not a company-published reconciliation.

Peer comparison is unhelpfully noisy: SJM at −92x and GIS at −226x carry impairment losses, MKC's 8.8x looks like a data error, leaving HRL at 29.5x and LW at 25.1x against a stated median of 25.1x. On its face TSN at 14.3x forward looks like a large discount. Two adjustments narrow it considerably.

First, the adjusted-to-GAAP gap is not a one-time artifact. FY2025 adjusted operating income of $2,267M compared to $1,098M GAAP, a $1,169M gap, and legal contingency accruals have recurred every year ($174M FY2024, $738M FY2025, $269M in nine months of FY2026). Charging a normalized $275-300M of annual legal cost against adjusted EPS takes FY2026 to roughly $3.30 and the multiple to about 17x. Second, book-value support is thinner than 1.10x P/B suggests: goodwill and intangibles are $14.94B, or 42% of assets, and tangible book is $8.91/share.

EV/EBITDA of 7.6x is fair for a protein processor at a cyclical trough, neither distressed nor cheap. The absolute yield of 3.6%, covered at 58% of nine-month FCF and grown 2% in FY2026, is the most defensible valuation support.

Analyst consensus (mean $68.75, median $67.50, high $78.00, low $56.00; 2 strong buy, 2 buy, 8 hold, 1 strong sell) sits well above spot. On a stock with rising estimates I would treat those targets as floors. Here estimates are falling in every period, which makes the high side of that range stale rather than predictive; the $56.00 low target is at spot.

Scenario values, all on the $56.58 basis:

- **Bear $45 (−20.5%)**: FY2027 estimates cut toward $3.60 as Beef losses persist near current run-rate and Prepared Foods margin stays below 13%, at 12.5x. Assumes a beef antitrust settlement in the $300-500M range and/or a non-cash Prepared Foods brand impairment compressing sentiment and book support.
- **Base $61 (+7.8%)**: 13.6x FY2027 consensus $4.48, equivalent to roughly 7.3x EV/EBITDA on $3.7B. Beef loss narrows modestly, Chicken holds ~10.5%, Prepared Foods stabilizes near 12.5-13%. With the dividend, total return ~11%.
- **Bull $74 (+30.8%)**: 14x normalized EPS near $5.30 as Beef losses narrow to −$200M by FY2028, cattle supply rebuilds and Prepared Foods returns to 13.5%.

Weighting bull 25%, base 45%, bear 30% gives roughly +5% price return plus a partially captured 3.6% dividend, call it 8-9% expected total return over twelve months. That is approximately fair, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

Tyson's moat is in Chicken genetics and integration and in Prepared Foods brands, not in Beef or Pork. Item 1's competition discussion is generic and claims no category share leadership, which is itself informative. Cobb-Vantress is one of the world's leading poultry breeding-stock suppliers, and full vertical integration means feed relief lands in Tyson's margin.

In Beef the industry structure is against every packer. Roughly 78% average fed-cattle plant utilization, cow-plant utilization of 55-60%, and new high-efficiency cow capacity (American Foods Group's Wright City; CS Beef's Kuna) pulling cattle from older plants have produced negative packer margins across 2026. Competitors are restructuring in the same direction: JBS halted cattle harvesting at Souderton and committed $30M to convert it to case-ready value-added packaging; Cargill's Fort Morgan plant has not processed cattle since 23 April over a Teamsters dispute. Tyson's Lexington closure and Amarillo shift reduction place it early rather than late in that consolidation.

Policy is tilting the other way. USDA announced up to $500M in Commodity Credit Corporation funding for small and mid-sized beef processors, which subsidizes sub-scale capacity during an overcapacity episode and slows the rationalization that would restore packer margins; Tyson, as one of four companies controlling roughly 80% of the market, is presumptively ineligible. The eligibility criteria are unverified and $500M spread across many processors is unlikely to move national capacity materially, but the political direction is unambiguous.

Trajectory read: Chicken stable-to-improving on a mix basis (management cites retail and foodservice volume up 3.8% against 1% total volume growth, with positive net price realization even as inputs softened, a characterization rather than an independently verified figure); Prepared Foods deteriorating on margin with flat volume; Beef structurally disadvantaged with reduced future leverage after capacity cuts. Net: stable-to-slightly-negative, consistent with the score decline from 6.5 in February.

## Risk Assessment

**Antitrust is the largest un-quantified exposure and it escalated in July.** On 16 July 2026 the court granted certain class certification motions in the beef litigation; cattle rancher/producer classes remain live, and Reuters reported that consumers and other beef buyers seeking "billions of dollars in damages" obtained class status. The beef accrual is $215M after $193M of payments. Broiler exposure has risen from $64M to $139M with the first Track Two trial scheduled for September 2027; Tyson's conditional DOJ leniency covers broiler criminal exposure only and depends on continued cooperation. The Antitrust Division has issued civil investigative demands on the beef business and is separately investigating poultry-industry HR/wage practices with no liability recorded. A referenced May 2026 criminal investigation into the "Big Four" appears only in a secondary opinion column, is not in the 10-Q, and should be treated as unconfirmed.

**Prepared Foods brand impairment is disclosed as near-threshold.** The FY2025 10-K states that for two quantitatively tested Prepared Foods brands with carrying values of ~$0.5B and ~$0.3B, "Had we assumed future growth rates consistent with those realized in fiscal 2025, we would have failed the impairment quantitative test." Nine-month FY2026 delivered margin contraction and 0.1% Q3 volume rather than the assumed acceleration. Impairment would be non-cash and would not affect the coverage covenant or the dividend, but it would remove the book-value support that makes 1.10x P/B look defensive.

**Other specifics:** Walmart at 18.7% of sales on short-term agreements; approximately 47% of the ~31,000 union-covered U.S. employees are under renewal negotiation or covered by agreements expiring in FY2026 (Cargill's Fort Morgan shows what a dispute costs); New World screwworm confirmed in the U.S. in June 2026, which is the reason the Mexican border closed and therefore sits directly on the one identified path to Beef relief; IRS notices of proposed adjustment of up to ~$127M; a ~$537M Mexican tax assessment with no liability recorded, now contractually assumed by the 2015 purchaser, meaningful de-risking whose value depends on undisclosed counterparty capacity; and participation in a multiemployer pension plan reported to have significant underfunding.

**Governance.** Tyson Limited Partnership and family members hold approximately 71.94% of voting power via 10-vote Class B stock; Tyson is a controlled company and has elected NYSE exemptions from majority-independent board and independent compensation/nominating committee requirements. Class A holders cannot influence board composition.

I found no material additions or deletions to risk factors between the FY2025 10-K and the Q3 10-Q. The new narrative disclosure is Middle East geopolitical tension raising transportation, freight, energy and cooking-oil costs, consistent with $150M of nine-month freight inflation.

### Options Market Signal

Post-print vol has normalized: near-term ATM IV fell from 35.8% on 22 July to 25.9%, essentially level with 25.3% one-year realized (19th percentile within the chain), with a ±3.4% implied move on the 21 August expiry. There is no cheap-vol entry signal and no priced near-term dislocation. Short interest of 3.1% of float and 2.9 days to cover means the bear case is uncrowded; there is no squeeze dynamic in either direction. Options positioning neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental read, which is appropriate for a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($74, +30.8%).** The cattle cycle has bottomed on USDA data, and Tyson enters the recovery with 7,000+ head/day of capacity permanently removed while competitors also rationalize. Beef losses narrow toward −$200M by FY2028, worth roughly $0.80 of EPS. Chicken's underlying Q3 margin was flat at ~11.2% once the $98M litigation accrual is added back, Pork swung $209M positive year over year, and a P&G-trained CEO taking over on 4 October reorients the company toward the branded and value-added assets that hold three-quarters of the balance sheet and effectively all the profit. Net leverage at 2.7x and $3,990M of liquidity mean the company can wait, paying a 3.6% covered dividend and buying stock, which it did more aggressively as the price fell (0.5M shares at $57.12 in June, ~0.9M at ~$54.40 post-quarter). At 14x normalized $5.30, the stock is $74.

**Bear case ($45, −20.5%).** Beef is now a demand problem, not only a spread problem: Q3 volume −15.9% against price +12.1% says the consumer is exiting at retail beef prices, and management has no near-term fix. Prepared Foods, the profit engine, lost 330bp of Q3 margin with only ~120bp explained by disclosed raw-material inflation, and its brand carrying values would have failed impairment testing on trailing growth assumptions. Estimates are falling in every period. Newly certified beef purchaser classes and a September 2027 broiler trial mean the legal accrual, which consumed ~$461M of cash in nine months, keeps being replenished. Free cash flow is flattered by sub-depreciation capex, $296M of cumulative cold-storage sales that created offsetting service commitments, and supplier-finance-assisted payables. Refinancing steps coupons from 3.55% to 5.1%. And the compensation framework insulates management from the charges driving the earnings decline: CEO total pay rose 51% to $34.47M in FY2025 while GAAP operating income fell 22% and five-year cumulative TSR of 106.07 trailed peers at 149.94, with the bonus metric (Adjusted Operating Income $2,267M) rising 25%. At 12.5x a cut $3.60, the stock is $45.

**Where I come out.** The bear case is more specific and better documented; the bull case is real but back-loaded to FY2027-28 and dependent on a herd rebuild management will not date. Probability-weighted expected total return of 8-9% against a 20% drawdown scenario is a fair-value outcome. This is the third consecutive HOLD, supported by three concrete offsets to the guidance cut: net leverage improving to ~2.7x, Chicken's underlying stability once the litigation accrual is isolated, and a dividend covered at 58% of free cash flow. Those offsets do not warrant a move to ACCUMULATE.

On the score trajectory (4.8 → 4.8 → 4.1 → 5.3 → 5.0 → 4.9): the marginal decline is not price-driven; the stock is essentially unchanged from the 22 July reference at $57.06. It reflects the 3 August guidance cut widening the Beef loss range, the first adjusted EPS miss in four quarters, Prepared Foods Q3 margin down 330bp, negative estimate revisions in all four periods, and the 16 July beef class certification. Valuation improves modestly (12.6x FY2027 with a covered yield); competitive position and risk carry the offset.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case $61** — 13.6x FY2027 consensus $4.48, or ~7.3x EV/EBITDA on $3.7B. **+7.8%** from $56.58; ~11% including the dividend. **Bull $74 (+30.8%). Bear $45 (−20.5%).** Horizon: twelve months, anchored on the Q4 FY2026 print and FY2027 guidance (early-to-mid November 2026).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. FY2027 Beef adjusted loss guided better than −$300M, or two consecutive quarters of Beef volume declines inside 5%.
2. Prepared Foods segment margin back above 13.5% for two consecutive quarters, with a disclosed bridge for the Q3 FY2026 shortfall.
3. FY2027 consensus EPS revisions turning positive for two consecutive months, or a price below roughly $48-50 that lifts base-case upside above 20%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A beef antitrust settlement or adverse judgment above ~$750M, or a step-up in aggregate legal accruals beyond the ~$300M annual run-rate.
2. A Prepared Foods brand or goodwill impairment coinciding with free cash flow falling below the ~$700M annual dividend, which would put coverage at risk.
3. Net leverage back above 3.25x, or Beef volume declines continuing at double-digit rates through Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q4 FY2026 report with FY2027 guidance and the first strategic commentary from Jeff Schomburger (effective 4 October 2026), plus confirmation of the August tender-offer terms and pro-forma net debt when the 8-Ks become readable. The 24 August Mexican border reopening is a monitorable but, on management's own sizing, not a 2026 earnings event.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Tyson at $56.58 offers 12.6x FY2027 consensus earnings, 7.6x EV/EBITDA, a 3.6% dividend covered at 58% of nine-month free cash flow, net leverage improving to ~2.7x, and $3,990M of liquidity behind a single coverage covenant. Chicken and Prepared Foods remain solidly profitable, Pork has inflected, and the company has removed real Beef capacity ahead of an industry consolidation that analysts say is required.

Those supports are balanced by a Beef segment that is 39% of sales, guided to a $500-650M adjusted loss, now shedding volume at 15.9% year over year with no management-identified near-term remedy; falling estimates in every reporting period; a $1,169M FY2025 gap between adjusted and GAAP operating income built on charge categories that recur annually; newly certified beef purchaser classes seeking damages management has not quantified; brand carrying values the company itself says would have failed impairment testing on trailing growth; and a governance and compensation structure (controlled company with NYSE exemptions, CEO pay up 51% into a 44-point TSR shortfall) that gives outside holders little leverage.

Expected total return of roughly 8-9% to a $61 base case, against a documented path to $45, is approximately fair. Existing holders should collect the dividend and wait for the November guidance. New capital should require either evidence that Prepared Foods margin has stabilized and Beef volume declines have compressed, or a price near $48-50.
