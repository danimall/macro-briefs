## Executive Summary

V2X delivered a strong first half of fiscal 2026: revenue up 19.9% to $2,510.8M, adjusted diluted EPS up 37% to $3.16, three consecutive guidance raises. The shares responded, rising roughly 58% year-to-date and touching $93.98 intraday on the August 3 earnings release. They closed that session at $86.38 after a 10% intraday reversal, fell to $77.94 two days later, and now sit at $84.32, about 10.3% below the high. The reversal is more informative than the headline, because the raised guidance itself contains the problem: at the $4,950M revenue midpoint, implied second-half revenue is $2,439.2M, which is 2.9% *below* the first half and roughly +2.3% against second-half FY2025. That arithmetic requires no assumption; subtract H1 actual from full-year guidance. It converts a "20% growth, guidance raised" story into a business guided to approximately flat year-over-year revenue for the next six months.

Two specific dependencies sit behind that guide. The LOGCAP V Kuwait task order, disclosed in the February 2026 10-K at $441.6M (9.9% of FY2025 revenue) with option language extending to June 2030, was descoped by the Army on May 12, 2026 to a $22.6M six-month extension through December 31, 2026, cutting backlog by $414.6M. Management guided to roughly $150M of further sequential Kuwait decline in H2. Separately, MD&A discloses $200.9M of H1 revenue from "discrete activities to support a national security mission" (8.0% of first-half revenue) with no customer, contract vehicle, or duration named anywhere in the filings. Reconciling the guided sequential change (−$71.6M) against the stated Kuwait (−$150M) and T-6 (+$60M) deltas leaves a +$18.4M residual, which implies the undisclosed program must largely persist. Stripping that revenue out, Q2 organic growth was approximately 7.1% and H1 approximately 10.3%.

Margins moved the wrong way while volume grew. Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 7.15% in Q2 from 7.65%, GAAP operating margin fell 63bp, SG&A rose 35.6% in H1 against 19.9% revenue growth, and time-and-materials revenue grew 379% while firm-fixed-price revenue (the contract type where a services contractor keeps its own productivity gains) declined 0.9%. Full-year guidance embeds a 7.17% adjusted EBITDA margin versus FY2025's 7.22%. Against that, the balance sheet is genuinely improving (net debt down $71.4M year-over-year, net leverage 2.4x against a 4.75x covenant, interest coverage ~4.9x), capex is negligible at 0.13% of revenue, and underlying operating cash flow improved roughly $110M year-over-year once the receivables-facility unwind is isolated. At 13.8x guided adjusted EPS and 9.9x EV/EBITDA the stock is not expensive. It is, however, priced for the growth continuing rather than for the deceleration management has guided to, and the risk/reward from $84.32 is skewed against the buyer. **REDUCE.**

## Company Overview

V2X is a pure-play U.S. government services contractor created by the July 2022 merger of Vectrus (base operations, IT/network, logistics) and Vertex Aerospace (aircraft maintenance and modification, training, aerospace sustainment). It reports as a single segment across base and facility operations, supply chain and logistics, aircraft MRO, training and simulation, engineering, IT/network communications, and cyber. Headcount is approximately 16,200. Capital intensity is minimal: H1 FY2026 purchases of capital assets were $3.4M on $2,510.8M of revenue, and management states capex has averaged about 0.4% of revenue over three years.

The customer base is almost entirely the U.S. government, with a mix rotation in H1 FY2026: Army revenue fell 4.2% to $862.2M (34.3% of revenue, down from 43.0%), Navy rose 8.2%, Air Force rose 85.1% to $383.0M, and "Other" rose 76.6% to $507.4M. Geographically, the U.S. grew 32.9% to 64.0% of revenue while the Middle East was flat at $638.9M (25.4%). The company is 95.7% prime contractor by revenue, with subcontractor revenue down 12.7%; being prime on more work generally improves margin capture and relationship durability.

The competitive moat is incumbency and scale on long-duration, geographically distributed operations-and-maintenance contracts where the customer's switching cost is operational disruption at a forward base or flight line. It is not intellectual property. Management is layering an AI positioning on top: three internal enterprise platforms and more than $8B of submitted bids incorporating AI solutions, per a third-party summary of the Q2 call rather than a filing disclosure. That claim should be treated as unverified positioning, not as a demonstrated revenue driver.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2022 $2,891M (Vertex from July 5, 2022), FY2023 $3,963M, FY2024 $4,320M (+9.0%), FY2025 $4,478M (+3.7%). FY2026 H1 was $2,510.8M (+19.9%), with Q2 at $1,256.6M (+16.5%). The reacceleration is real; its composition and durability are the question. Excluding the $200.9M of discrete national-security work (assuming, as MD&A framing implies, no comparable prior-year revenue), H1 growth was approximately 10.3% and Q2 approximately 7.1%. Adding the T-6 program's ~$40M H1 contribution, awarded after H1 FY2025, trims Q2 organic growth toward roughly 4.8%, which is an ordinary government-services rate.

**Contract mix and margin.** The mix shift is the most important operating fact in the quarter:

| Contract type | H1 FY2026 | H1 FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost-plus / cost-reimbursable | $1,487.6M | $1,270.7M | +17.1% |
| Firm-fixed-price | $762.4M | $769.2M | −0.9% |
| Time-and-materials | $260.7M | $54.4M | +379.1% |

The $206.3M H1 increase in T&M closely matches the $200.9M of discrete mission revenue, which suggests but does not establish that the undisclosed work is booked as T&M; the filings do not link them, and the T&M growth could equally reflect unrelated task orders. Either way, growth came entirely from structures where margin is capped. Adjusted EBITDA margin declined 50bp in Q2 to 7.15% and 15bp in H1 to 6.99%. GAAP operating margin fell 63bp in Q2 to 4.28%. Guidance at the $355M EBITDA midpoint on $4,950M implies 7.17% for the full year against FY2025's 7.22%, and requires H2 margin of 7.36% (a 37bp sequential improvement) while the higher-volume Kuwait work rolls off.

**Earnings quality.** H1 GAAP diluted EPS was $1.41 versus adjusted $3.16, a 124% gap. The bridge: $1.12 of acquired-intangible amortization (defensible, non-cash, and shrinking, with intangibles amortized to $194.2M from $239.8M), $0.51 of M&A/integration costs, $0.12 of debt-related items. The M&A/integration line tripled to $21.1M in H1 from $6.4M, nearly four years after the merger closed: 0.84% of revenue and about 12% of H1 adjusted EBITDA. The cash-flow reconciliation adds back only $9.4M of M&A/integration *payments*, down from $10.8M. The two figures are likely differently scoped but do not reconcile from the disclosures. If even half the $21.1M is a recurring cost of doing business, adjusted EPS is overstated by roughly $0.30-0.50 annualized.

Estimate-at-completion adjustments added $19.2M in FY2025 and $24.8M in FY2024, about 9.9% of FY2025 operating income and declining. Legitimate under percentage-of-completion accounting, but a judgment-driven component of reported profit.

**Cash flow.** This requires care because V2X sells receivables under a MUFG Master Accounts Receivable Purchase Agreement, with proceeds landing in operating cash flow:

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 FY2025 | H1 FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reported operating cash flow | $254.2M | $182.0M | −$66.9M | −$108.4M |
| MARPA contribution | +$146.2M | +$57.8M | +$9.0M | −$142.9M |
| Derived OCF ex-MARPA | $108.0M | $124.2M | −$75.9M | +$34.5M |

The headline H1 outflow of $108.4M is a facility unwind, not operational deterioration; the balance of receivables sold fell from $276.7M to $133.8M. Ex-MARPA, operating cash flow improved roughly $110M year-over-year; the company's own adjusted measure shows +$49.7M versus −$59.8M. The symmetric point matters equally: FY2024's $254.2M of headline OCF was 57% receivables monetization, so the true prior-year base was near $108M. FY2026 adjusted OCF guidance of $160-180M (unchanged) is a genuine step-up against the correct baseline. With capex near $11M, adjusted free cash flow approximates $149-169M, or 5.6-6.4% of market capitalization and 4.2-4.8% of enterprise value. Cash fell from $369.0M to $214.3M over H1 funding the unwind. I have not annualized the $71.8M Q2 adjusted OCF figure; H1 outflows in both years reflect annual incentive payouts (compensation and benefits liabilities fell $42.2M) and appropriations timing, and the company's full-year guide is the better anchor.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt $1,086.4M, cash $210.3M, net debt $876.1M (improved $71.4M year-over-year). Company-reported net leverage is 2.4x on TTM bank EBITDA of $365.6M, which includes $59.9M (16.4%) of "additional permitted add-backs." On stricter bases: 2.87x excluding those add-backs, 2.51x on derived TTM adjusted EBITDA of $349.3M. Covenants are 4.75x maximum net leverage and 2.00x minimum interest coverage; derived coverage is 4.91x. Headroom is wide on any definition. Debt was refinanced May 29, 2026 into an $829.4M first-lien balloon due December 2030 at a 6.18% effective rate, up from 5.82%, the fourth material credit amendment since mid-2024. Swap notional was reduced from $450M to ~$321.9M, with management declining to renew $100M of hedges, increasing floating-rate exposure.

Goodwill of $1,677.0M is 52.4% of assets and 148% of equity; goodwill plus intangibles is 58.5% of assets, leaving tangible book value at approximately −$737M. For an asset-light services business this is structurally normal, but it means the entire equity value rests on contract cash flows and makes impairment a live sensitivity if the growth outlook breaks.

## Growth Analysis

The forward picture is guided deceleration, not acceleration. At the guidance low end, implied H2 revenue is −0.8% year-over-year. Consensus reflects this: current-quarter EPS estimates were revised down 1.5% over 90 days and next-quarter down 2.2%, while current-year rose 2.9% to $6.21 and next-year rose 2.6% to $6.92. The full-year revisions are positive but modest, nowhere near the magnitude that signals a systematic underestimation of an acceleration, and they sit alongside near-term quarterly markdowns. Next-year consensus implies 11.4% EPS growth off a guided-flat-margin base, which requires either the discrete program persisting, T-6 ramping to ~$100M annualized, or successful M&A.

Backlog is mixed and the soft components grew fastest. Total backlog rose from $11,116M at December 2025 to $12,727M at July 3, 2026 despite the $414.6M Kuwait reduction, but 80.2% of that is unfunded, and the Q2 disclosure clarified that unfunded backlog includes unexercised options where exercise is judged probable. The objective measures grew more modestly: funded backlog +9.4% to $2,520M and remaining performance obligations +10.5% to $3,731M. Funded backlog covers about 6.1 months of revenue at the guided run rate. Q2 bookings were $600M for a book-to-bill of roughly 0.5x, which management attributed to award timing slipping past quarter end; trailing-twelve-month book-to-bill was ~1.4x against a 1.3-1.5x target. The company's bookings definition includes unexercised options, modifications, recompetes, and extensions, so 1.4x includes re-winning work already being performed and is not a clean net-new-growth measure.

Sector conditions are mildly supportive. [unverified — the research brief does not document specific defense budget growth by line item (intelligence, space, AUKUS) or an April 2026 executive order on fixed-price contracting.] The brief does support that V2X's firm-fixed-price revenue declined 0.9% while all growth came in cost-reimbursable and T&M, so a shift toward fixed-price vehicles would require V2X to either absorb more performance risk at ~4% GAAP operating margins or cede share to bidders willing to.

| Price / FY2027 consensus adj. EPS ($6.92) | 12.2x |
| EV / FY2026 guided adj. EBITDA ($355M mid) | 9.9x |

Two data notes. The profile's forward P/E of 29.09 duplicates the trailing figure and is an artifact; the economically meaningful forward multiple on guided adjusted EPS is 13.8x. And the peer table supplied is a poor comparison set: two negative-earnings space names (LUNR, FLY), a housewares/defense hybrid (NPK), and one relevant services comp in Parsons at 32.7x GAAP. V2X at ~29x GAAP sits modestly below PSN, but PSN carries materially higher margins. On the more relevant enterprise basis, 9.9x forward EV/EBITDA sits within the typical 9-12x band for large government-services contractors; I could not obtain a computed current peer EV/EBITDA table, so this is a general sector observation rather than a verified comparison.

The honest read is that V2X is fairly priced, not mispriced. The forward multiple discount to higher-margin peers is explained by the 7.1% EBITDA margin, the 2.4-2.9x leverage, and negative tangible book value. The gap between 13.8x adjusted and 29x GAAP is entirely intangible amortization plus M&A/integration charges; how much of the latter is truly non-recurring determines how much of the adjusted multiple to trust, and the fact that the line tripled four years post-merger while cash payments fell argues for skepticism.

What supports the price: a 5.6-6.4% adjusted free cash flow yield, and roughly $150M of annual debt paydown that transfers about 5.7% of market capitalization per year from creditors to equity holders if enterprise value holds. Those are real. What does not support it: management's own guide to flat H2 revenue and flat full-year margins, growth concentrated in the lowest-margin contract structures, and an 8%-of-revenue dependency with no disclosed duration.

Analyst targets have been raised post-Q2: mean $89.09, median $90.00, high $102, low $69, against Baird's $94 maintained alongside an August 4 downgrade to Neutral. That mean implies 5.7% upside. This conflicts with the research brief's assertion that the stock trades above nearly all published targets; that reflected targets located through May 2026, and I use the more current August 10 data. Recommendation mix has softened over three months from 2 strong buy / 5 buy / 4 hold / 1 sell to 1 / 4 / 6 / 1.

**Scenarios.**
- **Base $90 (+6.7%):** 13.0x FY2027 consensus $6.92, cross-checked at 10x EV/EBITDA on ~$370M FY2027 EBITDA less ~$800M net debt = ~$92/share. Assumes Kuwait successor at reduced scope, discrete program continuing at roughly the H1 run rate, margins near 7.2-7.3%.
- **Bull $105 (+24.5%):** Kuwait extended, discrete work persisting and growing, T-6 at ~$100M annualized, margin reaching 7.5%+ for FY2027 adjusted EPS near $7.25 at 14.5x; or an accretive tuck-in that raises mix.
- **Bear $63 (−25.3%):** Kuwait not extended past December 2026, discrete program rolls off, margins stay at 7.0%, FY2027 adjusted EPS $5.50-5.75 at 11x. This is roughly the April 2026 price level, well above the $49.59 52-week low.

Probability-weighting 20/55/25 gives an expected return of approximately +2.3%, below any reasonable cost of equity for a levered contractor with concentrated, partly undisclosed revenue.

## Competitive Landscape

V2X competes against Amentum, KBR, Leidos, Jacobs, Parsons, and the services arms of the primes, plus a long tail of mid-tier and small-business bidders benefiting from set-asides. Sector economics are set by the customer: cost-reimbursable and T&M structures cap margin, recompetes reset pricing, and the government can descope or terminate for convenience. The Kuwait sequence is the textbook case: a 10-K contemplating extension to June 2030, then a six-month descoped extension three months later.

Trajectory is genuinely mixed. Improving: Air Force revenue +85%, "Other" +77%, prime share at 95.7% with subcontract revenue down, roughly $1B of recent multiyear awards described as margin-accretive, and the T-6 win ramping. Deteriorating: Army revenue −4.2%, Kuwait descoped, firm-fixed-price revenue flat to down, and adjusted EBITDA margin compressing while revenue grew 20%. Growth won at capped margins is share gain of lower quality than the headline suggests.

The multi-year record is unflattering. The March 2026 proxy shows cumulative TSR indexed from FY2021 year-end reaching $109.71 for V2X against $220.22 for its peer group, roughly +10% versus +120% over four years, with the gap widening each year. That derating was company-specific, not sector-wide, and the causes Goldman cited when initiating Sell in December 2024 (low margins, high leverage versus peers) have narrowed on leverage but not on margin. FY2026 guidance concedes flat margins. The 2026 rally is a recovery from that derating plus the removal of a 14.5% sponsor overhang, and neither is a forward earnings driver.

## Risk Assessment

**Kuwait post-December 2026 (dated, unguided).** The task order expires December 31, 2026 at $22.6M of remaining value. Nothing in the filings addresses what follows. Kuwait revenue was $182.4M in H1 alone. A non-extension removes roughly $300M+ of annualized revenue with no offsetting disclosure.

**Undisclosed concentration.** The discrete national-security program is comparable in scale to Kuwait at its peak, with customer, vehicle, and duration unknown. The H2 guide most plausibly assumes continuation. No screen captures a concentration with no name.

**Recompete concentration.** The Q2 10-Q added or expanded language noting several large contracts each generating more than $150M of annual revenue. At 4.3% GAAP operating margin, losing one on price is a material earnings event.

**Middle East and Iran.** The Middle East is 25.4% of H1 revenue and flat year-over-year, and the Q2 10-Q added language on monitoring impacts from the conflict in Iran. This exposure is two-sided. Escalation could increase support demand through LOGCAP-type surge work, or it could force posture changes, personnel risk, or descoping. Management guided Middle East revenue flat to down for the year.

**Goodwill impairment.** $1,677.0M against $1,134.5M of equity and $2,642M of market capitalization. No impairment taken; a sustained break in the growth outlook puts it in play, and there is no asset cushion behind the debt.

**Earnings-quality items.** EAC adjustments at ~9.9% of FY2025 operating income and declining; a $21.1M M&A/integration add-back that does not reconcile to $9.4M of cash payments; $59.9M of permitted add-backs inside covenant EBITDA.

**Rates and refinancing cadence.** Effective rate rose to 6.18% from 5.82%, swap notional was cut by ~$128M, and the company has amended or refinanced four times since mid-2024. Maturity risk is remote (2030 balloon), but floating exposure increased.

**Governance gaps.** Four Item 5.02 filings outside annual-meeting cycles in ten months (August 14 and November 6 and 13, 2025; January 8, 2026) remain unidentified. Form 4 activity confirms CFO, GC, CHRO and other officers in seat through May 2026, so no finance-function vacancy is implied, but the pattern is unexplained.

**Contingencies.** Greenland drew $4.3M on a letter of credit on May 28, 2026 over contested prior-year tax claims, recorded as a receivable pending appeal (~0.09% of TTM revenue), but cash has left the facility. A prior cybersecurity incident involving data exfiltration was disclosed with insurance expected to cover most costs and no material financial impact quantified.

### Options Market Signal

The 10-day tenor prices a ±7.0% move and carries a 9.4% put/call skew described as elevated put demand, while the 38-day and 101-day tenors show skew of +0.2% and −1.8% respectively. The signal corroborates near-term two-sided risk around the Kuwait decision and Q3 print rather than confirming either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($105, +24.5%).** V2X is a deleveraging, asset-light cash generator trading at 12.2x next-year consensus and 9.9x EV/EBITDA with a 5.6-6.4% adjusted free cash flow yield. Capex at 0.13% of revenue means EBITDA converts to cash at a high rate; roughly $150M of annual debt paydown accretes about 5.7% of market cap per year to equity. Underlying operating cash flow improved ~$110M year-over-year ex-MARPA, net leverage is heading to management's ~2.0x target, and covenant headroom is wide at 4.9x coverage. Three of the last four quarters beat, guidance was raised twice in 2026, funded backlog is up 9.4% and RPO up 10.5%, and the Air Force and "Other" customer lines grew 85% and 77%. The sponsor overhang is fully cleared with no dilution to existing holders. Consensus mean and median targets sit above spot at $89-90 with a $102 high. Add roughly $1B of recent multiyear awards described as margin-accretive plus an evident M&A pipeline, and FY2027 adjusted EPS near $7.25 at 14.5x supports $105.

**Bear case ($63, −25.3%).** The growth is narrower and lower-quality than the headline. Ex-discrete organic growth was ~7% in Q2 and closer to ~4.8% excluding T-6. Guidance itself implies H2 revenue 2.9% below H1 and flat versus prior year. Growth came entirely from cost-reimbursable and T&M work; firm-fixed-price revenue, where productivity gains are retained, declined. Adjusted EBITDA margin fell 50bp in Q2, SG&A rose 35.6% against 19.9% revenue growth, and full-year guidance embeds margin *below* FY2025. Kuwait has been descoped and expires December 31, 2026 with nothing disclosed about a successor; an unnamed program worth 8% of H1 revenue appears necessary for the H2 guide to hold. Adjusted EPS adds back roughly $1.00 per year of M&A/integration cost four years after the merger, and GAAP EPS supports a 29x multiple. Goodwill is 148% of equity with tangible book at −$737M. Analyst sentiment is softening, two firms have downgraded on valuation during 2026, and the four-year TSR record versus peers (+10% vs +120%) shows the market has repeatedly priced this business at a discount for reasons that guidance concedes are unresolved. FY2027 adjusted EPS of $5.50-5.75 at 11x is $63.

**Resolution.** The bull case rests on cash flow and deleveraging, which are verifiable and real. The bear case rests on guided deceleration, margin mix, and two concentration exposures (one dated and one undisclosed) which are equally verifiable. From $84.32, base-case upside of roughly 7% against 25% downside, and a probability-weighted expected return near +2.3%, does not compensate for the resolution risk. That is a REDUCE, not an AVOID: the business is solvent, cash-generative, delevering, and covered by a $69 low target that is 18% below spot rather than 50%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength on any move back toward the $90-94 area (the consensus median-to-Baird-target zone). Existing holders with a low cost basis may retain a partial position, since the FCF yield and deleveraging provide genuine support, but position size should be reduced ahead of two unguided resolutions: the Kuwait successor decision by December 31, 2026, and the Q3 FY2026 report (early November) which will show whether the guided H2 sequential decline is landing as modeled. A reasonable structure is selling one-third to one-half of the position now and reassessing in November.

**Targets and math from $84.32.** Base $90 (+6.7%) at 13.0x FY2027 consensus $6.92, corroborated by 10x EV/EBITDA on ~$370M less ~$800M net debt. Bull $105 (+24.5%). Bear $63 (−25.3%). Timeframe 6-9 months, anchored to the Q3 print and the December Kuwait expiry.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.**
1. Disclosure of the discrete national-security program's contract vehicle and duration, or evidence in the Q3 10-Q that revenue held at or above the ~$100M quarterly run rate with a named multi-year vehicle.
2. A Kuwait successor award or follow-on task order announced before December 31, 2026 at $200M+ annualized value.
3. Adjusted EBITDA margin reaching 7.4%+ in Q3 *with* firm-fixed-price revenue growing year-over-year, indicating margin expansion is coming from mix rather than volume.
4. Price toward $70-73, which would put the shares at roughly 10.5x FY2027 consensus and an 7%+ adjusted FCF yield, restoring the asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. FY2026 revenue guidance cut, or Q3 revenue below approximately $1,190M (the implied quarterly run rate at the guidance midpoint).
2. Kuwait allowed to lapse after December 31, 2026 with no disclosed replacement, or a second $150M+ contract descoped or lost on recompete.
3. A leveraged acquisition that takes net leverage above 3.5x on company-defined bank EBITDA, reversing the deleveraging thesis that underpins the current multiple.
4. Any goodwill impairment charge, or adjusted EBITDA margin falling below 6.8% for a full quarter.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 FY2026 results, expected early November 2026, is the primary checkpoint; the Kuwait expiry on December 31, 2026 is the secondary one. Both fall within the horizon of this call.

## Conclusion

V2X is a competently run, cash-generative government services business that has done the two things it needed to do since the 2022 merger: delever and reaccelerate revenue. The market has paid for both, re-rating the shares roughly 58% year-to-date. What the market has not yet fully absorbed is what management's own guidance says about the next six months: revenue 2.9% below the first half, roughly flat year-over-year, at an adjusted EBITDA margin below FY2025. Growth came from time-and-materials and cost-reimbursable structures where margin is capped, while firm-fixed-price revenue declined. An 8%-of-revenue program with no disclosed customer or end date appears necessary for the second-half guide to hold, and the largest named contract has been descoped to a $22.6M stub expiring December 31, 2026 with no successor disclosed.

At 13.8x guided adjusted EPS, 9.9x EV/EBITDA and a 5.6-6.4% adjusted free cash flow yield, the stock is fairly valued rather than mispriced in either direction. Base case $90 offers 6.7%; the bear case at $63 costs 25%. Analyst sentiment is softening, two firms have downgraded on valuation this year, near-dated implied volatility is rich in backwardation, and the earnings-day reversal from $93.98 to $86.38 suggests other investors read the guidance the same way. Adjusted earnings that add back roughly $1.00 per share of M&A/integration cost four years post-merger, goodwill at 148% of equity, and negative tangible book value of $737M all argue for demanding a wider margin of safety than $84.32 provides.

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Trim into strength toward $90-94; revisit constructively at $70-73, on disclosure of the discrete program's duration, or on a Kuwait successor award.
