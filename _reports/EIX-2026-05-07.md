## Executive Summary

Edison International, the parent of Southern California Edison (SCE), presents a bifurcated investment case. The underlying regulated utility is executing well: core EPS grew 33% to $6.55 in FY2025, the 2025 General Rate Case authorizes $9.7B in base revenue escalating to $11.2B by 2028, and the service territory benefits from California's aggressive electrification mandates. At ~11.2x FY2026E core EPS of $6.12, the stock trades at roughly half the peer median P/E of 22.7x, offering a 5.1% dividend yield with 6% annual growth.

The discount is earned. SCE acknowledged its equipment "most likely caused" the January 2025 Eaton Fire that killed 19 people, destroyed over 6,000 structures in affluent Altadena, and generated approximately 20,000 individual plaintiffs. Neither management nor the auditor (PwC) can estimate the range of potential losses. Total liability could range from the currently accrued $1.1B to potentially $10B+ in a severe scenario. The bellwether jury trial is scheduled for January 2027. Until the magnitude of this liability becomes quantifiable, the valuation discount cannot be arbitraged with confidence.

The correct posture is to hold existing positions for the yield and rate base growth while monitoring legal developments, but new capital deployment requires either: (a) a price decline to $55-58 that more fully compensates for tail risk, or (b) meaningful legal resolution that bounds the liability range.

## Company Overview

Edison International is a utility holding company whose principal subsidiary, Southern California Edison, is one of the largest investor-owned electric utilities in the United States. SCE serves approximately 5.4 million customers across a 50,000-square-mile service territory spanning Southern, Central, and Coastal California.

SCE operates under a regulated model where revenue is decoupled from volumetric sales and instead determined by the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) and Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC). The authorized CPUC return on equity is 10.03% on a capital structure of 52% equity, 43% debt, and 5% preferred. The FERC-authorized ROE is 10.3%.

The company generates approximately 73% of its delivered electricity from carbon-free sources and is a central enabler of California's mandate for 100% clean electricity by 2045. SCE's capital investment program runs approximately $6.5B annually, focused on grid hardening, wildfire mitigation, battery storage, and electrification infrastructure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Core Earnings**

FY2025 revenue reached $19.3B (+10% YoY), driven by rate case mechanisms and wildfire cost recovery authorizations. The GAAP EPS of $11.55 includes a $2.94B one-time gain from CPUC approval of TKM and Woolsey fire settlement recoveries, making it unreliable as a valuation input. Core EPS of $6.55 represents the operational earning power and grew 33% from $4.93 in FY2024.

Current-year (FY2026) consensus core EPS is $6.12, essentially flat with limited revisions over 90 days (-0.6%). Next-year (FY2027) consensus sits at $6.51, implying roughly 6% growth aligned with rate base expansion.

**Cash Flow and Capital Structure**

Operating cash flow of $5.8B is robust, but annual capex of $6.5B produces negative free cash flow of -$715M. This is structural for a utility in an investment-intensive cycle — SCE requires continuous access to capital markets, issuing $4.6B in new long-term debt in 2025 alone.

Total debt of $38.5B against equity of $17.6B produces a debt-to-capitalization ratio of 0.69, approaching the 0.70 covenant ceiling. SCE's ratio is 0.57 against a 0.65 covenant limit. These ratios leave limited headroom for additional borrowing if Eaton Fire liabilities materially exceed insurance and fund recoveries.

**Dividend**

The $3.51/share annual dividend (raised 6% in February 2026) yields 5.1% at the current price. The target payout ratio of 45-55% of SCE core earnings provides reasonable coverage. SCE paid $2.22B in common dividends upstream to Edison International, against EIX's $1.27B in shareholder dividends — the pass-through math works unless SCE's earnings are impaired by liability charges.

**Earnings Beat Record**

Edison has beaten EPS estimates in all four reported quarters, with surprises ranging from 5% to 26.3% (Q4 2025). This pattern suggests conservative guidance and some upside to consensus estimates in normal operating conditions.

## Growth Analysis

SCE's rate base growth trajectory supports mid-to-high single-digit earnings growth:

- 2025 GRC authorizes base revenue escalation from $9.7B to $11.2B by 2028 (approximately 5% annual growth)
- California's electrification mandates (EV charging infrastructure, building electrification) expand the addressable load
- Battery storage deployment creates new rate base investment opportunities
- Grid hardening and wildfire mitigation spending is large and growing

Against this: CPUC denied 50% of SCE's Transportation Electrification Grid Readiness program funding on affordability grounds, signaling political limits on rate increases. Customer departures to Community Choice Aggregators (21% of bundled load) create incremental headwinds. SCE's rates are already among the highest nationally.

Estimate revisions are slightly negative in the near term (current quarter down 14.2% over 90 days), though full-year estimates are essentially stable. This near-term softness appears related to timing of cost recovery mechanisms rather than fundamental deterioration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $68.94, Edison trades at:
- 7.5x trailing GAAP EPS ($11.55) — meaningless due to one-time wildfire recoveries
- **11.2x FY2026E core EPS ($6.12)** — the relevant metric
- ~10.6x FY2027E core EPS ($6.51)
- 5.1% dividend yield

Peer medians: P/E 22.7x, dividend yield 3.4%. Edison's valuation discount is approximately 50% to peer median P/E. For context:
- FirstEnergy (FE): 24.7x
- Eversource (ES): 15.0x
- PPL: 23.2x
- Evergy (EVRG): 22.1x

The discount reflects wildfire liability uncertainty. If Eaton Fire liability is ultimately contained below $5B (net of fund recoveries and insurance), Edison's valuation should re-rate toward 15-17x core EPS, implying $92-104 per share. If liability is catastrophic (PG&E precedent), the equity is at risk of significant dilution.

Analyst consensus targets range from $62 (bear case) to $86, with a median of $77.50 — implying 12.4% upside from current levels. The distribution of analyst ratings (7 Buy/Strong Buy, 7 Hold, 3 Sell) reflects the divided market view on liability resolution.

The valuation offers genuine asymmetry if you believe the liability is containable under the AB 1054/SB 254 framework, but "cheap because it might blow up" is a valid market assessment given the $0 to $10B+ range of possible outcomes.

## Competitive Landscape

SCE's competitive position within its regulated territory is structurally protected — it holds a franchise monopoly for distribution and transmission services. The relevant "competition" is regulatory:

**Strengths:**
- Exclusive service territory with growing load (electrification tailwind)
- 10.03% authorized ROE exceeds cost of equity, creating value for shareholders when earned
- Scale advantages in grid modernization investment
- Regulatory relationships developed over decades
- AB 1054 wildfire fund provides structural protection absent from pre-2019 framework

**Vulnerabilities:**
- 21% load departure to Community Choice Aggregators reduces bundled customer base
- Customer-owned generation (rooftop solar) compresses volumetric throughput
- Political pressure on rates limits pricing flexibility
- 27% of service area in high-fire-risk zones creates recurring exposure
- PG&E's bankruptcy precedent demonstrates utility equity can be destroyed by wildfire liability

The addition of former Energy Secretary Jennifer Granholm to the board signals an effort to strengthen political relationships and navigate the regulatory environment more effectively.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Eaton Fire Liability (CRITICAL — unquantifiable)**

This is the dominant risk and the reason the stock cannot be rated BUY despite apparent cheapness. Key parameters:
- 20,000 plaintiffs, ~1,500 lawsuits
- 19 wrongful death claims (require negligence proof)
- Thousands of property claims in Altadena (median home value likely $1.5-2M+)
- SCE admits causation; strict liability applies to property damage
- Only $1.1B accrued against potential billions in total exposure
- PwC flagged as Critical Audit Matter — auditor cannot estimate range
- January 2027 bellwether trial will provide first signal on damages quantum
- LA District Attorney criminal investigation adds governance uncertainty

**Scenario analysis:**
- Best case: AB 1054 fund covers most liability, SCE's net cost contained below $3B → stock re-rates to $85-95
- Base case: Net cost of $4-6B absorbed over multiple years, dilutive equity raise possible → stock trades $65-75
- Worst case: PG&E analog, $15B+ in losses overwhelm fund, severe equity dilution → stock trades $35-50

**2. Capital Structure Stress**

Debt-to-cap at 0.69 against a 0.70 covenant ceiling leaves very limited additional debt capacity before covenant breach. If Eaton Fire costs require significant capital deployment before insurance/fund recoveries are received, the company may face a forced equity raise at distressed valuations.

**3. Rate Recovery/Regulatory Risk**

CPUC's denial of 50% of transportation electrification funding demonstrates political limits. Rising customer rates amid California's cost-of-living pressures create structural resistance to full cost recovery.

**4. Recurring Wildfire Exposure**

With 27% of the service territory in high-fire-risk zones and intensifying climate conditions, future wildfire events are virtually certain. Each creates another potential multi-billion-dollar liability exposure.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($85-95, +23-38% upside):**
- Eaton Fire liability contained within AB 1054/SB 254 framework at $3-4B net cost
- Rate base growth of 7-8% annually drives core EPS toward $7.00+ by FY2028
- Valuation re-rates from 11x to 15-16x as litigation uncertainty resolves
- Wildfire catastrophe reform legislation (endorsed by CEO in April 2026) further de-risks the California utility model
- 5.1% dividend yield provides income floor during the wait

**Bear Case ($48-55, -20-30% downside):**
- Eaton Fire liability exceeds fund capacity/proves materially larger than market expects
- CPUC finds imprudence, eliminating presumption of prudency and triggering $4.3B+ obligation
- Criminal investigation produces charges against executives
- Forced equity raise at $50-55 to fund liabilities, diluting shareholders 15-20%
- Rating agency downgrades increase borrowing costs, impairing future earnings

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Hold Position:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Eaton Fire liability range becomes estimable at below $5B total net cost — could come from bellwether trial outcome (January 2027) or comprehensive settlement framework
2. Price decline to $55-58 range, creating sufficient margin of safety (~9x FY2026E core EPS) to compensate for liability tail risk
3. Legislative reform (comprehensive catastrophe framework) that materially caps utility wildfire exposure

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Evidence that total Eaton Fire liability will exceed $8B net of insurance/fund recoveries
2. CPUC imprudence finding or DA criminal charges filed
3. Dividend cut or suspension signaling balance sheet stress
4. Debt-to-capitalization breaches 0.68 (approaching covenant)

**Reassessment timeline:** January 2027 bellwether trial is the key catalyst. Interim checkpoints: Q2 2026 earnings (July), any settlement framework announcements, CPUC Wildfire Fund adequacy determinations. The stock's binary nature makes it unsuitable for investors who cannot tolerate 20-30% downside while waiting 8-12 months for resolution.

## Conclusion

Edison International is a fundamentally sound regulated utility trading at a deep discount to peers due to an unquantifiable wildfire liability. The 5.1% yield and mid-single-digit core earnings growth provide reasonable compensation for patient holders, but the Eaton Fire's open-ended liability range — acknowledged as unknowable by both management and auditors — prevents conviction in either direction.

The market is pricing approximately a 50% probability-weighted haircut relative to peers, which is not unreasonable given the range of outcomes. Existing holders should maintain positions for the yield and optionality on a favorable resolution. New buyers should demand a wider margin of safety ($55-58 entry) or wait for the January 2027 trial to provide the first calibration point on damages quantum. At the current price of $68.94, the risk/reward is balanced but not compelling enough to warrant deployment of fresh capital.
