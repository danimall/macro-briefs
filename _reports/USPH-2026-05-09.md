## Executive Summary

U.S. Physical Therapy operates 783 outpatient physical therapy clinics across 44 states through a distinctive limited partnership model that retains founding therapists as minority partners. The stock has fallen ~36% from its October 2025 high of $93.50 to $59.29 following a Q1 2026 earnings miss that revealed accelerating labor cost pressure and margin compression. Revenue grew 7.9% YoY in Q1, but physical therapy segment margins compressed from 16.6% to 15.8%, and non-GAAP EPS of $0.46 missed consensus by 17%.

The investment case hinges on whether two 10-year hospital alliances (announced February 2026) and a new $450M credit facility can reignite growth and margin recovery in H2 2026. Management reaffirmed $102-106M adjusted EBITDA guidance for 2026, implying significant back-half acceleration. At ~10.5x forward EV/EBITDA with 6.8% FCF yield and 3.1% dividend yield, valuation is reasonable for a healthcare services compounder — but the CFO departure, rising leverage, and three consecutive quarterly earnings misses warrant caution before adding exposure.

The 36% drawdown from highs is partially explained by genuine fundamental deterioration (margin compression, CFO exit) rather than pure sentiment. The analyst consensus target range of $88-108 implies 49-82% upside, but this appears to lag the Q1 operational concerns. We hold pending evidence that hospital alliances are translating into improved revenue per visit and margin stabilization in Q2-Q3 2026.

## Company Overview

USPH operates through two segments: Physical Therapy Operations (~85% of revenue) providing outpatient PT/OT services across 783 clinics, and Industrial Injury Prevention Services (~15%) offering onsite workplace injury prevention through its Briotix Health subsidiary (~92% owned). Revenue sources are diversified across managed care (48.5%), Medicare/Medicaid (35.8%), workers' compensation (10.2%), and other payors.

The partnership model is the defining structural feature. USPH acquires 50-80% equity interests in multi-clinic practices, retaining founding therapists as minority partners who manage daily operations. This creates strong alignment but also generates a $313M redeemable non-controlling interest liability — a contingent obligation that distorts GAAP EPS and represents material future cash requirements.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Growth has been strong, driven by acquisitions and volume expansion. Revenue grew from $553M (2022) to $781M (2025), a 12.2% CAGR. Q1 2026 revenue of $198.3M represented 7.9% YoY growth, decelerating from 2025's 16.3% pace as the acquisition cadence slowed (49 clinics added in 2025 vs. 96 in 2024).

**Margins — the critical deterioration:** Physical therapy segment gross margin compressed from 19.2% (full year 2025) to 15.8% (Q1 2026). The mechanism is clear: adjusted operating cost per visit rose to $90.31 (from $86.15 for full year 2025, a 4.8% increase), while net revenue per visit grew only 0.8% ($105.76 to $106.49). Labor costs are the primary driver — adjusted salaries per visit jumped from $61.93 (2025) to $64.20 (Q1 2026). The company is experiencing the broader PT industry's therapist labor shortage reflected in wage inflation that reimbursement rates cannot offset.

**Cash Flow:** FCF of $61M in 2025 ($75M OCF less $14M capex) represents a 6.8% yield at current market cap. This is healthy for a services business, though rising debt ($161.8M at year-end 2025 to $203.9M in March 2026) funded by acquisition activity will increase interest expense.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $203.9M against the new $450M facility. Goodwill at $716M represents 58% of total assets — reflecting the premium paid for acquired practices. Altman Z-Score of 2.1 places the company in the "grey zone," driven by the goodwill-heavy, low-tangible-asset nature of the business rather than imminent distress. The $313M redeemable NCI is the more relevant liability concern.

**GAAP EPS distortion:** Reported Q1 2026 GAAP EPS was -$0.12 despite positive net income of $5M, because a $9.4M increase in the estimated value of minority partners' put rights flows through EPS. This creates a perverse dynamic where better operational performance increases the revaluation charge. The trailing P/E of 117x is essentially meaningless; the business should be evaluated on non-GAAP operating results ($2.63/share in 2025) or EV/EBITDA.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term catalysts:**
1. Hospital alliances beginning contribution in May 2026 — the Metro alliance (60 clinics into a NY hospital system) is expected to contribute ~$6M incremental EBITDA to USPH, with a second alliance adding ~$1.3M. Combined, this represents 7-8% EBITDA growth from these two initiatives alone.
2. Medicare reimbursement increased +1.75% for 2026 — the first rate increase in years after cumulative cuts of -10.9% over 2021-2025.
3. Same-store revenue inflected to +2.5% in Q1 2026 (from +0.1% in full year 2025).

**Growth concerns:**
1. Acquisition pace decelerating (49 clinics in 2025 vs. 96 in 2024) — though the $450M facility signals continued M&A intent.
2. Organic visit growth was strong (+14.9% in 2025) but comes with wage inflation that compresses margins.
3. Current-year EPS estimates have been revised down 8.8% over 90 days, signaling deteriorating near-term earnings expectations.

Management's 2026 EBITDA guidance of $102-106M implies 7-12% growth over 2025's $95M. At the midpoint ($104M), this requires significant improvement from Q1's implied run-rate (~$22M adjusted EBITDA in Q1, or $88M annualized), suggesting H2 must deliver ~$28M per quarter — a 27% step-up from Q1. The hospital alliances and seasonal visit patterns provide a plausible path, but execution risk is elevated.

- P/Non-GAAP Operating Results: $59.29 / $2.63 = 22.5x trailing; on 2026E of $2.73 = 21.7x
- FCF Yield: ~6.8%

The analyst consensus median target of $99 implies ~13x forward EBITDA — plausible in an optimistic scenario but requires material operational improvement from Q1 levels.

The 36% drawdown from highs is partially justified by fundamental deterioration. At current levels, downside is more limited given the dividend yield and reasonable EV/EBITDA, but upside requires proof of margin recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

USPH's partnership model provides meaningful competitive differentiation. By retaining founding therapists as equity owners, the company preserves the local referral relationships and entrepreneurial motivation that drive clinic performance. No public competitor has replicated this structure at scale. ATI Physical Therapy recently restructured from near-bankruptcy, and Select Medical operates through a corporate-employed model with lower margins.

The hospital alliance strategy adds a new competitive dimension. Embedding 60+ clinics within a hospital system's brand creates patient capture at the point of referral — a significant barrier to competitor entry. If USPH can replicate this model across other health systems nationally, it could accelerate organic growth without acquisition capital.

Industry headwinds are uniform: therapist labor shortages, Medicare reimbursement pressure, and rising operational complexity. USPH's scale (783 clinics) provides some insulation through centralized recruiting, ERP systems, and payor contracting leverage, but the Q1 margin data shows the company is not immune to these pressures.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Margin compression trajectory:** Q1's cost-per-visit increase of 4.8% YoY vs. revenue-per-visit growth of 0.8% is unsustainable. If labor cost inflation persists at this rate without offsetting revenue improvements, 2026 EBITDA guidance becomes unreachable.

**2. Redeemable NCI liability ($313M):** This is larger than total debt and represents a perpetual drag on per-share economics. When the business performs well, the revaluation increases, reducing EPS. When partners retire and exercise puts, cash outflows strain the balance sheet. Simultaneous exercise by multiple partners would require significant borrowing.

**3. CFO departure at critical juncture:** The loss of long-serving CFO Carey Hendrickson during simultaneous ERP implementation, $450M facility deployment, and complex hospital alliance accounting introduces execution risk. Interim CFO Jason Curtis may lack institutional depth.

**4. Acquisition integration risk:** $716M in goodwill reflects cumulative acquisition premiums. The Metro acquisition ($76.5M for 50 clinics) in October 2024 was the largest in history. Integration of 145 clinics over 2024-2025 while implementing new technology creates operational strain.

**5. Medicare policy risk:** 35.8% of revenue depends on Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement. Despite the +1.75% 2026 increase, ongoing sequestration (2% cap through 2027) and potential legislative cuts remain structural risks.

**6. Leverage increasing:** Debt rose from $162M to $204M in one quarter. The $450M facility signals intent to continue deploying debt capital for acquisitions.

## Conclusion

USPH presents a differentiated business model with proven long-term compounding ability, reasonable valuation, and a potentially transformative hospital alliance catalyst — counterbalanced by accelerating cost pressure, CFO vacancy, rising leverage, and a uniquely complex capital structure that confuses the market. The consensus target range of $88-108 appears to insufficiently account for the Q1 margin trajectory.

At ~10.5x forward EV/EBITDA, the stock is not expensive enough to avoid, but three consecutive quarterly earnings misses and the margin compression trend do not provide sufficient confidence for an entry recommendation. We hold pending Q2 2026 results as the first meaningful evidence window for hospital alliance execution and margin stabilization.
