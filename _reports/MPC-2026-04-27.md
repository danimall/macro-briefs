## Executive Summary

Marathon Petroleum is the largest independent refiner in the United States with 3.0 million barrels per calendar day of refining capacity across 13 facilities. The company benefits from two reinforcing macro forces: a widening US-global crude price divergence that mechanically expands refining margins, and a midstream growth engine (MPLX LP) delivering $2.5B+ in annual fee-based distributions. Q4 2025 demonstrated the earnings leverage — adjusted EPS of $4.07 beat consensus by 40%, driven by $18.65/barrel refining margins and access to discounted Venezuelan heavy crude.

The balance sheet has deteriorated materially over three years as MPC repurchased $24.1B in stock while MPLX added $6.5B in new debt for acquisitions. Consolidated debt-to-equity has risen from 0.92x to 3.46x, and interest expense surged 52% in 2025. Shares outstanding declined 41% since 2022, but this limits future flexibility if crack spreads compress. At ~10x consensus FY2026 earnings with rising estimate revisions (+67% over 90 days for the current fiscal year), MPC offers reasonable value for a refiner entering what could be a multi-quarter margin expansion driven by geopolitical supply disruption.

The stock has appreciated 63% over the past year and trades 12% below its 52-week high of $255.77. The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (45.4% IV vs. 30.3% HV), and analyst consensus targets ($247 mean) sit only modestly above the current price. The risk/reward favors ownership given the structural tailwinds, but position sizing should reflect the inherent cyclicality of refining margins and the balance sheet's reduced cushion.

## Company Overview

Marathon Petroleum operates through three segments:

**Refining & Marketing** (~$6.1B adjusted EBITDA in 2025): 13 refineries processing a 55/45 sweet/sour crude split, achieving 94% crude utilization. The segment's profitability is extraordinarily sensitive to crack spreads — each $1/barrel change in blended 3-2-1 crack spread translates to approximately $1.125B in annual adjusted EBITDA. Gulf Coast facilities with coking capacity can process discounted heavy crude (Venezuelan, Canadian), providing a margin advantage over simpler refineries.

**Midstream — MPLX LP** (~$6.75B adjusted EBITDA in 2025): MPC owns approximately 647 million MPLX common units (market value ~$34.6B at year-end 2025). MPLX gathers, transports, and stores crude oil, refined products, natural gas, and NGLs, with a majority of volumes serving MPC's refining operations through long-term fee-based agreements with minimum volume commitments. MPLX completed $3.3B in acquisitions in 2025 (Northwind $2.4B, BANGL $703M, Whiptail $235M) while divesting non-core Rockies assets for $980M, building out a Permian-to-Gulf Coast NGL value chain.

**Renewable Diesel** (–$110M adjusted EBITDA in 2025): Operates the Dickinson, ND facility and holds a JV interest in Martinez Renewables in California. This segment has been consistently unprofitable, with margins compressing from $304M (2023) to $151M (2025).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue has declined 25% from the 2022 peak ($177.5B to $132.7B) as refined product prices normalized from post-invasion levels. However, operating income rebounded 22% in 2025 ($6.8B to $8.3B) on higher crack spreads ($14.89 blended 3-2-1 vs. $14.03 in 2024) and improving operational efficiency. Net income to MPC rose to $4.0B ($13.22 diluted EPS), up from $3.4B ($10.08 EPS) in 2024.

The Q4 2025 quarter was exceptional: refining margins reached $18.65/barrel at 95% crude utilization, driven by access to discounted Venezuelan heavy crude following sanctions changes. Adjusted EPS of $4.07 beat the $2.91 consensus by 40%.

Operating cash flow of $8.3B and free cash flow of approximately $4.8B represent solid conversion, though FCF yield relative to the current $67B market cap is approximately 7.2% — adequate but not exceptional for a cyclical refiner.

### Balance Sheet Deterioration

The most concerning trend is the rapid leverage increase:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|--------|------|------|------|------|
| Long-term Debt | $25.6B | $25.3B | $24.4B | $30.5B |
| Stockholders' Equity | $27.7B | $24.4B | $17.7B | $17.3B |
| D/E Ratio | 0.92x | 1.04x | 1.38x | 3.46x |
| Interest Expense | — | — | $839M | $1.276B |

Consolidated D/E has nearly quadrupled in three years through two simultaneous forces: $24.1B in cumulative buybacks eroding equity, and $6.5B in MPLX debt issuance for acquisitions. The Altman Z-Score of 2.44 sits in the grey zone.

Two mitigating factors deserve emphasis. First, MPC and MPLX maintain separate credit profiles — MPLX creditors have no recourse to MPC's general credit. Second, both entities carry BBB/Baa2 stable investment-grade ratings, and MPC's liquidity is $6.6B ($1.5B cash plus $5.1B revolving credit). The financial structure is strained but not distressed. A downgrade below investment grade would, however, trigger higher crude procurement costs through unsecured purchasing restrictions, creating a nonlinear risk.

### Capital Return Program

MPC has authorized $60.05B in total buybacks since 2012 and repurchased $55.67B, leaving $4.38B remaining. The 2025 buyback pace slowed meaningfully ($3.4B vs. $9.1B in 2024) as the average repurchase price rose from $131 to $164. With the stock now above $220, further buyback activity at these levels will deliver diminishing per-share accretion. The quarterly dividend of $0.91/share ($3.64 annualized) yields approximately 1.6% at the current price.

Total shareholder returns (buybacks plus dividends) declined from $12.9B in 2023 to $10.3B in 2024 to $4.5B in 2025, reflecting both slower buyback activity and the reallocation of MPLX capital toward acquisitions.

### Structural Growth Drivers

**US-Global Crude Price Divergence:** US domestic crude prices are structurally lower than international benchmarks during periods of supply disruption. MPC's Gulf Coast refineries process domestic and discounted heavy crude (WCS, Venezuelan) while selling refined products priced off globally elevated benchmarks. This margin advantage is mechanical and persists as long as the divergence holds.

**MPLX Expansion:** The $2.7B 2026 capital program focuses on Permian-to-Gulf Coast NGL infrastructure. Midstream adjusted EBITDA grew 3.1% in 2025 and ~6% in 2024, with distributions to MPC increasing from $2.27B to $2.56B. This fee-based income stream provides earnings stability independent of crack spread cycles.

**Capacity Rationalization:** Valero is ceasing operations at its Benicia, CA refinery by April 2026, removing capacity from the West Coast market and tightening product supply in a region where MPC operates its Los Angeles refinery. This is a direct competitive tailwind.

**Venezuelan Heavy Crude Access:** Sanctions changes have given US Gulf Coast coking refiners access to heavily discounted Venezuelan crude. MPC's complex refinery configurations can process this crude at margins above lighter, sweeter alternatives.

### Counter-Factors

FY2027 consensus of $20.07 represents a 9% decline from FY2026's $22.10, implying the market expects some margin normalization. The renewable diesel segment (-$110M EBITDA) is a drag with no visibility on improvement.

## Competitive Landscape

MPC holds the #1 position among independent US refiners by capacity (3.0M bpd), ahead of Valero (~3.2M bpd including non-US) and Phillips 66 (~2.2M bpd). Three structural competitive advantages stand out:

**Refinery Complexity:** MPC's Gulf Coast facilities feature high Nelson Complexity Indices with extensive coking and hydrocracking capacity, enabling them to process discounted heavy/sour crude feedstocks. This complexity advantage widens during periods when light-heavy and sweet-sour crude differentials expand — precisely the current environment.

**Integrated Midstream:** The MPLX relationship provides captive logistics infrastructure at favorable economics while generating $2.5B+ in annual distributions. No other independent refiner has a comparable owned midstream platform at this scale.

**Geographic Diversification:** The 13-refinery footprint across Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent, and West Coast regions reduces exposure to any single regional market's supply/demand dynamics. West Coast capacity rationalization improves MPC's competitive position in that region specifically.

The competitive position is improving on the margin: rising utilization, access to discounted feedstocks, and competitor rationalization all point to share gains in refined product supply.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**Crack Spread Cyclicality (HIGH):** Each $1/barrel change in blended crack spread translates to ~$1.125B in annual EBITDA. If geopolitical tensions de-escalate and global product supply normalizes, margins could compress to $12-14/barrel, reducing FY2026 EPS by $5-8 versus current estimates.

**Balance Sheet Rigidity (MODERATE-HIGH):** $2.25B in near-term maturities creates refinancing risk if credit conditions tighten. The separate MPC/MPLX credit structures provide some insulation, but a rating downgrade at either entity would cascade through funding costs.

**California Regulatory Overhang (MODERATE):** SB X1-2 authorizes maximum gross gasoline refining margins with penalties, and AB X2-1 mandates minimum inventory requirements. The CEC voted in August 2025 to defer action for at least 5 years, but the statutory framework exists and could be activated with political changes.

**RIN Compliance Cost Escalation (MODERATE):** $1.33B in 2025, up 24% YoY. Small Refinery Exemption policy is politically volatile.

**Renewable Diesel Losses (LOW-MODERATE):** Negative $110M EBITDA with no improvement trajectory. If margins don't recover, this is a write-down candidate.

**Labor Relations (MODERATE):** Approximately 700 California employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements that expired January 31, 2026 and are currently operating under continued agreements subject to 24-hour termination notice by either party. An additional ~3,100 unionized employees have agreements expiring 2027-2031.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($275-$300)

Sustained elevated global product prices while US domestic crude remains discounted, widening crack spreads to $18-22/barrel through summer 2026. MPLX distributions grow to $2.8B+ as Permian NGL infrastructure ramps. Venezuelan heavy crude access persists, adding incremental margin versus pre-sanctions levels. FY2026 EPS reaches $24-26, and the stock re-rates toward 12x forward earnings. Remaining buyback authorization ($4.4B) provides price support.

### Bear Case ($160-$175)

Geopolitical de-escalation normalizes global product prices. Crack spreads compress to $11-13/barrel as global refining capacity additions (Middle East, Asia) come online. MPLX acquisition integration faces execution challenges. California activates margin caps. FY2026 EPS comes in at $14-16, and the stock trades at 10-11x, yielding $155-175. The $30.5B debt load becomes a more pressing concern in a low-margin environment.

2. S&P or Moody's places MPC or MPLX on negative credit watch or downgrades below BBB-/Baa3.
3. FY2026 consensus EPS falls below $18 (currently $22.10), indicating the market has priced out the margin expansion.

## Conclusion

Marathon Petroleum is well-positioned for a multi-quarter margin expansion driven by the US-global crude price divergence and access to discounted heavy feedstocks. The MPLX midstream platform provides an earnings floor through $2.5B+ in annual distributions and is itself a growth vehicle via Permian NGL infrastructure buildout. At ~10x forward earnings with sharply rising estimate revisions, the valuation compensates for the cyclical risk.

The deteriorating balance sheet (3.46x D/E, 52% interest expense growth) is the primary concern. This leverage was created by buybacks that generated substantial per-share value but reduced the company's margin of safety in a downturn. The business can service this debt at current margin levels, but severe crack spread compression would test financial flexibility.

Maintaining a BUY recommendation with a base case target of $265. Position sizing should reflect refining cyclicality — 2-3% of portfolio is appropriate, with willingness to add on pullbacks toward $200 where the FCF yield becomes more compelling.
