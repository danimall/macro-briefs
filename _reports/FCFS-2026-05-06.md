## Executive Summary

FirstCash Holdings operates 3,330+ pawn stores across the U.S., Mexico, and U.K., plus a retail POS payment solutions business (AFF). The company reported record Q1 2026 results on April 23: revenue again exceeded $1 billion in a single quarter (+26% YoY), adjusted EPS of $2.69 beat consensus by 14.3%, and management raised revenue guidance across all pawn segments. Same-store pawn receivables grew 19% in the U.S., 30% in Latin America, and 29% in the U.K., extending the streak to 11 consecutive quarters of double-digit domestic growth.

The business benefits from a convergence of macro tailwinds: record gold prices (75% of collateral is jewelry, driving $112M of scrap revenue in Q1 alone vs. $43M prior year), persistent inflation pushing consumers toward pawn liquidity, reduced remittances to Mexico that management has cited as boosting LatAm demand, and a "deep value" retail model with minimal exposure to tariff disruption on supply chains. At ~20.8x FY2026E EPS of $10.90 with consensus calling for 14% growth into FY2027, the stock trades at a premium to Consumer Finance peers, but those peers (Ally, Bread, SLM) are structurally different businesses with unsecured credit exposure and rate sensitivity that FCFS lacks entirely.

The primary risks are gold price reversal, AFF segment deterioration, and the stock's 67% appreciation over the trailing year limiting near-term upside relative to downside. The risk/reward favors ownership given the growth trajectory, though position sizing should reflect the stock's proximity to its 52-week high.

## Company Overview

FirstCash is the dominant global pawn operator with four reporting segments:

- **U.S. Pawn (1,207 stores):** Core business across 29 states, heavily concentrated in Texas (490 stores) and Sun Belt markets (80% of footprint). Generated record 29% pre-tax segment operating margins in Q1 2026.
- **Latin America Pawn (1,838 stores):** Primarily Mexico (1,732), with expansion into Guatemala, El Salvador, and Colombia. Benefiting from reduced U.S.-to-Mexico remittance flows.
- **U.K. Pawn (289 stores):** Acquired H&T Group in August 2025 for ~$500M total ($392M cash + $108M assumed debt). Already delivering 39% pre-tax segment operating margins.
- **AFF/Retail POS (~16,600 merchant locations):** Lease-to-own and retail finance products for sub-prime consumers. Declining 15-20% in FY2026 but being managed as a cash flow contributor rather than growth driver.

The pawn model is fundamentally differentiated from consumer finance: loans are non-recourse (no credit check, no obligation to repay), collateralized by physical assets, and generate revenue through both fees (4-25% monthly) and retail sales of forfeited merchandise. There are zero credit losses on the pawn portfolio because the collateral covers the loan.

## Growth Analysis

**Pawn growth drivers (structural, not cyclical):**

1. **Gold price leverage:** 75% of pawn collateral is jewelry. Record gold prices enable larger loan sizes and higher scrap margins. Q1 2026 wholesale scrap jewelry sales were $112M with a gross profit of approximately $36M (roughly 32% gross margin). Scrap revenue trajectory: $132M (FY2024) → $263M (FY2025) → $112M (Q1 2026 alone, implying $400M+ FY2026 annualized). While not costless, gold scrap provides a meaningful earnings tailwind at attractive margins when prices are elevated.

2. **Consumer stress demand:** Elevated inflation and depleted excess savings push more consumers toward non-bank lending. Pawn demand is counter-cyclical — it accelerates in economic stress.

3. **LatAm remittance tailwind:** Management has cited reduced U.S.-to-Mexico remittances from immigration enforcement as a contributor to strong pawn demand in Latin America, and Bloomberg has reported that Trump immigration policies are pressuring Mexico remittance flows. The Q1 10-Q attributes LatAm pawn receivable growth primarily to increased loan counts and larger loan sizes, driven in part by a higher mix of higher-value jewelry loans. This dynamic contributed to 30% LatAm same-store growth in Q1 2026.

4. **U.K. expansion:** H&T's 289 stores at 39% segment margins (above expectations) with technology integration pending. Platform migration should yield operating synergies and product expansion capability.

5. **Store count growth:** Organic new store openings + acquisition pipeline in fragmented markets (12,000-14,000 U.S. stores, mostly independents).

**Same-store pawn receivable growth has run at double digits for 11 consecutive quarters with April 2026 at "over 20%."** This provides 12+ months of revenue visibility given the 30-day average loan term and resulting fee accrual cadence.

**AFF headwind is a drag but manageable relative to pawn strength.** The segment represents only ~15% of consolidated net revenue (down from higher levels) and is expected to decline 15-20% in FY2026. Credit metrics deserve attention: lease delinquency rose to 24.3% from 22.6%, finance-receivable delinquency rose to 20.5% from 19.3%, and the finance-receivable provision rate increased to 29.5% from 25.7% in Q1 2026. Management is pivoting toward services verticals (elective medical now 18% of volumes vs. 7% in 2023) and off-balance sheet products. Gross transaction volume did grow to $242M from $236M YoY, suggesting the underlying merchant base remains healthy even as revenue recognition patterns shift.

**Consensus estimates:** FY2026E EPS $10.90 (+24% YoY), FY2027E $12.41 (+14% YoY). Estimate revisions are positive across all periods (90-day revision: +8.1% for current year, +6.1% for next year, per market data providers). The company has beaten EPS estimates for 4 consecutive quarters with an average beat of 9.1%.

- 20.8x FY2026E EPS ($10.90)
- 18.3x FY2027E EPS ($12.41)
- ~16x EV/adjusted EBITDA (TTM)
- Company-defined adjusted FCF yield: approximately 2.7% ($267M TTM / ~$9.9B market cap)
- PEG ratio: ~0.87x (20.8x P/E / 24% EPS growth)

The Consumer Finance peer median P/E of 10.7x (Ally at 10.7x, BFH at 7.0x, SLM at 6.5x) reflects structurally different businesses with unsecured credit exposure, rate sensitivity, and cyclical earnings. FCFS has no credit losses on its core pawn portfolio, counter-cyclical demand characteristics, and 20%+ EPS growth vs. peers growing mid-single-digits at best. The direct pawn peer EZPW trades at 20.5x — essentially the same multiple — confirming the sector premium is market-wide for pawn operators.

The stock is near its 52-week high ($227.26), having appreciated 67% from its low of $119.21. This appreciation is fundamentally driven: Q1 EPS grew 30%, guidance was raised, and estimates have risen 8-9% in 90 days. The re-rating reflects earnings delivery, not multiple expansion. On FY2027E of $12.41, the stock trades at 18.3x — reasonable for a 14%+ grower with counter-cyclical characteristics.

**Where is the market potentially wrong?** The consensus FY2027 estimate of $12.41 may underestimate gold-driven scrap revenue sustainability and U.K. synergy capture. If gold remains near current levels and same-store growth sustains at 15%+, FY2027 EPS could reach $13.00-13.50, implying 17-17.5x forward P/E at current prices.

**Valuation concern that prevents a higher score:** At 28x trailing P/E and near all-time-high prices, the stock requires continued execution to sustain the multiple. A gold price correction from current levels to $3,500 would compress scrap revenue meaningfully, and the stock's premium to Consumer Finance peers leaves limited margin of safety if growth decelerates. Additionally, company-defined free cash flow yield of ~2.7% is less compelling than operating cash flow metrics might suggest, reflecting the capital-intensive nature of growing the pawn loan portfolio.

## Competitive Landscape

FCFS operates the largest pawn network globally by a wide margin. With only one other U.S.-listed pawn operator (EZPW at $2B market cap with ~1,200 stores) and a fragmented independent landscape, FCFS benefits from:

- **Scale advantages:** Proprietary technology platform (loan origination, inventory management, pricing algorithms), centralized purchasing, and brand recognition across 3,330+ locations.
- **Acquisition capability:** Access to capital markets (just completed $750M bond offering) enables continued industry consolidation at attractive multiples.
- **Geographic diversification:** Three distinct markets (U.S., LatAm, U.K.) with different economic cycles provide earnings stability.
- **Minimal tariff exposure:** Pre-owned merchandise inventory is primarily sourced from pawn forfeitures and customer purchases domestically — significantly less exposure to import tariffs than new goods retailers, though the company itself acknowledges trade policy as a factor that could affect consumer spending and demand.

The competitive position is improving: H&T integration extends dominance into a third major market, same-store growth indicates wallet share gains, and the current macro environment (inflation + consumer stress + immigration policy + gold) creates a multi-year favorable operating environment.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Gold Price Reversal (High Impact, Moderate Probability):** If gold corrects 20-25% from current levels, scrap jewelry revenue could decline substantially. Based on Q1 2026 scrap gross profit of ~$36M (on $112M revenue less ~$77M cost), a correction to $3,500/oz would create an estimated $0.80–$1.00/share EPS headwind based on assumed volume and margin compression, though this estimate relies on simplifying assumptions about forfeiture rates, scrap volumes, and margin pass-through that the company does not directly disclose. Loan sizes would also compress, reducing fee income. Current gold levels are elevated on structural de-dollarization and geopolitical demand, but prices this high are historically anomalous and reversion risk exists.

**2. AFF Credit Deterioration:** Lease delinquency rates rose to 24.3% (from 22.6%) and finance-receivable delinquency to 20.5% (from 19.3%), with the finance-receivable provision rate increasing to 29.5% from 25.7%. The new OBS Loan product ($33M outstanding at March 31, 2026, $13.8M loss liability accrued) functions as a credit-risk transfer with recourse — FirstCash must reimburse the bank for principal plus accrued interest when an OBS loan becomes 90+ days past due. While small today, if this product scales rapidly, the contingent exposure could become meaningful. If the single bank partner (Utah charter, contract through August 2028) exits, the product becomes non-viable.

**3. Concentration Risk:** Texas represents 490 of 1,207 U.S. stores (41%). Mexico represents 1,732 of 3,334 total stores (52%). Currency, regulatory, and political risk in Mexico is meaningful — each 1-point change in the peso rate impacts EPS by approximately $0.10-0.12.

**4. Regulatory Risk:** CFPB enforcement is currently suspended but courts could reinstate authority. State-level 36% APR caps, if adopted broadly, would materially impair AFF economics. Mexico's PROFECO has enforcement authority over pawn operations.

**5. Valuation / Mean Reversion:** The stock at $227 near its all-time high with 28x trailing P/E is priced for continued execution. Any quarter where same-store growth decelerates below low-double-digits or gold corrects would likely trigger a 10-15% de-rating.

## Conclusion

FirstCash Holdings is a structurally advantaged business experiencing a rare convergence of macro tailwinds that management has positioned well to capture. The 11-quarter streak of double-digit same-store growth, record Q1 results, raised guidance, and 4-for-4 earnings beats provide strong fundamental support for the stock's appreciation. At 20.8x FY2026E with a PEG of 0.87x, the valuation is reasonable for the growth delivered, though not cheap in absolute terms.

The BUY recommendation rests on: (1) structural pawn demand tailwinds persisting through H2 2026 and beyond (inflation, consumer stress, immigration policy, gold), (2) continued earnings estimate revisions higher (consensus still below company's demonstrated trajectory), (3) cash flow generation supporting both deleveraging and shareholder returns, and (4) competitive position that improves with scale in a fragmented industry. Position sizing should be moderate (2%) given proximity to all-time highs and gold dependency, with willingness to add on any 10-15% pullback driven by gold volatility rather than fundamental deterioration.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrected company-defined FCF yield (~2.7% vs. originally stated ~5.4%) and consolidated GAAP net margin (~9.0% vs. originally stated ~19.8%) materially weaken the valuation support for the recommendation; the thesis now depends more heavily on continued earnings growth and multiple sustainability than on current cash-flow cheapness.
