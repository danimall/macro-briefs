**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Boyd Gaming is a geographically diversified regional casino operator with 27 properties across 11 states, a clean balance sheet following the July 2025 sale of its FanDuel stake, and an aggressive capital-return program. The stock trades at $86.58, up 17% over the trailing year and roughly 4% below its 52-week high of $89.96. On headline metrics the company looks extraordinarily cheap (a trailing P/E near 3.8x and a 45% net margin) but both figures are artifacts of a one-time $1.748 billion pre-tax gain on the FanDuel disposal. On underlying operations the picture is materially weaker: FY25 operating income fell 19.3% to $748 million, Adjusted EBITDAR declined to $1,353.8 million from $1,390.6 million, and Q1 2026 operating income dropped 18% year-over-year with EBITDAR down 6%.

The forward valuation is the legitimate version of the cheapness argument. At a consensus FY26 EPS of $7.20, the stock trades at roughly 12x forward earnings, well below the Casinos & Gaming peer median of 18.4x. That discount is real but partly earned: consensus estimates have been revised down across every horizon (current year –3.5% over 90 days, next year –4.0%), the most recent quarter missed by 8.5%, and EPS growth is being manufactured almost entirely through buybacks while operating EBITDAR shrinks. The FanDuel transaction traded a high-margin, growing income stream for one-time cash redeployed into share repurchases, and management has explicitly warned the associated market-access margin compression will worsen with a full year of impact in 2026.

The bull case has shifted from organic momentum to financial engineering plus takeover optionality. With the Boyd family controlling roughly 30% and a deleveraged balance sheet, BYD periodically surfaces as a consolidation candidate, and the stock rose about 5% on June 1 on Fertitta/Caesars M&A speculation. We rate this a HOLD: the forward valuation discount and credible M&A option value provide downside support, but there is no organic growth catalyst, estimate momentum is negative, and the operating trajectory outside the Midwest & South segment is deteriorating. We do not see a confident 12-month appreciation thesis, nor evidence of the fundamental impairment or catalyzed downside that would justify an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1975 and headquartered in Las Vegas, Boyd Gaming runs four reportable segments:

- **Midwest & South** — the EBITDA backbone, with FY25 revenue of $2,116.6 million and EBITDAR of $777.7 million. This is the company's growth engine and the segment carrying consolidated results.
- **Las Vegas Locals** — eight properties (Gold Coast, Orleans, Suncoast, Sam's Town), FY25 revenue $890 million, EBITDAR $420.5 million.
- **Downtown Las Vegas** — California/Fremont/Main Street, revenue $228.7 million, EBITDAR $80.5 million, with an unusual concentration: roughly 50–57% of room nights come from Hawaiian visitors.
- **Online** — revenue of $708.3 million but EBITDAR of only $63.1 million, distorted by zero-margin online tax reimbursements that pass through the top line.

The business is slot-centric and cash-based, with gaming generating roughly 64% of revenue. The competitive differentiation is regional density, gaming licenses, and the Boyd Rewards loyalty program rather than pricing power or destination scale. The portfolio is supplemented by Boyd Interactive (B2B/B2C online), online sports-betting market-access agreements (now repriced), and a management contract for Sky River Casino in California.

## Financial Analysis

The reported FY25 results require restatement to be interpretable. Net income of $1.84 billion and a 44.9% net margin reflect the pre-tax gain on the July 31, 2025 sale of Boyd's 5% FanDuel equity stake for $1.758 billion cash. The trailing P/E of 3.8x and any ROE figure derived from that net income number are meaningless for forward analysis.

Stripping out the gain, the operating trend is one of mild deterioration:

- **Operating income fell 19.3%** to $748 million (FY25) from $928 million (FY24).
- **Adjusted EBITDAR declined** to $1,353.8 million from $1,390.6 million.
- Reported revenue grew 4.1% to $4.09 billion, but $125.7 million was zero-margin online tax reimbursements; gaming revenue rose only 2.1%.
- FY25 absorbed $128.4 million of impairment charges (Las Vegas Locals and Midwest & South long-lived assets), up sharply from $10.5 million.

Q1 2026 confirmed the softening. Revenue rose 0.6% to $997.4 million, but operating income fell 18% to $164 million and Adjusted EBITDAR fell 6% to $317.4 million. Segment detail:

- Las Vegas Locals EBITDAR –6.2%, hit by destination softness since H2 2025 and Suncoast construction disruption.
- Downtown Las Vegas –9.7%.
- Online EBITDAR collapsed to $8.4 million from $23.3 million, the direct consequence of the FanDuel-related market-access repricing.
- Midwest & South +5.1%, the lone bright spot, carried by Treasure Chest's new land-based casino.

Q1 EPS rose to $1.37 from $1.31 solely because share count fell from ~93 million (2024) to ~74 million (April 2026). Operating performance moved the other direction.

On cash flow and the balance sheet, FY25 operating cash flow was $976.7 million against capex of $588 million, for free cash flow of roughly $388 million. The Altman Z-Score of 1.33 ("distress zone") is misleading: it reflects the structurally leveraged, asset-heavy casino model with a negative-working-capital design (current ratio 0.54 by intent), not genuine solvency risk. Boyd used FanDuel proceeds to cut credit-facility debt; total debt ended 2025 near $2.06 billion, rising to roughly $2.3 billion by Q1 2026 as buybacks and capex resumed. Two senior notes anchor the structure: $1 billion at 4.75% due December 2027 and $900 million at 4.75% due 2031. A $113.8 million per year triple-net GLPI master lease (extended to April 2031) is a senior, inflexible claim on cash flow. The January 2026 credit agreement ($1.45 billion revolver plus $1.2 billion delayed-draw Term A) provides ample liquidity (~$2.24 billion availability) and the company is covenant-compliant.

The financial health picture is solid but not exceptional: strong cash generation and manageable leverage for the business model, offset by a 2027 refinancing item, a heavy development capex cycle, and poor headline earnings quality once the one-time gain is removed.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is negative outside one segment. Adjusted EBITDAR declined in both FY25 and Q1 2026, and three of four segments shrank in the most recent quarter. The Online segment faces a structural step-down: as part of the FanDuel sale, Boyd terminated or repriced near-100%-margin market-access agreements. The lost revenue (~$56.5 million in 2025, ~$15.1 million in Q1 2026) flows almost entirely to EBITDAR, and management has explicitly stated this margin compression "will continue and further dilute" with a full year of impact. Online EBITDAR has roughly halved.

The estimate-revision data corroborates negative momentum. Consensus has fallen across every horizon: current quarter –4.1% over 90 days, current year –3.5%, next year –4.0%. This is the opposite of the revision velocity that signals an earnings trough. The Q1 2026 miss broke a three-quarter beat streak and reduces confidence in forward visibility.

The growth that does exist is development-driven and back-end-loaded: the $750 million Norfolk, Virginia resort (full opening late 2027, ~$300 million of 2026 capex), the Treasure Chest land-based expansion, and Cadence Crossing (opened March 2026). Total 2026 capex of $650–700 million pressures near-term free cash flow without delivering near-term EBITDAR. Portfolio pruning (Sam's Town Tunica closed, Sam's Town Shreveport sale expected Q3 2026, Eastside Cannery imploded) is sensible but subtractive in the near term.

The regional gaming customer is exposed to the same low-end-consumer pressure visible across discretionary categories. Management's own commentary on "softness in destination business" aligns with a broader squeeze on lower-income households. We do not see a credible path to organic EBITDAR growth in the next 12 months outside Midwest & South stabilization.

## Valuation Assessment

The forward valuation is the strongest part of the bull case. At consensus FY26 EPS of $7.20 and a current price of $86.58, the stock trades at roughly 12x forward earnings. The peer set:

| **Peer median** | **18.4** | **0.5%** | — |

BYD's ~12x forward multiple sits roughly a third below the peer median. The discount is partly justified — falling estimates, a high-margin income stream lost to the FanDuel sale, and EPS growth that depends on buybacks rather than operations. But the discount also reflects a market that has not credited the deleveraged balance sheet or the embedded takeover optionality. Analyst price targets (low $81, median $91, high $110, mean $93.73) bracket the current price with modest upside skew; the median implies roughly 5% upside, the high target roughly 27%.

We resist treating this as a deep-value buy. The cycle-adjustment logic that would justify a high valuation score — depressed trailing earnings about to inflect — does not apply here. Trailing earnings are inflated by a one-time gain, and forward estimates are falling, not rising. The appropriate framing is a quality regional operator at a defensible but earned discount, with the valuation providing downside support rather than asymmetric upside. A 6.5 valuation score reflects genuine multiple support against negative estimate momentum.

The capital structure also flatters per-share metrics: a ~20% reduction in share count over two years means continued buybacks (program raised to $3.3 billion authorization, plus $0.20 quarterly dividend) can hold EPS roughly flat even as EBITDAR erodes modestly. This supports the stock near current levels but is not a value-creation engine.

## Competitive Landscape

Regional gaming is mature and intensely competitive: tribal casinos, new-market entrants, iGaming, VLTs, and state lotteries all compete for the same discretionary dollar. Boyd competes on regional density and loyalty rather than destination scale, which gives it a defensible but narrow moat. The trajectory is roughly stable-to-modestly-eroding: Midwest & South is gaining share via new capacity, but the Las Vegas Locals and Downtown segments are contracting, and the Online segment lost its most profitable revenue line in the FanDuel restructuring.

The Downtown Las Vegas Hawaiian-visitor concentration is a structural single-market dependency tied to air capacity and fares. The Midwest & South segment, while the growth engine, is also where the Oct 1, 2025 goodwill and intangible fair-value tests "did not significantly exceed carrying value" — a competitive position strong enough to carry the company today but vulnerable to a regional consumer slowdown that could trigger writedowns against $958 million of goodwill and $1.4 billion of intangibles.

The consolidation theme is genuine. M&A activity in gaming (Fertitta/Caesars speculation) and Boyd's clean post-FanDuel balance sheet plus 30% family control make it a recurring takeout candidate. Texas Capital initiated a Buy in June 2026 citing multiple catalysts. This optionality supports the equity through scarcity value and consolidation appetite, but it is external to operating fundamentals.

## Risk Assessment

**Online/market-access cliff.** The most under-appreciated company-specific risk. The repriced market-access fees were near-100% margin, so the loss flows almost entirely to EBITDAR, and management has guided that the dilution will worsen through 2026.

**Discretionary-spending cyclicality.** The regional gaming customer is exposed to a weakening low-end consumer. Management already cites destination-business softness, consistent with broader pressure on lower-income households.

**Impairment risk.** Midwest & South intangibles and goodwill were close to carrying value at the October 2025 test. A regional slowdown could trigger non-cash writedowns.

**Refinancing.** The $1 billion notes due December 2027 carry a springing maturity (September 1, 2027) on the new facility if not refinanced. Manageable given liquidity, but a near-term to-do.

**Master-lease obligation.** The GLPI triple-net rent is a senior, inflexible claim on free cash flow through April 2031.

**Capex intensity.** 2026 capex compresses near-term free cash flow before the Norfolk resort opens in late 2027.

**Hawaiian concentration** at Downtown Las Vegas adds a single-market demand dependency.

These risks are largely known and priced, which is why the risk score sits mid-range rather than low. The impairment and online-margin items are the two that could deliver downside surprises not fully reflected in consensus.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-constructive and does not flag elevated downside fear. Near-term ATM IV is 31.4% versus 1-year realized of 27.2% — modestly rich (4.2pp spread) but unremarkable. The put/call skew is balanced at 1.5%, the put/call OI ratio is a call-tilted 0.40, and the put/call volume ratio is just 0.09. The term structure is flat (near 31.4% vs far 30.0%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term binary. Since mid-May the P/C OI ratio collapsed from 2.75 to 0.40 while the price rose 7.4% and IV fell 15.9pp, a meaningful unwind of bearish/hedging positioning as the stock recovered toward its highs. This is consistent with the M&A-speculation bid and does not contradict a HOLD: the options market is not pricing material near-term stress, and the implied move (±4.1% nearest expiry) is contained.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($95–110)
- Forward P/E re-rates as the FanDuel-related margin reset annualizes and comparisons ease into late 2026.
- Buybacks against the $3.3 billion authorization shrink the share count further, holding EPS up even with flat EBITDAR; the dividend provides a modest yield floor.
- M&A optionality crystallizes: consolidation appetite and the deleveraged balance sheet make BYD a credible takeout at a premium to the standalone multiple.
- Midwest & South momentum (Treasure Chest, Norfolk) reaccelerates consolidated EBITDAR into 2027.
- Bull target ~$110 corresponds to roughly 15x FY26 consensus EPS plus takeover premium.

### Bear Case ($72–78)
- Online-margin compression deepens as guided, and the low-end consumer slowdown drags Las Vegas Locals and Downtown EBITDAR further.
- A regional softening triggers a goodwill/intangible impairment in Midwest & South, denting reported earnings and confidence.
- Estimate revisions continue lower; the Q1 miss proves the start of a trend rather than a one-off.
- Buyback-driven EPS support proves insufficient against operating erosion, and the stock de-rates toward the trough of its 52-week range.
- Bear target ~$73–78, roughly 10–11x a downward-revised FY26 EPS, near the 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month HOLD with an `uncertain` rationale. The valuation discount and takeover optionality support the stock near current levels, but negative estimate momentum and deteriorating core EBITDAR prevent a confident appreciation thesis. We are not relying on dividend yield (0.9% is immaterial); the hold balances a real valuation cushion against a real lack of organic catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing or rising consolidated Adjusted EBITDAR, confirming the online-margin reset has annualized and core regional demand has bottomed.
- Positive consensus EPS revisions resuming (reversal of the current –3.5% to –4.0% trend) alongside a return to the prior beat cadence.
- A concrete, disclosed M&A development (formal approach, board process) that crystallizes the takeout option at a premium.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill or intangible impairment charge in Midwest & South, confirming the segment carrying the company is itself weakening.
- Adjusted EBITDAR declines accelerating beyond the current mid-single-digit pace, indicating the consumer slowdown is broadening across the portfolio.
- Estimate revisions deepening toward a $7.00 current-year consensus or lower with no offsetting buyback acceleration.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, late July) is the natural checkpoint — specifically Midwest & South EBITDAR direction and management commentary on the online-margin run-rate. The Sam's Town Shreveport sale (expected Q3 2026) and any movement on the December 2027 refinancing are secondary monitoring items.

## Conclusion

Boyd Gaming is a competently run, cash-generative regional operator with a deleveraged balance sheet, disciplined capital returns, and credible consolidation optionality. The forward valuation — ~12x consensus against an 18.4x peer median — is a genuine discount that provides downside support and is the principal reason this is not an AVOID. The operating story is moving the wrong way: core Adjusted EBITDAR is declining, a structural online-margin step-down from the FanDuel divestiture will worsen through 2026, estimates are falling across every horizon, and the most recent quarter missed. EPS growth is being manufactured through a ~20% reduction in share count rather than operations.

The investable case rests on the valuation floor plus takeover optionality, not organic momentum. That combination supports holding but does not justify a high-conviction long in a sector and consumer segment facing real cyclical headwinds. We rate BYD a HOLD with an overall score of 5.5, and would revisit toward BUY only on confirmed EBITDAR stabilization and a reversal in estimate revisions, or a concrete M&A development.
