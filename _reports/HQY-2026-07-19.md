**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HealthEquity is the largest U.S. administrator of Health Savings Accounts by account count, managing 10.6 million HSAs holding $37.1 billion in assets plus 7.2 million complementary consumer-directed benefits, for 17.8 million total accounts as of April 30, 2026. The business has delivered a multi-year earnings inflection: FY2026 net income rose 123% to $215M on 9% revenue growth to $1.31B, with adjusted EBITDA margin reaching 43% (46% in the most recent quarter). The company throws off substantial free cash flow ($455M in FY2026, a 35% margin), is deleveraging, and has expanded its buyback authorization to roughly $1.6B cumulative within a year.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the source of that inflection. Roughly 48% of revenue is custodial revenue — net interest earned on ~$17.5B of HSA cash held by insurance and bank partners. The 123% net-income jump rode a rising HSA-cash yield (3.11% → 3.53% → 3.84%), a rate-cycle tailwind rather than a purely operational achievement. The early warning is visible in the filings: the company's $3.5B-notional Treasury-bond forward hedge book flipped to a ~$46M derivative liability in Q1 FY2027, driving a $26M other-comprehensive loss (AOCI moved from -$5.7M to -$31.8M). The market is pricing lower forward rates, which pressures the largest and highest-margin revenue line into 2027-2028.

Consensus estimates have stalled in line with this shift: current-quarter and next-quarter EPS revisions are flat-to-negative over 30 and 90 days, even as full-year figures crept up ~2% on the guidance raise. At ~40x trailing and ~20.7x forward FY27 EPS ($4.72), the stock is not cheap for a business whose primary earnings driver is turning. The durable growth engine — HSA investment assets up 38% YoY to $19.6B, monetized through recurring, market-linked service fees — reduces long-term rate dependence but does not yet offset the near-term custodial risk. We rate HQY a HOLD (growth_hold) with a base case near current levels and prefer to add on weakness toward the low-$80s or on evidence the custodial book can defend yields as rates ease.

## Company Overview

HealthEquity operates a B2B2C model, reaching consumers through employers ("Clients") accessed via 200+ integrated "Network Partners" (health plans, benefits administrators, recordkeepers) and brokers. It is an IRS-approved non-bank custodian, a status that requires maintaining net worth above 2%/4% of passive/non-passive custodial funds. That capital requirement constrains some competitors and functions as a structural moat. HSA-asset market share has grown from 4% in 2010 to roughly 20% in 2025, making HQY the #2 provider by assets and #1 by account count per Devenir's HSA research.

Three revenue streams drive the P&L (FY2026):
- **Custodial revenue — $636.8M (48%, fastest-growing):** Interest earned on HSA cash held by insurance ("Enhanced Rates") and bank ("Basic Rates") partners plus Client-held funds. This is both the key value driver and the key sensitivity.
- **Service revenue — $485.0M:** Administration fees plus fees on invested HSA assets and marketplace activity.
- **Interchange revenue — $191.6M (15%):** Fees on payment-card swipes.

The competitive moat rests on IRS non-bank custodian status, high switching costs on individually-owned trust accounts, a bundled HSA+CDB single-source offering preferred by most employers, a proprietary multi-tenant cloud platform, and its service culture. Fidelity, Optum Bank (UnitedHealth), and Webster Bank are the principal competitors; Fidelity in particular is cited as gaining share on the strength of its brand and retirement-recordkeeping franchise.

Custodial revenue rose 17%, driven by average HSA-cash yield climbing from 3.11% to 3.53% and by Enhanced Rates participation rising from 49% to 58% of HSA cash. Cost of revenue fell from 35% to 30% of revenue, with service costs declining 7% on technology-driven efficiencies and lower fraud-reimbursement costs.

Q1 FY2027 (ended April 30, 2026) extended the trend: revenue +7% to $354.6M, net income +29% to $69.4M, HSA-cash yield up to 3.84%. Management raised FY2027 revenue guidance to $1.41–1.42B and added a $1.0B buyback authorization in May 2026.

Capital return is aggressive — $299M of buybacks in FY2026 and $123M in Q1 FY2027 alone — alongside debt prepayment ($100M in FY2026, $15M in Q1). Diluted share count is declining (~88.8M → ~85.0M). The balance sheet is sound: current ratio 3.27, D/E ~0.6, debt of ~$947M ($600M 4.50% fixed Senior Notes due 2029 plus ~$347M variable revolver at 5.00%). The Altman Z of 2.7 sits in the grey zone but is depressed by the goodwill/intangible base ($1.65B goodwill + ~$1.07B intangibles = ~81% of assets), reflecting the acquisition-driven history rather than distress.

The critical caveat for scoring: the FY2026/Q1 earnings surge was substantially a rate-cycle tailwind on the custodial float. Two of the margin gains are also partly non-recurring — declining fraud-reimbursement costs after a 2024-2025 account-takeover fraud spike, and lower stock comp tied to the prior CEO's forfeitures — and management has explicitly warned G&A and stock comp will normalize upward. The financial health score of 7.0 reflects genuine cash generation and a defensible balance sheet, tempered by the earnings-quality question and the impairment exposure in the asset base.

## Growth Analysis

The secular tailwind is quantified in HQY's own filings: per the 2025 KFF survey, average family health premiums are up 26% since 2020 and 53% since 2015, pushing employers and employees toward high-deductible plans and HSAs. The July 2025 "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" expanded HSA eligibility to Bronze and Catastrophic plans and broadened qualified services, enlarging the eligible population.

The healthiest growth vector is the HSA investment mix shift. HSA investment assets grew 38% YoY to $19.6B in Q1 (versus HSA cash roughly flat), as members shift cash into invested balances that generate recurring, market-linked service fees. This engine monetizes long-duration, sticky assets and gradually reduces reliance on the rate-sensitive cash book, the most important structural positive in the story.

Near-term earnings momentum, however, has stalled. Estimate revisions are flat-to-negative: current quarter -0.0% (30d) / +0.3% (90d), next quarter -0.5% / -1.0%, current year -0.3% / +2.0%, next year -0.8% / +2.0%. The full-year uptick came from the guidance raise; the forward quarters are drifting down. This is below the >30% 90-day revision velocity that flags a genuine inflection buy signal; consensus has plateaued as the rate tailwind that powered the last two years turns. The earnings-surprise record (four consecutive beats, +4.5% to +16.2%) supports confidence in near-term consensus, but a beat against a plateauing estimate is a different setup from a beat against rising estimates.

Growth prospects score 5.8: the multi-year revenue trajectory and the investment-asset engine are durable, but the largest revenue line faces a forward-rate headwind and consensus has stopped rising.

## Valuation Assessment

At $97.67, HQY trades at ~40x trailing EPS ($2.46) and ~20.7x forward FY27 consensus ($4.72). Against Managed Health Care peers, the trailing multiple sits below Molina (53.7x) and roughly at Humana (39.4x) and the peer median (39.4x), but the peer set is a poor comp — HQY is a fee-and-float financial-services business, not a risk-bearing managed-care insurer, and its economics track interest rates far more than medical loss ratios. On a forward basis, ~20.7x for a business guiding to high-single-digit revenue growth is neither cheap nor expensive; the PEG is roughly in line at ~1.5-2x depending on the earnings growth assumption.

The valuation debate turns on the durability of the custodial earnings base. If HSA-cash yields hold near 3.8% and Enhanced Rates participation continues climbing (slow repricing at ~10%/year insulates the near term), the forward multiple looks reasonable and buyback-driven EPS accretion supports upside. If forward rates decline as the hedge book's swing implies, custodial revenue compresses into 2027-2028 and the current multiple prices in an earnings level that may not persist. The market is not obviously mispricing the stock in either direction, so cheapness is not the thesis here.

Analyst targets (low $105, mean $117.4, median $115, high $135) sit above the current price, implying 7-38% upside to consensus. On an inflection stock with rising estimates these would be treated as floors, but estimates here are flat-to-falling on the forward quarters, so the targets more likely reflect optimism vulnerable to a rate-driven miss than a stale lag to reality. We do not treat proximity to targets as confirmation of upside. Valuation scores 5.2 — fairly priced, with the asymmetry not clearly favorable given the turning rate cycle.

## Competitive Landscape

HQY's competitive position is stable-to-improving. It is the scale leader in a consolidating market where many bank competitors treat HSAs as non-core, creating an acquisition pipeline (the BenefitWallet integration — 616K HSAs / $2.7B assets for $425M — is winding down, with integration expense down 88% in FY2026). The IRS non-bank custodian status is an under-appreciated moat: net-worth requirements constrain bank competitors from both organic growth and acquisitions, feeding HQY's roll-up strategy.

The offsetting pressure is well-capitalized entrants. Fidelity (brand plus retirement-recordkeeping strength) and Optum (UnitedHealth-backed) are named as gaining share and able to out-invest HQY. There is also a channel risk: some health plans have begun in-sourcing HSA offerings, which could erode the Network Partner distribution advantage. On balance, HQY's share has expanded to ~20% and its bundled single-source offering remains employer-preferred, but the trajectory is a market leader defending share against deeper-pocketed rivals rather than a challenger taking it. Competitive position scores 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest-rate sensitivity is the dominant risk.** Custodial revenue (48% of total) depends on yields, and a sustained decline compresses the largest and highest-margin line. Mitigants are real — the Enhanced Rates book reprices only ~10%/year, the HSA-cash maturity ladder locks in yields, and the company hedges with $3.5B-notional Treasury forwards. But those hedges swinging to a -$46M liability and driving a $26M AOCI loss in Q1 is a leading indicator that the market expects lower forward rates, a headwind the current valuation does not fully discount.

**Regulatory/tax dependence** is existential in the tail: substantially all revenue derives from tax-advantaged accounts, so any curtailment of HSA/CDB tax benefits would be severe. The near-term direction is favorable given the July 2025 legislation, but "Medicare for all"-style reform is a tail risk.

**Cybersecurity and fraud.** A 2024 breach has produced a consolidated putative class action (D. Utah) plus regulatory inquiries, with the company refiling a motion to compel arbitration in May 2026 and loss not yet estimable. The account-takeover fraud spike inflated service costs; the subsequent decline is a meaningful driver of recent margin improvement, so a reversal would pressure margins.

**Margin durability.** Part of the recent gains came from declining fraud costs and lower stock comp tied to the prior CEO's forfeitures — both partly non-recurring, with management flagging normalization upward.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment** given ~81% of assets are acquisition-related, and marketplace/GLP-1 exposure (a partner offers compounded GLP-1 medications, a regulatorily volatile area) round out the idiosyncratic risks. Risk factors score 5.0 — the interest-rate leading indicator plus unresolved litigation are specific, not-fully-priced catalysts, which caps this dimension.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent and offers no confirmation of near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 35.6% sits exactly at 1-year historical vol (spread ~0.0%), in the 4th percentile of the current chain, with a balanced put/call skew (-2.2%) and a very low put/call volume ratio (0.04). Term structure is in normal contango (near-term 35.6% < long-term 42.4%), and IV has fallen 2.1pp since mid-June while the stock rose 10.8%. The market is pricing an implied ±8.8% move near-term with no elevated put demand, consistent with a stock the market views as fairly valued rather than at a binary juncture.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($120-135):** HSA-cash yields hold near 3.8%, Enhanced Rates participation keeps climbing (slow repricing insulates the book even as short rates ease), and the HSA investment-asset engine (+38% YoY) continues compounding recurring fee revenue. The $1.6B buyback authorization shrinks the share count meaningfully, driving double-digit EPS growth on high-single-digit revenue. The July 2025 legislation expands the addressable population, and the roll-up strategy (constrained-competitor acquisitions) adds accounts. On ~$5.45 FY28 EPS at a ~22-24x multiple, the stock reaches the high-$120s to mid-$130s.

**Bear Case ($72-80):** Forward rates fall faster than the ladder can defend, custodial revenue compresses in 2027-2028 (the hedge-liability swing proves prescient), and the non-recurring margin tailwinds (fraud-cost decline, forfeiture-driven low stock comp) reverse. Fidelity and Optum accelerate share gains, and channel in-sourcing pressures the Network Partner model. Consensus resets lower and the multiple compresses toward the 52-week-low zone as the market re-rates the business as a rate play rather than a secular growth story.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $105.** Roughly 20-21x forward FY28 consensus EPS (~$5.10-5.45 blend), reflecting a business with high-single-digit revenue growth and buyback-supported EPS, discounted for the turning rate cycle. This sits at the low end of the analyst target range and implies ~7% upside from the current $97.67.

**Bull case target: $130** (~+33%) — yields hold and the investment-asset engine plus buybacks drive EPS above $5.45.

**Bear case target: $78** (~-20%) — forward-rate compression flows through custodial revenue and non-recurring margin gains reverse.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the key checkpoints being quarterly custodial-yield disclosures and any change in the Fed rate path. Q2 FY2027 earnings (roughly early September 2026) and Fed policy meetings are the near-term catalysts.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) HSA-cash yield holds or rises above 3.84% for two consecutive quarters despite rate cuts, demonstrating ladder/Enhanced Rates durability; (2) forward EPS revisions turn positive (>+5% over 90 days) on the strength of the investment-asset mix shift; (3) a pullback toward the low-$80s that restores forward multiple compression below the growth rate.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) custodial yield falls more than ~40bps sequentially with guidance cut; (2) forward-year consensus falls more than 5% over 90 days; (3) an adverse resolution of the cybersecurity litigation with a material, estimable loss, or a re-acceleration of fraud-reimbursement costs.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 FY2027 earnings and subsequent Fed policy meetings.

## Conclusion

HealthEquity is a high-quality, cash-generative scale leader in a structurally growing market with a genuine moat from its IRS non-bank custodian status and a healthy, rate-independent growth engine in its investment-asset book. The score trajectory has improved (6.2 → 6.9 → 6.8), and the fundamentals justify most of that improvement. But the recommendation history has oscillated between HOLD and a single BUY, and the current data argues for restraint: the earnings inflection that re-rated the stock was substantially a rate-cycle event, the hedge book is signaling forward-rate pressure on the custodial line, and consensus estimates have stalled rather than accelerated. This is neither the rising-estimate, cheap-relative-to-growth setup that warrants pressing a BUY nor a deteriorating business that warrants an AVOID.

We rate HQY a **HOLD (growth_hold)** with a base case of $105. The durable growth supports holding for appreciation over 12 months, but the asymmetry improves materially on a pullback toward the low-$80s or on hard evidence the custodial book can defend yields as rates ease.
