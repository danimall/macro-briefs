**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Citizens BancShares is a $229.7B top-20 U.S. bank that was reshaped by two deals: the January 2022 CIT merger and the March 2023 FDIC-assisted acquisition of Silicon Valley Bridge Bank, which roughly quadrupled assets from pre-2022 levels and generated a one-time $9.8B bargain-purchase gain. The franchise now combines a 125-year-old Southeast retail/commercial bank, the dominant U.S. venture/PE fund-banking business inherited from SVB, and a CIT-derived equipment-finance and Rail leasing operation. The balance sheet is strong: CET1 of 11.15%, $56.0B of HQLA (24% of assets) covering uninsured deposits 1.4x, and a much-repaired AFS securities mark ($162M loss versus $762M a year prior).

The investment debate is about earnings direction, not balance-sheet safety. Net income has normalized from the 2023 windfall ($11.5B) to $2.78B (2024) to $2.21B (2025), down 21% YoY, while net interest income fell 5% and NIM compressed 29bps to 3.25%. The bank is asset-sensitive (about 65% of loans float), so the Fed's easing cycle lowers asset yields faster than it lowers deposit costs (-100bps of rates implies roughly -5.8% NII). Purchase-accounting accretion, which has inflated reported NIM, is rolling off rapidly ($740M in 2023 → $251M in 2025). Management's 2026 NII guidance of $6.5–6.8B is flat-to-down from 2025, and consensus current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 7-8% over the past 90 days.

What offsets the earnings headwind is capital return. The company repurchased $3.03B of Class A stock in 2025 (cutting the Class A count ~12%) with ~$1.9B of authorization remaining through 2026, which supports per-share metrics even as net income declines. At ~11.2x trailing/forward EPS the stock is not expensive for the quality, and the Q1 2026 print (adjusted EPS $44.86, ~10.5% beat) shows core earnings power is intact. With negative estimate revisions, a guided NII plateau, and the $33.4B FDIC Purchase Money Note due March 2028 that must be refinanced well above its 3.50% rate, the setup is balanced. We rate FCNCA a HOLD.

## Company Overview

First Citizens is the Raleigh-based holding company for First-Citizens Bank & Trust Company, operating three reportable segments after a 2025 recast:

- **General Bank** ($74.8B deposits, $65.0B loans, $1.18B net income) — the legacy Southeast/Mid-Atlantic branch franchise plus wealth, SBA, and community association banking.
- **Commercial Bank** ($41.5B deposits, $82.9B loans, $975M net income) — the growth engine, now incorporating the former SVB Commercial businesses: Global Fund Banking (capital call lines), TMT, Healthcare/Life Sciences, factoring, and equipment finance.
- **Rail** ($8.9B net operating lease equipment, $87M net income) — ~128,400 railcars/locomotives at 96.2% utilization, repricing at 117% of expiring rates.

A Corporate/Direct Bank carries $44.8B of online savings deposits. The franchise's distinguishing features are a low-cost relationship deposit base, the niche venture/PE fund-banking franchise (capital call lines), and family stewardship oriented toward conservative balance-sheet management.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is normalizing.** The 2023 net income was 85%+ a bargain-purchase accounting gain. Diluted EPS of $165.24 (down from $189.41) was cushioned by buybacks. ROA fell to 0.96% (from 1.26%); ROE was approximately 9.9%.

**Net interest income is the core story.** NIM compressed to 3.25% (3.13% ex-accretion). The mechanism: an asset-sensitive book (~65% floating, indexed to SOFR/Prime) reprices down faster than the deposit base as the Fed cuts toward 3.50–3.75%. Two compounding effects make this worse than a simple rate story:

1. **Fading purchase-accounting accretion.** PAA fell from $740M (2023) to $481M (2024) to $251M (2025). This non-cash boost to reported NIM is rolling off, so headline margin will deteriorate even faster than core spread.
2. **Asset sensitivity in a cutting cycle.** Management's disclosed sensitivity is roughly -5.8% NII at -100bps and -11.3% at -200bps.

The 2026 guidance brackets the 2025 result, confirming a plateau rather than growth. Morningstar flags NIM headwinds for 2026.

**Loan growth is healthy and concentrated in the best book.** Loans grew 6% to $147.9B, almost entirely in Commercial Bank (+$7.64B), led by capital call lines (+$6.3B, +25%, to $31.8B). These carry a 0.09% loss rate versus the 1.06% portfolio average — high-quality, low-loss growth. Deposits grew 4% to $161.6B, with noninterest-bearing holding near 25%.

**Capital and liquidity are strong.** Total RBC was 13.71%, Tier 1 leverage 9.29% — all comfortably above requirements, though down 150-185bps YoY from buybacks and the loss of favorable RWA treatment after the FDIC Shared-Loss Agreement terminated in April 2025. HQLA plus $30.7B contingent sources cover uninsured deposits ($61.8B, 38% of total) at 140%. The AFS portfolio carries a short ~2.7-year duration, a far healthier mark than peers caught in the 2022-23 rate shock.

**Capital allocation defines the equity story.** Operating cash flow was $2.92B. The 2025 repurchases totaled 1.58M shares, shrinking the Class A count from 12.71M to 11.13M. A $4.0B repurchase program runs through year-end 2026 ($1.9B remaining as of Q1 2026; 449,845 shares bought in Q1 at ~$2,001). The dividend was raised to $2.10/quarter. The current ~0.43% yield is small; this is a buyback-driven, not income-driven, capital-return story.

One technical note: the Altman Z-Score of 0.21 ("distress") is structurally inapplicable to a bank with normal ~9.3x debt/equity and should be disregarded. The real financial-health read is the capital and liquidity profile above.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is muted and near-term earnings momentum is negative. The earnings surprise record is solid (3 beats, 1 small miss over four quarters; Q1 2026 beat by 10.5%), but consensus estimates are moving the wrong way:

- Current-quarter EPS estimate cut to $39.71, down 7.4% over 30 days and 8.0% over 90 days.
- Next-quarter down 2.6%/30d.
- Current-year roughly flat ($175.68), next-year down 1.8%/30d.

The negative revision trend reflects the NII plateau and NIM compression, and it is the dominant forward signal here. The growth that exists is concentrated in capital call lines and the Commercial Bank, geared to private-equity/venture fundraising cycles. Rail is a stable contributor with strong utilization and re-lease spreads.

Two transactions add modest growth optionality. The pending BMO branch acquisition (announced October 2025) brings ~138 branches, ~$5.7B deposits, and ~$1.1B loans across the Midwest/Great Plains/West, expected to close 2H 2026; it is deposit-funding-accretive and geography-expanding. The planned Q4 2026 unified-brand rollout (consolidating SVB and legacy identities) is an efficiency move that carries client-retention execution risk in the venture franchise.

Muted top-line growth and falling estimates mean the strong-inflection BUY signal (positive estimate revisions plus accelerating revenue) is absent. This is a stable franchise managing through a margin headwind, not an earnings inflection story.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$1,955–$2,053, FCNCA trades at roughly 11.2x trailing/forward EPS, about 11.1x consensus current-year, and ~1.0x tangible book of the asset base. That is a reasonable-to-modestly-cheap multiple for a top-20 bank with 11%+ CET1, strong liquidity, and disciplined capital return, but the discount is justified by the earnings trajectory rather than a clear mispricing.

The valuation case rests on cycle math: the bank loses NII as rates fall, but the buyback (~9% of market cap) mechanically supports EPS, and an eventual rate stabilization plus the BMO deposit base could re-stabilize NII into 2027. On normalized mid-cycle spread (ex-accretion NIM around 3.1%), core earnings power supports the current multiple but does not generate obvious asymmetry.

Analyst targets bracket the stock: low $2,050, median $2,152, mean $2,213, high $2,475 — implying mid-single to high-teens upside to the mean. Sentiment has improved (strong-buy/buy counts rose, holds fell over two months), and the low target sits essentially at the current price, which limits downside-target signaling. We do not treat these targets as floors here because estimates are falling, not rising — the standard "analyst targets lag improving estimates" logic runs the other way.

Net: fairly valued. The multiple is defensible and the buyback provides a per-share tailwind, but the falling-estimate backdrop and the 2028 funding overhang cap the valuation score in the 6 range rather than higher.

## Competitive Landscape

First Citizens occupies an unusual hybrid position across a conservative Southeast retail/commercial bank, the dominant U.S. venture/PE fund-banking franchise, and a CIT-derived equipment-finance and Rail leasing business. The capital-call lending niche is genuinely differentiated and growing fast at low loss rates, a competitive strength the market does not fully credit. The deposit franchise is low-cost and relationship-driven.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the fund-banking side and stable on the retail side, with the BMO branch deal adding geographic reach. Headwinds: as a Category IV bank ($100B+ assets), FCNCA faces enhanced prudential standards and potential Basel III Endgame capital increases; non-bank/private-credit providers and digital-asset firms seeking trust charters represent a disintermediation theme management emphasizes in the 10-K. The venture franchise's growth depends on PE/VC fundraising and exit activity, which is cyclical. On balance, the moat is solid and the niche franchise is widening, supporting a competitive-position score modestly above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

1. **FDIC Purchase Money Note refinancing (2028).** The single largest under-the-radar item is the $33.4B, 3.50% fixed-rate note payable to the FDIC, due March 2028. Management is voluntarily prepaying it (~$2.49B in December 2025, ~$500M/month into 2026), which is prudent but consumes excess liquidity. Whatever portion remains must be refinanced well above 3.50%, a structural NIM headwind into 2028.

2. **Fading purchase-accounting accretion.** PAA inflated reported NIM by 12-42bps annually and is rolling off; earnings quality is shifting from accounting tailwinds to compressing core spread.

3. **NDFI / capital-call concentration.** Finance and insurance is now 31.8% of commercial loans ($38.4B); loans to non-depository financial institutions are $37.6B ($31.8B capital call lines). Low-loss today, but it ties growth to PE/VC cycles, and regulators are increasing scrutiny of bank lending to NDFIs/private credit.

4. **Credit hot spots.** Net charge-offs rose to $640M (+$100M), including an $82M single supply-chain finance client charge-off. The general office CRE book ($2.0B, 1.35% of loans) shows 10.17% nonaccrual, 4.44% NCO, and 11.48% 30+ days past due. Early/growth-stage venture loans carry a 6.52% ALLL. The ALLL was released (-$110M) partly on a macro-scenario reweighting, leaving reserves at a cyclical low (1.06% of loans) that may need rebuilding if the cycle turns.

5. **Asset sensitivity in a cutting cycle.** The easing path is a direct earnings headwind.

6. **Integration / brand-conversion execution.** The Q4 2026 unified-brand rollout carries client-retention risk in the venture franchise.

7. **New 10-K risk factors** reflect the environment: IEEPA tariff uncertainty (SCOTUS struck down IEEPA tariffs in February 2026, leaving refund/replacement uncertainty), federal-government restructuring, AI model risk, and crypto/stablecoin disintermediation.

8. **Dual-class governance.** The Holding family and related entities control >50% of total voting power via Class B (16 votes/share), an explicit anti-takeover/entrenchment risk, though historically aligned with conservative stewardship.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing balanced, fair-value uncertainty rather than directional stress. Near-term ATM IV (30.5%) sits modestly above 1-year HV (27.6%), a fair 2.9% spread, with IV in the 21st percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is essentially neutral (1.8%), and the term structure is flat (near 30.5% ≈ far 30.8%), so the market is not concentrating risk around any single near-term catalyst. The headline put/call volume ratio of 3.00 looks alarming, but the put/call open-interest ratio of 0.79 is benign, indicating recent put volume is likely hedging or income overwriting rather than informed bearish positioning. Options confirm the balanced, range-bound HOLD thesis. Short interest is moderate at 6.0% of float with 8.6 days to cover — no squeeze setup and no crowded bearish bet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The buyback (at ~$2,000/share) compounds per-share value even as net income plateaus. Rate cuts eventually stabilize, the BMO deposit base lowers funding costs and reaccelerates NII into 2027, the capital-call franchise keeps growing at sub-0.10% loss rates, and core earnings power reasserts itself. CET1 above 11% and excess capital from the SVB windfall give management room to keep returning capital. On stable NII and continued buybacks, EPS can grind toward $190-200 (consensus next-year $198.46), supporting a re-rate to the analyst mean (~$2,213) or higher. Bull target ~$2,475 (analyst high), roughly +21% from current ($2,053).

### Bear Case
Rate cuts run deeper than expected, the asset-sensitive book keeps bleeding NII (-11% at -200bps), PAA fully rolls off, and the FDIC note refinances at materially higher cost into 2028. Office CRE and venture-credit pockets force reserve rebuilds from a cyclical-low ALLL, charge-offs rise, and EPS falls toward $150-160. With falling estimates and compressing returns, the multiple de-rates toward ~9-10x. Bear target ~$1,550-1,650, roughly -20% to -25% from current.

### Base Case
NII holds within the $6.5-6.8B guide, buybacks support per-share metrics, credit stays benign in aggregate, and EPS lands near consensus (~$176 current-year, ~$198 next-year). The stock tracks book value growth plus the buyback yield, drifting toward the analyst median (~$2,152), roughly +5% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is modest appreciation driven by buyback-supported EPS and eventual NII stabilization, but near-term estimate momentum is negative and the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NII guidance revised upward or quarterly NII inflects higher, with consensus current-year EPS revisions turning positive (>+3% over 90 days).
- Visible plan to refinance/term out the FDIC note at manageable cost, removing the 2028 overhang.
- Office CRE/venture credit metrics stabilize (nonaccrual and NCO rates flat-to-down) while the buyback authorization is extended at current price levels.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Reserve rebuild that meaningfully cuts EPS, or charge-offs accelerating beyond the $640M run-rate, particularly in capital-call/NDFI or venture books.
- NII guidance cut below $6.5B, or NIM compressing materially faster than the ex-accretion 3.13% trajectory.
- Capital ratios falling toward regulatory minimums forcing a buyback halt.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, plus monitoring of the BMO deal closing (2H 2026), the Q4 2026 brand transition, and the Fed's rate path. The FDIC note maturity (March 2028) is the key dated structural item.

## Conclusion

First Citizens is a well-run, well-capitalized franchise with a genuinely differentiated venture/PE fund-banking niche and a disciplined, family-aligned capital-return program. The balance sheet is safe, the Q1 2026 beat confirms intact core earnings power, and the buyback meaningfully supports per-share value. The forward earnings picture is flat-to-down: NII is guided to a plateau, NIM is compressing as the Fed cuts into an asset-sensitive book, the purchase-accounting tailwind is fading, consensus estimates are falling, and the FDIC note refinancing looms in 2028. At ~11x earnings the stock is fairly valued for that profile, with the analyst mean implying mid-teens upside and the low target essentially at the current price. The risk/reward is balanced, options pricing is neutral, and there is no near-term catalyst to force a re-rate. We rate FCNCA a HOLD, with the path to a BUY running through stabilizing NII guidance and positive estimate revisions.
