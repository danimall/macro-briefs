## Executive Summary

Texas Roadhouse is one of the strongest operators in casual dining, delivering $8.7M average unit volumes, sustained positive guest traffic growth, and a debt-free balance sheet. These attributes are rare in the restaurant sector and justify a premium valuation. However, the stock faces a concrete near-term challenge: 6-7% commodity inflation (primarily beef) is compressing margins while management deliberately limits menu price increases to ~1.9%, sacrificing profitability to preserve traffic. The result has been three EPS misses in four quarters and full-year 2025 EPS declining 5.7% despite 9.4% revenue growth.

Q1 2026 shows improvement: comp sales accelerated to 7.1%, restaurant margins improved sequentially, and EPS grew 9.6%. The CEO indicated "terrific momentum" with 6.5% comp sales continuing into Q2. However, consensus estimates have been revised downward (-4.8% for FY2026, -6.0% for FY2027 over 90 days), reflecting continued uncertainty about beef cost absorption. At ~25x forward earnings with falling estimates and a consumer environment under stress, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling at current levels.

The stock has declined 20% from its May 2025 highs. The decline reflects genuine fundamental deterioration (margin compression, earnings misses) that has not yet conclusively reversed. The Q1 improvement is encouraging but requires confirmation across multiple quarters before confidence in a sustained recovery warrants aggressive positioning.

## Company Overview

Texas Roadhouse operates 816 restaurants across three concepts: the flagship Texas Roadhouse steakhouse (744 units, $8.7M AUV), Bubba's 33 sports bar (56 units, $6.3M AUV), and the early-stage Jaggers fast-casual concept (16 units). Of these, 714 are company-operated and 102 are franchised — with the company actively consolidating domestic franchises (down from 56 to 36 in 2025).

The business model centers on a "managing partner" profit-sharing structure that aligns store-level operators with profitability, contributing to industry-leading unit economics. The company operates with zero long-term financial debt, funding growth primarily through operating cash flow.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** Revenue has grown ~47% over three years ($4.0B → $5.9B), driven by both new unit openings (~5% store week growth) and robust comparable restaurant sales. Q1 2026 accelerated to +12.8% YoY with 7.1% same-store sales growth.

**The Margin Problem:** Despite top-line strength, profitability contracted in 2025:
- Restaurant margin: 17.1% (2024) → 15.5% (2025) → 16.3% (Q1 2026)
- Operating margin: 9.6% (2024) → 8.1% (2025) → 9.0% (Q1 2026)
- Food & beverage as % of sales: 33.4% → 35.0% → 35.3%

The company absorbed 6.1% commodity inflation in 2025 while implementing only 1.4% menu price increases. This deliberate strategy builds long-term brand equity but produced a 6.5% net income decline ($434M → $406M) and EPS contraction ($6.47 → $6.10).

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:** Operating cash flow of $730M in FY2025 ($259M in Q1 2026 alone) supports significant capital deployment. Free cash flow of $342M translates to a 5.8% FCF margin and approximately 3.1% FCF yield at current market cap ($11B). The balance sheet carries zero financial debt against a $450M revolver (only $50M drawn in Q1 2026). ROE of 28.4% and ROA of 11.7% demonstrate capital efficiency.

**Capital Allocation:** In 2025, the company deployed $826M total: $388M capex (new stores + maintenance), $108M franchise acquisitions, $180M dividends (recently raised 10%), and $150M buybacks ($380M remaining on authorization). The dividend yield of ~1.9% provides modest income.

## Growth Analysis

**Positive Indicators:**
- Q1 2026 comp sales accelerated to +7.1% (from +4.9% FY2025)
- Guest traffic growth of 2.8% in 2025 — exceptional for casual dining where most peers see declines
- Q2 2026 started at +6.5% comp sales (first 5 weeks)
- Unit growth pipeline: 22 restaurants under construction; targeting ~30 openings in 2026
- Franchise consolidation adds incremental economics as royalty income converts to full restaurant economics

**Concerning Signals:**
- Bubba's 33 comp sales decelerating sharply: 2.8% (2025) → 0.9% (Q1 2026)
- Consensus FY2026 EPS estimate of $6.28 represents only 3% growth over FY2025's $6.10
- FY2027 EPS estimates have been cut 6% over 90 days (from $8.10 to $7.62)
- Current quarter estimate flat over 30 days; next quarter revised down 4.2% over 90 days

The growth trajectory is bifurcated: the flagship Texas Roadhouse concept is accelerating while Bubba's 33 is losing momentum. At 56 units, Bubba's is still small enough that its deceleration doesn't materially impair the consolidated thesis, but it raises questions about second-concept economics.

## Valuation Assessment

At $159.69 with trailing EPS of $6.10, TXRH trades at 26.2x trailing earnings. Using the FY2026 consensus of $6.28, forward P/E is 25.4x. Using FY2027 consensus of $7.62, next-year P/E is 21.0x.

**Peer Context:**
- Domino's (DPZ): 18.7x P/E
- Brinker (EAT): 14.3x P/E
- Wingstop (WING): 36.2x P/E
- Peer median: 36.2x

TXRH's 25-26x multiple sits below high-growth peers (WING, CAVA) but well above value-oriented casual dining operators (DPZ, EAT). The premium over DPZ and EAT is justified by TXRH's superior traffic growth, unit economics, and balance sheet quality. The discount to WING reflects TXRH's margin headwinds and slower growth profile.

**FCF yield:** At ~3.1%, this falls below the 4% threshold typically required for Consumer Discretionary BUYs in the current environment. The FCF yield would need to expand via either price decline to ~$130 or FCF growth toward $440M+ to clear this hurdle.

**Analyst targets:** Mean $193.60 (21% upside), median $190 (19% upside). Low target of $165 sits only 3% above current price. Recent target revisions have been downward (Mizuho to $190, Stifel to $170, Citi to $176).

## Competitive Landscape

Texas Roadhouse occupies a distinctive competitive position in casual dining:

1. **Traffic growth when peers decline:** TXRH posted +2.8% traffic growth in 2025 while many casual dining chains continue losing guests. Q1 2026 traffic growth likely accelerated further given the 7.1% comp sales with only 1.9% pricing.

2. **Unit economics moat:** $8.7M AUV is 2-3x the casual dining average. High volumes attract better managing partners, enable better food quality per dollar, and support higher labor investment.

3. **Value positioning in an inflationary environment:** By absorbing commodity inflation rather than passing it to guests, TXRH is building structural competitive advantage. When competitors raise prices 6-8%, TXRH raises 1.4-1.9%. This gap compounds over time into a meaningful value proposition that drives share gains.

4. **Domestic focus with minimal delivery exposure:** To-go sales at 13.6% of revenue are growing but the business avoids dependency on third-party delivery margins. This preserves economics relative to peers with 30%+ delivery mix.

The competitive position is strengthening. The risk is that the value strategy depends on eventual beef cost normalization — if the cattle cycle keeps costs elevated for 3+ years, the margin sacrifice becomes increasingly painful without commensurate pricing action.

## Risk Assessment

**Beef Cost Inflation (High Impact, High Probability):** The company sources from only 4 major beef suppliers and only 45% of 2026 costs are locked. With guidance for 6-7% commodity inflation continuing, margins remain under pressure. The cattle cycle is structurally tight (smaller herd sizes) with no clear resolution timeline.

**Consumer Spending Deceleration (Moderate Impact, Rising Probability):** Casual dining faces macro headwinds. TXRH's value positioning provides relative insulation, but a recession would pressure traffic even for best-in-class operators.

**Tariff Risk on Beef Imports:** The 10-K explicitly calls out tariff risk affecting food supplies. If tariffs impact Canadian beef imports (a key sourcing region), costs could rise beyond current guidance.

**GLP-1 Drug Adoption:** Management explicitly identifies GLP-1 agonists as a risk factor. Increasing adoption could reduce portion sizes or visit frequency. This is a slow-moving but potentially meaningful headwind for a steakhouse concept.

**Bubba's 33 Deceleration:** Comp sales dropping to 0.9% in Q1 2026 while the company accelerates unit openings (13.4% store week growth) creates risk of building into weakening demand for this sub-concept.

### Options Market Signal

This extreme short-dated IV likely reflects the immediate post-earnings period (Q1 results reported May 7). ## Consumer Discretionary BUY Gate Assessment

Addressing the four required conditions for a Consumer Discretionary BUY:

**(a) Positive sequential same-store sales:** YES — Q1 2026 comp sales of +7.1%, accelerating from +4.9% in FY2025. Q2 tracking at +6.5%.

**(b) FCF yield ≥ 4%:** NO — FCF yield is approximately 3.1% ($342M FCF / $11B market cap). This condition is not met.

**(c) Consumer confidence not in declining trend:** NO — Consumer backdrop is weakening.

**(d) Positive estimate revisions over trailing 90 days:** MIXED — Current quarter estimates revised +4.5% over 90 days, but FY2026 estimates are -4.8% and FY2027 estimates are -6.0%. The overall revision trend is negative.

**Assessment:** TXRH meets 1 of 4 conditions clearly, fails 2 clearly, and has mixed results on the 4th. A BUY recommendation is not supportable under these gate conditions despite the company's operational quality.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS surprises demonstrating sustained margin recovery (Q2 and Q3 2026)
2. FY2027 consensus EPS revisions turn positive (currently -6.0% over 90 days) indicating analyst confidence in margin normalization
3. Stock pulls back to $135-140 range (FCF yield approaches 4%) providing valuation support

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Traffic growth decelerates below +1% for any quarter (would indicate the value strategy is failing)
2. FY2026 EPS guidance/consensus drops below $5.80 (implying continued deterioration rather than stabilization)
3. Bubba's 33 posts negative comp sales for two consecutive quarters (indicating second-concept failure)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (Q2 2026, likely late July/early August). The Q2 result will test whether the 6.5% comp sales trend holds and margins continue improving. If margins compress again despite strong top-line, the deliberate under-pricing strategy may need more time to pay off than the market will tolerate.

## Conclusion

Texas Roadhouse is a best-in-class casual dining operator with structural competitive advantages that will compound over time. The managing partner model, value-first pricing strategy, and industry-leading unit economics create a durable franchise. However, the current investment case is complicated by genuine near-term headwinds: beef inflation is compressing margins, three of four recent quarters missed estimates, and the broader consumer environment is deteriorating. The Q1 2026 result shows encouraging signs of stabilization, but one quarter does not constitute a trend reversal.

At 25x forward earnings with declining estimates, a sub-4% FCF yield, and a weakening consumer backdrop, the stock does not offer sufficient margin of safety for a new BUY despite its ~20% decline from highs. The decline reflects real fundamental pressure, not mere sentiment. HOLD with a positive growth orientation — the underlying business is strengthening operationally, and confirmation of sustained margin recovery over Q2-Q3 2026 would warrant upgrading to BUY, particularly if the stock remains in the $155-170 range.
