**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ImmunityBio is a commercial-stage immuno-oncology company built around ANKTIVA (N-803), an IL-15 receptor superagonist approved in April 2024 for BCG-unresponsive non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) with carcinoma in situ. The investment case is two-sided, and after working through the evidence I conclude the balance of forces is close to neutral rather than clearly negative. Revenue is accelerating on real unit volume (+168% YoY in Q1 2026 to $44.2M, +15% sequentially), geographic approvals are expanding across five jurisdictions, and liquidity built to $380.9M from $242.8M over the prior quarter. Against this sits an explicit going-concern qualification, roughly $1.1B of senior and affiliate claims ahead of common equity, a funding model dependent on continuous dilution and on the founder's willingness (not obligation) to lend, and a fresh FDA promotional warning letter that spawned a securities class action.

The headline GAAP loss is misleading. The Q1 2026 net loss of -$632.8M (-$0.62/share) is roughly 84% non-cash fair-value remeasurement of warrant liabilities (-$295.4M) and the related-party convertible note (-$236.6M), both of which mark up because the stock rose. The underlying operating loss was -$69.8M and operating cash burn was -$75.4M, an improvement from -$85.9M a year earlier. Any framework keying off net income or the -$869.1M book equity will misread the operations; the correct lens is revenue trajectory, operating burn, and the liability waterfall. The current-year consensus EPS revision of -220.8% is largely this same accounting artifact, not fundamental deterioration, and next-year EPS flips positive to +$0.06.

I am moving to a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale. The stock has retraced from a 52-week high of $12.43 to ~$7.23, with the March OPDP warning letter and securities suit having driven a ~21% / ~$2B drawdown. The bear thesis (subordination, going concern, dilution, litigation) is largely known and visible after that pullback. The bull thesis (accelerating commercial traction, improving liquidity, two real 2026 sBLA catalysts) is concrete but contingent on binary regulatory outcomes I cannot reliably handicap. With 34.2% short interest and 10.4 days to cover, the bear case is also not cleanly actionable. When the positive and negative forces roughly cancel and the near-term catalysts are binary, HOLD is the defensible call.

## Company Overview

ImmunityBio's franchise is ANKTIVA (nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmln), a first-in-class antibody-cytokine fusion protein that activates NK cells, cytotoxic T cells, and memory T cells. It is the anchor of the company's "Cancer BioShield" platform, which also includes adenovirus-vectored vaccines, off-the-shelf and autologous NK-cell therapies, and immunomodulators. The approved indication is ANKTIVA plus BCG for BCG-unresponsive NMIBC with CIS, with or without papillary tumors (FDA, April 2024). ANKTIVA is administered with BCG, which is central to both its mechanism and its supply-chain risk.

Geographic approvals have expanded quickly: US FDA, UK MHRA (Jul 2025), Saudi SFDA (Jan 2026, including a conditional approval for ANKTIVA plus checkpoint inhibitors in metastatic NSCLC), EU/EMA conditional marketing authorization (Feb 2026), and Macau (Mar 2026). Commercial partners include Accord Healthcare (EU), Cigalah/Biopharma (Middle East), and Serum Institute of India (alternative BCG supply). The company operates as a single reportable segment around one approved product.

Control is concentrated. Founder, Executive Chairman, and Global CSO Dr. Patrick Soon-Shiong owns ~62.5% of votes; the company is a controlled company under Nasdaq rules and is deeply intertwined with his NantWorks ecosystem (shared services, related-party manufacturing facilities, a related-party clinical trial site, and related-party debt). CEO is Richard Adcock; CFO is David Sachs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue ramp is real and accelerating.** Product revenue went from $0.6M (FY2023) to $14.7M (FY2024) to $113.3M (FY2025). Q1 2026 product revenue was $44.2M, up 168% YoY from $16.5M and up 15% sequentially. Management reports the growth as unit-volume driven, not price-driven, which is the healthier read. Gross margin is artificially high (~99%; Q1 COGS was just $0.24M) because pre-approval inventory was already expensed to R&D; management explicitly warns COGS will rise as that "free" inventory depletes, so the reported gross margin should not be modeled forward.

**The GAAP loss is dominated by accounting marks.** ~$532M of the Q1 2026 net loss was non-cash fair-value losses on warrant liabilities and the related-party convertible note, both driven by the rising stock price. Annualized burn is roughly $300M. FY2025 operating loss was -$256.0M, improved from -$344.2M in 2024.

**The balance sheet is structurally distressed but liquidity improved.** Stockholders' equity was -$869.1M at 3/31/26, worsened from -$500.5M at YE25 largely because of the same fair-value remeasurement of liabilities. Accumulated deficit is -$4.36B. Total liabilities of $1.52B exceed total assets of $0.65B. Against ~$300M annual burn, the $380.9M cash plus marketable securities position is roughly 15 months of runway before further financing, and the financing machine is demonstrably functioning.

**The capital structure is the central story.** Three competing senior claims sit ahead of common equity:
- **Nant Capital convertible note** (Soon-Shiong affiliate): $480M principal at Term SOFR + 8.0% (~11.69%), maturing Dec 2027, conversion price $5.427. A Jan 2026 amendment allows partial conversion anytime; Soon-Shiong converted $25M into 4.6M shares on 3/31/26. Subordinated to the RIPA, carried at $678.4M fair value.
- **Oberland RIPA** (revenue-interest financing): $375M received total, including a new $75M tranche on Mar 30, 2026. Tiered payments of 5.625%–12.50% of worldwide net sales (ex-China), capped at 195% of cumulative purchaser payments, with a True-Up Payment due if cumulative payments by 12/31/2029 fall short. Secured by substantially all assets with a Put/foreclosure trigger on default. Senior to common and to the Nant note. Carried at $404.3M.
- **Altor CVRs**: ~$300.6M outstanding, payable if ANKTIVA worldwide sales exceed $1.0B before 12/31/2026. On the current ~$176M annualized run-rate this milestone looks unlikely to trigger, which removes a near-term cash demand but raises the SRS arbitration risk.

**Dilution is the funding model.** Shares outstanding rose from 697M (YE24) to 1.047B (May 2026). In Q1 2026 the company raised $223.9M ($102.1M ATM equity, $75M RIPA, $53.5M warrant exercises), with $395.4M of ATM capacity remaining and ~167M additional dilutive securities outstanding. Equity holders absorb the cost of keeping the company funded.

## Growth Analysis

The commercial inflection is concrete. ANKTIVA revenue has roughly 8x'd in a year and grew sequentially despite a documented TICE BCG supply shortage that caps the eligible patient pool. The earnings surprise record (3 beats, 1 narrow miss over four quarters) shows operating-loss narrowing tracking ahead of or near consensus.

Two 2026-27 catalysts could step up the revenue base materially:
1. **Papillary-only sBLA**: After a May 2025 Refuse-to-File, a Dec 2025 Type-B meeting and Feb 2026 resubmission led to FDA accepting the resubmitted sBLA (~May 19-20, 2026). Management still cautions a CRL or a required new randomized trial remains possible, so this is positive but not de-risked.
2. **BCG-naïve CIS (QUILT-2.005)**: fully enrolled, IDMC confirmed no additional enrollment needed, sBLA targeted for 2026. BCG-naïve is a far larger market than BCG-unresponsive and is the principal market-expansion lever.

NCCN guidelines now include ANKTIVA plus BCG for papillary-only disease (Category 2A), and five US patents on the combination were granted in May 2026. The broader "platform" thesis (NSCLC, pancreatic RMAT, GBM, lymphoma, tumor-agnostic lymphopenia) is early-stage and carries full clinical risk; I assign it minimal weight in the base case.

On the estimate-revision signal: the current-year revision is dominated by the fair-value accounting artifact discussed above and should not be read as fundamental deterioration. The next-year revision flipping to +$0.06 (+400%) is more informative about the operating trajectory. The revision data does not support a structural-impairment thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

IBRX trades at ~$7.23 with a ~$7.5B market cap. Conventional multiples are uninformative: there is no positive EPS, the trailing/forward P/E of -8.15 is meaningless, and book equity is negative. The relevant frame is enterprise value against a revenue ramp plus the liability waterfall.

On revenue, the company is at a ~$176M annualized run-rate against a ~$7.5B equity value (and higher EV once the senior/affiliate claims are added), implying a high single-digit-plus EV/forward-revenue multiple even on optimistic 2026-27 estimates. That is rich for a single-product company, but it is the structure of pre-scale specialty pharma; the multiple is a call option on the BCG-naïve and papillary expansions converting the run-rate into a multi-hundred-million-dollar franchise.

The analyst base is small but unanimously constructive: 1 strong buy, 2 buy, 0 hold/sell, with a mean target of $13.00 (~80% above current) and a recent upgrade. I weight this cautiously: the targets embed successful 2026 sBLA outcomes that management itself says are not assured, and the coverage is thin (3 analysts). The targets are credible as a bull-case anchor, not as a base case.

The valuation does not offer clean asymmetric upside, but neither is it a clear overvaluation that a low score would imply. It is contingent: cheap if both sBLAs land, expensive if either fails and dilution continues. I score this 4.5 — below neutral because the senior-claim overhang and dilution structurally cap equity upside even when the science works.

## Competitive Landscape

The BCG-unresponsive NMIBC space is becoming crowded. Ferring's Adstiladrin (approved gene therapy), J&J's TAR-200 (intravesical gemcitabine, strong recent data and the key emerging threat), and Merck's Keytruda (approved in CIS) all compete for the same patients. At AUA 2026, ImmunityBio presented cross-trial analyses positioning ANKTIVA on durability and safety, but there is no head-to-head data, which weakens the differentiation claim. ANKTIVA's mechanistic differentiation (immune activation, lymphopenia reversal) and its co-administration with the standard-of-care BCG backbone are genuine advantages, but the lack of comparative data and the dependence on a constrained BCG supply temper the competitive picture. The trajectory is improving (geographic expansion, guideline inclusion, patent grants) against intensifying competition, netting to a roughly stable-to-slightly-positive position.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Going concern — explicitly stated.** Management acknowledges substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern, alleviated only by reliance on Soon-Shiong's "intent and ability" to provide affiliate funding, which he is under no obligation to provide. This is an unusually direct single-person dependency.
2. **TICE BCG shortage.** Merck is the sole US TICE BCG supplier; the persistent shortage caps the addressable pool. Serum Institute's rBCG mitigation is unproven.
3. **FDA promotional/legal overhang.** A March 13, 2026 OPDP warning letter (over a TV ad the company says never aired and a Soon-Shiong podcast) triggered the drawdown noted above and a securities class action (Douglas v. ImmunityBio, class period Jan 19–Mar 24, 2026) plus two derivative suits. This is the sharpest concrete near-term risk and has already partly been absorbed by price.
4. **Related-party concentration / data-integrity risk.** Manufacturing and some clinical trials run through Soon-Shiong-affiliated entities; the 10-K flags that the FDA could question data integrity from the related-party site.
5. **CVR litigation.** SRS filed a JAMS arbitration (Nov 2025) seeking ~$164.2M + $50M interest; hearing set Jan 25, 2027.
6. **Customer concentration.** ~94% of Q1 2026 gross revenue from the top four customers (43/21/17/13%).
7. **Subordination.** Equity sits behind the ~$1.1B of senior/affiliate claims; the RIPA is secured by substantially all assets with a foreclosure trigger.
8. **Earnings volatility from fair-value accounting.** As the stock moves, warrant and note liabilities mark up or down, producing large GAAP swings that can spook investors regardless of operations.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not confirming a bearish thesis. Near-term ATM IV is 105.7% versus 99.4% one-year historical volatility (modestly rich), the put/call skew is call-heavy at -5.2% near-term, the put/call volume ratio is 0.47, and the term structure is in contango (105.7% near vs 125.9% long-dated). The longer-dated 6-month tenor shows a positive 19.0% put skew and a 57.1% implied move, which reflects the binary 2026 sBLA outcomes rather than directional bearishness. Combined with the 34.2% short interest and 10.4 days to cover, the positioning picture is a high-volatility, two-sided event stock, not a one-way short. The rich IV signals elevated uncertainty around the catalysts, not a clean entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Revenue compounds off the +168% YoY base as the papillary sBLA and BCG-naïve sBLA convert the addressable market from BCG-unresponsive to the much larger BCG-naïve population. Liquidity continues to build, operating burn keeps narrowing, geographic approvals add international revenue, and the franchise rerates toward the $12-15 analyst targets as consensus catches up to a multi-hundred-million-dollar run-rate. The founder backstops financing as needed, removing the going-concern overhang in practice.

**Bear Case.** Either 2026 sBLA receives a CRL or a demand for a new randomized trial, capping the revenue ramp at the BCG-unresponsive ceiling while the BCG shortage persists. Continuous ATM dilution and warrant exercises grind down per-share value, the RIPA's revenue-interest take (up to 12.5% of sales) drags on the very ramp the bulls need, the securities suit and SRS arbitration create cash and headline risk, and equity remains structurally subordinated to the senior claims. In this path the stock returns toward its prior $2-4 range as the speculative premium unwinds further.

These cases are close to symmetric, and the deciding variables are binary regulatory outcomes I cannot reliably forecast. That is the basis for HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the papillary sBLA action (a PDUFA-type decision following the May 2026 acceptance), the BCG-naïve sBLA submission/acceptance in 2026, and each quarterly print for revenue run-rate and cash burn.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FDA approval of either the papillary-only or BCG-naïve sBLA without a new randomized-trial requirement, materially expanding the addressable market.
- Capital-structure de-risking: a refinancing or paydown that reduces the RIPA/Nant senior claims relative to equity, or a clear demonstration that the company can self-fund without further large ATM dilution.
- Revenue run-rate clearing ~$300M+ annualized with operating burn narrowing toward breakeven.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A CRL or a demanded new RCT on a 2026 sBLA, combined with deteriorating liquidity (cash falling materially below ~$250M with no financing path).
- A material adverse development in the securities class action or SRS arbitration, or any FDA action questioning related-party data integrity that jeopardizes the core label.
- Evidence the founder financing backstop is withdrawn or constrained, which would activate the going-concern risk in earnest.

## Conclusion

I rate IBRX a **HOLD (uncertain)**. An AVOID would have been poorly supported: the speculative spike has already retraced, the OPDP/securities news already drove its drawdown, commercial traction is accelerating on real unit volume, liquidity built, operating burn is narrowing, and the financing machine is functioning. The bearish capital-structure and litigation concerns are real but largely known and visible after the pullback. With the short interest and the founder able to backstop financing at will, the bear case is also not cleanly actionable, leaving no clear downside asymmetry to capture.

What keeps this from being a BUY is equally specific: equity sits behind the senior and affiliate claims, the going-concern qualification is explicit and dependent on one individual, dilution is the operating funding model, governance is concentrated and conflicted, and the two value-creating 2026 catalysts are binary regulatory outcomes that management itself says are not assured. The positive and negative forces roughly cancel, and HOLD is the call that does not require predicting which way the catalysts break. For investors already holding, the position is worth maintaining into the 2026 sBLA decisions; for new capital, waiting for catalyst resolution or capital-structure de-risking offers a better entry.
