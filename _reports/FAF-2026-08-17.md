## Executive Summary

First American's operating recovery is real and is being delivered through cost structure rather than volume. Title-segment pretax margin expanded 310 basis points year over year in Q2 2026, from 12.6% to 15.7%, on domestic closed orders that *declined* 0.7%. Title personnel costs grew 9.5% against segment revenue growth of 16.9%, a spread made possible by a workforce that is 36% offshore and by capitalized automation investment now rolling into deployed product. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (average surprise 22.9%), current-year consensus EPS has been revised up 4.7% in 30 days to $7.14, and next-year consensus has risen 2.1% to $7.39.

The composition of that growth is where the caution sits. Revenue rose 15.0% in Q2 while average revenue per order rose 17.3% to $4,572 — price and mix, not units. Within the mix, refinance closed orders per day rose 12.3% and commercial direct premiums rose 34.1%, while purchase closed orders per day fell 3.4% and purchase *opened* orders per day fell 2.4%. Purchase transactions are the highest-ARPO, most recurring and most economically valuable order type, and they are still shrinking. The refinance leg carrying the recovery is the most interest-rate-elastic, lowest-margin category in the business, and it is the first category any GSE lender's-title waiver would target. [unverified — the specific claims that mortgage rates have moved to their highest level in over a year, that total application demand is below year-ago levels, and that the 30-year Treasury sits at a 2007 high on deficit supply, AI-related issuance and a Fed-independence premium are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data.] That backdrop, if it holds, would be the wrong one for a refinance-dependent earnings base.

At $73.86 the stock trades at 10.2x trailing-twelve-month EPS of $7.21, 10.3x current-year consensus and 10.0x next-year consensus, against a Property & Casualty peer median of 11.4x. It trades at 1.34x book of $55.10 and 2.03x tangible book of $36.44; the gap matters, because roughly one-third of stated equity is goodwill and intangibles concentrated in the title segment. Consensus itself implies only 3.5% EPS growth from 2026 to 2027, which is a reasonable read of a business whose cyclical fuel is a refinance wave. A base case of $83, derived from 11.2x next-year consensus, offers 12.4% upside plus a 3.0% dividend; a bear case of $58 on a post-refinance earnings reset offers 21.5% downside. That asymmetry supports holding, not buying.

## Company Overview

First American is the second-largest US title insurer by ALTA market share, competing against Fidelity National Financial, Old Republic and Stewart at the national underwriter level and against a fragmented independent-agent system at the point of distribution. Title Insurance and Services generated 93.6% of consolidated revenue in both 2025 and 2024.

The title segment is a bundle of five distinct economics rather than a single insurance operation. Direct operations perform the search and retain the full premium. Agency operations run through independent agents who retained 80.3% of agency-generated premium in Q2 2026, a roughly 20% gross-margin business at the premium line, on which First American nonetheless carries the full claims obligation. Escrow and closing services generate fee income and the customer float discussed below. Title plants — geographically indexed abstracts of public records carried at $690.2M — are licensed to third parties including competing title companies, and are the substrate for underwriting automation. First American Trust, FSB held $6.8B of assets and $6.2B of deposits at year-end 2025 and satisfies its regulatory requirement that 65% of assets be real-estate secured by holding agency MBS rather than originating loans. Adjacent services include warehouse lending, residential mortgage subservicing, 1031 exchange accommodation, appraisals and default services.

Home Warranty contributed $113.8M of Q2 revenue at a 21.3% pretax margin — small, but the most stable line because renewals do not depend on real-estate activity in the renewal year. The Corporate segment is structurally loss-making: $(56.2)M pretax in Q2 and $(87.2)M in H1, comprising corporate interest expense, shared services, a run-off P&C book and the venture investment portfolio.

One disclosure practice deserves emphasis for anyone owning this name: because title premium is earned in full at closing, First American has no contractual backlog. The $208.3M of deferred revenue is predominantly unearned home-warranty premium, not a forward book. The highest-frequency fundamental signal available is the monthly open and closed order count the company posts on its website roughly 10-12 days after each month-end.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings base.** Trailing-twelve-month diluted EPS reconciles as FY2025 $6.00 plus H1 2026 $3.33 less H1 2025 $2.12, or $7.21. At $73.86 that is 10.2x, consistent with the reported 10.23x trailing multiple. H1 2026 revenue was $3,955.3M, up 15.5%, and H1 net income attributable was $343.6M.

**The 2024 base year is a trap for screens.** FY2024 net income of $131M was depressed by roughly $401.6M of net investment losses from a portfolio rebalancing program and venture impairments. Title-segment pretax margin — the cleanest operating gauge — ran 8.6% (2023), 4.3% (2024) and 12.1% (2025), so operations genuinely troughed in 2024, but the GAAP net income line overstated the trough substantially. Any growth rate or ROE computed off 2024 net income measures a mark-to-market event. GAAP ROE for FY2025 computes to $621.8M over average equity of $5,204.0M, or 11.95%. The compensation-plan "ROE" disclosed in the proxy (10.6% for 2025, 8.2% for 2024, 6.6% for 2023) moves inconsistently with GAAP net income and is almost certainly an adjusted measure excluding investment marks; it should not be compared to GAAP ROE.

**Operating leverage is working in the right direction.** Q2 title revenue +16.9% against title expenses +12.8% produced pretax income +45.8% and the 310bp margin gain. The mechanism is disclosed plainly in the MD&A: a high proportion of fixed costs means margins expand as closed-order volume rises. This quarter margins expanded without volume rising, which points to structural cost improvement — offshore production scale and deployed settlement software — rather than pure cyclical gearing.

**Cash generation and capital intensity.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $362.2M against $309.0M a year earlier, after claim payments net of recoveries of $180.3M. Capex was $77.5M, down from $94.1M, extending a three-year decline: $278.7M (2023), $235.2M (2024), $192.4M (2025). FY2025 operating cash flow less capex was $758.4M. H1 2026 OCF less capex was $284.7M, but annualizing that figure would be unreliable — title activity is seasonally weighted to spring and summer, incentive-compensation payments are back-loaded, cash taxes paid in H1 were only $34.5M against $101.8M of tax expense, and accounts payable and accrued liabilities fell $113.0M. The FY2025 figure adjusted for the lower capex run rate is the more defensible anchor. Declining capitalized spend flatters near-term free cash flow and will, with a lag, reduce D&A, which was nearly flat in Q2 (+1.4%).

**Balance sheet.** Total assets grew from $16.23B to $18.94B in six months, almost entirely from bank deposit inflows rather than operating expansion: deposits rose $2,585.2M to $7,877.9M, cash rose to $2,624.8M, and debt securities rose to $9,774.7M. Corporate notes and contracts payable were flat at $1,546.5M with a fair value of $1,450.8M. Secured financings payable of $1,036.5M in the warehouse-lending business are matched against $1,152.2M of secured financings receivable. Debt-to-capitalization was 31.4%, or 21.5% on the company's adjusted basis excluding matched warehouse financings — a company-defined non-GAAP measure, but defensible given the offsetting receivable. The $900M revolver expiring May 2028 is fully undrawn; holding-company cash was $239.4M, down from $338.9M, against $427.8M of remaining-2026 insurance-subsidiary dividend capacity available without regulatory approval.

A standard Altman Z-Score screen places First American in nominal distress territory around 1.2. That is a methodology artifact: the working-capital and EBIT terms cannot be computed for a company presenting an unclassified balance sheet and reporting pretax income rather than EBIT, and the sales/assets term is structurally depressed by $10.7B of investments and $7.9B of deposits. The relevant solvency evidence is an undrawn revolver, flat corporate debt, 21.5% adjusted leverage, $2.6B of cash, and a portfolio 92.3% rated A- or better.

**The investment portfolio is the balance-sheet issue that matters.** At June 30 the debt portfolio carried amortized cost of $10,062.0M against fair value of $9,774.7M: gross unrealized losses of $326.2M against gains of $38.9M, a net pretax mark of $(287.3)M or 2.85% of cost, with $247.7M in positions held at a loss for twelve months or longer, concentrated in agency MBS ($170.4M) and municipals ($51.0M). AOCI widened from $(256.7)M to $(348.0)M in six months. Management attributes the losses to non-credit factors, which is credible given 63% of the portfolio is government-guaranteed agency MBS. Two consequences: reported book of $55.10 is understated by roughly $2.16 per share relative to an equity-ex-AOCI basis of $58.52, and the company has limited flexibility to rotate the portfolio without realizing losses, which is why purchases are being funded from deposit inflows ($2,296.5M of H1 debt-security purchases versus $1,393.7M a year earlier).

**The float internalization strategy.** Escrow deposits administered grew from $9.3B to $12.2B in six months, with the portion held at First American Trust rising from $3.7B to $4.9B. Like-kind exchange funds held at FA Trust jumped from $93.6M to $833.5M while total LKE funds administered barely moved. Subservicing client cash at FA Trust rose from $1.0B to $1.5B. These are third-party customer funds First American holds as custodian and does not own; what changed is custody location. Moving them from correspondent banks onto its own balance sheet lets the company capture the investment spread previously earned by those banks. The evidence it is working is title net investment income +11.5% in Q2; the cost is title interest expense +33.3%, explicitly attributed to higher interest on depositor funds and warehouse lending. The strategy converts First American into something closer to a spread lender, raising sensitivity to both rate level and curve shape, introducing genuine deposit-flight risk that management lists as its own risk factor, and — because escrow balances scale with transaction volume — amplifying rather than damping cyclicality.

**Reserve quality.** The loss provision has been pinned at 3.0% of title premiums and escrow fees for six consecutive periods, constructed identically each time: a 3.75% current-year ultimate rate less 0.75% of prior-year releases. Releases were $11.3M in Q2 and $21.2M in H1, versus $18.3M in H1 2025 and $39.8M for FY2025. FY2025 IBNR of $1,095.9M sat inside a disclosed actuarial range of roughly $948M to $1.3B, about $148M above the low end and modestly below the midpoint. Quantifying the contribution: $21.2M of H1 releases at the reported 22.8% effective rate is roughly $16.4M after tax, or about $0.16 of $3.33 in H1 diluted EPS — approximately 4.8%. Material but not thesis-changing. Total reserves declined from $1,169.6M to $1,151.6M while paid claims rose only modestly, consistent with a benign claims environment. One item to monitor: known title claims rose from $54.6M to $75.0M (+37%) while IBNR fell $42.0M. The natural reading is routine migration from IBNR to known within a shrinking total; the disclosure does not permit ruling out genuine emergence on older policy years, which would eventually pressure the 75bp release cadence.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision picture is positive but narrower than it first appears. Current-quarter consensus rose 3.6% in 30 days to $1.89. Next-quarter consensus, however, was cut 0.6% over 30 days and 1.5% over 90 days to $1.83, and next-year consensus of $7.39 implies only 3.5% growth over the upgraded current year. Revisions of this magnitude do not approach the threshold that signals a systematic underestimation of an earnings inflection; they are the normal aftermath of a 12.9% beat.

The volume decomposition is the substance of the growth question. Domestic closed orders of 137,300 were down 0.7% year over year. ARPO was up 17.3%, and the MD&A notes the ARPO gain was itself partially offset by mix shift toward lower-premium refinance transactions. Commercial direct premiums rose 34.1% (+$79.9M). The MBA forecast cited in the 10-Q projected industry purchase originations down 1.9% and refinance up 39.9% for the quarter, so First American's volumes are tracking the market, evidence of neither share gain nor share loss.

Two of the three growth legs are conditional. Refinance volume responds directly to rate movements. [unverified — the research brief and Phase 1 data do not contain a 10-year Treasury yield, a mortgage-rate level, or a statement that application demand is below year-ago levels; these specific figures cannot be sourced.] If the long end stays elevated or steepens further, the refinance leg compresses within one or two quarters, and it does so without any offsetting recovery in purchase volume. Commercial at +34.1% is the more interesting leg: it is the highest-ARPO category, the one where financial strength and coverage scope create genuine differentiation, and management singled it out on the call, but it is lumpy and driven by local financing availability rather than a durable secular driver. The third leg, information and other revenue at +11.6%, is genuinely diversifying: subservicing growth, non-insured information products and Canadian refinance activity.

The upside case rests on purchase volume inflecting against a cost base that has already been rebuilt. If the company can hold a 15%+ title margin on flat volume, a purchase recovery of even 10% would flow through with high incremental margin. Management's stated automation targets — Endpoint at scale by end-2027 and Sequoia toward 70% purchase automation — would compound that. Those figures come from a secondary summary of the earnings call rather than a filing, and should be treated as management ambition, not commitment. What the filings do corroborate is higher amortization of capitalized internal-use software from recently deployed digital settlement products, so the program exists and is being deployed.

## Competitive Landscape

The national underwriter tier is a four-firm oligopoly layered over fragmented agent distribution. Competition per the 10-K is "based primarily on service, quality, price, relationships and the ease of access and use of products," with two explicitly rate-like levers — pricing and agent splits — that the company adjusts based on competitive and regulatory conditions. Agent retention at 80.3% in Q2 (versus 80.0% a year earlier, with H1 2026 at 79.8% against 80.2% in H1 2025) is essentially flat and establishes no trend in either direction. Rising retention would be the visible signal of competition for agent relationships eroding underwriter economics; it is not present in the current data.

Three areas look genuinely defensible. The title plant and data estate is expensive to replicate, is being used to automate underwriting decisions, and generates licensing revenue from competitors — a position only Fidelity plausibly matches. Commercial title is where financial strength and coverage scope matter and where ARPO is highest. The bank plus adjacent services perimeter — warehouse lending, subservicing, 1031 accommodation, trust — is the widest among the four majors and drove the growth in information and other revenue. The offshore production base (6,959 of 19,102 employees, 36%) is the mechanical explanation for personnel costs growing 9.5% against 16.9% segment revenue growth.

The structural disadvantages are equally clear and are disclosed candidly: high fixed-cost intensity combined with regulated pricing means the business cannot price its way out of a volume trough, and roughly half of premium flows through agents who retain about 80% of it while the company retains the claims obligation. The regulatory structure that requires prior approval for any change of control — the company is simultaneously an insurance holding company and a savings-and-loan holding company — protects the franchise and simultaneously suppresses takeover optionality, a point management lists under its anti-takeover risk factor.

Trajectory assessment: operationally improving (margin, cost structure, commercial mix, data monetization), competitively stable (volumes tracking the market, agent splits flat), with the automation program the swing factor.

## Risk Assessment

**Purchase-volume dependence with purchase volume still falling.** This is the central operating risk. Purchase closed orders per day fell 3.4% in Q2 and 4.6% in H1, and opened orders fell 2.4%. The earnings level is carried by refinance and commercial. If rates stay elevated or rise, the refinance leg compresses quickly while purchase does not automatically recover.

**GSE title-waiver risk.** Risk factor 9 addresses modification of GSE and large-lender title insurance requirements; risk factor 17 contemplates GSE restructuring. The mechanism of harm is direct: a waiver or substitution of lender's title insurance on defined loan categories removes premium with essentially no offsetting cost reduction, and refinance loans are the obvious first target, precisely where current growth is concentrated. The current status of any specific FHFA pilot program could not be verified in this work and should not be assumed either way. This carries the highest asymmetry in the file.

**Rate regulation and pricing scrutiny.** Title rates are regulated in most jurisdictions. The 10-Q lists both rate regulation and heightened legislative and regulatory scrutiny of the title segment as forward-looking risk factors, and the MD&A concedes the structural vulnerability: high fixed service costs against revenue subject to regulatory and competitive pricing restraint.

**Venture portfolio impairment recurrence.** The Q2 $41.7M impairment on a single unnamed non-marketable equity position, driven by an internal valuation following an operational change at the underlying business, shows the mechanism that produced the 2024 earnings collapse is live. Cumulative gross unrealized gains on non-marketable equity are $280.5M against cumulative losses and impairments of $404.3M. Management states these are held primarily for strategic reasons, conceding financial return is not the primary test.

**Operational loss tail.** The company administers $12.2B of escrow deposits and $2.8B of like-kind exchange funds and lists errors and fraud involving funds transfers as a standalone risk. It filed an Item 1.05 material cybersecurity incident 8-K in December 2023, twice amended, with related insurance recoveries appearing as recently as $6.8M in Q2 2026 corporate revenue. The tail here is large relative to $622M of annual earnings.

**Undisclosed regulatory examinations.** Note 16 states governmental agencies are examining or investigating certain operations and that outcomes could require changes to business practices with material adverse impact. No matter, agency or exposure is identified, and none should be assumed.

**Governance and capital-allocation record.** Six Item 5.02 filings between April 2025 and April 2026 indicate unusual leadership and board churn; Mark Seaton, previously CFO, is now CEO, but the effective date, predecessor and circumstances could not be verified from the underlying 8-Ks. Board declassification, effective under a restated charter from May 14, 2026, is a real shareholder-friendly change but does not complete until the 2029 annual meeting. The buyback has under-delivered: 2.1M shares for $122.3M in FY2025 (~$58 average), then 0.9M shares for $54.0M in H1 2026 with zero repurchased in May, leaving $246.0M of a $300M authorization unused at 1.34x book. The dividend increase from $0.54 to $0.55 quarterly is 1.85%, below inflation, with hedged language ("at or above the current level"). Insider activity is one-directional: three sales totaling roughly $1.1M, including the Chief Accounting Officer selling 3,252.6 shares at $74.99 two days after the Q2 release, with no open-market purchases in the retrieved window. The dollar amounts are immaterial against retained holdings (Seaton holds 232,081 shares), and post-vest sales by a CAO and CLO are commonly liquidity-driven, so this is not a strong bearish signal, but insiders provided no confirming vote at a 1.34x book multiple following a 9% post-earnings rally.

**Comparability caveats.** The Q2 earnings release footnote discloses that refinance order counts and totals were revised in Q4 2025, with magnitude and direction unknown. Since ARPO is defined as direct premiums and escrow fees over closed orders, both refinance order growth and ARPO comparisons spanning that revision carry uncertainty.

The 1-month (29.8%), 3-month (30.5%) and 6-month (30.1%) tenors cluster almost exactly on realized volatility, so beyond the front-week artifact the options market is pricing no elevated uncertainty and no regime change. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($99, +34%).** Purchase volume stops contracting in late 2026 and inflects in 2027. Incremental purchase orders flow into a cost base rebuilt through offshoring and automation, and title margin moves from 15.7% toward 17-18%. Sequoia reaches meaningful purchase automation, Endpoint scales, and the D&A tailwind from three years of declining capex arrives. Commercial holds its cadence. Management deploys the $246M unused authorization plus incremental capacity at 1.3x book. EPS reaches $9.00 and the stock re-rates to 11x on that, or 13.4x current next-year consensus. The float internalization strategy compounds spread income as the deposit base grows with volume.

**Bear case ($58, -21.5%).** The rate backdrop stays unfavorable, the refinance wave ends within two quarters, and purchase volume continues its multi-year contraction. Closed orders fall outright, and the fixed-cost intensity that produced 310bp of margin expansion reverses with the same gearing. Commercial normalizes from a +34% comparison. A GSE lender's-title waiver on refinance loans removes premium directly. Venture impairments recur, the 0.75% reserve-release cadence stops as known claims emergence continues, and AOCI widens further. EPS resets to $5.50-6.00 and the multiple compresses to 10x, or the stock trades near 1.05x book. The March 2026 low of $56.20 is the reference point.

**What tips the balance.** The bull case requires a purchase-volume inflection that is not visible in any current data series. The bear case requires only that the refinance wave normalizes, which is the base rate for refinance waves. The base case — refinance moderating gradually while commercial and information revenue hold, margins flat at 15% — produces 12% upside plus a 3% yield and is the most probable single outcome. That is a fair return that does not clear the bar for new capital at 10x a possibly-peak earnings figure.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners are compensated: 12.4% to base, a covered 3.0% dividend, an undrawn revolver, 21.5% adjusted leverage and a book-value floor near $58. Prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at $73.86, 7.5% below the July 29 high of $79.87 and 31% above the March low.

**Reassessment timeline:** monthly order counts (posted 10-12 days after each month-end) are the near-continuous check; the Q3 2026 print, expected in late October, is the formal decision point.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Purchase closed orders per day turning positive year over year for two consecutive months in the published order data, confirming the highest-value leg has inflected while the cost base remains rebuilt.
2. Price of $66-68 or below with estimates intact — 9.0-9.2x next-year consensus and roughly 1.20x book, which would restore a 22%+ gap to base case and a genuine margin of safety.
3. Resumption of buyback at scale — deployment of a meaningful share of the $246M unused authorization below 1.4x book — combined with a dividend increase above 5%, which would answer the capital-allocation objection.
4. Commercial premium growth sustaining above 20% for two more quarters while information and other revenue holds double digits, which would establish a non-refinance growth base.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Title-segment pretax margin falling below 13% in any quarter, or total closed orders declining more than 5% year over year, indicating fixed-cost gearing has reversed.
2. Refinance closed orders per day turning negative year over year while purchase remains negative — the both-legs-down scenario that drives the bear case.
3. Announcement of a GSE or large-lender waiver or substitution program covering lender's title insurance on refinance originations.
4. Another venture impairment above $30M, or a break in the 0.75% prior-year reserve-release cadence, either of which would signal the earnings base is lower quality than reported.
5. Deposits at First American Trust declining materially quarter over quarter, indicating the float strategy is reversing and net interest income is at risk.

**Position guidance:** Hold existing exposure. Add only into the $66-68 band, or on confirmed purchase-order inflection. Do not add at or above $76 absent a change in the rate backdrop.

## Conclusion

First American is executing well against a mix it does not control. Title margin at 15.7% on flat volume, personnel costs growing at half the rate of revenue, four consecutive beats and current-year estimates up 4.7% in a month are all evidence of a management team that has rebuilt the operating base through the trough. The float internalization into First American Trust is a genuine and underappreciated earnings driver that a standard revenue screen misreads as organic growth.

What the price does not compensate for is the composition of the earnings. Roughly 10x forward looks like a discount until you note that consensus itself sees only 3.5% EPS growth next year, that about 4.8% of H1 EPS came from prior-year reserve releases, that Q2 GAAP EPS exceeded adjusted EPS on investment marks, and that the growth engine is a refinance wave. The purchase leg — larger, higher-ARPO, more durable — is still contracting after multiple years. Layer on an unresolved GSE title-waiver tail concentrated on refinance, a venture book that has been cumulatively value-destructive and produced a $41.7M charge last quarter, a five-year TSR record 69 percentage points behind the peer group with the gap widening every year, and a buyback that left $246M unused at 1.34x book while pausing entirely in May.
