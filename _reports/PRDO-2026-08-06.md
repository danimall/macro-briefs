## Executive Summary

Perdoceo is a three-institution online postsecondary holding company generating $846.1M of FY2025 revenue at a 23.2% GAAP operating margin, $216.6M of free cash flow (25.6% margin), and holding $680.0M of cash and short-term investments against $67.3M of interest-bearing obligations. Net cash of $612.6M equals 29.6% of market capitalization. Enterprise value is $1,458.5M, which puts the stock at 6.7x EV/FCF, 7.4x EV/GAAP operating income, and 10.6x the $3.11 FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS. Against an education-services peer median P/E of 15.6x, the discount is roughly 30%.

The discount reflects a specific cause rather than mispricing. The Reconciliation Act eliminates the Grad PLUS loan program for new borrowers effective **July 1, 2026**, replacing it with $20,500 annual / $100,000 aggregate Direct Unsubsidized caps for graduate students versus $50,000 / $200,000 for "professional-degree" students. Under the RISE committee's November 2025 consensus language, physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology and nursing are **not** classified as professional programs. Those are exactly the programs at University of St. Augustine for Health Sciences (USAHS), which was 18.6% of FY2025 revenue and 19.4% of Q1 2026 revenue, and where the 10-K states in the company's own words that "many graduate students previously relied on Grad PLUS loans." Existing borrowers are grandfathered up to three additional academic years, so the impact phases through the intake pipeline rather than landing at once: 2026 can look fine while 2027–2029 new-student cohorts absorb the change.

Two other things restrain the case. Organic growth is roughly 2.5%: of FY2025's $164.8M revenue increase, $147.5M (89.5%) was the arithmetic of USAHS's absence from the 2024 comparative, with CTU +4.1% and AIUS **−0.4%**. And enrollment momentum broke between measurement dates, from +7.3% at 12/31/25 (44,400) to **+1.1%** at 3/31/26 (48,740), with AIUS flipping from +11.2% to **−2.2%** after the fourth-quarter extra academic session that drove the December figure reversed. Q2 2026 results are scheduled for after the close on the report date, with the options market pricing a ±12.0% move at 65.2% near-term implied volatility against 35.0% realized and a 3.91 put/call open-interest ratio. HOLD, classified as uncertain: the balance sheet and cash yield support the current price, but how the July 1 loan-limit change lands in reported starts is unresolved and is about to be measured.

## Company Overview

Three reporting segments map one-to-one onto three accredited institutions, approximately 91% of whose 44,400 year-end 2025 students were in fully online programs:

| Segment | FY2025 revenue | % of total | Op. income | Margin | Enrollment 12/31/25 (y/y) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colorado Technical University | $461.6M | 54.6% | $180.6M | 39.1% | 29,950 (+6.6%) |
| American InterContinental University System | $226.2M | 26.7% | $36.0M | 15.9% | 10,560 (+11.2%) |
| Univ. of St. Augustine for Health Sciences | $157.6M | 18.6% | $3.2M | 2.0% | 3,890 (+2.6%) |

Student mix skews adult and vocational: 68% over age 30, 66% business studies, 22% health education, 12% IT; 63% bachelor's, 22% doctoral/master's. Total headcount 6,000, with USAHS employing 1,435 people to produce $157.6M of revenue against CTU's 2,039 for $461.6M; the campus-based clinical model is structurally more labor-intensive.

Three filing-grounded differentiators. First, CTU and AIU deliver instruction through `intellipath`, an adaptive learning platform held under perpetual license with personalized content developed in-house; USAHS runs third-party Blackboard, so this is an intra-portfolio asymmetry rather than a company-wide moat. Second, employer tuition-assistance engagements accounted for approximately **38.3% of CTU enrollments and 7.5% of AIUS enrollments** at 12/31/25 — lower revenue per student because of institutional grants, but lower recruiting and marketing cost, and the principal lever management cites for 90-10 Rule management. Third, regulatory standing: the consolidated Department of Education composite score was 3.0 for FY2024 and preliminarily 3.0 for FY2025 (the maximum), and in February 2025 CTU and AIUS were recertified through June 30, 2027 with AIUS removed from provisional certification.

Against roughly 25.7M national postsecondary enrollments, Perdoceo's 44,400 students are about 0.17% of the market. This is a niche cash generator in a fragmented industry, not a consolidator with pricing power.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and margin composition

Revenue *declined* 4.0% in FY2024 before the acquisition-driven step-up. The durable earnings engine has been margin expansion (18.6% → 25.6% over 2022–2024) and share-count reduction, not top-line growth.

The reported FY2025 margin decline from 25.6% to 23.2% is traceable and largely non-cash. D&A rose from $14.6M to $41.6M (+184%) on USAHS acquired-intangible amortization plus depreciation on a failed sale-leaseback asset, which alone accounts for 3.2 points of margin. Educational services and facilities expense rose to 23.3% of revenue from 17.7% on the USAHS clinical/campus cost base. The legacy cost structure improved on every line: advertising/marketing 13.3% of revenue from 14.8%, admissions 10.5% from 12.0%, administrative 21.2% from 22.1%, bad debt 3.5% from 4.9%. Ex-USAHS, total G&A rose only 1.3% ($4.9M).

Management's two non-GAAP measures are inconsistent. "Adjusted operating income" ($237.6M FY2025 vs. $188.9M FY2024, +25.8%) adds back **all** D&A, making it functionally operating EBITDA and flattering the acquisition that created the D&A. "Adjusted diluted EPS" ($2.61 vs. $2.42 GAAP) adds back only acquired-intangible amortization. Any comparison to consensus must specify the basis; the reported Q1 2026 beat ($0.90 actual vs. $0.86 estimate) is on the adjusted-EPS basis.

### Q1 2026 — real operating inflection, with three qualifications

Revenue $221.7M (+4.1%), operating income $63.1M (28.5% margin vs. 24.3%), net income $54.0M (+23.5%), GAAP diluted EPS $0.85 vs. $0.65, adjusted $0.90 vs. $0.70. Segment margins: CTU 41.9% (from 40.3%), AIUS 21.7% (from 19.5% despite enrollment −2.2%), **USAHS 14.7% ($6.3M operating income on $43.0M) versus −0.8% a year earlier**, with segment D&A already falling to $7.04M from $8.87M. Adding D&A back, USAHS produced roughly $13.3M of segment EBITDA on $43.0M of revenue, about a 31% EBITDA margin. Consolidated bad debt fell to 2.6% of revenue from 3.5%.

Qualifications: (a) the effective tax rate was 20.8% vs. 22.7%, benefiting 5.6 points from share-based-compensation tax effects and 1.2 points from reserve releases, against full-year 2026 guidance of 22.5%–23.5%; normalizing to 23.0% gives $68.1M pretax × 0.77 ÷ 63.431M diluted shares = **$0.83**, so roughly $0.02 of Q1 EPS is non-recurring rate benefit. (b) AIUS's December enrollment strength came from an additional fourth-quarter academic session, so part of Q1's AIUS revenue and margin is calendar pull-forward. (c) Capex was $1.7M against full-year guidance of ~1.5% of revenue (~$13M), so Q1 free cash flow is not run-rateable.

### Balance sheet and cash flow

FY2025 operating cash flow $225.2M vs. $161.6M; capex $8.6M per MD&A (the XBRL capex tag of $383K is unreliable and would overstate FCF). Free cash flow is therefore **$216.6M, a 25.6% margin**.

At 3/31/26: unrestricted cash $164.3M, short-term investments $514.8M, restricted cash $0.8M — $680.0M total. Interest-bearing obligations: finance leases $10.2M plus failed sale-leaseback financing $57.1M = $67.3M. **Net cash $612.6M**, or $9.77 per share and 29.6% of market capitalization. Total liabilities of $275.3M against $972.4M of equity is 0.28x; interest-bearing debt to equity is 0.07x. A screened debt/equity of 1.28x circulating in some datasets is not reconcilable to the filings and should be discarded. Current ratio 5.06, Altman Z 3.29, ROA 12.8%, ROE 16.4%.

The failed sale-leaseback deserves clarity: $154.1M of undiscounted future minimum payments against a $57.1M carrying liability reconciles as $152.8M of payments less $109.5M of imputed interest = $43.3M present value plus a $13.8M residual. Because sale-leaseback accounting failed, the asset stays on the balance sheet, no rent expense is recorded, and roughly $1.6M per quarter flows through amortization and interest. The economic obligation is a 25-year campus lease, not credit leverage, but it does inflate D&A, which is the same D&A management adds back in full.

Goodwill of $265.7M plus $73.8M of net intangibles equals 26.3% of assets and 34.0% of equity. Impairments were $14.3M (2023) and $4.5M (2024) but essentially nil in 2025.

One item to monitor: the current allowance for credit losses was $37.5M against $77.1M of gross current student receivables, a **48.6% reserve rate**, while net student receivables rose to $39.6M from $27.2M and Q1 write-offs of $9.9M exceeded a $5.7M provision, drawing the total allowance down to $41.7M from $45.4M. The near-50% reserve rate is structural for unsecured lending to non-traditional students, and quarter-end timing is genuinely noisy given 90–120 day Title IV documentation cycles. A declining provision against rising gross receivables is nonetheless the configuration that precedes a catch-up charge.

FY2025: 4.1M shares repurchased for $120.8M at an average $29.17 (≈6.3% of the 64.8M basic weighted-average count), plus $36.9M of dividends and equivalents — $157.7M, or 70% of operating cash flow. ## Growth Analysis

Separate the revenue line from the earnings line, because they tell different stories.

**Revenue growth is low single digit and decelerating.** Organic FY2025 growth was approximately 2.5% ((CTU +$18.2M − AIUS $0.9M) ÷ the $670.4M ex-USAHS 2024 base). Q1 2026 consolidated revenue grew 4.1%, flattered by the pulled-forward AIUS session. Enrollment, the leading indicator for revenue two to four quarters out, decelerated from +7.3% to +1.1% between the December and March measurement dates, with CTU's growth rate falling by more than two-thirds (+6.6% → +1.9%) and AIUS turning negative (+11.2% → −2.2%). Those measurement dates are seasonally non-comparable and the company warns that academic-calendar changes affect quarterly comparability, so one reading is not a trend. But the direction is the wrong one going into the July 1 loan-limit change, and management's own outlook is qualitative — "full year adjusted operating income to be higher for 2026 as compared to 2025."

**Earnings growth in 2026 is real but mostly mechanical.** Consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of $3.11 against FY2025's $2.61 implies +19.2%. The quarterly build is Q1 actual $0.90 + Q2E $0.80 + Q3E $0.74, requiring ~$0.67 in Q4 versus $0.59 last year. The drivers are USAHS D&A rolloff, USAHS margin normalization from 2.0% toward the mid-teens, the legacy cost structure, a lower tax rate, and a 4.8% smaller share count — not volume. FY2027 consensus of $3.34 implies only +7.4%, which is closer to the sustainable rate once the USAHS inflection is lapped.

**Estimate revisions are positive but thin and single-sourced.** Current year +2.0% over 90 days, next year +2.1%, current quarter +5.3%; next quarter was cut 2.6% over 30 days. All from one analyst. This is nowhere near the revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings inflection, and the coverage depth means the consensus figures carry little independent information. The four-quarter beat record (+5.0%, +7.1%, +4.5%, +1.1%) suggests modest, consistent conservatism in guidance — useful for calibrating confidence in $3.11, not evidence of accelerating momentum.

**The 2027+ question is USAHS intake.** The Grad PLUS shock applies industry-wide, so competing public and nonprofit PT/OT/SLP students face the same $20,500/$100,000 caps, and competitor program supply may contract. Whether the cap binds harder at USAHS depends on its total cost of attendance relative to public alternatives — an input I could not verify, and the single most important unresolved variable in the forecast. The RISE CIP-code alignment provision also leaves a genuine path to reclassification before final regulations land later in 2026. Management's stated mitigation is informing prospective students about private financing alternatives, which is a price-and-terms question, not a solution.

Growth score of 4.8 reflects sub-3% organic revenue growth, decelerating enrollment, a 2026 EPS bridge built on non-recurring and mechanical components, and a named funding change hitting the one segment that was inflecting.

A low multiple on a business whose revenue base is subject to administrative removal is not automatically cheap. The board's own revealed preference is informative: heavy repurchase at a $29.17 average in FY2025, a near-pause at $8.1M in Q1 2026 with the stock in the mid-$30s. At $33.03 the stock sits between those two zones, which is roughly what "fairly valued with an option on regulatory clarity" looks like.

Valuation score 6.4: genuinely inexpensive on cash flow and asset backing, with a structural rather than sentiment-driven reason for the discount and no identified near-term re-rating catalyst beyond the resolution of regulatory uncertainty, which could resolve either way.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's own framing is candid. Public and nonprofit institutions face state-funding pressure, enrollment declines, deferred facilities maintenance, research obligations and tenure constraints that limit expansion, and some limit enrollment deliberately to preserve selectivity. But the filing also concedes that "competition from online programs has increased as these institutions have broadened their online offerings" and that the for-profit share of the market "remains relatively small."

The binding competitive issue is regulatory asymmetry, stated plainly in the filing: the 90-10 Rule "is an additional annual eligibility test that applies exclusively to for-profit sector institutions," and the Gainful Employment rule "is designed to primarily impose additional requirements on for-profit sector programs." The same asymmetry appears at state level — 2025 California legislation (not enacted) would have joined SARA while continuing to require BPPE registration specifically for out-of-state **proprietary** institutions, with public and nonprofit institutions receiving broader reciprocity. USAHS cannot use SARA at all because its main campus is in California, the only non-member state, so it maintains state-by-state licensure.

Trajectory by segment: CTU is holding and modestly gaining (segment margin 39.1% FY2025 → 41.9% Q1 2026, employer-sponsored channel at 38.3% of enrollments providing a lower-CAC B2B moat and 90-10 headroom). AIUS is contracting (revenue −0.4% FY2025, enrollment −2.2% at March), with the FY2025 shortfall attributed specifically to Trident University, which the government shutdown "disparately impacted" — a discrete military/federal-adjacent exposure to appropriations timing separate from Title IV policy. USAHS is the improving asset operationally and the exposed asset regulatorily.

The clearest competitive positive in the last twelve months is a balance-sheet arbitrage rather than an operating one: USAHS's standalone composite score under prior ownership was 0.5 for 2024, and in January 2026 the Department released its $20.5M letter of credit and returned it from Heightened Cash Monitoring 1 to the advance payment method, citing the financial strength of Perdoceo's consolidated statements. Perdoceo's 3.0 composite score converted a regulatory liability into an asset. That is a repeatable acquisition capability, and it is the strongest argument that management can deploy the net cash productively.

Competitive position score 5.0: a defensible niche with one real proprietary channel and a genuine regulatory-standing advantage, offset by 0.17% market share, expanding nonprofit online competition, a shrinking AIUS, and an asymmetric regulatory regime aimed at the sector.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Grad PLUS elimination, effective July 1, 2026 (highest conviction).** RISE consensus (11/6/25) does not classify PT, OT, SLP or nursing as professional; proposed regulations published 1/30/26 with final rules expected later in 2026. The 10-K states that if the classification is not revised, "students in these programs will be subject to lower borrowing limits, which could negatively impact enrollment." USAHS carries $57.1M of failed-sale-leaseback financing and $154.1M of undiscounted future lease payments, and is the segment where margin was just inflecting. The three-year grandfather for pre-7/1/26 borrowers means 2026 reported results can look clean while 2027–2029 intake absorbs the change. Mitigating: the cap applies to all institutions, so competitor supply may also contract, and the CIP-code alignment provision leaves a reclassification path. The company explicitly says it cannot predict the extent.

**2. Two active False Claims Act qui tam cases, both D. Colo., both unquantified with no recognized liability.** *US ex rel. Fiorisce LLC v. Perdoceo and CTU* (filed under seal 2/25/21, unsealed 7/18/23, amended 5/19/23 and again 5/19/25; DOJ declined to intervene 2/3/23) alleges violations relating to federal financial-aid credit-hour requirements in connection with the learning management system — i.e., it attacks the credit-hour accounting embedded in `intellipath`, the platform the company describes as its differentiator. *US ex rel. Peters* (filed 11/13/23; DOJ declination learned 11/11/25; amended complaint 1/8/26; motion to dismiss filed 3/16/26) alleges incentive-compensation and misrepresentation violations. Treble damages and civil penalties are sought in both. Total accrued legal fees and settlements were $1.7M at 3/31/26. Government non-intervention materially reduces, without eliminating, the mandatory financial-responsibility trigger that a qui tam judgment with federal intervention would create.

**3. Cohort default rates are set to deteriorate from an artificial base.** Official three-year CDRs were 0% at all three institutions for the 2020, 2021 and 2022 cohorts — an artifact of CARES Act forbearance. The 10-K expects the 2023 cohort under 10% but explicitly warns the 2024 cohort "may be higher than historical levels," citing the September 2024 on-ramp expiry, SAVE-plan litigation forbearance, servicer transitions, and limited servicer outreach to delinquent borrowers. Thresholds: >10% triggers first-year disbursement delays (already standard practice here); >30% for three consecutive years or >40% in one year ends Title IV eligibility.

**4. 90-10 Rule.** Approximately 76% of aggregate tuition cash receipts came from Title IV in FY2025 (77% in FY2024), with preliminary FY2025 90-10 rates compliant and improved at all three institutions, audit ongoing. The American Rescue Plan expanded the numerator to include all federal assistance including DoD and VA benefits — a structural headwind for a military-serving operator. Favorable: a Department interpretive rule dated 7/7/25 allows non-federal distance-education revenue to count as non-federal, and the Reconciliation Act now permits institutions to self-impose lower program-level loan limits.

**5. Gainful Employment / earnings-premium accountability, unresolved and dual-track.** The 2023 GE rule was upheld 10/2/25 (N.D. Tex.) and is on appeal to the Fifth Circuit; no initial GE/FVT rates have ever been published and no enforcement has occurred. The AHEAD consensus (1/9/26) would eliminate Debt-to-Earnings and shorten ineligibility to two years, but adds a new administrative-capability standard requiring that at least half of aid recipients and half of Title IV funds not come from "low-earning outcome programs." The company cannot assess the impact because it lacks the underlying earnings data.

**6. Governance and disclosure items.** The proxy identifies Adjusted Operating Income as the **sole** performance measure — an EBITDA-like, absolute-dollar, non-per-share metric that credits acquisitions and scale while giving no direct weight to per-share outcomes or returns on capital, which is a live alignment question for a company holding $612.6M of net cash. The proxy's company-selected-measure values ($247.1M for 2025, $194.6M for 2024) exceed the 10-K's adjusted operating income ($237.6M, $188.9M) by about 4%; the plausible explanation is a retained legal-fee add-back, but I did not read the reconciling footnote and this is a question for management, not a finding. Other unresolved items: 8-K contents for 3/13/26 and 5/27/26 (Item 5.02 director/officer changes) were not read; Item 1A was not read directly, so I cannot identify which risk factors were added or modified versus FY2024; and the July 15, 2026 decline (open $36.12, close $32.34 on 2.31M shares, roughly 3.6x average volume) has no identified cause in the available record.

### Options Market Signal

The positioning is unusually loud for a $2B education name and points at the print. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 65.2% against 35.0% one-year realized — a 30.2-point premium at the 85th percentile of the current chain — with a backwardated term structure (65.2% at 14 days, 51.8% at 42 days, 45.9% at 105 days) and a ±12.0% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Put/call open interest is 3.91 and the one-month put/call skew is 8.5% (elevated put demand), while the three- and six-month skews are roughly flat at −0.1% and −1.6%. The reading: hedging concentrated in the front month around the Q2 report and the July 1 loan-limit transition, with no comparable stress priced further out. Since the June 17 snapshot, ATM IV collapsed from 171.1% to 65.2% on a 4.0% price gain while put/call OI held at 3.89 → 3.91 — the volatility level normalized but the protective positioning did not unwind. For a HOLD this corroborates: the market is pricing a two-sided event, and it is not the setup in which to add exposure hours beforehand. Short interest is 6.7% of float at 4.7 days to cover — moderate, neither crowded nor an all-clear.

Risk score 4.0: multiple named, dated, quantified regulatory and litigation exposures with asymmetric downside and no recognized liability, against a valuation that discounts some but not all of them.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $44 (+33%)

Final RISE regulations reclassify PT/OT/SLP under the professional-program definition via CIP-code alignment, or private financing fills the gap at acceptable terms, and USAHS intake holds. USAHS margin sustains the Q1 pace (14.7%, ~31% segment EBITDA) as acquired-intangible amortization continues rolling off, validating a $137.8M purchase price against roughly $33.5M of segment operating EBITDA (~4.1x). CTU's employer-sponsored channel (38.3% of enrollments) continues gaining, AIUS stabilizes as the Trident federal-appropriations drag laps, and the March enrollment reading proves to be calendar distortion. The company executes the remaining $91.9M of the buyback (4.4% of shares) at current prices, adding roughly 4 points to per-share earnings on top of the $0.60 dividend. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $3.40–3.50; a 12.5x multiple plus retained net cash gets to $44, which is also where a zero-growth FCF perpetuity at a 10% discount rate lands ($216.6M ÷ 0.10 + $612.6M net cash = $44.3/share). The deregulatory policy posture — the ten-year delay of the 2022 borrower-defense rules to July 1, 2035, the July 2025 90-10 interpretive rule, AHEAD's elimination of Debt-to-Earnings — proves net favorable.

### Bear Case — $25 (−24%)

Final regulations confirm the exclusion of PT/OT/SLP/nursing. USAHS new-student starts fall materially through the 2027 and 2028 intake cycles as the three-year grandfather rolls off, private credit proves unavailable at acceptable terms for a high-cost campus-based clinical doctorate, and the segment's operating leverage reverses toward single-digit margins against $154.1M of undiscounted campus lease obligations. CTU's deceleration to +1.9% continues and AIUS's −2.2% extends as nonprofit online capacity expands. The 2024 cohort default rates print materially above historical levels, pulling one or more institutions above 10% and inviting scrutiny. Either qui tam survives its motion to dismiss and moves toward discovery on a treble-damages theory attacking `intellipath` credit-hour accounting, forcing an accrual against a $1.7M base. Operating income falls toward $170M with FCF near $170M; a 6x EV/FCF multiple plus net cash gives ($1,020M + $680M − $67M) ÷ 62.7M = **$26**, and an adverse litigation development takes it below the $26.66 52-week low.

The bear case does not require a business collapse; it requires that a named federal funding mechanism be removed from 19% of revenue on a schedule already in statute. That is why the multiple is where it is.

## Conclusion

Perdoceo is a well-capitalized, high-return, low-growth cash generator trading at 6.7x EV/free cash flow with 29.6% of its market capitalization in net cash, roughly 30% below its peer group multiple. The financial quality is real and independently verifiable: $216.6M of FCF on $846.1M of revenue, 0.07x interest-bearing debt to equity, a 5.06 current ratio, a maximum 3.0 Department of Education composite score, and a share count down 4.8% year over year. The USAHS acquisition looks well-priced at roughly 4.1x segment operating EBITDA, and Perdoceo's consolidated balance sheet unlocked a $20.5M letter-of-credit release and removal from Heightened Cash Monitoring that the prior owner (0.5 standalone composite score) could not achieve.

Against that, the earnings base is exposed to a dated statutory change. Grad PLUS elimination on July 1, 2026 removes the primary funding source for many students in the segment representing 19% of revenue, under consensus language that excludes precisely USAHS's PT, OT, SLP and nursing programs. Organic revenue growth is roughly 2.5%, enrollment growth decelerated from +7.3% to +1.1% between measurement dates with AIUS turning negative, the 2026 EPS bridge leans on D&A rolloff, a below-guidance tax rate and buyback, two unreserved treble-damages qui tam cases remain live with one attacking the credit-hour accounting inside the company's own differentiating platform, and cohort default rates are set to rise from an artificial 0%.

The score trajectory (6.3 → 7.1 → 7.0 → 6.6 → 6.0, now 5.7) is declining, and the current data confirm rather than reverse that trend: enrollment momentum broke, the funding change moved from prospective to effective, and the buyback slowed by roughly 73% versus the FY2025 quarterly pace immediately after a larger authorization. Holders should stay for the cash yield and the option on favorable final regulations. New capital has a defined, near-dated set of things to observe first: the current enrollment reading, the Q3 print under the new loan limits, and the final RISE text. Those cost little to wait for and would convert a cheap-with-a-reason multiple into either a demonstrable mispricing or a confirmed value trap.
