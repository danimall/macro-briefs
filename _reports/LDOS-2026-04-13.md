## Executive Summary

Leidos Holdings is the largest IT services provider to the US government by revenue, with $17.2B in FY2025 sales, 87% derived from federal contracts. The company operates across defense technology, digital modernization, and energy infrastructure, three sectors with structural tailwinds strengthening in the current geopolitical environment. The stock trades at approximately 12x forward earnings and 14x trailing, a meaningful discount to the broader technology sector, with $49B in backlog providing nearly three years of revenue visibility.

FY2025 demonstrated the operating model's capacity for margin expansion: operating margins expanded 130 bps to 12.3%, free cash flow grew 26% to $1.63B (104% non-GAAP net income conversion), and the company returned $1.1B to shareholders through buybacks and dividends. The 1.3x book-to-bill ratio, combined with pending awards on programs like the $151B SHIELD IDIQ (Golden Dome) and $25B Defense Microelectronics contract, provides forward revenue visibility that is unusually strong even by defense contractor standards.

Key risks center on government budget dependency (87% of revenue), the recently completed $2.4B Entrust acquisition increasing pro forma leverage to 2.6x, and the "Prioritizing the Warfighter" executive order that introduces new regulatory constraints on capital allocation and executive compensation. Health & Civil segment margin normalization (from 23.7% toward a "robust above 20%" target) and a tripling of capex to $350M in FY2026 create near-term earnings headwinds. These risks are quantifiable and manageable, and the valuation provides adequate margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Leidos operates through four reporting segments aligned with its NorthStar 2030 strategy:

**National Security & Digital (44% of revenue, $7.6B):** The core franchise. Digital modernization, mission software, multi-domain solutions, cyber operations, and intelligence support. 10.0% operating margin. Recent wins include a $2.2B Air Force passive radar contract and $455M Cloud One Next program.

**Health & Civil (30%, $5.1B):** Managed health services (notably VA disability exams), FAA air traffic control, DoE nuclear/environmental cleanup. The highest-margin segment at 23.7% operating margin, though management has guided for modest contraction in FY2026 with the entry of a fourth vendor.

**Commercial & International (13%, $2.3B):** Energy infrastructure (utility grid engineering, grid modernization), security detection products, and international defense services. 7.2% operating margin, recovering from prior write-downs. The Entrust acquisition ($2.4B, completed March 30, 2026) adds 3,100 employees and substantially scales this segment's energy engineering capabilities.

**Defense Systems (13%, $2.2B):** Maritime/undersea autonomy, space/hypersonic systems, integrated air and missile defense, airborne ISR. 7.2% operating margin. Positioned on Golden Dome (SHIELD) and defense microelectronics programs.

The workforce of approximately 47,000 employees includes 53% with US security clearances and 38% with STEM degrees, creating meaningful barriers to entry that are difficult to replicate.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2025 revenue reached $17.2B, up 3.1% year-over-year. Normalizing for a 6-week government shutdown and a 53rd week in FY2024, organic growth was approximately 4%. Revenue growth has decelerated from 7.9% in FY2024 to 3.1%, but operating performance has been accelerating:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $15.4B | $16.7B | $17.2B |
| Operating Income | $0.62B* | $1.83B | $2.11B |
| Operating Margin | 4.0%* | 11.0% | 12.3% |
| Net Income | $199M* | $1.25B | $1.45B |
| Non-GAAP EPS | — | $10.21 | $11.99 |
| FCF | — | $1.30B | $1.63B |

*FY2023 distorted by $596M goodwill impairment.

The margin expansion is genuine: adjusted EBITDA margin reached 14.1% in FY2025, up 120 bps, driven by mix shift toward higher-value technology work and operational efficiency improvements. The Health & Civil segment at 23.7% operating margin is the primary profit engine, though this level is likely unsustainable as management has explicitly guided for normalization.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

Free cash flow of $1.63B (9.5% FCF margin) with 104% non-GAAP net income conversion demonstrates earnings quality. Operating cash flow has grown from $1.19B to $1.75B over three years.

Capital allocation in FY2025 was shareholder-friendly: $900M in buybacks (reducing share count 4.4%) and $211M in dividends, totaling $1.1B returned to shareholders. This trajectory faces pressure in FY2026 from (1) capex tripling to $350M for classified facility upgrades and production capacity, (2) debt service on the Entrust financing, and (3) the "Prioritizing the Warfighter" executive order's potential restrictions on buybacks during periods of contract underperformance.

### Balance Sheet

Pre-Entrust: $1.1B cash, $4.6B gross debt, 1.74x debt-to-equity, current ratio 1.7x. Post-Entrust (pro forma): approximately 2.6x leverage, incorporating $1.39B in new notes and a $1.4B bridge facility. The revolving credit facility was upsized from $1.0B to $1.5B in February 2026. Altman Z-Score of 2.82 places the company in the "grey zone," driven by the leveraged capital structure rather than operational weakness.

Goodwill of $6.3B (47% of total assets) remains a concentration risk. The $596M SES impairment in FY2023 demonstrated this is a live risk; the SES reporting unit still carries $311M in goodwill, and ongoing challenges in security detection products keep this as a monitoring item.

### Earnings Quality

Four consecutive quarterly beats with an average surprise of 12.3%. The progression — 16.5%, 18.5%, 10.3%, 3.8% — shows decreasing surprise magnitude, typical as analyst estimates catch up to operational improvement. Q4 still beat, albeit by a smaller margin, suggesting continued execution.

R&D spending increased to $187M (1.1% of revenue) from $128M in FY2023, a directionally positive signal for a company repositioning toward higher-value technology differentiation, though still low relative to pure technology peers.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Growth Drivers

**Backlog conversion:** The $49.0B total backlog (2.8x revenue) with 1.3x book-to-bill provides a strong revenue foundation. The $9.7B funded backlog alone represents approximately 56% of FY2026 guided revenue.

**Pending mega-awards:** The $151B SHIELD IDIQ (Golden Dome missile defense) and $25B Defense Microelectronics contract are not yet in backlog but represent potentially transformative program wins. Active US naval operations and heightened threat posture have increased these programs' urgency.

**Entrust integration:** The $2.4B acquisition adds energy infrastructure engineering at a time when grid modernization is a multi-decade secular growth opportunity. US data center power demand, renewable integration, and grid hardening create addressable market expansion well beyond traditional government IT services. Entrust results are excluded from initial FY2026 guidance, providing upside optionality once integrated.

**FY2026 guidance:** Revenue of $17.5B–$17.9B (up to 4% growth), non-GAAP EPS of $12.05–$12.45, operating cash flow of approximately $1.75B. Management expects H2 FY2026 acceleration as delayed awards flow through.

### Medium-Term Growth Trajectory

Analyst estimates show next-year EPS at $13.21, up 2.8% over 30 days and 2.5% over 90 days — positive revision momentum. Current-year estimates at $12.31 are up 0.8% over 30 days but down 0.4% over 90 days, suggesting the near-term is roughly stable while the medium-term is improving. This revision pattern is consistent with management's H2-weighted growth guidance.

The NorthStar 2030 strategy targets five growth pillars: space & maritime, energy infrastructure, digital modernization & cyber, mission software, and managed health services. The Kudu Dynamics ($293M, cyber) and Entrust ($2.4B, energy) acquisitions demonstrate active execution against this framework.

### Secular Tailwinds

The defense spending environment has shifted structurally. Active US naval operations, elevated threat levels across multiple theaters, and bipartisan support for defense budgets exceeding $1.5T create sustained demand for Leidos's core capabilities. Programs like Golden Dome, cyber operations, and space-based ISR are aligned with priority spending categories regardless of administration.

Energy infrastructure represents Leidos's most significant diversification opportunity. US high-voltage transmission construction has collapsed to approximately 322 miles per year against a requirement orders of magnitude higher. Grid modernization spending is supported by electrification trends, AI data center proliferation, and energy security concerns. Entrust positions Leidos to capture this secular demand.

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $156 (current price per analyst data), Leidos trades at:
- 12.0x forward P/E (on $12.31 current-year consensus)
- 14.0x trailing P/E
- 11.8x next-year P/E (on $13.21)
- ~12x FCF (on $1.63B FY2025 FCF against ~$19.7B market cap)

The peer comparison provided (Figma at -10.1x P/E) is not meaningful given Figma is a pre-profit software company. More appropriate comparisons are defense/IT services peers: SAIC (~14x forward), BAH (~22x forward), CACI (~16x forward), and GD IT/Mission Systems segments. Leidos trades at a discount to all direct peers.

The discount is partially justified by: (1) higher government revenue concentration (87%) creating budget risk, (2) post-Entrust leverage at 2.6x, and (3) Health segment margin normalization. However, the magnitude of the discount appears excessive given: (1) superior backlog visibility ($49B, 2.8x revenue), (2) consistent earnings beats (4/4 quarters), (3) strong FCF conversion (104%), and (4) pending mega-program positioning (Golden Dome, defense microelectronics).

Applying 14x to the $13.21 next-year EPS estimate yields approximately $185. Applying a more generous 16x (still below peers like BAH at 22x or CACI at 16x) yields approximately $211. The analyst consensus mean target of $205 and median of $204.50 appear reasonable, implying 31% upside from current levels.

The $91M FY2026 tax benefit from OBBBA legislation provides an additional undiscounted boost to near-term cash flow.

## Competitive Landscape

Leidos's competitive position benefits from three structural advantages:

**Clearance workforce:** 53% of employees holding US security clearances creates a barrier that cannot be replicated quickly. The clearance process takes 12-18 months for Secret-level and longer for Top Secret/SCI, and the pipeline of cleared personnel is constrained. This is particularly relevant as the 10-K acknowledges DoD is increasingly turning to commercial contractors and using Other Transaction Authorities (OTAs); Leidos's hybrid model (combining commercial technology integration with cleared personnel) positions it to compete across both traditional and non-traditional procurement channels.

**Incumbency on critical programs:** FAA air traffic control, VA disability health services, DOE nuclear cleanup, and intelligence community mission systems represent programs where switching costs are high and institutional knowledge creates deep competitive moats. Contract recompetes still carry risk, but incumbent win rates in these categories typically exceed 70%.

**Scale and diversification:** At $17.2B in revenue across 20+ agencies, Leidos has the scale to absorb individual program losses and the breadth to avoid single-contract dependency. No single contract represents more than 5% of revenue.

Competitive threats come from two directions: (1) traditional peers like BAH, SAIC, and CACI on government IT services, and (2) commercial technology companies increasingly competing for modernization contracts through OTAs. Leidos's NorthStar 2030 strategy explicitly addresses the latter by positioning as the integration layer between commercial technology and classified environments.

## Risk Assessment

### Government Budget and DOGE (HIGH)

With 87% of revenue from US government sources and approximately 49% from DoD/Intelligence Community, Leidos has existential exposure to federal spending decisions. FY2025 Q4 was impacted by a 6-week government shutdown. DHS appropriations were not passed, and DHS was shut down as of February 2026. DOGE-style efficiency initiatives, including Defense Secretary Hegseth's announced $5.1B in DoD contract cuts, create near-term uncertainty. Management has navigated these challenges to date, but the risk persists.

### Entrust Integration (MODERATE-HIGH)

The $2.4B acquisition increases pro forma leverage to approximately 2.6x and introduces meaningful integration risk. Entrust (3,100 employees, utility engineering focus) represents a cultural and operational expansion beyond Leidos's core government IT franchise. The bridge facility requires permanent refinancing within 364 days. Management excluded Entrust from initial FY2026 guidance, limiting investor visibility.

### "Prioritizing the Warfighter" Executive Order (MODERATE)

This executive order introduces restrictions on stock buybacks and dividends during periods of contract underperformance, restricts executive incentive compensation contingent on delivery metrics, and requires board-approved remediation plans within 15 days if the Secretary of Defense deems performance insufficient. This regulatory constraint could limit capital allocation flexibility and complicate talent retention.

### Health Segment Margin Normalization (MODERATE)

Entry of a fourth vendor and phase transitions are expected to compress margins, with management guiding to "robust profitability above 20%." A 370 bps decline to 20% on $5.1B of revenue would reduce operating income by approximately $189M, roughly 9% of consolidated operating income. This is quantifiable and likely already partially reflected in FY2026 guidance.

### Fixed-Price Contract Exposure (MODERATE)

43% of revenue comes from firm-fixed-price contracts, exposing Leidos to cost overrun risk. The 10-K notes that "modest annual price escalations in our bids for multi-year work may be insufficient" in the current inflationary environment. With oil above $100 and potential for further supply chain disruptions, cost inflation on hardware-intensive programs (radars, autonomous systems) is a tangible concern.

### Goodwill Concentration (LOW-MODERATE)

$6.3B in goodwill (47% of total assets) with demonstrated impairment risk (FY2023 $596M write-down). The SES reporting unit's remaining $311M in goodwill requires continued monitoring.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($210-235)

Defense spending enters a sustained upcycle supported by active military operations, bipartisan budget support, and Golden Dome/SHIELD program funding. Leidos wins material task orders on the $151B SHIELD IDIQ and $25B Defense Microelectronics contract, driving book-to-bill above 1.3x. Entrust integration succeeds, adding $500M+ in energy infrastructure revenue at accretive margins. Health segment margins stabilize above 22%. Multiple re-rates to 16-18x forward earnings as the market recognizes Leidos's hybrid defense/infrastructure positioning. At 18x on $13.21 next-year EPS: $238.

### Bear Case ($125-140)

DOGE-driven budget cuts materially impact DoD IT and digital modernization spending, particularly affecting the National Security & Digital segment. Entrust integration stumbles, margins compress, and the bridge facility refinancing occurs at unfavorable terms. Health segment margins fall below 20% as the fourth vendor captures meaningful share. The executive order restricts buybacks, removing a key shareholder return mechanism. Multiple compresses to 10x on reduced earnings estimates. At 10x on $11.50 (bear EPS): $115.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $200. Calculated as 15x next-year EPS of $13.21 = $198, rounded to $200. This represents a modest re-rating from the current ~12x forward multiple toward the low end of defense IT services peer range (SAIC 14x, CACI 16x), justified by the backlog quality, book-to-bill, and accelerating earnings growth.

**Bull case target:** $235. 18x on $13.21 = $238. Requires Golden Dome/SHIELD awards flowing through backlog and successful Entrust integration.

**Bear case target:** $130. 10x on $13.00 = $130. Represents a 17% decline from current levels.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalyst dates: Q1 FY2026 earnings (first quarter reflecting Entrust, expected late May/early June 2026), SHIELD IDIQ task order announcements (H2 2026), and FY2027 defense budget passage.

**Upside/downside from current (~$156):** Base case +28%, bull case +51%, bear case -17%. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.6:1 on base case, 3.0:1 on bull case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Book-to-bill falls below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters, indicating backlog erosion
2. Entrust bridge facility refinancing occurs at spreads >300 bps above existing debt, signaling credit market stress
3. Health & Civil operating margin falls below 18%, indicating structural deterioration beyond normal normalization

## Conclusion

Leidos offers a compelling risk/reward at approximately 12x forward earnings, supported by $49B in backlog, consistent earnings execution (4/4 beats), and positioning on transformative defense programs during a period of elevated military spending. The Entrust acquisition adds energy infrastructure diversification with multi-decade secular tailwinds, though it temporarily increases leverage and integration complexity.

The stock's discount to defense IT services peers reflects legitimate concerns about government budget dependency and DOGE risk, but the magnitude appears excessive given backlog quality, FCF conversion, and pending program awards. With analyst consensus targeting approximately $205 (31% upside) and estimate revisions trending positive, the setup favors patient accumulation.

**Recommendation: BUY** at current levels around $156, with a 12-18 month base case target of $200.
