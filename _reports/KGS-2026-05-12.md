## Executive Summary

Kodiak Gas Services is the leading provider of large horsepower contract compression infrastructure in the U.S., operating ~4.5 million fleet horsepower at 97.7% utilization under long-term, fixed-revenue contracts with investment-grade upstream and midstream customers. CEO Robert McKee describes the market as "uncharted territory": 180+ week lead times for new large gas compression engines create an effective supply constraint that gives incumbents meaningful pricing power and deters new entrants.

The stock has appreciated 107% over the past year, driven by legitimate fundamental improvement: 2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $715M (54.7% margin), the $675M DPS acquisition completed April 1, 2026, and raised full-year 2026 guidance of $820-860M. EPS estimates for the current quarter have been revised up +55% in the last 30 days. The Q1 2026 beat ($0.59 vs. $0.54 expected) and raised guidance triggered an immediate 8% move.

The stock now trades at $74.50, above the analyst consensus mean of $66.75 and approaching the highest price target of $82. At ~28x 2026E EPS ($2.68) and ~10.9-11.5x EV/2026E EBITDA, the valuation is fair for a high-quality infrastructure business with structural tailwinds, but does not offer asymmetric upside from current levels. The combination of 3.5-4.0x post-acquisition leverage, an open DOJ/FCPA investigation, DPS integration risk, and proximity to the top of the analyst target range creates a risk/reward profile that favors patience over chasing momentum.

## Company Overview

Kodiak Gas Services, headquartered in The Woodlands, Texas, IPO'd in July 2023 and has rapidly scaled through organic growth and acquisition to become the dominant U.S. provider of large horsepower (>1,000 HP) contract compression. The company's fleet of 4,736 compression units serves primarily Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale operators (82.8% of assets) under take-or-pay contracts with 3-7 year terms and annual inflation escalators.

**Segment breakdown:**
- Contract Services (~90% of revenue): Fixed monthly fee for company-owned and customer-owned compression. Customers bear fuel costs.
- Other Services (~10%): Station construction, maintenance, parts, freight — cross-sold with the core offering.

The April 2026 DPS acquisition positions Kodiak in distributed power generation, targeting 300-500 MW/year through 2030 to serve data centers and other behind-the-meter applications.

## Competitive Landscape

Kodiak occupies the strongest competitive position in U.S. contract compression:

1. **Supply scarcity moat:** 180+ week lead times for large HP engines create an effective capacity constraint that cannot be solved within 3-4 years. This is the company's primary moat.
2. **Scale advantage:** ~4.5M fleet HP with standardized equipment enables maintenance efficiencies and fleet optimization unmatched by smaller operators.
3. **Customer quality:** Top 4 customers (~32% of revenue) are investment-grade S&P 500 companies. Default risk is minimal.
4. **Contract structure:** Fixed-revenue, take-or-pay contracts with inflation escalators and 3-7 year terms provide exceptional revenue visibility. Only 9% of HP on month-to-month terms.
5. **Geographic positioning:** 82.8% in Permian/Eagle Ford — the lowest-cost, highest-activity basins — means Kodiak's fleet is deployed where production will grow first and decline last.

The DPS acquisition adds a second growth vertical, and the structural tightness of the compression market shows no signs of easing given lead times.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **DOJ/FCPA investigation (material tail risk):** Payments to FTO-associated officials in Mexico, inherited from the CSI acquisition. The company self-reported and divested Mexico operations, but the investigation is ongoing. Potential penalties range from immaterial to hundreds of millions. This is genuinely unquantifiable and unpriced.

2. **Post-acquisition leverage:** ~3.5-4.0x net debt/EBITDA in a rising-rate environment. While the March 2026 refinancing locked in favorable rates, total debt service remains ~$200M annually. A prolonged downturn in natural gas production could stress coverage.

3. **DPS execution risk:** The $675M acquisition enters a new market (distributed power). Management's 300-500 MW/year target requires building capabilities, securing customers, and executing on infrastructure buildout. Track record in compression is strong, but this is a different business.

4. **Geographic concentration:** 82.8% in two basins. Any regional disruption (pipeline constraints, regulatory action, production curtailments) has outsized impact.

5. **Texas sales tax liability:** $102.3M accrued contingent liability, up from $70.1M in 2024. An ongoing drag on cash flow.

6. **Customer concentration:** Top customer >10% of revenue. Loss or bankruptcy of a major customer would be material.

## Conclusion

Kodiak Gas Services is a high-quality infrastructure business operating in a structurally favorable market. The 97.7% utilization, 180+ week equipment lead times, and fixed-revenue contract structure create genuine competitive advantages that deserve premium valuation. The DPS acquisition adds credible optionality in power infrastructure.

However, the stock's 107% appreciation over 12 months has moved it from materially undervalued to fully valued. At $74.50, KGS trades above analyst consensus and within 10% of the highest target. The remaining upside under the bull case (+14-21%) does not adequately compensate for the downside risks: an open DOJ investigation, 3.5-4.0x leverage, DPS execution uncertainty, and valuation stretched 20-30% above compression peer multiples. The options market's extreme near-term implied volatility (59.3% vs. 33.8% realized) further suggests the stock is in a momentum-driven phase where position sizing discipline is essential.

The growth thesis is intact and accelerating — EPS estimates are rising rapidly, the compression market remains structurally tight, and DPS could prove transformative. This warrants maintaining exposure but not adding at current levels. A pullback to the $60-65 range would restore the risk/reward to BUY territory.
