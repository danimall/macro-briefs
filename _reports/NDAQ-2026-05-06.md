## Executive Summary

Nasdaq is completing a multi-year transformation from a pure exchange operator into a diversified financial technology platform. Financial Technology ARR grew to $1.82B (+18% YoY) in Q1 2026, Capital Markets Technology organic revenue grew 20% with bookings doubling, and the company generated $2.0B in FCF during FY2025 while returning $701M to shareholders in Q1 2026 alone. The stock trades at 22.7x forward earnings ($3.93 FY2026E), a 27% discount to the peer median P/E of approximately 31x, despite comparable or superior growth metrics and significantly better FCF conversion.

The key investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) the market continues to value Nasdaq primarily as an exchange (23% of revenue) rather than as the enterprise software/data platform it has become (77% of revenue), (2) structural tailwinds from regulatory complexity, financial crime proliferation, and passive investing secular growth support mid-teens organic ARR growth through 2027+, and (3) a potential shift toward a higher structural volatility regime — including the possibility of reduced Fed forward guidance under incoming Chair Kevin Warsh, geopolitical binary outcomes, and credit stress — could benefit both Market Services volumes and FinTech demand for risk/compliance tools.

The primary risks are SEC access fee cap compliance (November 2026), continued U.S. trading market share erosion, and $14.4B in goodwill from the Adenza acquisition. These are manageable given the company's diversification, and the market share loss in trading is occurring in what has become a minority revenue segment.

## Company Overview

Nasdaq operates across three segments that collectively generated $5.25B in net revenue during FY2025:

**Capital Access Platforms (41% of net revenue):** Listing services for approximately 4,570 companies on The Nasdaq Stock Market (as of March 31, 2026), Nasdaq-100 Index licensing ($882B ETP AUM tracking), market data, eVestment analytics, and corporate governance solutions. ARR of $1.37B growing 9% YoY.

**Financial Technology (35% of net revenue):** Verafin (financial crime management for 2,750+ banks), AxiomSL (regulatory reporting covering 170+ regulators), and Calypso (capital markets trading platform within the Capital Markets Technology subdivision). Nasdaq's broader market-technology and client ecosystem serves 135+ markets and regulators globally. ARR of $1.82B growing 18% YoY.

**Market Services (23% of net revenue):** Six U.S. options exchanges (29.1% combined share in FY2025), three U.S. cash equity exchanges (14.2% matched share in FY2025), plus Nordic/Baltic exchange operations. This segment is volume-sensitive and benefits directly from elevated market volatility.

The November 2023 Adenza acquisition ($10.5B) added AxiomSL and Calypso, materially expanding the FinTech segment and creating a front-to-back technology platform for banks and asset managers.

## Growth Analysis

Nasdaq's growth profile splits into two distinct components:

**High-Growth FinTech (35% of revenue, 18% ARR growth):** Verafin serves 2,750+ banks with AI-powered fraud/AML detection, growing 21% CAGR. AxiomSL addresses escalating regulatory complexity across 170+ jurisdictions. Calypso is winning enterprise capital markets deals at an accelerating pace (bookings doubled in Q1 2026). Financial Crime Management Technology revenue reached $93M in Q1 2026 (vs. $77M in Q1 2025), with ARR of $344M (vs. $295M).

Key growth drivers:
- 500+ clients now using AI agents in production (Verafin)
- Cloud-based ACV bookings at 80% of total — higher lifetime value and stickier
- Cross-sell opportunity across ~4,570 listed companies and 135+ marketplace/regulator clients
- Regulatory complexity is a permanent tailwind (DORA, Basel IV, SEC modernization)

**Moderate-Growth Index/Data (41% of revenue, 9% ARR growth):** Steady compounder driven by passive investing secular shift. $882B in tracking AUM with $99B net inflows in FY2025. New partnerships expand distribution. Private Capital Indexes launched (14,000+ funds, $11.4T AUM) — extending the franchise into private markets.

**Market Services (23%, volume-dependent):** Benefits from structural volatility increase. If the incoming Fed leadership reduces or eliminates forward guidance — as Kevin Warsh has suggested — this would likely elevate rate uncertainty and trading volumes. U.S. options ADV grew from 40.4M (2023) to 55.8M (2025). The 23/5 trading approval (launching December 2026) creates incremental volume opportunity.

**Consensus estimates:** FY2026E EPS $3.93 (+13% YoY), FY2027E $4.42 (+12.5%). Revisions are modestly positive (+1.1% over 90 days for FY2026E, +1.5% for FY2027E). These are not dramatic estimate revisions, but the stability is worth registering given macro uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

Nasdaq occupies a unique position as the only company simultaneously operating major exchanges, licensing exchange technology to competitors, running one of the world's most recognized market indexes, and selling enterprise financial crime/regulatory technology.

**FinTech competitive position is strengthening:** Calypso bookings doubled in Q1 2026. Verafin has 2,750+ bank clients with AI agents in production. AxiomSL covers 170+ regulators. The combination creates a compliance/technology platform that individual competitors (NICE Actimize, FIS, Temenos) cannot replicate.

**Trading competitive position has recently stabilized:** U.S. options share declined from 31.0% (2023) to 29.1% (FY2025), and cash equities from 16.5% (2023) to 14.2% (FY2025). CBOE, MEMX, and dark pools have been taking share. However, Q1 2026 data showed a rebound: U.S. equity options matched share improved to 30.1% (vs. 29.1% in Q1 2025) and U.S. cash equities matched share improved to 15.1% (vs. 14.6% in Q1 2025). Market Services net revenue grew 13% YoY to $317M in Q1 2026. The multi-year decline occurred against surging industry volumes (55.8M options ADV vs. 40.4M two years ago), meaning absolute revenue grew even as annual share declined.

**Index franchise is defensible and growing:** The Nasdaq-100 has no direct substitute as the global tech/growth benchmark. Record $99B net inflows in FY2025. New partnerships expand distribution without cannibalization.

Competitive pressure exists in the segment generating 23% of revenue (Market Services), while the company is gaining share in the segments generating 77% of revenue (FinTech + Capital Access).

## Risk Assessment

**SEC Access Fee Cap (Moderate — November 2026):** The SEC adopted Regulation NMS amendments significantly reducing fees exchanges can charge for access to quoted liquidity, including a reduction to $0.001 per share for protected quotations. The SEC granted temporary exemptive relief extending compliance until the first business day of November 2026. This compresses Market Services margins directly. However: (a) the segment is 23% of revenue, (b) all competitors face the same constraint, and (c) Nasdaq has time to adapt pricing structures. Estimated net revenue impact: approximately $30–50M annually (1–2% of total net revenue), though actual impact depends on fee/rebate restructuring and volume response.

**Market Share Erosion (Low-Moderate):** Multi-year trading share losses are real, though Q1 2026 showed stabilization/improvement. The losses are occurring in the context of volume growth and a strategic pivot toward higher-margin technology revenue. The risk is that volume growth stalls simultaneously with share losses.

**Goodwill ($14.4B) Impairment Risk (Low Probability / High Impact):** FinTech growth would need to materially deteriorate (below 10% ARR growth sustained for multiple quarters) to trigger concern. Current 18% growth provides substantial buffer.

**Leverage (Declining Risk):** Net debt/EBITDA ~3x is above Nasdaq's historical target but below the initial 4x post-Adenza level. Active paydown trajectory ($826M in FY2025) plus ~$2B annual FCF generation provide clear visibility to sub-2.5x by late 2027.

**AWS Dependency:** An October 2025 outage affected certain non-trading offerings. Full cloud migration of exchange infrastructure introduces operational risk, though it also enables 23/5 trading and global scalability.

## Conclusion

Nasdaq at $89 presents a compelling risk/reward for a 12-18 month holding period. The company is growing organically at 13%, expanding margins, generating ~$2B in annual FCF, aggressively buying back shares, and trades at a significant discount to slower-growing peers. The FinTech transformation is substantive: $1.82B in ARR growing 18% commands premium multiples that the blended company valuation does not yet reflect.

A potential shift toward a higher structural volatility regime could benefit both the Market Services and FinTech segments simultaneously: higher volumes for trading, and higher demand for risk management and compliance technology. However, this remains a scenario rather than a certainty, as Warsh's confirmation and policy decisions are still pending. The declining score trajectory from prior reports (7.1 → 6.7 → 6.6) reflected legitimate concerns about market share erosion and access fee risk, but Q1 2026 results demonstrating 13% organic growth, 50%+ FinTech booking acceleration, margin expansion, and stabilizing trading market share provide concrete evidence that the growth engine is overcoming these headwinds.

The access fee cap is a manageable regulatory headwind affecting 23% of revenue, and trading share losses are occurring in what is increasingly a minority contributor to the business. The stock warrants accumulation at $89 with a base case target of $107.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrected peer median P/E (~31x vs. previously stated 33x) reduces the headline fair-value estimate from ~$130 to ~$122; additionally, the Warsh/Fed forward-guidance thesis is speculative rather than confirmed, which softens one of the three stated thesis pillars. The base-case target of $107 (25x FY2027E) remains supported.
