## Executive Summary

Antero Resources is among the largest U.S. natural gas and NGL producers, undergoing a rapid operational transformation following its $2.8B acquisition of HG Energy's Marcellus assets. The company is integrating these assets at significantly improved operational efficiency, has already raised synergy guidance from $50M to $80M+, and expects to reach its 1x leverage target six months ahead of schedule (mid-2026 vs. year-end 2026). Production is guided at 4.1 Bcfe/d for full-year 2026 (Q1 2026 actual: 3.85 Bcfe/d), up ~20% from 2025, with a clear path to further growth.

At approximately 8.3x forward earnings (based on estimated FY26E EPS of $4.46), AR trades at a discount to its peer median (estimated at 11.4x) despite superior inventory depth, integrated midstream infrastructure, and unique LNG/NGL export positioning. The 90-day estimate revision of +35.5% for FY2026 EPS signals the market is still catching up to the fundamental improvement, though the recent 30-day revision of -8.6% for the current quarter introduces near-term caution around gas price trajectory.

The primary risks are commodity sensitivity (cash production expense guidance of $2.25–$2.35/Mcfe, with all-in economics including DD&A materially higher), post-acquisition leverage (net debt of $2.665B as of March 31, 2026), and a concerning 4-quarter earnings miss streak. The bull case rests on natural gas demand acceleration from LNG exports + data center build-out, successful HG integration compressing per-unit costs by $0.30/Mcfe, and potential capital returns once the term loan is retired.

## Company Overview

Antero Resources operates exclusively in the Appalachian Basin (West Virginia and Ohio), with full-year 2026 production guided at approximately 4.1 Bcfe/d from the Marcellus Shale (Q1 2026 actual production averaged 3.85 Bcfe/d). The company's revenue split is roughly 57% natural gas and 43% NGLs/oil, though this ratio fluctuates with commodity prices. As of year-end 2025, proved reserves totaled 19.1 Tcfe across approximately 537,000 net acres (86% held by production).

The business model includes three segments: (1) E&P operations generating the vast majority of value, (2) a 29% equity stake in publicly-traded Antero Midstream (AM), which provides gathering, compression, and water handling services, and (3) a marketing segment monetizing excess firm transportation capacity. Antero is the largest U.S. producer-exporter of NGLs, accessing international markets via Mariner East 2 for ethane and propane/butane.

The February 2026 closure of the HG Energy acquisition ($2.8B) approximately doubled the company's core Marcellus acreage position and added roughly 385,000 net acres. This was funded by $1.5B in Term Loan A debt and $750M in 2036 senior notes, partially offset by an $800M Utica Shale divestiture.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** Revenue recovered from a $4.33B trough in 2024 (Henry Hub averaged $2.27/Mcf) to $5.28B in 2025 (Henry Hub $3.43/Mcf), a 22% increase. Q1 2026 delivered approximately $1.95B in total revenue (~+44% YoY on a GAAP basis), reflecting both the HG volume addition and improved pricing. Operating income surged from near-zero in 2024 to $884M in 2025 (16.7% margin). Q1 2026 GAAP EPS came in at $1.72, though non-GAAP EPS of $1.15 was roughly in-line with consensus.

**Cash Flow:** Net cash provided by operating activities nearly doubled from $849M (2024) to $1.63B (2025), representing a 30.8% operating cash flow margin. After accounting for capital expenditures (D&C spending of $686M, unproved properties of $129M, other PP&E of $5M) and Martica distributions ($70M), 2025 adjusted free cash flow was approximately $745M, or roughly 14% of revenue. Q1 2026 alone generated $657M in adjusted free cash flow (operating cash flow of $859M less capex of $206M, less Martica distributions of $18M, plus transaction expenses of $22M) — which, when combined with Utica divestiture proceeds received in Q1 ($737M cash), totaled approximately $1.39B, funding roughly half of the HG acquisition cost in a single quarter. This cash generation velocity supports the accelerated deleveraging timeline.

**Balance Sheet:** As of March 31, 2026, total long-term debt stood at $2.665B, reflecting significant post-acquisition deleveraging. The company had already repaid $236M of the Term Loan A (reducing it to $1.264B outstanding), redeemed the remaining $365M of 7.625% 2029 notes, and reduced revolver borrowings to $72.5M. Management targets 1x net debt/EBITDAX by mid-2026, ahead of the original year-end 2026 guidance. The current ratio of 0.55 as of year-end 2025 appears concerning on the surface but is structural for E&P companies with large revolving credit facilities — Antero has $1.56B in unused revolver capacity. The company carries $960M in federal NOLs and $1.9B in state NOLs, meaning it is not currently a federal cash taxpayer.

**Cost Structure:** Antero's 2026 cash production expense guidance is $2.25–$2.35/Mcfe, with Q1 2026 all-in cash expense (lease operating + GP&T + production/ad valorem taxes) at $2.64/Mcfe. Including G&A, net marketing expense, and DD&A, full-cycle costs are materially higher. The shift toward dry gas development on HG acreage is expected to reduce corporate cash costs by approximately $0.30/Mcfe by eliminating processing/fractionation steps — a meaningful margin expansion catalyst.

**Capital Allocation:** Priorities are clearly sequenced: (1) retire the remaining $1.264B Term Loan A balance, (2) opportunistic share repurchases ($914M remaining authorization, representing ~8% of current market cap), and (3) potential capital returns acceleration once leverage targets are achieved. Management has indicated a focus on debt reduction with opportunistic buybacks; the timing of any more aggressive return-of-capital program will depend on deleveraging progress and commodity prices.

## Growth Analysis

**Production Growth:** 2026 guidance targets approximately 4.1 Bcfe/d for the full year (Q2 guided at 4.1 Bcfe/d, second half at ~4.2 Bcfe/d), approximately 20% above 2025's average of 3.44 Bcfe/d. Q1 2026 actual production averaged 3.85 Bcfe/d. Growth is primarily volume-driven from HG asset integration, though capital spending is also higher: 2026 guidance includes $1.0B in D&C capital plus $100M land and up to $200M in discretionary growth capital, compared to $686M in D&C spending in 2025. The company plans 70-80 net horizontal wells in 2026.

**Estimate Revision Velocity:** According to market data sources, FY2026 EPS estimates have increased approximately 35.5% over the past 90 days (from $3.29 to $4.46), a strong positive signal. FY2027 estimates rose approximately 15.2% over the same period. However, the most recent 30-day revision for the current quarter is reported at -8.6%, reflecting softening near-term gas price expectations.

**Structural Demand Catalysts:** Three independent demand vectors support longer-term natural gas pricing: (1) U.S. LNG exports reached near-record levels in early 2026, with EU gas storage at second-lowest levels on record exiting winter; (2) data center power demand is creating 5+ Bcf/d of incremental supply proposals under evaluation; (3) Middle East infrastructure disruptions have tightened global energy markets, with EIA noting Strait of Hormuz disruption and damage to Ras Laffan LNG export capacity. Management believes propane storage could fall below the 5-year average by late summer 2026.

**Integration Synergies:** The HG integration is tracking significantly ahead of expectations. Antero's Q1 2026 completion efficiency averaged 13.8 stages/day (compared to what management has described as significantly lower rates under the prior operator). Drilling cycle times compressed to a company record of just under 9 days per well, a 9% improvement from 2025. Synergy guidance already raised from $50M to $80M+ in the first quarter of ownership. The first HG pad — a 6-well pad with approximately 110,000 feet of total lateral length (averaging ~18,333 feet per well) — was turned-in-line in late April.

Consensus price target is reported at approximately $50.10 (median $50.00), implying ~36% upside from current levels. The low target of approximately $38.00 is near the current share price, while the high target of approximately $57.00 represents ~55% upside. Analyst price targets represent estimates, not guaranteed support levels.

**Base case valuation:** Applying 11x forward P/E (estimated peer median) to FY2026E EPS of $4.46 yields ~$49 per share — closely aligned with consensus. Applying a modest growth premium (12x) to FY2027E EPS of $4.76 yields ~$57 per share. On an EV/EBITDAX basis, if EBITDAX reaches $3.5–4.0B in FY2026 (consistent with ~4.1 Bcfe/d at realized prices of ~$4.00/Mcfe), and the stock trades at 5x EV/EBITDAX (industry standard), this supports an equity value of approximately $50–58 per share after netting $2.665B in net debt (as of March 31, 2026).

The 90-day estimate revision of +35.5% combined with a forward P/E below 9x represents a potential BUY signal in high-growth situations. The market may be systematically underestimating the fundamental improvement from HG integration and volume growth.

## Competitive Landscape

Antero's competitive advantages relative to Appalachian peers (RRC, EQT, CNX):

1. **Longest inventory life:** 1,279 potential drilling locations across proved/probable/possible categories provides 15+ years of development runway at current pace — among the deepest in the basin.

2. **Integrated midstream:** The 29% AM stake ensures controlled infrastructure access without third-party bottlenecks, though it also limits cost flexibility (CPI-escalated gathering fees).

3. **Diversified market access:** Firm transportation to Gulf Coast, Midwest, Atlantic Seaboard, and direct LNG export terminal connections. This portfolio approach reduces basis differential risk relative to peers selling entirely into the oversupplied Northeast.

4. **NGL export scale:** As the largest U.S. producer-exporter of NGLs, Antero captures international LPG premiums that pure-gas peers cannot access. Middle East supply disruptions create potential for outsized NGL margin expansion.

5. **Credit quality:** Antero's credit profile differentiates it in securing long-term supply agreements with creditworthy counterparties (utilities, LNG offtakers, industrial customers).

Competitive positioning is improving post-HG acquisition. The operational efficiency gains (13.8 stages/day, <9 day drill times) demonstrate Antero's ability to apply its operational template to acquired assets at a pace that exceeds market expectations.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (HIGH):** Antero requires sustained gas prices well above $3.00/Mcf for attractive profitability. A return to 2024 pricing ($2.27/Mcf Henry Hub) would drive operating income back to near-zero. Antero's Q1 2026 hedge book includes April–December 2026 Henry Hub swaps of 1.3 Bcf/d and collars of 575 MMcf/d. Henry Hub pricing context: Q1 2026 benchmark averaged $5.04/Mcf, while EIA's 2026 forecast averages $3.76/MMBtu and forward strip prices suggest $3.50–4.00/Mcf range. Each $0.50/Mcfe move in corporate Mcfe-equivalent realized price (including NGLs and before hedges) translates to approximately $750M in annual revenue sensitivity at guided production levels.

**Earnings Miss Streak (MODERATE):** Four consecutive quarterly misses (-0.6% to -45.2%) raise legitimate questions about management's guidance reliability and cost control. The Q1 2026 non-GAAP miss (-0.6%) was narrow and the GAAP beat was substantial (+47.8%), suggesting the issue is more about Wall Street modeling complexity (derivative marks, one-time items) than operational underperformance. Still, this pattern warrants monitoring.

**Post-Acquisition Leverage (MODERATE):** Net debt of $2.665B as of March 31, 2026 is manageable at current cash flow levels. The remaining $1.264B Term Loan A balance (maturing Feb 2029) is the near-term priority. At $657M quarterly adjusted FCF (Q1 2026), deleveraging to 1x by mid-2026 is achievable. Risk increases if gas prices fall materially, extending the deleveraging timeline and delaying capital return acceleration.

**$8.2B Minimum Volume Commitments (STRUCTURAL):** These "take-or-pay" transportation/processing obligations represent significant annual minimum fees regardless of production levels. Based on the 2025 10-K contractual obligations table, firm transportation alone totals approximately $1.2B annually in 2026–2028, with total contractual obligations of $1.9B in 2026, $1.7B in 2027, and $1.6B in 2028. Current production easily covers these, but this creates a floor under annual spending that limits management flexibility during downturns and effectively prevents production curtailment as a price defense mechanism.

**Geographic Concentration (MODERATE):** 100% Appalachian Basin exposure subjects the company to regional basis differentials, regulatory changes in West Virginia/Ohio, and single-basin geological risk.

## Conclusion

Antero Resources merits a BUY recommendation at $36.84. The stock trades at approximately 8.3x forward earnings — a significant discount to the estimated peer median — while delivering ~20% production growth, achieving integration synergies ahead of schedule, and sitting approximately 6 months from a leverage milestone that could unlock $914M in buyback capacity. The 90-day EPS estimate revision of +35.5% confirms the fundamental trajectory, and reported analyst consensus indicates a majority Buy/Strong Buy rating with a consensus target of approximately $50.

The 4-quarter miss streak and post-acquisition leverage are legitimate concerns that justify some discount, but the magnitude of that discount appears excessive given the operational evidence of successful integration and the structural demand tailwinds from LNG exports and data center power consumption. The asymmetry favors participation: ~36% upside to consensus target vs. 13-21% downside to bear case. Position sizing of 2-3% is warranted given commodity price sensitivity and remaining integration execution risk.
