## Executive Summary

APA Corporation is a mid-cap E&P with operations across the Permian Basin (62% of production), Egypt (31%), and the North Sea (7%), plus a high-optionality Suriname deepwater development carried predominantly by TotalEnergies. The stock has surged 162% from its April 2025 low of ~$15.20 to ~$40, driven by the sharp escalation in Brent crude, strong Q4 2025 earnings (40.4% EPS beat), and aggressive estimate revisions. At ~10x trailing earnings with current-year consensus at $5.40, the stock now sits at the analyst consensus median price target of $40, which limits near-term asymmetry.

The bull case centers on elevated Brent pricing flowing through to APA's international-heavy production mix (Egypt oil realized $85.70/bbl in Q1 2026), the Suriname FID with first oil in 2028, and continued deleveraging after $1.55B of debt reduction in 2025. The bear case rests on $881M (and potentially $1.2B undiscounted) of Gulf of America legacy decommissioning liabilities, North Sea wind-down costs in a 78% effective tax jurisdiction, chronic TSR underperformance vs. peers (APA 197 vs. peer group 295 on indexed basis from 2020), and the reality that the current oil price environment already appears embedded in the stock's 162% rally. With no insider buying, elevated options-implied volatility in steep backwardation, and 9.4% short interest, the risk/reward at $40 favors waiting for a pullback.

## Company Overview

APA Corporation operates through its subsidiary Apache Corporation as an independent oil and gas exploration and production company. Production in 2025 totaled 464,383 boe/d, split across three regions: the U.S. Permian Basin (287,539 boe/d), Egypt's Western Desert (146,182 boe/d across 7.5M gross acres), and the U.K. North Sea (30,662 boe/d, in wind-down mode).

The company's most significant growth option is the GranMorgu development in Suriname's Block 58, operated by TotalEnergies (50%). FID was reached in October 2024 for a 220,000 b/d FPSO, targeting first oil in 2028. APA has a uniquely favorable carry: TotalEnergies funds 87.5% of the first $10B in gross capex and 75% of the next $5B, plus APA receives $75M cash at first oil. APA also holds a 50% working interest in an Alaska North Slope discovery that flow-tested at 2,700 b/d, plus early-stage exploration in Uruguay.

Total proved reserves stand at 1,056 MMboe (71% liquids), implying an R/P ratio of approximately 6.2 years. Suriname contributed 74 MMboe (7% of proved reserves), booked upon FID — this figure will grow materially as development proceeds.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** Revenue declined 12% in 2025 to ~$9.1B from ~$10.2B, driven entirely by lower realized oil prices ($66.92/bbl vs. $78.08/bbl in 2024). Production was roughly flat at +2% boe/d. Despite lower revenue, net income nearly doubled to $1.43B ($3.99/share diluted) from $804M, because 2024 included $1.1B of non-recurring impairments (North Sea: $796M, U.S.: $315M). Operating cash flow reached a three-year high of $4.55B (+26% YoY), aided by Egyptian government receivable collections and aggressive cost reduction.

**Cost Structure:** Lease operating expense per boe fell 13% from $10.16 to $8.86, a meaningful efficiency gain. DD&A held flat at $13.41/boe. The company achieved $350M in annualized cost savings in 2025 and is targeting $450M by end-2026. Net margin hit 31.3%, strong for mid-cap E&P.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt was reduced by $1.55B during 2025 to $4.49B, with cash of $516M. Net debt stood at approximately $3.97B. The debt-to-equity ratio improved from ~0.95x to approximately 0.64x. The $4.0B undrawn revolving credit facility provides ample liquidity. In January 2025, management executed exchange/tender offers converting ~$2.5B of Apache subsidiary debt into APA parent-level notes, simplifying the capital structure. Altman Z-Score of 9.39 indicates no near-term distress. The current ratio of 0.82 is below 1.0 but typical for the sector and well-buffered by the credit facility.

**Capital Returns:** APA returned $640M to shareholders in 2025: $360M in dividends ($1.00/share) and $280M in buybacks (12.9M shares at an average price of $21.73). The company has repurchased 98.2M shares cumulatively since Q4 2021. The 60% FCF return framework, given $4.5B OCF against ~$2.1B capex guidance, implies substantial return capacity if commodity prices hold.

**Earnings Momentum:** Current-year EPS consensus has risen from $2.16 (90 days ago) to $5.40 (+149.5%). Next quarter estimates jumped from $0.51 to $1.75 (+245.4%). APA has beaten EPS estimates in all four reported quarters, with surprises ranging from 16.7% to 79.5%. This momentum reflects elevated Brent pricing flowing through APA's internationally diversified production base.

## Growth Analysis

APA's growth profile splits between near-term commodity leverage and a medium-term step-change from Suriname.

**Near-term (2026):** Growth is almost entirely price-driven. With elevated Brent pricing, current-year EPS of $5.40 represents a 35% increase over 2025's $3.99. However, the analyst consensus for next year (likely FY2027) is $3.96 — a 27% decline — reflecting the assumption that either oil prices normalize or the current elevated environment is unsustainable. U.S. production volumes should be roughly flat to modestly growing; the Permian program is focused on capital efficiency rather than volume growth. Egypt volumes depend on continued exploratory success (48% success rate on 52.5 net wells in 2025 — decent but not exceptional).

**Medium-term (2028+):** APA's 50% equity in GranMorgu's 220,000 b/d capacity translates to up to 110,000 b/d gross — though Staatsolie exercised a 20% back-in right, which will reduce APA's net share. Given the carry structure, the returns per dollar invested are exceptional. However, first oil is not expected until 2028, and deepwater mega-projects carry execution risk on cost and timeline.

**Frontier Optionality:** The Alaska discovery (50% WI, ~325K gross acres) represents additional optionality but requires further appraisal drilling before it can be valued with confidence. Uruguay is pre-exploration.

**Offsetting Headwinds:** The North Sea is in terminal decline, with production expected to cease before 2030. This represents a structural drag of ~6.6% of current production that must be replaced. Permian associated gas is a persistent problem: APA curtailed 88 MMcf/d of gas and 6,800 b/d of NGLs in Q1 2026 due to negative Waha hub pricing (-$0.35/Mcf realized), which directly offsets oil revenue uplift.

## Valuation Assessment

APA trades at ~10.2x trailing EPS ($3.99) and ~7.4x current-year consensus ($5.40) at a price of ~$40. Against the peer median P/E of 12.2x, APA's discount reflects both the elevated short-term earnings power from high Brent and the market's recognition of APA's structural liabilities.

On next-year estimates of $3.96 (which embed oil price normalization), APA trades at ~10.1x forward P/E — broadly in line with Devon (12.2x) and EQT (11.2x) but slightly below. The discount to peers is justified by: (1) the Gulf of America decommissioning liability, (2) North Sea wind-down costs in a punitive tax regime, and (3) management's TSR track record.

The stock now sits precisely at the analyst consensus median price target of $40 and near the mean of $41.33. The low target of $29 (27% downside) reflects a diplomatic resolution or oil normalization scenario. The high target of $56 (40% upside) reflects sustained elevated Brent with full earnings flow-through.

At $40, the asymmetry is roughly balanced. The bull case requires sustained elevated oil prices and Suriname execution; the bear case requires either oil normalization or crystallization of decommissioning costs beyond current accruals.

**Dividend yield:** 2.5% at current price, providing modest income support.

## Competitive Landscape

APA is the only Permian-focused mid-cap E&P with material international conventional production (Egypt) and a world-class deepwater growth option (Suriname). This differentiation cuts both ways.

**Advantages:** Egypt provides long-life conventional production with low decline rates and enhanced gas pricing. The Suriname carry structure is arguably the most asymmetric growth option in the E&P sector. Geographic diversification provides exposure to Brent pricing, which currently commands a significant premium to WTI.

**Disadvantages:** APA's Permian operations, while substantial at 287,539 boe/d, are lower-margin than pure-play Permian operators like Diamondback or EOG due to associated gas headwinds and the cost of managing a diversified international portfolio. The cumulative TSR gap (APA 197 vs. peer group 295, indexed from 2020) emerged in 2023 and has widened, driven by North Sea impairments, decommissioning charges, and market skepticism about the Callon Petroleum acquisition (closed 2024).

APA's competitive position is stable but not improving relative to peers. The market has consistently discounted APA's international complexity and legacy liabilities. Only the Suriname FID and sustained elevated oil prices have narrowed this gap.

## Risk Assessment

### Idiosyncratic Risks

**Gulf of America Decommissioning ($881M accrued, $0.9B–$1.2B undiscounted range):** This is APA's most distinctive risk. The Fieldwood Energy bankruptcy chain leaves Apache as the responsible party for legacy shelf decommissioning under BSEE orders. Costs are sensitive to derrick barge rates and regulatory evolution. A remaining $40M reimbursement asset is nearly exhausted. Apache has committed to a $400M loan facility for GOM Shelf LLC, creating additional cash exposure. This will be a multi-year cash drain with uncertain terminal cost.

**North Sea Wind-Down:** With a 78% effective tax rate (40% corporation tax + 38% Energy Profits Levy) and regulatory proposals for "clear seabed" decommissioning, the North Sea is a declining liability. LOE runs $34.03/boe — roughly 4x U.S. levels. The remaining 25 MMboe of proved reserves will produce negligible economic value, and decommissioning costs could be material.

**Egypt Sovereign Risk:** EGPC payment delays are a recurring feature of Egyptian operations. While government payments "nearly eliminated past due receivables" in Q3 2025, this pattern recurs during Egyptian economic stress. Currency risk (Egyptian pound) and nationalization risk are persistent.

**Commodity Price Sensitivity:** APA's estimate trajectory makes the sensitivity explicit: current-year EPS $5.40 at elevated Brent vs. next-year $3.96 at lower price assumptions. An oil normalization scenario (Brent to $85–95) would compress APA's EPS significantly and the stock accordingly.

**Permian Gas Headwinds:** This is a structural Permian Basin issue that persists until new pipeline capacity relieves the bottleneck.

### Options Market Signal

ATM IV of 81.5% is 35.9pp above one-year realized volatility of 45.6%, placing IV at the 83rd percentile. The term structure is in steep backwardation (81.5% near-term vs. 47.1% six-month), indicating the market expects an imminent repricing event. Near-term put skew is elevated at 23.4% for the 1-week tenor, though longer-dated skew is roughly neutral. The P/C OI ratio dropped from 0.77 to 0.42 over the past two weeks while the stock rallied 17.3%, suggesting put holders closed positions into strength, but the rich IV indicates the market is not confident this rally persists. This options profile is consistent with a stock that has run hard on macro tailwinds but faces binary commodity risk.

Short interest at 9.4% with 2.4 days to cover is elevated but not extreme — it signals skepticism without creating acute squeeze risk.

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**

3. Egypt EGPC payment delays re-emerge with past-due balance exceeding $500M

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings report (Q1 2026, likely May 2026). Key items: (1) realized pricing at elevated Brent — does it flow through or are hedges/gas headwinds larger than expected? (2) Updated decommissioning estimate. (3) Suriname progress update.

## Conclusion

APA presents a genuine growth option through Suriname's carried development and benefits meaningfully from the current elevated Brent environment, with estimate revisions confirming the earnings torque. However, the stock has already repriced from $15.20 to $40, leaving it at the analyst consensus median target. The combination of decommissioning liabilities, North Sea terminal decline, chronic peer-relative TSR underperformance, zero insider buying, and rich options-implied volatility in backwardation suggests the current price adequately compensates for near-term commodity tailwinds. The prior score trajectory has been declining (5.7 → 5.6), and while estimate revisions are strongly positive, the risk profile has not materially improved — rising oil prices have masked it. For new capital, wait for a pullback to $30–33 where the Suriname optionality becomes the dominant driver rather than pure commodity leverage.

HOLD — growth-oriented, with upgrade contingent on price pullback or fundamental catalyst crystallization.
