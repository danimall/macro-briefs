**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MDU Resources has completed a multi-year transformation from a diversified holding company into a pure-play, fully regulated energy delivery utility. The Knife River spinoff (May 2023) and Everus spinoff (October 2024) removed the construction-materials and construction-services businesses, cutting consolidated revenue from ~$7.0B in 2022 to ~$1.88B in 2025. What remains is a smaller, lower-volatility, three-segment regulated utility — electric, natural gas distribution, and FERC-regulated pipeline — operating across the Northern Plains and Pacific Northwest with an 87-year uninterrupted dividend record.

The investment debate reduces to a single tension. MDU is executing an aggressive rate-base growth plan (rate base +16% in 2025, ~$2.5B utility capex over five years) that is out-spending operating cash flow, producing negative free cash flow of ~$297M in 2025 and requiring continuous debt and equity issuance. Against that, it has acquired an above-peer growth vector: a large-volume data-center customer (Applied Digital) in North Dakota with a ramping 350 MW electric service agreement, plus a potential $2.7–$3.2B Bakken East pipeline that would materially expand the pipeline segment. The bull case is that this capex converts to earning rate base with regulatory recovery, driving above-sector EPS growth. The bear case is that persistent dilution and rising financing costs compress per-share returns before the growth is realized.

At $21.25 the stock trades at ~23x trailing EPS ($0.93), a premium to the multi-utility peer median of ~19.3x, and sits essentially at the low end of the analyst target range ($21 low, $23 median, $28 high). The valuation already reflects a fair amount of the data-center optionality, while the near-term earnings picture is soft — three misses in the last four quarters, mild-winter volume pressure, and next-quarter estimates revised down ~16%. We rate MDU a HOLD (growth_hold), with the thesis anchored to rate-base conversion of the data-center and pipeline pipeline, not to an attractive entry point today.

## Company Overview

MDU is a multi-utility holding company headquartered in Bismarck, North Dakota, with ~2,096 employees and CEO Nicole Kivisto. Following the two spinoffs, the business is now three regulated segments (FY2025 net income contribution):

- **Electric ($64.9M):** Montana-Dakota Utilities serves ~146,550 retail customers across North Dakota (67% of retail revenue), Montana, South Dakota, and Wyoming, with ~835 MW nameplate generation (~40% renewable) and MISO membership. Net electric plant ~$2.1B, rate base ~$1.8B.
- **Natural Gas Distribution ($56.1M):** Montana-Dakota, Cascade, and Intermountain serve ~1.08M customers across eight states, with Washington (35% of gas retail revenue) and Idaho (24%) the largest jurisdictions. Rate base ~$2.2B.
- **Pipeline ($68.2M):** WBI Energy Transmission — FERC-regulated, 3,800+ miles of pipe, ~350 Bcf of storage, strategically located near four gas basins including the Bakken. Net plant ~$1.0B.

MDU's competitive footing rests on regulated monopoly franchises with tracking mechanisms and decoupling that reduce regulatory lag, a strategically located Bakken-adjacent pipeline system, above-average customer growth (1.5% in 2025 versus the national average), and a long dividend history (target payout 60–70% of regulated earnings; quarterly dividend recently raised to $0.14 from $0.13, ~2.6% yield).

The distinctive feature of MDU relative to similarly sized utilities is its exposure to the AI data-center power-demand boom in North Dakota. Its Bakken footprint gives it both an electric growth vector (data-center load) and a pipeline growth vector (associated gas transport) that most small-cap utilities lack.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (continuing operations):** Revenue $1.875B (+6.7% YoY); operating income $290M; net income $190.4M. Reported net income fell from $281.1M in 2024, but that comparison is distorted by ~$100M of discontinued-operations income in 2024 — continuing-operations income actually rose from $181.1M to $191.4M. Diluted EPS was $0.93.

**Margins and returns** are typical for a rate-regulated utility: operating margin ~15.5%, net margin ~10.2%, ROE ~6.9%, ROA ~2.5%. These are structurally modest and are set by regulators, not by operating leverage.

**The central financial issue is capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $473M in 2025 against capex of $770M. The 2025 capex figure was inflated by the $264.6M Badger Wind Farm payment (49% interest, 122.5 MW, placed in service December 31, 2025). The company plans ~$2.5B of utility capex over the next five years, funded by a mix of operating cash flow, debt, and equity. This structural negative-FCF, external-financing dependence is the mechanism that transmits a higher-for-longer rate environment directly into earnings: electric segment interest expense jumped 50.6% YoY in Q1 2026 on debt issued for Badger Wind.

**Balance sheet:** Long-term debt $2.53B versus equity $2.77B (D/E ~2.75x); current ratio 0.84. The Altman Z-Score of 1.08 nominally sits in the "distress zone," but this is an artifact of the normal high-leverage, low-asset-turnover profile of a rate-regulated utility, not a genuine solvency signal — issuing entities hold investment-grade ratings. Weighted-average interest rate on long-term debt is 4.83%. The fair value of long-term debt ($2.28B) is below carrying value ($2.60B), reflecting the mark-to-market of legacy fixed-rate debt against higher current rates; refinancing at prevailing rates will lift the blended cost.

**A genuine yellow flag** surfaced in the filings: Intermountain Gas breached its minimum interest-coverage ratio for the period ended 9/30/2025, triggering an event of default and cross-defaults at MDU Energy Capital and the revolving facilities. Waivers were obtained and the company was compliant at both 12/31/2025 and 3/31/2026. The breach appears to have motivated the December 2025 equity offering. It is a cured issue, but it signals how tight the balance sheet became during the heavy-capex phase.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue $606M (down from $675M, mostly lower pass-through gas costs plus mild winter); net income $80.8M versus $82.0M; EPS $0.39 versus $0.40. Results missed Wall Street estimates on unusually warm winter weather that reduced volumes, only partially cushioned by decoupling mechanisms.

The earnings-surprise record is weak: three misses in the last four quarters (Q2 2025 -43%, Q3 2025 -18%, Q1 2026 -7.2%), with one narrow beat in Q4 2025. This reduces confidence in near-term consensus and reflects real weather and regulatory-lag sensitivity in non-decoupled jurisdictions.

## Growth Analysis

MDU's forward growth rests on three pillars, each with a different risk profile.

**Data-center load (the differentiated vector).** The existing Ellendale Applied Digital load is ~180 MW at full capacity, ramping through a 350 MW electric service agreement (~50 MW online in Q1 2026, +50 MW targeted by end of Q2 2026). Post-10-K, Montana-Dakota signed a new ESA for the proposed "Polaris Forge 3" AI factory in North Dakota (reported June 2026), and Applied Digital is expanding into Oliver County for a third data center (July 2026). Regulatory filings show the Ellendale load benefited other power customers by ~$38.4M, a favorable framing for rate-case approval. This load is purchased through the MISO market rather than requiring heavy dedicated capital, which limits MDU's capital outlay and shields other ratepayers from stranded-asset risk if the customer falters. This is the clearest instance of the data-center power-demand theme showing up in a small-cap regulated utility, and it gives MDU above-peer volume growth potential.

**Bakken East Pipeline (the transformational call option).** A potential $2.7–$3.2B, 350-mile pipeline, incremental to the existing capital plan. A binding Open Season concluded March 2026 with ~1.4 Bcf/day of requests, ~40% signed under Precedent Agreements, and North Dakota committing up to $50M/yr for 10 years. Phase 1 is targeted for November 2029. If it proceeds, it would materially expand the pipeline segment's asset base, but it requires large-scale financing that would extend the negative-FCF/dilution dynamic for years.

**Base rate-base growth.** Badger Wind is driving multiple rate cases (Montana interim +$10.4M effective 4/1/2026; South Dakota/Wyoming infrastructure riders). MISO's shift to "direct loss of load accreditation" (effective 2028) is accelerating the need for new capacity — MDU now identifies a need for additional generation by 2028 versus 2034 previously — which supports further rate-base additions.

The offset is soft near-term earnings. Current-quarter and current-year estimates are essentially flat (+0.5% CY over 90 days), but next-quarter estimates were cut ~16% over both 30 and 90 days. This is not the +30% revision velocity that would flag an earnings inflection; it is a mid-single-digit regulated compounder with a growth option that has not yet converted to earnings. Growth prospects remain the strongest dimension here, but the trajectory has moderated from the earlier read (6.5 → 6.0) as near-term estimates softened and financing costs rose.

MDU carries the highest or near-highest multiple in the group and the lowest dividend yield. The premium is defensible on the data-center/pipeline growth vector, but it is not a discount that offers asymmetric upside. On a growth-adjusted basis, a ~23x multiple on a mid-single-digit EPS compounder implies a PEG well above 2.0 unless the data-center and pipeline optionality converts, in which case forward EPS growth accelerates and the multiple looks more reasonable.

The analyst target structure reinforces the balanced read: low $21 (essentially the current price), median $23 (~8% upside), high $28 (~32% upside). The stock trades at the low end of that range, which on an inflection stock with rising estimates would be a floor. But MDU's near-term estimates are flat-to-down, the earnings record is three misses in four quarters, and the multiple is already above peers. Under the discipline that analyst targets are floors only on cheap, positively-revising names, MDU's proximity to the low target signals a full-to-fair valuation, not a mispricing.

A low P/E is not present here to trigger a value-trap concern, and the stock is not structurally impaired. But there is no identifiable catalyst that makes the current price a clear mispricing to the upside. The valuation score reflects that the multiple is at a premium with only modest near-term growth to justify it, offset by real (if unrealized) longer-term optionality. The stock trades essentially at the December 2025 offering price ($19.70) and just above the ~$20.85 director open-market purchase, pricing the growth call option skeptically while the company out-spends operating cash flow.

## Competitive Landscape

MDU's competitive position within its franchise territories is stable-to-improving. As a regulated monopoly across its service areas, its moat is the franchise itself, reinforced by tracking mechanisms and decoupling that dampen regulatory lag.

The improving element is the data-center relationship, which gives MDU electric-load and associated pipeline growth vectors above what most similarly sized utilities can access. The Bakken-adjacent footprint is differentiated: it positions WBI to serve associated gas transport demand and gives Montana-Dakota a large-volume electric customer base that supports both rate-base expansion and utilization.

The offsetting structural headwinds are concentrated in the gas distribution segment. Washington is advancing building codes limiting new gas hookups, which MDU is litigating (Ninth Circuit oral argument February 2026; Initiative 2066 before the Washington Supreme Court). Oregon's Climate Protection Program is under legal challenge, with Cascade joining a lawsuit in April 2026. These policies threaten long-term gas-distribution customer growth in the West Coast jurisdictions and are the primary reason the competitive-position trajectory is not stronger.

The Coyote Station coal plant (MDU owns 25%, cannot make unilateral decisions on its future) is a residual stranded-asset and compliance-cost risk, though the current administration's EPA relaxation of coal/GHG/methane rules (mercury rule repeal February 2026, GHG rule repeal proposed, methane waste charge postponed to 2034) has materially reduced near-term compliance-cost pressure.

## Risk Assessment

**Data-center customer concentration.** At full capacity, the existing Ellendale load equals roughly 21% of MDU's generation portfolio. The 10-K explicitly flags that high-volume customers increase revenue and earnings volatility and raise counterparty credit risk. Applied Digital is a speculative-grade counterparty. The mitigant is meaningful: load is sourced through the MISO market and does not require heavy dedicated capital, so a customer default would not strand large MDU-specific assets.

**Negative FCF, capital-markets dependence, and dilution.** The growth plan requires continuous debt and equity issuance. The December 2025 forward equity offering (10.15M + 1.52M shares at $19.70 via forward sale agreements; $81.3M / 4.3M shares settled March 2026) and the $400M ATM program (established August 2025, unused) create ongoing dilution — share count is rising from ~204M to ~209M with ~7.4M more shares issuable. Rising rates increase financing costs directly.

**Covenant tightness.** The cured Intermountain Gas coverage-ratio breach shows how stretched the balance sheet became at peak capex. It is resolved, but it is a reminder that the negative-FCF phase leaves little margin for error.

**Regulatory and weather sensitivity.** Q1 2026 illustrated earnings exposure to mild weather in non-decoupled jurisdictions. Rate-case lag remains a sector-wide constraint on earning the allowed return promptly on new rate base.

**Gas electrification policy.** The Washington and Oregon building-electrification and climate policies threaten long-term gas-distribution growth in the most economically important gas jurisdictions.

Most of these risks are already at least partly priced given the stock's position near the offering price and the skepticism embedded in its multiple. The concentration risk and the negative-FCF/dilution dynamic are the two that could still move the stock materially against expectations, which is why the risk score sits at neutral (5.0) rather than higher, consistent with the improvement from the prior read (4.0 → 5.0) as the covenant issue cured and the regulatory environment eased.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 29.7% versus 21.5% one-year historical volatility — modestly rich (spread ~8.2%), indicating the market is pricing somewhat elevated near-term uncertainty, likely around earnings and rate-case outcomes. The put/call skew is -15.0% (call-heavy), which leans bullish rather than defensive, and the term structure is roughly flat (near 29.7% ≈ far 28.4%), showing uncertainty spread evenly rather than concentrated on a single event. Short interest is 7.9% of float at 7.4 days to cover — not a crowded short, but enough to matter. The options positioning does not contradict the fundamental HOLD; the call-heavy skew is mildly supportive of the growth-option narrative. With IV rich to HV, this is not an especially attractive point to buy volatility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($28, ~32% upside).** The data-center relationship compounds — Polaris Forge 3 and the Oliver County expansion add durable large-volume load, and the Bakken East Pipeline reaches a final investment decision, adding materially to the pipeline asset base. Rate cases approve the Badger Wind and infrastructure-rider additions on schedule, and MISO's accelerated capacity need drives further rate-base growth. Rate base compounds at the high end of its recent range, EPS growth accelerates toward high-single-digits, and the multiple holds at a premium justified by above-peer growth. The ~$38.4M documented ratepayer benefit from the Ellendale load supports constructive regulatory treatment.

**Bear Case ($16–17, ~20-25% downside).** Higher-for-longer rates continue to lift financing costs on the perpetual external-funding model; dilution from the ATM and forward sale agreements erodes per-share growth; the data-center load ramps slower than expected or the speculative-grade counterparty falters; West Coast gas-electrification policy tightens the gas-distribution outlook; and the pipeline mega-project either stalls or forces a larger, more dilutive capital raise. Continued earnings misses compress the premium multiple toward or below the peer median of ~19x, pulling the stock into the high-teens.

**Base Case ($22–23, ~4–8% upside plus ~2.6% yield).** MDU delivers mid-single-digit rate-base-driven EPS growth, the data-center load ramps roughly on plan, the Bakken East pipeline advances but does not yet reach FID, and the multiple holds near current levels. Total return is dominated by modest capital appreciation plus the dividend, roughly in line with a regulated utility with a partially-realized growth option.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Base case target $22–23 over 12 months.**

The base case reflects ~23x on FY26 consensus of ~$0.97 (≈$22.30) plus the dividend, consistent with a mid-single-digit regulated compounder whose growth optionality is partially but not fully realized. This sits between the bear case (~$16–17, applying the ~19x peer median to depressed EPS and a compressed premium) and the bull case (~$28, the high analyst target, on accelerated rate-base growth and pipeline FID).

- **Base case:** ~$22–23 (+4% to +8% from $21.25, plus dividend)
- **Bull case:** ~$28 (+32%)
- **Bear case:** ~$16–17 (−20% to −25%)
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, tied to the Bakken East pipeline FID decision, Applied Digital load-ramp milestones (end-Q2 2026 +50 MW target), and 2026 rate-case outcomes.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Bakken East Pipeline reaches a final investment decision with committed offtake and a defined financing plan that does not require an outsized equity raise.
- Applied Digital load ramps on or ahead of schedule (Polaris Forge 3 ESA finalized, +50 MW milestones met) with constructive rate-case treatment, and EPS estimate revisions turn positive (+5% or more over 90 days).
- Pullback toward the low-$18s (near the December 2025 offering price and the director's ~$20.85 buy), where the growth option becomes cheaper relative to peers.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new covenant breach or a material, more-dilutive equity raise that signals the negative-FCF model is straining under higher rates.
- Applied Digital counterparty distress or a meaningful load-ramp delay that removes the differentiated growth vector.
- A string of further earnings misses combined with adverse West Coast gas-electrification rulings, compressing the multiple below the peer median with no offsetting catalyst.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings release and rate-case docket updates over the next two quarters; the Bakken East FID timeline (Phase 1 targeted November 2029, but FID decision materially earlier).

## Conclusion

MDU is a cleaner, lower-volatility regulated utility than it was three years ago, with a differentiated growth vector in North Dakota data-center load and a potentially transformational Bakken East pipeline. The trajectory of our scoring has improved modestly (5.1 → 5.4) as the covenant issue cured, the regulatory environment eased, and the data-center thesis matured, and the new data confirms that trend without reversing it. But the growth is funded by persistent negative free cash flow and ongoing dilution into a higher-for-longer rate environment, and the near-term earnings picture is soft.

At ~23x earnings and trading near the low end of the analyst target range, the stock already prices in a fair amount of the data-center optionality while offering only a 2.6% yield. The risk/reward is balanced: the bull case depends on capex converting cleanly to earning rate base, the bear case on dilution and financing costs outrunning that conversion. We rate MDU a HOLD on the growth thesis, and would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$18s or on confirmed acceleration of the data-center and pipeline vectors with positive estimate revisions.
