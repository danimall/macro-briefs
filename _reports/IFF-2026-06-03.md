**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Flavors & Fragrances is a turnaround in mid-execution where the reported financials systematically understate the operating reality. The trailing GAAP figures show declining revenue ($10.89B in 2025 vs. $12.44B in 2022), a negative operating margin, a distress-zone Altman Z-Score of 1.56, and -$1.41 trailing EPS. Strip out the non-cash goodwill impairments ($2.62B in 2023, $1.15B in Q1 2025) and the picture inverts: comparable currency-neutral sales grew +2% in 2025, Adjusted Operating EBITDA grew +7%, and Q1 2026 swung to a $169M profit ($0.66 EPS) with EBITDA margin expanding to 20.7%. The revenue decline is overwhelmingly divestiture- and FX-driven, not demand erosion.

The defining near-term event is the May 29, 2026 agreement to sell Food Ingredients to CVC Capital Partners for ~$4.3B enterprise value (~$3.8B net proceeds), at roughly 10x the unit's ~$430M 2025 EBITDA. The unit carried the lowest segment margin (~13%), so its exit lifts the consolidated margin profile and leaves a cleaner three-segment company (Taste, Scent, Health & Biosciences) with structurally higher growth and profitability. Proceeds extend a deleveraging campaign that has already cut debt from $10.4B to ~$5.85B, with net leverage now at 2.53x against a 3.75x covenant.

The case for patience rather than purchase rests on what the deleveraging math cannot resolve: two unquantifiable tail risks. EU, UK, Swiss, and Mexican regulators continue investigating alleged fragrance-industry anticompetitive conduct (the U.S. DOJ closed its probe in February 2026 and the U.S. direct-purchaser class settled, so the risk is narrowing but not gone), and the Health & Biosciences reporting unit — the crown-jewel margin segment — sat on only 9% goodwill headroom at the November 2025 test. A 50bp WACC move would push it underwater. Against these is a strong positive signal: director Paul Fribourg's ~$20.3M open-market purchase at ~$74.28 on June 1, 2026, immediately after the deal. The balance is a growth-oriented HOLD, accumulate on weakness.

## Company Overview

IFF is one of the largest global creators of flavors, fragrances, and specialty/biotech ingredients for consumer products, formed in its current shape by the 2018 Frutarom acquisition and the 2021 Reverse Morris Trust merger with DuPont's Nutrition & Biosciences division. Both transactions loaded the balance sheet with goodwill and debt that have dominated the equity story for five years. The business model depends on placement on multinational customers' core supplier lists, with a diverse base of ~20,000 customers (top 25 ≈ 32% of sales, none above 10%) and ~72% of revenue generated outside the United States.

Entering 2026 the company reported four segments (FY2025 revenue $10.89B): Health & Biosciences ($2.28B, ~26% EBITDA margin) in enzymes, cultures, and probiotics; Scent ($2.48B, ~21% margin); Taste ($2.48B, ~19% margin); and Food Ingredients ($3.28B, ~13% margin), now being divested. Post-CVC, IFF becomes a ~$7.5B-revenue company centered on its three highest-margin innovation cores, competing against Givaudan, DSM-Firmenich, and Symrise in flavors/fragrances and Novonesis and DSM-Firmenich in biosciences.

## Financial Analysis

The four-year trajectory shows a balance-sheet repair masked by impairment-driven GAAP losses:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $12.44B | $11.48B | $11.48B | $10.89B |
| Operating income (GAAP) | -$1.33B | -$2.11B | +$766M | -$382M |
| Goodwill | $13.36B | $10.64B | $9.08B | $8.27B |
| LT debt | $10.37B | $9.19B | $7.56B | $4.74B |
| Net income to IFF | — | -$2.59B | +$263M | -$361M |

The revenue decline maps almost entirely to divestitures and currency. On a comparable currency-neutral basis 2025 sales grew +2% and Adjusted Operating EBITDA grew +7%; Q1 2026 reported sales fell -4% but rose +3% currency-neutral. The GAAP losses are non-cash goodwill write-downs from an overpaid 2021 merger being unwound, not operating cash bleed. Q1 2026 net income reached +$169M versus a -$1.02B loss in Q1 2025 (which carried the impairment), and operating cash flow rose to $257M (9.4% of sales) from $127M.

Deleveraging is the dominant financial narrative. In 2025 IFF repurchased $2.0B of senior notes and booked a $488M gain on extinguishment. The incoming ~$3.8B of CVC proceeds, not yet reflected in the financials, would cut leverage materially further.

Free cash flow remains the soft spot. FY2025 FCF was $256M (2.4% margin), below the $409M dividend, with the gap funded by divestiture proceeds. The ~50% dividend cut in February 2024 (to $0.40/quarter) was made specifically to accelerate deleveraging — a rational reallocation, not distress. The Altman Z-Score is mechanically depressed by impairment-driven negative EBIT and a large goodwill-heavy asset base; given investment-grade ratings, a fully available $2B revolver, and 2.5x net leverage, it is not a genuine bankruptcy signal. Negative retained earnings (-$3.4B) reflect cumulative impairments.

## Growth Analysis

The underlying demand picture is steady rather than dynamic: food, beverage, home, and personal care end-markets, with tailwinds in naturals/clean-label, biotech-derived ingredients, and probiotics, offset by GLP-1/weight-management demand shifts (explicitly cited as a risk) and private-label pressure on multinational customers. Currency-neutral organic growth of +2-3% with EBITDA growth of +7-8% reflects productivity gains and favorable pricing more than volume acceleration.

The CVC sale is the structural growth lever. Removing the lowest-margin Food Ingredients unit concentrates the company in three segments growing faster than the divested business, with Health & Biosciences and Scent anchoring a higher-quality earnings mix. Whether this translates into a re-rating depends on execution through the separation — stranded costs, dis-synergies, and TSA obligations from serial divestitures are real friction.

The earnings-momentum signal is mixed and warrants caution. The company beat in three of the last four quarters (Q1 2026 by 15.3%), but near-term consensus is drifting down: current-quarter EPS estimates fell -3.4% over 30 days and -4.8% over 90 days, and next-quarter -2.1%/-3.7%. Full-year estimates have edged up (+2.2%/30d, +1.0%/90d), so the deterioration is concentrated in the immediate quarters. This is not the rising-estimate profile that would support a more aggressive stance; it tempers the Growth score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $73.24 the stock trades at ~22.7x forward earnings on consensus FY2026 EPS of ~$4.49 and ~16.4x on EV/EBITDA terms once the post-divestiture structure is considered. The trailing P/E is meaningless given impairment-distorted GAAP losses; forward and normalized metrics are the only sound basis.

Peer comparison in Specialty Chemicals is unusually unhelpful here because most comparables carry distorted multiples: DuPont at 152.7x, Albemarle and LyondellBasell at negative trailing P/Es, Solstice at 71.9x, with only PPG (16.2x) showing a clean number. The peer median P/E of 71.9x is not a meaningful anchor. IFF's nearest true comparables are the flavor/fragrance pure-plays Givaudan, DSM-Firmenich, and Symrise, which historically command low-to-mid-20s forward multiples for the higher-margin, less cyclical earnings stream that IFF is converging toward post-CVC. On that basis ~22.7x forward is roughly fair, not cheap.

The valuation argument that does carry weight is the sum-of-the-parts crystallization. The CVC deal values the lowest-quality segment at ~10x EBITDA; applying the same logic to the higher-margin retained segments implies the remaining company should support a richer multiple as the breakup completes and proceeds fund further deleveraging and buybacks ($427M remaining on the $500M authorization). Analyst targets — mean $91.1, median $91.0, range $71-$105 — sit well above spot, implying ~24% upside to the median. With estimates not yet inflecting upward and the stock at a fair (not depressed) forward multiple, I treat those targets as a reasonable ceiling rather than a stale floor. The valuation merits a moderate score: there is a specific catalyst (deal proceeds and SOTP re-rating) but no deep mispricing at the current multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

IFF operates in a consolidated specialty-ingredients oligopoly. The same names co-named in the antitrust probes — Givaudan, DSM-Firmenich, Symrise — are its direct competitors, which implies an industry-wide regulatory overhang rather than IFF-specific misconduct: a relative positive for competitive standing even as it is an absolute negative for risk. In Health & Biosciences, competition runs against Novonesis and DSM-Firmenich.

The CVC sale removes IFF from Food Ingredients (texturants, emulsifiers, protein), a segment with greater commodity exposure and lower differentiation, and concentrates the company in higher-IP-content franchises. CVC is a financial sponsor, so the unit becomes a standalone PE platform rather than fuel for a strategic competitor. Segment-level profitability is improving across all four segments on productivity and pricing, indicating no acute share loss. The Competitive Position score reflects a stable-to-improving trajectory with the prior-period modest decline (6.5 to 6.0) holding, as the breakup sharpens focus but introduces separation execution risk.

## Risk Assessment

**Antitrust investigations** are the most material idiosyncratic legal risk. The U.S. DOJ closed its probe in February 2026 and the U.S. direct-purchaser class settled (~$43M total provision), but EU, UK, Swiss, Mexican regulators, Canadian class actions, and new India/Singapore employment-practice probes remain open. Regulatory costs rose to $106M in 2025 from $73M. Potential fines are unquantifiable and flagged as potentially material.

**Goodwill impairment** remains live. $8.27B goodwill plus $5.9B intangibles persist; the Health & Biosciences reporting unit had only 9% headroom at the November 2025 test, meaning a ~50bp WACC increase could trigger another write-down. Taste was also flagged as sensitive after the reorganization.

**Other risks**: the N&B/DuPont tax indemnity (a "Spinco Tainting Act" could force a substantial DuPont tax indemnification); execution/separation costs across serial divestitures; $924M of EUR notes maturing within 12 months (manageable against the revolver); and IEEPA tariff refund uncertainty disclosed in the 10-Q. The Materials-sector classification itself warrants conservatism — this system has demonstrated no reliable edge picking winners in the group, which independently caps conviction.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and slightly favorable for entry. Near-term ATM IV of 32.7% sits essentially at the 33.1% one-year historical vol (spread -0.4%), with IV in the 14th percentile of the current chain — no event premium being demanded. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.3%), OI ratio 0.79, and the term structure is flat (32.7% near vs. 32.2% far), indicating uncertainty spread evenly rather than concentrated around a near-term binary. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±5.6%. P/C OI rose from 0.65 to 0.79 as the price fell 7.5% since early May, a modest defensive shift but not a stress signal. Cheap-to-fair IV makes this a reasonable accumulation window for a fundamentally constructive thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$95-105):** The CVC deal closes, proceeds drive net leverage below 2x, and accelerated buybacks plus a cleaner three-segment, higher-margin company prompt a re-rating toward the historical flavor/fragrance pure-play multiple. Currency-neutral organic growth holds at +3% with EBITDA margin expanding past 21%, antitrust exposure resolves at manageable cost, and no further impairment materializes. The stock re-rates to ~24x on rising forward EPS toward $5.00+.

**Bear case (~$58-62):** EU/UK/Swiss/Mexican antitrust fines land materially above provisions, and a WACC uptick triggers a Health & Biosciences impairment that revives the GAAP-loss narrative and pressures the multiple. Separation costs and dis-synergies erode the margin benefit of the divestiture, organic growth stalls toward flat amid GLP-1 and private-label pressure, and the stock retests the September 2025 low near $59.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, anchored to the CVC deal close (expected over the next several quarters), subsequent capital-allocation announcements, and the next two to three earnings prints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CVC deal closes on terms confirming ~$3.8B net proceeds, with management committing the bulk to debt reduction and buybacks, and leverage moving below 2x.
- Current- and next-quarter EPS estimate revisions turn positive (reversing the -3.4%/-4.8% drift), confirming an earnings inflection rather than a stabilization.
- Material narrowing or favorable resolution of the EU/UK/Swiss fragrance investigations (e.g., capped or settled fines).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new goodwill impairment in Health & Biosciences or Taste, signaling the impairment cycle is not over.
- Antitrust fines disclosed materially above booked provisions.
- Currency-neutral organic growth turning negative for two consecutive quarters, or a covenant-pressuring leverage reversal.

## Conclusion

IFF is a credibly improving turnaround whose GAAP optics understate operating progress: a value-crystallizing divestiture sharpens the company into its three highest-margin franchises on top of steady organic growth and the deleveraging already booked. The director's open-market purchase immediately after the deal is the strongest class of insider signal and directly relevant at the current price. Against this, two unquantifiable tail risks — fragrance antitrust fines across multiple jurisdictions and a thin goodwill cushion in the crown-jewel segment — plus near-term estimate erosion and a fair (not cheap) forward multiple keep this below the bar for a buy. The recommendation is HOLD with a growth lean. New capital is best deployed on weakness toward the high-$60s, where the risk/reward improves and the deal-completion optionality becomes more asymmetric. The improving score trajectory (4.7 → 5.5 → 5.75) is confirmed, not reversed, by the CVC announcement and the insider buy, but the antitrust and impairment overhangs prevent a step up to BUY.
