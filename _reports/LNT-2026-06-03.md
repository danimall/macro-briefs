**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Alliant Energy is a Midwest regulated electric and gas utility holding company (Interstate Power and Light in Iowa, Wisconsin Power and Light in Wisconsin) that has re-rated through 2026 on a credible and partially contracted data-center load-growth story. The company has executed five Electricity Service Agreements totaling roughly 3.4 GW of demand — more than 60% above its current peak — anchored by QTS's $10B Cedar Rapids development and Meta's Beaver Dam facility, with a further 2-4 GW pipeline in active negotiation. Management guides to a $13B capital program over 2026-2029 and a 7%+ EPS CAGR for 2027-2029 off a 2025 adjusted base of $3.24. This demand signal is large for an $18.4B utility and is the dominant driver of the equity.

The difficulty is that the market has largely recognized this. At $70.96 the stock trades at roughly 20.7x next-year consensus EPS ($3.43 current year, $3.69 next year), a modest discount to a peer median of 21.7x trailing, after a 14% year-to-date advance to a recent all-time high. The growth is funded externally: up to $2.4B of new common equity through 2029 (about 13% of the current market cap) plus substantial incremental debt, and the 7%+ CAGR is the number after absorbing that dilution. Layered on top are two underappreciated risks: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's accelerated phase-out of wind/solar tax credits (LNT monetized $285M of transferred credits in 2025, a real cash engine) and emerging Wisconsin regulatory friction, where the PSCW approved the Meta contract in May 2026 but ordered Alliant to build a standard data-center rate amid "growing public mistrust."

We rate LNT a HOLD with a constructive growth lean. The fundamental story has improved since our prior reviews on contracted GW, but the valuation no longer offers asymmetric upside, and the risk profile around dilution, tax policy, and social license to allocate data-center costs has widened. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s or on confirmation at the Q3 2026 / EEI update that the 2-4 GW pipeline is converting and the EPS trajectory is being revised higher.

## Company Overview

Alliant operates two principal regulated subsidiaries, Interstate Power and Light (IPL) in Iowa and Wisconsin Power and Light (WPL) in Wisconsin, plus a ~16% equity interest in American Transmission Co. (ATC) and a small non-utility/parent segment (AEF). FY2025 segment earnings broke down as Utilities & Corporate Services $875M (EPS $3.39), ATC Holdings $41M ($0.16), and Non-utility & Parent −$106M (−$0.41), netting to consolidated net income of $810M and diluted EPS of $3.14.

The economic engine is standard rate-base-growth regulated utility mechanics: LNT earns authorized returns (IPL retail electric blended ~9.3-11.0% ROE; WPL ~9.8% ROE) on invested capital, with fuel and commodity costs largely passed through. The distinguishing feature is geography and resource mix. Iowa and Wisconsin are Midwest data-center hotspots with cheap land, transmission readiness, and state tax incentives, and Iowa is wind-rich (roughly 15 GW of regional wind against ~6 GW of regional load). That lets LNT serve new load with low-fuel-cost energy plus production-tax-credit generation, using simple-cycle gas turbines and batteries for capacity rather than expensive baseload combined-cycle plants. The strategy explicitly favors speed-to-market and affordability.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue rose to $4.36B in 2025 from $3.98B in 2024 (+9.6%), driven principally by IPL/WPL base rate increases (~$333M of higher revenue requirements) and favorable weather. The multi-year path — $4.21B (2022), $4.03B (2023), $3.98B (2024), $4.36B (2025) — reflects a utility in the early innings of a rate-base build, not a secular grower yet. Net income climbed 17% to $810M from $690M, helped by higher revenue requirements and the absence of 2024 one-time charges (a $60M Lansing Generating Station valuation charge, restructuring/voluntary-separation costs, and an ARO charge on IPL steam assets). Operating margin of 27.7% and net margin of 21.9% are healthy and typical of a well-run regulated utility.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $1.17B (roughly flat YoY). The "free cash flow" figure in standardized datasets overstates discretionary cash here: true construction-and-acquisition spend was ~$1.9B in 2025 and rising, with the company guiding to ~$13B over 2026-2029 ($3.1B / $3.6B / $3.6B / $3.1B). This is an externally funded growth program, not a cash-return story.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is $12.1B, debt/total-cap is 62% (60% rating-agency-adjusted), and the current ratio is 0.8. These ratios are structural for a regulated utility and supported by regulated, recurring cash flows and investment-grade credit (Alliant BBB+/Baa2; IPL upgraded by S&P from BBB+ to A- in Q1 2026 even as the company levers up). The Altman Z-Score of 0.85 ("distress zone") is a false signal: the model is calibrated for industrials and systematically misreads capital-intensive utilities with high structural leverage and sub-1.0 current ratios. The S&P upgrade during a leveraging cycle is the more informative credit data point.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised 5% to $2.14/share for 2026 (~3.0% yield at the current price), against a 60-70% target payout. Financing for the build relies on up to $2.4B of new common equity through 2029, of which ~$1.3B has been raised via forward equity agreements covering needs through 2027, plus substantial new IPL/WPL debt and DOE conditional loan guarantee commitments (~$1.4B IPL, ~$1.6B WPL). The forward equity structure reduces near-term market-timing risk, but the dilution is real and is the cost the 7%+ CAGR target already absorbs.

**Earnings delivery.** The beat/miss record over the last four quarters is 3 beats, 1 miss, with the Q1 2026 print at $0.87 GAAP / $0.82 ongoing beating the $0.81 estimate by 7.1%. The one miss (Q3 2025, −6.9%) was a weather-sensitive cooling-season quarter. This is a normal regulated-utility cadence and supports moderate confidence in consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests almost entirely on data-center load and is the strongest it has been in our coverage history. As of the Q1 2026 update LNT had five fully executed ESAs totaling roughly 3.4 GW (three already under construction), plus a new 370 MW Iowa ESA signed in April 2026 (full ramp by 2030) and a separate 2-4 GW pipeline of "mature" opportunities with land control, transmission studies, and active negotiations. To serve the load, the company has contracted up to 1.1 GW of simple-cycle gas turbine capacity (in service ~2031), a 720 MW gas CT at Marshalltown (Bobcat Energy Center), ~1 GW of new Iowa wind approved at a 9.8% blended ROE, plus batteries and storage.

LNT's specific advantages — Iowa wind economics, transmission readiness, and state tax incentives — are independently verifiable. The contracting structure is also more shareholder-friendly than it first appears: recent ESAs are capacity-only and large users fund their own interconnection and upgrade costs, which aligns capital with revenue and limits the gap between spend and return. The Iowa retail electric base rate moratorium through September 2029 caps regulatory reset risk while preserving upside through the earnings-sharing mechanism and tax-credit retention.

The estimate picture is consistent but not accelerating: current-year EPS consensus is $3.43 (essentially flat over 90 days), next year $3.69 (−0.4% over 90 days), and next-quarter revisions are modestly positive (+3.8% over 90 days). There is no sign of the large upward revision velocity that would mark a fundamental shift; the growth is being delivered on schedule rather than surprising to the upside. Management has deliberately staged guidance conservatively, deferring the full resource plan and updated EPS/growth trajectory to the Q3 2026 call and EEI conference, where incremental load beyond 3 GW and updated MISO accreditation assumptions will be reflected. If the 2-4 GW pipeline converts there, it is a credible positive catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

At $70.96, LNT trades at roughly 20.7x next-year consensus EPS and ~19.2x the following year, with a 3.0% dividend yield. Against the electric-utility peer set, the multiple is broadly in line: peer median P/E is 21.7x (EVRG 21.8x, PPL 21.7x, FE 25.2x, ES 14.6x, EIX 7.6x). The dividend yield is below the peer median of 4.0%, which reflects the market's willingness to pay up for LNT's load-growth profile.

The valuation question is whether the data-center story justifies a premium that the market has not yet fully awarded but also has not ignored. At ~21x forward earnings the stock embeds a solid portion of the 7%+ CAGR. On a growth-adjusted basis, a ~21x multiple against a 7%+ EPS CAGR is a PEG near 3, rich in absolute terms, though typical for a regulated utility where the earnings stream is low-risk and rate-base growth is visible. Analyst targets (mean $79.13, median $80.25, low $74, high $85) sit 4-20% above the current price, implying modest upside before the dividend.

The key constraint on the valuation score is the dilution and the absence of a clear mispricing. The 7%+ CAGR is the number after absorbing the equity issuance, so the per-share growth is real but already reflects the share-count headwind. We do not see a specific reason the market is materially wrong about this name today; rather, the price reasonably reflects a known, contracted, but execution-and-policy-dependent growth story. That argues for a valuation score in the middle of the range: neither a value trap (the multiple is supported by visible growth) nor an asymmetric opportunity (there is no obvious gap between price and intrinsic value). A pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s (~18x forward) would restore the asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

LNT's competitive position is improving on trajectory, which is what matters most. The Iowa wind-plus-batteries-plus-peakers approach is a genuine cost advantage versus utilities forced to build expensive baseload CCGTs to serve new load, and it dovetails with the PTC economics and tax-credit monetization that keep customer bills competitive while preserving authorized ROE. Iowa and Wisconsin are among the most attractive Midwest data-center jurisdictions, and LNT's transmission readiness and state incentive backdrop (Iowa's Major Economic Growth Attraction program; Iowa/Wisconsin data-center sales-tax exemptions) are real structural draws. Regional comparators include MidAmerican (Berkshire) in Iowa and WEC Energy in Wisconsin, but LNT's resource mix and contracting momentum give it a credible claim to disproportionate large-load capture.

The competitive caveat is jurisdictional. Iowa, where LNT serves ~75% of communities and benefits from more land and stronger transmission, is the more receptive environment. Wisconsin is showing friction: the PSCW approval came with sharp criticism, ordering Alliant to create a standard rate for future data centers and demanding more transparency. The social and political license to allocate data-center costs is not guaranteed, and CEO Barton acknowledged she is "actively countering" rhetoric spilling over from PJM. The competitive moat is intact, but the regulatory durability of the model is being tested in one of its two states.

## Risk Assessment

**Tax-credit / OBBB Act risk (most underappreciated).** LNT monetized $285M of renewable tax credits in 2025 ($216M in 2024) by transferring them to other taxpayers — a material cash inflow supporting both customer affordability and the financing math. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) accelerates phase-out of wind/solar PTCs/ITCs (projects must begin construction within 12 months of enactment or be in service by 2027) and restricts credits tied to certain Chinese components. Management is racing to qualify projects within the window, but the loss of this cash engine over time is a real headwind that the market may be under-pricing.

**Stranded-asset / customer concentration.** The 10-K added a dedicated risk category for large-load customers: LNT may invest billions before realizing returns, and a hyperscaler could decline service, take less, or delay. Capacity-only ESAs and customer-funded interconnections mitigate this, but the capex and equity precede returns, so a hyperscaler pullback would leave stranded investment and dilution without the offsetting earnings.

**Regulatory / moratorium.** The Iowa moratorium stabilizes rates but limits the ability to reset if costs run ahead. The Wisconsin standard-rate order is a watch-item for cost-allocation politics.

**Construction and supply chain.** The build program faces tariff, labor, and equipment lead-time risk. A separate solar-tariff overhang — an unresolved U.S. Court of International Trade ruling (Aug 2025, stayed pending appeal) — could retroactively impose duties on completed Southeast Asian modules.

**Dilution.** The equity issuance through 2029 against an $18.4B cap is meaningful and is the cost the 7%+ per-share CAGR already absorbs; execution slippage would compress that number.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term event risk that is unusual for a utility. Near-term ATM IV is 32.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 15.4% — rich by 16.6 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (32.0% at one month versus 16.9% at six months), implying a ±5.4% move into the June 18 expiry. The skew is call-heavy (−5.8% near term, shifting positive at longer tenors) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.31, so the elevated near-term vol is not bearish positioning; it reads as a binary catalyst being priced rather than directional fear. The longer-dated structure (positive put skew at six months) is the more telling caution: hedging demand builds out into the period when the capex/equity program and tax-policy effects bite. None of this overrides the fundamental view, but it argues against chasing the stock into a near-term event window. Short interest is moderate at 9.9% of float with 9.2 days to cover — neither a squeeze setup nor an informed bearish consensus.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The 2-4 GW pipeline converts on top of the contracted load, and the Q3 2026 / EEI update revises the EPS CAGR above 7% with incremental load and improved MISO accreditation. Capacity-only ESAs and customer-funded interconnections keep capital aligned with revenue, the Iowa moratorium and tax-credit retention preserve ROE, and the wind/batteries/CT strategy holds the cost advantage. The IPL A- upgrade points to a credit-supportive leveraging path. In that scenario the multiple holds or expands modestly and EPS compounds at 8-9%, supporting a price toward $85 (analyst high) over 12-18 months.

**Bear case.** OBBB phases out the tax credits faster than expected, removing a $250M+ cash engine and pressuring affordability; Wisconsin (and potentially Iowa) regulators push back on data-center cost allocation, slowing contracting; one or more hyperscalers delay or pull, stranding upfront investment; and the equity issuance dilutes per-share growth below 7%. Combined with a utility-sector de-rate if long-end yields stay elevated, the stock compresses toward 17-18x forward EPS, or roughly $60-63 (−11% to −16% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth lean and a 12-month reassessment horizon anchored to the Q3 2026 call / EEI conference, where management has explicitly staged the updated resource plan and EPS trajectory.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The Q3 2026 / EEI update revises the 2027-2029 EPS CAGR meaningfully above 7%, or confirms conversion of a material portion of the 2-4 GW pipeline into executed ESAs.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s (~18x forward EPS) that restores asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Clarity that the OBBB tax-credit phase-out is largely grandfathered for LNT's in-flight projects, de-risking the cash engine.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A hyperscaler ESA cancellation, material delay, or shift to behind-the-meter self-supply that strands committed capex.
- Wisconsin (or Iowa) regulators imposing cost-allocation rules that materially slow data-center contracting or shift costs to retained ratepayers in a way that pressures the affordability case.
- EPS estimate revisions turning negative (>5% over 90 days) alongside an equity raise on unfavorable terms beyond the forward agreements.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, with the substantive review at the Q3 2026 / EEI update.

## Conclusion

Alliant has the most credible data-center growth story in its coverage history: 3.4 GW contracted, a 2-4 GW pipeline, a structural Iowa cost advantage, and a credit profile strong enough to absorb the leveraging cycle (IPL upgraded to A-). The fundamentals have improved, and we have lifted the growth and competitive scores accordingly. What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the absence of a valuation cushion. At ~21x forward earnings and a 3.0% yield, in line with peers and near an all-time high, the stock embeds much of the 7%+ CAGR, which is itself the number after the dilution. The two most underappreciated risks, the OBBB tax-credit phase-out and Wisconsin's data-center cost-allocation friction, are precisely the items that could turn an in-line print into a disappointment.

We would own LNT on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $60s or on a clear upward revision to the growth trajectory at the Q3 2026 update. At current levels the risk/reward is balanced: modest upside to the analyst mean ($79) plus a 3% dividend against a credible $60-63 bear case. HOLD, growth lean.
