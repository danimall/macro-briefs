**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Yum! Brands is an asset-light global franchisor that collects royalties on ~63,000 restaurants across KFC, Taco Bell, Pizza Hut, and Habit Burger & Grill. The business runs at a 31% operating margin and ~20% FCF margin on $8.21B of 2025 revenue, with results driven by franchisee system sales rather than direct restaurant operations. On June 16, 2026 management signed definitive agreements to divest Pizza Hut for ~$2.7B (LongRange Capital takes the non-China operations for ~$1.5B; Yum China acquires mainland China franchise rights for ~$1.2B) and simultaneously authorized a new $4B share-repurchase program. This high-grades the portfolio into two pillars: KFC as an international unit-growth engine and Taco Bell as a high-margin, high-comp U.S. brand.

The fundamental momentum is real but more modest than the GAAP headline implies. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS jumped 72% to $1.55, but that figure was flattered by a tax-rate swing (16.2% vs 41.0% in the prior-year quarter, which carried a one-time $92M Mexican tax reserve) and a $44M interchange settlement. The cleaner ex-Special-Items EPS grew 15% to $1.50, and Core Operating Profit grew 6%. Worldwide same-store sales were +3%, with Taco Bell carrying the load (+8% SSS, +16% operating profit) and Pizza Hut flat-to-negative (operating profit −14%), which is precisely why it is being sold.

Against that, the stock trades at 24.8x trailing earnings, a slight discount to the restaurant peer median of 26.8x, with consensus price targets (mean $173.7, median $175) implying ~14% upside. The cap on the rating is the balance sheet and a specific tail risk: a $4.7B IRS dispute against which YUM has recorded no reserve, more than twice annual net income, sitting on top of ~$12B of debt, negative shareholders' equity, and a near-term refinancing trigger. The divestiture and buyback give a credible 12-month appreciation path, which is why this is a hold rather than an avoid, but the asymmetry is not wide enough to justify a BUY in a sector where company-specific outperformance has been hard to find.

## Company Overview

Yum! Brands is a North Carolina-incorporated, Louisville-based franchisor operating in 155 countries. Approximately 97% of its units are run by ~1,500 independent franchisees and licensees, making this a royalty-collection and brand-management business with light capital intensity. YUM collects roughly 3–6% royalties on franchisee system sales plus advertising contributions and upfront fees.

The pre-divestiture brand structure (FY2025 10-K):
- **KFC** — 33,897 units, 99% franchised, 90% international; $36.4B system sales. The largest brand and primary international growth engine.
- **Taco Bell** — 9,030 units, 93% franchised, 86% U.S.; $18.4B system sales. The highest-margin and fastest-comping brand.
- **Pizza Hut** — 19,974 units, 99% franchised, 68% international; $12.8B system sales. The structural laggard, now being divested.
- **Habit Burger & Grill** — 384 units, mostly company-owned, operating at a loss.

Digital sales approached ~$40B and roughly 60% of system sales in 2025, supported by the proprietary Byte by Yum! platform (POS, ordering, kitchen/labor/inventory, AI). CEO Chris Turner (former CFO and COO) has articulated a long-term target of 75,000 KFC units globally. The company is also buying back franchised Taco Bell units (128 restaurants in the Southeast U.S. for $666M in Q4 2025) to capture more economics in its strongest concept while shedding the weakest one.

## Financial Analysis

Headline health screens are strong. Altman Z-Score of 3.83 sits in the safe zone, operating margin is 31.3%, net margin 19%, FCF margin ~20% (~$1.64B FCF in FY2025), and operating cash flow exceeded $2.0B. Revenue has compounded steadily: $6.84B (2022) → $7.08B (2023) → $7.55B (2024) → $8.21B (2025, +8.8% YoY), with net income reaching $2.08B in 2025.

Q1 2026 showed total revenue +15% to $2.06B and 400 net new units. The segment split is the analytically important part:
- **Taco Bell:** SSS +8%, operating profit +16%, company restaurant margin 23.6%.
- **KFC:** SSS +2%, operating profit +16% reported (+9% ex-FX), aided by an FX tailwind and unit growth.
- **Pizza Hut:** SSS flat, operating profit −14%.
- **Habit:** SSS +5%, still loss-making (−$7M).

The balance sheet requires interpretation rather than alarm. YUM carries negative shareholders' equity (~−$7.3B deficit) and ~$12.0B total debt. This is a deliberate leveraged-recap franchisor structure — years of debt-funded buybacks and dividends against a stable royalty stream — with management targeting ~4.0x net leverage/EBITDA. Credit ratings are sub-investment-grade but stable (BB+/Ba2), and ~96% of debt is fixed near a 4.5% effective rate via swaps through March 2028. The watch item: $1.74B was reclassified to short-term borrowings in Q1 2026 because a $750M Subsidiary Note maturing June 2027 triggers an acceleration clause on the Term Loan A and revolver unless refinanced. Management expects to refinance, and given the cash-flow profile this is a manageable rather than existential issue, but it is sensitive to credit-market conditions in a higher-for-longer rate path.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly: the dividend was raised to $0.75/quarter in 2026, buybacks ran $185M in Q1 2026, and the new $4B authorization signals a substantial near-term return of capital, likely funded in part by divestiture proceeds that can also reduce net leverage.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth profile improves once Pizza Hut, the drag on system-sales growth and the source of declining segment profit, is removed. The remaining two pillars are structurally better: KFC drives international unit expansion (toward the stated 75,000-unit ambition), and Taco Bell delivers high-single-digit comps with the best margins in the portfolio.

Consensus estimates are stable rather than rising. Current-year EPS sits at $6.76 (revised +1.4% over 90 days, flat over 30 days), and next-year at $7.49 (+0.3% over 90 days). This is a low-single-digit positive revision trend, not the >30% velocity that would mark a structural re-rating candidate. The earnings track record is mixed — two beats and two misses over the last four quarters — which argues for measured confidence in the forward numbers rather than the higher conviction a clean beat streak would support.

The genuine growth story is therefore qualitative: a cleaner, faster-growing two-brand mix and a redeployment of proceeds plus the buyback into per-share metrics. Against that, the longer-term demand questions management itself raised — GLP-1/weight-loss-drug adoption potentially shifting dietary demand away from QSR — are real but slow-moving. The growth case supports holding for the divestiture-driven simplification and buyback accretion; it does not support extrapolating an acceleration the estimates do not yet reflect.

## Valuation Assessment

YUM trades at 24.8x trailing earnings and ~22.5x the $6.76 current-year consensus. Against the restaurant peer median P/E of 26.8x, that is a modest discount, but the peer set is heterogeneous: DPZ at 18.0x, DRI at 22.2x, TXRH at 26.8x, CMG at 28.6x, and CAVA at a growth-extrapolated 169.7x. Adjusting for YUM's mid-single-digit core operating profit growth, the multiple is fair rather than cheap — closer to DRI's profile (a stable, dividend-paying, slower-growth name) than to the higher-growth peers.

Consensus targets bracket the case: low $147 (roughly flat to current), mean $173.7, median $175.0, high $200. The mean implies ~14% upside, the median ~15%. On a name with stable rather than rising estimates and a premium-but-not-extreme multiple, these targets reflect the same divestiture-and-buyback math the market already sees rather than lagging indicators consensus will chase upward. The 1.9% dividend yield provides modest support but trails the DRI/DPZ yields of 2.5–2.9%.

The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside today. The forward multiple sits above the company's organic growth rate, the catalyst is largely identified and partially priced (the stock is up ~10% over twelve months and trades only ~9% below its 52-week high of $169.39), and there is a quantifiable tail risk — the IRS exposure — that the multiple does not discount because no reserve is carried. A low-to-mid valuation score is appropriate: this is a quality compounder priced at quality-compounder levels, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Post-divestiture, YUM becomes a cleaner two-pillar franchisor with category leadership in chicken (KFC) and Mexican QSR (Taco Bell). Its durable advantages are globally iconic brands, scale-based purchasing through RSCS, and the Byte by Yum! technology stack, which differentiates it on digital ordering and franchisee operations as ~60% of system sales move through digital channels.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the portfolio level because management is high-grading. Selling Pizza Hut — a brand that had lost share in a fragmented pizza category, particularly to Domino's — removes the deteriorating asset and concentrates capital and management attention on the two concepts gaining or holding share. Taco Bell's +8% SSS materially outpaces U.S. QSR averages, and KFC's international unit growth runs ahead of most Western peers' expansion rates.

The headwinds are sector-wide and largely priced: third-party delivery aggregators charge margin-dilutive per-order fees, beef inflation pressures Taco Bell margins, and California's AB 1228 raises U.S. QSR labor costs. The company-specific competitive risk worth weighting is the rapid leadership turnover: a new CEO/Chairman (Turner) executing a major divestiture and strategy refresh alongside an unusually dense cadence of officer-change filings introduces execution risk that the strong trailing financials mask. On balance the moat is intact and the brand mix is getting better, supporting a moderately positive competitive read.

## Risk Assessment

The single most material idiosyncratic risk is the IRS dispute. Following a 2013–2015 audit, the IRS asserts a $2.1B tax underpayment plus $418M in penalties and ~$2.2B in accrued interest (~$4.7B total) tied to the 2014 brand-line reorganization. YUM has filed a Tax Court petition and recorded no reserve, judging its position more-likely-than-not to be sustained. An adverse ruling would be material against ~$2B of annual net income, and it is not priced into the multiple. This is the asymmetric tail that justifies a below-neutral risk score.

Other company-specific risks:
- **Yum China concentration:** a 3% license fee on 17,000+ mainland units exposes YUM to U.S.–China relations, RMB conversion limits, and a Master License Agreement terminable on Yum China insolvency. The Pizza Hut China sale increases Yum China's role.
- **Leverage and refinancing trigger:** $12B debt, sub-IG ratings, and the $750M Subsidiary Note acceleration clause requiring refinancing ahead of June 2027.
- **Franchisee health and secondary lease liability:** ~$325M undiscounted potential exposure on guaranteed/assigned leases.
- **Yum China spin-off tax tail:** the 2016 spin could be challenged under U.S. Sec. 355 or Chinese Bulletin 7 rules.
- **Geopolitical/"Western brand" boycott risk** in conflict-affected regions, and continuing Russia-exit tax true-ups.
- **GLP-1 demand shift** as a slow-moving structural overhang flagged by management itself.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term options chain shows ATM IV of 101.3% on the front-week expiry against 22.1% one-year historical vol, with the term structure in steep backwardation (101.3% near vs ~21.9% longer-dated). This is concentrated in the imminent expiry (the 1-month tenor sits at a benign 20.9%), and the put/call OI ratio has fallen sharply to 0.40 from 0.92, which is not a bearish skew. The front-week spike most plausibly reflects positioning around the Pizza Hut transaction and deal-related event risk rather than a directional bet against the stock. The balanced put/call skew (1.6%) and low 3.0% short interest (3.7 days to cover) indicate the market is not pricing a fundamental break. Expect short-term volatility around deal-completion headlines; options positioning does not contradict the fundamental hold view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
YUM closes the Pizza Hut sale, applies the proceeds to deleveraging and the new buyback, and emerges as a focused KFC/Taco Bell franchisor with above-system growth and ~20% FCF margins. Taco Bell sustains high-single-digit comps, KFC keeps adding net new units toward the 75,000-store vision, buyback accretion lifts EPS toward and beyond the $7.49 next-year consensus, and the IRS dispute resolves favorably or settles for an immaterial sum. The stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range. Bull case ~$195–200 (~28–31% upside), roughly the high analyst target on a clean two-brand multiple and successful capital return.

### Bear Case
The IRS litigation moves against YUM, forcing a multi-billion-dollar cash outflow against a leveraged balance sheet and constraining the buyback; beef inflation and AB 1228 labor costs compress Taco Bell margins; KFC international comps soften on FX reversal or boycott-related weakness; the June 2027 refinancing prices wider in a higher-for-longer environment; and GLP-1 adoption begins to dent U.S. QSR traffic. The multiple compresses toward the DPZ/low-peer end. Bear case ~$130 (~−15% from current), near the 52-week low of $137.33, reflecting multiple compression plus a partial IRS provision.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the Pizza Hut divestiture and buyback provide a specific, dated 12-month catalyst, but the risk/reward is not wide enough for a BUY given the multiple and the unreserved IRS tail.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Pizza Hut deal closing (expected over the coming quarters), the next earnings release confirming clean-portfolio segment economics, and any procedural development in the IRS Tax Court case.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pizza Hut sale closes on the ~$2.7B terms with proceeds visibly applied to deleveraging below the ~4.0x target AND the buyback executes at a pace adding >5% to per-share metrics.
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions turn meaningfully positive (>5% over 90 days) on sustained Taco Bell SSS at or above +6% and KFC ex-FX operating profit growth above +8%.
- A favorable IRS resolution or settlement at an immaterial fraction of the $4.7B exposure, removing the tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse IRS ruling, or recording of a material reserve, that forces a multi-billion-dollar liability against the leveraged balance sheet.
- Failure to refinance the $750M Subsidiary Note / Term Loan trigger ahead of June 2027 at acceptable spreads, or a credit-rating downgrade below BB+/Ba2.
- Two consecutive quarters of Taco Bell SSS deceleration below +3% or KFC same-store declines, signaling the core two-pillar thesis is breaking.

## Conclusion

YUM is a high-quality, asset-light franchisor executing a sensible portfolio simplification: selling the laggard (Pizza Hut, ~$2.7B), concentrating on its two best brands, and returning capital via a fresh $4B buyback. The fundamentals are solid — 31% operating margins, ~$1.64B FCF, steady high-single-digit revenue growth — and the divestiture is a genuine catalyst that supports holding through the next twelve months.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are concrete. The stock at 24.8x trailing earnings is priced for the quality it delivers, not below it; consensus targets imply only ~14% mean upside and estimates are flat rather than rising; and the $4.7B unreserved IRS exposure, on top of ~$12B of debt and a 2027 refinancing trigger, is a quantifiable tail that the multiple does not discount. The declining score trajectory of prior reviews reflected the absence of a catalyst; the Pizza Hut deal supplies one, which is why the rating stabilizes here rather than dropping to AVOID, but it does not yet justify an upgrade. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
