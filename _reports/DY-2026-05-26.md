## Executive Summary

Dycom Industries is a specialty contracting services company positioned at the intersection of multiple secular infrastructure themes: fiber-to-the-home deployment, data center electrical infrastructure, and federal broadband subsidies. The company's $1.95B acquisition of Power Solutions in December 2025 transformed its capabilities and nearly doubled its asset base, creating a differentiated platform that can serve data center clients with both external fiber connectivity and internal electrical systems.

The fundamental growth trajectory is strong. Revenue grew 17.9% in FY2026 to $5.55B, backlog stands at $9.54B (+23% YoY), and management is guiding for $6.85-7.15B in FY2027 revenue (24-29% growth). Operating cash flow nearly doubled to $643M. Four consecutive quarterly earnings beats averaging 14.3% confirm execution quality.

The core tension is valuation relative to concentrated risk. At ~34x adjusted FY2026 EPS and ~20x trailing EV/EBITDA, the stock prices substantial continued growth. Meanwhile, AT&T's acquisition of Lumen's mass markets fiber business could push AT&T concentration to ~36% of communications revenue, creating binary capex-dependency risk. Debt tripled to $2.84B, annual interest expense will approach $150M in FY2027, and the options market is pricing 74% near-term implied volatility against 37% historical — a substantial premium reflecting uncertainty around the earnings trajectory.

## Company Overview

Dycom operates through 38 subsidiaries providing specialty contracting services across two segments: Communications (98.3% of FY2026 revenue) and Building Systems (1.7%, acquired December 2025). The Communications segment delivers aerial and underground fiber construction, fiber placement and splicing, engineering, underground facility locating, and maintenance for telecom providers. Building Systems provides electrical, energy management, security, and fire safety systems for data centers and critical facilities in the DC/Maryland/Virginia corridor.

The company serves customers across all 50 states from hundreds of field offices with approximately 19,556 employees. Its customer base is dominated by AT&T (25.4%), Verizon/Frontier (14.0%), and Lumen (10.8%), with 86.6% of revenue under multi-year master service agreements — up from 77.7% in FY2024.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** FY2026 revenue of $5.55B represented 17.9% growth, accelerating from 12.6% in FY2025 and 9.6% in FY2024. Organic growth contributed approximately 6.5%, with the remainder from acquisitions. Q4 FY2026 revenue of $1.44B included $95.8M from Power Solutions in just 5 weeks of ownership.

**Profitability:** Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 13.3% (from 12.3% in FY2025). GAAP net income reached $281M (5.1% net margin). The gap between GAAP operating margin and adjusted EBITDA reflects substantial depreciation ($178M), amortization of acquisition-related intangibles, and one-time deal costs.

**Cash Flow Quality:** Operating cash flow of $643M exceeded net income by 2.3x, driven by depreciation/amortization and a 13-day improvement in DSO to 101 days. Free cash flow of $402M (7.2% FCF margin on revenue) provides meaningful debt service capacity. FY2027 capex guided lower at $210-220M net, suggesting FCF expansion potential.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $2.84B (from $933M pre-acquisition) creates net leverage of approximately 2.9x trailing EBITDA. The structure includes $500M of 4.50% senior notes due 2029, $1.54B Term Loan A due 2030, and $800M Term Loan B due 2033. Variable rate borrowings at 5.4%+ mean annual interest expense will run approximately $144-150M in FY2027, more than double FY2026's $66.5M (which only reflected partial-year acquisition debt).

**Liquidity:** $1.46B available ($709M cash + $746M revolver), with a healthy 2.74x current ratio. The Altman Z-Score of 1.99 reflects acquisition-induced leverage rather than operational distress.

**Backlog:** Total backlog of $9.54B (+23% YoY), with $6.36B executable within 12 months (+37% YoY). Building Systems contributes $1.21B with $1.11B in the near-term, indicating extremely high near-term execution visibility for the new segment.

## Growth Analysis

Dycom's growth thesis rests on three overlapping demand drivers:

**1. Fiber-to-the-Home (FTTH):** AT&T, Verizon, and other carriers continue multi-year fiber deployment programs. AT&T alone has stated plans to pass 50M+ locations with fiber. This represents the base case for Communications segment growth.

**2. Data Center/AI Infrastructure:** The Power Solutions acquisition positions Dycom to capture electrical infrastructure demand from hyperscalers. The DC/MD/VA corridor is the largest U.S. data center market. Management's thesis is that combining fiber connectivity (Communications) with electrical infrastructure (Building Systems) creates a differentiated, integrated offering.

**3. BEAD Federal Broadband ($42.5B):** State-level deployments beginning to flow. Timing is uncertain due to administrative processes, but this represents incremental upside not yet reflected in backlog.

FY2027 guidance of $6.85-7.15B implies 24-29% growth, with Q1 alone guided at $1.64-1.71B (30%+ YoY). Adjusted EPS guidance of $2.57-2.90 for Q1 FY2027 implies full-year adjusted EPS potential in the $14-15 range.

However, estimate revisions are modestly negative: current quarter estimates declined 6.4% over 90 days, and next quarter declined 8.9%. This diverges from the bullish guidance, suggesting analysts may be incorporating integration risk or macro uncertainty into near-term forecasts. Full-year FY2027 estimates remain stable at $14.23 (essentially flat over 90 days), indicating confidence in the annual trajectory despite quarterly timing shifts.

## Valuation Assessment

At $419.30 with approximately 30M diluted shares outstanding:
- **Market cap:** $12.6B
- **Enterprise value:** ~$14.7B (adding ~$2.1B net debt)
- **P/E on FY2026 GAAP EPS ($9.56):** 44x
- **P/E on FY2026 adjusted EPS (~$11.97):** 35x
- **EV/EBITDA on FY2026 ($738M):** 20x
- **Forward EV/EBITDA on FY2027E (~$980M at 14% margin on $7.0B):** ~15x
- **P/E on FY2027 consensus EPS ($14.23):** 29x

The analyst consensus target of $474 (median $475, range $415-$575) implies 13% upside. With 11 of 11 analysts at Buy or Strong Buy and the low target ($415) essentially at the current price, the stock has largely converged with consensus expectations.

Against peers: Quanta Services (PWR) trades at approximately 25-30x forward earnings; MasTec (MTZ) at 20-25x; EMCOR (EME) at 20-22x. Dycom's 29x forward P/E is at the upper end of this range, partially justified by its higher revenue growth rate (24-29% vs. peers' 10-15%) and data center optionality.

The valuation is not stretched relative to growth (PEG of ~1.5x on FY2027), but the margin of safety is thin. The stock has appreciated 83% over the past year, driven by genuine fundamental improvement, and now prices much of the known growth trajectory. At 15x forward EV/EBITDA on FY2027 estimates, downside to 12-13x (a reasonable range if growth disappoints or integration stumbles) implies $330-360, or 14-21% downside.

## Competitive Landscape

The specialty contracting services industry is highly fragmented. Dycom competes with Quanta Services (significantly larger), MasTec, EMCOR, and hundreds of regional players. Barriers to entry are moderate — the key differentiators are scale, customer relationships, and geographic coverage.

Dycom's competitive position is strengthening along several vectors:

**Master service agreements** now cover 86.6% of revenue (up from 77.7% in FY2024), creating recurring revenue visibility and switching costs.

**Combined fiber + electrical capability** is differentiated. Few competitors can offer end-to-end data center infrastructure including both external fiber connectivity and internal electrical systems. This cross-sell thesis is the strategic rationale for Power Solutions.

**National scale with decentralized execution** allows Dycom to serve AT&T, Verizon, and Lumen across all 50 states while maintaining local operational flexibility.

The competitive trajectory is positive. However, the moat is narrower than asset-light technology businesses — any well-capitalized competitor can replicate these capabilities over time. The relationships and track record provide 2-3 year visibility but not permanent structural advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer Concentration (Critical):** The combined AT&T/Lumen mass markets exposure at ~36% and Verizon/Frontier at 14% means three entities generate half of revenue, creating binary dependency. Any shift in AT&T's capex philosophy, insourcing strategy, or vendor rationalization would be material.

**Integration Risk (High):** Power Solutions is Dycom's largest acquisition, entering a new segment with a new management team. $1.11B of goodwill sits behind a segment with 5 weeks of operating history. Integration of employees into a different corporate culture requires execution.

**Leverage (Elevated):** Variable rate exposure at 5.4%+ amplifies any further rate increases, and the higher interest burden represents a structural headwind to EPS growth.

**Government Funding (Moderate):** BEAD program timing and scale remain uncertain. The current administration's advisory commission could redirect or delay funds. This is upside optionality that may not materialize on expected timelines.

**Tariff Exposure (Moderate):** While customers provide most materials, Dycom's fleet (vehicles, fuel, equipment) and certain construction materials face tariff risk. Fixed-price task contracts limit mid-contract pass-through ability.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($475-575, +13-37%):** Power Solutions integration succeeds and cross-sell thesis validates, generating 15%+ EBITDA margins within 18 months. BEAD funding flows begin in H2 FY2027, adding $500M+ incremental annual revenue. AT&T fiber expansion accelerates post-Lumen acquisition. FY2027 earnings reach $15-16 adjusted EPS, and the stock re-rates to 35x forward = $525-560.

**Bear Case ($310-350, -16-26%):** AT&T capex disappoints in 2027 (as telecom capex cycles typically compress after 3-4 years of expansion). Power Solutions integration struggles — key personnel depart or data center demand slows. BEAD delays persist. Interest expense drags EPS below consensus. Multiple compresses to 22-25x forward EPS of $13-14 = $290-350.

**Base Case ($430-470, +3-12%):** Execution continues in-line with guidance. Revenue reaches ~$7.0B, adjusted EBITDA margins expand to 13.5-14.0%, adjusted EPS of $14-15. Stock trades at 30-32x forward = $430-480. This aligns closely with current analyst consensus.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $350-370 range (25x forward FY2027 EPS), providing genuine margin of safety
2. AT&T publicly commits to accelerating fiber capex guidance for 2027-2028
3. Building Systems segment demonstrates 14%+ EBITDA margins for two consecutive quarters, validating acquisition economics

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. AT&T capex guidance for 2027 flat or declining, indicating deployment maturation
2. Building Systems backlog declining QoQ for two consecutive quarters
3. Leverage failing to decline below 2.5x net debt/EBITDA within 4 quarters

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (estimated late May/early June 2026 for Q1 FY2027 results). FY2027 guidance confirmation or revision will be the key data point. Also monitor AT&T's capital allocation commentary on their quarterly calls.

## Conclusion

Dycom is a well-managed business positioned at the center of powerful secular demand trends: fiber deployment, data center build-out, and federal broadband investment. The growth trajectory is validated by backlog, strong guidance, and consistent earnings beats. The Power Solutions acquisition is strategically logical even if execution remains unproven.

At $419, the stock discounts much of the visible growth trajectory, trading at 29x FY2027 consensus EPS with negative near-term estimate revisions and elevated options-implied uncertainty. Customer concentration at potentially 36% from a single carrier creates asymmetric downside risk that the market may be under-weighting during a period of AT&T capex expansion.

HOLD is appropriate. The growth thesis supports continued ownership for existing holders, but the risk/reward at current levels does not offer the asymmetric upside required for new capital deployment. A pullback to $350-370 (providing a 25x forward multiple with better margin of safety) or fundamental confirmation of Building Systems economics would warrant upgrading.
