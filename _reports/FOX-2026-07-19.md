**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fox Corporation is a focused U.S. live-news-and-sports operator that deliberately avoided the scripted-streaming arms race that impaired Warner Bros. Discovery and Paramount. The strategy produced peer-beating results through FY2025: revenue up 17% to $16.3B, adjusted EBITDA up 26% to $3.62B, and roughly $3.0B of free cash flow at an 18.4% margin. The business is investment-grade (Baa2/BBB), lightly levered, and returning capital aggressively: roughly $1.9B repurchased in the first nine months of FY2026 against a $12B authorization that nearly equals the company's ~$11.4B market cap.

The stock has fallen roughly 24% from its early-January high near $68 to ~$51.63, with the sharpest leg (a 12.4% drop) occurring in mid-June 2026 alongside new material-agreement and debt 8-Ks (June 15 and June 30). This magnitude of drawdown on a business whose underlying operations are healthy demands an explanation. The June decline is not explained by earnings; the Q3 FY2026 report on May 11 was a beat that sent shares up ~8%, and the off-cycle revenue reversion was well understood. The most plausible drivers are the June material-agreement/debt filings the research flagged as needing monitoring, the approaching Smartmatic trial (summary judgment argued December 2025, trial expected "later in 2026"), and rotation out of a Communication Services name after a strong 2025 run. The market is pricing a specific set of near-term binary risks that carry no accrual.

We hold. The fundamental core is attractive: cheap on look-through earnings, structurally FCF-positive, with the multi-year Murdoch succession overhang resolved and CEO continuity locked through 2030. Against that sits a genuine, unquantifiable $2.7B litigation tail with zero reserve, an unmonitored new debt event, and a FOX One streaming bet in its cash-consuming investment phase with no disclosed subscriber numbers. The combination produces a balanced setup rather than the asymmetric upside a BUY requires.

## Company Overview

Fox operates two reportable segments plus Corporate/Other:

- **Cable Network Programming (~44% of revenue):** FOX News, FOX Business, FS1, FS2, Big Ten Network (~61% owned), FOX Deportes. FOX News has led cable news for 20+ consecutive years.
- **Television (~56% of revenue):** the FOX broadcast network, Tubi (leading AVOD, ~11 billion hours viewed in FY25, ~2.2% of all U.S. TV viewing), 29 owned-and-operated stations covering 14 of the 15 largest DMAs, and FOX Entertainment Studios.
- **Corporate/Other:** FOX One (DTC streaming launched August 2025), Credible (66%-owned finance marketplace), and the FOX Studio Lot.

Revenue splits roughly 47% affiliate fees / 42% advertising / 11% other. The moat is a concentrated bet on live, appointment-based content (news and sports) which is more resistant to DVR ad-skipping and cord-cutting than scripted entertainment. Key long-term rights include the NFL (through ~2033, with an NFL one-time opt-out after the 2029 season), MLB and the World Series, college football via the Big Ten, NASCAR, the FIFA Men's World Cup, plus INDYCAR and LIV Golf.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was a cyclical peak.** Revenue of $16.3B was juiced by Super Bowl LIX (February 2025) and 2024-election political advertising; ad revenue jumped 26% to $6.87B. Net income attributable to FOX reached $2.26B (+51%), operating cash flow $3.32B. The balance sheet carried ~$5.4B cash against $6.6B long-term debt, a 2.91 current ratio, and modest net leverage.

**Q3/9M FY2026 shows the off-cycle reversion, which requires look-through.** Q3 revenue fell 9% YoY to $3.99B; 9-month revenue was down 1% to $12.9B. Management attributes the ~$435M nine-month ad decline almost entirely to the absence of the Super Bowl and 2024 election comps. Underneath, the business grew: 9-month adjusted EBITDA rose 1% to $2.71B despite lower revenue, with Cable segment EBITDA +4% and Television +15%. Distribution/affiliate revenue grew 3% as rate increases more than offset subscriber losses, evidence FOX retains affiliate pricing power against MVPDs.

**Reported GAAP volatility is largely non-operational.** The negative operating margin and swings in reported net income are driven by mark-to-market on FOX's ~$458M Flutter equity stake, booked through non-operating other. That line swung from a +$156M gain (9M FY25) to a −$785M loss (9M FY26), a ~$941M non-cash swing that suppressed reported EPS. Analysts should evaluate FOX on segment/adjusted EBITDA and FCF, not trailing GAAP EPS.

**Cash generation dipped on timing.** Nine-month OCF fell to $1.1B from $1.8B on the absence of Super Bowl/election receipts and higher sports-rights payments. This is a working-capital and rights-amortization timing effect within a lumpy sports/election cycle, not structural deterioration.

The four-quarter earnings surprise record (four consecutive beats, most recently +33.9% in the March 2026 quarter) confirms conservative guidance and reliable near-term visibility on the operational line, even as GAAP is muddied by Flutter.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests almost entirely on FOX One and affiliate-rate durability, and the near-term data is thin.

FOX One launched in August 2025 as a wholly-owned DTC service bundling all FOX brands, timed deliberately ahead of the 2026 FIFA World Cup (co-hosted by the U.S., Canada, and Mexico). External sources confirm it is live and marketing aggressively with World Cup promotions and free trials. Launch spend is visibly depressing Corporate/Other EBITDA (−$158M for 9M FY26). No subscriber figures have been disclosed, the single largest gap in the forward thesis; the FY26 Q4/annual report is the first natural window to judge whether the investment is converting to traction.

The structural offsets are real. Cord-cutting continues to erode subscriber counts across every network (FOX News fell from 67M to 61M year-over-year, FS1 similarly), and FOX has offset this with affiliate rate increases so far. Whether rate can keep outrunning subscriber attrition is the central secular question, and the answer is not knowable in advance. Sports-rights cost inflation is the largest and fastest-growing cost line, with the NFL opt-out after the 2029 season a live contractual overhang.

Revenue is intrinsically lumpy: Super Bowl rotation, the four-year World Cup cadence, and even-year elections make YoY comps highly misleading. FY26 is a trough comp year; FY27 gets the World Cup and 2026 midterm political spend as tailwinds. We score growth 5.5: the durable affiliate-rate and adjusted-EBITDA resilience is offset by an unproven streaming bet and secular subscriber erosion, with growth prospects the dimension that has declined most in prior reads (7.0 → 5.5) and no new data yet to reverse it.

FOX Class B trades below the peer-median P/E of 17.9 and below its own Class A shares at 17.9x; the voting-share discount is unusually wide, reflecting the dual-class control structure and Class B's smaller float. On adjusted earnings that strip the Flutter MTM loss, the true multiple is lower than the trailing figure suggests, since GAAP EPS is understated by the non-cash 9M FY26 loss.

The complicating factor is that trailing EPS reflects a cyclical peak. FY26 earnings are down on the off-cycle comp, so the trailing multiple flatters and the forward multiple normalizes higher before the FY27 World Cup/midterm tailwinds arrive. Cycle-adjusting across a full two-year period, FOX is reasonably but not aggressively cheap.

The stronger valuation support is the buyback. The authorization against the market cap, with ~$1.9B executed in 9M FY26 (including a $1.5B accelerated repurchase in October 2025) and ~$3.5B remaining, provides a structural bid and mechanically compounds per-share metrics. Share count fell meaningfully (Class B 236M → 221M since June 2025).

We resist scoring valuation high purely on the low multiple. The calibration lesson here is direct: cheapness has reliably marked value traps, and a compressed multiple with an unquantified $2.7B litigation tail and cyclical-peak trailing earnings is the pattern that warrants skepticism rather than a buy signal. The buyback and the wide voting-share discount justify a moderate 6.0, not more.

## Competitive Landscape

FOX competes in a secularly declining linear-TV ecosystem against far larger, more diversified peers (Disney/ESPN, Comcast/NBC, Paramount, WBD) plus streamers (Netflix, Amazon, Apple, YouTube) that are escalating bids for the live sports rights that are FOX's crown jewels. The counter-positioning is coherent: stay lean, own live news and sports, monetize via affiliate plus advertising, and avoid the capital-destructive scripted-streaming wars. That discipline produced peer-beating TSR (FY25 TSR of 224 vs. the peer group's 157) and superior margin/FCF conversion.

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. FOX News's dominance is durable and affiliate pricing power remains intact. Tubi has scaled to a meaningful share of U.S. TV viewing. But FOX is the smallest major player (~$11B cap), lacks a deep content library or international footprint, and faces rising sports-rights cost inflation as deep-pocketed streamers bid. FOX One is the bridge to a post-cable world but is unproven. The FCC's June 2025 reconsideration of the 39% national ownership cap is a potential positive, enabling station M&A. We score competitive position 6.0: a defensible, well-run niche with stable-to-improving positioning in its core, offset by structural scale disadvantage and rising rights costs.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile carries specific, unpriced tail exposures that keep this dimension low.

**Smartmatic ($2.7B claim, zero reserve).** Post-Dominion (~$800M settled April 2023), this is the single largest non-operating risk. Summary judgment was argued December 2, 2025; trial is expected "later in 2026 at the earliest." FOX carries no accrual, deeming loss "neither probable nor reasonably estimable," and a related Delaware derivative suit survived a motion to dismiss (December 2024). An adverse summary-judgment ruling or a large verdict/settlement in 2026 would be material and is not in the current price in any quantified way.

**New June 2026 debt/agreement 8-Ks.** The research flagged material-agreement and debt filings on June 15 and June 30 (Items 1.01/2.03) as unmonitored. These coincide precisely with the mid-June price collapse. Until the terms are known, this is an open, unresolved overhang that argues against adding risk.

**NFL opt-out after the 2029 season.** A one-time termination right on FOX's most valuable rights package is a material contractual overhang, distant but consequential.

**Cord-cutting.** Subscriber counts fell across every network. Rate increases have offset so far, but the offset is not guaranteed to persist against the ~47% affiliate-fee revenue base.

**FOX One execution and cash burn.** The streaming investment is in its spend phase (−$158M Corporate EBITDA drag) with no disclosed subscriber traction.

**Dual-class control.** The Murdoch trust controls ~43.9% of Class B voting power, and buybacks structurally concentrate that control. Overlapping News Corp directorships create acknowledged conflict-of-interest waivers.

Offsetting: the multi-year Murdoch succession overhang was resolved via the September 2025 Stockholders Agreement cementing Lachlan's control, and his contract extension through June 2030 (June 11, 2026 8-K) confirms continuity. That removes a genuine discount factor. The FanDuel call option (18.6% for ~$4.5B, expiring December 2030) is off-balance-sheet optionality with no funding obligation. We score risk 4.5: the Smartmatic tail plus the unresolved June debt event are specific, downside-skewed, and largely unpriced.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. One-month ATM IV of 38.9% sits rich to 33.8% trailing realized vol (a 5.1pp spread), with a put/call skew of 6.5% indicating elevated put demand and an implied ±11.0% move on the nearest expiry. The term structure is flat (near 38.9% ≈ far 37.5%), so uncertainty is spread evenly rather than concentrated on a single dated catalyst, consistent with a market that sees the Smartmatic timeline and FOX One trajectory as open-ended rather than resolving on a fixed date. IV has compressed 3.1pp since mid-June (42.0% → 38.9%) even as the price fell, suggesting the initial panic has eased. Put/call OI of 0.22 is not extreme. The signal reinforces caution: the market is paying up for downside protection into an uncertain 2026, which is the right posture for a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** FOX is structurally cheap on look-through earnings, generating ~$3B FCF with a buyback authorization nearly equal to its market cap providing a persistent bid and compounding per-share metrics. FY27 gets the FIFA World Cup and 2026 midterm political ad tailwinds; the off-cycle FY26 trough reverses. FOX One converts World Cup promotions into a durable DTC subscriber base, adding a growth vector the market has assigned no value. Affiliate rate power continues to outrun cord-cutting. Smartmatic settles for a manageable sum or is dismissed. The wide Class B voting-share discount to Class A closes. Bull target $65-68 (return to the January high, ~26-32% upside), reachable if FOX One shows traction and Smartmatic resolves benignly.

**Bear case.** The June debt/agreement filings reveal leverage or a commitment the market dislikes; the Smartmatic trial produces an adverse ruling or a nine-figure-plus verdict against zero reserve; FOX One burns cash without scaling and the market marks the streaming optionality to zero; sports-rights inflation compresses margins ahead of the NFL opt-out. Bear target $42-44 (recent lows, ~15-19% downside).

**Base case.** The off-cycle year troughs, buybacks support the shares, and FOX One remains an open question through the FY26 annual report. Smartmatic overhang persists into a 2026 trial without resolution. Fair value ~$52-55 on cycle-normalized earnings and continued repurchase support, roughly flat to modestly higher, which does not clear the bar for a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The fundamental core is sound and cheap, but the near-term risk/reward is balanced by an unquantified litigation tail, an unmonitored new debt event, and an unproven streaming bet. This is an honest uncertain hold, not a conviction growth or income call: the dividend yield (1.1%) is too small to anchor an income thesis, and FOX One traction is unknowable until disclosed.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FOX One discloses subscriber traction (e.g., >3M subscribers or clear World Cup-driven ramp) in the FY26 Q4/annual report, validating the growth vector.
- Smartmatic resolves via dismissal, favorable summary judgment, or a settlement below ~$500M, removing the largest tail risk.
- Terms of the June 2026 debt/agreement 8-Ks prove benign (e.g., opportunistic refinancing at favorable rates), and the stock re-rates toward peer-median 17.9x on normalized earnings.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse Smartmatic summary-judgment ruling or a verdict/settlement materially above $1B against zero reserve.
- Evidence FOX One cash burn is escalating without subscriber conversion, combined with the June debt filings revealing meaningful new leverage.
- Distribution/affiliate revenue turns negative YoY, signaling rate increases can no longer offset subscriber attrition.

**Reassessment timeline:** The FY2026 Q4/annual report (expected August 2026) is the key checkpoint: it should carry FOX One subscriber disclosure, updated Smartmatic litigation status, and clarity on the June debt actions. The Smartmatic trial development later in 2026 is the dominant binary.

## Conclusion

Fox is a well-run, FCF-rich live-content pure-play trading at a reasonable discount on look-through earnings, with a resolved succession structure, leadership continuity through 2030, and an aggressive buyback that provides genuine downside support. Those are real positives that the reported GAAP figures, distorted by Flutter mark-to-market and the off-cycle ad comp, obscure. The ~24% drawdown from the January high coincides with unmonitored June debt/agreement filings and an approaching Smartmatic trial against which FOX holds no reserve, and it reflects a market pricing specific near-term binary risks rather than mispricing a clean business. With FOX One's traction undisclosed and the largest litigation risk unresolved, the setup is balanced. We rate FOX a HOLD and would revisit toward a BUY on a benign Smartmatic resolution plus evidence FOX One is converting World Cup momentum into subscribers.
