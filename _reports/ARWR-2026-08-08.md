## Executive Summary

Arrowhead has spent the last nine months converting a platform story into a commercial one, and the conversion is genuine but incomplete. REDEMPLO (plozasiran) was approved for familial chylomicronemia syndrome in November 2025, and on July 22, 2026 the SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4 Phase 3 trials in severe hypertriglyceridemia (sHTG) hit their primary endpoint and all prespecified secondaries, with median triglyceride reductions of 79% and 81% and a statistically significant 78% reduction in acute pancreatitis events across the pooled population. The stock rose 18% that day to a 52-week high of $95.49. Detailed data are scheduled for an ESC Congress late-breaker on August 30, 2026, and management agreed on July 31 to pay $215.0M cash for a priority review voucher to compress the sHTG review from ten months to six, targeting an sNDA filing before year-end 2026 and FDA action around mid-2027.

The problem is what the current price already asks for. Product revenue, derived as total revenue less disclosed collaboration revenue and corroborated by the CFO on the Q2 FY26 call, was approximately $2.385M in the June quarter and $3.501M for the first nine months of FY2026. That is roughly $9.5M annualized against an enterprise value near $12.1B. Nine-month revenue of $413.0M is 27.9% below the prior-year period and is almost entirely non-recurring collaboration recognition; the nine-month operating loss of $(270.5)M and net loss attributable to Arrowhead of $(296.2)M, or $(2.10) per diluted share, reverse a $54.2M prior-year operating profit. Consensus loss estimates have widened sharply — current-year EPS from $(3.00) to $(3.66) over ninety days, next year from $(4.33) to $(4.94) over ninety days — which for a pre-revenue-scale biotech reflects launch and R&D spend rather than demand deterioration, but it does compress the financing runway.

We keep the rating at HOLD, the fifth consecutive such call, and we lower the overall score from 6.2 to 5.5. The downgrade is not a reaction to price, which is essentially flat since the prior review ($86.82 to $87.65). It reflects three specific developments: the Q3 print showed SG&A up 52.3% and R&D up 22.1% year over year, accelerating burn ahead of an sHTG launch build; the $215M PRV outflow removes roughly 16% of deployable liquidity; and Sarepta, which supplied 72.7% of nine-month collaboration revenue and holds a 30-day termination right, reported August 6 that its own Q2 revenue fell 34% year over year. A risk-adjusted sum-of-parts supports a base case near $102, +16% from spot, against a $55 bear case, -37%. That asymmetry is roughly fair, not compelling.

## Company Overview

Arrowhead is a Pasadena-based RNA interference company built around the proprietary TRiM (Targeted RNAi Molecule) delivery platform, which the company states silences genes in liver, lung, muscle, adipose and central nervous system tissue. The strategic bet is one delivery chemistry with many tissue-specific ligands, producing an unusually broad pipeline for a company of this size. That breadth is also a correlated exposure the company itself flags: the FY2025 10-K warns that "toxicology signals that may emerge in the course of testing of one particular candidate may apply broadly across our drug candidate platform."

The business runs on two tracks. Wholly-owned cardiometabolic assets include plozasiran (approved in FCS; Phase 3 complete in sHTG), zodasiran (Phase 3 YOSEMITE in HoFH, 70 patients enrolled, completion expected mid-2027), ARO-INHBE and ARO-ALK7 in obesity (Phase 1/2a), ARO-DIMER-PA, ARO-RAGE, ARO-MAPT, ARO-C3 and ARO-CFB. The second track is outbound licensing, which has funded the company: $500M upfront plus $325M of equity and five annual $50M installments from Sarepta; $200M upfront from Novartis for CNS targets; $300M plus $40M received from Takeda on fazirsiran with a US 50/50 profit share; GSK on HSD17B13 and HBV; Amgen on olpasiran (royalties already sold to Royalty Pharma, milestones retained); $25M upfront from Madrigal for ARO-PNPLA3 in June 2026; and $130M to majority-owned Visirna from Sanofi for Greater China plozasiran rights. Aggregate eligible milestones are disclosed as "up to $16.2 billion," a fully-successful-outcome gross figure that should not be probability-weighted anywhere near face value.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is lumpy, licensing-driven, and declining from a one-time peak.** FY2025 revenue was $829.4M, comprising $696.8M from Sarepta, $130.0M from the Visirna/Sanofi transaction and $2.6M from GSK. That produced FY2025 operating income of $98.3M and consolidated net income of $30.1M — but $31.7M of income was allocated to the Visirna non-controlling interest, leaving a net loss attributable to Arrowhead shareholders of $1.631M, or $(0.01) per share. The company's own proxy pay-versus-performance table uses −$1,631,000 as FY2025 net income. Screens keying off consolidated net income misclassified FY2025 as a profitable year; it was a licensing-funded near-breakeven interruption in a structurally loss-making P&L (FY2024 −$599.5M, FY2023 −$205.3M attributable). The same mechanism runs the other way now: in Q3 FY26, $15.4M of income was allocated to the NCI, so the attributable loss of $(194.3)M exceeds the consolidated loss of $(178.9)M. With NCI equity nearly exhausted at $0.512M after a $38.4M dividend to minority holders, this distortion should shrink prospectively.

**Nine-month FY2026 is a clean reversion to heavy losses.** Revenue $413.0M (−27.9%); R&D $548.7M (+27%); SG&A $134.9M (+56%); operating loss $(270.5)M against a prior-year $+54.2M. Collaboration revenue detail: Sarepta $297.6M, Novartis $74.9M, Madrigal $25.0M, Sanofi $12.0M, GSK zero. Sarepta is 72.7% of collaboration revenue and exercised its contractual step-in right on certain programs in Q2 FY26, reducing Arrowhead's remaining performance obligations and reimbursable R&D revenue, with scope finalized and a cumulative catch-up adjustment recorded in Q3.

**The balance sheet is strong in aggregate and expensively structured in detail.** Cash resources at 6/30/26 were $1,566.9M (cash and restricted cash $54.8M, of which $35.1M sits at the Visirna VIE, plus $1,547.2M of available-for-sale securities), up from $781.5M at 9/30/25 on roughly $972M of gross equity and convertible issuance. Total liabilities are $1,646.0M against Arrowhead stockholders' equity of $465.5M.

Three liabilities matter:

- **Sixth Street senior secured term facility.** $400M funded of a $500M commitment, 15.0% PIK, maturing 8/7/2031, first-priority security over substantially all assets including intellectual property. A minimum 2.0x MOIC applies on full repayment through 8/7/2028, capped at 2.5x. Carrying value is $181.4M after $319.5M of payments (including $158.9M of MOIC payments), but the disclosed Level 3 fair value is **$416.0M** — a $234.6M gap. Cash already paid plus the fair value of the remainder implies roughly $735.5M of total economic cost on $400M funded, about 1.84x cash-on-cash. The 10-Q's scheduled-repayment table totals $345.6M remaining, which does not reconcile with the ~$480M implied by the 2.0x MOIC floor; the $416.0M fair value is the most reliable single indicator of the remaining claim ahead of equity. Covenants require ≥$250M liquidity while market cap exceeds $2B and restrict licensing of six named core IP assets — a constraint on the very business-development engine that funds the company.
- **2026 convertible senior notes.** $700M principal at a **0.00% coupon** due January 2032, conversion price ~$87.07 (8,039,080 shares), capped calls purchased for $47.9M with a $119.33 cap. The facility caps convertible debt at "never above $700M"; Arrowhead issued exactly $700M. Fair value has risen to $843.7M. At $87.65 the notes are effectively at the strike.
- **Royalty Pharma monetization.** $250M received in 2022 plus $50M of milestones for all Amgen olpasiran royalties, accounted for as debt with no repayment obligation. Carrying value rose to $392.5M purely on $25.1M of non-cash accretion, and the effective rate was raised from 8.3% to 9.0% in Q3 FY26 on revised olpasiran pricing and launch assumptions.

Of $70.9M of nine-month interest expense, roughly $69.5M was non-cash. Reported interest overstates near-term cash cost; the obligation is deferred and back-loaded, and 15% PIK compounds principal.

**Cash flow and runway.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $(79.5)M, flattered by roughly $475M of collaboration inflows (Novartis $200M upfront, Sarepta $200M DM1 milestone and $50M installment, Madrigal $25M). Excluding those, gross operating outflow was roughly $554.5M over nine months, near **$185M per quarter**. Capex is trivial at $8.6M. Taking $1,566.9M of resources, subtracting the $215M PRV and the $250M covenant floor, leaves roughly $1,102M freely deployable — about six quarters at gross burn, extended toward seven or eight by contracted inflows (a further Sarepta installment of up to $50M, three more thereafter, and $100M from GSK on first Phase 3 HSD dosing). That reaches into late CY2027 or 2028, but it does **not** fund a full sHTG commercial build. Remaining capacity is a $500M Jefferies ATM ($76.1M drawn in nine months) and $100M of undrawn Sixth Street commitment. The convertible lever is used to its contractual ceiling.

**Dilution.** Shares outstanding rose from 135.702M to 141.135M in nine months, +4.0%. Two pre-funded warrant tranches totaling 2,467,828 shares are already included in weighted basic shares, so the economic base is roughly 143.60M. Maximum contingent dilution from outstanding instruments — 830,176 options, 5,794,654 RSUs and 8,039,080 convert shares — is 14,663,910 shares, equal to 10.21% of the existing base or **9.27% post-issuance ownership dilution**. The capped calls neutralize economic dilution from the notes between $87.07 and $119.33. Separately, shareholders approved 10,500,000 additional plan shares in March 2026, taking reserved shares from 9.85M to 20.16M — future capacity, not current dilution, but a governance signal.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term revenue line will keep falling and keep being misleading. Collaboration recognition is timing-driven and the Sarepta step-in has permanently reduced reimbursable R&D revenue. What matters is the sHTG label.

REDEMPLO's FCS launch is real but small: prescription volume roughly doubled quarter over quarter, more than 400 unique prescribers concentrated in preventive cardiology and endocrinology, and management describes favorable policies with the most significant US payers. At $2.4M a quarter, none of that supports the equity value. FCS is an ultra-rare orphan indication and a slow patient-finding ramp is normal.

sHTG is categorically larger. Ionis has publicly targeted $3B-plus peak sales for Tryngolza (olezarsen), which was approved in sHTG around June 24, 2026 — approximately twelve months before Arrowhead's PRV-accelerated action date. Arrowhead's differentiation rests on quarterly rather than monthly dosing, a threefold reduction in injection burden in a chronic and largely asymptomatic-between-events population, and on the acute pancreatitis outcome. We deliberately make no efficacy superiority claim against olezarsen: we do not have the corresponding CORE/CORE2 endpoint values, and cross-trial comparison across different protocols and populations would not support one. The claimed "100% event reduction" in the highest-risk subgroup (TG >880 mg/dL with prior AP history) is hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.

The obesity programs are the highest-optionality and least-priced element. ARO-INHBE plus low-dose tirzepatide nearly doubled weight loss at week 16 and roughly tripled reductions in visceral, total and liver fat at week 12 versus tirzepatide alone in obese type 2 diabetics; monotherapy at ≥200mg cut liver fat 44% versus placebo in subjects with baseline LFC above 8%. ARO-ALK7 achieved −88% mean adipose ALK7 mRNA at 200mg by week 8, the first clinical demonstration of RNAi adipocyte target silencing. That is the strongest external evidence that the extra-hepatic delivery claim is real, and it is what the Novartis (CNS) and Sarepta (muscle) deals validate commercially.

Growth prospects therefore score above average but not strongly: the addressable expansion is large, the mechanism evidence is accumulating, and the licensing engine continues to close deals (Madrigal in June). Against that, near-term reported growth is negative, all of the value-creating events sit twelve to thirty months out, and consensus loss estimates are widening faster than revenue visibility is improving.

## Valuation Assessment

No revenue or earnings multiple is meaningful here. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative; the peer table's median P/E of 39.8 is an artifact of mixing profitable (EXEL 17.7x, BMRN 39.8x) and loss-making (IONS −36.5x, AXSM −67.8x, MDGL −34.7x) names.

Enterprise value depends on debt treatment. Using principal and carrying values and excluding the non-recourse royalty liability: $12.38B + $0.70B + $0.18B − $1.57B ≈ **$11.7B**. Using disclosed fair values and including the royalty liability: $12.38B + $0.84B + $0.42B + $0.39B − $1.57B ≈ **$12.5B**, or roughly $12.7B adjusted for the PRV outflow. Because the royalty liability is settled solely from olpasiran royalties with no repayment obligation, a defensible central figure is **~$12.1B**.

The most informative comparison is Ionis, at a nearly identical $12B market capitalization with 2026 revenue guidance of $875–900M, Q2 2026 revenue of $268M including $119M of commercial revenue, first-mover status in both FCS and sHTG, and multiple other approved products. Arrowhead commands the same market value on roughly $9.5M of annualized product revenue. The bull rejoinder is that Arrowhead carries larger net cash, a broader extra-hepatic platform, and the obesity call option. That is a real argument, but it is an argument about optionality, not about a mispricing the market has overlooked.

A risk-adjusted sum-of-parts, with component ranges rather than false precision:

| Component | Risk-adjusted value | Per share (143.6M) |
|---|---|---|
| Net cash post-PRV, net of Sixth Street fair value ($1,352M − $416M) | $936M | $6.5 |
| Plozasiran franchise (FCS + sHTG, WW ex-Greater China, plus Sanofi royalties) | $5.5–7.0B | $38–49 |
| Partnered portfolio (Sarepta, Novartis, Takeda, GSK, Amgen milestones, Madrigal) | $2.5–3.5B | $17–24 |
| Obesity (ARO-INHBE, ARO-ALK7) | $2.5–4.5B | $17–31 |
| zodasiran and remaining wholly-owned | $0.5–1.0B | $3–7 |
| **Midpoint** | | **~$100–105** |

That lands on a **base case of $102**, +16.4% from $87.65, and coincides with the sell-side low target of $100 against a mean of $110.18 and a median of $104. Notably, no analyst in the coverage group of twelve carries a target below spot, and the recommendation mix improved from four strong buy / seven buy / two hold to four strong buy / eight buy / zero hold over two months. We treat that as corroboration, not as independent proof — the target range and the price both embed the same post-SHASTA extrapolation.

The valuation score is 5.0. There is no identifiable mispricing at $87.65: the de-risking event is three weeks old, the stock is 8.2% below its 52-week high, and the convertible strike at $87.07 sits directly under the current price, where convert-arbitrage hedging tends to dampen moves in both directions. The upside is real but the margin of safety is absent.

## Competitive Landscape

The APOC3 field is a two-horse race and Arrowhead is behind on the clock in both indications — roughly eleven months in FCS and twelve in sHTG. Formulary position, prescriber habit and payer step-edits accrue to first movers, and the $215M PRV purchase is a quantified admission of that. Paying 1.7% of market cap in cash for four months of earlier entry implies management values each month of acceleration at roughly $54M. That is rational only if the first-mover disadvantage is large. The alternative reading — that PRVs trade at market-clearing prices and the purchase also removes regulatory timing variance ahead of a launch build — is fair, but the FY2025 10-K's disclosure that Breakthrough and Fast Track designations did not accelerate the FCS review makes the purchase necessary rather than opportunistic.

Arrowhead's positive competitive trajectory sits on the platform rather than the commercial side. The Novartis CNS deal, the Sarepta muscle deal, the Madrigal MASH license and the ARO-ALK7 adipose knockdown collectively validate extra-hepatic delivery, which differentiates TRiM from liver-centric RNAi peers. A separate 10-K risk is directly competitive: another RNAi drug approved first could block Arrowhead's orphan exclusivity under the "sameness" framework, which is live given Ionis's overlapping APOC3 mechanism.

The most underweighted competitive issue is counterparty rather than clinical. Sarepta supplies 72.7% of collaboration revenue, owes four remaining $50M annual installments, may terminate on 30 days' notice prior to regulatory approval, and has already exercised its step-in right. Sarepta's own Q2 2026 revenue fell 34% year over year with adjusted EPS down 68%, and its shares traded near $17 in June, down more than 75% over five years. Sarepta has continued paying — the $200M DM1 milestone was earned in November 2025 and the first $50M installment arrived in February 2026 — and the step-in right may reflect a desire for control rather than retrenchment. But the concentration is extreme and the termination right is short.

## Risk Assessment

The risk score of 4.3 reflects several specific, unpriced, binary exposures rather than generic biotech volatility.

**Platform-correlated toxicology.** All candidates share TRiM chemistry, and the 10-K states that a toxicology signal in one candidate may apply broadly across the platform. Ten-plus programs provide far less diversification than the count implies. Offsetting this, the platform has now cleared an FDA approval and multiple Phase 3s with a favorable liver safety profile — meaningful accumulated evidence against a systemic chemistry problem.

**Ionis patent litigation with no accrual.** Arrowhead filed a declaratory-judgment action in Delaware on 9/10/2025 seeking invalidity of Ionis's US Patent 9,593,333; Ionis sued for infringement in the Central District of California on 9/11/2025 seeking damages over planned plozasiran commercialization. **No contingent liability was recorded at 6/30/26.** An adverse outcome could impose royalties on, or at the extreme enjoin, the only commercial product.

**Regulatory non-transitivity.** The 10-K states directly that FCS approval does not predict sHTG approval — rare-disease endpoints differ from broad-population requirements, and a larger, less severely afflicted population raises the safety-margin bar. Ionis's June 2026 sHTG approval de-risks the pathway conceptually; Arrowhead's filing is still pending.

**Single points of failure.** REDEMPLO drug substance and drug product are each single-sourced from one third-party manufacturer, and US distribution runs through an exclusive agreement with one specialty pharmacy whose potential financial failure the 10-K explicitly contemplates.

**Secured debt and covenant structure.** IP-collateralized 15% PIK debt with a MOIC floor, a $250M liquidity covenant, and restrictions on licensing six named core assets constrain the business-development strategy that funds operations.

**Financing.** Roughly $1.1B of deployable liquidity against $185M quarterly gross outflow, before sHTG launch spend. A raise is more likely than not before the sHTG launch is fully funded, and the convertible ceiling is exhausted.

**Unresolved item.** An 8-K filed 6/16/2026 under Item 5.02 reports an officer departure whose identity we could not establish. If the role is commercially critical it is thesis-relevant; we flag it as unverified rather than assume either way.

**Policy.** Most-favored-nation drug pricing, IRA, and proposed tariffs of up to 100% on imported branded pharmaceuticals are sector-wide and largely priced, but they compress the terminal value of a franchise whose economics depend on US premium pricing. The 10-K also concedes it is possible the company has already experienced a Section 382 ownership-change limitation on its NOLs.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 50.1% against 63.8% one-year realized, a −13.7 point spread and the 10th percentile within the current chain. Term structure is in contango (50.1% at one month, 53.5% at three, 56.0% at six), put/call skew is balanced at 1.8%, and open-interest put/call is 0.50 — call-tilted. ATM IV has fallen from 58.8% on July 24 to 50.1% on August 8 while the price rose 1.0%, a standard post-event vol crush.

The reading is that the options market treats the August 30 ESC late-breaker as low-risk confirmation rather than a binary. Given that the detailed dataset will invite direct comparison against olezarsen's CORE/CORE2 results — a comparison we regard as methodologically invalid but which the market will make anyway — that looks complacent. The September 18 tenor prices a ±14.9% move and captures ESC; for investors who want exposure to the sHTG and obesity optionality, cheap implied volatility argues for expressing it through calls rather than adding stock at the convertible strike. Short interest of 9.5% of float and 3.4 days to cover is moderate and creates no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($135, +54%)

SHASTA-3/4 is a hard-endpoint Phase 3 success on triglycerides and acute pancreatitis, and the PRV secures FDA action around mid-2027. Quarterly dosing wins meaningful share against monthly olezarsen in a market Ionis itself sizes above $3B in peak sales, and second-mover position in a large chronic indication with a differentiated administration schedule is a far better place to be than second in an orphan indication. The ESC dataset in August holds up and the sNDA files on time. Obesity is the re-rating vector: ARO-ALK7's 88% adipose knockdown and ARO-INHBE's near-doubling of weight loss on top of tirzepatide position Arrowhead as the only company with clinical evidence of RNAi adipocyte silencing, and Phase 2 combination data — or a partnership on Novartis/Sarepta-scale economics — would add $25–30 per share on its own. Contracted inflows (Sarepta installments, a $100M GSK trigger on first Phase 3 HSD dosing, Madrigal and Sanofi milestones) reduce equity financing need, and management's financing execution has been capable: $700M of zero-coupon paper with capped calls that neutralize dilution to $119.33.

### Bear Case ($55, −37%)

Being twelve months late into sHTG proves as costly as the $215M PRV purchase implies. Olezarsen holds formulary primacy and step-edits, plozasiran's ramp resembles the FCS launch, and the market discovers that quarterly dosing is a convenience argument rather than a share-shifting one. Burn of $185M per quarter meets a launch build, forcing an equity raise at a discount — the January 2026 offering priced at $64.50, 26% below spot. The Ionis litigation, carrying no accrual, resolves adversely and imposes royalties on the only approved product. Sarepta retrenches or terminates, removing 72.7% of collaboration revenue and the milestone tail that supports part of the pipeline's value. Obesity disappoints in a field crowded with orals, amylins and dual agonists. The Sixth Street facility's $416M fair value, $235M above carrying, is a claim ahead of equity that any carrying-value EV understates. At $55, roughly $7.8B of market value, the stock capitalizes cash plus partnered value plus a modest plozasiran franchise.

### Base Case ($102, +16%)

sHTG is approved in mid-2027 and plozasiran builds a real but second-place franchise; obesity advances to Phase 2 without yet being valued as a commercial asset; the licensing engine continues to close mid-sized deals; one dilutive financing occurs at or near current levels. Probability-weighting 25% bull, 45% base and 30% bear gives roughly $96, about +10% from spot. That is a positive expected return with no margin of safety and a downside dominated by events outside management's control.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold). Existing owners should stay; new capital should wait.**

**Base case target $102** — risk-adjusted sum-of-parts midpoint (net cash post-PRV net of Sixth Street fair value $6.5/sh + plozasiran $43.5 + partnered portfolio $20.5 + obesity $24 + other wholly-owned $5), coinciding with the sell-side low target of $100 and median of $104. **+16.4% from $87.65.**

**Bull case $135** — sHTG share capture ahead of expectations plus an obesity Phase 2 or partnership event. **+54%.**

**Bear case $55** — sHTG launch stalls behind Ionis, adverse patent outcome, or dilutive raise on a Sarepta retrenchment. **−37%.**

**Timeframe:** 12–18 months, anchored to dated events — ESC late-breaker August 30, 2026; sNDA filing before year-end 2026; PRV closing subject to HSR in Q4 FY26; FDA action around mid-2027; YOSEMITE completion mid-2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. REDEMPLO net product revenue disclosed at or above $15M in a single quarter, or an sHTG launch trajectory showing the ramp is materially faster than the FCS analogue.
2. Obesity Phase 2 combination data confirming the week-16 weight-loss effect, or a licensing deal for ARO-INHBE/ARO-ALK7 on Novartis-scale economics (≥$200M upfront).
3. A share price in the **$66–72 range** on unchanged fundamentals, which would move the base case to roughly +45% and restore a genuine margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Sarepta termination notice, a missed annual installment, or a further step-in that materially cuts collaboration revenue.
2. An adverse ruling or the recording of a material contingent liability in the Ionis patent litigation.
3. Any sHTG regulatory delay — a refuse-to-file, an sNDA slip past Q1 2027, or a CRL — or the ESC dataset showing an efficacy or safety profile that undercuts the quarterly-dosing case.
4. An equity raise priced more than 15% below the prevailing market alongside a covenant-liquidity approach toward the $250M floor.

**Next reassessment:** immediately after the ESC presentation on August 30, 2026, and again on the FY2026 10-K, which should disclose REDEMPLO net sales as a separate line, resolve the PRV closing, and clarify the June 2026 officer departure.

## Conclusion

Arrowhead is a better company than it was twelve months ago, and the score trajectory (5.7 to 6.2 over five reviews) reflected that correctly. SHASTA-3/4 converted the single largest binary in the story into a favorable outcome, the platform's extra-hepatic claim now has clinical corroboration in adipose tissue, and the balance sheet holds $1.57B of resources against modest capex. What has changed since the last review is not the thesis but the funding arithmetic and the counterparty picture: SG&A up 52%, R&D up 22%, a $215M cash outflow for the PRV, consensus losses widening 22% in ninety days, and Sarepta — 72.7% of collaboration revenue, holder of a 30-day termination right — reporting a 34% revenue decline of its own.

At $87.65 the stock sits 8.2% below its 52-week high, three weeks after an 18% de-risking pop, directly on top of a $87.07 convertible strike, and at the same market capitalization as a competitor generating roughly 90 times more revenue with a twelve-month head start in both shared indications. The sell-side range of $100 to $126 is entirely above spot and the base case supports roughly +16%, but the bear case is −37% and is driven by events — a patent ruling with no accrual, a launch race against an entrenched first mover, a probable financing — that no amount of platform quality controls. Own it if you own it. Add on weakness toward the $66–72 range, on disclosed sHTG launch traction, or on an obesity partnership that puts a market price on the least-valued part of the pipeline. **HOLD.**
