## Executive Summary

MGE Energy is a small regulated utility holding company serving approximately 170,000 electric and 180,000 gas customers in Dane County, Wisconsin, a single, economically strong metropolitan area anchored by UW-Madison, state government, and a growing technology sector. The company is in the early stages of a $2.0 billion capital expenditure program (2026-2030) focused on coal-to-gas/renewables transition, which drives the primary earnings growth thesis through rate base expansion.

The stock has declined approximately 16% from its 52-week high of $91.49 to $76.38, driven largely by the rising interest rate environment (30-year yields above 5%) pressuring utility valuations broadly, plus a sharp sell-off in early May (likely on the Q1 earnings release date or shortly after). Q1 2026 earnings of $1.32/share significantly beat consensus of $1.13, suggesting the underlying business is performing well. At ~19.6x forward earnings with a 2.5% dividend yield, the stock trades at a modest premium to electric utility peers (median 18.4x) but lacks a clear catalyst for re-rating given the financing headwinds and data center moratorium blocking the most compelling incremental growth narrative.

The core tension: a sound regulated utility with visible rate base growth executing an ambitious investment program, offset by external factors (interest rates, tariffs, political opposition to data centers) that constrain both valuation multiple expansion and earnings upside potential. This is a hold-for-income situation with modest total return expectations.

## Company Overview

MGE Energy operates through its principal subsidiary, Madison Gas and Electric Company (incorporated 1896), which holds a monopoly franchise for electric distribution in a 264 square-mile service area and gas distribution across seven south-central Wisconsin counties. The holding company structure includes:

- **Regulated Electric** (~63% of net income): 170,000 customers, ~1,100 MW of generation capacity including 253 MW solar, 93 MW wind, and 11 MW battery storage
- **Regulated Gas** (~12%): 180,000 customers, pure distribution (no production)
- **Nonregulated Energy** (~18%): Ownership in Elm Road coal units and UW-Madison cogeneration facility
- **Transmission** (~7%): 3.6% interest in American Transmission Company (ATC)

The company is a MISO market participant, regulated by the Public Service Commission of Wisconsin (PSCW) with an authorized ROE of 9.8% for the 2026-2027 rate period. Net-zero carbon electricity target by 2050.

## Growth Analysis

Growth for MGEE is mechanically driven by rate base expansion:

- Electric rate base: $1.24B (2025) → $1.35B (2026) → $1.54B (2027) — a 24% increase over two years
- Five-year capex plan: $2.0 billion (massive relative to a $3.0B market cap company)
- Authorized ROE: 9.8% — each dollar of approved rate base earns approximately $0.098 pre-tax for shareholders

The rate base growth supports 5-7% EPS CAGR through 2030, which combined with the 2.4% dividend yield, implies 7-9% total annual returns if the valuation multiple holds steady.

Growth catalysts:
1. **Renewable/gas conversion investments**: $914M in remaining generation/storage projects through 2030
2. **RockGen acquisition** ($203M pending PSCW approval): 168 MW natural gas addition
3. **Dane County population/economic growth**: Madison remains one of the fastest-growing metro areas in the Midwest
4. **Data center load growth** (long-term option): If moratoriums are eventually lifted, even one hyperscale facility in the service territory could add 50-100 MW of load to a system this size

Growth constraints:
1. **Earnings sharing mechanism**: ROE above 9.8% (+15bp) must be shared 50/50 with customers; above 9.8% (+75bp) is 100% returned — this structurally caps upside
2. **Data center moratoriums**: Both City of Madison and Dane County have blocked hyperscale development
3. **Solar tariff uncertainty**: Section 232 investigation and retroactive duty risk could increase project costs or force delays
4. **Tax credit phase-out**: Projects must begin construction by July 4, 2026 to qualify for full ITC/PTC — creating execution pressure

## Competitive Landscape

As the sole electric distribution utility in Dane County, MGEE faces no direct competition for its core customer base. The competitive dynamics that matter are:

1. **Regulatory relationship**: The PSCW sets rates, approves capital investments, and determines allowed returns. MGEE's constructive regulatory relationship is evidenced by the 2026/2027 rate case approval at 9.8% ROE.

2. **Economic vitality of service territory**: Madison/Dane County is anchored by UW-Madison (45,000+ students, major research institution), Wisconsin state government, Epic Systems (healthcare IT), and a growing biotech/tech sector. Population growth supports organic load growth without dependency on data centers.

3. **Relative size**: At $3B market cap with 726 employees, MGEE is tiny versus WEC Energy (~$30B) or Alliant Energy (~$15B). This creates both disadvantages (less procurement leverage, higher per-customer cost of compliance) and advantages (concentrated management attention, simpler operations).

4. **Clean energy positioning**: 253 MW solar + 93 MW wind already operational; net-zero by 2050 goal with clear intermediate targets. This positions the company well with the politically progressive Madison community and aligns with corporate/institutional customer preferences.

The competitive position is stable and improving as rate base investment grows the earnings power of the franchise. The primary competitive risk is that data center operators choose locations outside MGEE's territory (in Alliant or WEC territory), leaving MGEE without access to the largest new load growth opportunity in the Midwest.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Interest Rate / Financing Risk (HIGH):**
With $2.0B in capex against $263M annual OCF, MGEE must raise approximately $1.2-1.5B in external capital over five years. At current 30-year rates of 5.2%, each $100M debt tranche costs approximately $5.2M annually in incremental interest — directly compressing net income if not fully recovered through rate increases.

**2. Solar Tariff / Supply Chain (MODERATE-HIGH):**
Section 232 investigation, retroactive duty risk from USCIT ruling, and FEOC restrictions could increase solar project costs by 15-30% or delay construction past tax credit deadlines. For a company planning $500M+ in renewable investment, even a 15% cost overrun would meaningfully compress returns.

**3. Execution Risk on $580M Peak Capex Year (MODERATE):**
2027 capex is 2.2x the company's 2025 operating cash flow. Managing this spending while maintaining service quality, meeting regulatory requirements, and financing efficiently requires execution well beyond the company's historical experience base.

**4. Earnings Sharing Cap (MODERATE):**
The regulatory mechanism structurally limits upside to approximately 9.8% + 75bp = 10.55% ROE before 100% clawback. Any outperformance from favorable weather, efficiency gains, or load growth above forecast is returned to customers — creating an asymmetric payoff profile (limited upside, full downside exposure).

**5. Data Center Moratorium (MODERATE — OPTION VALUE AT RISK):**
The 18-month Dane County moratorium (introduced May 2026) and City of Madison restrictions effectively remove the data center load growth thesis from the near-term investment case.

## Conclusion

MGE Energy is a well-run, conservatively managed regulated utility executing a substantial capital investment program in an economically vibrant service territory. The fundamental earnings growth trajectory of 6-7% annually is visible and supported by approved rate base additions. However, at ~19.3x forward earnings — slightly above the peer median with degrading analyst sentiment and the stock trading above consensus targets — there is no compelling reason to own the stock for capital appreciation in the near term.

The thesis for owning MGEE is income: a 2.4% yield on a 50% payout ratio with visible earnings growth supporting continued dividend increases. Total return expectations are approximately 8-10% annually (dividend + EPS growth) if the multiple holds, but with meaningful downside risk if interest rates rise further or execution on the capex program falters. The stock is appropriately priced for what it is: hold for income, reassess on valuation compression or data center catalyst emergence.
