**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

MGE Energy is a small Wisconsin-based regulated utility ($3.0B market cap) serving ~170,000 electric and ~180,000 gas customers in the economically resilient Madison, WI area. The company is executing a $2.0 billion capital investment cycle over five years, expanding its rate base at roughly 10-12% annually. Q1 2026 earnings grew 15.8% YoY, reflecting early benefits of this investment program, with the Wisconsin PSC approving a 9.8% authorized ROE for 2026-2027.

The investment case hinges on whether rate base growth will translate into sufficient earnings accretion to offset dilution from required external financing ($500-700M needed over the next two years through debt and equity). At $76/share, the stock trades at approximately 19-20x forward earnings — a premium to the peer median P/E of 18.4x. This premium is justified by service territory quality and growth trajectory, but leaves limited room for error given solar tariff risks, tax credit phase-out pressures, and the general headwind of elevated long-term interest rates compressing utility valuations broadly.

Analyst consensus has degraded — the most recent strong buy was downgraded to hold, and the consensus target of $73.50 sits below the current price. Short interest at 5.6% with 4.8 days to cover suggests informed skepticism about near-term returns.

## Company Overview

MGE Energy, organized in 2001 as a holding company, operates primarily through its subsidiary Madison Gas and Electric. The business is structured across five segments: Regulated Electric (~63% of net income), Nonregulated Energy (~18%), Regulated Gas (~12%), Transmission Investments (~7% via a 3.6% stake in American Transmission Company), and a minimal corporate segment.

The service territory centers on Dane County, Wisconsin — home to the University of Wisconsin-Madison, state government, and a growing technology sector. This provides above-average customer growth for a Midwest utility and positions the company to capture data center demand, though the city of Madison has enacted a temporary moratorium on new large-scale data center development.

MGE's generation portfolio is transitioning from coal toward renewables and natural gas. The Elm Road units are scheduled to convert from coal to gas by 2032, while the company has $584M in planned solar investments and $224M in battery storage projects.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:**
Net income has grown from $111.0M (2022) to $135.9M (2025), representing a 6.9% CAGR. EPS grew from $3.07 to $3.72 over the same period. Q1 2026 came in at $1.32/share (+15.8% YoY), driven by rate base expansion. Full-year 2026 consensus sits at $3.95, implying ~6% growth from 2025.

**Operating Metrics:**
- Operating margin: 32.1% — strong for a regulated utility
- ROE: 10.4% (close to authorized 9.8%, indicating efficient capital deployment)
- ROA: 4.3%

**Balance Sheet & Leverage:**
Total assets reached $3.16B in 2025, up 12% YoY. The regulatory capital structure is 56% equity / 44% debt — conservative by utility standards. Long-term debt stands at $792M, with $94.5M in short-term commercial paper. The company holds $180M in committed credit facilities. Leverage is appropriate for the business model and well within the 65% maximum debt-to-total-capital covenant.

**Cash Flow Reality:**
Operating cash flow was $263M in 2025, but capital expenditures of $343M produced negative free cash flow of approximately -$80M. This gap widens materially in 2026 ($395M capex) and especially 2027 ($580M capex), requiring $500-700M of external financing. MGE issued $90M in new senior notes in January 2026 and has begun DRIP equity issuance. Shareholders should expect 1-2% annual dilution.

**Dividend:**
The $1.85/share annual dividend ($67.6M) yields approximately 2.4% at current prices. Dividend growth has been ~5% annually ($1.76 in 2024 to $1.85 in 2025). The payout ratio of ~50% provides reasonable coverage given regulated earnings stability.

## Growth Analysis

**Rate Base Expansion — The Core Driver:**
The authorized average electric rate base is growing from $1.24B (2025) → $1.35B (2026) → $1.54B (2027) — a 24% expansion over two years. At the authorized 9.8% ROE, this mechanically drives earnings growth of 8-10% annually from the electric segment alone. The pending RockGen natural gas plant acquisition ($203M, closing expected late 2027) would add further rate base once PSCW-approved.

**Rate Case Outcomes:**
December 2025 settlement approved electric rate increases of 0.15% (2026) and 3.63% (2027), with gas increases of 2.77% and 2.04%. The authorized ROE was raised 10bps to 9.8%. These outcomes confirm the constructive regulatory relationship.

**Data Center Optionality:**
The 10-K explicitly added data center risk factors and management notes "growing interest from large-load customers." For a utility with ~800 MW peak demand, even one 100-300 MW data center would be transformative. However, the Madison moratorium creates near-term uncertainty. This is a free call option that's difficult to value but could re-rate the stock materially if it materializes.

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current-year EPS estimates are essentially flat (30-day revision +1.2% for current quarter, -0.1% for full year). Next-year estimates have risen 3.0% over 90 days. These are modest, non-actionable revisions — consistent with a steady utility rather than an accelerating growth story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $76.09, MGEE trades at:
- Trailing P/E: 20.5x ($3.72 EPS for 2025)
- Forward P/E: ~19.3x ($3.95 consensus for 2026)
- Forward P/E (2027): ~17.9x ($4.25 consensus)
- Dividend yield: 2.4%

**Peer Comparison:**
The peer median P/E is 18.4x (range: 13.2x for Otter Tail to 43.8x for TXNM). MGEE's 19-20x multiple represents a modest premium to the median, warranted given above-average rate base growth and service territory quality. However, the market is pricing in much of the growth story.

**Rate Environment Pressure:**
With long-term interest rates elevated, the cost of capital for utilities has risen materially. The 2.4% dividend yield offers minimal income spread over risk-free rates, which has driven the sector-wide derating visible in MGEE's 16% price decline over the past year. This macro headwind persists as long as long rates remain elevated.

**Analyst Targets:**
The consensus price target of $73.50 (range: $70-$77) sits below the current price of $76.09. Morgan Stanley's $77 target (raised from $73 in February 2026) represents only 1% upside. This provides no margin of safety from a sell-side perspective.

## Competitive Landscape

**Monopoly Economics:**
As the sole electric provider in Dane County, MGE operates with no competitive threat to its core distribution business. The regulatory compact provides cost recovery assurance and reasonable returns on invested capital.

**Service Territory Quality:**
Madison, WI is one of the strongest small-city economies in the Midwest: University of Wisconsin-Madison anchors a healthcare-education-government complex, while the tech sector (Epic Systems, Exact Sciences) provides above-average growth. Population growth in Dane County has consistently exceeded state averages. This translates into steady customer additions without the commodity exposure of more industrial-focused utilities.

**Renewable Transition Positioning:**
MGE's pivot toward solar and battery storage positions it well for the clean energy transition, but creates execution risk around tariff impacts and supply chain constraints. The $584M solar pipeline is exposed to Section 301 tariffs (now at 50% on cells/modules), antidumping duties, and UFLPA enforcement.

**Size Constraints:**
At $3.0B market cap with minimal analyst coverage (1-2 analysts), MGEE attracts limited institutional interest and trades with lower liquidity than larger peers. This creates pricing inefficiency in both directions.

## Risk Assessment

**Solar Tariff and Supply Chain Risk (HIGH):**
Multiple overlapping tariff actions create cost uncertainty for the $584M solar pipeline. Section 301 tariffs raised to 50%, antidumping duties on Southeast Asian imports, UFLPA enforcement on polysilicon, and a pending Section 232 investigation on polysilicon could collectively increase project costs 15-30%. MGE expects regulatory cost recovery but timing lag creates earnings pressure.

**Financing Risk in Elevated Rate Environment (HIGH):**
The $500-700M financing need will be executed into a market with elevated long-term Treasury yields. New debt issuance costs substantially more than the legacy portfolio, increasing interest expense. If MGE needs to issue equity at current depressed levels ($76 vs. $91 52-week high), dilution per dollar raised is 20% worse than at peak prices.

**Tax Credit Phase-out (MODERATE-HIGH):**
The One Big Beautiful Bill Act terminates PTC/ITC for solar/wind placed in service after December 31, 2027 unless construction begins by July 4, 2026. MGE must begin construction on all qualifying projects within weeks. Battery storage is exempt, providing partial offset via the $224M storage pipeline.

**Earnings Sharing Mechanism Cap:**
If MGE earns above 9.7% ROE, it retains the first 15bps, shares 50% of the next 60bps, and refunds 100% of remaining excess with ratepayers. This structurally caps upside in favorable scenarios.

**Options Market Signal:**
The options market shows IV at 26.1% vs. 20.3% historical volatility — a 5.8pp spread indicating the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 26.1% > long-term 23.8%), suggesting specific imminent stress rather than long-term structural concern for a utility stock. Put skew at 9.8% is elevated. The 3-month tenor shows implied move of ±13.2%, substantial for a regulated utility. These signals are consistent with unresolved uncertainty around solar tariffs and the OBBBA deadline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($85-90, +12-18%):**
- Data center interest in Madison area materializes, adding 100+ MW of load growth
- Rate base expansion drives 8-10% annual EPS growth through 2028
- Long-term rates decline, re-rating utilities broadly
- Solar tariff costs fully recovered through rate cases with minimal lag
- Forward P/E of 20-21x on $4.25 (2027) = $85-89

**Bear Case ($65-68, -10-15%):**
- Long rates remain elevated, utility sector continues derating
- Solar project cost overruns create regulatory lag and temporarily depress ROE below authorized levels
- Equity dilution at depressed prices (1.5-2%/year) erodes per-share growth
- Data center moratorium persists, removing growth optionality
- Multiple compresses to 16x on $4.00 EPS = $64

**Base Case ($78-82, +3-8%):**
- Rate base growth delivers 6-7% EPS growth
- Modest multiple compression as rates remain elevated
- Dividend provides 2.4% income floor
- Total return of 5-9% over 12 months — roughly in line with utility cost of equity

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines below $70 (forward P/E < 17.5x), providing meaningful valuation support vs. growth trajectory
2. Data center development is announced within MGE's service territory, confirming load growth optionality
3. Long-term Treasury yields decline materially, triggering broad utility sector re-rating

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. PSCW denies or significantly delays cost recovery for solar tariff-related overruns
2. Equity issuance at prices below $70 (>2.5% dilution per year vs. expected 1-2%)
3. Q2/Q3 2026 earnings miss driven by higher-than-expected interest expense from financing activities

**Reassessment Timeline:**
Next natural reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings (expected early August). By then, the July 4, 2026 construction-begin deadline will have passed, clarifying tax credit eligibility for the solar pipeline.

## Conclusion

MGE Energy is a well-run, conservatively managed utility with genuine growth catalysts (rate base expansion, data center optionality) in an attractive service territory. At ~19-20x forward earnings with analyst targets below the current price, degrading sell-side sentiment, and significant near-term execution challenges (financing in elevated rate environment, solar tariff uncertainty, tax credit deadline), the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling in either direction.

The 16% price decline over the past year reflects legitimate macro headwinds (elevated long rates compressing utility multiples) rather than fundamental deterioration — but at current levels the stock is fairly priced for its growth trajectory, not cheap enough to warrant a strong conviction entry. The 2.4% dividend yield provides a modest income floor while key uncertainties resolve. Hold with a lean toward accumulation on further weakness below $70.
