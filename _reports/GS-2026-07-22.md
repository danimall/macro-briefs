**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Goldman Sachs is running at the highest profitability in its history. Q2 2026 produced record revenue of $20.3B (+39% YoY), EPS of $20.98 (+92% YoY, a 40.7% beat over the $14.91 consensus), and an ROE of 23.5% / ROTE of 25.5%, well above the firm's mid-teens through-cycle target and the ~13-15% delivered in 2025. Consensus is chasing the numbers upward: current-year EPS estimates have been revised +19% over 90 days to $70.78, and the firm has beaten every quarter for the last four. Global Banking & Markets posted a record $15.5B quarter, Goldman became the first bank ever to cross $1 trillion in announced M&A volume in a six-month period, and investment-banking backlog sits at a five-year high even after record production.

The analytical question is how much of this is repeatable. Management is candid that these are cyclical peak earnings levered to a specific "AI CapEx super-cycle," with CEO David Solomon flagging coming "recalibrations" and "more greed than fear" in the market. The record equities line (+60% intermediation, +91% financing) is concentrated in a rapidly built Asia prime-brokerage book, and the supplementary leverage ratio fell 40bps to 4.3%, the lowest among peers, as balance sheet was deployed into that financing. Three analysts pressed on concentration on the Q2 call; management declined to quantify. The durable offset is Asset & Wealth Management: $4 trillion in assets under supervision, record $3.4B quarterly management fees (+20%), and 34 consecutive quarters of long-term net inflows.

At $1,085.56 the stock trades at ~15.3x current-year consensus and ~14.7x next-year, near the top of its analyst target range (median $1,150) and up 54% over the trailing year. That is not expensive against the earnings being delivered, but the earnings are running hot, the multiple already reflects the momentum, and Financials as a sector has been a structurally negative-alpha group. This is a well-run franchise at a cyclical high with balanced rather than asymmetric risk/reward. We rate GS a HOLD (growth-hold rationale), continuing the sequence of HOLDs while acknowledging the fundamentals have improved even as the score trajectory has drifted lower on valuation and governance grounds.

## Company Overview

Goldman Sachs is a globally systemically important bank operating through three segments:

- **Global Banking & Markets (GBM)** — the profit engine, comprising M&A advisory, equity/debt underwriting, FICC, and Equities (intermediation plus financing/prime brokerage). Q2 2026 GBM revenue was a record $15.5B with a 25% first-half segment ROE.
- **Asset & Wealth Management (AWM)** — the strategic "durable revenue" priority. Total assets under supervision reached a record $4 trillion, wealth-management client assets ~$2T, alternatives AUM $459B, and management/other fees a record $3.4B/quarter (+20% YoY).
- **Platform Solutions** — the wound-down consumer business (~$221M/quarter). GreenSky was sold in 2024, the GM credit-card program transferred in 2025, and the Apple Card relationship is being unwound.

The central strategic thesis is the "One Goldman Sachs" flywheel: boardroom advisory relationships cascade into financing, capital-markets execution, prime brokerage, and wealth-management referrals (~900 IB-to-wealth referrals since 2025). Goldman's genuine differentiation is the #1 global M&A franchise, a ~$425B lead over its closest peer in H1 2026, and global scale in equities. The consumer strategy has been fully reversed, removing the drag that drove the 2023 earnings trough of $8.5B.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory (net income):** 2022 $11.3B → 2023 $8.5B (trough, consumer write-downs) → 2024 $14.3B → 2025 $17.2B (record). 2026 is running dramatically higher, with Q2 net income implying an annualized pace well above 2025.

**2026 segment drivers:**
- **Equities:** record $7.4B quarter, intermediation +60%, financing +91%, driven by a deliberate Asia prime-brokerage build-out tied to AI capital formation.
- **Investment banking:** equity underwriting +130% (SpaceX IPO and Alphabet raise as lead-left), debt underwriting +75% (best quarter on record), advisory +17%, backlog at a five-year high.
- **FICC:** +32%, financing at a record.

**Capital and returns:** CET1 12.9% (standardized), 150bps above the 11.4% requirement; SCB 3.4%. Share count fell from 352M (2022) to 313M (2025), roughly 11%, a structural EPS tailwind, with $4B repurchased in Q2 2026 alone. The dividend was raised 25% to $5.00/quarter, up 150% over five years. The efficiency ratio improved 320bps to 58.8% (H1 2026), and the compensation ratio fell to 31%.

**Reading a bank's balance sheet correctly:** standard FCF and Altman-Z screens are not meaningful for a G-SIB. Total assets of $1.81T, equity of ~$125B, ~13x leverage, $66.8B gross interest expense, and negative reported "operating cash flow" (-$45B in 2025) all reflect trading-inventory and financing-book mechanics, not distress. The relevant health metrics (CET1, SLR, ROE/ROTE, and inflow durability) are strong, with one caveat: the SLR is the lowest in the peer group and has been declining as balance sheet was deployed into low-RWA financing. Financial Health scores 7.5: the capital position is comfortable and the earnings engine is generating capital rapidly, but the thinning leverage ratio and the fact that the earnings base is running above through-cycle norms temper a higher mark.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision picture is positive. Current-quarter EPS estimates have moved +17.8% over 30 days, current-year +19.0% over 90 days to $70.78, and next-year +12.6% to $73.59. The firm has beaten consensus for four consecutive quarters, most recently by 40.7%. Under the framework that treats +30% 90-day revisions as a strong buy signal, GS is close but sits at ~19% on the current year: meaningful positive momentum, not the extreme velocity that overrides other considerations.

The critical qualifier is that this growth is largely cyclical, not structural, and largely sector-wide, not GS-specific. JPMorgan reported +86% equities-trading growth in the same quarter; the big banks collectively posted a "$55B quarter." The AI-capital-formation super-cycle driving equity underwriting, prime financing, and M&A is lifting the entire complex. Management is explicit that trading and banking "don't move in a straight line" and that recalibrations are coming.

The durable component is AWM: $4T AUS, 34 consecutive quarters of long-term net inflows, a record $3.4B quarterly fee base growing 20% YoY, and recent bolt-ons (Industry Ventures for VC secondaries, the announced Innovator Capital Management for buffered ETFs) plus new OCIO mandates from Verizon and Lockheed Martin (~$70B combined). This fee annuity is the most important structural bull case and the argument that a higher through-cycle ROE floor has been established. Growth Prospects scores 6.8: strong near-term momentum with rising estimates, tempered by the recognition that the peak is cyclical and much of it is beta to a sector-wide capital-markets boom.

## Valuation Assessment

At $1,085.56, GS trades at approximately 15.3x current-year consensus ($70.78) and 14.7x next-year ($73.59). On trailing EPS of $51.32 the multiple is ~21.2x. Against the peer set, Morgan Stanley trades at 19.3x, Schwab 17.6x, Raymond James 14.2x, with a peer-median P/E of 19.3x. On forward multiples, GS is cheaper than the closest large-cap peer.

The complication is that the "E" is cyclically elevated. A ~15.3x multiple on peak earnings is not the same as a 15.3x multiple on normalized earnings. If 2026 ROE of ~23% reverts toward the mid-teens through-cycle target as AI-cycle deal volumes and prime-financing revenue normalize, the effective forward multiple on normalized earnings is meaningfully higher. The valuation discipline here matters: a low multiple on peak earnings is a classic value-trap configuration, and the analytical record shows cheap-looking multiples sort the wrong way when the cheapness reflects cycle position rather than genuine mispricing.

Analyst targets (mean $1,134, median $1,150, high $1,325, low $730) put the stock at ~6% below median with a wide dispersion that reflects the durability debate rather than consensus conviction. GS is a cyclical at a fresh 52-week high with earnings running above trend, so the "analyst targets are floors" principle does not apply cleanly; the targets embed the same cyclical optimism as the price. The 1.9% dividend yield (recently raised 25%) provides modest support but is not a valuation anchor.

Valuation scores 5.5: the multiple is reasonable-to-cheap on reported earnings but full on normalized earnings, and there is no specific catalyst to argue the market is mispricing the stock in either direction. This continues the valuation-dimension slide (5.5 → 4.5 across recent reports); we hold at the midpoint rather than penalize further because the price has moved sideways-to-down from its mid-July high while estimates rose, which is a modest improvement in the entry multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

Goldman's competitive position is strong and, in the franchise businesses, improving. It is the #1 global M&A advisor, the first bank to cross $1 trillion in announced volumes in a six-month period, with a ~$425B lead over its nearest competitor. It is global-scale in equities, and the One Goldman Sachs cross-sell model is producing measurable referral flow into wealth management. Backlog at a five-year high after record production suggests share gains are not merely a one-quarter artifact.

The structural pressures cited in the 10-K are real but slow-moving: electronic-trading commission compression, fintech/stablecoin/digital-asset disintermediation, and the risk that rivals deploy AI faster. None of these is repricing the franchise today. The more immediate competitive nuance is that much of the 2026 strength is shared across the bulge bracket, so Goldman's relative share gains in advisory are the cleaner signal of genuine competitive advantage versus the sector-wide trading tailwind. Competitive Position scores 7.0: leadership in the businesses that matter most, with an improving trajectory in advisory and wealth, offset by the recognition that peers are riding the same cycle.

## Risk Assessment

**AI-CapEx cycle dependence (the swing factor).** The extraordinary 2026 results are levered to an AI-infrastructure financing super-cycle. A fade in AI CapEx, deal volumes, or single-stock dispersion would compress the exact lines driving the beat. Management's own "recalibrations" language points the same way.

**Asia equities-financing concentration (the most under-appreciated risk).** The record equities result concentrates in a rapidly scaled Asia prime/financing book, and the SLR fell to 4.3% (lowest among peers) as balance sheet was leaned into it. Three analysts probed client concentration on the Q2 call; management would not quantify. This is a genuine emerging exposure that could reverse violently in a de-grossing event.

**Regulatory/capital.** G-SIB surcharge, Basel III endgame finalization, and SCB volatility could constrain buybacks and dividends. Management is lobbying for a "more balanced" framework, an outcome that is not yet settled.

**Governance/compensation.** Solomon's 2025 total comp was $118.9M, with "compensation actually paid" of $309.8M (inflated by mark-to-market of PSU awards vesting into a soaring stock), layered on the contested ~$80M 2025 retention awards. Defensible on 5-year TSR (375 vs. peer 203), but the absolute magnitude and special-award structure are live say-on-pay watch-items.

**Structural.** Holding-company single-point-of-entry resolution (Group Inc. holders could bear complete losses while subsidiaries continue), social-media-driven deposit-run risk (SVB precedent), and legacy legal/reputational exposure. Two unresolved Item 5.02 executive/director changes (Feb and Mar 2026) could not be characterized from primary sources and remain an open thread.

Risk Factors scores 5.0: the AI-cycle dependence and the undisclosed Asia-financing concentration are material, specific, and not fully priced, while the governance and capital items are known but two-sided.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term caution. Near-term ATM IV of 36.2% is rich against 30.6% historical vol (a 5.6pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (36.2% near vs. 32.5% at six months), pricing imminent stress rather than a durable vol regime. Put/call skew is elevated at 3.8% near-term, and the put/call volume ratio of 1.13 leans defensive, though the six-month skew flips negative (-3.2%), suggesting longer-dated positioning is less bearish. Short interest is negligible at 2.2% of float (2.6 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short or squeeze dynamic. The market is hedging near-term event risk into a stock at a 52-week high, consistent with the balanced risk/reward view. This confirms rather than contradicts the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($1,325).** The AWM fee annuity ($4T AUS, 34 straight quarters of inflows, 20% fee growth) has established a structurally higher ROE floor, so the market is under-crediting the durability of what looks like peak earnings. The M&A franchise's record backlog and share lead extend the capital-markets cycle. Continued ~11%/five-year share-count reduction plus a 25%-raised dividend compound per-share value. If ROE holds in the high-teens rather than reverting to mid-teens, the stock re-rates toward the high analyst target.

**Bear Case ($730).** The AI-CapEx cycle recalibrates, deal volumes and single-stock dispersion fade, and the Asia prime-financing book, leaned into a 4.3% SLR, suffers a de-grossing event. ROE reverts toward the through-cycle mid-teens, earnings fall 25-35% from the 2026 peak, and a peak-cycle multiple compresses onto lower earnings. This matches the analyst low target and implies ~33% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The fundamentals justify continuing to own the franchise through the cycle, but the entry point near a 52-week high on cyclically elevated earnings, combined with structurally negative Financials-sector alpha, argues against adding at current levels.

**Base case target: $1,180.** ~16.5x normalized-forward EPS of ~$72 (splitting current-year $70.78 and next-year $73.59), a slight premium to today's forward multiple to reflect the improving AWM fee floor. This is ~9% above the current price and sits between the bear ($730) and bull ($1,325) cases.

**Bull case target: $1,325** (+22%). **Bear case target: $730** (-33%).

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next several quarterly prints (next earnings mid-October 2026) and Basel III endgame clarity.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Management quantifies the Asia prime-financing concentration and demonstrates it is diversified/capacity-managed, removing the single largest under-appreciated risk.
- AWM fee-revenue growth sustains 20%+ with continued net inflows for two additional quarters, evidencing a durably higher ROE floor rather than a cyclical peak.
- A pullback to the mid-$900s that compresses the multiple to ~13x normalized earnings, restoring asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Equities or investment-banking revenue declines >20% sequentially, signaling the AI-cycle recalibration has begun.
- SLR falls further below 4.3% while equities financing revenue stalls, indicating deteriorating balance-sheet economics.
- A binary regulatory outcome (Basel endgame, SCB) that materially constrains the buyback/dividend program.
- Adverse resolution of the two unexplained Item 5.02 changes or a governance escalation on say-on-pay.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (mid-October 2026) and any material regulatory-capital finalization.

## Conclusion

Goldman Sachs is executing at the top of its game: record revenue, a 23.5% ROE, the #1 M&A franchise, rising estimates, and a growing wealth-management fee annuity that is genuinely durable. The problem for a fresh investment is timing, not quality. The 2026 numbers are a self-described cyclical peak levered to an AI-capital-formation super-cycle and to a rapidly scaled, undisclosed Asia prime-financing book that pushed the leverage ratio to a peer-low 4.3%. At ~15.3x current-year earnings near a 52-week high, the stock is cheap on reported earnings and full on normalized earnings, with analyst targets that embed the same cyclical optimism as the price and a Financials-sector track record of negative alpha. The options market is hedging near-term stress into the strength.

We continue to rate GS a HOLD. The franchise is worth owning through the cycle, the AWM fee base is the credible bull case for a higher ROE floor, and the ~$4B/quarter buyback plus raised dividend support per-share value. The asymmetry is not there at $1,085: upside to our $1,180 base case is ~9% against ~33% bear-case downside, and the two decisive open questions (Asia-financing concentration and cycle durability) will not resolve until the next few prints. Add on a pullback toward the mid-$900s or on confirmation that the AWM-driven ROE floor is structural rather than cyclical.
