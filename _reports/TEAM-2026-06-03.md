**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Atlassian is a software-collaboration franchise built on Jira, Confluence, and Jira Service Management, with a low-touch land-and-expand model serving over 350,000 customers. The stock spent the 2025-26 period at the center of the "SaaS-pocalypse" debate (the fear that generative AI would commoditize developer and collaboration software) and de-rated from the low-$200s to a $56 intraday low (52-week low $56.01) before a Q3 FY2026 print on April 30 reversed the narrative. The quarter delivered revenue of $1.787B (+32% YoY) against ~$1.697B guidance, non-GAAP operating margin of 31.7% (vs. a 27.5% guidance ceiling), and non-GAAP EPS of $1.75 against a $1.35 consensus. The stock rose roughly 22-29% the next session and has since traded into the $85-116 band.

The investment question is whether the inflection is real or an artifact. Three things argue real: Cloud grew 29% standalone, RPO grew ~37% to roughly $4.0B (contractual forward backlog), and >$10K-Cloud-ARR customer count rose to 55,913 from 50,715 a year earlier. Three things argue caution: Data Center grew +44% precisely because it is being sunset (a pull-forward that will reverse in FY28-29), stock-based compensation runs ~23% of revenue and is the entire reason GAAP losses persist, and Rovo AI is bundled free, raising hosting cost today with monetization deferred to FY27+. On balance, the current-quarter estimate-revision velocity (+26% over 90 days), the four-quarter beat streak averaging ~17%, and a forward multiple near 20x on 25%+ organic growth produce a PEG below 1.0. This is the configuration the data has repeatedly rewarded: an inflection name where consensus is chasing the fundamentals upward and the stock is cheap relative to the rising estimates.

We rate TEAM a BUY with a 12-month base case of $155, a bull case toward $200, and a bear case near $80 (the analyst low target and roughly the level the stock found support at on the AI fear). The management-quality and risk dimensions carry the haircuts: three C-suite departures in a single quarter, ~85% founder voting control, and DC-mix lumpiness. But the company's aggressive buyback into its own weakness ($73.53 average in March, $85.04 for the quarter) and the founders' termination of their 10b5-1 selling plans near the lows on February 6 are credible confidence signals from the parties with the most information.

## Company Overview

Atlassian operates as a single reporting segment, with co-founder Mike Cannon-Brookes as CEO and chief operating decision maker. Revenue derives from three deployment models: Cloud ($1.13B in Q3, +29% YoY), Data Center ($561M, +44% YoY), and Marketplace/other ($94M). The company has repositioned its portfolio around "Collections" (curated bundles of apps and AI agents on a single Atlassian Cloud Platform and shared data model) with the Rovo AI layer at the center of the strategy.

The business is highly diversified: no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, roughly 52% of revenue is generated outside the Americas (EMEA the largest single region at ~43%), and more than half of FY25 revenue flows through solution partners. The competitive moat rests on Jira and Confluence functioning as systems of record, the third-party Marketplace network, and switching costs around the connected data model. The 10-K is candid that the market is "fragmented, highly competitive, with relatively low barriers to entry," which keeps the moat narrative honest rather than overstated.

The capital structure carries a dual-class feature: Class B shares (10 votes each), held substantially by co-founders Cannon-Brookes and Farquhar, control roughly 85% of voting power. This entrenches founder control and limits minority influence over any change of control.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue acceleration is high-quality.** Subscription revenue grew 33%. Nine-month revenue reached $4.806B (+25%). Gross margin runs ~85% GAAP and ~89% non-GAAP.

**The GAAP loss is an accounting artifact, not an operating problem.** Q3 GAAP net loss was $(98.4)M, or $(0.38)/share; nine-month net loss was $(192.9)M. That loss is almost entirely stock-based compensation ($408M in Q3, $1.21B over nine months), plus $279.5M of restructuring charges and intangible amortization. On a non-GAAP basis, net income was $456.5M ($1.75/share). The trailing GAAP P/E of -132x is not a usable metric here; the relevant gauge is the forward non-GAAP figure.

**Quantifying the SBC haircut.** SBC of $1.21B over nine months against nine-month non-GAAP earnings is the single largest economic offset, and it deserves explicit treatment. If one fully expenses SBC against the non-GAAP base (the most conservative framing) Q3 non-GAAP net income of $456.5M absorbing $408M of SBC would leave roughly $48M, which would push the economic multiple far above 20x. That framing overstates the dilution, because buybacks of $1.46B over nine months are actively retiring shares (11.8M in Q3 at $85.04 average) and offsetting a meaningful share of the issuance. A more realistic adjustment nets SBC against gross buyback so that only the residual dilution is charged to shareholders: with buybacks roughly tracking SBC dollar-for-dollar over the nine months ($1.46B repurchases vs. $1.21B SBC), the net share-count drag is modest, and the economic multiple sits closer to the mid-to-high 20s than to a triple-digit figure. The honest read is that the printed ~20x forward multiple understates the true economic multiple by perhaps 30-50%, landing the stock in the high-20s to low-30s on a fully-burdened basis, still reasonable for 25%+ organic growth, and well short of the >50x premium-multiple zone where rising estimates stop being a buy signal.

**Free cash flow declined YoY, with an identifiable cause.** Nine-month FCF was $844M, down from $1,055M, driven by restructuring cash payments, higher cash taxes, and Australian transfer-pricing tax dynamics. Q3 FCF was $561M. The decline is restructuring-driven (severance and lease impairments from the March 2026 10% workforce reduction) and should normalize as those cash payments complete in FY2026; it is not a sign of deteriorating unit economics.

**Balance sheet is adequate, and the cash drawdown is deliberate.** Cash fell to $1.14B from $2.51B at 6/30/25, with marketable securities liquidated ($352M in Q3). Long-term debt is $989M (5.25% 2029 notes, 5.50% 2034 notes), the $750M revolver is undrawn, and FCF easily covers interest. The cash decline reflects ~$1.46B of buybacks plus ~$1.23B of acquisitions (DX and The Browser Company) over nine months. The Altman Z-Score "distress" flag is a false positive, distorted by $2.41B of deferred revenue (a non-debt liability), the SBC-driven GAAP loss, and buyback-driven equity reduction. Recurring revenue and cash generation tell the real story.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has both durability and a known wrinkle. The durable elements: Cloud growth of 29% standalone, RPO up ~37% to ~$4.0B as contractual forward backlog, expansion-customer count (>$10K Cloud ARR) up to 55,913 from 50,715, and the Service Collection crossing $1B ARR with what management described as "record displacements" of competitors. Rovo usage was cited as growing 20%+ month-over-month. These point to a land-and-expand engine still compounding well above the multiple.

The wrinkle is Data Center. DC grew 44% specifically because it is being sunset under "Atlassian Ascend" (announced September 2025): no new term-license sales as of March 2026, existing-customer sales and expansions ending March 2028, maintenance ending March 2029. With DC at ~28% of revenue ($1.37B over nine months), this is a pull-forward that will reverse, and the new CFO is steering analysts toward underlying ARR rather than the headline growth number. The key durability question is whether Cloud at 29% can absorb the DC base as it migrates. Management framed a mid-to-high single-digit Cloud growth contribution from DC migrations, which would partly cushion the roll-off. The reversal is an FY28-29 event, outside the 12-month investment horizon, so it bears on the long-term terminal value rather than the near-term thesis.

Estimate revisions confirm the inflection at the consensus level. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose +26.1% over 90 days (to $1.50), current-year +15.6% (to $5.50), and next-year +12.6% (to $6.21), with no period showing deceleration. The four-quarter beat streak averages ~17% surprise (30.0%, 5.1%, 21.0%, 12.2%). That combination, accelerating estimate velocity plus a consistent beat pattern plus revenue +32%, is the strongest forward signal the data offers, and it is present here. We weight it heavily.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$109, TEAM trades near 20x the current-year non-GAAP EPS estimate of $5.50 and ~17.5x the next-year estimate of $6.21. Against 25%+ organic growth, that is a PEG below 1.0 on consensus and below 0.8 on next-year estimates. The trailing GAAP multiple is meaningless given the SBC-driven loss; the forward non-GAAP figure is the operative anchor.

On a fully-burdened basis that charges SBC against earnings net of buyback, as derived above, the effective economic multiple sits closer to the high-20s/low-30s. Even at that more conservative reading, the stock is not in the premium-multiple danger zone (>50x forward) where rising estimates fail to produce re-rating. It is in the band where cheap-relative-to-rising-estimates re-rates as consensus catches up.

Consensus targets frame the asymmetry: low $80.68, median $120, mean $143.18, high $480 (mean dragged upward by outliers). The current price sits below both the median and mean. On an inflection name with rising estimates and a forward multiple under 30x, these targets function as plausible floors, not ceilings; analysts revise upward within weeks of a stock reaching consensus when estimates are climbing. The median of $120 implies ~10% upside before any revision; the mean of $143 implies ~31%.

The valuation score of 7.5 reflects a specific mispricing thesis: the market de-rated TEAM on a structural AI-disruption fear (the stock fell roughly 75% from its 52-week high of $222.59 to its $56.01 low) that the Q3 print directly contradicts, and consensus estimates have not fully repriced the demonstrated growth. The market is being shown that AI is so far accretive (Rovo driving usage and displacements) rather than destructive, and the multiple has not yet fully re-rated to reflect it.

## Competitive Landscape

Atlassian's moat is ecosystem entrenchment: Jira and Confluence as systems of record, the Marketplace third-party app network, and switching costs around the connected data model. The 10-K's candor about low barriers to entry is appropriate; competition spans large suite vendors and point-solution startups, and the filing warns rivals with greater resources may incorporate AI faster, and that large customers may build internal tools.

The trajectory is improving rather than eroding. The Service Collection's growth, Rovo usage, and rising >$10K-ARR customer count all point to share gains and deepening lock-in, not compression. The Data Center sunset is a strategic forcing function: it consolidates the base onto Cloud, where Atlassian controls the platform, can layer AI, and improve long-term monetization, at the cost of near-term revenue-recognition and margin headwinds. The DX (engineering intelligence) and The Browser Company (Arc/Dia enterprise browser) acquisitions extend the platform into AI-era adjacencies. Within the broader software bifurcation (where data-infrastructure layers thrive while application-layer SaaS faces displacement) TEAM sits closer to the infrastructure-of-work side, with Rovo so far reading as a tailwind. That is the central reason the AI-disruption short thesis lost a data point at the Q3 print.

## Risk Assessment

**AI monetization is unproven (the central swing factor).** Rovo is bundled free into standard/premium/enterprise editions, a deliberate retention bet that raises hosting cost with no direct revenue. The eventual paid-AI/usage tier is the key FY27+ margin variable. The risk factors warn TEAM may be "unable to effectively monetize" AI and that competitors may "develop more effective AI products." This is genuine and not yet resolved.

**Data Center pull-forward masks organic deceleration.** The +44% DC growth will reverse; the headline +32% overstates durable organic growth. The CFO's redirection to ARR is appropriate, but it means the next several quarters will produce lumpy, decelerating-looking comparisons as DC rolls off.

**Stock-based compensation intensity (~23% of revenue).** $2.7B of unrecognized RSU comp remains; RSUs outstanding rose to 21.2M. Stock-price weakness pressures retention and recruiting (flagged directly in the 10-K). Buybacks partly offset dilution rather than purely returning capital.

**Leadership churn.** The President, CFO, and CTO all stepped down from executive roles in Q3 FY2026 — unusual concentration. New CFO James Chuong (March 2026), new CRO Brian Duffy (January 2025), and new Chief Product & AI Officer (November 2025) form a freshly assembled bench under founder-CEO Cannon-Brookes. Co-founder Farquhar moved to an advisory role in 2024 and is now a board member only. Execution now depends heavily on a green executive team.

**Margin pressure from Cloud/AI mix shift.** Management guides gross margin "approximately flat" with rising AI/hosting costs.

**Dual-class control.** Founders hold ~85% of votes; minority holders have limited influence.

These risks support a Risk score of 5.5, meaningfully below neutral. They are real and partly idiosyncratic (AI monetization, DC reversal, bench depth), but several are already reflected in a stock that fell roughly 75% from its high and in the GAAP-loss optics.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 95.3% sits well above one-year HV of 62.1% (a 33.1% spread, 83rd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (95.3% near-term vs. 76.8% six-month); the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the high-volatility re-rating the stock has experienced. The skew is call-heavy (-6.2% near-term, put/call volume ratio 0.46, put/call OI ratio 0.52), which does not corroborate the 12.5% short-interest bearish positioning; options flow leans bullish-to-neutral into the move. The rich IV signals elevated uncertainty rather than directional bearishness, and the call-tilted skew is a mild positive divergence against the short interest. The 2.1 days-to-cover figure means the short base is not deeply entrenched and could cover quickly on positive catalysts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$200).** Cloud sustains ~29% growth and successfully absorbs the migrating Data Center base; Rovo converts from a free retention tool to a monetized usage tier in FY27, lifting margins; the four-quarter beat streak continues and consensus estimates ratchet toward $7+ next-year EPS. At ~30x a $7 EPS, the stock approaches $210. The AI-disruption fear is fully repudiated, and the multiple re-rates back toward its pre-fear range. The aggressive buyback at $73-85 proves well-timed, retiring shares near a cyclical low.

**Bear Case (~$80).** The Data Center pull-forward unwinds faster than Cloud can absorb it, exposing organic deceleration into the low-20s and below; Rovo's free bundling permanently pressures margins without a viable paid path; the green executive bench stumbles on the enterprise GTM transition; and SBC dilution plus a softening enterprise-spend environment compress the multiple. The stock retests the analyst low ($80.68) and the level it found support at during the AI scare. Downside from ~$109 is roughly -27%.

**Base Case (~$155).** Organic growth settles in the mid-20s with DC providing a mid-to-high-single-digit Cloud migration tailwind; non-GAAP margins hold near 30%; consensus current-year EPS of $5.50 proves conservative and rises toward $6; the stock re-rates to ~26-28x forward as the AI-tailwind narrative consolidates. That implies ~$155, roughly +42% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $155.** ~28x the next-year non-GAAP EPS estimate of $6.21 (allowing for upward revision from the current $5.50/$6.21 path), reflecting a re-rating as the AI-tailwind thesis is confirmed across two to three more prints. This sits between the bear and bull cases.

**Bull case target: $200.** ~30x a re-rated ~$7 next-year EPS on sustained Cloud growth plus the first credible Rovo monetization data point.

**Bear case target: $80.** -27% from current; the analyst low target and the prior AI-fear support zone, reached if DC unwind outpaces Cloud absorption.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the next three fiscal quarters (next print expected late July/early August 2026 for Q4 FY2026, then Q1 FY2027), which will clarify (a) underlying ARR ex-DC, (b) Cloud growth durability, and (c) any Rovo monetization signal.

**Upside/downside from current (~$109):** +42% to base case, +83% to bull case, -27% to bear case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Cloud growth (standalone, ex-DC) decelerates below 22% YoY on the next two prints, signaling the DC pull-forward was masking real organic deceleration.
2. Non-GAAP operating margin falls below 26% (the prior-year level), indicating AI/hosting costs are eroding the economics faster than expected with no offsetting monetization.
3. Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn negative over a 90-day window, reversing the inflection signal that underpins the call.
4. Resumption of programmatic founder selling (re-establishment of 10b5-1 plans) at prices below current levels, reversing the constructive February signal.

## Conclusion

TEAM is a high-quality, cash-generative, founder-controlled software franchise that was repriced for an AI-disruption scenario the Q3 FY2026 results directly contradict. At ~20x forward non-GAAP EPS (high-20s/low-30s on a fully SBC-burdened basis) against 25%+ organic growth, a sub-1.0 PEG, current-quarter estimate revisions of +26% over 90 days, RPO up ~37% to $4.0B, and four consecutive beats, the stock clears the inflection-buy bar cleanly. The offsets (~23% SBC intensity, a Data Center pull-forward that flatters the headline and reverses in FY28-29 outside the 12-month window, unproven Rovo monetization, and three C-suite departures in one quarter) are real quality haircuts, but they describe a business growing well above its multiple, not a deteriorating one. Management is reinforcing the view with capital: $1.46B of buybacks executed at $73-85 (below current price) and founders terminating their selling plans near the lows on February 6.

The residual concerns do not constitute the roughly-balanced or insufficient-information condition required for a neutral rating. With organic growth above 25%, a PEG under 1.0, accelerating estimates, company and insider capital deployed at the lows, and consensus targets ($120 median, $143 mean) sitting above the current price as plausible floors, the evidence supports a **BUY** with a $155 base-case target over 12 months. The principal risk to size around is the lumpy near-term print quality from the DC roll-off; investors should monitor underlying Cloud ARR rather than headline revenue and respect the invalidation triggers above.
