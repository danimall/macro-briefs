## Executive Summary

Kaspi.kz is the dominant consumer digital ecosystem in Kazakhstan, combining payments, a marketplace, and a deposit-funded consumer lender inside two applications. The engagement data are exceptional: 77 monthly transactions per active consumer, 24.8 annual e-Commerce purchases per consumer, KZT 12.7 trillion (~$26.3bn) of transaction payment volume in a single quarter in a country of roughly 20 million people, and a biometric payments product (Kaspi Alaqan) that management says registered about a third of Almaty's adult population within three months of launch. On the evidence available, there is no credible domestic challenger.

The franchise's earnings have stopped growing. Audited consolidated IFRS net income rose 1.0% in FY2025 to KZT 1,067.7bn, fell 1% year-over-year in 1Q 2026, and was described as "stable" in 2Q 2026. Three forces explain this. First, Kazakhstan raised the corporate tax rate on banks from 20% to 25% effective January 2026, which management guides will lift Kaspi's consolidated effective rate by roughly 200bps, and the National Bank raised minimum reserve requirements in August 2025 with a further step-up in April 2026. Second, funding costs are compressing the largest profit pool: Fintech revenue grew 23% in 2Q 2026 but segment adjusted EBITDA grew only 6%. Third, the Türkiye build-out (Hepsiburada, plus the newly completed Hepsi Bank) contributed an estimated KZT 70-95bn of FY2025 net loss while being deliberately managed to around adjusted EBITDA breakeven. Management's own FY2026 guidance is approximately 5% consolidated adjusted EBITDA growth, a metric it switched to this year, from net income.

At $98.41 the shares trade at roughly 8.4-9.1x trailing earnings, about 8.5x our flat-earnings FY2026 sensitivity, 3.6x parent book value against a still-exceptional 51-53% ROE, and offer a run-rate gross dividend yield of roughly 8.1-8.5%. That is genuinely cheap in absolute terms, and there are two identifiable catalysts: the first Kazakh deposit-rate cut in more than two years (100bps on the three-month product, covering about a third of deposits, in August 2026), and management's statement that the tax and reserve effects will be "fully absorbed into our earnings base by year end," making 2027 a clean comparison. But the shares have already re-rated 43% from the March 13 low of $68.59 to within 2.5% of the 52-week high, sit 4% below the consensus mean target of $102.27, and the ~8% yield depends on separate Extraordinary General Meeting approvals each quarter; the KZT 1,000 second-quarter dividend is still only *proposed*, pending a September 9 vote. Probability-weighted, we see roughly high-single-digit expected total return dominated by the dividend. That is a HOLD for income, not an entry signal.

## Company Overview

Kaspi.kz is a Kazakhstan-domiciled foreign private issuer (CIK 0001985487) that reports on Form 20-F and 6-K under IFRS in Kazakhstani tenge. Two practical consequences shape this report. There is no 10-K, no Form 4 insider filings (Section 16 exemption), and no DEF 14A proxy, so structured insider-transaction and compensation analysis is unavailable for this name. Separately, the filed 20-F narrative (Item 3.D Risk Factors, Item 4 Business, Item 5 MD&A, legal proceedings, the segment note, and the Hepsiburada purchase-price allocation) could not be retrieved during this work. The risk discussion below is built from management's own disclosed headwinds in issuer releases plus our own analysis; it should not be read as a summary of the filed risk factors.

The business reports across three platforms plus Türkiye:

- **Payments** — card and account-to-account processing. 2Q 2026 TPV KZT 12.7trn (+13%), revenue KZT 169bn (+5%), adjusted EBITDA KZT 98bn ("broadly stable").
- **Marketplace** — 3P and 1P e-Commerce, e-Grocery, Delivery, Advertising, Classifieds. 2Q 2026 revenue KZT 508bn (+11%), adjusted EBITDA KZT 128bn (+9%), constant-currency GMV KZT 2.3trn (+15%).
- **Fintech** — consumer lending, deposits, the Kaspi Gold card. 2Q 2026 revenue KZT 455bn (+23%), adjusted EBITDA KZT 171bn (+6%). At 43% of consolidated 2Q adjusted EBITDA, this is the largest profit pool.
- **Türkiye** — Hepsiburada (itself still a separate SEC registrant, HEPS) acquired during 2025, plus Hepsi Bank (former Rabobank A.Ş.), which received BDDK approval June 24 and closed July 15, 2026 at a disclosed equity investment of roughly $300m.

One classification note matters for valuation. The market data label KSPI "Software – Infrastructure," and its SEC SIC code is 7389 (computer services). Neither fits. Forty-three percent of segment adjusted EBITDA comes from consumer lending funded by retail deposits, and the balance sheet carries KZT 11.1trn of assets against KZT 2.6trn of equity. The correct comparable set is emerging-market digital banks and marketplace-plus-lending hybrids, which trade in the mid-single to low-double-digit P/E range, not software multiples. Any argument that KSPI "should" trade at 40x because a US fintech does rests on this misclassification.

## Financial Analysis

### The two sets of numbers

Management's headline figures and the audited consolidated figures for FY2025 measure different things, and the gap is large.

| KZT bn | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,270.6 | 1,913.5 | 2,532.2 | **4,046.1** |
| Growth | +43.6% | +50.6% | +32.3% | **+59.8%** |
| Net income (total) | 588.8 | 848.8 | 1,056.8 | **1,067.7** |
| Growth | +35.3% | +44.1% | +24.5% | **+1.0%** |
| Net income attributable | 585.0 | 841.4 | 1,039.7 | **1,073.2** |
| Net margin | 46.3% | 44.4% | 41.7% | **26.4%** |
| Total assets | 5,121.6 | 6,821.9 | 8,377.1 | **11,081.7** |
| Parent equity | 819.2 | 1,078.1 | 1,517.2 | **2,491.8** |

The FY2025 release states revenue +19% and net income +10% "in line with guidance," under the stated convention that all references exclude Türkiye. Both sets of numbers are accurate. Reconciling them: implied Kazakhstan revenue of 2,532.2 × 1.19 = KZT 3,013bn leaves roughly KZT 1,033bn (~26% of consolidated) from Türkiye, and implied Kazakhstan net income of 1,056.8 × 1.10 = KZT 1,162bn against actual 1,067.7 implies a Türkiye/acquisition drag of about KZT 95bn; running the same bridge off attributable income gives about KZT 70bn. Treat the drag as a KZT 70-95bn range, not a point estimate, since management's "+10%" may not map exactly to the tagged IFRS net income line.

The net margin collapse from 41.7% to 26.4% is mostly mechanical, not economic. Hepsiburada recognizes first-party retail sales gross, so consolidating a gross-basis retailer into a group whose Kazakh marketplace revenue is largely net platform take compresses the blended margin without any underlying deterioration. The practical implication is that **consolidated revenue is now a low-quality metric for this company**, and +59.8% overstates economic growth. But the real deterioration is also present: management reported 4Q 2025 ex-Türkiye revenue +15% converting to net income +1%.

### Returns and capitalization

| Derived | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| ROE (total, on average equity) | 79.0% | **51.2%** |
| ROE (parent) | 80.1% | **53.5%** |
| ROA | 13.9% | **11.0%** |
| Equity/assets (year-end) | 18.8% | **23.5%** |
| Assets/equity | 5.33x | **4.26x** |

The ROE decline is a denominator event. Equity rose 65% because dividends were paused; profit was flat. Capitalization improved materially: equity/assets from 18.8% to 23.5%, leverage from 5.33x to 4.26x. For a deposit-funded lender that is a solvency improvement. A screen reading "ROE down 28 points" would misclassify balance-sheet fortification as distress. The caveat is that we could not obtain Kaspi Bank's regulatory capital ratios, which are the metrics that actually bind a licensed lender; group equity/assets is a proxy.

Credit standing corroborates the picture. In April 2026 Kaspi issued $600m of 5.900% senior unsecured notes due 2031, rated Baa3 (Moody's) / BBB– (Fitch). Investment-grade ratings at both agencies for a Kazakhstan-domiciled issuer is a meaningful credential and reduces near-term refinancing risk. Cost of risk was 2.2% in FY2025, characterized by management as broadly unchanged, and the average net loan portfolio grew 18% year-over-year in 2Q 2026.

What we do not have: the cash flow statement, capex, free cash flow, the absolute loan book and deposit base, NPL detail, total debt and maturity schedule, and goodwill from the Hepsiburada purchase accounting. For a lender, operating cash flow is a weak signal regardless — loan origination and deposit flows dominate. But the absence of the loan book, deposits, and capital ratios is a genuine gap that caps how high we can score financial health on evidence.

### The dividend

This is the most consequential item a surface data pull would miss. The parent-equity roll-forward implies distributions and other movements of –KZT 582bn in 2023, –KZT 601bn in 2024, and only –KZT 99bn in 2025. Management corroborates the fact directly: the FY2025 CEO letter says that following the Hepsiburada acquisition, the company "can now balance targeted growth investments and *resume* dividend distributions." Roughly KZT 500bn of annual shareholder distributions was redirected to the Türkiye build-out and then partially restored.

The restoration path: KZT 850/ADS recommended for 4Q 2025; KZT 850 approved at the June 11, 2026 EGM for 1Q 2026 (a stated 64% payout); KZT 1,000 proposed for 2Q 2026, to be voted September 9, 2026. On 2Q 2026 net income of KZT 259bn across ~189.9m shares (EPS KZT 1,364), KZT 1,000 is a 73.3% payout. If KZT 1,000 per quarter held, the annualized KZT 4,000 converts to $8.32 at KZT 480.5/USD — an **8.4% gross yield** on $98.41. Using declared 1H (850 + 1,000) plus 1,000 twice in 2H gives KZT 3,850, or $8.01 and **8.1%**.

Three reasons this is a sensitivity rather than a forecast: each quarterly dividend requires a separate EGM approval and there is no standing policy; USD receipts are exposed to tenge depreciation; and Kazakh withholding treatment for ADS holders was not verified. Management's own commitment language extends only to "at least the remainder of 2026." A payout climbing from 64% to 73% against flat earnings is durable only if earnings inflect.

## Growth Analysis

Current trading, from issuer releases:

| KZT bn | 1Q 2026 | y/y | 2Q 2026 | y/y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,080.6 | +31% | ~1,105 | **+15%** |
| Adjusted EBITDA | 368 | +9% | 397 | **+5%** |
| Net income | 251.9 | **–1%** | 259 | **stable** |

First-half 2026 net income of KZT 510.9bn compares with roughly KZT 513.5bn a year earlier. Reconstructing the prior-year seasonal split (1H25 ≈ 48.1% of FY2025, given the seasonally strongest fourth quarter) implies FY2026 net income of about KZT 1,062bn — essentially flat versus FY2025, and consistent with guidance of ~5% adjusted EBITDA growth less a ~200bps higher effective tax rate and higher reserve requirements. This is a seasonality-based sensitivity from one year of data, not company guidance.

The monetization engine inside Kazakhstan is working. FY2025 advertising revenue grew 64% and e-Grocery GMV 53%; the Marketplace take rate rose 80bps; in 2Q 2026 the e-Commerce 3P take rate rose 160bps to 16.1% with value-added-services revenue up 49% against e-Commerce revenue up 35%. Kasper, an AI shopping assistant launched in July, reached about 20% of consumers with access within a month, with roughly 80% of conversations producing a tailored recommendation. Management is explicit that Kasper combines third-party foundation models with proprietary intent-ranking and first-party transactional data; Kaspi owns the layer and the data, not the models.

Against that, three deceleration signals are hard to explain away. Kazakhstan Marketplace GMV grew only 11% in FY2025 and 3% in the fourth quarter; management directs attention to "underlying" figures excluding smartphones (+19% FY, +12% 4Q), which is a disclosure choice that flatters the trend — the reported number is +11%. Payments revenue is growing 5% on 13% volume growth, implying take-rate compression from roughly 1.43% to 1.33% of TPV (a 7% decline in the highest-margin platform); management attributes flat Payments EBITDA to Alaqan investment, but merchant-fee competition or mix shift toward lower-yield transaction types would produce the same arithmetic, and we cannot distinguish these from the disclosure available. And Fintech's 23% revenue growth converting to 6% EBITDA growth quantifies the funding-cost squeeze.

Türkiye is the geographic answer and is a different competitive problem: Hepsiburada operates against Trendyol and Amazon in a hyperinflationary economy with lira translation risk imported into the consolidated accounts via IAS 29. The turnaround evidence is real — disclosed 2025 order growth of –11%, +7%, +16%, +19% by quarter, next-day shipment coverage from ~47% to ~63% — but engagement is 6.7 annual purchases per consumer against 24.8 in Kazakhstan, and the fintech overlay is scheduled to expand "from 2027." Nothing in Türkiye contributes to 2026 earnings by design.

**On the estimate-revision signal:** current-year consensus rose 7.5% over 30 days and 1.9% over 90 days, but next-year consensus fell 6.2% over 90 days, and the current-quarter and next-quarter series carry a single analyst. This is not the +30% inflection pattern that reliably precedes re-rating. The company has missed consensus EPS in each of the last four reported quarters (–6.9%, –9.0%, –5.3%, –15.5%), which argues that sell-side models have persistently over-extrapolated growth and that current estimates deserve a haircut rather than confidence.

## Valuation Assessment

Working from the authoritative $98.41:

- **Trailing earnings.** The provider reports EPS of $11.03, giving 8.9x. Deriving from FY2025 attributable net income of KZT 1,073.2bn across ~189.9m shares gives KZT 5,651, or $11.76 at KZT 480.5/USD — 8.4x. At KZT 520/USD the multiple is 9.1x. Call it **8.4-9.1x trailing**, with the range driven by the FX convention rather than by the earnings.
- **Forward.** On the flat-earnings sensitivity (KZT 5,592/share, $11.64) the multiple is **~8.5x**. Forward and trailing are effectively the same because earnings are not growing.
- **Book.** Parent equity of KZT 2,491.8bn across ~189.9m shares is KZT 13,122, or $27.31 — **3.6x book** against 51-53% ROE. Cross-checking: 3.6x book divided by 51% ROE implies about 7.1x earnings, roughly consistent with the P/E if ROE normalizes toward the low-40s as the payout resumes.
- **Yield.** Roughly **8.1-8.4% gross** on the run-rate.
- **Market cap / consolidated revenue** 2.2x, though revenue quality is now mixed given gross-basis Turkish 1P sales.

Is this a mispricing or a justified discount? Both, in part. The justified portion: ~90% of profit comes from one ~20m-person emerging market whose government has just raised the bank tax by five percentage points; the reporting currency has structural depreciation risk against the dollar; governance visibility is limited by the FPI exemptions; analyst coverage is thin (seven ratings, 4 buy / 3 hold, and one-to-three analysts behind the estimate lines); and the dividend is not contractual. Emerging-market digital banks with concentrated sovereign exposure routinely trade at 6-12x. Kaspi at 8.5x is inside that band, not obviously below it.

The mispricing case rests on two dated catalysts rather than on the multiple alone. First, funding costs. Management's guidance explicitly assumes no rate cuts. Fintech's 23%-revenue-to-6%-EBITDA conversion is the single largest earnings drag; if the funding cycle is turning, guidance embeds conservative bias. Second, management states the tax and reserve effects are "fully absorbed into our earnings base by year end," which would make 2027 a clean comparison off a flat 2026 base.

The counterweight is entry timing. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, and consensus targets on cheap inflecting stocks are often stale — but this is not an inflecting earnings stream. With FY2026 net income tracking flat and next-year estimates cut 6.2% over 90 days, the re-rating from the March low of ~5.8x to 8.5x has already captured a meaningful part of what the catalysts are worth. What remains is the dividend plus a modest multiple move.

**Scenario framework:**
- **Base $105** — 9.0x on ~$11.60 of FY2026 EPS. Assumes the dividend is approved and held near KZT 1,000/quarter, tax and reserve headwinds annualize out, and the funding-cost relief is partial. +6.7% price, ~+15% total return including one year of dividends.
- **Bull $125** — 10.5x on ~$12.00-12.50 of 2027 EPS, requiring the Kazakh rate-cut cycle to extend, Payments take rate to stabilize, and Türkiye to move from a KZT 70-95bn drag toward neutral. Consistent with the high analyst target of $124.96. +27%.
- **Bear $70** — 6.5x on ~$10.00 of USD EPS after roughly 15% tenge depreciation, with the multiple compressing on either a further sovereign policy step, a trimmed dividend, or an adverse disclosure from the unread legal and risk sections. Near the 52-week low of $68.59. –29%.

Weighting 50/25/25 gives an expected price of about $101, or +2.7%, plus a dividend we would haircut to ~6% for approval and FX risk — high-single-digit expected total return with a wide distribution. That is fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

Level and trajectory diverge here, which is the crux of the rating.

The level of Kaspi's Kazakh moat is extraordinary. 7.4m e-Commerce consumers in a ~20m-person country, 77 monthly transactions per active consumer, government services embedded in the app, and the ability to enroll a third of Almaty's adults in a palm-based payment product in three months. Switching costs at that engagement depth are high, and we found no evidence of a credible domestic challenger.

The trajectory is mixed. National penetration is close to saturated, which means incremental growth must come from monetization (advertising, delivery, take rate, VAS) or from geography. Monetization is delivering — the 160bps 3P take-rate gain and 49% VAS growth are the marketplace playbook working. But Payments take rate is eroding, reported Marketplace GMV growth has slowed to 11% (3% in 4Q25), and the state is now extracting a larger share of the franchise's economics through the 25% bank tax and two-step reserve increases. A dominant position in a single small market whose government can raise the rent is a moat with a ceiling on its economics.

In Türkiye, Kaspi is a challenger with improving momentum against entrenched competition, importing hyperinflationary accounting and currency translation into the consolidated statements. Management's own disclosure of the engagement gap (6.7 vs 24.8 purchases per consumer; 29% vs 66% engaged-consumer growth) is credible and unflattering, which is to its credit. The transplant thesis — apply the super-app plus embedded-fintech model to 11.8m Turkish consumers, with Hepsi Bank as the vehicle from 2027 — is coherent but unproven, and consumes capital today.

## Risk Assessment

**Sovereign policy risk, confirmed and quantified.** The bank corporate tax increase excludes business lending. Management says the reserve-requirement increases "reduce the interest revenue we can generate." This is the clearest earnings headwind in the file and it is directed specifically at bank profitability in the one country where Kaspi earns nearly all its profit. It is partly self-limiting — management expects it fully absorbed by year-end 2026 — but the precedent is the risk.

**Funding-cost squeeze.** Currently the largest margin drag and the most plausible source of upside. Two-sided.

**Concentration.** Roughly 90%+ of profit from one ~20m-person economy, with the reporting currency exposed to depreciation that flows directly into USD EPS and USD dividend receipts. Our FX assumption of KZT 480.5/USD is load-bearing across the entire valuation and is derived from the issuer's own conversions; it may be a constant January 1, 2026 rate rather than a period average.

**Türkiye execution.** ~26% of consolidated revenue at roughly breakeven adjusted EBITDA and an estimated KZT 70-95bn net loss in FY2025, plus ~$300m of equity into Hepsi Bank. Non-controlling interests swung from +KZT 17.1bn of profit in 2024 to –KZT 5.5bn in 2025, consistent with minorities absorbing part of the Türkiye loss.

**Disclosure and comparability.** The 2026 guidance metric changed from net income to adjusted EBITDA, and Fintech's headline growth metric changed from TFV to average net loan portfolio, in the same year IFRS earnings went flat. Management's stated rationale — that net income is distorted across a 25%-tax high-rate Kazakh business and a hyperinflationary Turkish one — is defensible. It is also true that adjusted EBITDA excludes precisely the three items compressing returns: bank tax, funding costs, and acquisition amortization. Both readings fit the facts; we draw no inference about intent, but the effect on comparability is real. Separately, 2Q 2026 segment revenues sum to approximately consolidated revenue, which suggests Türkiye is now consolidated inside Marketplace whereas FY2025 segment commentary excluded it — this is arithmetic inference, not a stated fact, and it means cross-period segment growth comparisons may be measuring different perimeters.

**Verification gaps that we treat as risk, not as absence of risk.** The filed 20-F narrative — risk factors, legal proceedings, contingencies, related-party transactions, purchase accounting — was inaccessible. We could not verify sanctions or geopolitical exposure in either direction, and we make no factual claim about it; for a Kazakh payments and banking champion adjacent to Russia, an unexamined risk-factor and legal-proceedings section is a material unknown. No cash flow statement, loan book, deposit base, NPL detail, or bank regulatory capital ratios were obtained. No Form 4 or proxy data exists, so insider activity, compensation alignment, board independence, and related-party dealings are entirely unobserved.

**Dividend mechanics.** There is no standing dividend policy; each quarterly payment goes to a separate EGM with a scheduled repeat meeting the next day if quorum fails. It has functioned smoothly, but the ~8% yield rests on recurring discretionary approvals, and the KZT 1,000 for 2Q 2026 is currently a proposal pending September 9.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 37.8% sits 4.6 points above one-year realized volatility of 33.2%; the market is paying up for optionality rather than offering it cheaply. Implied volatility has risen from 34.1% to 37.8% since early June while the stock advanced 15%, which is unusual: rising IV into a rising price generally signals event risk being priced rather than complacency. The nearest expiry prices a ±3.3% move; the December tenor prices ±17.5%, and the term structure is flat (37.8% near versus 38.9% far), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated on a single date. Put/call open interest of 1.10 and the provider's read of elevated put demand (4.2% skew) indicate holders are protecting a position that has appreciated 43% off the low. Short interest of 1.1% of float with four days to cover means no crowded bearish positioning and no squeeze dynamic in either direction. The options market is not pricing a directional break; it is pricing a wide two-sided distribution around a September dividend vote and a currency, and entry volatility is expensive rather than cheap.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($125, +27%)

Kazakhstan's rate cycle turns. The August 2026 100bps deposit-rate cut is the first of several, reversing the Fintech margin compression that turned 23% revenue growth into 6% EBITDA growth. The bank tax and reserve step-ups annualize out of the base by December 2026, giving a clean 2027 comparison against a flat 2026. Monetization inside Kazakhstan keeps compounding — advertising, delivery, VAS and the 3P take rate at 16.1% and rising — offsetting Payments take-rate erosion. Türkiye moves from a KZT 70-95bn drag toward neutral as Hepsiburada order growth (already +19% in 4Q25) sustains and Hepsi Bank begins contributing from 2027. The dividend holds at KZT 1,000+ per quarter, delivering an 8%+ yield while the multiple expands from 8.5x toward 10.5x on visible earnings growth. Total return approaches 35% including dividends.

### Bear Case ($70, –29%)

Earnings stay flat for a third year. The tenge depreciates 15%, cutting USD EPS to ~$10.00 and USD dividend receipts proportionally. Payments take-rate erosion proves competitive rather than investment-driven, and Marketplace GMV growth of 3% in 4Q25 turns out to reflect genuine saturation rather than a smartphone base effect. Kazakhstan takes another bite — the 2026 tax increase establishes a precedent, and a systemically important lender earning 51% ROE is a standing target. A 73% payout on flat earnings proves unsustainable and the board trims the dividend, removing the total-return support. Türkiye requires more capital than the ~breakeven framing implies. The multiple compresses to 6.5x, back toward the March low.

### What would change our mind

The dividend approval on September 9 and the 3Q 2026 print in November are the near-term evidence. What we want to see is Fintech adjusted EBITDA growth reaccelerating toward revenue growth (evidence the funding cycle turned), Payments revenue growth closing the gap to TPV growth (evidence take-rate erosion was investment, not competition), and reported — not "underlying" — Kazakhstan Marketplace GMV growth improving off the 3% fourth-quarter print.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income). Horizon: 12 months, with the first checkpoints on September 9, 2026 (dividend EGM) and the November 3Q 2026 release.**

- **Base case $105** — 9.0x on ~$11.60 FY2026 EPS (KZT 5,592 at KZT 480.5/USD). +6.7% price, roughly +15% total return with a haircut dividend.
- **Bull case $125** — 10.5x on ~$12.00-12.50 of 2027 EPS. +27%. Matches the high analyst target of $124.96.
- **Bear case $70** — 6.5x on ~$10.00 of FX-impaired EPS. –29%. Near the 52-week low of $68.59 and below the low analyst target of $85.61.
- **Probability-weighted (50/25/25):** ~$101 price, +2.7%, plus a discounted ~6% dividend — high-single-digit expected total return with a ±25% distribution. Insufficient asymmetry for ACCUMULATE at $98.41; insufficient impairment for REDUCE.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Fintech segment adjusted EBITDA growth reaccelerating to within 5 points of Fintech revenue growth in either the 3Q or 4Q 2026 print, confirming the funding-cost cycle has turned.
2. A second and third Kazakh policy-rate or deposit-rate reduction extending the August 2026 cut, combined with management removing the "no rate cuts assumed" caveat from guidance.
3. Price weakness to $82-86 (roughly 7.0-7.4x forward, a ~9.5-10% run-rate yield) on unchanged fundamentals — that entry restores the asymmetry independent of any operational change.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. The September 9 EGM fails to approve KZT 1,000, or any subsequent quarterly dividend is set below KZT 850. The income thesis is the rating.
2. Tenge depreciation beyond roughly 10% against the dollar without an offsetting local-currency earnings acceleration.
3. Any further Kazakh legislative or NBK action raising the bank tax or reserve requirements beyond what is already in force, or a full-year 2027 guide implying a third consecutive flat-earnings year.
4. Disclosure from the 20-F or a subsequent 6-K of material legal proceedings, sanctions exposure, or related-party transactions that we have been unable to examine.

**Reassessment date:** the 3Q 2026 results release, expected roughly five to six weeks after quarter-end (mid-November 2026), with an interim check on the September 9 dividend vote.

## Conclusion

Kaspi.kz is a high-quality franchise at a reasonable price with flat earnings and an unusual set of evidence gaps. The Kazakh business is close to a national utility on the engagement metrics, and management communicates with more candor than most — publishing a table showing its own newly acquired Turkish asset is 3.7x worse on its key engagement measure is a credibility-positive disclosure choice. Capitalization improved materially through 2025, the company holds investment-grade ratings from two agencies, and the run-rate dividend yield near 8% is the most attractive quantitative feature of the equity.

What holds us at HOLD is the combination of a flat earnings base, an entry price 2.5% below the 52-week high after a 43% run, and a total-return case that depends on a dividend requiring quarterly shareholder approval in a currency that can depreciate. Consolidated net income has grown 1% in FY2025 and is tracking flat in FY2026; the company has missed consensus in four consecutive quarters; next-year estimates have been cut 6.2% over 90 days; and the guidance metric changed to adjusted EBITDA in the same year IFRS earnings went flat. The catalysts we can identify — the turning funding-cost cycle and the annualization of the tax and reserve headwinds — are real and dated, but a meaningful share of what they are worth has already been paid for in the move from ~5.8x to 8.5x. Existing holders should collect the yield and monitor the September 9 vote and the November print.
