## Executive Summary

Somnigroup International (formerly Tempur Sealy) is the world's largest bedding company, having transformed its business model through the $5.1B Mattress Firm acquisition completed in February 2025. The company now controls the full value chain from manufacturing to retail, operating 2,800+ stores globally. At $62.27, the stock sits 37% below its January 2026 high of $98.56, essentially erasing all gains since the Mattress Firm deal closed.

The decline reflects three simultaneous pressures: (1) a weak consumer environment depressing same-store sales growth, (2) falling analyst estimates (current quarter EPS revised down 21% in 90 days), and (3) market skepticism toward the announced Leggett & Platt acquisition — a third major deal before the second is fully integrated. While the competitive positioning is compelling and cash flow generation remains strong ($246.5M operating CF in Q1 alone), the risk/reward at current levels is balanced rather than clearly favorable. The company needs to demonstrate organic revenue acceleration and continued deleveraging before the stock can sustainably re-rate.

## Company Overview

Somnigroup International operates as the world's largest vertically integrated bedding company through three segments:

- **Mattress Firm** (47% of Q1 2026 revenue): 2,100+ U.S. retail stores plus e-commerce, acquired February 2025
- **Tempur Sealy North America** (31%): Manufacturing and wholesale/direct distribution of Tempur-Pedic, Sealy, and Stearns & Foster brands
- **Tempur Sealy International** (20%): Manufacturing, distribution, and retail across 100+ countries including the Dreams chain (200+ stores, U.K.)

The February 2025 name change from Tempur Sealy to Somnigroup International reflects the holding company structure following Mattress Firm's addition. The company now generates 63.5% of sales through direct-to-consumer channels, with no single customer exceeding 5% of global revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** The organic bedding business was flat for three consecutive years (FY2022-2024, ~$4.93B annually). FY2025 revenue jumped to $7.48B (+51.6%) entirely from the Mattress Firm stub period. Q1 2026 revenue of $1.80B grew 12.3% YoY, primarily from having Mattress Firm for a full quarter vs. partial in Q1 2025.

**Profitability:** Adjusted operating margin expanded to 11.9% in Q1 2026 (from 11.4% YoY), with North America gross margins improving 620 bps from acquisition synergies. Adjusted EPS of $0.59 grew 20.4% YoY. However, GAAP EPS ($1.84 trailing) vs. adjusted EPS ($2.70 FY2025) represents a 47% gap — unusually wide, driven by $264M in acquisition-related charges. Serial acquisitions mean "one-time" costs recur.

**Cash flow quality:** Operating cash flow of $800.1M in FY2025 (up from $666.5M in FY2024) and a record Q1 of $246.5M demonstrate the business can comfortably service its obligations. FCF margin of 8.5% ($633.2M) after $167M capex. Interest expense runs ~$225-230M annually.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $4.58B with net leverage at 3.07x (down from 3.21x at year-end 2025). Variable-rate debt of $2.87B creates ~$28.7M pre-tax income sensitivity per 100bp rate move. Current ratio of 0.78x and Altman Z-Score of 1.43 technically signal distress, though both are distorted by Mattress Firm's lease obligations and acquisition goodwill rather than operational weakness. The credit agreement allows 5.0x maximum leverage with ample headroom.

**Forward estimates:** FY2026 consensus EPS of $3.15 (down 5.4% over 90 days) and FY2027 of $3.91 (down 3.0%). Current quarter estimates slashed 21% in 90 days ($0.72 → $0.57), with a 19.6% decline in the last 30 days alone ($0.71 → $0.57), suggesting Q2 faces meaningful headwinds.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture bifurcates sharply between inorganic and organic:

**Inorganic (strong execution so far):** Mattress Firm synergies are running ahead of schedule. The 620 bps North America gross margin improvement in Q1 demonstrates meaningful cost extraction. Management's credit agreement includes $100M of additional expected synergies not yet fully realized.

**Organic (weak):** The underlying bedding industry was challenged in 2025 due to macroeconomic pressures, and Q1 2026 continued that pattern. Mattress Firm same-store sales were flat. The organic North America business showed minimal volume growth. Only the International segment delivered genuine organic growth at +7.2% constant currency.

**Forward growth drivers:**
- Stearns & Foster relaunch (2026) targeting premium consumers
- Leggett & Platt acquisition (~$2.5B all-stock, expected close year-end 2026) adding component manufacturing
- Continued Mattress Firm store refresh ($75M in 2026 capex allocated)
- International expansion, particularly in Asia-Pacific

**Growth concerns:** Discretionary spending on big-ticket home furnishings faces clear demand compression given current macroeconomic pressures. Mattress purchases are deferrable for 12-24 months, making the category highly cyclically sensitive.

## Valuation Assessment

At $62.27, SGI trades at:
- 19.8x FY2026 adjusted EPS ($3.15)
- 15.9x FY2027 adjusted EPS ($3.91)
- 33.8x trailing GAAP EPS ($1.84)
- ~8.5x EV/EBITDA (estimated, using ~$1.5B adjusted EBITDA on ~$4.58B debt + $13.1B market cap)

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 14.9x (Mohawk, La-Z-Boy, Leggett & Platt, Ethan Allen) sits materially below SGI's 19.8x forward multiple. This premium is partially justified by SGI's superior competitive positioning, higher margins (11.9% vs. peer averages in single digits), and stronger growth profile. However, the premium has compressed substantially from the ~30x+ levels seen in January 2026.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus mean of $97.25 (56% upside), low $76.00 (22% upside), high $115.00 (85% upside). The consensus target was set at higher prices and likely faces downward revision given the negative estimate trend. The low target of $76 represents ~24x FY26E — reasonable for a holding level but not a compelling risk/reward anchor.

**Valuation verdict:** At 20x forward earnings with falling estimates and consumer headwinds, SGI is neither obviously cheap nor expensive. The 37% drawdown has removed most of the acquisition euphoria premium, but the stock needs stabilizing estimates to re-rate. At $50-55 (16-17x FY26E), the risk/reward would become clearly asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

SGI's competitive position is genuinely differentiated and improving:

**Vertical integration moat:** No competitor matches SGI's design-to-retail value chain. The company manufactures (70+ facilities), distributes, and retails (2,800+ stores) under its own brands. This eliminates channel margin leakage and provides customer data access that pure manufacturers lack.

**Brand portfolio breadth:** Tempur-Pedic (premium/specialty), Stearns & Foster (luxury), Sealy (value/mid-market), and Sleepy's (private label) cover every price point. This positions SGI to capture trade-down behavior during consumer weakness rather than losing it entirely.

**Scale advantages:** $33M annual R&D spend (vs. essentially zero for most competitors), global procurement leverage, and manufacturing efficiency that subscale players cannot replicate.

**Regulatory barrier:** The 43% third-party floor slot requirement at Mattress Firm stores is both a constraint and a competitive barrier — it limits SGI's internal flexibility but also creates guaranteed distribution for competitors (Purple, Serta), preventing them from seeking alternative large-format retail partners.

**Key vulnerability:** The wholesale channel faces structural secular headwinds as DTC brands and e-commerce grow. SGI has addressed this by acquiring its own retail, but the remaining wholesale business (~36.5% of sales) remains exposed.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Consumer spending deterioration (HIGH probability, HIGH impact):** Mattress purchases are deferrable for 12-24 months, making the category highly cyclically sensitive. If recession materializes, mattress unit volumes could decline significantly. The current-quarter estimate revision trajectory reflects early evidence of this pressure.

**2. Acquisition execution complexity (MODERATE probability, HIGH impact):** SGI is pursuing L&P (~$2.5B all-stock) while still integrating Mattress Firm. L&P is a diversified industrial manufacturer (steel products, furniture, aerospace) — a very different business from bedding retail. L&P's own Q1 2026 showed 10% revenue decline and withdrawn guidance. The ~9% dilution from all-stock consideration will depress per-share metrics unless synergies materialize quickly.

**3. Leverage in rising-rate environment (MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact):** $2.87B in variable-rate debt creates ~$28.7M pre-tax income sensitivity per 100bp rate move. The company is deleveraging ($4.72B → $4.58B in one quarter), but the pace depends on maintaining FCF levels.

**4. Tariff uncertainty (LOW-MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact):** Management claims minimal material impact in 2026, but adjustable bed bases and certain components sourced from China face tariff exposure. The uncertainty creates margin visibility problems even if actual impact is manageable.

**5. Estimate revision momentum (HIGH probability, continuing):** The revision trend has not stabilized. Further cuts are possible if consumer weakness intensifies through H2 2026.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$85-95, 36-53% upside):**
- Mattress Firm synergies exceed targets; FY26 adjusted EPS comes in at $3.30-3.50
- L&P deal closes smoothly with immediate component margin capture
- Consumer spending stabilizes in H2 2026; Stearns & Foster relaunch drives ASP improvement
- International segment accelerates to 10%+ constant-currency growth
- Leverage reaches 2.5x by year-end, unlocking buyback acceleration
- Multiple re-rates to 25-27x on execution confidence

**Bear Case (~$45-50, 20-28% downside):**
- Consumer recession deepens; Mattress Firm same-store sales turn negative
- FY26 EPS revised to $2.50-2.70 as volume declines offset synergies
- L&P integration proves problematic; industrial segments deteriorate further
- Leverage remains above 3x, limiting capital return and increasing refinancing concern
- Multiple compresses to 17-18x on execution doubt and macro headwinds
- Variable-rate debt cost increases further if rates remain elevated

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current quarter estimates stabilize or reverse (30-day revision turns positive)
- Mattress Firm same-store sales turn positive (+2% or better for a full quarter)
- Stock reaches $50-55 range (16-17x FY26E), offering 2:1+ upside/downside asymmetry

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY26 EPS estimate falls below $2.80 (additional 11% cut from current $3.15)
- L&P deal terms deteriorate or antitrust challenges emerge requiring material divestitures
- Net leverage rises above 3.5x (reversing current deleveraging trajectory)
- Management cuts or suspends the dividend

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026) will provide a full quarter of Mattress Firm with transparent same-store comps, L&P deal progress, and updated synergy guidance.

## Conclusion

SGI is a well-positioned company operating in a challenging environment with elevated execution risk. The 37% drawdown reflects genuine fundamental headwinds (falling estimates, consumer weakness, acquisition complexity) rather than sentiment-driven mispricing. At 20x forward earnings with negative revision momentum, the stock requires estimate stabilization before offering compelling risk/reward. The competitive position is strong and the cash flow profile supports the leverage, but the timing for re-engagement depends on macro conditions that are currently deteriorating. HOLD with a clear path to upgrading if estimates stabilize near current levels or the price reaches the mid-$50s.
