**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

GDS Holdings is a leading carrier- and cloud-neutral data center operator concentrated in China's Tier-1 hubs (Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen), now structured as two distinct value pools: the domestically-listed China business and a ~19.9% minority stake in DayOne Data Centers, the deconsolidated pan-Asian platform that is preparing a dual Singapore/US IPO at a reported $10-20B valuation. The investment case rests on two pillars: real, AI-driven demand for Chinese data center capacity (record ~200MW net bookings in Q1 2026, pre-commitment on under-construction area up to 84.4%) and the prospect of crystallizing a DayOne stake the company marks at >$2.2B against GDS's ~$7B market cap.

The complication is that the reported numbers do not describe the operating business. FY2025 GAAP net income of RMB959M was flattered by a RMB2,364M deconsolidation gain and RMB716M of equity-method income, partly offset by a RMB1,561M Q4 impairment on leased data centers; core income from continuing operations before these items was a small loss of roughly RMB56M. Q1 2026's headline +247% net income surge and 78.8% net margin were almost entirely a RMB2,137M DayOne dilution gain. Normalized revenue and EBITDA growth runs a steadier ~8-11%, and the operating business sits near breakeven at the net level. Any scoring keyed off trailing GAAP earnings would badly misread this company.

The binding constraint is the balance sheet. With ~RMB46B total debt (including RMB4.3B convertible bonds now current), ~RMB1.6B annual interest expense consuming essentially all operating income, and 2026 capex guided to nearly double to ~RMB9B, GDS is not self-funding from operating free cash flow. The equity story depends on continued access to Chinese debt markets and on monetization channels: C-REIT, ABS, and DayOne share sales. That dependency, layered onto PRC regulatory/delisting overhang and AI-chip supply timing risk (the May 2026 Beijing-side halt of Nvidia H200 deployments), keeps this a HOLD despite genuine demand tailwinds. We rate GDS HOLD with a growth-hold rationale; the DayOne IPO is the catalyst worth waiting for, but the path to it carries enough refinancing and execution risk that the risk/reward is not asymmetric.

## Company Overview

GDS develops and operates high-performance data centers in China with a 26-year operating history, serving hyperscale cloud providers, large Chinese internet companies, financial institutions, telecom carriers, and multinationals. Its competitive position derives from scarce Tier-1 land and power resources, large net floor area, high power density, and deep relationships with the largest Chinese cloud customers. As a foreign private issuer, GDS files Form 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q; financial figures are RMB-denominated, with full-year numbers company-labeled "preliminary unaudited."

The structural reorganization completed in 2024 is central to understanding the equity. The former international business (Southeast Asia, Singapore-HQ'd) was carved out as DayOne Data Centers and deconsolidated, leaving GDS with a ~19.9% minority equity investment that is independently funded through Series C convertible preferred raises. GDS is therefore a China data center operator plus a listed proxy for a high-growth pan-Asian platform whose value the public market has not yet stamped. The company marks its residual DayOne stake at >US$2.2B (~US$11/ADS) at the April 2026 Series C price.

## Financial Analysis

**Operating scale and growth.** FY2025 net revenue reached RMB11,432M, up 10.8% YoY from RMB10,322M. Q1 2026 revenue of RMB3,367M was +23.6% reported but +7.9% excluding one-time items, the cleaner number. FY2026 revenue guidance of RMB12,400-12,900M implies +8.5% to +12.8%. Adjusted EBITDA was RMB5,403M in FY2025 (+10.8%, ~47.3% margin, stable), with FY2026 guidance of RMB5,750-6,000M. This is a steady high-single-digit to low-double-digit grower at the operating level, with margins holding.

**Earnings quality.** The reported profitability is dominated by non-operating items and must be adjusted to be meaningful. The 78.8% Q1 2026 net margin is not an operating figure. The earnings-surprise record (Q1 2026 +4,414%, Q4 2025 +7,306%) reflects these one-time marks against near-zero estimates, not operating beats. The operating business is roughly EBITDA-positive and breakeven-to-modestly-loss at the net level.

**Cash flow and leverage — the swing factor.** FY2025 operating cash flow was RMB3,365M against RMB4,611M capex, leaving the business modestly FCF-negative before monetization. With 2026 capex nearly doubling on the AI investment cycle, the FCF gap widens materially. As of March 31, 2026, total debt was ~RMB45.9B (short-term RMB9.4B including the now-current convertible bonds; long-term RMB36.5B) against cash of RMB14.8B, for net debt of ~RMB31B. The company raised RMB13.9B in 2025 and roughly US$685M in early 2026 (US$385M DayOne partial sale plus a US$300M convertible preferred), confirming that solvency depends on refinancing and monetization access rather than operating self-funding. Equity is ~RMB28.4B with an accumulated deficit of RMB2.4B (improved from RMB5.1B as gains flowed through).

**Operating KPIs are genuinely strong.** Area utilized rose 12.7% YoY to 520,929 sqm; utilization climbed to 77.3% (from 75.7%); commitment rate on in-service area was 92.8%; and pre-commitment on under-construction area jumped to 84.4% from 66% a year earlier, a strong forward-demand signal. Q1 2026 net new bookings of ~200MW were the highest single quarter on record.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is real and broad-based. China data center capacity is projected to nearly double by 2030 (power demand ~289 TWh, ~19% CAGR), with AI/HPC sharply raising power intensity. GDS's record bookings and 84% pre-commitment on construction pipeline indicate it is capturing a disproportionate share of this demand from its scarce Tier-1 positions. This sits within the structural AI infrastructure theme confirmed by hyperscaler capex commitments and multiple independent data points across the global build-out.

The estimate revision picture is mixed and requires care. Current-year EPS estimates have risen sharply (+14.3% over 30 days, +1,192% over 90 days to $4.85), but that revision is driven by DayOne dilution-gain marks flowing into GAAP, not by operating inflection. Next-year EPS estimates have been cut 35.8% over 30 days and 38.9% over 90 days to $1.09, reflecting consensus normalizing away from the one-time gains. This is the opposite of a clean earnings-inflection signal where forward estimates rise on operating momentum; the current-year revision is an accounting artifact. We therefore do not treat the +30% revision threshold as a clean BUY trigger here: the velocity reflects non-operating gains, and the forward-year cut is the more honest read of the operating trajectory.

The genuine growth lever is the conversion of record bookings into revenue as new capacity ramps, plus the realization of DayOne value. Revenue recognition lags bookings, and AI-chip availability (the H200 Beijing halt) can push tenant move-ins right, which explains why guidance disappointed even amid record demand. The durable operating growth is the ~8-11% normalized rate, with optionality from faster AI ramp and from DayOne.

## Valuation Assessment

GDS is best evaluated as a sum-of-the-parts rather than on a P/E multiple, given the non-operating earnings. The ~$7.0B market cap (200.4M ADS at ~$34.80, or ~$36.91 most recently) comprises: (1) a leveraged China data center business generating ~RMB5.4B adjusted EBITDA at ~47% margin, and (2) a ~19.9% DayOne stake the company marks at >US$2.2B. Net debt of ~RMB31B (~US$4.5B) is the offset.

On the China business, ~$11B-12B enterprise value (market cap plus net debt, less the DayOne mark) against ~RMB5.4B (~US$0.77B) EBITDA implies roughly 14-16x EV/EBITDA: not cheap for a leveraged, slow-growing, capital-intensive operator, but not stretched for a Tier-1 data center platform with 47% margins and record bookings. The asymmetric upside is DayOne: a successful IPO at the reported $10-20B valuation would crystallize a stake worth ~$2-4B, a material fraction of GDS's market cap, and could re-rate the parent. The Street is underwriting precisely this, with a mean target of ~$53.88 (range $36.15-$70.45) against ~$35-37 spot, implying ~50% upside concentrated in the DayOne re-rating and AI demand.

The trailing/forward P/E of ~19.3x is not meaningful here given the GAAP distortion; we discount it. The valuation is reasonable rather than compelling: there is identifiable upside (DayOne crystallization), but it is contingent on an unpriced media-sourced IPO and on continued refinancing access. We score valuation modestly above neutral: the catalyst is real but unconfirmed, and the leverage caps how much of any re-rating accrues to equity.

## Competitive Landscape

GDS occupies a strong competitive position with improving trajectory in its core market. The binding constraints in Chinese data centers are increasingly power availability and AI accelerator supply rather than demand, and GDS's scarce Tier-1 land/power resources, high power density, and record construction-pipeline pre-commitment indicate it is winning the capacity race in the densest demand hubs. Its pioneering use of domestic capital-recycling vehicles (C-REIT, ABS) differentiates it from peers and partially offsets its leverage by providing a monetization channel unavailable to most competitors.

The two-engine structure of domestic operations plus DayOne ownership gives GDS optionality on the highest-growth regional platform without bearing its direct funding burden. The principal competitive risk is customer concentration among a handful of large Chinese cloud/internet customers (the Alibaba/Tencent/ByteDance cohort), which the company's filings have historically flagged. Margin stability at ~47% and rising utilization suggest pricing and competitive position are holding rather than eroding.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and refinancing (primary).** The model requires continuous access to Chinese debt and monetization channels to cover the convertibles coming current, annual interest, and doubled 2026 capex against modestly negative operating FCF. Any tightening of China credit or capital markets is acute for the equity.

**Earnings-quality dependence.** GAAP profitability is driven by DayOne dilution and deconsolidation gains, not operations. The RMB1.56B 2025 impairment on leased data centers (lower sale prices, fixed lease terms) signals asset-value pressure in parts of the portfolio.

**China regulatory / VIE / delisting.** Standard PRC regulatory, data-security, and PCAOB/HFCAA-type risks apply, plus Tier-1 power-allocation and energy-efficiency regulation that constrains where capacity can be built.

**AI-chip supply timing.** Bookings are surging, but revenue conversion lags as capacity ramps, and GPU availability is a swing factor outside GDS's control. The H200 deployment halt illustrates how tenant move-ins can be pushed right.

**Dilution and capital structure.** Multiple convertible preferreds (e.g., US$300M Series B to Huatai at ~$54.43 conversion, ≥3.75% dividend) plus convertible bonds drive a diluted share count of 2.03B vs. 1.55B basic, meaningful potential dilution.

**Governance.** Audit Committee Chair Lim Ah Doo resigned in May 2026, replaced by Hua (Kathy) Chen. No irregularity has been disclosed, but audit-chair turnover during a complex non-operating-gain reporting period warrants monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is constructive-to-neutral and does not contradict the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV of 55.1% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 59.7% (cheap by 4.6%, 23rd percentile within the chain), and the 1-month put/call skew is call-heavy at -6.2%, with a P/C OI ratio of 0.53. The term structure is in contango (55.1% near vs. 64.1% at six months), a normal, non-stressed shape. The 3-month tenor shows a notably steeper put skew (-15.8%), suggesting some hedging into the summer window around earnings cadence and any DayOne IPO timing, but the near-dated picture leans bullish. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.1%. The signal is consistent with a market pricing a real but two-sided catalyst, not an imminent dislocation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Record bookings convert to revenue as AI-driven capacity ramps; the China business sustains ~10% revenue growth at stable ~47% EBITDA margins. The DayOne IPO prices at the upper end of the reported range, crystallizing a GDS stake worth $3-4B and forcing a sum-of-the-parts re-rating of the parent. Continued C-REIT/ABS monetization and refinancing access keep the balance sheet funded through the 2026 capex cycle. In this scenario the stock works toward the Street's mean/high targets of ~$54-70.

**Bear Case.** China credit tightens or capital-market access narrows precisely as the convertibles come current and capex doubles; refinancing terms worsen and equity bears the cost. The DayOne IPO is delayed or prices below the marked stake value, removing the catalyst. AI-chip supply constraints push tenant move-ins right, slowing revenue conversion and triggering further leased-asset impairments. Forward EPS estimates decline further as the one-time gains roll off. In this scenario the stock retraces toward the analyst-low target of ~$36 and potentially lower toward the prior 52-week low near $24.

The balance between these is genuinely two-sided, which is why we hold rather than buy. The demand thesis is confirmed and the DayOne optionality is real, but the equity sits on top of a leveraged structure whose viability depends on capital-market access we cannot underwrite with confidence, and the key crystallizing catalyst (the IPO) remains media-sourced and unpriced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the June 25, 2026 AGM, the next quarterly print, and any concrete DayOne IPO filing/pricing news, which media reports place within the coming months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed DayOne IPO filing or pricing at or above the marked stake value, which would crystallize sum-of-the-parts value and de-risk the balance sheet via realized monetization.
- Evidence that record bookings are converting to revenue ahead of schedule — i.e., normalized (ex-one-time) revenue growth accelerating above the 8-11% guided range with utilization continuing to climb past 77%.
- A successful refinancing of the current convertibles on favorable terms, removing the most acute near-term balance-sheet risk.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Deterioration in refinancing access — failed or sharply repriced debt issuance, or a covenant/liquidity warning.
- A DayOne IPO delay or down-round, or a markdown of the stake below current carrying value.
- Renewed material impairments on leased data centers, or a guidance cut on the FY2026 revenue / EBITDA framework.
- Escalation of PRC delisting/regulatory risk affecting ADR holders.

## Conclusion

GDS is a real beneficiary of the AI infrastructure build-out in China, with genuine demand evidence (record bookings, 84% pre-commitment, rising utilization) and meaningful hidden value in its DayOne stake. The case for owning it is the sum-of-the-parts re-rating that a DayOne IPO would crystallize, plus durable ~10% operating growth in a power-constrained market where GDS holds scarce Tier-1 positions. The case against high conviction is the leverage: ~RMB46B debt, interest consuming operating income, capex doubling, and an equity story that depends on continuous refinancing and monetization rather than self-funding cash flow, all set against PRC regulatory overhang, AI-chip supply timing risk, and reported earnings that are an accounting artifact rather than an operating signal.

We rate GDS **HOLD** (growth-hold). The catalyst path is identifiable and the upside is material if DayOne crystallizes, but the structure carries enough financing and execution risk that we want confirmation — an IPO filing, a clean refinancing, or accelerating normalized revenue — before treating this as a high-conviction long. Existing holders are positioned for the catalyst; new capital should wait for one of the upgrade triggers, with the analyst-low target of ~$36 and the prior 52-week low near $24 framing the downside.
