## Executive Summary

Angi has spent three years converting revenue into profit, shrinking from $1,891.5M of FY2022 revenue to $1,030.5M in FY2025 while moving operating income from -$126.3M to +$65.4M. The investable version of that story required one thing to be true: that the deliberately shed low-quality revenue (Network leads sourced from third-party sites, down 72% in FY2025) would give way to a growing proprietary business. Through FY2025 it did — Proprietary Revenue grew 17% to $800.6M. In the first half of 2026 it stopped. Proprietary Revenue fell 2% in H1 and 10% in Q2, and total revenue decline accelerated from -3.2% in Q1 to -11% in Q2.

The Q2 10-Q filed August 4 documents the mechanism. U.S. leads fell 13% while revenue per lead was flat at roughly $44.52 (versus $44.13), so this is volume, not price. Average monthly active U.S. Pros fell 17% to 106,000 even as Pros acquired in the quarter rose 13% to 27,000, which locates the problem in retention rather than acquisition. U.S. consumer marketing spend rose 18.8% to $187.9M in H1 against U.S. revenue down roughly 9%, taking marketing to 44.9% of U.S. revenue from 34.5%. The company's own FY2025 risk factor predicted this: third-party traffic arrangements "are generally more cost-effective than traditional marketing efforts," and losing them means sales and marketing as a percentage of revenue "would increase over the long-term." Removing Network demand removed a cheap channel, and paid replacement traffic converts worse.

Three things convert an operational problem into a capital-structure problem. Free cash flow in H1 2026 was -$21.4M (roughly breakeven adding back $21.5M of non-recurring restructuring cash payments) versus +$29.2M a year earlier. The $225.6M U.S. goodwill impairment, tested at a 16.0% discount rate versus 12.0% six months earlier, leaves the U.S. reporting unit's goodwill carried at approximately fair value with disclosed sensitivities showing ~$35.0M of further impairment on a 100bp discount-rate increase. And the $400M of 3.875% notes due August 2028 marked at 84.5 cents at June 30 (from 92.3 cents at December 31), implying a yield near 12% versus roughly 7% two quarters earlier; refinancing at that level would take annual interest from $15.5M to about $48M, or 47% of annualized Adjusted EBITDA. We move to REDUCE from HOLD: expected return from $4.84 is approximately flat with wide dispersion and a bearish skew, and there is no catalyst inside the next two quarters that resolves the erosion.

## Company Overview

Angi operates a two-sided digital marketplace matching homeowners with home-service professionals across 500-plus categories. Consumers pay nothing; Pros pay. The brands are Angi, Angie's List and HomeAdvisor domestically, plus HomeStars (Canada), MyBuilder (UK), MyHammer (Germany/Austria), Travaux (France) and Werkspot (Netherlands/Italy). Reporting is now two segments, U.S. (~87% of revenue) and International (~13%), renamed in Q4 2025 from Domestic/International, the second reporting-structure change in a year.

Gross margin runs near 95% because cost of revenue is essentially the pass-through on pre-priced Services bookings. The real variable cost sits in Selling & Marketing, which was 49% of FY2025 revenue and 58% in H1 2026. IAC spun Angi off on March 31, 2025 by special dividend; all Class B shares converted to a single class, and a 1-for-10 reverse split took effect March 24, 2025. Joey Levin is Executive Chairman; Jeff Kip is CEO.

Following the elimination of the fixed-fee advertising product, the U.S. business is effectively single-product: Q2 2026 U.S. revenue was 91% Lead revenue ($196.3M of $215.4M), 6% Services ($13.3M, -31%) and 3% membership subscription ($5.7M, -26%). The non-lead lines are shrinking faster than the whole.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 2026 (10-Q, filed August 4, 2026).** Revenue $248.0M versus $278.2M, -11%. U.S. $215.4M (-12%); International $32.6M (approximately flat). Operating loss -$233.7M versus +$17.7M, driven by the $225.6M U.S. goodwill impairment and a $9.6M trade-name impairment. Net loss -$230.7M, or -$5.70 per share. Adjusted EBITDA $28.2M (-14%), an 11% margin; U.S. Adjusted EBITDA $21.5M (-22%), International $6.8M (+25%). The tax line was a benefit of only $0.9M because the impairment is largely non-deductible.

**A disclosure that requires care.** U.S. Lead revenue rose 27% to $196.3M while U.S. Advertising revenue went from $64.2M to $0. Combined, the two lines were $218.4M in Q2 2025 versus $196.3M in Q2 2026, a 10.1% decline, which reconciles to the reported -10% Proprietary Revenue. The most plausible explanation is that fixed-fee advertising contracts migrated onto pay-per-lead pricing effective January 1, 2026; a presentation-only reclassification is an alternative reading, and no MD&A sentence explicitly reconciling the Advertising line to zero was located. Under either reading, the +27% Lead figure is not organic growth.

**Cash flow and quality of earnings.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $105.1M against $59.6M of capex, giving $45.5M of free cash flow. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $9.3M against $30.7M of capex. The H1 figure includes $21.5M of restructuring cash payments plus working-capital drags (-$10.8M payables, -$9.4M lease liabilities, -$3.9M receivables build); normalizing for restructuring puts H1 operating cash flow nearer $31M and free cash flow near zero. Home services is seasonally weighted to Q2/Q3, so a simple annualization would overstate the deterioration, but the honest range for 2026 free cash flow is somewhere between slightly negative and modestly positive against $45.5M last year.

Adjusted EBITDA is being flattered by a mix shift in technology spend. Product development expense fell 54% in Q2 ($23.6M to $10.9M) on the 350-person reduction, while depreciation rose 105% from higher capitalized-software amortization and accelerated write-down of the legacy platform. Capex rose to $15.0M in Q2 from about $12.3M. Total technology spend (expensed plus capitalized) fell 28%, not 54%. The cleaner metric: Adjusted EBITDA less capex was $13.2M in Q2 versus $20.8M (-36%), and $20.5M in H1 versus $35.8M (-43%), roughly 2.5x the reported Adjusted EBITDA decline. Capex guidance was also revised upward, from "5-10% below" FY2025's $59.6M in the 10-K to "consistent with 2025 capital expenditures of $59.6 million" in the 10-Q, while revenue guidance moved the other way.

**Balance sheet.** Cash fell from $303.7M to $188.7M in six months. Total current assets $256.2M against current liabilities $214.1M gives a current ratio of 1.20, down from 1.65. Goodwill fell from $890.1M to $662.1M; intangibles $157.1M; shareholders' equity $687.6M, or $16.95 per share on 40.556M shares. At $4.84 that is 0.29x book, but goodwill plus intangibles of $819.2M exceeds equity, so tangible book value is approximately -$131.6M, or -$3.24 per share. The book multiple is not a margin of safety; it measures the market's discount to carrying value of the intangibles.

**Debt.** $500M of 3.875% ANGI Group notes due August 15, 2028, of which $100M principal was repurchased in H1 2026 for $91.2M cash, producing an $8.4M extinguishment gain. $400M remains; net debt is $211.3M. A $175M senior secured revolver signed November 6, 2025 was undrawn at June 30. Two features matter: stated maturity is November 2030 but springs to 91 days before the notes mature (May 16, 2028) unless the notes are refinanced, and the consolidated net leverage covenant is 4.00:1.00 against roughly 2.06x on H1 Adjusted EBITDA annualized. Near-term solvency is not the question. Cash interest of $15.5M against roughly $102M of annualized Adjusted EBITDA is 6.6x covered, and nothing matures before August 2028. The question is the cost of the 2028 refinancing, and the bond market has already answered at roughly 12%.

**Earnings surprise and estimate revisions.** The adjusted EPS record over four quarters is two beats, one in line, one miss, with the miss most recent and large: $0.11 against $0.19 for the June quarter, -42.6%. (Note the basis mismatch: GAAP was -$5.70 including impairments; the surprise table is adjusted.) The revision picture is the dominant forward signal. Next-quarter consensus fell 65% in 30 days to $0.07; next-year consensus fell 29% over 90 days and 37% over 30 days to $0.50; the current-year figure of -$5.74 is mechanical, reflecting the impairment. Adjusted earnings power for 2027 has been cut roughly in half by the four analysts still covering the name.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis left in the U.S. business on current evidence, and there is a small one internationally.

Guidance credibility is the clearest casualty. Reported commentary from the August 2025 and November 2025 calls pointed to mid-single-digit 2026 revenue growth on recovering proprietary volume; February 2026 cut that to low single-digit growth; H1 2026 delivered -7%, deteriorating within the half to -11%. (Those guidance characterizations rest on secondary reporting of earnings calls and were not verified against company documents; the actuals are filing facts.) Three consecutive quarters of growth guidance preceding an accelerating decline raises the discount rate an investor should apply to any forward statement from this management team.

The operating drivers point down. Leads -13%, active Pros -17%, revenue per lead flat, revenue per active Pro +4.3%. The last figure indicates the marketplace is consolidating toward fewer, larger-spending Pros, which supports near-term revenue per Pro while reducing match liquidity. Supply-side stress shows in credit metrics: the allowance for credit losses rose from $15.9M at January 1 to $21.4M at June 30, with $20.8M of H1 provisions against $17.2M of write-offs.

International is the genuine bright spot and is under-discussed. Revenue was flat in Q2 and +3% in H1 (FX-aided) while Adjusted EBITDA grew 25% to $6.8M in Q2 at a ~20.8% margin versus 16.6%. FY2024 to FY2025 International Adjusted EBITDA grew from $16.0M to $27.3M, +71%. International now contributes roughly 24% of segment-level Adjusted EBITDA on 13% of revenue, and its goodwill carries an approximately $200M (~70%) valuation cushion against zero in the U.S. The European businesses run leaner self-serve models. If the U.S. platform migration is intended to import that model, International is the closest thing to proof of concept. It is also 13% of revenue, and cannot offset a U.S. business declining 12%.

Against the estimate-revision framework, this is the inverse of the recovery setup: revisions are falling sharply (-37% next year) while revenue growth is negative and decelerating. There is no basis here for treating rising estimates as a leading indicator, because estimates are not rising.

## Valuation Assessment

Enterprise value: $196.3M equity plus $398.5M carrying debt less $188.7M cash equals $406.1M. Using the bonds' June 30 market value of $338.0M, EV is approximately $345.6M.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / annualized H1 2026 Adj. EBITDA ($102.4M) | 4.0x (3.4x on market-value debt) |
| EV / FY2025 Adj. EBITDA ($140.1M) | 2.9x |
| EV / annualized H1 (Adj. EBITDA less capex, ~$41M) | ~9.9x |
| EV / TTM revenue (~$992.6M) | 0.41x |
| P / next-year consensus EPS ($0.50) | 9.7x |
| P / book ($16.95) | 0.29x |
| P / tangible book (-$3.24) | n/a (negative) |

The multiples look distressed because the market is pricing distress in the cash-flow stream, not because sentiment overshot. Two adjustments matter. First, the denominator: on Adjusted EBITDA less capex, the more honest proxy for cash generation given the capitalization shift, EV is near 10x a declining number. Second, capital structure: net debt of $211.3M is 52% of EV, so equity is a levered residual. A 20% cut to 2027 Adjusted EBITDA at a constant 4.5x multiple takes the equity from roughly $5 to roughly $3.

The sum-of-parts is the strongest bull argument and deserves fair treatment. International Adjusted EBITDA of $27.3M in FY2025, growing 25%, at 8-10x is worth $220-275M — roughly the entire net debt balance and more than the market capitalization. That implies the U.S. business, generating perhaps $85M of annualized segment Adjusted EBITDA, is valued near $150M, or under 2x. That is either a mispricing or an accurate assessment of a business whose active supplier base is shrinking 17% a year with marketing intensity 10 points worse. The evidence favors the second reading, and the two-year post-spin tax-sharing restrictions on stock repurchases, mergers and large asset sales run through approximately March 2027, which forecloses the mechanism by which a sum-of-parts discount would ordinarily be arbitraged.

Peer comparison is of limited use. Advertising-sector peers trade at 30.8x (DV), 49.6x (ZD), 21.3x (TTD) and 285x (OMC) trailing earnings; ANGI has no meaningful trailing P/E. The relevant peer observation is that the cohort is derating broadly (DV -39% from its 52-week high, TTD -82%), so ANGI's -75% is not purely idiosyncratic, though the acceleration in its own volume metrics is.

Sell-side targets — low $5.00, mean $9.20, median $10.00, high $14.00 — sit entirely above spot, but they rest on two to four estimates and have not been marked to the Q2 print or to a next-year number that fell 37% in a month. On a stock where forward estimates are collapsing rather than rising, consensus targets are lagging indicators of the old thesis rather than a floor.

Score: 4.5. Cheap on enterprise multiples, but the discount is compensation for structural erosion, negative tangible book, and a 2028 refinancing that the credit market prices at 12%.

## Competitive Landscape

Angi's own 10-K describes weak structural defensibility with unusual candor. Consumers pay nothing and "can easily switch among home services offerings (or use multiple home services offerings simultaneously) at no cost." For Pros, "the costs for switching to a competing platform over the long term are generally not prohibitive." Management names search engines, online marketplaces and social platforms as competitors that "can market their products and services online in a more prominent and cost-effective manner than we can."

Three vectors follow. First, the platforms Angi depends on compete with it: Angi relies on Google for paid and organic traffic while disclosing that search operators "offer products and services that compete directly with our products and services" and may change rankings "to promote their own competing products." Google Local Services Ads is the obvious instance, and this is a dependency Angi cannot exit.

Second, AI disintermediation targets Angi's exact function. The 10-K names both mechanisms: AI teaching homeowners to self-serve, and AI transforming search and therefore Angi's marketing channels. Angi's response is a fine-tuned large language model interface for service requests built on proprietary domain-knowledge libraries plus third-party model providers, which introduces its own supplier exposure. AI currently appears in Angi's P&L on the cost side — the January 2026 restructuring of ~350 employees was justified in part by "AI-driven efficiency improvements" — while appearing in the risk factors on the revenue side. Causal attribution of the traffic decline to AI search substitution is not established; Angi attributes weakness to macro conditions and the Network wind-down, and the available third-party evidence (trade reporting on contractors buying ChatGPT advertising) is directional only.

Third, the funnel is narrowing for reasons partly outside Angi's control: consent requirements on calls and texts, tightened email deliverability thresholds, and platform restrictions on consumer data access from Google, Apple, Meta and TikTok. Each reduces yield on acquired demand and compounds the homeowner-choice decision.

The trajectory reading is what matters for scoring. Active suppliers -17%, marketing intensity +10.4 points, single-product concentration rising, dependency on a competing distribution platform. Score: 2.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill: zero cushion with published sensitivities.** The May 31, 2026 test (50% income, 50% market approach) found the U.S. carrying value exceeded fair value by $225.6M. Post-charge, "the carrying value of the U.S. reporting unit's goodwill equals its approximate fair value as of June 30, 2026." Disclosed sensitivities: +100bp discount rate implies ~$35.0M further impairment; -100bp long-term growth rate implies ~$20.0M. The U.S. discount rate moved from 12.0% at both October 1 and December 31, 2025 to 16.0% at June 30, 2026. The FY2025 10-K showed a $109.7M cushion at year-end. The 10-Q added a new risk factor on impairment, the only material risk-factor change identified versus the 10-K. Impairment is non-cash and breaches no covenant identified (the revolver tests leverage on EBITDA), but it reduces book equity further and encodes management's own downward revision to forward cash flows.

**Refinancing.** $400M due August 2028, revolver maturity springing to May 2028, bonds at 84.5 cents implying ~12%, and H1 free cash flow negative to breakeven against $188.7M of cash. There is time, and there is a plausible path (asset sale, equity-linked issuance, secured capacity), but each path costs the equity something.

**Pro retention loop.** Angi's own language is that a shrinking Pro base produces "smaller and less diverse networks," reducing service requests. Fewer Pros degrade match quality, which degrades consumer experience, which reduces requests, which pushes more Pros out. The credit-loss allowance trend is consistent with Pro-side financial stress.

**Governance and structural constraints.** Five Item 5.02 filings in 2026, with the CFO succession effective March 27 (internal promotion of the Chief Accounting Officer) and the COO transition effective May 1/May 4, both within five weeks of the May 31 impairment test and of a platform migration whose risk factor specifically names "internal controls, accounting, and regulatory compliance." Two of the five filings (July 9 and August 4) remain unidentified; the August 4 filing accompanied the earnings release. The board is classified until 2032, directors are removable only for cause, blank-check preferred and authorized Class B/C shares may be issued to discourage a takeover, and the tax-sharing agreement restricts buybacks, mergers and large asset sales through approximately March 2027. The path for an outside party to force change is narrow.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by any insider from March through August 2026. Identified 2026 RSU grants total approximately 1,848,452 units, equal to 4.56% of shares outstanding, implying roughly 4.4% ownership dilution if fully vested and issued — an upper bound, since vesting schedules and performance conditions were not obtained and at least 0.3M units are excluded from diluted counts for unmet conditions. Grants dated August 3 (Kip 200,000, Levin 400,000, Boon 100,000, Orchard 100,000) preceded the August 4 disclosure of a $230.7M loss by one day. Grant calendars are typically pre-scheduled and retention equity at a depressed price is a defensible turnaround tool, so no motive is inferred. The economic effect is that leadership's equity was reset near a multi-year low while holders through the decline received no reset, and no insider bought stock at 0.29x book.

**Other.** Worker-classification exposure; EU Digital Services Act fines up to 6% of worldwide turnover; UK Online Safety Act; digital services taxes; $9.5M owed to IAC under the tax-sharing agreement with an IRS audit of IAC's FY2023 return covering Angi entities.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 113.7% on July 20 to 76.3% on August 8 while the stock fell 21.3%, the standard post-event vol crush: the August 4 print was the priced catalyst and it has resolved. At 76.3%, absolute implied volatility remains high for a $196M-cap equity, consistent with continued binary risk around the 2028 refinancing and further impairment rather than with complacency. The reported put/call open-interest ratio moving from 0.94 to 0.00 is almost certainly a data gap rather than a positioning signal and is not interpreted here. Short interest of 10.9% of float at 4.7 days to cover indicates meaningful but not extreme bearish positioning — enough to make squeeze risk relevant on any credible stabilization datapoint, not enough to suggest the bear case is fully crowded.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $9.50, +96%)

The 2026 revenue decline is transitional: the Advertising-to-Lead migration and Network runoff are one-time base effects that annualize away by Q1 2027, and the H1 marketing step-up is front-loaded brand investment with a payback longer than two quarters. International supplies the evidence that the leaner self-serve model works — flat revenue with Adjusted EBITDA +25% and a 20.8% margin — and the U.S. platform migration imports it. The cost base is already reset (350 heads, product development -54%), so stabilizing revenue at roughly $950M-$975M would produce $110-120M of Adjusted EBITDA. Refinancing the 2028 notes at 8% rather than 12% costs $32M rather than $48M. At 5.5x EV/EBITDA on $110M, enterprise value is $605M against roughly $210M of net debt, giving $395M of equity, or $9.75 per share. The sum-of-parts supports it independently: International alone at 8-10x covers net debt, leaving the U.S. business as free option value. Insiders now hold large equity stakes struck near $5, and Levin has committed not to dispose of 500,000 shares before March 31, 2031.

### Bear Case (target $2.25, -54%)

The 10% Proprietary Revenue decline is the true underlying rate and it is structural. Removing Network demand permanently raised customer acquisition cost, and the 10.4-point deterioration in marketing intensity does not reverse because paid channels are priced by auction against competitors including Google itself. Active Pros -17% with acquisition +13% means the retention loop is already running, and each turn degrades match quality. AI-mediated search and conversational interfaces absorb intent-matching, which is the entirety of Angi's function. 2027 revenue declines another 8-12%, Adjusted EBITDA falls toward $70-75M, and Adjusted EBITDA less capex approaches $20M. At that level a 4.00x covenant on a revolver that springs to May 2028 becomes live, refinancing $400M requires either dilutive equity-linked paper or an asset sale, and the tax-sharing agreement restricts the cleanest options until March 2027. Another goodwill charge of $35-55M arrives on the next test. At 3.5x on $72M of EBITDA, enterprise value is $252M against roughly $220M of net debt, leaving $32M of equity — a stub. The intermediate bear, in which the refinancing gets done expensively and EBITDA settles near $80M, prices the equity at $2.00-2.75.

### Balance of evidence

Weighting bear 40% at $2.25, base 35% at $5.00 and bull 25% at $9.50 gives an expected value near $5.00, or about +3% from $4.84. The distribution is wide, the skew is unfavorable, and the variance is not compensated. The base case is derived from 2027 Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $95M at 4.5x EV, less approximately $220M of net debt: $427M − $220M = $207M of equity, or $5.10 per share.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Base case $5.00 (+3%); bull case $9.50 (+96%); bear case $2.25 (-54%). Horizon: 12 months, with the Q3 2026 print (early November) the first checkpoint.**

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim into strength rather than sell into the post-print low; the name has twice rallied 20-30% off panic lows within six weeks (May 6 low $4.53 to $6.57 by June 1; July 23 low $5.30 to $6.44 by August 4), and 10.9% short interest at 4.7 days to cover supports similar bounces. Target reducing exposure on any move into the $6.00-6.75 range, which is where the prior two rallies stalled and where the stock trades at approximately 4.7x EV/annualized Adjusted EBITDA. Retain no more than a residual position pending evidence on the two open questions: revenue stabilization and the 2028 refinancing.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. Proprietary Revenue decline narrowing to better than -4% year over year in Q3 or Q4 2026, with U.S. leads decline narrowing to better than -6%.
2. Average monthly active U.S. Pros stabilizing sequentially (Q3 at or above Q2's 106,000), which would break the retention loop.
3. U.S. consumer marketing falling below 40% of U.S. revenue while revenue does not decline faster.
4. Any credible refinancing step for the 2028 notes — a new secured facility, a tender at par-adjacent levels, or bonds recovering above 92 cents — which would remove the term that most compresses equity value.
5. Clustered open-market insider purchases. None have occurred since at least March 2026.

**Triggers to move to AVOID.**
1. Q3 or Q4 2026 revenue decline worse than -12% year over year, or Adjusted EBITDA less capex turning negative on a trailing-twelve-month basis.
2. Adjusted EBITDA trending toward a run rate below $80M, which puts net leverage above 2.6x and starts to bring the 4.00x covenant into view before the springing revolver maturity.
3. A drawdown on the $175M revolver for operating purposes rather than a refinancing transaction.
4. Cash falling below $130M without a corresponding debt reduction.
5. A further goodwill or intangible impairment accompanied by a downward revision to the long-term growth assumption, which would confirm management's own model has moved lower again.

**Reassessment date.** Early November 2026 (Q3 2026 10-Q and earnings call). Q3 is the seasonal peak for home services, so it is the cleanest read available on whether the marketing spend of H1 produced volume. A secondary date is approximately March 31, 2027, when the post-spin tax-sharing restrictions on buybacks and large asset sales lapse and the strategic options widen.

## Conclusion

The scores across recent reviews (4.2 → 3.5 → 3.7 → 4.2, with recommendations AVOID, AVOID, AVOID, HOLD) reflected a genuine question: whether a company shedding low-quality revenue while doubling EBITDA had reached a defensible base. The prior review set $4.50-5.00 as an entry range contingent on the August 4 print. The stock is in that range, and the print resolved the question in the negative. Proprietary Revenue, the plank the HOLD rested on, fell 10% after growing 17% the prior year; the revenue decline accelerated from -3.2% to -11%; free cash flow turned negative-to-breakeven from +$29.2M; the goodwill cushion went from $109.7M to zero; and next-year consensus EPS fell 37% in a month. The 21% price decline since the last review is matched by fundamental deterioration that is specific, quantified and sourced to the filings, so the lower price does not by itself improve the risk/reward.

What keeps this at REDUCE rather than AVOID is the absence of near-term solvency risk and a real residual asset. Cash is $188.7M, nothing matures before August 2028, interest coverage is 6.6x, net leverage is roughly 2.06x against a 4.00x covenant, and the International segment is compounding Adjusted EBITDA at 25% with a standalone value plausibly covering the entire net debt balance. Enterprise value at 4.0x annualized Adjusted EBITDA is not a level at which to press a short, particularly with 10.9% of float already short at 4.7 days to cover and options positioning offering no confirmation.

What keeps it out of HOLD is the arithmetic. Expected value near $5.00 against a $4.84 price, with a -54% bear case that carries meaningful probability given accelerating volume erosion and a 12% market yield on the 2028 notes, is less than fair compensation for the dispersion. The equity is a levered residual on a cash stream that has declined 43% year over year on the Adjusted-EBITDA-less-capex measure, with negative tangible book value, buybacks and asset sales restricted until roughly March 2027, and a management team whose 2026 guidance missed by roughly 10 percentage points across three consecutive updates. Trim into strength; revisit in November on the Q3 volume and marketing-intensity data.
