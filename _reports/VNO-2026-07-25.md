**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Vornado is a New York City trophy-office and street-retail REIT — roughly 88-89% of NOI comes from the NY metro area and about 78% is office. Practically, it is a leveraged claim on Manhattan Class-A office and high-street retail (Fifth Avenue, Times Square, the PENN District). The investment case turns on a single tension: signed-lease economics are genuinely improving while the cash from those leases is deferred by free rent, and refinancing costs are rising now against a heavy maturity schedule.

The reported financials are misleading and require correction before anything else. FY2025 net income of $905M (EPS $4.20) is almost entirely a one-time, non-cash $803M gain on the 70-year NYU master lease at 770 Broadway. The recurring REIT metric, FFO, was roughly flat at $2.42/share (vs. $2.37 in 2024), and Q1 2026 FFO fell to $0.49 from $0.67 a year earlier. The trailing 10.5x P/E cited by data services is an artifact of the accounting gain and should be ignored. Occupancy and leasing are the real bright spots: NY office occupancy rose to 91.6% in Q1 2026 from 88.8% a year earlier, with new leases signed at $100+/sf and +9-11% mark-to-market spreads.

The balance sheet is where caution is warranted. VNO faces $925M of 2026 maturities and $2.4B in 2027, is refinancing at materially higher coupons (PENN 11 at 6.35%, new 2033 notes at 5.75% against maturing sub-4% debt), and carries several loans in maturity default, including the mortgage on its own headquarters at 888 Seventh Avenue. Against this, management is deploying capital offensively, buying Manhattan assets at claimed discounts and repurchasing stock below NAV, and faces a binary July 2026 decision on the 350 Park Avenue/Citadel development. The result is a stock with real two-sided optionality and no clear analytical edge. We rate it HOLD.

## Company Overview

Vornado operates through Vornado Realty L.P. (VNO owns ~90.9%) and holds a concentrated portfolio of Manhattan assets:

- **51 Manhattan properties**: ~19.2M sf office across 26 buildings, ~2.3M sf street retail across 45 buildings, and 1,331 residential units.
- **PENN District** flagship redevelopment, anchored by PENN 2 (~1.8M sf, ~$750M budget, ~$725M spent), a bet on the corridor around Penn Station and Moynihan Train Hall.
- **32.4% of Alexander's (ALX)**, which includes the Bloomberg headquarters at 731 Lexington.
- **THE MART** in Chicago (3.7M sf) and **70% of 555 California Street** in San Francisco (1.8M sf).
- The **Fifth Avenue and Times Square retail JV** (51.5%).

No single tenant accounts for more than 10% of revenue. Chairman and CEO Steven Roth controls the company through Interstate Properties (7.1% of VNO common), which also owns 26% of Alexander's. This creates a web of related-party arrangements: VNO manages Interstate's real estate for a 4% fee and provides management, development, and leasing services to Alexander's for fees.

## Financial Analysis

The single most important analytical step with VNO is to strip out the 2025 accounting distortion. The net income gain was driven by a non-cash sales-type lease — NYU's 70-year master lease of 770 Broadway, under which NYU prepaid $935M that VNO used to repay the $700M mortgage. This is not recurring, and it inflates every GAAP-based screen.

On the metric that matters:
- **FFO was essentially flat** at $2.42/diluted share.
- **Q1 2026 FFO fell to $0.49/share**; the prior-year quarter was itself inflated by a $76M gain on the 666 Fifth Ave/UNIQLO sale. Q1 2026 posted a GAAP net loss of $(0.12)/share.

Operating trends are mixed and the gap between GAAP and cash NOI is the central tell:
- Same-store NOI at share was positive (+5.4% FY25; +6.1% Q1'26), but **cash-basis same-store NOI declined (-5.5% FY25; -2.9% Q1'26)**. The gap reflects large free-rent periods on newly signed leases — GAAP straight-lines rent that isn't yet being collected. The signed-lease economics are real, but the cash arrives in 12-24 months.
- **555 California Street is a clear soft spot**: cash same-store NOI fell roughly 51% in Q1'26, reflecting San Francisco office weakness.

Revenue is flat (~$1.81B FY25), typical of a mature REIT recycling capital rather than growing.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Consolidated debt is ~$7.76B (Q1'26), plus ~$2.45B of pro-rata unconsolidated JV debt, at a weighted-average rate of ~4.8%. Liquidity is $2.6B (~$1.2B cash plus ~$1.4B undrawn revolver). Operating cash flow is modest (~$48M in Q1'26), reinforcing that much of the reported "earnings" is non-cash. Standard credit screens (Altman Z-Score of 15.6, ROA of 140%) are data artifacts for a REIT and should be disregarded.

**Dividend.** VNO pays a token $0.74/share once annually in Q4 — a ~1.9% yield, deeply cut from historical levels. Management is prioritizing balance-sheet flexibility, development funding, and buybacks over distributions. This is not an income vehicle.

The financial health score of 4.5 reflects the tension: genuine liquidity and trophy-asset cover against a refinancing wall at rising rates and specific asset-level distress.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story here in the traditional sense. Revenue is flat, and the recurring earnings metric (FFO) is flat-to-down. The path to higher FFO/share runs through three channels, each with meaningful offsets:

1. **Free-rent burn-off.** The negative cash same-store NOI reflects concessions on newly signed leases. As those free-rent periods expire over the next 1-2 years, cash NOI should catch up to the GAAP figure — a genuine tailwind, though gradual.
2. **Occupancy and mark-to-market.** Rising NY office occupancy and positive releasing spreads add incremental NOI. Trophy-asset demand is real in the current flight-to-quality environment.
3. **Development and capital recycling.** PENN 2 delivery, the potential 350 Park development, and recently acquired redevelopment sites (623 Fifth, 3 East 54th) offer future value creation, but require capital and time.

Working against these: rising interest expense on refinanced debt directly compresses FFO, and 555 California Street continues to drag. Consensus current-year EPS was revised down sharply (from -$0.02 to -$0.25 over 90 days), though EPS is not the right metric here and this rests on a single-analyst estimate and carries limited weight. There is no earnings-inflection signal of the kind that would warrant a cycle-adjusted valuation upgrade; this is a slow NAV-recovery grind, not a rapid re-rating setup. Growth prospects score 4.5.

## Valuation Assessment

VNO trades at $39.72, near the upper end of its 52-week range ($24.57-$43.37), having recovered roughly 61% off the March 2026 lows near $24.71. The correct valuation frame is FFO and NAV, not P/E.

On FFO, the stock trades at roughly 16.4x — a full multiple for a flat-FFO, high-leverage office REIT. Peer comparison is complicated by the sector's distorted GAAP metrics: among Office REITs, ARE trades at -8.4x, CUZ at -907x, SLG at -20.4x (all reflecting GAAP losses/near-zero earnings), while KRC (21.1x) and CDP (25.0x) carry positive multiples. The peer median P/E of 25.0x is not a reliable anchor given the negative and distorted readings across the group. On dividend yield, VNO's 1.9% sits well below the peer median of 4.8%, reflecting its deliberate distribution cut.

The genuine valuation debate is NAV. Management is voting with capital — buying back stock in Q1'26 at ~$29.08 (below current price and, per management, well below NAV) and acquiring Park Avenue Plaza at $950/sf, described as a "significant discount." A Ken Griffin affiliate independently valuing 350 Park high enough to exercise a development option corroborates that deep-pocketed buyers see value in premier Manhattan office at current pricing. That argues the stock could be trading below intrinsic real-estate value.

The offset is that this cheapness is exactly the kind that historically marks a value trap — a discounted office REIT with a refinancing wall, rising rates compressing FFO, and specific loan defaults. A compressed multiple in Real Estate is not a reliable buy signal, and "cheap" has sorted the wrong way. The NAV discount is real, but the catalysts to close it (free-rent burn-off, 350 Park resolution, rate relief) are gradual and partly out of management's control. Absent a specific near-term re-rating catalyst, the valuation is fairly balanced rather than asymmetric. Score: 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

The office sector is bifurcated. Trophy Class-A assets near transit are seeing genuine demand and rising rents, while commodity office is structurally impaired. VNO sits firmly on the favored side — LEED-certified trophy portfolio, the PENN District transit hub, Fifth Avenue retail. Rising occupancy and positive leasing spreads confirm the flight-to-quality benefit accruing to VNO's specific assets.

The competitive drawback is the cost of staying on the favored side: heavy tenant-improvement and free-rent concessions (visible in negative cash same-store NOI) and large landlord capex to keep buildings competitive against newer, more amenitized product. This is a structural cash-flow drag. San Francisco (555 California) is the weakest exposure and shows the downside of the same competitive dynamic in a softer market.

On trajectory, VNO's Manhattan position is stable-to-improving in the current cycle — occupancy is rising and mark-to-market is positive — which supports a competitive score modestly above midpoint at 5.5. The market leadership in NYC trophy is real, but the moat is capital-intensive to maintain.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing wall at higher rates.** VNO is refinancing successfully but at materially higher coupons, which steadily compresses FFO even as the assets perform. A higher-for-longer rate path is a direct, ongoing headwind to a financing-heavy REIT.

**Loans in maturity default.** The 888 Seventh Avenue mortgage matured in December 2025 unpaid and is under a forbearance to March 2027. 606 Broadway ($74.5M) is in default with +3% default interest. The 650 Madison JV (22.2%) received a default notice in October 2025 (since cured; VNO had already written this investment to zero). These are non-recourse, limiting direct balance-sheet contagion, but they signal genuine stress in specific assets.

**Concentration.** ~88% NYC, ~78% office, exposing VNO to WFH/hybrid trends, potential AI-driven space reduction, and NYC-specific regulatory risk (Local Law 97 carbon penalties, CLCPA).

**PENN 1 ground-rent litigation.** Annual rent could be reset to $15M or $20.22M (retroactive to 2023); the ground lessor won a vacatur of the favorable arbitration in October 2025, which VNO is appealing. An adverse outcome would raise a permanent cost at a flagship asset.

**350 Park/Citadel binary.** The July 2026 put-vs-invest decision has asymmetric outcomes: a clean $900M cash-in vs. tying up capital in a co-development.

**Key-man risk.** Heavy dependence on Steven Roth, with attendant related-party conflicts.

The 8-K record shows no recent executive departures and Q1 2026 disclosed no material changes to risk factors. Risk score of 4.5 reflects specific, identifiable downside catalysts (refinancing, defaults, litigation) partly offset by the fact that much of this is already priced (the stock traded to $24.71 in March) and by asymmetric recovery potential if rates ease or 350 Park resolves favorably.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is constructive-to-neutral, not bearish. Near-term ATM IV is 38.4% against 1-year historical vol of 33.3% — modestly rich but at only the 18th percentile within the current chain. The put/call skew is -6.2% (call-heavy) and the put/call volume ratio is a very low 0.06, indicating no meaningful put-hedging demand. The term structure is in contango (near-term 38.4% below 6-month 44.0%), a normal/complacent shape with the elevated 6-month vol likely pricing the July 350 Park decision and subsequent maturities. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float, 11,459 shares). None of this challenges a HOLD; it confirms the market is not positioned for imminent collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($45-48).** Manhattan trophy office has bottomed. Free-rent periods burn off over 2026-2027, closing the gap between GAAP and cash NOI and lifting FFO. NY office occupancy continues climbing past 92%, mark-to-market spreads stay positive, and 555 California stabilizes. The 350 Park/Citadel development proceeds and creates value, or the $900M put crystallizes cash. Rate relief lowers refinancing costs. Management's below-NAV buybacks and discounted acquisitions prove prescient, and the NAV discount closes. The stock re-rates toward the analyst high of $45 and beyond.

**Bear Case ($28-30).** The refinancing wall bites: the 2027 maturities get termed out at 6%+, compressing FFO. The 888 Seventh Avenue forbearance and 606 Broadway default foreshadow further asset-level distress if office fundamentals soften. The PENN 1 ground-rent reset goes against VNO. Rates stay higher for longer, and the office recovery stalls amid AI-driven space reduction. The 350 Park co-investment ties up capital in a multi-year development at the wrong point in the cycle. The stock retraces toward the analyst low of $28 (-30% from current).

The two cases are genuinely balanced. The leasing data supports the bull; the debt schedule and rate environment support the bear. Neither dominates on current evidence.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The current data neither confirms a clear deterioration nor establishes a compelling entry. This is an honest uncertain hold, not a conviction call.

**Reassessment timeline.** The July 2026 350 Park/Citadel decision is the immediate catalyst. Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which should reveal both the 350 Park outcome and updated leasing/refinancing progress.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The 350 Park put is exercised for $900M cash, or the co-investment is structured on clearly value-accretive terms, materially de-risking the capital-allocation picture.
- Cash same-store NOI turns positive (currently -2.9% Q1'26), confirming the free-rent burn-off thesis with 555 California stabilizing.
- The 2027 maturity wall is substantially termed out at coupons below 5.5%, or the long end eases materially, removing the FFO-compression overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Additional loans enter maturity default beyond 888 Seventh and 606 Broadway, signaling broadening asset-level stress.
- An adverse PENN 1 ground-rent ruling imposing the $20.22M annual reset retroactively.
- NY office occupancy reverses below 90%, or FFO/share guidance is cut, indicating the leasing recovery has stalled.

## Conclusion

Vornado is a well-located, high-quality Manhattan office portfolio wrapped in a leveraged balance sheet at an inconvenient point in the rate cycle. The leasing data is genuinely encouraging — rising occupancy, strong trophy-asset demand, positive mark-to-market — but it is offset by flat FFO, the 2027 refinancing wall at higher coupons, scattered loan defaults including on the company's own headquarters, and a token dividend that removes the income cushion. The 2025 net-income headline is an accounting distortion that should be disregarded.

Management is buying assets at claimed discounts and stock below NAV, which is the right posture if Manhattan office has bottomed, and a costly one if it hasn't. The July 2026 350 Park decision and the pace of free-rent burn-off will determine which. Until that resolves, the risk/reward is symmetric: real NAV support against real refinancing and rate risk, with the "cheap" valuation carrying the value-trap caveat that has repeatedly caught discounted office REITs. We rate VNO a HOLD and would look to the Q2 2026 print and the 350 Park outcome for a more decisive call.
