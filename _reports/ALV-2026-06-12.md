**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Autoliv is the world's largest supplier of automotive passive safety systems — airbags, seatbelts, steering wheels, and pedestrian-protection systems — operating in a three-firm global oligopoly alongside ZF/TRW and Joyson Safety Systems. The investment case rests on two durable pillars: a content-per-vehicle tailwind that lets the company grow organically even when global light-vehicle production (LVP) is flat or falling, and a disciplined capital-return program (a $2.5B buyback authorization through 2029 plus a dividend raised to $0.87/quarter). In Q1 2026 organic sales grew 0.8% against global LVP of -3.4%, a 4.2-point outperformance, and adjusted EPS of $2.05 beat consensus by roughly 7%, driving an 11% one-day stock move.

The stock trades at approximately 13.9x trailing and ~12.3x forward earnings, a clear discount to the automotive-parts peer median of ~20.7x. This presents as a quality compounder available at a reasonable price. The complicating factors are three: consensus EPS estimates are falling (current-quarter down 6.4% over 90 days, current-year down 1.5%), reported revenue growth is almost entirely FX-driven (organic was only 0.8% in Q1), and two material, unaccrued liabilities sit on the balance sheet — an escalating Stellantis side-curtain airbag recall now in active litigation ($0-$203M potential loss, much but not all expected to be insured, with another OEM reportedly investigating similar nonconformance) and an uninsured BMW antitrust claim of roughly €95M.

We rate ALV a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The business is healthy, management is well-aligned, and the valuation is undemanding. The deteriorating revision trend, near-zero organic growth, and the binary legal overhangs, which carry no accrual and would hit earnings directly if they go against the company, prevent us from underwriting the asymmetric upside that a BUY requires. The score sits at 6.25; the specific obstacles to an upgrade are concrete and falsifiable, and we lay them out below.

## Company Overview

Autoliv is a Delaware-incorporated holding company headquartered in Stockholm, operating principally through Autoliv AB and Autoliv ASP, Inc. It trades as ALV on the NYSE and as SDRs (ALIV SDB) on Nasdaq Stockholm. The company reports as a single segment but discloses a product split: in Q1 2026, Airbags, Steering Wheels & Other was roughly $1,863M (~68% of sales) and Seatbelts & Other roughly $890M (~32%).

Geographic exposure is unusually balanced for an auto supplier. Q1 2026 sales were Americas $863M, EMEA $835M, Asia-ex-China $563M, and China $492M. This diversification dampens the impact of any single regional production cycle and gives the company exposure to the fastest-growing safety-content markets (India, China) alongside mature ones.

The competitive moat derives from the safety-critical nature of the products. Airbag and seatbelt systems require multi-year OEM co-engineering, crash-test validation, and regulatory credibility that take years to build and cannot be substituted cheaply. The structural driver is rising mandated safety content per vehicle — more airbags, center airbags, pedestrian protection — which lets Autoliv grow content even where unit volumes stagnate. India organic sales rose 38% in Q1 2026 on exactly this dynamic.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability and revenue.** FY2025 net sales were approximately $10.8B with operating income of ~$1.09B (up from $659M in 2022, reflecting a multi-year margin recovery) and net income of $736M. Q1 2026 net sales were $2,753M, up 6.8% reported but only 0.8% organic, with the bulk of the headline growth from FX (+6.0pp). Gross margin expanded to 19.1% from 18.6%. GAAP operating margin fell to 8.6% from 9.9%, driven by lower R,D&E reimbursement timing, FX translation, and the absence of one-time gains booked in Q1 2025 (Russia exit, China property sale).

**Earnings quality and beat track record.** The company has beaten consensus in four consecutive quarters: +6.6% (Q1 2026), +8.7% (Q4 2025), +11.4% (Q3 2025), +1.6% (Q2 2025). This is a credible delivery record that supports confidence in the ~10.5-11% adjusted operating margin guidance for FY2026. The reported top line flatters the picture; investors should anchor on organic growth and adjusted margin rather than the headline.

**Cash flow.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative $76M and free operating cash flow negative $159M. This is a seasonal working-capital swing (strong March sales plus high December payables) and management guides to ~$1.2B operating cash flow for the full year, implying a sharp back-half reversal. We treat this as optical rather than structural, but it warrants verification in the Q2 and Q3 prints. Capex discipline is intact: capex/sales fell to 3.0% from 3.6%, with FY guidance below 5%.

**Capital allocation.** Share count has fallen from ~87M (2022) to ~75M (Q1 2026). The $2.5B repurchase authorization runs to December 2029, with $300-500M planned for 2026, though no shares were repurchased in Q1 (timing), making buyback activity back-half weighted.

**Balance sheet.** A screening Altman Z-Score of ~1.87 flags "leverage," but this is misleading. Management's own leverage metric is 1.3x net-debt-plus-pension/EBITDA, comfortably below its 1.5x ceiling. Net debt is $1,773M against total equity of $2,644M; the elevated book D/E reflects years of buybacks shrinking the equity base, not distress. ROCE and ROE both run ~22%, and TTM adjusted EBITDA is ~$1.52B. The headline leverage screens overstate risk. We score Financial Health 7.0.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is content-per-vehicle, not unit volume. Outperformance was strongest in China (+15pp vs. LVP, as the company grew ~30% with domestic Chinese OEMs while global-OEM volume there fell ~10%) and Asia-ex-China (+6.8pp), in line in EMEA, and negative in the Americas (-4.5pp) on adverse mix (high volume in low-content South America, lower content on some replacement models).

The secular driver of tightening safety regulation in emerging markets partially decouples the company from auto-production cyclicality. India at +38% organic is the clearest expression. The race to win Chinese domestic EV/OEM platforms is the decisive competitive battleground, and Autoliv is currently gaining share there as legacy global-OEM volume declines.

Strategic optionality is emerging in two-wheeler safety: the first motorcycle airbag (co-developed with Yamaha for the Tricity scooter) and a wearable airbag vest (with RS Taichi). These are early-stage but address a large, under-penetrated market that is especially relevant in India and Southeast Asia where Autoliv is already winning.

The constraint on the growth score is the estimate-revision trend, which is unambiguously negative. Current-quarter consensus EPS has fallen 6.4% over 90 days and 2.0% over 30 days; current-year is down 1.5% over 90 days. FY2026 guidance itself assumes roughly 0% organic growth on LVP of -1% with ~3% positive FX. This is a company defending margins and compounding through buybacks in a flat-to-declining volume environment, not one accelerating. That profile supports a HOLD, not the positive revision momentum a BUY requires. We score Growth Prospects 5.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At $128.22, ALV trades at ~13.9x trailing EPS of $9.55 and ~12.3x the FY2026 consensus of ~$10.44, with a ~2.8% dividend yield. Against the Automotive Parts & Equipment peer median P/E of 20.7x, this is a meaningful discount. The closer comparables tell a more nuanced story: Lear at 14.5x and Gentex at 14.4x trade near ALV's multiple, while BorgWarner (44.3x) and Dana (negative earnings) skew the median. On the most relevant comps, ALV is roughly in line to slightly cheap.

The valuation case for a higher score would require demonstrating a specific mispricing. Three candidates exist. First, the consistent earnings beats (averaging ~7%) suggest consensus may be modestly conservative, which would compress the forward multiple further on actuals. Second, the buyback (~3-5% of shares against a ~$9.6B cap) provides a mechanical EPS tailwind that consensus may under-model. Third, the ~22% ROCE/ROE quality is not reflected in a low-teens multiple.

Against these, the offsetting factors are concrete: estimates are falling, not rising, so the "consensus is too low" argument cuts against the revision data; the buyback is back-half weighted and discretionary; and two unaccrued liabilities (Stellantis $0-$203M, BMW ~€95M uninsured) could produce lumpy charges that the multiple does not price. Analyst consensus targets sit at a mean of $132.18 and median of $134.00, with a low of $110 and high of $150 — the current price sits below the mean, implying modest upside to consensus but not the asymmetric setup that would justify treating targets as stale floors. Because estimates are declining, we do not treat the consensus target as a lagging indicator here.

The net read is a fair-to-modestly-cheap multiple on a quality business with flat near-term growth and real binary tail risk. That supports a Valuation score of 6.2, better than fair value but not high enough to override the growth and risk concerns.

## Competitive Landscape

Autoliv operates in a high-barrier oligopoly. The combination of scale, OEM co-engineering relationships, crash-test validation history, and regulatory credibility makes the competitive structure stable, with the three incumbents (Autoliv, ZF/TRW, Joyson) sharing the global occupant-safety market. The trajectory is favorable on two fronts: the company is outgrowing LVP via content, and it is winning share with Chinese domestic OEMs as those platforms displace global-OEM volume in China.

The watch item is the Americas, where Autoliv underperformed LVP by 4.5pp in Q1 on mix. This is a near-term mix issue rather than evidence of share loss, but it bears monitoring across the next two quarters. Gross margin expansion is consistent with pricing power and operating leverage rather than competitive compression. We score Competitive Position 6.8, reflecting an improving trajectory in the structurally important regions with a single regional soft spot.

## Risk Assessment

The defining risk for a safety supplier is product liability, recall, and warranty exposure. Three active matters drive the idiosyncratic risk score:

1. **Stellantis side-curtain airbag recall (most acute).** Began Q2 2025 (~250k vehicles), expanded by 178k in March 2026, and Stellantis filed suit against an Autoliv subsidiary on April 16, 2026. No accrual has been taken. Management has flagged that another customer is investigating similar nonconformance, raising the possibility of additional recalls.

2. **BMW antitrust claim.** Stemming from the resolved 2019 EC investigation, BMW filed a German complaint in October 2024, amended in March 2026 to ~€58M plus interest (~€95M total). Loss is "reasonably possible," no accrual is taken, and insurance would likely not mitigate this, so an adverse ruling hits earnings directly.

3. **ARC inflator matters.** The defective inflators were manufactured by ARC Automotive/Delphi, not Autoliv. Autoliv's exposure is limited to ARC inflators it incorporated into modules after its 2009 Delphi acquisition. It is a co-defendant in the consolidated ARC Inflator MDL; loss is "reasonably possible," with no accrual and no estimable range. This is materially less severe than press coverage of the ARC scandal might suggest, but it is an open contingency.

Additional risks: a Honda seatbelt-buckle recall (~449k vehicles, accrued, net exposure ~$12M beyond insurance); 2025 tariffs costing ~40bps of operating margin in Q1 (FY2026 dilution ~20bps after >70% customer recovery); raw-material inflation of ~$90M gross expected in FY2026 (mostly mitigable); and China LVP cyclicality (down ~10% YoY in Q1, offset by the domestic-OEM pivot).

The Risk score of 5.0 reflects that two of these liabilities are unaccrued and binary, with the Stellantis matter actively escalating into litigation and potentially spreading. These are specific, dated, falsifiable exposures the market is not fully pricing given the absence of accruals.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sending a benign signal. Near-term ATM IV is 28.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 26.7% — fairly priced, with IV at only the 2nd percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.6%, and the term structure is in contango (28.3% near vs. 35.7% long-dated), a normal configuration. The implied nearest-expiry move is ±3.0%. The collapse in the options-delta snapshot (ATM IV 558.2% → 28.3%) reflects the resolution of the April earnings event rather than a fundamental shift; the post-earnings P/C OI ratio fell to 0.65, consistent with reduced hedging demand. The elevated 3.00 put/call volume ratio is a single-session reading and is not corroborated by the 0.65 OI ratio, so we read it as noise rather than informed bearish positioning. Options are not flagging the legal overhangs as imminent catalysts, consistent with our view that these are tail risks on an uncertain timeline, not dated events.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Autoliv compounds through the content-per-vehicle tailwind (organic outgrowth of LVP by 4+ points), defends ~10.5-11% adjusted margins, and executes the $300-500M 2026 buyback to drive mid-single-digit EPS growth. The Stellantis and BMW matters resolve favorably or within insured limits. Consensus, which has been too low four quarters running, re-rates the stock toward the peer median as the China domestic-OEM share gains and emerging-market content story become more visible.
