## Executive Summary

Trustmark Corporation has completed a multi-year earnings transformation, growing adjusted EPS from $2.60 in 2023 to $3.70 in 2025 — a 42% cumulative improvement (approximately 19% CAGR) driven by a 2024 securities portfolio restructuring ($182.8M loss taken to reinvest at 350bps higher yields) and disciplined NIM expansion from 3.32% to 3.80%. The company delivered record net income of $224M in FY2025 and reported Q1 2026 EPS of $0.95 (above consensus estimates), with management guiding NIM of 3.80-3.85% and mid-single digit loan growth for 2026. Capital returns are accelerating from near-zero in 2023 to $100M authorized buybacks in 2026, creating a structural 2%+ annual EPS tailwind from share count reduction alone.

At $44.92 per share, the stock trades at approximately 11.5x forward P/E on estimated FY2026 EPS of $3.90, a modest discount to estimated peer medians — justified by Trustmark's superior NIM, proven earnings trajectory, and active capital return posture. The 2.2% dividend yield provides a base income return. The primary risks are CRE concentration (particularly nursing homes/senior living at $716M), geographic hurricane exposure, and the possibility that C&I charge-off normalization signals early cycle stress. The stock has appreciated 30% over the past year as the market has recognized the transformation, but forward estimates continue rising (+2.8% over 90 days for FY2026), indicating the fundamental improvement is not fully priced.

## Company Overview

Trustmark Corporation is a $19B-asset bank holding company headquartered in Jackson, Mississippi, operating through its principal subsidiary Trustmark Bank (formerly Trustmark National Bank) across six states: Mississippi (dominant home market), Alabama, Florida Panhandle, Georgia (Atlanta), Tennessee (Memphis), and Texas (Houston). The company employs approximately 2,543 full-time equivalent associates.

The business operates in two reportable segments: General Banking (95% of income) encompassing commercial and consumer banking plus mortgage operations, and Wealth Management (5%) managing $10.9B in AUM/AUA. An Equipment Finance division operates nationally from Atlanta. Trustmark converted from a national bank charter (OCC) to a Mississippi state charter in August 2025; potential regulatory flexibility and cost benefits from this conversion have not yet been quantified in filings.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability & Returns:**
- FY2025 net income: $224.1M ($3.70 diluted EPS), record results
- ROA: 1.21% | ROATE: 12.97% | ROE: 10.86%
- Efficiency ratio improving with operating leverage from securities restructuring
- Q1 2026 EPS: $0.95, above consensus estimates

**Net Interest Margin:**
NIM expanded from 3.32% (2023) → 3.51% (2024) → 3.80% (2025), with Q4 2025 at 3.83%. The improvement is driven by two factors: (1) the securities portfolio restructuring significantly boosted securities income — taxable AFS securities interest increased $22.1M (39.5%) in 2025, with the yield on taxable AFS securities rising to 4.44% from 3.13%, principally due to higher-yielding securities purchased in 2025 and the 2024 restructuring; and (2) deposit costs declining from 2.70% to 2.26% as rate cuts take hold. Management guides 3.80-3.85% for full year 2026, which appears achievable given their deposit franchise strength.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total loans (LHFI): $13.7B, +4.5% in 2025
- Total deposits: $15.5B, +2.6% in 2025
- Loan-to-deposit ratio: 88% (manageable)
- Uninsured deposits: 35.3% of total (moderate)
- CET1 ratio: 11.72% | Total capital: 14.41% — well above minimums
- Tangible book value: $30.28/share (+13.5% YoY)

**Credit Quality:**
- Net charge-offs: 13bps of average loans (2025), improved from 19bps (2024)
- Provision for credit losses: $14.3M (2025) vs. $37.3M adj. (2024) — 62% decline
- ACL/LHFI: 1.15% (conservative)
- Criticized loans declined $123M through first three quarters of 2025
- Watch item: C&I NCOs elevated at 0.61% in 2025 vs. 0.44% in 2024, though total portfolio NCOs remained low and improved to 0.13% of average LHFI from 0.19% in 2024

**Cash Flow & Capital Return:**
- Operating cash flow: $244M; FCF: $232M (capex only $12M)
- 2025 buybacks: $80M (2.2M shares retired)
- 2026 authorization: $100M; targeting $70M-$80M in repurchases for the year; repurchased 477,000 shares valued at $19.8M through Q1 2026
- Dividend: $1.00/share annual ($0.25/quarter), 24.7% payout ratio
- Combined return (buyback + dividend) represents ~5% of market cap annually

## Growth Analysis

Trustmark's growth profile is moderate but predictable, consistent with a well-run community bank in the Sun Belt:

**Loan growth:** Mid-single digit guided, driven by Houston, Atlanta, Birmingham, and Huntsville expansion. Equipment Finance provides incremental diversification. 29 hires in Q3 2025 alone (21 production-related) signal ongoing investment in production capacity.

**Earnings growth:** Consensus expects approximately $3.90 EPS for FY2026 (+5.4%) and $4.10 for FY2027 (+5.1%). The combination of NIM stability, loan growth, expense discipline, and ~2% annual share count reduction from buybacks supports mid-to-high single digit EPS growth without heroic assumptions.

**Estimate revisions:** FY2026 estimates have risen 2.8% over 90 days and 2.6% over 30 days. FY2027 estimates up 1.1% over 90 days. The trend is positive though not at the magnitude (30%+) that would indicate a dramatic re-pricing. This is a steady-state compounder at this point in the cycle.

**M&A optionality:** CEO Dewey has repeatedly referenced acquisition interest in "high-growth regional markets and sizable targets." With CET1 at 11.72% and excess capital generation, Trustmark has capacity for a transformative deal. This is not priced into estimates but represents upside optionality — with accompanying execution risk.

## Valuation Assessment

At $44.92, Trustmark trades at:
- Approximately 11.5x FY2026E EPS of ~$3.90
- Approximately 11.0x FY2027E EPS of ~$4.10
- 1.48x tangible book value ($30.28)
- 2.2% dividend yield

**Peer comparison (as of report date, sourced from market data providers):** The regional bank peer median forward P/E is estimated at approximately 11.8x. Trustmark's forward multiple is slightly below peers, despite delivering superior NIM (3.80% vs. peers typically in the 3.20-3.50% range), stronger earnings momentum (EPS up 42% cumulatively over two years vs. flatter trajectories at most peers), and more aggressive capital returns.

**Price target context:** Analyst consensus mean target is estimated at approximately $47.45 (based on available market data as of report date). With estimates continuing to rise, consensus targets may lag improving fundamentals.

**Fair value assessment:** A 12.5x multiple on FY2026E of $3.90 yields $48.75. A 12.5x multiple on FY2027E of $4.10 yields $51.25. Given the earnings trajectory and buyback support, 12-13x forward is reasonable for a bank generating 13% ROATE with expanding margins. The current ~11.5x prices in minimal credit for continued execution.

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:** Strong Mississippi deposit franchise, proven ability to attract talent from larger institutions in growth markets, strong mortgage servicing operation ($9B serviced), and wealth management providing fee diversification.

**Challenges:** Small market shares in growth states limit near-term contribution from expansion markets. Competition for deposits remains intense — management noted "surprisingly competitive" conditions. The bank lacks the technology investment scale of larger peers and faces fintech disruption in consumer banking.

**Strategic trajectory:** The combination of organic hiring, equipment finance growth, and stated M&A interest positions Trustmark to expand beyond its Mississippi base, but this remains in early stages. The charter conversion to a state bank may provide modest regulatory savings and flexibility, though these benefits have not yet been quantified.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration:** $3.3B in non-farm, non-residential properties (24.2% of loans) plus $2.1B other real estate secured. Nursing homes/senior living at $716M (5.2% of loans) represent a sector with structural challenges. Multifamily exposure grew 30% in 2025 as construction loans converted to permanent financing — monitoring needed given supply concerns in Alabama, Texas, and Georgia.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** Asset-sensitive positioning means a 200bp rate decline would compress net interest income by approximately 4.8% based on management's modeled sensitivity analysis. In the current environment where rates are held steady (or potentially cut later in 2026-2027), Trustmark faces gradual loan yield compression offset by deposit cost management. The 3.80-3.85% NIM guidance assumes a stable rate environment.

**Geographic/Hurricane Risk:** Substantial Gulf Coast exposure across Mississippi, Alabama, Florida Panhandle, and Houston. A major hurricane season could produce significant loan losses and operational disruption.

**C&I Credit Deterioration:** C&I NCOs rose to 0.61% in 2025 from 0.44% in 2024 and 0.06% in 2023. While overall portfolio credit metrics improved (total NCOs declined to 0.13% from 0.19%), this category bears watching particularly given tariff uncertainty affecting commercial borrowers.

**Macro Sensitivity:** Regional banks face outsized risk in a recession. Trustmark's loan book would come under stress, particularly in CRE and C&I. The 1.15% ACL provides cushion but may prove insufficient in a severe downturn.

**Short Interest:** At 6.4% of float with 6.2 days to cover, short interest is modestly elevated for a community bank. This does not represent extreme bearish positioning but suggests some institutional skepticism, likely related to CRE concentration or rate sensitivity.

- Loan growth exceeds 5% as expansion markets accelerate

## Conclusion

Trustmark Corporation has executed a legitimate earnings transformation over 2023-2025, driven by decisive management action (securities restructuring, expense discipline, talent acquisition). The stock trades at approximately 11.5x forward earnings, slightly below estimated peer medians despite demonstrably superior NIM, earnings momentum, and capital return trajectory. With estimates rising, buybacks reducing share count, and M&A optionality providing upside, the risk/reward skews positive at current levels.

The primary concern preventing higher conviction is the macro environment: CRE concentration (particularly nursing homes) and geographic hurricane exposure create tail risks that are difficult to hedge at the individual security level. The C&I charge-off trend also warrants monitoring. At ~11.5x forward with a 2.2% yield and 13% ROATE, the market is not paying for the quality improvement already delivered, and modest estimate revisions suggest further upside as analysts catch up to the trajectory.

The specific reason this warrants a BUY rather than HOLD: the market prices Trustmark as a slightly-below-average regional bank despite measurably above-average execution — 42% cumulative adjusted EPS growth over two years (approximately 19% annualized), NIM 30-50bps above most peers, and capital returns accelerating from zero to ~5% of market cap annually. When the quality premium is zero despite demonstrated superior results, the valuation is mispriced. The catalyst for re-rating is continued quarterly execution (next earnings ~July 2026), potential M&A announcement, and further buyback-driven EPS accretion.
