## Executive Summary

First Bancorp is a $12.7 billion asset community bank holding company headquartered in North Carolina, operating 113 branches across the Carolinas. The company has completed a securities portfolio restructuring in 2024-2025 that temporarily depressed earnings but positioned net interest margin for rapid expansion, from 2.89% in 2024 to 3.40% in 2025 and 3.67% in Q1 2026. This drives a clean earnings trajectory of approximately $4.69 in FY2026 and $5.08 in FY2027, representing 75% and 8% growth respectively off the depressed 2025 base.

The stock has appreciated 37% over the past 12 months as the market recognized this earnings recovery. At $59.09, FBNC trades at approximately 12.6x forward FY2026 EPS, a 7.7% premium to the regional bank peer median of 11.7x. While the fundamental recovery justifies some premium (above-peer NIM, excellent efficiency ratio, strong capital position), the premium creates limited margin of safety given elevated CRE exposure, macro uncertainty in the rate environment, and options markets pricing near-term stress. The stock trades 6% below its 52-week high of $62.64 and well within analyst target range of $64-68.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation is the valuation premium versus peers. FBNC's ROE of 7.2% trails what would typically justify a 12.6x forward P/E. The NIM expansion story is now consensus (3 analysts rate Buy/Strong Buy, 1 rates Hold), meaning the securities restructuring benefit is priced in. Upside from here requires either further NIM expansion beyond 3.67% (limited given rate cut expectations), acceleration in loan growth, or successful M&A — none of which are high-probability near-term catalysts.

## Company Overview

First Bancorp operates as the fourth-largest commercial bank holding company headquartered in North Carolina through its wholly-owned subsidiary, First Bank. The franchise spans 100 branches in North Carolina and 13 in South Carolina, serving metropolitan markets including Charlotte, Raleigh/Triangle, Greensboro/Winston-Salem, Asheville, Wilmington, and Greenville/Columbia/Charleston.

The loan portfolio is heavily weighted toward commercial real estate (54% total CRE including owner-occupied, non-owner-occupied, and multi-family), with residential 1-4 family at 20% and C&I at 12%. Approximately 87% of loans are secured by real estate and 71% are fixed-rate. Total deposits are $10.7 billion with a strong 32% noninterest-bearing composition and negligible brokered deposits ($4.9M).

The company grew through acquisitions historically (GrandSouth Bancorp in 2023, Select Bancorp in 2021) but has been in organic-only mode since 2023, demonstrating franchise value with 7.8% loan growth and 2.1% deposit growth in 2025 without M&A.

Q1 2026 delivered $1.13 EPS (beating consensus $1.11 by 2.1%), with NIM of 3.67% and efficiency ratio of 49.05%. Net charge-offs were minimal at $1.4M (0.06% annualized). The Q1 run-rate annualizes to $4.52, supporting the $4.69 full-year estimate given seasonal improvement.

**Capital Position:** CET1 at 14.10% and total risk-based capital at 16.12% are well above regulatory minimums. Tangible common equity per share has improved materially as AOCI losses from the securities portfolio shrink (unrealized losses from $368M to $194M during 2025). The new $40M buyback program provides capital return flexibility.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $203M against minimal capex ($4.2M) produces $199M in free cash flow, enabling the dividend ($0.99/share annual, ~1.7% yield) plus buybacks plus retained growth capital.

**Asset Quality:** NPAs at 0.30% of assets, net charge-offs at 0.10% of average loans, and allowance coverage of 340% of nonaccrual loans indicate credit quality remains sound. The Hurricane Helene reserve has been substantially released ($13M taken in 2024, only $1.9M remaining).

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects for FBNC center on three vectors:

1. **NIM expansion nearing peak:** The securities restructuring benefit is now largely captured. NIM went from 2.89% (2024) to 3.67% (Q1 2026), a 78bp expansion. Further expansion depends on continued deposit repricing downward, but with Fed rates potentially holding higher for longer, the pace of improvement should moderate.

2. **Organic loan growth:** 7.8% growth in 2025 was strong for a community bank. Management has introduced a loan participation initiative as an additional growth vector. The Carolinas remain favorable demographics markets with net population inflows.

3. **M&A optionality:** With a 14.1% CET1 and no acquisitions since 2023, FBNC has significant capacity for deals. However, this is option value, not a timed catalyst.

Analyst consensus shows FY2027E EPS of $5.08, representing 8.3% growth over FY2026E, a deceleration from the FY2025-to-FY2026 recovery year. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (+2.5% over 90 days for FY2026, +3.7% for FY2027) but not at levels that signal a major fundamental reassessment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $59.09, FBNC trades at:
- 12.6x FY2026E EPS ($4.69)
- 11.6x FY2027E EPS ($5.08)
- ~2.1x tangible book value per share (~$28.22)
- ~22x trailing EPS (distorted by 2024-2025 securities losses)

**Peer comparison is the critical context.** Regional bank peers trade at a median P/E of 11.7x with a median yield of 2.2%. FBNC at 12.6x forward and 1.7% yield is at a 7.7% P/E premium with a lower yield. The premium can be partially justified by:
- Superior NIM (3.67% vs. likely 3.0-3.3% for peers)
- Better efficiency ratio (49% vs. typical 55-60%)
- Stronger organic loan growth trajectory

However, FBNC's ROE of 7.2% does not justify the premium. The ROE should improve toward 10-12% as the full-year 2026 earnings normalize, but until that's demonstrated over multiple quarters, the market is partially pricing in expectations rather than proven delivery.

The analyst consensus target of $66.50 (mean) implies 12.5% upside. This is reasonable but not exceptional for a stock trading at a peer premium, and the tight target range ($64-$68) suggests limited disagreement about fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

FBNC's competitive position in the Carolinas is solid but not exceptional. The bank benefits from:
- Strong noninterest-bearing deposit base (32%) providing low-cost funding
- Geographic diversification across multiple NC/SC metros
- Local decision-making advantage over national bank competitors
- Minimal brokered deposit reliance

Challenges include:
- Crossing the $10B threshold subjects the bank to Durbin Amendment interchange limits and CFPB oversight
- Competition from both larger super-regionals (Truist, Wells Fargo) and smaller community banks in the same markets
- Fintech/stablecoin disintermediation risk (explicitly acknowledged in risk factors)
- 5-year TSR trailing peer group by 44 percentage points (168% vs. 211%), suggesting the market has historically assigned a discount

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (Primary):** With 54% of loans in various CRE categories, FBNC is heavily exposed to commercial real estate valuations. In a stagflationary environment with office utilization still below pre-COVID levels and elevated interest rates increasing cap rates, CRE exposure represents the most significant downside risk. Office exposure is approximately 9% of total loans.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** While NIM has expanded during the current cycle, the 71% fixed-rate loan book means further rate increases provide limited benefit while rate cuts would gradually compress margins as the higher-yielding securities and loans mature. If the Fed remains on hold or hikes, the near-term trajectory holds, but longer-duration assets begin to reprice.

**Goodwill Risk:** $478.8M in goodwill represents 3.8% of assets. A sustained stock price decline or economic deterioration could trigger impairment testing challenges.

**Regional Economic Exposure:** Carolinas-concentrated; any localized economic weakness (manufacturing, real estate) affects the entire franchise simultaneously.

**Auditor Change:** The March 2025 auditor change (reported in 8-K) warrants monitoring, though no restatements or disagreements were disclosed.

**Leadership Transition:** The appointment of G. Adam Currie as First Bank CEO in October 2025, along with multiple 8-K filings related to officer changes and employment agreements in early 2026, suggests an active succession planning process. Richard Moore remains holding company CEO, but the groundwork for eventual transition appears underway.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 42.8% significantly exceeds 1-year historical volatility of 27.7%, a 15.1pp premium. The term structure is in **backwardation** (42.8% near-term vs. 32.8% at 6-month), indicating the market is pricing a catalyst or stress event within the next 1-2 weeks. Put/call skew at the 3-month tenor is 22.2%, suggesting institutional protection buying extends through the summer.

This diverges from the consensus analyst bullishness (1 Strong Buy, 2 Buy, 1 Hold). When options markets and fundamental analysts disagree, the options signal warrants caution — it likely reflects positioning around macro risks (credit cycle concerns, CRE repricing fears) that equity analysts aren't incorporating into targets.

Short interest at 5.4% of float with 8.2 days to cover is modestly elevated for a community bank, corroborating that some sophisticated investors hold a bearish view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($66-68, 12-15% upside):**
- NIM sustains above 3.60% through 2026, driving earnings above $4.69 consensus
- Buyback program executed aggressively at discount to peer valuations
- Loan growth accelerates to 8-10% on strong Carolina demographics
- M&A deal announced at accretive terms, expanding franchise
- ROE normalizes to 10%+ as earnings fully recover, justifying premium valuation

**Bear Case ($45-48, 20-24% downside):**
- CRE values decline on higher cap rates, credit losses accelerate
- NIM peaks in Q1 2026 and begins compressing as higher-rate deposits reprice
- Consumer weakness in macro environment impacts loan demand and credit quality
- Regional bank sector derates broadly on credit cycle fears
- Goodwill impairment charge if stock declines materially

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $52-54 range (11.0-11.5x FY2026E), compressing the peer premium to minimal levels
2. Q2 2026 earnings demonstrate NIM sustainability above 3.65% with continued strong credit quality AND management signals a specific M&A target or accelerated buyback
3. Estimate revisions accelerate beyond +5% over 30 days, indicating consensus is significantly behind actual performance trajectory

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. NIM declines below 3.40% in any reported quarter, indicating the expansion cycle has reversed
2. Net charge-offs exceed 0.30% of average loans for any quarter (3x current levels)
3. Nonperforming assets exceed 0.75% of total assets
4. Management announces a dilutive acquisition (>5% TBV dilution without clear earnings accretion timeline)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (estimated July 2026). Key monitoring points: Q2 NIM trajectory, CRE loan book performance, deposit cost trends, and any M&A announcements.

## Conclusion

First Bancorp has executed a textbook securities portfolio restructuring that positions the bank for a clean earnings recovery in 2026. The financial health metrics are strong (14.1% CET1, 49% efficiency, 0.10% NCOs), and the 7.8% organic loan growth demonstrates franchise value. However, at 12.6x forward earnings with an ROE that hasn't yet normalized to justify the peer premium, the stock offers limited asymmetric upside. The options market's backwardation structure and elevated put demand signal that sophisticated participants see near-term risk the fundamental picture doesn't capture, likely related to macro/CRE concerns given the 54% CRE concentration in an environment of elevated rates and stagflationary pressure.

Rating: HOLD. The earnings recovery is real and will likely continue, but the market has already recognized and priced the securities restructuring story. Additional upside requires either a cheaper entry point or evidence that operating momentum exceeds current consensus expectations; neither condition is met today.
