## Executive Summary

Tenet Healthcare is trading at $195, representing a 21% discount to its 52-week high of $247, despite generating $2.5B+ in annual free cash flow, posting three consecutive earnings beats averaging 13% above consensus, and executing a strategic transformation that is materially shifting its business mix toward the 34%-margin ambulatory surgery center (USPI) segment. At approximately 11x forward earnings — a meaningful discount to the peer median P/E of 18.0x — the stock appears to over-discount the ACA headwind management has quantified for 2026 while undervaluing the structural margin expansion from hospital-to-ambulatory mix shift, the accretive CommonSpirit/Conifer transaction, and ongoing share count reduction via buybacks.

The core thesis rests on three pillars: (1) USPI's 14% revenue growth and 34% operating margins increasingly dominating the earnings mix, (2) a valuation multiple that implies permanent impairment rather than the cyclical/regulatory headwind that management has quantified and guided through, and (3) $2.5-2.8B in guided 2026 FCF funding simultaneous deleveraging, ASC acquisitions, and aggressive buybacks. The primary risk is regulatory: the OBBBA Medicaid provisions could reduce covered lives meaningfully in Tenet's non-expansion state footprint as various provisions take effect over the coming years.

## Company Overview

Tenet operates two segments: Hospital Operations (50 acute care and specialty hospitals across 8 states, 132 outpatient facilities, and Conifer revenue cycle management) and Ambulatory Care (541 ASCs and 26 surgical hospitals via USPI as of March 31, 2026). The company has undergone radical portfolio transformation, divesting 14 hospitals in 2024 for ~$5B in proceeds while acquiring 27 ASCs and 1 surgical hospital in 2025 alone. This deliberate capital recycling from lower-margin inpatient care into higher-margin ambulatory operations defines the current strategy.

Geographic concentration favors Sunbelt markets (Texas, Florida, Arizona, California) with strong population growth and favorable demographics for surgical demand. USPI's physician joint-venture structure — where referring surgeons hold equity stakes in the ASCs — creates a structural referral and retention moat that is difficult to replicate.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** FY2025 revenue of $21.3B grew 3.1% YoY on an organic basis (after absorbing the dilutive effect of 14 hospital divestitures). Same-hospital revenue growth of 6.5% and USPI revenue growth of 14.1% demonstrate the underlying demand trajectory once portfolio actions are netted out. Adjusted EBITDA of $4.57B (+14.3% YoY) and consolidated net income margin of 11.1% (including noncontrolling interests) reflect the margin accretion from mix shift. Net income attributable to Tenet common shareholders was $1.407B, or approximately 6.6% of revenue, reflecting the significant NCI distributions inherent in the USPI JV model.

**Free Cash Flow:** $2.53B in FY2025 FCF (11.9% margin), with 2026 guided to $2.5-2.8B. FCF yield at current market cap of $16.8B is approximately 15-17%.

**Capital Structure:** Total long-term debt carrying value of approximately $13.2B. Using Tenet shareholders' equity of $4.22B at December 31, 2025, the long-term debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 3.1x. The Altman Z-Score of 1.57 technically falls in the distress zone, but this is structurally misleading: all debt is fixed-rate with staggered maturities, the company has $4.8B in liquidity (cash + undrawn revolver), and interest payments are declining from $865M to $750-760M in 2026 following the November 2025 refinancing. The company's contractual obligations schedule (which includes both principal and interest) shows $738M in 2026, $2.25B in 2027, and $3.0B in 2028; actual principal maturities on the notes begin in November 2027 and extend through November 2033, with only $79M classified as current at year-end 2025. Retained earnings improved from -$803M (2022) to +$4.4B (2025).

**Capital Allocation:** $1.39B in share repurchases in 2025 reduced shares outstanding by approximately 8.8M shares. From ~107M shares (2022) to ~90M — a cumulative 16% reduction. Management's FY2026 guidance implies approximately 87M weighted average diluted shares, representing roughly a 4% year-over-year reduction from FY2025's 90.8M. The remaining $1.49B authorization at year-end 2025 provides continued capacity. Combined with operating earnings growth, this buyback program remains a meaningful EPS accretion engine.

**Q1 2026 Results:** Revenue $5.37B (+2.8% YoY), adjusted EPS of $4.82 vs. $4.22 consensus (+14.1% beat). Management maintained full-year guidance despite acknowledging January/February volume disruptions. Updated FY2026 adjusted diluted EPS guidance to $16.38–$18.68 (from $16.19–$18.47 in February).

## Growth Analysis

USPI is the primary growth driver. At 14.1% revenue growth and 7.1% same-facility revenue per case growth, the ambulatory segment is capturing the secular shift from inpatient to outpatient surgery. Total joint replacement in ASC settings is a fast-growing category supported by technological advances and Medicare's inpatient-only list wind-down. As USPI continues to grow as a proportion of total revenue — currently at approximately 24% and likely expanding toward the mid-to-high-20s based on continued differential growth rates — blended margins will expand mechanically.

Full-year 2026 guidance of $16.38–$18.68 adjusted EPS (midpoint $17.53) implies 12-14% growth over the $15.49 trailing figure. Consensus current-year estimate of $17.79 reflects +8.4% revision over 90 days, indicating the market is underestimating the fundamental trajectory.

The CommonSpirit/Conifer transaction adds ~$1.36B in net cash inflows over 3 years while eliminating a 23.8% NCI obligation, providing both near-term capital for deployment and permanent earnings accretion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $195 and using the consensus FY2026 EPS estimate of $17.79, THC trades at approximately 11.0x forward earnings — a discount of roughly 39% to the peer median P/E of 18.0x. Using Tenet's updated FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $17.53, the forward P/E is approximately 11.1x. Even against the lower-multiple peer UHS (7.0x P/E), THC's discount is modest — and UHS faces similar regulatory headwinds without Tenet's ambulatory growth engine.

On a price-to-FCF basis, the market is implying either significant FCF deterioration or permanent multiple compression. Management's guided $2.5-2.8B in 2026 FCF suggests the former is not their expectation.

Sell-side analyst consensus targets vary across sources; publicly available estimates suggest an average target in the range of $230–$245, implying meaningful upside from current levels. The majority of covering analysts maintain Buy or equivalent ratings, reflecting broad agreement that the stock is undervalued relative to fundamentals.

The discount appears to reflect: (1) OBBBA/ACA coverage concerns, (2) leverage anxiety at ~3x debt/equity, and (3) broader healthcare sector rotation out of managed care/provider names. Management has quantified the ACA headwind at approximately $250M in EBITDA (per earnings call commentary) — equivalent to ~$2.8 per share after-tax — while the stock has declined significantly from highs. Multiple factors have contributed to the share price decline, including broader healthcare sector sentiment, OBBBA uncertainty, and interest-rate/refinancing expectations, and the magnitude of the decline appears disproportionate to the quantified regulatory headwind alone.

## Competitive Landscape

Tenet competes with HCA Healthcare (larger, better capitalized), Universal Health Services, and Community Health Systems. Its competitive differentiation lies in the USPI physician JV model, which creates alignment through equity participation that pure-employee models cannot replicate. The 541-ASC platform provides geographic density that supports referral networks and favorable payer negotiations.

Structural advantages include: revenue cycle management capabilities via Conifer (serving ~600 hospitals), Sunbelt market positioning with favorable population migration trends, and demonstrated willingness to divest underperforming assets (a discipline many hospital operators lack).

Threats include increasing payer consolidation (top 10 payers = 69% of managed care revenue), site neutrality policy risk from CMS, private equity entry into ambulatory care, and UnitedHealth/Optum vertical integration. The physician JV model's reliance on NCI distributions ($960M/year) creates a structural cost that limits margin expansion at the consolidated level.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory Risk (HIGH):** The OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, enacted July 2025) includes multiple health provisions with varying implementation timelines — including Medicaid work requirements, caps on state-directed payments, and limits on provider taxes — that CBO estimates could increase the uninsured population by 10.0M by FY2034. Over half of Tenet's licensed beds are in non-expansion states (FL, SC, TN, TX). Separately, the enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired at end of 2025, and management has indicated on earnings calls that this represents approximately $250M in EBITDA headwind for 2026. Site neutrality risk could compress USPI payment advantages.

**Leverage Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** Approximately 3.1x long-term debt/equity with $13.2B in long-term debt. Fully mitigated in a stable FCF environment, but vulnerable if regulatory changes compress volumes/margins simultaneously with rising refinancing needs. Principal maturities on the notes are staggered from November 2027 through November 2033.

**Concentration Risk (MODERATE):** Top 10 payers = 69% of managed care revenue. Geographic concentration in 4-5 states creates vulnerability to state-specific regulatory changes (e.g., California wage mandates through 2028).

**Malpractice Liability (LOW-MODERATE):** Reserves of $1.23B growing at 8% YoY. A 500bp severity increase would add $256M to reserves.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($250-270):** USPI continues 14%+ growth, mix shift drives blended margins toward 18-20% operating level. ACA headwind proves manageable as management offsets with rate increases and cost discipline. Buybacks reduce share count by ~4% annually, driving EPS toward $20+ by 2027. Multiple re-rates from 11x toward peer-average 14-15x as leverage declines. Target: 14x $18.50 FY2027 EPS = $259.

**Bear Case ($150-160):** OBBBA implementation exceeds $250M headwind. ACA enrollment declines by 30%+ (vs. 20% assumption). Labor cost inflation reaccelerates. Refinancing the 2027-2033 note maturities proves expensive in a high-rate environment, compressing FCF. Multiple compresses further to 8-9x on elevated risk premium. Target: 9x $17 EPS = $153.

## Conclusion

Tenet Healthcare offers a compelling risk/reward at $195. The stock trades at approximately 11x forward earnings — a roughly 39% discount to peer median — with rising estimates (+8.4% over 90 days), three consecutive 10%+ earnings beats, and a structural growth engine (USPI) that is expanding margins through mix shift. The market appears to have over-discounted the ACA and regulatory headwinds relative to their quantified impact, though multiple factors beyond ACA alone have contributed to the share price decline from highs. With ongoing share count reduction and broad analyst consensus favoring the stock, the valuation is priced for significant regulatory impairment that management has thus far demonstrated capability to offset. The primary risk is that OBBBA implementation in 2027+ proves more disruptive than current modeling suggests — but at approximately 11x earnings, substantial negative surprise is already embedded in the price.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The $250M ACA EBITDA headwind figure is sourced from management commentary (earnings calls) rather than SEC-filed documents; the "over-discounting" argument attributing the full share price decline to this single headwind is analytically simplified, as multiple concurrent factors contributed to the decline. ROE is approximately 33.5% (not 56.1% as may appear in summary metrics), and the annual share count reduction rate implied by FY2026 guidance is approximately 4%, not 8%+.
