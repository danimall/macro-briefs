## Executive Summary

Radian Group is in the early stages of a fundamental business transformation. The February 2026 closure of its $1.67 billion Inigo acquisition converts Radian from a mono-line U.S. private mortgage insurer into a diversified specialty insurance company with meaningful Lloyd's market exposure. In Q1 2026 (reflecting only 2 months of Inigo), the specialty segment contributed $164 million in net premiums earned — approximately 41% of total net premiums earned — at an 85% combined ratio. On a run-rate basis, management expects specialty insurance to roughly double Radian's pre-acquisition annual revenue, though Q1 2026 does not reflect a full quarter of Inigo activity.

The stock trades at approximately $35.72, essentially at Q1 2026 book value of $35.67. For a business generating 14.7% adjusted operating ROE with a 4-quarter earnings beat streak and consensus estimates rising 9.9% over 90 days, this valuation represents a clear mispricing. The market continues to assign a mono-line MI multiple (0.9-1.0x book) despite a business profile that now resembles diversified specialty insurers trading at 1.3-1.5x book.

The primary risks are execution-related: CEO transition (Thornberry retiring as CEO effective August 12, 2026, remaining as strategic advisor through December 2026; Weinbach becoming CEO August 13, 2026), interim CFO appointment, and integration of a Lloyd's syndicate by a management team with acknowledged "limited prior experience" in specialty insurance. These are legitimate concerns. However, the valuation at book value prices in substantial execution risk already, and the early Inigo results (85% combined ratio) suggest the business itself is performing well regardless of who manages it from Philadelphia.

## Company Overview

Radian Group Inc. operates as a holding company with two primary segments following the Inigo acquisition:

**Mortgage Insurance (Radian Guaranty):** One of six GSE-approved private mortgage insurers in the U.S., with $282.5 billion insurance in force, $74.7 billion direct primary risk in force, and 18% market share. The mortgage insurance portfolio is approximately 91% recurring premium by risk in force, providing exceptional visibility into MI-segment revenues. PMIERs Available Assets of $5.4 billion represent a 41% cushion above regulatory minimums.

**Specialty Insurance (Inigo, Lloyd's Syndicate 1301):** A fast-growing Lloyd's managing agent writing specialty insurance and reinsurance across property, casualty, and financial lines. Among the fastest-growing syndicates since its 2021 launch. Q1 2026 results showed $164M net premiums earned at an 85% combined ratio.

The company is also divesting non-core Title, Real Estate Services, and Mortgage Conduit businesses (expected by Q3 2026).

- Cash flow yield: ~15% (based on FY2025 operating cash flow)
- Dividend Yield: 2.8%

**Peer comparison:**
- Peer median P/E: 12.2x
- RDN forward P/E: ~6.7x — a 45% discount to peers
- Peer median dividend yield: 1.4%; RDN: 2.8%

**Why the market is wrong:** The market prices Radian as a declining mono-line mortgage insurer with limited growth. The Inigo acquisition changes the earnings profile by adding a high-growth specialty segment. Diversified specialty insurers (RNR, ACGL, etc.) command significantly higher multiples. The market has not yet repriced this business mix shift because:
1. The acquisition closed only 4 months ago (February 2026)
2. Only one quarter of combined results has reported (and that includes only ~2 months of Inigo)
3. C-suite turnover creates uncertainty about execution continuity
4. The holding company liquidity draw-down raised balance sheet concerns

These are *temporary* information gaps, not structural impairments. As 2-3 more quarters of combined results demonstrate the earnings power and stability of the diversified model, re-rating should follow.

**Target valuation:** At 1.3x book value (conservative for a diversified insurer generating 14.7% ROE) on estimated year-end 2026 book value of ~$37, fair value is approximately $48. This aligns with the RBC $47 target and consensus mean of $43.

## Competitive Landscape

**Mortgage Insurance:**
Radian operates in a six-player oligopoly (Arch, Enact, Essent, MGIC, NMI, Radian) with 18% market share. Competitive dynamics are stable — pricing is the primary differentiator, with black-box risk-based pricing models now standard. A new potential entrant (Anza Mortgage Insurance Corp.) has been flagged but has not yet achieved GSE approval. The oligopoly structure and GSE approval barriers create meaningful moats.

**Specialty Insurance:**
Inigo (Syndicate 1301) competes in the Lloyd's market against established syndicates. Its competitive advantage is recent vintage — newer syndicates benefit from underwriting at current-cycle pricing without legacy reserves. However, rate softening in 2026 after a benign 2025 loss year means the pricing tailwind is fading.

**Competitive position trajectory:** The diversification into specialty insurance gives Radian a differentiated positioning versus pure-play MI peers (Enact, Essent, MGIC, NMI), potentially commanding a premium multiple. None of Radian's MI competitors have pursued this diversification path.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration Execution (High):** Management's acknowledged "limited prior experience" in Lloyd's specialty insurance is the most critical risk. The Inigo CEO (Watson) has been retained via significant RSU grants, providing operational continuity at the subsidiary level. But holding company governance of a Lloyd's syndicate requires expertise Radian's board may lack. Mitigant: Inigo operated as an independent entity before acquisition and has existing management infrastructure.

**C-Suite Turnover (Medium-High):** CEO Thornberry retires as CEO and resigns from the board effective August 12, 2026, then remains as strategic advisor through December 2026. Weinbach becomes CEO-elect June 1, 2026 and CEO August 13, 2026. Interim CFO (Kobell) in place following CFO Sumita Pandit's departure on February 12, 2026. CEO and CFO transitions occurring simultaneously during the critical integration phase create elevated execution uncertainty.

**Capital Flexibility Reduction (Medium):** $600M intercompany loan triggers Pennsylvania Insurance Dept. dividend approval for 3-5 years. Holding company liquidity dropped to $391M. Holding company debt-to-capital rose to 20.2% at Q1 2026 (from 18.3% at year-end 2025) reflecting the Inigo financing. The company must rebuild capital buffers while simultaneously integrating Inigo and returning capital to shareholders. Management expects to fully repay $200M credit draw in 2026 and receive $600M+ from Radian Guaranty, so the liquidity trajectory should normalize by year-end.

**Mortgage Credit Cycle (Medium):** Default inventory growing (25,230 vs. 22,021 in 2023). Average claim paid rising ($49K vs. $27K in 2023). Favorable reserve development declining. If housing turns or unemployment rises materially, the MI loss ratio could normalize to historical averages (15-25%), compressing MI profitability. Current 7.1% loss ratio is still well below long-term averages.

**Rate Environment Impact (Medium):** Lower rates → refinancing → policy runoff → declining IIF. Higher rates → constrained originations → lower NIW. The "Goldilocks" scenario (rates stable at current levels, 6.5-7%) is currently in place but could shift.

**Options Market Signal:**
Near-term ATM IV of 34% vs. 23.9% historical vol represents a 10.1pp premium — the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (34% near-term vs. 27.3% 6-month), which may reflect trader positioning around the ongoing information absorption regarding the transformation's trajectory rather than a specific identified catalyst. The call-heavy put/call skew (-22.4%) is constructive — options participants are positioned for upside.

## Conclusion

Radian Group at approximately $35.72 represents a compelling valuation at 1.0x book value for a business generating 14.7% ROE with rising consensus estimates (+9.9% over 90 days). Q1 2026 total revenues increased 58% year-over-year to $466 million, and management expects the Inigo acquisition to roughly double annual revenue on a run-rate basis. The transformation from mono-line MI to diversified specialty insurer is the specific mispricing the market has not yet reflected — only one quarter of combined results exists (including only ~2 months of Inigo), and the analyst community is beginning to upgrade (BofA double-upgrade, RBC initiation at $47). The approximately 6.7x forward P/E sits at a 45% discount to peer median of 12.2x.

The risks are genuine: C-suite turnover during a critical integration phase, reduced capital flexibility from the intercompany loan, post-acquisition leverage increase, and entry into an unfamiliar business line that introduces catastrophe and underwriting-cycle volatility absent from the steady MI stream. But these risks are priced at book value. If even half the re-rating thesis materializes (1.15x book), the stock trades to $42-43 representing 18-20% upside. Combined with 2.8% dividend yield, total return potential exceeds 20% over 12 months with identifiable catalysts (additional quarters of combined reporting, analyst upgrades, divestiture completion).

BUY with a 12-18 month horizon targeting $43 base case.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The "revenue doubled" claim is premature (Q1 YoY growth was 58%, not 100%); only ~2 months of Inigo results are included in Q1; post-acquisition leverage is higher than initially cited (20.2% vs. 18.3%); and the annualized specialty run-rate is mechanically extrapolated from a partial quarter. These corrections reduce certainty in the near-term earnings trajectory but do not invalidate the directional thesis.
