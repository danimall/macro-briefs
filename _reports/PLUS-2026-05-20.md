## Executive Summary

ePlus Inc is a $2.2B technology solutions provider experiencing meaningful growth acceleration driven by AI infrastructure demand. The company's most recent quarter (Q3 FY26, ended December 2025) showed 24.6% revenue growth and 164% operating income growth from continuing operations. At ~15x forward earnings on $5.21 consensus for FY26 and $5.54 for FY27, the stock appears reasonably priced for its growth trajectory. The company divested its financing business in June 2025 for $180M, simplifying the story and freeing capital.

The stock rallied from $63 to $93 between August and December 2025, gave back much of those gains, and now sits at $82 — down 12% from the 52-week high and down 6% from a prior BUY recommendation in early May. The fundamental thesis (AI infrastructure demand → revenue acceleration → margin expansion) remains intact, but estimate revisions have turned slightly negative over the last 30 days (current year flat, next year -4.2%), and the SLED segment faces real DOGE-related headwinds. The stock's inability to hold above $90 despite strong earnings suggests the market may be pricing in demand moderation or tariff-related margin risk that hasn't yet materialized in reported numbers.

The appropriate stance is to maintain a positive medium-term view but acknowledge that entry timing was poor and additional deterioration in estimate revisions would warrant a downgrade. The fundamental case for re-rating requires the next quarter (Q4 FY26, likely reported in May/June 2026) to confirm continued momentum.

## Company Overview

ePlus provides integrated technology solutions to approximately 4,600 enterprise customers across the United States. Following its June 2025 divestiture of the domestic financing business, the company operates three segments:

- **Product** (78% of revenue): Resale of hardware, software, maintenance, and subscriptions from vendors including Cisco (29-34% of product sales), Arista, Dell, HPE, NVIDIA, and Palo Alto Networks.
- **Professional Services** (11%): Consulting, deployment, configuration, and staff augmentation — enhanced by the August 2024 Bailiwick acquisition ($125M).
- **Managed Services** (8%): Recurring revenue from outsourced IT management including SOC services, cloud management, and support desks.

The customer base spans telecom/media/entertainment (23% of tech revenue), SLED (17%), technology (15%), healthcare (14%), and financial services (9%). Verizon remains the largest single customer at 16% of net sales, declining from 22% two years ago.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth — Real Acceleration:**
The headline numbers show FY25 revenue declined 7% to $2.07B, but this was an artifact of the shift to net-basis revenue recognition for subscriptions/maintenance. Underlying demand never weakened. The current fiscal year proves this: 9-month revenue from continuing operations of $1.86B represents 22.2% YoY growth, with Q3 alone at $615M (+24.6%). Gross billings of $982M in Q3 (vs. $850M prior year) show volume momentum is accelerating.

**Margin Expansion:**
- Gross margin: 25.8% in Q3 FY26 vs. 25.4% prior year, driven by product mix shifts (+170bps) and higher vendor incentives (+140bps)
- Operating margin: 7.1% in Q3 vs. 3.3% prior year — dramatic expansion from operating leverage and the Bailiwick acquisition annualizing
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: 8.7% vs. 5.5%

**Balance Sheet:**
The post-divestiture balance sheet is exceptionally clean: $326M cash, essentially zero debt (the financing-related non-recourse debt left with the divested business), and $978M in equity. Current ratio is 2.1x. Altman Z-Score of 3.08 places the company firmly in the safe zone.

**Working Capital Consumption:**
The 9-month operating cash flow of -$223M from continuing operations is the primary concern. This reflects: (a) inventory doubling to $241M as management pre-purchases AI-related memory chips amid global shortages, and (b) accounts receivable surging in line with billings growth. This is growth-related consumption, not deterioration, but it means FCF will remain negative until the inventory position normalizes — management guides this could take "at least the next few quarters."

**Capital Return:**
ePlus initiated its first-ever quarterly dividend ($0.25/share, ~1.2% yield) in August 2025 and bought back 272,900 shares ($21M) in the first nine months. Combined shareholder return: ~$37M.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story rests on three pillars:

1. **AI Infrastructure Demand:** AI is driving purchases across the entire technology stack — compute/GPUs, networking (interconnects), security (protecting AI workloads), cloud services, and advisory/deployment services. Q3 growth by product line: Cloud +50%, Networking +27%, Security +13%. This is broad-based, not concentrated in a single category.

2. **Services Expansion:** Gross billings for services grew 34% in the first nine months. The Bailiwick acquisition adds scale in professional services (particularly retail/hospitality deployments), and managed services create recurring revenue. Over time, the services mix should increase, improving margin quality.

3. **Customer Diversification:** Telecom/media/entertainment grew 40% YoY in Q3 ($176M), healthcare grew steadily, and technology/financial services remain solid. The diversification away from Verizon dependency is a structural positive.

**Headwinds:** SLED revenue declined 16% in Q3 ($60M vs. $71M) due to government spending cuts associated with DOGE. At 17% of tech revenue, this is a meaningful but not existential drag. Tariff uncertainty could slow enterprise purchasing decisions if cost pass-through creates sticker shock.

**Estimate Trajectory:** Current-year consensus is $5.21 (+16.3% from 90 days ago), but the 30-day revision trend is flat to slightly negative. Next-year consensus fell 4.2% in the last 30 days (from $5.79 to $5.54). This deceleration in upward revision momentum is the most concerning near-term signal — it suggests the market may be front-running a growth moderation.

## Valuation Assessment

At $82.58, PLUS trades at approximately:
- ~16x FY26E earnings ($5.21 consensus)
- ~15x FY27E earnings ($5.54 consensus)
- Forward P/E of 14.6x per Yahoo Finance data

Relative to peers:
- PC Connection (CNXN): 18.9x P/E
- Insight Enterprises (NSIT): 15.8x P/E
- ScanSource (SCSC): 13.0x P/E
- Arrow Electronics (ARW): 32.6x P/E
- Peer median: 15.8x

PLUS trades roughly in line with the peer median P/E, but is growing revenue 22%+ while most peers are flat or declining. On a PEG basis (14.6x P/E ÷ ~20% EPS growth = 0.73 PEG), the stock appears undervalued relative to its growth rate. The lone analyst covering the stock has a $115 target, implying 39% upside.

The valuation math: At 16-18x the $5.50 midpoint FY27 estimate, fair value ranges from $88-99. If the company can sustain 15%+ growth and margins continue expanding toward the guidance of +41% EBITDA growth, a higher multiple (18-20x) would imply $99-110. The market is currently applying a below-growth-rate multiple, likely reflecting concern about SLED headwinds, tariff pass-through risk, and working capital consumption.

## Competitive Landscape

ePlus operates in a fragmented but competitive market. Key dynamics:

**Positive competitive signals:** Revenue growth of 22%+ while peers (CNXN, NSIT, SCSC) show flat or declining revenue indicates share gains. The company's 30+ year relationships with 4,600 customers and deep vendor certifications create switching costs — changing your IT solutions provider is operationally disruptive.

**Risks to moat:** OEMs are building direct sales channels (Cisco+, Dell direct). Cloud marketplaces (AWS Marketplace, Azure Marketplace) could disintermediate traditional VARs for software and subscription purchasing. The professional services opportunity exists precisely because technology is complex — if AI tools simplify deployment, advisory value diminishes over time.

**Bailiwick acquisition:** Adds capability in retail/hospitality technology rollouts with 441 employees. The lower-margin profile (more third-party subcontractor delivery) dilutes professional services margins by 190bps but adds revenue breadth. Integration execution will determine whether this becomes a growth engine or a margin drag.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Cisco Vendor Concentration (29-34% of product sales):** Loss of Cisco partnership or competitive displacement by alternative vendors would be severely damaging. Cisco's own direct-selling initiatives represent a slow structural risk.

2. **Verizon Customer Concentration (16% of net sales):** Though declining, any significant Verizon capex reduction would create a material quarterly gap. The telecom vertical's 40% Q3 growth may partially represent Verizon spending — a reversion would be painful.

3. **Inventory Risk ($241M, doubled from $120M):** AI-related memory chip hoarding carries write-down risk if demand softens or supply normalizes faster than expected. DIO expanded from 13 to 22 days.

4. **DOGE/Government Spending (SLED = 17% of tech revenue):** Federal funding cuts flowing through to state/local/education budgets create a multi-quarter headwind with uncertain duration.

5. **Tariff Pass-Through Risk:** Management indicates tariffs will be "largely passed on to customers," but this assumes price elasticity is low. If enterprise buyers delay or reduce purchases in response to 10-30% price increases on hardware, volume could suffer.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 40.4% is elevated relative to 32.7% realized vol (7.7pp spread), with term structure in backwardation (40.4% near vs. 35.8% at 6-month). This indicates the market prices near-term uncertainty — likely around the upcoming quarterly report (Q4 FY26). Put/call OI ratio of 0.72 and call-heavy skew (-6.7%) suggest options positioning remains moderately bullish despite the elevated vol. IV collapsed from 63.2% to 40.4% over the past two weeks as post-earnings volatility normalized, suggesting the current level reflects residual uncertainty rather than acute stress.

Short interest at 5.4% of float with 7.1 days to cover is moderately elevated — enough to indicate some bearish conviction but not enough to create a meaningful squeeze catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q4 FY26 earnings (expected May/June 2026) showing continued 20%+ revenue growth AND estimate revisions turning positive again
2. Stock pulling back to $75 or below without fundamental deterioration (creating 20%+ upside to fair value)
3. FY27 consensus rising above $5.75 (reversing recent downward revision)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q4 FY26 revenue growth decelerating below 15%
2. Gross margin compression below 24% (indicating tariff absorption or mix deterioration)
3. Verizon revenue declining >20% YoY in any quarter
4. Management cutting FY26/FY27 guidance

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q4 FY26, expected May-June 2026) is the primary catalyst. The most recent earnings were reported on February 4, 2026 — the Q4 report should come approximately early May 2026. Secondary catalyst: any Cisco or Verizon-specific news that impacts the concentration risks.

## Conclusion

ePlus has a genuine growth story backed by AI infrastructure demand, with strong Q3 results and a clean balance sheet. At ~15x forward earnings with 20%+ growth, the valuation is attractive on a PEG basis. However, the prior BUY recommendation issued at $83.45 has resulted in a -2% loss, and the estimate revision trend has deteriorated over the last 30 days. The stock is range-bound between $74-90 since February, suggesting the market wants to see another confirming quarter before re-rating.

The fundamental thesis has not deteriorated — revenue growth is robust, margins are expanding, and the company is gaining share in a large addressable market. But the absence of positive estimate revision momentum removes the near-term catalyst for appreciation. The appropriate approach is to hold existing positions with a bullish lean, waiting for either: (a) price weakness below $75 creating a better entry, or (b) the next earnings report confirming sustained momentum and reigniting upward revisions.
