## Executive Summary

Graham Holdings is a family-controlled diversified holding company that reports a 9.4x trailing P/E, a 1.05x price-to-book ratio and 7% revenue growth. Each of those figures needs adjustment before it can be used. The 9.4x multiple is computed on last-twelve-month GAAP diluted EPS of roughly $123.70, of which the largest single component (a $137.0M pretax pension settlement gain recognised in June 2026) was *reclassified out of accumulated other comprehensive income into net income* rather than generated. The 10-Q makes this explicit: AOCI "unrealized gain on pensions" fell from $601.1M to $432.1M over the first half while retained earnings rose, and Q2 2026 comprehensive income was $120.7M against net income of $282.4M. Total common equity actually declined from $4,794.0M to $4,761.6M over H1 2026 despite $310.2M of reported net income. On management's own adjusted basis ($19.46 in Q2 and $36.19 in H1) the stock trades at approximately 17.1x the current-year consensus of $69.00 and 17.6x next year's $67.00.

The operating businesses beneath that accounting are a mixed set. Adjusted first-half EPS grew 39% year over year, which is real, but the drivers are identifiable and partly non-repeating: a $15.8M political-advertising swing plus Winter Olympics and Super Bowl inventory at the NBC affiliates, a 33% manufacturing revenue increase almost entirely from the July 2025 Arconic Architectural acquisition, and a 12.6% effective tax rate versus 29.8% a year earlier. Against that, Kaplan International revenue fell 7% reported and 10% constant currency on "broad declines at the Pathways businesses in the UK, Australia and the US"; CSI Pharmacy grew revenue 31% in H1 while segment operating income fell 20%; automotive operating income fell 15% on new and used vehicle gross-profit normalisation; and management guides that "retransmission revenue net of network fees in 2026 [is] expected to decline compared with 2025, and this trend is expected to continue." Consensus next-year EPS of $67.00 sits below the current-year $69.00, consistent with the loss of the political-advertising cycle and the January 2026 restriction of UK apprenticeship levy funding for Level 7 programmes, which represent roughly half of Kaplan's UK Professional Education business and whose pipeline effect builds from 2027.

We are lowering the overall score to 5.8 from 6.4 and maintaining HOLD. The downgrade is not a response to price action; it reflects three specific inputs: the identification of the headline earnings multiple as an accounting artifact rather than genuine cheapness, dated new headwinds in the two largest profit pools (UK Level 7 from 2027, retransmission economics with an unusually short 18-month CBS renewal at WKMG), and first-half capital returns of $138.3M against roughly $81.2M of free cash flow, funded by a $110.2M drawdown of corporate cash and $50.0M of securities sales. Our base case is $1,250 (+6.2%), bull $1,420 (+20.6%), bear $950 (-19.3%).

## Company Overview

GHC is the successor to the Washington Post Company, now operating six unrelated business lines with a large non-operating balance sheet attached. FY2025 revenue of $4,911.6M split as follows: Education (Kaplan) $1,744.3M or 36%; Automotive $1,133.2M or 23%; Healthcare $815.0M or 17%; Manufacturing $436.3M; Television Broadcasting $425.1M; Other Businesses roughly $360M at a $94.2M operating loss.

Reportable segment operating income before amortisation and impairments totalled $440.9M in FY2025 across the five operating divisions, against which sit the $94.2M Other Businesses loss and $67.4M of corporate cost, reconciling to the $279.3M company-selected performance measure used in the proxy. Segment margins diverge sharply: television 27.7% in FY2025 (38.6% in the political year 2024), Kaplan Higher Education 16.2%, CSI Pharmacy 11.5%, manufacturing 7.3%, automotive 2.5%. Automotive is 23% of revenue and roughly 8% of segment profit.

The "Consumer Discretionary / Education Services" classification is misleading for valuation purposes. Education generated 36% of revenue and about 34% of segment operating profit; healthcare is the growth engine at 17% of revenue and 22% of segment profit. The proxy's peer-group TSR index stood at 73.22 (below its $100 base) against GHC's 216.22, which is what one expects when a peer set is education-weighted and the subject is not.

**Share count and market capitalisation.** The data feed reports a $3.85B market capitalisation, which at $1,177.145 implies roughly 3.27M shares, consistent with Class B float only. The 10-Q discloses **4,251,268 shares outstanding at 30 June 2026**, giving an implied capitalisation of approximately **$5.00B** and book value per share of **$1,120** ($4,761.6M ÷ 4,251,268), or 1.05x price-to-book. All multiples in this report use the filed share count. This discrepancy should be resolved before relying on any screened multiple for GHC.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings quality is the central issue

FY2025 pretax income of $449.7M included $127.5M of non-operating pension and postretirement benefit income and $200.2M of net gains on marketable equity securities. Removing both leaves $122.0M of pretax income against $234.9M of operating income; roughly 73% of FY2025 pretax income came from two non-cash, non-operating sources. FY2024 was more extreme: pretax income of $1,024.7M on operating income of $215.5M, because non-operating pension income contained a $653.4M settlement gain from the October 2024 group annuity purchase.

The June 2026 transaction repeated the pattern at smaller scale: $113.9M of plan assets purchased an irrevocable annuity settling $124.3M of obligation for approximately 1,080 retirees, producing a $137.0M pretax gain ($101.3M after tax, $23.21 per share). The gain is a reclassification, not creation of value. Annuitisation does deliver genuine economics (permanent transfer of longevity and investment risk plus reduced administration), so the transaction is not cosmetic. But the *earnings statement* is uninformative: comprehensive income of $145.1M for H1 against net income of $313.1M is the more useful figure, and equity fell over the period.

Two other H1 2026 items warrant naming. The KLG divestiture (closed 1 May 2026) produced a $24.2M pretax impairment and loss on sale but a **$69.6M US tax benefit** ($15.93 per share) whose realisation the company says depends on forthcoming IRS guidance; a partially offsetting **$19.2M Pillar Two top-up accrual** ($4.39 per share) could reverse entirely if the UK enacts the OECD Side-by-Side Safe Harbor. Securities gains added $33.0M pretax ($5.62 per share after tax). The H1 effective tax rate was 12.6% versus 29.8%.

A useful correction to the historical record: FY2025 net interest expense of $110.5M against roughly $720-880M of debt looked anomalous. The 10-Q resolves it. H1 2025 interest expense included **$67.6M of fair-value adjustment on a mandatorily redeemable noncontrolling interest** at Graham Healthcare Group, largely reflecting a substantial increase in CSI's estimated fair value, including a $66.2M increase triggered by the February 2025 settlement of the GHC One interest for $205M. The obligation balance has fallen to $7.4M and H1 2026 recorded a $0.9M credit. True cash-relevant net interest was $29.0M in H1 2026, implying roughly $60M annually, materially below what GAAP showed in 2024-25.

### Cash flow

FY2025 operating cash flow was $347.2M against $71.9M of cash capital expenditure, giving free cash flow of roughly $275.3M. H1 2026 operating cash flow fell to $120.4M from $140.8M, with capex of $39.2M leaving approximately $81.2M of free cash flow. The decline is attributable to a $128.0M working-capital swing (versus +$22.4M a year earlier) from inventory purchases and the absence of the prior-year MRNCI accrual.

We will not annualise a half-year. Cash flows are strongly seasonal: Kaplan International collects tuition in advance and deferred revenue fell $90.3M in H1; television advertising is back-half weighted in political years, and the November 2026 midterms fall in H2; the automotive floor plan swings with inventory. As a labelled sensitivity only: doubling H1 free cash flow gives roughly $162M, while holding FY2025's $275.3M and subtracting the incremental capex implied by management's $90-100M FY2026 guidance gives roughly $250M. The company's own Adjusted Operating Cash Flow measure (operating income before amortisation and impairment, plus depreciation, plus pension expense) was $232.7M in H1, up 17%, suggesting a full-year figure near $470-490M on that definition.

### Balance sheet

At 30 June 2026: total assets $8,141.1M; cash $156.8M (down from roughly $267M at year-end) plus restricted cash $51.3M; marketable equity securities and other investments $1,089.3M against a cost basis of $271.0M, including Berkshire Hathaway at $557.2M, more than 5% of total assets, and Markel at $124.6M (Markel's CEO Thomas Gayner sits on GHC's board); investments in affiliates $208.6M; goodwill $1,600.6M; prepaid pension asset $2,723.6M against a $640.3M benefit obligation; deferred tax liabilities $850.9M; total debt $900.4M at a 5.7% weighted average rate; equity $4,761.6M.

Debt structure is comfortable after the November 2025 refinancing: $493.8M net of 5.625% notes due December 2033, a $400M revolver maturing November 2030 with $168.8M undrawn, real-estate and capital term loans of $89.4M and $60.2M due 2028, and $24.6M of 8% CSI seller notes to April 2028. A separate $117.1M vehicle floor plan sits in accounts payable at 5.7%. Ratings are Ba1 stable (Moody's, November 2025) and BB stable (S&P, June 2026): sub-investment grade, which is why GHC pays a spread, but affirmed with stable outlooks and no covenant issues.

An Altman Z-Score of 2.44 places GHC in the grey zone; we treat that as low-information here. The score's inputs include EBIT/assets and sales/assets, both mechanically depressed by a $2.72B non-operating pension asset and a $1.09B securities portfolio. Actual solvency evidence points the other way: $1.25B of cash plus marketable securities against $900M of debt, no required pension contributions in 2024, 2025 or expected in 2026, and covenant compliance.

**Financial health: 7.0.** Strong asset backing and no maturity cliff, offset by sub-investment-grade ratings, a rising capex plan, and first-half capital returns exceeding free cash flow.

Forward drivers are policy-determined rather than competitive. Separately, the CMS Home Health Rule for 2026 reduces aggregate Medicare payments to home health agencies by 1.3% versus CY2025, and Graham Healthcare Group derives more than 90% of home health/hospice/palliative revenue from Medicare and Medicare Advantage.

Estimate revisions are mixed and rest on a single estimator, so their information content is limited: current quarter $17.46 (-4.4% over 30 days, -8.7% over 90 days), current year $69.00 (+4.2% over 30 days, -0.9% over 90 days), next year $67.00 (+1.5%/+1.8%). The structurally important point is that next year is below this year. The beat record is strong on the adjusted basis (+29.9%, +26.8%, -19.3%, +16.3% over the last four quarters), but a single-analyst consensus is easy to beat.

**Growth prospects: 5.3.** Real first-half improvement, identifiable non-repeating drivers, and a 2027 air pocket in both television and UK professional education.

## Valuation Assessment

**Multiples on a matched basis.** LTM GAAP diluted EPS of roughly $123.70 gives 9.5x, which is the feed's figure and is not usable, because it capitalises AOCI recycling and securities marks. LTM adjusted EPS of $60.89 gives 19.3x. Forward: 17.1x the $69.00 current-year consensus and 17.6x next year's $67.00. Peer median for education services is 17.2x (LOPE 18.5x, ATGE 18.8x, LAUR 17.2x, DUOL 15.6x, LRN 11.6x). GHC is therefore at the peer median on adjusted forward earnings while growing more slowly than most of that set. The multiple is fair, not cheap.

**Price to book.** 1.05x reported book of $1,120 per share. Book quality requires adjustment in both directions. Working against it: the $2.72B prepaid pension asset is 57% of equity and cannot be distributed to shareholders without punitive excise and income tax; net of the associated deferred tax it is roughly $2.04B, and applying a further 50% haircut for extraction cost reduces adjusted book by roughly $1.0B to about $884 per share, putting the stock at 1.33x adjusted book. Working in its favour: the operating businesses generating $279.3M of operating income before amortisation and impairment are almost certainly worth more than their carrying value, and $812.1M of unrealised securities gains are already marked.

**Sum of the parts.** Using annualised Adjusted Operating Cash Flow of roughly $480M for the consolidated operating platform (a blend of television at 7-8x, education at 8-10x, healthcare at 10-12x, manufacturing at 7-8x and automotive retail at 5-6x, net of the Other Businesses drag and corporate cost), a 7.0-8.5x range gives $3.36B-$4.08B. Adding securities at approximately $919M after tax on the embedded gain, affiliates at $208.6M, cash at $156.8M, and the pension surplus at 30-40% of face ($817M-$1,089M), less $900.4M of debt:

- Conservative: $3.36B + $919M + $209M + $157M + $817M − $900M = $4.56B → **$1,073/share**
- Central: $3.72B + $919M + $209M + $157M + $953M − $900M = $5.06B → **$1,190/share**
- Favourable: $4.08B + $919M + $209M + $157M + $1,089M − $900M = $5.55B → **$1,306/share**

**Enterprise value cross-check.** Market capitalisation of $5.00B plus $900.4M debt plus $117.1M floor plan less $156.8M cash, $1,089.3M securities and $208.6M affiliates gives an EV of roughly $4.57B, or 9.5x estimated FY2026 Adjusted Operating Cash Flow with no credit for the pension surplus, and roughly 7.8x if the surplus is credited at 30%. Neither reading is compelling on either side.

**The sole sell-side view is bearish.** One analyst covers the name with a sell rating and a $990 target, 16% below the current price. We would not weight a single target heavily, but the only published estimate of fair value is materially lower than spot, and the "analyst targets are floors" heuristic does not apply to a stock at a peer-median multiple with a sole sell rating.

**Valuation: 5.0.** Fair value. The apparent cheapness of the headline multiple is an accounting artifact; on matched bases the stock sits at the peer median with below-peer growth and asset backing that is genuine but partly trapped. The margin of safety has narrowed since our April review: at roughly $1,060 the same base case offered low-double-digit upside; at $1,177.145 it offers about 6%.

## Competitive Landscape

Kaplan's advantages are contractual rather than scale-based, and they are real: sole authorised assessment provider for the Solicitors Qualifying Examination in England and Wales (awarded 2018), a 30-year Transition and Operations Support Agreement with Purdue Global from March 2018, a state-wide Illinois test-prep contract covering all Illinois public university students plus five community colleges, and a 2024 California State Bar contract for exam question development. Kaplan Higher Education served 74,968 students in 2025; Supplemental Education served roughly 782,280 students and 3,420 B2B clients including 165 Fortune 500 companies. The binding constraint on this division is government policy in three countries, not competitive displacement.

Television broadcasting is consolidating around an unsettled regulatory frame. The FCC eliminated the two-top-four ownership restriction in July 2025, a national ownership cap proceeding is open, and in November 2025 the FCC opened a proceeding on the network-affiliate relationship. Relaxed caps cut both ways for a seven-station group: GHC gains optionality as an acquirer of in-market duopolies, but Nexstar, Sinclair, Gray and Tegna can outbid it, and stronger networks can extract more from smaller affiliate groups. The **WKMG/CBS renewal signed in May 2026 covers only 1 July 2026 to 31 December 2027 (eighteen months)** against a four-year KSAT/ABC deal signed in March 2026. A materially shorter term on one affiliation is consistent with unresolved economics and limited affiliate leverage; it is equally consistent with CBS aligning affiliate expiries to a common calendar. The filings do not distinguish the two readings. All seven stations were broadcasting ATSC 3.0 at year-end 2025, though no datacasting revenue is quantified.

CSI Pharmacy is taking share in a structurally expanding home-infusion market (site-of-care shift plus growing immunoglobulin indications), but licensure is state-by-state and supply depends on plasma availability and third-party manufacturers. The 20% first-half operating income decline on 31% revenue growth indicates GHC is currently trading margin for footprint.

Automotive at eight dealerships is sub-scale against AutoNation, Lithia, Penske and Group 1, and the 2.5% FY2025 segment margin is unremarkable. Manufacturing's 33% first-half revenue growth is acquisition-driven, with operating income held back by amortisation and Arconic transition costs.

**Competitive position: 5.4.** Improving in healthcare, defensible in supplemental education and the independent Jacksonville station, deteriorating in Kaplan International pathways, retransmission and automotive gross profit.

## Risk Assessment

**Purdue Global fee recognition is discretionary and retrospectively adjustable.** Kaplan is entitled to 13% of Purdue Global revenue plus an 8% service fee on costs, but only subject to Purdue Global having cash available after covering its own academic costs. The 10-Q states Kaplan will assess quarterly "whether to record all or part of the fee in the future **and whether to adjust fee amounts recognized in earlier periods**." Kaplan Higher Education's 16.2% margin is the highest in Kaplan, and its incremental costs are largely fixed, so a fee reduction would fall almost entirely to operating income. Current trend is favourable: the receivable fell from $87.4M at year-end to $25.5M at 30 June, $16.3M of the $20M advance was repaid in Q2, and enrolments rose 5%.

**Pension plan concentration underwrites 57% of book equity.** At 30 June the plan held one common stock and one private fund together representing $1,232.0M, roughly 37% of plan assets; approximately 41% of assets are internally managed with the majority in Berkshire Hathaway; total equity exposure is 80%. A severe equity drawdown would compress the surplus, reduce future non-operating pension income (calculated off a 6.25% expected return) and, at the extreme, restore contribution requirements. The cushion is currently about 4.3x the $640.3M obligation, so this is a sensitivity rather than a near-term threat.

**Tax items are unresolved in both directions.** The $69.6M KLG tax benefit depends on forthcoming IRS guidance for ultimate realisation; the $19.2M Pillar Two accrual could reverse entirely if the UK enacts the Side-by-Side Safe Harbor. Together these are roughly $20 per share of two-sided exposure.

**Other Businesses remains a structural drain:** operating losses of $98.1M, $99.1M and $94.2M in 2023-25, improving to $(41.3)M in H1 2026, with Framebridge still described as investment-stage and continuing to open stores.

**Contingencies doubled:** reasonably possible losses above accruals rose from roughly $10M in the 10-K to roughly $20M in the Q2 10-Q, still immaterial in absolute terms. Borrower Defense to Repayment claims seeking discharge of roughly $35M of loans remain outstanding.

**Governance and liquidity.** Dual-class structure with Class A electing a majority of directors; the automotive dealerships GHC owns 90% of are operated by an Ourisman-affiliated entity paid $8.4M in management fees in 2025; the portfolio holds $124.6M of Markel stock while Markel's CEO serves on the board. All are disclosed. Trading liquidity is thin: average daily volume of roughly 25,100 shares against 135,934 shares short (8.1% of float, 6.9 days to cover), which cuts both ways: a squeeze on good news, and poor exit liquidity on bad.

**Unexplained item.** Equity in affiliates swung from +$15.0M for H1 (including +$13.1M from non-managed affiliates) to $(19.9)M in Q2 alone (including $(17.9)M non-managed), implying roughly +$31M in Q1 followed by $(17.9)M in Q2, a ~$49M quarter-over-quarter swing on a $208.6M carrying value. The 10-Q itemises only the $5.8M Realm impairment. This remains unresolved.

**Risk factors: 5.6.** Deep asset cushion and no maturity cliff offset by several live, specific and unpriced items.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $1,420 (+20.6%)

Healthcare margins normalise as CSI's pharmacy build-out anniversaries, converting 30%-plus revenue growth into segment profit growth; H2 2026 television benefits from midterm political advertising on top of the H1 Olympics and Super Bowl; the Pillar Two accrual reverses ($4.39 per share) and the KLG tax benefit is confirmed; Other Businesses continues to narrow toward breakeven, adding $40-50M of annual operating income relative to 2024; and management exhausts the remaining 352,011-share authorisation (8.3% of shares outstanding) near current levels, compounding book value per share. Book value per share reaches roughly $1,200 by mid-2027 and the market pays 1.18x, or equivalently roughly 20.5x adjusted earnings of $69-71. Optionality exists in relaxed FCC ownership rules allowing accretive in-market station acquisitions, and in continued use of the pension surplus as acquisition currency: the 2025 purchase price allocation shows $71.2M of cash plus the assumption of $107.5M of net pension obligations, with management stating that "a significant portion of the purchase price [for Arconic Architectural Products] was funded by the Company's assumption of certain pension obligations." That mechanism is differentiated and unavailable to most acquirers of legacy industrial assets.

### Bear case — $950 (-19.3%)

2027 delivers the air pocket the consensus already partly signals: no political advertising, retransmission revenue net of network fees declining as guided, the UK Level 7 apprenticeship funding restriction working through a business representing half of UK professional education, Australian visa allocations constraining Kaplan Business School, and CMS home health rates down 1.3%. Kaplan International's -10% constant-currency decline continues rather than stabilising. Purdue Global fee recognition is reduced or retrospectively adjusted, removing high-margin revenue. Automotive gross profit continues normalising. Adjusted EPS settles nearer $62-65 and the market applies 14-15x, giving $900-975, with the 52-week low of $929.76 and the sole sell-side target of $990 as reference points. Trapped pension capital and a controlled share structure prevent the asset backing from being realised.

### Base case — $1,250 (+6.2%)

Adjusted EPS of roughly $69 in 2026 and $67-70 in 2027, book value per share advancing to roughly $1,190-1,200 on retained earnings plus buyback accretion at 1.05x book, and the market continuing to pay 1.05x book and roughly 18x adjusted earnings. This corresponds to the central sum-of-the-parts output of $1,190 plus a year of per-share book growth. Total return including the 0.64% dividend is roughly 7%.

Probability-weighting 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear gives an expected return of approximately +3.5%, or 4.1% including the dividend, below what we would require to initiate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (fourth consecutive). Rationale: uncertain.** The evidence base is unusually rich for a company that gives no guidance and holds no analyst calls, but the base case sits within 6% of the current price with roughly symmetric bull and bear cases. The 0.64% dividend yield does not support an income thesis, and the 12-month appreciation case is thin enough that we will not classify this as a growth hold.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 10-Q and press release, expected late October 2026. That print is the first clean read on (a) whether CSI's operating income inflects positively as pharmacy build-out costs anniversary, (b) H2 political advertising strength at the seven stations, and (c) whether Kaplan International's constant-currency decline stabilises. The FY2026 10-K (expected late February 2027) will be the first document showing 2027 Level 7 pipeline effects and the retransmission trajectory in full-year form.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. Price at or below $1,050, which on our central sum-of-the-parts of $1,190 restores a double-digit margin of safety, or below $1,000 for a BUY.
2. CSI Pharmacy operating income growing year over year for two consecutive quarters alongside revenue growth above 20%; the current 31%/-20% divergence resolving in the right direction would add roughly $15-20M of annualised segment profit and validate the healthcare thesis.
3. Kaplan International constant-currency revenue returning to flat or positive for two consecutive quarters, indicating pathway enrolment has bottomed.
4. UK enactment of the OECD Side-by-Side Safe Harbor, reversing the $19.2M accrual, combined with confirmation of the $69.6M KLG tax benefit, roughly $20 per share of resolved two-sided exposure.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE:**
1. Any reduction in the Purdue Global fee recognised, or any retrospective adjustment to fees recognised in prior periods.
2. Full-year 2026 free cash flow below $180M (versus $275.3M in FY2025) with buybacks and dividends still exceeding it, indicating capital returns funded structurally by cash drawdown and securities sales rather than operations.
3. FY2027 consensus falling below $62 (currently $67), which at 15x implies $930.
4. A material compression of the pension surplus (the prepaid asset falling below $2.2B) which would remove the principal support for book value and for the acquisition-funding mechanism.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** maintain. The asset backing at 1.05x reported book, the $352,011-share remaining authorisation acting as a persistent bid, and the absence of any near-term maturity make the downside orderly rather than abrupt. Prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at $1,177.145; the $1,050 area is where the same base case becomes attractive.

## Conclusion

Graham Holdings is a well-run, conservatively financed holding company trading at approximately fair value once its earnings statement is put on a comparable basis. The headline 9.4x P/E does not describe the business: it capitalises a $137.0M pension settlement gain that was reclassified out of accumulated other comprehensive income, plus mark-to-market securities gains, in a period when comprehensive income was $145.1M against $313.1M of reported net income and total equity declined. On management's own adjusted numbers the stock trades at roughly 17.1x current-year and 17.6x next-year consensus (the education-services peer median) while consensus next-year EPS sits below this year's and the two largest recurring profit pools face dated policy headwinds in 2027.

What supports the shares is asset backing and capital discipline: $1.25B of cash and marketable securities against $900M of debt, a $2.72B pension surplus that has been used both to fund severance and to acquire operating businesses, book value of $1,120 per share, and buybacks retiring 3-4% of shares annually at roughly book value. What limits them is that the surplus is partly trapped, the growth is concentrated in a segment whose incremental margins are currently negative, and the price now offers roughly 6% upside to our central estimate against 19% downside in the bear case.

We are lowering the overall score to 5.8 from 6.4 on three specific inputs (the reclassification of the earnings multiple from cheap to fair, the emergence of dated 2027 headwinds in UK professional education and retransmission, and first-half capital returns exceeding free cash flow) and maintaining HOLD.
