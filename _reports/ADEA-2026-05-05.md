## Executive Summary

Adeia is a pure-play intellectual property licensing company with a portfolio of ~13,750 patents spanning media technologies (content discovery, advertising, personalization) and semiconductor IP (hybrid bonding/DBI®, advanced packaging, thermal management). The business model is extraordinarily capital-efficient: $1.8M in annual capex, 60% adjusted EBITDA margins, and $156M in annual free cash flow on $443M of 2025 revenue. The March 2026 AMD license agreement validated Adeia's semiconductor IP against a tier-1 logic chipmaker and opened the door to additional licensees across the AI hardware stack.

The stock has appreciated 172% over the past 12 months, driven by the AMD deal, Microsoft signing, and Disney resolution. At $33.63, Adeia trades at 34x trailing earnings and approximately 21x trailing FCF — reasonable for an asset-light, high-margin IP business with secular growth tailwinds, but no longer offering asymmetric upside given the CEO's announced departure, 2026 revenue guidance that appears flat vs. 2025, and options markets pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The risk/reward profile favors patient investors waiting for a pullback to the $25-27 range rather than initiating positions at current levels.

## Company Overview

Adeia was spun off from Xperi Holding Corporation in October 2022, retaining the IP licensing operations. The company generates revenue by licensing its patent portfolio to technology companies across two segments:

**Media IP** (~75% of revenue): Licensed to MVPDs (Charter, Comcast, DISH), OTT platforms (Disney, Amazon, YouTube TV), consumer electronics manufacturers (Samsung, LG, Roku, TCL), and emerging verticals (e-commerce via L'Oréal, automotive). Revenue here is predominantly recurring via per-subscriber or per-unit royalties.

**Semiconductor IP** (~25% of revenue, growing): Licensed to memory manufacturers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, Kioxia) and logic chipmakers (AMD, UMC). Covers hybrid bonding (Direct Bond Interconnect), advanced packaging, and RapidCool thermal management. This segment has the strongest secular tailwind given AI's demand for chiplet architectures and high-bandwidth memory.

The company employs approximately 150 people. Its cost structure comprises R&D (~15% of revenue) to refresh and expand the patent portfolio, SG&A (~27%), and litigation expense (~5-6%). There is virtually no physical infrastructure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** FY2025 revenue of $443M grew 18% YoY, inflated by the Disney long-term agreement which contributed ~$57M in non-recurring recognition. Underlying recurring revenue grew 3% to $351M. FY2026 guidance of $395-435M (midpoint $415M) implies an apparent decline, but adjusted for the Disney one-timer, underlying growth is 10-15% driven by AMD, Microsoft, and new customer additions. Non-Pay-TV recurring revenue grew 28% YoY in Q1 2026.

**Margins:** Operating margin expanded to 39.5% in FY2025 from 34.2% in FY2024. Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 60% in Q1 2026. The near-zero variable cost structure means incremental revenue from new licenses drops almost entirely to operating income.

**Cash Generation:** OCF of $158M in FY2025 (down from $212M in FY2024 due to working capital timing on large deals). FCF of $156M given negligible capex ($1.8M). FCF yield on current market cap: approximately 4.2%.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt of ~$290M (Term Loan B at SOFR + 2.50%, maturing June 2028). Debt reduced from $620M at spin-off to under $400M by Q1 2026 — aggressive deleveraging. Current ratio of 3.81x provides ample liquidity. Interest expense declining ($52.5M in 2024 → $40.4M in 2025) as principal decreases.

**Capital Allocation:** Debt reduction ($60M/year), dividends ($22M/year, $0.20/share = 0.6% yield), buybacks ($20M at average $13.55 in 2025 — these shares are now worth approximately 2.5x purchase price), and tuck-in patent acquisitions ($7M). Remaining buyback authorization: $160M.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is driven by three vectors with distinct timelines:

**1. Semiconductor IP (highest growth, 3-5 year runway):** The AMD deal validates hybrid bonding IP in logic semiconductors. The total addressable market includes Intel, Nvidia (via TSMC packaging), and other logic manufacturers adopting chiplet architectures. HBM scaling for AI (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron already licensed) provides recurring growth as production volumes increase. RapidCool thermal technology addresses power density constraints in AI datacenters — an emerging licensing opportunity. Management targets this segment reaching $150M+ in revenue.

**2. Media IP diversification (steady, 2-3 year runway):** Traditional Pay-TV declining 3-5% annually, but being offset by OTT platform additions (Disney, Amazon, Microsoft/Xbox) and new verticals (e-commerce, gaming, automotive). L'Oréal license demonstrates applicability of computer vision/recommendation IP beyond traditional media. Non-Pay-TV recurring revenue growing 28% YoY.

**3. Enforcement pipeline (lumpy, 12-18 months):** DISH Network (license expired March 2026, litigation filed April 2026) and DIRECTV represent known unlicensed revenue. Based on historical pattern (Disney resolved in ~12 months, AMD in ~4 months), these likely convert to signed multi-year agreements.

The $500M long-term revenue target implies 12-15% CAGR from FY2025 recurring revenue base. Current consensus estimates $1.44 EPS for FY2026 and $1.50 for FY2027, suggesting the market expects modest growth. The semiconductor opportunity could support a steeper trajectory if additional logic licensees sign.

## Valuation Assessment

At $33.63, Adeia trades at:
- 34x trailing EPS ($0.99)
- 23x FY2026E EPS ($1.44)
- ~21x trailing FCF ($156M / ~$3.3B enterprise value)
- ~13x trailing EV/EBITDA (~$260M EBITDA)
- 7.6x FY2025 revenue

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 15.1x (KD, TDC, DXC, ASGN) is misleading — these are IT services businesses with fundamentally different economics (lower margins, higher labor costs, lower growth). The more relevant comparable is InterDigital (IDCC), a wireless patent licensing company trading at approximately 16-18x forward earnings with similar margin profiles. Adeia's 23x forward P/E represents a premium to InterDigital, partially justified by the semiconductor growth vector but demanding continued execution.

**The valuation is neither compelling nor egregious.** At $12-13 in mid-2025, the stock was clearly mispriced relative to FCF generation. At $33.63, after a 172% rally and with CEO departure uncertainty, the discount has been largely closed. The mean analyst target of $33 suggests the stock is at fair value; the high target of $40 implies 19% upside in a bull case.

**Why the market may not be wrong at current levels:** The 34x trailing P/E reflects lumpy 2025 reported earnings ($0.99 EPS) depressed by litigation costs and timing. Normalized on FY2026E ($1.44), the multiple compresses to 23x. For a business with 60% EBITDA margins, 5% FCF yield, and semiconductor-driven growth, 23x is reasonable. The specific reason this is not a BUY at current price: the stock is at its 52-week high, the CEO departure injects uncertainty into deal-making continuity, and consensus estimates are flat (no positive revision momentum in the last 30 days).

## Competitive Landscape

**Patent exclusivity:** IP licensing is inherently monopolistic — Adeia's patents cannot be replicated. The portfolio spans 13,750+ assets with expirations through 2045 and an 80% internally-generated composition.

**Proven enforcement:** The AMD resolution in 4 months from litigation filing demonstrates portfolio strength. Historical success across Disney, Optimum, and now AMD creates licensing urgency for unlicensed parties.

**Secular positioning:** Hybrid bonding is becoming the standard interconnect technology for advanced semiconductors. As chiplet architectures proliferate (driven by AI), Adeia's DBI® patents become more valuable. This is a widening moat.

**Diversification progress:** Non-Pay-TV recurring revenue growing 28% YoY indicates successful diversification away from the declining MVPD base. Three new customers signed in Q1 2026 (Microsoft plus others).

**Risk:** Competitive position depends on continued R&D investment and patent acquisition to refresh the portfolio as older patents expire. The $66M annual R&D spend plus tuck-in acquisitions must be maintained.

## Risk Assessment

**CEO Transition (High Severity, Near-Term):** Paul Davis announced departure for Q4 2026 on May 4. He has led the company through separation and all major recent deal activity. The successor's deal-making effectiveness is uncertain, and the 6-month transition period creates a window where potential licensees may delay negotiations. This is the primary reason for caution at current prices.

**Customer Concentration (Moderate Severity, Ongoing):** Top 5 customers at 55.7% of revenue. DISH litigation (expired license) represents both risk (if they prevail in court) and opportunity (if they settle). Loss of any top-3 customer would create a material revenue gap.

**Revenue Lumpiness (Moderate Severity, Structural):** Non-recurring revenue ($92M in 2025, $38.5M in Q1 2026) creates quarters that look exceptionally strong or weak depending on deal timing. This generates volatility around earnings that may not reflect underlying business health.

**Debt Maturity (Low Severity Currently, Rising by 2027):** $378M balloon due June 2028. At current FCF generation and cash position, the company can reduce this to ~$100-150M by maturity, making refinancing manageable. However, if FCF deteriorates or credit markets tighten significantly, refinancing risk increases.

**Pay-TV Secular Decline (Moderate Severity, Long-Term):** Cord-cutting continues at 3-5% annually. Recurring media revenue from MVPDs is declining. The offset (OTT, e-commerce, gaming) must continue scaling to maintain total revenue growth.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38-40, +13-19%):** AMD deal proves replicable — Intel or Nvidia/TSMC signs within 12 months, pushing semiconductor revenue above $150M. DISH settles quickly, adding $30-40M in recurring revenue. New CEO is hired from a strong IP/licensing background, removing uncertainty. Revenue approaches $500M target by 2027. Stock re-rates to 25x forward earnings on $1.80+ EPS = $45.

**Bear Case ($22-25, -26% to -34%):** CEO departure creates deal-making vacuum; DISH litigation drags on 2+ years. Semiconductor licensing proves one-off (AMD deal doesn't replicate). Pay-TV decline accelerates. Revenue stalls at $380-400M. Stock returns to 15-16x normalized earnings (~$22-25). Debt refinancing at unfavorable terms in 2028.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $25-27 range (17-20x FY2026E EPS), providing adequate margin of safety for CEO transition uncertainty
2. New CEO announced with strong IP licensing credentials, removing succession overhang
3. Second tier-1 logic semiconductor licensee signed (Intel, Nvidia/TSMC), validating AMD was not idiosyncratic

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. DISH/DIRECTV litigation results in adverse ruling that weakens patent enforceability broadly
2. Two consecutive quarters of declining recurring revenue (indicating Pay-TV decline overwhelming OTT growth)
3. Debt refinancing fails or requires materially higher rates (SOFR + 4%+ or above)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late July/early August 2026 for Q2 results). CEO successor announcement expected by Q3/Q4 2026.

## Conclusion

Adeia operates an exceptional business: asset-light, high-margin, with a widening moat in semiconductor IP driven by AI's demand for advanced packaging. The AMD deal validated semiconductor licensing against a tier-1 counterparty and could propel that segment from 25% to 40%+ of revenue over 3-5 years. Management has demonstrated excellent capital allocation (debt reduction, buybacks at $13.55).

However, the stock has already appreciated 172% in 12 months, pricing in much of this optionality. At 23x FY2026 earnings and 21x FCF with a CEO departure imminent, flat estimate revisions over the past 30 days, and options markets pricing elevated near-term stress, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The analyst consensus mean of $33 is essentially the current price. The CEO departure introduces 6-12 months of uncertainty around deal-making continuity (Adeia's core value creation activity), and at $33.63 the stock offers no margin of safety for execution risk during this transition period. A pullback to $25-27 or clarity on succession would shift the recommendation to BUY.
