**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Occidental Petroleum has reshaped its capital structure over the past two quarters. The January 2, 2026 sale of OxyChem to Berkshire Hathaway for an adjusted $9.5B (a $3.1B after-tax gain) funded a ~$6.7B debt paydown in Q1 2026, cutting principal debt from $20.4B at year-end 2025 to ~$13.8B, with another ~$0.4B retired post-quarter. Cash rose to $3.8B, the full $4.15B revolver is undrawn, and there are no remaining 2026 maturities. This deleveraging, and the prospect of restoring full investment-grade footing (S&P still rates the company BB+, one notch below IG), is the catalyst driving the stock's ~40% year-to-date gain and a wave of analyst upgrades, including Barclays moving to Overweight with a $72 target on May 26.

The trade-off is that OXY is now an undiversified, oil-weighted E&P with the counter-cyclical chemicals earnings stream removed and, after Iran-war price volatility, no incremental 2026 hedges beyond existing crude collars (100 Mbbl/d, $55 floor / ~$76 ceiling). Q1 2026 GAAP net income of $3.36B is misleading: roughly $3.1B was the one-time OxyChem gain, and continuing-operations income actually fell ~72% year-over-year to $236M (from $830M) on lower realized prices and a $339M collar derivative loss. The deleveraging story is the bull case; the underlying earnings power is commodity-dependent.

We rate OXY a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale anchored on the continuing deleveraging path toward management's ~$10B principal-debt target, beyond which excess cash shifts to net-debt reduction and opportunistic buybacks. At ~$58.57 against a current-year consensus EPS of $5.62 (~10.4x forward, after estimates rose +266.7% over 90 days on the oil-price recovery), the stock is not cheap on normalized earnings power and carries a structurally wider commodity-failure tail given de-hedging and the loss of OxyChem. The $8.3B Berkshire 8% preferred ($680M/year), retained Passaic/Diamond Alkali environmental liabilities, and an unresolved ~$2.3B Tronox tax contingency are claims and risks that cap common-holder upside. We see balanced risk/reward at current levels.

## Company Overview

Following the OxyChem divestiture, Occidental is a pure-play upstream oil & gas plus midstream/carbon-management company. It is one of the largest U.S. producers, with worldwide output of ~1,419 Mboed in FY2025 (~84% U.S.) and proved reserves of ~4.6 billion Boe, oil-weighted (2,162 MMbbl oil, 1,150 MMbbl NGL, 7,745 Bcf gas). The core engine is the Permian Basin (~800 Mboed), complemented by the DJ/Rockies, the offshore Gulf of America, and international positions in Oman (largest independent producer), Algeria, and Qatar/UAE via Dolphin and Al Hosn Gas.

The company reports two segments: Oil & Gas, and Midstream & Marketing (which now houses Oxy Low Carbon Ventures). Its differentiation rests on deep CO2-enhanced-oil-recovery expertise, which underpins both legacy EOR and the carbon-management strategy headlined by STRATOS, the world's largest direct air capture facility, designed for up to 500,000 tons CO2/year, with operations expected to begin in 2026 (initial 250kt from trains 1–2). Five CO2 sequestration hubs are under development across 310,000+ acres in Texas and Louisiana. Midstream includes a 40.8% economic interest in publicly traded Western Midstream (WES), Al Hosn sulfur, the DEL pipeline, and CO2 infrastructure.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025–Q1 2026 narrative is dominated by the balance-sheet transformation, against a backdrop of weaker commodity realizations.

**Earnings and realizations.** FY2025 total net sales were ~$19.8B (oil & gas $16.1B). Worldwide realized oil fell from ~$75/bbl in 2024 to ~$66/bbl in 2025, and domestic gas realizations remained weak, often 25–50% of NYMEX given Permian/Waha basis. Q1 2026 oil & gas segment income was $1.0B versus $1.7B a year earlier, hit by lower prices across all commodities and the crude-collar loss. Trailing GAAP EPS of $1.35 reflects this underlying decline, even as the headline GAAP figure was inflated by the OxyChem gain.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow before working capital was ~$8.8B, with FCF before working capital of ~$3.3B (down from $4.9B in 2024) on disciplined capex of ~$5.5B, the majority directed to the Permian. Q1 2026 operating cash flow from continuing operations was $1.4B (down from $2.0B), depressed by a $1.1B trade-receivables build tied to a March commodity-price spike, a working-capital timing effect rather than a structural deterioration.

**Balance sheet.** The ~$6.7B Q1 repayment using OxyChem proceeds incurred a $237M early-extinguishment loss. Near-term maturities are negligible: only $48M (2027) and $14M (2028), with $13.3B thereafter. Substantially all debt is fixed-rate, insulating the company from the rate environment. Ratings stand at Baa3 (Moody's) / BBB (Fitch) / BB+ (S&P), and the deleveraging is explicitly aimed at securing and defending IG status.

**Capital returns.** The common dividend rose ~8% from $0.24 to $0.26/quarter in Q1 2026. The $3.0B buyback authorization has $1.22B remaining, but repurchases are minimal while deleveraging takes priority. The dividend yield of ~1.8% is below the peer median of ~2.3%, reflecting the capital-return subordination to debt reduction and the Berkshire preferred.

**Earnings quality and surprise record.** OXY has beaten consensus EPS in each of the past four quarters, including a 77.5% beat in Q1 2026 ($1.06 actual vs. $0.60 estimate), though the Q1 beat was distorted by the OxyChem gain. The beat pattern lends some confidence to forward estimates, but the magnitude of recent estimate revisions is principally an oil-price recovery artifact rather than structural improvement, and should be read accordingly.

## Growth Analysis

OXY's near-term earnings trajectory is dominated by oil price, not volume or structural change. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose +21.4% over 30 days and +518.5% over 90 days; current-year estimates rose +26.1% / +266.7% over the same windows. The mechanism is a recovery in WTI/Brent realizations from a depressed base plus the conflict-driven supply premium, not a durable expansion in production capacity or cost structure. Consensus current-year EPS of $5.62 implies oil realizations meaningfully above a normalized mid-$60s base; if oil reverts toward $60 or lower, those estimates compress as fast as they rose.

Volume growth is modest. Production was ~1,428 Mboed in Q1 2026, broadly flat to FY2025's ~1,419 Mboed, with capex held disciplined. The Permian and DJ remain the growth and capital-allocation priority. The structural growth optionality lies in carbon management — STRATOS DAC commencing in 2026 and five CO2 sequestration hubs — but these are capital-intensive, depend on nascent markets and evolving regulation (DOE carbon-capture funding was targeted for cancellation then partially restored in 2026), and carry uncertain economics. The CEO pivot from a carbon-vision leader to a drilling-focused operator (discussed below) raises a question about the long-run commitment to the costliest carbon ambitions.

The cleaner growth driver over a 12-month horizon is the deleveraging-driven equity-value capture: as debt falls toward $10B, a larger share of enterprise value accrues to equity, and the improving rating lowers the cost of capital. This is a real, mechanical source of upside that is partly independent of oil, though only partly, since reaching the $10B target depends on FCF, which depends on oil.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$58.57, OXY trades at ~10.4x current-year consensus EPS of $5.62 and ~14.6x next-year EPS of $4.01. The forward multiple sits at the upper end of the independent E&P peer group: DVN at 12.8x, EOG at 13.7x, EQT at 10.4x trailing, against a peer median of ~13.7x. On current-year forward earnings, OXY looks reasonable to slightly cheap.

The complication is that the $5.62 current-year figure embeds elevated oil realizations. On a normalized mid-cycle oil base of ~$65–70 WTI, the next-year $4.01 estimate is the more defensible anchor, putting the stock at ~14.6x, in line with quality E&P peers, not at a discount. The trailing P/E of ~80x reflects depressed continuing-ops earnings and is not a useful metric here; the appropriate lens is forward and cycle-normalized, on which the stock is fairly valued rather than mispriced.

The deleveraging adds a layer the simple P/E misses. Reducing principal debt toward $10B transfers roughly $10B of value from creditors to equity over time and supports an IG re-rating that lowers the cost of capital. This is a legitimate reason the market could be underpricing the equity relative to a static earnings multiple. Set against it: the $8.3B Berkshire 8% preferred sits ahead of common and consumes ~$680M annually (~$509M through 9M 2025), and 83.9M Berkshire warrants struck at $59.59, right at the current price, represent dilution that activates as shares clear that level. These claims meaningfully cap the equity's share of any re-rating until the preferred is redeemed.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $55, mean $65.54, median $65, high $75 (Barclays). With the stock at $58.57, consensus implies ~11% upside to the median. The recent upgrade cycle (three banks turning positive in six trading days) reflects the higher-for-longer oil thesis plus the deleveraging story. We treat these targets as a reasonable central tendency rather than a floor, because OXY trades near a fair forward multiple on cycle-normalized earnings, not at a clear discount, and because the entire bull case is conditional on oil. We assign a Valuation score of 4.5: the multiple is fair, not asymmetric, and the wide commodity-failure tail (more severe now given de-hedging and the loss of OxyChem's counter-cyclical earnings) skews the distribution against the common holder at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

OXY competes with the largest U.S. independents (ConocoPhillips, EOG, Diamondback, Devon) and the integrated majors (Exxon, Chevron) in the Permian. Its differentiators are scale across the Permian and DJ, CO2-EOR mastery, and carbon-management optionality (DAC/CCUS) that peers largely lack. As an internationally exposed producer (Oman, Algeria, Qatar/UAE), OXY tends to underperform purely domestic peers such as EOG and Devon during conflict-driven episodes, when domestic-only producers capture the price premium without the regional volume and operational risk.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than improving. Production is roughly flat, capex is disciplined, and the Permian position is strong but not gaining share in a way the market hasn't priced. The carbon-management franchise is a genuine differentiator that could matter over a multi-year horizon, but its economics remain unproven and policy-dependent. The deleveraging improves OXY's cost-of-capital position relative to where it sat, which is a real competitive gain, but it restores rather than extends an advantage. We score Competitive Position 5.5: a quality asset base with a defensible Permian franchise and a unique carbon platform, offset by flat volumes, international price-capture drag, and an unproven carbon economics case.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity price leverage.** This is the dominant risk. As a now-undiversified, oil-weighted E&P, OXY's cash flow is highly sensitive to WTI/Brent, and the OxyChem sale removed the counter-cyclical chemicals stream. Management entered crude two-way collars but announced it will add no further hedges after Iran-war volatility hurt realized prices, a deliberate bet on higher-for-longer oil that leaves common holders fully exposed if oil rolls over. At sub-$60 WTI, FCF compresses quickly against capex, the preferred dividend, and the remaining debt, which would stall the deleveraging path.

**Berkshire preferred overhang.** The preferred sits ahead of common. Redemption becomes economically attractive as debt falls, but it is a large competing claim on capital that subordinates common returns.

**Environmental and legacy liabilities.** Total environmental remediation reserves are ~$1.86B across 151 sites, with up to $1.9B in additional reasonably-possible losses. The Diamond Alkali / Lower Passaic River Superfund site is the standout: a court assigned OXY's subsidiary ~85% of OU2/OU4 cleanup (EPA estimates ~$1.4B for OU2, ~$440M for OU4), driving a $925M charge in Q4 2024. OXY is appealing. OXY retained these legacy liabilities and signed a guaranty indemnifying Berkshire for pre-closing OxyChem liabilities, so the chemical-era tail did not leave with the asset.

**Tronox tax dispute.** An unresolved IRS dispute over Anadarko's $5.2B Tronox settlement deduction represents a ~$2.3B contingency; an adverse Tax Court ruling could require repayment of ~$1.4B in taxes plus ~$1.0B interest. A separate 2022 reorganization generating a $2.7B tax benefit is also under IRS review.

**Effective tax rate creep.** The continuing-ops effective tax rate rose to ~39% in Q1 2026 (from 29%) as OxyChem's domestic income departed, shifting the jurisdictional mix toward higher-taxed international income.

**Carbon-management execution.** STRATOS/DAC and CCUS depend on nascent markets, evolving regulation, and government funding, and are capital-intensive with uncertain economics.

Taken together, these risks are concentrated and partly binary, and are not fully priced into a stock trading at a fair forward multiple. We score Risk Factors 4.0.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 45.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 34.4%, leaving IV rich (a 10.6pp spread) and at the 68th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (45.0% near-term vs. 36.7% long-term), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with the oil-price binary and the broader Hormuz situation. Skew is call-heavy (-3.7% near-term) with a low put/call volume ratio of 0.40 and OI ratio of 0.43, so positioning is not bearishly tilted; if anything, options flow leans constructive. ATM IV compressed 9.7pp (54.7%→45.0%) over the prior two weeks as some event premium cleared. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float), so there is no squeeze dynamic. The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty driven by the commodity backdrop rather than a directional bearish view, which is consistent with our balanced stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$72–75).** Oil holds in the mid-$60s or higher on tightening global supply and disciplined U.S. shale. OXY completes its deleveraging toward $10B principal debt within 12–18 months, S&P restores full investment-grade status lowering the cost of capital, and the company redeems a portion of the Berkshire preferred, freeing ~$680M of annual cash flow for common returns. Carbon-management optionality (STRATOS commencing 2026) provides a longer-dated re-rating kicker. This matches the Barclays $72 target and the $75 Street high.

**Bear Case (~$42–45, roughly -25%).** Oil reverts toward or below $60. With no incremental hedges, FCF compresses against capex, the preferred dividend, and debt, stalling the deleveraging path and deferring the IG upgrade. An adverse Tronox tax ruling or escalation of the Passaic environmental exposure consumes cash that would otherwise reduce debt. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low of $38.80, where it traded as recently as December 2025.

**Base Case (~$65).** Oil stays firm enough — mid-$60s realizations — for OXY to continue deleveraging at a measured pace, defending its rating trajectory while paying the modest dividend. The equity re-rates gradually as debt falls and the Berkshire warrant overhang and preferred claim are slowly worked down. This is consistent with the consensus median of $65, implying ~11% upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, early August) and any movement in the S&P rating outlook. The June FOMC and the trajectory of oil prices are the most important external variables, given the de-hedged commodity exposure.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Oil realizations stabilize in the mid-$60s or higher AND OXY confirms principal debt has fallen below ~$12B, with management reaffirming the $10B target on schedule, turning the deleveraging re-rating from conditional to near-certain.
- S&P upgrades OXY to full investment grade, lowering the cost of capital and removing the rating overhang.
- Redemption of a meaningful tranche of the Berkshire preferred, freeing ~$680M/year and removing the largest cap on common-holder upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- WTI sustained below $60 with continuing-ops FCF turning negative against capex and the preferred dividend, stalling the deleveraging path.
- An adverse Tronox Tax Court ruling crystallizing the ~$2.3B liability, or a material escalation of the Passaic environmental exposure beyond current reserves.
- Reversal of the positive estimate-revision trend — current-year EPS estimates falling more than 20% on commodity weakness — which would confirm the recent revisions were a transient oil-price artifact.

## Conclusion

OXY has executed a credible, fast balance-sheet repair: principal debt cut by roughly a third in one quarter, an ~8% dividend hike, and a clear path toward $10B and full investment grade. That deleveraging catalyst is the substance behind the rally and the bullish analyst pivot, and it gives us a specific, monitorable growth thesis for holding over the next 12 months. The offsetting reality is that the company is now an undiversified, deliberately de-hedged oil producer whose entire upside case is conditional on oil staying above the mid-$60s, sitting beneath the Berkshire preferred claim, the warrant overhang at spot, a retained multi-billion-dollar environmental tail, and the tax contingency. At ~10.4x current-year and ~14.6x normalized next-year EPS, the stock is fairly valued rather than mispriced, with a structurally wider downside tail than before the OxyChem sale.

We rate OXY a HOLD (growth_hold), with the deleveraging path toward $10B principal debt and the associated IG re-rating as the catalyst that justifies holding. The risk/reward is balanced — ~11% upside to the consensus median against ~25% downside in a commodity-reversion scenario — and we would upgrade to BUY on confirmation that debt reduction is proceeding on schedule with oil firm, or downgrade to AVOID on sustained sub-$60 oil or crystallization of the Tronox/Passaic liabilities.
