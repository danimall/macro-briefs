**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Entergy is a nearly pure-play regulated electric utility serving roughly 3 million customers across Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, and Texas. The investment case is unusually clean for a utility: the company sits at the physical center of the Gulf South data-center buildout, giving it a rate-base growth pipeline that most US utilities, which face flat retail demand, cannot match. Q1 2026 industrial GWh sales rose 15% year over year, with Mississippi industrial volume up 91% on data-center load and Arkansas, Texas, and Louisiana each up double digits. Management frames roughly a 13% EPS CAGR through 2030 backed by a $67B capital plan, and the pipeline is anchored by named hyperscale counterparties (Meta in Louisiana, AWS in Mississippi).

The offsetting reality is that this growth is expensive to fund and largely priced into the stock. Debt-to-capital sits at 65.9% as of March 31, 2026, essentially at the credit-facility covenant ceiling, and the plan requires roughly $6.6B of new equity plus $3B of junior subordinated debentures through 2029. That funding mix dilutes the per-share compounding that the headline 13% EPS growth implies, and it does so into a higher-for-longer rate environment where the cost of the negative-free-cash-flow model is rising. At ~22x consensus next-year EPS ($5.08) and ~29x trailing, the stock carries a meaningful premium to the electric-utility peer median of ~17.4x, with a below-peer 2.25% dividend yield.

We rate ETR a HOLD (growth hold). The data-center load story is genuine, well-documented in the filings, and partly de-risked by customer pre-funding mechanisms (contributions-in-aid-of-construction, $294M of Q1 customer advances, $8.0B of contracted fixed-consideration revenue extending to 2048). But the combination of a stretched balance sheet, continuous dilution, concentrated single-counterparty exposure to Meta's AI capex, and rising regulatory/political friction leaves the risk/reward roughly balanced at the current price. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the low-$90s or on evidence that rate-base returns are outpacing dilution.

## Company Overview

Entergy operates a single reportable "Utility" segment through five state operating companies (Entergy Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, New Orleans, and Texas) plus System Energy Resources, which owns 90% of the Grand Gulf nuclear plant and sells power under the Unit Power Sales Agreement. The business is a regulated, cost-of-service model overseen by four state regulators (APSC, LPSC, MPSC, PUCT), the New Orleans City Council, and FERC. This structure delivers earnings stability and rate-based returns while capping upside and creating recovery-timing risk.

The company operates a large nuclear fleet (ANO 1 & 2, River Bend, Waterford 3, and Grand Gulf), all currently in the NRC's best oversight category, though Grand Gulf received a preliminary "white" finding on an emergency diesel generator in March 2026 that could move it to Column 2. Entergy simplified its portfolio by selling its Louisiana and New Orleans natural gas distribution businesses on July 1, 2025, making it a nearly pure-play electric utility. A stock split roughly doubled the share count to ~458M shares.

The defining feature of Entergy's situation is geographic. The Gulf South has become a premier destination for hyperscale data centers because of cheap land, water access, gas availability, and pro-development state governments. As the regulated monopoly owning the wires and generation in that region, Entergy captures rate-based returns on the enormous new generation and transmission required to serve that load. Meta and AWS are customers, not partners: Entergy provides regulated power and earns returns on the grid assets it builds; it does not own or develop the data centers themselves. A Google/Alphabet load relationship in Arkansas has been referenced in prior reporting but is not confirmed in the primary filings reviewed.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from ~$11.9B in 2024 to ~$13.0B in 2025, and net income rose from ~$1.47B to ~$1.86B. Q1 2026 total operating revenue was $3.19B, up 12% year over year, with net income attributable to Entergy of $384.9M (+7%). EPS was $0.84 basic / $0.83 diluted, essentially flat year over year because of the higher share count, an early illustration of the dilution drag that runs through this story.

The growth engine is industrial volume, which rose 15% year over year in Q1 2026 (total retail +4%, total sales +8%), driven by data-center load. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.03 beat consensus ($0.94), and management touted another major hyperscale agreement in Louisiana. The earnings-surprise record over the trailing four quarters is mixed: one beat and three narrow misses (the misses within 3.2%), which suggests consensus is calibrated close to reality rather than systematically conservative.

The central financial story is capital expenditure. Consolidated planned capex is $13.2B (2026), $16.8B (2027), $15.4B (2028), and $11.8B (2029), an enormous ramp dominated by generation. Q1 2026 construction expenditures alone were $2.25B, and construction work-in-progress grew to $7.7B. This scale of spending is the source of both the growth thesis and the balance-sheet strain.

Leverage is the pressure point. Debt-to-capital reached 65.9% at March 31, 2026, up from 64.3% at year-end 2025, sitting right at the 65% credit-facility reference ceiling (the covenant's defined calculation differs). Total long-term debt is ~$32.7B, and the company is issuing aggressively via multiple 2026 mortgage-bond issuances at 4.9%–5.75% coupons. Funding the plan requires roughly $6.6B of equity through 2029 (ATM plus forward sales, with ~$1.9B already contracted) plus $3B of junior subordinated debentures ($1.3B issued November 2025). This is a structurally negative-FCF model that is not self-funding.

Cash-flow quality has a favorable wrinkle: operating cash flow was $829M in Q1 2026 (up from $536M), helped by ~$294M of customer advance payments tied to large-customer agreements, a mechanism where new data-center customers pre-fund construction. One caution on earnings quality: interest and investment income spiked to $216M in Q1 2026 (vs. $33M), driven largely by decommissioning-trust rebalancing and carrying charges on retained nuclear PTC proceeds. This inflates the reported "other income" line and is not core recurring earnings.

The dividend was raised to $0.64/quarter, a shareholder-friendly but capital-competing use of cash at a time when free cash flow is deeply negative.

## Growth Analysis

Entergy's growth pipeline is among the most visible in the regulated-utility universe, and it is broadly confirmed rather than speculative. The Meta campus in Richland Parish, Louisiana has expanded to a 5 GW / $50B+ project, the largest in Meta's fleet. Entergy Louisiana signed a second Meta electric service agreement in March 2026 and filed for 5,278 MW of new combined-cycle gas generation (~$12.9B) plus a $1.4B 500kV line and battery storage, with an LPSC decision targeted for December 2026. AWS announced Mississippi campus expansions in April 2026, and Entergy Mississippi signed a new Hinds County agreement.

The demand signal is corroborated in the hard operating data, not just press releases: Mississippi industrial volume up 91%, Arkansas +20%, Texas +18%, Louisiana +8% in Q1 2026. This is consistent with the broader data-center load surge now visible across regulated utilities and power-generation infrastructure, a demand shift confirmed at the grid, transmission, and generation layers even as the chip layer has shown some order-timing softness.

Management's ~13% EPS CAGR through 2030 is the framing metric. The estimate-revision picture supports positive near-term momentum: current-quarter estimates were revised +1.9% over 30 days, next-quarter +6.0%, and next-year +2.7% over 90 days, while current-year sits flat. These are steady, modestly positive revisions, not the >30% velocity that would signal a mispriced earnings trough. Entergy is a durable mid-teens rate-base compounder rather than a cyclical snapback.

The critical qualifier for growth durability is the funding mix. A 13% earnings CAGR funded with ~$6.6B of equity issuance produces materially lower per-share growth. The Q1 pattern of 12% revenue growth and 7% net income growth translating to flat EPS is the mechanism in miniature. Whether the growth thesis delivers for shareholders depends on rate-base returns outpacing dilution, which is not guaranteed at ~66% leverage in a high-rate regime.

## Valuation Assessment

At $110.19, ETR trades at ~29x trailing EPS ($3.91) and ~22x consensus next-year EPS ($5.08). The electric-utility peer set is meaningfully cheaper: Exelon at 16.6x, PEG at 17.4x, WEC at 22.5x, EIX at 7.9x, with a peer median of ~17.4x. VST at 24.7x is the closest comparable, but Vistra is a merchant-heavy name with a different risk profile. Entergy's 2.25% dividend yield is below the ~3.4% peer median, further reducing the valuation cushion.

The premium is partly justified: Entergy's load-growth pipeline is genuinely superior to peers facing flat demand, and a higher rate-base growth rate warrants a higher multiple. The question is whether ~22x forward and a ~27% premium to the median already capture that superiority. On balance, we think it largely does. Analyst targets bracket the current price with a mean of $124.31 and median of $126.00 (range $91–$139), implying ~13% upside to the mean. On a stock trading above 20x forward with modest single-digit estimate revisions, we treat consensus targets as fair anchors rather than stale floors; the "targets are floors" dynamic applies to cheap, high-velocity names, which ETR is not.

Two structural caveats weigh on the valuation score. First, the negative-FCF, dilution-funded model means per-share value creation is slower than the headline EPS CAGR suggests, and higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of that model. Second, in this scoring framework a low or moderate multiple has repeatedly been a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal; here the issue is the opposite, in that the multiple is not cheap, and there is no independent catalyst identified that would argue the market is underpricing the stock. We score valuation 4.5: not a value trap, but a premium multiple with limited margin of safety and a specific dilution/leverage headwind that the growth narrative tends to obscure.

## Competitive Landscape

Entergy's competitive advantage is structural and geographic. It owns the regulated wires and generation across a region that has become one of the most attractive US destinations for hyperscale computing. As a monopoly utility, it earns rate-based returns on the new generation and transmission needed to serve that load, a rare and highly visible organic runway compared with most peers.

Several dynamics reinforce the position. Entergy entered an exclusivity agreement with a major turbine vendor for a minimum of 24 power-island equipment sets, securing scarce gas-turbine supply amid industry-wide shortages, a real competitive edge given multi-year equipment lead times. The customer pre-funding mechanism (contributions-in-aid-of-construction, minimum monthly bills, make-whole termination payments, $8.0B of contracted fixed revenue through 2048) de-risks the buildout relative to speculative capacity additions. Regulators are also constructing frameworks (LPSC "Lightning Initiative," Entergy's "Fair Share Plus" guidelines) designed to make large customers pay incremental costs and protect existing ratepayers, which improves the political sustainability of the growth.

The trajectory is improving on the demand side but faces countervailing regulatory tightening. Participation in MISO creates interconnection-queue and transmission-cost-allocation risk as load accelerates. We score competitive position 6.5: a strengthening structural position on load growth and turbine-supply security, tempered by the fact that the regulatory permission to convert that load into recoverable rate base is not a rubber stamp.

## Risk Assessment

**Data-center concentration and stranded-asset risk.** This is the defining risk. Entergy is committing $12.9B for 5,278 MW of new Louisiana generation largely to serve Meta. The company's own forward-looking disclosures now explicitly warn of "concentration of business and credit risk with a small number of customers in an industry based on emerging technologies, including AI," and the "potential for stranded assets" including "capital investments associated with unrealized customer growth expectations." Contractual protections (Meta parent guaranty, contributions-in-aid-of-construction, minimum bills, early-termination make-whole payments) are robust on paper, but the concentration in one AI-driven counterparty is material. If Meta scales back AI capex, a live question given the broader debate over AI-capex durability, the exposure is significant.

**Regulatory and recovery risk.** Multiple certifications are pending (Waterford 6, Westlake, Babel-Webre 500kV, Cypress Solar, Jefferson Power Station). The APSC set the Jefferson benchmark ~$90M below Entergy's cost estimate and mandated all-source competitive solicitations plus independent cost monitors, a clear signal that regulators are scrutinizing the buildout closely.

**Political/ratepayer backlash in Louisiana.** Governor Landry issued an executive order requiring data centers to fully fund their power needs; a PSC commissioner is challenging Entergy's confidentiality on Meta load forecasts; and NYT reporting scrutinized the Meta deal. This raises the risk of adverse conditions or delays.

**Storm exposure.** Winter Storm Fern (January 2026) caused ~$480M in restoration costs and spiked January gas purchases to $483M. Hurricane and severe-weather exposure is chronic, with recovery reliant on securitization and storm reserves.

**Leverage and refinancing.** At ~66% debt-to-capital in a sustained high-rate environment, with reliance on continuous equity issuance, the financing model is the financial pressure point. Nuclear operational risk (Grand Gulf "white" finding) and nuclear PTC monetization uncertainty round out the specific tail risks.

Several of these (storm exposure, regulatory lag, nuclear operations) are permanently priced features of the business. What tilts the risk score to 5.0 rather than higher is the combination of stranded-asset concentration in a single AI counterparty and elevated leverage that is not fully priced given the premium multiple. These are asymmetric downside risks the market has partly discounted but not fully.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 25.2% sits modestly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 20.2%, and the term structure is in backwardation (25.2% near-term vs. 20.4% at six months), indicating the market is pricing some imminent event stress, consistent with the upcoming earnings cadence and the December 2026 LPSC decision on the Meta generation filing. Put/call skew is balanced (2.6%) and put/call volume (0.36) and open interest (0.17) ratios are low, so positioning is not bearish. The signal is neutral-to-slightly-cautious: the market expects a ±5% near-term move but is not leaning short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Entergy converts its Gulf South load-growth pipeline into approved, recoverable rate base at the ~13% EPS CAGR management guides, funded on schedule without a credit downgrade. The LPSC approves the Meta generation filing in December 2026, hyperscale demand proves durable, and the customer pre-funding mechanism continues to de-risk the buildout. The stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range as rate-base growth outpaces dilution. Bull target ~$139 (~26% upside), roughly the top of the analyst range and consistent with sustained mid-teens rate-base growth on a premium multiple.

**Bear Case.** Meta or other hyperscalers pull back AI capex, triggering stranded-asset exposure on committed generation; Louisiana regulatory/political friction delays or reshapes the plan; and higher-for-longer rates plus the ~$6.6B equity issuance compress per-share growth well below the headline CAGR. A leverage-driven credit concern or a disallowance on the Jefferson-style cost benchmarks pressures the multiple back toward the peer median. Bear target ~$91 (~17% downside), the analyst low, reflecting a de-rating toward peer utility multiples on impaired growth confidence.

**Base Case.** The plan proceeds broadly on track with normal regulatory lag and friction, EPS compounds in the high single digits to low double digits on a per-share basis after dilution, and the stock trades in a range as the premium multiple is held in check by the funding overhang. Base target ~$124 (~13% upside), the analyst mean, implying ~22x the FY26 consensus of ~$5.08 with modest re-rating room.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth hold with a 12-month horizon tied to the December 2026 LPSC decision on the Meta generation filing and the pending certifications.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A constructive LPSC ruling on the 5,278 MW Louisiana generation filing that confirms recoverable rate base without punitive conditions, de-risking the largest single element of the plan.
- Evidence that per-share EPS growth is tracking toward the low double digits net of dilution (i.e., estimate-revision velocity turning meaningfully positive on the current-year and next-year lines, beyond the current +2.7%).
- A pullback toward the low-$90s, where the multiple compresses toward the peer set and the risk/reward turns asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material Meta/hyperscale capex pullback or contract renegotiation that surfaces stranded-asset exposure.
- A credit-rating action or a debt-to-capital breach beyond the ~65% covenant reference that forces a larger or worse-priced equity raise.
- Adverse Louisiana regulatory/political action (e.g., an LPSC denial or onerous cost-allocation conditions) that structurally impairs the growth plan.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings, and the December 2026 LPSC decision as the key dated catalyst.

## Conclusion

Entergy offers one of the clearest organic-growth stories in the regulated-utility sector: a $67B capital plan and ~13% EPS CAGR anchored by confirmed Gulf South data-center load and partly de-risked by customer pre-funding and $8.0B of contracted revenue through 2048. The competitive position (regulated monopoly plus secured turbine supply in the premier US data-center region) is genuinely improving.

The offset is that the growth is expensive to fund and largely priced. At ~22x forward and a ~27% premium to the peer median with a below-median yield, the stock leaves little margin of safety. The ~66% debt-to-capital ratio, the ~$6.6B of equity dilution through 2029, the concentrated Meta counterparty risk, and rising regulatory scrutiny are the specific offsets to the growth narrative. The trajectory of our scores over the past two quarters, with financial health, management alignment, and risk each stepping down as the leverage and dilution picture crystallized, reflects a maturing understanding that the per-share compounding will run below the headline EPS CAGR.

We recommend HOLD. The business is sound and the load-growth thesis is real, but at the current price the risk/reward is balanced. We would become a buyer on a pullback toward the low-$90s or on a constructive LPSC ruling that de-risks the Meta generation buildout without compressing returns.
