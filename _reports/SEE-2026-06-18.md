**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sealed Air is no longer a publicly traded security. Clayton, Dubilier & Rice (CD&R) completed its take-private acquisition on April 9, 2026, at $42.15 per share in cash, an enterprise value of approximately $10.3 billion. SEE has been delisted from the NYSE, and all public equity holders, including insiders, were cashed out at the deal price. There is no remaining public common stock to buy, hold, or short. The merger-arbitrage trade that existed in late 2025 and early 2026 (buying below $42.15 to capture the spread to close) is fully resolved. Any forward equity analysis is moot.

For context on the asset CD&R now owns, the underlying business is a cash-generative but structurally challenged packaging company. Sealed Air generated roughly $5.36 billion in 2025 net sales across two segments: Food (~67% of sales, anchored by the CRYOVAC and LIQUIBOX brands) and Protective (~33%, anchored by BUBBLE WRAP, AUTOBAG, and Instapak). Food is resilient, with segment adjusted EBITDA of $829 million in 2025 (23.1% margin), supported by formula-based pricing and the completed $160 million CTO2Grow cost program. Protective is in secular decline, with segment adjusted EBITDA falling from $362 million (2023) to $310 million (2025) on post-pandemic e-commerce normalization, customer churn, and industrial weakness. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA has been roughly flat at $1.1 billion across 2023–2025.

The headline reported earnings improvement in 2025 (net income of $505.5 million, EPS $3.43, versus $264.7 million in 2024) is largely an accounting artifact. The GAAP effective tax rate fell from 41.2% to 7.4% on roughly $170 million of reversed uncertain tax positions tied to the resolution of historical IRS audits. Operationally, the business was flat to slightly declining. Combined with sub-investment-grade credit (Moody's Ba1 / S&P BB+), debt-to-equity of 4.67x, and an Altman Z-Score of 1.45 in distress territory, this was a classic LBO candidate: a brand-rich, cash-flowing, levered business that materially underperformed its peer group on total shareholder return (five-year cumulative TSR of 92.83 versus 142.41 for peers). The recommendation is AVOID, driven entirely by the fact that the equity no longer exists, not by an active short thesis.

## Company Overview

Sealed Air operates two reportable segments serving global packaging end markets:

- **Food (~$3.59 billion 2025 sales, ~67% of total):** Sold largely direct to industrial food processors across fresh red meat, poultry, processed meats, seafood, fluids/liquids, and cheese. Flagship brands are CRYOVAC (barrier bags and films) and LIQUIBOX (liquids systems). Roughly 45% of Food sales use formula-based pricing that lags raw-material (resin) costs by approximately six months, providing margin smoothing.
- **Protective (~$1.77 billion 2025 sales, ~33% of total):** Protective transit packaging for e-commerce, industrial, consumer goods, and medical devices. Brands include BUBBLE WRAP, SEALED AIR, AUTOBAG, and Instapak. Approximately 60% is sold through distributors.

The company has ~16,100 employees, products sold in 119 countries, and 49% of sales outside the United States. No single customer accounts for more than 10% of sales. Petrochemical-based resins and paper pulp constitute roughly one-third of cost of goods sold, the primary input-cost exposure. Sealed Air retains its name and Charlotte, NC headquarters as a CD&R portfolio company; the legal acquisition vehicles were affiliates of CD&R ("Sword Purchaser, LLC" / "Sword Merger Sub, Inc.").

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has been in a slow multi-year decline: $5.64 billion (2022), $5.49 billion (2023), $5.39 billion (2024), $5.36 billion (2025). The trajectory is bifurcated by segment. Food segment adjusted EBITDA rose to $829 million in 2025 from $775 million in 2023, with modest volume softness offset by formula pricing and the CTO2Grow productivity program, which concluded in Q3 2025 delivering its full $160 million annualized savings target. Protective segment adjusted EBITDA fell to $310 million, with margins compressing from 18.4% to 17.6%.

Reported 2025 net earnings nearly doubled the prior year, but this was tax-driven, reflecting the roughly $170 million reversal of uncertain tax positions following the resolution of historical IRS audits. The brief associates the historical asbestos/Cryovac–W.R. Grace tax legacy with the IRS audit resolution. Underlying performance is better captured by adjusted EBITDA, which was essentially flat at $1.11–$1.13 billion across 2023–2025 (21.2% margin in 2025). Adjusted EPS rose modestly to $3.34.

Operating cash flow was $628 million in 2025, down from $728 million in 2024 on higher tax and incentive payments. Free cash flow was $458 million (8.6% margin). The quality of that cash flow warrants caution: the company leans heavily on receivables factoring, supply-chain financing (~$147 million in payables to participating banks), and in December 2025 converted its U.S. securitization to off-balance-sheet "true sale" treatment, routing roughly $151 million of receivables proceeds through operating cash flow that would otherwise sit on the balance sheet. This flatters both reported leverage and operating cash flow.

The balance sheet was the central standalone weakness. The Altman Z-Score of 1.45 sits in distress territory, driven by high leverage and negative working capital rather than operating losses. Debt-to-equity was 4.67x; total debt $4.01 billion, net debt $3.67 billion, against thin equity of $1.24 billion. Working capital was negative $182 million with a current ratio of 0.91 (down from 1.2x in 2024), largely a technical effect of reclassifying $625 million of Senior Secured Notes due October 2026 to current liabilities. The company was actively deleveraging (total debt down $394 million in 2025) and was covenant-compliant at a 2.95x leverage ratio versus a 4.50x maximum. Credit ratings were placed on negative watch/review following the CD&R announcement, as LBO structures typically add leverage.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is negative and has been for three consecutive years. Food provides stability but not growth; volume is roughly flat, with EBITDA gains coming from pricing and cost reduction rather than expansion. Protective faces structural headwinds: e-commerce volumes normalized after the pandemic surge, fulfillment customers have churned, and industrial demand has been weak. There is no organic growth engine that re-rates this business as a standalone equity. The forward growth case for CD&R is operational: optimize the Protective segment away from quarterly scrutiny, extract further cost savings, and pursue bolt-on M&A in a fragmenting, consolidating packaging industry. None of that accrues to a public shareholder, as none exists.

The two most recent reported quarters showed EPS beats (Q4 2025 +4.3%, Q3 2025 +23.5%), but those reflect tax tailwinds and cost-program execution rather than demand acceleration. Growth prospects for the equity are effectively zero given the equity's elimination.

## Valuation Assessment

The relevant valuation question was answered by the transaction itself. CD&R paid $42.15 per share in cash, which represents roughly 9x the ~$1.1 billion adjusted EBITDA run rate. That price embedded a control premium over an underperforming public stock. Public shareholders received that consideration in full at close.

For any investor evaluating SEE today, there is no public security to value. The merger-arbitrage spread (the gap between the pre-close trading price and the deal price) has collapsed to zero with deal consummation. There is no discounted-cash-flow exercise, peer-multiple comparison, or forward P/E that produces an actionable equity recommendation, because the shares no longer trade. Peer-multiple context is informative only for assessing whether CD&R paid a reasonable price, not for any forward equity decision.

## Competitive Landscape

Packaging is a fragmented, consolidating industry. Sealed Air's competitive position is mixed: durable brand equity (BUBBLE WRAP and CRYOVAC are category-defining), deep direct relationships with food processors, and integration of materials with automation provide genuine moats in Food. The Protective segment's position is eroding, with customer churn and pricing pressure indicating a weaker competitive standing in e-commerce fulfillment. The five-year cumulative TSR shortfall against peers quantifies the standalone underperformance that made the company a take-private candidate. CD&R cited deep customer and supplier relationships as the strategic rationale and intends to operate with a longer-term horizon. The competitive picture is stable-to-improving in Food and deteriorating in Protective, netting to a roughly average position that contributed to the public-market discount.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant fact overrides conventional risk analysis: there is no public equity to bear risk. For completeness, the standalone risk profile that CD&R now owns includes:

- **Leverage and refinancing:** The most material standalone risk. High debt, sub-investment-grade ratings, $372 million of variable-rate exposure, and reliance on receivables securitization, factoring, and supply-chain financing. Under private ownership, leverage will increase, pressuring credit ratings further.
- **Protective secular decline:** Continued volume churn, e-commerce normalization, and industrial softness.
- **Raw-material volatility:** Resin and pulp costs (~one-third of COGS) with a ~6-month repricing lag in Food.
- **Regulatory/ESG:** PFAS litigation and regulation, EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, Extended Producer Responsibility, plastic taxes, and anti-plastic sentiment, all of which threaten a plastics-heavy portfolio.
- **FX and trapped cash:** 49% of sales overseas; Argentina hyperinflation losses (~$15 million in 2025); ~$30 million of cash trapped in Russia/Ukraine (Russia ~2% of sales).
- **Quality-of-earnings:** Off-balance-sheet working-capital engineering flatters reported leverage and operating cash flow.

These risks are now borne by CD&R as the equity owner and by holders of Sealed Air's outstanding bonds, which remain part of a more highly levered, PE-owned capital structure with expected rating pressure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
There is no bull case for the public equity because it has been eliminated. For the prior arbitrage trade, the bull case was already realized: the deal closed at $42.15 cash, faster than the mid-2026 guidance (early HSR termination on December 23, 2025; shareholder approval February 25, 2026; close April 9, 2026), delivering a clean, low-risk spread capture. That trade is over.

### Bear Case
The bear case is equally moot for equity holders, who were cashed out at a fixed price regardless of business performance. The downside risk that remains belongs to creditors: a leveraged buyout layered onto an already sub-investment-grade balance sheet increases the probability of further rating downgrades, particularly if Protective's secular decline accelerates or resin costs spike against the six-month Food pricing lag.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** There is no path to re-entry in the common equity. SEE has been delisted and its shares retired. Re-entry would only become possible through a future re-IPO of Sealed Air by CD&R, which is not currently contemplated and would be a new security with its own capital structure. Credit investors evaluating Sealed Air's outstanding bonds should monitor leverage post-LBO, the October 2026 Senior Secured Notes maturity (~$625 million), and rating-agency actions.

**Timeline:** The headwind here is permanent for equity investors: the security no longer exists. No reassessment of SEE common stock is warranted because there is nothing to reassess.

## Conclusion

Sealed Air is no longer an investable public equity. CD&R closed its take-private acquisition, and SEE has been delisted from the NYSE. All public shareholders, including insiders, received cash consideration at the deal price. The merger-arbitrage opportunity that existed in late 2025 has fully resolved with no remaining spread.

The recommendation is **AVOID**, driven solely by the fact that there is no security to own. The underlying business CD&R now controls is a cash-generative but structurally challenged packaging company: a resilient Food segment offsetting a secularly declining Protective segment, flat consolidated adjusted EBITDA near $1.1 billion, a balance sheet carrying sub-investment-grade leverage, and reported 2025 earnings flattered by a one-time tax reversal rather than operational improvement. Those characteristics, combined with material TSR underperformance versus peers, explain why it became a leveraged-buyout target. For investors, the only forward-looking exposure now available is in Sealed Air's outstanding bonds, which sit within a more highly levered, privately owned capital structure facing continued rating pressure. The dimension scores below reflect the standalone business quality CD&R acquired and should not be read as an actionable equity view, since none exists.
