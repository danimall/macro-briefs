**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zoom is two stories layered on top of each other. The first is a mature, financially fortress-grade SaaS business: 77.9% gross margins, 25.1% GAAP operating margins, roughly 40% free-cash-flow margins, $7.7B of cash and marketable securities against zero debt, and a share count shrinking ~6% per year through a $4.7B cumulative buyback program. The second is a backdoor private-AI portfolio, anchored by a ~$51M investment in Anthropic now carried at $1,266.9M (roughly 4.5% of Zoom's $28B market cap), with Anthropic having filed confidentially for an IPO around June 1, 2026. The stock's ~31% appreciation over the trailing twelve months and its move near a 52-week high reflect the second story more than the first.

The operating business is the problem. Q1 FY2027 revenue grew 5.5% to $1,239.0M, described by management as "among our best growth rates in recent years," which underscores how far the company has decelerated from its pandemic peak. The enterprise net dollar expansion rate is 99%, meaning the installed base is net shrinking on a same-customer basis as seat downsells offset upsells. Online churn ticked up to 3.0%/month. FY27 guidance of $5.08–5.09B implies ~4–5% growth. The bull case rests entirely on AI re-accelerating expansion, a thesis that is currently unmonetized (management offers "certain AI features at no additional cost") and unproven at the revenue line.

I rate ZM a HOLD. The financial quality and capital-return discipline are real and protect per-share value, but I cannot underwrite the operating reacceleration that a BUY would require. Net dollar expansion below 100% is the single most disqualifying data point for a software long, and the estimate-revision momentum (+3.3% over 90 days for the current year) is positive but nowhere near the magnitude that would signal the market is systematically underestimating the business. Much of the Anthropic windfall is already embedded in the current price, which compresses the asymmetry. The most honest classification of this hold is uncertain: the bull and bear cases are genuinely balanced, and the largest swing factor (Anthropic's IPO mark and the DOJ investigations) sits outside the operating model.

## Company Overview

Zoom Communications (renamed from Zoom Video Communications in late 2024) operates a single-segment, subscription-based collaboration and communications platform, repositioning from a pandemic-era video-conferencing point solution toward an "AI-first, open work platform." The product portfolio spans two families:

- **Zoom Workplace** (collaboration): Meetings, Phone, Team Chat, Canvas, Whiteboard, Workvivo, all integrated with the built-in **AI Companion**.
- **Zoom Business Services** (customer-facing): Contact Center, Virtual Agent, Revenue Accelerator, Events, and Webinars.

The strategic differentiator is a "federated" AI architecture that routes tasks across multiple LLMs (OpenAI, Anthropic, NVIDIA, and Zoom's own models) while deliberately not training models on customer audio/video/chat content, a trust and privacy positioning. Founder Eric S. Yuan remains President & CEO.

Revenue mix in Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended April 30, 2026) was Enterprise 61% / Online 39%, with Americas 72%, EMEA 16%, APAC 12%. Customers contributing more than $100K in trailing-twelve-month revenue numbered 4,534, up 8.2% from 4,192 a year earlier. The company competes against larger, bundled rivals: Microsoft 365 (Teams), Google Workspace (Meet), Cisco Webex, RingCentral/8x8/Avaya in UCaaS, and Five9/Genesys/NICE in contact center.

## Financial Analysis

Zoom's financial profile is among the cleanest in software.

| Metric (Q1 FY2027, quarter ended Apr 30, 2026) | Value | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,239.0M | +5.5% |
| Gross margin | 77.9% | +160 bps |
| GAAP income from operations | $310.5M (25.1% margin) | +28.5% |
| Net income (GAAP) | $425.7M | +67% |
| Operating cash flow | $521.6M | +6.6% |
| Free cash flow | $500.5M (~40% margin) | +8% |

**Earnings quality requires normalization.** GAAP net income of $425.7M includes a $152.3M pre-tax strategic-investment gain (12.3% of revenue), attributable to private AI investments other than Anthropic (which was already marked to its February 2026 round). Stripping out these mark-to-market swings, underlying operating net income is closer to ~$300M. This matters for valuation: the reported trailing/forward P/E of ~15.6x flatters a business whose operating earnings power per share is meaningfully lower than the headline EPS of $6.18 implies. Investors should value the operating business and the investment portfolio separately rather than capitalizing a blended, gain-inflated EPS.

**Margin discipline is genuine.** Operating expenses were roughly flat YoY despite revenue growth: S&M fell 4.9% (aided by a non-recurring change extending deferred commission amortization from 3 to 5 years, worth ~$22M this quarter), G&A fell 5.9%, and R&D rose 11% as headcount shifted toward AI. Stock-based compensation declined to $179M from $202M a year prior, addressing a historical dilution concern.

**Balance sheet is pristine.** $7.7B of cash and marketable securities, zero debt, and ~$70M/quarter of interest income. The earnings power, cash position, and absence of leverage make conventional distress metrics inapplicable; refinancing and solvency risk are effectively nil.

**Capital allocation is disciplined.** The company repurchased $361.7M of stock in Q1 (4.17M shares at ~$86.75 average) and has authorized $4.7B cumulatively across four tranches. Diluted weighted-average share count fell to 294.4M from 312.8M a year earlier, a ~6% reduction. No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the thesis breaks down for a long position. The multi-year revenue trajectory shows a clear plateau: FY22 ~$4.10B → FY23 $4.39B → FY24 $4.53B → FY25 $4.67B → FY26 $4.87B, with FY27 guided to $5.08–5.09B (~4–5%). The annual revenue CAGR since FY22 is roughly 4%.

The structural mechanism behind the stall sits in the net dollar expansion rate of 99% (Enterprise). A SaaS business with NDER below 100% is shrinking on a same-customer basis: upsells are being more than offset by seat downsells and churn. All net growth then has to come from new-logo acquisition, which is itself slowing — large-customer count grew 8.2% YoY, decelerating from prior periods, while online churn rose to 3.0%/month from 2.8%. The 10-K attributes the soft expansion to "slower hiring and higher seat-count downsells," a demand picture tied to weak enterprise headcount growth.

The bull case for reacceleration is AI monetization: paid AI Companion users reportedly up ~184%, a new paid Custom AI Companion add-on, and a steady product cadence (AI Companion 3.0 on Zoom Phone, Expert Assist 3.0 and Virtual Agent 3.0 in Contact Center, live voice translation, the December 2025 $98M BrightHire acquisition). Engagement is real. Revenue contribution is not yet visible. Management explicitly offers core AI features at no additional cost and warns that if the expected benefits do not materialize, "the associated investment costs could further negatively impact our margins." Until AI converts engagement into incremental ARPU and lifts NDER back above 100%, the operating growth story is a low-single-digit grower.

Estimate revisions corroborate a modest, not inflecting, picture: current-year EPS revised +3.5% over 30 days and +3.3% over 90 days; next-year +2.6%/+3.1%. These are healthy but far below the >30% revision velocity that signals the market is systematically underestimating a fundamental change. The earnings track record is solid (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters; Q1 FY27 beat by 6.2%), which lends confidence to the consensus rather than upside surprise to it.

## Valuation Assessment

ZM trades at ~15.6x reported forward EPS, but the reported number is inflated by non-operating investment gains. A cleaner sum-of-the-parts:

- **Net cash and securities**: $7.7B, or roughly $29/share.
- **Anthropic stake**: $1,266.9M, or roughly $4.50/share — a stake that could re-rate materially higher at IPO (a subsequent round was reported near a ~$900B valuation), but which is non-operating and already partly reflected in the recent rally.
- **Operating business**: at $106 with ~$29/share of net cash and ~$4.50/share of Anthropic value, the market is paying roughly $72/share for the operating company. Against ~$5/share of normalized underlying operating EPS (stripping investment gains), that is roughly 14–15x for a ~5% grower with sub-100% net retention. Fair, not cheap, and not asymmetrically mispriced.

Consensus targets — mean $113.92, median $116.50, range $79–135 — imply ~7–10% upside to the median from current levels, with named Buys at Morgan Stanley ($105), Benchmark ($125), and Rosenblatt ($130). The analyst-target-as-floor logic that applies to cheap, accelerating stocks does not apply cleanly here: the multiple is already reasonable, but the growth rate is low and the recent re-rating was driven by the Anthropic event rather than operating reacceleration. The valuation does not offer the asymmetric upside that a BUY requires; the per-share value is supported on the downside by cash plus buyback plus the Anthropic floor, which is what makes this a HOLD rather than an AVOID.

## Competitive Landscape

Zoom is a best-of-breed product competing against bundled incumbents, and its competitive trajectory is the binding constraint on the long thesis. The 99% NDER is the clearest evidence that the moat is not expanding wallet share fast enough to offset attrition. The defining structural threat, called out explicitly in the 10-K, is Microsoft's ability to give Teams away inside an existing Microsoft 365 subscription enterprises already pay for. Google Meet inside Workspace poses the same dynamic. In adjacencies, Zoom is the challenger: Phone and Contact Center take on entrenched UCaaS and CCaaS vendors (RingCentral, Five9, NICE, Genesys).

Zoom's genuine edges are product reliability, brand, an open developer ecosystem, and a model-agnostic federated AI architecture that lets it adopt the best LLMs rather than betting on one. The strategy — expand beyond Meetings into Phone, Contact Center, and Workvivo, and convert the large free/online base into paid AI seats — is coherent. But the application-collaboration layer is the category where AI-enabled bundlers can compress standalone pricing, and Zoom has not yet demonstrated it can hold or grow share through that pressure. The competitive trajectory reads as stable-to-deteriorating, which caps the competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

- **Growth stall / commoditization by bundlers.** The defining risk. NDER at 99% plus ~5% top-line growth means the entire bull case depends on AI re-accelerating expansion against incumbents who can bundle to zero.
- **AI monetization unproven and currently free.** Engagement is rising but revenue contribution is nascent, and management has flagged margin risk if the investment does not pay off. Zoom also depends on third-party LLM providers (OpenAI, Anthropic) for core features.
- **DOJ investigations open.** The SEC investigation concluded July 30, 2025 with no enforcement action and the related $18M accrual was reversed — a clear positive. But two DOJ grand-jury investigations into Zoom's interactions with the Chinese government, data storage, and encryption claims remain open with no estimable loss range. This is a genuine, unquantified tail.
- **Strategic-investment earnings volatility.** Investment gains/losses now swing GAAP net income materially ($152.3M gain this quarter vs. a $13.6M loss a year prior). This injects equity-market beta and valuation noise into an otherwise steady cash-flow story, and an Anthropic IPO disappointment or a down-round in the broader portfolio would reverse part of the recent narrative.
- **China R&D concentration and geopolitics.** A significant portion of R&D resides in China; the 10-K flags potential government interference and customer-perception risk.
- **Dual-class control.** Class B carries 10 votes/share; Yuan controls ~37.7% of voting power on ~7.1% of equity, with insiders at ~45.9%. Public Class A holders have minimal governance influence.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing an imminent event. Near-term ATM IV of 67.0% sits well above 1-year realized volatility of 40.7% (a rich 26.3pp spread, 85th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (67.0% near-term vs. 49.4% at six months). The skew is call-heavy (-10.8%) with low put/call ratios (volume 0.43, OI 0.35) and short interest is minimal at 2.5% of float. This is consistent with positioning around the Anthropic IPO catalyst rather than downside hedging; the market is pricing a binary upside event, not stress. The implication for a fundamental investor is that near-term price action is likely to be dominated by Anthropic headlines rather than operating results, which argues against chasing the stock near a 52-week high into rich, event-driven volatility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($125–135).** AI Companion monetization converts the ~184% paid-user growth into measurable ARPU lift, pushing enterprise NDER back above 100% and reaccelerating revenue toward high-single digits. The Anthropic IPO marks the stake well above its current $1,266.9M carrying value (a reported subsequent round near ~$900B would imply multiples of the current mark), crystallizing $1.5B+ of non-operating value. Continued ~6% annual share shrink compounds per-share FCF. At ~18–20x a reaccelerating, gain-inclusive earnings stream plus a re-rated Anthropic stake, the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear Case ($79–85).** Microsoft's Teams bundling drives further seat downsells; NDER stays below 100% and revenue growth fades toward 2–3%. AI features remain free, pressuring margins as R&D and inference costs rise without offsetting revenue. The Anthropic IPO disappoints or the broader strategic portfolio takes a down-round, reversing the GAAP earnings tailwind and the valuation premium. DOJ investigations escalate. The stock de-rates to ~12x operating earnings plus cash, near the analyst low of $79 (~25% downside).

The bear-case downside (~20–25%) exceeds the ≤15% threshold that a high-conviction long would require, and the bull case depends on an operating reacceleration I cannot yet observe in the data plus an Anthropic mark already partly priced. That combination defines a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Anthropic IPO pricing (potentially within weeks of the ~June 1, 2026 confidential filing) and Q2 FY2027 earnings (late August 2026), where NDER and AI monetization disclosures will be the decisive data points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Enterprise net dollar expansion rate recovers above 100% in a reported quarter, confirming the installed base has stopped shrinking.
2. Management quantifies incremental AI revenue (paid Custom AI Companion / add-on ARPU) at a scale that lifts organic revenue growth guidance above ~7%.
3. A pullback toward the high-$80s/low-$90s that restores asymmetric upside, with the Anthropic stake providing a hard valuation floor while the operating business is priced near 12x.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Revenue growth decelerates below ~3% YoY with NDER falling further below 99%, indicating bundler-driven displacement is accelerating.
2. A DOJ enforcement action or a material adverse development in the China investigations.
3. A down-round or IPO disappointment in the strategic-investment portfolio that reverses GAAP earnings and removes the Anthropic valuation support.

## Conclusion

Zoom is a high-quality, cash-generative business trading at a reasonable multiple, run by a founder whose pay alignment (total comp ~$1.28M, negative compensation-actually-paid) is among the cleanest in large-cap software, and supported by a disciplined buyback that shrinks the share count ~6% per year. The issue is that the operating business grows ~5% with net dollar expansion below 100%, faces a structural bundling threat from Microsoft, and depends on an AI monetization thesis that remains free and unproven at the revenue line. The stock's recent strength has been driven materially by a non-operating Anthropic stake heading toward IPO, value that is now largely reflected at $106 near the 52-week high.

The result is a balanced setup with no asymmetric edge: per-share value is well-supported on the downside by cash, buyback, and the Anthropic floor, but the upside requires an operating reacceleration I cannot underwrite from current data and an Anthropic re-rating that is partly priced. I rate ZM a HOLD (uncertain), and would become more constructive on confirmed NDER recovery above 100%, quantified AI revenue, or a pullback that restores margin of safety in the operating business.
