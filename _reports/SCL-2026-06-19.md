**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Stepan is a B2B merchant producer of intermediate and specialty chemicals (surfactants 71% of 2025 sales, polymers 25%, and specialty products 4%) with a five-decade record of dividend increases and a meaningful family ownership legacy. The franchise is high-quality on a through-cycle basis, but its current condition is not. Stepan is in the trough of a multi-year profitability collapse that took net income from $147M in 2022 to roughly $40-50M in 2023-2025, with gross margin compressing from 15.4% to 11.6% and free cash flow falling to $25.4M (1.1% of sales) on a heavy capex cycle. The most recent quarter (Q1 2026) was a GAAP net loss of $41.4M, and even excluding a $65.4M restructuring charge, adjusted EPS nearly halved year-over-year to $0.45 and adjusted EBITDA fell 14%. This reflects underlying deterioration rather than one-time noise.

The bull case rests entirely on a turnaround: Project Catalyst, announced February 2026, targets roughly $100M of pre-tax savings over two years, paired with a ramp of the new Pasadena, TX alkoxylation facility that depressed 2025 margins. Both are plausible but unproven, and a near-complete new C-suite (CEO, CFO, GC, and CHRO all installed within the past 18 months) is running the playbook. Consensus is cutting estimates aggressively: current-quarter EPS estimates fell 42.7% over 90 days, current-year 27.9%, and the four-quarter earnings record shows three misses including a 105% miss in Q4 2025.

At $53.94, the stock trades at roughly 21x the current-year consensus EPS of $2.54 and ~14x the next-year $3.92 estimate, but those forward numbers are themselves falling, and the next-year figure embeds full Project Catalyst delivery that has not occurred. Against specialty-chemical peers at a median 21.3x trailing P/E with positive earnings, Stepan offers no identifiable mispricing the market is missing: a structurally pressured core franchise (1,4-dioxane and ethylene oxide regulation directly threaten its largest surfactant lines), weak European polymers, thin FCF, grey-zone balance-sheet metrics, and zero insider buying despite a ~50% decline from 2022 highs. We rate the stock AVOID.

## Company Overview

Stepan Company, founded 1959 and headquartered in Northfield, Illinois, manufactures intermediate and specialty chemicals sold business-to-business across consumer, industrial, agricultural, oilfield, construction, and pharmaceutical end markets. The company operates ~22.9M shares for a market capitalization near $1.2B and employs roughly 2,328 people across manufacturing sites in the US, UK, France, Colombia, Brazil, Mexico, Singapore, Germany, the Netherlands, Poland, and China. Approximately 44% of sales are international.

The three reporting segments:

- **Surfactants (~71% of 2025 sales, ~$1.67B):** Cleaning, wetting, and foaming agents used in laundry detergents, dish soaps, shampoos, body washes, fabric softeners, agricultural emulsifiers, and oilfield applications. Stepan is one of the world's leading *merchant* (non-captive) surfactant producers.
- **Polymers (~25%, ~$584M):** Polyurethane polyols for rigid-foam thermal insulation and CASE applications, polyester resins, and phthalic anhydride (PA).
- **Specialty Products (~4%, ~$82M):** Food/flavor/nutritional/pharmaceutical chemicals, including medium-chain triglycerides (MCT).

The competitive model relies on product performance, technical service, custom formulation, and capacity rather than commodity price alone, though Stepan competes against large global producers and faces a persistent "make-vs-buy" threat from large customers capable of insourcing.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature of Stepan's financials is a sustained decline from the 2021-22 destocking-driven peak, now overlaid with restructuring losses.

**Revenue and earnings.** Sales peaked at $2.77B in 2022, fell to $2.33B (2023) and $2.18B (2024), and recovered modestly to $2.33B in 2025 (+7%). That recovery was almost entirely raw-material pass-through pricing and mix, with surfactant *volume falling 2%*. Reported FY2025 net income of $46.9M ($2.05 diluted EPS) was flattered by $15.9M of one-time asset-sale gains (Philippines and Lake Providence, LA divestitures), partly offset by a $6.2M Mexico goodwill impairment. Adjusted net income declined to $41.7M ($1.82) from $50.5M ($2.20) the prior year.

**Margins.** Operating margin was 3.4%. The core Surfactant segment's operating income fell 21% in 2025, with North American gross profit down 20% (-$20M) on Pasadena startup costs, higher oleochemical raw materials, and a Millsdale environmental reserve. Polymers operating income rose 7% to $43.3M and Specialty rose 23% to $25.6M, but they are too small to offset the surfactant decline.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $147.9M against capex of $122.5M. The heavy capex cycle reflects the Pasadena build; gross PP&E grew from $1.07B (2022) to $1.22B (2025). Management has guided 2026 capex down to $105-115M, a deliberate liquidity lever.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $132.7M; total debt $626.7M; net debt $494M, or 28% of net-debt-plus-equity. Equity is $1,244M. Covenant headroom is adequate (net leverage cap 3.50x; minimum net worth $750M vs. $1,244M equity). The Altman Z-score sits at 2.25 (the grey zone), reflecting thin EBIT relative to assets. New debt is being issued at materially higher cost: $75M of 6.17% senior notes in May 2025 against legacy notes at 2.3-3.95%, and 2025 net interest expense rose 56% as Pasadena capitalized interest fell away.

**Q1 2026.** A GAAP net loss of $(41.4M), $(1.81)/share, driven by a $65.4M pretax Project Catalyst restructuring charge ($62.4M of asset impairments: Fieldsboro NJ $43M, Millsdale IL $14.3M, Stalybridge UK $5.1M). Revenue rose 2%, but that was entirely FX; consolidated volume fell 3%. Net debt rose to $510.9M on additional revolver borrowing.

FCF quality is the binding constraint. Despite ~$208M of EBITDA, only $25M reached free cash flow in 2025, and 2026 carries restructuring cash outflows, dividend commitments (Dividend King status to protect), higher-cost debt service, and no buybacks. The $30M Millsdale-area land sale (agreed April 2026, closing 2H26) and the reduced capex guide are the visible flexibility levers.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth in the trailing data: the 2025 revenue increase was price and FX, not volume, and Q1 2026 volume fell 3%. Forward growth is entirely a function of two execution-dependent items.

**Project Catalyst.** The ~$100M pre-tax savings target over two years is the central growth lever. First steps are concrete: closing the Fieldsboro, NJ site and decommissioning select assets at Millsdale and Stalybridge in 1H26. Full-year 2026 restructuring expense is expected at $70-80M, of which $65.4M was recognized in Q1. If delivered, this would meaningfully rebuild EBITDA off a depressed base. The 10-K added a *new* risk factor explicitly flagging the possibility of falling short, a tell from management itself.

**Pasadena ramp.** The new specialty alkoxylation facility started up April 2025. It was the single largest drag on 2025 North American surfactant margins (startup costs, lost capitalized interest), and it is the same asset that must now ramp utilization to drive future specialty-surfactant growth and to make the ~$60M US Federal NOL / ~$32M net DTAs realizable. This is the swing factor on both margins and a potential future valuation-allowance charge.

**Segment-level dynamics.** Genuinely positive: North American phthalic anhydride volume more than doubled in 2025 on a competitor's market exit plus the non-recurrence of 2024 Millsdale operational issues. Agricultural (crop productivity) and oilfield demand is growing, and management is steering toward these higher-value applications. Specialty Products is the fastest-growing segment (MCT-driven), though only 4% of sales. Offsetting these: weak consumer/commodity laundry demand and a deteriorating European polymers business (Q1 2026 European polymer gross profit -49% on soft construction demand and competitive pressure), with $47.8M of European polymers goodwill flagged as an impairment watch item.

The most important forward signal is consensus direction, and it is clearly negative. EPS estimates fell 42.7% (current quarter), 27.9% (current year), and 15.6% (next year) over the past 90 days, with the declines accelerating into the 30-day window for the near-term quarters. This is the opposite of the positive estimate-revision velocity that would support a constructive growth view. The earnings track record reinforces low visibility: three misses in the last four quarters, including a 105% miss in Q4 2025 (adjusted loss of $(0.02) vs. $0.35 consensus).

## Valuation Assessment

At $53.94 the stock screens as a value trap rather than a mispriced opportunity. Trailing EPS is negative on a reported basis after the Q1 restructuring charge, so trailing P/E is meaningless. On forward consensus, the stock trades at roughly 21x the current-year estimate of $2.54 and ~14x the next-year $3.92 estimate. Two problems with treating the next-year figure as cheap: first, that estimate is itself falling (down 15.6% over 90 days); second, it embeds full Project Catalyst delivery and a Pasadena ramp that have not happened.

Against the specialty-chemicals peer set, there is no discount to point to. Peer median trailing P/E is 21.3x with positive earnings (IOSP 18.8x, MTX 14.9x, AVNT 21.3x). Stepan's forward multiple of ~21x current-year is in line to slightly rich versus peers that are actually earning money, and its dividend yield of 2.9% is above the 1.5% peer median but is supported by only $25M of trailing FCF, a coverage profile that is adequate today but vulnerable if the turnaround stalls.

The inflection-stock framework does not apply here, and applying it would be the central error. That framework rewards rising estimates plus accelerating revenue. Stepan has the opposite: estimates are falling sharply and volume is contracting. A high valuation score requires a specific reason the market is wrong. The identifiable catalysts (Project Catalyst, Pasadena) are real but two-sided and already partly reflected in the falling next-year estimate; the structural overhangs (1,4-dioxane, ethylene oxide, European polymers) argue the market's caution is warranted, not excessive. The lone sell-side price target of $75 implies ~39% upside, but it sits against a single covering analyst and assumes the turnaround executes; it is a normalized-earnings target, not a floor on a stock with falling estimates.

A cheap-looking forward multiple on falling estimates, combined with a 73-point five-year TSR underperformance versus peers, is a value-trap signature rather than an entry signal.

## Competitive Landscape

Stepan's competitive position is stable-to-eroding rather than improving. The surfactant franchise retains genuine scale and technical-service advantages as a leading merchant producer, but it faces large global competitors and the persistent make-vs-buy threat from large customers. North American surfactant operating income fell 36% in Q1 2026; share and pricing pressure are evident, particularly Latin American margins hit by Mexican competition and Asian results hurt by the Philippines divestiture and an export-permit delay.

The polymers picture is mixed: a clear North American win (PA volume doubling on a competitor exit) against a clearly deteriorating European business (gross profit -49% in Q1 2026, goodwill on impairment watch). Specialty Products is small but growing.

Two structural threats are specific to Stepan's chemistry rather than generic sector risk. 1,4-dioxane is a carcinogenic byproduct of Stepan's ethoxylated surfactants and ether sulfates; New York caps (1-2 ppm), proposed California rules, the EPA's TSCA "unreasonable risk" finding now entering risk-management, and an expected EU limit could structurally reduce demand for a core, higher-margin product line and accelerate customer reformulation toward alternatives. Ethylene oxide regulation (2024 fenceline-monitoring rule, proposed state restrictions) directly affects Winder GA, Millsdale IL, and Pasadena TX. These are not cyclical headwinds that reverse with the economy; they pressure the durability of the franchise's most important products.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution risk on the turnaround.** The entire bull case depends on delivering Project Catalyst savings and ramping Pasadena, executed by a leadership team assembled within the last 18 months. The newly added 10-K risk factor on Catalyst is management acknowledging the delivery is uncertain.

**Regulatory risk to the core franchise.** 1,4-dioxane and ethylene oxide both threaten Stepan's largest, highest-margin surfactant lines (structural, ongoing, and chemistry-specific).

**Environmental/legal.** Estimated liability range of $19.8M-$46.4M, accrued at the *low end* ($19.8M). Active obligations at Maywood NJ (chemical + radiological), Wilmington MA (uncapped 5%-of-cost), D'Imperio NJ, and an unresolved March 2024 EPA Notice/Finding of Violation on air regulations at Millsdale IL.

**Financial flexibility.** Thin FCF, grey-zone Z-score (2.25), rising cost of debt (6.17% new notes), and 56% higher net interest expense in 2025 leave less cushion than the 28% net-debt-to-cap headline implies.

**NOL realizability.** ~$60M Federal NOL / ~$32M net DTAs require sufficient future US income; insufficiency forces a valuation allowance and an earnings hit, a risk directly tied to whether Pasadena ramps.

**Macro.** Stepan sits in the Materials sector and Specialty Chemicals industry, both of which face raw-material (petroleum/oleochemical) volatility, soft consumer-products demand, weak European construction, and meaningful FX translation exposure. These are cyclical headwinds the market broadly prices, but they currently run against the company.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 42.9% versus 37.8% one-year historical volatility (rich by ~5 points), with a put/call skew of 4.1% indicating elevated put demand, and the term structure is in backwardation (42.9% near-term vs. 38.5% longer-dated), consistent with the next earnings print and restructuring milestones. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.5%. (The reported jump in ATM IV to 634% in the options-delta feed is a thin-options data artifact and is not informative.) Short interest is low at 2.9% of float (3.2 days to cover), so the bearish setup is uncrowded, with no squeeze pressure supporting the stock. The options signal corroborates, rather than challenges, the cautious fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Stepan is a high-quality, family-influenced specialty franchise at a cyclical and self-inflicted earnings trough. Project Catalyst delivers its savings target, Pasadena ramps to profitability and restores North American surfactant margins, the doubled North American PA volume holds, the ag/oilfield mix shift continues, capex falls to guide, and the land sale plus lower capex rebuild FCF. Adjusted EBITDA grows in 2026 as management guides, next-year EPS of ~$3.92 proves achievable, and the stock re-rates toward the $75 analyst target. Bull case ~$72.

**Bear Case.** Catalyst savings arrive slower and smaller than targeted, Pasadena utilization disappoints and forces an NOL valuation allowance, European polymers goodwill is impaired, 1,4-dioxane/EtO regulation accelerates customer reformulation away from core surfactants, estimates keep falling, and FCF stays thin while higher-cost debt compounds. The dividend is maintained but at the cost of any buyback or deleveraging. The stock revisits the 52-week low. Bear case ~$42, roughly 22% below current.

The base case sits closer to the bear end of this range given the trajectory of estimates and the absence of any insider conviction. Base case ~$50, modestly below current price.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated event is the next quarterly earnings report (Q2 2026, expected late July 2026), where the market will look for early evidence of Project Catalyst savings flowing through adjusted EBITDA and Pasadena ramp progress. Given the miss record and estimates falling 11-12% in the last 30 days alone, a miss or weak guidance is plausible. This is a "may disappoint" setup rather than a hard, asymmetric catalyst like a debt wall or binding covenant breach.

**Timing window.** The next earnings print is roughly five to six weeks out, with the implied move at ±9.5%. Beyond that, Catalyst milestones are spread across 1H-2H 2026, so the bearish thesis would require carrying the position through multiple quarters of uncertain delivery.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Uncrowded short interest means limited squeeze risk but also no informed-short consensus; the bearish view is not widely shared, which cuts both ways.

**Borrow and options signal.** The options market confirms near-term caution, which supports a bearish lean but also raises the cost of expressing it via puts. Borrow is unlikely to be expensive at 2.9% SI.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$42 (-22%) against an upside risk to ~$72 (+33%) if the turnaround surprises positively or the single $75 target proves directional. That is worse than 1:1 reward:risk to the downside, and the stock pays a 2.9% dividend that the short pays away. The asymmetry favors avoiding ownership, not actively shorting.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** This is a mediocre-to-troughing business mid-restructuring, not an actively collapsing one. There is no hard, dated catalyst with asymmetric downside, the reward:risk is below 2:1, and the dividend plus turnaround optionality create real upside-surprise risk. Avoid owning it; do not short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit the stock constructively on concrete evidence the turnaround is converting to earnings, specifically: (1) two consecutive quarters of *positive* adjusted EBITDA growth with management quantifying realized Project Catalyst savings tracking toward target; (2) a stabilization or upturn in consensus EPS revisions (the current 90-day declines reversing to flat-or-rising); (3) evidence of Pasadena utilization ramping with North American surfactant operating income recovering toward 2024 levels; or (4) a valuation reset that compresses the forward multiple to a clear discount to the ~21x peer median (roughly below $42 on the current-year estimate) accompanied by clustered open-market insider buying that is currently absent.

**Timeline.** The headwinds (regulatory pressure on core surfactants, weak European polymers, restructuring execution, thin FCF) are likely to persist through at least 2026. The earliest plausible reassessment point is the Q2 2026 print in late July, with the more decisive test being whether full-year 2026 EBITDA growth materializes as guided. Until estimate revisions stop falling and Catalyst savings show up in adjusted results, the burden of proof rests with the company.

## Conclusion

Stepan is a good business going through a bad stretch that is partly cyclical and partly self-inflicted. The franchise quality, family alignment, Dividend King status, and the real North American PA tailwind are not in dispute. What is in dispute is the path back to earnings power, and the current evidence does not support paying ~21x falling forward earnings for an unproven, execution-dependent turnaround run by a brand-new management team, against structural regulatory pressure on the core surfactant chemistry, weak European polymers, thin free cash flow, a grey-zone balance sheet, and a complete absence of insider conviction buying despite a ~50% decline from the 2022 highs.

Estimate revisions are the most reliable forward signal available here, and they are pointing sharply down. That alone argues against ownership; combined with the valuation offering no identifiable mispricing versus profitable peers, the conclusion is clear. We rate Stepan AVOID and would require demonstrable proof (realized Catalyst savings flowing to EBITDA and stabilizing estimates) before reconsidering.
