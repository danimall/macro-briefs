**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BorgWarner is a diversified Tier 1 automotive propulsion supplier whose reported earnings have been badly distorted by two consecutive years of large, non-cash EV-segment impairments. The naive screen (net income down 71% from $944M in 2022 to $277M in 2025, GAAP diluted continuing EPS of $1.28, a 44x trailing P/E, and an Altman Z-score in the "grey zone" at 2.27) materially understates the underlying business. Roughly $1.25B of cumulative 2024-2025 impairments are non-cash write-offs of overpaid EV acquisitions (PowerDrive goodwill fully eliminated; Battery & Charging goodwill written down to $143M). On a segment-adjusted basis, operating income rose year-over-year to $1,798M from $1,696M, free cash flow was robust at $1,179M (8.2% margin), and the Q1 2026 print showed operating margin jump to 9.5% from 6.7%, diluted EPS rise to $1.16 from $0.72, and all four segments improve sequentially.

The investment debate centers on three questions. First, whether the Q1 margin improvement is durable or a one-quarter artifact of the charging-business exit and restructuring. Second, whether the new industrial/data-center power-generation line, guided to ~$300M of revenue by 2027 via a turbine-generator supply agreement, scales into a non-auto-cyclical growth vector. Third, whether the EV-loss segments can reach profitability before secular ICE decline erodes the cash-generating "Foundational" book (Drivetrain at 18.4% margins, Turbos & Thermal at 15.2%). These are real positives, but the stock has already discounted much of the good news.

The shares have appreciated 122% off the 52-week low of $32.24 to $74.85, with most of the move concentrated since the February 2026 earnings catalyst. Forward consensus EPS is $5.20 (current year) and $5.86 (next year), placing the stock at ~14.4x forward, in line with peers and not cheap. Estimate revisions are essentially flat (current-year +0.0% over 90 days; current-quarter -2.4% over 90 days), so there is no positive earnings-momentum signal driving further re-rating. Combined with a broad-based cluster of insider selling into the rally with zero open-market buying, the risk/reward after the run is balanced. We rate BWA a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale anchored on the durability of the margin recovery and the industrial-power optionality.

## Company Overview

BorgWarner (Delaware-incorporated, 1987; ~37,500 employees; HQ Auburn Hills, MI) sells propulsion components to nearly every major OEM globally. Roughly 84% of sales are outside the U.S. and ~21% from China. The business runs across four segments (FY2025 sales / segment-adjusted operating margin):

- **Turbos & Thermal Technologies** — $5,772M, 15.2% margin. Turbochargers, emissions and thermal systems. Light-vehicle turbos alone are ~21% of company sales (the only product line over 10%). The primary profit engine.
- **Drivetrain & Morse Systems** — $5,654M, 18.4% margin. Transmission components, chains, transfer cases, AWD/4WD. The highest-margin segment and second profit pillar.
- **PowerDrive Systems** — $2,347M, (3.5)% margin. EV power electronics (inverters, eMotors, integrated drive modules). Growing fast (+21% in 2025) but structurally loss-making.
- **Battery Energy Systems** (renamed in Q1 2026 after the charging exit) — $590M, (6.6)% margin. Declining and loss-making.

Management frames the portfolio as **Foundational** (ICE + hybrid; ~82% of revenue, $11.7B) versus **eProducts** (EV-related; ~18%, $2.6B). The strategy: Foundational cash funds the EV transition while a "balanced portfolio" absorbs adoption volatility. Customer concentration is meaningful, with Volkswagen at 13%, Ford at 12%, and top 10 customers at 71% of sales. End markets are ~82% light vehicle, 10% commercial, 5% off-highway, 3% aftermarket.

## Financial Analysis

The central financial tension is flat-to-declining revenue alongside collapsing reported profitability, where the collapse is almost entirely non-cash impairment driven.

**Revenue:** $15.8B (2022) → $14.2B (2023) → $14.1B (2024) → $14.3B (2025). The 2022→2023 step-down reflects the PHINIA spin-off (Fuel Systems & Aftermarket divested July 2023). Post-spin, revenue has been flat at ~$14B as auto production held flat-to-down and EV adoption stalled.

**Reported earnings:** Net income attributable to BWA fell from $944M (2022) to $277M (2025); diluted continuing EPS dropped to $1.28 in 2025 from $1.63 in 2024. The driver is impairments, not operating decay. FY2025 impairments totaled $624M (a $423M Battery & Charging goodwill write-down, $410M of it in Q4, plus $174M PP&E and $27M intangibles); FY2024 was $646M including a $577M write-off that fully eliminated PowerDrive goodwill. Goodwill has fallen from $3.0B (2023) to $2.05B (2025). The impairments were non-deductible, pushing the 2025 effective tax rate to 36% from 21%. Non-comparable items totaled $(3.63)/share in 2025, meaning adjusted EPS ran several dollars above the GAAP figure.

**Cash flow is the standout positive.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,648M (up from $1,382M), capex fell to $469M (3.3% of sales, reflecting reduced eProduct investment), and FCF reached $1,179M, an 8.2% margin. The reported losses are not consuming cash, which is the single most important fact in distinguishing this from a genuinely distressed supplier.

**Balance sheet:** Current ratio 2.07, net unfunded pension just $36M, total long-term debt ~$3.9B (all fixed-rate, only $5M maturing through end-2026), $2.1-2.3B cash, $2.0B undrawn revolver, ~$4.1-4.3B total liquidity. Ratings are investment grade and stable (Baa1/BBB+/BBB+). The Altman Z of 2.27 is depressed by GAAP EBIT/assets screening near-zero due to impairments, not by leverage or liquidity stress. Net leverage is modest.

**Q1 2026 detail:** Sales $3,533M (+1% YoY), gross margin 19.2% (from 18.2%). All four segments improved sequentially. Battery Energy Systems loss narrowed to $(2)M / (2.0)% from $(22)M / (14.7)% after the charging exit; PowerDrive narrowed to $(36)M / (6.1)%. The portfolio pruning is visibly converting losses toward breakeven faster than the cautious full-year guide implies.

**Earnings track record:** Four consecutive beats (Q1'26 +4.9%, Q4'25 +12.7%, Q3'25 +5.8%, Q2'25 +10.6%), supporting confidence in the forward consensus and management's tendency toward conservatism.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed, the reason this dimension scores below the financial-health and conviction levels elsewhere.

The negative: the revenue base is flat-to-declining. Management guides 2026 sales to ~$14.0-14.3B, flat to down ex-FX, explicitly citing further Battery Energy Systems weakness. Foundational (ICE) products are in secular decline ($12.2B in 2023 → $11.7B in 2025) even as they fund the entire enterprise. Estimate revisions confirm the absence of an earnings-momentum tailwind: current-quarter EPS estimates are down 2.4% over 90 days, current-year flat (+0.0%), next-year essentially flat (+0.1%). There is no positive revision-velocity signal that would justify treating this as a re-rating candidate.

The positives are real but unproven at scale. The data-center/industrial power-generation pivot is the most important new growth thread: management guides to ~$300M of "power generation industrial solutions" revenue by 2027 via a turbine-generator supply agreement serving AI-data-center power demand. This leverages existing turbomachinery and power-electronics assets rather than requiring large M&A, and it directly addresses the "what replaces ICE cash flows" question with a non-auto-cyclical, presumably higher-margin revenue stream. The structure is specific: BorgWarner is the equipment supplier of turbine generators into the data-center market, not a hyperscaler partner in which BWA is the customer. The separate Generac-hyperscaler deal in early June is a different company and should not be conflated.

PowerDrive is growing +21% and the EV-segment losses are narrowing rapidly, so a path to a less dilutive (eventually accretive) eProducts book exists. New business awards in May 2026 (a variable-turbine-geometry turbocharger and an EGR cooler) confirm the Foundational franchise continues to take share. The realistic growth profile is low-single-digit revenue with margin-driven EPS expansion as EV losses fade, credible but already partly in the price.

## Valuation Assessment

At $74.85, BWA trades at 44.3x trailing GAAP EPS of $1.28, a meaningless figure given the impairment distortion, and at ~14.4x forward consensus EPS of $5.20 (current year) / ~12.8x next-year $5.86. The forward multiple is the relevant lens.

Against peers in Automotive Parts & Equipment, the picture is in-line, not cheap. Peer medians are P/E 14.5 and yield 1.9%: Autoliv 13.9x, Lear 14.5x, Gentex 14.4x, Dorman 20.7x, Dana negative. BWA's ~14.4x forward is right at the peer median P/E. BWA's 0.95% dividend yield is below the 1.9% peer median, so there is no absolute-yield support relative to the group. The case for a premium would rest on the data-center power optionality and the margin-recovery trajectory; the case against is the flat revenue base and the fact that the stock has already absorbed the impairment-clearing re-rating.

The stock sits essentially at the consensus mean target of $75.47 and median of $75.00, with a target range of $48 to $95. With estimate revisions flat, the consensus targets are not stale and do not function as floors here; there is no rising-estimate dynamic to pull targets higher. The prior 122% appreciation is not itself a reason for a low valuation score; what justifies the modest score is that current forward fundamentals (flat revenue, peer-median multiple, below-peer yield, flat revisions) leave limited asymmetric upside from today's price. The IEEPA tariff-refund recovery (SCOTUS invalidation Feb 20, 2026) is genuine optionality not in the numbers, but its size and timing are unknown. We score Valuation a 4.5, fairly valued to slightly full after the run, with the upside requiring the industrial-power story to scale on a multi-year horizon.

## Competitive Landscape

BorgWarner competes with Bosch, Denso, Garrett Motion, Hitachi, Magna Powertrain, Valeo, Schaeffler, and CATL, plus EV start-ups, holding ~5,000 patents. Its competitive levers are technology, application engineering, quality, price, and launch support.

The trajectory is stable-to-modestly-positive in the high-margin franchises and pressured in EV and China. The Drivetrain and Turbos segments are genuine cash machines and continue to win awards, distinguishing BWA from pure-play EV-component suppliers chasing scale at any cost. Management's response to EV-adoption volatility (pruning unprofitable charging lines, restructuring PowerDrive with ~$100M targeted savings, and leaning on Foundational profitability) is more disciplined than peers. The data-center turbine-generator move places BWA in the broader AI-power-demand thematic alongside GE Vernova and Caterpillar using existing competencies.

The headwinds keep this from scoring as an improving moat. China (21% of sales) faces domestic OEMs insourcing components and competing aggressively on price; noncontrolling-interest income already declined on weaker Chinese Foundational demand. OEM insourcing of e-motors and inverters threatens PowerDrive's addressable market. Foundational ICE is in structural secular decline. The net is a company defending strong positions in mature segments while fighting share erosion in growth segments, stable overall, hence a 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**EV-segment capital destruction:** PowerDrive and Battery Energy Systems have produced back-to-back years of large impairments, the Q4'25 write-down triggered by "dual sourcing actions by certain customers, pricing pressures from competitors and EV adoption delays in North America." Remaining Battery goodwill ($143M) is highly sensitive to further deterioration. Management guides 2026 sales down ex-FX citing continued Battery weakness.

**Tariff and trade exposure:** BWA imported ~$918M into the U.S. in 2025 (68% Mexico, 9% Canada, 6% South Korea). Tariffs added ~$108M to 2025 cost of sales; only ~$80M was recovered from customers (partial and lagged). USMCA renewal in 2026 is a live risk given the Mexico-heavy sourcing. The IEEPA-refund recovery is a potential cash benefit of uncertain magnitude and timing. The Q1'26 10-Q raised the commodity-volatility outlook for 2026 versus the more benign 10-K language.

**Customer concentration and OEM pricing power:** VW + Ford = 25% of sales, top 10 = 71%. Contracts are requirements-based with no minimum volumes; OEMs demand annual price-downs; UAW labor actions at Ford/GM/Stellantis flow straight to BWA volumes.

**Secular ICE decline:** The bull case requires EV/industrial businesses to reach profitability before the Foundational cash engine erodes.

**Governance signals:** A May 28, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02) flags an executive departure/election (filing detail inaccessible; appears tied to the Turbos & Thermal segment) worth monitoring. Separately, a broad-based cluster of open-market insider selling clustered in May 2026 into the rising stock (including a CEO Fadool sale of 29,000 shares on 5/13 and sales by multiple VPs, the CHRO, and a director), with no open-market insider buying observed. Most were modest relative to remaining holdings and aligned with post-earnings/RSU-vesting windows, but the breadth and CEO participation temper the insider signal.

These risks are largely known and partly priced, hence a mid-range 5.5 rather than a low score. The EV-segment impairments are arguably "clearing the decks," and the balance sheet provides ample buffer. The unquantified tariff/USMCA outcome and the China pressure are the items most capable of moving the stock against an otherwise improving operational read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90-95):** The Q1'26 margin improvement proves durable, EV-segment losses approach breakeven through 2026, and the data-center turbine-generator line scales toward the ~$300M 2027 guide at attractive industrial margins. FCF holds at ~$1.2B, funding continued buybacks (new $1B authorization, share count down from 235.5M in 2022 to 205.3M weighted in Q1'26) and the doubled dividend. The IEEPA tariff refund delivers a one-time cash benefit. Re-rating toward ~16x on rising forward EPS of ~$5.86+ supports the $95 analyst high.

**Bear Case ($48-52):** EV adoption delays deepen, triggering further Battery/PowerDrive impairments; USMCA renewal raises the tariff burden with incomplete customer recovery; China price competition and OEM insourcing erode the high-margin Foundational book faster than the data-center pivot can offset. Revenue declines accelerate, the margin recovery stalls, and the multiple de-rates toward the low end of the peer range and the $48 analyst low, roughly a 36% decline from current.

The base case sits closer to fair value: low-single-digit revenue, margin-driven EPS expansion to ~$5.86 next year, a peer-median multiple, and the stock range-bound around the consensus $75 target as the industrial-power story plays out over several years.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August 2026), which will test the durability of the Q1 margin improvement and provide an update on the data-center turbine-generator ramp and tariff-refund progress.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 operating margin sustains ≥9.5% with EV-segment losses narrowing to combined breakeven, confirming the recovery is structural rather than a charging-exit artifact.
- The data-center power line shows contracted backlog visibility toward the ~$300M 2027 guide, validating a non-auto-cyclical growth vector.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions turn positive (>+5% over 90 days), signaling consensus is catching up to the margin recovery (currently absent).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further material EV-segment impairment, or a guidance cut to 2026 revenue/margin signaling the recovery has reversed.
- Adverse USMCA renewal or tariff escalation that raises net unrecovered cost meaningfully above the ~$28M 2025 gap.
- Operating margin slips back below ~7% or FCF falls materially below the ~$1.2B run-rate.

## Conclusion

BorgWarner is a financially sound, cash-generative diversified Tier 1 supplier whose GAAP earnings have been distorted by two years of EV-segment impairments now largely behind it. The operational story is improving: Q1 2026 margins recovered, segment losses are narrowing, FCF is robust, and the data-center power pivot offers credible non-auto-cyclical optionality. The reasons we stop short of a BUY: the stock has already re-rated to the consensus mean target with no remaining margin of safety on forward fundamentals, estimate revisions are flat, the revenue base is flat-to-declining, the dividend yield trails peers, and a broad cluster of insider selling into the rally with zero open-market buying tempers the bullish read. The forward multiple is at the peer median, not below it.

We rate BWA a **HOLD** (growth-hold). The 12-month thesis is that the margin recovery and the ~$300M-by-2027 industrial-power line justify holding through the next several quarters of execution evidence, with the Q2 print as the key checkpoint. We would turn more constructive on confirmation that the recovery is durable and that the data-center backlog is building, and would exit on renewed impairments, an adverse USMCA outcome, or a margin reversal below 7%.
