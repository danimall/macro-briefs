## Executive Summary

Chord Energy is the dominant pure-play Williston Basin E&P operator, running 4-5 rigs across 1.3 million net acres with production of ~277,000 Boepd. The stock has rallied approximately 78% from its 52-week low near $84 but remains substantially undervalued relative to both its PV-10 reserve value ($9.1B vs. ~$9.7B EV) and forward cash flow generation (~$1.4B at $80 oil, implying an estimated 16-17% FCF yield on current market cap).

The central disconnect is between the stock's negative reported trailing P/E (distorted by a fully cleared $539M goodwill impairment) and the underlying earnings power. Q1 2026 delivered $4.56 adjusted EPS, meaningfully beating consensus estimates, and current-year estimates have been revised up 324% over 90 days — from $4.67 to $19.81 — as oil prices recovered. At 7.3x current-year consensus earnings and 9.4x next-year estimates, this is priced as a declining asset when the company is actually generating record operating cash flows.

The primary risk is geographic concentration: virtually 100% of operated production comes from the Williston Basin, with specific DAPL transportation risk that could materially impair realized pricing if the pipeline faces disruption. This concentration, combined with insider selling (albeit compensation-related), prevents a higher-conviction position sizing. However, the magnitude of the valuation discount to intrinsic value — with PV-10 alone covering 94% of enterprise value at conservative SEC pricing — creates sufficient margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Chord Energy emerged from Oasis Petroleum's 2021 restructuring, merged with Whiting Petroleum in July 2022, acquired Enerplus in May 2024, and purchased ExxonMobil's XTO Williston assets in October 2025 for $542M. The company is now the largest acreage holder and top producer in the Williston Basin with 5,025 gross (3,937 net) operated producing wells.

The company runs a "maintenance plus" capital program — prioritizing FCF generation and shareholder returns over production growth. With ~89% of reserves operated and substantially all acreage held by production, Chord has full control over pace and capital allocation with minimal lease expiration risk.

A key technological differentiator is the scaling of 4-mile lateral wells: 12 producing, 33 drilled, with ~50% of total inventory consisting of 4-mile locations. These longer laterals generate superior economics through greater lateral contact per surface location and reduced per-well infrastructure costs.

## Growth Analysis

Chord's growth profile is deliberately constrained — "maintenance plus" means flat-to-modest production growth (~2-5% annually) with maximum FCF conversion. This is appropriate for a mature basin operator.

Growth drivers include:
1. **4-mile lateral scaling:** Expected to constitute 60% of 2026 spuds. Superior well economics (more lateral feet per dollar of surface costs) should improve capital efficiency.
2. **Base production optimization:** AI-driven artificial lift management and bifurcated engineering teams are reducing base decline rates, creating production upside without incremental capital.
3. **Commodity price leverage:** Each $10/bbl improvement in realized oil prices adds approximately $550-600M in annual oil revenue on current oil production volumes (~161 MBopd midpoint guidance for FY2026), before royalties, taxes, hedges, and differentials.
4. **Potential Marcellus divestiture:** Non-core asset generating revenue at $3.15/Mcf (45.2 Bcf in 2025). A sale would simplify the story and provide dry powder.

Current-year estimates at $19.81 reflect a 324% upward revision over 90 days, driven by the recovery in oil prices. Next-year estimates at $15.47 (revised up 76% over 90 days) reflect analyst expectations of some commodity price normalization.

## Competitive Landscape

Chord holds a structural competitive advantage as the last large publicly traded pure-play Williston Basin operator. Following Hess's acquisition by Chevron and Marathon Oil's acquisition by ConocoPhillips, Chord faces no public peer of comparable scale in the basin. This creates:

- **Consolidation optionality:** Natural acquirer for any remaining assets in the basin, and a potential acquisition target for larger E&P companies seeking Bakken exposure.
- **Operational efficiency:** Largest acreage position enables contiguous 4-mile lateral designs that smaller operators cannot replicate.
- **Service cost advantages:** Scale secures priority rig crews, frac spreads, and negotiating leverage on supplies.
- **Lower royalty burden:** Higher proportion of fee acreage vs. Permian peers where state/federal royalties are higher.

Against Permian operators (SM, MTDR, MGY), Chord offers a higher oil cut (56%), lower competition intensity, and superior infrastructure buildout — but lower long-term inventory depth and growth optionality.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geographic Concentration (HIGH):** Single-basin exposure to the Williston means any disruption — weather, regulatory, transportation — hits 100% of production. No diversification buffer exists.

**2. DAPL Transportation Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):** The Final EIS was completed December 2025 with a formal Army Corps decision expected. Three of five alternative actions in the EIS would require abandonment or reroute of the contested segment. If DAPL were shut, Williston Basin crude differentials would weaken materially before rail alternatives scale. The current administration is likely sympathetic, but legal uncertainty from ongoing tribal challenges persists.

**3. Commodity Price Volatility (MODERATE):** ~1/3 of 2026 oil volumes are hedged via three-way collars (sub-floor at $45-48/bbl). At $80+ oil, FCF generation is robust. Below $60 oil, capital returns would compress to base dividend only. The asymmetry is favorable at current pricing levels.

**4. Acquisition Integration:** Three major deals in three years increases execution complexity. The $539M goodwill impairment acknowledged overpayment for Enerplus at the time. Goodwill is now zero — eliminating that specific impairment risk — though the company still faces potential impairment risk on oil and gas property carrying values if commodity prices decline materially, as disclosed in the 10-K. Synergies from contiguous acreage are being captured through 4-mile lateral designs.

**5. Insider Selling:** CFO, CSO, COO, CAO, and one director all sold shares in 2026 at $113-146. While primarily compensation-related (vesting events), the absence of any open-market purchases despite management's stated view that shares are cheap creates a minor credibility tension.

## Conclusion

Chord Energy offers a compelling risk-reward setup for investors willing to accept single-basin concentration and commodity price exposure. The estimated 16-17% FCF yield at $80 oil, 324% 90-day estimate revision momentum, and near-PV-10 EV valuation create specific, quantifiable mispricing that should resolve as forward earnings replace the impairment-distorted trailing figures in screening tools. The Q1 earnings beat demonstrates that consensus remains behind the fundamental trajectory. I recommend BUY with 2-3% position sizing, acknowledging that DAPL risk and geographic concentration limit conviction relative to more diversified energy names.
