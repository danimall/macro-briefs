## Executive Summary

Carlisle Companies is a high-quality industrial compounder operating in one of the most structurally attractive end markets in U.S. construction: commercial roofing replacement. The company benefits from an oligopolistic market structure (4 players dominate North American single-ply roofing), a massive and growing re-roofing replacement wave (70%+ of U.S. commercial buildings are 25+ years old), and a capital allocation machine that has reduced shares outstanding by 22% since 2022. At approximately $355–$405 (depending on timing), the stock trades at an estimated 17–20x forward earnings, which appears attractive relative to the company's quality profile, given 19% FCF margins and demonstrated pricing power.

The Q1 2026 earnings beat (+7.8% vs. consensus) confirmed margin improvement momentum, and management reaffirmed full-year guidance for low single-digit revenue growth and 50bps margin expansion. The primary near-term catalysts are: (1) CWT margin progression toward 20%+ from 17.3%, (2) pricing realization from mid-April increases offsetting raw material inflation, and (3) continued share count reduction (~$1B targeted for 2026). The main risks are customer concentration (33% of revenue from two distributors, including QXO/Beacon at 16.7%), raw material cost inflation exceeding pricing realization, and continued new construction weakness from elevated interest rates.

## Company Overview

Carlisle Companies has transformed from a diversified industrial conglomerate into a pure-play building envelope specialist following the 2024 divestiture of Carlisle Interconnect Technologies for ~$2B in proceeds. The company now operates two segments:

**Carlisle Construction Materials (CCM)** — 74% of revenue, 29% adjusted EBITDA margins. Produces single-ply commercial roofing systems (TPO, EPDM, PVC membranes), polyiso insulation, and engineered metal panels. Approximately 70% of CCM's commercial roofing revenue derives from re-roofing/replacement work — a non-discretionary demand stream driven by roof life cycles of 20-25 years.

**Carlisle Weatherproofing Technologies (CWT)** — 26% of revenue, 17.3% adjusted EBITDA margins. Produces waterproofing membranes, air/vapor barriers, spray foam insulation, and polystyrene products. More exposed to residential construction and currently underperforming on margins, with a management target of 20%+.

The company's Vision 2030 targets $40 adjusted EPS and 25%+ ROIC through organic growth, margin expansion, disciplined M&A, and aggressive share repurchases.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability**

FY2025 revenue was $5.02B, essentially flat (+0.3% YoY) as organic decline of -2.9% was offset by +3.2% from acquisitions. Q1 2026 revenue of $1.05B declined 4% YoY due to severe winter weather impacts, but adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 50bps to 22.3%, indicating the underlying profit trajectory remains intact.

Operating income of $1.0B (20% margin) and adjusted EBITDA of $1.225B (24.4% margin) in FY2025 reflect some compression from FY2024 (22.8% operating, 26.6% EBITDA), driven by lower volumes reducing fixed cost absorption. Management's consistent pricing power — demonstrated again with mid-April 2026 price increases — partially offsets these headwinds.

**Cash Flow Quality**

FY2025 free cash flow of approximately $970M represents a 19.3% FCF margin on just $131M of capex (2.6% of revenue). This asset-light cash generation is exceptional for an industrial company and enables the aggressive capital return program. Q1 2026 operating cash flow of -$44.7M was distorted by a one-time $125M tax settlement; underlying OCF was approximately $80M, consistent with seasonal patterns.

**Capital Allocation Driving EPS**

Share count has declined from 51.8M (2022) to 40.5M (Q1 2026) — a 22% reduction. Management is targeting up to $1B in 2026 repurchases; at prices of ~$355-375, this would imply approximately 6-7% full-year share count reduction. After the approximately $250M reportedly repurchased in Q1 2026, the remaining ~$750M authorization would retire approximately 5% of the current share base over the balance of the year. Combined with the annualized dividend of $4.40/share (based on the $1.10 quarterly rate established in 2026, representing a ~1.2% yield), total shareholder returns are well-supported.

**Balance Sheet**

Long-term debt of $2.88B is manageable against $1.225B in EBITDA, implying ~1.7x net leverage (after $771M cash). The debt is well-laddered with the next major maturity ($600M) in 2027. The $1B issuance in August 2025 at 5.25-5.55% effectively funded accelerated buybacks — a leveraged return strategy that works at current FCF multiples but adds downside risk if fundamentals deteriorate. Altman Z-Score of 3.1 confirms adequate safety margin.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth: Modest Near-Term, Structurally Positive Medium-Term**

Management guides for low single-digit revenue growth in FY2026. Consensus estimates project EPS of $20.99 (FY2026) and $23.51 (FY2027), implying 12-15% annual EPS growth driven primarily by:

1. **Re-roofing wave acceleration** — The installed base of 25+ year-old commercial roofs continues growing, providing a structural floor on volumes even without economic recovery.
2. **CWT margin expansion** — Management targets moving from 17.3% to 20%+ EBITDA margins through footprint consolidation, automation, and polystyrene resin in-sourcing, which could add $40M+ in EBITDA if achieved.
3. **Share count reduction** — Up to 6-7% annual EPS accretion from buybacks at current prices.
4. **Pricing realization** — Oligopolistic market enables cost pass-through; mid-April price increases expected to flow through Q2-Q3 2026.

**Estimate Revision Trends**

Current year estimates are up +1.8% over 90 days and +0.8% over 30 days. Next year estimates are up +2.5% over 90 days. These are positive but not explosive — consistent with a steady compounder rather than a high-growth story. The current quarter estimate is slightly down (-2.4% in 30 days) likely reflecting seasonal timing concerns, though next quarter estimates rose +4.4% over 90 days.

**Vision 2030 Path to $40 EPS**

From a current ~$21 FY2026 EPS base, reaching $40 by 2030 requires a 17% CAGR. The math: 5-7% revenue growth + 100-150bps annual margin expansion + 5-7% share count reduction gets to approximately 14-18% annual EPS growth. This is ambitious but mechanically achievable given the buyback trajectory and CWT margin opportunity.

## Valuation Assessment

CSL trades at approximately 17-20x forward earnings depending on the reference price used (the stock has ranged from approximately $355 to $405+ in recent months). Against its quality profile — 19% FCF margins, oligopolistic market structure, demonstrated pricing power, and 22% share count reduction track record — this valuation appears attractive, though a precise peer discount is difficult to quantify without a clearly defined comparable peer set.

On a forward FCF basis, at ~$1B expected FY2026 FCF and a market cap of approximately $14.3–16.4B (depending on share price), CSL trades at approximately 14-16x FCF — reasonable to attractive for a company with this quality profile and return on capital.

Analyst consensus targets suggest upside potential, though estimates vary by source and timing. A JPMorgan upgrade in March 2026 raised its price target to $420 from $400, maintaining an Overweight rating.

The specific reason the market may be mispricing CSL: investors are anchoring to the Q2 2025 earnings miss (-6.5%) and flat FY2025 organic growth, treating the company as a no-growth industrial when the re-roofing cycle is a structural multi-year tailwind that hasn't yet reaccelerated. The combination of share buybacks + re-roofing acceleration + CWT margin improvement creates a compounding EPS story that may not be fully reflected in the current multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

**Oligopolistic Dominance**

North American single-ply commercial roofing is controlled by four manufacturers: Carlisle SynTec, GAF (Standard Industries, private), Elevate (Holcim), and Johns Manville (Berkshire Hathaway). This structure prevents destructive price competition and supports 29% EBITDA margins in CCM — margins that have been stable despite cyclical volume fluctuations.

**Switching Costs and Contractor Loyalty**

Roofing contractors invest in Carlisle-specific training, warranty systems, and installation equipment. Carlisle's labor-saving innovations (adhesive application systems replacing mechanical fastening) increase content per square foot while reducing labor requirements — directly addressing the construction labor shortage and deepening contractor preference.

**Re-Roofing Structural Advantage**

With approximately 70% of commercial roofing demand from replacement/re-roofing, Carlisle's CCM segment is less cyclical than most construction-exposed companies. Roof replacement is non-discretionary once failure occurs, and the aging of the installed base creates a steadily growing demand pipeline independent of new construction activity. While this re-roofing exposure represents a substantial portion of consolidated revenue given CCM's 74% share, the company retains meaningful exposure to new construction and residential markets through the balance of its portfolio.

**Distribution Risk**

The 33% revenue concentration in two customers — QXO/Beacon Roofing Supply (16.7% of FY2025 consolidated revenue) and ABC Supply (16.3%) — is the primary competitive vulnerability. QXO's acquisition of Beacon in April 2025 means Carlisle's single largest customer relationship is now part of a larger, potentially more powerful distribution platform. This consolidation could shift bargaining power over time, though management has not flagged immediate adverse effects. No other customer exceeds 10% of revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-Term Risks:**
1. **Raw material inflation exceeding pricing** — MDI and TPO resins are experiencing double-digit inflation. If benzene/propylene prices spike further (possible under Middle East escalation scenarios), margins could compress before pricing catches up (typical 1-2 quarter lag).
2. **Customer concentration and distributor consolidation** — QXO's acquisition of Beacon adds uncertainty around future pricing and distribution dynamics for Carlisle's largest disclosed customer relationship.
3. **CWT execution** — Integration of four acquisitions (Henry, PFB, ThermaFoam, Bonded Logic) while restructuring manufacturing footprint introduces execution risk. Henry trade name at $219M with <10% impairment headroom may require a material impairment charge if CWT revenue expectations do not materialize or discount rates increase.

**Medium-Term Risks:**
4. **Interest rate persistence** — Elevated rates suppress new construction (~30% of CCM revenue). If rates don't moderate through 2027, organic growth remains constrained.
5. **Leveraged buyback strategy** — If a downturn reduces EBITDA 20-25%, leverage approaches concerning levels, potentially forcing buyback slowdown.

**Options Market Signal:**
Based on available market data (which is dynamic and may shift), near-term implied volatility appeared moderately elevated relative to historical volatility, suggesting the options market is pricing some near-term uncertainty — likely around raw material/tariff concerns and the upcoming Q2 earnings season. This is consistent with short-term macro uncertainty rather than company-specific deterioration.

Short interest has been moderately elevated for the sector, suggesting some skeptics remain on the growth thesis. However, levels are well below those that would indicate crowding or squeeze risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($430-450, upside dependent on entry price):**
- CWT margins reach 20%+ as management targets, adding $40M+ EBITDA
- Re-roofing volumes accelerate in 2027 as the aging roof pipeline matures
- Pricing fully offsets raw material inflation with margin expansion
- Share count drops below 38M by year-end 2027
- FY2027 EPS of $24-25 earns 18-19x multiple = $430-475

**Bear Case ($295-320):**
- Persistent raw material inflation outpaces pricing; margins compress 200bps
- New construction remains depressed through 2027; organic growth stays negative
- Customer loss or pricing concession to top-2 distributors
- Henry impairment charge + CWT misses margin targets
- Multiple contracts to 14-15x on execution concerns = $295-350

**Base Case ($400-420):**
- Low single-digit organic growth plus acquisitions deliver $21 EPS in FY2026
- CWT margins improve to 19-20% as guided
- Buybacks reduce share count ~6-7% annually
- FY2027 EPS of $23.50 at 17.5x = $411

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target:** $410 — derived from 17.5x FY2027E EPS of $23.51. The company's quality characteristics should support multiple stability or modest expansion as the re-roofing cycle accelerates and CWT margins improve.

**Bull case target:** $450 — 19x FY2027E EPS reflecting successful CWT margin expansion, re-roofing acceleration, and continued buyback execution.

**Bear case target:** $310 — 14.5x reduced FY2026 EPS of ~$21 if margin compression materializes and organic growth remains negative.

**Upside/downside assessment:** Risk/reward is most attractive at prices below $375, where the base case offers meaningful upside against moderate downside. At prices approaching $400+, the base-case upside narrows considerably and the risk/reward becomes less compelling.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: Q2 2026 earnings (July — pricing realization visibility), Q3 2026 earnings (October — CWT margin progression), and 2027 guidance release (February 2027 — re-roofing volume acceleration).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of EBITDA margin below 22% (indicating pricing failure)
2. Loss of a top-2 customer or announced distribution arrangement change
3. Buyback pace decelerating below $200M/quarter without clear reason (signals management concern about leverage or cash flow)

## Conclusion

Carlisle represents a high-quality industrial compounder with clear catalysts for value creation over the next 12-18 months. The combination of an oligopolistic market structure, a large recurring re-roofing demand base within its commercial roofing business, 19% FCF margins, and aggressive share count reduction creates a durable compounding engine that should deliver 12-15% annual EPS growth. The stock is appropriately rated **BUY** at prices in the mid-$350s to low-$370s range, with a base case target of $410 representing meaningful upside against moderate downside risk.

The key differentiator from a HOLD is the specific, measurable catalyst of CWT margin improvement (verifiable quarterly) combined with mechanical EPS uplift from buybacks, against a valuation that embeds limited premium for the structural re-roofing tailwind.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The BUY recommendation is price-sensitive: if the current trading price is closer to $400–$410 rather than $355, the base-case upside to $410 is minimal and the recommendation would more appropriately be HOLD or selective BUY on pullbacks. Additionally, the QXO/Beacon consolidation risk (Carlisle's largest customer at 16.7% of revenue) is more directly relevant than originally framed.
