## Executive Summary

Infosys enters FY2027 with the strongest balance sheet in its peer group and the weakest growth guidance of its modern history. The June 2026 quarter delivered $5,082M of revenue (+2.85% year over year), a 21.09% operating margin, and $955M of free cash flow at 116.5% of net profit. Gross cash and investments total $4,048M against no separately disclosed borrowings and $563M of non-current lease liabilities. The dividend, roughly $0.457 per ADR at the quoted 3.65% yield, is covered at approximately 56% of consensus FY27 EPS and comfortably covered by cash flow. Share count is falling 2.3-2.4% per year on two independent derivations from the reported EPS-versus-profit gap.

The problem sits in the revenue line and, more precisely, in price. Management cut FY27 constant-currency revenue growth guidance to 1.5-3.0% from 1.5-3.5% while retaining the 20-22% margin band. Against an eight-year USD revenue CAGR of approximately 7.8% (FY2018 $10.9bn to an estimated FY2026 ~$20.0bn), the company is now guiding to roughly a quarter to two-fifths of its own decade-long growth rate. The most informative number in the quarter is not disclosed by the company but derived: rupee revenue grew 14.0% against USD revenue growth of 2.85%, implying ₹94.87/USD versus ₹85.57 a year earlier, a ~10.9% rupee depreciation. For a business with a rupee cost base and USD/EUR/GBP revenue, that should be a large gross-margin tailwind. Operating margin expanded 29 basis points and net margin fell 26 basis points. Something absorbed nearly all of an unusually favorable currency move, most plausibly the AI-led productivity pass-through that Motilal Oswal, Elara Capital and Kotak each named in the same week, though hedge timing, onsite cost mix and newly acquired lower-margin revenue with intangible amortization are non-exclusive alternative explanations I cannot apportion.

The entry point has materially worsened since the news. The ADR traded at $10.35 in early NYSE trade on July 24 and closed at $12.53 on the current basis, a gain of roughly 21% from that print, during a period in which consensus EPS was revised down on all four forecast horizons (next-year estimate cut 4.1% in thirty days) and five brokerages cut price targets 17-25%. The ADR now sits above the consensus mean target of $11.85 and median of $11.58, with an analyst high of $14.10 and low of $9.00. Estimate revisions are negative and the analyst distribution has migrated toward hold (11 of 14 versus 8 of 13 three months ago). Falling estimates remove the argument that consensus targets act as a floor. Recommendation: **REDUCE**.

## Company Overview

Infosys is India's second-largest IT services company by revenue, behind Tata Consultancy Services, with 328,062 employees and 2,027 active clients as of June 30, 2026. Delivery spans application development and maintenance, cloud and infrastructure operations, enterprise package implementation (SAP, Oracle Fusion), engineering services, business-process management, and the Finacle core-banking software franchise. Two branded platform layers, Topaz (AI) and Cobalt (cloud), wrap most engagements.

Q1 FY27 mix: North America 56.4%, Europe 32.1%, rest of world 9.0%, India 2.5%. Verticals disclosed: Financial Services 27.9%, Manufacturing 15.9%, Energy/Utilities/Resources/Services 13.4%; the remaining ~42.8% (Retail, Communication, Hi-Tech, Life Sciences, Other) was not disclosed in available sources. Voluntary attrition was 13.0% on a trailing-twelve-month basis, up from 12.6% sequentially and down from 14.4% a year earlier.

A structural note on source availability: Infosys is a foreign private issuer reporting under IFRS. It files a Form 20-F (most recently June 15, 2026) rather than a 10-K, and furnishes quarterly results on Form 6-K. Direct retrieval of the 20-F failed, so Item 3.D risk factors, the legal-proceedings note, and executive compensation disclosures were unavailable. Financial figures below derive from the issuer's July 23, 2026 results release, which states the condensed statements were audited and taken on record by the Board — unusual for an interim period and modestly confidence-raising, but not independently verified against the corresponding 6-K.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement, quarter ended June 30, 2026 (USD millions, IFRS):**

| | Q1 FY27 | Q1 FY26 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 5,082 | 4,941 | +2.85% |
| Gross profit | 1,600 | 1,525 | +4.9% |
| Operating expenses | 528 | 497 | +6.2% |
| Operating profit | 1,072 | 1,028 | +4.3% |
| Other income, net | 91 | 110 | −17.3% |
| Net profit (after NCI) | 819 | 809 | +1.24% |

Derived margins: gross 31.48% versus 30.86% (+62bp); operating expenses 10.39% of revenue versus 10.06% (a 33bp deleverage); operating margin 21.09% versus 20.80% (+29bp); effective tax rate 29.49% versus 28.91%; net margin 16.11% versus 16.37% (−26bp).

The chain matters. Gross margin improved 62 basis points; opex growth of 6.2% against revenue growth of 2.9% cut the operating-margin gain to 29 basis points; a $19M decline in other income and a 58 basis point higher tax rate converted 4.3% operating-profit growth into 1.24% net-profit growth and a lower net margin. Basic EPS grew 3.7% against 1.24% net-profit growth, implying a ~2.4% reduction in share count; the rupee lines (EPS +14.9% against PAT +12.25%) imply −2.30%. Two independent derivations landing at 2.3-2.4% is reasonable evidence of continuing buyback activity, though no specific program was verified from a filing.

Sequentially, rupee revenue rose 3.9% while rupee net profit fell 8.6% (₹7,769 crore versus ₹8,501 crore). Operating margin rose ~20 basis points quarter over quarter, so the sequential earnings decline originated below the operating line, most plausibly lower other income and a normalized tax rate after a favorable March-quarter item. That is inference; no Q4 FY26 one-off was verified. The decline was anticipated — ET Now modeled ₹7,834 crore and Nomura ₹7,679.70 crore against ₹7,769 crore actual. Guidance, not the print, was the disappointment.

**Balance sheet (USD millions):** cash and equivalents $2,287M, current investments $837M, non-current investments $924M — $4,048M of gross cash and investments, down from $4,648M at March 31 as $528M of current investments funded acquisitions. Total assets $16,359M, total liabilities $6,685M, total equity $9,674M. Current ratio 1.86x, down from 1.98x. Working capital $4,817M, or 29.4% of total assets. The only explicitly disclosed debt-like item is $563M of non-current lease liabilities. Annualized Q1 EBIT over total assets is 26.2%. Solvency is not a debatable question here.

Two caveats on that reading. "Other current liabilities and provisions" is $3,515M — 21.5% of total assets and the largest single liability — with composition undisclosed in the condensed extract, so the absence of short-term borrowings could not be fully verified. And total equity fell $166M sequentially despite $820M of net profit before minority interests, implying roughly $986M of other movements. Those movements comprise the FY26 final dividend, any buyback, share-based compensation, and negative foreign-currency translation reserve movement. Since the functional currency is the rupee and the reporting currency is USD, a ~3% sequential rupee depreciation would mechanically reduce translated equity by roughly $250-300M on a $9.8bn base. The $986M should not be read as cash returned to shareholders.

**Cash flow quality is genuinely good.** Free cash flow of $955M equals 116.6% of net profit and 18.8% of revenue. Unbilled revenue rose $150M (current plus non-current) while trade receivables fell $146M, leaving billed plus current unbilled receivables essentially flat sequentially ($5,348M to $5,328M) against growing revenue. DSO on billed receivables is 63.9 days; 95.4 days including current unbilled. The mix shifted toward milestone billing consistent with large-deal ramps, without total collection exposure increasing.

I decline to annualize one quarter of FCF as a forecast. As a labeled sensitivity: four quarters at $955M is $3.82bn, or a 7.3-7.6% yield on the $50.0-52.6bn market capitalization derived below. That figure almost certainly overstates the year, because wage increases deferred to October 2026 and January 2027 land entirely in H2 FY27.

**Goodwill and intangibles rose $466M sequentially** (from $1,576M to $2,042M), tracing to two US acquisitions announced March 25-26, 2026 and closed in the June quarter: Optimum Healthcare IT (healthcare consulting, up to $465M all cash including earnouts) and Stratus (property & casualty insurance technology, 450+ people, $95M). The $465M is a maximum including contingent consideration; the $466M balance-sheet change is a movement in an ending balance, not a statement of cash paid.

## Growth Analysis

The long-run comparison is the cleanest framing available. USD revenue was $10,939M in FY2018 (SEC XBRL, FY2018 20-F) and approximately $20.0bn in FY2026 (derived by summing Q1 FY26 of $4,941M, an interpolated Q4 FY26 of ~$5,042M, and interpolated middle quarters — an estimate with roughly ±$100M of uncertainty). That is a ~7.8% eight-year CAGR. FY2027 guidance is 1.5-3.0% constant currency.

Q1 reported constant-currency growth was 2.4% year over year and 1.0% sequentially. A third-party pre-results estimate (Deven Choksey) put the two closed acquisitions at roughly 0.8pp of growth contribution, implying organic growth near 1.6-2.0%. That estimate is not a company disclosure, but if approximately right, organic growth is running near zero in real terms.

The bookings data cuts the other way and deserves weight: $3.6bn of large-deal total contract value with 61% net new, a clear beat against Kotak's $2.5-2.75bn expectation, and BofA noted deal wins "moved up nicely." AI-related work is 8.2% of revenue, though the release does not define scope. Named engagements are substantive: a Mercedes-Benz Group renewal covering a large hybrid cloud and data center estate moving to an AI-led operating model, a seven-year Bendigo Bank engagement, expansions at DNB Bank, GlobalFoundries, Spark New Zealand, Cox Communications and Sentara, plus Finacle wins at Sterling Bank of Asia and a go-live at Bank of Sydney.

Note the counterparty roles precisely. In the OpenAI relationship, Infosys is a deployment partner helping enterprises apply Codex — OpenAI's product — to legacy code modernization and code review. With Harness, Harness owns the platform and Infosys supplies deployment expertise. At DNB, NICE Actimize owns X-Sight. Infosys captures services revenue around third-party intellectual property rather than owning the underlying tools. In an environment where AI tooling compresses billable effort, that positioning determines who keeps the productivity gain, and the pricing evidence suggests the client keeps a meaningful share.

Unearned revenue of $1,222M is a contract liability, not backlog. No remaining-performance-obligation or backlog figure was disclosed. TCV is a period flow of new bookings, not a stock of contracted revenue. These should not be conflated.

Estimate momentum is negative across every horizon: current quarter $0.21 (−2.2% over 30 days, −2.5% over 90), next quarter $0.20 (−3.1%/−3.6%), current year $0.81 (−2.7%/−2.9%), next year $0.85 (−4.1% over 30 days versus −2.6% over 90). The next-year cut accelerated in the most recent thirty days. Earnings delivery has been inconsistent: two beats and two misses in four quarters, including a −12.6% miss in December 2025 and a −2.1% miss in June 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

Share count must be derived. Net profit of $819M divided by basic EPS of $0.20 implies ~4,095M shares; two-decimal rounding puts true EPS between $0.195 and $0.205, so basic shares fall in a ~3,995M-4,200M band. At $12.53 per ADR (one ADR equals one equity share), market capitalization is **$50.0bn-$52.6bn**.

Multiples on that basis:
- 15.5x consensus FY27 EPS of $0.81
- 14.7x consensus FY28 EPS of $0.85
- Ex-cash: net cash and investments of $4,048M less $563M of leases is ~$3,485M, or ~$0.85 per share; enterprise value per share ~$11.68, or **14.4x FY27**
- Dividend yield 3.65% ($0.457 per ADR implied), ~56% payout on FY27 consensus
- FCF yield sensitivity 7.3-7.6% on an unadjusted Q1 run rate, biased high by the H2 wage step-up

The question is whether 14-15x on a ~5% earnings grower with a 3.65% covered yield is asymmetric. Jefferies models a ~5% recurring EPS CAGR post-results. On that basis PEG is roughly 3.0, and the static-multiple total return is approximately 3.65% yield plus ~5% EPS growth, or ~8-9% annually — an ordinary equity return with no margin of safety against multiple compression. The cheapness is real in absolute terms and explained by the growth problem; it is not evidence of mispricing.

Three specific facts argue against paying up here. First, the ADR at $12.53 sits above the consensus mean ($11.85, −5.4% from spot) and median ($11.58, −7.6%), with the analyst high at $14.10 (+12.5%) and low at $9.00 (−28.2%). Second, the post-results INR targets, translated at the derived ₹94.87/USD, are Jefferies ₹1,020 ≈ $10.75, Morgan Stanley ₹1,075 ≈ $11.33, Elara ₹1,150 ≈ $12.12, Motilal Oswal ₹1,170 ≈ $12.33 and BofA ₹1,300 ≈ $13.70 — the ADR trades above three of five. Third, the ADR gained roughly 21% from its July 23-24 low while every one of those five houses cut targets 17-25% and consensus was revised down. Multiple expansion into falling estimates is the specific configuration in which a low headline P/E has historically been least informative.

One reconciliation I could not resolve: the ADR's advance from $10.35 on July 24 to $12.53 requires either the local share price recovering to roughly ₹1,189 at an unchanged rupee, rupee appreciation to roughly ₹82.9/USD at an unchanged local price, or a combination. If the move is local-price recovery, the ADR has re-rated ahead of most of the sell-side range on unchanged-to-worse estimates. If it is currency, the ADR gain is mechanical and carries an offsetting USD-margin headwind, since rupee appreciation raises the USD cost of an INR-denominated cost base. Neither reading supports adding capital at this level.

**Valuation score: 4.0.** A compressed multiple attached to structural revenue-per-unit-of-work pressure, negative revisions, no identified re-rating catalyst, and a known H2 margin step-down.

## Competitive Landscape

Whether the deceleration is company-specific or industry-wide is unresolved. Morgan Stanley wrote that the FY27 cut "could reflect Infosys-specific reasons," while also noting growth "has become more challenged for the industry as a whole." No comparable Q1 FY27 growth figures for TCS, Wipro, HCLTech or Accenture were available, which is a real gap in this assessment.

What the sell-side converges on is pricing. Elara: "Infosys is not getting better pricing due to intensifying competition." Motilal Oswal: "continued pricing pressure from AI-led productivity deflation," with productivity pass-through on the existing book of business a near-term headwind. Kotak: "AI deflation headwinds." Three independent houses describing the same mechanism in the same week is reasonable triangulation on the existence of a pricing problem; none quantified it.

The derived currency arithmetic is the strongest available corroboration. A ~10.9% rupee depreciation against a rupee cost base should have moved gross margin materially more than 62 basis points and operating margin materially more than 29. Alternative explanations — hedge lag on realized rates, rising onsite USD-denominated cost share, dilution from newly acquired lower-margin revenue with intangible amortization (Kotak forecast precisely this offset), and AI productivity pass-throughs — are not mutually exclusive, and I cannot apportion between them. The composite conclusion is that pricing and delivery-cost headwinds consumed nearly all of a large tailwind.

Two readings of the AI evidence can both be true. AI mandates are growing as a share of revenue (8.2%) while total revenue growth decelerates, because AI compresses billings on the legacy installed base faster than new AI work replaces them. On that framing, 8.2% is evidence of relevance rather than net growth, and 1.5-3.0% CC guidance is the net of the two forces. The alternative is that the deceleration is predominantly cyclical — macro and tariff-driven client budget caution — in which case growth recovers with the spending cycle and 14-15x is a cyclical trough multiple. The available evidence does not permit choosing, and that distinction is the central valuation question for this stock. The market is being asked to underwrite the cyclical reading at a price above consensus.

On the credit side of the ledger: leader positions in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for Cloud ERP Services, HFS Horizons rankings in data modernization and AI, SAP S/4HANA and financial crime compliance, and Whitelane's #1 ranking for banking IT services in Europe. Client count grew 8.9% to 2,027. Client count establishes logo breadth and says nothing about revenue concentration, and no top-client concentration data was available.

## Risk Assessment

**Pricing deflation from AI (primary).** Corroborated by three brokerages and visible in the currency-versus-margin arithmetic. The structural risk is that AI compresses billable effort in the installed base faster than new AI mandates replace it, a revenue-per-unit-of-work problem that deal-win volume does not automatically solve.

**H2 FY27 margin step-down (specific, dated).** Wage increases arrive in three parts across October 2026 and January 2027. Axis Direct attributed expected Q1 margin strength partly to the absence of wage hikes plus the Project Maximus cost program. The 21.1% Q1 margin therefore sits in the upper half of the 20-22% guided band under favorable timing. Retaining that band while cutting revenue guidance implies management expects to defend margin through cost action rather than volume.

**"The US government investigation."** The July 23 safe harbor lists "the outcome of pending litigation and the US government investigation" — definite article, singular, named separately from general litigation, cross-referenced to the FY2026 20-F. Three targeted searches returned nothing and the 20-F legal-proceedings note was unreachable. This may be long-standing language relating to a dormant matter, or a specific material exposure. It is an unresolved verification gap, and I make no claim about agency, subject matter, timeline or amount.

**Customer concentration, unmeasured.** No top-5 or top-10 client revenue share was available. Separately, Elara cited "a contract termination by a client" as a contributor to the guidance cut — single-source, uncorroborated, no counterparty or size named.

**Immigration and tariff channels.** With 56.4% of revenue from North America and cross-border delivery, US visa policy is a direct cost-and-capacity channel. Manufacturing (15.9%) plus Energy/Utilities/Resources (13.4%) is ~29% of revenue in verticals most exposed to goods-trade disruption and capital-budget compression.

**CEO transition.** Ashiss Kumar Dash is CEO Designate effective July 23, 2026, with intended appointment as CEO and Managing Director from April 1, 2027 for a five-year term subject to shareholder approval; Salil Parekh serves through March 31, 2027. Process quality is high — internal candidate, eight-month overlap, contractual term completion rather than abrupt exit, and Chairman Nandan Nilekani's explicit endorsement. But Dash's specific prior title and business group were not confirmed in any available source, executive compensation and incentive structure are undisclosed (no DEF 14A as a foreign private issuer), and Motilal Oswal noted the succession "could introduce near-term uncertainty." Morgan Stanley expects investors to monitor the transition.

**Integration and earn-out risk.** $560M headline maximum deployed in one quarter across two US targets, funded from investments. Earn-out milestones are undisclosed.

**Currency translation for USD holders.** The ADR is down 21.8% over twelve months while local shares held far better, a gap driven substantially by ~11% rupee depreciation. That channel can continue; the broader emerging-market dollar cushion has been removed.

**What is not a risk here.** No refinancing cliff, no covenant exposure, no cash burn, no accounting red flags, and FCF exceeding net income with flat combined receivables. Bankruptcy risk is negligible. Short interest of 4.8% of float with 6.5 days to cover is uncrowded.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.9% against 39.2% one-year realized is fairly priced (28th percentile within the current chain), with a contango term structure to 44.2% at six months and a ±6.6% implied move on the 10-day expiry. Put/call open-interest ratio is 1.80, and the September (38-day) tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 2.61 — the heaviest in the chain — indicating active downside hedging demand around the next results window rather than near-term panic. The 3-month and 6-month skew readings are −10.8% and −9.8%; the sign convention is not defined in the data, so I draw no directional inference from those values beyond noting they differ sharply from the "balanced" −0.8% near-term reading. Overall: no volatility mispricing that would change entry timing, and positioning consistent with the fundamental caution rather than contradicting it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($14.50, +15.7%)

The deceleration is predominantly cyclical. Client budgets recover as tariff and rate uncertainty resolves, and the $3.6bn of Q1 large-deal TCV with 61% net new converts into revenue through H2 FY27 and FY28. AI mandates scale past 8.2% of revenue fast enough to become net accretive rather than deflationary, and the two US acquisitions add vertical domain depth in healthcare IT and P&C insurance that supports pricing where commodity capacity does not. The rupee tailwind arrives with hedge lag, delivering the margin benefit that Q1 did not show, and Project Maximus absorbs the October/January wage step so the year lands at or above the 21% midpoint. Dash's transition is uneventful, and the 2.3-2.4% annual share-count reduction plus a 3.65% yield compounds a mid-single-digit EPS line into a high-single-digit total return. At 17x FY28 EPS of $0.85 the ADR is worth $14.50, roughly 3% above the analyst high.

### Bear Case ($9.00, −28.2%)

AI productivity deflation is structural, not cyclical. Effort-based billings in the installed base compress faster than new AI mandates replace them, and Infosys captures services revenue around third-party tooling (Codex, Harness, NICE Actimize) rather than owning the intellectual property, so clients keep the productivity gain. FY28 constant-currency growth stays at or below FY27's, the H2 wage step-up plus acquisition amortization pushes operating margin to the 20% floor, and EPS drifts toward $0.72-0.75 as estimate cuts continue at the current pace (next-year consensus already −4.1% in thirty days). The unidentified US government investigation surfaces with quantified exposure, or the reported client termination proves material to a concentration profile investors cannot currently measure. The multiple compresses to 11-12x on the DXC/Accenture de-rating template, taking the ADR to $9.00 — the analyst low, and 12.6% below the 52-week low of $10.298. Continued rupee depreciation compounds the loss for USD holders.

### Probability-Weighted View

Assigning 20% to the bull case, 50% to base ($12.75, 15x FY28 EPS of $0.85, +1.8%) and 30% to bear gives a price return of approximately −4.4%. Adding the 3.65% dividend yields an expected total return near zero over twelve months. That is below the return required for equity risk, with downside (−28%) roughly twice the upside (+16%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Horizon: 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the October 2026 Q2 FY27 results.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than around a future catalyst. The ~21% gain off the July 23-24 low, against consensus cuts on all four horizons and target cuts of 17-25%, is the favorable side of the trade for a seller. Reduce to a residual income-oriented position sized to the 3.65% covered yield, or exit fully if the position was held for growth. Do not add.

**Base case target: $12.75** — 15.0x FY28 consensus EPS of $0.85, holding the current multiple. +1.8% from $12.53, or +5.4% including the dividend.

**Bull case target: $14.50** — 17.1x FY28 consensus EPS of $0.85. +15.7%.

**Bear case target: $9.00** — 11.1x FY27 consensus EPS of $0.81, or 12.0x a downgraded $0.75. −28.2%, matching the analyst low.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Q2 FY27 constant-currency revenue growth at or above 3.0% year over year with FY27 guidance raised from 1.5-3.0%.
2. Operating margin at or above 21.0% in a quarter that fully absorbs the October wage increase, demonstrating that Project Maximus and the currency position offset the step-up.
3. Consensus FY28 EPS stabilizing — no further downward revision over a subsequent 60-day window after the current $0.85.
4. Price at or below $10.50-11.00, restoring roughly 16-23% upside to the base case and a 4.2-4.4% yield.

**Upgrade trigger to ACCUMULATE:** all of the above plus disclosure of top-10 client concentration and resolution or quantification of the referenced US government investigation.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. FY27 revenue guidance cut a second time, or FY28 initial guidance below 1.5% constant currency.
2. Operating margin guidance cut below the 20% floor, or a reported quarter below 20%.
3. Quantified material exposure from the US government investigation, or confirmation that the reported client termination represents a top-10 relationship.
4. Dividend reduction, or buyback suspension in favor of further acquisitions.

**Reassessment timeline:** October 2026 (Q2 FY27 results, first quarter containing part of the wage increase), then January 2027 (Q3, full wage effect plus initial commentary on the April 1 leadership handover). The FY2028 guidance in April 2027 under a new CEO is the point at which the cyclical-versus-structural question becomes answerable.

## Conclusion

Infosys is a high-quality business with a deteriorating price. The balance sheet is close to unimpeachable: $4,048M of gross cash and investments, no material borrowings, $955M of quarterly free cash flow at 116.5% of net profit, flat combined receivables against growing revenue, a covered 3.65% dividend, and a share count shrinking 2.3-2.4% annually. Nothing in the financial statements suggests distress or aggressive accounting, and the June-quarter results were audited.

The growth engine is the problem. FY27 constant-currency guidance of 1.5-3.0% is a quarter to two-fifths of the company's own eight-year USD CAGR of ~7.8%. Roughly 0.8pp of Q1's 2.4% constant-currency growth appears attributable to acquisitions, implying organic growth near 1.6-2.0%. A ~10.9% rupee depreciation that should have expanded margins materially produced 29 basis points of operating-margin gain and a 26 basis point decline in net margin — consistent with the AI-led pricing deflation three brokerages named independently, though hedge timing, cost mix and acquisition dilution are unresolved contributors. H2 FY27 must absorb wage increases deferred to October and January.

At $12.53 the ADR trades at 15.5x FY27 and 14.7x FY28 consensus, above the consensus mean of $11.85 and median of $11.58, after a ~21% rally off its post-guidance-cut low during which consensus EPS was cut on every horizon and five houses cut targets 17-25%. Expected total return over twelve months is approximately zero on a 20/50/30 probability weighting, with 28% downside to the analyst low against 16% upside to a level 3% above the analyst high. The low multiple is explained by the growth problem rather than being evidence against it. Trim; revisit at $10.50-11.00 or on evidence that margin holds through the wage step-up while estimates stop falling.
