**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Horizon is a $84 billion-asset super-regional bank built around a high-growth Southeast/Gulf Coast footprint and FHN Financial, a national fixed-income franchise that gives it fee diversification most regional peers lack. The 1Q26 print was clean: diluted EPS of $0.53 (up 29% YoY), net interest margin of 3.52% (+10bp YoY), ROTCE of 15.1%, an efficiency ratio improving to 58.5%, and net charge-offs of just 18bp. The standalone-value-creation playbook the company adopted after the TD Bank merger collapsed in 2023 is working: aggressive buybacks below tangible book, deposit-cost repricing as the Fed eased, and a deliberate capital-stack optimization (issuing 6.75% Series H preferred while redeeming older series and buying back common).

The problem for the stock is that the market already knows this. At $24.81, FHN trades at roughly 1.7x tangible book ($14.34) and ~12.5x earnings, a slight premium to the regional-bank peer median P/E of 12.0. The EPS growth that drove the share price up 27% over the past year was substantially financial engineering: the ~7% YoY reduction in the share count did much of the per-share lifting, and that engine is decelerating, not accelerating. Consensus EPS estimates have flattened — 0.0% revision over the trailing 30 days, +1.0% over 90 days for the current year. This is a well-managed bank delivering exactly what is priced.

We rate FHN HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The 12-month case rests on continued buyback accretion, modest NIM resilience, and re-emergence as a potential M&A target in a consolidating regional landscape — real but already partially discounted. The specific obstacles to a BUY are quantifiable: stalled estimate revisions (the buyback inflection is maturing, not building), thin CRE/office reserve coverage (ALLL/NPL of 64% against rising hospitality and office NPLs), and the absence of any valuation discount to exploit in a sector where peer-multiple regional banks have not historically delivered index-beating selection returns. We would upgrade on a pullback toward tangible-book-supported levels or a re-acceleration in estimates; we would downgrade on a meaningful CRE credit deterioration or a NIM reversal.

## Company Overview

First Horizon Corporation, headquartered in Memphis, is a financial holding company whose sole bank subsidiary, First Horizon Bank, operates more than 400 banking centers across 12 southern states and 450+ business locations across 23 states, with roughly 7,400 employees. The franchise is concentrated in the high-in-migration Southeast and Gulf Coast: Tennessee (~35% of deposits), Florida (17%), Louisiana (12%) and North Carolina (11%). The current footprint was largely built through the 2020 merger of equals with IBERIABANK, which expanded the Louisiana/Gulf Coast presence.

The bank reports three segments:
- **Commercial, Consumer & Wealth** — the core bank plus niche commercial verticals (asset-based lending, CRE, equipment finance, energy, healthcare, transportation) and wealth/trust. Generated $393M of 1Q26 pre-tax income.
- **Wholesale** — mortgage warehouse lending, franchise/correspondent banking, and FHN Financial, a fixed-income sales/trading/underwriting business that produced $53M of fixed-income revenue in 1Q26. This is the differentiator versus typical regional peers and a source of both fee diversification and earnings cyclicality.
- **Corporate** — funding, capital management and run-off portfolios (a $85M loss in 1Q26).

Loan composition: C&I 57% ($36.5B), CRE 21% ($13.4B), consumer real estate 21% ($13.9B), credit card/other 1%. Total loans $64.4B against deposits of $66.5B (97% loan-to-deposit).

## Financial Analysis

FHN is a healthy, well-capitalized bank with improving profitability:

- **Earnings.** 1Q26 net income to common was $257M. The operating contribution and the buyback contribution are both present, and separating them matters because the ~7% YoY share-count reduction amplified the per-share growth materially beyond the operating gain.
- **Net interest margin.** The mechanism: interest-bearing deposit costs fell 44bp (Fed cuts in Sep/Oct/Dec 2025) while asset yields fell only 21bp, so the deposit-repricing tailwind outpaced asset-yield compression. NIM expansion depends on this favorable repricing continuing amid intensifying deposit competition.
- **Returns.** ROA 1.30%, ROTCE 15.12% (non-GAAP), ROCE 12.26% — all improved YoY and solid for the peer set.
- **Efficiency.** Down from 60.1%, despite noninterest expense rising 4% (personnel +$10M, software +$6M). Operating leverage is positive.
- **Credit.** Provision fell to $15M (from $40M). ALLL/loans 1.13%, ACL/loans 1.28%. NPLs steady at 0.94%; NPAs flat at $617M.
- **Capital.** CET1 10.53% (down 10bp as capital funded loan growth and buybacks), Tier 1 11.94%, Total 13.74%, TCE/TA 8.27% — comfortably above well-capitalized-plus-buffer.
- **Liquidity.** $31.2B available against 42% uninsured deposits ($28.0B).

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates: FHN has beaten EPS in each of the last four quarters (6.2%, 12.0%, 13.8%, 7.4%). That consistency argues the current $2.15 full-year consensus is achievable and perhaps modestly conservative.

**Capital allocation is the defining feature.** The October 2025 $1.2B buyback authorization (up from $1.0B) has had ~$435M deployed life-to-date at an average ~$23.18, with ~$233M repurchased in 1Q26 alone. Weighted shares fell from ~517M (1Q25) to ~480M (1Q26). The dividend was raised to $0.17/quarter from $0.15. In March 2026 FHN issued $400M of 6.75% Series H preferred (Tier 1) and redeemed lower-coupon Series C/E, optimizing the capital stack while funding common repurchases below tangible book. Buying back stock at ~1.4x stated book and ~1.7x tangible book is accretive to per-share metrics and aligned with shareholders, but it is a maturing engine rather than a compounding one.

## Growth Analysis

FHN's forward growth is moderate and increasingly dependent on capital return rather than balance-sheet or revenue expansion. Loans grew just $221M QoQ (C&I growth offsetting CRE and consumer runoff); deposits fell $1.0B QoQ on brokered runoff. The organic balance sheet is roughly flat. NII growth (+$36M YoY) came from margin, not volume.

The critical forward indicator is estimate revisions, and the signal is neutral-to-flat. Next-year consensus is $2.35, +0.2% over 90 days. There is no estimate-revision velocity — the kind of +30% 90-day move that signals a genuine earnings inflection consensus is scrambling to catch. FHN's improvement is real but already in the numbers. The implied forward EPS growth from $2.15 to $2.35 (~9%) is respectable for a regional bank and will be driven substantially by continued buybacks plus modest operating leverage, not by an accelerating top line.

Growth optionality exists in three areas: (1) continued deposit-cost repricing sustaining NIM if the Fed holds or eases gently; (2) FHN Financial fixed-income revenue recovering with favorable bond-market conditions; (3) re-emergence as an M&A target in the consolidating Southeast — the TD deal would have valued the company around $25/share in 2022, and the standalone story has since outperformed peers by a wide margin (5-year TSR 222.7 vs. peer 152.7). None of these is a near-term, dated catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

At $24.81, FHN trades at:
- ~12.5x trailing/forward earnings (forward P/E 12.5 on $2.15 consensus)
- ~1.7x tangible book value ($14.34)
- ~1.4x stated book value ($17.72)
- 2.75% dividend yield

Against the regional-bank peer set, this is a peer-level-to-slightly-premium valuation, not a discount:

FHN trades slightly above the peer median P/E with a higher dividend yield. The premium is defensible given above-peer ROTCE (15.1% vs. typical low-teens) and superior multi-year TSR, but it is a premium, with no mispricing gap to close. This is not a trough-earnings inflection where trailing multiples mislead. Estimates are flat, not surging, so cycle-adjusted and trailing valuation converge, and the stock is fairly priced for what it delivers.

The analyst target range ($25.00 low, $30.00 high, $26.87 mean) implies ~8% upside to the mean and ~21% to the high. With estimates not rising, the consensus targets are not stale; they reflect the same modest-growth reality the price embeds. The combination of a peer-level multiple, flat estimate revisions, and modest implied upside is why valuation scores neutral rather than favorably. A low P/E alone does not earn a high valuation score; there must be a specific reason the market is wrong, and on the current evidence the market has FHN about right.

## Competitive Landscape

FHN's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving. Three durable edges: (1) a desirable, high-in-migration Southern/Gulf Coast footprint; (2) FHN Financial, a national fixed-income franchise providing fee diversification most regional peers cannot match; (3) specialized commercial verticals (mortgage warehouse, equipment finance, franchise finance, ABL). The 3Q25 outsourcing of retail brokerage/wealth management improved the economics of that fee stream (brokerage fees +12% YoY).

Credit metrics are running better than the post-2023 regional-bank fear case (18bp NCOs, 0.94% NPLs), and the efficiency ratio is improving — both signs of competent execution holding share. The industry backdrop in 2026 is favorable for NIM (deposit repricing) but features persistent deposit competition and sector-wide CRE/office concern. FHN is neither gaining share aggressively nor losing it; the trajectory is positive but incremental, which supports a score modestly above neutral rather than a high score.

## Risk Assessment

1. **CRE/office is the asymmetric risk.** CRE is $13.4B (21% of loans), of which office is ~$2.6B (20% of CRE) and multi-family ~$4.4B (33%). CRE carries the thinnest coverage in the book — ACL/loans only 1.21% and ALLL/NPL coverage just 64% — and CRE NPLs rose QoQ, driven by hospitality. Roughly $1.0B of CRE is graded substandard/doubtful/loss. Management reviews most CRE NPLs individually and concludes no specific reserve is required based on collateral values, a judgment that could prove optimistic if Southeast CRE values soften. This downside vector is not fully reflected in a 1.7x-tangible-book multiple.

2. **Financial-services industry concentration.** ~24% of the C&I book is sensitive to the financial sector (mortgage-company loans 13% + finance/insurance 11%, including ~$1.5B ABL to consumer-finance companies). This creates correlated risk if the financial sector stresses, relevant given the ongoing private-credit redemption-gating environment.

3. **Geographic/catastrophe risk.** Heavy Florida/Gulf Coast exposure brings hurricane risk and — flagged in unusual detail by management — a property-insurance affordability crisis that could erode the in-migration growth thesis underpinning the footprint premium.

4. **Funding and deposit competition.** NIM tailwinds depend on continued favorable deposit repricing amid competition that management expects to persist into 2026.

5. **Rate sensitivity.** Modestly asset-sensitive: +100bp → +1.9% NII; -100bp → -2.6% NII. A sharp downward rate move would pressure margin asymmetrically.

6. **FHN Financial cyclicality.** Fixed-income trading revenue fluctuates with bond-market conditions and declined in 2Q25 on less favorable conditions.

7. **Basel III Endgame.** The revised 2026 proposal is out for comment; FHN (>$50B, <$100B assets) is assessing capital-rule impact — a potential overhang on buyback capacity.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term caution into an otherwise calm name. Near-term ATM IV is 33.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 25.9% (rich by 7.9pp), and the term structure is in backwardation (33.8% near-term versus 28.2% at six months), which the market typically prices ahead of an identifiable near-term event. Put/call skew is balanced (0.1%) and put/call volume and OI ratios are extremely low (0.08 and 0.07), so there is no directional bearish positioning; the elevated near-term IV reflects general uncertainty (next earnings, Fed path, the June 24 stress-test cycle) rather than a specific bearish bet. ATM IV rose 3.8pp over the past month as the price rose 2.3%, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 0.14 to 0.07. The market expects a ±7.4% move into the next catalyst but is not positioned bearishly, consistent with a HOLD — neither a cheap-vol entry signal nor a warning of informed downside positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($30, ~21% upside)
Deposit-cost repricing continues to outrun asset-yield compression, holding NIM near or above 3.52%; credit stays benign with NCOs under 25bp and CRE concerns prove contained; the buyback continues retiring stock below tangible book, driving EPS toward $2.35+ in 2027; and FHN re-emerges as an M&A target in the consolidating Southeast, where a strategic acquirer pays a control premium to ~1.7x tangible book. At ~13x $2.35 EPS the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range.

### Bear Case ($20, ~19% downside)
Southeast CRE values soften, exposing the thin 64% ALLL/NPL office and hospitality coverage; provisions rise materially and force reserve builds that compress EPS; deposit competition forces FHN to pay up, reversing the NIM tailwind; and the multiple compresses back toward tangible book (~1.4x, ~$20) as the buyback-driven EPS story is recognized as decelerating. A Florida insurance-driven erosion of the in-migration thesis amplifies the de-rating.

### Base Case ($26, ~5% upside)
Operating performance holds roughly steady, buybacks deliver ~9% EPS growth to $2.35 in 2027, NIM is resilient but not expanding meaningfully, and the multiple stays around 12.5x. The stock tracks earnings growth plus the 2.75% dividend, a high-single-digit total return with no multiple re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the 2Q26 earnings release (mid-July 2026), with the June 24 Fed stress-test results and the Basel III Endgame comment process as interim inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions re-accelerate above +5% over 90 days, signaling the operating (not just buyback-driven) story is compounding.
- The stock pulls back toward ~1.4-1.5x tangible book (~$20-21) on no fundamental deterioration, restoring an asymmetric entry.
- A concrete M&A development or a NIM print expanding meaningfully above 3.60% with deposit costs still falling.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE/office credit deterioration: a quarter with CRE NPLs rising materially and a provision build that cuts EPS, or evidence the 64% ALLL/NPL coverage is forcing reserve catch-up.
- NIM contracts below ~3.40% on deposit-cost pressure (the funding tailwind reverses).
- Net charge-offs rise above ~35bp, signaling broader credit normalization beyond the average book.

## Conclusion

First Horizon is a well-run super-regional executing a disciplined, shareholder-aligned playbook: NIM expansion from deposit repricing, benign credit, positive operating leverage, and accretive buybacks below tangible book that have driven 27% price appreciation over the past year. Management's capital-stack optimization and strong multi-year TSR versus peers are credible evidence of competent stewardship. The score trajectory has been steadily improving (6.1 → 6.6 → 6.0 this read), and the underlying fundamentals confirm rather than reverse that trend.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and falsifiable: (1) the EPS engine is buyback-driven and decelerating, evidenced by estimate revisions that have flattened to 0.0% over 30 days and +1.0% over 90 days, leaving no inflection for consensus to chase; (2) the stock trades at a peer-level-to-premium multiple (~12.5x, ~1.7x tangible book) with no valuation discount to exploit, so upside to the consensus mean target is only ~5-8% and the targets are not stale because estimates are not rising; and (3) the CRE/office book carries genuinely thin reserve coverage (ALLL/NPL 64%) against rising hospitality and office NPLs, an asymmetric downside risk that a full multiple does not adequately compensate. We rate FHN HOLD, growth-hold, and would move to BUY on either a re-acceleration in estimates or a pullback toward tangible-book support.
