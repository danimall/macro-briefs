**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cardinal Health is one of three companies that move the majority of pharmaceuticals through the U.S. supply chain, a scale-driven oligopoly with McKesson and Cencora. The investment story over the past eighteen months has been a strategic transformation under pressure from activist Elliott Management: the company pivoted into higher-margin specialty care delivery (oncology via Navista, gastroenterology and urology via The Specialty Alliance) and home health (the ADS diabetes-supply business), while running a cost program and disciplined buybacks. The result is genuine operating momentum — three consecutive non-GAAP EPS guidance raises in four months to $10.70–$10.80, Q3 FY26 non-GAAP EPS +35%, nine-month non-GAAP EPS +36% — and a stock that has risen 41% over the trailing twelve months to $221.

The problem for a new buyer is that most of this is now in the price. At ~21x the current-year non-GAAP EPS estimate of $10.77, CAH trades at a premium to McKesson (20.2x) and roughly in line with Cencora (21.1x on a higher-quality earnings base) despite carrying the heaviest idiosyncratic risk load of the three: CVS at 30% of revenue, a reopened West Virginia opioid case, a DOJ civil investigative demand into its MSO model, state legislation threatening corporate ownership of physician practices, and a $184M goodwill impairment that represents the first visible crack in the specialty roll-up. The trailing 33x GAAP P/E overstates expensiveness; the right denominator is the ~$10.77 non-GAAP number, but even on that basis the valuation cushion is thin.

The widening gap between GAAP and non-GAAP earnings is driven by recurring share-based compensation tied to The Specialty Alliance physician units ($216M of expense in nine months, against a $1.265B mark-to-market liability). Management excludes this from adjusted EPS; it is a recurring economic cost of the MSO strategy rather than a one-time item. We rate CAH a HOLD with a constructive lean. The business is improving and the activist thesis has worked, but the risk/reward at $221 does not clear the bar for a fresh long. We would accumulate on a pullback toward the high-$180s, where the stock traded as recently as early May.

## Company Overview

Cardinal Health operates two reportable segments plus an "Other" bucket, fiscal year ending June 30:

- **Pharmaceutical & Specialty Solutions ("Pharma")** — roughly 92% of revenue ($204.6B FY25). Distributes branded, generic, specialty and OTC pharmaceuticals, provides manufacturer services, and now houses the rapidly expanding MSO platforms. Core profit drivers are the generics program run through the Red Oak Sourcing joint venture with CVS (extended to June 2029), branded distribution-service-agreement fees, specialty distribution, and BioPharma Solutions.
- **Global Medical Products & Distribution ("GMPD")** — $12.6B FY25. Manufactures and sources Cardinal-brand medical/surgical/lab products and distributes national brands. Structurally weak: goodwill has been fully written off ($1.2B FY23, $675M FY24), margins are chronically thin, and the segment is now absorbing tariff costs. Q3 FY26 segment profit fell 36% on roughly $200M of IEEPA tariffs.
- **"Other"** — Nuclear & Precision Health, at-Home Solutions (Edgepark plus ADS), and OptiFreight Logistics. Smallest but fastest-growing (+19% FY25, +31–34% in Q3 FY26) and higher-margin.

The business is enormous, low-margin and high-velocity: FY25 revenue of $222.6B produced a gross margin near 3.7% and a net margin near 0.7%. The moat is logistics scale, manufacturer relationships, and generic-sourcing leverage. The strategic differentiation is moving downstream into specialty care delivery and the home, chasing margin and the industry shift away from the hospital.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $181B (FY22) → $205B (FY23) → $226.8B (FY24) → $222.6B (FY25, −2%). The FY25 decline is fully explained by the loss of the OptumRx distribution contracts, which represented 17% of FY24 revenue and rolled off at the end of June 2024. Stripping that out, the underlying business is growing: Q3 FY26 revenue +11% to $60.9B, nine-month +17% to $190.6B, driven by branded and specialty pharma plus the acquisitions.

**Earnings quality is the central issue.** FY25 non-GAAP operating earnings rose 15% to $2.79B and non-GAAP EPS rose 9% to $8.24. Momentum accelerated into FY26: Q3 non-GAAP EPS $3.17 (+35%), nine-month $8.35 (+36%). GAAP diverged sharply: Q3 GAAP EPS was $1.69, down 20%, on the goodwill impairment and higher Specialty Alliance share compensation. The GAAP/non-GAAP gap is widening for three reasons: acquisition-related amortization, recurring acquisition-related cash and share-based comp tied to the physician units ($243M in nine months versus $20M a year prior), and impairments. The share-comp piece deserves scrutiny: at $216M over nine months against a liability-classified obligation that re-marks each quarter, this is a recurring cost of the roll-up strategy, not a clean one-time exclusion. Valuing CAH purely on management's non-GAAP figure overstates economic earnings by a non-trivial amount.

**Earnings surprise track record** supports confidence in forward estimates: four straight beats, by 1.1%, 15.9%, 9.4% and 12.6%. The 12.6% beat in the March quarter is the relevant data point: visibility into the core business is good.

**Balance sheet — negative equity is a buyback artifact, not distress.** Stockholders' equity is −$2.78B (FY25), −$2.67B at Q3 FY26, reflecting roughly $7.2B of cumulative treasury stock as share count fell from 327M to ~235M. The Altman Z-Score of 4.27 sits in the safe zone; covenant compliance on the ≤3.75x net leverage test is intact. Debt rose to ~$8.9B to fund the acquisition spree, lifting nine-month interest expense 91% to $269M. Cash was $3.9B; the current ratio of 0.94 is normal for a distributor running on negative working capital.

**Cash flow:** FY25 operating cash flow $2.4B, FCF $1.85B, depressed by the OptumRx working-capital unwind and $798M of opioid payments. Nine-month FY26 OCF rebounded to $3.5B, and management guides FY26 adjusted FCF of $3.3–3.7B. Capital allocation: ~$5.3B of acquisitions in FY25, $1.0B of buybacks in nine months FY26 against $1.8B remaining on the authorization, and a modest dividend ($2.04 annualized, ~1% yield, low single-digit growth). The dividend is well covered but is not the reason to own this stock.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on two legs. The first is the recovery and resilience of the core distribution and generics business, which is throwing off the operating leverage behind the guidance raises. The second is the specialty/MSO pivot: Specialty Networks ($1.2B), ION oncology ($1.1B), GIA gastroenterology ($2.8B), Urology America (~$370M), ADS diabetes supplies ($1.1B), and Solaris Health urology ($1.9B in November 2025). The Specialty Alliance now consolidates GIA, Urology America and Solaris into a multi-specialty MSO that CAH owns ~76% of; Navista is the oncology MSO. The strategic logic is sound: care is migrating out of hospitals into physician offices and the home, and CAH is buying a seat at the higher-margin end of that flow.

Estimate revisions are positive but decelerating: current-year EPS +4.5% over 90 days and flat over 30 days, next-year +3.6% over 90 days and +0.2% over 30 days. This is steady upward drift, not the +30% velocity that signals a forced re-rating. The growth is real and broad enough across segments to be durable, but the consensus has largely caught up to it. Next-year EPS of $12.00 implies ~11% growth off the FY26 midpoint, a reasonable but not spectacular trajectory for a name now trading at a premium to peers.

The fastest-growing pieces — Other (+31–34%) and specialty — are the right places to be, but they are still small relative to the $200B-plus distribution base, which limits how much they move the consolidated growth rate over the next twelve months.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline 33x trailing P/E is misleading because it uses GAAP EPS of $6.45, which is depressed by impairments and acquisition charges. The cleaner lens is ~21x the current-year non-GAAP estimate, or ~18.5x the next-year estimate.

Against peers, this is not cheap. McKesson trades at 20.2x and Cencora at 21.1x. CAH at ~21x sits at or above both, despite carrying a heavier idiosyncratic risk load and a lower-quality earnings base (its non-GAAP number excludes a recurring share-comp cost that the peers do not carry to the same degree). Adjusting for the Specialty Alliance share comp, CAH's true economic multiple is somewhat higher than the headline 21x suggests.

There is no specific, identifiable mispricing here that argues the market is wrong. The estimate revisions are positive but modest, the stock is up 41% over twelve months, and it trades below the consensus mean target of $245 and just above the low target of $215. The signal that would justify a higher valuation score — a compressed forward multiple below the growth rate, or revision velocity of +30% — is absent. On the other side, the multiple is not stretched to a level that demands an AVOID; ~18.5x next-year earnings for an oligopolist with double-digit specialty growth is defensible.

The valuation is fair-to-slightly-full. The stock has re-rated as the activist thesis played out, and that re-rating now prices in continued execution. The asymmetry from $221 is roughly balanced: meaningful downside if CVS or MSO risks surface, modest upside as the consensus target migrates higher on continued beats. We score valuation 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

CAH operates in a stable three-firm distribution oligopoly where scale and generic-sourcing leverage (Red Oak) are decisive and switching is costly. Its competitive trajectory is mixed. On distribution it is holding share and the core business is performing well, but the OptumRx loss in FY24 demonstrated that even a Big 3 distributor can lose a transformational contract. On specialty/MSO, CAH is gaining ground against a less consolidated field, building a differentiated downstream platform that the peers have pursued less aggressively. On GMPD, the position is weakest, competing against Medline and Owens & Minor in a tariff-exposed, fully-impaired manufacturing business.

The net trajectory is modestly positive: the specialty pivot is widening the moat at the higher-margin end even as GMPD drags. The Navista/ION impairment is a reminder that the roll-up is not executing flawlessly, and the customer-concentration overhang caps how strongly we can score competitive position. We carry it at 6.8, consistent with the prior 7.0 trajectory and reflecting a stable-to-slightly-improving competitive position with one large structural vulnerability (CVS) that prevents a higher mark.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk load here is heavier than for most distributors and not fully priced:

1. **CVS concentration — 30% of revenue, 26% of receivables; top 5 = 43%.** The OptumRx episode proves this is a live risk, not a theoretical one. The Red Oak generics JV runs to June 2029. Any adverse CVS contract change would be severe and is the single largest point of fragility.
2. **Opioid liability** — $4.3B accrued at Q3 FY26, payments through 2038, monitor through 2027. The Cabell County/Huntington WV case, previously won at trial, was vacated by the 4th Circuit in October 2025 and remanded, reopening a tail. Roughly 92 private suits and 17 class actions remain.
3. **MSO execution and regulatory risk** — the $184M Navista/ION impairment is the first crack in the roll-up. A DOJ civil investigative demand (November 2023) targets a 2022 rheumatology MSO/GPO deal under Anti-Kickback/False Claims statutes. Oregon has enacted, and other states have proposed, laws restricting corporate ownership of physician practices, a direct threat to the strategy's core structure.
4. **Tariffs** — ~$200M IEEPA tariffs paid, mostly in GMPD, which drove the 36% segment-profit decline. The Supreme Court ruled IEEPA tariffs unlawful in February 2026, but refunds are uncertain and the government substituted Section 122/301/232 tariffs.
5. **Drug-pricing policy** — a Most-Favored-Nation executive order could pressure branded pharma economics; GLP-1s drive revenue but contribute minimal profit.
6. **Tax** — an IRS NOPA (February 2026) asserts ~$160M plus interest on the 2017 Patient Recovery restructuring.

The risk score declined from 6.5 to 5.5 in the prior report and we hold it at 5.5: the catalysts above are specific and several (WV remand, DOJ CID, state MSO laws) carry asymmetric downside that the current valuation does not appear to discount.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility of 43.5% sits well above one-year realized of 29.6% and the term structure is in backwardation (43.5% at one week versus 26.5% at three months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress concentrated in the very front end. The put/call skew of 19.2% shows elevated demand for downside protection. This front-week spike likely reflects event-hedging around a near-term catalyst rather than durable concern; the one-month and three-month tenors are far calmer (27.6% and 26.5%) with near-balanced skew. ATM IV has collapsed from 80% to 43.5% over the past month as the stock rose 18%, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 1.60 to 0.52, indicating bearish positioning has unwound as the price recovered. Short interest is low at 3.9% of float (3.1 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The options picture is mildly cautionary at the front end but not a fundamental warning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($245–275):** The core distribution and generics business sustains double-digit profit growth, the specialty/MSO platforms scale into higher-margin, faster-growing earnings, and Other (Nuclear, OptiFreight, at-Home) compounds at 20%-plus. Management delivers a fourth and fifth guidance raise, FY27 EPS pushes toward $13.50, and the consensus target migrates to the high-$200s. Continued buybacks against the remaining authorization accelerate per-share growth on a shrinking share count. CVS renews and MSO regulatory risks prove manageable. At 20x a $13.50 forward number, the stock reaches the mid-$270s.

**Bear Case ($170–185):** A CVS contract change or renegotiation pressures the largest revenue block; the MSO roll-up requires further impairments and runs into state corporate-practice restrictions and the DOJ inquiry; the WV opioid case reopens material liability; and tariffs continue to squeeze GMPD. The market re-rates the stock back toward peer multiples (~18x) on a lower-quality earnings base and the recurring share-comp cost becomes a focus, taking the stock toward the high-$170s, roughly where it traded in early May.

The base case sits between: continued execution at a decelerating revision pace, a fair multiple, and a stock that grinds toward $235–240 over twelve months as estimates drift up, with downside risk if any of the idiosyncratic catalysts surface.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (constructive lean), 12-month horizon.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$180s ($185–190) on no fundamental deterioration, which would restore valuation cushion and push next-year P/E below ~16x.
- Evidence the MSO platform is scaling cleanly — two consecutive quarters of specialty/MSO segment profit growth with no further impairments, plus benign resolution of the DOJ CID.
- A CVS contract renewal or extension that removes the largest concentration overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any adverse CVS contract development, or evidence of customer attrition in the top 5.
- A further material MSO goodwill impairment, or adverse state corporate-practice-of-medicine rulings that impair the roll-up structure.
- A guidance cut or a miss that reverses the three-raise momentum, or material adverse movement in the WV opioid case.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next reassessment at Q4 FY26 earnings (early August 2026), which will set initial FY27 guidance and reveal whether the third guidance raise was delivered.

## Conclusion

Cardinal Health has done the hard work: the activist-driven specialty pivot has produced real, broad-based operating momentum, the guidance raises, and a tripling of the stock over several years. The business is financially healthy despite the optically alarming negative book equity, which is a buyback artifact rather than a solvency signal. But the easy money in the re-rating has been made. At ~21x current-year and ~18.5x next-year non-GAAP EPS, the stock trades at a premium to McKesson and roughly in line with Cencora while carrying a heavier idiosyncratic risk load and a lower-quality earnings base where a recurring $200M-plus annual share-comp cost is excluded from the headline number.

The estimate-revision trend is positive but modest, not the velocity that would force a re-rating, and the stock sits below the consensus mean target. There is no identifiable mispricing that argues the market is wrong at $221. We rate CAH a HOLD with a constructive lean and an overall score of 6.1, consistent with the gently declining trajectory from prior reports. The right entry is on weakness toward the high-$180s, where the risk/reward turns clearly favorable. For existing holders, the thesis remains intact and the business is improving; we see no reason to sell, but also no reason to add at the current price.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report originally framed CAH as trading at a "meaningful premium to Cencora" on a "higher-quality earnings base," but Cencora's P/E is 21.1x — at or below CAH's ~21x — so CAH is roughly in line with, not below, Cencora; the report also overstated valuation tension by saying the stock trades "within $14 of the consensus mean target of $245" when at $221.43 it is ~$24 (~11%) below the mean target, leaving more upside-to-target than stated.
