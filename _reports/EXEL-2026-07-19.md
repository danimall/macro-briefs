**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Exelixis is a profitable, single-franchise oncology company built around cabozantinib (CABOMETYX), which generates roughly 91% of total revenue. The business is financially pristine: no debt, ~$1.4B in cash and marketable securities, 96% gross margins, ~36% ROE, and roughly $876M of free cash flow in FY2025. Management has returned nearly all of that cash to shareholders, repurchasing $2.59B of stock since March 2023 and retiring 86.8M shares (from ~318M to ~251M). This biotech self-funds a large pipeline while shrinking its share count aggressively.

The equity is a barbell of two binary outcomes, both resolving substantially within 2026. First, the CABOMETYX composition-of-matter patent expires August 14, 2026; the defense against generic entry rests on later formulation/method patents that a Delaware court upheld (setting earliest MSN generic entry at January 15, 2030), but that ruling is on appeal at the Federal Circuit, with oral argument held June 4, 2026 and a consolidated trial set for November 2, 2026. Second, the company's successor asset, zanzalintinib, has an NDA accepted for metastatic colorectal cancer with a PDUFA date of December 3, 2026, following a Phase 3 (STELLAR-303) that met its ITT overall-survival endpoint but failed its second co-primary endpoint (OS in patients without liver metastases) at final analysis in June 2026.

After a 24% run over the trailing year to ~$56, near its 52-week high of $57.57 and above the analyst mean target of $50.93, the risk/reward has flattened. Estimate revisions have turned negative over 30 days (current-year down 1.2%, next-year down 2.1%) despite a positive 90-day trend, and Truist downgraded the stock to Hold in late June with a $54 target. The fundamentals remain sound and the balance sheet keeps improving, but with the stock trading through the mean target ahead of two unresolved binaries, we move to HOLD. The outcome distribution is genuinely two-sided and both key catalysts land within the next six months.

## Company Overview

Exelixis develops and commercializes oncology therapeutics. Its anchor product, cabozantinib, is a multi-kinase inhibitor (targeting MET, AXL, VEGF receptors, and RET) sold as CABOMETYX (tablets) and COMETRIQ (capsules). The lead indication and primary revenue driver is first-line advanced renal cell carcinoma (RCC) in combination with Bristol-Myers Squibb's nivolumab (Opdivo). CABOMETYX is also approved in hepatocellular carcinoma, radioiodine-refractory differentiated thyroid cancer, and, since March 2025, pancreatic and extra-pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (NET), the newest growth driver.

The commercial model is capital-light. Exelixis sells CABOMETYX directly in the U.S. and licenses ex-U.S. rights to Ipsen (ex-U.S./ex-Japan) and Takeda (Japan), collecting royalties (~$166M from Ipsen in FY2025) and milestones. Two smaller discovery products (COTELLIC via Genentech; MINNEBRO via Daiichi Sankyo) are licensed out. The company runs as a single reportable segment and outsources all manufacturing. A legacy obligation routes a 3% royalty on all cabozantinib sales to Royalty Pharma; the U.S. royalty reverts to GSK in September 2026.

The strategic thesis is a franchise transition: converting cabozantinib's cash flows into a successor franchise led by zanzalintinib, a next-generation oral TKI, supplemented by internal and partnered biotherapeutics and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs). Whether that transition succeeds is the central question for the equity.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet and cash-generation profile are exceptional for a biotech of this scale.

- **Revenue trajectory:** Total revenue grew from $1.83B (FY2023) to $2.17B (FY2024) to $2.32B (FY2025, +7%). Net product revenue grew a stronger +17% in FY2025 to $2.12B, with CABOMETYX up ~18% on roughly 16% unit-volume growth (RCC refills plus the NET launch) and a ~1% net price uplift. Total-revenue growth trailed product growth because license revenue fell 39% ($349M to $214M) on milestone timing — FY2024 included a one-time $150M Ipsen commercial milestone. That decline is a mix/timing artifact, not a deterioration in the franchise.
- **Profitability:** Net income rose to $782.6M (diluted EPS $2.78) in FY2025 from $521.3M in FY2024. ROE is ~36%, ROA ~27.5%, net margin ~34%, and FCF ~$876M. R&D declined 9% to $825M (fewer milestones, less cabozantinib spend), while SG&A rose 5% on zanza pre-launch and NET sales-force build-out.
- **Q1 2026:** Total revenue $610.8M (+10%), net product $555.0M (+8%), net income $210.5M ($0.79 GAAP / $0.87 non-GAAP diluted EPS). EPS is being amplified by a falling share count.
- **Balance sheet:** Altman Z-Score 5.23 (deep safe zone), current ratio 3.56, D/E 0.32, ~$1.4B cash/securities, no debt.
- **Tax:** OBBBA (July 2025) delivered a $191M federal cash tax benefit in FY2025 through restored domestic R&E expensing. Exelixis qualifies for the IRA small-biotech exception through IPAY 2028.

Earnings quality is high and the beat record is consistent: four straight beats over the last four quarters (10.1%, 7.0%, 9.3%, 14.0%). FCF conversion comfortably exceeds net income. The one caveat on earnings quality is that per-share growth is partly manufactured by buybacks — diluted share count fell from ~288M (Q1 2025) to ~267M (Q1 2026). Underlying product-revenue growth is healthy but decelerating to a high-single/low-double-digit rate as the RCC franchise matures, with NET now the incremental driver.

FY2026 guidance calls for total revenue of $2.525–2.625B, net product revenue of $2.325–2.425B, R&D of $875–925M (rising as zanza trials scale), SG&A of $575–625M, and a 21–23% tax rate. Guidance explicitly excludes any zanza launch revenue, leaving un-modeled upside if the December PDUFA is favorable.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is solid but decelerating and increasingly dependent on the successor asset. The CABOMETYX RCC franchise is maturing; NET is the incremental in-line driver, and a 3% WAC increase took effect in January 2026. Product revenue in the mid-single to low-double digits is a reasonable base-case trajectory for 2026 absent a zanza contribution.

The durable growth case rests on zanzalintinib, which management positions as a next-generation cabozantinib. The clinical program is broad: STELLAR-303 (metastatic CRC, with atezolizumab), STELLAR-304 (non-clear-cell RCC, with nivolumab, topline expected H2 2026 — the next key catalyst), STELLAR-311 (NET vs. everolimus), STELLAR-316 (CRC MRD+ with Natera's Signatera, mid-2026), STELLAR-201 (meningioma, initiated April 2026), STELLAR-202 (squamous NSCLC, H2 2026), and a STELLAR-002 CRPC cohort. Merck's collaboration in RCC (LITESPARK-033/-034 with WELIREG/belzutifan), where Exelixis retains all global commercial rights, provides external validation.

The June 2026 STELLAR-303 readout complicates the growth thesis. The trial met its ITT overall-survival endpoint (HR 0.80, p=0.0045), which supports the NDA, but the second dual primary endpoint — OS in patients without liver metastases — did not reach statistical significance at final analysis (interim HR 0.79, p=0.0875). Approval may still be granted on the ITT win, but a narrower-than-hoped label would cap the commercial opportunity in first-line CRC. The mixed estimate-revision picture (see Executive Summary) is not the accelerating upgrade signal that would flag a clear turn in the growth trajectory.

Balance of evidence: growth is real, self-funded, and diversified across a genuine pipeline, but it is decelerating in the core franchise and the successor asset just delivered a mixed readout. We score growth prospects at the middle of the range.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$56, EXEL trades at ~18.4x trailing and forward earnings (trailing and forward P/E converge given the flat near-term earnings profile). On consensus current-year EPS of $3.53 and next-year of $3.96, the forward multiple is ~15.8x and ~14.1x respectively.

Against biotech peers, the multiple looks cheap on the surface: the peer median P/E is 39.8x, and several comparables (BBIO, AXSM, IONS) are unprofitable. But this is a situation where a low multiple should be read as a discount for a specific reason rather than a buy signal. The market is applying a patent-cliff discount to a single-franchise company whose composition-of-matter protection expires in weeks and whose defense is under appeal. The discount exists because the terminal value is genuinely uncertain.

The valuation case is two-sided. If the Federal Circuit upholds the 2030 generic-entry date and zanza approval proceeds with a workable label, ~15x forward earnings on a durable, cash-rich franchise with continued buybacks is inexpensive, and the un-modeled zanza revenue provides upside. If the appeal is reversed or the December PDUFA disappoints, the earnings power the multiple is applied against becomes questionable, and the "cheap" multiple re-rates lower. With the stock trading at $55.92 versus a mean analyst target of $50.93 and median of $50.00, the current price is already above consensus fair value, so the asymmetry that would justify a high valuation score is absent. The analyst-target-as-floor logic does not apply here because estimate revisions are flat-to-negative and the stock sits above, not below, consensus.

We score valuation at 5.5: cheaper than peers on headline multiples, but fairly priced once the binary risks are underwritten, with no clear catalyst-driven mispricing at the current level.

## Competitive Landscape

CABOMETYX competes in intensely contested tumor settings (RCC, HCC, and NET) against other TKIs and immuno-oncology combinations. Its durability rests on the nivolumab combination in first-line RCC and the newer NET franchise. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the franchise level: the RCC opportunity is maturing, and the entire value case depends on landing a successor before generic entry.

Zanzalintinib is the mechanism for extending the moat, and the Merck partnership signals external confidence in the RCC opportunity. But the mixed STELLAR-303 readout tempers confidence in how much competitive ground zanza can hold in first-line CRC, a large and crowded indication. The pipeline is not de-risked: the company discontinued XL495 (PKMYT1), STELLAR-305 (head & neck), XB064, and XB033, evidence that the portfolio carries real attrition. The ADC/biotherapeutics programs (XL309, XB010, XB628, XB371) are early and speculative.

Trajectory rather than level drives the score. The competitive position is being actively defended through litigation and a successor asset, but neither defense is yet secured, and the core franchise is past its growth peak. We score competitive position at 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CABOMETYX patent cliff / generic litigation — the dominant risk.** The composition-of-matter '473 patent expires August 14, 2026. The defense rests on later formulation/method patents ('439/'440/'015), which the Delaware District Court upheld in October 2024. MSN has appealed to the Federal Circuit, and Exelixis dropped its cross-appeal. A consolidated trial (MSN, Azurity, and others) is set for November 2, 2026. Sun (Dec 2025) and Biocon (Jul 2025) settled; Handa (505(b)(2) capsules), Teva, and Cipla remain. With ~91% of revenue concentrated in one molecule, a Federal Circuit reversal would materially accelerate the cliff and is the single largest binary risk to the equity.

**2. Zanzalintinib clinical/regulatory risk (elevated after June 2026).** The STELLAR-303 second-endpoint failure clouds the December 3, 2026 PDUFA and the breadth of any label. STELLAR-304 topline in H2 2026 is the next data catalyst.

**3. U.S. drug-pricing/policy.** IRA Medicare negotiation (small-biotech exception through IPAY 2028), MFN pricing initiatives, 340B disputes, and a Section 232 proclamation (April 2026) threatening up to 100% pharma tariffs.

**4. Collaboration and customer concentration.** Ex-U.S. revenue depends on Ipsen/Takeda; zanza RCC upside depends partly on Merck. Top customers are concentrated (Cencora 22%, McKesson 18%, CVS 16%, Accredo 10%).

Both dominant risks are specific, dated, and asymmetric, and neither is fully priced given the stock sits above the mean analyst target. That supports a low risk-factors score of 4.5.

Near-term ATM IV is 38.5% versus 1-year historical vol of 39.4% (fair, slightly cheap), IV percentile is a low 6% within the current chain, and put/call skew is -9.2% (call-heavy) with a put/call volume ratio of just 0.19 and OI ratio of 0.02. The combination suggests a market that is calm now but pricing volatility into the H2 catalyst cluster.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$62):** The Federal Circuit upholds the 2030 generic-entry date, removing the near-term cliff overhang. The FDA approves zanza in CRC on December 3 with a usable label, adding un-modeled 2027 revenue on top of guidance that already excludes it. STELLAR-304 delivers positive nccRCC data in H2 2026. Continued buybacks against the ~$1.4B cash position and ~$876M annual FCF compound EPS. On $3.96+ next-year EPS re-rated toward the high teens with cliff risk resolved, the stock reaches the $62 analyst high.

**Bear Case (~$41):** The Federal Circuit reverses, pulling generic entry forward and forcing a re-rating of terminal earnings power. Or the December PDUFA yields a narrow label / delay that undercuts the zanza commercial thesis. With ~91% single-molecule concentration, either outcome compresses the multiple and the earnings base. The low analyst target of $41 (~27% below current) reflects this scenario.

**Base Case (~$52):** The 2030 date holds on appeal (or at minimum is not reversed before year-end), zanza wins approval on a serviceable-but-narrower label, and product revenue grows in the high-single digits with buybacks supporting EPS. The stock trades roughly in line with the current level and analyst mean, with the binaries partially de-risked but no dramatic re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD, classified as uncertain: the outcome distribution is genuinely two-sided, both key catalysts resolve within six months, and the stock trades above consensus fair value.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Favorable Federal Circuit ruling affirming the January 15, 2030 generic-entry date, removing the near-term cliff overhang.
- FDA approval of zanza in CRC on/around December 3, 2026 with a broad, commercially meaningful label.
- Positive STELLAR-304 nccRCC topline in H2 2026, plus a turn in estimate-revision velocity back to positive on both 30- and 90-day windows.
- A pullback toward the low-$40s that restores asymmetry against a $52 base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Federal Circuit reversal or adverse November 2, 2026 trial outcome that accelerates the generic cliff.
- A zanza PDUFA rejection, delay, or restrictive label that undercuts the successor-franchise thesis.
- Product-revenue growth decelerating below ~5% YoY, signaling the core franchise is rolling over faster than the successor can offset.

**Reassessment timeline:** The natural checkpoints are the STELLAR-304 topline (H2 2026), the November 2, 2026 consolidated trial, and the December 3, 2026 PDUFA. The Federal Circuit appeal ruling could arrive at any point following the June 4 oral argument. We would revisit the rating on any of these events and at the next earnings release.

## Conclusion

Exelixis is a financially fortress-like, buyback-heavy oncology company with a maturing cash-cow franchise and a genuine pipeline, trading at a discount to unprofitable biotech peers. The score trajectory has been positive (6.6 → 7.1 → 7.1), driven by improving financial health and management-quality reads, and the balance sheet continues to strengthen. But two things have changed since the prior BUY: the STELLAR-303 second-endpoint failure in June 2026 introduced real ambiguity into the December PDUFA and the successor-franchise thesis, and the stock has run 24% to trade above the analyst mean and median targets ahead of unresolved binaries. Estimate revisions have turned modestly negative over 30 days, and Truist downgraded to Hold.

The fundamentals do not justify an AVOID: this is a high-quality, cash-generative business with no debt and disciplined capital return. But at ~$56, above consensus fair value and with two asymmetric binaries (the patent-cliff appeal and the zanza label) both resolving within six months, the risk/reward is balanced rather than favorable. We move to HOLD and would look to upgrade on a favorable appeal ruling, a clean zanza approval, or a pullback that restores asymmetry.
