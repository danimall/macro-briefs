## Executive Summary

Cheniere Energy is the largest U.S. LNG exporter and second-largest globally, operating a toll-road business model with 90%+ of production contracted under long-term take-or-pay agreements through the mid-2030s. The stock trades at approximately 10.5–11x forward EV/EBITDA—a reasonable multiple for a company with $290.6B in contracted revenue backlog, 27% revenue growth in 2025, and a trajectory toward $6.75B–$7.25B adjusted EBITDA in 2026. The recent pullback from its $300.89 high to ~$260 creates a more attractive entry point while the fundamental thesis has only strengthened.

The current geopolitical environment is generating a structural tailwind: Qatar LNG supply disruption from the Iran conflict, the EU's legislated ban on Russian LNG (with long-term contract imports outlawed from January 1, 2027 and short-term imports banned from April 25, 2026), and rising Asian demand from data center power requirements are converging to make U.S. LNG infrastructure the marginal global supply source for the next decade. Cheniere's ~49 mtpa of expansion capacity (which would nearly double current operations) positions it to capture this demand growth while maintaining its fee-based, low-commodity-risk profile. The $10B buyback authorization through 2030 signals management's confidence in sustained free cash flow generation.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates two liquefaction and export facilities: Sabine Pass (Louisiana, 30+ mtpa) and Corpus Christi (Texas, 30+ mtpa including trains under construction). The business model is structured as a toll-road: customers pay a fixed fee per MMBtu (inflation-adjusted annually) regardless of whether they take delivery, plus a variable fee at 115% of Henry Hub that passes through feedstock costs. This structure eliminates most commodity price and volume risk from Cheniere's economics.

As of year-end 2025, approximately 30 long-term counterparties (energy majors, utilities, trading houses) are under contract, with no single customer exceeding 10% of revenue. The company has exported over 4,610 cumulative cargoes to 40+ countries. Corpus Christi Stage 3 (Trains 5-7) is 94% complete with first LNG from Train 5 produced in February 2026 and Trains 5-7 expected to reach substantial completion by year-end 2026. Midscale Trains 8&9 are 32% complete for 2H 2028 delivery.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings:** FY2025 revenue was $20.0B (+27% YoY), operating income $9.1B (+49% YoY, 45.6% margin), and net income $5.3B (+64% YoY). EPS of $24.13 reflects both operating improvement and significant derivative mark-to-market gains ($3.6B pre-tax from IPM agreements). Operating cash flow was $5.5B; free cash flow after approximately $2.6B in growth capital investment was approximately $2.5B.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $22.8B ($22.5B long-term + $306M current). Using Cheniere stockholders' equity of $7.9B, debt/equity is approximately 2.9x—high in absolute terms but characteristic of infrastructure assets with contracted cash flows. This is project-financed debt backed by take-or-pay contracts extending through the 2030s and 2040s. Credit ratings were upgraded to BBB+/BBB in 2025 (S&P and Fitch), confirming the market's assessment that the leverage is appropriate for the asset profile. Available liquidity is $8.8B (cash plus undrawn credit facilities).

**Capital Return:** In 2025, Cheniere returned $3.2B to shareholders ($2.7B buybacks at avg $221.55, $451M dividends). The share count declined from 251M (2022) to 220M (2025)—a 12% reduction. The February 2026 $10B buyback authorization through 2030 represents ~18% of current market cap and signals management expects sustained excess cash generation beyond growth capex requirements.

**Earnings Quality Caveat:** The $2.9B net derivative asset on the balance sheet (from IPM mark-to-market) creates GAAP earnings volatility. Adjusted EBITDA ($6.75B–$7.25B guided for 2026) is the correct operating metric. Quarterly GAAP EPS can deviate substantially from consensus due to derivative mark-to-market swings, making quarter-to-quarter results unpredictable. Investors should focus on distributable cash flow and adjusted EBITDA rather than GAAP net income.

**2026 Guidance:** $6.75B–$7.25B adjusted EBITDA, 51-53M tonnes LNG production. With Stage 3 ramp continuing through 2026, production volumes will step higher through the year.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term capacity additions:** Stage 3 Trains 5-7 completing through 2026 add incremental volumes to the contracted base. Trains 8&9 (expected 2H 2028) add ~5 mtpa. These are under fixed-price Bechtel EPC contracts, limiting cost overrun risk.

**Long-term expansion pipeline:** ~49 mtpa of potential expansion (SPL Expansion ~20 mtpa, CCL Expansion ~24 mtpa) would nearly double current capacity. FERC applications are pending/filed. These are brownfield expansions leveraging existing infrastructure, pipelines, and permits—a cost and timeline advantage competitors cannot replicate.

**Demand drivers:** Global LNG demand is forecast to grow from ~410 mtpa to 671 mtpa by 2040 (WoodMac). Three specific structural drivers: (1) EU ban on Russian LNG by 2027 requiring replacement supply, (2) Asian data center/AI power demand growth requiring incremental gas generation, (3) MENA region industrialization. The current Iran conflict is additionally disrupting Qatar's supply, accelerating demand for U.S. volumes.

**Estimate revision momentum:** Available consensus data indicate modest positive revisions to forward EPS estimates over recent months, though the magnitude of revisions varies by source and timeframe. The trailing P/E of ~10.8x (on $24.13 EPS inflated by derivatives) should be contextualized against forward consensus estimates that are materially lower due to expected derivative normalization.

## Valuation Assessment

At $260.56, with approximately 210.2M shares outstanding, Cheniere's equity market capitalization is approximately $54.7B. Adding $22.8B in total debt and subtracting approximately $1.6B in cash and restricted cash yields a simplified enterprise value of approximately $76B. This implies:

- ~10.5x 2026 guided adjusted EBITDA at the top end ($7.25B)
- ~10.9x at the midpoint ($7.0B)
- ~10.8x trailing EPS ($24.13, derivative-inflated)
- ~17.8x current year consensus EPS (~$14.61)

Available analyst consensus data suggest a mean price target in the range of $274–$276, with a low estimate near $232 and a high near $301–$322. The majority of covering analysts maintain Buy or equivalent ratings.

The key valuation argument: this is a company with a $290.6B contracted revenue backlog, investment-grade credit, 90%+ contracted volumes, and a structural demand tailwind lasting through at least 2030. Comparable infrastructure assets (pipelines, regulated utilities) trade at similar or higher multiples with lower growth profiles. The current multiple does not adequately price in (a) the expansion optionality (~49 mtpa potential), (b) the $10B buyback reducing the share count by an additional 15-20% by 2030, or (c) the geopolitical premium for being the world's most reliable incremental LNG supplier.

At $260, investors are paying approximately 10.5–11x EV/EBITDA for an infrastructure business with multi-decade contracted cash flows, which compares to midstream peers trading at 8-12x with less growth and lower contract quality.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's competitive position is exceptional:

1. **Scale and first-mover advantage:** Largest U.S. exporter, second globally only to QatarEnergy (whose supply is currently disrupted).
2. **Take-or-pay contract structure:** Volume risk eliminated. Customers pay whether they lift cargo or not.
3. **Henry Hub feedstock advantage:** U.S. natural gas at ~$3.50/MMBtu vs. international prices of $12+ creates structural arbitrage that underpins contract economics.
4. **Brownfield expansion economics:** Existing permitted sites with pipeline infrastructure cannot be replicated. New greenfield LNG projects take 7-10 years from inception to first cargo. Cheniere's expansions leverage existing assets and can achieve FID within 2-3 years.
5. **Operational track record:** 4,610+ cargoes delivered. No sustained outages. Stage 3 construction on schedule.
6. **Regulatory moat:** FERC permitting for new LNG facilities is a multi-year, politically contentious process. Cheniere's existing permits and expansion applications at existing sites have lower regulatory risk than greenfield competitors.

The primary competitive risk is long-term global LNG supply growth from Qatar (North Field expansion), Australia, and emerging African projects. However, 90% contracting insulates Cheniere from the spot market pricing impact of this new supply.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Iran Peace Deal Binary (Near-term, High Impact):** A confirmed peace deal would normalize Qatar LNG supply, removing the "Qatari vacuum" premium from LNG pricing and Cheniere's stock. However, Cheniere's contracted model means cash flows are minimally impacted by spot LNG prices—the risk is primarily to the market's willingness to pay a geopolitical premium multiple.

**2. Near-term Oversupply Risk:** 20 mtpa of new global capacity came online in 2025. If the Iran situation resolves and Qatar normalizes simultaneously, spot LNG prices could compress, pressuring sentiment even though Cheniere's contracted economics are protected.

**3. Leverage:** $22.8B in debt is appropriate for the business model but creates sensitivity to interest rate environments. Weighted average rate of 4.65% is manageable with $5.5B operating cash flow, and active debt reduction ($652M in 2025) demonstrates commitment to de-leveraging.

**4. GAAP Earnings Volatility:** IPM mark-to-market swings can produce large earnings surprises in either direction, making quarter-to-quarter EPS unpredictable. This creates the risk of a dramatic "miss" that is purely derivative-related but still moves the stock.

**5. Trade Policy (Section 301):** Requirement for U.S.-built LNG carriers beginning 2029 could increase transportation costs. Compliance path unclear given no existing U.S. LNG shipbuilding capacity.

**6. Board Leadership Transition:** An April 2026 8-K (Item 5.02) indicates that Board Chairman G. Andrea Botta will retire after the 2026 annual meeting, with CEO Jack Fusco succeeding him as Chairman and Patricia Collawn serving as Lead Director. This is an orderly succession rather than an unexpected departure. The clustered insider selling in late March 2026 ($28.5M combined across CFO, CCO, CLO, and Director Botta at $291-300) occurred at the 52-week high and likely reflects profit-taking, but the volume of selling across multiple executives is notable.

### Options Market Signal

Options market data (as of report date) indicate elevated near-term implied volatility significantly above historical realized volatility, suggesting the market is pricing a specific binary event—most likely the Iran peace deal resolution. Steep backwardation in the IV term structure confirms this is event-driven rather than structural. Elevated put demand shows protective positioning into the event. Once the binary resolves (either direction), the near-term IV premium should collapse rapidly. For fundamental investors, this represents temporary noise rather than a thesis challenge.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($310-340):** Peace deal fails or proves temporary, oil remains elevated, LNG demand remains structurally tight. Stage 3 Trains 6-7 complete on schedule in 2026, production volumes hit 53 mtpa, adjusted EBITDA reaches the high end of guidance ($7.25B+) in FY2026. Buyback accelerates at current prices, reducing share count. Market re-rates on recognition of toll-road durability + growth optionality. SPL/CCL expansions achieve FID in 2027, doubling long-term capacity outlook.

**Bear Case ($210-230):** Peace deal confirmed and holds, Qatar supply normalizes—though the timeline for normalization is highly uncertain given conflicting reports of infrastructure damage severity (ranging from weeks to multi-year repair timelines). Spot LNG prices compress. Market removes geopolitical premium, stock reverts to pre-crisis valuation. GAAP earnings show derivative losses from IPM mark-to-market reversals. Near-term oversupply narrative dominates despite contracted protection.

**Base Case ($290-310):** Geopolitical ambiguity persists (most probable), LNG remains critical for European and Asian energy security. Stage 3 completion drives production and EBITDA growth. Buyback steadily reduces share count. Market gradually recognizes expansion optionality.

## Conclusion

Cheniere Energy at $260 represents a compelling risk/reward for a high-quality infrastructure business. The stock trades at ~10.5-11x forward EV/EBITDA with a $290.6B revenue backlog, ~49 mtpa of expansion capacity, and a $10B buyback program that will meaningfully reduce the share count. The toll-road model provides downside protection regardless of geopolitical outcomes while the structural LNG demand story (European diversification, Asian growth, AI power demand) provides multi-year upside optionality.

The near-term binary around the Iran peace deal creates volatility but does not change the fundamental calculus: even in a scenario where Qatar fully normalizes (the timeline for which remains highly uncertain given conflicting assessments of infrastructure damage), Cheniere's contracted cash flows are protected and the current multiple already reflects modest expectations. Maintain BUY with a 12-18 month outlook.
