## Executive Summary

Clean Harbors is North America's dominant hazardous waste management company, operating the continent's largest network of commercial incinerators (10 units) and hazardous waste landfills (6 Subtitle C). The company's competitive position is exceptional and improving: it is the only operator capable of providing cost-effective, end-to-end PFAS destruction at commercial scale, positioning it as a primary beneficiary of a multi-decade, multi-billion-dollar remediation opportunity. Landfill volumes grew 34% YoY in Q1 2026 from PFAS-related work alone.

The stock has appreciated approximately 30% over the past year, reflecting market recognition of the PFAS catalyst and the Kimball incinerator ramp. At $297.75 (current price) and approximately 40x trailing / ~34x forward FY26 EPS, CLH trades at a significant premium to both its environmental services peers (median P/E 22.8x) and its own historical range. Estimate revisions are positive (+7.3% on FY26 over 90 days), and full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance has been raised. However, the company has missed consensus EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters, the most recent miss was -9% in Q3 2025, and the SKSS segment remains exposed to commodity spread compression. The combination of premium valuation, uneven execution track record, and proximity to the consensus analyst target ($325 mean) prevents a BUY recommendation despite the strong competitive position and growth thesis.

## Company Overview

Clean Harbors operates through two segments: Environmental Services (~86% of revenue) and Safety-Kleen Sustainability Solutions (SKSS, ~14%). The Environmental Services segment provides hazardous/non-hazardous waste collection, transport, treatment, recycling, and disposal through 100+ facilities. The SKSS segment is North America's largest used-oil re-refiner, collecting 243 million gallons annually and processing it into base oils and blended lubricants.

The company serves 350,000+ customers including a majority of Fortune 500 companies, employs 22,155 people, and operates 20,000+ vehicles. Revenue in 2025 was $6.03B, up 2.4% from $5.89B in 2024. Adjusted EBITDA reached $1.17B (19.4% margin), with the company generating $442M in free cash flow.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Growth decelerated from 8.9% in 2024 (acquisition-driven via HEPACO at ~$220M contribution) to 2.4% in 2025 and 1.9% in Q1 2026 on an organic basis. The headline growth rate masks underlying strength in Environmental Services (ES segment EBITDA margin improved 60bps to 25.9%) offset by SKSS headwinds (margins compressed from 19.3% to 16.4% over two years).

**Cash flow generation:** Operating cash flow of $867M in 2025 (+$89M YoY) is strong. Adjusted FCF of $509M translates to roughly a 3.2% yield on the current $15.7B market cap — below typical value thresholds but acceptable for a business with CLH's growth profile and asset intensity.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $2.76B against $954M cash yields net debt of approximately $1.8B, or ~1.5x net debt/EBITDA. The maturity profile is well-managed (term loans due 2032, notes due 2029/2031/2033). Current ratio of 2.33x provides ample liquidity. The D/E ratio of 2.78x is elevated in absolute terms but typical for an asset-heavy environmental services company with predictable, contract-based cash flows.

**Capital allocation shift:** Management repurchased $250M in shares in 2025 (up from $55M in 2024), signaling confidence in intrinsic value. The pending Depot Connect acquisition ($130M) continues the tuck-in strategy. Guided CapEx of $450-510M for 2026 includes strategic growth investments (SDA unit, fleet expansion).

**2026 guidance (raised after Q1):** Adjusted EBITDA of $1.24B-$1.30B (6-11% growth), implying the EBITDA margin expands toward 20%+ as Kimball ramps and SKSS spreads normalize.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. PFAS remediation (primary catalyst):** Clean Harbors' 10 commercial incinerators achieve 99.9999% PFAS destruction efficiency (validated by September 2025 EPA study). No competitor operates at this scale. The company published its comprehensive disposal framework to the EPA and Senate EPW Committee in April 2026, positioning itself as the de facto standard-setter. Landfill volumes up 34% in Q1 2026 from PFAS work represent early innings of what management characterizes as a multi-decade opportunity as EPA drinking water standards (4 PPT for PFOA/PFOS) drive nationwide remediation.

**2. Kimball incinerator ramp:** The 10th incinerator adds ~6-7% capacity to the network. Utilization across the other 9 units was 89% in 2025. As Kimball ramps to full utilization by end-2026, incremental revenue flows through at high margins (fixed infrastructure costs already absorbed).

**3. SKSS recovery + SDA unit:** The $210-220M SDA unit (completion ~2028) will produce premium 600N base oil, capturing higher-value product pricing. In the interim, SKSS benefits from improved oil pricing spreads in 2026 (management cited "SKSS spread tailwind" in Q1 guidance raise).

Forward estimate revisions support the thesis: FY26 EPS estimates have risen 7.3% over 90 days to $8.62, and FY27 estimates are at $9.54 (+3.7% over 90 days). The revision trend is positive across all periods.

## Valuation Assessment

At $297.75, CLH trades at approximately:
- 40x trailing EPS ($7.28)
- ~34.5x forward FY26 EPS ($8.62 consensus)
- ~31.2x forward FY27 EPS ($9.54)
- ~13.8x EV/2026E EBITDA (using $17.5B EV and $1.27B midpoint)
- PEG ratio: ~2.9x (using ~12% normalized EPS growth)

Relative to peers: the Environmental & Facilities Services peer median P/E is 22.8x. CLH's premium reflects superior competitive positioning, PFAS optionality, and higher growth. However, the magnitude of the premium (1.5x the peer median) incorporates substantial PFAS value already. Casella Waste (CWST) trades at 802x due to near-breakeven earnings — not comparable. ABM Industries at 15.8x and HCSG at 22.8x lack CLH's moat but illustrate how rich CLH's multiple has become.

The analyst consensus target of $325 (range: $304-$350) implies approximately 9% upside from current levels. At $297.75, CLH already trades near the low end of the analyst target range ($304), limiting near-term re-rating potential unless estimates accelerate further.

The EV/EBITDA multiple of 13.8x on 2026 guidance is reasonable for a business of this quality growing EBITDA 6-11%. The P/E premium is the binding constraint — it requires sustained 12-15% EPS growth to justify, which demands both PFAS acceleration and SKSS recovery materializing simultaneously.

## Competitive Landscape

Clean Harbors' competitive position is exceptional and widening:

- **Permit barriers:** Obtaining new hazardous waste incineration permits takes years of regulatory process. No competitor has announced plans to build new commercial hazardous waste incinerators in North America. The asset base is effectively irreplaceable on any relevant investment horizon.
- **PFAS monopoly at scale:** CLH is the only company offering cost-effective, commercial-scale PFAS destruction across the full chain (sampling → collection → transport → treatment → destruction). Competitors can address individual steps but cannot match the integrated offering.
- **Customer lock-in:** Because generators retain perpetual environmental liability, they conduct extensive audits before selecting disposal partners. Once a relationship is established, switching costs are high and defaults to proven operators with the strongest safety records.
- **Safety record:** TRIR of 0.49 (2025) is industry-leading and directly impacts customer retention, particularly among Fortune 500 companies with strict vendor safety requirements.
- **Network density:** 100+ facilities and 20,000+ vehicles provide geographic coverage that smaller competitors cannot replicate economically.

PFAS demand is pulling customers toward CLH's integrated offering, Kimball adds capacity at the margin, and captive industrial incinerator closures continue to shift volumes to the commercial market.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution risk (elevated):** Three EPS misses in four quarters is concerning. The Q3 2025 miss of -9% ($2.21 vs. $2.43 estimate) was substantial. While Q1 2026 showed a modest beat (+2.6%), the pattern suggests either inconsistent operational delivery or systematic analyst over-estimation. At a 40x P/E, the margin of safety for execution missteps is minimal.

**SKSS commodity exposure:** Oil spread compression has driven segment margins from 19.3% (2023) to 16.4% (2025). The current macro environment creates both upside potential (higher base oil values) and risk (spread volatility). This segment contributes meaningful revenue but unpredictable margins.

**Valuation compression risk:** At 40x trailing and 34.5x forward P/E, CLH is priced for near-perfect execution. In the current rate environment, multiple compression is a structural headwind for all duration assets. A re-rating to 28-30x forward earnings (still a premium to peers) would imply $240-260, representing 13-19% downside.

**PFAS regulatory uncertainty:** While CLH benefits from the current framework (high-temperature incineration as the gold standard), regulators could: (a) mandate alternative technologies, (b) restrict incineration near populated areas, or (c) delay implementation timelines. CLH's proactive engagement with EPA/Congress mitigates but does not eliminate this risk.

**Leverage in rising rates:** While net leverage (1.5x) is manageable, the 2.78x D/E ratio creates sensitivity to refinancing costs. Annual interest of $143M at 5.32% effective rate could increase materially at next refinancing if rates remain elevated or move higher.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing no unusual stress or catalyst, consistent with steady growth at a full valuation. The flat IV term structure and balanced skew suggest the market has already absorbed the Q1 earnings report and sees no imminent binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($340-350, +14-17%):** PFAS volumes accelerate faster than expected as EPA enforcement tightens. Kimball reaches full utilization by Q3 2026. SKSS spreads widen on sustained elevated oil prices. FY26 EBITDA exceeds the high end of guidance ($1.30B+). Estimates revise further upward, and the multiple holds at 35-37x forward P/E on $9.50+ FY27 EPS.

**Bear Case ($240-260, -13-19%):** Industrial slowdown compresses Environmental Services volumes. SKSS margins remain depressed. Multiple compresses to 28-30x forward as rising rates pressure growth multiples. Another quarterly miss reinforces execution concerns. PFAS regulatory timeline extends, pushing volume inflection to 2027+.

**Base Case ($310-325, +4-9%):** Results track guidance (EBITDA $1.24-1.30B). PFAS growth continues at current trajectory. Multiple stabilizes around 33-35x forward FY27 EPS. Consistent with analyst consensus targets.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $260-270 (representing ~30x forward FY26 EPS) without fundamental deterioration — improved risk/reward
2. Two consecutive quarters of EPS beats by >5%, confirming the miss pattern was temporary
3. Evidence of PFAS regulatory mandate specifically requiring high-temperature incineration, creating a formal regulatory moat

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2 2026 EPS miss combined with guidance reduction — would confirm execution issues at premium valuation
2. Regulatory decision that restricts or penalizes incineration as a PFAS disposal method
3. Net leverage exceeds 2.5x EBITDA or operating cash flow declines >15% YoY

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, likely late July/early August). The PFAS regulatory framework evolution and Kimball utilization metrics from Q2 results will be the most informative data points.

## Conclusion

Clean Harbors possesses a genuinely irreplaceable competitive position that is improving as PFAS remediation demand accelerates. The business generates strong cash flows, maintains manageable leverage, and has multiple visible growth vectors (PFAS, Kimball ramp, SKSS recovery, tuck-in M&A). Estimate revisions are positive across all time periods.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation at $297.75 is the combination of premium valuation (40x trailing, 34.5x forward) and demonstrated execution inconsistency (3 misses in 4 quarters). At this multiple, the stock requires continuous operational delivery to avoid de-rating. The Q3 2025 miss demonstrates the downside: the stock fell from ~$246 to $201 (18% drawdown) over the following weeks. The current price already incorporates the consensus PFAS growth thesis and sits near the analyst target floor ($304).

A position is warranted for investors with a 12-18 month horizon who can tolerate volatility, as the underlying business quality is high and the PFAS catalyst is structural. However, the current risk/reward (9% upside to consensus target vs. 13-19% downside to bear case) does not clear the threshold for a new position at today's price. Better entry potential exists on pullbacks toward $260-270.
