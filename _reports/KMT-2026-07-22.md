**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kennametal presents a textbook quality-of-earnings problem. On the surface, the numbers are the best in years: Q3 FY26 sales rose 22% (organic +19%), operating margin jumped to 13.4% from 9.1%, adjusted EPS more than doubled to $0.77, and management raised FY26 guidance to $3.75-$4.00 adjusted EPS on $2.33-$2.35B revenue. Consensus current-year EPS estimates have been revised up ~60% over 90 days, and current-quarter estimates by ~142%. On the standard screening dimensions this looks like a strong inflection.

The reality is narrower. Management explicitly attributes roughly $2.45 of the FY26 EPS guidance to a price-versus-raw-material timing benefit: tungsten spiked from ~$900 to ~$3,000 per metric ton, and Kennametal has been able to raise prices and surcharges ahead of the flow-through of higher-cost inventory. That is a timing gain, not durable margin expansion, and it reverses as raw-material costs catch up or tungsten normalizes. The counterweight sits in the cash flow statement: nine-month operating cash flow fell to $69.7M from $129.7M, free operating cash flow to ~$18M from ~$63M, and full-year FCF is guided to roughly negative 30% of adjusted net income, driven by a $216M inventory build. Management paused buybacks and drew on its revolver for the first time in years, which reveals more about the underlying condition than the EPS headline. Barclays cut the stock to Underweight with a $33 target on this thesis.

We rate KMT a HOLD. The stock at ~$34.72 trades at roughly 19x a guided EPS figure that is structurally inflated by the timing benefit; on normalized ex-timing earnings power, the effective multiple is materially higher. The valuation is not compelling, the strongest forward signal (rising estimates) is contaminated by the timing artifact, cash generation is negative, and there is no dated catalyst that resolves the setup favorably. We would need evidence that normalized earnings power is durably higher, or a price that discounts the eventual timing reversal, before turning constructive.

## Company Overview

Kennametal is a Pittsburgh-based industrial technology company founded in 1938 on a tungsten carbide breakthrough. It manufactures cutting tools, wear-resistant components, and metallurgical powders for metalworking and extreme-wear applications, selling on a productivity/value basis rather than pure price. The moat rests on materials science (tungsten carbides, ceramics, and super-hard materials) and on Kennametal's position as an upstream cemented-carbide producer that supplies carbide components to many smaller competitors. Internal tungsten recycling partially mitigates third-party raw-material reliance.

The business runs two segments. Metal Cutting (~62% of FY25 sales, $1.22B) sells high-performance tooling under the Kennametal and WIDIA brands into General Engineering, Transportation, Aerospace & Defense, and Energy. Infrastructure (~38%, $747M) sells engineered tungsten carbide and ceramic components, earth-cutting tools, metallurgical powders, and armor/penetrators into Earthworks (mining/construction), General Engineering, Energy, and Aerospace & Defense. Roughly 60% of sales are international, with principal operations in Western Europe, China, and India, and about 8,124 employees (~1,900 unionized).

The stock has run 40% over the trailing twelve months to $34.72, off a 52-week low of $17.62 set on the August 2025 earnings gap. It sits below the $43.81 high reached on the May 2026 guidance raise, and shares gave back most of that spike within days.

## Financial Analysis

FY22 through FY25 was a steady erosion. Revenue slipped from $2.01B to $1.97B, operating income fell 34% from $218M to $143M, and operating margin compressed roughly 350bps from 10.8% to 7.3%. FY25 organic sales fell 4% across most end markets, with only Aerospace & Defense growing (+6%). The reported FY25 margin was further flattered by three one-time items: a ~$23M restructuring benefit, ~$13M of incremental IRA advanced-manufacturing production credit, and a ~$12M net benefit from the Rogers, AR tornado insurance recovery. Underlying profitability was weaker than the 7.3% headline implies.

FY26 reversed sharply on the surface. Nine-month sales reached $1.62B, up 12% (organic +11%). Nine-month operating income was $169.7M versus $111.8M, and Infrastructure Q3 operating margin rose to 18.1% from 10.7%. Nine-month diluted EPS was $1.49 versus $0.91.

The driver of that margin jump is the crux of the case. Management attributes the Infrastructure improvement to favorable timing of pricing versus raw-material costs of ~$39M in Q3 and ~$64M for nine months. Kennametal repriced ahead of the flow-through of higher-cost inventory. Strip out the timing benefit and normalized earnings power is far closer to the depressed FY25 run-rate plus modest volume and restructuring gains. The mechanism is symmetrical: when tungsten stabilizes or reverses, the higher-cost inventory flows through the P&L while pricing gives back, compressing reported earnings even if volumes hold.

The cash flow statement confirms the artifact. Inventories rose ~$216M on higher tungsten values, and primary working capital swelled to $819M (32.4% of sales) from $654M. Cash fell to $106.9M from $140.5M, and the revolver was drawn ($15.3M). Earnings roughly doubled while operating cash flow fell ~46%, the classic divergence that flags low-quality reported earnings.

The balance sheet remains investment-grade and conservatively levered. Total debt is ~$614M against equity of $1.35B (D/E ~0.45), predominantly fixed-rate (<1% variable). The revolver was refinanced in November 2025 (Seventh Amended Credit Agreement, ~$700M-equivalent, maturing November 2030, leverage covenant ≤3.75x). The current ratio of 2.46 is strong but inflated by ballooning inventory. Altman Z of 2.59 sits in the grey zone.

The earnings surprise record is real but should be read against the timing dynamic. Over the last four quarters KMT posted three beats and one miss: +13.5% (Mar-26), +22.9% (Dec-25), +43.2% (Sep-25), and -13.9% (Jun-25). The recent beats coincide precisely with the window in which the tungsten timing benefit was building.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate revision picture is arresting on its face: next-year EPS revised +48.8% over 90 days, next-quarter +157.4%. Under normal circumstances, revisions of this magnitude alongside +12% organic revenue growth would be a strong forward signal. Here they are not, because the revisions are tracking the timing benefit, not a durable step-change in earnings power. The tell is in the shorter window: over the last 30 days, current-quarter, current-year, and next-year estimates are all flat-to-negative (-0.7%, -0.4%, -0.9%). The 90-day surge has stalled, which is consistent with analysts recognizing that the tungsten tailwind is a one-off rather than a new baseline.

Underlying volume trends are mixed. General Engineering and Transportation remain soft, with European vehicle production a specific drag; EMEA grew only +2% constant-currency in Q3. The genuine secular positives are Aerospace & Defense (rearmament and build-rate recovery, plus new defense orders) and Energy/power generation, where data-center and AI-driven power demand is an emerging tailwind — Kennametal cited traction in power generation tied to that demand and share gains in Earthworks and A&D. These are real, but they are not large enough to offset the eventual reversal of a $2.45/share timing benefit. Transportation carries longer-term EV-transition risk to internal-combustion tooling demand.

Management has expanded its cost program to ~$110M in savings by end of FY27 (+$10M), while deliberately delaying some FY27 facility closures to preserve growth capacity. That is a sensible bet on volume recovery, but it defers the margin benefit and leaves normalized FY27 earnings dependent on end-market demand that is currently only partially recovering.

The honest growth question is what normalized, ex-timing earnings power looks like once tungsten stabilizes. If A&D and Energy wins plus the cost program lift the underlying figure meaningfully above the FY25 run-rate, the story improves. But that has not yet shown up in volumes, and the multi-year record (FY22-FY25 operating income down 34%) argues for caution. We score growth below the midpoint: the headline revisions are strong but contaminated, the 30-day trend has rolled over, and durable organic growth remains unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.72, KMT trades at ~19.3x trailing/forward EPS on the reported basis. This is where the cycle-adjustment question inverts the usual logic. Normally a high trailing multiple at a cyclical trough understates cheapness. Here the opposite holds: the forward EPS being capitalized is inflated by the non-recurring timing benefit, so the true multiple on normalized earnings power is materially higher than 19x. Backing out the timing benefit puts normalized EPS closer to a low-$1 to ~$1.50 range, which implies a normalized multiple well into the 20s-to-30s on earnings that are not growing organically. That is not a compelling entry.

Peer comparison is of limited use here because the sector screen shows wide dispersion and distortion: AIN at -34.6x (loss), PRLB at 73.5x, EPAC at 22.1x, KAI at 32.9x, TNC at 51.7x, for a peer median of 51.7x. On reported EPS, KMT looks cheap against that median, but the peer median is skewed by companies with genuine growth or one-off earnings depressions, and KMT's reported EPS is itself distorted upward by the timing gain. Adjusting for that, the apparent discount evaporates. The valuation lessons from this system's own record are relevant: a low headline multiple is a warning to look for a value trap, not a buy signal on its own, and cheapness has historically sorted the wrong way. Here the "cheapness" is an artifact of inflated earnings, which is the value-trap pattern in reverse.

Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly: low $31, mean $34.86, median $34.00, high $39.00. On a stock at ~19x with rising 90-day estimates, targets might read as stale floors. That guardrail does not apply here, because the estimate revisions are contaminated by the timing benefit, and the 30-day trend is flat-to-down. The consensus sitting essentially at spot, with Barclays at Underweight ($33), reflects analysts looking through the beat rather than lagging it. The rating distribution (0 buys, 5 holds, 1 sell, 2 strong sells) confirms a skeptical Street.

We score valuation below the midpoint. The price does not offer asymmetric upside; on normalized earnings the multiple is stretched, and there is no identified catalyst to close a gap in the bull's favor.

## Competitive Landscape

The tooling and wear industry is large and fragmented, with several hundred fabricators. Kennametal differentiates on brand (Kennametal, WIDIA), application engineering, and its position as an upstream cemented-carbide producer that supplies many smaller competitors. Internal recycling adds a genuine cost and supply advantage.

The defining industry dynamic is the tungsten shock. Because China dominates tungsten supply and has tightened critical-mineral exports, prices roughly tripled. Kennametal's vertical integration, recycling capability, and demonstrated pricing power (surcharges and list increases) let it turn the disruption into a near-term margin tailwind, a real competitive edge during the shock. The same integration cuts the other way: large inventory dollar exposure produces the cash-flow hit, and the margin benefit is a timing artifact. The competitive position is genuinely stable-to-improving on the operational front (share gains cited in Earthworks and A&D, defense order wins, power-generation traction), which supports a modestly above-midpoint score. The near-term margin lead over peers is not a durable moat expansion; it is a shared industry shock that Kennametal happens to be well-positioned to arbitrage temporarily.

Chronic shareholder underperformance tempers the competitive read. Cumulative TSR of 92 versus a peer group at 267 (indexed) shows KMT has been a persistent sector laggard, consistent with the multi-year revenue and margin decline. The bull case requires the A&D/Energy secular wins and the cost program to lift normalized earnings once the tungsten benefit fades, which has not yet been demonstrated.

## Risk Assessment

**Tungsten price shock and timing reversal (dominant risk).** The ~$2.45/share timing benefit is the largest single swing factor. When tungsten stabilizes or reverses, reported earnings could fall sharply even with stable volumes. The 10-Q added language flagging elevated impairment risk from rising tungsten prices tied to tightening supply, geopolitical factors, and trade policy.

**Cash flow and working capital.** The revolver draw and buyback pause are direct consequences of the working-capital drain. If tungsten stays elevated, the drain persists; if it reverses, earnings compress. Either resolution is unfavorable in the near term.

**Cyclical end-market demand.** General Engineering and Transportation remain soft, and Transportation carries EV-transition risk. Prolonged demand weakness could also trigger a goodwill impairment on the $282.7M balance (all in Metal Cutting), where management moved to a quantitative Step 1 test in FY25.

**Litigation.** MachiningCloud sued Kennametal in February 2025 seeking >$330M in damages for breach of contract, now in federal court in the Western District of California. No liability is accrued, and the case is early-stage, but the potential exposure is material against a $2.6B market cap.

**Trade/tariff and regulatory.** Net tariff drag was ~$4M in FY25. A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating certain IEEPA tariffs adds uncertainty around the surcharge mechanism that supports current pricing. Separately, tungsten/cobalt hard-metal dust is under study for potential hazardous reclassification, which could raise costs.

**Earnings quality.** Recurring "Bolivia preferential exchange rate" gains in other income are a low-quality, geographically idiosyncratic earnings source; combined with the FY25 one-timers and the FY26 timing gain, reported figures overstate underlying earnings quality in both directions across years.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. One-month ATM IV of 61.9% is rich versus 46.9% trailing historical volatility (a 15-point spread) and sits at the 62nd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (61.9% near-term versus 50.6% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent catalyst, consistent with the next earnings print testing whether the timing benefit is unwinding. The put/call volume ratio is elevated at 14.5, though put/call open interest is balanced at 0.80 and the one-month skew is roughly flat (1.0%). The implied one-month move is ±14%. Combined with 11.0% short interest (4 days to cover), the positioning is modestly bearish but not extreme. The rich IV argues against paying up for near-term optionality and reinforces waiting for the next print rather than pre-positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$45).** Tungsten stays structurally elevated on China export controls, extending the pricing benefit longer than the market expects while volumes recover in A&D and Energy/power generation. The $110M cost program lifts normalized margins, working capital normalizes as inventory values plateau, and FCF turns positive in FY27. Normalized ex-timing EPS steps up toward $3+ durably, justifying a mid-teens multiple on genuine earnings. Defense order wins and data-center power demand provide a multi-year organic tailwind, and share gains compound. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the low-$40s as the timing benefit proves less transient than feared.

**Bear case (~$26).** Tungsten stabilizes or reverses, the timing benefit unwinds over two to four quarters, and reported EPS falls back toward the $1.50-$2.00 range. Cyclical weakness in General Engineering, Transportation, and EMEA persists, volumes stay soft, and the market recapitalizes normalized earnings at a mid-cycle multiple, taking the stock toward the mid-$20s. A goodwill impairment or an adverse MachiningCloud outcome adds downside. This is roughly the Barclays view (Underweight, $33), extended further if demand disappoints.

**Base case (~$34).** The timing benefit fades gradually over FY27 while cost savings and modest A&D/Energy volume growth partially offset the reversal. Normalized earnings settle above the depressed FY25 run-rate but below the FY26 guided figure. FCF recovers toward positive but not robustly. The stock trades roughly sideways, oscillating between the bear and bull scenarios as each quarterly print reveals the pace of the timing unwind. Consensus targets clustering at ~$34-$35 reflect this outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings print is the natural checkpoint, as it will show whether the timing benefit is beginning to unwind and how quickly inventory values and working capital are normalizing. Given the FY (June 30) calendar, the Q4/full-year FY26 report and FY27 guidance are the decisive events; that guide will reveal management's own view of normalized earnings power.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that normalized, ex-timing EPS is durably stepping up — specifically, FY27 guidance that implies underlying earnings power meaningfully above the FY25 run-rate excluding the tungsten timing gain, supported by organic volume growth (not pricing) in A&D and Energy.
- Free operating cash flow turning clearly positive with working capital normalizing (primary working capital falling back toward the high-20s % of sales), enabling a buyback resumption.
- A pullback that discounts the timing reversal — a price in the mid-$20s that reflects normalized earnings would restore asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Confirmation that the timing benefit is reversing faster than volumes can offset, i.e., sequential margin compression in Infrastructure back toward pre-shock levels alongside falling organic volumes.
- A goodwill impairment charge on Metal Cutting, or a materially adverse development in the MachiningCloud litigation.
- Continued FCF deterioration forcing further revolver draws, or a leverage covenant approach.

## Conclusion

Kennametal is a case where the headline metrics overstate the underlying reality. The bulk of the FY26 earnings surge (~$2.45 of the guided EPS) is a non-recurring tungsten price-versus-cost timing benefit that reverses as raw-material costs catch up, and the cash flow statement corroborates this. The 30-day estimate trend has already rolled over even as the 90-day figure stays elevated, consistent with analysts recognizing the timing artifact.

At ~19x reported EPS, and a materially higher multiple on normalized earnings, the valuation offers no asymmetric upside. The Street is skeptical, the competitive position is stable-to-improving but not durably re-rating, and near-term options pricing signals expected stress into the next print. The genuine positives (A&D and Energy/power-generation wins, the $110M cost program, investment-grade balance sheet) are real but not yet large enough to offset the eventual timing reversal, and multi-year TSR underperformance argues against extending the benefit of the doubt.

We rate KMT a HOLD. There is no confident directional call here: the bull thesis depends on the timing benefit proving less transient than management itself indicates, and the bear thesis depends on a reversal whose pace is genuinely unknown. The prudent posture is to wait for the next print and FY27 guidance to reveal normalized earnings power, and to reassess on either a durable step-up in underlying earnings (upgrade) or a price that discounts the reversal (also an upgrade to the entry case). We would turn cautious if the timing unwind accelerates ahead of volume recovery.
