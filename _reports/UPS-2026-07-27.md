**Research Date: July 27, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UPS is roughly two-thirds of the way through a deliberate, management-chosen revenue contraction. The company is voluntarily cutting its Amazon volume by ~50% from 2024 levels (targeting completion by June 2026), shedding the lowest-margin pieces in its network in favor of higher-margin healthcare logistics (>$11B revenue, targeting global #1), SMB volume (now >30% of US volume via the Digital Access Program), and International. The strategic logic is coherent: revenue per piece rose 6.6% in 2025 even as average daily volume fell 7.0%, and the US Domestic non-GAAP adjusted operating margin improved 20bps to 7.7% despite the volume loss. The bet is that stranded costs can be stripped out faster than the shed revenue disappears.

The transition is not going smoothly on a reported basis. Q1 2026 was a genuine warning: revenue fell 1.6% to $21.2B, operating profit dropped 23.9% to $1.27B, and US Domestic operating margin collapsed to 3.6% from 6.8% a year earlier, driven by Ground Saver/USPS insourcing costs, excess staffing, higher third-party aircraft lease costs after the MD-11 retirement, and weather. Management insists these pressures "abate in Q2," which is the pivotal near-term checkpoint. The single most important overhang is the dividend: the $6.56/share declared in 2025 equaled diluted EPS exactly (a ~100% GAAP payout), total shareholder returns of $6.4B exceeded free cash flow of ~$4.77B, and buybacks were zeroed entirely in Q1 2026 while the dividend held. The company's own stock-option pricing model bumped its assumed dividend yield to 6.65% (from 5.21% a year earlier), a signal that the market prices elevated payout risk.

Since the prior review, the shares have recovered from ~$96 immediately after the Q1 margin collapse (reported May 6) back to ~$115 by late July, and now sit ~40% off the $82 52-week low. Forward estimates are broadly rising (current-year +1.8%, current-quarter +4.5% over 90 days), the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters, and the analyst tape is net-constructive (14 buy vs. 12 hold, with 1 sell and 2 strong sell). At ~$115 the stock trades essentially at the mean analyst target ($115.04) and inside our own fair-value range ($105-115). A stock that is fairly valued, with an offsetting dividend risk that has not yet triggered and rising forward estimates, is a HOLD. This corrects the prior call, which issued a bearish rating into rising estimates and a stock that had already re-rated up ~40% off its lows.

## Company Overview

UPS is the world's largest integrated package delivery company, operating in 200+ countries through three reporting units:

- **U.S. Domestic Package** (~67% of revenue; $59.5B in 2025): time-definite ground and air delivery within the US. This is the segment absorbing the Amazon reduction and the transition costs.
- **International Package** (~21%; $18.6B): the profit anchor, with a 15.5% operating margin (down 230bps YoY on trade-policy pressure), spanning EMEA, Americas, and Asia with cross-border brokerage.
- **Supply Chain Solutions** (~12%; $10.6B): forwarding, logistics, healthcare logistics, and digital businesses (Roadie, Happy Returns, UPS Capital).

The strategic mantra is "Customer First, People Led, Innovation Driven," deliberately steering the mix toward higher-margin verticals. Competitive advantages are an integrated global air-and-ground network, dense US delivery density, brokerage/customs capability, and brand. The company runs a dual-class structure (Class A = 10 votes; Class B publicly traded). Carol Tomé remains CEO; Brian Dykes is CFO. Board members include former Fed governor Kevin Warsh and ex-Sherwin-Williams CEO John Morikis.

## Financial Analysis

The defining dynamic is a deliberate revenue contraction to improve mix and margins.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $100.3B | $91.0B | $91.1B | $88.7B |
| Operating income | $13.1B | $9.1B | $8.5B | $7.9B |
| Operating margin | 13.1% | 10.0% | 9.3% | 8.9% |
| Net income | $11.5B | $6.7B | $5.8B | $5.6B |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $6.75 | $6.56 |

Several threads run through this trajectory:

- **The mix-shift is partially working, but slowly.** The "trade volume for margin" thesis is showing up in the data, but Q1 2026 exposed the transition cost: US Domestic GAAP operating margin cratered to 3.6% because cost takeout lagged revenue loss. Management's claim that these costs "abate in Q2" is the single most important operational checkpoint of 2026.
- **Cash flow quality is deteriorating.** Operating cash flow fell from $10.1B (2024) to $8.5B (2025). 2025 OCF was aided by a new accounts-receivable factoring program (~$730M) that did not exist in 2024, a working-capital lever that flatters the underlying cash trend.
- **Earnings quality flags.** 2025 GAAP EPS was propped by $0.30/share of sale-leaseback real-estate gains and a $0.13 tax valuation-allowance reversal, both non-operating items. Non-GAAP adjusted EPS was $7.16, but the adjustments (Transformation costs) have recurred for years, undercutting the "one-time" framing.
- **Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt rose to $24.1B (from $21.3B); D/E ~4.5x; equity thin ($16.2B) against a $73B asset base. The Altman Z of 2.07 sits in the grey zone: this is a capital-intensive, leveraged, pension-heavy business ($6.6B pension obligations, $3.1B self-insurance reserves) executing a large self-inflicted restructuring. Near-term liquidity is adequate: $5.8B cash, undrawn $3B revolvers, no commercial paper outstanding. Piotroski scores 5/5 on available signals.

**Capital allocation is the crux of the debate.** In 2025 UPS returned $6.4B ($5.4B dividends + $1.0B buybacks) against $4.77B FCF, funded partly by incremental debt. UPS then halted buybacks entirely in Q1 2026 ($0 vs. $1.0B a year earlier) while maintaining the $1.64/quarter dividend. Capex is being cut to ~$3.0B in 2026 (from $3.7B). Management is defending the dividend with capex cuts and factoring, but the margin of safety is thin. A $1.2B Driver Choice Program separation charge hits mostly Q2 2026, further pressuring the near-term cash picture.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic top-line growth story here in 2026: revenue is contracting by design, and consensus reflects it. The relevant growth question is whether earnings can inflect off the transition trough as stranded costs come out.

The estimate revision data is modestly constructive, with next-year at $8.02 (up 1.4%). The earnings track record supports some confidence in the forward numbers: three beats in four quarters, including a +32.7% surprise in Q3 2025 and +7.0% in Q4 2025, with a small 1.7% miss in Q2 2025 and a +3.8% beat in Q1 2026. This is not the +30%/90-day inflection velocity that flags a strong BUY signal; it is a business grinding through a self-imposed reset with estimates edging up rather than surging.

The genuine growth pockets are real but small relative to the whole. Healthcare crossed $11B in 2025 and the Frigo-Trans and Andlauer acquisitions (~$2.0B cash) build the platform. SMB volume via the Digital Access Program is structurally higher-margin than enterprise. International, at 15.5% margin, remains the profit anchor and the reason the mix-shift is credible, though it is under trade-policy pressure (de minimis exclusions, China-US tariffs cost 230bps of International margin).

The durable growth thesis rests on the "post-Amazon" argument: that shedding the lowest-margin volume mechanically lifts blended margins once stranded costs are removed. Q1 2026 shows this working in reverse so far, with costs lagging. Until Q2 demonstrates the promised cost abatement, the growth-off-the-trough case is unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$115, UPS trades at ~16.1x the current-year consensus EPS of $7.13 and ~14.3x the next-year $8.02. Against the Air Freight & Logistics peer median P/E of 26.9, this is a substantial discount, but the discount is warranted. FedEx trades at 18.0x and is also cyclically pressured; the higher peer multiples (EXPD 26.9x, CHRW 38.7x, GXO 43.5x, HUBG 26.3x) sit on asset-light forwarding and contract-logistics models that carry different capital intensity and margin profiles than UPS's owned integrated network. The relevant comparison is FedEx, and on that basis UPS is roughly in line-to-slightly-cheaper, appropriately so given the deeper self-inflicted disruption.

The valuation case is genuinely two-sided. On our own numbers, applying ~15x to $7.13 current-year EPS yields ~$107; ~15x to next-year $8.02 yields ~$120. That brackets the current price and supports a fair-value range of $105-115. The mean analyst target of $115.04 confirms the stock sits essentially at fair value, with a median of $117.50 offering marginal upside and a low of $76.00 / high of $135.00 defining the tails.

The 5.7% dividend yield provides real support: if the payout holds, it is a meaningful component of total return and a floor on the stock. But applying the discipline that a low multiple is not itself a buy signal, the ~16x forward multiple compresses partly because forward EPS is depressed by transition costs, and partly because the market prices dividend-coverage risk. This is a value-trap-adjacent setup where cheapness reflects real transition and payout risk rather than clear mispricing. The estimate revisions are positive but too shallow to argue the market is systematically underestimating an inflection. Fairly valued, not obviously cheap.

## Competitive Landscape

UPS competes with FedEx, DHL, USPS, regional carriers, freight forwarders, and, most consequentially, customers building in-house logistics. The largest customer being deliberately shed, Amazon, is also the prime competitive threat: at 10.6% of 2025 consolidated revenue, UPS is voluntarily cutting this ~50%. This is a defensible strategic response to a customer that was simultaneously the biggest counterparty and the biggest structural threat, but it removes a large chunk of network density that fixed costs were built to serve.

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. The moat elements (integrated global network, US delivery density, brokerage/customs capability) remain intact. Density economics degrade as volume falls, which is exactly the source of the Q1 stranded-cost problem. The higher-margin verticals (healthcare, International) are where UPS is trying to build durable advantage, and healthcare in particular has scale and specialization that create switching costs. But the sector backdrop is soft: B2B declines in retail/manufacturing, weak air/ocean forwarding demand. Net, this is a market leader in a mature industry actively giving up share by choice, betting the retained book is structurally more profitable. The trajectory is neither clearly widening nor clearly collapsing.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Dividend sustainability** — the pivotal, well-documented risk, offset by adequate liquidity ($5.8B cash, undrawn $3B revolver, no CP), capex cuts to $3.0B, and management's demonstrated commitment (dividend held while buybacks were cut). A cut is a live possibility, not a base case.
2. **Amazon concentration and the volume-reduction gamble** — the #1 company-specific risk. If cost takeout continues to lag revenue loss (as Q1 2026 suggests), profitability compresses further.
3. **Q1 2026 margin collapse** — US Domestic to 3.6% is the exact failure mode the 10-K warns about. Q2 is the checkpoint; the $1.2B Driver Choice charge complicates the read.
4. **Goodwill impairment risk in SCS** — Global Freight Forwarding ($877M) and Healthcare Logistics ($738M) reporting units have only "limited excess" of fair value over carrying value; a 100bps discount-rate rise or 10% cash-flow cut could trigger impairment. A Q4 2025 interim GFF test found no impairment yet.
5. **Trade policy** — de minimis exclusions and tariffs hurt International margins. Separately, the Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs creates an unrecognized ~$500M refund receivable (cash timing positive, not income).
6. **Labor** — Teamsters master agreement expires July 2028; pilot and mechanics contracts amendable sooner. The 2023 negotiation itself caused lasting volume diversion.
7. **Louisville MD-11 accident (Nov 2025)** — litigation/environmental exposure; fleet retirement ($182M charge) created capacity constraints and forced costlier third-party 767 leases, a direct contributor to Q1 aircraft cost pressure.

The Q1 2026 10-Q states no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 79.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 31.2%: richly priced, at the 87th percentile within the current chain, with a steep backwardation (79.2% at 1 week vs. 38.0% at 6 months). The nearest-expiry implied move is ±7.0%, consistent with a Q2 earnings event pricing imminent binary stress around the cost-abatement question. Put/call skew is mildly elevated (5.3% near-term, turning negative at longer tenors: -3.4% at 6 months), and put/call OI is a balanced 0.60. The market is hedging the near-term earnings print but is not structurally bearish on longer horizons. Rich near-term IV argues against chasing on either side into the print; a buyer of the shares would be better served waiting for the post-earnings volatility crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($130-135):** Q2 2026 confirms management's cost-abatement claim, US Domestic margin recovers toward 6-7%, and the mix-shift math starts working as designed, with blended margins rising as low-margin Amazon volume exits and healthcare/SMB/International grows. The dividend holds, the yield anchors total return, and the stock re-rates toward the peer/FedEx range on demonstrated earnings inflection. The ~$500M IEEPA refund and OBBBA tax tailwinds add cash. Next-year EPS of $8.02+ at ~16x supports $130+.

**Bear Case ($76-90):** Cost takeout keeps lagging, Q2 disappoints, the $1.2B Driver Choice charge compounds cash pressure, and the dividend is cut to preserve the balance sheet as leverage rises and the Altman Z stays in the grey zone. A dividend cut removes the yield floor and triggers a re-rating lower; SCS goodwill impairment adds a headline charge. The low analyst target of $76 defines this tail.

The base case is a fairly-valued stock ($105-115) where the retained book is genuinely more profitable but the transition drag persists longer than management guides, and the dividend holds by a thin margin. Neither tail is clearly more probable than fair value from here, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings, which will test management's "costs abate in Q2" claim directly and reveal dividend-coverage trajectory alongside the Driver Choice charge. FY2026 full-year results and the 2027 capital-allocation framework are the subsequent milestones.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 US Domestic operating margin recovers meaningfully off the 3.6% Q1 trough (toward 5-6%+), confirming stranded-cost takeout is catching up to revenue loss.
- Dividend coverage improves — FCF returns to comfortably exceeding shareholder returns, or buybacks resume, signaling the payout is de-risked.
- A pullback into the mid-$90s that widens the yield above 6.5% while forward estimates hold, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut, or explicit management signaling of one, which removes the yield floor and confirms the coverage bear case.
- Q2/Q3 2026 US Domestic margin fails to recover or deteriorates further, indicating the cost-takeout thesis is structurally failing rather than transitionally lagging.
- SCS goodwill impairment triggered, or leverage rising with the Altman Z falling deeper into distress territory.

## Conclusion

UPS is a fairly-valued turnaround with a live but not-yet-triggered dividend overhang. The shares trade at ~16x forward earnings, essentially at fair value and at the mean analyst target, having already recovered ~40% off the $82 low and absorbed the Q1 margin collapse. Forward estimates are rising modestly, the earnings track record is net-positive, and the analyst tape leans constructive. Against that, the transition is genuinely painful, cost takeout is lagging revenue loss, and the ~100% GAAP payout dividend is defended with debt, factoring, and capex cuts rather than organic free cash flow.

This balance of fair pricing, offsetting risks, no attractive short (short interest is only 3.3% and the reward:risk from here is roughly symmetric), and rising forward estimates supports a HOLD. The prior AVOID was issued into a stock that had already re-rated higher on rising estimates, which is a recurring failure mode; the corrected call recognizes that a view already in the price is not a short. Own it for the ~5.7% yield if you can tolerate the coverage risk, and watch Q2 margins and dividend coverage as the swing factors. **HOLD.**
