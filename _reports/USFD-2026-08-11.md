## Executive Summary

US Foods delivered its strongest quarter in the available dataset on August 6, 2026: net sales of $10,532M (+4.5%), independent restaurant case volume of +5.1% (organic +5.0%), adjusted EBITDA of $604M (+10.2%), and a record adjusted EBITDA margin of 5.7%. Adjusted diluted EPS of $1.44 beat the Zacks non-GAAP consensus of $1.37 by ~5%. The independent-restaurant result was the best since Q4 2023 and the fifth consecutive quarter of acceleration, with management citing a 21st consecutive quarter of independent share gains and net new account generation at a three-year high. The operating thesis — shift mix toward independents, healthcare and hospitality, let low-margin chain volume attrit, and convert the mix shift into gross-profit-per-case expansion — is working. Gross profit per case rose 5% (+$0.41) against operating expense per case of +3.7% (+$0.21).

The problem is price. Since the June review at $91.91 the shares have advanced roughly 20% to $110.135, while consensus next-year EPS was cut 1.5% over 90 days (to $5.45 from $5.54) and current-year consensus of $4.71 sits at the very bottom of the guided $4.70–4.94 range. Price up with estimates down is multiple expansion, and it has taken the stock to 22.8x the FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $4.82, 20.2x next-year consensus, and 13.5x EV/FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of ~$2,145M. Enterprise value is $29.0B ($23.8B equity + $5.24B debt − $56M cash). Normalized FY2026 free cash flow of roughly $950M–$1.0B against that market capitalization is a 4.0–4.2% yield, and H1 FY2026 free cash flow of $552M was *below* the prior-year $569M despite net income rising $52M. The sell-side mean target of $112.20 is 1.9% above spot.

Underneath the headline, the quality of the Q2 margin gain is a third smaller than it looks. Reported gross margin expanded 60bp to 18.2%, but a $5M LIFO benefit this year against a $14M LIFO expense last year accounts for a $19M favorable swing; excluding LIFO the expansion was approximately 41bp, corroborated by adjusted EBITDA margin — which excludes LIFO by construction — expanding only 29bp. Roughly a quarter of the guided 18–24% adjusted EPS growth comes from a 5.2% reduction in diluted shares rather than operations. We are downgrading to REDUCE from three consecutive HOLDs: base case $115 (+4.4%), bull $127 (+15.3%), bear $88 (−20.1%), producing a probability-weighted expected return near zero with unfavorable downside skew.

## Company Overview

US Foods is the second-largest broadline foodservice distributor in the United States, supplying approximately 250,000 customer locations from over 70 distribution facilities plus more than 90 CHEF'STORE cash-and-carry outlets, using more than 6,500 trucks and roughly 4,000 sales associates. Headcount was approximately 30,000 at December 27, 2025. The company reports as a single operating segment; the resource-allocation metric used by the CODM is consolidated GAAP net income.

The economics are simple: buy from a fragmented supplier base (no supplier exceeded 5% of FY2025 purchases), warehouse, resell at a markup, deliver on an owned fleet. Three profit sources: resale gross margin, vendor consideration (supplier rebates and promotional allowances — identified in the 10-K as a critical accounting estimate), and the mix premium on Exclusive Brands, which were approximately 35% of FY2025 net sales and ~35% of organic broadline cases in Q2 FY2026.

FY2025 product mix: meats and seafood $13,974M (35% of sales), dry grocery $6,685M, refrigerated/frozen $6,635M, dairy $4,214M, equipment/disposables $3,631M, beverage $2,338M, produce $1,947M.

The differentiation programs are operational rather than promotional: MOXē (e-commerce and business management, image-based ordering, menu cost tooling), the Check suite (POS, website, online ordering), Scoop innovation launches, and Pronto — a small-truck, six-day, late-cutoff service that reached $1B of sales in 2025, is guided to ~$1.3B in 2026, and was raised to >$1.7B for 2027 from a prior $1.5B estimate. Pronto operates in 52 markets with Pronto Next Day in 35.

The 10-K is unusually candid about the limits of the moat: "We generally do not have exclusive distribution agreements with our customers... The cost of switching distributors is very low, as are the barriers to entry." That disclosure should discipline any durable-advantage framing.

FY2025's 4.1% sales growth decomposed into +1.0% total case volume and 2.6% food-cost inflation; organic case volume was only +0.4%, so acquisitions supplied roughly 60bp of the total. Within FY2025 volume: independents +3.3%, healthcare +4.4%, hospitality +2.9%, chain −3.5%. Q2 FY2026 was materially better: total cases +1.9% (organic +1.7%), independents +5.1%, healthcare +3.5%, hospitality +4.4%, chain −1.5% (which management said was 30bp better than Black Box industry traffic).

**Margin quality.** The ex-LIFO adjustment above matters for extrapolation. Anyone modeling 60bp of annual gross-margin expansion is over-extrapolating; the operating improvement is closer to 30–41bp. The direction of the LIFO swing bears watching: a benefit implies deflation in some categories, and the 10-K's low-margin risk factor is explicit that deflation compresses absolute profit dollars even at a constant gross margin percentage.

**Named cost programs** (issuer statements from the Q2 call, not audited): Strategic Vendor Management delivered >$50M of incremental COGS savings in H1 and is on track to exceed $300M cumulative under a plan ending 2027; inventory management adds ~$10M of gross profit in 2026 after $35M in 2025; indirect procurement >$20M year to date, targeted at >$75M in 2026.

**Cash flow is the weakest part of the picture.** H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was $725M — exactly flat versus H1 FY2025 — despite net income rising $52M, because receivables consumed $299M versus $230M. H1 free cash flow of $552M was down from $569M. This business is front-half weighted on cash: FY2025 full-year FCF of $965M against H1 FCF of $569M implies H2 FY2025 FCF of only ~$396M, primarily because capex is back-half weighted ($161M H1 versus ~$249M H2). Applying a similar shape to the H1 FY2026 base gives normalized FY2026 FCF of roughly $950M–$1.0B — flat to modestly up on FY2025 — against FY2026 capex guidance of $400–440M. Do not annualize H1.

One presentation item: the H1 cash-tax line of $93M "includes $73 million of cash paid to counterparties to purchase federal transferable tax credits." That makes the $93M non-comparable to the prior-year $80M, and the discount to face value and P&L treatment are not disclosed.

**Balance sheet (June 27, 2026).** Total debt $5,237M, cash $56M, net debt $5,181M. Composition includes $433M ABL (2031), two incremental term loans ($609M 2028, $709M 2031), five senior note tranches from 4.63% to 7.25% (2028–2033) and $591M of finance leases. Approximately 34% floats; a 1% rate move is ~$19M of annual interest, against which a $450M-notional 5%-strike cap runs only to April 30, 2027, a partial hedge. No long-term maturities before 2028. The ABL was upsized on May 28, 2026 from $2,300M to $2,500M and extended to 2031, leaving $1,752M available and ~$1.8B of total liquidity. Restricted payment capacity was ~$3.1B.

Net leverage reproduces cleanly: TTM adjusted EBITDA of $2,012M ($1,932M − $937M + $1,017M) against $5,181M net debt = 2.58x, inside the 2–3x target and consistent with management's stated 2.6x. FY2025 adjusted EBITDA covered net interest of $305M 6.3x. The headline 2.24 "debt-to-equity" screen is total liabilities over equity; interest-bearing debt to equity is 1.21x. Book equity of $4,279M is entirely a function of $5,796M goodwill plus $753M intangibles, implying tangible book equity of approximately −$2.27B. That is normal for an asset-light, acquisitive distributor (Sysco's equity/assets is 6.8% versus US Foods' 30.9%) and is a caution about which ratios to trust rather than a solvency concern.

**Capital allocation is buybacks, full stop.** FY2024 $948M, FY2025 $926M, H1 FY2026 ~$499M inclusive of fees and excise tax — cumulative ~$2.37B against cumulative FCF of ~$2.35B. A November 2025 ASR retired ~3.1M shares at an $80.42 VWAP; Q2 FY2026 repurchases averaged $85.37 across three tranches. Diluted weighted-average shares fell from 250M (FY2023) to 221M (Q2 FY2026), −11.6%. $640M remains authorized. No dividend, no deleveraging, and no dry powder for a large transaction without a leverage step-up. The repurchases executed at $80–86 look well-timed against $110; incremental repurchases at $110 buy a 4.0–4.2% FCF yield, a materially thinner spread over the after-tax cost of debt.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance, reaffirmed at Q2: net sales +4% to +6%, total case volume +2.5% to +4.5%, adjusted EBITDA +9% to +13%, adjusted EPS +18% to +24%, with a 53rd week contributing roughly 1 percentage point to volume and EBITDA growth.

Two arithmetic checks temper the guidance.

**Volume will land near the low end.** H1 total case volume was +1.6% (organic +1.4%). To average +2.5% for the year, H2 needs about +3.4%; the extra week supplies roughly 2 points of that, so H2 organic needs ~+1.4% — achievable at the H1 run rate. To reach +4.5%, H2 needs roughly +7.4%, or ~+5.4% organic, nearly four times the H1 organic pace. The sales range is more attainable because inflation and mix contribute 2.3–2.6 points.

**The top half of the adjusted EPS range requires acceleration.** Building from the EBITDA guidance midpoint of $2,145M, less ~$490M D&A (H1 actual $237M in a 53-week year), less ~$305M net interest, taxed at 25%, yields ~$1,013M of adjusted net income, or $4.63 on ~219M average diluted shares — below the $4.82 midpoint. Bottom-up: H1 adjusted EPS was $2.21, so H2 must deliver $2.49–$2.73 against H2 FY2025's ~$2.12 (+17.5% to +28.8%). Consensus agrees with the conservative read: current-year estimate is $4.71, at the bottom of the guided range, and 15 analysts have trimmed next-year to $5.45 from $5.54 over 90 days.

**Growth composition.** Adjusted EBITDA growth guided at +9–13% versus adjusted EPS at +18–24%. Roughly 5 points of that gap is share count, the balance operating leverage over largely fixed interest expense. Legitimate value creation at a 4% FCF yield exceeding the after-tax cost of debt, but the share-count contribution decays arithmetically as the base shrinks and as the repurchase price rises.

The genuine growth engine is independent share capture. Total industry case volume is barely growing — US Foods grew total cases 1.0% in FY2025 while claiming 21 consecutive quarters of independent share gains — which means nearly all of the independent growth is taken from Sysco, Performance Food Group and regional distributors rather than supplied by market expansion. That is higher-quality growth than a rising tide, and simultaneously harder to sustain and more likely to invite price response. The new companywide seller compensation plan launched in June 2026, aligned to independent growth, exclusive-brand penetration and Pronto adoption, is both a 2027 catalyst and a live execution risk; sales-force attrition was flat year over year.

Early-stage technology initiatives (a "Visit Assistant Insights" tool that delivered >700,000 seller insights in six weeks, a generative-AI sales chatbot in pilot, Descartes routing with AI demand forecasting, autonomous inventory-scanning robots in one warehouse expanding to six more) are credible but too early to underwrite in estimates.

Peer comparison requires care because the supplied peer P/Es are trailing GAAP and the group is heterogeneous: PFGC 54.7x (mix includes convenience and vending, heavy amortization), CHEF 52.5x (specialty), ANDE 12.7x (agribusiness, not a broadline comp), SYY 23.0x. Sysco is the only clean comparable, and it is the operating-margin and ROA leader (3.8% and 6.8% on its most recent fiscal year versus US Foods' FY2025 3.0% and 4.8%). On trailing GAAP, US Foods at 37.5x carries a 63% premium to Sysco. On comparable adjusted forward bases the premium narrows to something modest, justified by faster EBITDA growth. The stock is now priced for the growth differential to persist, with no discount remaining for the risk that it does not.

The historical anchor is instructive. In October 2025 the shares traded near $75, roughly 15.6x the FY2026E adjusted EPS midpoint. Today they trade at 22.8x the same number. Estimates have not risen over that window — they have fallen slightly. The entire move is multiple expansion, and it has carried the stock through the sell-side mean target of $112.20 (1.9% above spot). The $61 low target is a stale outlier; the $120 median implies +9.0% and the $127 high +15.3%.

We normally treat consensus targets as lagging indicators on names with rising estimates and reasonable multiples. That condition is not met here: revisions are negative, not positive, and the multiple has expanded 46% in ten months. When price and target converge on a name whose estimates are drifting down, the target is a genuine ceiling rather than a stale floor.

Free cash flow support is thin. A 4.0–4.2% yield on flat-to-modestly-up FCF, with the entire yield committed to repurchases and none to a dividend or to deleveraging, does not create an absolute-value floor. Receivables growing $299M on 3.6% sales growth warrants monitoring, though the allowance moved only from $30M to $32M, consistent with growth-driven rather than collection-driven expansion.

**Base case $115.** 21x next-year consensus $5.45 = $114.45; cross-checked at 13.0x an FY2027E EBITDA of ~$2.32B (EV $30.2B less $5.18B net debt over ~211M shares = ~$118). Call it $115, +4.4% from the basis.

**Bull case $127.** FY2026 adjusted EPS at the high end ($4.94), FY2027 at ~$5.70, 22–23x on continued independent acceleration and cost-program delivery. +15.3%.

**Bear case $88.** Multiple reverts to 17–18x forward on independent traffic following chain traffic lower, or a GPO pricing renegotiation, on FY2027 EPS of ~$5.10. −20.1%.

Weighting 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear gives an expected return of approximately −0.8%. A more charitable 55/20/25 split produces roughly +0.5%. The distribution is skewed against the buyer.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is three broadliners (Sysco, US Foods, Performance Foodservice), system distributors serving large chains, and specialists, with adjacent pressure from club stores, cash-and-carry, online wholesalers and grocery.

| | USFD (FY2025) | SYY (FY~Jun 2025) | PFGC (FY~Jun 2025) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating margin | 3.0% | 3.8% | 1.3% |
| Net margin | 1.7% | 2.2% | 0.5% |
| FCF margin | 2.4% | 2.0% | 1.1% |
| ROA | 4.8% | 6.8% | 1.9% |
| Asset turnover | 2.83x | 3.04x | 3.54x |
| Equity/assets | 30.9% | 6.8% | 25.0% |
| Altman Z | 3.86 | 3.65 | 4.42 |

Fiscal periods differ and PFG's mix includes convenience and vending, so its 1.3% operating margin is not a clean broadline read. Sysco's 99.9% ROE is an artifact of a 6.8% equity/assets ratio, not superior operating economics — ROA is the informative comparison, and Sysco leads. US Foods generates the highest FCF margin of the three at 2.4%. Q2 FY2026 GAAP operating margin of 4.2% suggests the gap to Sysco has narrowed, but comparing one strong quarter to a full year overstates the convergence.

Trajectory is the strongest element of the case: 21 consecutive quarters of independent share gains, 23 consecutive quarters of healthcare share gains, net new accounts at a three-year high, Pronto scaling with raised 2027 targets, private label at 35% penetration, and gross-profit-per-case growth outpacing opex-per-case growth. Against that, the company's own disclosure of near-zero switching costs and low barriers to entry, plus explicit reference to competitors deploying AI/ML to pressure industry margins, means the share gains must be re-earned every quarter and are the natural target of a price response.

The dormant Performance Food Group approach — externally reported (Seeking Alpha/MSN, August 7, 2025) as a statement by CEO Flitman that US Foods approached PFG about a combination and PFG declined, with no mention in either the FY2025 10-K or the Q2 FY2026 10-Q — represents real but low-probability optionality. A #2/#3 broadline combination would face heavy antitrust scrutiny, and $3.1B of restricted payment capacity at 2.58x leverage would not fund it without a significant leverage increase or equity issuance. It should not be underwritten as a catalyst.

## Risk Assessment

**1. A single GPO governs pricing on ~14% of net sales.** Approximately 27% of FY2025 sales were made under GPO-negotiated terms, including ~14% to members of one unnamed GPO — roughly $5.5B of annual revenue — under agreements generally terminable on 30 days' to six months' notice, with the 10-K acknowledging the company "has experienced some pricing pressure from customers associated with a GPO." This is a pricing-terms concentration rather than a credit concentration (no customer exceeded 2% of sales), so loss of the relationship would not mechanically forfeit the volume; the exposure is primarily to margin renegotiation. It is nonetheless the largest identifiable single event risk in the filings and is invisible to standard concentration screens. Identity, terms and renewal timing are undisclosed.

**2. Restaurant demand.** Management characterized the market as "pressured but stable" with continued industry foot-traffic challenges. Chain volume has declined for consecutive periods (−3.5% FY2025, −1.5% Q2 FY2026). If independents follow chains lower, the mix-shift machine stalls at 22.8x forward earnings.

**3. Zero switching cost, disclosed by the company.** Most customers buy on individual purchase orders with no obligation to continue.

**4. Labor.** Approximately 6,600 of 30,000 associates are union-represented across 58 CBAs; 11 CBAs covering ~2,100 associates come due in FY2026, and the 10-K warns specifically about sympathy strikes propagating across facilities. Multiemployer pension contributions were $60M in FY2025 and $35M in H1 FY2026 (~$70M annualized), a rising line.

**5. Rates and hedging.** ~34% of $5,237M floats, with only $450M notional capped through April 2027; a 1% move is ~$19M pre-tax, roughly 1.5% of FY2026E adjusted net income. Modest in isolation, but covenants restrict dividends, additional debt, liens and mergers, constraining strategic optionality.

**6. Goodwill.** $5,796M is 40% of total assets and ~136% of book equity. The FY2025 annual test was qualitative only for both goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles, and it still produced a $13M impairment on abandoned trademarks. A qualitative-only test on a balance this large leaves less disclosed cushion than a full fair-value test.

**7. Structural demand shift.** The Business section now explicitly cites pharmaceutical therapies reshaping dining behavior — lighter options, smaller plates, vegetable-forward menus. Meats and seafood are 35% of sales. Slow burn, not near-term, but it argues against paying a premium multiple for terminal-value certainty.

**8. Governance.** The CEO combined the Chair role between the February 2026 10-K and the August 2026 10-Q, reducing independent leadership at the top of the board; the May 14, 2026 restated charter and bylaws were not reviewed and whether a lead independent director was designated is unverified. CEO total compensation rose 61% in FY2025 to $18.06M without an explanation available in the structured data. Offsetting: performance measures (Adjusted EBITDA, Distribution Cost Per Case, Adjusted ROIC, Independent Market Share) are unusually well matched to the strategy, and five-year cumulative TSR of 229.27 versus a peer index of 179.96 supports the pay outcome.

**9. Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director through June 2026. The CFO sold 20,000 shares at $91.19 in March 2026 alongside a gift and trust transfers — routine estate planning for a nine-year CFO, and not a directional signal, but there is no offsetting buying to corroborate the bull case at $110. The CEO retains 497,587 shares (~$55M), so alignment is substantial.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is maximally complacent at the highs. Near-term ATM IV of 26.0% sits slightly below 1-year realized volatility of 28.9% and in the 20th percentile of the current chain, with put/call skew at −6.6% (call-heavy) and a put/call open-interest ratio of **0.03** — effectively no hedging demand across 112 contracts. IV collapsed from 37.7% to 26.0% between August 3 and August 11 while the price rose 9.4%, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.88 to 0.03: the earnings event resolved, and holders removed protection rather than adding it. The term structure is mildly backwardated (26.0% near versus 23.1% at six months), consistent with residual near-term event pricing rather than a regime signal.

For a fresh long, this is unfavorable. Cheap volatility and absent puts mean the options market is not pricing the specific downside catalysts identified above (GPO renegotiation, independent traffic rollover, CBA cycle). It also means protection is inexpensive for existing holders who prefer to hedge rather than trim: the January 2027 tenor prices a ±13.6% move with a −4.2% skew. Short interest of 5.3% and 3.5 days to cover indicate the bearish case is uncrowded — more room for a downside move, but also no informed short base corroborating it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($127, +15.3%)

Independent case volume has accelerated for five consecutive quarters and reached +5.1%, net new accounts are at a three-year high, and the new seller compensation plan went live in June — the setup for H2 acceleration exists. The 53rd week lands in Q4 and mechanically helps. Named cost programs (>$300M cumulative Strategic Vendor Management by 2027, >$75M indirect procurement in 2026) support continued per-case spread expansion, which was reflected in Q2 (GP/case +5% versus opex/case +3.7%). Adjusted EBITDA per case reached $2.73 (+8.3%). Pronto's 2027 target was raised to >$1.7B from $1.5B. Leverage at 2.58x sits at the low end of target with no maturities before 2028 and $1.8B of liquidity, and $640M of authorization plus ~$3.1B of restricted-payment capacity supports continued share-count reduction. If FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at the high end ($4.94) and FY2027 reaches $5.70, 22–23x delivers $127 — matching the sell-side high. Dormant PFG consolidation optionality is a free call.

### Bear Case ($88, −20.1%)

The multiple, not the operations, is the risk. At 22.8x FY2026E adjusted EPS and 13.5x EV/EBITDA the stock is at a multi-year high multiple on a business whose organic case volume grew 1.4% in H1 and whose FY2026 free cash flow is tracking flat at a 4.0–4.2% yield. Consensus already sits at the bottom of the guided EPS range, and our own build-up ($4.63) lands below the midpoint, so the near-term surprise distribution is skewed to disappointment rather than beats. The single-GPO relationship covering ~14% of sales can be repriced on as little as 30 days' notice. Independent restaurant traffic is "pressured but stable," and the 21-quarter share-gain streak was earned in a market whose total volume is barely growing, so a demand downturn hits a distributor that has already harvested the easy chain runoff. A quarter of guided EPS growth is share count, and repurchases at $110 rather than $85 are materially less accretive. Reversion to 17–18x forward on FY2027 EPS of ~$5.10 puts the stock at $88.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base $115 (+4.4%), bull $127 (+15.3%), bear $88 (−20.1%). Probability-weighted expected return of approximately −1% to +0.5% across reasonable weightings, with a downside case 4.6x the magnitude of the base-case upside.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength now rather than waiting for a catalyst; the Q2 beat and the post-print re-rating have already occurred. Reduce position size by roughly half of the exposure held before the August move, retaining a core holding to participate if H2 independent volume accelerates as management expects. Do not add. Holders unwilling to sell for tax or mandate reasons should note that January 2027 protection is available with implied volatility below realized and skew mildly call-heavy, an inexpensive substitute for trimming. The next natural full-exit decision point is the Q3 FY2026 print in early November 2026.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.**
1. Price below $95 (≈19.7x FY2026E adjusted EPS midpoint, ≈17.4x next-year consensus) with independent case volume still ≥+4% — that restores a base-case upside of ~20% against unchanged fundamentals.
2. Q3 FY2026 total case volume ≥+3.0% organic with adjusted EBITDA margin holding ≥5.5%, plus next-year consensus revised *up* rather than down. This would validate the guidance top half and justify the current multiple.
3. FY2026 free cash flow tracking above $1.05B (i.e., H2 FCF above ~$500M), demonstrating that earnings growth is converting to cash rather than into receivables.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. Disclosure of a GPO renegotiation, non-renewal, or an identified step-down in GPO-related pricing.
2. Independent restaurant case volume decelerating below +3.0% in any quarter, or turning negative — the mix-shift engine stalling.
3. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut, or FY2027 consensus falling below $5.20 (a further 4.6% reduction), while the stock holds above 21x forward.
4. Net leverage rising above 3.0x to fund repurchases or a large acquisition.

**Reassessment timeline.** Early November 2026 (Q3 FY2026 print, which will settle whether the +2.5–4.5% volume guidance lands near 2.5% as the H1 data implies), with an interim check on the FY2026 CBA renegotiation cycle covering ~2,100 associates.

## Conclusion

US Foods is a better business than it was a year ago. Independent case volume is accelerating, adjusted EBITDA margin set a record at 5.7%, per-case gross profit is outrunning per-case operating expense, the cost programs are delivering disclosed run-rates, the ABL has been upsized and extended to 2031 with no maturities before 2028, and leverage sits at 2.58x inside a 2–3x target. Management is paid on independent market share, distribution cost per case and adjusted ROIC — the right instrumentation for this strategy — and executed repurchases at $80–86 that look well-timed against today's price.

None of that is the question. The question is what the improvement is worth, and the answer is that the market has already paid for it and then some: a roughly 20% advance in eight weeks while forward estimates were cut, taking the multiple from 15.6x FY2026E adjusted EPS last October to 22.8x today, through a sell-side mean target now 1.9% below spot, on a 4.0–4.2% free cash flow yield where free cash flow is flat and every dollar of it is committed to repurchases that are themselves less accretive at $110 than at $85. A third of the headline gross-margin expansion was a LIFO swing. A quarter of guided EPS growth is share count. Consensus sits at the bottom of the guided range because the arithmetic of the guidance requires H2 acceleration the H1 volume data does not support.
